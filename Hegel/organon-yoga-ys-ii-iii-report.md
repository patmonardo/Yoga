# Organon Yoga: Complete Commentary on Yoga Sūtras II and III

## Working master report

## Editorial priority

This report now precedes the projected Organon Gītā Report.

The reason is systematic. The Kośa study has supplied the differentiated
causal, ethical, physical, temporal, and Path structures required to complete
the Saṃyama-bhūmis. Yoga Sūtras II and III can therefore be interpreted as one
continuous Practical Reason:

```text
YS II
    constructs and purifies the practical Bhūmi

YS III
    applies Saṃyama through the Bhūmis
    and produces their determinate Jñānas
```

The Organon Gītā Report remains necessary, but it should follow this work so
that its account of Kriyā, Sannyāsa, Jñāna, and Yoga can presuppose the complete
Yoga architecture rather than anticipate it incompletely.

## Governing thesis

The BrahmaCakra supplied the transcendental Principle of Yoga. It established
the unity of the Path but did not yet differentiate all the grounds through
which that unity becomes practical and knowable.

The present report argues:

> Yoga Sūtras II and III together exhibit Practical Reason as Kriyā Yoga and
> Saṃyama. Book II constructs the disciplined causal ground of practice. Book
> III performs Schema Inference upon the appearances proper to each Bhūmi,
> producing determinate Jñāna and culminating in non-arising.

The complete movement is:

```text
Transcendental Principle
    → conditioned appearance
    → Kriyā
    → purified Bhūmi
    → Saṃyama
    → Schema Inference
    → Jñāna
    → Vairāgya
    → Non-Arising
```

## Why the Kośa is required

Yoga supplies the universal operator:

```text
Dhāraṇā
    → holds the Artha in a determinate position

Dhyāna
    → sustains its continuous determination

Samādhi
    → realizes cognition and Artha in unity

Saṃyama
    → operates that unity as Practical Reason
```

But Saṃyama becomes determinate only through a Bhūmi. The Kośa supplies the
relational content required to construct such a ground:

```text
Dhātu
    determinate domain

Indriya
    governing efficacy

Loka
    organized field of appearance

Karma
    practical production and causal propagation

Anuśaya
    conditioned structure deposited in the continuum

Mārga
    reflexive reorganization of causal practice

Jñāna
    the Path's determinate comprehension of its field
```

The Kośa is therefore not supplementary Buddhist terminology added to Yoga.
It provides the differentiated Dharmaskandha through which the
Saṃyama-bhūmis can become complete.

## The eleven Saṃyama-bhūmis

The controlling architecture is an eleven-Bhūmi system.

```text
Bhūmis 1–5
    Natural Object

Bhūmis 6–10
    Spiritual Object

Bhūmi 11
    return of the complete structure into its Principle
```

Sāṃkhya's fivefold Object is traversed twice:

| Natural Object | Spiritual Object |
|---:|---:|
| Bhūmi 1 | Bhūmi 6 |
| Bhūmi 2 | Bhūmi 7 |
| Bhūmi 3 | Bhūmi 8 |
| Bhūmi 4 | Bhūmi 9 |
| Bhūmi 5 | Bhūmi 10 |

The second traversal does not add another external Object. It comprehends and
reconstructs each natural determination through Puruṣa-jñāna.

The presently established stations are:

| Bhūmi | Determination | Primary philosophical resource |
|---:|---|---|
| 1–3 | first rational articulation of the Natural Object | Hegel |
| 4 | Ethics | Kośa Dharmaskandha and Yoga discipline |
| 5 | Physics | Kośa Dharmaskandha and Yoga cosmophysical determination |
| 6 | beginning of the Spiritual Object through Puruṣa-jñāna | Yoga–Sāṃkhya |
| 7–10 | spiritual reconstruction of the remaining fivefold Object | to be determined verse by verse |
| 11 | principial return and genetic closure | Fichte |

The assignments of Bhūmis 7–10 must emerge from the ordered Yoga text and the
Kośa traversal. They are not to be filled by analogy in advance.

## Natural Object: Bhūmis 1–5

The Natural Object is not nature imagined as a collection of external things.
It is the fivefold construction through which objective appearance becomes a
determinate field for Saṃyama.

Hegel supplies the rational articulation of the first three Bhūmis. Bhūmis 4
and 5 require the concentrated development of Ethics and Physics.

### Bhūmi 4: Ethics

The Ethical Bhūmi must organize at least:

```text
intention
course of action
body and speech
restraint and non-restraint
livelihood
wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate quality
causal result
discipline
cultivation
transition into Path
```

The Karma chapter of the Kośa is indispensable here. Its fruit analyses show
that action cannot be represented by a linear deed-result arrow. A complete
ethical determination requires source, result, relation-type, quality, time,
Bhūmi, and Path-status.

### Bhūmi 5: Physics

The Physical Bhūmi must organize at least:

```text
body
sense-capacity
elemental construction
domain
world-system
space
time
motion
causal support
environmental efficacy
conditions of manifestation
```

Physics here is metaphysical: it determines how an objective field is
constructed and how its appearances can stand in valid relations. It is not a
substitute for empirical natural science, but the rational organization of the
natural Object that makes concrete inquiry possible.

The immense Dharmaskandha should focus especially on these Ethical and
Physical Bhūmis. Its entries must be organized as determinations within a
ground, not accumulated as isolated dictionary items.

## Spiritual Object: Bhūmis 6–10

The Spiritual Object begins with Puruṣa-jñāna.

```text
Natural Object
    appears self-standing through constructed grounds

Puruṣa-jñāna
    comprehends the Principle for which the field operates

Vairāgya
    removes the apparent ultimacy of the constructed field

Spiritual Object
    reconstructs the fivefold Object as a system of Meaning
```

Bhūmi 6 requires the dissolution of the four constructed elemental grounds
extending from wind through earth. Dissolution does not discard their
determinate content. It negates their claim to independent self-sufficiency
and returns their relations to the Principle that makes them intelligible.

Puruṣa-jñāna therefore has a special station:

```text
ordinary Jñāna
    → knowledge of a determination within a Bhūmi

Puruṣa-jñāna
    → knowledge of the Principle and telos
      of the entire field of appearances
```

This is the beginning of Sannyāsa as the Non-Arising Path.

## Sannyāsa and the Non-Arising Path

Sannyāsa must not be reduced to the external omission of action. Its complete
form is the practical non-reproduction of an invalid causal schema.

```text
afflictive appearance
    → Saṃyoga
    → appropriation
    → karmic production
    → renewed appearance

Seeing the Truths
    → Puruṣa-jñāna
    → Vairāgya
    → Viyoga
    → Non-Arising
```

The Path is the truth of karmic courses of action because it is a higher-order
course:

```text
Karmapatha
    → action operating within conditioned causality

Mārga
    → action comprehending and reorganizing
      the causal form of action

Sannyāsa
    → the Path no longer reproducing
      the invalid schema of conjunction
```

## Saṃyama as Schema Inference

The Path is a type of Schema Inference.

```text
Principle
    → supplies the universal rule

Bhūmi
    → supplies the determinate field of appearances

Schema
    → determines how the Principle applies in that field

Saṃyama
    → executes the valid inferential transitions

Jñāna
    → is the complete relational structure thereby disclosed
```

Formally:

```text
Saṃyama(Principle, Bhūmi, AppearancesOfBhūmi)
    → Jñāna(Principle, Bhūmi)
```

Jñāna is not an isolated conclusion. It is the complete crystal structure of
valid secondary inferences generated from one Principle within one Bhūmi.

The output of one Path operation can become the transformed ground of the
next:

```text
SchemaInference(P, B0) → J1 / B1
SchemaInference(P, B1) → J2 / B2
SchemaInference(P, B2) → J3 / B3
```

The Path is dialectical because each valid Kriyā transforms the conditions of
the next inference.

## Compulsory commentary method

Every sūtra in Books II and III will be studied through the same controlled
sequence:

```text
1. Sanskrit witness
2. Padaccheda and morphology
3. Grammar and close construe
4. Literal translation
5. Vyāsa determination
6. Kośa determination
7. Principle
8. Bhūmi and its proper appearances
9. Schema Inference
10. Kriyā or operation
11. Jñāna or result
12. transition to the next sūtra and Bhūmi
```

The Kośa determination should use its systematic authoring sequence where
applicable:

```text
Dhātu → Indriya → Loka → Karma → Anuśaya → Mārga → Jñāna
```

Not every sūtra will instantiate every coordinate equally. An absent or
inapplicable coordinate must be explained rather than filled artificially.

## Source discipline

The commentary will keep four voices identifiable:

```text
Patañjali
    Sanskrit sūtra and its syntax

Vyāsa
    historical Yoga Bhāṣya determination

Vasubandhu and the Kośa
    differentiated causal and practical architecture

Organon synthesis
    Hegelian and Fichtean comprehension of systematic necessity
```

The purpose of this distinction is not to weaken the Organon thesis. It allows
the stronger interpretation to be demonstrated: the Hegelian-transcendental
and Kośa-informed reading explains the sequence, relations, and practical
necessity of the Yoga text more powerfully than isolated lexical commentary.
