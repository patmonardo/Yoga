## 3. Literal Translation

### Introductory Prose

Then, seeing no means other than knowledge of the Self for rescuing Arjuna,
whose mind was confused about dharma, who possessed false knowledge, and
who was submerged in a great ocean of grief, Bhagavān Vāsudeva, wishing out
of compassion to raise Arjuna by leading him to knowledge of the Self,
speaks:

### Verse Commentary

“Those who should not be grieved for,” and so forth. Those who are not to
be grieved for are the ungrievable—Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and the others—because
they are of good conduct and because they are eternal in their ultimate
nature. You grieved for those who are not to be grieved for, [thinking,]
“They are dying because of me; what shall I do with royal pleasures and
the like, bereft of them?”

You speak words of those who possess discernment, of intelligent people.
Thus you display in yourself this contradiction of folly and wisdom, like
a madman: this is the meaning. For the *paṇḍitāḥ*, the knowers of the Self,
do not grieve for those whose vital breaths have gone—the dead—or for those
whose vital breaths have not gone—the living. Those whose understanding
concerns the Self are *paṇḍitāḥ*, since they have understanding concerning
the Self, as the Śruti says, “Having become learned” (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka
Upaniṣad* 3.5.1). But in ultimate reality you grieve for those who are
eternal and not to be grieved for; therefore you are deluded: this is the
meaning.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The introduction states why Vāsudeva teaches: Śaṅkara presents knowledge
of the Self as the means for raising Arjuna from his confusion and grief.
The verse commentary then proceeds in four steps:

```text
identify Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and others as not to be grieved for
    → state why: good conduct and eternity in their ultimate nature
    → expose the contradiction between Arjuna's wise-sounding speech
      and his grief
    → define the discerning as knowers of the Self, then apply that
      definition to Arjuna's grief
```

## 5. The Contradiction in Arjuna's Speech

Śaṅkara reads *prajñā-vādān* as speech belonging to discerning or
intelligent people. Arjuna speaks in that register, but his grief shows
that he has not attained the understanding implied by it. The commentary
describes the two as a contradiction displayed in one person, “like a
madman.” This is the local argument; it does not require importing a
broader diagnosis of Arjuna's character.

## 6. The Meaning of *Paṇḍita*

Śaṅkara glosses *paṇḍitāḥ* as *ātmajñāḥ*, knowers of the Self. He then
explains *paṇḍā* as understanding whose subject is the Self, and supports
the word-family with the cited Śruti phrase *pāṇḍityaṃ nirvidya*
(*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.5.1). This is the Bhāṣya's contextual
determination of the word in this verse.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The introduction and the verse commentary are separate textual movements:
the former frames the teaching as a response to Arjuna's condition, while
the latter explains the verse's contrast between his speech and grief.
Śaṅkara grounds the term *paṇḍita* in knowledge of the Self and applies
that determination to the specific case. Broader claims about Advaita
metaphysics are outside the scope of this passage-level report.
