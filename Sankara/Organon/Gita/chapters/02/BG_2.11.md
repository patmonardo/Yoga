# Bhagavad Gītā 2.11

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> श्रीभगवानुवाच —
>
> अशोच्यानन्वशोचस्त्वं प्रज्ञावादांश्च भाषसे ।
>
> गतासूनगतासूंश्च नानुशोचन्ति पण्डिताः ॥ २.११ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> śrī-bhagavān uvāca —
>
> aśocyān anvaśocas tvaṃ prajñā-vādāṃś ca bhāṣase |
>
> gatāsūn agatāsūṃś ca nānuśocanti paṇḍitāḥ || 2.11 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
aśocyān anvaśocas tvam
    aśocyān + anvaśocaḥ + tvam

prajñā-vādāṃś ca bhāṣase
    prajñā-vādān + ca + bhāṣase

gatāsūn agatāsūṃś ca nānuśocanti paṇḍitāḥ
    gata-asūn + agata-asūn + ca + na + anuśocanti + paṇḍitāḥ
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| aśocyān | accusative masculine plural of *aśocya* | those who should not be grieved for |
| anvaśocaḥ | second-person singular imperfect of *anu-√śuc* | you grieved for |
| tvam | nominative singular pronoun | you, Arjuna |
| prajñā-vādān | accusative masculine plural compound | words or claims of discernment |
| ca | conjunction | and |
| bhāṣase | second-person singular middle present of *bhāṣ* | you speak |
| gata-asūn | accusative masculine plural compound | those whose vital breaths have gone; the dead |
| agata-asūn | accusative masculine plural compound | those whose vital breaths have not gone; the living |
| na | negation | not |
| anuśocanti | third-person plural present of *anu-√śuc* | they grieve for |
| paṇḍitāḥ | nominative masculine plural of *paṇḍita* | the discerning; Śaṅkara specifies knowers of the Self |

## 4. Grammar

The first half addresses Arjuna directly:

```text
aśocyān anvaśocaḥ tvam
    you grieved for those who should not be grieved for

prajñā-vādān ca bhāṣase
    and you speak words of discernment
```

The second half states what the *paṇḍitāḥ* do not grieve for. *Gatāsūn* and
*agatāsūn* are coordinated accusative plurals, distinguished by whether the
vital breaths have departed. Śaṅkara reads them as the dead and the living,
respectively, and defines the *paṇḍitāḥ* here as knowers of the Self.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

You grieved for those who should not be grieved for, and you speak words of
discernment. The discerning do not grieve for the dead or for the living.

### Bhāṣya-informed Study Translation

The Blessed Lord said: You grieve for those who should not be grieved for,
even as you speak in the language of discernment. Those who know the Self
grieve neither for the dead nor for the living.

## 6. Philosophical Translation and Commentary Reading

Śaṅkara makes the verse an explicit contrast between Arjuna's speech and
his understanding. Arjuna speaks as though he were discerning, yet grieves
for people whom the commentary describes as well-conducted and ultimately
eternal. The *paṇḍita* is not merely a learned speaker in this passage:
Śaṅkara identifies the term with one whose understanding concerns the
Self.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Working rendering | Passage-specific determination |
|---|---|---|
| *aśocya* | not to be grieved for | Śaṅkara applies it to Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and others, citing their good conduct and ultimate nature |
| *prajñā-vāda* | words of discernment | Speech that sounds wise; the commentary contrasts it with Arjuna's grief |
| *gatāsūn / agatāsūn* | the dead / the living | Literally, those whose vital breaths have gone / not gone |
| *paṇḍita* | discerning person | Śaṅkara's local gloss is *ātmajña*, a knower of the Self |
| *paramārthatas* | in ultimate reality | A term in the Bhāṣya's application of the verse, not in the verse itself |

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
Arjuna grieves for those described as not to be grieved for
    while speaking in the language of discernment
        → his conduct and speech conflict
            → the discerning, defined here as knowers of the Self,
              do not grieve for either the dead or the living
```

The final application is Śaṅkara's: Arjuna grieves for those who are
ultimately eternal and not to be grieved for; this exposes his lack of the
discernment implied by his speech.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The identification of *paṇḍitāḥ* with knowers of the Self is grounded in
Śaṅkara's Bhāṣya and should not be mistaken for a lexical definition that
applies without qualification in every context. The commentary also gives
the immediate imagined content of Arjuna's grief: “They are dying because
of me; what shall I do, bereft of them, with royal pleasures and the like?”
