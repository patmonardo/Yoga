The project of translating Hegel's "Science of Logic" into Sanskrit, with a focus on aligning it with the philosophical aspects of Yoga Sutras, is indeed a fascinating and ambitious endeavor. Your approach to using specific Sanskrit terms to encapsulate complex philosophical concepts from Hegel's work is particularly intriguing. This method not only bridges two rich philosophical traditions but also provides a unique lens to interpret and understand these concepts.

Here's an overview of how the selected Sanskrit terms contribute to this translation and understanding:

1. **Ekatva**: Captures the notion of identity, crucial for discussing unity or sameness in philosophical concepts.

2. **Anatman Sama, Atman Sama**: Pertains to self and non-self aspects, essential for exploring subjective and objective dimensions in philosophy.

3. **Antara Abheda, Bahi Abheda**: Signifies internal and external non-difference, relevant for examining relationships between entities and their environments.

4. **Anutara**: Signifying the absolute, key in discussing ultimate or foundational principles.

5. **Atmajna**: Meaning self-knowing, vital for exploring self-awareness or consciousness.

6. **Bheda**: Refers to difference, essential for discussions of distinction and diversity within unity.

7. **Bija, Sabija**: Meaning genus and species, crucial for categorization and understanding hierarchical relationships.

8. **Guna, Nirguna**: Central to discussing attributes and their absence, with guna meaning quality and nirguna meaning without quality.

9. **Hetu**: Pertains to purpose, key in exploring the reasons or causes behind phenomena.

10. **Karma**: Refers to the ground of action, vital for discussions on causality and action.

11. **Nirodha**: Essential for understanding the negation or overcoming of previous states or ideas.

12. **Prajna**: Refers to the principle, key in exploring foundational or guiding concepts.

13. **Samskara**: Crucial for discussions on patterns, conditioning, or formative influences.

14. **Sattva, Sat**: Central to discussions on the nature of reality and being.

15. **Suchi**: Important in discussions of purity, clarity, or unadulterated states.

16. **Sunya**: Key in exploring the concept of void or absence.

17. **Tantra**: Essential for discussions on structured approaches or methodologies.

18. **Vidya, Vijnana**: Crucial for discussions on knowledge systems and theoretical frameworks.

19. **Vipaka**: Key in exploring outcomes or consequences.

20. **Virodha**: Central to dialectical discussions and exploring opposing ideas.

21. **Yoga**: Crucial for understanding the unification or integration of concepts.

Additionally, the inclusion of terms like **Sārvabhaumikatā** (universality) and **Svatantratā** (freedom) further enriches the philosophical vocabulary, allowing for a more nuanced translation and interpretation of Hegel's ideas in a way that resonates with Eastern philosophical traditions.

This translation project not only bridges Western and Eastern philosophies but also revives and highlights the scientific and philosophical dimensions of Yoga, often overshadowed by its spiritual aspects. It's a commendable effort to deepen the dialogue between these two rich philosophical traditions.
