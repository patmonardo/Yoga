# Distributing Hegel's Relative Necessity across Yoga Sūtra IV.29–32

## The problem

In `dharma-greater-varttika.tex`, Yoga IV.29–32 are printed together before
Hegel's complete section:

> B. Relative Necessity or Real Actuality, Possibility, and Necessity

The four Sūtras form one movement. IV.29 gives its governing actuality,
Dharma-Megha Samādhi. IV.30–32 state three necessary consequences which can
look like separate saintly attainments if detached from the Science of
Knowing.

Hegel's order must not be altered. The task is to locate four semantic
transitions inside his three numbered moments:

```text
B.1  Real Actuality
B.2  Real Possibility and the complete circle of conditions
B.3  Real Necessity and the exposure of its relativity
```

As in the Contingency section, one numbered moment must be divided. Here B.3
contains both the positive form of Real Necessity and the demonstration of
its remaining relativity. Those are the natural grounds for IV.31 and IV.32.

## Proposed distribution

```text
IV.29    Real Actuality as self-manifesting, content-filled power

IV.30    Real Possibility as the self-sublating circle of conditions

IV.31    Real Necessity: what is really possible cannot be otherwise

IV.32    Relative Necessity exposes its contingency and yields
         the unity called Absolute Actuality
```

This distribution preserves every step of Hegel's text and lands exactly at
the opening of Absolute Necessity in IV.33.

## IV.29 — Dharma-Megha as Real Actuality

`prasaṃkhyāne 'py akusīdasya sarvathā viveka-khyāter dharma-meghaḥ samādhiḥ`

> For one without investment even in complete discriminative knowledge,
> whose Viveka is continuous in every way, there is Dharma-Megha Samādhi.

### Hegelian span

Use B.1 in full, beginning:

> The necessity which has resulted is formal because its moments are formal.

Continue through the transition to content-filled Real Actuality:

> This actuality is real actuality.

Include the decisive manifestation:

> What is actual can act; something announces its actuality by what it
> produces.

End with Real Actuality's first distinction from its possibility:

> it is in one of the determinations of form and hence distinguished, as
> immediate existent, from the in-itself or possibility.

### Why it fits Dharma-Megha

Formal Necessity ended IV.28 as the immediate conversion of possibility and
actuality. Its moments lacked self-subsistent shape and its unity remained
indifferent to difference.

In B.1, that unity receives manifold content:

```text
formal Necessity
empty immediate conversion of modal forms
                    ↓ receives content
Real Actuality
the unity preserves itself throughout a manifold world
```

Dharma-Megha is precisely this saturation of the field by Dharma. The Cloud
is not one empty state beyond determinate content; it is the actuality that
preserves itself throughout the manifold and manifests itself through what
it produces.

```text
Dharma-Megha                    Hegelian Real Actuality

Viveka continuous              immanent reflection preserved
through every determination    throughout manifold existence

no investment in result        no external dependence for actuality

Dharma pours into the field    actuality announces itself by producing
```

### Why IV.29 governs the whole section

IV.30–32 are not additional principles beyond Dharma-Megha. They unfold what
this Real Actuality necessarily does:

```text
Real Actuality
   ├── sublates its external conditions           IV.30
   ├── establishes identity of possibility
   │   and necessity                              IV.31
   └── completes the limited sequence of
       Relative Necessity                         IV.32
```

The saintly results are consequences of the Science of Knowing becoming
content-filled Actuality.

## IV.30 — Kleśa-Karma as the circle of conditions

`tataḥ kleśa-karma-nivṛttiḥ`

> From that, Kleśa and Karma cease.

### Hegelian span

Use B.2, beginning:

> This possibility, as the in-itself of real actuality, is itself real
> possibility, at first the in-itself full of content.

Continue through the analysis of circumstances and conditions:

> Real possibility thus constitutes the totality of conditions, a dispersed
> actuality which is not reflected into itself.

Include the culmination:

> Whenever all the conditions of a fact are completely present, the fact is
> actually there.

Continue through the twofold self-sublation of Real Possibility and end at:

> What disappears is consequently this, that actuality was determined as
> the possibility or the in-itself of an other, and, conversely, the
> possibility as an actuality which is not that of which it is the
> possibility.

### Why it fits Kleśa-Karma

Real Possibility is not empty thinkability. It is the actual manifold of
circumstances required for a fact:

```text
conditions and circumstances
           ↓ complete circle
the fact is actually there
```

This is the objective structure of Karma. Karma is not a supernatural entry
in a moral ledger. It is the manifold of actual conditions through which a
formative Principle produces its fact.

Kleśa supplies the contradictory formative determination; Karma supplies
and reproduces its real conditions:

```text
Kleśa
conditioned contradiction held as true
        ↓ enacted through
Karma
the real circle of conditions
        ↓ produces
the afflicted fact
```

### Why cessation is self-sublation

The circle of conditions does not disappear through an external miracle. As
Real Possibility, it contains both possibility and actuality. When complete,
it sublates its scattered status and is the fact itself.

```text
dispersed actuality
conditions existing for another fact
                    ↓ conditions complete
self-sublating Real Possibility
                    ↓
actuality no longer outside its possibility
```

What ceases is the external circuit in which conditions, fact, and ground
stand apart and reproduce one another as Kleśa-Karma.

`Nivṛtti` is therefore the immanent completion of conditioned production,
not the extinction of all Kriyā.

### The first saintly consequence

The first “Siddhi” after Dharma-Megha is purification:

```text
the complete Actuality no longer depends upon
an alien circle of afflictive conditions
                    ↓
Kleśa-Karma ceases
```

This is the Hegelian side of the Vajropama moment already identified through
Fichte: the complete relation can no longer be fractured by an external
condition.

## IV.31 — Infinite Knowledge as Real Necessity

`tadā sarvāvaraṇa-malāpetasya jñānasyānantyāj jñeyam alpam`

> Then, because Knowledge is infinite when every covering impurity has fallen
> away, the knowable is little.

### Hegelian span

Begin B.3:

> The negation of real possibility is thus its self-identity; inasmuch as in
> its sublating it is thus within itself the recoiling of this sublating, it
> is real necessity.

Use the decisive proposition:

> What is really possible can no longer be otherwise; under the given
> conditions and circumstances, nothing else can follow.

Continue through:

> Real possibility and necessity are, therefore, only apparently
> distinguished.

End provisionally with:

> Real possibility is therefore a connection full of content.

### Why it fits infinite Knowledge

The coverings have fallen when possibility is no longer imagined as a realm
of arbitrary alternatives outside Actuality. All real conditions are
present within the content-filled connection.

```text
before
indefinitely many merely conceivable alternatives
appear to stand outside Knowing

after
Real Possibility contains the complete actual conditions
and is identical with Necessity
```

Thus the knowable becomes `little`:

> Nothing independently possible remains outside the necessary connection
> for Knowledge to chase as a foreign object.

This is not an empirical person possessing infinitely many facts. It is the
infinitude of the Principle that contains the conditions through which any
determinate fact is really possible.

### The second saintly consequence

```text
all coverings removed
        ↓
Real Possibility is transparent as Necessity
        ↓
under the complete conditions nothing else can follow
        ↓
Knowledge infinite; independent jñeya little
```

The saintly formulation is the experienced actuality of a modal result.

## IV.32 — The completion and limit of Relative Necessity

`tataḥ kṛtārthānāṃ pariṇāma-krama-samāptir guṇānām`

> Then, because the Guṇas have accomplished their Artha, the sequence of
> their transformations comes to completion.

### Hegelian span

Continue B.3 from:

> But this necessity is at the same time relative.

Include its presupposition:

> For it has a presupposition from which it begins; it takes its start from
> the contingent.

Continue through the distinction between manifold conditions and the unity
which has not yet reflected itself into itself.

Include the conclusion:

> Real necessity thus contains contingency.

End the section with:

> In itself, therefore, we have here the unity of necessity and contingency;
> this unity is to be called absolute actuality.

### Why it fits the Guṇas' completed sequence

Real Necessity is necessary under a determinate set of actual conditions.
Its content is therefore limited, and that limitation remains contingent.

```text
given these complete conditions
this fact cannot be otherwise

but

the determinate content of these conditions
still begins from contingency
```

That is the exact limit of Guṇa-transformation. The Guṇas have generated the
complete real conditions and their necessary result. Their determinate
content cannot supply the unconditioned ground of the whole sequence.

The sequence has therefore accomplished its Artha and must close:

```text
manifold Guṇa-conditions
        ↓ form Real Possibility
necessary determinate actuality
        ↓ exposes remaining contingency
unity of Necessity and Contingency
        ↓
Absolute Actuality
```

### `Kṛtārtha`, not failure

Relative Necessity does not collapse because its derivation was false. It
completes the exact task it could perform:

- it grounds a determinate fact in its complete real conditions;
- it proves that nothing else can follow under those conditions;
- it reveals that the determinate content of the conditions remains
  contingent;
- it therefore produces the need for Absolute Necessity.

This is `kṛtārtha`: the Guṇas have accomplished their purpose.

Their transformation stops not at an unexplained dead end but at the unity
of Necessity and Contingency called Absolute Actuality.

### The third saintly consequence

The third Siddhi is completed Artha:

```text
the real conditions have produced everything they can determine
                       ↓
their own limitation becomes explicit
                       ↓
conditioned transformation has no further task
```

This prepares IV.33, where Absolute Necessity will no longer begin from a
contingent presupposition.

## Dharma-Megha and its Triple Siddhi

The complete distribution is:

```text
IV.29 — Dharma-Megha
Real Actuality preserves itself through manifold content
and manifests itself through what it produces
                       |
       ┌───────────────┼────────────────┐
       ↓               ↓                ↓
IV.30              IV.31             IV.32
purification       infinite Jñāna     completed Artha

conditions         Real Possibility   Relative Necessity
self-sublate;      is identical with  exposes its own
Kleśa-Karma        Necessity; no       contingency and
ceases             alien knowable      closes its sequence
```

The Triple Siddhi is not external ornamentation. It is the threefold
explication of Dharma-Megha as content-filled Real Actuality.

## The Fichtean five-fold synthesis

Hegel's Relative Necessity again exhibits the five-fold form:

```text
1. Real Actuality
   content-filled manifestation                         IV.29

2. Real Possibility
   complete manifold of actual conditions              IV.30

3. Actuality → Possibility
   actuality is dispersed as conditions of another     IV.30

4. Possibility → Actuality
   complete conditions sublate themselves into fact    IV.30–31

5. Real Necessity
   the full connection in which nothing else can follow IV.31

reflection of the fifth into its limit
   Relative Necessity contains Contingency              IV.32
```

The final reflection is not a sixth item externally added to the synthesis.
It is the completed fifth term comprehending the limitation of its own
content and thereby passing into Absolute Actuality.

## A–U–M across Relative Necessity

```text
A — Appearance
Real Actuality manifests a manifold, content-filled world

U — Reflection / thinking
Real Possibility gathers its complete circumstances and conditions

M — Knowing
Real Necessity comprehends their identity and then
recognizes the contingency remaining in its determinate content
```

M again contains both opposed directions:

```text
Actuality → conditions of Possibility
Possibility → necessary Actuality
```

Knowing is the complete reciprocal construction, not the reflective pole
alone.

## Comparison with Fichte

| Yoga | Hegel | Fichte |
|---|---|---|
| IV.29 | Real Actuality preserves itself through manifold content and acts | pure certainty supports every `what` and projects itself necessarily |
| IV.30 | the complete circle of real conditions sublates its external form | living Light is immediately self-projecting without a gap |
| IV.31 | Real Possibility is identical with Necessity; nothing else can follow | Living Knowing distinguishes its finite Concept or image through the necessary gap |
| IV.32 | Relative Necessity exposes the contingency of its limited content and yields Absolute Actuality | self-construction and the Being of Knowing are reconstructed from certainty, while the highest presupposition remains |

### The complementary emphases

Fichte follows the genesis of certainty and the self-projection of Light.
Hegel follows the modal status of the content produced:

```text
Fichte
Why must Knowing project and reconstruct itself?

Hegel
Under what conditions is the projected content
actual, really possible, and necessary?
```

At IV.32 both reach the same limit. Fichte still must justify why living
certainty is principle-providing. Hegel still must overcome the contingent
presupposition of determinate Real Necessity.

Both therefore require Absolute Necessity.

## Why the saintly results remain secondary

IV.30–32 are genuine and powerful. But their order shows that they are not
the governing Principle:

```text
Dharma-Megha as complete Real Actuality
                    ↓
purity, infinite Knowledge, and completed transformation
follow necessarily
```

The danger is to reverse the order and seek purification, omniscience, or
cessation as privately possessed accomplishments. That would violate
`akusīda`: non-investment even in the highest discriminative result.

The Science of Knowing comes first. Saintliness is what its actuality looks
like when lived.

## Recommended second-pass boundaries

For the individual Hegel reports:

```text
IV.29
B.1 in full: formal Necessity receives content and becomes Real Actuality

IV.30
B.2 in full: Real Possibility as conditions through their twofold self-sublation

IV.31
B.3 opening: Real Necessity and the identity of Real Possibility with Necessity

IV.32
B.3 remainder: the relativity, contingency, and completion of Real Necessity
```

This division preserves Hegel's sequence, gives IV.29 its governing status,
explains IV.30–32 as the Triple Siddhi, and reaches Absolute Actuality at the
exact point where IV.33 begins Absolute Necessity.
