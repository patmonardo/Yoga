# Using the Kośa Method in Organon Reports

## Purpose

This chapter defines a practical method for using the *Abhidharmakośa*
Organon in reports on Yoga, the Bhagavad Gītā, and Sāṃkhya in comparison with
Kant. It is a method of systematic reconstruction, not a claim that these
texts teach Vasubandhu's doctrines or that their terms are interchangeable.

The Kośa is exceptionally embeddable in the project's Hegelian Yoga Dharma
Science. It is not merely an external comparison or a list of Buddhist
analogies. Within this construction, Hegelian Logic articulates the necessary
forms of the system; Yoga develops those forms through its staged mastery of
the Natural and Spiritual Objects; and the Kośa supplies an unusually rich
Practical-Reason architecture for working out Dharma in determinate
operation, consequence, continuity, Path, and knowledge.

This integration does not make the historical systems identical. It makes
the Kośa's Dharmaskandha especially capable of being reconstructed as the
practical content of Yoga's Bhūmi-science: the Kośa helps develop
Sarvadharma at each stage, while the Hegelian-Yoga architecture determines
how that content is situated, developed, and comprehended. The test is not
whether the vocabularies can be aligned, but whether the integrated account
explains the source sequence and yields a coherent practical Dharma-jñāna.

## Paired discipline

Each report must hold two complementary readings together without merging
them:

1. **Textual discipline**: establish what the source says, how its terms
   function in context, and how the argument proceeds in its own order.
2. **Organon discipline**: reconstruct the source as a system of
   determinations, relations, operations, and transformations, then test
   that reconstruction against the source.

The paired reading has a third, practical check: show what the reconstructed
system lets an interpreter do. An Organon reading should yield a procedure
for distinguishing, applying, or testing determinations, not only a
description of them.

```text
source passage
    -> close determination
    -> system relation
    -> operation or inference
    -> result and test
    -> return to the source
```

The return to the source is essential. If the Organon reconstruction cannot
explain the passage's wording, its place in the sequence, or its consequences,
revise the reconstruction rather than forcing the passage to fit.

## Sarvadharma as the unit of analysis

The unit of analysis is **Sarvadharma for a Bhūmi**: the complete,
systematically comprehended field of Dharma at that stage. This does not mean
collecting a maximum number of separate dharmas, building a catalogue, or
claiming that one has personally experienced an undifferentiated Light.
Sarvadharma is the organized whole whose relations can be known, developed,
and tested as practical Dharma-jñāna.

The controlling Yoga architecture is:

```text
Bhūmis 1-5    mastery of the Natural Object
Bhūmis 6-10   mastery of the Spiritual Object
Bhūmi 11      the Discriminator
```

The ten object-Bhūmis are stages, not ten final inventories. Their top-level
organization depends on Sāṃkhya's fivefold Object. Each Bhūmi therefore asks
for a complete science of its Object at that stage; the fivefold structure is
traversed in the Natural and Spiritual determinations, then brought to the
Discriminator. The exact assignment of a particular sūtra to a Bhūmi must be
established from the Yoga and Sāṃkhya sequence, not guessed from a list of
attainments.

Each Bhūmi is a top-level stage of a mental power's mastery of its Object.
The Bhūmi names the stage; it does not by itself specify all the practical
knowledge or operations required for mastery. The Kośa method develops that
content as Sarvadharma for the stage.

The Kośa sequence

```text
Dhātu -> Indriya -> Loka -> Karma -> Anuśaya -> Mārga -> Jñāna
```

is a practical method for developing that stage-wide Dharma-jñāna. Its
members are interdependent dimensions of one science, not seven independent
topics, a checklist to fill, or the definition of Sarvadharma. Their
systematic use asks:

| Kośa dimension | Practical question |
|---|---|
| **Dhātu** | What principial domain makes this stage determinate? |
| **Indriya** | What operative power enables mastery or cognition of that domain? |
| **Loka** | What complete object-field is disclosed through that power? |
| **Karma** | What practical operations develop or transform the field? |
| **Anuśaya** | What conditions, persists, or limits the present mastery? |
| **Mārga** | How does practice reorganize those conditions toward the stage's end? |
| **Jñāna** | What stage-wide knowledge follows from comprehending the whole relation? |

The questions are not mandatory slots for each verse. Their purpose is to
derive how a Bhūmi's complete science works. A report may focus on one sūtra
as an operation within that science, but must not mistake the sūtra's local
result for Sarvadharma itself.

## Procedure for a passage or textual unit

### 1. Fix the source unit

Record the exact work, chapter or book, verse or sūtra, source file, and
commentarial witness. Preserve the source's order. When editions disagree,
record the variant that affects the analysis rather than silently choosing
one.

### 2. Establish the close reading

For Sanskrit passages, record the text, segmentation where needed, grammar,
key lexical alternatives, and a close translation. Distinguish the root
text's wording from a commentator's explanation. If a report uses a
translation already in the repository, verify that the translation actually
matches the source before treating it as evidence.

### 3. State the local problem

Write one question that the passage answers or complicates. Identify its
antecedent and consequence in the surrounding argument. Do not begin with
the desired Hegelian, Fichtean, Kantian, or Kośa correspondence.

### 4. Identify system roles

List only the terms and relations needed to answer the local problem. State
whether a term functions as a domain, capacity, field, operation, causal
condition, retained determination, path, or knowledge. Give each role a
source-based warrant.

### 5. Develop the Bhūmi's Sarvadharma

Use the Kośa sequence to determine the Bhūmi as an integrated practical
system. A verse-level result is evidence or an operation within that system;
it is not, by itself, the whole science of the stage. For every proposed
correspondence, record:

- the source passage or Kośa passage supporting it;
- the Bhūmi and Object relation it belongs to;
- how the relevant Kośa moments work together in the proposed account;
- the practical Dharma-jñāna the relation makes possible;
- the Organon function it is proposed to perform;
- the difference that remains between the systems.

If a dimension is not supported, leave its role unassigned. Do not convert
that absence into a missing individual dharma to be supplied by speculation.

### 6. Specify the practical operation

Describe the interpreter's operation in executable terms:

```text
input:
    the determinate passage, its context, and its source terms

operation:
    distinguish the roles, trace dependencies, and apply the stated rule

output:
    a conclusion, transformation, or knowledge claim

preconditions:
    what must already be established

failure conditions:
    what would make the inference invalid or underdetermined
```

“Practical” here means usable as a disciplined interpretive or inferential
procedure. It does not, by itself, prescribe physical, devotional, or
therapeutic practice.

### 7. Test the reconstruction

Use at least these tests:

1. **Text test**: does the reading preserve the source's terms and syntax?
2. **Sequence test**: does it explain why this passage occurs here?
3. **Relation test**: does it account for the actual relation among the
   terms, rather than matching isolated vocabulary?
4. **Consequence test**: does it predict or clarify what the next passage
   does?
5. **Difference test**: does it preserve the source system's commitments
   that resist the comparison?
6. **Counter-reading test**: what plausible alternative reading would
   undermine the proposed mapping?

Revise or downgrade a mapping that fails these tests.

## Evidence and claim types

Mark significant claims by status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Textual** | Directly supported by the source wording or structure. |
| **Commentarial** | Attributed to an identified historical or project commentary. |
| **Systematic** | A reconstruction of relations that explains multiple source passages. |
| **Comparative** | A proposed relation between distinct systems, with differences stated. |
| **Speculative** | A generative hypothesis not yet established by the preceding evidence. |

For machine-oriented use, each claim should carry a stable identifier,
source anchor, status, premises, inference or relation, conclusion, and
known counterexample or open question. Confidence must not substitute for
evidence or be used to make a speculative claim appear textual.

## Applying the method in each Report

### Organon Yoga

Yoga is the primary site of integration in this set. Work in canonical
sūtra order, establishing Yoga's account of Citta, practice, transformation,
and knowledge. Within that sequence, use the Kośa's practical architecture
to develop the Sarvadharma of each Bhūmi: how its field is constituted, what
capacity enables mastery, how action and retained conditioning function,
and what the Path changes. The Kośa is embedded in this account as a source
of systematic practical determination, not appended as an optional
cross-cultural comparison.

For Yoga Sūtras II and III, first establish how each sūtra operates within
the eleven-stage architecture and its relevant fivefold Object. Then use the
Kośa to develop the practical Dharma-jñāna of that Bhūmi. The relation is not
“one sūtra, one dharma”; the sūtra's operation contributes to a systematic
stage.

The existing master architecture proposes particular lines of inquiry:

```text
YS II:
    Kriyā, kleśa, karmāśaya, Saṃyoga, Viyoga, limbs, and discipline

YS III:
    Dhāraṇā, Dhyāna, Samādhi, Saṃyama, transformations, and knowledge
```

Treat these as hypotheses to establish passage by passage and Bhūmi by
Bhūmi. In particular:

- determine how the Kośa moments jointly articulate the Bhūmi's Sarvadharma;
- state whether a relation is textual, commentarial, or a project-level
  reconstruction;
- show how Saṃyama or another operation develops mastery of the Natural or
  Spiritual Object and contributes to the stage's complete knowledge;
- do not assign unresolved sūtras to a Bhūmi merely to complete a schema;
- keep Vyāsa, Hegel, Fichte, and the Organon synthesis distinguishable.

For example, YS III.25, III.26, and III.29 should not be expanded into
generic claims about paranormal perception, modern astronomy, or subtle-body
anatomy. Establish their textual operation and Bhūmi placement; then show how
the Kośa method develops the corresponding stage-wide knowledge of the
Object. Their reported results belong within Sarvadharma; they are not
Sarvadharma as isolated attainments. The practical product is a traceable
account of the system a practitioner must master, not an assertion that
“light” alone supplies its content.

### Organon Gītā

Begin with the Gītā's own sequence and the available verse-level material.
Identify the local work done by each passage before grouping passages into
systematic movements. Use the Kośa dimensions selectively to investigate
how the Gītā relates a field of action to capacities, conduct, consequence,
continuity, discipline, and knowledge.

For example, where a passage concerns action, ask what makes the action
intelligible, what conditions it, what it changes, and how the text
distinguishes action from its appropriation or result. Do not import Kośa
definitions of karma or liberation into the Gītā without textual support.
Record differences between the Gītā's own account and the Kośa comparison.

The practical product is a passage-grounded account of the Gītā's complete
system of action, knowledge, discipline, and its other central
determinations. The Kośa method helps derive their relations as a science;
it does not require enumerating isolated dharmas or projecting Yoga's
eleven-Bhūmi arrangement onto the Gītā. The Report should make clear which
operations belong to the source text and which belong to the Organon
reconstruction.

### Organon Sāṃkhya: Kantian commentary

The [Sāṃkhya report area](Samkhya/README.md) begins with a bounded
source and inference register. It is an entry point for this method, not
a completed reconstruction of the Kārikā sequence.

Use the Kārikā sequence to reconstruct Sāṃkhya's own proof and system before
introducing Kant. For each proposed correspondence, identify the problem
Sāṃkhya is solving, the role of the relevant determination, and the Kantian
concept that offers a comparison. State the point of non-equivalence
alongside the correspondence.

Use the Kośa method here as a **procedure for developing practical
Dharma-jñāna**, not as a classification checklist:

- identify the domain and its distinctions;
- identify the cognitive or practical powers that operate with those
  distinctions;
- trace what follows in appearance, action, conditioning, path, or
  knowledge;
- test whether a Kantian comparison clarifies that transition or merely
  renames it.

Do not equate Sāṃkhya's tattvas with Kant's categories, or treat Kant as
endorsing Sāṃkhya's claims about Prakṛti and Puruṣa. Distinguish an
architectural analogy, a functional comparison, and a substantive
equivalence; the last requires the strongest evidence and may be rejected.

The practical product is a controlled comparative procedure that can
identify where Kant sharpens, limits, or fails to translate a Sāṃkhya
determination, while preserving the fivefold Object as a systematic whole.

## Report record template

Use this compact record for major passages, claims, or inferential steps:

```yaml
id: stable-report-local-id
source:
  work: ""
  location: ""
  witness: ""
  repository_file: ""
claim:
  statement: ""
  status: textual | commentarial | systematic | comparative | speculative
  source_terms: []
system:
  roles: []
  relations: []
kosa_dimensions:
  stage: ""
  object: natural | spiritual | discriminator | not-yet-established
  systematic_relation: ""
  dhatu_indriya_loka_karma_anusaya_marga_jnana: ""
operation:
  inputs: []
  rule: ""
  outputs: []
  preconditions: []
  failure_conditions: []
comparison:
  correspondence: ""
  non_equivalence: ""
tests:
  passed: []
  unresolved: []
  counter_reading: ""
```

This is a reporting schema, not a claim that all sources naturally divide
into these fields. Preserve source-specific structures where this template
does not fit.

## Completion criteria

A practical-method section in any Report is ready when:

- the close reading and Organon reading remain distinguishable;
- each proposed Kośa connection is relational and source-anchored;
- the operation can be stated with inputs, rule, outputs, and failure
  conditions;
- unsupported or inapplicable dimensions are marked rather than
  backfilled;
- at least one counter-reading or limitation is recorded;
- another reader or system can trace each important conclusion back to its
  source.
