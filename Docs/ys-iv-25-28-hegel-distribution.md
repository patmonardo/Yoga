# Distributing Hegel's Contingency across Yoga Sūtra IV.25–28

## The problem

In `dharma-greater-varttika.tex`, the four Sūtras IV.25–28 are printed
together before Hegel's complete section:

> A. Contingency or Formal Actuality, Possibility, and Necessity

Hegel's text must remain in its original sequence. The task is therefore not
to rearrange paragraphs so that they resemble the Sūtras. It is to identify
the internal transitions at which each Sūtra becomes the best compressed
statement of the movement already occurring.

The section has three numbered moments but the Yoga sequence has four
Sūtras. The correct distribution cannot be one Hegelian number per Sūtra.
The second moment, formal Possibility, contains two distinct movements and
provides the required fourth division.

## Proposed distribution

```text
IV.25    Formal Actuality → reflected Possibility

IV.26    Possibility as deficiency, ought, and self-sublation

IV.27    Reflected Actuality → Contingency

IV.28    Restlessness of Contingency → formal Necessity
```

This follows Hegel's order without moving any text.

## IV.25 — The immediate self becomes merely possible

`viśeṣa-darśina ātmabhāva-bhāvanā-vinivṛttiḥ`

> For one who sees the distinction, the construction “I am this” ceases.

### Hegelian span

Begin with section A.1:

> Actuality is formal inasmuch as, as a first actuality, it is only
> immediate, unreflected actuality.

Continue into the opening of A.2:

> This possibility is actuality reflected into itself.

The controlling proposition is:

> What is actual is possible.

### Why it fits

`Ātma-bhāva` begins as immediate formal Actuality:

```text
“I am this.”
```

Reflection discovers that this actual formation contains possibility:

```text
“This is one possible determination.”
```

The formation remains actual, but it loses the right to present itself as
the absolutely necessary Self. That loss is `vinivṛtti`.

### Proper stopping point

IV.25 should stop before the full development of opposite possibilities and
Contingency. Its precise result is only:

```text
immediate self-Actuality
          ↓ Reflection
possible self-determination
```

Our first IV.25 report went farther, using the completed doctrine of
Contingency. On the second pass it should be narrowed to this boundary.

## IV.26 — Possibility acquires a gradient toward Actuality

`tadā viveka-nimnaṃ kaivalya-prāgbhāraṃ cittam`

> Then Citta slopes toward Viveka and bears forward into Kaivalya.

### Hegelian span

Continue within A.2 from Possibility's two moments:

> Possibility is something deficient, that it points to an other, to
> actuality, and is completed in this other.

Then include:

> the ought-to-be of the totality of form.

Continue through the development in which formal Possibility contains the
opposite possibility and becomes contradiction, ending with:

> it consequently becomes actuality.

### Why it fits

Possibility is not a passive container of alternatives. It is deficient with
respect to the totality and points beyond itself toward Actuality.

This is exactly a modal slope:

```text
mere Possibility
       ↓ experienced as deficient
ought-to-be of complete form
       ↓
self-sublation toward Actuality
```

`Viveka-nimna` is the inward gradient. `Kaivalya-prāgbhāra` is the weight
toward completion.

The connection is especially strong because Hegel's Possibility already
contains an `ought`; Yoga expresses the same modal determination as Citta's
orientation.

### The role of contradiction

Formal Possibility allows both A and not-A. When developed, it discovers its
own contradiction and cannot remain mere inward possibility.

```text
A is possible
not-A is possible
       ↓
formal Possibility cannot determine the actual
       ↓
it sublates itself toward Actuality
```

Viveka is therefore not merely choosing the preferable possibility. It
comprehends why the form of mere possibility is inadequate.

## IV.27 — Other Pratyayas are the contingency of reflected Actuality

`tac-chidreṣu pratyayāntarāṇi saṃskārebhyaḥ`

> In the openings of that movement, other cognitions arise from Saṃskāras.

### Hegelian span

Begin with A.3:

> This actuality is not the first actuality but reflected actuality,
> posited as unity of itself and possibility.

Continue through the definition:

> This unity of possibility and actuality is contingency.

And especially:

> The contingent is an actual which is at the same time determined as only
> possible, an actual whose other or opposite equally is.

### Why it fits

Prajñā has become Citta's actual inclination, but an alternative Pratyaya can
still arise. Hegel supplies its modal definition:

```text
present Viveka-Pratyaya
is actual

another Pratyaya from Saṃskāra
is equally possible
```

The `chidra` is the opening in which the actual displays its merely possible
status and its opposite acquires actuality.

This is not yet the complete restlessness of Contingency. IV.27 identifies
the fact that another determination can arise because the present actuality
has not established absolute necessity.

### Exact correspondence

```text
Yoga                            Hegel

present Citta-gradient          reflected Actuality

opening                         Actuality contains Possibility

other Pratyaya                  the opposite is equally possible

Saṃskāra                        the retained ground of its emergence

result                          Contingency
```

## IV.28 — Contingency relinquishes itself into Necessity

`hānam eṣāṃ kleśavad uktam`

> Their relinquishment is as already explained for the Kleśas.

### Hegelian span

Continue from Hegel's two-sided determination of the contingent:

> The contingent thus has no ground because it is contingent; and for that
> same reason it has a ground, because it is contingent.

Then include the conversion of possibility and actuality into one another:

> This absolute restlessness of the becoming of these two determinations is
> contingency.

End with the transition:

> this identity of the two, of each in the other, is necessity.

The remainder of the section states the resulting formal Necessity and
prepares Relative Necessity at IV.29.

### Why it fits

The alternative Pratyayas are not relinquished by deleting possible content.
Their contingent independence is relinquished by comprehending the complete
conversion of Actuality and Possibility.

```text
the Pratyaya appears groundless
“it simply arose”

the Pratyaya has a ground
“it arose from Saṃskāra”

both determinations convert into one another
                   ↓
their isolated authority is relinquished
                   ↓
the identity governing both is Necessity
```

This is `kleśavat`. A Kleśa is removed by comprehending its genesis and
contradiction, not by attacking the existence of Citta.

### Why IV.28 must reach Necessity

IV.29 begins Relative Necessity. Therefore IV.28 must finish by deriving the
formal Necessity from which the next section starts.

The textual architecture itself requires this boundary:

```text
IV.25–28  A. Contingency → formal Necessity

IV.29–32  B. Relative Necessity

IV.33–34  C. Absolute Necessity
```

## The four Sūtras as one five-fold modal movement

The distribution can also be expressed through the Fichtean five-fold
synthesis:

```text
1. Actuality
   immediate “I am this”                         IV.25

2. Possibility
   reflection of the actual into itself         IV.25–26

3. Actuality → Possibility
   the actual loses absolute immediacy           IV.25

4. Possibility → Actuality
   contradiction self-sublates into the actual  IV.26

5. Contingency / formal Necessity
   both directions form one restless unity      IV.27–28
```

The four Sūtras do not correspond mechanically to five terms. They articulate
the two terms, their reciprocal transitions, and their resulting modal unity
according to Yoga's own semantic sequence.

## A–U–M across Contingency

```text
A — Appearance
formal Actuality: the determination immediately is

U — Reflection / thinking
Possibility: the actual is reflected inwardly and opposed

M — Knowing
Contingency and its return into Necessity are comprehended
as the complete reciprocal movement
```

Again, M contains the five-fold articulation. It is not a third inert state.

## Comparison with Fichte

The same fourfold distribution clarifies the Fichte reports:

| Yoga | Hegel | Fichte |
|---|---|---|
| IV.25 | immediate Actuality becomes merely possible | completed genesis appears as immediate self-fact |
| IV.26 | deficient Possibility points toward completed Actuality | the ungrounded “we are knowing” gives the Science its remaining direction |
| IV.27 | reflected Actuality contains its possible opposite | the objective insight retains a factical opening in its form |
| IV.28 | restless Contingency returns into formal Necessity | retained method relinquishes returning factical assumptions |

Fichte explains the genetic and methodological form of each transition.
Hegel supplies its exact modal determination.

## Recommended second-pass boundaries

For the revised individual reports:

```text
IV.25
A.1 through the opening definition of Possibility in A.2

IV.26
Possibility as deficiency and ought through its self-sublation into Actuality

IV.27
A.3 through the initial definition of Contingency and its possible opposite

IV.28
the two sides and absolute restlessness of Contingency through formal Necessity
```

This distribution preserves Hegel's order, follows the semantic order of the
four Yoga Sūtras, and lands exactly at Relative Necessity in IV.29.
