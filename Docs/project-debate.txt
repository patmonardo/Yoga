1.  Absolute Oneness Beyond Relation: This seems closely aligned with apophatic or negative theology approaches; an ineffable ground of being that, paradoxically, is only graspable when all attempts at conceptualization are left behind. Any relation, even subject-object, introduces duality and therefore a departure from this pure absolute.

2. Self within Dyad as Appearance:  This suggests our usual sense of individual selfhood - and all knowing that seems to arise from it - may be akin to an illusory projection within a  greater non-dual state. This evokes Yogic, and possibly Mahayana Buddhist approaches on the ultimate nature of selfhood.

3. "Meaning's Trap": Here lies a profound critique of  reasoning itself! When we try to fix an experience in thought (through its relationship with something else - whether an object 'out there' or concepts  created to grapple with it), we remain confined in a system of references within the Self-Notself.  To even be 'meaningful', duality is required, while your 'Absolute' seems to lie in a timeless state beyond this meaning-generating matrix.
