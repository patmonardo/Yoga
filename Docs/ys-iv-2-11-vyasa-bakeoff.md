# The Vyāsa Bakeoff

## Can the Bhāṣya be converted into Citta-Dharma?

This test compares Vyāsa's reading of Yoga Sūtra IV.2-11 with the
Fichte-Bhāṣya and Hegel-Vārttika developed in the Dharmapada reports.

The question is not whether Vyāsa can be made to say what we already think.
It is whether his commentary supplies a stronger account of the Sūtra's
plain sequence, and whether what is strongest in it can enter the Yoga
System without importing an external Puruṣa or a mythology of manufactured
bodies and minds.

The verdicts mean:

- **Preserve:** Vyāsa gives the technical determination we need.
- **Convert:** preserve the causal or logical structure, but translate its
  ontological setting into Citta-Dharma.
- **Reject:** the explanation blocks rather than clarifies the sequence.

The Sanskrit Bhāṣya used here is the Āgāśe edition presented by Wisdomlib.

---

## IV.2 — Nature fills out another form

`jaty-antara-parinamah prakrty-apurat`

> Transformation into another form of life occurs through Nature's filling.

### Vyāsa

The previous formation disappears and a subsequent formation arises through
the entry of new constituents. The natures of body and senses fulfil their
proper modifications by an *āpūra*, a filling or influx, while dharma and
other factors serve as occasions.

### Bakeoff

Vyāsa gets the central distinction right: transformation is not the arbitrary
replacement of one finished object by another. Nature supplies the material
power of the new determination. His bodily and species-oriented application
is narrower than the Sūtra's value for a Doctrine of Essence, but it does not
damage its logic.

**Verdict: Preserve and generalize.** Nature's power fills out a determinate
form. This gives the material side of genesis; Fichte and Hegel explain why
the form is intelligible as one transformation.

---

## IV.3 — The occasion removes the barrier

`nimittam aprayojakam prakrtinam varana-bhedas tu tatah ksetrikavat`

> The occasion does not drive Nature; it only breaks the obstruction, as a
> farmer opens a channel.

### Vyāsa

Dharma does not push the powers of Nature into action. An effect cannot make
its own cause operate. The farmer does not carry the water into the field;
he removes the barrier and the water flows by its own power. Dharma likewise
removes adharma, its contrary, rather than supplying Nature's activity.

### Bakeoff

This is nearly perfect. It distinguishes the internal power of formation
from the external condition that releases it. It also prevents both magical
efficient causation and an external intelligence from micromanaging Nature.

**Verdict: Preserve.** This is one of Vyāsa's decisive contributions to the
Yoga System.

---

## IV.4 — Cittas arise from Asmitā

`nirmana-cittany asmita-matrat`

> Constructed Cittas arise from I-am-ness alone.

### Vyāsa

Vyāsa asks whether the many bodies produced by a yogin possess one Citta or
many. His answer is that the yogin constructs Cittas from Asmitā alone; the
constructed bodies thereby become ensouled or minded.

### Bakeoff

The setting is the weakest point in the whole section. It turns a profound
claim about the construction of determinate cognition into a siddhi story
about a yogin fabricating bodies. Yet the formal discovery is indispensable:
Asmitā is not one more empirical content. It is the repeatable form `I am`
from which a determinate Citta can be constructed.

**Verdict: Convert.** Reject manufactured creatures as the governing sense;
retain Asmitā as the generative form of constructed Cittas.

---

## IV.5 — One Citta articulates the many

`pravrtti-bhede prayojakam cittam ekam anekesam`

> In the differentiation of their activity, one Citta is the unifying
> principle of the many.

### Vyāsa

Vyāsa sees the exact problem: how can the activities of many Cittas proceed
according to one intention? He answers that the yogin produces one directing
Citta which sets all the constructed Cittas to their differentiated work.

### Bakeoff

Vyāsa deserves credit for recognizing that plurality without unity cannot
amount to coordinated cognition. But his directing Citta remains one member
or executive placed over the others. That merely relocates the problem: what
unifies the manager with the managed?

Eka Citta must instead be the synthetic unity expressed by the principle:

> The `I think` must be able to accompany the many Cittas.

It is not another Citta among them, a master mind, or an external Puruṣa. It
is their unity as cognitions. In this respect Eka Citta is isomorphic to the
pāramārthika standpoint: not a second world behind the series, but the truth
of its unity.

**Verdict: Convert radically.** Preserve the unity problem; reject the
executive-Citta solution.

---

## IV.6 — Dhyāna produces an unconditioned construction

`tatra dhyana-jam anasayam`

> Among these, the Citta born of meditation is without karmic deposit.

### Vyāsa

Vyāsa distinguishes constructed Cittas arising through birth, herbs, mantra,
ascetic heat, and samādhi. Only the meditation-born Citta is without āśaya:
because the yogin's kleśas have been exhausted, its activity is no longer
driven by attachment and therefore deposits neither merit nor demerit.

### Bakeoff

Once separated from the siddhi catalogue, this is an exact specification.
The issue is not the annihilation of Citta but whether cognition reproduces
the kleśa-karma circuit. A Citta may operate without depositing another
condition for compulsory operation.

**Verdict: Preserve.** Translate `constructed` genetically, not magically.

---

## IV.7 — Action ceases to color its agent

`karmasuklakrsnam yoginas tri-vidham itaresam`

> The yogin's action is neither white nor black; that of others is threefold.

### Vyāsa

Vyāsa distinguishes black, white-black, white, and neither-white-nor-black
action. The last belongs to one whose kleśas are exhausted: it is not white
because its fruit is renounced, and not black because harmful appropriation
has ceased.

### Bakeoff

This is much stronger than a merely moral colour chart. Vyāsa explains how
action can remain fully actual yet cease manufacturing an identity through
its expected result. That is precisely the transition from conditioned
practical reason to action no longer accumulated as a possession of the
agent.

**Verdict: Preserve.** This is directly usable in the Kośa cross-product.

---

## IV.8 — Only congruent Vāsanās manifest

`tatas tad-vipakanugunanam evabhivyaktir vasananam`

> From those actions, only Vāsanās congruent with their maturation become
> manifest.

### Vyāsa

Not every latent disposition appears on every occasion. A karmic maturation
selectively manifests only the Vāsanās congruent with the kind of existence
and experience then becoming actual.

### Bakeoff

This is a genuine knowledge-processing rule. The latent field is not a sack
of memories discharged at random. A present configuration selects the
dispositions capable of specifying it. Vyāsa's rebirth examples can be
bracketed without losing the rule.

**Verdict: Preserve.** Vāsanā runs through the constructed Cittas by selective
manifestation; in that precise sense it also helps build them.

---

## IV.9 — Smṛti and Saṃskāra preserve one form

`jati-desa-kala-vyavahitanam apy anantaryam smrti-samskarayor eka-rupatvat`

> Even across differences of birth, place, and time, continuity remains,
> because memory and formation have one form.

### Vyāsa

An experience produces a conforming Saṃskāra; that Saṃskāra can produce a
memory of the same form; memory again reinforces formation. Thus a Vāsanā
may become manifest when its appropriate cause appears despite large
intervals of birth, place, or time.

### Bakeoff

Vyāsa supplies the missing transmission mechanism. Continuity does not
require a little object or substantial memory travelling unchanged through
the interval. What persists is reproducibility of form across experience,
formation, and recollection.

**Verdict: Preserve the mechanism; convert the cosmology.** This joins Yoga
to the Kośa analysis of serial Cittas far better than a permanent psychic
container would.

---

## IV.10 — The Vāsanā-series has no first empirical member

`tasam anaditvam casiso nityatvat`

> These Vāsanās are beginningless because the wish for continuance is
> perennial.

### Vyāsa

Vyāsa grounds beginninglessness in the persistent wish, `May I not cease;
may I continue.` The fear of death is not adequately explained as a lesson
first learned in the present life, so he treats the tendency as without an
empirical beginning.

### Bakeoff

The enduring insight is not an infinitely old mental substance. Every
conditioned Citta already presupposes the drive to maintain and repeat its
determination. Consequently no first Vāsanā can be found within the series
that Vāsanā itself makes possible.

**Verdict: Convert.** Preserve beginninglessness as structural
presupposition; do not turn it into an independently eternal entity.

---

## IV.11 — Remove the collection, not Citta

`hetu-phalasrayalambanaih sangrhitatvad esam abhave tad-abhavah`

> Because Vāsanās are held together by cause, result, support, and object,
> when these are absent, those Vāsanās are absent.

### Vyāsa

Vyāsa gives a six-spoked circuit:

```text
dharma / adharma
       ↓
pleasure / pain
       ↓
attachment / aversion
       ↓
effort through mind, speech, and body
       ↓
help or injury to others
       ↓
renewed dharma / adharma
```

Avidyā leads this revolving wheel and is the root of the kleśas. He then
defines the four collectors of Vāsanā:

- **Hetu:** the causal wheel just described.
- **Phala:** the result in which dharma and the other factors become present.
- **Āśraya:** the operative mind that supports the Vāsanās.
- **Ālambana:** the object whose presence manifests a corresponding Vāsanā.

When these are absent, the Vāsanās dependent upon them are absent.

### Bakeoff

Vyāsa passes the decisive test. He does **not** say that Eka Citta is killed.
He says that Vāsanā is collected and sustained by a determinate causal
complex; remove that complex and no hidden Vāsanā-substance remains behind.

Fichte explains why the derivative subject-object processor can fall away
without eliminating knowing. Hegel explains why the conditions are the whole
determinate existence of the Vāsanā rather than accessories attached to it.
Vyāsa supplies the concrete causal wheel that both accounts require.

**Verdict: Preserve and complete.** IV.11 removes `I am thus`; it does not
remove the `I think` by which the presence and absence of the construction
are known.

## Final scorecard

| Sūtra | Vyāsa's strongest contribution | Yoga-System verdict |
|---|---|---|
| IV.2 | transformation by Nature's filling | Preserve and generalize |
| IV.3 | occasion removes obstruction; Nature acts | Preserve |
| IV.4 | Asmitā is the generative source of Cittas | Convert the siddhi setting |
| IV.5 | the many require a principle of unity | Convert the executive Citta |
| IV.6 | meditation-born Citta deposits no āśaya | Preserve |
| IV.7 | action without karmic coloration | Preserve |
| IV.8 | selective manifestation by congruence | Preserve |
| IV.9 | one form across memory and formation | Preserve; convert cosmology |
| IV.10 | no first member within conditioned striving | Convert structurally |
| IV.11 | Vāsanā exists only as a fourfold collection | Preserve and complete |

## Judgment

Vyāsa can be converted—but not by wholesale adoption and not by defeat.

His great strength is **causal psychology**. From IV.6 through IV.11 he gives
an extraordinarily economical account of how kleśa, action, maturation,
selective Vāsanā, memory, formation, support, and object constitute a Citta
stream. This material should become the technical Bhāṣya of the Yoga System.

His failure is concentrated in **the ontology of unity** at IV.4-5. He sees
that many Cittas require one Citta, but imagines the one as a directing mind
constructed to coordinate other constructed minds. The Fichte-Hegel account
is stronger because Eka Citta is the condition under which the many are
cognitions at all. It is not an external Puruṣa and not a manager inside the
series.

The resulting division of labour is exact:

```text
Vyāsa          gives the conditioned causal machinery of Citta.
Fichte         gives the transcendental unity of its knowing.
Hegel          gives the self-determination and disappearance of its grounds.
Yoga × Kośa    gives the serial specification of the constructed Cittas.
```

So the bakeoff does not expel Vyāsa. It converts him from metaphysical master
into technical analyst. Once IV.4-5 are reconstructed, IV.2-11 becomes a
remarkably powerful specification of knowledge processing:

> Nature generates forms; occasions release them; Asmitā makes them Cittas;
> Eka Citta unifies their plurality; Vāsanā selectively specifies their
> succession; and liberation removes the conditions of that specification
> without abolishing knowing.

That is Citta-Dharma, and it completes Reflection at the threshold of
Appearance.
