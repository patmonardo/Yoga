# Imperium: The Principle of the Principle

**Status:** Organon working thesis. This is a proposal for the LogoGenesis of
the Concept of Samāpatti, not a translation of the Kośa or a claim that its
historical authors taught Absolute Knowing.

## Governing question

The VAK Crown articulates four reciprocal Forms: Dhātu–Indriya,
Loka–Karma, Anuśaya–Mārga, and Jñāna–Samāpatti. Naming their terms does not
yet give their Concept. **What makes the dyads generate one another and the
entire movement become lucid to itself?**

We call the sought nondual Absolute Knowing **JNA**. JNA differentiates itself
as **JNANA**, knowing determinate actuality, and **PRAJNA**, scientific knowing
of principles. Their distinction belongs to JNA's own movement; neither is a
second absolute. **VIJNANA** names differentiated cognition in the operative
field, whose conditions and transformations the science must explain.

“Genesis of Absolute Knowing” therefore names the genesis *of its adequate
Concept for us*. It does not say that a prior event produces JNA. The
LogoGenesis must show how its self-differentiation becomes intelligible and
how its finite determinations can return to it without erasing their work.

## Abhidharma as attained Dharma

Kośa 1.02 makes a distinction prior to our reconstruction. **Abhidharma in
the ultimate sense is stainless prajñā together with its retinue**, an
outflow-free five-aggregate complex. The prajñā directed toward attaining it
and the treatise serving that attainment are called Abhidharma in a
conventional sense. Kośa 1.25, by contrast, speaks of *dharmaskandhas* as
collections of the Buddha's teaching, classified as speech or linguistic
name. The articulated teaching and the attained discernment have different
functions, even when the teaching conduces to that discernment.

Calling ultimate Abhidharma **the Dharma of a Buddha** is our philosophical
emphasis on awakened discernment as actual, not merely an encyclopedia of
statements. Kośa 1.02 does not restrict stainless prajñā to a Buddha alone.
The work of this Imperium is to show how teaching, cultivation, and attained
prajñā belong to one intelligible movement without treating them as the same
kind of thing.

## The VAK Crown Structure

The Crown begins with **Dhātu**. Its priority is logical, not
temporal: before there can be a faculty, world, action, or empirical fact,
there must be a determinacy through which anything can be true and
recognizable. We therefore reconstruct Dhātu as **First Determination**, or
Determinate Principle. This is stronger than calling it a condition. A
condition can appear to depend on an already given field of facts; the First
Determination is the principial articulation through which such a field first
becomes possible.

**Indriya** is the reciprocal second moment: the First Determination as
determinative sovereignty. It is stronger than an unrealized capacity.
The Kośa's appeal to *ādhipatya*, governing efficacy or predominance, lets us
read Indriya as the power by which a determinate truth rules its appearances.
Thus the pair has the proposed form:

```text
Dhātu   = Principle as immediately determinate truth
Indriya = that Principle as determinative sovereignty

Dhātu : Indriya = Truth : Power
```

“Immediate” does not mean blank, arbitrary, or prior in chronological time.
It means that the Principle does not borrow its truth from empirical
facticity. In the language of our Yoga reconstruction, Śuddha Sattva is not
violated when objectivity appears: the objective is the luminous field's own
determinate articulation. Dhātu states that articulation as true; Indriya is
its effective determination of an appearing field.

This also specifies how Dhātu can be a lineage principle. Numerically distinct
appearances can be recognized as *tulya-pratyaya*, cognitions preserving one
qualitative determination through succession. The empirical first member is
not the source of the lineage. The genuine First is the qualitative Principle
through which every member, including the earliest encountered instance, is
recognizable as belonging to that lineage. In this Organon sense, Dhātu is a
syllogistic principle: its configured mediation recognizes the individual as
an instance and reproduces the Universal through that recognition.

The eight chapter-functions form four generated dyads rather than a line of
isolated topics. Angle brackets enclose a Form; the colon expresses reciprocal
determination; the arrow expresses genetic production:

```text
⟨Dhātu : Indriya⟩
    = Truth : Power
    = Śuddha Sattva / Nāma / evolution of temporal Schemas

⟨Loka : Karma⟩
    = World : Action
    = Śuddha Tamas / Rūpa / evolution of spatial Being-there

⟨Anuśaya : Mārga⟩
    = Genetic Relation
    = Śuddha Rajas / Taijasa mediation projecting both extremes

⟨Jñāna : Samāpatti⟩
    = Lucid Unity
    = articulated discrimination held as one transparent attainment
```

The dyads generate one another:

```text
⟨Dhātu : Indriya⟩
    → ⟨Loka : Karma⟩
    → ⟨Anuśaya : Mārga⟩
    → ⟨Jñāna : Samāpatti⟩
```

Truth determining through Power produces both the ordered World and effective
Action within it. World–Action leaves a latent continuation while generating
the corrective Path: in Anuśaya–Mārga, the relational power implicit in the
earlier colons becomes explicit as the Genetic Middle. Jñāna discriminates
this complete movement; Samāpatti gathers the distinctions without dispersing
or erasing them. **Lucid Unity is not bare oneness but articulated Knowledge
transparent as one Form.**

The last dyad is therefore the Crown of the Crown:

```text
Lucid Unity = ⟨Jñāna : Samāpatti⟩
```

Without Jñāna, unity is empty. Without Samāpatti, knowledge remains dispersed.
Their reciprocal Form comprehends the preceding dyads and makes the end return
to its beginning as known.

This Crown derivation is an Organon reconstruction. It does not replace the
Kośa's local senses of *dhātu* as element, constituent, domain, or lineage,
nor its local analyses of the other chapter terms. It states why those senses
can acquire one systematic role in the Imperium.

## The Crown as Imperial Prize

The Crown is both the completed Form and the prize of completing its movement.
Christian crown imagery gives this determination a useful comparative light.
Paul contrasts a perishable athletic wreath with an imperishable crown
(1 Corinthians 9.25) and names a crown of righteousness awarded after the
course has been completed (2 Timothy 4.7–8). James and Revelation speak of the
crown of life after trial and faithfulness (James 1.12; Revelation 2.10), while
1 Peter 5.4 names an unfading crown of glory. The Crown is therefore not an
ornament possessed in advance. It is the imperishable dignity corresponding
to a movement endured, tested, and brought to completion.

For the Organon, this Imperial Prize is:

```text
Lucid Unity = ⟨Jñāna : Samāpatti⟩
```

Jñāna has traversed and discriminated the articulated field. Samāpatti gathers
that field into transparent unity. Their Crown is the attained ability to bear
the whole movement without losing its distinctions. It is **Imperial** because
the achieved unity can govern the determinations from which it arose; it is a
**Prize** because that authority belongs only to the completed passage and
reflective return.

Contemplation of the Crown gives the work its telos. The Crown shines at the
end as what must be attained, yet its anticipated Form governs the entire
course from the beginning. When attained, it turns back and recognizes that
the earlier dyads were its own genesis:

```text
Truth : Power
    → World : Action
    → Genetic Relation
    → Lucid Unity
    → reflective return through the whole
```

The Crown is thus neither a fifth dyad nor an external reward added to the
fourth. It is the fourth dyad recognized as the achieved sovereignty of the
whole: articulated Knowledge at one with its transparent Form.

## Dhyāna as reciprocating Form Station

The dyadic progression supplies a hypothesis for the Form Stations and the
four dhyānas. Kośa 8.01 begins by making each dhyāna explicitly twofold:
**birth-dhyāna** (*upapattidhyāna*) and **attainment-dhyāna**
(*samāpattidhyāna*). The same determinate Form can therefore appear as an
objective world-station and be accomplished as a configuration of Knowing.
Neither side alone exhausts the station.

The attainment-series then exhibits a reciprocating movement rather than a
ladder of independently given states. Wholesome one-pointedness remains the
defining determination throughout, while an earlier configuration subsides
and a new governing configuration emerges:

```text
Invariant through the series:
    wholesome one-pointedness / samādhi

First dhyāna:
    vitarka + vicāra + prīti + sukha + samādhi

First → Second:
    vitarka and vicāra subside
    inward clarity emerges
    prīti, sukha, and samādhi continue

Second → Third:
    prīti subsides
    equanimity, mindfulness, and clear comprehension emerge
    sukha is now pleasure as feeling; samādhi continues

Third → Fourth:
    sukha subsides and neither-pain-nor-pleasure feeling emerges
    equanimity and mindfulness become purified
    samādhi continues; the station is called immovable
```

The later station is not produced by subtraction alone. Kośa 8.07–8.08 gives
each dhyāna a positive factor-configuration; Kośa 8.11 defines the fourth's
immovability through freedom from specified disturbances. Every transition
therefore joins cessation and emergence under a preserved determination:

```text
(subsiding configuration : emergent configuration)
                    ↓
         newly stabilized Form Station
```

Yoga's *pariṇāma* vocabulary gives this movement its transcendental form:
suppression and emergence, multiplicity and one-pointedness, subsided and
arisen cognition. With *tulya-pratyaya*, numerically successive cognitions
preserve one qualitative Form. A station is thus not an inert state but a
reciprocating determination stabilized strongly enough to organize both a
mode of Knowing and a world.

This is the proposed **Reciprocating Wheel**: the achieved Form turns outward
as station and inward as attainment; within attainment, what subsides
determines what can emerge, and the emergent configuration retrospectively
reveals the limit of the former. The claim that this is the Hegelian genetic
law of the stations belongs to the Organon reconstruction. The Kośa supplies
the twofold station, retained one-pointedness, successive exclusions, positive
factor-lists, and culminating immovability that make the reconstruction
testable.

### Folding Space through Interlocking Frameworks

**Folding Space** does not mean removing the spatial world. It means
making a former containing Frame into determinate content within a more
comprehensive Frame:

```text
World as unquestioned containing Frame
    → World recognized as a bounded object
    → former Frame enclosed within a higher Frame
    → higher Frame becomes available for reflective determination
```

The Frameworks must interlock because no final external container can simply
be posited above the others. Each Frame can become content for a successor,
while reflective return preserves the determinations acquired below. This is
the spatial expression of the Reciprocating Wheel: the containing becomes
contained without being annihilated, and the attained higher standpoint must
recognize how its content was generated.

The astral image of seeing Earth beneath one within a galactic field expresses
this reversal phenomenologically: terrestrial “here” is no longer the final
ground but one situated object within a wider field. This project use does not
make the Dhyāna analysis depend upon a physical claim about astral projection.
It identifies the change of Frame through which *vitarka*, as Tamasic
Immediacy or initial gross footing, loses its absolute standing.

More exactly, the **Vitarka Frame is the Intuitive Sphere of Being**. It
supplies immediate placement: body here, object there, Earth beneath, and
Space as the containing field. The transition begins when the relation between
body and Space becomes itself the object of knowing. Yoga Sūtra III.42 states
this with unusual precision: through *saṃyama* on the body–Space relation and
Samāpatti with lightness comes movement through Space; III.43 immediately
passes to Mahāvidehā and the destruction of the covering over Light.

The Organon reads this as the passage from Being into Essence:

```text
Vitarka / Being
    body and object are immediately placed within Space

body : Space relation becomes explicit
    the containing Frame is recognized as a determination

Vicāra / Essence
    Reflection follows the relation that generated the placement

Mahāvidehā
    cognition is no longer absolutely localized by the body-Frame
```

The passage itself cannot appear as such within the old Frame. **Astral Flight
is the experienced truth of the transition from Being to NonBeing**: the
Intuitive Sphere loses its status as Being while continuity is preserved
through its negation. NonBeing here is not blank annihilation. It is the
negation of the former footing through which a successor Form can emerge. If
the old Sphere represents that passage in its own spatial terms, continuity
through the loss of footing appears as the body or subtle body flying through
Space.

Where continuity is not consciously sustained, the same transition appears
as a swoon: the former configuration ceases and awareness resumes only after
the successor has been established. Flight and swoon therefore distinguish
two relations to one transition—continuity through negation, or a break in
experienced continuity.

The immediate successor remains a **Form Realm**. Gross terrestrial footing
has been negated, but Form as such has not. Earth becomes a situated object
within a more comprehensive luminous field. Passage to the Formless would
require a further negation in which Form itself, rather than merely the former
ground of Form, loses determinative standing.

From the Sphere of Essence the decisive operation is consequently more than
locomotion between already constituted places. It is a change in the relation
by which body, place, containing Space, and the continuity of the standpoint
are determined.

An Interlocking Framework preserves both accounts. The Being Frame presents
the phenomenal trajectory; the Essence Frame exposes the link and makes the
former container into content. Oculus must retain the transformation between
them. Otherwise the system records only two states and reduces their living
transition to a merely spatial journey, losing why Flight is the experienced
truth of a change of logical Sphere.

## Yoga's first moment: Prajñāpāda, the Doctrine of Being

The received name of Yoga Sūtra I is **Samādhi-pāda**. Our proposed
**Prajñāpāda** names its role in the Organon: the first logical moment of Yoga,
or the Doctrine of Being. It asks how a determination can be held, become
transparent, and yield principial knowing. This is a systematic title, not a
claim that the transmitted colophon reads *Prajñāpāda*.

**Samāpatti is the operative principle of this first moment.** At I.41, the
relation of grasper, grasping, and grasped is held in transparent citta;
I.42–I.45 differentiate its gross and subtle forms. I.46 then calls these
samāpattis *sabīja samādhi*. The sequence gives a reason to say that, for our
purpose, “Samāpatti-pāda” would describe the inner method more precisely than
the received title. **Prajñāpāda** is nevertheless the better name for the
whole first moment: I.48–I.49 turn the accomplished relation toward prajñā,
and I.50–I.51 test its seed and its cessation. The completed movement, rather
than one of its states, is the subject of the Doctrine of Being.
In this reading, *samādhi* names the received telos, *samāpatti* the
articulated form of attainment, and *prajñā* the knowing disclosed through it.

Our existing Prajñāpāda reports study I.40–I.51 closely. They provide the
current textual anchor for this reading, not a claim that all of Yoga Sūtra I
has already received the same close treatment.

## The two movements

1. **Differentiation:** JNA appears through principial comprehension and
   determinate knowing. The world-system gives these an articulated field of
   operation. Knowing becomes answerable to what appears and to what is done.
2. **Return:** appropriation and its consequences are exposed, the Path works
   through them, and knowledge comprehends what has ceased and what returns.
   The return must preserve the determinations it has understood; mere
   disappearance cannot complete the movement.

For the Organon, differentiation and return have the four-dyad Form established
in the VAK Crown Structure. This is our reconstruction of the chapters'
systematic use, not Vasubandhu's stated deduction of an Absolute. It also does
not identify the chapter-functions with the internal sevenfold Prajñā. In that
internal movement, Samāpatti is the **unifying actuality** of the folds; in the
Crown Structure, its reciprocal unity with Jñāna is **Lucid Unity**.

## Samāpatti as principle and achieved end

At the opening of Chapter 8, the Kośa distinguishes attainment-dhyāna from
birth-dhyāna and identifies the former, in its defining nature, with wholesome
one-pointedness; with its accompaniment, it has the five aggregates. This
provides a concrete problem of unity: how can a defining determination and
its differentiated accompaniment be one attainment?

Our stronger claim is that **Samāpatti is the Concept of that unity when the
entire movement is comprehended**. It must be able to hold together:

- the principle known by PRAJNA;
- the determinate world known by JNANA and differentiated in VIJNANA;
- the practical transformation accomplished through action and Path;
- cessation and the intelligible return from cessation.

In Yoga's first moment, Samāpatti is the principle by which determinacy can
become transparent to Knowing. At the end of our Kośa movement, Samāpatti
must be comprehended as the achieved Concept of the movement that has passed
through world, action, Path, and knowledge. **The end makes its own beginning
intelligible.** The Concept does not collect finished chapters into a list;
it determines why their passage and return belong to one science. The Kośa's
final verse is an injunction against negligence by seekers of liberation; it
does not itself announce this Organon Concept.

## Return to the beginning as the test of certainty

The Kośa's *darśanamārga* is defined by seeing a truth not previously seen.
Calling Yoga **Absolute Jñāna Mārga** is our further placement: the work of
Knowing presupposes, in logical order, a disclosure of what must be known.
That does not make Yoga Sūtra I a historical sequel to Kośa Chapter 6 or
claim that its practitioner has already completed the Kośa's Path of Seeing.

The achieved Samāpatti at the end is certain only if Knowing can return to
Prajñāpāda's first determination and comprehend why the whole passage was
necessary. The beginning is then known as a beginning, rather than simply
repeated. Dharma-megha gives this return its fuller determination: the dharmas
first have relative certainty in their distinct places and relations. At the
end, their whole chain is comprehended as one necessary movement. **They
achieve Absolute Certainty together**, through the return that makes each
relative determination intelligible within the whole. The end does not keep
certainty for itself and leave the preceding dharmas behind. In this Organon
proposal, Absolute Certainty belongs to the completed movement and its
reflective return, rather than to the mere intensity of an attained state.

This also locates the question of sensibility. Yoga's Samāpatti can serve as
our principle for the *intelligibility of manifestation*: a relation of
grasper, grasping, and grasped can become transparent as one determinate act.
The Kośa's *vijñapti* is more specific: bodily or verbal action that makes
something known. Its *avijñapti* is ethically qualified efficacy that does
not make itself known in that way. Neither technical term, by itself, names
the condition that makes all sensibility possible. The first is the nearer
analogy for appearance; the second tests whether a determination remains
operative without present disclosure. Their unity is work for the Concept,
not an identity given by the Sanskrit words.

### Kantian apperception: a question for our avijñapti

Kant distinguishes empirical apperception, consciousness of one's changing
inner state, from transcendental apperception, the original unity through which
representations can belong to one consciousness and be combined in knowledge.
This gives the Organon a question to retain: **can our avijñapti name the
operative but undisplayed continuity of unification, while vijñapti names its
determinate disclosure in experience and action?** The analogy concerns the
relation of unshown efficacy to appearance; it does not identify Kant's
transcendental apperception with the Kośa's ethically qualified avijñapti.
Nor does Kant's unity of apperception, by itself, supply the sensible manifold:
the distinction between what is given and its synthesis remains to be worked
through in our account of Samāpatti.

One proposed Organon mapping is to read **VIJNANA as the site of a
transcendental apperceptive function** and **JNANA as ordinary, empirical
apperception**.
The proposal asks how a unified act makes the changing nāma-rūpa field
knowable and how that act becomes explicit knowledge. It cannot be obtained
by equating the Sanskrit terms with Kant's: Kośa 1.16 defines vijñāna as
object-specific apprehension in six groups; at the rebirth junction in 3.21,
the whole five-aggregate state receives the predominant designation
*vijñāna*. Kośa 7.01 includes worldly and noble cognition under *jñāna*.
Whether the unity Kant calls transcendental apperception belongs to VIJNANA
itself or to JNA's articulation through Samāpatti remains to be derived.

### Future response: object in general and the Kośa

A further Kantian prompt is the distinction between phenomenon and noumenon
under the question of an object in general. For our reconstruction, ask
whether the object-pole of manifestation extends from sensible form to what
can be presented as a mental object, while vijñānaskandha names the
object-directed apprehension rather than that pole itself. This is a
functional distinction, not a claim that cognition can never become an
object of subsequent cognition.

The Kośa's classifications keep the question exact. Its compact coverage in
1.18 joins **rūpaskandha, manāyatana, and dharmadhātu**; 1.16 places
vijñānaskandha at manāyatana and the other mental factors, avijñapti, and
the unconditioned at dharmadhātu. The word *dharmaskandha* at 1.25 instead
names a collection of teaching, not a further object-aggregate. Hence the
proposed Organon relation between sensibility in general and the object in
general must be derived across these different classifications. Kant's
noumenon also first marks a limit on sensible cognition; it cannot simply
be assigned to the Kośa's dharmadhātu or avijñapti.

Kośa 3.30 supplies the term for the complementary side of *rūpa*:
**nāma tv arūpiṇaḥ skandhāḥ**, “name, however, is the non-rūpa
aggregates.” *Nāma* gathers four aggregates, including vijñānaskandha;
there is no additional *nāma-skandha*. Hence “object, rather than
vijñāna” above distinguishes roles in an act of apprehension, not two
exhaustive kinds of aggregate. *Dharmaskandha* names collections of the
teaching, whose inclusion the Kośa determines by whether Buddha-word is
speech or linguistic name; that use of *name* must not be silently equated
with the four-aggregate *nāma* of 3.30. It does not name the unity of the
aggregates. Our further proposal is that **a Buddha's Samāpatti comprehends
the relation among formed presentation, nonmaterial operations, cognition,
and articulate teaching**. That unity is the Organon question, not a sixth
skandha or an identity asserted by the Kośa.

## The proposed form of the Concept

- **Universal:** JNA is self-determining Knowing, not an empty sameness behind
  its appearances.
- **Particular:** JNANA and PRAJNA distinguish determinate knowing from the
  comprehension of principles. VIJNANA specifies their finite cognitive
  articulation; the world-system and Path expose what these distinctions do.
- **Singular:** Samāpatti is the achieved unity in which the differentiated
  movement is known as this movement and can begin again with its result
  preserved.

The third moment must be derived from the first two. If we simply call a
collected state “the Singular,” or identify JNA with a special experience,
the Concept has not yet been generated.

## The Principle of the Principle

A candidate principle must account for its own operation. For this project,
that means it must:

1. **Generate determinations** rather than merely name a genus.
2. **Explain their difference and efficacy** in a world of cognition and
   action.
3. **Expose their limit**, including appropriation, error, and the failure of
   a finite standpoint to comprehend its own ground.
4. **Specify a lawful return** through correction, cessation, and renewed
   articulation.
5. **Preserve what has been learned** so the return is intelligible as the
   movement's own result.

This self-applying requirement is what we mean by *the Principle of the
Principle*. **Imperium** names the systematic order in which each domain and
act can be placed, tested, corrected, and recognized within that movement.

## First tests

- **Avijñapti:** explain how a determination remains practically effective
  when no current act makes it known. A Speech Seed may illuminate vows and
  verbal action, but must also meet bodily action and nonverbal cases.
- **Samāpatti:** derive the unity of defining concentration and its
  differentiated accompaniment without confusing attainment with rebirth.
- **Cessation and return:** distinguish the disappearance of an occurrent
  cognition from the achieved comprehension of what ceased and how knowing
  reappears.
- **Speech and judgment:** show why a spoken determination such as
  “desecration” or “dissolution” answers to the act and its governing
  principle, rather than gaining truth from utterance alone.

The thesis remains open until these passages can be shown in detail. Its
present achievement is to state what the Concept must do and where its tests
lie.
