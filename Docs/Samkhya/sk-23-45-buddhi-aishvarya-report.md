# Sāṃkhya Kārikā 23 and 43–45: Buddhi, Aiśvarya, and Non-Obstruction

## Bootstrap Organon report

### Why these verses

Yoga I.29 names *antarāya-abhāva*, absence of obstacles, in the sequence
of Īśvara practice. Sāṃkhya Kārikā 45 names *avighāta*, non-obstruction,
as a result of *aiśvarya*. The similarity is worth studying because it
prevents us from treating absence of obstacles as a unique signature of
Yoga's Īśvara. The nearby Kārikās show that *aiśvarya* belongs to
Sāṃkhya's account of Buddhi and its dispositions. [Local Kārikā
witness](../../Reference/Samkhya.txt); [Yoga I.29
study](../../Patanjali/YS/01-prajna/YS-I.29.md).

### Source sequence

Kārikā 23 describes Buddhi as *adhyavasāya*, determination, and names
*dharma*, *jñāna*, *virāga*, and *aiśvarya* as its sāttvika form; the
opposite is tāmasa:

> अध्यवसायो बुद्धिर्धर्मो ज्ञानं विरागाइश्वर्यम् ।
> सात्त्विकमेतद्रूपं तामसमस्माद् विपर्यस्तम् ॥ २३॥

Kārikā 43 says that dispositions such as *dharma* are innate, natural,
or acquired and may have an instrument or effect as support. Kārikās
44–45 then assign different consequences to *dharma*, *adharma*,
*jñāna*, error, *vairāgya*, *rāga*, *aiśvarya*, and its opposite.
The relevant close statement is:

> ऐश्वर्यादविघातो विपर्ययात् तद्विपर्यासः ॥ ४५॥

“From *aiśvarya*, non-obstruction; from its opposite, the contrary.”
The transmitted local text has editorial numerals in other lines. These
quotations preserve the wording relevant to this first report; a
critical witness comparison remains future work.

### First determination

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Buddhi is determination, and *aiśvarya* appears in its sāttvika form. | Textual | SK 23 |
| *Aiśvarya* yields *avighāta*. | Textual | SK 45 |
| Yoga's Īśvara practice is one route associated with absence of obstacles. | Textual | YS I.23–29 |
| SK *aiśvarya* and YS *Īśvara* are the same principle. | Unestablished | The shared effect does not prove identity. |

The two works use related vocabulary at different places in their
arguments. Sāṃkhya predicates *aiśvarya* within the account of Buddhi;
Yoga I.24 calls Īśvara a *puruṣa-viśeṣa*. We can investigate how
non-obstruction functions in both systems while keeping those predicates
distinct.

### Organon operation to test

**Input:** a path condition described as obstructed. **Rule:** identify
the power or practice credited with removing obstruction in the passage.
**Output:** a source-specific account of *avighāta* or
*antarāya-abhāva*. **Failure condition:** infer identity of the powers
solely from the shared result. This is a preliminary comparative
procedure, not yet a Kantian reconstruction of the whole Kārikā system.

### The immeasurables question

The local Kārikā witness does not name *maitrī*, *karuṇā*, *muditā*, and
*upekṣā* as a fourfold set. Yoga I.33 and [Kośa VAK
8.29–31](../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.29_bhasya.md) do name
that set. A future Sāṃkhya report should locate the exact Sāṃkhya
passage or commentary intended, identify its date and textual setting,
and compare its four terms and stated result with Yoga and Kośa. The
historical claim that one text originated the practice remains open.
