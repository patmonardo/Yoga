# Dhyana-Born Citta and the Resolution of Contradiction

## Yoga Sutra IV.6 with Hegel

`tatra dhyana-jam anasayam`

> Among these, the Citta born from Dhyana has no latent deposit.

Fichte interpreted this Citta genetically: it arises from a consciously
performed act rather than from an inherited factical presupposition. Hegel
places the passage under **contradiction**.

A determination of reflection becomes contradictory when it claims both to
be self-subsistent and to depend upon excluding or containing its other. Its
independence is thus constituted by what it denies. The contradiction is not
an accidental defect added from outside; it is the determination's own
movement toward dissolution into ground.

The ordinary deposited Citta carries such an unresolved structure:

```text
I am this determination
        |
        | but this determination depends on what it excludes
        v
latent contradiction reproduces another conditioned Citta
```

Dhyana does not merely suppress one side. It sustains the determination until
the opposed moments reveal their mutual implication and lose their claim to
independent subsistence. The resulting Citta is `anasaya`: it does not carry
the unresolved opposition forward as a latent deposit.

This does **not** make `anasaya` lexically mean “contradiction-free.” Its plain
meaning remains without karmic reservoir. Hegel explains how a determination
can cease to be deposited: its contradiction is comprehended and returned to
ground rather than unconsciously reproduced.

The Hegelian Varttika is:

> The Dhyana-born Citta has no deposit because reflection has completed the
> contradiction of its determinations instead of preserving their opposition
> as an inherited tendency.

Citta remains. What is absent is the unresolved contradiction that would
force its determinate form to repeat itself.
