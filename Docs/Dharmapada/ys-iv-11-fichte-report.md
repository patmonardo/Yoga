# When the Supports Are Absent

## Yoga Sutra IV.11 with Fichte

## The Sutra

`hetu-phala-asraya-alambana-sangrhitatvad esam abhave tad-abhava`

### Plain translation

> Because the Vāsanās are held together by cause, result, support, and object,
> when these are absent, the Vāsanās are absent.

`Eṣām` refers to the factors just enumerated; `tad-abhāva` states the
corresponding absence of the Vāsanā-complex under discussion. The Sutra does
not say that Citta, Cit, or knowing becomes absent.

The structure is conditional and exact:

```text
Hetu + Phala + Āśraya + Ālambana
                  |
                  | collect and sustain
                  v
                Vāsanā

absence of the four -> absence of the supported Vāsanā
```

Beginninglessness in IV.10 did not make Vāsanā unconditioned. IV.11 now
specifies the present architecture through which it exists.

## The four factors

For the present essay they can be rendered plainly:

- `hetu`: the cause generating the disposition;
- `phala`: the result through which it is confirmed and continued;
- `āśraya`: the support or locus in which it subsists;
- `ālambana`: the object upon which its activity depends.

A Vāsanā is not a hidden thing that first exists independently and later
enters these relations. It exists as collected through them. Its absence when
they are absent is therefore not the destruction of a substance. It is the
cessation of a supported process.

## Fichte's contradiction of derivative knowing

Fichte begins with a contradiction between enactment and assertion in all
derivative knowing. Such knowing says that an insight is merely possible and
still awaits its condition. Yet in order to state this possibility, it must
already enact and possess the determinate seeing whose condition it seeks.

```text
what derivative knowing says:   the insight is not yet actual

what derivative knowing does:   it already enacts the insight as determinate
```

This contradiction belongs to reconstruction. Reconstruction presupposes an
original content, retains it, and produces it in another form. It therefore
cannot be the absolute source of the content it reconstructs.

The constructed I survives by continually placing four things around its
act: an originating cause, a resulting appearance, a supporting consciousness,
and an object toward which consciousness is directed. These make the act
appear derivative and preserve the gap between knowing and being.

## A Fichtean reading of the four supports

The correspondence should remain functional rather than lexical:

| Yoga IV.11 | Fichtean function |
|---|---|
| `hetu` | the posited "from" through which derivative knowing explains itself |
| `phala` | the determinate insight or appearance produced |
| `āśraya` | the reconstructing We or factical consciousness taken as bearer |
| `ālambana` | the object or determinate content set over against seeing |

Together these sustain reconstruction as a self-enclosed cognitive formation.
As long as they are taken as independently subsisting terms, the constructed
I must repeat the same contradiction between doing and saying.

Fichte does not resolve the contradiction by annihilating knowing. He removes
the independence of the terms.

## Pure reason remains

Fichte returns to what remains when every relation has been abstracted: pure
light or reason. This remainder is not produced by our choice to abstract. It
remains by itself. Inner being, light, and reason are different names for one
indivisible singularity.

The crucial advance is that light and genesis cannot be separated:

> There is no light without creation, and creation is only in and through
> light.

Pure reason says what it does and does what it says. The opposition between a
supporting subject and a supported object is no longer needed to explain its
act.

```text
derivative knowing
subject -> reconstructs -> object

pure reason
light = living genesis = manifestation of genesis
```

This is the Fichtean meaning of `tad-abhāva`. The supported reconstruction
becomes absent when the separable terms that collect it are absent. Light is
not absent. On the contrary, light appears more purely when it is no longer
misrepresented as the property of a reconstructing I.

## Eka Citta and Paramarthika

Eka Citta is isomorphic to the pāramārthika standpoint insofar as it is the
unity of knowing rather than another conditioned item within what is known.
Fichte calls this unity the qualitative oneness of light penetrating the
entire `from` or genesis.

The many Cittas and their Vāsanās are appearances within this genesis. When a
particular conditioned complex is unsupported, that complex does not arise.
Eka Citta does not die with it, because Eka Citta was never one of its four
supports.

```text
Vāsanā-complex absent     conditioned specification no longer arises

Eka Citta remains         light knows genesis without that specification
```

Fichte's striking phrase that the We must perish therefore receives its
proper restriction. What perishes is the We as an independently existing
bearer of reconstruction. The light in which this false claim is recognized
does not perish.

## Fichte-Bhasya

> Vāsanā persists only within the derivative structure that distinguishes a
> cause, a result, a supporting consciousness, and an opposed object. When
> these cease to stand as independent terms, their conditioned construction
> is absent. Pure light remains as the qualitative unity of genesis and its
> manifestation.

IV.11 therefore completes Reflection without killing Citta. It removes the
architecture through which the constructed I mistakes derivative cognition
for the source of knowing.
