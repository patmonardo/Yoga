# Dharma-Megha as Real Actuality

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.29 with Hegel and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`prasaṃkhyāne 'py akusīdasya sarvathā viveka-khyāter dharma-meghaḥ samādhiḥ`

### Simple translation

> For one who has no investment even in complete discriminative knowledge,
> and whose Viveka is continuous in every way, there is Dharma-Megha
> Samādhi.

The Sūtra gives two conditions and their necessary actuality:

```text
no investment even in complete discriminative knowledge
                            +
Viveka continuous throughout the whole field
                            ↓
                 Dharma-Megha Samādhi
```

Hegel gives this Samādhi its exact modal determination:

> Dharma-Megha is Real Actuality—the unity that preserves itself throughout
> manifold content and manifests itself through what it produces.

## The textual boundary

The Hegelian material for IV.29 is section B.1 of “Relative Necessity or Real
Actuality, Possibility, and Necessity.”

It begins with formal Necessity receiving content and becoming Real
Actuality. It ends when Real Actuality distinguishes itself from the Real
Possibility already implicit within it.

This boundary preserves Hegel's order:

```text
IV.29    B.1  Real Actuality
IV.30    B.2  Real Possibility and conditions
IV.31    B.3a Real Necessity
IV.32    B.3b Relativity and Absolute Actuality
```

## Why formal Necessity is insufficient

IV.28 ended with formal Necessity. Actuality and Possibility converted into
one another, and each rejoined itself within the other.

But their unity remained immediate:

```text
Actuality ↔ Possibility

necessary conversion
but still without self-subsistent content
```

Hegel calls this necessity formal because its moments are simple
determinations. Their unity is indifferent to the difference between them.

This cannot yet be Dharma-Megha. A Cloud of Dharma cannot be an empty modal
formula. It must saturate and determine an actual manifold.

## Formal Necessity receives content

Because the formal unity is indifferent to its differences, it can contain
them as a manifold content.

```text
formal unity of Actuality and Possibility
                         ↓ receives
variety of determinate content
                         ↓
Real Actuality
```

Real Actuality is not the empty assertion that something exists. It is the
complete form-unity of inward Principle and outward manifestation carrying
an articulated content.

This is the beginning of Dharma-Megha: Dharma becomes the living content of
the whole field.

## Why the Cloud is full

`Megha`, the Cloud, should not be interpreted as an indeterminate mist in
which all distinctions disappear.

Hegel's Real Actuality is explicitly manifold:

```text
one self-preserving actuality
        ↓ articulated as
many properties, relations, and determinate forms
```

The Cloud is “full” because Viveka has penetrated every determination. Its
unity does not exclude difference; it preserves itself through difference.

```text
not:

Viveka removes the manifold


but:

Viveka becomes continuous throughout the manifold
```

That is `sarvathā viveka-khyāti`.

## The actual world does not dissolve into Appearance

Hegel initially describes Real Actuality as the Thing of many properties or
the concretely existing world. But this is no longer the earlier Thing that
dissolved into Appearance.

Why not? Because Real Actuality already contains immanent Reflection:

```text
earlier concrete existence
external properties point beyond themselves
and dissolve into Appearance

Real Actuality
external manifold is simultaneously
the actuality's inward self-relation
```

Its externality does not alienate it from itself. Outward manifestation is
the way its inward Principle preserves itself.

## Appearance and Reflection are actual together

The A–U–M structure now acquires real content:

```text
A — Appearance
the manifold content and its outward properties

U — Reflection / thinking
the inward self-relation preserved throughout the manifold

M — Knowing
Real Actuality as the unity that manifests itself in both
```

A does not become Reality by itself, and U is not Knowing by itself. Their
complete actuality is M: the one content existing as outward manifestation
and inward self-relation.

Dharma-Megha is this M saturated with determinate Dharma.

## `Akusīda`: no private investment

`Prasaṃkhyāna` is the highest discriminative contemplation. But its result
can still be appropriated:

```text
Viveka produces insight
        ↓
Ahaṃkāra claims the result
        ↓
“my wisdom, my attainment, my saintliness”
```

`Akusīda` removes this investment. The Scientist does not settle into the
result as private cognitive capital.

Hegel's Real Actuality explains why. Truth is not an inward possession
standing apart from the world. It is actual only as the self-preserving unity
manifested throughout objective content.

```text
not invested in a private knower
                    ↓
Prajñā becomes the Dharma of the whole actual field
```

## What is actual can act

Hegel's decisive statement is:

> What is actual can act; something announces its actuality by what it
> produces.

Real Actuality cannot remain a contemplative inwardness. Its outward product
is the announcement of what it inwardly is.

```text
inward clarity
      ↓ necessarily manifests as
constructive activity
      ↓ produces
an actuality bearing the same Principle
```

This is why Dharma is constructive and why Good Karma matters. If the
supposed insight continually produces a world governed by the old Kleśas,
the insight has not yet become Real Actuality.

## Manifestation is not transition

Hegel distinguishes manifestation from two earlier relations.

### Not transition

In transition, one immediate something passes into another and loses itself:

```text
A → B
A ceases to be what it was
```

Real Actuality does not leave itself behind when it acts.

### Not mere appearing

In appearing, a Thing possesses its Being only through relation to an other:

```text
Thing → appears for another
```

Real Actuality does not depend upon an external observer to complete it.

### Manifestation

```text
Real Actuality
      ↓ relates to another self-subsistent actuality
      ↓ while preserving its own immanent Reflection
manifestation of itself
```

Its production is neither loss nor dependence. It is self-expression.

## Dharma-Megha is constructive manifestation

The Cloud of Dharma can now be stated without relying upon saintly imagery:

> Dharma-Megha is Knowing whose complete inward discrimination has become
> the productive form of its outward actuality.

```text
continuous Viveka
the whole manifold is transparent to its Principle
                    ↓
no investment
the Principle is not enclosed within a private subject
                    ↓
Real Actuality
the Principle manifests through what it produces
```

The “rain” of Dharma is the objective production of intelligible and
non-afflicted relations.

## Prayer, the Immeasurables, and Actuality

Prayer can establish orientation:

```text
hold the highest Artha before Citta
```

The Immeasurables cultivate the practical form through which that orientation
becomes actual:

```text
friendliness, compassion, sympathetic joy, equanimity
        ↓ repeatedly enacted
construct non-afflicted relations and Saṃskāras
```

OṂ-Artha remains the theoretical Mission: complete intelligibility of All
This. The Immeasurables are not a retreat from that Mission. They are one
way its Knowing acquires Real Actuality in a suffering world.

```text
OṂ-Artha                     Immeasurables
complete intelligibility    constructive practical relation
              \             /
               Dharma-Megha
          Knowing made actual
```

Prayer alone cannot replace this construction, but it can keep Citta turned
toward its Principle while action makes that Principle real.

## Heaven and hell as products of Actuality

Hegel's criterion is unforgiving: an actuality announces itself by what it
produces.

```text
Kleśa-informed holding-to-be-true
        ↓ produces
afflicted Karma and social actuality
        ↓
a constructed Netherworld


Prajñā-informed Dharma
        ↓ produces
Good Karma and non-afflicted relation
        ↓
an actuality capable of liberation
```

This does not require an external Being arbitrarily assigning the result.
The product manifests the constructive Principle.

The claim about what occurs after biological death remains a further
metaphysical thesis. But the immanent production of hellish and liberating
worlds is already part of Real Actuality.

## Real Actuality contains Real Possibility

Although Real Actuality preserves itself through its manifold content, it
still contains an in-itself or possibility.

At first the two remain distinguished:

```text
Real Actuality
the immediately existing manifold

Real Possibility
the complete conditions implicit within that actuality
```

This distinction prepares IV.30. The actual product must disclose the real
conditions through which it could and did arise.

Dharma-Megha therefore contains the entire Triple Siddhi implicitly, but
their conditions have not yet been unfolded.

## Fichte's IV.29: pure certainty

Fichte reaches IV.29 by abstracting from every hypothetical subordinate term
until only pure certainty remains.

Pure certainty is:

- not certainty about a particular `what`;
- grounded unconditionally in itself;
- unshakable persistence in one qualitative form;
- the condition and support of every determinate `what`;
- living Light that necessarily projects itself.

```text
pure certainty
      ↓ supports
every determinate content
      ↓ necessarily self-projects
actual Knowing
```

Fichte gives the inward genesis of the Principle that Hegel now calls Real
Actuality.

## Fichte and Hegel compared

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| pure certainty is grounded in itself | Real Actuality preserves itself in manifold content |
| certainty supports every determinate `what` | formal Necessity receives content |
| certainty is unshakable qualitative persistence | immanent Reflection remains throughout external multiplicity |
| living Light necessarily projects itself | the actual announces itself through what it produces |
| the factical scientist cannot own the ground | `akusīda` prevents investment in the highest knowledge |
| certainty unites Light and Being | Dharma-Megha unites continuous Viveka and actual manifestation |

Fichte explains why the Principle is inwardly self-grounding. Hegel explains
what it means for that Principle to be real: it must preserve and manifest
itself throughout a determinate world.

## Their mutual correction

Fichte prevents Real Actuality from becoming mere effectiveness. A thing is
not ultimately actual merely because it produces effects; its production
must arise from self-grounding certainty.

Hegel prevents pure certainty from remaining an inward spiritual possession.
Certainty is real only when it manifests itself through determinate content
and action.

```text
Fichte
self-grounding inward certainty
                    +
Hegel
self-manifesting outward actuality
                    ↓
Dharma-Megha
Knowing actual throughout its complete field
```

## Why the saintly consequences follow

IV.29 is the governing Principle for IV.30–32:

```text
Dharma-Megha as Real Actuality
                  |
      ┌───────────┼───────────┐
      ↓           ↓           ↓
IV.30           IV.31       IV.32
conditions      possibility Artha of the
of Kleśa-Karma  becomes     conditioned
self-sublate    necessity   sequence completes
```

Purification, infinite Knowledge, and completed transformation are genuine.
But they are results of Real Actuality, not trophies acquired by an empirical
saint.

Seeking them as possessions would contradict the explicit condition
`akusīda`.

## Provisional result

IV.29 says:

> When Viveka is continuous throughout every determination and even the
> highest knowledge is no longer privately appropriated, the Science of
> Knowing becomes Dharma-Megha—content-filled Real Actuality.

Hegel shows that this Actuality preserves its inward self-relation throughout
the manifold and announces itself by what it produces. Dharma-Megha is not
withdrawal into empty unity. It is Knowing made objectively constructive.

Fichte supplies its inward ground in pure, self-grounding certainty. Hegel
supplies its outward criterion in manifestation.

```text
pure certainty
        ↓ becomes content-filled
continuous Viveka
        ↓ becomes constructive
Real Actuality
        ↓
Dharma-Megha Samādhi
```

IV.30 can now analyze the Real Possibility implicit within this Actuality:
the complete conditions whose self-sublation ends the Kleśa-Karma circuit.
