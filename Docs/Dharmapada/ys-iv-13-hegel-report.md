# Guṇa-Nature as Concrete Existence

## Yoga Sūtra IV.13 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`te vyakta-sūkṣmā guṇātmānaḥ`

> Those Dharmas, whether manifest or subtle, have the Guṇas as their
> nature.

`Te` carries forward the temporally differentiated Dharmas of IV.12.
`Vyakta` and `sūkṣma` distinguish their manifest and subtle forms.
`Guṇātmānaḥ` states their common nature: both are Guṇa-formed.

The Sūtra therefore joins a real difference to a deeper identity:

```text
subtle Dharma                      manifest Dharma
reflected inward                   standing forth
         \                         /
          \_____ Guṇa-nature ____/
```

The subtle does not inhabit a second reality behind the manifest. The
manifest does not become detached from the nature through which it came
forth. They are different forms of one concrete existence.

## Why IV.13 follows IV.12

IV.12 established that past and future Dharmas are according to their own
forms because their paths differ. That result could still be misunderstood
as a plurality of separate temporal realms.

IV.13 prevents the separation:

```text
IV.12    difference of temporal path
                         ↓
IV.13    identity of Guṇa-nature throughout the difference
```

A Dharma may withdraw into subtlety or advance into manifestation without
ceasing to belong to the same qualitative process. Temporal difference is a
difference in how Guṇa-nature concretely exists.

## The principle of concrete existence

Hegel proposes a counterpart to the principle of sufficient reason:

> Whatever is, exists concretely.

The principle of sufficient reason says that what is has a ground and is
mediated. The principle of concrete existence says that mediation does not
remain behind the result. Ground and condition sublate their separation, and
their content stands forth in immediacy.

```text
ground + conditions
        ↓ self-sublation of mediation
concrete existence
```

Concrete existence is therefore neither sheer immediacy nor mediation with
its ground still located elsewhere. It is **mediated immediacy**: essence
that has come forth and is present in its own determination.

This is the Hegelian force of `vyakta`. The manifest Dharma is not a surface
added to a concealed essence. It is essence itself standing forth as
concrete existence.

## The subtle does not remain behind

Hegel's most important proposition here is not:

> Essence has concrete existence.

That formulation leaves essence on one side and treats existence as a
predicate added to it. Hegel's proposition is stronger:

> Essence is concrete existence.

Essence passes completely into concrete existence. Its self-emptying leaves
nothing of essence behind as a hidden reserve.

Applied to IV.13:

```text
not:

subtle essence behind  +  manifest shell before us

but:

subtle Guṇa-determination  ->  manifest Guṇa-determination
             one nature throughout
```

The subtle has not vanished into nothing when the Dharma becomes manifest.
It has sublated its merely inward form. Conversely, a manifest Dharma that
becomes subtle does not leave Guṇa-nature behind; it alters the mode in which
that nature subsists.

Thus `guṇātmānaḥ` gives the identity needed for the movement between
`sūkṣma` and `vyakta`.

## Ground vanishes in the subject matter

Hegel illustrates this structure through proofs of God's concrete existence.
A proof appears to supply a ground from outside the subject matter. But if
the ground were genuinely external, it would make the Absolute dependent
upon another. In true mediation, the ground vanishes in what it grounds.

The point is logical rather than theological:

> A successful ground does not remain as a scaffolding outside the concrete
> existent. It has become the existent's own reflected immediacy.

This protects IV.13 from an external-production model. The Guṇas are not
hidden causes that manufacture Dharmas and then remain behind them. To say
that Dharmas are `guṇātman` is to say that their own concrete existence is
the Guṇa-process made immediate.

## Conditioned and unconditioned

Hegel makes a difficult double claim. Whatever exists concretely is grounded
and conditioned, because it has come through mediation. Yet concrete
existence is also groundless and unconditioned, because the distinction
between ground and grounded has been sublated in the result.

These are not two different things:

```text
conditioned      as having come through ground and condition
unconditioned    as no longer referring to an external ground
```

The manifest Dharma therefore does not float free of its genesis. Nor does
it depend upon a ground standing somewhere behind it. Its conditions are
preserved as its own determinate nature.

This gives `guṇātman` a precise systematic function: Guṇa-nature is the
immanent mediation present in the Dharma, whether the Dharma is subtle or
manifest.

## Hegel and Fichte

Fichte determined the one qualitative Light as an absolute `from`. Its
disjoined terms arise through its genesis while the Light remains one across
them. Hegel now determines the ontological result of that genesis.

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| one Light is an absolute `from` | essence passes into concrete existence |
| qualitative oneness permeates subtle/manifest disjunction | mediation becomes self-identical immediacy |
| the terms arise within one genesis | the existent has the mediation as its own nature |

Fichte shows how qualitative identity remains alive throughout genesis.
Hegel shows why the result is not merely an appearance constructed by
knowing: essence has itself become concrete existence.

Yoga's `guṇātmānaḥ` holds both results together. The different Dharmas
share one qualitative nature, and that nature concretely exists in their
manifest and subtle forms.

## Citta-Dharma

The subtle Vāsanā and the manifest Citta-determination must not be treated as
two independently subsisting entities joined by an external causal link.

```text
subtle Saṃskāra / Vāsanā
             ↓ manifestation
present Citta-Dharma

= one Guṇa-process in different forms of concrete existence
```

The manifest determination is conditioned, but its conditions are not a
second psychic world concealed behind it. They have passed into its present
quality, intensity, direction, and capacity for further transformation.

Likewise, subtlety is not non-being. It is Guṇa-nature reflected inward,
without the immediate subsistence of manifestation.

The practical discipline is exact: attend to the manifest Citta so fully
that its subtle determination is known as immanent within its appearance,
not inferred as a mysterious object behind it.

## Not yet the Thing

Hegel distinguishes three levels of being:

1. immediate Being;
2. concrete existence proceeding from Essence;
3. Objectivity proceeding from the Concept.

IV.13 occupies the second level. Manifest and subtle Dharmas have become
concrete existents, but their unity has not yet been determined as a Thing.
Concrete existence must first gather its reflected determinations into a
negative unity.

That transition belongs to IV.14:

```text
IV.13                               IV.14

Guṇa-nature concretely exists   ->   unity of transformation
as manifest and subtle                  constitutes the Thing
```

## Hegel-Vārttika

> Manifest and subtle Dharmas are Guṇa-nature because essence does not
> remain behind its Appearance. The mediation of the subtle determination
> sublates itself into manifest immediacy, and this immediacy retains that
> mediation as its own qualitative nature. Essence is not other than its
> concrete existence.

IV.13 therefore supplies the first principle of concrete existence for the
Yoga system:

> **Whatever Dharma exists, whether manifest or subtle, exists as the
> concrete self-expression of Guṇa-nature.**

IV.14 can now determine how these differentiated concrete existents possess
the unity required for `vastu-tattva`, the truth of the Thing.
