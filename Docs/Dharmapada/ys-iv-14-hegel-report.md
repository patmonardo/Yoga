# The Thing Is the Unity of Its Transformation

## Yoga Sūtra IV.14 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`pariṇāmaikatvād vastu-tattvam`

> The truth of the Thing consists in the unity of its transformation.

The sentence is compact enough to invite a misleading picture: first there
is a Thing, then it undergoes changes, and beneath those changes it somehow
remains one. But the grammar gives `pariṇāmaikatva`, the oneness of
transformation, as the reason for `vastu-tattva`, the truth or actuality of
the Thing.

The stronger reading is therefore:

> The Thing is not an inert bearer that survives transformation. Its truth
> is the transformation comprehended as one.

## From Guṇa-nature to the Thing

IV.13 established that manifest and subtle Dharmas have the Guṇas as their
common nature. But common nature alone does not yet make one Thing. We still
need to know how the differentiated determinations belong together.

```text
IV.13                            IV.14

manifest and subtle             their differentiated movement
are Guṇa-nature          ->     is one transformation
                                           ↓
                                         Vastu
```

IV.14 supplies the missing unity. Guṇa-nature is not a collection of
qualities scattered through different temporal phases. Its manifestation
and withdrawal are moments of one `pariṇāma`. That unity is what allows us
to speak of **the Thing**.

## Concrete existence becomes Thing

Hegel begins from the result reached at IV.13. Concrete existence is
mediated immediacy: Essence has passed into an immediate determination
without leaving its mediation behind.

Concrete existence now determines itself as a Thing because it is a
**negative unity**. It contains different determinations, but those
determinations are not merely placed next to one another. The Thing is the
unity that differentiates itself and returns to itself through them.

```text
concrete existence
        ↓ contains reflected difference
negative unity
        ↓
Thing
```

This negative unity is the Hegelian clarification of `pariṇāmaikatva`.
The transformation is one not because difference has been suppressed, but
because the differences are moments of one self-relating movement.

## The false Thing-in-itself

At first, reflection divides the Thing into two sides:

```text
Thing-in-itself             external existence
essential unity             manifold appearances
```

On this picture, the Thing-in-itself is an indeterminate substrate, while
color, weight, smell, motion, and other determinations arise only through
external relations. The Thing supposedly remains untouched behind them.

Hegel shows why this cannot stand. An indeterminate Thing-in-itself has no
content by which it could be this Thing rather than another. All of its
determinateness has been placed outside it. What was supposed to secure the
Thing's identity has become an empty abstraction.

Yoga IV.14 blocks the same mistake. If the unity of the Thing consisted in a
substrate outside transformation, then `pariṇāma` would be unessential to
`vastu-tattva`. The Sūtra says the opposite: the Thing's truth follows from
the unity of transformation itself.

## Property is reflected determination

The external determinations of a Thing initially look like properties added
to a substrate. Hegel asks what a property actually is.

A property appears only in relation:

- color appears in relation to vision;
- weight appears in a determinate interaction;
- hardness appears as resistance to another body.

Yet a property is not invented by the observer. The Thing responds in a
characteristic way because the determination belongs to it. Property is
therefore both:

```text
reference to another    and    the Thing's own determination
```

Hegel calls this a reflected quality. The Thing remains itself in its
relation to another precisely by expressing its own determination through
that relation.

This gives a concrete meaning to unity through transformation. The Thing is
not first self-contained and only afterward related. Its self-identity is
active in the transformations through which its properties become manifest.

## Relation does not manufacture the Thing

This point matters for the transition to the plurality of Cittas in IV.15.
If color appears only in relation to an eye, it may seem that Citta
manufactures the colored Thing.

Hegel's analysis avoids both extremes:

```text
not: property is a finished item hidden inside the Thing

not: property is freely projected by an external observer

but: property is the Thing's own determination expressed in relation
```

The relation is necessary for the property to appear, but the appearing
determination belongs to the Thing's own transformation. Different Cittas
may enter different relations with the same Thing without creating its
`vastu-tattva`.

## The Thing exists in its properties

Once the properties are understood as the Thing's own reflected
determinations, the empty substrate can no longer remain separate from them.
The Thing-in-itself is present in its properties, not hidden behind them.

This produces an inversion:

```text
first appearance

Thing-in-itself = essential
properties      = external additions

result of reflection

empty substrate = abstraction
properties      = the Thing's determinate subsistence
```

Without its determinate transformations, nothing remains of the Thing except
the word “one.” Its actual unity is the continuity that differentiates
itself through its properties.

That is `pariṇāmaikatva`: not numerical oneness beneath alteration, but
one reflected movement whose determinations persist by expressing themselves
through difference.

## Guṇa transformation

The three Guṇas should therefore not be imagined as three independent
materials poured into a container called the Thing. They are mutually
determining functions within one transformation.

```text
             sattva
            /      \
           /        \
       rajas -------- tamas
           \        /
            \      /
        one Guṇa transformation
                  ↓
                Vastu
```

Their distinction is real, but each acquires its determinate function only
within the movement of the whole. The `vastu` is not a fourth entity added
to the Guṇas. It is their differentiated transformation as one.

## Hegel and Fichte

Fichte described the one Light as the living `from` that generates and
comprehends its different terms. Hegel now follows that unity into the
structure of the Thing.

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| one Light permeates the genetic `from` | one negative unity persists through reflected determinations |
| the terms arise within one living transition | the Thing exists through the expression of its properties |
| reconstruction must return dead terms to genesis | reflection returns the empty substrate to its determinate relations |

Fichte makes the unity of transformation visible from the side of knowing.
Hegel shows that the Thing itself is not intelligible apart from this
self-differentiating unity.

## Citta and objectivity

IV.14 secures the Thing before IV.15 distinguishes the paths of different
Cittas. This order is essential.

A Citta encounters the Thing through determinate properties. Those
properties become manifest in relation to Citta, but they express the
Thing's own Guṇa-transformation. Consequently:

```text
Citta does not manufacture the Thing

Citta enters a determinate relation
through which the Thing expresses a property
```

The Thing is objective because its transformation is one across these
relations. Objectivity does not require a featureless substance hidden
beyond every appearance. It requires that the different appearances be
moments of one real transformation rather than unrelated products of
private Cittas.

## The limit of the Thing

Hegel does not allow the Thing to rest here. Once the Thing is reduced to
the unity expressed through its properties, the properties begin to claim
their own subsistence. The Thing threatens to become merely a collection of
independent “matters” or stuffs.

That instability prepares IV.15:

```text
Thing as one transformation
             ↓ differentiated properties
plural determinations with distinct cognitive paths
```

The unity of the Thing has been established, but its relation to the
different Cittas through which its properties appear must still be
determined.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> The truth of the Thing is the unity of its transformation. It is not an
> indeterminate substrate concealed behind its properties, for apart from
> its determinate relations such a substrate is nothing. The Thing is the
> negative unity that differentiates itself into properties and preserves
> itself in their manifestation.

IV.14 therefore gives `vastu-tattva` a rigorous meaning:

> **The Thing is actual because its manifold Guṇa-determinations are the
> properties of one self-related transformation.**

IV.15 can now distinguish the one path of that Thing from the different
paths of the Cittas that know it.
