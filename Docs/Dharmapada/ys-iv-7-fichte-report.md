# Action Beyond White and Black

## A report on Yoga Sutra IV.7 and Fichte

## Purpose

This report tests Yoga Sutra IV.7 alongside the conclusion of Fichte's
sixteenth lecture in the 1804 *Science of Knowing*.

IV.6 distinguished the Citta born from Dhyana as `anasaya`, without a karmic
deposit. IV.7 gives the practical consequence. When action proceeds from this
Citta, it is no longer classified by the colored results that bind ordinary
action to continued maturation.

## The Sutra

`karma-asukla-akrsnam yogina trividham itaresam`

### A close translation

> The yogin's karma is neither white nor black; that of others is threefold.

### A plain translation

> The yogin acts without producing either bright or dark karma; ordinary
> action remains dark, bright, or mixed.

The terms are straightforward but their relation is important:

- `karma`: action together with its ethically and causally effective form;
- `asukla`: not white or bright;
- `akrsna`: not black or dark;
- `yogina`: of the yogin;
- `trividham`: threefold;
- `itaresam`: of the others.

The threefold karma of others is black, white, or black-and-white. The yogin's
karma is not a fourth color alongside them. It is action released from the
entire opposition of karmic coloration.

```text
ordinary karma                 yogin's karma

black                          neither black
white                          nor white
black-and-white                not another color
```

The point is not moral indifference. The yogin has not become neutral between
good and evil. Rather, action no longer deposits a result that determines a
future Citta through pleasure, pain, merit, or demerit.

## The transition from IV.6

IV.6 and IV.7 form one argument:

```text
Dhyana-born Citta is without Asaya
                    |
                    v
its Karma deposits neither white nor black maturation
```

If Citta is no longer generated from a latent karmic store, its action also
ceases to replenish that store. Freedom concerns both directions of the
cognitive series:

```text
past deposit --X--> present Citta --X--> future colored deposit
```

This does not remove activity. It changes the Dharma of activity. Karma
remains action, but it no longer functions as a mechanism binding one
conditioned Citta to another through colored maturation.

## Fichte's `should`

Fichte asks his listeners to reflect upon the essence of the "should." At
first the should appears hypothetical:

> If this insight should arise, then such a construction must be posited.

Yet the should contains something more than an uncertain possibility. It is
an inner self-construction: an activity that posits and sustains itself from
within. If it depended upon an external ground, it would cease to be this
free should and become a mechanically imposed must.

```text
external must                 inner should

determined by another         posits itself
supported from outside        rests upon itself
conditioned result            living activity
```

Fichte describes the should as an inward, living, qualitative self-making. It
does not first exist as a thing and then perform an act. It is only through
its continuing self-postulation.

This is the decisive commentary on action beyond white and black.

## Fichte as Bhasya

Read as a Bhasya on IV.7, Fichte distinguishes action determined by an
external consequence from action whose principle lies wholly in its own
living self-determination.

White and black karma classify action through the determinate maturation it
produces. The pure should cannot be defined by such an externally awaited
result. Its actuality lies in the act itself.

| Yoga Sutra IV.7 | Fichte's lecture |
|---|---|
| karma of others is threefold | action conditioned by determinate empirical consequences |
| black and white | opposed values within the field of conditioned appearance |
| the yogin's karma is neither | action no longer grounded in either conditioned result |
| the yogin | the standpoint of inward self-determining activity |
| uncolored action | the should as pure qualitative self-making |

The yogin's karma is therefore not causally blank. It is intensely active,
but the action does not seek its principle in what will be returned to the
agent as a result.

The simple synthesis is:

> Action is free when it is determined by the truth of the act rather than by
> the conditioned identity the act will produce for its agent.

## Kant within the Sutra

The Kantian background should be stated plainly. An action is not free merely
because it happens to produce a good result. If the anticipated result is
what determines the will, the action remains conditioned. Even white karma
belongs to the mechanism of consequence.

```text
black karma       action for a destructive conditioned result
white karma       action for a beneficial conditioned result
mixed karma       action producing both
uncolored karma   action not determined by conditioned result
```

This does not make white and black equivalent. Their empirical and ethical
consequences differ. But neither, simply as a karmic color, supplies the form
of freedom. Freedom appears when action is no longer undertaken in order to
construct a future empirical self through its reward or punishment.

Fichte radicalizes Kant's point. The should bears the characteristics of
living being itself: it creates and supports its own actuality. It is not
merely obedience to a rule represented by consciousness. It is the immediate
ideal self-construction of being.

## Construction and subject matter

Fichte says that in the pure should, construction and subject matter,
ideality and reality, cannot be separated. This explains why the free act
does not need a colored result to validate it.

In ordinary action:

```text
intention now  ->  result later  ->  action receives its value
```

In the pure act:

```text
construction = subject matter
ideality      = reality
```

The meaning of the act is present in its performance. Nothing external must
be added to make it what it ought to be.

This also prevents an easy misunderstanding. "Neither white nor black" does
not mean that the yogin's action lacks determination. Fichte calls the should
the absolute determinateness of its essence. The action is free from karmic
color precisely because it is determined from within.

## Citta as Cit, Citi, Citta

The opening Dharma sequence can now be seen as an exposition of Citta's own
Dharma.

```text
Cit       the power of consciousness
Citi      consciousness actively determining and knowing
Citta     the determinate cognitive formation
```

This formulation remains provisional, but it illuminates IV.4-7:

- the many Cittas are determinate formations;
- Eka Citta is their synthetic cognitive unity;
- Dhyana makes the genesis of a Citta present rather than deposited;
- the action of that Citta no longer produces colored determination.

Citta is therefore not a container in which representations occur. It is
conscious power becoming determinate while maintaining the possibility of
freedom from its own deposited determinations.

## Kośa contact

The contact with Kośa is explicit here. Its karma analysis also recognizes
black, white, mixed, and neither-black-nor-white karma. The last is not merely
an especially pure member of the same series. It is `anasrava` and `avipaka`:
it opposes continued karmic activity and does not generate the maturation
defining the colored classes.

This confirms the transition from IV.6:

```text
anasaya Citta       without karmic deposit
       |
       v
asukla-akrsna Karma without colored maturation
```

Yoga states the practical form compactly. Kośa supplies the detailed analysis
of how intention, action, continuum, and maturation are classified. Fichte
asks for the rational ground of action that no longer receives its principle
from conditioned consequence.

## Does the comparison hold?

The comparison is strong if kept precise:

1. The yogin's action escapes the opposition of white and black maturation.
2. Fichte's should escapes determination by an external ground.
3. Neither is inactive or indeterminate.
4. Both locate determination within the living act itself.

The comparison would become excessive if Fichte's should were simply equated
with technically `anasrava` karma, or if neither-white-nor-black were taken to
mean that ordinary ethical distinctions are unreal. Yoga, Kośa, Kant, and
Fichte do not give identical accounts of action.

The shared rational structure is enough:

> Free activity is not determined by the conditioned result through which an
> empirical agent seeks to perpetuate itself.

## Provisional result

The plain translation is:

> The yogin's action is neither white nor black; the action of others is
> threefold.

Fichte's Bhasya is:

> The should is an inner, living self-construction. It is not supported by an
> external result but posits and sustains itself through its own activity.

The resulting Dharma thesis is:

> When Citta acts from its present self-knowing genesis, karma remains action
> but ceases to manufacture a future conditioned identity for the agent.

IV.7 therefore belongs to the definition of Citta itself. Citta is not free
by escaping action. It becomes free when its activity no longer returns as a
deposit that constructs what Citta must become next.
