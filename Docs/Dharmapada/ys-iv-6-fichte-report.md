# The Citta Born from Dhyana Has No Deposit

## A report on Yoga Sutra IV.6 and Fichte

## Purpose

This report tests Yoga Sutra IV.6 alongside the corresponding passage of
Fichte's sixteenth lecture in the 1804 *Science of Knowing*.

The previous Sutras established a series:

```text
IV.4  many determinate Cittas are constructed
IV.5  one Citta supplies unity through their differentiated activity
IV.6  among these, the Citta born from Dhyana has no karmic deposit
```

The question is now genetic. From what does a determinate cognition arise?
Does it issue automatically from an inherited disposition, or can Citta arise
through the free and conscious activity of Dhyana?

## The Sutra

`tatra dhyana-jam anasayam`

### A close translation

> Among these, that which is born from Dhyana has no deposit.

### A plain translation

> The Citta born from meditation carries no store of latent karma.

The Sanskrit is extremely compact:

- `tatra`: there, among these, or in this case;
- `dhyana-jam`: born or generated from Dhyana;
- `anasayam`: without an `asaya`, deposit, reservoir, underlying disposition,
  or karmic store.

The omitted subject is supplied by the preceding Sutras: Citta. IV.6
distinguishes one genesis of Citta from others. Its distinctive character is
not simply that it happens while someone meditates. It is generated from
Dhyana and therefore does not arise from the latent deposit that ordinarily
conditions cognition and action.

```text
ordinary genesis                 Dhyana-genesis

latent deposit -> new Citta      Dhyana -> new Citta
                  carries seed             no deposit
```

`Anasaya` should not initially be translated as "without contradiction" or
as an absolutely groundless state. It denies a deposited conditioning ground,
not every intelligible principle.

## Fichte's genetic question

Fichte now turns the preceding insight upon itself:

> Under what condition did this insight arise?

He had assumed, at least hypothetically, an ideal self-construction of being.
To assume it means to project it into the form of external existence without
yet possessing a ground for that projection. An irrational gap appears:

```text
living being   | gap |   factically projected construction
```

The projected construction initially looks like an independent fact. Yet
Fichte refuses to accept this factical appearance as absolute. He asks for
the genesis of the projection itself.

Once the supposedly independent fact is traced back to projection, its false
absoluteness is annulled. What remains is not a ready-made object but the
projection as an act:

```text
apparently given existence
          |
          | genetic reflection
          v
projection itself as act
```

This is the Fichtean hinge for `dhyana-jam`.

## Fichte as Bhasya

Read as a Bhasya on IV.6, Fichte distinguishes a cognition inherited as a
fact from a cognition whose genesis is performed and known as an act.

Ordinary consciousness encounters an object as simply there. Its genesis is
concealed, and it therefore carries an unexamined deposit of presupposition.
Philosophical Dhyana suspends this factical validity and regenerates the
cognition from its act.

| Yoga Sutra IV.6 | Fichte's lecture |
|---|---|
| `tatra`, among these Cittas | among the many ideal constructions of consciousness |
| `dhyana-jam`, born from Dhyana | insight generated by carrying out the reflective act |
| `anasayam`, without deposit | no inherited factical existence remains as an unexplained ground |

The Dhyana-born Citta does not merely receive a content deposited by a prior
cognition. It sees the act through which the content is projected. In this
sense its genesis is transparent.

The central formulation is:

> Dhyana does not inherit the object as a finished fact; it regenerates the
> act through which the object becomes present for Citta.

## Dhyana as sustained genetic attention

For this essay, Dhyana need not be inflated into a mysterious power. It is
the sustained activity through which cognition remains with its subject
matter long enough for the subject matter's genesis to become evident.

Ordinary understanding relies upon unnoticed assistance. It makes
distinctions successfully without always displaying their grounds. Fichte
acknowledges that this often works, but regards it as insufficient for
philosophy. Philosophy must not depend upon the happy accident that an
unexamined distinction happens to be understood.

This gives Dhyana a plain scientific office:

```text
ordinary understanding     uses an unnoticed inherited distinction

Dhyana                      sustains attention until the distinction's
                            generating ground becomes evident
```

The result is `anasaya` in a precise sense. The new cognition is not secretly
governed by an undisclosed deposit of assumptions. Its determining act has
been brought into view.

## Groundless appearance and grounded act

Fichte's discussion contains an apparent paradox. The projection first lacks
a principle, yet philosophy must provide its genetic principle. Does this
contradict `anasaya`?

No. Two senses of ground must be separated:

1. The projection has no higher ground as a finished external fact within the
   sphere of external existence.
2. The projection does have a principle when comprehended as an act within
   the genesis of knowing.

```text
as external fact      ungrounded and merely presupposed

as conscious act      genetically intelligible
```

Likewise, a Citta without `asaya` is not a Citta without intelligibility. It
is free from a latent deposit precisely because its genesis lies openly in
Dhyana rather than covertly in accumulated conditioning.

## The principle found in the conditioned

Fichte then makes a striking discovery. The factical projection was required
for the absolute insight to arise. The insight is conditioned by the very
projection whose principle it seeks. The principle is therefore discovered
in what it conditions.

```text
factical projection
        |
        | makes absolute insight possible
        v
absolute insight
        |
        | explains the necessity of the projection
        v
genetic comprehension
```

This is not a vicious circle if the second encounter with the projection is
different from the first. Initially it is accepted as a fact. Finally it is
comprehended as a necessary act within the production of insight.

Dhyana names the activity that sustains this return. It does not erase the
starting appearance; it transforms our relation to its genesis.

## A Kośa contact

The word `asaya` gives IV.6 an immediate practical importance. A cognitive
series normally carries dispositions forward. Earlier acts condition later
Cittas even when the conditioning is not manifest in the present content.

The Dhyana-born Citta interrupts this automatic inheritance. This need not
mean that all causal continuity is magically destroyed. It means that this
specific cognition is not generated as the maturation of a karmic deposit.
Its relevant generating ground is Dhyana.

That resonates with the Kośa distinction already important to the project:

```text
received or deposited determination
                versus
cognition defined by its present genesis in Samadhi
```

We should not equate the systems yet. The useful contact is methodological:
a cognition is most deeply characterized by how it is generated, not merely
by the object it happens to present.

## Does the comparison hold?

The comparison holds at the level of genesis:

1. Both texts distinguish among modes of cognitive production.
2. An ordinary appearance carries a concealed conditioning background.
3. A higher cognition is generated through an explicit contemplative act.
4. Its freedom consists in not being determined by the concealed deposit.

The comparison would become excessive if `asaya` were translated directly as
Fichte's irrational gap, or if Fichte's philosophical projection were treated
as a technical account of karma. `Asaya` belongs to Yoga's analysis of latent
conditioning. Fichte analyzes presupposition and the genesis of validity.

The Varttika joins them through a narrower claim: cognition becomes free when
its determining genesis is performed knowingly rather than inherited as an
unexamined fact.

## Provisional result

The plain translation is:

> Among the many Cittas, the one born from Dhyana has no latent deposit.

Fichte's Bhasya is:

> Genetic insight annuls the apparent independence of factical existence.
> What remains is projection understood as an act, whose principle is found
> within the movement of knowing itself.

The resulting Dharma thesis is:

> A cognition is free not because it has no genesis, but because its genesis
> is present in Dhyana rather than concealed in an inherited disposition.

This makes IV.6 the practical result of IV.4-5. The many Cittas need not be
abolished, and their synthetic unity need not be transferred to an external
witness. Dhyana generates a Citta in which the activity of cognition becomes
transparent to itself and ceases to be driven by its latent deposit.
