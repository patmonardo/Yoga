# Other Cognitions Arise in the Openings

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.27 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`tac-chidreṣu pratyayāntarāṇi saṃskārebhyaḥ`

### Simple translation

> In the openings of that movement, other cognitions arise from Saṃskāras.

The terms are exact:

- `tat`: that—the Citta inclined toward Viveka and bearing toward Kaivalya;
- `chidreṣu`: in its openings, gaps, fissures, or intervals;
- `pratyaya-antarāṇi`: other cognitions or alternative determinations;
- `saṃskārebhyaḥ`: from Saṃskāras.

IV.26 established Citta's new direction. IV.27 makes clear that direction is
not yet uninterrupted actuality.

```text
Viveka ────────► Kaivalya
          gap
           ↑
     another Pratyaya
       from Saṃskāra
```

## A gradient is not yet continuity

Prajñā-Saṃskāra has made discrimination the inclination of Citta. But Citta
contains countless other Saṃskāras accumulated through its history. These
formations do not instantly disappear when its governing direction changes.

```text
new governing gradient
Prajñā ──► Viveka ──► Kaivalya

older retained formations
Saṃskāra ──► alternative Pratyaya
```

The alternative cognition is not an alien force invading from outside. It
is Citta's own past actuality reappearing within its present movement.

## What is a `chidra`?

A `chidra` is not simply a chronological pause during which the Yogi forgets
to concentrate. It is a structural opening where the Principle has not yet
completely determined its field.

```text
Principle known                Principle fully actual
      |                               |
      └──── incomplete mediation ─────┘
                       ↑
                    chidra
```

Within that opening, a previous Saṃskāra can supply the determination that
Prajñā has not yet made continuous. Thus the old cognition momentarily
occupies the place of the new Principle.

This is Absolute Modality in process: necessity has become Citta's
direction, but contingency still appears in the gaps of its realization.

## `Pratyayāntara`: another holding-to-be-true

`Pratyaya` here is more than a stray thought. It is another determinate way
in which Citta holds something to be true.

```text
Prajñā's determination
“this formation is known through its Principle”

                 interrupted by

Saṃskāra's determination
“this familiar formation is immediately true”
```

The old cognition does not usually announce itself as a memory from the
past. Because Saṃskāra is the retained Being of a former act, it can return
with the appearance of present immediacy.

This is why Dharma as holding-to-be-true matters. IV.27 describes a conflict
between different established modes of holding.

## Saṃskāra is not merely stored content

If Saṃskāra were only a passive memory-image, it could not interrupt Viveka.
It is a formative disposition capable of producing a new Pratyaya.

```text
past cognition
      ↓ retained as
Saṃskāra
      ↓ activated in an opening
present Pratyaya
```

The arising Pratyaya can have a new object while retaining an old law of
construction. What returns is therefore not necessarily the same empirical
content. It may be the same way of organizing, appropriating, or holding the
content as true.

## Prajñā and the other Saṃskāras

Prajñā itself is Saṃskāra because it is the enduring Being of Knowing
established as Sattva. IV.27 therefore does not oppose a pure, formationless
Prajñā to Saṃskāra in general. It distinguishes their governing Principles.

```text
Prajñā-Saṃskāra                 other Saṃskāras

retains insight into           retain determinate products
the genesis and law            and modes of appropriation
of knowing

produces Viveka                produce other Pratyayas
```

The problem is not that formations exist. The problem is whether a
formation makes its own genesis transparent or conceals it.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya: do not force the next step

Fichte announces that a crucial unresolved term remains: “we know,” or “we
are knowing.” Yet he refuses to attack it immediately. He says he will wait
until the issue arises of itself.

This is methodologically important. A merely imposed solution would repeat
the hypothetical `should`:

```text
we decide what the ground must be
              ↓
we insert it into the Science
              ↓
the factical We remains the hidden ground
```

The Science must allow its own content to generate the problem and its
resolution. Otherwise transcendental knowing would still be governed by an
unexamined Saṃskāra of philosophical construction.

## Objective insight and the remaining opening

Fichte says that an objective insight has arisen. It is immediately
compelling, intrinsically determinate, and clear.

Its content is:

> Providing absolute self-genesis with a Principle yields absolute
> non-genesis—the Being of Knowing.

```text
absolute self-genesis
          +
its Principle
          ↓
absolute non-genesis
the enduring Being of Knowing
```

The insight is genuine. Nevertheless, the form in which *we perform* the
insight still contains an unexplained factical moment. This is its `chidra`.

```text
content of insight             form of insight

objectively compelling        “we” achieve and articulate it
categorically determinate     through an act still ungrounded
              \              /
               \            /
             unresolved opening
```

## Turning from form to content

Fichte now proposes a realistic move: turn from the form of the insight to
its content in order to explain the form from the content.

```text
not:

factical act of knowing ──► validates the content


but:

self-grounding content ──► explains the act and form of knowing
```

This movement is the Fichtean counterpart of restoring Viveka in the gap.
The opening cannot be closed by another arbitrary act of the empirical
knower. The content must demonstrate why its own form of cognition is
necessary.

## The realistic move

Calling this move `realistic` is significant. Fichte does not mean a return
to an independently existing object outside Knowing. He means that the
content, rather than our reflective performance, must become determinative.

```text
idealistic residue
we appear to construct truth

realistic completion
truth's content constructs the necessity of our insight
```

The Science of Knowing becomes adequate only when the Artha carries the
knowing of it. The factical knower ceases to be the unexplained support.

This gives `pratyayāntara` its deepest philosophical form: the alternative
cognition is the lingering belief that *our act* supplies the truth of the
Principle.

## Citta and the Science of Knowing

IV.27 sharpens the distinction we left open in IV.26.

```text
Science of Citta
discerns Pratyayas, Saṃskāras, and the openings in their continuity

Science of Knowing
must show how the content of truth generates and grounds
the very form in which Citta knows it
```

Citta is the complete articulated field in which this problem becomes
actual. Citi-Śakti is the power through which the field can know its own
Principle. The Science of Knowing is completed only when this power explains
both the content known and the form of its knowing.

## Absolute Modality

The modal structure is now clearer:

```text
necessity
Citta bears toward Kaivalya through Viveka

contingency
other Pratyayas arise in the openings from older Saṃskāras

actuality
the necessary Principle must comprehend and transform
the conditions producing those interruptions
```

The interruptions do not refute the necessity. They display what necessity
must still master in order to become complete actuality.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.27 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| openings remain in the movement of Viveka | an unexplained factical form remains within objective insight |
| other Pratyayas arise from Saṃskāras | the factical `we` can reappear as the assumed ground of Science |
| retained formations produce present cognition | adopted philosophical maxims can tacitly govern construction |
| Viveka must become continuous | the content must explain the form of insight |
| the gap cannot be closed by force | the unresolved problem must arise and resolve itself genetically |

## Provisional result

IV.27 says:

> Although Prajñā has oriented Citta toward Kaivalya, older Saṃskāras can
> still generate alternative ways of holding-to-be-true wherever the new
> Principle has not become continuous.

Fichte identifies the corresponding gap inside the Science of Knowing. Its
content is objectively compelling, but the form “we know this” has not yet
been derived from that content. If the factical `we` is allowed to supply
the ground, an older mode of construction has returned within
transcendental insight.

The remedy is not suppression. The Science turns from its reflective form
to its objective content so that the content itself can explain why and how
it is known.

```text
IV.26    Prajñā gives Citta the gradient toward Kaivalya

IV.27    Saṃskāras disclose the openings where that gradient
         has not yet become uninterrupted actuality
```

IV.28 can now determine how these interrupting formations are relinquished.
