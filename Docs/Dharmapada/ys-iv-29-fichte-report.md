# Dharma-Megha as Relative Necessity

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.29 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`prasaṃkhyāne 'py akusīdasya sarvathā viveka-khyāter dharma-meghaḥ samādhiḥ`

### Simple translation

> For one who has no investment even in complete discriminative knowledge,
> and whose discernment is continuous in every way, there is Dharma-Megha
> Samādhi.

The terms establish a conditional necessity:

- `prasaṃkhyāne api`: even regarding complete discrimination or the highest
  discriminative knowledge;
- `akusīdasya`: of one who has no investment, gain-seeking attachment, or
  wish to appropriate its result;
- `sarvathā viveka-khyāteḥ`: when discriminative vision is complete and
  continuous in every respect;
- `dharma-meghaḥ samādhiḥ`: the Samādhi called the Cloud of Dharma.

```text
no investment even in discriminative knowledge
                         +
continuous Viveka in every respect
                         ↓
              Dharma-Megha Samādhi
```

This is not an accidental reward added to Viveka. Given the complete
conditions, Dharma-Megha follows necessarily.

## Deep Modality

IV.26–28 presented the first modal movement:

```text
IV.26    necessity becomes Citta's inclination
IV.27    contingency appears in its openings
IV.28    contingent cognition is returned to its ground
```

IV.29 enters Deep Modality. The question is now whether the achieved Science
itself will become an object of appropriation.

```text
Viveka knows the conditioned formation
                       ↓
Ahaṃkāra may appropriate Viveka
                       ↓
“I possess the highest knowledge”
```

`Akusīda` prevents this final investment. Even Prajñā is not held as private
capital belonging to a factical knower.

## The saintly term is secondary to Knowing

`Dharma-Megha Samādhi` is exalted saintly vocabulary, shared with the larger
Indian contemplative field in which the Kośa also operates. But Yoga places
it inside a rigorous development of Knowing.

The Samādhi does not arrive because a special personality has accumulated
spiritual prestige. It follows from two cognitive determinations:

```text
Viveka is uninterrupted
        +
its result is not appropriated
        ↓
Knowing is no longer obstructed
by either error or ownership of truth
```

The saintly state is therefore secondary to the Science. It names the
actuality of Knowing once its conditions have been fulfilled.

## `Prasaṃkhyāna` and the last possible investment

`Prasaṃkhyāna` is complete discriminative contemplation. It can distinguish
the entire field of Citta and its Principles. Yet even this achievement can
be converted into an object:

```text
Prajñā knows the structure
            ↓ appropriated by Ahaṃkāra
“my insight, my attainment, my power”
```

This would preserve an unexplained factical `I` at the center of Absolute
Knowing. The content might be true while its mode of possession contradicted
that truth.

`Akusīda` means that Knowing does not settle into this possession. It takes
no profit even from the highest result.

## `Sarvathā viveka-khyāti`

IV.27 showed other Pratyayas arising in the openings of Viveka. IV.29 says
`sarvathā`: discrimination has become continuous in every way.

```text
earlier

Viveka ───── gap ───── Viveka
              ↑
      Saṃskāra produces
      another Pratyaya


now

Viveka ─────────────────────►
the whole field is transparent to its Principle
```

Continuity does not mean monotonous repetition of a single thought. It means
that no determination can establish itself outside the Science of its
genesis.

## Why a Cloud of Dharma?

Dharma is the holding through which an Artha has Being and validity. The
cloud image suggests not a single isolated truth but a saturated field from
which Dharma pours without private possession.

```text
not:

one knower stores a treasury of true propositions


but:

the complete field of Knowing is saturated with its Principle
                         ↓
Dharma determines every arising Artha
```

The cloud does not own the rain. In the same way, the Scientist of Knowing
does not appropriate the Dharma manifested through the Science.

This is why `akusīda` and `dharma-megha` belong together.

## Relative necessity

Dharma-Megha is necessary relative to its stated conditions:

```text
IF
Viveka is complete and uninterrupted
AND
there is no investment even in its highest result

THEN
Dharma-Megha Samādhi necessarily follows
```

This necessity is stronger than probability or spiritual encouragement. But
it remains relative because the conditions are still explicitly posited.

Deep Modality must therefore ask:

> What makes the conditions themselves necessary expressions of Knowing?

Fichte's passage begins exactly this derivation.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya: from factical insight to its ground

Fichte reviews the result already reached:

> Giving absolute self-genesis a Principle produces non-genesis—the Being
> of Knowing.

The insight has been seen, methodically reflected upon, and justified as a
fact. It is possible and actual in Knowing because it is possible and actual
in us, and we are Knowing.

But the last statement remains underived.

```text
factical justification
we have the insight; therefore it is possible in Knowing

genetic justification still required
the content must show why this insight necessarily knows itself
```

Fichte now investigates the self-presenting content in order to convert
factical certainty into genetic necessity.

## What remains after abstraction

The insight originally related two subordinate terms: self-genesis and the
Being of Knowing. Since these terms remained hypothetical, Fichte abstracts
from them.

What remains is:

> certainty pure and as such.

```text
self-genesis ── relation ── Being of Knowing
      ↓ abstract from the hypothetical terms

              pure certainty
```

Pure certainty is not certainty about a particular object. It is the inner
certainty through which anything can be held as true at all.

This is Fichte's path into Dharma as the pure power of holding-to-be-true.

## Certainty lies in itself

The material `what` of a cognition cannot ground its certainty. A content
may explain what is held, but not why its holding has the form of certainty.

Fichte therefore concludes:

> Certainty lies unconditionally in and through itself.

```text
particular “what”
supplies determinate content

pure certainty
supplies the self-grounding form of holding-to-be-true
```

Certainty is not assembled from the sum of possible predicates. Like Being,
it is closed into itself and is the condition and support of every `what`.

## Kant and the support of every `what`

Fichte credits Kant with establishing that Being is not a predicate derived
from the sum of all possible realities. Being is the condition and support
of every determinate `what`.

But Fichte says Kant left this second determination factical. The Science of
Knowing must derive it genetically.

```text
Kant
Being is not another predicate among predicates

Fichte's remaining task
derive how self-enclosed Being becomes
the condition and support of every determinate `what`
```

This is also the task of Dharmapāda. Citi-Śakti must be shown not merely as
an occult light beyond Citta, but as the power that supports every Dharma and
every Artha within Citta.

## Light and Being meet as certainty

Fichte says that Light had previously been grasped as absolute qualitative
oneness, still almost an occult quality. Pure certainty now supplies the
property through which Light immediately shows itself to be one with Being.

```text
Light                         Being
living Knowing               self-enclosed actuality
       \                     /
        \                   /
             pure certainty
```

Certainty is Citi-Śakti understood as the self-grounding power of Knowing.
It does not merely illuminate a determination; it holds the determination in
the form of truth.

## Certainty as unshakable persistence

Fichte asks how pure certainty can be described. His answer is:

> an unshakable continuance and resting in the same unchangeable oneness.

This is remarkably close to the function attributed to Vajropama Samādhi:
the certainty is diamond-like because its qualitative oneness cannot be
altered by the changing contents passing through it.

The comparison is ours; Fichte does not use the Buddhist term. But the
structural correspondence is clear:

```text
changing determinate contents
              ↓
certainty persists in one qualitative form
              ↓
Knowing is not displaced by what it knows
```

Trust in this unshakable certainty is not faith in Fichte's personality. It
is confidence that the Principle can demonstrate its invariance through its
own performance.

## Quality as negation of changeability

Certainty persists in the same `what`, but it is indifferent to which
particular content that `what` may be. Only the pure form of qualitative
oneness matters.

Fichte therefore derives Quality as:

> the absolute negation of changeability and multiplicity as such.

```text
changeable manifold
        ↓ negated as ground
unchangeable qualitative oneness
        ↓
form of pure certainty
```

This does not abolish multiplicity. By negating changeability as the ground
of certainty, it simultaneously posits the manifold as what is supported by
unchangeable Quality.

The relation begins to derive Appearance from Being.

## “We are what we say”

Fichte then tests the description of certainty. Could its construction ever
be performed differently? However many times it is repeated, certainty must
be constructed as persistence in the same qualitative oneness.

Consequently:

> We are what we say, and we say what we are.

```text
the Science describes certainty
                  =
certainty persists through the Science's act of description
```

The content and the performance coincide. Knowing does not merely represent
certainty as an external object; certainty demonstrates itself as the
unchangeable form of the knowing act.

This is the slate-cleaning force of `Buddhi-eva`: when Knowing is seen purely
as Knowing, no foreign substrate is required to carry its certainty.

## Certainty as the immanent `I` or `We`

Fichte now says that certainty, grounded completely in itself, is absolute,
immanent, and self-enclosed. In itself it is `I` or `We`.

This cannot mean the empirical personality. Absolute certainty is
inaccessible and hidden precisely because it never goes outside itself. The
moment it is objectified and spoken about, it appears in a form that is not
its absolute immanence.

```text
absolute certainty
self-enclosed immanent I / We
             ↓ expression
objectified certainty
Appearance of what never becomes an external object
```

The contradiction between immanence and its manifestation is not dismissed.
It becomes the next object of the Science: Appearance must be deduced from
the self-enclosed Principle.

## Certainty projects itself

Fichte finally identifies certainty as immediately living
`principle-providing`. Light is projection and intuition with inner
necessity.

It projects nothing other than itself:

```text
certainty / living Light
          ↓ provides itself a Principle
inner qualitative self-projection
          ↓ necessarily expresses itself
intuition of one self-grounded process
```

The projection is not a special secondary act. It follows immediately from
the life of certainty. Knowing's description of certainty is therefore
certainty's own necessary expression within Knowing.

Here relative necessity begins to deepen into immanent necessity. The
condition no longer merely stands outside the result; the living Principle
produces its own condition and expression.

## Bhavāgra and the Scientist of Knowing

Within our comparative system, the Scientist of Knowing has reached the
limit at which every determinate appearance is seen from the standpoint of
its Principle—the function we associate with Bhavāgra. This is not a claim
that Fichte adopted the Buddhist cosmological term or reported a visionary
journey.

The structural claim is exact:

```text
all determinate “whats” are set aside
                 ↓
pure certainty remains as their condition and support
                 ↓
Knowing encounters its own highest immanent form
```

Dharma-Megha is what follows when even this standpoint is not appropriated
as an achievement of the factical scientist.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.29 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| Viveka is continuous in every respect | certainty persists unchangeably in one qualitative form |
| no investment remains even in the highest knowledge | abstraction removes every particular `what` and the factical ownership of insight |
| Dharma-Megha follows necessarily under these conditions | pure certainty is grounded unconditionally in itself |
| Dharma saturates the complete field | certainty is the condition and support of every `what` |
| Samādhi is secondary to the Science of Knowing | the state follows from the self-expression of living Light |
| relative necessity begins Deep Modality | factical certainty begins its conversion into genetic necessity |

## Provisional result

IV.29 says:

> When Viveka has become continuous and even complete discriminative
> knowledge is no longer appropriated, Knowing necessarily enters
> Dharma-Megha Samādhi.

This is relative necessity because the result follows from explicitly stated
conditions. Fichte then begins deriving the deeper necessity of those
conditions. Abstracting from every determinate content leaves pure certainty,
which is grounded in itself, persists as unchangeable qualitative oneness,
and supports every possible `what`.

Certainty is the meeting of Light and Being—Citi-Śakti as the self-grounding
power to hold Artha as true. Its manifestation is not externally added. The
living Principle necessarily projects and describes itself.

```text
IV.26–28
necessity masters contingency within Citta
                    ↓
IV.29
Knowing relinquishes ownership of its own highest result
                    ↓
pure certainty begins to disclose the immanent necessity
of Dharma-Megha
```

IV.30 can now state what necessarily ceases from this Samādhi.
