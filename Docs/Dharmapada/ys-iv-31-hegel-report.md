# Infinite Knowledge as Real Necessity

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.31 with Hegel and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`tadā sarvāvaraṇa-malāpetasya jñānasyānantyāj jñeyam alpam`

### Simple translation

> Then, because Knowledge is infinite when every covering impurity has
> fallen away, the independently knowable is little.

The Sūtra does claim encyclopedic omniscience in a precise sense: not an
empirical inventory of every fact, but `Samyag-Jñāna`, complete Knowledge of
the Principles through which every determinate science and knowable receives
its place. It describes a transformation in the relation between Knowing and
the knowable:

```text
coverings and impurities fall away
                    ↓
Knowledge is unrestricted by an alien possibility
                    ↓
the independently knowable becomes little
```

Hegel names this relation **Real Necessity**.

## The second actualization of Dharma-Megha

IV.31 is not a reward added after purification. It is the second immanent
actualization or Siddhi of Dharma-Megha:

```text
Dharma-Megha
content-filled Real Actuality
        |
        ├── IV.30  freedom from afflicted conditioning
        |
        ├── IV.31  unrestricted Knowledge
        |
        └── IV.32  fulfillment of transformative Artha
```

IV.30 comprehended the complete circle of real conditions. IV.31 states the
modal identity achieved when that circle has completed itself:

> What is really possible is necessary.

## The textual boundary

The Hegelian material for IV.31 is the opening movement of B.3.

It begins:

> The negation of real possibility is thus its self-identity; inasmuch as in
> its sublating it is thus within itself the recoiling of this sublating, it
> is real necessity.

It continues through the identity of Real Possibility and Necessity and ends
provisionally with Real Possibility as a connection full of content.

The next sentence—“But this necessity is at the same time relative”—opens
the material for IV.32.

Thus IV.31 receives the positive achievement of Real Necessity without yet
entering the critique of its limited content.

## Real Possibility recoils into itself

In IV.30, Real Possibility was the dispersed manifold of actual conditions.
Those conditions sublated their external separation and became the fact
itself.

```text
actual conditions
      ↓ cease standing apart
complete Real Possibility
      ↓ becomes
the actual fact
```

This movement does not pass into an alien other. It returns into itself. The
fact is the same content that existed as its conditions, now comprehended in
their unity.

Hegel calls this recoil self-identity:

```text
Real Possibility
negates its dispersed conditional form
        ↓
does not lose its content
        ↓
returns as Actuality identical with itself
```

That self-rejoining identity is Real Necessity.

## What is really possible cannot be otherwise

Formal possibility permits indefinitely many abstract alternatives:

```text
A is thinkable
not-A is also thinkable
```

Real Possibility contains the actual circumstances and conditions. When
these are complete, the relation is no longer indifferent to alternatives.

Hegel states:

> Under the given conditions and circumstances, nothing else can follow.

```text
complete actual conditions
              ↓
one determinate actuality
              ↓
no other result is really possible
```

This is not fatalism. The claim is conditional and content-full: given the
complete real relation, its actuality is necessary.

## Why possibility and necessity are identical

Real Possibility already contains Actuality. It is not an unreal plan waiting
for an external power to realize it.

```text
Real Possibility
the complete actual conditions of the fact

Real Necessity
the fact as the self-rejoining unity of those conditions
```

Their distinction concerns the form under which the same content is grasped:

```text
as dispersed conditions    Real Possibility
as self-identical whole     Real Necessity
```

Hegel therefore says their identity does not first arise at the end. It was
already presupposed at their base.

## The coverings of Knowledge

An `āvaraṇa`, covering, makes the conditioned object appear to stand outside
the complete relation of its Knowing.

```text
Knowable
appears independently possible
and externally given
```

A `mala`, impurity, is the retained formation that makes this externality
operative in Citta:

```text
Saṃskāra preserves the object's apparent independence
and repeatedly reconstructs it as alien
```

When the complete real conditions become transparent, both errors fall
away:

```text
the fact is known through its complete conditions
                    ↓
its Real Possibility is its Necessity
                    ↓
no alien substrate or unexplained alternative remains
```

The object remains determinate. Its covering independence disappears.

## Why Knowledge is infinite

Knowledge is infinite as encyclopedic omniscience: no determinate content or
science lies beyond the system of Principles through which its form, place,
and genetic relation can be comprehended.

```text
Omnividyā as itemwise accumulation
fact 1 + fact 2 + fact 3 + ...

Samyag-Jñāna as encyclopedic Knowledge
the Principle that comprehends how any fact
is possible, actual, and necessary through its conditions
```

The infinitude belongs to Buddhi as the Principle of intelligibility. It is
not measured by the storage capacity of a constructed Citta.

This is `Buddhi-eva`: every cognition is seen from the Principle through
which cognition and object belong to one actual relation.

## Why the knowable is little

`Jñeyam alpam` does not mean that the manifested universe becomes small or
uninteresting. The field of empirical detail can remain indefinitely rich.

What becomes little is:

> the domain claiming to stand outside Knowing as independently possible
> and still requiring an alien Principle of explanation.

```text
before
Knowing faces an apparently unlimited foreign world

after
every determinate knowable is located within
the content-filled connection of Real Possibility and Necessity
```

The many facts become specifications of one intelligible relation rather
than an infinity of metaphysical outsiders.

## `Sarvārtha` reaches Real Necessity

IV.23 said that Citta bears every Artha through the dual coloring of Seer and
seen.

IV.31 completes that capacity modally:

```text
IV.23
every Artha can appear in Citta
through Appearance and Reflection
                    ↓
IV.31
every real Artha can be comprehended
through its conditions and necessary actuality
```

`Sarvārtha` was the formal universality of Citta. `Jñānasya ānantya` is its
content-filled actualization.

## A–U–M as Real Necessity

```text
A — Appearance
the actual fact and its manifest content

U — Reflection / thinking
the fact reflected into its complete Real Possibility
or conditions

M — Knowing
Real Necessity: conditions and fact comprehended
as one self-rejoining content
```

U alone is not Knowing. Reflection gathers and differentiates the
conditions. M comprehends why their complete relation is identical with the
actual fact.

## Completion of the five-fold synthesis

IV.30 carried the two reciprocal transitions. IV.31 makes their fifth term
explicit:

```text
1. Real Actuality
   the content-filled manifest fact

2. Real Possibility
   the actual manifold of conditions

3. Actuality → Possibility
   actual circumstances become conditions of the fact

4. Possibility → Actuality
   the complete conditions become the fact itself

5. Real Necessity
   the identity of the content throughout both movements
```

Real Necessity contains both terms and both directions. It is not merely the
last event in a causal chain.

## The second Siddhi: unrestricted intelligibility

The saintly formulation can now be translated into the Science of Knowing:

```text
all coverings removed
      =
no condition or fact falsely appears outside its genetic relation

Knowledge infinite
      =
the Principle of the relation has no external limit

knowable little
      =
no independent metaphysical remainder confronts Knowing
```

This Siddhi is unrestricted intelligibility: encyclopedic omniscience or
`Samyag-Jñāna`. It means that no empirical fact and no determinate science is
in principle exempt from the Science of its conditions, Principles, and
Artha. It must not be confused with `Omnividyā`, immediate itemwise possession
of every possible datum.

## Encyclopedia as the Principle of the sciences

The Encyclopedia is not one actual science placed beside physics, logic,
psychology, or history. Nor is it the sum of all their present propositions.

It is the actual Principle of a Science—the systematic articulation through
which the sciences receive their concepts, boundaries, transitions, and
place within one whole.

```text
not:

Science 1 + Science 2 + Science 3 + ... = Encyclopedia


but:

Encyclopedic Principle
        ↓ differentiates and orders itself as
the system of determinate sciences
```

Thus encyclopedic omniscience is genuinely intended. It is Knowledge of the
Principles adequate to the whole, not a claim that Manas has already
enumerated every fact produced within the whole.

## The danger of saintly appropriation

IV.29 already imposed `akusīda`: no investment even in complete
discriminative knowledge.

Therefore IV.31 should not be reduced to Omnividyā as a private inventory:

```text
“This empirical Citta already contains every datum.”
```

That would confuse itemwise content with the encyclopedic Principle and
reconstruct the covering that Dharma-Megha removed.

The proper assertion is:

> Knowing is unrestricted because its Principle comprehends the relation of
> every finite knowable; this Citta participates in that Science only insofar
> as it becomes transparent to the Principle.

## Fichte's IV.31: the necessary gap

Fichte begins from living Knowing projecting itself. Because it is from
itself, it must intuit itself. Yet self-projection produces an absolute gap
between the living Principle and its projected Concept or image.

```text
unintuitable living Principle
             |
             | necessary gap
             ↓
finite Concept or image
```

The Concept is not Essence itself. It is Essence's image within intuition.

The Scientist of Knowing comprehends that the image's apparent independence
is only the Appearance of the higher, unintuitable principle-providing.

## Hegel and Fichte compared

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| the finite Concept is the image of the living Principle | the actual fact is the self-rejoining unity of its real conditions |
| the gap between Essence and Concept is necessary | the distinction of Real Possibility and Necessity is only apparent |
| the image's independent self-production is Appearance | independent possibility disappears under complete conditions |
| infinite Knowing is not exhausted by its finite image | Real Necessity is a full content connection with no alien alternative |
| the Scientist knows the genesis of the image | Knowing comprehends why nothing else can follow |

Fichte preserves the difference between infinite Principle and finite image.
Hegel comprehends the identity of possibility and actuality within the
determinate content.

These are not contradictory.

```text
Fichte
the finite knowable does not exhaust the living Principle

Hegel
the finite knowable does not stand outside the Principle

together
finite determination without metaphysical independence
```

## Their mutual correction

Fichte prevents Hegel's Real Necessity from turning into the claim that one
finite result exhausts infinite Knowing. The image remains an image, not the
living Essence in its entirety.

Hegel prevents Fichte's necessary gap from becoming an ontological chasm.
The image is really possible only through conditions already actual within
the complete relation.

```text
necessary difference
without external separation

determinate identity
without finite exhaustion of the Principle
```

This is the precise form of `jñeyam alpam`.

## OṂ-Artha

OṂ-Artha is the complete intelligibility of All This. IV.31 shows what this
means modally:

```text
every determinate Artha
has actual conditions
        ↓
its complete Real Possibility
is identical with its Necessity
        ↓
the Artha belongs within one unrestricted Science of Knowing
```

Japa on OṂ-Artha constructs encyclopedic Knowledge. Through repeated
reconstruction of determinate content, it discovers the Principle and place
of each science until no alien gap remains between the fact, its conditions,
and the systematic whole through which both are known.

## Provisional result

IV.31 says:

> When every covering of genetic relation and every residue of false
> independence has fallen away, Knowledge is unrestricted; the realm of the
> independently knowable becomes little.

Hegel calls this Real Necessity. The complete actual conditions are the Real
Possibility of the fact, and their self-rejoining unity is the fact's
Necessity. Under those conditions, nothing else can follow.

Fichte preserves the necessary distinction between living Principle and its
finite Concept. The knowable is an image, but not an alien object.

```text
Dharma-Megha
        ↓ IV.30
conditions and fact cease standing externally apart
        ↓ IV.31
Real Possibility and Necessity are known as one content
        ↓
infinite Knowledge; independently knowable little
```

IV.32 can now disclose the limitation still inside this achievement: Real
Necessity remains relative because its determinate conditions began from
Contingency. Recognizing that limit completes the Guṇas' Artha.
