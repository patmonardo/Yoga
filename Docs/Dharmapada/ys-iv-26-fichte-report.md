# Citta Inclines toward Kaivalya

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.26 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`tadā viveka-nimnaṃ kaivalya-prāgbhāraṃ cittam`

### Simple translation

> Then Citta slopes toward discrimination and bears forward into Kaivalya.

The image is dynamic:

- `tadā`: then, after the construction of selfhood has ceased;
- `viveka-nimnam`: inclined, descending, or flowing toward discrimination;
- `kaivalya-prāgbhāram`: weighted, oriented, or borne forward toward
  Kaivalya;
- `cittam`: Citta itself.

Citta is not killed. Its direction changes.

```text
before IV.25                    after IV.25

Citta flows toward             Citta flows toward
appropriation                  discrimination
“I am this”                    “this is this”
        ↓                              ↓
Ahaṃkāra                       Kaivalya
```

## From the Absolute to Absolute Modality

IV.22–25 have supplied the exposition of the Absolute:

```text
IV.22    Buddhi knows itself

IV.23    Citta bears every Artha through its dual coloring

IV.24    its composite activity works for a higher Artha

IV.25    the composite ceases to construct itself as Ātman
```

This is the Science of Citta, and therefore substantially the Science of
Knowing. Yet the two expressions are not simply interchangeable.

```text
Science of Citta
the articulated actuality of knowing

Science of Knowing
Citta together with the Principle and genesis
of its articulated actuality
```

IV.26 crosses into Absolute Modality. The question is no longer merely what
the Absolute contains or how it is exposed through Citta. The question is
how this complete actuality determines its own movement.

## What `nimna` says

`Nimna` evokes the downward slope along which water naturally flows. Once
discrimination is established, Viveka is not an externally imposed rule
that Citta must repeatedly force itself to obey. It has become Citta's own
gradient.

```text
Viveka as external instruction
“Citta should discriminate”
                 ↓ becomes actual
Viveka as immanent inclination
“Citta flows through discrimination”
```

This is already modal. A mere possibility has become an actual tendency.
The structure of Citta itself now carries the movement forward.

## What `prāgbhāra` says

`Prāgbhāra` intensifies the image. Citta does not merely face Kaivalya as a
distant goal. Its entire weight bears in that direction.

```text
Viveka                     Kaivalya
the slope                  the direction of the weight
     \                     /
      \                   /
       transformed Citta
```

Kaivalya is therefore present first as directionality. It has not yet been
described as a completed state. It is the telos immanent in the now
discriminating activity of Citta.

## Not another act of suppression

IV.26 does not say that a Yogi continually attacks Citta until it stops.
That would leave the old division intact:

```text
controlling subject  versus  dangerous Citta
```

Instead, the insight of IV.25 has transformed the Principle of Citta's
motion. Its Vṛttis can continue, but they no longer necessarily return to
the appropriation “I am this.”

```text
appearance
    ↓
Manas determines it
    ↓
Buddhi discriminates its Principle
    ↓
Citta moves toward Kaivalya
```

The liberation of Citta is accomplished through its intelligibility.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya: the remaining fact

Fichte now identifies a decisive unresolved term within the proof completed
so far:

> We know, or we are knowing.

That proposition is immediately intelligible. But, Fichte says, its
Principle has by no means yet become clear.

```text
already established

providing self-genesis with a Principle
yields non-genesis, the Being of Knowing

still ungrounded

“we know” / “we are knowing”
```

This is exactly why IV.26 describes an inclination rather than completed
Kaivalya. The Science has exposed the structure of Knowing, but the factical
`we` performing the insight still remains within it as an unexplained term.

## The final residue of Ahaṃkāra

The statement “we are knowing” can be read at two levels:

```text
factical reading
we, these determinate knowers, perform the Science

pāramārthika reading
Knowing is actual and knows its own Principle
```

If the first is silently treated as the ground of the second, Ahaṃkāra has
returned at the foundation of transcendental science. The factical `we`
would claim to produce Absolute insight.

Fichte does not yet solve the problem. He isolates it as perhaps the most
important remaining part of the investigation.

This is the modal pressure of IV.26:

> Knowing has become oriented toward a state in which its actuality no
> longer depends upon an unexplained factical knower.

## From the hypothetical `should` to the slope

In IV.24, Fichte found a transcendental `should`: given the Principle, the
connection should follow. The Science still appeared to depend upon maxims
adopted by a free `we`.

IV.26 transforms that structure:

```text
hypothetical modality
“we should apply the Principle”
                 ↓
actual modality
the Principle gives Citta its own direction
                 ↓
absolute modality
the movement must justify the knowing in which it occurs
```

`Viveka-nimna` is not simply an ethical command. It is the immanent tendency
of knowing once its construction has become transparent.

## Prajñā as the new gradient

Prajñā-Saṃskāra now has a precise function. It is not one more formation
competing with innumerable Vāsanās. It changes the direction in which the
whole field operates.

```text
innumerable Vāsanās
condition particular movements of Citta

Prajñā-Saṃskāra
conditions the direction of the complete movement
                    ↓
              viveka-nimna
```

Prajñā is the Being of Knowing established as Sattva. Because it has become
Saṃskāra, discrimination is no longer a momentary insight. It has become the
enduring inclination of Citta.

This is how the pāramārthika Principle becomes actual within a determinate
cognitive history without being reduced to that history.

## The modality of Kaivalya

At IV.26, Kaivalya has three modal determinations:

```text
possibility
Citta can discriminate its construction

actuality
Prajñā has established discrimination as Citta's inclination

necessity
the complete movement bears toward Kaivalya
```

Necessity here is not an external fate pushing Citta. It is the weight of
its own comprehended actuality.

Kaivalya is therefore not mere isolation from the world. It is the
completion toward which Knowing moves when its own Principle, rather than
Ahaṃkāra's appropriations, governs its activity.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.26 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| Citta inclines toward Viveka | the Science continues to penetrate the Principle of its insight |
| Citta bears toward Kaivalya | the remaining investigation is determined by an unresolved foundational term |
| the movement has become immanent | the fact “we are knowing” must be grounded from within Knowing |
| Prajñā becomes Citta's enduring direction | genetic insight must cease to depend on an unexplained factical `we` |
| Kaivalya first appears as modal necessity | the Science must complete its self-justification |

## Provisional result

IV.26 says:

> Once false self-construction ceases, Prajñā becomes the gradient of Citta:
> it flows through discrimination and bears toward Kaivalya.

Fichte identifies the corresponding unfinished task. The Being of Knowing
has been derived, but the apparently immediate fact that *we are knowing*
has not yet been genetically grounded.

This prevents us from announcing Kaivalya too soon. The Absolute has been
exposed through the Science of Citta; Absolute Modality must now show how
Knowing's movement arises necessarily from its own Principle.

```text
exposition of the Absolute
what the complete Science of Citta is
                  ↓
Absolute Modality
how Knowing necessarily determines its own course
                  ↓
viveka-nimnaṃ kaivalya-prāgbhāraṃ cittam
```

IV.27 can therefore reveal what still appears in the openings of this
movement when older Saṃskāras interrupt its new direction.
