# One Citta Through Many Activities

## A report on Yoga Sutra IV.5 and Fichte

## Purpose

This report tests Yoga Sutra IV.5 alongside the corresponding conclusion of
Fichte's sixteenth lecture in the 1804 *Science of Knowing*.

IV.4 introduced a plurality of constructed Cittas arising through the bare
form of I-am-ness. These should not be pictured as fantastic creatures or as
a collection of independently manufactured minds. They are the many
determinate constructions or moments in which cognitive activity appears.

IV.5 now asks what makes this differentiated plurality one intelligible
activity.

## The Sutra

`pravrtti-bhede prayojakam cittam ekam anekesam`

### A close translation

> In the differentiation of activities, one Citta directs the many.

### A translation emphasizing the cognitive series

> Although its activities differentiate, one Citta carries the many Cittas
> forward.

The first translation is lexically safer. The second makes explicit the
connection to IV.4 and should be treated as commentary rather than as a
replacement for the Sanskrit.

- `pravrtti-bhede`: when activity differentiates, or within the distinction
  of activities;
- `prayojakam`: impelling, directing, putting into operation;
- `cittam ekam`: one Citta;
- `anekesam`: of the many.

The Sutra contains both sides at once:

```text
one Citta                  many differentiated activities
    |                       /    |    |    \
    +--------------------> p1   p2   p3   p4
            directs
```

The one is not merely one more item alongside the many. It is their principle
of connection and directed movement.

## The problem inherited from IV.4

If we read `cittani` in IV.4 through the Abhidharma sense of successive and
conditioned cittas, the problem becomes exact:

```text
citta 1 -> citta 2 -> citta 3 -> citta 4
```

Each citta is determinate. Each arises and gives way to another. Yet the
series is not an accidental heap, because its moments exhibit continuity,
direction, recollection, cultivation, and consequence.

IV.5 names the unity within this difference. The activities are genuinely
many, but one Citta is effective through them. Unity does not abolish
succession; succession is how unity operates.

This reading avoids the image of a yogin creating several independent minds
and then controlling them from a privileged headquarters. The philosophical
question is simpler and deeper:

> How can the many acts of cognition constitute one developing cognition?

## Fichte's answer

Fichte begins from the hypothesis that being is ideally constructed. If that
construction occurs, it must occur through being's own immanent essence. The
ideal is therefore not attached to being from outside. It is posited
organically within essential being, without a real hiatus or disjunction.

This gives Fichte an absolute **that**:

> Being necessarily constructs itself ideally.

But it does not yet give him the **how**:

> We do not yet see how absolute essence constructs itself ideally.

This distinction is crucial. The unity of the process has been established,
but its differentiated operation has not yet been derived.

```text
THAT: one immanent genesis is necessary

HOW:  its differentiated activities remain to be exhibited
```

Fichte nevertheless insists that no real gap exists within essence. The gap
exists only for the philosophical "we," which has not yet regenerated the
absolute genesis within consciousness.

## Fichte as Bhasya

Read as a Bhasya on IV.5, Fichte identifies the one Citta with the immanent
unity of genesis and the many with its ideal differentiations in
consciousness.

| Yoga Sutra IV.5 | Fichte's lecture |
|---|---|
| `pravrtti-bhede`, differentiation of activities | the articulation of ideal construction |
| `anekesam`, of the many | the manifold determinate appearances of knowing |
| `cittam ekam`, one Citta | the uninterrupted immanence of living being |
| `prayojakam`, directing or putting into activity | the one genesis effective throughout its differentiations |

The one does not direct the many from outside. That would reintroduce the gap
Fichte has excluded. The one is their immanent direction: the same living
genesis acting in and as its differentiated appearances.

The structure is therefore not:

```text
master Citta  ->  externally controls  ->  subordinate Cittas
```

It is:

```text
one genesis
    |
    +-- differentiates as citta 1
    +-- differentiates as citta 2
    +-- differentiates as citta 3
    +-- remains one through the series
```

## Genesis of the genesis

Fichte gives a remarkable name to consciousness at this point: not absolute
and pure genesis itself, but the **genesis of the genesis**.

Absolute genesis is the living movement. Consciousness reconstructs that
movement and comes to know it as genesis. The second genesis does not produce
the first. It regenerates the first in the form of insight.

```text
first genesis       living being differentiates itself
       |
       v
second genesis      consciousness reconstructs that differentiation
```

This clarifies the relation between the one and the many Cittas. A particular
citta-moment does not contain the whole series as a finished object. Yet the
series can become conscious of its own law. The unity that was merely active
through the moments is regenerated as known unity.

The one Citta can thus be understood in two inseparable ways:

1. As the operative continuity immanent in the differentiated activities.
2. As that continuity becoming explicit for consciousness through a genesis
   of its genesis.

This distinction should remain provisional until IV.4 and IV.5 are reviewed
together. It may become necessary to distinguish Citta's operative unity from
Asmitā's formal "I am."

## The force of `prayojaka`

IV.3 denied that the external `nimitta` is `prayojaka`: the occasion does not
impel nature's powers. IV.5 now restores `prayojaka`, but in a different
place. The directing efficacy belongs to one Citta within the plurality.

```text
IV.3  external nimitta     is not prayojaka
IV.5  one immanent Citta   is prayojaka of the many
```

This is a decisive progression. Causal power is first removed from the
external occasion and then recognized as the immanent unity of differentiated
activity.

Fichte makes the same move. The philosophical We does not externally produce
being. The ideal construction proceeds directly from being's own immanent
essence. Direction belongs to the living process, not to an observer standing
outside it.

## A first Kośa contact

The Abhidharma analysis of many cittas makes the Sutra philosophically
ordinary rather than fantastic. Cognition is exhibited as a conditioned
series of determinate events. The difficulty is not how one magician controls
several artificial personalities. It is how continuity and cultivation are
possible when cognition is articulated as many cittas.

IV.5 answers without denying the many:

> Difference belongs to the activities; unity belongs to their direction.

This does not yet settle whether the one Citta is a numerically persistent
substance, a causal series, a governing form, or the recollected unity of
genesis. Those alternatives must not be collapsed prematurely. Fichte's
contribution is to require immanence: whatever the unity is, it cannot be a
foreign agent imposed upon the series.

## Does the comparison hold?

The comparison is strong because both passages distinguish unity of genesis
from plurality of operation:

1. The many activities remain genuinely differentiated.
2. Their connection cannot come from an external agent.
3. One immanent genesis is effective throughout them.
4. Consciousness must reproduce this genesis in order to know its unity.

The interpretation would become excessive if it claimed that Fichte teaches
the Abhidharma theory of momentary cittas, or that `cittam ekam` simply means
absolute being. The terms belong to different systems. The correspondence is
logical: a single immanent activity maintains itself through many determinate
expressions.

There is also a productive tension. An Abhidharma account may resist any
substantial one Citta behind the series. Fichte likewise cannot allow the one
to become an inert substrate. The unity must be the living law of genesis,
actual only in its self-differentiation and reconstruction.

## Provisional result

The simple translation is:

> In the differentiation of activities, one Citta directs the many.

Fichte's Bhasya is:

> The ideal differentiations of knowing do not fall outside living being.
> One immanent genesis is active throughout them, although consciousness must
> still regenerate this genesis in order to comprehend how it is one.

The resulting Dharma thesis is:

> One Citta is not another mind placed above the many. It is the immanent
> continuity by which differentiated acts belong to one cognitive genesis.

IV.4 and IV.5 must now be read together:

```text
IV.4  many constructed Cittas arise in the form of I-am-ness
IV.5  one Citta is immanently directive through their different activities
```

The joint problem is whether Asmitā supplies only the repeatable form of each
citta—"I am"—while one Citta names the deeper continuity that generates,
connects, and comprehends the entire series.
