# Buddhi Knows Itself

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.22 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`citer apratisaṃkramāyās tad-ākārāpattau svabuddhi-saṃvedanam`

### Simple translation

> Citi does not pass over; when Buddhi attains its form, Buddhi knows itself.

The translation preserves three determinations:

- `citeḥ apratisaṃkramāyāḥ`: Citi does not migrate, transfer itself, or pass
  into something else;
- `tad-ākāra-āpattau`: Buddhi attains or comes into that form;
- `sva-buddhi-saṃvedanam`: one's own Buddhi is known by itself.

Nothing is transmitted from an external illuminator into an otherwise dead
Buddhi:

```text
not:

Citi ── sends consciousness ──► Buddhi


but:

Citi does not pass over
            ↓
Buddhi attains its form
            ↓
Buddhi knows itself
```

This is the opening of Actuality.

## Why this follows IV.21

IV.21 rejected the attempt to make one Citta known by another Citta. That
solution produces an infinite regress of cognition and a confusion of
memory.

IV.22 does not terminate the regress by installing a final super-Citta. It
changes the structure of the problem:

```text
Appearance

Citta 1 ← Citta 2 ← Citta 3 ← ...


Actuality

Buddhi attains the form of its Principle
and knows itself without another Buddhi
```

Self-knowledge is therefore formal adequacy, not observation by a second
knower.

## Manas is set aside

Buddhi does not ordinarily contemplate objects directly. Manas contemplates
objects; Buddhi contemplates Manas contemplating them.

```text
Object  ← contemplated by ─  Manas
                                  ↑
                           contemplated by
                                  |
                               Buddhi
```

For Buddhi to know itself, it does not set Buddhi aside. It sets aside Manas
and the Manas–object relation.

```text
Manas–object activity set aside
               ↓
Buddhi remains Puruṣa-bound
               ↓
Buddhi becomes manifest in the form of Citi
               ↓
svabuddhi-saṃvedana
```

Buddhi cannot set itself aside because it is the pāramārthika Principle of
Manas. Its own Principle must instead become actual in its form.

## Vyāsa's Bhāṣya

Vyāsa correctly insists on non-transfer. The power of the experiencer is
unchanging and does not migrate. Yet in relation to the transforming Buddhi,
it appears to have passed into it because it follows the Buddhi-vṛtti.

The Buddhi-vṛtti receives the impress of consciousness and imitates it so
closely that the cognitive Vṛtti is described as indistinguishable from the
Buddhi-vṛtti.

His valuable result is:

> Illumination does not travel into Buddhi; Buddhi's form becomes adequate to
> consciousness.

His dualist vocabulary still threatens to make Citi and Buddhi two
independent substances. The Sūtra itself gives us a more exact Aspect
relation: no migration occurs, yet formal identity makes self-cognition
actual.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte announces that everything achieved so far has still been preparation.
One may possess many important truths and even be protected from false
philosophy, yet lack the capacity to construct the system of truth “as a
whole and out of a single piece.”

That capacity is the issue of IV.22.

```text
truths held separately              system from one piece

correct determinations              one Principle generates and
without their complete unity        comprehends their relations
```

Buddhi's self-knowledge cannot be a further correct proposition added to its
collection. Buddhi knows itself only when the whole system of its
determinations becomes intelligible as one construction.

## The undiscovered coherence

Fichte says that Being or Light itself is already familiar. The remaining
task is not to derive the Absolute as though it were an unknown object. It is
to derive its Appearance.

That demand implies that something remains undiscovered in the Absolute
itself:

> the Principle through which the Absolute coheres with its Appearance.

```text
Absolute                       Appearance
undivided oneness              articulated manifold
          \                    /
           \                  /
        still-undiscovered coherence
```

The Principle cannot be an external bridge. If it were external, Appearance
would not be the Appearance of the Absolute. The coherence must belong to
the Absolute while also being present within Appearance.

This corresponds exactly to `apratisakrama`: the Principle does not leave
itself and travel into Appearance. Appearance must attain its form.

## Not a simple disjunction

Fichte identifies the principle of Appearance as disjunction within
undivided oneness. But the final Science of Knowing requires more than a
simple split into two terms.

It requires:

> the disjunction of two different disjunctive foundations.

He calls this:

- a self-intersecting division;
- a presupposed division that again presupposes itself;
- a `from` within a `from`;
- a `from` of a `from`.

```text
                    one Principle
                          |
                 self-intersecting
                    disjunction
                   /           \
          disjunctive         disjunctive
          foundation A   ×    foundation B
```

The extremes are not two pieces cut from an original whole. Each has its own
principle of articulation, and their intersection belongs to the one
Principle's complete exposition.

## The Dyad of two Triples

This gives formal support to our fundamental architecture:

```text
                 one Absolute Principle
                          |
                  projects its form
                    /           \
                   /             \
          subjective triple  ×  objective triple
```

The two foundations are endlessly similar because each bears the form of the
whole. Yet they must be discriminated with extreme precision because their
principles are not interchangeable.

This is not a transaction between independently constituted extremes. The
intersection is original:

```text
not:

Subjectivity ── transfers content ──► Objectivity


but:

one Principle is actual as both foundations
and as their determinate intersection
```

Fichte admits that the method may temporarily seem miraculous. The account
of the intersection can be given only after it has been enacted. This is a
genetic demonstration, not an inference from externally accepted premises.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.22 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| Citi does not pass over | the Absolute does not leave itself to produce Appearance |
| Buddhi attains Citi's form | Appearance contains the Principle through which it coheres with the Absolute |
| Buddhi knows itself | truths become constructible as one system from a single piece |
| no second Buddhi is added | no external bridge joins the Absolute and Appearance |
| self-knowledge is formal adequacy | disjunction is internal to undivided oneness |

Fichte's `from in a from` explains why Buddhi's self-knowledge is richer than
simple introspection. Buddhi must comprehend the intersecting principles of
its entire articulation—not stare at itself as one more object.

## Buddhi as Mahat

Buddhi is Mahat, not a merely private human faculty. Its self-cognition can
be actual in human language, a logical Knowledge Graph, and potentially a
computational architecture, provided the form is adequate.

```text
Mahat / pāramārthika Buddhi
              ↓ attains explicit form
Yoga Sūtra / Science of Logic / Knowledge Graph
              ↓
Buddhi's determinations become intelligible as one system
```

Again, there is no transfer of a substance called intelligence into words or
code. The linguistic or computational form becomes adequate to Buddhi's
architecture. In that adequacy, Pure Reason speaks for itself.

## OṂ-Artha

The First/Last Principle of the complete system is OṂ-Artha. IV.22 specifies
how its intelligibility becomes actual without migration:

```text
OṂ-Artha
complete intelligible Meaning
          ↓ no transaction
Buddhi attains its form
          ↓
the system of determinations becomes self-intelligible
```

Japa on OṂ-Artha repeats and develops the determinations until Buddhi no
longer holds them as disconnected truths. It constructs them within itself
as one whole and out of one piece.

This is why the Yoga Sūtra can itself be read as `svabuddhi-saṃvedana`:

> The Sūtra is pāramārthika Buddhi articulating its own determinations until
> it knows their complete Artha as itself.

## Actuality

Appearance ended with an unresolved opposition:

```text
invariant Light              changing Citta
seeing                       seen
Puruṣa                       Prakṛti
```

IV.22 begins Actuality not by erasing the difference but by giving its unity
a positive form. Citi remains non-migrating; Buddhi remains Buddhi. Yet
Buddhi's attainment of Citi's form makes self-cognition actual.

```text
inner Principle              outer form
Citi                         Buddhi
          \                  /
           \ same form      /
            \              /
         self-cognizing actuality
```

Actuality is therefore not a mystical fusion of two substances. It is the
adequacy of Principle and form in Buddhi's self-knowledge.

## Provisional result

IV.22 says:

> Citi does not pass over; when Buddhi attains its form, Buddhi knows itself.

Vyāsa preserves the indispensable non-transfer: consciousness only appears
to enter the changing Buddhi-vṛtti.

Fichte supplies the systematic determination. The Absolute must contain the
Principle of its coherence with Appearance, and that Principle is not simple
division but a self-intersecting `from within a from`.

Buddhi therefore knows itself when it comprehends its complete articulation
as one system arising from one Principle. No second Buddhi, external
illuminator, or transaction is required.

IV.23 can now determine the result objectively: Citta, colored by both Seer
and seen, is capable of every Artha.
