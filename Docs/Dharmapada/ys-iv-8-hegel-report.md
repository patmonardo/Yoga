# Congruent Manifestation and Determinate Ground

## Yoga Sutra IV.8 with Hegel

`tatas tad-vipaka-anugunanam eva-abhivyakti vasananam`

> From that, only those Vāsanās congruent with the resulting maturation become
> manifest.

Fichte emphasized conditional manifestation: appearance is not arbitrary but
must agree with its ground. Hegel now gives this relation the form of
**determinate ground**.

A ground has determinate content. The grounded is not produced from an empty
universal; ground and grounded possess one content in different forms. Their
formal mediation explains `anuguna`, congruence.

```text
Vāsanā as ground-content
          |
          | formal mediation under suitable Vipāka
          v
the same content manifested as disposition
```

`Abhivyakti` is manifestation rather than creation because the content of the
ground and the grounded is identical. Yet only the Vāsanā whose content fits
the determinate result can occupy this ground-relation.

Hegel also exposes the limitation of a merely formal explanation. To say that
a disposition manifests because its ground has the same content can become
tautological: this appears because this is what was disposed to appear. The
real conditions of manifestation must therefore be exhibited. That movement
leads from formal ground toward condition in IV.9.

The Hegelian Varttika is:

> A Vāsanā manifests when its latent content and the determinate field of
> maturation form one ground-relation. Congruence is the identity of content
> maintained through the difference between latency and appearance.
