# Citta as Reflection

## Hegel on Yoga Sutra IV.2-10

The nine Sutras traverse the complete movement of Reflection:

| Sutra | Citta-Dharma | Hegelian determination |
|---|---|---|
| IV.2 | transformation from nature's fullness | essential and unessential |
| IV.3 | the external occasion lacks productive power | shine |
| IV.4 | determinate Cittas arise through I-am-ness | reflection |
| IV.5 | Eka Citta is one through differentiated activity | identity and difference |
| IV.6 | Dhyana-born Citta carries no latent deposit | contradiction returned to ground |
| IV.7 | the yogin acts beyond karmic coloration | absolute ground |
| IV.8 | congruent Vāsanā becomes manifest | determinate ground |
| IV.9 | one form continues as Samskara and Smrti | condition |
| IV.10 | the reproductive wish has no first moment | absolutely unconditioned |

The overall movement is:

```text
Cit      negative power of self-relation
  |
  v
Citi     reflection actively positing and recollecting determination
  |
  v
Citta    determinate reflection appearing as a cognitive formation
```

Hegel's central contribution is to prevent every term from becoming a
separate substrate:

- Prakrti is not inert matter behind its forms.
- Nimitta is not an independent external producer.
- Asmita is not a little substance manufacturing minds.
- Eka Citta is not a master-mind or an external Purusa.
- Samskara is not an object stored in a metaphysical warehouse.
- Vasana is not an eternal entity merely because its series is beginningless.

Citta is Reflection: the movement by which determination is posited, retained,
manifested, opposed, recollected, and returned to ground while the movement
remains one within its differences.

This also clarifies the relation between Fichte and Hegel:

```text
Fichte    reconstructs the genesis of appearance from living knowing

Hegel     shows why every opposition within that genesis must transform
          itself into a moment of one reflective movement
```

IV.2-10 do not destroy Citta. They make its conditioned operations
intelligible. IV.11 states the consequence with unusual precision: Vāsanās
exist only as collected through cause, result, support, and object. Their
absence when those supports are absent completes Reflection without harming
Eka Citta. The completed analysis is in
[`ys-iv-11-fichte-hegel-synthesis.md`](./ys-iv-11-fichte-hegel-synthesis.md).
