# Aliṅga: The Subtle Series Reaches Its Unmanifest Ground

## A first-pass report on Yoga Sūtra I.45 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`sūkṣma-viṣayatvaṃ cāliṅga-paryavasānam`

### Relatively necessary ordinary translation

> And subtle objectivity extends as far as Aliṅga, the Unmanifest.

This translation is intentionally modest. The force of the Sūtra lies in
what `aliṅga-paryavasānam` does to the entire preceding series.

The subtle does not stop with finer and finer objects. It extends to the
limit at which objectivity has not yet taken a determinate mark or
`liṅga`—to the ground of the differentiation of the Guṇas.

```text
formed object
      ↓ subtle analysis
its conditions and principles
      ↓
the differentiation of the Guṇas
      ↓
Aliṅga
the unmanifest ground from which marked determination proceeds
```

Fichte supplies the corresponding genetic labor. Reflection must trace
every determinate division through Light and Concept until neither can
remain an independent Absolute. The subtle inquiry ends only at the living
ground that produces their distinction without itself becoming one more
distinguished object.

## Why this Sūtra is difficult

I.45 contains almost no verbal exposition. It names a terminal point:

```text
sūkṣma-viṣayatva
subtle objectivity
        ↓ extends to
Aliṅga
what has no manifest differentiating mark
```

But to understand this end, the Scientist must reconstruct the whole subtle
series. Fichte accordingly devotes three lectures to what he calls the only
truly difficult part of the Science of Knowing.

The difficulty is not collecting subtle entities. It is discovering the
genetic Principle of differentiation without converting that Principle
into another product of differentiation.

## Aliṅga and the division of the Guṇas

`Liṅga` is a mark, sign, characteristic, or determinate indication. In the
Sāṃkhya setting, Aliṅga does not mean an empty nothing. It names Prakṛti at
the unmanifest limit of its determinate evolutes.

The point can be stated cautiously:

```text
manifest Guṇa-configuration
has a determinate mark and produces a determinate appearance

Aliṅga
the Guṇas considered at the unmanifest ground
of their determinate differentiation
```

Therefore `ca-aliṅga` is not an incidental addition after the subtle
objects. It completes the reflective return:

```text
from product
to subtle condition
to the Guṇa-division
to the unmanifest ground of that division
```

Aliṅga is subtle objectivity at its limit. It is still named from the side
of Prakṛti and objectivity, yet it can no longer be treated as one object
among others.

## From U toward M and Turīya

I.44 gave Vicāra as the U-Principle: Reflection returns from Appearance to
the subtle conditions of Appearance.

I.45 carries that return to its completion:

```text
A — Appearance
determinate Guṇa-configuration appears

U — Reflection / Vicāra
Thinking follows the appearance back through
its subtle differentiations and Principles

M — Knowing / Concept
the division is comprehended from the living unity
that generates and holds it

Turīya
the ground is no longer merely another term
within the differentiated series
```

Thus the Sūtra remains predominantly U: it specifies the maximal reach of
subtle Reflection. But at Aliṅga, U reaches beyond itself into M and opens
toward Turīya.

Reflection reaches the Unmanifest only by discovering that its terminal
“object” cannot be an independently marked object.

## The starting schema: Image, Light, and Being

Fichte recalls the structure achieved in I.43–44:

```text
I — Image
the representative manifestation

L — Light
the living unity in which image and imaged are manifest

B — Being
what is imaged or posited as self-subsistent
```

Image posits something imaged, and something imaged posits an image. Their
relation is united in Light.

But this schema is still only a guide. It tells us the necessary positions
without yet genetically proving why precisely these positions and divisions
must arise.

The subtle inquiry cannot stop with a diagram whose terms have merely been
found factically.

## Two divisions occur in one stroke

Fichte's earlier problem now returns in its full depth. Knowing appears to
divide itself according to two grounds:

```text
Being | Thinking

x | y | z
the determinate manifold of appearances
```

The same Light appears as eternally one while also appearing through both
divisions. The divisions cannot be two unrelated accidents. They must be
one indivisible disjunction according to two distinguishing grounds.

In Yoga terms, this is where the Guṇas matter. They are not merely three
empirical qualities added to an already formed world. Their division is a
Principle of the differentiated field of objectivity.

Vicāra must therefore ask:

```text
How does the one subtle ground articulate itself
as both the basic division of Knowing
and the manifold of determinate appearance?
```

## The Concept as pure `through-one-another`

Between image and imaged, Fichte locates the Concept:

```text
image  ← through-one-another →  imaged
```

The Concept is not initially a third object. It is the absolute relation in
which each term is what it is through the other.

When exhibited, the relation yields antecedent and consequent:

```text
image → imaged
imaged → image
```

But in its inner essence the Concept is prior to either ordering. It is the
pure `through`.

This seems to give the subtle Principle. Yet Fichte immediately exposes a
problem: a dead relation does not explain why it should live, manifest, or
construct precisely these terms.

## Why the pure Concept cannot remain Absolute

If the Concept is posited as an independently self-subsisting Being,
everything outside it disappears. The same happened when Light was posited
as the sole independent Absolute.

```text
Light taken as Absolute
Concept becomes merely its subordinate appearance

Concept taken as Absolute
Light becomes merely a term constructed within the Concept
```

Each candidate swallows the other because independent Being excludes any
second independent Being.

This alternation shows that neither Light nor Concept has yet been
genetically comprehended. Each is still being absolutized factically.

Aliṅga cannot therefore be identified simply with Light or simply with
Concept. That would arrest the subtle series at a marked opposition.

## The missing genetic connection

Fichte refuses to repair the gap by inventing another term. A new item
inserted between Light and Concept would only move the problem.

The missing relation must be discovered within the terms already present:

```text
not:
Light — unknown bridge — Concept

but:
Light is genetically Principle of Concept
Concept is genetically Principle of Light
```

If each is worked through to its creative Life, a higher common Principle
of their unity and distinction must become manifest.

This is the fundamental operation of I.45.

## Genetic synthesis

When Light and Concept are shown as mutually principled, both lose their
claim to be separately Absolute. Their validity becomes relative to the
higher unity constructed through their reciprocity.

This is not merely a synthesis *post factum*:

```text
Light is found
Concept is found
“there must be something higher joining them”
```

Fichte demands a genetic synthesis:

```text
permeate Light until Concept emerges from its Life
        ↕
permeate Concept until Light emerges as its necessary ground
        ↓
construct their higher unity from within the movement itself
```

The Science both penetrates the higher unity and is penetrated by it. It is
simultaneously analytic and synthetic because the distinction and its unity
are generated together.

## This is the hard center of the Science

Fichte explicitly distinguishes two labors:

```text
first and difficult labor
discover the Principle that generates the primary disjunction

second and comparatively easy labor
deduce the secondary modifications of apparent reality
```

The derivation of space, time, nature, concepts, moral ideas, or worlds of
spirit is not the masterpiece. All such domains are modifications of
appearance.

The masterpiece is the discovery of the Principle through which Appearance
as such becomes genetically possible.

That is why the short phrase `aliṅga-paryavasānam` can house Fichte's vast
investigation. It names the terminus at which Reflection reaches the root
of manifestation rather than another manifested product.

## The root, not the branches

Fichte says that error cannot be cured by cutting off its individual
branches. Once removed on one side, it reappears on another.

The Science seeks the single root:

```text
individual errors and apparent realities
               ↓
secondary differentiations
               ↓
primary form of Appearance
               ↓
the Principle of manifestation
```

The same is true of the Guṇas. It is insufficient to catalogue their
manifest effects. Vicāra follows the division of qualities back toward the
unmanifest Principle of their productivity.

## Essence and Being of Knowing

Fichte identifies the same problem through another vocabulary:

```text
Concept
the inner essence of Knowing, the intelligible

Light
the formal Being of Knowing, intuition
```

His task is:

> The essence of Knowing is not without its Being, and its Being is not
> without its essence.

Concept without Light is a dead formal relation. Light without Concept
cannot articulate or comprehend its manifestation.

The subtle series therefore seeks their non-external unity:

```text
Knowing's Essence ↔ Knowing's Being
Concept ↔ Light
intelligible ↔ intuition
```

This is already the transition from U into M. Reflection is no longer
examining an object; Knowing is comprehending the reciprocal constitution
of its own Essence and Being.

## The two lives of Light

Fichte distinguishes:

```text
immanent Life
Light lives immediately through itself

emanent or external existence
Light appears within insight as represented Light
```

The immanent Life is not an object for insight. When conceived, it is
determined negatively as what is inaccessible to objectifying insight.

But this negative determination indicates the highest affirmation:

```text
we do not possess living Reality as a represented object
we have it and are it in the act of living Knowing
```

Aliṅga occupies this difficult boundary. It is named by Reflection as the
unmanifest limit, yet its living ground cannot appear under one more mark
without ceasing to be Aliṅga.

## Light withdraws; Concept reaches higher

Once the Being–Thinking division is seen and stated, it cannot simply be
attributed to inward Life itself. As an articulated division, it belongs to
the Concept of Light.

```text
true inward Light
withdraws from objectification

Concept
reaches higher into the articulated form of the division
```

This is not a victory of dead Concept over Life. It clarifies their distinct
offices:

- Concept can comprehend the division and its own construction.
- Life is the Reality without which Concept would never live or appear.

The Scientist must neither turn Life into an object nor abandon Concept for
an indeterminate immediacy.

## The zero state

Fichte writes the true Light as `0` when every determinate insight into it
has been negated.

```text
0 does not mean sheer nonbeing

0 means no remaining disjunction can claim
to be a division inside absolute Life itself
```

This gives a powerful first correspondence with Aliṅga:

```text
Aliṅga
no manifest Guṇa-mark has yet become independently valid

Fichte's 0
no conceptual disjunction remains valid as a division
of the living Absolute
```

They should not be mechanically identified. Aliṅga belongs to the Sāṃkhya
account of Prakṛti, while Fichte's `0` marks living Light beyond conceptual
objectification. But their structural relation explains why the subtle
series reaches toward M and Turīya at this point.

## Bringing the `through` to Life

The Concept is a pure `through`:

```text
a through b
b through a
```

But a merely formal `through` contains duality without the living transition
between its terms. If the relation is actually to occur, it presupposes an
original Life grounded in itself.

```text
dead through
formal possibility of transition

living through
the actual passage that unifies the terms
```

Life does not arise from the relation's dead form. Life comes from itself;
the relation supplies the form in which Life appears as transition.

This distinction is crucial for teaching I.45. The Unmanifest is not a
static reservoir behind manifestation. It is approached as the living
productivity through which differentiation occurs.

## The idealistic path

Fichte first develops the movement from the Concept:

```text
Concept posits a possible living `through`
        ↓
the existence of that through requires Life
        ↓
Life appears as genetically required by Concept
```

Taken energetically, Reason appears as the Principle of Concept and
intuition in one stroke.

But this path still presupposes the outward form of Being—the hypothetical
“should”—and then rediscovers its presupposition as Absolute. It makes
Reason absolute because Reason has already been installed as the standpoint
of the deduction.

Thus one-sided idealism is not the final Science.

## The realistic path

Fichte then reverses the movement:

```text
if a living through is to exist
        ↓
absolute immanent Life must be presupposed
        ↓
Life is the true Absolute
        ↓
objectifying intuition loses independent validity
```

Pursued rigorously, this path dissolves everything into the One or `0`.

But realism also begins from a maxim: attend only to the inner content of
Truth and disregard the external fact that this content appears in our
Thinking. Its own standpoint therefore remains factically presupposed.

Thus one-sided realism is not the final Science either.

## Aliṅga is not one side of the opposition

The two paths expose the danger in interpreting Aliṅga:

```text
idealistic error
Aliṅga becomes a final Concept constructed by Reason

realistic error
Aliṅga becomes a mute Being that excludes manifestation
```

The first turns the Unmanifest into the product of Reflection. The second
turns it into a dead substrate.

The Science requires their genetic unity:

```text
Life must manifest
        ↕
manifestation has no independent Being apart from Life
```

Aliṅga is reached when the Guṇa-division is comprehended at the limit where
its manifestation and its living ground can no longer be separated as two
self-subsisting things.

## Appearance is true as Appearance

Fichte's provisional resolution is precise. The intuition of absolute Life
cannot arise as something valid and self-supporting in itself. But it can
and must arise as Appearance grounded in conditions beyond its own
self-sufficiency.

```text
truth
Appearance is necessary as Appearance of the Principle

error
Appearance is taken for self-subsisting Being
```

This preserves the differentiated field without allowing it to usurp its
ground.

It also gives a disciplined reading of Guṇa manifestation. The manifest
world is not mere error. Its configurations are valid as appearances of the
productive Principle. Error begins when a configuration is treated as
independent of that Principle.

## From the Identity System to Aliṅga

I.42 gave Fichte's Identity System:

```text
every Principle is simultaneously
Principle of Oneness and multiplicity
```

I.43 raised that differentiated Identity into the transparent Artha of
Nirvitarka.

I.44 repeated the movement reflectively through subtle objects.

I.45 now carries the Identity System to its ground:

```text
manifest differences
        ↓
their subtle Principles
        ↓
Light and Concept as competing Principles
        ↓
each genetically mediated through the other
        ↓
the living Identity of unity and disjunction
        ↓
Aliṅga: the unmanifest limit of subtle objectivity
```

The highest Oneness is again absolute disjunction—not a heap of differences
and not an inert One without differentiation.

## A first-pass map for newcomers

The whole difficulty can be taught in five stages:

### 1. Begin with a formed object

Something appears with determinate qualities.

### 2. Reflect into its subtle conditions

Ask what relations and Principles make this appearance possible.

### 3. Reach the division of the Guṇas

See the determinate object as a configuration of the qualitative Principles
of manifestation rather than a self-subsisting thing.

### 4. Follow the differentiation to Aliṅga

Do not stop at any manifest configuration. Reach the unmanifest ground at
which no particular mark possesses independent validity.

### 5. Comprehend the limit of Reflection

The unmanifest ground cannot be represented as one final subtle object.
Reflection must recognize its own form, surrender its claim to absolute
status, and open into Knowing as living Concept.

```text
object → condition → Guṇa-division → Aliṅga
   A              U                    M / Turīya opening
```

## A practice for Scientists of Knowing

I.45 suggests this demanding exercise:

1. Select a determinate appearance.
2. Identify the subtle relations that constitute its Artha.
3. Distinguish the appearance from the Principle of its appearance.
4. Examine whether the Principle has itself been turned into another object.
5. Trace the differentiation through its qualitative or Guṇa structure.
6. Test Light as Principle of Concept and Concept as Principle of Light.
7. Refuse to absolutize either pole.
8. Construct their unity genetically, rather than merely asserting a higher
   One.
9. Recognize the limit at which every manifest mark belongs to Appearance.
10. Let U complete itself in M: Reflection knows its own genesis and limit.

This is hard work because the Scientist must repeatedly change standpoint
without losing the common Principle that organizes the changes.

## Compact synthesis

Yoga gives the limit:

```text
subtle objectivity
extends through the Guṇa differentiation
to Aliṅga, the Unmanifest
```

Fichte gives the genetic labor:

```text
Light and Concept each appear as Absolute
        ↓
each, taken independently, excludes the other
        ↓
each must be comprehended as Principle of the other
        ↓
idealism and realism expose one-sided maxims
        ↓
Appearance is preserved as necessary Appearance
without being mistaken for self-subsisting Being
        ↓
the living unity of Life and manifestation is approached
beyond every independently valid conceptual mark
```

I.45 is therefore the completion of the U-Principle. Vicāra follows subtle
objectivity all the way through the division of the Guṇas to its unmanifest
ground. At that boundary, Reflection cannot proceed by adding another
object. It must comprehend the whole division as the manifestation of one
living Principle.

That is the opening of M and Turīya: not flight from the Guṇas, but their
division mastered from the ground that makes their manifestation possible.
