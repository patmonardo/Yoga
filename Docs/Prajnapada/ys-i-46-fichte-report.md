# Sabīja Samādhi: The Completion of Seeded Knowing

## A report on Yoga Sūtra I.46 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`tā eva sabījaḥ samādhiḥ`

### Relatively necessary ordinary translation

> These very Samāpattis are Samādhi with seed.

The Sūtra is a boundary statement. It does not introduce another member of
the preceding series. It gathers the whole series and determines its status:

```text
Savitarka
Nirvitarka
Savicāra
Nirvicāra extending to Aliṅga
        ↓
these very ones
        ↓
Sabīja Samādhi
```

Even when subtle objectivity reaches Aliṅga, the Samādhi remains seeded.
The decisive transition to Nirbīja therefore still lies ahead.

## Why Fichte says almost nothing here

In the source text, I.46 receives only the Sūtra itself. The next lecture
begins immediately under I.47.

That brevity is philosophically appropriate. I.46 is not a new
construction. It is the retrospective determination of everything achieved
in I.41–45.

The vast Fichtean development attached to I.45 has carried subtle
Reflection to its furthest limit. I.46 now says:

> However profound that construction may be, it still possesses a seed.

The work is complete as one sphere, but the Science is not complete.

## What is the seed?

The seed should not be reduced to a little psychic object stored somewhere
inside Citta. At this level, the seed is the determining Principle through
which a whole field of cognition can arise.

```text
seed
the retained ground of determinability
from which a determinate Samāpatti can be generated
```

Each preceding Samāpatti retains such a ground:

- Savitarka retains the mixed determination of Word, Artha, and Knowledge.
- Nirvitarka retains Artha as the determinate content that shines.
- Savicāra retains the subtle Principle under reflective consideration.
- Nirvicāra retains subtle objectivity through its limit at Aliṅga.

The form becomes increasingly transparent, but a determinate field remains
generated and held.

## Sabīja does not mean impure or defective

Seeded Samādhi is not false cognition. It includes extraordinary degrees of
purification and genetic comprehension.

```text
ordinary cognition
is governed unknowingly by inherited determinations

Sabīja Samādhi
masters the determinate seed and comprehends
the field generated through it
```

The limitation is exact: Knowing is still Knowing *of* a determinable
content or Principle. Even when the object is Aliṅga, the relation remains
oriented by the seed of subtle objectivity.

Sabīja is accomplished conditioned Knowing, not failed transcendence.

## The full seeded sequence

The four Samāpattis form one systematic development.

### 1. Savitarka

```text
Śabda — Artha — Jñāna
present together in conceptual mixture
```

The seed is articulated determination as given through Word, memory, and
Knowledge.

### 2. Nirvitarka

```text
memory and representative form become transparent
        ↓
Artha alone shines
```

The mixture is purified, but Artha remains the determinate seed.

### 3. Savicāra

```text
Reflection returns from formed Artha
to its subtle relations and Principles
```

The seed is no longer merely a presented object. It is the subtle ground
considered through Reflection.

### 4. Nirvicāra

```text
Reflection comprehends its own objectification
        ↓
the subtle Principle shines through it
        ↓
subtle objectivity extends to Aliṅga
```

The reflective form becomes transparent, but the terminus is still the
unmanifest ground of objective differentiation.

### The verdict of I.46

```text
tā eva — these very ones
        ↓
all belong to one sphere
        ↓
Sabīja Samādhi
```

## Why Aliṅga is still a seed

Aliṅga has no manifest differentiating mark, but it is still approached as
the terminus of `sūkṣma-viṣayatva`—subtle objectivity.

This gives the crucial distinction:

```text
unmanifest objectivity
is not the same as freedom from the seed of objectivity
```

Aliṅga is the ground of the Guṇa-division before a particular marked
configuration is manifest. It therefore contains the productivity of
determination, even though no individual gross determination is foregrounded.

```text
manifest seed
a determinate Guṇa-configuration

Aliṅga as seed
the unmanifest ground of Guṇa-differentiation itself
```

The seed has become universal and maximally subtle. It has not yet been
removed as seed.

## The Fichtean seed: the retained absolute standpoint

Fichte's I.45 investigation repeatedly discovers a term that claims to be
the Absolute:

```text
Light as Absolute
        ↕
Concept as Absolute
        ↕
immanent Life as Absolute
        ↕
Reason as Absolute
```

Each standpoint generates a coherent field. Each can explain its products
from the Principle it presupposes. But each retains a factical root: it has
made one standpoint absolute without yet generating the standpoint itself.

That unmastered presupposition is structurally a seed.

```text
given Principle
        ↓ genetic development
complete field of consequences
```

The consequences may be rigorously generated, yet the initial Principle is
still held as the basis of the construction. The Science must therefore
rise to the genesis of the supposed highest Principle itself.

## Idealism and realism as two seeds

Fichte exposes two opposing maxims.

### Idealistic seed

```text
begin from the energy of Reflection
        ↓
make Reason the genetic Principle
        ↓
derive intuition and Being through it
```

The derivation is consistent, but Reflection has installed itself as the
absolute starting point.

### Realistic seed

```text
begin from self-subsistent immanent Life
        ↓
make content alone absolutely valid
        ↓
deny independent validity to Reflection
```

This too is consistent, but it begins from the maxim that the content alone
must count.

Both standpoints are factical at their root. Each contains an ungenerated
decision about what is to count as Absolute.

```text
idealism retains the seed of Reflection
realism retains the seed of immanent content
```

The Science can move between them and comprehend their genesis. Therefore
it is already higher than either, but it has not yet entirely lost itself
in the highest genesis.

## Sabīja as the sphere of determinate Principle

The decisive point can be expressed generally:

```text
wherever Knowing stands in a determinate Principle
and constructs a field through that Principle,
there is seed
```

This remains true even when the Principle is extremely subtle, universal,
or called Absolute.

The seed is not necessarily an empirical residue. It can be a metaphysical
presupposition, a reflective maxim, a distinction, or the pure standpoint
from which a science generates its field.

Sabīja is therefore the entire Science of conditioned determinability
brought to perfection.

## A–U–M completed within Sabīja

The sequence now gives a complete A–U–M construction:

```text
A — Appearance / Vitarka
Śabda–Artha–Jñāna become explicit and Artha shines

U — Reflection / Vicāra
Knowing returns through subtle Principles to Aliṅga

M — Conceptual comprehension
the whole field is held as one genetically articulated system
```

But M here is still the completion of the seeded sphere. The Concept
comprehends the determinate seed and its entire development.

```text
M completes A and U
        ↓
the complete A–U–M is recognized as Sabīja
        ↓
the possibility of Nirbīja opens
```

This is why I.46 both completes OṂ and marks the need to pass beyond the
seeded articulation of OṂ.

## The boundary between the Science of Knowing and Being qua Being

The preceding sequence is a Science of how a determinate field becomes
known:

```text
appearance
reflection into its subtle conditions
comprehension of its generative Principle
```

As long as a seed remains, Being is considered through the Principle that
determines and manifests it.

Nirbīja raises a different question:

```text
not:
What Being is generated through this seed?

but:
What is Being qua Being when even the seed
of its determinate manifestation is suspended?
```

I.46 is the frontier between these inquiries.

It does not yet state Nirbīja Samādhi; I.51 will name that culmination. But
by gathering every preceding form under Sabīja, it makes Nirbīja logically
necessary as the next sphere of the Science.

## Essence qua Essence and Being qua Being

The analogy with our Dharmapāda is exact and illuminating.

Absolute Reflection does not merely reflect another content. It makes
Reflection itself thematic:

```text
ordinary Reflection
Essence mediates Appearance

Absolute Reflection
Essence comprehends Essence qua Essence
```

Likewise, Nirbīja cannot merely offer a subtler seed:

```text
Sabīja
Being is known through a determining seed

Nirbīja
Being qua Being is approached without a retained seed
of determinate objectivity
```

Thus the move beyond Sabīja is not from “something” to a blank nothing. It
is from conditioned Being to the Science of Being as such.

## Seedlessness is not indeterminacy

One danger must be avoided. If all determination were simply discarded,
Nirbīja would amount to unconscious blankness. That would not be a Science
of Being.

The transition becomes possible only after the entire seeded structure has
been mastered:

```text
not knowing less

but knowing the complete genesis of determination
so thoroughly that no determinate seed
needs to be retained as an external support
```

The road to Nirbīja passes through maximal discrimination, not around it.

This is why the complex labor of I.41–45 is indispensable. Seedlessness
without mastery of the seed would be mere absence. Seedlessness after that
mastery is freedom from dependence upon the seed.

## The completed sphere

I.46 allows the first six Sūtras of this Prajñāpāda sequence to be seen as
one whole:

```text
I.40  mastery of attention from smallest to greatest
I.41  transparent Citta as the Samāpatti midpoint
I.42  Savitarka: Word–Artha–Knowledge in mixture
I.43  Nirvitarka: Artha alone shining
I.44  the same purification as Savicāra–Nirvicāra
I.45  subtle objectivity extends to Aliṅga
I.46  all these are Sabīja Samādhi
```

I.46 closes the bracket. Nothing in the preceding development is rejected.
Every moment receives its systematic place as a form of seeded Samādhi.

## A teaching formulation for newcomers

The point can be taught simply:

1. A seed is a determining ground capable of generating a field of
   cognition.
2. Gross meditation retains an evident object as seed.
3. Subtle meditation follows that object back to increasingly universal
   Principles.
4. Aliṅga is the unmanifest ground of the differentiated objective field.
5. Because Aliṅga remains the terminus of subtle *objectivity*, it remains a
   seed.
6. Therefore every Samāpatti described so far is Sabīja.
7. Nirbīja must concern not another object but freedom from the determining
   seed as such.

The beginner need not master every detail of Fichte's idealism–realism
conflict to understand the structural result: a Principle remains a seed
whenever Knowing begins from it without yet generating the Principle's own
necessity.

## Compact synthesis

Yoga closes the seeded sphere:

```text
these very Samāpattis
from Savitarka through Aliṅga
        ↓
Sabīja Samādhi
```

Fichte explains why the seed persists:

```text
Knowing adopts a Principle
        ↓
the Principle generates a coherent field
        ↓
Reflection masters the field genetically
        ↓
but the Principle still contains a factical root
or retained standpoint
        ↓
the whole construction remains seeded
```

I.46 is therefore both completion and judgment. The entire Science of
determinate Samāpatti has been accomplished, from articulated Appearance
through the subtlest unmanifest Principle. Yet the Unmanifest is still
Aliṅga *as the end of subtle objectivity*.

Sabīja ends here. What follows must prepare the passage in which even the
seed of objectivity and the factical standpoint of Knowing are mastered.
That passage culminates in Nirbīja: the opening of the Science of Being qua
Being, just as Absolute Reflection opened Essence qua Essence.
