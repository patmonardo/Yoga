
Here's a brief look at how these terms contribute to the translation and understanding of philosophical concepts:

1. **Aikyam (ऐक्यम्)**: This term captures the notion of identity, essential in discussing the unity or sameness of concepts or entities.

2. **Anatman Sama, Atman Sama**: These could relate to the self and non-self aspects, crucial in discussing the subjective and objective dimensions in philosophy.

3. **Antara Abheda, Bahi Abheda**: These terms signify internal and external non-difference, relevant in exploring the relationship between entities and their environment or context.

4. **Anutara (अनुत्तर)**: Signifying the absolute, this term is key in discussing ultimate or foundational principles.

5. **Atmajna (आत्मज्ञ)**: Meaning self-knowing, it's vital for exploring self-awareness or consciousness.

6. **Bheda (भेद)**: Referring to difference, this term is essential for discussions of distinction and diversity within unity.

7. **Bija (बीज)**, **Sabija**: These terms, meaning genus and species, are crucial for categorization and understanding hierarchical relationships.

8. **Guna (गुण)**, **Nirguna**: With guna meaning quality and nirguna meaning without quality, these terms are central to discussing attributes and their absence.

9. **Hetu (हेतु)**: Meaning purpose, this term is key in exploring the reasons or causes behind phenomena.

10. **Karma (कर्म)**: Referring to the ground of action, this term is vital for discussions on causality and action.

11. **Nirodha (निरोध)**: Meaning sublation, it's essential for understanding the negation or overcoming of previous states or ideas.

12. **Prajna (प्रज्ञा)**: Referring to the principle, this term is key in exploring foundational or guiding concepts.

13. **Samskara (संस्कार)**: Meaning model, it's crucial for discussions on patterns, conditioning, or formative influences.

14. **Sattva (सत्त्व)**, **Sat (सत्)**: These terms, referring to truth and essence, are central to discussions on the nature of reality and being.

15. **Suchi (शुचि)**: Meaning pure, this term is important in discussions of purity, clarity, or unadulterated states.

16. **Sunya (भाव)**: Referring to nothingness, it's key in exploring the concept of void or absence.

17. **Tantra (तन्त्र)**: Meaning system, this term is essential for discussions on structured approaches or methodologies.

18. **Vidya (विद्या)**, **Vijnana (विज्ञान)**: These terms, referring to science and doctrine, are crucial for discussions on knowledge systems and theoretical frameworks.

19. **Vipaka (विपाक)**: Meaning result, it's key in exploring outcomes or consequences.

20. **Virodha (विरोध)**: Referring to contradiction, this term is central to dialectical discussions and the exploration of opposing ideas.

21. **Yoga (योग)**: Meaning unity, it's crucial for understanding the unification or integration of concepts.

Freedom

Sanskrit Translation: स्वतंत्रता (Svatantratā)
Definition: The power or right to act, speak, or think as one wants without hindrance or restraint. In philosophy, it extends to the concept of free will and the absence of necessity, coercion, or constraint in choice or action.
