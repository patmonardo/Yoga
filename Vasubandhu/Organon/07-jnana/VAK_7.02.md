# VAK_7.02 — The Primary Division of Knowledge

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सास्रवानास्रवं ज्ञानम् आद्यं संवृतिज्ञानकम् ।
अनास्रवं द्विधा धर्मज्ञानम् अन्वयमेव च ॥ ७.२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sāsravānāsravaṃ jñānam ādyaṃ saṃvṛtijñānakam |
anāsravaṃ dvidhā dharmajñānam anvayam eva ca || 7.2 ||
```

**Textual status.** These Sanskrit blocks follow the normalized local
kārikā study. Both the underlying verse e-text and the Bhāṣya quotation
transmit `saṃvṛtijñāpakam`, while the local study reads
`saṃvṛtijñānakam`. The commentary's prose explicitly identifies the category
as `saṃvṛtijñāna`: `yat sāsravaṃ tat saṃvṛtijñānam`. This supports the
technical identification but does not by itself establish the exact verse
reading. The displayed normalization remains provisional; the transmitted
form is recorded here rather than silently replaced.

After distinguishing knowledge from view, the chapter turns to the
comprehensive classification of knowledge. The Bhāṣya announces ten
knowledges, then begins with their summary division.

## 4. Grammar

**First division.** `Jñānam` is the subject and `sāsravānāsravam` its
compressed nominal predicate. The Bhāṣya introduces this with `samāsena tu`,
“in summary, however,” after answering that all knowledge is included under
ten knowledges. The expanded construction is:

```text
jñānam [sāsravaṃ ca anāsravaṃ ca]
```

The twofold statement gives a summary classification, not a denial of the
announced tenfold account.

**Reference of “first.”** `Ādyam` picks out `sāsravaṃ jñānam`, the first
member of that division. The Bhāṣya confirms the reference through the
relative-correlative construction `yat sāsravaṃ tat saṃvṛtijñānam`:
“what is contaminated is conventional knowledge.”

**Internal division.** In `anāsravaṃ dvidhā`, supply `jñānam` and the
copula. The two following designations specify the division: `dharmajñānam`
and `anvaya[jñānam]`. The prose expands this as `dvidhā bhidyate`, “is
divided in two.”

**Close construe.** Knowledge—[is] contaminated and uncontaminated; the
first—[is] conventional knowledge; uncontaminated [knowledge]—[is] twofold:
dharma-knowledge and indeed anvaya-[knowledge].

**Bhāṣya explanatory grammar.** `Saṃvṛtigrahāt` is an ablative giving the
ground of the name, qualified by `prāyeṇa`, “for the most part.”
`Ajñānasaṃvṛtatvāt` is a second explanatory ablative, “because of being
covered by ignorance”; `ity apare` attributes this explanation to others.
The two explanations must retain their different attribution.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Knowledge is contaminated and uncontaminated. The first is conventional
> knowledge. Uncontaminated knowledge is twofold: dharma-knowledge and
> anvaya-knowledge.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Knowledge, in summary, is contaminated or uncontaminated. The former is
> conventional knowledge; the latter divides into dharma-knowledge and
> anvaya-knowledge.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The field of knowledge is articulated first through its relation to the
> contaminants. Conventional knowledge belongs within that field as its
> contaminated member. The uncontaminated member has its own internal
> differentiation into dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge. The summary
> therefore preserves both a common cognitive field and differences within it.

Knowledge-status and uncontaminated status are distinct determinations.
Calling a cognition conventional does not remove it from knowledge; calling
it uncontaminated does not exhaust its specification. The twofold division
must be developed further to determine the forms of knowing involved.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| `anvayajñāna` | anvaya-knowledge | The other uncontaminated form; “subsequent” and “homologous” remain explanatory candidates. |
| `sarvajñānasaṃgraha` | comprehensive inclusion of knowledge | Bhāṣya question answered by ten knowledges; the verse gives their initial summary division. |

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
Knowledge
    ├── Contaminated knowledge = Conventional knowledge
    └── Uncontaminated knowledge
            ├── Dharma-knowledge
            └── Anvaya-knowledge
```

The first division concerns contamination-status. The second subdivides the
uncontaminated branch. The result can be collected as three named forms,
while the nested derivation explains how they belong together.

```text
Knowledge = ContaminatedKnowledge ∪ UncontaminatedKnowledge
ContaminatedKnowledge ∩ UncontaminatedKnowledge = ∅

ContaminatedKnowledge = ConventionalKnowledge

UncontaminatedKnowledge = DharmaKnowledge ∪ AnvayaKnowledge
```

The explanation of a name must be distinguished from a restriction of
object-range:

```text
UsuallyApprehendsConventions(ConventionalKnowledge)
    does not entail
OnlyApprehendsConventions(ConventionalKnowledge)
```

Likewise, the alternative explanation “covered by ignorance” is an
attributed account of the designation. It should not silently replace the
primary division with a simple opposition of false and true cognition.

The tenfold classification has been announced, not yet fully derived.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Transition from VAK 7.01.** The first verse distinguished the extensions
of knowledge and view within prajñā. This verse takes knowledge as the field
to be divided. Its contaminated branch preserves the earlier recognition
that worldly prajñā, including worldly views, belongs to knowledge.

**Bhāṣya control.** The principal explanation of `saṃvṛtijñāna` is qualified
by “for the most part.” The explanation through ignorance is attributed to
others. Both explain the name in the commentary; neither belongs in the
translation of the kārikā. The Bhāṣya also announces ten knowledges before
its summary division, while the verse itself states that initial division.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** In our developing
Yoga system, this division gives Yoga Vidyā an articulated account of the
knowledge through which practice proceeds. Yoga Technē, as its kriyā–karma
companion, brings the practical question: which form of knowing is operative
in an undertaking, and what transformation can it support?

The two project activities therefore require a shared field of cognitive
distinctions. Conventional knowledge has a place in that field; its practical
role must be determined through the work being undertaken. Uncontaminated
knowledge also requires further specification before its operative difference
is understood. Vidyā articulates these distinctions; Technē develops and
tests their significance within disciplined practice.

This reciprocal relation is our reconstruction. The present verse does not
equate Vidyā with uncontaminated knowledge or Technē with conventional
knowledge. Both activities require the entire classification. Detailed
assignments of practical efficacy await the source's further determinations.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-informed study. The exact
`saṃvṛtijñāpakam` / `saṃvṛtijñānakam` verse reading and the final English
rendering of `anvaya` remain open. The technical category and the nested
classification are explicit in the prose.
