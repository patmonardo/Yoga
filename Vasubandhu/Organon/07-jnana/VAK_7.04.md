# VAK_7.04 — Truth-Division and the Terminal Knowledges

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ते एव सत्यभेदेन चत्वारि एते चतुर्विधे ।
अनुत्पादक्षयज्ञाने ते पुनः प्रथमोदिते ॥ ७.४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
te eva satyabhedena catvāri ete caturvidhe |
anutpādakṣayajñāne te punaḥ prathamodite || 7.4 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The closing construction is
unfinished at this verse boundary: “those two, however, when first arisen …”
receives its predicate at the opening of VAK 7.05. The Bhāṣya explicitly
establishes this temporal reading.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
te | eva | satya-bhedena | catvāri | ete | catur-vidhe |
anutpāda-kṣaya-jñāne | te | punaḥ | prathama-udite
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `te` | nominative dual neuter pronoun | those two: dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge |
| `eva` | restrictive/emphatic particle | those very, precisely |
| `satya-bhedena` | instrumental singular masculine compound | through distinction according to the truths |
| `catvāri` | nominative plural neuter numeral | four [knowledges] |
| `ete` | nominative dual neuter demonstrative | these two, with reference controlled by the following construction |
| `caturvidhe` | nominative dual neuter adjective | fourfold, divided into four kinds |
| `anutpāda-kṣaya-jñāne` | nominative dual neuter compound designation | knowledge of non-arising and knowledge of exhaustion |
| `te` | nominative dual neuter pronoun | those two: the non-arising and exhaustion knowledges |
| `punaḥ` | adverb / connective particle | again, in turn; however here |
| `prathama-udite` | nominative dual neuter compound adjective | first arisen; qualifying the two knowledges |

`Anutpāda-kṣaya-jñāne` gathers two knowledge-designations under the dual
ending. Its decomposition supplies the two singular names
`anutpādajñāna` and `kṣayajñāna`; it does not make each name separately dual.

`Prathama + udite` gives `prathamodite`. Although “stated” could suggest
itself from the isolated spelling, the commentary's `prathamotpanne`
establishes “first arisen” in this context.

## 4. Grammar

**Two knowledges classified as four.** `Te eva` refers to the two
uncontaminated knowledges established in VAK 7.02–7.03. `Satyabhedena`
states the basis of classification; `catvāri` supplies the resulting four
knowledge-categories, with `jñānāni` understood. The Bhāṣya expands:

```text
te eva dharmajñānānvayajñāne
    satyabhedena
    punaś catvāri jñānāni bhavanti
```

The change from dual to plural expresses reclassification according to
truth-object. It does not abolish the dharma/anvaya distinction.

**The two terminal knowledges.** The compressed `ete caturvidhe /
anutpādakṣayajñāne` relates the two terminal designations to the fourfold
field. The Bhāṣya resolves the construction through “these very
dharma- and anvaya-knowledges, fourfold, having a nature that is not view,”
which are called exhaustion-knowledge and non-arising-knowledge.
`Adṛṣṭisvabhāve`, “having non-view nature,” is a commentarial specification,
not an additional word in the kārikā.

**The unfinished temporal clause.** The final `te` refers to exhaustion-
and non-arising-knowledge. `Prathamodite` agrees with this nominative dual
subject. English naturally renders the participial qualification as “when
first arisen”; it is not a locative dual meaning “in the two first stated.”
The Bhāṣya confirms:

```text
prathamotpanne tu kṣayajñānānutpādajñāne …
```

The predicate lies in VAK 7.05. Preserving the continuation is necessary
to construe VAK 7.04 accurately; it does not require treating the next
verse as already studied.

**Close construe.** Those very two—through truth-distinction—[are] four
[knowledges]. These two, non-arising- and exhaustion-knowledge—[are]
fourfold. Those two, however, first arisen …

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Those very two are four through distinction according to the truths.
> These two—knowledge of non-arising and knowledge of exhaustion—are
> fourfold. Those two, however, when first arisen …

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Those same dharma- and anvaya-knowledges are classified according to
> their truth-objects as four: knowledge of suffering, origination,
> cessation, and the Path. Those same fourfold dharma- and anvaya-knowledges,
> when their nature is not view, are called knowledge of exhaustion and
> knowledge of non-arising. These two, however, when they first arise …

The explanation follows the Bhāṣya, Pradhan 392|13–392|20. The predicate
supplied at 393|01–393|03, in the continuation into VAK 7.05, identifies
the first-arising cases as suffering-anvaya-knowledge and
origination-anvaya-knowledge. Its object and aspect qualifications belong
to the next study. No one-to-one assignment of the two terminal names to
those two truth-categories is asserted here.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The same uncontaminated knowing admits further determination through
> the truth it takes as object. Its domain-relative distinction persists
> within the four truth-knowledges. Exhaustion and non-arising likewise
> belong to this articulated field, under the further determination that
> their nature is not view. Their general fourfold range must then be
> distinguished from the specific character of their first arising.

The passage joins three determinations without collapsing them:
domain-relative knowledge, truth-object, and the terminal mode that lacks
view-nature. The final clause introduces a fourth question, the occasion
of first arising. A general classification and its initial actualization
need not have the same scope.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Satyabheda` — distinction according to the truths.** The classificatory
basis by which the two uncontaminated knowledges receive the four names
suffering-, origination-, cessation-, and Path-knowledge.

**`Tadālambanatva` — having those as object-supports.** The Bhāṣya's stated
ground for the fourfold naming. The truths function here as the objects
according to which knowledge is distinguished.

**`Catvāri` / `caturvidha` — four / fourfold.** `Catvāri` counts the four
truth-knowledge categories. `Caturvidhe` characterizes the dual knowledge
field as fourfold. A two-by-four table can display their intersections,
but its eight cells are not eight additional coordinate names in the
announced tenfold classification.

**`Adṛṣṭisvabhāva` — having a nature that is not view.** The Bhāṣya's
further determination of the knowledges called exhaustion and non-arising.
It recalls VAK 7.01. “Not view” should remain a technical predicate rather
than becoming a general assertion that cognition or discrimination has
ceased.

**`Kṣayajñāna` / `anutpādajñāna` — exhaustion-knowledge / non-arising-knowledge.**
The terminal designations are placed within the already differentiated
uncontaminated field. The present unit does not yet give their complete
individual definitions or distinguish all conditions of their possession.

**`Prathamodita` / `prathamotpanna` — first arisen.** The kārikā expression
and its explicit Bhāṣya gloss. They determine an initial occurrence, not
the earlier position of names in a list.

## 8. Logical Determination

The truth-classification crosses the domain-relative classification:

| Truth-object | Dharma-knowledge | Anvaya-knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| Suffering | Suffering-dharma-knowledge | Suffering-anvaya-knowledge |
| Origination | Origination-dharma-knowledge | Origination-anvaya-knowledge |
| Cessation | Cessation-dharma-knowledge | Cessation-anvaya-knowledge |
| Path | Path-dharma-knowledge | Path-anvaya-knowledge |

The table displays eight intersections under four truth-category names.
The categories overlap across classificatory grounds; they must not be
counted as mutually exclusive entries in one flat list.

```text
UncontaminatedKnowledge
    classified by domain-relation:
        DharmaKnowledge / AnvayaKnowledge
    classified by truth-object:
        Suffering / Origination / Cessation / Path
```

The Bhāṣya then locates the terminal designations within that field:

```text
ExhaustionKnowledge or NonArisingKnowledge
    belongs within fourfold Dharma/Anvaya knowledge
    AND has nature that is not View
```

This local determination does not distinguish exhaustion-knowledge from
non-arising-knowledge individually. Further source determinations are
needed for that distinction.

```text
GeneralTruthRange(TerminalKnowledges)
    must be distinguished from
TruthRangeAtFirstArising(TerminalKnowledges)
```

VAK 7.04 opens the latter question and VAK 7.05 supplies its predicate.
The first-arising qualification must not be generalized to every later
occurrence, nor should the general fourfold range be projected onto each
first occurrence.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya correction.** The exploratory kārikā study took `prathamodite`
as “first stated” and referred it backward to the first two knowledges.
The commentary's `prathamotpanne` requires “when first arisen,” referring
to exhaustion- and non-arising-knowledge. This official study incorporates
the correction while leaving the research witness intact.

**Continuity with VAK 7.01.** The absence of view-nature does not remove
these knowledges from the truth-articulated field. The predicates
“knowledge,” “view,” “dharma/anvaya,” and “truth-object” answer distinct
questions about cognition. Their systematic relation determines the
meaning of each designation here.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our Yoga system
can take this passage as a discipline for articulating accomplishment.
Yoga Vidyā determines what is known, with respect to which domain and
which truth. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, asks how an
undertaking reaches its determinate accomplishment and how that
accomplishment is known.

The terminal knowledge remains internally specified. “The work is
accomplished” therefore requires an account of the work, its field, and
the relevant knowing. The distinction between general range and first
arising also gives our reconstruction a concrete constraint: a developed
capacity and its initial actualization must be represented separately.

Vidyā and Technē meet here through the intelligibility of accomplishment.
This is our constructive development of the source distinction. The
verse itself establishes the truth-classification, the Bhāṣya supplies
the non-view qualification, and the continuation specifies first arising.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The temporal
meaning of `prathamodite` is settled by the local prose; the compressed
middle clause is expanded through that prose rather than treated as
self-explanatory. The next verse remains the required continuation.
