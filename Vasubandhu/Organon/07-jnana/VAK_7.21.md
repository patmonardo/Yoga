# VAK_7.21 — Completion-Knowledge and the Dispute over Cultivation

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अतोऽभिसमयान्त्याख्यं तदनुत्पत्तिधर्मकम् ।
स्वाधोभूमि निरोधेऽन्त्यं स्वसत्याकारं यत्निकम् ॥ ७.२१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ato 'bhisamayāntyākhyaṃ tad anutpattidharmakam |
svādhobhūmi nirodhe 'ntyaṃ svasatyākāraṃ yatnikam || 7.21 ||
```

The local kārikā's IAST has `yātnikam`; its Devanāgarī and
the Bhāṣya's quoted lemma support `yatnikam`, printed here.
The running source also has `svadhobhūmi`; the compound is
resolved as `sva-adho-bhūmi`, with the kārikā witness's `svā-`.
These are stated reading decisions, not changes to the witnesses.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

sva-adho-bhūmi | nirodhe | antyam | sva-satya-ākāram | yatnikam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ataḥ` | causal adverb | therefore, for this reason |
| `abhisamayāntyākhyam` | nominative singular neuter compound | named for the end of direct realization |
| `tat` | nominative singular neuter pronoun | that conventional knowledge |
| `anutpattidharmakam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | having non-arising as its nature |
| `svādhobhūmi` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective | belonging to its own and lower levels |
| `nirodhe` | locative singular masculine | in the case of cessation |
| `antyam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | the last [application of mindfulness] |
| `svasatyākāram` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective | having the aspects of its own truth |
| `yatnikam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | produced through effort or application |

The referent remains the special conventional knowledge cultivated
at the three anvaya completion-points in VAK 7.20. These predicates
do not describe conventional knowledge universally.

## 4. Grammar

**One subject, several determinations.** Supply `saṃvṛtijñānam`
with `tat`. The neuter predicates successively specify its name,
arising-status, level, mindfulness classification, aspects, and
dependence on effort. `Ataḥ` connects the name to cultivation at
the end of each relevant truth's direct realization.

**Two distinct uses of “end.”** In `abhisamayāntyākhyam`, the
end is a completion-point of truth-realization. In `nirodhe
'ntyam`, the commentary supplies `smṛtyupasthānam`: the last
of the four applications of mindfulness, namely dharma.
It does not mean the final cognition in the Path sequence.

**Relative level.** The Bhāṣya supplies `darśanamārgasya`:
“its own” level is the level of the Path of Seeing through
which this knowledge is cultivated. `Adhas` extends the range
downward. This is a range of cultivated knowledge, not a
claim that its object lies below the practitioner.

**Truth matching.** The prose resolves `sva` in `svasatyākāram`
through the truth whose realization occasions acquisition.
The same truth supplies the aspects and the object.

**Close construe.** Therefore that [conventional knowledge] is
called completion-of-realization [knowledge]; it is of a nature
not to arise. It belongs to the same and lower levels; in the
cessation case it is the last mindfulness application; it has
its own truth's aspects and is produced through application.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Therefore it is called knowledge at the end of direct
> realization; it is of a nature not to arise. It belongs
> to its own and lower levels. In the case of cessation,
> it is the last [application of mindfulness]. It has the
> aspects of its own truth and is produced through effort.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The conventional knowledge cultivated at the three completion-
> points is accordingly named for the end of direct realization.
> In the account stated by the verse, it is never actualized:
> its cultivation is explained through acquisition. The Bhāṣya
> challenges that explanation and endorses the former teachers'
> account: the supramundane Path produces a support capable of
> superior worldly knowledge of the truths after emergence.
> The Vaibhāṣikas reject this alternative.
>
> The cultivated conventional knowledge belongs to the level
> of the Path of Seeing and the levels below it. At cessation's
> completion it is dharma-mindfulness; at the other two completion-
> points it ranges over all four applications. Its aspects and
> object correspond to the truth just realized. Acquired through
> Seeing, it is knowledge produced through application. Including
> its entourage, it comprises four aggregates in the desire domain
> and five in the form domain.

The study translation distinguishes the verse's account from
the prose's criticism and alternative; it does not combine
them into one unanimous explanation. Source: Pradhan
406|07–407|17.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> What does cultivation accomplish? One account registers
> acquisition even where the acquired knowledge can never
> become present. The commentary asks what this acquisition
> explains, and finds an effective account in a changed support:
> through the Path, subsequent worldly knowing becomes possible
> in a superior form. The question concerns the relation between
> a cultivated determination and the continuum's power to know.

The dispute is substantive. A future dharma classified as
never arising and a support capable of subsequent manifestation
are different explanations of cultivation. The Bhāṣya's positive
judgment of the former teachers' account must remain visible,
alongside the Vaibhāṣikas' explicit refusal of it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Abhisamayāntika` — belonging to the end of direct realization.**
The prose's designation for the special conventional knowledge.
The three relevant completions are those of suffering,
origination, and cessation, carried forward from VAK 7.20.

**`Anutpattidharmaka` — of a nature not to arise.** The initial
answer is categorical: never actualized. This adjective does
not identify the knowledge with `anutpādajñāna`, the separately
enumerated knowledge of non-arising.

**`Alabdhalābha` / `prāpti` — acquisition of what was not acquired /
acquisition.** The initial explanation of cultivation. When asked
how something impossible to actualize is acquired, the appeal
to acquisition itself is challenged as circular.

**`Pūrvācāryāḥ` — former teachers.** The attribution in this
passage. It does not itself name them Sautrāntikas; the study
retains the source's attribution.

**`Saṃmukhībhāvasamarthāśrayalābha` — acquisition of a support
capable of manifestation.** The alternative explanation of
acquisition. Through the power of the supramundane Path, one
who has emerged actualizes superior worldly knowledge with
the truths as its object. “Support” preserves `āśraya` without
imposing a modern anatomical or computational identity.

**`Prāyogika` — produced through application.** The explicit
gloss of `yatnika`. The reason given is acquisition through
the Path of Seeing; the passage supplies no independent
exercise manual or specific physical technique.

**`Saparivāra` — with its entourage.** The aggregate count
includes accompanying dharmas. Four aggregates in the desire
domain and five in the form domain do not make knowledge
itself numerically four or five aggregates.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Why the Path completion is excluded.** The Bhāṣya gives two
grounds: the Path-truth has not previously been directly realized
through the worldly path, and its realization is not exhaustive
in the relevant sense. Suffering can be fully known, origination
abandoned, and cessation realized; the whole diversity of the
Path cannot all be cultivated here.

The objection that origination has not all been abandoned is
answered within the Seeing scope: everything abandonable by
Seeing that truth has been abandoned. The corresponding
counteractive paths have many kinds, and are not all cultivated.
This does not assert that all cultivation-abandonable afflictions
have already been removed. Another teachers' explanation—being
an entourage of the Path of Seeing—is rejected as requiring
proof itself.

**The cultivation dispute.**

| Account or step | Claim | Status in the prose |
|---|---|---|
| Initial account | The special conventional knowledge never becomes present | Explicit answer matching the verse |
| Acquisition explanation | It is cultivated because previously unacquired knowledge is acquired | Questioned |
| Appeal to `prāpti` | Acquisition explains how it is acquired | Rejected as failing to establish cultivation |
| Former teachers | The Path produces a support capable of superior worldly knowing after emergence | Endorsed as a successful explanation |
| Vaibhāṣika response | Refusal of that alternative | Explicitly recorded |

**Level and truth restrictions.**

| Determination | Range |
|---|---|
| Seeing based in anāgamya | Conventional knowledge at anāgamya and desire levels |
| Seeing based in the fourth dhyāna | Conventional knowledge at seven levels |
| Suffering completion | Suffering's aspects and object; four mindfulness applications |
| Origination completion | Origination's aspects and object; four mindfulness applications |
| Cessation completion | Cessation's aspects and object; dharma-mindfulness only |

The seven levels are desire, anāgamya, the intermediate dhyāna,
and the four dhyānas, using the established level inventory.
The source explicitly gives the two-level and seven-level
endpoints. “Four applications” describes the cultivated range,
not four simultaneously occurring cognitive acts.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Acquisition needs explanatory content.** The commentary's
criticism is sharper than a neutral listing of alternatives.
“Because acquired, therefore acquired” does not establish
cultivation. The former teachers' account succeeds by specifying
what has changed: the acquisition of a support capable of
manifesting the relevant knowledge.

**The alternative has a specified outcome.** It concerns superior
worldly knowledge with truths as its object, actualized after
emergence through the power of the supramundane Path. Recollection,
verbal exposition, and teaching are not individually specified
here. The affirmative capacity reading therefore has source
support, but those particular applications remain project work.

**Textual caution.** The running source contains transcription
noise, including `tadānutpattidharmakam`, against the kārikā's
`tad anutpattidharmakam`, and damaged verbal forms in the level
examples. The surrounding prose secures the never-arising
claim and the two/seven level counts. The former teachers'
support explanation is explicit independently of the difficult
following `gotre ... gautrikaṃ` sentence; no additional theory
of lineage or disposition is built on that wording here.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** This
passage gives our Yoga system a precise question: what does
practice make the knowing continuum capable of doing? Yoga
Vidyā articulates the truth, its aspects, and the determination
of knowledge. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns
the undertaking through which the support gains the power
to actualize that knowing.

For this reconstruction we take up the former teachers' account
endorsed in the Bhāṣya: cultivation is intelligible through a
transformation that makes subsequent knowing possible. This
is an explicit interpretive choice within a recorded dispute.
It is not a translation of the verse's never-arising predicate,
nor a claim that the Vaibhāṣikas accept the same account.

The truth-specific restrictions matter for the reconstruction.
A cultivated capacity has a determinate object, aspect-range,
and support. Technē must articulate the conditions connecting
the undertaking to that capacity and its exercise; Vidyā must
articulate what makes the resulting knowledge knowledge of
this truth. Their relation develops the user's Yoga-system
project without attributing its vocabulary to Vasubandhu.
