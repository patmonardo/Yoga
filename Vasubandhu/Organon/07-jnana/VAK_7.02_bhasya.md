# VAK_7.02 Bhāṣya — The Primary Division of Knowledge

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sāsravānāsravaṃ jñānam ādyaṃ saṃvṛtijñānakam |
> anāsravaṃ dvidhā dharmajñānam anvayam eva ca || 7.2 ||

> Knowledge is contaminated and uncontaminated. The first is conventional
> knowledge. Uncontaminated knowledge is twofold: dharma-knowledge and
> anvaya-knowledge.

The Bhāṣya first finishes 7.01's worldly clause, then asks by how many
knowledges all knowledge is included, answers “ten,” and only then gives
this summary (`samāsena tu`). Object-ranges of the uncontaminated pair wait
for 7.03. `Anvaya` is left unforced in English.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.02.md`](./VAK_7.02.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The five views and worldly right view: this sixfold worldly prajñā is
> view; the other [worldly prajñā] is not view. But both this and the other
> are knowledge.
>
> By how many is all knowledge included? By ten knowledges.
>
> In summary, however: knowledge is contaminated and uncontaminated.
>
> Of those two, the first is conventional knowledge.
>
> What is contaminated, that is conventional knowledge — because, for the
> most part, it apprehends conventions such as pot, cloth, woman, man, and
> so on. Others say: because it is covered by ignorance.
>
> Uncontaminated knowledge is twofold: dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge.
>
> Uncontaminated knowledge is divided in two: dharma-knowledge and
> anvaya-knowledge.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
close 7.01 (six worldly views; rest worldly prajñā is knowledge)
    → how is all knowledge included?
    → answer: ten
    → summary division: contaminated / uncontaminated
    → contaminated = conventional knowledge
         principal ground: apprehension of conventions (prāyeṇa)
         other teachers: covered by ignorance
    → uncontaminated divides: dharma-knowledge / anvaya-knowledge
```

The ten are announced, not derived. This verse is the first nested cut of
that announced whole. Voices: Vasubandhu on the principal name-explanation;
`ity apare` for the covering-by-ignorance etymology. No school label is
supplied for those others.

## 5. Conventional Knowledge and the Two Names of Saṃvṛti

The identity is explicit and biconditional in force:

```text
yat sāsravaṃ tat saṃvṛtijñānam
```

Contaminated knowledge is not expelled from `jñāna`. 7.01 already required
that: all worldly prajñā is knowledge. 7.02 names that whole contaminated
branch.

Two explanations of the designation, kept apart:

| | Ground | Attribution | Force |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `saṃvṛtigraha` of pot, cloth, woman, man, etc. | Vasubandhu, qualified by `prāyeṇa` | typical object, not exclusive range |
| 2 | `ajñānasaṃvṛtatva` | `apare` | covering, not yet adjudicated |

`Prāyeṇa` blocks the examples from becoming a definition. Object-range of
conventional knowledge is not closed in this unit.

The second explanation must not replace the first with a true/false split.
Covered-by-ignorance is an account of the *name*, recorded and left standing.

## 6. The Uncontaminated Cut

```text
anāsravaṃ jñānaṃ dvidhā bhidyate
    = dharmajñānam
    + anvayajñānam
```

Purity does not cancel determination. The uncontaminated member is immediately
articulated. The verse does not yet say what each takes as object; 7.03 does.

`Dharmajñāna` is a technical path-form. It is not, in this sentence,
knowledge of all dharmas, and it is not yet the Organon thesis that Dharma
is holding-to-be-true. That thesis, if used, belongs in §8.

`Anvayajñāna` is named only. “Subsequent” and “homologous” remain candidates.
Forcing one English word here would pre-decide 7.03.

Nested result, not a flat three:

```text
Knowledge
    ├─ Contaminated = Conventional knowledge
    └─ Uncontaminated
            ├─ Dharma-knowledge
            └─ Anvaya-knowledge
```

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

Confirmed and already in the Organon kārikā translation:

- distributive reading of `sāsravānāsravam`;
- `adyam` = the contaminated member;
- `dvidhā` divides only the uncontaminated member;
- `anvayam` supplies `jñānam`.

Attributed to the commentary, not read back into the verse words:

- the question `kiyatā sarvajñānasaṃgrahaḥ` and the answer “ten”;
- the leftover 7.01 sentences that open this unit;
- `prāyeṇa` and the list of conventions;
- `ity apare` and covering by ignorance;
- the gloss `dvidhā bhidyate`.

The `jñāpakam` / `jñānakam` syllable is a witness problem, not a doctrinal
one.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

7.01 asked which holdings-to-be-true count as knowledge or view. 7.02 takes
knowledge as the field and cuts it by Practical Reason: whether contaminants
still belong to the holding, then how the uncontaminated holding is specified
as path-form. That is not Pure Reason's table of categories. It is the Kośa
Path set next to Yoga: the same Analytic demand for determinate division,
now under Path-conditions.

Dharma as holding-to-be-true may illuminate why the first uncontaminated form
is called `dharmajñāna` — knowledge as holding determined — but the verse
does not say that. `Anvaya` would then be the holding carried along the same
order into the other domains. 7.03 must earn that, or drop it.

Conventional knowledge stays inside Dharma in this sense: worldly holding is
still holding-to-be-true. The ārya specification is the uncontaminated cut,
not the expulsion of the conventional. An Arhat reading that treated only
the uncontaminated as “real knowledge” would flatten 7.01–7.02 and neglect
the ārya path this chapter is building toward — powers, fearlessness,
instruction — where knowing must still include the world it teaches.

Yoga Vidyā uses the whole nested field; Yoga Technē uses the whole field.
Neither maps onto uncontaminated / conventional as Concept / Being. Ayatana
is not in play. Concept-level closure is not claimed for a summary cut.
