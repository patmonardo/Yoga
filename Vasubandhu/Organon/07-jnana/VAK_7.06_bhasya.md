# VAK_7.06 Bhāṣya — Cognitive Range, Path-Moments, and Preparation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tad dharmānvayadhīpakṣyam anyo 'nyaṃ darśanakṣaṇau |
> śrāvako vetti khaṅgas trīn sarvān buddho 'prayogataḥ || 7.6 ||

> That [other-mind knowledge], belonging to the dharma- and anvaya-cognition
> sides, [does not know] the other side reciprocally. A disciple knows two
> moments of Seeing; the rhinoceros-like solitary realizer knows three; a
> Buddha knows all, without preparatory application.

Negative force is carried from 7.05's `na vetti tat`. `Darśanakṣaṇau`
belongs with `śrāvakaḥ vetti`, not with the reciprocal restriction.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.06.md`](./VAK_7.06.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Other-mind knowledge belonging to the dharma-knowledge side does not know
> citta belonging to the anvaya-knowledge side. Other-mind knowledge
> belonging to the anvaya-knowledge side does not know that belonging to the
> dharma-knowledge side.
>
> In the Path of Seeing, other-mind knowledge is not present. But that Path
> as object-support does exist.
>
> Having made a preparatory application, wishing to know another's Seeing by
> other-mind knowledge, a disciple knows the first two moments of Seeing:
> the acceptance of dharma-knowledge concerning suffering, and the
> corresponding dharma-knowledge. Because taking the anvaya-side as object
> requires another preparatory application: while he undertakes that
> application, this one has reached the sixteenth citta; so he does not know
> the interval.
>
> A Pratyekabuddha knows three moments: the first two and the eighth —
> origination-anvaya-knowledge — because of lightness of preparation. Others
> say: the first, second, and fifteenth.
>
> A Buddha, however, knows all moments of the Path of Seeing without
> preparatory application.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
reciprocal side-restriction (dharma-pakṣya / anvaya-pakṣya)
    → occurrence in Seeing ≠ Seeing as object
    → prepared observation of another's Seeing
         śrāvaka: two (first kṣānti + dharma-jñāna of suffering)
         pratyekabuddha: three (principal 1, 2, 8; others 1, 2, 15)
         Buddha: all Seeing-moments, aprayogataḥ
```

## 5. Sides, Location, Object

The target of the reciprocal rule is citta of the other side, not necessarily
another act of paracitta. A person may prepare again and exercise the other
side; the rule binds the knowing as sided, not the practitioner as forever
one-sided.

```text
occurs in darśanamārga    = false for paracittajñāna
can take darśanamārga as object    = true
```

First-pass reading that the two moments fail to know each other is refused.

## 6. Preparation and Rank

The interval is relational: object-sequence continues while prayoga is made.
Present-object rule of 7.05 is preserved; missed moments are missed because
they are no longer the present target when preparation finishes.

| Knower | Moments | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Śrāvaka | 1, 2 | further prayoga loses the interval |
| Pratyekabuddha (principal) | 1, 2, 8 | `mṛduprayogatva` |
| Pratyekabuddha (`apare`) | 1, 2, 15 | reported, not merged |
| Buddha | all Seeing-moments | `aprayogataḥ` |

“All” is scoped to Seeing. VAK 6.28: fifteen Seeing-moments; sixteenth
begins Cultivation. Do not import “all sixteen” from the first-pass summary.
`Aprayogataḥ` is absence of this intervening preparation, not absence of
every condition or all prior cultivation.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- Reciprocal `anyaḥ anyam` = sided paracitta vs other-side citta.
- `Darśanakṣaṇau` = disciple's two objects.
- `Khaṅga` = Pratyekabuddha in the prose.
- Two Pratyekabuddha lists stay separately attributed.
- Opening syllable `ta`/`tad` remains a witness item.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Jñāna–Samāpatti is pristine because the Idea is allowed to stay determinate:
sides, location vs object, preparation vs sequence. Appearance would say
“clairvoyance of the Path.” The Grand Synthesis — almost the wrong word —
is only this: the same holding-to-be-true, now graded by ārya rank without
becoming a Concept-triad.

1.01 already distinguished Buddha from śrāvaka/pratyekabuddha by *sarvathā*
versus leftover non-afflicted ignorance. 7.06 is that distinction as timing:
the disciple's prayoga is the interval in which the observed Path outruns
him. The Buddha's `aprayogataḥ` is not omniscience as a blob. It is knowing
the articulated sequence without that gap. That is the Singular as executing
instance, still inside Practical Reason.

Hegel does not time a Path-moment against a preparation. The overlay stays
voluntary: without it the counts fall into Appearance (2, 3, all).
