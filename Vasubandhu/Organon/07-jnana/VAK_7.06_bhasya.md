# VAK_7.06 Bhāṣya — Cognitive Range, Path-Moments, and Preparation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> na dharmānvayadhīpakṣyam anyo 'nyaṃ darśanakṣaṇau |
> śrāvako vetti khaṅgas trīn sarvān buddho 'prayogataḥ || 7.6 ||

> Dharma-side knowledge of another's Consciousness cannot know anvaya-side
> Consciousness, nor can the reverse. In another's Path of Seeing, a prepared
> disciple knows its first two moments; a solitary realizer knows three; a Buddha knows every
> Seeing-moment without preparatory application.

The working `na` makes the negative force explicit, against local `ta`.
`Darśanakṣaṇau` belongs with `śrāvakaḥ vetti`, not with the reciprocal restriction.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.06.md`](./VAK_7.06.md).

## 3. Continuous Translation

> What further? “The dharma- and anvaya-knowledge sides do not know one
> another.” Knowledge of another's Consciousness belonging to the
> dharma-knowledge side does not know Consciousness belonging to the
> anvaya-knowledge side. Knowledge of another's Consciousness belonging to
> the anvaya-knowledge side does not know [Consciousness] belonging to the
> dharma-knowledge side. [This is] because the two have as Supports the
> [respective] counteragents for the desire domain and the higher domains.
>
> Knowledge of another's Consciousness does not occur in the Path of Seeing.
> But [such knowledge] taking that Path as Support does exist.
>
> There, wishing to know [another's] Path of Seeing through knowledge of
> another's Consciousness, [a knower] undertakes preparatory application.
> “A disciple knows the first two moments of Seeing; the rhinoceros-like
> solitary realizer knows three; the Buddha knows all, without preparatory
> application.” The disciple knows two moments from the Path of Seeing by
> knowledge of another's Consciousness: acceptance of dharma-knowledge
> concerning suffering and dharma-knowledge concerning suffering. For taking
> the anvaya-knowledge side as Support requires another preparatory
> application. By the time he undertakes that application, [the observed
> practitioner] has reached the sixteenth Consciousness; hence he does not
> know the intervening [moments].
>
> A solitary Buddha knows three moments: the first two and the eighth,
> origination-anvaya-knowledge, because of the lightness of preparatory
> application. Others say the first, second, and fifteenth.
>
> A Buddha, however, knows all moments of the Path of Seeing without
> preparatory application.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
reciprocal side-restriction (dharma-pakṣya / anvaya-pakṣya)
    → occurrence in Seeing ≠ Seeing as Support
    → prepared observation of another's Seeing
         śrāvaka: two (first kṣānti + dharma-jñāna of suffering)
         pratyekabuddha: three (principal 1, 2, 8; others 1, 2, 15)
         Buddha: all Seeing-moments, aprayogataḥ
```

## 5. Sides, Location, Support

The target of the reciprocal rule is Consciousness of the other side, not necessarily
another act of this Knowledge. A person may prepare again and exercise the other
side; the rule binds the knowing as sided, not the practitioner as forever
one-sided.

```text
occurs in darśanamārga    = false for paracittajñāna
can take darśanamārga as Support    = true
```

The reading that the two moments fail to know each other is refused.

## 6. Preparation and Rank

The interval is relational: the observed sequence continues while prayoga is made.
Present-Condition rule of 7.05 is preserved; missed moments are missed because
they are no longer the present target when preparation finishes.

| Knower | Moments | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Śrāvaka | 1, 2 | further prayoga loses the interval |
| Pratyekabuddha (principal) | 1, 2, 8 | `mṛduprayogatva` |
| Pratyekabuddha (`apare`) | 1, 2, 15 | reported, not merged |
| Buddha | all Seeing-moments | `aprayogataḥ` |

“All” is scoped to Seeing. VAK 6.28: fifteen Seeing-moments; sixteenth
begins Cultivation. Do not import “all sixteen”.
`Aprayogataḥ` is absence of this intervening preparation, not absence of
every condition or all prior cultivation.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- Reciprocal `anyaḥ anyam` = sided knowledge of another's Consciousness versus Consciousness of the other side.
- `Darśanakṣaṇau` = disciple's two targets.
- `Khaṅga` = Pratyekabuddha in the prose.
- Two Pratyekabuddha lists stay separately attributed.
- Working `na` is supported by the Sanskrit–Chinese collation and both Chinese verses; local `ta` remains a witness item.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is reconstruction.

Jñāna–Samāpatti is pristine because the Idea is allowed to stay determinate:
sides, location vs object, preparation vs sequence. Appearance would say
“clairvoyance of the Path.” The Grand Synthesis — almost the wrong word —
is only this: the same holding-to-be-true, now graded by ārya rank without
becoming a Concept-triad.

1.01 already distinguished Buddha from śrāvaka/pratyekabuddha by *sarvathā*
versus leftover non-afflicted ignorance. 7.06 is that distinction as timing:
the disciple's prayoga is the interval in which the observed Path outruns
him. The Buddha's `aprayogataḥ` is not omniscience as a blob. It is knowing
the articulated sequence without that gap. That is the Singular as executing
instance, still inside Practical Reason.

Hegel does not time a Path-moment against a preparation. The overlay stays
voluntary: without it the counts fall into Appearance (2, 3, all).
