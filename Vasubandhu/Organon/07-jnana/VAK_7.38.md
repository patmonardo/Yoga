## 4. Grammar

**The first three in order.** Supply `pratisaṃvidaḥ`
with `tisraḥ`. `Yathākramam` assigns dharma-, artha-,
and nirukti-pratisaṃvid to name, meaning, and speech
respectively. The singular `jñānam` states the knowledge
character distributively; it does not fuse the three
into one indistinguishable act.

**The first field expanded.** The prose names
`nāmapadavyañjanakāya`: bodies or collections of names,
phrases, and syllabic/verbal units. Its compressed following
forms refer to meaning and speech. Thus `dharma` here
receives a particular designation-field, rather than
automatically meaning all dharmas as possible objects.

**The fourth retains the predicate.** The commentary
explicitly says `avivartyaṃ jñānam iti vartate`:
“unfailing knowledge” continues to govern the fourth.
It is therefore knowledge concerning the stated fields,
not simply an unqualified assertion that the fourth
is mastery itself.

**Two fields, qualified expression.** The final dual
locative joins appropriate/free expression with Path-
mastery. The prose gloss is damaged around its samādhi
phrase. The next verse explicitly gives speech and
Path as the fourth's objects, confirming the two-field
structure without requiring a conjectural full restoration.

**Close construe.** The three are respectively unfailing
knowledge of name, meaning, and speech. The fourth is
[unfailing knowledge] concerning fitting and free expression
and mastery of the Path.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The three are respectively unfailing knowledge of name,
> meaning, and speech. The fourth is such knowledge
> concerning fitting and free expression and mastery
> of the Path.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Dharma-pratisaṃvid is unfailing knowledge concerning
> bodies of names, phrases, and verbal units; artha-
> pratisaṃvid concerns meaning; nirukti-pratisaṃvid concerns
> speech. The fourth, pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid, is likewise
> unfailing knowledge, concerning appropriate and unimpeded
> expression and mastery associated with the Path.

Source: Pradhan 418|13–418|18. The immediate continuation
at 418|19–20 confirms speech and Path as the fourth's
object-fields. Their full knowledge-category and level
analysis belongs to VAK 7.39.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Communicable knowledge has internally distinct fields.
> Designation, meaning, and speech receive ordered knowledge
> determinations. The fourth concerns fitting and free
> expression together with Path-mastery, while retaining
> the same character of unfailing knowledge. The distinction
> between knowing and the field known remains operative
> throughout the fourfold account.

The verse is a classification, not an assertion that
four stages must occur sequentially in every utterance.
Nor does it explicitly define the fourth as a synthesis
of the first three. Its own fields must be retained.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Avivartyajñāna`.** Unfailing or non-reversing knowledge.
The commentary explicitly extends this determination
to all four. The passage does not equate this term
with the Buddha's unrestricted knowledge throughout
the knowable field in VAK 7.30.

**`Nāma`, `pada`, `vyañjana`, `kāya`.** Names, phrases,
verbal or syllabic units, and their bodies or collections.
These are contextual working renderings of the first
field's technical expression. They should not be flattened
into one generic “word,” nor identified without argument
with modern computational tokens or database labels.

**`Artha`.** Meaning or the matter meant. Its scope is
not settled here by the English word alone. The ensuing
analysis explicitly investigates what is taken as artha;
that determination must control the later category count.

**`Vāk`.** Speech. Its distinction from the name/phrase/
verbal-unit field is part of the ordered account, even
though both concern language.

**`Yuktamuktābhilāpa`.** Fitting and free expression.
“Fluent” is a useful study rendering of the second
qualification, but should not make fluency alone the
definition of the fourth knowledge.

**`Mārgavaśitva`.** Mastery of the Path. This is included
in what the fourth knowledge concerns. The source does
not here state that every speech act itself constitutes
the liberating Path.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A precise correction to the first pass.** Rendering
the fourth simply as “mastery concerning expression
and the Path” loses the Bhāṣya's explicit continuation
of `avivartyaṃ jñānam`. The official translation preserves
unfailing knowledge concerning those fields.

**Source-form cautions.** The separate verse source supports
`yuktamukta`, whereas the running Bhāṣya's quoted lemma
has `yuktomukta`. The prose has damaged `yuktamuta` and
`samādhivaśisaṃprakyāne`. The study uses the supported
verse reading and acknowledges the samādhi-related prose
without fabricating its exact restoration. The first
prose sentence is also compressed around the meaning/
speech terms; the named triad and `yathākramam` secure
the ordered mapping.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
The fourfold account gives our Yoga system a disciplined
way to distinguish designation, meaning, expression,
and the particular knowing associated with articulate
practice. Yoga Vidyā determines what is known under
each field; Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion,
concerns the undertaking through which expression and
Path-related mastery are exercised.

The source's distinctions should remain stronger than
a generic model of eloquence. Possessing a designation,
understanding what is meant, and knowing speech are
different determinations. Their relation can inform
our exposition method without converting their ordered
list into a mandatory temporal pipeline.

Likewise, the fourth must retain its own object-fields.
Our reconstruction may investigate how fitting expression
serves the communication of knowledge, but the source
does not simply call it the synthesis of the first
three. Meaning emerges through these specified relations,
not through replacing them with an attractive general
analogy.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The ordered first-three mapping and common knowledge
predicate are explicit. The fourth's two-field structure
is supported by the verse and continuation; its damaged
prose gloss remains flagged.
