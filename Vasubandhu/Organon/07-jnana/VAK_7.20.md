# VAK_7.20 — Same-Kind Cultivation and the Three Completion-Points

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
यथोत्पन्नानि भाव्यन्ते क्षान्तिज्ञानानि दर्शने ।
अनागतानि तत्रैव सांवृतं चान्वयत्रये ॥ ७.२० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
yathotpannāni bhāvyante kṣāntijñānāni darśane |
anāgatāni tatraiva sāṃvṛtaṃ cānvayatraye || 7.20 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The Bhāṣya changes
the question from possession to cultivation: at which stage are
which knowledges cultivated? Its answer concerns future acceptances
and knowledges corresponding to the presently arising kind, with
an additional conventional cultivation at three specified moments.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
yathā-utpannāni | bhāvyante | kṣānti-jñānāni | darśane |
anāgatāni | tatra | eva | sāṃvṛtam | ca | anvaya-traye
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `yathotpannāni` | nominative plural neuter participial expression with `yathā` | according to what has arisen |
| `bhāvyante` | third-person plural passive of causative `√bhū` | are cultivated |
| `kṣāntijñānāni` | nominative plural neuter coordinated compound | acceptances and knowledges |
| `darśane` | locative singular neuter | in Seeing, the Path of Seeing |
| `anāgatāni` | nominative plural neuter adjective | future |
| `tatra` | locative adverb | there, in that same Path |
| `eva` | emphatic particle | precisely, indeed |
| `sāṃvṛtam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | conventional [knowledge] |
| `ca` | conjunction | also |
| `anvayatraye` | locative singular neuter compound | at the triad of anvaya-[knowledges] |

`Yathā + utpannāni` yields `yathotpannāni`; `tatra + eva`
yields `tatraiva`. The future qualifier is essential to the
first clause and must be read across the pāda boundary.

## 4. Grammar

**Future counterparts, not one event in two times.**
`Anāgatāni kṣāntijñānāni` is the subject of `bhāvyante`.
`Yathotpannāni` specifies correspondence with what has arisen.
The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed construction:

```text
yad yad eva utpadyate — kṣāntiḥ jñānaṃ vā
    tajjātīyam anāgatam bhāvanāṃ gacchati
```

Whatever acceptance or knowledge arises, the future of that
same kind enters cultivation. The presently arisen cognition
and the future cultivated dharmas are distinguished through
type-correspondence, not identified as numerically one event.

**Aspect restriction.** The prose adds `tadākārā eva catvāraḥ`,
the four corresponding aspects only. The range includes the
four aspects appropriate to the presently arising kind; it
is not a claim that only the single presently operative aspect
is cultivated or that all sixteen are cultivated indiscriminately.

**Conventional knowledge also.** Supply the singular passive
`bhāvyate` with `sāṃvṛtam`. `Tatra eva` preserves the location
in Seeing; `anvayatraye` specifies the three moments within it.
The commentary names suffering-, origination-, and cessation-
anvaya-knowledge. The compound does not include Path-anvaya-
knowledge as a fourth member.

**Close construe.** In Seeing, future acceptances and knowledges
are cultivated according to the kinds that have arisen. There
also, at the three anvaya-knowledges, conventional knowledge
[is cultivated].

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In Seeing, future acceptances and knowledges are cultivated
> according to what has arisen. There also, at the three
> anvaya-knowledges, conventional knowledge is cultivated.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the Path of Seeing, when an acceptance or knowledge arises,
> future cognition of that same kind is cultivated, with its
> four corresponding aspects. In that same Path, conventional
> knowledge is also cultivated at suffering-, origination-,
> and cessation-anvaya-knowledge. This additional cultivation
> does not occur at the corresponding dharma-knowledge moments,
> because direct realization of the truth is not yet complete.

This follows Pradhan 405|18–406|06. The reasons concerning
Path-anvaya-knowledge and the special conventional knowledge's
status are developed in the continuation associated with
VAK 7.21; they should not be replaced here by an inferred
promise of later verbal articulation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Cultivation follows the determinate form of the presently
> arising cognition. Its future field is specified by the
> same cognitive kind and the corresponding truth-aspects.
> At three completion-points of truth-realization, a further
> conventional cultivation is included. The Path therefore
> has a cultivation structure that differs from both its
> sequence of actual events and its possession-count.

“Future” names the temporal status of what is cultivated.
It does not by itself mean that every such dharma will later
occur. Likewise, “cultivated” must retain its technical scope
until the commentary explains the relation between acquisition
and actualization. General language about reproducible capacity
would otherwise settle a question the next passage disputes.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Bhāvanā` — cultivation.** Here the future cultivation that
occurs with a presently arising acceptance or knowledge. It
is neither the total possessed set from VAK 7.19 nor merely
repetition of the present event.

**`Tajjātīya` — of that same kind.** The Bhāṣya's explicit
restriction. Future cultivation corresponds to the particular
acceptance or knowledge that arises, rather than extending
automatically to every previously named knowledge-category.

**`Tadākārā eva catvāraḥ` — its corresponding four aspects only.**
The future aspect-range remains matched to the truth involved.
The fourfold specification is stronger than a loose statement
that cultivation has something to do with the same truth.

**`Kṣānti` — acceptance / receptive determination.** Although
uncontaminated acceptances are excluded from `jñāna` in VAK
7.01, they are explicitly included in this cultivation account.
The coordinated `kṣāntijñānāni` preserves the distinction.

**`Anāgata` — future.** The status of the cultivated counterparts.
Future classification and guaranteed later arising are not
identical predicates in the source's discussion.

**`Sabhāgajñānākārabhāvanā` — cultivation of corresponding
knowledge and aspects.** The prose's formulation of the
restriction in Seeing. The accompanying reason contains
the corrupt `gotrāṇāmaporatilabdhatvāt`; the same-kind rule
is explicit even though the exact ground requires textual
review. No broad theory of `gotra` is inferred from that
damaged expression.

**`Akṛtsnasatyābhisamaya` — incomplete direct realization of
the truth.** The stated reason that the additional conventional
cultivation does not occur at dharma-knowledge. The corresponding
anvaya moment completes the truth's domain-distribution; this
is completion of that truth's Seeing, not completion of all
Path-work or all cultivation-abandonable afflictions.

## 8. Logical Determination

The main rule has a present trigger and a future cultivated
field:

```text
ArisesInSeeing(c)
AND [Acceptance(c) OR Knowledge(c)]
    → FutureCultivationOfSameKind(c)
       with the four corresponding truth-aspects
```

This is not the rule “all possessed knowledge is cultivated
at every Seeing-moment.” Nor does it entail later actualization
of every future item included in cultivation.

The special conventional addition is:

| Present cognition | Moment in the established sequence | Conventional cultivation |
|---|---:|---|
| Suffering-anvaya-knowledge | 4 | Included |
| Origination-anvaya-knowledge | 8 | Included |
| Cessation-anvaya-knowledge | 12 | Included |
| Corresponding dharma-knowledges | 2, 6, 10 | Not included: truth-realization incomplete |

The Bhāṣya names the cognitions; the numbers are supplied
from the established sequence. Path-anvaya-knowledge is not
in this triad. It is also the sixteenth moment, classified
as Cultivation rather than strict Seeing; the continuation
provides further reasons for its exclusion from the special
conventional cultivation.

Three relations are now independently required:

```text
possessesKnowledgeCategory
presentlyActualizesCognition
cultivatesFutureCognition
```

Their distinction explains why VAK 7.19's seven possessed
categories are not simply repeated as the future cultivation
range of each subsequent moment.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The first-pass availability claim needs qualification.**
The exploratory study described cultivation as reproducible
future availability and developed a sequence toward recollection,
exposition, and teaching. The present prose establishes
same-kind future cultivation and the conventional addition;
it does not yet establish those specific later uses.

**Control from the immediate continuation.** VAK 7.21's
discussion asks whether the special conventional knowledge
is ever made present. One account denies this and explains
cultivation through acquisition; that explanation is then
challenged, and earlier teachers' account connects cultivation
to superior worldly knowing after emergence. This bounded
look at the continuation prevents importing only one outcome
into VAK 7.20. The full dispute belongs to the next study.

**Four aspects, one kind.** The Bhāṣya specifies the four
corresponding aspects, not merely the one currently occurring
aspect. At the same time, the same-kind restriction prevents
a general sixteen-aspect or ten-knowledge expansion at every
moment. Both limits must remain visible.

**Textual caution.** The difficult `gotrāṇāmaporatilabdhatvāt`
is preserved as an unresolved reason-clause. The surrounding
question and answer secure the restriction without licensing
a silent restoration of the compound or a definitive account
of its `gotra` terminology.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our
Yoga system now needs a third relation alongside actual
knowing and possessed knowledge: what is cultivated through
the act. Yoga Vidyā articulates the determinate kind and
aspect-range. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, develops
the undertaking through which cultivation is conditioned by
that specific cognitive occurrence.

The relation is precise enough to resist a generic notion
of accumulating capacity. The present act does not cultivate
everything; its kind and truth-aspects constrain the result.
The additional conventional cultivation also requires a
specified completion-point. These conditions give our
reconstruction a disciplined account of how practice changes
the knowing continuum.

Its actualization remains a further question. Vidyā and
Technē must therefore distinguish a cultivated determination
from a later successful exercise, and let the source's
forthcoming dispute sharpen that relation. This develops
the Yoga-system interpretation without preempting the
Bhāṣya's explanation.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The same-kind/four-aspect rule and three conventional
completion-points are explicit. The `gotra` reason-clause
remains uncertain; the special cultivation's actualization
is reserved for VAK 7.21.
