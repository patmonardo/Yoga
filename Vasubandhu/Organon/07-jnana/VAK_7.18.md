# VAK_7.18 — The Ten Dharma Classes and the Knowing's Own Complex

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
त्रैधातुकामला धर्मा अकृताश्च द्विधा द्विधा ।
सांवृतं स्वकलापान्यदेकं विद्यादनात्मतः ॥ ७.१८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
traidhātukāmalā dharmā akṛtāś ca dvidhā dvidhā |
sāṃvṛtaṃ svakalāpānyad ekaṃ vidyād anātmataḥ || 7.18 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The first half
supplies the ten dharma classes announced in VAK 7.17. The
second asks how far a single conventional cognition can range,
excluding its own complex. This completes the project's first
working block, VAK 7.01–7.18; that block division is editorial.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

sāṃvṛtam | sva-kalāpa-anyat | ekam | vidyāt | anātmataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `traidhātukāmalāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound grouping | belonging to the three domains, and uncontaminated |
| `dharmāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | dharmas, here classified into groups |
| `akṛtāḥ` | nominative plural masculine adjective | unmade, unconditioned |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `dvidhā dvidhā` | repeated distributive adverb | each divided twofold |
| `sāṃvṛtam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | conventional [knowledge] |
| `svakalāpānyat` | accusative singular neuter compound | what is other than its own complex |
| `ekam` | nominative singular neuter numeral | one [knowledge] |
| `vidyāt` | third-person singular optative active of `√vid` | could know |
| `anātmataḥ` | adverbial formation in `-tas` | as non-self |

The first compound is resolved distributively through the prose:
desire-domain, form-domain, formless-domain, and uncontaminated
conditioned dharmas. It does not identify domain-bound dharmas
as all uncontaminated. The unconditioned is separately specified
by `akṛtāḥ`.

## 4. Grammar

**The tenfold division.** The opening predicates are elliptical;
the Bhāṣya supplies division with `kriyante`, “are made [into
classes].” Each of the four conditioned fields is divided by
association and non-association; the unconditioned receives a
different division, wholesome and indeterminate. The repeated
`dvidhā` therefore does not impose one identical binary predicate
on every field.

**One conventional knowing.** `Ekaṃ sāṃvṛtam [jñānam]` is the
subject of `vidyāt`. The optative expresses possibility: “could
know,” rather than a command that the reader should know.
`Svakalāpānyat` is its object, expanded in prose as
`svasmāt kalāpād anyān sarvadharmān`, all dharmas other than
its own complex. `Anātmataḥ` specifies the aspect of that knowing.

**The limitation belongs to the same act.** The Bhāṣya defines
the complex through the knowing itself and the dharmas co-arising
with it. The exclusion is thus more extensive than the isolated
cognitive factor. It does not establish that those dharmas can
never be known by a different cognition.

**Close construe.** The three-domain and uncontaminated dharmas,
and the unconditioned, [are] each twofold. One conventional
[knowledge] could know, as non-self, what is other than its own
complex.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Dharmas belonging to the three domains and uncontaminated
> dharmas, and the unconditioned, are each divided twofold.
> One conventional knowledge could know as non-self what is
> other than its own complex.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Conditioned dharmas form eight classes: those of the desire,
> form, and formless domains, and uncontaminated conditioned
> dharmas, each divided into associated and non-associated.
> Unconditioned dharmas form two classes, wholesome and
> indeterminate. These ten classes are assigned as objects
> to the different knowledges.
>
> A single knowledge cannot know absolutely all dharmas. A
> conventional knowledge can know all dharmas other than its
> own complex under the aspect of non-self. Its own complex
> comprises that cognition itself and the dharmas co-arising
> with it.

This follows Pradhan 404|12–405|04. The subsequent prose specifies
the hearing-, reflection-, and cultivation-derived forms and
adds a level-delimitation to cultivated knowing; those conditions
must accompany the broad formulation rather than disappear
under “all.”

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The object-field receives a tenfold articulation through
> conditioned status, domain, association, and the ethical
> distinction of the unconditioned. The different knowledges
> have different ranges within it. A single conventional act
> can make a comprehensive non-self determination, while its
> own co-arising complex remains excluded from that act's
> object-position.

The universal wording and the particular cognitive occurrence
must be distinguished. The passage neither guarantees that
every conventional cognition knows the full field nor requires
every form of cultivated cognition to encompass all levels
simultaneously. The local non-self predicate is retained as
the stated aspect, without turning it into a separate
metaphysical organizing principle for the study.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The ten dharma classes.** The following identifiers are study
labels for displaying the Bhāṣya's distribution:

| Label | Field | Division |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Desire-domain conditioned | Associated |
| D2 | Desire-domain conditioned | Non-associated |
| D3 | Form-domain conditioned | Associated |
| D4 | Form-domain conditioned | Non-associated |
| D5 | Formless-domain conditioned | Associated |
| D6 | Formless-domain conditioned | Non-associated |
| D7 | Uncontaminated conditioned | Associated |
| D8 | Uncontaminated conditioned | Non-associated |
| D9 | Unconditioned | Wholesome |
| D10 | Unconditioned | Indeterminate |

**`Saṃprayukta` / `viprayukta` — associated / non-associated.**
The conditioned division concerns the cognitive association
field, including citta and caittas on the associated side.
The complementary non-associated class is not limited to
`cittaviprayuktasaṃskāra` as a narrow technical list; it also
must accommodate the other non-associated conditioned dharmas
of each field, including rūpa where applicable.

**`Amala` / `anāsrava` — uncontaminated.** Here one of the four
conditioned fields. Its separation from `akṛta` prevents the
uncontaminated from being identified wholesale with the
unconditioned.

**`Akṛta` / `asaṃskṛta` — unmade / unconditioned.** The verse
and prose designations. Its wholesome/indeterminate division
differs from the associated/non-associated division applied
to conditioned dharmas.

**`Svakalāpa` — own complex.** The cognition and the dharmas
co-arising with it. “Own” marks their relation to the particular
cognitive occurrence; it should not be reduced to a statement
about possession of a permanent subject.

**`Viṣaya-viṣayibheda`, `ekālambanatva`, `saṃnikṛṣṭatva`.**
The prose grounds the exclusion through object/knower
distinction, having a common object-support, and proximity.
The usual contextual construe distinguishes the knowing
itself, its associated co-arising factors sharing the object,
and the other proximate co-arising dharmas. The compressed
reason-clause does not explicitly enumerate this allocation.
In particular, `ekālambanatva` here should not be made a
universal one-individual-object rule for every kind of knowing.

**`Śrutamaya`, `cintāmaya`, `bhāvanāmaya` — derived from hearing,
reflection, cultivation.** The prose specifies hearing and
reflection in the desire-domain case, and hearing and cultivation
in the form-domain case. It then invokes delimited levels
and rejects the consequence of simultaneous dispassion from
everything. This guards the scope of cultivated realization;
it should not be used to erase the preceding comprehensive
conventional formulation.

## 8. Logical Determination

The object-field contains:

```text
4 conditioned fields × 2 association classes = 8
2 unconditioned ethical classes = 2
Total = 10 dharma classes
```

The Bhāṣya gives the complete assignment:

| Knowledge | Dharma classes as possible objects | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Conventional | D1–D10 | 10 |
| Dharma | D1, D2, D7, D8, D9 | 5 |
| Anvaya | D3, D4, D5, D6, D7, D8, D9 | 7 |
| Suffering | D1–D6 | 6 |
| Origination | D1–D6 | 6 |
| Cessation | D9 | 1 |
| Path | D7, D8 | 2 |
| Other-mind | D1, D3, D7 | 3 |
| Exhaustion | D1–D9 | 9 |
| Non-arising | D1–D9 | 9 |

These counts differ from VAK 7.17 because the objects are now
dharma classes, not knowledge-categories. Cessation-knowledge
had zero knowledge-category objects but has one dharma class
here. Other-mind knowledge had ten knowledge-category objects
but has three associated-dharma classes here. Neither is a
contradiction.

The own-complex exclusion concerns an individual act:

```text
OwnComplex(k) = {k} plus DharmasCoarisingWith(k)

ObjectFieldOfTheSpecifiedConventionalKnowing(k)
    excludes OwnComplex(k)
```

This is compatible with cognition becoming the object of
another cognition. The statement does not confer a sufficient
condition for actual knowing merely because an object lies
outside the excluded complex.

The closing level qualification also prevents this inference:

```text
ComprehensiveConventionalFormulation
    → SimultaneousDispassionFromAllLevels
```

The source explicitly rejects that consequence. Cognitive
formulation, actual object-range under particular conditions,
and liberative counteraction must remain separately determined.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The second matrix is now concrete.** The ten classes answer
the assignment introduced in VAK 7.17. Their association and
ethical divisions are not interchangeable, and their object-
counts must not be confused with the preceding counts of
knowledges as objects.

**The own-complex limit is local and precise.** The Bhāṣya
asks whether one knowledge knows all dharmas, answers negatively,
and introduces the conventional knowing with its exclusion.
This establishes a limit on the specified act without itself
deducing a higher form of knowing that overcomes it. The
project's theories of reflection must engage with the stated
limit rather than silently supply its transcendence.

**Level qualification and textual caution.** The raw source
has `saṃkṛtā`, `anātvato`, and `jsvabhāva`. The later
`tasya vyavacchinnabhūmyālambanatvāt` is compressed in its
pronoun reference; its connection to the cultivation-derived
case is supported by the following argument about dispassion.
The study preserves that qualification and its contextual
attachment without claiming a critically settled punctuation.
The own-complex reason-clause likewise remains available for
close philological review.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our
Yoga system now has an articulated object-field against which
the ranges of its knowing can be distinguished. Yoga Vidyā
determines the classes and the relations by which they can
be known. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, develops
the particular undertaking whose effective range must meet
the conditions of the cognitive occurrence.

The final discussion makes the distinction between formulation
and accomplishment especially concrete. A comprehensive
determination does not by itself establish simultaneous
liberation from all levels. Practice must still be specified
through its objects, support, aspect, and counteractive effect.
The knowing's own complex also belongs to the conditions of
its operation, even when excluded from that same act's objects.

For our reconstruction, Vidyā and Technē therefore require
both a map of the field and an account of the situated act.
The first eighteen studies supply the source-grounded
distinctions for that work. Their integration as a Yoga
system remains our explicit constructive undertaking.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The ten classes and their knowledge-object distribution are
explicit. The compressed reason-clause and final level
qualification remain marked for review. This completes the
editorial first block without advancing to VAK 7.19.
