# VAK_7.52 Bhāṣya — Disputed Persistence after Death and Mastery of Construction

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> mṛtasyāpyastyadhiṣṭhānaṃ nāsthirasya apare tu na |
> ādāvekamanekena jitāyāṃ tu viparyayāt || 7.52 ||

> According to one account, sustaining determination may remain after a
> person's death, but not for an unstable thing; other teachers deny post-
> mortem sustaining determination. At first, many construction-Consciousnesses
> produce one manifestation; when the higher knowledge is mastered, one
> Consciousness produces many, as many as desired.

Ethical status of construction-Consciousnesses and five sources of ṛiddhi: 7.53.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Does sustaining determination continue only while [the maker] lives,
> or also after death? It exists even for one who has died: the skeletal
> framework of Ārya Mahākāśyapa remains by his sustaining
> determination. But that does not apply to an unstable thing: there
> is no sustaining determination of an unstable entity. The source then
> reads, “because of Ārya Kāśyapa's sustaining of flesh and the like”
> [the local clause is damaged and lacks the expected negative].
>
> Others, however, deny it. Others say there is no sustaining
> determination of one who has died; the skeletal framework's
> persistence is instead due to the power of deities.
>
> Does one produce only a single manifestation with a single
> Consciousness? At first, one [manifestation] is produced with many
> [construction-Consciousnesses]; when [the higher knowledge] is
> mastered, the relation reverses. At first one produces one
> manifestation through more than one construction-Consciousness
> [the source uses singular instrumental forms]. But when the higher
> knowledge is mastered, one produces many with a single
> Consciousness—as many as one wishes to produce.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
question: after death?
A: yes, if the thing is stable (bones cited)
B: no — deities hold the bones
separate question: how many Consciousnesses / how many products
novice many→1; mastered 1→many as wished
```

## 5. Do Not Harmonize

Same remains, two causes. Bones are not called a constructed apparition.
Stable ≠ eternal. Reversal is production-count, not a new persistence theory
and not a lift of 7.50–51 ground-rules. Mastery ≠ āsravakṣaya. School name of
`apare` not supplied here.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `asthirasya` = unstable *thing*;
- `apare tu na` denies post-mortem adhiṣṭhāna;
- `jitāyām` = mastered abhijñā.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is reconstruction.

A lasting result does not name its cause. Vidyā keeps the two accounts apart.
Technē of mastery is a changed ratio of operations to products — still under
instruction's rank. Appearance would either canonize the bones or call mastery
cheap magic.
