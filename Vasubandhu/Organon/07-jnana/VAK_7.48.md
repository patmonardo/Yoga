# VAK_7.48 — Ṛddhi as Samādhi and Three Modes of Movement

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ऋद्धिः समाधिः गमनं निर्माणं च गतिस्त्रिधा ।
शास्तुर्मनोजवा अन्येषां वाहिन्यप्याधिमोक्षिकी ॥ ७.४८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text

śāsturmanojavā anyeṣāṃ vāhinyapyādhimokṣikī || 7.48 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness, retaining

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

yields `śāsturmanojavā`. `Vāhinī + api + ādhimokṣikī`
yields `vāhinyapyādhimokṣikī`. The three feminine movement
adjectives agree with `gatiḥ`, expressed once and subsequently
understood.

## 4. Grammar

**Definition with an attributed standpoint.** `Ṛddhiḥ
samādhiḥ` is a nominal identity statement. The prose
introduces it with `vaibhāṣikanyāyena`, “according to
the Vaibhāṣika account.” Its instrumental explanation,
`ṛdhyaty anena`, means “one accomplishes by means of this.”
Thus concentration is identified as the accomplishing
means, rather than movement itself being defined as samādhi.

**What is accomplished?** The prose asks `kiṃ tena
samṛdhyati`, “what is accomplished through it?” and
answers with the next verse words: movement and construction.
This secures their relation to the opening definition.
They are two accomplished operations, not two additional
synonyms inserted into the identity statement.

**Subdivision of movement.** `Gatis tridhā` classifies
only the movement branch. It does not say that construction
also has the same three forms. The prose enumerates
body-conveying, resolve-based, and mind-swift movement.

**Possessive genitives.** `Śāstuḥ` and `anyeṣām` assign
modes to bearer classes. The prose restricts mind-swift
movement to a Buddha and expressly grants him the other
two without needing to state them in the verse. “Others”
therefore does not mean that the first two modes are
excluded from the Buddha.

**The additive particle.** `Api` participates in the
compressed enumeration of the modes available to others.
It must not be used to infer that they possess mind-swift
movement as well: the prose explicitly says that mode
belongs to the Buddha alone, not another.

**Close construe.** Ṛddhi is concentration; [through it
are accomplished] movement and construction. Movement
is threefold. The Teacher has mind-swift movement; others
have body-conveying and also resolve-based movement.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Ṛddhi is concentration; movement and construction
> [are accomplished through it]. Movement is threefold.
> The Teacher has mind-swift movement; others have
> body-conveying and also resolve-based movement.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> According to the Vaibhāṣika account, ṛddhi is the
> concentration by which movement and construction are
> accomplished. Movement has three modes: conveying
> the body, movement through resolve, and movement
> swift as mind.
>
> Mind-swift movement belongs only to a Buddha: even
> a very distant place is reached within the time of
> an arising of mind. The other two modes also belong
> to him. Śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas have body-conveying
> movement, which carries the body successively like
> a bird, and resolve-based movement, which travels
> swiftly by determining the distant as near.

Source: Pradhan 425|04–425|17, local Bhāṣya checked
against the running source. The prose then introduces
construction as desire-domain or form-domain at 425|17–18;
its detailed analysis belongs to VAK 7.49.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The account distinguishes the means of accomplishment
> from what is accomplished through it. Movement and
> construction are separate operations; movement itself
> has differentiated modes whose distribution depends
> on the bearer.

A shared general capacity does not erase differences
in its modes. Conversely, the Buddha's distinctive
mind-swift mode does not deprive him of the two modes
shared with the other specified practitioners.

These are determinations within the source's account
of extraordinary efficacy, not independently established
physical mechanisms or travel measurements.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ṛddhi`.** Extraordinary efficacy or accomplishment.
The Vaibhāṣika definition identifies it here with samādhi
as an accomplishing means. Translating it merely as a
spectacular event would lose the distinction between
the means and its accomplishments.

**`Samādhi`.** Concentration. The statement does not
establish that every occurrence of concentration produces
extraordinary movement or construction. Its context is
the definition of the ṛddhi under discussion.

**`Gamana / gati`.** Going or movement. The former names
one of the two operations accomplished; the latter
introduces its threefold subdivision. No separate mechanism
is implied merely by changing the noun.

**`Nirmāṇa`.** Construction or a produced manifestation.
The present verse names this operation but does not yet
supply its material constitution. “Illusion” and “creation
from nothing” would each add a determination not established
by this passage.

**`Śarīravāhinī`.** Body-conveying movement. The prose
explains it as successive conveyance of the body, like
a bird. It does not name a separate vehicle or power-field
that carries the practitioner.

**`Ādhimokṣikī`.** Resolve-based movement. The explanation
is rapid going through determination of the distant as
near. This states the source's operative description,
not a modern theory of altered spacetime or a denial
that travel occurs.

**`Manojavā`.** Mind-swift movement. The prose explains
its speed by comparison with mind and describes travel
to a very distant place in the time of a mental arising.
“Swift as mind” does not mean that the mind alone travels
while the practitioner remains behind.

**`Anuktasiddha`.** Established without being stated.
The other two movements are admitted for the Buddha
even though the verse explicitly singles out his distinctive
mode. This supplies a necessary control on the bearer table.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Means and accomplished operations.**

```text
Vaibhāṣika definition:
    Ṛddhi = samādhi as accomplishing means

Accomplished through it:
    Movement
    Construction

Movement:
    Body-conveying
    Resolve-based
    Mind-swift
```

The relation “accomplished through” is not the same as
identity. Nor does the general definition establish
that all instances of samādhi possess all the specified
operations without the cultivation conditions discussed
earlier in the chapter.

**Bearer distribution.**

| Mode | Buddha | Śrāvaka / pratyekabuddha | Description in the prose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body-conveying | Admitted without separate verse statement | Admitted | Successive bodily conveyance, like a bird |
| Resolve-based | Admitted without separate verse statement | Admitted | Rapid movement by determining the distant as near |
| Mind-swift | Explicitly exclusive to Buddha | Excluded | Very distant travel in the time of a mental arising |

The table states the source's distribution among these
classes, not universal possession by every member of each
class. The passage does not revoke the earlier admission
of five higher knowledges for ordinary persons merely
because its present contrast names these three bearer classes.

**The inconceivable Buddha-domain.** Following the
mind-swift account, the prose invokes the Blessed One's
statement that the Buddha-domain is inconceivable. This
is a scriptural appeal inside the exposition, not an
additional measurable definition of speed. The passage
supplies no conversion of a mental arising into seconds
and no independent account of an intervening trajectory.

**Construction opens next.** The prose introduces two
kinds, desire-domain and form-domain construction. These
are existential-domain classifications. They must not
be confused with the three movement modes or with the
eighteen cognition-domains discussed in Chapter 1.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Attribution and scope.** The opening explicitly marks
the Vaibhāṣika account. The official study therefore
retains that attribution instead of presenting “ṛddhi
is samādhi” as an unqualified definition across Buddhist
and Yoga traditions. The following explanations articulate
the modes within this local account.

**Corrections to the research wording.** The research
kārikā describes others as being borne through a power-field.
The prose instead says that the body is carried successively
like a bird; it supplies no such field. “Concentrated
cognition made operative” is a project philosophical
rendering, while the literal account identifies samādhi
as that by which the accomplishments occur.

**Witness cautions.** The prose prints `manasa ivāsyā
rava iti` in explaining `manojavā`. The expected sense
of speed is secured by the compound and the following
travel explanation, but the printed `rava` is a textual
problem, not an entirely straightforward etymology.
The running source additionally has `cittotpādakālelna`,
regularized to `cittotpādakālena` in the local extract.
The intrusive quotation marks in the running scriptural
citation are also transcription noise. The witnesses
remain unchanged; no speculative restoration is displayed
as transmitted Sanskrit.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the distinctions among the means,
operations, and bearer conditions. Yoga Technē, its
kriyā–karma companion, concerns their effective exercise.
Their relation is useful here because visible accomplishment
alone does not specify how a capacity is constituted.

For Organon representation, the source supports preserving
an accomplishing means, its operation families, the
modes of a particular operation, and their bearer constraints.
A single property such as “can move rapidly” would erase
the distinction between resolve-based and mind-swift movement.

This is a structural project use of the passage. It does
not turn the source's descriptions into verified mechanisms,
nor make samādhi a generic computational engine. The
positive gain is a more precise account of the relations
that a capability description must preserve.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The Vaibhāṣika attribution, accomplishing-means definition,
two operation families, three movement modes, Buddha-only
mind-swift restriction, and admission of the other two
for the Buddha have been checked against the local
extract and running source. The speed-gloss irregularity
remains marked for textual review.
