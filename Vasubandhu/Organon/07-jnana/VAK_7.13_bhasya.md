# VAK_7.13 Bhāṣya — Sixteen Aspects and the Manner of Knowing

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dravyataḥ ṣoḍaśākārāḥ prajñākāras tayā saha |
> ākārayanti sālambāḥ sarvam ākāryate tu sat || 7.13 ||

> The aspects are sixteen in substantial distinction. Prajñā is the aspect.
> Together with it, the object-bearing [dharmas] apprehend under an aspect;
> whatever exists, however, is apprehended under an aspect.

The verse identifies aspect with prajñā. The prose objects and offers
`ālambanagrahaṇaprakāra`. That reformulation is commentary, not read back
into the kārikā line.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.13.md`](./VAK_7.13.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> What dharma is this that is called an aspect? Prajñā is the aspect.
>
> Then prajñā itself would not be aspect-bearing, because it is not associated
> with another prajñā. Rather: aspect is the manner in which all citta and
> caittas take their object-support.
>
> Together with it, the object-bearing [dharmas] apprehend under an aspect.
> Prajñā and all the other object-bearing dharmas apprehend under an aspect.
> Whatever exists, however, is apprehended under an aspect.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
nāmataḥ / dravyataḥ count (7 nominally-sixteen vs 16 substantial)
    → kārikā: sixteen substantially
    → several series explaining the sixteen
    → what dharma is ākāra?
         thesis: prajñā
         objection: then prajñā has no aspect
         proposal: manner of taking ālambana, of all citta/caittas
    → with prajñā, all sālamba dharmas apprehend
    → all that exists can be apprehended
```

## 5. Sixteen, Substantially

Not sixteen names for seven things. Not sixteen substances or sixteen
simultaneous factors.

| Truth | Aspects |
|---|---|
| Suffering | anitya, duḥkha, śūnya, anātman |
| Origination | hetu, samudaya, prabhava, pratyaya |
| Cessation | nirodha, śānta, praṇīta, niḥsaraṇa |
| Path | mārga, nyāya, pratipad, nairyāṇika |

Multiple series in the prose (causal roles, chanda-forms, counteraction
of views) stay plural. Do not fuse them into one unmarked glossary.
`Anitya` via conditions is dependence on *pratyaya*, not a theory of
mental representations.

## 6. What an Aspect Is

```text
verse start:  ākāra = prajñā
objection:    no second prajñā → prajñā would not be sākāra
proposal:     ākāra = ālambanagrahaṇaprakāra of citta and caittas
```

`Ākārayati` = take the object under a mode. Not “produce an image.”

```text
sālamba dharma  → apprehends under an aspect
existent        → can be apprehended under an aspect
```

The second does not give the first. An object need not cognize. A cognizing
dharma can later be an object. Neither rule is same-moment self-knowing or
omniscience.

Prajñā: named as aspect in the verse; active with the other sālamba dharmas;
available as object. `Ālambana` qua object: only apprehended.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `dravyataḥ` answers `nāmataḥ`;
- `tayā saha` goes forward with `sālambāḥ ākārayanti`;
- `sarvaṃ sat` is the passive side, range not event;
- manner-of-taking definition is the prose's correction.

Literal line keeps “Prajñā is the aspect.”

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Ākāra is how jñāna flows around artha — the *manner* of holding, not a
second inner knower and not the artha itself. That is Vidyā. The sixteen
are the Principle's aspects, now named. Technē still assigns who uses which
slice (7.10–7.12).

Nirodha in the table is Conceptual/Arthic: one of four cessation-aspects.
Meta-nirodha is this inquiry into aspect itself — paramārtha in kāmadhātu,
divine entertainment that is not comedy: the desire-realm is where the
manner of holding is determined. Sublation parked. Appearance would print
the 4×4 list and call it knowledge.

Inference remains secondary. The series that oppose views are Technē
functions of aspects, not the generation of the Principle.
