# VAK_7.40 Bhāṣya — Linguistic Levels, Complete Acquisition, and Six Final-Limit Qualities

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāmadhyāneṣu dharme vit vāci prathamakāmayoḥ |
> vikalābhir na tallābhī ṣaḍ ete prāntakoṭikāḥ || 7.40 ||

> Dharma-discrimination occurs on five levels: the desire domain and the
> four dhyānas. Nirukti-discrimination occurs in the desire domain and first
> dhyāna. Whoever has acquired one of the four has all four; an incomplete
> set is not their acquisition. These six qualities are acquired through
> final-limit dhyāna.

`Dharme vit` kept. Prose: `dharmapratisaṃvit pañcabhūmikā`. Definition of
prāntakoṭika-dhyāna: 7.41.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> “The knower of dharma is in the desire domain and the dhyānas” [the verse's
> `dharme vit` is compressed]. Dharma-pratisaṃvid belongs to five levels:
> the desire domain and four dhyānas, because above them there are no
> name-collections.
>
> “Concerning speech, in the first [dhyāna] and the desire [domain].”
> Speech and *nirukti* have one meaning here. Nirukti-pratisaṃvid belongs
> to the desire domain and first dhyāna, because above them there is no
> *vitarka*.
>
> In the *Prajñapti*, however, the discriminating knowledges themselves
> are explained: “Unfailing Knowledge concerning words and verbal units,
> their meaning, designations such as singular, dual, plural, feminine,
> and masculine, and unimpeded expression concerning them [the quoted
> ending is damaged].” From this, their order is established. *Nirukti*
> is derivational explanation, as in “It is *rūpa* because it is affected,”
> and similar examples. Others say *pratibhāna* is successively arising
> inspiration.
>
> It is reported that the prior preparations for these discriminating
> knowledges are, respectively, calculation, the Buddha's word, the
> science of language [local `śabdavaidyā` uncertain], and the science
> of reasoning. Someone not skilled in these cannot produce them, it is
> said. Others describe the Buddha's word alone as the preparation for
> all four.
>
> Whoever has one of the discriminating knowledges necessarily has all
> four. “One is not an acquirer of these with an incomplete set”: one
> does not acquire the discriminating knowledges through an incomplete set.
>
> As for these qualities already taught, beginning with *araṇā*:
> “these six are connected with the final limit.” They are acquired by
> the power of final-limit dhyāna.

## 5. Object-Availability Sets the Ceiling

Not arbitrary floors. Name-complexes gone → no dharma-pratisaṃvid. Vitarka
gone → no nirukti-pratisaṃvid. Ordinary linguistic skill ≠ the fourfold
attainment. Complete possession ≠ interchangeable functions.

Shared acquisition-ground ≠ identical intrinsic level. The next verse
distinguishes the six acquired qualities from the sixfold constitution
of final-limit fourth dhyāna.

Preparation: calculation, Buddha-word, language science, and reasoning
science, respectively, for four discriminating knowledges. Local
`śabdavaidyā` remains uncertain; [Xuanzang's parallel](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%98%BF%E6%AF%98%E9%81%94%E7%A3%A8%E4%BF%B1%E8%88%8D%E8%AB%96/%E5%8D%B7027)
corroborates this fourfold list. Another account makes Buddha-word the
preparation for all four. No medical discipline is named by the parallel.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- first pāda = five-level dharma-pratisaṃvid;
- `vāci` = nirukti, two levels;
- `vikalābhiḥ` denies incomplete lābha;
- `ete` = the six from araṇā.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is reconstruction.

Vidyā snaps: designation / meaning / speech / fitting Path-speech as distinct
Concepts inside the Idea; co-acquisition is the system, not a mush. Technē is
then manageable — prepare the whole, cultivate the power, do not harvest one
fragment and call it the four. Appearance would either identify the four or
allow a partial trophy. 7.41 will name that power.
