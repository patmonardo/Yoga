# VAK_7.45 — Ethical Classification and the Three Ignorance-Removing Vidyās

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अव्याकृते श्रोत्रचक्षुरभिज्ञे इतराः शुभाः ।
तिस्रो विद्याः अविद्यायाः पूर्वान्तादौ निवर्त्तनात् ॥ ७.४५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
avyākṛte śrotracakṣurabhijñe itarāḥ śubhāḥ |
tisro vidyāḥ avidyāyāḥ pūrvāntādau nivarttanāt || 7.45 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness, including
`nivarttanāt` with doubled `t`. Word separation follows
that witness rather than imposing continuous external sandhi.
The Devanāgarī renders the same reading.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
avyākṛte | śrotra-cakṣus-abhijñe | itarāḥ | śubhāḥ |
tisraḥ | vidyāḥ | avidyāyāḥ | pūrva-anta-ādau | nivarttanāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `avyākṛte` | nominative dual feminine adjective | ethically indeterminate [the two higher knowledges] |
| `śrotracakṣurabhijñe` | nominative dual feminine compound | higher knowledges of hearing and sight |
| `itarāḥ` | nominative plural feminine pronoun/adjective | the others, here the remaining four |
| `śubhāḥ` | nominative plural feminine adjective | wholesome, glossed as `kuśalāḥ` |
| `tisraḥ` | nominative plural feminine numeral | three |
| `vidyāḥ` | nominative plural feminine noun | knowledges that dispel the specified ignorance; vidyās |
| `avidyāyāḥ` | genitive singular feminine | of ignorance |
| `pūrvāntādau` | locative singular compound ending in `ādi` | concerning the prior limit and the rest |
| `nivarttanāt` | ablative singular neuter action noun | because of turning back or removal |

`Cakṣus` appears as `cakṣur-` before the vowel of
`abhijñā`; `tisraḥ` appears as `tisro` before `vidyāḥ`.
`Pūrva + anta + ādi` yields `pūrvāntādi`, whose locative
is `pūrvāntādau`. The prose specifies what “and the rest”
includes, rather than leaving it to philosophical inference.

## 4. Grammar

**Dual subject and predicate.** `Śrotracakṣurabhijñe`
names two higher knowledges, not one composite faculty.
`Avyākṛte` agrees with this feminine dual. The prose
reverses the order to divine sight and hearing without
changing membership.

**The remaining four.** `Itarāḥ śubhāḥ` supplies
`abhijñāḥ` from context. The prose explicitly says
`anyāś catasraḥ kuśalāḥ`: extraordinary efficacy,
other-mind knowledge, former-abode recollection, and
influx-exhaustion knowledge are wholesome.

**Three receive another classification.** `Tisro vidyāḥ`
is a nominal statement with three of the higher knowledges
as its contextual subject. This is a second classification,
not a division of the four wholesome members into three
vidyās and one remainder. Divine sight, already classified
as indeterminate, belongs to the vidyā triad under the
name death-and-rebirth knowledge.

**The causal ablative.** `Nivarttanāt` supplies the reason
for the designation as vidyā. `Avidyāyāḥ` depends on the
action noun: removal of ignorance. `Pūrvāntādau` specifies
the field concerning which the ignorance is removed.
The prose's `yathākramam`, “respectively,” fixes the
mapping to past, future, and middle in the stated order.

**Close construe.** The hearing and sight higher knowledges
are indeterminate; the others are wholesome. Three [higher
knowledges are] vidyās because they turn back ignorance
concerning the prior limit and the remaining specified fields.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The higher knowledges of hearing and sight are ethically
> indeterminate; the others are wholesome. Three are vidyās
> because they turn back ignorance concerning the prior
> limit and the rest.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Divine hearing and divine sight are ethically indeterminate;
> their discriminative knowing is associated with auditory
> and visual consciousness respectively. The other four
> higher knowledges are wholesome.
>
> Direct cognitive realizations concerning former abodes,
> death and rebirth, and exhaustion of the influxes are
> called the three vidyās of one beyond training. They
> receive this designation because they respectively
> dispel delusion concerning the prior limit, the future
> limit, and the middle.

Source: Pradhan 423|10–423|20 in the local Bhāṣya and
running source. “Middle” is provisionally understood as
the present, between past and future; the source here
names it without a longer explanation. The qualification
of “beyond training” begins with VAK 7.46 and is reserved
for that study.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Extraordinary access, ethical constitution, and removal
> of ignorance are distinct determinations of knowing.
> A cognition's extraordinary range does not determine
> its ethical class. Its designation as vidyā depends
> on the particular delusion it dispels.

The two classifications intersect: death-and-rebirth
knowledge is included both among the indeterminate
perceptual higher knowledges and among the three vidyās.
The passage therefore does not license a simple ladder
from indeterminate perception to wholesome knowledge
and then to vidyā.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Avyākṛta`.** Ethically indeterminate. Here this is a
definite Abhidharma classification, not uncertainty about
whether the cognition should be classified. It does not
mean that the same cognition is sometimes wholesome and
sometimes unwholesome according to its later use.

**`Śubha / kuśala`.** Wholesome. The prose uses `kuśala`
to explain the verse's `śubha`. The classification applies
to the four named higher knowledges, not to every act
performed by someone who possesses such a capacity.

**`Vijñānasaṃprayuktaprajñā`.** Discriminative knowing
associated with consciousness. The two indeterminate
higher knowledges are prajñā associated with visual or
auditory consciousness. The source identifies a cognitive
constitution; it does not derive neutrality merely from
having material objects.

**`Āśrayavaśena`.** According to the basis. The four-dhyāna
assignment is explained through the eye and ear faculties
that support the respective cognitions. A statement about
the ground of the basis must not be transferred without
qualification to the associated sensory consciousness.

**`Ānantaryamārga`.** Uninterrupted path. This supplies
the alternative basis for the four-dhyāna designation.
It is a technical path-function, not simply a synonym
for any mental event that happened immediately before.

**`Pradhānika / vāhulika`.** Stated according to the
principal or predominant case. These explain why a general
Abhidharma definition may call higher knowledge wholesome
prajñā although two of its six members are indeterminate.
The transmitted `vāhulika` is retained as the witness form.

**`Vidyā`.** Knowledge named through its removal of a
specified ignorance. “Science” is an established Organon
rendering, but the translation retains `vidyā` so that
the source's local criterion remains visible without
importing modern scientific institutions or methods.

**`Pūrvānta / aparānta / madhya`.** Prior limit, future
limit, and middle. The Bhāṣya explicitly assigns these
three fields of delusion to the three vidyās in order.
The last must not disappear into the broader phrase
“liberative termination,” even though exhaustion is the
object of the corresponding higher knowledge.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The two classifications.**

| Higher knowledge | Ethical class | Included among the three vidyās |
|---|---|---|
| Extraordinary efficacy | Wholesome | No |
| Divine hearing | Indeterminate | No |
| Other mind | Wholesome | No |
| Former-abode recollection | Wholesome | Yes |
| Divine sight / death and rebirth | Indeterminate | Yes |
| Influx-exhaustion | Wholesome | Yes |

Neither classification can replace the other. The opening
of the verse classifies all six ethically; its second
half selects three by their ignorance-removing function.
The bearer qualification introduced in prose requires
the following verse's further distinction.

**First objection: the four dhyānas.** The Bhāṣya identifies
the eye and ear higher knowledges with prajñā associated
with the corresponding sensory consciousnesses. It then
asks how their assignment to four dhyānas is established.

The first answer makes the designation according to the
basis: their supporting eye and ear faculties belong to
the four dhyānas. The alternative makes it according to
the uninterrupted path. These are alternative accounts
of the designation, not two stages which the text requires
to be combined into one procedure.

```text
Four-dhyāna designation:
    explanation A: according to supporting eye/ear faculties
    explanation B: according to uninterrupted path

Do not infer:
    every associated sensory consciousness belongs to all four dhyānas
```

**Second objection: wholesome prajñā.** An Abhidharma
formulation defines higher knowledge as wholesome prajñā.
The objection presses that formulation against the two
indeterminate members. Vasubandhu answers that the definition
states the principal or predominant case. He does not
reclassify the two members or deny the four wholesome ones.

**Why these three vidyās?**

| Vidyā | Direct realization | Delusion dispelled, respectively |
|---|---|---|
| Former-abode knowledge | Former modes of existence | Prior limit, `pūrvānta` |
| Death-and-rebirth knowledge | Death and subsequent arising | Future limit, `aparānta` |
| Influx-exhaustion knowledge | Exhaustion of the influxes | Middle, `madhya` |

The source supplies the correspondence, not a claim that
the first knows an absolute beginning of the world or
the second predicts every future event. The range limits
of the preceding studies still constrain these capacities.
Nor does removal of a specified delusion by the first
two establish their identity with final exhaustion of
all influxes.

The final member makes present exhaustion known; “present”
here is the contextual interpretation of `madhya`. A
stronger account of why this dispels middle-related
delusion should remain distinguished from what this
brief prose sentence explicitly states.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Corrections to the research interpretation.** The
research kārikā explains indeterminacy through the uses
to which a perceptual instrument can be put. That can
serve as a project analogy, but the Bhāṣya's local account
identifies prajñā conjoined with eye and ear consciousness.
The ethical classification of the cognition and the
ethical evaluation of a larger undertaking are different
questions.

The same research file contrasts hearing and sight with
the vidyās as though the former merely expand access
while the latter remove ignorance. Divine sight's inclusion
in death-and-rebirth vidyā prevents that contrast from
becoming an exclusive division. Likewise, “origin, process,
termination” must remain a reconstruction rather than
replace the explicit past–future–middle correspondence.

**Source boundary and witness cautions.** The local
Bhāṣya file extends through Pradhan 424|04. Its discussion
beginning with `aśaikṣyantyā` belongs to VAK 7.46. This
study stops at 7.45's explanation of the threefold naming;
it does not use the file boundary as the verse boundary.

The running source and local extract preserve `vāhuliko`
and the doubled consonant in `nivarttanāt`. These readings
are left visible. The quoted definition's `prajñe` occurs
before the closing quotation particle; its function is
clear from the definition of wholesome prajñā. No revision
of the underlying witnesses is made.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
For Organon, “science” can name an articulated knowing
whose significance is determined by the ignorance it
overcomes. This is a project development of the verse's
criterion for the three vidyās, not a general source
definition of every science.

Yoga Vidyā articulates the relation between a knowledge
and the delusion it dispels. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma
companion, concerns the cultivation and practical operation
through which knowing becomes effective. The earlier
verses' acquisition conditions remain relevant, while
this verse adds ethical constitution and the distinct
criterion for the vidyā designation.

A systematic representation should therefore preserve
relations among cognitive constitution, support, ethical
class, and ignorance-removing function. Death-and-rebirth
knowledge is the decisive local example: its indeterminate
classification and its vidyā designation must both survive
the representation. One broad label such as “advanced”
or “good” would lose the source's determination.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The two indeterminate and four wholesome members, the
two explanations of ground-designation, the predominant-
case reply, and the ordered vidyā correspondence are
checked against the local extract and running source.
The strict and derivative senses of “beyond training”
remain the next verse's task.
