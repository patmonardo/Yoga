# VAK_7.01 — Knowledge, View, and Acceptance

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नामलाः क्षान्तयो ज्ञानं क्षयानुत्पादधीर्न दृक् ।
तदन्योभयथार्या धीः अन्या ज्ञानं दृशश्च षट् ॥ ७.१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
nāmalāḥ kṣāntayo jñānaṃ kṣayānutpādadhīr na dṛk |
tadanyobhayathāryā dhīḥ anyā jñānaṃ dṛśaś ca ṣaṭ || 7.1 ||
```

**Textual status.** The Sanskrit above follows the normalized local kārikā
study. The underlying verse e-text has `nāmalā` and `dak`; the Bhāṣya e-text
also has `tadnyobhayathāryā`. The displayed `nāmalāḥ`, `dṛk`, and
`tadanyobhayathāryā` therefore represent the working study readings, not a
diplomatic transcription. The prose explicitly supplies `na dṛṣṭiḥ` and
`anyā 'nāsravā prajñā`, supporting the latter two readings. The opening
resolves as `na amalāḥ kṣāntayaḥ jñānam`.

Jñānanirdeśa opens by distinguishing the acceptances and knowledges already
operative in the Path. Its first question concerns their relation to view.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
na | amalāḥ | kṣāntayaḥ | jñānam |
kṣaya-anutpāda-dhīḥ | na | dṛk |
tad-anyā | ubhayathā | āryā | dhīḥ |
anyā | jñānam | dṛśaḥ | ca | ṣaṭ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `na` | negative particle | not; negates classification as knowledge |
| `amalāḥ` | nominative plural feminine of `amala` | stainless, uncontaminated; qualifies acceptances |
| `kṣāntayaḥ` | nominative plural feminine of `kṣānti` | acceptances or receptive determinations |
| `jñānam` | nominative singular neuter | knowledge, used as a classificatory predicate |
| `kṣaya-anutpāda-dhīḥ` | nominative singular feminine compound ending in `dhī` | cognition of exhaustion and of non-arising |
| `dṛk` | nominative singular of feminine root noun `dṛś` | view |
| `tad-anyā` | nominative singular feminine | other than those; qualifies noble cognition |
| `ubhayathā` | indeclinable adverb | in both ways: as knowledge and view |
| `āryā` | nominative singular feminine | noble; qualifies `dhīḥ` |
| `dhīḥ` | nominative singular feminine | discriminative cognition |
| `anyā` | nominative singular feminine | the other [cognition] |
| `dṛśaḥ` | nominative plural feminine of `dṛś` | views |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `ṣaṭ` | numeral, nominative plural here | six |

The sandhi `tadanyobhayathāryā` resolves through `tad-anyā + ubhayathā +
āryā`. The Bhāṣya identifies the first remainder as uncontaminated `prajñā`
and the second as worldly `prajñā`; these are contextual determinations of
the two occurrences of “other.”

## 4. Grammar

The verse consists of nominal classifications with the copula understood.
In `amalāḥ kṣāntayaḥ [na] jñānam`, the plural subject receives the singular
category-predicate “knowledge.” The negation does not mean that the
acceptances are impure: it excludes uncontaminated acceptances from `jñāna`.

The singular compound `kṣaya-anutpāda-dhīḥ` gathers the cognitions of
exhaustion and non-arising. The Bhāṣya expands the expression into the two
knowledges. `Na dṛk` denies view-status to them.

`Tad-anyā āryā dhīḥ ubhayathā` means “noble cognition other than those [is]
in both ways.” The Bhāṣya specifies the exclusions: acceptances,
exhaustion-knowledge, and non-arising-knowledge. Both predicates, knowledge
and view, apply to the remaining uncontaminated prajñā.

In `anyā jñānam`, supply `dhīḥ`. The Bhāṣya resolves this “other” as all
worldly prajñā. `Dṛśaś ca ṣaṭ` then counts the six worldly forms that also
qualify as views; it does not limit all knowledge to six kinds.

**Close construe.** Uncontaminated acceptances—[are] not knowledge;
exhaustion-and-non-arising cognition—[is] not view; noble cognition other
than these—[is] both; other cognition—[is] knowledge; and six [of its forms
are] views.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The uncontaminated acceptances are not knowledge. The cognition of
> exhaustion and non-arising is not view. Noble cognition other than those
> is both. Other cognition is knowledge, and six are views.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The uncontaminated acceptances count as view, but not as knowledge: the
> underlying tendency to doubt that they are to abandon has not yet been
> abandoned, while their nature is discerning examination. The knowledges of
> exhaustion and non-arising count as knowledge but not as view, because
> their disposition is without examination and further searching. All other
> uncontaminated discriminative cognition is both knowledge and view. All
> worldly discriminative cognition is knowledge; six worldly forms are also
> views—the five views and worldly right view.

The reasons for the exclusions, acceptance's positive view-status, and the
identification of the six are supplied by the Bhāṣya, Pradhan 391|02–391|15.
The final prose sentences confirm that the remaining worldly prajñā is not
view, although both the six and the rest are knowledge.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Discriminative cognition is determined through distinct functions.
> Acceptance already examines the truth, yet its corresponding work against
> doubt is not completed. Exhaustion-knowledge and non-arising-knowledge have
> knowledge-status without the examining and searching disposition that
> defines view here. Other noble cognition combines the two determinations.
> Worldly cognition also belongs to the field of knowledge, with view
> distinguishing six of its forms.

“Accomplished ascertainment” can illuminate the contrast between acceptance
and knowledge in the noble Path. It cannot serve without qualification as a
definition of every `jñāna` in this verse: the worldly classification also
includes the five afflicted views. Knowledge-status here does not by itself
certify rightness or liberating completion.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kṣānti` — acceptance / receptive determination.** Here specifically
uncontaminated acceptance in the Path. The term has a discerning function;
ordinary patience is not its operative sense in this passage. The restriction
`amalāḥ` must remain attached to the exclusion from knowledge.

**`Jñāna` — knowledge.** A technical classification whose scope includes
worldly prajñā as well as noble knowledge. The present verse distinguishes
its extension from that of view before developing the chapter's taxonomy.

**`Dṛk`, `dṛśaḥ`, `dṛṣṭi` — view.** The verse uses the root noun `dṛś`; the
commentary explains it with `dṛṣṭi`. In this argument its positive criterion
is discerning examination, not merely holding an opinion. Both uncontaminated
acceptances and the five afflicted views fall within its extension.

**`Dhī` and `prajñā` — discriminative cognition.** `Dhī` is the verse's
expression; `prajñā` is the commentary's explicit expansion. Their contextual
relation identifies the field being classified, without positing an enduring
cognitive bearer behind its forms.

**`Kṣayajñāna` / `anutpādajñāna` — knowledge of exhaustion / non-arising.**
The two knowledges jointly designated by the verse's compound. The local
argument establishes their exclusion from view. Their fuller contents are
left to the ensuing chapter sequence.

**`Vicikitsā-anuśaya` — underlying tendency to doubt.** The Bhāṣya's ground
for excluding uncontaminated acceptance from knowledge is that the doubt to
be abandoned by it has not yet been abandoned. This does not say that
acceptance is itself an afflicted doubt-cognition.

**`Santīraṇa` and `parimārgaṇa` — discerning examination and searching.**
Commentarial vocabulary grounding view-status and the two final knowledges'
exclusion from it. The witness has the irregular form `santiraṇātmakattvāt`;
the normalized technical term is displayed here as `santīraṇa`.

**`Laukikī samyagdṛṣṭi` — worldly right view.** The sixth worldly view,
alongside the five views already classified in the afflictive field. The
local prose refers to those five collectively rather than enumerating them.

## 8. Logical Determination

The following table combines the kārikā's classifications with the Bhāṣya's
explicit resolutions:

| Form of prajñā | Knowledge | View |
|---|---|---|
| Uncontaminated acceptances | No | Yes |
| Exhaustion-knowledge and non-arising-knowledge | Yes | No |
| Remaining uncontaminated prajñā | Yes | Yes |
| Five worldly views and worldly right view | Yes | Yes |
| Remaining worldly prajñā | Yes | No |

Knowledge and view overlap without either being reducible to the other.
The distinction crosses the noble/worldly division: the intersection includes
both remaining noble cognition and the six worldly views.

These are classifications within the present prajñā-domain, not a universal
claim about everything that might be called cognition. Nor does the matrix
alone establish a chronological sequence through every class.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya control.** Vasubandhu asks why acceptance is not knowledge and why
right knowledge is not necessarily view. His answer supplies distinct
grounds: the status of the doubt to be abandoned, and the presence or absence
of examining cognition. The acceptance's view-status is positive and
explicit, despite its exclusion from knowledge.

**Source caution.** The local prose transmits `agrahīṇatvāt` in the doubt
clause and `kṣayānamanutpādajñānaṃ` in the two-knowledge clause, alongside
the irregular examination expression noted above. This study follows the
local Bhāṣya witness's contextual interpretation: non-abandonment of the
corresponding doubt, and the two named knowledges. It does not silently
replace those strings or claim a critically established Sanskrit reading.
Exact restoration remains open to textual review.

**Continuity with Mārga.** The preceding chapter closed by relating
truth-cognition to its object and actual abandoning efficacy. This opening
now distinguishes knowledge and view within the cognitive forms employed
by that Path. Practical position helps determine the classification, while
the worldly cases preserve its wider scope.

**Organon interpretation.** Meaning emerges here through relations among
cognitive form, examining function, and the abandonment appropriate to a
Path-position. A project representation should retain the classified form,
each predicate, its stated ground, and its textual provenance. The attractive
sequence “acceptance → knowing examination → completed knowledge” is a
restricted interpretive orientation to the noble cases; the full verse also
requires the worldly branch. This reconstruction remains distinct from the
kārikā and the Bhāṣya's own explanations.

**Review status.** Provisional study informed by the local Bhāṣya and checked
against the available e-text; textual irregularities remain explicitly open.
