# VAK_7.30 Bhāṣya — Supporting Levels and Unobstructed Buddha-Knowledge

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Constitution has been stated; level is now stated. “The former-abode and
> passing-away-and-arising powers are in the dhyānas.” Passing away is precisely
> what `cyuta` denotes here. The powers of Knowledge of former abodes and of
> passing away and rebirth belong to the four dhyāna levels.
>
> “The remainder—in all levels.” The remaining powers are included in all
> levels. Those are eleven: the desire domain, anāgamya, intermediate dhyāna,
> the dhyānas, and the formless levels. All [ten powers] have a human Basis in
> Jambūdvīpa, since Buddhas do not arise elsewhere.
>
> This tenfold Knowledge is not called a power in anyone else; it is called a
> power only in the Buddha. “Why is it power in his case? Because it is
> unobstructed.” His Knowledge operates without obstruction throughout the
> knowable; that is why it is a power. Others' Knowledge is obstructed: in
> some cases it does not arise even when they wish [to know], so it does not
> deserve the designation “power.” The elder Śāriputra's rejection of a man
> seeking ordination and his ignorance of the limits of the births and so on
> of a bird troubled by a hawk are examples here. Thus the Buddhas' mental
> power, owing to their unobstructed Knowledge, is boundless like the knowable.

## 5. Four Coordinates

Knowledge-category constitution / meditative level / personal Basis /
unobstructed operation. Level is distinct from the knowable field.
The eleven-level range does not lift each constituent Knowledge's own
level restrictions. Unobstructed operation does not assert one Knowledge act
taking all knowables simultaneously.

Same ten kinds in another ≠ bala. Power is a quality of operation, not a
eleventh substance.

## 6. Śāriputra

The source cites Śāriputra's refusal of a man seeking ordination and his lack
of knowledge of the limits of a hawk-threatened bird's births. These examples
illustrate obstruction in an eminent disciple; the local prose does not
narrate the stories beyond those references.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- first compound = two powers on four dhyāna levels;
- `śeṣitam` + `sarvabhūmiṣu` = eight on eleven levels;
- `kena` question, `avyāhatam yataḥ` answer.

## 8. Interpretation

**Organon reconstruction.** Knowledge-category range, meditative level,
embodied Basis, and freedom from obstruction are distinct coordinates of a
power. The Buddha's mental power is determined here by unobstructed operation
throughout the knowable, while the two named powers still have the four-dhyāna
level rule. A model of the powers must preserve both claims.
