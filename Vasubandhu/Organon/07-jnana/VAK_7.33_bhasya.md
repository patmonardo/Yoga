# VAK_7.33 Bhāṣya — Great Compassion: Constitution, Greatness, and Eight Distinctions

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> mahākṛpā saṃvṛtidhīḥ saṃbhārākāragocaraiḥ |
> samatvād ādhimātryāc ca nānākaraṇam aṣṭadhā || 7.33 ||

> Great compassion has conventional Knowledge as its nature. It is great
> through accumulation, aspect, and object-field, through equality and
> surpassing intensity. Its distinction from compassion is eightfold.

Last of the eighteen. Prose uses `mahākaruṇā`. Five grounds of greatness ≠
the eightfold comparison with ordinary compassion.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Great compassion must now be stated. It is said: “Great compassion is
> conventional Knowledge.” Great compassion has conventional Knowledge as
> its nature. Otherwise it could not be established as taking all beings as
> Support, nor as having the aspects of threefold suffering. As with
> compassion. Why is this called great compassion?
>
> “Through accumulation, aspect, and range; because of equality and surpassing
> degree.” By accumulation: because it comes forth from a great accumulation
> of merit and Knowledge. By aspect: because it has the aspect of threefold
> suffering. By Support: because it takes the three domains as Support. By
> equality: because it operates equally toward all beings. Under the damaged
> heading for degree, the witness again says “because it operates equally
> toward all beings.” By surpassing degree: because there is nothing still
> greater than it.
>
> What is the distinction between compassion and great compassion? “The
> distinction is eightfold.” By nature: non-hatred and non-delusion,
> respectively. By aspect: one and three forms of suffering. By Support: one
> and three domains. By level: the four dhyānas and the fourth dhyāna. By
> continuum: disciples and others, and the Buddha. By acquisition: detachment
> from the desire domain and from Bhavāgra. By rescue: non-rescue and rescue.
> And by the final, damaged expression concerning compassion, whose paired
> values this Sanskrit does not establish securely.

## 5. Conventional Here Is Not Inferior

Conventional Knowledge is the stated constitution, allowing all beings as
Support and the three suffering-aspects. Not every conventional cognition is
great compassion. Support-range (three domains) differs from meditative level
(fourth dhyāna) and acquisition (Bhavāgra-detachment). Equal operation
≠ identical act toward every being. Protection ≠ every being immediately
liberated. Four-dhyāna / kāma-detachment locate *technical* compassion, not
every kindness.

Threefold suffering: duḥkha-duḥkhatā, vipariṇāma, saṃskāra — study gloss;
this sentence only says three.

Do not replace the eighth pair with adhimātratva from the greatness-list.

## 6. Two Tables, Not One

Greatness: accumulation, aspect, field, equality, unsurpassed degree.

Comparison (secure six + rescue + open eighth): nature, aspect, Support,
level, continuum, acquisition, paritrāṇa, last wording.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `saṃvṛtidhīḥ` = constitutive, with the all-beings reason;
- instrumentals + two ablatives = five grounds;
- `nānākaraṇam aṣṭadhā` = a second question.

## 8. Interpretation

**Organon reconstruction.** Great compassion joins a Knowledge constitution
to a field of beings, aspects of suffering, a meditative level, and a stated
rescue distinction. The five grounds explain why it is called great; the
eight comparisons distinguish it from compassion. A model of the quality
must keep those two questions and their relations separate.
