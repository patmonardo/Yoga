# VAK_7.26 — Cultivated Levels and the History of Acquisition

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
यद्वैराग्याय यल्लाभस्तत्र चाधश्च भाव्यते ।
सास्रवाश्च क्षयज्ञाने लब्धपूर्वं न भाव्यते ॥ ७.२६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
yadvairāgyāya yallābhas tatra cādhaś ca bhāvyate |
sāsravāś ca kṣayajñāne labdhapūrvaṃ na bhāvyate || 7.26 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness, also supported
by the separate verse source. The running Bhāṣya's
`yardvarāgyāya` is a damaged transcription. Its introductory
question asks on which path knowledge of how many levels
is cultivated. The topic shifts from category-count to
level-range and acquisition history.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
yat-vairāgyāya | yat-lābhaḥ | tatra | ca | adhaḥ | ca |
bhāvyate | sa-āsravāḥ | ca | kṣaya-jñāne |
labdha-pūrvam | na | bhāvyate
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `yadvairāgyāya` | relative compound with dative singular `vairāgya` | for detachment from whichever [level] |
| `yallābhaḥ` | relative compound with nominative singular `lābha` | acquisition of whichever [level] |
| `tatra` | locative adverb | there, at the specified level |
| `ca ... ca` | coordinating particles | both ... and |
| `adhaḥ` | adverb | below, at lower levels |
| `bhāvyate` | third-person singular passive | is cultivated |
| `sāsravāḥ` | nominative plural masculine adjective | contaminated [qualities] |
| `kṣayajñāne` | locative singular neuter | at knowledge of exhaustion |
| `labdhapūrvam` | nominative singular neuter adjective used substantivally | what has been acquired before |
| `na` | negative particle | not |

`Yat + lābhaḥ` yields `yallābhaḥ`; `ca + adhaḥ` yields
`cādhaḥ`. `Labdhapūrvam` is the subject of the final passive,
not merely an adverb meaning “previously.” The masculine
plural `sāsravāḥ` is supplied with `guṇāḥ` by the commentary.

## 4. Grammar

**Two level relations.** The first half is compressed. The
prose supplies the level for whose detachment a path operates
and the level acquired through detachment. `Tatra ... adhaḥ`
specifies cultivation at the relevant level and below. The
two relatives must not be collapsed into one vague “whatever
attainment is made for detachment.”

**The omitted knowledge qualification.** The introductory
prose first gives conventional knowledge's rule, then says
uncontaminated knowledge is not restricted merely to the
level of the path. The first half of the verse answers
that second question. Its downward extension therefore
concerns uncontaminated knowledge, not every knowledge
category without qualification.

**A change of subject in the third pāda.** With `sāsravāḥ`,
supply plural `guṇāḥ ... bhāvyante`. The prose explicitly
speaks of contaminated qualities at exhaustion-knowledge.
The grammatical change matters: the sentence is broader
than a list of contaminated knowledges alone.

**The final restriction.** The commentary resolves the
previously acquired item as something lost and then acquired
again: `yad vihīnaṃ punar labhyate`. Its reason is
`bhāvitotsṛṣṭatvāt`, because it had been cultivated and
subsequently relinquished.

**Close construe.** For whichever [level's] detachment [the
path operates], and whichever [level] is acquired, there
and below [uncontaminated knowledge] is cultivated. At
exhaustion-knowledge, contaminated [qualities] also [are
cultivated]. What has been acquired before is not cultivated
[anew in this acquisition sense].

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For whichever level's detachment the path operates, and
> whichever level is acquired, there and below knowledge
> is cultivated. At exhaustion-knowledge, contaminated
> qualities also are cultivated. What was acquired before
> is not cultivated.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Future conventional knowledge is cultivated at the level
> of the current path and at the level first acquired.
> Uncontaminated knowledge has a wider range: besides the
> path's level, the account includes the level toward whose
> detachment the path operates, the level acquired through
> detachment, and the corresponding lower levels. This
> applies to both kinds of path, beginning with preparation.
>
> At knowledge of exhaustion, wholesome contaminated qualities
> of all levels that are acquired through exhaustion-knowledge
> also enter cultivation. These include mindfulness of breathing,
> applications of mindfulness, immeasurables, and liberations.
>
> What is acquired for the first time is cultivated. Something
> previously cultivated, subsequently lost, and now reacquired
> is not cultivated anew in this sense.

Source: Pradhan 410|03–410|17. At 410|18 the commentary asks
whether cultivation is acquisition alone and answers no,
opening VAK 7.27's fourfold account. That transition controls
the scope of the final sentence here.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Cultivation has both a level-distribution and a history.
> The present path's level, its target of detachment, and
> the level gained through detachment are distinct coordinates.
> At exhaustion-knowledge, wholesome contaminated qualities
> also enter the cultivated field. Yet a first acquisition
> and recovery after loss remain different events, even
> when the recovered attainment appears the same now.

This does not establish that an advanced practitioner
automatically exercises every lower-level capacity. Nor
does the exclusion of reacquisition mean that continued
practice is useless. The passage is determining future
cultivation through acquisition; the continuation explicitly
refuses to reduce every sense of cultivation to that one.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Saṃvṛtijñāna` — conventional knowledge.** Its introductory
rule names the path's own level and the level first acquired.
Do not substitute the uncontaminated downward-extension
rule for this more restricted statement.

**`Anāsrava` — uncontaminated.** Qualifies the knowledges
whose wider level-range the first half determines. The
prose's `dvividho 'pi mārgaḥ` carries the worldly and
supramundane distinction of the cultivation discussion.

**`Sarvabhūmika` — belonging to all levels.** The breadth
of the contaminated qualities cultivated at exhaustion-
knowledge. Together with `kṣayajñānalābhika`, it specifies
the relevant qualities across levels; it does not assign
every individual quality to every possible support level.

**`Kṣayajñānalābhika` — acquired through exhaustion-knowledge.**
Restricts the qualities entering this cultivation. The
special breadth is not an assertion that every contaminated
dharma, including unwholesome states, is cultivated.

**`Svacittādhirājya` — sovereignty over one's own mind.**
The commentary's affirmative image for the occasion. It
describes wholesome dharmas as coming forward through
their acquisitions, with an analogy to the reception of
sovereignty. This is source vocabulary, not an Organon
addition to the Sanskrit account.

**`Apūrva` / `labdhapūrva`.** Not previously acquired /
previously acquired. The contrast becomes precise through
the prose's account of loss and reacquisition.

**`Bhāvitotsṛṣṭa` — cultivated and subsequently relinquished.**
The history explaining the exclusion. Present possession
alone does not reveal whether this condition holds.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Knowledge or quality field | Level rule or qualification |
|---|---|
| Future conventional knowledge | Path-level and level first acquired |
| Uncontaminated knowledge | Not confined to path-level; includes the specified detachment/acquisition levels and lower levels |
| Contaminated qualities at exhaustion-knowledge | All-level range of the wholesome qualities acquired through that knowledge |

The level rule must be applied within the established
support possibilities of each knowledge or quality. It
does not manufacture uncontaminated knowledge at a level
where the chapter has not admitted it.

The historical restriction is:

```text
FirstAcquisition(x)
    → CultivatedInTheAcquisitionSense(x)

PreviouslyCultivated(x)
AND SubsequentlyRelinquished(x)
AND ReacquiredNow(x)
    → NotNewlyCultivatedInThatSense(x)
```

The first rule belongs to the present cultivation context;
it is not a universal claim about acquisition of arbitrary
objects or unwholesome states. The second is not a rule
that no previously acquired dharma can ever be practiced.

The prose first asks whether everything acquired is cultivated,
then qualifies the answer by first acquisition. Reading the
unqualified wording at 410|15 as an independent universal
maxim would erase the clarification that immediately follows.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Qualities, not “impure knowledges.”** The research kārikā
study translates `sāsravāḥ` as impure knowledges. The prose's
`guṇāḥ` and subsequent `sarvakuśaladharma` establish wholesome
contaminated qualities here. Mindfulness of breathing,
immeasurables, and liberations must remain visible as the
broader field now under discussion.

**Textual limits in the examples and similes.** The source
prints `a4ubhā...` at the beginning of its list. The later
items are readable, but that damaged first item is not
silently restored. The first simile mentions release through
the cutting of a rope, but its `peḍā...` wording needs review.
The following sovereignty image is clearer, though it too
contains transcription noise. These images support the
positive account of release and wholesome qualities coming
forward; they do not justify a detailed reconstruction of
the damaged comparison.

**Reacquisition is the exact case.** The first-pass “no double
counting” explanation is too broad if it treats the verse
as merely subtracting everything already possessed. The
Bhāṣya specifies prior cultivation, relinquishment, and
reacquisition. This history is part of the determination,
not dispensable background.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Yoga
Vidyā articulates the kind and level of knowledge, while
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, articulates the
undertaking through its target, acquisition, and history.
This verse brings those determinations together without
reducing cultivation to a numerical inventory.

Our Yoga system should distinguish the first establishment
of a capacity from its restoration after loss and from
its continued exercise. Those can lead to similar present
availability while differing in how that availability
has arisen. The distinction between acquisition and other
forms of cultivation will receive its explicit source
articulation in the next kārikā.

The sovereignty image also gives a positive direction to
the reconstruction. Completion includes wholesome practical
qualities entering cultivation. Vidyā's determination of
liberative knowledge and Technē's cultivation of the practical
field belong in one articulated account, with each quality's
actual conditions retained. “All levels” is not a license
to claim indiscriminate present mastery.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The knowledge/quality distinction, level rules, and exact
reacquisition restriction are source-checked. The damaged
first example and wording of the similes remain flagged.
