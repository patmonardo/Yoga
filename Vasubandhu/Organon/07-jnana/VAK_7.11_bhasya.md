# VAK_7.11 Bhāṣya — Other-Mind Knowledge: Aspect, Singularity, and Discernment

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tathā paramanojñānaṃ nirmalaṃ samalaṃ punaḥ |
> jñeyasvalakṣaṇākāram ekaikadravyagocaram || 7.11 ||

> Pure knowledge of another mind is likewise [four-aspected]. The
> contaminated form, however, has the knowable's own characteristic as
> its aspect. [Both] range over one individual entity at a time.

`Tathā` points to 7.10's four-aspect distribution, not the sixteen.
The following prose (396–398) is a long test of sūtra mind-pairs; this
file keeps the verse-determinations and the method-rule, not every pair.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.11.md`](./VAK_7.11.md).

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> anāsravaṃ paracittajñānaṃ tatheva /
> svasatyākāratvāc caturākāraṃ mārgajñānatvāt /
> sāsravaṃ paracittajñānaṃ jñeyānāṃ citta-caittānāṃ yat svalakṣaṇaṃ tad ākārayati /
> ubhayam api tu ekaikadravyagocaram /
> yadā cittaṃ gṛhṇāti na tadā caittān / yadā vedanāṃ na tadā saṃjñām /

Closing field-statement (398|20–23), compressed:

> present other-citta and caittas; kāma / rūpa / uncontaminated where allowed;
> obtained in Cultivation; absent from Seeing and the uninterrupted Path;
> not emptiness- or signless-samādhi; not kṣaya- or anutpāda-jñāna.

**Witness.** Pradhan 396|04–398|23. `Paramatojñānam`, `labhayam api tu`,
`gṛhlāti`, `dugapadgrahaṇam` unrestored. Working `ubhayam` for the shared
singular range. Voice: Vasubandhu, objector on sūtra wording, Vaibhāṣika
readings of the pairs. Method-rule: `varaṃ śāstravirodho na sūtravirodhaḥ`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Uncontaminated knowledge of another mind is likewise [four-aspected],
> because it has the aspects of its own truth — Path-knowledge.
>
> Contaminated knowledge of another mind takes as aspect the own
> characteristic of the knowable cittas and caittas.
>
> Both, however, have one individual entity at a time as range: when it
> apprehends mind, it does not then apprehend the mental factors; when it
> apprehends feeling, it does not then apprehend recognition.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
pure paracitta = Path-knowledge → four Path-aspects
contaminated paracitta → aspect = svalakṣaṇa of the knowable citta/caitta
both → one dravya per act
objection: sūtra “knows a passionate mind as passionate”
    → not yugapadgrahaṇa (cloth and stain)
    → long pair-debate (sūtra over incompatible śāstra)
    → does not inherit the target's object or aspect
    → closing field-restrictions
```

## 5. Aspect of the Knowing ≠ Aspect of the Target

| Form | Aspect of this knowing | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Pure | four Path-aspects | one entity at a time |
| Contaminated | own characteristic of the knowable mental dharma | one entity at a time |

```text
paracitta knows cognition C
AND C has object O and aspect A
    does not entail
paracitta knows O or operates under A
```

“This mind is passionate” ≠ “this mind is passionate toward that form.”
The second would take the form as object.

`Dravya` here = the individually apprehended dharma, not a substrate.
`Samala` = sāsrava, not automatically akuśala.

Association, possession (`prāpti`), and having-as-object stay distinct.
The prose rejects possession and mere object-taking as the sense of
“with passion” for this knowing.

## 6. What the Long Debate Is For

Scripture classifies another mind (passionate / free of passion, sluggish /
agitated, …). Classification is not simultaneous grasp of mind + factor,
and not identity with that mind. Cultivation instructions differ for līna
and uddhata; a flattened “afflicted” reading loses the work. Hence sūtra
over śāstra when they collide on these words.

Those pairs are not re-litigated here. The method-rule and the three-relation
cut (knowing's aspect / entity known / that entity's own aspect and object)
are the Organon takeaway.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `tathā` = four Path-aspects via `mārgajñānatva`;
- `jñeyasvalakṣaṇa` is the contaminated aspect;
- `ekaikadravyagocara` covers both forms (`ubhayam` working);
- later field-limits are commentary, not verse words.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Nirodha as a truth is Conceptual/Arthic: cessation as what is held. This
chapter's work is higher — meta-nirodha: determining knowing without taking
the target's artha as one's own. That is paramārtha *in* kāmadhātu: not
flight to ūrdhva, not conventional aparamārtha. Other-mind knowledge that
stayed in Appearance would claim to live inside the other's passion. The
Idea knows the condition and leaves the other's object unstolen.

Yoga Vidyā: three relations kept apart. Yoga Technē: cultivation still
needs līna ≠ uddhata. Philosophical Cognition sees both sides of a pair;
it does not undo the other's citta (Seeing both ≠ undoing both). Sublation
= nirodha remains parked.
