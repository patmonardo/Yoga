# VAK_7.27 — Four Modes of Cultivation and Their Domains

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
प्रतिलम्भनिषेवाख्ये शुभसंस्कृतभावने ।
प्रतिपक्षविनिर्धावभावने सास्रवस्य तु ॥ ७.२७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
pratilambhaniṣevākhye śubhasaṃskṛtabhāvane |
pratipakṣavinirdhāvabhāvane sāsravasya tu || 7.27 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The running
Bhāṣya prints `vinirdhava` in its verse quotation, against
the witness's `vinirdhāva` and the prose's `vinirdhāvana`.
The preceding question is decisive: is acquisition alone
cultivation? The answer is no; cultivation is fourfold.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
pratilambha-niṣevā-ākhye | śubha-saṃskṛta-bhāvane |
pratipakṣa-vinirdhāva-bhāvane | sa-āsravasya | tu
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `pratilambha` | masculine noun in compound | acquisition |
| `niṣevā` | feminine noun in compound | practice, exercise |
| `ākhye` | nominative dual feminine adjective | the two called / named |
| `śubhasaṃskṛtabhāvane` | nominative dual feminine compound | the two cultivations of wholesome conditioned dharmas |
| `pratipakṣa` | compound member | counteraction, counteragent |
| `vinirdhāva` | compound member | expulsion, driving out |
| `pratipakṣavinirdhāvabhāvane` | nominative dual feminine compound | the two cultivations of counteraction and expulsion |
| `sāsravasya` | genitive singular substantive adjective | of the contaminated [class of dharmas] |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | whereas, but |

The prose uses `niṣevaṇa` and `vinirdhāvana` in naming
the corresponding modes. These longer forms are retained
when discussing the commentary's terminology.

## 4. Grammar

**Two nominative pairs.** `Ākhye` agrees with the first
`bhāvane`; both are feminine nominative dual. The second
`bhāvane` is also nominative dual. The verse names two
pairs, rather than locating an activity “in cultivation.”
The research file's locative analysis obscures this syntax.

**The first domain.** `Śubhasaṃskṛta` qualifies the dharmas
whose two modes are acquisition and practice. The prose
expands it as `kuśalasaṃskṛtānāṃ dharmāṇām`: wholesome
conditioned dharmas. Both qualifications must be retained.

**The second domain.** `Sāsravasya` is a collective singular;
the prose supplies plural `sāsravāṇāṃ dharmāṇām`. It specifies
what counteraction and expulsion concern. Cultivation “of”
contaminated dharmas in these senses does not mean increasing
their contamination.

**One and both.** Within the acquisition/practice pair, the
prose says `anāgatānām ekā pratyutpannānām ubhe`: for future
dharmas one, for present dharmas both. The antecedent pair
controls this statement. It does not say that future
wholesome contaminated dharmas have only one mode under
the entire fourfold classification.

**Close construe.** The two called acquisition and practice
are cultivations of wholesome conditioned dharmas; the
two cultivations of counteraction and expulsion, however,
concern contaminated dharmas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The two called acquisition and practice are cultivations
> of wholesome conditioned dharmas. The two cultivations
> of counteraction and expulsion, however, concern the
> contaminated.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Cultivation is not acquisition alone. Its four modes are
> acquisition, practice, counteraction, and expulsion.
> Acquisition and practice concern wholesome conditioned
> dharmas: within this pair, future dharmas have acquisition,
> while present dharmas have both acquisition and practice.
> Counteraction and expulsion concern contaminated dharmas.
>
> Thus wholesome contaminated dharmas admit all four modes;
> uncontaminated dharmas admit the first two, while defiled
> and indeterminate dharmas admit the latter two. External
> Abhidharmikas count six by adding restraint and dissociation.
> The Kāśmīras include those additions within counteraction
> and expulsion.

Source: Pradhan 410|18–411|07. The closing transition at
411|07–08 returns to qualities cultivated at exhaustion-
knowledge and prepares the Buddha-specific account in
VAK 7.28.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Cultivation is determined through its relation to the
> dharma concerned. Wholesome conditioned dharmas are
> acquired and exercised; contaminated dharmas are subject
> to counteraction and expulsion. Because wholesome and
> contaminated are not mutually exclusive, one class can
> admit both pairs. The meaning of cultivation emerges
> through these relations rather than through a single
> generic idea of repetition or accumulation.

The classification distinguishes eligible modes, not
four simultaneous operations performed on every instance.
Its first pair also distinguishes future acquisition
from present exercise. This gives the preceding acquisition
calculus its place within a wider account of bhāvanā.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Pratilambhabhāvanā` — cultivation as acquisition.** The
mode central to the preceding future-cultivation discussion.
The present verse does not erase VAK 7.26's restriction
concerning recovery of something cultivated and relinquished.

**`Niṣevaṇabhāvanā` — cultivation as practice / exercise.**
The second mode, associated here with present wholesome
conditioned dharmas. “Repeated practice” is a useful working
rendering, but the passage's explicit distinction is present
exercise versus future acquisition; it does not quantify
repetitions or prescribe a schedule.

**`Pratipakṣabhāvanā` — cultivation through counteraction.**
The counteractive relation directed toward contaminated
dharmas. It should not be restricted here to preventing
only dharmas that have never arisen; that temporal limitation
is not stated in this passage.

**`Vinirdhāvanabhāvanā` — cultivation through expulsion.**
The mode of driving out or removal. It does not mean
strengthening the contaminated dharma named as its domain.

**`Kuśalasāsrava` — wholesome and contaminated.** The overlap
that admits all four modes. Translating `sāsrava` simply
as “unwholesome” would make the source's matrix unintelligible.

**`Saṃvarabhāvanā` — cultivation as restraint.** The first
addition in the external Abhidharmikas' sixfold account,
associated with the faculties.

**`Vibhāvana` — dissociation / removal.** A provisional
working rendering for the second addition, associated
with the body. The abbreviated and damaged quotation
does not justify an elaborate account of its procedure.

**`Antarbhūta` — included within.** The Kāśmīra response
classifies the two additions within the existing pair.
It disputes the need for extra independent categories,
not merely the words used to designate their functions.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Dharma class | Acquisition | Practice | Counteraction | Expulsion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wholesome contaminated | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Uncontaminated conditioned | Yes | Yes | — | — |
| Defiled / indeterminate | — | — | Yes | Yes |

This is a class-level distribution of cultivation modes.
The first two columns retain the verse's conditioned
restriction: the prose's abbreviated “uncontaminated”
must not be expanded to make unconditioned cessation
an object of productive practice.

Within the acquisition/practice pair:

| Temporal status of wholesome conditioned dharma | Applicable positive modes |
|---|---|
| Future | Acquisition |
| Present | Acquisition and practice |

The table does not imply that every present event creates
a historically unprecedented capacity. The temporal
distribution and the prior-acquisition restriction answer
different questions and must both be retained.

The alternative taxonomy is:

```text
External Abhidharmikas:
    FourModes + Restraint + Dissociation = SixModes

Kasmira account:
    Restraint and Dissociation are included within
    Counteraction and Expulsion.
```

The order supports the corresponding pairing—restraint
with counteraction, dissociation with expulsion—but the
closing compound states their inclusion together. The
study does not infer a separate detailed proof of each
mapping from this compressed sentence.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The four right efforts are not the local explanation.**
The research kārikā study says the Bhāṣya aligns these
modes with producing and strengthening wholesome states,
and preventing and abandoning unwholesome states. The
present source passage does not make that alignment.
Its own explanation uses wholesome conditioned versus
contaminated dharmas, their overlap, and future versus
present status within the first pair. A comparison with
the four right efforts would require separate attribution.

**Wholesome contamination is structurally important.** The
four-mode row is not an incidental exception. It prevents
two false identifications: wholesome equals uncontaminated,
and cultivation always means positively increasing its
named object. Each mode determines a different relation
to the dharma concerned.

**Textual caution in the sixfold account.** The running
source contains `kujñalasaṃskṛta`, `pūrvī`, and a damaged
opening to the quotation about six faculties. The immediately
following `kuśalasāsrava` and the verse's `śubhasaṃskṛta`
secure the wholesome reading. The faculties/body allocation
of the two additions is clear, but the abbreviated quotation
and its wording are not reconstructed as a complete sūtra
passage. The source supplies no individual school identity
for the external Abhidharmikas beyond that designation.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** This
verse gives our Yoga system an articulated account of what
cultivation does. Yoga Vidyā determines the dharma through
its qualities, temporal status, and relations. Yoga Technē,
its kriyā–karma companion, determines whether the undertaking
acquires, exercises, counteracts, or removes.

The same lexical dharma-name is therefore insufficient
to determine an operation. Its being wholesome, conditioned,
contaminated, future, or present changes the relevant relation.
This is a precise instance of our principle that meaning
emerges in a system: the term acquires its operative sense
through the determinations that connect it to the whole.

Vidyā and Technē remain project terms. They articulate our
reconstruction of the fourfold account without replacing
its Sanskrit categories or its recorded disagreement. The
sixfold alternative also reminds us that distinct practical
descriptions need not require six irreducible functions;
their relations must first be examined.

**Transition to the Buddha's qualities.** The commentary
now recalls its general account of qualities cultivated
at exhaustion-knowledge. VAK 7.28 distinguishes those
belonging exclusively to a Buddha. The fourfold clarification
completes the present cultivation discussion before that
new determination begins.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The dual grammar, temporal distribution, overlapping
dharma classes, and four/six taxonomy dispute are source-
checked. The damaged quotation remains explicitly limited.
