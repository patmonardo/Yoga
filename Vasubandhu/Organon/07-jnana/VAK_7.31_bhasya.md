# VAK_7.31 Bhāṣya — Bodily Strength: Measure and Material Constitution

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nārāyaṇabalaṃ kāye saṃdhiṣv anye daśādhikam |
> hastyādisaptakabalaṃ spraṣṭavyāyatanaṃ ca tat || 7.31 ||

> The Buddha's strength is Nārāyaṇa-strength in the body; others say it is in
> the joints. The strength of the seven beginning with the elephant increases
> tenfold; that strength is the tangible Essence.

The shared word `bala` does not make bodily strength an eleventh Knowledge-power.
Here *spraṣṭavyāyatana* is the Tangible Essence.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> “Nārāyaṇa-strength in the body.” The Buddha's strength in his body is
> described as Nārāyaṇa-strength. “Others [say], in the joints”: Nārāyaṇa-
> strength is in each individual joint, according to others.
>
> Bhadanta says that his bodily strength, like his mental strength, is
> boundless. Otherwise [his body] would not be capable of bearing boundless
> Knowledge-power. The joints of the Buddha, a solitary Buddha, and a
> wheel-turning monarch are respectively [compared to] a nāga-knot, a chain,
> and a hook.
>
> What, then, is the measure of Nārāyaṇa-strength? “Tenfold greater: the
> strength of the seven beginning with the elephant.” The strength of ten
> ordinary elephants is that of one gandhahastin. The same successive tenfold
> increase is to be stated for mahānagna, praskandin, varāṅga, cānūra, and
> Nārāyaṇa. Others say that successive tenfold increase through the ordinary
> elephant, gandhahastin, mahānagna, and praskandin amounts to half a
> Nārāyaṇa-strength, twice which is Nārāyaṇa-strength. The greater reckoning
> is fitting.
>
> “And that is the Tangible Essence.” That bodily strength in everyone has
> the nature of the Tangible Essence: it is precisely a special determination
> of the Great Elements. Others say it is derived Form, a distinct item beyond
> the seven. The powers have been stated.

## 5. Keep the Questions Apart

Nārāyaṇa is a grade-name, not a theological identification. Series is
traditional comparison, not newtons. Six tenfold steps → a derived million;
the prose does not print that figure as a measurement.

Bhadanta's adequacy-of-support is recorded, not converted into a law that
knowledge-range equals mechanical force. Preference for the larger reckoning
≠ endorsement of infinity.

`Sarvasyaiva`: tangible classification is general. Magnitude claims above it
are Buddha-specific. Seven derived tangibles ≠ the whole tangible inventory
(Great Elements remain).

Joint-images (nāgagranthi / chain / peg for Buddha, pratyekabuddha, cakravartin)
are images, not anatomy.

## 6. Two Balas

Cognitive power: jñāna, unobstructed. Bodily power: rūpa, tangible. Same
lexeme, different domain.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- first claim = named bodily strength;
- `anye` = per-joint;
- `daśādhikam` = tenfold steps of the seven;
- `tat` = bodily strength as spraṣṭavya-Essence.

## 8. Interpretation

**Organon reconstruction.** Bodily strength belongs to the Tangible Essence
under the principal material account. It can be compared by magnitude,
localized in the body or each joint, and analyzed as Great-Element
determination or derived Form. Bhadanta's body–Knowledge adequacy argument
is an attributed account, not a conversion of Knowledge into bodily force.
