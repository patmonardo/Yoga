# VAK_7.31 — Bodily Strength: Measure, Support, and Material Constitution

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नारायणबलं काये संधिष्वन्ये दशाधिकम् ।
हस्त्यादिसप्तकबलं स्प्रष्टव्यायतनं च तत् ॥ ७.३१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
nārāyaṇabalaṃ kāye saṃdhiṣv anye daśādhikam |
hastyādisaptakabalaṃ spraṣṭavyāyatanaṃ ca tat || 7.31 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. After the
boundless mental power of VAK 7.30, the commentary explicitly
turns to bodily strength. The shared word `bala` does not
make bodily strength an additional knowledge-power.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
nārāyaṇa-balam | kāye | saṃdhiṣu | anye | daśa-adhikam |
hasti-ādi-saptaka-balam | spraṣṭavya-āyatanam | ca | tat
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `nārāyaṇabalam` | nominative singular neuter compound | Nārāyaṇa-strength |
| `kāye` | locative singular masculine | in the body |
| `saṃdhiṣu` | locative plural masculine | in the joints |
| `anye` | nominative plural masculine | others [say] |
| `daśādhikam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | greater by a factor of ten, as glossed |
| `hastyādisaptakabalam` | nominative singular neuter compound | strength of the sevenfold series beginning with the elephant |
| `spraṣṭavyāyatanam` | nominative singular neuter compound | the tangible sense-field |
| `ca` | particle | also, and |
| `tat` | nominative singular neuter pronoun | that bodily strength |

`Saṃdhiṣu + anye` yields `saṃdhiṣv anye`; `hasti + ādi`
yields `hastyādi`. The prose's ten ordinary elephants
equaling one gandhahastin resolves `daśādhika` as tenfold
increase, not addition of ten unspecified units.

## 4. Grammar

**The stated account and another localization.** Supply
the Buddha's bodily strength as the subject of the first
claim. With `anye`, supply a speech verb. The prose expands
the alternative distributively as `sandhau sandhau`, in
each joint. This is stronger than locating strength vaguely
somewhere among the joints.

**The measure belongs to a series.** `Daśādhikam` qualifies
the strength in the seven-member comparison. The commentary
explains successive tenfold increases and then records
another reckoning. The verse should not be read as a
single unspecified quantity ten greater than an elephant.

**Material classification.** `Tat` resumes bodily strength.
The prose resolves `spraṣṭavyāyatanam` through
`spraṣṭavyāyatanasvabhāvam`: its nature belongs to the
tangible sense-field. This is a classificatory statement,
not a claim that the Buddha's strength is the entirety
of everything tangible.

**Close construe.** Nārāyaṇa-strength is in the body;
others [say it is] in each joint. The strength of the
sevenfold series beginning with the elephant increases
tenfold. That bodily strength also belongs to the tangible
sense-field.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Nārāyaṇa-strength is in the body; others place it in
> the joints. The strength of the sevenfold series beginning
> with the elephant increases tenfold. That strength belongs
> to the tangible sense-field.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Buddha's bodily strength is described as Nārāyaṇa-
> strength. Some locate that strength in every joint.
> Bhadanta instead says his bodily strength is boundless
> like his mental strength, arguing that otherwise the
> body could not sustain boundless knowledge-power.
>
> The measure of Nārāyaṇa-strength is explained through
> a seven-member series beginning with ordinary elephant-
> strength and increasing tenfold at each grade. A different
> reckoning is also recorded; the commentary finds the
> larger measure more fitting.
>
> Bodily strength, in everyone, belongs to the tangible
> sense-field. The principal account identifies it as a
> special determination of the great elements. Others
> regard it as derived form, distinct from the seven
> [derived tangibles].

Source: Pradhan 413|04–413|19. The comparisons and their
textual limits are detailed below; their traditional
magnitudes are not modern measurements of force.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Bodily strength receives its own determination. The
> commentary asks where it is located, how it is measured,
> and what material category it belongs to. Extraordinary
> magnitude does not remove it from the tangible field.
> The relation of body to boundless knowledge enters as
> an attributed argument about adequacy of support, not
> as an identity of cognition and material strength.

The common classification survives the disagreement:
both the great-element and derived-form accounts treat
strength materially. The claim of boundlessness is a
separate issue from that classification.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Nārāyaṇabala`.** Nārāyaṇa-strength, the named culmination
of the principal comparison-series. Its local function is
a traditional strength measure. The name alone does not
establish a theological identification of the Buddha.

**`Sandhi`.** Bodily joint or articulation. The commentary
also compares the joint-structures of Buddha, pratyekabuddha,
and wheel-turning monarch through `nāgagranthi`, chain,
and peg imagery respectively. The first term is preserved
as a technical image; the passage does not provide enough
anatomical description to turn it into a modern joint model.

**`Bhadanta`.** The authority to whom the boundless-body
argument is attributed. The passage gives this designation;
it does not supply an individual identification here.

**`Daśottaravṛddhi`.** Successive tenfold increase. The
initial explicit equivalence controls the proportional
reading of the named series.

**`Mahābhūtaviśeṣa`.** A special determination of the great
elements. This is the principal material account of bodily
strength, not an additional immaterial substance.

**`Upādāyarūpa`.** Derived form. The alternative account
places strength here as an item distinct from the seven
derived tangibles. “Seven” must not be mistaken for the
total tangible inventory, which also includes the great
elements in the established material classification.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The principal comparison-series.**

| Grade | Source designation | Relation to preceding grade |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Ordinary elephant (`prākṛtahastin`) | Starting comparison |
| 2 | `gandhahastin` | Tenfold |
| 3 | `mahānagna` | Tenfold |
| 4 | `praskandin` | Tenfold |
| 5 | `varāṅga` | Tenfold |
| 6 | `cānūra` | Tenfold |
| 7 | `nārāyaṇa` | Tenfold |

These are the names in the local source. They are not
silently regularized into names from another version
of the comparison. Six tenfold steps would arithmetically
give one million ordinary-elephant units; that figure
is a derivation from the series, not a separately stated
physical measurement in the prose.

The alternative passage proceeds through ordinary elephant,
gandhahastin, mahānagna, and praskandin, then describes
half-Nārāyaṇa strength and its doubling to Nārāyaṇa.
Its compressed, damaged wording makes the precise attachment
of the final tenfold step uncertain. No definitive alternative
total is calculated here. The closing preference for the
larger measure is preserved without using it to settle
every detail of that reading.

**Separate the assertions and voices.**

| Question | Account |
|---|---|
| Location | Nārāyaṇa-strength in the body |
| Alternative location | Others: in each joint |
| Unlimited magnitude | Bhadanta: bodily strength boundless like mental strength |
| Comparative magnitude | Principal series and alternative reckoning |
| Material constitution | Principal account: great-element determination |
| Alternative constitution | Others: distinct derived form |

Bhadanta's reason is that otherwise the bodily support
would not sustain boundless knowledge-power. It is an
argument recorded in the Bhāṣya, not a modern physical
law or a sufficient basis for converting knowledge-range
into a quantity of bodily force.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The strength is bodily, the classification general.**
The examples concern the Buddha's extraordinary strength,
but the final prose says `sarvasyaiva`, of everyone.
The tangible classification applies to bodily strength
generally. Its generality must not be confused with the
Buddha-specific magnitude claims preceding it.

**The joint comparison belongs to the source.** The research
discussion does not develop the intervening Buddha/
pratyekabuddha/wheel-turning-monarch comparison. It is
included here with its limits. The source's `nāgagrandhi`
and `śaṅkalā` show transcription irregularities; knot,
chain, and peg are comparison-images, not verified anatomy.

**Preserve disagreement about magnitude.** The text records
Bhadanta's boundless account alongside graded measures.
Its remark favoring the larger reckoning does not expressly
identify that preference with endorsement of Bhadanta's
infinite-strength thesis. Those statements remain distinct.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Our Yoga system must distinguish cognitive determination
from the bodily support of its exercise. Yoga Vidyā
articulates knowing and its scope; Yoga Technē, its
kriyā–karma companion, concerns embodied undertaking and
the conditions under which it can be performed. Their
relation does not erase the material character of strength.

Bhadanta's support argument gives this relation a definite
philosophical form: the support must be adequate to the
operation it sustains. We can investigate that relation
as reconstruction while retaining its source attribution
and avoiding a literal engineering inference from boundless
knowledge to infinite mechanical force.

The principal material account is equally significant.
Strength is a determination of the great elements; the
alternative treats it as derived form. Technē therefore
needs a determinate account of bodily capacity, rather
than treating every use of `bala` as the same kind of
power. The word acquires its meaning through the domain
and relations in which it is used.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
Localization, attributed boundlessness, the principal
sevenfold series, joint comparison, and rival material
accounts are source-checked. The alternative numerical
reckoning remains limited by its compressed wording.
