# VAK_7.07 Bhāṣya — Accomplishment and Knowledge of Non-Repetition

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kṣayajñānaṃ hi satyeṣu parijñātādiniścayaḥ |
> na parijñeyam ity ādir anutpādamatir matā || 7.7 ||

> Knowledge of exhaustion is indeed ascertainment concerning the truths,
> beginning with “fully known.” That beginning with “not to be fully known”
> is held to be cognition of non-arising.

The Bhāṣya asks what distinguishes the two terminal knowledges already
located in 7.04–7.05, then unfolds `parijñāta-ādi` and `na parijñeyam ity
ādi` through the four truth-tasks. A further question — how uncontaminated
knowledge knows in this form — receives two attributed answers.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.07.md`](./VAK_7.07.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Now, what is the difference between exhaustion-knowledge and
> non-arising-knowledge?
>
> Knowledge of exhaustion is indeed ascertainment concerning the truths,
> beginning with “fully known.” That beginning with “not to be fully known”
> is held to be cognition of non-arising.
>
> What is exhaustion-knowledge? One knows: “Suffering has been fully known
> by me.” One knows: “Origination has been abandoned; cessation has been
> realized; the Path has been cultivated.” Taking that as basis, that which
> is knowledge, seeing, science, understanding, awakening, prajñā,
> illumination, penetration — this is called exhaustion-knowledge.
>
> What is non-arising-knowledge? One knows: “Suffering has been fully known
> by me and is not again to be fully known” — up to “the Path has been
> cultivated and is not again to be cultivated.” “Taking that as basis” is
> stated at length in the treatise.
>
> How does one know thus by uncontaminated knowledge? Having emerged, one
> knows thus by the cognition subsequently acquired from it. Therefore the
> difference of those two is by the difference of that. The Kashmiris say:
> it is made known in the treatise. Others say: one knows thus by
> uncontaminated knowledge as well.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
question: difference of the two terminal knowledges
    → kṣaya = ascertainment of accomplished truth-tasks
    → anutpāda = that ascertainment + not again to be done
    → śāstra-series of names around the knowing
    → how can anāsrava-jñāna know in this form?
         Kāśmīra: tatpṛṣṭhalabdha after emergence
         apare: uncontaminated knowledge also
```

Same four truths, two modal determinations. Possession-conditions of each
knowledge are not settled here. Completion does not by itself entail
non-repetition-knowledge.

## 5. Four Tasks, Two Ascertainments

| Truth | Task | Exhaustion formulation | Non-arising addition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suffering | full knowing | fully known | not again to be fully known |
| Origination | abandonment | abandoned | not again to be abandoned |
| Cessation | realization | realized | not again to be realized |
| Path | cultivation | cultivated | not again to be cultivated |

The four operations stay distinct. They are not four copies of “understanding.”
The table is content of the ascertainment, not a claim that one event takes
all four truths at once. `Yāvat` in the prose abbreviates; the study expands
it without inventing four separately quoted clauses.

Non-repetition is scoped to these liberative tasks. It does not forbid every
later wholesome act or all further exercise of knowledge.

The śāstra-list (`jñāna`, `darśana`, `vidyā`, … `abhisamaya`) gathers names
around this knowing. It does not cancel 7.01: the two terminal knowledges
are not view. `Darśana` in the list is a textual problem, not a reversal.

## 6. How the Form Is Known

The kārikā does not say how the first-person formulations occur. The Bhāṣya
does, and splits:

```text
Kāśmīra
    uncontaminated knowledge
        → emerge (vyutthita)
        → subsequently acquired cognition (tatpṛṣṭhalabdha)
        → “I have … / not again to be …”
    difference of the two knowledges shown in the difference of that subsequent cognition

apare
    one knows thus by uncontaminated knowledge also
```

First-pass quotation stops at the Kashmiris and treats subsequent cognition
as *the* refinement. That leaks a school answer into the unit's conclusion.
Both accounts remain attributed. The first-person wording is a formulation
used to define content; its cognitive status is exactly what is disputed.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

Confirmed:

- `hi` introduces the difference-question's answer;
- `parijñāta` / `parijñeya` is the grammatical hinge (done / still to be done);
- `ādi` stands for the four-task series;
- `anutpādamati` = `anutpādajñāna` in the prose.

Attributed to the commentary, not read into the verse words:

- the four explicit predicates and `me`;
- `punaḥ` in the non-repetition series;
- the śāstra name-list;
- the how-question and both answers.

No rewrite of the Organon literal line is required.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Mārga synthesizes the four tasks. Jñāna as Grand Synthesis — almost the wrong
word — is the Idea determining those tasks as *known accomplished* and as
*not again required*. That is Practical Reason closing a holding-to-be-true,
not the Concept generating four truths out of a triad.

Kṣaya is not *Glauben*. It is ascertainment that the work is done. Anutpāda
is not a second Belief that “it will be alright.” It is the same field held
as not to be done again. Kant’s *Wissen* is nearer these cells than Opinion;
it is still not Kośa-`jñāna` over the whole chapter table.

The Kashmiri split (immediate anāsrava / subsequent articulation) is a real
resource: realization ≠ judgment that realization has occurred. The other
teachers refuse to exile the explicit knowing from uncontaminated cognition.
Choosing would be our overlay. Leaving both standing is fidelity. Appearance
would flatten the pair into “enlightenment happened.”

Buddha Mind as Singular would be exhaustive closure of both ascertainments
without leftover task. This verse only defines the two modes. 1.01's
*sarvathā* remains the measure, not a result proven here.
