# VAK_7.16 — Mindfulness Classification and Knowledges as Objects

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
स्मृत्युपस्थानमेकं धीर्निरोधे परचित्तधीः ।
त्रीणि चत्वारि शेषाणि धर्मधीर्गोचरो नव ॥ ७.१६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
smṛtyupasthānam ekaṃ dhīr nirodhe paracittadhīḥ |
trīṇi catvāri śeṣāṇi dharmadhīgocaro nava || 7.16 ||
```

**Textual status.** The local Devanāgarī witness prints `धर्मधीर्गोचरो`,
with an additional `r` after `dhī`. The local IAST and the underlying
Bhāṣya quotation have the compound `dharmadhīgocaraḥ`. The Sanskrit
blocks retain that witness difference explicitly; the construe follows
the compound and the prose's unambiguous `dharmajñānasya`.

The verse completes the mindfulness-classification and begins a new
table of knowledges that can be objects of other knowledges. That
table continues in VAK 7.17.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
smṛti-upasthānam | ekam | dhīḥ | nirodhe | para-citta-dhīḥ |
trīṇi | catvāri | śeṣāṇi | dharma-dhī-gocaraḥ | nava
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `smṛtyupasthānam` | nominative singular neuter compound | establishment or application of mindfulness |
| `ekam` | nominative singular neuter numeral | one |
| `dhīḥ` | nominative singular feminine | knowing, here knowledge |
| `nirodhe` | locative singular masculine | concerning cessation |
| `paracittadhīḥ` | nominative singular feminine compound | knowledge of another mind |
| `trīṇi` | nominative plural neuter numeral | three [applications of mindfulness] |
| `catvāri` | nominative plural neuter numeral | four [applications of mindfulness] |
| `śeṣāṇi` | nominative plural neuter adjective | the remaining [knowledges] |
| `dharmadhīgocaraḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | the object-range of dharma-knowledge |
| `nava` | nominative plural numeral | nine [knowledges] |

`Smṛti + upasthāna` gives `smṛtyupasthāna`. The Bhāṣya explicitly
calls `dhī`, `prajñā`, and `jñāna` equivalent expressions here.
Their contextual equivalence should govern this passage without
erasing distinctions established in other contexts.

## 4. Grammar

**Cessation-knowledge.** `Dhīḥ nirodhe` is the subject of the nominal
classification `smṛtyupasthānam ekam`. The different genders belong
to the subject and category-predicate. The prose identifies the one
as `dharmasmṛtyupasthānam`.

**Other-mind knowledge across the line boundary.** Join
`paracittadhīḥ` to `trīṇi` in the next line, supplying the plural
`smṛtyupasthānāni`. The Bhāṣya gives feeling, mind, and dharmas as
the three. The number counts classifications, not three simultaneously
apprehended entities.

**The remaining knowledges.** `Śeṣāṇi` qualifies understood `jñānāni`.
Their predicate is `catvāri [smṛtyupasthānāni]`. The commentary
excludes cessation- and other-mind knowledge explicitly. The rule
states the remaining categories' range; it does not override a
specific cessation-knowledge classification in an overlapping case.

**A new object-count.** The Bhāṣya asks which knowledge has how many
knowledges as objects. The final pāda answers with `nava [jñānāni]`
as the object-field of dharma-knowledge. It supplies the relational
expansion:

```text
dharmajñānasya
    nava jñānāni ālambanam
    anyatra anvayajñānāt
```

The numeral counts knowledge-categories; the singular `gocaraḥ`
names their collective object-field.

**Close construe.** Knowledge concerning cessation [is classified
under] one mindfulness-application; other-mind knowledge [under]
three; the remaining knowledges [under] four. Nine [knowledges are]
the object-range of dharma-knowledge.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Knowledge concerning cessation is one application of mindfulness;
> knowledge of another mind is three; the remaining knowledges are
> four. Nine knowledges are the object-range of dharma-knowledge.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Cessation-knowledge is included under mindfulness of dharmas alone.
> Other-mind knowledge is included under mindfulness of feeling,
> mind, and dharmas. The knowledges other than these two have the
> fourfold mindfulness classification: body, feeling, mind, and
> dharmas. Turning to knowledges as objects, dharma-knowledge has
> nine knowledge-categories as its possible object-field, with
> anvaya-knowledge excluded.

This follows Pradhan 403|08–403|18. The first part concerns
`smṛtyupasthānasaṃgraha`, inclusion under mindfulness-applications;
the final clause concerns `ālambana`, knowledge as object. Neither
is a further claim about meditative level or domain-support.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Knowing receives further determination through the mindfulness
> classification appropriate to its objects. Cessation has the
> dharma classification; other-mind knowledge admits the three
> mental classifications; the remaining knowledge field admits
> all four. The inquiry then turns to knowing itself as a possible
> object, beginning with dharma-knowledge's nine-category range.

The dharma of `dharmasmṛtyupasthāna` and the technical designation
`dharmajñāna` occupy different roles. Mindfulness of dharmas is
one of four applications; dharma-knowledge is one of the ten
knowledge-designations. Shared vocabulary does not make these
classifications interchangeable.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Smṛtyupasthāna` — establishment / application of mindfulness.**
The fourfold classification through body, feeling, mind, and dharmas.
The Bhāṣya presents inclusion under these applications, not merely
four optional attitudes that can be chosen independently of the
knowledge's object.

**`Dharmasmṛtyupasthāna` — mindfulness of dharmas.** The sole
classification of cessation-knowledge. Its field cannot be reduced
to conditioned mental events: cessation is included here. The
present passage does not enumerate its entire extension.

**`Vedanā`, `citta`, and `dharma` applications.** The three under
which other-mind knowledge falls. Its ability to know feeling,
mind, and other mental factors explains the differentiated
classification; it does not imply simultaneous apprehension of
them. VAK 7.11's one-entity-at-a-time restriction remains intact.

**`Śeṣāṇi` — the remaining knowledges.** All knowledge-designations
other than cessation and other-mind knowledge. The fourfold range
belongs to these categories considered broadly. A particular
instance retains the more specific qualifications it also bears.

**`Dhī`, `prajñā`, `jñāna` — knowing, discriminative knowing,
knowledge.** The explicit `paryāyāḥ` statement resolves the verse's
terminology locally. It does not redefine every prajñā as knowledge
without regard to VAK 7.01's exclusions.

**`Dharma-dhī` — dharma-knowledge.** The technical knowledge-category,
not unrestricted “knowledge of all dharmas.” Its knowledge-object
range excludes anvaya-knowledge according to the prose.

**`Gocara` / `ālambana` — object-range / object-support.** The
kārikā and prose expressions for the final relation. The nine
count possible knowledge-category objects, not nine cognitions
necessarily known together or nine new kinds of knowledge.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Knowledge category | Mindfulness classification | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Cessation | Dharmas | 1 |
| Other-mind | Feeling, mind, dharmas | 3 |
| Remaining categories, broadly considered | Body, feeling, mind, dharmas | 4 |

The overlapping taxonomy requires specific restrictions to persist:

```text
DharmaKnowledge(x) AND CessationKnowledge(x)
    → DharmaMindfulnessClassification(x)
```

The broader fourfold range of dharma-knowledge does not make that
cessation-knowledge instance body-, feeling-, or mind-mindfulness.

The final pāda begins a different table:

```text
KnowledgeObjectCategories(DharmaKnowledge)
    = TenKnowledgeCategories minus {AnvayaKnowledge}
```

Those nine names are conventional, dharma, suffering, origination,
cessation, Path, other-mind, exhaustion, and non-arising knowledge.
This category-level range is subject to the distinctions already
established: it does not admit every individual instance under
every overlapping name without qualification.

```text
hasMindfulnessClassification
    is distinct from
hasKnowledgeAsObject
```

Dharma-knowledge's inclusion among its own possible object-categories
does not establish same-moment self-cognition. A category may be
both knowing category and object-category through different instances.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Two classificatory transitions.** The Bhāṣya declares level and
support complete, announces mindfulness-inclusion, and then asks
how many knowledges serve as objects of each knowledge. The verse
therefore joins two successive inquiries without merging them.

**Mental factors remain individually knowable.** The research
kārikā study's wording about another mental event “together with”
its associated factors can suggest simultaneous apprehension.
The threefold classification concerns the range across possible
objects. The one-entity rule of VAK 7.11 continues to apply.

**Anvaya is excluded explicitly.** The prose gives the exclusion
directly but does not develop a new causal explanation for it in
this local sentence. The official study retains the rule without
reducing it to an unrestricted inference from domain vocabulary.
The further object-counts belong to VAK 7.17.

**Textual caution.** The Devanāgarī/IAST discrepancy in the final
compound is recorded above. The readable prose gives
`dharmajñānasya`, making the intended relation secure. The study
does not silently alter the research witnesses.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our Yoga
system gains a differentiated account of attention within knowing.
Yoga Vidyā articulates the object and the mindfulness classification
appropriate to it. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, develops
the practice under those determinations. A broad capacity's name
does not remove the conditions of the particular cognitive act.

The final clause adds the possibility of knowing knowledge. Our
reconstruction can develop reflective examination as a determinate
practice whose object may itself be a cognitive occurrence. That
relation requires its own range and exclusions; it is not licensed
as unrestricted access merely by calling it reflection.

Vidyā and Technē thus meet both in the disciplined attention of
the practice and in the examination of its knowing. The source
supplies specific classifications and the first knowledge-object
entry. Their further development as a Yoga system remains our
explicit Organon interpretation.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The one/
three/four distribution and nine-category object-range are explicit.
The Sanskrit script discrepancy is marked, and the wider object
matrix remains for the next verse.
