# VAK_7.19 Bhāṣya — Progressive Possession of Knowledge

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ekajñānānvito rāgī prathame 'nāsravakṣaṇe |
> dvitīye tribhir ūrdhvas tu caturṣv ekaikavṛddhimān || 7.19 ||

> One still attached to the desire domain possesses one knowledge
> at the first uncontaminated moment, suffering-dharma acceptance:
> conventional knowledge. At the second, he possesses three:
> conventional, dharma, and suffering knowledge. At four later
> moments his possession grows by one knowledge each time, reaching
> seven at Path-dharma knowledge.

The new question asks who possesses how many knowledge kinds. The
7.17–7.18 Support tables are complete; cultivation begins in 7.20.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.19.md`](./VAK_7.19.md).

## 3. Continuous Translation

> This is to be stated: who is possessed of how many knowledges?
>
> The ordinary person, first, is possessed of one: conventional knowledge.
> The detached person, however, also possesses Knowledge of another's
> Consciousness. The noble practitioner, in turn:
>
> “One still attached is endowed with one knowledge at the first
> uncontaminated moment.” One not detached from the desire domain,
> at suffering-dharma acceptance, is possessed of conventional
> knowledge alone.
>
> “At the second, with three”: at suffering-dharma knowledge, with
> conventional, dharma, and suffering knowledge.
>
> “Subsequently, at four [moments], he increases by one each time”
> [local `catuṣrvekaika-`]. After that, an increase of one knowledge
> each is to be understood at four moments. At suffering-anvaya
> knowledge, anvaya-knowledge increases. At origination-, cessation-,
> and Path-dharma knowledge, origination-, cessation-, and Path-knowledge
> increase; at Path-dharma knowledge he is possessed of seven knowledges.
>
> The detached practitioner is to be understood throughout as
> possessed of one more: Knowledge of another's Consciousness.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
ordinary baseline: conventional
    + Knowledge of another's Consciousness if already detached
Seeing, not previously detached:
    moment 1 kṣānti → still 1 (conventional); acceptance is not jñāna
    moment 2 suffering-dharma-knowledge → 3
    then +1 at four named moments → 7 at Path-dharma-knowledge
detached case: +Knowledge of another's Consciousness throughout
```

## 5. Possession ≠ Present Act ≠ Cultivation

One present knowledge at moment 2 instantiates two new categories (dharma
and suffering). Conventional knowledge remains possessed without being the
present Path knowledge. 7.01 still excludes uncontaminated acceptances
from `jñāna`.

Knowledge of another's Consciousness possessed by the detached practitioner
does not occur in Seeing (7.06). Possession and present occurrence differ.

Moment numbers 4, 6, 10, 14 are from the established sixteen-moment sequence,
not printed in this prose.

## 6. The Seven (Non-Detached, by Path-Dharma-Knowledge)

Conventional, dharma, anvaya, suffering, origination, cessation, Path.
The two terminal knowledges and Knowledge of another's Consciousness
are absent from this non-detached series.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `rāgī` = kāmāvītarāga;
- first moment = duḥkhadharmajñānakṣānti;
- `tribhiḥ` named;
- four increases named;
- detached +1 is commentary contrast, not a second verse subject.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is reconstruction.

The Organon can represent possession, present occurrence, and later
cultivation as distinct relations. This sequence shows why an
uncontaminated acceptance does not add a Knowledge category, while
the second moment adds two categories through one present act. The
detached practitioner's additional possession does not place Knowledge
of another's Consciousness in the Path of Seeing.
