## 4. Grammar

**Scope of the first predicate.** The running source
introduces this verse with “these five higher knowledges.”
Thus `svādhobhūviṣayāḥ` resumes the five non-exhaustion
capacities from VAK 7.43. The Condition-range rule is relative to the
ground of the particular capacity, not simply to the
highest attainment its bearer has ever possessed.

**Acquisition with a qualification.** `Labhyāḥ` and
`ucitāḥ` agree with the understood feminine plural
`abhijñāḥ`. The Bhāṣya determines `ucitāḥ` as
`janmāntarābhyastāḥ`, cultivated in previous lives.
`Virāgataḥ` gives the means or condition of acquisition.
The sentence does not say that practice is unnecessary:
the next prose statement explicitly requires application
for the production of all five.

**The third and the first.** `Tṛtīyā` numbers a higher
knowledge: the third is knowledge of another's consciousness. `Ādyam`
numbers a mindfulness-application: the first is body
mindfulness. They belong to different enumerations.
The latter must not be translated as “the first higher
knowledge” and then added as an extra member of the list.

**Constitution through mindfulness.** `Trīṇi upasthānāni`
is a neuter plural nominal predicate of the feminine
singular `tṛtīyā`. The differing genders are appropriate
to nouns naming different classifications. The prose
identifies feeling, consciousness, and dharma mindfulness through
the consciousness-and-mental-factor object-field.

**Close construe.** [The five] have their own and lower
levels as cognitive Conditions. The previously cultivated ones,
however, are obtainable through detachment. The third
[is constituted by] three mindfulness-applications;
[extraordinary efficacy, divine hearing, and divine sight
are] the first application. The final bracketed subject
is the Bhāṣya's expansion of the uncertain verse wording.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> They have their own and lower levels as their Conditions. The previously
> cultivated ones, however, are obtainable through detachment. The third is
> three applications of mindfulness. [Extraordinary efficacy, divine hearing,
> and divine sight are] the first application.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five [higher knowledges] have their own and lower levels as Conditions.
> Those cultivated in previous lives, however, are acquired through detachment.
> The third [higher knowledge comprises] three applications of mindfulness;
> [extraordinary efficacy, divine hearing, and divine sight comprise] the first
> [application].


## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The reach of a cultivated knowledge remains relative
> to its ground. Its acquisition-history, conditions of
> production, and direct object-field specify different
> aspects of its capacity. A determination available
> alongside its exercise need not belong to what that
> capacity itself directly knows.

The final distinction is made explicit by the Bhāṣya's
account of divine sight and the accompanying knowledge
that determines conduct. The association of cognitions
does not erase the distinction between their objects
and functions.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Svādhobhūviṣaya`.** Having one's own and lower levels
as cognitive **Conditions** (*viṣaya*). The Bhāṣya applies this to going and
creating through ṛddhi, hearing sound, knowing another
consciousness, recollecting former existence, and seeing death
and rebirth. The rule determines vertical range; it is
not a guarantee of unrestricted access to every particular
object within that range.

**`Janmāntarābhyasta`.** Cultivated in another birth or
previous lives. This prose qualification determines the
verse's `ucita`; generic familiarity is too loose here.

**`Virāga / vairāgya`.** Detachment or dispassion. Here
it conditions acquisition of previously cultivated
capacities. It is not defined as the automatic manifestation
of every power whenever attachment is reduced.

**`Prayoga`.** Deliberate application or preparatory effort.
The prose distinguishes acquisition through detachment
from production through application. “Activation” is a
possible project analogy for the latter, not its literal
technical definition.

**`Vaiśeṣikyaḥ`.** Special or superior capacities, qualifying
higher knowledges in the prose. Their acquisition is
through application. “Special excellence” expresses the
contrast but should not become a numerical scale absent
from the passage.

**`Smṛtyupasthāna`.** Application or establishment of
mindfulness. Here it classifies cognition by its object:
body, feeling, consciousness, and dharmas. It does not prescribe
three successive diagnostic questions for every instance
of knowledge of another's consciousness.

**`Rūpālambana`.** Having material form as **Support**. In the
body-mindfulness classification this includes sound and
other material Essences; it is wider than visible
form alone.

**`Abhijñāparivārajñāna`.** **Knowledge** accompanying a
higher knowledge. The Bhāṣya specifies a distinct cognition
arising in noble persons through which the relevant
judgment about conduct is known.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Condition range relative to level.**

| Capacity | Application of the own-or-lower rule |
|---|---|
| Extraordinary efficacy | Going to or creating at its own or a lower ground |
| Divine hearing | Hearing sounds belonging to its own or a lower ground |
| knowledge of another's consciousness | No apprehension of a higher-ground consciousness |
| Former-abode recollection | No recollection of the higher-ground object excluded here |
| Death-and-rebirth knowledge | No seeing of a higher-ground object |

The source expressly derives the exclusion of formless-
level consciousness from knowledge of another's consciousness and former-abode
recollection: those levels lie above their dhyāna grounds.
This supplies a Condition-range limit in addition to the
acquisition account of VAK 7.43. It does not deny every
kind of knowledge concerning the formless domains.

**Acquisition and production.**

```text
Previously cultivated in another birth:
    acquisition through detachment

Special higher knowledges:
    acquisition through application

All five:
    production through application
```

These are qualified relations. They do not justify the
research kārikā's general formula that every acquisition
requires both prior-life cultivation and detachment.

**Mindfulness constitution and material fields.**

| Higher knowledge | Mindfulness constitution | Support determination here |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge of another's consciousness | Feeling, consciousness, dharmas | Consciousness and mental factors |
| Extraordinary efficacy | Body | Four external material Essences, excluding sound |
| Divine hearing | Body | Sound Essence |
| Divine sight / death and rebirth | Body | Visible-form Essence |
| Former-abode recollection | All four | Remaining case without a narrower restriction |
| Influx-exhaustion | All four | Remaining case without a narrower restriction |

The four external fields for ṛddhi are visible form,
smell, taste, and the tangible. “External” here refers
to object-side āyatanas. It does not make the dharma
Essence a fifth material object or reduce body
mindfulness to contemplation of one's anatomical body.

The threefold classification of knowledge of another's consciousness
follows its consciousness-and-mental-factor objects: feeling has
its own mindfulness class, consciousness has its own, and other
mental factors fall under dharma mindfulness. These
classes do not imply three simultaneous mental acts.

**The scriptural objection and reply.** The objection
cites descriptions in which knowledge of death and
rebirth knows beings as endowed with bodily misconduct.
If divine sight takes visible form as object, how can
it itself establish that further determination?

Vasubandhu replies that it does not know this by that
higher knowledge. A different accompanying knowledge
arises in noble persons and supplies the judgment.
The passage does not thereby explain every possible
moral judgment, or assign the same companion cognition
to every possessor of divine sight.

```text
Divine sight:
    direct object: visible form

Accompanying knowledge in noble persons:
    supplies the cited determination of conduct

Together in the described account:
    distinct cognitive functions
    no expansion of divine sight's direct object-field
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source and reconstruction.** The research kārikā's
claim that higher cognition contains the lower as a
comprehended moment is a philosophical reconstruction.
The Bhāṣya establishes an asymmetric ground-relative
range; it does not provide that explanation of hierarchy.
Likewise, the three mindfulness classes are an object-
based constitution, not an explicit teaching protocol.

**Witness cautions.** The study's `śrotrarddhir cakṣuṣi`
is an editorially segmented working form; the separate
kārikā source prints `śrotrddhircakṣuṣi`, and the running
source prints `śrotrardvivakṣuṣi`. The study also
normalizes witnessed `trīpyupasthānāni` to `trīṇy upasthānāni`.
Neither working reading is a restored witness.
The prose's explicit enumeration of ṛddhi, divine hearing,
and divine sight secures the intended group.

The running source also contains `ṛddhacyabhhijñā`, `noktarām`,
and `cittacaittālamlbanatvāt`,
alongside damaged `atucittāḥ prayogataḥ` between the
acquisition question and the quoted kārikā. [Xuanzang's parallel](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%98%BF%E6%AF%98%E9%81%94%E7%A3%A8%E4%BF%B1%E8%88%8D%E8%AB%96/%E5%8D%B7027)
confirms the contrast between previously unfamiliar exceptional powers
acquired by application and familiarized kinds acquired by detachment,
without restoring the local Sanskrit fragment. The continuous prose
secures the range, previous-birth qualification, and
application requirement; the exact damaged readings
remain for collation.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the knowledge-field, its admissible
range, and the difference between direct knowledge and
accompanying determination. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma
companion, articulates cultivation-history, acquisition,
and the application required for production.

Their relation is concrete here. A developed capacity
must be understood both through what it knows and through
the conditions under which it becomes available. Moreover,
the determination of a situation can involve distinguishable
cognitions working together. The directly perceived field
does not absorb the companion judgment merely because
both contribute to one practical account.

For Organon representation, this supports recording the
capacity's ground, object-field, acquisition conditions,
and companion cognition separately, with explicit relations
between them. This is a project use of the source distinctions;
it does not identify the Bhāṣya's cognitive powers with
a software implementation or establish an empirical model
of their operation.
