# VAK_7.12 — Fourteen Aspects and the Disputed Limit of Sixteen

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
शेषे चतुर्दशाकारे शून्यानात्मविवर्जिते ।
नामलः षोडशभ्योऽन्य आकारोऽन्येऽस्ति शास्त्रतः ॥ ७.१२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
śeṣe caturdaśākāre śūnyānātmavivarjite |
nāmalaḥ ṣoḍaśabhyo 'nya ākāro 'nye 'sti śāstrataḥ || 7.12 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The remaining two
knowledges are exhaustion and non-arising. The second half introduces
a disagreement about whether the sixteen exhaust uncontaminated
aspects; the Bhāṣya identifies the positions and tests the textual
argument for a further aspect.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
śeṣe | caturdaśa-ākāre | śūnya-anātma-vivarjite |
na | amalaḥ | ṣoḍaśabhyaḥ | anyaḥ | ākāraḥ |
anye | asti | śāstrataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `śeṣe` | nominative dual neuter adjective, used substantivally | the remaining two [knowledges] |
| `caturdaśākāre` | nominative dual neuter possessive compound | having fourteen aspects |
| `śūnyānātmavivarjite` | nominative dual neuter participial compound | excluding emptiness and non-self [aspects] |
| `na` | negative particle | not |
| `amalaḥ` | nominative singular masculine adjective | stainless, uncontaminated; qualifies aspect |
| `ṣoḍaśabhyaḥ` | ablative plural numeral | other than the sixteen |
| `anyaḥ` | nominative singular masculine adjective | another |
| `ākāraḥ` | nominative singular masculine | aspect |
| `anye` | nominative plural masculine pronoun | others [say] |
| `asti` | third-person singular present of `√as` | exists |
| `śāstrataḥ` | adverbial formation in `-tas` | on the authority of the treatise |

`Na + amalaḥ` yields `nāmalaḥ`. The negation denies another
uncontaminated aspect; it does not qualify an aspect as impure.
The ending `-e` in the opening three forms is dual nominative here,
not locative singular. The prose explicitly supplies the two knowledges.

## 4. Grammar

**The remaining pair.** The first half is a nominal sentence:

```text
śeṣe [kṣayānutpādajñāne]
    caturdaśākāre
    śūnyānātmavivarjite
```

The two predicates agree with the dual subject. The Bhāṣya names
exhaustion- and non-arising-knowledge and expands the exclusion with
`śūnyānātmākārau varjayitvā`, leaving out the emptiness and non-self
aspects. This is a determinate remainder in the chapter's distribution.

**The negative thesis.** Supply `asti` in the first of the opposed
statements:

```text
ṣoḍaśabhyaḥ anyaḥ amalaḥ ākāraḥ na [asti]
```

`Ṣoḍaśabhyaḥ` is the ablative governed by the comparison with `anyaḥ`.
The Bhāṣya attributes this thesis to the Kashmiris.

**The alternative thesis.** `Anye` introduces a different speaker:

```text
anye [āhuḥ]: asti [anyaḥ amalaḥ ākāraḥ], śāstrataḥ
```

The singular existential statement admits an aspect beyond the sixteen;
it does not establish a complete seventeen-member inventory. The prose
identifies these others as teachers outside Kashmir and supplies their
appeal to a śāstra passage.

**Close construe.** The remaining two [are] fourteen-aspected, excluding
emptiness and non-self. No uncontaminated aspect other than the sixteen
[exists]. Others [say]: one exists, on the authority of the treatise.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The remaining two have fourteen aspects, emptiness and non-self
> being excluded. No uncontaminated aspect exists beyond the sixteen;
> others say that one does, on the authority of the treatise.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Exhaustion-knowledge and non-arising-knowledge have fourteen aspects:
> the sixteen with emptiness and non-self left out. Although ultimate,
> these knowledges participate in conventional expression, as subsequent
> statements concerning exhausted birth and no further existence are
> made on their strength. As to whether an uncontaminated own-
> characteristic aspect exists beyond the sixteen, the Kashmiris deny
> it. Teachers outside Kashmir affirm it, appealing to a passage in
> the analytical treatise.

The first explanation follows Pradhan 399|01–399|04; the disputed
extension and its textual examination occupy 399|05–399|20. The
Bhāṣya argues about how the cited passage should be construed rather
than leaving the disagreement as two unsupported assertions.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The two terminal knowledges receive a precise aspect-range, with
> two aspects excluded. The account connects their range to the
> conventional articulation of accomplishment supported by them.
> A further question then concerns the completeness of the established
> system: must every uncontaminated aspect fall within the sixteen,
> or does the treatise attest another mode of knowing?

The exclusion of two aspects from these knowledges does not amount
to asserting the opposites of emptiness and non-self. It restricts
their mode of operation. Likewise, the ordinary first-person form
of the supported statement does not itself establish a permanent
subject as a doctrinal conclusion.

The numerical result and the disputed completeness claim have
different scopes. Fourteen follows from the specified subtraction;
whether sixteen is exhaustive requires a further argument.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Śeṣe` — the remaining two.** Specifically exhaustion- and
non-arising-knowledge, after the preceding distributions. The dual
is essential to both the reference and the grammar.

**`Śūnya` / `anātman` — empty / non-self.** Here the names of two
excluded aspects within the sixteenfold field. Their detailed
determinations belong to the ensuing aspect exposition. The local
exclusion should not become a general denial of their doctrinal role.

**`Saṃvṛtibhajana` — participation in conventional expression.** The
Bhāṣya's explanation of the terminal knowledges' relation to statements
of completion. `Pāramārthikayor api`, “although the two are ultimate,”
preserves the distinction between their status and conventional usage.

**`Tadbalānuvyavahāra` — subsequent expression on their strength.**
The source's formulation is transmitted with `tadvala-`. The expression
relates the knowledge to the later declaration it supports. It resumes
the explanatory line encountered in VAK 7.07 without erasing the
alternative account preserved in that earlier discussion.

**`Anāsravaḥ svalakṣaṇākāraḥ` — an uncontaminated own-characteristic
aspect.** The prose's explicit question. VAK 7.11 assigned own-
characteristic apprehension to contaminated other-mind knowledge;
the present discussion asks whether an uncontaminated aspect outside
the sixteen is admitted at all.

**`Śāstrataḥ` — from the treatise.** The appeal concerns a specific
Abhidharma analytical passage, not an unspecified sūtra or generic
scriptural testimony. The interpretation of that passage is itself
examined.

**`Bahirdeśaka` — teacher from outside Kashmir.** The proponents of
the affirmative position in the prose. The geographic attribution
is explicit; a more specific school identification is not needed
to represent this local disagreement.

**`Anyatrāvacana` — absence of the expression elsewhere.** The ground
given against the proposed weaker reading of the treatise: the same
wording is absent in a parallel passage where that reading would
lead one to expect it.

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
AspectRange(ExhaustionKnowledge)
    = SixteenTruthAspects minus {EmptinessAspect, NonSelfAspect}

AspectRange(NonArisingKnowledge)
    = SixteenTruthAspects minus {EmptinessAspect, NonSelfAspect}

16 − 2 = 14
```

These are knowledge-kind ranges, not fourteen simultaneous aspects
of each act. The absence of an aspect is not the assertion of its
contradictory opposite.

The two positions on completeness are separately attributed:

| Position | Claim | Source ground |
|---|---|---|
| Kashmiris | No uncontaminated aspect outside the sixteen | Explicitly stated doctrinal boundary |
| Teachers outside Kashmir | An additional uncontaminated aspect exists | Interpretation of the cited śāstra |

The treatise passage describes a domain-unconnected cognition knowing
desire-domain dharmas under familiar truth-aspects and includes
`asty etat sthānam, asty etad vastu`, approximately “there is this
case/ground; there is this entity.” Two construals are tested:

```text
Aspect reading:
    the formulas express a mode under which the cognition knows

Proposed weaker reading:
    the formulas merely state that a case of knowing in the
    previously listed ways is possible
```

The reply rejects the weaker reading by comparison with a passage
about seeing-abandonable cognition and its distorted aspects. That
parallel does not insert the same existential formula. The stated
expectation under the weaker reading is therefore not met.

This supports the textual argument for extension without supplying
a complete new taxonomy or an unqualified final theory of all
uncontaminated cognition.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Exact reference replaces an indefinite remainder.** The research
kārikā file left `śeṣe` open and treated the opening forms as locatives.
The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies a nominative dual subject. The
official study incorporates that determination while preserving the
original research file.

**The dispute is examined.** Vasubandhu's exposition presents the
Kashmiri restriction and the external teachers' appeal to śāstra.
The following objection proposes a less expansive construal of the
quotation; the reply tests it against parallel usage and rejects it.
The discussion therefore provides more than a report of disagreement,
while stopping short of enumerating a newly expanded aspect-system.

**Conventional articulation and its ground.** The declaration about
exhausted birth is made on the strength of the terminal knowledge.
The study retains that relation without translating an aspect-
exclusion into a claim that realization is constituted by ordinary
verbal self-description. VAK 7.07 remains the place where the
different accounts of such explicit knowing were distinguished.

**Textual caution.** The raw e-text includes `śīṇā`, `tadvala-`,
`śranye`, `vahirdeśakāḥ`, and `yogavihitatī vijānoyāt`. The argument
is recoverable from the question, quoted formulas, objection, and
parallel comparison, but the damaged strings are not silently
presented as critically established Sanskrit. The working English
for `sthāna` and `vastu` remains modest and contextual.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our Yoga
system must distinguish the accomplishment of work, the knowing that
supports its declaration, and the form in which that declaration
is expressed. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma activity, brings the
question of completed and secured work; Yoga Vidyā articulates the
grounds and limits of the knowledge through which completion is
understood.

The second half adds a methodological requirement for their joint
development. An established classification may need extension, but
the proposed extension must be tested through the source's actual
relations and usage. Here the test is concrete: does a phrase name
an aspect of knowing or merely assert the possibility of a case?
That semantic distinction changes what the text licenses us to add.

In our reconstruction, the fourteenfold determination and the
dispute over sixteen therefore perform different work. One fixes
the range of specified capacities; the other tests the completeness
of the system used to articulate them. Preserving both makes the
Vidyā–Technē project capable of exact determination and reasoned
revision.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The remaining
pair and its fourteen aspects are explicit. The competing completeness
claims remain attributed, and no definitive seventeen-aspect system
is inferred. Minor corruptions in the prose remain open for review.
