# VAK_7.15 — Meditative Level and Basis of Actualization

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ध्यानेष्वन्यमनोज्ञानं कामरूपाश्रयं च तत् ।
कामाश्रयं तु धर्माख्यमन्यत्त्रैधातुकाश्रयम् ॥ ७.१५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dhyāneṣv anyamanojñānaṃ kāmarūpāśrayaṃ ca tat |
kāmāśrayaṃ tu dharmākhyam anyat traidhātukāśrayam || 7.15 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The opening completes
the level distribution from VAK 7.14; the rest specifies Basis.
The Bhāṣya closes by declaring the determination of `bhūmi` and
`āśraya` complete, confirming that these are distinct relations.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |

| `anyamanojñānam` | nominative singular neuter compound (`anya-mano-jñāna`) | Knowledge of another's Consciousness; the prose uses `paracittajñāna` |
| `kāmarūpāśrayam` | nominative singular neuter possessive compound (`kāma-rūpa-āśraya`) | having a desire- or form-domain Basis for actualization |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `tat` | nominative singular neuter pronoun | that [Knowledge of another's Consciousness] |

| `tu` | contrastive particle | whereas, however |
| `dharmākhyam` | nominative singular neuter adjective (`dharma-ākhyam`) | that called dharma-[knowledge] |

`Manas` appears as `mano-` in the compound; `rūpa + āśraya` yields
`rūpāśraya`. The domains in the Basis compounds concern the
knower's Basis, distinct from the Support of the Consciousness known.

## 4. Grammar

**Completion of the level account.** The initial nominal clause is:

```text
anyamanojñānam — dhyāneṣu
```

The Bhāṣya specifies `caturṣv eva dhyāneṣu nānyatra`: in the four
dhyānas only, nowhere else. The preliminary and intermediate
concentrations included in Dharma-knowledge's six levels are not
therefore included for Knowledge of another's Consciousness.

**A second predicate of the same knowledge.** `Tat` resumes
`anyamanojñānam`; `ca` adds `kāmarūpāśrayam`. The prose explains
this through the passive `saṃmukhīkriyate`, “is made present”:

```text
kāmarūpadhātvoḥ
    tat paracittajñānaṃ saṃmukhīkriyate
```

The Knowledge itself is the subject of actualization. The observed
Consciousness is not the grammatical subject of this clause.

**Dharma-knowledge's Basis.** `Dharmākhyam` has `jñānam`
understood and receives `kāmāśrayam` as predicate. The commentary
adds `eva` and explicitly excludes actualization in the form and
formless domains. This Basis condition coexists with the six
meditative levels established in VAK 7.14.

**The remaining field.** `Anyat` excludes Knowledge of another's
Consciousness and Dharma-knowledge: eight designations remain.
The raw explanatory phrase is compressed; the verse's contrast and
[Xuanzang's Chinese parallel](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%98%BF%E6%AF%98%E9%81%94%E7%A3%A8%E4%BF%B1%E8%88%8D%E8%AB%96/%E5%8D%B7026)
support this working reading without restoring local Sanskrit letters.
As knowledge categories overlap, the remainder must
be interpreted with the already stated restrictions intact.

**Close construe.** Knowledge of another's Consciousness [is] in the
dhyānas, and that [Knowledge has] a desire-or-form Basis.
Dharma-[knowledge], however, [has] a desire-domain Basis; the other
eight [have possible] Bases across the three domains.

The Bhāṣya's language of being made present explains `āśraya` as Basis.
It concerns actualization, without denying every possible
relation of possession to knowledge that is not currently made present.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Knowledge of another's Consciousness is in the dhyānas and has a
> desire- or form-domain Basis. Dharma-knowledge, however, has a
> desire-domain Basis; the rest have Bases across the three domains.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Knowledge of another's Consciousness belongs to the four dhyānas alone
> and is made present with a desire- or form-domain Basis. Dharma-knowledge
> is made present only with a desire-domain Basis; the other eight knowledge
> designations have possible Bases across the three domains.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A knowledge's meditative level and the support from which it
> becomes actual must be specified separately. Other-mind knowledge
> has the four dhyānas as its levels and desire- or form-domain
> support for its actualization. Dharma-knowledge retains its
> six-level distribution while requiring desire-domain support.
> The remaining field admits support throughout the three domains.

There is no contradiction between a six-level dharma-knowledge
range and a single-domain support condition. They concern different
relations. Nor does counteraction across three domains, established
under conditions in VAK 7.09, remove the present support restriction.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Knowledge field | Meditative level | Domain-support for actualization |
|---|---|---|
| Other-mind knowledge | Four dhyānas only | Desire or form |
| Dharma-knowledge | Six levels, from VAK 7.14 | Desire only |
| Remaining field, with these exclusions preserved | As previously specified | Across the three domains |

The two specific rules are:

```text
ActualOtherMindKnowledge(x)
    → Level(x) belongs to FourDhyanas
    AND SupportDomain(x) belongs to {Desire, Form}

ActualDharmaKnowledge(x)
    → SupportDomain(x) = Desire
```

The remainder's general support-range must not override either
specific rule in an overlapping classification:

```text
TruthKnowledge(x) AND DharmaKnowledge(x)
    → DesireDomainSupport(x)
```

An individual knowledge therefore requires at least these distinct
relations to be kept apart:

```text
hasMeditativeLevel
hasActualizationSupport
hasObjectSupport
hasCounteractiveRange
```

From other-mind knowledge's desire/form support one cannot infer
that its only objects are desire/form minds. VAK 7.11 also included
uncontaminated cognition in its possible object-field. From a
three-domain counteractive range one cannot infer three-domain
actualization support for dharma-knowledge.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Correction of the earlier object reading.** The exploratory
kārikā study interpreted `kāmarūpāśrayam` partly through the minds
apprehended. The Bhāṣya's subject and verb are explicit: that
other-mind knowledge is made present in the two domains. The
official study incorporates this correction and preserves the
research witness unchanged.

**Continuity with the earlier distributions.** The four-dhyāna
restriction completes the knowledge-level account left open in
VAK 7.14. The following support clauses add another classification.
The concluding `kṛto bhūmyāśrayanirdeśaḥ`, the determination of
level and support is complete, makes their distinction visible
in the source itself.

**Textual caution.** The raw quotation has `dharmāruyam` where
the local kārikā reads `dharmākhyam`, and the remainder gloss
reads `paracittajñānaṃ dharmajñānanirmuktam`. The latter is not
a cleanly transmitted coordination; the working sense excludes
other-mind and dharma-knowledge in accordance with the local
Bhāṣya analysis and the verse structure. Exact restoration remains
open. The positive support clauses are directly stated in prose.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our Yoga
system must distinguish a capacity's formal level from the support
through which it becomes actual. Yoga Vidyā articulates those
relations and their limits. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion,
develops the undertaking under the support conditions appropriate
to the capacity being exercised.

This gives actualization a determinate place in their companionship.
A capacity's name, object, or counteractive reach is insufficient
to say where it can be brought into presence. The practitioner-
support and the cognitive level must also be specified. Conversely,
being supported in a domain does not identify everything that
the cognition may know or counteract.

For our reconstruction, successful practice requires these
relations to work together without being reduced to a single
notion of “where.” Vidyā determines the distinctions; Technē
realizes the operation under their joint conditions. The Kośa's
specific support distribution remains the source determination
from which this project interpretation develops.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The
level/support distinction and actualization predicates are explicit.
The damaged remainder gloss remains marked for textual review.
