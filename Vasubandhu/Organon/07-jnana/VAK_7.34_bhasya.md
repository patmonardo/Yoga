# VAK_7.34 Bhāṣya — Buddha-Equality through Cause, Fruit, and Beneficence

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> saṃbhāradharmakāyābhyāṃ jagataś cārthacaryayā |
> samatā sarvabuddhānāṃ nāyurjātipramāṇataḥ || 7.34 ||

> All Buddhas are equal through accumulation, perfection of the Dharma-body,
> and activity for the world's welfare; they differ in lifespan, birth, and
> bodily measure.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Are all Buddhas alike in every respect? He says: no. “All Buddhas are equal
> through accumulation and Dharma-body, and through activity for the world's
> welfare; not with respect to lifespan, birth, and measure.”
>
> Equality of all Buddhas is by three reasons: from fulfillment of the whole
> accumulation of merit and Knowledge; from perfection of the Dharma-body;
> and by activity for the world's welfare. Difference is made by lifespan,
> birth, clan, and measure: longer or shorter life; kṣatriya or brāhmaṇa
> birth; clans such as Kāśyapa or Gautama; smaller or larger measure —
> according to the time.
>
> By attending to this threefold excellence the wise can produce intense love
> and reverence toward the Blessed Buddhas: excellence of cause, of fruit,
> and of beneficence. Excellence of cause is fourfold: practice of the whole
> accumulation of merit and Knowledge; practice over a long time;
> uninterrupted practice; and respectful practice.
>
> Excellence of fruit is fourfold: excellence of Knowledge, of abandonment,
> of effective power, and of the material body [the last two members are
> joined in the damaged witness]. Excellence of beneficence is fourfold:
> complete release from the three bad destinies and the suffering of saṃsāra;
> or establishment in the three vehicles and in a good destiny [two compressed
> formulations of fourfold benefit].
>
> Excellence of Knowledge, in turn, is fourfold: Knowledge not taught by
> another, Knowledge everywhere, Knowledge in every respect, and effortless
> Knowledge. Excellence of abandonment is fourfold: abandonment of all
> afflictions; final abandonment; abandonment even of residual traces; and
> abandonment of every obstruction to concentrations and attainments.
>
> Excellence of effective power is fourfold: mastery in creating, transforming,
> and determining external things; mastery concerning lifespan [the second
> compound is damaged]; mastery in movement through obstruction and space,
> at a distance and quickly, and in entry with smallness and largeness [the
> third compound is damaged]; and the excellence of various distinctive
> marvelous qualities [wording damaged].
>
> Excellence of the material body is fourfold: excellence of the major marks,
> of the subsidiary marks, of strength, and of bones firm as diamond. This is
> the Buddhas' greatness in summary. When differentiated, it becomes
> infinitely various. Only Buddhas could know and state it all, were they to
> sustain their lives for many innumerable eons.
>
> Thus the Tathāgatas are treasuries of immeasurable, extraordinary qualities,
> Knowledge, effective power, and beneficence [the compound is damaged]. Yet
> foolish people, their resolve impaired by poverty of their own qualities
> [wording damaged], hear of such abundant qualities but do not honor the
> Buddha or his Dharma. The wise, by contrast, commit themselves to the
> Blessed One and his Dharma even with their very marrow.
>
> Firmly confident in them even through faith alone [the opening words are
> damaged], they overcome heaps of evils whose maturation is not fixed;
> surpassing divine and human prosperity, they become directed toward
> Nirvāṇa [the second `abhibhūya` is retained as printed]. Hence the
> Tathāgatas are called the unsurpassed field of merit: [the explanatory
> compound is damaged; it points to unfailing, desirable, excellent, swift,
> and final fruit]. For the Blessed One has said: “Those who perform even
> [uncertain] acts of honor for the Victor, the Guide, having reached a
> manifold heaven, will obtain the deathless state” [quotation damaged].
>
> These, then, are called the eighteen distinctive qualities of the Buddhas.

## 5. Respect-Relative Equality

Not one numerically identical person. Not identical biographies. Bodily
*excellence* (marks, strength, adamantine bones) ≠ uniform *size*.
`Dharmakāya` here = perfected Dharma-constitution — no later trikāya imported.

Cause four: all accumulations practiced; long; uninterrupted; respectful.
Fruit four: Knowledge, abandonment, effective power, material body.
Knowledge four: uninstructed; everywhere; in every respect; effortless —
effortless now does not cancel long preparation.
Abandonment four: all kleśas; definitive; even residual traces; obstacles
to samādhi and attainment.
Beneficence: release from three bad destinies and saṃsāra-suffering / or
establishment in three vehicles and good destiny — compressed; destinies and
vehicles not listed in the sentence.

Confidence overcoming evil specifies *unfixed* maturation. Do not drop that.
Damaged prabhāva compounds: heads named, procedures not reconstructed.

## 6. The Praise Is Argument

Reflection on the three excellences → love and reverence. Buddhas as
unsurpassed puṇyakṣetra. Attributed doctrine, not a prediction printed as fact
about a present reader's karma.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- dual + third instrumental = three grounds;
- `jagataḥ` with arthacaryā;
- `na` denies equality only under āyus-jāti-pramāṇa;
- gotra added in prose.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is reconstruction.

Idea holds Concept: same completed jñāna-constitution, different Qualitative
Concepts of life-span, clan, measure. Formation → fruit → welfare is the
science, not two dialectics. Appearance would say “all Buddhas identical” or
“therefore none equal.” Singular remains Buddha Mind, not a merged biography.
