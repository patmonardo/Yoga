# VAK_7.51 — Construction, Speech, and Sustained Manifestations

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
स्वभूमिकेन निर्माणं भाषणं त्वधरेण च ।
निर्मात्रैव सहाशास्तुः अधिष्ठायान्यवर्त्तनात् ॥ ७.५१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
svabhūmikena nirmāṇaṃ bhāṣaṇaṃ tvadhareṇa ca |
nirmātraiva sahāśāstuḥ adhiṣṭhāyānyavarttanāt || 7.51 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness, retaining
`varttanāt`. The compound junction `sahāśāstuḥ` must
be resolved through the Bhāṣya: the restriction applies
to someone other than the Teacher.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sva-bhūmikena | nirmāṇam | bhāṣaṇam | tu | adhareṇa | ca |
nirmātrā | eva | saha | a-śāstuḥ |
adhiṣṭhāya | anya-varttanāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `svabhūmikena` | instrumental singular neuter adjective here | by a mind belonging to the manifestation's own ground |
| `nirmāṇam` | nominative singular neuter here | construction |
| `bhāṣaṇam` | nominative singular neuter here | speaking or causing speech |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | however |
| `adhareṇa` | instrumental singular neuter adjective here | by a lower-ground mind |
| `ca` | additive particle | also, including same-ground causation |
| `nirmātrā` | instrumental singular of `nirmātṛ` | with the constructor |
| `eva` | restrictive particle | only |
| `saha` | indeclinable governing instrumental | together with |
| `aśāstuḥ` | genitive singular of negatively qualified `śāstṛ` | of one other than the Teacher |
| `adhiṣṭhāya` | absolutive of adhi-√sthā | having established or sustained |
| `anyavarttanāt` | ablative singular neuter compound | because of another [mind's] operation |

`Tu + adhareṇa` yields `tvadhareṇa`; `nirmātrā + eva`
yields `nirmātraiva`; `saha + aśāstuḥ` yields
`sahāśāstuḥ`. `Adhiṣṭhāya + anya` yields `adhiṣṭhāyānya-`.
The negative member in `aśāstuḥ` is essential to the
prose-controlled reading.

## 4. Grammar

**Same ground as the product.** `Svabhūmikena` supplies
`nirmāṇacittena`, “by a construction-mind.” The prose
excludes constructing a manifestation of one ground
with a construction-mind of another ground. The reference
of “own” is therefore the constructed manifestation,
not simply the producer's highest attained dhyāna.

**Speech is separately qualified.** `Bhāṣaṇam` introduces
a different operation. The Bhāṣya explicitly explains
`ca` as including `svabhūmikena`: speech may be caused
by a same-ground mind as well as by a lower-ground one.
The prose then restricts which cases take which mind;
it does not allow any arbitrarily lower mind.

**The non-Teacher restriction.** `Nirmātrā eva saha`
means “only together with the constructor.” `Aśāstuḥ`
specifies the non-Buddha case. Reading merely `śāstuḥ`
would reverse the doctrine, since the prose expressly
says `buddhād anyasya`, “of someone other than the Buddha.”

**The final causal explanation.** `Adhiṣṭhāya` supplies
an understood object, the construction. The causal
ablative then refers to another mind's operation. The
prose expands the sequence: sustain the construction
with the wish that it remain, then initiate speech
with another mind.

This answers an objection about the absence of construction-
mind when speech-mind occurs. It does not assert that
the two minds are simultaneously current.

**Close construe.** Construction [is effected] by a
same-ground construction-mind; speech, however, also
by a lower-ground mind. For one other than the Teacher,
[the manifestation speaks] only with the constructor.
[Speech remains possible] because, after sustaining
[the construction], another mind operates.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Construction is by a mind of its own ground; speech,
> however, also by a lower one. For one other than the
> Teacher, it speaks only together with the constructor.
> Having sustained it, another mind operates, explaining
> how speech is possible.

The final sentence expands the compressed causal phrase
through the prose question it answers.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A manifestation is constructed by a construction-mind
> of the same ground. A desire-domain or first-dhyāna
> manifestation is made to speak by a mind of its own
> ground; one of a higher ground is made to speak by
> a first-dhyāna mind, because the initiator of manifest
> expression is absent above that level.
>
> For someone other than a Buddha, the constructed
> manifestations speak with the constructor; if there
> are many, they speak simultaneously. A Buddha's
> manifestations may speak before or after, as he wishes.
>
> Speech does not require the construction-mind to remain
> current: the construction is sustained with the intention
> that it persist, and speech is initiated with another mind.

Source: Pradhan 427|05–428|01, local Bhāṣya checked
against the running source. The next question, whether
sustaining determination continues after death, opens
the discussion of VAK 7.52.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Producing a manifestation, sustaining it, and initiating
> speech through it are distinct operations. The product
> can remain available across a change in the current
> cognition when its persistence has been established.
> Temporal coordination with the constructor is itself
> a separate determination.

The Buddha's distinctive latitude here concerns speech
before or after his own, as desired. It is not a claim
that only the Buddha can make manifestations speak at
the same time as their constructor.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Svabhūmika`.** Belonging to the same ground. In the
construction rule the comparison is immediate producing
mind to product. This differs from VAK 7.50's comparison
of source dhyāna to resulting construction-mind.

**`Bhāṣaṇa`.** Speech or making speak. The operation
has its own cognitive ground conditions. Its separation
from construction prevents attributing all functions
of a manifestation to one undifferentiated producing mind.

**`Vijñaptisamutthāpaka`.** Initiator of manifest expression.
In this context its absence above the first dhyāna explains
the lower-ground mind needed for speech. “No speech
initiator at that ground” is not the same claim as
“no higher-ground manifestation can speak.”

**`Yugapat`.** Simultaneously. The prose applies this
to multiple manifestations of a non-Buddha constructor.
The cited verse pairs their speech with his speech
and their silence with his silence.

**`Yatheccham`.** As desired. This qualifies the Buddha's
control over the temporal arrangement. It does not give
the manifestations independent intentions or autonomous
mental continua.

**`Adhiṣṭhāna`.** Sustaining establishment or determination.
The verse uses the absolutive `adhiṣṭhāya`; the prose
specifies the intention that the construction remain.
“Persistence” captures its local function, not a universal
translation for every occurrence of the term.

**`Avasthānakāmatā`.** The wish that it remain. This
qualifies the sustaining act. It is part of the source's
answer to the proposed disappearance of the manifestation
when another mind becomes current.

**`Anyena manasā`.** With another mind. The prose identifies
the change in current cognitive operation through which
speech is initiated after sustaining the construction.
It does not say “with another person's mind.”

## 8. Logical Determination

**Construction and speech have different ground rules.**

| Manifestation's ground | Construction-mind ground | Speech-producing mind ground |
|---|---|---|
| Desire domain | Desire domain | Desire domain |
| First dhyāna | First dhyāna | First dhyāna |
| Second dhyāna | Second dhyāna | First dhyāna |
| Third dhyāna | Third dhyāna | First dhyāna |
| Fourth dhyāna | Fourth dhyāna | First dhyāna |

A higher source dhyāna can yield lower-ground construction-
minds under VAK 7.50. That does not conflict with this
table: the resulting mind must match its product's ground.
The source attainment, immediate producing mind, and
constructed manifestation are three distinct coordinates.

**Temporal coordination.**

| Constructor | Relation of manifestation speech to constructor speech |
|---|---|
| Non-Buddha | Together with the constructor; many speak simultaneously |
| Buddha | Before or after, as desired |

The cited gāthā supports the first row: when the one
speaks, the constructed forms speak with him; when he
is silent, they are all silent. The next prose sentence
specifies the Buddha's different temporal control.
“Before or after” does not imply that simultaneous
speech is prohibited for a Buddha.

**Objection and response.** The objection reasons that
when speech-mind is present construction-mind is absent,
and from that absence proposes the absence of the
constructed manifestation. The response rejects this
second inference through sustaining determination.

```text
Construct the manifestation
    → sustain it with the intention that it remain
    → initiate speech with another mind

Construction-mind no longer current
    does not by itself entail
Manifestation no longer present
```

The answer is phrased generally in the prose after the
Buddha-specific temporal contrast. It should not be turned
without further evidence into a claim that sustaining
construction across a mind change is exclusive to Buddhas.
The distinctive Buddha determination explicitly stated
here is the freedom of speech timing.

The account does not introduce simultaneous independent
cognitive agents inside the constructions. It distinguishes
the producer's operations and the sustained manifestation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Correction of the first pass.** The research kārikā's
padaccheda drops the negative in `aśāstuḥ`, and its
philosophical translation assigns exclusive synchronized
speech to the Teacher. The Bhāṣya explicitly establishes
the opposite scope: the non-Buddha case is bound to
speech with the constructor; the Buddha's manifestations
may speak before or after as desired. The original
research file remains intact.

**Witness cautions.** The running source and local
extract contain `vahūnāṃ` and `vahavo`, and irregular
spacing and quotation marks in the cited gāthā. The
source's `pūrva` in the Buddha sentence has the contextual
force of “before,” paired with `paścāt`. The contrast
is secured by complete prose statements, not inferred
from the irregular forms alone.

The verse's doubled `tt` in `varttanāt` is preserved.
The sandhi `sahāśāstuḥ` is resolved as `saha aśāstuḥ`
with explicit prose control; no alteration of the verse
is needed to retain the negative meaning.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the distinctions among production,
persistence, and speech, together with their respective
ground conditions. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion,
concerns the ordered exercise of those operations.
Their relation becomes concrete when one operation's
product remains available while another operation occurs.

For Organon, this supports distinguishing a producing
operation from the condition that maintains its result.
Changing the active operation need not remove an already
established result. That structural analogy is more
precise than describing the whole passage as parallel
execution or independent agent creation.

The source's sustaining determination is not a software
storage mechanism. Our implementation analogies should
preserve the distinctions without importing unasserted
claims about concurrency, autonomous agents, or unlimited
persistence. The next verse explicitly takes up further
limits on persistence.
