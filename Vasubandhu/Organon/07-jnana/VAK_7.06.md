# VAK_7.06 — Cognitive Range, Path-Moments, and Preparation

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तद् धर्मान्वयधीपक्ष्यम् अन्योऽन्यं दर्शनक्षणौ ।
श्रावको वेत्ति खङ्गस्त्रीन् सर्वान् बुद्धोऽप्रयोगतः ॥ ७.६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
tad dharmānvayadhīpakṣyam anyo 'nyaṃ darśanakṣaṇau |
śrāvako vetti khaṅgas trīn sarvān buddho 'prayogataḥ || 7.6 ||
```

**Textual status.** The underlying kārikā and Bhāṣya e-texts transmit `ta`
at the opening. The local kārikā study provisionally supplies `tad`; this
working reading is retained here without claiming that the exact textual
restoration is settled. The prose unambiguously establishes the reciprocal
dharma/anvaya restriction. Devanāgarī `खङ्ग` here follows the IAST `khaṅga`;
the local study instead prints `खड्ग`. This script-level difference is
recorded rather than silently treated as agreement between witnesses.

The opening reciprocal construction is compressed and textually difficult.
Its lexical decomposition does not by itself resolve every agreement or
ellipsis. The paired prose clauses provide the controlling syntax.

## 4. Grammar

**The reciprocal restriction.** Read the opening in continuity with
`na vetti tat` in VAK 7.05. The negative force is carried forward; this
verse does not independently repeat `na`. The Bhāṣya supplies the two
complete statements:

```text
dharmajñānapakṣyaṃ paracittajñānam
    anvayajñānapakṣyaṃ cittaṃ na jānāti

anvayajñānapakṣyaṃ [paracittajñānam]
    dharmajñānapakṣyaṃ [cittam] na jānāti
```

The knowing is other-mind knowledge; the target is cognition belonging to
the other side. The target need not itself be an act of other-mind knowledge.

**The two moments belong with the disciple.** A new construction begins
at `darśanakṣaṇau`, crossing the pāda boundary:

```text
darśana-kṣaṇau — śrāvakaḥ vetti
```

The disciple is the nominative subject; the two moments are accusative
objects. Attaching this dual to the preceding reciprocal restriction
would incorrectly make the moments fail to know each other.

**Parallel clauses.** Supply `vetti` after the remaining subjects and
`darśanakṣaṇān` with the plural numerals:

```text
khaṅgaḥ — trīn [darśanakṣaṇān vetti]
buddhaḥ — sarvān [darśanakṣaṇān vetti] aprayogataḥ
```

`Aprayogataḥ` qualifies the Buddha's knowing. The verse gives the numbers;
the Bhāṣya identifies the disciple's two and reports two accounts of the
Pratyekabuddha's three.

**Close construe.** That [other-mind knowledge], belonging to the dharma-
and anvaya-cognition sides, [does not know] the other side reciprocally.
Two moments of Seeing the disciple knows; three the solitary realizer;
all the Buddha, without preparatory application.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> That [other-mind knowledge], belonging to the dharma- and anvaya-cognition
> sides, [does not know] the other side reciprocally. A disciple knows two
> moments of Seeing; the rhinoceros-like solitary realizer knows three;
> a Buddha knows all, without preparatory application.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Other-mind knowledge belonging to the dharma-knowledge side does not know
> anvaya-side cognition, and anvaya-side other-mind knowledge does not know
> dharma-side cognition. Other-mind knowledge does not itself occur in the
> Path of Seeing, although that Path can be its object. Having prepared to
> know another's Seeing, a disciple knows its first two moments: acceptance
> of dharma-knowledge concerning suffering and the corresponding
> dharma-knowledge. A Pratyekabuddha knows three: according to the principal
> account, the first, second, and eighth; others say the first, second, and
> fifteenth. A Buddha knows all moments of Seeing without preparatory
> application.

The expansions and alternative attribution follow the Bhāṣya, Pradhan
393|15–394|04. “All” remains qualified by “moments of Seeing.” VAK 6.28
distinguishes its fifteen moments from the full sixteen-moment realization
sequence; the sixteenth begins Cultivation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The kind of cognition used to know another determines which cognitive
> side can become its object. Knowing the Path of Seeing also differs
> from undergoing that Path. For a prepared knower, the extent of access
> depends on whether further preparation is required while the observed
> sequence continues. The disciple apprehends two moments; the solitary
> realizer three; the Buddha apprehends the entire Seeing sequence without
> a preparatory interval.

The passage distinguishes cognitive affiliation, occurrence within a Path,
and a Path's availability as object. It then relates range to preparation.
These relations make the differing capacities determinate; the counts
alone would not explain their operation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dharmajñānapakṣya` / `anvayajñānapakṣya` — belonging to the dharma- /
anvaya-knowledge side.** The commentary applies these predicates separately
to the knowing and the target cognition. The restriction concerns the
relation between the two sides, beyond the superiority restrictions in
VAK 7.05.

**`Darśanamārga` as occurrence and as object.** The paired prose statements
are decisive: `darśanamārge paracittajñānaṃ nāsti`, other-mind knowledge
is absent in the Path of Seeing; `tadālambanaṃ tv asti`, knowledge taking
that Path as object nevertheless exists. The locative relation and the
object relation must remain distinct.

**`Darśanakṣaṇa` — moment of Seeing.** A determinate moment in the observed
Path. The disciple's two are the first suffering-dharma acceptance and
knowledge. “Two” counts objects known in the sequence, not two moments
performing reciprocal observation.

**`Prayoga` — preparatory application.** A further application is needed
to take the anvaya-side cognition as object. The Bhāṣya explains that
while the disciple undertakes this preparation, the observed practitioner
has reached the sixteenth cognition; the intervening moments are missed.
This is a relational timing explanation, not a measured duration.

**`Mṛduprayogatva` — lightness of preparatory application.** The stated
reason for the Pratyekabuddha's greater range. The principal account names
the eighth moment, origination-anvaya-knowledge, after the first two.
The alternative first/second/fifteenth enumeration belongs to “others”
and is not silently combined with the principal account.

**`Aprayogataḥ` — without preparatory application.** The Buddha's knowing
does not require the intervening preparation at issue. This local adverb
does not assert the absence of every condition, all prior cultivation,
or all activity whatsoever.

**`Khaṅga` — rhinoceros-like solitary realizer.** The Bhāṣya uses
`pratyekabuddha` in explaining the three-moment range. The retained
Sanskrit designation and the explanatory name identify the same local
subject without requiring a larger classification of solitary realizers.

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
DharmaSideParacittaKnowledge
    does not know AnvayaSideCognition

AnvayaSideParacittaKnowledge
    does not know DharmaSideCognition
```

The rule attaches to the particular side of the knowing. A practitioner
can require another preparation to know the other side; the rule does
not establish a permanent inability of that person to exercise either form.

```text
OccursWithin(ParacittaKnowledge, DarsanaMarga) = false
CanHaveAsObject(ParacittaKnowledge, DarsanaMarga) = true
```

The comparative account concerns the prepared observation described in
the prose:

| Knower | Moments known | Preparation qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Disciple | First and second | Further preparation misses the intervening sequence |
| Pratyekabuddha: principal account | First, second, eighth | Lighter preparation |
| Pratyekabuddha: other teachers | First, second, fifteenth | Alternative enumeration reported separately |
| Buddha | All moments of Seeing | Without preparatory application |

The temporal reasoning is:

```text
Knowing the next cognitive side requires further preparation
AND the observed Path continues during preparation
    → some present moments pass without being known
```

This preserves VAK 7.05's present-object restriction. “All moments” does
not mean a single act simultaneously apprehends past, present, and future
mind. The local claim concerns access throughout the articulated sequence.

The sixteenth cognition in the timing explanation marks arrival beyond
strict Seeing; it is not a reason to count sixteen Seeing-moments.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Correction of the exploratory study.** The first-pass file treats
`darśanakṣaṇau` as a reciprocal limitation between two moments. The
Bhāṣya instead identifies them as the disciple's first two objects of
knowledge. Its further distinction between occurring in Seeing and
knowing Seeing as object controls the whole paragraph.

**Scope of “all.”** The local Bhāṣya study's final summary says “all
sixteen,” but its quoted Sanskrit says all moments of Seeing. The
fifteen/sixteen distinction established in VAK 6.28 prevents importing
that numerical expansion into the official translation. Both research
witnesses remain untouched.

**Textual caution.** Besides initial `ta`, the raw prose includes
`kāmadhāturdhvadhātupratikṣālambanatvāttayoḥ`, `darjñanamārga`, and
`prayogamāra mate`. The paired restriction, the Path/object distinction,
and the enumerations are explicit in the surrounding prose. The damaged
domain-ground clause is not printed as a silently restored quotation.
Its working sense concerns the two sides' respective domain-related
counteragents as objects; its exact Sanskrit restoration remains open.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our Yoga system
can now articulate preparation as part of a knowledge-capacity's practical
determination. Yoga Vidyā distinguishes the knowing operation, its target,
and the target's place in the Path. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion,
develops the preparation through which the operation can meet that target
while it is available.

Their relation becomes concrete in the interval between preparation and
exercise. A capacity's general object-range does not ensure that an
occasion for knowing is successfully met. The Bhāṣya makes the observed
sequence's movement and the knower's preparation jointly decisive.

For our reconstruction, developed mastery therefore includes the relation
between what is intelligible, what can be undertaken, and when its exercise
is effective. The Buddha's `aprayogataḥ` supplies the source's culminating
case within this comparison. Broader identification with Absolute Knowing
belongs to further Organon argument; the local determination is already
precise in its range and preparatory mode.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The principal
and alternative Pratyekabuddha enumerations remain attributed separately.
The opening reading and damaged prose forms remain available for textual
review; the corrected clause boundary is controlled by the commentary.
