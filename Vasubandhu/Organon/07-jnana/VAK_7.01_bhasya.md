# VAK_7.01 Bhāṣya — Knowledge, View, and Acceptance

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nāmalāḥ kṣāntayo jñānaṃ kṣayānutpādadhīr na dṛk |
> tadanyobhayathāryā dhīḥ anyā jñānaṃ dṛśaś ca ṣaṭ || 7.1 ||

> The uncontaminated acceptances are not knowledge. The cognition of
> exhaustion and non-arising is not view. Noble cognition other than those
> is both. Other cognition is knowledge, and six are views.

The Bhāṣya supplies the grounds the verse compresses: non-abandonment of
the corresponding doubt; examining nature (`santīraṇa`); absence of
examining and searching disposition in the two final knowledges; resolution
of the two occurrences of “other” as remaining uncontaminated `prajñā` and
all worldly `prajñā`; and identification of the six as the five views plus
worldly right view.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.01.md`](./VAK_7.01.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Acceptances are spoken of, and knowledges are spoken of; likewise right
> view and right knowledge.
>
> Why, then, are the acceptances not knowledge, and why is right knowledge
> not necessarily view?
>
> “The uncontaminated acceptances are not knowledge.”
>
> Because the underlying tendency to doubt that is to be abandoned by them
> has not yet been abandoned.
>
> They are views, however, because their nature is discerning examination.
> And just as the acceptances are view but not knowledge, so conversely:
>
> “The cognition of exhaustion and non-arising is not view.”
>
> The knowledge of exhaustion and the knowledge of non-arising are not view,
> because their disposition is without examination and further searching.
>
> “Noble cognition other than those is both.”
>
> Uncontaminated prajñā other than the acceptances, the knowledge of
> exhaustion, and the knowledge of non-arising is both view and knowledge.
>
> “The other is knowledge.”
>
> All worldly prajñā is knowledge.
>
> “And six are views.”
>
> The five views and worldly right view.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya opens Jñānanirdeśa by refusing to treat four traditional names as
one function:

```text
kṣānti / jñāna
samyagdṛṣṭi / samyagjñāna
```

It then assigns two independent predicates — knowledge and view — across
the field of `prajñā` / `dhī`:

```text
framing pair
    → why acceptance is not knowledge
    → why acceptance is nevertheless view
    → converse: why the two final knowledges are not view
    → remaining uncontaminated prajñā is both
    → all worldly prajñā is knowledge
    → exactly six worldly kinds are also view
```

No school opponent is staged. Voice throughout is Vasubandhu's own question
and resolution. The worldly branch is not an afterthought: it prevents the
noble sequence from being read as the whole extension of `jñāna`.

## 5. Grounds of the Two Exclusions

The commentary does not define knowledge and view by a shared genus plus a
difference of degree. It gives distinct grounds.

**Acceptance excluded from knowledge.** The doubt-tendency (`vicikitsā-anuśaya`)
that this acceptance is to abandon has not yet been abandoned
(`tatpraheyasya … agrahīṇatvāt`, transmitted form). Acceptance is already
noble and uncontaminated. What it lacks is completed abandonment of the
obstacle that is its own work. The exclusion is therefore Path-positional,
not a charge of impurity.

**Acceptance included in view.** The same acceptances are views because
their nature is discerning examination (`santīraṇātmakatvāt`). View-status
here is a function of examining, not of correctness. That is why afflicted
views can later sit in the same predicate.

**Final knowledges excluded from view.** Exhaustion-knowledge and
non-arising-knowledge are knowledge, but not view, because their disposition
(`āśaya`) is without examination and further searching
(`asantīraṇa-aparimārgaṇa`). The exclusion marks completion, not defect.

The two predicates therefore cross:

| Form of prajñā | Knowledge | View | Stated ground |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uncontaminated acceptances | No | Yes | doubt not yet abandoned; examining nature |
| Exhaustion- and non-arising-knowledge | Yes | No | no examining/searching disposition |
| Remaining uncontaminated prajñā | Yes | Yes | other than the three named forms |
| Five views + worldly right view | Yes | Yes | six worldly view-kinds |
| Remaining worldly prajñā | Yes | No | all worldly prajñā is knowledge |

Knowledge-status alone does not certify rightness. The five afflicted views
are knowledge in this classification.

## 6. The Two “Others” and the Six

The verse uses `tad-anyā` and `anyā` without naming their referents. The
Bhāṣya resolves both.

```text
tad-anyā āryā dhīḥ
    = uncontaminated prajñā other than
      acceptances, exhaustion-knowledge, and non-arising-knowledge
    = both view and knowledge

anyā
    = all worldly prajñā
    = knowledge
```

`Dṛśaś ca ṣaṭ` does not restrict worldly knowledge to six items. It counts
the worldly forms that also satisfy view: the five views and worldly right
view (`laukikī samyagdṛṣṭi`). The remainder of worldly prajñā stays inside
knowledge and outside view.

This keeps the chapter's later taxonomy from collapsing `jñāna` into noble
ascertainment only. Conventional knowledge already belongs to the field that
7.01 opens.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

The Organon kārikā translation already tracks these decisions and does not
need rewriting on their account:

- `na` in the first pāda governs the predicate `jñānam`, not the purity of
  the acceptances.
- `kṣaya-anutpāda-dhīḥ` is one compound naming two knowledges.
- `ubhayathā` means as knowledge and as view.
- `dṛśaś ca ṣaṭ` counts view-kinds within worldly knowledge, not all
  knowledges.

What the Bhāṣya adds, and what must remain attributed to the commentary:

- the doubt-tendency as ground of the first exclusion;
- `santīraṇa` / `parimārgaṇa` / `āśaya` as the vocabulary of view and its
  cessation;
- the explicit expansions `anāsravā prajñā` and `laukikī prajñā`;
- the list of the six.

“Accomplished ascertainment” may explain the noble contrast between
acceptance and the two final knowledges. It is not a definition of every
`jñāna` in the verse, because worldly afflicted views are included under
knowledge. That gloss stays out of the literal translation.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction. It does not emend Sections 3–7.

Jñānanirdeśa opens after Mārga. The Path has already been determined as
efficacious cognition of the truths. 7.01 now takes the same cognitive field
and holds it as Essence: two predicates, distinct grounds, a crossed matrix.
The kārikā is the Being of the distinction — four compact clauses. The
Bhāṣya is the reflection that gives each clause its why.

Concept is not reached here. Ayatana-style capacity/field pairing is not
the model. What begins to close is only the reciprocity of *function* and
*Path-position* within `prajñā`:

```text
examining still at work, obstacle not yet abandoned
    = view, not knowledge

examining + ascertainment in the remaining noble forms
    = both

ascertainment without further search
    = knowledge, not view
```

That noble sequence is real and must not be denied. It is also not the
whole verse. Worldly prajñā is knowledge; six of its kinds are views,
including the five afflicted views. Knowledge as classified determination
is therefore wider than knowledge as liberating completion. The chapter will
have to recover the Singular — Buddha Mind as exhaustive knowing — by a later
route (powers, fearlessnesses, instruction). 7.01 only opens the Particular:
the theory of how `dhī` takes the two predicates.

For the Rational Moral Agent the same caution holds. An agent that “knows”
in the worldly sense may still be in view as affliction. Acceptance on the
Path is already examining and not yet knowledge. Final knowledge is no
longer view. Moral closure cannot be read off a single cognitive label.
