# VAK_7.26 Bhāṣya — Cultivated Levels and the History of Acquisition

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> yadvairāgyāya yallābhas tatra cādhaś ca bhāvyate |
> sāsravāś ca kṣayajñāne labdhapūrvaṃ na bhāvyate || 7.26 ||

> For whichever level detachment is sought, and whichever level is acquired,
> uncontaminated Knowledge is cultivated there and below. At Knowledge of exhaustion,
> contaminated qualities too are cultivated; what was acquired before is not
> cultivated.

New question: on which path, knowledge of how many *levels*. Category-count
was 7.22–7.25. Four senses of cultivation: 7.27.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Now, on which path is Knowledge of how many levels cultivated?
> Conventional Knowledge first: future [Knowledge] belonging to the
> level of the path and to the level first acquired is cultivated.
> Uncontaminated [Knowledge], however, is not only [of] the level
> of the path. What, then?
>
> “For detachment from whichever [level], and acquisition of whichever
> [level], [Knowledge] is cultivated there and below.” For detachment
> from whichever level, a path of either kind, beginning with the
> preparatory path, may operate; and when a level is acquired through
> detachment, uncontaminated Knowledges of that level and lower levels
> enter cultivation.
>
> “And contaminated [qualities] at exhaustion-Knowledge”: at
> exhaustion-Knowledge, contaminated qualities of all levels, acquired
> through that Knowledge, also enter cultivation: contemplation of the
> unattractive [local `a4ubhā-`, damaged], mindfulness of breathing,
> applications of mindfulness,
> immeasurables, liberations, and others. They are as if breathing out
> when a rope is cut, in the analogy of a `peḍā` [image uncertain].
> For one who has attained sovereignty over their own Consciousness,
> all wholesome dharmas come forward through their acquisitions, as
> territories come forth with offerings when sovereignty is attained
> [local `ādhirājyaporaptau`].
>
> Whatever is acquired, all that is cultivated. What is acquired for
> the first time is cultivated. “What was acquired before is not
> cultivated”: what was lost and then acquired again is not cultivated
> [local `na tt`], because it had been cultivated and relinquished.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
conventional: path-level + first-acquired level
uncontaminated: target-level + acquired-level + lower eligible levels
kṣaya: + wholesome contaminated qualities of that acquisition, all-level range
history: first acquisition counts; reacquisition after bhāvita-utsṛṣṭa does not
```

## 5. Coordinates, Not One Rule

`Sāsravāḥ` = `guṇāḥ` — wholesome contaminated qualities, not “impure knowledges,”
not unwholesome states. `Sarvabhūmika` does not give every quality every level.
Level-extension does not invent uncontaminated Knowledge where the chapter
forbade it.

`Labdhapūrvam` is not “anything already possessed.” Exact case: cultivated,
relinquished, reacquired. Possession now does not show that history.

“Whatever is acquired is cultivated” at 410|15 is immediately qualified.
Do not freeze the unqualified line.

## 6. Sovereignty Image

`Svacittādhirājya`: sovereignty over one's own Consciousness; wholesome
dharmas come forward through their acquisitions. The image compares this
with territories (*viṣaya*) bringing offerings to a sovereign; *viṣaya*
is used territorially here, rather than for the technical cognitive Condition.
Neither image says every quality is presently exercised.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- first half answers the *uncontaminated* level-question;
- conventional has its own narrower preface;
- third pāda changes subject to qualities;
- fourth pāda = reacquisition after loss.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is Organon reconstruction. Cultivation has a level relation and
an acquisition history: first establishment differs from restoration after
loss. The sovereign image gives a positive account of wholesome qualities
entering the cultivated field at exhaustion-Knowledge. The following 7.27
question tests whether cultivation is exhausted by acquisition.
