# VAK_7.29 — The Remaining Powers: Definition Determines the Count

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ध्यानाद्यक्षाधिमोक्षेषु धातौ च प्रतिपत्सु तु ।
दश वा संवृतिज्ञानं द्वयोः षट् दश वा क्षये ॥ ७.२९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dhyānādyakṣādhimokṣeṣu dhātau ca pratipatsu tu |
daśa vā saṃvṛtijñānaṃ dvayoḥ ṣaṭ daśa vā kṣaye || 7.29 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The opening
requires `nava`, “nine,” from the end of VAK 7.28. This
study completes the powers' knowledge-category classification;
their level-distribution begins in VAK 7.30.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dhyāna-ādi-akṣa-adhimokṣeṣu | dhātau | ca | pratipatsu | tu |
daśa | vā | saṃvṛti-jñānam | dvayoḥ | ṣaṭ | daśa | vā | kṣaye
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dhyānādi` | compound member | dhyānas and related attainments |
| `akṣa` | compound member | faculties, expanded as `indriya` |
| `adhimokṣeṣu` | locative plural, terminating the compound | concerning resolutions / inclinations |
| `dhātau` | locative singular masculine | concerning domain / disposition |
| `pratipatsu` | locative plural feminine of `pratipad` | concerning courses or paths |
| `tu` | particle | however |
| `daśa` | numeral | ten [knowledges] |
| `vā` | alternative particle | or |
| `saṃvṛtijñānam` | nominative singular neuter | conventional knowledge |
| `dvayoḥ` | genitive dual used here | of the two [powers] |
| `ṣaṭ` | numeral | six [knowledges] |
| `kṣaye` | locative singular masculine | concerning exhaustion [of influxes] |

`Dhyāna + ādi + akṣa` yields `dhyānādyakṣa`.
The commentary's expansion prevents reading `akṣa` as
the eyes alone or collapsing the opening compound into
one undifferentiated meditation-power.

## 4. Grammar

**The carried predicate.** Supply `nava jñānāni` for the
opening fields. The prose names four distinct powers:
knowing dhyāna and related attainments; higher and lower
faculties; diverse inclinations; and diverse domains.
Each receives the same ninefold classification.

**The path alternative.** `Pratipatsu tu daśa vā` carries
“nine” as the other alternative. The Bhāṣya explicitly
calls `vā` an indication of difference of opinion and
then resolves the scope: with the fruit, ten; without
it, nine, excluding cessation-knowledge.

**The dual.** `Saṃvṛtijñānaṃ dvayoḥ` assigns conventional
knowledge to two powers. The preserved prose here names
only recollection of former abodes. The following level
discussion pairs that power with death-and-rebirth knowledge,
securing the second member without silently repairing
the incomplete sentence.

**The exhaustion alternative.** `Kṣaye` is expanded as
`āsravakṣaya`. Six or ten depends on how the full designation
`āsravakṣayajñāna` is understood. The alternatives concern
the constitution of a Buddha-power, not the nine/ten future-
cultivation counts at exhaustion-knowledge in VAK 7.25.

**Close construe.** Concerning dhyānas and related attainments,
faculties, inclinations, and domains, [there are nine
knowledges]; concerning paths, however, ten or [nine].
Of the two [powers], [the constitution is] conventional
knowledge. Concerning influx-exhaustion, six or ten.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Concerning dhyānas and related attainments, faculties,
> inclinations, and domains, there are nine knowledges;
> concerning paths, however, ten or nine. The two powers
> consist of conventional knowledge. Concerning exhaustion,
> there are six or ten.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Four powers each comprise nine knowledge categories,
> excluding cessation-knowledge: knowing dhyānas, liberations,
> concentrations, and attainments; knowing higher and lower
> faculties; knowing diverse inclinations; and knowing
> diverse domains or dispositions.
>
> The power of knowing courses leading everywhere comprises
> ten categories if the course is understood together with
> its fruit; otherwise it comprises nine, excluding cessation-
> knowledge. Recollection of former abodes and knowledge
> of death and rebirth are conventional knowledge.
>
> The influx-exhaustion power comprises six categories if
> influx-exhaustion knowledge means knowing cessation:
> dharma, anvaya, cessation, exhaustion, non-arising, and
> conventional knowledge. If it means knowledge in the
> continuum of one whose influxes are exhausted, it comprises
> all ten categories.

Source: Pradhan 411|18–412|09. The identification of the
second conventional power is controlled by the immediate
continuation at 412|12.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The knowledge-range follows the determination of the
> power's field. Including the fruit with a course changes
> the range from nine to ten. Understanding exhaustion-
> knowledge through its object, or instead through the
> attained condition of the knowing continuum, changes
> the range from six to ten. A shared designation does
> not settle its scope without this further determination.

These alternatives do not mean that one Buddha has fewer
powers than another. They classify the same named power
under different analyses. Their numbers concern overlapping
knowledge categories, not a count of simultaneous events.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dhyānavimokṣasamādhisamāpattijñānabala`.** The power of
knowing dhyānas, liberations, concentrations, and attainments.
This is the prose's full expansion of `dhyānādi`; the four
terms should remain visible rather than being flattened
into generic meditation.

**`Indriyaparāparajñānabala`.** The power of knowing higher
and lower faculties. `Akṣa` receives its contextual meaning
through this expansion.

**`Nānādhimuktijñānabala`.** The power of knowing diverse
inclinations or resolutions. The prose uses `adhimukti`
where the verse has `adhimokṣa`. No further distinction
between those forms is asserted by this passage.

**`Nānādhātujñānabala`.** The power of knowing diverse
domains or dispositions. This occurrence must be determined
within the present power-list; it does not automatically
denote either the eighteen cognition-domains or the three
existential domains examined elsewhere in the Kośa.

**`Sarvatragāminī pratipad`.** A course leading everywhere.
The study keeps this breadth without narrowing all its
destinations to liberation. The local argument concerns
whether its fruit is included, not an enumeration of destinations.

**`Saphalā pratipad`.** A course taken together with its
fruit. This is the explicit condition for ten categories
in the path-power's analysis.

**`Kṣīṇāsravasaṃtāna`.** The continuum of one whose influxes
are exhausted. This shifts the determination from the
object known to the condition of the continuum in which
knowing occurs. It does not make every knowledge-category
identical with cessation-knowledge.

## 8. Logical Determination

The following completes the ten-power category table begun
in VAK 7.28. The first two rows are carried from that study.

| Power-field | Count | Qualification |
|---|---:|---|
| Possibility and impossibility | 10 | All categories |
| Karmic maturation | 8 | Excludes cessation and Path |
| Dhyāna and related attainments | 9 | Excludes cessation |
| Higher and lower faculties | 9 | Excludes cessation |
| Diverse inclinations | 9 | Excludes cessation |
| Diverse domains / dispositions | 9 | Excludes cessation |
| Courses leading everywhere | 10 or 9 | Includes fruit / excludes fruit |
| Recollection of former abodes | 1 | Conventional knowledge |
| Death and rebirth | 1 | Conventional knowledge |
| Influx-exhaustion | 6 or 10 | Cessation-object / exhausted-influx continuum |

The two alternative analyses require distinct conditions:

```text
CourseIncludesFruit
    → AllTenCategories
CourseExcludesFruit
    → AllTenExceptCessationKnowledge

InfluxExhaustionDeterminedAsKnowingCessation
    → {Dharma, Anvaya, Cessation, Exhaustion, NonArising, Conventional}
InfluxExhaustionDeterminedByExhaustedInfluxContinuum
    → AllTenCategories
```

The sixfold list prevents identifying “knowing cessation”
here with only one mutually exclusive slot in the tenfold
inventory. The source includes conventional knowledge too.
Its broad semantic determination and the technical category
`nirodhajñāna` must be distinguished while preserving the
source's wording and enumeration.

The broad continuum reading also does not undo Buddha-
specificity. The section concerns a Buddha's powers;
defining the knowledge-range through an influx-exhausted
continuum does not confer Buddha-power on every liberated
practitioner. VAK 7.30 asks what warrants the designation
“power” for the Buddha in particular.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Meaning before counting.** The Bhāṣya supplies the reason
for its alternatives through the scope of the expression.
For the path-power, the distinction is course-with-fruit
versus course without fruit. For influx-exhaustion, it is
knowledge determined through cessation versus knowledge
determined through the liberated continuum. These are
different semantic relations, not interchangeable examples
of a vague “broader scope.”

**The incomplete dual explanation.** At 412|05 the source
prints only `pūrvanivāsānusmṛtijñānabalaṃ ca saṃvṛtijñānam`.
The verse requires two powers. At 412|12, the source groups
former-abode and death/rebirth knowledge in the level-rule.
The latter supplies the missing identification locally;
the original incomplete line remains untouched.

**Transcription caution.** The running witness contains
`dhhimokṣeṣu`, `saḍ`, `jñānāniṃ`, and `kṣīṇasrava`.
The kārikā witness and explicit six-member enumeration
secure the readings used in the study. These defects
are not converted into alternative doctrinal categories.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
This verse closely serves our principle that meaning
emerges in a system. Yoga Vidyā must determine whether
an expression names an object-field, a course with its
fruit, or knowing within an attained continuum. Yoga
Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, preserves the relation
between the course, its accomplishment, and the cultivated
power under discussion.

The count is available only after these relations have
been resolved. A model that stores “path-power: ten” or
“exhaustion-power: six” without the defining condition
loses the argument. Our reconstruction should therefore
retain the alternative determinations as explicit analyses,
with their resulting ranges, rather than forcing one
unqualified number onto each term.

This remains an Organon interpretation of the source's
analysis. The Sanskrit supplies the conditions and counts;
it does not itself introduce our Vidyā–Technē terminology.
