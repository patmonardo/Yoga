# VAK_7.03 — The Object-Ranges of the Three Knowledges

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सांवृतं सर्वविषयं कामदुःखादिगोचरम् ।
धर्माख्यम् अन्वयज्ञानं तूर्ध्वदुःखादिगोचरम् ॥ ७.३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sāṃvṛtaṃ sarvaviṣayaṃ kāmaduḥkhādigocaram |
dharmākhyam anvayajñānaṃ tūrdhvaduḥkhādigocaram || 7.3 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The three knowledges named
in VAK 7.02 now receive their object-ranges. The Bhāṣya's qualification
`saṃbhavataḥ`, “according to possibility,” governs the universal range of
conventional knowledge.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `sāṃvṛtam` | nominative singular neuter adjective; `jñānam` understood | conventional knowledge, corresponding to `saṃvṛtijñāna` |
| `sarva-viṣayam` | nominative singular neuter possessive compound | having all objects as its possible range |
| `kāma-duḥkha-ādi-gocaram` | nominative singular neuter possessive compound | desire-domain suffering and what follows it: its origination, cessation, and counteragent |
| `dharma-ākhyam` (`dharmākhyam`) | nominative singular neuter compound adjective; `dharma + ākhyam` | called dharma-knowledge; takes the preceding range |
| `anvaya-jñānam` | nominative singular neuter compound | anvaya-knowledge |
| `tu` | contrastive particle; joined to following `ūrdhva` as `tūrdhva` | whereas, marking the second uncontaminated range |
| `ūrdhva-duḥkha-ādi-gocaram` | nominative singular neuter possessive compound | suffering in the form and formless domains, with its origination, cessation, and counteragent |

## 4. Grammar

**Conventional knowledge.** Supply `jñānam` after `sāṃvṛtam`:

```text
sāṃvṛtaṃ [jñānam] — sarvaviṣayam
```

The predicate identifies its possible object-range. It does not say that
every instance of conventional knowledge takes every object at once.

**Dharma-knowledge across the verse boundary.**
`Kāmaduḥkhādigocaram` belongs with `dharmākhyam` in the next pāda:

```text
dharma-ākhyaṃ [jñānam] — kāma-duḥkha-ādi-gocaram
```

It must not be attached to conventional knowledge as a restriction of
`sarvaviṣayam`. The Bhāṣya explicitly supplies `dharmajñānasya`, “of
dharma-knowledge,” when explaining this range.

**Anvaya-knowledge.** The final clause supplies its own subject:

```text
anvayajñānaṃ tu — ūrdhva-duḥkha-ādi-gocaram
```

`Tu` marks the contrast between the desire-domain and higher-domain ranges.
The same `duḥkhādi` structure occurs on each side.

**Bhāṣya construe.** The prose first identifies `kāmāvacaraṃ duḥkham`,
suffering belonging to the desire domain, then adds
`tat-samudaya-nirodha-pratipakṣāḥ`, its origination, cessation, and
counteragent. It repeats this construction for suffering belonging to the
form and formless domains. The domain qualification directly characterizes
suffering; the remaining terms are related to that suffering. Thus “the
four truths with respect to the desire domain” is more precise than a
claim that cessation and the uncontaminated Path are themselves
desire-domain dharmas.

**Close construe.** Conventional [knowledge]—[has] all objects [as its
range]; [knowledge] called dharma—[has] desire-domain suffering and so
forth [as its range]; anvaya-knowledge, however—[has] higher-domain
suffering and so forth [as its range].

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Conventional knowledge has all objects as its range. That called
> dharma-knowledge has desire-domain suffering and so forth as its range.
> Anvaya-knowledge, however, has higher-domain suffering and so forth as
> its range.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Conventional knowledge can take any dharma, conditioned or unconditioned,
> as its object where possible. Dharma-knowledge ranges over desire-domain
> suffering, its origination, cessation, and counteragent; anvaya-knowledge
> ranges over the same truth-series with respect to suffering in the form
> and formless domains.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The conventional field of knowing admits every kind of dharma as a
> possible object. The two uncontaminated knowledges are distinguished
> through their relation to suffering and its truth-structure: suffering,
> its origination, its cessation, and the counteragent. Dharma-knowledge
> concerns this structure with respect to the desire domain; anvaya-knowledge
> concerns the corresponding structure with respect to the higher domains.

Breadth of possible objects and uncontaminated status are different
determinations. An unconditioned object can belong to conventional
knowledge's range; its presence as an object therefore does not by itself
establish that the knowing is uncontaminated.

The correspondence between the two uncontaminated ranges preserves a common
truth-structure. It does not make their particular objects numerically
identical or establish, by itself, a method of inference from one to the other.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Knowledge | Object-range | Bhāṣya control |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional | All conditioned and unconditioned dharmas | According to possibility |
| Dharma | Desire-domain suffering, its origination, cessation, and counteragent | Domain directly qualifies suffering; the remaining members are related to it |
| Anvaya | Form- and formless-domain suffering, its origination, cessation, and counteragent | Both higher domains are gathered into this range |

For this local representation, let the relational truth-field be:

```text
TruthField(domain) =
    SufferingBelongingTo(domain)
    + ItsOrigination
    + ItsCessation
    + ItsCounteragent

ObjectRange(DharmaKnowledge) = TruthField(DesireDomain)
ObjectRange(AnvayaKnowledge) = TruthField(FormAndFormlessDomains)
```

The plus signs collect related members; they do not mean that every
knowledge-event simultaneously apprehends all four.

```text
PossibleObjectRange(ConventionalKnowledge)
    = ConditionedDharmas ∪ UnconditionedDharmas
    [qualified by saṃbhavataḥ]
```

This is a range-level statement. It should not become the event-level rule
“each conventional cognition knows every dharma.”

The possible range of conventional knowledge includes objects of the
uncontaminated knowledges. Consequently, overlapping object-ranges do not
erase the contamination-status distinction established in VAK 7.02.
Object-range differentiates the two uncontaminated forms within that
already established branch.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The qualification from VAK 7.02 receives its ground.** Conventional
knowledge apprehends conventional formations “for the most part.” The
present verse and Bhāṣya show why that explanation did not restrict its
objects to pots, cloth, and persons: even unconditioned dharmas belong to
its possible range.

**Bhāṣya control.** Pradhan 392|06–392|12 supplies the conditioned and
unconditioned expansion of `sarva`, the qualification `saṃbhavataḥ`, and
the three relatives covered by `ādi`. `Pratipakṣa` names the Path as
counteragent here. These clarifications determine the study translation;
the prose's explanatory sequence remains in the paired Bhāṣya study.

**Knowing the counteragent.** The Bhāṣya uses `pratipakṣa` in unfolding the
four truths. The account of knowledge therefore includes the means of
counteraction among its objects. Knowing a counteragent and actually
performing its operation still require their respective determinations;
this object-range statement does not identify them automatically.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Here our Yoga
system gains a more concrete relation between knowing and practice. Yoga
Vidyā articulates suffering together with its origination, cessation, and
counteragent in a determinate domain. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma
companion, develops the undertaking through which that articulated relation
becomes practical work.

Their reciprocity requires domain-specific determination. A general account
of a counteragent must acquire a determinate field of application; the
practice must in turn make intelligible what it addresses and what change
it seeks. The pairing of dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge provides a
source-grounded example of a shared truth-structure differentiated through
its domain-relations.

This is the project's reconstruction of the passage's significance. It
does not reduce the two knowledges to a generic technique of transferring
solutions between domains. Their precise Path-conditions and efficacy must
continue to be learned from the source sequence.

**Textual and review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-informed study. The raw
Bhāṣya e-text contains forms such as `saṣkṛtāsaṃskṛtā`, `kāmaduḥkhadigocaram`,
`dharmakhyam`, `dukhaṃ`, and `rūpārupyāvacaraṃ`; the local research witness
normalizes these in its Sanskrit quotation. This study follows its readable
technical forms while preserving the underlying files. The normalized
reading and the working translation of `anvaya` remain available for review.
