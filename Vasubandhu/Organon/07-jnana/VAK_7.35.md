# VAK_7.35 — Shared Qualities and the Distribution of Attainment

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
शिष्यसाधारणा अन्ये धर्माः केचित् पृथग्जनैः ।
अरणाप्रणिधिज्ञानप्रतिसंविद्गुणादयः ॥ ७.३५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
śiṣyasādhāraṇā anye dharmāḥ kecit pṛthagjanaiḥ |
araṇāpraṇidhijñānapratisaṃvidguṇādayaḥ || 7.35 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness, with its
editorial compound-hyphens removed. The preceding prose
closes the eighteen unshared Buddha-qualities. This verse
opens another class of qualities belonging to Buddhas
but shared, as applicable, with other practitioners.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
śiṣya-sādhāraṇāḥ | anye | dharmāḥ | kecit | pṛthagjanaiḥ |
araṇā-praṇidhijñāna-pratisaṃvid-guṇa-ādayaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `śiṣyasādhāraṇāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | shared with disciples |
| `anye` | nominative plural masculine adjective | other |
| `dharmāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | qualities, here glossed as `guṇāḥ` |
| `kecit` | nominative plural masculine indefinite | some |
| `pṛthagjanaiḥ` | instrumental plural masculine | with ordinary persons |
| `araṇā` | feminine noun in compound | freedom from conflict; technical designation |
| `praṇidhijñāna` | neuter compound member | knowledge through resolve |
| `pratisaṃvid` | compound member | discriminating knowledge |
| `guṇādayaḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound ending | qualities beginning with these, and so forth |

The final compound names a series. Its members are not
one synthetic faculty called “conflict-resolution-discrimination.”
`Praṇidhijñāna` remains one technical designation within
that series.

## 4. Grammar

**The subject and its bearer.** `Anye dharmāḥ` is the
subject. The prose supplies `buddhānām`, of Buddhas,
and glosses `śiṣya` as `śrāvaka`. The statement concerns
other qualities of Buddhas, not qualities outside Buddhahood
altogether.

**Sharing and its limit.** `Pṛthagjanaiḥ` supplies the
persons with whom some qualities are shared; carry
`sādhāraṇāḥ` into that clause. `Kecit` must remain operative:
the verse does not assign every listed quality to ordinary
persons. The prose reinforces this with `yathāyogam`,
as applicable.

**An open enumeration.** The ending `ādi` is expanded
by the Bhāṣya into further higher knowledges and meditative
qualities. It neither closes the class at three names
nor authorizes an unrestricted inventory of every possible
human capacity.

**Close construe.** Other [Buddha-]qualities are shared
with disciples, some with ordinary persons: qualities
beginning with araṇā, knowledge through resolve, and
discriminating knowledges, and so forth, as applicable.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Other qualities are shared with disciples, some with
> ordinary persons—qualities beginning with freedom from
> conflict, knowledge through resolve, and discriminating
> knowledges.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Other excellent qualities of Buddhas are shared with
> śrāvaka disciples, and some also with ordinary persons,
> according to the quality concerned. These include araṇā,
> knowledge through resolve, discriminating knowledges,
> higher knowledges, dhyānas, formless attainments,
> immeasurables, liberations, spheres of mastery, totality-
> spheres, and related qualities.

Source: Pradhan 416|17–417|02. The immediate continuation
introduces araṇā before its classification in VAK 7.36;
that introduction is noted below without advancing the
official verse sequence.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A quality's belonging to a Buddha does not establish
> that it belongs exclusively to Buddhas. The completed
> account of unshared qualities is followed by a field
> of shared attainments. Their distribution requires
> further determination: which quality, which practitioner,
> and under what conditions. Commonality of a named quality
> does not by itself establish equality of its range
> or exercise in every bearer.

This is a distinction of sharing, not a reversal of
the preceding Buddha-specific account. The phrase “as
applicable” prevents the shared list from becoming a
universal possession claim about all disciples or all
ordinary persons.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Sādhāraṇa` / `āveṇika`.** Shared / unshared. These
specify a quality's distribution among practitioners.
They do not independently tell us its knowledge-category,
supporting level, object, or acquisition condition.

**`Śiṣya` / `śrāvaka`.** Disciple / hearer-disciple. The
second is the Bhāṣya's explicit determination of the
first here; the verse is not referring indiscriminately
to every kind of student.

**`Pṛthagjana`.** Ordinary person, a technical practitioner-
status rather than a judgment of everyday competence.
The source includes some refined qualities within this
status without conferring noble attainment by that fact.

**`Araṇā`.** Freedom from conflict. The continuation makes
its relational character clear: an arhat seeks to avoid
becoming the object occasioning afflictions in others.
The term is not exhausted by the arhat's own inner calm.

**`Praṇidhijñāna`.** Knowledge through resolve. This is
an introductory rendering; its procedure and restrictions
belong to the ensuing individual analysis.

**`Pratisaṃvid`.** Discriminating knowledge. The collective
heading is named here; its detailed divisions should
not be inferred merely from a general gloss of discrimination.

**`Yathāyogam`.** As applicable. This short prose qualification
governs assignment of the enumerated qualities. It is
essential to an accurate distribution table.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya expands the catalogue as follows:

| Named quality or group | Working designation |
|---|---|
| `araṇā` | Freedom from conflict |
| `praṇidhijñāna` | Knowledge through resolve |
| `pratisaṃvid` | Discriminating knowledges |
| `abhijñā` | Higher knowledges |
| `dhyāna` | Meditative absorptions |
| `ārūpya` | Formless attainments |
| `apramāṇa` | Immeasurables |
| `vimokṣa` | Liberations |
| `abhibhvāyatana` | Spheres of mastery |
| `kṛtsnāyatana` | Totality-spheres |

This is an expanded list of kinds, not ten additional
unshared Buddha-qualities. The source's final `ādi`
also leaves the catalogue open.

The supported distribution claim is:

```text
Some other qualities of Buddhas are shared with sravakas.
Some of the relevant qualities are shared with ordinary persons.
Assignments are made as applicable to each quality.
```

It does not establish:

```text
Every ordinary person possesses every listed quality.
Every sravaka possesses every listed quality.
Shared quality-kind entails identical range or mastery.
```

**The opening araṇā explanation.** At 417|02–05, a certain
arhat knows that beings' suffering arises from afflictions
and recognizes himself as a distinguished recipient of
offerings. Wishing to avoid others' afflictions arising
with himself as their object, he produces the relevant
knowledge. The prose names attachment, hatred, and pride
as afflictions not arising toward him in this account.
It explains the name through a course that does not
provoke conflict in another.

This introduction establishes the direction of the
undertaking. Its knowledge nature, support, bearer, and
further limits are the subject of VAK 7.36, so the present
study does not turn the introductory wording into an
unqualified power to eliminate every affliction in others.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Do not infer distribution from the list alone.** The
catalogue and its bearer-classes are related by `yathāyogam`.
That relation requires the individual analyses which
follow. A matrix assigning every entry to both śrāvakas
and ordinary persons would erase the source's qualification.

**The introduction of araṇā is practical and relational.**
The arhat's concern is how others respond to him. “Freedom
from conflict” therefore names an undertaking directed
toward afflictions occasioned in others, rather than
mere withdrawal from disagreement. The detailed scope
must remain controlled by the next verse.

**Textual caution.** The long running catalogue contains
compressed and irregular joins around the formless,
immeasurable, and mastery-sphere terms. The study separates
the recognizable technical members for reading without
editing the witness. The araṇā introduction also contains
`bhena` where the instrumental-relative construction is
damaged; its preceding purpose-clause and following
examples secure the relational sense summarized here.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Our Yoga system now has to articulate shared forms of
knowledge and practice across different attained conditions.
Yoga Vidyā determines the quality-kind and its field;
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns the
undertaking through which it is acquired or exercised
by a particular practitioner.

Sharing a named kind is the beginning of that analysis.
The scope, support, and conditions still have to be
determined. This lets our reconstruction preserve continuity
of practice without treating ordinary, disciple, and
Buddha attainment as interchangeable.

Araṇā gives the first concrete orientation: one's conduct
can be examined through the afflictive response it occasions
in another. That is a precise contact for Technē, but
the project's practical interpretation should not exceed
the particular arhat undertaking described by the source.
The coming analysis will supply its constraints.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The sharing qualification, expanded catalogue, and opening
araṇā explanation are source-checked. Individual possession
and support assignments remain for their respective verses.
