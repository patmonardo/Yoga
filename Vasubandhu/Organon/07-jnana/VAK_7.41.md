# VAK_7.41 — Final-Limit Dhyāna: Integrated Range and Maximum Development

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तत् षड्विधं सर्वभूम्यनुलोमितम् ।
वृद्धिकाष्ठागतं तच्च बुद्धान्यस्य प्रयोगजाः ॥ ७.४१ ॥
```

The local research kārikā supplies only IAST. This
Devanāgarī is an editorial transcription of that reading,
not an independently supplied witness. Its compressed
`buddhānyasya` is retained; the Bhāṣya construes the
meaning as `buddhād anyasya`, “for one other than a Buddha.”

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The separate

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

vṛddhi-kāṣṭhā-gatam | tat | ca | buddha-anyasya | prayoga-jāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `tat` | nominative singular neuter pronoun | that final-limit fourth dhyāna |
| `ṣaḍvidham` | nominative singular neuter adjective | sixfold |
| `sarvabhūmyanulomitam` | nominative singular neuter participial compound | made to conform sequentially with all levels |
| `vṛddhikāṣṭhāgatam` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective | brought to the summit of increase |
| `tac ca` | pronoun and conjunction | and that [attainment / qualification] |
| `buddhānyasya` | transmitted compressed compound | for one other than a Buddha |
| `prayogajāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | arising from deliberate application |

The final plural `prayogajāḥ` is governed through the
qualities (`guṇāḥ`) supplied by the prose. The verse moves
from describing the singular dhyāna to describing the
plural qualities dependent on its power.

## 4. Grammar

**What is sixfold?** The Bhāṣya first calls final-limit
fourth dhyāna `ṣaḍātmakam`, then names araṇā, resolve-
knowledge, and three discriminating knowledges as its
forms. It separately says nirukti-pratisaṃvid is acquired
through that dhyāna's power but is not fourth-dhyāna-grounded.
Thus the sixfold dependency-set and the five intrinsically
fourth-dhyāna forms must be distinguished.

**Two coordinated predicates.** `Sarvabhūmyanulomitam`
and `vṛddhikāṣṭhāgatam` qualify the final-limit fourth
dhyāna. The first names ordered conformity across levels;
the second names maximal intensification. Neither is
merely a decorative synonym for “excellent.”

**The final contrast.** The Bhāṣya resolves the last clause:
for someone other than a Buddha, these qualities are
`prāyogika`, produced through deliberate application,
and not obtained merely through detachment. For a Buddha,
nothing is `prāyogika`; manifestation of the complete
qualities depends only on intention.

**Close construe.** That final-limit [dhyāna] is sixfold,
made conformable in sequence to all levels, and brought
to the summit of increase. For one other than a Buddha,
these [qualities] arise from deliberate application.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> That [final-limit dhyāna] is sixfold, made conformable
> to all levels, and brought to the summit of increase.
> For one other than a Buddha, these [qualities] arise
> from deliberate application.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The final-limit attainment is the fourth dhyāna cultivated
> in two determinate ways. It is made conformable to all
> levels by ascending from a desire-domain mind successively
> through the dhyānas and formless attainments to neither-
> perception-nor-nonperception, descending in reverse to
> a desire-domain mind, and ascending again to the fourth
> dhyāna. It is brought to the summit of increase by
> development from weak to medium and from medium to strong.
>
> The six qualities beginning with araṇā are acquired
> through the power of this cultivation. Nirukti-pratisaṃvid
> is acquired through it without itself being supported
> on the fourth dhyāna. For a non-Buddha these qualities
> arise through deliberate application, not merely through
> attaining detachment. For a Buddha, no quality is
> application-produced: through mastery of all dharmas,
> manifestation of the complete qualities depends only
> upon intention.

Source: Pradhan 420|07–421|03. The last Buddha/non-Buddha
distinction is stated by the Bhāṣya and should not be
reduced to an unstated psychology of habituation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Final-limit cultivation has two dimensions: ordered
> integration across the hierarchy of levels and maximum
> development in degree. The resulting qualities form
> a common dependency-system without acquiring one identical
> support. Their mode of manifestation then differs:
> deliberate application for the non-Buddha, intention-
> dependent presence under complete mastery for the Buddha.

Effortless Buddha manifestation is not described here
as the Buddha repeating the preparatory sequence invisibly
or rapidly. The source gives a stronger modal difference:
nothing is application-produced for a Buddha. At the
same time, it does not say that intention is absent;
intention is the stated condition of manifestation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Prāntakoṭika-dhyāna`.** Final-limit dhyāna: the fourth
dhyāna cultivated through all-level conformity and maximal
increase. `Prānta` and `koṭi` both contribute the sense
of reaching an extremity; the prose also allows `koṭi`
to mean increase or type, comparing `catuṣkoṭika`.

**`Sarvabhūmyanulomita`.** Made conformable to all levels.
The Bhāṣya defines this procedurally through an ordered
forward, reverse, and forward traversal. “Adaptable” may
describe its result, but it does not replace the specified
cultivation sequence.

**`Vṛddhikāṣṭhāgata`.** Having reached the summit of increase.
Its explicit developmental series is weak, medium, strong.
The claim concerns the highest degree of this cultivation,
not a universal quantitative maximum for every possible quality.

**`Mṛdu`, `madhya`, `adhimātra`.** Weak, medium, and strong.
These are relational degrees in the intensification of
the cultivated attainment, not judgments about three
practitioner personalities.

**`Prayogaja` / `prāyogika`.** Arising from deliberate
application / application-produced. The source assigns
this mode to a non-Buddha's qualities and expressly denies
that they arise merely with the acquisition of detachment.

**`Vairāgyalābhika`.** Acquired through attainment of
detachment. It is negated for the non-Buddha qualities
under discussion; detachment alone is insufficient.

**`Sarvadharmeśvaratva`.** Mastery over all dharmas.
This is the Bhāṣya's ground for the Buddha's distinctive
mode of manifestation. It should not be generalized into
political sovereignty or ownership of entities.

**`Icchāmātrapratibaddha`.** Dependent only upon intention.
This describes the manifestation of the complete collection
of qualities for a Buddha. It does not say that all qualities
are continuously manifest at once.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The technical traversal.**

```text
Forward:
    Desire-domain mind
    → First dhyana → Second → Third → Fourth
    → Infinite Space → Infinite Consciousness
    → Nothingness → Neither-Perception-Nor-Nonperception

Reverse:
    Neither-Perception-Nor-Nonperception
    → ... → First dhyana → Desire-domain mind

Final forward movement:
    Desire-domain mind
    → First → Second → Third → Fourth dhyana
```

The source names the endpoints and states sequential
ascent and reverse descent. The intermediate formless
names in this display are the established expansion of
the hierarchy, supplied for study readability. The final
movement terminates at the fourth dhyāna because that
is the attainment being cultivated to the final limit.

**The intensification.**

```text
Weak → Medium → Strong
Strong degree at the limit = SummitOfIncrease
```

All-level conformity and summit of increase are jointly
necessary determinations of the defined final-limit dhyāna.
The first concerns ordered range-integration; the second
concerns degree of development.

**The six and the five.**

| Relation to final-limit dhyāna | Qualities |
|---|---|
| Forms explicitly enumerated on its fourth-dhyāna ground | Araṇā, resolve-knowledge, dharma-, artha-, and pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid |
| Acquired through its power but intrinsically lower-grounded | Nirukti-pratisaṃvid |
| Full dependency-set | All six qualities |

This resolves the compressed enumeration without making
nirukti fourth-dhyāna-grounded and without reducing the
sixfold count to five.

**Modes of manifestation.**

```text
NonBuddha(q):
    Manifest(q) requires DeliberateApplication
    DetachmentAcquisition alone does not yield q

Buddha(q):
    Manifest(q) depends only on Intention
    because of MasteryOverAllDharmas
```

The second rule concerns the complete quality collection.
It does not entail simultaneous manifestation without
intention or collapse intention into the resulting quality.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Procedure and result must remain connected.** The research
kārikā file interprets all-level conformity mainly as
flexible accommodation in teaching. The Bhāṣya gives a
specific contemplative procedure. Adaptability may be
developed as an Organon consequence, but it should follow
from the forward–reverse–forward cultivation rather than
replace it in the source account.

**Buddha-mode is not described as internalized preparation.**
The first pass says that deliberate mediations have become
internalized. That can serve as a project analogy, but
the prose explicitly says a Buddha has nothing prāyogika.
Its positive determination is manifestation dependent
only on intention through mastery of all dharmas. The
official study preserves that stronger source claim.

**Textual caution.** The running source has `tatṣaḍivadhaṃ`,
`prantakoṭikam`, `buddhatuṇāḥ`, and a compressed final
`pratibadhaḥ`. The separate verse witness and continuous
prose secure the sixfold, final-limit, Buddha-quality,
and intention-dependent readings. The apparently five-member
enumeration is resolved through the explicit nirukti
qualification, not by inventing a seventh quality.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
This is a decisive Yoga Technē passage. It supplies an
actual cultivation course: range is integrated through
ordered traversal, and strength is developed through
graded intensification. Yoga Vidyā determines the hierarchy
of levels and the differentiated qualities made available;
Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, articulates the operations
that produce the final-limit attainment.

Their relation is reciprocal. The ordered field determines
what the practice must traverse; the completed practice
makes the differentiated knowledge-capacities effectively
available. Yet the six capacities retain distinct objects
and supports. Systematic unity here is an achieved relation,
not a fusion of functions.

The Buddha/non-Buddha distinction adds the culmination.
For the non-Buddha, deliberate application remains necessary.
For the Buddha, the quality becomes present dependent
only on intention. Our reconstruction can understand
this as the passage from cultivated Technē to free mastery,
provided it keeps the source's explicit denial of Buddha
`prayoga` and does not substitute a merely automated routine.
