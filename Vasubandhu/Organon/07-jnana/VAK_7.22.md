# VAK_7.22 — Detachment and the Two Sevenfold Cultivation Ranges

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
षोडशे षट् सरागस्य वीतरागस्य सप्त तु ।
सरागभावनामार्गे तदूर्ध्वं सप्तभावना ॥ ७.२२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ṣoḍaśe ṣaṭ sarāgasya vītarāgasya sapta tu |
sarāgabhāvanāmārge tadūrdhvaṃ saptabhāvanā || 7.22 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The Bhāṣya
explicitly carries forward `bhāvyante`, “are cultivated.”
The sixteenth moment belongs to the Path of Cultivation,
as established in VAK 6.28; it remains the sixteenth moment
of the direct-realization sequence, not of strict Seeing.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ṣoḍaśe | ṣaṭ | sa-rāgasya | vīta-rāgasya | sapta | tu |
sa-rāga-bhāvanā-mārge | tat-ūrdhvam | sapta-bhāvanā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ṣoḍaśe` | locative singular ordinal | at the sixteenth [moment] |
| `ṣaṭ` | numeral | six [knowledges] |
| `sarāgasya` | genitive singular adjective used substantivally | for one not detached from desire |
| `vītarāgasya` | genitive singular adjective used substantivally | for one detached from desire |
| `sapta` | numeral | seven [knowledges] |
| `tu` | adversative particle | but, whereas |
| `sarāgabhāvanāmārge` | locative singular compound | in the Path of Cultivation of one still undetached |
| `tadūrdhvam` | adverbial expression | after that |
| `saptabhāvanā` | nominative singular feminine compound | cultivation of seven |

`Tat + ūrdhvam` yields `tadūrdhvam`. The commentary resolves
the reference temporally: after the sixteenth moment.
`Sarāga` names an attainment-status here; it does not require
a presently manifest episode of desire.

## 4. Grammar

**The supplied verb and subject.** Read `ṣaṭ jñānāni bhāvyante`
and `sapta jñānāni bhāvyante`. The genitives specify whose
cultivation is being counted; an English “for” construction
expresses this without treating them as Sanskrit instrumentals.

**The sixteenth moment.** The Bhāṣya supplies
`mārgānvayajñānakṣaṇe`: the moment of Path-anvaya-knowledge.
The two conditions, `sarāgasya` and `vītarāgasya`, qualify
the practitioner at that same moment.

**The continuing rule.** In the second half, `saptabhāvanā`
is a nominal predicate: there is cultivation of seven.
The prose expands the compressed compound through
`yāvan na vītarāgo bhavati tāvat`: for as long as the
practitioner has not become detached. The restriction
belongs to the practitioner's status, not to an assertion
that the noble path itself is an afflicted cognition.

**Close construe.** At the sixteenth [moment], six [knowledges
are cultivated] for one still undetached, but seven for one
detached. After that, in the Path of Cultivation while the
practitioner remains undetached, there is cultivation of seven.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> At the sixteenth moment, six knowledges are cultivated
> for one with desire, but seven for one detached from it.
> After that, in the Path of Cultivation while desire
> remains, there is cultivation of seven.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> At the sixteenth moment, Path-anvaya-knowledge, a practitioner
> not yet detached from the desire domain cultivates six
> knowledge categories: dharma, anvaya, suffering, origination,
> cessation, and Path. For one already detached, knowledge
> of another mind is cultivated as the seventh.
>
> After that moment, for as long as detachment from desire
> has not been attained, seven categories are cultivated
> in the preparatory, uninterrupted, liberation, and superior
> paths: the same six together with conventional knowledge.
> If the current path is worldly, conventional knowledge
> is present; if supramundane, one of the four truth-specific
> dharma-knowledges is present.

Source: Pradhan 407|18–408|07. The present cognition and
the cultivated range answer different questions. The
sixteenth-moment cognition is classified as Path-knowledge
and as anvaya-knowledge concerning Path; this does not
require two separate simultaneous cognitive events.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The field cultivated through a path-moment depends on
> where the practitioner stands and what detachment has
> already been attained. The same moment can cultivate
> a different range for different practitioners. Later,
> different present cognitions can cultivate the same
> range. The count becomes meaningful only through the
> composition of the set and the conditions of cultivation.

The two occurrences of “seven” are not interchangeable.
At moment sixteen for the detached practitioner, the addition
is knowledge of another mind. In the subsequent undetached
case, the addition is conventional knowledge. Neither count
is a count of seven simultaneous acts of knowing.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ṣoḍaśa` — sixteenth.** Path-anvaya-knowledge, the first
moment classified as Cultivation in the sixteen-moment
truth-realization sequence. The research study's “final
moment of the Path of Seeing” requires this correction.

**`Sarāga` / `vītarāga` — undetached / detached from desire.**
The local distinction concerns detachment from the desire
domain. “Detached” here does not by itself mean complete
arhatship or freedom from attachment to every higher domain.

**`Pratyutpanna` — presently occurring.** The prose explicitly
distinguishes present knowledge from the larger cultivated
range. Its “two knowledges” at moment sixteen are overlapping
knowledge determinations of the Path-anvaya cognition.

**`Paracittajñāna` — knowledge of another mind.** The seventh
cultivated category for the detached practitioner at moment
sixteen. Its inclusion is a cultivation assertion, not an
assertion that another mind is then being cognized.

**`Saṃvṛtijñāna` — conventional knowledge.** The seventh
category in the later, still-undetached cultivation range.
This rule should not be identified without qualification
with the special completion-knowledge discussed in VAK 7.21.

**The four path functions.** `Prayoga` is preparation;
`ānantarya`, the uninterrupted counteractive path;
`vimukti`, the liberation path; and `viśeṣa`, the superior
or further-advance path. The Bhāṣya includes all four under
the subsequent undetached sevenfold rule.

**The four dharma-knowledges.** Dharma-knowledge concerning
suffering, origination, cessation, or Path. These are
truth-specific determinations within dharma-knowledge,
not four additional members of the tenfold inventory.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let `B` name the following six-category cultivated set:

```text
B = {DharmaKnowledge, AnvayaKnowledge, SufferingKnowledge,
     OriginationKnowledge, CessationKnowledge, PathKnowledge}
```

| Path position and status | Cultivated set | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Moment sixteen, undetached from desire | B | 6 |
| Moment sixteen, detached from desire | B plus knowledge of another mind | 7 |
| After moment sixteen, still undetached | B plus conventional knowledge | 7 |

The last row applies across the four specified path functions.
It does not state a universal sevenfold rule for every later
attainment; subsequent verses extend the conditional account.

For the last row, present cognition varies while the cultivated
set remains the same:

| Current mode | Present cognition | Cultivated set |
|---|---|---|
| Worldly | Conventional knowledge | B plus conventional knowledge |
| Supramundane | One of four truth-specific dharma-knowledges | B plus conventional knowledge |

Thus path position and detachment determine the local cultivated
set. Worldly or supramundane mode determines the stated present
cognition; within this row it does not change the sevenfold set.

The categories overlap. The membership count is not a partition
into mutually exclusive kinds of individual cognition. Keep
three relations distinct:

```text
possessesKnowledgeCategory
presentlyActualizesCognition
cultivatesKnowledgeCategory
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A doubtful word is not an explanatory foundation.** The running
source reads `purāgatāni ṣaṭ bhāvyante`. The research Bhāṣya
study builds a vocabulary entry for `purāgata`, “previously
arisen / attained.” This reading is doubtful in the inherited
future-cultivation context; `anāgatāni` is a possible intended
reading, but is not silently substituted here. The explicit
six-item list and carried cultivation question secure the
count independently of this word. “Previously attained” is
therefore not presented as a verified technical explanation.

**Two determinations of the present cognition.** The prose has
`dve jñāne pratyutpanne`, followed by Path-knowledge and
Path-anvaya-knowledge. The established cross-classification
of knowledge allows both descriptions of the present event.
Reading this as two numerically separate cognitions would
confuse categories with occurrences.

**Cultivation after Seeing.** VAK 7.20 restricted Seeing's
future cultivation to corresponding kinds and aspects.
VAK 7.22 supplies broader sets at and after moment sixteen.
The strict fifteen-moment boundary from VAK 6.28 makes this
transition intelligible without assigning contradictory
rules to the same stage.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Yoga
Vidyā articulates the composition of the cultivated knowledge
field. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, articulates
the path undertaking in relation to attained detachment
and current path function. Their relation must include
the practitioner's history: the same present realization
does not cultivate an identical range in every continuum.

The distinction between the two sevens gives this reconstruction
a concrete discipline. A numerical measure of development
cannot replace an account of what has been developed. Likewise,
the visible present act does not exhaust the range cultivated
through it. Our model must record both the current exercise
and the conditioned cultivation range.

The capable-support interpretation developed in VAK 7.21 remains
an explicit project choice. This verse supplies cultivation
counts and conditions; it does not independently settle every
question about when each cultivated item becomes actual.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The three sets, their conditions, and the present-cognition
distinction are explicit. The doubtful `purāgatāni` reading
remains open to textual review.
