# VAK_7.10 — The Distribution of Knowledge-Aspects

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
धर्मज्ञानान्वयज्ञानं षोडशाकारमन्यथा ।
तथा च सांवृतं स्वैः स्वैः सत्याकारैश्चतुष्टयम् ॥ ७.१० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dharmajñānānvayajñānaṃ ṣoḍaśākāram anyathā |
tathā ca sāṃvṛtaṃ svaiḥ svaiḥ satyākāraiś catuṣṭayam || 7.10 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā study. The raw Bhāṣya quotation
has `ṣoḍḍaśākāram`; the working reading is `ṣoḍaśākāram`, also found
in the explanatory prose. The verse distributes aspect-ranges; the
Bhāṣya explicitly reserves the detailed teaching of the sixteen aspects
for later.

## 4. Grammar

**The shared sixteenfold range.** The initial compound gathers dharma-
and anvaya-knowledge under a singular neuter expression. Its predicate
`ṣoḍaśākāram` is distributed to both. It does not identify them as one
knowledge-kind or allocate only eight aspects to each.

```text
dharmajñānam — ṣoḍaśākāram
anvayajñānam — ṣoḍaśākāram
```

**“Otherwise” belongs with conventional knowledge.** The construction
crosses the pāda boundary:

```text
anyathā tathā ca — sāṃvṛtam [jñānam]
```

The commentary explicitly expands conventional knowledge as
`ṣoḍaśākāram anyathākāraṃ ca`, sixteen-aspected and otherwise-aspected.
Thus `anyathā` is not an exception appended to the aspect-range of
dharma- or anvaya-knowledge.

**The four truth-knowledges.** `Catuṣṭayam` denotes suffering-,
origination-, cessation-, and Path-knowledge. The instrumental phrase
specifies how each operates. The prose supplies `pravartante`, “proceed
or operate,” and `ekaikaṃ caturākāram`, “each four-aspected.”

```text
catuṣṭayam
    [pravartate] svaiḥ svaiḥ satyākāraiḥ
```

The collective subject is singular in the verse; its members receive
their respective fourfold determinations in the explanation.

**Close construe.** Dharma- and anvaya-knowledge—[are] sixteen-aspected.
Otherwise and thus also—[is] conventional knowledge. The group of
four—[operates] by its own respective truth-aspects.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge have sixteen aspects.
> Conventional knowledge is otherwise and also thus. The group of
> four [operates] through the aspects proper to its respective truths.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Dharma- and anvaya-knowledge each range over all sixteen truth-aspects.
> Conventional knowledge can take these same aspects and others besides.
> The four truth-knowledges—suffering, origination, cessation, and Path—each
> operate through the four aspects proper to its own truth.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The field of truth-aspects is shared without making the knowledges
> identical. Dharma- and anvaya-knowledge each admit the sixteenfold
> range. Conventional knowledge admits that range and further modes of
> apprehension. Each truth-knowledge, however, is specified through
> the four aspects of its own truth.

The classification concerns ranges of possible operation. “Sixteen-
aspected” does not state that a single cognitive event simultaneously
apprehends sixteen aspects. Likewise, a conventional cognition's use
of a truth-aspect does not by itself establish uncontaminated status.
The distinctions already drawn by nature, domain-relation, and
counteraction remain relevant.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Knowledge-kind | Aspect-range |
|---|---|
| Dharma-knowledge | Sixteen truth-aspects |
| Anvaya-knowledge | Sixteen truth-aspects |
| Conventional knowledge | Sixteen truth-aspects and other aspects |
| Suffering-knowledge | Four aspects proper to suffering |
| Origination-knowledge | Four aspects proper to origination |
| Cessation-knowledge | Four aspects proper to cessation |
| Path-knowledge | Four aspects proper to Path |

Let `A(t)` denote the four aspects proper to truth `t`:

```text
TruthAspectField =
    A(Suffering) ∪ A(Origination) ∪ A(Cessation) ∪ A(Path)

AspectRange(DharmaKnowledge) = TruthAspectField
AspectRange(AnvayaKnowledge) = TruthAspectField

AspectRange(ConventionalKnowledge)
    includes TruthAspectField and further aspects

AspectRange(TruthKnowledge(t)) = A(t)
```

These are knowledge-kind range statements. For a cognition classified
both as dharma-knowledge and suffering-knowledge, the more specific
truth-classification locates it within the suffering-aspect range.
The broader dharma classification does not erase that restriction.

```text
SharedAspectRange does not entail IdenticalKnowledgeKind
TruthAspectUse does not entail UncontaminatedStatus
KindLevelRange does not entail SimultaneousUseOfAllAspects
```

The passage therefore adds a further relation to the preceding studies:

```text
knowledge-kind
    hasObjectRange
    hasCounteractiveRange
    hasAspectRange
```

These relations must be represented together, with their different
grounds and scopes preserved.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya control of the pāda boundary.** The placement of `anyathā`
at the end of the first line can obscure its connection to conventional
knowledge. The prose explicitly joins the sixteen-aspected and
otherwise-aspected predicates under `saṃvṛtijñāna`.

**Scope of the translation.** Pradhan 395|17–396|02 distributes the ranges
and says the sixteen aspects will be taught later. The informed translation
names the four truth-knowledges and their respective four aspects, while
leaving the later enumeration in its own source location.

**Shared form and different status.** VAK 7.03 allowed conventional
knowledge a broad object-range. Here it also shares the sixteen
truth-aspects. Neither object alone nor aspect alone can therefore
serve as an adequate test of uncontaminated knowledge. Their systematic
position remains part of the determination.

**The source's sequence.** The research kārikā file supplies a standard
list of sixteen aspects. The Bhāṣya itself postpones that teaching.
The official study retains the range and fourfold distribution here,
leaving the detailed terms to their proper place in the exposition.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our Yoga
system now gains a sharper account of the relation between knowing
a field and operating within it. Yoga Vidyā articulates the aspects
under which a truth can be known. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma
companion, develops the practice through which the appropriate
determination becomes operative in a particular undertaking.

The shared truth-aspects show why recognizing a form is not enough
to establish its practical status. Conventional and uncontaminated
knowing can participate in the same aspect-field while retaining
different determinations. Our reconstruction must therefore connect
the mode of apprehension with the knowing's conditions, Path-position,
and efficacy.

The distributive `svaiḥ svaiḥ` gives this companionship a further
discipline: a specific task requires the determinations proper to
its truth-field. Possession of the broader conceptual map does not
replace the precise operation appropriate to that task. This is our
Vidyā–Technē development of the distribution, kept distinct from
the verse's own classificatory statement.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The scope of
`anyathā` and the four-aspect distribution are explicit in the prose.
The minor `ṣoḍḍaśa` reading is recorded; the detailed aspect-list
remains reserved for the later passage.
