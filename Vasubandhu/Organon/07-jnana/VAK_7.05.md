# VAK_7.05 — First Arising and the Limits of Other-Mind Knowledge

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दुःखहेत्वन्वयज्ञाने चतुर्भ्यः परचित्तवित् ।
भूम्यक्षपुद्गलोत्क्रान्तं नष्टाजातं न वेत्ति तत् ॥ ७.५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
duḥkhahetvanvayajñāne caturbhyaḥ paracittavit |
bhūmyakṣapudgalotkrāntaṃ naṣṭājātaṃ na vetti tat || 7.5 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The opening compound
completes the last clause of VAK 7.04. A new statement begins with
`caturbhyaḥ paracittavit`. Keeping this boundary intact corrects a
substantive misreading in both local research studies.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
duḥkha-hetu-anvaya-jñāne | caturbhyaḥ | para-citta-vit |
bhūmi-akṣa-pudgala-utkrāntam | naṣṭa-ajātam | na | vetti | tat
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `duḥkha-hetu-anvaya-jñāne` | nominative dual neuter compound designation | suffering-anvaya-knowledge and origination-anvaya-knowledge |
| `caturbhyaḥ` | ablative plural numeral here | from four [knowledge-categories] |
| `para-citta-vit` | nominative singular root-noun compound ending in `-vid` | knowledge that knows another mind |
| `bhūmi` | compound member | meditative level |
| `akṣa` | compound member | faculty, explained through `indriya` |
| `pudgala` | compound member | person, here practitioner standing |
| `utkrāntam` | accusative singular neuter participial compound ending | exceeding [the knower's corresponding capacity] |
| `naṣṭa-ajātam` | accusative singular neuter coordinated expression | ceased or not yet arisen |
| `na` | negative particle | not |
| `vetti` | third-person singular present active of `√vid` | knows |
| `tat` | nominative singular neuter pronoun | that [knowledge of another mind] |

`Hetu` is resolved by the Bhāṣya as `samudaya`, origination. It does not
introduce an additional truth-category. `Hetu + anvaya` yields
`hetvanvaya`; `bhūmi + akṣa` yields `bhūmyakṣa`.

The Bhāṣya glosses `vit` through `vetti` and explicitly supplies `jñānam`.
The referent is therefore the knowing, rather than a new practitioner-type
called “the knower of another mind.”

## 4. Grammar

**Completion of VAK 7.04.** Read across the verse boundary:

```text
te punaḥ prathamodite
    duḥkha-hetu-anvaya-jñāne
```

“Those two, however, when first arisen, [are] suffering- and
origination-anvaya-knowledges.” The nominative dual predicate completes
the preceding dual subject, exhaustion- and non-arising-knowledge.
It is not a locative expression and does not enumerate the four categories
of other-mind knowledge in the following clause.

The dual names the available truth-classifications at first arising. It
does not assign exhaustion exclusively to suffering and non-arising
exclusively to origination.

**A new classification.** `Caturbhyaḥ paracittavit` is a compressed nominal
statement. The Bhāṣya expands:

```text
paracittajñānaṃ caturbhyo jñānebhyo draṣṭavyam
```

“Knowledge of another mind is to be understood from four knowledges.”
The ablative expresses classification here; “is causally produced by four
knowledges” would add a genesis the sentence does not assert.

**The range restriction.** `Tat` is the subject of `na vetti`. The
accusatives identify excluded objects: what exceeds the corresponding
level, faculty, or practitioner standing, and what has ceased or has not
yet arisen. The Bhāṣya identifies the positive object-field as presently
occurring other-mind and mental factors.

**Close construe.** [Those two, when first arisen, are] suffering- and
origination-anvaya-knowledges. Other-mind knowledge—[is classified] from
four. It does not know what exceeds [it] in level, faculty, or person,
or what has ceased or has not yet arisen.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> [Those two, when first arisen, are] suffering- and origination-anvaya-
> knowledges. Knowledge of another mind is [classified] from four.
> It does not know what exceeds it in level, faculty, or person, nor
> what has ceased or has not yet arisen.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> When exhaustion-knowledge and non-arising-knowledge first arise, they
> belong to suffering- or origination-anvaya-knowledge: they take the
> aggregates of the summit of existence as object under the aspects of
> suffering and origination. Knowledge of another mind is classified under
> four knowledge-categories—dharma, anvaya, Path, and conventional knowledge.
> Its range is restricted: it cannot know cognition exceeding its meditative
> level, faculty-grade, or practitioner standing. It cannot know past or
> future cognition, because its objects are presently occurring mind and
> mental factors of another.

The first-arising explanation and restrictions follow Pradhan
393|01–393|13. The fourfold list at 393|06–393|07 is textually damaged;
the explicit recapitulation at 394|23 confirms dharma, anvaya, Path, and
conventional knowledge. This is a targeted textual control, not an advance
to the later kārikā study.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The first actualization of terminal knowledge has a specified object and
> aspect, within its broader truth-classified range. Other-mind knowledge
> then receives a different determination: it belongs within four existing
> knowledge-categories and operates within a graded, present object-field.
> Its capacity is intelligible through both its classificatory relations
> and the limits on what it can know.

The opening and the rest of the verse concern different operations.
The first specifies initial occurrence; the second locates a knowledge-kind
and restricts its range. Preserving this distinction prevents a temporal
predicate from becoming a false derivation of another cognitive capacity.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Duḥkha-hetv-anvayajñāne` — suffering- and origination-anvaya-knowledges.**
The predicate carried over from VAK 7.04. The Bhāṣya explains the
truth-pair through the aspects under which the Bhavāgra aggregates are
taken as object.

**`Bhavāgrika-skandha` — aggregates belonging to the summit of existence.**
The specified object of the first-arising terminal knowledges. The source
relates their initial truth-classification to this object rather than
leaving first arising as an unspecified experience of completion.

**`Vajropama` — vajra-like [Path].** In the opening commentary, Vasubandhu
asks whether it shares the object of the terminal knowledges. His answer
is conditional: when it takes suffering or origination as object, yes;
when it takes cessation or Path as object, no. Continuity of the Path
sequence does not require identical objects in every case.

**`Paracittavit` / `paracittajñāna` — knowledge of another mind.** The
verse's compressed expression and the commentary's expanded name.
Its fourfold classification concerns portions of dharma-, anvaya-,
Path-, and conventional knowledge. These are overlapping categories,
not four simultaneous ingredients of each act.

**`Niyama` — restriction / governing rule.** The Bhāṣya introduces the
exclusions with `tasya punar ayaṃ niyamaḥ`: “for it, however, this is the
restriction.” The limits belong to the determination of the capacity.

**`Bhūmi` — meditative level.** The commentary specifies lower and higher
dhyāna levels. Knowledge based on the lower does not know cognition
belonging to the higher.

**`Akṣa` / `indriya` — faculty / faculty-grade.** The verse's term is
expanded by the Bhāṣya through differences in practitioners' faculties.
The passage refers to `śraddhādhimukta` and `samayamukta` in relation to
`dṛṣṭiprāpta` and `asamayavimukta`. The damaged wording does not justify
printing a silently reconstructed Sanskrit sentence.

**`Pudgala` — person / practitioner standing.** The relevant distinction
is the lower practitioner's inability to know the higher. The e-text's
rank enumeration is corrupted; it refers to advanced disciples,
Pratyekabuddha, and Buddha. The general ordering rule is more secure than
an exact restoration of the entire list.

**`Naṣṭa` / `ajāta` — ceased / not yet arisen.** The Bhāṣya explains these
as past and future. The exclusion is local to the other-mind knowledge
under discussion, not a denial of every possible knowledge of past or future.

**`Vartamāna-paracitta-caitta-viṣayatva` — having present other-mind and
mental factors as objects.** The reason for the temporal restriction.
The inclusion of caittas in the possible object-field does not assert
that mind and all associated factors are apprehended together in one act.

## 8. Logical Determination

The first-arising statement is separately qualified:

```text
FirstArising(ExhaustionKnowledge or NonArisingKnowledge)
    → SufferingAnvayaKnowledge or OriginationAnvayaKnowledge
    → Object: BhavagraAggregates
    → Aspects: Suffering or Origination
```

For other-mind knowledge, the Bhāṣya's recapitulation states that it is
one knowledge and a portion of four:

```text
ParacittaKnowledge
    overlaps DharmaKnowledge
    overlaps AnvayaKnowledge
    overlaps PathKnowledge
    overlaps ConventionalKnowledge
```

This does not mean that it exhausts any of those four categories, or that
every instance belongs to all four. In particular, conventional knowledge
and the uncontaminated classifications retain the distinction established
in VAK 7.02.

The exclusions concern separate dimensions:

| Dimension | Excluded target |
|---|---|
| Meditative level | Cognition of a higher level than the knowing capacity |
| Faculty-grade | Cognition exceeding the knower's faculty-grade |
| Practitioner standing | Cognition of a higher practitioner relative to the lower |
| Time | Past or future mind and mental factors |

```text
HigherInLevel(target, knower)
OR HigherInFaculty(target, knower)
OR HigherInPractitionerStanding(target, knower)
OR NonPresent(target)
    → NotKnownByThisParacittaKnowledge(target)
```

Passing these exclusions is necessary, not sufficient, for an actual
knowing. The verse does not guarantee that every present, non-superior
cognition is known. The following verse adds further determinations.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Correction of both research studies.** The local kārikā study attaches
`duḥkhahetvanvayajñāne` to the following fourfold classification; the
Bhāṣya study retains that identification despite recognizing the
continuation from VAK 7.04. The source separates the two statements.
Its later explicit list at 394|23 reads
`dharmānvayamārga saṃvṛtijñānānām`, confirming the four categories.
The official study corrects the analysis while preserving both originals.

**Textual caution.** The immediate listing is transmitted as
`dharmānvayajñānamārgaḥ saṃvṛtijñānebhyaḥ`. The practitioner clauses
include damaged forms such as `dṛṣṭiprāptāsamayavimuktārga` and
`anāgāmyahañcchāvaka`. The fourfold classification has the independent
internal control just noted; the precise practitioner wording remains
open for textual review.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** The passage
gives our Yoga system two related tasks. Yoga Vidyā articulates the
knowledge-capacity, its objects, and the conditions limiting its range.
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, must develop practice with those
determinations intact. A practical capacity acquires its meaning through
what it can accomplish under specified conditions.

The first-arising clause contributes the distinction between a developed
capacity's general range and its initial actualization. The other-mind
discussion contributes a disciplined relation between capacity and target:
level, faculty, practitioner standing, and temporal status jointly matter.
In our reconstruction, practice must account for these relations rather
than treating the name of a capacity as proof of its successful exercise.

This is an Organon development of the source's classificatory method.
The kārikā and Bhāṣya provide the particular doctrinal claims; a comparative
identification with Yoga's own account of other-mind knowledge requires
its own textual study.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The sentence
boundary and fourfold classification are corrected through the Sanskrit
prose and its explicit recapitulation. Exact restoration of the damaged
practitioner clauses remains open.
