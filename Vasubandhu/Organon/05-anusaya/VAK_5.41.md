# VAK_5.41

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
संयोजनादिभेदेन पुनस्ते पञ्चधोदिताः ।
द्रव्यामर्शनसामान्याद् दृष्टी संयोजनान्तरम् ॥ ५.४१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
saṃyojanādi-bhedena punas te pañcadhoditāḥ |
dravyāmarśana-sāmānyād dṛṣṭī saṃyojanāntaram || 5.41 ||
```

VAK 5.41 opens a new fivefold division of the same anuśayas: fetter, bond,
anuśaya, secondary affliction, and paryavasthāna. Within the first division,
the five views are consolidated into two distinct fetters on explicitly stated
grounds.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `saṃyojana-ādi-bhedena` | instrumental singular masculine compound | through the division beginning with fetters |
| `punaḥ` | indeclinable adverb | again, under another division |
| `te` | nominative plural masculine pronoun | those same anuśayas |
| `pañcadhā` | indeclinable adverb | fivefold, in five ways |
| `uditāḥ` | nominative plural masculine past passive participle of `ud-√vac` | stated or taught |
| `dravya-āmarśana-sāmānyāt` | ablative singular neuter compound | because of commonality in real-item constitution and in grasping |
| `dṛṣṭī` | nominative dual feminine | the two classes of views |
| `saṃyojana-antaram` | nominative singular neuter used distributively | each a distinct fetter, another fetter-class |


The dual `dṛṣṭī` refers to two grouped classes, not two individual views. The
Bhāṣya identifies them as the three views comprising the view-fetter and the
two grasping-views comprising the grasping-fetter.

## 4. Grammar

**Renewed fivefold division**

```text
te [eva anuśayāḥ]
    punaḥ
    saṃyojana-ādi-bhedena
    pañcadhā uditāḥ
```

`Te` resumes the same anuśayas; `punaḥ` marks reclassification rather than a
new inventory. The instrumental compound supplies the principle of division,
and `pañcadhā` is specified by the Bhāṣya as:

```text
saṃyojana | bandhana | anuśaya | upakleśa | paryavasthāna
```

**Ground of the two view-fetters**

```text
dravya-sāmānyāt
āmarśana-sāmānyāt ca
    dṛṣṭī
    saṃyojana-antaram [bhavataḥ]
```

The ablative states the causal ground of classification. The compact singular
`saṃyojanāntaram` is distributive in sense: the two view-groups are established
as distinct fetters. The Bhāṣya specifies the dravya-ground numerically—each
group comprises eighteen real items—and applies commonality of grasping to the
two parāmarśa-views.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Through the division beginning with fetters, those [same anuśayas] are again
> taught fivefold. Because of commonality in real-item constitution and in
> grasping, the two [classes of] views are each a distinct fetter.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Those same anuśayas are taught again fivefold by divisions beginning
> with fetters. Through commonality of real factors and of grasping, the
> two classes of views are distinct fetters.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The same afflictive determination acquires a different classificatory
> position when the governing relation changes. As anuśaya it lies along and
> can recur; as saṃyojana it binds under a specified relation and context.
> Hence the presence of view as an associated anuśaya does not entail the
> present operation of the view-fetter. Within the fetter taxonomy, the views
> themselves are organized into two genera through determinate commonality:
> equal real-item constitution grounds their coordination, while the pair of
> parāmarśa-views shares the positive operation of grasping. Classification
> therefore preserves both the identity of the afflictive field and the
> difference of the relations through which it is judged.

The source's compact `grāhya-grāhaka-bheda` marks an asymmetry between what is
taken up and the operation that takes hold; its exact scope should not be
expanded beyond the Bhāṣya's concise statement.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Saṃyojana` — fetter**

An afflictive determination considered as binding or fastening the continuum.
The Bhāṣya enumerates nine: attraction, hostility, conceit, ignorance, view,
grasping, doubt, envy, and avarice.

**The new fivefold division**

```text
saṃyojana       fetter
bandhana        bond
anuśaya         underlying afflictive tendency
upakleśa        secondary affliction
paryavasthāna   enveloping or dominating manifestation
```

These are five classifications of the same afflictive field, not five wholly
separate inventories.

**`Anunaya-saṃyojana` — fetter of attraction**

Defined as attachment across all three domains (`traidhātuka-rāga`). The Bhāṣya
says that the remaining fetters are to be correlated with the earlier field
as applicable.

**`Dṛṣṭi-saṃyojana` — view-fetter**

The three-view group, with eighteen counted factors in this Bhāṣya. Its
members are body-view, extreme view, and false view in the wider chapter
classification; this unit does not repeat their names.

**`Parāmarśa-saṃyojana` — grasping-fetter**

The grouped fetter comprising grasping at views and grasping at discipline and
observance. These likewise constitute eighteen dravyas and share
`parāmarśa-svabhāva`, the nature of grasping.

**`Dravya-sāmānya` — commonality in dravya-constitution**

Here the Bhāṣya gives a specific enumerative ground: the three-view group and
the two-view group each comprise eighteen counted factors. This ground should
not be replaced by an unstated common cognitive substrate.

**`Āmarśana-sāmānya` — commonality of grasping**

The common grasping nature attributed to the two parāmarśa views. The local
sentence is compact; it does not specify a full theory of their operation.

**`Grāhya-grāhaka-bheda` — distinction between grasped and grasper**

The Bhāṣya's concluding distinction between what is grasped and what grasps.
Its exact application to the two view groups remains open.

**`Sarvatraga` and `asarvatraga` — universal and non-universal**

The path-stage counterexample depends on this distinction. The relevant
universal view has already been abandoned, and no remaining non-universal
member of the view-fetter bears the required Support and conjunction relations.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse first separates field, division, and class:

```text
UnderlyingField = Anuśayas

ClassifyBy(UnderlyingField, FivefoldAfflictiveMode)
    → {Saṃyojana, Bandhana, Anuśaya, Upakleśa, Paryavasthāna}
```

The view-fetters are produced by determinate grounds:

```text
MemberCount(ThreeViewGroup) = 18
MemberCount(TwoGraspingViewGroup) = 18
    → DravyaCommonalityBetweenGroups

ParāmarśaNature(ViewGrasping)
AND ParāmarśaNature(DisciplineObservanceGrasping)
    → GroupUnder(ParāmarśaSaṃyojana)
```

The Bhāṣya's path-stage example proves non-equivalence of predicates:

```text
SamudayaKnowledgeArisen
AND NirodhaKnowledgeNotYetArisen
AND DharmaConjoinedWithParāmarśaView(x)
AND AbandonedBySeeingCessationOrPath(x)
    → DṛṣṭyAnuśayaLiesAlongByConjunction(x)
    AND BoundByAnunayaSaṃyojanaThroughSupport(x)
    AND NOT BoundByDṛṣṭiSaṃyojana(x)
```

Therefore:

```text
DṛṣṭyAnuśaya(x, context)
    ⇏ DṛṣṭiSaṃyojana(x, context)
```

Classification is context-indexed because the relevant Support relations,
conjunctions, and abandonments change across the Path.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** VAK 5.41 begins a second series of classifications.
The Bhāṣya first lists nine fetters, then uses a technically precise path-stage
case to show that fetter and anuśaya cannot be treated as synonyms with
identical extension.

**The counterexample.** After knowledge of arising has occurred but before
knowledge of cessation has arisen, dharmas conjoined with the two parāmarśa-
views to be abandoned through seeing cessation and the Path still have
view-anuśaya lying along through conjunction. Yet the view-fetter does not bind
them, because the universal view has already been abandoned and no suitable
non-universal view-fetter remains in the required relations.

This makes the distinction operationally exact:

```text
same dharma and same broad afflictive family
    + changed Path-context
        → different truth-value under a different classificatory relation
```

**Commonality without flattening.** The three views and two grasping-views are
not arbitrarily bundled, but neither are their groups generated by one vague
similarity. The Bhāṣya preserves two levels: equality in eighteen-item
constitution coordinates the groups, while shared grasping-nature internally
unifies the parāmarśa pair.

**Organon interpretation.** Classification must be represented at least as a
four-place determination:

```text
Classifies(
    AfflictiveFactor,
    ClassificationPrinciple,
    PathContext,
    ResultingClass
)
```

Entity identity alone cannot settle class membership. A proposed Dataset must
retain the current abandonments, Support, conjunction, and governing relation.
The `grāhya/grāhaka` distinction suggests typed roles within the grasping
relation, though its application here remains philologically compact. This is
an Organon architectural proposal, not a data model supplied by Vasubandhu.
