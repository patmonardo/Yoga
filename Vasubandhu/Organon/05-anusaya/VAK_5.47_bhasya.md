# VAK 5.47 Bhāṣya — Eight Enveloping Afflictions

## Kārikā Anchor

> āhrīkyam anapatrāpyam īrṣyā mātsaryam uddhavaḥ |
> kaukṛtyaṃ styānamiddhaṃ ca paryavasthānam aṣṭadhā || 5.47 ||

> Shamelessness, lack of moral concern, envy, avarice,
> excitation, remorse, torpor, and sleepiness: the
> enveloping afflictions are eightfold.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

What are the enveloping afflictions? The term can also
include the kleśas themselves here, since a sūtra speaks
of suffering conditioned by the envelopment of desire.
In the *Prakaraṇaśāstra*, however, envelopment is eightfold:
shamelessness, lack of moral concern, envy, avarice,
excitation, remorse, torpor, and sleepiness. The following
passage adds the Vaibhāṣika tenfold count.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The passage distinguishes three **contexts of use**. The
sūtra wording permits a kleśa such as desire itself to be
called a paryavasthāna. The *Prakaraṇaśāstra* supplies the
eight-member enumeration in this verse. The next unit gives
a Vaibhāṣika count of ten. This is the same difference in
comparison domain that mattered in the objection at 5.42.

The eight names are enumerated, not arranged into four
explicit pairs. Nor does this passage define
*paryavasthāna* as a discrete later stage of manifestation
after *upakleśa*: broader sūtra usage already prevents
treating the eightfold list as the term's fixed extension.
The individual definitions await the prose following the
5.48 fragment.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The source form `āhlīkyam` is
kept in the continuous Sanskrit; “shamelessness” gives its
locally established sense without installing a silent
emendation. The prose later identifies `uddhavaḥ` through
`auddhatya`, supporting “excitation” provisionally.
“Enveloping affliction” is a conventional rendering of
*paryavasthāna* here, with its extension tied to the cited
source or school.

**Organon reconstruction.** A class name can have different
documented extensions under different schemes. An account
of the Science of Citta should retain the textual setting
alongside each membership assertion. This is a project-level
use of the source's distinct enumerations, not a claim that
one unqualified list exhausts the concept.
