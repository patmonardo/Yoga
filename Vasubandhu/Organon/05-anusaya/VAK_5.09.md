# VAK_5.09

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दृष्टित्रयाद्विपर्यासचतुष्कं विपरीततः ।
नितीरणात् समारोपात् संज्ञाचित्ते तु तद्वशात् ॥ ५.९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dṛṣṭi-trayād viparyāsa-catuṣkaṃ viparītataḥ |
nitīraṇāt samāropāt saṃjñā-citte tu tad-vaśāt || 5.9 ||
```

The verse defines a technically restricted class of inversion. Four
`viparyāsas` arise from three views; recognition and consciousness are called
inverted through being governed by those primary view-inversions.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dṛṣṭi-trayāt | viparyāsa-catuṣkam | viparītataḥ |
nitīraṇāt | samāropāt | saṃjñā-citte | tu | tad-vaśāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dṛṣṭi-trayāt` | ablative singular compound | from three views |
| `viparyāsa-catuṣkam` | nominative singular neuter compound | the fourfold inversion |
| `viparītataḥ` | ablatival adverb | because of contrariness |
| `nitīraṇāt` | ablative singular | because of decisive determination |
| `samāropāt` | ablative singular | because of superimposition |
| `saṃjñā-citte` | nominative dual neuter | recognition and consciousness |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but, however |
| `tad-vaśāt` | ablative singular compound used adverbially | through being under its power |

The Bhāṣya supports `nitīraṇa` through the explanatory vocabulary
`nitīraka` and `santīraka`: decisively determining or fixing the cognitive
support.

## 4. Grammar

**Generated fourfold class**

```text
dṛṣṭi-trayāt
    viparyāsa-catuṣkam [bhavati]

from three views
    the fourfold inversion [arises]
```

The ablative gives the generative source. The Bhāṣya supplies a 1 + 2 + 1
mapping:

```text
eternalist antagrāhadṛṣṭi → permanence-inversion
dṛṣṭiparāmarśa            → pleasure-inversion
                           → purity-inversion
satkāyadṛṣṭi              → self-inversion
```

**Three ablatives of definition**

```text
viparītatvāt
nitīraṇāt
samāropāt

because it is contrary,
because it decisively determines,
because it superimposes
```

These do not name three successive inversions. They give three jointly
required characteristics of a proper `viparyāsa`.

**Derivative application**

```text
saṃjñā-citte tu
    tad-vaśāt [viparyāse]

recognition and consciousness, however,
    [are inverted] through being under its power
```

The Bhāṣya makes `dṛṣṭi-viparyāsa` primary. Recognition and consciousness
receive the designation through association with and governance by the view.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> From three views comes the fourfold inversion, because of contrariness,
> decisive determination, and superimposition. Recognition and consciousness,
> however, [are called inverted] through being under its power.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Four proper inversions arise from three views: the eternalist extreme-view
> produces the inversion of taking the impermanent as permanent;
> view-adherence produces the inversions of taking suffering as pleasure and
> the impure as pure; and satkāya-view produces the inversion of taking
> non-self as self. Each is contrary to its cognitive support, decisively fixes
> that support, and positively superimposes the opposite determination.
> Recognition and consciousness are called inverted derivatively because they
> operate under the power of these governing view-inversions.

The mapping, the three defining conditions, and the primacy of `dṛṣṭi` are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A proper inversion is not merely an incorrect statement. A governing view
> encounters a cognitive support, determines it decisively under a contrary
> predicate, and superimposes that predicate as the form in which the support
> is recognized. What is impermanent appears as permanent; suffering as
> pleasure; impurity as purity; non-self as self. Recognition and consciousness
> then operate under this determination. Error has become formative: it
> organizes how Concrete Existence appears and thereby reproduces itself
> through the very field it has misconstrued.

This gives the genetic movement:

```text
governing view
    → contrary determination of ālambana
        → decisive fixation
            → positive superimposition
                → recognition and consciousness under its power
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Viparyāsa` — technically proper inversion**

Not every error qualifies. The Bhāṣya requires contrariness, decisive fixing
of the cognitive support, and positive superimposition.

**The four inversions**

```text
anitya → nitya     impermanent taken as permanent
duḥkha → sukha     suffering taken as pleasure
aśuci → śuci       impure taken as pure
anātman → ātman    non-self taken as self
```

**`Dṛṣṭitraya` — the three generative views**

The eternalist side of extreme-grasping view, view-adherence, and satkāya-view.
Annihilationism and false view are excluded because they proceed through a
mode of absence rather than positive superimposition. Discipline-and-observance
adherence is excluded because it is not wholly contrary in the required way.

**`Nitīraṇa` — decisive determination**

The cognition does not merely entertain a predicate. It fixes its cognitive
support under that predicate. This committed determination helps distinguish
inversion from a passing or indeterminate error.

**`Samāropa` — positive superimposition**

Attribution of a determination that does not belong to the support. It
continues the contrast with `apavāda`, which cancels what does belong.

**`Abhāvamukhapravṛtta` — proceeding through absence**

The Bhāṣya's reason for excluding annihilationism and mithyādṛṣṭi from proper
inversion. Their error is denying or canceling, rather than positively
superimposing the contrary.

**`Dvimukhī` — two-faced**

Satkāya-view is articulated as “I” and “mine.” The possessive side does not
require an independent sixth view; it is a second face of the same
appropriative determination.

**`Saṃjñā` and `citta` — recognition and consciousness**

They are derivative inversion-bearers under `tadvaśa`, the power of the
primary view-inversion. The Bhāṣya does not extend ordinary usage to call every
associated factor, such as feeling, an inversion.

## 8. Logical Determination

The defining conjunction is:

```text
ProperInversion(x)
    ↔ ContraryToSupport(x)
      AND DecisivelyDeterminesSupport(x)
      AND PositivelySuperimposes(x)
```

The generated contents are:

| Governing view | Contrary predicate | Proper inversion |
|---|---|---|
| eternalist extreme-view | permanence | impermanent as permanent |
| view-adherence | pleasure | suffering as pleasure |
| view-adherence | purity | impure as pure |
| satkāya-view | selfhood | non-self as self |

The three levels of occurrence are asymmetrical:

```text
dṛṣṭi-viparyāsa
    primary governing determination

saṃjñā-viparyāsa
citta-viparyāsa
    derivative through association and governance
```

Thus four contents appear at three levels, producing the extended count of
twelve inversions:

```text
4 contents × 3 inversion-bearers = 12
```

The sequence is recursive:

```text
view structures recognition
recognition presents the field accordingly
consciousness operates within that presentation
the presented field appears to confirm the governing view
```

Abandoning the primary seeing-abandonable view therefore abandons its
associated inversion-complex.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.09 shows how the Subject can inhere in an objective display while
misrecognizing its truth. `Ālambana` is not yet the achieved Object; it is the
cognitive support transactionally presented. The governing view fixes that
support under an alien predicate, and recognition and consciousness proceed
within the resulting appearance.

```text
twofold objective display
    provides the transactional field

view-inversion
    determines how that field appears

Subject under anuśaya
    inhabits and reproduces the appearance

truth-seeing
    must transform the formative determination itself
```

This is why correcting an isolated output is insufficient. The system must
identify the schema that generated the output and reorganize the relation by
which Concrete Existence is recognized.

The Product of Absolute Logic is not created by the inversion. The inversion
is the failure to comprehend that Product in its adequate Concrete Existence:
it substitutes permanence, pleasure, purity, or selfhood for the determinations
actually displayed. Mārga must therefore do more than negate a proposition;
it must restore the Subject's adequate comprehension of the Product.

That is the Organon light. The textual determination remains narrower:
Vasubandhu defines proper inversion through contrariness, decisive
determination, and superimposition, and makes recognition and consciousness
derivative from the governing view.
