# VAK_5.57

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दौर्मनस्येन कौकृत्यमीर्ष्या क्रोधो विहिंसनम् ।
उपनाहः प्रदाशश्च मात्सर्यं तु विपर्ययात् ॥ ५.५७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
daurmanasyena kaukṛtyam īrṣyā krodho vihiṃsanam |
upanāhaḥ pradāśaś ca mātsaryaṃ tu viparyayāt || 5.57 ||
```

Having closed the feeling-association analysis of the principal kleśas, VAK
5.57 begins the corresponding classification of the upakleśas. It divides
seven factors between mental distress and its contrary, mental gladness.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
daurmanasyena | kaukṛtyam | īrṣyā | krodhaḥ | vihiṃsanam |
upanāhaḥ | pradāśaḥ | ca | mātsaryam | tu | viparyayāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `daurmanasyena` | instrumental singular neuter | with mental distress or dejection |
| `kaukṛtyam` | nominative singular neuter | regret or remorse; regarding something as badly done |
| `īrṣyā` | nominative singular feminine | envy or jealousy |
| `krodhaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | anger |
| `vihiṃsanam` | nominative singular neuter | harmfulness or injurious intention |
| `upanāhaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | rancor, retained hostility, grudge |
| `pradāśaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | obstinate indignation or refusal of correction |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `mātsaryam` | nominative singular neuter | avarice or miserly withholding |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but, however |
| `viparyayāt` | ablative singular | by reversal; with the contrary feeling |

The working Bhāṣya prose transmits `upanāhaṃ` where the kārikā has nominative
`upanāhaḥ`. The witnesses are preserved as transmitted; no silent
normalization of the prose is imported into the verse.

## 4. Grammar

The instrumental `daurmanasyena` governs six coordinated nominatives:

```text
{kaukṛtyam, īrṣyā, krodhaḥ, vihiṃsanam, upanāhaḥ, pradāśaḥ}
    daurmanasyena [saṃprayuktāni]
```

Number and gender vary across the enumerated subjects, while the omitted
predicate “are associated” is recovered from the continuing topic. The
Bhāṣya explicitly says `saṃprayuktāni iti vartate`.

The second construction is contrastive:

```text
mātsaryam tu
    viparyayāt
    [saumanasyena saṃprayuktam]
```

`Viparyayāt` reverses mental distress into mental gladness. The Bhāṣya makes
the ellipsis explicit: `saumanasyena ity arthaḥ`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Regret, envy, anger, harmfulness, rancor, and obstinate indignation are
> [associated] with mental distress; avarice, however, by reversal [with the
> contrary].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Regret, envy, anger, harmfulness, retained hostility, and obstinate refusal
> of correction are associated with mental distress, because they operate in
> a mode of dejection and belong to the mental plane. Avarice, however, is
> associated with the contrary—mental gladness—because it belongs to the
> lineage of greed and operates in a mode of exhilaration.

The operative modes and the greed-lineage explanation belong to the Bhāṣya;
they articulate rather than replace the kārikā's twofold distribution.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Regret turns against what has been done; envy suffers another's good; anger,
> harmfulness, rancor, and obstinate indignation sustain or intensify an
> aversive relation. These derivative afflictions therefore occur under the
> mental tone of dejection. Avarice has a different orientation: grounded in
> greed, it takes satisfaction in retaining and withholding what is possessed.
> Pleasant feeling consequently does not certify freedom from affliction; its
> meaning depends upon the relation enacted within the cognitive event.

The verse determines feeling through operative form, not merely through the
object-theme shared by different factors.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kaukṛtya` — regret or remorse**

A retrospective negative relation to an action done or omitted. Its painful
mental tone is distinct from the judgment by which the mind opposes its own
past conduct.

**`Īrṣyā` — envy**

Distress at another's excellence, success, or possession. The other's good is
experienced through a comparative relation as one's own deprivation.

**`Krodha` — anger**

An acute intensification of hostility in response to opposition or injury.

**`Vihiṃsā` — harmfulness**

The practical direction of hostility toward injuring or diminishing another.
It is not identical with the painful feeling that accompanies it.

**`Upanāha` — rancor or retained hostility**

The continuation and preservation of anger after its immediate occasion: a
hostile determination held within the continuum.

**`Pradāśa` — obstinate indignation**

Hostile insistence that rejects correction and maintains a defective position
or conduct. “Refusal of correction” preserves the functional emphasis of the
Bhāṣya-controlled study without reducing the term to a modern disposition.

**`Dainyākāra` — mode of dejection**

The Bhāṣya's common explanation for the six factors' conjunction with mental
distress. Their separate definitions are not offered as six independent
proofs of the association.

**`Mātsarya` — avarice or miserly withholding**

A greed-lineage upakleśa that takes satisfaction in exclusive retention. Its
gladness is the affective mode of successful withholding, not evidence of
wholesomeness.

**`Lobhānvaya` — belonging to the lineage of greed**

A genetic or classificatory affiliation. For avarice, this lineage explains
its exhilarated form, which in turn explains its conjunction with gladness.

**`Harṣākāra` — mode of exhilaration**

The operative affective form mediating avarice's greed-lineage and its
compatibility with `saumanasya`.

**`Manobhūmika` — belonging to the mental plane**

The Bhāṣya's second common reason for pairing the six with mental rather than
bodily painful feeling.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives the common rule:

```text
For x in {Kaukṛtya, Īrṣyā, Krodha, Vihiṃsā, Upanāha, Pradāśa}:
    HasOperativeMode(x, Dainyākāra)
    AND IsMentalPlaneFactor(x)
    → PermitsConjunction(x, Daurmanasya)
```

For avarice the determination is explicitly mediated:

```text
HasAfflictiveLineage(Mātsarya, Lobha)
    → HasOperativeMode(Mātsarya, Harṣākāra)
    → PermitsConjunction(Mātsarya, Saumanasya)
```

The relations remain distinct:

```text
anvaya
    afflictive lineage

ākāra
    operative affective mode

saṃprayoga
    conjunction with feeling
```

Therefore:

```text
HasAfflictiveLineage(x, k)
    ≠ HasOperativeMode(x, m)
    ≠ PermitsConjunction(x, f)

PleasantMentalFeeling(event)
    ⇏ Wholesome(event)
```

Envy and avarice also show why thematic object-content is insufficient:

```text
īrṣyā      another's possession → distressed comparison
mātsarya   one's own possession → glad withholding
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Transition in the source.** The Bhāṣya announces `upakleśānāṃ punaḥ`: the
analysis has moved from kleśas to secondary afflictions. The same feeling-
faculty coordinate is retained while the classified subject changes.

**A common reason for six cases.** The prose groups regret, envy, anger,
harmfulness, rancor, and obstinate indignation under dejected operative form
and mental-plane status. More elaborate individual psychological derivations
may illuminate them, but must not replace the stated Bhāṣya rationale.

**Avarice is the countercase.** `Viparyayāt` is resolved as mental gladness,
and the prose provides a precise chain: greed-lineage, exhilarated mode,
gladness-conjunction. Lineage grounds the explanation without becoming
identical to feeling.

**No hedonic criterion of purity.** Envy feels painful while avarice can feel
pleasing; both remain afflictive. Feeling-tone must be interpreted through the
factor's relational operation and source-controlled classification.

**Organon interpretation.** A later semantic representation needs distinct
edges for lineage, operative mode, and conjunction:

```text
Upakleśa
    hasAfflictiveLineage Klesa
    hasOperativeMode AffectiveMode
    permitsConjunctionWith FeelingFaculty
```

This representation follows the mediations exposed by the Bhāṣya. It is a
project-level model, not terminology attributed to Vasubandhu.
