# VAK 5.55 Bhāṣya — Afflictions and Feeling Faculties

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sukhābhyāṃ saṃprayukto hi rāgo dveṣo viparyayāt |
> mohaḥ sarvair asaddṛṣṭir mano-duḥkha-sukhena tu || 5.55 ||

> Attachment is conjoined with bodily pleasure and mental gladness; hostility
> with the reverse pair, bodily pain and mental distress. Delusion is conjoined
> with all five feeling-faculties; false view with mental distress and mental
> gladness.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Of these five faculties beginning with pleasure, with which
faculty is each affliction or secondary affliction conjoined?
Attachment is conjoined with the two pleasant faculties:
bodily pleasure and mental gladness. Hostility is conjoined
with the reverse pair, the two painful faculties: bodily pain
and mental distress. Attachment and hostility have respectively
glad and dejected modes, and both belong to the six-Cognition
plane.

Delusion is conjoined with all five feeling faculties,
because ignorance can be conjoined with every kleśa.
False view is conjoined with mental pain and pleasure:
mental distress and mental gladness. The prose identifies
the factor as *mithyādṛṣṭi* and assigns those feelings,
respectively, in relation to meritorious and evil actions.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The passage maps **conjunction** (`saṃprayoga`), not
identity or genetic production. The Bhāṣya resolves the
Kārikā's compact pairs as follows:

| Afflictive factor | Feeling faculties named here |
|---|---|
| Attachment (*rāga*) | Bodily pleasure (*sukha*), mental gladness (*saumanasya*) |
| Hostility (*dveṣa*) | Bodily pain (*duḥkha*), mental distress (*daurmanasya*) |
| Delusion (*moha* / *avidyā*) | All five |
| False view (*mithyādṛṣṭi*) | Mental distress and mental gladness |

The fifth faculty is neutral feeling or equanimity
(*upekṣā*). The present verse states delusion's relation
to all five; 5.56 will qualify the apparently narrower
rows with a general equanimity rule. Thus the pairs here
should not be frozen as complete lifetime ranges before
the immediate continuation is read.

For false view, `puṇyakarmaṇāṃ pāpakarmaṇāṃ ca yathākramam`
sets a strict order: mental distress in relation to meritorious
actions, mental gladness in relation to evil actions. The
Bhāṣya does not explain the psychological route in this unit.
Feeling supplies an affective register; false view remains a
distinct affliction.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Sukhābhyām` refers to bodily
pleasure plus mental gladness, not to two indistinguishable
pleasant feelings. `Viparyayāt` reverses that pair to
bodily pain plus mental distress. `Asaddṛṣṭi` is specified
by the prose as `mithyādṛṣṭi`, so “false view” is narrower
than “defective view” in general. `Sarvaiḥ` ranges over
all five feeling faculties, including equanimity.

**Organon reconstruction.** A cognitive event can be
determined by its feeling faculty, cognitive Basis,
and afflictive factor without identifying those relations.
Conjunction does not by itself make a pleasant feeling
wholesome or make the feeling the cause of the kleśa.
This is a project-level distinction for the Science of
Citta, separate from the source's explicit compatibility
statements.
