# VAK_5.16

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
न रागस्तस्य वर्ज्यत्वान्न द्वेषोऽनपकारतः ।
न मानो न परामर्शौ शान्तशुद्ध्यग्रभावतः ॥ ५.१६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
na rāgas tasya varjyatvān na dveṣo 'napakārataḥ |
na māno na parāmarśau śānta-śuddhy-agrabhāvataḥ || 5.16 ||
```

The verse explains why attachment, resistance, conceit, and the two
overvaluations cannot take the uncontaminated cessation and path as their
cognitive supports.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
na | rāgaḥ | tasya | varjyatvāt | na | dveṣaḥ | anapakārataḥ |
na | mānaḥ | na | parāmarśau | śānta-śuddhi-agra-bhāvataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `na` | negative particle | not |
| `rāgaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | attachment, passion |
| `tasya` | genitive singular pronoun | with respect to it; of it |
| `varjyatvāt` | ablative singular abstract noun | because of being relinquishable |
| `dveṣaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | resistance, hostility, aversion |
| `anapakārataḥ` | ablative singular | because there is no injury or harm |
| `mānaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | conceit, self-elevation |
| `parāmarśau` | nominative dual masculine | the two overvaluing adherences |
| `śānta-śuddhi-agra-bhāvataḥ` | ablative compound | because of being peaceful, pure, and supreme |

The dual `parāmarśau` denotes `dṛṣṭiparāmarśa` and
`śīlavrataparāmarśa`. The repeated ablatives give reasons for the exclusions,
but the Bhāṣya is required to distribute peace, purity, and supremacy across
conceit and the two overvaluations.

## 4. Grammar

**Attachment and relinquishability**

```text
tasya [anāsravasya ālambane] rāgaḥ na
varjyatvāt [rāgasya]

attachment does not [arise] with it as support,
because attachment is relinquishable
```

The compressed pronoun and abstract noun permit ambiguity in isolation. The
Bhāṣya fixes the intended reasoning: `rāga` itself is `varjanīya`, something to
be relinquished. A positive orientation toward an uncontaminated dharma would
instead be wholesome aspiration.

**Resistance and absence of injury**

```text
dveṣaḥ na, an-apakārataḥ

resistance does not [take them as support],
because [cessation and path] do no injury
```

**Three coordinated exclusions**

```text
mānaḥ na
parāmarśau na
śānta-śuddhi-agra-bhāvataḥ
```

The Bhāṣya distributes the rationale: peace excludes self-elevation; actual
purity excludes the false purity-grasp of discipline-overvaluation; genuine
supremacy excludes grasping an inferior view as supreme.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Attachment does not [take the uncontaminated as support], because it is
> relinquishable; nor resistance, because there is no injury; nor conceit, nor
> the two overvaluations, because [cessation and path] are peaceful, pure, and
> supreme.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Attachment cannot take cessation or path as an uncontaminated cognitive
> support, because afflicted attachment is itself relinquishable, unlike
> wholesome aspiration toward a wholesome dharma. Resistance cannot, because
> cessation and path cause no injury. Conceit and the two overvaluations cannot,
> because cessation and path are genuinely peaceful, pure, and supreme.

The contrast with wholesome aspiration and the precise allocation of the
three object-characters are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An affliction is possible only where its characteristic relation can be
> enacted. Cessation and path may be denied, doubted, or left unknown, but they
> cannot sustain every defective relation. They provide no injury for
> resistance, no disturbance for self-elevation, no false purity for the
> overvaluation of observances, and no inferior content to be falsely elevated
> as supreme. A truthful predicate applied to an adequate object is not
> transformed into error merely because the same word can occur in an
> afflicted judgment.

The governing principle is:

```text
affliction-form
    requires a determinate object-condition

object lacks that condition
    → the affliction-form is structurally inadmissible
```

This is a semantics of relations, not a blacklist of predicates.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Rāga` and `kuśaladharmacchanda`**

The Bhāṣya distinguishes afflicted attachment from wholesome aspiration toward
a wholesome dharma. Therefore a positive orientation toward liberation is not
automatically attachment. The mode of relation determines the classification.

**`Varjanīya` — to be relinquished**

Attachment is relinquishable. If an alleged attachment toward an
uncontaminated dharma had the character of wholesome aspiration, it would no
longer be the `rāga` that the path abandons.

**`Dveṣa / pratigha` — resistance or hostility**

The verse uses `dveṣa`; the Bhāṣya explains through `pratigha`. Its required
object-condition is an `apakāravastu`, something represented as injurious.
Cessation and path do not possess that character.

**`Māna / unnati` — conceit as self-elevation**

Conceit requires an elevation of the appropriated self. The peaceful character
of cessation and path does not provide the appropriate support for this
operation.

**`Śīlavrataparāmarśa` — overvaluation of disciplines and observances**

Its purity-grasp falsely assigns purity or liberative efficacy. Since
cessation and path are `bhūtārthaśuddha`, genuinely pure in their determination,
truthfully recognizing that purity is not this affliction.

**`Dṛṣṭiparāmarśa` — overvaluation of views**

The Bhāṣya defines its relevant operation as taking what is inferior to be
supreme. Since cessation and path are genuinely supreme, predicating supremacy
of them does not instantiate the erroneous relation.

**`Śānta`, `śuddha`, `agra` — peaceful, pure, supreme**

These are positive object-determinations of cessation and path. Their truth is
what blocks specific afflicted relations; they are not merely honorific
labels.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse establishes a compatibility rule:

```text
RequiresObjectCondition(AfflictionType, Condition)
AND Lacks(ObjectSupport, Condition)
    → CannotCharacteristicallyTake(AfflictionType, ObjectSupport)
```

Applied locally:

```text
Resistance requires Injury
CessationAndPath lack Injury
    → NoResistanceRelation

DisciplineOvervaluation requires FalselyAttributedPurity
CessationAndPath possess GenuinePurity
    → NoDisciplineOvervaluationRelation

ViewOvervaluation requires InferiorTakenAsSupreme
CessationAndPath are GenuinelySupreme
    → NoViewOvervaluationRelation
```

Surface predicates do not determine relation-type:

```text
Desires(x, WholesomeDharma)
    ⇏ Attachment(x)

Predicates(Pure, GenuinelyPureObject)
    ⇏ DisciplineOvervaluation(x)

Predicates(Supreme, GenuinelySupremeObject)
    ⇏ ViewOvervaluation(x)
```

The classification depends upon correspondence between predicate, object, and
operative intention.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.16 shows the higher Objectivity of Mārga operating as a norm, not merely
appearing as a content. Path and cessation determine which relations are
possible toward them because their objective characters—peace, actual purity,
and supremacy—are already determinate. Objectivity therefore constrains the
Subject's transaction; it is not whatever the Subject happens to predicate.

This makes the unity of Theory and Path-bhūmi exact:

```text
Theory
    determines what cessation and path truly are

Path-bhūmi
    gives that determination achieved actuality

Objective adequacy
    distinguishes a true relation from an afflicted imitation
```

All four terms operate within this Path Objectivity:

```text
hetu
    the actual object-character that grounds or excludes a relation

phala
    the cognition and practical orientation produced through that relation

āśraya
    the achieved Path-level in which wholesome orientation can operate

ālambana
    cessation and path as the objectively determined truth-field
```

Thus `ālambana` is not passive material onto which any predicate may be
projected. Its own determination enters into the possibility of the relation.
Nor is `āśraya` a neutral container: at the higher Path-level it is the
achieved basis capable of `kuśaladharmacchanda` rather than `rāga`. The actual
object-character functions as `hetu`; the resulting adequate or inadequate
cognition is `phala`.

This corrects a merely subjective psychology of affliction. Error is neither
a free-floating state inside the Subject nor a stain located in the Object.
It is the concrete failure of their determinate relation. Conversely, truthful
recognition of purity and supremacy is not overvaluation because the objective
truth answers to the predicate.

The higher Objectivity of Mārga therefore judges and transforms the relation
from within. It preserves positive aspiration while abandoning attachment,
true valuation while abandoning overvaluation, and determinate elevation into
the Path while abandoning self-elevation.

This is Organon reconstruction. Its textual ground is the Bhāṣya's
object-specific compatibility arguments and its explicit distinction between
afflicted attachment and wholesome aspiration.
