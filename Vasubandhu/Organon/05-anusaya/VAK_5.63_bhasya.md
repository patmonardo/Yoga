# VAK 5.63 Bhāṣya — One Exhaustion, Repeated Disconnection

## Kārikā Anchor

> sakṛt kṣayo visaṃyogalābhas teṣāṃ punaḥ punaḥ |
> pratipakṣodayaphalaprāptīndriyavivṛddhiṣu || 5.63 ||

> Their exhaustion occurs once; acquisition of disconnection
> from them occurs repeatedly, when the counteragent arises,
> the fruits are attained, and the faculties develop.

`Teṣām` refers to the afflictions of the prose question.
The three expressions in the second line name six possible
occasions in the Bhāṣya's count: one counteragent occasion,
four fruit attainments, and one faculty transition.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Does advancing to a further special stage of the path
produce a further abandonment of the afflictions? No. Every
affliction has a single exhaustion. Each is exhausted by
the path of abandonment appropriate to it. What can occur
again and again is the acquisition of disconnection from
those afflictions. On how many occasions? On six: when the
counteragent arises, when the fruits are attained, and when
the faculties develop.

Here “counteragent” means the path of liberation
(`vimuktimārga`). The fruits are the four fruits of the
ascetic path; development of the faculties means transition
of faculties (`indriyasaṃcāra`). On those occasions one
acquires disconnection from an affliction. The rule must be
applied as appropriate: for some afflictions it occurs on
six occasions, for others on as few as two.

That very same disconnection receives the designation
`parijñā` in these different conditions. There are two
*parijñās*: knowledge as *parijñā* and abandonment as
*parijñā*. The former is knowledge with outflows
(`sāsravaṃ jñānam`); the latter is abandonment itself.
The name of the cause is applied to its result.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The question distinguishes **further path progress** from
**further destruction of an already destroyed kleśa**.
`Sakṛt kṣayaḥ` rejects the latter: a given affliction is
exhausted once, by its corresponding `prahāṇamārga`.
`Visaṃyogalābhaḥ punaḥ punaḥ` names a different relation:
disconnection may be acquired repeatedly under changed
path conditions. The text does not say the same kleśa
returns between these occasions.

The prose narrows `pratipakṣa` here to `vimuktimārga`.
That is a local use within the six-occasion count, not a
retraction of 5.61's four counteragent functions. Four
`śrāmaṇyaphalas` supply four further occasions, and
`indriyasaṃcāra` the sixth. `Yathāyogam` blocks the
inference that each disconnection is acquired on all six:
the range explicitly runs from six down to two.

The same `visaṃyoga` can then be called `parijñā` under
various conditions. The text distinguishes knowledge as
*parijñā* from abandonment as *parijñā*, and explains the
second naming as a transfer from cause to result. The
running reading `sāsravaṃ jñānam` is retained; its doctrinal
scope needs separate review before replacing it with an
expected form. This transitional gloss is necessary for
reading the next verse's question about how many
abandonment-*parijñās* there are.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Kṣaya` is the affliction's
exhaustion; `visaṃyogalābha` is acquisition of disconnection
from it. “Repeatedly” qualifies the latter, never repeated
destruction of the same affliction. The six occasions are
the Bhāṣya's one-plus-four-plus-one unpacking of the verse's
three expressions. `Sāsravaṃ` is rendered literally “with
outflows” and remains a review point.

**Organon reconstruction.** Our Anuśaya:Mārga dyad can
distinguish the irreversible severance of a specific
afflictive relation from repeated determinations of its
attainment along the path. This is a project-level way to
state the source's difference between `kṣaya` and
`visaṃyogalābha`; it does not posit renewed production of
the destroyed kleśa or an implemented state machine.
