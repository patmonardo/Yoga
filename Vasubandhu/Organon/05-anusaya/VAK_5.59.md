# VAK_5.59

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कामे निवारणान्येकविपक्षाहारकृत्यतः ।
द्वयकैताः पञ्चता स्कन्धविघातविचिकित्सनात् ॥ ५.५९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kāme nivaraṇāny ekavipakṣāhārakṛtyataḥ |
dvayakaitāḥ pañcatā skandhavighātavicikitsanāt || 5.59 ||
```

VAK 5.59 leaves the completed feeling-conjunction matrix and reclassifies
selected afflictive factors by their practical function as hindrances.

**Textual caution.** The kārikā witness transmits `dvayakaitāḥ` and
`skandhavighāta`; the Bhāṣya anchor gives `dvyekatā` and `skandhavidhāta`, and
the prose explicitly explains `dvayor ekatā dvyekatā`, “the unity of two.”
The transmitted verse above is preserved verbatim. The translation follows
the prose-secured sense without silently replacing the witness.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kāme | nivaraṇāni | eka-vipakṣa-āhāra-kṛtyataḥ |
[dvy-ekatā / transmitted dvayakaitāḥ] | pañcatā |
skandha-vighāta-vicikitsanāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `kāme` | locative singular neuter | in the desire-domain |
| `nivaraṇāni` | nominative plural neuter | hindrances; factors classified by Path-obstructing function |
| `eka-vipakṣa-āhāra-kṛtyataḥ` | ablative singular compound | because of one counter-condition, nourishment, and function |
| `dvayakaitāḥ` | transmitted, grammatically problematic form | resolved by the Bhāṣya through `dvyekatā`, the unity of two |
| `pañcatā` | nominative singular feminine abstract noun | fivefoldness, the state of being five |
| `skandha-vighāta` | compound member | obstruction of the Path-aggregates; Bhāṣya anchor reads `vidhāta` |
| `vicikitsanāt` | ablative singular action noun | because of doubting or uncertainty |

Here `skandha` is controlled by the prose as the organized Path-complex of
ethical discipline, concentration, and discernment—not the ordinary list of
five appropriated aggregates.

## 4. Grammar

The opening is a locative predication:

```text
nivaraṇāni
    kāme [santi]
```

The technical five hindrances belong to the desire-domain. The long ablative
compound gives the reason that each of two pairs counts as one hindrance:

```text
dvyekatā
    eka-vipakṣa-āhāra-kṛtyataḥ
```

The prose parses `dvyekatā` as `dvayor ekatā`: unity of two. It also equates
`vipakṣa`, `pratipakṣa`, and `anāhāra` in this context, so the shared relations
are nourishment, non-nourishment/counteragent, and operation.

The final ablative compound explains fivefoldness:

```text
pañcatā
    skandha-vighāta-vicikitsanāt
```

The compact compound permits competing assignments of the paired hindrances
to the Path-aggregates. The Bhāṣya preserves these accounts rather than
settling them through grammar alone.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The hindrances are in the desire-domain. [There is] unity of two because of
> one counter-condition, nourishment, and function. [There is] fivefoldness
> because of obstruction of the aggregates and doubt.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five hindrances belong to the desire-domain. Torpor and sleepiness count
> as one hindrance, and agitation and remorse as another, because the members
> of each pair share one nourishment, one non-nourishment or counteragent, and
> one operative effect. The classification is fivefold because these
> hindrances obstruct the Path-aggregates of discipline, concentration, and
> discernment, while doubt prevents determination regarding the Truths.

The Bhāṣya preserves more than one account of how the paired hindrances are
assigned to concentration and discernment; the study translation therefore
does not force a single disputed ordering into the kārikā.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A hindrance is not merely a named mental factor but a practical unit of
> obstruction. Numerically distinct factors can form one intervention-unit
> when they are fed by the same conditions, opposed by the same remedy, and
> disable the same capacity. The five hindrances articulate failures of
> ethical orientation, collectedness, discernment, and determinate relation to
> truth. Their unity is functional and Path-relative: it neither erases the
> distinct factors nor derives from their lexical similarity.

The verse thus shifts the question from “what kind of factor is this?” to
“what sustains it, what counters it, and what does it prevent?”

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The five `nivaraṇas` — hindrances**

```text
1. kāmacchanda          sensual desire
2. vyāpāda              ill will
3. styāna-middha        torpor and sleepiness
4. auddhatya-kaukṛtya   agitation and remorse
5. vicikitsā            doubt
```

The technical fivefold class belongs to the desire-domain because the sūtra
characterizes it as a complete unwholesome mass.

**`Nivaraṇa` — hindrance**

A factor or paired structure considered under its obstruction of the Path.
This functional classification does not replace its other determinations as
kleśa, upakleśa, or paryavasthāna.

**`Āhāra` — nourishment**

The conditions that feed, renew, and maintain a hindrance. The analysis treats
obstruction as relationally sustained rather than as an isolated item.

**`Anāhāra`, `vipakṣa`, `pratipakṣa` — non-nourishment and counteragent**

The Bhāṣya gives these one practical meaning here: the contrary condition
that does not feed and directly opposes the hindrance.

**The nourishment of `styāna-middha`**

```text
tandrā              drowsiness or languor
arati               dissatisfaction with engagement
vijṛmbhikā           yawning
bhakte asamatā       immoderation in food
cetaso līnatva       sinking or slackness of mind
```

Its counter-condition is `ālokasaṃjñā`, the perception or conception of
light. Its shared function is `citta-laya`, driving the mind toward sinking.

**The nourishment of `auddhatya-kaukṛtya`**

The prose lists discursive preoccupation with relatives, locality, the
transmitted `amara-vitarka`, and recollection of former laughing, play,
amusement, and enjoyment. `Amara-vitarka` is preserved as transmitted rather
than conjecturally resolved.

Its counter-condition is `śamatha`, calming or tranquility. Its shared
function is keeping the mind unpacified (`avyupaśānta-citta`).

**`Skandha` — Path-aggregate in this passage**

The prose names `śīla-skandha`, `samādhi-skandha`, and `prajñā-skandha`:
functional accumulations of discipline, concentration, and discernment.

**`Vicikitsā` — doubt**

Uncertainty concerning the Truths where concentration and discernment fail.
It is counted as its own hindrance rather than folded into either paired unit.

## 8. Logical Determination

The paired classification is relation-indexed:

```text
SameNourishment(x, y)
AND SameCounterCondition(x, y)
AND SameObstructiveFunction(x, y)
    → SameHindranceUnit(x, y)
```

For the first pair:

```text
HindranceUnit(Styāna, Middha)
    nourishment: five sinking conditions
    counterCondition: Ālokasaṃjñā
    function: CittaLaya
```

For the second:

```text
HindranceUnit(Auddhatya, Kaukṛtya)
    nourishment: four classes of discursive recollection
    counterCondition: Śamatha
    function: AvyupaśāntaCitta
```

Functional unity does not erase numerical difference:

```text
SameHindranceUnit(x, y)
    ⇏ SameDharma(x, y)
```

The principal received account of fivefoldness gives:

```text
Kāmacchanda + Vyāpāda      → obstruct Śīla-skandha
Styāna + Middha            → obstruct Prajñā-skandha
Auddhatya + Kaukṛtya       → obstruct Samādhi-skandha
Lack of Samādhi + Prajñā   → Vicikitsā concerning the Truths
```

But an alternative (`apare`) reverses the two middle assignments:

```text
Styāna + Middha            → obstruct Samādhi-skandha
Auddhatya + Kaukṛtya       → obstruct Prajñā-skandha
```

Both assignments must remain attributed rather than synthesized into a false
single source position.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Desire-domain restriction.** Torpor, agitation, and doubt may be discussed
across domains elsewhere, but the canonical set of five nivaraṇas is here
restricted to the desire-domain through its characterization as wholly
unwholesome.

**Factor identity versus intervention-unit identity.** Torpor is not
sleepiness, and agitation is not remorse. Each pair counts as one only under
the practical relations of nourishment, counter-condition, and obstructive
function.

**Plural source voices.** A received explanation assigns the pairs to
discernment and concentration; `apare` reverses them according to which factor
threatens the practitioner exercising each capacity. A further view (`anye`)
organizes the hindrances sequentially around entry into samādhi, cultivation
of calming and insight, and subsequent examination of Dharma. The Bhāṣya does
not flatten these rationales.

**Internal practical reason.** The passage itself already supplies a complete
intervention schema:

```text
hindrance
    ← nourished by conditions
    → opposed by counter-condition
    → disables Path-capacity
```

No external psychological reconstruction is needed to make the classification
operational.

**Organon interpretation.** A later Dataset can preserve factor-level and
intervention-level identities separately:

```text

    hasNourishment ConditionSet
    hasCounterCondition PathFactor
```

This is a source-preserving formalization, not vocabulary back-projected into
the kārikā or Bhāṣya.
