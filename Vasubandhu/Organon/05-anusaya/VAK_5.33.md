# VAK_5.33

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ततोऽन्तग्रहणं तस्माच्छीलामर्शस्ततो दृशः ।
रागः स्वदृष्टौ मानश्च द्वेषोऽन्यत्रेत्यनुक्रमः ॥ ५.३३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
tato 'ntagrahaṇaṃ tasmāc chīlāmarśas tato dṛśaḥ |
rāgaḥ svadṛṣṭau mānaś ca dveṣo 'nyatrety anukramaḥ || 5.33 ||
```

The verse completes the genetic sequence of the ten anuśayas begun in VAK
5.32. The Bhāṣya calls the whole series their `pravṛttikrama`, their order of
arising or manifestation.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
tataḥ | anta-grahaṇam | tasmāt | śīla-āmarśaḥ | tataḥ | dṛśaḥ |
rāgaḥ | sva-dṛṣṭau | mānaḥ | ca | dveṣaḥ | anyatra | iti | anukramaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `tataḥ` | ablatival adverb | from that, thereafter |
| `anta-grahaṇam` | nominative singular neuter | grasping an extreme |
| `tasmāt` | ablative singular pronoun | from that |
| `śīla-āmarśaḥ` | nominative singular masculine, abbreviated expression | attachment to discipline and observance |
| `dṛśaḥ` | compressed form involving `dṛś` or `dṛṣṭi` | of or with respect to view; resolved by the Bhāṣya as view-attachment |
| `rāgaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | attachment |
| `sva-dṛṣṭau` | locative singular feminine | with respect to one's own view |
| `mānaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | conceit or self-elevation |
| `dveṣaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | hostility or aversion |
| `anyatra` | locative adverb | elsewhere; toward what is other or opposed |
| `iti anukramaḥ` | closing nominal statement | such is the sequence |

The Bhāṣya explicitly carries `āmarśa` forward in the compressed `tato dṛśaḥ`,
yielding `dṛṣṭiparāmarśa`, attachment to or overvaluation of views.

## 4. Grammar

**Continuation from self-view**

```text
tataḥ [satkāyadṛṣṭeḥ] anta-grahaṇam
tasmāt [anta-grahaṇāt] śīla-āmarśaḥ
tataḥ [śīla-vrata-parāmarśāt] dṛṣṭi-parāmarśaḥ
```

The repeated ablatival expressions mark derivational transitions. Their
antecedents and the fuller technical terms are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

**Affective determinations of view**

```text
sva-dṛṣṭau rāgaḥ mānaḥ ca
anyatra dveṣaḥ
```

Attachment and conceit take one's own view as their field. Hostility arises
“elsewhere,” which the main Bhāṣya explanation construes as an opposed other
view.

**Closure**

```text
iti anukramaḥ
```

This names the preceding relations as an ordered sequence. The Bhāṣya sharpens
it to the `pravṛttikrama` of the ten kleśas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> From that comes grasping an extreme; from that, attachment to discipline and
> observance; from that, attachment to views. Then attachment and conceit arise
> regarding one's own view, and hostility elsewhere—such is the sequence.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> From personality-view comes the extreme view that grasps the self as either
> permanent or annihilated. From this comes attachment to discipline and
> observance, because purity is expected through the very extreme adopted.
> That supposed means is then grasped as supreme, producing attachment to
> views. One clings to one's own view and becomes elevated through it, while
> hostility arises toward a view opposed to it. Such is the order in which the
> ten afflictions arise.

The permanent/annihilated alternatives, expected purification, explicit
`dṛṣṭiparāmarśa`, and grounds of attachment, conceit, and hostility are supplied
by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Once the aggregates have been appropriated as self, that constructed self
> demands an absolute fate: permanence or annihilation. The extreme is then
> converted from a speculative determination into a promised practice of
> purification. The practice is elevated into the supreme doctrine; the
> doctrine becomes an object of possessive adhesion and a ground of comparative
> elevation; and whatever contradicts it is determined as hostile. Cognitive
> error has thereby become practical program, identity, status, and conflict.

The stages are not interchangeable. Holding a view, clinging to it, elevating
oneself through it, and hating its contrary are distinct relations generated
in an intelligible order.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Antagrāhadṛṣṭi` — extreme view**

The Bhāṣya expands `antagrahaṇa` as grasping the posited self under the extremes
of permanence (`śāśvata`) or annihilation (`uccheda`). The extreme presupposes
the self-construction produced in VAK 5.32.

**`Śīlavrataparāmarśa` — attachment to discipline and observance**

The full Bhāṣya term behind the verse's `śīlāmarśa`. It arises because one
expects purification through precisely the extreme one has grasped.

**`Dṛṣṭiparāmarśa` — attachment to views**

The Bhāṣya's explicit resolution of `tato dṛśaḥ`, carrying `āmarśa` forward.
What promised purity is now grasped as foremost or supreme.

**`Abhiṣvaṅga` — affective adhesion**

The ground of `rāga` toward one's own view: the view is no longer merely judged
true but clung to as one's own.

**`Unnati-gamana` — movement into elevation**

The ground of `māna`: the adopted view becomes a basis upon which one raises
oneself comparatively.

**`Pratyanīka` — opposed or counterposed**

How the main Bhāṣya explanation determines the other view that becomes the
object of hostility.

**`Pravṛttikrama` — order of arising**

The Bhāṣya's characterization of the entire ten-affliction sequence. It marks
a genetic intelligibility and need not mean that every empirical occurrence
must mechanically instantiate every stage.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 5.32–5.33 form one mediated series:

```text
avidyā / moha
    → vicikitsā
        → mithyāniścaya / mithyādṛṣṭi
            → satkāyadṛṣṭi
                → antagrāhadṛṣṭi
                    → śīlavrataparāmarśa
                        → dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
                            → rāga toward own view
                            → māna through own view
                            → dveṣa toward the opposed view
```

The Bhāṣya supplies a reason for each transition:

```text
FalseSelf
    → requires an extreme temporal determination

AdoptedExtreme
    → is treated as a means of purification

SupposedMeansOfPurity
    → is elevated as supreme view

OwnSupremeView
    → becomes object of attachment
    → becomes ground of self-elevation
    → determines its contrary as opposed

OpposedView
    → becomes object of hostility
```

This differentiates four predicates often collapsed into belief:

```text
Holds(agent, view)
≠ ClingsTo(agent, view)
≠ ElevatesSelfThrough(agent, view)
≠ Hates(agent, opposedView)
```

The fourfold circulation is visible at every transition: the result and
objective determination achieved in one operation becomes the causal ground
and support for the next. Yet each passage requires a new mediating act; no
term merely changes its label.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya presents a `pravṛttikrama`, grounding each
affliction's emergence in a prior determination. False self becomes extreme
view; extreme view becomes a purificatory discipline; discipline becomes
supreme doctrine; doctrine becomes attachment, conceit, and hostility.

**Alternative interpretation of `anyatra`.** Other teachers do not construe
the hostility simply as directed toward a rival doctrine held by someone else.
They take it as hostility toward a view that arose within one's own continuum
and was subsequently abandoned, because the relevant afflictions take a view
in one's own continuum as object. This technical alternative must remain open.

**The production of opposition.** The opposed view is not initially present as
an enemy. It acquires that determination only after one's own view has become
possessed, elevated, and identity-bearing:

```text
other view
    → contrary of my supreme view
        → threat to my elevation
            → object of hostility
```

This is a determinate production of the hostile object, not merely a subjective
feeling added to a ready-made external object.

**Yoga–Organon reconstruction.** The Path must undo more than the terminal
hostility. Because each Product has become the ground of the next operation,
liberation requires tracing the dependency backward—from antagonism through
identity-investment and absolutized practice to the false self-determination
and original non-comprehension that generated the series.
