# VAK_5.11

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
विभवेच्छा न चार्यस्य सम्भवन्ति विधादयः ।
नास्मिता दृष्टिपुष्टत्वात् कौकृत्यं नापि चाशुभम् ॥ ५.११ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
vibhavecchā na cāryasya saṃbhavanti vidhādayaḥ |
nāsmitā dṛṣṭipuṣṭatvāt kaukṛtyaṃ nāpi cāśubham || 5.11 ||
```

The verse continues the `tathā` of VAK 5.10. It asks why several formations
classified as cultivation-abandonable nevertheless do not manifest in an
`ārya`.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vibhava-icchā | na | ca | āryasya | saṃbhavanti |
vidhā-ādayaḥ | na | asmitā | dṛṣṭi-puṣṭatvāt |
kaukṛtyam | na | api | ca | aśubham
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `vibhava-icchā` | nominative singular feminine compound | wish or thirst for nonexistence |
| `āryasya` | genitive singular masculine | for or in the noble practitioner |
| `saṃbhavanti` | third-person plural present | arise, occur, become manifest |
| `vidhā-ādayaḥ` | nominative plural masculine | the modes beginning with the conceit-forms |
| `asmitā` | nominative singular feminine | I-am determination; here interpreted as `asmimāna` |
| `dṛṣṭi-puṣṭatvāt` | ablative singular compound | because of being nourished by views |
| `kaukṛtyam` | nominative singular neuter | remorse, regret over what has been done |
| `aśubham` | nominative singular neuter | unwholesome |

The Bhāṣya glosses `vibhavecchā` as `vibhavatṛṣṇā`, thirst for nonexistence,
and reads the series marked by `ādi` as extending through that thirst.

## 4. Grammar

**Governing subject-location**

```text
āryasya
    vibhavecchā na [saṃbhavati]
    vidhādayaḥ na saṃbhavanti
    asmitā na [saṃbhavati]

for the noble practitioner,
    the wish for nonexistence,
    the modes beginning with conceit,
    and I-am conceit do not arise
```

The plural verb directly agrees with `vidhādayaḥ`; it governs the coordinated
singular subjects by ellipsis. The Bhāṣya extends `ādigrahaṇa`, the force of
“beginning with,” across the cultivation-abandonable series through
`vibhavatṛṣṇā`.

**Causal ablative**

```text
dṛṣṭi-puṣṭatvāt
    because they are nourished by views
```

The cause explains non-manifestation: once the nourishing view is broken, its
dependent formation lacks the strength to arise.

**Final coordinated negation**

```text
kaukṛtyam na api ca aśubham [saṃbhavati]

nor does unwholesome remorse arise
```

The Bhāṣya shows that `aśubham` qualifies `kaukṛtyam`; it is not the predicate
“remorse is not unwholesome.” The context `āryasya` and the verb “arise” remain
understood.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In the noble practitioner, the wish for nonexistence and the modes beginning
> with [conceit] do not arise; nor does I-am conceit, because they are nourished
> by views. Neither does unwholesome remorse arise.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Although they remain classified as cultivation-abandonable, the conceit-
> modes, I-am conceit, the afflictive occupation beginning with deliberate
> killing, the relevant thirsts for existence and nonexistence, and unwholesome
> remorse do not manifest in a noble practitioner. Each depends upon a view
> already destroyed through seeing: satkāya-view nourishes the conceits;
> false view nourishes harmful occupation; annihilationist view nourishes
> thirst for nonexistence; eternalist view nourishes a portion of thirst for
> existence; and doubt generates unwholesome remorse.

The extended series and its specific dependency relations are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Truth-seeing transforms more than explicit belief. By destroying a governing
> view, it disables formations that depended upon that view for their power of
> manifestation. Those formations may remain formally assigned to later
> cultivation, yet they can no longer become active in the noble continuum.
> Seeing therefore acts both directly, by abandoning view, and indirectly, by
> breaking the support through which dependent conceit, craving, harmful
> occupation, and unwholesome remorse could arise. Liberation develops through
> differentiated states: classification, availability, manifestation, and
> completed abandonment cannot be collapsed.

The Bhāṣya's image is `bhagnapṛṣṭha`, “broken-backed”:

```text
nourishing view destroyed
    → dependent affliction loses its operative support
        → it no longer rises into manifestation
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vibhavatṛṣṇā` — thirst for nonexistence**

The Bhāṣya defines `vibhava` here as `traidhātukī anityatā`, impermanence
belonging to the three domains, and `tṛṣṇā` as wishing directed toward that.
This is not liberation: annihilationist desire remains an afflicted relation
to the passing away of appropriated existence.

**A portion of `bhavatṛṣṇā` — thirst for existence**

The `tathā` carried from VAK 5.10 also includes a particular form of thirst for
existence, nourished by eternalist view. Attachment to being and desire for
non-being oppose one another in content while remaining dependent on extreme
views.

**`Vidhādayaḥ` — the series beginning with conceit-modes**

The Bhāṣya includes the ninefold comparative conceits, `asmimāna`, harmful
afflictive occupation, the relevant thirst for existence, and thirst for
nonexistence within the view-nourished series.

**`Asmitā` — I-am conceit in this unit**

The commentary interprets the kārikā through `asmimāna`, the conceit “I am,”
nourished by satkāya-view. It should not be silently equated with every use of
Yoga's technical `asmitā` without comparative analysis.

**`Dṛṣṭipuṣṭa` — nourished by view**

A dependency relation, not mere agreement with a doctrine. The view supplies
the operative condition through which a secondary formation can manifest.

**`Bhagnapṛṣṭhatva` — having its back broken**

The Bhāṣya's metaphor for causal disablement. The dependent formation has not
necessarily completed its formal cultivation-abandonment, but its sustaining
structure has been broken.

**`Kaukṛtya` — remorse**

The relevant member is unwholesome remorse. It is cultivation-abandonable but
does not arise in an ārya because it is generated from doubt, already
abandoned through seeing.

## 8. Logical Determination

The dependency graph is explicit:

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
    → nourishes conceit-modes and asmimāna

mithyādṛṣṭi
    → nourishes harmful afflictive occupation

annihilationist extreme-view
    → nourishes thirst for nonexistence

eternalist extreme-view
    → nourishes a portion of thirst for existence

vicikitsā
    → generates unwholesome remorse
```

Seeing breaks the upstream view, producing an indirect consequence:

```text
AbandonedBySeeing(View)
AND DependsForManifestation(SecondaryAffliction, View)
    → NotManifestableIn(SecondaryAffliction, Arya)
```

But:

```text
NotManifestable(x)
    ⇏ FormallyAbandoned(x)

CultivationAbandonable(x)
AND NotYetAbandoned(x)
    ⇏ PresentlyManifest(x)
```

At least four states must remain distinct:

```text
classified as cultivation-abandonable
operatively capable of manifestation
presently manifest
formally abandoned
```

VAK 5.11 therefore refines `hetu–phala`: removing a supporting cause can
produce the result of non-manifestability before the dependent determination
itself reaches completed abandonment.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse gives the Product an internal state architecture. A determination
cannot be represented adequately by one Boolean property such as present or
absent. Its status includes its type, remaining support, capacity to execute,
actual manifestation, and stage of transformation.

```text
Anuśaya
    available disposition

View-nourishment
    enabling support for manifestation

Paryavasthāna / samudācāra
    active occurrence

Karma
    enacted consequence

Prahāṇa
    completed removal
```

The noble Subject is not defined merely by possessing a true proposition. Its
truth-seeing has altered what operations remain possible within Concrete
Existence. The Subject's achieved state is manifested as a changed field of
possible transactions.

This supplies an implementation principle of unusual precision:

```text
remove or transform a generative support
    → dependent operations become non-executable
without pretending
    → their full lifecycle has already been erased
```

That is a determinate result of Reason in the Product. It prevents both errors:
reporting an affliction as active merely because it remains classified as
un-abandoned, and reporting it as completely eliminated merely because its
current support has been disabled.

This is Organon reconstruction. The textual basis is Vasubandhu's distinction
between cultivation-abandonable status and actual manifestation, together
with the Bhāṣya's explicit dependency graph of view-nourished formations.
