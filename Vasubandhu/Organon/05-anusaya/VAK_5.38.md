# VAK_5.38

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
यथोक्ता एव साविद्या द्विधा दृष्टिविवेचनात् ।
उपादानानि अविद्या तु ग्राहिका नेति मिश्रिता ॥ ५.३८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
yathoktā eva sāvidyā dvidhā dṛṣṭivivecanāt |
upādānāni avidyā tu grāhikā neti miśritā || 5.38 ||
```

VAK 5.38 reclassifies the yokes of VAK 5.37 as four appropriations. Ignorance
is mixed into the appropriation-complexes, while the former view-yoke is
divided into appropriation through views and appropriation through discipline
and observance.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
yathā-uktāḥ | eva | sa-avidyāḥ | dvidhā | dṛṣṭi-vivecanāt |
upādānāni | avidyā | tu | grāhikā | na | iti | miśritā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `yathā-uktāḥ eva` | nominative plural masculine with restrictive particle | precisely those previously stated, namely the yokes |
| `sa-avidyāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | together with ignorance |
| `dvidhā` | indeclinable adverb | in two, twofold |
| `dṛṣṭi-vivecanāt` | ablative singular neuter compound | through separation of the views |
| `upādānāni` | nominative plural neuter | appropriations or graspings |
| `avidyā tu` | nominative singular feminine with adversative particle | ignorance, however |
| `grāhikā` | nominative singular feminine agent adjective | grasping or seizing |
| `na iti` | negation followed by reason-marking `iti` | on the ground that it is not |
| `miśritā` | nominative singular feminine past passive participle | mixed or included in combination |

`Upādāna`, from `upa-ā-√dā`, denotes taking up, laying hold of, or
appropriating. Here it names a functional classification of afflictions, not a
new set of underlying dharmas.

## 4. Grammar

**Formation of the four appropriations**

```text
yathoktāḥ eva [yogāḥ]
    sa-avidyāḥ
    dvidhā dṛṣṭi-vivecanāt
        upādānāni [bhavanti]
```

The Bhāṣya identifies the elided referent of `yathoktāḥ` as the previously
stated yokes. `Sa-avidyāḥ` qualifies them as recombined with ignorance.
`Dṛṣṭi-vivecanāt` states the ground for a further division: six discipline-
and-observance views are extracted from the thirty-six-member view-yoke,
leaving thirty views in appropriation through views.

**Why ignorance is mixed**

```text
avidyā tu
    grāhikā na iti
        miśritā
```

The feminine forms agree with `avidyā`. `Iti` marks the stated reason:
ignorance is mixed among the other appropriations because it is not itself a
grasping power. The Bhāṣya sharpens the reason with two predicates:
`asaṃprakhyāna-lakṣaṇa`, characterized by non-discernment, and `apaṭu`, not
sharp.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Precisely those [yokes] previously stated, together with ignorance, are the
> appropriations, [the view-group becoming] twofold through separation of the
> views. Ignorance, however, is mixed [among them] because it is not a
> grasper.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The previously established yokes become four appropriations when ignorance
> is distributed into them and the view-yoke is divided in two. Desire-yoke
> with ignorance becomes appropriation of desire; existence-yoke with
> ignorance becomes appropriation of self-doctrine; thirty of the views become
> appropriation through views; and the six discipline-and-observance views
> become their own appropriation. Ignorance does not constitute a fifth
> appropriation because its non-discerning, unsharp operation does not itself
> seize; it is therefore included within the other appropriation-complexes.

The identities of the four classes, their exact counts, and the reasons for
separating discipline-and-observance are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Under the relation of appropriation, the afflictive field is reorganized
> according to determinate grasping. Ignorance permeates and conditions this
> field, but does not independently perform its positive act of seizure;
> therefore what roothood separated, grasping-power recombines. At the same
> time, discipline-and-observance must be distinguished from views in general
> because it converts a finite practice into a counterfeit path, directing
> conduct through a false claim to purification. Appropriation is thus neither
> mere obscurity nor mere contact with an object: it is the practical taking-up
> of a determination as governing one's existence and way.

The same factor can be a separate root under one relation and a distributed
condition under another without contradiction.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Upādāna` — appropriation or grasping**

An afflictive operation of taking hold of existence or of a determination.
The Bhāṣya explains the term through `bhava-grahaṇa`, grasping existence.

**`Kāmopādāna` — appropriation of desire**

The desire-yoke recombined with desire-domain ignorance: thirty-four items,
consisting of twenty instances of craving, hostility, conceit, and ignorance,
four doubts, and ten paryavasthānas.

**`Ātmavādopādāna` — appropriation of self-doctrine**

The existence-yoke recombined with higher-domain ignorance: thirty-eight
items, consisting of thirty instances of craving, conceit, and ignorance and
eight doubts. The technical class-name should not be expanded into a claim
that every member is itself a formulated self-view.

**`Dṛṣṭyupādāna` — appropriation through views**

Thirty views remaining after the six discipline-and-observance views have been
extracted from the view-yoke.

**`Śīlavratopādāna` — appropriation through discipline and observance**

The six discipline-and-observance views treated separately because they oppose
the Path and deceive both householders and renunciants.

**`Grāhikā` — grasping or seizing power**

The operative criterion for an independent appropriation. It denotes a
positive taking-hold, not merely a condition that allows grasping to occur.

**`Asaṃprakhyāna-lakṣaṇa` — characterized by non-discernment**

The Bhāṣya's determination of ignorance. Because ignorance fails to articulate
its object clearly, it does not independently execute appropriation.

**`Mārgapratidvandvitva` — opposition to the Path**

One reason for isolating discipline-and-observance appropriation. It installs
an ineffective practice in the functional place of the Path.

**`Ubhayapakṣavipralambhana` — deception of both parties**

The second reason. Householders mistake fasting and similar observances for a
path to heaven; renunciants mistake mere abstention from desired objects for
the attainment of purification.

**Exact appropriation counts**

```text
kāmopādāna             34
ātmavādopādāna         38
dṛṣṭyupādāna           30
śīlavratopādāna         6
```

## 8. Logical Determination

The fourfold reclassification can be stated as:

```text
Kāmayoga + DesireDomainAvidyā
    → Kāmopādāna

Bhavayoga + HigherDomainAvidyā
    → Ātmavādopādāna

Dṛṣṭiyoga − ŚīlavrataViews
    → Dṛṣṭyupādāna

ŚīlavrataViews
    → Śīlavratopādāna
```

Ignorance changes structural position when the governing relation changes:

```text
Under Roothood:
    Avidyā → SeparateInflux, SeparateFlood, SeparateYoke

Under GraspingPower:
    Avidyā → MixedIntoAppropriations
```

The decisive distinction is:

```text
Conditions(x, Grasping)
    ⇏ Executes(x, Grasping)

NonDiscerning(Avidyā)
AND NotSharp(Avidyā)
    → NOT IndependentGrasper(Avidyā)
```

The separation of discipline-and-observance adds a teleological criterion:

```text
Practice(x)
AND FalselyTakenAsSufficientPath(x)
    → OpposesActualPath(x)
    → ClassifyAs(Śīlavratopādāna)
```

Classification follows not isolated properties but the relation among a
factor, its operation, and the end it claims to attain.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya begins with the four yokes, redistributes
ignorance into the desire- and existence-based complexes, and divides the
view-yoke into two. The result is not five appropriations but four, because
ignorance lacks independent grasping power.

**Ground is not operation.** VAK 5.36 separated ignorance as the generative
root of the afflictions. VAK 5.38 does not revoke that determination; it judges
the same factor under a new universal:

```text
ignorance grounds the obscured field of appropriation
    ≠
ignorance performs determinate appropriation
```

Failure to discern enables the seizure of a determination without itself
being that seizure. Removing ignorance and releasing appropriation are
therefore internally related but distinguishable operations of the Path.

**The counterfeit Path.** Discipline-and-observance becomes independently
significant when a limited practice is grasped as sufficient for an end it
cannot produce. Its error is systematic rather than merely lexical:

```text
finite observance
    → appropriated as the complete means
        → expected heaven or purification
            → displacement of the actual Path
```

The householder and renunciant examples differ in content but share this
formal defect. An act receives a false position within the means–end system.

**Organon interpretation.** The Dataset must distinguish conditioning,
execution, mixture, and appropriation rather than collapsing them into generic
association:

```text
Avidyā
    conditions Appropriation
    isMixedInto AppropriationComplex
    doesNotIndependentlyExecute Grasping

ŚīlavrataView
    appropriates PracticeAsSufficientPath
    displaces ActualPath
```

This is a relational determination of meaning: the error becomes intelligible
through the practice adopted, the end projected, the sufficiency claimed, and
the Path thereby obstructed.
