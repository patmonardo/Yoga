# VAK_5.62

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
वैलक्षण्याद्विपक्षत्वाद्देशविच्छेदकालतः ।
भूतशीलप्रदेशाध्वद्वयानामिव दूरताः ॥ ५.६२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
vailakṣaṇyād vipakṣatvād deśa-viccheda-kālataḥ |
bhūta-śīla-pradeśādhva-dvayānām iva dūratāḥ || 5.62 ||
```

The distancing counteragent of VAK 5.61 raises a prior question: what can
“distance” mean? VAK 5.62 distinguishes four grounds of remoteness and the
Bhāṣya supplies an example for each.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vailakṣaṇyāt | vipakṣatvāt | deśa-vicchedāt | kālataḥ |
bhūta-śīla-pradeśa-adhva-dvayānām | iva | dūratāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `vailakṣaṇyāt` | ablative singular neuter | through difference of characteristic |
| `vipakṣatvāt` | ablative singular neuter | through being opposed or contrary |
| `deśa-vicchedāt` | ablative singular masculine | through separation in place |
| `kālataḥ` | ablatival adverb | with respect to time |
| `bhūta` | compound member | the great elements, example of characteristic-distance |
| `śīla` | compound member | discipline, paired with misconduct as opposition-distance |
| `pradeśa` | compound member | regions or places, example of spatial distance |
| `adhva-dvayānām` | genitive plural/dual sense within compound | of the two temporal courses, past and future |
| `iva` | comparative particle | as, like, as in the cases of |
| `dūratāḥ` | nominative plural feminine | distances or modes of remoteness |

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed illustrative compound: great elements;
discipline and misconduct; eastern and western oceans; past and future.

## 4. Grammar

The four ablative expressions state four different grounds of `dūratā`:

```text
dūratā
    vailakṣaṇyāt
    vipakṣatvāt
    deśavicchedāt
    kālataḥ
```

They are coordinate alternatives, not stages of increasing distance. The
genitive compound governed by `iva` supplies parallel examples in the same
order:

```text
mahābhūtas                    → vailakṣaṇya
śīla and dauḥśīlya            → vipakṣatva
distant regions/oceans        → deśa-viccheda
past and future               → kāla
```

The plural `dūratāḥ` signals differentiated kinds of remoteness. The prose's
rapid question-and-answer sequence subsequently tests the precise ground of
the fourth kind.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Distances arise through difference of characteristic, through opposition,
> through separation in place, and through time, as in the cases of the
> elements, discipline, regions, and the two temporal courses.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Remoteness is fourfold: the great elements are remote from one another by
> difference of characteristic even when they arise together; discipline and
> misconduct are remote as contraries; the eastern and western oceans are
> remote through spatial separation; and the future and past are temporally
> remote because a dharma has not yet attained its own characteristic or has
> fallen away from it.

The final explanation is Vasubandhu's preferred resolution after several
proposed accounts of temporal remoteness have been tested and rejected.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> To call something distant does not yet determine the relation. Co-present
> dharmas can be remote by unlike marks; discipline and misconduct are remote
> through active contrariety; regions are remote through place; and past and
> future are remote through a dharma's differing relation to its own
> characteristic. Distance is therefore not reducible to spatial interval or
> mere non-identity. Its meaning emerges only when the ground of separation is
> specified.

For counteraction, this matters especially: a counteragent is distant from an
affliction through opposition, not merely because it is qualitatively other,
elsewhere, or later.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dūrībhāva` / `dūratā` — distancing or remoteness**

The general relation differentiated here by four grounds. The immediate
doctrinal context is the `dūrībhāva-pratipakṣa` of VAK 5.61.

**`Vailakṣaṇya-dūratā` — distance by characteristic difference**

Illustrated by the great elements. Even when co-arisen, they remain distinct
through their unlike defining characteristics. Co-presence therefore does
not erase qualitative remoteness.

**`Vipakṣa-dūratā` — distance by opposition**

Illustrated by discipline and misconduct. Opposition is stronger than mere
difference: one determination stands contrary to the other.

**`Deśa-viccheda-dūratā` — distance by spatial separation**

Illustrated by widely separated regions, specifically the eastern and western
oceans. This is the ordinary locative form of distance but not its universal
model.

**`Kāla-dūratā` — temporal remoteness**

Illustrated by past and future. The Bhāṣya makes this the dialectically
difficult case and refuses to rest with a merely quantitative interval.

**`Adhvanānātva` — difference of temporal course**

A tested explanation of temporal distance. It is rejected because it would
also make the present distant merely by belonging to a different course.

**`Akāritṛtva` — absence of present activity**

Another rejected ground. If inactivity alone made past and future remote, the
unconditioned could not straightforwardly be maintained as near.

**`Dharmasvalakṣaṇa` — a dharma's own characteristic**

Vasubandhu's preferred reference for temporal remoteness: the future has not
yet attained it (`asaṃprāptatva`), while the past has fallen away from it
(`pracyutatva`).

## 8. Logical Determination

The general predicate is qualified by its ground:

```text
Distance(x, y, ground)
```

The four forms are:

```text
CharacteristicDistance(x, y)
    ← UnlikeDefiningCharacteristics(x, y)

OppositionDistance(x, y)
    ← ContraryDeterminations(x, y)

SpatialDistance(x, y)
    ← SeparatedByPlace(x, y)

TemporalDistance(dharma, temporalStatus)
    ← RelationToOwnCharacteristic(dharma, temporalStatus)
```

These relations cannot be substituted:

```text
NonIdentity(x, y)
    ≠ Opposition(x, y)
    ≠ SpatialSeparation(x, y)
    ≠ TemporalRemoteness(x, y)
```

The preferred temporal rules are:

```text
Future(d)
AND NotYetAttained(OwnCharacteristic(d))
    → TemporallyRemote(d)

Past(d)
AND FallenAwayFrom(OwnCharacteristic(d))
    → TemporallyRemote(d)
```

The rejected proposals remain part of the reasoning record:

```text
TemporalCourseDifference alone  insufficient
AbsenceOfPresentActivity alone   insufficient
InterveningPresent alone         insufficient
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Contextual transition.** The fourfold analysis is not an unrelated ontology
of space and time. It clarifies the distance named by the distancing
counteragent, while distinguishing that Path-relation from other legitimate
senses of remoteness.

**Difference is not opposition.** Great elements can differ while co-arising;
discipline and misconduct stand as contraries. Only the second relation
captures the determinate opposition central to a counteragent.

**Dialectical testing.** The Bhāṣya does not simply report that past and
future are temporally far. It tests different temporal courses, absence of
activity, and mediation by the present, exposing a difficulty in each before
marking the preferred view with `evaṃ tu yuktaṃ syāt`.

**Temporal distance is characteristic-relative.** Future and past are remote
in contrary directions: not-yet-attainment and having-fallen-away. This is not
a claim about measured chronological duration.

**Organon interpretation.** A later model should reify the ground of distance
rather than overload one unqualified edge:

```text
DistanceAssertion
    hasRelatumA
    hasRelatumB
    hasDistanceGround
    hasTemporalOrientation
    hasArgumentStatus
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This is a project-level preservation of the source distinction, not formal
vocabulary attributed to Vasubandhu.
