# VAK_5.39

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अणवोऽनुगताश्चैते द्विधा चाप्यनुशेरते ।
अनुबध्नन्ति यस्माच्च तस्मादनुशयाः स्मृताः ॥ ५.३९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
aṇavo 'nugatāś caite dvidhā cāpy anuśerate |
anubadhnanti yasmāc ca tasmād anuśayāḥ smṛtāḥ || 5.39 ||
```

After showing that the same afflictive field is designated as influx, flood,
yoke, and appropriation, VAK 5.39 explains the name `anuśaya` through four
determinations: subtlety, accompaniment, twofold lying-along, and continued
binding.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
aṇavaḥ | anugatāḥ | ca | ete | dvidhā | ca | api | anuśerate |
anubadhnanti | yasmāt | ca | tasmāt | anuśayāḥ | smṛtāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `aṇavaḥ` | nominative plural masculine | subtle or minute in operation |
| `anugatāḥ` | nominative plural masculine past passive participle of `anu-√gam` | accompanying or following along |
| `ca ete` | conjunction with nominative plural pronoun | and these [afflictive tendencies] |
| `dvidhā` | indeclinable adverb | in two ways |
| `ca api` | connective and additive particles | and also |
| `anuśerate` | third-person plural present middle of `anu-√śī` | they lie along, lodge upon, or adhere latently |
| `anubadhnanti` | third-person plural present active of `anu-√bandh` | they continue to bind |
| `yasmāt ... tasmāt` | ablatival relative-correlative pair | because ... therefore |
| `anuśayāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | anuśayas, underlying afflictive tendencies |
| `smṛtāḥ` | nominative plural masculine past passive participle | are called, recognized, or traditionally designated |

`Aṇu` here concerns subtle operation and consequent difficulty of discernment;
it does not by itself assert that an anuśaya is a material atom. `Anuśerate`
supports the traditional designation by wordplay upon `anuśaya`, while the
Bhāṣya supplies its exact two relational modes.

## 4. Grammar

**Three initial predicates**

```text
ete
    aṇavaḥ
    anugatāḥ ca
    dvidhā ca api anuśerate
```

`Ete` refers to the anuśayas discussed throughout the chapter. The adjective
`aṇavaḥ`, participle `anugatāḥ`, and finite middle verb `anuśerate` coordinate
three determinations. `Dvidhā` qualifies `anuśerate`, not `anugatāḥ`: according
to the Bhāṣya, anuśayas lie along in relation to an object-support and through
conjunction.

**Reason and designation**

```text
yasmāt ca anubadhnanti
    tasmāt anuśayāḥ smṛtāḥ
```

The relative-correlative construction gives continued binding as a further
reason for the name. The passive `smṛtāḥ` marks the recognized designation:
for these stated reasons they are called anuśayas.

**Bhāṣya expansion**

```text
aṇavaḥ
    → sūkṣma-pracāratvāt durvijñānatayā

anugatāḥ
    → prāpti-anuṣaṅgataḥ

dvidhā anuśerate
    → ālambanataḥ
    → saṃprayogataḥ

anubadhnanti
    → aprayogeṇa
    → prativārayataḥ api
    → punaḥ punaḥ saṃmukhībhāvāt
```

The prose changes the verse's compressed naming account into four explicit
functional reasons.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> These are subtle and accompanying; they also lie along in two ways. And
> because they continue to bind, they are therefore called anuśayas.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The anuśayas operate subtly and are therefore difficult to discern; they
> accompany the continuum through association with acquisition (`prāpti`);
> and they lie along in two determinate relations, through object-support and
> through conjunction. They continue to bind because, even without deliberate
> application and even when one tries to restrain them, they repeatedly become
> manifest. For these reasons they are called anuśayas.

The association with `prāpti`, the two modes of lying-along, and the criterion
of recurrent manifestation are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An anuśaya is known neither by constant manifestation nor by an isolated
> hidden presence. Its mode is subtle; it accompanies a continuum under the
> source's technical relation of acquisition; it inheres relationally through
> an object taken up and through conjunction with cognition; and its unabandoned
> power becomes evident when affliction returns without being deliberately
> produced and even against attempted restraint. Latency is therefore a
> determinate structure of possible recurrence. What lies along continues to
> bind by supplying the ground upon which affliction can become present again.

This philosophical rendering is an analytical reconstruction of the Bhāṣya's
relations. It does not replace the Vaibhāṣika category of `prāpti` with a
modern theory of disposition.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Aṇu` — subtle or minute**

Explained through `sūkṣma-pracāratva`, subtlety of operation, and
`durvijñānatā`, difficulty of being discerned. The term determines how the
anuśaya operates and is known.

**`Anugata` — accompanying**

Explained by `prāptyanuṣaṅga`, association with acquisition or possession.
The Bhāṣya thus gives a technical Abhidharma ground for continuity rather than
merely saying that the anuśaya persists through time.

**`Prāpti` — acquisition or possession**

A Vaibhāṣika technical relation by which a dharma is acquired by or belongs to
a continuum. Its doctrinal status must remain distinct from the Organon's
relational modeling of persistence.

**`Anuśayana` — lying along**

The operation expressed by `anuśerate`. It occurs in two ways: through
object-support (`ālambanataḥ`) and through conjunction (`saṃprayogataḥ`).

**`Ālambana` — object-support**

The dharma in relation to which an anuśaya operates as its object. This is one
mode of lying-along and must not be collapsed into mental conjunction.

**`Saṃprayoga` — conjunction**

Association with the citta and caittas occurring together in a cognitive
event. It is the second mode of lying-along.

**`Anubandhana` — continued binding**

The power of an unabandoned anuśaya to condition renewed manifestation and
thus prolong bondage.

**`Aprayoga` — absence of deliberate application**

The recurrence does not require a present intentional effort to manufacture
the affliction.

**`Saṃmukhībhāva` — becoming present or manifest**

Literally, coming face-to-face. Repeated manifestation despite restraint is
the Bhāṣya's practical evidence of continued binding.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya define an anuśaya through a relational conjunction:

```text
Anuśaya(x)
    → SubtleOperation(x)
    AND AssociatedWithPrāpti(x)
    AND [LiesAlongByObjectSupport(x)
         OR LiesAlongByConjunction(x)]
    AND ContinuesToBind(x)
```

The two modes of lying-along remain typed and irreducible:

```text
HasAsObjectSupport(x, y)
    ≠ ConjoinedWith(x, y)
```

The recurrence criterion supplies an inference from events to an unabandoned
ground:

```text
NoDeliberateApplication(x)
AND AttemptedRestraint(x)
AND RepeatedManifestation(x)
    → EvidenceOf(UnabandonedAnuśaya(x))
```

This does not equate the latent determination with its manifest event:

```text
PresentAsAnuśaya(x)
    ≠ ManifestAsKleśaEvent(x)

NotManifestNow(x)
    ⇏ WithoutConditioningPower(x)
```

The manifest recurrence is the consequence through which the subtle ground
becomes cognitively available.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya explicitly says that the anuśayas
themselves were designated in the sūtras as influxes, floods, yokes, and
appropriations. VAK 5.39 now asks for the meaning of `anuśaya`, beginning a
sequence of functional explanations rather than introducing another inventory.

**Four determinations, one name.** The name is not exhausted by a one-word
gloss such as “latency”:

```text
subtle operation
    → difficult discernment

association with prāpti
    → accompaniment of the continuum

object-support and conjunction
    → two typed modes of lying-along

recurrent manifestation
    → continued bondage
```

The term becomes meaningful through the complete system of mode, relation,
transition, and consequence.

**Recurrence under resistance.** The strongest practical determination is
that an anuśaya may become manifest without present deliberate application and
even when opposed. Attempted restraint can inhibit an event without yet
removing its generative condition. The Path must therefore distinguish:

```text
temporary nonmanifestation
    ≠ abandonment of the anuśaya

event-restraint
    ≠ destruction of the ground of recurrence
```

**Organon interpretation.** A latent determination should be represented not
as an isolated Boolean attribute but as a typed relational and temporal
structure:

```text
Anuśaya
    isPossessedBy Continuum
    hasObjectSupport Dharma
    mayBeConjoinedIn CognitiveEvent
    mayManifestAs KleśaEvent
    continuesToBind Continuum
```

This is project architecture, not a restatement of source ontology. Its aim is
to preserve the distinctions made by the source: acquisition, object-support,
conjunction, manifestation, restraint, recurrence, and abandonment.
