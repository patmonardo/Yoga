# VAK_5.49

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कौकृत्यं विचिकित्सातः क्रोधेर्ष्ये प्रतिघान्वये ।
अन्ये च षट्क्लेशमलाः माया शाठ्यं मदस्तथा ॥ ५.४९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kaukṛtyaṃ vicikitsātaḥ krodherṣye pratighānvaye |
anye ca ṣaṭ kleśa-malāḥ māyā śāṭhyaṃ madas tathā || 5.49 ||
```

VAK 5.49 completes the genetic account of the ten Vaibhāṣika
`paryavasthānas` and then opens a new sixfold class, the stains of affliction.
The Bhāṣya distinguishes their origin, class-membership, and defining
operations.

**Textual caution.** The working Bhāṣya e-text reads `kodherṣye` where the
kārikā witness has `krodherṣye`. Its explanatory prose `pratighasamutthe`
makes the intended relation unambiguous, but the source variant is not silently
normalized.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kaukṛtyam | vicikitsātaḥ | krodha-īrṣye | pratigha-anvaye |
anye | ca | ṣaṭ | kleśa-malāḥ | māyā | śāṭhyam | madaḥ | tathā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `kaukṛtyam` | nominative singular neuter | remorse or regret |
| `vicikitsātaḥ` | ablative singular feminine | from doubt |
| `krodha-īrṣye` | nominative dual | anger and envy |
| `pratigha-anvaye` | nominative dual predicate compound | belonging to or arising in dependence upon hostility |
| `anye ca` | nominative plural with conjunction | and other [factors] |
| `ṣaṭ kleśa-malāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | six stains of affliction |
| `māyā` | nominative singular feminine | deceit |
| `śāṭhyam` | nominative singular neuter | crookedness or duplicity |
| `madaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | intoxication or infatuation |
| `tathā` | indeclinable adverb | likewise, with the remaining members following |

The Bhāṣya glosses `pratighānvaye` as `pratighasamutthe`, “arisen from
hostility,” giving the compound a genetic rather than merely taxonomic force.

## 4. Grammar

**Completion of the paryavasthāna genealogy**

```text
kaukṛtyam
    vicikitsātaḥ [utthitam]

krodha-īrṣye
    pratigha-anvaye
```

The ablative supplies doubt as the source of remorse. The dual subject anger
and envy takes the dual predicate `pratighānvaye`; the Bhāṣya's plural-dual
sense is explicit in `pratighasamutthe`.

**Opening of the six stains**

```text
anye ca
    ṣaṭ kleśa-malāḥ

māyā
śāṭhyam
madaḥ
tathā [pradāśaḥ upanāhaḥ vihiṃsā ca]
```

`Anye` marks a new group of upakleśas rather than further members of the ten
paryavasthānas. The verse names the first three; the Bhāṣya immediately supplies
the complete six before the next kārikā assigns their source-affiliations.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Remorse arises from doubt; anger and envy arise in dependence upon hostility.
> There are also six other stains of affliction: deceit, crookedness,
> intoxication, and likewise [three further stains].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Remorse is an outflow of doubt, while anger and envy arise from hostility.
> With these affiliations the ten envelopments are established as upakleśas
> that are derivatives of kleśas. A new group then begins: six stains of
> affliction—deceit, crookedness, intoxication, obstinate resistance to proper
> correction, sustained resentment, and harmfulness. Deceit deceives another;
> crookedness bends the mind away from disclosing matters as they are;
> obstinacy firmly grasps a blameworthy matter and rejects reasoned admonition;
> resentment repeatedly enlarges a basis of injury; and harmfulness injures
> others through striking, harshness, and similar conduct.

The closure of the tenfold genealogy, full six-member list, and operational
definitions are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The analysis completes one question and opens another. Remorse, anger, and
> envy receive their genetic determinations, allowing all ten envelopments to
> be gathered as derivatives of affliction. The text then asks not where the
> next six factors arise—that comes afterward—but what special class they
> occupy and what each does. Deceit alters another's apprehension, crookedness
> prevents straightforward disclosure, obstinacy refuses correction,
> resentment perpetuates injury, and harmfulness actively injures. Origin,
> classification, and operation are three distinct judgments; a factor is not
> adequately understood when only one of them has been supplied.

The general significance of `mala`, stain, remains provisional here because
the Bhāṣya provides a list and definitions rather than an explicit universal
theory of staining.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kaukṛtya` — remorse or regret**

Here explicitly assigned to doubt. The passage establishes a source-relation
without supplying a fuller psychological sequence from indecision to remorse.

**`Vicikitsā` — doubt**

The afflictive source of remorse in this local genetic analysis.

**`Pratighānvaya` — dependence upon hostility**

The kārikā's relation for anger and envy. The Bhāṣya strengthens it through
`pratighasamuttha`, arisen from hostility.

**`Kleśa-niḥṣyanda upakleśa` — upakleśa derivative of affliction**

The Bhāṣya's collective determination of all ten Vaibhāṣika
paryavasthānas after their individual affiliations have been completed.

**`Kleśa-mala` — stain of affliction**

A sixfold class of upakleśas introduced here. The source identifies and
defines its members but does not yet warrant reducing `mala` to “behavioral
residue,” “externalized affliction,” or another completed general theory.

**`Māyā` — deceit**

Defined as deceiving another (`paravañcanā`). Its source-affiliation is given
in VAK 5.50.

**`Śāṭhya` — crookedness or duplicity**

Defined securely as crookedness of mind (`citta-kauṭilya`) through which one
does not reveal matters as they actually are. The remainder of the transmitted
definition is textually compact and is not over-reconstructed.

**`Mada` — intoxication or infatuation**

The Bhāṣya refers back to an earlier definition rather than restating it here.

**`Pradāśa` — obstinate resistance to correction**

Defined as firm grasping of a blameworthy matter
(`sāvadyavastu-dṛḍhagrāhitā`) through which one does not accept proper or
reasoned admonition.

**`Upanāha` — sustained resentment**

Defined as repeated enlargement or intensification of a basis of injury or
resentment (`āghātavastu-vahulīkāra`).

**`Vihiṃsā` — harmfulness**

Defined as injuring or harassing another through striking, harshness, and the
like.

**The six stains**

```text
māyā       deceit
śāṭhya     crookedness
mada       intoxication
pradāśa    obstinate resistance to correction
upanāha    sustained resentment
vihiṃsā    harmfulness
```

## 8. Logical Determination

The remaining paryavasthāna affiliations are:

```text
NiḥṣyandaOf(Kaukṛtya, Vicikitsā)
ArisesFrom(Krodha, Pratigha)
ArisesFrom(Īrṣyā, Pratigha)
```

Together with VAK 5.48, the Bhāṣya draws a closure:

```text
For x in TenVaibhāṣikaParyavasthānas:
    Upakleśa(x)
    AND KleśaNiḥṣyanda(x)
```

The next move is classificatory, not another derivation:

```text
ClassifyAs(
    {Māyā, Śāṭhya, Mada, Pradāśa, Upanāha, Vihiṃsā},
    KleśaMala
)
```

The Bhāṣya then adds operational predicates:

```text
Māyā(x)     → DeceivesAnother(x)
Śāṭhya(x)   → PreventsTruthfulDisclosure(x)
Pradāśa(x)  → RejectsProperAdmonition(x)
Upanāha(x)  → RepeatedlyIntensifiesResentment(x)
Vihiṃsā(x)  → InjuresAnother(x)
```

Therefore:

```text
HasOrigin(x, y)
    ≠ ClassifiedAs(x, c)
    ≠ HasOperation(x, o)
```

All three relations may determine one factor without becoming interchangeable.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** VAK 5.49 finishes the genealogy begun in 5.48. The
Bhāṣya then explicitly gathers all ten paryavasthānas as affliction-derived
upakleśas. Only after this closure does `anye ca` open the six stains.

**From `anvaya` to arising.** A weak translation such as “anger and envy belong
to the lineage of hostility” understates the Bhāṣya. Its gloss
`pratighasamutthe` licenses the stronger determination “arise from hostility.”

**Class before genealogy.** The six stains are first enumerated and defined.
Their root-affiliations follow in VAK 5.50–5.51. The study therefore does not
silently infer an origin from a concrete function—for example, it does not
derive deceit from attachment merely because deceit may protect an advantage.

**Path contact.** These definitions disclose intervention-relevant differences
without yet prescribing their remedies. Deceiving another, refusing correction,
perpetuating resentment, and inflicting injury are distinct operations even
when they share the wider class of afflictive stain.

**Organon interpretation.** A factor requires at least three separable relation
types:

```text
Factor
    hasOrigin Ground
    memberOf Classification
    performs Operation
```

The Dataset should preserve the source that establishes each edge. This
prevents a functional definition from masquerading as a causal explanation or
a class-name from replacing both.
