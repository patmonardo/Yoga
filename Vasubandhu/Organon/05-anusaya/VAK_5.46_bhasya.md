# VAK 5.46 Bhāṣya — Upakleśa Beyond the Kleśas

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ye 'py anye caitasāḥ kliṣṭāḥ saṃskāraskandha-saṃjñitāḥ |
> kleśebhyas te 'py upakleśās te tu na kleśa-saṃjñitāḥ || 5.46 ||

> Other afflicted mental factors, designated as included in the Base of
> formations and distinct from the kleśas, are also upakleśas, though they
> are not designated kleśas.

## 3. Continuous Translation

“Other afflicted mental factors, designated as belonging to
the Base of formations, are also upakleśas in addition to
the kleśas; they are not, however, designated kleśas.”
Those other afflicted mental dharmas, mental factors included
in the Base of formations and distinct from the kleśas, are
upakleśas. They are the ones listed in the *Kṣudravastuka*.
Here, however, we will describe only those included among
the enveloping afflictions and afflictive stains.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The grammar is decisive: `anye kleśebhyaḥ` means “other
than the kleśas.” The ablative is governed by `anye` and
does not here state that these factors are produced *from*
the kleśas. A causal derivation of particular factors may
be given later, but it cannot be installed as the definition
of *upakleśa* at 5.46.

The resulting class relation is asymmetric. Every kleśa
counts as an upakleśa under the Consciousness-afflicting function
stated at the end of 5.45. Some further upakleśas are
afflicted `caitasika` factors in the Base of formations
that do not receive the kleśa designation. The reference
to the *Kṣudravastuka* points to a wider inventory; the
present chapter explicitly narrows its coming treatment to
enveloping afflictions and afflictive stains.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Caitasāḥ` and the prose's
`caitasikāḥ` are rendered “mental factors,” while Consciousness (*citta*) is
kept distinct as what these factors afflict. `Kleśebhyaḥ`
is separative in this construction. “Secondary affliction”
can serve as an English label for *upakleśa* only with the
present inclusion made explicit: a kleśa is also an
upakleśa here. `Paryasthāna` is retained in the Sanskrit
instead of silently normalized.

**Organon reconstruction.** The Science of Citta needs to
preserve both the broad predicate “afflicts Consciousness” and the
narrower kleśa designation, together with their different
grounds. This is a project-level reading of an overlapping
classification, separate from the Kośa's source doctrine
and any implemented model.
