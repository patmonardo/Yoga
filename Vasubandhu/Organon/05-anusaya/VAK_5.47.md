# VAK_5.47

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आह्रीक्यमनपत्राप्यमीर्ष्यामात्सर्यमुद्धवः ।
कौकृत्यं स्त्यानमिद्धं च पर्यवस्थानमष्टधा ॥ ५.४७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
āhrīkyam anapatrāpyam īrṣyā mātsaryam uddhavaḥ |
kaukṛtyaṃ styānamiddhaṃ ca paryavasthānam aṣṭadhā || 5.47 ||
```

VAK 5.47 gives the Prakaraṇaśāstra's eightfold enumeration of
`paryavasthāna`. The Bhāṣya also cites broader sūtra usage of the term.

**Textual caution.** The kārikā witness reads `uddhavaḥ`; following prose
identifies the factor as `auddhatya`. The running verse also transmits
`āhlīkyam` where the official kārikā has `āhrīkyam`.

## 4. Grammar

**Eight-member enumeration**

```text
āhrīkyam
anapatrāpyam
īrṣyā
mātsaryam
uddhavaḥ [identified in following prose as auddhatyam]
kaukṛtyam
styānam
middham ca
    paryavasthānam aṣṭadhā
```

The eight nouns are appositional members of the collective predicate. The
verse enumerates; it does not grammatically establish four pairs, a causal
order, or a distinct ontological stage of manifestation.

**Context of the enumeration**

```text
sūtra usage: kleśas may also be called paryavasthāna
Prakaraṇaśāstra: eight named factors
```

The meaning of the class-label must therefore be indexed to its textual and
doctrinal context.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Shamelessness, lack of moral concern, envy, avarice, excitation, remorse,
> torpor, and sleepiness: envelopment is eightfold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the Prakaraṇaśāstra, envelopment is eightfold: shamelessness, lack of
> moral concern, envy, avarice, excitation, remorse, torpor, and sleepiness.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> “Envelopment” does not have one context-free extension. A sūtra may use the
> word even for a kleśa; the Prakaraṇaśāstra selects eight named factors here.
> Their enumeration fixes a determinate scope for this classification without
> implying four formal pairs or a causal order among them.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Paryavasthāna` — envelopment or dominating affliction**

A contextual functional designation. The Bhāṣya does not define it here as a
separate ontological stage reached when an upakleśa becomes manifest; it even
permits kleśas themselves to receive the designation in sūtra usage.

**`Āhrīkya` — shamelessness**

One of the eight named by the Prakaraṇaśāstra. Its definition belongs outside
the present Bhāṣya unit.

**`Anapatrāpya` — lack of moral concern**

Another member of the eight, distinct from shamelessness in the enumeration.

**`Īrṣyā` — envy**

Named here as an envelopment; its definition follows in the next unit.

**`Mātsarya` — avarice or stinginess**

Named here as an envelopment alongside envy.

**`Auddhatya` — excitation or restlessness**

The standard technical identification of the transmitted `uddhavaḥ`, confirmed
in the following prose. The kārikā reading remains visible.

**`Kaukṛtya` — remorse or regret**

One of the eight named factors. Its further qualification is taken up after
this verse.

**`Styāna` — torpor**

Listed separately from sleepiness.

**`Middha` — sleepiness**

The eighth member of the Prakaraṇaśāstra list. Its definition and afflicted
status qualification belong to the following prose.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya moves from the chapter's restricted
upakleśa scope to the question “What are the paryavasthānas?” Before giving the
eightfold list, it notes that kleśas themselves can bear this designation in a
sūtra. It then attributes the eight to the Prakaraṇaśāstra.

**Two attested scopes.** The local passage distinguishes:

```text
sūtra usage
    → may include kleśas

Prakaraṇaśāstra
    → eight named factors
```

The next unit takes up the Vaibhāṣika count that matters for 5.42's objection.

**No four-pair doctrine.** The order may invite useful comparisons, but the
Bhāṣya does not arrange the eight into four formal pairs. Nor does it define
`paryavasthāna` as the final manifest stage of a root-to-derivative causal
sequence. Such structures must not be presented as source doctrine.
