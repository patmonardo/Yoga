# VAK_5.47

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आह्रीक्यमनपत्राप्यमीर्ष्यामात्सर्यमुद्धवः ।
कौकृत्यं स्त्यानमिद्धं च पर्यवस्थानमष्टधा ॥ ५.४७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
āhrīkyam anapatrāpyam īrṣyā mātsaryam uddhavaḥ |
kaukṛtyaṃ styānamiddhaṃ ca paryavasthānam aṣṭadhā || 5.47 ||
```

VAK 5.47 gives the Prakaraṇaśāstra's eightfold enumeration of
`paryavasthāna`. The Bhāṣya distinguishes this list from both broader sūtra
usage and the tenfold Vaibhāṣika enumeration.

**Textual caution.** The kārikā witness reads `uddhavaḥ`, while the Bhāṣya
prose supplies the expected technical term `auddhatya`. Its source excerpt also
transmits such forms as `āhlīkya` and `bhrakṣa`. These witness-level variants
are recorded rather than silently installed in the kārikā.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
āhrīkyam | anapatrāpyam | īrṣyā | mātsaryam | uddhavaḥ |
kaukṛtyam | styānam | middham | ca | paryavasthānam | aṣṭadhā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `āhrīkyam` | nominative singular neuter | shamelessness, absence of moral self-restraint |
| `anapatrāpyam` | nominative singular neuter | lack of moral concern or embarrassment |
| `īrṣyā` | nominative singular feminine | envy |
| `mātsaryam` | nominative singular neuter | avarice or stinginess |
| `uddhavaḥ` | transmitted nominative singular masculine | excitation; explained as `auddhatya` in the prose |
| `kaukṛtyam` | nominative singular neuter | remorse or regret |
| `styānam` | nominative singular neuter | torpor or mental incapacity |
| `middham` | nominative singular neuter | sleepiness or languor |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `paryavasthānam` | nominative singular neuter collective | envelopment or dominating affliction |
| `aṣṭadhā` | indeclinable adverb | eightfold |

The list contains nouns of different grammatical genders, coordinated under
the collective classification `paryavasthānam aṣṭadhā`.

## 4. Grammar

**Eight-member enumeration**

```text
āhrīkyam
anapatrāpyam
īrṣyā
mātsaryam
uddhavaḥ [prose: auddhatyam]
kaukṛtyam
styānam
middham ca
    paryavasthānam aṣṭadhā
```

The eight nouns are appositional members of the collective predicate. The
verse enumerates; it does not grammatically establish four pairs, a causal
order, or a distinct ontological stage of manifestation.

**Three contextual extensions in the Bhāṣya**

```text
sūtra usage:
    kleśas api paryavasthānam

Prakaraṇaśāstra:
    aṣṭadhā

Vaibhāṣika method:
    these eight + krodha + mrakṣa = ten
```

The meaning of the class-label must therefore be indexed to its textual and
doctrinal context.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Shamelessness, lack of moral concern, envy, avarice, excitation, remorse,
> torpor, and sleepiness: envelopment is eightfold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Prakaraṇaśāstra recognizes eight envelopments: shamelessness, lack of
> moral concern, envy, avarice, excitation, remorse, torpor, and sleepiness.
> The term has broader sūtra usage in which kleśas themselves may also be
> called envelopments. According to the Vaibhāṣika system the enumeration is
> ten, adding anger and concealment. Envy is vexation at another's success;
> avarice is a gripping of mind opposed to giving Dharma, material goods, or
> skill; excitation is non-pacification of mind; and sleepiness is contraction
> of mind that renders the body unable to sustain itself. Only afflicted
> sleepiness and afflicted remorse count here as envelopments.

The alternative extensions, factor definitions, and afflicted-form
restrictions are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> “Envelopment” does not possess one context-free extension. In sūtra usage it
> can include the kleśas themselves; one Abhidharma treatise selects eight
> factors; the Vaibhāṣika system adds two more. These are not contradictions
> once the authoritative scheme and operative scope are retained. Within the
> enumerated field, each factor receives an intensional determination through
> what it does: envy turns another's success into vexation, avarice blocks
> giving through mental contraction, excitation prevents pacification, and
> afflicted sleepiness contracts mind into bodily incapacity. The list becomes
> meaningful through these operations rather than through bare numerical
> membership.

The verse itself enumerates eight; the additional scope and definitions belong
to the Bhāṣya and are kept distinct from the kārikā's wording.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Paryavasthāna` — envelopment or dominating affliction**

A contextual functional designation. The Bhāṣya does not define it here as a
separate ontological stage reached when an upakleśa becomes manifest; it even
permits kleśas themselves to receive the designation in sūtra usage.

**`Āhrīkya` — shamelessness**

One of the eight. The Bhāṣya says it has already been explained and does not
repeat its full definition in this passage.

**`Anapatrāpya` — lack of moral concern**

Likewise already explained. It remains distinct from shamelessness despite
their coordination in the list.

**`Īrṣyā` — envy**

Defined as `parasaṃpattau cetaso vyāroṣa`, vexation or irritation of mind at
another's success, prosperity, or excellence.

**`Mātsarya` — avarice or stinginess**

Defined as a gripping of mind (`cittāgraha`) opposed to giving Dharma,
material goods, or skill. The three prohibited gifts make its operation more
specific than generic possessiveness.

**`Auddhatya` — excitation or restlessness**

Defined as non-pacification of mind (`cetaso 'vyupaśama`). This prose term
provides the technical identification of the kārikā's transmitted `uddhavaḥ`.

**`Kaukṛtya` — remorse or regret**

Previously explained. The Bhāṣya's `kaukṛtyaṃ ca` extends the afflicted-only
restriction: remorse qualifies as an envelopment only when afflicted.

**`Styāna` — torpor**

Previously explained in the text. It remains a separate member from
sleepiness, although both may involve incapacity.

**`Middha` — sleepiness**

Defined as contraction of mind (`cittābhisaṃkṣepa`) rendering the body unable
to sustain itself. Only its afflicted form is an envelopment.

**`Krodha` — anger**

Added in the Vaibhāṣika tenfold list. Defined as hostility toward sentient or
non-sentient objects excluding the more specific factors ill will and
harmfulness.

**`Mrakṣa` — concealment**

The second Vaibhāṣika addition, defined as concealment of a fault
(`avadya-pracchādana`).

**`Vaibhāṣika-nyāya` — Vaibhāṣika system or method**

The explicit attribution governing the tenfold enumeration. It must not be
silently merged with the Prakaraṇaśāstra's eightfold list.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya moves from the chapter's restricted
upakleśa scope to the question “What are the paryavasthānas?” Before giving the
eightfold list, it notes that kleśas themselves can bear this designation in a
sūtra. It then attributes eight to the Prakaraṇaśāstra and ten to Vaibhāṣika
method.

**Eight and ten.** The two enumerations must remain visible as distinct Views:

```text
Prakaraṇaśāstra
    → eight

Vaibhāṣika-nyāya
    → eight + anger + concealment
```

This directly clarifies the domain-sensitive objection of VAK 5.42. When the
comparison field expands to ten, anger and concealment become counterexamples
to the claimed uniqueness of envy and avarice.

**No four-pair doctrine.** The order may invite useful comparisons, but the
Bhāṣya does not arrange the eight into four formal pairs. Nor does it define
`paryavasthāna` as the final manifest stage of a root-to-derivative causal
sequence. Such structures must not be presented as source doctrine.

**Meaning through operation.** The prose definitions determine several
members relationally:

```text
envy
    another's success → mental vexation

avarice
    possible giving → obstructive mental grip

excitation
    mind → non-pacification

sleepiness
    mental contraction → bodily incapacity
```
