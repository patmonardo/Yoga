# VAK_5.03

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दृष्टयः पञ्च सत्कायमिथ्यान्तग्रहदृष्टयः ।
दृष्टिशीलव्रतपरामर्शाविति पुनर्दश ॥ ५.३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dṛṣṭayaḥ pañca satkāya-mithyā-antagraha-dṛṣṭayaḥ |
dṛṣṭi-śīlavrata-parāmarśāv iti punar daśa || 5.3 ||
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| dṛṣṭayaḥ | noun; feminine; nominative plural (lemma: dṛṣṭi) | views, the class now divided |
| pañca | numeral | five species of view |
| satkāya-mithyā-antagraha-dṛṣṭayaḥ | compressed coordination; feminine; nominative plural | satkāya-view, false view, and extreme-grasping view |
| dṛṣṭi-śīlavrata-parāmarśau | compressed coordination; masculine; nominative dual | adherence to views and adherence to discipline and observance |
| iti | indeclinable | closes the fivefold enumeration |
| punar | indeclinable (form of punaḥ before voiced d) | again, in turn |
| daśa | numeral | ten anuśayas after the view division |

The first compressed expression supplies a shared *dṛṣṭi* ending to three
view names: *satkāyadṛṣṭi*, *mithyādṛṣṭi*, and *antagrahadṛṣṭi*. The dual
*parāmarśau* supplies a shared ending to *dṛṣṭiparāmarśa* and
*śīlavrataparāmarśa*. The local Bhāṣya witness writes *śīlavṛta* and
*antagrāha* in its continuous Sanskrit; the kārikā study retains its
transmitted *śīlavrata* and *antagraha* forms.

## 4. Grammar

**Fivefold predication**

```text
dṛṣṭayaḥ pañca [santi]
    satkāya-dṛṣṭiḥ
    mithyā-dṛṣṭiḥ
    antagraha-dṛṣṭiḥ
    dṛṣṭi-parāmarśaḥ
    śīlavrata-parāmarśaḥ

the views are five: ...
```

The first three members share `dṛṣṭi` as their final compound member. The
last two are compressed into the dual `parāmarśau`, with `dṛṣṭi` and
`śīlavrata` supplying its two prior members:

```text
dṛṣṭi-śīlavrata-parāmarśau
    = dṛṣṭi-parāmarśaḥ
      and śīlavrata-parāmarśaḥ
```

**Recalculation**

```text
iti punaḥ daśa [anuśayāḥ bhavanti]

thus, in turn, [the anuśayas become] ten
```

The Bhāṣya makes the suppressed operation explicit: by making the `dṛṣṭi`
among the six anuśayas fivefold, they become ten.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The views are five: satkāya-view, false view, extreme-grasping view,
> adherence to views, and adherence to discipline and observance. Thus, in
> turn, [the anuśayas are] ten.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The views are five: satkāya-view, false view, extreme-grasping view,
> adherence to views, and adherence to discipline and observance. With the
> single view among the six anuśayas counted as five, they are in turn ten.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Afflicted view is not one indeterminate failure of cognition. It has five
> distinct forms, each of which binds through a determinate way of construing,
> grasping, or authorizing its field. By unfolding the genus `dṛṣṭi`, the
> initial six anuśayas become ten basic types. These types then form the input
> to a larger system: each must be located in its operative realm and
> distinguished according to whether it is abandoned through seeing or
> through cultivation. Affliction becomes scientifically tractable when its
> kind, range, and condition of removal are jointly determined.

The verse itself supplies the fivefold division and the number ten. The
Bhāṣya supplies the next horizon—the ninety-eightfold Abhidharma matrix—without
yet carrying out its detailed derivation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dṛṣṭi` — afflicted view**

Here `dṛṣṭi` is neither visual perception nor opinion in general. The Bhāṣya
uses `dṛṣṭisvabhāva`, “having the nature of view,” to classify five of the ten
basic anuśayas. The other five are anuśayas without being view by nature.

**The five views**

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Provisional determination |
|---|---|---|
| `satkāyadṛṣṭi` | satkāya-view | view concerning the existing collection of Bases |
| `mithyādṛṣṭi` | false view | a path-relevant false determination |
| `antagrahadṛṣṭi` | extreme-grasping view | fixation upon an extreme determination |
| `dṛṣṭiparāmarśa` | adherence to views | grasping or overvaluing a view as authoritative |
| `śīlavrataparāmarśa` | adherence to discipline and observance | grasping practices or observances under a false valuation |

These renderings remain controlled and provisional. The present Bhāṣya names
the five but does not yet supply their full definitions; later units must
determine, rather than merely inherit, their exact doctrinal content.

**`Parāmarśa` — adherence, grasping, or overvaluation**

More than the occurrence of a view or practice. It marks a reflexive taking
hold of the determination and assigning it a binding authority or value.

**`Dṛṣṭisvabhāva` — having the nature of view**

The Bhāṣya's classificatory predicate for the five named species. It yields a
clean partition of the ten:

```text
view by nature:
    five dṛṣṭis

not view by nature:
    rāga, pratigha, māna, avidyā, vicikitsā
```

**`Darśanaprahātavya` and `bhāvanāprahātavya`**

To be abandoned through seeing and to be abandoned through cultivation. The
Bhāṣya introduces these as the Path-axis along which anuśayas in the three
realms are further differentiated.

**Realm predicates**

`Kāmāvacara`, `rūpāvacara`, and `ārūpyāvacara` locate an anuśaya in the desire,
Form, or Formless realm. A realm is an operative coordinate, not a detached
label added after the type has already been fully determined.

## 8. Logical Determination

The tenfold base is produced by substitution within the original sixfold
classification:

```text
six initial anuśayas
    - one undivided dṛṣṭi
    + five determinate dṛṣṭis
    = ten basic anuśayas
```

This gives two primary classes:

```text
TenAnusayas
    ├── FiveViewNatureAnusayas
    └── FiveNonViewNatureAnusayas
```

The Bhāṣya then announces a higher determination:

```text
basic anuśaya type
    × operative realm
    × mode of abandonment
    → determinate operative case
```

This should not be read as a simple unrestricted multiplication. The unequal
realm totals—36, 31, and 31—show that not every basic type occupies every
coordinate in the same way. VAK 5.04 onward must derive the admissible cases.

The sequence from VAK 5.01 is now:

```text
5.01    functional ground
        anuśaya roots bhava

5.02    affective differentiation
        rāga divides by its mode and realm

5.03    cognitive differentiation
        dṛṣṭi divides by the form of afflicted cognition

next    operative matrix
        type is distributed by realm and mode of abandonment
```

Meaning here emerges through successive relations. The number ninety-eight
does not name ninety-eight isolated entities; it records the determinate
positions produced by a classificatory system.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.03 exposes the method beneath the Abhidharma enumeration. The science
does not merely collect names of errors. It begins with a governing class,
articulates internal species, and then asks of each species:

```text
What kind of affliction is it?
In which realm can it operate?
By what mode of Path is it removed?
```

This is the Interpretation of the passage: determination becomes adequate only
through position in a system of difference, range, and transformation. Error
is not represented by the undifferentiated predicate `false`; it has a form,
an operative field, and a corresponding condition of abandonment.

A restrained computational analogy follows from the text:

```text
false content
    is not identical with
attachment to a view as authoritative

attachment to a view
    is not identical with
attachment to a procedure as sufficient
```

Thus `mithyādṛṣṭi`, `dṛṣṭiparāmarśa`, and `śīlavrataparāmarśa` anticipate
distinct failure modes: misdetermination, reflexive overcommitment to a
determination, and overcommitment to a practice or rule as though conformity
alone guaranteed release. This is an Organon interpretation of the typed
distinctions, not a substitute for their later textual definitions.

The Path is already internal to the classification. An affliction is not
fully determined until the exposition can say not only what it is and where
it operates, but how it ceases.
