# VAK_5.66

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अनागम्यफलं सर्वा ध्यानानां पञ्च वाथवा ।
अष्टौ सामन्तकस्यैका मौलारूप्यत्रयस्य च ॥ ५.६६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
anāgamya-phalaṃ sarvā dhyānānāṃ pañca vāthavā |
aṣṭau sāmantakasyaikā maulārūpya-trayasya ca || 5.66 ||
```

VAK 5.64–5.65 established what the nine parijñās are. VAK 5.66 now relates
those completed abandonments to the meditative bases whose Paths can yield
them as fruits.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
anāgamya-phalam | sarvāḥ | dhyānānām | pañca | vā | athavā |
aṣṭau | sāmantakasya | ekā | maula-ārūpya-trayasya | ca
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `anāgamya-phalam` | nominative singular neuter compound, predicative | fruit of the preliminary concentration below the first dhyāna |
| `sarvāḥ` | nominative plural feminine | all nine parijñās |
| `dhyānānām` | genitive plural neuter | of the fundamental dhyānas |
| `pañca` | numeral | five parijñās, on the Vaibhāṣika account |
| `vā athavā` | disjunctive particles | or, alternatively |
| `aṣṭau` | numeral | eight parijñās, according to Bhadanta Ghoṣaka |
| `sāmantakasya` | genitive singular neuter | of the preliminary concentration of the sphere of infinite space |
| `ekā` | nominative singular feminine | one parijñā |
| `maula-ārūpya-trayasya` | genitive singular compound | of the three fundamental formless attainments |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |

`Phala` expresses a result relation, not identity: the meditative basis
supports a Path whose result is a determinate parijñā.

## 4. Grammar

The first construction distributes a singular result-predicate over the full
set:

```text
sarvāḥ [nava parijñāḥ]
    anāgamya-phalam
```

The next construction supplies two alternative counts for the dhyānas:

```text
dhyānānām
    pañca
    vā athavā aṣṭau
```

The Bhāṣya attributes five to the Vaibhāṣikas and eight to Bhadanta Ghoṣaka.
The disjunction therefore records competing mappings, not uncertainty about
the total number of parijñās.

The final two genitives each govern an understood `phalam`:

```text
ākāśānantyāyatana-sāmantakasya
    ekā [parijñā phalam]

maulānām ārūpyāṇāṃ trayāṇām
    ekā [parijñā phalam]
```

The prose also states that `dhyānāntara`, the intermediate dhyāna, is to be
treated like the dhyānas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> All [nine parijñās] are fruits of the anāgamya. Of the dhyānas there are
> five—or alternatively eight. One is [the fruit] of the preliminary
> concentration, and one of the three fundamental formless attainments.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> All nine parijñās can be fruits of the preliminary concentration below the
> first dhyāna. According to the Vaibhāṣikas, the fundamental dhyānas have five
> as fruits—the five consisting in abandonment of form- and formless-domain
> afflictions. According to Bhadanta Ghoṣaka they have eight, adding the three
> desire-domain seeing-abandonment parijñās, while abandonment of the lower-
> part fetters remains exclusively a fruit of anāgamya. The preliminary
> concentration of the sphere of infinite space yields the destruction of
> attachment to form; each of the three fundamental formless attainments can
> yield the complete exhaustion of all fetters.

The intermediate dhyāna follows the same rule as the fundamental dhyānas.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Completed abandonment and its meditative basis are different determinations.
> One basis can support Paths yielding many completions, while several bases
> can share one possible fruit. The widest assignment belongs to anāgamya;
> fundamental and formless bases receive more restricted sets. The five/eight
> dispute concerns attribution of a recognized completion to a basis, not the
> existence of the completion or the ninefold system itself.

The Bhāṣya states these capacities and gives the specific ground of Ghoṣaka's
additional three; it does not here derive the complete distribution from a
general metaphysics of transitional and fundamental states.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Anāgamya` — preliminary concentration below the first dhyāna**

A meditative basis capable of supporting Paths whose fruits include all nine
parijñās. The passage asserts this full range without separately explaining a
general abstract reason for it.

**`Maula-dhyāna` — fundamental dhyāna**

On the Vaibhāṣika account, a basis for five higher-domain parijñās:

```text
higher-domain suffering + origin seeing-abandonment
higher-domain cessation seeing-abandonment
higher-domain Path seeing-abandonment
destruction of attachment to form
complete exhaustion of all fetters
```

**The Vaibhāṣika five**

These five have as their nature abandonment of afflictions belonging to the
form and formless domains. Desire-domain abandonment is excluded because it
is assigned as a fruit of anāgamya.

**Bhadanta Ghoṣaka's eight**

Ghoṣaka adds the three desire-domain seeing-abandonment parijñās. Even one
already free from desire-domain passion can obtain through the Path of seeing
a fresh undefiled acquisition of disconnection
(`anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti-lābha`).

**`Avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa-parijñā` — lower-part-fetter abandonment**

Excluded even from Ghoṣaka's eight and stated to be exclusively a fruit of
anāgamya. Hence his alternative does not erase the distinction between
anāgamya and fundamental dhyāna.

**`Dhyānāntara` — intermediate dhyāna**

Explicitly treated like the dhyānas for the present fruit-distribution.

**`Ākāśānantyāyatana-sāmantaka` — preliminary concentration of infinite space**

Its single parijñā-fruit is `rūparāga-kṣaya`, destruction of attachment to
form.

**The three `maula-ārūpyas` — fundamental formless attainments**

Each has the same single possible parijñā-fruit:
`sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna`, complete exhaustion of all fetters. The prose
does not assign three different fruits to the three bases.

## 8. Logical Determination

The fundamental relation is mediated:

```text
MeditativeBasis
    supports Path
        yieldsAsFruit Parijñā
```

Therefore:

```text
MeditativeBasis(b)
    ≠ Parijñā(p)

HigherMeditativeLevel(b)
    ⇏ WiderParijñāFruitRange(b)
```

The shared assignments are:

```text
FruitSet(Anāgamya) = AllNineParijñās

FruitSet(ĀkāśānantyāyatanaSāmantaka)
    = {RūparāgaKṣayaParijñā}

For b in ThreeFundamentalFormlessAttainments:
    FruitSet(b) = {AllFetterExhaustionParijñā}
```

The disputed assignment is provenance-qualified:

```text
Vaibhāṣika:
    Count(FruitSet(FundamentalDhyānas)) = 5

BhadantaGhoṣaka:
    Count(FruitSet(FundamentalDhyānas)) = 8
```

Ghoṣaka's inference depends on VAK 5.63's distinction:

```text
AlreadyFreeFromDesirePassion(person)
AND Arises(PathOfSeeing, person)
    → Acquires(person, UndefiledDisconnectionAcquisition)

FreshAcquisitionOfDisconnection
    ⇏ RepeatedDestructionOfSameAffliction
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A new classificatory axis.** VAK 5.64–5.65 classified parijñās by abandoned
range and Path-mode. VAK 5.66 asks which meditative basis can support the Path
whose fruit each completion is. Content and production-basis must remain
distinct.

**Stable ontology, disputed attribution.** Vaibhāṣikas and Ghoṣaka accept the
nine parijñās. Their disagreement concerns whether three desire-domain
seeing-completions can also count as fruits of fundamental dhyāna.

**Internal confirmation of VAK 5.63.** Ghoṣaka's account requires fresh
undefiled acquisition of disconnection by one already free from lower
passion. This is intelligible only when acquisition of disconnection differs
from renewed destruction.

**No inferred theory of anāgamya.** The text assigns all nine to anāgamya but
does not here explain that breadth through its supposed transitional
structure. Such an account may be comparative interpretation, but it is not
Bhāṣya-confirmed in this unit.

**Organon interpretation.** A semantic Dataset should represent the fruit
mapping as an attributed ternary relation:

```text
PathFruitAssertion
    hasMeditativeBasis
    supportsPath
    yieldsParijñā
    assertedBy DoctrinalPosition
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This retains shared entities while allowing incompatible attribution counts
without corrupting the ontology.
