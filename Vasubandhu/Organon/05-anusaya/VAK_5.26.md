# VAK_5.26

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ते भावलक्षणावस्थान्यथान्यथिकसंज्ञिताः ।
तृतीयः शोभनोऽध्वानः कारित्रेण व्यवस्थिताः ॥ ५.२६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
te bhāva-lakṣaṇāvasthānyathānyathika-saṃjñitāḥ |
tṛtīyaḥ śobhano 'dhvānaḥ kāritreṇa vyavasthitāḥ || 5.26 ||
```

The verse names the four Sarvāstivāda explanations announced in VAK 5.25 and
provisionally prefers the third, which determines temporal courses through
operative activity.

The four compressed designations are unpacked by the Bhāṣya and attributed to
Dharmatrāta, Ghoṣaka, Vasumitra, and Buddhadeva respectively.
## 4. Grammar

**Fourfold naming**

```text
te
bhāvānyathika-
lakṣaṇānyathika-
avasthānyathika-
anyathānyathika-saṃjñitāḥ

those are designated as proponents of difference
through mode, characteristic, state, and relative otherness
```

The long compound distributes four explanatory principles across the four
positions. It does not assert that one theory contains all four alterations.

**Provisional evaluation and rationale**

```text
tṛtīyaḥ śobhanaḥ
adhvānaḥ kāritreṇa vyavasthitāḥ

the third is preferable:
the temporal courses are distinguished by operative activity
```

The singular `tṛtīyaḥ` refers to the state-difference theory. `Adhvānaḥ` is
the plural subject of `vyavasthitāḥ`; `kāritreṇa` names the differentiating
instrument or principle.
The Bhāṣya's judgment that the third is preferable is comparative among
these four; its further challenge to the role of activity begins in 5.27.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> They are designated as those [who explain difference] through mode of being,
> characteristic, state, and relative otherness. The third is preferable: the
> temporal courses are distinguished by operative activity.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Dharmatrāta, Ghoṣaka, Vasumitra, and Buddhadeva are named for difference in
> mode of being, characteristic, state, and relative position, respectively;
> Vasumitra's third account is preferable, for the three times are
> distinguished by operative activity.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> If one dharma is to remain real across three times, temporal difference needs
> a mediating principle. Four candidates locate the difference in mode,
> characteristic, state, or relation. Functional state is initially superior:
> it distinguishes what has not yet operated, what is operating, and what has
> completed operation. This is a comparative preference among four accounts;
> whether activity itself explains the temporal difference remains the question
> taken up in the next verse.

The dialectical sequence is:

```text
common requirement
    preserve reality and temporal difference

four candidate mediations
    mode / mark / state / relation

provisional selection
    state determined through function

next inquiry in 5.27
    does activity explain temporal difference?
```

Preference within a doctrinal set is not yet final truth.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Bhāvānyathatva` — alteration of mode of being**

Dharmatrāta holds that a dharma changes its mode across time without changing
its substance. The Bhāṣya compares a reshaped golden vessel and milk becoming
curd, then associates this transformation account with the Sāṃkhya position.

**`Lakṣaṇānyathatva` — alteration of characteristic**

Ghoṣaka holds that a dharma bears the characteristic of its current time while
not being separated from the other two temporal characteristics. Vasubandhu
objects that this collapses the temporal courses into one another.

**`Avasthānyathatva` — alteration of state**

Vasumitra holds that the dharma is differently designated upon reaching a
different state, not by becoming a different substance. A single counter has
different values according to its numerical position.

**`Anyathānyathatva` — relative otherness**

Buddhadeva determines temporal designation through relation to what precedes
and follows, as one woman may be called mother or daughter. His prose also
says this is a difference of state, not substance; the earlier-and-later
relation specifies how his state difference is construed. The objection is
that all three times could then be generated within any one temporal course.

**`Kāritra` — operative activity or function**

The principle of Vasumitra's preferred account:

```text
not yet functioning   → future
presently functioning → present
function completed    → past
```

The 5.27 Bhāṣya will ask whether past causes that still give results possess
activity, and whether activity is different from or identical with the dharma.

**`Tṛtīyaḥ śobhanaḥ` — the third is preferable**

An internal comparative judgment, not Vasubandhu's unqualified final
endorsement. The sustained criticism immediately following the verse is
essential to its force.

**`Adhvasaṃkara` — confusion of temporal courses**

The failure in which past, present, and future characteristics cease to remain
distinct. It is explicitly charged against the second theory and arises in a
different form against the fourth.

## 8. Logical Determination

The common problem is:

```text
ExistsAcrossTimes(Dharma)
AND Past ≠ Present ≠ Future
    → RequiresTemporalMediator(Dharma)
```

The four proposals select different mediators:

```text
TemporalDifferenceBy(Mode)
TemporalDifferenceBy(Characteristic)
TemporalDifferenceBy(State)
TemporalDifferenceBy(Relation)
```

Vasumitra's state rule is:

```text
NotYetPerformsFunction(x) → Future(x)
PerformsFunction(x)       → Present(x)
PerformedAndCeased(x)     → Past(x)
```

These rules state the locally preferred account's criterion. They do not
establish why activity occurs at one time rather than another; that question
belongs to 5.27.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya's explicit Sāṃkhya comparison concerns Dharmatrāta's
transformation theory (`pariṇāmavāda`). The following Puruṣa–buddhi
reading is an Organon–Yoga comparison. In that register, the four
temporal accounts can be presented for discriminative testing, like
something brought before Puruṣa for a “thumbs up or down.” The third
receives a provisional preference; 5.27 resumes the examination of
its activity criterion.

Puruṣa should not be converted into a psychological executive choosing among
theories. In the Yoga reconstruction, its standpoint is the non-appropriative
condition under which buddhi's presentation can be discriminated without being
taken as the Seer itself:

```text
buddhi presents determinate form
    → discriminative testing exposes its relations
        → adequacy or inadequacy becomes manifest
```

The “thumb” is therefore the result of the determination's own intelligibility.
Vasumitra receives a provisional affirmation here. The renewed questions
in 5.27 test whether that success is adequate.

This is also Hegel's demand upon the middle term. A mediator cannot merely stand
between identity and difference or attach labels to them. It must generate
their unity through its own movement. Otherwise mediation is either a second
unexplained thing or only another name for the original terms.

The fourfold architecture can be mapped here as an Organon extension.
`Āśraya`, `ālambana`, `hetu`, and `phala` are not terms used in this
5.26 unit; the diagram assigns project functions, not Sanskrit glosses:

```text
āśraya
    the analytic Basis of a proposed temporal account

ālambana
    the temporal determination taken as Support for inquiry

hetu
    kāritra proposed as the differentiating reason

phala
    the resulting temporal designation
```

In this mapping, 5.26 states the activity criterion that connects the
proposed `hetu` to the temporal `phala`. Whether that criterion explains
the temporal difference is taken up in 5.27.

The Aṣṭāṅga–Bhūmi distinction now receives a criterion of adequacy:

```text
Path in itself
    must contain a genuinely genetic mediation

Path in appearance
    must exhibit the difference produced by that mediation
```

An apparent bhūmi is not validated by a new name or state-label. It must be the
actual result of the Path's inner operation. Conversely, the Path in itself is
not adequate if it cannot produce the transformed appearance it claims to
mediate.

This is Organon–Yoga–Hegel reconstruction. Its textual ground is the four
Sarvāstivāda theories, the explicit comparison of Dharmatrāta with
Sāṃkhya transformation, and the provisional preference for state
difference through activity. The further critique belongs to 5.27.
