# VAK_5.44

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अगन्तुकामता मार्गविभ्रमो मार्गसंशयः ।
इत्यन्तराया मोक्षस्य गमनेऽतस्त्रिदेशना ॥ ५.४४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
agantukāmatā mārgavibhramo mārgasaṃśayaḥ |
ity antarāyā mokṣasya gamane 'tas trideśanā || 5.44 ||
```

VAK 5.44 records an alternative explanation for the three lower fetters
singled out in VAK 5.43. They correspond to three obstacles encountered in
directed travel and, by analogy, in going toward liberation.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
a-gantu-kāmatā | mārga-vibhramaḥ | mārga-saṃśayaḥ | iti |
antarāyāḥ | mokṣasya | gamane | ataḥ | tri-deśanā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `a-gantu-kāmatā` | nominative singular feminine abstract formation | unwillingness or lack of desire to go |
| `mārga-vibhramaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | deviation or going astray from the route |
| `mārga-saṃśayaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | doubt concerning the route |
| `iti` | quotative and enumerative closure | thus, ending the triad |
| `antarāyāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | obstacles or impediments |
| `mokṣasya` | genitive singular masculine | of or toward liberation |
| `gamane` | locative singular neuter action noun | in going or proceeding |
| `ataḥ` | ablatival adverb | therefore, for this reason |
| `tri-deśanā` | nominative singular feminine compound | teaching of the three, threefold teaching |

The compounds locate three distinct interruptions within one directed process:
refusal to begin, deviation through a different route, and uncertainty about
the route.

## 4. Grammar

**Enumeration of obstacles**

```text
a-gantu-kāmatā
mārga-vibhramaḥ
mārga-saṃśayaḥ
    iti antarāyāḥ
```

The three singular nouns form the plural subject of `antarāyāḥ`. `Iti` closes
the list and presents its members collectively as obstacles.

**Teleological setting and conclusion**

```text
mokṣasya gamane
    [ete antarāyāḥ]

ataḥ
    tri-deśanā [bhavati]
```

The locative `gamane` supplies the process in which they obstruct, while the
genitive `mokṣasya` specifies its end. `Ataḥ` draws the pedagogical conclusion:
therefore scripture teaches the abandonment of three.

**Voice attribution**

The Bhāṣya introduces the kārikā with `apare punar āhuḥ`, “others, however,
say.” The whole travel explanation is therefore reported as an alternative to
the `mukha-mūla-graha` account of VAK 5.43.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Unwillingness to go, deviation from the route, and doubt concerning the
> route—these are obstacles in going toward liberation; therefore there is the
> teaching of three.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> According to an alternative explanation, travel to another region can fail
> through unwillingness to depart, deviation caused by adopting another
> route, or doubt about the route. The same three obstacles govern movement
> toward liberation: personality-view produces fright at liberation and thus
> unwillingness to go; grasping at discipline and observance leads one to adopt
> another path and deviate from liberation; and doubt produces uncertainty
> concerning the Path. To make the abandonment of these three obstacles
> evident, the Blessed One taught the abandonment of precisely these three
> afflictions.

The ordinary-travel analogy, three affliction-obstacle mappings, and
pedagogical rationale are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Liberative movement can be interrupted at three non-equivalent positions.
> The destination can inspire refusal, a different route can replace the route
> to that destination, or the route can remain cognitively unresolved. These
> are not three quantities of one generic obstruction. They negate departure,
> orientation, and determination respectively within a single movement toward
> an end. The three fetters are thus taught together because their abandonment
> restores the articulated possibility of going: willingness to depart,
> fidelity to the route, and sufficient resolution concerning it.

This processual reconstruction develops the reported analogy while retaining
its status as an alternative explanation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Agantukāmatā` — unwillingness to go**

The first obstacle to travel. In liberative movement the Bhāṣya derives it
from personality-view: one becomes frightened by liberation and does not wish
to proceed toward it.

**`Mokṣād utrāsa` — fright at liberation**

The intermediate state linking personality-view to unwillingness. The Bhāṣya
does not further analyze this fright here as a particular theory of personal
extinction.

**`Mārga-vibhrama` — deviation from the route**

Going astray by adopting another route. In the liberative analogy it is
produced by grasping at discipline and observance.

**`Anyamārga-saṃśrayaṇa` — resorting to another path**

The precise Bhāṣya explanation of deviation. It warrants positive
misdirection, but this passage does not specify that the alternative must be a
subordinate Buddhist practice absolutized as the whole Path.

**`Mārga-saṃśaya` — doubt concerning the route**

The third obstacle, correlated directly with `vicikitsā`. The passage does not
decide whether the doubt concerns the Path's existence, efficacy, or one's
capacity to follow it.

**`Antarāya` — obstacle or impediment**

A factor defined by where it interrupts a directed movement. Here the common
movement is `mokṣa-gamana`, going toward liberation.

**`Deśāntara-gamana` — travel to another region**

The ordinary model from which the Bhāṣya transfers the threefold structure to
liberation.

**`Tri-deśanā` — teaching of three**

The pedagogical selection of personality-view, discipline-and-observance
grasping, and doubt in order to illuminate the abandonment of the complete
threefold obstacle structure.

**`Dyotayan` — making evident**

The Bhāṣya's description of the teaching's purpose: the Blessed One names the
three afflictions to reveal the abandonment of the corresponding obstacles.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya maps each affliction through an intermediate obstacle:

```text
Satkāyadṛṣṭi
    → FrightAt(Liberation)
        → UnwillingToDepart

Śīlavrataparāmarśa
    → Adopts(AnotherPath)
        → DeviatesFrom(Liberation)

Vicikitsā
    → Doubts(Path)
        → PathMovementUnresolved
```

Successful directed travel presupposes three distinct conditions:

```text
WillingnessToDepart(agent)
CorrectRoute(agent, destination)
ResolvedOrientation(agent, route)
```

The obstacles negate different relations:

```text
Agantukāmatā
    → NOT WillingnessToDepart

MārgaVibhrama
    → NOT CorrectRoute

MārgaSaṃśaya
    → NOT ResolvedOrientation
```

Therefore:

```text
CommonEnd(ThreeObstacles, Liberation)
    ≠ SameModeOfObstruction(ThreeObstacles)
```

Their unity is teleological; their difference is positional within the
mediated process of going.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** `Apare punar āhuḥ` attributes VAK 5.44's explanation
to others. It supplements rather than silently replaces VAK 5.43's genetic
account through entrances and roots.

**From ordinary travel to liberation.** The Bhāṣya first establishes the
structure in travel to another region:

```text
refusal to depart
    → no journey begins

adoption of another route
    → movement proceeds in the wrong direction

doubt about the route
    → movement lacks determinate orientation
```

It then transfers these same relations to `mokṣa-gamana`. The analogy is
controlled by the structure of directed movement, not by an assertion that
liberation is literally another geographical place.

**Why these three.** The Blessed One's teaching is described as pedagogically
selective: abandoning these three afflictions makes evident the removal of the
three complete obstacle-types. This is a second rationale for the same
scriptural triad, distinct from the root-and-derivative rationale of 5.43.

**Organon interpretation.** The verse supplies a minimal action schema:

```text
Agent
    adopts Destination
    selects Route
    resolves Movement
```

Diagnosis must preserve the location of failure. Refusal, misdirection, and
uncertainty require different determinations even when they prevent the same
end. This action architecture is the project's reconstruction of the
Bhāṣya's analogy and must remain distinct from its reported doctrinal voice.
