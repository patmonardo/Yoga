# VAK_5.05

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
चत्वारो भावनाहेयास्त एवाप्रतिघाः पुनः ।
रूपधातौ तथारूप्ये इत्यष्टानवतिर् मताः ॥ ५.५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
catvāro bhāvanāheyās ta evāpratighāḥ punaḥ |
rūpadhātau tathārūpye ity aṣṭānavatir matāḥ || 5.5 ||
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| catvāraḥ | numeral; masculine; nominative plural | four desire-realm anuśayas |
| bhāvanā-heyāḥ | compound adjective; masculine; nominative plural | to be abandoned through cultivation |
| te | pronoun; masculine; nominative plural (lemma: tad) | those same classes in the completed desire-realm system |
| eva | indeclinable | precisely those same |
| apratighāḥ | privative adjective; masculine; nominative plural | without resistance (*pratigha*) |
| punaḥ | indeclinable | again, in turn |
| rūpa-dhātau | compound noun; masculine; locative singular | in the Form realm |
| tathā | indeclinable | likewise |
| ārūpye | noun; neuter; locative singular | in the Formless realm |
| iti | indeclinable | concludes the enumeration |
| aṣṭā-navatiḥ | numeral; feminine; nominative singular | ninety-eight |
| matāḥ | past passive participle; masculine; nominative plural (lemma: mata) | held or reckoned of the anuśayas |

Sandhi and word division: *bhāvanāheyās ta* = *bhāvanāheyāḥ te*;
*evāpratighāḥ* = *eva apratighāḥ*; *tathārūpye* = *tathā ārūpye*;
*ity aṣṭānavatir matāḥ* = *iti aṣṭānavatiḥ matāḥ*.

The Bhāṣya resolves the range of *te eva*: it resumes the entire
thirty-sixfold desire-realm classification, not just the four anuśayas
named at the beginning of the verse.

`A-pratigha` is privative: the higher-realm systems repeat the prior
classification with the five modes of `pratigha` removed.

## 4. Grammar

**Cultivation remainder in the desire-realm**

```text
catvāraḥ bhāvanā-heyāḥ [santi]

four are to be abandoned through cultivation
```

The Bhāṣya identifies them as `rāga`, `pratigha`, `māna`, and `avidyā`. They
are abandoned after the truths have been seen, through `mārgābhyāsa`, repeated
cultivation of the Path.

**Anaphora across the completed classification**

```text
te eva apratighāḥ punaḥ rūpadhātau

those same [classes], again without resistance,
[occur] in the Form realm
```

Although proximity could make `te eva` appear to refer only to the four just
mentioned, the Bhāṣya first completes the thirty-six desire-realm cases and
then explicitly removes their five `pratigha` modes. The referent is therefore
the complete prior system.

**Parallel realm and concluding total**

```text
tathā ārūpye
    likewise in the Formless realm

iti aṣṭānavatiḥ matāḥ
    thus they are held to be ninety-eight
```

The Form realm and Formless realm each contain thirty-one cases.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Four are to be abandoned through cultivation. Those same [classes], again
> without resistance, occur in the Form realm and likewise in the Formless
> realm. Thus they are held to be ninety-eight.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Four are abandoned through cultivation. Those same anuśayas, without
> resistance, occur again in the Form realm and likewise in the Formless
> realm. Thus they are reckoned as ninety-eight.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The abstract sixfold root-system becomes a complete field only through its
> relations. In the desire-realm, each anuśaya is differentiated according to
> the cognitive support on which it rests and whether it is abandoned by
> truth-seeing or by subsequent cultivation. The resulting thirty-sixfold
> system is then transposed into the two higher realms under a determinate
> constraint: the five modes of resistance do not occur there. Thirty-six,
> thirty-one, and thirty-one therefore constitute ninety-eight situated forms
> of affliction—not ninety-eight unrelated psychological entities, but a
> generated total of possible systematic positions.

The Bhāṣya supplies the rule connecting affliction with correction:

```text
anuśaya rests upon a truth-specific cognitive support
    → it is abandoned through seeing that truth

anuśaya falls outside those seeing-specific cases
    → it is abandoned through cultivation
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Bhāvanāheya` — to be abandoned through cultivation**

The four in the desire-realm are attachment, resistance, conceit, and
non-knowing. Their cultivation-mode is not a fifth truth but a distinct manner
of removal after the truths have been seen.

**`Dṛṣṭasatya` — one for whom the truths have been seen**

The Bhāṣya's marker of the transformed standpoint from which cultivation
continues. Initial truth-disclosure does not imply that every affective and
habitual determination has already ceased.

**`Mārgābhyāsa` — cultivation of the Path**

Repeated realization of the Path through which the residual four are
abandoned. It distinguishes durable transformation from the initial event of
seeing.

**`Ālambana` — cognitive support**

The decisive relational term in the Bhāṣya's rule:

```text
ye yad-darśana-heya-ālambanāḥ
te tad-darśana-heyāḥ

those whose cognitive supports belong to what is abandoned
through a given seeing are abandoned through that seeing
```

`Ālambana` is not merely a detached external object. It is that with respect
to which the afflictive cognition operates and by which its removal class is
determined.

**`Prakāra` — mode or variety**

Satkāya-view has one removal-mode; several views and doubt have four;
discipline-and-observance adherence has two; attachment, resistance, conceit,
and non-knowing have five each.

**`Ākāra`, `prakāra`, and `dhātubheda`**

The Bhāṣya's closing formula for the differentiation of the basic six:
aspect, mode, and distinction of realm. These are generative coordinates,
not three additional anuśayas.

**`Apratigha` — without resistance**

The form and formless systems omit all five modes of `pratigha`. This passage
states and calculates that exclusion but does not yet give a separate
psychological explanation of why resistance is absent there.

## 8. Logical Determination

The desire-realm is generated by removal-mode:

```text
12 views
 4 doubts
 5 attachments
 5 resistances
 5 conceits
 5 non-knowings
--
36 desire-realm cases
```

The two higher realms are constrained transformations of that system:

```text
Form realm:
    36 - 5 pratigha modes = 31

Formless realm:
    36 - 5 pratigha modes = 31

total:
    36 + 31 + 31 = 98
```

The complete derivation is therefore:

```text
six basic anuśayas
    ↓ aspect and internal species
ten basic classes
    ↓ cognitive support and removal-mode
thirty-six desire-realm cases
    ↓ realm constraint excluding pratigha
thirty-one Form realm cases
    + thirty-one Formless realm cases
    ↓
ninety-eight determinate positions
```

The governing rule is relational:

```text
what an anuśaya rests upon
    determines
which seeing abandons it
```

The classification of bondage thereby contains a map of its possible
correction.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.05 completes the movement from root to system:

```text
5.01    anuśaya as root of bhava
5.02    internal division of attachment
5.03    internal division of view
5.04    truth-specific removal in the desire-realm
5.05    cross-realm completion of the ninety-eight
```

This confirms why the chapter feels psychological while remaining an
Organon. The elementary terms name modes of attachment, resistance, conceit,
non-knowing, view, and doubt; but none is allowed to remain an isolated inner
state. Each receives its meaning through a system of relations:

```text
afflictive type
    + cognitive support
    + mode of abandonment
    + existential realm
    = determinate anuśaya-position
```

The result is neither merely subjective nor merely objective. The disposition
is subjective as a manner of orientation and appropriation, while its realm,
support, and correction-condition give it objective systematic
determinateness.

The strongest Organon light comes from the `ālambana` rule. A failure is
scientifically specified only when its supporting relation and its appropriate
correction are known. The taxonomy of affliction is already a virtual
architecture of the Path.

That last formulation is Organon reconstruction. The textual basis is the
Bhāṣya's explicit generation of the ninety-eight through aspect, mode, and
realm, together with its rule relating cognitive support to abandonment.
