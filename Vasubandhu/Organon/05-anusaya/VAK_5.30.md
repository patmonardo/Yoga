# VAK_5.30

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
स्वकाधरत्रयोर्ध्वैकामलानां रूपधातुजाः ।
आरूप्यजास्त्रिधात्वाप्तत्रयानास्रवगोचराः ॥ ५.३० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
svakādharatrayordhvaikāmalānāṃ rūpadhātujāḥ |
ārūpyajāstridhātvāptatrayānāsravagocarāḥ || 5.30 ||
```

The verse continues the condensed object-support matrix begun in VAK 5.29.
The same three abandonment-classes are retained while their domain is varied
from the form-domain to the formless-domain.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
svaka | adhara-traya | ūrdhva-eka | amalānām | rūpa-dhātu-jāḥ |
ārūpya-jāḥ | tri-dhātu-āpta-traya | anāsrava-gocarāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `svaka` | compound member | belonging to their own domain |
| `adhara-traya` | compound member | the corresponding three cittas of the lower domain |
| `ūrdhva-eka` | compound member | one citta of the higher domain |
| `amalānām` | genitive plural | of the undefiled [cittas] |
| `rūpa-dhātu-jāḥ` | nominative plural compound | dharmas belonging to the form-domain |
| `ārūpya-jāḥ` | nominative plural compound | dharmas belonging to the formless-domain |
| `tri-dhātu-āpta-traya` | compound member | the corresponding three cittas belonging to each of the three domains |
| `anāsrava-gocarāḥ` | nominative plural compound | objects of undefiled citta |

The Bhāṣya carries forward from VAK 5.29 the three classes abandoned by seeing
suffering, seeing origination, and cultivation. They are not restated in the
kārikā's second half.

## 4. Grammar

**Form-domain dharmas**

```text
rūpa-dhātu-jāḥ
    [svaka-traya
     adhara-traya
     ūrdhva-eka
     amala]-gocarāḥ
```

The governing `gocara` relation is compressed across the first half and made
explicit at the end of the verse. The Bhāṣya expands the count: three cittas
of their own form-domain, three of the lower desire-domain, one cultivation-
abandonable citta of the higher formless-domain, and undefiled citta.

**Formless-domain dharmas**

```text
ārūpya-jāḥ
    [tri-dhātu-āpta-traya
     anāsrava]-gocarāḥ
```

The Bhāṣya construes `tridhātvāptatraya` distributively: the corresponding
three citta-classes in each of the three domains. With undefiled citta, the
total object-range is ten cittas.

**Textual caution**

The working Bhāṣya e-text transmits `svadā...` where the kārikā witness reads
`svakā...`. Its prose explanation with `svadhātukānām`, `adharadhātukānām`,
and `ūrdhvadhātukasya` confirms the intended own/lower/higher structure. The
variant should remain open for a later textual-witness review.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Those belonging to the form-domain are objects of their own three [cittas],
> the lower three, one higher [citta], and the undefiled [citta]. Those belonging
> to the formless-domain are objects of the corresponding three belonging to
> the three domains and of undefiled [citta].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The three indicated classes of form-domain dharmas can be objects of eight
> cittas: the corresponding three cittas of their own domain, the corresponding
> three of the lower desire-domain, the cultivation-abandonable citta of the
> higher formless-domain, and undefiled citta. The same three classes of
> formless-domain dharmas can be objects of ten cittas: the corresponding three
> in each of the three domains, together with undefiled citta.

The exact totals, the carried-forward three classes, and the restriction of
the single higher citta to the cultivation-abandonable class are supplied by
the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Domain-membership determines where a dharma belongs, but not the complete
> range of cognition from which it can be presented. Form-domain dharmas may be
> apprehended from below, within their own domain, from one specified standpoint
> above, and by undefiled cognition. Formless-domain dharmas are accessible to
> the corresponding cognition-types in all three domains and to undefiled
> cognition. The hierarchy is therefore an ordered field of determinate access,
> not a set of cognitively sealed compartments.

The Bhāṣya does not infer sameness of cognition from shared access. The
differences of domain, abandonment-type, and purity remain constitutive of each
object-relation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Rūpadhātujā` — belonging to the form-domain**

Here it qualifies the same three dharma-classes treated for the desire-domain
in VAK 5.29: those abandoned by seeing suffering, seeing origination, and
cultivation.

**`Ārūpyaja` — belonging to the formless-domain**

The corresponding three dharma-classes at the highest of the three conditioned
domains.

**`Adhara` and `ūrdhva` — lower and higher**

Relational domain-indexes. They do not imply inferiority or superiority of the
particular cognition's truth; they locate its arising relative to the dharma
being taken as object.

**`Gocara` and `ālambana` — range and object-support**

`Gocara` predicates cognitive range in the verse. The Bhāṣya restates each
entry by saying that the dharmas are `ālambana` of the enumerated cittas.

**`Amala` and `anāsrava` — undefiled**

The verse employs both terms across its halves. The Bhāṣya treats them as the
undefiled citta-class appended to each contaminated domain-based count.

**Eight and ten cittas**

The Bhāṣya makes the compressed arithmetic explicit:

```text
form-domain dharmas:
    3 own + 3 lower + 1 higher + 1 undefiled = 8

formless-domain dharmas:
    (3 corresponding × 3 domains) + 1 undefiled = 10
```

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
A(C, D) = citta-class C may take dharma-class D as ālambana
```

VAK 5.29–5.30 generate three patterned object-ranges for the same three
abandonment-types:

```text
Domain(D) = Desire     → |{C : A(C,D)}| = 5
Domain(D) = Form       → |{C : A(C,D)}| = 8
Domain(D) = Formless   → |{C : A(C,D)}| = 10
```

The relation is typed in both directions:

```text
D = Domain × AbandonmentType
C = Domain × AbandonmentType or Undefiled

Admissible(C, D)
    depends on both determinations
```

Accordingly:

```text
BelongsToDomain(D, x)
    ≠ CognizableOnlyFromDomain(D, x)

A(C, D)
    ≠ SameDomain(C, D)

A(C, D)
    ⇏ AnuśayaAdheres(C, D)
```

The matrix supplies the admissible `āśraya–ālambana` presentation. Further
conditions determine whether this presentation becomes an afflictive
`hetu–phala` operation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The variable held constant is abandonment-type; the
variable changed is domain. Form-domain objects admit eight citta-relations,
and formless-domain objects admit ten. Together with VAK 5.29, this closes the
first block of the matrix: the three domain-specific classes abandoned by
seeing suffering, seeing origination, and cultivation.

**A relational hierarchy.** The sequence `5 → 8 → 10` does not rank the objects
by intrinsic worth. It registers a widening field of admissible presentation.
The higher domains remain determinate precisely because access across their
boundaries is governed rather than indiscriminate.

**Yoga–Organon reconstruction.** A bhūmi is not a private enclosure. As a
determinate appearance of the Path, it must be intelligible from its own level,
from other specified levels, and ultimately from undefiled cognition. What
changes across the levels is not merely content but the structured reach of
the standpoint that presents it.

**The emerging science.** The Bhāṣya has now replaced a large collection of
questions with a small generative apparatus:

```text
hold abandonment-type constant
vary domain
derive admissible object-relations
use those relations to infer possible anuśaya-adherence
```

This is more than taxonomic economy. It is a science of determinate cognitive
access: the objects, standpoints, and transitions are mutually indexed so that
new results can be derived without flattening their differences. This is an
Organon reconstruction of the Bhāṣya's formal achievement, not terminology
asserted by Vasubandhu.
