# VAK 5.58 Bhāṣya — Feeling Range and Level

## Kārikā Anchor

> māyā śāṭhyam atho mrakṣo middhaṃ cobhayathā madaḥ |
> sukhābhyāṃ sarvagā upekṣā catvāry anyāni pañcabhiḥ || 5.58 ||

> Deception, crookedness, concealment, and sleep go with
> either mental gladness or mental distress. Intoxication
> goes with the two pleasant feelings; neutral feeling is
> universally compatible. The other four go with all five
> feeling faculties.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Deception, crookedness, concealment, and sleep are
conjoined in both ways, with mental gladness and mental
distress. Sometimes a glad person deceives another;
sometimes a distressed person does. The same applies
through the series as far as sleep.

Intoxication is conjoined with the two pleasant feelings:
with pleasure in the third dhyāna and with mental gladness
below it; above it, with neutral feeling. For neutral
feeling goes everywhere: all the anuśayas are conjoined
with it. There is no exclusion of neutral feeling anywhere,
as there is in the case of ignorance.

The four others are shamelessness, lack of concern for
reproach, torpor, and restlessness. These four
*paryavasthānas* are conjoined even with all five feeling
faculties, because they belong respectively to the great
unwholesome and the great afflictive classes of factors.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

`Ubhayathā` is not left indefinite: the prose specifies
`saumanasya` and `daurmanasya`. The example of deceiving
another while glad or distressed illustrates the two
possibilities, and `evaṃ yāvat svapiti` extends that rule
through the listed factors. A factor's identity cannot be
read from one affective tone alone.

The `madaḥ sukhābhyām` clause is qualified by *bhūmi*.
The two pleasant feelings are pleasure in the third dhyāna
and mental gladness below it. Above the third dhyāna the
commentary assigns neutral feeling. Thus a flat statement
that intoxication only ever accompanies pleasant feeling
would omit the prose's higher-level case.

`Sarvagā upekṣā` supplies the ground for the higher-level
case and recalls the general rule of 5.56. The prose
explicitly says **all anuśayas** can be conjoined with
neutral feeling. It does not explicitly extend this line's
universal quantifier to every secondary affliction. Its
comparison with ignorance is compressed and should not be
expanded into a further rule without checking the wider
doctrinal context. Neutral feeling alone does not certify
absence of an anuśaya.

Finally, `catvāry anyāni` is resolved as four
*paryavasthānas*: `āhlīkya` (expected `āhrīkya`),
`anapatrāpya`, `styāna`, and `auddhatya`. Their conjunction
with all five feeling faculties is grounded in class
membership: the first pair belongs to the unwholesome
great factors, the second to the afflictive great factors.
Class membership is the stated reason for broad feeling
range, not a claim that feeling and affliction are identical.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The anchor follows the separate
kārikā witness where the running text is visibly irregular.
The continuous Sanskrit retains the running witness.
“Neutral feeling” renders `upekṣā` as a feeling faculty in
this matrix; it is not a claim of liberative equanimity.
“Sleep” for `middha` and the English names for the four
other factors are working translations pending the later
lexical review.

**Organon reconstruction.** The passage supports a
compatibility relation indexed by factor, feeling faculty,
and level. The case of `mada` makes the level index
explicit; the four *paryavasthānas* add a distinct class
membership ground. These are project-level distinctions for
the Science of Citta, drawn from the source's argument and
kept separate from a proposed implementation.
