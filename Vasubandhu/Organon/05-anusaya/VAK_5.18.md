# VAK_5.18

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नानास्रवोर्ध्वविषयाः अस्वीकाराद् विपक्षतः ।
येन यः संप्रयुक्तस्तु स तस्मिन् संप्रयोगतः ॥ ५.१८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
nānāsravordhva-viṣayāḥ asvīkārād vipakṣataḥ |
yena yaḥ saṃprayuktas tu sa tasmin saṃprayogataḥ || 5.18 ||
```

The verse states exceptions to VAK 5.17's general rule of adherence through
Support and then establishes adherence through conjunction as a second,
distinct relation.

The initial sandhi resolves as `na + anāsrava`, not as an independent word
`nānā`. The verb `anuśerate`, “adhere,” is carried forward from VAK 5.17.

## 4. Grammar

**Exception to adherence through Support**

```text
anāsrava-viṣayāḥ [anuśayāḥ] na [ālambanataḥ anuśerate]
ūrdhva-viṣayāḥ [anuśayāḥ] na [ālambanataḥ anuśerate]

anuśayas with uncontaminated Conditions do not adhere there through Support;
anuśayas with higher Conditions do not adhere there through Support
```

The two causal ablatives explain the shared negation:

```text
a-svīkārāt
    because the Thing taken as Support is not appropriated

vipakṣataḥ
    because it stands opposed
```

**Correlative conjunction rule**

```text
yena [dharmeṇa] yaḥ [anuśayaḥ] saṃprayuktaḥ,
saḥ tasmin saṃprayogataḥ [anuśete]

whichever anuśaya is conjoined with a dharma,
it adheres in that dharma by conjunction
```

The Bhāṣya gives `tu` restrictive force: this rule holds only so long as the
anuśaya remains unabandoned.
It also distinguishes taking a Support from gaining afflictive footing in
it: non-appropriation and counteragency block the latter without denying
that the Support can be cognized.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Those whose Conditions are uncontaminated or higher do not [adhere in them],
> because of non-appropriation and because [those Conditions] stand opposed.
> But whichever [anuśaya] is conjoined with a [dharma], it adheres in that
> [dharma] by conjunction.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Anuśayas whose Conditions are uncontaminated or higher do not adhere in
> them through Support: those Conditions are not appropriated and oppose
> them. But an unabandoned anuśaya adheres by conjunction in the
> dharma with which it is associated.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> To cognize a Support is not necessarily to establish oneself within it.
> An affliction can refer to nirvāṇa, path, or a higher level while failing to
> appropriate that Support as a field in which it can reproduce itself. Such a
> Support opposes the affliction and denies it footing. Conjunction is different:
> an unabandoned affliction belongs internally to the dharmas with which it
> co-arises, not because it takes them as its Support, but because together they
> constitute one afflicted event.

The verse differentiates:

```text
reference
    what cognition takes as Support

appropriation
    what affliction can incorporate as its field

conjunction
    what co-constitutes the afflicted event
```

The text specifically denies that reference alone entails afflictive footing;
conjunction is established by a different relation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Anāsrava-viṣaya` — having an uncontaminated Condition**

False view, doubt, or ignorance may take nirvāṇa or Path as Condition. Yet the
affliction does not thereby adhere in the uncontaminated Condition. VAK 5.18 thus
distinguishes having an `ālambana` from finding afflictive footing in it.

**`Ūrdhva-viṣaya` — having a higher level as Condition**

A lower-level cognition may be directed toward a higher `bhūmi`. Aspiration
toward that level is wholesome `dharmacchanda`, not necessarily attachment;
the higher level remains opposed to lower-level affliction.

**`Svīkāra / asvīkāra` — appropriation / non-appropriation**

The Bhāṣya says that self-view and craving appropriate a Thing, allowing
other anuśayas to follow and settle there like dust on damp cloth.
Uncontaminated dharmas and higher levels do not enter this appropriative
structure.

**`Vipakṣa` — counteragent or opposing factor**

Nirvāṇa and path oppose the afflictions directed toward them; a higher level
opposes afflictions of a lower level. A Support can therefore be cognitively
present while functionally preventing the affliction's establishment.

**`Pratiṣṭhā` — footing or establishment**

The Bhāṣya's image is that the afflictions gain no footing on these Supports,
as feet gain no footing on a heated stone. Objective direction is compatible
with failure of inherence.

**`Ānuguṇya` — conformity or congeniality**

An alternative explanation, attributed only to “others,” reads `anuśaya` here
through congeniality: the uncontaminated and higher Supports are not congenial
to the relevant afflictions.

**`Saṃprayogataḥ anuśayana` — adherence through conjunction**

An unabandoned anuśaya adheres to the dharma with which it is conjoined. This
is an internal event-relation rather than an intentional relation toward a
Support.

**`Visabhāgadhātu / visabhāgabhūmi` — different domain / different level**

The closing question excludes anuśayas universally ranging toward a different
domain, then answers with universally ranging anuśayas directed toward a
different level. The distinction is stated in the Sanskrit and corroborated
by the Chinese parallel; because the Sanskrit is compressed, the exact scope
of the implied same-domain case remains an inference.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse's Condition and the Bhāṣya's three relations must be typed:

```text
HasCondition(x, y)
TakesAsSupport(x, y)
AppropriatesIntoAfflictedField(x, y)
ConjoinedWith(x, z)
```

Taking as Support does not entail adherence through Support:

```text
TakesAsSupport(Affliction, UncontaminatedOrHigherSupport)
AND CounteragentTo(UncontaminatedOrHigherSupport, Affliction)
    → NotSupportAdherentIn(Affliction, UncontaminatedOrHigherSupport)
```

Adherence through Support requires positive compatibility:

```text
TakesAsSupport(x, y)
AND AppropriableByAffliction(y)
AND CompatibleWith(y, x)
    → CanBecomeSupportAdherentIn(x, y)
```

Conjunctive adherence follows another rule:

```text
ConjoinedWith(Anusaya, Dharma)
AND NotAbandoned(Anusaya)
    → ConjunctivelyAdherentIn(Anusaya, Dharma)
```

Therefore:

```text
SupportReference ≠ AfflictiveAppropriation ≠ EventConjunction
```

This prevents intentional direction from being mistaken for constitutive
inherence.

The closing example also preserves the domain/level difference:

```text
NotDifferentDomainUniversal(x)
AND DifferentLevelUniversal(x)
AND ConjoinedWith(x, Dharma)
    → MayAdhereByConjunctionWithoutSupportAdherence(x)
```

The same-domain implication follows from the excluded different-domain class;
it is an inference about the example, not an extra Sanskrit phrase.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.18 clarifies the higher Objectivity of Path more forcefully than an
image of ascent alone could do. A higher Path-bhūmi is not merely a more remote
Condition represented from below. It has achieved actuality as `vipakṣa`: its
determinate form actively excludes the lower affliction from establishing
itself there.

```text
lower affliction
    can refer to higher Objectivity

higher Objectivity
    cannot be appropriated as the affliction's dwelling
    because it is the counteragent to that affliction
```

This is why `ālambana` and `āśraya` cannot be collapsed. Path can be the
Support of an afflicted cognition without becoming the concrete
Basis in which that affliction inheres. The distinction is not the old
external opposition of Subject and Object. It is an internal discrimination
within the Product between objective reference and achieved ground.

All four determinations are present in this higher Path relation:

```text
hetu
    Path as counteracting reason that prevents afflicted establishment

phala
    non-adherence of the affliction and the possible higher determination

āśraya
    higher bhūmi as achieved Basis, unavailable as a Basis for lower affliction

ālambana
    that same Path or higher level as objectively apprehended truth-field
```

The same determination can therefore stand as `ālambana` for the lower
cognition while refusing to serve as its `āśraya`. In its own achieved
actuality it functions as counteracting `hetu`, whose `phala` is the failure of
affliction to gain footing and the opening of wholesome aspiration.

Conjunction adds the complementary side. When an unabandoned anuśaya is
conjoined with dharmas, it becomes immanent in their event-complex. Thus the
Product contains both outward objective reference and inward constitutive
participation, but the two are governed by different relations.

This is Organon reconstruction. Its textual ground is the distinction between
`ālambanataḥ` and `saṃprayogataḥ`, the Bhāṣya's account of appropriation and
counteragency, and its restriction to the unabandoned anuśaya.
