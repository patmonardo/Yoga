# VAK_5.31

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
निरोधमार्गदृग्घेयाः सर्वे स्वाधिकगोचराः ।
अनास्रवास्त्रिधात्वन्त्यत्रयानास्रवगोचराः ॥ ५.३१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
nirodhamārgadṛggheyāḥ sarve svādhikagocarāḥ |
anāsravās tridhātvantyatrayānāsravagocarāḥ || 5.31 ||
```

The Bhāṣya treats this verse as the completion of the sixteen-citta by
sixteen-dharma object-support ordering begun in VAK 5.29.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
nirodha-dṛk-heyāḥ | mārga-dṛk-heyāḥ | sarve | sva-adhika-gocarāḥ |
anāsravāḥ | tri-dhātu-antya-traya-anāsrava-gocarāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `nirodha-dṛk-heyāḥ` | nominative plural compound | dharmas to be abandoned by seeing cessation |
| `mārga-dṛk-heyāḥ` | nominative plural compound | dharmas to be abandoned by seeing the path |
| `sarve` | nominative plural masculine | all, in every domain |
| `sva-adhika-gocarāḥ` | nominative plural compound | having the previous range plus their own corresponding citta as object-range |
| `anāsravāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | undefiled dharmas |
| `tri-dhātu-antya-traya` | compound member | the final three citta-types in each of the three domains |
| `anāsrava-gocarāḥ` | nominative plural compound | objects also of undefiled citta |

The bare compound `svādhika` could sound like an indefinite “own and
additional” range. The Bhāṣya determines it exactly: the previously established
range is retained, and the citta corresponding to the dharma's own abandonment-
type is added.

## 4. Grammar

**Cessation- and path-seeing classes**

```text
nirodha-dṛk-heyāḥ mārga-dṛk-heyāḥ sarve
    sva-adhika-gocarāḥ
```

`Sarve` distributes the rule across all three domains. `Sva-adhika` is resolved
by the Bhāṣya separately for the cessation-class and path-class: each receives
its own corresponding citta in addition to the object-range already computed
for that domain in VAK 5.29–5.30.

**Undefiled dharmas**

```text
anāsravāḥ
    [tri-dhātu-antya-traya-anāsrava]-gocarāḥ
```

The Bhāṣya resolves `antya-traya` as the final three citta-types within each
domain: those abandoned by seeing cessation, seeing the path, and cultivation.
The final `anāsrava` adds undefiled citta itself.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> All [dharmas] to be abandoned by seeing cessation and by seeing the path
> have their own [corresponding citta] in addition as their object-range.
> Undefiled [dharmas] are objects of the final three [cittas] belonging to the
> three domains and of undefiled [citta].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In every domain, dharmas abandoned by seeing cessation or seeing the path
> retain the object-range previously established for that domain and add the
> citta belonging to their own corresponding abandonment-class. Their ranges
> therefore contain six cittas in the desire-domain, nine in the form-domain,
> and eleven in the formless-domain. Undefiled dharmas are objects of ten
> cittas: the cessation-seeing, path-seeing, and cultivation classes in each
> of the three domains, together with undefiled citta.

The additive operation, its resulting counts, and the identification of the
“final three” are explicit determinations of the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The remaining classes are not entered as unrelated facts. Their cognitive
> ranges are generated by transforming a result already achieved: preserve the
> prior domain-range and add the cognition whose abandonment-type corresponds
> to the object itself. Even undefiled dharmas have a determinate rather than
> unlimited field of accessibility. The completed system thus coordinates what
> a dharma is, where it belongs, how it is abandoned, and which cognition can
> present it—before any conclusion about anuśaya is drawn.

The matrix does not reduce the object to the cognition or the cognition to its
object. Their determinations remain distinct and become concrete through the
qualified `ālambana` relation joining them.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Nirodha-dṛg-heya` — abandoned by seeing cessation**

One of the five abandonment-classes within each conditioned domain. Its object-
range is the previously established domain-range plus the cessation-seeing
citta of that same domain.

**`Mārga-dṛg-heya` — abandoned by seeing the path**

The parallel class whose added “own” citta is the corresponding path-seeing
citta.

**`Svādhika` — with one's own added**

Not a vague excess. The Bhāṣya gives it the exact operational form:

```text
ExtendedRange = PreviousDomainRange ∪ {OwnCorrespondingCitta}
```

**`Antya-traya` — the final three**

The citta-types abandoned by seeing cessation, seeing the path, and cultivation
within each of the desire, form, and formless domains.

**`Anāsrava` — undefiled**

Here both an object-class and a citta-class. Undefiled dharmas are accessible
to nine final domain-based citta-types plus undefiled citta, making ten.

**`Ālambana-vyavasthā` — ordered determination of object-supports**

The Bhāṣya's description of the now-completed relation-system between sixteen
citta-classes and sixteen dharma-classes.

**`Diṅmātra` — a directional indication**

The Bhāṣya announces that what follows will demonstrate how to apply the
completed ordering to anuśaya questions, rather than enumerate every answer.

## 8. Logical Determination

For each domain `d`, let `B(d)` be the object-range established in VAK
5.29–5.30 for the suffering-, origination-, and cultivation-abandonable
dharmas. VAK 5.31 defines:

```text
Range(NirodhaClass, d)
    = B(d) ∪ {NirodhaCitta(d)}

Range(MargaClass, d)
    = B(d) ∪ {MargaCitta(d)}
```

Therefore:

```text
|B(Desire)|   = 5    → extended range = 6
|B(Form)|     = 8    → extended range = 9
|B(Formless)| = 10   → extended range = 11
```

For undefiled dharmas:

```text
Range(UndefiledDharma)
    = {NirodhaCitta(d), MargaCitta(d), CultivationCitta(d)
       for d in the three domains}
      ∪ {UndefiledCitta}

    cardinality = (3 × 3) + 1 = 10
```

The completed procedure is:

```text
Type(Dharma)
    → DeriveAdmissibleCittaRange(Dharma)
        → ApplyAnuśayaConditions(Citta, Dharma)
            → DetermineAdherence
```

The first two operations establish a possible `āśraya–ālambana` transaction;
the third determines whether an afflictive `hetu–phala` production actually
occurs. Possibility, presentation, and production are not collapsed.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** VAK 5.31 completes all five abandonment-classes in
the three domains and adds the undefiled class: sixteen dharma-classes in all.
The same sixteenfold typing applies to cittas. Their `ālambana-vyavasthā` is
now complete, and the Bhāṣya turns next from construction of the matrix to its
application.

**The Product becomes a new premise.** `Svādhika` directly exhibits the
circulation already discovered in this chapter. The ranges produced by VAK
5.29–5.30 are preserved as the ground of a new operation; one determinate citta
is added, yielding the new ranges 6, 9, and 11:

```text
prior phala–ālambana determination
    → present hetu–āśraya of extension
        → new determinate range as Product
```

This is a project-level logical articulation. The Bhāṣya itself supplies the
additive derivation but not the fourfold Organon vocabulary.

**Objectivity and the Path.** The Path is becoming executable because its
object-field is no longer merely named. Every dharma-class now has a rule-
governed cognitive range, and the next operation can use this completed
Objectivity to diagnose where anuśaya adheres. The matrix is the Path's
determinate presentation; its application is the Path acting upon that
presentation.

**A new science.** The decisive advance is the transition:

```text
classification
    → relational ordering
        → derived extension
            → concrete application
```

This is not simply an ontology plus commentary. It is a finite calculus in
which prior results become premises for further determinations, while each
result remains accountable to domain, path-operation, object, and standpoint.
