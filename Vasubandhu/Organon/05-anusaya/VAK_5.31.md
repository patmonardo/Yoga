# VAK_5.31

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
निरोधमार्गदृग्घेयाः सर्वे स्वाधिकगोचराः ।
अनास्रवास्त्रिधात्वन्त्यत्रयानास्रवगोचराः ॥ ५.३१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
nirodhamārgadṛggheyāḥ sarve svādhikagocarāḥ |
anāsravās tridhātvantyatrayānāsravagocarāḥ || 5.31 ||
```

The Bhāṣya treats this verse as the completion of the sixteen-consciousness-class by
sixteen-dharma-class object-support ordering begun in VAK 5.29.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `nirodha-dṛk-heyāḥ` | nominative plural compound | dharmas to be abandoned by seeing cessation |
| `mārga-dṛk-heyāḥ` | nominative plural compound | dharmas to be abandoned by seeing the path |
| `sarve` | nominative plural masculine | all, in every realm |
| `sva-adhika-gocarāḥ` | nominative plural compound | having the previous range plus their own corresponding consciousness as object-range |
| `anāsravāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | undefiled dharmas |
| `tri-dhātu-antya-traya` | compound member | the final three consciousness types in each of the three realms |
| `anāsrava-gocarāḥ` | nominative plural compound | objects also of undefiled consciousness |

The bare compound `svādhika` could sound like an indefinite “own and
additional” range. The Bhāṣya determines it exactly: the previously established
range is retained, and the consciousness corresponding to the dharma's own abandonment-
type is added.
## 4. Grammar

**Cessation- and path-seeing classes**

```text
nirodha-dṛk-heyāḥ mārga-dṛk-heyāḥ sarve
    sva-adhika-gocarāḥ
```

`Sarve` distributes the rule across all three realms. `Sva-adhika` is resolved
by the Bhāṣya separately for the cessation-class and path-class: each receives
its own corresponding consciousness in addition to the object-range already computed
for that realm in VAK 5.29–5.30.

**Undefiled dharmas**

```text
anāsravāḥ
    [tri-dhātu-antya-traya-anāsrava]-gocarāḥ
```

The Bhāṣya resolves `antya-traya` as the final three consciousness types within each
realm: those abandoned by seeing cessation, seeing the path, and cultivation.
The final `anāsrava` adds undefiled consciousness itself.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> All [dharmas] to be abandoned by seeing cessation and by seeing the path
> have their own [corresponding consciousness] in addition as their object-range.
> Undefiled [dharmas] are objects of the final three [consciousness classes] belonging to the
> three realms and of undefiled [consciousness].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In every realm, dharmas abandoned by seeing cessation or the path are
> objects for the previously established ranges plus their own corresponding
> consciousness class. Undefiled dharmas are objects for the final three classes
> of consciousness in each of the three realms—those abandoned by seeing cessation,
> seeing the path, and cultivation—and for undefiled consciousness.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The remaining classes are not entered as unrelated facts. Their cognitive
> ranges are generated by transforming a result already achieved: preserve the
> prior realm-range and add the consciousness class whose abandonment-type corresponds
> to the object itself. Even undefiled dharmas have a determinate rather than
> unlimited field of accessibility. The completed system thus coordinates what
> a dharma is, where it belongs, how it is abandoned, and which cognition can
> present it—before any conclusion about anuśaya is drawn.

The matrix does not reduce the object to the cognition or the cognition to its
object. Their determinations remain distinct and become concrete through the
qualified `ālambana` relation joining them.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Nirodha-dṛg-heya` — abandoned by seeing cessation**

One of the five abandonment-classes within each conditioned realm. Its object-
range is the previously established realm-range plus the cessation-seeing
consciousness of that same realm.

**`Mārga-dṛg-heya` — abandoned by seeing the path**

The parallel class whose added “own” consciousness is the corresponding path-seeing
consciousness.

**`Svādhika` — with one's own added**

Not a vague excess. The Bhāṣya gives it the exact operational form:

```text
ExtendedRange = PreviousRealmRange ∪ {OwnCorrespondingConsciousness}
```

**`Antya-traya` — the final three**

The consciousness types abandoned by seeing cessation, seeing the path, and cultivation
within each of the desire, form, and formless realms.

**`Anāsrava` — undefiled**

Here both an object-class and a consciousness class. Undefiled dharmas are accessible
to nine final realm-based consciousness types plus undefiled consciousness, making ten.

**`Ālambana-vyavasthā` — ordered determination of object-supports**

The Bhāṣya's description of the now-completed relation-system between sixteen
consciousness classes and sixteen dharma-classes.

**`Diṅmātra` — a directional indication**

The Bhāṣya announces that what follows will demonstrate how to apply the
completed ordering to anuśaya questions, rather than enumerate every answer.

## 8. Logical Determination

For each realm `d`, let `B(d)` be the object-range established in VAK
5.29–5.30 for the suffering-, origination-, and cultivation-abandonable
dharmas. VAK 5.31 defines:

```text
Range(NirodhaClass, d)
    = B(d) ∪ {NirodhaConsciousness(d)}

Range(MargaClass, d)
    = B(d) ∪ {MargaConsciousness(d)}
```

Therefore:

```text
|B(Desire)|   = 5    → extended range = 6
|B(Form)|     = 8    → extended range = 9
|B(Formless)| = 10   → extended range = 11
```

For undefiled dharmas:

```text
Range(UndefiledDharma)
    = {NirodhaConsciousness(d), MargaConsciousness(d), CultivationConsciousness(d)
       for d in the three realms}
      ∪ {UndefiledConsciousness}

    cardinality = (3 × 3) + 1 = 10
```

The completed procedure is:

```text
Type(Dharma)
    → DeriveAdmissibleConsciousnessRange(Dharma)
        → ApplyAnuśayaConditions(Consciousness, Dharma)
            → DetermineAdherence
```

The first two operations establish a possible `āśraya–ālambana` transaction;
the third determines whether an afflictive `hetu–phala` production actually
occurs. Possibility, presentation, and production are not collapsed.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** VAK 5.31 completes all five abandonment-classes in
the three realms and adds the undefiled class: sixteen dharma-classes in all.
The same sixteenfold typing applies to consciousness classes. Their `ālambana-vyavasthā` is
now complete, and the Bhāṣya turns next from construction of the matrix to its
application.

**The Product becomes a new premise.** `Svādhika` directly exhibits the
circulation already discovered in this chapter. The ranges produced by VAK
5.29–5.30 are preserved as the ground of a new operation; one determinate consciousness
is added, yielding the new ranges 6, 9, and 11:

```text
prior phala–ālambana determination
    → present hetu–āśraya of extension
        → new determinate range as Product
```

This is a project-level logical articulation. The Bhāṣya itself supplies the
additive derivation but not the fourfold Organon vocabulary.

**Objectivity and the Path.** The Path is becoming executable because its
object-field is no longer merely named. Every dharma-class now has a rule-
governed cognitive range, and the next operation can use this completed
Objectivity to diagnose where anuśaya adheres. The matrix is the Path's
determinate presentation; its application is the Path acting upon that
presentation.

**A new science.** The decisive advance is the transition:

```text
classification
    → relational ordering
        → derived extension
            → concrete application
```

This is not simply an ontology plus commentary. It is a finite calculus in
which prior results become premises for further determinations, while each
result remains accountable to realm, path-operation, object, and standpoint.
