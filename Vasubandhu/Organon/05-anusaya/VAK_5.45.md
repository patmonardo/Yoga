# VAK_5.45

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
पञ्चधैवोर्ध्वभागीयं द्वौ रागौ रूप्यरूपिजौ ।
औद्धत्यमानमोहाश्च विद्वशाद् बन्धनत्रयम् ॥ ५.४५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
pañcadhaivordhvabhāgīyaṃ dvau rāgau rūpyarūpijau |
auddhatyamānamohāś ca vidvaśād bandhanatrayam || 5.45 ||
```

VAK 5.45 completes the fetter classification with five higher-part fetters and
then begins the classification of three bonds. The Bhāṣya is indispensable
because this classificatory boundary is compressed within the kārikā.

**Textual caution.** The working witness reads `vidvaśād`, while the Bhāṣya
prose explains the bond-triad through dependence upon the three feelings. The
transmitted kārikā reading is preserved here without silent emendation; its
precise restoration requires comparison with critical and printed witnesses.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
pañcadhā | eva | ūrdhva-bhāgīyam | dvau | rāgau | rūpi-arūpi-jau |
auddhatya-māna-mohāḥ | ca | vidvaśāt | bandhana-trayam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `pañcadhā eva` | indeclinable adverb with restrictive particle | precisely fivefold |
| `ūrdhva-bhāgīyam` | nominative singular neuter collective adjective | belonging to the higher part |
| `dvau rāgau` | nominative dual masculine | two attachments |
| `rūpi-arūpi-jau` | nominative dual masculine compound | arising in the form and formless domains |
| `auddhatya-māna-mohāḥ ca` | nominative plural coordinative expression | and restlessness, conceit, and delusion or ignorance |
| `vidvaśāt` | transmitted ablative form, textually suspect | exact lexical force unresolved; prose points to governance by the three feelings |
| `bandhana-trayam` | nominative singular neuter compound | triad of bonds |

The first five items are fixed by the Bhāṣya as form-attachment, formless-
attachment, restlessness, conceit, and ignorance. `Bandhana-trayam` does not
refer to the immediately preceding three nouns; it begins a new classification
whose members are desire, hostility, and delusion.

## 4. Grammar

**Five higher-part fetters**

```text
ūrdhva-bhāgīyam [saṃyojanam]
    pañcadhā eva

dvau rāgau
    rūpi-arūpi-jau

tau
    auddhatya-māna-mohāḥ ca
```

The collective neuter `ūrdhvabhāgīyam` is explained by the list of five. The
Bhāṣya's prose supplies `tau` after the two attachments and then adds the three
remaining fetters. It explicitly closes the unit with `samāptaḥ
saṃyojana-prasaṅgaḥ`, “the discussion of fetters is complete.”

**Opening of the bond classification**

```text
bandhanāni katamāni?
    trīṇi bandhanāni

rāgaḥ bandhanam sarvaḥ
dveṣaḥ bandhanam sarvaḥ
mohaḥ bandhanam sarvaḥ
```

The Bhāṣya's new question proves that `bandhana-trayam` crosses into another
taxonomy. Surface adjacency cannot override this discourse boundary.

**Textually uncertain causal phrase**

```text
vidvaśāt [transmitted]
    ↔ trivedanā-vaśa-vartīni bandhanāni [prose control]
```

The prose securely relates the bonds to the three feelings, but it does not
license silently rewriting the kārikā in the official study.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The higher-part [fetter-group] likewise is precisely fivefold: the two
> attachments born in the form and formless [domains], together with
> restlessness, conceit, and delusion. [Under the transmitted, uncertain phrase
> `vidvaśāt`,] there is a triad of bonds.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five higher-part fetters are attachment to the form-domain, attachment
> to the formless-domain, restlessness, conceit, and ignorance. Because these
> remain unabandoned, one does not pass beyond the higher domains. This
> completes the discussion of fetters. A new classification then begins: all
> desire, all hostility, and all delusion constitute the three bonds. They are
> taught as three through their relation to the three feelings—desire lies
> latent with respect to pleasant feeling through object-support and
> conjunction, hostility with respect to painful feeling, and delusion with
> respect to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling.

The hard boundary between fetter and bond, the three bond-members, their
feeling-relations, and the alternative own-continuum restriction are supplied
by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Passage beyond the desire-domain does not itself end bondage: five subtler
> fetters retain the continuum within the higher domains until they too are
> abandoned. Yet the verse then changes its question. It no longer asks what
> prevents cosmological transcendence, but how affliction binds across the
> complete field of feeling. Desire, hostility, and delusion correspond to
> pleasant, painful, and neutral feeling, not as identical terms but as
> afflictive modes conditioned through those hedonic relations. One field is
> thus partitioned first by domain-retention and then by feeling-conditioned
> bondage. The classifications meet in their members but differ in the reason
> that produces each arrangement.

This reconstruction follows the Bhāṣya's discourse boundary and does not infer
taxonomy from mere proximity in the verse.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ūrdhvabhāgīya-saṃyojana` — higher-part fetter**

One of five fetters whose non-abandonment prevents passage beyond the higher
domains. “Higher” concerns the domains to which they bind, not ethical approval
of the fetters themselves.

**The five higher fetters**

```text
rūpa-rāga       attachment to the form-domain
ārūpya-rāga     attachment to the formless-domain
auddhatya       restlessness or excitation
māna            conceit
avidyā           ignorance
```

The Bhāṣya's explicit enumeration controls the compressed kārikā syntax.

**`Ūrdhvadhātv-anatikrama` — failure to pass beyond the higher domains**

The common function of the five when unabandoned. It supplies the rationale
for their classification as higher-part fetters.

**`Samāptaḥ saṃyojana-prasaṅgaḥ` — the fetter discussion is complete**

A decisive discourse marker. It separates the five higher fetters from the
three bonds that follow within the same verse.

**`Bandhana` — bond**

A new functional classification comprising all desire, all hostility, and all
delusion. It must not be restricted to restlessness, conceit, and ignorance.

**`Trivedanā` — the three feelings**

Pleasant, painful, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Their complete
threefold field supplies the ground for the threefold bond classification.

**`Vedanā-vaśa-vartin` — operating in dependence upon feeling**

The semantic force of the Bhāṣya's imperfectly transmitted prose explanation.
It establishes a conditioning relation, not identity between feeling and bond.

**`Ālambana` and `saṃprayoga` — object-support and conjunction**

Desire lies latent with respect to pleasant feeling in both ways. The passage
then correlates hostility with painful feeling and delusion with neutral
feeling.

**`Svāsāṃtānika-ālambana` — object belonging to one's own continuum**

The Bhāṣya records this as an alternative basis for the restriction or fixed
correspondence (`niyama`). The alternative is retained without being collapsed
into the primary three-feeling mapping.

## 8. Logical Determination

The first classification is generated by domain-retention:

```text
Unabandoned(x)
AND MemberOf(x, {Rūparāga, Ārūpyarāga, Auddhatya, Māna, Avidyā})
    → PreventsTranscendenceOf(x, HigherDomains)
    → ClassifyAs(x, HigherPartFetter)
```

The second classification is generated by relation to feeling:

```text
PleasantFeeling(?v)
    → RāgaLiesLatent(?v, by ObjectSupport or Conjunction)

PainfulFeeling(?v)
    → DveṣaLiesLatent(?v)

    → MohaLiesLatent(?v)
```

Therefore:

```text
HigherFetterClassification
    hasRationale DomainRetention

    hasRationale FeelingConditionedBinding

    ⇏ SameClassification
```

The verse is a precise warning against syntactic over-grouping:

```text
Adjacent(auddhatya, māna, moha, bandhana-trayam)
    ⇏ MembersOf({auddhatya, māna, moha}, BondTriad)
```

Bhāṣya discourse structure overrides an otherwise tempting surface parse.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya first enumerates five higher fetters and
grounds them in failure to transcend the higher domains. It then declares the
fetter discussion complete, asks a new question about bonds, and identifies
the bond-triad as desire, hostility, and delusion.

**Textual caution.** `Vidvaśād` remains unresolved at the kārikā level. The
prose makes the intended doctrinal relation to the three feelings clear, but a
semantic control is not permission to manufacture a critical emendation. The
official study therefore keeps witness and interpretation visibly distinct.

**Three feelings, three bonds.** The correspondence differentiates modes of
bondage across the whole hedonic field:

```text
pleasant
    → desire

painful
    → hostility

neither-painful-nor-pleasant
    → delusion
```

This is a relation of conditioning and latency. The feelings are not themselves
identified with the afflictions.

**Transition in the chapter.** VAK 5.45 is a hinge in the fivefold sequence of
VAK 5.41:

```text
saṃyojana completed
    → bandhana defined
        → anuśaya already explained
            → upakleśa becomes the next topic
```

The same afflictive factor may enter multiple Views, but only under the relation
