# VAK_5.40

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आसयन्त्यास्रवन्त्येते हरन्ति श्लेषयन्त्यथ ।
उपगृह्णन्ति चेत्येषामास्रवादिनिरुक्तयः ॥ ५.४० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
āsayanty āsravanty ete haranti śleṣayanty atha |
upagṛhṇanti cety eṣām āsravādi-niruktayaḥ || 5.40 ||
```

VAK 5.40 completes the semantic sequence begun in VAK 5.39. It explains the
designations `āsrava`, `ogha`, `yoga`, and `upādāna` through the characteristic
operations of the same afflictive factors.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
āsayanti | āsravanti | ete | haranti | śleṣayanti | atha |
upagṛhṇanti | ca | iti | eṣām | āsrava-ādi-niruktayaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `āsayanti` | third-person plural present causative of `ā-√śī` | they cause to remain or settle |
| `āsravanti` | third-person plural present of `ā-√sru` | they flow or stream |
| `ete` | nominative plural masculine pronoun | these afflictive factors |
| `haranti` | third-person plural present of `√hṛ` | they carry away |
| `śleṣayanti` | third-person plural present causative of `√śliṣ` | they make adhere, fasten, or bind |
| `atha` | connective adverb | and then, moreover |
| `upagṛhṇanti` | third-person plural present of `upa-√grah` | they seize, take hold of, or appropriate |
| `ca iti` | conjunction followed by quotative particle | and thus |
| `eṣām` | genitive plural masculine | of these [terms or factors] |
| `āsrava-ādi-niruktayaḥ` | nominative plural feminine compound | explanations of the terms beginning with `āsrava` |

Although the verse contains five verbs, it does not define five classifications.
The Bhāṣya explicitly assigns both `āsayanti` and `āsravanti` to `āsrava`; the
remaining verbs explain `ogha`, `yoga`, and `upādāna` respectively.

## 4. Grammar

**Verbal sequence**

```text
ete
    āsayanti
    āsravanti
    haranti
    śleṣayanti
    atha upagṛhṇanti ca
```

The common subject `ete` governs all five coordinated verbs. Sandhi produces
`āsayanty āsravanty` and `śleṣayanty atha`. The actions, rather than five new
subjects, provide the grounds of designation.

**Concluding nominal clause**

```text
iti
    eṣām
    āsrava-ādi-niruktayaḥ [bhavanti]
```

`Iti` closes the quoted sequence of functional explanations. The plural
`niruktayaḥ` is the predicate of an elided copula: such are the explanations
of the names beginning with influx.

**Bhāṣya's two explanatory levels**

```text
initial nirukti:
    āsayanti saṃsāre + āsravanti ... → āsravāḥ

preferred account:
    āsravati ebhiḥ saṃtatiḥ viṣayeṣu → āsravāḥ
```

The phrase `evaṃ tu sādhīyaḥ syāt`, “but this would be better,” explicitly
marks the second account as preferable. It changes the explanation from loose
verbal association to the determinate operation of a continuum flowing toward
objects through the afflictions.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> These cause [beings] to remain, they flow, they carry away, they fasten, and
> then they take hold; such are the explanations of their designations
> beginning with “influx.”

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> They are initially called influxes because they cause beings to remain in
> saṃsāra and flow through the six wounds of the sense-spheres from the summit
> of existence down to Avīci; floods because they carry away; yokes because
> they fasten; and appropriations because they take hold. Vasubandhu then
> offers the better functional account: through influxes the continuum flows
> toward its objects; floods carry one away by excessive force; yokes bind one
> to manifold suffering even without extreme manifestation; and appropriations
> establish recurrent adherence through graspings beginning with desire.

The cosmic range, six sense-sphere wounds, boat simile, and preferred causal
explanations are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Afflictive names become adequate when they disclose operations. Influx is
> the current by which the continuum moves toward its objects when opposing
> effort relaxes. Flood is that current intensified into a force whose bearer
> is carried in its direction. Yoke is the structural bond to manifold
> suffering, a bond that need not announce itself through maximum intensity.
> Appropriation is repeated adherence through which objects and determinations
> are continually taken up. These are not interchangeable metaphors but
> distinct relations within one afflictive process. The superior explanation
> is therefore the one that reveals how the relation is actually generated.

The Bhāṣya itself licenses the movement from verbal derivation to causal-
functional determination by calling the latter account “better.”

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Nirukti` — explanatory derivation**

A semantic or etymological account that connects a technical designation with
an operation. The Bhāṣya distinguishes a serviceable initial wordplay from a
more adequate functional explanation.

**`Āsrava` — influx or contaminating current**

Initially explained through causing beings to remain in saṃsāra and flowing
through the six sense-sphere wounds. Preferably explained as that through which
the continuum flows toward objects.

**`Āyatana-vraṇa` — sense-sphere wound**

The six āyatanas figured as openings through which afflictive influx flows.
This is the Bhāṣya's metaphor and should not replace the chapter's more exact
relational account of āyatana.

**`Saṃtati` — continuum**

The temporally connected series that flows toward objects through the
influxes. It is the grammatical subject of Vasubandhu's preferred derivation.

**`Pratisrotas` and `anusrotas` — against and with the stream**

The directions in the sūtra's boat simile. Counter-current movement requires
great effort; when that effort ceases, downstream movement occurs with little
difficulty.

**`Ogha` — flood**

Explained by `adhimātra-vega`, excessive force or velocity. Its bearer is
carried away through conformity with its direction (`tadanuvidhāna`).

**`Yoga` — yoke or bond**

That which binds to manifold suffering (`vividhaduḥkhasaṃyojana`). Its binding
efficacy is not identical with extreme intensity of present manifestation.

**`Upādāna` — appropriation**

Explained through frequent or repeated adherence (`ābhīkṣṇyānuṣaṅga`) arising
from appropriations beginning with desire.

**`Sādhīyas` — better or more adequate**

Vasubandhu's evaluative marker for the second explanation. Adequacy increases
when the derivation specifies the causal relation within the continuum.

## 8. Logical Determination

The preferred explanations supply four non-equivalent operation-types:

```text
Āsrava(x)
    → FlowsTowardObjectThrough(Continuum, x)

Ogha(x)
    → HasExcessiveVelocity(x)
    AND CarriesBearerByConformity(x)

Yoga(x)
    → BindsTo(x, ManifoldSuffering)

Upādāna(x)
    → RepeatedlyProducesAdherence(x)
```

The yoke determination explicitly blocks a mistaken inference:

```text
NotExtremelyManifest(x)
    ⇏ LacksBindingPower(x)
```

The boat simile gives a transition structure:

```text
CounterEffortPresent
    → UpstreamResistancePossible

CounterEffortCeases
    → ExistingCurrentCarriesContinuumDownstream
```

Finally, the Bhāṣya orders two kinds of explanation:

```text
NominalResemblance(x, name)
    < CausalFunctionalGround(x, name)
```

This is not a rejection of nirukti. It is an internal refinement from a verbal
pointer to the operation that warrants the designation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The first two verbs jointly explain `āsrava`.
`Āsayanti` must therefore not be mapped back to `anuśaya`. The Bhāṣya first
offers a conventional fourfold nirukti and then explicitly prefers a more
determinate account grounded in the behavior of the continuum.

**The current and counter-effort.** The boat simile describes neither fatalism
nor effortless liberation. It determines an asymmetry:

```text
afflictive current already operative
    + cessation of counter-effort
        → easy conformity with the stream

movement against that current
    → sustained countervailing exertion
```

The simile concerns the causal efficacy of an established current. The later
Path analysis must determine how its conditions are abandoned, not merely how
one momentarily resists its effects.

**Intensity and structural bondage.** Flood and yoke answer different
questions. Flood emphasizes overpowering momentum; yoke emphasizes connection
to suffering. A quieter affliction may fail the criterion of flood-like force
while still maintaining the bond:

```text
phenomenal intensity
    ≠ structural efficacy
```

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 5.40 supplies a model for functional semantics:

```text
term
    → characteristic operation
        → affected relata
            → transition produced
                → consequence sustained
```

A Dataset should therefore preserve the underlying afflictive factors while
projecting typed Views for flow, transport, bondage, and appropriation. The
term becomes meaningful through the whole relation, not through a detached
gloss. This architecture is an Organon reconstruction, not terminology placed
into Vasubandhu's mouth.
