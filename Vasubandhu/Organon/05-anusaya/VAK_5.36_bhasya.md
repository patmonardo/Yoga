# VAK 5.36 Bhāṣya — One Existence Āsrava, Separate Ignorance

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> avyākṛtāntarmukhā hi te samāhitabhūmikāḥ |
> ata ekīkṛtāḥ mūlam avidyety āsravaḥ pṛthak || 5.36 ||

> The Form and Formless Realm anuśayas are unified as one existence āsrava
> because they are ethically indeterminate, inward-directed, and situated on
> concentrated planes. Ignorance, the root of all these afflictions, is
> classified as a separate āsrava.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Why are the anuśayas belonging to the Form and Formless
Realms, taken together, spoken of as one existence āsrava
[the question's `bhavāgra` is anomalous]? “For they are
ethically indeterminate, inward-directed, and situated on
concentrated planes; therefore they are unified.” Both
groups are ethically indeterminate, operate inwardly, and
belong to concentrated planes; through this threefold
similarity they are made one. For the very reason that
attachment to existence (`bhavarāga`) was so named, the
existence āsrava is so named.

Thus ignorance in the three domains is established as the
ignorance āsrava. These are fifteen counted items. Why is
it separately classified? Since ignorance is the root of
all these, “the āsrava is separate.”

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The two higher-domain groups share three stated traits:
ethical indeterminacy, inward-directed operation, and
location on concentrated planes. Their common kind of plane
does not make the two realms one and the same plane. The
Bhāṣya invokes the earlier naming of `bhavarāga` as a
parallel for the single existence āsrava.

The passage then identifies ignorance across the three
domains as fifteen counted items and asks why it receives
a separate āsrava. The answer is its root relation to the
other afflictions. This is a different ground from the
threefold similarity used for the higher-domain grouping.
The passage asserts the root relation; it does not here
describe a complete causal cycle or an individual course
of liberation.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Avyākṛta` is ethically
indeterminate, not a claim of liberation. `Antarmukha`
is explained by inward-directed operation
(`antarmukhapravṛtta`); `samāhitabhūmika` locates the
anuśayas on concentrated planes. The running `ūbhaye` and
`sādhamyerṇa` are anomalous; the three predicates establish
the sense “both” and “threefold similarity.” The question's
`bhavāgra` is retained in Sanskrit while translated
contextually as existence āsrava. `Mūlam avidyā` is the
explicit ground for separate classification.

**Organon reconstruction.** The first classification
collects factors across realms through shared properties;
the second separates ignorance by its root relation. These
are distinct grounds for grouping and division. The
fifteen-item count is a source claim; modeling their
membership and root relation as separate predicates is a
project-level determination.
