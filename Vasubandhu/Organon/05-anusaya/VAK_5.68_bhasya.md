# VAK 5.68 Bhāṣya — When Abandonment Counts as Parijñā

## Kārikā Anchor

> anāsravaviyogāpter bhavāgravikalīkṛteḥ |
> hetudvayasamudghātāt parijñā dhātvatikramāt || 5.68 ||

> Abandonment receives the name *parijñā* through
> uncontaminated acquisition of disconnection, disabling
> of the summit of existence, and eradication of the two
> causes; for knowledge-fruit abandonment, also through
> transcendence of a domain.

The four grounds are distributed by the prose. The first
three qualify the *kṣānti*-fruit cases; domain-transcendence
is introduced as an additional ground for *jñāna*-fruit
abandonment. The verse alone should not be turned into a
four-condition rule for every one of the nine *parijñās*.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Why is not each individual abandonment established as a
*parijñā*? First, abandonment that is a fruit of *kṣānti*
receives that designation because of uncontaminated
acquisition of disconnection, the disabling of the summit
of existence, and eradication of the two causes. Where
these three grounds are present, an abandonment is called
*parijñā*.

An ordinary person has neither uncontaminated acquisition
of disconnection nor disabling of the summit of existence;
such an abandonment therefore does not receive the name.
For an ārya's *kṣānti*-fruit abandonment, up through the
*anvayajñāna-kṣānti* concerning suffering there is
uncontaminated acquisition of disconnection, but the summit
of existence has not yet been disabled. At the
*anvayajñāna* concerning suffering, both are present, but
the two causes have not yet been eradicated: the universal
cause belonging to afflictions abandoned through seeing
origin has not been abandoned. In the subsequent
*dharmajñānas* and *anvayajñānas*, all three grounds are
present. In those states the abandonment receives the name
*parijñā*.

An abandonment that is the fruit of *jñāna* receives the
designation from those three grounds and from a fourth:
transcendence of a domain, when one passes beyond that
domain. Others propose a fifth ground, disconnection from
both bonds upon complete dispassion toward a whole domain:
when a type has been abandoned, one is also disconnected
there from another affliction taking it as object. The
expositor does not count this separately, because it is
already covered by eradication of the two causes and
transcendence of the domain.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The inquiry is about **the threshold for a technical
designation**, not whether an individual affliction can be
abandoned at all. The sequence of cases tests that
threshold. The ordinary person's abandonment lacks the
first two qualifications; early noble stages acquire the
first, then the second, but still lack the third. The
origin-seeing universal cause explains the delay. Only when
all three are present does the relevant *kṣānti*-fruit
abandonment count as a *parijñā*. This now gives a local
reason why 5.64 grouped suffering and origin in its first
completed abandonment.

The fourth ground is tied to *jñāna*-fruit abandonment when
a domain is passed beyond. `Bhavāgra-vikalīkaraṇa` means
rendering the highest conditioned level ineffective in the
specified path relation; the prose does not identify it
with destroying that level as a thing. `Hetudvaya` remains
twofold in the wording, but only one member of the pair is
locally identified by name. The later clarification pass
must not fill the other member by guesswork.

The reported `apare` view proposes a fifth qualification,
`ubhaya-saṃyoga-viyoga`. The reply accepts the described
disconnection but rejects its **independent count**: it is
already included under the third and fourth grounds. This
is a genuine disagreement over classification, not a denial
that object-taking afflictions may cease to bind.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Anāsrava` is “without
outflows” or “uncontaminated” here; `viyogāpti` is the
acquisition of disconnection, not mere non-occurrence.
`Bhavāgra` is the summit of existence, and `vikalīkṛti`
its disabling. `Dhātvatikrama` is passage beyond a domain.
The anchor's “also” makes explicit the Bhāṣya's allocation
of the fourth ground to *jñāna*-fruit abandonment.

**Organon reconstruction.** In our Anuśaya:Mārga reading,
local removal and qualified completion are distinct. A
*parijñā* designation becomes justified when the relevant
path relations have been achieved, not whenever one
afflictive item ceases to appear. This project-level
formulation follows the source's staged test while leaving
its twofold causal terminology open for textual study.
