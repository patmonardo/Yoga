# VAK 5.28 Bhāṣya — Abandonment and Disconnection

## Kārikā Anchor

> prahīṇe duḥkhadṛggheye saṃyuktaḥ śeṣasarvagaiḥ |
> prāk prahīṇe prakāre ca śeṣais tadviṣayair malaiḥ || 5.28 ||

> Even when the class abandoned by seeing suffering has been
> abandoned, one remains bound to it through other universal
> afflictions. Likewise, one can remain bound to a previously
> abandoned grade through the remaining defilements that take
> that grade as their object.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Now this is examined. If a `vastu` has been abandoned, is
the person disconnected from that `vastu`? Conversely,
wherever the person is disconnected, has that `vastu` been
abandoned? Wherever there is disconnection, the corresponding
`vastu` has indeed been abandoned. But something can have
been abandoned while the person is not yet disconnected
from it. For example: “When the class to be abandoned by
seeing suffering has been abandoned, one remains bound to it
through the remaining universal afflictions; and when a grade
was abandoned earlier, one remains bound through the other
defilements that have that grade as their object.”

When knowledge of suffering has arisen and knowledge of
origination has not yet arisen, the class to be abandoned by
seeing suffering has been abandoned. Yet one is still
connected with that class through universal afflictions
to be abandoned by seeing origination, because they take it
as their support. Similarly, among the nine grades of the
class to be abandoned by cultivation, when an earlier grade
has been abandoned one must understand that connection with
it can continue through remaining afflictions that take it
as their support.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya tests two implications. Disconnection from a
`vastu` entails its abandonment for that person. The converse
fails: abandonment of a class or grade does not yet remove
every relation of bondage to it. What remains is a different,
unabandoned affliction taking the abandoned `vastu` as
`ālambana` or `viṣaya`.

The first example fixes the path interval exactly: after
`duḥkhajñāna` has arisen but before `samudayajñāna` has
arisen. The class abandoned through seeing suffering has
been removed, while universal afflictions abandoned through
seeing origination can still take it as their object. The
second example applies the same relational pattern within
the nine grades abandoned by cultivation. A grade eliminated
earlier can still be the object of a remaining affliction.

Thus `prahīṇa` names an achieved abandonment, while
`visaṃyukta` names a stronger relational result. The text
does not say that the abandoned affliction itself remains
operative. It locates the remaining bond in other afflictions
whose object is the already abandoned class or grade.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Vastu` is kept as “object” or
“matter” rather than being equated automatically with an
affliction. `Tadviṣaya` in the Kārikā and `tadālambana`
in the prose name the object relation that allows bondage
to continue. `Śeṣasarvaga` is specified by the prose as
universal afflictions to be abandoned by seeing origination;
the two path knowledges must not be collapsed into one event.

**Organon reconstruction.** Abandonment of one factor and
disconnection from its former field are different operations.
For the Science of Citta, a model must track not only which
factor has ceased but which remaining factors still take
that field as object. This relational reading is project-level;
the specific path sequence remains the Kośa's doctrine.
