# VAK 5.28 Bhāṣya — Abandonment and Disconnection

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> prahīṇe duḥkhadṛggheye saṃyuktaḥ śeṣasarvagaiḥ |
> prāk prahīṇe prakāre ca śeṣais tadviṣayair malaiḥ || 5.28 ||

> After the class abandoned by seeing suffering has been removed, one remains
> connected with it through the remaining universal afflictions; likewise,
> after an earlier cultivation grade is removed, one remains connected with it
> through defilements that still have that grade as their Condition.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Now this is examined: when a Thing (`vastu`) has been abandoned,
is a person disconnected from that Thing? Conversely, where
a person is disconnected, has that Thing been abandoned? Where
there is disconnection, the Thing has indeed been abandoned.
But a Thing may have been abandoned without the person yet
being disconnected from it. For example: “When what is to be
abandoned by seeing suffering has been abandoned, one remains
bound to it through the remaining universal afflictions; and
when a grade was abandoned earlier, one remains bound through
the remaining defilements that have it as their Condition.”

When knowledge of suffering has arisen and knowledge of
origination has not yet arisen, the class to be abandoned by
seeing suffering has been abandoned. Yet one is still bound
with respect to that class through universal afflictions to be
abandoned by seeing origination, because they take it as their
Support. Similarly, among the nine grades in the class to be
abandoned by cultivation, when a grade has been abandoned
earlier, one must understand that bondage with respect to it
continues through the remaining afflictions that take it as
their Support.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya tests two implications. Disconnection from a Thing
entails its abandonment for that person. The converse fails:
abandonment of a class or grade does not yet remove every
relation of bondage to it. A remaining affliction can still
take the abandoned Thing as Support (`ālambana`). The verse
calls that Thing its Condition (`viṣaya`); the two terms name
related but distinct offices, not interchangeable words.

The first example fixes the path interval exactly: after
`duḥkhajñāna` has arisen but before `samudayajñāna` has
arisen. The class abandoned through seeing suffering has been
removed, while universal afflictions to be abandoned through
seeing origination can still take it as Support. The second
example applies the same relation within the nine grades to
be abandoned by cultivation. An earlier abandoned grade can
still be the Support of a remaining affliction.

Thus `prahīṇa` names an achieved abandonment, while
`visaṃyukta` names the stronger relational result. The text
does not say that the abandoned Thing remains operative. It
locates the continuing bond in other afflictions directed
toward the already abandoned class or grade.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Vastu` is Thing: the target of
abandonment or disconnection in the opening question. It is
not automatically an affliction. `Tadviṣaya` in the Kārikā
marks that Thing as Condition; `tadālambana` in the prose
marks it as Support taken by a remaining affliction. These
converge on the same case without making the terms synonyms.
`Śeṣasarvaga` is specified by the prose as universal
afflictions to be abandoned by seeing origination; the two
path knowledges must not be collapsed into one event.

**Organon reconstruction.** Abandonment of a determinate
class or grade and disconnection from that Thing are different
operations. For the Science of Citta, a model must track the
abandoned class and the remaining afflictions still directed
toward it. This relational reading is project-level; the
specific path sequence remains the Kośa's doctrine.
