# VAK 5.57 Bhāṣya — Feeling and the Secondary Afflictions

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> daurmanasyena kaukṛtyam īrṣyā krodho vihiṃsanam |
> upanāhaḥ pradāśaś ca mātsaryaṃ tu viparyayāt || 5.57 ||

> Remorse, envy, anger, harmfulness, sustained resentment, and obstinate
> fault-clinging are conjoined with mental distress. Avarice, by contrast,
> is conjoined with mental gladness.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Now, concerning the secondary afflictions: remorse, envy,
anger, harmfulness, sustained resentment, and obstinate
fault-clinging are conjoined with mental distress. The words
“are conjoined” carry over. These factors operate in a mode
of dejection and belong to the mental level.

Avarice, however, goes with the opposite feeling: that is,
with mental gladness. It belongs to the lineage of greed
and operates in a mode of exhilaration.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

After the kleśas' conjunction with feeling faculties in
5.55–5.56, `upakleśānāṃ punaḥ` opens the corresponding
inquiry for secondary afflictions. `Saṃprayuktāni` supplies
the verse's governing relation. The list names factors
conjoined with *daurmanasya*; it does not define them as
mental distress itself.

The Bhāṣya gives two grounds for that conjunction: their
dejected mode (`dainyākāra`) and their location on the
mental level (`manobhūmika`). It does not derive a separate
affective rule for each of the six named factors. These
grounds also explain why the relevant painful feeling is
mental distress rather than bodily pain.

`Viparyayāt` is resolved by the prose as `saumanasyena`:
avarice is conjoined with mental gladness. Here the text
gives two explanatory grounds: avarice belongs to greed's
lineage (`lobhānvaya`) and operates in an exhilarated mode
(`harṣākāra`). Lineage, mode, and conjunction with gladness
are distinct determinations. The text does not specify a
temporal or genetic sequence among these three. The pleasant
feeling does not make avarice wholesome.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** “With its opposite” renders the
verse's `viparyayāt`; “mental gladness” makes explicit the
Bhāṣya's resolution. “Obstinate fault-clinging” follows
the definition given in 5.50; this unit specifies its
feeling association rather than redefining it.
The mismatch `upanāhaṃ` / `upanāhaḥ` awaits textual review.

**Organon reconstruction.** This passage supports separate
relations for a factor's afflictive affiliation, operative
mode, mental location, and simultaneous feeling. In a
Science of Citta schema, the conjunction with a feeling
should therefore be represented independently of the
factor's lineage. This is a project-level inference from
the Bhāṣya's explanatory order, not a claim that Vasubandhu
supplies such a schema.
