# VAK 5.41 Bhāṣya — Five Divisions and Two View Fetters

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> saṃyojanādi-bhedena punas te pañcadhoditāḥ |
> dravyāmarśana-sāmānyād dṛṣṭī saṃyojanāntaram || 5.41 ||

> Those same anuśayas are taught again fivefold by divisions beginning
> with fetters. Through commonality of real factors and of grasping, the
> two classes of views are distinct fetters.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Those same anuśayas are again divided fivefold: fetter
(*saṃyojana*), bond (*bandhana*), anuśaya, secondary
affliction (*upakleśa*), and enveloping affliction
(*paryavasthāna*). There are nine fetters: attraction,
hostility, conceit, ignorance, view, grasping, doubt, envy,
and avarice. The fetter of attraction is attachment in the
three domains; the remaining terms are to be connected as
applicable. Three views make up the view fetter; two views
make up the grasping fetter.

Accordingly one may ask: can someone be bound by the
attraction fetter in relation to dharmas conjoined with view,
but not by the view fetter, even though a view anuśaya still
lies along there? Yes. This can occur after knowledge of
arising has appeared and before knowledge of cessation has
appeared, with dharmas conjoined with the two grasping views
that are to be abandoned by seeing cessation and the Path.
One is bound by the attraction fetter with these as Support.
The view fetter does not bind: its universal member
has been abandoned, and no remaining non-universal member
of that fetter has the required Support and conjunction
relations. Yet a view anuśaya lies along there through
conjunction: namely, the two grasping views themselves.

Why are three views called the view fetter separately, and
the other two the grasping fetter separately? “Through
commonality of factors and of grasping, the two view groups
are distinct fetters.” The three views comprise eighteen
factors; so do the two grasping views. Their equal factor
counts are reported as one ground for separation. The latter
two are distinguished from the rest by their grasping nature
[`śeṣāiti` is uncertain]; they are also separately specified
through commonality of grasping, because of a distinction
between what is grasped and what grasps.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The opening fivefold scheme names **five ways of dividing the
same anuśayas**. The passage develops its first member,
the nine fetters, rather than explaining all five headings
in detail. The apparent repetition of *anuśaya* in the list
is part of the source's wording and does not add a new
underlying inventory.

The path stage example is a deliberate counterexample to
equating `dṛṣṭisaṃyojana` and `dṛṣṭyanuśaya`. The first
designation is restricted here to a three-view fetter; the
second can apply to the two grasping views conjoined with
the dharmas in question. The distinction also depends on
which views have already been abandoned and on the
Support and conjunction relations. The prose does not
license treating “view” as one unqualified predicate at
every stage of the Path.

The last half-verse answers a different question: why the
five views are grouped as three and two. The first stated
ground is enumerative: eighteen factors in each group.
This should not be silently replaced with a claim that all
five share one cognitive substrate. The second ground is
the grasping nature shared by the two *parāmarśa* views.
`Grāhya-grāhaka-bheda` adds a distinction between the
grasped and the grasper, but the source states it compactly;
its exact reach requires review. `Kila` again qualifies
the reported enumerative rationale.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Dṛṣṭī` is dual because it
refers to two *groups* of views, not merely two individual
views. `Dravya-sāmānya` is rendered through the prose's
explicit eighteen-and-eighteen count. `Saṃyukta` is
“bound” in the fetter question, while `saṃprayukta` names
conjunction; they must stay distinct. The damaged forms in
the continuous Sanskrit have not been normalized, though
the conventional translation follows the locally coherent
reading.

**Organon reconstruction.** This passage requires a
classification to retain its governing relation, Support,
conjunction, and Path stage. One and the same afflictive
family can support a true view-anuśaya statement and a
false view-fetter statement in the example's context. The
`grāhya`/`grāhaka` pair further suggests typed roles in
grasping. These are project-level consequences, separate
from the Kośa's technical account and from any implemented
data model.
