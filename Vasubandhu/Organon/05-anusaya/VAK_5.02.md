# VAK_5.02

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
षड्रागभेदात् सप्तोक्ताः भवरागो द्विधातुजः ।
अन्तर्मुखत्वात् तन्मोक्षसंज्ञाव्यावृत्तये कृतः ॥ ५.२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ṣaḍrāgabhedāt saptoktāḥ bhavarāgo dvidhātujaḥ |
antarmukhatvāt tanmokṣasaṃjñāvyāvṛttaye kṛtaḥ || 5.2 ||
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| ṣaṭ | numeral | the six anuśayas carried forward from 5.01 |
| rāga-bhedāt | compound noun; masculine; ablative singular (lemma: rāga-bheda) | because attachment is divided |
| sapta | numeral | seven after the division |
| uktāḥ | past passive participle; masculine; nominative plural (lemma: ukta) | are spoken of or counted |
| bhava-rāgaḥ | compound noun; masculine; nominative singular (lemma: bhava-rāga) | attachment to existence, the second form of rāga |
| dvi-dhātu-jaḥ | compound adjective; masculine; nominative singular (lemma: dvi-dhātu-ja) | arising in two domains; qualifies *bhavarāgaḥ* |

| tat-mokṣa-saṃjñā-vyāvṛttaye | compound noun; feminine; dative singular (lemma: tat-mokṣa-saṃjñā-vyāvṛtti) | in order to exclude a conception of those domains as liberation |
| kṛtaḥ | past passive participle; masculine; nominative singular (lemma: kṛta) | designated as a distinct attachment |

Word division and sandhi: *ṣaḍrāgabhedāt* = *ṣaṭ rāga-bhedāt*;
*saptoktāḥ* = *sapta uktāḥ*; *antarmukhatvāt* = *antar-mukhatvāt*;
*tanmokṣasaṃjñāvyāvṛttaye* begins with *tat* before *mokṣa*.

The Bhāṣya identifies the division presupposed by `rāga-bheda`:

```text
rāga
    → kāmarāga
    → bhavarāga
```

It also identifies the two domains of `dvidhātuja` as `rūpadhātu` and
`ārūpyadhātu`. These specifications come from the commentary rather than from
the compounds considered in isolation. The opening completes the suspended
`te punaḥ` of 5.01.

## 4. Grammar

**Completion of the preceding enumeration**

```text
ṣaṭ [anuśayāḥ]
    rāga-bhedāt
sapta uktāḥ

the six [anuśayas],
    because of the division of attachment,
are stated as seven
```

The ablative `rāga-bhedāt` gives the reason for the change in number. No new
anuśaya is simply added from outside; one member of the sixfold list is
divided.

**Definition by domain**

```text
bhava-rāgaḥ
    dvi-dhātu-jaḥ [asti]

attachment to existence
    arises in two domains
```

The compound `dvidhātuja` agrees with `bhavarāgaḥ`. The Bhāṣya resolves its
range as attachment arising in the form and formless domains.

**Cause and purpose**

```text
antar-mukhatvāt
    because of its inward-directedness

tat-mokṣa-saṃjñā-vyāvṛttaye
    for excluding the conception of that as liberation

kṛtaḥ
    [the designation bhavarāga] is established
```

`Kṛtaḥ` is masculine singular and most directly resumes `bhavarāgaḥ`: this
attachment is established as a distinct designation. The reference of `tat`
is compressed. The Bhāṣya reports an explanation according to which the term
excludes the conception that the two higher domains are liberation.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Because attachment is divided, the six are stated as seven. Attachment to
> existence arises in two domains. Because of its inward-directedness, it is
> designated in order to exclude the conception of that as liberation.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Because attachment is divided, the six anuśayas are spoken of as seven.
> Attachment to existence arises in the form and formless domains; because
> it turns inward, it is separately designated to prevent those domains
> from being conceived as liberation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Attachment does not disappear merely because its object is no longer
> sensuous. It can withdraw from outward desire and preserve itself as
> attachment to refined conditioned existence. In the form and formless
> domains this predominantly takes the form of attachment to samāpatti: the
> attainment is savored together with the mode of existence that supports it.
> Because such attachment proceeds inwardly, it can resemble release. The
> sevenfold classification exposes the retained binding relation within that
> appearance of transcendence.

The Bhāṣya therefore does not condemn samāpatti:

```text
samāpatti
    ≠ bhavarāga

attachment to and savoring of samāpatti
    = a principal form of bhavarāga
```

Nor does it make outwardness evil and inwardness liberating. The discriminant
is whether `rāga` remains operative in the relation to the attainment.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Rāgabheda` — internal division of attachment**

The Sūtra division cited by the Bhāṣya is `kāmarāga` and `bhavarāga`. The count
of anuśayas changes because the apparently single determination `rāga` has two
doctrinally distinct forms.

**`Kāmarāga` — attachment to sensual desire**

The first species of attachment. It is stated in the Bhāṣya as the contrast
through which `bhavarāga` becomes intelligible.

**`Bhavarāga` — attachment to existence**

Attachment arising in the form and formless domains. The commentary sharpens
`bhava` as `ātmabhāva`, one's constituted mode of existence. Bhavarāga is thus
not restricted to a desire for future life; it includes enjoyment of one's
present refined mode of conditioned existence.

**`Dvidhātuja` — arising in two domains**

```text
rūpadhātu
    form-realm

ārūpyadhātu
    formless-realm
```

`Dhātu` here means domain, not physical element. The two domains are grouped
because attachment within them is classified as bhavarāga rather than
kāmarāga.

**`Samāpattirāga` — attachment to samāpatti**

A Bhāṣya determination naming the predominant form of attachment among beings
of the two higher domains. It is the appropriative relation to samāpatti, not
samāpatti itself.

**`Antarmukhapravṛtta` — proceeding inwardly**

The Bhāṣya's expansion of `antarmukhatva`. Inward direction explains why this
attachment may fail to look like attachment and so may be misrecognized as
liberation.

**`Mokṣasaṃjñāvyāvṛtti` — exclusion of the conception of liberation**

The classification blocks a false identification. The phrase `ekeṣām iti`
marks the explanation concerning the two domains as a reported position,
“according to some,” rather than an unqualified authorial assertion.

**`Āsvāda` — savoring or enjoyment**

The active relation by which beings enjoy samāpatti together with its support
and thereby enjoy their own `ātmabhāva`.

**`Anuśaya`, `kleśa`, and `paryavasthāna` — disputed relation**

The Bhāṣya presents the Vaibhāṣika claim that the affliction itself is
*anuśaya*, then favors the Sautrāntika account: a dormant affliction is
called *anuśaya* and its manifest operation *paryavasthāna*. Dormancy is
the continuum's acquired capacity to produce affliction again, not a
separate hidden substance. These are positions in the commentary's dispute,
not extra words in the kārikā.

**`Saṃprayukta` and `viprayukta` — associated and dissociated**

The debate asks whether an anuśaya is associated with consciousness and
faculties or exists as a dissociated item. The favored account rejects a
separate item to which either relation would apply.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 5.02 divides one genus according to the domain and mode of its attachment:

```text
Rāga
    ├── Kāmarāga
    │       attachment through sensual desire
    └── Bhavarāga
            attachment to constituted existence
            in rūpa- and ārūpyadhātu
```

Accordingly:

```text
six initial anuśayas
    - rāga
    + kāmarāga
    + bhavarāga
    = seven stated anuśayas
```

The Bhāṣya gives two related determinations of the second species:

```text
samāpattirāga
    → inward-directed operation
        → possible appearance of liberation
            → need for explicit discrimination

savoring samāpatti with its support
    → savoring one's ātmabhāva
        → attachment to existence
```

The actual opposition is therefore relational:

```text
not:
    outwardness versus inwardness

but:
    appropriative attachment versus freedom from attachment
```

Refinement changes the object and mode of attachment without necessarily
removing its binding form.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The opening pair now supplies the first complete movement of Anuśayanirdeśa:

```text
VAK 5.01
    anuśaya is the root through which karma becomes productive of bhava

VAK 5.02
    rāga persists even where sensual desire has been surpassed
    by appropriating refined existence and samāpatti
```

This gives the Organon a precise discriminator. A change in content, domain,
or cognitive refinement is not by itself a change in the underlying relation:

```text
sensual content withdrawn
    does not entail
appropriation overcome
```

Samāpatti remains the unity of its determinate cognitive operation; it is not
introduced here as another afflictive member alongside the anuśayas. What is
classified is `samāpattirāga`, the attachment that takes the attainment and
its supporting mode of existence as what is to be enjoyed.

For the Yoga–Kośa project, this is a textual constraint on any higher
reconstruction: inwardness, abstraction, stability, or power cannot certify
liberation. The relevant question is whether the attainment is known and used
without being appropriated as the affirmation of one's conditioned existence.
That formulation is Organon interpretation; the textual basis is the
Bhāṣya's distinction between samāpatti and attachment to samāpatti.
