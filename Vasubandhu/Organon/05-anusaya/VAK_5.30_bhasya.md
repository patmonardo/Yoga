# VAK 5.30 Bhāṣya — Form and Formless Supports in the Matrix

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> svakādharatrayordhvaikāmalānāṃ rūpadhātujāḥ |
> ārūpyajāstridhātvāptatrayānāsravagocarāḥ || 5.30 ||

> The three Form Realm Dharma classes lie in the cognitive range of three
> corresponding Form Realm Consciousness classes, three lower Desire Realm
> classes, one higher Formless Realm class to be abandoned through cultivation,
> and undefiled Consciousness. The corresponding three Formless Realm Dharma
> classes lie in the range of three Consciousness classes in each of the three
> domains and Consciousness without influx.

## 3. Continuous Translation

“The Form Realm Dharmas [of the same three classes] lie in
the cognitive range of their own three, the lower three, one
higher, and the undefiled.” The same three kinds of Form
Realm Dharmas are Supports of eight Consciousness classes:
the corresponding three of their own domain, the corresponding
three of the lower domain, one of the higher domain to be
abandoned through cultivation alone, and one without influx.

“The Formless Realm Dharmas lie in the range of the three
corresponding classes belonging to the three domains and of
Consciousness without influx.” The same three kinds of
Formless Realm Dharmas are Supports of ten Consciousness
classes: the corresponding three kinds across the three
domains, and one without influx. Thus the Dharmas of all
three domains to be abandoned by seeing suffering, seeing
origination, and cultivation have been stated.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The three abandonment classes established in 5.29 remain
fixed while the domain of the Dharma changes. For Form Realm
Dharmas, the Bhāṣya counts **3 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 8** admissible
Consciousness classes: three corresponding Form Realm
classes, three Desire Realm classes below, one Formless Realm
class above to be abandoned through cultivation, and one
without influx. The higher-domain member is only one; this
row does not contain three higher-domain classes.

For Formless Realm Dharmas, the count is **3 + 3 + 3 + 1 = 10**:
the corresponding three Consciousness classes in each of the
Desire, Form, and Formless Domains, and one without influx.
The closing sentence says the three classes in all three
domains have been covered. The remaining two abandonment
classes begin in 5.31.

These are Support (`ālambana`) relations. The counts do not
by themselves say that every admissible Consciousness is
afflicted or that a specified number of anuśayas lie latent
in each Dharma. The matrix provides the relation needed for
that further inference.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** The prose's `svadhātuka`,
`adharadhātuka`, and `ūrdhvadhātuka` determine the own,
lower, and higher parts of the first verse half despite its
`svadā-` reading. `Traidhātukānāṃ triprakārāṇām` distributes
the same three classes across all three domains in the
second half. The verse distinguishes `amala` (undefiled)
from `anāsrava` (without influx); the prose calls the last
Consciousness class `anāsrava` in both counts. `Gocara` marks
cognitive range in the verse, while `ālambana` names Support
in the Bhāṣya.

**Organon reconstruction.** The passage keeps a Dharma's
domain, a Consciousness's domain, and its abandonment class
as separate coordinates. One can establish the admissible
Support relation before assessing anuśaya adherence. This
is a project-level use of the Kośa matrix; Consciousness
(`citta`) is not thereby identified with Basis (`āśraya`).
