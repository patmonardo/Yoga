# VAK_5.55

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सुखाभ्यां संप्रयुक्तो हि रागो द्वेषो विपर्ययात् ।
मोहः सर्वैरसद्दृष्टिर्मनोदुःखसुखेन तु ॥ ५.५५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sukhābhyāṃ saṃprayukto hi rāgo dveṣo viparyayāt |
mohaḥ sarvair asaddṛṣṭir mano-duḥkha-sukhena tu || 5.55 ||
```

VAK 5.54 distributed afflictive factors by cognitive support. VAK 5.55 now
asks with which of the five feeling-faculties each kleśa or upakleśa can be
conjoined.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sukha-ābhyām | saṃprayuktaḥ | hi | rāgaḥ | dveṣaḥ | viparyayāt |
mohaḥ | sarvaiḥ | asat-dṛṣṭiḥ | manas-duḥkha-sukhena | tu
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `sukhābhyām` | instrumental dual neuter | with the two pleasant feeling-faculties: bodily pleasure and mental gladness |
| `saṃprayuktaḥ` | nominative singular masculine, past passive participle of `sam-pra-√yuj` | conjoined or associated within one cognitive event |
| `hi` | explanatory particle | indeed; for |
| `rāgaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | attachment, appropriative attraction |
| `dveṣaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | hostility, aversive rejection |
| `viparyayāt` | ablative singular | conversely; by reversal |
| `mohaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | delusion or obscuration |
| `sarvaiḥ` | instrumental plural | with all five feeling-faculties |
| `asaddṛṣṭiḥ` | nominative singular feminine compound | false view; identified by the Bhāṣya as `mithyādṛṣṭi` |
| `mano-duḥkha-sukhena` | instrumental singular neuter collective compound | with mental pain and mental pleasure, namely distress and gladness |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but, however |

The verse does not call feeling an affliction. Its instrumental forms mark the
feeling with which an independently determined affliction is compatible.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda supplies the governing predicate:

```text
rāgaḥ
    sukhābhyām saṃprayuktaḥ hi
```

`Sukhābhyām` is a dual whose members the Bhāṣya specifies as `sukha` and
`saumanasya`. In the second construction the predicate and an instrumental
dual are understood by ellipsis:

```text
dveṣaḥ
    [duḥkhābhyām saṃprayuktaḥ]
    viparyayāt
```

Here `viparyayāt` reverses the pleasant pair into bodily pain and mental
distress. The third construction extends conjunction universally:

```text
mohaḥ
    sarvaiḥ [saṃprayuktaḥ]
```

The final construction restricts false view to mental feeling:

```text
asaddṛṣṭiḥ tu
    mano-duḥkha-sukhena [saṃprayuktā]
```

The singular collective compound embraces the two mental members,
`daurmanasya` and `saumanasya`. The Bhāṣya fixes `asaddṛṣṭi` here as
`mithyādṛṣṭi`, not defective view in general.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Attachment is indeed conjoined with the two pleasant [feelings]; hostility,
> by reversal, [with the two painful feelings]. Delusion is conjoined with
> all; false view, however, with mental pain and pleasure.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Attachment is associated with the two pleasant feeling-faculties—bodily
> pleasure and mental gladness—whereas hostility is associated with their
> reverse, bodily pain and mental distress. Delusion is associated with all
> five feeling-faculties. False view (`mithyādṛṣṭi`), however, is associated
> with mental distress and mental gladness.

The Bhāṣya relates the distress and gladness accompanying false view,
respectively, to meritorious and evil actions. That explanation determines
the relevant feeling-pair without making the feeling itself false view.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Every afflicted occurrence has more than one determination. Feeling gives
> the event its agreeable, disagreeable, or neutral tone; the kleśa gives it
> an appropriative, aversive, or obscured orientation. Attachment can inhabit
> bodily pleasure or mental gladness, hostility their painful opposites, and
> delusion every affective register. False view remains a reflective mental
> determination and can therefore be either distressed or pleased. Affective
> tone and cognitive-afflictive orientation are conjoined but irreducible.

The verse thus establishes a compatibility matrix. It does not claim that a
pleasant occurrence is wholesome, that pain is hostility, or that feeling
generates the affliction with which it co-arises.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Saṃprayoga` — conjunction or association**

Synchronic co-occurrence of factors within one cognitive event. It differs
from temporal production, genetic derivation, and mere external proximity.

**The five feeling-faculties**

```text
sukha          bodily pleasure
duḥkha         bodily pain
saumanasya     mental gladness
daurmanasya    mental distress
upekṣā         equanimous or neutral feeling
```

This verse begins their distribution among the afflictions; the following
kārikā continues and qualifies the matrix.

**`Sukhābhyām` — the two pleasant faculties**

The Bhāṣya resolves the dual as bodily pleasure and mental gladness.
Attachment is compatible with both because rāga can occur in sensory as well
as mental consciousness.

**`Viparyayāt` — by reversal**

The inverse of the pleasant pair is bodily pain plus mental distress. This
pair is assigned to hostility, whose occurrence has the mode of dejection
(`dainya`) rather than gladness (`harṣa`).

**`Moha` / `avidyā` — delusion or ignorance**

Compatible with all five feelings. The Bhāṣya grounds this range in avidyā's
capacity for association with every kleśa (`sarvakleśasaṃprayogitva`), not in
a free phenomenological generalization.

**`Asaddṛṣṭi` — false view**

Explicitly identified by the Bhāṣya with `mithyādṛṣṭi`. As a mental view it
can be conjoined with the two mental polarities, distress and gladness, but
not with bodily pleasure or bodily pain.

**`Harṣa` and `dainya` — gladness and dejection**

Bhāṣya vocabulary explaining the contrary affective modes in which attachment
and hostility operate.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse specifies permitted conjunctions:

```text
CompatibleFeeling(Rāga)
    = {Sukha, Saumanasya}

CompatibleFeeling(Dveṣa)
    = {Duḥkha, Daurmanasya}

CompatibleFeeling(Moha)
    = {Sukha, Duḥkha, Saumanasya, Daurmanasya, Upekṣā}

CompatibleFeeling(Mithyādṛṣṭi)
    = {Daurmanasya, Saumanasya}
```

The relation has strict logical limits:

```text
ConjoinedWith(x, feeling)
    ≠ IdenticalWith(x, feeling)
    ≠ ProducedBy(x, feeling)

ConjoinedWith(x, PleasantFeeling)
    ⇏ Wholesome(x)
```

VAK 5.54 and 5.55 therefore supply independent coordinates:

```text
AfflictiveOccurrence
    hasCognitiveSupport ConsciousnessGroup
    hasConjoinedFeeling FeelingFaculty
```

The first relation answers where the factor can occur; the second answers the
affective tone with which it can occur. Neither relation can replace the
other.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya frames the verse as an explicit question
about the five feeling-faculties. It identifies both dual pairs, grounds
moha's universal range, and fixes the apparently broader `asaddṛṣṭi` as
`mithyādṛṣṭi`.

**Feeling is not affliction.** Pleasure registers agreeability; attachment
appropriates the agreeable. Pain registers disagreeability; hostility rejects
the disagreeable. Their regular conjunction does not erase their distinct
functions in the event.

**Universal obscurability.** Because moha is compatible with every feeling,
no affective polarity by itself establishes clarity. Pleasure, pain, gladness,
distress, and neutrality can each occur under obscuration.

**False view and mental polarity.** False view is not affectively uniform. It
can take satisfaction in a false position or be distressed in its negation.
The invariant is the erroneous view, not one fixed hedonic tone.

**Organon interpretation.** A semantic Dataset should represent feeling and
affliction as distinct Features joined through a provenance-bearing event:

```text
CognitiveEvent
    hasFeeling FeelingFaculty
    hasAfflictiveOrientation Klesa
    hasConsciousnessSupport ConsciousnessGroup
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This is a later modeling contact point, not a claim that the source teaches
the project's architecture. The doctrinal content remains the compatibility
relations established by the kārikā and Bhāṣya.
