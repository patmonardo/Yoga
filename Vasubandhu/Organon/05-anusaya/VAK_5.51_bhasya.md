# VAK 5.51 Bhāṣya — Sources of Two Stains and Two Modes of Abandonment

## Kārikā Anchor

> dṛṣṭyāmarśāt pradāśas tu śāṭhyaṃ dṛṣṭi-samutthitam |
> tatrāhrīkyānapatrāpyastyānamiddhoddhavā dvidhā || 5.51 ||

> Obstinate fault-clinging arises from grasping at view,
> and crookedness from view. Among the enveloping
> afflictions, shamelessness, lack of moral concern, torpor,
> sleepiness, and excitation have two modes of abandonment.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Obstinate fault-clinging arises from grasping at view,
while crookedness arises from view. The latter is fitting
in light of a stanza asking, “What is crooked? An evil
view.” Crookedness is a derivative of view.

Which of these factors must be abandoned in which way?
Among the ten enveloping afflictions already stated,
shamelessness, lack of moral concern, torpor, sleepiness,
and excitation are twofold. These five are abandoned
through seeing or through cultivation because they can be
conjoined with kleśas of both kinds. A factor conjoined
with a kleśa abandoned by a particular seeing is itself
abandoned by that corresponding seeing.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The first half completes the six stains' source relations
left open in 5.50. *Pradāśa* derives from `dṛṣṭyāmarśa`,
grasping at view. *Śāṭhya* arises from view; the prose calls
it a `dṛṣṭiniḥṣyanda`, a derivative of view. The brief
stanza quotation supports that relation but does not define
every instance of crookedness as a formulated view.

The question `ka eṣāṃ kiṃprahātavyaḥ` shifts to a new axis:
**mode of abandonment**. The five named *paryavasthānas*
are `dvidhā` because of their conjunction
(`saṃprayoga`) with kleśas to be abandoned either through
seeing or through cultivation. The factor's name alone
does not settle its Path classification. The prose states
the seeing side exactly: the factor follows the specific
seeing through which its conjoined kleśa is abandoned.
The analogous cultivation side is supplied by the twofold
explanation, while the next verse treats the remaining
factors.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Dṛṣṭyāmarśa` is kept as
“grasping at view,” distinct from bare `dṛṣṭi`, the source
assigned to crookedness. `Dvidhā` is resolved by the
Bhāṣya as seeing-abandoned and cultivation-abandoned,
not two different intrinsic substances. The source's
`āhlīkya` is left visible in Sanskrit; “shamelessness”
follows the established technical sense. `Uddhava` is
interpreted as excitation through the prose's earlier
`auddhatya` explanation.

**Organon reconstruction.** Origin, conjunction, and mode
of abandonment are separate determinations. For these five
factor kinds, Path status depends on the kleśa with which
a concrete occurrence is conjoined. This relational reading
serves the Science of Citta, but the source doctrine remains
the specified `saṃprayoga` rule rather than a claim about
an implemented data model.
