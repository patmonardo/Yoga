# VAK_5.08

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ईश्वरादिषु नित्यात्मविपर्यासात् प्रवर्तते ।
कारणाभिनिवेशोऽतो दुःखदृग्घेय एव सः ॥ ५.८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
īśvarādiṣu nityātma-viparyāsāt pravartate |
kāraṇābhiniveśo 'to duḥkha-dṛg-gheya eva saḥ || 5.8 ||
```

VAK 5.07 defined `śīlavrataparāmarśa` as taking a non-cause for a cause
or a non-path for a Path. VAK 5.08 explains why one causal form of this view
is abandoned through seeing suffering rather than seeing origination.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
īśvara-ādiṣu | nitya-ātma-viparyāsāt | pravartate |
kāraṇa-abhiniveśaḥ | ataḥ | duḥkha-dṛk-gheyaḥ | eva | saḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `īśvara-ādiṣu` | locative plural masculine | with respect to Īśvara and similar supposed causes |
| `nitya-ātma-viparyāsāt` | ablative singular compound | from inversion concerning permanence and self |
| `pravartate` | third-person singular present middle | proceeds, arises, becomes operative |
| `kāraṇa-abhiniveśaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | fixation upon something as cause |
| `ataḥ` | ablative adverb | therefore; from this ground |
| `duḥkha-dṛk-gheyaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | to be abandoned through seeing suffering |
| `eva` | restrictive particle | precisely, only |
| `saḥ` | nominative singular masculine pronoun | that fixation |

`Abhiniveśa` is not neutral causal inquiry. It is settled adherence or
entrenched fixation. `Viparyāsa` names a positive inversion rather than a mere
absence of information.

## 4. Grammar

**Genesis of causal fixation**

```text
īśvara-ādiṣu
    nitya-ātma-viparyāsāt
kāraṇa-abhiniveśaḥ pravartate

with respect to Īśvara and similar cases,
    from inversion concerning permanence and self,
fixation upon a cause proceeds
```

The locative gives the field in which the fixation operates; the ablative
gives its generative ground. The grammatical subject is
`kāraṇābhiniveśaḥ`.

**Inference to abandonment class**

```text
ataḥ saḥ
    duḥkha-dṛk-gheyaḥ eva

therefore that [fixation]
    is abandoned precisely through seeing suffering
```

`Ataḥ` connects the removal-mode to the reconstructed genesis. The Bhāṣya
explains that seeing suffering abandons the permanence-grasping and
self-grasping from which the derivative causal fixation arose.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Fixation upon a cause such as Īśvara proceeds from inversion concerning
> permanence and self. Therefore that fixation is to be abandoned precisely
> through seeing suffering.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> One fixes upon Īśvara, Prajāpati, or another supposed first cause after
> construing it as permanent, unitary, self, and agent. Because this causal
> fixation is generated by grasping permanence and self—and those graspings
> are abandoned through seeing suffering—the derivative fixation upon such a
> cause is abandoned through that same seeing.

The Bhāṣya extends the classification to austerities and ritual practices
mistaken for causes of purification or superior rebirth. Its general formula
is: one apprehends what is not a cause as a cause.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A false causal doctrine is not adequately diagnosed from its surface claim
> about origination. Its genesis must be recovered. Conditioned existence is
> first inverted into something permanent and self-identical; this supposed
> self is then projected as one agent standing behind the process; finally,
> that agent is fixed as its cause. Seeing suffering removes the underlying
> permanence/self construction and thereby deprives the derivative causal
> posit of its ground. Correction proceeds genetically: it transforms the
> prior determination that makes the later error appear necessary.

The sequence is:

```text
conditioned field
    → construed as permanent self
        → construed as one agent
            → fixed as external first cause
```

The target is not causal intelligibility as such. It is the hypostatization of
Reason as a permanent, unitary agent externally related to its Product.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Īśvarādi` — Īśvara and analogous supposed causes**

The pluralizing `ādi` makes Īśvara an instance of a class. The Bhāṣya also
names Prajāpati and any other principle grasped as permanent, one, self, and
agent-cause.

**`Nityātmaviparyāsa` — inversion concerning permanence and self**

The Bhāṣya unfolds the compound through `nitya`, `eka`, `ātman`, and `kartṛ`.
The explanatory cause is posited as permanent, unitary, self-subsistent, and
an agent. This prior construction generates the causal fixation.

**`Kāraṇābhiniveśa` — fixation upon a cause**

Not the inquiry into causes practiced throughout Abhidharma. It is entrenched
commitment to a falsely constituted causal principle.

**`Nityagrāha` and `ātmagrāha`**

Grasping permanence and grasping self. Since seeing suffering abandons these
generative misdeterminations, the causal fixation produced by them is assigned
to the same seeing-abandonment class.

**`Vipratipanna` — mistaken with respect to**

The commentary also says that this view is mistaken with respect to suffering
and generalizes the point to views having a sāsrava cognitive support. This
shows that classification depends upon underlying support and misconstrual,
not only the surface predicate “cause.”

**`Parīkṣya` — to be examined**

Vasubandhu closes the broader discussion with `tasmāt parīkṣya eṣo 'rthaḥ`,
“therefore this matter must be examined.” This is an explicit reservation
about whether the inherited truth-specific classifications have been rendered
fully coherent.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a genetic dependency rather than a flat error label:

```text
NityaGraha(x)
AND AtmaGraha(x)
AND AgentProjection(x)
    → KaranaAbhinivesa(x)
```

The correction operates at the generative ground:

```text
DuhkhaDarsana
    → abandons NityaGraha
    → abandons AtmaGraha
    → removes the ground of KaranaAbhinivesa
```

This refines the classification rule from VAK 5.05:

```text
surface topic of a view
    does not by itself determine
its proper truth-specific correction

genetic ground + cognitive support
    determine
the relevant seeing-abandonment class
```

The Bhāṣya then tests its own rule. If all sāsrava-supported views are mistaken
regarding suffering, what distinguishes Path-abandonable adherence? If denial
of the Path does not itself promise purification, should classification follow
the alternative Path adopted instead? Analogous problems arise for origination
and cessation.

The conclusion is deliberately non-dogmatic:

```text
tasmāt parīkṣya eṣo 'rthaḥ
    therefore this matter must be examined
```

The inherited matrix is retained, but its rationale remains subject to
critical examination.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.08 sharply distinguishes the Absolute Concept from an external first
agent. The error criticized here is not that Reason has a Product. It is that
Reason and Product are separated, after which Reason is represented as a
permanent personal maker standing outside the conditioned field.

```text
false representation:
    permanent self-agent
        externally produces
    an otherwise separate world

Absolute-logical movement:
    Reason self-determines
        and has its truth in
    its Product
```

This distinction belongs to the Organon reconstruction, not to Vasubandhu's
explicit vocabulary. Yet it lets the Kośa polemic illuminate rather than
simply oppose our telos. The Absolute is not rescued by reasserting an
eternal agent; it is comprehended by refusing the externalization that makes
cause and consequence independent things.

The verse also reveals a necessary diagnostic principle for implementation:

```text
do not correct only the visible conclusion

trace the conclusion to
    its supporting determination,
    the prior inversion that generates it,
    and the transformation that removes that ground
```

Most strikingly, the Bhāṣya applies this critical principle to its own
classification. `Parīkṣya` means that the mapping from error to corrective
truth cannot be treated as an unquestionable lookup table. The System must be
able to examine whether its own classifications preserve the genesis and
actual correction-condition of the determination.

That self-examination is a genuine emergence of the Middle: inherited doctrine
becomes a Product presented before Reason, tested for adequacy, and retained
only through its rational mediation.
