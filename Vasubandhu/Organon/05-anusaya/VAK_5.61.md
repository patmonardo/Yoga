# VAK_5.61

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
प्रहाणाधारभूतत्त्वदूषणाख्यश् चतुर्विधः ।
प्रतिपक्षः प्रहातव्यः क्लेश आलम्बनान् मतः ॥ ५.६१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
prahāṇādhārabhūtatva-dūṣaṇākhyaś caturvidhaḥ |
pratipakṣaḥ prahātavyaḥ kleśa ālambanān mataḥ || 5.61 ||
```

VAK 5.61 differentiates the counteragent introduced in 5.60 and then asks in
what respect an affliction is abandoned.

**Textual caution.** The received compound is compressed and corrupt or
uncertain. The working Bhāṣya e-text has the still less transparent
`prahāṇādhārabhūtatvadūṣaṇāruyaḥ`, but its prose explicitly enumerates
`prahāṇa`, `ādhāra`, `dūrībhāva`, and `vidūṣaṇā`. The kārikā witness above is
preserved verbatim; the four-member interpretation is Bhāṣya-controlled.

## 4. Grammar

The first sentence is a nominal classification:

```text
pratipakṣaḥ
    caturvidhaḥ
    [four designations]-ākhyaḥ
```

The Bhāṣya, not the damaged compound alone, establishes the four referents.

The second sentence is likewise nominal:

```text
kleśaḥ
    prahātavyaḥ mataḥ
    ālambanāt
```

The gerundive agrees with `kleśaḥ`; `mataḥ` marks the position as “held” or
“considered.” The ablative initially identifies separation from the object-
support: the affliction no longer arises taking that object. The Bhāṣya then
tests and qualifies this answer, especially for past affliction.

The received enumeration order differs from the operational Path-order later
recommended by Vasubandhu. Grammatical listing must therefore not be mistaken
for temporal sequence.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The counteragent is held to be fourfold, called abandonment, support,
> distancing, and discrediting. An affliction is considered to be abandoned
> with respect to its object-support.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Counteraction has four functions: the uninterrupted path directly abandons
> the affliction; the immediately following noble path supports and maintains
> the abandonment obtained; a subsequent path distances the already severed
> acquisition; and a preparatory path discredits a domain by seeing it as
> defective. An affliction is initially said to be abandoned with respect to
> its object-support because it no longer arises toward that support, but for
> an affliction in one's own continuum the decisive criterion is severance of
> its acquisition.

Other teachers include the path of liberation itself among the distancing
counteragents. This alternative remains attributed rather than absorbed into
Vasubandhu's primary allocation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Opposition is not one homogeneous negative force. Before severance, a field
> loses its compelling appearance; severance then occurs, its result is
> maintained, and the abolished acquisition is placed progressively farther
> away. These functions converge upon abandonment without becoming synonyms.
> Nor is an affliction exhausted merely by detaching it from associated mental
> factors or by knowing an object: abandonment must be determined through the
> precise object-, association-, and acquisition-relations of the continuum.

The Path is thus an articulated transformation whose preparatory, decisive,
stabilizing, and distancing operations must be kept distinct.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Prahāṇa-pratipakṣa` — counteragent of abandonment**

The `ānantarya-mārga`, uninterrupted path, insofar as it performs the actual
severance of the affliction.

**`Ādhāra-pratipakṣa` — counteragent of support or maintenance**

The noble path immediately following the uninterrupted path—identified in
the operational ordering as `vimukti-mārga`—which maintains the abandonment
already obtained. `Ādhāra` here is not a support feeding the affliction.

**`Dūrībhāva-pratipakṣa` — counteragent of distancing**

The path subsequent to liberation, operationally the `viśeṣa-mārga`, which
places the already severed acquisition farther away. Other teachers also
include the liberation path because it already performs distancing.

**`Vidūṣaṇā-pratipakṣa` — counteragent of discrediting**

The Path-operation that regards a domain as defective and undermines its
attractive standing. Vasubandhu places this first operationally as the
`prayoga-mārga` when suffering and origin are its objects.

**Received order and operational order**

```text
received taxonomy:  prahāṇa → ādhāra → dūrībhāva → vidūṣaṇā
Path operation:      vidūṣaṇā → prahāṇa → ādhāra → dūrībhāva
                     prayoga    ānantarya  vimukti   viśeṣa
```

The first answers which kinds there are; the second answers how their
functions are ordered in transformation.

**`Saṃprayoga` — association**

An affliction cannot simply be detached from the mental factors conjoined
with it as though it persisted separately. Hence abandonment is not explained
as separation from its associated factors.

**`Ālambana` — object-support**

The initial standpoint from which abandonment is described: the affliction no
longer arises taking that object-support. This explanation is intelligible for
future affliction but does not settle the past case universally.

**`Prāpti-cheda` — severance of acquisition**

The decisive criterion for saying that an affliction belonging to one's own
continuum has been abandoned. For afflictions in another continuum and for
material or undefiled dharmas, the relevant abandonment concerns one's own
afflictions that take them as objects.

## 8. Logical Determination

The general class differentiates by Path-function:

```text
Pratipakṣa
    = {Discrediting, Abandoning, Maintaining, Distancing}
```

In operational order:

```text
SeesDomainAsDefective(PrayogaPath)
    → Discredits(Domain)

Arises(ĀnantaryaPath)
    → Severs(Affliction)

Follows(VimuktiPath, ĀnantaryaPath)
    → Maintains(ObtainedAbandonment)

Arises(ViśeṣaPath)
    → Distances(SeveredAcquisition)
```

The classifications are non-identical:

```text
TaxonomicOrder(fourCounteragents)
    ≠ OperationalOrder(fourCounteragents)
```

The second half distinguishes three relations:

```text
TakesAsObject(kleśa, ālambana)
ConjoinedWith(kleśa, associatedFactor)
PossessedThrough(continuum, prāptiOfKleśa)
```

For one's own continuum:

```text
Severed(PrāptiOf(kleśa, ownContinuum))
    → Abandoned(kleśa, ownContinuum)
```

Therefore neither of these is universally sufficient:

```text
CompletelyKnown(ObjectSupport(kleśa))
    ⇏ Abandoned(kleśa)

SeparatedFromAssociatedFactors(kleśa)
    [not the available mode of abandonment]
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya-controlled recovery.** The fourfoldness is certain from
`caturvidhaḥ`; the names and their senses are secure from the prose, not from
an unsupported division of the corrupt transmitted compound.

**Four operations, one telos.** Discrediting, severing, maintaining, and
distancing all oppose affliction, but each occupies a different position in
the Path's work. Their unity as counteragents does not erase their functional
difference.

**Attributed disagreement.** Vasubandhu locates distancing after liberation;
`apare` allow the liberation path itself to count because distancing has
already begun. The edition preserves the disagreement rather than selecting a
synthetic formulation.

**The object answer is provisional.** The statement that affliction is
abandoned from its object-support generates a temporal difficulty for past
affliction. Vasubandhu also rejects complete knowledge of the object as an
exceptionless replacement and turns to severance of acquisition in one's own
continuum.

**Transition.** The distancing counteragent and the problem of relational
separation prepare VAK 5.62's fourfold analysis of distance.

**Organon interpretation.** A source-preserving model needs role-typed
counteraction and attributed ordering:

```text
CounteragentOperation
    hasPathRole
    occursInOperationalOrder
    belongsToReceivedTaxonomy
    actsOnAfflictiveRelation
    assertedBy SourceVoice
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This is a modeling consequence of the distinctions in the text, not an
Organon vocabulary attributed to Vasubandhu.
