# VAK 5.44 Bhāṣya — Three Obstacles to Going toward Liberation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> agantukāmatā mārgavibhramo mārgasaṃśayaḥ |
> ity antarāyā mokṣasya gamane 'tas trideśanā || 5.44 ||

> Unwillingness to go, deviation by taking another path, and doubt about the
> path obstruct going toward liberation; therefore three fetters are taught.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Others say: “Unwillingness to go, deviation from the route,
and doubt about the route are obstacles to going toward
liberation; therefore the teaching names three.”

There are three obstacles in traveling to another region:
unwillingness to go, deviation through taking another route,
and doubt about the route. The same three obstruct going
toward liberation. Through personality view, one becomes
frightened by liberation and unwilling to go. Through
grasping at discipline and observance, one takes another
path and deviates from liberation. Through doubt, one doubts
the Path. To make evident the abandonment of these
obstacles, the Blessed One taught the abandonment of these
three afflictions in particular.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

This is an **attributed alternative** to 5.43's account of
the three through entrances and roots. The prose first
sets out a travel analogy, then maps its three interruptions
onto the three lower fetters singled out in scripture:

| Named fetter | Obstacle to liberation |
|---|---|
| Personality view | Fear of liberation and unwillingness to go |
| Grasping at discipline and observance | Resort to another path and deviation |
| Doubt | Uncertainty about the Path |

The analogy distinguishes refusal to set out, proceeding by
another route, and uncertainty about the route. The second
is specifically `anyamārga-saṃśrayaṇa`, adoption of another
path; the text does not specify which practice constitutes
that path. Nor does it further explain why liberation is
frightening to one with personality view. The final
sentence gives the purpose of the threefold teaching:
making evident the abandonment of these three obstacle
types, rather than supplying a new list of all afflictions
abandoned at stream entry.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Agantukāmatā` is lack of desire
to go, rendered “unwillingness to go.” `Mārga-vibhrama`
is deviation in the route relation, explained by choosing
another route. The prose's `mokṣavibhrama` is retained in
sense as deviation from liberation. `Trideśanā` concerns
why three afflictions are taught, and the opening attribution
must remain attached to the entire explanation.

**Organon reconstruction.** The analogy locates three
different failures within one directed movement: refusing
its end, taking a route away from it, and remaining uncertain
about the route. This can inform a project-level account of
practical orientation, but it remains our reconstruction
of a reported explanation, separate from Vasubandhu's
unattributed doctrinal claims.
