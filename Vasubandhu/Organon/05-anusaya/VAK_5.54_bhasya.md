# VAK 5.54 Bhāṣya — Afflictions by Consciousness Support

## Kārikā Anchor

> samānasiddhā dṛg-gheyā manovijñāna-bhūmikāḥ |
> upakleśāḥ svatantrāś ca ṣaḍ-vijñānāśrayāḥ pare || 5.54 ||

> Those abandoned through seeing, with an additional pair
> whose names are uncertain in this witness, belong to the
> mental-consciousness level. Independent upakleśas do too;
> the others have the six consciousnesses as their range.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Of these anuśayas, how many belong to the mental level, and
how many can belong to the six consciousness groups? In
brief, all those abandoned through seeing are at the level
of mental consciousness. The prose adds two
cultivation-abandoned cases under an uncertain transmitted
expression; both are wholly mental. Independent upakleśas
are likewise understood as mental-level factors even
though they are abandoned through cultivation.

The remaining kleśas and upakleśas can occur with the six
consciousness groups. Which ones? Craving, hostility, and
ignorance in their cultivation-abandoned forms, together
with the upakleśas conjoined with them: shamelessness, lack
of moral concern, torpor, excitation, and the factors
already taught among the *kleśa-mahābhūmikas*.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The text now asks about **cognitive support**, after
separately classifying factors by source, abandonment,
ethical value, and domain. It places all seeing-abandoned
afflictions at the mental-consciousness level. The prose
also states that two further cultivation-abandoned cases
are wholly mental, but the available reading does not
securely name them. That uncertainty is lexical; their
number and assigned support are clear in the passage.

Independent upakleśas are mental-level factors despite
their cultivation-abandoned status. Thus cultivation-
abandonment alone does not imply six-consciousness range.
The remainder includes the specified cultivation-abandoned
craving, hostility, and ignorance and their associated
upakleśas. `Ṣaḍvijñāna` covers the five sensory
consciousness groups and mental consciousness; it should
not be conflated with a sixfold set of objects or with the
three cosmological domains.

The prose names both `saṃprayoga` and `āśraya` in this
unit. Conjunction with a kleśa explains which upakleśas
accompany the six-consciousness remainder; the range of
consciousness support is a different relation. Neither is
the same as the genetic `niḥṣyanda` relation of 5.48–5.51.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Manovijñānabhūmika` is
“belonging to the mental-consciousness level,” while
`ṣaḍvijñānāśraya` gives a range over six consciousness
groups. The malformed or opaque `samānasiddhā` is not
translated as though its elements were securely parsed;
the prose supports only the additional pair and its Path
status. “Independent” retains the technical `svatantra`
classification established in 5.52.

**Organon reconstruction.** A factor's cognitive support,
its conjoined companions, its source, and its Path status
need separate predicates. The uncertain pair shows that a
study can preserve known count and support while leaving
names unresolved. This is a project-level discipline for
the Science of Citta, not a claim that the Kośa specifies
a modern data model.
