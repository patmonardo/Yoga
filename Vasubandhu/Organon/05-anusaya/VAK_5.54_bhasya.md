# VAK 5.54 Bhāṣya — Afflictions by Cognitive Basis

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> samānasiddhā dṛg-gheyā manovijñāna-bhūmikāḥ |
> upakleśāḥ svatantrāś ca ṣaḍ-vijñānāśrayāḥ pare || 5.54 ||

> Those abandoned through seeing, together with two cultivation-abandoned
> factors whose names are uncertain in this witness, belong to the plane of
> mental Cognition. Independent secondary afflictions do likewise; the
> remaining afflictions and secondary afflictions have the six Cognitions
> as their Basis.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Of these anuśayas, how many belong to the mental level, and
how many to the six Cognition groups? In brief, all those
abandoned through seeing are at the level of mental Cognition.
The prose adds two
cultivation-abandoned cases under an uncertain transmitted
expression; both are wholly mental. Independent upakleśas
are likewise understood as mental-level factors even
though they are abandoned through cultivation.

The remaining kleśas and upakleśas have the six Cognitions
as their Basis and belong to the six Cognition planes. Which
ones? Attachment, hostility, and
ignorance in their cultivation-abandoned forms, together
with the upakleśas conjoined with them: shamelessness, lack
of moral concern, torpor, excitation, and the factors
already taught among the *kleśa-mahābhūmikas*.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The text now asks about **cognitive Basis**, after
separately classifying factors by source, abandonment,
ethical value, and domain. It places all seeing-abandoned
afflictions at the mental-cognition level. The prose
also states that two further cultivation-abandoned cases
are wholly mental, but the working Sanskrit does not
securely name them. The Chinese parallel supports conceit
and sleepiness; their number and assigned Basis are clear
from the local prose.

Independent upakleśas are mental-level factors despite
their cultivation-abandoned status. Thus cultivation-
abandonment alone does not imply six-cognition range.
The remainder includes the specified cultivation-abandoned
attachment, hostility, and ignorance and their associated
upakleśas. `Ṣaḍvijñāna` covers the five sensory
cognition groups and mental cognition; it should
not be conflated with a sixfold set of objects or with the
three cosmological domains.

The passage names both `saṃprayoga` and `āśraya`.
Conjunction with a kleśa explains which upakleśas
accompany the six-Cognition remainder; the range of
cognitive Basis is a different relation. Neither is
the same as the genetic `niḥṣyanda` relation of 5.48–5.51.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Manovijñānabhūmika` is
“belonging to the mental-Cognition level,” while
`ṣaḍvijñānāśraya` gives six Cognitions as Basis across their groups.
The malformed or opaque `samānasiddhā` is not
translated as though its parts were securely parsed;
the local prose supports only the additional pair and its Path
status. The Chinese parallel supplies a tentative identification,
which does not silently alter the Sanskrit witness. “Independent” retains the technical `svatantra`
classification established in 5.52.

**Organon reconstruction.** A factor's cognitive Basis,
its conjoined companions, its source, and its Path status
need separate predicates. The uncertain pair shows that a
study can preserve known count and Basis while leaving
names unresolved. This is a project-level discipline for
the Science of Citta, not a claim that the Kośa specifies
a modern data model.
