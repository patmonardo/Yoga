# VAK_5.32

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
द्विधा सानुशयं क्लिष्टमक्लिष्टमनुशायकैः ।
मोहाकाङ्क्षा ततो मिथ्यादृष्टिः सत्कायदृक्ततः ॥ ५.३२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dvidhā sānuśayaṃ kliṣṭam akliṣṭam anuśāyakaiḥ |
mohākāṅkṣā tato mithyādṛṣṭiḥ satkāyadṛk tataḥ || 5.32 ||
```

The verse first applies the completed object-support ordering to the predicate
`sānuśaya`, then opens a genetic account of how the ten anuśayas arise.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dvidhā | sa-anuśayam | kliṣṭam | akliṣṭam | anuśāyakaiḥ |
moha | ākāṅkṣā | tataḥ | mithyā-dṛṣṭiḥ | satkāya-dṛk | tataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dvidhā` | adverb | in two ways |
| `sa-anuśayam` | nominative or accusative neuter singular | bearing or accompanied by anuśaya |
| `kliṣṭam` | nominative neuter singular, qualifying understood `cittam` | defiled citta |
| `akliṣṭam` | nominative neuter singular, qualifying understood `cittam` | non-defiled citta |
| `anuśāyakaiḥ` | instrumental plural | through anuśayas that actually lie latent or adhere |
| `moha` | compound or sandhi form of `mohaḥ` | delusion |
| `ākāṅkṣā` | nominative singular feminine | doubt or wavering; glossed as `vicikitsā` |
| `tataḥ` | ablatival adverb | from that, thereafter |
| `mithyā-dṛṣṭiḥ` | nominative singular feminine | false view |
| `satkāya-dṛk` | nominative singular feminine, shortened from `satkāya-dṛṣṭi` | view of a real collection, self-view |

The Bhāṣya requires the cautious translation “non-defiled” for `akliṣṭa` here:
such a citta is not conjoined with affliction, yet may still stand as object of
an unabandoned anuśaya.

## 4. Grammar

**Two grounds for `sānuśaya`**

```text
kliṣṭaṃ [cittam] dvidhā sānuśayam
akliṣṭaṃ [cittam] anuśāyakaiḥ [sānuśayam]
```

The first clause predicates `sānuśaya` of defiled citta “in two ways.” The
second carries the predicate forward elliptically but restricts its ground:
non-defiled citta is so only through anuśayas actually adhering in the sense
specified by the Bhāṣya.

**The genetic sequence**

```text
mohāt ākāṅkṣā
tataḥ mithyā-dṛṣṭiḥ
tataḥ satkāya-dṛk
```

The compact sandhi `mohākāṅkṣā` is understood causally from the Bhāṣya: from
delusion, doubt. Each `tataḥ` then marks a further transition. The Bhāṣya
supplies the mediating acts omitted by the verse.

**Source caution**

The working Bhāṣya e-text transmits `avidyāyyogāt`; context strongly indicates
a form equivalent to `avidyāyogāt`, “through conjunction with ignorance.” The
official study does not silently emend the witness.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Defiled [citta] bears anuśaya in two ways; non-defiled [citta bears it only]
> through anuśayas that actually adhere. From delusion comes doubt; from that,
> false view; from that, the view of a real collection.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A defiled citta is called `sānuśaya` on two grounds: through an unabandoned
> anuśaya actually adhering by conjunction or object-relation, and through
> direct accompaniment by a conjoined anuśaya even when strict latent adherence
> is not predicated. A non-defiled citta is called `sānuśaya` only when an
> unabandoned anuśaya takes it as object and thereby actually adheres. In the
> genesis of the ten anuśayas, ignorance produces delusion concerning the
> truths; delusion gives rise to doubt, doubt is wrongly resolved as false view,
> and false view becomes fixation upon the aggregates as self.

The distinction between accompaniment and actual `anuśayana`, the role of
false ascertainment, and the explanation of self-view are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A cognition's relation to affliction cannot be read from a single undivided
> predicate. The same designation—“bearing anuśaya”—may rest upon present
> conjunction, upon an unabandoned affliction's object-relation, or upon mere
> accompaniment by a conjoined but abandoned affliction. Non-defiled cognition
> excludes conjunction, but not every possible latent object-relation. Having
> differentiated these relational grounds, the analysis turns to becoming:
> non-comprehension opens a divided field, the divided field is falsely
> resolved, and the false resolution hardens into self-identification.

The result is not a leap from ignorance to false belief. Each transition has a
distinct product that becomes the condition of the next operation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Sānuśaya` — bearing or accompanied by anuśaya**

A broader predicate than actual latent adherence. Its truth must be accompanied
by a statement of its ground.

**`Anuśayāna` — actually lying latent or adhering**

The Bhāṣya's stricter relational determination. An unabandoned anuśaya actually
adheres when it is conjoined with the citta or takes that citta as object.

**`Ananuśayāna` — not actually adhering**

An anuśaya may no longer satisfy the strict latent relation because it has been
abandoned, while a citta conjoined with it is nevertheless called `sānuśaya`
through accompaniment.

**`Sahitatva` — being accompanied by**

The Bhāṣya's ground for the second way in which defiled citta bears the
`sānuśaya` predicate.

**`Kliṣṭa` and `akliṣṭa` — defiled and non-defiled**

These distinguish conjunction-status. They do not alone state whether an
unabandoned anuśaya can take the citta as object.

**`Ākāṅkṣā` and `vicikitsā` — doubt**

The kārikā's `ākāṅkṣā` is glossed by the Bhāṣya as `vicikitsā`: divided
uncertainty after opposed alternatives have been presented.

**`Mithyāniścaya` — false ascertainment**

The mediating operation between doubt and false view. The divided field is not
merely left open; it is closed under the wrong determination.

**`Satkāyadṛṣṭi` — view of a real collection**

Self-view generated when the aggregates are excluded from their correct
determination as suffering and fixated upon as self. The Bhāṣya marks this
transition with `kila`, giving it the nuance of a received scholastic account.

## 8. Logical Determination

The first half blocks predicate collapse:

```text
Sānusaya(Citta)
    ⇏ one unique ground of attribution
```

For defiled citta:

```text
Kliṣṭa(c)
AND (
      Unabandoned(a) AND
      [ConjoinedWith(a,c) OR TakesAsObject(a,c)]
    )
    → Sānusaya(c) by Anuśayana

Kliṣṭa(c)
AND ConjoinedWith(a,c)
    → Sānusaya(c) by Accompaniment
```

For non-defiled citta:

```text
Akliṣṭa(c)
AND Unabandoned(a)
AND TakesAsObject(a,c)
    → Sānusaya(c) by Anuśayana

Akliṣṭa(c)
    → NOT ConjoinedWith(a,c)
```

The second half is a mediated genesis:

```text
Ignorance
    → non-comprehension of the truths
        → opposed alternatives become unresolved
            → doubt
                → false ascertainment
                    → false view
                        → appropriation of aggregates as self
                            → satkāyadṛṣṭi
```

Each `phala–ālambana` of the preceding operation becomes material for the next
`hetu–āśraya`, but only through a newly specified act. Doubt does not become
false view by identity; `mithyāniścaya` is the operation that produces the
transition.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The matrix is now applied to the grounds upon which
citta is called `sānuśaya`. The Bhāṣya distinguishes conjunction, object-
relation, actual latent adherence, and accompaniment. It then asks how the ten
anuśayas arise and begins their genetic ordering from ignorance.

**A predicate determined by its reason.** `Sānuśaya` is not meaningful as an
isolated label. Its meaning is the structured reason why it is predicated:

```text
same predicate
    + different relational ground
        = different determinate meaning
```

Here the completed `ālambana-vyavasthā` becomes operational. It allows one to
distinguish a non-defiled citta standing as object of affliction from a defiled
citta conjoined with affliction.

**Genesis rather than enumeration.** The sequence beginning with `moha` moves
from failure of determination to divided presentation, from division to false
judgment, and from false judgment to identity-fixation. The decisive middle
operation is the Bhāṣya's `mithyāniścaya`: uncertainty becomes systematic error
only when the opposition is falsely resolved.

**Yoga–Organon reconstruction.** This gives the Path both diagnostic and
therapeutic depth. It must distinguish the manifest state from the reason that
produced it, then intervene at the proper transition. Correcting a finished
self-view is not identical with preventing doubt from being falsely resolved;
each belongs to a different moment of the generated appearance.
