# VAK_5.17

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सर्वत्रगा अनुशयाः सकलामनुशेरते ।
स्वभूमिमालम्बनतः स्वनिकायमसर्वगाः ॥ ५.१७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sarvatragā anuśayāḥ sakalām anuśerate |
svabhūmim ālambanataḥ svanikāyam asarvagāḥ || 5.17 ||
```

The verse states the general rule for the scope of adherence by cognitive
support. VAK 5.18 will introduce its exceptions and the distinct relation of
adherence by conjunction.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sarvatragāḥ | anuśayāḥ | sakalām | anuśerate | sva-bhūmim |
ālambanataḥ | sva-nikāyam | asarvagāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `sarvatragāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | universally ranging anuśayas |
| `anuśayāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | underlying afflictive dispositions |
| `sakalām` | accusative singular feminine | the whole, entire |
| `anuśerate` | third-person plural present middle of `anu-√śī` | adhere to, lie along, persist in relation to |
| `sva-bhūmim` | accusative singular feminine | their own level or sphere |
| `ālambanataḥ` | ablative used adverbially | by way of cognitive support |
| `sva-nikāyam` | accusative singular masculine | their own abandonment-class |
| `asarvagāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | non-universal anuśayas |

The Bhāṣya fixes `nikāya` here as one of the five classes determined by mode of
abandonment, not an unspecified collection or social group.

## 4. Grammar

**Universal adherence**

```text
sarvatragāḥ anuśayāḥ
sakalāṃ sva-bhūmim
ālambanataḥ anuśerate

the universal anuśayas adhere,
by way of cognitive support,
to their whole own level
```

`Sakalām` and `svabhūmim` agree in the accusative feminine singular and form
the object of `anuśerate`. `Ālambanataḥ` specifies the mode in which adherence
is predicated.

**Non-universal restriction**

```text
asarvagāḥ [anuśayāḥ]
sva-nikāyam [ālambanataḥ anuśerate]

the non-universal [anuśayas]
adhere [by way of cognitive support] to their own class
```

The verb and adverbial qualifier are understood by ellipsis from the first
half. The contrast is therefore within one and the same object-support
relation: whole-level range versus own-class restriction.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The universal anuśayas adhere, by way of cognitive support, to their entire
> own level; the non-universal ones [adhere] to their own class.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Universal anuśayas adhere by taking their entire own `bhūmi`, across all five
> abandonment-classes, as cognitive support. Non-universal anuśayas adhere
> within their own `bhūmi` only to their own truth- or cultivation-specific
> abandonment-class, and not to another.

The fivefold extension of the level and the technical sense of `svanikāya` are
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An anuśaya's universality is the scope of its objective relation, not an
> unbounded property lodged inside it. The universal disposition can adhere
> across every abandonment-class articulated within its own level; the
> non-universal disposition remains related only to its own determinate class.
> Universality therefore traverses a differentiated whole, while particularity
> occupies one difference internal to that same whole.

The verse gives the structure:

```text
universal
    all five classes within one determinate level

non-universal
    one class within that same kind of level
```

Neither term is intelligible without both `bhūmi` and `nikāya`.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Anuśerate` — they adhere or lie along**

The Bhāṣya frames this verb through two possible relations: adherence by
object-support (`ālambanataḥ`) and adherence by conjunction
(`saṃprayogataḥ`). VAK 5.17 gives the general rule for the first. Anuśaya is
therefore not being treated merely as a concealed item resident in a
continuum.

**`Ālambanataḥ anuśayana` — adherence by cognitive support**

An affliction adheres to a field by taking it as its `ālambana`. The breadth
of this objective relation determines whether its scope is universal or
non-universal.

**`Svabhūmi` — one's own level**

The enclosing range within which the present rule operates. “Universal” does
not here abolish the level-boundary, even though VAK 5.13 established distinct
cross-domain possibilities for nine universal anuśayas.

**`Pañcaprakāra` — the five modes**

The Bhāṣya analyzes a complete own level through five abandonment-classes:

```text
seeing suffering
seeing origination
seeing cessation
seeing path
cultivation
```

**`Svanikāya` — one's own abandonment-class**

A non-universal anuśaya abandoned through seeing suffering takes the
suffering-seeing class as support; one abandoned through cultivation takes
the cultivation-abandonable class. The rule excludes other classes within the
same level.

**`Utsarga` and `apavāda` — general rule and exception**

The Bhāṣya explicitly calls VAK 5.17 the general rule and announces exceptions
in the following verse. The present scope statements must therefore not be
treated as exceptionless final definitions.

## 8. Logical Determination

The scope architecture has two levels:

```text
Bhumi
    contains five AbandonmentClasses

Nikaya
    is one determinate AbandonmentClass within Bhumi
```

The general rules are:

```text
UniversalAnusaya(x)
AND OwnLevel(x, L)
    → ObjectRange(x, EveryAbandonmentClassWithin(L))

NonUniversalAnusaya(x)
AND OwnClass(x, C)
    → ObjectRange(x, C)
```

Consequently:

```text
Universal(x)
    ⇏ UnrestrictedAcrossAllLevels(x)

NonUniversal(x)
    ⇏ WithoutObjectiveRange(x)
```

Both have a positive range; they differ in quantification across the internal
classes of a level.

The rule is expressly provisional:

```text
GeneralScopeRule(x)
    subjectTo ExceptionRulesIn(VAK_5_18)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.17 lets us distinguish level, class, and scope without reducing one to
another. A `bhūmi` is the achieved and articulated level of operation; a
`nikāya` is a determinate class within that level; `ālambana` is the objective
relation through which the anuśaya ranges across the one or remains restricted
to the other.

This matters for Path as higher Objectivity. The Path-bhūmi is not a homogeneous
height attained above the concrete differentiations of Theory. It is the
higher level precisely as an articulated whole containing the five modes of
seeing and cultivation:

```text
Theory
    differentiates the truth- and cultivation-classes

Path-bhūmi
    holds those determinations together as an achieved level

Objective scope
    determines how a disposition ranges through that articulated whole
```

The fourfold structure is now visible as a scope architecture:

```text
hetu
    the determining rule that generates the range

phala
    the concrete extension or restriction produced by that rule

āśraya
    the bhūmi as achieved basis of the differentiated field

ālambana
    the class or whole level taken in the objective relation
```

This does not identify `bhūmi` lexically with `āśraya`; it states their Organon
relation. The achieved level supplies the concrete basis within which an
object-range is possible. Likewise, the universal is not an abstraction above
the five classes. It is the rule immanent in their totality, whereas the
non-universal is a determination restricted to one class of that same field.

The verse also refines the subjective-psychological tone of Anuśaya. To say a
disposition “lies within” the Subject is insufficient. Its reality includes
where and how it adheres: through what objective support, across which level,
and within which class. Subjective inherence becomes concrete only through
this articulated objective range.

This is Organon reconstruction. Its textual ground is the kārikā's explicit
`ālambanataḥ`, the Bhāṣya's fivefold `svabhūmi`, its technical determination
of `svanikāya`, and its distinction between object-support and conjunction.
