# VAK_5.19

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ऊर्ध्वमव्याकृताः सर्वे कामे सत्कायदर्शनम् ।
अन्तग्राहः सहाभ्यां च मोहः शेषास्त्विहाशुभाः ॥ ५.१९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ūrdhvam avyākṛtāḥ sarve kāme satkāya-darśanam |
antagrāhaḥ sahābhyāṃ ca mohaḥ śeṣās tv ihāśubhāḥ || 5.19 ||
```

The verse changes the classificatory axis. It asks which of the ninety-eight
anuśayas are unwholesome and which are ethically indeterminate.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ūrdhvam | avyākṛtāḥ | sarve | kāme | satkāya-darśanam |
anta-grāhaḥ | saha | ābhyām | ca | mohaḥ | śeṣāḥ | tu | iha | aśubhāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ūrdhvam` | adverbial accusative | above; in the form and formless domains |
| `avyākṛtāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | ethically indeterminate |
| `sarve` | nominative plural masculine | all |
| `kāme` | locative singular | in the desire-domain |
| `satkāya-darśanam` | nominative singular neuter | body-view; viewing the aggregate-complex as self or mine |
| `anta-grāhaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | grasping an extreme; extreme-view |
| `saha ābhyām` | indeclinable plus instrumental dual | together or conjoined with those two |
| `mohaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | delusion, ignorance |
| `śeṣāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | the remaining anuśayas |
| `tu` | adversative particle | but |
| `iha` | adverb | here, in the desire-domain |
| `aśubhāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | unwholesome; explained as `akuśala` |

The instrumental dual in `sahābhyām` restricts `moha`: the indeterminate
ignorance is the ignorance conjoined with the two named views, not every form
of ignorance in the desire-domain.

## 4. Grammar

**Higher-domain rule**

```text
ūrdhvam sarve [anuśayāḥ] avyākṛtāḥ

above, all [anuśayas] are ethically indeterminate
```

`Ūrdhvam` is interpreted by the Bhāṣya as the form and formless domains.
`Sarve` and `avyākṛtāḥ` agree with the understood `anuśayāḥ`.

**Desire-domain exceptions**

```text
kāme
satkāya-darśanam [avyākṛtam]
anta-grāhaḥ [avyākṛtaḥ]
saha ābhyāṃ mohaḥ ca [avyākṛtaḥ]
```

The predicate `avyākṛta` is carried forward by ellipsis. The differing genders
of the three subjects account for the changing forms that would be required
if the predicate were repeated.

**Residual rule**

```text
śeṣāḥ tu iha aśubhāḥ

but the remaining ones here are unwholesome
```

`Iha` returns to `kāme`; the residual classification concerns the remaining
desire-domain anuśayas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Above, all are ethically indeterminate. In the desire-domain, body-view,
> grasping an extreme, and the delusion conjoined with those two [are
> indeterminate]; but the remaining ones here are unwholesome.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> All anuśayas of the form and formless domains are ethically indeterminate.
> In the desire-domain, body-view, extreme-view, and the ignorance conjoined
> with those two are likewise indeterminate; the remaining desire-domain
> anuśayas are unwholesome. The innate form of body-view is indeterminate,
> whereas earlier teachers classify its conceptually constructed form as
> unwholesome.

The karmic rationale, the innate/constructed distinction, and its attribution
to earlier teachers are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Affliction and ethical evil are not identical determinations. A cognition
> may remain contaminated and binding without possessing the causal structure
> of an unwholesome act. Its ethical status depends upon the domain in which it
> occurs, the factors with which it is conjoined, the way it was generated,
> the conduct with which it is compatible, and the maturation it can produce.
> The same named view can therefore receive a different ethical determination
> when its mode of genesis and practical efficacy differ.

The verse and Bhāṣya separate:

```text
false or contaminated determination
    from
other-directed harmful operation
    from
painful karmic maturation
```

These can be related without being collapsed.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Avyākṛta` — ethically indeterminate**

Neither wholesome nor unwholesome in the strict karmic classification. It does
not mean cognitively correct, unafflicted, or soteriologically complete.

**`Aśubha / akuśala` — unwholesome**

The kārikā uses `aśubha`; the Bhāṣya explicitly explains the remaining
desire-domain anuśayas as `akuśala`. Their classification concerns ethical and
karmic efficacy, not simply the presence of cognitive error.

**`Kliṣṭa` — contaminated or afflicted**

All anuśayas are afflicted, but not all are unwholesome. Higher-domain
anuśayas furnish the clearest case of `kliṣṭa` together with `avyākṛta`.

**`Duḥkhavipāka` — painful maturation**

The Bhāṣya argues that if higher-domain afflicted dharmas were unwholesome,
they would yield painful maturation. That result does not occur there because
the relevant cause of other-directed injury is absent.

**`Paravyābādha` — injury directed toward others**

A causal condition used to discriminate strict unwholesomeness. The absence
of this condition in the higher domains explains their indeterminate
afflictions.

**Compatibility with `dāna` and `śīla`**

Body-view and extreme-view may coexist with giving and ethical discipline. A
person may perform both while expecting personal happiness after death. The
false view therefore does not automatically determine the entire practical
complex as unwholesome.

**`Mokṣānukūla` — favorable to liberation**

The Bhāṣya says that even annihilationist view may be favorable to liberation
in a limited respect. This does not make the view true; it distinguishes the
truth-status of a cognition from the practical direction one of its moments
may support.

**`Sahajā` and `vikalpitā satkāyadṛṣṭi`**

Innate body-view, found even in animals, is indeterminate. Earlier teachers
classify the constructed or conceptually elaborated form as unwholesome. The
same doctrinal species is thus differentiated by genesis.

## 8. Logical Determination

At least three status dimensions must remain independent:

```text
ContaminationStatus(x) ∈ {Afflicted, Unafflicted}
EthicalStatus(x)       ∈ {Wholesome, Unwholesome, Indeterminate}
MaturationType(x)      ∈ {Pleasant, Painful, Indeterminate}
```

Therefore:

```text
Afflicted(x)
    ⇏ Unwholesome(x)

FalseView(x)
    ⇏ NecessarilyProducesOtherDirectedHarm(x)

CompatibleWithWholesomePractice(x)
    ⇏ CognitivelyTrue(x)
```

The higher-domain argument is genetic:

```text
LacksCause(x, OtherDirectedInjury)
    → LacksResult(x, PainfulMaturationOfThatKind)
    → EthicallyIndeterminate(x)
```

The desire-domain exception adds conjunction and genesis:

```text
ConjoinedWith(Ignorance, IndeterminateView)
    → EthicallyIndeterminate(Ignorance)

Innate(BodyView)
    → EthicallyIndeterminate(BodyView)

ConceptuallyConstructed(BodyView)
    → EthicallyUnwholesome(BodyView)
        [attributed to earlier teachers]
```

Ethical status is consequently a determined result of relations, provenance,
and efficacy rather than an immutable value attached to a lexical class.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.19 shows why the higher Objectivity of Path needs both Theory and Path-
bhūmi. Theory can diagnose a determination as false or contaminated. But its
practical and ethical status becomes known only through the achieved complex
of cause, support, object, conjunction, action, and result.

This is not a weakening of truth. It is a refusal to confuse theoretical
defect with practical evil:

```text
wrong determination of an object
    may coexist with wholesome action

practical alignment toward release
    may occur within a view that remains theoretically false

refined meditative existence
    may exclude coarse unwholesomeness while retaining contamination
```

All four determinations now form an exact diagnostic circuit:

```text
hetu
    mode of genesis and operative cause, including harm or its absence

phala
    ethical efficacy and karmic maturation actually produced

āśraya
    domain and conjoined event-complex in which the determination operates

ālambana
    the object-complex as falsely, innately, or conceptually determined
```

No single term can decide the classification alone. The `ālambana` may be
falsely determined while the `phala` is not painful maturation; the higher
`āśraya` may refine conduct while retaining afflicted cognition; a constructed
`hetu` may turn an innate indeterminate view into an unwholesome doctrinal
formation. Ethical status is the concrete result of the entire circuit.

This explains how the shock can be handled scientifically. The system does
not excuse error, but neither does it demonize every error as the same form of
evil. Path Objectivity can preserve whatever is practically aligned with
liberation, expose what remains cognitively false, and transform each according
to its actual cause and consequence.

The Subject is therefore educated rather than merely condemned. The Path
discriminates what must be corrected in knowledge, what must be abandoned in
will, what can be retained as wholesome practice, and what result each
determination truly generates.

This is Organon reconstruction. Its textual ground is the Bhāṣya's karmic
argument, its compatibility test, its conjunction rule, and the reported
distinction between innate and constructed body-view.
