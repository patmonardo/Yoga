# VAK_5.21

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
द्वैधोर्ध्ववृत्तेर्नातोऽन्यौ चत्वार्येवेति बाह्यकाः ।
तृष्णादृङ्मानमोहास्ते ध्यायितृत्वादविद्यया ॥ ५.२१ ॥
```

The kārikā-only witness gives a closely related initial form, but the Bhāṣya
transmits and distributively explains `द्वैधोर्ध्ववृत्तेः`: twofold movement
belongs to doubt and upward movement to conceit. The official study adopts the
Bhāṣya-supported reading while retaining the variant as an open critical-text
matter.

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dvaidhordhva-vṛtter nāto 'nyau catvāry eveti bāhyakāḥ |
tṛṣṇā-dṛṅ-māna-mohās te dhyāyitṛtvād avidyayā || 5.21 ||
```

The verse tests the Kāśmīra account of indeterminate roots from VAK 5.20 and
then records an alternative four-root doctrine.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dvaidha-ūrdhva-vṛtteḥ | na | ataḥ | anyau | catvāri | eva | iti |
bāhyakāḥ | tṛṣṇā | dṛk | māna | mohāḥ | te | dhyāyitṛ-tvāt | avidyayā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dvaidha-vṛtteḥ` | ablative singular compound | because of divided or twofold movement |
| `ūrdhva-vṛtteḥ` | ablative singular compound | because of upward movement |
| `na ataḥ anyau` | negative, ablative adverb, nominative dual | therefore the other two are not [roots] |
| `catvāri eva` | nominative neuter plural numeral with restriction | four only |
| `iti bāhyakāḥ` | quotative plus nominative plural | thus the outside authorities maintain |
| `tṛṣṇā-dṛk-māna-mohāḥ` | nominative plural enumeration | craving, view, conceit, and delusion |
| `te` | nominative plural pronoun | those; those indeterminate ones |
| `dhyāyitṛ-tvāt` | ablative singular abstract noun | because of the existence of meditator-types |
| `avidyayā` | instrumental singular | through or under the power of ignorance |

The Bhāṣya applies the two members of the initial compound separately:
`dvaidha-vṛtti` to doubt and `ūrdhva-vṛtti` to conceit.

## 4. Grammar

**Kāśmīra exclusion**

```text
dvaidha-vṛtteḥ [vicikitsā]
ūrdhva-vṛtteḥ [mānaḥ]
ataḥ anyau na [mūle]

because [doubt] moves in two directions
and [conceit] moves upward,
the other two are therefore not roots
```

The ablatives provide distinct reasons for the dual `anyau`. The predicate
`mūle` or `mūlāni` is understood from the preceding discussion.

**Alternative enumeration**

```text
catvāri eva [avyākṛta-mūlāni]
iti bāhyakāḥ:
tṛṣṇā, dṛk, māna, mohaḥ

the outside authorities say there are exactly four
[roots of the indeterminate]:
craving, view, conceit, and delusion
```

**Reason from meditator-types**

```text
dhyāyitṛ-tvāt avidyayā

because [three kinds] of meditator exist
under the power of ignorance
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the threefold distribution: meditators dominated by
craving, view, or conceit, all governed by ignorance.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Because of divided movement and upward movement, the other two are therefore
> not [roots]. The outside authorities say there are only four: craving, view,
> conceit, and delusion—those [indeterminate roots]—because of the existence of
> meditators under ignorance.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> On the Kāśmīra account, doubt is not a root because it moves in two directions
> and is unstable, while conceit is not a root because its defining movement is
> upward; roots are stable and downward-growing. Outside authorities instead
> recognize four indeterminate roots—craving, view, conceit, and ignorance—on
> the evidence of three kinds of meditator dominated respectively by craving,
> view, and conceit, all operating under the power of ignorance.

The stability and downward-growth criterion, the three meditator-types, and
the governing role of ignorance are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A root is not every factor that precedes or influences another. On one
> account, roothood belongs to a determination whose inner form is stable and
> grounding: doubt fails because it oscillates, and conceit fails because it
> elevates. On the competing account, a root is disclosed through what it
> repeatedly produces: craving, view, and conceit each organize a recognizable
> type of meditator, while ignorance governs all three. Ground may therefore be
> sought either in supporting form or in generated appearance.

The disagreement concerns the criterion of ground:

```text
inner form
    what is stable enough to support a field?

manifested product
    what repeatedly generates a determinate practitioner-type?
```

The two tests are related, but the text does not make them equivalent.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dvaidha-vṛtti` — divided movement**

Doubt alternates between opposed possibilities. The Bhāṣya makes its
`calatva`, instability, the reason it is unfit to serve as a root on the
Kāśmīra criterion.

**`Ūrdhva-vṛtti` — upward movement**

Conceit has `unnati`, self-elevation, as its defining mark. That direction is
contrasted with the downward-growing support characteristic of a root.

**`Sthira` and `adho-vṛtti` — stable and downward-moving**

The secure predicates in the Bhāṣya's ordinary-world root analogy. The
surrounding transmitted phrase is textually difficult, so no stronger
philological reconstruction is imposed here.

**`Bāhyakāḥ` — outside authorities**

Teachers outside the immediately defended Kāśmīra position. This passage does
not securely identify a more specific school, so the attribution remains
cautious.

**The four alternative roots**

```text
indeterminate craving
indeterminate view
indeterminate conceit
indeterminate ignorance
```

The pronoun `te`, as explained by the Bhāṣya, carries the indeterminate
qualification.

**`Dhyāyin` — meditator**

The alternative school appeals to three manifested types: the meditator
dominated by craving, by view, or by conceit.

**`Avidyā-vaśa` — under the power of ignorance**

Ignorance is both one member of the four-root list and the common governing
condition of the other three practitioner-types. Its role is therefore not
merely serial or additive.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Kāśmīra criterion can be represented as:

```text
Root(x)
    requires Stable(x)
    AND GroundingDirection(x)
```

Therefore:

```text
DividedMovement(Doubt)
    → Unstable(Doubt)
    → NotRoot(Doubt)

UpwardMovement(Conceit)
    → NotGroundingDirection(Conceit)
    → NotRoot(Conceit)
```

The alternative criterion reasons from products:

```text
RepeatedlyGenerates(x, MeditatorType)
    → RootAccordingToAlternativeSchool(x)
```

with a governing condition:

```text
CravingDominatedMeditator
ViewDominatedMeditator
ConceitDominatedMeditator
    all DependOn(Ignorance)
```

The two predicates must remain distinct:

```text
HasGroundingForm(x)
    ≠ GeneratesRecognizablePractitionerType(x)
```

The doctrinal dispute asks whether roothood is established from internal form
or from recurrent generated actuality.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.21 gives an unexpectedly exact form to the distinction between Path in
itself and Path in appearance. The Kāśmīra account looks toward the inner form
of ground: stability and supporting direction. The outside authorities look
toward manifestation: the type of meditator that a determination repeatedly
produces.

```text
Path in itself
    internal organization determining the function of each moment

Path in appearance
    practitioner- and bhūmi-forms generated through that organization
```

But the verse also supplies the necessary warning: an appearing meditator is
not therefore an appearance of the true Path. Higher meditation can be
organized by craving, view, or conceit under the power of ignorance. Bhūmi as
mere attained state is insufficient; it becomes a Path-bhūmi only when its
manifest form expresses the internally adequate articulation of Path.

This makes the Aṣṭāṅga relation indispensable:

```text
Aṣṭāṅga
    supplies the inner criterion by which an aṅga has Path-function

Bhūmi
    exhibits whether that function has become achieved actuality

false meditative appearance
    exhibits attainment whose governing root is not the Path
```

The complete determination must join both tests. Inner form without appearance
has not become actual; appearance without the inner form may be an organized
product of ignorance. The Concrete Path is the identity of its rational
organization with its adequate manifestation.

The fourfold circuit shows how to test that identity:

```text
hetu
    the governing root or inner generative determination

phala
    the practitioner-type and attained state that appear from it

āśraya
    the stable supporting field in which the practice is established

ālambana
    the objective truth-field toward which the meditation is directed
```

A meditative `phala` alone does not prove an adequate `hetu`. Its `āśraya` may
be stabilized while its governing condition remains ignorance, and its
`ālambana` may be a higher state without being correctly determined. Path
Objectivity must therefore validate the whole circuit.

This is Organon–Yoga reconstruction. Its textual ground is the opposition
between the Kāśmīra structural criterion and the alternative inference from
generated meditator-types, together with the Bhāṣya's statement that all three
types operate under ignorance.
