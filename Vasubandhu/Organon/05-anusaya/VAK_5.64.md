# VAK_5.64

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
परिज्ञा नव कामाद्यप्रकारद्वयसंक्षयः ।
एका द्वयोः क्षये द्वे ते तथोर्ध्वं तिस्र एव ताः ॥ ५.६४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
parijñā nava kāmādya-prakāra-dvaya-saṃkṣayaḥ |
ekā dvayoḥ kṣaye dve te tathordhvaṃ tisra eva tāḥ || 5.64 ||
```

VAK 5.63 stated that the same disconnection receives the designation
`parijñā` in different Path-states. VAK 5.64 now announces nine parijñās and
enumerates the first six, all belonging to abandonment through seeing.

**Textual caution.** The working Bhāṣya e-text prints `nisra` in one prose
recapitulation where the kārikā and context require `tisra`, “three.” The
research witness remains unchanged; the secure contextual sense is recorded
without silently correcting that source file.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
parijñāḥ | nava | kāma-ādya-prakāra-dvaya-saṃkṣayaḥ | ekā |
dvayoḥ | kṣaye | dve | te | tathā | ūrdhvam | tisraḥ | eva | tāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `parijñāḥ` | nominative plural feminine | completed abandonments bearing the designation parijñā |
| `nava` | nominative plural feminine | nine |
| `kāma-ādya` | compound member | beginning in the desire-domain |
| `prakāra-dvaya` | compound member | the first pair of truth-indexed classes: suffering and origin |
| `saṃkṣayaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | complete exhaustion |
| `ekā` | nominative singular feminine | one parijñā |
| `dvayoḥ` | genitive dual | of the remaining two classes, cessation and Path |
| `kṣaye` | locative singular masculine | upon the exhaustion or abandonment |
| `dve te` | nominative dual feminine | those two parijñās |
| `tathā` | indeclinable | likewise |
| `ūrdhvam` | adverb | above, in the higher domains |
| `tisraḥ eva tāḥ` | nominative plural feminine plus particles | precisely those three |

The Bhāṣya fixes the four `prakāras` by the Four Truths and identifies the
relevant operation as abandonment of seeing-abandonable afflictions.

## 4. Grammar

The opening is a complete nominal assertion:

```text
parijñāḥ nava
```

The remainder enumerates six of the nine. In the desire-domain:

```text
kāmadhātau
    ādyasya prakāra-dvayasya saṃkṣayaḥ
        ekā [parijñā]

    dvayoḥ [prakārayoḥ] kṣaye
        dve [parijñe]
```

The first `prakāra-dvaya` is suffering-seeing-abandonable plus origin-seeing-
abandonable. The genitive dual `dvayoḥ` then refers distributively to the
cessation- and Path-seeing-abandonable classes, each yielding one parijñā.

The final clause repeats the threefold structure collectively above the
desire-domain:

```text
ūrdhvam
    tathā
    tisraḥ eva tāḥ
```

The Bhāṣya combines the form and formless domains for this enumeration. The
verse states the grouping but does not itself give its rationale.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> There are nine parijñās. In the desire-domain, complete exhaustion of the
> first pair of classes is one; upon exhaustion of the [remaining] two, there
> are those two. Likewise above, there are precisely those three.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> There are nine completed abandonments designated `parijñā`. In the desire-
> domain, abandonment of the afflictions abandoned through seeing suffering
> and origin together constitutes one parijñā; abandonment of those abandoned
> through seeing cessation constitutes a second, and abandonment of those
> abandoned through seeing the Path a third. The same threefold division
> applies collectively to the form and formless domains, producing the first
> six parijñās.

The final three belong to cultivation-abandonment and are supplied in VAK
5.65.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A completed discrimination is not counted for every isolated destruction.
> The Path designates a parijñā when abandonment has completed a doctrinally
> delimited class: suffering and origin together, cessation, or Path, first in
> the desire-domain and then across the higher domains. Knowledge is the
> causal determination; abandonment is its accomplished result and receives
> the cause's name. Their connection licenses the designation without making
> cognitive cause and practical result identical.

The source gives a topology of completed abandonment. It does not here explain
the rationale for joining suffering and origin or separating cessation and
Path, so that rationale remains an open question.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Parijñā` — complete discrimination / designated completed abandonment**

The Bhāṣya distinguishes two senses:

```text
jñāna-parijñā     complete discrimination as knowledge
prahāṇa-parijñā   complete discrimination as abandonment
```

The ninefold enumeration concerns the second.

**`Jñāna-parijñā` — parijñā as knowledge**

The cognitive causal side. The source defines it with the transmitted phrase
`sāsravaṃ jñānam`; its wider doctrinal implications are not settled in this
verse and should remain open for later clarification.

**`Prahāṇa-parijñā` — parijñā as abandonment**

The abandonment itself (`prahāṇam eva`), treated as a completed result rather
than as one more cognition.

**`Hetūpacāra` — application of the cause-name to the result**

The semantic relation explaining why the abandonment-result is called
`parijñā`: it is produced through complete discriminative knowing. This is a
name-transfer across a fixed causal relation, not an identity assertion.

**`Prakāra-dvaya` — pair of classes**

The Bhāṣya identifies the pair as afflictions abandoned through seeing
suffering and those abandoned through seeing origin.

**The three seeing-abandonment groups**

```text
{duḥkha-darśana-heya, samudaya-darśana-heya}
{nirodha-darśana-heya}
{mārga-darśana-heya}
```

The classification provides three parijñās per domain-range, not four.

**`Kāma` and `ūrdhva` — desire-domain and higher-domain range**

The first three parijñās concern the desire-domain. The next three repeat the
same truth-indexed grouping collectively for the form and formless domains.

## 8. Logical Determination

The designation applies at class-completion level:

```text
IndividualAfflictionDestruction
    ≠ CompletedAbandonmentClass
    ≠ ParijñāDesignation
```

For the desire-domain:

```text
CompleteAbandonment(
    {DuḥkhaSeeingAbandonables,
     SamudayaSeeingAbandonables},
    DesireDomain
) → Parijñā_1

CompleteAbandonment(NirodhaSeeingAbandonables, DesireDomain)
    → Parijñā_2

CompleteAbandonment(MārgaSeeingAbandonables, DesireDomain)
    → Parijñā_3
```

For the higher-domain range:

```text
ApplySameThreefoldPartition(FormDomain + FormlessDomain)
    → {Parijñā_4, Parijñā_5, Parijñā_6}
```

The classification is indexed by:

```text
TruthIndexedClass
    × DomainRange
    × AbandonmentMode(Seeing)
```

The naming relation is:

```text
Causes(JñānaParijñā, PrahāṇaResult)
    → MayTransferDesignation(
          Parijñā,
          from JñānaCause,
          to PrahāṇaResult
      )

MayTransferDesignation
    ⇏ Identity(Jñāna, Prahāṇa)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Nine abandonments, not nine cognitions.** The ordinary semantic pull of
`parijñā` toward knowing must be controlled by the explicit Bhāṣya distinction.
The present enumeration counts accomplished abandonments.

**Grouped completion.** One parijñā can cover many affliction-destructions.
Its unit is a completed truth- and domain-indexed class, not one lexical factor
or one moment of destruction.

**The asymmetry remains unexplained here.** Suffering and origin count
together; cessation and Path separately. The prose states this architecture
but supplies no explicit reason in this passage. Any claim that the pair forms
one “diagnostic complex” would be comparative reconstruction, not presently
confirmed Bhāṣya doctrine.

**Higher domains are collective.** Form and formless afflictions yield three
additional seeing-abandonment parijñās together. The reason for combining
those domains should likewise remain open pending further source control.

**Relation to 5.63.** A kleśa is destroyed once, the same disconnection can be
acquired repeatedly, and an appropriately completed state of that
disconnection receives a parijñā designation. These are three different
levels of determination.

**Organon interpretation.** A later Dataset should attach designation to a
completed abandonment partition:

```text
ParijñāDesignation
    designates AbandonmentCompletion
    covers TruthIndexedClass
    covers DomainRange
    hasAbandonmentMode Seeing
    licensedBy Hetūpacāra
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This formalization preserves source relations and explicitly leaves the
unexplained grouping rationale open.
