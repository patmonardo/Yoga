# VAK_5.69

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नैकया पञ्चभिर्यावद्दर्शनस्थः समन्वितः ।
भावनास्थः पुनः षड्भिरे्कया वा द्वयेन वा ॥ ५.६९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
naikayā pañcabhiryāvad darśanasthaḥ samanvitaḥ |
bhāvanāsthaḥ punaḥ ṣaḍbhir ekayā vā dvayena vā || 5.69 ||
```

VAK 5.69 changes the question from the constitution and production of the
nine parijñās to present possession: who, at which Path-state, is endowed with
how many?

**Textual caution.** The Devanāgarī witness transmits `षड्भिरे्कया`, while the
IAST and syntax resolve the phrase as `ṣaḍbhir ekayā`, “with six, or with
one.” The witness is preserved verbatim rather than silently regularized.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
na | ekayā | pañcabhiḥ | yāvat | darśana-sthaḥ | samanvitaḥ |
bhāvanā-sthaḥ | punaḥ | ṣaḍbhiḥ | ekayā | vā | dvayena | vā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `na` | negation | not, none |
| `ekayā` | instrumental singular feminine | with one parijñā |
| `pañcabhiḥ` | instrumental plural | with five parijñās |
| `yāvat` | limit particle | as far as, up through |
| `darśanasthaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | one standing in the Path of Seeing |
| `samanvitaḥ` | nominative singular masculine past passive participle of `sam-anu-√i` | endowed with, presently possessed of |
| `bhāvanāsthaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | one standing in the Path of Cultivation |
| `punaḥ` | contrastive connective | but, in turn |
| `ṣaḍbhiḥ` | instrumental plural | with six parijñās |
| `ekayā vā` | instrumental singular plus particle | or with one |
| `dvayena vā` | instrumental singular neuter collective expression | or with two |

The understood noun after every numeral is `parijñā`. The governing relation
is current endowment, not the historical occurrence of every prior attainment.

## 4. Grammar

The first half establishes a range whose exact Path-moments are supplied by
the Bhāṣya:

```text
darśanasthaḥ
    [na ekayā] ... pañcabhiḥ yāvat
    samanvitaḥ
```

The compressed `naikayā pañcabhir yāvat` covers possession of none and then
one through five as seeing proceeds. It is not merely an indefinite range.

The second half contrasts the cultivation-stage possibilities:

```text
bhāvanāsthaḥ punaḥ
    ṣaḍbhiḥ
    ekayā vā
    dvayena vā
    [parijñābhiḥ samanvitaḥ]
```

The alternatives describe different attainment-states, not a free variation
in counting the same state. `Samanvitaḥ` applies throughout and asks what is
presently possessed.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> One standing in the Path of Seeing is endowed with none and, progressively,
> with as many as five. One standing in the Path of Cultivation, however, is
> endowed with six, or with one, or with two.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A worldling and a noble practitioner through the patience of dharma-
> knowledge concerning origin possess no parijñā. During the Path of Seeing,
> the count becomes one at origin-dharma-knowledge, two at origin-subsequent-
> knowledge, three at cessation-dharma-knowledge, four at cessation-
> subsequent-knowledge, and five at Path-dharma-knowledge. At Path-subsequent-
> knowledge, now standing in cultivation, the practitioner possesses six so
> long as desire-domain dispassion has not been attained. According to later
> dispassion and attainment states, the practitioner is instead endowed with
> one or two specified parijñās.

The reason several earlier parijñās are then established as one is raised but
not answered here; VAK 5.70 supplies that explanation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The history of liberating events and the practitioner's present possession-
> state are not the same account. During seeing, completed abandonments become
> countable in an exact sequence from zero through five; entry into cultivation
> establishes six. Later states may be technically represented by one or two
> parijñās without the lower number by itself implying loss or regression.
> Cardinality receives its meaning only from the identity and scope of the
> completed abandonments presently possessed.

The verse establishes the state-indexed counts; it does not yet provide the
principle explaining their later consolidation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Samanvāgata` — endowed with or presently possessed of**

The governing relation. It distinguishes current technical possession from
the historical production of an abandonment or acquisition of disconnection.

**`Darśanastha` — standing in the Path of Seeing**

The context of the exact zero-to-five sequence. Counts change at determinate
knowledge-moments, not by an unlocated gradual accumulation.

**The seeing-stage sequence**

```text
through samudaya-dharmajñāna-kṣānti   → 0
samudaya-dharmajñāna                   → 1
samudaya-anvayajñāna                   → 2
nirodha-dharmajñāna                    → 3
nirodha-anvayajñāna                    → 4
mārga-dharmajñāna                      → 5
```

This sequence confirms the completion threshold established in VAK 5.68.

**`Bhāvanāstha` — standing in the Path of Cultivation**

At `mārga-anvayajñāna` the noble practitioner is treated as cultivation-
standing and possesses six seeing-abandonment parijñās, provided desire-domain
dispassion has not been attained.

**`Kāmavairāgya` — dispassion from the desire-domain**

Whether obtained earlier or later, its relevant possession-state is one:
`avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa-parijñā`, abandonment of the lower-part fetters. The
same one remains in the stated fall through a form-domain paryavasthāna.

**Arhatship**

The arhat is endowed with one parijñā of a different and terminal scope:
`sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā`, complete exhaustion of all fetters.

**`Rūpavairāgya` — dispassion from the form-domain**

Its possession-state comprises two:

```text
avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa-parijñā
rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā
```

These two remain even after the stated fall through a formless-domain
paryavasthāna.

**`Paryavasthāna` — active afflictive enveloping**

Here it marks a fall from an attainment. The examples show that such a fall
does not automatically erase every previously established lower-domain
parijñā.

## 8. Logical Determination

The count is a function of state:

```text
PresentParijñāCount(person)
    = f(PathMoment,
        DomainDispassionState,
        AttainmentOrFallState,
        PresentlyPossessedParijñāSet)
```

For seeing:

```text
CountAt(SamudayaDharmajñānaKṣānti) = 0
CountAt(SamudayaDharmajñāna)        = 1
CountAt(SamudayaAnvayajñāna)        = 2
CountAt(NirodhaDharmajñāna)         = 3
CountAt(NirodhaAnvayajñāna)         = 4
CountAt(MārgaDharmajñāna)           = 5
```

At entry into cultivation:

```text
CountAt(MārgaAnvayajñāna)
    = 6
    if NotYetAttained(Kāmavairāgya)
```

Later cases include:

```text
Possesses(KāmavairāgyaState,
          {LowerPartFetterAbandonment})

Possesses(RūpavairāgyaState,
          {LowerPartFetterAbandonment,
           FormAttachmentDestruction})

Possesses(ArhatState,
          {AllFetterExhaustion})
```

Therefore:

```text
LaterPathState(person)
    ⇏ GreaterNumericalParijñāCount(person)

SameCount(personA, personB)
    ⇏ SameParijñāScope(personA, personB)
```

One at desire-domain dispassion and one at arhatship have equal cardinality
but radically different parijñā identities and ranges.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Exact Path moments.** The Bhāṣya transforms the kārikā's compressed zero-to-
five range into a determinate sequence. Before origin is completed, earlier
abandonments do not yet receive the technical designation.

**Six marks the seeing completion.** At Path-subsequent-knowledge the six
seeing-abandonment parijñās are possessed and the practitioner is now located
in the cultivation Path.

**No monotonic counter.** Later counts may be one or two. The passage is not
counting everything ever produced but what the practitioner is currently
technically endowed with.

**No premature theory of consolidation.** The Bhāṣya itself asks why only one
parijñā is established for the non-returner and arhat. The explanation through
`saṃkalana` belongs to VAK 5.70 and should not be back-filled into this verse
as though already stated.

**Falls preserve specified completions.** A form-domain paryavasthāna leaves
the lower-part-fetter parijñā; a formless-domain paryavasthāna leaves both the
lower-part and form-attachment completions. Possession depends on which
disconnections remain established.

**Organon interpretation.** A later Dataset needs event history and current
normalized state as separate objects:

```text
PractitionerState
    occursAtPathMoment
    hasDomainDispassionState
    hasFallState
    presentlyPossesses ParijñāSet
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This is a project-level representation of `samanvāgama`, not formal vocabulary
attributed to the source.
