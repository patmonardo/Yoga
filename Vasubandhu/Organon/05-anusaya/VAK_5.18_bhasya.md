# VAK 5.18 Bhāṣya — Object and Association as Distinct Adherence

## Kārikā Anchor

> nānāsravordhvaviṣayā asvīkārād vipakṣataḥ |
> yena yaḥ saṃprayuktas tu sa tasmin saṃprayogataḥ || 5.18 ||

> Anuśayas with uncontaminated or higher objects do not adhere in
> those objects, because the objects are not appropriated and stand
> opposed. But an anuśaya associated with a dharma adheres in it
> through that association.

The first half qualifies 5.17's general rule of adherence through
an object; the second gives adherence through association.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

“Those whose objects are uncontaminated or higher do not
adhere.” Anuśayas taking uncontaminated supports do not adhere
in those supports by way of object; nor do those taking a higher
level as support. Why? Because the object is not appropriated
and because it stands opposed.

When self-view and thirst have appropriated something, other
anuśayas can follow and settle there, like dust on damp cloth.
Uncontaminated dharmas and a higher level are not appropriated
in that way. So the anuśayas directed toward them do not adhere
there by way of their object. If someone at a lower level
aspires toward a higher one, that aspiration is a wholesome
desire for a dharma. Nirvāṇa and the path are opposed to the
afflictions directed toward them, as the higher level is opposed
to afflictions of lower levels. Those afflictions gain no
foothold there, just as feet cannot settle on a heated stone.

Others explain “anuśaya” here through congeniality: these
supports are not congenial to those afflictions, as dryness is
not congenial to a vāta disorder. Thus the text has spoken of
adhering by way of object.

“But whichever anuśaya is associated with a dharma adheres in
that dharma through association.” The verb “adheres” continues
from the preceding verse. An anuśaya associated with a dharma
adheres in it by association. The particle `tu` qualifies the
statement: this holds while the anuśaya has not been abandoned.
The final lines indicate possible cases of association without
adherence by object, and reopen the status of afflictions ranging
across different levels. Their compressed syntax does not permit
a more exact classification here without further witness work.

## Argument and Voices

This passage distinguishes three relations: taking something as
`ālambana`, appropriating it through self-view and thirst, and
being `saṃprayukta` with another dharma. Mere object-reference
does not guarantee **object-based adherence**. The object must
offer a footing compatible with afflictive appropriation.
Uncontaminated supports and higher levels can be represented
by an affliction while standing opposed to its establishment.

Association works differently. An unabandoned anuśaya adheres
in what it is associated with through that association, even
where the object relation is barred. The Bhāṣya's `tu` explicitly
limits this to the period before abandonment. This returns to
the 5.02 debate: the terms for association, manifestation, and
continuing disposition must not be collapsed.

`Ity apare` attributes the congeniality explanation to unnamed
others. The damp-cloth and heated-stone comparisons belong to
the running explanation; the vāta analogy belongs to the
reported alternative. Neither analogy should be converted into
a literal physical substrate theory of anuśaya.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The initial `nānāsrava-` divides as
`na anāsrava-`, not as an independent “various.” `Asvīkāra`
is non-appropriation; it does not mean that the affliction
cannot cognize the object at all. `Dharmacchanda` distinguishes
wholesome aspiration for a higher level from afflicted thirst.
The final conditional prose is retained and marked as compressed
rather than silently merged into a tidy rule.

**Organon reconstruction.** Representing an object, being able
to appropriate it, and arising in association with a factor are
different relations. This is a strong constraint for the Science
of Citta and a more exact way to articulate the danger of
clinging to view: a view's object alone does not explain the
affliction; one must determine how the object is taken up and
which other factors participate. This remains a project-level
use of the Kośa, separate from any implemented architecture.
