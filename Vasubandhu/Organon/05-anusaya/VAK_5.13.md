# VAK_5.13

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नवोर्ध्वालम्बना एषां दृष्टिद्वयविवर्जिताः ।
प्राप्तिवर्ज्याः सहभुवो येऽप्येभिस्तेऽपि सर्वगाः ॥ ५.१३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
navordhvālambanā eṣāṃ dṛṣṭi-dvaya-vivarjitāḥ |
prāpti-varjyāḥ sahabhuvo ye 'py ebhis te 'pi sarvagāḥ || 5.13 ||
```

The verse further determines the `sarvatraga` classification introduced in
VAK 5.12: nine anuśayas can range upward, and most dharmas co-arising with a
universal anuśaya share its universal status.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
nava | ūrdhva-ālambanāḥ | eṣām | dṛṣṭi-dvaya-vivarjitāḥ |
prāpti-varjyāḥ | saha-bhuvaḥ | ye | api | ebhiḥ | te | api | sarvagāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `nava` | cardinal numeral | nine |
| `ūrdhva-ālambanāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | having a higher domain as object-support |
| `eṣām` | genitive plural pronoun | of these [eleven universal anuśayas] |
| `dṛṣṭi-dvaya-vivarjitāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | excluding the two views |
| `prāpti-varjyāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | excluding acquisitions |
| `saha-bhuvaḥ` | nominative plural masculine | co-arisen; arising together |
| `ebhiḥ` | instrumental plural pronoun | with these [universal anuśayas] |
| `ye ... te` | correlative pronouns, nominative plural | whichever ... those |
| `sarvagāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | universal; universally ranging |

The initial sandhi is decisive:

```text
nava + ūrdhva-ālambanāḥ
    → navordhvālambanāḥ
```

It is not to be divided as `na vā ūrdhvālambanāḥ`. The Bhāṣya confirms the
numeral by identifying “the other nine anuśayas.”

## 4. Grammar

**Ninefold predication**

```text
eṣām [ekādaśānām]
dṛṣṭi-dvaya-vivarjitāḥ nava
ūrdhva-ālambanāḥ [bhavanti]

of these [eleven], nine, excluding the two views,
have a higher domain as their object-support
```

The numeral `nava` and the predicate adjective `ūrdhvālambanāḥ` agree with the
understood masculine plural `anuśayāḥ`. `Dṛṣṭi-dvaya-vivarjitāḥ` qualifies the
nine by subtraction from the eleven.

**Correlative extension**

```text
ye api ebhiḥ saha-bhuvaḥ,
prāpti-varjyāḥ,
te api sarvagāḥ

whichever [dharmas] arise together with these,
excluding acquisitions,
those too are universal
```

The `ye ... te` construction makes co-arising the condition for extension of
the predicate. `Prāpti-varjyāḥ` marks an explicit exception; co-occurrence
alone does not license indiscriminate inheritance.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Of these, nine—excluding the two views—have higher [domains] as their
> object-support. Whatever [dharmas] arise together with these, excluding
> acquisitions, are also universal.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Of the eleven universal anuśayas, nine can take a higher or different domain
> as their object-support; body-view and extreme-view are excluded. Other
> dharmas co-arising with a universal anuśaya also share its universal status,
> but acquisitions do not, because their results are heterogeneous.

The identity of the two views, the different-domain formulation, and the
reason for excluding acquisition are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Universality is neither confinement to one level nor absence of every
> boundary. Nine universal anuśayas can direct their determining relation
> beyond their present domain toward a higher one. The two views structured by
> self-appropriation cannot do so. Within an actual afflicted event,
> universality also belongs to the co-arisen complex, though not to the
> acquisition relation. A determination may therefore extend vertically and
> communicate its character through a concrete conjunction, but only under
> rules that specify what can cross a domain and what can inherit a function.

VAK 5.13 differentiates:

```text
object-range across domains
    from
shared status within a co-arisen event
    from
possession through acquisition
```

These are three relations, not three expressions for the same fact.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ūrdhvālambana` — having a higher domain as object-support**

The nine can take one or two different higher domains as `ālambana`, according
to the Bhāṣya. Their same-domain universality from VAK 5.12 therefore does not
exhaust their possible objective range.

**The two excluded views**

The Bhāṣya identifies them as `satkāyadṛṣṭi`, body-view or the view of a real
personal aggregate, and `antagrāhadṛṣṭi`, extreme-view. The inherited account
holds that the former requires apprehension as self or mine and that the
latter arises upon that self-appropriation.

**`Visabhāgadhātu-sarvatraga` — universal with respect to a different domain**

This Bhāṣya expression complements `sabhāgadhātu-sarvatraga` in VAK 5.12.
Sameness or difference of domain specifies the direction of the
`ālambana` relation; it does not introduce an unconditioned universality.

**`Mithyājñāna` — false cognition**

When an inhabitant of the desire domain regards Brahmā as a being and as
permanent, the Abhidharmikas classify this cognition as false cognition rather
than either of the two excluded views. Vasubandhu challenges the distinction:
the thesis must be demonstrated, not merely asserted.

**`Sahabhu` — co-arisen**

A dharma arising together with a universal anuśaya participates in the same
afflicted event. The verse extends universal status to such dharmas subject to
the explicit exception.

**`Prāpti` — acquisition**

The relation by which a dharma is acquired or possessed. The Bhāṣya excludes
acquisitions from universal status because they have many or heterogeneous
results (`anekaphalatva`).

**`Sarvatraga-hetu` — universal-cause relation**

The prose following the verse distinguishes being a universal anuśaya from
functioning as a cause through the universal-cause relation. Classification
and causal office overlap without being identical.

## 8. Logical Determination

The eleven from VAK 5.12 divide by cross-domain object-range:

```text
ElevenUniversalAnusayas
    − {SatkayaDrsti, AntagrahaDrsti}
    = NineHigherDomainObjectAnusayas
```

The exclusion is relational:

```text
SatkayaDrsti(x)
    requires ApprehendsAsSelfOrMine(x)

AntagrahaDrsti(x)
    depends upon SatkayaDrstiStructure(x)

NoCrossDomainSelfAppropriation(x)
    → NoCrossDomainRangeForEitherView(x)
```

This is the Abhidharmika rationale reported in the Bhāṣya. Vasubandhu does not
allow the taxonomy to validate itself:

```text
NamesAs(FalseCognition, x)
AND Denies(View, x)
    ⇏ DemonstratesTheDifference(x)
```

The second half gives a controlled inheritance rule:

```text
CoArisesWith(x, UniversalAnusaya)
AND NotAcquisition(x)
    → Universal(x)
```

Yet type and causal function remain non-identical:

```text
UniversalAnusaya(x)
    ⇏ PresentlyFunctionsAsUniversalCause(x)

FunctionsAsUniversalCause(x)
    ⇏ IsAnusaya(x)
```

The Bhāṣya begins a four-corner analysis precisely to preserve these possible
non-coincidences.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.13 transforms the `ālambana` determination from a flat field into a
directed architecture. In VAK 5.12 the whole of one's own domain established
horizontal scope. Here nine anuśayas can cross upward into a different domain:

```text
determination in present domain
    → higher-domain objective support
```

This is not the Subject escaping every condition. It is a determinate form in
one level taking another level as its objective display. The two excluded
views reveal why range depends upon logical constitution: because their form
requires appropriation as self or mine, their `ālambana` cannot be extended by
mere analogy to every cognition concerning a higher being.

The Brahmā objection is especially important for the Organon. A classification
cannot be saved simply by redescribing its counterexample. If one cognition is
called `dṛṣṭi` and another, apparently analogous cognition, `mithyājñāna`, the
differentiating ground must be made explicit. Determinate Reason demands the
rule of the distinction.

The second half shows how the Product is internally articulated. A universal
anuśaya is not an isolated atom; in manifestation it is a structured event
whose co-arisen moments share its operative scope. But `prāpti` proves that
participation, acquisition, and causal function cannot be flattened into one
relation:

```text
type of determination
object-range of determination
co-arisen participation
acquired possession
causal function
```

The Concrete Universal is thus neither a detached generality nor the simple
sum of its members. It is the rule immanent in an event-complex, communicated
through specified relations and checked by determinate exceptions. The
Product becomes genuinely concrete because its moments share a determination
without losing their different functions.

This is Organon reconstruction. The textual basis is the Bhāṣya's
cross-domain analysis, its co-arising rule, its exclusion of acquisition, and
Vasubandhu's explicit demand that the inherited distinction be demonstrated.
