# VAK 5.31 Bhāṣya — Completing and Applying the Support Matrix

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirodhamārgadṛggheyāḥ sarve svādhikagocarāḥ |
> anāsravās tridhātvantyatrayānāsravagocarāḥ || 5.31 ||

> In each of the three domains, Dharmas to be abandoned by seeing cessation
> or the Path lie in the previously established cognitive range with their
> own corresponding Consciousness class added. Dharmas without influx lie
> in the range of the final three Consciousness classes in each domain—those
> to be abandoned by seeing cessation, seeing the Path, and cultivation—and
> of Consciousness without influx.

## 3. Continuous Translation

“In every domain, those [Dharmas] to be abandoned by seeing
cessation or the Path have their own [Consciousness class]
added to their cognitive range.” For Dharmas to be abandoned
by seeing cessation or the Path, their own Consciousness is
the class to be abandoned by that same seeing. The Dharmas
should be understood as Supports of that additional class.
How so? Desire Realm Dharmas to be abandoned by seeing
cessation are Supports of six Cognition classes: the five
previously stated and, in addition, their own class to be
abandoned by seeing cessation. The Path-seeing class works
the same way: the five previously stated and its own
Path-seeing class. The same procedure applies to Form and
Formless Realm Dharmas; with their own corresponding
Consciousness added, they are Supports of nine and eleven
Consciousness classes respectively. Thus the five kinds of
Dharma in the three domains have been stated.

“Dharmas without influx lie in the cognitive range of the
last three [Consciousness classes] in the three domains and
of Consciousness without influx.” These Dharmas are Supports
of ten Consciousness classes: in each of the three domains,
the final three classes to be abandoned by seeing cessation,
seeing the Path, and cultivation, plus the class without
influx.

The same matter is then gathered in a further three-line
stanza: “In the three domains, the [Dharmas] to be abandoned
by seeing suffering, seeing origination, or cultivation, and
the undefiled [Dharmas], are in the ranges of five, eight,
ten, and ten Cognition classes; all those to be abandoned by
seeing cessation or the Path have their own class added to
the range.” This stanza summarizes the prior matrix; it is
not another half of 5.31.

Having learned this ordering of sixteen Dharma classes as
Supports for sixteen Consciousness classes, how should the
anuśaya inquiry be applied? The text offers an indication.
Suppose the question is how many anuśayas lie latent in a
Cognition that takes the pleasure faculty as Support. One
must first examine the pleasure faculty. It is sevenfold:
one Desire Realm kind to be abandoned by cultivation, five
Form Realm kinds, and one without influx.

Taken together, these seven kinds serve as Support for twelve
kinds of Cognition: four Desire Realm kinds, excluding the
one to be abandoned by seeing cessation; five Form Realm
kinds; two Formless Realm kinds, to be abandoned by seeing
the Path and by cultivation; and one without influx. This
is the twelvefold Cognition taking the pleasure faculty as
Support. As applicable, one should then know that anuśayas
lie latent in it from four Desire Realm classes, Form Realm
anuśayas taking conditioned Dharmas as Supports, two
Formless Realm classes, and the universally ranging
anuśayas.

How many anuśayas lie latent in a Cognition taking that
pleasure-faculty-taking Cognition as Support? Of which
Cognition is the twelvefold Cognition just described a
Support? It is a Support of those same twelve kinds and of
two further Formless Realm kinds to be abandoned by seeing
suffering and seeing origination. This gives fourteen kinds
of Cognition whose Support is a pleasure-faculty-taking
Cognition. After adding those two Formless Realm kinds, one
should understand that, as applicable, anuśayas from four
Desire Realm classes and four Formless Realm classes lie
latent there, together with Form Realm anuśayas taking
conditioned Dharmas as Supports. Other cases are to be
followed by this same method.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

`Svādhika` extends each previously established domain range
by the Consciousness class of the Dharma's own cessation- or
Path-seeing abandonment type. The arithmetic is **5 + 1 = 6**,
**8 + 1 = 9**, and **10 + 1 = 11**. The Dharma class without
influx has a separate rule: the last three Consciousness
classes in each domain, plus one without influx, giving
**3 × 3 + 1 = 10**. The further summary stanza restates
**5, 8, 10, 10** and the additive rule.

The worked example applies the finished matrix twice. First,
the sevenfold pleasure faculty is Support for twelve kinds
of Cognition: **4 + 5 + 2 + 1 = 12**. The text then lists
anuśayas that can lie latent in this twelvefold group, with
`yathāsaṃbhavam` restricting the list to applicable cases.
Second, the twelvefold pleasure-faculty-taking Cognition is
itself Support for fourteen kinds: the same twelve and two
further Formless Realm kinds. The possible anuśaya list
changes accordingly, including four Formless Realm classes.

Support access does not by itself establish anuśaya adherence.
Nor does the nested Support relation turn the pleasure
faculty into a Cognition. The passage determines which
Consciousness or Cognition classes can take which Dharma or
Cognition classes as Support before the latent-affliction
question is answered.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Svādhika` is “with its own
corresponding class added,” as determined by the prose.
`Antyatraya` is the final three abandonment classes in each
domain: cessation-seeing, Path-seeing, and cultivation.
`Diṅmātra` announces an indication of method, not a full
enumeration. `Sukhendriyālambanālambana` is nested: the
second Cognition takes as Support a Cognition whose Support
is the pleasure faculty. `Saṃskṛtālambana` is “taking
conditioned Dharmas as Supports.” The text shifts between
`citta` (Consciousness) and `vijñāna` (Cognition) in its
matrix explanation; their local counting office overlaps,
but their controlled renderings remain distinct. The
`dvādaśadharmasya` reading remains uncertain as recorded
above.

**Organon reconstruction.** The completed matrix gives a
finite typed relation and a method for iterating it. A
Science of Citta can represent the Dharma or Cognition taken
as Support, the Consciousness class taking it, and the
anuśaya condition separately. This is a project-level use
of the Kośa method; the passage does not identify
Consciousness with Basis (`āśraya`) or itself specify a
software implementation.
