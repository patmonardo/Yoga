# VAK 5.32 Bhāṣya — Bearing Anuśaya and the First Genetic Sequence

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dvidhā sānuśayaṃ kliṣṭam akliṣṭam anuśāyakaiḥ |
> mohākāṅkṣā tato mithyādṛṣṭiḥ satkāyadṛk tataḥ || 5.32 ||

> Defiled Consciousness bears anuśaya in two ways: through anuśayas that adhere and
> through abandoned anuśayas still conjoined with it. Non-defiled Consciousness bears it
> only through unabandoned anuśayas that adhere by taking it as Support. From delusion
> arises doubt, from doubt false view, and from false view the view of the
> Bases as self.

## 3. Continuous Translation

If a Consciousness (`citta`) is called “bearing anuśaya”
through certain anuśayas, do those anuśayas lie latent in
that Consciousness? Some do: those conjoined with it and not
abandoned, and those not abandoned that take it as Support.
Some do not: those conjoined with it and abandoned; the
running clause also says “and those taking it as Support,”
but does not make that expression's scope clear. On this
ground the verse says: “Defiled Consciousness bears anuśaya
in two ways; non-defiled Consciousness through anuśayas that
actually lie latent.”

Defiled Consciousness bears anuśaya through anuśayas that do
lie latent: those not abandoned that are conjoined with it
or take it as Support. It also bears the designation through
anuśayas that no longer lie latent: abandoned ones conjoined
with it, because it is accompanied by them. Non-defiled
Consciousness bears anuśaya only through not-abandoned
anuśayas that lie latent by taking it as Support.

How do these ten anuśayas come into operation? At the outset,
through conjunction with ignorance, a person is confused
about the truths. Suffering does not appeal to him, and so on
through the Path. Thus: “From delusion, doubt.” When the
confused person hears two alternatives, doubt (`vicikitsā`)
arises: “Is this suffering, or is it not suffering?” and so
forth.

“From that, false view.” From doubt, false view comes into
operation: for a person in doubt, wrong hearing and reflection
lead to a false ascertainment [the corresponding Sanskrit
compound is uncertain]. “There is no suffering,” and so
forth, is the example. “From that, the view of the Bases as
self.” From false view, it is said (`kila`), self-view comes
into operation: having excluded the Bases from the character
of suffering, one clings to them as self.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The opening question distinguishes the designation
`sānuśaya` from actual `anuśayana` in a Consciousness. A
not-abandoned anuśaya can lie latent through conjunction
with Consciousness or by taking it as Support. An abandoned
anuśaya conjoined with defiled Consciousness no longer lies
latent, but the Consciousness can still be called “bearing
anuśaya” through accompaniment (`sahitatva`). For
non-defiled Consciousness, the Bhāṣya gives only the route of
a not-abandoned anuśaya taking it as Support. The earlier
compressed `tadālambanāśca` is not enough to add another
ground to the later explanation.

The second half begins an order of arising for the ten
anuśayas. Ignorance confuses the truths; hearing opposed
alternatives gives rise to doubt; a false ascertainment
produces false view; and rejection of the Bases as suffering
allows fixation on them as self. These are successive
relations in the Bhāṣya's account, not identifications of
doubt, false view, and self-view. `Kila` marks the last
transition as reported; the unit does not establish an
exceptionless chronology of every individual thought.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Sānuśaya` is “bearing anuśaya,”
while `anuśerate` is “lies latent” or “adheres”; they are
not synonyms. `Anuśāyaka` names an anuśaya actually lying
latent. `Ālambana` is Support, including when Consciousness
is what an anuśaya takes. `Akliṣṭa` is “non-defiled”; it
does not preclude being taken as Support by an unabandoned
anuśaya. `Ākāṅkṣā` is explained as `vicikitsā` (doubt).
The prose supports reading `avidyāyyogāt` as conjunction
with ignorance, but the anomalous form is preserved. The
Chinese parallel gives wrong hearing and reflection as the
context of `mithyāniścaya`; it does not license a silent
Sanskrit emendation of `mithyāśramaṇacittānāṃ`.

**Organon reconstruction.** The first half requires separate
relations for a factor's conjunction with Consciousness,
its Support, its abandonment, and its actual adherence. The
second gives a provisional genetic sequence from confusion
through doubt and false ascertainment to self-view. This is
a project-level analytical use of the Kośa, not a claim that
the sequence exhausts all possible paths of thought.
