# VAK 5.32 Bhāṣya — Bearing Anuśaya and the First Genetic Sequence

## Kārikā Anchor

> dvidhā sānuśayaṃ kliṣṭam akliṣṭam anuśāyakaiḥ |
> mohākāṅkṣā tato mithyādṛṣṭiḥ satkāyadṛk tataḥ || 5.32 ||

> Defiled citta bears anuśaya in two ways; non-defiled
> citta does so through anuśayas that actually adhere.
> From delusion comes doubt, then false view, then self-view.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

If a citta is called `sānuśaya` through certain anuśayas,
do those anuśayas actually adhere in it? Some do: those
conjoined with the citta and unabandoned, and those that
take it as object and are unabandoned. Some do not: those
conjoined with the citta but abandoned, and those taking it
as object under the corresponding abandoned condition.

Thus “defiled citta bears anuśaya in two ways; non-defiled
citta through adhering anuśayas.” A defiled citta bears
anuśaya through unabandoned afflictions that adhere by
conjunction or object relation. It also bears an abandoned
but conjoined anuśaya that no longer adheres in the strict
sense, because the citta is accompanied by it. A non-defiled
citta bears anuśaya only through unabandoned afflictions
that take it as object and actually adhere.

Then the text asks how the ten anuśayas come into operation.
First, through conjunction with ignorance, one is confused
about the truths; suffering and the path do not commend
themselves to one. From delusion comes doubt. When a confused
person hears two sides, doubt arises: “Is this suffering,
or is it not?” From doubt comes false view, through a
false resolution of uncertainty; “There is no suffering”
is the example. From false view, it is said, self-view
emerges: one rejects the aggregates as suffering and
fixates on them as self.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The first half returns to a relation that the object matrix
alone could not decide. `Sānuśaya`, “bearing anuśaya,” has
a wider extension than an anuśaya's actual `anuśayana` in
a citta. Unabandoned factors can adhere through association
or through taking the citta as object. An abandoned factor
conjoined with a defiled citta can still warrant the broader
description `sānuśaya` through accompaniment (`sahitatva`),
though it no longer adheres in the stricter sense. A
non-defiled citta admits only the object-related route of
an unabandoned anuśaya. The passage thereby keeps association,
object relation, abandonment, and adherence distinct.

The second half changes from classification to an account
of progression. Ignorance clouds the truths; hearing opposed
alternatives leads the confused person into `vicikitsā`;
doubt may be settled falsely as `mithyādṛṣṭi`; and that
false determination can issue in `satkāyadṛṣṭi`. The
sequence does not identify doubt with false view or
self-view. Each is a further determination. The `kila`
before the final transition marks the explanation as
reported in the Bhāṣya's voice; its status should remain
visible rather than made an unqualified psychological law.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Sānuśaya` is rendered “bearing
anuśaya,” while `anuśerate` is “adheres”; they are not
treated as synonyms. `Anuśāyaka` identifies an anuśaya
actually adhering. `Akliṣṭa` is “non-defiled” here, not
an assertion that no unabandoned affliction can take that
citta as object. `Ākāṅkṣā` is explained by the prose as
`vicikitsā`. The uncertain `avidyāyyogāt` is read in
context as conjunction with ignorance, and the malformed
closing `ityevmādi` as “and so forth.”

**Organon reconstruction.** The first half requires separate
relations for a factor's presence with citta, its object,
its abandonment status, and its active adherence. The second
half then describes a genetic movement from confusion
through doubt and false judgment to self-identification.
That is a project-level analytical use of the Kośa, separate
from a claim that the sequence exhausts all possible paths
of thought or has been implemented in software.
