# VAK 5.50 Bhāṣya — Six Stains, Four Stated Sources

## Kārikā Anchor

> pradāśa upanāhaś ca vihiṃsā ceti rāgajau |
> māyā-madau pratighaje upanāha-vihiṃsane || 5.50 ||

> Obstinate fault-clinging, sustained resentment, and
> harmfulness complete the six stains. Deceit and
> intoxication are born of attachment; resentment and
> harmfulness are born of hostility.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Obstinate fault-clinging, sustained resentment, and
harmfulness complete the six stains. Of these, deceit is
deceiving another. Crookedness is crookedness of citta,
through which one does not disclose matters as they are;
the remainder of this clause is uncertain. Intoxication
was explained earlier. Obstinate fault-clinging is firm
adherence to a blameworthy matter, through which one does
not accept proper admonition. Sustained resentment is
repeated enlargement of a basis of injury. Harmfulness is
injuring others by striking, harshness, and the like.

Of the six afflictive stains, deceit and intoxication are
the two born of attachment; sustained resentment and
harmfulness are the two born of hostility. The source
affiliations of obstinate fault-clinging and crookedness
come in the next verse.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The source establishes the complete six-member class by
joining 5.49's deceit, crookedness, and intoxication to
5.50's obstinate fault-clinging, resentment, and
harmfulness. It then defines the factors before giving
four source relations. This prevents a factor's operation
from being confused with its origin.

| Stain | Local operation or status | Source stated here |
|---|---|---|
| Deceit (*māyā*) | Deceiving another | Attachment |
| Crookedness (*śāṭhya*) | Non-straight disclosure | Deferred to 5.51 |
| Intoxication (*mada*) | Definition referred back | Attachment |
| Obstinate fault-clinging (*pradāśa*) | Refusing proper admonition | Deferred to 5.51 |
| Sustained resentment (*upanāha*) | Enlarging a basis of injury | Hostility |
| Harmfulness (*vihiṃsā*) | Injuring others | Hostility |

`Rāgajau` and `pratighaje` state origins for two pairs.
They do not say that members of a pair perform the same
act. The prose also corrects a tempting but wrong reading
of *pradāśa*: the person firmly holds a blameworthy matter
and refuses correction; this is not defined here as exposing
someone else's fault. The text gives no further mechanism
for why attachment generates its two named stains.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** “Obstinate fault-clinging” keeps
the `dṛḍhagrāhitā` of the prose visible, while the rendering
of `nyāyasaṃjñapti` as “proper admonition” is provisional.
“Sustained resentment” renders the multiplication in
`āghātavastuvahulīkāra`. The `śāṭhya` clause securely
begins with `cittakauṭilya` and failure to reveal what is;
its remaining syntax should not be forced into a polished
definition from this witness.

**Organon reconstruction.** Membership in one class, a
factor's operation, and its stated source are three
different relations. Here all six members and most
operations are known, while only four sources are assigned.
The Science of Citta can preserve that partial but exact
state of knowledge. This is a project-level use of the
Bhāṣya, not an implementation claim.
