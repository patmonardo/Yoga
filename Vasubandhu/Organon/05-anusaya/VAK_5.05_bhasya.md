# VAK 5.05 Bhāṣya — From Thirty-Six to Ninety-Eight

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> catvāro bhāvanāheyās ta evāpratighāḥ punaḥ |
> rūpadhātau tathārūpye ity aṣṭānavatir matāḥ || 5.05 ||

> Four are abandoned through cultivation. Those same anuśayas, without
> resistance, occur again in the Form realm and likewise in the Formless
> realm. Thus they are reckoned as ninety-eight.

The half-verses are interleaved with prose. In `ta eva`, “those same”
resumes the completed Desire Realm classification, not merely the
four cultivation-abandoned kinds.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Four are to be abandoned through cultivation”: attachment,
resistance, conceit, and ignorance. They are abandoned through
cultivation of the path after the truths have been seen.

Accordingly, satkāya-view has one mode, being abandoned through
seeing suffering. Extreme-grasping view is the same in this respect.
False view has four modes, being abandoned through seeing suffering,
origination, cessation, and the path. The same holds for adherence
to views and for doubt. Adherence to discipline and observance has
two modes, being abandoned through seeing suffering and the path.
Attachment, resistance, conceit, and ignorance each have five modes:
they may be abandoned through seeing each of the four truths or
through cultivation.

What distinguishes those abandoned through seeing suffering, and
so on up to those abandoned through cultivation? Those whose
cognitive supports belong to what is abandoned through seeing a
given truth are themselves abandoned through seeing that truth;
the remainder are abandoned through cultivation. Thus there are
twelve views, four doubts, five attachments, five resistances, five
conceits, and five instances of ignorance. These are thirty-six
anuśayas belonging to the Desire Realm.

“Those same, again without resistance, in the Form Realm”: removing
the five modes of resistance leaves thirty-one in the Form Realm.
“Likewise in the formless domain”: there too are thirty-one.
“Thus ninety-eight are held.” In this way, the Abhidharmikas hold
that the six anuśayas amount to ninety-eight through distinctions
of aspect, mode, and domain.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The commentary completes the Desire Realm count begun in 5.04:

| Kind | Cases |
|---|---:|
| Views | 12 |
| Doubt | 4 |
| Attachment | 5 |
| Resistance | 5 |
| Conceit | 5 |
| Ignorance | 5 |
| **Desire Realm total** | **36** |

The two higher domains repeat the classification minus the five
resistance cases: `36 − 5 = 31` in each. Hence `36 + 31 + 31 = 98`.
The source explicitly calls this the Abhidharmikas' reckoning.

The prose's `evam antagrāhadṛṣṭiḥ` assigns extreme-grasping view
the same one mode as satkāya-view. The research excerpt and its
prose summary incorrectly group it with the four-mode false view;
the running source's sentence boundary corrects that reading.

The test for a seeing-abandoned type is its `ālambana`: what it
depends on as a cognitive support. A type whose support belongs to
the relevant truth's abandonment class is assigned to that seeing;
the remainder are assigned to cultivation. This is the text's
rule for generating the matrix, not mere arithmetic after the fact.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Mārgābhyāsa` is cultivation of the path
after the truths have been seen; it does not erase the distinction
between seeing and cultivation. `Ālambana` is rendered “cognitive
support” to keep its technical relational role visible. The
unusual forms in the e-text require comparison with another witness
before normalizing the Sanskrit.

**Organon reconstruction.** This unit shows how a basic determination
acquires a domain, a truth-specific removal condition, and a mode
of abandonment. The count is generated by those relations. Any
eventual formalization must preserve the source's distinctions
before using them in our Science of Citta; no software behavior is
claimed by this textual result.
