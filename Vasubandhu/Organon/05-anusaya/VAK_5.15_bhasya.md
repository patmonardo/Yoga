# VAK 5.15 Bhāṣya — Own-Level Cessation and the Path's Range

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> svabhūmy-uparamo mārgaḥ ṣaḍbhūmi-navabhūmikaḥ |
> tad-gocarāṇāṃ viṣayo mārgo hy anyo 'nya-hetukaḥ || 5.15 ||

> Cessation is confined to its own level. Those whose range is the Path have
> the six-level dharmajñāna Path or the nine-level anvayajñāna Path as their
> Condition, according to domain, since one path is a cause of another.

The first clause carries `nirodha` from 5.14. The Bhāṣya
distributes the six- and nine-level qualifications across the
two knowledge-sides of the path.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Now, concerning these further: for false view and the other anuśayas
that take cessation as Support, only the cessation of their own level
is their Support: for Desire Realm anuśayas, only cessation of the Desire Realm,
and so on up to those at the summit of existence, for which the
cessation of that summit alone is the Support.

For Desire Realm anuśayas taking the Path as Support, the whole
six-level Path on the side of `dharmajñāna` is their Support,
including its portion that counteracts the form and formless
domains. For afflictions belonging to those higher domains,
including ones at levels not directly seen, the nine-level Path
on the side of `anvayajñāna` is their Support. One path can be a
cause for another, so this Support has a wider reach than
own-level cessation.

An objection notes that `dharmajñāna` and `anvayajñāna` are
also mutual causes. The answer restricts the range: the
`anvayajñāna` side is not a counteragent to the Desire Realm,
so Desire Realm path-supporting anuśayas do not take it as
their Support.

But then, since `dharmajñāna` counteracts the form and formless
domains, should it be a Support for path-supporting afflictions
of those levels? No: the whole `dharmajñāna` side is not such
a counteragent. In particular, the `dharmajñāna` of suffering
and origination does not counteract them. Nor is it the
counteragent for all seeing-abandonable afflictions across both
higher domains. Because these and related counteragent conditions
are absent, it is not their Support.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The key contrast is local cessation versus a path articulated
across multiple levels. For cessation-supporting afflictions,
`svabhūminirodha` fixes the Support to the affliction's own
level. For path-supporting afflictions, the prose identifies
six-level `dharmajñānapakṣa` for the Desire Realm and
nine-level `anvayajñānapakṣa` for the higher domains.

The two objections prevent a crude “mutual cause means any path
can serve as Support for any path-related affliction” reading.
Causal connection matters, but so does the specific `pratipakṣa`
relation. `Anvayajñāna` does not counteract the Desire Realm;
the `dharmajñāna` side does not comprehensively counteract
the higher-domain cases. The technical range of `ālambana`
thus requires both the path's causal relations and its actual
counteragent scope.

The closing compressed sentence is difficult in the e-text.
The translation above gives its evident argumentative force,
which the Chinese parallel corroborates, while the exact
Sanskrit division of the higher-domain classes remains provisional
pending another Sanskrit witness. The source supports a restricted
conclusion, not a simple one-way contrast between
the two kinds of knowledge.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Dharmajñāna` and `anvayajñāna`
remain transliterated; rendering either as generic “knowledge”
would lose the allocation of six and nine levels. `Uparama`
is rendered “cessation” here in light of the prose's explicit
`nirodha`. The verse's `viṣaya` is Condition, its `gocara` is
range, and the Bhāṣya's `ālambana` is Support. Their local
relation does not make them lexical equivalents in every context.

**Organon reconstruction.** The source requires a relation to
carry its domain, level, causal reach, and counteragent scope.
Our Science of Citta can make use of that precision when it
relates cessation and path to knowing. No formal data model is
licensed merely by the verse's six- and nine-level numbers.
