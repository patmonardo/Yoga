# VAK 5.06 Bhāṣya — Who Abandons What, and How

## Kārikā Anchor

> bhavāgrajāḥ kṣāntivadhyā dṛggheyā eva śeṣajāḥ |
> dṛgbhāvanābhyām akṣāntivadhyā bhāvanayaiva tu || 5.06 ||

> Those born at the summit of existence and counteracted by kṣānti
> are abandoned only through seeing. Those born on the remaining
> levels and counteracted by kṣānti are abandoned through seeing or
> cultivation, according to the practitioner. Those not counteracted
> by kṣānti are abandoned only through cultivation.

The Bhāṣya supplies the distribution hidden in the verse's
`dṛgbhāvanābhyām`.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

It was said that certain anuśayas are to be abandoned through
seeing. Are they necessarily abandoned only through seeing? No.
What then? “Those born at the summit of existence and counteracted
by kṣānti are abandoned only through seeing.” The anuśayas born on
the summit-level that are to be removed by `anvayajñānakṣānti`
are abandoned through seeing
alone, not through cultivation.

“Those born on the remaining levels, through seeing and
cultivation.” The word “counteracted by kṣānti” continues to govern
this clause. On the other levels, the relevant anuśayas to be
removed by `dharmajñānakṣānti` or `anvayajñānakṣānti` are
abandoned through seeing for noble
practitioners, but through cultivation for ordinary persons.

“Those not counteracted by kṣānti, however, only through
cultivation.” On every level, anuśayas to be removed by knowledge
are always abandoned through cultivation for both groups.

Some others say that outsiders never abandon those anuśayas classed
as to be abandoned through seeing. They appeal to the
Mahākarmavibhāgasūtra: it reports the occurrence of views taking
the desire-domain as cognitive support among people free from
desire who speculate about the past, including eternalists,
partial eternalists, and proponents of uncaused arising.
Form-domain afflictions cannot, they argue, have the desire-domain
as support for people free from desire. These views must therefore
remain connected with the desire-domain and un-abandoned.

The Vaibhāṣikas answer that the relevant earlier afflictions fell
away at the very moment the views arose, as in the case of
Devadatta. The comparison is compressed; this local unit does not
explain the Devadatta case further.

## Argument and Voices

The first answer qualifies the previous classification. An
affliction's being called `darśanaprahātavya` does not entail one
invariant removal event for every practitioner. The precise
distribution is:

| Class | Practitioner | Mode of abandonment |
|---|---|---|
| Summit-level, kṣānti-counteracted | Applicable practitioner | Seeing only |
| Other levels, kṣānti-counteracted | Noble practitioner | Seeing |
| Other levels, kṣānti-counteracted | Ordinary person | Cultivation |
| Knowledge-counteracted, any level | Both | Cultivation |

This is the Bhāṣya's explanation of the instrumental dual
`dṛgbhāvanābhyām`: it distributes the two modes by practitioner
status, rather than requiring both at once in one case. `Kṣānti`
is a technical path term here; the prose contrasts what is
counteracted by kṣānti with what is counteracted by `jñāna`.

The closing controversy has distinct voices. `Apare` introduces
the objection about outsiders and the cited sūtra. `Iti
vaibhāṣikāḥ` explicitly assigns the final answer to the
Vaibhāṣikas. The compressed Devadatta analogy warrants no stronger
historical or causal reconstruction from this passage alone.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Ārya` and `pṛthagjana` are rendered
“noble practitioner” and “ordinary person” to preserve the
practitioner distinction on which the rule depends. `Bāhyaka`
in the appended dispute denotes an outsider to the Buddhist path;
it should not be silently equated with every ordinary person.
`Samudācāra` is the occurrence or active manifestation of a view,
not proof by itself of its uninterrupted persistence.

**Organon reconstruction.** The mode of correction is a relation
among the affliction, its level, its counteragent, and the
practitioner's status. A fixed label on an isolated affliction
would lose this relational determination. That is a conceptual
constraint for our Science of Citta, not a claim that the Kośa
already specifies a software architecture.
