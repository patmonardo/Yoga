# VAK 5.67 Bhāṣya — Path and Knowledge as Fruit Grounds

## Kārikā Anchor

> āryamārgasya sarvāḥ dve laukikasya anvayasya ca |
> dharmajñānasya tisras tu ṣaṭ tatpakṣasya pañca ca || 5.67 ||

> All nine *parijñās* are fruits of the noble path. Two
> are fruits of the mundane path, and two of
> *anvayajñāna*. Three are fruits of *dharmajñāna*;
> six belong to its side, and five to the other side.

The last “side” is supplied as *anvayajñāna-pakṣa* by the
prose. `Pakṣa` includes the corresponding *kṣānti* and
*jñāna* moments, not a second name for one knowledge alone.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

All nine *parijñās* are fruits of the noble path. Two are
fruits of the mundane path: the *parijñās* of abandonment
of the lower-part bonds and exhaustion of attachment to
form. Two are also fruits of *anvayajñāna*: the last two
*parijñās*. Three are fruits of *dharmajñāna*: the last
three, because it is a counteragent to afflictions to be
abandoned by cultivation across all three domains.

Six *parijñās* are fruits of the *dharmajñāna* side, namely
those belonging to its *kṣānti* and *jñāna* moments. Five
are fruits of the *anvayajñāna* side, those belonging to
its corresponding *kṣānti* and *jñāna* moments. Saying
“side” includes both *kṣānti* and *jñāna*.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The subject is still the same nine completed abandonments.
Verse 5.66 classified them by **meditative basis**; this
verse classifies them by **path or knowledge operation**.
`Āryamārga` is the inclusive class whose fruits are all
nine. The mundane path has two named fruits, and its
restricted range must not be confused with the ninefold
range of the noble path.

`Anvayajñāna` has the last two as fruits: exhaustion of
attachment to form and the final exhaustion of all fetters.
`Dharmajñāna` has the last three, including lower-part
abandonment. The prose's stated ground is its function as
counteragent to cultivation-abandonable afflictions in
**all three domains**. Its counteractive reach here cannot
be inferred merely from an English label such as
“knowledge of dharmas.”

`Pakṣa` enlarges the unit being counted. The *dharmajñāna*
side includes the corresponding *kṣāntis* and *jñānas* and
has six fruits; the *anvayajñāna* side has five. This is not
a claim that a single knowledge event suddenly has more
fruits. The Bhāṣya gives the count for the whole side,
`pakṣagrahaṇena hi kṣāntijñānāni gṛhyante`. It does not
here list each of the six and five separately; those
members should be reconstructed from the earlier ninefold
scheme in the later clarification pass.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** “Fruit” preserves `phala` as
the source relation rather than reducing it to a generic
causal edge. `Anvayasya` is specified as `anvayajñāna` by
the prose. `Paścime` means the last two in that case and
`paścimā eva` the last three in the *dharmajñāna* case.
The final `pañca` is assigned to *anvayajñāna-pakṣa* by the
following gloss. The source's `kṣantijñāna` spelling is
retained without silently equating *kṣānti* and *jñāna*.

**Organon reconstruction.** An achieved abandonment can be
considered as fruit of a comprehensive path, a specific
knowledge, or a larger knowledge-side. These relations are
nested but distinct. For our Anuśaya:Mārga reading, this
suggests representing the operative scale along with the
fruit relation. It is a project-level formal consequence
of the Kośa's explicit distinctions, not an additional
source taxonomy.
