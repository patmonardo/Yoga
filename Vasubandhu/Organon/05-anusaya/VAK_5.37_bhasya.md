# VAK 5.37 Bhāṣya — Views among Influxes, Floods, and Yokes

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tathaughayogā dṛṣṭīnāṃ pṛthagbhāvas tu pāṭavāt |
> nāsraveṣv asahāyānāṃ na kilāsyānukūlatā || 5.37 ||

> Floods and yokes follow the same classification, except that views stand
> separately because of their sharpness. Among the āsravas, views have no
> separate place, for unaided views, it is said, are unsuited to the settling
> function.

## 3. Continuous Translation

The floods and yokes should be understood in the way these
āsravas were stated, except that views have a separate place
because of their sharpness. The desire āsrava itself is the
desire flood and desire yoke; likewise the existence āsrava
is the existence flood and existence yoke, apart from views.
Views, it is said, are set apart among floods and yokes
because they are sharp.

Among āsravas, however, views are not separately established:
when unaided they are said to be unsuited to settling. The
derivation of *āsrava* from “they cause to settle” will be
explained later. Views alone, it is said, are unsuited to
settling because of their sharpness. Thus they are not
separately established among the āsravas, but are established
in mixture.

Thus the desire flood contains twenty-nine factors: fifteen
instances of attachment, hostility, and conceit; four of
doubt; and ten envelopments. The existence flood contains
twenty-eight: twenty of attachment and conceit, and eight of
doubt. The view flood contains thirty-six factors; the
ignorance flood, fifteen. The yokes are to be understood in
the same way as the floods.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The commentary changes the **partition**, not the inventory.
In the flood and yoke classifications, the thirty-six views
are removed from the desire and existence groups and given
their own class. Ignorance also retains its separate class.
The resulting counts are 29 + 28 + 36 + 15 = 108; the yoke
classes repeat this partition. The desire and existence
counts should therefore not be read as the full membership
of the corresponding influxes.

The same sharpness (`pāṭava`, glossed as `paṭutva`) explains
two different placements. It warrants a separate view flood
and view yoke. Yet views *alone* (`asahāyānām`, glossed by
`kevalā dṛṣṭya`) are not suited to the settling operation
invoked for an influx, so they are included there in
mixture. This is a claim about independent classification,
not the exclusion of views from the influxes. The two
`kila` qualifications present the explanation as received;
the passage does not pause to test its etymology.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** The second half of the verse is
elliptical. Its subject, views, and its contrast with their
separate place among floods and yokes are recovered from the
first half and the prose. `Āsya` is interpreted through the
commentary's `āsayanti`, whose fuller derivation is expressly
deferred. “Settling” is therefore provisional; a physical
picture of slow seepage is not asserted by this unit.
`Asahāya` specifies views considered without accompanying
factors; it does not mean they never accompany any.

**Organon reconstruction.** A single afflictive factor can
keep its identity while receiving distinct classificatory
relations. Here a view belongs to an influx in mixture but
is counted independently as a flood and as a yoke. The
relation and its stated ground have to be retained alongside
membership. This is a project-level formal reading of the
Kośa's classification, not an additional source doctrine or
an implementation claim.
