# VAK_5.70

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तासां संकलनं धातुवैराग्यफललाभतः ।
एकां द्वे पञ्च षट् कश्चिज्जहात्याप्नोति पञ्च न ॥ ५.७० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
tāsāṃ saṃkalanaṃ dhātu-vairāgya-phala-lābhataḥ |
ekāṃ dve pañca ṣaṭ kaścij jahāty āpnoti pañca na || 5.70 ||
```

VAK 5.70 answers the question deferred by 5.69: why are several previously
possessed parijñās later established as only one? It then closes the parijñā
discussion and the Anuśayanirdeśa by specifying the possible relinquishment
and acquisition counts.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
tāsām | saṃkalanam | dhātu-vairāgya-phala-lābhataḥ |
ekām | dve | pañca | ṣaṭ | kaścit | jahāti | āpnoti | pañca | na
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `tāsām` | genitive plural feminine | of those parijñās |
| `saṃkalanam` | nominative singular neuter | consolidation, establishment together as one |
| `dhātu-vairāgya-phala-lābhataḥ` | ablative compound | through dispassion toward a domain and attainment of a fruit |
| `ekām` | accusative singular feminine | one parijñā |
| `dve` | accusative dual feminine | two parijñās |
| `pañca` | accusative plural | five parijñās |
| `ṣaṭ` | accusative plural | six parijñās |
| `kaścit` | nominative singular masculine | some practitioner, a certain person |
| `jahāti` | third-person singular present of `√hā` | relinquishes |
| `āpnoti` | third-person singular present of `ā-√āp` | obtains or acquires |
| `pañca na` | numeral with negation | but not five |

The second half is elliptical. The Bhāṣya fixes the permitted counts:
relinquishment of one, two, five, or six; acquisition of one, two, or six,
never five.

## 4. Grammar

The first sentence states a causal rule:

```text
tāsāṃ parijñānāṃ saṃkalanam
    dhātu-vairāgyataḥ
    phala-lābhataḥ ca
```

The singular `saṃkalanam` is glossed by the prose as `ekatvena vyavasthāpanam`,
establishment as one. At the relevant stages, domain-dispassion and fruit-
attainment coincide; all the relevant abandonments are gathered and one
parijñā is designated.

The second sentence coordinates objects of `jahāti` and then contrasts the
elliptical acquisition series:

```text
kaścit
    ekām, dve, pañca, ṣaṭ [parijñāḥ] jahāti

kaścit
    [ekām, dve, ṣaṭ] āpnoti
    pañca na [āpnoti]
```

The asymmetry is grammatical and doctrinal: five belongs to the relinquishment
set but not the acquisition set.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Their consolidation occurs through dispassion toward a domain and through
> attainment of a fruit. A certain practitioner relinquishes one, two, five,
> or six; one acquires [one, two, or six], but not five.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Previously distinct parijñās are established as one when dispassion toward
> an entire domain and attainment of a Path-fruit occur; the relevant
> abandonments are then gathered under one parijñā designation. Depending on
> the Path-transition, a practitioner may relinquish one, two, five, or six
> parijñās, and may acquire one, two, or six—but no one newly acquires five.

Relinquishing a parijñā in this accounting does not itself mean that the
affliction destroyed by the earlier Path is thereby destroyed a second time
or that every prior liberative effect is simply reversed.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A completed Path-state is not represented by indefinitely adding every
> earlier designation. When an entire domain loses its binding force and a
> fruit is attained, previously distinguished abandonments are established as
> one comprehensive parijñā. The historical destructions, acquisitions of
> disconnection, technical completion-designations, and presently possessed
> set must therefore remain distinct. A numerical decrease can express a new
> classification of achieved scope rather than a return of the original
> affliction.

The source defines consolidation as establishment as one; it does not here
warrant a stronger claim about ontological fusion or the fate of every earlier
acquisition.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Saṃkalana` — consolidation**

The Bhāṣya's exact gloss is `ekatvena vyavasthāpana`, establishment as one.
All relevant abandonments are gathered and one parijñā is designated. This is
securely a technical classification of completed abandonments; deeper claims
about fusion of dharmas are not made here.

**`Dhātu-vairāgya` — dispassion toward a domain**

One trigger of consolidation. It concerns freedom from the binding range of
an entire domain, not merely temporary absence of one local affliction.

**`Phala-lābha` / `phala-prāpti` — attainment of a fruit**

The second trigger. At the relevant states, domain-dispassion and fruit-
attainment occur together, licensing establishment of the abandonments as one
parijñā.

**`Jahāti` — relinquishes**

Loss of a parijñā from the currently possessed technical set. Its meaning is
stage-sensitive and must not be equated without qualification with reversal
of the original affliction-destruction.

**`Āpnoti` / `labhate` — acquires**

Entry of a parijñā into the current possession-set. The acquisition counts
are one, two, or six; five is excluded.

**One relinquished**

One who falls from arhatship or from desire-domain dispassion relinquishes one
parijñā.

**Two relinquished**

A non-returner free from passion for form who falls from desire-domain
dispassion relinquishes two.

**Five relinquished**

A `vītarāgapūrvī`, one previously free from passion, relinquishes five at
`mārga-anvayajñāna` upon acquisition of the lower-part-fetter abandonment
parijñā.

**Six relinquished**

An `ānupūrvika`, one proceeding in regular sequence, relinquishes six upon
attainment of desire-domain dispassion.

**The acquisition cases**

One acquires a parijñā not previously possessed; two are acquired by one who
falls solely from formless-domain dispassion; six are acquired by one who
falls from the fruit of non-returning. No transition produces five as a newly
acquired possession-set.

## 8. Logical Determination

The consolidation rule is:

```text
Attains(person, DomainDispassion(d))
AND Attains(person, CorrespondingFruit)
    → ConsolidateAsOne(
          RelevantCompletedAbandonments(person, d),
          Parijñā
      )
```

The layers remain distinct:

```text
AfflictionDestruction
    ≠ DisconnectionAcquisition
    ≠ ParijñāDesignation
    ≠ PresentParijñāPossession
```

Permitted cardinalities are asymmetric:

```text
RelinquishmentCount ∈ {1, 2, 5, 6}
AcquisitionCount    ∈ {1, 2, 6}
AcquisitionCount    ≠ 5
```

The clearest consolidation transition is:

```text
PreviouslyPossesses(person, FiveSeeingParijñās)
AND Acquires(person, LowerPartFetterAbandonmentParijñā)
    → RelinquishesCurrentCount(person, 5)
```

This does not entail:

```text
RelinquishesParijñāDesignation(person, p)
    → RepeatedDestructionOrRestorationOfOriginalAffliction(p)
```

The path-state graph is directed rather than numerically reversible: a set may
be relinquished even though the same cardinality is never newly acquired.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Answer to VAK 5.69.** Non-returner and arhat are established with one
parijñā because domain-dispassion and fruit-attainment gather the relevant
abandonments and designate them as one.

**Consolidation is an accounting relation.** The prose defines the operation
as establishment as one. It does not say that earlier afflictions return or
that distinct dharmas fuse into a new substance.

**Exact transition cases.** The Bhāṣya specifies who relinquishes one, two,
five, or six and who acquires one, two, or six. These are typed Path-person
and fall/attainment transitions, not arbitrary arithmetic operations.

**Why five is one-way.** Five occurs during the serial accumulation of the
Path of Seeing and can be relinquished when a wider completion is acquired.
No later transition generates it as a newly acquired possession-set. The
asymmetry belongs to the Path-order.

**Chapter closure.** The Bhāṣya explicitly concludes both the parijñā topic
and the fifth Kośa section, the Exposition of the Latent Afflictions. The
chapter ends by specifying how freedom from affliction is destroyed once,
acquired as disconnection, qualified as complete, presently counted, and
consolidated.

**Organon interpretation.** A later system must separate event history from
current canonical state:

```text
PathTransition
    triggeredBy {DomainDispassion, FruitAttainment, Fall}
    relinquishes CurrentParijñāPossession
    acquires CurrentParijñāPossession
    consolidates AbandonmentCompletion
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This models the source's state-accounting without back-projecting
“sublation” or another external category into the Bhāṣya.
