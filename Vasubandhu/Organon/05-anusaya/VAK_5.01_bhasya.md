# VAK 5.01 Bhāṣya — The Root of Conditioned Existence

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> mūlaṃ bhavasyānuśayāḥ ṣaḍ rāgaḥ pratighas tathā |
> māno 'vidyā ca dṛṣṭiś ca vicikitsā ca te punaḥ || 5.01 ||

> The anuśayas are the root of conditioned existence. They are six:
> attachment, resistance, conceit, ignorance, view, and doubt. These,
> in turn—

The suspended ending continues in 5.02. The present study ends with the
Bhāṣya's corresponding `te punaḥ`.

## 3. Continuous Translation

It was said that “the diversity of the world is born from karma.” Those
actions accumulate under the power of the anuśayas; without the anuśayas
they cannot bring existence into manifestation. Therefore one should
understand: “The anuśayas are the root of existence.”

For an affliction in operation performs ten functions: it strengthens
the root, establishes the continuum, prepares the field, produces an
outflow, brings forth karmic existence, takes up its own accumulation,
deludes with regard to the Support, bends the stream of
Cognition, departs from the wholesome side, and extends for the sake
of binding, in the mode of not passing beyond the realm.

How many anuśayas are there? In summary, six. Which? “Attachment,
resistance, conceit, ignorance, view, and doubt.” [The transmitted
`athāgrahaṇaṃ` is uncertain.] The reference to attachment serves to
indicate that the others also adhere to their Supports. This will be
explained later. “These, in turn—”

The sixth function, translated provisionally as “takes up its own
accumulation,” rests on the uncertain `arigṛhṇāti`. The final function
uses the likely sense of `bandhana` while retaining `pandhanārthaṃ` in
the Sanskrit above. The bracketed sentence records uncertainty in the
closing clause rather than silently assigning `athāgrahaṇaṃ` a meaning.

## 4. Argument and Doctrinal Determination

Vasubandhu recalls Chapter 4's thesis about karma and adds the condition
under which karma becomes productive: the anuśayas govern its
accumulation, and without them karma cannot produce `bhava`. This is the
Bhāṣya's reason for calling them the root, rather than a bare
restatement of the Kārikā's metaphor.

The text then describes ten functions of **an active kleśa**. Its
movement runs from strengthening a root and maintaining a continuum,
through karmic and cognitive effects, to departure from the wholesome
and binding. The passage moves between `anuśaya` and `kleśa`, but it
does not yet settle whether they name distinct entities or different
aspects of affliction. Later Chapter 5 argument must determine that
relation; a simple equation or a fixed latent-versus-manifest ontology
would anticipate it.

The sixfold enumeration is explicitly summary classification. The
closing `te punaḥ` is syntactically open and must be read with 5.02,
where attachment is divided. No prose from that next unit belongs to
this study.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Bhava` means conditioned existence here:
the prose specifies its production by accumulated karma. `Ālambana`
is rendered “Support” in both occurrences; it should not be merged with
Basis (*āśraya*), Condition (*viṣaya*), or Object, the comprehended truth
of a thing. `Vijñānasrotas` is the stream of
Cognition, not a term for consciousness (*citta*). `Anuśaya` remains transliterated
until the chapter's own analysis establishes its relation to active
affliction. “Adhere to” in the final explanatory sentence is a
provisional rendering of `anuśāyita`, not a claim that the six are
always manifest.

**Organon reconstruction.** The transition from Karma to Anuśaya is a
move from action's production of a world to the afflictive condition of
action's continuing efficacy. This is a project-level articulation of
the commentary's causal sequence, not a further claim made by the
Kārikā itself. The ten functions also show why a path must address the
orientation and reproduction of a continuum, rather than isolate one
visible episode of attachment or aversion.
