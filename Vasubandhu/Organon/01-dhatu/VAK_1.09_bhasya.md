## 5. Eleven

The faculties are five, named. The meanings are five, each its own, and the
commentary calls them conditions. *Avijñapti* is singular, and joined by
*eva ca*. Eleven. That is the whole of the form-base.

The reason is not in the count. *Tadvijñānāśrayāḥ* places it as what
the clarity supports. The reason of the eye, and the rest. Not a twelfth
member of form. Principle is not this word. Principle is Dhātu, and Dhātu
is not enumerated.

## 6. Clarity and Support

*Rūpaprasāda* is what the five are. A clarity of form, not a thing on the
desk. In order: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, supporting the reason of
visible form, of sound, of odor, of taste, and of the tangible.

The scripture calls the eye an internal essence, a clarity of form
depending on the four great elements. The essence is cited. It is not
enumerated. The dependence is *upādāya*. Not appropriation.

The other reading is the Prakaraṇa's: the eye is the clarity of form that
supports the reason of the eye. The commentary says the treatise agrees, so
taken. Both readings keep the support. Neither adds a member.

## 5. Eleven Members of the Form Base

`Etāvān rūpaskandhaḥ`, “this is the whole of the Form Base,” closes the
enumeration:

```text
five faculties
+ five meanings
+ one avijñapti
= eleven Form-Base members
```

The singular *avijñaptiḥ* contributes one member. The cognitions supported
by the faculties occur in the second line's relational determination;
they are not additional members of the Form Base.

The meanings are each respective to a faculty. The Bhāṣya calls them
*viṣaya* and identifies them as visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the
tangible. This is the specification of the five meanings, not an extra
five-member category.

## 6. Clarity and Support

The five faculties are *rūpaprasāda*, Form clarities. The Bhāṣya
connects each clarity to the cognition corresponding to its condition:

| faculty | condition / meaning | supported cognition |
|---|---|---|
| eye | visible form | eye-cognition |
| ear | sound | ear-cognition |
| nose | odor | nose-cognition |
| tongue | taste | tongue-cognition |
| body | tangible | body-cognition |

The cited scripture calls the eye an internal Essence (*āyatana*), a
Form clarity dependent on the four Great Elements. The Prakaraṇa reading
makes explicit that the eye is support of eye-cognition; the Bhāṣya
says the treatise agrees when read this way. Both statements preserve
support as the relation. Neither adds a member to the Form Base.

2. The meanings are respective conditions of the faculties.
3. The faculties are Form clarities and supports of corresponding

4. The supported cognitions are not additional members of the Form Base.
5. The scripture names the eye as an internal Essence and a clarity of

6. *Upādāya* here means dependence, not appropriation.
7. The five meanings are named; their further explanation remains ahead.

## 8. Interpretation

This is the first detailed specification of the Form Base within the Base
system. The count is exact: five faculties, five meanings, and one
*avijñapti*. The second line then crosses from what is included in the Base
to the sensory cognitions supported by its faculties. Those cognitions are
essential to the relation, but they do not enter the eleven-member count.

The Kośa explicitly articulates the Dharma System through the Defined
Concept `<Base, Essence, Principle>`. This passage enacts that system: it
specifies Form Base, cites the eye's Essence classification, and states the
support relation to Discriminative Cognition. Kant's principles disclose
their systematic unity.

The distinctions matter: a faculty is not an anatomical organ substituted
for *indriya*; a meaning is not a second inventory; and a supported
cognition is not another Form-Base member. The Form Base is one subsystem
of the larger Dharma System, not the whole system.

The Kośa explicitly presents *rūpaprasāda* (“Form clarities”) as the
transition from the sensory manifold returned by the faculties to the
clarity Saṃjñā presents through Manas, yielding True Meanings. This carries
the analysis beyond Form Base into Dharma Base. The same *avijñapti* is
classified in both; Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it and governs Manas.
Vijñāna joins and governs Perception and Conception: their unity is
Inconceivable as a homogeneous operation, while its Idea is disclosed in
Vijñāna-skandha.
