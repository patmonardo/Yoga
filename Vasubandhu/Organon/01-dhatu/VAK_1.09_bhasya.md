# VAK_1.09 Bhāṣya — The Form-Base

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> rūpaṃ pañcendriyāṇy arthāḥ pañcāvijñaptir eva ca /
> tadvijñānāśrayā rūpaprasādāś cakṣurādayaḥ // 1.09 //

> The Form Base comprises five Faculties—eye, ear, nose, tongue, and
> body—their five corresponding meanings—visible Form, sound, odor, taste,
> and the tangible—and *avijñapti*. The five Faculties are clarities of
> Form, each serving as the support of the Cognition corresponding to its
> meaning.

The commentary enumerates, cites the scripture, and aligns the Prakaraṇa. The five meanings are the next verse. They are not taken here.

Span: Pradhan `[005|21]`–`[006|07]`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

It has been said: five bases, beginning with form. Among them, form is the five faculties, the five meanings, and *avijñapti*.

The five faculties are the eye, the ear, the nose, the tongue, and the body.

The five meanings are the five conditions of those very faculties, beginning with the eye, each its own, called visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the tangible.

And *avijñapti*. So much is the form-base.

There, those five meanings, beginning with visible form, have been stated. The clarities of form, beginning with the eye, are supports of the corresponding cognition. The five clarities, whose nature is form, that are supports of the cognitions of visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the tangible, are to be known in order as eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body.

As the Blessed One said: “The eye, monk, is an internal essence, a clarity of form depending on the four Great Elements,” and the rest.

What have been stated, beginning with the eye, are supports of the corresponding cognition, clarities of form, beginning with the eye. The meaning is: supports of the cognition of the eye and the rest. So taken, the Prakaraṇa too is in agreement: “What is the eye? A clarity of form that is the support of the cognition of the eye,” and the rest.

The five faculties have been indicated. The five meanings are to be indicated.

## 5. Interpretation

Marked. Not the conventional line.

The base starts here. Eleven members, and the cognition not among them. The faculty supports the cognition. It does not become it. Faculty and meaning are the two sides. The commentary’s condition is the gloss, not a second list. The eye is a clarity, and a support. It does not see a finished object.

*Vijñāna* on this page is cognition. Principle is not this verse. The eye is already called an internal essence. That citation is the door, not the enumeration.

**Grammar–Logic pilot.** The paired kārikā now parses the enumeration and
the Faculty predication with the Chapter 8 feature-grammar pattern.
Lambda semantics composes the Faculty definition; separate FOL records
the exhaustive Form classification and the five corresponding support
relations. The finite model distinguishes Form Base from visible Form and
support from identity. It does not infer that a Faculty alone suffices to
produce Cognition.

For the Organon inquiry, the important opening is positive: Form includes
the capacities supporting Cognition, not only what is visibly presented.
The cited internal Essence and its dependence on the four Great Elements
require further determination. Neither an anti-metaphysical prohibition
nor a completed transcendental theory is supplied by this unit.

## 6. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. *Rūpa* is form, the form-base, eleven.
2. *Indriya* is faculty. Organ is not this page.
3. *Artha* is meaning, lowercase. *Viṣaya* is the gloss, condition, not a second list.
4. *Avijñapti* is named, not defined.
5. *Rūpa-prasāda* is clarity of form.
6. *Vijñāna* is cognition. It is not a member.
7. *Upādāya* in the citation is depending on the four Great Elements. It is not the appropriation of 1.08.
