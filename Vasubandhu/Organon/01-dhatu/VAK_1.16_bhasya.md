# VAK_1.16 Bhāṣya — Cognition Base, Mind Essence, and the Principles

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vijñānaṃ prativijñaptiḥ mana āyatanaṃ ca tat /
>
> dhātavaḥ sapta ca matāḥ ṣaḍvijñānāny atho manaḥ // 1.16 //

> Cognition is apprehension with respect to each object. That is also
> Mind Essence, and seven Principles are accepted: six Cognition
> Principles and Mind Principle.

The Bhāṣya explains `prativijñapti` as apprehension with respect to each
object. It then classifies the same vijñāna-skandha through āyatana and
dhātu. Its closing summary establishes five Bases, twelve Essences, and
eighteen Principles. The
conventional account and the Organon Sciences of Base, Essence, and
Principle are kept distinct below.

## 3. Continuous Textual Translation

> Cognition is apprehension with respect to each object. Apprehension,
> *upalabdhi*, with respect to each object is called the Cognition Base.
> It comprises six classes of cognition, from eye-cognition through
> mind-cognition. That Cognition Base which has been stated is Mind Essence
> in the Science of Essence. In the Science of Principles, that same Base
> is determined as seven Principles. Which seven? The six Cognition
> Principles and Mind Principle: the Eye-Cognition Principle through the
> Mind-Cognition Principle, together with Mind Principle.
>
> Thus five Bases, twelve Essences, and eighteen Principles are determined
> here. Form Base, apart from *avijñapti*, accounts for ten sensory Essences
> and ten sensory Principles. The three Bases beginning with Vedanā, together
> with *avijñapti* and the unconditioned, are determined as Dharma Essence
> and Dharma Principle. Cognition Base is determined as Mind Essence, the
> six Cognition Principles, and Mind Principle.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The passage first determines object-directed apprehension, then its six
groups, then classifies Cognition Base under āyatana and dhātu. A summary
gathers the preceding classificatory work:

```text
vijñāna
    → apprehension with respect to each object
    → six groups, eye-cognition through mind-cognition
    → mind-base under āyatana
    → seven Principles under Dhātu

completed Science
    → five Bases
    → twelve Essences
    → eighteen Principles
```

The question `katame sapta`, “which seven?”, is answered directly by the
six vijñāna-Principles and manodhātu. No separately speaking opponent or
school attribution occurs in this unit. The apparent difficulty of counting
seven from six becomes the next unit's explicit question.

## 5. Apprehension with Respect to Each Object

`Prativijñapti` receives a syntactic expansion:
`viṣayaṃ viṣayaṃ prati vijñaptiḥ`. The repeated accusative
`viṣayaṃ viṣayam` is distributive, “each object,” and `prati` states the
relation with respect to that object. `Upalabdhiḥ`, apprehension, supplies
the direct explanatory gloss.

The Bhāṣya's syntactic explanation fixes the conventional rendering as
object-specific apprehension. In the Organon reading, however, *prati*
marks the relation through which *vijñapti* is raised: a particular
apprehension is mapped to its universal Principle. This is Higher Reflection
and Principle Science, not a claim that the dictionary meaning of *prati*
alone contains the entire operation.

The local use of `vijñapti` also needs to be distinguished from the outward
manifestation discussed in 1.11–1.13. Here its gloss is apprehension. The
shared word does not make this apprehension an act of communicating an
intention to another person.

The distributive wording does not impose one isolated material atom per
act of apprehension. In 1.10, the commentary allowed apprehension of a
collection while retaining the own-character of its sensory faculty.
“Each object” here specifies the Bhāṣya's distributive apprehension; it
does not reverse that account of a composite sensory field. The Organon
determination of the Science of Principles is stated separately below.

## 6. Cognition Base, Mind Essence, Seven Principles

The singular `saḥ` resumes the *vijñānaskandha*, Cognition Base,
which the prose divides into `ṣaḍ vijñānakāyāḥ`, six groups. The stated
endpoints, eye-cognition and mind-cognition, delimit the familiar sixfold
series. “Groups” retains the classificatory sense of `kāya`; the word alone
does not establish a particular temporal mechanism of succession.

`Ya eṣa vijñānaskandha uktaḥ` fixes the subject of the next statement.
The verse's `tat`, “that,” refers to Cognition Base already defined. That
same Base is named `mana āyatanam`, mind-base, in the
āyatana classification. In the Organon Science of Essence this is Mind Essence, a
Capacity/Field and Essential Relation, not a substance. The statement does
not posit an independent mind-substance; it classifies the same
Cognition Base under another relation.

The repeated `sa eva`, “that same,” maintains the subject in the dhātu
classification. It supplies seven positions: the six Cognition Principles
(*vijñānadhātus*) together with Mind Principle (*manodhātu*). In the Organon
terminology these are six Cognition Principles and Mind Principle.
Mind-Cognition Principle (*manovijñānadhātu*) belongs to the six; it is
distinct from the additional Mind Principle (*manodhātu*).

The text has not yet explained how Cognition Base is related to six
Cognition Principles alongside Mind Principle, nor has it described
the specific function of *manodhātu*. That explanatory work belongs to
the following inquiry in 1.17. Here the exact count and referents are
preserved without importing the next argument prematurely.

## 7. The Completed Map and Its Limits

The concluding prose states the distribution precisely:

| Base (Skandha) | Essence (Āyatana) | Principle (Dhātu) |
|---|---|---|
| Form Base apart from *avijñapti* | ten sensory Essences | ten sensory Principles |
| Vedanā Base, Saṃjñā Base, Saṃskāra Base, *avijñapti*, and the unconditioned | Dharma Essence (*dharmāyatana*) | Dharma Principle (*dharmadhātu*) |
| Cognition Base (*vijñānaskandha*) | Mind Essence (*mana-āyatana*) | six Cognition Principles and Mind Principle (*manodhātu*) |
| total | twelve Essences | eighteen Principles |

The first row excludes *avijñapti* from the ten sensory Essences and
Principles associated with Form Base; it does not remove *avijñapti* from
Form Base. The second places it alongside the three named Bases and the
unconditioned under Dharma Essence and Dharma Principle. The third gives
one Essence and seven Principles for Cognition Base. The Sanskrit names the three determinations *skandha*, *āyatana*, and
*dhātu*; the Organon states them as Base, Essence, and Principle. Thus the
arithmetic is
`10 + 1 + 1 = 12` and
`10 + 1 + 7 = 18`.

The five Bases are Form, Feeling, Reflection, Formations, and Cognition.
The unconditioned have no Base determination here. *Avijñapti* retains its
Form Base determination while also entering Dharma Essence and Dharma
Principle. The map records one content under its distinct projections, not
additional substances.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. `Prativijñapti` means apprehension with respect to each object, explicitly
   glossed by `upalabdhi`.
2. The six groups distinguish eye-cognition through mind-cognition within
   the vijñānaskandha.
3. `Tat` refers to that same Base: in the Science of Essence it is
   mana-āyatana, Mind Essence in the Organon reading.
4. Cognition Base is determined through six Cognition Principles
   (*vijñānadhātus*) and Mind Principle (*manodhātu*). Their relation is
   reserved for 1.17.
5. The completed summary excludes avijñapti from the ten non-Dharma
   āyatana and dhātu positions associated with rūpa-skandha, while also
   placing it in dharmāyatana and dharmadhātu.
6. The unconditioned remain within those latter classifications without
   becoming constituents of a base.

## 9. Organon Reading: Transcendental and Speculative Reasoning

The textual claim remains exact: the Bhāṣya glosses
*prativijñapti* as *upalabdhi* with respect to each *viṣaya*.
The Organon asks what scientific operation this relation
supports. Here *vijñapti* is Higher Reflection (*saṃjñā*);
*prativijñapti* is its real, object-relative movement, in
which the particular is mapped to a universal Principle.
*Vijñāna* is Higher Vijñapti as Principle Science, not
consciousness (*citta*). It operates on the Plane of Dhātus:
its Principles are universal starting points for reasoning.

```text
particular object / condition (viṣaya)
    → prativijñapti: Higher Reflection relating particular to universal
    → Principle Science (vijñāna)
    → universal Principle as starting point
    → Rule System as inferential middle
    → singular next-step judgment / valuation
```

This movement has three distinct philosophical articulations.
Kant's synthetic unity of apperception names the
transcendental condition under which a particular is
determinable as an objective case under a universal rule; it
is not empirical *citta* or a lexical meaning of *vijñāna*.
Fichte's *Tathandlung* names the self-positing act of Absolute
Knowing, the return from the apparently given case to the
self-relating activity that posits and reconstructs its
conditions—not an empirical ego or substance. Hegel's
speculative syllogism of the Concept (*Begriff*) mediates
Universal, Particular, and Singular: the Rule System is the
inferential middle through which universal Principle and
particular conditions determine the singular next-step
judgment. These are Organon constructions, not historical
attributions to Vasubandhu or lexical meanings of the Sanskrit.

The Bhāṣya's `tat` and `sa eva` preserve the same
*vijñānaskandha* through the Sciences of Base, Essence, and
Principle. Its Essence is *mana-āyatana*: Mind Essence as
Capacity/Field and Essential Relation, Reflective Science,
the Seer as Reflector—not a substance. Manas is the Produced
Cognitive Instrument, equipped with Mind Essence and Mind
Principle; it is not identical with either.

The closing summary preserves distinct Base paths. Form Base
apart from *avijñapti* is distributed across ten sensory
Essences and ten sensory Principles. Vedanā Base, Saṃjñā
Base, and Saṃskāra Base, together with *avijñapti* and the
unconditioned, are classified under Dharma Essence
(*dharmāyatana*) and Dharma Principle (*dharmadhātu*).
*Avijñapti* retains its Form Base determination while entering
both Dharma projections; the unconditioned have no Base
determination here. Cognition Base has Mind Essence and six
Cognition Principles plus Mind Principle. The crosswalk is
one system of distinct determinations, not a flat taxonomy
and not a migration of substances.

Within the six Cognition Principles, *manovijñānadhātu* is
Mind-Cognition Principle, the project's Transcendental
Logic / Abstract Reason. The additional *manodhātu* is Mind
Principle, Ordinary Logic. Their relation is the next verse's
dialectical problem.
