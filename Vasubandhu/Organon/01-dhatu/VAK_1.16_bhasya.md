# VAK_1.16 Bhāṣya — Principle, Mind-Essence, and the Seven

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vijñānaṃ prativijñaptiḥ mana āyatanaṃ ca tat /
>
> dhātavaḥ sapta ca matāḥ ṣaḍvijñānāny atho manaḥ // 1.16 //

Literal:

> Principle is respective apprehension. That is also the mind-essence. Seven
> principles are accepted: the six principles, and mind.

Bhāṣya-informed:

> Principle is apprehension with respect to each condition. The principle-base
> is six groups, from the eye-principle through the mind-group principle. That
> same base is the mind-essence. As principles, seven are accepted: those six,
> and the mind-principle.

The commentary glosses the definition, divides the base into six, places that
same base as mind-essence, and counts seven. It then closes the classification.
The question whether the mind-principle is something beside the six opens the
next verse. It is not taken here.

The unit runs from the definition through the close. Span follows the running
source from the definition to the count. Word division is editorial. The local
e-text has *saspta*, *bhavaṇti*, *dhatavaḥ*, and a damaged join of the two
mind-names. Read *sapta*, *bhavanti*, *dhātavaḥ*, and *manovijñānadhātur
manodhātuś ca*. The mark before *vijñapti* in the close is an avagraha:
*trayo 'vijñaptiḥ*. Not a critical text.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Principle is respective apprehension. Apprehension, *upalabdhi*, with respect
to each condition, is what is called the principle-base.

That, again, is six groups of principle: the eye-principle through the
mind-group principle.

The principle-base just stated is, in the essence-arrangement, also the
mind-essence.

In the principle-arrangement, that same base is seven principles. Which seven?
The six principles, and mind: the eye-principle through the mind-group
principle, and the mind-principle.

Thus five bases, twelve essences, and eighteen principles are set out here.
The form-base, apart from *avijñapti*, is ten essences and ten principles. The
three bases beginning with feeling, *avijñapti*, and the unconditioned are the
dharma-essence and the dharma-principle. The principle-base is the
mind-essence, the six principles, and the mind-principle.

*Katame sapta* is answered here. No opponent is named.

## 5. Each Condition

*Viṣayaṃ viṣayaṃ prati*. The repeated accusative is distributive. Each
condition. *Prati* is the respect. *Upalabdhi* is the gloss.

The word *vijñapti* is shared with 1.11. The gloss in this unit is
apprehension, not a notice sent to another. Each condition does not force one
isolated atom for each act. At 1.10 the commentary allowed apprehension of a
collection, the organ keeping its own character.

## 6. One Base, an Essence, Seven Principles

*Saḥ* resumes the principle-base and divides it into six groups. *Kāya* is the
group. *Ya eṣa* fixes *tat*: the principle-base already defined. In the
essence-arrangement it is the mind-essence. Same base. Not a mind-substance
beside the principle.

*Sa eva* carries that subject into the principles. The mind-group principle is
one of the six. The mind-principle is the further one. How the base stands to
both is the next verse.

## 7. The Close, and the Form System

| Base | Essence | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| form-base, apart from *avijñapti* | ten essences | ten principles |
| feeling, reflection, formations, with *avijñapti* and the unconditioned | dharma-essence | dharma-principle |
| principle-base | mind-essence | six, and the mind-principle |
| five | twelve | eighteen |

The arithmetic is `10 + 1 + 1 = 12` and `10 + 1 + 7 = 18`.

The form-base is the Form System. Five organs, five meanings, and the
continuity. Apart from the continuity, the system is the ten. The continuity
stays in the form-base and enters the dharma pair. One content, two
determinations. Not a further substance, and not matter set beside form.

A matter system would treat the ten as stuff, and form as a later appearance.
That is the opposite. The ten are organs and meanings. The principles of 1.12
are principles of form. The worldly names of 1.13 point at color and
configuration, which are form. Nothing in the close is matter.

The unconditioned have no base. They sit in the dharma pair.

## 8. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. *Prativijñapti* is apprehension with respect to each condition.
2. The six run from the eye-principle through the mind-group principle.
3. *Tat* is that same base. It is the mind-essence.
4. The seven are the six and the mind-principle. Their relation is the next
   verse.
5. *Avijñapti* is excluded from the ten and included in the dharma pair. It
   remains form.
6. The unconditioned sit in that pair and are not a base.
7. The form-base is a Form System. It is not a matter system.

## 9. Interpretation

Reflection takes the mark. The principle is the higher reflection: the same
work, raised so that a condition is determined under a principle rather than
left a stray impression. *Prati* is the respect in which that raising meets
each condition. Not a second word for reflection, and not consciousness.

The Form System is what the principle is set toward, on the side of form. The
organs and the meanings are the ten. The continuity is form that does not
inform, and so is not among the ten. The principle does not convert that
system into matter in order to have something to apprehend. Apprehension with
respect to each condition is apprehension of form, of the dharma pair, and of
the principle's own groups. The condition is not a material substrate.

*Tat* and *sa eva* refuse a second substance. Mind-essence is the
essence-determination of the principle-base. It is not the organ the others
serve, and it is not the mind-principle. Those are equipped. They are not
identical.

Calling the seventh a further cognition closes the question the next verse has
not asked.
