## 4. Grammar

Three grounds come first: one adverbial accusative of purpose and two ablatives of reason. Two nominative neuter singular clauses follow, each headed by *ekam*. The single passive *ucyate* closes both. Distinction is distributed to both names; primacy goes with Form; the gathering goes with Dharma.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Grounds span [0, 3); form naming [3, 6); dharma naming [6, 9); the root spans [0, 9). Distinction is distributed to both names. No claim that the Form Base is the Form Essence is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** For the sake of distinction, one Essence is called Form, because of primacy; and one, having the designation Dharma, is called so, because many dharmas and the highest are gathered.

*Ekam* is projected as "exactly one Essence bears this name," not as a count of Essences. Uniqueness is scoped to the name, not to the grounds: other Essences may share resistance or be dharma by own-nature without being called Form or Dharma. `CalledBy` is designation, not a speech event. The ablatives ground the names; they do not define the Essences.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For the sake of distinction, because of primacy, and because many dharmas and the highest are gathered, one Essence is called Form, and one is called Dharma.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> For distinction, one Essence is called Form because of primacy, and one is called Dharma because it gathers many dharmas and the highest.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

For distinction, one Essence is called Form, and one is called Dharma.

The Form Base holds ten Essences. Nine already have names: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, sound, odor, taste, tangible. The one that remains is called Form. Primacy is the other reason. It resists contact. It can be shown. The world already calls it Form.

The other Essence is called Dharma because of what it gathers. Feeling, Reflection, and Formations are gathered there. Nirvāṇa is gathered there, and not in the others. The many are not the highest. The highest is not the many.

Form and Dharma are the two names. The ten are not one Essence called Form. All dharmas are not one Essence called Dharma.

**Model checks.** Ten distinct Essences belong to the Form Base; nine have specific names, and the remaining one is the Form Essence. A second Essence called Form, or a second called Dharma, falsifies the derived formula; so does removing primacy from Form or the highest from the Dharma gathering. The prose identities are recorded but not derived from the verse tokens.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya distinguishes the naming grounds: Form is prominent through resistance, visibility, and ordinary usage; Dharma is a general name because the Essence gathers many dharmas, including feeling, reflection, and formations, and also the highest dharma, nirvāṇa. An alternative account based on twentyfold variety and three eyes remains attributed to 'others.'

The hinge is the repeated *ekam*. Form and Dharma are a dyad of names, not two words for the Essence arrangement. The three that stand between the Form Base and the Cognition Base are what the Dharma Essence gathers. They remain Bases. There is no sixth Base called Dharma.
