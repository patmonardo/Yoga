# VAK_1.24 — Naming the Form Essence and Dharma Essence

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विशेषणार्थं प्राधान्याद्बहुधर्माग्रसंग्रहात् ।
>
> एकमायतनं रूपमेकं धर्माख्यमुच्यते ॥ १.२४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> viśeṣaṇārthaṃ prādhānyād bahudharmāgrasaṃgrahāt /
>
> ekam āyatanaṃ rūpam ekaṃ dharmākhyam ucyate // 1.24 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

```text
viśeṣaṇa-artham | prādhānyāt | bahu-dharma-agra-saṃgrahāt |
ekam | āyatanam | rūpam | ekam | dharma-ākhyam | ucyate
```

**Compound and sandhi**

| Verse form | Analysis |
|---|---|
| viśeṣaṇārtham | viśeṣaṇa + artham |
| prādhānyād bahu- | prādhānyāt + bahu-; external sandhi voices `t` before `b` |
| bahudharmāgrasaṃgrahāt | bahu-dharma-agra-saṃgrahāt |
| dharmākhyam | dharma-ākhyam |

The Bhāṣya opens the last compound into two gathered contents:

```text
bahūnāṃ dharmāṇāṃ saṃgrahaḥ
    the gathering of many dharmas

agrasya nirvāṇa-dharmasya saṃgrahaḥ
    the gathering of the highest Dharma, nirvāṇa
```

“Many” and “highest” are separate determinations: the verse does not call
all the gathered dharmas highest.

| Form | Morphology | Force in the design |
|---|---|---|
| viśeṣaṇārtham | accusative neuter singular used adverbially | for the sake of distinction |
| prādhānyāt | ablative singular | because of primacy or prominence |
| bahu-dharma-agra-saṃgrahāt | ablative singular compound | because many dharmas and the highest Dharma are gathered |
| ekam āyatanam | nominative neuter singular | one Essence |
| rūpam | nominative neuter singular | Form; one Essence is named Form |
| ekam dharmākhyam | nominative neuter singular | one [Essence] is named Dharma |
| ucyate | third-person singular present passive | is called |

Project terms used here: *skandha* is **Base**, *āyatana* is **Essence**,
*rūpa* is **Form**, and *dharma* remains **Dharma**. Thus *rūpāyatana* is
Form Essence and *dharmāyatana* is Dharma Essence.

## 4. Grammar

The verse sets up two naming questions:

```text
ten Essences belong to the Form Base
    but one is named Form

many dharmas are gathered in Dharma Essence
    and one Essence is named Dharma
```

`Viśeṣaṇārtham` gives the shared purpose: each Essence position remains
distinct. The Bhāṣya says the ten are understood individually, arranged
through Condition and condition-bearing capacity
(*viṣaya-viṣayin*), not collapsed into one collective position.

The Bhāṣya assigns primacy (*prādhānya*) to the Form Essence. The other
nine Essences within the Form Base already have specific names: eye, ear,
nose, tongue, body, sound, smell, taste, and tangible Form. The remaining
visible Essence is therefore named Form. It is primary in ordinary
experience: it resists contact, can be seen and pointed out as “this, here,
there,” and is commonly called Form.

The Dharma Essence is named for what it gathers: many dharmas and nirvāṇa,
the highest Dharma. The Bhāṣya distributes the reasons precisely:
distinction applies to both names; primacy explains Form; gathering explains
Dharma.

The single *ucyate* carries both designations: one Essence is called Form,
and one is called Dharma.

## 5. Translation

### Close Translation

> For distinction, because of primacy, and because many dharmas and the
> highest Dharma are gathered, one Essence is called Form and one is called
> Dharma.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Within the ten Essences included in the Form Base, each position remains
> distinct. The prominent visible Essence is named Form because it is
> primary in ordinary perception: it resists contact, is visible, and can
> be pointed out as “this, here, there.” One Essence is named Dharma because
> it gathers many dharmas, including nirvāṇa, the highest Dharma.

The first rendering keeps the verse's compressed structure. The second
makes the allocation of its reasons visible.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Form Base contains ten Essences: five faculties and five corresponding
meanings. Each retains its own position; one is named Form because visible
Form is primary. Dharma Essence is named through its gathering of many
dharmas, including nirvāṇa, the highest Dharma.

**Organon reading**

> Within the Absolute Base, the Form and Dharma determinations are held
> together without being collapsed. The Absolute Base is Absolute Dharma
> itself, discriminating all dharmas through Form and Law. Verse 1.24 names
> the Form Essence through primacy and the Dharma Essence through gathering.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes membership from naming:

```text
MemberOf(x, FormBase)
    ↛ Named(x, Form)

GatheredIn(x, DharmaEssence)
    ↛ Named(x, Dharma)
```

The two names are determined differently:

```text
FormEssence
    → one distinct Essence within the Form Base
    → named through the primacy of visible Form

DharmaEssence
    → one Essence named Dharma
    → gathers many dharmas
    → includes Nirvana, the highest Dharma
```

The Bhāṣya also reports an alternative explanation from others:

```text
FormEssence
    → gross through its twentyfold variety
    → field of the fleshly, divine, and noble wisdom-eyes
```

This remains an attributed alternative, not part of the preceding account.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 1.23 orders faculties and their corresponding Essences and Cognitions.
VAK 1.24 names the Form Essence through primacy and the Dharma Essence
through its gathering of many dharmas and nirvāṇa. The two naming reasons
preserve difference within the system: Form is prominent, while Dharma
Essence gathers a manifold.

This distinction belongs within the Absolute Base, which spans and combines
Rūpa-skandha and Dharma-skandha. The Absolute Base is Absolute Dharma
itself, discriminating all dharmas through Form and Law. Here *dharma*
remains Dharma; it is not a translation of *āyatana* or a synonym for
Essence.
