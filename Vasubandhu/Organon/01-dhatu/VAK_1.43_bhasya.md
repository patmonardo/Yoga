# VAK_1.43 Bhāṣya — Both Eyes, Reaching, and Contact

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ubhābhyām api cakṣurbhyāṃ paśyati vyaktadarśanāt /
>
> cakṣuḥśrotramano 'prāptaviṣayaṃ trayam anyathā // 1.43 //

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Seeing may occur through one Eye or both, with clearer seeing through their coordinated operation. Eye and Ear disclose their Conditions without physical contact; Mind, being non-Form, cannot physically reach its Condition. Nose, Tongue, and Body operate through physical contact with their Conditions, though some teachers classify hearing as both contacted and non-contacted.

### Anchor note

The Bhāṣya names the other three as Nose, Tongue, and Body, and
explains “otherwise” as having Conditions reached through contact. It then
asks what reaching itself means. The ensuing atomic-contact debate belongs
to this unit, rather than to the next kārikā.

## 3. Continuous Translation

If the Eye sees, does one see visible Forms with one Eye or with
both? There is no fixed restriction here. One sees with both Eyes
also, because seeing is clearer. Abhidharma analysts say that one
also sees with both Eyes. For when both are open, seeing is clearer.
With one Eye open and the other half-closed, there is apprehension
of two moons and the like, not [merely] from alteration of one
[Eye]. Nor does the separation of the supports entail a separation
of visual Cognition, since Cognition is not situated spatially as Form
is.

If the Eye sees, the Ear hears, and so on through Mind cognizing,
are their Conditions physically contacted or not? Eye, Ear, and Mind have
Conditions not physically contacted. One sees visible Form from a distance
but does not see eye ointment placed directly on the Eye; one hears
sound from a distance. If their Conditions had to be physically reached,
the divine Eye and Ear would not arise here in meditators, just as
[such Faculties do not arise] in the case of the Nose and the others.

If the Eye has a Condition not physically contacted, why does it not see
every such Condition, including what is distant or screened off? — First,
why does a magnet not attract all iron it does not touch? The same
difficulty would also arise if the Eye operated through contact. Why
would it not see everything it touches, such as eye ointment or an
applicator-stick? Likewise, the Nose and the others have Conditions
reached through contact, but not every reached thing is disclosed:
they do not apprehend smells arising at the same time and the like.
In the same way, the Eye may operate without contact without
disclosing every Condition it does not contact. Mind, because it is
not Form, cannot physically reach its Condition.

Some, however, consider the Ear to have both contacted and
non-contacted Conditions, because sound is heard inside the Ear as well.
The remaining three, called Nose, Tongue, and Body, operate otherwise:
their Conditions are reached through contact. How does the Nose have a
Condition reached through contact? Because smell is not apprehended by
one who is not breathing.

What is this “reaching”? Arising without an interval. But do atoms
touch one another, or not? The Kāśmīras say that they do not touch.
Why? If they touched with their whole being, the constituents would
merge. If they touched through one part, they would have parts;
but atoms are partless.

How, then, is sound produced? — For this very reason [touching is
not admitted]: if they touched, one struck hand would stick to the
other, and one stone to another. — Why does an assemblage not
scatter when struck? — Because it is held together by the Wind
Principle. One Wind Principle operates to scatter, as in cosmic
contraction; another to hold together, as in cosmic expansion.

How, then, are the three called Faculties with Conditions reached
through reaching without an interval? — Their being without an
interval is precisely that there is nothing between them.
Alternatively, there is no fault in saying that assemblages touch,
since they have parts. On that account the following passage in
the Vibhāṣā also makes sense. Having asked whether what is touching
arises from a touching cause or from a non-touching cause, it replies:
“It depends on the cause. Sometimes something non-touching arises
from a touching cause, when it scatters; sometimes something touching
from a non-touching cause, when it accumulates; sometimes something
touching from a touching cause, when things already accumulated
accumulate; sometimes something non-touching from a non-touching
cause, as with dust at a window.”

The venerable Vasumitra says: “If atoms touched, they would persist
into the next moment.” The venerable one says: “They do not touch;
but where there is no interval, the designation ‘touching’ is used.”
And the venerable one's position is to be accepted. Otherwise,
if atoms had intervals between them, what would prevent motion
through the empty spaces between them, given that they are admitted
to be resistant? Nor are assemblages something apart from atoms.
Those very atoms constituting the assemblages are touched, just as
they are affected as Form.

Moreover, if a distinction of directional parts is imagined in an
atom, it would follow that it has parts whether it touches or does
not touch. If no such distinction is imagined, then having parts
does not follow even for a touching atom.

The supplied brackets expose elliptical connections. The closing
argument about directional parts is retained alongside the preceding
non-touching account; it should not be omitted to make the discussion
appear more uniform than it is.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The first question continues the preceding account of the Eye's
seeing. The answer expressly allows seeing with both Eyes and
attributes that view to the Abhidharma analysts. A second question
classifies the six Faculties by whether their Conditions are reached
through contact. The challenge about unlimited non-contact seeing
is answered by analogy and by showing that a contact account would
face a parallel difficulty.

The alternative account of hearing belongs to unnamed “some.” Its
reason, hearing sound within the Ear, follows immediately and is
restored here. The remaining three Faculties then prompt the
definition of reaching and the extended discussion of atoms.

The Kāśmīras explicitly deny atomic touching. A later alternative
allows assemblages to touch, and invokes the Vibhāṣā. Vasumitra and
“the venerable one” supply separately attributed formulations. The
latter is not named in this local passage, so no personal identification
is supplied here. The commentary expressly recommends that position,
then presses the relation of assemblages to atoms and the argument
about directional parts.

## 5. Both Eyes and the Unity of Cognition

The first line uses the instrumental dual: “with both Eyes.” The
particle “also” accords with the preceding statement that there is
no fixed restriction. The verse does not require both Eyes for every
instance of seeing. Clearer seeing is the stated reason for admitting
the two-Eye case.

The doubled-moon example is a report within the argument, not an
instruction for an experiment or a modern physiological explanation.
The reading “one Eye open” must be distinguished from the research
summary's “one Eye closed.” The brief negative clause does not warrant
an elaborate theory about intrinsic alteration of a Faculty.

The separation at issue is between the physical supports. The argument
denies that their spatial separation entails spatially divided
Cognition. It is not primarily about losing or interrupting one Eye,
and it introduces no third entity between the Eye and its Cognition.

## 6. Contact and the Limits of Cognition

| Faculties | Relation to the Condition | Explanation or qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Eye and Ear | Conditions not physically contacted | Distance examples and the divine Eye and Ear |
| Mind | Condition not physically contacted | Mind is not Form and cannot physically reach its Condition |
| Nose, Tongue, and Body | Conditions reached through contact | The Nose is illustrated through breathing |
| Ear, according to some | Both contacted and non-contacted Conditions | Sound is also heard inside the Ear |

Physical reaching concerns whether a Faculty contacts its Condition,
not whether a Condition exists or Cognition can occur. This is distinct
from the mental factor Contact. The passage explains physical reaching
as arising without an interval.

The magnet analogy blocks an inference from non-contact to unlimited
operation. It does not claim that the Eye operates magnetically. The
example of smells arising at the same time supplies a limit within
the contact classification: not every reached Condition is disclosed.
The classification of hearing belongs to the Abhidharma argument
presented here; it should not be rewritten as a modern account of
sound transmission. The divine-Faculty example is a premise of this
text, not an empirical demonstration supplied by this study.

## 7. Reaching, Atoms, and Assemblages

The answer “arising without an interval” shifts the question from
ordinary physical contact to continuity without a gap. The Kāśmīra
argument excludes two proposed modes of atomic contact: whole-to-whole
contact would merge the substances, while partial contact would
introduce parts into what is held to be partless.

The sound and cohesion questions test the consequences of this denial.
The replies invoke the unwanted sticking of things that strike one
another and the Wind Principle's holding or scattering function.
These are the text's arguments; they should not be expanded into a
claim that it establishes modern mechanics.

The assemblage alternative distinguishes wholes with parts from the
partless atoms at issue. Its four Vibhāṣā cases track whether contact
or non-contact in a cause corresponds to contact or non-contact in
the result. Keeping all four cases makes the citation intelligible
as an account of accumulation and scattering.

Vasumitra's next-moment consequence is stated without a full derivation.
The other venerable teacher treats “contact” as a designation where
there is no interval. The commentary prefers that position, then
requires both that resistance remain intelligible and that assemblages
not be treated as constituents other than atoms.

The final argument about directional parts returns to the original
objection. If directional differentiation establishes parts, it does
so whether or not contact is admitted. Without that differentiation,
contact alone does not establish parts. This challenges the sufficiency
of the earlier partlessness argument; it should not be converted into
an unqualified assertion that atoms literally touch.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Determinations

- “With both Eyes also” is non-exclusive.
- Separated physical supports do not spatially divide Cognition.
- The remaining three Faculties are Nose, Tongue, and Body.
- The alternative account of hearing includes its own stated reason.
- Non-contact operation does not disclose every Condition that is
  not physically contacted.
- Physical reaching is explained as arising without an interval.
- The atomic and assemblage discussion continues through the final
  argument concerning directional parts.

## 9. Interpretation

The verse combines two determinations: coordinated operation of both
Eyes can make seeing clearer, and the six Faculties are classified by
whether they physically contact their Conditions. The Bhāṣya denies
that either access mode implies unlimited range. Its continuation
distinguishes physical contact, continuity without an interval, and
the designation of touching in the atomic debate.

In the governing model, Vijñāna as Discriminative Cognition joins and
governs Perception and Conception. Their unity is Inconceivable as a
homogeneous operation, and its Idea is disclosed in Cognition Base.
Vijñāna governs Manas and guides the reading of Dharma Base. Form Base
and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti* distinctly; Vijñāna bears
a *prati* relation to it and remains distinct from consciousness and Mind.
