# VAK_1.43 Bhāṣya — Both Eyes, Reaching, and Contact

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ubhābhyām api cakṣurbhyāṃ paśyati vyaktadarśanāt /
>
> cakṣuḥśrotramano 'prāptaviṣayaṃ trayam anyathā // 1.43 //

> One sees with both Eyes also, because seeing is clearer.
> Eye, Ear, and Mind have fields not physically reached; the other
> three operate through contact.

The Bhāṣya names the other three as Nose, Tongue, and Body, and
explains “otherwise” as having fields reached through contact. It then
asks what reaching itself means. The ensuing atomic-contact debate belongs
to this unit, rather than to the next kārikā.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

If the Eye sees, does one see visible Forms with one Eye or with
both? There is no fixed restriction here. One sees with both Eyes
also, because seeing is clearer. The Ābhidharmikas say that one
also sees with both Eyes. For when both are open, seeing is clearer.
With one Eye open and the other half-closed, there is apprehension
of two moons and the like, not [merely] from alteration of one
[Eye]. Nor does the separation of the supports entail a separation
of visual Cognition, since Cognition is not situated spatially as Form
is.

If the Eye sees, the Ear hears, and so on through Mind cognizing,
are their fields physically reached or not? Eye, Ear, and Mind have
fields not physically reached. One sees visible Form from a distance
but does not see collyrium placed directly on the Eye; one hears
sound from a distance. If their fields had to be physically reached,
the divine Eye and Ear would not arise here in meditators, just as
[such Faculties do not arise] in the case of the Nose and the others.

If the Eye has a field not physically reached, why does it not see
every such field, including what is distant or screened off? — First,
why does a magnet not attract all iron it does not touch? The same
difficulty would also arise if the Eye operated through contact. Why
would it not see everything it touches, such as collyrium or an
applicator-stick? Likewise, the Nose and the others have fields
reached through contact, but not every reached thing is disclosed:
they do not apprehend co-arisen smells and the like. In the same way,
the Eye may operate without contact without disclosing every
unreached field. Mind, because it is not Form, cannot physically
reach a field.

Some, however, consider the Ear to have both contacted and
non-contacted fields, because sound is heard inside the Ear as well.
The remaining three, called Nose, Tongue, and Body, operate otherwise:
their fields are reached through contact. How does the Nose have a
field reached through contact? Because smell is not apprehended by
one who is not breathing.

What is this “reaching”? Arising without an interval. But do atoms
touch one another, or not? The Kāśmīras say that they do not touch.
Why? If they touched with their whole being, the constituents would
merge. If they touched through one part, they would have parts;
but atoms are partless.

How, then, is sound produced? — For this very reason [touching is
not admitted]: if they touched, one struck hand would stick to the
other, and one stone to another. — Why does an assemblage not
scatter when struck? — Because it is held together by the Wind
Principle. One Wind Principle operates to scatter, as in cosmic
contraction; another to hold together, as in cosmic expansion.

How, then, are the three called Faculties with fields reached
through reaching without an interval? — Their being without an
interval is precisely that there is nothing between them.
Alternatively, there is no fault in saying that assemblages touch,
since they have parts. On that account the following passage in
the Vibhāṣā also makes sense. Having asked whether what is touching
arises from a touching cause or from a non-touching cause, it replies:
“It depends on the cause. Sometimes something non-touching arises
from a touching cause, when it scatters; sometimes something touching
from a non-touching cause, when it accumulates; sometimes something
touching from a touching cause, when things already accumulated
accumulate; sometimes something non-touching from a non-touching
cause, as with dust at a window.”

The venerable Vasumitra says: “If atoms touched, they would persist
into the next moment.” The venerable one says: “They do not touch;
but where there is no interval, the designation ‘touching’ is used.”
And the venerable one's position is to be accepted. Otherwise,
if atoms had intervals between them, what would prevent motion
through the empty spaces between them, given that they are admitted
to be resistant? Nor are assemblages something apart from atoms.
Those very atoms constituting the assemblages are touched, just as
they are affected as Form.

Moreover, if a distinction of directional parts is imagined in an
atom, it would follow that it has parts whether it touches or does
not touch. If no such distinction is imagined, then having parts
does not follow even for a touching atom.

The supplied brackets expose elliptical connections. The closing
argument about directional parts is retained alongside the preceding
non-touching account; it should not be omitted to make the discussion
appear more uniform than it is.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The first question continues the preceding account of the Eye's
seeing. The answer expressly allows seeing with both Eyes and
attributes that view to the Ābhidharmikas. A second question
classifies the six Faculties by whether their fields are reached
through contact. The challenge about unlimited non-contact seeing
is answered by analogy and by showing that a contact account would
face a parallel difficulty.

The alternative account of hearing belongs to unnamed “some.” Its
reason, hearing sound within the Ear, follows immediately and is
restored here. The remaining three Faculties then prompt the
definition of reaching and the extended discussion of atoms.

The Kāśmīras explicitly deny atomic touching. A later alternative
allows assemblages to touch, and invokes the Vibhāṣā. Vasumitra and
“the venerable one” supply separately attributed formulations. The
latter is not named in this local passage, so no personal identification
is supplied here. The commentary expressly recommends that position,
then presses the relation of assemblages to atoms and the argument
about directional parts.

## 5. Both Eyes and the Unity of Cognition

*Ubhābhyām ... cakṣurbhyām* is instrumental dual, “with both Eyes.”
*Api*, “also,” accords with the preceding *nātra niyamaḥ*, “there
is no fixed restriction.” The verse does not require both Eyes for
every instance of seeing. *Vyaktadarśanāt* gives the stated reason
for admitting the two-Eye case: clearer seeing.

The doubled-moon example is a report within the argument, not an
instruction for an experiment or a modern physiological explanation.
The reading “one Eye open” must be distinguished from the research
summary's “one eye closed.” The ensuing brief negative clause does
not warrant an elaborate theory about intrinsic alteration of a
faculty.

*Āśrayaviccheda* here concerns the separation of the supports. The
argument denies that their spatial separation entails a spatially
split Cognition. It is not primarily about losing or interrupting
one Eye. Nor does it introduce a third entity between the Eye support
and its Cognition. The distinction supported here is between the
spatially separated supports and the Cognition depending on them.

## 6. Contacted Fields and the Limits of Cognition

| Faculties | Relation to the field | Explanation or qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Eye and Ear | Fields not physically contacted | Distance examples and the divine Eye and Ear |
| Mind | Field not physically contacted | Not-Form; cannot physically reach a field |
| Nose, Tongue, and Body | Fields reached through contact | The Nose is illustrated through breathing |
| Ear, according to some | Both contacted and non-contacted fields | Sound is also heard inside the Ear |

*Prāpta* and *aprāpta* concern physical reaching, not whether a field
exists or Cognition can occur. They must not be equated with the mental
factor *sparśa*, Contact. The
passage proceeds to give its own technical explanation of physical
reaching as *nirantarotpatti*, arising without an interval.

The magnet analogy blocks an inference from non-contact to unlimited
operation. The reply does not claim that the Eye operates magnetically.
The example of co-arisen smells and the like supplies a
specific limit within the contact classification: not every reached
field is disclosed. The research summary's broad capacity account
needs that explicit example.

The classification of hearing belongs to the Abhidharma argument
presented here. It should not be silently rewritten as a modern
account of sound transmission. Similarly, the divine-Faculty example
is a doctrinal premise of this text, not an empirical demonstration
supplied by this study.

## 7. Reaching, Atoms, and Assemblages

The answer *nirantarotpattiḥ* shifts the question from ordinary
“touching” to arising without an interval. The Kāśmīra argument then
excludes two proposed modes of atomic touching: touching wholly
would merge the substances, while touching partially would introduce
parts into what is held to be partless.

The sound and cohesion questions test the explanatory consequences
of this denial. The replies invoke the unwanted sticking of things
that strike one another and the Wind Principle's holding or scattering
function. These are the text's arguments; they should not be expanded into a claim
that it has established modern mechanics.

The assemblage alternative distinguishes touching wholes with parts
from the partless atoms at issue. Its four Vibhāṣā cases track the
relation between a cause's touching or non-touching condition and
the corresponding condition of the result. Keeping all four cases
makes the citation intelligible as an account of accumulation and
scattering.

Vasumitra's next-moment consequence is stated without a full derivation.
The venerable one's formulation treats “touching” as a designation
where no interval exists. The commentary's preference for it is
followed by two constraints: resistance must remain intelligible,
and assemblages cannot be treated as constituents other than atoms.

The final directional-parts argument returns to the original objection.
If directional differentiation establishes parts, it does so whether
or not touching is admitted. Without that differentiation, touching
alone does not establish parts. This challenges the sufficiency of
the earlier partlessness argument; it should not be converted into
an unqualified final assertion that atoms literally touch.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- Read “with both Eyes also” non-exclusively.
- Preserve the distinction between separated supports and Cognition's
  lack of spatial division like Form.
- Identify the remaining three as Nose, Tongue, and Body.
- Restore the reason given for the alternative account of hearing.
- Distinguish non-contact operation from disclosure of every
  field not physically contacted.
- Explain reaching through arising without an interval, rather than
  leaving “contact” undefined.
- Include the atomic and assemblage discussion through the final
  argument concerning directional parts.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The verse adds an access-mode determination to the Faculty's relation
with its field. Eye, Ear, and Mind do not physically reach their fields;
Nose, Tongue, and Body operate through contact. The Bhāṣya then blocks
the inference that either mode gives unlimited access: a Faculty has a
bounded field of operation.

The two-Eye discussion adds coordinated support. Both Eyes may support
clearer visual Cognition, but spatially distinct supports do not imply
spatially divided Cognition. The atomic discussion keeps physical
reaching, arising without an interval, and the designation “touching”
distinct rather than treating them as interchangeable.

For the Organon model, the verse specifies an operation within the
Principle Pipeline:

```text
Faculty
    → access mode
    → bounded field
    → conditioned Cognition
```

Here the visual Faculty relates to a visible Form field; Mind's field
is Essence and is not physically reached. This is a project model of
the Bhāṣya's distinctions, not the Bhāṣya's own terminology or a claim
that it formulates a theory of representation.
