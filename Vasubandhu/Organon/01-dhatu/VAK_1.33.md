## 4. Grammar

Two sentences. The first has the five Cognition Principles of 1.32 as understood subject: *avikalpakāḥ* is masculine plural, and the instrumental compound restricts the negation to two kinds of vikalpa. The second identifies the two: dual *tau* resumes them, and two feminine noun phrases answer, each with agreeing adjectives. *Sarvā eva* removes the concentration restriction that *vyagrā* imposes on the first.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Negation spans [0, 2); identification [2, 10); the root spans [0, 10). Two sentences. No claim that the five lack all vikalpa is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** [The five] are without vikalpa by examining and recollective vikalpa. Those two are: mental prajñā, unconcentrated; smṛti, all of it, mental.

The instrumental restricts the negation: the five lack only the examining and the recollective. They keep the intrinsic kind, vitarka, which 1.32 gave them. The name *avikalpaka* is therefore by the missing two, as the Bhāṣya's one-footed horse is called footless. Both identifications are biconditionals: examining vikalpa is exactly unconcentrated mental prajñā; recollective vikalpa is exactly mental smṛti, concentrated or not.

The instrumental restricts the negation. It does not deny the third kind.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> They are without vikalpa in respect of examining and recollective vikalpa. Those two are unconcentrated mental prajñā, and all mental smṛti.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five sensory Cognition Principles lack examining and recollective discrimination. Those two are, respectively, unconcentrated mental discriminative comprehension and all mental mindfulness.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The five are with the pair, and still called without vikalpa. The name covers two of the three. The one they have is vitarka, and that is the intrinsic kind. Examining is unconcentrated mental prajñā. Recollecting is all mental smṛti. Neither is a Cognition of the five.

The five do have prajñā and smṛti, as every Cognition does; theirs are not mental, so they are not vikalpa. What makes prajñā and smṛti vikalpa is association with Mind-Cognition.

**Model checks.** Vikalpa has exactly three kinds. The five Cognition Principles possess vitarka (intrinsic vikalpa) and also prajñā and smṛti of their own, which are not mental and so are not vikalpa. Mental prajñā is examining only when unconcentrated; mental smṛti is recollective whether concentrated or not. Giving one of the five an examining vikalpa, or making concentrated prajñā examine, falsifies the theory. The prose identities are recorded but not derived from the verse tokens.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya distinguishes intrinsic *vikalpa* (identified with *vitarka*) from examining and recollective discrimination, and compares the five Cognitions to a one-footed horse called footless.

The hinge is the instrumental. Without it, the five could not be with vitarka and without vikalpa. The horse is the commentary's own measure of the name.
