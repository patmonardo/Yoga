# VAK_1.10 Bhāṣya — Counts Within the Meanings

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> rūpaṃ dvidhā viṃśatidhā śabdas tv aṣṭavidhaḥ rasaḥ /
>
> ṣoḍhā caturvidho gandhaḥ spṛśyam ekādaśātmakam // 1.10 //

Literal:

> Visible form is twofold and twentyfold. Sound, however, is eightfold.
> Taste is sixfold. Odor is fourfold. The tangible has eleven as its nature.

Bhāṣya-informed:

> Visible form is twofold, color and configuration, and the same is counted
> as twenty. Sound is eightfold. Taste is sixfold. Odor is fourfold. The
> tangible has eleven as its nature: the four great elements, and seven
> further. The counts are the verse. How a principle takes one or many is
> the prose.

The commentary counts, then says how the taking works. *Avijñapti* is next.
It is not taken here. Span: Pradhan `[006|07]`–`[007|28]`.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> There, first: visible form is twofold. Color and configuration. Color is
> fourfold, beginning with blue; the others are its divisions. Configuration
> is eightfold, from long through uneven. That same visible-form essence is
> further said to be twentyfold. Namely: blue, yellow, red, white, long,
> short, round, circular, raised, lowered, even, uneven, cloud, smoke, dust,
> mist, shadow, sunlight, light, and darkness. Some include the sky as one
> color and recite twenty-one.
>
> Even is an even configuration. Uneven is an uneven configuration. Mist is
> fog. Sunlight is the sun's radiance. Light is the radiance of moon, stars,
> fire, herbs, and jewels. Shadow is where visible forms are seen. Darkness
> is the reverse. The rest, being easy, is not elaborated.
>
> There is a visible-form essence known by color and not by configuration:
> what is called blue, yellow, red, white, shadow, sunlight, light, and
> darkness. There is one known by configuration and not by color. The local
> line is damaged; it concerns a portion whose nature is bodily *vijñapti*.
> There is one known both ways: the remaining visible-form essence. Others
> say only sunlight and light are known by color, for a limit such as long
> is seen in blue and the rest.
>
Objection. How is one substance known both ways?

Answer. Because both are discerned there. This *vid* has the sense of
knowing, not the sense of existence.

> Then the same would follow for bodily *vijñapti*. The visible-form essence
> has been stated.
>
> Sound, however, is eightfold. Fourfold, as caused by taken-up or
> not-taken-up great elements, and as designated sentient or not sentient.
> Then eightfold, by the division agreeable and disagreeable. Caused by
> taken-up great elements, as the sound of a hand or of voice. Caused by
> not-taken-up great elements, as the sound of wind, trees, or a river.
> Designated sentient: the sound that is vocal *vijñapti*. Designated not
> sentient: the other. Others say there is also sound caused by both taken-up
> and not-taken-up great elements, as born of the conjunction of hand and
> drum. That is not to be accepted, just as one color-atom is not held to
> depend on two sets of four elements. Sound has been stated.
>
> Taste is sixfold, by the division sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter,
> astringent. Odor is fourfold, because pleasant and unpleasant are each
> even and uneven. In the treatise, however, it is threefold: “pleasant,
> unpleasant, and even.”
>
> The tangible has eleven as its nature. The tangible has the nature of
> eleven substances: the four great elements, smoothness, roughness,
> heaviness, lightness, cold, hunger, and thirst. The elements will be
> stated later. Smoothness is softness. Roughness is harshness. Heaviness is
> that by which things are weighed against one another. Lightness is the
> reverse. Cold makes the desire for warmth. Hunger makes the desire to eat,
> by designation of the effect for the cause. As it is said: “Pleasant is
> the arising of the Buddhas, pleasant the teaching of the Dharma, pleasant
> the concord of the Saṅgha, pleasant the austerity of the concordant.” In
> the form-realm, hunger and thirst are absent. The rest are present. Even
> if garments there are not weighed one by one, collected they are weighed.
> Cold that harms is absent. What favors is said to be present.
>
> Of this manifold visible form that has been stated, sometimes the
> eye-principle arises from one substance, when its kind is cut off, and
> sometimes from many, when there is no cutting off. As when one sees from
> afar an array of an army, of many colors and configurations, or a heap of
> jewels. The ear-principle and the rest are to be understood so. But some
> say the body-principle arises from at most five tangibles: the four great
> elements and one such as smoothness. Others say from all eleven.
>
Objection. Then, because the support is the collected, the five groups of
principles would have a general condition, not a particular-character
condition.

Answer. No fault. They are accepted as having a particular-character
condition with respect to the particular character of the essence, not the
particular character of the substance.

> This is examined. When the body and the tongue reach a condition at once,
which principle arises first? That whose condition is keener. When the
condition is reached equally, the tongue-principle arises first, because
the continuum is bent by the desire to eat.

> The five organ-meanings have been stated, and how they are taken.

## 5. The Twenty

Color is four: blue, yellow, red, white. Configuration is eight: long,
short, round, circular, raised, lowered, even, uneven. Eight further:
cloud, smoke, dust, mist, shadow, sunlight, light, darkness. Twenty. The
sky is not in the verse.

Known by color and not by configuration: the four colors, shadow, sunlight,
light, darkness. Known by configuration and not by color: the damaged line,
bodily *vijñapti*. Known both ways: the rest. The objection that one
substance cannot be both is met by the sense of *vid*.

## 6. Sound, Taste, Odor, Tangible

Sound is four, then eight. Taken-up: hand, voice. Not taken-up: wind, trees,
river. Sentient: vocal *vijñapti*. Not sentient: the other. Agreeable and
disagreeable double the four. Hand and drum together is refused. One
color-atom does not depend on two sets of four elements. Neither does this
sound.

Taste is six, named. Odor is four in the verse, three in the treatise. Both
stay.

The tangible is eleven: four great elements, smoothness, roughness,
heaviness, lightness, cold, hunger, thirst. The elements are later. Hunger
and cold are named from the desire they make. In the form-realm, hunger and
thirst are absent.

## 7. One and Many

The eye-principle arises from one substance when the kind is cut off, and
from many when it is not. An army, a heap of jewels, from afar. The same
for ear and the rest. Body is disputed: five at most, or all eleven.

The objection would make the five groups take a general condition. The
reply keeps the particular character at the essence, not at the substance.
Many substances, one essence. The count does not become a second system.

Tongue and body at once: the keener condition first. Equal: the tongue,
because the continuum is bent by the desire to eat.

## 8. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. Visible form is color and configuration, counted as twenty. Not
   twenty-one.
2. Sound is eightfold. Taste six. Odor four in the verse, three in the
   treatise.
3. The tangible has eleven as its nature. The elements are later.
4. *Vid* is knowing, not existence.
5. One or many substances do not make a general condition against the
   essence.
6. The five organ-meanings are stated. *Avijñapti* is not.

## 9. Interpretation

The working Concept of Meaning is what makes the count a science and not a
list to exhaust. Five meanings were named. This verse says what each
contains. Color and configuration stay a dyad inside one meaning. The
twenty do not found a second base.

The complete system is not this count. Essence is the field the commentary
already uses, *rūpāyatana*. Principle is what arises, from one or from many.
Neither is a member added to the five. Meaning holds the fruit. The triad
still holds the system.
