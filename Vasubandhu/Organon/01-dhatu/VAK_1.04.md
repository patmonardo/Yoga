## 4. Grammar

The feature grammar separates *sāsravānāsravāḥ* into its coordinated members. The nominal clauses supply a copula semantically, without adding a spoken token. The change of subject is decisive: dharmas receive the classification; the *āsravas* perform the persistence.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Division spans [0, 3); classification [3, 6); reason [6, 10); the root spans [0, 10). The locative *teṣu* anaphorically restricts to the conditioned other than the Path. No *ālambana* relation is generated by the verse tokens.

**Close construe.** Dharmas are impure and pure. Conditioned, excluding the Path, are impure, because the *āsravas* persist in them.

The change of subject is decisive. Dharmas receive the classification; the *āsravas* perform the persistence. The verse does not say that dharmas are impure because dharmas persist. The Bhāṣya tests this relation against *ālambana*: an *āsrava* can arise taking Cessation or the Path as *ālambana* without persisting in either. Those distinctions are not extra overt tokens.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Dharmas are impure and pure. Conditioned, excluding the Path, are impure, because the *āsravas* persist in them.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Dharmas are impure and pure. Conditioned dharmas other than the Truth of the Path are impure, because the *āsravas* persist in them.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A dharma's impurity is determined by the persistence of the *āsravas* in it, not by conditioned existence alone and not merely by being taken as *ālambana*. The conditioned Path is pure: conditioned operation and the persistence of impurity are distinguishable.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Kārikā, derived.** The root SEM states the exclusive division, the class specification, and the persistence reason. The Path-exclusion qualifies the conditioned dharmas; it is not a third interchangeable predicate. The *ālambana* counterexample and the purity of the Path are prose elaborations.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** Both verse translations retain *āsrava* and distinguish it from the classifying predicate impure. The study translation resolves *mārga* as the Truth of the Path from the Bhāṣya. "In them" preserves the locative.

The feature grammar, chart checkpoints, and FOL theories keep the change of subject explicit. Grammar identifies what persists and in what; the Bhāṣya shows why mere *ālambana*-relation is insufficient. The warranted determination is persistence of the *āsravas*, not persistence as such.
