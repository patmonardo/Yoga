# VAK_1.06 — Disjunction and the Obstruction of Arising

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> प्रतिसंख्यानिरोधो यो विसंयोगः पृथक् पृथक् ।
>
> उत्पादात्यन्तविघ्नोऽन्यो निरोधोऽप्रतिसंख्यया ॥ १.०६ ॥॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pratisaṃkhyānirodho yo visaṃyogaḥ pṛthak pṛthak /
>
> utpādātyantavighno 'nyo nirodho 'pratisaṃkhyayā // 1.06 //

## 4. Grammar

The two cessations from 1.05 are now defined:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    = visaṃyogaḥ pṛthak pṛthak
    = disjunction, separately and separately

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    = utpāda-atyanta-vighnaḥ
    = absolute obstruction of arising
```

The first is attained through discrimination. The second is not. It is the absolute block on a future arising when conditions fail. Neither is produced as a conditioned event.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Cessation through discrimination is disjunction, in each case separately. The other cessation, not through discrimination, is the absolute obstruction of arising.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Cessation through discrimination is disjunction from Impure dharmas, separately for each conjunction. Discrimination here is a particular prajñā directed to the Noble Truths, beginning with suffering. The other cessation is not attained by that prajñā. It is the absolute obstruction of a future arising, got by deficiency of conditions.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> One cessation is disjunction by discrimination, one conjunction at a time. The other is non-arising: a future dharma is blocked because its conditions fail. Liberation and mere non-production are not the same.

Techne reading:

> In our Yoga Vidyā, *nirodha* can be read as **Sublation**: a determination founders upon its ground when the relation that sustained its contradictory appearance is resolved. The first cessation is this movement as discriminative disjunction from Impure dharmas, separately for each conjunction, through a particular *prajñā*. The other is the absolute obstruction of a future arising because its conditions are lacking. Both belong to the unconditioned, but their grounds differ.

“Sublation,” “foundering to ground,” and “contradiction” are the Techne's
logical determinations. The Sanskrit definitions remain *visaṃyoga* and
*utpādātyantavighna*; the Bhāṣya explains the first through Noble-Truth
discrimination and the second through deficient conditions. The latter
cannot be made into an act of discriminative release.

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
ākāśa
    = non-obstruction

pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    = disjunction by discrimination
    = separately, one conjunction at a time

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    = obstruction of arising
    = deficiency of conditions
```

```text
Impure, capable of arising             first only
Pure and conditioned, unable to arise second only
Impure, unable to arise               both
Pure, capable of arising              neither
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

Cessation through discrimination is disjunction from Impure dharmas, one
conjunction at a time. The Bhāṣya argues that if realizing one such
cessation realized all of them, cultivation of the remaining counteragents
would be pointless. This protects the distinct work of the Path.

The other cessation concerns a future arising absolutely blocked by a
deficiency of conditions. In the Bhāṣya's example, while eye and mind are
occupied with one form, other sensible fields pass; cognitions of those
past fields cannot later arise from them. The four-corner test above keeps
these two cessations distinct while allowing both to apply in one case.

For our Yoga Vidyā, *dhāraṇā* and sustained meditation on these distinctions
are an inner discipline of attention. The source's criterion for the first
cessation remains more exact: a particular *prajñā* considering the Noble
Truths attains a particular disjunction. This lets the first eight kārikās
serve as a field of Samyama without making every meditative stilling into
the cessation defined here.

This closes the opening definition of Abhidharma in our Techne. The Teacher
and the purpose of instruction (1.01–1.03), the Pure / Impure determination
(1.04), and the Pure field with its three unconditioned members (1.05–1.06)
have been articulated. The next movement takes up the conditioned dharmas
under their divisions. In our Organon, those divisions lie beneath the
Concept and become intelligible through its determinations; this is the
systematic transition, not a phrase attributed to the kārikā.

The Kantian comparison has two levels. The introductory discussion of the
**perfection of cognition** asks how knowledge becomes clear and distinct.
The Logic's treatment of the **Concept** then takes up definition and
logical division. Our Techne follows that order: Abhidharma first
determines the knowing and its end; the conditioned dharmas can then be
divided beneath the Concept. The Kośa's own criteria decide each division,
including the distinct grounds of the two cessations.

later treatment of concepts and their division in the
[Abbott translation of the *Logic*](https://persweb.wabash.edu/facstaff/helmang/Akademie-paged-trans/Abbott/Abbott-logic.html).
