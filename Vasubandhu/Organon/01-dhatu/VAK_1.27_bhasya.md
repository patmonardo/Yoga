# The Principles

## VAK_1.27 Bhāṣya — Assigning by Characteristic and Scope

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tathānye 'pi yathāyogaṃ skandhāyatanadhātavaḥ /
>
> pratipādyā yathokteṣu saṃpradhārya svalakṣaṇam // 1.27 //

> Likewise, other Bases, Essence Bases, and Principles are to be
> established, as appropriate, among those already stated, after carefully
> determining each one's own characteristic.

The Bhāṣya extends the Dharma-collection example into a general rule and
applies it to several scriptural classifications. Its examples distinguish
the principal nature of an item from the same item considered together with
its attendants.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Just as these eighty thousand Dharma-collections have been placed within
> these very five Bases, so other scriptural Bases, Essence Bases, and
> Principles are to be placed, as appropriate, among those already stated,
> after carefully considering each one's own characteristic. Whatever
> other Bases, Essence Bases, and Principles are stated in other sūtras are
> likewise to be placed among these very classifications, after carefully
> considering each one's characteristic as established in this treatise.
>
> First, among the five training-collections of discipline, samādhi,
> prajñā, liberation, and knowledge-and-vision [of liberation], the
> discipline-collection is included in the Form Base; the remaining four
> in the Formations Base. Of the ten totality Essence Bases, eight are
> included in the Dharma Essence Base because their nature is non-greed.
> With their attendants, however, they are included through the Mind and
> Dharma Essence Bases because they have the nature of five Bases. The
> same applies to the Essence Bases of mastery.
>
> The totalities of infinite space and infinite cognition, and the four
> attainments beginning with the attainment of infinite space, are
> included through the Mind and Dharma Essence Bases because they have the
> nature of four Bases. The five liberation Essence Bases are included in
> the Dharma Essence Base because their nature is prajñā. With their
> attendants, however, they are included through the Sound, Mind, and
> Dharma Essence Bases.
>
> Of the two further cases, non-percipient beings are classified
> through ten Essence Bases because the smell and taste Essence Bases are
> absent. Those who have reached neither-perception-nor-non-perception are
> classified through the Mind and Dharma Essence Bases. Likewise,
> sixty-two Principles are taught in the *Bahudhātuka*. Their inclusion
> is to be understood as appropriate in each case.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The general rule is followed by applications. Each application identifies
what is being considered, determines its characteristic and scope, and
states its inclusion:

```text
additional scriptural classification
    → consider its characteristic as established in the treatise
    → distinguish principal nature from the item with attendants
    → place it in the appropriate established arrangement
```

No separate school is named. The final reference says that sixty-two
Principles are taught in the *Bahudhātuka* and directs their appropriate
inclusion; it does not enumerate all sixty-two here. The next study begins
with a specific question arising from that broader scriptural field.

## 5. Characteristic before Assignment

`Saṃpradhārya`, an absolutive, makes careful consideration prior to
`pratipādyāḥ`, the required assignment. The prose expands `svalakṣaṇam`
through `svaṃ svaṃ lakṣaṇam`, each one's characteristic, and qualifies
it by `yathāvihitam asmiñ śāstre`, as established in this treatise.

`Yathāyogam` therefore does not permit classification by resemblance of
names alone. The relevant characteristic and scope constrain the
assignment. The opening comparison takes the already classified
Dharma-collections as its model, without making every scriptural use of
*skandha* a teaching-collection of the same sort.

The five training-collections illustrate the procedure. The discipline
collection is included under the Form Base; the remaining four under the
Formations Base. These named training-collections do not add five new
fundamental Bases to the established five.

## 6. Principal Nature and the Attended Complex

The recurring `saparivāra`, “with attendants,” changes the scope of what
is being classified. The principal nature is not discarded; the
classification is expanded to include the accompanying complex.

| Case | Principal account | With attendants |
|---|---|---|
| eight totality Essence Bases | non-greed; Dharma Essence Base | five-Base nature; Mind and Dharma Essence Bases |
| Essence Bases of mastery | same treatment by explicit analogy | same treatment by explicit analogy |
| five liberation Essence Bases | prajñā; Dharma Essence Base | Sound, Mind, and Dharma Essence Bases |

The five-Base complex of the first row does not require all ten material
Essence Bases as constituents. Its Form component can be included through
the Dharma Essence Base, as the earlier *avijñapti* classification makes
possible. The local sentence gives the resulting inclusion but does not
enumerate each attendant or explicitly name that material component. This
is an explanation of compatibility with the preceding scheme, not an
additional sentence in the translation.

The Sound Essence Base in the liberation account is explicitly present
in the transmitted classification. The unit does not list the five
practices or explain which attendant supplies sound in each case. The
classification is preserved without manufacturing those missing details.

## 7. Four-Base Attainments and the Two Further Cases

The totalities of infinite space and infinite cognition are grouped with
the four attainments beginning with infinite space. They are assigned
through the Mind and Dharma Essence Bases because their nature is four
Bases. Form is the omitted Base within this formless classification.
“Infinite cognition” retains the controlled rendering of *vijñāna*; the
attainment's name does not define every cognition as infinite.

The two further cases are explained through their occupants or those who
have reached them. For non-percipient beings, the stated absence of smell
and taste yields ten Essence Bases. For those reaching neither-perception-
nor-non-perception, the assignment is through the Mind and Dharma Essence
Bases.

The statement about non-percipient beings must not be turned into a claim
of presently occurring cognition during non-perception merely because the
tenfold count includes the Mind Essence Base. The sentence states a
classification and explicitly explains two exclusions; it does not
explain the mode in which every remaining Essence Base is present or
acquired. Classification and current operation remain distinct.

The latter case likewise names an attainment and its occupants under the
mental Essence Bases; it does not make the attainment an extra Essence
Base outside the twelve. The final statement about sixty-two Principles
generalizes the assignment without supplying a complete table for that
discourse.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. Additional scriptural classifications are to be placed within the
   established arrangements, not treated as unspecified new principles.
2. Careful consideration of each characteristic precedes assignment.
3. A principal factor and that factor with its attendants can receive
   different inclusions because their scope differs.
4. Non-greed and prajñā supply explicit principal natures in the examples;
   the attended complexes receive broader assignments.
5. Four-Base constitution and the absence of smell and taste provide
   other stated grounds of inclusion.
6. The sixty-two-Principle reference directs further application without
   claiming to enumerate every placement in this unit.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage makes assignment depend on what a scriptural name denotes in
context. A principal factor and that factor with attendants do not have
the same scope, even when the heading remains unchanged. The arrangement
must preserve that difference before fixing the placement.

For the Organon reconstruction, this is a concrete method of
determination: establish the content and scope, then establish its
systematic placement. The broader complex neither erases the principal
factor's nature nor transfers that nature indiscriminately to every
attendant.

The five liberation Essence Bases are classified principally through
prajñā, while their attended complex includes the Sound, Mind, and Dharma
Essence Bases. The passage distinguishes the named practice's principal
determination from the wider complex in which it is considered. It does
not itself label this relation as the manifestation of *paramārtha*
within *saṃvṛti*; it gives a concrete case for further inquiry.

The *dharma-skandha* at the opening of the unit remains a special teaching-
collection in the conventional discussion, not an additional Base. The
project's Essence Base is a distinct structural term in the nested
Knowledge Base / Essence Base / Form Base architecture. The commentary's
assignment examples do not collapse these two senses.

## 10. Working Status

This is a working revision of the twenty-seventh Bhāṣya study. It
standardizes *skandha*, *āyatana*, and *dhātu* as Base, Essence Base, and
Principle; preserves the principal/attendant distinction; and retains the
source repairs and interpretive limits recorded above. The sixty-two
Principles are not enumerated here. The focus on the eighteen Principles
and the method that determines their role remain for the next movement of
the chapter.
