# VAK_1.22 Bhāṣya — The Unconditioned, and the Order of the Bases

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> skandheṣv asaṃskṛtaṃ noktam arthāyogāt kramaḥ punaḥ /
> yathaudārikasaṃkleśabhājanādyarthadhātutaḥ // 1.22 //

> The unconditioned is not stated among the Bases, since the meaning of
> collection does not apply. The order, however, is according to grossness,
> affliction, the meanings beginning with a vessel, and the Realms and levels.

The commentary excludes, then orders, then returns to the separation in 1.21.
The order of the six faculties opens 1.23. It is not taken here.

## 3. Continuous Translation

The unconditioned is not stated among the Bases, because their defining
meaning does not apply. It cannot be included in any of the five: it is not
Form, nor any of the others through Cognition. Nor can it be called a sixth
Base. Why? A Base means a collection. Unlike Form and the others, the
unconditioned is not divided into past and the remaining distinctions so that
its instances could be gathered into one and reckoned an Unconditioned Base.

The expression “Bases of appropriation” makes known the ground of affliction;
“Bases” makes known the ground of affliction and purification. The
unconditioned does not serve either purpose in that way. Therefore it is not
established among the Bases: their meaning does not apply.

Others. As the cessation of a pot is not a pot, the cessation of the bases
cannot be a base.

The same argument would wrongly exclude the unconditioned from the Principles
and Essences as well. Another account of the Bases has now been given.

The order, then, follows grossness, affliction, the meanings beginning with a
vessel, and Realm or level.

Form is the grossest, because it is resistant. Among what is without form,
Feeling is gross in its activity: people say, “Feeling in my hand, feeling in
my foot.” Reflection is grosser than the two that remain, and Formations
are grosser than Cognition. Thus the grosser comes first.

Or, in beginningless saṃsāra, women and men delight in one another's Form.
That delight comes from greed for the savor of Feeling; the greed comes from
inverted Reflection; the inversion comes from afflictions, which also afflict
Consciousness. Thus the order can be explained by affliction.

Or consider the meanings beginning with a vessel. The five Bases, beginning
with Form, serve as vessel, food, seasoning, maker, and eater, respectively.

Or consider Realm or level. The Desire Realm is distinguished by the forms of
sensual pleasure; the dhyānas by Feeling; three formless levels by Reflection;
and the summit of existence by Formations alone. These are stations for
Cognition, which is established in them. Thus the order of the Bases displays
field and seed.

For these reasons there are five Bases, neither fewer nor more. The order also
explains why Feeling and Reflection are set apart from the Formations: they are
grosser, they cause the sequence of affliction, they are called vessel and
seasoning here, and two Realm or level groups are distinguished by them.

The last sentence keeps “vessel” as transmitted. The analogy's “food” is not
substituted.

## 5. Why the Meaning Does Not Apply

A base is a collection, gathered into one across the sūtra's distinctions.
The unconditioned is not that gathering. Three unconditioned dharmas have
already been named. The argument is not that the unconditioned has no plurality.
It is that this gathering does not fit.

Bases of appropriation name the ground of affliction. Bases name the ground of
affliction and purification. The unconditioned is neither, in that manner. That
does not remove it from the system. It removes it from the base.

## 6. The Four Grounds

Grossness places the more manifest first. Resistance introduces form. Feeling
is located in the hand and the foot. Reflection is grosser than formations and
the cognition-base. Formations are grosser than the cognition.

Affliction runs back. Attraction to form is explained by greed for the savor.
That greed by inverted reflection. That inversion by the afflictions.
Consciousness is afflicted by them. The list is not a forward chain of
production.

The meal assigns five roles. Form, vessel. Feeling, food. Reflection,
seasoning. Formations, maker. Cognition, eater. The recap says vessel and
seasoning for the two that were separated. The discrepancy stays.

The levels are not the eighteen. Desire Realm, dhyānas, three formless,
summit. Predominance, not deletion. Cognition is established in the
stations, as seed in a field.

## 7. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. *Arthāyogāt* means the meaning of base does not apply.
2. The unconditioned is neither in the five nor a sixth collection.
3. The pot analogy is others', and it fails for essence and principle.
4. Four ordering accounts. The promise of three is kept as a discrepancy.
5. Affliction explains backward. It does not produce the list forward.
6. The meal is five roles. The recap's vessel for feeling is kept as
   transmitted.
7. The levels are stations of Cognition. Five, neither fewer nor more.

## 8. Interpretation

The base gathers. The unconditioned is not a gathering of that kind. It remains
an essence and a principle. Exclusion from the base is not exclusion from the
system.

The order is four grounds on one sequence. They are not interchangeable.
Grossness is presentation. Affliction is an explanation that runs back.
The meal is a cooperation. The levels are predominance, and the cognition is
established in them. None of the five is a substance that owns the rest. The
unconditioned is not a substrate either. It is what the collection cannot take
up.

1.21 is answered here, with the count left as the text left it. 1.23 is not
opened.

The paired kārikā now includes verse-order tokens, neuter agreement,
derived exclusion and four-ground semantics, and separate prose
correspondences. Contextual Realm/level is distinguished from the
eighteen Principles; *vijñāna* is restored as Cognition in the continuous
translation and argument. The single anchor matches the revised
Bhāṣya-informed translation. Both transmitted discrepancies remain.
