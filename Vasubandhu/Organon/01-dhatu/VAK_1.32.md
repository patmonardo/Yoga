## 4. Grammar

Three clauses answer the Bhāṣya's three questions. Each predicate is a masculine plural bahuvrīhi or participle agreeing with *dhātavaḥ*, stated or understood. *Hi* is restrictive: it licenses the exclusive reading of the first clause, so the five have the kind "with both" and no other. *Tri-prakārāḥ* gives the last three at least three kinds. *Ubhaya-varjitāḥ* gives the ten "without both"; the Bhāṣya adds *nityam*, always.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Five spans [0, 4); last [4, 7); rest [7, 9); the root spans [0, 9). Three clauses. No claim that the three kinds are three Principles is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** For the five Cognition Principles are with vitarka and vicāra. The last three are of three kinds. The rest are without both.

A kind is a way a Principle occurs with respect to the pair: with both, vicāra alone, vitarka alone, or without both. *Hi* makes the first clause exclusive. *Tri-prakārāḥ* is not exclusive: the Bhāṣya finds a fourth kind, vicāra itself in the Desire Realm and the first concentration, inside the Dharma Principle, and says it does not fall within the three. That clarifies the verse; it does not contradict it.

The three kinds are not three Principles. They are three ways the last three occur.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The five Cognition Principles are with vitarka and with vicāra. The last three are of three kinds. The rest are without both.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five Cognition Principles are accompanied by both vitarka and vicāra. The last three—Mind Principle, Dharma Principle, and Mind-Cognition Principle—are of three kinds; the remaining ten are without both.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The five Cognitions do not occur without the pair. The last three take the pair, or one of the pair, or neither, by the level. The ten whose nature is Form take neither, because association is not their way.

Vitarka and vicāra are not counted twice. Each is without a second of itself, and each is with the other. That is why a level with both has four kinds, not three.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The five Cognition Principles are always associated with both terms. The last three vary by level: desire and the first concentration have both, intermediate concentration has vicāra alone, and the second concentration through the summit has neither. The commentary separately classifies vitarka as with vicāra alone, vicāra in desire and the first concentration as with vitarka alone, and unassociated Dharma-Principle (and vicāra in the intermediate concentration) as without both. Thus the cited fourfold distinction on a level with both clarifies, rather than alters, the verse's threefold statement; the remaining ten are Form-natured and unassociated.

The hinge is hi. The five are not sometimes with the pair. The last three are the ones that vary, and the variation is the level. The four kinds are required because vitarka and vicāra cannot associate with a second of themselves.
