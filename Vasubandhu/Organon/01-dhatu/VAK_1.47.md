# VAK_1.47 — The Ear, the Three, and Mind

## 4. Grammar

Three clauses. In the first, *tathā* is anaphoric. It carries the fixed rule of 1.46 and applies it to whatever sense *śrotram* names: the Faculty is not lower than the body, and neither the Condition nor the Cognition is higher than the Faculty.

In the second, the Bhāṣya names the form: a general rule (*utsarga*), then an exception (*apavāda*) begun to qualify it. The grammar builds that form directly. The genitive plural *trayāṇām* (CNT=3, the three of 1.44) gives the domain, *sarvam* the members, and *sva-bhūmikam* the predicate. The following nominative, *kāya-vijñānam*, does not add a fourth member. It is subtracted from the domain of *sarvam* and given its own predicate, *adhara-sva-bhūmi*. The general rule thus holds only where the exception does not.

In the third, *a-niyatam* is a predicate operator like *sarvataḥ* in 1.46. Given the Mind as Faculty, it asserts that Mind is found on the body's plane, above it, and below it.

**Close construe.** Likewise the Ear. Of the three, however, everything is of its own plane only; Body-Cognition is of a lower plane or its own. Mind is not fixed.

"Own plane" is the plane of the body one is born in. The exception lowers Body-Cognition and nothing else: the body and the Tangible stay.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Likewise the Ear. Of the three, however, everything is of its own plane. Body-Cognition is of a lower plane or its own. Mind is not fixed.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Ear follows the rule of the Eye. For the three beginning with the Nose, however, Faculty, Condition, and Cognition are all of their own plane, except that Body-Cognition is of a lower plane or its own. Mind is not fixed.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The ear takes the eye's rule. Not lower than the body. Sound not higher than the ear. Cognition not higher than the ear. Relative to the Cognition, Sound is open. Relative to the body, both are open.

The three that reach do not take that freedom. Nose, tongue, and body: Faculty, Condition, and Cognition stay on their own plane. The exception is Body-Cognition. The body and the Tangible stay. The Cognition may drop. Own plane for one born in the Desire Realm or the first dhyāna. A lower plane for one born in the second or above.

Mind is the open one. Same plane as the body, or higher, or lower. A body of five planes does not fix the mind. Attainment and rebirth are where that is worked. Not here. The incidental discussion closes.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Key-term evidence.** *Viṣaya* appears in *kāya-viṣaya-vijñānāni*: body, Condition, Cognition, the three members that the general rule holds on one plane. The Bhāṣya's own terms for the form of the verse are *utsarga* (general rule) and *apavāda* (exception).

**Translation note.** *Sarvam* is spelled out as Faculty, Condition, and Cognition, following *kāya-viṣaya-vijñānāni*. The exception is rendered with "except that", which makes the *utsarga*–*apavāda* form visible in English.

The hinge is the exception inside the general rule. The three stay. Body-Cognition may be lower. Mind is not held to a plane on this page.
