**Feature chart checkpoints.** Enumeration spans [0, 8); definition [8, 11); the root spans [0, 11). The singular *rūpam* names the collection. The second line is a nominal predication with an understood copula. No claim that Cognition is a member of Form is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** Form is five Faculties, five meanings, and avijñapti. The eye and the rest are clarities of Form supporting their corresponding Cognitions.

`Faculty` in this local signature means these five sensory Faculties, not every Faculty discussed in chapter 2. The Bhāṣya resolves *cakṣurādayaḥ* as the five named Faculties and the *tad-* reference as the corresponding sensory Cognitions. The grammar does not insert Cognition into the Form enumeration.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Form is the five Faculties, the five meanings, and *avijñapti*. The clarities of Form, beginning with the eye, are supports of the corresponding Cognition.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Form Base comprises five Faculties—eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body—their five corresponding meanings—visible Form, sound, odor, taste, and the tangible—and *avijñapti*. The five Faculties are clarities of Form, each serving as the support of the Cognition corresponding to its meaning.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Eleven, and no twelfth. Form collects five Faculties, five meanings, and *avijñapti*.

The Faculty is a clarity of Form. It supports the corresponding Cognition; the eye supports Eye-Cognition. Cognition is supported, not counted.

Meaning stays lowercase. The commentary glosses it as condition. Faculty and meaning are the two sides, not two inventories. *Avijñapti* is the eleventh, singular, and not yet defined.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The hinge is the count, then the support. The five Faculties are indicated. The five meanings are named and left for the next verse. The scripture calls the eye an internal Essence, a clarity of Form depending on the four Great Elements. That citation is not this enumeration. The Prakaraṇa agrees: the eye is a clarity of Form that supports Eye-Cognition.

**Interpretation.** The passage makes Form more extensive than visible appearance and gives the Faculties a relational determination through the Cognition they support. These are positive grounds for metaphysical inquiry, not merely a list of sensible things. The formal layer preserves that opening without identifying a support with what it supports.
