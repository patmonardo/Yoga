# VAK_1.07

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> ते पुनः संस्कृता धर्मा रूपादिस्कन्धपञ्चकम् ।
>
> स एवाध्वा कथावस्तु सनिःसाराः सवस्तुकाः ॥ १.०७ ॥॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> te punaḥ saṃskṛtā dharmā rūpādiskandhapañcakam /
>
> sa evādhvā kathāvastu saniḥsārāḥ savastukāḥ // 1.07 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |

| **punaḥ** | indeclinable | resumes and specifies; not a new inventory |

The second line does not add four extra classes beside the five aggregates. It redescribes the same conditioned field.

## 4. Grammar

```text
saṃskṛtā dharmāḥ
    = rūpādi-skandha-pañcakam
```

`Pañcakam` is a collective singular predicate of a plural subject.

```text
saṃskṛta-field
    = adhvan
    = kathāvastu
    = saniḥsāra
    = savastuka
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the two derivations of `adhvan`, the Prakaraṇa constraint on `kathāvastu`, the identification of `niḥsāra` with nirvāṇa, and the attributed Vaibhāṣika gloss of `vastu` as `hetu`. Those belong to the study translation, not to the close construe.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Those conditioned dharmas, again, are the five aggregates beginning with form. That very same [field] is the temporal course, the basis of discourse, possessed of an exit, and possessed of a *vastu*.

The literal is harsh. It is kept so the Pruden comparison stays possible. It is not the reading.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The conditioned dharmas are the five aggregates beginning with form. They are called conditioned because they are made by conditions that come together and act together; nothing is produced by a single condition. That same field is the temporal courses, because it has the states of having gone, going, and being about to go — or because it is consumed by impermanence. It is the basis of discourse, because meaningful speech takes the conditioned as its basis; otherwise the Prakaraṇa statement that the bases of discourse are included in the eighteen domains would be contradicted. It is possessed of an exit, because nirvāṇa is the exit from everything conditioned. It is possessed of a *vastu*. The Vaibhāṣikas say that here the word *vastu* expresses cause. That is their gloss, not the reading.

The etymology uses *kṛtā* (“made”). The headword remains *conditioned*, not *created*, and not *constructed*.

## 6. Rational Comment

Presupposed, not imported: Yoga:Kośa is one dyad. The Yoga Sūtra is the *paramārtha*. Hegel is the bhāṣya of that key. This comment does not bring the Logic in.

The inventory English fails on *vastu*. Pruden fails the same way. The term is the key of the verse.

```text
sa eva
    the same Conditioned field
    not four extra heaps

adhvan
    the course
    gone, going, about to go
    YS 4.12: past and future exist in their own form
             because the dharmas differ as course

kathāvastu
    discourse taking the vastu
    not a topic of conversation

saniḥsāra
    possessed of an exit
    the exit is nirvāṇa
    not a sixth aggregate

savastuka
    possessed of vastu
```

*Vastu* is Appearance of Essence. The basis is the Thing and its properties. That is Essential Relation, not Absolute Relation.

```text
YS 4.13   te vyaktasūkṣmā guṇātmānaḥ
          manifest or subtle, of the nature of the guṇas

YS 4.14   pariṇāmaikatvād vastutattvam
          the reality of the vastu
          is the oneness of the transformation

YS 4.15   vastusāmye cittabhedāt tayor vibhaktaḥ panthāḥ
          the vastu is the same
          the cittas differ
          their paths divide
```

Essence must appear. The thing is not a substrate behind its properties. Through its properties it becomes cause, and it is not yet actual cause. Thinghood passes into property. What remains without properties is an empty compass.

So the Vaibhāṣika sentence, *vastu* expresses *hetu*, catches the moment the thing becomes cause through its properties. It is not the reading. Substance does not Appear. The properties do.

Skandha is this gathering as Being. Essential Relation is the next grade of the same movement, not a second list parked offstage. This verse is not yet the Concept. Vijñāna is what makes Skandha, Āyatana, Dhātu a Concept. That is not written into the literal.

```text
viṣaya       = Condition
saṃskṛta    = Conditioned
asaṃskṛta   = Unconditioned
pratyaya    ≠ viṣaya
Construct   ≠ Condition
```

Conditioned *means* created-by-conditions. The headword is not *created*. A Rational Dharma does not Persist. What persists is Impure. That cut is presupposed here. It is not this verse’s sentence.

## 9. Interpretive Note

1.04–1.06 cut Pure and Impure, and named the three unconditioned. 1.07 gives the Conditioned their body: five aggregates, one field.

The local witness at `[004|26]` omits *saṃjñā*. The kārikā says five. The omission is not patched.

Nirvāṇa is the exit from the whole conditioned, Path included. Not a sixth aggregate. Not a mood.
