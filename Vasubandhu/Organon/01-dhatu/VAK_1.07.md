## 4. Grammar

The conditioned subject is masculine plural; its predicate is a neuter singular collective. This is nominal predication, not adjective agreement. The second line resumes that collective with *saḥ eva*. Singular *adhvā* and *kathāvastu* do not impose their number on the final plural *saniḥsārāḥ* and *savastukāḥ*.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Classification spans [0, 5); resumption [5, 11); the root spans [0, 11). The singular resumption takes up the collective field; the final plural predicates resume its Dharmas. No claim that the four determinations are synonymous is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** Those conditioned Dharmas are the five Bases beginning with Form. That same field is the course, kathāvastu, and, in its Dharmas, saniḥsāra and savastuka.

The resumption preserves one field across four functions, without making the four predicates synonymous. The first clause classifies conditioned Dharmas by the five Bases; it does not assert only five individual Dharmas. The Bhāṣya's glosses (temporal course, meaningful discourse, nirvāṇa as exit, *vastu* as cause) are not independently derived by the four lexical predicates.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Those Conditioned dharmas are the five Bases beginning with Form. That same field is the course, the *kathāvastu*, the *saniḥsāra*, and the *savastuka*.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The conditioned dharmas are the five Bases beginning with the Form Base. These same dharmas are past, present, and future; subjects of discourse; have an exit in nirvāṇa; and possess a real basis.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Conditioned dharmas are the Base: the five Bases beginning with the Form Base, gathered as one. That same field is the Path of Dharma, Essential Reflection, Essential Relation, and Appearance.

```text
Base
    rūpādi-skandha-pañcakam

Path of Dharma
    adhvā

Essential Reflection
    kathāvastu

Essential Relation
    saniḥsāra

Appearance
    savastuka
```

## 8. Logical Determination

**Kārikā, derived.** The root SEM states the classification and the four determinations over the same restricted domain. These predicates preserve distinctions for further interpretation; they are not assertions that course, reflection, relation, and appearance are identical. The Bhāṣya's specific glosses and the claim that conditioned means impure are prose elaborations or rejected theories.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The inventory English broke the kārikā. The quad is the wheel, and it stays in the philosophical translation. The Form Base is the empirical root of appearance. That is the extension, not the literal.

The witness at `[004|26]` omits the Reflection Base. Not patched. The kārikā says five. Nirvāṇa-as-exit and *vastu*-as-cause remain glosses in the bhāṣya. What persists is Impure. A Rational Dharma does not Persist. Yoga is the key of *vastu*. It is not pasted into the literal.
