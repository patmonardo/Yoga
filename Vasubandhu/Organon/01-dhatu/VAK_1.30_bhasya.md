# VAK_1.30 Bhāṣya — Ethical Classification and Fourteen Principles in the Form Realm

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tridhānye kāmadhātvāptāḥ sarve rūpe caturdaśa /
>
> vinā gandharasaghrāṇajihvāvijñānadhātubhiḥ // 1.30 //

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The remaining ten Principles have wholesome, unwholesome, or ethically
> indeterminate instances. All eighteen Principles are connected with the
> Desire Realm. Fourteen belong to the Form Realm: the Smell and Taste
> Principles are absent because beings there are detached from gross
> ingested nourishment, and the corresponding olfactory- and
> gustatory-cognition Principles are absent because their objects are
> unavailable. The Nose and Tongue faculty Principles themselves remain.

The Bhāṣya's debate is needed to prevent the closing compound from being
misread as excluding the faculty Principles along with their cognitions.

### Anchor note

“The others” are the ten Principles remaining after the eight ethically
indeterminate Principles of 1.29. “Threefold” means wholesome,
unwholesome, and ethically indeterminate. A new classification begins
with “all belong to the Desire Realm.” The verse therefore crosses a
topical boundary; its opening must not be translated as threefold
Realm-membership.

The unit runs from the explanation of *tridhā 'nye* through the conclusion
that fourteen Principles belong to the Form Realm, at printed pages
20.10–21.19 of the local running Sanskrit. The formless-realm enumeration
belongs to 1.31.

Spacing and evident transcription defects have been regularized:
*rupe*, *spraṣṭavya viśeṣeṇa*, *vyavahārśca*, *indariyasatṛṣṇasya*,
*puruṣendraiyam*, *ahīnendariyā*, *puruśendriyasya*, and
*ghrāṇajivhendriye*. The broken *vinā'; śrayaśobhaiva* is read
*vināśrayaśobhaiva*, following the local research transcription. The
witness's *nivarttate* is retained below as a textual difficulty, rather
than silently changed to a verb of production. The compact ethical
compounds and *tadanyāvyākṛtau* are retained; their construal is explained
in the translation and analysis.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The other ten Principles are wholesome, unwholesome, and ethically
indeterminate. Of these, the seven [six Cognition Principles plus the
Mind Principle] are wholesome when associated with non-greed and the like,
unwholesome when associated with greed and the like, and otherwise
ethically indeterminate.

The Dharma Principle is wholesome insofar as it consists of non-greed
and the like, is associated with them, or arises from them; discerning
cessation is also wholesome. It is unwholesome insofar as it consists of
greed and the like, is associated with them, or arises from them. The
remainder is ethically indeterminate.

The Form and Sound Principles, insofar as they are included in bodily and
verbal manifestation and arise from wholesome or unwholesome mind, are
respectively wholesome or unwholesome. Apart from these, they are
ethically indeterminate. Their wholesome and other statuses have now
been explained.

Of these eighteen Principles, how many belong to the Desire Realm and
how many to the Form Realm? All belong to the Desire Realm. “Belonging”
means not separated from it, connected with the Desire Realm. In the Form
Realm there are fourteen Principles, excluding Smell, Taste,
Olfactory-Cognition, and Gustatory-Cognition.

The Smell and Taste Principles do not occur there, because they constitute
morsel-made food and those born there are free from passion for that food.
Consequently the Olfactory- and Gustatory-Cognition Principles are also
absent, because their objects are absent.

Then the Tangible Principle should also be absent there, since it too
constitutes morsel-made food. — The Tangible Principle that is not of the
nature of food exists there. — The same consequence should apply to Smell
and Taste. — There is no enjoyment of Smell and Taste apart from
ingestion. The Tangible Principle, however, serves as support for
Faculties, as a basis, and as a covering. Therefore Smell and Taste serve
no purpose there for those free from passion for ingestion, whereas the
Tangible Principle does.

Others say: through dependence on meditative attainment, visible Forms
are seen here, sounds are heard, and the body is benefited by a special
Tangible Principle accompanied by pliancy. Therefore precisely these
three occur in birth resulting from meditation, but Smell and Taste do not.

Then the Nose and Tongue Faculties should also be absent, since they
serve no purpose. — They have a purpose: without them the bodily basis
would lack beauty, and there would be no speech or ordinary communication.
— If that is the purpose, let the bodily sites alone exist for beauty and
speech; there need be no Faculty. — A bodily site without a Faculty cannot
occur, as with the site of the Male Faculty. — Its non-occurrence is
reasonable because it serves no purpose. The bodily sites of the Nose and
Tongue, however, have a purpose; their existence even without a Faculty
is therefore reasonable.

Even a Faculty serving no purpose can be produced, as with those certain
to die in the womb. — Granted that it may serve no purpose, it is not
without a cause. What causes a Faculty to arise? A particular karma of
one who thirsts for that Faculty. And one who is free from thirst for the
object is surely free from thirst for the Faculty too. Thus the Nose and
Tongue Faculties should not occur in those free from passion for their
objects.

Or why does the Male Faculty not also [arise there]? — Because it would
impair beauty. — Why would it not be beautiful in those whose genital site
is enclosed in a sheath? Moreover, arising depends on causes,
not on usefulness. Thus even something that impairs beauty would arise
if its cause were present.

But then the sūtra is contradicted: “[They are] complete, without
deficient Faculties.” — They are complete and without deficiency with
respect to the Faculties that exist there. What contradiction is there?
Otherwise the consequence would extend to the Male Faculty as well.

They explain it, however, in this way: the Nose and Tongue Faculties do
exist there, but the Smell and Taste Principles do not. Craving becomes
active toward the six [internal] Essences through one's own embodied
existence, rather than through their objects. In the case of the Male
Faculty, however, it operates through sexual contact. Thus it is
established that fourteen Principles belong to the Form Realm.

The bracketed “arise there” gives the contextual demand of the male-faculty
objection. The transmitted *kiṃ na nivarttate* literally suggests “why does
it not cease/turn back?”, which does not readily fit the reply about
beauty and the ensuing insistence on causal production. The research
translation's “remain” is also interpretive. The reading needs further
textual checking; the source has not been silently emended.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The opening is direct classificatory exposition: the six Cognition
Principles and Mind Principle, the Dharma Principle, and the Form and
Sound Principles exhaust the remaining ten.
The commentary explicitly closes this topic before asking about realms.

The form-realm discussion then proceeds through objections and replies.
First comes the absence of alimentary objects and their cognitions;
next, the continued availability of non-alimentary tangible objects.
*Anye punar āhuḥ* introduces an alternative explanation grounded in
meditative attainment. It is a reported account, not an unmarked claim
by the author.

The longest debate concerns faculties without their usual objects.
Beauty, speech, bodily sites, purposeless production, karmic causation,
and scriptural completeness are successively tested. These arguments
are not cumulative endorsements: later objections expose limits in
earlier defenses.

*Evaṃ tu varṇayanti*, “they explain it, however, in this way,” introduces
the concluding defense of fourteen. Its explanation distinguishes craving
for embodied existence from craving through particular objects. The
conclusion is explicit; a named school and Vasubandhu's personal assent
to every preceding argument are not.

## 5. The Threefold Ethical Classification

The seven Principles are the six kinds of sensory and mental cognition
plus the Mind Principle. Their ethical determination here is by association
(*saṃprayoga*). The text does not say that every instance is wholesome,
unwholesome, and indeterminate simultaneously: these are alternative
statuses within each Principle.

The Dharma Principle requires a wider account. Its compound
*alobhādisvabhāvasaṃprayuktasamutthaḥ* coordinates being of the nature of
non-greed and the like, being associated with them, and arising from
them. The negative ethical series is parallel. Discerning cessation
(*pratisaṃkhyānirodha*) is expressly added on the wholesome side. The
classification therefore cannot be reduced to association with a present
citta alone.

For the Form and Sound Principles, the important restriction is
*kāyavāgvijñaptisaṃgṛhītau*: bodily and verbal manifestation. Their
wholesome or unwholesome status follows the citta from which those
manifestations arise. This does not classify every pleasant Form or
agreeable sound as wholesome. The remaining cases are indeterminate.

The numeral checks the construal: six Cognition Principles plus the Mind
Principle, one Dharma Principle, and two manifestation Principles make
ten. Together with the eight from 1.29, the eighteen are accounted for.
This is the missing opening in the local research commentary and the
decisive correction to its translation of *tridhānye*.

## 6. Realm Connection and the Four Exclusions

*Āptāḥ* is glossed by *aviyuktāḥ* and *pratisaṃyuktāḥ*: not separated,
connected. “Belong to” preserves this technical relation without reducing
it to occupying a geographical location. Here *rūpadhātu*, the Form Realm,
must also be distinguished from *rūpadhātu*, the visible Form Principle
among the eighteen. Context determines the scale of classification.

| Excluded from the Form Realm | Immediate explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Smell Principle | Connection with morsel-made food |
| Taste Principle | Connection with morsel-made food |
| Olfactory-Cognition Principle | Absence of its object |
| Gustatory-Cognition Principle | Absence of its object |

The compound following *vinā* names these four. It does not exclude the
Nose and Tongue Faculties themselves. All five Faculties, the Form
Principle, Sound Principle, Tangible Principle, Mind Principle, Dharma
Principle, and the four remaining Cognition Principles yield fourteen.

The food argument is an argument within the transmitted cosmology.
Its statement about enjoyment of smell and taste through ingestion is
preserved as such; it should not be inflated into a universal empirical
definition of smelling. The objection concerning the Tangible Principle
already forces a distinction between a Principle and one of its uses as
food.

The alternative meditative account links the Form Principle, Sound
Principle, and a special Tangible Principle accompanied by *prasrabdhi*
to birth through meditation. It does not assert that every sensory
cognition operates during every
absorption. Its precise experiential and temporal scope is not fully
specified in this paragraph.

## 7. Faculty, Bodily Site, Purpose, and Cause

*Indriya* and *adhiṣṭhāna* cannot be interchanged. A nasal bodily site and
the olfactory Faculty are distinguishable in the argument; likewise the
tongue's contribution to speech does not itself establish a gustatory
Faculty. The research rendering “ordinary designation” for
*vyavahāra* is too narrow here: the following *vacanārtham*, “for speech,”
clarifies the practical function under discussion.

The embryonic example separates production from subsequent use. A
faculty may arise although it will never perform its ordinary work.
The counterargument grants that point and asks for a cause. Thus
*niḥprayojanā* and *nirhetukā* mark different claims: purposeless does
not mean causeless.

The final explanation addresses the proposed causal inference directly.
Freedom from passion for an external object does not automatically
remove every form of craving connected with the corresponding internal
basis. Attachment through one's own embodied existence supplies the
relevant distinction. The Male Faculty is treated differently because
its craving is explained through sexual contact.

The scripture's “complete” is also interpreted with restricted scope:
complete in the Faculties that belong there. It cannot establish that
every possible Faculty must occur, since that would include the Male
Faculty too. This is an argument about the scope of predication, not a
license to ignore the quoted sūtra.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- *Tridhā anye* completes ethical classification: the other ten admit
  wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate instances.
- *Kāmadhātvāptāḥ sarve* begins the realm classification independently.
- *Rūpe* means the Form Realm here, not the visible Form Principle.
- The four exclusions are two objects and their two cognitions; the
  corresponding Faculties remain in the concluding account.
- Non-alimentary tangible Form remains available; a Principle is not
  exhausted by one use of it.
- The defense of retained faculties ultimately distinguishes causal
  attachment to embodied existence from attachment through objects.

These decisions govern this edition's anchor and translation. The
original research kārikā and commentary files remain unchanged.

## 9. Interpretation

The Kośa presents Vijñāna as Discriminative Cognition joining and
governing Perception and Conception. Their unity is Inconceivable as a
homogeneous operation; its Idea is disclosed in the Cognition Base.
Vijñāna governs Manas and guides the reading of Dharma Base. Form Base
and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti* distinctly, and Vijñāna
bears a *prati* relation to it. Vijñāna remains distinct from *citta*
and *manas*.

For the Techne, a Faculty is a **Representation Factory**: a productive
capacity for a determinate mode of presentation, not simply a bodily part
whose existence is defined by a purpose assigned to it. This is project
vocabulary, not a literal translation of *indriya*.

The Bhāṣya itself supplies a crucial distinction by refusing to identify
*indriya* with *adhiṣṭhāna*, the bodily site. It also separates Faculty,
object, operation, and resulting cognition. In the Form Realm, the Nose
and Tongue Faculties remain although the Smell and Taste Principles and
their corresponding Cognition Principles do not. A Representation Factory
can therefore persist without its familiar object relation or a current
act of cognition. Its nature is not exhausted by one use.

The source's *kāyavāgvijñapti* means bodily and verbal manifestation in the
conventional translation. In the incoming Techne, *vijñapti* names
Ordinary Knowing, or Conceiving: here it is manifested bodily and verbally
and receives ethical determination through the *citta* from which it
arises. Keep it distinct from sensory Knowing (*vijñāna*) and from
Transcendental Knowing (*prati-vijñapti*), which this passage does not
discuss.

The debate does entertain arguments from beauty, speech, usefulness,
attachment, and karmic cause. They are successive proposals and objections,
not a single settled proof that Faculties exist for the sake of a bodily
or social function. Our reading should abstract the Faculty's
representational capacity from those proposed purposes while retaining
the physical site as a distinct relation.

The closing statement distinguishes the six internal Essences from their
external objects and locates craving's relation through embodied
existence rather than through those objects. Craving associated with the
Male Faculty is treated separately through sexual contact. The Techne
keeps internal Essence, external object, Faculty, and embodied condition
distinct while relating them as the Bhāṣya does.
