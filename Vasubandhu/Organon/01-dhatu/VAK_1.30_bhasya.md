# VAK_1.30 Bhāṣya — Ethical Classification and the Fourteen Domains of the Form Realm

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tridhānye kāmadhātvāptāḥ sarve rūpe caturdaśa /
>
> vinā gandharasaghrāṇajihvāvijñānadhātubhiḥ // 1.30 //

> The others are threefold. All belong to the desire realm. In the form
> realm there are fourteen, excluding smell, taste, and the olfactory-
> and gustatory-cognition domains.

“The others” are the ten domains remaining after the eight ethically
indeterminate domains of 1.29. “Threefold” means wholesome, unwholesome,
and ethically indeterminate. A new classification begins with “all belong
to the desire realm.” The verse therefore crosses a topical boundary;
its opening must not be translated as threefold realm-membership.

The unit runs from the explanation of *tridhā 'nye* through the conclusion
that fourteen domains belong to the form realm, at printed pages
20.10–21.19 of the local running Sanskrit. The formless-realm enumeration
belongs to 1.31.

Spacing and evident transcription defects have been regularized:
*rupe*, *spraṣṭavya viśeṣeṇa*, *vyavahārśca*, *indariyasatṛṣṇasya*,
*puruṣendraiyam*, *ahīnendariyā*, *puruśendriyasya*, and
*ghrāṇajivhendriye*. The broken *vinā'; śrayaśobhaiva* is read
*vināśrayaśobhaiva*, following the local research transcription. The
witness's *nivarttate* is retained below as a textual difficulty, rather
than silently changed to a verb of production. The compact ethical
compounds and *tadanyāvyākṛtau* are retained; their construal is explained
in the translation and analysis.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The other ten domains are wholesome, unwholesome, and ethically
indeterminate. Of these, the seven [cognition-domains] are wholesome when
associated with non-greed and the like, unwholesome when associated with
greed and the like, and otherwise ethically indeterminate.

The dharma-domain is wholesome insofar as it consists of non-greed and
the like, is associated with them, or arises from them; discerning
cessation is also wholesome. It is unwholesome insofar as it consists of
greed and the like, is associated with them, or arises from them. The
remainder is ethically indeterminate.

The visible-form and sound domains, insofar as they are included in
bodily and verbal manifestation and arise from wholesome or unwholesome
citta, are respectively wholesome or unwholesome. Apart from these,
they are ethically indeterminate. Their wholesome and other statuses
have now been explained.

Of these eighteen domains, how many belong to the desire realm and how
many to the form realm? All belong to the desire realm. “Belonging” means
not separated from it, connected with the desire realm. In the form realm
there are fourteen domains, excluding smell, taste, and the olfactory-
and gustatory-cognition domains.

Smell and taste do not exist there, because they constitute morsel-made
food and those born there are free from passion for that food.
Consequently olfactory and gustatory cognition are also absent, because
their objects are absent.

Then the tangible domain should also be absent there, since it too
constitutes morsel-made food. — The tangible that is not of the nature of
food exists there. — The same consequence should apply to smell and
taste. — There is no enjoyment of smell and taste apart from ingestion.
The tangible, however, is used as the support of faculties, as a support,
and as a covering. Therefore smell and taste serve no purpose there for
those free from passion for ingestion, whereas the tangible does.

Others say: through dependence on meditative attainment, visible forms
are seen here, sounds are heard, and the body is benefited by a special
tangible accompanied by pliancy. Therefore precisely these three occur
in birth resulting from meditation, but smell and taste do not.

Then the nose and tongue faculties should also be absent, since they
serve no purpose. — They have a purpose: without them the bodily support
would lack beauty, and there would be no speech or ordinary communication.
— If that is the purpose, let the bodily sites alone exist for beauty and
speech; there need be no faculty. — A bodily site without a faculty cannot
occur, as with the site of the male faculty. — Its non-occurrence is
reasonable because it serves no purpose. The bodily sites of nose and
tongue, however, have a purpose; their existence even without a faculty
is therefore reasonable.

Even a faculty serving no purpose can be produced, as with those certain
to die in the womb. — Granted that it may serve no purpose, it is not
without a cause. What causes a faculty to arise? A particular karma of
one who thirsts for that faculty. And one who is free from thirst for the
object is surely free from thirst for the faculty too. Thus the nose and
tongue faculties should not occur in those free from passion for their
objects.

Or why does the male faculty not also [arise there]? — Because it would
impair beauty. — Why would it not be beautiful in those whose private
organ is enclosed in a sheath? Moreover, arising depends on causes,
not on usefulness. Thus even something that impairs beauty would arise
if its cause were present.

But then the sūtra is contradicted: “[They are] complete, without
deficient faculties.” — They are complete and without deficiency with
respect to the faculties that exist there. What contradiction is there?
Otherwise the consequence would extend to the male faculty as well.

They explain it, however, in this way: the nose and tongue faculties do
exist there, but smell and taste do not. Craving becomes active toward
the six [internal] bases through one's own embodied existence, rather
than through their objects. In the case of the male faculty, however,
it operates through sexual contact. Thus it is established that fourteen
domains belong to the form realm.

The bracketed “arise there” gives the contextual demand of the male-faculty
objection. The transmitted *kiṃ na nivarttate* literally suggests “why does
it not cease/turn back?”, which does not readily fit the reply about
beauty and the ensuing insistence on causal production. The research
translation's “remain” is also interpretive. The reading needs further
textual checking; the source has not been silently emended.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The opening is direct classificatory exposition: seven cognition-domains,
the dharma-domain, and visible form and sound exhaust the remaining ten.
The commentary explicitly closes this topic before asking about realms.

The form-realm discussion then proceeds through objections and replies.
First comes the absence of alimentary objects and their cognitions;
next, the continued availability of non-alimentary tangible objects.
*Anye punar āhuḥ* introduces an alternative explanation grounded in
meditative attainment. It is a reported account, not an unmarked claim
by the author.

The longest debate concerns faculties without their usual objects.
Beauty, speech, bodily sites, purposeless production, karmic causation,
and scriptural completeness are successively tested. These arguments
are not cumulative endorsements: later objections expose limits in
earlier defenses.

*Evaṃ tu varṇayanti*, “they explain it, however, in this way,” introduces
the concluding defense of fourteen. Its explanation distinguishes craving
for embodied existence from craving through particular objects. The
conclusion is explicit; a named school and Vasubandhu's personal assent
to every preceding argument are not.

## 5. The Threefold Ethical Classification

The seven domains are the six kinds of sensory and mental cognition
plus manas. Their ethical determination here is by association
(*saṃprayoga*). The text does not say that every instance is wholesome,
unwholesome, and indeterminate simultaneously: these are alternative
statuses within each domain.

The dharma-domain requires a wider account. Its compound
*alobhādisvabhāvasaṃprayuktasamutthaḥ* coordinates being of the nature of
non-greed and the like, being associated with them, and arising from
them. The negative ethical series is parallel. Discerning cessation
(*pratisaṃkhyānirodha*) is expressly added on the wholesome side. The
classification therefore cannot be reduced to association with a present
citta alone.

For visible form and sound, the important restriction is
*kāyavāgvijñaptisaṃgṛhītau*: bodily and verbal manifestation. Their
wholesome or unwholesome status follows the citta from which those
manifestations arise. This does not classify every pleasant color or
agreeable sound as wholesome. The remaining cases are indeterminate.

The numeral checks the construal: seven cognition-domains, one
dharma-domain, and two domains of manifestation make ten. Together with
the eight from 1.29, the eighteen are accounted for. This is the missing
opening in the local research commentary and the decisive correction
to its translation of *tridhānye*.

## 6. Realm Connection and the Four Exclusions

*Āptāḥ* is glossed by *aviyuktāḥ* and *pratisaṃyuktāḥ*: not separated,
connected. “Belong to” preserves this technical relation without reducing
it to occupying a geographical location. Here *rūpadhātu*, the form realm,
must also be distinguished from *rūpadhātu*, the visible-form domain
among the eighteen. Context determines the scale of classification.

| Excluded from the form realm | Immediate explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Smell-domain | Connection with morsel-made food |
| Taste-domain | Connection with morsel-made food |
| Olfactory-cognition domain | Absence of its object |
| Gustatory-cognition domain | Absence of its object |

The compound following *vinā* names these four. It does not exclude the
nose and tongue faculties themselves. All five faculties, visible form,
sound, the tangible, manas, the dharma-domain, and the four remaining
cognition-domains yield fourteen.

The food argument is an argument within the transmitted cosmology.
Its statement about enjoyment of smell and taste through ingestion is
preserved as such; it should not be inflated into a universal empirical
definition of smelling. The objection concerning the tangible already
forces a distinction between a domain and its alimentary use.

The alternative meditative account links visible form, sound, and a
special tangible accompanied by *prasrabdhi* to birth through meditation.
It does not assert that every sensory cognition operates during every
absorption. Its precise experiential and temporal scope is not fully
specified in this paragraph.

## 7. Faculty, Bodily Site, Purpose, and Cause

*Indriya* and *adhiṣṭhāna* cannot be interchanged. A nose-shaped bodily
site and the olfactory faculty are distinguishable in the argument;
likewise the tongue's contribution to speech does not itself establish
a gustatory capacity. The research rendering “ordinary designation” for
*vyavahāra* is too narrow here: the following *vacanārtham*, “for speech,”
clarifies the practical function under discussion.

The embryonic example separates production from subsequent use. A
faculty may arise although it will never perform its ordinary work.
The counterargument grants that point and asks for a cause. Thus
*niḥprayojanā* and *nirhetukā* mark different claims: purposeless does
not mean causeless.

The final explanation addresses the proposed causal inference directly.
Freedom from passion for an external object does not automatically
remove every form of craving connected with the corresponding internal
basis. Attachment through one's own embodied existence supplies the
relevant distinction. The male faculty is treated differently because
its craving is explained through sexual contact.

The scripture's “complete” is also interpreted with a restricted domain:
complete in the faculties that belong there. It cannot establish that
every possible faculty must occur, since that would include the male
faculty too. This is an argument about the scope of predication, not a
license to ignore the quoted sūtra.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- *Tridhā anye* completes ethical classification: the other ten admit
  wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate instances.
- *Kāmadhātvāptāḥ sarve* begins the realm classification independently.
- *Rūpe* means the form realm here, not the visible-form domain.
- The four exclusions are two objects and their two cognitions; the
  corresponding faculties remain in the concluding account.
- Non-alimentary tangible form remains available; a domain is not
  exhausted by one use of it.
- The defense of retained faculties ultimately distinguishes causal
  attachment to embodied existence from attachment through objects.

These decisions govern this edition's anchor and translation. The
original research kārikā and commentary files remain unchanged.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

For the Organon inquiry, the passage offers a precise way to distinguish
a capacity, its bodily site, its object, its operation, and the causes
of its production. These distinctions become necessary exactly where
the ordinary faculty–object–cognition conjunction is interrupted. A
retained faculty does not by itself entail a corresponding present act
of cognition.

This bears on the project's question of how learning and prajñā manifest:
possessing a basis, encountering an object, and actually understanding
are different determinations. The present passage does not explain the
acquisition of prajñā or establish its belonging to paramārtha. It gives
a narrower analytic discipline for asking that further question without
treating capacity as achievement.

The causal debate is equally useful. An explanation of what something
is useful for is insufficient to explain its arising. The commentary
makes the reader ask what produces the capacity, which attachment
sustains that production, and whether object-detachment also removes
that attachment. These are concrete questions within its account of
rebirth and faculties; their extension to an Organon account of learning
remains project-level interpretation.

Provisional thirtieth study in the Bhāṣya edition. The local kārikā,
research commentary, and running Sanskrit have been compared. The
ethical opening omitted from the research unit is restored, and its
incorrect realm-based construal of “threefold” is corrected here.

The difficult *nivarttate* remains visible with a bracketed contextual

unprovided school labels. This is comparison of the available local
