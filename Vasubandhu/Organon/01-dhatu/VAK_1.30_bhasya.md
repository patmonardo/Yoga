# VAK_1.30 Bhāṣya — Threefold, and Fourteen

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tridhānye kāmadhātvāptāḥ sarve rūpe caturdaśa /
> vinā gandharasaghrāṇajihvāvijñānadhātubhiḥ // 1.30 //

> The other ten are threefold. All eighteen are connected with the desire
> realm; fourteen occur in the Form realm, excluding the Principles of smell,
> taste, nose-Cognition, and tongue-Cognition.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The others are threefold.
>
> The other ten principles are wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate. Of them, the Cognition Principle, the seven — six cognition-principles and the mind-principle — associated with non-greed and the rest is wholesome; associated with greed and the rest, unwholesome; otherwise, indeterminate.
>
> The dharma-principle is wholesome when it has the nature of non-greed and the rest, is associated with them, or has arisen from them, and so is the cessation through discrimination. It is unwholesome when it has the nature of greed and the rest, is associated with them, or has arisen from them. The rest is indeterminate.
>
> The visible-form and sound principles, included in bodily and verbal vijñapti and arisen from a wholesome or unwholesome consciousness, are wholesome or unwholesome. Other than that, they are indeterminate. The wholesome and the rest have been stated.
>
> Of these eighteen principles, how many are connected with the desire realm, how many with the form realm?
>
> All are connected with the desire realm.
>
> Connected means not separated, connected with the desire realm.
>
> In the form realm, fourteen.
>
> Fourteen principles in the form realm, excluding the smell, taste, nose-cognition, and tongue-cognition principles.
>
> For smell and taste are not there, because they are morsel food, and those free of passion for that are born there. Hence nose-cognition and tongue-cognition are not there either, because the support is absent.
>
> Then the tangible principle also should be absent there, because it is morsel food. — What is not of the nature of food is there. — The same would follow for smell and taste. — There is no enjoyment of smell and taste apart from ingestion. The tangible, however, is there as the support of an organ, as a basis, and as a covering. So for those free of passion for ingestion, smell and taste are of no use there, but the tangible is not.
>
> Others say: depending on the attainment of concentration, forms are seen here and sounds are heard, and the body is favored by a special tangible accompanied by pliancy. So just these three occur in a birth from concentration, and smell and taste do not.
>
> Then the nose and tongue organs should be absent, because they are of no use. — They have a use. Without them the support would not be beautiful, and there would be no discourse of them. — If that is the use, let the site alone be, for beauty and for speech. Let there be no organ. — A site that is not an organ does not occur, as with the site of the male organ. — That absence is fitting, because it is of no use. The site of nose and tongue has a use, so its occurrence without the organ is fitting. — The production of an organ can be of no use, as with those certain to die in the womb. It may be of no use. It is not without a cause. And what is the cause of an organ's arising? A special action of one who craves the organ. And one free of craving for the condition is certainly free of craving for the organ too. So the nose and tongue organs should not occur for those free of passion for that condition. — Then why does the male organ not cease? — Because it does not make for ugliness. — Why should the concealed bladder and the private part not be beautiful? — And arising is not by force of a use. By what, then? By force of a cause. So even what does not make for beauty does arise when the cause is present.
>
> Then the sūtra is contradicted: “not deficient, not lacking organs.” — Not deficient by the organs that are there, not lacking those organs. What contradiction is there? Otherwise the same would follow for the male organ.
>
> They explain it thus. The nose and tongue organs are there. Smell and taste are not. For the activity of craving toward the six essences proceeds by way of the person, not by way of the condition. Toward the male organ, it proceeds by way of sexual contact. Therefore this is established: fourteen principles are connected with the form realm.

## 4. Movement

The ethical threefold closes the question opened at 1.29. The realm question begins at the second phrase of the verse. The Form Realm debate is objection and answer: food, support, use, site, cause, and the sūtra. The formless count is not this unit.

## 5. Decisions

1. The others are the ten, not the eighteen.
2. Threefold is wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate.
3. The seven are the Cognition Principle: six cognition-principles and the mind-principle, classified by association.
4. All eighteen are connected with the desire realm.
5. The form realm has fourteen. The four excluded are smell, taste, nose-cognition, and tongue-cognition.
6. Nose and tongue, the organs, are not excluded.
7. Absence of the support removes the cognition. A use does not, by itself, remove the organ.

## 6. Interpretation

The realm name and the principle name share a stem. They are not the same cut. The desire realm is not one of the eighteen. The form realm is not the visible-form principle. The two excluded cognitions are cognition-principles. Cognition is not principle. The formless count is the next verse.
