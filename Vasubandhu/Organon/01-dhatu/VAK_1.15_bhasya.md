## 3. Continuous Translation

The others, apart from the four, are the formations-base. The formations apart
from the four — form, feeling, reflection, and the principle-base — are the
formations-base.

In a sūtra, however, the Blessed One said six groups of volition, because of
predominance. For that volition is predominant in the active forming, because
it has the nature of karma. For this very reason the Blessed One said: it
actively forms the conditioned; therefore it is called the formations-base of
appropriation.

Otherwise, since the remaining associated factors and the dissociated
formations would not be included in a base, they would not have the truth of
suffering and of origin. Full comprehension and abandonment would not apply to
them either. And the Blessed One said: I do not declare the ending of
suffering without directly knowing and fully comprehending even a single
dharma. Likewise, without abandoning, is said. Therefore their inclusion in
the formations-base must necessarily be accepted.

formations-base, in the essence-and-principle arrangement, together with
*avijñapti* and the unconditioned, are called the dharma-essence and the
dharma-principle. These seven are designated the dharma-essence and the
dharma-principle.

## 6. Inclusion and the Work of the Path

The counterfactual begins with `anyathā`, otherwise. If the remaining
mental factors and dissociated formations were excluded here, they would
not be gathered in any Base. The argument traces a consequence for
the truths and their corresponding practical tasks:

```text
exclusion from the base classification
    → exclusion from suffering and origin
    → absence of full comprehension and abandonment with respect to them
```

The genitives `śeṣāṇāṃ caitasikānāṃ viprayuktānāṃ ca saṃskārāṇām`
identify the factors whose inclusion is at issue. The modal `na syāt` and
dual `na syātām` state what would follow from the proposed exclusion.
The closing necessity, `avaśyam ... abhyupagantavyaḥ`, answers that
counterfactual: their inclusion must be accepted.

The quoted negative absolutives `anabhijñāya` and `aparijñāya` concern
failure to know directly and comprehend fully; the parallel `aprahāya`
concerns failure to abandon. Together they ground the refusal to leave
relevant factors outside the work required for ending suffering.

This argument concerns inclusion within the scope of the truths and Path.
It does not make the act of classifying a factor itself accomplish its
comprehension or abandonment. Nor should the reference to suffering and
origin be generalized to make every conditioned formation sāsrava: the
conditioned anāsrava Path has already been distinguished in the earlier
studies. The argument must retain the relevant scope of the factors under
discussion and the different tasks that apply to them.

## 7. The Seven Constituents of the Dharma Essence and Dharma Principle

**From the three Bases to Essence and Principle**

`Ete punas trayaḥ` is explicitly resolved as the Feeling, Reflection,
and Formations Bases. `Saha` governs their accompaniment by *avijñapti* and
the unconditioned. The locative `āyatanadhātuvyavasthāyām` specifies the
arrangement in which the names *dharmāyatana* and *dharmadhātu* apply.

```text
ete punas trayaḥ                  these three again
vedanā-saṃjñā-saṃskāra-skandhāḥ   Feeling, Reflection, Formations Bases
āyatana-dhātu-vyavasthāyām        in the Essence and Principle arrangement
dharma-āyatana-dhātu-ākhyāḥ       bearing the Dharma designations
saha avijñaptyasaṃskṛtaiḥ         together with avijñapti and the unconditioned
```

*Ākhyā* supplies the naming construction. *Dharma* applies to both
*āyatana* and *dhātu*. *Saha* governs the instrumental compound whose
members are *avijñapti* and *asaṃskṛta*. The locative
*vyavasthāyām* states the arrangement under which this naming holds.

## 9. Interpretation

**The remainder and the work of inclusion**

The Formations Base is defined by remainder: conditioned formations
other than the Form Base, Feeling Base, Reflection Base, and principle-base. The
Bhāṣya's appeal to six groups of volition does not narrow that remainder.
*Cetanā* is prominent because it is karmic in character and active in
forming the conditioned; predominance explains the sūtra's emphasis,
not the exhaustive content of the Base.

The inclusion argument then makes the classification consequential.
To exclude the remaining associated and dissociated formations would
leave them outside the scope of suffering and origin, full comprehension,
and abandonment. Yet classification is not itself comprehension or
abandonment, and the argument does not make every conditioned formation
impure. A Kantian-transcendental reading can ask what systematic
completeness requires for every relevant Dharma to be determinable within
the Path's field. That is an Organon question, not a claim that the
Bhāṣya teaches Kantian doctrine or that the Path's operations are
equivalent to Kantian synthesis.

**The seven under Dharma Essence and Dharma Principle**

The second line names Feeling, Reflection, and Formations Bases again
under the Dharma Essence and Dharma Principle arrangements. *Saha*
brings *avijñapti* and the three unconditioned dharmas into the naming.
The Bhāṣya's count is three, one, three:

| Constituent | Base | Essence | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feeling | Feeling Base | Dharma Essence | Dharma Principle |
| Reflection | Reflection Base | Dharma Essence | Dharma Principle |
| Formations | Formations Base | Dharma Essence | Dharma Principle |
| *avijñapti* | Form Base | Dharma Essence | Dharma Principle |
| space | none | Dharma Essence | Dharma Principle |
| cessation through discrimination | none | Dharma Essence | Dharma Principle |
| cessation without discrimination | none | Dharma Essence | Dharma Principle |

This is the Organon's cross-reading of the Bhāṣya's count, not a claim
that the Kārikā enumerates seven homogeneous Bases. *Avijñapti* remains
Form and retains its Form Base; the unconditioned remains unconditioned
and has no Base. *Dharmaskandha* (Dharma Base) is not named here and is
not interchangeable with *dharmāyatana* (Dharma Essence) or
*dharmadhātu* (Dharma Principle).

In the project's Kantian frame, Principle Science examines how
principial determinations make a system intelligible. Here that reading
must preserve the differences the Bhāṣya insists on, rather than
transforming the shared designation into a single kind of substance.
In the Organon's current technical model, Principles are Starting Points
for rule systems that determine subsequent valuations. This verse does
not specify those rules; it identifies the constituents that remain
within the system's scope of determination.

The word *vijñānaskandha* in the first clause names the principle-base;
it is not by itself the Organon's term *vijñāna* for Principle Science.
The Base–Essence–Principle projections are determinations of one Dharma,
not three separate Dharmas. The sevenfold cross-mapping is an
interpretation of the source's enumeration, not a translation of the
Kārikā.
