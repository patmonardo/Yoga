# VAK_1.37 Bhāṣya — Maturation, Reinforcement, and Causal Continuation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vipākajaupacayikāḥ pañcādhyātmaṃ vipākajaḥ /
> na śabdo 'pratighā aṣṭau naiḥṣyandikavipākajāḥ // 1.37 //

> The five internal Faculty Principles are maturation-born and reinforced
> through increase. Sound is not maturation-born. The eight non-resistant
> Principles have instances born of causal continuity and of maturation.

*Vipākajaḥ* at the end of the first line is completed by *na śabdaḥ*
at the beginning of the second. The line break must not turn it into
a positive assertion that Sound is maturation-born. The eight-Principle
statement identifies causal classes among their instances; it does not
make every member a product of both causes.

The anchor follows the kārikā witness with sandhi applied (*śabdo 'pratighā*);
the running Bhāṣya text reads the unsandhied *śabdaḥ apratighā*.

## 3. Continuous Translation

How many Principles arise through maturation, how many are reinforced
through increase, how many arise through causal continuity, how many
possess substance, and how many are momentary? The reply begins:

The five internal Principles, beginning with the Eye Faculty, are
maturation-born and reinforced through increase. No separate
continuity-born class is counted for them, because there is no such
continuity apart from the two just named.

Why call them maturation-born? They arise from a maturation Ground;
the word for Ground is omitted from the compound, as a middle term can
be omitted in “ox-cart.” Alternatively, karma that has reached the time
of its result is called maturation because it ripens; what arises from
that karma is maturation-born. Strictly speaking, the ripened result is
the maturation. Here the result's name is transferred to its cause,
just as a cause's name can be transferred to its result: “These six
Contact Essences should be understood as old karma.”

What is reinforced through increase? That which is built up by food,
conditioning, sleep, and particular states of concentration. Some also
name celibate discipline. The reply is that this prevents injury; it
does not itself produce increase. The continuing reinforcement protects
the continuing maturation like a surrounding wall.

Sound can arise through reinforcement or corresponding causal
continuity, but it is not maturation-born. Why? Because it proceeds
from exertion.

But the Prajñaptiśāstra says that thoroughly cultivating abstention
from harsh speech produces the great-person mark of a Brahmā-like
voice. How can Sound be excluded? Some explain this as a three-member
sequence: karma produces Great Elements, which produce Sound. Others
give a five-member sequence: karma produces maturation-born Great
Elements; these give rise to reinforced Great Elements; these to
continuity-born Great Elements; and these to Sound.

Objection: bodily Feeling also arises from karma-born Great Elements.
By that reasoning, would it too cease to count as maturation?
Reply: it would, if the same conflict with reasoning arose in its case
as in Sound's.

The eight non-resistant Principles have continuity-born and
maturation-born instances. They are the seven Cognition Principles and the
Dharma Principle. Their continuity-born instances arise from
homogeneous and universally operative Grounds; their maturation-born
instances arise from a maturation Ground. They are not reinforced
through increase, because non-resistant Principles [do not accumulate].

The bracketed final phrase follows the research repair of the damaged
word. “Conditioning” leaves *saṃskāra* in the reinforcement list broad;
this paragraph does not specify a regimen of bodily treatment or exercise.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The introductory question announces five headings, but this verse
develops the first three for the Faculties, Sound, and eight
non-resistant Principles. It is not a complete answer to the entire
opening question.

The explanation of *vipākaja* offers alternative analyses of the word,
followed by an example of figurative transfer between cause and result.
The reinforcement account introduces an addition by “some” and then
qualifies it. Neither that addition nor the two answers concerning
voice receives a named school attribution.

The Prajñaptiśāstra is explicitly named as the source of the vocal-mark
objection. The bodily-feeling objection tests the proposed causal
succession accounts, and a brief conditional reply follows. Assigning
every turn to Vasubandhu's personal position would conceal the exchange.
The unit returns to direct classification in its final sentences.

## 5. Maturation and the Omitted Middle Member

*Vipākahetor jātāḥ* expands *vipākaja* as born from a
*maturation Ground*. The omitted member is *hetu*. *Gorathavat*
illustrates the grammatical mechanism through a familiar compound,
an ox-cart, whose relation is understood without spelling out every
intermediate word.

The alternative treats karma at the time of fruition as “maturation.”
The prose then distinguishes the result's own designation as ripening
from the transferred use of that name for the cause. The six
Contact Essences called “old karma” illustrate transfer in the reverse
direction: naming the result by its cause.

These are explanations of technical naming and causation. They do not
license an identity of every result with its cause. The transfer is
expressly described as *upacāra*.

For the five Faculty Principles, *tadvyatiriktaniṣyandābhāva* is also
important. The denial of a separate homogeneous-continuation class does
not deny Faculty continuity altogether. It says there is no such
continuation outside the maturation and upacaya classes already
specified.

## 6. Reinforcement and Protection from Injury

*Aupacayika* is explained through *upacita*, built up. Food,
conditioning, sleep, and distinctions of concentration supply the
listed conditions. The classification concerns upacaya-reinforcement;
it should not be equated with the atomic accumulation distinguished in
1.35. A Form-Principle can be atomically accumulated without that alone
deciding its present causal class.

The proposed addition of *brahmacarya* receives a precise reply:
*anupaghātamātra*, merely non-injury, is distinguished from *upacaya*,
increase. Preserving something and positively building it up are not
being counted as the same contribution here.

The wall analogy relates two continua: upacaya protects maturation. It
does not identify them or make their distinction one between two
self-standing continua. Nor does the paragraph
specify that every listed condition has the same effect or operates
by the same mechanism.

## 7. Sound, Indirect Karmic Dependence, and the Counterexample

*Īhātaḥ pravṛtti* is the stated reason for excluding Sound from
maturation-born status. “Exertion” preserves the initiating-activity
sense without silently restricting every Sound to conscious human
speech. The vocal-mark objection then supplies a case in which a
Sound-related excellence is expressly connected with karma.

The two replies give these successions:

| Reported account | Ordered members |
| --- | --- |
| Third-member succession | Karma → Great Elements → Sound |
| Fifth-member succession | Karma → maturation-born Great Elements → upacaya-reinforced Great Elements → homogeneous-continuation Great Elements → Sound |

The numbers count members of the stated succession, not three or five
causal links. Both retain a karmic connection while distinguishing
the eventual Sound from a direct classification as maturation-born.

The bodily-feeling counterexample prevents distance in a causal chain
from doing all the explanatory work. Bodily Feeling also arises from
karma-born Great Elements. The reply invokes whether the Sound-like conflict
with reasoning applies; it does not accept a universal rule that
mediation excludes maturation.

Accordingly, “everything produced through present activity is excluded
from maturation” would be broader than this unit establishes. The
source argues a particular exclusion and tests its justification.

## 8. The Eight Non-resistant Principles and the Kārikā's Decisions

The eight are the seven Cognition Principles plus the Dharma Principle.
Their homogeneous-continuation instances are defined by *sabhāgahetu* and
*sarvatragahetu*, while maturation-born instances are defined by
*vipākahetu*. “Continuation” here names specific causal relations,
not simply persistence through time.

The Dharma Principle includes unconditioned dharmas. They cannot be
made products of these causes merely by belonging to that Principle.
The verse identifies the relevant generated classes within the
Principles; the announced further classifications still await 1.38.

The resulting translation decisions are:

- Keep the negation with *vipākajaḥ*: Sound is not maturation-born.
- Treat the five as the five sensory Faculties, not all internal
  Principles without restriction.
- Preserve the reason why no additional continuation class is counted
  for those Faculties.
- Distinguish reinforcement from mere protection against injury.
- Preserve both mediated voice accounts and the bodily-feeling test.
- Read the final causal predicates according to instances and causes,
  without making the unconditioned causally produced.

## 9. Interpretation

After 1.36 distinguishes the Faculty from the outer configuration that
cuts and is cut, 1.37 asks how the Faculty itself is generated and
maintained. Maturation supplies one causal history; food, sleep, and
concentration can reinforce its living continuity. Protection from
injury is a further, weaker claim than positive increase. These
distinctions give the Instrument a temporal and causal account, without
making the Faculty into the material implement it employs.

The Sound exchange distinguishes a karmic causal history from direct
membership in the class of maturation-results. The objection about
bodily Feeling prevents the proposed sequence from becoming a blanket
rule: each classification still needs its own reason.

These distinctions may help frame how prajñā is cultivated and
manifests within saṃvṛti: establishing conditions, sustaining a capacity,
and the occurrence of understanding require separate explanations.
The source does not here classify prajñā's relation to paramārtha or
introduce a Seer for whose sake the Faculties exist. Those would be
additional Organon determinations, beyond the causal account given.
