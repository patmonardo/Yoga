`Dhātu` is Principle. *Vijñāna* is Cognition. The Bhāṣya identifies the larger list as six Principles: earth, water, fire, wind, space, and Cognition. The verse's plural `janmaniśrayāḥ` predicates birth-support of the six together, not of the singular Cognition Principle alone.

**Grammar tokens.** Verse-order IAST with sandhi opened (`vijñānadhātur` → *vijñāna-dhātuḥ*, `vijñānaṃ` → *vijñānam*, `sāsravaṃ` → *sa-āsravam*) and compound boundaries marked. The subject of the last clause, *ime ṣaḍ dhātavaḥ*, is supplied by the Bhāṣya inside the rule (`SUPPLIED=prose`), not as a covert leaf.

## 4. Grammar

Three clauses. The first identifies an opening as what is named the Space Principle, with a reported dual nature. The second identifies the Cognition Principle as Impure Cognition. The third, with its plural predicate, needs the six Principles as subject. Agreement binds *chidram* with its naming compound, and *vijñānam* with *sa-āsravam*; the final predicate must be plural.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Space spans [0, 4); cognition [4, 7); support [7, 8); the root spans [0, 8). Three clauses. No claim that the Space Principle is unconditioned is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** An opening is what is named the Space Principle: light and darkness, it is said. The Cognition Principle is Impure Cognition. [These six Principles] are supports of birth.

`Named(x,n)` is designation within this six-Principle teaching, not an identity of the classified Dharma with everything bearing the name. `ReportedNature` keeps *kila*: the light-and-darkness account is received, not asserted outright. The Cognition clause restricts by conjunction (Cognition, Impure); it does not define Cognition. The plural predicate ranges over all six, not only over the Cognition Principle.

`Ālokatamasī` is dual. The bracketed subject of the final clause is made explicit by the Bhāṣya: `ime ṣaḍ dhātavaḥ`, these six Principles.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> An opening is named the Space Principle—light and darkness, it is said. The Cognition Principle is Impure Cognition; these [six] are supports of birth.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the teaching of six Principles, an opening is called the Space Principle—light and darkness, it is said. The Cognition Principle is Impure Cognition; these six Principles are supports of birth.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The same name does not guarantee the same determination. In this particular teaching, “space” names a conditioned opening and “Cognition” is restricted to Impure Cognition. The six Principles are considered together by their shared function as supports of birth; this does not make them six new members outside the established eighteen Principles.

**Interpretation**

Four of the six Principles enter the Tangible Principle. The conditioned Space Principle enters the Form Principle. Impure Cognition is placed among the seven Cognition Principles: the six Cognition Principles, and the Mind Principle. The restriction does not define Cognition in every context.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** Kila marks the reported light-and-darkness explanation; the Bhāṣya locates the opening in doors, windows, mouth, nose, and similar places, and explains light and darkness as night and day. It restricts the Cognition Principle here to Impure Cognition and treats the six as supports from rebirth-linking through death; their assignments are four in the tangible Principle, the fifth in the visible-Form Principle, and the sixth among the seven Cognition Principles. The Bhāṣya's reading cittasthaṃ is retained but remains difficult; the local HTML does not independently confirm it.

VAK 1.28 is the worked case following the assignment procedure of 1.27. The *Bahudhātuka* teaching gives six Principles. The Bhāṣya specifies the two initially unexplained members: the Space Principle is an opening apprehended through light and darkness, and the Cognition Principle is Impure Cognition. It then maps the six into the eighteen Principles: four into the Tangible Principle, one into the Form Principle, and one among the seven Cognition Principles.

The Space Principle here is not unconditioned space. The shared word *ākāśa* does not erase the distinction between non-obstruction and the conditioned opening described in this passage. Likewise, the restriction to Impure Cognition applies to this birth-support list; it does not define Cognition in every context.

The six-Principle teaching is assigned into the eighteen. The Space Principle here is the conditioned opening, not unconditioned space. The Cognition Principle here is Impure Cognition, one member among the seven, not Cognition as such.

The six Principles are supports of a life-continuum extending, in the Bhāṣya's account, from rebirth-linking Cognition through death Cognition. The classification is functional as well as referential: the included Cognition is the Cognition common to that birth-span. The difficult `cittasthaṃ` belongs to the Bhāṣya's explanation of *aghasāmantaka-rūpa*, not to this kārikā's wording, and remains unresolved in the separate commentary study.
