# VAK_1.17 Bhāṣya — Mind Is the Sixth Support

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ṣaṇṇām anantarātītaṃ vijñānaṃ yad dhi tan manaḥ /
>
> ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhyarthaṃ dhātavo 'ṣṭādaśa smṛtāḥ // 1.17 //

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Mind is not a seventh Principle beside the six. Whichever of those six
has just ceased is called Mind, and called the Mind Principle, in its
office as support. The five have supports of their own. The
Mind-Cognition Principle has no further support of that kind. Eighteen
are taught so that this sixth support be established.

The support does not require a successor. An arhat's final consciousness
remains mind. Another cause is wanting, and no next principle arises.

### Anchor note

The commentary opens the question 1.16 left standing, answers it, meets
a count-objection, and tests the answer on an arhat's final
consciousness. The next study is what the bases, essences, and principles
include.

The unit runs from the objection through the arhat's final consciousness.
What the bases include is the next study.

Word division is editorial. The running source's *kiṃ narhi*, *aṣṭādāśa*,
*ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhyartha*, and the damaged join on eighteen are read
as *kiṃ tarhi*, *aṣṭādaśa*, *ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhyarthaṃ*, and
*'ṣṭādaśa*. *Prasiddhacyartha*, *manodhāturūpadiṣṭaḥ*,
*ālambanaṣṭkavyavasathānād*, and *sasmanantarātītaṃ* are normalized from
the surrounding syntax. *Ucyeta* is kept: "would be called."
*Cakṣurdhātvāyatanādayaḥ* is kept and read as the five supports beginning
with the eye. These are normalizations, not a critical text.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Objection. But it was said that the six groups of Cognition are the
Cognition Base. What, then, is this Mind Principle, apparently other
than those?

Answer. It is certainly nothing other. What, then? Of those very six,
whichever Cognition is immediately past, that is Mind. Whichever
Cognition has ceased immediately before would be called the
Mind Principle. As the same son becomes father of another, and the same
fruit is the seed of another.

Objection. Then, by substance, there are seventeen Principles, or
twelve, since the six Cognition Principles and the Mind Principle
include one another. Why establish eighteen?

Answer. Even so, eighteen Principles are taught in order to establish
the sixth support. The five Cognition Principles have five supports,
beginning with the eye. The sixth, the Mind-Cognition Principle, has no
other support. Therefore the Mind Principle is taught, so that its
support be established. Thus, by the establishment of six supports, six
supported Cognition Principles, and six Objects, there are eighteen
Principles.

Objection. Then an arhat's final consciousness will not be Mind. There
is nothing for which it would be immediately past.

Answer. No. It too stands in the status of Mind. A next Cognition does
not arise because another cause is wanting.

## 5. Just Ceased

*Ṣaṇṇām* selects from the six. *Teṣām eva* excludes an additional
entity. *Yat ... tat* is the verse's own hinge.

*Anantarātīta* is glossed *samanantara-niruddha*: ceased immediately
before. Not "past" in general. Not the store of what is remembered.
Immediate precedence.

The two pictures keep one relatum under two names. They do not found a
theory of the person, or of the seed. They show the grammar of the
answer: not a second substance.

## 6. Why Eighteen

*Dravyataḥ* is the objection's measure. Seventeen, or twelve, are its
proposals, from mutual inclusion. They are not a replacement of the
eighteen, and the twelve here is not the twelve Essences.

The reply is a purpose. *Ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhyartham*: so that the sixth
support be established. The five already have supports, beginning with
the eye. The Mind-Cognition Principle has no further support of its own.
The Mind Principle is taught as that support.

| Relation | The six |
|---|---|
| *āśraya*, support | five, beginning with the eye, and the Mind Principle |
| *āśrita*, the supported | the six Cognition Principles |
| *ālambana*, object | the six Objects |

Support and Object are not the same relation. The commentary does not
merge them.

## 7. The Arhat's Last Consciousness

*Caramaṃ cittam* is the limit. If Mind meant "that for which a next
Cognition arises," this last consciousness would not be Mind. The reply
denies it.

*Manobhāvenāvasthitatvāt*: it stands in the status of Mind.
*Anyakāraṇavaikalyāt*: another cause is wanting, so no next Cognition
arises. The missing cause is not named in this unit. It is not supplied
from elsewhere.

Office and production stay apart. A formula that said "Mind is whatever
produces the next Cognition" would fail the commentary's own test.

## 8. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. The Mind named in the first line is not an additional Cognition
   beyond the six; it is whichever of the six has just ceased.
2. Immediately past means ceased immediately before.
3. Son and father, fruit and seed, are pictures of one relatum under two
   names.
4. Eighteen stands, against the objection's seventeen and twelve. The
   twelve is a substance-count.
5. The Mind Principle is the sixth support of the Mind-Cognition
   Principle; it is not another Cognition.
6. An arhat's final consciousness remains Mind. No successor arises
   because another cause is wanting.

## 9. Interpretation

The passage distinguishes the support role of the Mind Principle from
the supported role of the six Cognition Principles, but not as wholly
separate entities: it says that the Mind Principle is one of those very
six, the Cognition that has just ceased. The Mind-Cognition Principle is
the sixth supported Cognition Principle. These terms name different
positions in the argument and are not interchangeable. The eighteenfold
count is made by support, supported, and object despite this overlap.
*Vijñāna* means Cognition, not Principle; *dhātu* is rendered Principle
in these compounds.

The arhat's final-consciousness objection tests whether the support role
requires a succeeding Cognition. The Bhāṣya says it does not: the final
consciousness remains Mind although no successor arises. This supports
the distinction between a support relation and the production of a
subsequent Cognition; it does not establish a further technical
framework.

Keep *citta* (consciousness), *manas* (Mind), Mind Principle, and
Mind-Cognition Principle distinct.

## 10. Review Status

Terminology and argument checked against the Sanskrit passage.
*Vijñāna* is Cognition, *citta* is consciousness, and *manas* is Mind.
The six *vijñāna-dhātus* are six Cognition Principles;
*manodhātu* is Mind Principle; *manovijñāna-dhātu* is
Mind-Cognition Principle. The final-consciousness reply keeps Mind's
support role distinct from the production of a successor.
