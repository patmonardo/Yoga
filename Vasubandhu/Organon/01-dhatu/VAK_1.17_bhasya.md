# VAK_1.17 Bhāṣya — Mind Is the Sixth Support

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ṣaṇṇām anantarātītaṃ vijñānaṃ yad dhi tan manaḥ /
>
> ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhyarthaṃ dhātavo 'ṣṭādaśa smṛtāḥ // 1.17 //

Literal:

> Whichever principle among the six is immediately past, that indeed is
> mind. The eighteen principles are taught in order to establish the sixth
> support.

Bhāṣya-informed:

> Mind is not a seventh Principle beside the six. Whichever of those six
> has just ceased is called Mind, and called the Mind Principle, in its
> office as support. The five have supports of their own. The
> Mind-Cognition Principle has no further support of that kind. Eighteen
> are taught so that this sixth support be established.

The commentary opens the question 1.16 left standing, answers it, meets
a count-objection, and tests the answer on an arhat's final
consciousness. The next study is what the bases, essences, and principles
include.

*Cakṣurdhātvāyatanādayaḥ* is kept and read as the five supports beginning

## 3. Continuous Translation

Objection. But it was said that the six groups of Principle are the
principle-base. What, then, is this Mind Principle, other than those?

Answer. It is certainly nothing other. What, then? Of those very six,
whichever Principle is immediately past, that is Mind. Whichever
Principle has ceased immediately before would be called the
Mind Principle. As the same son becomes father of another, and the same
fruit is the seed of another.

Objection. Then, by substance, there are seventeen Principles, or
twelve, since the six Principles and the Mind Principle include one
another. Why establish eighteen?

Answer. Even so, eighteen Principles are taught in order to establish
the sixth support. The five Principles have five supports, beginning
with the eye. The sixth, the Mind-Cognition Principle, has no other
support. Therefore the Mind Principle is taught, so that its support be
established. Thus, by the establishment of six supports, six supported,
and six Objects, there are eighteen Principles.

Objection. Then an arhat's final consciousness will not be mind. There
is nothing for which it would be immediately past.

Answer. No. It too stands in the status of mind. A next principle does
not arise because another cause is wanting.

## 5. Just Ceased

*Ṣaṇṇām* selects from the six. *Teṣām eva* excludes a further principle.
*Yat ... tat* is the verse's own hinge.

*Anantarātīta* is glossed *samanantara-niruddha*: ceased immediately
before. Not "past" in general. Not the store of what is remembered.
Immediate precedence.

The two pictures keep one relatum under two names. They do not found a
theory of the person, or of the seed. They show the grammar of the
answer: not a second substance.

## 6. Why Eighteen

*Dravyataḥ* is the objection's measure. Seventeen, or twelve, are its
proposals, from mutual inclusion. They are not a replacement of the
eighteen, and the twelve here is not the twelve Essences.

The reply is a purpose. *Ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhyartham*: so that the sixth
support be established. The five already have supports, beginning with
the eye. The Mind-Cognition Principle has no other. Mind is taught as
that support.

| Relation | The six |
|---|---|
| *āśraya*, support | five, beginning with the eye, and Mind |
| *āśrita*, the supported | the six Principles |
| *ālambana*, object | the six Objects |

Support and Object are not the same relation. The commentary does not
merge them.

## 7. The Arhat's Last Consciousness

*Caramaṃ cittam* is the limit. If mind meant "that for which a next
principle arises," this last consciousness would not be mind. The reply
denies it.

*Manobhāvenāvasthitatvāt*: it stands in the status of mind.
*Anyakāraṇavaikalyāt*: another cause is wanting, so no next principle
arises. The missing cause is not named in this unit. It is not supplied
from elsewhere.

Office and production stay apart. A formula that said "mind is whatever
produces the next" would fail the commentary's own test.

## 8. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. The Mind Principle is not other than the six. It is one of them, just
   ceased.
2. Immediately past means ceased immediately before.
3. Son and father, fruit and seed, are pictures of one relatum under two
   names.
4. Eighteen stands, against the objection's seventeen and twelve. The
   twelve is a substance-count.
5. Mind is the support of the Mind-Cognition Principle.
6. An arhat's final consciousness remains mind. No successor, because
   another cause is wanting.

## 9. Interpretation

Read with 1.16, Mind Principle is an office of one of the six Principles,
not a seventh substance. Present, that Principle is one of the six;
immediately ceased, it is Mind, the sixth support of the
Mind-Cognition Principle. The relation changes; the Principle does not
become another entity.

The Kantian-transcendental question is what makes the sixth
Mind-Cognition Principle determinable without a further sensory support.
The Bhāṣya answers relationally: the immediately past Principle occupies
the support position. The eighteenfold matrix comprises six supports,
six supported Principles, and six Objects. *Ālambana* is Object,
distinct from *āśraya* (support) and *viṣaya* (Condition). The Organon's
Principles are Starting Points for rule systems, but this verse does not
specify those rules. The Kantian frame does not make Mind Principle a
Kantian category.

In the project architecture, Mind-Cognition Principle is Transcendental
Logic (Abstract Reason / Syllogistic Reasoning), while Mind Principle is
Ordinary Logic. Their support relation here does not make them synonyms.
These are Organon determinations, not the conventional translation.

The arhat's final-*citta* objection tests whether support can be reduced
to production. It cannot: the final consciousness remains in the status
of Mind although no successor arises, because another cause is wanting.
Thus support is not a sufficient cause, and the office persists without
a subsequent Principle.

In the project architecture, *manas* is the Produced Cognitive
Instrument, equipped with Mind Essence and Mind Principle. This is not a
lexical gloss of the Bhāṣya, and its production/ownership relation
remains open. Keep *citta* (consciousness), *manas* (Mind), Mind Essence,
and Mind Principle distinct.
