## 5. Just Ceased

*Ṣaṇṇām* selects from the six. *Teṣām eva* excludes a further principle.
*Yat ... tat* is the verse's own hinge.

*Anantarātīta* is glossed *samanantara-niruddha*: ceased immediately before.
Not past in general. Immediate precedence.

The two pictures keep one relatum under two names. They do not found a theory
of the person, or of the seed.

## 7. The Arhat's Last Consciousness

*Caramaṃ cittam* is the limit. If mind meant that for which a next principle
arises, this last consciousness would not be mind. The reply denies it.

It stands in the status of mind. Another cause is wanting, so no next
principle arises. The missing cause is not named in this unit. It is not
supplied from elsewhere.

Office and production stay apart. A formula that said mind is whatever
produces the next would fail the commentary's own test.

## 9. Interpretation

The seventh name is an office. Present, the principle is one of the six. Just
ceased, that same principle is mind, the support of the mind-group principle.
The change is of relation, not of substance.

The five supports belong to the Form System: eye and the rest, organs. The
sixth support is not a sixth organ. It is the immediately past principle, in
the office of mind. That is why eighteen, and not a matter-count of twelve.

The arhat's case keeps the distinction the verse needs. A support is not a
sufficient cause. The office remains when the next does not arise.

Mind is not consciousness. The objection uses *citta* for the last moment, and
*manas* for the office. The answer keeps both. Consciousness stands as mind.
It does not become a second principle.

## 9. Interpretation

Read with 1.16, Mind Principle is an office of one of the six Principles,
not a seventh substance. Present, that Principle is one of the six;
immediately ceased, it is Mind, the sixth support of the
Mind-Cognition Principle. The relation changes; the Principle does not
become another entity.

The Kantian-transcendental question is what makes the sixth
Mind-Cognition Principle determinable without a further sensory support.
The Bhāṣya answers relationally: the immediately past Principle occupies
the support position. The eighteenfold matrix comprises six supports,
six supported Principles, and six Objects. *Ālambana* is Object,
distinct from *āśraya* (support) and *viṣaya* (Condition). The Organon's
Principles are Starting Points for rule systems, but this verse does not
specify those rules. The Kantian frame does not make Mind Principle a
Kantian category.

In the project architecture, Mind-Cognition Principle is Transcendental
Logic (Abstract Reason / Syllogistic Reasoning), while Mind Principle is
Ordinary Logic. Their support relation here does not make them synonyms.
These are Organon determinations, not the conventional translation.

The arhat's final-*citta* objection tests whether support can be reduced
to production. It cannot: the final consciousness remains in the status
of Mind although no successor arises, because another cause is wanting.
Thus support is not a sufficient cause, and the office persists without
a subsequent Principle.

In the project architecture, *manas* is the Produced Cognitive
Instrument, equipped with Mind Essence and Mind Principle. This is not a
lexical gloss of the Bhāṣya, and its production/ownership relation
remains open. Keep *citta* (consciousness), *manas* (Mind), Mind Essence,
and Mind Principle distinct.
