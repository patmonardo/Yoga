# VAK_1.08

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> ये सास्रवा उपादानस्कन्धास्ते सरणा अपि ।
>
> दुःखं समुदयो लोको दृष्टिस्थानं भवश्च ते ॥ १.०८ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> ye sāsravā upādānaskandhās te saraṇā api /
>
> duḥkhaṃ samudayo loko dṛṣṭisthānaṃ bhavaś ca te // 1.08 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

ye | sāsravāḥ | upādāna-skandhāḥ | te | saraṇāḥ | api |
duḥkham | samudayaḥ | lokaḥ | dṛṣṭi-sthānam | bhavaḥ | ca | te

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **ye** | nominative masculine plural relative | those among the aggregates of 1.07 |
| **sāsravāḥ** | nominative masculine plural adjective | with outflows; lodging-criterion already fixed at 1.04 |
| **upādāna-skandhāḥ** | nominative masculine plural compound | aggregates of appropriation |
| **te** | nominative masculine plural demonstrative | those same, not a new class beside them |
| **saraṇāḥ** | nominative masculine plural adjective | accompanied by conflict |
| **api** | indeclinable | also; adds a paryāya, not a further restriction |
| **duḥkham** | nominative neuter singular | suffering |
| **samudayaḥ** | nominative masculine singular | origin |
| **lokaḥ** | nominative masculine singular | world |
| **dṛṣṭi-sthānam** | nominative neuter singular compound | station of views |
| **bhavaḥ** | nominative masculine singular | becoming |
| **ca te** | conjunction + demonstrative | and they; the list remains coextensive with the sāsrava aggregates |

The relative clause is the restriction. Not every aggregate of 1.07 is an aggregate of appropriation.

## 4. Grammar

```text
ye skandhāḥ sāsravāḥ
    = upādānaskandhāḥ
    = saraṇāḥ
    = duḥkha / samudaya / loka / dṛṣṭisthāna / bhava
```

`Ye … te` marks identity under restriction. The Bhāṣya draws the immediate corollary:

```text
upādānaskandha → skandha
skandha ↛ upādānaskandha
```

because there are anāsrava saṃskāras (the Path as conditioned).

The second line is not a second inventory. It is a list of *anvarthaparyāya*: names that fit their meanings and remain coextensive here.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Those [aggregates] which are with outflows are the aggregates of appropriation; they are also accompanied by conflict. They are suffering, origin, world, the station of views, and becoming.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Those conditioned aggregates in which outflows can settle are the aggregates of appropriation. Whatever is an aggregate of appropriation is an aggregate, but not every aggregate is an aggregate of appropriation: there are formations without outflows. Here the appropriations are the afflictions. The aggregates are so called because they arise from appropriation, like fire from grass or chaff; or because they stand under appropriation, like the servants of a king; or because appropriations arise from them, like flowers and fruit from a tree. These same dharmas are called dharmas with outflows.

They are also accompanied by conflict. The afflictions are conflicts, because they injure oneself and others. Because those conflicts lie latent in the aggregates, the aggregates are accompanied by conflict, as they are with outflows.

They are suffering, because they are contrary to the noble ones. They are origin, because suffering arises from them. They are world, because they dissolve. They are the station of views, because views stand in them by lying latent there. They are becoming, because they come to be. These are names of the dharmas with outflows, each matching its meaning.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The five aggregates of 1.07 are the whole conditioned. This verse cuts that field by the lodging of outflow. Appropriation-aggregates are not a sixth pile and not a poetic synonym for “person.” They are the sāsrava restriction of the same five.

This remains the conditioned Thing determined in 1.07, not another object
beside it. Here its features enter the reciprocal operation of appropriation
and affliction: the field is produced by, governed by, and productive of that
operation. This is the Organon continuation of the Bhāṣya's three
derivations, not a replacement for its conventional account.

The three derivations of *upādānaskandha* give a closed circuit, not a one-way grasp:

```text
afflictive appropriation
    produces the aggregates
    governs the aggregates
    is produced again from the aggregates
```

Organon rendering (project reconstruction; not Vasubandhu's wording):

```text
sāsrava skandha
    = Being already determined as consequential
    = the same field as conflict, suffering, origin, dissolving world,
      station of views, becoming
```

“Consequential” remains the 1.04 Organon analogy for *sāsrava*. It is not the translation. Seeing or manifestation alone does not entail appropriation.

**Import rule.** *Sāsrava* does not change its 1.04 criterion. *Upādāna* in this chapter is affliction, not the later four clingings as a separate table. *Loka* will mean many things in Ch. 3; this card locks only the 1.08 derivation. *Dṛṣṭi* is not yet the Organon reading of *darśana* as Kantian Intuition.

## 8. Logical Determination

From 1.07 and 1.04:

```text
Skandha(x) ↔ Conditioned(x)
Sāsrava(x) ↔ OutflowsCanSettleIn(x)
UpadanaSkandha(x) ↔ Skandha(x) ∧ Sāsrava(x)
```

Corollary stated by the Bhāṣya:

```text
UpadanaSkandha(x) → Skandha(x)
Skandha(x) ↛ UpadanaSkandha(x)
∃y (Samskara(y) ∧ Anasrava(y))
```

Three relations, not one:

```text
ProducedFrom(aggregates, upadana)
GovernedBy(aggregates, upadana)
Produces(aggregates, upadana)
```

Latency parallel:

```text
Sāsrava(x)  ↔ Asrava lies latent in x
Saraṇa(x)   ↔ Rana (= kleśa) lies latent in x
Drstisthana(x) ↔ Drsti stands in x by anuśayana
```

Coextensive paryāya with distinct grounds:

```text
SāsravaField
    = duḥkha          because contrary to the Āryas
    = samudaya        because suffering arises from it
    = loka            because it dissolves
    = dṛṣṭisthāna     because views lodge there
    = bhava           because it comes to be
```

Object-taking remains distinct from lodging, as already at 1.04:

```text
Alambana(asrava, Path) ↛ Sāsrava(Path)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

1.07 gave the conditioned their body. 1.08 gives that body its bondage-determination. The cut is exact: Path-formations stay aggregates and stay conditioned; they do not become aggregates of appropriation.

The three examples are the doctrine, not decoration. Fire from grass: the field is generated from affliction. King's men: the field stands under affliction. Tree and fruit: the field generates affliction again. A science that keeps only the middle image (grasping as governance) misses the reproductive circuit.

*Loka* is the caution-word for the rest of the chapter and for Ch. 3. Appearance-field is an Organon temptation and a rejected translation here. The derivation in play is dissolution. If a later unit derives *loka* otherwise, that unit must say so.

*Dṛṣṭisthāna* continues lodging-language. Views do not visit the aggregates as a topic. They stand in this field by *anuśayana*. Psychologizing that as a mood of “having views” puts the station on the empirical floor. The card refuses that.

Organon, marked as project:

The appropriated aggregate-field is Being already under consequence. It is not Essence and not Concept. The circuit appropriation → field → appropriation is Practical Reason's first clear machine in the chapter: a field that reproduces the operation that produced it. Vidyā sees the three relations as one pattern. Technē looks for the interruption. The interruption is not named here; 1.06 already distinguished principled disjunction from mere blocked arising.

Do not Mara the Āryas in the *duḥkha* derivation. “Contrary to the noble ones” is the text's ground. It is not a complaint about pain.
