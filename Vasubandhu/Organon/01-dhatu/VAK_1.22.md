# VAK_1.22 — The Unconditioned, and the Order of the Bases

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> स्कन्धेष्वसंस्कृतं नोक्तमर्थायोगात् क्रमः पुनः ।
>
> यथौदारिकसंक्लेशभाजनाद्यर्थधातुतः ॥ १.२२ ॥॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

## 4. Grammar

```text
asaṃskṛtam
    skandheṣu na uktam
    artha-ayogāt

kramaḥ punaḥ
    yathā audārika
    saṃkleśa
    bhājana-ādi-artha
    dhātutaḥ
```

A base means a collection. The unconditioned is not divided as past and the rest, and gathered into one. It is not a member of the five, and it is not a sixth base. The pot analogy is others', and it is refused: the same inference would exclude the unconditioned from the dharma-essence and the dharma-principle.

*Kramaḥ punaḥ* opens the order. 1.21 promised three ways. This unit gives four accounts. The discrepancy is kept.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The unconditioned is not stated among the bases, because the meaning does not apply. The order, again, is according to grossness, affliction, the meaning beginning with a vessel, and principle.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

<<<<<<< HEAD
A base is a collection. The unconditioned is not gathered that way, so it is neither one of the five nor a sixth base. It remains in the dharma-essence and the dharma-principle. The five are ordered from gross to subtle; by the progress of affliction; as vessel, food, seasoning, maker, and eater; and by the levels, from the desire-principle to the summit. For these reasons there are five, neither fewer nor more.
=======
> Because Base (*skandha*) means a collectable multiplicity, the unconditioned cannot be made a member—or a sixth Base—without violating the category’s meaning. The five Bases are ordered through four convergent explanations: from gross to subtle; through the progress of Affliction; as vessel, food, seasoning, maker, and eater; and by Principle across levels of existence. These differentiated Base-configurations serve as stations in which Principle is established as seed in a field.

The second translation preserves the systematic force of `arthāyoga` and
makes the fourfold explanation readable without importing it into the
Kārikā's individual words.

## 6. Systematic Placement

<<<<<<< HEAD
Again, two lines. The first is the exclusion. The second is the order.

The unconditioned belongs to the system. It does not belong to the base. A base gathers a multiplicity across past, future, present, and the rest of the sūtra's list. The unconditioned is not that gathering. A sixth base would keep the word and drop the operation. The pot analogy fails for the same reason: cessation of a pot is not a pot, but the unconditioned is still an essence and a principle. The ranges differ.

The order is four accounts, not one.

Gross first. Form is resistant. Feeling is located: in my hand, in my foot. Reflection is grosser than the two that remain. Formations are grosser than the principle-base. What is grosser is stated first.

Affliction next, and the direction is not the order of the list. Attraction to form is from greed for the savor of feeling. That greed is from inverted reflection. That inversion is from the afflictions. Consciousness is afflicted by them. The list runs forward. The explanation runs back.

The meal is five roles. Form is the vessel. Feeling is the food. Reflection is the seasoning. Formations are the maker. The principle is the eater. The closing recap says vessel and seasoning for feeling and reflection. The earlier analogy said food and seasoning. The discrepancy is kept.

The last account is the levels. The desire-principle is distinguished by the forms of sensual pleasure. The dhyānas by feeling. The first three formless by reflection. The summit by formations alone. These are the stations in which the principle is established, as seed in a field. The stations are not a deletion of the principle.

Five, neither fewer nor more. Feeling and reflection are apart from the formations because they are grosser, because they are causes in the affliction sequence, and because two levels are distinguished by them.
=======
The unconditioned is excluded from Base classification because it is not a
collectable multiplicity; it remains included in Dharma Essence and Dharma
Principle. The four accounts explain the order of the five Bases without
turning that order into an eighteen-Principle inventory. The full census
remains five Bases, twelve Essences, and eighteen Principles.

=======
```text
Base(x)
    → CollectableMultiplicity(x)
    ∧ AdmitsTemporalAndComparativeDifferentiation(x)

Unconditioned(u)
    → ¬CollectableMultiplicity(u)
    → ¬Base(u)

Unconditioned(u)
    → MemberOf(u, DharmaEssence)
    ∧ MemberOf(u, DharmaPrinciple)
```

The result is differential inclusion, not exclusion from reality.

The first ordering is gross-to-subtle:

```text
Form Base (rūpa)
    → Feeling Base (vedanā)
    → Reflection Base (saṃjñā)
    → Formations Base (saṃskāra)
        → principle-base (vijñānaskandha)

most resistant/manifest
    → most subtle
```

The second traces the explanatory grounds of attraction:

```text
Afflictions
    → inverted Reflection
    → greed for the savor of Feeling
    → attraction to Form

consciousness (Citta) is afflicted by the Afflictions
```

This is an explanatory relation, not a simple forward temporal chain. The
third ordering account presents functional cooperation:

```text
Form       = vessel
Feeling    = food
Reflection = seasoning
Formations = maker
principle-base = eater
```

The fourth orders the levels by Principle:

```text
Desire Principle           → predominance of desirable Form
dhyāna levels              → predominance of Feeling
first three formless levels → predominance of Reflection
summit of existence        → predominance of Formations

these configurations
    → stations or fields of Principle

Principle (vijñāna)
    → established in them as seed in field
```

The convergence of four explanations establishes:

```text
FiveBases
    → NeitherFewerNorMore
    ∧ OrderedNonArbitrarily
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

<<<<<<< HEAD
The hinge is *arthāyogāt*, then *kramaḥ punaḥ*. Interpretation of the four grounds is in the Bhāṣya.
=======
The hinge is *arthāyogāt*: the defining operation of Base does not fit the
unconditioned. This excludes it from Base classification, not from the
Dharma-system. *Kramaḥ punaḥ* introduces four distinct grounds for one
five-Base order; their differences and the textual discrepancies are
developed in the paired Bhāṣya.
