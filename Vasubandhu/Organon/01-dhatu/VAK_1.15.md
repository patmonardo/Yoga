## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The others, apart from the four, are the Formations Base. These three, again, together with *avijñapti* and the unconditioned, are called the Dharma Essence and the Dharma Principle.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Apart from the four, the others are the Formations Base. These three—Feeling, Reflection, and Formations—together with *avijñapti* and the unconditioned are also named the Dharma Essence and the Dharma Principle.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Again, two lines. The first gathers the remainder. The second names the three under Essence and Principle, and brings two accompaniments with them.

Apart from the four. The commentary names them: Form, feeling, Reflection, and the Cognition Base. The others are formations, and those formations are one Base. The unconditioned does not enter this remainder.

The sūtra says six groups of volition. Predominance, not exhaustion. Volition is predominant in the active forming, because it has the nature of karma. It actively forms the conditioned; therefore it is called the Formations Base of appropriation. The name can be taught from the chief member. The Base is not the chief member alone.

Otherwise the remaining associated factors, and the dissociated formations, would not be gathered in a Base. They would not be suffering and origin. Full comprehension and abandonment would not apply to them. The Blessed One does not declare the ending of suffering without directly knowing and fully comprehending even a single dharma. Likewise, without abandoning. Their inclusion must be accepted. The argument is inclusion. It does not make the classifying itself the abandonment.

These three, again. Feeling, Reflection, formations. Not the Cognition Base. Together with *avijñapti* and the unconditioned, they bear the names Dharma Essence and Dharma Principle.

Seven, in the commentary. Three Bases. *Avijñapti*. Space, cessation through discrimination, cessation without discrimination. *Avijñapti* remains Form, and remains in the Form Base. The unconditioned has no Base. The shared name does not make them one kind.

The placement left open at 1.14 is made here. The ten did not include the continuity. The continuity enters the Dharma Essence and the Dharma Principle, and does not leave the Form Base.

The next verse defines the Cognition Base. Not this one.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya identifies the four as Form, feeling, Reflection, and the Cognition Base, and “these three” as feeling, Reflection, and formations. Together with *avijñapti* and the unconditioned, they bear the names Dharma Essence and Dharma Principle. The commentary further counts seven constituents.

The hinges are *anye* and *saha*. Interpretation of the seven is in the Bhāṣya.
