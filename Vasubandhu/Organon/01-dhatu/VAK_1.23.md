# VAK_1.23 — The Order of the Six Faculties

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> प्राक्पञ्च वार्त्तमानार्थ्यात् भौतिकार्थ्याच्चतुष्टयम् ।
>
> दूराशुतरवृत्त्यान्यत् यथास्थानं क्रमोऽथवा ॥ १.२३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> prāk pañca vārttamānārthyāt bhautikārthyāc catuṣṭayam /
>
> dūrāśutaravṛttyānyat yathāsthānaṃ kramo 'thavā // 1.23 //

Devanāgarī.

## 4. Grammar

The verse compresses an ordering of the six Faculties within the Essence
and Principle arrangements. The Bhāṣya states the governing
dependency:

```text
Faculty order
    → corresponding Condition order
    → corresponding Principle order
```

The first ablative gives a temporal criterion:

```text
prāk pañca vārttamāna-arthyāt
    the five sensory Faculties come first
    because their Conditions are present
```

Manas has an unrestricted Condition-range. The further division of that
range is given in a damaged compound; its exact scope remains unresolved.

The second ablative selects four within the five:

```text
bhautika-arthyāt catuṣṭayam
    eye, ear, nose, and tongue come first
    because their objects are derived Form
```

The Body Faculty follows because its Conditions may include the Elements
themselves, derived Form, or both.

The instrumental `dūra-āśutara-vṛttyā` supplies two comparative operational
criteria for the remaining internal order:

```text
eye before ear
    → operation at a greater distance

nose before tongue
    → quicker apprehension before food contacts the tongue
```

`Athavā` introduces an alternative explanation. The Faculties may instead
be ordered according to the bodily location of their supports: eye, ear,
nose, tongue, and then a damaged clause concerning the body. The reading
that places the body mostly below is provisional. Manas depends upon the
sense Faculties but has no determinate spatial site in this account.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The five come first because their objects are present; the group of four because their objects are derived Form. The remaining order follows operation at a greater distance or more quickly—or, alternatively, according to location.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The five sensory Faculties precede Manas because their Conditions are present, whereas Manas has a wider Condition-range. Among the five, eye, ear, nose, and tongue precede the Body Faculty because their Conditions are derived Form, while the Body Faculty may apprehend Elements, derived Form, or both. Eye precedes ear by the comparison of distance, and nose precedes tongue by the earlier apprehension of food's odor; alternatively, bodily location explains the order, though the body-site clause is damaged.

The Bhāṣya orders Conditions and Principles through the Faculties; it does
not claim that Faculties produce their Conditions.

## 6. Systematic Placement

The Bhāṣya orders six Faculties within the Essence and Principle
arrangements. The corresponding Conditions and Principles follow that
sequence. These are ordered relations, not a claim that Faculties produce
their Conditions.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives the governing relation:

```text
Order(Six Faculties)
    → Order(Corresponding Conditions)
    → Order(Corresponding Principles)
```

The criteria apply to distinct comparisons:

```text
five sensory Faculties before Manas
    present Conditions

four Faculties before the Body Faculty
    derived-Form Conditions

Eye before Ear
    farther operation

Nose before Tongue
    quicker operation in the food example

alternative
    bodily sites in order; body clause damaged
    Manas depends on the Faculties but has no bodily site
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

The verse orders Faculties, Conditions, and Principles by several local
criteria; it does not make the Faculties causes that produce their
Conditions. Manas has a wider Condition-range than the five sensory
Faculties, but the damaged compound does not license a more exact account.
The alternative location clause is also partly damaged. These textual
limits remain open rather than being filled by a larger theory absent from
the verse.
