**Feature chart checkpoints.** First spans [0, 3); second [3, 5); third [5, 7); alternative [7, 10); the root spans [0, 10). Two ablatives give reasons for precedence. No claim that the Faculties produce the Conditions is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** The five come first, because their Conditions are present; the four, because their Conditions are Element-dependent Form; the rest, by farther or quicker operation. Or the order is according to site.

The second clause's "first" and its comparison with Body are supplied from the Bhāṣya's carry-forward, not inserted as a token. The remainder compound joins two comparisons that apply to different pairs: distance for eye and ear before nose and tongue, then eye before ear; quickness for nose before tongue. The parse records both grounds; §8 assigns them. Site is an alternative account of the same six-fold order, not a fifth cut in one elimination.

The commentary orders the six, beginning with the eye, among the Essences and the Principles. The order of their Conditions and of their Cognitions follows that order. It does not say the Faculties produce the Conditions.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The five come first, because their Conditions are present. The four, because their Conditions are Element-dependent Form. The rest, by farther or quicker operation. Or the order is according to location.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five Faculties come first because their Conditions are present. Of these, four precede the Body because they take Element-dependent Form as Condition. The remaining order follows distance or quicker operation—or, alternatively, bodily location.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Again, two lines. The first is the cut. The second is the remainder, and an alternative.

The five Faculties work in the present. That is why they come first. Mind is not that restriction. Some of its Conditions are present. The rest of the compound is damaged, and it is not repaired. The secure claim is the contrast: present, against an unrestricted range.

Of the five, four take Element-dependent Form. The body is not restricted that way. Sometimes Great Elements, sometimes Element-dependent Form, sometimes both. *Prāk* carries forward. The four Faculties are first among the five, not a new list of six.

Eye and ear Faculties take a distant Condition, so they precede nose and tongue. Of those two, the eye operates farther: a river seen, its sound not heard. Nose and tongue do not operate at a distance. Nose is quicker in the example given: the odor of food before the food reaches the tongue. The phrase is damaged. The example is kept. It is not a measure of speed.

Or the sites. Eye site uppermost, then ear, nose, tongue. The body-site clause is damaged. Mind depends on those Faculties and has no place. That is the alternative. It does not redefine the Faculty as the gross site.

This closes the base, and only the Form System. The Faculties and the meanings are ordered here. The Dharma System is not this verse.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya identifies the five as Faculties preceding Mind because their Conditions are present, and the first four as preceding Body because their Conditions are Element-dependent Form. Mind also takes non-present Conditions, but the associated compound is damaged; Body may take Great Elements, Element-dependent Form, or both. Eye and ear have distant Conditions, with eye operating farther (the river example); nose precedes tongue because it takes odor before food reaches the tongue, though this phrase is damaged. Alternatively, the sequence follows bodily sites from eye through tongue; the body-site phrase is damaged, and Mind depends on the Faculties without being localized.

The hinge is *prāk*, then *athavā*. Interpretation of the close of the Form System is in the Bhāṣya.
