# VAK_1.04 Bhāṣya — Impure and Pure

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sāsravānāsravā dharmāḥ saṃskṛtā mārgavarjitāḥ /
> sāsravāḥ āsravās teṣu yasmāt samanuśerate // 1.04 //

> Dharmas are impure and pure. Conditioned dharmas other than the Truth of
> the Path are impure, because the *āsravas* persist in them.

The commentary calls this *sarvadharmāṇāṃ samāsanirdeśaḥ*, the summary designation of all dharmas. It specifies *mārga* as the Truth of the Path, and distinguishes an *āsrava* taking something as *ālambana* from its persistence through a dharma.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> katame sāsravā dharmā ity āha / saṃskṛtā mārgavarjitāḥ sāsravāḥ /

> āsravā upajāyante na tv anuśerate tatreti na tayoḥ sāsravatvaprasaṅgaḥ /
> yathā 'tra nānuśerate tat paścād anuśayanirdeśa eva jñāpayiṣyāmaḥ /

This unit runs from the transition question at `[003|05]` through *uktāḥ sāsravāḥ* at `[003|14]` in `Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The opening question also closes the preceding unit. More consequentially, the local text at `[003|12–13]` reads `na tinuśerate`; `na tv anuśerate` above is a contextual reconstruction, not a verified source reading. The witness remains an edited, non-critical working text.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Which, then, are those dharmas for whose discrimination the Abhidharma instruction is given? He says: dharmas are impure and pure. This is the summary designation of all dharmas.
>
> Among them, which dharmas are impure? He says: conditioned, excluding the Path, are impure. That is, conditioned dharmas other than the Truth of the Path are impure. Why? Because the *āsravas* persist in them.
>
> Granted, *āsravas* also arise taking the Truth of Cessation and the Truth of the Path as *ālambana*. But they do not persist in either. It does not follow that those two are impure. How they fail to persist in them will be explained later, in the Exposition of the Anuśayas. The impure dharmas have now been stated.

Here “persist in” renders *samanuśerate* and its reprise *anuśerate*: the claim concerns the *āsravas*' relation to those dharmas, not mere duration. The passage promises a fuller account later; this translation does not supply that account in advance.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
Which dharmas are to be discriminated?
    -> all dharmas: impure / pure
    -> which are impure?
        -> conditioned, excluding the Truth of the Path
    -> why?
        -> the āsravas persist through them
    -> qualification
        -> taking as ālambana is not persistence
    -> further account deferred to the Anuśayanirdeśa
```

The commentary gives the criterion of impurity without collapsing it into taking. Cessation and the Path can be *ālambana* for an arising, and not thereby impure.

The qualification is an objection and reply, not an incidental example.
If taking as *ālambana* were sufficient for impurity, Cessation and the Path
would also be impure. The reply denies the required persistence, not the
arising of the *āsravas*. It preserves the classification by distinguishing
the two relations. The closing promise defers the fuller account of
non-persistence; this unit does not supply it.

## 5. Conditioned and Unconditioned

The verse determines the impure side:

```text
conditioned, other than the Path
    = impure

conditioned Truth of the Path
    = pure
```

The next verse determines the unconditioned as pure. The division is not conditioned / unconditioned. It is impure / pure. Conditioned and unconditioned are further determinations inside it.

```text
impure
    = conditioned, excluding the Path

pure
    = conditioned Path
    + unconditioned
```

The Path is decisive. It is conditioned and pure. Purity is not the absence of the conditioned.

## 6. Interpretation

**Grammar becomes a logical question.** The kārikā first predicates impure
and pure of dharmas. It then specifies the impure member and gives a
reason. In the reason-clause the subject changes: the *āsravas* persist;
the dharmas are what they persist in. Thus "a dharma persists" and "the
*āsravas* persist in a dharma" are not interchangeable propositions.

The Organon inquiry begins with that distinction. What makes an Impure
Dharma a term through which impurity persists? The locative makes the
relation available for inquiry, but does not answer by itself whether its
support is a capacity, a continuum, or another determination. Neither
*āśraya* nor *sāra* occurs here. They cannot be supplied as grammatical
subjects or as established equivalents for this relation.

**Hegelian Ground question.** The Bhāṣya's "Why?" requires a warrant for the
predicate impure. The relation of persistence supplies that warrant.
Its necessity is tested negatively: if merely taking as *ālambana*
sufficed, the Path and Cessation would receive the wrong classification.
The counterexample excludes that proposed warrant. It does not yet
demonstrate the full mechanism of the relation that replaces it.

This is how Hegel can work in the translation rather than decorate it.
We ask what grounds a determination and whether the alleged ground
actually distinguishes its cases. We do not identify a Sanskrit case
with a logical category, equate *ālambana* with Object, or claim that
the Ground–Condition movement has already been derived from this verse.

**Persistence and the Pure Conditioned.** The Path forces us to
distinguish conditioned operation from persistence of the *āsravas*.
It gives the inquiry a positive direction: conditioned Dharma can operate
without the persistence that defines impurity. The passage does not reduce
purity to the disappearance of all formed activity.

The proposed Dharma qua *sāra* can now be tested more precisely. If it
names essential bearing activity, what distinguishes bearing conditioned
operation from sustaining the *āsravas*? A proposed Form–Emptiness reading
must explain that difference, not erase it by making all dependent
persistence impure. These are questions generated by the pilot, not
additional doctrines attributed to the Bhāṣya.

## 7. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. *Sāsrava* and *anāsrava* are impure and pure.
2. *Mārga* is the Truth of the Path.
3. *Samanuśerate* is persist, with the *āsravas* as subject. Mere duration is not the criterion.
4. Retain *ālambana*; "taking as *ālambana*" names the relation, not a settled English equivalent for the noun. It does not entail persistence.
5. Conditioned and unconditioned are not synonyms for impure and pure. The conditioned Path is pure.
6. Keep the reason for the classification distinct from the fuller account of non-persistence deferred to the Anuśayanirdeśa.
7. Keep the Dharma qua *sāra* and Form–Emptiness proposals active as interpretive questions; neither supplies absent Sanskrit terms.
