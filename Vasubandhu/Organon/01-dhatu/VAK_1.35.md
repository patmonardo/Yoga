# VAK_1.35 — Element, Element-Dependent Form, and Aggregation

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> स्प्रष्टव्यं द्विविधं शेषा रूपिणो नव भौतिकाः ।
>
> धर्मधात्वेकदेशश्च सञ्चिता दश रूपिणः ॥ १.३५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> spraṣṭavyaṃ dvividhaṃ śeṣā rūpiṇo nava bhautikāḥ /
>
> dharmadhātvekadeśaś ca saṃcitā daśa rūpiṇaḥ // 1.35 //

The portion of the Essence Principle is grammatically added to the
Element-dependent classification. The Bhāṣya identifies it as
`avijñapti`. It is not added to the subsequent count of ten atomically
aggregated Principles.

## 4. Grammar

The first predicate divides the Tangible Principle:

```text
spraṣṭavyaṃ dvividham
    the tangible is twofold

    four Great Elements
    + seven Element-dependent tangible qualities beginning with smoothness
```

The remaining nine Form-bearing Principles are Element-dependent:

```text
five sensory Faculty Principles
    eye, ear, nose, tongue, body

four non-tangible sensory-field Principles
    visible Form, Sound, smell, taste
```

`Bhautika` is explicitly explained by `bhūteṣu bhavatvāt`: it arises in
or depends upon the Great Elements. Dependence does not mean identity.

The conjunction in `dharmadhātvekadeśaś ca` continues this predicate:

```text
one portion of the Essence Principle
    = avijñapti
    = Element-dependent Form
```

The final clause begins a separate question and predicate:

```text
saṃcitā daśa rūpiṇaḥ
    the ten Form-bearing Principles are aggregated
```

The Bhāṣya defines `saṃcita` strictly as constitution by aggregates of
atoms (*paramāṇu-saṃghāta*). Avijñapti is Form-bearing and
Element-dependent, but it is not one of these ten atomic aggregates.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The Tangible Principle is twofold. The remaining nine Form-bearing Principles are Element-dependent, as is one portion of the Essence Principle. The ten Form-bearing Principles are aggregated.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The Tangible Principle comprises both the four Great Elements and seven Element-dependent tangible qualities. The other nine Form-bearing Principles depend on the Great Elements, and avijñapti—the Element-dependent Form portion of the Essence Principle—shares this status. The five sensory Faculties and five sensory-field Principles are atomically aggregated.

The expanded translation keeps avijñapti within Element-dependent Form
while excluding it from the atomic meaning of `saṃcita`.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Form has distinct Element-dependent and constitutional determinations. The Great Elements are directly tangible; the Faculties, sensory qualities, and avijñapti depend upon them without being identical with them. Atomic aggregation characterizes ten Form-bearing Principles, but not every Element-dependent Form determination. Dependence is therefore broader than atomic aggregation.

Organon rendering:

> The Principle processor now distinguishes three predicates: being a Great Element, being Element-dependent, and being atomically aggregated. They cross-classify rather than describe three successive stages of production. Avijñapti is the boundary case: an Element-dependent Form determination in the Essence Principle, neither one of the Great Elements nor one of the ten atomically aggregated Form-bearing Principles.

This is a testable Organon hypothesis, not a translation claim. In the
Fichtean nondual reading, the Element and its dependent Form are
distinguishable moments within one system of determination, not isolated
substances. Hegelian logic preserves the difference and the specific
relation of dependence. The move toward *prakāśa* is the system's
intelligibility through those distinctions. This verse gives the later
Loka inquiry a precise problem to test: what counts as an Element, what
depends on it, and which of those determinations are perceptible or
atomically aggregated.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Tangible Principle contains both Element and Element-dependent
determinations:

```text
TangiblePrinciple
    = FourGreatElements
      ∪ SevenElementDependentTangibleQualities
```

The Element-dependent classification is:

```text
Bhautika
    = FiveSensoryFacultyPrinciples
      ∪ FourNonTangibleSensoryObjectPrinciples
      ∪ SevenElementDependentTangibleQualities
      ∪ Avijnapti
```

The atomic-aggregation classification is separate:

```text
AtomicallyAggregatedPrinciples
    = FiveSensoryFacultyPrinciples
      ∪ FiveSensoryObjectPrinciples

Samcita(x)
    ↔ ConstitutedAs(x, AtomicAggregate)
```

Element-dependence means:

```text
Bhautika(x)
    → DependsOn(x, FourGreatElements)

DependsOn(x, FourGreatElements)
    ↛ IdenticalWith(x, FourGreatElements)
```

Avijñapti exposes the crossing of these predicates:

```text
Avijnapti
    → FormBearing
    ∧ Bhautika
    ∧ PartOf(EssencePrinciple)
    ∧ ¬Samcita
```

Thus the distinctions are not a production ladder:

```text
ElementStatus
    ≠ ElementDependence
    ≠ AtomicAggregation
```

The predicates overlap in particular Principles, but none can replace
the others.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 1.35 separates three questions: what is an Element, what depends on
Elements, and what is constituted as an atomic aggregate. The Tangible
Principle alone includes both the four Great Elements and seven
Element-dependent tangible qualities. The other sensory Faculties and
sensory-field Principles depend on the Elements without thereby being identical
to them.

The Bhāṣya attributes the reduction of ten Essence Bases to nothing but
Great Elements to Buddhadeva and rejects it. The cited account differentiates
the four Element characteristics from visible Form, Sound, smell, and
taste, while the Tangible Principle includes both Elements and what
depends on them. The sensory Faculties apprehend different kinds of
determination; one cannot flatten the full Form system into the four
Elements alone.

The eye-flesh objection and its reply sharpen the same logic: a Faculty
may occur inseparably with Form in the fleshy mass without being
identical to that Form. The discussion of the “person of six Principles”
is likewise kept to its context of descent into the womb. The Bhāṣya
rejects collapsing mental factors into Citta, since factors such as
feeling and recognition are described as dependent on Citta, not simply
identical with it.

Avijñapti is the critical boundary case: it is an Element-dependent Form
determination included in the Essence Principle, yet it is not one of the
ten atomically aggregated Form Base Principles. It is neither a Great
Element nor merely an imaginary mental item:

```text
avijñapti
    = Element-dependent Form
    + Essence-Principle membership
    − ordinary sensory disclosure
    − membership in the ten atomic aggregates
```

This gives the Loka inquiry into subtle Elements a testable framework:
keep elemental status, dependence, sensory apprehension, and atomic
constitution distinct. The Fichtean nondual / Hegelian Organon reading
may treat these as differentiated moments within one self-determining
system, but that is an interpretive hypothesis. The text establishes
their classifications, not a complete theory of *prakāśa* or Prajñā.

For the Principle processor, 1.35 specifies independent parameters:

```text
elemental status
element-dependence
mode of sensory apprehension
atomic aggregation
Essence-Principle membership
```
