# VAK_1.46 — Not Lower, Not Higher

Higher and lower are planes (*bhūmi*), not height. The planes are the Desire Realm and the four dhyānas. The genitive gives the standard of the comparison.

## 4. Grammar

Three clauses: two fixed rules and one open rule. Each fixed rule is a negated comparison. A nominative is compared, through a comparative adjective, with a genitive standard. The adjective agrees with the nominative (neuter singular). Every member is said of one act of seeing, so each word denotes a role in a seeing situation (`\s x.EyeIn(s,x)`), and the comparison is between the planes of the members.

In the second rule, *vijñānam ca* has no predicate of its own. *Ca* makes it take the comparative and the standard of *rūpam*: Cognition, too, is not higher than the Eye. *Vijñānam* also gives the reference (REF=cog) that the pronoun *asya* must match in the next clause.

The third clause turns with *tu*. *Sarvataḥ* is a relation operator. Given two roles, it asserts all three alternatives: lower, higher, and the same. It applies once to Visible Form against *asya*, and through the dual *ubhe* twice against the Body: once for Visible Form, once for Cognition.

**Close construe.** Not lower than the Body is the Eye. Higher, a Visible Form is not, than the Eye; and Cognition. But of this, the Visible Form; and of the Body, both, in every way.

The Bhāṣya reads *asya* as the Eye-Cognition just named (*anantaroktasya cakṣur-vijñānasya*). The open clause does not say the alternatives may occur. It says all three occur.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The Eye is not lower than the Body. A Visible Form is not higher than the Eye, and Cognition too. But relative to this, a Visible Form; and relative to the Body, both, in every way.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Eye is never of a lower plane than the Body. A Visible Form is never of a higher plane than the Eye, nor is Eye-Cognition. Relative to that Cognition, however, a Visible Form is of a higher, lower, or the same plane; and relative to the Body, so are both.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The four are not locked to one plane. The rule is the lock that remains. The eye may stand with the body or above it. It does not stand below the body. A Visible Form may stand with the eye or below it. It does not stand above the eye. Cognition does not stand above the eye. Relative to the Cognition, the Visible Form is free: higher, lower, or the same. Relative to the body, both are free in the same three ways.

The range is narrower than the freedom. Body, eye, and Visible Form run through five planes, the Desire Realm through the fourth dhyāna. Eye-Cognition runs through two, the Desire Realm and the first dhyāna. A second-dhyāna eye still sees by a first-dhyāna Cognition. The open alternatives do not put Eye-Cognition in the second, third, or fourth.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Kārikā, derived.** The root SEM states in every seeing, the Eye is not of a lower plane than the Body; the Visible Form and the Cognition are not of a higher plane than the Eye; against the Cognition, the Visible Form occurs higher, lower, and the same; against the Body, both occur higher, lower, and the same. The plane ranges, the second-dhyāna eye with first-dhyāna Cognition, and the rejections of one-plane or Cognition-above-Eye are prose or rejected theories.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Key-term evidence.** *Viṣaya* appears in the Bhāṣya's gloss of the open clause: the Visible Form is the Condition (*viṣaya*) of Eye-Cognition "in every way", above, below, and on its own plane. The Condition is free of the plane of its Cognition; the fixed rule binds it only to the eye.

**Translation note.** *Asya* is rendered "that Cognition" with the Bhāṣya, and *sarvataḥ* is spelled out as higher, lower, or the same plane. Higher and lower translate plane (*bhūmi*), not position.

The hinge is the fixed rule after the cases. The cases differ. The eye does not go below the Body. The Visible Form does not go above the eye. Cognition does not go above the eye.
