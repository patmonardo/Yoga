# VAK_1.25 Bhāṣya — Dharma-Collections and Their Inclusion

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dharmaskandhasahasrāṇi yāny aśītiṃ jagau muniḥ /
>
> tāni vāṅnāma vety eṣāṃ rūpasaṃskārasaṃgrahaḥ // 1.25 //

> The eighty thousand Dharma-collections proclaimed by the Sage—speech or
> name—are included in the Form Base and the Formations Base.

The Bhāṣya places this verse under a wider question: do scriptural
designations using *skandha*, *āyatana*, and *dhātu* refer to things outside
the established classifications, or are they included within them? It
answers that they are included, not separate. The Dharma-collections are
the first example. Two accounts of Buddha-word's nature are reported:
speech, or name.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> anyāny api skandhāyatanadhātusaṃśabditāny upalabhyante sūtreṣu /
> teṣāṃ kim ebhir eva saṃgraho veditavya āhosvid vyatirekaḥ / ebhir eva
> saṃgraho na vyatirekaḥ / tatra tāvat dharmaskandhasahasrāṇi yāny
> aśītiṃ jagau muniḥ / tāni vāṅnāma vety eṣāṃ rūpasaṃskārasaṃgrahaḥ /
> yeṣāṃ vāksvabhāvaṃ buddhavacanaṃ teṣāṃ tāni rūpaskandhasaṃgṛhītāni /
> yeṣāṃ nāmasvabhāvaṃ teṣāṃ saṃskāraskandhena /

The natural unit runs from the question at printed location 017.04–05
through the two positions at 017.09. The following question about the
measure of a Dharma-collection introduces 1.26; it is not answered in this
unit.

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The running Sanskrit and
research witnesses agree on the substantive wording. No conjectural
lexical repair is needed. The number is eighty thousand, expressed by
`aśītiṃ` with `sahasrāṇi`. The sandhi `vāṅnāma veti` is retained and
resolved in the analysis below.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Other things designated in the sūtras by the terms aggregate, sense-base,
> and element are also found. Should these be understood as included within
> these very classifications, or do they stand apart from them? They are
> included within these very classifications; they do not stand apart.
>
> First, the eighty thousand Dharma-collections proclaimed by the Sage are
> speech or name; their inclusion is in Form or Formations. For those who
> hold that Buddha-word has speech as its nature, those collections are
> included in the Form Base. For those who hold that it has name as its
> nature, they are included in the Formations Base.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya moves from the general question of scriptural designation to a
specific case:

```text
other scriptural designations using skandha, āyatana, and dhātu
    → included in these very classifications, not separate

first example: the eighty thousand Dharma-collections
    → speech-nature account: Form Base
    → name-nature account: Formations Base
```

The general answer is stated directly. The two analyses of Buddha-word are
introduced through `yeṣāṃ ... teṣām`, “for those who ... for them.” No
group is named, and neither account is selected as the final position.

## 5. Scriptural Names and Inclusion

`Skandhāyatanadhātusaṃśabditāni` refers to things designated in the sūtras
through the terms *skandha*, *āyatana*, and *dhātu*. Conventionally these
may be rendered aggregate, sense-base, and element; the Organon terms are
Base, Essence, and Principle. The occurrence of another designation raises
a question; it does not by itself establish a further member outside the
established classifications. `Ebhir eva`, “by these very
[classifications],” points back to those systems. `Na vyatirekaḥ` denies
separate standing.

In this example, *dharma-skandha* names a special scriptural body or
collection of teaching. Its use of *skandha* does not make it one more
member of the five Bases. The Bhāṣya's question is where this teaching
collection belongs under the two analyses of Buddha-word, not whether its
name creates an additional ontological Base.

The passage is specific about the assignments it makes: the speech account
places the teaching in the Form Base; the name account places it in the
Formations Base. These are the two placements stated for this example.

## 6. The Two Analyses of Buddha-Word

`Vāṅnāma veti` resolves as `vāk nāma vā iti`: “speech, or name.” The
Bhāṣya develops the alternatives explicitly:

| Reported nature of Buddha-word | Stated inclusion |
|---|---|
| `vāksvabhāva`, speech as its nature | Form Base |
| `nāmasvabhāva`, name as its nature | Formations Base |

In the first statement, `buddhavacanam` is qualified by
`vāksvabhāvam`: for those who hold Buddha-word to have speech as its
nature, the collections are gathered into the Form Base. In the second,
the prior material is understood: for those who hold it to have name as
its nature, the collections are included by the Formations Base.

The sound account is intelligible through the earlier inclusion of sound
in Form. The name account treats linguistic name as a formation. The
Bhāṣya does not further analyze the relations among sound, words, phrases,
syllables, and meaning here; those distinctions should not be added to its
translation.

The alternatives should not be turned into one endorsed account in which
speech is merely a vehicle and name its semantic content. Such a model may
be explored separately, but the commentary reports positions about what
Buddha-word is by nature and states the inclusion corresponding to each.

## 7. Dharma-Collection and the Base–Essence–Principle System

Two uses must be kept distinct:

| Level | Term | Determination |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional reading of this passage | *dharma-skandha* | a collection or body of teaching; not an additional member of the five Bases |
| Controlled Organon vocabulary | *skandha* / *āyatana* / *dhātu* | Base / Essence / Principle |
| Systematic projections of Dharma | Dharma Base / Dharma Essence / Dharma Principle | three distinct determinations of one Dharma |

Thus **Dharma-collection** is the right rendering for the eighty thousand
scriptural units in this passage. Do not infer that this use of
*dharma-skandha* names an additional ontological Base, or translate
*dharma* as Essence. The controlled system keeps Base, Essence, and
Principle distinct, as it keeps Dharma Base, Dharma Essence, and Dharma
Principle distinct.

The distinction also prevents a false inference from the shared word
*skandha*. The eighty thousand teaching collections are not eighty thousand
additional Bases. The Bhāṣya's immediate example is the inclusion of this
teaching-collection in the Form Base or Formations Base according to the
reported nature of Buddha-word.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. Additional sūtra designations do not automatically stand outside the
   established classifications.
2. *Dharma-skandha* here means a teaching collection, not an additional
   member of the five Bases.
3. The number given is eighty thousand; the unit's measure is left to the
   question opening 1.26.
4. Speech and name are two reported accounts of Buddha-word's nature; the
   Bhāṣya does not choose between them.
5. Speech is included in the Form Base; name is included in the Formations
   Base.
6. The Base–Essence–Principle mapping is distinct from the conventional
   rendering of *dharma-skandha* here as Dharma-collection.

## 9. Interpretation

The Kośa explicitly includes scriptural designations within its established
classifications rather than treating each designation as a separate
system-member. Here the Dharma-collection is classified through the
Bhāṣya's two accounts of Buddha-word: as speech, it is included in the Form
Base; as name, in the Formations Base. The content taught is not thereby
reduced to audible sound; the local argument places Buddha-word according
to its reported nature.

The Absolute Base spans and combines Rūpa-skandha and Dharma-skandha. This
unity is Absolute Dharma itself, discriminating all Dharmas through Form
and Law. VAK 1.25 shows the teaching itself entering this discrimination:
the speech account places it in Form Base, while the name account places
it in Formations Base. These reported accounts remain distinct.

Keep the conventional *dharma-skandha* in this verse—“Dharma-collection”—
distinct from Dharma-skandha as a systematic Base determination. The shared
compound does not make the eighty thousand teaching collections additional
Bases. Verse 1.26 asks how one Dharma-collection is measured; 1.27 extends
the discussion to other scriptural designations. Within the 1.24–1.28
sequence, this is the inclusion test: the scriptural designation is located
within established classifications rather than added as a new slot.

## 10. Working Status

This revision preserves the source's inclusion question, its distinction
between the conventional Dharma-collection and a systematic Base, and the
two unselected accounts of Buddha-word. The measure question remains with
1.26.
