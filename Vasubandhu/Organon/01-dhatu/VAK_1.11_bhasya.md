# VAK_1.11 Bhāṣya — The Continuity Called Avijñapti

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // 1.11 //

Literal:

> The wholesome or unwholesome continuity which, depending on the great
> elements, belongs even to one whose consciousness is otherwise directed or
> absent: that indeed is called *avijñapti*.

Bhāṣya-informed:

> *Avijñapti* is a wholesome or unwholesome stream. It continues when
> consciousness is directed elsewhere, in the two attainments without
> consciousness, and also while consciousness is present and not distracted.
> Dependence on the great elements distinguishes it from a similar stream of
> acquisition. It has the nature of form and action, and does not inform
> another as *vijñapti* does. In summary, it is wholesome or unwholesome form
> arisen from *vijñapti* or from absorption.

The commentary explains the states, the stream, the distinction, and the
name. Which great elements are meant opens 1.12. It is not taken here.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> *Avijñapti* is now to be explained. It is stated thus.
>
> The wholesome or unwholesome continuity which, depending on the great
> elements, belongs even to one whose consciousness is otherwise directed or
> absent: that indeed is called *avijñapti*.
>
> “Of one whose consciousness is otherwise directed” means also of one who has
> a different consciousness. “Even of one without consciousness” means also of
> one who has entered the attainment of non-perception or the attainment of
> cessation. By the word “even” it is known: also of one who has consciousness
> and is not otherwise directed.
>
> “Which continuity” means which stream. “Wholesome or unwholesome” means
> *kuśala* or *akuśala*.
>
> There is also a stream of acquisition, wholesome or unwholesome, of this
> sort. To distinguish from that, it is said: depending on the great elements.
>
> The Vaibhāṣikas say the meaning of *upādāya* is causal, because the elements
> have the status of cause, beginning with production.
>
> In “that indeed is *avijñapti*,” the word “indeed” is for making known the
> assignment of this name. Although it has the nature of form and action, it
> does not inform another as *vijñapti* does. Hence *avijñapti*. “Is called”
> shows a statement of the teacher.
>
> In summary, however, *avijñapti* is wholesome or unwholesome form arisen
> from *vijñapti* or from absorption.

The causal reading is ascribed to the Vaibhāṣikas. The teacher is not named
more narrowly. No separate objector speaks.

## 5. Scope and Stream

| Phrase | Determination |
|---|---|
| otherwise directed | a different consciousness |
| without consciousness | non-perception, or cessation |
| even | also present and not otherwise directed |
| continuity | a stream |
| wholesome or unwholesome | *kuśala* or *akuśala* |

*Vikṣipta* here is not agitation. It is consciousness directed elsewhere.
*Acittaka* is the two attainments. *Api* is the point: the conspicuous cases
do not exhaust the class.

*Pravāha* gives the continuity a series. Not one deposit. The ethical
character qualifies the stream. It is not a present judgment about a past act.

## 6. Acquisition

A stream of acquisition can also be wholesome or unwholesome. Polarity plus
continuity does not identify *avijñapti*. Dependence on the great elements
does.

*Upādāya* in the verse is dependence. The Vaibhāṣikas take it as cause,
beginning with production. That is marked. The prose does not say how every
later moment of the stream is supported. That question is later.

## 7. The Name

*Hi* signals the naming. The negative prefix is the contrast: nature of form
and action, and it does not inform another. Non-disclosure is a function. It
is not a denial of form, or of action.

*Ucyate* shows a teacher's statement. The summary adds two origins. The verse
does not. Outward manifestation is not the whole account.

## 8. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. Otherwise directed is a different consciousness. Without consciousness is
   the two attainments.
2. *Api* includes one present and not distracted.
3. Continuity is a stream. The polarity is wholesome or unwholesome.
4. Dependence on the great elements distinguishes acquisition.
5. The causal force of *upādāya* is Vaibhāṣika.
6. Form and action, and it does not inform another.
7. The summary adds *vijñapti* or absorption. Not the verse.

## 9. Interpretation

Named, then counted around, then defined. The word stays. What the organ
sends arrives here as a continuity that does not inform another. Consciousness
may be elsewhere, absent, or present. The stream does not wait on the
occurrent state.

Acquisition is the near miss. Ethical and continuing, and not this. The great
elements are the cut. Cause is the Vaibhāṣika reading of that cut, not a
second definition.

The base includes this eleventh. The essence and the principle are not
enumerated here. The definition is the classification and its conditions.
Which elements, and the later dispute about form-status, stay shut.

The reading is once, here. 1.12 is not opened.
