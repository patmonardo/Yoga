# VAK_1.11 Bhāṣya — The Continuity Called Avijñapti

Source: [`VAK_1.11.md`](./VAK_1.11.md).

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // 1.11 //

> The wholesome or unwholesome continuity which, depending on the
> great elements, belongs even to a distracted or consciousness-absent
> person—that indeed is called *avijñapti*.

The Bhāṣya explains the states named in the verse, extends their
scope through *api*, distinguishes this stream from acquisition,
and states why the name *avijñapti* applies. Its last sentence adds
two origins. The working vocabulary is *citta* = consciousness,
*skandha* = base, *āyatana* = essence, and *dhātu* = principle.
The latter three terms belong to the surrounding classification
and Organon reconstruction; they are not words of this verse.

## 3. Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

> *Avijñapti* must now be explained. It is stated as follows: the
> wholesome or unwholesome continuity which, depending on the great
> elements, belongs even to one whose consciousness is otherwise
> directed or absent is called *avijñapti*.
>
> “Of one whose consciousness is otherwise directed” means also of
> one who has a different consciousness. “Even of one without
> consciousness” means also of one who has entered the attainment
> of non-perception or the attainment of cessation. The word “even”
> also brings in one who has consciousness and is not distracted.
> “Continuity” means a stream. “Wholesome or unwholesome” means
> *kuśala* or *akuśala*.
>
> There is also a similar stream of acquisition with regard to the
> wholesome and the unwholesome. To distinguish this continuity
> from that one, the verse says “depending on the great elements.”
> The Vaibhāṣikas say that *upādāya* has a causal meaning, because
> the elements have causal functions beginning with production.
>
> In “that indeed is *avijñapti*,” the word “indeed” indicates
> the assignment of this name. Although it has the nature of form
> and action, it does not make something known to another as
> *vijñapti* does; hence it is *avijñapti*. “Is called” indicates
> a statement of the teacher. In summary, *avijñapti* is wholesome
> or unwholesome form arising from *vijñapti* or samādhi.

The causal interpretation is explicitly ascribed to the
Vaibhāṣikas. The source does not identify the *ācārya* more
narrowly here, and it presents no separately speaking objector.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
avijñapti is to be explained
    → distracted, consciousness-absent, and undistracted conscious cases
    → anubandha is a stream; śubha/aśubha are kuśala/akuśala
    → elemental dependence distinguishes it from acquisition
    → Vaibhāṣika causal construe of upādāya
    → a form-and-action that does not inform another
    → summary: wholesome/unwholesome form from vijñapti or samādhi
```

The verse defines by continuity, ethical character, and elemental
dependence. The Bhāṣya specifies the extension of that definition
and explains the technical name. Its closing summary supplies
origins that the Kārikā does not itself state.

## 5. States, Scope, and Stream

**Lexical determination and construe**

| Sanskrit | Construe | Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|---|
| *vikṣiptacittakasya* | genitive of the person | *tadanyacittasya*: one with a different consciousness |
| *acittakasya* | second case resolved from the compound | one in non-perception or cessation attainment |
| *apiśabdena* | instrumental, “by the word even” | extends scope to the undistracted conscious person |
| *yo 'nubandhaḥ* | relative with its subject | *yaḥ pravāhaḥ*: a continuing stream |
| *śubhāśubhaḥ* | qualification of that stream | *kuśalākuśalaḥ*: wholesome or unwholesome |

*Tadanyacitta* makes *vikṣipta* an otherwise directed
consciousness in this context; psychological agitation is not
required. *Acittaka* is illustrated by the two technical
attainments. *Api* is decisive because the conspicuous cases
do not exhaust the class: *avijñapti* may continue while
consciousness is present and undistracted.

*Pravāha* gives *anubandha* a temporal determination. The passage
names a continuing series, not one static deposit. It does not
yet explain the full mechanics of that series. Nor does it
claim that its ethical character is merely a present judgment
about a past act: *kuśala/akuśala* qualifies the stream under
discussion.

## 6. Elemental Dependence and Acquisition

The Bhāṣya introduces *prāptivāha*, a stream of acquisition,
because it too can be wholesome or unwholesome. Ethical polarity
plus continuity therefore does not uniquely identify
*avijñapti*. The differentiating phrase is
*mahābhūtāny upādāya*: dependence on the great elements.

```text
wholesome or unwholesome stream
    → could also be prāptivāha

stream depending on the great elements
    → distinguished as avijñapti
```

*Upādāya* is a relation of dependence in the verse. The next
sentence explicitly attributes its causal interpretation to
the Vaibhāṣikas: *hetvartha upādāyārthaḥ*, because the elements
have causal roles beginning with production (*janana*).
The prose establishes a material differentiator. It does not
give a complete account of how every later moment of the
stream is supported; the 1.13 Bhāṣya presses that question.

## 7. Naming, Non-Disclosure, and Origins

*Hi* is explained as signaling the naming: this is what is
called *avijñapti*. The next sentence supplies the reason for
the negative prefix. Although the determination has
*rūpa-kriyā-svabhāva*, the nature of form and action, it does
not inform another person (*paraṃ na vijñāpayati*) as
*vijñapti* does. Non-disclosure is thus a contrast in function,
not a denial of form, action, or all possible cognition of it.

*Ucyate* is then taken as a teacher's statement. The Bhāṣya
does not specify the teacher's identity in this sentence.
Its own concluding *samāsatas tu* gives a compact definition:
*avijñapti* is wholesome or unwholesome form arising from
*vijñapti* or samādhi. The two origins matter. The account
cannot be reduced to the residue of an outward bodily or
verbal manifestation.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. *Vikṣipta* is explained as consciousness directed elsewhere;
   *acittaka* is illustrated by two technical attainments.
2. *Api* includes the undistracted person with consciousness.
3. *Anubandha* is *pravāha*, a stream, and its polarity is
   *kuśala/akuśala*.
4. Dependence on the great elements distinguishes this stream
   from a similar stream of acquisition.
5. The causal force of *upādāya* is a marked Vaibhāṣika reading.
6. *Avijñapti* has form-and-action character without informing
   another as *vijñapti* does.
7. The closing summary adds origins from *vijñapti* or samādhi.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

**Organon reconstruction: continuity across determinations**

The form-base here includes an ethically determined material
stream that persists across several states of consciousness.
The Bhāṣya's tests prevent three reductions: equating the
stream with a current conscious act, treating it as any
wholesome or unwholesome acquisition, and identifying material
action only with outward disclosure.

```text
<skandha, āyatana, dhātu> = <base, essence, principle>

avijñapti
    form-base in the classification of rūpa
    dharma-essence and dharma-principle in 1.15
```

This is a cross-verse Organon reconstruction. The 1.11 unit
establishes the form classification and its differentiating
conditions; 1.15 supplies the later essence and principle
placements. A machine that carries the content across these
arrangements must keep its ethical polarity, stream character,
elemental dependence, and non-disclosive function intact.

The material grounding remains contested in the later 1.13
Bhāṣya. That debate tests whether affecting a manifestation
or elemental support suffices to account for a stream that can
outlast its originating supports. The present passage does not
settle that difficulty. Its positive contribution is an exact
set of relations the eventual principial account must preserve.
