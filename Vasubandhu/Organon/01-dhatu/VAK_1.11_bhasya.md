# VAK_1.11 Bhāṣya — Avijñapti

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // 1.11 //

From [`VAK_1.11.md`](./VAK_1.11.md):

The wholesome or unwholesome binding, even of one whose consciousness is distracted or who is unconscious, depending on the great elements: that is called *avijñapti*.

`Citta` is consciousness. `Manas` is mind, and it is not this word. Span: Pradhan `[007|29]`–`[008|10]`.

## 3. Continuous Translation

*Avijñapti* is now to be explained.

“Of one whose consciousness is distracted” means also of one who has a different consciousness. “Even of one unconscious” means also of one who has entered the attainment of non-perception or the attainment of cessation. Those two are the special states. Only there is consciousness absent in his sense. By the word “even,” one who is undistracted and has consciousness is also understood.

“The binding” means the stream. A socket. What flows is this stream, not a message to another. “Wholesome or unwholesome” means *kuśala* or *akuśala*.

A similar stream of acquisition also exists for the wholesome and the unwholesome. To distinguish this from that, he says “depending on the great elements.” The Vaibhāṣikas say *upādāya* has a causal sense, because the elements function as causes beginning with production.

In “that indeed is *avijñapti*,” *hi* marks the giving of the name. Although it has the nature of form and action, it does not make something known to another, as *vijñapti* does. Hence *avijñapti*. “Is called” indicates the teacher’s statement.

In sum: *avijñapti* is wholesome or unwholesome form arisen from *vijñapti* or from samādhi.

## 4. Review

He stays on *avijñapti*. The two attainments are his technical absence. Acquisition is brought in and cut out. The elements are the distinction. Causal *upādāya* is Vaibhāṣika. The four principles are not this page.
