# VAK_1.11 Bhāṣya — Avijñapti Defined

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // 1.11 //

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> Whatever wholesome or unwholesome continuity belongs even to one whose mind is directed elsewhere or who is without mind, depending upon the great Elements — that indeed is called avijñapti.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Avijñapti must now be explained. It is stated as follows: the wholesome or unwholesome continuity that belongs even to one whose mind is distracted or absent, depending upon the great Elements, is called avijñapti.
>
> “Of one whose mind is distracted” means also of one who has a different mind. “Even of one without mind” means also of one who has entered the attainment of non-perception or the attainment of cessation. By the word “even,” one who is undistracted and has mind is also understood. “The continuity” means the stream. “Wholesome or unwholesome” means kuśala or akuśala.
>
> A similar stream of acquisition also exists with regard to the wholesome and the unwholesome. To distinguish this from that, “depending upon the great Elements” is stated. The Vaibhāṣikas say that “depending upon” has a causal meaning, because the Elements function as causes beginning with production.
>
> In “that indeed is avijñapti,” the word “indeed” indicates the assignment of that name. Although it has the nature of material form and action, it does not make something known to another as manifestation does; hence it is avijñapti. “Is called” indicates the teacher's statement. In summary, avijñapti is wholesome or unwholesome material form arising from manifestation or samādhi.

## 5. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. *Vikṣipta* = a different mind (*tadanyacitta*), not necessarily agitation.
2. Mindless cases are the two attainments, not every lapse of attention.
3. *Api* includes the undistracted person with mind. Avijñapti is not “what happens when citta is dead.”
4. *Anubandha* is a stream (*pravāha*), not a static deposit.
5. Dependence on the Elements distinguishes this stream from acquisition. Causal *upādāya* is Vaibhāṣika.
6. Non-disclosure is *paraṃ na vijñāpayati* — not “unknowable in every respect.”
7. Origins from *vijñapti* and from samādhi belong to the summary, not to the kārikā wording.

## 6. Organon Note

Marked as project reconstruction. Vidyā is not a leak.

```text
vijñapti  :  avijñapti
Concept   :  Idea
savitarka :  nirvitarka
```

Conventional *upādāya* remains dependence. Science reads the same cell as projection: **avijñapti Projects the mahābhūtas**. It does not hide in them. Content is the *saṃyoga* of Bhūtādi and Tanmātra. The Elements are what that Content exposes.

This is the outer projector — one wielder of Māyā. The inner projector is the remaining skandhas. *Api* already refuses citta-killing: the stream runs with mind present.

Samādhi as origin is Discipline-adjacent, not existence-dhyāna as the whole. Do not print *satkārya* or Sarvāstivāda as Vasubandhu's official badge on this page. The conventional line has spoken.
