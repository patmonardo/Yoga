# VAK_1.11 Bhāṣya — Avijñapti Defined

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // 1.11 //

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> The wholesome or unwholesome binding, even of one whose consciousness is distracted or who is unconscious, depending on the great elements: that is called *avijñapti*.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> *Avijñapti* must now be explained. It is stated as follows: the wholesome or unwholesome continuity that belongs even to one whose consciousness is distracted or absent, depending upon the great elements, is called *avijñapti*.
>
> “Of one whose consciousness is distracted” means also of one who has a different consciousness. “Even of one without consciousness” means also of one who has entered the attainment of non-perception or the attainment of cessation. By the word “even,” one who is undistracted and has consciousness is also understood. “The continuity” means the stream. “Wholesome or unwholesome” means *kuśala* or *akuśala*.
>
> A similar stream of acquisition also exists with regard to the wholesome and the unwholesome. To distinguish this from that, “depending upon the great elements” is stated. The Vaibhāṣikas say that “depending upon” has a causal meaning, because the elements function as causes beginning with production.
>
> In “that indeed is *avijñapti*,” the word “indeed” indicates the assignment of that name. Although it has the nature of form and action, it does not make something known to another as *vijñapti* does; hence it is *avijñapti*. “Is called” indicates the teacher’s statement. In summary, *avijñapti* is wholesome or unwholesome form arising from *vijñapti* or from samādhi.

## 5. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. *Vikṣipta* = a different consciousness (*tadanyacitta*), not necessarily agitation.
2. Absence of consciousness is the two attainments, not every lapse of attention.
3. *Api* includes the undistracted person with consciousness. *Avijñapti* is not what happens only when consciousness is gone.
4. *Anubandha* is a stream (*pravāha*), not a static deposit.
5. Dependence on the elements distinguishes this stream from acquisition. Causal *upādāya* is Vaibhāṣika.
6. Non-disclosure is *paraṃ na vijñāpayati* — not unknowable in every respect.
7. Origins from *vijñapti* and from samādhi belong to the summary, not to the kārikā wording.
