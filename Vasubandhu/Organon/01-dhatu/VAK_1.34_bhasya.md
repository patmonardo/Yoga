# VAK_1.34 Bhāṣya — Object-Taking and Organic Appropriation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sapta sālambanāś cittadhātavo 'rdhaṃ ca dharmataḥ /
>
> navānupāttās te cāṣṭau śabdaś cānye nava dvidhā // 1.34 //

> The seven cognitive Principles take objects, as does a portion of the
> Essence Principle. Nine are unappropriated: those eight and Sound. The
> other nine are twofold.

The Bhāṣya identifies the “half” as the associated mental-factor portion
of the Essence Principle. In the second classification it explicitly
counts the whole Essence Principle among the nine unappropriated
Principles. The compact resumption “those eight” must be read with that
change of scope.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

How many take an object, and how many do not?

The seven cognitive Principles take objects. The visual, auditory,
olfactory, gustatory, bodily, and mental-Cognition Principles, together
with the Mind Principle, are these seven. They take objects because
they apprehend an object-field.

And a portion of the Essence Principle: the portion consisting of
associated mental factors takes an object. It is therefore established
that the ten Form Base Principles and the unassociated portion of the
Essence Principle do not take objects.

How many are appropriated, and how many unappropriated? Nine are
unappropriated. Which nine? The seven just described as taking objects,
together with a portion of the eighth [are recalled]. Those eight, and
Sound: these are the nine unappropriated Principles—the seven cognitive
Principles, the Essence Principle, and the Sound Principle.

The other nine are twofold: appropriated and unappropriated. Of these,
the eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body Faculties are appropriated when
present; when past or future, they are unappropriated.

The visible-Form, smell, taste, and tangible-Form Principles are
appropriated when present and inseparable from a Faculty. Other instances are
unappropriated: for example, [those] in hair, body-hair, nails, and teeth
apart from their roots; in feces, urine, saliva, mucus, blood, and the
like; and in earth, water, and so forth.

What does “appropriated” mean? That which Citta and mental factors have
taken up as a supporting basis, because they reciprocally follow one
another in benefit and injury. It is what is called “animate” in
ordinary usage.

The bracketed phrase in the resumption marks the compressed reference
to the previous count. The explicit enumeration that follows supplies
the whole Essence Principle; the translation does not invent a second
half-Principle to make the arithmetic work.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

Two questions organize the unit. The first classifies Principles by
taking an object. The second classifies them by appropriation as a living
support. Each receives an enumeration, and the latter also receives a
temporal distinction, Form examples, and a definition.

There is no marked rival-school voice or sustained objection. “What
does appropriated mean?” asks for the force of the term already used.
The final appeal to what “in the world” is called *sacetana* cites
ordinary usage; it is not introduced as a scriptural quotation.

The sequence matters. Citta and mental factors are first classified as
object-taking and then as unappropriated. In the definition they take
up Form as a supporting basis. The two predicates therefore cannot be
treated as synonyms for participation in cognition.

## 5. Having an Object Is an Active Cognitive Relation

*Viṣayagrahaṇāt*, “because of apprehending an object-field,” supplies
the reason the seven are *sālambana*. They comprise the six
Cognition Principles and the Mind Principle. “Taking an object” describes their
apprehensive relation; it does not mean being physically supported by
an external thing.

The distinction also runs in the other direction. A visible form may
be an object of visual cognition while its Form Principle is
*anālambana*: it does
not itself apprehend an object. Consequently *anālambana* should not
be expanded into “unavailable to cognition” or “outside experience.”

For the Essence Principle, *yac caitasikasvabhāvam* identifies the
relevant portion as associated mental factors. The rest is described as
unassociated.
*Ardham*, “half,” marks this division into portions; the prose supplies
no numerical equality between their members.

The unassociated portion must not be restricted without warrant to
unassociated formations alone. In the established inventory the
Essence Principle also includes avijñapti and the unconditioned. The
present division turns on mental association and object-taking, not
solely on the subdivisions of the Formations Base.

## 6. Why the Second Count Includes the Whole Essence Principle

The first enumeration reaches seven Principles plus a portion of an
eighth. The second states nine whole Principles: the seven cognitive
Principles, the Essence Principle, and Sound. This explicit list governs
*te cāṣṭau*.

| Principle or portion | Takes an object? | Appropriation status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seven cognitive Principles | Yes | Unappropriated |
| Associated mental-factor portion of Essence Principle | Yes | Unappropriated |
| Unassociated portion of Essence Principle | No | Unappropriated |
| Sound Principle | No | Unappropriated |
| Five sensory Faculty Principles | No | Twofold according to time |
| Visible Form, smell, taste, tangible Form | No | Twofold according to time and Faculty-inseparability |

This makes the two classifications reviewable without combining their
counts. The nine twofold Principles are five sensory Faculties plus four
Form-object Principles. Sound is expressly excluded from that group, even
though it may be produced by a living body. The passage states its
classification without separately arguing the cause of that exclusion.

“Unappropriated” here does not mean disconnected from a living being,
unconscious, or ethically neutral. Citta and mental factors themselves
are included among the unappropriated Principles. The predicate concerns
the relation defined at the end of the unit.

## 7. Present Faculties, Form Inseparability, and Reciprocal Effects

For the five sensory Faculties, the prose says present instances are
appropriated and past or future instances unappropriated. It does not
add a condition that a faculty must be currently producing a sensory
cognition. The research phrase “presently functioning” can obscure
this difference between being a present faculty and exercising it in
an occurrent act.

For the four Form-object Principles, two conditions are conjoined:
*pratyutpanna*, present, and *indriyāvinirbhāgin*, inseparable from a
Faculty. Present external Form is therefore not automatically
appropriated. Nor does merely being located within the body suffice,
as the listed excretions and other bodily substances show.

The examples are locative: the relevant visible Form, smell, taste,
and tangible Form occur “in” the listed things. *Mūlavarjeṣu* excludes roots
from the hair, body-hair, nail, and tooth examples. The prose then lists
blood among the unappropriated examples without giving a further
qualification here. That feature of the transmitted list is retained;
it should not be silently rewritten to fit a modern account of tissue
or circulation.

The definition uses *adhiṣṭhānabhāvena*, “as a supporting basis,” and
*upagṛhīta*, “taken up.” *Anyonyānuvidhāna* gives reciprocity: benefit
and injury on one side are correspondingly followed on the other.
This is more specific than ownership, spatial inclusion, or merely
being a cause of a mental event. An external event can affect cognition
without thereby becoming appropriated Form.

The final *sacetana* is rendered “animate” to connect the definition
with ordinary recognition of a living support. “Ensouled” would import
an unnecessary substantial soul into the English. The remark also does
not reverse the earlier result by making Form Base Principles into
object-taking cognitions.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- The seven are six Cognition Principles and the Mind Principle; they take objects
  because they apprehend an object-field.
- The object-taking portion of the Essence Principle consists of associated mental
  factors, without a claim of numerical equality between halves.
- The appropriation count includes the whole Essence Principle, together
  with the seven cognitive Principles and Sound.
- The other nine are five sensory Faculties and four Form-object Principles.
- Present status governs appropriation of faculties; present status
  and Faculty-inseparability jointly govern the four Form-object Principles.
- Appropriation means being taken up as a supporting basis with
  reciprocal responsiveness to benefit and injury.

The original research kārikā and commentary are preserved. These
clarifications belong to the present edition.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage separates cognition's relation to an object from its
relation to an embodied support. The object is apprehended; Form is
taken up as a basis through reciprocal benefit and injury. Neither
relation can stand in for the other. The seven cognitive Principles and
the entire Essence Principle remain unappropriated, while Sound is
unappropriated without being object-taking. This makes the two axes
explicit rather than collapsing them into a single "mental versus
physical" division.

The Fichtean nondual hypothesis treats object and subject as internally
related moments of one cognitive system, not independent substances
connected from outside. Hegelian logic preserves the distinction:
object-taking, cognitive activity, and appropriated Form are determinate
relations within one living whole, not interchangeable terms. The
Bhāṣya's reciprocal test gives this Organon proposal a concrete criterion
for embodied hosting, while guarding against the claim that Form itself
apprehends.

The broader Prajñā/Vijñāna theory can ask how self-relating discernment
is represented through a living host. This verse does not name Prajñā,
Vijñāna, *saṃvṛti*, or *paramārtha*, and it does not explain how
understanding is acquired. Its contribution is to distinguish what is
apprehended, what apprehends, and what supports the living cognitive
continuum. The intended *prakāśa* is the intelligibility of those
relations within the Organon, not a claim made by this enumeration.
