**Close construe.** The Dharma-collections, which, eighty thousand, the Sage proclaimed: those are speech or name, so it is said; of these the inclusion is in Form and Formations.

`IncludedUnder(c,a,b)` is inclusion *relative to an account*: on account *a*, collection *c* is included in Base *b*. The verse pairs the accounts with the Bases respectively; it does not assert that any collection is in both Bases at once. `ReportedAccount` and `Alternative` keep *vā* as a report of two positions, not a choice. `InTotal` attaches the count to the whole; the model does not enumerate eighty thousand items.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The eighty thousand Dharma-collections which the Sage proclaimed — speech, or name — their inclusion is in Form and Formations.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Sage proclaimed eighty thousand Dharma-collections: they are included in the Form Base if Buddha-word is speech by nature, or in the Formations Base if it is name by nature.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The eighty thousand are speech, or name. Their inclusion is in Form, or in Formations.

Speech by nature: the Form Base. Name by nature: the Formations Base. The verse reports both. It does not decide.

The collection is not a further Base. A name in a sūtra—Base, Essence, or Principle—does not stand apart from the places already stated.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The two assignments report positions about Buddha-word: those taking it as speech by nature place the collections in the Form Base; those taking it as name by nature place them in the Formations Base. Neither view is selected, and the collections are not an additional Base.

The hinge is `vā`. The classification receives its own teaching. Speech enters Form. Name enters Formations. The disagreement is left standing.
