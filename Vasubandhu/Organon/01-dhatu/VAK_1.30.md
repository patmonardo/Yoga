# VAK_1.30 — Ethical Classification and Fourteen Principles in the Form Realm

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> त्रिधान्ये कामधात्वाप्ताः सर्वे रूपे चतुर्दश ।
>
> विना गन्धरसघ्राणजिह्वाविज्ञानधातुभिः ॥ १.३० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tridhānye kāmadhātvāptāḥ sarve rūpe caturdaśa /
>
> vinā gandharasaghrāṇajihvāvijñānadhātubhiḥ // 1.30 //

The long closing compound excludes four—not six—Principles. `Ghrāṇa` and
`jihvā` qualify `vijñāna`: the olfactory- and gustatory-cognition
Principles are absent, but the Nose and Tongue faculty Principles
themselves remain.

## 4. Grammar

`Tridhā anye` completes the ethical classification begun in 1.29: the
remaining ten Principles occur as wholesome, unwholesome, and ethically
indeterminate. The next Bhāṣya question begins a distinct classification
by realm:

```text
kāmadhātu
    Desire Realm

rūpadhātu
    Form Realm

ārūpyadhātu
    Formless Realm
```

The realm distribution begins after the opening ethical clause. This verse
gives the Desire and Form Realm counts; VAK 1.31 supplies the Formless Realm.

The Bhāṣya glosses `āpta` precisely:

```text
āpta
    = aviyukta
    = pratisaṃyukta
    = not separated from and connected with a realm
```

It therefore marks realm-membership, not merely spatial presence.

All eighteen Principles belong to the Desire Realm. Fourteen belong to
the Form Realm:

```text
18
− Smell Principle
− Taste Principle
− olfactory-cognition Principle
− gustatory-cognition Principle
= 14
```

The Smell and Taste Principles are absent because their enjoyment depends upon gross
ingested nourishment (`kavaḍīkārāhāra`), from which beings born in the Form
Realm are detached. Their two cognitions are absent because their
object-supports are absent (`ālambanābhāva`).

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The remaining [ten] are threefold [ethically]. All [eighteen] belong to
> the Desire Realm. In the Form Realm there are fourteen, excluding the
> Smell, Taste, olfactory-cognition, and gustatory-cognition Principles.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The remaining ten Principles have wholesome, unwholesome, or ethically
> indeterminate instances. All eighteen Principles are connected with the
> Desire Realm. Fourteen belong to the Form Realm: the Smell and Taste
> Principles are absent because beings there are detached from gross
> ingested nourishment, and the corresponding olfactory- and
> gustatory-cognition Principles are absent because their objects are
> unavailable. The Nose and Tongue faculty Principles themselves remain.

The Bhāṣya's debate is needed to prevent the closing compound from being
misread as excluding the faculty Principles along with their cognitions.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A realm is a determinate configuration of capacities, objects, and acts
> of cognition. Desire sustains the complete eighteen-Principle
> configuration. Form preserves fourteen Principle lineages while
> excluding the Smell and Taste object complex and the cognitions that
> require it. The absence of an object removes its corresponding cognition
> without necessarily removing the embodied faculty associated with it.

Techne reading:

> A Principle instrument belongs to a Realm when that configuration supports
> its causes, objects, functions, and modes of attachment. A Realm is not
> an external container into which finished Principles are placed; it is
> the systematic relation that determines which Principle lineages and
> their derivative relations can be sustained there.

## 8. Logical Determination

The realm configurations are:

```text
PrinciplesOf(DesireRealm)
    = AllEighteenPrinciples

PrinciplesOf(FormRealm)
    = AllEighteenPrinciples
      − {SmellPrinciple,
         TastePrinciple,
         OlfactoryCognitionPrinciple,
         GustatoryCognitionPrinciple}
```

The object-consciousness dependency is:

```text
AbsentIn(SmellPrinciple, FormRealm)
    → NoAvailableAlambana(OlfactoryCognitionPrinciple, FormRealm)
    → AbsentIn(OlfactoryCognitionPrinciple, FormRealm)

AbsentIn(TastePrinciple, FormRealm)
    → NoAvailableAlambana(GustatoryCognitionPrinciple, FormRealm)
    → AbsentIn(GustatoryCognitionPrinciple, FormRealm)
```

But object-absence does not automatically eliminate the corresponding
faculty:

```text
AbsentIn(SmellPrinciple, FormRealm)
    ↛ AbsentIn(NoseFacultyPrinciple, FormRealm)

AbsentIn(TastePrinciple, FormRealm)
    ↛ AbsentIn(TongueFacultyPrinciple, FormRealm)
```

The Bhāṣya's accepted explanation retains internal attachment through
embodied existence while excluding attachment through those external
objects:

```text
AttachmentThroughEmbodiedBasis(Faculty)
    may persist without
AttachmentThroughExternalObject(FacultyObject)
```

The first-pass Principle rule is:

```text
BelongsToRealm(x, r)
    ↔ CompatibleWith(x,
        CausesOf(r),
        AvailablePrinciplesOf(r),
        FunctionsOf(r),
        ModesOfAttachmentOf(r))
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 1.29 began the examination of the Principle Processor by typing the
eighteen Principles by visibility, resistance, and ethical status. VAK 1.30
completes the ethical classification of the remaining ten, then examines
their connection with Realm configurations. Realm is not simply a cosmic
location: `āpta` is glossed as connected and not separated, making the
membership relation causal and functional as well as locational.

The Form Realm is not merely the Desire Realm with four Principles deleted.
The Bhāṣya explains the exclusions through determinate relations:

```text
detachment from ingestion
    → Smell and Taste Principles lose their function

absence of those objects
    → the corresponding Cognition Principles lack objects

continued embodiment
    → Tangible and faculty Principles remain
```

The long Bhāṣya debate concerning nose and tongue is philosophically useful.
It prevents faculty, object, and consciousness from being collapsed into a
single sensory atom. Their relations are lawful, but their presence is not
perfectly coextensive. An object Principle may be absent and its
corresponding cognition therefore unavailable while the internal faculty
Principle remains as part of the embodied basis.

In the incoming Techne, this adds Realm compatibility to an analytically
typed Principle:

```text
Principle Processor
    = intrinsic determinations
    + lawful relations
    + Realm compatibility
```

This verse also tests the distinction between sensory Knowing and
Conceiving. The olfactory- and gustatory-*vijñāna-dhātus* are excluded
because their objects are absent; the Nose and Tongue Faculties remain.
The Bhāṣya separately uses *kāyavāgvijñapti* for bodily and verbal
manifestation. In the incoming Techne, *vijñapti* is Ordinary Knowing,
or Conceiving: here that Knowing is gathered into bodily and verbal
manifestation and can receive ethical determination through the citta
from which it arises. This is not *prati-vijñapti*, Transcendental
Knowing, which this passage does not discuss.

The Form Realm thus preserves the Nose and Tongue faculty lineages while
withholding the gross objects and sensory Knowing relations that depend
on them. This tests the distinction without making the Faculty, its
object, its Knowing, and its manifested action interchangeable.

The Kant–Fichte–Hegel Techne reads the Realm configuration through the
relations among Faculties, objects, cognition, and attachment. The six
internal Essences and their external objects remain distinct from the
eighteen-Principle classification, while their relations are made
explicit in the Bhāṣya.

The Kośa presents Vijñāna as Discriminative Cognition joining and
governing Perception and Conception. Their unity is Inconceivable as a
homogeneous operation; its Idea is disclosed in the Cognition Base.
Vijñāna governs Manas and guides the reading of Dharma Base. Form Base
and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti* distinctly, and Vijñāna
bears a *prati* relation to it. Vijñāna remains distinct from *citta*
and *manas*.
