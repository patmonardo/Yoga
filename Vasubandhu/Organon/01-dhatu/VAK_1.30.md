## 4. Grammar

Three clauses. *Tridhā anye* closes the ethical question of 1.29: the others are threefold. *Kāma-dhātu-āptāḥ sarve* opens the realm question: the participle agrees with *sarve*. *Rūpe caturdaśa* reuses *āptāḥ* by ellipsis; *vinā* takes the instrumental of exclusion. The realm stem *dhātu* in *kāma-dhātu* and *rūpe* (*rūpa-dhātau*) names a Realm: a Principle of Principles, not one of the eighteen.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The others are threefold. All are connected with the Desire Realm. In the Form Realm, fourteen, excluding the Odor, Taste, nose-Cognition, and tongue-Cognition Principles.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The other ten are threefold. All eighteen are connected with the Desire Realm; fourteen occur in the Form Realm, excluding the Principles of Odor, Taste, nose-Cognition, and tongue-Cognition.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The ethical cut finishes the ten. The realm cut begins a new question. Threefold is not three realms.

The Form Realm drops four Principles: two Conditions, and the two Cognitions that take them. It does not drop the two Faculties. Absence of the support removes the Cognition. Absence of a use does not, by itself, remove the Faculty.

The levels stay distinct, and Principle is recursive. The Form Base is a Base; the Visible Form Principle and the other Form-bearing Principles are first-order Principles; the Form Realm is a Principle of Principles, a Principled World for rebirth. The Form Base gathers exactly the ten Form-bearing Principles without being one of them. The Form Realm shares the name *rūpa-dhātu* with the Visible Form Principle and is not it.

**Model checks.** Ten others, all threefold. Eighteen in the Desire Realm; fourteen in the Form Realm. Principles are recursive: the eighteen are first-order Principles; a Realm is a Principle of Principles, constituted by the eighteen it admits, and is not one of the eighteen. The Base is not a Principle at either order. Removing a Faculty from the Form Realm, or admitting a Cognition whose support is absent, falsifies the theory. The prose identities are recorded but not derived from the verse tokens.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya identifies the other ten as the Principles left after the preceding count and explains threefold as wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate. All eighteen are connected with the Desire Realm. In the Form Realm, Odor and Taste are absent, so nose- and tongue-Cognition lack their Conditions; the Faculties themselves remain, for support and bodily appearance. Other proposed explanations of the exclusion remain attributed in the commentary.

The hinge is the verse boundary. *Tridhā anye* closes the ethical question. *Kāmadhātvāptāḥ* opens the realms. The exclusion is four Principles.
