# VAK_1.02 — Abhidharma as Attained Knowing, Path, and Treasury

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> प्रज्ञामला सानुचराभिधर्मः तत्प्राप्तये यापि च यच्च शास्त्रम् ।
>
> तस्यार्थतोऽस्मिन् समनुप्रवेशात् स चाश्रयोऽस्येत्यभिधर्मकोशम् ॥ १.०२ ॥॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> prajñāmalā sānucarābhidharmaḥ tatprāptaye yāpi ca yacca śāstram /
>
> tasyārthato 'smin samanupraveśāt sa cāśrayo 'syety abhidharmakośam // 1.02 //

The supplied nouns in the elliptical sequence must remain visible:

```text
yā [prajñā] api ... tat-prāptaye
    whatever prajñā also [is directed] to attaining that

yat ca śāstram [tat-prāptaye]
    and whatever treatise [is directed] to attaining that
```

The Kārikā itself does not state that the second prajñā is `sāsrava`. That determination is supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 4. Grammar

### First predication: ultimate Abhidharma

```text
prajñā amalā sānucarā = abhidharmaḥ

subject feature agreement
    prajñā       feminine nominative singular
    amalā        feminine nominative singular
    sānucarā     feminine nominative singular

predicate
    abhidharmaḥ  masculine nominative singular
```

The difference in gender is expected: the adjectives agree with `prajñā`, while `abhidharmaḥ` is a predicate noun.

### Extended relative construction: conventional Abhidharma

```text
tat-prāptaye
    ├── yā [prajñā] api
    └── yat ca śāstram
```

The dative `tat-prāptaye` gives the common purpose governing both the unexpressed `prajñā` and the expressed `śāstram`: they are ordered toward attainment of that, namely the stainless prajñā already defined.

The Bhāṣya assigns the two levels explicitly:

```text
pāramārthika Abhidharma
    amalā prajñā sānucarā
    stainless prajñā with attendants

sāṃketika Abhidharma
    yā [prajñā] tat-prāptaye
    yat śāstram tat-prāptaye
    prajñā and treatise directed to its attainment
```

It further determines the first as an `anāsrava` five-aggregate complex, and the path-prajñā as `sāsrava` prajñā constituted through hearing, reflection, cultivation, or birth-acquisition. These are Bhāṣya determinations, not words contained in the Kārikā.

### The two title derivations

The last half-verse permits two directions of construal, made explicit by the Bhāṣya:

```text
1. tasya arthataḥ asmin samanupraveśāt

   Abhidharma ──enters by principal meaning──▶ this treatise
   contained                                      container

2. saḥ ca āśrayaḥ asya

   Abhidharma ──serves as supporting source──▶ this treatise
   source                                         extracted work
```

In the first derivation, this treatise occupies the place of a treasury because it contains the principal meaning of śāstric Abhidharma. In the second, Abhidharma is itself the treasury or source from which this treatise is drawn.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Stainless prajñā, with its attendants, is Abhidharma; so also is whatever [prajñā] is for the attainment of that, and whatever treatise [is for its attainment]. Because that enters into this by meaning—and because that is the supporting basis of this—[this treatise is called] the *Abhidharmakośa*.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Stainless prajñā together with its attendants is Abhidharma in the ultimate sense: the discrimination of dharmas, with its retinue, a stainless complex of the five aggregates. In the conventional sense, so too are the prajñā with outflows constituted by hearing, reflection, and cultivation, and the prajñā acquired by birth, each with its attendants, and the treatise that is equipment for attaining that stainless prajñā. A dharma is so called because it bears its own mark. Abhidharma is this dharma facing the ultimate dharma, nirvāṇa, or facing the mark of a dharma. This work is called the *Abhidharmakośa* either because the principal meaning of śāstric Abhidharma is contained within it, or because it has been drawn from Abhidharma as its supporting treasury.

The second translation makes the Bhāṣya's determinations explicit. It is not a word-for-word rendering of the Kārikā alone. Neither facing in the name-definition is dropped. *Amalā* stays stainless in this line.

## 6. Philosophical Translation and Techne Reading

> Abhidharma is first the Discriminator: stainless discrimination of dharmas, occurring with its full accompanying complex. By designation, the name also extends to prajñā still on the way, and to the treatise that functions as equipment for attaining it. The Kośa mediates between these orders: it gathers Abhidharma's principal meaning, yet is itself drawn from Abhidharma as its source.

Techne reading:

> In our Yoga Vidyā, *prajñā* is the larger **Vidyā**, the **Science of Principles**. The Bhāṣya specifies *dharmapravicaya*—discrimination of dharmas—as an operation of this science; it does not reduce the whole Vidyā to that operation. The treatise and modes of learning belong to the science by their directedness toward its attainment. The Kośa holds Abhidharma's principal meaning and is itself drawn from Abhidharma as source.

The Bhāṣya names the first sense *pāramārthika* and the second *sāṃketika*.
Our Techne names the former Pure Prajñā; this does not replace the verse's
“stainless” or make the directed means identical with the attainment.

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
ABHIDHARMA
│
├── ultimate (pāramārthika)
│   └── stainless prajñā with attendants
│       └── discrimination of dharmas
│       └── an anāsrava five-aggregate complex
│
└── conventional (sāṃketika)
    ├── sāsrava prajñā directed toward the ultimate
    │   ├── hearing-constituted
    │   ├── reflection-constituted
    │   ├── cultivation-constituted
    │   └── birth-acquired
    └── śāstra serving as equipment for attainment

dharma
    = bears its own mark

Abhidharma
    = this dharma facing nirvāṇa
      or facing the mark of a dharma
```

Techne names, not the verse lines: *prajñā* = Science of Principles;
*anāsrava* = Pure; *sāsrava* = Impure.

The title then expresses reciprocal dependence:

```text
Abhidharma ──principal meaning enters──▶ Kośa
Kośa ──is drawn from──────────────▶ Abhidharma

first relation:  Kośa is treasury-like for Abhidharma
second relation: Abhidharma is the treasury-source of the Kośa
```

The two relations must not be reduced to a single generic notion of “support.” They reverse the direction of containment and dependence. The two facings must not be reduced to one.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 1.02 determines the relation between realized discrimination, the path that produces it, and the textual equipment that serves that path.

```text
sāsrava prajñā and textual equipment
    are directed toward
stainless prajñā with attendants
```

The Bhāṣya characterizes attained *prajñā* through its operation,
*dharmapravicaya*, together with its attendants. It then gives the means
directed toward that attainment: hearing,
reflection, cultivation, birth-acquired *prajñā*, and the treatise as
equipment. A candidate for Abhidharma can enter through instruction
without being credited prematurely with attained Pure Prajñā.

The name *Abhidharma* keeps two orientations together: toward nirvāṇa and
toward the mark of a dharma. The title *Kośa* likewise has two directions:
the principal meaning enters the book, while the book is drawn from its
source. For our Kant-informed Techne, this is an explicit architecture of
a science: an end, an act of knowing, a field of discrimination, and
transmissible means ordered to that act. The practical necessity of
instruction is argued in 1.03; the field's first comprehensive division
follows in 1.04.
