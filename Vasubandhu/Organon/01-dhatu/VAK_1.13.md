# VAK_1.13 — Worldly Names, the Form-Base, and Avijñapti

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> पृथिवी वर्णसंस्थानमुच्यते लोकसंज्ञया ।
>
> आपस्तेजश्च वायुस्तु धातुरेव तथापि च ॥ १.१३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

## 4. Grammar

```text
earth = color and configuration named by worldly designation
water and fire = [the same verb is understood]
wind, however = the Principle itself, and also named in that way
```

The Bhāṣya explains that people point out earth, water, and fire through
visible color and configuration. Wind may refer to the Wind Principle
itself, yet a visible storm may also be called wind.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

By worldly designation, color and configuration are called “earth”; [so
too] water and fire. Wind, however, is the Principle itself, and yet also
in that way.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

What people point out as earth, water, or fire is color and configuration
named by worldly convention. “Wind” may name the Wind Principle itself, or
a visible storm by its color and configuration.

## 6. Systematic Placement

The verse distinguishes worldly naming from the technical Principle. The
Bhāṣya explains how the names apply; the visible appearance and the
Principle are not interchangeable.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| varṇa | color | visible appearance |

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
earth, water, fire
    → named through visible color and configuration

wind
    → may name the Wind Principle
    → may also name a visible storm
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

The contrast marks two uses of “wind”: the Wind Principle and the worldly
name for a visible storm. The Bhāṣya supplies both uses.
