# VAK_1.13 — Worldly Designation and the Principle

**Feature chart checkpoints.** First clause spans [0, 4); extension [4, 7); wind clause [7, 13); the root spans [0, 13). The wind clause records both the Principle use and the visible use. No claim that all names designate Principles is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** Color and configuration are called earth by worldly designation; water and fire likewise. Wind, however, is the Principle itself—and also in that way.

The projection records naming relations, not identity between a name and its referent. The Bhāṣya resolves "likewise" for water and fire and "in that way" for wind. Those resolutions are explicit in the productions. *Eva* identifies wind's Principle referent; the final concession prevents an exclusive Principle-only reading.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> By worldly designation, color and configuration are called earth. Water and fire too. Wind, however, is the Principle itself — and yet also in that way.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> By worldly designation, earth, water, and fire are color and configuration; wind, however, is also the Principle itself, though in that way too it is called from color and configuration.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Again, two lines. The first is what the world points at. The second is the wind exception, and the concession that keeps the exception from becoming a rule.

Earth, water, and fire, as named, are color and configuration. Pointing shows the visible complex. It does not show hardness, cohesion, or heat. Those are the Principles of 1.12. Designation and Principle are not the same determination. The common word and the Principle are not coextensive.

Wind is the case that keeps the pair honest. The world can call the Wind Principle itself wind. Impulsion can wear the ordinary name. And yet a blue storm or a circular storm is also called wind. *Tu* distinguishes. *Tathāpi ca* gives the visible use back. One name, two determinations. The input can be named, and the shown can be named, and they are not the same.

The verse stops there. The commentary asks why the series through *avijñapti* is the Form Base. Being affected is the reason offered: alteration, or obstruction. That fits what can be touched and shown. It does not fit the continuity as stated.

The repairs fail in order. Affected through the *vijñapti* it arises from, as a shadow moves with the tree: then the continuity would cease when the manifestation ceases. It does not. Affected because the supporting Great Elements are affected: then Eye-Cognition would be Form, because its Faculty is affected. The inequality offered is continuing dependence against a mere condition of arising. The criticism rejects the analogy, and states the fact: *avijñapti* does not cease when the Great Elements that supported it have ceased. The last reply keeps the classification. It does not explain the later moment.

What the verse expresses is the cut. Worldly designation points at color and configuration. The Principle is not that pointing, except in the wind case. The continuity is the member the commentary cannot reduce to either. The grounding stays contested.

The next verse defines reflection. Not this one.
