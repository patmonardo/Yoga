# VAK_1.37 — Causal Genesis and Reinforcement of Principles

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विपाकजौपचयिकाः पञ्चाध्यात्मं विपाकजः ।
>
> न शब्दोऽप्रतिघा अष्टौ नैःष्यन्दिकविपाकजाः ॥ १.३७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vipākajaupacayikāḥ pañcādhyātmaṃ vipākajaḥ /
>
> na śabdo 'pratighā aṣṭau naiḥṣyandika-vipākajāḥ // 1.37 //

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |

| vipākajāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | maturation-born |
| aupacayikāḥ | nominative masculine plural adjective | reinforced through upacaya |
| pañca | numeral | the five sensory Faculty Principles |

`Naiḥṣyandika` should not be confused with `sāsrava`, “with outflows.” Here
it names production through a corresponding causal flow or homogeneous
continuity, specified in the Bhāṣya through `sabhāgahetu` and
`sarvatragahetu`.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause assigns two origins to the five internal Faculty
Principles:

```text
pañca adhyātmam
    Eye, Ear, Nose, Tongue, and Body Faculties

vipākajāḥ
    maturation-born

aupacayikāḥ
    reinforced through upacaya
```

The Bhāṣya analyzes `vipākaja` as an abbreviated
`vipāka-hetu-ja`: born from a maturation-cause. `Aupacayika` is explained
through increase by nourishment, conditioning, sleep, and distinctions
of concentration. Some add celibate discipline, but Vasubandhu notes
that this may merely prevent injury rather than produce positive
reinforcement.

The next clause is singular:

```text
śabdaḥ na vipākajaḥ
    sound is not maturation-born
```

The Bhāṣya does not deny every causal mode to Sound. It may be
upacaya-reinforced or continuity-born. It is excluded specifically from
direct maturation because its present occurrence proceeds from effort
(`īhātaḥ pravṛtti`).

The final clause concerns the eight non-resistant Principles:

```text
seven Cognition Principles
+ Dharma Principle
    → continuity-born or maturation-born
```

These are permitted causal classes, not a claim that each instance
arises from both causes. The eight are not upacaya-reinforced; the
Bhāṣya attributes this to the absence of accumulation in non-resistant
Principles.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The five internal [Faculty Principles] are maturation-born and upacaya-reinforced. Sound is not maturation-born. The eight non-resistant Principles include continuity-born and maturation-born instances.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The five sensory Faculties are maturation-born and upacaya-reinforced through nourishment, conditioning, sleep, and distinctions of concentration. Sound is not itself maturation-born because its immediate production proceeds from effort, although it may be upacaya-reinforced or arise through causal continuity. The seven Cognition Principles together with the Dharma Principle include continuity-born and maturation-born instances, but are not upacaya-reinforced.

For the eight Principles, these are permitted causal classes among their
instances; the verse does not say that each instance is produced
simultaneously by both causes.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A Principle is determined not only by its present character but also by the manner in which its continuity is generated. The Faculties possess a maturation-born constitution that can be maintained and reinforced through living conditions. Sound is an event initiated through present effort rather than a direct maturation-result. The non-resistant Cognition Principles and the Dharma Principle lack upacaya reinforcement yet remain subject to causal continuity and maturation.

Techne reading:

> The Principle Processor distinguishes maturation, reinforcement, and continuity as causal determinations. The internal Faculties are maturation-born and reinforced through living conditions; Sound is not directly maturation-born even where its causal history includes karma. The non-resistant Cognition Principles and Dharma Principle are causally continuous without being upacaya-reinforced.

## 8. Logical Determination

The faculty genesis types are:

```text
FiveInternalFacultyPrinciples
    → Vipakaja
    ∧ Aupacayika

Upacaya
    ← Nourishment
    ∨ Conditioning
    ∨ Sleep
    ∨ ConcentrationDifference
```

Preservation and increase are non-equivalent:

```text
PreventsInjury(x)
    ↛ PositivelyIncreases(x)
```

The two faculty continuities cooperate:

```text
VipakaContinuity
    → supplies maturation-born Faculty-capacity

UpacayaContinuity
    → protects and reinforces VipakaContinuity
```

Sound is classified by immediate production:

```text
ProducedThroughPresentEffort(Sound)
    → ¬Vipakaja(Sound)

IndirectlyConditionedByKarma(Sound)
    ↛ DirectlyVipakaja(Sound)
```

The eight non-resistant Principles have causal but non-upacaya genesis:

```text
EightApratighaPrinciples
    → (MayHaveNaisyandikaInstances
        ∨ MayHaveVipakajaInstances)
    ∧ NoAupacayikaInstances
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 1.37 adds causal lineage to the Principle. The question is no longer
only what a Principle is capable of doing; it is how that capacity came to
be present and how its continuity is maintained.

The Indriya receives an especially powerful determination:

```text
karma reaching fruition
    → maturation-born Faculty-continuity

nourishment, sleep, conditioning, concentration
    → reinforced Faculty-continuity

Faculty-operation
    → actual apprehensive capacity
```

Thus the verse treats the internal Faculties as Principles with a causal
history and maintainable continuity, not as isolated or self-explaining
capacities. Maturation and reinforcement are distinct contributions to
their continued operation.

Sound reveals the difference between remote ancestry and immediate genesis.
A disciplined karmic history may condition the Form-support of a beautiful
voice, yet the actual Sound proceeds through present effort. The Logic must
therefore classify an event by its operative mode of production rather
than assigning the same causal label to everything downstream from karma.

The eight non-resistant Principles make the complementary point. Their
lack of upacaya reinforcement does not imply absence of causal lineage:

```text
not-upacaya-reinforced
    ≠ non-causal
```

Cognition Principles and the Dharma Principle include instances produced
through homogeneous causal continuity and instances that arise as
maturation-results. Inwardness does not exempt a state from causal genesis.

The Principle Processor therefore distinguishes:

```text
causal maturation
    + living reinforcement
    + present operation
```

The verse establishes these causal determinations for the Principles; it
does not collapse causal origin, reinforcement, and operation into one
relation.

Vijñāna is Discriminative Cognition joining and governing Perception and
Conception; their unity is Inconceivable as homogeneous operation, and its
Idea is disclosed in the Cognition Base. Vijñāna governs Manas and guides
Dharma Base; Form Base and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti*
distinctly, with Vijñāna bearing a *prati* relation to it. Vijñāna remains
distinct from consciousness (*citta*) and Mind (*manas*).
