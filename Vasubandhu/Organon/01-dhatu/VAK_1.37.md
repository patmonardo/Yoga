# VAK_1.37 — Maturation, Reinforcement, and Causal Continuation

The line break joins *vipākajaḥ* with *na śabdaḥ*: Sound is not maturation-born. *Vipākaja* is *vipāka-hetu-ja* with the middle member dropped, as in *go-ratha*.

`Naiḥṣyandika` should not be confused with `sāsrava`, “with outflows.” Here it names production through a corresponding causal flow, specified in the Bhāṣya through `sabhāgahetu` and `sarvatragahetu`.

**Grammar tokens.** Verse-order IAST with sandhi opened (`vipākajaupacayikāḥ` → *vipākaja-aupacayikāḥ*, `pañcādhyātmaṃ` → *pañca adhyātmam*, `śabdo 'pratighā` → *śabdaḥ apratighāḥ*) and compound boundaries marked. *Vipāka-ja* occurs three times: in the first dvandva, alone, and in the last dvandva.

```text

```

## 4. Grammar

Three clauses. The first predicates a plural dvandva of the five internal Principles; the numeral's count matches the class. The second is singular: *vipākajaḥ* agrees in number with *śabdaḥ*, and that agreement is what binds the end of the first line to the beginning of the second. The third predicates a plural dvandva of the eight non-resistant Principles; the count matches the class of 1.29, eighteen less ten resistant.

Each dvandva predicates two modes of a Principle, and a mode is predicated through its instances: some instance is born that way. The verse does not say each instance is born both ways.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Five spans [0, 3); sound [3, 6); eight [6, 9); the root spans [0, 9). Three clauses. No claim that each instance is born both ways is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** The five internal are maturation-born and upacaya-reinforced. Sound is not maturation-born. The eight non-resistant are continuity-born and maturation-born.

The negation in the second clause is narrow: it denies one mode to Sound, not every mode. The Bhāṣya gives Sound upacaya and continuity instances, and excludes maturation because Sound proceeds from effort. Upacaya is denied to the eight by the Bhāṣya, not by the verse: the non-resistant lack accumulation.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The five internal [Faculty Principles] are maturation-born and upacaya-reinforced. Sound is not maturation-born. The eight non-resistant Principles include continuity-born and maturation-born instances.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five internal Faculty Principles are maturation-born and reinforced through increase. Sound is not maturation-born. The eight non-resistant Principles have instances born of causal continuity and of maturation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

A Principle is determined not only by its present character but also by the manner in which its continuity is generated. The Faculties possess a maturation-born constitution that can be maintained and reinforced through living conditions. Sound is an event initiated through present effort rather than a direct maturation-result. The non-resistant Cognition and Dharma Principles lack upacaya reinforcement yet remain subject to causal continuity and maturation.

Interpretation:

The Principle of the Agent now receives a genesis protocol. Its Faculties are inherited capacities whose maturation-continuity is protected and reinforced by living conditions. Cognition Principles are non-resistant without being causally ungrounded. The Agent is therefore not posited outside causality; it is a differentiated system of generated and maintained capacities.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Key-term evidence.** Here *hetu* first does work in the chapter. *Vipākaja* is *vipāka-hetu-ja*, and the Bhāṣya keeps the dropped *hetu* by name; *naiḥṣyandika* is generated by *sabhāga-hetu* and *sarvatra-ga-hetu*. The mode of genesis is the Ground. Effort (*īhā*) is not a Ground of this kind but what Sound proceeds from, and it is enough to block maturation; the remote karmic ancestry of the Brahmā voice is not. The Ground in force is the immediate one. 2.49ff will give the six Grounds their protocol.

**Translation note.** The eight non-resistant Principles are the seven Cognition Principles and the Dharma Principle; the Bhāṣya distinguishes their continuity-born and maturation-born instances.

VAK 1.37 adds causal lineage to the Principle. The question is no longer only what a Principle is capable of doing; it is how that capacity came to be present and how its continuity is maintained.

The Indriya receives an especially powerful determination:

```text
karma reaching fruition
    → maturation-born Faculty-continuity

nourishment, sleep, conditioning, concentration
    → reinforced Faculty-continuity

Faculty-operation
    → actual apprehensive capacity
```

Thus the Faculty is not reducible to visible configuration, is not an independent Seer, and is not a merely abstract function. It is an internal capacity with a causal history and a maintainable continuity. This is part of the constitution of the Agent, not an incidental biological note.

Sound reveals the difference between remote ancestry and immediate genesis. A disciplined karmic history may condition the Form-support of a beautiful voice, yet the actual Sound proceeds through present effort. The Logic must therefore classify an event by its operative mode of production rather than assigning the same causal label to everything downstream from karma.

The eight non-resistant Principles make the complementary point. Their lack of upacaya reinforcement does not imply absence of causal lineage:

```text
not-upacaya-reinforced
    ≠ non-causal
```

Cognition and Dharma flow through homogeneous causal continuities and may also arise as maturation-results. Inwardness does not exempt a state from causal genesis.

The Principle of the Agent has consequently acquired a genetic architecture:

```text
Agent capacity
    = inherited causal lineage
    + living reinforcement
    + present operation
```

Later Yoga will work upon this constituted Agent. It will not create its faculties ex nihilo; it will regulate, reinforce, transform, and eventually purify modes whose causal structures the Kośa has already made explicit.
