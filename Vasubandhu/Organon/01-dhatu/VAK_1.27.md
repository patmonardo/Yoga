**Close construe.** Likewise, other Bases, Essences, and Principles also are, as appropriate, to be assigned among those already stated, having determined the own-characteristic.

The rule is a universal over designations from other sūtras. Each must have an own-characteristic, determined before assignment, and a place among the established that the characteristic fits. `ToBeAssigned` renders the gerundive's obligation, not a completed act. `Established` ranges over the five, the twelve, and the eighteen already stated; the rule cannot introduce a place. The dvandva is a disjunction of name-kinds, not a claim that each designation bears all three names.

The absolutive precedes the gerundive. A scriptural name is not yet a place. The place is among the five, the twelve, and the eighteen already stated.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Likewise, other Bases, Essences, and Principles also are to be assigned, as appropriate, among those already stated, having determined the own-characteristic.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Likewise, other Bases, Essences, and Principles are to be assigned as appropriate among those already stated, after determining each one's own characteristic.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Likewise the others. Determine the own-characteristic. Then assign.

The name Base, or Essence, or Principle, in another sūtra, is not a new member. It is assigned among those already stated, as appropriate.

Principal nature and the attended complex are not the same scope. Eight totalities are non-greed, and so the Dharma Essence. With their attendants they are five Bases, and so the Mind Essence and the Dharma Essence. The change of place is the change of scope. It is not two systems.

The classification does not grow. The five, the twelve, and the eighteen remain the places.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya applies the rule to scriptural designations by determining each nature as set out in the treatise before assigning it among established classifications. It distinguishes principal nature from an item with its attendants: the eight totalities are non-greed by nature, but with attendants span five Bases and Mind and Dharma Essences; analogous distinctions apply to masteries and liberations. Other examples include the formless attainments and the sixty-two Principles, whose detailed assignments are not enumerated here.

The hinge is the absolutive. A scriptural name poses the problem. The own-characteristic answers it. Attendants change the scope, not the system. The classification remains the places already stated.
