The scope of Pure comes from the division in 1.04. The roster names the cessations using the Bhāṣya's referents, without inserting those names into the witness. The Bhāṣya's “where Form can move” explains non-obstruction; it does not classify space as a conditioned container.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The pure are the Truth of the Path and also the threefold unconditioned: space and the two cessations. Among these, space is non-obstruction.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Path Truth is Pure, and the unconditioned is also threefold: space and the two cessations, cessation through discrimination and cessation not through discrimination. Among these, space is non-obstruction, where Form can move.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The pure member named in the previous verse is enumerated here. It is not the unconditioned as such.

The Truth of the Path is conditioned, and pure. Space and the two cessations are unconditioned, and pure. The reason is the same failure: the *āsravas* do not persist in them. Persistence was the test of the impure. Its absence is the test of the pure.

Space is defined. The two cessations are named. Their definitions are the next verse.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Kārikā, derived.** The root SEM states the pure classification, the unconditioned roster, and the definition of space. Pure does not mean unconditioned: the Path is included without being one of the three. The specific identities of the two cessations and the gloss “where Form can move” are prose elaborations.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya explains that the Path Truth and the three unconditioned dharmas are Pure because the outflows do not persist through them; it identifies the two cessations and defines space as non-obstruction.

The verse completes the division opened in 1.04. Four dharmas are pure. One of them is conditioned. Space receives its definition. The two cessations do not.
