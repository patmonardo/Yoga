# VAK_1.46 Commentary — Plane Relations of Body, Eye, Form, and Cognition

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> na kāyasyādharaṃ cakṣur ūrdhvaṃ rūpaṃ na cakṣuṣaḥ /
>
> vijñānaṃ cāsya rūpaṃ tu kāyasyobhe ca sarvataḥ // 1.46 //

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Eye Faculty may be on the Body's plane or a higher plane, but never a lower one. Visible Form and Eye-Cognition may each be on the Eye's plane or a lower one, but never a higher one. Relative to Eye-Cognition, visible Form may be higher, lower, or on the same plane; relative to the Body, both visible Form and Eye-Cognition may stand in any of those three relations.

The commentary assigns Body, Eye, and visible Form five possible planes:
the Desire Realm and the first through fourth meditative levels.
Eye-Cognition is restricted to two: the Desire Realm and the first
meditative level.

### Anchor note

The commentary specifies the distinct plane ranges of the four factors
and resolves the pronouns. The alternatives in the final clause remain
subject to those ranges; they do not allow every possible combination.

## 3. Continuous Translation

When someone situated in a Body sees visible Forms with an Eye, must
that Body, Eye, visible Form, and Cognition all belong to one plane,
or can they belong to different planes? They can differ.

For someone born in the Desire Realm who sees Forms of that same
plane with their own Eye, all four belong to that plane.

[The following sentence is defective. It mentions the person's own
Forms, Forms from the first meditative level, and “all three at that
level,” but the relations among them cannot be fully recovered.]

For that person, when seeing Forms of their own plane with an Eye
from the second meditative level, Body and Form belong to their own
plane, the Eye to the second level, and Cognition to the first.
When they see Forms of the first level, Cognition and Form belong
to that level, the Body to the Desire Realm, and the Eye to the
second level. When they see Forms of the second level, Eye and Form
belong to that level, the Body to the Desire Realm, and Cognition
to the first level. Apply the same pattern when an Eye from the
third or fourth level sees Forms from that or a lower level.

For someone born at the first meditative level who sees Forms of
their own plane with their own Eye, all four belong to that plane.
When they see lower Forms, the other three remain at their own plane.
When they see Forms of their own plane with an Eye from the second
level, three belong to their own plane and the Eye to the second.
When they see Forms from the Desire Realm, Body and Cognition remain
at their own plane, the Forms are lower, and the Eye belongs to the
second level. When they see Forms from the second level, Eye and Form
belong to that level and the other two remain at their own plane.
Apply the same pattern with an Eye from the third level.

For someone born at the second or a later meditative level, apply
the cases of seeing Forms from their own or another level with Eyes
from their own or another level as appropriate.

This, however, is the fixed rule: the Eye is not lower than the
Body. Body, Eye, and visible Form belong to five planes, from the
Desire Realm through the fourth meditative level. Eye-Cognition
belongs to two: the Desire Realm and the first meditative level.
The Eye is on the Body's plane or a higher one, never a lower one.
A visible Form from the Eye's plane or a lower plane can be a
Condition for Eye-Cognition. A Form from a higher plane cannot be seen
by an Eye from a lower plane.

Eye-Cognition, like visible Form, is not on a higher plane than
the Eye. For the immediately preceding Eye-Cognition, visible Form
may be a Condition from a higher, lower, or the same plane. Relative
to the Body, both visible Form and Cognition may each be from any
of those three planes.

“Higher” and “lower” refer to the stated planes, not physical
height. These final alternatives remain subject to the stated
ranges and restrictions; they do not add Eye-Cognition at the
second through fourth meditative levels.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The opening question distinguishes four factors in a visual event:
Body, Eye, visible Form, and Cognition. The examples first show
that their planes need not coincide. The text then states rules
that organize those distributions.

The surviving examples are more extensive than the research
excerpt's rule-only presentation. Their restoration shows why
“one's own plane” must be read relative to the person's birth plane,
while “that plane” follows the particular Eye or Condition just named.

No rival school is explicitly named in this unit. The exposition
works within the level-classification it states. The defective
opening example is a source problem, not evidence of a
competing doctrine.

## 5. Four Factors, Two Different Ranges

| Factor | Planes stated in this passage |
|---|---|
| Body | Desire Realm and first through fourth meditative levels |
| Eye Faculty | Desire Realm and first through fourth meditative levels |
| Visible Form | Desire Realm and first through fourth meditative levels |
| Eye-Cognition | Desire Realm and first meditative level |

The Body is where the person is situated; keep it distinct from the
Eye Faculty used in seeing. The passage does not assign the Body's
plane automatically to every Faculty, Condition, or Cognition
associated with that person.

Likewise, a higher-plane Eye does not entail Eye-Cognition at
that same plane. The examples place Eye-Cognition at the first
meditative level even when the Eye is at the second. This is why
all four factors must be tracked separately.

## 6. The Relative-Level Rules

The verse gives three restrictions and two comparisons:

| Comparison | Rule |
|---|---|
| Eye relative to Body | Same plane or higher |
| Visible Form relative to Eye | Same plane or lower |
| Eye-Cognition relative to Eye | Same plane or lower |
| Visible Form relative to Eye-Cognition | Higher, same, or lower |
| Visible Form and Eye-Cognition relative to Body | Higher, same, or lower |

The verse carries the restriction “not higher than the Eye” forward
to Eye-Cognition. “That Cognition” refers to the Eye-Cognition just
mentioned. “Both” refers to visible Form and Cognition, not to Body
and Eye.

“In every relation” means higher, lower, and on the same plane. It
does not mean that every assignment of five levels to four factors
is allowed. Eye-Cognition's two-plane range and the restrictions
relative to the Eye remain in force. These comparisons do not prove
that every combination satisfying them occurs.

The damaged 35.04–05 sentence is not used to populate this table.
The intact examples involving an Eye from the second meditative level
already demonstrate the key
point: Body, Faculty, Condition, and Cognition may differ without
violating the stated ordering.

The text instructs the reader to extend certain cases to higher
eyes and higher birth levels “as appropriate.” That instruction
should remain subject to the fixed rules rather than becoming
a license to invent additional levels of visual cognition.

## 8. The Commentary's Decisions for the Verse

- Interpret higher and lower through plane-level comparison, not spatial position.
- Distinguish Body, Eye, visible Form, and Cognition throughout.
- Preserve five possible planes for the first three and two for Eye-Cognition.
- Carry the negative comparison with the Eye into Eye-Cognition.
- Resolve “that Cognition” as Eye-Cognition and “both” as Form and Cognition.
- Restrict “in every relation” by the already stated ranges and rules.
- Preserve the damaged opening example as uncertain rather than
  completing it silently from the later rule.

## 9. Interpretation

The commentary distinguishes the planes of Body, Eye, visible Form,
and Eye-Cognition. The Eye is not below the Body, while visible Form
and Eye-Cognition are not above the Eye. The final comparisons allow
higher, lower, or same-plane relations within the stated ranges. The
plane language does not describe physical height.

Within this Principle analysis, the four factors remain distinct
cognitive Principles rather than an undifferentiated inventory.

In the governing model, Vijñāna as Discriminative Cognition joins and
governs Perception and Conception. Their unity is Inconceivable as a
homogeneous operation, and its Idea is disclosed in Cognition Base.
Vijñāna governs Manas and guides the reading of Dharma Base. Form Base
and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti* distinctly; Vijñāna bears
a *prati* relation to it and remains distinct from consciousness and Mind.
