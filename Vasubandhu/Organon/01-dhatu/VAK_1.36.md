# VAK_1.36 — The Outer Four

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
छिनत्ति छिद्यते चैव बाह्यं धातुचतुष्टयम् ।
दह्यते तुलयत्येवं विवादो दग्धृतुल्ययोः ॥ १.३६ ॥
```

Cutting is defined. Burning and weighing are the same four, then disputed.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The outer tetrad of Principles cuts and is cut. It is burned, and it
> weighs. Likewise. There is a dispute about the burner and the weigher.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The four external Principles—Visible Form, Smell, Taste, and Tangible—cut
> and are cut; they are burned and weigh. Whether this same fourfold group is
> the burner and the weigher is disputed.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The outer four do the work and undergo it. Visible, smell, taste, tangible: axe and wood are names of that tetrad. Cutting is not a split of the organ. It is a divided arising of a connected stream. The Faculty does not become two. The severed limb is without it. Clarity keeps the Faculty from cutting, and from being burned, and from weighing. Sound is cut off, so it is not in the count.

The dispute is only about the burner and the weigher. One side keeps the four. The other names the Fire Principle, and heaviness. The fire is the Element. It is not a new Principle.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya identifies the four as Visible Form, Smell, Taste, and Tangible, and explains cutting as division in a stream of clusters. The Faculties are not cut or burned. It records two positions on the burner and weigher: the same fourfold group, or Fire Principle alone as burner and heaviness alone as what is weighed.

The hinge is the Faculty. A severed limb does not divide it. The dispute does not reopen the tetrad. It only asks what burns and what is weighed.
