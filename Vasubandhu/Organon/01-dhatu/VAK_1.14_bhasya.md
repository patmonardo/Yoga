# VAK_1.14 Bhāṣya — The Same Ten, Feeling, and Reflection

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> indriyārthās ta eveṣṭā daśāyatanadhātavaḥ /
> vedanānubhavaḥ saṃjñā nimittodgrahaṇātmikā // 1.14 //

> The five Faculties and five meanings are accepted as ten Essences and the
> same ten Principles. Feeling is experience; Reflection has taking up a
> mark as its nature.

The commentary distributes the ten, then defines feeling and reflection. The
next verse takes up formations. It is not taken here.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Those already described as belonging to the Form Base—the Faculties and their
meanings—are the very same ten accepted as Essences and Principles.

Arranged as Essences, they are ten: Eye Essence, Visible Form Essence, and so
on through Body Essence and Tangible Essence. Arranged as Principles, these
same ten are Eye Principle, Visible Form Principle, and so on through Body
Principle and Tangible Principle.

The Form Base and its arrangement as Essences have now been stated. Feeling
and the remaining Bases must be explained. First, Feeling is experience.
Experience is threefold—pleasant, painful, and neither painful nor pleasant—
and constitutes the Feeling Base. Divided according to the contact from which
it arises, Feeling forms six groups, from Feeling born of eye-contact through
Feeling born of Mind-contact.

Reflection has taking up a mark as its nature. Taking up marks such as blue,
yellow, long, short, woman, man, *mitramitra*, pleasant, painful, and the
rest constitutes the Reflection Base. Divided in the same way as Feeling,
Reflection forms six groups.

## 5. The Same Ten

*Ya eva rūpaskandhasvabhāvā uktāḥ* resumes the Form Base. *Te eva* carries
the same Faculties and meanings. *Daśa* is distributed.

| Arrangement | Opens | Closes | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| essence | eye-essence, visible-form-essence | body-essence, tangible-essence | ten |
| principle | eye-principle, visible-form-principle | body-principle, tangible-principle | the same ten |

The lists alternate Faculty and meaning. *Yāvat* covers the middle. *Avijñapti*
is in the Form Base and not among these ten. *Ukto rūpaskandhaḥ* closes that
account before the new definitions.

## 6. Feeling

Feeling is undergoing. Three tones: pleasant, painful, neither. Six groups,
by contact, from the eye through mind. The tones are how it is undergone. The
groups are the contact it is born from. Not nine bases. No order against
reflection is stated.

## 7. Reflection

The nature is the taking up of the mark. The commentary gives the marks:
blue and yellow, long and short, woman and man, pleasant and painful, and the
transmitted *mitramitra*. The operation is reflection. The English does not
replace the Sanskrit.

*Asau saṃjñāskandhaḥ* gathers it as the reflection-base. *Vedanāvat* transfers
the six groups. It does not transfer the three tones, and it does not identify
the two bases.

Worldly designation in 1.13 was a name for color and configuration. This
reflection is the taking up of the mark. Not the same word in force.

## 8. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. The ten essences and the ten principles are the same five Faculties and five
   meanings.
2. Each arrangement has ten. *Avijñapti* is not among them.
3. Feeling is three tones of undergoing, and six groups by contact.
4. Reflection takes up the mark, and has six groups on the same pattern.
5. The Form Base account closes in this unit. Formations begin after it.

## 9. Interpretation

*Te eva* is the hinge. Changing the arrangement does not create another Faculty
or another meaning. Ten essences, and the same ten principles. Identity is
kept. The arrangements are not two inventories.

The five Faculties serve in relation to their meanings. Essence and Principle
classify those same members without becoming interchangeable projections.
Form and Essence may articulate the philosophical reading where useful;
Dharma Base, Dharma Essence, and Dharma Principle remain protocol names.

Feeling undergoes. Reflection takes the mark. Blue is a mark. Long is a mark.
The mark is not yet the principle. The six groups run from eye-contact through
mind-contact. Mind is the sixth support of the group. Not a seventh substance,
and not stated as such here.

The continuity of 1.13 is absent from the ten. It remains in the Form Base,
and it is not given an essence or a principle in this verse. That placement
is addressed at 1.16. It is not supplied here.

**One over many.** The proposed case is identifying an instance as a cat
without *vijñapti*. It is not an account of first disclosing multiple cats
and then abstracting their common kind. This directs the *avijñapti*
inquiry toward determination operative without disclosure. The verse's
mark-taking definition does not assert a requirement for *vijñapti*.
Identifying, disclosing, and retaining a determination remain distinct
functions whose relation we are investigating. The *aviśeṣa–viśeṣa*
comparison is our research direction, not terminology introduced by this
unit.
