# VAK_1.45 — Named by the Basis

Two ablatives give two grounds. The first makes the Eye and the rest Bases. The second, with *ataḥ*, takes that result and adds the ground of the name. *Taiḥ* is plural because it points back to *cakṣuḥ-ādayaḥ*.

## 4. Grammar

Two sentences joined by *ataḥ*. In the first, *āśrayāḥ* and *cakṣuḥ-ādayaḥ* agree as masculine plural nominatives: a predicate noun and its subject with no verb. The ablative *tad-vikāra-vikāritvāt* is the ground. *Tad* points to the Cognitions those Faculties are Bases of: each such Cognition is altered when its Faculty is altered.

In the second, *vijñānam* is the subject of the passive *nirucyate*, and *taiḥ* is the agent of the naming. Its number must match the plural of the first sentence (NUM is shared across the two sentences), so the name is taken from the Eye and the rest and from nothing else. *Ataḥ* is not an empty "therefore". It carries the Basis relation of the first sentence into the second: Cognition is named by what is its Basis. The ablative *a-sādhāraṇatvāt* adds the second ground: that Basis is not in common with another Cognition.

**Close construe.** Because of being altered by their alteration, the Eye and the rest are Bases. From this, for they are not in common, Cognition is designated by them.

Cognition arises dependent on both Basis and Support. The verse picks out the Basis twice: once by alteration, once by not being in common.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Because of being altered by their alteration, the Eye and the rest are Bases. Therefore, because they are not in common, Cognition is designated by them.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Eye and the other Faculties are Bases, because Cognition is altered by their alteration. Therefore, because they are not shared, Cognition is designated by them.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Both are required. Only one is the Basis. The cut is the alteration. Benefit and impairment of the eye, acuity and dullness, are followed by the Cognition. A change in the Visible Form is not followed in that way. The dependence on the eye is the stronger one. That is why it is the Basis.

The name follows the Basis, not the Support. Eye-Cognition, not Form-Cognition. The eye is not in common. It cannot be the Basis of another Cognition. A Visible Form can be the Support of Mind-Cognition and of another Eye-Cognition. The drum names the sound. The barley names the sprout. The Basis names the Cognition.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Key-term evidence.** *Āśraya* and *ālambana* are set against each other in one Bhāṣya sentence: the eye cannot become the Basis (*āśrayī-bhavitum*) of another Cognition, but a Visible Form becomes the Support (*ālambanī-bhavati*) of Mind-Cognition and of another Eye-Cognition. That fixes the two renderings: Basis is unshared and named; Support is shared and not named.

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya says the Cognitions alter with changes in their Faculties, while an alteration of Visible Forms and the other Conditions does not similarly change them. Cognition depends on both a Faculty as Basis and a Visible Form as Support; the Support may be shared by more than one Cognition, while the Basis is specific.

The hinge is the name. The Support is shared. The Basis is not. Cognition is designated by the Basis that is not in common.
