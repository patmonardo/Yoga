**Feature chart checkpoints.** Reasons span [0, 2); placement [2, 7); the root spans [0, 7). The two initial ablatives give reasons for placement. No claim that separate Bases are not mental factors is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** Because of being causes of the roots of dispute and of saṃsāra, and because of the reason of order, Feeling and Reflection are placed as separate Bases apart from the other mental factors.

The projection records the asserted reasons and the placement, not a material implication that claims causal sufficiency. The Bhāṣya resolves the causal compound's distribution; the root grammar does not yet assign the two named fixations. The reason of order is acknowledged by name, not demonstrated here. Separation is represented as a classification relation, not a historical event in which Feeling and Reflection ceased being mental factors.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Because they are causes of the roots of dispute and of saṃsāra, and because of the reason of order, feeling and reflection are established as two distinct bases apart from the other mental factors.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Because they are causes of the roots of dispute and of saṃsāra, and because of the reason of order, feeling and reflection were established as distinct Bases apart from the other mental factors.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Again, two lines. The first names the reasons. The second names the placement.

The question is why two mental factors receive separate Bases rather than being gathered with the rest in the Formations Base. Not because they cease to be mental factors. Their operations are the principal hinges of the two roots of dispute, and of continued wandering.

Feeling is undergoing. The hinge is the savor of that undergoing. Under that savor, one clings to sensual desire. Reflection takes up the mark. The hinge is the inverted mark. Under that inversion, one clings to views. Principal, not sole.

The one who wanders is greedy for the savor of feeling and inverted in reflection. The two determinations join in one subject. They are not two independent inventories.

The last reason is the order of the bases. It is promised in three ways. It is not given here.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya names fixation on sensual desire and fixation on views as the two roots of dispute. Feeling, through its savor, and reflection, through inversion, are their respective principal causes and are also principal causes of saṃsāra. It announces but defers a threefold demonstration of the reason of order.

The hinge is *pṛthak*: apart, and still mental factors. Interpretation of the two roots is in the Bhāṣya.
