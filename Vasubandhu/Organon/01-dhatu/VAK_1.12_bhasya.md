## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> “Depending upon the great Elements” was stated. Which Elements are these? The Elements are the earth-*dhātu* and the water-, fire-, and wind-*dhātus*. These four are *dhātus* because they sustain their own-characters and derived form; they are called the four great Elements. Their greatness is due to their magnitude as the support of all other form. Alternatively, it is because of their extensive presence in masses of earth, water, fire, and wind in which the corresponding Element operates prominently.
>
> In what functions are these *dhātus* established, and what are their own-natures? Established in functions beginning with support. Earth, water, fire, and wind are respectively established in sustaining, gathering, ripening, and spreading. Spreading is increase and extension. These are their functions. Their own-natures, respectively, are hardness, cohesiveness, heat, and impulsion.
>
> Hardness is the earth-*dhātu*. Cohesiveness is the water-*dhātu*. Heat is the fire-*dhātu*. Impulsion is the wind-*dhātu*. It is called impulsion because through it the stream of Elements is impelled, by being produced in another place, as in the impulsion of a lamp-flame.
>
> In the treatises and in a sūtra: “What is the wind-*dhātu*? Light movement.” But that lightness is also said to be derived form. Therefore wind is the dharma whose own-nature is impulsion: its own-nature is made evident through its function.

## 5. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. *Dhātu* is explained by *dhāraṇa*, not by an empty box-word.
2. Greatness: magnitude-as-support, or presence in masses. *Skandha* here is a mass, not the five aggregates.
3. Function and own-nature are two lists.
4. *Vyūhana* is increase and spreading, not “motion” alone.
5. Lightness is derived form; wind is impulsion.
6. *Karmaṇā svabhāvo 'bhivyaktaḥ* — function makes own-nature evident.
