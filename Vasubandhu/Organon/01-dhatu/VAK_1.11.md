**Feature chart checkpoints.** Relative clause spans [0, 7); naming [7, 11); the root spans [0, 11). The relative and correlative bind to one continuity. No claim that ethical stream alone suffices, or that dependence alone suffices, is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** Which wholesome or unwholesome continuity belongs even to one otherwise directed or without consciousness, depending on the Great Elements—that indeed is called *avijñapti*.

The logical projection treats the relative as a classification rule: ethical stream plus Elemental dependence warrants the name. Its scope includes the two conspicuous consciousness cases; the Bhāṣya expands *api* to the undistracted case too. Dependence is not silently strengthened into the Vaibhāṣika cause-gloss.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The wholesome or unwholesome continuity which, depending on the Great Elements, belongs even to one whose consciousness is otherwise directed or absent: that indeed is called *avijñapti*.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The wholesome or unwholesome continuity that persists even when consciousness is directed elsewhere or absent depends on the Great Elements and is called *avijñapti*.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Again, a definition. Named at 1.09. Counted around at 1.10. Defined here. The word stays Sanskrit.

The continuity is the subject. Wholesome or unwholesome is its character. *Api* keeps the minded case. The conspicuous cases are not the whole class: otherwise directed, without consciousness, and also present and undistracted. The commentary reads the first as a different consciousness, and the second as the attainment of non-perception or the attainment of cessation. Continuity is a stream.

Ethical continuity alone would also fit acquisition. *Mahābhūtāny upādāya* is the differentiator. The Vaibhāṣikas take *upādāya* as cause, because the Great Elements produce and the rest. That reading is marked. It is not the verse's only word.

The name is the contrast. Form and action, and yet it does not inform another as *vijñapti* does. The summary adds two origins, *vijñapti* or absorption. The verse does not. Outward action is not the whole.

Which Great Elements are meant is the next question. Not this verse.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya explains that *api* also includes a person whose consciousness is present and not otherwise directed; it specifies non-perception and cessation attainments as examples of absence of consciousness. It further glosses *avijñapti* as Form and action that does not inform another as *vijñapti* does.

The hinge is *upādāya*, then *api*. Interpretation of the name is in the Bhāṣya.
