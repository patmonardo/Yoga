# The Principles

## VAK_1.11 — The Continuity Called Avijñapti

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विक्षिप्ताचित्तकस्यापि योऽनुबन्धः शुभाशुभः ।
>
> महाभूतान्युपादाय स ह्यविज्ञप्तिरुच्यते ॥ १.११ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // 1.11 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

**Sentence words**

```text
vikṣipta-acittakasya | api | yaḥ | anubandhaḥ | śubha-aśubhaḥ |
mahābhūtāni | upādāya | saḥ | hi | avijñaptiḥ | ucyate
```

**Sandhi and compound members**

| Verse form | Resolution | Force |
|---|---|---|
| vikṣiptācittakasyāpi | vikṣipta + acittakasya + api | even of one otherwise directed or without consciousness |
| yo 'nubandhaḥ | yaḥ + anubandhaḥ | the continuity which |
| śubhāśubhaḥ | śubha + aśubhaḥ | wholesome or unwholesome |
| mahābhūtāny upādāya | mahābhūtāni + upādāya | depending on the Great Elements |
| sa hy avijñaptir ucyate | saḥ + hi + avijñaptiḥ + ucyate | that indeed is called *avijñapti* |

The first compound is compressed. The Bhāṣya explains its two cases
separately as *vikṣiptacittakasya* and *acittakasya*. *Vikṣipta*
is clarified by *tadanyacitta*, consciousness directed elsewhere;
*acittaka* refers here to two specified attainments. *Api* also
brings the undistracted conscious case into scope. These are
commentarial determinations, not additional verse words.

*Anubandha* names a continuing attachment or connection; the
Bhāṣya glosses it as *pravāha*, a stream. *Upādāya*, an absolutive
from *upa-ā-√dā*, marks dependence in the verse. Its specifically
causal construal is attributed in the prose to the Vaibhāṣikas.

## 4. Grammar

**Relative and correlative definition**

The frame is *yaḥ anubandhaḥ ... saḥ avijñaptiḥ ucyate*:
the continuity which satisfies these conditions is called
*avijñapti*. *Śubhāśubhaḥ* agrees with the masculine
*anubandhaḥ*. The predicate name *avijñaptiḥ* is feminine;
the gender difference belongs to the naming construction.

The genitive identifies whose continuity is in question. The
verse foregrounds distraction and absence of consciousness;
*api* prevents the definition from being restricted to those
states, as the Bhāṣya explicitly explains. *Mahābhūtāny upādāya*
qualifies the continuity by dependence on the Great Elements.
The verse does not itself say that this dependence is causal in
the precise Vaibhāṣika sense, or name the stream of acquisition
from which the Bhāṣya distinguishes it.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The wholesome or unwholesome continuity which, depending on the
Great Elements, belongs even to a distracted or consciousness-absent
person—that indeed is called *avijñapti*.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

*Avijñapti* is a wholesome or unwholesome Form-stream that
continues when consciousness is directed elsewhere, during the
specified attainments without consciousness, and also while
consciousness is present and undistracted. Its dependence on the
Great Elements distinguishes it from a similar stream of
acquisition. It has the nature of Form and action but does not
make something known to another as *vijñapti* does.

The prose further summarizes its possible origin in *vijñapti*
or samādhi. The stream, the comparison with acquisition, the
non-disclosure, and those two origins are Bhāṣya explanations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

**Organon reconstruction**

The Form Base includes a continuity whose ethical determination
persists across changes in occurrent consciousness. *Avijñapti*
is neither an outward manifestation nor merely a remembered act.
Form here is Form Theory, not matter. The verse specifies dependence
on the Great Elements; the Organon treats this as a Form determination,
not as a reduction to material substance. The Bhāṣya makes the stream's
temporal character precise with *pravāha* and distinguishes it from
another ethically marked stream by its dependence on the Great Elements.

For the Base / Essence Base / Principle machine, this creates a concrete
classification demand. The content belongs to the Form Base as a
Form-determination. In 1.15 the same content is placed under
Dharma-skandha—the project term for the Essence Base, not one of the five
Bases—and the Dharma Principle. The machine must preserve the same
*avijñapti* through these different determinations without recasting it as
a mental factor. This cross-mapping belongs to the Organon's
reconstruction across verses; it is not fully stated in 1.11.

The precise Form determination remains a question. The Bhāṣya's
later debate in 1.13 tests how dependence on the Great Elements
can justify Form-status when a stream continues beyond its
originating manifestation and supports. The present verse and
prose establish the classification and its differentiating
conditions; they do not settle that later objection.

by a claim that the determination is unreal or unknowable.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The key restriction is *mahābhūtāny upādāya*: ethical continuity
alone would also fit the acquisition stream. The key extension
is *api*: the named distracted and consciousness-absent cases
do not exhaust the range. The Bhāṣya's closing summary adds
the two origins and thereby keeps *avijñapti* from being reduced
to a residue of outward action alone.

The classification is consequential for the Organon, but the question
of Form-status remains open. The later 1.13 argument must
be read before claiming that elemental dependence supplies a
complete account of why *avijñapti* belongs to the Form Base.
