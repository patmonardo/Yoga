# VAK_1.11 — The Continuity Called Avijñapti

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विक्षिप्ताचित्तकस्यापि योऽनुबन्धः शुभाशुभः ।
>
> महाभूतान्युपादाय स ह्यविज्ञप्तिरुच्यते ॥ १.११ ॥॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // 1.11 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
vikṣiptācittakasya   → vikṣipta + acittakasya
api                  → even
yo 'nubandhaḥ         → yaḥ + anubandhaḥ
śubhāśubhaḥ           → śubha + aśubhaḥ
mahābhūtāny upādāya → mahābhūtāni + upādāya
sa hy avijñaptiḥ     → saḥ + hi + avijñaptiḥ + ucyate
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| vikṣipta-acittakasya | genitive singular | of one otherwise directed, or without consciousness |
| api | particle | even; the scope is wider |
| yaḥ ... saḥ | relative and correlative | which continuity is so called |
| anubandhaḥ | nominative masculine | the continuity |
| śubha-aśubhaḥ | nominative masculine | wholesome or unwholesome |
| mahābhūtāni | accusative plural | the great elements |
| upādāya | absolutive | depending on |
| hi | particle | indeed; the naming |
| avijñaptiḥ | nominative feminine | the name |
| ucyate | passive | is called |

The compound is compressed. The commentary separates *vikṣiptacittakasya*
and *acittakasya*. *Api* also brings in one whose consciousness is present
and not otherwise directed.

## 4. Grammar

```text
yaḥ anubandhaḥ
    śubha-aśubhaḥ
    vikṣipta-acittakasya api
    mahābhūtāni upādāya
        → saḥ avijñaptiḥ ucyate
```

A relative definition. *Śubhāśubhaḥ* agrees with *anubandhaḥ*. The name
*avijñaptiḥ* is feminine. The gender difference belongs to the naming.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

*Avijñapti* is a wholesome or unwholesome stream. It continues when
consciousness is directed elsewhere, in the two attainments without
consciousness, and also while consciousness is present and not distracted.
Dependence on the great elements distinguishes it from a similar stream of
acquisition. It has the nature of form and action, and does not inform
another as *vijñapti* does. In summary, it is wholesome or unwholesome form
arisen from *vijñapti* or from absorption.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The key restriction is *mahābhūtāny upādāya*: ethical continuity
alone would also fit the acquisition stream. The key extension
is *api*: the named otherwise-directed and consciousness-absent cases
do not exhaust the range. The Bhāṣya's closing summary adds
the two origins and thereby keeps *avijñapti* from being reduced
to a residue of outward action alone.

The classification is consequential for the Organon, but the question
of Form-status remains open. The later 1.13 argument must
be read before claiming that elemental dependence supplies a
complete account of why *avijñapti* belongs to the Form Base.
