# VAK_1.29 Bhāṣya — Visible, Resistant, Indeterminate

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sanidarśana eko 'tra rūpaṃ sapratighā daśa /
>
> rūpiṇo 'vyākṛtā aṣṭau ta evārūpaśabdakāḥ // 1.29 //

> One here is visible: form. Ten are resistant. Eight form-bearing ones are indeterminate: those same, excluding form and sound.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Of these eighteen principles that have been stated, how many are visible, and how many are not?
>
> One here is visible: form.
>
> For it can be pointed out: this, here, there. The remainder are not visible.
>
> How many are resistant, and how many are not?
>
> Ten are resistant.
>
> The form-bearing ones.
>
> The ten principles included in the form-base are resistant.
>
> Resistance means striking, and it is threefold: obstruction, condition, and support.
>
> Obstruction is the blocking of another's arising in one's own place. A hand is struck by a hand, or by a stone. A stone is struck by either.
>
> Condition-resistance belongs to the eye and the rest, as possessors of a condition, toward visible form and the rest as conditions. As the Prajñapti says: there is an eye struck in water but not on land, as with fish; on land but not in water, as generally with humans; in both, as with the dolphin, the frog, the piśāca, the fisherman, and the rest; in neither, setting those cases aside. There is an eye struck at night but not by day, as with the titīla, the owl, and the rest; by day but not at night, as generally with humans; by night and by day, as with the dog, the jackal, the horse, the leopard, the cat, and the rest; in neither, setting those cases aside. This is condition-resistance.
>
> Support-resistance belongs to consciousness and the concomitants, toward their own supports.
>
> What, then, is the difference between condition and support? That in which something has its function is its condition. What is grasped by consciousness and the concomitants is the support.
>
> Why is what is operating in its own condition, or in its support, said to be struck? Because it does not operate by another. Or the particle here marks the resistance that is the operation in its own condition.
>
> Here the resistance of the ten is to be known by obstruction, because they obstruct one another.
>
> Are those resistant by condition-resistance also resistant by obstruction? Four alternatives. The first: the Cognition Principle, the seven — six cognition-principles and the mind-principle — and the associated portion of the dharma-principle. The second: the five conditions. The third: the five organs. The fourth: the portion of the dharma-principle excluding the associated.
>
> Are those resistant by condition-resistance also resistant by support-resistance? The implication runs back. Whatever is resistant by support is also resistant by condition. There can be resistance by condition alone, not by support: the five organs.
>
> Where others can strike a mind about to arise, that is to be known as resistant; the reverse is accepted as not resistant. So the Venerable Kumāralāta. The resistant and the non-resistant have been stated.
>
> Of these eighteen principles, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many indeterminate?
>
> Eight are indeterminate.
>
> Which eight? Those ten stated as resistant, excluding form and sound.
>
> The five organs, and the smell, taste, and tangible principles. These eight are indeterminate because they are not declared wholesome or unwholesome. Others say: because they are not declared with respect to maturation. Then the same would follow for the Pure as well.

## 4. Movement

Three questions. The middle one opens the three resistances, then returns to obstruction for the count of ten. The Prajñapti examples are reported. Kumāralāta is named. The maturation account is others, and it is answered.

## 5. Decisions

1. The one visible is the visible-form principle, not the form-base.
2. The ten resistant are the ten principles in the form-base.
3. The count uses obstruction. Condition-resistance and support-resistance are not the count.
4. The four alternatives are a comparison. They do not enlarge the ten.
5. The eight are those ten minus visible form and sound.
6. Indeterminate means not declared wholesome or unwholesome. The maturation account would take the Pure with it, and fails.
7. The final compound excludes two names. It does not empty the eight of form.

## 6. Organon Reading

Visibility, resistance, and ethical status stay three. The processor is being shown how to cut, not given a new list. The seven in the comparison are the Cognition Principle: six cognition-principles and the mind-principle. They are not the ten. Cognition is not principle. The Pure is not indeterminate by the rejected account.
