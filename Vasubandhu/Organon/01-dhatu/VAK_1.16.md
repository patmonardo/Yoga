## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Cognition is respective apprehension. And that is the Mind Essence. Seven principles are accepted: the six Cognitions, and Mind.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Cognition Base is respective apprehension of each Condition. It is also the Mind Essence. In the Principle arrangement, it is counted as seven Principles: the six Cognition Principles and the Mind Principle.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

What is defined is the Base. As Essence it is Mind; as Principle it is seven, and the last of these is the Mind Principle.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya explains Cognition as apprehension with respect to each Condition and divides it into six groups, from Eye-Cognition through Mind-Cognition. It reads the final *manaḥ* as *manodhātu* (Mind Principle), not as Mind alone; the seven Principles are the six Cognition Principles and the Mind Principle.

The verse cross-classifies the Cognition Base as Mind Essence and seven Principles. The next verse specifies the support-role of the Mind Principle.
