## 4. Grammar

Three clauses. The first completes the realm count of 1.30: the participle agrees with the three-member compound. The second opens a new cut: *ete trayaḥ* resumes the three, and the dvandva predicates both Impure and Pure. The third contrasts the rest with *tu*: Impure only. All adjectives are masculine plural, agreeing with *dhātavaḥ*.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Formless spans [0, 2); both [2, 5); rest [5, 8); the root spans [0, 8). Three clauses. No claim that Pure means Formless is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** Connected with the Formless are the Mind, Dharma, and Mind-Cognition Principles. These three are Impure and Pure. But the rest are Impure.

Impure and Pure are predicated of a Principle through its instances: a Principle has Pure instances, Impure instances, or both. *Tu* sets the fifteen against the three, so their Impurity is exclusive; the Bhāṣya says so with *ekānta-sāsravāḥ*.

The second line is a new cut. It is not restricted to births in the Formless.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Connected with the Formless are the Mind Principle, the Dharma Principle, and the Mind-Cognition Principle. These three are Impure and Pure. The rest are Impure.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Connected with the Formless are the Mind Principle, Dharma Principle, and Mind-Cognition Principle. These three are Impure and Pure; the remaining fifteen are Impure.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Formless drops the ten whose nature is Form, and the five Cognitions that take them. What remains is Mind, Dharma, and Mind-Cognition. That is a birth-cut, not a denial that the Concept has Form.

The Pure and the Impure are a second cut. These three admit both. The fifteen do not. Pure here is Path or unconditioned. It is not the Formless.

**Model checks.** Eighteen Principles; three connected with the Formless, fifteen not. The fifteen are exactly the ten Form-natured Principles and the five Cognitions that take them as support and Condition. Each Principle has an Impure instance; only the three have Pure instances. Making a Form-natured Principle Formless-connected, or giving one of the fifteen a Pure instance, falsifies the theory. The Formless Realm is a Principle of Principles constituted by the three. The prose identities are recorded but not derived from the verse tokens.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The three are Mind, Dharma, and Mind-Cognition Principles; they are connected with the Formless Realm because the ten Form-natured Principles and the five Cognition Principles taking them as support and Condition do not occur there. The Pure instances belong to the Path-truth or the unconditioned; the other instances are Impure. The remaining fifteen are exclusively Impure.

The hinge is the second line. The three names carry forward. The Pure and the Impure do not mean three Formless things that are both at once. They mean these three Principles have instances of both.
