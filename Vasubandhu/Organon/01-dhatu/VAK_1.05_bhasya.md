# VAK_1.05 Bhāṣya — The Pure, and Space

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> anāsravā mārgasatyaṃ trividhaṃ cāpy asaṃskṛtam /
> ākāśaṃ dvau nirodhau ca tatrākāśam anāvṛtiḥ // 1.05 //

> The Path Truth is Pure, and the unconditioned is also threefold: space
> and the two cessations, cessation through discrimination and cessation
> not through discrimination. Among these, space is non-obstruction, where
> Form can move.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Which dharmas are pure?

The Truth of the Path is pure, and so is the threefold unconditioned.

What is the threefold? Space and the two cessations.

Which are the two? Cessation through discrimination, and cessation not through discrimination.

Thus the threefold unconditioned beginning with space, together with the Truth of the Path, are pure dharmas.

Why? Because the *āsravas* do not persist in them.

Among the three unconditioned dharmas just enumerated, space is non-obstruction. Space has the nature of non-obstruction: it is where form can move.

The previous cut holds. Persistence fails in all four.

## 6. Space

```text

    = anāvṛti
    = anāvaraṇa-svabhāva

```

Not a container. Not an infinite substance.

## 7. Interpretation

Marked. Not the conventional line.

Pure is the failure of persistence. The Path is still conditioned. The three unconditioned are not produced through a before. The test is the same verb as 1.04. It fails here.

## 8. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. The subject is the pure dharmas.
2. The Path is conditioned and pure. The other three are unconditioned and pure.
3. The two cessation-names are supplied here. Definitions wait for 1.06.
4. Space is non-obstruction, where form can move.
5. Pure is the headword. Persist is the verb.
