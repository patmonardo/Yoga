## 4. Grammar

Two cuts, one constituent each. The Support cut predicates *sālambanāḥ* of the seven, with the numeral agreeing in count with the class, and adds a half of the Dharma Principle. The Appropriation cut counts nine Unappropriated as a list, eight resumed by *te* and Sound, and gives the other nine, the complement in the eighteen, as twofold. The count feature `CNT` checks the arithmetic: 7 for the consciousness Principles, 8 + 1 = 9 for the list, and 9 again for the others.

**Feature chart checkpoints.** Support cut spans [0, 6); appropriation cut [6, 15); the root spans [0, 15). Two cuts. No claim that the two cuts coincide is generated by the verse.

**Close construe.** Seven consciousness Principles have a Support, and a half with respect to the Dharma Principle. Nine are Unappropriated: those eight, and Sound. The other nine are twofold.

The first cut quantifies over the consciousness Principles and asserts only that some portion of the Dharma Principle has a Support; which portion, the verse leaves to the Bhāṣya. The anaphor *te … aṣṭau* is resolved in the lexicon by the Bhāṣya's own naming: the seven and the whole Dharma Principle. Unappropriated is said of every instance; twofold means some instance is Appropriated and some is not.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Seven consciousness Principles have a Support, and a half with respect to the Dharma Principle. Nine are Unappropriated: those eight, and Sound. The other nine are twofold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Seven consciousness Principles and the mental-factor portion of the Dharma Principle take a Support. Nine are Unappropriated: those eight and Sound. The other nine are twofold.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Taking a Support is the seven, and the associated half of the Dharma Principle. The ten whose nature is Form do not take a Support. Neither does the unassociated half.

Appropriation is a different cut. The nine that never are appropriated are the seven, the whole Dharma Principle, and Sound. The other nine are Appropriated only when present and, for the four meanings, only when not separate from the Faculty. Appropriated is the basis citta has taken up. Benefit and injury run both ways. That is what is called animate.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Key-term evidence.** The Bhāṣya glosses *sālambana* by *viṣaya-grahaṇāt*. Having a Support (*ālambana*) is grasping a Condition (*viṣaya*). 1.29 kept the two apart: a Condition is where a thing acts, a Support is what citta and the mental factors grasp. Here the grasped Condition is the Support. Neither is a Ground (*hetu*).

**Translation note.** The seven consciousness Principles are the five sensory Cognitions, the Mind-Cognition Principle, and the Mind Principle; the relevant half of the Dharma Principle is its mental-factor portion. The nine unappropriated are those seven, the Dharma Principle, and Sound. “Appropriated” means taken up by consciousness and mental factors as a basis through mutual benefit or injury—the animate, as the world calls it.

The hinge is the change of scope. The first count uses a half. The second count uses the whole Dharma Principle. Sound joins the nine. The definition of appropriated is the close.
