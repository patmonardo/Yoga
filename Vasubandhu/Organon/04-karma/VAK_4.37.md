# VAK_4.37

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
असंवरस्य क्रियया लाभोऽभ्युपगमेन वा ।
शेषाविज्ञप्तिलाभस्तु क्षेत्रादानादरेहनात् ॥ ४.३७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
asaṃvarasya kriyayā lābho 'bhyupagamena vā /
śeṣāvijñaptilābhas tu kṣetrādānādarehanāt // 4.37 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
asaṃvarasya kriyayā lābhaḥ abhyupagamena vā /
śeṣa-avijñapti-lābhaḥ tu kṣetra-ādāna-ādara-īhanāt //
```

The second half is densely compressed. The Bhāṣya controls its distribution:
remaining avijñapti arises through a specially efficacious field, through a
deliberate undertaking, or through action pursued with sufficient earnestness.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
asaṃvarasya
kriyayā
lābhaḥ
abhyupagamena
vā

śeṣa-avijñapti-lābhaḥ
tu
kṣetra-ādāna-ādara-īhanāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `asaṃvarasya` | genitive masculine singular | of non-restraint |
| `kriyayā` | instrumental feminine singular | through action or performance |
| `lābhaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | acquisition |
| `abhyupagamena` | instrumental masculine singular | through acceptance or deliberate adoption |
| `vā` | disjunctive particle | or |
| `śeṣāvijñaptilābhaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | acquisition of the remaining avijñapti |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but; in the remaining case |
| `kṣetra` | compound member | an efficacious field |
| `ādāna` | compound member | taking up; clarified as `samādāna` |
| `ādara` | compound member | earnestness, seriousness, intensity |
| `īhana` | ablative member from `īhā` | striving, active performance |

**`kriyā`**

The Bhāṣya specifies the operation of killing (`vadhaprayogakriyā`) performed
by persons born into the relevant occupational lineage. Birth into the
lineage by itself is not said to produce non-restraint; performance makes the
harmful practical form operative.

**`abhyupagama`**

For someone outside the occupational lineage, deliberate adoption of the
livelihood can generate non-restraint:

```text
vayam api anayā jīvikayā jīviṣyāmaḥ
    “We too shall live by this livelihood.”
```

`Abhyupagama` is therefore not casual approval. It is the adoption of a mode
of life as one’s own practical determination.

**`śeṣāvijñapti`**

The remaining avijñapti is what remains after the two standing forms already
analyzed:

```text
saṃvara
    restraint

asaṃvara
    non-restraint

saṃvarāsaṃvaretara avijñapti
    avijñapti other than restraint and non-restraint
```

It covers persistent karmic determinations generated by particular wholesome
or unwholesome acts without constituting either complete restraint or complete
non-restraint.

**`kṣetra`**

A field is a recipient or relational basis possessing special karmic
efficacy. The Bhāṣya’s example is giving a monastery or similar material
support among `aupadhika puṇyakriyāvastu`, materially based meritorious deeds.

**`ādāna` as undertaking**

The prose explains this member through `samādānam ādatte`, taking up an
undertaking. Examples include resolving not to eat before honoring the Buddha
or promising regular offerings on appointed days.

**`ādara` and `īhā`**

The Bhāṣya joins these functionally:

```text
ādareṇa tadrūpeṇa
kriyām īhate
```

> One pursues a wholesome or unwholesome action with such earnestness [that
> avijñapti arises].

They should not be forced into two wholly independent acquisition routes.

## 4. Grammar

**Two acquisitions of non-restraint**

```text
asaṃvarasya lābhaḥ
    kriyayā
    abhyupagamena vā
```

> Non-restraint is acquired through action or through deliberate adoption.

The instrumental phrases give alternative means of acquisition. The Bhāṣya
correlates them with different practical situations: enacted occupational
activity within a lineage and adoption of that livelihood by someone outside
it.

**Acquisition of remaining avijñapti**

```text
śeṣa-avijñapti-lābhaḥ tu
    kṣetrāt
    samādānāt
    ādareṇa īhanāt
```

> The remaining avijñapti, however, is acquired through an efficacious field,
> through undertaking, or through sufficiently earnest performance.

The Bhāṣya’s alternatives—`kṣetraṃ vā`, `athavā samādānam`, and then earnest
performance—are syntactically decisive. A mechanically segmented four-item
list would obscure the relation between earnestness and striving.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Non-restraint is acquired through action or through deliberate adoption.
> The remaining avijñapti, however, is acquired through a field, undertaking,
> or earnest striving.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Non-restraint is acquired either by performing the harmful occupational
> activity or by deliberately adopting that livelihood as one’s own. Other
> avijñapti—neither complete restraint nor complete non-restraint—arises
> through relation to a specially efficacious field, through taking up a
> continuing practice, or through pursuing a wholesome or unwholesome action
> with sufficient earnestness and effort.

The occupational distinction, the material-support example, and the joining
of earnestness with performance are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A persistent practical form can arise in more than one direction. Repeated
> life-activity may generate a standing non-restraint, while deliberate
> adoption of a harmful livelihood may institute that standing form in
> advance of its future enactments. Particular actions outside complete
> restraint and non-restraint generate persistence only under determinate
> conditions: the relational efficacy of their field, an undertaking that
> gives conduct continuity, or an earnestness that makes the action more than
> an indifferent occurrence.

“Repeated” here describes the occupational horizon, not an additional
condition stated by the kārikā. The Bhāṣya’s immediate acquisition example is
performance of the relevant operation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Action-based acquisition**

```text
occupational lineage
    + performance of the killing-operation
    → non-restraint acquired
```

The lineage supplies the practical context. The deed supplies the stated
acquisition condition.

**Livelihood-based undertaking**

```text
not born into the lineage
    + “I shall live by this livelihood”
    → non-restraint acquired
```

This is stronger than intending one isolated harmful act. The object of the
undertaking is a continuing mode of subsistence and action.

**`jīvikā`**

`Jīvikā` is livelihood. Its appearance makes the relation to Right Livelihood
concrete: a livelihood is not merely income classified from outside but an
organized practical commitment capable of generating a standing karmic form.

**The specially qualified field**

```text
material gift
    + karmically efficacious recipient-field
    → meritorious avijñapti
```

The field is not a magical container. It names the relational determination
through which the same general class of visible transfer can possess a
different karmic efficacy.

**`aupadhika puṇyakriyāvastu`**

This is a basis of meritorious action involving material support. Donation of
a monastery exemplifies how the object-field can be sufficient for the
arising of avijñapti from the giving.

**Undertaken practices**

The Bhāṣya’s examples have a recurring temporal structure:

```text
present undertaking
    → rule for future occasions
    → continuing practical determination
```

The undertaking may concern homage before eating or regular offerings on
lunar, monthly, or fortnightly occasions.

**Earnest wholesome or unwholesome action**

`Ādara` is ethically neutral with respect to the valence of the action. Both
wholesome and unwholesome actions may be pursued with a seriousness capable
of generating avijñapti.

```text
ethical quality
    kuśala or akuśala

degree of engagement
    sufficient earnestness
```

These are distinct determinations.

**Structural boundary**

The Bhāṣya closes:

```text
acquisition explained for
    restraint
    non-restraint
    other avijñapti

next topic
    abandonment
```

VAK 4.37 is therefore the end of the acquisition block, not merely another
example within it.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Two directions of genesis**

```text
actual performance
    → standing harmful form

adopted standing livelihood
    → future corresponding performance
```

The directions are reciprocal but not identical. One begins with enacted
activity; the other begins with a determination of the kind of life to be
lived.

**Three routes for other avijñapti**

```text
qualified relation
    efficacious field

temporal institution
    deliberate undertaking

qualitative actualization
    earnest performance
```

The visible event alone does not exhaust the conditions under which a
persistent karmic determination arises.

**Event and generated form**

```text
same visible class of action
    + different field
    + different undertaking
    + different earnestness
    → different persistence-result
```

The verse discriminates genesis rather than inferring persistence from motion
alone.

**The middle category**

```text
restraint
    standing abstentional form

non-restraint
    standing harmful form

remaining avijñapti
    persistent determination from particular action
    without either complete standing system
```

This prevents the dyad of restraint and non-restraint from becoming an
exhaustive opposition.

**Acquisition is typed by its cause**

```text
mode of acquisition
    determines
kind and scope of persistent form
```

The causes are not interchangeable labels. Occupational action, livelihood
adoption, field, undertaken recurrence, and earnest action each specify a
different genetic relation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**How activity becomes form**

VAK 4.37 asks the central genetic question directly:

```text
under what conditions does an occurrence
become a continuing practical determination?
```

Its answer is plural. There is no single occult mechanism called avijñapti
that replaces the articulated genesis. Vasubandhu specifies performance,
adoption, field, undertaking, and earnestness as distinct pathways.

**Livelihood as instituted agency**

The declaration “we shall live by this livelihood” is especially revealing.
The person does not merely predict future actions but determines a practical
identity whose ordinary actuality will consist in those actions:

```text
livelihood adopted
    → field of expected activity organized
    → non-restraint stands as practical form
```

This brings Right Livelihood into the Karma analysis without requiring the
verse to name the Eightfold Path explicitly.

**The field is relational efficacy**

`Kṣetra` prevents an act from being analyzed as an isolated subject–predicate
unit. Giving is determined through the recipient-field in which it occurs.
The field does not act as a transcendent hidden object; it is an explicit
relation contributing to the act’s karmic efficacy.

**Earnestness as qualitative actuality**

Two actions with similar visible outlines need not possess the same practical
depth:

```text
episodic occurrence
    may cease with the event

earnestly pursued action
    may generate a continuing determination
```

This does not make intensity a private metaphysical substance. It is one
Aspect of the complete action through which its persistence-result is
determined.

**Acquisition block completed**

The movement from VAK 4.26 through 4.37 can now be seen as an extended genesis
of practical forms:

```text
modes of acquiring restraint
    → duration and possession
    → scope by beings, times, limbs, and causes
    → modes of acquiring non-restraint and other avijñapti
```

VAK 4.38 will reverse the question: not how the standing determination
arises, but how it is abandoned.

This is the Organon contact. The occupational cases, three routes for other
avijñapti, and explicit transition to abandonment belong to the Bhāṣya.
