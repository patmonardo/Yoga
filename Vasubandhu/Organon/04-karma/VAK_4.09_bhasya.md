# VAK_4.09 Bhāṣya — Four Grounds of Ethical Classification

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> saṃprayogeṇa tadyuktāḥ samutthānāt kriyādayaḥ /
> viparyayeṇākuśalaṃ paramāvyākṛte dhruve // 4.09 //

> Dharmas associated with the wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya` are
> wholesome through association. Bodily and verbal actions and related
> products are wholesome through originating from such wholesome Dharmas,
> although the Bhāṣya leaves a difficulty about certain acquisitions
> unresolved. The unwholesome follows the contrary pattern: saṃsāra is
> unwholesome ultimately; unwholesome roots and factors are unwholesome
> intrinsically; associated Dharmas and their products are unwholesome through
> association and origination. Relative to karmic maturation, contaminated
> Dharmas may be wholesome or indeterminate. Space and non-analytical
> cessation are the two unconditioned Dharmas that are indeterminate without
> qualification.

“Those factors” refers to the wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya` in
VAK 4.08. The kārikā study is `VAK_4.09.md`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Those associated with them are wholesome through association.” Dharmas
associated with the wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya` are wholesome
through association: without association with those factors they would not
be wholesome. They are like water mixed with medicine.

“Actions and the rest are wholesome through origination.” Bodily and verbal
actions and formations dissociated from consciousness—including arising and
related determinations, acquisition, the attainment of cessation, and the
attainment of non-perception—are wholesome through being produced by Dharmas
associated with those wholesome roots and related factors. They are compared
to a wet-nurse's milk produced after drinking a medicinal preparation.

The Bhāṣya then asks how acquisitions produced by a consciousness of a
different ethical class can be wholesome. It gives compressed examples:
reconnection with wholesome roots through doubt and acquisition connected
with return to or loss of a realm. The difficulty is left for explanation.

As wholesomeness has been explained, “the unwholesome is determined
conversely.” Saṃsāra is unwholesome ultimately because it consists in the
continuation of all suffering and is the opposite of supreme security. The
unwholesome roots, shamelessness, and lack of moral concern are unwholesome
intrinsically. Dharmas associated with them are unwholesome through
association; bodily and verbal actions and related products are unwholesome
through origination. The comparisons include illness and harmful medicine.

An objection follows: since contaminated Dharmas belong to saṃsāra, would
none of them be wholesome or indeterminate? The reply is that saṃsāra was
called unwholesome ultimately. Relative to karmic maturation, a contaminated
Dharma not classified with respect to maturation is called indeterminate;
one with a desirable maturation is called wholesome. If what is ultimately
indeterminate is sought, the two permanent [Dharmas] are named: space and
non-analytical cessation, the two unconditioned Dharmas that are
unobscured-indeterminate without qualification.

The damaged `kukśalam` is interpreted as `kuśalam` from the syntax and
contrast with desirable maturation. This does not establish that every
indeterminate Dharma is incapable of being a maturation-result.

## 4. Movement and Voices

The Bhāṣya completes the four grounds introduced in VAK 4.08. It explains
wholesomeness through association and origination, raises a complication
about acquisitions, and then applies the converse arrangement to
unwholesomeness. An objection asks whether calling saṃsāra ultimately
unwholesome erases the working categories of wholesome and indeterminate
contaminated Dharmas. The reply separates ultimate evaluation from
classification relative to karmic maturation and identifies two unconditioned
Dharmas as indeterminate without qualification.

The objection beginning `evaṃ tarhi` is not Vasubandhu's conclusion. The
question about `prāpti` is expressly left open. The study preserves both
limits rather than forcing a resolution.

## 5. Association and Origination

`Saṃprayoga` and `samutthāna` classify different relations. Associated
Dharmas are wholesome because they are conjoined with intrinsically
wholesome roots and factors. Bodily and verbal actions and other products
are wholesome because they arise from such an associated complex. The
medicine-mixed water and medicated-milk comparisons illustrate the
difference between association and production.

The `prāpti` question limits the simple originating-condition rule: an
acquisition may be produced by a consciousness of a different ethical
class. The Bhāṣya gives examples but leaves the question to be explained.

## 6. The Converse and the Objection

The four grounds recur for the unwholesome. Saṃsāra is unwholesome
ultimately; unwholesome roots and factors are unwholesome intrinsically;
associated Dharmas are unwholesome through association; and actions and
related products are unwholesome through origination.

The objection exposes an apparent collapse: if all contaminated existence
belongs to saṃsāra, how can contaminated Dharmas still be wholesome or
indeterminate? The reply changes the criterion. Ultimate evaluation of
saṃsāra and ethical classification relative to `vipāka`, karmic maturation,
answer different questions and do not cancel one another.

## 7. The Two Ultimately Indeterminate Dharmas

The dual `paramāvyākṛte dhruve` is resolved as `ākāśa` and
`apratisaṃkhyānirodha`: space and non-analytical cessation. Both are
unconditioned and, in the Bhāṣya's wording, unobscured-indeterminate without
qualification. This is distinct from the use of `avyākṛta` for contaminated
Dharmas classified relative to karmic maturation.

## 8. Translation and Textual Limits

“By association,” “by originating condition,” and “conversely” preserve the
distinct relations in the verse. The witness's `kukśalam` is treated as a
damaged form of `kuśalam`, but remains visible in the Sanskrit block. The
compressed list of acquisitions and attainments is segmented editorially,
with the Chapter 2 classification noted above. Neither the scope of the
`prāpti` examples nor every detail of their causal relation is settled here.

## 9. Interpretation

The Bhāṣya distinguishes four grounds of ethical classification and applies
them to both wholesome and unwholesome Dharmas. Its qualification concerning
`vipāka` prevents ultimate evaluation of saṃsāra from erasing ethical
distinctions among contaminated Dharmas. Its account of certain `prāptis`
remains an explicit open question.

In the Kośa's broader system, Discriminative Cognition (*vijñāna*) governs
Mind (*manas*) and guides Dharma Base; it remains distinct from
consciousness (*citta*) in the causal discussions. The same `avijñapti` is
classified in Form Base and Dharma Base, with *vijñāna* bearing the stated
*prati* relation to it.
