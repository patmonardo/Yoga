# VAK_4.09 Bhāṣya — Four Grounds of Ethical Determination

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> saṃprayogeṇa tadyuktāḥ samutthānāt kriyādayaḥ /
> viparyayeṇākuśalaṃ paramāvyākṛte dhruve // 4.09 //

> Dharmas conjoined with those factors are wholesome by association;
> actions and the rest are wholesome by origination. The unwholesome is
> determined conversely. Two permanent dharmas are ultimately
> indeterminate.

“Those factors” refers to the wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya`
in 4.08. The Kārikā study is `VAK_4.09.md`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Those conjoined with them are wholesome through association.” Dharmas
associated with the wholesome roots, moral shame, and moral concern are
wholesome through association: without association with those factors,
they would not be wholesome. They are like water mixed with medicine.

“Actions and the rest are wholesome through origination.” Bodily and
verbal actions, together with formations dissociated from mind—arising
and similar factors, acquisition, the attainment of cessation, and the
attainment of non-perception—are wholesome through being produced by dharmas
associated with those wholesome roots and other factors. They are like
the milk of a nurse affected by a medicinal drink. Yet one must ask how
acquisitions produced by a mind of a different class are
wholesome: examples concern the reconnection of wholesome roots amid
doubt and the return or loss of a level. The text leaves this difficulty
for explanation.

As wholesomeness has been explained, “the unwholesome is determined
conversely.” In what way? Saṃsāra is unwholesome in the ultimate sense
because it consists in the continuation of all suffering and is the
opposite of supreme security. The unwholesome roots, shamelessness, and
lack of moral concern are unwholesome by nature. Dharmas associated
with them are unwholesome by association. Bodily and verbal action,
arising and similar formations, and acquisitions produced by them are
unwholesome by origination. Illness, harmful medicine, and related
examples supply the comparison.

But then, since everything contaminated is within saṃsāra, nothing
contaminated would be indeterminate or wholesome. The reply is that
saṃsāra was called unwholesome in the ultimate sense. Relative to
karmic maturation, a contaminated dharma for which no maturation is
specified is called indeterminate, whereas one with a desirable
maturation is called wholesome. If one seeks what is
indeterminate in the ultimate sense, “the two permanent [dharmas] are
ultimately indeterminate”: the two unconditioned dharmas, space and
non-analytical cessation, are unobscured-indeterminate without
qualification.

The line about `prāpti` and its examples is difficult in the current
witness and is translated provisionally. “For which no maturation is
specified” renders `vipākaṃ tu prati ... na vyākriyate`; it does not
assert that every indeterminate dharma is incapable of being a result
of maturation. Its precise scope requires comparison with a critical
witness.

## 4. Movement and Voices

The Bhāṣya completes the fourfold account opened in 4.08. It explains
goodness through association and origination, registers a difficulty
about acquisitions, then reverses all four grounds for the
unwholesome. An objection asks whether calling saṃsāra ultimately
unwholesome abolishes the working categories of wholesome and
indeterminate contaminated dharmas. The answer separates ultimate
predication from classification relative to karmic maturation and
names two ultimately indeterminate unconditioned dharmas.

The objection beginning `evaṃ tarhi` is an argumentative voice, not
Vasubandhu's conclusion. The question about `prāpti` is explicitly
left open; the study should not force a solution into this unit.

## 5. Association and Origination

`Saṃprayoga` and `samutthāna` determine goodness through different
relations. Associated mental dharmas are wholesome because they are
conjoined with intrinsically wholesome factors. Bodily and verbal
actions and other products are wholesome because they arise from
such a complex. The medicine-water and medicated-milk examples
distinguish association from causal production.

The `prāpti` question matters precisely because an acquisition may be
produced by a mind of another kind. The simple rule “good producer,
good product” cannot be applied without qualification in every case.
The Bhāṣya marks that limit rather than hiding it.

## 6. The Converse Account and Its Objection

The four grounds recur for the unwholesome. Saṃsāra is called
ultimately unwholesome; unwholesome roots, shamelessness, and lack of
moral concern are unwholesome by nature; associated dharmas inherit
the classification by association; produced actions and formations
do so by origination.

The objection exposes an apparent collapse: if all conditioned life
belongs to saṃsāra, why retain wholesome or indeterminate contaminated
dharmas? The reply changes the criterion. Ultimate assessment of
saṃsāra and classification by `vipāka`, karmic maturation, answer
different questions. Neither cancels the other.

## 7. The Two Ultimately Indeterminate Dharmas

The verse's dual `paramāvyākṛte dhruve` is resolved as `ākāśa` and
`apratisaṃkhyānirodha`: space and non-analytical cessation. Both are
unconditioned and, in the source's terminology, unobscured-indeterminate
without qualification. This is narrower than the ordinary use of
`avyākṛta` for contaminated dharmas classified relative to maturation.

## 8. Translation and Doctrinal Limits

“Ultimately” translates `paramārthataḥ` within this ethical argument;
“by nature,” “by association,” and “by origination” preserve its
distinct grounds of predication. The text does not use one flat sense
of `kuśala` or `akuśala` in every clause. The damaged `kukśalam` is
understood as `kuśalam` from syntax and the desired-maturation
contrast, while remaining visible in the Sanskrit block.

The compressed list names the conditioned `nirodhasamāpatti`, the
attainment of cessation, alongside `asaṃjñisamāpatti`; it does not
introduce an independent `nirodha` here. The closing
`apratisaṃkhyānirodha` is the distinct unconditioned dharma. This
segmentation is editorial and is supported by the Chapter 2 treatment
of the two attainments.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

**Textual determination.** The ethical predicate belongs through a
specified relation: ultimate status, intrinsic nature, association,
or origination. The same term can therefore have a different ground
without its distinctions disappearing. The qualification concerning
`prāpti` and the contrast with `vipāka` keep the scheme responsive to
hard cases.

**Organon reconstruction.** LOKA:KARMA can use this as a disciplined
grammar of attribution. A graph of relations might display intrinsic
factors, associated factors, and produced actions, but the graph's
edges would need their own warrants. The Bhāṣya provides these as
doctrinal relations, not as an invitation to call all association or
causation the same sort of mediation.
