# VAK_4.97 — Locus of the Three Obstructions

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
त्रिषु द्वीपेष्वानन्तर्यं षण्डादीनां तु नेष्यते ।
अल्पोपकारालज्जित्वाच्छेषे गतिषु पञ्चसु ॥ ४.९७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
triṣu dvīpeṣv ānantaryaṃ śaṇḍhādīnāṃ tu neṣyate /
alpopakārālajjitvāc cheṣe gatiṣu pañcasu // 4.97 //
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `triṣu` | locative masculine plural | in three |
| `dvīpeṣu` | locative masculine plural | continents; specified in the Bhāṣya |
| `ānantaryam` | nominative neuter singular | the technical action-class discussed in 4.96 |
| `śaṇḍha-ādīnām` | genitive plural compound | for persons classified as *śaṇḍha* and related cases in the source |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but |
| `na iṣyate` | passive, third-person singular, with negation | is not accepted |
| `alpa-upakārāt` | ablative singular | because of lesser benefit or beneficence |
| `alajjitvāt` | ablative singular abstract | because of absence of the relevant inhibition |
| `śeṣe` | nominative neuter dual, with `āvaraṇe` understood | the remaining two obstructions |
| `gatiṣu` | locative feminine plural | in the destinies |
| `pañcasu` | locative feminine plural | five |

The Bhāṣya supplies the omitted positive predicate in the first pāda and
identifies the three continents. In the second pāda, `śeṣe` refers to the
two remaining obstructions from 4.96, affliction-obstruction and
maturation-obstruction; it does not refer to other actions.

## 4. Grammar

**Locus of the *ānantarya* action-class**

```text
triṣu dvīpeṣu
    ānantaryam [iṣyate]

śaṇḍha-ādīnām tu
    [ānantaryam] na iṣyate
```

> The *ānantarya* action-class is accepted in the three continents, but not
> for persons classified as *śaṇḍha* and related cases.

The affirmative predicate is supplied from the Bhāṣya's explanation; the
verse states its contrastive exclusion explicitly.

**Reasons for the specified exclusion**

```text
alpa-upakārāt
alajjitvāt
```

> Because of lesser beneficence and absence of the relevant inhibition.

The Bhāṣya distributes the reasons respectively: lesser parental
beneficence, and the persons' lack of the specified inhibition toward their
parents. This is its historically situated rationale.

**The remaining obstruction-types**

```text
śeṣe [āvaraṇe]
    gatiṣu pañcasu [iṣyete]
```

> The remaining two obstructions are present in the five destinies.

The commentary identifies these as affliction-obstruction and
maturation-obstruction. It names the Kuru condition among humans and
non-percipient beings among gods as instances of the latter.

## 5. Translation

### Literal

> The *ānantarya* action-class is accepted in the three continents, but not
> for persons classified as *śaṇḍha* and related cases, because of lesser
> beneficence and absence of the relevant inhibition. The remaining two
> obstructions are found in the five destinies.

### Bhāṣya-informed

> Among these obstructions, where is each to be understood? The actions of
> *ānantarya* are admitted in the three continents—not in Uttarakuru, the
> other destinies, or another Principle; even there, only for women and men.
> They are not admitted for persons classified in the text as *śaṇḍha* and
> related cases. The Bhāṣya recalls its earlier explanation concerning
> non-restraint and adds lesser parental beneficence and lesser inhibition
> toward parents, assigning these reasons respectively to the parents and
> the persons. It attributes the claimed difference in beneficence to
> embodied condition and parental affection. It extends the exclusion to
> animals and pretas, while Bhadanta allows a possible case among those of
> exceptional intelligence, such as a well-bred horse. A human who kills
> nonhuman parents is also said not to incur *ānantarya*. The Bhāṣya then
> clarifies that the “remaining” two are affliction-obstruction and
> maturation-obstruction, which occur across the five destinies; it
> identifies the Kuru condition among humans and non-percipient beings
> among gods as instances of the latter.

The agent restrictions and their rationale are the Bhāṣya's classifications,
not general empirical claims. The source's category *śaṇḍha* is retained
without equating it with any present-day identity.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The *ānantarya* action-class is accepted in the three continents, but not for persons classified as *śaṇḍha* and related cases, because of lesser beneficence and absence of the relevant inhibition. The remaining two obstructions are found in the five destinies.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

- **Three continents** (*triṣu dvīpeṣu*) — Jambudvīpa, Pūrvavideha, and
  Aparagodānīya; Uttarakuru is explicitly excluded from the *ānantarya*
  locus in the Bhāṣya.
- **Action of *ānantarya*** — the technical action-class introduced in
  4.96; this verse specifies its locus and disputed agent restrictions.
- ***Śaṇḍha* and related cases** (*śaṇḍhādi*) — a historical classification
  in the source. Its membership is not mapped onto present-day categories
  here.
- **Beneficence** (*upakāra*) — in the Bhāṣya, parental benefit attributed
  to the relation under discussion.
- **Inhibition** (*alajjitva*, explained with *apatrāpya*) — the inhibition
  toward parents that the Bhāṣya says is relevant to the classification.
- **Remaining two** (*śeṣe*) — affliction-obstruction and
  maturation-obstruction, not additional action-types.
- **Five destinies** (*pañca gati*) — the scope assigned by the Bhāṣya to
  the two remaining obstructions; maturation-obstruction is illustrated by
  the Kuru condition among humans and non-percipient beings among gods.
- **Bhadanta** — the authority cited for a possible *ānantarya* case among
  exceptionally intelligent animals; this is a reported qualification.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let `A` be the action-obstruction of 4.96, `K` affliction-obstruction, and
`V` maturation-obstruction.

| Obstruction | Distribution stated in 4.97 |
|---|---|
| `A` | *ānantarya* is admitted in three continents, with the Bhāṣya's further restrictions and reported qualification |
| `K` | one of the remaining two; included across the five destinies |
| `V` | one of the remaining two; included across the five destinies, with Kuru and non-percipient instances specified |

The text's primary restriction is:

```text
ānantarya
    → three continents
    → not Uttarakuru, other destinies, or another Principle
    → within those continents, further agent conditions are specified
```

The final phrase resolves as:

```text
śeṣe
    = kleśāvaraṇa + vipākāvaraṇa
    ≠ other ānantarya actions
```

The Bhāṣya records a disputed exception for exceptionally intelligent
animals and separately excludes a human killing nonhuman parents. These
qualifications remain attributed positions, not universalized rules.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The verse clarifies the scope of the three-obstruction scheme rather than
adding another action-type. Its compressed `śeṣe` depends on the structure
of 4.96: the two obstructions remaining after action-obstruction are the
afflictive and maturation forms. The commentary's historical agent
classification is ethically difficult; this study preserves its argument
and attributions while refusing to project its categories onto present-day
persons.

### Former Philosophical Translation

> The technical classification of *ānantarya* is restricted by the source
> to specified world-loci and agent conditions. The visible description of
> an act is therefore not enough, within this system, to establish that
> classification. The verse then distinguishes this restricted action-class
> from affliction-obstruction and maturation-obstruction, whose scope
> extends across the five destinies.
