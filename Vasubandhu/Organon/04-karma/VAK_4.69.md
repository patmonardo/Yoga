# VAK_4.69

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तदनन्तरसम्भूतेरभिध्याद्यास्त्रिमूलजाः ।
कुशलाः सप्रयोगान्ता अलोभद्वेषमोहजाः ॥ ४.६९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

**The mental courses**

`Abhidhyā-ādyāḥ` comprises:

```text
abhidhyā          covetousness
vyāpāda           hostility
mithyādṛṣṭi       false view
```

These determinate mental courses must remain distinct from `lobha`, `dveṣa`,
and `moha`, the general unwholesome roots.

**The wholesome roots**

The transmitted compound lacks a repeated written privative before every
member, but the Bhāṣya confirms the required analysis:

```text
alobha    non-greed
adveṣa    non-hatred
amoha     non-delusion
```

## 4. Grammar

**Immediate antecedence**

```text
tat-anantara-saṃbhūteḥ
    abhidhyā-ādyāḥ
    tri-mūla-jāḥ [bhavanti]
```

> Because they arise immediately after those roots, covetousness and the
> other mental courses are born from the three roots.

The ablative compound gives the reason for the causal designation. The
Bhāṣya distributes the rule:

```text
lobhāt anantaram utpannāḥ
    lobha-jāḥ

dveṣāt anantaram utpannāḥ
    dveṣa-jāḥ

mohāt anantaram utpannāḥ
    moha-jāḥ
```

What arises immediately after greed is called greed-born; what arises after
hatred, hatred-born; what arises after delusion, delusion-born.

**The wholesome process**

```text
kuśalāḥ
    sa-prayoga-antāḥ
    alobha-adveṣa-amoha-jāḥ [bhavanti]
```

> The wholesome courses, together with preparation and concluding phase, are
> born from non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion.

The Bhāṣya glosses `sa-prayogāntāḥ` as `sa-prayoga-pṛṣṭhāḥ`, fixing `anta`
here as the aftermath rather than merely an abstract ending.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Because they arise immediately after those roots, covetousness and the
> others are born from the three roots. The wholesome courses, together with
> preparation and aftermath, are born from non-greed, non-hatred, and
> non-delusion.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Covetousness, hostility, and false view are designated greed-born,
> hatred-born, or delusion-born according to the unwholesome root immediately
> preceding their occurrence. Wholesome karmic courses, including their
> preparation and aftermath, arise from a wholesome mind, which is necessarily
> associated with non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion. Their preparatory,
> principal, and subsequent phases are abstentions from the corresponding
> phases of an unwholesome course.

The rule of immediate antecedence, the wholesome-mind explanation, and the
threefold abstention analysis are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A root is not yet a determinate course of action. On the unwholesome side,
> the root becomes causally legible through the mental course that immediately
> follows it. On the wholesome side, non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion
> are necessarily conjoined with the wholesome mind from which the entire
> practical process—preparation, principal restraint, and aftermath—arises.
> Ethical determination therefore proceeds from ground through a generated
> mind into an articulated course.

Wholesomeness here is not merely outward behavioral conformity. Its process
must be generated by a wholesome consciousness.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Tad-anantara-saṃbhūti` — arising immediately after that**

A rule of proximate causal classification. The relevant `tat` is the root
after which the mental course immediately arises, not the preparatory phase of
VAK 4.68.

**`Abhidhyā` — covetousness**

A determinate appropriative fixation upon what belongs to another. It is a
mental karmapatha, more specific than greed as a general root.

**`Vyāpāda` — hostility**

The determinate wish that another be harmed. It is more specific than hatred
or aversion as a general root.

**`Mithyādṛṣṭi` — false view**

A determinate denial or distortion of the morally causal order. It is more
specific than delusion as a general root.

**`Mūla` — root**

The generative ethical ground from which a course arises. VAK 4.69 does not
identify root and course; it states the causal relation by which the course is
classified as root-born.

**`Alobha`, `adveṣa`, and `amoha`**

The three wholesome roots:

```text
alobha
    non-greed; non-appropriative orientation

adveṣa
    non-hatred; non-hostile orientation

amoha
    non-delusion; discriminative clarity
```

`Amoha` is not merely a blank absence of confusion. In the Abhidharma
classification it is a positive wholesome root associated with discerning
consciousness.

**`Kuśala-citta-samutthita` — arisen from a wholesome mind**

The Bhāṣya's genetic explanation of wholesome karmapathas. The wholesome mind
is necessarily associated (`saṃprayukta`) with the three wholesome roots.

**`Virati` — abstention**

The Bhāṣya determines the wholesome course by abstention from the corresponding
unwholesome process:

```text
abstention from unwholesome preparation
    = wholesome preparation

abstention from the principal unwholesome course
    = principal wholesome course

abstention from unwholesome aftermath
    = wholesome aftermath
```

## 8. Logical Determination

**Root and course**

The following identities are rejected:

```text
lobha = abhidhyā
dveṣa = vyāpāda
moha = mithyādṛṣṭi
```

The relation is genetic:

```text
general root
    → immediate causal antecedence
    → determinate mental course
    → root-born classification
```

This allows a mental course to be classified by the root immediately preceding
it without reducing its specific content to that root.

**Two causal relations**

```text
UNWHOLESOME MENTAL COURSE
    immediately antecedent root
        → determines root-born designation

WHOLESOME KARMIC PROCESS
    wholesome mind necessarily associated
    with alobha + adveṣa + amoha
        → generates preparation, principal course, and aftermath
```

Proximate antecedence and necessary association are different explanatory
relations and must remain separately represented.

**Wholesome reversal through all three phases**

```text
unwholesome process          wholesome process

prayoga             →       abstention in preparation
maula               →       principal restraint
pṛṣṭha              →       continued restraint
```

The reversal is not applied only to the principal act. It transforms the
whole temporally articulated process.

**Bhāṣya example: novice ordination**

```text
entering the residence
venerating the community
requesting a preceptor
first and second formal acts
    = wholesome preparation

third formal act
vijñapti + momentary avijñapti
    = principal wholesome course

subsequent declarations
continuing avijñapti
    = wholesome aftermath
```

This confirms that the three-phase form belongs to wholesome karmic
determination as well as wrongdoing.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Ground becomes operative through process**

The root does not stand beside the course as an isolated explanatory label.
Its Meaning is exhibited through a genetic order:

```text
ethical root
    → quality of consciousness
    → practical preparation
    → principal determination
    → persistent aftermath
```

For the wholesome course this order is explicitly supplied by the Bhāṣya:

```text
alobha / adveṣa / amoha
        ↓ necessary association
kuśala-citta
        ↓ generation
prayoga → maula → pṛṣṭha
```

**Abstention is positive practical determination**

`Virati` must not be flattened into mere non-occurrence. Entering discipline,
formally undertaking it, reaching principal restraint, and sustaining it are
positive moments of one wholesome process. The absence of wrongdoing becomes
karmically determinate through its wholesome ground and enacted form.

**Behavior and ethical genesis**

The same outward non-performance could arise from incapacity, fear,
calculation, or wholesome restraint. The present account determines the
wholesome course through its generative consciousness, not through visible
conformity alone.

This is the Organon contact. Immediate causal classification, necessary
association with wholesome roots, abstention across the three phases, and the
ordination example belong to the Bhāṣya; the account of their unity as the
genesis of Practical Form is the project synthesis.
