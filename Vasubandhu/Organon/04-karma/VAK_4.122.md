# VAK 4.122 — Misconduct, Abstention, and Purified Discipline

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दौःशील्यमशुभं रूपं शीलं तद्विरतिर्द्विधा ।
प्रतिक्षिप्ताच्च बुद्धेन विशुद्धं तु चतुर्गुणम् ॥ ४.१२२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
dauḥśīlyam aśubhaṃ rūpaṃ śīlaṃ tadviratir dvidhā /
pratikṣiptāc ca buddhena viśuddhaṃ tu caturguṇam // 4.122 //
```

The Bhāṣya reads the two modes of abstention as `vijñapti` and
`avijñapti`, then explains the four qualities of purified discipline by
quoting 4.123a.

The Bhāṣya specifies the two modes as `vijñapti` and `avijñapti`.
It extends the object of abstention beyond misconduct by nature to
conduct prohibited for one who has undertaken the training.

## 4. Grammar

The first line defines misconduct and discipline:

```text
dauḥśīlyam = aśubhaṃ rūpam
śīlam = tad-viratiḥ
```

> Misconduct is unwholesome Form; discipline is abstention from it.

The abstention is twofold:

```text
viratiḥ
    dvidhā = vijñapti + avijñapti
```

The second line extends discipline to abstention from what the Buddha
prohibited. This is a different distinction from the two modes: the
twofold division concerns how abstention is classified, while the
prohibition determines another object of abstention.

`Viśuddhaṃ tu caturguṇam` introduces purified discipline, whose four
qualities the Bhāṣya supplies by quoting the first half of 4.123.

## 5. Translation

### Scientific English Rendering

> Misconduct is unwholesome Form; discipline is abstention from it in two
> modes, and also abstention from what the Buddha has prohibited. Purified
> discipline, however, has four qualities.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Misconduct is unwholesome Form; discipline is abstention from it in two
> modes, and also abstention from what the Buddha has prohibited. Purified
> discipline, however, has four qualities. The two modes are manifestation,
> through which one refrains, and non-disclosing continuity, which is that
> abstention. The same twofold abstention applies to what is not misconduct
> by nature but has been prohibited by the Buddha, such as eating at an
> improper time; for one who has undertaken the training, acting contrary
> to that rule becomes misconduct. The four qualities are that discipline
> is unharmed by misconduct and its causes, rests on its counteragent, and
> is directed toward peace and nirvāṇa rather than a particular rebirth.

The two modes, the training qualification, the example of improper-time
eating, and the four qualities are explained in the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Discipline is not mere non-occurrence. It is an abstention with two
> distinguishable determinations: its manifestation and the
> non-disclosing continuity that is the abstention itself. Its scope also
> depends on the training undertaken: some conduct is unwholesome by
> nature, while other conduct becomes misconduct through violating a
> received prohibition. Purified discipline further requires freedom
> from misconduct and its causes, support from its counteragent, and
> orientation toward nirvāṇa.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

- **`Dauḥśīlya`** — misconduct, identified as unwholesome Form.
- **`Śīla`** — discipline, defined here as abstention.
- **`Virati`** — abstention or restraint; in this passage it has two
  modes.
- **`Vijñapti`** — manifestation or disclosure; here, the mode through
  which abstention is enacted.
- **`Avijñapti`** — non-disclosing continuity; the Bhāṣya identifies
  this with the abstention itself. Keep it distinct from `vijñapti`.
- **`Prakṛtyā dauḥśīlya`** — misconduct by nature, distinguished from
  a rule made binding through an undertaken training.
- **`Buddhena pratikṣipta`** — prohibited by the Buddha; the example is
  eating at an improper time.
- **`Samāttaśikṣa`** — one who has undertaken the training; violation
  makes the prohibited conduct misconduct for that person.
- **`Smṛtyupasthāna`** — establishment of mindfulness, named as support
  for purified discipline through its relation to the counteragent.
- **`Anāsravaśīla`** — discipline without influx, named among another
  teacher's four kinds of discipline.
- **`Nirvāṇapariṇāmita`** — directed toward nirvāṇa.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya distinguishes object from mode:

```text
Objects of abstention:
    misconduct by nature
    conduct prohibited within an undertaken training

Modes of abstention:
    vijñapti
    avijñapti
```

The four qualities of purified discipline, supplied through 4.123a, are:

```text
not harmed by misconduct
not harmed by its causes, such as greed and other afflictions
resting on the counteragent, through establishments of mindfulness
resting on peace and directed toward nirvāṇa, not a particular rebirth
```

These four qualities are not the same classification as the four kinds
of discipline listed by other teachers later in the Bhāṣya.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya closes the giving-based basis of meritorious action and turns
to discipline. It treats abstention as an action-form with a manifest
mode and a non-disclosing continuity; it does not reduce discipline to
an empty gap in behavior. At the same time, `avijñapti` remains distinct
from `vijñapti`, as required by the Dhātu dyad, rather than becoming a
second name for the same manifestation.

The distinction between misconduct by nature and a prohibited training
rule also matters: the same behavior can have different status depending
on whether the agent has undertaken that training. The later alternatives
are attributed to other teachers, not silently combined with the
Bhāṣya's four qualities of purified discipline.
