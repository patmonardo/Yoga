# VAK_4.09 — Four Grounds of Ethical Classification

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> संप्रयोगेण तद्युक्ताः समुत्थानात् क्रियादयः ।
>
> विपर्ययेणाकुशलं परमाव्याकृते ध्रुवे ॥ ४.९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> saṃprayogeṇa tadyuktāḥ samutthānāt kriyādayaḥ /
>
> viparyayeṇākuśalaṃ paramāvyākṛte dhruve // 4.9 //

VAK 4.08 introduced four grounds of ethical classification. VAK 4.09
completes the account: it explains wholesomeness and unwholesomeness through
association and originating condition, then distinguishes ultimate
classification from classification relative to karmic maturation.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
saṃprayogeṇa tad-yuktāḥ /
samutthānāt kriyā-ādayaḥ /
viparyayeṇa akuśalam /
parama-avyākṛte dhruve
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| saṃprayogeṇa | instrumental singular | through association |
| tad-yuktāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | those associated with the factors named in VAK 4.08 |
| samutthānāt | ablative singular | through an originating condition |
| kriyā-ādayaḥ | nominative masculine plural | actions and related products |
| viparyayeṇa | instrumental singular | by the converse arrangement |
| akuśalam | nominative neuter singular used substantivally | the unwholesome [is classified] |
| parama-avyākṛte | nominative neuter dual compound | the two ultimately indeterminate [Dharmas] |
| dhruve | nominative neuter dual adjective | the two permanent [Dharmas] |

`Tad` refers back to the three wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya`.
The Bhāṣya identifies the two permanent Dharmas as space and
non-analytical cessation.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda uses `saṃprayogeṇa` and `samutthānāt` as the grounds for two nominal predications. In the second, `viparyayeṇa` marks the converse; `paramāvyākṛte dhruve` completes the final nominal clause.

## 5. Translation

### Close Translation

> Those associated with them are wholesome through association; actions and
> the rest are wholesome through their originating condition. The unwholesome
> is classified conversely. The two permanent [Dharmas] are ultimately
> indeterminate.

### Bhāṣya-informed Study Translation

> Dharmas associated with the wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya` are
> wholesome through association. Bodily and verbal actions and related
> products are wholesome through originating from such wholesome Dharmas,
> although the Bhāṣya leaves a difficulty about certain acquisitions
> unresolved. The unwholesome follows the contrary pattern: saṃsāra is
> unwholesome ultimately; unwholesome roots and factors are unwholesome
> intrinsically; associated Dharmas and their products are unwholesome through
> association and origination. Relative to karmic maturation, contaminated
> Dharmas may be wholesome or indeterminate. Space and non-analytical
> cessation are the two unconditioned Dharmas that are indeterminate without
> qualification.

## 6. Interpretation

The Bhāṣya completes the four grounds begun in VAK 4.08 while retaining
their distinctness. Association and origination classify different
relations; neither replaces intrinsic or ultimate classification. The
objection about contaminated Dharmas is answered by separating the ultimate
evaluation of saṃsāra from the ethical categories assigned relative to
karmic maturation.

In the Kośa's broader system, Discriminative Cognition (*vijñāna*) governs
Mind (*manas*) and guides Dharma Base; it remains distinct from
consciousness (*citta*) in the causal discussions. The same `avijñapti` is
classified in Form Base and Dharma Base, with *vijñāna* bearing the stated
*prati* relation to it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| `saṃprayoga` | association | relation through which joined Dharmas receive an ethical classification |
| `samutthāna` | originating condition | causal relation through which actions and related products receive an ethical classification |
| `kriyā` | action | bodily and verbal action in the Bhāṣya's examples |
| `cittaviprayukta-saṃskāra` | formation dissociated from consciousness | includes arising and related determinations in the Bhāṣya's list |
| `prāpti` | acquisition | a case that complicates a simple originating-condition rule |
| `viparyaya` | converse | applies the contrary ethical classifications to the unwholesome |
| `vipāka` | karmic maturation | basis for classifying contaminated Dharmas as wholesome or indeterminate |
| `avyākṛta` | ethically indeterminate | in the closing clause, the unconditioned Dharmas' unobscured-indeterminate status |
| `ākāśa` | space | one of the two unconditioned Dharmas named |
| `apratisaṃkhyānirodha` | non-analytical cessation | the other unconditioned Dharma named |

## 8. Textual Notes

The natural unit runs from [202|13] through [203|05] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`; `idaṃ vicāryate` at [203|05–06] opens
the next inquiry.

The running witness has rough forms including `samutthā nāt`,
`kāyavāvakkarmaṇi`, and `kukśalam`. The study preserves these in its source
record and marks its editorial readings in the translation. The compressed
`prāptinirodhāsaṃjñisamāpattayaḥ` is segmented as `prāpti`,
`nirodhasamāpatti`, and `asaṃjñisamāpatti`, following their separate
treatment in Chapter 2. The difficulty about certain `prāptis` is left open
by the Bhāṣya itself.
