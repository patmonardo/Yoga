# VAK_4.57

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कुशलस्यावितर्कस्य कर्मणो वेदना मता ।
विपाकश्चैतसिक्येव कायिक्येवाशुभस्य तु ॥ ४.५७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
kuśalasyāvitarkasya karmaṇo vedanā matā /
vipākaś caitasiky eva kāyiky evāśubhasya tu // 4.57 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
kuśalasya avitarkasya karmaṇaḥ vedanā matā /
vipākaḥ caitasikī eva kāyikī eva aśubhasya tu //
```

The Bhāṣya fixes the `avitarka` range here as extending from intermediate
dhyāna through the summit of existence.

**`avitarka`**

This is not a general description of quiet thought. It technically marks karma
from `dhyānāntara` through `bhavāgra`, where `vitarka` is absent.

**`caitasikī vedanā`**

Mental feeling: feeling arising with and belonging to the mental complex. It
does not mean an undifferentiated consciousness-substance.

**`kāyikī vedanā`**

Bodily feeling: feeling associated with bodily sensory support. Here it is the
exclusive painful maturation-feeling directly produced by unwholesome karma.

## 4. Grammar

**Wholesome non-discursive karma**

```text
kuśalasya avitarkasya karmaṇaḥ
vedanā vipākaḥ
caitasikī eva matā
```

> For wholesome karma belonging to the non-discursive range, the feeling that
> constitutes maturation is held to be exclusively mental.

`Caitasikī` agrees with `vedanā`; `vipākaḥ` states the relation in which that
feeling is being considered.

**Unwholesome karma**

```text
aśubhasya [karmaṇaḥ] tu
[vedanā vipākaḥ]
kāyikī eva [matā]
```

> For unwholesome karma, however, the maturation-feeling is exclusively bodily.

The genitive, subject, and predicate are carried forward into the contrasting
clause.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For wholesome karma without vitarka, the feeling held to constitute its
> maturation is exclusively mental; for unwholesome karma, however, it is
> exclusively bodily.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> For wholesome karma belonging to the non-discursive range—from the
> intermediate dhyāna through the summit of existence—the feeling that
> constitutes its maturation is exclusively mental, because bodily feeling
> necessarily belongs to a vitarka-and-vicāra mode. For unwholesome karma, the
> painful maturation-feeling is exclusively bodily, because mental painful
> feeling is `daurmanasya`, and `daurmanasya` is not itself karmic maturation.

The exact meditative range and both exclusion arguments are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A karmic result is determined not only by its affective quality but by the
> locus in which that quality can become actual. The structure of higher
> wholesome karma excludes bodily feeling as its maturation; the structure of
> unwholesome maturation excludes mental distress from serving as its direct
> fruit. Cause, result-quality, and result-locus form a typed relation in which
> no shared predicate licenses arbitrary substitution among its terms.

Mental distress may accompany bodily suffering without thereby becoming the
direct karmic maturation specified here.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Wholesome `avitarka` range**

```text
dhyānāntara
    through
bhavāgra
    → avitarka wholesome karma
```

Its maturation-feeling is mental only.

**Why bodily feeling is excluded**

```text
bodily feeling
    → necessarily belongs to savitarka-savicāra structure

avitarka karma
    → lacks that structure

therefore
    bodily feeling cannot be its maturation
```

**Why mental pain is excluded from unwholesome maturation**

```text
mental painful feeling
    = daurmanasya

daurmanasya
    ≠ vipāka

therefore
    direct painful maturation of unwholesome karma is bodily
```

**Accompaniment and direct maturation**

```text
mental distress accompanies painful result
    does not entail
mental distress is the vipāka produced by that karma
```

This preserves causal role as well as phenomenological presence.

**`vedanā-pradhāna vipāka`**

The Bhāṣya introduces the inquiry by saying maturation is considered
principally through feeling. As VAK 4.47 already established, this does not
make feeling the whole maturation-complex.

**Typed causal restrictions**

```text

    + AVITARKA(K)
    → MENTAL_FEELING_LOCUS(VIPAKA(K))
    → not BODILY_FEELING_LOCUS(VIPAKA(K))
```

```text
UNWHOLESOME(K)
    → BODILY_PAIN_LOCUS(VIPAKA(K))
```

**Shared quality does not erase causal difference**

```text
bodily pain
mental distress
    may both be painful

but

only bodily pain
    functions here as direct vipāka of unwholesome karma
```

**Citta-caitta application**

`Caitasikī` marks a feeling belonging to the differentiated mental complex.
The verse applies the citta-caitta architecture to the locus of karmic result;
it does not reduce that architecture to a generic Subject called
“consciousness.”

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Karma determines the admissible mode of actuality**

Ground is not complete until it determines where and how its result can arise:

```text
karmic Ground
    → result quality
    → result domain
    → result time
    → result locus
```

VAK 4.57 supplies the final distinction locally between bodily and mental
feeling.

**Mental and bodily are functional loci**

The verse does not posit two substances. It distinguishes two modes within the
psychophysical continuum, each governed by determinate causal constraints.

**Direct result versus reaction to result**

Mental distress can arise in response to bodily pain. Vasubandhu's claim is
not that this never happens, but that the distress is not the direct vipāka of
the unwholesome karma. This distinction is essential for precise Kriyā/Karma
analysis.

**Dharmapāda contact**

Karma as Ground does not merely ensure that “something happens.” It carries a
typed specification of the actuality it grounds. The result is determined
through quality, field, time, and locus.

This is the Organon contact. The range of `avitarka`, bodily-feeling condition,
and exclusion of `daurmanasya` from vipāka belong to the Bhāṣya.
