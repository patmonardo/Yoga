# VAK_4.94

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अप्रहेयस्य ते त्वेकं द्वे चत्वारि यथाक्रमम् ।
अयोगविहितं क्लिष्टं विधिभ्रष्टं च केचन ॥ ४.९४ ॥
```

**Two argumentative units**

```text
first half
    completes the abandonment-status fruit matrix

second half
    begins a classification of action by proper constitution
```

The Bhāṣya reports multiple positions in the second unit. The kārikā does not
erase the distinction between afflicted genesis and procedural deviation.

## 4. Grammar

**Non-abandonable source karma**

```text
apraheyasya [karmaṇaḥ]
    te [darśana-heyādayo dharmāḥ]
    ekam dve catvāri [phalāni]
    yathākramam
```

> For non-abandonable karma, those same result-classes are related through
> one, two, and four fruits respectively.

The repeated `yathākramam` explicitly preserves the correspondence for the
middle member as well as the first and last. The Bhāṣya calls this an
abbreviated recitational construction.

**First account of improper constitution**

```text
kliṣṭaṃ karma
    ayoga-vihitam [iti eke]
```

> Some hold afflicted karma to be improperly constituted.

The Bhāṣya supplies the causal reason: it arises from non-methodical or unwise
attention.

**Second account**

```text
vidhi-bhraṣṭam [karma]
    ca [ayoga-vihitam iti]
    kecana [manyante]
```

> And some hold action that has fallen from appropriate procedure to be
> improperly constituted.

`Kecana` marks reported doctrinal positions rather than an unqualified final
definition imposed by the kārikā itself.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For non-abandonable karma, those are respectively one, two, and four
> results. Some hold afflicted karma to be improperly constituted, and also
> action that has fallen away from proper procedure.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Karma not to be abandoned can have seeing-abandonable dharmas as results
> only through dominant conditioning; cultivation-abandonable dharmas through
> activity-production and dominant conditioning; and non-abandonable dharmas
> through correspondence, disconnection, activity-production, and dominant
> conditioning, with maturation excluded. The treatises then distinguish
> properly constituted, improperly constituted, and neither-properly-nor-
> improperly constituted action. According to one reported account, afflicted
> karma is improperly constituted because it arises from non-methodical
> attention. According to another, action is also improperly constituted when
> its execution deviates from the appropriate way of going, standing, eating,
> moving, or otherwise acting.

The exact fruit memberships, the recitational explanation, the threefold
action-classification, and its alternative definitions are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Once the causal-result matrix is complete, the inquiry turns inward to the
> validity of action itself. An operation can fail at its ground: attention
> apprehends the field improperly and generates afflicted action. It can also
> fail in execution: even where a procedure is available, the act departs from
> its appropriate form. Rational action therefore requires both a valid
> genesis and a performance adequate to its rule. Ethical quality and
> procedural conformity are related but cannot be collapsed.

The form of Kriyā is:

```text
Principle and appropriate attention
    → valid constitution of the act

Schema or vidhi
    → appropriate mode of performance

actual execution
    → conformity or deviation

constitution-status
    → properly constituted
      improperly constituted
      or neither
```

This is a direct logical grammar of Rational Yoga: Yoga is not mere activity,
but activity constituted and executed in accordance with its determinate
ground.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Apraheya` — not to be abandoned**

A Path classification, not a claim that a dharma is causally inactive. The
non-abandonable self-relation admits four fruit-types: correspondence,
disconnection, activity-production, and dominant conditioning.

**`Yathākramam` — respectively**

The explicit operator binding the three counts to the established target
order. Its repetition prevents the abbreviated recitation from leaving the
middle correspondence indeterminate.

**`Peyāla-dharma` — abbreviated recitational construction**

The Bhāṣya's description of the verse's compressed enumerative style. Meaning
must be expanded through the controlled order; the words cannot be read as an
unstructured sequence of numerals.

**`Ayoga-vihita` — improperly constituted action**

Action whose formation or performance lacks the appropriate Yoga, conjunction,
or application. The Bhāṣya preserves two explanatory routes: afflicted
genesis from improper attention and unsuitable execution through procedural
deviation.

**`Yoga-vihita` — properly constituted action**

The counterpart reported by the Bhāṣya. Some identify it with wholesome
karma; another formulation includes action that has not fallen from
appropriate procedure. “Proper” therefore has both ethical and operational
dimensions.

**`Naiva-yoga-vihita-nāyoga-vihita` — neither properly nor improperly constituted**

The third class blocks a forced binary. Whatever belongs to neither account
is preserved as neither, rather than assigned by default to one pole.

**`Kliṣṭa` — afflicted**

Karma generated under affliction. In one reported definition, its afflicted
genesis makes it `ayoga-vihita`.

**`Ayoniśomanaskāra` — non-methodical or unwise attention**

An improper mode of attending to a field. It is not merely lack of information;
it constitutes the causal presentation from which afflicted karma arises.

**`Vidhi-bhraṣṭa` — fallen from appropriate procedure**

Action whose actual performance departs from the relevant mode. The examples
are deliberately embodied and ordinary: walking, standing, eating, and moving
about.

**`Ayukta-vidhāna` — unsuitable constitution or procedure**

The Bhāṣya's explanation of why procedurally deviant action is improperly
constituted. A mismatch between appropriate and actual mode is itself a
determinant of the action.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Completion of the abandonment-status matrix**

Let:

```text
V = vipāka
N = niṣyanda
D = visaṃyoga
P = puruṣakāra
A = adhipati
```

The final row is:

```text
F(NonAbandonable, SeeingAbandonable)
    = {A}

F(NonAbandonable, CultivationAbandonable)
    = {P, A}

F(NonAbandonable, NonAbandonable)
    = {N, D, P, A}
```

**Completed VAK 4.93–4.94 matrix**

| Source karma | Seeing-abandonable result | Cultivation-abandonable result | Non-abandonable result |
|---|---|---|---|
| seeing-abandonable | `N, P, A` | `V, N, P, A` | `A` |
| cultivation-abandonable | `P, A` | `V, N, P, A` | `D, P, A` |
| non-abandonable | `A` | `P, A` | `N, D, P, A` |

The numerical projection is:

| Source karma | Seeing-abandonable | Cultivation-abandonable | Non-abandonable |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| seeing-abandonable | 3 | 4 | 1 |
| cultivation-abandonable | 2 | 4 | 3 |
| non-abandonable | 1 | 2 | 4 |

**Constitution of action**

Let an action have:

```text
G = genesis or attentional ground
Q = ethical quality
M = appropriate mode or vidhi
E = actual execution
C = constitution-status
```

The reported accounts yield:

```text
G = ImproperAttention
    → Q = Afflicted
    → C may be ImproperlyConstituted

E deviatesFrom M
    → C may be ImproperlyConstituted

Q = Wholesome
    or E conformsTo M
    → C may be ProperlyConstituted

neither determination applies
    → C = NeitherProperNorImproper
```

The “may be” preserves the reported plurality of definitions rather than
silently fusing them into one adjudicated equivalence.

**Non-equivalences**

```text
improper constitution          ≠ only unwholesome ethical quality
procedural deviation           ≠ necessarily identical with afflicted genesis
proper procedure               ≠ complete ethical goodness by itself
absence from one class         ≠ automatic membership in its opposite
```

**Two sites of practical failure**

```text
genetic defect
    improper attention
        → malformed apprehension
        → afflicted action

executive defect
    departure from vidhi
        → unsuitable performance
        → procedurally malformed action
```

Rational evaluation must track both.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.94 concludes the long fruit-matrix by showing that non-abandonable does
not mean beyond causality. Non-abandonable karma can correspond, disconnect,
actively produce, and dominantly condition non-abandonable dharmas. What falls
away is maturation, not causal efficacy as such. The Path does not arrive at
an inert absence; its non-abandonable determinations remain positively
operative.

The transition to `ayoga-vihita` then changes the question from what results
an act can have to whether the act is validly constituted. This is a necessary
advance. A causal matrix describes the propagation of action, but Practical
Reason must also judge the genesis and execution of the operation itself.

The Bhāṣya's ordinary examples are philosophically important. Rational Yoga
is not confined to spectacular meditation or abstract moral intention. How
one stands, walks, eats, and moves can instantiate or violate an appropriate
procedure. Kriyā is embodied Schema Inference: the universal rule must become
adequate singular performance.

At the same time, procedural correctness must not replace ethics. An action
may be technically conforming while its attentional genesis remains afflicted;
or it may arise from a wholesome orientation yet be executed defectively.
The reported positions preserve these two dimensions even where individual
teachers emphasize one.

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy makes their unity intelligible. Practical
Reason is not satisfied by a good universal held inwardly, nor by an external
performance that accidentally conforms to a rule. The Concept must give
itself objective actuality through an execution adequate to its Principle.
`Yoga-vihita` names this unity at the level of action; `ayoga-vihita` names its
failure in genesis, execution, or both.

This passage therefore belongs at the heart of the future Yoga II commentary.
Kriyā Yoga requires appropriate attention, a valid procedural schema, and an
embodied operation conforming to that schema. Saṃyama will elevate the same
form: Principle, Bhūmi, application, and Jñāna must be internally adequate.
