# VAK_4.41

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
असंवरः संवराप्तिमृत्युद्विव्यञ्जनोदयैः ।
वेगादानक्रियार्थायुर्मूलच्छेदैस्तु मध्यमा ॥ ४.४१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
asaṃvaraḥ saṃvarāptimṛtyudvivyañjanodayaiḥ /
vegādānakriyārthāyurmūlacchedais tu madhyamā // 4.41 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
asaṃvaraḥ saṃvara-āpti-mṛtyu-dvi-vyañjana-udayaiḥ /
vega-ādāna-kriyā-artha-āyus-mūla-cchedaiḥ tu madhyamā //
```

The second compound names six causes of loss. Here `artha` is the Idea as a
purposive determination realized in an enduring `vastu`, not merely an external
object and not an abstract meaning detached from practical realization.

| `mṛtyu` | compound member | death |
| `dvivyañjanodaya` | compound member | arising of both sexual characteristics |

| `vega` | compound member | force, momentum, projected impulse |
| `ādāna` | compound member | taking up or undertaking |
| `kriyā` | compound member | activity, performance |
| `artha` | compound member | Idea; purposive determination realized through a practical project |

| `madhyamā` | nominative feminine singular | the intermediate [avijñapti] |

**`saṃvarāpti`**

Non-restraint is cut off when the person undertakes prātimokṣa restraint or
acquires meditation-born restraint through samādhi. The Bhāṣya gives the
ground as:

```text
pratidvandva-balīyastva
    the opposing determination is stronger
```

**`mṛtyu` and `dvivyañjanodaya`**

Death removes the living support (`āśrayatyāga`). The arising of both sexual
characteristics disrupts the qualifying support (`āśrayavikopana`). These are
the same two support-relations distinguished in VAK 4.38.

**`madhyamā`**

The feminine adjective refers to avijñapti free from both comprehensive
restraint and comprehensive non-restraint:

```text
saṃvarāsaṃvaravinirmuktā avijñapti
```

“Intermediate” names a third class, not a weak mixture of the two standing
forms.

**The six severances**

The Bhāṣya expands the compact compound as:

```text
vega-ccheda
ādāna-tyāga
kriyā-viccheda
artha-viccheda
āyuś-ccheda
kuśalamūla-ccheda
```

The expressions are not six interchangeable synonyms. Each terminates a
different sustaining relation.

## 4. Grammar

**Loss of non-restraint**

```text
asaṃvaraḥ
saṃvara-āpti-mṛtyu-dvi-vyañjana-udayaiḥ
[tyajyate]
```

> Non-restraint is relinquished through acquisition of restraint, death, or
> the arising of both sexual characteristics.

The verb “is relinquished” is carried forward from the abandonment discussion.
The instrumental plural compound contains three causes.

**Loss of intermediate avijñapti**

```text
madhyamā [avijñaptiḥ] tu
vega-ādāna-kriyā-artha-āyus-mūla-cchedaiḥ
[tyajyate]
```

> Intermediate avijñapti, however, is relinquished through severance of its
> force, undertaking, activity, Idea, lifespan, or root.

`Cheda` distributes across the compound, although `ādāna` is expressed in the
prose as relinquishment of the undertaking. The understood feminine
`avijñaptiḥ` explains `madhyamā`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Non-restraint is relinquished through acquisition of restraint, death, or
> the arising of both sexual characteristics. The intermediate [avijñapti],
> however, is relinquished through severance of force, undertaking, activity,
> Idea, lifespan, or root.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Established non-restraint is cut off when a stronger contrary restraint is
> acquired, when the bearer dies, or when the qualifying bodily support is
> disrupted by the arising of both sexual characteristics. Avijñapti that is
> neither complete restraint nor complete non-restraint ceases in six ways:
> when its projected force is exhausted, its undertaking is relinquished, its
> activity is discontinued, the enduring realization of its Idea is destroyed, its
> bearer’s life ends, or its supporting wholesome roots are severed.

The superior strength of the contrary restraint and the sixfold prose
distribution are supplied by the Bhāṣya; its examples show the `vastu` through
which the `artha`, the practical Idea, remains realized.
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A comprehensive harmful form and a project-specific persistent form do not
> cease by the same logic. Established non-restraint requires a stronger
> contrary determination; mere inactivity removes neither its acquired form
> nor its practical history. Intermediate avijñapti persists only while its
> particular causal complex remains operative—momentum, undertaking,
> performance, realized Idea, living bearer, and wholesome root. Its loss is
> therefore dependency-severance rather than conversion by a comprehensive
> opposite.

This distinction does not make non-restraint indestructible. Death and
transformation of its support remain independent causes of loss.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The stronger contrary**

```text
non-restraint possessed
    + genuine restraint acquired
    → non-restraint cut off
```

The Bhāṣya describes the contrary as stronger. This is not the coexistence of
restraint with a particular grave offense discussed in VAK 4.39. Complete
non-restraint is a standing practical form, and acquisition of comprehensive
restraint displaces it.

**Mere inactivity is insufficient**

Even abandonment of weapons and nets with an intention not to act does not,
in the absence of restraint, cut off non-restraint.

The medical analogy is exact:

```text
avoid the cause of an established disease
    ≠ cure the established disease

stop the external occupation
    ≠ acquire the contrary form
```

Behavioral cessation and termination of an acquired standing determination
must remain distinct.

**Temporary upavāsa problem**

Suppose a bearer of non-restraint undertakes one-day upavāsa. When the
temporary restraint ends, two positions are recorded:

```text
Position 1
    non-restraint returns
    because the intention to abandon it was not final

Position 2
    non-restraint does not return automatically
    reacquisition depends on a new vijñapti
```

The second position preserves a strict acquisition rule:

```text
prior history of non-restraint
    ≠ automatic present reacquisition
```

The short exchange is anonymous, so no school attribution is forced.

**Projected force: `vega`**

Intermediate avijñapti may be projected by a force of serene confidence or
affliction:

```text
prasāda-vega
kleśa-vega
```

When the force is exhausted, the avijñapti ceases.

**Potter’s-wheel analogy**

```text
initial impulse
    → wheel continues moving

projected force exhausted
    → movement ceases
```

Persistence after the initiating moment does not require an independently
self-subsisting entity. It can be a conditioned continuation generated by the
initial force.

**Relinquishing undertaking**

The bearer may explicitly decide:

```text
alam samādānena
    “Enough of this undertaking.”
```

The project-specific avijñapti then ceases because the practical relation that
instituted it has been abandoned.

**Cessation of activity**

Where continuing performance sustains the avijñapti, failure to perform what
was undertaken cuts that continuity. This cause is distinct from verbal
relinquishment: the project may cease in practice without a formal statement.

**Destruction of the realized `artha`**

The Bhāṣya names the concrete `vastu` in which practical Ideas have been
realized:

```text
caitya
park or monastery
dwelling
bed or seat
mechanism
net
```

A meritorious construction or harmful device may continue to function as the
objective realization of an earlier practical Idea. Destruction of that
`vastu` removes the realized basis of the associated avijñapti.

Thus `artha` is best translated here as “Idea”: not a merely subjective thought,
but the purposive determination that has attained objective existence in a
`vastu`. The Bhāṣya's concrete examples specify its embodiment rather than
reducing `artha` to “Object.”

**Life and wholesome root**

Death removes the bearer of intermediate avijñapti. Severance of wholesome
roots terminates wholesome intermediate avijñapti by destroying its causal
basis.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Two cessation logics**

```text
comprehensive non-restraint
    → terminated by stronger comprehensive contrary
    or loss/disruption of support

intermediate avijñapti
    → terminated by severance
    of its project-specific dependency
```

The distinction follows the different modes of persistence established in
VAK 4.37.

**Action stopped versus form removed**

```text
no current harmful action
    does not entail
no standing non-restraint
```

This prevents visible inactivity from being mistaken for ethical conversion.

**Six dependencies**

| Dependency | Loss condition |
|---|---|
| Projected force | exhaustion of momentum |
| Undertaking | explicit relinquishment |
| Activity | discontinuation of performance |
| Realized Idea | destruction of its enduring `vastu` |
| Life | death of bearer |
| Wholesome root | severance of causal support |

**The Idea's objective continuation**

The `artha` cases add a distinctive structure:

```text
initiating act ceases
    but its Idea continues objectively in the constructed `vastu`
    → associated avijñapti continues

realizing `vastu` destroyed
    → associated continuation ceases
```

Persistence may therefore be mediated through an Idea that has achieved an
enduring practical actuality, not only through an internal intention or
repeated bodily motion.

**Reacquisition requires a cause**

The second upavāsa position refuses automatic return:

```text
temporary contrary ends
    does not by itself regenerate prior non-restraint

new vijñapti
    supplies reacquisition condition
```

Loss of an opposing state and positive acquisition of the former state are
different transitions.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Stopping is not conversion**

VAK 4.41 gives a severe but lucid practical teaching. A harmful occupation is
not overcome merely by temporary inactivity:

```text
tools laid down
    action presently absent

restraint acquired
    practical orientation transformed
```

The first removes an occasion. The second generates the stronger contrary
determination capable of cutting the standing non-restraint.

**Persistence without a hidden substance**

The potter’s wheel gives a positive account of avijñapti persistence:

```text
initiating force
    → projected continuation
    → conditioned persistence
    → exhaustion
```

The continuation need not be visible at every moment, but neither is it a
transcendent entity placed behind events. Its duration is intelligible through
the force that projected it and the conditions that continue to sustain it.

**The Idea becomes objective**

The monastery and net examples show two ethical valences of the same logical
form:

```text
wholesome construction
    continues serving its field

harmful device
    continues performing its harmful function
```

An Idea can persist objectively in what action has made. Its realized `vastu`
becomes a continuing causal participant until destroyed.

**The middle is a genuine third determination**

Intermediate avijñapti is not halfway between virtue and vice. It is middle
with respect to systematic scope:

```text
not comprehensive restraint
not comprehensive non-restraint
but a real persistent form of a determinate practical Idea
```

This prevents the dyadic plane from being mistaken for an exhaustive list of
all karmic persistence.

This is the Organon contact. Strong contrary determination, the medical and
potter’s-wheel analogies, the anonymous upavāsa dispute, and the six
dependency severances belong to the Bhāṣya.
