# VAK 4.60 Bhāṣya — Allocating the Four Colors of Karma

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
aśubhaṃ rūpakāmāptaṃ śubhaṃ caiva yathākramam /
kṛṣṇaśuklobhayaṃ karma tatkṣayāya nirāsravam // 4.60 //
```

> Unwholesome karma, wholesome karma belonging to the form realm,
> and wholesome karma belonging to the desire realm are respectively
> black, white, and both; outflow-free karma serves to exhaust them.

The Bhāṣya specifies that “both” concerns the mixture of karmas
within a continuum, not a single intrinsically black-and-white act.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Unwholesome karma, wholesome karma belonging to the form realm,
and wholesome karma belonging to the desire realm are, respectively,
black, white, and both; outflow-free karma serves to exhaust them.”
Unwholesome karma is entirely black because it is afflicted, and
its maturation is black because that maturation is unpleasant.
Wholesome karma of the form realm is entirely white because it is
not mixed with unwholesome karma; its maturation is white because
it is pleasant.

Why is karma of the formless realm not mentioned? The cited
explanation restricts this formulation to where maturation occurs
both in an intermediate existence and in an existence at birth, and
where the threefold karma of body, speech, and mind is possible. Yet
the formless case is mentioned in another sūtra. “Wholesome karma
belonging to the desire realm is black-and-white because it is
intermixed with unwholesome karma; its maturation is black-and-white
because the maturation is mixed.” This is established with respect
to a *continuum*, not with respect to an individual nature: no
single karma or maturation is both black and white, since the two
are mutually opposed.

But would unwholesome karma then also be black-and-white because
it is mixed with wholesome karma? Not necessarily. In the desire
realm, unwholesome karma is strong and need not be mixed with
wholesome karma; wholesome karma is weaker and is mixed with
unwholesome karma. Outflow-free karma leads to the exhaustion or
abandonment of the other three karmas. It is not black because it
is unafflicted; it is called “not white” because it lacks *white
maturation*. “Not white” is an expression used with a particular
intention: the Blessed One calls the non-learner's dharmas wholly
wholesome and blameless, and a treatise also calls wholesome and
unobstructed indeterminate dharmas “white.” Outflow-free karma is
without maturation because it does not belong to a realm and
opposes the course of continued existence.

## 4. What the Colors Classify

The first three colors coordinate the quality of karma with the
quality of its maturation. The mixed third class is a judgment
about the **continuum** in the desire realm. Vasubandhu explicitly
rejects a black-and-white `svabhāva` for one karma or one result.
The fourth class has a different function: outflow-free action
works toward exhausting the first three. Its designation “not
white” is restricted by the sūtra's intended sense of *having white
maturation*, not a denial of its wholesome character.

The exclusion of the formless realm is also restricted to this
sūtra's stated range, since the prose acknowledges its inclusion
elsewhere. The grounds named here are two modes of maturation and
the possibility of bodily, verbal, and mental karma.

## 5. Organon Contact Point

Our classification should mark whether a predicate belongs to a
single act or to a continuum containing different acts. The
Bhāṣya's `saṃtānataḥ` versus `svabhāvataḥ` distinction prevents a
mixed history from being misread as an intrinsically contradictory
individual act. This is a formal comparison, not a replacement for
the sūtra's karmic account.
