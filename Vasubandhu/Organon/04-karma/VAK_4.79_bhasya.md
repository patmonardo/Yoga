# VAK 4.79 Bhāṣya — Severance of the Wholesome Roots

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
mūlacchedastvasaddṛṣṭyā kāmāptotpattilābhinām /
phalahetvapavādinyā sarvathā kramaśo nṛṣu // 4.79 //
```


> Severance of the wholesome roots occurs through false view. It concerns
> desire-domain roots acquired by birth, through a view denying result and
> ground; completely, successively, among humans.

False view now appears at its strongest practical degree. It no longer
merely misclassifies one action: it can remove the generative bases through
which wholesome action arises.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Severance of the roots occurs through false view.” The wholesome roots are
severed by false view that has become fully intense.

But the treatise asks: “What are the intense unwholesome roots? Those
unwholesome roots through which one cuts off the wholesome roots, and those
which one first effaces when approaching freedom from desire.” Because false
view is brought forth by the unwholesome roots, its action is attributed to
them. Fire itself burns a village, but because thieves supplied the fire one
says that the thieves burned the village.

Which wholesome roots are severed? “Those belonging to the desire domain
and acquired by birth.” Desire-domain wholesome roots are severed because
the person is not presently endowed with roots belonging to the form and
formless domains.

How, then, should one understand the statement in the *Prajñaptibhāṣya* that
this person has severed wholesome roots belonging to all three domains? It
means that acquisition of those higher roots has been made remote because
the continuum has been rendered an unsuitable basis for them. Only roots
acquired by birth are directly severed; those produced through practice
have already been lost before the final severance.

Through false view having what object are they severed? “Through a view
denying result and ground.” One denial concerns the ground: “There is no
good conduct and no bad conduct.” The other concerns the result: “Wholesome
and unwholesome karma have no fruit or maturation.” Some compare these two
denials respectively to the uninterrupted and liberation paths.

The severing view takes a contaminated object rather than an uncontaminated
one, and an object belonging to its own domain rather than another domain.
One explanation appeals to the weakness of an anuśaya that operates merely
through association. The witness is compressed here, so the precise scope
of that subsidiary explanation remains provisional.

How is the severance complete? Some say that all nine grades of wholesome
roots are severed at once, like what is abandoned by the path of seeing.
The preferred explanation instead gives full force to “successively.” Nine
grades of false view sever nine grades of wholesome roots, as with the
afflictions abandoned through cultivation, through the strongest of the
strong finally severing the weakest of the weak.

This preserves the treatise's statement about the subtle-associated roots:
the final wholesome roots abandoned are those whose dispersal gives one the
designation “one whose wholesome roots are severed.” Another passage speaks
of the strongest unwholesome roots as those through which wholesome roots
are severed. That statement refers to completion, because they perform the
exhaustive final severance. If even one grade remained unsevered, it could
serve as the ground for all the roots to arise again.

Some say false view severs the roots through its manifest arising, as does
the path of seeing. The preferred account describes both modes. Some say
that restraint is abandoned first and the wholesome roots are cut off
afterward. The alternative explanation says that the restraint belonging
as result to a particular citta is abandoned through abandonment of that
citta. The relation between restraint-loss and root-severance is preserved
as a transmitted dispute.

Where are wholesome roots severed? “Among humans.” This occurs among human
beings, not in the lower destinies, because afflicted and unafflicted
Prajñā there lack firmness; and not among gods, because karmic result is
directly evident to them.

It occurs in three continents, excluding Northern Kuru. Others restrict it
to Jambūdvīpa because the other continents lack an evil disposition. But a
text contradicts that narrower view by saying that one from Jambūdvīpa can
possess as few as eight faculties and that the same is true of persons from
Eastern Videha and Godānīya.

## 4. Direct Operation and Attributed Cause

The source distinguishes the immediate severing Power from the roots that
generate and sustain it:

```text
unwholesome roots
    bring forth and support intensified false view
        ↓
fully intensified false view
    directly severs the wholesome roots
```

The village-fire analogy explains attributed agency without confusing it
with immediate operation. The thieves burn the village in the broader
practical sense because they supply the fire; the fire performs the direct
burning. Likewise the unwholesome roots are credited with severance because
they generate the false view that performs it.

## 5. What Is Severed

The direct object is carefully restricted:

```text
desire-domain roots acquired by birth
    → directly severed

practice-produced wholesome roots
    → already lost before final severance

form and formless-domain roots
    → not presently possessed and therefore not directly severed
    → their future acquisition is made remote
```

“Roots of all three domains are severed” therefore describes a transformation
of the continuum's eligibility. The continuum becomes an unsuitable basis
for acquiring the higher roots. Direct loss and exclusion of future
acquisition must remain distinct.

## 6. Complete Through Sequence

The two transmitted models are:

```text
SIMULTANEOUS ACCOUNT
    all nine grades severed at once
    analogous to abandonment by the path of seeing

SUCCESSIVE ACCOUNT
    nine grades of false view
        progressively sever
    nine grades of wholesome roots
```

The preferred account protects both words of the verse:

```text
sarvathā
    complete scope: no grade remains

kramaśaḥ
    successive mode: completeness is achieved through order
```

Total severance is therefore a completed sequence rather than an abstract
instantaneous nothing. The final stage is decisive because any surviving
grade could ground regeneration of the complete set.

## 7. Why Human Existence

The Bhāṣya places this radical transformation between two limits:

```text
lower destinies
    Prajñā lacks the firmness required to establish the severing denial

gods
    karmic result is too directly evident for that denial to stabilize

humans
    false view can become reflectively and practically firm
```

This is not praise of human reason by itself. It identifies the human domain
as capable of powerful principial determination in either direction.
Prajñā can disclose the practical order, while false view can become strong
enough to destroy the continuum's wholesome generative capacity.

## 8. Organon Contact Point

Root-severing false view is a failure at the level of practical architecture:

```text
deny valid Ground
    + deny determinate Result
        ↓
erase the efficacy of the wholesome/unwholesome distinction
        ↓
progressively disable wholesome generation
        ↓
render the continuum an unsuitable basis for higher acquisition
```

An agent can make a local error while retaining the principles required for
correction. Here the agent denies the ground-result relation that makes
error, consequence, and correction intelligible. This is schema corruption,
not merely one defective output.

The passage also gives a governance rule: monitor degradation by grade.
Complete failure may be assembled through successive losses of provenance,
causal discrimination, consequence tracking, and corrective capacity.
Preserving one operative grade can preserve the possibility of regeneration.

These agent and schema formulations are the Organon reconstruction. The
objects of severance, causal attribution, domain restrictions, nine-grade
dispute, restraint dispute, and human locus belong to the Bhāṣya.
