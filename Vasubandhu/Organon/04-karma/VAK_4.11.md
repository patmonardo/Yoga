# VAK_4.11 — Which Cognitions Initiate or Accompany Action?

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
प्रवर्तकं दृष्टिहेयं विज्ञानमुभयं पुनः ।
मानसं भावनाहेयं पञ्चकं त्वनुवर्तकम् ॥ ४.११ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
pravartakaṃ dṛṣṭiheyaṃ vijñānam ubhayaṃ punaḥ /
mānasaṃ bhāvanāheyaṃ pañcakaṃ tv anuvartakam // 4.11 //
```

VAK 4.10 distinguished origination that initiates an action from origination
that accompanies its performance. VAK 4.11 assigns those roles to kinds of
Cognition. The kārikā states three cases; the Bhāṣya completes the
fourfold classification by adding uncontaminated Cognition as neither.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause identifies `dṛṣṭiheyaṃ vijñānam` as `pravartakam`. The next clause predicates `ubhayam` of `mānasaṃ bhāvanāheyam`. The final contrastive `pañcakam tu` is predicated as `anuvartakam`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Cognition abandoned by seeing is initiating. Mind-Cognition abandoned
> through cultivation does both; the five [sensory Cognitions], however, are
> accompanying.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Cognition abandoned by seeing is initiating. Mind-Cognition abandoned through
> cultivation does both; the five sensory Cognitions, however, are accompanying
> only.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Cognition abandoned by seeing is initiating. Mind-Cognition abandoned through cultivation does both; the five [sensory Cognitions], however, are accompanying.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya adds a fourth case: uncontaminated Cognition is neither initiating nor accompanying. It also identifies the fivefold group as the five sensory Cognitions; the kārikā does not name this fourth member explicitly.

### Textual Notes

The Bhāṣya unit begins with `tatra ca` at [203|19], gives the first
recitation of VAK 4.11 at [203|20], and ends with `nobhayam anāsravam` at
[204|11] in `Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The following question,
`kiṃ khalu yathā pravartakaṃ`, opens VAK 4.12.

The running witness alternates `pravartaka` and `pravarttaka`. The exchange
concerning produced Form, Great Elements, and abandonment is compressed;
fine speaker assignments and the exact scope of the cited Abhidharma claim
remain provisional. The fourfold conclusion itself is explicit.

### Interpretation

The fourfold account classifies Cognitions by their role relative to
manifestation, not by an all-purpose measure of their efficacy. The Bhāṣya
distinguishes an initiating causal basis from the condition present during
performance and resists automatically transferring a cause's path
classification to produced Form.

In the Kośa's broader system, Discriminative Cognition (*vijñāna*) governs
Mind (*manas*) and guides Dharma Base; keep it distinct from the
consciousness (*citta*) named in the Bhāṣya's causal explanation and from
manifestation (*vijñapti*). The same `avijñapti` is classified in Form Base
and Dharma Base, with *vijñāna* bearing the stated *prati* relation to it.
