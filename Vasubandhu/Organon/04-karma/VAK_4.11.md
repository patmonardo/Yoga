# VAK_4.11 — Which Cognitions Initiate or Accompany Action?

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> प्रवर्तकं दृष्टिहेयं विज्ञानमुभयं पुनः ।
>
> मानसं भावनाहेयं पञ्चकं त्वनुवर्तकम् ॥ ४.११ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pravartakaṃ dṛṣṭiheyaṃ vijñānam ubhayaṃ punaḥ /
>
> mānasaṃ bhāvanāheyaṃ pañcakaṃ tv anuvartakam // 4.11 //

VAK 4.10 distinguished origination that initiates an action from origination
that accompanies its performance. VAK 4.11 assigns those roles to kinds of
Cognition. The kārikā states three cases; the Bhāṣya completes the
fourfold classification by adding uncontaminated Cognition as neither.

## 4. Grammar and Argument

The first clause assigns initiation to Cognition abandoned by seeing:

```text
dṛṣṭi-heyaṃ vijñānam
    pravartakam

Cognition to be abandoned by seeing
    is initiating
```

The Bhāṣya explains that this mind is the causal basis for the `vitarka` and
`vicāra` that produce manifestation. It does not accompany the performance:
the outward-facing consciousness active during the action is not that
initiating consciousness.

The next clause assigns both roles to Mind-Cognition abandoned through
cultivation:

```text
mānasaṃ [vijñānam] bhāvanā-heyam
    ubhayam

Mind-Cognition to be abandoned through cultivation
    performs both functions
```

The final clause assigns accompaniment alone to the five sensory Cognitions:

```text
pañcakam tu
    anuvartakam

the five [sensory Cognitions], however,
    accompany performance
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the fourth member:

| Function relative to manifestation | Cognition |
|---|---|
| Initiates only | Cognition abandoned by seeing |
| Accompanies only | The five sensory Cognitions |
| Initiates and accompanies | Mind-Cognition abandoned through cultivation |
| Neither | Uncontaminated Cognition |

The opening objection challenges how an initiating consciousness's path
classification relates to the Form it produces. The Bhāṣya's exchange is
compressed and marks the claim concerning Form as requiring proof. It does
not support a general rule that an effect inherits every classification of
its cause.

## 5. Translation

### Close Translation

> Cognition abandoned by seeing is initiating. Mind-Cognition abandoned
> through cultivation does both; the five [sensory Cognitions], however, are
> accompanying.

### Bhāṣya-informed Study Translation

> Cognition abandoned by seeing initiates manifestation but does not
> accompany its performance. Mind-Cognition abandoned through cultivation
> can both initiate and accompany manifestation. The five sensory
> Cognitions accompany it only. The Bhāṣya adds uncontaminated Cognition as
> neither initiating nor accompanying.

The fourth case and the identification of the fivefold group as the five
sensory Cognitions come from the Bhāṣya, not from an explicit fourth member
in the verse.

## 6. Interpretation

The fourfold account classifies Cognitions by their role relative to
manifestation, not by an all-purpose measure of their efficacy. The Bhāṣya
distinguishes an initiating causal basis from the condition present during
performance and resists automatically transferring a cause's path
classification to produced Form.

In the Kośa's broader system, Discriminative Cognition (*vijñāna*) governs
Mind (*manas*) and guides Dharma Base; keep it distinct from the
consciousness (*citta*) named in the Bhāṣya's causal explanation and from
manifestation (*vijñapti*). The same `avijñapti` is classified in Form Base
and Dharma Base, with *vijñāna* bearing the stated *prati* relation to it.

## 8. Textual Notes

The Bhāṣya unit begins with `tatra ca` at [203|19], gives the first
recitation of VAK 4.11 at [203|20], and ends with `nobhayam anāsravam` at
[204|11] in `Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The following question,
`kiṃ khalu yathā pravartakaṃ`, opens VAK 4.12.

The running witness alternates `pravartaka` and `pravarttaka`. The exchange
concerning produced Form, Great Elements, and abandonment is compressed;
fine speaker assignments and the exact scope of the cited Abhidharma claim
remain provisional. The fourfold conclusion itself is explicit.
