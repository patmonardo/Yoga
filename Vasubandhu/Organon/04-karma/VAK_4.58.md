# VAK_4.58 — Disturbance of Consciousness and Its Conditions

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
चित्तक्षेपो मनश्चित्ते स च कर्मविपाकजः ।
भयोपघातवैषम्यशोकैश्चाकुरुकामिनाम् ॥ ४.५८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
cittakṣepo manaścitte sa ca karmavipākajaḥ /
bhayopaghātavaiṣamyaśokaiś cākurukāminām // 4.58 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
citta-kṣepaḥ manaḥ-citte saḥ ca karma-vipāka-jaḥ /
bhaya-upaghāta-vaiṣamya-śokaiḥ ca a-kuru-kāminām //
```

The Bhāṣya distinguishes mental derangement born from maturation from the
bodily maturation that immediately conditions it.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `cittakṣepaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | disturbance of Consciousness |
| `manaścitte` | locative neuter singular compound | in Mind Cognition; glossed as `manovijñāna` |
| `saḥ` | nominative masculine singular pronoun | it; that derangement |
| `karma-vipāka-jaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | born from karmic maturation |
| `bhaya` | compound member | fear |
| `upaghāta` | compound member | injury or violent disturbance |
| `vaiṣamya` | compound member | disequilibrium or bodily imbalance |
| `śokaiḥ` | instrumental masculine plural | through grief |
| `ca` | conjunction | and; also |
| `akurukāminām` | genitive masculine plural compound | among Desire Realm beings other than the Kurus |

**`cittakṣepa`**

This is disturbance of Consciousness, including disruption of stable
orientation and recollection. The Bhāṣya distinguishes it from `vikṣepa`,
afflictive distraction.

**`manaścitta`**

The Bhāṣya glosses this as `manovijñāna`, Mind Cognition. The five
sensory Cognition-series are excluded because they are `avikalpaka`.

**`vipākaja`**

“Born from maturation” expresses mediate causal origin. It does not state that
the manifest derangement is itself the immediate maturation.

**`akurukāminām`: population phrase**

The Kārikā's `a-kuru-kāminām` means Desire Realm beings other than the
Kurus. The running Bhāṣya witness has the irregular `akuruṇāṃ kāminām`;
the parallel Chinese Bhāṣya explicitly excludes Northern Kuru (`除北俱盧`)
while retaining the other Desire Realm beings. See [Xuanzang's
translation, T1558, chapter 15](https://deerpark.app/cbeta/T1558/15).
The earlier reading “without compassion” mistook this geographical
exclusion for a moral qualification.

## 4. Grammar

**Locus of derangement**

```text
citta-kṣepaḥ
manaḥ-citte [bhavati]
```

> Disturbance of Consciousness occurs in Mind Cognition.

The locative identifies the cognitive series in which the disturbance occurs.

**Karmic causal ancestry**

```text
saḥ ca
karma-vipāka-jaḥ [bhavati]
```

> It may also be born from karmic maturation.

The suffix `-ja` marks origin from maturation rather than identity with it.

**Proximate causes and range**

```text
bhaya-upaghāta-vaiṣamya-śokaiḥ ca
[citta-kṣepaḥ bhavati]

a-kuru-kāminām
```

> It also occurs through fear, injury, bodily imbalance, and grief among the
> beings of the Desire Realm other than the Kurus.

The instrumental compound supplies proximate causes; the genitive gives the
range of beings in the text's cosmology.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Disturbance of Consciousness occurs in Mind Cognition. It may be born from
> karmic maturation and may also arise through fear, injury, bodily imbalance, or
> grief among Desire Realm beings other than the Kurus.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Disturbance of Consciousness occurs in Mind Cognition; it is born from karmic
> maturation, and also from fear, injury, imbalance, and grief among Desire
> Realm beings other than the Kurus.
## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Disturbance of Consciousness occurs in Mind Cognition. It may be born from karmic maturation and may also arise through fear, injury, bodily imbalance, or grief among Desire Realm beings other than the Kurus.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Why disturbance occurs in Mind Cognition**

```text
five sensory Cognition-series
    avikalpaka
    → not locus of cittakṣepa

Mind Cognition (`manovijñāna`)
    supports recollection and practical differentiation
    → locus of cittakṣepa
```

This is Vasubandhu's technical argument, not a modern clinical account.

**Remote karmic ground**

The Bhāṣya cites intentional acts such as deranging others, intoxicating them
against their will, terrifying them, causing dangerous injury, or making them
lose recollection. Such karma may later condition one's own derangement.

**Direct maturation and downstream consequence**

```text
past karma
    → disturbance of mahābhūtas as vipāka
    → constitutional disequilibrium
    → disturbed Mind Cognition
    → loss of recollection and orientation
```

The disturbed Consciousness is `vipākaja`, but the bodily disturbance is
the immediate `vipāka`.

**Proximate conditions**

```text
bhaya
    fear

upaghāta
    injury, including blows to vital points

vaiṣamya
    bodily constitutional imbalance

śoka
    grief
```

The Bhāṣya uses inherited physiological language of wind, bile, and phlegm;
that model remains historically and textually bounded.

**`cittakṣepa` and `vikṣepa`**

```text
cittakṣepa
    derangement of mental functioning

vikṣepa
    afflictive distraction or defilement
```

Vasubandhu treats them as logically independent: either, both, or neither can
be present. The first quadrant is damaged in the running Sanskrit witness;
Xuanzang's parallel Bhāṣya gives a disturbed but unafflicted Consciousness.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Typed causal pipeline**

```text
PAST_KARMA
    → VIPAKA(BODILY_CONSTITUTION)
    → IMBALANCE
    → MENTAL_DERANGEMENT
```

not:

```text
PAST_KARMA
    → MENTAL_DERANGEMENT_AS_DIRECT_VIPAKA
```

**Born from maturation is not identical with maturation**

```text
CAUSED_VIA_VIPAKA(X, Y)
    does not entail
IS_VIPAKA(Y)
```

This preserves the distinction established in VAK 4.57.

**Remote and proximate causation**

```text
remote ground
    karmic maturation

proximate conditions
    fear, injury, imbalance, grief

manifest effect
    cittakṣepa
```

More than one causal level can be true of the same occurrence.

**Pathology and affliction are independent predicates**

| | Afflictively distracted | Not afflictively distracted |
|---|---|---|
| Mentally deranged | both | deranged only |
| Not mentally deranged | distracted only | neither |

The distinction prevents mental disturbance from being treated automatically
as moral or afflictive fault.

The disturbed but unafflicted quadrant is supported by the Chinese parallel;
the running Sanskrit witness's first member is corrupt.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The exclusion of the sensory series and the mediate bodily
causal sequence are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The Bhāṣya excludes the five sensory
cognition-series and explains a mediate route: karmic maturation disturbs the bodily
constitution through the Great Elements, after which Mind Cognition and
recollection are disordered. The running Bhāṣya witness reads
`akuruṇāṃ kāminām`, but the Kārikā's `a-kuru-kāminām` and Xuanzang's parallel
translation identify the range as Desire Realm beings other than those of
Northern Kuru. The Sanskrit prose spelling remains irregular.

**Practical Dependent Origination**

This verse displays the operational form directly:

```text
karmic Ground
    → embodied maturation
    → altered condition
    → manifest mental effect
```

Practical Dependent Origination specifies which link is action, which is
maturation, which is proximate condition, and which is appearance.

**Theoretical and Practical wheels**

In the project synthesis:

```text
Theoretical Dependent Origination
    determines the grounds and conditions of coming-to-be

Practical Dependent Origination
    tracks how action configures, transmits, and actualizes those grounds
```

The Kośa brings the second into application without severing it from the first.

**Dhātu enters as a mediating constitution**

The physiological `dhātu` (Principle) in the Bhāṣya names a constitutional factor or
condition through which karma becomes effective. It should not be confused
without analysis with the three cosmological Realms or the eighteen-Principle
classification. The shared term marks determination of a functioning field,
but its local reference must remain controlled.

**No moralization of mental disturbance**

Vasubandhu's distinction between `cittakṣepa` and `vikṣepa` is crucial. A
disturbed mental condition and an afflictively corrupted condition are not the
same judgment.

This is the Organon contact. The sensory exclusion, examples, mediate bodily
cause, humoral language, and fourfold distinction belong to the Bhāṣya; the
twofold Dependent Origination formulation is project synthesis.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> A karmic Ground need not appear immediately in the form of its final
> consequence. Past action first becomes actual as a matured bodily condition;
> that transformed basis then conditions a disturbance of mental functioning.
> Ground, immediate maturation, proximate condition, and manifest effect are
> distinct moments of one dependent causal process. To say that derangement is
> “born from maturation” therefore preserves mediation rather than identifying
> the appearance with its Ground.

The same manifest condition may also arise through present fear, injury,
imbalance, or grief without every case being reduced to one karmic explanation.
