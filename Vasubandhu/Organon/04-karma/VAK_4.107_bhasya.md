# VAK 4.107 Bhāṣya — Karma at Three Path Thresholds

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ānantaryasabhāgāni pañcamaṃ stūpabhedanam /
> kṣāntyanāgāmitārhattvaprāptau karmātivighnakṛt // 4.107 //

> The analogues of the immediate-result actions; destruction of a stūpa is the
> fifth. Karma is a powerful obstruction at the attainments of acceptance,
> non-returnership, and arhatship.

The first half completes the set begun in 4.106. The second half introduces
three distinct attainment thresholds and their corresponding obstructions.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

## 3. Continuous Translation

The analogues of the immediate-result actions; destruction of a stūpa is
the fifth. These five correspond in order to the five immediate-result
actions. The prose restates the five cases: sexual misconduct with one's
mother when she is an arhat; killing a bodhisattva established in certainty;
killing a trainee; taking away an access or means associated with the
Saṅgha's comfort; and destroying a stūpa.

Other karma, too, that possesses maturation becomes especially obstructive
at three points. Which three?

At the attainments of acceptance, non-returnership, and arhatship, karma is
a powerful obstruction. As one advances from the summit stage toward
acceptance, karma leading to bad destinies appears as an obstacle because
one is passing beyond its maturation ground. It is like creditors appearing
when a person is about to leave the country.

For one approaching the fruit of non-returnership, Desire Realm karma
appears as an obstacle in the same way, except for action whose maturation
is to be experienced in the present life. For one approaching arhatship,
form- and Formless Realm karma appears as an obstacle in the same way.

## 4. Three Thresholds and Their Maturation Grounds

| Attainment approached | Karma named as obstructive | Bhāṣya's explanation |
|---|---|---|
| `kṣānti`, acceptance | karma leading to bad destinies | The maturation ground is being passed beyond |
| fruit of non-returnership | Desire Realm karma, except present-life-maturation action | The same rationale is carried forward |
| arhatship | form- and Formless Realm karma | The same rationale is carried forward |

The opening claim concerns `anyad api karma savipākam`, “other karma, too,
that possesses maturation.” It is therefore broader than the five
`ānantarya-sabhāga` analogues. The first explanation, `tadvipākabhūmyatikramāt`,
locates the obstruction in passing beyond the ground where that karma would
mature. The creditor simile illustrates a claim pressed at departure.

## 5. Interpretation

The passage treats Path progression as a threshold at which an outstanding
maturation becomes especially pressing. The sequence is specific: karma
leading to bad destinies at `kṣānti`, Desire Realm karma at the fruit of
non-returnership, and form- and Formless Realm karma at arhatship. The
present-life-maturation exception is stated only for the second case.

The simile supports a model of transition-sensitive obstruction: as one
leaves a maturation ground, a claim associated with that ground presses
before departure. It does not say that every action must obstruct, that
the obstruction always succeeds, or that the Path guarantees a single
exclusive class of future births. This is relevant to the earlier
*bhavāgra-cetanā* discussion, but remains distinct from it: 4.105 ranks
worldly maturation-result, while 4.107 describes karmic obstructions at
attainment thresholds. A Yoga or Tantra account of future birth may extend
this structure, but it is not the Bhāṣya's explicit claim here.
