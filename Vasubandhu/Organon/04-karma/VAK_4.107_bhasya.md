# VAK 4.107 Bhāṣya — Karma at Three Path Thresholds

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ānantaryasabhāgāni pañcamaṃ stūpabhedanam /
kṣāntyanāgāmitārhattvaprāptau karmātivighnakṛt // 4.107 //
```

> These are analogues of the immediate-result offenses; breaking a stūpa
> is the fifth. Karma becomes especially obstructive at the attainment
> of acceptance, non-returnership, and arhatship.

The first half completes 4.106's list. The second half introduces a
broader claim about karma whose possible place of maturation is being
passed beyond. It is **not restricted to the five analogues**.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The previously named actions are analogous, in order, to the five
immediate-result offenses; breaking a stūpa is the fifth. The commentary
briefly restates the five acts. **Other** karma possessing a maturation
also becomes particularly obstructive at three times. Which times?

At the attainment of acceptance, non-returnership, and arhatship. As
someone approaches acceptance from the summit stage, actions leading to
bad destinies stand in the way, because the person is about to pass
beyond the realms in which those actions could mature. It is like
creditors confronting someone about to leave the country.

As someone approaches the fruit of non-returnership, desire-realm
actions obstruct in the same way, except for karma to be experienced in
this very life. As someone approaches arhatship, actions belonging to
the form and formless realms similarly obstruct.

## 4. Three Thresholds, Three Maturation Domains

| Attainment approached | Karma that obstructs | Domain being passed beyond |
|---|---|---|
| `kṣānti`, acceptance | karma leading to bad destinies | bad rebirths |
| `anāgāmiphala`, fruit of non-returnership | desire-realm karma, except present-life karma | future desire-realm rebirth |
| `arhattva`, arhatship | form- and formless-realm karma | further rebirth in those realms |

The Bhāṣya explains the first obstruction with
`tadvipākabhūmyatikramāt`: passage beyond that karma's maturation
ground. Its `tathaiva` extends the same rationale to the latter two
thresholds. The present-life exception matters because such karma does
not require a future desire-realm rebirth to mature.

The creditor simile describes a causal claim pressing at the point of
departure. It does not say that every accumulated act blocks attainment,
or that these obstructions must always succeed.

## 5. Organon Contact Point — Project Reconstruction

The source gives a useful way to model an agent's transition:

```text
karma with outstanding maturation in domain D
    + Path transition beyond D
    → acute obstruction at that threshold
```

This is an Organon abstraction from the three cases and the creditor
simile. The Bhāṣya's own terms are `karma`, `vipākabhūmi`, `prāpti`, and
`vighna`.
