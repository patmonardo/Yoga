# VAK_4.04 — Is Avijñapti a Distinct Dharma?

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> त्रिविधामलरूपोक्तिवृद्ध्यकुर्वत्पथादिभिः ।
>
> क्षणादूर्ध्वमविज्ञप्तिः कामाप्तातीतभूतजा ॥ ४.४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> trividhāmalarūpoktivṛddhyakurvatpathādibhiḥ /
>
> kṣaṇād ūrdhvam avijñaptiḥ kāmāptātītabhūtajā // 4.4 //

The first pāda is a compressed Vaibhāṣika proof-dossier for the real existence
of `avijñapti`. It invokes scriptural classifications of Form, the continuing
increase of merit, the completion of an action-path by one not personally
performing the final deed, and further related grounds. The Bhāṣya then answers
this dossier at length from Sautrāntika and Yogācāra positions.

The second pāda turns from proof to material genealogy. It specifies how
desire-domain `avijñapti` is generated after its first moment.

The first compound does not mean one undifferentiated “threefold pure Form.”
The Bhāṣya separates two scriptural claims:

```text
trividha-rūpa-ukti
    teaching of three kinds of Form

amala / anāsrava-rūpa-ukti
    teaching of uncontaminated Form
```

The final compound is likewise commentary-resolved:

```text
kāmāpta avijñapti
    desire-domain avijñapti

atīta-mahābhūta-jā
    generated from past Great Elements
```

## 4. Grammar

The instrumental plural ending governs the compressed proof-list:

```text
trividha-rūpa-uktyā
amala-rūpa-uktyā
vṛddhyā
akurvat-patha-ādinā

by the teaching of threefold Form,
by the teaching of uncontaminated Form,
by increase,
by the action-path of one not acting personally,
and by further grounds
```

The Kārikā leaves the conclusion unstated. The Bhāṣya supplies the governing
question and Vaibhāṣika answer:

```text
kathaṃ jñāyate [avijñaptiḥ asti]?
    how is it known [that avijñapti exists]?
```

The second pāda is a nominal determination:

```text
kṣaṇāt ūrdhvam
    avijñaptiḥ kāmāptā
    atīta-bhūta-jā

beyond the [first] moment,
    desire-domain avijñapti
    is born from past Great Elements
```

`Prathamāt`, “after the first,” and `mahābhūtāni`, “Great Elements,” are
Bhāṣya specifications rather than words displayed in the Kārikā.

The Bhāṣya further distinguishes two causal roles:

```text
past mahābhūtas
    āśraya
    producing basis of pravṛtti, initiation

present bodily mahābhūtas
    saṃniśraya
    sustaining support of anuvṛtti, continuation
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> [Its existence is argued] from the teaching of threefold and uncontaminated
> Form, from increase, from the action-path of one not personally acting, and
> from further grounds. Beyond the first moment, desire-domain `avijñapti` is
> born from past Great Elements.

The bracketed conclusion, the separation of the two `rūpa` teachings, the
reference of `patha` to an action-path, and the expansion of `bhūta` to great
material determinations are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Vaibhāṣikas argue for a distinct real Form Dharma from
> the scriptural teaching of three classes of Form and uncontaminated Form,
> from the continued increase of merit, from completion of an action-course
> when its instigator does not personally perform the final deed, and from
> further considerations such as restraint and the completeness of the Noble
> Eightfold Path. Beyond its first moment, desire-domain `avijñapti` is
> generated from past Great Elements as its producing basis, while present
> bodily Great Elements supply its continuing support.

The attribution to the Vaibhāṣikas, the further examples, and the distinction
between producing and sustaining material bases are commentary determinations.

## 6. Bhāṣya-Based Synthesis

The first pāda records the Vaibhāṣika grounds for affirming `avijñapti`;
the Bhāṣya then gives the Sautrāntika replies and their account of continuum
transformation. The second pāda states the Vaibhāṣika account of the
desire-realm `avijñapti`: past Great Elements produce it, while present
bodily Great Elements support its continuation. The cited Path and
restraint examples test how the schools explain conduct when no corresponding
bodily or verbal action is presently performed.

`Saṃtati-pariṇāma-viśeṣa` is not a denial of real consequence. It says:

```text
the prior action does not remain numerically identical;

the continuum is now differently capable
because that action occurred.
```

The Vaibhāṣika model is also causally articulated rather than inert: the past
producing basis and present sustaining support perform different functions.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The common problem**

```text
manifest action
    occurs and perishes

yet:
    merit can increase
    commissioned action can become a completed karmapatha
    restraint can remain effective
    the Noble Path can remain eightfold during samāpatti
    future fruit can arise

therefore:
    present manifestation does not exhaust karmic determination
```

Both schools accept this problem. They disagree about its middle term.

**Vaibhāṣika inference**

```text
continuing efficacy beyond manifestation
    requires a continuing karmic determination

that determination
    = avijñapti-rūpa
    = dravyāntara
    = distinct real non-manifest material dharma
```

The proof is cumulative rather than resting on one example:

```text
scriptural Form classifications
    + merit-growth
    + commissioned karmapatha
    + Dharma-domain classification
    + eightfold Path completeness
    + prātimokṣa restraint
    + restraint as barrier
        → avijñapti exists
```

**Sautrāntika counter-model**

The Sautrāntika does not infer a separate bearer:

```text
cetanā / completed activity
    → cultivates the same continuum
        → subtle specific transformation
            → altered capacity for future fruit and conduct
```

For giving:

```text
dāna-cetanā
    → cultivated donor-continuum

recipient benefits from gift
    → further subtle transformation

transformed continuum
    → increased future-result capacity
```

For commissioned action:

```text
instigator's undertaking
    → another completes the deed
        → instigator-continuum is transformed
            → completed karmapatha by causal designation
```

The Sautrāntika objection is not that `avijñapti` is invisible. The objection
is that a new dharma separate from the operative psycho-physical continuum is
introduced where transformation of that continuum already explains the
result.

**The Eightfold Path test**

The Vaibhāṣika argues:

```text
during samāpatti:
    no present right speech
    no present right bodily action
    no present right livelihood

without avijñapti:
    only five Path-members would remain

but the Noble Path is eightfold

therefore:
    the three practical members exist as avijñapti
```

The Sautrāntika replies:

```text
entry into the Path
    → acquisition of transformed āśaya and āśraya

after emergence:
    wrong speech/action/livelihood no longer proceed
    right speech/action/livelihood proceed

therefore:
    the three names apply through their causal sign
    without three presently operative hidden entities
```

This is a contest between two accounts of Path-completeness:

```text
simultaneous possession of eight entities
        ↔
one achieved Path whose transformation governs all eight functions
```

**Restraint as a falsifiable efficacy**

The Sautrāntika adds a practical test:

```text
if avijñapti automatically blocked misconduct,
    loss of recollection could not lead to violation

but training rules can be broken when recollection is lost

therefore restraint operates through
    undertaking + recollection + habituated disposition,
not necessarily through an automatic material barrier
```

**Vaibhāṣika material mechanics**

After asserting the real entity, the Vaibhāṣika distinguishes its causes:

```text
first moment:
    initiation of avijñapti

beyond the first moment:
    past mahābhūtas = producing āśraya
    present bodily mahābhūtas = sustaining saṃniśraya
```

The Bhāṣya's wheel analogy gives the relation:

```text
hand-impulse
    initiates rotation

ground
    supports continued rolling
```

Production and maintenance must not be flattened into one causal function.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya presents two explanations of continuity. The Vaibhāṣikas defend
`avijñapti` as a distinct real Form Dharma; the Sautrāntikas explain the
cited cases through a specific transformation of the continuum. The Path,
restraint, merit, and commissioned-action examples are arguments within this
dispute, not a general claim that one account applies to every kind of action.
