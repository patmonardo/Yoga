# VAK_4.15 — Four Restraint Statuses by Scope of Abstention

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
पञ्चाष्टदशसर्वेभ्यो वर्ज्येभ्यो विरतिग्रहात् ।
उपासकोपवासस्थश्रमणोद्देशभिक्षुता ॥ ४.१५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
pañcāṣṭadaśasarvebhyo varjyebhyo viratigrahāt /
upāsakopavāsasthaśramaṇoddeśabhikṣutā // 4.15 //
```

VAK 4.14 distinguished eight disciplinary names from four restraint-types.
This verse identifies those four by the scope of abstention undertaken:
five, eight, ten, or all of the prohibited actions. The Bhāṣya supplies
the respective mapping and the contents of each scope.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| pañca | numeral | five |
| aṣṭa | numeral | eight |
| daśa | numeral | ten |
| sarvebhyaḥ | ablative plural | from all |
| varjyebhyaḥ | ablative plural adjective | from the things to be avoided |
| virati-grahāt | ablative singular compound | through undertaking abstention |
| upāsaka | compound member | lay-follower status |
| upavāsa-stha | compound member | one established in the *upavāsa* observance |
| śramaṇa-uddeśa | compound member | novice designation |
| bhikṣutā | feminine abstract noun | monk status |

The Bhāṣya specifies that the four ranges and statuses correspond
respectively. It uses *virati-samādāna*, “undertaking abstention,” to
explain the kārikā's *virati-graha*.

## 4. Grammar

The coordinated numerals `pañca`, `aṣṭa`, `daśa`, and `sarvebhyaḥ` qualify `varjyebhyaḥ`; `virati-grahāt` gives the undertaking. The compound in the second pāda places the four status designations in corresponding order.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Through undertaking abstention from five, eight, ten, or all things to
> be avoided, there is, respectively, lay-follower status, the status of
> one established in *upavāsa*, novice designation, and monk status.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> By undertaking abstention from five, eight, ten, or all things to be
> avoided, one respectively becomes a lay follower, an observer of
> *upavāsa*, a novice, or a monk.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Through undertaking abstention from five, eight, ten, or all things to be avoided, there is, respectively, lay-follower status, the status of one established in upavāsa, novice designation, and monk status.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

## 8. Logical Determination

Five / eight / ten / all things to be avoided → the four restraint statuses
in the listed order. The eightfold observance groups adornment with
entertainment; the novice count separates them and adds the refusal of
gold and silver.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya specifies the respective order and enumeration. In the eightfold undertaking, adornment is grouped with entertainment; the novice's tenfold count separates them and adds abstention from accepting gold and silver. The Bhāṣya calls the complete undertaking *prātimokṣa* restraint.

### Textual Notes

The Bhāṣya unit runs from [206|22] through [207|07] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The next kārikā begins at [207|08].

The prose witness reads `pañccabhyo` at [206|25], retained in the
continuous Sanskrit text; the kārikā has `pañca`. The list at [207|03–06]
is densely compounded. Its category divisions are provisional and follow
the stated totals and the Bhāṣya's instruction that adornment and
entertainment be counted separately for the tenfold undertaking.

### Interpretation

The verse makes undertaken abstention—not the status-name alone—the
organizing principle of the fourfold scheme. Its counts describe distinct
scopes of practical discipline, including the Bhāṣya's regrouping of
adornment and entertainment in the novice count.

In the Interpretation, this gives practical Cetanā a specified field of
restraint: what is undertaken and the range of action from which one
abstains. This is an Organon application of the Bhāṣya's explicit focus
on *virati-samādāna*.
