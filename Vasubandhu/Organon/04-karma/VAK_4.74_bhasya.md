# VAK 4.74 Bhāṣya — Governed Relation and Semantic Action

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
agamyagamanaṃ kāmamithyācāraścaturvidhaḥ /
anyathāsaṃjñino vākyamarthābhijñe mṛṣāvacaḥ // 4.74 //
```

> Sexual misconduct is fourfold approach to what is not to be approached.
> False speech is the statement of one whose recognition is otherwise,
> directed to one capable of understanding its meaning.

The two definitions determine bodily and verbal conduct through complete
relations. The first classifies sexuality through person, bodily locus,
place, and time. The second constitutes false speech through recognition,
statement, and recipient-side understanding.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Sexual misconduct is fourfold approach to what is not to be approached.”
Sexual approach to what is not to be approached has four forms. One may
approach an impermissible person: someone committed to another, one's
mother or daughter, or a maternal or paternal relative. One may approach
through an impermissible bodily locus, even with one's own spouse. One may
act in an impermissible place, such as openly, at a sanctuary, or in a
monastery. Or one may act at an impermissible time.

What is an impermissible time? The commentary lists pregnancy, nursing, or
being under an observance. Some restrict the last case to an observance
undertaken with the husband's permission. The inherited condition “without
mistake” continues to apply.

If someone approaches another's spouse while recognizing that person as
their own spouse, the completed course does not arise. If someone approaches
one person while recognizing that person as another's spouse, one opinion
holds that the course arises because there is preparation directed toward
another's spouse and use of the relevant basis. Others hold that it does
not arise because the preparation was directed elsewhere, as in the case
of killing.

When someone approaches a nun, against whom is the sexual misconduct
committed? The Bhāṣya answers: against the lord of the territory, because
he cannot tolerate it. Yet she is under an observance and is therefore not
to be approached by him either. In the case of an unmarried young woman,
the relevant relation is to the person to whom she has been given; if she
has not been given, to her protector, ultimately the king. These examples
record the guardianship and disciplinary assumptions of the received
social classification.

“False speech is the statement of one whose recognition is otherwise,
directed to one capable of understanding its meaning.” If a speaker
recognizes the matter otherwise than the meaning stated, and the person
about whom or to whom the statement is made understands its meaning, that
statement is false speech.

What if the recipient does not understand? It is disordered speech. The
verse says “statement.” Since a statement can consist of many phonetic
units, which one bears the course of karma? The final unit, together with
the non-manifest determination, constitutes the course at the point where
the recipient understands the meaning. The preceding phonetic units are
preparation.

The expression “one understanding the meaning” raises a further question.
Does it mean a hearer who has actually understood the meaning, or one
capable of understanding it? What follows from the distinction? If it means
one who has already understood, then because sentence-meaning is an object
of mental consciousness, and because the verbal manifestation has ceased
together with auditory consciousness, only the non-manifest action would
constitute the principal course. If it means someone capable of
understanding, this fault does not arise.

But how can someone be called capable of understanding merely when auditory
consciousness has arisen? The Bhāṣya closes: let the interpretation be
whatever avoids the fault. The timing problem is thus exposed but not
fully resolved in this unit.

## 4. Four Coordinates of Sexual Misconduct

The fourfold classification is:

```text
agamya
    prohibited person or governing relation

anaṅga
    prohibited bodily locus

adeśa
    prohibited place

akāla
    prohibited time
```

The same generic bodily activity receives different practical
determinations according to its complete relational field. `Agamya` should
not be treated as an intrinsic property of a person. In this source it is a
status produced by kinship, commitment, guardianship, discipline, or other
governing conditions.

The examples also disclose the historical limits of that field. They
presuppose patriarchal guardianship and jurisdictional relations that the
study records as Bhāṣya doctrine rather than silently converting into
present norms. The durable technical contribution is the requirement that
an act be classified across interpersonal, embodied, spatial, temporal,
and recognitional coordinates.

The mistaken-object case remains disputed:

```text
prohibited actual basis recognized as permitted
    → no completed course

different actual basis recognized as prohibited
    → view one: course completed through intention and use
    → view two: preparation only because the intended basis differs
```

The commentary reports both positions without settling the second case.

## 5. False Speech as Semantic Action

The completed course has three principal poles:

```text
speaker's recognition
    ≠
asserted meaning
    →
recipient capable of understanding that meaning
```

This distinguishes lying from factual error. A speaker who states what they
recognize, even when that recognition is mistaken, does not satisfy the
definition given here. False speech requires a deliberate divergence between
recognition and assertion.

The recipient is constitutive rather than incidental. Without uptake, the
utterance does not reach completion as false speech and is classified here
as disordered speech. Verbal karma is therefore semantic action performed
within a relation, not acoustic production considered by itself.

The analysis of a multi-unit statement repeats the three-phase structure:

```text
earlier phonetic units
    = preparation

meaning-completing final unit + avijñapti
    = principal course
```

Yet the source immediately recognizes a difficulty. Auditory consciousness
apprehends sound; mental consciousness apprehends the sentence meaning. By
the time semantic comprehension occurs, the manifest speech-event may have
ceased. The proposal that the recipient need only be capable of
understanding protects a manifest component at completion, but the Bhāṣya
does not fully explain that capacity relation here.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

VAK 4.74 provides a powerful model for the VAK LogicSet. A verbal course
cannot be classified from message content alone:

```text
<Speaker Recognition,
 Asserted Meaning,
 Linguistic Sequence,
 Recipient Capacity,
 Recipient Uptake,
 Completion Point>
```

The `nāmakāya` basis from VAK 4.71 is now active. A statement is a configured
semantic whole whose earlier elements prepare a meaning that becomes
practically effective in reception. This is why a language model's output
cannot be judged solely as a sequence of tokens: the course belongs to the
relation between recognition, asserted Form, intended recipient, and uptake.

For applied Science of Knowing, false speech is a failure of semantic
copulation: the speaker knowingly joins a subject and determination contrary
to their own recognition and succeeds in presenting that unity to another.
This Organon formulation extends the Bhāṣya's schema; it does not replace
the historical definitions and examples.
