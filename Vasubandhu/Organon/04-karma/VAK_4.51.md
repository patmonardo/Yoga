# VAK_4.51

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
चतुष्कोटिकमित्यन्ये निकायाक्षेपणं त्रिभिः ।
सर्वत्र चतुराक्षेपः शुभस्य नरके त्रिधा ॥ ४.५१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
catuṣkoṭikam ity anye nikāyākṣepaṇaṃ tribhiḥ /
sarvatra catur ākṣepaḥ śubhasya narake tridhā // 4.51 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
catuḥ-koṭikam iti anye nikāya-ākṣepaṇam tribhiḥ /
sarvatra catuḥ-ākṣepaḥ śubhasya narake tridhā //
```

The Bhāṣya identifies the two axes of the four-corner analysis as fixity of
temporal stage and fixity of maturation.

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `catuṣkoṭikam` | nominative/accusative neuter singular | having four alternatives; four-cornered |
| `iti` | quotative particle | thus |
| `anye` | nominative masculine plural | others; here the Dārṣṭāntikas |
| `nikāyākṣepaṇam` | nominative/accusative neuter singular compound | projection of a rebirth-continuum |
| `tribhiḥ` | instrumental plural | by three [kinds of karma] |
| `sarvatra` | adverb | everywhere; throughout the realms and destinies |
| `caturākṣepaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | occurrence or accumulation of all four |
| `śubhasya` | genitive neuter singular | of wholesome karma |
| `narake` | locative masculine singular | in hell |
| `tridhā` | adverb | threefold |

**`catuṣkoṭika`**

The four alternatives are generated by crossing two questions:

```text
avasthā-niyata
    is the temporal stage fixed?

vipāka-niyata
    is maturation itself fixed?
```

**`nikāyasabhāga`**

The Bhāṣya uses `nikāyasabhāga` for the homogeneous aggregate-continuum or
class-continuity established in rebirth. No permanent bearer is implied.

**`ākṣepaṇa`**

Here it is the casting forth or projection of a future rebirth-continuum. The
later projecting/completing distinction should not be imported before the
text introduces it more fully.

## 4. Grammar

**The four-corner position**

```text
anye
[karma] catuṣkoṭikam [kurvanti]
iti
```

> Others analyze karma through four alternatives.

The Bhāṣya identifies these others as the Dārṣṭāntikas and supplies the
omitted analytic content.

**Projection by three temporal kinds**

```text
nikāya-ākṣepaṇam
tribhiḥ [karmabhiḥ bhavati]
```

> A rebirth-continuum is projected by three [of the four temporal kinds].

Present-life-result karma is excluded because its fruit occurs within the
already established life.

**General rule and exception**

```text
sarvatra catur-ākṣepaḥ

śubhasya tu
narake tridhā [ākṣepaḥ]
```

> In general all four kinds occur throughout; but in hell wholesome karma is
> threefold.

The Bhāṣya marks the first as the general rule and the second as its exception.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Others analyze karma through four alternatives. A rebirth-continuum is
> projected by three [of the four temporal kinds]. In general all four kinds
> occur throughout; in hell wholesome karma is threefold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Dārṣṭāntikas analyze karma through four alternatives by distinguishing
> whether its temporal stage is fixed and whether maturation itself is fixed.
> Of the four temporal kinds, three can project a future rebirth-continuum;
> present-life-result karma cannot, since it fruits within the life already
> established. All four kinds may generally occur throughout the three domains
> and destinies as appropriate. In hell, however, wholesome karma is only
> threefold because no desirable present-life maturation occurs there.

The identity of the two axes, exclusion of present-life karma from projection,
and explanation of the hell exception are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Karmic determination is not exhausted by a single opposition between fixed
> and unfixed. The necessity of fruition and the necessity of its temporal
> placement are orthogonal: a karma may be fixed in one respect and unfixed in
> the other. Its causal function must then be distinguished again, because
> producing a result within the present continuum is not the same as projecting
> a future continuum. Modality, temporal index, and world-projecting function
> are distinct determinations of one practical field.

The four corners express structured alternatives, not skepticism about whether
anything can be determined.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`avasthā-niyata`**

Fixed with respect to temporal stage: present life, next life, or a later life.

**`vipāka-niyata`**

Fixed with respect to whether maturation must occur.

**The Dārṣṭāntika four corners**

| | Maturation fixed | Maturation unfixed |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal stage fixed | both fixed | stage fixed only |
| Temporal stage unfixed | maturation fixed only | neither fixed |

This yields four relations, not four arbitrary verbal possibilities.

**Eightfold consequence**

The Dārṣṭāntikas allow each of the four temporal classes to be fixed or unfixed
with respect to maturation, yielding eight karmic types.

**`nikāyākṣepaṇa`**

```text
present-life-result karma
    → fruit within existing continuum
    → does not project next rebirth-continuum

other three temporal kinds
    → can project future nikāyasabhāga
```

**Four karmas in one moment**

The Bhāṣya gives a compressed example: one instigates three other persons and
personally undertakes a fourth act; if all four actions reach completion
simultaneously, four karmic types can be accumulated in one moment.

**General rule and hell exception**

```text
all three dhātus and all destinies
    → four kinds occur as appropriate

hell
    → wholesome karma lacks present-life-result form
    → only three wholesome temporal kinds
```

## 8. Logical Determination

**Orthogonal modal axes**

```text
WHEN_FIXED(K)
    does not entail
WHETHER_FIXED(K)

WHETHER_FIXED(K)
    does not entail
WHEN_FIXED(K)
```

The four-corner analysis is the cross-product of these independent
determinations.

**Courtroom form of the distinction**

```text
whether the case must reach judgment
    = maturation fixity

when the case is scheduled
    = temporal-stage fixity
```

A fixed schedule does not logically guarantee adjudication under the
Dārṣṭāntika analysis, nor does necessary adjudication by itself fix the date.

**Present fruition versus future projection**

```text
RESULT_WITHIN(current continuum)
    ≠
PROJECTS(future continuum)
```

This is a functional distinction, not merely another temporal label.

**Destination constrains available karma**

```text
hell lacks desirable present-life maturation
    → wholesome present-life-result karma unavailable there
```

The concrete structure of the destination restricts the applicable class.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Degrees of karmic actuality**

The courtroom analogy becomes exact only after distinguishing:

```text
an action has generated karma
the karma must mature
the time of maturation is fixed
the karma projects a future continuum
```

None of these propositions can be substituted automatically for another.

**Projection is stronger than delayed consequence**

`Nikāyākṣepaṇa` means that karma can establish the rebirth-continuum within
which later experience occurs. It is not merely an isolated effect appearing
at a later date. Yet this verse makes only the narrow claim that three temporal
types can perform that function.

**Dhātu and gati constrain application**

The classification is tested across all domains and destinies, then qualified
by the affective possibilities of hell. Universal-looking classifications are
again mediated by the concrete field in which they are applied.

**No permanent defendant**

The projected `nikāyasabhāga` is causal class-continuity, not the transportation
of an unchanged person into another courtroom. Karma connects continuums
without requiring a permanent self beneath them.

This is the Organon contact. The Dārṣṭāntika matrix, single-moment example,
projection rule, distribution, and hell exception belong to the Bhāṣya.
