# VAK_4.72

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
समं प्राक् च मृतस्यास्ति न मौलोऽन्याश्रयोदयात् ।
सेनादिष्वेककार्यत्वात् सर्वे कर्तृवदन्विताः ॥ ४.७२ ॥
```

**The two attribution problems**

```text
one lethal project across a transition of support
    diachronic attribution

one lethal project distributed across many participants
    collective attribution
```

The first half contracts attribution across time; the second extends it across
participants united in one undertaking.

## 4. Grammar

**Death before or simultaneously with the victim**

```text
samam [mṛtasya]
prāk ca mṛtasya
    asti na maulaḥ
```

> For one who has died simultaneously or beforehand, there is no principal
> karmic course.

The Bhāṣya controls the placement of the negation: `asti na maulaḥ` must be
read “there is no principal course,” not “the principal course exists.” The
comparison is with the victim's death: the instigator dies at the same time as
the victim or before the victim.

**Reason from support-transition**

```text
anya-āśraya-udayāt
    [maulaḥ na asti]
```

> There is no principal course because another support arises.

The ablative gives the reason. The support that undertook preparation has
ceased; a later support was not the preparatory instigator.

**Collective implication**

```text
senā-ādiṣu
    eka-kāryatvāt
    sarve
    kartṛ-vat anvitāḥ [bhavanti]
```

> In armies and similar collectives, because there is one common undertaking,
> all are connected with the act like the immediate doer.

The locative establishes the collective case; the ablative states the ground
of shared attribution.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For one who has died simultaneously or beforehand, there is no principal
> course, because another support arises. In armies and similar collectives,
> because there is one common undertaking, all are connected like the doer.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> If one arranges another's death but dies before the victim or simultaneously
> with the victim, one does not acquire the principal karmic course of killing.
> Until the victim dies, principal completion has not occurred; once the
> preparatory support has ceased, the later support that arises did not perform
> that preparation, and the completed course is not transferred to it. By
> contrast, when members of an army, hunting party, or raiding force jointly
> undertake a killing-project, all are karmically connected with the killing
> like its immediate performer because they share one practical operation and
> function as mutual instigators.

The arranged-killing scenario, the later-support reasoning, the collective
examples, and mutual-instigation analysis are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Causal efficacy by itself does not determine who bears the principal karmic
> completion. Across time, attribution is restricted when the support that
> initiated the project has ceased before the defining result occurs. Across
> persons, attribution is extended when multiple participants constitute one
> common undertaking. Practical unity is therefore neither permanent
> substance nor isolated bodily execution: it is the determinate relation of
> a bearer or participants to the process that reaches completion.

The verse coordinates two apparently opposed results:

```text
support-difference
    can break attribution

unity of undertaking
    can extend attribution
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Maula karmapatha` — principal karmic course**

The completed course distinguished from preparation and aftermath. For
killing, the Bhāṣya locates principal completion at the victim's death, not at
the prior intention, command, assault, or other causal preparation.

**`Āśraya` — support**

The psycho-physical support through which the karmic process is undertaken and
borne. It should not be reduced to a permanent agent or merely to the physical
body.

**`Anyāśraya` — another support**

The support arising after the former support has ceased. The Bhāṣya describes
it as belonging to another `nikāyasabhāga`; it is not the support that carried
out the preparation.

**`Nikāyasabhāga` — membership-series**

The class-continuity belonging to a particular form of rebirth. Its mention
marks the difference between the support that initiated the lethal project
and the later support arising after death.

**`Ekakāryatva` — unity of undertaking**

Having one common practical operation. `Kārya` here should not be flattened
into a merely external result: the participants jointly set out for the
purpose of killing.

**`Kartṛ` — doer**

The person who immediately performs the lethal action. VAK 4.72 denies that
immediate physical performance exhausts the field of responsible agency.

**`Prayoktṛ` — instigator**

One who sets the action in motion or causes it to be undertaken. In the
collective case, the Bhāṣya says the participants are effectively mutual
instigators.

**`Samanvāgata` / `anvita` — connected with**

Karmically possessing or being connected with the determination. It states
who bears the principal course without requiring that each person execute the
same bodily movement.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Completion condition for killing**

```text
intention / arrangement / assault
    = preparation

victim remains alive
    = no principal killing-course yet

victim dies
    = defining completion-event
```

The result-event is necessary, but it does not alone settle attribution.

**Diachronic restriction**

```text
support A
    undertakes preparation
        ↓
support A ceases before or with victim's death
        ↓
support B arises
        ↓
support B did not undertake the preparation
        ↓
principal killing-course is not transferred to B
```

Thus:

```text
continuation of causal result
    ≠
continuation of the bearer of principal completion
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly allows the result to be fulfilled even though the former
instigator is not touched by the completed fault of killing.

**Synchronic extension**

```text
many participants
    + shared intention toward killing
    + one common undertaking
    + one immediate executor
        ↓
all connected like the executor
```

The collective is not posited as a separate super-agent. Attribution is
distributed through reciprocal practical instigation.

**The coercion qualification**

The Bhāṣya says that someone forcibly taken along is ordinarily connected with
the undertaking. It recognizes an exception for one who goes with the firm
resolution not to kill a living being even to save their own life. This is a
commentarial determination of practical non-participation, not wording in the
Kārikā.

```text
physical presence under force
    does not by itself prove practical refusal

settled refusal of the killing-project
    breaks unity with that undertaking
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Agency is not identical with physical execution**

The verse refuses a “whose hand did it?” reduction. In a shared operation,
planning, authorization, coordination, and mutual instigation can unite many
participants with the completed course even when one supplies the fatal
movement.

**Causation and attribution remain distinct**

The death of the initiating support does not erase the causal efficacy of the
arrangement: the victim may still die. Yet the principal course does not
simply migrate along the causal chain into a new support. The system therefore
records both:

```text
what caused the result
who bears the completed karmapatha
```

These questions intersect without being identical.

**Two forms of practical unity**

```text
unity through time
    depends upon continuity of the relevant support through completion

unity across persons
    depends upon participation in one common undertaking
```

This yields distributed responsibility without introducing a permanent agent
or substantial collective self.

This is the Organon contact. The temporal rule, support analysis, army and
related examples, mutual instigation, and stringent refusal exception belong
to the Bhāṣya; the formulation in terms of two forms of Practical Unity is the
project synthesis.
