# VAK 4.84 Bhāṣya — The Seven Wholesome Bodily and Verbal Courses of Action

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ārūpyāsaṃjñisattveṣu lābhataḥ sapta śeṣite /
saṃmukhībhāvataś cāpi hitvā sanarakān kurūn // 4.84 //
```

> In the Formless Realm and among non-percipient beings, the seven wholesome
> bodily and verbal Courses of Action occur by acquisition. In the remainder
> they also occur by present manifestation, excluding hell beings and the
> Kurus.

The three wholesome mental Courses of Action were distributed in 4.83. The
“seven” are the wholesome bodily and verbal Courses of Action: the seven
abstentions corresponding to the three bodily and four verbal unwholesome
Courses of Action.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

```iast
ārūpyāsaṃjñisattveṣu lābhataḥ sapta

kāyikavācikāḥ sapta kuśalā ārūpyeṣv asaṃjñisattveṣu samanvāgamata eva.
ārūpyopapannānām āryāṇām atītānāgatānāsravasaṃvarasamanvāgamāt,
asaṃjñisattvānāṃ ca dhyānasaṃvarasamanvāgamāt.

yadbhūmyāśrayeṇāryeṇānāsravaṃ śīlam utpāditaṃ nirodhitaṃ bhavati,
tenārūpyeṣv atītena samanvāgato bhavati. pañcabhūmyāśrayeṇa tv anāgatena.

śeṣite /

saṃmukhībhāvataś cāpi hitvā sanarakān kurūn // 4.84 //

śeṣaḥ kṛtaḥ śeṣitaḥ. yo 'nyo dhātuḥ śeṣito gatir vā, tatrāite sapta
kuśalāḥ karmapathāḥ saṃmukhībhāvato 'pi saṃvidyante 'nyatra
narakottarakurubhyaḥ.

te punas tiryakpreteṣu saṃvaranirmuktāḥ, rūpadhātau tu
saṃvarasaṃgṛhītāḥ, anyatrobhayathā.

ta ete daśākuśalāḥ karmapathāḥ kuśalāś ca—
```

The final phrase begins the transition to 4.85 and grammatically gathers the
ten unwholesome and ten wholesome Courses of Action for the coming analysis
of their results. It is included because it closes the distribution just
completed; the new predicate concerning results begins with the next kārikā.

The running witness has `ārūpyoopapannānām` at `[253|14–16]`, an evident
vowel duplication normalized above as `ārūpyopapannānām`. It also prints
`śesaḥ` at `[253|21]`; the expected form is `śeṣaḥ`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

In the Formless Realm and among non-percipient beings, the seven wholesome
bodily and verbal Courses of Action exist only through endowment.

For noble beings reborn in the Formless Realm, this is because they are
endowed with past and future without-Influx restraint. Non-percipient beings
are endowed with concentration-born restraint.

When a noble being has produced and then ceased without-Influx restraint in
dependence on a particular ground, that being, while in the Formless Realm,
remains endowed with the past restraint associated with that ground. Future
restraint is endowed in dependence upon five grounds.

“In the remainder” means that a remainder has been made: whichever other
realm or destiny remains. There the seven wholesome Courses of Action also
exist by present manifestation, except among hell beings and in Uttarakuru.

Among animals and hungry ghosts these seven are independent of formal
restraint; in the Form Realm they are included within restraint; elsewhere
they occur in both ways. These, then, are the ten unwholesome and ten
wholesome Courses of Action whose results will next be explained.

## 4. What the Seven Are

The seven are the bodily and verbal abstentions:

1. abstention from killing,
2. abstention from taking what is not given,
3. abstention from sexual misconduct,
4. abstention from false speech,
5. abstention from divisive speech,
6. abstention from harsh speech,
7. abstention from frivolous or disconnected speech.

Unlike the three wholesome mental Courses of Action, these seven have bodily
or verbal modes. Their present manifestation ordinarily requires bodily or
verbal support. This makes their distribution narrower than the universal
distribution stated at the end of 4.83.

## 5. Endowment Without Present Manifestation

The Formless Realm supplies no body or speech through which the seven could
presently operate. For non-percipient beings, the Bhāṣya likewise specifies
endowment rather than present bodily or verbal enactment. In both cases the
seven belong to a continuum through `samanvāgama`.

```text
continuum is endowed with restraint
    while
conditions for its present exercise are absent
```

For noble beings in the Formless Realm, the endowment specifically concerns
restraint classified as without-Influx across temporal modes. For
non-percipient beings, it derives from concentration-born restraint. Thus
“not now manifest” does not mean that
the restraint has no standing in the continuum.

## 6. Past and Future Without-Influx Restraint

The temporal qualification is exact. Past endowment corresponds to the
ground upon which the noble being actually produced and ceased the
without-Influx restraint. Future endowment extends across five supporting
grounds.

```text
past restraint
    indexed to the ground of actual prior production

future restraint
    available in relation to five supporting grounds
```

The passage therefore combines three coordinates:

```text
Course of Action × temporal mode × supporting ground
```

The present formless existence does not erase the restraint's past and future
relations merely because it prevents current bodily and verbal manifestation.
This is a technical Sarvāstivāda account of temporal endowment; it should not
be silently replaced by a modern memory or disposition theory.

## 7. Manifestation in the Remaining Fields

`Śeṣita` is explained directly: the text has made a remainder by removing the
cases already treated. In every remaining suitable realm or destination, the
seven can also become presently manifest, with two exceptions:

```text
hells       — excluded
Uttarakuru  — excluded
```

The mode of their manifestation then differs:

| Realm or destination | Status of the seven when manifest |
|---|---|
| animals and hungry ghosts | independent of formal restraint |
| Form Realm | included within restraint |
| other remaining cases | either within restraint or independent of it |

Abstaining from an act and possessing a formally constituted restraint are
therefore not identical. A wholesome Course of Action can occur without the continuing
juridical structure of `saṃvara`; in other environments its manifestation is
precisely an expression of that structure.

## 8. The Completed Distribution

VAK 4.82–4.84 now yield a layered classification:

```text
10 unwholesome Courses of Action
    classified by destiny,
    endowment,
    manifestation,
    and non-restraint

10 wholesome Courses of Action
    3 mental Courses of Action distributed universally
    7 bodily/verbal Courses of Action distributed by support and restraint
```

The classification is not a mere list of moral predicates. It determines
where each Course of Action can belong, where it can appear, and under what practical
status its appearance occurs.

## 9. Interpretation

This distribution distinguishes a Course of Action's standing in a continuum
from its present execution:

```text
<Endowment : Manifestation>
    =
<operative standing : present execution>
```

The realm-and-destination distribution does not create the Course-of-Action
type; it conditions whether bodily or verbal manifestation is available and
how that manifestation relates to formal restraint. Endowment is not a
present deed, but neither is it an inert object hidden inside an agent.

The Bhāṣya's account further indexes endowment to temporal mode and
supporting ground. Thus its practical structure is not a simple agent-to-act
sequence:

```text
RestraintStanding {
    courseOfAction
    continuum
    temporalMode
    supportingGround
    formalStatus
    manifestableHere
}
```

Within the Bhava Chakra, realm and destination condition the modes in which
wholesome action belongs and manifests. The source's Sarvāstivāda account of
past and future Dharmas and the five supporting grounds remains explicit in
that interpretation.

## 10. Review Status

The complete 4.84 unit `[253|13]–[253|24]` is included. It covers the seven
Courses of Action as endowments in the Formless Realm and among non-percipient
beings, the temporal and ground-indexed account of without-Influx restraint,
manifestation in the remaining fields, and the three restraint statuses. It stops before the 4.85
predicate assigning three kinds of result. Two witness irregularities are
recorded explicitly.
