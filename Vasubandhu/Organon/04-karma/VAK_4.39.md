# VAK_4.39

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
पातनीयेन चेत्येके सद्धर्मान्तर्धितोऽपरे ।
धनर्णवत्तु काश्मीरैरापन्नस्येष्यते द्वयम् ॥ ४.३९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
patanīyena cety eke saddharmāntardhito 'pare /
dhanarṇavat tu kāśmīrair āpannasyeṣyate dvayam // 4.39 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
patanīyena ca iti eke sat-dharma-antardhitaḥ apare /
dhana-ṛṇa-vat tu kāśmīraiḥ āpannasya iṣyate dvayam //
```

The verse reports three positions. It does not identify the Kāśmīra
wealth-and-debt analogy as Vasubandhu’s own final conclusion. The Bhāṣya
subjects that position to sustained criticism.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis


| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `patanīyena` | instrumental neuter singular | through an offense entailing downfall or defeat |
| `ca` | conjunction | also; as an additional cause |
| `iti` | quotative particle | thus; marking the reported view |
| `eke` | nominative masculine plural | some authorities |
| `saddharmāntardhitaḥ` | ablative expression | through disappearance of the true Dharma |
| `apare` | nominative masculine plural | other authorities |
| `dhanarṇavat` | indeclinable comparative compound | like wealth and debt |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but; however |
| `kāśmīraiḥ` | instrumental masculine plural | according to the Kāśmīra authorities |
| `āpannasya` | genitive masculine singular | of one who has incurred the offense |
| `iṣyate` | third-person singular present passive | is maintained; is accepted doctrinally |
| `dvayam` | nominative neuter singular | the pair; both restraint and misconduct |

**`patanīya`**

The Bhāṣya identifies the relevant class as the four root downfall offenses
whose commission is proposed to cause loss of bhikṣu or śrāmaṇera restraint.
The argument later uses the closely related Vinaya term `pārājika`, defeat.

**`saddharmāntardhāna`**

Disappearance of the true Dharma includes disappearance of operative
disciplinary trainings, boundaries, and formal ecclesiastical acts. Some
therefore infer loss of restraint. The Bhāṣya ultimately restricts its effect
to the impossibility of new acquisition.

**`dhana-ṛṇa-vat`**

The analogy permits simultaneous possession:

```text
wealth
    + debt

restraint
    + grave misconduct
```

Debt does not make wealth nonexistent, and wealth does not make debt
nonexistent. The analogy expresses the Kāśmīra theory of coexisting opposed
determinations.

**`dvayam`**

The Bhāṣya makes the pair explicit:

```text
śīla
    discipline or restraint

dauḥśīlya
    misconduct
```

Disclosure of the offense is compared to repayment of debt, after which the
person is again characterized by discipline rather than outstanding fault.

## 4. Grammar

**First reported theory**

```text
patanīyena ca [tyāgaḥ]
iti eke
```

> Some say that abandonment also occurs through a downfall offense.

The noun `tyāgaḥ` is carried forward from VAK 4.38. `Iti` marks the position
as a report.

**Second reported theory**

```text
sat-dharma-antardhitaḥ [tyāgaḥ]
apare
```

> Others [say abandonment occurs] through disappearance of the true Dharma.

The parallel construction carries forward both the topic and the omitted
verb of doctrinal assertion.

**Kāśmīra position**

```text
kāśmīraiḥ tu
āpannasya
dhana-ṛṇa-vat
dvayam iṣyate
```

> The Kāśmīras, however, maintain that the offender possesses both, as [one
> may possess] wealth and debt.

The instrumental `kāśmīraiḥ` marks the authorities by whom the doctrine is
maintained. The genitive `āpannasya` identifies the bearer of the two
determinations.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Some say [prātimokṣa restraint is abandoned] also through a downfall
> offense; others, through disappearance of the true Dharma. The Kāśmīras,
> however, maintain that the offender has both [restraint and misconduct], as
> one may have both wealth and debt.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Some authorities hold that committing one of the four root downfall
> offenses destroys bhikṣu or śrāmaṇera restraint. Others hold that
> prātimokṣa restraint is lost when the true Dharma and its disciplinary
> institutions disappear. The Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣikas reject both simple losses
> and maintain instead that the grave offender still possesses the earlier
> restraint together with the newly incurred misconduct, just as one person
> may possess both wealth and debt.

The identification of the four offenses, the monastic restraint-types, and
the pair as `śīla` and `dauḥśīlya` are supplied by the Bhāṣya. Its subsequent
argument does not leave the Kāśmīra position unchallenged.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A contrary determination need not automatically erase a previously
> acquired practical form; opposition and non-possession are different. Yet
> this coexistence principle has a limit. A grave act may not merely oppose
> one rule within the form: it may destroy a root-constituting condition upon
> which the remainder’s capacity depends. The problem is therefore not
> settled either by simple cancellation or by unrestricted coexistence. One
> must determine whether the violated part is local or constitutive of the
> whole.

The disappearance of an institution introduces a different distinction. The
loss of procedures capable of generating new restraint does not retroactively
destroy restraint already acquired.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Downfall-loss theory**

```text
one of four root offenses
    → complete loss of monastic restraint
```

This position treats the offense as more than a breach under an acknowledged
rule. It destroys the standing disciplinary status.

**Kāśmīra coexistence theory**

The Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣikas argue:

```text
disturbance of one part
    does not justify
loss of the whole restraint
```

Therefore the offender has discipline and misconduct simultaneously. The
continuum is not reduced to a single moral-status flag.

**`āviṣkaraṇa`**

Disclosure or manifestation of the offense is compared to paying a debt. In
the Kāśmīra model, it removes the outstanding characterization by misconduct
without requiring the claim that restraint had vanished and been newly
created.

**Definitive scripture and `abhisāhasa`**

An objection cites the Buddha’s statement that the defeated person becomes
an `aśramaṇa`, an `aśākyaputrīya`, and falls from bhikṣu-status. The Kāśmīra
reply restricts this to `paramārthabhikṣutva`, bhikṣuhood in the highest
sense.

The Bhāṣya calls that reply an `abhisāhasa`, an overbold interpretive move:

```text
nītārtha statement
    definitive in meaning

forced redirection
    made to say something else
```

The criticism is both hermeneutical and practical: such reinterpretation can
provide conditions for misconduct and affliction.

**Four senses of `bhikṣu`**

The Vinaya recognizes several uses of the word. In the present disciplinary
question, the relevant bhikṣu is one fully ordained through the formal act
with the motion as fourth (`jñapticaturthakarmopasaṃpanna`).

The offender was not previously a bhikṣu merely in the special ultimate
sense. Consequently, saying that only ultimate bhikṣuhood was lost does not
answer the Vinaya claim concerning the ordained status at issue.

**The palm-tree analogy**

The counterargument distinguishes two kinds of part:

```text
non-constitutive part damaged
    → whole may continue

root-constituting part severed
    → remainder loses capacity for growth
```

A palm tree cut at the head cannot sprout, develop, or increase. The analogy
answers the claim that disturbance of one part can never destroy the whole.

**`mūlabhūta`**

`Mūlabhūta` means root-constituting or fundamental. A root offense is said to
breach the boundary of bhikṣuhood and, through intense shamelessness, cut the
root of restraint.

```text
maulī āpatti
    → saṃvaramūlaccheda
    → complete abandonment becomes intelligible
```

**Residual designation**

Some scriptural language may continue to call the offender a śramaṇa or
bhikṣu by outward form. The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
name or appearance remains
    ≠ effective capacity remains
```

Images of burnt wood, dried matter, rotten seed, and a dead being express
survival of outward designation after generative capacity has been ruined.

**Disappearance of the true Dharma**

The conclusion is exact:

```text
Vinaya procedures disappear
    → no new acquisition of restraint

restraint already acquired
    → not thereby abandoned
```

Institutional generation and continued possession have different conditions.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Three initial models**

```text
Model A
    root offense → restraint lost

Model B
    true Dharma disappears → restraint lost

Kāśmīra model
    root offense → restraint remains + misconduct acquired
```

The verse reports these models; the Bhāṣya tests their grounds.

**Opposition and cancellation**

```text
contrary conduct
    may negate conformity
    without negating possession
```

This is the truth captured by the wealth-and-debt analogy. It prevents every
failure from being treated as though the practitioner had never acquired a
vow.

**Local breach and root destruction**

```text
one quantitative part
    may be merely local

one qualitative part
    may constitute the root of the whole
```

Therefore “only one part was disturbed” does not settle whether the whole
persists. The logical question is the part’s genetic function.

**Capacity of the remainder**

The palm-tree image shifts the test:

```text
does some remainder exist?
    insufficient question

can the remainder still grow and function as this whole?
    decisive question
```

Residual rules, appearance, or institutional name do not alone demonstrate
continued possession of effective monastic restraint.

**Acquisition condition and persistence condition**

```text
operative true Dharma and Vinaya acts
    condition new acquisition

their later disappearance
    does not automatically negate
an already established restraint
```

The condition that generated a form need not remain a continuously present
condition of its possession.

**Bhāṣya outcome**

The passage does not merely place three equally weighted opinions side by
side. It rejects disappearance of the Dharma as loss of already acquired
restraint and gives powerful scriptural and genetic reasons against the
Kāśmīra claim that even a root downfall leaves the whole restraint intact.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Contradiction can be immanent without being terminal**

The Kāśmīra model captures an important practical truth:

```text
restraint persists
    + contrary act occurs
```

This explains ordinary breach, remorse, disclosure, and restoration without
turning every failure into total loss. The person remains determined by a vow
that current conduct contradicts.

**But not every contradiction is local**

The counterargument introduces the decisive concept of a constitutive part.
A whole cannot be understood as the arithmetic sum of equivalent pieces:

```text
branch
    part whose loss the organism survives

head or root
    part through which the organism remains capable of growth
```

Thus the correct question is not simply whether one rule or several rules
were violated. It is whether the act destroys the generative condition of the
disciplinary form.

**Semantic violence in interpretation**

`Abhisāhasa` is a warning against saving a favored system by relocating the
meaning of a definitive statement. The Kāśmīra distinction between ordinary
and ultimate bhikṣuhood may be intelligible elsewhere, but here it fails to
answer the institutional subject named by the Vinaya.

This is directly relevant to our method: a sophisticated philosophical
distinction cannot be inserted merely because it rescues an interpretation.
The textual subject and level must first warrant it.

**Institution and acquired actuality**

The final result concerning the disappearance of the Dharma is equally
important:

```text
institution loses reproductive capacity
    new status cannot be generated

already instituted determination
    does not vanish retroactively
```

The social condition of genesis and the immanent conditions of persistence
must be separately identified.

This is the Organon contact: coexistence, root-destruction, residual form,
generative capacity, and institutional acquisition. The school positions,
scriptural dispute, and final conclusion belong to the Bhāṣya.
