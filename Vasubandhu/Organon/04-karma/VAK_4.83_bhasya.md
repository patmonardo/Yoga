# VAK 4.83 Bhāṣya — Courses of Action in Uttarakuru and the Desire-Realm

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
saptamaḥ svayam apy atra kāme 'nyatra daśāśubhāḥ /
śubhās trayas tu sarvatra saṃmukhībhāvalābhataḥ // 4.83 //
```

> Here in Uttarakuru, the seventh Course of Action also occurs by present
> manifestation. Elsewhere in the Desire-Realm, all ten unwholesome Courses of
> Action also occur by present manifestation. But the three wholesome mental
> Courses of Action occur everywhere by present manifestation and acquisition.

The verse completes the regional distribution begun in 4.82. `Atra` carries
forward Uttarakuru; `anyatra` then excludes both Uttarakuru and the hells
from its general statement about the rest of the Desire-Realm.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

At `[252|26]` the running witness reads `asaṃbhinnapralāpaḥ`, although the
seventh Course of Action and the context require `saṃbhinnapralāpaḥ`. The normalized
reading is printed above and the witness form is preserved here. The witness
also has `bhriyanta` at `[253|08–09]`; `mriyante`, “they die,” is the secure
contextual reading.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Here in Uttarakuru, the seventh Course of Action—disordered or frivolous
afflicted speech—also occurs by present manifestation, for its inhabitants
sometimes sing with afflicted consciousness. Courses of Action beginning
with killing do not occur because their dispositions are not unwholesome,
their lifespans are fixed, possessions and possession of women are absent,
and there is no purpose for the relevant acts.

How, then, is there non-celibacy among them? When a man wishes to have sexual
relations with a woman, he takes her by the arm and approaches the foot of a
tree. If she is an eligible partner, the tree covers them and they have
intercourse. If she is ineligible, the tree does not cover them and they do
not have intercourse.

Elsewhere in the Desire-Realm, all ten unwholesome Courses of Action also
occur through present manifestation. “By present manifestation” continues to
govern. Outside hell and Uttarakuru, all ten are found in the Desire-Realm
through present manifestation. Among animals, hungry ghosts, and gods, they
occur apart from formal non-restraint; among humans, they may also be
included within non-restraint.

Although one god does not kill another god, a god can kill a being situated
in another destiny. Others maintain that gods themselves die when their heads
are cut through the middle.

The unwholesome Courses of Action have now been stated. The three wholesome
mental Courses of Action occur everywhere through present manifestation and
acquisition. In all five destinies throughout the three realms,
non-covetousness, non-hostility, and right view are found both as presently
manifest and as acquired by the continuum.

## 4. Uttarakuru: The Seventh Course of Action

VAK 4.82 said that the three mental unwholesome Courses of Action are only
endowments in Uttarakuru. VAK 4.83 adds one actively manifested Course of
Action: the seventh,
`saṃbhinnapralāpa`.

```text
occasional afflicted singing
    → frivolous or disconnected speech manifests
```

The example is exacting. Pleasant or musical expression is not condemned as
such. The Bhāṣya specifies singing performed with an afflicted mind. The
determining factor is the afflicted practical character of the occurrence.

The other unwholesome Courses of Action lack their required supports. Fixed lifespan
blocks killing; the property regime blocks theft; the sexual convention
blocks misconduct; lack of malicious purpose blocks the remaining verbal
Courses of Action. A favorable world does not abolish action. It configures
which Courses of Action can reach completion.

## 5. The Tree as a Public Eligibility Test

The tree episode answers a narrow doctrinal objection. If sexual misconduct
is absent in Uttarakuru, how can sexual activity occur? The tree makes the
eligibility relation manifest:

```text
eligible relation   → tree provides cover → intercourse occurs
ineligible relation → no cover            → intercourse does not occur
```

The story belongs to the source's cosmography. Its logical function is clear:
sexual activity and sexual misconduct are not coextensive. Misconduct
requires violation of a determinate relational boundary; the Uttarakuru
environment externalizes that boundary and prevents its transgression.

## 6. Elsewhere in the Desire-Realm

`Anyatra` means outside the two exceptional environments already analyzed:

```text
Desire-Realm − {hell, Uttarakuru}
```

Across that remainder, every unwholesome Course of Action can become manifest. Yet its
relation to restraint differs by destiny:

| Destiny | Relation to restraint classification |
|---|---|
| animals, hungry ghosts, gods | outside both restraint and non-restraint |
| humans | may be included within non-restraint |

The distinction matters because occurrence of an unwholesome Course of Action is broader
than possession of the technical Abhidharma state called non-restraint.
Nonhuman beings can manifest a Course of Action without possessing that juridical or
continuing practical status.

The dispute about gods protects the universality of the killing Course of
Action in the remaining Desire-Realm. One account denies intra-divine killing but permits
a god to kill a being in another destiny. The alternative allows divine death
through bisection of the head. The Bhāṣya records both rather than resolving
the disagreement here.

## 7. The Three Wholesome Mental Courses of Action

The closing half-verse changes from unwholesome to wholesome Courses of Action:

1. non-covetousness (`anabhidhyā`),
2. non-ill-will (`avyāpāda`),
3. right view (`samyagdṛṣṭi`).

Unlike bodily and verbal Courses of Action, these three occur universally
across all five destinies and all three realms. Both modes are affirmed:

```text
present manifestation + endowment
```

This universality is carefully limited to the three mental Courses of Action.
VAK 4.84 will classify the seven wholesome bodily and verbal Courses of Action, whose dependence
upon body, speech, and restraint gives them a different distribution.

## 8. Interpretation

The verse refines the Loka–Karma relation. A World System supplies more than
a location for an already complete action. It can expose, suppress, or make
decidable the relational conditions through which a Course of Action becomes complete.

```text
CourseOfActionManifestation =
    f(agent-state,
      world-setting-law,
      available-object,
      eligibility-relation,
      practical-purpose,
      completion-condition)
```

The tree is a vivid model of an externally legible constraint: the world
itself returns whether the proposed relation is eligible. In an Organon
Program Feature, the corresponding determination would have to be represented
as an explicit rule and an auditable decision, rather than as unexplained
environmental magic.

The Bhāṣya's Uttarakuru cosmography, restraint classifications, divine-killing
dispute, and five-destiny claim ground this model of how world-settings expose
or inhibit the conditions for a Course of Action to become manifest.
