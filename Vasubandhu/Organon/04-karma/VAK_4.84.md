# VAK_4.84 — The Seven Wholesome Bodily and Verbal Courses of Action

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आरूप्यासंज्ञिसत्त्वेषु लाभतः सप्त शेषिते ।
संमुखीभावतश्चापि हित्वा सनरकान् कुरून् ॥ ४.८४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
ārūpyāsaṃjñi-sattveṣu lābhataḥ sapta śeṣite /
saṃmukhībhāvataś cāpi hitvā sa-narakān kurūn // 4.84 //
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `ārūpye` | locative neuter singular, metrically `ārūpya` in compound coordination | in the Formless Realm |
| `asaṃjñi-sattveṣu` | locative masculine plural | among non-percipient beings |
| `lābhataḥ` | ablative used adverbially | by acquisition |
| `sapta` | nominative plural numeral | the seven wholesome bodily and verbal Courses of Action |
| `śeṣite` | locative singular from `śeṣita` | in what remains; in the remaining realms or destinies |
| `saṃmukhībhāvataḥ` | ablative used adverbially | by present manifestation |
| `ca api` | coordinating and additive particles | also; in addition to acquisition |
| `hitvā` | absolutive of √`hā` | having excluded; excepting |
| `sa-narakān` | accusative masculine plural | those together with hell beings; hell beings included in the exclusion |
| `kurūn` | accusative masculine plural | the Kurus, here the inhabitants of Uttarakuru |

**The seven Courses of Action**

The numeral carries forward the wholesome counterparts of the three bodily
and four verbal unwholesome Courses of Action:

```text
bodily     abstention from killing, taking what is not given,
           and sexual misconduct

verbal     abstention from false, divisive, harsh,
           and disordered or frivolous afflicted speech
```

The verse therefore concerns the distribution of wholesome bodily and verbal
Courses of Action. The three wholesome mental Courses of Action discussed in
VAK 4.83 are not included in this numeral.

## 4. Grammar

**Acquisition without manifestation**

```text
ārūpye asaṃjñi-sattveṣu
    sapta
    lābhataḥ [vidyante]
```

> In the Formless Realm and among non-percipient beings, the seven exist by
> acquisition.

The locatives delimit two cases in which the seven belong to a continuum but
do not become presently manifest. The ellipsed verb is supplied from the
ongoing distribution inquiry.

**The residual field**

```text
śeṣite [dhātau gatau vā]
    sapta
    saṃmukhībhāvataḥ ca api [vidyante]
```

> In the remaining realm or destiny, the seven also exist by present
> manifestation.

`Śeṣite` is not a name for one further place. It is a residual-scope operator:
after the two acquisition-only cases have been removed, it ranges over the
remaining realms and destinies.

**The exception within the residual field**

```text
hitvā
    sa-narakān
    kurūn
```

> Excepting hell beings and the Kurus.

The absolutive restricts present manifestation, not acquisition as such. The
Bhāṣya identifies the Kurus as the inhabitants of Uttarakuru.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> In the Formless Realm and among non-percipient beings, the seven wholesome
> bodily and verbal Courses of Action occur by acquisition. In the remainder
> they also occur by present manifestation, excluding hell beings and the
> Kurus.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the Formless Realm and among non-percipient beings, the seven wholesome
> bodily and verbal Courses of Action occur by acquisition. In the remainder
> they also occur by present manifestation, excluding hell beings and the
> Kurus.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> In the Formless Realm and among non-percipient beings, the seven wholesome bodily and verbal Courses of Action occur by acquisition. In the remainder they also occur by present manifestation, excluding hell beings and the Kurus.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kuśala karmapatha` — wholesome Course of Action**

Here the seven bodily and verbal Courses of Action are abstentions opposed to
the seven unwholesome bodily and verbal Courses of Action. The term specifies ethically determined
conduct, not mere physical inactivity.

**`Lābha` — acquisition or endowment**

A continuum-level possession relation. A Course of Action can belong to a continuum by
acquisition although it is not presently manifest. `Lābha` must therefore not
be translated as the occurrence of a current act.

**`Saṃmukhībhāva` — present manifestation**

The event-level actualization of a Course of Action in present conduct. Its addition by
`ca api` marks a mode distinct from, though compatible with, acquisition.

**`Ārūpya-dhātu` — Formless Realm**

The Bhāṣya's claim is qualified: noble beings born there remain endowed with
without-Influx restraint generated in dependence on appropriate levels. It does
not assert indiscriminately that every being in the Formless Realm possesses
the seven.

**`Anāsrava-saṃvara` — without-Influx restraint**

Restraint belonging to the noble path. The Bhāṣya explains that a noble being
in the Formless Realm may remain endowed with past restraint that was
generated and ceased, and with future restraint supported by five levels.
This temporal endowment accounts for the seven there without present bodily
or verbal manifestation.

**`Asaṃjñi-sattva` — non-percipient being**

A being in the non-percipient attainment of rebirth, not a general expression
for unconscious or inanimate entities. The seven belong here through concentration-born
restraint rather than current bodily or verbal enactment.

**`Dhyāna-saṃvara` — concentration-born restraint**

Restraint arising with meditative concentration. It supplies the endowment of
the seven among non-percipient beings.

**`Saṃvara-saṃgṛhīta` — included within restraint**

Wholesome conduct formally comprised by restraint. The Bhāṣya assigns this
mode exclusively to the seven as they occur in the Form Realm.

**`Saṃvara-nirmukta` — independent of formal restraint**

Wholesome conduct not included in an acquired restraint. The seven occur only
in this mode among animals and pretas. Thus wholesome abstention and formal
restraint are not coextensive.

**`Śeṣita` — the remaining field**

A relational remainder after specified cases have been excluded. Its meaning
depends on the preceding partition of realms and destinies rather than on an
independent lexical referent.

**`Naraka` and `Uttarakuru` — exceptions to manifestation**

Hell beings and the inhabitants of Uttarakuru are excluded here from present
manifestation of these seven wholesome Courses of Action. The exclusion does not license
the claim that mere absence of a corresponding violation is itself a presently
manifest wholesome Course of Action.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Four questions must remain distinct**

```text
1. Which Course-of-Action type is under discussion?
   the seven wholesome bodily and verbal Courses of Action

2. Does the Course of Action belong to the continuum?
   acquisition / endowment

3. Is the Course of Action presently operative?
   present manifestation

4. Is its operation included in formal restraint?
   restraint-included / restraint-independent
```

Collapsing any two of these produces a false distribution.

**Distribution table**

| Location or class | Endowment | Present manifestation | Relation to restraint |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| qualified noble beings in the Formless Realm | yes | no | without-Influx restraint |
| non-percipient beings | yes | no | concentration-born restraint |
| hell beings | not denied by this line | no | not classified here |
| inhabitants of Uttarakuru | not denied by this line | no | not classified here |
| animals and pretas where manifested | yes | yes | restraint-independent |
| Form Realm where manifested | yes | yes | restraint-included |
| other remaining cases | yes | yes | either mode is possible |

The cautious wording in the exception rows matters: `hitvā` restricts the
claim of present manifestation. It does not by itself settle every possible
endowment relation.

**Non-equivalences**

```text
endowed                         ≠ presently manifest
wholesome abstention            ≠ formal restraint in every case
absence of present violation    ≠ present wholesome Course of Action
realm membership                ≠ one uniform mode of ethical presence
```

**Modal partition**

```text
seven wholesome bodily-verbal Courses of Action
├── acquisition only
│   ├── noble continuum in the Formless Realm: without-Influx restraint
│   └── non-percipient continuum: concentration-born restraint
└── acquisition plus possible manifestation
    ├── hell and Uttarakuru: manifestation excluded
    ├── animals and pretas: outside formal restraint
    ├── Form Realm: within formal restraint
    └── other remaining cases: either relation to restraint
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The identifications of the seven, the qualified formless
case, the two kinds of restraint, and the final threefold distribution are
supplied by the Bhāṣya. The Bhāṣya specifies endowment alone in these two
cases: noble beings born in the Formless Realm remain endowed with past and
future without-Influx restraint, while non-percipient beings are endowed
through concentration-born restraint. In the remaining realms and destinies,
the seven can become presently manifest. Their manifestation is independent of
formal restraint among animals and pretas, included within restraint in the
Form Realm, and either way elsewhere.

VAK 4.84 prevents a behaviorist reduction of wholesome karma. The verse first
separates belonging from occurring: a restraint can remain an operative
determination of a continuum even when the bodily and verbal supports
required for enactment are unavailable. The Bhāṣya then prevents the
opposite reduction, according to which every wholesome manifestation would
have to be the exercise of formally undertaken restraint. Animals and pretas can
manifest the seven independently of such restraint, while the Form Realm
admits them only as restraint-included.

The resulting system has four logical levels: the universal specification of
the seven, their acquisition by a continuum, their possible actualization,
and the formal classification of that actualization within or outside
restraint. “Wholesome” names the ethical determination of the Course of Action;
`saṃvara` names one structured mode in which that determination can belong and
operate. Their distinction should be developed, not erased.

For the Organon, this articulates capacity and actualization together:
`lābha` is a relational determination of a continuum, and
`saṃmukhībhāva` is its present operation. Their distinction explains how
wholesome restraint can remain operative without being a current bodily or
verbal deed.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> A wholesome Course of Action is not exhausted by a presently observable
> deed. It may belong operatively to a continuum even where the bodily or
> verbal conditions for its current enactment are absent. Conversely, present
> wholesome abstention need not derive from a formally acquired restraint.
> Realm and destination determine the available mode of the Course of Action:
> endowment without
> manifestation, manifestation within restraint, manifestation outside
> restraint, or both. Ethical determination therefore requires the relation
> among Course-of-Action form, continuum, mode of presence, and realm—not
> inference
> from an isolated external non-event.

The source distinction may be represented as:

```text
wholesome Course-of-Action form
    → continuum-endowment
    → possibility of present manifestation
    → relation to formal restraint
    → realm-and-destiny distribution
```

This relational display expresses the Bhāṣya's distribution in Organon terms:
it makes explicit how continuum-endowment and present manifestation are
conditioned by the realm-and-destiny structure.
