# VAK 4.114 Bhāṣya — Four Benefits and Three Determinants of Giving

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
svaparārthobhayārthāya nobhayārthāya dīyate /
tadviśeṣaḥ punar dātṛvastukṣetraviśeṣataḥ // 4.114 //
```

> Giving can be for one's own benefit, another's benefit, both, or
> neither. Its distinctive fruit further depends on the giver, the
> object given, and the recipient field.

The four cases concern actual benefit and possible karmic maturation,
not four declared motives. The second half opens the next analysis of
what makes one gift more distinguished than another.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Giving can benefit the giver, the other, both, or neither. A noble
person who has not abandoned passion, or a dispassionate ordinary
person, gives to a shrine for the giver's own benefit; the shrine
receives no assistance. A dispassionate noble person gives to living
beings for their benefit, excepting a result experienced in the present
life. The recipients are assisted, while the giver has wholly passed
beyond the realm where that gift's future maturation could occur.

When a person who has not abandoned passion, or a dispassionate
ordinary person, gives to living beings, both benefit. When a
dispassionate noble person gives to a shrine, again setting aside a
present-life result, neither receives that ordinary kind of benefit.
The gift is made simply from reverence and gratitude.

Giving was described generally as yielding abundant enjoyment. Its
particular excellence, however, varies with the distinction of the
giver, the gift-object, and the recipient field.

## 4. Benefit Is Not the Same as Wholesomeness

| Case | Donor and recipient | Whose benefit? |
|---|---|---|
| shrine gift by a donor still eligible for future maturation | noble not free of passion, or dispassionate ordinary person | giver only |
| gift to beings by a dispassionate noble | recipients helped; future maturation ground passed beyond | others only, apart from present-life result |
| gift to beings by a donor still eligible for future maturation | recipients helped and future fruit possible | both |
| shrine gift by a dispassionate noble | reverence and gratitude, without recipient assistance or future donor fruit | neither in these specified senses, apart from present-life result |

The final case is not a failed or unwholesome gift. `Gaurava` and
`kṛtajñā` explicitly give it a positive ground. “Neither” negates the
two benefit relations being counted here: direct assistance to another
and future maturation for the donor. The repeated
`sthāpayitvā dṛṣṭadharmavedanīyaṃ` prevents that classification from
erasing a possible result in this life.

## 5. The Next Question: Excellence of the Gift

The second half does not rank the four benefit cases. It identifies
three further determinants of a gift's particular quality:

```text
giver (`dātṛ`) + object (`vastu`) + field (`kṣetra`)
    → distinction of the giving and its fruit
```

The following verses examine these in detail. At this point the
Bhāṣya only states the threefold principle, so the specific rankings
belong to the later studies.

## 6. Organon Contact Point — Project Reconstruction

For our account of agency, this passage gives two separate questions
for the same practical event: who actually benefits, and what makes
the event's karmic result distinctive? The source's four cases and
three determinants provide the data; expressing them as two axes of an
action model is our reconstruction.
