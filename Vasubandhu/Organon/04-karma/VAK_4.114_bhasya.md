# VAK 4.114 Bhāṣya — Four Benefits and the Distinction of Giving

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> svaparārthobhayārthāya nobhayārthāya dīyate /
> tadviśeṣaḥ punar dātṛvastukṣetraviśeṣataḥ // 4.114 //

> Giving is for one's own benefit, another's benefit, both, or neither. Its further
> distinction depends on the distinction of the giver, the thing given, and the
> recipient field.

The Bhāṣya separates benefit-scope from the further distinctions of giver,
thing, and field.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

This giving is performed for one's own benefit, another's benefit, both,
or neither.

A noble person not yet free from passion, or an ordinary person who is
free from passion, gives to a caitya. That giving is for the donor's own
benefit alone, because it provides no assistance to others.

When a noble person free from passion gives to other living beings,
setting aside a result to be experienced in this life, the giving is for
their benefit, because it assists them; it is not for the donor's benefit,
because the ground for its maturation has been entirely passed beyond.

When a person still subject to passion, or an ordinary person free from
passion, gives to other living beings, the giving benefits both.

When a noble person free from passion gives to a caitya, setting aside a
result to be experienced in this life, the giving benefits neither. It is
given solely from reverence and gratitude.

Giving was stated generally to have abundant enjoyment as its result.
Its further distinction depends on the distinction of the giver, the thing
given, and the recipient field.

## 4. Four Benefit-Relations

| Donor and recipient | Actual recipient assistance | Future donor maturation | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Donor still eligible for future maturation gives to a caitya | None | Possible | Donor's benefit alone |
| Dispassionate noble gives to living beings | Yes | Not beyond any present-life result | Others' benefit alone |
| Donor still eligible for maturation gives to living beings | Yes | Possible | Benefit to both |
| Dispassionate noble gives to a caitya | None | Not beyond any present-life result | Neither, in the stated senses |

The final case remains positively grounded in reverence and gratitude. The
classification “neither” concerns the two benefit-relations under discussion;
it does not mean that the giving is unwholesome. The repeated
`sthāpayitvā dṛṣṭadharmavedanīyaṃ` preserves the present-life-result
qualification.

## 5. Interpretation

The four cases classify relations of benefit, not simply the giver's
declared purpose. Actual assistance to recipients and future maturation for
the donor can vary independently. A donor may benefit without assisting the
recipient; recipients may benefit without future maturation for a donor who
has passed beyond that maturation ground; or both may benefit. In the
“neither” case, reverence and gratitude still ground the act.

The second half shifts to three determinants of the giving's distinction:
the giver, the thing given, and the recipient field. Read with 4.113, this
also supports the user's distinction: Skandhas/Bases are gathered content
and frames, not Mind itself. The donor, the thing given, and the field are
distinct roles within that frame; none should be collapsed into Mind or
into another role.
