# VAK 4.56 Bhāṣya — Five Exceptional Fields

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ye nirodhāraṇāmaitrīdarśanārhatphalotthitāḥ /
teṣu kārāpakārasya phalaṃ sadyo 'nubhūyate // 4.56 //
```

> For those who have emerged from cessation, non-contention,
> loving-kindness, the Path of Seeing, or the fruit of arhatship,
> the result of benefiting or harming them is experienced at once.

The Bhāṣya's prose specifies that this result is certain **in the
present life**. The verse's `sadyaḥ` does not establish an exact
interval between the act and its result.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What kind of field is there in relation to which karma necessarily
becomes something to be experienced in this very life? He says: the
community of monks headed by the Buddha; and, among individual
persons, five. “For those who have emerged from cessation,
non-contention, loving-kindness, the Path of Seeing, or the fruit of
arhatship, the result of benefiting or harming them is experienced
at once.”

One who has emerged from the attainment of cessation obtains supreme
peace of mind, because the attainment resembles nirvāṇa. It is as
though that person had gone to nirvāṇa and returned. The continuum
of one who has emerged from `araṇā` proceeds suffused with an
intention of non-contention toward immeasurable beings and with an
exceedingly elevated cultivation of immeasurable merit. The continuum
of one who has emerged from loving-kindness likewise proceeds
suffused with an intention for the happiness of immeasurable beings
and with an exceedingly elevated cultivation of immeasurable merit.

For one who has emerged from the Path of Seeing, the entire set of
factors to be abandoned by seeing has been abandoned; the continuum
is spotless through a fresh transformation of its basis. For one
who has emerged from the fruit of arhatship, the entire set of
factors to be abandoned by cultivation has been abandoned; the
continuum is pure through a fresh transformation of its basis.

Therefore the result of benefits or harms done toward these people
is certainly obtained in this very life. Those emerging from the
remaining stages of the Path of Cultivation, however, do not have a
continuum with the same freshly transformed basis and purified
intention, because the nature of their fruit is not yet complete.
They are therefore not fields of merit in the same exceptional way.

## 4. The Field and Its Five Instances

The question carries forward `kṣetraviśeṣa`, the exceptional field
of 4.55. Vasubandhu first names the Saṅgha headed by the Buddha,
then specifies five individual cases under the verse's `utthitāḥ`,
“those emerged.” Their present condition matters to the argument:

| Emergence from | Bhāṣya's stated condition |
|---|---|
| Cessation attainment | Supreme peace after an attainment likened to nirvāṇa |
| `araṇā` | Non-contentious intention toward immeasurable beings and cultivation of merit |
| Loving-kindness | Intention for immeasurable beings' happiness and cultivation of merit |
| Path of Seeing | Abandonment of what seeing abandons; freshly spotless continuum |
| Fruit of arhatship | Abandonment of what cultivation abandons; freshly pure continuum |

The nirvāṇa comparison describes the peace of cessation attainment;
it does not identify temporary cessation with final nirvāṇa.
`Āśrayaparivṛtti` here names the transformation of the basis in the
two path cases. This passage alone does not license importing every
later Yogācāra determination of that term.

The negative boundary is equally specific. The other stages of the
Path of Cultivation are not **equally** potent fields under this
rule; Vasubandhu does not say their practitioners lack merit or
that actions toward them have no karmic result.

## 5. Timing of the Result

The Kārikā says `sadyaḥ`, “at once.” The concluding prose says
`dṛṣṭa eva dharme niyataṃ prāpyate`: the result is certainly obtained
in this very life. This secures the timing class without supplying a
clock time. Both benefit and harm are included; the text does not
collapse their ethical difference into the single fact of
present-life fruition.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our practical account of karma, the recipient is a determined
field of action: the condition of the recipient's continuum enters
the explanation of the action's result and timing. This is a
possible Organon comparison with field-sensitive action. The five
attainments, the special fruition rule, and its boundary remain
Vasubandhu's doctrinal claims, not a general law of engineered
systems.
