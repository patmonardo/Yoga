# VAK_4.49 — Five Grounds of Being Experienced

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
स्वभावसंप्रयोगाभ्यामालम्बनविपाकतः ।
संमुखीभावतश्चेति पञ्चधा वेदनीयता ॥ ४.४९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
svabhāvasaṃprayogābhyām ālambanavipākataḥ /
saṃmukhībhāvataś ceti pañcadhā vedanīyatā // 4.49 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
svabhāva-saṃprayogābhyām ālambana-vipākataḥ /
saṃmukhī-bhāvataḥ ca iti pañcadhā vedanīyatā //
```

The compound expressions expand into five grounds: intrinsic nature,
association, object-support, maturation, and present manifestation.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `svabhāva` | compound member | intrinsic nature; own character |
| `saṃprayogābhyām` | instrumental dual | by intrinsic nature and association |
| `ālambana` | compound member | object-support of cognition or feeling |
| `vipākataḥ` | ablative adverbial formation | by or through maturation |
| `saṃmukhībhāvataḥ` | ablative adverbial formation | through present manifestation |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `iti` | quotative particle | thus; completing the enumeration |
| `pañcadhā` | adverb | fivefold; in five ways |
| `vedanīyatā` | nominative feminine singular abstract noun | the condition of being designated “to be experienced” |

**Expansion of the five grounds**

```text
svabhāvataḥ
saṃprayogataḥ
ālambanataḥ
vipākataḥ
saṃmukhībhāvataḥ
```

**`ālambana`**

`Ālambana` is the object insofar as cognition or feeling takes it as support.
It names a functional object-relation, not the intrinsic nature of the object.

**`saṃmukhībhāva`**

This is present manifestation or being made presently actual. It must remain
distinct from continued possession of a determination, as established in VAK
4.44.

## 4. Grammar

**Five instrumental or ablative grounds**

```text
svabhāva-saṃprayogābhyām
ālambana-vipākataḥ
saṃmukhībhāvataḥ ca
```

> By intrinsic nature and association, by object-support and maturation, and
> by present manifestation.

The instrumental dual expresses the first pair. The adverbial forms in
`-taḥ` express the remaining grounds.

**The governing assertion**

```text
iti
vedanīyatā
pañcadhā [bhavati]
```

> Thus the condition of being “to be experienced” is fivefold.

The five terms do not enumerate five feelings. They enumerate five relations
that license the designation `vedanīya`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> The condition of being “to be experienced” is fivefold: by intrinsic nature,
> association, object-support, maturation, and present manifestation.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Being designated “to be experienced” is fivefold: through its own nature,
> association, an object, maturation, and present manifestation.
## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The condition of being “to be experienced” is fivefold: by intrinsic nature, association, object-support, maturation, and present manifestation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**1. `svabhāvavedanīyatā`**

```text
vedanā
    is vedanīya by intrinsic nature
```

Here the bearer of the designation is feeling itself.

**2. `saṃprayogavedanīyatā`**

```text
sparśa
    is vedanīya through association with feeling
```

Contact is not identical with feeling; it is conjoined with it.

**3. `ālambanavedanīyatā`**

```text
viṣaya
    is vedanīya as the object-support of feeling
```

The object is what feeling takes up, not feeling's intrinsic nature.
The antecedent of feminine `tām` in the e-text is uncertain; the translation
does not assign it a more specific referent.

**4. `vipākavedanīyatā`**

```text
karma
    → produces maturation
    → maturation has a feeling-character
    → karma is designated vedanīya
```

This is the precise sense governing the karmic classification.

**5. `saṃmukhībhāvavedanīyatā`**

When pleasant feeling is presently occurring, the other two feelings are
absent. One does not experience the pleasant feeling through a second feeling
standing over against it. By bringing that feeling into present manifestation,
one is said to experience it.

**Bathing and drinking analogy**

The Bhāṣya compares the relational usage to something “for bathing” or “for
drinking.” The designation expresses what a thing enables or produces rather
than identifying its intrinsic nature.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Typed predication**

The apparent proposition:

```text
X IS VEDANIYA
```

remains semantically incomplete until its relation is specified:

```text
VEDANIYA(X, by intrinsic nature)
VEDANIYA(X, by association)
VEDANIYA(X, by object-support)
VEDANIYA(X, by maturation)
VEDANIYA(X, by present manifestation)
```

**The karmic mediation**

```text
KARMA(K)
    → PRODUCES(K, MATURATION(R))
    → CHARACTERIZED_BY(R, FEELING(F))
    → VEDANIYA_BY_MATURATION(K, F)
```

Karma, maturation, and feeling remain distinct terms within one causal
relation.

**No illicit conversion**

```text
karma is pleasant-to-be-experienced
    does not entail
karma is pleasant feeling
```

The predicate is transferred through maturation, not through identity.

**Five relations, not five lexical accidents**

The polysemy is controlled by a systematic relation in each case. The word is
not vaguely ambiguous; its determinate Meaning changes with the ground of
predication.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The assignment of examples to the five grounds and the
concluding application to karma are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The five grounds are
illustrated respectively by feeling itself, contact associated with feeling, an
object taken through feeling, karma through its maturation, and a feeling through
present occurrence. On this basis karma is called pleasant-, painful-, or
neutral-to-be-experienced because of its maturation, not because karma itself is
feeling.

**Vasubandhu analyzes the semantic copula**

This verse directly supports the project's insistence that surface syntax
does not settle semantic determination:

```text
X is P
    may conceal
identity, association, object-relation,
causal result, or present manifestation
```

The task is not to reject predication but to recover the mediation that makes
it true.

**Source terms and project architecture**

The source grounds these designations in `svabhāva`, `saṃprayoga`,
`ālambana`, `vipāka`, and `saṃmukhībhāva`. It does not invoke Dhātu or a
feeling-Faculty; those terms should not be inserted into its conventional
analysis.

**Object in ordinary usage**

Here “object” is appropriate for `ālambana` because the text means what
feeling takes as its support. Nothing requires importing the technical
Hegelian sense of Objectivity into the ordinary translation. The philosophical
analysis concerns the relation under which the object functions.

**Present manifestation is not reflexive duplication**

The Bhāṣya avoids positing one feeling that separately experiences another.
The actualization of the feeling is itself the relevant experiential event.

This is the Organon contact. The five grounds, their examples, and the return
to karmic designation belong to the Bhāṣya.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> One predicate can be valid through several irreducibly different mediations.
> To say that something “is experienced” may express own-nature, association,
> object-relation, causal production, or present actuality. The surface copula
> does not reveal which relation grounds the assertion. In the karmic case,
> the predicate returns from the matured result to its cause: karma is called
> “pleasant-to-be-experienced” because it produces a maturation whose
> experiential character is pleasure.

Semantic identity must therefore not be inferred from sameness of predicate.
