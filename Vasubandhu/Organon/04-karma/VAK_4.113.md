# VAK 4.113 — Giving as a Wholesome Action

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दीयते येन तद्दानं पूजानुग्रहकाम्यया ।
कायवाक्कर्म सोत्थानं महाभोग्यफलं च तत् ॥ ४.११३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
dīyate yena tad dānaṃ pūjānugrahakāmyayā /
kāyavākkarma sotthānaṃ mahābhogyaphalaṃ ca tat // 4.113 //
```

The verse defines giving as the means through which something is given,
qualifies it by the wish to honor or benefit others, and names bodily or
verbal action with its activation as well as its result.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

The Bhāṣya expands `yena dīyate` as the cluster of factors by which the
giving-action is brought into operation. The thing given and the action
through which it is given are distinct.

## 4. Grammar

The relative-correlative construction gives the definition:

```text
yena [dravyaṃ] dīyate
    tat dānam [bhavati]
```

> Giving is that through which something is given.

The instrumental `pūjānugrahakāmyayā` qualifies the action's motive:

```text
pūjā-anugraha-kāmyayā
    yena [dravyaṃ] dīyate
```

> Something is given through the wish to honor or benefit others.

The final clauses specify the means and result:

```text
tat dānam
    kāya-vāk-karma
    sa-utthānam
    mahā-bhogya-phalam
```

The Bhāṣya explains `utthāna` as the cluster of factors through which bodily
or verbal action is brought forth.

## 5. Translation

### Scientific English Rendering

> Giving is that through which something is given from the wish to honor
> or benefit others. It is bodily or verbal action together with its
> activation, and it has abundant enjoyment as its result.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Giving is that through which something is given from the wish to honor
> or benefit others. It is bodily or verbal action together with its
> activation, and it has abundant enjoyment as its result. A thing may
> also be handed over through passion and other afflictions, but the
> Bhāṣya excludes those cases from the giving defined here. It explains
> the activation as a cluster of factors that brings the action into
> operation. A cited verse says that when a person gives a personal
> resource with a wholesome Mind, the wholesome Bases present at that
> moment are called giving.

The distinction between thing given and giving-action, the motive
qualification, the activating cluster, the cited verse, and the
abundant-enjoyment result are supplied or explained by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Giving is not merely the resource that changes hands, nor is it any
> transfer regardless of motive. The Bhāṣya defines it through a
> recipient-directed wish, bodily or verbal action, and the factors that
> activate that action. The same outward transfer can therefore belong
> to a different action-class when passion rather than the stated wish
> governs it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

- **`Dāna`** — giving as the action through which a thing is given, not
  the thing considered by itself.
- **`Pūjā-kāmatā`** — wish to honor others.
- **`Anugraha-kāmatā`** — wish to benefit or assist others.
- **`Kāya-vāk-karma`** — bodily or verbal action through which the
  transfer is effected.
- **`Utthāna`** — the cluster of factors by which the action is brought
  into operation; the Bhāṣya does not reduce it here to a single factor.
- **`Śubha-manas`** — wholesome Mind, the wording used in the Bhāṣya's
  cited verse.
- **`Kuśala-skandha`** — wholesome Base. The cited verse refers to the
  wholesome Bases present at the moment of giving.
- **`Dānamaya-puṇyakriyāvastu`** — the basis of meritorious activity
  constituted as giving. The Bhāṣya explains `-maya` through examples of
  a grass-formed house and a leaf-formed vessel.
- **`Mahā-bhogya-phala`** — abundant enjoyment as the result of this
  giving-action.

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
Given:
    wish to honor or benefit others
    + bodily or verbal action
    + activating cluster
    → giving as the action through which a thing is given
    → abundant enjoyment as its result
```

The Bhāṣya distinguishes this from two insufficient conditions:

```text
thing transferred alone
    ≠ giving-action

transfer generated by passion or other affliction
    ≠ the wholesome giving defined here
```

The cited verse identifies the wholesome Bases present at the moment as
giving; the analysis does not equate the giving-action with the transferred
thing alone.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The preceding verse explicitly names *cetanā* in its analysis of
meritorious activity. This verse does not: its definition names the wish
to honor or benefit, bodily or verbal action, and an activating cluster.
The cited verse speaks of a wholesome Mind and wholesome Bases. Those
expressions should not be silently collapsed into *cetanā*, nor should
`utthāna` be narrowed to volition alone.

In the project reading, the Bases are the content and frames of the
giving-event, not Mind itself. The quoted line keeps wholesome Mind
(`śubha-manas`) distinct from wholesome Bases (`kuśala-skandha`); the
Bases name the gathered content/frame of the event, not a mental
substance.

The source unit is `[268|09]–[268|20]`. The witness reads `daddānam`
at `[268|09–10]`; the study separates `tad dānam` according to the
relative-correlative syntax. The cited verse has `tat kṣaṇaṃ` as two
words. The study stops at the colon introducing the next topic at
`[268|20]`; 4.114 begins at `[268|21]`.
