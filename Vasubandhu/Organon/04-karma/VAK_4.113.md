# VAK_4.113

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दीयते येन तद्दानं पूजानुग्रहकाम्यया ।
कायवाक्कर्म सोत्थानं महाभोग्यफलं च तत् ॥ ४.११३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dīyate yena tad dānaṃ pūjānugrahakāmyayā /
kāyavākkarma sotthānaṃ mahābhogyaphalaṃ ca tat // 4.113 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dīyate | yena | tat | dānam | pūjā-anugraha-kāmyayā |
kāya-vāk-karma | sa-utthānam | mahā-bhogya-phalam | ca | tat
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dīyate` | third-person singular present passive of `√dā` | something is given |
| `yena` | instrumental neuter singular relative pronoun | by or through which |
| `tat` | nominative neuter singular correlative pronoun | that |
| `dānam` | nominative neuter singular | giving as karmic activity |
| `pūjā` | compound member | honor or reverence |
| `anugraha` | compound member | benefit, assistance, favor |
| `kāmyayā` | instrumental feminine singular action noun | through the wish for |
| `kāya-vāk-karma` | nominative neuter singular collective compound | bodily or verbal karma |
| `sa-utthānam` | nominative neuter singular | together with its activating complex |
| `mahā-bhogya-phalam` | nominative neuter singular compound | having abundant enjoyment as its fruit |
| `ca` | coordinating particle | and |

**Relative-correlative definition**

```text
yena dīyate
    tat dānam
```

> That through which something is given is giving.

The construction defines `dāna` through the effective karmic means of
transfer, not through the material object transferred.

**Restrictive qualification**

```text
pūjā-kāmyayā
    or
anugraha-kāmyayā
```

The Bhāṣya explains that this qualification is necessary because transfer can
also be generated by passion and other afflictions.

## 4. Grammar

**Giving as the means by which transfer occurs**

```text
yena
    [dravyaṃ] dīyate
    tat dānam [bhavati]
```

> That by which an object is given is giving.

`Yena` is instrumental and refers to the practical activity-complex. The
thing given may conventionally be called a gift, but it is not technically
identical with the giving-karma defined here.

**Recipient-directed purpose**

```text
pūjā-anugraha-kāmyayā
    yena dīyate
```

> Through the wish to honor or benefit others, something is given.

The instrumental gives the determining purpose. The Bhāṣya takes `pūjā` and
`anugraha` as two wholesome recipient-relations that distinguish this species
from afflictively motivated relinquishment.

**Execution and activation**

```text
tat [dānam]
    kāya-vāk-karma
    sa-utthānam [asti]
```

> That giving is bodily or verbal karma together with its activation.

The collective compound includes either bodily or verbal execution.
`Sa-utthāna` adds the aggregate by which the manifest deed is brought into
operation; it should not be reduced to an isolated private intention.

**Characteristic maturation**

```text
tat
    mahā-bhogya-phalam [asti]
```

> It has abundant enjoyment as its fruit.

The final predicate states a worldly maturation-result. It does not by itself
identify giving with liberative disconnection.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> That through which something is given, from the wish to honor or benefit,
> is giving. It is bodily or verbal karma together with its activation, and it
> has abundant enjoyment as its fruit.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Giving is not the material object handed over but the wholesome practical
> complex through which that object actually becomes given. Because transfer
> can also arise from passion and other afflictions, meritorious giving is
> restricted here to transfer performed from the wish to honor an excellent
> field or to benefit another. It consists in bodily or verbal karma together
> with the aggregate that activates that manifestation. At the moment when a
> person gives personal property with a wholesome mind, the wholesome
> aggregates participating in the event are collectively designated giving;
> its characteristic maturation is abundant resources and enjoyment.

The exclusion of object-identity, afflictively motivated transfer, restrictive
force of the two motives, definition of activation, collective wholesome-event
analysis, and maturation are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Giving is a coordinated transformation of relation. A recipient-directed
> purpose activates bodily or verbal performance; that performance makes a
> formerly possessed resource available to another; and the whole wholesome
> event acquires meritorious causal efficacy. Neither inward benevolence,
> external motion, nor the transferred object constitutes giving in isolation.

The rational circuit is:

```text
wish to honor or benefit
    → activating wholesome complex
    → bodily or verbal manifestation
    → effective relinquishment and recipient-access
    → abundant future resource-field
```

The same external redistribution can belong to a different karmic species
when a different Principle governs the act.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dāna` — giving as karmic activity**

That through which an object actually becomes given. The term designates the
wholesome practical event rather than the material object considered apart
from intention and execution.

**`Deya` — object to be given**

The transferable material or resource. Although ordinary speech can call it
the “gift,” its role as object must remain distinct from `dāna` as the activity
effecting relinquishment.

**`Pūjā-kāmatā` — wish to honor**

Giving directed toward an excellent field through reverence. The recipient
is determined through qualities worthy of honor; this continues the earlier
analysis of `guṇakṣetra`.

**`Anugraha-kāmatā` — wish to benefit**

Giving directed toward need, support, or welfare. The recipient is determined
as one to be assisted rather than primarily as an object of reverence.

**Restrictive wholesome motive**

The two wishes are not optional illustrations. They exclude transfers
generated by passion and other afflictions from the meritorious species under
analysis.

**`Kāya-vāk-karma` — bodily or verbal karma**

Giving can be effected bodily by delivering or transferring an object and
verbally by authorizing ownership or access. Speech is practically effective
when it changes the recipient's normative relation to the resource.

**`Utthāna` — activating complex**

Explained as the `kalāpa`, aggregate or cluster, by which bodily or verbal
karma is brought into operation. It links wholesome purpose with manifest
execution.

**`Kuśalāḥ skandhāḥ` — wholesome aggregates in the giving-event**

A cited traditional verse collectively designates the wholesome aggregates
present when a person gives personal property with a wholesome mind as
`dāna`. Giving is therefore a structured event, not one isolated dharma or
motion.

**`Mahābhogyaphala` — abundant enjoyment as fruit**

The characteristic worldly maturation of giving. Making resources available
to others conditions a future field in which resources are abundantly
available to the giver's continuum.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
A = giver
R = recipient
O = gift-object
M = wish to honor or benefit
U = activating wholesome aggregate
E = bodily or verbal execution
T = effective transfer
D = meritorious giving
```

Then:

```text
GroundedIn(A, M)
Activates(U, E)
Performs(A, E)
Transfers(E, O, R)
MakesAvailable(T, O, R)
    → Constitutes(D)
```

But:

```text
GiftObject(O) alone
    does not entail D

ExternalTransfer(T) alone
    does not entail D

PrivateIntention(M) without execution
    does not entail CompletedGiving(D)
```

**Same result, different karmic species**

```text
Transfers(A1, O1, R1)
Transfers(A2, O2, R2)
ExternallySimilarTransfer(A1, A2)

GroundedIn(A1, WholesomeHonorOrBenefit)
GroundedIn(A2, Affliction)
    → KarmicallyDifferent(A1, A2)
```

**Fruit relation**

```text
Constitutes(D)
    → TendsToward(D, AbundantEnjoymentMaturation)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.112 established that merit, activity, and basis are functional
determinations within a concrete whole. VAK 4.113 now gives the first complete
traversal of such a whole. The giving-event includes recipient-field,
gift-object, wholesome purpose, activating aggregate, bodily or verbal
manifestation, effective transfer, and maturation.

No isolated component carries the full meaning:

```text
object without activity
    = something giveable, not yet giving

purpose without execution
    = intended giving, not yet completed transfer

transfer without wholesome ground
    = redistribution, not necessarily meritorious dāna
```

This is Practical Reason becoming objective. The Principle—honor or benefit—
does not remain in subjective representation. Through activation and embodied
or verbal Kriyā, it transforms an objective relation of possession and access.
The recipient can now use what was previously unavailable.

For the five-layered Object, `dāna` is not a monolithic item but a systematic
event:

```text
semantic end
    → activating mental complex
    → manifest body/speech
    → altered resource relation
    → karmic consequence
```

Samyama as Schema Inference comprehends these layers as one course while
assigning each determination to its proper role. The term `dāna` names their
concrete unity, not a dictionary synonym for any one of them.

The abundant-enjoyment fruit preserves the relational form of the deed:
making resources available to another conditions future availability of
resources. Yet this remains worldly maturation. Giving improves the field of
conditioned becoming; it is not by itself the disconnection-result that ends
becoming.
