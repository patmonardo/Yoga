# VAK 4.115 Bhāṣya — The Giver and the Manner of Giving

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dātā viśiṣṭaḥ śraddhādyaiḥ satkṛtyādi dadāty ataḥ /
> satkārodārarucitā kālānācchedyalābhitā // 4.115 //

> A giver distinguished by faith and related qualities gives respectfully and in
> other specified ways. Consequently, there is receipt of honor, taste for excellent
> enjoyments, timely acquisition, and acquisition not liable to seizure or
> destruction.

This develops the giver-determinant named in 4.114. The Bhāṣya expands
the compressed `satkṛtyādi` and explicitly pairs four manners with four
results in order.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

First, the giver is distinguished through faith, discipline, learning, and
related qualities. Through the distinction of that giver, the fruit-bearing
of the giving is itself distinguished. Such a giver gives:

“Respectfully and in other specified ways.”

The giver gives respectfully, with one's own hand, at the proper time, and
without harming others.

Consequently, from giving in those ways, four distinctions arise in order:
the giver receives honor; develops a taste for excellent enjoyments;
acquires resources at the proper time, not after the time has passed; and
acquires resources not liable to seizure. The giver's enjoyments are not
taken by others or destroyed by fire and similar causes.

Thus it was said that the giver is distinguished, and through that
distinction the giving is distinguished.

## 4. Four Ordered Manners and Results

| Manner of giving | Result stated in the Bhāṣya |
|---|---|
| Respectfully (`satkṛtya`) | Receipt of honor |
| With one's own hand (`svahastam`) | Taste for excellent enjoyments |
| At the proper time (`kāle`) | Acquisition at the proper time, not after it has passed |
| Without harming others (`parānupahatya`) | Enjoyments not taken by others or destroyed by fire and similar causes |

`Yathākramam` fixes the one-to-one correspondence. `Udārarucitā` is
explained as one result—the taste for excellent enjoyments—not as two
independent results. The last result is the Bhāṣya's karmic claim, not a
general empirical rule about property.

## 5. Interpretation

The giver's faith, discipline, learning, and related qualities enter the
giving-event through its manner. The Bhāṣya then describes the maturation
as preserving those distinctions in corresponding results: respectful
conduct is paired with honor, personal giving with a taste for excellent
enjoyments, timely giving with timely acquisition, and non-injurious giving
with acquisition not interrupted by seizure or destruction.

This is the giver-axis within the frame established by 4.114. It does not
identify the giver's qualities with Mind itself, nor does it redefine the
Skandha/Base as Mind. The project distinction remains: Bases gather content
and frames; this passage specifies a relation within the giving-frame.
