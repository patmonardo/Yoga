# VAK 4.115 Bhāṣya — The Giver and the Manner of Giving

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
dātā viśiṣṭaḥ śraddhādyaiḥ satkṛtyādi dadāty ataḥ /
satkārodārarucitā kālānācchedyalābhitā // 4.115 //
```

> A giver distinguished by faith and related qualities gives
> respectfully and in other fitting ways. Consequently, the giver
> receives honor, develops a taste for excellent enjoyments, obtains
> goods at the proper time, and obtains goods that cannot be taken away.

The Bhāṣya gives four manners of giving and explicitly pairs them, in
order, with four aspects of the result.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

First, the giver is distinguished by faith, moral discipline, learning,
and related qualities. Because of this distinction of the giver, the
gift's capacity to bear fruit is itself distinguished. Such a giver
gives respectfully, personally, at the right time, and without
injuring others.

From giving in those ways, four distinctive results follow in that
order. Respectful giving leads to receiving respect. Personal giving
leads to a taste for excellent enjoyments. Giving at the right time
leads to obtaining goods when they are needed, rather than after the
occasion has passed. Giving without harming others leads to goods
that cannot be taken away: others do not seize them, nor are they
destroyed by fire and similar causes. Thus the giver's distinction
helps determine the gift's distinctive fruit.

## 4. The Four Ordered Correspondences

| Manner of giving | Result specified by the Bhāṣya |
|---|---|
| `satkṛtya` — respectfully | reception of honor (`satkāra`) |
| `svahastam` — with one's own hand | taste for excellent enjoyments (`udāreṣu bhogeṣu ruci`) |
| `kāle` — at the proper time | timely acquisition |
| `parānupahatya` — without injuring others | acquisitions not subject to seizure or destruction |

`Yathākramaṃ catvāro viśeṣāḥ` establishes this one-to-one order. In
particular, the second result concerns the giver's **taste** for
excellent enjoyments, not a separate pair of “abundance” and “taste.”
The Bhāṣya states these correspondences as karmic results; it does
not provide a general empirical law of property security.

## 5. Giver Quality and Gift Manner

`Śraddhādi` is explained by faith, discipline, learning, and related
qualities. `Satkṛtyādi` concerns how a distinguished giver acts. The
Bhāṣya therefore connects the person's formation to the mode of the
deed, then to the form of its result. The material gift alone does
not supply this determination.
