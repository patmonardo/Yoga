# VAK 4.19 Bhāṣya — Present and Past Prātimokṣa Restraint

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
prātimokṣasthito nityam atyāgād vartamānayā /
avijñaptyānvitaḥ pūrvāt kṣaṇād ūrdhvam atītayā // 4.19 //
```

> One established in prātimokṣa restraint is continually endowed with
> present avijñapti, so long as he has not abandoned it; after the first
> moment, he is also endowed with past avijñapti.

The complete running unit for this verse extends from [209|04] through
[209|11–12] in `Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The next sentence
begins the application to non-restraint in 4.20.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
idaṃ vicāryate: kaḥ katamayā vijñaptyā 'vijñaptyā vā kiyantaṃ kālaṃ
samanvāgata iti? tatra:

prātimmokṣasthito nityam atyāgād vartamānayā /
avijñaptyā 'nvitaḥ.

yaḥ prātimokṣāsaṃvarasthaḥ pudgala uktaḥ sa yāvat tām avijñaptir
na tyajati tāvat tayā vartamānayā nityaṃ samanvāgataḥ.

pūrvāt kṣaṇād ūrdhvam atītayā.

prathamāt kṣaṇād ūrdhvam atītayā 'pi samanvāgataḥ.
atyāgād iti sarvatrādhikṛtaṃ veditavyam.
```

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The running witness has
`prātimmokṣasthito` in its first citation and
`prātimokṣāsaṃvarasthaḥ` in the prose. Both are retained here as source
readings, not presented as alternative doctrines. The official Kārikā
anchor follows its separate Kārikā witness. The running source also has
an extraneous `[` after the [209|11] marker; it has not been made part of
the Sanskrit passage.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

This is to be examined: who is endowed with which manifest or non-manifest
action, and for how long? First: “One established in prātimokṣa restraint
is continually endowed with present avijñapti, so long as he has not
abandoned it.” The person described as established in prātimokṣa
restraint is continually endowed with that avijñapti in its present mode
for as long as he does not abandon it.

“After the first moment, [he is endowed] with past [avijñapti].” After
the first moment he is also endowed with avijñapti in its past mode. The
phrase “so long as it has not been abandoned” should be understood as
governing throughout.

## 4. What Is Being Timed

The opening question names both `vijñapti` and `avijñapti`, but this
first answer treats the **avijñapti of the person established in
prātimokṣa restraint**. The question is not simply whether an earlier
vow was once performed. It asks which action a bearer is endowed with,
in which temporal mode, and for how long. The Kārikā's `anvitaḥ` is
explained by the prose's `samanvāgataḥ`, “endowed with.”

At the first moment there is present avijñapti. After that first moment,
the bearer is endowed both with the present avijñapti of the current
moment and with avijñapti belonging to a past moment. The Bhāṣya says
`prathamāt kṣaṇād ūrdhvam`: **after the first moment**. It does not say
that past avijñapti is possessed at the very first moment.

`Nityam` means continuously within this conditioned duration. It does
not mean that a single item of avijñapti is eternally present. Nor is
“past” merely a modern psychological memory of having taken a vow:
`atītayā 'pi samanvāgataḥ` predicates possession of past avijñapti in
the text's temporal analysis.

## 5. The Scope of Non-Abandonment

The final sentence prevents an unconditional reading. Vasubandhu says
explicitly that `atyāgāt`, “because it has not been abandoned,” is to be
understood **throughout**. It governs the present and past possession
described here and carries forward into the following sequence. The
Bhāṣya does not yet explain in this unit all the ways the restraint may
be abandoned; it specifies the condition under which the present
possession claim holds.

This also preserves two different temporal questions: whether a moment
of avijñapti is present or past, and whether the person still possesses
it under the non-abandonment condition. Passing into the past does not,
by itself, answer the second question.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our practical World System, the passage tests how a standing
commitment can remain operative without flattening its temporal
structure. The bearer, the current determination, the past
determination, and the condition of non-abandonment must remain
distinguishable. An Organon account that reduces the vow to one
unchanging object, or treats its past merely as remembered information,
would miss the relation the Kośa is articulating. This is the Organon's
use of the passage, not Vasubandhu's own terminology for it.
