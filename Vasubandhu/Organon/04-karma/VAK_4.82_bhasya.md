# VAK 4.82 Bhāṣya — Endowment and Manifestation by Destiny

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> bhinnapralāpapāruṣyavyāpādā narake dvidhā /
> samanvāgamato 'bhidhyāmithyādṛṣṭī kurau trayaḥ // 4.82 //

> In hell, disordered or frivolous speech, harsh speech, and ill will occur in
> both modes; covetousness and false view occur by endowment. In Kuru, the
> three occur by endowment.

The preceding verse classified how many Courses of Action can occur with one
volition. This verse shifts the coordinate: it asks which unwholesome Courses
of Action occur in particular destinations, either by present manifestation
or by endowment.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
kasyāṃ gatau kati karmapathā akuśalāḥ kuśalā vā saṃmukhībhāvataḥ
samanvāgamato vā?

bhinnapralāpapāruṣyavyāpādā narake dvidhā /

ity ete trayaḥ karmapathā narake dvābhyāṃ saprakārābhyāṃ santi:
saṃmukhībhāvataḥ samanvāgamabhāvataś ca. paridevanāt saṃbhinnapralāpaḥ;
parasparaparitāpanāt pāruṣyaṃ rukṣasaṃtānatayā; parasparadveṣād vyāpādaḥ.

samanvāgamato 'bhidhyāmithyādṛṣṭī

na saṃmukhībhāvataḥ, rañjanīyavastvabhāvāt karmaphalapratyakṣatvāc ca.
karmakṣayeṇa maraṇān na prāṇātipātaḥ. dravyastrīparigrahābhāvān
nādattādānakāmamithyācārau. prayojanābhāvān na mṛṣāvādaḥ. ata eva na
paiśunyaṃ, nityabhinnatvāc ca.

kurau trayaḥ // 4.82 //

samanvāgamata iti vartate. abhidhyāvyāpādamithyādṛṣṭayo na
saṃmukhībhāvataḥ, amamāparigrahatvāt, snigdhasaṃtānatvād,
āghātavastvabhāvād, apāpāśayatvāc ca.
```

The witness's `samanvāgamabhāvataś` is retained in sense as “by the state of
endowment.” The opening question governs this whole regional enumeration: it concerns
both unwholesome and wholesome Courses of Action, although 4.82 itself begins
with the unwholesome distribution.

## 3. Continuous Translation

In which destination do how many wholesome or unwholesome Courses of Action
occur, through present manifestation or through endowment?

In hell, disordered or frivolous speech, harsh speech, and ill will occur in
both modes. These three Courses of Action exist there through both present
manifestation and endowment. Disordered speech occurs through lamentation;
harsh speech through mutual torment and the roughness of the continua; ill
will through mutual hatred.

Covetousness and false view occur there by endowment, but not through present
manifestation. There is no attractive basis, and karmic result is directly
evident. There is no killing because death occurs through exhaustion of the
action sustaining the hell existence. There is neither taking what is not
given nor sexual misconduct because property and possession of women are
absent. There is no false speech because it has no purpose. For the same
reason there is no divisive speech, and also because hell beings are always
already divided from one another.

In Uttarakuru, the three mental unwholesome Courses of Action occur by
endowment. The phrase “by endowment” continues to govern. Covetousness, ill
will, and false view do not become presently manifest there because there is
no possessive appropriation, because the inhabitants' continua are gentle,
because there is no basis for injury, and because their dispositions are not
unwholesome.

## 4. Two Modes of Presence

The introductory question establishes the controlling distinction:

```text
samanvāgama     — endowment within the continuum
saṃmukhībhāva   — present manifestation
```

`Dvidhā`, “in both ways,” says that the first three Courses of Action are
both possessed and actively manifested in hell. The next two are possessed without becoming
manifest there. Uttarakuru repeats the second pattern for all three mental
unwholesome Courses of Action.

This prevents two invalid inferences:

```text
not presently manifest  ⇏  not endowed
endowed                 ⇏  presently manifest
```

The Course of Action belongs to a continuum in one determination; whether it
can arise as an event in a given world-setting is another determination.

## 5. The Hell Configuration

The Bhāṣya derives manifestation from the concrete structure of hell rather
than from a generic statement that every evil must occur there.

| Course of Action | Mode in hell | Given condition |
|---|---|---|
| frivolous speech | endowment and manifestation | lamentation |
| harsh speech | endowment and manifestation | mutual torment and rough continua |
| ill will | endowment and manifestation | mutual hatred |
| covetousness | endowment only | no attractive object |
| false view | endowment only | karmic result is directly evident |

The remaining five Courses of Action are absent for distinct reasons:

- **killing:** a hell being dies when the action sustaining that existence is
  exhausted, rather than by another being's lethal act;
- **taking what is not given and sexual misconduct:** there is no relevant
  property or sexual possession;
- **false speech:** deception has no practical purpose;
- **divisive speech:** it likewise has no purpose, and the beings are already
  divided.

The result is a setting-specific causal analysis. Extreme suffering does not
mean that every unwholesome Course of Action is manifest. Each Course of
Action still requires its own object, purpose, and completion condition.

## 6. The Uttarakuru Configuration

In Uttarakuru, covetousness, ill will, and false view remain endowments but do
not arise presently. The Bhāṣya assigns a separate absence-condition to each:

```text
no possessive “mine” or appropriation  → no manifest covetousness
gentle continuum and no injury-basis   → no manifest ill will
absence of an unwholesome disposition  → no manifest false view
```

The favorable setting constrains expression without erasing every inherited
relation of endowment. This is precisely why the two modes must be retained.
The next kārikā continues the Uttarakuru enumeration with bodily and verbal
Courses of Action; 4.82 establishes only the three mental Courses of Action.

## 7. Course of Action, Setting, and Condition

The verse adds a third term to the analysis of action:

```text
continuum endowed with a Course of Action
    + world-setting supporting its objects and purposes
    + sufficient present conditions
        → possible manifestation
```

Absence of any one support can block manifestation. The Course of Action itself does
not change its definition from one destiny to another. What changes is the
world of objects and relations in which its completion would have to occur.

This sharpens the practical meaning of the project's Loka–Karma relation: a
Course of Action is never merely an inner item. Its manifestation presupposes
a world structure
containing the relevant agents, objects, possessions, injuries, purposes,
and causal endpoints.

## 8. Interpretation

At the project level, 4.82 requires separate state and execution relations:

```text
CourseOfActionState {
    continuum
    endowedCourseOfAction
}

CourseOfActionEvent {
    manifestCourseOfAction
    worldSetting
    object
    purpose
    completionCondition
}
```

The Root Agent must not execute a Course of Action merely because it occurs in
its inherited or available state. Execution requires a world projection in
which every constitutive condition is satisfied. Conversely, lack of present
execution does not prove that the determination has been removed from the
agent's state.

The source's distribution of Courses of Action across hell and Uttarakuru
supplies concrete constraints for the model: endowment is not execution, and
each manifest Course of Action requires its own relational, purposive, and
dispositional conditions.
