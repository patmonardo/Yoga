# VAK_4.71

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
मिथ्यादृष्टेस्तु मोहेन शेषाणां त्रिभिरिष्यते ।
सत्त्वभोगावधिष्ठानं नामरूपं च नाम च ॥ ४.७१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
mithyādṛṣṭes tu mohena śeṣāṇāṃ tribhir iṣyate /
sattva-bhogāv adhiṣṭhānaṃ nāmarūpaṃ ca nāma ca // 4.71 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
mithyā-dṛṣṭeḥ | tu | mohena |
śeṣāṇām | tribhiḥ | iṣyate |
sattva-bhogau | adhiṣṭhānam |
nāma-rūpam | ca | nāma | ca
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `mithyā-dṛṣṭeḥ` | genitive feminine singular | of false view |
| `tu` | adversative or transitional particle | but; however |
| `mohena` | instrumental masculine singular | through delusion |
| `śeṣāṇām` | genitive masculine plural | of the remaining courses |
| `tribhiḥ` | instrumental masculine plural | through the three roots |
| `iṣyate` | third-person singular present passive of `√iṣ` | is held; is accepted |
| `sattva-bhogau` | nominative masculine dual | living being and possession or object of enjoyment |
| `adhiṣṭhānam` | nominative neuter singular, collective predicate | basis; that upon which a course operates |
| `nāma-rūpam` | nominative neuter singular | name-and-form |
| `ca ... ca` | correlative conjunctions | and ... and |
| `nāma` | nominative neuter singular | name; linguistic designation |

**The remainder**

`Śeṣāṇām` is determined by subtraction from the ten courses. Six were named
in VAK 4.70, and false view is assigned here. The three remaining courses are:

```text
mṛṣāvāda             false speech
paiśunya              divisive speech
saṃbhinnapralāpa      disordered speech
```

**Four bases**

The verse names:

```text
sattva        living being
bhoga         possession or object of enjoyment
nāmarūpa      name-and-form
nāma          name
```

The Bhāṣya specifies the last as `nāmakāya`, a name-complex rather than an
isolated word.

## 4. Grammar

**Completion through delusion**

```text
mithyā-dṛṣṭeḥ tu
    [niṣṭhā]
    mohena [bhavati]
```

> False view, however, reaches completion through delusion.

`Niṣṭhā` or `samāpanam` is understood from VAK 4.70. The genitive identifies
the course whose completion is being classified; the instrumental identifies
its completion-root.

**The variable-root remainder**

```text
śeṣāṇām
    [niṣṭhā]
    tribhiḥ iṣyate
```

> The completion of the remaining courses is held to be through the three.

The Bhāṣya expands `tribhiḥ` as greed, hatred, or delusion. It does not require
all three roots to be simultaneously present.

**The fourfold distribution of bases**

```text
sattva-bhogau
nāmarūpam ca
nāma ca
    adhiṣṭhānam [bhavanti]
```

> Living being and possession, name-and-form, and name are the bases.

The dual `sattva-bhogau` is coordinated with the two neuter singulars and
predicated collectively as `adhiṣṭhānam`. The exact correspondence is not
expressed syntactically in the verse alone; the Bhāṣya distributes the four
bases successively across the four groups of courses.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> False view, however, reaches completion through delusion; the remaining
> courses are held to reach completion through the three. Living being and
> possession, name-and-form, and name are the bases.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> False view reaches completion through delusion when delusion becomes
> intense. False speech, divisive speech, and disordered speech may each reach
> completion through greed, hatred, or delusion. The ten courses are also
> arranged as four groups according to their objective bases: killing,
> hostility, and harsh speech operate upon living beings; sexual misconduct,
> covetousness, and taking what is not given operate upon possessions or
> objects of enjoyment; false view has name-and-form as its basis; and the
> three remaining speech-courses have the structured name-complex as theirs.

The identities of the remainder, the intensified-delusion explanation, and
the exact `3 + 3 + 1 + 3` distribution are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A course of action is determined neither by inward affliction alone nor by
> an outward event considered in isolation. An afflictive root becomes
> determinate as a particular karmapatha, and that course realizes its
> practical character upon a specific basis: a living being, a possession,
> name-and-form, or a linguistic complex. The completed course is therefore a
> structured relation among root, practical form, and objective basis.

`Objective basis` does not mean a self-standing object abstracted from the
course. `Adhiṣṭhāna` names that upon which this particular karmic determination
is established or directed.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Mithyādṛṣṭi` — false view**

A determinate denial or distortion of the morally causal order. It is not
identical with `moha`: delusion is the completing root, while false view is the
specific mental karmapatha brought to culmination.

**`Adhimātra` — intensified**

The Bhāṣya says delusion become intense (`adhimātrabhūta`) brings false view
to completion. Bare association with delusion is therefore not yet the full
completion claim.

**`Śeṣa` — remainder**

False speech, divisive speech, and disordered speech. They are structurally
variable with respect to completion-root:

```text
greed      for acquisition or advantage
hatred     for injury or division
delusion   through confused determination
```

The examples in this display clarify the functional possibilities; the
Bhāṣya itself establishes the three-root alternative.

**`Adhiṣṭhāna` — objective basis**

That upon which a karmapatha operates or is established. It gives the
objective side of the course without turning that basis into an isolated
substance or generic object.

**`Sattva` — living being**

The basis of killing, hostility, and harsh speech. These courses are completed
in practical relation to a sentient being.

**`Bhoga` — possession or object of enjoyment**

The basis of sexual misconduct, covetousness, and taking what is not given.
The translation preserves both possession and access-to-enjoyment rather than
reducing `bhoga` to an abstract experience.

**`Nāmarūpa` — name-and-form**

The basis assigned to false view. The text states this allocation but does not
here give a further analysis of how false view takes name-and-form as its
basis; that interpretive question should remain open rather than be filled by
a project doctrine.

**`Nāmakāya` — name-complex**

The Bhāṣya's specification of the Kārikā's `nāma`: the structured linguistic
complex serving as the basis of false, divisive, and disordered speech. It is
more determinate than an isolated lexical item.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Completion-root map concluded**

```text
DVEṢA
    killing
    hostility
    harsh speech

LOBHA
    sexual misconduct
    covetousness
    taking what is not given

MOHA
    false view

LOBHA / DVEṢA / MOHA
    false speech
    divisive speech
    disordered speech
```

The first two groups belong to VAK 4.70. VAK 4.71 states the latter two, with
the Bhāṣya identifying the remainder.

**Objective-basis map**

```text
3 courses                       ADHIṢṬHĀNA
killing                         living being
hostility                       (sattva)
harsh speech

3 courses                       ADHIṢṬHĀNA
sexual misconduct              possession / object of enjoyment
covetousness                    (bhoga)
taking what is not given

1 course                        ADHIṢṬHĀNA
false view                      name-and-form
                                (nāmarūpa)

3 courses                       ADHIṢṬHĀNA
false speech                    name-complex
divisive speech                 (nāmakāya)
disordered speech
```

**Three non-interchangeable dimensions**

```text
root
    afflictive ground of completion

course
    determinate practical form

basis
    that upon which the course operates
```

Therefore:

```text
root ≠ course ≠ basis
```

Yet they are not detached classifications. A completed karmapatha is the
determinate relation connecting all three.

**Speech as flexible mediation**

The three speech-courses have one kind of basis, `nāmakāya`, while allowing
three different completion-roots. Their linguistic medium remains constant as
their afflictive function varies.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**From internal root to objective determination**

The introduction of `adhiṣṭhāna` prevents the analysis from remaining an
inward psychology of motive:

```text
subjective root
    → determinate karmapatha
    → objective basis
```

The root becomes objectively effective only through a course operating upon a
determinate field. Conversely, the basis does not determine the ethical act by
itself; it becomes the basis of this course through the rooted activity
directed upon it.

**The name-complex is a practical medium**

The Bhāṣya's `nāmakāya` matters because speech does not operate upon isolated
dictionary items. Falsehood, division, and disorder work through structured
linguistic designation within a communicative relation. Language can mediate
acquisition, hostility, or deluded determination precisely because its
practical function is not exhausted by reference.

**The limit concerning `nāmarūpa`**

The assignment of false view to name-and-form is textually clear, but its
full rationale is not developed in this immediate unit. The official study
therefore preserves the allocation without treating `nāmarūpa` as a finished
external object or importing a later Organon architecture into the Bhāṣya.

This is the Organon contact. The completion mappings, four bases, and exact
group distribution belong to the Kārikā and Bhāṣya; the account of their unity
as relational Practical Form is the project synthesis.
