# VAK_4.38

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
प्रातिमोक्षदमस्त्यागः शिक्षानिक्षेपणाच्च्युतेः ।
उभयव्यञ्जनोत्पत्तेर्मूलच्छेदान्निशात्ययात् ॥ ४.३८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
prātimokṣadamas tyāgaḥ śikṣānikṣepaṇāc cyuteḥ /
ubhayavyañjanotpatter mūlacchedān niśātyayāt // 4.38 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
prātimokṣa-damaḥ tyāgaḥ śikṣā-nikṣepaṇāt cyuteḥ /
ubhaya-vyañjana-utpatteḥ mūla-cchedāt niśā-atyayāt //
```

The Bhāṣya distributes the list: ordinary prātimokṣa restraint has four
causes of abandonment; upavāsa restraint has those four plus expiration at
the end of the night.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
prātimokṣa-damaḥ
tyāgaḥ

śikṣā-nikṣepaṇāt
cyuteḥ

ubhaya-vyañjana-utpatteḥ

mūla-cchedāt

niśā-atyayāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `prātimokṣadamaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | prātimokṣa discipline or restraint |
| `tyāgaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | abandonment, relinquishment, loss |
| `śikṣānikṣepaṇāt` | ablative singular compound | through laying down the training |
| `cyuteḥ` | ablative feminine singular from `cyuti` | through falling away; through death |
| `ubhayavyañjanotpatter` | ablative feminine singular compound before sandhi | through the arising of both sexual characteristics |
| `mūlacchedāt` | ablative singular compound | through severance of the roots |
| `niśātyayāt` | ablative singular compound | through the passing or expiration of the night |

**`dama`**

Vasubandhu gives the etymological explanation:

```text
dāmyanti anena iti damaḥ
    it is dama because by it [the faculties] are restrained
```

He then states that `saṃvara` is intended. `Dama` therefore does not introduce
a new kind of karmic form in this verse.

**`tyāga`**

`Tyāga` is the loss or abandonment of an acquired determination. It is the
counterpart of the acquisition (`pratilambha`) analyzed through VAK 4.37.

```text
pratilambha
    how restraint becomes possessed

tyāga
    how possession of restraint ceases
```

**`śikṣānikṣepaṇa`**

Laying down the training is expanded as formal repudiation of the training
rules before a person capable of understanding the declaration. It is not a
private feeling of discouragement and is not identical with a particular
breach.

**`cyuti`**

Here falling away means death, explained as abandonment of the present
homogeneous life-basis (`nikāyasabhāgatyāga`). It terminates the living support
of this prātimokṣa status.

**`ubhayavyañjanotpatti`**

The Bhāṣya specifies the simultaneous manifestation of both sexual
characteristics. Within the disciplinary system presupposed by the text, this
is treated as disruption of the qualifying bodily support.

**`mūlaccheda`**

The Bhāṣya makes the object explicit: `kuśalamūlasamuccheda`, severance of the
wholesome roots. This is the loss of a sustaining causal basis, not merely the
commission of one unwholesome act.

**`niśātyaya`**

The passing of the night applies specifically to upavāsa restraint. It is the
completion of the duration established by the one-day-and-night undertaking.

## 4. Grammar

**Main predication**

```text
prātimokṣa-damaḥ tyāgaḥ
    śikṣā-nikṣepaṇāt
    cyuteḥ
    ubhaya-vyañjana-utpatteḥ
    mūla-cchedāt
    niśā-atyayāt
```

The nominative expression names the topic: abandonment of prātimokṣa
restraint. The ablatives enumerate causal conditions through which the loss
occurs.

**Distribution across restraint-types**

The kārikā presents one compressed list, but the Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
ordinary prātimokṣa restraint
    first four causes

upavāsa restraint
    first four causes
    + passing of the night
```

The fifth cause is therefore not a universal termination condition for every
prātimokṣa category.

**Why these causes are effective**

The Bhāṣya supplies a parallel explanation:

```text
śikṣānikṣepaṇa
    → samādāna-viruddha-vijñapti-utpāda

cyuti
    → āśraya-tyāga

ubhayavyañjanotpatti
    → āśraya-vikopana

kuśalamūlasamuccheda
    → nidāna-ccheda

niśātyaya
    → tāvat eva ākṣepa
```

The surface event and the ground through which it terminates restraint must
remain distinct.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Prātimokṣa restraint is abandoned through laying down the training, through
> death, through the arising of both sexual characteristics, and through
> severance of the roots; [upavāsa restraint is also abandoned] through the
> passing of the night.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Ordinary prātimokṣa restraint is lost in four ways: through formally laying
> down the training, through death, through the simultaneous arising of both
> sexual characteristics, or through severance of the wholesome roots.
> Upavāsa restraint is lost through those same four causes and additionally
> when the night ends and the duration projected by its undertaking is
> complete.

The formal communicative condition, the specification of wholesome roots,
and the distribution of the fifth cause are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A standing discipline does not disappear through one homogeneous negation.
> It can cease because the bearer manifests a declaration contrary to the
> undertaking, because death removes its living support, because the
> qualifying bodily support is disrupted, because its wholesome causal basis
> is severed, or because its internally projected duration has been fulfilled.
> Each termination event is effective only by negating a determinate condition
> upon which the restraint depends.

The upavāsa case is cessation through completion, not failure. The same
undertaking that generates the temporary restraint also determines the limit
at which it ends.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Formal renunciation**

The Bhāṣya requires:

```text
training rules repudiated
    + intention to relinquish
    + declaration before an understanding person
    → laying down the training
```

The decisive ground is `samādānaviruddhavijñapti`: a manifestation contrary
to the original undertaking. The prior vow was instituted through a manifest
practical act; its formal relinquishment likewise requires manifestation.

**Breach and abandonment**

```text
breach
    conduct contradicts an acknowledged rule

laying down the training
    bearer terminates the standing undertaking itself
```

VAK 4.38 does not say that every breach automatically destroys the restraint.
The next verse opens the dispute concerning grave offenses.

**`āśrayatyāga`**

Death causes abandonment because the living support is relinquished. This
does not say that every karmic result of prior conduct is erased. It says that
the present possession of this disciplinary restraint no longer has its
bearer.

**`āśrayavikopana`**

The arising of both sexual characteristics is assigned a different ground:
not complete loss of the support, but disruption of the support as qualified
for the disciplinary form.

```text
death
    loss of support

bodily transformation
    disruption of support
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly distinguishes the relations.

**`nidānaccheda`**

Severance of wholesome roots removes the causal basis (`nidāna`) sustaining
wholesome restraint. A particular offense and destruction of the causal root
must not be treated as equivalent events.

**`ākṣepa` and temporal projection**

The upavāsa undertaking projects restraint for a determinate span:

```text
undertaking
    one day and night

projected duration exhausted
    → restraint ends
```

`Tāvad eva ākṣepa` means that its causal projection extends precisely that
far, not indefinitely.

**Five dimensions of termination**

| Event | Ground of loss | Dimension |
|---|---|---|
| Laying down training | contrary manifestation | commitment |
| Death | loss of support | bearer |
| Both sexual characteristics arise | disruption of support | bodily qualification |
| Wholesome roots severed | causal basis severed | sustaining ground |
| Night ends | projected duration exhausted | time |

## 8. Logical Determination

**Lifecycle transition**

```text
acquisition
    establishes possession

support and conditions
    sustain possession

abandonment
    negates a constitutive condition
```

The verse begins a dependency analysis of cessation rather than presenting
loss as brute disappearance.

**Event and ground**

```text
event
    formal declaration

ground
    contradiction of undertaking
```

```text
event
    death

ground
    loss of support
```

An event becomes a cause of abandonment through the precise relation it
alters.

**Support has differentiated predicates**

The distinction between loss and disruption shows that `āśraya` is not an
undifferentiated bearer:

```text
support exists
support has qualifying determinations
```

Death negates the first relation for the present life. The bodily
transformation described by the text alters the second.

**Fulfillment and destruction**

```text
wholesome roots severed
    restraint loses its causal ground

upavāsa term completed
    restraint fulfills its own measure
```

Both result in non-possession, but their logical character is opposite. One
is destruction of support; the other is completion of form.

**No inference from breach yet**

The listed conditions do not license:

```text
any transgression
    → abandonment of the entire restraint
```

Whether a grave offense has that effect is a contested determination reserved
for VAK 4.39.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Cessation requires its ground**

Vasubandhu does not stop after naming the five events. He asks why each event
has the power to terminate restraint. The resulting structure is exact:

```text
surface event
    → altered dependency
    → loss of standing determination
```

This is the proper response to a field dominated by momentary appearance.
The restraint’s cessation is comprehended through the relation that no longer
holds, not merely observed as one more vanishing.

**Manifest institution and manifest relinquishment**

The first cause returns to `vijñapti`:

```text
undertaking manifested
    → restraint instituted

contrary declaration manifested
    → restraint relinquished
```

The verbal act is not a report about an invisible inner event. In the
disciplinary procedure it is the act that changes the practical relation.

**Persistence is conditional architecture**

A restraint persists through coordinated determinations:

```text
undertaking remains valid
living support remains
support remains qualified
wholesome causal root remains
projected term remains open
```

This does not turn restraint into a permanent substance. It gives an account
of what must remain true for the acquired form to continue.

**The truth of temporary restraint**

The night’s expiration is especially important. A temporary form does not
become unreal because it ceases at its limit:

```text
internally bounded persistence
    → completed actuality
    not defective eternity
```

Upavāsa ends because it succeeds in persisting exactly as long as undertaken.

This is the Organon contact. The five events, their distribution, and the five
grounds of efficacy belong explicitly to the Bhāṣya.
