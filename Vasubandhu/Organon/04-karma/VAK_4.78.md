# VAK_4.78

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
व्यापादः सत्त्वविद्वेषो नास्तिदृष्टिः शुभाशुभे ।
मिथ्यादृष्टिस्त्रयो ह्यत्र पन्थानः सप्त कर्म च ॥ ४.७८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

| `na-asti-dṛṣṭiḥ` | nominative feminine singular compound | the view that there is no such thing; denial-view |

**The three mental courses completed**

```text
abhidhyā          covetousness
vyāpāda           ill will
mithyādṛṣṭi       false view
```

The verse defines the latter two and then explains why all ten are called
`karmapatha` despite an asymmetry between the mental three and the bodily and
verbal seven.

## 4. Grammar

**Definition of ill will**

```text
sattveṣu vidveṣaḥ
    vyāpādaḥ [bhavati]
```

> Ill will is hostility toward living beings.

The Bhāṣya adds the decisive specification `parapīḍākāra-pravṛtta`: the
hostility proceeds in the form of harming another.

**Definition of false view**

```text
śubhe ca aśubhe ca karmaṇi
    na asti iti dṛṣṭiḥ
        mithyā-dṛṣṭiḥ [bhavati]
```

> False view is the view that wholesome and unwholesome karma do not exist.

The locative dual states the field concerning which denial is made. The
Bhāṣya explains that this is only the opening example of a wider repudiation.

**Three paths and seven actions**

```text
atra [daśasu karmapatheṣu]
    trayaḥ panthānaḥ [karmaṇaḥ]
    sapta karma ca [panthānaḥ ca]
```

> Here the three are paths of karma, while the seven are both karma and paths
> of karma.

The omitted complements are restored by the Bhāṣya's explicit explanation.
`Hi` marks this as the reasoned clarification of the compound `karmapatha`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Ill will is hostility toward living beings. False view is the denial of
> wholesome and unwholesome karma. For here the three are paths, while the
> seven are also karma.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Ill will is hostility directed toward a living being and proceeding in the
> form of harming another. False view is the denial of wholesome and
> unwholesome karma; taken in its full extension, it repudiates karma, karmic
> result, and the noble ones, of which the verse states only an initial
> example. Covetousness, ill will, and false view are paths of karma because
> the volition associated with them is carried along their direction and
> formed under their influence. The three bodily and four verbal courses are
> themselves karma and are also paths upon which their originating volition
> proceeds.

The harmful orientation, full range of false view, and dynamic explanation of
`karmapatha` are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The three mental courses are determinate orientations through which
> volition becomes karmically formed. Covetousness projects appropriation;
> ill will projects harm to a living being; false view repudiates the
> moral-causal order that makes wholesome and unwholesome activity
> intelligible. They are called paths because volition moves according to
> their direction. The seven bodily and verbal courses are realized activity
> and at the same time routes through which that activity proceeds toward
> further consequence.

`Path` therefore has genetic force. It does not merely label membership in a
list.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vyāpāda` — ill will**

Hatred determined toward the harm of a living being. It is more specific than
`dveṣa`, the general unwholesome root.

**`Sattva-vidveṣa` — hostility toward living beings**

The objective basis is a sentient being. The Bhāṣya's
`parapīḍākāra-pravṛtta` makes the practical direction explicit:

```text
another living being
    → hostility takes the form of harming
    → vyāpāda
```

Momentary irritation or dislike is not thereby the completed mental
karmapatha.

**`Nāsti-dṛṣṭi` — denial-view**

The principial position “there is no” with respect to the practical realities
named by the doctrine. Here the verse begins with denial of wholesome and
unwholesome karma.

**`Mithyādṛṣṭi` — false view**

Not ordinary error but a determinate repudiation of the moral-causal order.
The Bhāṣya cites denials such as:

```text
there is no giving
there is no offering or sacrifice
there is no good conduct
there is no bad conduct
```

and says the full class repudiates:

```text
karma
karma-phala       karmic result
ārya              the noble ones
```

The Kārikā gives only `ādimātra`, an initial sample of this extension.

**`Karmapatha` — path or course of karma**

The Bhāṣya explains the compound dynamically. A path carries, channels, and
gives direction to volition; it is not merely a static moral category.

**`Vāha` — conveyance**

The Bhāṣya's metaphor for how volition associated with the three mental
courses is carried by them and goes according to their course.

**The Vaibhāṣika asymmetry**

```text
three mental courses
    path of karma
    but not themselves karma in this strict account

seven bodily and verbal courses
    themselves karma
    and paths of karma
```

The larger Bhāṣya unit also reports a Dārṣṭāntika alternative treating the
mental three themselves as mental karma. The official determination here
preserves the operative Vaibhāṣika explanation stated directly around the
Kārikā and marks the alternative without collapsing it into that account.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Root, mental course, and volition**

```text
lobha
    → abhidhyā
    → associated cetanā moves as appropriation

dveṣa
    → vyāpāda
    → associated cetanā moves as harm

moha
    → mithyādṛṣṭi
    → associated cetanā moves under denial
```

The arrows do not state identity. They distinguish general afflictive ground,
determinate mental path, and volition formed along that path.

**Why the mental three are paths**

```text
mental karmapatha
    supplies determinate orientation
        ↓
cetanā associated with it
    is carried along that orientation
        ↓
karma is formed accordingly
```

This is a genuine mediating function: the path determines how volition
proceeds.

**Why the external seven are both**

```text
cetanā
    → produces bodily or verbal course

bodily or verbal course
    = karma as realized activity
    + path upon which the producing cetanā proceeds
```

The realized course is both product and pathway.

**Three mental corruptions**

```text
abhidhyā
    negates another's possessive relation
    through projected appropriation

vyāpāda
    negates another's well-being
    through projected harm

mithyādṛṣṭi
    negates the practical order itself
    through principial denial
```

False view is therefore meta-level in scope, although the verse defines it
through concrete doctrinal denials rather than an abstract theory of
second-order judgment.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Determination as channeling**

The strongest result is the Bhāṣya's own genetic image:

```text
root
    supplies general afflictive ground

mental karmapatha
    specifies the direction

cetanā
    travels and is formed along that direction

bodily or verbal karmapatha
    realizes the process as activity
```

A path is meaningful through the movement it conducts. Detached from volition
and realized action, it would be a mere list-item; in the system it is the
route of practical formation.

**False view attacks the condition of practical intelligibility**

Covetousness and ill will distort determinate relations to possessions and
living beings. False view denies the efficacy of the field within which those
relations are judged. This explains its special completion through intensified
delusion without reducing it to ignorance generally.

**No premature identification with manifestation modes**

The mental-path / bodily-verbal-action distinction is not simply identical
with `avijñapti` / `vijñapti`. VAK 4.78 is explaining the strict status of the
ten as karma and path; its own categorial asymmetry should be preserved.

This is the Organon contact. The definitions, expanded false-view range,
conveyance account, and Vaibhāṣika asymmetry belong to the Bhāṣya; the explicit
root-orientation-volition-realization sequence is the project synthesis.
