# VAK 4.121 Bhāṣya — Shrine Gifts, Recipient-Free Merit, and Bad Fields

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
caitye tyāgānvayaṃ puṇyaṃ maitryādivad agṛhṇati /
kukṣetre 'pīṣṭaphalatā phalabījāviparyayāt // 4.121 //
```

> At a shrine, merit follows relinquishment even without someone
> receiving the gift, as with loving-kindness. Even in a deficient
> field, a wholesome seed does not turn into an undesirable kind of
> fruit merely because the field is deficient.

The commentary immediately qualifies the last point: a defective
field can diminish the fruit or leave the gift fruitless.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The earlier discussion allowed a passion-bearing donor to give to a
shrine for the donor's own benefit. How can there be merit if nobody
uses the offering? The Bhāṣya distinguishes merit arising from
relinquishment and merit arising from another's use of what is given.
Only the first occurs at a shrine.

An opponent objects that nobody receives or benefits. The response:
if merit required actual assistance to another, there could be no
merit from cultivating loving-kindness and the other immeasurables or
right view. Such merit arises from one's own mind even without a
recipient accepting something. Devotion to a virtuous Teacher who
has passed away can likewise generate merit.

But then why perform an offering or an act of honor at all? Because
the devotion that brings bodily or verbal action into being is
stronger than devotion left at intention alone. The Bhāṣya uses a
negative comparison: hostile action toward someone wrongly believed
to be a living enemy creates more demerit than the hostile intention
alone. Likewise, enacted honor toward the departed Teacher creates
more merit than devotion alone.

Does a good giving-seed planted in a bad field yield bad fruit? No:
just as grape seed does not produce bitter neem fruit, a gift moved
by the wish to benefit another does not invert into undesirable
fruit. A defective field can, however, make its fruit small or nil.

## 4. Two Distinctions

| Question | Bhāṣya's distinction |
|---|---|
| Must another consume the gift for merit? | no; relinquishment-merit can arise without consumption-merit |
| Can a bad field turn good giving into bad fruit? | no inversion of fruit-kind; its defect can reduce or eliminate fruit |

The no-recipient argument does not make bodily giving superfluous.
Enactment intensifies the motivating devotion. Nor does the seed
analogy make recipient fields irrelevant: the explicit final sentence
preserves their effect on the magnitude of fruit.

## 5. Organon Contact Point — Project Reconstruction

The passage separates intention, enacted expression, recipient use,
and fruit. That makes it useful for an action model in which a
devotional act can be effective even without a consuming recipient,
while a recipient's condition still affects the result. This is our
formal reading of the source's distinctions.
