# VAK_4.50

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नियतामनियतं तच्च नियतं त्रिविधं पुनः ।
दृष्टधर्मादिवेद्यत्वात् पञ्चधा कर्म केचन ॥ ४.५० ॥
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

`Tat` carries forward the three `vedanīya` karmas discussed in VAK 4.47–4.49.

**`niyata`**

The Bhāṣya defines the opposition through necessity of fruition. `Niyata`
karma is necessarily to be experienced, after which its time is specified.

**`aniyata`**

```text
na avaśya vedanīyam
    not necessarily to be experienced
```

This is stronger than temporal uncertainty. The issue is whether fruition
must occur, not merely whether its date is known.

**`dṛṣṭadharma`**

“The visible dharma” means the presently existing life in contrast to the
immediately following and more remote lives.

## 4. Grammar

**The modal division**

```text
tat [karma]
niyatam aniyatam ca [bhavati]
```

> That karma is both determinate and indeterminate.

The paired compound distributes the previously discussed `vedanīya` karmas
according to whether fruition is necessary.

**The temporal division of determinate karma**

```text
niyatam [karma]
punaḥ trividham
dṛṣṭa-dharma-ādi-vedyatvāt
```

> Determinate karma is in turn threefold because it is to be experienced
> beginning in the present life.

The Bhāṣya expands `ādi` into the present life, the immediately following
rebirth, and a later life.

**The alternative count**

```text
kecana
karma pañcadhā [icchanti]
```

> Some authorities hold karma to be fivefold.

The verb “hold” is understood. The Bhāṣya explains that these authorities
divide indeterminate karma into two.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> That karma is determinate or indeterminate. Determinate karma is again
> threefold because it is to be experienced beginning in the present life.
> Some authorities hold karma to be fivefold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Karma is either determinate—its maturation must be experienced—or
> indeterminate, whose maturation is not necessary. Determinate karma is
> threefold: it matures in the present life, in the immediately following
> rebirth, or in a later life. Together with indeterminate karma these make
> four classes. Some authorities count five by dividing indeterminate karma
> according to whether its maturation is itself determinate or indeterminate.

The definition of indeterminate karma, expansion of the three temporal forms,
standard fourfold count, and explanation of the alternative fifth class are
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Karmic causality includes distinct modal and temporal determinations. First
> one asks whether the causal formation must attain fruition at all. Only if
> fruition is necessary does one then determine its temporal index: this life,
> the next life, or a more remote life. Causal efficacy therefore does not by
> itself entail necessary actualization, while necessary actualization does not
> by itself specify when the result must appear.

The temporality of karma belongs to its differentiated causal form rather than
being an external delay imposed upon an otherwise identical cause.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The two-stage determination**

```text
first question
    must this karma mature?

second question
    if it must mature, when?
```

Necessity and temporal placement are logically distinct.

**`dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya`**

Karma produced and matured in the same present life.

**`upapadyavedanīya`**

Karma whose maturation occurs in the second life—the immediately following
rebirth.

**`aparaparyāyavedanīya`**

Karma whose maturation occurs after the next rebirth, in a later life.

**The standard fourfold classification**

```text
1. present-life maturation
2. next-life maturation
3. later-life maturation
4. indeterminate maturation
```

**The alternative fivefold classification**

Some authorities divide the fourth member:

```text
indeterminate as to being experienced
    + maturation determinate

indeterminate as to being experienced
    + maturation indeterminate
```

The scholastic relation between these two determinations is retained rather
than reduced to ordinary uncertainty.

**Near-fruiting and remote-fruiting karma**

Against the proposal that present-life karma might still mature in another
life, the Vaibhāṣikas distinguish karma with a near fruit from karma with a
remote fruit. Their analogy is botanical: different seeds characteristically
fruit after different intervals.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Modality before temporality**

```text
KARMA(K)
    → FRUITION_MODALITY(K)
        necessary
        or not necessary
```

For necessary fruition:

```text
NECESSARY_FRUITION(K)
    → TEMPORAL_INDEX(K)
        present life
        next life
        later life
```

**Do not collapse the two indeterminacies**

```text
avyākṛta
    ethical indeterminacy

aniyata
    non-necessity of karmic fruition
```

The same English word “indeterminate” marks different axes and must remain
typed by its context.

**Causal efficacy without necessary fruition**

```text
karma is causally formed
    does not entail
its fruit must become actual
```

This is the modal contribution of `aniyata`.

**Temporal range belongs to causal differentiation**

```text
near-fruiting cause
    → temporally near result

remote-fruiting cause
    → temporally remote result
```

The Vaibhāṣika analogy treats temporal range as belonging to the kind of
causal formation, not as a merely accidental postponement.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Kriyā and karma separate more sharply**

The visible performance of an action does not disclose the complete temporal
and modal form of its karma:

```text
kriyā
    presently performed activity

karma
    acquired causal determination
    with a possible necessity and temporal range of fruition
```

The distinction does not sever karma from action; it explains why the causal
determination cannot be read directly from the momentary appearance of the
performance.

**Necessity is determinate, not fatalistic**

The text itself distinguishes karma whose fruition is necessary from karma
whose fruition is not. Consequently, “karma” does not mean that every formed
cause must inevitably produce a corresponding experience.

**Temporal indexing applies the continuity theory**

Present, next, and later lives require a causal continuum capable of retaining
differentiated temporal efficacy without a permanent bearer. The verse does
not solve that entire problem here, but its classification presupposes the
continuity architecture established elsewhere in the Kośa.

**Position of the controversy**

Some authorities loosen the connection between the name “present-life karma”
and actual present-life maturation. The Vaibhāṣikas reject this and defend
real temporal types through the seed analogy. Both positions remain attributed
in the report.

This is the Organon contact. The modal gloss, fourfold and fivefold counts,
controversy, and seed analogy belong to the Bhāṣya.
