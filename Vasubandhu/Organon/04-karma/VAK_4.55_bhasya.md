# VAK 4.55 Bhāṣya — Present-Life Fruition and Complete Detachment

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
dṛṣṭadharmaphalaṃ karma kṣetrāśayaviśeṣataḥ /
tadbhūmyatyantavairāgyād vipāke niyataṃ hi yat // 4.55 //
```

> Karma may bear fruit in this life through a distinctive field or
> intention; through complete detachment from its level, so too may
> karma that is fixed with respect to maturation.

The unit opens with the question at [232|05] and ends at [232|15–16].
The question about which fields guarantee present-life fruition at
[232|17] begins the next unit.

Word division, punctuation, and verse numbering are editorial.
`Saṃghastrīvādasamudācārāt` is compressed and its exact narrative
details are uncertain. The source uses `śrūyate` (“it is reported”)
for the bodily-change example; neither example is presented here as
an independently verified event.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What sort of karma should be understood as having a result in this
life? “Karma has a present-life result because of a distinctive
field or intention.” Karma to be experienced in this life may arise
through the special character of its field. Thus a change of bodily
sex characteristics is reported after conduct involving speech
about a woman associated with the Saṅgha; the precise action in the
compressed source phrase remains uncertain. Or it may arise through
a distinctive intention, as in the account of a person described as
`śaṇḍha` who, after freeing cattle from castration, acquired male
characteristics.

Alternatively, karma may become present-life-result karma “through
complete detachment from its level.” If one becomes completely
detached from the level to which a wholesome or unwholesome karma
belongs, that karma becomes one to be experienced in this life.
What kind of karma, for someone born at that level, does this
concern? “That which is fixed with respect to maturation.”

This rule applies to karma fixed in maturation but not fixed to a
different temporal stage, and not to indeterminate karma. Karma
already fixed to another stage has its fixed maturation there:
complete detachment from that level is not possible for one who
bears it before that maturation. And for indeterminate karma,
complete detachment brings non-maturation instead.

## 4. Three Routes, One Narrow Modal Rule

The first half presents two grounds for present-life fruition:
`kṣetraviśeṣa`, a special field, and `āśayaviśeṣa`, a special
underlying intention. The Bhāṣya illustrates each with an inherited
bodily-change account. Their function here is to exemplify the
claimed timing of karmic result; the examples do not establish a
general biological mechanism.

The second half adds `tadbhūmyatyantavairāgya`, complete detachment
from the karma's level. Vasubandhu immediately limits its scope:

| Karma's status | Result of complete detachment in this account |
|---|---|
| Maturation fixed, time unfixed | Present-life fruition |
| Already fixed to another time | It matures at that time; prior complete detachment is excluded |
| Maturation indeterminate | Non-maturation |

This retains the two axes of 4.50–4.51: fixity of `vipāka` and
fixity of `avasthā`. The conclusion is not that detachment makes
every pending karma ripen at once.

## 5. Practical Determination

For a case of outstanding karma, the question is first whether its
result is fixed, then whether its time is already fixed, and only
then what complete detachment from its level entails. The Bhāṣya
uses these distinctions to decide between present fruition,
fruition at its assigned stage, and no fruition. That is the
practical precision of the passage.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

Our OM account can use this as a comparison for a determination
whose future field closes: a result that must occur but has no
fixed later time is brought into the present, whereas an optional
result can fail to arise. This is an Organon analogy. Vasubandhu's
claim is restricted to karmic maturation and complete detachment
from a `bhūmi`.

## 7. Review Status

The complete unit [232|05]–[232|16] is translated. The field and
intention examples are presented as textual reports, and the
detachment rule is restricted to its stated modal class. The
compressed example remains marked for review. The running source
and research witness are unchanged.
