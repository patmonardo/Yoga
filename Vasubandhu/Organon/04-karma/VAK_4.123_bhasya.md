# VAK 4.123 Bhāṣya — Cultivation as Perfuming the Mind

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
dauḥśīlyataddhetvahataṃ tadvipakṣaśamāśritam /
samāhitaṃ tu kuśalaṃ bhāvanā cittavāsanāt // 4.123 //
```

> Purified discipline is undamaged by misconduct or its causes and
> rests on their opponent and on peace. Cultivation is concentrated
> wholesome activity, because it perfumes the mind.

The first half was quoted and explained in the 4.122 Bhāṣya unit;
the running commentary on 4.123 here begins with the second half.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What is cultivation? Concentrated wholesome activity. What counts
as “concentrated” here? What has the nature of concentration and
co-arises with it. Why is it called cultivation? Because it perfumes
the mind. Concentrated wholesome activity powerfully infuses the
continuum with qualities, making it partake of them, as flowers
perfume sesame seeds.

Giving has already been said to lead to abundant enjoyment. What,
then, are the characteristic results of discipline and cultivation?
The next verse answers.

## 4. `Bhāvanā` as a Transformation of the Continuum

The source ties `bhāvanā` to `cittavāsanā`: a quality comes to
pervade the mind's continuum. The flower-and-sesame analogy is not
an assertion that mind is literally a substance absorbing fragrance;
it conveys how repeated concentrated wholesome activity conditions
the continuum.

The phrase `samāhitaṃ kuśalam` includes what has the nature of
concentration **and what co-arises with it**. This keeps the source's
scope broader than a bare, isolated concentration factor.

## 5. Organon Contact Point — Project Reconstruction

For an agent architecture, one might model cultivation as a process
that changes later dispositions rather than merely logging one
successful episode. That is a project analogy. The Bhāṣya states
the transformation of `citta` and its continuum through wholesome
concentration, not a software update rule.
