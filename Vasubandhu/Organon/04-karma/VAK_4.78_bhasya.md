# VAK 4.78 Bhāṣya — Why They Are Courses of Karma

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
vyāpādaḥ sattvavidveṣo nāstidṛṣṭiḥ śubhāśubhe /
mithyādṛṣṭistrayo hyatra panthānaḥ sapta karma ca // 4.78 //
```

> Hostility is hatred of living beings. False view is the view that
> wholesome and unwholesome karma do not exist. Here the three are paths
> of karma, while the seven are both karma and paths.

The verse completes the definitions of the mental courses and then explains
the compound `karmapatha`. In the Vaibhāṣika account, the mental three
channel associated volition; the bodily and verbal seven are themselves
karma and also the courses upon which their originating volition proceeds.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Hostility is hatred of living beings.” Hostility toward living beings is
`vyāpāda` when it proceeds in the form of harming another.

“False view is the view that wholesome and unwholesome karma do not exist.”
The view that wholesome and unwholesome karma do not exist is false view.
For example: “There is no giving, no offering, no oblation; there is no good
conduct and no bad conduct,” and so forth, through the denial that there are
worthy or perfected persons in the world. In its complete form, false view
repudiates karma, karmic result, and the noble ones. The verse displays only
the beginning of this range.

Such are the defining characteristics of the ten courses of karma. What does
the expression “courses of karma” mean?

“Here the three are paths, while the seven are also karma.” Covetousness,
hostility, and false view are called courses of karma because they are paths
for karma. The volition associated with them is borne by their conveyance
and travels according to their movement, because under their influence it
is configured in that manner.

The seven beginning with killing are karma because they are bodily and
verbal karma. They are also paths for karma because the volition from which
they arise proceeds while taking them as its basis. Thus one group consists
of paths of karma, while the other consists of both karma and paths of karma;
the common plural `karmapatha` is established even though the members are
not of identical form.

The wholesome counterparts—non-covetousness and the others, together with
abstention from killing and the remaining courses—are to be understood in
the same manner.

Preparatory and subsequent acts are karma and stand in relation to the
courses because their activity is directed toward those courses and rooted
in them. Yet, as explained earlier, the ten are selected according to the
gross or salient forms. Through the increase and decrease of these courses,
the internal and external states of the world likewise undergo increase and
decrease.

What about the Dārṣṭāntikas, who maintain that covetousness and the other
mental courses are themselves mental karma? How are these also paths of
karma? They themselves should be asked. Still, one can say that they are
both karma and paths leading toward good and bad destinies, or that they
carry one another.

All ten unwholesome courses of karma oppose the manifestation of wholesome
karma.

## 4. The Remaining Mental Definitions

Hostility is more determinate than dislike:

```text
dveṣa
    general root of hatred
        ↓
living being as objective basis
        ↓
orientation taking the form of another's harm
        ↓
vyāpāda
```

The mental course is already practical because it projects injury to a
living being as an end.

False view is likewise more determinate than error or uncertainty. It denies
the practical order through which karma, result, and liberation are
intelligible:

```text
moha
    general root of delusion
        ↓ intensification
principial denial of karma, result, and noble attainment
        ↓
mithyādṛṣṭi
```

The verse gives denial of wholesome and unwholesome karma as an initial
sample (`ādimātra`), not as the complete extension.

## 5. Path as the Conveyance of Volition

The Bhāṣya explains `patha` dynamically:

```text
mental course
    supplies a determinate direction and conveyance
        ↓
associated cetanā
    travels according to that direction
        ↓
volition is configured under its governing Power
```

The three mental courses are therefore not passive contents accompanying
action. They are routes of formation. Covetousness directs volition as
appropriation; hostility directs it as harm; false view directs it under a
denial of practical law.

The seven bodily and verbal courses have a doubled status:

```text
karma
    realized bodily or verbal activity

path of karma
    objective basis upon which originating volition proceeds
```

This is the precise answer to our earlier question about the movement from
Course of Action toward Path. A course is already path-like because it
carries, directs, and configures practical Power.

## 6. Salience and the Wider Process

The discussion of preparation and aftermath must be held together with VAK
4.66. They are karmic activities ordered toward or rooted in a course, while
the canonical ten select the especially salient forms:

```text
wider karmic process
    preparation → principal course → aftermath

ten karmapathas
    selected governing forms within that process
```

This resolves the apparent tension in the compressed prose. The surrounding
acts derive their intelligibility from the principal course without every
minor act becoming an additional member of the canonical ten.

The claim that their increase or decrease affects both internal and external
states expands karmapatha beyond private morality. Courses structure the
quality of agents and the configuration of the shared world. The passage
does not elaborate that cosmological relation here, so its precise mechanism
remains open.

## 7. The Dārṣṭāntika Alternative

The Vaibhāṣika distinction is:

```text
three mental courses
    paths of karma

seven bodily and verbal courses
    karma + paths of karma
```

The Dārṣṭāntikas take the mental three themselves as mental karma. The
Bhāṣya's first response is pointed: their own position must explain how the
same items are also paths. It then offers two possible reconstructions:

```text
mental course
    is karma
    and a path toward good or bad destiny

or

mental courses
    mutually carry and condition one another
```

The source thus preserves more than one account of the compound. Our study
does not collapse the Dārṣṭāntika position into the Vaibhāṣika conveyance
model.

## 8. Organon Contact Point

VAK 4.78 gives the basic architecture of practical programming:

```text
Root
    supplies general Power

Mental Course
    determines direction

Cetanā
    is configured and travels through that direction

Bodily or Verbal Course
    realizes the determination as action

Aftermath
    continues the completed configuration
```

The mental course functions like a governing policy or practical syllogistic
middle. It mediates between general root and executed action. False view is
especially powerful because it can invalidate the very distinction by which
the practical process would correct itself.

This explains why the Kośa is Technē. It does not merely name prohibited
outputs; it models the path through which volition becomes configured,
executed, and world-producing. The programming analogy and layered
architecture are Organon reconstructions; the conveyance account, strict
three/seven distinction, salience qualification, and Dārṣṭāntika alternative
belong to the Bhāṣya.
