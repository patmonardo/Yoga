# VAK_4.90

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सर्वेऽतीतस्य चत्वारि मध्यमस्याप्यनागताः ।
मध्यमा द्वे अजातस्य फलानि त्रीण्यनागताः ॥ ४.९० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sarve 'tītasya catvāri madhyamasyāpy anāgatāḥ /
madhyamā dve 'jātasya phalāni trīṇy anāgatāḥ // 4.90 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sarve | atītasya | catvāri | madhyamasya | api | anāgatāḥ |
madhyamāḥ | dve | ajātasya | phalāni | trīṇi | anāgatāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `sarve` | nominative plural | all result-dharmas with respect to the three times |
| `atītasya` | genitive singular | of past karma |
| `catvāri` | nominative neuter plural | four fruit-relations |
| `madhyamasya` | genitive singular | of present karma, the middle temporal member |
| `api` | additive particle | also, likewise |
| `anāgatāḥ` | nominative plural | future result-dharmas |
| `madhyamāḥ` | nominative plural | present result-dharmas |
| `dve` | nominative neuter dual | two fruit-relations |
| `ajātasya` | genitive singular | of not-yet-arisen, future karma |
| `phalāni` | nominative neuter plural | results |
| `trīṇi` | nominative neuter plural | three fruit-relations |
| `anāgatāḥ` | nominative plural | future result-dharmas |

**Bhāṣya-resolved segmentation**

The compact verse distributes four source-time/result-time pairings:

```text
past karma    → past, present, and future dharmas: four relations
present karma → future dharmas:                  four relations
present karma → present dharmas:                 two relations
future karma  → future dharmas:                  three relations
```

`Sarve` does not mean all five fruit-types. The Bhāṣya glosses it as
`traiyadhvikāḥ`, dharmas belonging to all three times.

## 4. Grammar

**Past karma and dharmas of all times**

```text
atītasya [karmaṇaḥ]
    sarve [traiyadhvikā dharmāḥ]
    catvāri [phalāni]
```

> For past karma, dharmas of all three times are results in four ways.

The syntactic proximity of `sarve` to `atītasya` permits an initially tempting
“all fruits of the past” reading. The Bhāṣya decisively assigns `sarve` to the
temporal range of result-dharmas and `catvāri` to the fruit-relations.

**Present karma and future dharmas**

```text
madhyamasya [karmaṇaḥ] api
    anāgatāḥ [dharmāḥ]
    [catvāri phalāni]
```

> Future dharmas are likewise four results of present karma.

The four are carried forward from the preceding clause.

**Present karma and present dharmas**

```text
madhyamāḥ [pratyutpannā dharmāḥ]
    dve [phalāni]
    [madhyamasya karmaṇaḥ]
```

> Present dharmas are two results of present karma.

`Madhyamāḥ` denotes the present result-dharmas, not the middle two members of
the five-fruit list. The Bhāṣya identifies the two as activity-produced and
dominant result.

**Future karma and future dharmas**

```text
ajātasya [anāgatasya karmaṇaḥ]
    anāgatāḥ [dharmāḥ]
    trīṇi phalāni
```

> For future karma, future dharmas are results in three ways.

Here `ajāta`, not yet arisen, is explained as future karma; `anāgata` marks
the temporal status of the candidate result-dharmas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For past karma, all are four; for the middle one also, the future are four.
> The present are two; for the not-yet-arisen, future results are three.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Past karma can have result-dharmas belonging to the past, present, or future
> through four relations—maturation, correspondence, activity-production, and
> dominant conditioning; disconnection is excluded. Present karma can have
> future dharmas through those same four relations, but present dharmas through
> only activity-production and dominant conditioning. Future karma can have
> future dharmas through three relations—maturation, activity-production, and
> dominant conditioning—while correspondence and disconnection are excluded.

The temporal distribution and every exact fruit-membership are supplied by
the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Causal efficacy has no complete determination apart from the temporal
> relation of its terms. The same present karma possesses one result-profile
> with simultaneously present dharmas and another with future dharmas. Its
> causal meaning is therefore not an intrinsic signature carried unchanged
> through time; it is articulated by the temporal difference or simultaneity
> through which source and result are joined.

The minimal determination is now:

```text
source karma
    × source-time
    × result-dharma
    × result-time
    × fruit-relation
```

Hegelian-transcendental comprehension makes time internal to the causal
relation. Past, present, and future are not empty containers in which a fixed
cause is placed. They determine which form of efficacy the cause can have:
immediate production, dominant conditioning, correspondence, or maturation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Atīta` — past**

The temporal determination of karma whose present operation has passed.
Within the Sarvāstivāda analysis, past does not simply mean an absolute
nothing; past karma can stand in determinate fruit-relations to dharmas of all
three times.

**`Pratyutpanna` or `madhyama` — present**

The Bhāṣya's `pratyutpanna` resolves the verse's “middle” member of the
past-present-future sequence. Both causal karma and result-dharma can occupy
the present, but their simultaneous relation admits only two fruit-types here.

**`Anāgata` — future**

Not yet come. It qualifies result-dharmas in three clauses and, through the
Bhāṣya's explanation of `ajāta`, the future source-karma in the last clause.

**`Ajāta` — not arisen**

The verse's designation of future karma from the standpoint of non-arising.
It emphasizes that the causal operation has not yet become present.

**`Traiyadhvika` — belonging to the three times**

The Bhāṣya gloss of `sarve`. It ranges over past, present, and future
result-dharmas and prevents the false inference that past karma has all five
fruit-types.

**Present-to-present `puruṣakāra-phala`**

An activity-produced result can be co-present with the causal operation. It
expresses the active bringing-forth of associated dharmas rather than later
maturation or temporal outflow.

**Present-to-present `adhipati-phala`**

Dominant or governing efficacy can operate among present dharmas. It is the
second relation compatible with simultaneity in this classification.

**Temporal restriction of `niṣyanda`**

Correspondence belongs to past karma across all result-times and to present
karma with future dharmas, but not to present-present or future-future pairs
in this verse. Causal continuity requires an appropriate temporal structure.

**Temporal restriction of `visaṃyoga`**

Disconnection is excluded throughout this temporal table. That does not deny
its reality in the immediate path; it shows that the present verse classifies
a range in which disconnection is not one of the admitted result-relations.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Exact temporal profiles**

Let:

```text
V = vipāka
N = niṣyanda
D = visaṃyoga
P = puruṣakāra
A = adhipati
```

Then:

```text
FruitRelations(PastKarma, PastDharma)    = {V, N, P, A}
FruitRelations(PastKarma, PresentDharma) = {V, N, P, A}
FruitRelations(PastKarma, FutureDharma)  = {V, N, P, A}

FruitRelations(PresentKarma, PresentDharma) = {P, A}
FruitRelations(PresentKarma, FutureDharma)  = {V, N, P, A}

FruitRelations(FutureKarma, FutureDharma) = {V, P, A}
```

The verse does not state every logically imaginable source-time/result-time
pair. Unlisted pairs should not be assigned empty sets solely from this verse.

**Temporal matrix stated by the Bhāṣya**

| Source karma | Result-dharma time | `vipāka` | `niṣyanda` | `visaṃyoga` | `puruṣakāra` | `adhipati` |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| past | past, present, or future | yes | yes | no | yes | yes |
| present | future | yes | yes | no | yes | yes |
| present | present | no | no | no | yes | yes |
| future | future | yes | no | no | yes | yes |

**Same source, different temporal relation**

```text
PresentKarma × PresentDharma = {P, A}

PresentKarma × FutureDharma  = {V, N, P, A}
```

Therefore:

```text
source-time alone             ≠ complete causal profile
result-time alone             ≠ complete causal profile
same causal karma             ≠ one timeless relation-set
temporal difference           = determinant of causal form
```

**Why simultaneity is restricted**

```text
present operation
    → can actively bring forth present dharmas
    → can dominantly condition present dharmas

but present-present relation here
    → is not maturation
    → is not temporal correspondence
    → is not disconnection
```

The present is the station of operative production; maturation and outflow
require a differently mediated temporal relation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.90 adds time to the relational algebra completed in 4.88–4.89. Ethical
quality, contamination-status, path-function, result-quality, and fruit-type
were already insufficient in isolation. Now the same causal source supports
different result-sets according to whether its candidate result is present or
future. Causal identity is the unity of these mediations, not a hidden power
abstracted from them.

The grammatical compression stages this conceptual difficulty. `Sarve` looks
like it could quantify the five fruits; it actually quantifies the three times
of result-dharmas. `Madhyamā dve` looks like it could select two middle fruits;
it actually joins present dharmas to present karma through two relations. The
Bhāṣya restores the missing relational coordinates and thereby restores the
meaning.

The present-present restriction is philosophically revealing. In the
operative now, karma can actively produce and dominantly condition co-present
dharmas. But maturation and correspondence express more developed temporal
mediations. The result is not simply “later,” nor is the present an
infinitesimal point: each temporal relation supports determinate causal forms.

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy lets us understand why the temporal terms
cannot be added as external metadata. An operation becomes what it is through
its passage into actuality, its persistence as a past determination, and its
orientation toward what has not yet arisen. Time is the self-differentiation
of the causal movement. The Kośa's matrix is consequently a logic of temporal
actualization, not merely a chronology of rewards.

This also sharpens the higher concept of Path. A karmic course ordinarily
propagates its determination through temporal relations it does not comprehend.
Path is the course that takes this conditioned propagation into its own
practical organization: it orders present activity toward a future in which
the bond itself is no longer reproduced. The truth of action is thus not
timeless escape from causality, but disciplined transformation of its temporal
actualization.
