# VAK_4.07 Bhāṣya — Ethical Force and the Realms of Action

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nāvyākṛtāsty avijñaptiḥ tridhānyat aśubhaṃ punaḥ /
> kāme rūpe 'py avijñaptir vijñaptiḥ savicārayoḥ // 4.07 //

> Non-disclosing continuity is never ethically indeterminate: it is either
> wholesome or unwholesome. Manifestation and volition, by contrast, may each
> be wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate. Unwholesome karma is confined
> to the desire realm. Non-disclosing continuity occurs in both the desire
> realm and the Form realm, but not in the formless realms. Bodily and verbal
> manifestation occurs only in the two levels where `vicāra` remains
> operative—the desire realm and the first dhyāna.

The Bhāṣya identifies “the other” as both `vijñapti` and `cetanā`, and
the two levels as the desire realm and first dhyāna. The Kārikā study
is `VAK_4.07.md`.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“There is no ethically indeterminate non-disclosing continuity.” Why? An
ethically indeterminate consciousness is weak. It cannot project forceful
karma that would continue even after that consciousness has ceased.

“The other action is threefold.” Other karma is wholesome,
unwholesome, or ethically indeterminate. What is this other karma?
Manifestation and volition.

“The unwholesome, however, is in the desire realm.” Unwholesome karma
proper is to be understood as belonging to the desire realm and not
elsewhere, because the roots of the unwholesome, shamelessness, and lack
of moral concern have been abandoned [above it]. Wholesome and
indeterminate karma, by contrast, occur at all levels as ethical classes;
this does not mean that each mode of karma occurs at every level. The damaged
Sanskrit reason is left unresolved; parallel Chinese translations explain
that the verse does not separately rule them out.

“Non-disclosing continuity exists also in the Form realm”: the word “also”
includes the desire realm. It does not exist in the formless realms,
because Elements are absent there. Where body and speech
operate, there are restraints of body and speech.

An interlocutor asks whether one absorbed in a formless attainment
would then possess uncontaminated non-disclosing continuity, as in the case
under discussion. The answer distinguishes an uncontaminated factor,
which does not belong to a realm, from non-disclosing continuity belonging
specifically to the formless realms. The latter could not depend on
Elements of a dissimilar class. Someone who has entered a formless
attainment, turned away from all form and removed the perception of
Form, cannot thereby produce Form. Discipline counters
misconduct, and the misconduct at issue belongs to the desire realm.
The formless attainments stand far from the desire realm by support,
mode, object, and antidote. For these reasons the Vaibhāṣikas say there
is no non-disclosing continuity in those realms.

“Manifestation occurs in the two levels possessing *vicāra*.” It occurs
only in the desire realm and first dhyāna, not above them.

The formless-realm exchange is compressed and its exact distinction
between an uncontaminated factor and a realm-specific one requires
comparison with a better witness. The passage's explicit conclusion
and the two named levels are clear.

## 4. Movement and Voices

The Bhāṣya first classifies karma by ethical quality. `Avijñapti` excludes
the indeterminate because an indeterminate consciousness cannot project
karma continuing beyond itself; manifestation and volition can have all
three qualities. It then restricts unwholesome karma to the desire
realm, places non-disclosing continuity in desire and Form, excludes the
formless realms, and finally restricts manifestation to desire and
first dhyāna.

The question about formless attainment is an interlocutor's challenge.
The denial of formless-realm `avijñapti` is explicitly attributed to
the Vaibhāṣikas at the end of its argument. No named school is given
for every earlier classificatory statement.

## 5. Why Indeterminate `Avijñapti` Is Excluded

The causal reason is stronger than a bare taxonomic rule:

```text
avyākṛta citta → too weak to project forceful karma
forceful projection → required for continuation after that citta ceases
therefore no avyākṛta avijñapti
```

`Avijñapti` is thus not the hidden residue of every act. The account
requires an originating determination with sufficient force to support
continued efficacy. `Vijñapti` and `cetanā` are “the other” and remain
threefold: wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate.

## 6. Ethical Quality and Realm

The classification crosses distinct axes. Unwholesome roots and the
paired moral deficiencies are absent above the desire realm, so
unwholesome karma is assigned there. `Avijñapti` nevertheless extends
into the Form realm. The formless realms lack the Elements needed for this
Element-supported mode of action in the Vaibhāṣika account.
The damaged `sarvatrā prativedhāt` clause remains uncertain; no conclusion
about all-realm occurrence is required to establish the separate restriction
of *vijñapti* to two levels.

The source further reasons from the function of restraint as a
countermeasure to misconduct, and from the distance of formless
attainment from the desire realm. These reasons belong to the
attributed Vaibhāṣika position; the study should not silently turn
them into a universal claim that no ethical discipline can operate in
any sense during formless attainment.

## 7. Manifestation and Vicāra

The locative dual `savicārayoḥ` is resolved by the prose as exactly
two levels: desire and first dhyāna. The verse is classifying
`vijñapti`, manifest bodily and verbal karma. It is not claiming that
all cognition or all karmic efficacy ends above first dhyāna. The
following verse asks a narrower further question about afflicted
manifestation within the desire realm.

## 8. Translation and Doctrinal Limits

“Ethically indeterminate” renders `avyākṛta`; “unwholesome” renders
`akuśala` and the verse's `aśubha`. `Ākṣip` is rendered “project” to
retain the causal claim that an originating mind can establish a
continuing karma. `Vicāra` remains a technical contemplative factor;
the two relevant levels are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

The line ending `prativedhāt` needs independent Sanskrit witness
comparison. The [parallel Chinese translations](https://www2.hf.uio.no/polyglotta/index.php?cid=503127&level=1&page=fulltext&vid=511&view=fulltext)
support the sense “because the verse does not separately exclude them”;
they do not establish the missing Sanskrit letters. Several compressed
clauses in the formless argument also remain provisional.

## 9. Interpretation

VAK 4.07 distinguishes ethical quality, mode of karma, and realm. The Bhāṣya
excludes ethically indeterminate `avijñapti` because indeterminate
consciousness is too weak to project forceful karma that continues after it
ceases. It identifies the remaining modes as `vijñapti` and `cetanā`, each
threefold; it then states the restrictions on unwholesome karma,
`avijñapti`, and `vijñapti`. The formless-realm explanation is attributed to
the Vaibhāṣikas.

In the Kośa's broader system, Discriminative Cognition (*vijñāna*) governs
Mind (*manas*) and guides Dharma Base; it remains distinct from the
consciousness (*citta*) whose causal strength is discussed here. The same
`avijñapti` is classified in Form Base and Dharma Base, with *vijñāna* bearing
the stated *prati* relation to it.
