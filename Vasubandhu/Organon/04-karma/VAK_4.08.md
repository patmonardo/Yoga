# VAK_4.08 — Manifestation and Two Grounds of Goodness

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कामेऽपि निवृता नास्ति समुत्थानमसद्यतः ।
>
> परमार्थशुभो मोक्षः स्वतो मूलह्य्रपत्रपाः ॥ ४.८ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kāme 'pi nivṛtā nāsti samutthānam asad yataḥ /
>
> paramārthaśubho mokṣaḥ svato mūlahryapatrapāḥ // 4.8 //

VAK 4.07 classified karma by ethical quality and realm. VAK 4.08 first
explains the absence of obscured-indeterminate manifestation in the desire
realm, then begins a fourfold account of the grounds on which a Dharma is
classified as wholesome. This verse determines the first two grounds;
VAK 4.09 continues with the remaining two.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
kāme api nivṛtā [vijñaptiḥ] na asti /
samutthānam asat yataḥ /
paramārtha-śubhaḥ mokṣaḥ /
svataḥ mūla-hrī-apatrapāḥ
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| kāme | locative singular | in the desire realm |
| api | particle | even; also |
| nivṛtā | nominative feminine singular adjective | obscured; the Bhāṣya supplies `vijñaptiḥ` as its subject |
| na asti | negation and third-person singular present | does not exist |
| samutthānam | nominative neuter singular | originating condition |
| asat | nominative neuter singular adjective | absent; qualifies the originating condition |
| yataḥ | causal adverb | because |
| paramārtha-śubhaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | wholesome ultimately |
| mokṣaḥ | nominative masculine singular | liberation; identified by the Bhāṣya with nirvāṇa |
| svataḥ | indeclinable ablatival adverb | intrinsically; by its own nature |
| mūla-hrī-apatrapāḥ | compressed list in the nominative plural | the wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya`, as resolved by the Bhāṣya |

The verse leaves `vijñaptiḥ` understood in its first clause. The Bhāṣya
supplies it explicitly. Its final compound is likewise compressed; the
commentary resolves it as the three wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya`.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda places the understood subject in the locative `kāme`; `yataḥ` introduces the causal clause `samutthānam asat`. The second pāda contains two nominal predications: `mokṣaḥ paramārthaśubhaḥ`, and the factors `mūla`, `hrī`, and `apatrapā` qualified by `svataḥ`.

## 5. Translation

### Close Translation

> Even in the desire realm, obscured [manifestation] does not occur, because
> its originating condition is absent. Liberation is wholesome ultimately;
> the roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya` are wholesome intrinsically.

### Bhāṣya-informed Study Translation

> Obscured-indeterminate manifestation does not occur in the desire realm; the
> Bhāṣya reports it in Brahmaloka. The commentary explains its absence in the
> desire realm, and above Brahmaloka, by the absence of the required
> originating consciousness. Liberation—nirvāṇa—is wholesome ultimately,
> because it is the complete pacification of suffering and supreme security.
> The three wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya` are wholesome by intrinsic
> nature, not through dependence on another wholesome factor.

The Brahmaloka example, the alternative higher-dhyāna account, the
`vitarka`-`vicāra` condition, and the explanations of the two ethical grounds
are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Interpretation

The first half explains a restriction on obscured-indeterminate
manifestation. The second half prevents that causal explanation from becoming
a universal rule for ethical classification: the Bhāṣya distinguishes four
grounds—ultimate, intrinsic, associative, and originating-condition—and
explains only the first two here.

In the Kośa's broader system, Discriminative Cognition (*vijñāna*) governs
Mind (*manas*) and guides Dharma Base; it is distinct from the consciousness
(*citta*) discussed as a producing condition in this passage. The same
`avijñapti` is classified in Form Base and Dharma Base, with *vijñāna*
bearing the stated *prati* relation to it.

## 8. Textual Notes

The natural unit runs from [201|18] through [202|12] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`; `saṃprayogeṇa tadyuktāḥ` at [202|13]
begins VAK 4.09.

The running witness reads `nivṛktāvyākṛtā`, `kṣiptavāt`, and `svabhāevna`,
and gives the final verse phrase in a rough form. These readings are retained
in the source record; the study follows the Bhāṣya's explanation without
silently normalizing the witness. The Sanskrit describes Mahābrahmā's
deceitful act as bodily action, while the [parallel Chinese translations](https://www2.hf.uio.no/polyglotta/index.php?cid=503127&level=1&page=fulltext&vid=511&view=fulltext)
describe speech and self-praise. The precise form of that anecdotal act
remains unsettled.
