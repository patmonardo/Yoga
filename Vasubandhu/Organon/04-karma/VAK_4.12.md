# VAK_4.12 — Ethical Variation Within an Action

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
प्रवर्तके शुभादौ हि स्यात्त्रिधाप्यनुवर्तकम् ।
तुल्यं मुनेः शुभं यावन्नोभयं तु विपाकजम् ॥ ४.१२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
pravartake śubhādau hi syāt tridhāpy anuvartakam /
tulyaṃ muneḥ śubhaṃ yāvan nobhayaṃ tu vipākajam // 4.12 //
```

VAK 4.10 distinguished initiating from accompanying cognition; VAK 4.11
classified kinds of Cognition by those functions. VAK 4.12 asks whether
the ethical quality of the accompanying cognition must match the initiator.
The Bhāṣya says no as a general rule, then gives a qualified Buddha-case and
excludes maturation-born cognition from both functions.

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |

| śubha-ādau | locative singular compound | wholesome and the other ethical classes |
| hi | emphatic particle | indeed; marks the stated possibility |
| syāt | third-person singular optative | may be; can be |
| tridhā | indeclinable adverb | in three ways: wholesome, unwholesome, indeterminate |
| api | particle | even; still |
| anuvartakam | nominative neuter singular | the accompanying Cognition |
| tulyam | nominative neuter singular adjective | corresponding; matching |
| muneḥ | genitive singular | of the Sage; identified by the Bhāṣya as the Buddha |
| śubham | nominative neuter singular | wholesome |
| yāvat | limit adverb | up to; within the limit of |
| na ubhayam | negation and nominative neuter singular | neither [initiating nor accompanying] |
| vipāka-jam | nominative neuter singular compound adjective | born from karmic maturation |

The Bhāṣya expands `śubha-ādi` into the three ethical classes:
`kuśala`, `akuśala`, and `avyākṛta`. It explains the compressed Buddha-clause
as an asymmetric correspondence, not an exceptionless identity.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause places the initiating condition in the locative `pravartake śubhādau` and predicates `tridhāpy anuvartakam` of what accompanies it. The second clause gives a parallel nominal phrase for the Sage; the final `na ubhayam ... vipākajam` is a negated nominal predication.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> When the initiator is wholesome and so forth, the accompanying Cognition
> may nevertheless be threefold. For the Sage, [the two are] corresponding,
> within the wholesome limit. Maturation-born [Cognition], however, is
> neither.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> When the initiator is wholesome and so forth, the accompanier may nevertheless
> be of any of the three ethical kinds. For the Sage, they correspond within the
> wholesome range. Maturation-born Consciousness is neither.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> When the initiator is wholesome and so forth, the accompanying [Cognition] may nevertheless be threefold. For the Sage, [the two are] corresponding, within the wholesome limit. Maturation-born [Cognition], however, is neither.

## 8. Logical Determination

For ordinary agents, the accompanier can take any of the three ethical
qualities whatever the initiator's quality. The Buddha case restricts this
range asymmetrically: wholesome initiation has wholesome accompaniment,
while indeterminate initiation can have either indeterminate or wholesome
accompaniment. Maturation-born Consciousness performs neither function.
The subsequent objection tests which function determines the ethical
quality of manifestation; the reply favors initiation while qualifying
which causal initiator is relevant when another Consciousness intervenes.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** For ordinary action-series, the accompanier may be wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate whatever the initiator's quality. For the Buddha, a wholesome initiator has a wholesome accompanier; an indeterminate initiator may be followed by an indeterminate or wholesome accompanier, while a wholesome initiator is not followed by an indeterminate one. Maturation-born Consciousness is neither initiating nor accompanying because it proceeds without deliberate construction. These qualifications come from the Bhāṣya.

### Textual Notes

The Bhāṣya unit begins with `kiṃ khalu yathā pravartakaṃ tathaivānuvartakaṃ
bhavati` at [204|11] and ends with `avasitaḥ prasaṅgaḥ` at [205|11] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The following passage begins the
discussion of `avijñapti` and restraint.

The running witness reads `vipāpkajam`, `kuślaṃ`, and a compressed list of
indeterminate Buddha-Cognitions; these are retained in the Bhāṣya study.
The final phrase `anyavyavahitaṃ hetusamutthānaṃ` is translated
provisionally as causal origination separated by an intervening cognition.
The closing objection and this qualification require further witness
comparison.

### Interpretation

The verse distinguishes causal continuity from sameness of ethical quality
at every cognitive stage. The Buddha-case is specially qualified; the
Bhāṣya also preserves a disagreement about whether the Buddha has
indeterminate consciousness. Its final exchange assigns ethical
classification of manifestation to the initiating causal sequence while
qualifying which initiator is relevant.

In the Kośa's broader system, Discriminative Cognition (*vijñāna*) governs
Mind (*manas*) and guides Dharma Base; it remains distinct from
consciousness (*citta*) and manifestation (*vijñapti*) in this passage. The
same `avijñapti` is classified in Form Base and Dharma Base, with
*vijñāna* bearing the stated *prati* relation to it.
