# VAK_4.01 — Cetanā and the Differentiated World

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कर्मजं लोकवैचित्र्यं चेतना तत्कृतं च तत् ।
>
> चेतना मानसं कर्म तज्जं वाक्कायकर्मणी ॥ ४.१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> karmajaṃ lokavaicitryaṃ cetanā tatkṛtaṃ ca tat /
>
> cetanā mānasaṃ karma tajjaṃ vākkāyakarmaṇī // 4.1 //

The opening statement joins Karmanirdeśa directly to the differentiated World
described in Lokanirdeśa. The Kārikā first names that World as karma-born and
then identifies Cetanā as karma itself before distinguishing the action
produced from it.
The Bhāṣya specifies that `loka` here includes both the world of beings and the
receptacle-world, and that the relevant karma is the karma of beings.

| lokavaicitryam | nominative neuter singular compound | differentiated manifoldness of the world |
| cetanā | nominative feminine singular | volition; action-directing mental determination |
| tatkṛtam | nominative neuter singular compound | what is produced or done by that volition |
| ca | coordinating particle | and |

The final pāda is compressed. `Tajjaṃ` is neuter singular whereas
`vākkāyakarmaṇī` is neuter dual. The Bhāṣya resolves the intended relation by
identifying the action generated by volition (`cetanājanita`, `cetayitvā
karma`) with bodily and verbal action. A literal reading therefore supplies
`karma` with `tajjaṃ` rather than silently regularizing the witness.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause predicates `karma-jam` of `loka-vaicitryam`. The second identifies `tat karma` with the coordinated terms `cetanā` and `tat-kṛtam`. The final line predicates `mānasaṃ karma` of Cetanā and names the action produced from it as bodily and verbal.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The differentiation of the world is born from karma. That [karma] is
> volition and what is produced by it. Volition is mental action; action born
> from it is verbal and bodily action.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The manifold differentiation of the world of beings and their
> receptacle-world is generated by the karmas of beings. Karma comprises
> Cetanā (volition) itself and action produced after intending: Cetanā is
> mental action, while the bodily and verbal actions produced from it are
> action after having willed.

The specification of the two worlds, the attribution to beings' karmas, and
the equivalence of `tatkṛta` with `cetayitvā karma` are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Cetanā in the Verse

The verse defines karma through two connected but distinct modes: Cetanā,
and what is produced from Cetanā. It then identifies Cetanā as mental karma,
while `tajjaṃ` refers to the bodily and verbal actions born from it. Cetanā
is therefore named as karma in its own right, not merely as an intention
preceding karma. The Bhāṣya clarifies this distinction through
`cetanā karma` and `cetayitvā karma`.

The distinction is not between karma and a prior mental cause. Cetanā is
already mental karma; `tatkṛta` and `cetayitvā karma` name what is produced
from that volitional determination. This local definition does not make
Cetanā a synonym for all mental activity.

`Tatkṛta` establishes genetic dependence:

```text
cetanā
    → bodily and verbal action
```

It does not yet explain how a vanished action remains efficacious. That
problem opens with `vijñapti` and `avijñapti` in the following unit.

## 8. Logical Determination


`karma-ja` → differentiated world; `karma` → Cetanā and what it produces; Cetanā → mental action; its product → bodily and verbal action.

## 9. Interpretive Note

Karmanirdeśa begins as the existential return into the ground of Loka.
Lokanirdeśa displayed a World already differentiated into beings,
receptacles, destinies, embodiments, durations, enjoyments, and dissolutions.
The opening question of Chapter 4 is not simply, “Which deeds are good or
bad?” It is:

```text
How does this differentiated existence come to be?
```

The first answer is `karmaja`: it is born from activity. The second answer
locates the nucleus of that activity in `cetanā`. Thus the chapter moves from
the accomplished fact of existence to the activity by which existence is
determined.

This does not identify `cetanā` with an independent metaphysical subject.
The Kārikā says positively that volition **is mental action**. Nor does it say
that mind alone immediately creates the World. It articulates a mediated
sequence in which inward determination generates bodily and verbal action,
and karmic efficacy generates differentiated result.

The Bhāṣya's mixed-karma objection prevents a crude correspondence theory.
Attractive enjoyments can coexist with disagreeable embodiment because a
continuum's karmic determination can be mixed. Karma is therefore not a
single deed mirrored by a single visibly matching result; it is a structured
causal complex whose differentiated results can appear on different sides of
existence.

The controlled Organon formulation is:

```text
Karma
    = existentially effective determination

not because every action creates being from nothing,
but because volitional activity enters the causal production
of the mode of conditioned existence that can be lived and undergone.
```

This is the chapter's first determination, not yet its completed theory. The
remaining inquiry must establish expression, persistence, ethical quality,
appropriation, maturation, and release.
