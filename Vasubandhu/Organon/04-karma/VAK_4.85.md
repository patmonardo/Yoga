# VAK_4.85

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सर्वेऽधिपतिनिष्यन्दविपाकफलदा मताः ।
दुःखनान्मारणादोजोनाशनात्त्रिविधं फलम् ॥ ४.८५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sarve 'dhipati-niṣyanda-vipāka-phaladā matāḥ /
duḥkhanān māraṇād ojo-nāśanāt trividhaṃ phalam // 4.85 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sarve | adhipati-niṣyanda-vipāka-phala-dāḥ | matāḥ |
duḥkhanāt | māraṇāt | ojas-nāśanāt | trividham | phalam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `sarve` | nominative masculine plural | all the wholesome and unwholesome courses of action |
| `adhipati-phala` | determinative compound | dominant result; conditioning of the broader field |
| `niṣyanda-phala` | determinative compound | outflow or corresponding result |
| `vipāka-phala` | determinative compound | maturation result |
| `-dāḥ` | nominative masculine plural from `-da` | giving, productive of |
| `matāḥ` | nominative masculine plural past passive participle of √`man` | held, recognized, understood |
| `duḥkhanāt` | ablative singular action noun | from causing suffering |
| `māraṇāt` | ablative singular action noun | from killing or causing death |
| `ojas-nāśanāt` | ablative singular compound | from destroying vitality |
| `trividham` | accusative neuter singular | threefold |
| `phalam` | accusative neuter singular | result, fruit |

**Sandhi in the second pāda**

The transmitted forms are resolved as:

```text
duḥkhanāt + māraṇāt
    → duḥkhanān māraṇāt

māraṇāt + ojas-nāśanāt
    → māraṇād ojo-nāśanāt
```

The apparently anomalous `duḥkhanān` is therefore not treated as a separate
lexeme. The Bhāṣya confirms three coordinated causal ablatives and assigns one
to each result.

**Scope of “all”**

The Bhāṣya expands the understood subject:

```text
ten unwholesome karmapathas
and
ten wholesome karmapathas
```

The second half uses killing as the explanatory model; the same differentiated
result-structure is then extended to the other courses.

## 4. Grammar

**General predication**

```text
sarve [karmapathāḥ]
    adhipati-phala-dāḥ
    niṣyanda-phala-dāḥ
    vipāka-phala-dāḥ
    matāḥ
```

> All courses of action are held to give dominant, corresponding, and
> maturation results.

The long compound coordinates three objects of `-dāḥ`. It does not name one
undifferentiated composite fruit. `Matāḥ` marks the received doctrinal
determination.

**Three causal ablatives**

```text
duḥkhanāt
māraṇāt
ojas-nāśanāt
    trividhaṃ phalam [bhavati]
```

> From causing suffering, causing death, and destroying vitality, the result
> is threefold.

The Bhāṣya fixes the one-to-one construal:

```text
causing suffering       → maturation result
causing death           → corresponding result
destroying vitality     → dominant result
```

This mapping is commentary-dependent. The kārikā gives the coordinated
grounds; the Bhāṣya identifies their exact result-relations through the
example of killing.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> All are held to give dominant, corresponding, and maturation results. From
> causing suffering, causing death, and destroying vitality, the result is
> threefold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> All ten unwholesome and all ten wholesome courses of action are understood
> to produce results in three differentiated causal registers: maturation of
> the agent's mode of existence, a later result corresponding to the course,
> and conditioning of the broader field. Killing displays the rationale. By
> causing another to suffer, one undergoes suffering through maturation in
> hell; by ending another's life, one later becomes short-lived through a
> corresponding result; and by destroying another's vitality, one conditions
> a field whose medicines and supports possess little vitality. The other
> unwholesome courses are to be analyzed through the same structure, while
> wholesome courses yield the contrary results through systematic reversal.

The rebirth, human, environmental, obstruction-ground, and wholesome-reversal
details belong to the Bhāṣya's explanation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A deed does not vanish into the past when its visible performance ends. Its
> determination becomes objective in several irreducible relations: it forms
> the agent's future actuality, returns as a corresponding determination of
> the agent's life, and conditions the world in which agency must operate. The
> consequence is neither a detached reward nor the mechanical persistence of
> an identical thing. It is the differentiated actuality of the deed itself,
> propagated according to the aspect of life that the deed negated or
> sustained.

The Hegelian-transcendental comprehension of the passage is:

```text
subjective purpose enacted
    → objective deed
    → differentiated consequence
        ├── actuality of the agent
        ├── corresponding determination of its life
        └── constitution of its objective field
    → objectivity returns into the constitution of subjectivity
```

Karma therefore completes neither as a vanished event nor as a ledger entry.
It completes as a world-producing and agent-producing determination. This is
why the Karma chapter can conclude Objective Logic and prepare the transition
to Anuśaya: what action has produced objectively will appear next as the
conditioned interior organization of the agent.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vipāka-phala` — maturation result**

Result at the level of a future mode of existence. For unwholesome courses
that have been practiced, cultivated, and made abundant, the Bhāṣya gives
rebirth in hell as the maturation result. For wholesome courses it gives the
contrary direction, exemplified by divine rebirth after cultivated abstention
from killing.

**`Niṣyanda-phala` — outflow or corresponding result**

A later determination specially corresponding to the qualitative form of the
course: killing with short life, theft with loss of possessions, divisive
speech with broken friendships, and so forth. `Outflow` must not suggest that
one numerically identical substance travels from the deed into the result.

**`Sādṛśya-viśeṣa` — special relation of similarity**

The Bhāṣya's reason for calling the later determination a `niṣyanda-phala`.
This distinctive correspondence differentiates the result conceptually even
though it does not constitute an entirely separate causal universe beyond the
broader classes of maturation and dominant result.

**`Adhipati-phala` — dominant result**

The conditioning of the external or shared field. Repeated courses deform or
sustain the efficacy of surroundings: vitality, terrain, seasons, fruits,
medicinal supports, and other conditions of embodied life. `Adhipati` here
names governing causal efficacy, not a sovereign entity standing behind the
field.

**`Āsevita`, `bhāvita`, `bahulīkṛta` — practiced, cultivated, made abundant**

The Bhāṣya stresses repetition and stabilization. Strong fruition is not
explained by an isolated physical occurrence alone but by a karmic
determination repeatedly made effective in a continuum.

**`Antarāya-hetu` — ground of obstruction**

The decisive clarification concerning short life. Killing does not directly
produce the very wholesome lifespan later lived by a human. It operates as an
obstruction-ground relative to that lifespan, limiting a result that has its
own productive karmic grounds.

**`Duḥkhana` — causing suffering**

In the killing analysis, injury to sentient experience corresponds to the
agent's later suffering through maturation.

**`Māraṇa` — causing death**

Interruption of another's life corresponds to the agent's later shortness of
life. The correspondence is causally mediated and not numerical identity.

**`Ojas-nāśana` — destruction of vitality**

The deed's impairment of operative vitality corresponds to diminished
vitality in the environmental field and its supports.

**Wholesome reversal**

The Bhāṣya directs that the wholesome results be understood by reversal of
the unwholesome pattern. Abstention from killing, for example, leads toward a
favorable maturation, long life, and strengthened supporting conditions.
Reversal is systematic determination, not the arbitrary attachment of an
opposite reward.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Three registers of efficacy**

| Result | Primary register | Killing example | Logical relation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vipāka-phala` | mode of existence | suffering in hell | maturation of the agent |
| `niṣyanda-phala` | corresponding later life | short life | special causal correspondence |
| `adhipati-phala` | broader objective field | supports with little vitality | dominant conditioning |

The three are not merely consecutive times. They discriminate what the same
completed determination does in different relations.

**The full unwholesome correspondence supplied by the Bhāṣya**

| Course | Corresponding result | Dominant field-result |
|---|---|---|
| killing | short life | external things have little vitality |
| taking what is not given | loss of possessions | destructive environmental disturbances |
| sexual misconduct | unsatisfactory partner relations | polluted or dusty surroundings |
| false speech | frequent false accusation | foul-smelling conditions |
| divisive speech | broken friendships | uneven and broken terrain |
| harsh speech | disagreeable sounds | barren and hostile ground |
| disordered speech | words without authority | irregular seasons |
| covetousness | intense greed | dried-up fruits |
| ill will | intense hatred | bitter fruits |
| false view | intense delusion | meager or fruitless yields |

These correspondences express the deed's qualitative form across subjective,
relational, and environmental registers. The historically described field is
retained as the Bhāṣya's causal exposition rather than reduced to modern
physical causation.

**Obstruction is not sole production**

```text
karma producing a human lifespan
    → productive ground of that lifespan

past killing
    → obstruction-ground relative to that lifespan
    → shortness of the lifespan
```

Therefore:

```text
causal contribution             ≠ sole productive ground
corresponding result             ≠ numerically identical prior result
qualitative similarity           ≠ unmediated repetition
one deed                         ≠ one undifferentiated consequence
```

**Internal rationale of threefold result**

```text
killing
├── negates sentient well-being
│   └── vipāka: suffering of the agent
├── negates the course of life
│   └── niṣyanda: obstruction and short life
└── negates operative vitality
    └── adhipati: weakened vitality of the field
```

The deed is universal in consequence because its determinate negativity is
not confined to the victim at the instant of action. The same form returns
through the agent, its subsequent relations, and its world.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.85 gives a rational articulation of karmic consequence rather than a
catalogue of supernatural rewards and punishments. The Bhāṣya asks what aspect
of the deed is actualized in each result. Causing suffering, ending life, and
destroying vitality are distinguishable determinations within killing; each
establishes a correspondingly distinct causal relation. Karma is intelligible
because the result preserves the deed's form through transformation, not
because an invisible object travels unchanged across time.

The objection concerning short human life is philosophically decisive. Human
life is itself a wholesome maturation, so killing cannot simply be declared
the producer of that very life. Vasubandhu instead determines killing as an
`antarāya-hetu`: it obstructs and qualifies a life produced through other
grounds. This is a sophisticated account of conditioned causality in which
several determinations cooperate without being collapsed into one total
cause.

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy makes the architecture explicit. In action,
subjective purpose passes into objectivity; once objectified, the deed acquires
relations and consequences that exceed the agent's private intention. Yet
those consequences are not alien accidents. They are the truth of what the
action was. `Vipāka` shows the deed forming the actuality of the agent;
`niṣyanda` shows its determinate form returning within later life; `adhipati`
shows it constituting the objective field of agency.

This gives the Karma chapter its systematic station. Loka is not a finished
world merely encountered by subjects. Through Karma, practical activity
continually produces both the character of the world and the conditions under
which subjects reappear within it. Objective Logic therefore reaches its
completion when objectivity reveals itself as the result of action and returns
as the presupposition of the acting subject. Anuśaya can then begin from that
result as inwardly sedimented conditioning.
