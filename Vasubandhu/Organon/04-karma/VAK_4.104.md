# VAK_4.104 — Intention, Preparation, and the Path

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
बुद्धे न ताडनेच्छस्य प्रहारान्नोर्ध्वमर्हति ।
नानन्तर्यप्रयुक्तस्य वैराग्यफलसम्भवः ॥ ४.१०४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
buddhe na tāḍanecchasya prahārān nordhvam arhati /
nānantaryaprayuktasya vairāgyaphalasaṃbhavaḥ // 4.104 //
```

The first half is exceptionally compressed. The Bhāṣya resolves it
through two cases: intention only to strike in the Buddha case, and a
person who becomes an arhat only after an earlier action. Its reading of
`prahārān nordhvam arhati` is therefore followed cautiously below.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
buddhe | na | tāḍana-icchasya | prahārān | na | ūrdhvam | arhati |
na | ānantarya-prayuktasya | vairāgya-phala-saṃbhavaḥ
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `buddhe` | locative masculine singular | in the Buddha case |
| `na` | negative particle | excludes the stated classification |
| `tāḍana-icchasya` | genitive masculine singular compound | for one whose intention is only to strike |
| `prahārān` | sandhied form in a compressed phrase | the Bhāṣya resolves it as “after the blow” |
| `na` | negative particle | negates the second case |
| `ūrdhvam` | indeclinable adverb | afterward |
| `arhati` | compressed/ambiguous form | understood by the Bhāṣya as the victim becoming an arhat afterward |
| `ānantarya-prayuktasya` | genitive masculine singular compound | for one engaged in preparation for an immediate-result action |
| `vairāgya-phala-saṃbhavaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | possibility of dispassion or a Path fruit |

The first-half syntax is not expanded beyond the Bhāṣya's two cases. The
question preceding the final line adds the condition that the preparation
has not been reversed (`avyāvartite`).

## 4. Grammar

The Bhāṣya resolves the first half as two exclusions:

```text
intention only to strike in the Buddha case
    → no immediate-result classification

action directed at a non-arhat
    + arhat status attained only afterward
    → no retroactive arhat-killing classification
```

The second half states:

```text
ānantarya-prayuktasya
    vairāgya-phala-saṃbhavaḥ na [asti]
```

The genitive identifies the person engaged in the preparation; the
Bhāṣya's preceding question specifies that this preparation remains
unreversed. Its prose then contrasts this with an ordinary action-course
that does not arise again after the noble Path arises, because the support
has become wholly opposed to it.

## 5. Translation

### Scientific English Rendering

> In the Buddha case, [there is no immediate-result classification] for
> one whose intention is only to strike; nor [does it apply] when the person
> becomes an arhat after the blow. For one engaged in an immediate-result
> action, the possibility of dispassion or fruit does not arise.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Intention merely to strike in the Buddha case does not produce the
> immediate-result classification; nor does an earlier action against a
> non-arhat become an arhat-killing action if the person becomes an arhat
> only afterward. For one engaged in an immediate-result action, the
> possibility of dispassion or a Path fruit does not arise. The Bhāṣya's
> question specifies that the preparation remains unreversed. It then
> contrasts this with another action-course: when the noble Path arises,
> that course does not arise again because its support is wholly opposed
> to it.

The final contrast is Bhāṣya prose, not an additional clause of the
kārikā. The witness's `anyakarmaphalapathaprayogaṃ` in that prose is
uncertain; the precise compound is not silently normalized here.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The classification depends on the field toward which the action is
> prepared and on that field's status at the time of preparation. A later
> change in the victim's status does not retroactively alter the earlier
> undertaking. The verse's final claim concerns an immediate-result
> preparation; the Bhāṣya qualifies it as unreversed. In the contrasting
> case, the arising of the noble Path changes the support so that an
> incompatible ordinary action-course does not arise again.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

- **`Tāḍanecchā / tāḍanābhiprāya`** — desire or intention only to strike.
  The Bhāṣya contrasts it with intention to kill
  (`vadhābhiprāya`).
- **`Prayoga`** — preparation or operative undertaking directed toward a
  particular field and end.
- **`Avyāvartita`** — unreversed or not abandoned. This condition occurs
  in the Bhāṣya's question; do not silently insert it into the verse.
- **`Ānantarya`** — the immediate-result classification discussed here.
- **`Vairāgya`** — dispassion; `phala` is the Path fruit at issue in the
  Bhāṣya's question.
- **`Āśraya`** — support. The Bhāṣya says the arising of the noble Path
  makes the support wholly opposed to the ordinary action-course.
- **`Abhiprāya` / `icchā`** — intention or desire in the stated cases.
  The passage does not name *cetanā*; these terms are not silently
  equated with the project's technical rendering **volition**.

## 8. Logical Determination

```text
Immediate-result classification:
    intended field is qualified at the time of preparation
    + the relevant action-course is undertaken

No retroactive qualification:
    non-arhat at time of action
    + arhat status only afterward
    → earlier action is not reclassified as arhat-directed

Attainment:
    unreversed ānantarya preparation
    → no arising of dispassion or Path fruit

Contrasting course:
    ordinary action-course prepared
    + noble Path arises
    → no later arising of that action-course
       because the support is opposed to it
```

The last relation is the Bhāṣya's explanatory contrast. Its final
Sanskrit compound is uncertain in the witness.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The passage separates intention, preparation, field-status, completion,
and later attainment rather than treating the physical result as a
self-sufficient classification. It also distinguishes two process
directions: an unreversed immediate-result preparation excludes
dispassion or Path fruit, while the arising of the noble Path excludes
the later arising of an incompatible ordinary action-course.

The intention terms here are *icchā* and *abhiprāya*, not *cetanā*. The
passage therefore informs the study of agency without supplying a new
definition of volition.
