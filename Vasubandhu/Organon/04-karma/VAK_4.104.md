# VAK_4.104

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
बुद्धे न ताडनेच्छस्य प्रहारान्नोर्ध्वमर्हति ।
नानन्तर्यप्रयुक्तस्य वैराग्यफलसम्भवः ॥ ४.१०४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
buddhe na tāḍanecchasya prahārān nordhvam arhati /
nānantaryaprayuktasya vairāgyaphalasaṃbhavaḥ // 4.104 //
```

The sandhied sequence `prahārān nordhvam` represents the Bhāṣya-resolved
construction `prahārāt na ūrdhvam`: “nor when [the victim] becomes an arhat
after the blow.” The kārikā is exceptionally compressed; the two exclusion
cases cannot be recovered safely from surface wording alone.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
buddhe | na | tāḍana-icchasya | prahārāt | na | ūrdhvam | arhati |
na | ānantarya-prayuktasya | vairāgya-phala-saṃbhavaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `buddhe` | locative masculine singular | with respect to a Buddha as the qualified field |
| `na` | negative particle | not; no immediate-result karma in the stated case |
| `tāḍana-icchasya` | genitive masculine singular compound | for one whose intention is merely to strike |
| `prahārāt` | ablative masculine singular | after the blow |
| `na` | negative particle | nor; no immediate-result karma in the second case |
| `ūrdhvam` | adverb | afterward, subsequently |
| `arhati` | compressed form resolved by the Bhāṣya through later arhat-status | when the victim becomes an arhat afterward |
| `ānantarya-prayuktasya` | genitive masculine singular | for one engaged in preparation for an immediate-result karma |
| `vairāgya-phala-saṃbhavaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | possibility of dispassion or a noble fruit |

**Bhāṣya-resolved double exclusion**

```text
Buddha-field + bloodshed + intention merely to strike
    → not the Buddha-bloodshed ānantarya

blow against a non-arhat + later attainment of arhatship + death from blow
    → not the arhat-killing ānantarya
```

**Bhāṣya-resolved final condition**

```text
ānantarya-prayoga
    + avyāvṛtti, non-reversal
    → no arising of vairāgya or Path-fruit
```

The non-reversal condition is stated in the prose and must be attributed to
it rather than inserted into the literal translation.

## 4. Grammar

**Intention merely to strike a Buddha**

```text
buddhe
    tāḍana-icchasya
    [ānantaryaṃ] na [bhavati]
```

> With respect to a Buddha, there is no [immediate-result karma] for one who
> wished merely to strike.

The locative identifies the qualified field; the genitive identifies the
agent through the intended end. The Bhāṣya contrasts `tāḍanābhiprāya`,
intention to strike, with `vadhābhiprāya`, intention to kill.

**Arhatship acquired after the blow**

```text
prahārāt ūrdhvam
    [arhan syāt]
    [tadā api ānantaryaṃ] na [bhavati]
```

> Nor [is there immediate-result karma] if the victim becomes an arhat after
> the blow.

The prose supplies the full temporal case: the victim was not an arhat when
struck, later became one, and subsequently died from the earlier wound. The
assailant's preparation was not directed toward an arhat.

**Exclusion of dispassion and fruit**

```text
ānantarya-prayuktasya
    vairāgya-phala-saṃbhavaḥ
    na [asti]
```

> For one engaged in preparation for an immediate-result karma, there is no
> possibility of dispassion or fruit.

The genitive depends on `saṃbhavaḥ`. The negative at the beginning of the
compound sentence governs the possibility of both `vairāgya` and `phala`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> With respect to a Buddha, [there is no immediate-result karma] for one who
> intended merely to strike; nor [is there arhat-killing] when arhatship comes
> after the blow. For one engaged in preparation for an immediate-result
> karma, there is no possibility of dispassion or fruit.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Causing a Buddha's blood to flow does not constitute the immediate-result
> karma when the agent intended merely to strike rather than to kill. Nor does
> an earlier assault become the immediate-result karma of killing an arhat if
> the victim was not an arhat when struck but attained arhatship afterward and
> later died from the wound. In neither case was the operative preparation
> directed toward the requisite qualified field. Once preparation for an
> immediate-result karma has been undertaken and remains unreversed, however,
> neither liberating dispassion nor a fruit of the noble Path can arise.

The two complete scenarios, the intention-to-kill requirement, the
field-directed analysis of preparation, and the non-reversal condition are
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The ethical identity of an activity is determined through the unity of its
> projected end, qualified object, operative preparation, and completion. A
> physical result cannot by itself rewrite the end that was undertaken, and a
> later transformation of the object cannot retroactively become the field of
> an earlier preparation. Yet an unreversed preparation for the most radical
> course already closes the possibility of the Path-transformation that might
> interrupt an ordinary unwholesome course.

The rational form is:

```text
projected end
    + object as qualified at the time of preparation
    + operative undertaking
    + appropriate completion
    → complete karmic determination
```

And the practical opposition is:

```text
ordinary unwholesome preparation
    → remains interruptible by Path-arising

unreversed immediate-result preparation
    → excludes dispassion and Path-fruit
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vadhābhiprāya` — intention to kill**

The Bhāṣya's required projected end for the immediate-result karma involving
bloodshed against a Buddha. Hostility and physical injury do not suffice when
the intention was limited to striking.

**`Tāḍanābhiprāya` — intention merely to strike**

The excluded intention. Even when the blow causes blood to flow, the act does
not acquire the technical classification whose preparation is directed
toward killing the Buddha.

**`Qualified karmic field`**

The object under the determination relevant to the karma: Buddha or arhat,
not merely a person who later bears that status. The field must be qualified
when the agent undertakes the preparation toward it.

**`Prayoga` — preparation or operative undertaking**

The practical mediation between intention and completion. `Prayoga` is
directed toward an end in a determinate field; it is not a neutral physical
motion whose object can be reclassified retroactively.

**`Avyāvartita` — unreversed**

The Bhāṣya's qualification of the preparation that blocks dispassion and
fruit. The trajectory has been entered and not abandoned.

**`Vairāgya` — liberating dispassion**

The release from attachment whose arising is excluded while immediate-result
preparation remains operative and unreversed.

**`Phala` — noble Path-fruit**

A liberating result of the noble Path. The verse makes Path-capacity the
present criterion of the karmic course, not merely its later painful
maturation.

**`Āśraya` — transformed basis**

In the Bhāṣya's contrast case, arising of the noble Path transforms the basis
so completely that it is utterly opposed to completion of an ordinary
unwholesome karmapatha already under preparation.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
A = agent
I = projected intention
F(t) = object-field as qualified at time t
P = operative preparation
R = eventual physical result
K = technical immediate-result classification
```

The Bhāṣya rejects result-only classification:

```text
R alone
    does not entail K
```

Instead:

```text
DirectedToward(P, I, F(t₁))
AppropriateCompletion(P, R)
    → eligible for K
```

**No retroactive field substitution**

```text
F(t₁) = NonArhat
P directed toward F(t₁)

F(t₂) = Arhat
DeathFromEarlierBlow(t₃)

therefore
P was not preparation toward an ArhatField
```

**Intention and physical result**

```text
IntendsStrike(A)
CausesBuddhaBloodshed(A)
    → not BuddhaBloodshedAnantarya(A)

IntendsKill(A)
CausesBuddhaBloodshed(A)
    → eligible for BuddhaBloodshedAnantarya(A)
```

**Opposed practical trajectories**

```text
UnreversedAnantaryaPreparation(A)
    → not Possible(Vairagya(A))
    → not Possible(PathFruit(A))

NoblePathArises(A)
    → TransformsBasis(A)
    → not Possible(CompletionOfOrdinaryUnwholesomePath(A))
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.103 established that the karmic field is historically and relationally
determined. VAK 4.104 now adds the complementary determination from the
agent-side: the field must be intended and practically engaged under the
qualification relevant to the karmic course. Neither a later status nor a
bare physical result can replace that original directedness.

This is a precise theory of Kriyā. Practical activity is not reducible to
what happens externally, to a private intention abstracted from execution, or
to an object considered outside the course. It is the organized movement:

```text
Principle of action
    → projected end
    → qualified Bhūmi-field
    → operative preparation
    → completion
```

The second half then brings the Path into the present structure of Karma.
Ordinary unwholesome preparation remains open to a decisive counter-course:
if the noble Path arises, the `āśraya` is transformed and the incompatible
karmapatha cannot be completed. This is the positive efficacy of Path as
Practical Reason—the Principle changes the basis from which action proceeds.

Unreversed immediate-result preparation has the opposed structure. It does
not merely schedule a painful future result; it closes the present possibility
of dispassion and fruit. In the language of the Sannyāsa non-arising Path, the
course of non-arising can interrupt an ordinary karmic trajectory by
transforming its basis, whereas the exceptional unreversed trajectory is
defined precisely by excluding that liberating interruption.

Thus VAK 4.104 gives two schema paths through one conditioned field:

```text
unwholesome preparation
    → Path arises
    → basis transformed
    → projected karma does not arise

immediate-result preparation remains unreversed
    → Path cannot arise
    → karmic trajectory remains operative
```

This is why Path is the truth of the karmic course: it determines whether the
course is practically reversible and whether its projected end can still be
negated before completion.
