# VAK 4.21 Bhāṣya — Present Restraint and the Middle Bearer

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> The one in concentration and the one established in the noble Path
> are respectively endowed with present [avijñapti]. If one standing

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“The one in concentration and the one established in the noble Path are
endowed with present [avijñapti].” The two who possess meditation and
uncontaminated restraint are endowed with present avijñapti,
respectively, when they have entered concentration and the noble Path;
not when they have emerged from them. So much for those established in
restraint and non-restraint.

Now for one standing in the middle: “If such a person has [avijñapti],
at first [he is endowed] with the middle [temporal mode].” A middle
person is one established neither in restraint nor in non-restraint.
Such a person does not necessarily have avijñapti. But if he does have
avijñapti included under bad conduct, a component of discipline, or
the like, he is at first endowed with it in the middle temporal mode.
For present avijñapti stands between past and future.

“Afterward [he is endowed] in two temporal modes”: after the first
moment, with past and present avijñapti. The qualification “so long as
it has not been abandoned” continues to apply.

## 4. Present Possession Follows the Corresponding State

The first half completes the temporal account of the two
`cittānuvartin` restraints from 4.17. The commentary assigns them in
order: the person entered into concentration has present meditation
restraint; the person entered into the noble Path has present
uncontaminated restraint. `Na tu vyutthitau`, “but not when emerged,”
limits **present** possession to actual entry into the relevant state.
It should not be expanded into a denial of the past and future
possession treated in 4.20.

## 5. The Middle Bearer and the Middle Time

`Madhyastha` names a bearer established in neither `saṃvara` nor
`asaṃvara`. It is a third classificatory position, not by itself a
spiritual Middle Way or a synthesis of the other two. Nor does it
guarantee avijñapti: `tasya nāvaśyam avijñaptir asti` expressly denies
that inference.

If this person does acquire avijñapti through a relevant deed, the
first moment is possessed as `madhyā`, the **present**, called middle
because it lies between past and future. From the next moment onward,
the two modes are **past and present**. The inherited `atyāga`
condition still governs possession. There is no claim here of future
possession for this occasional avijñapti.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our practical World System, this passage prevents a false binary.
A person can stand in neither established restraint nor established
non-restraint and still perform an action with a continuing karmic
determination. It also prevents a false permanence: the present force
of a mind-following restraint depends on the corresponding actual
state. If the Organon calls its own ongoing work a vow, it must still
distinguish a standing commitment, an actual present enactment, and an
occasional deed. This is a project use of the Kośa distinction, not a
translation of `madhyastha` into an Organon office.
