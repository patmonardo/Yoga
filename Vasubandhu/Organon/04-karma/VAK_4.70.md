# VAK_4.70

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
वधव्यापादपारुष्यनिष्ठा द्वेषेण लोभतः ।
परस्त्रीगमनाभिध्यादत्तादानसमापनम् ॥ ४.७० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
vadha-vyāpāda-pāruṣya-niṣṭhā dveṣeṇa lobhataḥ /
para-strī-gamanābhidhyādattādāna-samāpanam // 4.70 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vadha | vyāpāda | pāruṣya | niṣṭhā | dveṣeṇa |
lobhataḥ | para-strī-gamana | abhidhyā | adatta-ādāna |
samāpanam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `vadha` | compound member from `√han` | killing; destruction of life |
| `vyāpāda` | masculine compound member | hostility; the wish to harm |
| `pāruṣya` | neuter compound member | harsh speech |
| `niṣṭhā` | nominative feminine singular | culmination; completed status |
| `dveṣeṇa` | instrumental singular | through hatred |
| `lobhataḥ` | ablative singular used causally | from or through greed |
| `para-strī-gamana` | compound member | literally, going to another's wife; here sexual misconduct |
| `abhidhyā` | compound member | covetousness |
| `adatta-ādāna` | compound member | taking what has not been given |
| `samāpanam` | nominative neuter singular | completion; bringing to fulfillment |

**Two triads**

The verse arranges six unwholesome courses according to the root operative at
their completion:

```text
vadha + vyāpāda + pāruṣya
    through dveṣa

para-strī-gamana + abhidhyā + adattādāna
    through lobha
```

The standard course-name for killing is `prāṇātipāta`; `vadha` gives its
destructive act. `Para-strī-gamana` reflects the transmitted social phrasing;
the doctrinal category is the broader `kāma-mithyācāra`, sexual misconduct.

## 4. Grammar

**Completion through hatred**

```text
vadha-vyāpāda-pāruṣya-niṣṭhā
    dveṣeṇa [bhavati]
```

> The completion of killing, hostility, and harsh speech is through hatred.

The compound is distributive: `niṣṭhā` belongs to each of the three courses.
The instrumental `dveṣeṇa` expresses the root through which each reaches
culmination.

**Completion through greed**

```text
lobhataḥ
    para-strī-gamana-abhidhyā-adatta-ādāna-samāpanam
    [bhavati]
```

> From greed comes the completion of sexual misconduct, covetousness, and
> taking what is not given.

`Samāpanam` is again distributive across the three members. In this context
`niṣṭhā`, `samāpti`, and `samāpana` belong to the same technical field of
bringing a karmapatha to completed status.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The completion of killing, hostility, and harsh speech is through hatred.
> From greed comes the completion of going to another's wife, covetousness,
> and taking what is not given.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Killing, hostility, and harsh speech reach completion through hatred, when
> a rejecting and harsh mind becomes immediately operative. Sexual misconduct,
> covetousness, and taking what is not given reach completion through greed.
> The Bhāṣya completes the tenfold classification: false view culminates
> through delusion become intense, while false speech, divisive speech, and
> disordered speech may each be completed through greed, hatred, or delusion.

Only the hatred-triad and greed-triad occur in the Kārikā. The completion-root
of false view and the variable roots of the other three speech-courses are
given by its immediate Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The affliction that first generates an action-process need not be the
> affliction under which its defining course reaches fulfillment. At
> completion, destructive rejection gives killing, hostility, and harsh
> speech their determinate character; appropriation gives sexual misconduct,
> covetousness, and theft theirs; intensified delusion completes false view.
> Yet false, divisive, and disordered speech remain formally compatible with
> all three afflictive grounds. The completed course and its completion-root
> are therefore distinct but internally related determinations.

The taxonomy requires both questions:

```text
What course has been completed?
Through which root has it been completed?
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Niṣṭhā`, `samāpti`, and `samāpana` — completion**

Terms for culmination or bringing-to-completion. Here they identify the point
at which a preparatory karmic process has fulfilled the defining conditions
of a karmapatha.

**`Dveṣa` — hatred**

The afflictive root characterized by aversion and rejection. The Bhāṣya
explains the hatred-triad through the immediate presence of a rejecting,
harsh mind (`parityāga-paruṣa-citta-saṃmukhībhāva`).

**The hatred-completed triad**

```text
prāṇātipāta / vadha    killing
vyāpāda                hostility
pāruṣya                harsh speech
```

These courses reach completion through the destructive or injurious form of
hatred.

**`Lobha` — greed**

The afflictive root of attachment and appropriation. It completes courses
whose defining movement concerns improper possession, enjoyment, or taking.

**The greed-completed triad**

```text
kāma-mithyācāra        sexual misconduct
abhidhyā                covetousness
adattādāna              taking what is not given
```

**`Moha` — delusion**

The afflictive root that obscures correct determination. The Bhāṣya says false
view is brought to completion not by delusion's bare presence but by delusion
that has become intense (`adhimātrabhūta`).

**The variably completed speech-courses**

```text
mṛṣāvāda             false speech
paiśunya              divisive speech
saṃbhinnapralāpa      disordered speech
```

According to the Bhāṣya, any of these can reach completion through greed,
hatred, or delusion.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The complete ten-course map**

```text
DVEṢA
    killing
    hostility
    harsh speech

LOBHA
    sexual misconduct
    covetousness
    taking what is not given

MOHA
    false view

LOBHA / DVEṢA / MOHA
    false speech
    divisive speech
    disordered speech
```

The last four mappings are Bhāṣya determinations completing the six-member
classification stated by the verse.

**Genesis and completion**

VAK 4.68 established:

```text
preparation for an unwholesome course
    may arise from any of the three roots
```

VAK 4.70 now determines:

```text
completed course
    reaches niṣṭhā under a characteristic root
    or, in three speech-courses, any of the three roots
```

Therefore:

```text
root somewhere in causal history
    ≠
root operative at completion
```

**Course-form and completion-ground**

The speech-courses show that one formal course can have different subjective
completion-grounds:

```text
same karmapatha-form
    × greed
    × hatred
    × delusion
```

Conversely, a single root can complete several objectively different courses.
The classification is many-to-many in the speech field, not a universal
one-root/one-course correspondence.

**False view as completed principle**

```text
moha
    → intensification
    → mithyādṛṣṭi reaches completion
```

False view is not simply interchangeable with delusion. It is the determinate
principial course brought to completion through intensified delusion.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Two axes of ethical determination**

The ten courses name objective practical forms; the roots name the afflictive
grounds through which those forms are completed:

```text
objective course-form
        ×
subjective completion-ground
```

Neither axis is sufficient alone. Naming false speech does not yet disclose
whether it served acquisition, hostility, or deluded assertion. Naming greed
does not yet disclose whether it culminated in theft, sexual misconduct,
covetousness, or a speech-course.

**The completed act does not narrate its whole genesis**

Killing for payment may have greed in its preparatory history while reaching
completion through the destructive rejection classified as hatred. Theft
undertaken to injure an enemy may begin from hatred while reaching completion
as an appropriative course through greed. These examples express the formal
distinction established by VAK 4.68–4.70; they are not separate examples in
the Kārikā.

**Delusion as genetic deformation of principle**

In false view, delusion becomes sufficiently intense to determine a principial
denial or inversion of moral causality. This is stronger than lack of
information:

```text
obscuration
    → intensification
    → false orientation becomes determinate
```

This is the Organon contact. The six verse mappings, the four Bhāṣya mappings,
and the explanations of the completing roots belong to the source layers; the
two-axis account of course-form and completion-ground is the project
synthesis.
