# VAK 4.57 Bhāṣya — The Locus of Feeling as Karmic Maturation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
kuśalasyāvitarkasya karmaṇo vedanā matā /
vipākaś caitasiky eva kāyiky evāśubhasya tu // 4.57 //
```

> The feeling held to be the maturation of wholesome karma without
> `vitarka` is exclusively mental; for unwholesome karma, it is
> exclusively bodily.

This classifies *feeling as maturation*, not every feeling that may
occur in the life of someone affected by such karma.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Maturation, moreover, has feeling as its predominant aspect. This
is now examined. Could a karma mature with mental feeling alone as
its maturation and not bodily feeling? Could it mature with bodily
feeling alone and not mental feeling? He says: “The feeling held to
be the maturation of wholesome karma without `vitarka` is
exclusively mental.” Karma without `vitarka` extends from the
intermediate dhyāna up to the summit of existence. For such
wholesome karma without `vitarka`, only mental feeling is its
maturation. Why not bodily feeling? Because bodily feeling must
possess `vitarka` and `vicāra`.

“For unwholesome karma, however, it is exclusively bodily.” Only
bodily feeling is the maturation of unwholesome karma. Why not
mental feeling? Its maturation is painful feeling, while painful
mental feeling is distress (`daurmanasya`); and distress, as
already explained, is not maturation.

## 4. Two Exclusion Arguments

The Bhāṣya resolves its two questions by testing which *locus* of
feeling can qualify as `vipāka`:

| Karma | Feeling that can be its maturation | Excluded locus and reason |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesome, without `vitarka` | Mental | Bodily feeling requires `vitarka` and `vicāra` |
| Unwholesome | Bodily | Painful mental feeling is `daurmanasya`, which is not `vipāka` |

Vasubandhu specifies the first range as intermediate dhyāna through
the summit of existence. The second claim does not deny that mental
distress may occur; it excludes mental distress from the technical
class of karmic maturation. The argument therefore depends on the
earlier classification of `daurmanasya`, to which the prose refers
with “as already explained.”

## 5. Organon Contact Point

In our action model, a result's kind and its admissible locus are
distinct determinations. Here the Kośa constrains the locus of
`vedanā-vipāka` by the karma's meditative structure and by the
classification of distress. That is a useful comparison for typed
results; it is not a claim that all mental or bodily experience can
be deduced from these two karmic classes.
