# VAK 4.42 Bhāṣya — Loss by Realm Shift, Abandonment by Path

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
kāmāptaṃ kuśalārūpaṃ mūlacchedordhvajanmataḥ /
pratipakṣodayāt kliṣṭam arūpaṃ tu vihīyate // 4.42 //
```

> Wholesome nonmaterial dharma belonging to the desire realm is
> relinquished through severance of wholesome roots or rebirth
> in a higher realm. Defiled nonmaterial dharma, however, is
> abandoned through the arising of its counteragent.

The complete running unit is [225|22]–[226|01–02] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The question about which
beings can possess restraint or non-restraint at [226|03] opens
4.43.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
kāmāptaṃ kuśalārūpaṃ mūlacchedordhvajanmataḥ /

kāmāvacaraṃ kuśalam arūpasvabhāvaṃ dvābhyāṃ kāraṇābhyāṃ
parityajyate /
kuśalamūlasamucchedād rūpārūpyadhātūpapattito vā /

pratipakṣodayāt kliṣṭam arūpaṃ tu vihīyate // 4.42 //

kliṣṭaṃ tv arūpasvabhāvaṃ sarvam eva pratipakṣodayād vihīyate /
yasyopakleśaprakārasya yaḥ prahāṇamārgas tenāsau saparivāraḥ
parityajyate nānyathā /
```

Word division, punctuation, and the verse-end label are editorial.
The Bhāṣya's `arūpasvabhāva` governs `arūpa` in both halves:
nonmaterial by nature. It does not denote the formless realm
(`ārūpyadhātu`) or `avijñapti`, which this Abhidharma system
classifies as material (`rūpa`). The latter confusion in the
earlier Kārikā interpretation is corrected here.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Wholesome nonmaterial dharma belonging to the desire realm is
relinquished through severance of the roots or birth above.” A
wholesome dharma of nonmaterial nature belonging to the desire
realm is relinquished in two ways: through complete severance of
the wholesome roots or through rebirth in the Form or Formless
Realm.

“Defiled nonmaterial dharma, however, is abandoned through the
arising of its counteragent.” Every defiled nonmaterial dharma
is abandoned through the arising of its appropriate counteragent.
For each type of defilement, the path that abandons that type
relinquishes it together with its associated factors, and not
otherwise.

## 4. Two Classes, Three Conditions

This short unit completes the preceding account of abandonment
with a careful asymmetry:

| Dharma under discussion | Condition of loss |
|---|---|
| Desire-realm wholesome, nonmaterial | Severance of wholesome roots |
| Desire-realm wholesome, nonmaterial | Rebirth in a higher realm |
| Defiled, nonmaterial | Arising of the specific counteragent |

The first condition cuts a sustaining wholesome root; the second
changes the realm to which the possessed dharma belongs. Neither
is presented as the path's targeted abandonment of a defilement.
The third condition *is* that targeted operation: the arising
of `pratipakṣa`, specified in prose as the `prahāṇamārga`
applicable to a given `upakleśaprakāra`. The type of defilement
is relinquished with its `parivāra`, the associated complex.
The Bhāṣya says `nānyathā`, “not otherwise.” It does not assert
a numerical one-to-one mapping between every defilement and
every distinct path-token.

This separates the fact that a defilement is presently not
manifest from the stronger claim that it has been abandoned by
its counter-path. The latter is what the second half explains.

## 5. Organon Contact Point

For our wheel, “adhyāsa ceases” must specify what actually
does the work of cessation. 4.42 gives us a demanding comparison:
the absence of an afflicted appearance is weaker than its
abandonment through an applicable counter-path. The emergence
of the Idea of the True and Good can be studied as a positive
counter-determination, while the Kośa's explicit claim remains
about defiled dharmas and their paths of abandonment. This is
an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's terminology here.
