# VAK_4.88 — The Result Profile of Wholesome Action

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अनास्रवं पुनः शेषं त्रिभिरव्याकृतं च यत् ।
चत्वारि द्वे तथा त्रीणि कुशलस्य शुभादयः ॥ ४.८८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
anāsravaṃ punaḥ śeṣaṃ tribhir avyākṛtaṃ ca yat /
catvāri dve tathā trīṇi kuśalasya śubhādayaḥ // 4.88 //
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `anāsravam` | nominative neuter singular | action without Influx |
| `punaḥ` | indeclinable | in turn; continuing the classification |
| `śeṣam` | nominative neuter singular | the remainder; action without Influx outside the Path of Abandonment |
| `tribhiḥ` | instrumental plural numeral, with `phalaiḥ` understood | with three results |
| `avyākṛtam` | nominative neuter singular | indeterminate action, neither wholesome nor unwholesome |
| `ca` | coordinating particle | and, also |
| `yat` | relative nominative neuter singular | whatever action is indeterminate |
| `catvāri` | nominative neuter plural | four results |
| `dve` | nominative neuter dual | two results |
| `tathā` | indeclinable | likewise; then in the ordered series |
| `trīṇi` | nominative neuter plural | three results |
| `kuśalasya` | genitive singular | of wholesome action |
| `śubhādayaḥ` | nominative plural compound | those beginning with wholesome: wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dharmas |

**Two classifications meet in one verse**

The first half completes the result-profiles begun in VAK 4.87:

```text
remaining action without Influx → three results
indeterminate action            → three results
```

The second half begins a new matrix:

```text
wholesome action as cause
    → wholesome resulting dharmas:     four results
    → unwholesome resulting dharmas:   two results
    → indeterminate resulting dharmas: three results
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the exact memberships and confirms that the numbers are
to be read respectively.

## 4. Grammar

**Remaining action without Influx and indeterminate action**

```text
śeṣam anāsravaṃ [karma]
    tribhiḥ [phalaiḥ sa-phalam]

yat ca avyākṛtaṃ [karma]
    [tat api tribhiḥ phalaiḥ sa-phalam]
```

> The remaining action without Influx has three results, and whatever action
> is indeterminate likewise has three.

`Śeṣam` is determined retrospectively: it excludes the action without Influx
in the Path of Abandonment already classified in 4.87. For both classes, the
Bhāṣya excludes maturation and disconnection.

**The ordered result-quality series**

```text
kuśalasya [karmaṇaḥ]
    śubhādayaḥ [dharmāḥ phalāni bhavanti]
    catvāri
    dve
    tathā trīṇi
    [anukramam]
```

> For wholesome action, the result counts for dharmas beginning with
> wholesome are four, two, and three, respectively.

`Śubhādayaḥ` initiates the conventional ordered series wholesome,
unwholesome, and indeterminate. The Bhāṣya makes the distributive force of the
three numerals explicit through `anukramam`, “respectively.”

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> The remaining action without Influx, and whatever action is indeterminate,
> each have three results. For wholesome action, the result counts for
> dharmas beginning with wholesome are four, two, and three, respectively.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Other action without Influx, and indeterminate action, each have three
> results. For wholesome action, dharmas beginning with wholesome have four,
> unwholesome two, and indeterminate three results, respectively.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The remaining action without Influx, and whatever action is indeterminate, each have three results. For wholesome action, the result counts for dharmas beginning with wholesome are four, two, and three, respectively.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Śeṣam anāsravam` — remaining action without Influx**

Action without Influx other than action in the uninterrupted Path of
Abandonment. Because it does not generate maturation and does not itself
perform the abandoning operation, it lacks both maturation and disconnection
results.

**`Avyākṛta` — ethically indeterminate**

Neither wholesome nor unwholesome. This does not mean unintelligible,
unclassified, or causally inert. Indeterminate action retains corresponding,
activity-produced, and dominant results.

**`Śubhādayaḥ` — the series beginning with wholesome**

A compact ordered reference to wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate
resulting dharmas. It marks the result-quality axis rather than three further
classes of causal action.

**`Anukramam` — respectively**

The Bhāṣya's explicit distributive instruction. It binds four results to
wholesome dharmas, two to unwholesome dharmas, and three to indeterminate
dharmas resulting from wholesome action.

**`Vipāka-phala` — maturation result**

In the wholesome-action row, maturation belongs among indeterminate
resulting dharmas, not wholesome ones. The ethical quality of the action and
the quality of its maturation-result must therefore remain distinct.

**`Niṣyanda-phala` — corresponding result**

Belongs here only where wholesome action is related to wholesome resulting
dharmas. Its similarity-structure does not cross into the unwholesome or
indeterminate result columns.

**`Visaṃyoga-phala` — disconnection-result**

Appears among wholesome resulting dharmas. Disconnection is a positive
liberative determination, not an ethically neutral absence and not a generic
effect of every wholesome action.

**`Puruṣakāra-phala` — activity-produced result**

The broad causal relation through which an operation actively brings forth
dharmas. In this row it can connect wholesome action with wholesome,
unwholesome, or indeterminate resulting dharmas.

**`Adhipati-phala` — dominant result**

The equally broad relation of governing or conditioning efficacy. Like
`puruṣakāra`, it crosses all three result-quality columns for wholesome
action.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Completion of the general profiles**

Let:

```text
V = vipāka
N = niṣyanda
D = visaṃyoga
P = puruṣakāra
A = adhipati
```

Then:

```text
RemainingActionWithoutInflux = {N, P, A}
IndeterminateAction          = {N, P, A}
```

The two profiles have both equal cardinality and equal membership, but remain
different action classes because Influx status and ethical quality are
independent determinations.

**Wholesome-action result matrix**

| Resulting dharma-quality | `vipāka` | `niṣyanda` | `visaṃyoga` | `puruṣakāra` | `adhipati` | Count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| wholesome | no | yes | yes | yes | yes | 4 |
| unwholesome | no | no | no | yes | yes | 2 |
| indeterminate | yes | no | no | yes | yes | 3 |

Formally:

```text
ResultRelations(WholesomeAction, WholesomeDharmas)
    = {N, D, P, A}

ResultRelations(WholesomeAction, UnwholesomeDharmas)
    = {P, A}

ResultRelations(WholesomeAction, IndeterminateDharmas)
    = {V, P, A}
```

**Three independent coordinates**

```text
action-quality
    wholesome / unwholesome / indeterminate

resulting-dharma quality
    wholesome / unwholesome / indeterminate

result-relation
    maturation / correspondence / disconnection
    / activity-production / dominant conditioning
```

A scalar expression such as `ResultCount(WholesomeAction) = 4` destroys the
meaning because it suppresses both resulting-dharma quality and
result-relation type.

**Non-equivalences**

```text
ethical quality of cause       ≠ ethical quality of every result
same result-count              ≠ same causal class
effect following a cause       ≠ maturation-result in every case
causal relation                ≠ external link between fixed terms
```

**Breadth of result-relations**

```text
Puruṣakāra and Adhipati
    cross all three result qualities

Niṣyanda
    preserves qualitative correspondence here

Visaṃyoga
    belongs to wholesome liberative determination

Vipāka
    yields indeterminate resultant dharmas
```

The taxonomy differentiates modes of dependence rather than merely kinds of
later events.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The exact memberships, the ordered reading of the counts, and the exclusions
are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The Bhāṣya-based expansion formerly embedded in the study translation is retained here: Action without Influx outside the Path of Abandonment, and indeterminate action, each have three results: corresponding, activity-produced, and dominant; maturation and disconnection are absent. For wholesome action, the result counts for dharmas beginning with wholesome are four, two, and three, respectively. Wholesome dharmas relate through corresponding, disconnection, activity-produced, and dominant results; unwholesome dharmas through activity-produced and dominant results; and indeterminate dharmas through maturation, activity-produced, and dominant results.

VAK 4.88 marks a major advance in the causal logic of the action chapter. Up
to this point, the question could still appear to be a property of one causal
class: how many results does this action possess? The second half makes clear
that this scalar model is insufficient. A result is always a dharma of some
ethical quality standing to an action through a determinate relation.
The adequate form is a matrix.

This explains how wholesome action can have unwholesome dharmas among its
results without “turning into” unwholesomeness. They are not its maturation or
corresponding continuation. They stand to it through the broader relations of
activity-production or dominant conditioning. The result-relation mediates the
passage from cause-quality to result-quality; it cannot be omitted without
producing a moralized caricature of causality.

The maturation column is equally instructive. Wholesome action matures as an
indeterminate resultant dharma. The value of the cause determines the
direction of maturation, but the resultant state is not itself a fresh morally
responsible act. Cause-quality is preserved through transformation rather than
copied as an identical predicate.

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy reveals the five results as forms of causal
mediation. The terms joined by a relation do not possess their complete
meaning prior to the relation: wholesome cause, indeterminate maturation,
liberative disconnection, and dominant field-conditioning acquire their
determinate stations within the whole matrix. This is why dictionary entries
cannot disclose the architecture. The Concept is the organized relation of
the determinations.

The transition also deepens the conclusion of Objective Logic. Action no
longer appears as a linear sequence from act to consequence. It is the
structured network through which qualities, operations, and fields determine
one another. The agent that emerges from this network will enter Anuśaya as
the inwardly conditioned result of these objective mediations.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> An action does not transmit its ethical quality unchanged into every
> result. Wholesome action relates differently to wholesome, unwholesome, and
> indeterminate dharmas because causal efficacy is mediated by distinct
> result-forms. Maturation yields an indeterminate resultant dharma;
> disconnection belongs only within the wholesome liberative field; active
> production and dominant conditioning can cross ethical qualities. The
> action is therefore understood not by repeating its ethical label in every
> result, but through the articulated system of relations by which its
> efficacy becomes actual.

The new form is:

```text
ethical quality of action
    × quality of resulting dharma
    × mode of causal relation
    = determinate result-structure
```

Hegelian-transcendental comprehension shows why this matrix is necessary.
Cause and effect are not fixed things connected by an external arrow; each is
determined through the specific mediation that joins it to the other. The
five results name those mediations.
