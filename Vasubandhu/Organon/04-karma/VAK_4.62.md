# VAK_4.62

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नवमे चेतना या सा कृष्णशुक्लक्षयाय च ।
शुक्लस्य ध्यानवैराग्येष्वन्त्यानन्तर्यमार्गजा ॥ ४.६२ ॥
```

The Bhāṣya distinguishes intrinsic destruction from abandonment through the
elimination of afflictions that take wholesome karma as their object-support.

**`navama`**

This is the ninth and final uninterrupted path of desire-domain detachment. It
completes what the first eight paths began in VAK 4.61.

**`antyānantaryamārgajā`**

The feminine compound qualifies the understood `cetanā`: volition arising in
the final uninterrupted path for each detachment from a dhyāna.

**`kṣaya / prahāṇa`**

The Bhāṣya again interprets exhaustion through abandonment, then asks exactly
what kind of abandonment applies to wholesome karma.

## 4. Grammar

**The ninth desire-domain path**

```text
navame [kāma-vairāgya-ānantarya-mārge]
yā cetanā
sā
kṛṣṇa-śukla-kṣayāya ca [bhavati]
```

> The volition in the ninth uninterrupted path is for the exhaustion of
> black-and-white karma as well.

The Bhāṣya adds that this path also abandons the ninth and final type of black
unwholesome karma.

**The final paths of dhyāna-detachment**

```text
dhyāna-vairāgyeṣu
antya-ānantarya-mārga-jā [cetanā]
śuklasya [kṣayāya bhavati]
```

> The volition arising in the final uninterrupted path of each detachment from
> a dhyāna is for the exhaustion of white karma.

There are four such volitions, one for the final path of detachment from each
of the four dhyānas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The volition in the ninth [uninterrupted path] is for the exhaustion of
> black-and-white karma; that arising in the final uninterrupted paths of
> detachment from the dhyānas is for the exhaustion of white karma.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The volition in the ninth uninterrupted path of desire-domain detachment
> abandons black-and-white wholesome karma together with the ninth and final
> type of black unwholesome karma. The volition arising in the final
> uninterrupted path of detachment from each of the four dhyānas abandons
> white karma. Wholesome karma is not destroyed in its intrinsic nature; its
> abandonment consists in removing every remaining affliction that can take
> it as an object-support.

The double target of the ninth path, fourfold dhyāna distribution, and
relational account of wholesome abandonment are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The Path negates unwholesome and wholesome karmic determinations in different
> ways. The final desire-domain operation completes the abandonment of the last
> black type and the mixed wholesome field. White karma, however, is not
> annihilated as positive content: the Path removes every afflictive relation
> that can appropriate it as an object. Abandonment is complete when the final
> supporting kleśa-relation has disappeared, even though the wholesome dharma
> remains capable of manifestation.

Negation is therefore relational transformation rather than destruction of
intrinsic being.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Completion of desire-domain abandonment**

```text
first eight uninterrupted paths
    → abandon first eight types of black karma

ninth uninterrupted path
    → abandons ninth black type
    → abandons desire-domain black-and-white wholesome karma
```

The ninth path closes the complete desire-domain target-set.

**Four volitions for white karma**

```text
final path of first-dhyāna detachment
final path of second-dhyāna detachment
final path of third-dhyāna detachment
final path of fourth-dhyāna detachment
    → four volitions abandoning white karma
```

**`svabhāvaprahāṇa` rejected**

```text
abandonment of wholesome karma
    ≠ destruction of its intrinsic nature
```

The proof is that the wholesome dharma can become presently manifest even
after being called abandoned.

**`tadālambanakleśaprahāṇa` affirmed**

```text
abandonment of wholesome karma
    = abandonment of every kleśa
      that takes that karma as ālambana
```

So long as even one such affliction remains, abandonment is incomplete.

**Why only the final uninterrupted path**

The final path is required because only then has the last object-taking
affliction been eliminated. Earlier paths weaken the relation-system without
yet exhausting it.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Target-sensitive path operation**

```text
PATH_STAGE
    → TARGET_KARMA
    → MODE_OF_ABANDONMENT
```

The ninth desire-domain path has two targets; each final dhyāna path targets
white karma belonging to that level.

**Intrinsic and relational negation**

```text
INTRINSIC_NEGATION(X)
    → destroy X itself

RELATIONAL_NEGATION(X)
    → eliminate every afflicted relation R to X
```

Vasubandhu chooses the second for wholesome karma.

**Abandonment does not entail nonmanifestability**

```text
ABANDONED(X)
    does not entail
X cannot become manifest
```

Instead:

```text
ABANDONED(X)
    iff
no remaining kleśa takes X as ālambana
```

**Completion is exhaustion of the relation-system**

```text
one object-taking kleśa remains
    → abandonment incomplete

last object-taking kleśa removed
    → abandonment complete
```

Finality belongs to the exhaustion of the system of relations.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**This is the exact logic of `tadabhāva`**

The non-being at issue is not necessarily the non-being of the positive
dharma:

```text
not
    wholesome karma becomes absolute nothing

but
    afflicted appropriation of wholesome karma becomes absent
```

The negative expression is intelligible only after its target-level is
discriminated.

**Kleśa as Ground of the afflicted relation**

Because kleśa grounds the distorted appropriation, removal of every relevant
kleśa removes the afflicted practical structure while leaving wholesome
manifestation possible.

```text
kleśa-ground absent
    → afflicted relation absent
    → positive dharma need not be absent
```

**Practical liberation preserves positive content**

The Path does not purify by annihilating everything it encounters. It removes
the relations through which positive contents become supports of attachment
and renewed karmic production.

**The final path is systemic completion**

The last operation is final because no supporting affliction remains, not
because its intensity simply exceeds the earlier paths. Completion is
structural exhaustion.

This is the Organon contact. The double target, four dhyāna-paths, rejection of
intrinsic destruction, and object-taking-affliction account belong to the
Bhāṣya.
