# VAK 4.44 Bhāṣya — Restraint Possessed Without Present Manifestation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
kāmarūpajadevānāṃ dhyānajaḥ anāsravaḥ punaḥ /
dhyānāntarāsaṃjñisattvavarjyānām apy arūpiṇām // 4.44 //
```

> Meditation-born restraint occurs among gods born in the desire
> and form realms. Uncontaminated restraint occurs there too,
> with the stated exclusions, and also belongs to formless beings.

The verse continues the distribution begun in 4.43. Its complete
Bhāṣya unit is [226|25]–[227|03] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. At [227|04] Vasubandhu
explicitly turns to karmas taught in the sūtras; that sentence
opens the next study rather than extending this verse.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
kāmarūpajadevānāṃ dhyānajaḥ

dhyānasaṃvaraḥ kāmarūpadhātūpapannānāṃ devānām ūrdhvaṃ nāsti /

anāsravaḥ punaḥ /
dhyānāntarāsaṃjñisattvavarjyānām apy arūpiṇām // 4.44 //

anāsravasaṃvaras tu kāmarūpadhātūpapannānām apy asti
dhyānāntarikāsaṃjñisatvopapannān varjayitvā /
ārūpyopapannānām api teṣāṃ tu samanvāgamato 'sti
na saṃmukhībhāvataḥ /
```

Word division, punctuation, and verse numbering are editorial.
The running source has `asaṃjñisatva-` in the explanatory prose,
while the verse has `asaṃjñisattva-`; both readings remain visible.
`Saṃvaraḥ` is understood in the Kārikā from the discussion in
4.43. The prose, not the compact verse alone, fixes the final
distinction between possession and present manifestation.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Meditation-born [restraint occurs] among gods born in the desire
and form realms.” Gods born in those two realms can have
meditation-born restraint; it does not occur above them.

“Uncontaminated [restraint] also [occurs among them], excluding
those of the intermediate dhyāna and the non-percipient beings,
and [it occurs] among the formless as well.” Uncontaminated
restraint occurs among beings born in the desire and form realms,
apart from those born in the intermediate dhyāna and among
non-percipient beings. It belongs also to those born in the
formless realm; for them it exists **by possession**
(`samanvāgama`), **not by present manifestation**
(`saṃmukhībhāva`).

## 4. Distribution of the Two Higher Restraints

The source gives two different ranges. `Dhyānaja-saṃvara`
belongs to gods born in the desire and form realms; the prose
says `ūrdhvaṃ nāsti`, it is absent above those levels.
`Anāsrava-saṃvara` has the stated exclusions within the
desire and form realms but can also belong to formless-born
beings. The Bhāṣya's last sentence controls how that final
claim is understood.

| Bearer in this passage | Dhyānaja restraint | Anāsrava restraint |
|---|---|---|
| Gods born in desire or form realms | Present in the stated range | Present, except the specified births |
| Beings born in the intermediate dhyāna or non-percipient class | Not separately resolved here | Excluded |
| Formless-born beings | Absent | Possessed, not presently manifest |

The table concerns the distribution of the two higher
restraints among the nonhuman bearers discussed here. It does
not replace 4.43's statement that eligible humans can have all
three restraints, including prātimokṣa.

## 5. Possession and Manifestation

`Samanvāgama` says a determination still belongs to a
continuum. `Saṃmukhībhāva` says it is presently manifest.
The final sentence asserts the first and denies the second
for uncontaminated restraint in formless-born beings. Hence
the verse's `api`, “also,” must not be inflated into a claim
that the restraint is visibly or presently operative there.

This distinction closes the inquiry into which beings can
bear the three restraints. It also complements the acquisition
and loss analyses of 4.35–4.42: **acquired**, **possessed**, and
**manifest** are different predicates. A continuing possession
need not be a present occurrence.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our System, a standing vow and its current exercise cannot
be identified without remainder. The Kośa gives a precise
case in which a determination belongs to a continuum although
it is not manifest at that moment. That helps us ask whether
the Idea of the True and Good has been acquired, is sustained,
or is now operative in an action. This is an Organon comparison;
the source's claim concerns `anāsrava-saṃvara` and its bearers.
