# VAK_4.05

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> स्वानि भूतान्युपादाय कायवाक्कर्म सास्रवम् ।
>
> अनास्रवं यत्र जातोऽविज्ञप्तिरनुपात्तिका ॥ ४.५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> svāni bhūtāny upādāya kāyavākkarma sāsravam /
>
> anāsravaṃ yatra jāto 'vijñaptir anupāttikā // 4.5 //

VAK 4.04 distinguished the past producing basis from the present sustaining
support of desire-domain `avijñapti`. VAK 4.05 now asks which great material
determinations support bodily and verbal karma at each level. It crosses two
independent classifications:

```text
karmic status
    sāsrava / anāsrava

material localization
    desire-domain or dhyāna-level mahābhūtas
```

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed `yatra jātaḥ`: it refers to the practitioner
who, born in a given level, produces uncontaminated bodily or verbal karma.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
svāni                      → svāni
bhūtāni                    → bhūtāny (before vowel)
upādāya                    → upādāya
kāya-vāk-karma             → kāyavākkarma
sa-āsravam                 → sāsravam
an-āsravam                 → anāsravam
yatra                      → yatra
jātaḥ                      → jāto (before vowel)
avijñaptiḥ                 → 'vijñaptiḥ (after -o)
an-upāttikā                → anupāttikā
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| svāni | accusative neuter plural reflexive adjective | its own; Bhāṣya: proper to the same domain or meditative level |
| bhūtāni | accusative neuter plural | great material determinations / great elements |
| upādāya | absolutive of `upa-ā-√dā` | taking as basis; arising dependently upon |
| kāyavākkarma | nominative neuter singular collective compound | bodily and verbal karma |
| sāsravam | nominative neuter singular adjective | with outflows; contaminated |
| anāsravam | nominative neuter singular adjective | without outflows; uncontaminated |
| yatra | relative adverb | where; in whichever level |
| jātaḥ | nominative masculine singular past participle | born; Bhāṣya: the practitioner born in that level |
| avijñaptiḥ | nominative feminine singular | non-manifest action / non-disclosive form |
| anupāttikā | nominative feminine singular adjective | non-appropriated |

`Svāni` does not merely mean matter individually owned by the performer. The
Bhāṣya gives it a level-relative sense:

```text
karma belonging to bhūmi X
    → mahābhūtas belonging to bhūmi X
```

`Upādāya` preserves dependence without identity. Bodily and verbal karma are
materially supported determinations; they are not simply identical with their
supporting mahābhūtas.

## 4. Grammar

The first hemistich states the same-level rule:

```text
kāya-vāk-karma sāsravam
    svāni bhūtāni upādāya [utpadyate]

contaminated bodily and verbal karma
    arises depending upon its own great elements
```

The Bhāṣya expands the range:

```text
desire-domain karma
    → desire-domain mahābhūtas

first-dhyāna karma
    → first-dhyāna mahābhūtas

...

fourth-dhyāna karma
    → fourth-dhyāna mahābhūtas
```

The second hemistich is compressed. The commentary supplies the full relation:

```text
anāsravaṃ tu kāya-vāk-karma
    yasyāṃ bhūmau jātaḥ [pudgalaḥ]
    tat utpādayati
    tad-bhūtāni upādāya [utpadyate]

uncontaminated bodily and verbal karma
    depends upon the mahābhūtas of the level
    in which the person producing it is born
```

Thus masculine `jātaḥ` agrees with the understood practitioner, not with
neuter `anāsravaṃ karma`. The earlier apparent gender difficulty is therefore
resolved without emending the witness.

The final predication is direct:

```text
avijñaptiḥ
    anupāttikā

non-manifest action
    is non-appropriated
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Contaminated bodily and verbal karma depends upon its own great material
> determinations. Uncontaminated [bodily and verbal karma depends upon those
> belonging to the level] where [the practitioner] is born. Non-manifest
> action is non-appropriated.

The bracketed terms are syntactically supplied from the Bhāṣya.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Contaminated bodily and verbal karma arises in dependence upon the great
> material determinations proper to its own domain or meditative level.
> Uncontaminated bodily and verbal karma has no separately uncontaminated
> material substrate; it depends upon the mahābhūtas of the level in which the
> practitioner producing it is born and arises through the force of that
> level. `Avijñapti` is not appropriated as part of the living bodily support.

The same-level series through the fourth dhyāna, the nonexistence of
uncontaminated mahābhūtas, arising through the level's force, and the explicit
referent of `jātaḥ` are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The purity of an action does not require a second, intrinsically pure matter.
> An uncontaminated determination can become bodily or verbal actuality only
> through the conditioned material basis available in the practitioner's
> present ground. Its soteriological status and the status of its material
> support must therefore remain distinct. `Avijñapti` sharpens this distinction:
> although the Vaibhāṣika classifies it as materially derived form, it is not
> appropriated into the living organism as an ordinary bodily constituent.

The higher determination does not abolish its conditioned support, and the
support does not reduce the higher determination to its own domain-bound
status.

The decisive distinctions are:

```text
anāsrava
    ≠ belonging to a fourth “pure-material” domain

materially supported
    ≠ identical with material support

rūpa-classified
    ≠ necessarily appropriated bodily matter
```

## 8. Logical Determination

**Same-level support for contaminated action**

```text
For each ordinary material level X:

    sāsrava bodily/verbal karma of X
        → depends upon mahābhūtas of X
```

The relation is systematically level-indexed, not merely organism-indexed.

**Uncontaminated action crosses two axes**

```text
Axis 1 — soteriological status:
    anāsrava
    not bound to kāma, rūpa, or ārūpya as an ordinary contaminated dharma

Axis 2 — material actuality:
    bodily/verbal expression requires level-specific mahābhūtas
```

Therefore:

```text
anāsrava action
    + practitioner born in bhūmi X
        → bodily/verbal karma supported by mahābhūtas of X
```

No contradiction follows because the relation is mediation, not predicative
identity:

```text
uncontaminated karma
    operates through
conditioned level-specific matter

it does not follow that
    the supporting matter is itself uncontaminated
```

**Pure action does not imply pure matter**

The rejected inference would be:

```text
anāsrava action
    → must possess anāsrava mahābhūtas
```

The Bhāṣya instead gives:

```text
no anāsrava mahābhūtas exist

yet anāsrava bodily/verbal karma arises

therefore its material actuality is mediated by
    the practitioner's present bhūmi
```

This blocks a duplication of substances at the material level.

**Materiality and appropriation**

```text
avijñapti
    is classified as derived rūpa
    depends upon mahābhūtas
    is non-manifest
    is anupāttika
```

Hence:

```text
Material(x)
    ↛ AppropriatedLivingBody(x)
```

The verse does not yet fully explain how a non-appropriated material
determination belongs to a particular continuum. It fixes the classification
that subsequent verses must develop.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.05 disciplines the `avijñapti` controversy by separating levels that a
substance-style reading would collapse.

```text
purity-status of action
    ≠ purity-status of material substrate

material dependence
    ≠ material identity

material classification
    ≠ bodily appropriation
```

The first result is especially important for the four-volume Path schema.
Volume IV's uncontaminated samādhi does not need a second transcendent body in
order to determine the bodily and verbal members treated in Volume II. The
Path can become materially actual through the conditioned support of a bhūmi
without being reduced to that support.

```text
anāsrava Path-determination
    → operates through bhūmi-specific materiality
        → bodily/verbal vijñapti and avijñapti
```

This gives a stronger transcendental relation than either crude dualism or
reductionism:

```text
not:
    pure action requires pure matter

not:
    pure action is merely ordinary matter

but:
    pure determination becomes actual
    through a conditioned material basis
    whose difference it preserves
```

The result also constrains our criticism of Vaibhāṣika `avijñapti`. The theory
does not simply imagine an invisible piece of the organism: it explicitly
classifies `avijñapti` as non-appropriated. The remaining question is sharper:

```text
How can a materially grounded but non-appropriated determination
belong to and remain effective within one karmic continuum?
```

That question should remain open. Neither the Kārikā nor this short Bhāṣya
unit completes the relation between non-appropriated form and the individuation
of responsibility.
