# VAK_4.64

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अशैक्षं कायवाक्कर्म मनश्चैव यथाक्रमम् ।
मौनत्रयं त्रिधा शौचं सर्वं सुचरितत्रयम् ॥ ४.६४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
aśaikṣaṃ kāyavākkarma manaś caiva yathākramam /
maunatrayaṃ tridhā śaucaṃ sarvaṃ sucaritatrayam // 4.64 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
a-śaikṣam kāya-vāk-karma manaḥ ca eva yathā-kramam /
mauna-trayam tridhā śaucam sarvam sucarita-trayam //
```

The base kārikā witness prints `sarva`; the official study follows the
Bhāṣya-supported syntactic normalization `sarvaṃ` in agreement with neuter
`sucaritatrayam`, while recording the witness variation here.

**`aśaikṣa`**

`Aśaikṣa` means beyond training, not untrained. It names one for whom nothing
further remains to be cultivated: the arhat.

**The grammatical asymmetry**

```text
bodily karma
verbal karma
mind
```

The third member is `manas`, not `manaskarma`. The Bhāṣya makes this difference
explicit and doctrinally decisive.

**`mauna`**

True silence is perfected restraint, not acoustic muteness or mere absence of
outward activity.

## 4. Grammar

**The three silences**

```text
aśaikṣam kāya-karma
aśaikṣam vāk-karma
aśaikṣam manaḥ

yathā-kramam
mauna-trayam [bhavati]
```

> The bodily karma, verbal karma, and mind of the one beyond training are,
> respectively, the three silences.

The Bhāṣya restricts `karma` to the bodily and verbal members. Mental silence
is the restrained mind itself.

**Threefold purity**

```text
tridhā śaucam
sarvam sucarita-trayam [bhavati]
```

> Threefold purity is the complete threefold good conduct.

The three members are bodily, verbal, and mental good conduct. `Sarvam`
indicates the whole extension of each.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The bodily karma, verbal karma, and mind of one beyond training are,
> respectively, the three silences. Threefold purity is the complete threefold
> good conduct.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Perfected bodily abstention and perfected verbal abstention in the arhat are
> bodily and verbal silence; the arhat's restrained mind itself—not a supposed
> mental manifestation parallel to bodily and verbal `vijñapti`—is mental
> silence. The arhat is the sage in the highest sense because all afflictive
> chatter has ceased. Bodily, verbal, and mental purity consist in the complete
> corresponding forms of good conduct, which remove the stain of bad conduct
> temporarily or completely.

The asymmetry of the mental member, absence of mental `vijñapti`, restriction
to the arhat, and genetic definition of purity are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> True silence is perfected activity whose afflictive Ground has ceased. Body
> and speech manifest restraint through abstaining action; mind does not need a
> fictitious inward `vijñapti`, because the restrained mind itself is the
> inward silence from which those manifestations proceed. For another observer,
> the epistemic direction reverses: bodily and verbal action are signs from
> which the inward determination is inferred. Purity is likewise not an
> immediate static quality but good conduct generated through removal of the
> stain of bad conduct.

The inner is neither reduced to outward behavior nor sealed away from it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Bodily and verbal silence**

```text
aśaikṣa bodily karma
    → bodily silence

aśaikṣa verbal karma
    → verbal silence
```

These can be `virati-svabhāva`, abstention by nature.

**Mental silence**

```text
aśaikṣa mind itself
    → mental silence

not
    mental karma modeled as a third vijñapti
```

The Bhāṣya gives the reason: there is no mental `vijñapti` corresponding to
bodily and verbal manifestation.

**`cittaṃ hi paramārthamuniḥ`**

```text
for mind is the sage in the highest sense
```

The inward condition is inferred from bodily and verbal karmas. Their outward
appearance is evidence of mind, not a replacement for it.

**Why only the arhat**

```text
sarva-kleśa-jalpa-uparati
    cessation of all afflictive chatter
    → paramārtha-muni
    → genuine aśaikṣa mauna
```

Silence is not merely the temporary suppression of speech.

**Threefold purity**

```text
all bodily good conduct
    → bodily purity

all verbal good conduct
    → verbal purity

all mental good conduct
    → mental purity
```

Purity removes the stain of bad conduct either temporarily or completely.

**Pedagogical purpose**

The Bhāṣya says this teaching discriminates genuine silence and purity from
false constructions of them—especially merely external muteness or ritual
cleanliness.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Ontological and epistemic directions**

```text
ontological direction
    restrained mind
        → perfected bodily action
        → perfected verbal action

epistemic direction for another
    bodily and verbal action
        → inference
        → restrained mind
```

These directions are reciprocal without being identical.

**No artificial symmetry**

```text
body     → karma / vijñapti / abstention
speech   → karma / vijñapti / abstention
mind     → mind itself restrained
```

The triplicity does not license repetition of one mechanism at every level.

**Silence as determinate negation**

```text
abstract silence
    = lack of outward occurrence

true mauna
    = perfected activity
      after cessation of kleśa-jalpa
```

**Purity generated through removal**

```text
bad-conduct stain
    → removed by good conduct
    → purity
```

Purity is determined through its genetic relation to the contrary it removes.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The *abhāva* of kleśa becomes silence**

VAK 4.64 gives positive content to cessation:

```text
kleśa-jalpa absent
    → not blank mind
    → mind as paramārtha-muni
    → perfected bodily and verbal restraint
```

The absence of the afflictive Ground frees activity rather than abolishing it.

**Vijñapti and avijñapti reach their limit**

Body and speech admit manifest action and abstention-forms. Mind must not be
forced into that structure by inventing a mental `vijñapti`. The Bhāṣya itself
protects the difference of levels.

**Appearance and inference**

Another person does not directly inspect the inward determination. Body and
speech provide its appearances; inference returns from those appearances to
mind. This is a disciplined relation between inward Ground and outward sign.

**Threshold into conduct**

The preceding block analyzed how karmic reproduction is exhausted. This verse
now establishes the positive normative field:

```text
exhaustion of afflictive relation
    → perfected silence
    → threefold purity
    → analysis of good and bad conduct
```

This is the Organon contact. The mental asymmetry, inference, abstention,
afflictive chatter, three purities, and pedagogical purpose belong to the
Bhāṣya.
