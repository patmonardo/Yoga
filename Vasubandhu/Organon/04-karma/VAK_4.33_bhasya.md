# VAK 4.33 Bhāṣya — The Determinate Scope of a Lay Vow

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
mithyācārātigarhyatvāt saukaryād akriyāptitaḥ /
yathābhyupagamaṃ lābhaḥ saṃvarasya na saṃtateḥ // 4.33 //
```

> Because sexual misconduct is especially blameworthy, because
> abstaining from it is practicable, and because non-performance is
> acquired as restraint, restraint is acquired according to the
> undertaking, not merely according to a person's continuum.

The Kārikā witness reads `na saṃtate`, while the running Bhāṣya
anchor reads `na saṃtateḥ`; this study follows the latter and does
not silently resolve the difference. The natural unit runs from
[217|20–21] through [218|07–08] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The question about false
speech at [218|09–10] opens the next discussion.

Word division, punctuation, and verse numbering are editorial.
The source's `abrahmacryād` and the awkward double negative
`na ... brahmacaryaṃ na kartavyam` are retained. In context the
contrast is clear—abstention from sexual misconduct rather than
complete celibacy—but the latter sentence cannot be translated as
though its transmitted wording were unproblematic.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Why is abstention from non-celibacy established as a training rule
in the other restraints, but abstention from sexual misconduct in
the case of an upāsaka? “Because misconduct is especially
blameworthy, because abstaining is practicable, and because
non-performance is acquired.” Sexual misconduct is strongly
condemned in ordinary life: it injures the spouses of others and
leads toward an unfortunate rebirth. Non-celibacy is not condemned
in the same way. For people living in households, abstaining from
sexual misconduct is practicable, while abstaining from all sexual
activity is difficult; they might not be willing to undertake so
difficult a rule.

Noble persons also acquire a restraint of non-performance with
respect to sexual misconduct, since they do not engage in it even
in other births; the same is not said of complete celibacy. Hence
the upāsaka's training rule is established as abstention from
sexual misconduct, lest a noble trainee in another birth become
unrestrained with respect to a limb of lay restraint. A fixed
non-performance (`akriyāniyama`) is a restraint of non-doing
(`akaraṇasaṃvara`).

Some upāsakas marry. Have they acquired restraint even with respect
to the women who become their wives, or have they not? They have;
otherwise their acquisition of restraint would be merely partial.
How, then, is the restraint not disturbed? “Restraint is acquired
according to the undertaking, not according to the continuum.”
Their acquisition follows what they undertook. What did they
undertake? “I abstain from sexual misconduct.” They did not
undertake a prohibition of sexual activity as such with respect to
the woman's continuum [the transmitted sentence expressing this
contrast is defective]. Accordingly, the restraint is acquired
with respect to the limb of sexual misconduct, not the broader
prohibition of non-celibacy. Marriage therefore does not disturb
the restraint.

## 4. Why This Lay Rule Has Its Scope

The opening question is a comparison between **lay restraint** and
restraints requiring celibacy. The three reasons do different work:
the misconduct rule targets an especially blameworthy act; it can
be undertaken while living in a household; and it fits the
`akaraṇasaṃvara` attributed to noble persons across births. The
last point explains the rule's continuity when the trainee's
birth and circumstances change. None of the three says that
celibacy itself is blameworthy.

The marriage objection then tests the vow's extent. Saying that
the future wife lay outside the field of restraint would make the
restraint partial (`prādeśika`). The answer instead specifies the
**action prohibited**. A spouse is not excluded as a person; the
undertaking prohibits sexual misconduct, while it does not
establish total celibacy. The question about the woman's
`saṃtāna` concerns the proposed basis of the prohibition, and the
verse makes the actual undertaking decisive.

## 5. Translation and Doctrinal Limits

`Yathābhyupagamam` is “according to the undertaking.” It fixes
the content under which restraint is acquired. `Na saṃtateḥ` is
best read in light of the immediately preceding question about the
woman who becomes a wife: mere reference to her continuum does not
turn the lay undertaking into a vow of complete celibacy. The
Kārikā witness's `na saṃtate` deserves separate textual review.

The prose phrase `na tv atra saṃtāne mayā brahmacaryaṃ na
kartavyam` has an apparent negation problem. The conventional
translation expresses the contrast established by the surrounding
prose; it does **not** claim to have repaired this Sanskrit sentence.
The source's final `nābrahmacaryāt` independently confirms that
the acquired lay restraint is not the celibacy restraint.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

Our vow to know the Self and let adhyāsa cease requires the same
care with **determinate content**. A practical commitment gains
force from what is actually undertaken and sustained, not from
silently widening its predicate. In our System the emergence of
the Idea of the True and Good is a positive determination of
action. Here the Kośa supplies a narrower example: a lay rule
whose moral force and continuity depend on its precise scope.
This is an Organon comparison, not a claim that the Kośa's lay
sexual rule states our Prime Commandment.
