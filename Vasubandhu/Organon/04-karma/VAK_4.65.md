# VAK_4.65

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अशुभं कायकर्मादि मतं दुश्चरितत्रयम् ।
अकर्मापि त्वभिध्यादि मनोदुश्चरितं त्रिधा ॥ ४.६५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
aśubhaṃ kāyakarmādi mataṃ duścaritatrayam /
akarmāpi tv abhidhyādi manoduścaritaṃ tridhā // 4.65 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
aśubham kāya-karma-ādi matam duścarita-trayam /
akarma api tu abhidhyā-ādi manaḥ-duścaritam tridhā //
```

The second half explicitly includes three mental bad conducts that are not
karma in their own nature.

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `aśubham` | nominative neuter singular | unwholesome |
| `kāyakarmādi` | nominative neuter singular compound | bodily karma and the remaining verbal and mental karmas |
| `matam` | nominative neuter singular past participle | held; considered |
| `duścaritatrayam` | nominative neuter singular compound | the threefold bad conduct |
| `akarma` | nominative neuter singular | not karma in the strict sense |
| `api tu` | concessive and contrastive particles | even though; but nevertheless |
| `abhidhyā` | compound member | covetousness |
| `ādi` | compound member | beginning with; including the remaining two |
| `manoduścaritam` | nominative neuter singular compound | mental bad conduct |

**The three mental bad conducts**

```text
abhidhyā
    covetousness

vyāpāda
    ill will or hostility

mithyādṛṣṭi
    false view
```

**`akarma`**

The term must remain strong. These three are not merely “not outward actions”;
the Bhāṣya says their own nature is not karma and that they are distinct from
`cetanā`.

**`duścarita`**

Bad conduct is a wider practical classification than karma. It includes both
strict unwholesome karma and certain kleśic mental determinations.

## 4. Grammar

**Threefold unwholesome conduct**

```text
aśubham kāya-karma
aśubham vāk-karma
aśubham manaḥ-karma

yathā-kramam
duścarita-trayam matam
```

> Unwholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma are held to be the threefold bad
> conduct.

`Ādi` extends bodily karma through the verbal and mental members.

**Three non-karmic mental bad conducts**

```text
abhidhyā-ādi
akarma api tu
manaḥ-duścaritam
tridhā [bhavati]
```

> Covetousness and the other two, although not karma, nevertheless constitute
> threefold mental bad conduct.

The concessive `api` and contrastive `tu` emphasize that classification as bad
conduct does not entail classification as karma.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Unwholesome bodily karma and the rest are held to be the threefold bad
> conduct. But covetousness and the others, although not karma, constitute
> threefold mental bad conduct.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Unwholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma are respectively bodily, verbal,
> and mental bad conduct. Mental bad conduct is broader than mental karma: it
> also includes covetousness, ill will, and false view, although these are
> kleśic determinations distinct from volition and are not karma in their own
> nature. Volition may proceed through these afflictive avenues, which explains
> why a sūtra can indicate mental karma through them without identifying kleśa
> and karma.

The Dārṣṭāntika position, Vaibhāṣika resolution, and distinction from `cetanā`
are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Practical deformation is broader than completed karmic action. Covetousness,
> hostility, and false view already constitute bad mental conduct because they
> configure the avenue through which volition proceeds, yet they are not
> themselves volition or karma. The kleśa is therefore neither identical with
> the act nor externally unrelated to it: it determines the practical direction
> through which `cetanā` becomes karmic activity. Ground, mediation, and action
> remain distinct within one genesis.

False view is consequently practical deformation without being redescribed as
strict karma in Vasubandhu's taxonomy.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Threefold bad conduct as karma**

```text
unwholesome bodily karma
    → bodily bad conduct

unwholesome verbal karma
    → verbal bad conduct

unwholesome mental karma
    → mental bad conduct
```

**Mental bad conduct beyond karma**

```text
abhidhyā
vyāpāda
mithyādṛṣṭi
    → mental bad conduct
    → kleśic determinations
    → not strict karma
```

**`cetanārthāntarabhūta`**

The Bhāṣya says the three are a distinct determination or Meaning from
`cetanā`:

```text
kleśic orientation
    ≠ volition
```

Here `artha` marks the determinate doctrinal content distinguishing the terms;
it should not be flattened into an external Object.

**Dārṣṭāntika position**

The Dārṣṭāntikas identify covetousness, ill will, and false view themselves as
mental karma, citing the `Saṃcetanīya-sūtra`.

**Category-collapse objection**

The reply is:

```text
if these kleśas themselves are karma
    → karma and kleśa become identical
```

The Vaibhāṣika account rejects that identity.

**Vaibhāṣika resolution**

```text
kleśa
    supplies the avenue or orientation

cetanā
    proceeds through that avenue

mental karma
    arises as volitional activity
```

The sūtra indicates volition by naming the kleśas through which it proceeds.

**Definition of bad conduct**

The Bhāṣya calls bodily, verbal, and mental conduct blameworthy because it has
an undesirable result. `Duścarita` is determined through its resultant
direction, not merely its outward form.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Overlapping but non-identical extensions**

```text
some BAD_CONDUCT is KARMA
some BAD_CONDUCT is KLEŚA

but

KARMA ≠ KLEŚA
```

Therefore:

```text
MENTAL_KARMA
    is properly contained within
MENTAL_BAD_CONDUCT
```

**Genetic mediation**

```text
KLEŚA(K)
    → provides avenue A

CETANĀ(C)
    → proceeds through A

KARMA
    → volitional activity generated through that mediation
```

Difference is preserved through relation.

**Two false reductions**

```text
wrong reduction 1
    kleśa = karma

wrong reduction 2
    kleśa and karma are externally unrelated

textual determination
    kleśa mediates the course of cetanā without becoming cetanā
```

**Conduct begins before completed action**

```text
mental orientation
    can already be duścarita
    before or apart from completed strict karma
```

The practical field is broader than the field of executed karmic acts.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Kleśa as Ground, not karma**

The user's Ground formulation is confirmed at the level of mediation:

```text
kleśa
    grounds the deformed avenue

cetanā
    actualizes activity through that avenue

karma
    is the resulting strict volitional determination
```

But the Kośa insists that the Ground and the action are not identical.

**Status of Rational Karma**

“Rational Karma” remains useful as our higher architectural designation for
the rational Ground governing practical genesis. In Vasubandhu's strict local
taxonomy, however:

```text
mithyādṛṣṭi
    = kleśa and mental bad conduct
    ≠ karma in its own nature
```

The project term must therefore not be inserted into the primary textual layer.

**Testing the avidyā hypothesis**

The verse supports the claim that false rational determination becomes
practically effective by configuring volition, but it also demands a mediation:

```text
false view
    → avenue of cetanā
    → mental karma
```

This is more precise than calling false view itself karma.

**Preparation for karmapatha**

Because conduct is broader than karma, a karmapatha cannot be defined simply
as one more subtype of karmic substance. It is a course through which practical
determination proceeds. VAK 4.66 selects ten such paths.

This is the Organon contact. The school dispute, categorical objection,
scriptural resolution, and undesirable-result definition belong to the Bhāṣya;
Rational Karma is project synthesis.
