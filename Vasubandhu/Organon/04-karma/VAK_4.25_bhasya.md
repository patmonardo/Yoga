# VAK 4.25 Bhāṣya — Manifestation and Non-Manifestation Come Apart

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
vijñaptyaivānvitaḥ kurvan madhyastho mṛducetanaḥ /
tyaktānutpannavijñaptir avijñapty āryapudgalaḥ // 4.25 //
```

> A person in neither restraint nor non-restraint, acting with weak
> intention, is endowed with manifest action alone. A noble person
> whose manifest action has been relinquished or has not yet arisen
> is endowed with non-manifest action [alone].

The Bhāṣya unit begins with the four-cornered question at [211|12]
and runs through its closing statement at [211|20–21] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The following question at
[211|22] begins 4.26, on how restraints are acquired.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
syād vijñaptyā samanvāgato nāvijñaptyeti catuṣkoṭikam. tatra tāvat:

vijñaptyaivānvitaḥ kurvan madhyastho mṛducetanaḥ.

mṛdvyā cetanayā kuśalam akukśalaṃ vā kurvan naiva saṃvare
nāsaṃvare sthito vijñaptyaiva samanvāgato bhavati nāvijñaptyā,
prāgevāvyākṛte, anyatraupadhikapuṇyakriyāvastukarmapathebhyaḥ.

tyaktānutpannavijñaptir avijñapty āryapudgalaḥ.

avijñaptyaiva samanvāgato na vijñaptyā yenāryapudgalena
janmāntaraparivṛttau na tāvad vijñaptaṃ vā punar vihīnam.

uktaṃ saṃvarāsaṃvaramadhyasthānāṃ
vijñaptyavijñaptisamanvāgamanavyavasthānam.
```

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The running witness's
`akukśalaṃ` is retained above, though the intended contrast is plainly
`kuśala` / `akuśala`. Its compressed
`anyatraupadhikapuṇyakriyāvastukarmapathebhyaḥ` is not silently
resolved into a more specific class than the wording secures. The
last explanation of the noble person is also compressed; the
translation below marks its two alternatives without claiming that
all temporal details are settled.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Can a person be endowed with manifest action but not with non-manifest
action? A four-cornered analysis is made. First: “A person standing in
neither restraint nor non-restraint, acting with weak intention, is
endowed only with manifest action.” Such a person, performing a
wholesome or unwholesome action with weak intention, is endowed with
vijñapti alone and not with avijñapti. This is all the more so with
indeterminate action, apart from the specified action-courses
connected with material bases of merit-making.

“A noble person whose manifest action has been relinquished or has
not yet arisen is endowed with non-manifest action.” A noble person is
endowed with avijñapti alone, not vijñapti, when, after transition to
another birth, the relevant manifest action has not yet occurred, or
when it has subsequently been relinquished.

The arrangement of possession of vijñapti and avijñapti among those
established in restraint, non-restraint, and neither has now been
stated.

## 4. Manifest Action Without Non-Manifest Action

The first case gives `mṛducetanā`, **weak volition**, a decisive role.
A `madhyastha` may act in a wholesome or unwholesome way yet be
endowed only with the manifest action. Manifestation by itself does
not guarantee the additional avijñapti. The rule is about this
person, this weakness of intention, and possession of the two action
modes; it is not a reduction of all avijñapti to volition.

`Prāgevāvyākṛte` adds “all the more with indeterminate action,” but
`anyatra ...` limits that extension with a technical exception
involving material bases of merit-making and action-courses. The
exception's exact scope deserves further textual comparison. It
should not be dropped merely to make the rule look universal.

## 5. Non-Manifest Action Without Manifest Action

The second case reverses the possession relation. An `āryapudgala`
can be endowed with avijñapti without vijñapti in the circumstances
specified by the Bhāṣya: a change of birth before the relevant
manifest action has occurred, or after its relinquishment. This is
stronger than saying the person is merely inactive at a given
instant: the text explicitly concerns what is **possessed**.

The introductory `catuṣkoṭikam` promises four logical positions, but
this Kārikā and the following prose explicitly give the two crossed
cases. They suffice to show that neither kind of possession entails
the other. The omitted corners are not fabricated as further
quotations from Vasubandhu.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

This closes a useful test for our practical account. A visible
performance may fail to establish a continuing non-manifest
determination; a continuing determination may be possessed when no
corresponding manifest act is possessed. The distinction matters for
our vow analogy and for any future Dataset account of acts: an event
record is not, by itself, proof of a standing commitment, and a
standing commitment need not be continuously displayed as an event.
These are Organon consequences drawn from the Kośa cases, not
Vasubandhu's own software or vow theory.
