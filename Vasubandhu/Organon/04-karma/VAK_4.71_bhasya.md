# VAK 4.71 Bhāṣya — Completion Root and Objective Basis

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
mithyādṛṣṭes tu mohena śeṣāṇāṃ tribhir iṣyate /
sattvabhogāv adhiṣṭhānaṃ nāmarūpaṃ ca nāma ca // 4.71 //
```

> False view reaches completion through delusion; the remaining courses
> are held to reach completion through the three. Living being and object
> of enjoyment, name-and-form, and name are their respective bases.

The first half completes the classification by root begun in VAK 4.70.
The second reorganizes the ten courses into four groups according to the
`adhiṣṭhāna` upon which each group operates.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“False view, however, reaches completion through delusion,” for delusion
that has become intense brings it to completion.

“The remaining courses are held to reach completion through the three.”
Which are the remaining courses? False speech, divisive speech, and
disordered speech. Any of these can reach completion through the three:
through greed, through hatred, or through delusion.

These courses of karma have now been stated in four groups—three, three,
one, and three. Their respective bases are “living being and object of
enjoyment, name-and-form, and name.” Killing and the other members of its
group have a living being as their basis. Sexual misconduct and the other
members of its group have an object of enjoyment or possession as their
basis. False view has name-and-form as its basis. False speech and the
other members of its group have a name-complex as their basis.

## 4. The Completed Root Classification

VAK 4.70–4.71 together yield the complete map:

```text
DVEṢA
    killing
    hostility
    harsh speech

LOBHA
    sexual misconduct
    covetousness
    taking what is not given

MOHA
    false view

LOBHA / DVEṢA / MOHA
    false speech
    divisive speech
    disordered speech
```

False view is not identical with delusion. `Moha` becomes sufficiently
intense to complete the determinate course `mithyādṛṣṭi`. This confirms
the relation developed in 4.69: the root is a general generative Power;
the mental course is its determinate practical conclusion.

The remaining speech courses are structurally more variable. Their common
linguistic form can serve appropriation, hostility, or deluded assertion.
Knowing that a statement is false, divisive, or disordered therefore does
not by itself identify its completion root.

## 5. Adhiṣṭhāna: Where the Course Takes Its Stand

The four groups have four objective bases:

```text
3  killing, hostility, harsh speech
   → sattva: living being

3  sexual misconduct, covetousness, taking what is not given
   → bhoga: possession or object of enjoyment

1  false view
   → nāmarūpa: name-and-form

3  false speech, divisive speech, disordered speech
   → nāmakāya: name-complex
```

`Adhiṣṭhāna` adds the objective coordinate that the root analysis alone
could not supply. It is that upon which the course is established or
directed. The completed practical Form is consequently relational:

```text
Root
    governing Power

Course
    determinate practical Form

Adhiṣṭhāna
    objective basis upon which it takes its stand
```

The Bhāṣya does not explain here why false view has `nāmarūpa` as its basis.
That assignment should remain a precise open determination rather than be
filled by importing a later doctrine. By contrast, the basis of the three
speech courses is specified as `nāmakāya`, the organized complex of names,
not an isolated dictionary term.

## 6. Four Groups and the Practical Matrix

The explicit `3 + 3 + 1 + 3` grouping reorganizes the same ten courses
through a second axis. The completion-root classification and the
objective-basis classification do not replace one another:

```text
completion root
    through which Power does the course culminate?

objective basis
    upon what field does that determination operate?
```

This is precisely the kind of crossing required by the Dialectical Matrix.
Each course occupies a position formed by at least three coordinates:

```text
<Root, Course, Adhiṣṭhāna>
```

The speech group makes the matrix visible. One stable basis—`nāmakāya`—
supports three different courses, and each of those courses can reach
completion through any of three roots. Practical meaning belongs to the
whole configuration rather than to a word, behavior label, or motive taken
alone.

## 7. Organon Contact Point

The Fichtean formulation can now be expanded:

```text
Course takes its Stand on Root
    as subjective Principle.

Course takes its Stand upon Adhiṣṭhāna
    as objective field.
```

The karmapatha mediates the two. It is the determinate Power through which
a root becomes objectively effective upon a basis. For our agent
architecture, a practical judgment therefore needs to represent the
governing root, the course-form, and the field upon which it operates.

This also clarifies why dictionary thinking is inadequate. `Nāmakāya` is
already configured language. Falsehood, division, and disorder are
operations performed through that configuration under a governing root.
The Organon reading treats this as a practical schema; the four groups and
their bases belong to the Kārikā and Bhāṣya themselves.
