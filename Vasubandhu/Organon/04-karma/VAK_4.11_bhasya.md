# VAK_4.11 Bhāṣya — Which Cognitions Initiate or Accompany Action?

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> pravartakaṃ dṛṣṭiheyaṃ vijñānam ubhayaṃ punaḥ /
> mānasaṃ bhāvanāheyaṃ pañcakaṃ tv anuvartakam // 4.11 //

> Cognition abandoned by seeing initiates action. Mental cognition
> abandoned by cultivation can do both; the five sensory cognitions
> only accompany action.

The Bhāṣya completes this as a fourfold distribution, adding
uncontaminated cognition as neither. The Kārikā study is `VAK_4.11.md`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

In this matter, “cognition abandoned by seeing initiates action.” A
mind to be abandoned through seeing initiates manifestation because it
serves as the causal basis for the initial and sustained applications
of thought that produce the manifestation. It does not accompany the
performance: when outward-facing mind is active during the deed,
that earlier mind is absent.

But then form produced by such a mind would itself be something to be
abandoned through seeing. What would follow? An Abhidharma rule would
be contradicted: there is no form to be abandoned through seeing,
since material form is not opposed by knowledge or ignorance in that
way. An interlocutor says that this very position needs proof. Would
the great elements produced from the same mind also be abandoned
through seeing? They would not, just as they do not become wholesome
or unwholesome merely from that cause. Someone suggests accepting the
consequence, but this cannot be done: the elements cannot properly be
classified either as abandoned through seeing or as not to be
abandoned at all. Why? A non-afflicted dharma does not conflict with
knowledge or ignorance. Thus there is no conflict
with the sūtra: its sequence concerns **causal initiation**, not the
mind present during execution or a transfer of its path status to
material form.

“Mental cognition abandoned by cultivation can do both.” Mental
cognition to be abandoned through cultivation can initiate action
and accompany it. “The fivefold group, however, accompanies action.”
The five sensory cognitions only accompany. This gives a fourfold
alternative: mind abandoned through seeing only initiates; the five
sensory cognitions only accompany; mental cognition abandoned through
cultivation does both; uncontaminated cognition does neither.

The middle objection-and-reply sequence is especially compressed in
the running witness. The translation preserves its argumentative
outcome without claiming that every brief voice has been securely
identified.

## 4. Argument and Voices

The verse distributes the roles defined in 4.10 across types of
cognition. First, a mind abandoned by seeing can initiate manifest
action indirectly through `vitarka` and `vicāra`, but is absent during
outward performance. A challenge asks whether its material product
must inherit the same path classification. The reply denies that
transfer and confines the scriptural sequence to causal initiation.
The closing prose supplies the other three positions in a
`catuṣkoṭika`, a fourfold alternative.

The challenges concerning material form and great elements are
dialectical; the source does not securely assign each sentence to
one named school. The Abhidharma rule about form is cited within
the exchange, not presented as a new verse claim.

## 5. The Fourfold Distribution

| Relation to action | Cognition |
|---|---|
| Initiates only | Mind abandoned by seeing |
| Accompanies only | Five sensory cognitions |
| Both | Mental cognition abandoned by cultivation |
| Neither | Uncontaminated cognition |

The table is the Bhāṣya's explicit `catuṣkoṭika`. These are functional
roles relative to bodily or verbal manifestation. The classification
does not deny that uncontaminated cognition has other causal or
liberative functions.

## 6. Why Seeing-abandoned Mind Is Initiating Only

The commentary names that mind a `nidāna`, causal basis, for the
`vitarka` and `vicāra` from which manifestation arises. Its
contribution can precede the deed. The outward-facing mind present
during bodily or verbal action is different, so the initiating mind
does not also accompany the performance. This is the precise sense
in which the scriptural movement from wrong view to wrong speech and
action can be accepted after 4.10.

## 7. The Produced-Form Objection

The objection tests whether a cause's status transfers to its
material product. If a mind abandoned by seeing produces bodily
form, must that form too be abandoned by seeing? The exchange rejects
this automatic transfer. The great elements expose the problem:
their production by such a mind does not suffice to give them the
same ethical or path status. The source invokes the Abhidharma
distinction between what knowledge and ignorance oppose and a
non-afflicted material dharma.

The argument should be kept local. It does not say that material
conditions are irrelevant to action; it says that one specific
classification of the initiating mind does not simply become the
classification of its material product.

## 8. Translation and Doctrinal Limits

“Cognition” renders `vijñāna` here and keeps it distinct from the
action term `vijñapti`. `Mānasaṃ` is resolved by the prose as
`manovijñāna`; `pañcakam` as five sensory `vijñānakāyāḥ`.
`Dṛṣṭiheya` and `bhāvanāheya` refer to modes of abandonment on the
path, not to visual versus nonvisual cognition.

`Akliṣṭa` is rendered “non-afflicted.” It should not be assimilated to
`anāsrava`, “uncontaminated,” which names the fourth case in the
Bhāṣya's classification. The `vitarka` and `vicāra` are said to
produce the `vijñapti`; the seeing-abandoned mind is their causal
basis (`nidāna`). The objection's `nāpy aprahātavyāni` also excludes
placing the great elements in the class “not to be abandoned”; the
reply is not limited to denying abandonment through seeing.

The middle source exchange is rough enough that fine speaker
attribution and the exact scope of the Abhidharma objection remain
provisional. The fourfold conclusion is explicit.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

**Textual determination.** The same action-process can have one
cognitive condition that initiates it and another that accompanies
its execution. The Bhāṣya does not posit one inner state persisting
unchanged throughout the act. Nor does it let the path status of an
initiating cognition pass automatically into material form.

**Organon reconstruction.** For LOKA:KARMA, this is a disciplined
model of divided causal roles. A graph can represent initiation and
accompaniment as different edges, but the warrant for each edge comes
from the Bhāṣya's argument. The fourfold distribution is a useful
logical shape; it should not be expanded into a complete psychology
without reading the next distinctions.
