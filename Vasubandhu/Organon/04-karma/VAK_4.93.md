# VAK_4.93

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
त्रीणि चत्वारि चैकं च दृग्घेयस्य तदादयः ।
ते द्वे चत्वार्यथ त्रीणि भावनाहेयकर्मणः ॥ ४.९३ ॥
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
trīṇi | catvāri | ca | ekam | ca | dṛk-heyasya | tad-ādayaḥ |
te | dve | catvāri | atha | trīṇi | bhāvanā-heya-karmaṇaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `trīṇi` | nominative neuter plural | three fruit-relations |
| `catvāri` | nominative neuter plural | four fruit-relations |
| `ekam` | nominative neuter singular | one fruit-relation |
| `dṛk-heyasya` | genitive singular compound | of karma to be abandoned through seeing |
| `tad-ādayaḥ` | nominative masculine plural compound | result-dharmas beginning with that same class |
| `te` | nominative masculine plural demonstrative | those same ordered result-classes |
| `dve` | nominative neuter dual | two fruit-relations |
| `atha` | connective particle | then, next |
| `bhāvanā-heya-karmaṇaḥ` | genitive singular compound | of karma to be abandoned through cultivation |

**The ordered target series**

`Tad-ādayaḥ` and `te` establish one continuing order:

```text
1. darśana-heya
   dharmas to be abandoned through seeing

2. bhāvanā-heya
   dharmas to be abandoned through cultivation

3. apraheya
   dharmas not to be abandoned
```

The verse supplies the first two source rows of a directed three-by-three
matrix. VAK 4.94 will give the third row.

## 4. Grammar

**Karma abandoned through seeing**

```text
dṛg-gheyasya [karmaṇaḥ]
    tad-ādayaḥ [dharmāḥ]
    trīṇi catvāri ekam [phalāni]
    [anukramam]
```

> For karma to be abandoned through seeing, result-dharmas beginning with
> that class are related through three, four, and one fruits respectively.

The distributive order is implicit in the three consecutive numerals and made
exact by the Bhāṣya.

**Karma abandoned through cultivation**

```text
bhāvanā-heya-karmaṇaḥ
    te [tad-ādayaḥ dharmāḥ]
    dve catvāri atha trīṇi [phalāni]
    [anukramam]
```

> For karma to be abandoned through cultivation, those same result-classes
> are related through two, four, and then three fruits respectively.

The demonstrative `te` preserves both the target classes and their order; it
does not introduce a new set of dharmas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For that which is to be abandoned through seeing, those beginning with that
> class are three, four, and one results. For karma to be abandoned through
> cultivation, those same are two, four, and then three.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Karma to be abandoned through seeing can have seeing-abandonable dharmas as
> results through correspondence, activity-production, and dominant
> conditioning; cultivation-abandonable dharmas through maturation,
> correspondence, activity-production, and dominant conditioning; and
> non-abandonable dharmas only through dominant conditioning. Karma to be
> abandoned through cultivation can have seeing-abandonable dharmas as results
> through activity-production and dominant conditioning;
> cultivation-abandonable dharmas through maturation, correspondence,
> activity-production, and dominant conditioning; and non-abandonable dharmas
> through disconnection, activity-production, and dominant conditioning.

The third target class and the exact fruit-membership of every cell are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The mode in which a determination can be overcome is itself causally
> significant. What is removed through seeing, what requires cultivation, and
> what is not to be abandoned do not form three static bins. Their directed
> relations disclose a genetic order. A determination exposed and abandoned
> by seeing can govern the non-abandonable field only externally through
> dominant efficacy; a determination whose abandonment requires cultivation
> can stand to that field through actual disconnection and active production.
> Rational insight identifies the ground of error, while cultivated practice
> reorganizes the continuum so that freedom becomes stable actuality.

The Path form is:

```text
Seeing the Truths
    → determines what must be abandoned by insight

Cultivation
    → works through what persists after insight

Disconnection
    → becomes an actual result in relation to the non-abandonable
```

This does not identify abandonable karma with the Path operation that abandons
it. It shows that the classification of the causal material already encodes
the distinct work of seeing and cultivation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Darśana-heya` or `dṛg-heya` — abandoned through seeing**

A determination removable through the Path of Seeing the Truths. The term
classifies what is abandoned, not the seeing-operation itself. Its causal
profile differs according to the abandonment-status of the result-dharma.

**`Bhāvanā-heya` — abandoned through cultivation**

A determination that persists beyond initial insight and must be removed
through repeated development of the Path. `Bhāvanā` here is active cultivation
and stabilization, not imaginative representation.

**`Apraheya` — not to be abandoned**

The third target class, explicit in the Bhāṣya though only implied by the
kārikā's ordered series. It includes dharmas whose status is not that of an
obstruction requiring removal.

**`Heya` — to be abandoned**

A functional Path determination. It specifies the mode under which a dharma
stands to liberative transformation, rather than marking the dharma as a
substance to be physically destroyed.

**Seeing-abandonable to non-abandonable**

Only `adhipati-phala` is admitted. The source can condition the
non-abandonable result-field, but it is not its maturation, corresponding
continuation, disconnection-result, or activity-produced result.

**Cultivation-abandonable to non-abandonable**

`Visaṃyoga`, `puruṣakāra`, and `adhipati` are admitted. This cell connects the
material requiring cultivation with achieved separation, active production,
and governing efficacy.

**Equal count, different structure**

The count three occurs in two different cells:

```text
seeing-abandonable → seeing-abandonable
    {niṣyanda, puruṣakāra, adhipati}

cultivation-abandonable → non-abandonable
    {visaṃyoga, puruṣakāra, adhipati}
```

The replacement of correspondence by disconnection expresses a change in
causal form invisible at the level of number.

The matrix makes the passage from insight to stabilized transformation

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.93 begins the fruit-system's most explicitly Path-oriented indexing.
The categories are defined not by what dharmas look like but by how they can
be overcome. Abandonment-status is therefore a relation between a determinate
obstruction and the operation adequate to it.

Seeing and cultivation must not be collapsed. Seeing the Truths can destroy a
false schema whose persistence depends upon ignorance of the causal whole.
But not every conditioned tendency disappears when its false principle is
first exposed. Some determinations require repeated counter-operation until
the continuum itself has been reorganized. `Bhāvanā-heya` names this second
practical problem.

The two three-member cells express the transition exactly. In the
seeing-to-seeing cell, correspondence remains: the causal material continues
within its own class. In the cultivation-to-non-abandonable cell,
correspondence has been replaced by disconnection. The same formal count
contains an opposed practical meaning—continuation in one case, separation in
the other.

This directly supports Rational Yoga. Schema Inference does not end when the
Principle has been intellectually recognized. The Path must determine which
appearances fall through insight and which conditioned formations require
repeated Kriyā. Saṃyama is Practical Reason because it joins valid cognition
to the cultivation capable of producing a transformed Bhūmi.

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy makes the genetic necessity visible. The
truth first appears as negative judgment upon a false determination; it then
must become objective in a reformed actuality. Seeing is the decisive
comprehension, cultivation its realization, and disconnection the achieved
relation in which the former obstruction no longer governs the continuum.
