# VAK_4.14 — Eight Names, Four Restraint-Types, and Compatibility

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अष्टधा प्रातिमोक्षाख्यो द्रव्यतस्तु चतुर्विधः ।
>
> लिङ्गतो नामसंचारात् पृथक् ते चाविरोधिनः ॥ ४.१४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> aṣṭadhā prātimokṣākhyo dravyatas tu caturvidhaḥ /
>
> liṅgato nāmasaṃcārāt pṛthak te cāvirodhinaḥ // 4.14 //

VAK 4.13 distinguishes restraint, non-restraint, and a third class that is
neither. This verse does not explain that third class. It follows the
restraint branch and examines *prātimokṣa*: eight disciplinary names, four
restraint-types, and the relation between successively undertaken restraints.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
aṣṭadhā prātimokṣa-ākhyaḥ /
dravyataḥ tu caturvidhaḥ /
liṅgataḥ nāma-saṃcārāt /
pṛthak te ca avirodhinaḥ
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| aṣṭadhā | indeclinable adverb | in eight ways |
| prātimokṣa-ākhyaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound adjective | called *prātimokṣa* |
| dravyataḥ | ablatival adverb | by distinct restraint-type |
| tu | contrastive particle | but |
| caturvidhaḥ | nominative masculine singular adjective | fourfold |
| liṅgataḥ | ablatival adverb | according to *liṅga*, specified by the Bhāṣya |
| nāma-saṃcārāt | ablative singular compound | because disciplinary names transfer |
| pṛthak | indeclinable adverb | separately |
| te | nominative masculine plural pronoun | those restraints, specified by the Bhāṣya |
| avirodhinaḥ | nominative masculine plural adjective | mutually compatible |

The Bhāṣya identifies *liṅga* here with the sex-characteristic that governs
the disciplinary names in its classification. Its referent for *te* is the
successively undertaken lay, novice, and monastic restraints.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda predicates `aṣṭadhā` and `caturvidhaḥ` of the understood restraint, contrasting `nāmatas` with `dravyataḥ`. In the second, `liṅgataḥ nāmasaṃcārāt` gives the causal relation; `pṛthak` and `avirodhinaḥ` predicate of `te`.

## 5. Translation

### Close Translation

> *Prātimokṣa* restraint is eightfold by designation, but fourfold by
> distinct restraint-type. Because names transfer according to *liṅga*,
> those [successively undertaken restraints] are separate and also
> compatible.

### Bhāṣya-informed Study Translation

> *Prātimokṣa* restraint has eight disciplinary designations but four
> distinct restraint-types. The Bhāṣya explains that some designations
> transfer with a change in the sex-characteristic it describes, without
> requiring the abandonment of an existing restraint or the acquisition
> of a new one. Lay, novice, and monastic restraints undertaken in
> succession, by contrast, arise separately because their causal grounds
> differ. They can nevertheless coexist: undertaking a later restraint
> does not abandon an earlier one.

The explanations of name-transfer, causal grounds, and coexistence come
from the Bhāṣya; the kārikā states the counts and their contrast.

## 6. Interpretation

In the Organon reading, the verse distinguishes a change in designation
from a change in practical determination, and a distinct determination
from an opposed one. Its account of successively undertaken restraints
connects fuller discipline to a reorganization of the conditions for
action, not simply to an accumulating list of rules. This supports reading
restraint as a practical determination of Cetanā, while the Bhāṣya's
explicit grounds here are distinct causal bases and compatibility.

This verse analyzes *prātimokṣa* restraint only. It does not identify
instances of the third *avijñapti* class, “neither restraint nor
non-restraint,” named in 4.13.

## 7. Logical Determination

Eight designations → four restraint-types; name-transfer is distinct from type; successive restraints are separate and compatible.
