# VAK_4.55 — Present-Life Result and Complete Detachment

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दृष्टधर्मफलं कर्म क्षेत्राशयविशेषतः ।
तद्भूम्यत्यन्तवैराग्याद् विपाके नियतं हि यत् ॥ ४.५५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
dṛṣṭadharmaphalaṃ karma kṣetrāśayaviśeṣataḥ /
tadbhūmyatyantavairāgyād vipāke niyataṃ hi yat // 4.55 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
dṛṣṭa-dharma-phalam karma kṣetra-āśaya-viśeṣataḥ /
tad-bhūmi-atyanta-vairāgyāt vipāke niyatam hi yat //
```

The Bhāṣya distinguishes three routes to a present-life result: exceptional
field, exceptional intention, and complete detachment from the relevant level.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `dṛṣṭadharmaphalam` | nominative neuter singular compound | having fruit in the present life |
| `karma` | nominative neuter singular | karma |
| `kṣetra` | compound member | field; recipient-field |
| `āśaya` | compound member | underlying intention or purposive disposition |
| `viśeṣataḥ` | ablative adverbial form | because of exceptional character |
| `tadbhūmi` | compound member | the level belonging to that karma |
| `atyantavairāgyāt` | ablative masculine singular compound | through complete detachment |
| `vipāke` | locative masculine singular | with respect to maturation |
| `niyatam` | nominative neuter singular | fixed; determinate |
| `hi` | explanatory particle | for; indeed |
| `yat` | relative pronoun | whatever karma |

**`kṣetraviśeṣa`**

An exceptional recipient-field gives an action unusual karmic efficacy. This
develops the `guṇakṣetra` teaching of VAK 4.54.

**`āśayaviśeṣa`**

`Āśaya` is the underlying intention, disposition, or purposive orientation
from which the action proceeds. The Bhāṣya presents its exceptional character
as a ground for a present-life result.

**`atyantavairāgya`**

The rule concerns complete detachment from the `bhūmi` to which the karma
belongs. Treating this as closure of a future field is an interpretive model;
the Bhāṣya's stated consequence depends on the karma's maturation and temporal
fixity.

## 4. Grammar

**Exceptional field or intention**

```text
karma
kṣetra-viśeṣataḥ vā
āśaya-viśeṣataḥ vā
dṛṣṭa-dharma-phalam [bhavati]
```

> Karma may bear fruit in the present life because of an exceptional field or
> exceptional intention.

The compact compound is distributed into two alternative ablative grounds by
the Bhāṣya.

**Complete detachment**

```text
yat [karma]
vipāke niyatam
tat-bhūmi-atyanta-vairāgyāt
dṛṣṭa-dharma-phalam [bhavati]
```

> Karma fixed with respect to maturation may bear fruit in the present life
> through complete detachment from its corresponding level.

`Vipāke` specifies the respect in which karma is fixed. The prose excludes
karma already fixed to another temporal stage and karma whose maturation is
indeterminate.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Karma has its result in the present life because of an exceptional field or
> intention; likewise through complete detachment from its corresponding level,
> when it is fixed with respect to maturation.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Karma may have its result in the present life because of an exceptional field
> or intention; it may also do so through complete detachment from its level,
> when its maturation is determinate.
## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Karma has its result in the present life because of an exceptional field or intention; likewise through complete detachment from its corresponding level, when it is fixed with respect to maturation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Exceptional field**

```text
exceptional recipient-field
    → intensified karmic relation
    → present-life result
```

The Bhāṣya reports a transformation of sexual characteristics after someone
called the Saṅgha “women.” The source does not supply the surrounding
narrative. The account exemplifies field-conditioned present-life result;
its inherited bodily categories remain text-bound.

**Exceptional intention**

The second traditional example concerns a person classified by the source as
`śaṇḍha` who releases cattle from castration and acquires male characteristics.
Its doctrinal function is narrower than its inherited narrative form:

```text
exceptional purposive orientation
    → intensified wholesome karma
    → result in the present life
```

**Karma fixed in maturation**

```text
vipāka-niyata
    maturation must occur

avasthā-aniyata
    its temporal stage is not fixed
```

Under this rule, only this combination becomes present-life-result karma
through complete detachment.

**Karma fixed to another stage**

If maturation is already fixed to a later temporal stage, it must occur there.
The Bhāṣya says complete detachment from that level cannot take place before
the scheduled maturation.

**Indeterminate karma**

```text
aniyata karma
    + complete detachment from its field
    → non-maturation
```

Closure does not force actuality where actuality was never necessary.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Three routes to a present-life result**

```text
EXCEPTIONAL_FIELD(K)       → PRESENT_LIFE_RESULT(K)
EXCEPTIONAL_INTENTION(K)   → PRESENT_LIFE_RESULT(K)
```

For field closure:

```text
VIPAKA_FIXED(K)
    + TEMPORAL_STAGE_UNFIXED(K)
    + COMPLETELY_DETACHED(agent, FIELD(K))
    → PRESENT_LIFE_RESULT(K)
```

**Indeterminate karma follows another rule**

```text
VIPAKA_UNFIXED(K)
    + FIELD_PERMANENTLY_CLOSED(K)
    → MATURATION_MAY_NOT_OCCUR(K)
```

**Three independent variables**

| Variable | Values |
|---|---|
| Maturation necessity | fixed / unfixed |
| Temporal stage | fixed / unfixed |
| Future field | open / permanently closed |

The outcome depends on their combination, not on “detachment” alone.

**Scope of the detachment rule**

```text
complete detachment
    changes the outcome of the karma under discussion
    according to its maturation and temporal fixity
```

This passage does not state that detachment prevents the production of future
karma; it specifies the result of detachment for karma already classified by
its modal status.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The examples of exceptional field and intention and the exact
modal limitation on the detachment rule are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The commentary
illustrates the exceptional field with speech calling the Saṅgha “women,”
and exceptional intention with a `śaṇḍha` freeing cattle from castration.
Complete detachment makes the karma
present-life-result only when maturation is fixed but its temporal stage is not
fixed. Karma fixed to another stage matures there; indeterminate karma instead fails
to mature through detachment.

**Karma as Ground**

The Dharmapāda connection is exact at the level of sufficient ground:

```text
karma
    is not merely a past event

karma
    is the determinate Ground
    of a possible or necessary actuality
```

VAK 4.55 then differentiates that Ground rigorously by maturation necessity,
temporal placement, and availability of its field.

**Absolute Ground does not mean universal fatalism**

The Bhāṣya's modal discrimination is essential:

```text
necessary karmic ground
    must mature

indeterminate karmic ground
    may fail to mature
```

“Absolute” must therefore name the completeness of the grounding relation, not
the claim that every karmic tendency inevitably appears.

**Closing a field changes the pending causal system**

Here complete detachment affects the maturation of outstanding karma according
to its status: karma certain to mature but not fixed to another stage has a
present-life result, while indeterminate karma may fail to mature.

**Field and intention are equally real determinants**

Present-life result can arise from the recipient-side or the agent-side of the
relation. Karma is neither purely subjective intention nor merely external
contact with an object.

This is the Organon contact. The three routes, traditional examples, and modal
limitations belong to the Bhāṣya; Karma as Absolute Ground is the project
synthesis.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> Complete detachment has different consequences for karmas with different
> modal status. If maturation is certain but its temporal stage is unfixed,
> detachment from the karma's level is accompanied by a present-life result. If
> the stage is fixed elsewhere, the maturation remains assigned there; if
> maturation is indeterminate, complete detachment is accompanied by
> non-maturation.

Exceptional field and exceptional intention supply two further routes by which
causal power contracts the interval between action and actuality.
