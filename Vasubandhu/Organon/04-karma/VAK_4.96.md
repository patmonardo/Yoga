# VAK_4.96

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आनन्तर्याणि कर्माणि तीव्रक्लेशोऽथ दुर्गतिः ।
कौरवासंज्ञिसत्त्वाश्च मतमावरणत्रयम् ॥ ४.९६ ॥
```

The first-pass kārikā witness has an orthographically compressed reading in
the second line. The text printed here follows the fuller reading preserved
with the Bhāṣya witness; its division into Uttarakuru beings and
non-percipient beings is confirmed by the prose explanation. The working
e-text is non-critical, so this remains a provisional reading.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ānantaryāṇi | karmāṇi | tīvra-kleśaḥ | atha | durgatiḥ |
kauravāḥ | asaṃjñi-sattvāḥ | ca | matam | āvaraṇa-trayam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |

| `karmāṇi` | nominative neuter plural | karmas; the five immediate-result deeds |
| `tīvra-kleśaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | continual or persistently recurring affliction |

**Bhāṣya-resolved enumeration**

```text
ānantaryāṇi karmāṇi
    → karmāvaraṇa

tīvrakleśaḥ
    → kleśāvaraṇa

trividhā durgatiḥ + auttarakauravāḥ + asaṃjñisattvāḥ
    → vipākāvaraṇa
```

The prose is essential for recovering this threefold distribution. The
kārikā names the instances; the Bhāṣya supplies the three formal labels and
explains their common function.

## 4. Grammar

**The first obstruction**

```text
ānantaryāṇi karmāṇi
```

> The karmas whose maturation admits no intervening result.

`Ānantaryāṇi` attributes a special order of maturation to the karmas. The
Bhāṣya offers two connected explanations: no other karma or birth-result can
intervene so as to displace their maturation, and after the agent's death no
intervening birth stands between that death and the consequent hell-birth.
It does not mean merely “quick” in ordinary chronological time.

**The second obstruction**

```text
tīvra-kleśaḥ
```

> Continual affliction.

The compound is grammatically transparent but lexically underdetermined
without the commentary. Here the Bhāṣya explicitly distinguishes `tīvra`,
frequent or continual, from `tīkṣṇa`, exceedingly strong. Accordingly,
“intense affliction” would obscure the argument.

**The third obstruction**

```text
atha
    durgatiḥ
    kauravāḥ
    asaṃjñisattvāḥ ca
```

> Then bad destiny, the Uttarakurus, and non-percipient beings.

The prose expands singular `durgatiḥ` as the three bad destinies. The
Uttarakurus and non-percipient beings are added as favorable or specialized
modes of existence that are nevertheless unsuitable for Path-arising.

**The governing predication**

```text
[etat] āvaraṇatrayaṃ matam
```

> This is recognized as the triad of obstructions.

The singular neuter predicate gathers the enumerated members into one
threefold classification.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The immediate-result karmas, continual affliction, and then bad destiny,
> the Uttarakurus, and non-percipient beings are recognized as the threefold
> obstruction.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Three obstructions are recognized. Karmic obstruction consists in the five
> deeds whose maturation admits no intervening result. Afflictive obstruction
> consists not simply in an occasional affliction of extreme force, but in
> persistently recurring affliction that leaves no interval for counter-effort.
> Resultant obstruction consists in existence in the three bad destinies,
> among the Uttarakurus, or among non-percipient beings. Each obstructs the
> noble Path or the wholesome roots preparatory to it.

The five deeds, the contrast between `tīvra` and `tīkṣṇa`, the exact object of
obstruction, and the explanation of `ānantarya` are Bhāṣya determinations,
not wording expressed in full by the kārikā.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A conditioned continuum can lose the practical capacity for the Path in
> three different ways: through a decisive deed whose maturation cannot be
> displaced, through an afflictive continuity that suppresses every interval
> for counter-practice, or through a resultant placement in which the
> conditions of Path-generation are unavailable. Obstruction is therefore
> measured neither by unpleasantness alone nor by the sheer magnitude of a
> state, but by the closure of a determinate practical possibility.

The rational structure is:

```text
determining deed
    → fixes an unavoidable karmic sequence

continual affliction
    → removes the interval for practical opposition

resultant mode of existence
    → withholds the conditions for Path-generation

common truth
    → incapacity for the Path or its preparatory wholesome roots
```

In Rational Yoga terms, a Bhūmi is not merely an appearance presented to a
spectator; it is the structured field within which Kriyā-Jñāna can or cannot
be generated. VAK 4.96 determines three ways in which the conditioned field
can close against the Practical Reason that would transform it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Āvaraṇa-traya` — the three obstructions**

The functional triad of karmic, afflictive, and resultant obstruction. Their
unity lies in what they obstruct: the noble Path and the wholesome roots that
prepare for it. They are not three synonyms for moral badness.

**`Karmāvaraṇa` — karmic obstruction**

The five `ānantarya` karmas: killing one's mother, killing one's father,
killing an arhat, causing schism in the Saṅgha, and maliciously causing blood
to flow from a Tathāgata's body. This list and label are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

**`Ānantarya-karma` — karma admitting no intervening result**

A deed whose maturation cannot be displaced by another karma or
birth-result. The term determines the ordering of karmic fruition, not mere
temporal speed.

**`Kleśāvaraṇa` — afflictive obstruction**

Obstruction constituted by continual affliction. The practical difficulty is
that uninterrupted recurrence leaves no opportunity for exertion toward its
removal.

**`Tīvra` and `tīkṣṇa`**

In this passage, `tīvra` is explained as frequent, recurring, or continual,
whereas `tīkṣṇa` means very strong or excessive in degree. An occasional
`tīkṣṇa` affliction can be opposed; `tīvra` affliction blocks the interval in
which opposition could begin.

**`Parākramakāla` — opportunity for exertion**

The temporal opening required for counter-practice. This Bhāṣya expression
makes the theory of obstruction explicitly practical: transformation needs
an interval in which effort can be initiated.

**`Vipākāvaraṇa` — resultant obstruction**

A mode of resultant existence structurally incompatible with generating the
Path. It includes the three bad destinies, Uttarakuru, and non-percipient
existence.

**`Āryamārga-prāyogika kuśalamūla` — wholesome roots preparatory to the noble Path**

The Bhāṣya names these, together with the noble Path itself, as what the
three obstructions obstruct. The obstruction can therefore close preparation
before Path-realization proper arises.

**Ranking in the Bhāṣya**

Afflictive obstruction is said to be the worst, followed by karmic
obstruction. The subsequent Vaibhāṣika explanation is textually insecure in
the working witness; its detailed mechanism should remain provisional.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
P = arising of the noble Path
R = arising of wholesome roots preparatory to P
K = an ānantarya karma
C = continual affliction
V = a Path-incompatible resultant existence
```

Then the common relation is:

```text
Obstructs(K, P)
Obstructs(K, R)

Obstructs(C, P)
Obstructs(C, R)

Obstructs(V, P)
Obstructs(V, R)
```

But the mechanisms remain distinct:

```text
K
    → no intervening karmic result can displace maturation

C
    → no interval for counter-effort
    → no effective preparatory practice

V
    → resultant conditions are incompatible with Path-generation
```

**Continuity is not intensity**

```text
HighIntensity(x) + Occasional(x)
    → interval for counter-effort remains possible

Continual(x)
    → interval for counter-effort is suppressed
    → practical obstruction
```

Thus:

```text
degree of affliction
    ≠ sufficient criterion of afflictive obstruction

continuity that closes practical opposition
    = decisive criterion in this passage
```

**Resultant obstruction is not suffering alone**

```text
PleasantResult(x)
    does not imply
PathCapable(x)
```

Uttarakuru existence shows that a comparatively fortunate result can still
be an obstruction when it lacks the determinate conditions for the Path.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.95 distinguished the one karma that projects a rebirth-class from the
many karmas that complete its concrete features. VAK 4.96 now evaluates a
conditioned existence by a higher criterion: can the continuum generate the
Path within this field?

This transition confirms that the Path is the truth of the karmic courses of
action. Karma does not reach its complete systematic meaning merely when it
produces a result. Its result must be judged according to whether it sustains
or closes the practical capacity for transformation. A karmic determination
is therefore fully intelligible only through its relation to Path-generation.

The analysis of continual affliction is especially exact. Practical negation
requires a temporal difference within the conditioned stream: an interval in
which the present determination can be opposed and another course initiated.
An occasional but powerful kleśa still permits such a difference. Continual
kleśa closes it. The obstruction is consequently not raw strength but the
suppression of the middle through which Kriyā could turn the conditioned
field against its given course.

This is a precise point of contact with the proposed account of Samyama as
Practical Reason. Samyama applies a Principle to the appearances of a Bhūmi
and generates their valid structure as Jñāna. `Āvaraṇa` names determinations
that prevent that application from becoming operative: deed fixes the
sequence, afflictive recurrence prevents counter-application, or resultant
placement deprives practice of its field. This is an Organon reconstruction;
the textual doctrine itself is the narrower claim that these conditions
obstruct the noble Path and its preparatory wholesome roots.
