# VAK_4.96 — Three Obstructions to the Noble Path

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आनन्तर्याणि कर्माणि तीव्रक्लेशोऽथ दुर्गतिः ।
कौरवासंज्ञिसत्त्वाश्च मतमावरणत्रयम् ॥ ४.९६ ॥
```

| `kauravāḥ` | nominative masculine plural | people of Uttarakuru |

| `matam` | nominative neuter singular participle | is held or regarded |
| `āvaraṇa-trayam` | nominative neuter singular | the triad of obstructions |

The sandhi `kauravāsaṃjñisattvāś ca` is divided as `kauravāḥ
asaṃjñisattvāḥ ca`: people of Uttarakuru and non-percipient beings. The
Bhāṣya supplies the three formal obstruction-types and their precise
instances.

## 4. Grammar

**The enumerated conditions**

```text
ānantaryāṇi karmāṇi
tīvra-kleśaḥ
atha durgatiḥ, kauravāḥ, asaṃjñi-sattvāḥ ca
    āvaraṇa-trayam matam
```

> Actions of *ānantarya*, recurrent affliction, and then bad destinies,
> Uttarakuru people, and non-percipient beings are held to be the three
> obstructions.

The verse compresses the grouping. The Bhāṣya distributes its members as
action-obstruction, affliction-obstruction, and maturation-obstruction:

```text
five ānantarya actions
    → action-obstruction

recurrent affliction
    → affliction-obstruction

three bad destinies + Uttarakuru + non-percipient existence
    → maturation-obstruction
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> The actions of *ānantarya*, recurrent affliction, and then bad destinies,
> people of Uttarakuru, and non-percipient beings are held to be the three
> obstructions.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The actions of immediate retribution, persistent affliction, and then bad
> destinies, inhabitants of Uttarakuru, and non-percipient beings are held to be
> the three obstructions.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The actions of *ānantarya*, recurrent affliction, and then bad destinies, people of Uttarakuru, and non-percipient beings are held to be the three obstructions.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

- **Obstruction** (*āvaraṇa*) — a condition that obstructs the noble Path or
  its preparatory wholesome roots.
- **Action-obstruction** (*karmāvaraṇa*) — the five actions of
  *ānantarya*.
- **Affliction-obstruction** (*kleśāvaraṇa*) — recurrent affliction that
  prevents an interval for effort toward removal.
- **Maturation-obstruction** (*vipākāvaraṇa*) — resultant existence in the
  three bad destinies, Uttarakuru, or non-percipient states.
- **Recurrent** (*tīvra*, explained as *abhīkṣṇika*) — frequent or continual;
  in this passage, the obstructive condition.
- **Intense** (*tīkṣṇa*, explained as *adhimātra*) — very strong in degree;
  occasional intensity alone does not constitute the obstruction described.
- **Action of *ānantarya*** — one of the five specified actions; the Bhāṣya
  explains the designation through two accounts of non-interruption.
- **Preparatory wholesome roots** (*āryamārga-prāyogika kuśalamūla*) — the
  wholesome roots preparatory to the noble Path, also named as obstructed.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let `P` be the noble Path and `R` the wholesome roots preparatory to it.

| Obstruction class | Specified condition | What it obstructs |
|---|---|---|
| action-obstruction | five actions of *ānantarya* | `P` and `R` |
| affliction-obstruction | recurrent affliction | `P` and `R` |
| maturation-obstruction | three bad destinies, Uttarakuru, non-percipient existence | `P` and `R` |

The mechanisms are not interchangeable:

```text
action-obstruction
    → maturation cannot be displaced under the first explanation

affliction-obstruction
    → recurrence removes the interval for counter-effort

maturation-obstruction
    → resultant conditions do not support Path-preparation
```

`Tīvra` and `tīkṣṇa` are not equivalent here:

```text
occasional, very intense affliction
    → may still leave time for counter-effort

recurrent affliction
    → no interval for effective effort
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The five actions, the recurrent-versus-intense distinction, the target of
obstruction, and the explanations of *ānantarya* are specified by the
Bhāṣya. The Bhāṣya-based expansion formerly embedded in the study translation is retained here: The five actions of *ānantarya* are action-obstruction. Recurrent affliction, not merely affliction of occasional extreme force, is affliction-obstruction. The three bad destinies, people of Uttarakuru, and non-percipient beings are maturation-obstruction. These conditions obstruct the noble Path and the wholesome roots preparatory to it.

The Bhāṣya's common criterion is practical: the obstructions concern the
noble Path and the wholesome roots that prepare for it. The three bad
destinies are not the whole of maturation-obstruction; Uttarakuru and
non-percipient existence are also included, showing that painfulness alone
does not define the class. The argument about affliction likewise turns on
recurrence and opportunity for counter-effort, not simply peak force.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> Access to the noble Path and its preparation can be closed through three
> distinct determinations: an action whose maturation cannot be displaced,
> an affliction whose recurrence leaves no interval for counter-effort, or a
> resultant mode of existence incompatible with Path-preparation. The
> criterion is not simply painfulness or intensity; each obstruction names
> a different way the practical conditions for the Path are unavailable.
