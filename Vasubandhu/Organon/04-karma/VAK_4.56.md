# VAK_4.56 — Exceptional Field: Saṅgha and Five Emergent States

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ये निरोधारणामैत्रीदर्शनार्हत्फलोत्थिताः ।
तेषु कारापकारस्य फलं सद्योऽनुभूयते ॥ ४.५६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
ye nirodhāraṇāmaitrīdarśanārhatphalotthitāḥ /
teṣu kārāpakārasya phalaṃ sadyo 'nubhūyate // 4.56 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
ye nirodha-araṇā-maitrī-darśana-arhat-phala-utthitāḥ /
teṣu kāra-apakārasya phalam sadyaḥ anubhūyate //
```

The repeated force of `utthita`, “emerged,” applies to all five states. The
Bhāṣya also names the Bhikṣu Saṅgha headed by the Buddha as an exceptional
field before discussing these five individual cases.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `ye` | nominative masculine plural relative pronoun | those who |
| `nirodha` | compound member | cessation attainment |
| `araṇā` | compound member | non-contention or non-conflict attainment |
| `maitrī` | compound member | loving-kindness |
| `darśana` | compound member | seeing; here the Path of Seeing |
| `arhatphala` | compound member | fruit of arhatship |
| `utthitāḥ` | nominative masculine plural past participle | emerged; arisen from |
| `teṣu` | locative masculine plural correlative pronoun | toward or in relation to them |
| `kāra` | compound member | benefit; service |
| `apakārasya` | genitive masculine singular | of injury or harm |
| `phalam` | nominative neuter singular | fruit; result |
| `sadyaḥ` | adverb | immediately; at once |
| `anubhūyate` | third-person singular passive | is experienced |

**`utthita / vyutthita`**

The verse does not merely name persons who possess an attainment. It concerns
persons just emerged from a powerful attainment or path-event.

**`kāra / apakāra`**

Benefit and injury express opposite relations to the same exceptional field.
The Bhāṣya says that the result of either is certain within the present life;
it does not specify an exact interval after the act.

**`sadyaḥ`**

The verse says “at once”; the Bhāṣya's concluding prose specifies that the
result is certainly obtained in the present life, without establishing an
exact interval after the act.

## 4. Grammar

**The five relative members**

```text
ye
nirodhāt utthitāḥ
araṇāyāḥ utthitāḥ
maitryāḥ utthitāḥ
darśanāt utthitāḥ
arhat-phalāt utthitāḥ
```

> Those who have emerged from cessation, non-contention, loving-kindness, the
> Path of Seeing, or the fruit of arhatship.

The compressed compound distributes the participle across five ablative
relations.

**Benefit and injury toward them**

```text
teṣu
kāra-apakārasya
phalam
sadyaḥ anubhūyate
```

> The fruit of benefit or injury directed toward them is experienced
> immediately.

The locative `teṣu` expresses the exceptional recipient-field in relation to
which the action is performed.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> For those emerged from cessation, non-contention, loving-kindness, the Path
> of Seeing, or the fruit of arhatship, the result of benefit or injury done
> toward them is experienced immediately.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> For those emerging from cessation, non-contention, loving-kindness, the Path
> of Seeing, or the fruit of arhatship, the result of benefit or harm is
> experienced at once.
## 6. Philosophical Translation

> For those emerged from cessation, non-contention, loving-kindness, the Path of Seeing, or the fruit of arhatship, the result of benefit or injury done toward them is experienced immediately.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Emerged from cessation**

The person emerging from `nirodhasamāpatti` possesses exceptional peace of
Consciousness because the attainment resembles nirvāṇa. The Bhāṣya strikingly compares
the person to one who has gone to nirvāṇa and returned.

**Emerged from non-contention**

The continuum is pervaded by an elevated orientation toward preventing
conflict among immeasurable beings and by immeasurable cultivation of merit.

**Emerged from loving-kindness**

The continuum is pervaded by an elevated intention for the happiness of
immeasurable beings and by immeasurable cultivation of merit.

**Emerged from the Path of Seeing**

All afflictions abandoned by seeing have just been abandoned. The Bhāṣya calls
the continuum freshly stainless through transformation of its basis.

**Emerged from arhat-fruition**

All factors abandoned by cultivation have just been abandoned. The continuum
is freshly purified through a more complete transformation of its basis.

**`āśrayaparivṛtti`**

Here this means transformation or alteration of the supporting psychophysical
continuum. The expression should not be loaded automatically with every later
Yogācāra determination of “transformation of the basis.”

**Negative boundary**

Persons emerging from the remaining incomplete stages of the Path of
Cultivation are not assigned the same field-power because their fruit-nature
and fresh purification are not complete in the required way.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The recipient's emergent condition determines the exceptional field**

```text
ATTAINMENT_OR_PATH_EVENT(recipient)
    → CONDITION_OF_CONTINUUM_AFTER_EMERGENCE(recipient)
    → EXCEPTIONAL_FIELD(recipient)
```

The Bhāṣya specifies peace of Consciousness after cessation, intentions and
merit after `araṇā` and loving-kindness, and fresh Basis transformation after
the Path of Seeing and arhat fruit. It does not assign Basis transformation
to all five cases.

Then:

```text
ACTION(agent, recipient)
    + EXCEPTIONAL_FIELD(recipient)
    → RESULT_CERTAIN_IN_PRESENT_LIFE(action)
```

**Status alone is insufficient to explain the rule**

```text
abstract rank
    ≠ complete field-determination

freshly actualized continuum-state
    → exceptional field-power
```

The timing relative to the attainment is doctrinally significant.

**Benefit and injury retain their distinction**

```text
benefit toward exceptional field
    → result certain in present life

injury toward exceptional field
    → result certain in present life
```

The source establishes present-life certainty for both; it does not specify an
exact interval or collapse their ethical difference.

**Continuation of sufficient-ground analysis**

```text
recipient's specified emergent condition
    is sufficient ground
    for a result certain in the present life
```

This specifies one of the field-based grounds introduced in VAK 4.54–4.55.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The emphasis on freshness, the state of each continuum, and
the present-life necessity of the result are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The Bhāṣya also
includes the community of monks headed by the Buddha as a field, then characterizes
the five recipients as freshly peaceful, benevolently oriented, or purified. Their
benefit or injury certainly yields a result in this life; the verse says “at once”
(`sadyaḥ`), while the prose does not fix an exact interval.

**The recipient is an active moment of Karma as Ground**

```text
agent
    + action
    + recipient in a determinate state
    → complete karmic relation
```

The field is not an inert Object receiving an already complete act. Its
determination participates in grounding the causal force and timing of the
result.

**The emergent condition has relational force**

The Bhāṣya distinguishes the peaceful, benevolently oriented, and purified
continua. For the two path cases, it expressly attributes the purified
continuum to fresh Basis transformation. Action toward these recipients
encounters the condition attained in each case.

**Dharmapāda contact**

The sufficient Ground is now explicitly relational:

```text
subjective orientation of agent
    is not the whole ground

objective determination of recipient-field
    enters the ground itself
```

This supports the Dharmapāda architecture without making the primary
translation Hegelian.

**Doctrinal limit**

The five cases and their explanations belong to Vasubandhu's karmic system.
Their broader logical form can be retained without turning every encounter
with a spiritually accomplished person into the technical rule stated here.

This is the Organon contact. The five fields, their emergent conditions, and
the negative boundary belong to the Bhāṣya.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> The causal force of an action depends partly on the state of its recipient
> field. A continuum emerged from cessation, immeasurable benevolence, seeing,
> or arhat-fruition is not treated as interchangeable with one in another
> state. The Bhāṣya classifies benefit or injury directed toward these
> recipients as certain to yield a result in the present life, without
> assigning an exact clock-time interval. Field-state therefore participates
> in the account of karmic efficacy.

The rule applies to both benefit and injury without erasing their ethical
distinction.
