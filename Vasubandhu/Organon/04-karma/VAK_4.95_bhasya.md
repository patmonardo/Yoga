# VAK 4.95 Bhāṣya — Projecting and Completing a Birth

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ekaṃ janmākṣipaty ekam anekaṃ paripūrakam /
nākṣepike samāpattī acitte prāptayo na ca // 4.95 //
```

> One action projects one birth; what completes it is multiple. The two
> non-mental attainments are not projectors, nor are acquisitions in a
> non-mental state.

The verse distinguishes the action that determines the unified membership of
a birth from the many actions that fill out that birth's particular features.
The Bhāṣya then tests the one-to-one formula against remembered multiple
rebirths and against a coordinated plurality of intentions.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Does one action project only one birth, or does it not? And does one birth
have only one action projecting it, or many? The established position is as
follows: one action projects one birth. One action projects only one birth,
not many births. “Birth” here is the designation of membership in a class of
beings (`nikāyasabhāga`), for when that membership is acquired one is said to
have been born.

But the elder Aniruddha said: “Through the maturation of that single offering
of alms, I was reborn seven times among the Thirty-three gods, down to my
present birth in a wealthy Śākya family.” After obtaining prosperity and
remembering his former births, however, he performed further merit. He was
indicating the point from which that series arose. It is like a person who,
starting with one dinar, earns a thousand and says, “Through one dinar I
obtained this wealth.”

Others say that, taking that offering as a basis, a great stream of intentions
to give arose in him, and that each intention appropriated some particular
result.

Even multiple actions can project one birth, lest membership in the class of
beings be projected piecemeal. The following compressed sentence associates
one action with the afflicted membership of the class of beings; its exact
force is difficult in the running witness.

What completes a birth is multiple. Just as a painter sketches a figure with
one drawing implement and completes it with many, so among equally human
beings one person possesses all faculties, major limbs, and minor parts and
shines with excellence of color, shape, stature, and strength, while another
is deficient in one or another of these.

Nor is action alone a projector of birth. Other factors possessing maturation
can also project it. In every case, however, the two non-mental attainments
are not projectors. Although they are non-mental, they do not project class
membership because they have no maturation and do not coexist with action.
Nor do acquisitions project it, because that would make one action have
multiple birth-results.

## 4. What `Janma` Means Here

The Bhāṣya specifies that “birth” means `nikāyasabhāga`: membership in a
determinate class of beings. The projected unity is therefore not every
empirical detail of a life. It is the common membership through which this
continuum counts as having been born in that class.

```text
projected:
    unified class membership of one birth

completed:
    faculties, limbs, appearance, stature, strength,
    and other particular determinations
```

This distinction explains how one birth can be unified without every feature
being attributed to one karmic cause.

## 5. One Action and Multiple Births

The kārikā's primary restriction is:

```text
one projecting action → one birth
```

Aniruddha's statement appears to violate it by deriving seven divine births
and a wealthy human birth from one offering. The first answer treats the
offering as the initial capital of a longer causal history. Former-birth
memory and prosperity enabled further meritorious action; the elder names the
origin of the series, just as accumulated wealth can be traced to one initial
coin.

The second answer analyzes the “single offering” into a stream of giving
intentions. The visible act supplies one basis, while multiple volitional
moments appropriate distinct results.

Both answers preserve a finer causal multiplicity beneath ordinary singular
speech.

## 6. Can Several Actions Project One Birth?

The prose explicitly allows a plurality to project one birth so that
`nikāyasabhāga` is not projected in fragments. This must be retained beside
the one-action/one-birth formula:

```text
one action does not project several births

but

several coordinated actions may project one undivided birth membership
```

The relation is therefore many-to-one in one direction, while excluding
one-to-many projection in the other. The unity belongs to the projected class
membership, not necessarily to the numerical count of cooperating causal
moments.

The damaged sentence immediately following may qualify this for afflicted
class membership, but the running witness does not justify forcing a more
precise resolution.

## 7. Projecting and Completing Causes

The painter analogy gives the formal distinction:

```text
initial outline    → projection of the unified figure
many later strokes → completion of its determinate features
```

Likewise:

```text
ākṣepaka   → establishes the birth's unified class trajectory
paripūraka → supplies its particular endowments and deficiencies
```

Projection and completion are causal roles. The same general kind of karmic
factor may stand in one role in one relation and another elsewhere; the verse
does not posit two independent substances called projector and completer.

## 8. What Cannot Project

The two non-mental attainments are the attainment of non-perception and the
attainment of cessation. They cannot project birth because they lack
maturation and do not arise together with action in the required manner.

`Prāpti`, acquisition or endowment, also must not be confused with projection.
Marking that a continuum possesses an action or dharma does not itself perform
the action's birth-projecting function.

This continues an important distinction from 4.82–4.84:

```text
endowed with a causal determination
    ≠
currently executing its causal operation
```

## 9. Organon Contact Point

For the Causal Algebra for Agency, 4.95 introduces two typed projection roles:

```text
ProjectedInstance {
    classMembership
    projectingCauses[]
    completingCauses[]
    completedFeatures[]
}
```

The cardinality constraints are asymmetric:

```text
one projector does not project many instance-memberships
many coordinated projectors may establish one membership
many completing causes may fill one projected instance
```

This gives the Root Agent a clean world-producing discipline. Projection
creates the unified instance identity. Completion populates its properties.
A possession record or dormant attainment cannot be mistaken for an executing
projector.

The instance terminology is the Organon reconstruction. Rebirth,
`nikāyasabhāga`, the attainments, and the disputed causal accounts belong to
the Bhāṣya.
