# VAK_4.89 — Completion of the Action-quality Matrix

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अशुभस्य शुभाद्या द्वे त्रीणि चत्वार्यनुक्रमम् ।
अव्याकृतस्य द्वे त्रीणि त्रीणि चैते शुभादयः ॥ ४.८९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
aśubhasya śubhādyā dve trīṇi catvāry anukramam /
avyākṛtasya dve trīṇi trīṇi caite śubhādayaḥ // 4.89 //
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
aśubhasya | śubha-ādyāḥ | dve | trīṇi | catvāri | anukramam |
avyākṛtasya | dve | trīṇi | trīṇi | ca | ete | śubha-ādayaḥ
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `aśubhasya` | genitive singular | of unwholesome action |
| `śubha-ādyāḥ` | nominative plural compound | resulting dharmas beginning with wholesome |
| `dve` | nominative neuter dual | two results |
| `trīṇi` | nominative neuter plural | three results |
| `catvāri` | nominative neuter plural | four results |
| `anukramam` | indeclinable | in sequence, respectively |
| `avyākṛtasya` | genitive singular | of indeterminate action |
| `ca` | coordinating particle | and, also |
| `ete` | nominative plural demonstrative | these same classes just specified |
| `śubha-ādayaḥ` | nominative plural compound | wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate resulting dharmas |

**The inherited column order**

Both rows use the same result-quality sequence established in VAK 4.88:

```text
1. wholesome resulting dharmas
2. unwholesome resulting dharmas
3. indeterminate resulting dharmas
```

Thus:

```text
unwholesome action   → 2, 3, 4
indeterminate action → 2, 3, 3
```

The numbers count admissible technical result-relations, not distinct events
and not quantities of merit or demerit.

## 4. Grammar

**Unwholesome causal action**

```text
aśubhasya [karmaṇaḥ]
    śubhādyāḥ [dharmāḥ phalāni]
    dve trīṇi catvāri
    anukramam
```

> For unwholesome action, dharmas beginning with the wholesome are results
> under two, three, and four relations, respectively.

`Anukramam` fixes the distribution and prevents the numerals from being read
as an unstructured aggregate.

**Indeterminate causal action**

```text
avyākṛtasya [karmaṇaḥ]
    ete śubhādayaḥ [dharmāḥ phalāni]
    dve trīṇi trīṇi [anukramam]
```

> For indeterminate action, these same classes beginning with the wholesome
> are results under two, three, and three relations.

The demonstrative `ete` carries forward both the three result-quality classes
and their order. The distributive force of `anukramam` continues by ellipsis.

## 5. Translation

### Scientific English Rendering

> For unwholesome action, the results for dharmas beginning with wholesome
> number two, three, and four, respectively. For indeterminate action, these
> same result-quality classes number two, three, and three.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Wholesome dharmas stand to unwholesome action through activity-produced
> and dominant results; unwholesome dharmas through corresponding,
> activity-produced, and dominant results; and indeterminate dharmas through
> maturation, corresponding, activity-produced, and dominant results.
> Wholesome dharmas stand to indeterminate action through activity-produced
> and dominant results; both unwholesome and indeterminate dharmas stand to
> it through corresponding, activity-produced, and dominant results.

The exact relation-memberships and the cross-quality examples involving
personality-view and extreme-view are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Causality does not proceed by copying the ethical quality of an action into
> every result. Unwholesome and indeterminate actions can stand in determinate
> relations to dharmas of every ethical quality. What joins source and result
> is not bare qualitative identity but a specific result-relation: maturation,
> correspondence, activity-production, or dominant conditioning. The complete
> matrix develops the causal structure of an isolated Course of Action.

This completion also reveals the higher station of the Path:

```text
Course of Action
    acts within the causal matrix
    and ordinarily reproduces its conditioned relations

Path as Course of Action
    comprehends the causal matrix practically
    and makes disconnection itself the result of action
```

The Path is not externally opposed to action. It is action raised to reflexive
universality: a Course of Action capable of acting upon the causal form of
Courses of Action. It is therefore the truth of a Course of Action, not
merely one more item beside it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Aśubha` or `akuśala` — unwholesome**

The ethical quality of the causal action. It constrains but does not simply
duplicate the quality of every resulting dharma. An unwholesome cause can
stand to a wholesome dharma through activity-produced or dominant result.

**`Avyākṛta` — ethically indeterminate**

Neither wholesome nor unwholesome. Indeterminacy is limited to ethical status;
the dharma remains precisely determined by causal source, result-type,
function, temporal station, and doctrinal relation.

**`Anukramam` — respectively**

The grammatical operator that binds each count to its ordered result-quality
column. Without this order the verse's compressed causal matrix would be
unrecoverable.

**`Satkāya-dṛṣṭi` — personality-view**

One of two indeterminate views invoked by the Bhāṣya to establish
cross-quality correspondence. It can be a corresponding result of unwholesome
universally pervasive dharmas and can itself stand as corresponding source for
unwholesome dharmas abandoned by seeing the truths.

**`Antagrāha-dṛṣṭi` — extreme-view**

The second indeterminate view in the same reciprocal explanatory pattern. Its
ethical indeterminacy does not prevent a determinate causal continuity with
unwholesome afflictive dharmas.

**`Sarvatraga` — universally pervasive**

An unwholesome causal class whose operation extends across the relevant field.
The Bhāṣya uses it to explain how indeterminate views can arise as
corresponding results of unwholesome dharmas.

**`Duḥkha-darśana-heya` — abandoned by seeing suffering**

A class of afflictive dharmas abandoned through insight into suffering. It
helps specify the causal lineage in which the two indeterminate views and
unwholesome resulting dharmas can be related through `niṣyanda`.

**`Niṣyanda-phala` — corresponding result**

Requires a doctrinal continuity or special similarity, but not universal
identity of ethical quality. The neutral views and unwholesome afflictions
show that correspondence can cross the wholesome/unwholesome/indeterminate
partition where the causal lineage warrants it.

**`Vipāka-phala` — maturation result**

Appears in this verse only where unwholesome action relates to indeterminate
resulting dharmas. Maturation is ethically indeterminate even though its cause
is ethically unwholesome.

**`Puruṣakāra-phala` and `adhipati-phala`**

Activity-produced and dominant results are the two universally distributed
relations in the completed quality-matrix. Every source-quality can relate to
every result-quality through both.

**`Visaṃyoga-phala` — disconnection-result**

Absent from every cell treated in this verse. Across the completed matrix it
appears only where wholesome action relates to wholesome liberative dharmas.
This restriction anticipates the higher functional station of Path.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Rows completed by this verse**

Let:

```text
V = vipāka
N = niṣyanda
D = visaṃyoga
P = puruṣakāra
A = adhipati
```

Then:

```text
ResultRelations(UnwholesomeAction, WholesomeDharmas)
    = {P, A}

ResultRelations(UnwholesomeAction, UnwholesomeDharmas)
    = {N, P, A}

ResultRelations(UnwholesomeAction, IndeterminateDharmas)
    = {V, N, P, A}

ResultRelations(IndeterminateAction, WholesomeDharmas)
    = {P, A}

ResultRelations(IndeterminateAction, UnwholesomeDharmas)
    = {N, P, A}

ResultRelations(IndeterminateAction, IndeterminateDharmas)
    = {N, P, A}
```

**Completed VAK 4.88–4.89 matrix**

| Causal action | Wholesome resulting dharmas | Unwholesome resulting dharmas | Indeterminate resulting dharmas |
|---|---|---|---|
| wholesome | `N, D, P, A` | `P, A` | `V, P, A` |
| unwholesome | `P, A` | `N, P, A` | `V, N, P, A` |
| indeterminate | `P, A` | `N, P, A` | `N, P, A` |

The visible numerical table is only a projection:

| Causal action | Wholesome | Unwholesome | Indeterminate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| wholesome | 4 | 2 | 3 |
| unwholesome | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| indeterminate | 2 | 3 | 3 |

**General laws of the matrix**

```text
P and A
    occur in every cell

N
    occurs where causal continuity or special similarity is admitted

V
    occurs only with indeterminate resultant dharmas here

D
    occurs only in the wholesome-to-wholesome liberative cell
```

Therefore:

```text
qualitative identity            ≠ universal principle of causation
ethical difference              ≠ absence of causal relation
same numerical count            ≠ same relation-set
action-quality plus resulting-dharma quality
                                ≠ adequate without result-type
```

**Path as reflexive Course of Action**

The preceding analysis of the uninterrupted Path in VAK 4.87 can now be situated
within the completed matrix:

```text
ordinary course
    → produces determinate results within conditioned causality

wholesome liberative course
    → includes visaṃyoga as its result

Path
    → organizes courses so that action acts upon
      the conjunction between affliction and continuum
```

The Path is higher-order not because it ceases to be operative action, but
because its operation has the causal system itself as its practical content.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.89 completes one of the most powerful pieces of causal analysis in the
action chapter. The two verses encode a ternary relation: action quality,
result-type, and resulting-dharma quality. A dictionary can define each
result, but
only the completed system reveals what each means. `Vipāka`, `niṣyanda`,
`visaṃyoga`, `puruṣakāra`, and `adhipati` are differentiated ways in which one
determination becomes actual through another.

The cross-quality `niṣyanda` cases are especially important. Personality-view
and extreme-view are ethically indeterminate, yet they can continue from
unwholesome pervasive afflictions and can generate unwholesome afflictions
through correspondence. Similarity therefore means continuity of determinate
causal form, not identity of an abstract ethical predicate.

The matrix also explains the systematic elevation from action to Path. A
Course of Action is already an organized practical determination whose
operation propagates through agent, continuum, and world. But ordinary courses
remain immersed in the causal matrix they enact. The Path is the course in
which this system becomes practically reflexive. It does not merely substitute
wholesome contents for unwholesome ones; it introduces `visaṃyoga`, the
operation that transforms the bond through which affliction reproduces itself.

Thus the Path is the truth of Courses of Action. “Truth” here does not
mean a separate proposition about action. It means that the implicit Concept of
course becomes explicit: action comprehends its conditions, orders its means,
and produces freedom as a determinate result. The higher concept preserves
body, speech, consciousness, cultivation, and causal efficacy, but reorganizes them
under the telos of disconnection.

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy makes this ascent intelligible. Practical
activity first appears conditioned by an objective causal world; then the
activity discovers that this world is also its own production. At the level
of Path, action returns upon that production and determines the conditions
under which it acts. Action becomes self-comprehending practice. That is why
the Path is not an external remedy appended after the analysis of action: it
is the immanent truth toward which the concept of Course of Action has been
moving.
