# VAK_4.46 — Immovable Karma and the Level of Maturation

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कामधातौ शुभं कर्म पुण्यमानेञ्जमूर्ध्वजम् ।
तद्भूमिषु यतः कर्मविपाकं प्रति नेञ्जति ॥ ४.४६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
kāmadhātau śubhaṃ karma puṇyam āneñjam ūrdhvajam /
tadbhūmiṣu yataḥ karmavipākaṃ prati neñjati // 4.46 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
kāma-dhātau śubham karma puṇyam āneñjam ūrdhva-jam /
tad-bhūmiṣu yataḥ karma-vipākam prati na iñjati //
```

The Bhāṣya carries `śubham` forward into the second determination: `āneñja`
is wholesome karma belonging to the Form and Formless Realms.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `kāmadhātau` | locative masculine singular compound | in the Desire Realm |
| `śubham` | nominative neuter singular | wholesome; auspicious |
| `karma` | nominative neuter singular | karma; action |
| `puṇyam` | nominative neuter singular | meritorious |
| `āneñjam` | nominative neuter singular | immovable; non-shifting |
| `ūrdhvajam` | nominative neuter singular compound | produced above; born in the higher realms |
| `tadbhūmiṣu` | locative feminine plural compound | in those levels |
| `yataḥ` | causal relative adverb | because; since |
| `karmavipākam` | accusative masculine singular compound | karmic maturation |
| `prati` | postposition governing accusative | with respect to; regarding |
| `na iñjati` | third-person singular present with negation | does not move or shift |

**`dhātu` and `bhūmi`**

The verse uses both terms and does not make them interchangeable:

```text
dhātu
    realm of karmic existence and operation in this compound

bhūmi
    determinate level or station within the ordered field
```

The Desire Realm contains multiple destinies and classes of beings. Higher
karma is called immovable because its maturation does not shift from its
corresponding `bhūmi`, not merely because it remains somewhere in a broadly
named realm. Here, the conventional rendering “Realm” does not replace the
Organon rendering Dhātu as Principle.

**`āneñja`**

The term derives from the negative of movement expressed by `iñjati`. Its
meaning is relational here: non-shifting with respect to the level of
maturation, not absolutely motionless or unconditioned.

**`ūrdhvaja`**

“Born above” is explained by the Bhāṣya as wholesome karma pertaining to the
Form and Formless Realms.

## 4. Grammar

**Meritorious karma**

```text
kāma-dhātau
śubham karma
puṇyam [bhavati]
```

> Wholesome karma in the Desire Realm is meritorious.

The locative supplies the realm in which the wholesome karma is classified
as `puṇya`.

**Immovable karma**

```text
[śubham karma]
ūrdhva-jam
āneñjam [bhavati]
```

> Wholesome karma produced above is immovable.

The Bhāṣya explicitly repeats the force of `śubham`. Thus `ūrdhvajam` should
not be generalized to every event occurring in a higher realm.

**The causal definition**

```text
yataḥ karma
tad-bhūmiṣu
vipākam prati
na iñjati
```

> Because karma does not shift from those levels with respect to maturation.

`Vipākam prati` specifies the respect under which non-movement is predicated.
`Tadbhūmiṣu` gives the corresponding levels to which the maturation is fixed.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Wholesome karma in the Desire Realm is meritorious; wholesome karma
> produced above is immovable, because with respect to maturation karma does
> not shift from those levels.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Wholesome karma in the Desire Realm is meritorious; wholesome karma born above
> is immovable, because it does not shift from its level with respect to
> maturation.
## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Wholesome karma in the Desire Realm is meritorious; wholesome karma produced above is immovable, because with respect to maturation karma does not shift from those levels.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The completed triad**

```text
puṇya
    wholesome karma of the Desire Realm

apuṇya
    unwholesome karma

āneñja
    wholesome karma of the Form and Formless Realms
    with maturation fixed by level
```

The classifications combine ethical quality with realm and maturation.

**The objection from moved dhyānas**

The Bhāṣya raises an apparent contradiction. The Buddha has described the
first three dhyānas as `señjita`, moved or perturbed, because determinations
such as `vitarka` and `vicāra` remain movements within samādhi. How, then, can
karma connected with those dhyānas be called immovable?

**Different respects of predication**

```text
dhyāna
    moved with respect to perturbation within samādhi

higher-realm karma
    immovable with respect to maturation-level
```

The predicates differ because their specified relations differ.

**`avyavasthāna`**

The Bhāṣya calls Desire-Realm maturation non-fixed. Karma associated with one
destiny may mature in another, and karma associated with one divine class may
mature in another class.

```text
Desire-Realm karma
    + the power of other conditions
    → alternative admissible maturation-locus
```

This is structured mobility, not arbitrary causation.

**`vyavasthita-vipākatva`**

The positive definition of `āneñja` is “having fixed maturation.” Karma of one
higher `bhūmi` cannot mature in a different `bhūmi`.

```text
higher karma generated for level X
    → maturation at level X
    → never maturation at level Y
```

## 8. Logical Determination

**Dhātu supplies the field; bhūmi fixes the station**

```text
kāmadhātu
    contains multiple gatis and divine classes
    → maturation may shift among admissible loci

rūpa / ārūpya fields
    differentiated into bhūmis
    → higher karma is fixed to its corresponding bhūmi
```

This prevents `dhātu` from being flattened into a place-name. It is an ordered
field of possible karmic operation; `bhūmi` expresses a more determinate
station within that order.

**Relational invariance**

```text
ORIGIN_LEVEL(K) = X
    → MATURATION_LEVEL(K) = X
```

For higher karma, the mapping is invariant. `Āneñja` names this law of the
relation, not an absence of activity.

**Conditional mobility in the Desire Realm**

```text
ORIGIN_SPECIFICATION(K) = X
    + OTHER_CONDITIONS
    → MATURATION_LOCUS(K) may be Y
```

The Bhāṣya's examples remain within the Desire-Realm field: gods, humans,
animals, and pretas. The movement does not imply maturation in any realm
whatsoever.

**No contradiction across different respects**

```text
MOVED(x, samādhi-constitution)
    and
IMMOVABLE(x, maturation-relation)
```

Contrary predicates generate no contradiction when their relational respects
are different and explicitly controlled.

**Dhātu theory in application**

The verse confirms three layers:

```text
ethical determination
    wholesome / unwholesome

realm determination
    desire / form / formless

level-indexed maturation
    mobile within an admissible field
    or fixed to a corresponding bhūmi
```

The complete account therefore requires both realm and internal
differentiation. Dhātu gives the systematic field; bhūmi gives the indexed
position required by this karmic law.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The extension of `śubha` to the higher-realm clause, the
identification of the higher realms, the exact fixity of maturation, and the closing
statement about `apuṇya` are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The Bhāṣya carries “wholesome”
into the higher-realm clause and identifies the Form and Formless Realms. It
contrasts movable Desire-Realm maturation with the higher karma's fixed maturation
placement; the ordinary designation “demeritorious” for unwholesome karma is also
commentary.

**The first application confirms and refines the Principle**

VAK 4.46 does not use the three dhātus as containers into which already formed
karma is dropped. The field of genesis enters the determination of what the
karma is:

```text
wholesome + Desire-Realm
    → puṇya

wholesome + higher-realm + fixed bhūmi-maturation
    → āneñja
```

Realm is therefore internal to the karmic classification.

**Necessity as lawful activity**

Immovability does not require the cessation of movement. The dhyānas may be
moved with respect to their internal constitution while their karmic result
remains fixed with respect to level. Necessity belongs to the law connecting
the terms:

```text
activity
    → determinate production
    → invariant maturation relation
```

This is stronger than treating stability as a static property of a substance.

**Conditions and admissible variation**

The Bhāṣya does not oppose law to conditioning. In the Desire Realm, other
conditions can redirect the precise locus of maturation. The field determines
the admissible range within which conditional variation operates.

That is important for our developing Principle framework:

```text
Dhātu
    is neither an external container
    nor one isolated causal factor

Dhātu
    is the differentiated field
    within which determinate causal transformations are possible
```

The new karma block therefore supports the Principle while showing that the
complete picture also requires `bhūmi`, the internal determination of level.

This is the Organon contact. The objection, scriptural comparison, examples of
mobile maturation, and definition through fixed maturation belong to the
Bhāṣya; the explicit Principle framework is project interpretation.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> Karma is determined not only by ethical quality but by the relation between
> its realm of production and its level of maturation. Desire-Realm karma is
> comparatively mobile: intervening conditions can alter the destiny or class
> in which its result appears. Higher wholesome karma is immovable because
> its originating level and maturation-level stand in an invariant relation.
> This is not inert permanence, but fixity within a specified causal relation.

This is a determination of relative invariance. The Bhāṣya's “moved”
predicate concerns disturbance within samādhi; “immovable” concerns the
level at which karma matures. Since the predicates concern different
determinations, they do not contradict one another.
