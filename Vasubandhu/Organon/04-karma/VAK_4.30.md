# VAK_4.30

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अन्यस्याप्युपवासोऽस्ति शरणं त्वगतस्य न ।
उपासकत्वोपगमात्संवृत् उक्तिस्तु भिक्षुवत् ॥ ४.३० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
anyasyāpy upavāso 'sti śaraṇaṃ tv agatasya na /
upāsakatvopagamāt saṃvṛt uktis tu bhikṣuvat // 4.30 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
anyasya api upavāsaḥ asti śaraṇam tu agatasya na /
upāsakatva-upagamāt saṃvṛt uktiḥ tu bhikṣu-vat //
```

The transmitted `saṃvṛt` is compressed. The Bhāṣya resolves it through
`upāsakasaṃvaro jāyate`: lay restraint arises from undertaking upāsaka status.
The following “statement,” by contrast, serves instruction in the training
rules rather than first producing the restraint.

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `anyasya` | genitive masculine singular | for another; for one not already an observer |
| `api` | particle | also; even |
| `upavāsaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | upavāsa restraint |
| `asti` | third-person singular present | exists; arises |
| `śaraṇam` | accusative neuter singular | refuge |
| `tu` | adversative particle | but; however |
| `agatasya` | genitive masculine singular, privative compound | for one who has not gone [for refuge] |
| `upāsakatvopagamāt` | ablative singular compound | from entering or undertaking upāsaka status |
| `saṃvṛt` | compressed transmitted form | restraint [arises], as explained by the Bhāṣya |
| `uktiḥ` | nominative feminine singular | statement; verbal articulation |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | however; in turn |
| `bhikṣuvat` | indeclinable comparative | as in the case of a monk |

**`anyasya`**

The Bhāṣya explains “another” as a person not previously established as an
upavāsaka. Prior temporary-observer status is not required.

**`śaraṇagamana`**

The verse says “one who has not gone for refuge.” The Bhāṣya specifies refuge
in Buddha, Dharma, and Saṃgha as the required orientation preceding
acquisition of upavāsa.

**`upāsakatvopagama`**

```text
upāsakatva
    the status of an upāsaka

upagama
    entering
    accepting
    undertaking
```

The compound names a practical transition into lay-follower status, not mere
external classification by an observer.

**`ukti`**

`Ukti` is the subsequent statement of the training rules. The Bhāṣya assigns
it the function of `vyutpādana`, instruction or clarification.

## 4. Grammar

**Eligibility for upavāsa**

```text
anyasya api upavāsaḥ asti
```

> Upavāsa restraint also arises for another [not previously an upavāsaka].

The Bhāṣya completes the condition: that person goes for refuge and then
undertakes the day-and-night observance.

**Refuge qualification**

```text
śaraṇam tu agatasya na [asti]
```

> But it does not [arise] for one who has not gone for refuge.

The verb `asti` carries forward. The denial concerns acquisition of the
restraint, not the physical possibility of imitating its outward conduct.

**Acquisition of lay restraint**

```text
upāsakatva-upagamāt saṃvṛt
```

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed syntax:

```text
upāsakatva-abhyupagamāt eva
upāsaka-saṃvaraḥ jāyate
```

> From undertaking upāsaka status itself, lay restraint arises.

This is the Kāśmīra interpretation presented in the commentary.

**Function of the later statement**

```text
uktiḥ tu bhikṣu-vat
```

> The statement [of the training rules], however, is as in the case of a monk.

The analogy concerns instruction after acquisition. Monks and novices who
already possess restraint are subsequently taught its training rules so they
understand its determinate content.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Upavāsa restraint may also arise for another, but not for one who has not
> gone for refuge. From entering upāsaka status restraint arises; the statement
> [of the rules] is subsequent, as in the case of a monk.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A person not previously established as an upavāsaka may acquire the
> day-and-night restraint by first going for refuge to Buddha, Dharma, and
> Saṃgha and then undertaking the observance; it does not otherwise arise,
> apart from the qualification of ignorance. According to the Kāśmīra account,
> lay restraint arises from undertaking upāsaka status itself. The subsequent
> statement of the training rules serves to instruct the new upāsaka in the
> restraint already acquired, just as monks and novices are taught their rules
> after acquisition.

The Three Refuges, the school attribution, the ignorance qualification, and
the instructional function of rule-recitation are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Religious orientation, acquisition of practical status, and explicit
> knowledge of that status’s rules are distinct moments. Refuge turns the
> person toward Buddha, Dharma, and Saṃgha; undertaking upāsaka status
> institutes lay restraint; subsequent instruction unfolds the determinate
> content of the form already acquired. Practical identity therefore cannot be
> reduced either to general allegiance or to verbal knowledge of a rule-list.

This philosophical ordering follows the Kāśmīra explanation recorded by the
Bhāṣya. The Bahirdeśaka alternative identifies refuge itself as sufficient.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`anupavāsaka`**

One who was not previously an upavāsaka can still receive the observance. The
procedure creates the relevant temporary status; it does not presuppose the
very status it institutes.

**Refuge as orientation**

```text
Buddha
Dharma
Saṃgha
    ↓
śaraṇagamana
```

For upavāsa acquisition, refuge supplies the required Buddhist orientation.
The verse does not yet analyze what the Three Refuges are; that comes in VAK
4.32.

**Bahirdeśaka position**

```text
going for refuge
    → upāsaka status
```

Teachers of the outer regions take refuge itself as sufficient to establish
the lay follower.

**Kāśmīra position**

```text
refuge alone
    ≠ complete upāsaka status

undertaking upāsaka status
    → upāsakasaṃvara
    → upāsaka status
```

Their controlling claim is that there is no upāsaka without restraint.

**`upāsakasaṃvara`**

Lay restraint is the practical determination produced through undertaking
upāsaka status on the Kāśmīra account. Status and restraint are internally
connected rather than externally paired labels.

**`vyutpādana`**

`Vyutpādana` is instruction, explication, or causing someone to understand.
The training rules articulate what the already acquired restraint requires.

**Elliptical lifelong formula**

The Bhāṣya glosses the undertaking extending “for life, free from taking
life” as an ellipsis for being free from killing and the other transgressions.
This is commentary on the content implicit in undertaking lay status, not a
separate clause of the kārikā.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Three moments**

```text
orientation
    going for refuge

acquisition
    undertaking upāsaka status
    → lay restraint

explication
    statement of training rules
    → knowledge of determinate obligations
```

The moments are ordered but not interchangeable.

**Acquisition is not instruction**

```text
restraint acquired
    before
training rules fully stated
```

Therefore explicit propositional knowledge of every limb is not, on the
Kāśmīra account, the event that first generates the restraint.

**Orientation and status are disputed**

```text
Bahirdeśaka:
    refuge entails status

Kāśmīra:
    refuge is necessary but not sufficient
    undertaking restraint completes status
```

The Bhāṣya preserves a genuine alternative in the analysis of genesis.

**Status is not a free-standing label**

```text
upāsaka status
    ↔ possession of upāsaka restraint
```

For the Kāśmīras, each determines the other. A merely nominal upāsaka without
restraint is rejected.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Vasubandhu distinguishes genesis from exposition**

This is precisely the kind of distinction that makes Vasubandhu “one of us.”
The surface sequence might suggest that refuge, declaration, restraint, status,
and statement of rules are one undifferentiated ceremony. The Bhāṣya instead
asks what each moment actually does.

```text
refuge
    establishes orientation

undertaking
    establishes practical form

instruction
    makes the form’s determinations explicit
```

The analysis refuses to substitute the final verbal exposition for the
genetic act by which the status arose.

**Practical form can precede explicit comprehension**

The recipient may acquire restraint before being instructed in every rule it
contains. This does not make the rules accidental. It means the acquired whole
can precede the bearer’s explicit differentiation of its limbs.

VAK 4.29 showed the eightfold whole differentiating into functional limbs.
VAK 4.30 now shows the same movement subjectively:

```text
whole practical status acquired
    → determinate rules subsequently unfolded
    → bearer comes to comprehend what is already binding
```

The genetic order and the order of explicit knowledge are therefore distinct.

**The dispute concerns the Copula of identity**

What makes someone an upāsaka?

```text
this person is an upāsaka
```

The Bahirdeśakas ground the Copula in refuge. The Kāśmīras ground it in
restraint acquired through undertaking. Both reject mere external naming, but
they locate the sufficient determination differently.

At the Organon level, the stronger Kāśmīra structure is:

```text
person undertakes upāsaka status
    → restraint arises

restraint arises
    → person is determinately an upāsaka
```

The status and its practical content reciprocally determine one another.

**Beyond artificial ethical commentary**

An artificial system merely states who belongs to a category and lists the
rules attached to it. Vasubandhu reconstructs the transitions:

```text
who is eligible?
what orientation is presupposed?
what act generates the status?
what does later instruction add?
```

This is a living practical philosophy rather than commentary pasted onto a
failed taxonomy.

This is an Organon development. The school positions, acquisition sequence,
and distinction between restraint and subsequent instruction are explicit in
the Bhāṣya.
