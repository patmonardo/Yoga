# VAK_4.100

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
भिक्षुर्दृक्चरितो वृत्ती भिनत्त्यन्यत्र बालिशान् ।
शास्तृमार्गान्तरक्षान्तौ भिन्नो न विवसत्यसौ ॥ ४.१०० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
bhikṣur dṛkcarito vṛttī bhinatty anyatra bāliśān /
śāstṛmārgāntarakṣāntau bhinno na vivasaty asau // 4.100 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
bhikṣuḥ | dṛk-caritaḥ | vṛttī | bhinatti | anyatra | bāliśān |
śāstṛ-antara-mārga-antara-kṣāntau | bhinnaḥ | na | vivasati | asau
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `bhikṣuḥ` | nominative masculine singular | a monk; the qualified divider |
| `dṛk-caritaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | one whose conduct is governed by doctrinal view |
| `vṛttī` | nominative masculine singular in `-in` | possessing the requisite conduct; socially credible |
| `bhinatti` | third-person singular causative/transitive present of `√bhid` | splits, divides |
| `anyatra` | indeclinable adverb | elsewhere; away from the Teacher's presence |
| `bāliśān` | accusative masculine plural | spiritually immature ordinary beings |
| `śāstṛ-antara-mārga-antara-kṣāntau` | locative feminine singular compound | upon assent to another teacher and another path |
| `bhinnaḥ` | nominative masculine singular past passive participle | divided |
| `na vivasati` | third-person singular present with negation | does not remain overnight |
| `asau` | nominative masculine singular demonstrative | that [Saṅgha or schism] |

**Bhāṣya-resolved sequence**

```text
qualified bhikṣuḥ
    → anyatra bāliśān bhinatti

bāliśānām
    śāstrantara-kṣāntiḥ
    + mārgāntara-kṣāntiḥ
    → saṃghaḥ bhinnaḥ

asau
    na vivasati
```

The first-pass syntax leaves `vṛttī`, `anyatra`, and the exact criterion of
completion compressed. The Bhāṣya explains them through credible conduct,
absence from the Buddha's immediate presence, and the followers' assent.

## 4. Grammar

**The qualified divider**

```text
bhikṣuḥ
    dṛk-caritaḥ
    vṛttī
    bhinatti
```

> A monk, governed by doctrinal view and possessing [credible] conduct,
> divides.

The three nominatives determine one agent. According to the Bhāṣya, the
divider must be a monk, not a layperson, nun, or other outsider; governed by
view rather than merely craving; and not of visibly broken conduct, since
speech that lacks credibility will not be accepted.

**Locus and recipients**

```text
anyatra
    bāliśān
    bhinatti
```

> Elsewhere, he divides the spiritually immature.

The adverb is explained as elsewhere than the place where the Blessed One is
immediately present. `Bāliśān` is the accusative object. The Bhāṣya glosses it
as ordinary beings, excluding nobles because Dharma is directly realized by
them; a reported alternative also excludes ordinary beings who have attained
`kṣānti`.

**Constitutive condition of completed division**

```text
śāstṛ-antara-mārga-antara-kṣāntau
    [saṃghaḥ] bhinnaḥ [bhavati]
```

> Upon assent to another teacher and another path, the Saṅgha is divided.

The locative states the condition under which the predicate `bhinnaḥ`
becomes valid. The compound contains two coordinated objects of assent:
another teacher and another path.

**Temporal limit**

```text
asau
    na vivasati
```

> It does not remain overnight.

`Vi-√vas` is literal here. The Bhāṣya states that the Saṅgha is necessarily
reunited before this special schism has endured through the night.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> A monk, governed by doctrinal view and possessing [credible] conduct,
> divides the spiritually immature elsewhere. Upon assent to another teacher
> and another path, [the Saṅgha] is divided; it does not remain so overnight.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A monk whose conduct is governed by doctrinal view, and whose outward
> discipline leaves his speech capable of commanding acceptance, can divide
> spiritually immature ordinary beings when he acts away from the Buddha's
> immediate presence. The Saṅgha is formally divided only when those followers
> accept someone other than the Tathāgata as teacher and accept another path
> in place of the Path he taught. This special division cannot last beyond
> that same night.

The exclusion of other agents and noble recipients, the contrast with
craving-governed conduct, the credibility condition, the interpretation of
`anyatra`, and the temporal limit are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A representation becomes institutionally divisive only when it is received
> as authoritative and reorganizes recognition around a rival teacher and
> path. The divider must speak from within the institution and retain enough
> normative credibility for uptake; the recipients must still depend upon
> mediated representation rather than direct realization. Schism is completed
> neither by utterance alone nor by private belief alone, but by socially
> effective assent that transforms the community's principle of unity.

The rational mediation is:

```text
false representation
    → reception as credible speech
    → assent to rival teacher and path
    → collective non-concord
```

Recognition is therefore the middle term between the divider's verbal Kriyā
and the altered actuality of the spiritual Object.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Bhikṣu` — monk as internal agent**

Only a monk can perform the technical schism described here. The restriction
shows that this is an internally mediated rupture of the monastic Saṅgha, not
every hostile attack upon it from outside.

**`Dṛk-carita` / `dṛṣṭi-carita` — governed by doctrinal view**

The divider acts from a doctrinal determination. The Bhāṣya contrasts this
with one governed merely by craving (`tṛṣṇā-carita`): the relevant rupture
reorganizes the normative content of teacher and Path.

**`Vṛttī` — possessing conduct**

The terse kārikā term is resolved by the prose through the divider's
receivability as a speaker. One of manifestly broken conduct has
`anādeyavākya`, speech that will not be accepted, and therefore cannot bring
the special schism to completion.

**`Ādeyavākya` — speech capable of acceptance**

Credible or authoritative speech. This does not mean true speech: it names
the practical capacity of an utterance to be taken up by others.

**`Anyatra` — elsewhere**

Away from the Buddha's immediate presence. The Bhāṣya explains that a rival
normative center cannot be established in that presence because the
Tathāgata's authority and receivable speech prevail.

**`Bāliśa` — spiritually immature**

Glossed as `pṛthagjana`, ordinary beings. Noble persons are excluded because
of `pratyakṣadharmatva`, their direct realization of Dharma. A reported view
also excludes ordinary persons who have attained `kṣānti`.

**`Kṣānti` — assent or acceptance**

Here the followers' acceptance of another teacher and another path. It is
the recognitive mediation through which false speech becomes an objective
communal division.

**`Śāstrantara` and `mārgāntara` — another teacher and another path**

The two coordinated contents of assent. The Saṅgha is divided when rival
normative authority and rival practice are accepted together.

**`Na vivasati` — does not remain overnight**

The strict temporal limit of this special type of schism. The Bhāṣya says
reconciliation necessarily occurs before the division has passed the night;
the next verse will distinguish this from procedural schism.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
D = qualified monastic divider
F = false doctrinal speech
C = credible or receivable authority
A = followers' assent
T' = rival teacher
P' = rival path
S = Saṅgha
B = collective non-concord
```

Then:

```text
Performs(D, F)
HasCredibility(D, C)
Receives(Followers, F)
Assents(Followers, T')
Assents(Followers, P')
Produces(F + C + A, B)
Bears(S, B)
```

But:

```text
Utters(D, F) alone
    does not entail B

PrivateBelief(Followers, F) alone
    does not entail B

RecognizesAsTeacher(Followers, T')
    + RecognizesAsPath(Followers, P')
    → CompletedSchism(S)
```

**Qualified susceptibility**

```text
DependsOnMediatedRepresentation(x)
    → RedirectableByRivalAuthority(x)

DirectlyRealizesDharma(x)
    → not RedirectableByRivalAuthority(x)
```

This is the Bhāṣya's doctrinal explanation for the distinction between
ordinary and noble recipients, not a general epistemology silently asserted
by the verse.

**Temporal condition**

```text
SpecialSanghaSchism(B)
    → ReunitedBeforeOvernight(S)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.98 identified non-concord as a determination borne by the Saṅgha. VAK
4.99 identified false speech as the causal karma borne by the divider. VAK
4.100 now supplies the middle without which those two poles cannot be joined:
the speech must be accepted by followers as the principle of another teacher
and another path.

This is precisely a Practical-Reason structure. A claim becomes practically
actual when it functions as a Principle organizing a field of appearances
and courses of action. Here the false claim organizes the communal Bhūmi: it
redistributes allegiance, authorizes a rival Path, and thereby generates a
new collective relation. Its semantic falsity and its practical efficacy are
different determinations and must both be preserved.

The verse also gives a sharper account of the spiritual Object. The Saṅgha's
unity is not mere proximity or numerical aggregation. It is a unity of
normative recognition directed toward Teacher and Path. Consequently, its
division is completed when recognition divides, even before the members or
their physical setting are replaced.

For Samyama as Schema Inference, the valid movement is:

```text
Principle
    → recognized application within a Bhūmi
    → organized practical actuality
```

VAK 4.100 exhibits the negative form of that same mediation. A false
principle, when accepted, produces a false organization that is nevertheless
actual as a conditioned collective state. Jñāna must therefore distinguish
the truth of the Principle from the reality of the relations generated by
its acceptance.
