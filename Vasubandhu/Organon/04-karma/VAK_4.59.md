# VAK_4.59

```sanskrit

कृष्णशुक्लादिभेदेन पुनः कर्म चतुर्विधम् ॥ ४.५९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
vaṅkadoṣakaṣāyoktiḥ śāṭhyadveṣajarāgaje /
kṛṣṇaśuklādibhedena punaḥ karma caturvidham // 4.59 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:


The first half correlates three predicates with three afflictive origins. The

**Ordered correlation**

```text
śāṭhya-ja
    → vaṅka

dveṣa-ja
    → doṣa

rāga-ja
    → kaṣāya
```

The compounds are not a loose list of synonyms. Their order carries the
genetic relation.

**`kaṣāya` and `rañjana`**

The Bhāṣya explains stain through the coloring produced by attachment.
`Kaṣāya` names the action's acquired coloration by its afflictive ground.

## 4. Grammar

**Three predicates from three grounds**

```text
śāṭhya-je [karmaṇi]
vaṅka-uktiḥ

dveṣa-je [karmaṇi]
doṣa-uktiḥ

rāga-je [karmaṇi]
kaṣāya-uktiḥ
```

> “Crookedness,” “fault,” and “stain” are the respective designations for
> karma born from deceit, hatred, and attachment.

The locative compounds state the generated karma in relation to which each
designation applies. The understood `karmaṇi` is supplied by context.

**Fourfold karma**

```text
punaḥ
karma catur-vidham
kṛṣṇa-śukla-ādi-bhedena [bhavati]
```

> Again, karma is fourfold according to the division beginning with black and
> white.

The instrumental `bhedena` gives the principle of division. The remaining
members are supplied by the cited sūtra and developed in VAK 4.60.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> “Crookedness,” “fault,” and “stain” are expressions for what is born,
> respectively, from deceit, hatred, and attachment. Again, karma is fourfold
> according to the division beginning with black and white.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Bodily, verbal, or mental karma generated by deceit is called crooked because
> it follows a devious course; karma generated by hatred is called corrupting
> because it follows the corruption of mind; and karma generated by attachment
> is called staining because it follows attachment's affective coloring. A
> further sūtra classification distinguishes black karma with black maturation,
> white karma with white maturation, mixed black-and-white karma, and karma
> that is neither black nor white and conduces to the exhaustion of karma.

The application across body, speech, and mind and the complete scriptural
enumeration are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The detailed allocation of the four
classes belongs to VAK 4.60.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Practical predicates arise from the Ground that generates the action.
> Deceit gives action a crooked course, hatred gives it a corrupting force, and
> attachment colors it with its object. These determinations can enter bodily,
> verbal, or mental activity because the kleśa is not an external label placed
> upon an already completed deed; it shapes the action's genesis. The verse
> then advances from origin in affliction to the relation between karma and its
> maturation, including a fourth activity ordered toward karmic exhaustion.

The second movement is announced here but becomes determinate only in the
following verses.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Crookedness from deceit**

```text
śāṭhya
    → devious or crooked course
    → vaṅka karma
```

The Bhāṣya uses `kuṭilānvayatva`, participation in crookedness, as the
explanation.

**Fault from hatred**

```text
dveṣa
    → corruption of mind
    → doṣa karma
```

The term does not mean a merely accidental mistake. It names action following
the damaging determination of hatred.

**Stain from attachment**

```text
rāga
    → affective coloring
    → kaṣāya karma
```

The action is “stained” because attachment has colored its practical relation.

**Three channels**

Each genetic relation can appear as:

```text
bodily karma
verbal karma
mental karma
```

The channels differ while the generating kleśa remains the ground of the
predicate.

**Fourfold scriptural schema**

```text
black karma
    with black maturation

white karma
    with white maturation

black-and-white karma
    with black-and-white maturation

neither-black-nor-white karma
    without maturation
    conducive to karmakṣaya
```

The fourth is structurally different, but its exact determination must wait
for VAK 4.60–4.62.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Predicate from afflictive ground**

```text
AFFLICTIVE_GROUND
    → GENERATED_ACTION
    → LICENSED_PREDICATE
```

Specifically:

```text
DECEIT(K)      → CROOKED(K)
HATRED(K)      → CORRUPTING(K)
ATTACHMENT(K)  → STAINED(K)
```

**Channel does not alter genetic identity**

```text
same afflictive ground
    → bodily, verbal, or mental realization
    → same ground-derived predicate
```

**Two classification levels**

```text
first half
    action classified by afflictive genesis

second half
    action classified by relation to maturation
```

The two grounds of division must remain distinct.

**The fourth class changes practical direction**

```text
ordinary karma
    → produces maturation

fourth announced class
    → no maturation of its own
    → conduces to exhaustion of karma
```

This is still the cited sūtra schema; the mechanism follows.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Actual kleśa becomes practical form**

The verse is an unusually direct account of the kleśa–karma transition:

```text
kleśa
    → configures the genesis of action
    → determines its bodily, verbal, or mental course
    → licenses its practical predicate
```

Kleśa is not simply a feeling hidden behind action. It becomes the formative
Ground of the action's actuality.

**Testing the Yoga hypothesis**

The present verse supports a distinction between a rationally misdetermined
Ground and its differentiated practical kleśas, but it does not name `avidyā`
or `asmitā`. What it positively shows is that deceit, hatred, and attachment
function as immediate genetic grounds of practical deformation. The more exact
Yoga mapping remains a hypothesis to test across the sequence.

**From Rational to Practical Karma**

In the project synthesis:

```text
Rational Karma
    determines the Ground under which appearance is taken

Practical Karma
    realizes that determination through kleśa-shaped activity
    and its maturation
```

The Kośa supplies the operational differentiations required for the second.

**Opening toward karmic exhaustion**

The fourth black-and-white classification introduces an activity whose
direction is not another ordinary fruit but the exhaustion of karma. The
Practical wheel therefore contains a possible operation upon its own karmic
production.

This is the Organon contact. The genetic explanations and cited fourfold schema
belong to the Bhāṣya; the Rational/Practical Karma distinction is project
synthesis.
