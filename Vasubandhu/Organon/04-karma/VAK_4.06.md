# VAK_4.06 — Two Genealogies of Non-disclosing Continuity

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> नैःष्यन्दिकी च सत्त्वाख्या निष्यन्दोपात्तभूतजा ।
>
> समाधिजौपचयिकानुपात्ताभिन्नभूतजा ॥ ४.६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> naiḥṣyandikī ca sattvākhyā niṣyandopātta-bhūtajā /
>
> samādhijā aupacayikānupāttābhinna-bhūtajā // 4.6 //

VAK 4.05 determined the level-relative Great-Element support of bodily and verbal
karma. VAK 4.06 now differentiates `avijñapti` according to its genesis and
according to the organization of its supporting Elements. The Bhāṣya divides the
verse between ordinary, non-concentrated `avijñapti` and the `avijñapti` born
from samādhi.

```text
non-concentrated level
    → homogeneous-outflow continuation
    → designated in relation to a sentient continuum

samādhi-born restraint
    → produced through cultivation
    → non-appropriated
    → seven restraints share one undivided Great-Element basis
```

The verse contrasts two modes of production. The Bhāṣya further distinguishes
the common Great-Element basis of concentration-born restraints from the
separate bases of prātimokṣa restraint.

## 4. Grammar

Both hemistichs share the understood subject `avijñaptiḥ`. The first coordinates `naiḥṣyandikī`, `sattvākhyā`, and `niṣyandopāttabhūtajā` as predicates. The second coordinates `samādhijā`, `aupacayikā`, `anupāttā`, and `abhinnabhūtajā`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal

> [Non-disclosing continuity] is homogeneous-outflow-derived and designated in
> relation to a sentient being, born from homogeneous-outflow-derived,
> appropriated Great Elements. That born from samādhi is
> cultivation-produced, non-appropriated, and born from an undivided
> Great-Element basis.

### Bhāṣya-informed

> At a non-concentrated level, `avijñapti` is a homogeneous causal
> continuation designated in relation to a sentient continuum. It arises in
> dependence upon outflow-derived, appropriated Great Elements and upon
> consciousness and mental factors. The `avijñapti` born from samādhi—the
> restraints of dhyāna and of the uncontaminated path—is produced through
> cultivation and is non-appropriated. Its seven abstentions arise from one
> undivided, common Great-Element basis, unlike the seven distinct bases of
> prātimokṣa restraint.

Dependence upon `citta-caitta`, the non-concentrated level, the identification
of the two samādhi restraints, the enumeration of seven abstentions, and the
contrast with `prātimokṣa` are determinations supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> [Non-disclosing continuity] is homogeneous-outflow-derived and designated in relation to a sentient being, born from homogeneous-outflow-derived, appropriated Great Elements. That born from concentration is cultivation-produced, non-appropriated, and born from an undivided Great-Element basis.

Three distinctions must remain controlled:

```text
sattvākhyā
    ≠ an enduring self or substance

abhinna material support
    ≠ absence of seven distinct normative functions

non-disclosing
    ≠ causally or conceptually indeterminate
```

## 8. Logical Determination


Ordinary non-disclosing continuity → the first set of predicates; meditation-born continuity → the second set, including cultivation-produced, non-appropriated, and common-Element origin.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya distinguishes two sources of `avijñapti`: ordinary causal
continuation, supported by appropriated Great Elements and mental factors,
and restraint born from samādhi, supported by a common undivided set of Great
Elements. It contrasts the latter with prātimokṣa restraint, whose seven
non-disclosing continuities have distinct bases. This unit does not develop a
broader account of the Path or of samādhi's place in the whole chapter.

### Former Philosophical Translation

### Interpretation

The first line describes ordinary `avijñapti` as a homogeneous continuation
associated with a sentient continuum and dependent on appropriated Great
Elements and mental factors. The second describes restraint born from samādhi:
it is cultivated, non-appropriated, and supported by one undivided set of Great
Elements. The Bhāṣya contrasts this shared basis with the distinct bases of
prātimokṣa restraint.
