# VAK_4.79

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
मूलच्छेदस्त्वसद्दृष्ट्या कामाप्तोत्पत्तिलाभिनाम् ।
फलहेत्वपवादिन्या सर्वथा क्रमशो नृषु ॥ ४.७९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
mūlacchedas tv asaddṛṣṭyā kāmāptotpatti-lābhinām /
phala-hetv-apavādinyā sarvathā kramaśo nṛṣu // 4.79 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
mūla-cchedaḥ | tu | asat-dṛṣṭyā |
kāma-āpta-utpatti-lābhinām |
phala-hetu-apavādinyā |
sarvathā | kramaśaḥ | nṛṣu
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `mūla-cchedaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | severance of the wholesome roots |
| `tu` | adversative or transitional particle | but; now |
| `asat-dṛṣṭyā` | instrumental feminine singular | by false or invalid view |
| `kāma-āpta` | compound member | belonging to the desire domain |
| `utpatti-lābhinām` | genitive plural compound | of those acquired by birth |
| `phala-hetu-apavādinyā` | instrumental feminine singular compound | by that which denies result and ground |
| `sarvathā` | indeclinable | completely; in every respect |
| `kramaśaḥ` | indeclinable | successively; in graded order |
| `nṛṣu` | locative masculine plural | among humans |

**What the genitive qualifies**

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed `kāmāptotpatti-lābhinām` as a qualification
of the wholesome roots:

```text
desire-domain wholesome roots
    acquired by birth
```

It does not mean merely “persons who have acquired a desire-domain birth.”
`Kuśala-mūlānām` is understood as the object of `cheda`.

## 4. Grammar

**Instrument of severance**

```text
asat-dṛṣṭyā
    phala-hetu-apavādinyā
    [kuśala-]mūla-cchedaḥ [bhavati]
```

> Severance of the wholesome roots occurs through false view that repudiates
> result and ground.

The two instrumentals agree. The second specifies the content of the false
view that can perform the severance.

**The roots severed**

```text
kāma-āptānām
utpatti-pratilābhikānām
kuśala-mūlānām
    chedaḥ
```

> The wholesome roots belonging to the desire domain and acquired by birth
> are severed.

The fuller phrase `utpatti-pratilābhika` is supplied by the Bhāṣya. Cultivated
or practice-produced roots are not the direct object at the final moment
because they have already fallen away.

**Scope, manner, and locus**

```text
sarvathā
    complete scope

kramaśaḥ
    successive manner

nṛṣu
    human locus
```

The result is total, but the preferred explanation makes its realization
graded rather than instantaneous.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The severance of the roots, however, is through false view—of those
> belonging to the desire domain and acquired by birth—through a view denying
> result and ground; completely, successively, among humans.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Desire-domain wholesome roots acquired by birth are severed among humans by
> fully intensified false view that denies karmic result and its ground. The
> severance reaches all nine grades and, according to the explanation preferred
> in the Bhāṣya, occurs successively as nine grades of false view remove the
> nine grades of wholesome roots. Practice-produced wholesome roots have
> already fallen away before this final severance. A transmitted alternative
> instead holds that all nine grades are severed at once.

Full intensification, the birth-acquired/practice-produced distinction, the
nine-grade mechanism, and the competing temporal accounts are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> False view becomes root-severing not through error alone but when its denial
> of karmic ground and consequence becomes fully formative for the continuum.
> It progressively removes the generative capacities through which wholesome
> action could arise until no residual grade remains. The outcome is complete,
> yet its preferred explanation is mediated and sequential: total ethical
> incapacity is produced through a determinate series of negations.

The view attacks the practical field at both ends:

```text
ground
    → action
    → result

denial of ground
    + denial of result
    → severance of wholesome generation
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kuśala-mūla` — wholesome root**

The generative basis of wholesome activity. In the established threefold
classification:

```text
alobha       non-greed
adveṣa       non-hatred
amoha        non-delusion
```

The verse concerns operative possession and generative capacity within a
continuum, not annihilation of eternal substances.

**`Mūlaccheda` / `samuccheda` — root-severance**

Loss of the relevant wholesome roots such that the continuum no longer
possesses them and is rendered an unsuitable basis for corresponding higher
wholesome acquisitions.

**`Adhimātra-paripūrṇā mithyādṛṣṭi` — fully intensified false view**

The Bhāṣya's restriction on the direct cause. False view in general does not
sever roots; it must reach a fully developed intense degree.

**Causal attribution to the unwholesome roots**

Scripture may say that the unwholesome roots perform the severance. The
Bhāṣya explains this indirectly:

```text
unwholesome roots
    bring forth and support false view

fully intensified false view
    directly severs wholesome roots
```

Its analogy is that thieves may be said to burn a village although fire is
the immediate burner and the thieves supplied it.

**`Upapatti-pratilābhika` — acquired by birth**

Wholesome roots possessed through the mode of birth. These desire-domain roots
are directly severed.

**`Prāyogika` — produced through practice**

Wholesome roots generated through deliberate application or cultivation.
According to the Bhāṣya, they have already fallen away before the final act of
root-severance.

**`Phala` and `hetu` — result and ground**

The two poles denied by the operative false view:

```text
hetu-apavāda
    there is no good conduct and no bad conduct

phala-apavāda
    good and bad karma have no fruit or maturation
```

`Hetu` is retained as ground in the project vocabulary while its causal
function remains explicit.

**`Navaprakāra` — nine grades**

The Bhāṣya's expansion of the complete field of wholesome roots and the graded
false view that severs them.

**`Bhājana` / `abhājana` — suitable or unsuitable basis**

The continuum becomes unsuitable for acquiring higher-domain wholesome roots.
This explains how direct severance of desire-domain roots also places those
higher acquisitions far away.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Direct and indirect causes**

```text
unwholesome roots
    → generate and support root-severing false view

fully intensified false view
    → directly performs severance
```

Causal responsibility and immediate operation remain distinct without being
unrelated.

**What is directly severed**

```text
desire-domain birth-acquired wholesome roots
    → directly severed

practice-produced wholesome roots
    → already lost before final severance

higher-domain wholesome roots
    → not directly cut here;
      their acquisition is rendered remote
      because the continuum is unsuitable
```

**Complete scope through successive realization**

```text
sarvathā
    all nine grades are finally absent

kramaśaḥ
    graded false view removes them successively
```

Preferred Bhāṣya account:

```text
nine grades of false view
    progressively sever
nine grades of wholesome roots

final strongest false view
    severs final weakest wholesome grade
        ↓
no residual grade remains
        ↓
complete root-severance
```

A surviving grade could ground re-arising of the whole set; completed
severance therefore requires that none remain.

**Attributed alternative**

```text
all nine grades
    severed at once
    like what is abandoned by the path of seeing
```

The Bhāṣya preserves this account but favors the sequence that gives full
force to `kramaśaḥ`.

**Why among humans**

The Bhāṣya explains:

```text
lower destinies
    afflicted and unafflicted prajñā lack sufficient firmness

gods
    karmic result is directly evident

humans
    root-severing false view can become firmly established
```

It also records disagreement about geographical restriction among human
continents and rejects an overly narrow Jambūdvīpa-only account.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Error within a system and denial of the system**

An ordinary unwholesome course violates a determinate practical relation
while still operating within the field of karma and result. Root-severing
false view denies the structure that gives such distinctions efficacy:

```text
ordinary violation
    acts wrongly within practical order

root-severing false view
    denies ground, consequence,
    and the objective difference of conduct
```

This is why its effect reaches generative capacity rather than one isolated
act.

**A view becomes causally formative**

The passage is not describing a merely verbal opinion. Through intensification,
view determines the continuum's future capacity:

```text
view
    → governing orientation
    → repeated graded exclusion
    → incapacity for wholesome generation
```

**Totality is an achieved result**

`Sarvathā kramaśaḥ` distinguishes complete extension from successive mode.
The whole negation is not an abstract nothing applied at once in the preferred
account; it is the outcome of determinate graded severances.

This is the Organon contact. The object of severance, intensification rule,
causal attribution, nine-grade dispute, human restriction, and domain
distinctions belong to the Bhāṣya; the contrast between internal violation and
destruction of generative capacity is the project synthesis.
