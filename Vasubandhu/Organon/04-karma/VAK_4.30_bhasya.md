## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
anyasyāpy upavāso 'sti śaraṇaṃ tv agatasya na.

anupavāsako 'pi yas tam ahorātraṃ buddhadharmasaṃghān śaraṇaṃ
gatvopavāsaṃ gṛhṇāti tasyotpadyate upavāsasaṃvaro nānyathā,
anyatrājñānāt.

sūtra uktaṃ: "yataś ca mahānāman gṛhī avadātavasanaḥ puruṣaḥ
puruṣendriyeṇa samanvāgato buddhaṃ śaraṇaṃ gacchati dharmaṃ
saṃghaṃ śaraṇaṃ gacchati vācaṃ ca bhāṣate upavāsakaṃ ca māṃ dharaya;
iyatā upāvāsako bhavati" iti. tat kiṃ śaraṇagamanād evopavāsako
bhavati?

bhavatīti bahirdeśakāḥ. na vinā saṃvareṇeti kāśmīrāḥ.
yat tarhi sūtra uktam? nāsty atra virodhaḥ, yasmād asyotpadyate
tata eva:

upāsakatvopagamāt saṃvṛt.

upāsakatvābhyupagamād evāsyopāsakasaṃvaro jāyate.
"yad evābhyupagacchaty upāsakaṃ māṃ dhārayety ādy agreṇa
yāvajjīvaṃ prāṇāpetam" iti. prāṇātipātādyapetam ity artho
madhyapadalopāt. laghusaṃvarasyāpi vyutpādanārthaṃ śikṣāpadānām:

uktis tu bhikṣuvat.

yathaivaṃ bhikṣur labdhasaṃvaro 'pi punaḥ śikṣāpadāni grāhyate
śrāmaṇeraś ca vyutpādanārtham itaś cāmutaś ca te saṃvara iti,
tathopavāsako 'pi; na tu vinā saṃvareṇopāsako 'sti.
```

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The running sūtra citation
has `upavāsakaṃ` and `upāvāsako`, while the Kārikā and its later
explanation use `upāsaka`; the variation is retained rather than
quietly made uniform. The Kārikā's `saṃvṛt` is compressed and is
explained in the prose by `upāsakasaṃvaro jāyate`. The phrase
`anyatrājñānāt` and the ellipsis around `prāṇāpetam` warrant
caution; Vasubandhu himself supplies the latter's intended sense
as `prāṇātipātādyapetam`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Does the upavāsa belong only to one already an observer of upavāsa,
or also to someone else? “Upavāsa can also belong to another, but
not to one who has not gone for refuge.” Even a person who was not
previously an upavāsa observer acquires the day-and-night restraint
if, having gone for refuge to Buddha, Dharma, and Saṃgha, that
person receives the upavāsa. Otherwise it does not arise, apart
from the qualification concerning ignorance mentioned here.

A sūtra is cited: a white-clothed householder, described as endowed
with the male faculty, goes for refuge to Buddha, Dharma, and
Saṃgha and says, “Regard me as an upavāsaka”; in this way he is
called an upavāsaka. Does going for refuge alone make one an
upavāsaka? The teachers of the outer regions say yes. The
Kāśmīras say there is no such status without restraint.

What, then, of the cited sūtra? On the Kāśmīra reply there is no
conflict: “Restraint [arises] from undertaking upāsaka status.”
The lay restraint arises as soon as the person accepts that status.
The acceptance “Regard me as an upāsaka” carries the sense of
being free for life from killing and the other transgressions,
Vasubandhu says, with the middle member of the expression omitted.

The later statement of the training rules is for instruction, as in
the case of a monk. Monks and novices may receive instruction in
the rules even after obtaining restraint, so they understand its
determinate requirements. The same applies to the lay observer.
On this account there is no upāsaka without restraint.

## 4. Two Eligibility and Status Questions

The first question concerns **temporary upavāsa**. Prior status as
an `upavāsaka` is not required; refuge followed by reception of
the observance is the stated route. The terse `anyatrājñānāt`
qualifies the strictness of the rule but is not developed here.

The second question concerns **lay upāsaka status** and is
contested. The sūtra citation prompts two school readings:

| Position reported | Ground treated as sufficient |
|---|---|
| Bahirdeśaka | Going for refuge |
| Kāśmīra | Undertaking upāsaka status and thereby acquiring restraint |

The prose's use of `upavāsaka` in the citation and `upāsaka` in the
later explanation should not be hidden. The local argument moves
between temporary observance and lay status, and the precise
textual relation of these forms remains a source question.

## 5. Acquisition and Later Instruction

The Kāśmīra explanation distinguishes the **acquisition** of
`upāsakasaṃvara` from the subsequent **statement** of its training
rules. The declaration accepting status already has practical
force; the later `śikṣāpada` recitation serves `vyutpādana`, making
the requirements understood. The monk and novice comparison is
given for this sequence, not to erase differences between lay and
monastic restraints.

The end of the unit states the Kāśmīra conclusion that no upāsaka
exists without restraint. The next verse challenges that conclusion
with the scriptural classifications of lay followers by how many
rules they keep. We should therefore treat the conclusion as a
position still under examination, not as the final settlement of
the debate.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our Jñāna methodology, this is a case where one word,
“membership,” would hide three different operations: orientation
through refuge, acquisition of a standing restraint, and later
articulation of its rules. The Kośa's school dispute tests which
operation actually grounds the practical status. In our System,
the analogous task is to state what brings a Pure Reason vow into
force and what only teaches its content afterward. This is an
Organon comparison, not an attribution of our vows to the sūtra.
