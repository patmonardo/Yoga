# VAK 4.126 — Method-Generated Action across Body, Speech, and Mind

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
योगप्रवर्तितं कर्म ससमुत्थापकं त्रिधा ।
लिपिमुद्रे सगणनं काव्यं संख्या यथाक्रमम् ॥ ४.१२६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
yogapravartitaṃ karma sasamutthāpakaṃ tridhā /
lipimudre sagaṇanaṃ kāvyaṃ saṃkhyā yathākramam // 4.126 //
```

Here `yoga` is locally glossed as a specific method or means
(`upāyaviśeṣa`); the Bhāṣya does not use it here to mean contemplative
Yoga. The examples are classified by their operative mode, not simply
listed as arts or kinds of knowledge.

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |

| `lipimudre` | coordinated nominative dual | writing and sealing |

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed sequence by assigning the first two
activities to bodily action, calculation and poetry to verbal action, and
`saṃkhyā` to mental action.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause describes method-generated action together with its
initiating or producing factor:

```text
yogapravartitaṃ karma
    sa-samutthāpakam
    tridhā [bhavati]
```

> Action set in motion by a specific method, together with what gives rise
> to it, is threefold.

The Bhāṣya identifies the three modes as bodily, verbal, and mental
action. Its ordered assignment of examples is:

```text
lipi + mudrā      → kāyakarma
gaṇanā + kāvya    → vākkarma
saṃkhyā           → manaskarma
```

It further explains `saṃkhyā` as `manasā saṃkalanaṃ dharmāṇām`: the
mental collecting or reckoning of Dharmas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Action set in motion by a specific method, together with what gives rise

> reckoning, in the corresponding order.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Action set in motion by a specific method, together with what gives rise to it, is threefold: writing and sealing; calculation and poetry; and reckoning, in the corresponding order.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

- **`Yoga`** — in this Bhāṣya, a specific means or method
  (`upāyaviśeṣa`) through which an activity is set in motion.
- **`Pravartita`** — set in motion or brought into operation.
- **`Samutthāpaka`** — what gives rise to or initiates the action. The
  verse includes this factor in its description of method-generated
  action.
- **`Kāyakarma`** — bodily action; the Bhāṣya assigns writing and sealing
  to it.
- **`Vākkarma`** — verbal action; the Bhāṣya assigns calculation and
  poetry to it.
- **`Manaskarma`** — mental action; here it is the mind's collecting or
  reckoning of Dharmas.
- **`Gaṇanā` and `saṃkhyā`** — related to counting or number, but not
  interchangeable here: the Bhāṣya classifies `gaṇanā` as verbal action
  and defines `saṃkhyā` as mental collection.
- **`Pañcaskandhasvabhāva`** — “of the nature of the five Bases,” the
  Bhāṣya's characterization of the first four activities.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya form a two-part determination:

```text
method + initiating factor
    → action brought into operation
    → differentiated as bodily, verbal, or mental
```

The five worldly activities are ordered by operative mode:

```text
writing, sealing       → bodily action
calculation, poetry    → verbal action
mental reckoning       → mental action
```

Therefore the subject matter alone does not determine the kind of action:

```text
calculation expressed through speech ≠ mental reckoning of Dharmas
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The verse supplies the ordered examples; the body–speech–mind assignments,
the definition of `saṃkhyā`, and the five-Base determination are supplied
by the Bhāṣya. The informed translation also included: The Bhāṣya identifies the three modes as bodily, verbal, and mental action: writing and sealing are bodily; calculation and poetry are verbal; and `saṃkhyā` is mental action, the mind's collecting or reckoning of Dharmas. It says the first four activities are of the nature of the five Bases.

VAK 4.125 classifies wholesome activity by its developmental orientation:
merit, nirvāṇa, or penetration. VAK 4.126 turns to a different question:
how familiar worldly skills are set in motion and instantiated as action.
The body–speech–mind triad is therefore not a second list of ends or
values; it differentiates the operation through which an activity occurs.

This is why `gaṇanā` and `saṃkhyā` cannot be flattened into the same
dictionary gloss. The Bhāṣya places calculation with speech, then defines
`saṃkhyā` as Mind's act of collecting Dharmas. A number-related practice
changes classification with its operative mode, not merely its topic.

The Bhāṣya calls the first four activities `pañcaskandhasvabhāva`—of the
nature of the five Bases—then separately identifies `saṃkhyā` as mental
action. In the Organon reading, this does not make the Bases Mind itself:
the Base determination concerns the activity's structured content and
frame, while `manaskarma` names the mental operation that collects
Dharmas.

The source span is `[275|01]–[275|06]`. The question at `[275|01]` asks
about the nature of familiar worldly practices; the next passage, beginning
at `[275|07]`, turns to designations for kinds of Dharmas.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> A learned activity is not defined only by its product or subject matter.
> It is methodically set in motion, considered together with what brings
> it about, and differentiated according to its mode of action. Writing
> and sealing enact bodily action; calculation and poetry enact verbal
> action; mental reckoning collects Dharmas through Mind. Thus a practice
> that concerns number can belong either to speech or to mind, depending
> on how it is actually performed.
