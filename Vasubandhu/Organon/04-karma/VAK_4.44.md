# VAK_4.44 — Restraint Possessed Without Present Manifestation

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कामरूपजदेवानां ध्यानजः अनास्रवः पुनः ।
ध्यानान्तरासंज्ञिसत्त्ववर्ज्यानामप्यरूपिणाम् ॥ ४.४४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
kāmarūpajadevānāṃ dhyānajaḥ anāsravaḥ punaḥ /
dhyānāntarāsaṃjñisattvavarjyānām apy arūpiṇām // 4.44 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:


The understood substantive is `saṃvaraḥ`, carried forward from VAK 4.43.
The Bhāṣya supplies the exact distribution compressed by the verse.

**Two occurrences of `-ja`**

The same suffix expresses two different relations:

```text
kāma-rūpa-ja
    born in the Desire or Form Realm

dhyāna-ja
    generated by meditation
```

The first classifies the bearer by realm of birth. The second classifies the
restraint by its generating condition. They must not be conflated.

**`anāsrava`**

```text
an-
    without

āsrava
    influx, contamination
```

Uncontaminated restraint accompanies the noble path. It is not simply a
particularly pure conventional vow.

**`dhyānāntara`**

The intermediate meditation is a determinate meditative station between the
first and second dhyānas. The Bhāṣya excludes beings born there from the
present distribution of uncontaminated restraint.

**`asaṃjñisattva`**

This is the established cosmological class of non-percipient beings. Their
exclusion concerns the absence of the cognitive actuality required here.

## 4. Grammar

**Meditation-born restraint**

```text
kāma-rūpa-ja-devānām
dhyāna-jaḥ [saṃvaraḥ asti]
```

> Meditation-born restraint occurs among gods born in the Desire and Form
> Realms.

The genitive expresses the range of bearers. `Saṃvaraḥ` and the existential
verb are understood from the preceding verse and the surrounding discussion.

**Uncontaminated restraint**

```text
anāsravaḥ [saṃvaraḥ] punaḥ
[kāma-rūpa-ja-devānām]
dhyāna-antara-asaṃjñi-sattva-varjyānām
api arūpiṇām [asti]
```

> Uncontaminated restraint, in turn, occurs among those born in the Desire and
> Form Realms, excluding those born in the intermediate dhyāna and
> non-percipient beings, and also among formless beings.

The kārikā is elliptical. The Bhāṣya restores the Desire-and-Form Realm range
for `anāsrava` and explains how the exclusions attach. It then qualifies the
final `arūpiṇām`: among formless beings the restraint exists by possession,
not by present manifestation.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Meditation-born [restraint belongs] to gods born in the Desire and Form
> Realms. Uncontaminated [restraint], in turn, [belongs to those born in those
> Realms], excluding those born in the intermediate dhyāna and non-percipient
> beings, and also to formless beings.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Meditation-born restraint occurs among gods born in the Desire and Form
> Realms. Uncontaminated restraint occurs among beings born in those Realms,
> except those born in the intermediate dhyāna and non-percipient beings; it
> also occurs among the formless.
## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Meditation-born [restraint belongs] to gods born in the Desire and Form Realms. Uncontaminated [restraint], in turn, [belongs to those born in those Realms], excluding those born in the intermediate dhyāna and non-percipient beings, and also to formless beings.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`dhyānaja-saṃvara`**

Meditation-born restraint arises with meditation rather than through the
verbal undertaking that institutes prātimokṣa restraint.

```text
actualized dhyāna
    → meditation-born restraint

realm of birth alone
    does not generate it
```

Gods born in either the Desire or Form Realm may possess the relevant
meditative ground. Above the Form Realm, this restraint is absent.

**`anāsrava-saṃvara`**

Uncontaminated restraint is generated by the uncontaminated noble path:

```text
noble-path cognition
    → acquisition of uncontaminated restraint
```

Its range therefore follows prior path-acquisition, not simply the hierarchy
of realms.

**`samanvāgama`**

`Samanvāgama` is possession or endowment: a dharma belongs to the continuum as
an acquired determination.

```text
continuum possesses D
    ≠
D is presently occurring
```

This term is decisive for the formless case.

**`saṃmukhībhāva`**

`Saṃmukhībhāva` is present manifestation—the dharma's being immediately
actual or operative now. The Bhāṣya denies this relation, not possession, for
uncontaminated restraint among formless beings.

**The exclusions**

The intermediate-dhyāna and non-percipient births are excluded from the
relevant Desire-and-Form Realm distribution. The exclusion is functional:
the required cognitive and path conditions are not actualized there.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Generation and distribution**

```text
restraint type
    → has a determinate generating condition

generating condition
    → determines its possible range
```

Thus the three restraints are differentiated by genesis:

| Restraint | Generating condition | Distribution emphasized here |
|---|---|---|
| Prātimokṣa | formal undertaking | eligible humans |
| Meditation-born | meditation | eligible humans and desire/form gods |
| Uncontaminated | noble path | eligible path-bearers, including possession in formless existence |

**Possession does not entail manifestation**

```text
POSSESSES(C, D)
    does not entail
MANIFESTS(C, D, now)
```

The distinction allows a continuum to be determined by what it has acquired
without reducing that determination to its presently appearing state.

**Realm of birth and present cognition**

```text
realm in which a being is born
    ≠ necessarily
level or kind of cognition presently actualized
```

The realm classifies the support; the generating cognition determines the
restraint.

**The complete distribution**

```text
eligible human
    → all three restraints possible

eligible desire/form god
    → meditation-born restraint possible
    → uncontaminated restraint possible under stated exclusions

formless being
    → no meditation-born restraint
    → uncontaminated restraint possible by possession
    → not presently manifest
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The upper limit of meditation-born restraint, the restored
range of uncontaminated restraint, and the distinction between possession and
present manifestation are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The prose specifies that
meditation-born restraint does not occur above the Form Realm. Formless beings
possess uncontaminated restraint without its present manifestation; the verse does
not state that qualification explicitly.

**The nonmanifest is not the nonexistent**

VAK 4.44 supplies an especially clear determination of persistence:

```text
not presently manifest
    does not entail
not possessed
```

The continuum includes more than the state currently presented. Yet the
Bhāṣya does not appeal to a hidden permanent substance. It distinguishes two
conditioned relations to one acquired dharma: continued possession and
present manifestation.

**A Kant-clean limit on appearance**

What appears at the present moment does not exhaust what determines the
continuum. The distinction is transcendental when it specifies the conditions
under which a determination belongs to and can become manifest within that
continuum. It would become transcendent only if possession were converted
into a separately existing entity behind the series.

**The practical Idea and its actuality**

The verse does not use `artha`, but it sharpens the distinction developed in
VAK 4.41. A practical determination can belong to a continuum without being
presently exhibited. Its objectivity is therefore not reducible to an Object
currently before consciousness. At the same time, its mode of actuality must
be specified: here the Bhāṣya says possession, not present manifestation.

**Close of the Abhidharma restraint section**

The prose following the verse explicitly changes the subject:

```text
VAK 4.18–4.44
    Abhidharma analysis of bodily and verbal karma,
    restraint, non-restraint, and intermediate avijñapti

VAK 4.45 onward
    exposition of karmas enumerated in the sūtras
```

VAK 4.44 therefore completes the distribution of the standing practical
forms. The verse is not merely another cosmological inventory; it closes the
analysis by distinguishing what can be acquired, where it can belong, and
whether it can presently appear.

This is the Organon contact. The realm distribution, exclusions, and
`samanvāgama`–`saṃmukhībhāva` distinction belong to the Bhāṣya.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> A practical determination need not be presently manifest in order to belong
> to a continuum. Meditation-born restraint requires a meditative actuality
> whose range ends with the Form Realm. Uncontaminated restraint follows the
> acquisition of the noble path and therefore has a different distribution:
> it can remain possessed even in a formless existence in which it cannot be
> brought into present manifestation. Possession is thus neither sensible
> appearance nor current performance, but the continued belonging of an
> acquired determination.

This does not make the possessed restraint an eternal or transcendent entity.
It remains a conditioned acquisition belonging to a determinate continuum.
