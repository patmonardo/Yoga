# VAK_4.66 — Ten Courses Selected from Conduct

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
विपर्ययात्सुचरितं तदौदारिकसंग्रहात् ।
दश कर्मपथा उक्ता यथायोगं शुभाशुभाः ॥ ४.६६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
viparyayāt sucaritaṃ tadaudārikasaṃgrahāt /
daśa karmapathā uktā yathāyogaṃ śubhāśubhāḥ // 4.66 //
```

The ten courses are selected from the broader fields of good and bad conduct;
they are not presented as an exhaustive inventory of ethically qualified acts.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

**`viparyaya`**

Good conduct is determined by reversing the bad conduct of VAK 4.65. The
Bhāṣya supplies wholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma together with
non-covetousness, non-hostility, and right view.

**`audārika`**

“Gross” does not mean merely physically large. Here the Bhāṣya uses the term
for salient forms selected as the principal courses in this classification.

**`karmapatha`**

A course or path of karma is a selected practical route through which karmic
determination proceeds. It is narrower than the whole conduct-field.

## 4. Grammar

**Good conduct through reversal**

```text
duścaritasya viparyayāt
sucaritam [bhavati]
```

> By reversal of bad conduct, there is good conduct.

The ablative gives the relation through which the positive counterpart is
determined.

**Selection of the ten**

```text
tat-audārika-saṃgrahāt
daśa karma-pathāḥ
yathā-yogam
śubhāḥ aśubhāḥ ca
uktāḥ
```

> Because the principal forms of that conduct are selected, ten wholesome and
> unwholesome courses of karma are taught as applicable.

The singular compound refers collectively to the wider fields of good and bad
conduct. `Yathāyogam` distributes wholesome courses from good conduct and
unwholesome courses from bad conduct.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> By reversal there is good conduct. Because the principal forms of that
> conduct are comprehended, ten courses of karma are taught, wholesome and
> unwholesome as applicable.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Good conduct is the converse [of bad conduct]. By selecting the salient
> forms of those conducts, ten courses of karma are taught, wholesome or
> unwholesome as applicable.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> By reversal there is good conduct. Because the principal forms of that conduct are comprehended, ten courses of karma are taught, wholesome and unwholesome as applicable.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Good conduct**

The Bhāṣya gives:

```text
wholesome bodily karma
wholesome verbal karma
wholesome mental karma
non-covetousness
non-hostility
right view
```

The last three are the positive counterparts of the non-karmic mental bad
conducts in VAK 4.65.

**Why right and false view are ethically qualified**

The Bhāṣya asks how view can be wholesome or unwholesome without itself
containing an intention to benefit or injure another. The answer is:

```text
tanmūlatvāt
    because they are roots of such conduct
```

View determines the avenue of volition without becoming volition itself.

**The tenfold distribution**

```text
three bodily courses
four verbal courses
three mental courses
    = ten karmapathas
```

Their detailed composition begins in the following verses. This Kārikā closes
the definitional sequence at the level of selection; the next inquiry begins
the applied examination of these courses.

**Bad conduct excluded from the ten**

Examples include:

```text
preparatory phases
subsequent phases
binding and other lesser afflicted bodily conduct
unselected verbal misconduct
mental volition as such
```

The three bodily bad karmapathas are selected because they decisively deprive
another of life, possessions, or sexual relation.

**Good conduct excluded from the ten**

Examples include:

```text
preparatory and subsequent wholesome phases
abstention from intoxicants and similar disciplines
giving and reverential activity
pleasant or affectionate speech
mental volition as such
```

Exclusion from the ten does not mean ethical irrelevance.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Nested practical extensions**

```text
CONDUCT
    contains
GOOD_CONDUCT and BAD_CONDUCT

GOOD/BAD_CONDUCT
    contains more than
TEN_KARMIC_COURSES
```

Therefore:

```text
karmapatha
    ⊂ conduct
```

**Selective comprehension**

```text
complete conduct-field
    → discriminative selection by audārika criterion
    → ten principal courses
```

The abstraction preserves the field from which the selected forms derive
their Meaning.

**Root and action remain distinct**

```text
right or false view
    → root-condition
    → orientation of volition (*cetanā*)
    → qualified karma
```

The ethical qualification of view follows its genetic function.

**Principal form is not the whole causal episode**

```text
preparation
principal completed course
subsequent phase
```

Only the principal decisive forms are selected here. Later verses differentiate
these phases more exactly.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya identifies good conduct as wholesome bodily, verbal, and mental
karma together with non-covetousness, non-hostility, and right view. It explains
that views have ethical quality as roots of beneficial or harmful conduct,
even without a present intention to benefit or harm another. The ten courses
select salient forms from the broader fields of good and bad conduct.
Preparatory and subsequent phases, lesser bodily misconduct, the uncertain
verbal example, giving, abstention from intoxicants, pleasant speech, and
mental volition as such remain ethically significant without belonging to
the selected ten.

The positive extension of good conduct, selection criterion, and examples
outside the ten are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

**Transcendental Form Theory**

The ten karmapathas are forms organizing the practical field:

```text
not
    ten isolated moral Objects

but
    ten principal routes
    through which conduct acquires determinate karmic structure
```

This is the precise sense in which Karma theory functions transcendentally.

**Rational Ground and practical course**

View determines the rational orientation; volition proceeds through that
orientation; the karmapatha gives the principal practical form. The sequence
can be stated without calling view itself karma:

```text
root determination
    → volition
    → practical course
    → karmic result
```

**The taxonomy is deliberately incomplete**

The ten are operationally privileged, not ontologically exhaustive. This
prevents the canonical list from flattening the richer field of discipline,
giving, speech, preparation, and consequence.

**Progression of VAK 4.64–4.66**

```text
4.64
    perfected silence and threefold purity

4.65
    broad field of good and bad conduct

4.66
    selection of ten principal karmic courses
```

The chapter has moved from the whole normative field to its decisive formal
articulation.

This is the Organon contact. The positive conduct-list, question about views,
root explanation, selection criterion, and excluded examples belong to the
Bhāṣya; Transcendental Form Theory is the project synthesis.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> The complete practical field contains more determinations than its canonical
> taxonomy. Through discrimination, the principal forms are selected as ten
> courses that make the operative structure of wholesome and unwholesome
> conduct explicit. The classification is therefore a Transcendental Form
> theory: it identifies the decisive routes through which practical
> determination becomes karmically organized without pretending that only ten
> ethically meaningful events exist.

View belongs to this field as a root governing the direction of action, not as
strict karma identical with the volition it conditions.
