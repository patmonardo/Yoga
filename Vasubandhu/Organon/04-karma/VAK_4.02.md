# VAK_4.02 — Manifest Action, Configuration, and Momentariness

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> ते तु विज्ञप्त्यविज्ञप्ती कायविज्ञप्तिरिष्यते ।
>
> संस्थानं न गतिर्यस्मात् संस्कृतं क्षणिकं व्ययात् ॥ ४.२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> te tu vijñaptyavijñaptī kāyavijñaptir iṣyate /
>
> saṃsthānaṃ na gatir yasmāt saṃskṛtaṃ kṣaṇikaṃ vyayāt // 4.2 //

VAK 4.01 identifies Cetanā as mental karma and distinguishes the bodily and
verbal actions produced after intending. `Te` resumes those two actions. The
Bhāṣya makes the opening compression explicit: bodily and verbal karma each
have both a `vijñapti` and an `avijñapti` mode. These terms do not rename
Cetanā.

`Vijñapti` belongs to the semantic field of causing something to be distinctly
known or manifest. It must remain distinct from `vijñāna`:

```text
vijñāna
    Cognition (Discriminative Cognition)

vijñapti
    manifestation / making-known through action
```

`Avijñapti` is not simple nonexistence or failure to act. The controlled
technical rendering is **non-disclosing continuity**. The verse names it as a
mode of bodily and verbal karma, but its positive constitution must be
established by the following units.

## 4. Grammar

`Te tu` resumes a dual subject; `vijñaptyavijñaptī` supplies its dual predicate. The next clause predicates `saṃsthānam`, and denies `gati`, of bodily manifestation. `Yasmāt` introduces the final causal clause, with `saṃskṛtaṃ`, `kṣaṇikam`, and `vyayāt` forming its predicate.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Those two, however, are manifestation and non-manifestation. Bodily
> manifestation is held to be configuration, not motion, because the
> conditioned is momentary owing to its perishing.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Bodily and verbal action each possess a manifest and a non-manifest mode.
> Bodily manifestation is held to be the body's determinate configuration
> under the direction of consciousness, not the motion of one enduring thing
> from place to place; for whatever is conditioned is momentary and perishes
> where it arises.

The distributive **each**, the body's being configured `cittavaśena`, “under
the direction of consciousness,” and the denial of passage by one identical
thing into another place are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Cetanā and Bodily Manifestation

VAK 4.01 identifies Cetanā as mental karma and distinguishes the bodily and
verbal actions produced from it. VAK 4.02 takes up those two actions and
assigns both modes to each. The Bhāṣya then treats bodily manifestation in
particular: it is the body's configuration under the direction of
consciousness, not the transfer of one enduring conditioned thing between
places. The verse grounds this rejection of motion in momentariness. It names
`avijñapti` but does not yet establish its constitution.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| vijñapti | manifestation / disclosure | expressive mode of bodily or verbal karma |
| avijñapti | non-disclosing continuity | mode assigned to each bodily and verbal action; positive determination remains for subsequent verses |
| vijñapti-avijñapti-svabhāva | having manifestation and non-manifestation as natures | Bhāṣya's distributive analysis of bodily and verbal karma |
| kāya-vijñapti | bodily manifestation | bodily configuration generated under the determination of consciousness |
| cittavaśena | under the direction or power of consciousness | Bhāṣya-only condition distinguishing expressive configuration from mere shape |
| tathā tathā saṃsthāna | configuration in this or that determinate manner | Bhāṣya expansion of bodily manifestation |
| saṃsthāna | configuration | determinate bodily arrangement rather than enduring transit |
| gati | motion / going | rival account of bodily karma as the motion of a moving body |
| prasyandamāna | moving / flowing forth | Bhāṣya's characterization of the body in the rival account |
| saṃskṛta | conditioned | dependently produced and necessarily perishing occurrence |
| kṣaṇa | moment | Bhāṣya: acquisition of existence immediately followed by destruction |
| kṣaṇika | momentary | possessing such a moment; not persisting into a second moment |
| ātmalābha | acquisition of its own existence | Bhāṣya expression for the arising of a conditioned occurrence |
| anantaravināśin | immediately perishing | Bhāṣya determination of the momentary |
| vyaya | perishing / passing away | verse's ground for the momentariness of the conditioned |
| deśāntarasaṃkrānti | transference to another place | what an enduring-motion account requires and momentariness excludes |
| akasmika-vināśa | destruction without a separately producing cause | Bhāṣya claim initiating the proof of momentariness |
| anumānasādhya | to be established by inference | Bhāṣya's methodological judgment about destruction and succession |

The working distinction is:

```text
saṃsthāna
    the presently determinate articulation of the body

gati
    the alleged transit of one identical conditioned entity
    across successive places
```

The first is maintained here; the second is rejected.

## 8. Logical Determination


Bodily and verbal action → the two forms named in the verse; bodily manifestation → configuration, not movement; the final clause relates conditioned existence to momentariness and perishing.

## 9. Textual Connection

VAK 4.01 identifies Cetanā as mental karma and distinguishes the bodily and
verbal actions produced from it. VAK 4.02 takes up those produced actions,
states that each has both modes, and examines bodily manifestation. The
Bhāṣya's account of configuration and momentariness addresses bodily action;
the positive constitution of `avijñapti` and the wider account of karmic
continuity remain for the following verses.
