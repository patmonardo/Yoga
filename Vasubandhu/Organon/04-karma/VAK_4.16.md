# VAK_4.16 — Restraint as Action: Names and Moments

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> शीलं सुचरितं कर्म संवरश्चोच्यते पुनः ।
>
> आद्ये विज्ञप्त्यविज्ञप्ती प्रातिमोक्षक्रियापथः ॥ ४.१६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> śīlaṃ sucaritaṃ karma saṃvaraś cocyate punaḥ /
>
> ādye vijñaptyavijñaptī prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ // 4.16 //

The verse gives four names for *prātimokṣa* restraint and then distinguishes
the first moment of undertaking it. The Bhāṣya explains how restraint can
be called both action and non-action, and how its initial moment differs
from its continuation.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| śīlam | nominative neuter singular | discipline |
| sucaritam | nominative neuter singular | good conduct |
| karma | nominative neuter singular | action |
| saṃvaraḥ | nominative masculine singular | restraint |
| ucyate | third-person singular present passive | is called |
| punaḥ | adverb | further; also |
| ādye | locative singular | in the first [moment], as specified by the Bhāṣya |
| vijñapti-avijñaptī | nominative feminine dual | manifestation and non-disclosing continuity |
| prātimokṣa-kriyāpathaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | course of *prātimokṣa* activity |

The Bhāṣya glosses *ādye* as the first moment of undertaking restraint,
not as the first two names in the preceding list.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda coordinates four names—`śīlam`, `sucaritam`, `karma`, and `saṃvaraḥ`—with the passive `ucyate`; `punaḥ` modifies that predication. In the second, `ādye` is locative singular, `vijñaptyavijñaptī` is dual, and `prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ` completes the nominal construction.

## 5. Translation

### Literal

> Restraint is also called discipline, good conduct, action, and
> containment. In the first [moment], manifestation and non-disclosing
> continuity are the course of *prātimokṣa* activity.

### Bhāṣya-informed

> Restraint is called discipline because it is undertaken as abstention
> from improper actions; good conduct because learned persons praise it;
> action because its nature is activity; and restraint because it
> contains body and speech. The objection that non-disclosing continuity
> is non-action is answered by distinguishing the deed prevented from
> the restraint itself: evil is not performed through the restraint, yet
> the restraint is produced through manifestation and consciousness.
> Others explain it as a cause and a result of activity. In the first
> moment of undertaking restraint, both manifestation and non-disclosing
> continuity are called *prātimokṣa*; in later moments, the continuing
> state is *prātimokṣasaṃvara*, not *prātimokṣa* in that strict sense.
> The commentary also calls it an action-course insofar as it belongs
> to the fundamental phase.

The cause-and-result explanation is explicitly attributed to “others.”
The first-moment gloss and the distinction between *kriyāpatha* and
*karmapatha* come from the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Restraint is also called discipline, good conduct, action, and containment. In the first [moment], manifestation and non-disclosing continuity are the course of prātimokṣa activity.

## 8. Logical Determination

Restraint receives four names; the first undertaking moment pairs manifestation with non-disclosing continuity; later restraint continues as a distinct moment.

### Interpretation

The Bhāṣya does not treat restraint as mere inactivity. It is
non-action with respect to the evil deed it prevents, but activity with
respect to the restraint's own production. The distinction lets the
commentary call restraint *karma* without claiming that it is the
prohibited deed.

For the Organon, this supports reading restraint as a positively
undertaken practical determination. Cetanā is not named in this unit;
connecting the undertaking to the project's Cetanā framework is an
Organon interpretation, not the Bhāṣya's wording here.
