# VAK_4.16 — Restraint as Action: Names and Moments

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
शीलं सुचरितं कर्म संवरश्चोच्यते पुनः ।
आद्ये विज्ञप्त्यविज्ञप्ती प्रातिमोक्षक्रियापथः ॥ ४.१६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
śīlaṃ sucaritaṃ karma saṃvaraś cocyate punaḥ /
ādye vijñaptyavijñaptī prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ // 4.16 //
```

The verse gives four names for *prātimokṣa* restraint and then distinguishes
the first moment of undertaking it. The Bhāṣya explains how restraint can
be called both action and non-action, and how its initial moment differs
from its continuation.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| śīlam | nominative neuter singular | discipline |
| sucaritam | nominative neuter singular | good conduct |
| karma | nominative neuter singular | action |
| saṃvaraḥ | nominative masculine singular | restraint |
| ucyate | third-person singular present passive | is called |
| punaḥ | adverb | further; also |
| ādye | locative singular | in the first [moment], as specified by the Bhāṣya |
| vijñapti-avijñaptī | nominative feminine dual | manifestation and non-disclosing continuity |
| prātimokṣa-kriyāpathaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | course of *prātimokṣa* activity |

The Bhāṣya glosses *ādye* as the first moment of undertaking restraint,
not as the first two names in the preceding list.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda coordinates four names—`śīlam`, `sucaritam`, `karma`, and `saṃvaraḥ`—with the passive `ucyate`; `punaḥ` modifies that predication. In the second, `ādye` is locative singular, `vijñaptyavijñaptī` is dual, and `prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ` completes the nominal construction.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Restraint is also called discipline, good conduct, action, and
> containment. In the first [moment], manifestation and non-disclosing
> continuity are the course of *prātimokṣa* activity.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> It is called discipline, good conduct, action, and restraint. In the first
> moment, manifestation and non-disclosing continuity are called
> *prātimokṣa* and an action-course.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Restraint is also called discipline, good conduct, action, and containment. In the first [moment], manifestation and non-disclosing continuity are the course of prātimokṣa activity.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| `śīla` | discipline | undertaking abstention from improper actions; also explained through coolness |
| `sucarita` | good conduct | praised by the learned |
| `karma / kriyā` | action / activity | restraint is produced activity despite the limited sense of `akriyā` |
| `vijñapti / avijñapti` | manifestation / non-disclosing continuity | both named at the first moment of undertaking restraint |
| `citta` | Consciousness | named with manifestation as producing non-disclosing continuity |
| `prātimokṣa / prātimokṣa-saṃvara` | first-moment designation / restraint across moments | both apply initially; only the second continues in later moments |
| `karmapatha / maula / pṛṣṭha` | action-course / fundamental phase / following phase | explains why the initial and later moments receive different names |

## 8. Logical Determination

Restraint receives four names, each with a stated ground. At the first
moment of undertaking, manifestation and non-disclosing continuity are
called *prātimokṣa* and an action-course; in later moments the restraint
continues without those first-moment designations. The following phase
is distinct from the fundamental action-course.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya explains *śīla* through abstention from improper actions, good conduct through learned praise, action through activity, and restraint through containing body and speech. In response to the objection that *avijñapti* is non-action, it distinguishes the prevented evil deed from the restraint itself, which is produced through manifestation and consciousness. “Others” explain it as cause and result of activity. The first-moment manifestation and non-disclosing continuity are called *prātimokṣa*; the later continuing state is *prātimokṣasaṃvara*, not *prātimokṣa* strictly. It is called an action-course insofar as it belongs to the fundamental phase.

### Textual Notes

The Bhāṣya unit runs from [207|07] through [207|21] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The next question begins at [207|22]
and belongs to 4.17.

The Kārikā witness reads *prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ*. The running Bhāṣya
citation has *prātimokṣakiyāpathaḥ*, while the explanatory prose uses
*karmapatha*. The word-formation sentence at [207|19] is rendered closely
in the Bhāṣya study, with its grammatical force left provisional. The
witness's `kṣaṇeṣui` at [207|20–21] is normalized as *kṣaṇeṣu*.

### Interpretation

The Bhāṣya does not treat restraint as mere inactivity. It is
non-action with respect to the evil deed it prevents, but activity with
respect to the restraint's own production. The distinction lets the
commentary call restraint *karma* without claiming that it is the
prohibited deed.

For the Organon, this supports reading restraint as a positively
undertaken practical determination. Cetanā is not named in this unit;
connecting the undertaking to the project's Cetanā framework is an
Organon interpretation, not the Bhāṣya's wording here.
