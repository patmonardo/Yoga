# VAK 4.122 Bhāṣya — Abstention and Purified Discipline

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
dauḥśīlyam aśubhaṃ rūpaṃ śīlaṃ tadviratir dvidhā /
pratikṣiptāc ca buddhena viśuddhaṃ tu caturguṇam // 4.122 //
```

> Misconduct is unwholesome Form; discipline is abstention from it in two
> modes, and also abstention from what the Buddha has prohibited. Purified
> discipline, however, has four qualities.

The Bhāṣya first explains the two modes of abstention, then quotes 4.123a
to identify the four qualities of purified discipline.

## 3. Continuous Scientific English Rendering

The discipline-based basis of meritorious action is to be explained. It
is stated:

“Misconduct is unwholesome Form; discipline is abstention from it in two
modes.”

For unwholesome Form is called misconduct; therefore abstention is
discipline. This abstention is twofold: that by which one refrains,
through manifestation, and that abstention itself, which is
non-disclosing continuity.

Discipline is not abstention only from misconduct. What else?

“And from what has been prohibited by the Buddha.”

Even what is not misconduct by nature but has been prohibited by the
Blessed Buddha—such as eating at an improper time—is also a basis for
twofold abstention as discipline. For one who has undertaken the
training, misconduct arises from acting contrary to it.

“Purified, however, is fourfold.”

That discipline has four qualities; the reverse is impure. How is it
fourfold? The verse says:

“Not harmed by misconduct and its causes; resting on its counteragent
and on peace.” [4.123a]

It is not harmed by misconduct; it is not harmed by its causes either,
such as greed and other afflictions and secondary afflictions. It rests
on the counteragent to misconduct because it is grounded in the
establishments of mindfulness. It rests on peace and not on a particular
rebirth because it is directed toward nirvāṇa.

Other teachers say that it is purified in five ways: by the root
action-courses, by the adjacent action-courses, not harmed by thoughts,
protected by mindfulness, and directed toward nirvāṇa.

Other teachers say that discipline is fourfold: discipline maintained
from fear of livelihood, praise, punishment, or a bad destination;
aspirational discipline produced by craving for existence, enjoyment,
and honor; discipline in conformity with the awakening factors, held by
those with right view for liberation; and purified discipline, discipline
without influx, because it is stainless. The discussion of discipline
is concluded.

## 4. Distinctions in the Bhāṣya

| Classification | Members or force |
|---|---|
| Modes of abstention | Manifestation (`vijñapti`) and non-disclosing continuity (`avijñapti`) |
| Objects of abstention | Misconduct by nature; conduct prohibited within an undertaken training |
| Four qualities of purified discipline | Not harmed by misconduct; not harmed by its causes; grounded in its counteragent; resting on peace and directed toward nirvāṇa |
| Alternative fivefold account | Root and adjacent action-courses; not harmed by thoughts; protected by mindfulness; directed toward nirvāṇa |
| Alternative fourfold account | Fear-based; aspirational; aligned with awakening factors; purified discipline without influx |

The twofold mode of restraint, four qualities of purified discipline, and
other teachers' four types are distinct classifications.

## 5. Interpretation

The Bhāṣya defines discipline as abstention, not simply as the absence
of an action. Its two modes preserve the distinction between
manifestation and non-disclosing continuity. It also distinguishes
misconduct by nature from an act that becomes misconduct for someone
who has undertaken a training and violates its prohibition.

The four qualities describe the conditions supporting purified discipline;
they should not be collapsed into the alternative fivefold or fourfold
accounts attributed to other teachers. The inclusion of the 4.123a
quotation makes the relation explicit: 4.122 announces four qualities,
and the following half-verse supplies their names.
