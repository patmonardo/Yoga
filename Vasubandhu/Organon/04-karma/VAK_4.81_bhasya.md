# VAK 4.81 Bhāṣya — Volition and Simultaneous Courses of Action

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> yugapad yāvad aṣṭābhir aśubhaiḥ saha vartate /
> cetanā daśabhir yāvac chubhair naikāṣṭapañcabhiḥ // 4.81 //

> Volition occurs simultaneously with up to eight unwholesome Courses of
> Action; with up to ten wholesome Courses of Action, but not with one, eight,
> or five.

The verse asks how many Courses of Action can occur together with one
volition. The Bhāṣya constructs the permitted numbers from concrete
configurations rather than treating the ten Courses of Action as freely combinable
items.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The discussion returns to the Courses of Action and asks how many can occur
simultaneously with one arising volition.

Volition occurs with up to eight unwholesome Courses of Action. It occurs
with one when covetousness or another mental Course of Action becomes
manifest alone, or when, through preparation by a person whose consciousness
is not presently afflicted, one Form-based Course of Action reaches
completion.

It occurs with two when a person with a hostile consciousness kills a living
being, or when a person governed by covetousness commits taking what is not
given, sexual misconduct, or frivolous speech.

It occurs with three when a person with a hostile consciousness
simultaneously kills another person's living being and takes it away. Would
taking what is not given, then, no longer necessarily reach completion
through greed? That rule applies when the agent has no different state of
consciousness at completion. Three also occur when a person governed by
covetousness or another mental Course of Action causes two Form-based Courses
of Action to reach completion through the corresponding preparation.

It occurs with four when a person intent on division utters false speech or
harsh speech. There is then one mental Course of Action and three verbal
Courses of Action. Four also occur when a person under covetousness or
another mental Course of Action causes three other Form-based Courses of
Action to reach completion through the corresponding preparation.
Combinations with five, six, and seven are constructed in the same manner.

Volition occurs with eight when someone has prepared six Courses of Action
and, personally committing sexual misconduct, causes all of them to reach
completion together. So much for unwholesome Courses of Action.

With wholesome Courses of Action, volition occurs with up to ten. Having
stated this general rule, the verse supplies an exception: not with one,
eight, or five.

It occurs with two in the five wholesome sensory Cognitions and, in the
formless attainments, with the Knowledges of Exhaustion and Non-arising. It
occurs with three in a Mind-Cognition conjoined with right view. It occurs
with four when a person's present consciousness is unwholesome or
indeterminate and they undertake the restraint of a lay follower or novice.
It occurs with six when that restraint is undertaken while one of the five
sensory Cognitions is wholesome.

It occurs with seven when that restraint is undertaken in a wholesome
Mind-Cognition, and also when a person with unwholesome or indeterminate
consciousness undertakes monastic restraint. It occurs with nine when
restraint is undertaken with one of the five wholesome sensory Cognitions;
in a Mind-Cognition conjoined with the Knowledges of Exhaustion or
Non-arising; and in that same Mind-Cognition when it belongs to Dhyāna. In
other wholesome Mind-Cognition, volition occurs with ten when monastic
restraint is undertaken.

Every volition accompanying Dhyāna restraint or without-Influx restraint
also belongs here, apart from the Knowledges of Exhaustion and Non-arising.
Outside complete restraint, however, volition can occur with one Course of
Action when a person in another state of consciousness undertakes abstention
from a single limb. It can also occur with five or eight Courses of Action
in wholesome Mind-Cognition when two-limbed or five-limbed abstention is
undertaken at once.

## 4. The Question Is Event-level Conjunction

The question is not how many Courses of Action a person possesses across a
lifetime. It asks how many occur `yugapat`, simultaneously, with one presently
arisen volition. Three layers must therefore be kept apart:

```text
endowment across the continuum
present manifestation of a Course of Action
simultaneous conjunction within one volition-event
```

The Bhāṣya counts Courses of Action at the moment of completion. Preparation
may have been distributed across time or delegated to others; several
prepared Courses of Action can nevertheless complete together under the
relevant volition.

## 5. Unwholesome Conjunctions: One Through Eight

The unwholesome series is constructive. Vasubandhu supplies configurations
for one, two, three, four, and eight, then directs the reader to compose five,
six, and seven analogously.

| Number | Illustrative structure |
|---:|---|
| 1 | one mental Course of Action alone, or one Form-based Course of Action completing from prior preparation |
| 2 | ill will plus killing; or covetousness plus one specified bodily or verbal Course of Action |
| 3 | ill will plus killing and taking; or one mental Course of Action plus two Form-based completions |
| 4 | one mental and three verbal Courses of Action, or one mental plus three Form-based completions |
| 5–7 | constructed by extending the same completion logic |
| 8 | six prepared Courses of Action plus two Courses of Action involved in personally performed sexual misconduct |

The objection about taking what is not given is significant. A Course of
Action normally said to complete from greed can complete while hostility is
the agent's present governing consciousness. Vasubandhu limits the greed rule
to a case in which the consciousness has not changed by the time of
completion. The root associated with a normal completion and the actual
consciousness at a complex simultaneous completion must not be mechanically
identified.

No configuration of nine or ten unwholesome Courses of Action is admitted
here. The different bodily, verbal, and mental requirements prevent total
simultaneous composition.

## 6. Wholesome Conjunctions: The Regular Series

The wholesome series is organized by Cognition and restraint. Its regular
numbers are:

```text
2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10
```

The changing total records the combination of present wholesome Cognition
with abstentions acquired through a particular restraint undertaking. A
wholesome Mind-Cognition with right view supplies three mental Courses of
Action. Lay or novice restraint adds four abstentions; monastic restraint
supplies seven. The five sensory Cognitions and the special Knowledges alter
which mental Courses of Action can be present, producing the stated totals.

Thus the number is not a degree on a single moral scale. It is the cardinality
of a configuration generated by:

```text
kind of consciousness
    + Courses of Action present in that Cognition
    + restraint being undertaken or already accompanying it
```

## 7. Why One, Five, and Eight Reappear

The kārikā excludes wholesome totals of one, five, and eight under the
general, fully classified restraint pattern. The end of the Bhāṣya then
supplies bounded exceptions:

- **one** occurs outside a complete restraint when a single abstention is
  undertaken while the agent's concurrent consciousness has another character;
- **five** can occur when two abstention limbs are undertaken together in a
  wholesome Mind-Cognition;
- **eight** can occur when five abstention limbs are undertaken together in
  a wholesome Mind-Cognition.

This is not a cancellation of the verse. The Bhāṣya marks a change in scope
with `saṃvaranirmuktena tu`, “but apart from restraint.” The verse states the
regular combinations within the established restraint classes; partial
undertakings permit the otherwise excluded totals.

## 8. Volition as Practical Unity

`Cetanā`, volition, is counted alongside the Courses of Action with which it
occurs, yet it is not merely another item in their heap. One volition
coordinates a structured event in which several determinations can complete
simultaneously.

```text
one volition
    → preparation of several possible Courses of Action
    → bodily, verbal, and mental determinations
    → one completion-event with a constrained cardinality
```

This makes the passage practically agential. The unity lies in volitional
organization; the plurality lies in the Courses of Action that attain their determinate
completion through it.

## 9. Interpretation

For the Organon, 4.81 supplies a combinatorial grammar of action. A Program
Feature should not reduce one volition to one output. It must admit one
governing practical determination whose preparations produce several
coordinated completions:

```text
PracticalEvent =
    <Volition,
     Preparations,
     FormCoursesOfAction,
     MentalCoursesOfAction,
     CompletionRelation,
     RestraintContext>
```

The permitted cardinality is derived from the relations among these fields.
It is not an arbitrary validation constant. The Bhāṣya demonstrates the
method: construct each allowed total from Cognition, restraint, preparation,
and completion; then state the exceptional scope explicitly.

Vasubandhu's analysis enumerates jointly occurring Courses of Action, the
relevant Cognitions, and the restraint classes; the event-schema makes their
constrained conjunction explicit.
