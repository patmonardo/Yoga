# VAK_4.63 — Two Alternative Allocations of Black Karma

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अन्ये नरकवेद्यान्यकामवेद्यं द्वयं विदुः ।
दृग्धेयं कृष्णमन्येऽन्यत्कृष्णशुक्लं तु कामजम् ॥ ४.६३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
anye narakavedyānyakāmavedyaṃ dvayaṃ viduḥ /
dṛgdheyaṃ kṛṣṇam anye 'nyat kṛṣṇaśuklaṃ tu kāmajam // 4.63 //
```

Word division:

```iast
anye naraka-vedyam anya-kāma-vedyam dvayam viduḥ /
dṛk-dheyam kṛṣṇam anye anyat kṛṣṇa-śuklam tu kāma-jam //
```

The verse records two alternative interpretations by other teachers. Neither
silently replaces the principal classification of VAK 4.60.

**`anyakāmavedya`**

The Bhāṣya specifies karma whose maturation is experienced in a Desire Realm
destination other than hell.

**`dṛgdheya`**

This is the compact equivalent of `darśanaprahātavya`, what is to be abandoned
by seeing.

**`anyat`**

The Bhāṣya identifies the remainder as Desire Realm karma to be abandoned by
cultivation, `bhāvanāprahātavya`.

## 4. Grammar

**First alternative**

```text
anye
dvayam
naraka-vedyam
anya-kāma-vedyam [ca]
yathā-kramam [kṛṣṇaṃ kṛṣṇaśuklaṃ ca]
viduḥ
```

> Other teachers understand the pair as karma to be experienced in hell and
> karma to be experienced elsewhere in the Desire Realm, respectively.

The labels black and black-and-white are supplied from the established topic
and correlated in order by the Bhāṣya.

**Second alternative**

```text
anye
dṛk-dheyam [karma]
kṛṣṇam [viduḥ]

anyat tu kāma-jam [karma]
kṛṣṇa-śuklam [viduḥ]
```

> Still others call karma abandoned by seeing black; the remaining
> Desire Realm karma is black-and-white.

The repeated verb is understood. `Anyat` contrasts the cultivation-abandoned
remainder with the seeing-abandoned class.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**First alternative: maturation-field**

```text
hell
    only unwholesome karma matures
    → hell-result karma called black

other Desire Realm destinations
    wholesome and unwholesome karma mature
    → their result-field called black-and-white
```

The predicate is assigned through the composition of the maturation-field.

**Second alternative: mode of abandonment**

```text
darśanaprahātavya
    unmixed with wholesome karma
    → black

bhāvanāprahātavya
    includes wholesome and unwholesome karma
    → black-and-white
```

The predicate is assigned through the Path relation governing abandonment.

**Status of the alternatives**

The Bhāṣya attributes both accounts to other teachers:

```text
anye ācāryāḥ
anye punar āhuḥ
```

They are preserved as alternative taxonomies rather than silently merged with
VAK 4.60.

**Same vocabulary, different intension**

```text
black by ethical character
black by maturation-field
black by abandonment-mode
```

The extension and Meaning of the term change with the classificatory ground.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Classification requires a typed ground**

```text
TERM + GROUND_OF_DIVISION
    → DETERMINATE_MEANING
```

The term alone underdetermines the taxonomy.

**Principal and alternative classifications**

```text
PRINCIPAL_4_60(K)
    grounds color in ethical character and continuum-relative intermixture

ALTERNATIVE_A(K)
    grounds color in maturation-field

ALTERNATIVE_B(K)
    grounds color in mode of abandonment
```

These are different functions over overlapping karmic material.

**No inference from shared label to identity**

```text
BLACK_A(K)
    and BLACK_B(K)
    does not entail
the two classifications have identical intension
```

**Dhātu and Path as distinct classificatory grounds**

```text
field-based classification
    → where and with what other results karma matures

Path-based classification
    → how the karmic determination is abandoned
```

The same karma-field can be discriminated through either relation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The first group correlates the pair with black and
black-and-white karma, respectively. In hell, only unwholesome karma matures;
elsewhere in the Desire Realm, both wholesome and unwholesome karma mature. The
second group calls karma abandoned by the Path of Seeing black because it is unmixed
with wholesome karma. The remaining Desire Realm karma is abandoned through
cultivation and is black-and-white because it comprises both wholesome and
unwholesome karma. The first teachers correlate the pair with black and
black-and-white karma: in hell only unwholesome karma matures, whereas elsewhere in
the Desire Realm both wholesome and unwholesome karma mature. The other teachers
call the karma abandoned by seeing black because it is unmixed with wholesome karma;
the remaining Desire Realm karma is abandoned by cultivation and contains both
classes.

The grounds supplied for both alternatives and the identification of the
remainder are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

**Abhidharma is a science of controlled discrimination**

The verse does not leave us with arbitrary polysemy. It shows how different
schools select different lawful grounds:

```text
ethical nature
continuum composition
maturation-field
mode of abandonment
```

Every classification must state which relation it is tracking.

**Meaning is relationally generated**

The term “black” does not carry one self-sufficient semantic object through
all contexts. Its determinate Meaning arises from the classificatory operation
in which it functions. This is exactly why translation must preserve the
ground rather than merely standardize the noun.

**Completion of the black-and-white block**

VAK 4.59–4.63 has moved through:

```text
afflictive genesis of practical predicates
    → four karmic colors
    → continuum versus intrinsic predication
    → typed path-operations of exhaustion
    → alternative grounds of classification
```

The thread ends not with another object but with the method for determining
the objects of karmic science.

**Rational and Practical Karma**

Rational discrimination identifies the correct ground of classification;
Practical discrimination identifies the causal or Path-operation tracked by
that ground. The two remain distinguishable while functioning together in
Abhidharma application.

This is the Organon contact. The alternative teachers, maturation-field
arguments, and abandonment-mode arguments belong to the Bhāṣya; the explicit
Rational/Practical formulation is project synthesis.

**Former Philosophical Translation.**
> A classificatory predicate receives determinate Meaning only through its
> ground of division. “Black” can name karma by intrinsic ethical quality, by
> the field in which its maturation occurs, or by the Path-operation through
> which it is abandoned. These uses need not be confused or reduced to one
> definition. The shared word remains controlled by the relation that licenses
> its application in each doctrinal presentation.

The verse therefore closes the block by reflecting the method of Abhidharma
classification back upon its own terminology.
