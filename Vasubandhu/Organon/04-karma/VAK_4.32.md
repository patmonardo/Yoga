# VAK_4.32

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
बुद्धसङ्घकरान् धर्मानशैक्षानुभयांश्च सः ।
निर्वाणं चेति शरणं यो याति शरणत्रयम् ॥ ४.३२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
buddhasaṃghakarān dharmān aśaikṣān ubhayāṃś ca saḥ /
nirvāṇaṃ ceti śaraṇaṃ yo yāti śaraṇatrayam // 4.32 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
buddha-saṃgha-karān dharmān aśaikṣān ubhayān ca saḥ /
nirvāṇam ca iti śaraṇam yaḥ yāti śaraṇa-trayam //
```

The compound is distributed by the Bhāṣya. Buddha-refuge concerns the
`aśaikṣa` dharmas that make one a Buddha; Saṅgha-refuge concerns both
`śaikṣa` and `aśaikṣa` dharmas that make the noble Saṅgha; Dharma-refuge
concerns nirvāṇa.

| `buddhasaṃghakarān` | accusative masculine plural compound | making or constituting Buddha and Saṅgha |
| `dharmān` | accusative masculine plural | dharmas; determinate factors |
| `aśaikṣān` | accusative masculine plural | belonging to one beyond training |
| `ubhayān` | accusative masculine plural | both kinds: `śaikṣa` and `aśaikṣa` |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `saḥ` | nominative masculine singular pronoun | that person; he |
| `nirvāṇam` | accusative neuter singular | nirvāṇa; cessation |

| `yāti` | third-person singular present from `√yā` | goes; approaches |
| `śaraṇatrayam` | accusative neuter singular | the triad of refuges; the Three Refuges |

**Distribution of the compressed compound**

The Bhāṣya resolves the verse as three objects:

```text
Buddha-refuge
    aśaikṣa buddhakara dharmas

Saṅgha-refuge
    both śaikṣa and aśaikṣa saṃghakara dharmas

Dharma-refuge
    nirvāṇa
```

`Aśaikṣān` belongs specifically to the Buddha-making dharmas. `Ubhayān`
belongs to the Saṅgha-making dharmas and includes both those still in training
and those beyond training.

**`-kara`**

Compound-final `kara`, from `√kṛ`, means making, producing, or constituting.
The Bhāṣya insists on this exact force:

```text
aśaikṣa dharmas alone are the Buddha
    not asserted

aśaikṣa dharmas make one a Buddha
    asserted
```

The difference prevents a constitutive relation from being reduced to bare
identity.

**`aśaikṣa` and `śaikṣa`**

```text
śaikṣa
    one still training on the noble Path

aśaikṣa
    one beyond further training
```

The Buddha-refuge concerns completed awakening. The Saṅgha-refuge includes
the Path-dharmas of both groups among the eight noble persons.

**`śaraṇa`**

The Bhāṣya defines its Meaning directly:

```text
trāṇārthaḥ śaraṇārthaḥ
```

> The meaning of refuge is protection.

The protection is not limited to immediate security; reliance upon the Three
Refuges culminates in complete release from suffering.

## 4. Grammar

**Relative-correlative frame**

```text
yaḥ śaraṇa-trayam yāti
saḥ ... śaraṇam [yāti]
```

`Yaḥ` introduces the person who goes to the Three Refuges; `saḥ` resumes that
person in the main clause. The finite verb `yāti` serves both the general act
and the distributed refuge-objects.

**Buddha-refuge**

```text
saḥ aśaikṣān buddha-karān dharmān śaraṇam yāti
```

> That person goes for refuge to the aśaikṣa dharmas that make one a Buddha.

The accusative plural expressions agree. The Bhāṣya identifies these as
`kṣayajñāna` and its associated dharmas: determinations through whose
predominance the support is called Buddha and through whose acquisition there
is capacity for complete awakening.

**Saṅgha-refuge**

```text
saḥ ubhayān saṃgha-karān dharmān śaraṇam yāti
```

> That person goes for refuge to both kinds of dharmas that make the Saṅgha.

`Ubhayān` supplies the pair omitted by compression: `śaikṣa` and `aśaikṣa`.

**Dharma-refuge**

```text
saḥ nirvāṇam ca iti śaraṇam yāti
```

> And that person goes to nirvāṇa as the Dharma-refuge.

The Bhāṣya specifies `pratisaṃkhyānirodha`, cessation through discriminative
comprehension.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Whoever goes to the Three Refuges takes as refuge the aśaikṣa dharmas that
> make a Buddha, both kinds of dharmas that make the Saṅgha, and nirvāṇa.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Whoever goes to the Three Refuges takes refuge in the aśaikṣa dharmas that
> constitute one as a Buddha, especially knowledge of exhaustion and its
> associated factors; in the śaikṣa and aśaikṣa Path-dharmas through which the
> eight noble persons constitute the Saṅgha; and in nirvāṇa, specifically
> cessation through discriminative comprehension, as the Dharma-refuge.

The examples `kṣayajñāna`, the eight noble persons, and
`pratisaṃkhyānirodha` are Bhāṣya determinations. “Constitute” renders `-kara`
without turning the constituting dharmas into a complete identity-definition
of Buddha or Saṅgha.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Refuge passes through the visible bearer to the liberating determinations
> through which that bearer is what it is. The Buddha is neither reduced to a
> physical body nor identified without remainder with an abstract collection:
> the completed Path-dharmas constitute the embodied support as Buddha. The
> noble Saṅgha is constituted through the Path-dharmas acquired by trainees
> and those beyond training. The Dharma-refuge terminates in nirvāṇa itself,
> the discriminatively attained pacification of defilement and suffering.

This does not deny the Buddha or Saṅgha as bearers. It preserves the explicit
Bhāṣya distinction between the support and the dharmas that constitute its
liberating determination.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`buddhakara-dharma`**

A Buddha-making dharma is an aśaikṣa determination through whose predominance
the personal support is called Buddha, or through whose acquisition the
capacity for complete awakening is attained. The principal example is
`kṣayajñāna` together with its associated dharmas.

**`saṃghakara-dharma`**

These are the śaikṣa and aśaikṣa Path-dharmas through whose acquisition the
eight noble persons become the noble Saṅgha. Conventional aggregation alone
is not the strict refuge-object described here.

**All Buddhas and all Saṅghas**

The Bhāṣya asks whether one takes refuge in one Buddha or all Buddhas, and in
one Saṅgha or all Saṅghas. In both cases the answer is: all, by defining
characteristic (`lakṣaṇataḥ`), because the Path is not different in its
defining characteristic.

This is universality of Path-character, not numerical fusion of personal
continuities.

**`kṣayajñāna`**

Knowledge of exhaustion is an aśaikṣa knowledge belonging to completed
liberation. Here it is named as the leading Buddha-making determination, not
as the whole Buddha considered apart from the support and its associated
factors.

**`pratisaṃkhyānirodha`**

The Dharma-refuge is nirvāṇa as cessation through discriminative
comprehension. The Bhāṣya grounds its unity in a single characteristic:

```text
pacification of one’s own defilements and suffering
pacification of others’ defilements and suffering
    → one characteristic: peace
```

**`āśraya`**

`Āśraya` is the support upon which the Buddha-making dharmas depend. The
Vaibhāṣikas use this relation to explain why maliciously causing the Tathāgata
to bleed damages what depends upon that support and constitutes an immediate
offense.

Vasubandhu’s more basic reply is textual: the treatise never asserted that
the aśaikṣa dharmas alone are the Buddha.

**`vāgvijñapti`**

The Bhāṣya asks for the intrinsic nature of acts of going for refuge and
answers:

```text
śaraṇagamana
    has verbal manifestation as its intrinsic nature
```

Refuge is therefore an enacted verbal determination, not merely an unspoken
sentiment or private belief.

**`trāṇa` and `dvārabhūta`**

`Trāṇa` is protection, the Meaning of refuge. `Dvārabhūta` means functioning
as a gateway. The acts of going for refuge are the gateway to undertaking all
restraints because they orient the practical agent toward the liberating
ground and end of discipline.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Constitutive relation without identity collapse**

```text
support
    bears the determinations

constituting dharmas
    make the support Buddha or Saṅgha

therefore
    bearer is not external to the determinations
    bearer is not numerically identical with them alone
```

Vasubandhu tests the false identity explicitly. If the Buddha were simply
the aśaikṣa dharmas, the Buddha would cease to be Buddha whenever an ordinary
mind was present. Likewise, a noble person would cease to belong to the
Saṅgha whenever the Path-state was not manifest. The analogous claim that
morality itself is the monk is equally unacceptable.

**Three refuge-objects**

```text
Buddha
    completed Buddha-making dharmas

Saṅgha
    trainee and beyond-training Saṅgha-making dharmas

Dharma
    nirvāṇa
```

The first two relations are constitutive; the third identifies the strict
Dharma-refuge with cessation.

**One defining characteristic across many bearers**

```text
many Buddhas
    one defining Path-character

many Saṅghas
    one defining Path-character
```

Refuge reaches all by characteristic. Universal determination does not erase
the plurality of the continuities in which it is realized.

**Intrinsic nature and Meaning**

```text
svabhāva of going for refuge
    vāgvijñapti

artha of refuge
    trāṇa

final efficacy
    complete release from suffering
```

The manifested act, its Meaning, and its liberating result occupy different
logical levels.

**Gateway relation**

```text
verbal taking of refuge
    → instituted orientation
    → gateway to undertaking restraint
```

The refuge analysis therefore belongs internally to Karma-nirdeśa. It
determines the manifest act through which entry into the restraint-system is
made possible.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Determination constitutes the bearer**

This passage offers an unusually exact account of constitution:

```text
Buddha-making dharmas
    are not an accidental property of the Buddha

Buddha-making dharmas
    are not an isolated substance called “the Buddha”

they are the determinations
    through which this support is Buddha
```

The Bhāṣya itself supplies the safeguards against both extremes. The physical
body is not sufficient, since it does not exhibit the relevant difference
before and after awakening. Yet the support is not abolished, since the text
says “Buddha-making dharmas,” not “those dharmas alone are the Buddha.”

**Refuge follows the truth of the bearer**

The movement is not from a concrete person to a supposedly more real hidden
object. It is from immediate appearance to explicit determination:

```text
visible Buddha
    → determinations through which the support is Buddha

visible community
    → Path-dharmas through which it is noble Saṅgha

spoken Dharma
    → nirvāṇa as the cessation it communicates and serves
```

The bearer remains; refuge apprehends it according to its Path-significance.

**Verbal manifestation returns to the center**

VAK 4.32 brings the refuge discussion back to `vijñapti`. Going for refuge is
not defined as a concealed interior state but as `vāgvijñapti`, a verbal act
that makes the practical orientation manifest and operative.

```text
orientation toward liberation
    becomes manifest in speech

manifest refuge
    becomes the gateway to restraint
```

This does not deny intention. It specifies the intrinsic nature of the
technical act of refuge within the practical sequence.

**Protection is liberation-directed**

The Meaning of refuge is not safety supplied by an external protector. It is
protection through reliance upon Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha, culminating in
complete release from suffering. The cited contrast with mountains, forests,
trees, and shrines rejects merely fear-driven shelter in favor of refuge
joined to seeing the Four Noble Truths and following the noble Path.

This is the restrained Organon contact: constitutive determination, manifest
practical institution, and liberation-directed Meaning. No independent
governing intelligence or transcendent bearer is introduced.
