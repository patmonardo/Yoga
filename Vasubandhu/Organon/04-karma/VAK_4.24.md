# VAK_4.24

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
निवृतानिवृताभ्यां च नातीताभ्यां समन्वितः ।
असंवरो दुश्चरितं दौःशील्यं कर्म तत्पथः ॥ ४.२४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
nivṛtānivṛtābhyāṃ ca nātītābhyāṃ samanvitaḥ /
asaṃvaro duścaritaṃ dauḥśīlyaṃ karma tatpathaḥ // 4.24 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
nivṛta-anivṛtābhyām ca na atītābhyām samanvitaḥ /
asaṃvaraḥ duścaritam dauḥśīlyam karma tat-pathaḥ //
```

The prepared Bhāṣya witness begins with the second half of the kārikā. The
commentary on the first half has therefore been read directly from the local
primary Sanskrit witness and is identified as Bhāṣya material below.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
nivṛta-anivṛtābhyām
ca
na
atītābhyām
samanvitaḥ

asaṃvaraḥ
duścaritam
dauḥśīlyam
karma
tat-pathaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `nivṛtānivṛtābhyām` | instrumental dual | with the obscured and unobscured [neutral vijñaptis] |
| `ca` | conjunction | and; further |
| `na` | negation | not |
| `atītābhyām` | instrumental dual | with the past forms of those two |
| `samanvitaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | endowed with; possessed of |
| `asaṃvaraḥ` | nominative masculine singular | non-restraint |
| `duścaritam` | nominative neuter singular | bad conduct; misconduct |
| `dauḥśīlyam` | nominative neuter singular | bad discipline; opposition to śīla |
| `karma` | nominative neuter singular | action; karma |
| `tatpathaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | its path; the corresponding action-course |

**The omitted `avyākṛta`**

The Bhāṣya expands the first compound as:

```text
nivṛta-avyākṛta
    obscured-indeterminate

anivṛta-avyākṛta
    unobscured-indeterminate
```

Thus `nivṛta` and `anivṛta` do not oppose restraint and non-restraint. They
differentiate two kinds of ethically indeterminate or neutral vijñapti.

**`asaṃvara`**

```text
a-saṃvara
    non-restraint
```

The privative prefix does not name simple absence. The middle position from
VAK 4.21 already supplies the case of one established in neither restraint nor
non-restraint. `Asaṃvara` is a positive acquired contrary determination.

**`duścarita`**

```text
dus + carita
    badly conducted
    misconduct
```

The Bhāṣya grounds this name in censure by good people and in the production
of undesirable fruit.

**`dauḥśīlya`**

`Dauḥśīlya` is the condition opposed to `śīla`. “Bad discipline” preserves
that explicit relation better than a generic moral label.

**`tatpatha`**

The Bhāṣya explains “its path” as `karmapatha`, path of action, because the
determination is included in the fundamental or principal phase (`maula`) of
the action-course.

## 4. Grammar

**Completion of the vijñapti rule**

```text
na [kaścit]
atītābhyām
nivṛta-anivṛta-[avyākṛtābhyām vijñaptibhyām]
samanvitaḥ
```

> No one is endowed with past obscured-indeterminate or
> unobscured-indeterminate vijñapti.

The subject “no one” and the nouns `avyākṛta` and `vijñapti` are supplied by
the Bhāṣya. The dual morphology covers both kinds.

**Five co-referential predicates**

```text
asaṃvaraḥ
duścaritam
dauḥśīlyam
karma
tat-pathaḥ
```

The change of grammatical gender does not multiply the referents. Vasubandhu
calls the expressions `paryāyaśabdāḥ`, alternative designations of the same
non-restraint complex.

**Grounds of predication**

The prose assigns a distinct ground to each name:

```text
asaṃvara
    because body and speech are not restrained

duścarita
    because it is censured and yields undesirable fruit

dauḥśīlya
    because it is opposed to śīla

karma
    because it is bodily and verbal action

karmapatha
    because it belongs to the fundamental action-course
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> And no one is endowed with the past obscured-indeterminate or
> unobscured-indeterminate vijñaptis. Non-restraint is [also called]
> misconduct, bad discipline, action, and the corresponding path of action.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> No one remains endowed with past manifest action of either neutral kind,
> obscured or unobscured, because the acquisition of such a weak dharma does
> not continue. Non-restraint is also called misconduct because it is censured
> and produces undesirable fruit, bad discipline because it opposes śīla,
> karma because it is bodily and verbal action, and a path of action because it
> belongs to the fundamental phase of the karmic course.

The causal explanation and the grounds for the alternative names are supplied
by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Manifest action does not acquire the same temporal persistence in every
> ethical class: past neutral vijñapti, whether obscured or unobscured, is too
> weakly acquired to remain attached to the continuum. Non-restraint, by
> contrast, is not empty lack but an organized contrary practical form. The
> same determination can be named from its failure to restrain, its censured
> conduct, its opposition to discipline, its nature as bodily-verbal action,
> and its place in the fundamental action-course.

“Organized practical form” is an interpretive expression. The source itself
gives the alternative names and their distinct explanatory grounds.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`nivṛtāvyākṛta`**

Obscured-indeterminate action is ethically neutral yet associated with an
obscuring condition. The verse concerns past manifest action of this class,
not every obscured-neutral dharma.

**`anivṛtāvyākṛta`**

Unobscured-indeterminate action is neutral without that obscuring condition.
Its past vijñapti is likewise not possessed.

**Weak acquisition**

The recovered Bhāṣya gives the rule:

```text
weak dharma
    → weak acquisition
    → acquisition does not remain attached
```

When asked why the dharma is weak, Vasubandhu answers that it is made by
consciousness (`cittakṛta`).

**`jaḍā` and `paratantrā`**

An interlocutor could object that an obscured-neutral consciousness should
then also lack continuing acquisition. Vasubandhu distinguishes vijñapti:

```text
vijñapti
    jaḍā       inert or insentient
    paratantrā dependent upon another

consciousness
    is not so in the same way
```

A vijñapti produced by a weak consciousness is weaker still. This is the
Bhāṣya’s reason that its past neutral forms are not possessed.

**`paryāyaśabda`**

The five expressions are alternative names. Their co-reference does not make
their meanings interchangeable in every context, because each predicate is
licensed by a different ground.

**`maulasaṃgṛhīta`**

To be included in the fundamental phase is to belong to the principal
completion of an action-course rather than merely its preparation or aftermath.
This is why the non-restraint complex can be called a `karmapatha`.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Occurrence does not guarantee past possession**

VAK 4.23 gave the general rule that, after the first moment, vijñapti may be
possessed as past. VAK 4.24 restricts it:

```text
past wholesome or unwholesome vijñapti
    may be possessed subject to non-abandonment

past neutral vijñapti
    obscured or unobscured
    is not possessed
```

The ethical-causal strength of the determination affects its temporal
attachment.

**Dependency does not mean unreality**

The Bhāṣya calls vijñapti dependent and inert. This explains the weakness of a
neutral manifestation produced by weak consciousness; it does not deny that
the manifest action occurred.

**Determinate negation**

```text
absence of restraint
    may leave a person in the middle position

asaṃvara
    is restraint’s contrary made into
    a standing bodily-verbal karmic determination
```

The prefix `a-` therefore marks a positive contrary here, not bare logical
non-being.

**One determination, several Aspect-predicates**

```text
one asaṃvara complex
    → non-restraint
    → misconduct
    → bad discipline
    → karma
    → karmapatha
```

The identity of the referent and the difference of the grounds must be held
together.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The return to vijñapti gains a limit**

VAK 4.23 returned from avijñapti to vijñapti and established manifestation as
present during production, past afterward, and never possessed as future.
VAK 4.24 now prevents that rule from becoming mechanical. Not every past
manifestation remains acquired. Neutral vijñapti lacks the strength to attach
itself as a continuing past possession.

Thus the return to manifestation is already internally differentiated:

```text
manifest action
    → actual occurrence
    → ethical-causal qualification
    → corresponding strength or weakness of acquisition
```

The temporal predicate depends upon what kind of action has appeared, not
merely upon the passage of its moment.

**The second privative dyad**

The chapter now gives another `a-` opposition:

```text
saṃvara
    restraint

asaṃvara
    determinate non-restraint
```

As with `vijñapti / avijñapti`, the negative member is not nothing. But the
structure is not identical. `Avijñapti` negates manifestation while preserving
karmic efficacy; `asaṃvara` negates restraint by instituting the contrary
practical orientation.

The `madhyastha` proves the positivity of the latter. If `asaṃvara` meant every
mere absence of restraint, no third position would remain. Instead:

```text
saṃvara
    established restraint

asaṃvara
    established contrary orientation

madhyastha
    established in neither
```

The dyadic plane therefore rests within a wider field capable of distinguishing
opposition from simple absence.

**The negative series mirrors the positive series**

VAK 4.16 named the restraint complex through discipline, good conduct, karma,
restraint, and action-course. VAK 4.24 supplies its counter-series:

```text
positive practical series       negative practical series

śīla                            dauḥśīlya
sucarita                        duścarita
saṃvara                         asaṃvara
karma                           karma
karmapatha                      karmapatha
```

The common formal predicates `karma` and `karmapatha` do not neutralize the
ethical opposition. They show that Karma is the Concept capable of
differentiating itself into opposed practical contents.

This confirms the user’s determination from the preceding discussion:

```text
Karma as Concept
    → disciplined action
    → undisciplined action

each becomes manifest and non-manifest
through its own practical course
```

**The Copula distributes predicates by grounds**

The alternative names display the Ideal Copula in a precise classificatory
form. To say “asaṃvara is karma” does not reduce non-restraint to a synonym of
every action. The predication is valid because this complex is bodily and
verbal action. To say it “is karmapatha” is valid because it belongs to the
fundamental phase.

```text
same determination
    + different ground
    → different valid predicate
```

Reciprocation here occurs between the determined complex and its predicates:
the complex licenses each name, while the names disclose distinct Aspects of
what the complex is.

This is an Organon synthesis. Vasubandhu’s explicit teaching is the absence of
past possession for neutral vijñapti and the five grounded designations of
non-restraint.
