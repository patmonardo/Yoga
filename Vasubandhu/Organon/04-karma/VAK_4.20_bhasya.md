# VAK 4.20 Bhāṣya — Temporal Possession Across Three Restraints

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
tathaivāsaṃvarastho 'pi dhyānasaṃvaravān sadā /
atītājātayā āryas tu prathame nābhyatītayā // 4.20 //
```

> One established in non-restraint is likewise [endowed with present
> avijñapti and, after the first moment, with past avijñapti]. One
> possessing meditation restraint is continually endowed with past and
> future avijñapti. A noble person is likewise [endowed with
> uncontaminated avijñapti], but not with past avijñapti in the first
> moment.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
yathā prātimokṣasaṃvarastha uktaḥ:

tathaivāsaṃvarastho 'pi.

asaṃvarastho 'pi yāvad asaṃvaraṃ na tyajati tāvan nityam avijñaptyā
vartamānayā samanvāgataḥ; kṣaṇād ūrdhvam atītayā 'pi.

dhyānasaṃvaravān sadā atītājātayā.

dhyānasaṃvarasya lābhī nityam atītānāgatābhyām avijñaptibhyāṃ
samanvāgataḥ ātyāgāt. prathame hi kṣaṇe sa janmāntaratyaktaṃ
dhyānasaṃvaram atītaṃ labhate.

āryas tu prathame nābhyatītayā.

āryas tu pudgalo 'py evam anāsravyā. ayaṃ tu viśeṣaḥ: sa prathame
kṣaṇe nātītayā samanvāgato mārgasya pūrvam anutpāditatvāt.
```

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The running witness reads
`ātyāgāt` at [209|18–19], printed above as transmitted; the immediately
preceding general rule in 4.19 reads `atyāgāt`. The Kārikā's compressed
`atītājātayā` is explained in prose by
`atītānāgatābhyām avijñaptibhyām`, “past and future avijñaptis.”

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

As the case of one established in prātimokṣa restraint has been stated,
so too is the case of one established in non-restraint. For as long as
such a person does not abandon non-restraint, he is continually endowed
with present avijñapti and, after the first moment, with past avijñapti
as well.

One possessing meditation restraint is continually endowed with past
and future avijñapti. The person who has obtained meditation restraint
is endowed with past and future avijñaptis so long as it has not been
abandoned. For even in the first moment he obtains, as past, meditation
restraint that had been abandoned in another birth.

The noble person, however, is not endowed with past avijñapti in the
first moment. The noble person is similarly endowed with uncontaminated
avijñapti, but with this difference: in the first moment he is not
endowed with past avijñapti, because the Path had not previously arisen.

## 4. The Extension to Non-Restraint

`Tathaiva`, “likewise,” carries the temporal pattern of 4.19 across to
`asaṃvara`. This is an acquired non-restraint, not merely the absence
of a vow. As long as it has not been abandoned, its bearer possesses
present avijñapti; from the next moment onward the bearer also
possesses past avijñapti. The general `atyāga` condition applies to
both restraint and non-restraint. Thus the text analyzes the duration
of a practical determination without treating its ethical value as
the rule for temporal possession.

## 5. Past and Future Meditation Restraint

The prose resolves `atītājātayā` into two temporal modes: **past** and
**future**. Its explanation of past possession is specific. At the
first moment of present attainment, a person can obtain as past a
meditation restraint abandoned in another birth. Past possession is
therefore possible even at the first moment of this attainment.

This should not be translated as a memory of an earlier meditative
experience. The Bhāṣya predicates `samanvāgata`, being endowed with
past avijñapti, under its Abhidharma account of acquisition. Likewise,
`sadā` is bounded by non-abandonment; it does not say meditation
restraint is absolutely eternal.

## 6. The Noble Person's First Moment

The noble person is treated similarly with regard to uncontaminated
restraint, but the first Path moment has no **past** uncontaminated
avijñapti. Vasubandhu supplies the reason: `mārgasya pūrvam
anutpāditatvāt`, the Path had not arisen before. This is the precise
contrast with meditation restraint, whose past instance can be
obtained again. It does not deny the first noble Path moment itself.

The present mode of meditation and uncontaminated restraint is treated
in the next Kārikā half, 4.21. Importing its rule here would blur the
boundary of this unit.

## 7. Organon Contact Point

For our Organon vows, the useful challenge is to keep **normative
content**, **acquisition history**, and **temporal possession** distinct.
The same temporal pattern can apply to restraint and non-restraint,
while two restraints differ because one kind has a previous occurrence
and the first noble Path has none. An Organon account that reads the
vow's rational content directly from its mere duration would fail this
case. The Kośa provides the practical distinctions; our Pure Reason
vows must determine their own grounds without erasing those
distinctions.
