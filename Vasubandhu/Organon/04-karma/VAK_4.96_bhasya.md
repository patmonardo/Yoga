# VAK 4.96 Bhāṣya — Three Obstructions to the Noble Path

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ānantaryāṇi karmāṇi tīvrakleśo 'tha durgatiḥ /
kauravāsaṃjñisattvāś ca matam āvaraṇatrayam // 4.96 //
```

> The immediate-result actions, persistent affliction, and then the bad
> destinies, the Kurus, and non-percipient beings are held to constitute
> the three obstructions.

The verse assigns these examples respectively to obstruction by action,
by affliction, and by resultant existence. The Bhāṣya then specifies what
they obstruct, why *tīvra* matters, and what “immediate” means.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The Blessed One has stated three obstructions: obstruction by action,
obstruction by affliction, and obstruction by maturation. What is their
character?

The five actions whose results immediately follow are obstruction by action:
killing one's mother, killing one's father, killing an arhat, splitting the
Saṅgha, and maliciously drawing blood from the body of a Tathāgata.

Persistent affliction is obstruction by affliction. Affliction is of two
kinds: *tīvra*, meaning recurrent, and *tīkṣṇa*, meaning very strong. It is
the recurrent kind that obstructs, as in the cases the text names. Even an
affliction that surges with great force can be removed if it occurs only
occasionally. A continually recurring complex of afflictions is much
harder to remove. When affliction is constant, the person finds no time to
exert effort against it: a mild occurrence conditions a moderate one, and
the moderate one conditions a stronger one.

The three bad destinies are obstruction by maturation. Certain favorable
births are also included: birth among the people of Uttarakuru and among
non-percipient beings.

What do these obstruct? The noble path and the wholesome roots preparatory
to it. Other actions that fix one in a painful destiny or in other limiting
birth conditions could also have been named. These examples are singled out
because they can be clearly identified through five considerations: their
support, result, destiny, mode of birth, and person.

Of these obstructions, afflictive obstruction is the least severe; action
obstruction comes next. According to the Vaibhāṣikas, the latter two
obstructions can leave a person beyond remedy even in a second birth,
through a succession of further consequences.

What does “immediate-result” mean? One interpretation says that no other
action yielding a result in an intervening birth can interrupt the maturation
of such an action; its result cannot be pushed aside. Alternatively, after
the agent dies there is no interval before going toward birth in hell. The
term names the absence of such an interval for a person connected with the
relevant dharma.

## 4. Three Different Sites of Obstruction

| Obstruction | Site | Specified instance |
|---|---|---|
| `karmāvaraṇa` | completed action | the five immediate-result actions |
| `kleśāvaraṇa` | recurrent affliction | affliction that leaves no interval for its removal |
| `vipākāvaraṇa` | resultant birth | bad destinies, Uttarakuru, non-percipient existence |

The kinds are distinct. An action may already have been completed; an
affliction may continually arise now; a resultant life may lack the
conditions required for the Path. The common predicate “obstruction” is
fixed by what these conditions prevent.

## 5. What Is Obstructed

The Bhāṣya states the criterion explicitly:

```text
the noble path
    and
the wholesome roots preparatory to the noble path
```

This is narrower than “unpleasant existence” or “bad conduct.” Uttarakuru
is counted despite being a favorable human environment. Non-percipient
existence is counted despite its contemplative association. The issue is
whether the relevant existence permits preparation for and entry into the
noble path.

The five examples are selected because their obstruction is readily
discerned by support, result, destiny, birth, or person. The passage does
not say that no other limiting conditions exist.

## 6. Recurrent and Acute Affliction

The distinction between `tīvra` and `tīkṣṇa` carries the argument:

```text
tīkṣṇa — high intensity on an occasion
tīvra  — frequent recurrence with little interval
```

An acute episode may leave time for counteraction. The recurring sequence
occupies the interval in which counteraction would be cultivated:

```text
mild → moderate → strong → renewed affliction
```

The obstruction lies in a temporal pattern, not simply a high value of
intensity. The claim that the afflictive obstruction is the least severe of
the three should therefore not be confused with a claim that recurrent
affliction is easy to overcome.

## 7. The Scope of `Ānantarya`

The commentary gives two explanations:

1. Another action's result cannot intervene and displace the specified
   maturation.
2. The agent's death is followed without an intervening birth by the
   movement toward hell birth.

Both concern the order of karmic result. “Immediate” need not mean that
the visible act is followed instantly, within the same life, by its full
result.

The five named actions are the source's doctrinal examples. Their gravity
is part of the Abhidharma account; the Organon use of this classification
does not require treating every contemporary moral failure as an
`ānantarya` action.

## 8. Relation to VAK 4.95

VAK 4.95 asked what projects a birth and what fills it out. VAK 4.96 asks
when action, ongoing affliction, or the resulting birth can close access to
the Path. The sequence therefore adds a constraint to the projection model:

```text
projected birth
    → particular conditions of existence
    → possible support for, or obstruction of, Path entry
```

The same karmic architecture must represent both the production of an
existence and its consequences for future practice.

## 9. Organon Contact Point

For the Causal Algebra for Agency, an obstruction is a typed constraint on
a specific transition:

```text
Obstruction {
    source: completedAction | recurrentAffliction | resultantState
    blockedTransition: preparationForPath | entryIntoPath
    duration
    counteractionWindow
}
```

The recurrent-affliction analysis is especially useful. A process may be
less intense at any one moment yet block repair by continually consuming
the time and attention needed to perform it. The agent must track recurrence
and the availability of a corrective interval, rather than measuring only
peak severity.

This is the Organon reconstruction. The five immediate actions, destinies,
Vaibhāṣika severity judgment, and noble-path criterion belong to the
Bhāṣya.
