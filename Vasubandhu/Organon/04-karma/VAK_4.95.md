# VAK_4.95

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
एकं जन्माक्षिपत्येकमनेकं परिपूरकम् ।
नाक्षेपिके समापत्ती अचित्ते प्राप्तयो न च ॥ ४.९५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ekaṃ janmākṣipaty ekam anekaṃ paripūrakam /
nākṣepike samāpattī acitte prāptayo na ca // 4.95 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ekam | janma | ākṣipati | ekam | anekam | paripūrakam |
na | ākṣepike | samāpattī | acitte | prāptayaḥ | na | ca
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ekam` | nominative neuter singular, with `karma` understood | one projecting karma |
| `janma` | accusative neuter singular | one birth, specified by the Bhāṣya as one rebirth-class |
| `ākṣipati` | third-person singular present of `ā-√kṣip` | projects, propels, determines |
| `ekam` | accusative neuter singular agreeing with `janma` | one birth |
| `anekam` | nominative neuter singular collective | multiple; many completing karmas |
| `paripūrakam` | nominative neuter singular | completing, filling out |
| `na ākṣepike` | nominative feminine dual | are not projecting |
| `samāpattī` | nominative feminine dual | the two attainments |
| `acitte` | feminine dual adjective | non-mental; qualifying the two attainments |
| `prāptayaḥ` | nominative feminine plural | acquisitions or possession-relations |
| `na ca` | negative coordination | and are not, either |

**Bhāṣya-resolved syntax of the second half**

```text
acitte samāpattī
    na ākṣepike

prāptayaḥ
    na [ākṣepikāḥ] ca
```

`Acitte` qualifies the two non-mental attainments. It does not extend across
the clause to make `prāptayaḥ` “acquisitions in a non-mental state.” The
Bhāṣya excludes the acquisitions separately.

## 4. Grammar

**One projecting determination**

```text
ekam [karma]
    ekam janma
    ākṣipati
```

> One karma projects one birth.

The two occurrences of `ekam` occupy distinct syntactic roles: the first is
the nominative source, the second the accusative projected result. The Bhāṣya
makes the relation reciprocal at the level of projection: one karma projects
one rebirth-class, and one rebirth-class is not jointly projected by several
karmas.

**Multiple completing determinations**

```text
paripūrakam [karma]
    anekam [asti]
```

> Completing karma is multiple.

The singular collective construction predicates multiplicity of the
completing causal role. It does not make the one projector perform every act
of completion.

**Excluded projectors**

```text
acitte samāpattī
    na ākṣepike

prāptayaḥ
    na [ākṣepikāḥ] ca
```

> The two non-mental attainments are not projecting, nor are acquisitions.

The negative predicate is carried forward by ellipsis to `prāptayaḥ`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> One [karma] projects one birth; the completing [karma] is multiple. The two
> non-mental attainments are not projecting, nor are acquisitions.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> One projecting karma determines one unified rebirth-class, and that class is
> not projected jointly or piecemeal by several karmas. The concrete features
> of the resulting life, however, are filled out by many completing karmas,
> just as a painter establishes a figure with one stroke and completes it with
> many. The two attainments in which active citta is absent do not project a
> rebirth-class, and neither do acquisition-relations merely by making a dharma
> belong to a continuum.

The identification of birth with `nikāyasabhāga`, the one-to-one projection
rule, the painter analogy, the Aniruddha discussion, and the reasons for the
exclusions are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The unity of an existence and the multiplicity of its concrete
> determinations arise through different causal functions. Projection
> establishes the universal placement: this continuum is born within this
> determinate class of life. Completion particularizes that unity through
> faculties, form, proportion, strength, and the other conditions that make
> the life concretely this life. Neither function is sufficient for the
> concrete whole, yet neither may be collapsed into the other.

The rational form is:

```text
projecting determination
    → establishes one unitary ground of existence

completing determinations
    → articulate the manifold features of that ground

concrete birth
    → unity of projected class and completed particularity
```

In the language of Rational Yoga, one Principle projects the unity of a Bhūmi;
the complete crystal of valid conditioned determinations fills out its
actuality. Unity is not assembled from fragments, and multiplicity is not
erased by the Principle.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ākṣepaka-karma` — projecting karma**

Karma that determines one rebirth through projection of its
`nikāyasabhāga`. Projection answers what unified class of embodied existence
the continuum enters; it does not exhaustively produce every feature of the
life.

**`Paripūraka-karma` — completing karma**

The multiple karmas that fill out an already projected birth through its
concrete faculties, bodily features, measure, strength, prosperity, and
deficiencies. Completion presupposes the projected unity it particularizes.

**`Nikāyasabhāga` — common class-character of a rebirth-group**

The Bhāṣya's precise referent for `janma` in the projection rule. When this
class-determination is acquired, the continuum is said to have been born as
that kind of being.

**`Khaṇḍaśaḥ ākṣepa` — fragmented projection**

The unacceptable consequence of supposing that several karmas jointly
project one rebirth-class. The class-unity would be projected piecemeal rather
than as one determination.

**The painter analogy**

One stroke outlines the figure; many strokes complete it. The analogy is not
merely illustrative: it differentiates constitutive placement from concrete
completion while showing their unity in one result.

**The Aniruddha example**

Aniruddha attributes seven divine births and a later Śākya birth to one gift.
Vasubandhu explains the gift as the originating occasion for later merit, like
one coin that begins a fortune. A reported alternative treats it as support
for a continuum of giving-intentions, each appropriating a distinct result.
Neither account permits one numerically single projecting karma to project
many births.

**`Adhiṣṭhāna` — support or basis**

In the reported alternative, the original gift supports a great stream of
intentions to give. Support for a causal series must not be identified with
every operative member of that series.

**`Acitta-samāpatti` — non-mental attainment**

The dual refers to the two attainments without active citta: the attainment of
non-perception and the attainment of cessation. They do not project a
rebirth-class because they do not coexist with karma, according to the
Bhāṣya.

**`Prāpti` — acquisition or possession-relation**

The relation through which a dharma belongs to a continuum. Acquisition is
not itself the causal operation that projects the continuum into a
rebirth-class. Belonging and projecting remain distinct.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Projection and completion**

Let:

```text
Kp = projecting karma
N  = one nikāyasabhāga
J  = the concretely completed birth
Kc₁ ... Kcₙ = completing karmas
```

Then:

```text
Projects(Kp, N)
ExactlyOneProjectedClass(Kp)
ExactlyOneProjector(N)

Completes(Kc₁, J)
Completes(Kc₂, J)
...
Completes(Kcₙ, J)
```

The completed structure is:

```text
N
    → supplies the unitary rebirth-class of J

{Kc₁ ... Kcₙ}
    → supply differentiated conditioned features of J
```

**Non-equivalences**

```text
one projecting karma           ≠ one cause of every life-feature
many completing karmas         ≠ many joint projectors
originating occasion           ≠ sole projector of every later birth
support for a causal stream    ≠ every intention in that stream
prāpti                         ≠ projection
attainment                     ≠ birth-projecting karma
```

**Why one projector**

```text
if several karmas jointly projected N
    → projection would be distributed across fragments
    → N would lack one constitutive class-determination

therefore
    → one projecting karma establishes one N
```

**Why many completers**

```text
one class-determination
    is compatible with multiple concrete instances

differences among those instances
    require multiple completing causal determinations
```

Unity belongs to the projected universal; difference belongs to its causal
completion.

**Excluded relations**

```text
NonMentalAttainment(x)
    → not ProjectsRebirthClass(x)

AcquisitionRelation(x)
    → not ProjectsRebirthClass(x)
```

These rules prevent a state, possession-marker, or absence of active citta
from being confused with the specific projecting operation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.95 concludes this phase of the Karma chapter by distinguishing the unity
of a birth from the multiplicity of its constitution. “One karma projects one
birth” is easily misunderstood as a monocausal doctrine in which one deed
manufactures an entire life. The Bhāṣya blocks that reading by identifying the
projected term as `nikāyasabhāga`, the one class-determination under which a
continuum counts as born within a determinate rebirth-group.

Concrete existence is richer than its class-placement. Human beings share one
human rebirth-class while differing in faculties, limbs, appearance,
proportion, strength, prosperity, and impairment. Completing karmas account
for this determinate multiplicity without fragmenting the unity of birth. The
projector and completers are not competing explanations; they occupy different
logical levels of the same actuality.

This is a powerful form for the Saṃyama-bhūmis. A Bhūmi is not constructed by
aggregating unrelated appearances until a general label emerges. Its Principle
establishes a prior unity within which the appearances become positions of one
field. Yet the Principle remains abstract until the manifold relations,
operations, rationales, and consequences have completed that field.

```text
Principle without completion
    → abstract projected outline

completion without Principle
    → fragmented manifold without one Bhūmi

Principle completed through determinations
    → concrete Jñāna of the Bhūmi
```

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy makes the painter analogy fully
intelligible. The universal does not arise as the sum of particulars; it
projects the unity in which they can be particulars of one whole. But the
universal becomes actual only through their differentiated completion. The
concrete Concept is therefore neither bare unity nor external multiplicity,
but the self-particularizing whole.

The exclusions are equally important for Rational Yoga. A non-mental
attainment is a state, and `prāpti` is a relation of belonging. Neither is by
that fact the Kriyā that projects a new ground. Samāpatti, acquisition, Karma,
and causal projection must retain their distinct logical offices if the Yoga
system is to remain operationally precise.
