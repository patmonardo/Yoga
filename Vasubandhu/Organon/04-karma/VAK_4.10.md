# VAK_4.10 — Initiating and Accompanying Action

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
समुत्थानं द्विधा हेतुतत्क्षणोत्थानसंज्ञितम् ।
प्रवर्तकं तयोः आद्यं द्वितीयमनुवर्तकम् ॥ ४.१० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
samutthānaṃ dvidhā hetu-tat-kṣaṇotthāna-saṃjñitam /
pravartakaṃ tayoḥ ādyam dvitīyam anuvartakam // 4.10 //
```

VAK 4.09 described actions and related products as wholesome or unwholesome
through their originating condition. VAK 4.10 distinguishes two functions
within that account: a causal condition that initiates action and a
contemporaneous condition that accompanies its performance.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |

| samutthānam | nominative neuter singular | origination; originating condition |
| dvidhā | indeclinable adverb | in two ways; twofold |
| hetu-tat-kṣaṇa-utthāna-saṃjñitam | nominative neuter singular compound | designated as causal origination and origination in that very moment |
| pravartakam | nominative neuter singular | initiating; setting into operation |
| tayoḥ | genitive dual pronoun | of those two |
| ādyam | nominative neuter singular ordinal adjective | the first |
| dvitīyam | nominative neuter singular ordinal adjective | the second |
| anuvartakam | nominative neuter singular | accompanying; continuing with |

The Bhāṣya resolves the compound as `hetusamutthāna` and
`tatkṣaṇasamutthāna`. It explains the first as projecting or initiating
action, and the second as present during the action's performance.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda predicates `dvidhā` of `samutthānam` and names its two forms in the compound `hetu-tat-kṣaṇotthāna-saṃjñitam`. In the second, `tayoḥ` introduces the pair; `ādyam` and `dvitīyam` correspond respectively to `pravartakam` and `anuvartakam`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation
> Origination is twofold, designated as causal origination and origination in
> that very moment. Of those two, the first is initiating; the second is
> accompanying.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Origination is twofold: causal origination and origination in that very
> moment. Of these, the first initiates, and the second accompanies.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Origination is twofold, designated as causal origination and origination in that very moment. Of those two, the first is initiating; the second is accompanying.

## 8. Logical Determination

An initiating condition projects action; a contemporaneous condition
accompanies its performance. Their distinct roles explain how an earlier
wrong view can initiate the action named in a scriptural sequence without
being the Consciousness present when the action occurs. The Bhāṣya tests
the need for accompaniment with the corpse comparison, then qualifies it
with restraint arising without Consciousness. Its opening questions about
the ethical status of Great Elements and concentration-born *avijñapti*
remain open.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya explains the first origination as the prior condition that projects action and the second as the condition present during its performance; without the latter, the projected action would not occur, like action in a corpse. It raises but leaves unresolved the ethical classification of Great Elements and concentration-born *avijñapti*, especially restraint arising without consciousness.

### Textual Notes

The running witness reads `kukśalatvaprasaṅgaḥ` and has rough spacing in

as transmitted rather than silently completed. The opening questions about
the ethical status of the Great Elements, concentration-born
`avijñapti`, and the divine Eye and Ear are explicitly left for further
effort in the Bhāṣya.

### Interpretation

The Bhāṣya distinguishes causal initiation from accompaniment during
performance. This lets an earlier condition—such as wrong view in the
scriptural sequence—initiate an action without being identified with the
condition present when bodily or verbal manifestation occurs. The questions
about concentration-born `avijñapti`, divine faculties, and restraint without
consciousness remain unresolved in this unit.

In the Kośa's broader system, Discriminative Cognition (*vijñāna*) governs
Mind (*manas*) and guides Dharma Base; it is distinct from consciousness
(*citta*) in the causal account here. The same `avijñapti` is classified in
Form Base and Dharma Base, with *vijñāna* bearing the stated *prati*
relation to it.
