# VAK 4.118 — Five Immeasurable Recipient Fields

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
मातृपितृग्लानधार्मकथिकेभ्योऽन्त्यजन्मने ।
बोधिसत्त्वाय चामेया अनार्येभ्योऽपि दक्षिणा ॥ ४.११८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
mātṛpitṛglānadhārmakathikebhyo 'ntyajanmane /
bodhisattvāya cāmeyā anāryebhyo 'pi dakṣiṇā // 4.118 //
```

The Bhāṣya confirms five recipient fields. `Antyajanmane bodhisattvāya`
denotes one recipient: a Bodhisattva in the final birth.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
mātṛ-pitṛ-glāna-dhārma-kathikebhyaḥ | antya-janmane |
bodhisattvāya | ca | ameyā | anāryebhyaḥ | api | dakṣiṇā
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| `mātṛ-pitṛ-glāna-dhārma-kathikebhyaḥ` | dative plural compound | to mother, father, a sick person, and a Dharma speaker |
| `antya-janmane` | dative singular compound | to one in the final birth |
| `bodhisattvāya` | dative singular masculine | to a Bodhisattva |
| `ca` | indeclinable conjunction | and |
| `ameyā` | nominative singular feminine adjective | immeasurable |
| `anāryebhyaḥ` | dative plural masculine | to non-noble persons |
| `api` | concessive particle | even |
| `dakṣiṇā` | nominative singular feminine | offering |

The feminine `ameyā` agrees with `dakṣiṇā`. The Bhāṣya explains the
immeasurability as pertaining to the offering's result, not to the
quantity of the thing given.

## 4. Grammar

The dative recipients share one predicate:

```text
[mātṛ-pitṛ-glāna-dhārma-kathikebhyaḥ]
    antyajanmane bodhisattvāya ca
    dakṣiṇā ameyā [bhavati]
```

> An offering to mother, father, a sick person, a Dharma speaker, and a
> Bodhisattva in the final birth is immeasurable.

The concessive phrase `anāryebhyo 'pi` adds that this result holds even
when these five recipients are ordinary persons, as the Bhāṣya specifies.
It does not make the five recipients into one spiritual-status class.

## 5. Translation

### Scientific English Rendering

> An offering to one's mother, father, a sick person, a Dharma speaker, or
> a Bodhisattva in the final birth is immeasurable, even when these
> recipients are not noble persons.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> An offering to one's mother, father, a sick person, a Dharma speaker, or
> a Bodhisattva in the final birth is immeasurable, even when these
> recipients are not noble persons. The Bhāṣya says the result of offering
> to these five is immeasurable even when they are ordinary persons.
> It classifies the Dharma speaker among benefactors: a great beneficial
> friend who gives the eye of the Science of Principles to people blinded
> by ignorance, makes clear what is even and uneven, and—through a
> textually uncertain verb—brings about the Dharma-body without influx.
> In brief, the Dharma speaker performs Buddha-work.

The fivefold count, the ordinary-person qualification, and the placement
of the Dharma speaker among benefactors come from the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The field of an offering is not measured by the recipient's noble status
> alone. The Bhāṣya identifies distinct grounds: prior relation to parents,
> present need in the sick, beneficent instruction from the Dharma speaker,
> and the Bodhisattva's final birth. The Dharma speaker's field extends
> through what the instruction enables in others; this is why the Bhāṣya
> places the speaker among benefactors.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

- **`Dakṣiṇā`** — offering; here its result is characterized as
  immeasurable.
- **`Ameyā` / `aprameyā`** — immeasurable. The verse uses `ameyā`; the
  Bhāṣya describes the result with `aprameya`.
- **`Phala`** — result. The verse's immeasurability is qualified by
  `phalataḥ` in the Bhāṣya; retain this separately from the lead-in's
  `vipāka`.
- **`Vipāka`** — maturation. The Bhāṣya first cites a sūtra about
  immeasurable maturation from giving to one practicing for stream-entry
  and a still greater maturation from giving to a stream-enterer.
- **`Anārya` / `pṛthagjana`** — non-noble / ordinary person. The Bhāṣya
  says these fields can have immeasurable result even when their recipients
  are ordinary persons.
- **`Dhārmakathika`** — Dharma speaker, placed by the Bhāṣya in the
  benefactor category.
- **`Antyajanman` / `caramabhavika`** — final birth / one in the final
  existence; the Bhāṣya identifies this recipient as the Bodhisattva
  approaching Buddhahood.
- **`Prajñācakṣus`** — the “eye” of the Science of Principles, which the
  Dharma speaker gives to those blinded by ignorance.
- **`Anāsrava-dharmakāya`** — Dharma-body without influx. The Bhāṣya
  witness's verb governing this phrase is uncertain.
- **`Buddhakṛtya`** — Buddha-work, the Bhāṣya's summary of the Dharma
  speaker's activity.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya's five fields are:

```text
mother                       → prior relation / beneficence
father                       → prior relation / beneficence
sick person                  → suffering and need
Dharma speaker               → beneficence through instruction
final-birth Bodhisattva      → final existence before Buddhahood
```

The result claim and the opening comparison are distinct:

```text
gift to one practicing for stream-entry
    → immeasurable vipāka, in the cited sūtra

gift to a stream-enterer
    → still greater vipāka, in that comparison

offering to the five listed fields
    → immeasurable phala, even when the recipients are ordinary persons
```

The source does not equate `phala` with `vipāka` here; the translation
preserves each term's local role.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.117 distinguished recipient fields by destination, suffering,
beneficence, and qualities. VAK 4.118 applies that account to five cases.
The Dharma speaker is explicitly placed under beneficence, not merely
because of personal qualities, but because the speaker enables others to
see and act through instruction. The passage therefore treats a recipient
field as a relation with practical reach.

This is an analysis of roles within the offering-event, not an
identification of the recipient field with Mind. Bases gather content and
frames; they are not Mind itself. The verse does not name `cetanā`, so the
volitional dimension is not inserted into its translation.

The source spans `[270|26]–[271|07]`, including the sūtra comparison
preceding the kārikā. At `[271|04–05]`, the witness reads
`dikṣeptavyaḥ`; `nikṣeptavyaḥ` is a conjectural repair. At `[271|05–07]`,
it reads `ātinivartayitā`; `abhinirvartayitā` is also conjectural. The
translation marks the latter phrase's verb as uncertain rather than
presenting the correction as certain.
