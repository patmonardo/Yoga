## 3. Continuous Translation

> With the Teacher, the Eye of the World, closed, and the body of
> witnesses largely passed away, this teaching has been made confused
> by unrestrained bad reasoners who have not seen reality.

The Sanskrit unit is verse alone; this translation renders that
verse and does not imply a separate prose explanation.

## 4. Movement of the Verse

Two locative absolutes set the scene: the Teacher, called the Eye of
the World, has closed, and the body of witnesses has largely passed
away. The main clause then says that this teaching has been made
confused. The instrumental expressions describe the agents of that
disordering as bad reasoners who have not seen reality and act without
restraint. The verse supplies no named opponents or further prose
account of their activity.

## 5. Source Explanation

`Lokacakṣuṣi` names the Teacher in apposition; it is not a second
person. `Bhūyasā` limits the loss of witnesses to “largely,” while
`ākulam` describes the teaching as confused, not extinguished.
The three instrumental descriptions belong to one group of agents.
The `ku-` in `kutārkikaiḥ` makes the criticism specific to bad
reasoners; the verse does not condemn reasoning as such. The contrast
between witnesses and those who have not seen `tattva` is suggestive,
but the verse does not define the witnesses or identify the opponents.

## 6. Organon Determination

Represent the Teacher's closing and the large loss of witnesses as
the stated circumstances, and the bad reasoners' action as the
attributed cause of disorder. Keep the verse's polemic distinct from
an independently verified historical inventory. No separate
Bhāṣya speech supplies a positive theory of reasoning here.

## 7. Comparative Synthesis

The Organon may compare the verse's concern about unchecked reasoning
with its own requirement to test interpretation against sources.
That comparison is a project inference, not an additional claim made
by a transmitted prose commentary. The verse itself places the loss
of living witnesses in the setting of the teaching's disordering.
