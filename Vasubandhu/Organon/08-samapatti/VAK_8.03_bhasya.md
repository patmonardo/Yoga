# VAK_8.03 Bhāṣya — Formless Naming, Absence of Form, and Its Renewed Arising

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vibhūtarūpasaṃjñākhyāḥ saha sāmantakais tribhiḥ |
> nārūpye rūpasadbhāvaḥ rūpotpattis tu cittataḥ || 8.03 ||

> The four formless attainments, together with the access
> stages of the latter three, are designated as having
> form-perception passed away. There is no presence of Form
> in the formless domain; the arising of Form, however, is
> from Consciousness conditioned by the cause whose maturation
> that Form is.

Modes of attention that name the four: 8.04.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.03.md`](./VAK_8.03.md).

## 3. Continuous Translation

> [434|10–13] These same formless states, along with three access stages, are
> called those in which perception of Form has passed away. The access stage
> of the infinite-space sphere does not receive that designation, because it
> has the fourth dhyāna as its Support: there, perception of Form has not
> passed away or departed.
>
> [434|13–17] As for the statement “four Bases,” it must first be established
> that Form is absent in the formless states. If Form were present, why would
> they be called formless? Because Form is slight, as in the example of a
> slightly tawny thing? What slight Form is supposed there? If only bodily
> and verbal restraint, how could these restraints exist without body and speech?
> What is derived from the Elements cannot stand when the Elements are absent.
> If [the answer is] “as with restraint without influx,” [the reply is that]
> Elements with influx are present in that comparison; the exclusion of
> [materiality] has also been stated for [formless] attainment.
>
> [434|18–22] Or if body and material faculties too are present, how can
> these be called slight Form? If because their size is small, the same
> would follow for invisible water-creatures. If because of transparency,
> it would follow for beings in the intermediate state and in the Form Realm.
> If because nothing is more transparent, only the peak of existence would
> be formless. If [the opponent says] its rebirth differs, as attainment does,
> what difference is there, since the Form of dhyāna rebirth too is not
> grasped by faculties of a lower ground? If the designations of the two
> [other cases] fit their meanings but that of the formless realm does not,
> what is the reason for this?
>
> [434|22–25] Suppose one argues that Form exists in the formless states
> because life and heat are spoken of together; because name and Form are
> spoken of as depending on each other, like two bundles of reeds; because
> “name-and-Form has Cognition as its Condition” is stated; and because the
> coming and going of Cognition apart from Form and the remaining formations
> is denied. No.
>
> [434|25–435|04] First one must examine the assertion that life and heat
> occur together: does it concern life in the desire realm, or all life?
> Likewise, does the mutual dependence of name and Form concern name in the
> desire and Form realms, or all name? When name-and-Form is said to have
> Cognition as its Condition, is every Cognition said to condition
> name-and-Form, or is all name-and-Form said to be conditioned by Cognition?
> And when the coming and going of Cognition apart from Form and the other
> [formations] is denied, is it denied in the absence of all of them, or
> even in the absence of one? If one says these questions cannot be
> examined because the statement makes no distinction, that goes too far.
>
> [435|05–08] For it would then follow that even external heat cannot
> exist without life; that even external Form depends on name; and, from
> the teaching of four kinds of food, as with the four stations of Cognition,
> that morsel-food exists in the Form and formless realms. If one replies
> that morsel-food does not follow, because scripture speaks of passing
> beyond the gods who eat morsel-food and speaks of joy as food, then Form
> in the formless states likewise does not follow.
>
> [435|09–13] The formless states are called “escape from Forms,” and
> “peaceful releases beyond Forms”; scripture also speaks of “formless
> beings” through complete transcendence of perceptions of Form. If their
> own Form were present, they would necessarily perceive it. If one says
> the reference is only to gross Form of a lower ground, the same answer
> applies to morsel-food. Moreover, since dhyānas too escape a lower
> ground, they would count as formless. Why, then, are the formless states
> not called an escape from feeling and the rest?
>
> [435|14–19] Because they escape feeling of a lower ground; but they
> have crossed the entire class of Form, not the classes of feeling and
> the rest. Hence they are called an escape from Forms. The saying that
> one existence is not an escape from another concerns its inability to
> escape itself and the fact that the escape is neither complete nor final.
> And the Blessed One said of the dhyānas, “whatever is there belonging to
> Form, up to what belongs to Cognition,” but of the formless states,
> “whatever is there belonging to feeling, up to what belongs to Cognition.”
> If the class of Form were present there, why would he not have said
> “what belongs to Form”? Therefore:
>
> [435|20–23] “There is no presence of Form in the formless domain.” How,
> then, after Form has been interrupted for many kalpas, does it arise
> again for those who fall from that existence? “The arising of Form,
> however, is from Consciousness.” Form arises from Consciousness
> conditioned by the cause whose maturation is that Form, when it has
> obtained functioning.
>
> [435|23–26] How does Consciousness operate without relying on Form?
> Why should it not? Because that is not seen here? Then [by the same
> reasoning] it should not operate in the Form Realm without morsel-food.
> Why? Because that is not seen here. It has already been explained how
> it operates. This is settled; the next matter must be stated.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
name: form-perception gone (minus first access)
"slight form" tested as restraint, small size, transparency, and rebirth
life/heat, name/Form, and Cognition dependencies tested for scope
food analogy and explicit scriptural escape-from-Form sayings
enumeration omits rūpa → absence now
arising later ≠ hidden form now
cittataḥ qualified by vipāka-cause
```

## 5. Now ≠ Later

Access ≠ principal attainment. Domain constitution ≠ a sitting meditator's body
vanishing. Later Form ≠ the same old lump stored somewhere. `Cittataḥ` ≠ Consciousness
makes Form at will.

## 6. Who Gets the Name

| state | form-perception gone |
|---|---|
| four principal ārūpyas | yes |
| access to infinite space | no (4th dhyāna still the object) |
| access to the other three | yes |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- three sāmantakas = latter three accesses;
- first half = present constitution;
- second half = later genesis.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is reconstruction.

Absence from the present constitution is not absence from lawful genesis. Vidyā
keeps those two questions apart. Technē of return is karmically qualified citta,
not a stash of subtle rūpa. Appearance would either smuggle form back in or deny
rebirth-body. Do not sell "Meaning = regenerate the file."
