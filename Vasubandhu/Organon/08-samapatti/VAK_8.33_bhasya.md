# VAK_8.33 Bhāṣya — Cessation-Attainment: Entry and Emergence

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirodhas tu samāpattiḥ sūkṣmasūkṣmād anantaram |
> svaśuddhakādharāryeṇa vyutthānaṃ cetasā tataḥ || 8.33 ||

> Cessation, however, is an attainment, immediately after the most subtle of the
> subtle. From it emergence occurs through an own-level pure or lower-level noble
> mind.

Verse: `adharārya`. Running prose of the second alternative: `sāsraveṇa`. Both
kept. Objects of the releases: 8.34.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.33.md`](./VAK_8.33.md).

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Cessation of perception and feeling is the eighth release — the cessation-
> attainment already taught. Release: turning away from perception and feeling,
> or from all conditioned dharmas. Others: release from an obstruction to
> attainment.
>
> At the summit, perception is already subtle. Making it still subtler, one enters
> cessation immediately after. Emergence: a *pure* mind of the summit, or a mind
> of nothingness — verse: noble; this prose: contaminated.

## 5. This Nirodha ≠ Those Cessations

Not pratisaṃkhyā / apratisaṃkhyā of 8.26. Not an uncontaminated bhavāgra. Not
four minds in the compound — two pairs. Emergence-mind ≠ cognition during the
attainment. Deferred: sublation = nirodha — not applied here.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `nirodha` = saṃjñāveditanirodha;
- `sūkṣmasūkṣma` is an operation, not a nickname;
- mode of exit B left dual-witness.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Cessation is still an attainment with a door and two exits. Vidyā keeps the
architecture. Technē is the last thinning of perception — content going quiet,
not a stolen empty light. Appearance would call this the Sahasrāra poured out.
The text says: perception, then subtler, then this sitting. Yama does not Mara
it. He asks whether Pure Reason was raised. The verse names a samāpatti.
