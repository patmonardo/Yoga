# VAK_8.33 Bhāṣya — Cessation-Attainment: Entry and Emergence

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirodhas tu samāpattiḥ sūkṣmasūkṣmād anantaram |
> svaśuddhakādharāryeṇa vyutthānaṃ cetasā tataḥ || 8.33 ||

> Cessation, however, is an attainment entered immediately after the
> subtlest of the subtle [perception]. From it, emergence occurs through a
> pure mind of its own [summit] level or through a noble mind of a lower level.

Verse: `adharārya`. Running prose of the second alternative: `sāsraveṇa`. Both
kept.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.33.md`](./VAK_8.33.md).

## 3. Continuous Translation

> “Cessation, however, is an attainment”: cessation of perception
> and feeling is the eighth release, the cessation-attainment. It
> was described earlier. [It is called release] because of turning
> away from perception and feeling, or from all conditioned things.
> Others say it is release because it frees one from an obstruction
> to attainment.
>
> How does one enter it? “Immediately after the subtlest of the
> subtle.” At the summit of existence, perception is subtle [the
> local `bhavāgraṃ hi saṃjñā sūkṣmaṃ` has uneven agreement]; making
> it subtler still, one enters cessation. For those who have entered,
> “emergence from it [occurs] with an own-level pure or a lower-level
> noble mind.” Emergence from it occurs either with a pure mind of
> the summit of existence or with a mind bearing influxes on the ground
> of the sphere of nothingness [the prose reads `sāsraveṇa`, against
> the verse's `adharāryeṇa`].
>
> Thus its attainment-mind is with influx; its emergence-mind is
> [described as] immediately following a with-influx [mind]. The
> local `sāsravānāntarya tu vyutthānacittam` remains compressed;
> the sentence does not settle the verse/prose disagreement.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening identifies the eighth release as the already described
cessation-attainment. Vasubandhu offers turning away from perception
and feeling, or from all conditioned things, as explanations of its
release name; *apare* give release from an obstruction to attainment.
The entry question is answered by making the already subtle perception
at the summit still subtler. The verse supplies two emergence alternatives:
an own-level pure mind and a lower-level noble mind. The prose agrees on
the summit-level pure alternative but calls the nothingness-ground mind
*with influx*. Its final sentence summarizes the attainment and emergence
minds in compressed wording.

## 5. Bhāṣya Explanation and Textual Conflict

`Saṃjñāveditanirodha` specifies the cessation here; the passage does not
identify it with the unconditioned cessations discussed at 8.26.
`Sūkṣmasūkṣmāt` is explicated by `sūkṣmataraṃ kṛtvā`: a further
refinement of perception before entry. Neither that entry condition nor
the described emergence mind asserts cognition during the attainment.

The verse's `svaśuddhakādharāryeṇa cetasā` is construed as two paired
alternatives, own-level pure and lower-level noble. The prose explicitly
locates the second at the sphere of nothingness but calls its mind
`sāsraveṇa` (with influx). Noble and with-influx classifications are
materially different. The final `sāsravānāntarya` may describe the
emergence mind's immediate antecedent, but the compressed syntax does
not resolve the second alternative's mode. Both witness claims remain
separately recorded. The standalone local kārikā witness in
`vakobhau.txt` also prints `svaśuddhakādharāryeṇa`; it offers no
independent prose reading to settle `sāsraveṇa`.

## 6. Organon Determination

Record attainment identity, the immediately preceding refinement, and
each emergence alternative as distinct relations. Attach the noble mode
to the verse and the with-influx mode to the prose; do not collapse them
into an unqualified exit classification. Pure (*śuddhaka*) here names
the summit-level option and does not by itself mean without influx.

## 7. Comparative Synthesis

The unit supplies a determinate sequence of entry, attainment, and
emergence for a specific cessation of perception and feeling. It does
not identify that attainment with the Organon Concept or establish an
observing mind within it. Any Yoga comparison must preserve the local
attainment sense and the unresolved witness conflict.

## 8. Review Status

The complete natural unit [455|20]–[456|08–09] was collated with the
running source. [455|18–19] closes 8.32 and [456|09] opens 8.34.
Uncertainty remains in `bhavāgraṃ hi saṃjñā sūkṣmaṃ`, in the final

verse `adharāryeṇa` versus prose `sāsraveṇa` conflict. The running
readings remain visible; no critical emendation is claimed.
