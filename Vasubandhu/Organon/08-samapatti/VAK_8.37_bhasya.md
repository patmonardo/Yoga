# VAK_8.37 Bhāṣya — Acquisition, Habituation, and Basis

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirodha uktaḥ vairāgyaprayogāptaṃ tu śeṣitam |
> tridhātvāśrayam ārūpyasaṃjñaṃ śeṣaṃ manuṣyajam || 8.37 ||

> Cessation-release has been explained. The remainder, however, is
> acquired through dispassion or through application. What is designated
> formless can have a Basis in any of the three domains; the rest is

## 3. Continuous Translation

> “Cessation has been explained”: the cessation-release was already
> explained earlier in all its aspects. “What remains is acquired
> through dispassion or application”: the releases and related
> practices other than cessation are acquired through dispassion and
> through deliberate application, according as they have or have not
> been practiced before.
>
> “What is designated formless has a Basis in the three domains; the
> rest arises among humans”: the formless releases and the two formless
> totalities have a Basis in the three domains; the remainder has only
> a human Basis. This is because it is produced through the efficacy
> of instruction.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The first statement sets aside the cessation-release as already
explained. The next gives dispassion and application as acquisition
routes for the remaining releases and related practices, according
to prior habituation. The final classification assigns the formless
releases and two formless totalities possible Bases in all three
domains. The other practices are generated only with a human Basis,
which the prose explains by the efficacy of instruction. The question
about production of formless attainments in the form and formless
domains begins the next unit within the same printed line.

## 5. Bhāṣya Explanation

The two `śeṣa` expressions have different scopes. `Śeṣitam` follows
the exclusion of cessation from the acquisition rule; the later
`śeṣam` contrasts the named formless group with other practices in
this discussion. The concise `ucitānucitatvāt` explains variation
through prior practice or its absence; it does not supply a detailed
biography of practitioners. The source gives no new account of
cessation-release acquisition here.

`Traidhātukāśraya` and `manuṣyāśraya` concern **Basis** (*āśraya*),
not contemplative Support (*ālambana*) or cognitive Condition
(*viṣaya*). The former spans desire, form, and formless domains for
the named formless practices. The latter restricts the generation of
the other practices to a human Basis. `Upadeśasāmarthya` gives the
local reason for that production rule without deciding all later
possession cases.

## 6. Organon Determination

Represent acquisition route, habituation, practice group, production
Basis, and later possession separately. The three-domain Basis rule
applies to formless releases and the two formless totalities; the
human-Basis rule applies to the remainder as delimited here. A route
of acquisition is not a new species of attainment.

## 7. Comparative Synthesis

For Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē, the passage supplies a bounded
relation between prior formation, application, and instructional
efficacy. It does not turn habituation into an automatic mechanism or
make human Basis a claim about the practice's cognitive Support.
