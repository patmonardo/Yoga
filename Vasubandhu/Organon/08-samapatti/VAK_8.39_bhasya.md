# VAK_8.39 Bhāṣya — Transmission, Realization, and Their Bearers

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> saddharmo dvividhaḥ śāstur āgamādhigamātmakaḥ |
> dhātāras tasya vaktāraḥ pratipattāra eva ca || 8.39 ||

> The Teacher's true Dharma is twofold, consisting of transmission, which is
> Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma, and of realization, which is the factors
> conducive to awakening. Its bearers are precisely the expounders, who
> sustain transmission, and the practitioners, who sustain realization.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> How long, then, will this true Dharma remain—the Dharma in which
> distinctions among such dharmas are discerned? “The Teacher's true
> Dharma is twofold, consisting of transmission and realization.”
> Here transmission consists of Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma;
> realization consists of the factors conducive to awakening. This
> is the true Dharma in its two forms.
>
> “Its sustainers are expounders and practitioners”: for those who
> retain the transmission are its expounders, while those who sustain
> realization are practitioners. Therefore the true Dharma is to be
> understood as remaining for as long as these remain.
>
> They say that these [sustainers] remain for a thousand years [the
> witness separates `varṣasahasra mavasthānam`]. For realization,
> this is so. But transmission lasts a longer time, according to
> others [the reach of *apare* over the preceding qualification is open].

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The question moves from the classification of meditative practices
to the duration of the Teacher's true Dharma. The verse answers by
distinguishing transmitted teaching (*āgama*) from realization
(*adhigama*) and naming their sustainers. The prose identifies the
contents of each: Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma for the former;
awakening factors for the latter. The true Dharma persists while
its corresponding expounders and practitioners persist. The prose
then reports a thousand years for “these.” `Adhigamasyaivam` applies
the duration to realization; *apare* explicitly reports a longer
duration for transmission.

## 5. Bhāṣya Explanation

`Āgamādhigamātmaka` gives two constituents of the true Dharma, not
two names for one undifferentiated activity. `Dhātāraḥ` is explained
through retaining and expounding transmission and practicing
realization. The text does not require the two functions to belong
to disjoint populations. The persistence statement is relational:
so long as those sustaining functions continue, the true Dharma
continues in the stated sense.

The initial `teṣāṃ … āhuḥ` reports a duration for “these,” referring
back to the sustainers; it does not itself restrict the thousand
years to realization. `Adhigamasyaivam` then applies that duration
to realization. The closing `ityapare` explicitly attributes the
longer transmission duration to others; whether it also governs
`adhigamasyaivam` remains uncertain. Neither report supplies a
calendar starting date or establishes a present-day historical diagnosis.

## 6. Organon Determination

Represent transmission and realization with their distinct contents
and sustaining functions. Store the initial thousand-year report,
the realization qualification, and the explicitly *apare*
longer-transmission report separately. The
functional relation between expounder and practitioner need not be
encoded as an exclusive division of persons.

## 7. Comparative Synthesis

The Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē comparison can distinguish preserved,
expounded content from enacted practice. Here, however, *adhigama*
means realization through the awakening factors, not generic acquired
skill. The comparison must not turn the alternative duration report
into an unqualified claim that transmission without realization is
empty or that both forms always have identical historical duration.
