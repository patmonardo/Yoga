# VAK_8.24 Bhāṣya — Three Samādhis and the Sixteen Truth-Aspects

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ānimittaḥ śamākāraiḥ śūnyatānātmaśūnyataḥ |
> pravartate 'praṇihitaḥ satyākārair ataḥ paraiḥ || 8.24 ||

> Signless samādhi proceeds through the four aspects of cessation;
> emptiness samādhi through the two aspects, non-self and emptiness;
> and non-directed samādhi through the remaining aspects of the truths.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Again, three [samādhis] are stated: emptiness samādhi, the
> non-directed, and the signless. Among these: “The signless [proceeds]
> with aspects” [the witness reads `samākāraiḥ`; the paired verse
> provisionally reads `śamākāraiḥ`, “aspects of peace”]. Signless
> samādhi is associated with the four aspects of the truth of cessation.
> Nirvāṇa is signless because it is free from ten signs; a samādhi
> taking that as Support is signless. The ten signs are the five
> sensory Conditions, female and male, and the three marks of the
> conditioned.
>
> “[Emptiness] proceeds through non-self and emptiness.” Emptiness
> samādhi is associated with the two aspects, non-self and emptiness
> [the source's closing compound is damaged].
>
> “The non-directed [proceeds] through the other aspects of the
> truths.” It is associated with the remaining truth-aspects and has
> ten aspects [the source's samādhi name is irregular]. It is called
> non-directed because of recoil from impermanence, suffering, and
> their causes, and because the Path, being like a raft, must
> necessarily be relinquished. [It is] oriented toward passing beyond
> that. But emptiness and non-self do not occasion recoil, because
> they have something in common with nirvāṇa.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
new triad: emptiness, non-directed, signless
signless: four cessation aspects; nirvāṇa as Support
nirvāṇa free of ten signs: five sensory Conditions, female/male,
    three marks of the conditioned
emptiness: non-self and emptiness aspects
non-directed: the other ten truth-aspects and the stated rationale
```

## 5. Two Different Tens

The ten signs from which nirvāṇa is free are not the ten aspects
assigned to non-directed samādhi. Signlessness retains four
cessation-aspects and a specified Support; it does not mean lack
of all cognitive content. The five *viṣaya* in the sign list are
five sensory Conditions, not five Essences.

## 6. Aspect Partition

| samādhi | truth-aspects | further explanation |
|---|---|---|
| signless | four cessation aspects | nirvāṇa as Support |
| emptiness | non-self and emptiness | two aspects |
| non-directed | remaining ten | recoil and eventual relinquishment of the Path |

The Path is compared to a raft that must eventually be relinquished.
The prose does not say to abandon its present work. Emptiness and
non-self do not occasion the same recoil because of their stated
commonality with nirvāṇa.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- the first instrumental is resolved as the four cessation-truth aspects;
- `tadālambanaḥ` gives nirvāṇa as Support of signless samādhi;
- `ataḥ paraiḥ` gives the remaining ten truth-aspects;
- the ten signs form a separate inventory from those ten aspects.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is Organon reconstruction. Keep aspect assignment,
Support, and the explanation of each name distinct. The three
samādhis are differentiated by the Bhāṣya's own classifications;
no contentless or purely metaphysical reading is needed to account
for signlessness.
