## 3. Continuous Translation

> How many access attainments are there? “Eight access attainments for
> these.” One for each, through which one enters that [principal
> attainment]. Are they also threefold, and is feeling the same in
> them? It is said: no. “For they are pure and neither painful nor
> pleasant.” They are pure and associated with the faculty of neutral
> feeling, because of [the damaged `yantabāhyatvāt`] and because
> aversion to the lower ground has not ceased. Because they are a Path
> of dispassion, they are not associated with savoring.
>
> “And the first is noble.” The first access attainment is *anāgamya*;
> it is pure and uncontaminated. Although rebirth-linking by
> access-level Consciousness is afflicted, afflictedness is denied of
> one who is collected.
>
> “Some [say] threefold.” Some, however, maintain that *anāgamya* is
> also associated with savoring. Both “access attainment” and
> “intermediate dhyāna” are spoken of. Do these mean the same thing
> or different things? Access attainment is a Path of dispassion. “Intermediate
> dhyāna is without *vitarka*.” Intermediate dhyāna is dhyāna itself
> unassociated with *vitarka*, because it is a distinct variety of
> dhyāna. For this very reason it is not established among the second
> and later dhyānas, since that distinction is absent.

## 5. Distinctions

Neutral feeling (*upekṣendriya*) is not ethical indeterminacy. The
reported afflicted rebirth-linking Consciousness at an access level
does not establish an afflicted collected attainment. The *kecit*
proposal of a third mode for *anāgamya* remains attributed; it does not
replace the principal account. The two terms “access attainment” and
“intermediate dhyāna” are expressly queried and differentiated by
Path function and absence of *vitarka*.

## 6. Principal and Attributed Accounts

| account | access modes | feeling |
|---|---|---|
| principal | pure; first access also uncontaminated | neutral feeling |
| *kecit* for first access | savoring-associated mode also admitted | no revised feeling stated |

The damaged `yantabāhyatvāt` follows the neutral-feeling clause.
Its exact force is unresolved. The following lower-ground-aversion
ground is kept with it; the Path of dispassion directly explains the
exclusion of savoring. The unpunctuated witness leaves the exact
attachment of the first two grounds open.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `eṣām` refers to the eight principal attainments, one access each;
- `āryam` identifies the uncontaminated mode of first access;
- `tridhā kecit` reports an alternative savoring-associated mode;
- `atarka` opens the separate intermediate-dhyāna determination.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is Organon reconstruction. An access attainment has its
own mode, feeling, and dispassion work. The intermediate dhyāna is
identified by the absence of *vitarka*. These determinations should
remain separate when the two are represented in the Organon.
