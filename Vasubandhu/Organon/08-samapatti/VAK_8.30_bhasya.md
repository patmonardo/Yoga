# VAK_8.30 Bhāṣya — Equanimity, Aspiration, and the Immeasurables' Range

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> upekṣālobhaḥ ākāraḥ sukhitā duḥkhitā vata |
> modantām iti sattvāś ca kāmasattvās tu gocaraḥ || 8.30 ||

> Equanimity has non-greed as its nature. The aspects of the immeasurables
> are: “How happy beings are!”, “Alas, beings suffer!”, “May beings
> rejoice!”, and simply “beings.” Their range is beings of the desire
> domain.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> “Equanimity is non-greed.” Equanimity has non-greed as its nature.
> How, then, does it counter ill will? Because [ill will] is drawn
> by greed. It is fitting, however, that [equanimity] has both
> [non-greed and non-hatred] as its nature.
>
> Now, for friendliness and the others: “The form is: ‘How happy
> beings are!’, ‘Alas, beings suffer!’, ‘May beings rejoice!’, and
> ‘beings’.” Attending to “How happy beings are!” one enters
> friendliness. Attending to “Alas, beings suffer!” one enters
> compassion; to “May beings rejoice!” sympathetic joy; and,
> attending simply to “beings,” one enters equanimity, because
> of impartiality.
>
> If the beings are not in that condition, how is resolving them to
> be happy not a mistaken representation? Because the intention is
> “May they be [happy].” Or because the underlying intention is not
> mistaken, since this is recognized as resolve. Alternatively,
> what fault would there be even if it were contrary to fact?
> If [you say] it would be unwholesome: no, because it is rooted in
> the wholesome and counteracts ill will and the other [afflictions].
>
> Their forms have been stated. “But desire-domain beings are their
> range.” Desire-domain beings are their Supports, because these
> [immeasurables] counteract ill will and the others that take those
> beings as Supports. As for the instruction to resolve upon one
> direction, the container indicates what is contained in it.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
upekṣā: verse non-greed; prose proposes both non-greed and non-hatred
four contemplative forms, including simply “beings” for equanimity
objection: how can happiness be resolved for beings not now happy?
reply 1: “may they be”; reply 2: intention is not mistaken, as resolve
conditional reply: even if contrary to fact, wholesome-root defense
kāma beings: verse range, prose Support; direction names container
```

## 5. Aspiration and Assertion

The adjective “happy” in the first form is not itself an optative.
The reply `santu` supplies the wish: “May they be.” A second reply
appeals to the non-mistaken intention and the recognition of resolve.
A third, conditional reply asks what fault follows even if the form
is contrary to fact, then rejects unwholesomeness because the
cultivation has a wholesome root and opposes ill will and the rest.
This last reply is local to the stated cultivation; it is not a
general permission to make false factual claims.

## 6. Form and Range

| immeasurable | contemplative form |
|---|---|
| friendliness | “How happy beings are!”; interpreted as “May they be happy” |
| compassion | “Alas, beings suffer!” |
| sympathetic joy | “May beings rejoice!” |
| equanimity | simply “beings,” from impartiality |

The verse's *gocara* names a range: desire-domain beings. The prose
specifies those beings as Supports (*ālambana*) because the opposed
afflictions also take them as Supports. The direction formula names
a container to indicate its contained beings; it does not make empty
space the Support.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `upekṣā 'lobhaḥ` assigns non-greed, then the prose proposes a combined non-greed/non-hatred nature;
- `sattvāś ca` supplies equanimity's fourth contemplative form;
- `sukhitāḥ` receives its aspirational force from `santu`, not morphology alone;
- `kāmasattvās tu gocaraḥ` is explained by desire-domain beings as Supports.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is Organon reconstruction. Mental nature, contemplative
form, intention, range, and Support are distinct determinations.
The source's resolve analysis permits an aspiration for beings who
are not presently happy, without asserting that the wished-for
condition is already actual. Equanimity's impartiality is a stance
toward beings, not the neutral-feeling faculty.
