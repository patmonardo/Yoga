# VAK_8.01 Bhāṣya — Dhyāna as Attainment and the Question of Collectedness

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dvidhā dhyānāni catvāri proktās tadupapattayaḥ |
> samāpattiḥ śubhaikāgryaṃ pañcaskandhās tu sānugam || 8.01 ||

> The four dhyānas are twofold, as corresponding rebirths
> and as attainment; the rebirths have been explained.
> Attainment-dhyāna is wholesome one-pointedness of Consciousness;
> taken with its accompaniment, however, it has the
> nature of the five Bases.

Birth-side already in Lokanirdeśa. This chapter takes the attainment-side.
Differentiation of the four, and ārūpya: 8.02.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.01.md`](./VAK_8.01.md).

## 3. Continuous Translation

> [432|02–04] Under the heading of Knowledge, the qualities constituted by
> Knowledge have been set out. Their own natures must now be explained; hence
> the dhyānas are taken up first, since they are the Basis of all qualities.
>
> [432|05–08] “The dhyānas are twofold”: in summary, they are twofold through
> the distinction between rebirth-dhyāna and attainment-dhyāna. These, moreover,
> are four, from the first dhyāna through the fourth. The corresponding dhyāna
> rebirths need not be stated again.
>
> [432|08–14] For in the World chapter “their corresponding rebirths were
> stated.” How were they stated? “Separately, separately, dhyāna there has three
> levels, while the fourth has eight levels.” Attainment-dhyāna, however, must
> be explained; hence the statement follows.
>
> [432|15–18] “Attainment is wholesome one-pointedness”: taken without its
> accompaniment, dhyāna is wholesome one-pointedness of Consciousness, because
> its nature is samādhi. “But with its accompaniment, the five Bases”: with its
> retinue, it is to be understood as having the nature of the five Bases. What
> is this one-pointedness? Having one Support.
>
> [432|19–433|02] Then it would follow that the Consciousnesses themselves,
> having one Support, are samādhi, and that samādhi is no distinct mental factor.
> The Consciousnesses themselves are not samādhi. Rather, the Principle by which
> they operate one-pointedly is samādhi. That factor is the one-pointedness
> of Consciousness.
>
> [433|02–05] But, if it is argued that, because Consciousness is momentary,
> every Consciousness is one-pointed, since it has no distraction toward a
> second [object], the associated samādhi would be pointless. And why not
> accept that the Consciousnesses have one Support precisely because of samādhi?
> Moreover, because samādhi is a great-ground factor, it would follow that all
> Consciousnesses are one-pointed. No, because samādhi may be weak.
>
> [433|05–07] According to others (*apare*), the one-pointed Consciousnesses
> themselves are samādhi. For, they say, training in higher Consciousness and
> the four dhyānas are taught in the sūtra with purification of Consciousness
> as primary.
>
> [433|08–09] What does “dhyāna” mean? “They contemplate by it” means “they
> know by it,” because one whose Consciousness is collected knows things as
> they are. For this verbal root means thinking; and it is established doctrine
> that thinking is Prajñā.
>
> [433|09–12] Then every samādhi would have to be a dhyāna. No: the name is
> assigned to what has excellence, as in the case of the sun. What has
> excellence? The samādhi endowed with factors. For, because tranquillity and
> insight proceed yoked together, it is said to be a pleasant abiding in this
> present life and a pleasant course of practice. Through it, they contemplate
> all the more.
>
> [433|13–14] How can an afflicted [state] be dhyāna? Because it is wrong
> contemplation. Would this extend the name too far? No. The designation is
> applied to its counterpart, as with a rotten seed. And the Blessed One has
> also spoken of unwholesome dhyānas.

## 5. Defining Nature ≠ Retinue

Five Bases ≠ the five first-dhyāna factors. One object ≠ the full account
of collectedness. Universal occurrence ≠ equal strength. Do not merge `apare`
into the distinct-factor reply. Afflicted naming does not cancel `śubha` in the
definition. `Sarvaguṇāśraya` is why this chapter starts here — not that every
property of everything is born from concentration.

## 6. Two Accounts of Samādhi

| | what collectedness is |
|---|---|
| main reply | a distinct dharma by which minds operate one-pointed |
| `apare` | the one-pointed minds themselves |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `dvidhā` = birth / attainment;
- `samāpattiḥ` = attainment-dhyāna;
- `sānugam` = with retinue, five-Base.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is Organon reconstruction.

Jñāna as Idea needed a ground of operation. That ground is not a third eye.
Vidyā: collected Consciousness *is* the condition of knowing-as-it-is — `dhyāyanti` =
`prajānanti`. Technē: śamatha and vipaśyanā yoked, not trance and not bare
analysis. Appearance would stop at one object. The Concept of one-ālambana stays
inside the Idea of excellent samādhi. Birth-dhyāna remains World; this is Path-
work of collectedness. Do not import Marga Hell yet.
