# VAK_8.01 — Dhyāna as Attainment and the Question of Collectedness

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
द्विधा ध्यानानि चत्वारि प्रोक्तास्तदुपपत्तयः ।
समापत्तिः शुभैकाग्र्यं पञ्चस्कन्धास्तु सानुगम् ॥ ८.०१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dvidhā dhyānāni catvāri proktās tadupapattayaḥ |
samāpattiḥ śubhaikāgryaṃ pañcaskandhās tu sānugam || 8.01 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the normalized reading already
adopted in the local kārikā study. The running source
has `śubhaikāgyraṃ`; `śubhaikāgryaṃ` corrects the
transposed consonants, with the prose's `kuśalacittaikāgratā`
controlling the meaning. This normalization is disclosed,
not presented as a mechanically identical transcription.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dvidhā | dhyānāni | catvāri | proktāḥ | tad-upapattayaḥ |
samāpattiḥ | śubha-ekāgryam | pañca-skandhāḥ | tu | sa-anugam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dvidhā` | adverb | twofold, as birth and attainment |
| `dhyānāni` | nominative plural neuter | dhyānas |
| `catvāri` | nominative plural neuter numeral | four |
| `proktāḥ` | nominative plural feminine participle here | already explained, agreeing with the rebirths |
| `tadupapattayaḥ` | nominative plural feminine compound | the births corresponding to those dhyānas |
| `samāpattiḥ` | nominative singular feminine | attainment, here attainment-dhyāna |
| `śubhaikāgryam` | nominative singular neuter compound | wholesome one-pointedness |
| `pañcaskandhāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | the five aggregates |
| `sānugam` | neuter singular compound adjective used in the compressed construction | with its accompaniment |

`Śubha + ekāgrya` yields `śubhaikāgrya`; `sa + anuga`
yields `sānuga`. The feminine plural `proktāḥ` refers
to `upapattayaḥ`, not to the neuter `dhyānāni`.

## 4. Grammar

**Four, considered in two ways.** `Dhyānāni catvāri`
counts first through fourth dhyāna. `Dvidhā` distinguishes
birth-dhyāna and attainment-dhyāna. It does not reduce
the four to two or add four new attainments.

**The rebirths have already been explained.** `Proktās
tadupapattayaḥ` is a separate nominal statement with
past participle. The Bhāṣya points to Lokanirdeśa and
therefore turns here to attainment rather than repeating
the rebirth exposition.

**The definition is local.** `Samāpattiḥ śubhaikāgryam`
is a nominal definition. The prose explicitly supplies
`samāpattidhyāna`: the present statement concerns dhyāna
as attainment. It must not automatically define every
attainment mentioned anywhere in the Kośa as this one
wholesome mental factor.

**With accompaniment.** `Pañcaskandhās tu sānugam`
contrasts the defining nature with the accompanied
constitution. The neuter `sānugam` does not directly
agree with feminine `samāpattiḥ`; the compressed clause
is clarified by the prose's `saparivāraṃ ... pañcaskandhasvabhāvam`.
An understood neuter dhyāna or its just-stated constitution
provides the contextual construal. It is not necessary
to call `sānugam` an intrinsically indeclinable word.

**Close construe.** The four dhyānas are twofold. Their
corresponding births have been explained. As attainment,
dhyāna is wholesome one-pointedness; with its accompaniment,
however, it has the constitution of the five aggregates.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The four dhyānas are twofold; their corresponding
> births have been explained. Attainment is wholesome
> one-pointedness; with its accompaniment, however,
> it is the five aggregates.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The four dhyānas are considered as corresponding
> rebirths and as meditative attainments. The rebirths
> were explained in the chapter on the world; the present
> account concerns attainment-dhyāna.
>
> In its defining nature, dhyāna is wholesome one-pointedness
> of mind, since its nature is samādhi. Taken together
> with its accompaniment, it has the nature of all five
> aggregates. One-pointedness is having one object-support.

Source: Pradhan 432|02–433|14, local Bhāṣya checked
against the running source. The subsequent dispute about
samādhi and the explanation of the name dhyāna are
developed below, rather than inserted wholesale into
the verse translation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The attainment has a defining cognitive determination
> and a fuller accompanied constitution. Its unity is
> then examined: does collectedness name the minds
> themselves, or a distinct factor through which they
> operate one-pointedly? The exposition preserves both
> positions and tests the meaning of unity rather than
> treating one object as a sufficient explanation of dhyāna.

The excellence receiving the dhyāna designation is
explained through its factors and through tranquillity
and insight operating together. The passage connects
collected mind with knowing things as they are.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Upapattidhyāna / samāpattidhyāna`.** Dhyāna as
corresponding birth and as meditative attainment. The
first belongs to the already given cosmological account;
the second is the present focus. Attaining dhyāna and
being reborn in its corresponding domain are not identical.

**`Kuśalacittaikāgratā`.** Wholesome one-pointedness of
mind, the prose expansion of `śubhaikāgrya`. The ethical
qualification is part of the definition, not an optional
adjective added after concentration has been defined.

**`Ekālambanatā`.** Having one object-support. This
explains `ekāgratā`, but immediately raises the question
whether samādhi is distinct from the minds so supported.
The definition initiates argument rather than settling
all questions of cognitive constitution.

**`Caitasika dharmāntara`.** A distinct mental factor.
The dispute asks whether samādhi is such a factor or
the one-pointed minds themselves. It is not a dispute
about whether collected cognition occurs at all.

**`Mahābhūmika`.** A mental factor counted as occurring
throughout minds in the position under discussion.
The objection asks whether universal samādhi would imply
universal developed collectedness; the reply invokes
weakness of samādhi. Presence and strength are distinguished.

**`Saparivāra / sānuga`.** With accompaniment or retinue.
The accompanied dhyāna is five-aggregate in constitution.
The passage does not enumerate every accompanying dharma
here or turn the five aggregates into five dhyāna factors.

**`Aṅgasamāyukta`.** Endowed with factors. This identifies
the excellent samādhi to which the name dhyāna applies
in the ensuing explanation. The detailed factor analysis
must be allowed to develop in the following verses.

**`Śamatha / vipaśyanā`.** Tranquillity and insight.
Their yoked operation explains the excellent attainment's
pleasant abiding here and now and its happy mode of
progress. Neither is silently replaced by a project
faculty or an external philosophical category.

**`Yathābhūtaprajñāna`.** Knowing things as they are.
The prose uses the collected mind's capacity for such
knowing to explain `dhyāyanti` through `prajānanti`.
This is an attributed lexical-doctrinal explanation,
not a claim that every form of attention is liberating insight.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The chapter transition.** The opening refers back to
the qualities treated under knowledge and begins with
dhyānas because they support the qualities under discussion.
The phrase `sarvaguṇāśrayatvāt` gives the reason for
starting here. Its scope should not be inflated into
a thesis that all properties of everything derive from
meditative concentration.

| Question | Determination |
|---|---|
| How many dhyānas? | Four |
| In what two respects? | Birth and attainment |
| Which respect is developed here? | Attainment |
| Defining nature? | Wholesome one-pointedness, samādhi |
| With accompaniment? | Five aggregates |

**The samādhi dispute.** The first objection says that
if one-pointedness means one object-support, the minds
themselves could be samādhi; no additional associated
factor would be required. The reply distinguishes the
minds from the dharma by which they operate one-pointedly.

The following exchange tests momentariness, non-distraction,
and the usefulness of an associated samādhi. Its compressed
wording should not be expanded into a wholly reconstructed
sequence of speakers beyond what the argument secures.
The universal-factor objection and weakness reply are
clear: occurrence in every mind does not entail an
equal or developed degree of collectedness.

Others, expressly marked `apare`, identify samādhi with
the one-pointed minds themselves. Their appeal is to
scriptural teaching of the four dhyānas as higher-mind
training and as principally purifying mind.

```text
Distinct-factor account:
    Samadhi is the dharma by which minds operate one-pointedly.
    Universal occurrence need not mean developed strength.

Other teachers:
    The one-pointed minds themselves are samadhi.
    Higher-mind training and purification support this account.
```

The two accounts are not harmonized into one doctrine.
The second is not merely a synonym for the first: it
denies the need to identify an additional mental factor
as the explanatory entity.

**Why not every samādhi is dhyāna.** The prose explains
“they meditate by it” as “they know,” since a collected
mind knows as things are. It then restricts the name
to the excellent form, using the comparison with
`bhāskara`, the sun or light-maker: a designation can
be reserved for an outstanding instance.

The excellent samādhi is endowed with factors and
carries tranquillity and insight yoked together. The
source connects these with pleasant abiding in the
present life and a pleasant path of progress.

**Afflicted dhyāna.** The closing question asks how an
afflicted state receives the dhyāna name. Wrong contemplation
is mentioned, and the reply to overextension restricts
the name to a counterpart resembling the relevant state,
like a rotten seed still bearing the name seed. The
prose also notes scriptural mention of unwholesome dhyānas.
This explains an extension of the name without removing
`śubha` from the initial definition of the wholesome attainment.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Definition and accompaniment.** “Five aggregates” must
not erase the defining samādhi determination, while
“one-pointedness” must not erase the accompanied constitution.
These answer different questions. Likewise, the five
aggregates are not the numerical list of limbs of the
first dhyāna.

**Voice discipline.** The associated-factor reply and
the alternative attributed to `apare` remain distinct.
The study does not assign an unnamed school to the
alternative merely from its philosophical resemblance
to another known position. The source's own attribution
is sufficient for this provisional pass.

**Witness cautions.** The opening `asya svabhāvānāṃ tu
kartavya` is awkward; its exact restoration remains
unresolved although the turn to dhyāna as support is
explicit. The running source has `śubhaikāgyraṃ`,
`smādhiḥ`, and `tatpratirūpa eva tatsaṃjñāvineveśāt`;
the local extract regularizes some forms. The momentariness
exchange around `dvitīyasya tasmādavikṣepa` is compressed
and warrants caution in assigning every clause to a
speaker. No silent restoration is imposed on the witnesses.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates what collected knowing is and
how its unity is determined. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma
companion, concerns the cultivated operation through
which this knowing becomes effective. The source supplies
a precise meeting point in the yoked functioning of
tranquillity and insight.

For Organon, mere numerical unity of object is not
yet a complete account of unified cognitive operation.
The debate asks what explains that operation, while
the excellence discussion connects its developed form
to knowing as things are. The project can develop this
relation without erasing the source's dispute over whether
a distinct samādhi factor is required.

Chapter 8 therefore begins with its own distinctions:
birth and attainment, defining nature and accompaniment,
one-object support and operative collectedness, and the
excellence of factor-endowed samādhi. These should guide
our reconstruction before a larger architecture is imposed.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The twofold four-dhyāna classification, local attainment
definition, accompanied five-aggregate constitution,
samādhi dispute, strength distinction, knowing-based
explanation, and qualified use of the dhyāna name have
been checked against the local extract and running source.
Damaged prose remains marked for review.
