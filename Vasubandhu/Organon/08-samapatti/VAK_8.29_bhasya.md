# VAK_8.29 Bhāṣya — Four Immeasurables: Supports, Counterforces, and Mental Natures

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> apramāṇāni catvāri vyāpādādivipakṣataḥ |
> maitry adveṣo 'pi karuṇā muditā sumanaskatā || 8.29 ||

> There are four immeasurables, because they oppose ill will and the rest:
> friendliness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity. Friendliness
> is non-hatred, and compassion is so too; sympathetic joy is gladness.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Now it is time to explain the qualities dependent on samādhi, and
> they are explained: “The immeasurables are four.” They are
> friendliness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity, because
> they take immeasurable beings as Supports. Why exactly four?
> “Because they counteract ill will and the rest.” The four are for
> abandoning these in corresponding order among those in whom ill will,
> harmfulness, discontent, or sensual desire together with ill will
> predominate.
>
> If contemplation of the impure and equanimity both counter sensual
> desire, what is the distinction? The Vaibhāṣikas say: contemplation
> of the impure counters attachment to visible appearance, while
> equanimity counters sexual desire. But this is the fitting account:
> contemplation of the impure counters sexual desire, while
> equanimity counters attachment to mother, father, child, and kin.
>
> Here: “Friendliness is non-hatred.” Friendliness has the nature of
> non-hatred. “Compassion too”: compassion is likewise [non-hatred].
> “Sympathetic joy is gladness.” Sympathetic joy has the nature of
> gladness.

## 5. Three Relations

The name “immeasurable” is explained by the beings taken as
Supports. The fourfold count is explained by counteractive purposes.
The final clauses identify mental natures. Support, counteraction,
and nature should not be collapsed: friendliness and compassion
share non-hatred as nature while answering different counteractive
purposes. Equanimity's nature begins in 8.30.

## 6. Attributed Answers

| voice | contemplation of the impure counters | equanimity counters |
|---|---|---|
| Vaibhāṣikas | attachment to visible appearance | sexual desire |
| expositor's preferred account | sexual desire | attachment to mother, father, child, and kin |

The source introduces the second answer with `evaṃ tu yujyate`.
It is the expositor's judgment, not a continuation of the
Vaibhāṣika report. Familial attachment here is a specified
counteractive target; the passage does not identify care for
relatives with that attachment.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `apramāṇāni` is explained by immeasurable beings as Supports;
- `vyāpādādivipakṣataḥ` is expanded into four corresponding counteractive purposes;
- `api` carries non-hatred from friendliness to compassion;
- `sumanaskatā` is assigned to sympathetic joy alone.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is Organon reconstruction. The source supplies three
separate determinations for each immeasurable: the Support range,
its counteractive work, and its mental nature. A shared nature does
not erase differences of function. The attributed dispute should
remain attributed in comparative use.
