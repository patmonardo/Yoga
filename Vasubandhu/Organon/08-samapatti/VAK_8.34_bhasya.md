# VAK_8.34 Bhāṣya — Release Objects and the Test of Mastery

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāmāptadṛśyaviṣayāḥ prathamāḥ ye tv arūpiṇaḥ |
> te 'nvayajñānapakṣordhvasvabhūduḥkhādigocarāḥ || 8.34 ||

> The first three releases have desire-domain visible Form as their Condition,
> taking the visible Form Essence as Support in its unpleasant or pleasant
> aspect as appropriate. The formless releases range over suffering on their
> own and higher grounds, its cause and cessation, and the whole Path on the
> *anvayajñāna* side. Their Supports also include non-analytical cessation
> and, for one release, space.

Eight Essences of mastery: 8.35. Anvayajñāna stays the ūrdhva-side Path-form — not
English “inference.”

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.34.md`](./VAK_8.34.md).

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Of these releases, “the first have desire-domain visible Form as their
> Condition”: the visible Form Essence belonging to the desire domain
> is their Support, unpleasant or pleasant as appropriate. “Those that
> are formless range over suffering and the rest on their own and higher
> grounds and on the *anvayajñāna* side”: the Supports of the formless
> releases are suffering on their own and higher grounds, its cause and
> cessation, and the whole Path on the *anvayajñāna* side. One should
> also mention non-analytical cessation, and space for one [release].
>
> Why is there no release in the third dhyāna? Because attachment to
> color belonging to the second-dhyāna ground is absent, and because
> of disturbance by pleasure [the local `sukhamaṇḍeñjitatvāt` is
> compressed]. Therefore one produces the beautiful release: to
> gladden a continuum sunk by unattractive contemplation, or in
> order to find out, “Have the two unattractive releases been
> accomplished?” They are indeed accomplished if affliction does
> not arise even when one attends to [forms] as beautiful.
>
> For two reasons yogins produce releases and the like: to keep
> afflictions at a distance and to gain mastery over attainments.
> [They also do so] to bring forth qualities such as non-conflict
> and noble supernormal power. That [noble power] is the means by
> which transformations of Things, *adhiṣṭhāna*, relinquishment of
> lifespan, and the like are performed [the local compound is dense].
> Why is direct realization stated only of the third and eighth
> [releases], not the others? Because they are foremost and stand at
> the limits of domain and ground.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening divides the releases by Condition and range. The first
three have desire-domain visible Form as Condition and the visible Form
Essence as Support, either unpleasant or pleasant as appropriate. The
formless four range over specified suffering, cause, cessation, and
Path Supports; the prose adds non-analytical cessation and space for
one unspecified member. The next question explains the absence of a
third-dhyāna release, then gives two alternative purposes for the
beautiful release: refreshing a sunken continuum and testing whether
the first two have been accomplished. The final questions name the
general purposes of release cultivation, noble power among its further
results, and the reason direct realization is expressly said of the
third and eighth.

## 5. Bhāṣya Explanation

The verse's *viṣaya* marks a Condition, while *gocara* marks range. The
prose supplies *ālambana*, Support, for the specific objects. It calls
the first three's Support the desire-domain visible Form Essence
(*rūpāyatana*). In the formless inventory, the *anvayajñāna* qualifier
belongs to the whole Path, not to suffering, its cause, or its
cessation. Non-analytical cessation and space for one unnamed release
are additional Supports.

The beautiful release tests the earlier unattractive releases through
attention to the beautiful without renewed affliction. Gladdening the
sunken continuum is another stated purpose. The test is local; the
passage does not claim immunity from every affliction in all settings.
Likewise, the two general reasons for producing releases, distancing
afflictions and mastery of attainments, are distinct from the further
bringing forth of non-conflict and noble supernormal power. The dense
power-example compound is not normalized into a complete technical
inventory here.

## 6. Organon Determination

Represent Condition, range, and Support separately. Store the first
three's pleasant or unpleasant visible Form Support as context-sensitive.
Store the formless four's truth and Path Supports as an inventory, not
a claim that each release simultaneously takes every listed item.
For the beautiful release, represent gladdening and testing as
alternative aims, and non-arising of affliction under the beautiful
aspect as the test named here.

## 7. Comparative Synthesis

For Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē, the shift from unattractive to beautiful
attention provides a bounded test of cultivated freedom from affliction.
The source gives a specific criterion and practice purpose; it does not
settle a general theory of appearance or make the reported noble power
an instruction manual. The comparative interpretation should retain
that limit.
