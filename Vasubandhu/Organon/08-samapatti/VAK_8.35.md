# VAK_8.35 — Eight Bases of Mastery

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अभिभ्वायतनान्यष्टौ द्वयमाद्यविमोक्षवत् ।
द्वे द्वितीयवत् अन्यानि पुनः शुभविमोक्षवत् ॥ ८.३५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
abhibhvāyatanāny aṣṭau dvayam ādyavimokṣavat |
dve dvitīyavat anyāni punaḥ śubhavimokṣavat || 8.35 ||
```

The local verse is retained with analytical hyphens
removed. The running source distributes its clauses
through the commentary. The spelling abhibhvāyatana
is retained; its analytical expansion is given below.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
abhibhū-āyatanāni | aṣṭau | dvayam | ādya-vimokṣa-vat |
dve | dvitīya-vat | anyāni | punaḥ | śubha-vimokṣa-vat
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `abhibhvāyatanāni` | nominative plural neuter compound | bases of mastery |
| `aṣṭau` | numeral | eight |
| `dvayam` | nominative singular neuter collective | a pair, the first two |
| `ādyavimokṣavat` | indeclinable comparison in `-vat` | like the first release |
| `dve` | nominative dual neuter numeral | two, here the next pair |
| `dvitīyavat` | indeclinable comparison | like the second [release] |
| `anyāni` | nominative plural neuter pronoun | the others, the remaining four |
| `punaḥ` | indeclinable | again; in turn |
| `śubhavimokṣavat` | indeclinable comparison | like the beautiful release |

The analytical abhibhū + āyatana gives abhibhvāyatana.
Āyatana supplies the sense of a base or sphere;
the commentary determines the specific mastery intended.
Here śubha preserves the beautiful aspect of the
third release and need not be replaced by a
generic claim of purity.

## 4. Grammar

**An eightfold subject with three comparative predicates.**
Abhibhvāyatanāni aṣṭau supplies an implicit “are.”
The following clauses distribute the eight into
2 + 2 + 4 through comparisons with releases.
They do not identify all eight with one release.

**Collective singular and numerical dual.** Dvayam
presents the first pair as a unit. Dve then
selects the third and fourth members, as the
prose explicitly confirms. Anyāni gives the remainder;
its number is four by the total and commentary.

**The comparisons are not assertions of identity.**
The suffix -vat means “like.” Dvitīyavat supplies
vimokṣa by ellipsis. The concluding prose specifies
precisely why correspondence with releases does not
make the two practice classifications interchangeable.

**Punaḥ marks the remaining group.** It does not
instruct the practitioner to repeat the beautiful
release after each of the preceding mastery bases.
This verse classifies rather than prescribing an
invariable temporal sequence of exercises.

**Close construe.** There are eight bases of mastery:
the first pair is like the first release;
the next two are like the second release;
the remaining four are like the beautiful release.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> There are eight bases of mastery. A pair
> is like the first release; two are like
> the second; the others, in turn, are like
> the beautiful release.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The eight bases of mastery are arranged as follows.
> With internal perception of form, one sees external
> forms, limited or extensive, attractive or unattractive,
> and knows and sees them as mastered. These
> first two bases correspond to the first release.
>
> Without internal perception of form, the same
> limited/extensive distinction gives the third and fourth
> bases, corresponding to the second release. Again
> without internal perception of form, seeing blue,
> yellow, red, and white gives the remaining four,
> corresponding to the beautiful release.
>
> The distinction is this: through the releases
> there is merely turning away; through these
> bases there is mastery of the object, through
> determination at will and the non-arising of affliction.

Source: Pradhan 457|01–457|12, checked against the local
Bhāṣya study and running source. The enumeration
and operational distinction are commentary, not additional
words silently inserted into the kārikā.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A shared contemplative pattern can sustain distinct
> practical functions. The first three releases provide
> the patterns for eight bases of mastery. Mastery
> is specified by intentional command in relation
> to the object together with non-arising of affliction.
> The object relation and its affective consequence
> both enter the account of accomplishment.

This formulation follows the commentary's two reasons.
“Command” concerns the contemplative object relation here;
it does not by itself establish unrestricted power
to change external things.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Abhibhvāyatana`.** Base or sphere of mastery.
The commentary explains the term through mastery
of the ālambana, the object of contemplation.
The field's scale and appearance are articulated
within this particular eightfold classification.

**`Adhyātmaṃ rūpasaṃjñī / adhyātmam arūpasaṃjñī`.**
With / without internal perception of form.
These are qualifications of the practitioner in
the enumerative formula. Absence of internal form-perception
does not remove the external visible-form object.

**`Parītta / adhimātra`.** Limited / extensive.
The second base repeats the first formula with
the extensive field; the next pair repeats this
distinction without internal form-perception.

**`Suvarṇa / durvarṇa`.** Attractive / unattractive
in appearance. Here suvarṇa is read in its
contrast with durvarṇa, not as a separate gold
object added to the later list of four colors.

**`Nīla / pīta / lohita / avadāta`.**
Blue / yellow / red / white. These
supply the four separately counted objects of
the final group, without internal form-perception.

**`Abhibhūya jānāti / abhibhūya paśyati`.** Having
mastered, one knows / sees. Abhibhūya is an
absolutive; the formula presents knowing and seeing
in the accomplished relation of mastery.

**`Vaimukhyamātra`.** Mere turning away. This is
the commentary's local comparative characterization of
release in distinguishing it from the mastery bases.

**`Ālambanābhibhavana`.** Mastery of the object.
Ālambana here is the object-support of contemplation,
not the practitioner's bodily or rebirth support.

**`Yatheccham adhimokṣa / kleśānutpāda`.** Determination
at will / non-arising of affliction. The
commentary gives both as explanatory grounds for
object-mastery. Non-arising here should not be silently
converted into permanent eradication of every kleśa.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The eight members.**

| Base | Internal form-perception | External form | Release correspondence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Present | Limited, attractive or unattractive | First release |
| 2 | Present | Extensive, attractive or unattractive | First release |
| 3 | Absent | Limited, attractive or unattractive | Second release |
| 4 | Absent | Extensive, attractive or unattractive | Second release |
| 5 | Absent | Blue | Beautiful release |
| 6 | Absent | Yellow | Beautiful release |
| 7 | Absent | Red | Beautiful release |
| 8 | Absent | White | Beautiful release |

Attractive and unattractive are included within the
first four formulas. Counting them as separate bases
would inflate the stated total. Likewise the final
four are not further multiplied by the limited/extensive
distinction in this enumeration.

**The source's explicit cross-reference.** The prose
names the first and second mastery bases under
the first release and the third and fourth
under the second. It then assigns the remaining
four to the beautiful release. These ordinal
references concern releases, not first, second, and
third dhyāna respectively.

**Correspondence with a difference.** The final prose
first affirms the comparisons and then adds
ayaṃ tu viśeṣaḥ, “but this is the distinction.”
The bases share the relevant release patterns
while their distinguishing function is ālambanābhibhavana.
The comparison cannot therefore be encoded as
identity between a release and a mastery base.

**Two grounds for mastery.** The ablative expressions
yatheccham adhimokṣāt and kleśānutpādāt are joined
by ca. Determination at will and non-arising
of affliction are both explicit. A description
limited to vivid visualization would omit the
second; one limited to lack of affliction would
omit the first.

**The scope of turning away.** The preceding
study already described mastery of attainment among
the general purposes of release cultivation. Here
the narrower comparison identifies the distinctive
object-mastery of the eight bases. Reading “mere
turning away” as a denial of all positive
functions of releases would flatten that context.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source voice and scope.** The preserved unit
is expository and introduces no competing school
position. Its account supplies an eightfold enumeration,
a threefold correspondence, and a functional distinction.
It does not specify a new universal theory
of perception or an unrestricted object-transforming power.

**Witness cautions.** The running source writes
vahirdhā and parīttāni in the first formula;
the study renders their contextual senses without
altering the witness. Unlike the problematic second-release
formula noted at 8.32, this unit explicitly
has adhyātmam arūpasaṃjñī for the second pair
and final four. That clear local reading is
retained without silently repairing the earlier passage.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates internal form-perception, external
object determination, correspondence with release, and
the criterion of mastery as distinct relations.
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns the
effective exercise of determination without affliction
arising in the stated contemplative relation.

The research's contrast between freedom from domination
and positive command is useful project language
for the passage. It should preserve the precise
object and criterion rather than make “negative
freedom” the exhaustive meaning of every release.
Similarly, the Organon use of āyatana as a
mediated field is an interpretive development; the
word alone does not establish the whole project's
account of Essential Relation.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The eight members, 2 + 2 + 4
correspondence, explicit absence of internal form-perception,
and two explanatory grounds for mastery have
been checked against both local witnesses and
the running source. Research originals remain untouched.
