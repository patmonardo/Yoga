# VAK_8.35 Bhāṣya — Eight Essences of Mastery

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> abhibhvāyatanāny aṣṭau dvayam ādyavimokṣavat |
> dve dvitīyavat anyāni punaḥ śubhavimokṣavat || 8.35 ||

> The eight Essences of mastery are these. The first two are like
> the first release: with internal perception of form, one sees external
> forms, limited or extensive, attractive or unattractive, and knows and
> sees them as mastered. The next two are like the second release,
> the same without internal perception of form. The remaining four are
> like the beautiful release: without internal perception of form, one
> sees blue, yellow, red, and white as mastered.

Ten kṛtsnāyatanas: 8.36.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> “There are eight Essences of mastery.” Internally perceiving form,
> one sees external forms, limited, attractive and unattractive; having
> mastered those forms, one knows them and sees them as mastered. One
> has such a perception: this is the first Essence of mastery. The
> extensive [forms are treated] in the same way. Without internal
> perception of form, [one does] just the same: these are four.
> Again, without internal perception of form, one sees blue, yellow,
> red, and white; thus there are eight.
>
> Of them, “two are like the first release”: the first and second
> Essences of mastery correspond to the first release. “Two are like
> the second”: the third and fourth Essences correspond to the
> second release. “The others, in turn, are like the beautiful
> release”: the remaining four correspond to the beautiful release.
> But there is this difference: by those [releases], merely turning
> away; by these [Essences of mastery], mastery of the Support,
> because one resolves [it] as desired and because affliction does
> not arise.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening enumerates two limited and extensive cases with internal
form-perception, two without it, and four color cases without it. The
verse then maps the pairs to the first and second releases and the four
colors to the beautiful release. The closing distinction is functional:
those releases supply mere turning away in this comparison, while the
Essences of mastery supply mastery of the Support (*ālambana*) through
resolve at will and non-arising of affliction.

## 5. Bhāṣya Explanation

The first formula says that one knows and sees the external forms as
mastered. The second and third groups explicitly lack internal
form-perception; this does not remove the external forms they see.
“Limited” and “extensive” count two cases in each of the first two
groups. Attractive and unattractive qualify their appearance without
doubling the count. Blue, yellow, red, and white supply the final four.

The comparisons with releases concern a pattern, not class identity.
`Vaimukhyamātram` gives the releases' function in this local contrast.
`Ālambanābhibhavana` gives the Essences' distinctive mastery of the
Support. The text specifies resolve as desired and non-arising of
affliction; it does not assert unrestricted transformation of external
Things or permanent eradication of every affliction.

## 6. Organon Determination

Represent the eight Essences as a 2 + 2 + 4 partition. For each member,
record internal form-perception, external Form Support, size or color,
and its corresponding release pattern separately. Keep the local
contrast between turning away and mastery of Support tied to the
stated contemplative operation.

## 7. Comparative Synthesis

The Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē comparison can distinguish the range
of forms contemplated from the practitioner's mastery of their Support.
The prose grounds mastery in directed resolve and the non-arising of
affliction. This is a bounded comparison of contemplative functions,
not a general theory of power over external forms.
