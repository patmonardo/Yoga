# VAK_8.37 — Acquisition, Habituation, and Support

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
निरोध उक्तः वैराग्यप्रयोगाप्तं तु शेषितम् ।
त्रिधात्वाश्रयमारूप्यसंज्ञं शेषं मनुष्यजम् ॥ ८.३७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
nirodha uktaḥ vairāgyaprayogāptaṃ tu śeṣitam |
tridhātvāśrayam ārūpyasaṃjñaṃ śeṣaṃ manuṣyajam || 8.37 ||
```

The local verse is retained with analytical hyphens

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
nirodhaḥ | uktaḥ | vairāgya-prayoga-āptam | tu | śeṣitam |
tri-dhātu-āśrayam | ārūpya-saṃjñam | śeṣam | manuṣya-jam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `nirodhaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | cessation, here cessation-release |
| `uktaḥ` | nominative singular masculine past passive participle of √vac | has been explained |
| `vairāgyaprayogāptam` | nominative singular neuter compound predicate | acquired through dispassion or application |
| `tu` | indeclinable | but; as for |
| `śeṣitam` | nominative singular neuter, substantivized | what remains after setting cessation aside |
| `tridhātvāśrayam` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective | having support in the three domains |
| `ārūpyasaṃjñam` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective, substantivized | what bears the designation “formless” |
| `śeṣam` | nominative singular neuter, substantivized | the rest after the formless group is distinguished |
| `manuṣyajam` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective | human-born; generated on human support |

Dhātu + āśraya gives dhātvāśraya; prayoga + āpta
gives prayogāpta. Āpta is the acquired/obtained
participle of √āp. The neuter singulars collect
several practices under one description.

## 4. Grammar

**Cessation is the initial exception.** Nirodha uktaḥ
means that cessation-release has already been explained.
It does not say that cessation is produced
by the two acquisition routes now given for
the remaining practices.

**The acquisition compound compresses two routes.**
Vairāgya and prayoga qualify acquisition. The prose
unpacks the compound as vairāgyalābhikāni prāyogikāṇi
ca and explains the variation by habituation
or lack of habituation. Translating “through dispassion
and application” without explanation could misleadingly require
both as one uniform acquisition procedure.

**The formless designation is the subject of the support predicate.**
Tridhātvāśrayam agrees with ārūpyasaṃjñam: what is
called formless has three-domain support. The phrase
does not assign such support to all practices
mentioned in the preceding clause.

**The two remainders have different scopes.** Śeṣitam
first excludes cessation from the acquisition discussion.
Śeṣam later excludes the specified formless practices
from the remaining support classification. Keeping these
successive selections distinct resolves the second line.

**Human-born concerns the practice's production.** Manuṣyajam
is explained by manuṣyāśrayam eva and by production
through the efficacy of instruction. The human
qualification belongs to the non-formless remainder, not
to the immediately preceding formless group.

**Close construe.** Cessation has been explained. The
remaining practices are acquired through dispassion or
application. Those designated formless have support in
the three domains; the rest are generated
on human support.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Cessation has been explained. The remainder, however,
> is acquired through dispassion or application.
> What is designated formless has support in
> the three domains; the rest is human-born.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Cessation-release has already been explained in all
> its relevant respects. The other releases and
> related practices can be obtained through dispassion
> or through deliberate application, according to prior
> habituation or lack of habituation.
>
> The formless releases and the two formless
> totalities can have support in any of
> the three domains. The remaining practices require
> human support for their production, because they
> are generated through the efficacy of instruction.

Source: Pradhan 458|11–458|18, through upadeśasāmarthyenotpādanāt,
checked against the local research witnesses and
running source. The preceding ordinary/noble bearer statement
belongs to the closing discussion of 8.36.
The following question about production in the
form and formless domains opens the next discussion.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An attainment's acquisition depends on the practitioner's
> formation as well as the practice's classification.
> Habituation permits one acquisition account; lack of
> habituation calls for deliberate cultivation. The support
> on which a practice is produced is a
> further condition, with instruction explicitly explaining
> the human support of the non-formless remainder.

This is a reconstruction of the commentary's
practical argument. It does not turn the two
acquisition routes into two different kinds of
ultimate attainment or an exhaustive theory of learning.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Nirodhavimokṣa`.** Cessation-release. It is set aside
here by reference to its previous explanation.
It should not be included in the remainder
merely because it is the eighth release.

**`Vairāgya / vairāgyalābhika`.** Dispassion / obtained
through dispassion. These name an acquisition route.
The local contrast does not establish that
every dispassion-related acquisition is an uncontaminated attainment.

**`Prayoga / prāyogika`.** Deliberate application /
acquired through such application. Here prayoga concerns
cultivation as an acquisition route, not a new
assignment to one of the access-attainment levels.

**`Ucita / anucita`.** Habituated / unhabituated.
The prose's ucitānucitatvāt gives the reason for
the two acquisition descriptions. Its ordered pairing
supports dispassion-acquisition for the habituated case and
application for the unhabituated case.

**`Ārūpyasaṃjña`.** Designated as formless. Saṃjñā
functions here in the sense of a designation;
the compound does not introduce another perception-attainment
or a new formless level.

**`Traidhātukāśraya`.** Having support across the three
domains: desire, form, and formless. These are
the three existential domains, not the eighteen
dhātus of the earlier cognition-domain classification.

**`Manuṣyāśraya`.** Human support. The predicate concerns
the bearer/support on which the practice is
produced; it does not mean that humans
are its contemplative object.

**`Upadeśasāmarthya`.** Efficacy of instruction. This
is the source's reason for the human-production
rule. It is more specific than a general
appeal to human intelligence or superior motivation.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Acquisition and prior habituation.**

| Scope | Acquisition account | Commentary's condition |
|---|---|---|
| Releases and related practices other than cessation | Acquisition through dispassion | Habituation |
| Same practice complex | Acquisition through deliberate application | Lack of habituation |
| Cessation-release | Referred to its previous explanation | Excluded from this two-route generalization |

The relation is not a permanent partition
of the practice inventory into dispassion-only and
application-only species. The commentary explains variation
through the practitioner's habituation. It does not
specify a practice-duration threshold in this unit.

**Support classification.**

| Group | Support statement |
|---|---|
| Four formless releases | Support possible in all three domains |
| Two formless totalities | Support possible in all three domains |
| Remaining three form-related releases | Human support for production |
| Eight bases of mastery | Human support for production |
| First eight totalities | Human support for production |
| Cessation-release | Previously treated; not newly classified by the remainder rule |

The enumeration expands the prose's explicit formless
group and its remainder using the preceding
inventories. There are no additional “formless mastery
bases” inserted into the eightfold mastery classification.

**Support is neither object nor attainment-level.** A
formless practice may have a human bearer in
the desire domain. That does not make its
attainment-level desire-domain or replace its own stated
object-range with a human object. The axes
examined in 8.36 and 8.37 remain distinct.

**Production is the stated issue.** The explanation
uses utpādana, production, together with instruction.
The study therefore states the human rule in
terms of generation. It does not infer an
additional universal rule about retention, loss, or
later possession after every possible rebirth transition.

**Instruction is a local explanatory condition.** The
text explains the human restriction through instruction's
efficacy. It does not supply a general doctrine
that teaching is impossible outside human existence.
The following question specifically asks about producing
special dhyāna and formless attainments in the
higher domains; its answer belongs to the next unit.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Correction of the first-pass construe.** The research
kārikā translation attaches the human-generation statement
to the formless group and broadens three-domain
support to the whole remainder. The local
Bhāṣya study corrects this, and the running
prose explicitly confirms the correction: formless releases
and formless totalities have three-domain support; the
rest have human support. The official translation
follows that controlled construe. Originals remain untouched.

**Source voice and limits.** The passage is
expository and introduces no competing school authority.
Its short habituation clause supports the ordered
pairing used above, but gives no detailed
history of the practitioner's prior training. The
account should not be supplemented with an
invented schedule or automatic-acquisition mechanism.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates practice-type, acquisition route,
prior habituation, and support as separate determinations.
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns the
effective cultivation and instruction through which capacities
are produced in the specified circumstances.

The research's “history of formation” is useful
project language for the role of habituation.
The source gives conditions for acquiring these
particular practices. A general account of transcendental
training or the constitution of the agent would
be a further Organon development, not a translation
of ucitānucitatva or upadeśasāmarthya by themselves.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The cessation exclusion, two acquisition routes, habituation
explanation, restricted three-domain support, human-production rule,
and instructional reason have been checked against
the local witnesses and running source. The
first-pass support reversal is corrected in this
official study without changing the research files.
