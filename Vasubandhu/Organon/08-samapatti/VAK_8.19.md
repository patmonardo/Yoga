# VAK_8.19 — Completed Skipping Attainment and Its Supports

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

## 4. Grammar

**Completion of the preceding preparation clause.**
The absolutives in VAK 8.18 describe prior traversal.
Here vyutkrāntakasamāpattiḥ receives its defining predicate,
visabhāgatṛtīyagā. The prose distinguishes the preparation
from the point at which the attainment is accomplished.

**Third is counted from the predecessor.** Going
from first to third dhyāna skips the second. The
next move, from third dhyāna to infinite space,
skips fourth dhyāna. The ordinal is relative, not
a requirement that every destination be third dhyāna.

**Dissimilar qualifies mode.** The explicit example
alternates sāsrava and anāsrava. The perfected practice
therefore changes mode as well as skipping an intervening
level; ordinal distance alone describes only part
of its determination.

**The possessive belongs to the attainment.** In the
support rule, “own level” means the level of the
attainment made present. Its support may belong to
that level or a lower one. Reversing this relation
would give the wrong direction of eligibility.

**The final clause is contextually restricted.** The
prose asks why someone reborn above does not make
a lower attainment present. “The lower is without
use” answers that question. It is not an unrestricted
judgment about every lower attainment or every practitioner.

**Close construe.** Skipping attainment goes to the
third attainment in a dissimilar mode. Dhyānas and
formless attainments generally have supports at their
own or lower levels; for one reborn above, a
lower attainment serves no purpose in the stated account.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Skipping attainment goes to the third in a dissimilar
> mode. The dhyānas and formless attainments have
> supports only at their own or lower levels.
> The lower is without use.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Skipping attainment is accomplished when, after the
> preparation, one enters the third attainment in
> a different mode: for example, from contaminated
> first dhyāna to uncontaminated third dhyāna, then
> to contaminated infinite space, then to uncontaminated
> nothingness, and likewise in reverse. A jump to
> the fourth relative station is too distant.
>
> In the three continents, only an arhat liberated
> independently of occasion produces this attainment,
> because both freedom from defilement and mastery
> over samādhi are required.
>
> As a general rule, dhyānas and formless attainments
> are made present on supports at their own level
> or below. Someone reborn above does not make a
> lower attainment present because, being inferior,
> it supplies no relevant purpose. The following
> verse states an exception to this general rule.

Source: Pradhan 446|06–446|21, checked against the local
Bhāṣya study and running source. The transition at
446|22 explicitly announces the exception; its detailed
analysis remains for VAK 8.20.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Completion requires command of both level-distance
> and difference of mode. The commentary also distinguishes
> freedom from affliction from mastery of samādhi:
> neither qualification alone suffices for this accomplishment.
> A separate inquiry then concerns the existential
> support upon which an attainment can be made present.

Rebirth-support and a currently entered attainment must
remain distinct. The support rule cannot be generalized
into a prohibition on descending in practice after
having mastered a higher attainment, since reverse
traversal is explicitly part of this very account.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Visabhāga`.** Dissimilar in mode: contaminated versus
uncontaminated in the example. It does not mean
opposed ethical qualities such as wholesome versus defiled.

**`Abhiniṣpanna`.** Accomplished or perfected. This marks
the passage from the preparation of VAK 8.18 to
the alternating-mode skip sequence.

**`Asamayavimukta`.** Liberated independently of occasion.
Retained as a technical arhat classification: it is
not a claim about existence outside time. The
prose identifies this practitioner by the conjunction
of freedom from defilement and mastery of samādhi.

**`Dṛṣṭiprāpta`.** The one attained through view. In
the contrast here, sharp faculties yield mastery
of samādhi, but complete freedom from defilements
is lacking.

**`Samayavimukta`.** Liberated depending on occasion.
In the present contrast, freedom from defilements
is present but the required mastery of samādhi is lacking.

**`Āśraya`.** Support, here determined through the
level of rebirth. It must not be equated with
the immediately preceding attainment in a traversal.

**`Saṃmukhīkaroti`.** Makes present, brings into operation.
The question concerns actual manifestation of the
attainment on a support, not merely its abstract
availability or acquisition of possession.

**`Prayojana`.** Purpose or use. The prose explains
the general exclusion of a lower attainment for
a higher-born support through lack of a relevant
purpose, and immediately proceeds to an exception.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The accomplished example.**

| Entry | Level | Mode | Level skipped from preceding entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First dhyāna | Contaminated | — |
| 2 | Third dhyāna | Uncontaminated | Second dhyāna |
| 3 | Infinite space | Contaminated | Fourth dhyāna |
| 4 | Nothingness | Uncontaminated | Infinite knowledge |

The same is then traversed in reverse. Each move
has two level-steps and a change of mode. The
prohibition on the fourth relative station concerns
a greater leap, not ordinary entry into fourth dhyāna.

**Both practitioner qualifications are needed.**

| Practitioner in the prose comparison | Freedom from defilements | Required samādhi mastery |
|---|---|---|
| Asamayavimukta arhat | Present | Present |
| Dṛṣṭiprāpta | Not complete | Present through sharp faculties |
| Samayavimukta | Present | Lacking |

The passage locates production in the three continents.
This study preserves that restriction without importing
a further geographical explanation absent from the
local unit. The table describes the source's comparison,
not a general ranking of every spiritual capacity.

**The support examples.** Bhavāgra attainment is made
present at bhavāgra and on lower supports down to
the desire domain. The remaining attainments are
likewise made present on their own level or below.
This describes the general level relation; it does
not imply that every being at an eligible level
can enter every corresponding attainment without qualification.

**Rebirth is not mastery.** The research first pass
speaks of a practitioner who has mastered a higher
ground finding a lower attainment useless. The prose's
question instead specifies ūrdhvopapanna, one reborn
above. Retaining that distinction prevents the utility
claim from contradicting reverse meditative traversal.

**A general rule with a stated exception ahead.**
The next sentence says that, having given the
general rule, the text gives an exception. VAK 8.20
concerns bringing uncontaminated nothingness into operation
for a being reborn at the summit. The present
support relation must therefore be stored and interpreted
as a general rule, not an exceptionless impossibility.

**Relation to first-level knowledge in VAK 8.13.**
Making lower-level sensory or manifestation-initiating
knowledge operative is not automatically entering a
lower samāpatti. The present use claim concerns the
lower attainment; it should not erase the earlier
account of first-dhyāna knowledge functioning for higher-born beings.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Two inquiries share the verse.** The opening completes
the skipping-attainment account; the second part opens
the support inquiry. Practitioner eligibility for the
special accomplishment and support eligibility for
dhyāna/ārūpya manifestation answer different questions.
Neither should silently replace the other.

**Witness cautions.** The running source contains the
isolated `dhyānāśrayā eva` between quoted verse fragments,
and its final reference marker reverses page numbers.
The following explicit bhavāgra example and generalization
to the remaining attainments control the support reading.
The verse's vṛthādharam and the running vṛthā'dharam
are understood through the same prose explanation;
no critical emendation of the witnesses is undertaken.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the distance, mode difference,
practitioner qualifications, and support relation. Yoga
Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns the effective
exercise that distinguishes preparation from accomplishment.
The completed example requires the coordinated operation
of distinctions already established in the preceding studies.

For Organon representation, separate traversal position
from rebirth-support. Preserve the general rule together
with its announced exception, rather than turning
“only” into an unqualified prohibition detached from
the next sentence. This is project reconstruction,
not a further system attributed to the Bhāṣya.
