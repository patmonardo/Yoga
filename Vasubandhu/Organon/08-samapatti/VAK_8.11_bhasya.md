# VAK_8.11 Bhāṣya — The Fourth Dhyāna's Immovability

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> aṣṭāpakṣālamuktatvād āniñjaṃ tu caturthakam |
> vitarkacārau śvāsau ca sukhādi ca catuṣṭayam || 8.11 ||

> Because it is free from eight disturbances, the fourth is immovable: vitarka
> and vicāra, the two breaths, and the four beginning with pleasure.

Feelings of dhyāna *rebirth*: 8.12. Do not move this list there.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.11.md`](./VAK_8.11.md).

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> trīṇi ca dhyānāni … sāpakṣālatvāt /
> ke punas te 'pakṣālāḥ /
> vitarkavicārau sukhaduḥkhe saumanasyadaurmanasye śvāsapraśvāsāś ca /
> eṣām aṣṭānām eko 'py apakṣālaś caturthe nāsty atas tad āneñjyam uktam /
> vitarkavicāraprītisukhair akampanīyatvād … nirvātapradīpanidarśanād ity apare /
> dvayor dhyānayoḥ saumanasyam uktaṃ prītivacanāt / tṛtīye sukhaṃ caturthe upekṣā /

**Witness.** Pradhan 441|11–19. Voices: Vasubandhu; `apare`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The Blessed One called the three dhyānas subject to movement — because they
> still have disturbances. What are those disturbances? Vitarka and vicāra;
> pleasure and pain; pleasant-mindedness and displeasure; in-breath and out-breath.
> Not even one of these eight is in the fourth; hence it is called immovable.
>
> Others: immovable because it cannot be shaken by vitarka, vicāra, rapture, or
> pleasure — the sūtra's lamp in a windless place.
>
> In the first two, pleasant-mindedness is stated through the word prīti; in the
> third, pleasure; in the fourth, equanimity.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
first three still have apakṣāla
eight named; none in the fourth
alternative: four shakers + lamp
feeling-map of attainment (not rebirth)
```

## 5. Finite Exclusion

Vitarka as first-dhyāna limb ≠ kleśa here. All eight do not first vanish only
at four; the pair is already gone from two. Neutral feeling remains; it is not
the upekṣā-factor of the aṅga-list. No breath-holding recipe. Immovable ≠
permanent ≠ liberated.

## 6. The Eight

| | |
|---|---|
| application | vitarka, vicāra |
| breath | śvāsa, praśvāsa |
| four | sukha, duḥkha, saumanasya, daurmanasya |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `sukhādi catuṣṭaya` = those four feelings;
- `śvāsau` = both breaths;
- second explanation is `apare`.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Stability is determinate negation of a listed set — not an emptied field. Vidyā:
the fourth still *is* collected Knowing with neutral feeling and purified limbs.
Technē does not become a wind-stop. Appearance would call the lamp the Absolute.
It is an image for unshaken attainment.
