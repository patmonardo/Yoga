# VAK_8.11 Bhāṣya — The Fourth Dhyāna's Immovability

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> aṣṭāpakṣālamuktatvād āniñjaṃ tu caturthakam |
> vitarkacārau śvāsau ca sukhādi ca catuṣṭayam || 8.11 ||

> The fourth dhyāna, however, is immovable because it is free
> from eight disturbances: vitarka and vicāra, the two breaths,
> and the four beginning with pleasure, namely pleasure, pain,
> pleasant-mindedness, and displeasure.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> [441|11–13] The Blessed One called the first three dhyānas subject to
> movement because they still have disturbances. “The fourth, however,
> is immovable because it is free from eight disturbances.”
>
> [441|14–17] What are those disturbances? “Vitarka and vicāra, the two
> breaths, and the four beginning with pleasure”: vitarka and vicāra;
> pleasure and pain; pleasant-mindedness and displeasure; and inhalation
> and exhalation. Not even one of these eight disturbances is present in
> the fourth dhyāna. Hence it is called immovable.
>
> [441|17–18] Others (*apare*) say that the fourth dhyāna is immovable
> because vitarka, vicāra, joy, and pleasure cannot shake it, as the
> sūtra illustrates with a lamp in a windless place.
>
> [441|19] Pleasant-mindedness is stated for the first two dhyānas by
> the mention of joy; pleasure is stated for the third, and equanimity
> for the fourth.

## 5. Finite Exclusion

Vitarka as first-dhyāna limb ≠ kleśa here. All eight do not first vanish only
at four; the pair is already gone from two. Neutral feeling remains; it is not
the upekṣā-factor of the aṅga-list. No breath-holding recipe. Immovable ≠
permanent ≠ liberated.

## 6. The Eight

| | |
|---|---|
| application | vitarka, vicāra |
| breath | śvāsa, praśvāsa |
| four | sukha, duḥkha, saumanasya, daurmanasya |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `sukhādi catuṣṭaya` = those four feelings;
- `śvāsau` = both breaths;
- second explanation is `apare`.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is reconstruction.

Stability is determinate negation of a listed set — not an emptied field. Vidyā:
the fourth still *is* collected Knowing with neutral feeling and purified limbs.
Technē does not become a wind-stop. Appearance would call the lamp the Absolute.
It is an image for unshaken attainment.
