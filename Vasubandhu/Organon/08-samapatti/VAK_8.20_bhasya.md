# VAK_8.20 Bhāṣya — The Summit Exception and Attainment Support Range

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> āryākiñcanyasāṃmukhyād bhavāgre tv āsravakṣayaḥ |
> satṛṣṇāḥ svabhavālambāḥ dhyānaṃ sadviṣayaṃ śubham || 8.20 ||

> At the summit of existence, however, the destruction of the outflows
> occurs through the direct presence of noble nothingness. Those
> accompanied by craving take own-ground existence as Support. Wholesome
> dhyāna has what exists as its Condition; conditioned and unconditioned
> Things are eligible Supports.

Reading: `sadviṣayam` (what exists), not `ṣaḍviṣayam` (six fields). Formless
Support restrictions: 8.21.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.20.md`](./VAK_8.20.md).

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Having stated a general rule, he gives an exception: “At the
> summit of existence, however, the outflows are exhausted through
> making noble nothingness present.” For one reborn at the summit,
> the outflows are exhausted by making the uncontaminated sphere of
> nothingness present. How can someone reborn there make that
> attainment present? Because there is no [uncontaminated attainment]
> of the summit's own ground, and because that [nothingness
> attainment] has been cultivated.
>
> What is the Support (*ālambana*) of these dhyānas and formless
> attainments? “Those accompanied by craving take existence of their
> own ground as Support.” Those associated with savoring take their
> own-ground existence as Support. “Existence” (*bhava*) here means
> contaminated Thing (*sāsravaṃ vastu*). They do not take a lower
> ground as Support because they are free from passion for it, nor
> a higher ground because the grounds are delimited by craving, nor
> the uncontaminated, since that would entail their being wholesome.
>
> “Wholesome dhyāna has what exists as its Condition.” Wholesome
> dhyāna means both pure and uncontaminated dhyāna. It can take as
> Support whatever exists, whether conditioned or unconditioned.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
8.19 general Basis rule → summit exception
new question: ālambana, the Support of attainment
satṛṣṇa: own-ground contaminated Thing
śubha dhyāna: all existent Things eligible as Support
```

## 5. Range ≠ Omniscience

The exception makes no uncontaminated summit attainment native to
that ground. *Sva-bhava* means own-ground existence, not *svabhāva*
(intrinsic nature). *Sarvālambana* states an eligible class of Supports;
it does not assert that every existent is attended at once. The last
clause names wholesome dhyāna, leaving formless restrictions to 8.21.

## 6. Two Fields

| mode | eligible Supports |
|---|---|
| *satṛṣṇa* | contaminated Thing of its own ground |
| *śubha* dhyāna | whatever exists, conditioned or unconditioned |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- first half = exception, not a new native summit mode;
- `sadviṣayam` = sarvālambana;
- śubha here includes śuddhaka and anāsrava dhyāna.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is reconstruction.

The summit exception joins a being's Basis to an already cultivated
lower attainment that can be made present there. The later question
changes to that attainment's Support and distinguishes the limited
Support of craving-associated modes from the broad eligible range
of wholesome dhyāna. This is the Organon reconstruction of two
different source relations.
