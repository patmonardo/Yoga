# VAK_8.23 Bhāṣya — Intermediate Dhyāna and the Vitarka–Vicāra Classification

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tridhā aduḥkhāsukhaṃ tac ca mahābrahmaphalaṃ ca tat |
> savitarkavicāro 'dhaḥsamādhiḥ parato 'dvayaḥ || 8.23 ||

> That intermediate dhyāna is threefold, neither painful nor pleasant,
> and has Mahābrahmā as its result. Samādhi below it is accompanied
> by vitarka and vicāra; beyond it, samādhi is without both.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> Now that intermediate dhyāna is “threefold”: associated with savoring,
> pure, and uncontaminated. “And it is neither painful nor pleasant.”
> There is neither pleasure nor pain here; “neither painful nor pleasant”
> means that it is associated with the faculty of neutral feeling. It
> is not associated with rapture, because it proceeds with effort. For
> this very reason its progress is difficult. What, then, is the
> distinctive result of that intermediate dhyāna? It is “fruiting in
> Mahābrahmā.” For, having cultivated that intermediate dhyāna, one
> becomes Mahābrahmā.
>
> Again, gathering all samādhis together, three samādhis are stated
> in a sūtra: samādhi with *vitarka* and *vicāra*; without *vitarka*,
> with *vicāra* alone; and without *vitarka* or *vicāra*. Among these,
> intermediate dhyāna has already been identified as the samādhi
> without *vitarka* and with *vicāra* alone, because only *vitarka*
> was negated. Hence: “Below, samādhi has *vitarka* and *vicāra*.”
> Samādhi below intermediate dhyāna has both: first dhyāna and
> *anāgamya*.
>
> “Beyond [it], without the two.” It is “without the two” because
> neither of the pair is present there. Samādhi above intermediate
> dhyāna has neither *vitarka* nor *vicāra*, from the access attainment
> of the second dhyāna up to the summit of existence.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
intermediate dhyāna: three modes, neutral feeling, no rapture
its effortful course: difficult progress, not painful feeling
cultivation result: becoming Mahābrahmā
sūtra: three configurations of vitarka and vicāra
8.23 assigns lower, intermediate, and higher ranges
```

## 5. Three Distinct Determinations

The three modes of intermediate dhyāna are savoring-associated,
pure, and uncontaminated. Neutral feeling and difficult progress
answer different questions: the former concerns associated feeling;
the latter concerns the effortful course. The Mahābrahmā statement
is a cultivation-result claim; the passage does not distribute it
across the three modes separately.

## 6. The Sūtra's Pair

| range | *vitarka* | *vicāra* |
|---|---|---|
| *anāgamya* and first dhyāna | present | present |
| intermediate dhyāna | absent | present |
| second-dhyāna access through the summit | absent | absent |

“Without the two” (*advaya*) negates this named pair. “*Vicāra*
alone” is relative to the pair and does not deny every other mental
factor. No fourth, *vitarka*-only configuration is supplied.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `tridhā` now names intermediate dhyāna's three modes, not the attributed first-access claim of 8.22;
- `aduḥkhāsukham` means association with the neutral-feeling faculty;
- `duḥkhā pratipat` concerns progress, not painful feeling;
- `advayaḥ` negates *vitarka* and *vicāra* from second-dhyāna access upward.

## 8. Interpretation

This section is Organon reconstruction. Mode, feeling, effort, result,
and factor configuration are separate determinations. The source's
ordered factor ranges classify samādhis; they do not require a
particular practitioner to pass through them in that sequence.
