# VAK_6.21 Bhāṣya — Support, Acquisition, and Loss of the Preparatory Roots

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāmāśrayāṇy agradharmān dvayāśrayān labhate 'ṅganā |
> bhūmityāgāt tyajaty āryas tāny anāryas tu mṛtyunā || 6.21 ||

> The four roots conducive to penetration have the Desire Realm as their living
> support. A woman obtains the Highest Mundane Dharmas with both supports. A
> Noble practitioner relinquishes them by leaving their ground; an ordinary
> practitioner relinquishes them at death.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes living support, conditions of production and
manifestation, and status-dependent relinquishment.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

All four roots have a support in the Desire Realm. The first three initially
arise only among humans, on the three continents. Where they have already
been produced, they may become manifest among gods. The fourth may initially
arise among gods too. Women and men obtain the first three with either
bodily support.

A woman obtains the Highest Dharmas with either support; a man obtains them
only with male support. The commentary explains this by acquisition of
non-analytic cessation concerning female status. Its transmitted wording at
this point is uneven, and the explanation remains provisional.

How are the roots relinquished? A Noble practitioner relinquishes those
acquired on a given meditative ground by leaving that ground, and not
otherwise. Leaving a ground means moving to another. An ordinary
practitioner, however, relinquishes them through death: by leaving the
present class of life (`nikāyasabhāga`), whether or not a change of ground
has occurred.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary assigns a Desire Realm living support to all four roots.
It distinguishes their initial production from later manifestation,
records the bodily-support classification, and then specifies two different
conditions for loss according to the practitioner's status.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Kāmāśrayāṇi` concerns living support, not the
six concentration grounds of 6.20. `Utpadyante` is initial arising;
`saṃmukhībhāva` is later manifestation. The sex-support rule is reported
as this Bhāṣya's received classification. `Bhūmityāga` is a change of
meditative ground; `mṛtyu` is specified as relinquishment of a present
`nikāyasabhāga`. The reading `dvacyāśrayān` is preserved in the witness
rather than silently regularized.

**Organon determination.** A future reflective Model may type living
support, meditative ground, production history, manifestation, and loss
condition separately. The status of the practitioner changes the loss
relation. This is project synthesis, not a grammatical claim of the verse.

## 6. Support, Production, and Loss

| Relation | Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|
| Living support | Desire Realm for all four roots |
| Initial production | First three among humans of three continents; fourth also among gods |
| Later manifestation | The first three may appear among gods if produced previously |
| Bodily support | Both for the first three; Highest Dharmas differentiated by woman/man |
| Noble practitioner's loss | Departure from the ground of acquisition |
| Ordinary practitioner's loss | Death, even without departure from that ground |
