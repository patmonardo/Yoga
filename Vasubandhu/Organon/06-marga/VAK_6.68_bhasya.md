# VAK_6.68 Bhāṣya — Ten Dharmas and Their Thirty-Seven Names

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> śraddhā vīryaṃ smṛtiḥ prajñā samādhiḥ prītyupekṣaṇe |
> praśrabdhiśīlasaṃkalpāḥ prajñā hi smṛtyupasthitiḥ || 6.68 ||

> Confidence, energy, recollection, discriminative comprehension, collectedness, joy, and equanimity; pliancy, ethical discipline, and resolve. For the establishment of recollection is discriminative comprehension.

The verse names the ten and begins the account of how named Path-functions are counted by constituent dharma.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The ten constituent dharmas are confidence, energy, recollection, Prajñā, collectedness, joy, equanimity, pliancy, ethical discipline, and resolve. How is that count obtained? The establishments of recollection are Prajñā. The right abandonments are energy, and the bases of spiritual power are collectedness. These three groups have the respective natures of Prajñā, energy, and collectedness.

The faculties and powers are counted directly according to their names: confidence, energy, recollection, collectedness, and Prajñā. The establishments of recollection, the awakening-factor of investigation of dharmas, and right view are Prajñā. The right abandonments, the awakening-factor of energy, and right effort are energy. The bases of spiritual power, the awakening-factor of collectedness, and right collectedness are collectedness. The awakening-factor of recollection and right recollection are recollection.

What remains? The awakening-factors of joy, pliancy, and equanimity, along with right resolve and the ethical Path-factors. These are the remaining five constituent dharmas; thus all Bodhi-aligned factors are ten by constituent dharma. The Vaibhāṣikas count eleven: bodily and verbal actions are distinct, so the ethical Path-factors constitute two dharmas. The commentary then introduces a qualification to the statement that the first three groups have the respective natures just named; 6.69 states it explicitly.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The passage answers the ten-dharma question with a list, then demonstrates the mapping from multiple named functions to recurrent dharmas. It treats the faculties and powers by their names, works through Prajñā, energy, collectedness, and recollection, and accounts for the remaining five. A Vaibhāṣika alternative distinguishes bodily and verbal ethical action and raises the count to eleven. The last sentence sets up 6.69's qualification by predominance.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Prajñā hi smṛtyupasthitiḥ` is rendered as the establishments of recollection counting as Prajñā in their governing operation, not an identity of every associated factor. The distinction between name and constituent dharma governs the reduction from thirty-seven to ten. The Vaibhāṣika count of eleven is attributed to its school; it is not silently merged into the kārikā's ten.

**Organon determination.** A Path-function can bear one canonical name while being counted under a dharma that governs its operation. This preserves both the thirty-seven distinct functions and their recurring constituents. The next verse limits any stronger inference that each practice-complex consists only of its named principal dharma.
