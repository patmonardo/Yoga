# VAK_6.76 Bhāṣya — Unconditioned Liberation, Conditioned Liberation, and Knowing

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> asaṃskṛtā kleśahānam adhimuktas tu saṃskṛtā |
> sāṅgaḥ saiva vimuktī dve jñānaṃ bodhir yathoditā || 6.76 ||

> The unconditioned [liberation] is abandonment of afflictions; the conditioned [liberation] is the liberative determination. The former is counted with the factors. That same conditioned liberation is the two liberations. Knowledge is Bodhi, as previously stated.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Liberation is conditioned and unconditioned. Unconditioned liberation is the abandonment of afflictions; conditioned liberation is the liberative determination of one beyond training. The unconditioned liberation is joined with the factors of one beyond training, whose factors are conditioned. That same conditioned liberation is the two liberations named in the sūtra: liberation of consciousness and liberation through Prajñā. The Base of liberation should be understood in the same way.

An objection cites a sūtra on the purification of liberation: consciousness becomes dispassionate and liberated from desire, hatred, and delusion, and effort and energy serve to complete or sustain the Base of liberation. Thus, is liberation simply `adhimokṣa`? Others answer that liberation is the purity of consciousness when Knowing of reality has removed desire and the other afflictions. Having discussed liberation, the commentary asks which right Knowing is distinct from right view. It answers with the Bodhi previously stated: Knowing of exhaustion and Knowing of non-arising.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening distinguishes the two categories of liberation. `Sāṅgaḥ` then counts unconditioned liberation together with the conditioned factors of one beyond training, while `saiva vimuktī dve` refers to conditioned liberation as the two scriptural liberations. The cited sūtra prompts an attributed alternative: purity of consciousness after Knowing of reality removes afflictions. The closing question moves from liberation to right Knowing and identifies it with the two Knowings that defined Bodhi in 6.67.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Asaṃskṛta-vimukti` is unconditioned abandonment; `saṃskṛta-vimukti` is the Aśaikṣa's `adhimokṣa` in the initial account. The prose resolves `sāṅgaḥ` as association with conditioned Aśaikṣa factors, not a change in the unconditioned liberation's category. `Ceto-vimukti` is liberation of consciousness; `prajñā-vimukti` is liberation through Prajñā. The alternative marked `apare` identifies liberation with purity of consciousness after `tattvajñāna` removes afflictions. `Samyagjñāna` is right Knowing, identified with `kṣayajñāna` and `anutpādajñāna`. The verse translation follows the primary resolution and leaves the alternative in this commentary study.

**Organon determination.** Abandonment, conditioned liberative actuality, and Knowing of completion form distinguishable determinations in the completed Path. This threefold coordination is a project reading of the source's categories and its return to Bodhi, not an additional classification asserted by the kārikā.
