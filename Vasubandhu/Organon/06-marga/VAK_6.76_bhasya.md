# VAK_6.76 Bhāṣya — Abandonment, Living Freedom, Knowledge — Three, Not One

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> asaṃskṛtā kleśahānam adhimuktas tu saṃskṛtā |
> sāṅgaḥ saiva vimuktī dve jñānaṃ bodhir yathoditā || 6.76 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> The unconditioned [liberation] is abandonment of the afflictions, whereas the
> conditioned [liberation] is the liberative determination. [The former is counted]
> together with the factors. That very [conditioned liberation] is the two
> liberations. Knowledge is Bodhi, as previously stated.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> sāṅgā śaikṣāṅgeṣu paṭhyate / na punar asaṃskṛtā saṃskṛtā bhavati /
> saiva … ceto-vimuktiḥ prajñā-vimuktiś ca /
> tattvajñānena rāgadveṣamohāpagamāc cittaviśuddhir vimuktir ity apare /
> samyagjñānaṃ yathoditā bodhiḥ / kṣayajñānam anutpādajñānaṃ ca /

**Witness.** Pradhan 388|03–19. Voice: Vasubandhu; alternative on purity of mind.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Unconditioned liberation: abandonment of the afflictions. Conditioned: the
> Aśaikṣa's *adhimokṣa*. Counted with the factors when joined to them. It does not
> itself become conditioned.
>
> That same conditioned liberation is the two in the sūtra: mind-liberation and
> prajñā-liberation. The liberation-aggregate is read the same way. Others: after
> knowledge of reality has removed desire, hatred, and delusion, purity of mind is
> liberation.
>
> Right knowledge is Bodhi as already said: knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge
> of non-arising.
