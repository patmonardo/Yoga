# VAK_6.04 Bhāṣya — Conventional and Ultimate: What Cognition Survives

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> yatra bhinnena tadbuddhir anyāpohe dhiyā ca tat |
> ghaṭārthavat saṃvṛtisat paramārthasad anyathā || 6.4 ||

> Where, upon division or mental exclusion of other things, cognition of that
> no longer arises, that is conventionally real, as with the meaning “pot”;
> ultimately real is otherwise.

The pot illustrates physical division; water illustrates cognitive exclusion.
The Bhāṣya supplies the converse test for ultimate reality and retains truth
for valid conventional claims.

## 3. Continuous Translation

The truth of origin is now discussed. An objection says this goes beyond the
sūtra, which names craving alone. The reply: craving is named there because it
is principal; other factors too are origin. How is that known? Elsewhere the
Blessed One also names them. One cited verse calls action, craving, and
ignorance causes of formations in a future life. Another passage speaks of
five kinds of seed as a designation for Cognition with appropriation, and of
the earth element as a designation for the four stations of Cognition. The
sūtras thus speak with a particular intention, while the Abhidharma states
the defining characteristic.

The sūtra names craving alone when it states the cause of continued coming
into being. The verse names action, craving, and ignorance when it states
causes of both differentiated rebirth and continued coming into being. The
sūtra says that action is the cause of differentiated rebirth and craving
the cause of continued coming into being. A further sūtra, speaking of
causes, conditions, and origins in the relation of seed and field, also names
Cognition and the other factors. What is the distinction? Differentiated
rebirth is the arising of an individual existence in a particular domain,
destination, womb, and so on. Continued coming into being is reconnection
with another existence without regard to those differences. Action causes
the former; craving for existence causes the latter. A seed determines
whether a sprout is rice, barley, or another kind, whereas water enables
sprouting generally: this is the comparison.

What reasoning makes craving a cause of continued coming into being? Those
free from craving are not reborn. Both those with craving and those without
it die, but only those with craving are seen to be born again. Since there is
no birth without craving, craving is understood as a cause of renewed
existence. The continuity also bends toward what is craved: where craving
is present, the continuum of Consciousness is repeatedly seen to incline.
The same is inferred for another existence. No other factor grasps an
individual existence as craving does; a comparison involving a dry object
and an unguent is damaged in this witness. Nor does another cause cling to
it as love of self does. This is the argument.

The Blessed One has stated the four truths, and also the two truths,
conventional truth and ultimate truth. What distinguishes these two? Where
division into parts causes cognition of that named unity to cease, it is
conventionally real. Thus, when a pot is broken into shards, pot-cognition no
longer occurs. The same applies when other dharmas are excluded in thought
and cognition of the named unity ceases. With water, removing visible form
and other dharmas in thought removes water-cognition.

The designation has been made upon those very dharmas by convention. Hence
people who say “the pot exists” and “water exists” speak truly, not falsely:
this is conventional truth. Ultimate truth is otherwise. Even after the
relevant division, cognition of that remains; even after exclusion of other
dharmas in thought, it is ultimately real. Visible form is an example:
when the Thing is divided atom by atom and other dharmas, such as taste, are
excluded in thought, cognition of visible form's own nature still occurs.
Feeling and the other dharmas are to be understood likewise. What exists in
this ultimate way is ultimate truth.

The former teachers give a further criterion: what is apprehended in the
same way by supramundane knowledge, or by the mundane knowledge acquired
after it, is ultimate truth; what is apprehended otherwise is conventional
truth. The commentary reports this as their account.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening discussion distinguishes craving's prominence in the sūtra from
the broader causal account of origin in the Abhidharma. It separates action's
role in differentiated rebirth from craving's role in renewed coming into
being, then turns from the four truths to the two truths. Physical division is
tested with a pot, cognitive exclusion with water, and persistence of
own-nature cognition with visible form and feeling. It then establishes that
conventional speech can be true. The former teachers' criterion is reported
after the main analysis rather than inserted into the kārikā.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** The verse leaves the loss of `tad-buddhi` implicit;
the prose supplies `na bhavati`. `Anyathā` introduces the persistence of
`svabhāva-buddhi` under analysis. `Ghaṭārtha` retains *artha* as Meaning;
the Bhāṣya's pot is its concrete example. `Apoha` here is the exclusion of
other dharmas in thought, with no further theory of names implied.

**Organon determination.** The Report system can represent the two tests as
operations upon a configuration and its cognition. A future Feature Grammar
can parse the relative-correlative frame; the LogicFrame can represent the
two tests and their distinct results. This is a project model of the
passage's analysis, not a formalism in the Bhāṣya.

## 6. The Two Analytic Tests

| Operation | Conventional case | Ultimate case |
|---|---|---|
| Division into parts | Pot-cognition ceases when the pot becomes shards. | Visible-form cognition remains under atom-by-atom division. |
| Exclusion of other dharmas in thought | Water-cognition ceases when supporting dharmas are excluded. | Visible-form own-nature cognition remains when other dharmas are excluded. |

The Bhāṣya's `satyam eva ... na mṛṣā` confirms that a conventional claim can
be true. Analytical non-finality does not make the pot or water nonexistent.
