# VAK_6.51 Bhāṣya — Stainless Path and Its Twofold Fruit

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> śrāmaṇyam amalo mārgaḥ saṃskṛtāsaṃskṛtaṃ phalam |
> ekānnavatis tāni muktimārgāḥ saha kṣayaiḥ || 6.51 ||

> Śrāmaṇya is the stainless Path. Its fruit is conditioned and unconditioned, in eighty-nine cases: liberation Paths together with their corresponding abandonments.

The uninterrupted Path is the work of śrāmaṇya; conditioned liberation
Paths and unconditioned abandonments are its paired fruits.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The four previously stated fruits are fruits of śrāmaṇya. What is
śrāmaṇya? It is the stainless Path: the uncontaminated Path. By that
Path one becomes a śramaṇa, because afflictions are pacified. The
quoted sūtra says that many harmful, unwholesome dharmas are stilled
by it, continuing through those leading to aging and death. An
ordinary person is not a śramaṇa in the strict sense because the
pacification is not complete.

The fruit of this śrāmaṇya is conditioned and unconditioned. Although
the sūtra states four śrāmaṇya fruits, the detailed reckoning gives
eighty-nine cases. They are liberation Paths together with their
corresponding exhaustions. Eight uninterrupted Paths and eight
liberation Paths concern what must be abandoned by seeing. For what
must be abandoned by cultivation, nine grounds each contain nine
grades of affliction; there are as many uninterrupted and liberation
Paths for them, eighty-one of each. The uninterrupted Paths are
śrāmaṇya itself. The liberation Paths are its conditioned fruits,
as outflow and result of activity. The abandonments of those
afflictions are its unconditioned fruits. Thus the paired cases
number eighty-nine.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary defines śrāmaṇya by the uncontaminated Path, explains
why ordinary practice is not the strict case, and divides its fruit
into conditioned liberation Paths and unconditioned abandonments.
It derives the eighty-nine paired cases from eight by seeing and
eighty-one by cultivation, distinguishing the uninterrupted work
from both kinds of fruit.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** The running verse's `śrāvaṇyam` and
`mārgaṃḥ` are preserved in §2; the study uses the prose's śrāmaṇya
and grammatical `mārgaḥ`. `Amala` is glossed `anāsrava`,
uncontaminated. `Kṣaya` denotes an unconditioned abandonment, not a
second conditioned cognition. Eighty-nine counts paired cases, each
with a conditioned liberation Path and an unconditioned abandonment.

**Organon determination.** The operative uninterrupted Path, its
conditioned successor, and the unconditioned abandonment are distinct.
Treating their articulation as one course of action and fruit is
project synthesis; the source supplies their specific classifications.

## 6. Path and Fruit Reckoning

| Source category | Count | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Abandonment by seeing | 8 | Uninterrupted Paths with corresponding liberation Paths |
| Abandonment by cultivation | 9 × 9 = 81 | Nine grades across nine grounds, each with the same Path pairing |
| Paired cases | 89 | Each liberation Path with corresponding unconditioned abandonment |

The Path of uninterrupted counteraction is śrāmaṇya; the liberation
Path is its conditioned fruit, and affliction-abandonment is its
unconditioned fruit.
