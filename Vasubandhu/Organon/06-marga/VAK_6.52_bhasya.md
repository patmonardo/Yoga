# VAK_6.52 Bhāṣya — Four Fruits and Five Criteria

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> catuṣphalavyavasthā tu pañcakāraṇasaṃbhavāt |
>
> pūrvatyāgo ’nyamārgāptiḥ kṣayasaṃkalanaṃ phale || 6.52 ||

> Although the Path has many individual fruits, four principal fruits are established where five conditions occur together. At each, the candidate's preceding Path is relinquished, a previously unattained fruit Path is acquired, and the abandonments are gathered under one attainment. These are the three conditions named here; the next kārikā adds acquisition of the eight Knowings and cultivation of the sixteen Truth-aspects.

The four principal fruits are positions where five criteria concur.
This kārikā names the first three; the source immediately continues
with the final two.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

An objection follows the eighty-nine paired cases: must the Buddha's
list of four fruits be expanded? No. Though fruits are numerous, a
fruit is established at an abandonment-Path stage in which five
reasons occur together. Which five? Relinquishment of the previous
Path, attainment of another Path, and gathering of abandonments at
the fruit. The next line adds acquisition of the eight Knowings and
cultivation of the sixteen aspects beginning with impermanence.

The commentary explains: the previous Path is relinquished and a
previously unattained Path acquired because the candidate's Path is
relinquished and the fruit Path attained. The abandonments are
gathered because the whole is acquired through one attainment. Eight
Knowings are acquired simultaneously, comprising four kinds each of
Dharma-Knowing and Anvaya-Knowing. The sixteen Truth-aspects beginning
with impermanence are cultivated. All five reasons occur at each of
the four principal fruits.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The objection contrasts the many fruits counted in 6.51 with the
four named in the sūtra. The answer identifies five jointly present
criteria at each principal fruit, then explains the three in 6.52
and the two named in 6.53's first line. Relinquishment, new Path
attainment, and unified acquisition of abandonments define the
transition of Path status.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Pūrva` is the previous candidate Path;
`anya` is glossed `apūrva`, previously unattained. `Kṣayasaṃkalana`
is explained as `prahāṇasaṃkalana` through one acquisition of the
whole. The source's `pañakāraṇa` is retained in §2, while the five
are explicitly enumerated in the prose and shared opening of 6.53.
`Jñāna` is Knowing; the eight are four Dharma-Knowings and four
Anvaya-Knowings.

**Organon determination.** A principal fruit is an attained Path
position where previous candidate status is relinquished, a new Path
attained, and abandonments gathered. Reading this transition as a
reorganization of accomplished content is project synthesis.
