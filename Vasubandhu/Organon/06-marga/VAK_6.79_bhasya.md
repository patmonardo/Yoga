# VAK_6.79 Bhāṣya — Object and Cut Do Not Automatically Marry

Last verse of Mārga-nirdeśa. The chapter ends on a matrix, not a mood.

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirvidyate duḥkha-hetu-kṣānti-jñānair virajyate |
> sarvair jahāti yair evaṃ catuṣkoṭika-saṃbhavaḥ || 6.79 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> One becomes disenchanted through the receptivities and knowledges concerning
> suffering and its cause; one becomes dispassionate through all those by which one
> abandons. Thus a fourfold alternative is possible.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_6.79.md`](./VAK_6.79.md).

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> duḥkhe samudayakṣāntijñānair eva nirvidyate nānyaiḥ /
> sarvair api duḥkha-samudaya-nirodha-mārga-kṣāntijñānair virajyate / yaiḥ kleśān prajahāti /
> nirvidyata eva … kleśān aprajahat / virajyata eva … prāmodyavastvālambanatvāt /
> ubhayaṃ … / nobhayam … /
> vītarāgaḥ satyāni paśyan … na prajahāti /
> jñānais tu prayoga-vimukti-viśeṣamārgasaṃgṛhītair na prajahāti /

**Witness.** Pradhan 389|21 ff. Voice: Vasubandhu. End of chapter prose.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> One is disenchanted only by the receptivities and knowledges of suffering and
> origin — not by the others. Those take a basis fit for turning away.
>
> One is dispassionate by receptivities and knowledges of all four Truths — through
> those by which she actually abandons kleśas. Cessation and Path take a basis of
> gladness.
>
> Four corners: disenchanted only; dispassionate only; both; neither. Already
> dispassionate, seeing the Truths, she need abandon nothing further. Knowledges held
> in Application, Release, and Further Distinction also need not abandon.

## 6. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- *sarvaiḥ* is restricted by *yaiḥ kleśān prajahāti*;
- *hetu* here is *samudaya*;
- *catuṣkoṭi* is a Path-matrix, not four truth-values.
