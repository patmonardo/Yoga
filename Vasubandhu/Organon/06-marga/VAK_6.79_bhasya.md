# VAK 6.79 Bhāṣya — Disenchantment, Dispassion, and Four Cases

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirvidyate duḥkha-hetu-kṣānti-jñānair virajyate |
> sarvair jahāti yair evaṃ catuṣkoṭika-saṃbhavaḥ || 6.79 ||

> One becomes disenchanted through acceptances and Knowings concerning suffering and its cause; one becomes dispassionate through those acceptances and Knowings concerning all four Truths by which one abandons afflictions. Thus a fourfold possibility arises.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Does one also become dispassionate toward the Thing toward which one becomes disenchanted? There are four cases. How so? “One becomes disenchanted through acceptances and Knowings concerning suffering and its cause.” One becomes disenchanted only through the acceptances and Knowings concerning suffering and origination, not through others. “[One becomes] dispassionate through all [those] by which one abandons.” One becomes dispassionate through acceptances and Knowings concerning all four—suffering, origination, cessation, and Path—when one abandons afflictions through them. “Thus a fourfold possibility arises.”

Thus the four cases are established. One is only disenchanted through acceptances and Knowings concerning suffering and origination, while [not] abandoning afflictions, because these take a Thing of disenchantment as Support. One is only dispassionate through acceptances and Knowings concerning cessation and Path while abandoning afflictions, because these take a Thing of gladness as Support. One is both through the former while abandoning afflictions. One is neither through the latter while not abandoning afflictions.

In this regard, someone already dispassionate, seeing the Truths through *dharmajñānakṣānti*, does not abandon afflictions. Nor does one abandon them through Knowings included in the Paths of Application, Release, and Further Distinction.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening question tests whether the Thing that supports disenchantment must also support dispassion. The verse gives different conditions: suffering and origination as the Support for disenchantment, actual affliction-abandonment as the condition for dispassion, available through acceptances and Knowings concerning all four Truths. The prose then populates all four corners and gives examples of Path moments that do not abandon afflictions. The conclusion keeps what is taken as Support distinct from what a Path moment does.

## 5. Witness and Translation Decisions

**The first corner.** At [390|09], the local witness has `kleśān prajahat`, literally “while abandoning afflictions,” after `nirvidyata eva`, “only disenchanted.” This conflicts with the stated rule that abandonment yields dispassion and with the later “both” case, which also has `prajahat`. [Xuanzang's Chinese Kośa, T1558 25 at 134b03–07](https://github.com/DILA-edu/cbeta-normal-text/blob/main/T/T29/T29n1558/025.txt#L2048-L2055) explicitly makes the first corner suffering/origination acceptances and Knowings that **do not** cause afflictions to be abandoned. The translation therefore marks the contextual negation as **[not]**, without altering the Sanskrit. The Sanskrit reading remains a witness uncertainty, not a silently corrected text.

**Technical distinctions.** `Jñāna` is Knowing; `kṣānti` is acceptance in this Path context. `Ālambana` is Support and `vastu` is Thing in `nirvedavastvālambanatvāt` and `prāmodyavastvālambanatvāt`. The latter phrase does not say that cessation and Path are themselves disenchanted; their Things are supports of gladness. `Duḥkhahetu` is unfolded as suffering and origination. The final non-abandoning examples restrict a broad claim that every encounter with a Truth cuts an affliction.

**Organon determination.** The four cases cross the kind of Thing taken as Support with the Path moment's actual abandoning efficacy. This two-coordinate formulation reconstructs the stated cases; the source gives the question, the rules, and the examples.

## 6. Four Cases

| Case | Thing taken as Support | Abandons afflictions? | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Suffering or origination | No; supplied against local `prajahat` | Disenchantment only |
| Second | Cessation or Path | Yes | Dispassion only |
| Third | Suffering or origination | Yes | Both |
| Fourth | Cessation or Path | No | Neither |

The Bhāṣya's last examples show why Truth-content alone cannot settle the second coordinate.
