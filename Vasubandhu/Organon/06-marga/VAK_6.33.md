# VAK_6.33 — The Ninefold Refinement of Fault and Path

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नवप्रकारा दोषा हि भूमौ भूमौ तथा गुणाः ।
मृदुमध्याधिमात्राणां पुनर्मृद्वादिभेदतः ॥ ६.३३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
nava-prakārā doṣā hi bhūmau bhūmau tathā guṇāḥ |
mṛdu-madhyādhimātrāṇāṃ punar mṛdv-ādi-bhedataḥ || 6.33 ||
```

The gradual analysis begins by grading both affliction and its opposing
Path within each ground. The Bhāṣya explains the nine grades and their
inverse pairing.


The kārikā supplies the common ninefold scale. The Bhāṣya identifies the *guṇas* more precisely as the uninterrupted and liberation paths and explains their inverse pairing with the faults.

## 4. Grammar

**nava-prakārāḥ** predicates a ninefold differentiation of both nominative subjects, **doṣāḥ** and, through **tathā**, **guṇāḥ**. The distributive repetition **bhūmau bhūmau** gives the classification recurrent scope: it applies in each relevant ground.

The ablative **mṛdu-ādi-bhedataḥ** expresses the reason for the number nine. The three primary grades collected by **mṛdu-madhya-adhimātrāṇām** are **again** (*punaḥ*) differentiated by the same sequence beginning with mild:

```text
mild, medium, strong
    each divided into
mild, medium, strong
    = nine grades
```

The verse itself states neither the order in which the faults are abandoned nor the exact pairing of Path-grade with fault-grade. Those are Bhāṣya determinations.

The inverse pairing is supplied by the prose; the verse itself gives the
recursive ninefold grading in each ground.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In every ground, faults are of nine kinds, and so are the qualities,
> because mild, medium, and strong are each divided again beginning
> with mild.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In each ground, afflictions are ninefold, and so too are the qualities; mild,
> medium, and intense are each further divided beginning with mild.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The gradual Path does not confront obstruction as one undifferentiated mass. Every ground contains an ordered field of faults and an equally articulated series of powers capable of removing them.

Yet these series do not rise together. The coarse obstruction is exposed and removed first by the Path in its initial strength. What remains is weaker as affliction but subtler as a task; consequently, the Path must become more powerful and discriminating. Progress is therefore a refinement in which each completed removal produces the more exact field of the next movement.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Nava-prakāra` — nine grades**

The nine grades result from recursively applying one threefold distinction:

| Primary grade | Internal grades |
|---|---|
| mild (*mṛdu*) | mild-mild, mild-medium, mild-strong |
| medium (*madhya*) | medium-mild, medium-medium, medium-strong |
| strong (*adhimātra*) | strong-mild, strong-medium, strong-strong |

This is an ordered qualitative matrix, not a numerical measurement.

**`Bhūmi` — each ground**

Here **bhūmi** is a ground or level within the graded path-field. The repeated locative gives distributive force. The Bhāṣya extends the recurrence as far as the summit of existence (*bhavāgra*).

**`Doṣa` — fault or affliction**

**Doṣa** denotes the fault or afflictive factor to be abandoned. The Bhāṣya treats the early object as coarse and powerful, while the final residue is mild but subtle. Magnitude and difficulty of discrimination must therefore be distinguished.

**`Guṇa` — opposing Path quality**

The Bhāṣya specifies the positive series as the qualities called **ānantarya-mārga** and **vimukti-mārga**: the uninterrupted Path that effects abandonment and the liberation Path that possesses the resulting disconnection. Each pair is graded within the ninefold progression.

**Inverse correspondence — Path grade and fault grade**

The Bhāṣya pairs Path-power and affliction in reverse order:

| Path-grade | Affliction abandoned |
|---|---|
| mild-mild | strong-strong |
| mild-medium | strong-medium |
| mild-strong | strong-mild |
| medium-mild | medium-strong |
| medium-medium | medium-medium |
| medium-strong | medium-mild |
| strong-mild | mild-strong |
| strong-medium | mild-medium |
| strong-strong | mild-mild |

The strongest Path cannot be present at the outset; once it is present, the strongest affliction cannot remain. The order must therefore be developmental rather than a direct matching of equal strengths.

**Coarse and subtle — order of removal**

Vasubandhu compares the process to cleaning cloth: coarse dirt is removed before subtle dirt. He also compares it to illumination: slight light dispels coarse darkness, while stronger light is required to eliminate subtle darkness. The final task is difficult not because its affliction is greater, but because its residue is finer.

**Bright and dark dharmas — causal asymmetry**

The Bhāṣya says that bright or wholesome dharmas are powerful whereas dark or unwholesome dharmas are weak. This explains why even a momentary mild Noble Path can uproot a strong affliction reinforced throughout beginningless saṃsāra. This is a local account of causal asymmetry, not a general metaphysics of good and evil.

## 8. Logical Determination

The kārikā first defines a recursive grading function:

```text
Grade = {mild, medium, strong}
NinefoldGrade = Grade × Grade

for each Ground:
    FaultSeries   : NinefoldGrade
    QualitySeries : NinefoldGrade
```

The Bhāṣya then defines an order-reversing correspondence:

```text
Path[1] removes Fault[9]
Path[2] removes Fault[8]
...
Path[9] removes Fault[1]
```

Thus Path-growth and residual affliction move oppositely:

```text
Path power:          mild → strong
remaining fault:  coarse → subtle
```

Each successful operation alters the conditions of the next:

```text
fault abandoned
    → field purified
    → subtler residue becomes the determinate task
    → stronger Path-grade becomes necessary
    → next abandonment
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, VAK 6.33 articulates the internal morphology of renewed *prayoga*. The new End is not an indefinitely distant totality; it is the next determinate grade of residue. Because the End is finite and exact, a definite course of action is possible.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya identifies the `guṇas` as the paired Path
functions, extends the grading through the summit of existence, and gives the
inverse order of Path-power and affliction. The cloth and light analogies
remain in the continuous translation.


VAK 6.32 established that fruit-position does not automatically become further candidacy. VAK 6.33 now supplies the morphology of the renewed course. The practitioner does not merely resolve to “advance”; the field itself is differentiated into finite tasks, and each Path-grade has a determinate work.

This also deepens the principle of leaving results behind. A completed abandonment is retained as a real transformation, but it cannot be made the final object of possession. Its truth is shown by the changed field it produces: what had been concealed as subtle residue now becomes the next End.

Accordingly, the Path does not transcend form by becoming indeterminate. It transcends each completed form through a more exact determination. The movement is severe because increasing purification does not make discrimination unnecessary; it demands a stronger light for a subtler darkness.

The ninefold classification belongs to the kārikā. The identification of the qualities as uninterrupted and liberation Paths, the inverse correspondence, the cloth and light analogies, and the causal power of bright dharmas belong to the Bhāṣya. The morphology of renewed *prayoga*, the finite next End, and the transcendence of achieved form are Organon synthesis.
