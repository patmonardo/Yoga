# VAK 6.65 — The Fourfold Operation of Mārga

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

अशैक्षपरिपूर्णत्वं द्वाभ्यां मार्गः समासतः ।
विशेषमुक्त्यानन्तर्यप्रयोगाख्यश्चतुर्विधः ॥ ६.६५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

aśaikṣaparipūrṇatvaṃ dvābhyāṃ mārgaḥ samāsataḥ |
viśeṣamuktyānantaryaprayogākhyaś caturvidhaḥ || 6.65 ||

The opening completes the preceding account of relative completeness. The remainder changes level: after the many divisions of persons and Path-stages, the Bhāṣya asks what Mārga is **in summary**.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

aśaikṣa-paripūrṇatvam | dvābhyām | mārgaḥ | samāsataḥ |
viśeṣa-mukti-ānantarya-prayoga-ākhyaḥ | catur-vidhaḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **aśaikṣa-paripūrṇatvam** | neuter nominative singular compound | completeness proper to one beyond training |
| **dvābhyām** | instrumental dual | by two respects: faculty and attainment |
| **mārgaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | the Path as the subject of the summary classification |
| **samāsataḥ** | indeclinable adverb | in summary, comprehensively |
| **viśeṣa** | compound member | further distinction or development |
| **mukti** | compound member | release |
| **ānantarya** | compound member | immediacy; direct abandonment without an interval |
| **prayoga** | compound member | application or effective preparation |
| **ākhyaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | bearing the names, called |
| **catur-vidhaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | fourfold |

The metrical order of the four names is **viśeṣa–mukti–ānantarya–prayoga**. Their explanatory operational order is **prayoga–ānantarya–vimukti–viśeṣa**.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause is elliptical: the copula “is” is understood between **aśaikṣaparipūrṇatvam** and **dvābhyām**. The instrumental dual is supplied by the Bhāṣya as **indriya** and **samāpatti**.

In the second sentence, **mārgaḥ** is qualified by both **caturvidhaḥ** and the long name-compound ending in **-ākhyaḥ**. The syntax states a classification rather than narrating four events in their chronological order.

The Bhāṣya nevertheless defines three members by their operative relations:

```text
prayoga
    that immediately preceding ānantarya

ānantarya
    that which abandons the obstruction

vimukti
    that which first arises free from it
```

**Viśeṣa** is then defined residually as Path other than those three.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The completeness of one beyond training is through two. In summary, the Path is fourfold, bearing the names Further Distinction, Release, Immediacy, and Application.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

An Aśaikṣa is classified as complete with respect to two dimensions, faculty and contemplative attainment, since possession of the final fruit is already entailed by being beyond training. Considered comprehensively, Mārga is fourfold: the Path of Application, which immediately prepares the effective Path; the Path of Immediacy, which abandons the obstruction; the Path of Release, which first arises free from it; and the Path of Further Distinction, comprising Path-development beyond those three.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The completed Path-person remains internally articulable through operative faculty and contemplative attainment, although final fruit can no longer distinguish completeness: without that fruit there would be no Aśaikṣa.

When the classifications of persons are gathered back into Mārga itself, the Path exhibits four functions. It prepares the determinate transformation, directly removes the obstruction, first actualizes freedom from that obstruction, and continues beyond that first freedom toward stronger, higher, and ultimately final realization.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Aśaikṣa completeness**

For a trainee, 6.64 allowed fruit to function as one dimension of relative completeness. For an Aśaikṣa:

```text
absence of final fruit
    → not an Aśaikṣa at all
```

Fruit is therefore presupposed rather than counted again. The two remaining respects are:

```text
indriya
    faculty

samāpatti
    contemplative attainment
```

The Bhāṣya gives three configurations:

```text
faculty alone
    occasion-independent and prajñā-liberated

attainment alone
    occasion-dependent and doubly liberated

faculty + attainment
    occasion-independent and doubly liberated
```

These differences concern the fullness of capacity and attainment, not whether liberation from the afflictions has been achieved.

**The many Path-divisions**

Vasubandhu observes that many valid distinctions have accumulated:

```text
mundane / supramundane
Seeing / cultivation / beyond-training
Application / Immediacy / Release / Further Distinction
```

The question **samāsataḥ** asks for their operative summary, not the cancellation of the earlier classifications.

**Prayoga-mārga — Path of Application**

The Bhāṣya defines it relationally as that immediately after which the Path of Immediacy arises.

```text
prayoga-mārga
    configures the proximate condition
        → ānantarya-mārga
```

It is not application in the generic sense of any effort. It is effective preparation for the abandoning operation.

**Ānantarya-mārga — Path of Immediacy**

This is the Path by which the relevant **āvaraṇa**, obstruction, is abandoned.

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    directly effects prahāṇa
```

“Immediacy” preserves the absence of an intervening moment between this effective operation and the release that follows. “Uninterrupted Path” is a possible conventional rendering, but should not obscure its direct abandoning function.

**Vimukti-mārga — Path of Release**

The Bhāṣya distinguishes removal from the first actuality free of what was removed:

```text
ānantarya
    abandons the obstruction

vimukti
    first arises free from that obstruction
```

Thus determinate negation and released actuality are adjacent but non-identical Path-functions.

**Viśeṣa-mārga — Path of Further Distinction**

Vasubandhu first defines this broadly:

```text
viśeṣa-mārga
    Path other than the preceding three
```

“Further Distinction” is therefore preferable here to a vague “special Path.” The Bhāṣya explains its continued Path-status through:

```text
greater intensity within the same class
progressively higher attainment
entry into nirvāṇa without remainder
```

These explanations show positive advancement, but the residual definition cautions against forcing every occurrence into one rigid fourth temporal moment.

**Why all four are Mārga**

The Bhāṣya defines Mārga teleologically:

```text
eṣa hi nirvāṇasya panthā
    this is the road to nirvāṇa

etena tadgamanāt
    because by it one goes there
```

Vimukti and Viśeṣa remain Path because first freedom is still capable of intensification, higher attainment, and final consummation.

## 8. Logical Determination

The principal operational relations are:

```text
Prayoga
    immediatelyPrecedes Ānantarya

Ānantarya
    abandons Obstruction

Vimukti
    firstArisesFreeFrom Obstruction

Viśeṣa
    developsBeyond InitialRelease
```

The crucial non-identities are:

```text
Preparation ≠ EffectiveAbandonment
EffectiveAbandonment ≠ FirstReleasedActuality
FirstReleasedActuality ≠ EntireDevelopmentOfFreedom
```

And the Aśaikṣa rule is:

```text
Aśaikṣa(x)
    → possessesFinalFruit(x)

therefore

FinalFruit
    is a constitutive presupposition,
    not a variable axis of Aśaikṣa completeness
```

The fourfold summary is not merely a list of kinds. It articulates the relations necessary for the Path to be an effective course to its End.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse gives exact Kośa form to the Path as an objective course of action. The End—nirvāṇa—is not merely appended as a later result. It determines why these operations together count as Mārga: they are the road by which that End is reached.

Yet the End does not abolish movement at the first occurrence of release:

```text
application
    prepares the movement

immediacy
    performs its determinate negation

release
    is the first actuality of the End

further distinction
    develops that actuality toward consummation
```

This strongly supports the project insight that Beginning and End are the two sides of one root principle of movement. Prayoga is not an arbitrary beginning; it is preparation already governed by the End. Vimukti is not a result lying inert outside action; it becomes an operative Path-position from which further realization proceeds.

That is an Organon reconstruction, not terminology supplied by Vasubandhu. The source’s own secure determination is already powerful: removal, released actuality, and the further development of freedom cannot be collapsed into one undifferentiated event.
