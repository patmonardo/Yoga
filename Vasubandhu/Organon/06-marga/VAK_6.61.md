# VAK 6.61 — The Measure and Ground of Faculty-Transformation

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

दृष्ट्याप्ततायामेकैकः अनास्रवाः नृषु वर्धनम् ।
अशैक्षो नव निश्रित्य भूमीः शैक्षस्तु षट् यतः ॥ ६.६१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

dṛṣṭyāptatāyām ekaikaḥ anāsravāḥ nṛṣu vardhanam |
aśaikṣo nava niśritya bhūmīḥ śaikṣas tu ṣaṭ yataḥ || 6.61 ||

The working verse is textually awkward, and the corresponding prose witness has a damaged form where it immediately glosses the intended reference as **dṛṣṭiprāptāyāṃ pratividhyamānāyām**. The Bhāṣya securely determines the doctrinal sense without warranting a silent reconstruction of the kārikā.

The first pāda's agreement is compressed. The Bhāṣya resolves **ekaikaḥ** by explicitly stating one **ānantarya-mārga** and one **vimukti-mārga**.

## 4. Grammar

The locative **dṛṣṭyāptatāyām** sets the transformation under discussion: becoming *dṛṣṭiprāpta*. Distributive **ekaikaḥ** gives one of each member of the familiar Path-pair. Plural **anāsravāḥ** characterizes all Paths effecting noble faculty-transition, while **nṛṣu** locates the strengthening among humans.

In the second line, **aśaikṣaḥ** and **śaikṣaḥ** are contrasted subjects of **niśritya**. The former relies upon nine grounds, the latter upon six. The terminal **yataḥ** deliberately opens a causal continuation; the Bhāṣya's explanation crosses into the beginning of 6.62.

The verse consequently determines four axes:

```text
measure of Path-work
quality of Path
existential basis
available contemplative grounds
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

In attaining the seeing-attained condition, there is one of each; [the Paths] are uncontaminated. Strengthening occurs among humans. The one beyond training relies upon nine grounds, but the trainee upon six, because—

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Transformation into the *dṛṣṭiprāpta* condition requires one uninterrupted Path and one liberation-path, preceded in every case by a single preparatory Path. All Paths effecting faculty-transition for noble persons are uncontaminated, and such strengthening occurs only among humans, where regression is possible. An Aśaikṣa can rely upon nine contemplative grounds, whereas a Śaikṣa can rely upon six, excluding the formless attainments; the causal explanation continues into the next verse.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Path measures transformation according to the depth of the form being changed. Becoming seeing-attained requires one direct counteraction and one achieved liberation; becoming unshakeable required nine pairs because the weaker morphology had been habituated more deeply. Transformation is neither arbitrary nor uniform.

Nor does pure Path-capacity operate from an abstract nowhere. It requires an uncontaminated means, an existential field in which faculty-mobility occurs, and a contemplative ground containing the fruit/path complex that can actually be replaced.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**One pair for the dṛṣṭiprāpta transition**

The Bhāṣya gives the exact structure:

```text
one prayoga-mārga
    → one ānantarya-mārga
    → one vimukti-mārga
    → dṛṣṭiprāpta condition
```

The preparatory Path is one in every faculty-transition. What varies is the number of direct counteractions and liberating acquisitions: one pair here, but nine pairs for transition to *akopya* in 6.60.

**Depth and measure of transformation**

```text
dṛṣṭiprāpta transition
    one direct removal
    + one liberated acquisition

akopya transition
    nine direct removals
    + nine liberated acquisitions
```

The number of Path-moments corresponds to the resistance of the prior faculty-configuration, not merely to the rank of the desired label.

**All Paths are uncontaminated**

The Bhāṣya states that noble faculty-transition cannot occur through a contaminated Path:

```text
sāsrava-mārga
    ≠ means of Ārya indriya-saṃcāra

anāsrava-mārga
    → transforms noble Path-capacity
```

This does not deny worldly preparation generally. It specifies the quality of the Paths that directly accomplish the noble faculty-transition under discussion.

**The human basis**

The Bhāṣya's reason is narrow and technical: faculty-transition occurs among humans because **parihāṇi**, regression, is possible there.

```text
human field
    admits relevant mobility and regression
    → admits indriya-saṃcāra
```

The verse does not explain this through suffering, *saṃvega*, or a general superiority of human life. Those determinations from 6.55 should not be imported into this distinct argument.

**Nine grounds for the Aśaikṣa**

The Bhāṣya enumerates:

```text
anāgamya                 1
dhyānāntara              1
four dhyānas             4
three lower ārūpyas      3
                         —
                         9
```

These are bases from which the one beyond training can effect faculty-transition.

**Six grounds for the Śaikṣa**

The trainee's range excludes the formless grounds:

```text
anāgamya                 1
dhyānāntara              1
four dhyānas             4
                         —
                         6
```

The reason is supplied by the immediate continuation into 6.62: faculty-strengthening replaces a fruit together with its weak-faculty Path by the corresponding sharp-faculty fruit/Path, and no Anāgāmin fruit is included in a formless ground. This explanation is recorded here only to resolve **yataḥ**; its full analysis belongs to 6.62.

**Transformation as replacement**

The continuation also blocks an additive model:

```text
weak-faculty fruit/path
    → relinquished

corresponding sharp-faculty fruit/path
    → acquired
```

Ground matters because the actual Path-complex being transformed must exist on that ground.

## 8. Logical Determination

The number of moments is indexed to the transformation:

```text
TransitionTo(SeeingAttained)
    → requiresExactly(OneUninterruptedPath)
    → requiresExactly(OneLiberationPath)

TransitionTo(Unshakeable)
    → requiresExactly(NineUninterruptedPaths)
    → requiresExactly(NineLiberationPaths)
```

The Path-quality and existential restriction are:

```text
FacultyTransitionOfArya
    → effectedBy(UncontaminatedPath)
    → occursAmong(Humans)
```

The operative field differs by status:

```text
Aśaikṣa
    → mayRelyOn(NineGrounds)

Śaikṣa
    → mayRelyOn(SixGrounds)
    → excludes(FormlessGrounds)
```

The exclusion is relational rather than merely hierarchical:

```text
not Contains(FormlessGround, AnagaminFruit)
    → cannotReplaceThere(AnagaminWeakFacultyPath,
                         AnagaminSharpFacultyPath)
```

Thus capability is determined jointly by Path-status, fruit, and available ground.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.61 makes faculty-development into a genuine Path-operation rather than a vague increase in personal strength. The current morphology is directly counteracted, the new morphology is positively possessed, and the amount of mediation is proportionate to how deeply the former configuration has been constituted.

In the Organon reconstruction, *dhātu–indriya* again appears as Beginning and End in one process. The ground is the determinate domain in which transformation is possible; faculty is the formed capacity being transformed; and the paired Path-moments are the course through which one morphology becomes another.

But the field is not generated by subjective aspiration alone. A transition is unavailable where the corresponding fruit/path complex does not exist. The Bhāṣya therefore keeps Practical Meaning objective: the possible action is defined by the real relation among practitioner-status, meditative ground, existing fruit, and uncontaminated Path.
