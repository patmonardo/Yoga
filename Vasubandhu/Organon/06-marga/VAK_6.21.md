# VAK 6.21 — Support, Acquisition, and Loss of the Preparatory Roots

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

कामाश्रयाण्यग्रधर्मान् द्वयाश्रयान् लभतेऽङ्गना ।
भूमित्यागात्त्यजत्यार्यस्तान्यनार्यस्तु मृत्युनाः ॥ ६.२१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

kāmāśrayāṇy agradharmān dvayāśrayān labhate ’ṅganā |
bhūmityāgāt tyajaty āryas tāny anāryas tu mṛtyunā || 6.21 ||

The local Devanāgarī witness ends **मृत्युनाः**, while the IAST and syntax require instrumental **mṛtyunā**, “through death.” The study preserves the Devanāgarī witness as received and marks the grammatical construal rather than silently altering the source layer.

## 4. Grammar

**kāmāśrayāṇi** is a complete initial predication whose understood subject is the four *nirvedhabhāgīyāni* of 6.20: “[All four] have the desire-domain as their support.” The neuter plural agrees with that inherited subject.

The next clause has **aṅganā** as subject and **agradharmān dvayāśrayān** as its accusative object: “a woman obtains Highest Mundane Dharmas [connected with] both supports.” The Bhāṣya supplies the contrasting rule that a man obtains them only with male support.

The second line contains two contrasted clauses. In **āryaḥ tāni tyajati**, the Noble practitioner is subject and the four preparatory roots are the object; **bhūmityāgāt** states the condition of their relinquishment. In **anāryas tu mṛtyunā**, the verb and object are carried over: “but the non-Noble [relinquishes them] through death.”

The parallel construction makes the difference exact:

```text
Noble practitioner     → loss by abandonment of the ground
non-Noble practitioner → loss by death
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

[The four] have the desire-domain as their support. A woman obtains the Highest Dharmas with both supports. A Noble practitioner relinquishes them through abandonment of the level, but a non-Noble through death.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

All four factors conducive to penetration depend upon a living support in the desire-domain, even when their meditative ground belongs to concentration. A woman may obtain the Highest Mundane Dharmas in connection with either female or male bodily support, whereas a man obtains them only with male support. A Noble practitioner loses these roots only by leaving the meditative level on which they were acquired; an ordinary practitioner loses them at death with the ending of the present life-continuum.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The cultivation-ground of a Path-state and the living continuum that supports it are not identical. The preparatory roots may operate upon refined concentration-levels while remaining embodied in a desire-domain support. The Path is therefore situated through intersecting conditions rather than assigned to one undifferentiated “level.”

Acquisition also has a history. According to the Bhāṣya, capacities first generated under one set of conditions may later become manifest under another. Most decisively, the manner in which a preparation is lost depends upon the practitioner's status: for the ordinary person it remains contingent upon the present life-continuum, whereas for the Noble practitioner its loss is tied specifically to departure from its attained meditative ground.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**āśraya**

Here **āśraya** is the living psycho-physical support of the preparatory roots. All four are **kāmāśraya**, supported within the desire-domain. This does not contradict 6.20, which located their cognitive operation on concentration-grounds.

**bhūmi**

**Bhūmi** is the meditative or cognitive ground upon which a root was obtained. The distinction is:

```text
āśraya — the living continuum supporting the cognition
bhūmi  — the concentration-level on which it operates
```

A desire-domain living support can consequently sustain a cognition belonging to a form-domain concentration-ground.

**Initial production and later manifestation**

The Bhāṣya states that the first three roots—Heat, Summits, and Acceptance—initially arise only among human beings on the three inhabited continents. If produced previously, however, they may later become manifest among gods. The Highest Mundane Dharmas may first arise among gods as well.

This distinguishes:

```text
utpāda             — first production of the capacity
saṃmukhībhāva      — its later presence or manifestation
```

The capacity therefore possesses a determinate causal history; manifestation need not repeat the conditions of first acquisition.

**dvayāśraya and the received sex-support classification**

The Bhāṣya records a technical Abhidharma classification. The first three roots can be obtained with either female or male bodily support. For the Highest Mundane Dharmas, a woman can obtain forms related to either support, whereas a man obtains only those related to male support. The explanation invokes non-analytic cessation (*apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha*) with respect to femaleness.

This is a received physiological classification local to the passage. It should be recorded accurately without being expanded into a general philosophical claim about sex or spiritual capacity.

**bhūmityāga and bhūmisaṃcāra**

For a Noble practitioner, relinquishment occurs through abandoning the level on which the root was acquired. The Bhāṣya explains this abandonment as **bhūmisaṃcāra**, movement to another meditative level. Apart from such a change, the Noble practitioner does not lose the root.

**mṛtyu and nikāyasabhāgatyāga**

The ordinary practitioner loses the roots through death, explained as relinquishing the present **nikāyasabhāga**, the current life-continuum or class-membership. The Bhāṣya says this occurs whether or not there has also been movement between meditative levels.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 6.21 distinguishes four dimensions of a Path-capacity:

```text
support       = the living continuum in which it occurs
ground        = the meditative level on which it was acquired
genesis       = the conditions of its first production
manifestation = its becoming present after acquisition
```

The loss relation then varies by practitioner-status:

```text
if practitioner is Noble:
    relinquishment requires abandonment of the attained ground

if practitioner is non-Noble:
    relinquishment occurs with death of the present continuum
```

This passage does not yet enumerate the moments of the Path of Seeing. It completes the support-conditions of the preparations that bring that Path on.

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, operative Meaning now has a **support-signature**:

```text
PathMeaning = content
            + cultivated power
            + living support
            + cognitive ground
            + acquisition history
            + manifestation conditions
            + loss conditions
```

Meaning is therefore not adequately represented as a proposition detached from the continuum in which it is formed, sustained, manifested, and relinquished.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.20 classified the preparatory roots by function, production-mode, and meditative ground. VAK 6.21 adds living support and mode of loss. The result is a multidimensional account: a cognition can have a desire-domain embodiment, a form-domain concentration-ground, a prior history of acquisition, and a status-dependent mode of continuity.

This is particularly important for the Path as Practical Meaning. The Truths do not become efficacious in an abstract knower outside conditions. Their determinations exist as capacities of a concrete continuum. Yet those capacities are not reducible to the present bodily occasion, since prior production can support later manifestation.

The difference between Noble and ordinary loss reveals a transformed mode of possession. What is contingent upon death for the ordinary practitioner becomes tied to the specific architecture of attained grounds for the Noble practitioner. The Path changes not only what is cognized, but the conditions under which the acquired cognition persists or is lost.

The kārikā states desire-domain support, the sex-support rule for the Highest Dharmas, and the two modes of relinquishment. The geographical conditions of first production, later divine manifestation, the rule for male practitioners, non-analytic cessation, movement between levels, and *nikāyasabhāga* are Bhāṣya determinations. “Support-signature” is the Organon's reconstruction.
