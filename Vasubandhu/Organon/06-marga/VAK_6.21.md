# VAK 6.21 — Basis, Acquisition, and Loss of the Preparatory Roots

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कामाश्रयाण्यग्रधर्मान् द्वयाश्रयान् लभतेऽङ्गना ।
भूमित्यागात्त्यजत्यार्यस्तान्यनार्यस्तु मृत्युना ॥ ६.२१ ॥
```

## 4. Grammar

**kāmāśrayāṇi** is a complete initial predication whose understood subject is the four *nirvedhabhāgīyāni* of 6.20: “[All four] have a Desire Realm Basis.” The neuter plural agrees with that inherited subject.

The next clause has **aṅganā** as subject and **agradharmān dvayāśrayān** as its accusative object: “a woman obtains Highest Mundane Dharmas [connected with] both bodily Bases.” The Bhāṣya supplies the contrasting rule that a man obtains them only with a male Basis.

The second line contains two contrasted clauses. In **āryaḥ tāni tyajati**, the Noble practitioner is subject and the four preparatory roots are the object; **bhūmityāgāt** states the condition of their relinquishment. In **anāryas tu mṛtyunā**, the verb and object are carried over: “but the non-Noble [relinquishes them] through death.”

The parallel construction makes the difference exact:

```text
Noble practitioner     → loss by abandonment of the ground
non-Noble practitioner → loss by death
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The four have a Desire Realm Basis. A woman obtains the Highest Dharmas
> with both bodily Bases. A Noble practitioner relinquishes them through leaving
> the ground; an ordinary practitioner does so through death.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The four roots conducive to penetration have a Desire Realm living Basis.
> A woman obtains the Highest Mundane Dharmas with both bodily Bases. A
> Noble practitioner relinquishes them by leaving their ground; an ordinary
> practitioner relinquishes them at death.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The cultivation ground of a Path state and the living continuum that sustains it are distinct. The preparatory roots may occur on concentration grounds while having a Desire Realm living Basis. The Path is therefore situated through several specified relations.

Acquisition also has a history. According to the Bhāṣya, capacities first generated under one set of conditions may later become manifest under another. Most decisively, the manner in which a preparation is lost depends upon the practitioner's status: for the ordinary person it remains contingent upon the present life-continuum, whereas for the Noble practitioner its loss is tied specifically to departure from its attained meditative ground.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Āśraya` — living Basis**

The living Basis of the roots. All four have a Desire Realm Basis (*kāmāśraya*), though they occur on concentration grounds.

**`Bhūmi` — ground**

The meditative level on which a root was obtained. A Desire Realm Basis can sustain a cognition on a Form Realm concentration ground.

**`Utpāda` / `saṃmukhībhāva` — first production and later manifestation**

Heat, Summits and Acceptance are first produced only among humans on the three continents, but may later become manifest among gods. The Highest Mundane Dharmas may first arise among gods.

**`Dvayāśraya` — twofold bodily Basis classification**

A received classification. The first three roots may be obtained with a female or male Basis. The Highest Mundane Dharmas may be obtained by a woman with either bodily Basis, but by a man only with a male Basis. Local to this passage.

**`Bhūmi-saṃcāra` — moving between grounds**

A Noble practitioner loses a root only by moving to another meditative level.

**`Nikāya-sabhāga-tyāga` — relinquishing class-membership**

An ordinary practitioner loses the roots at death, with or without a change of level.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 6.21 distinguishes four dimensions of a Path-capacity:

```text
Basis         = the living continuum in which it occurs
ground        = the meditative level on which it was acquired
genesis       = the conditions of its first production
manifestation = its becoming present after acquisition
```

The loss relation then varies by practitioner-status:

```text
if practitioner is Noble:
    relinquishment requires abandonment of the attained ground

if practitioner is non-Noble:
    relinquishment occurs with death of the present continuum
```

This passage does not yet enumerate the moments of the Path of Seeing. It specifies the living Basis and acquisition and loss conditions of the preparations that bring that Path on.

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, operative Meaning now has a **Basis profile**:

```text
PathMeaning = content
            + cultivated power
            + living Basis
            + cognitive ground
            + acquisition history
            + manifestation conditions
            + loss conditions
```

Meaning is therefore not adequately represented as a proposition detached from the continuum in which it is formed, sustained, manifested, and relinquished.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya supplies the distinction between living
Basis and meditative ground, the conditions of first production and later
manifestation, the rule for men, and the explanations of ground departure and
death. Its bodily Basis classification is reported as the passage's own,
without extending it into a general claim about spiritual ability.


VAK 6.20 classified the preparatory roots by function, mode of production, and meditative ground. VAK 6.21 adds living Basis and mode of loss. A cognition can have a Desire Realm living Basis, a Form Realm concentration ground, a prior history of acquisition, and a status dependent mode of continuity.

This is particularly important for the Path as Practical Meaning. The Truths do not become efficacious in an abstract knower outside conditions. Their determinations exist as capacities of a concrete continuum. Yet those capacities are not reducible to the present bodily occasion, since prior production can support later manifestation.

The difference between Noble and ordinary loss reveals a transformed mode of possession. What is contingent upon death for the ordinary practitioner becomes tied to the specific architecture of attained grounds for the Noble practitioner. The Path changes not only what is cognized, but the conditions under which the acquired cognition persists or is lost.

The kārikā states Desire Realm Basis, the bodily Basis rule for the Highest Dharmas, and the two modes of relinquishment. The geographical conditions of first production, later divine manifestation, the rule for male practitioners, nonanalytic cessation, movement between levels, and *nikāyasabhāga* are Bhāṣya determinations. “Basis profile” is the Organon's reconstruction.
