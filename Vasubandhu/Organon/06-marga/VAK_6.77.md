# VAK 6.77 — The Path Abandons; the Aśaikṣa Mind Arises Free

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

विमुच्यते जायमानमशैक्षं चित्तमावृतेः ।
निरुध्यमानो मार्गस्तु प्रजहाति तदावृतिम् ॥ ६.७७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

vimucyate jāyamānam aśaikṣaṃ cittam āvṛteḥ |
nirudhyamāno mārgas tu prajahāti tad-āvṛtim || 6.77 ||

The verse distinguishes the presently operative final Path-moment from the Aśaikṣa mind arising immediately after it. Abandonment and liberated actuality are contiguous, causally ordered, and non-identical.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

nirudhyamānaḥ | mārgaḥ | tu | prajahāti | tad-āvṛtim

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |

| **tad-āvṛtim** | feminine accusative singular | that obstruction |

The repeated obstruction is not affliction taken vaguely. The Bhāṣya identifies it precisely as **kleśa-prāpti**, acquisition or possession of the afflictions, insofar as it obstructs the arising of the Aśaikṣa mind.

## 4. Grammar

The first line predicates passive release of **aśaikṣaṃ cittam** during its arising. The ablative **āvṛteḥ** states what it is released from.

The adversative second line changes both subject and voice:

```text
Aśaikṣa mind
    vimucyate
    is released

Path
    prajahāti
    actively abandons
```

Although **nirudhyamāna** literally presents the Path as ceasing, Vasubandhu glosses it **vartamāna**, while present. The Path does not act after it has ceased; it abandons the obstruction in its presently operative final moment.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The Aśaikṣa mind, while arising, is released from the obstruction; but the Path, while ceasing, abandons that obstruction.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The Aśaikṣa mind, in the very process of arising, is free from the obstruction to its arising. The diamond-like final Path, while presently operative in its moment of cessation, abandons that obstruction—the acquisition of the afflictions. Once the Aśaikṣa mind has arisen, it is already liberated.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Path does not persist as a subject that first performs release and later possesses its result. One determinate moment removes the condition preventing freedom; the subsequent mind arises under the newly accomplished condition, free from that obstruction.

Abandonment belongs to the operative Path; liberation belongs to the actuality made possible by that operation. Their succession is the objective genesis of the mind beyond training.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The obstruction is kleśa-prāpti**

The Bhāṣya asks what obstructs the arising Aśaikṣa mind and answers:

```text
kleśa-prāpti
    acquisition or possession of the afflictions
        = utpatti-āvaraṇa
          obstruction to its arising
```

The point is not merely that an affliction is present as a passing mental event. Its acquisition holds the continuum under the determination in which the Aśaikṣa mind cannot yet arise.

**Vajropama-samādhi**

The obstruction is abandoned in the diamond-like samādhi:

```text
vajropama-samādhi
    final Śaikṣa Path-moment
    maximally firm counterforce
    abandons kleśa-prāpti
```

Its immediately following moment is the first Aśaikṣa mind.

**Abandonment and release**

The verbal distinction is exact:

```text
mārgaḥ prajahāti
    the Path abandons

cittaṃ vimucyate
    the mind is released
```

Therefore:

```text
prahāṇa
    operation removing obstruction

vimukti
    unobstructed actuality resulting from removal
```

This gives temporal precision to the fourfold Mārga of 6.65:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    abandons the obstruction

vimukti-mārga
    first arises free from it
```

**Jāyamāna: liberation while arising**

The treatise statement cited by the Bhāṣya speaks of the future Aśaikṣa mind “while arising.” At this point:

```text
obstructing acquisition has been abandoned
    → arising is no longer prevented
    → the new mind arises released
```

Once it has arisen, the Bhāṣya says, it is already liberated. The arising participle marks the transition rather than an incomplete degree of freedom.

**Nirudhyamāna means presently operative**

A misleading construal would be:

```text
Path has already ceased
    and then performs abandonment
```

Vasubandhu blocks this by glossing:

```text
nirudhyamāna
    = vartamāna
      while present
```

The Path abandons in its own present moment, which is also the moment passing into cessation.

**Other minds released from the same obstruction**

The Bhāṣya notes that non-arising future minds and future mundane minds can also be called released from this obstruction. What is removed is the acquisition of afflictions that would accompany and qualify such minds.

This does not mean that every mundane mind of a Śaikṣa is already liberated. Vasubandhu distinguishes:

```text
mundane mind accompanied by kleśa-prāpti
    not released in the relevant sense

future mundane mind after that acquisition is abandoned
    released from the same obstruction to arising
```

The verse foregrounds the Aśaikṣa mind because its arising is the immediately determined consequence of vajropama-samādhi.

**Momentary succession**

The analysis requires no enduring agent-substance linking the two functions:

```text
present final Path-moment
    performs abandonment and ceases

subsequent Aśaikṣa mind
    arises unobstructed
```

Continuity is causal and dharmic: the prior operation establishes the condition under which the subsequent actuality occurs.

## 8. Logical Determination

The transition is:

```text
KleśaPrāpti
    obstructsArising AśaikṣaMind

VajropamaPathMoment
    abandons KleśaPrāpti

AbandonmentOf(KleśaPrāpti)
    removes ArisingObstruction

AśaikṣaMind
    arisesImmediatelyAfter VajropamaPathMoment
    arisesFreeFrom ArisingObstruction
```

The functional non-identity is:

```text
AgentOfAbandonment = PathMoment
BearerOfReleasedActuality = AśaikṣaMind

therefore

AbandonmentOperation ≠ LiberatedState
```

The temporal restriction is:

```text
Ceased(PathMoment)
    → no longer performs Abandonment

PresentAndCeasing(PathMoment)
    → performs Abandonment
```

Thus effectivity belongs to the present Path-moment, while freedom characterizes the consequent arising.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse realizes in a single transition the fourfold form stated in 6.65. The Path’s immediate operation is not itself the liberated mind; it makes that mind possible by removing precisely what prevented its arising.

```text
ānantarya
    final Path actively abandons

vimukti
    Aśaikṣa mind arises unobstructed
```

This is an objective genesis without a persistent substrate migrating from bondage into freedom. The identity of the movement lies in determinate succession: the first moment’s entire meaning is to remove the condition whose absence constitutes the possibility of the second.

For the Organon reconstruction, the Path is the copula between obstruction and liberated actuality—not an inert “between,” but the effective negation that makes the successor possible. The Bhāṣya’s own claim remains exact and narrower: kleśa-prāpti obstructs arising, vajropama-samādhi abandons it while present, and the Aśaikṣa mind arises released.
