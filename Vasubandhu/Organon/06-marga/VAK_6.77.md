# VAK 6.77 — The Path Abandons; the Aśaikṣa Consciousness Arises Free

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```text
विमुच्यते जायमानमशैक्षं चित्तमावृतेः ।
निरुध्यमानो मार्गस्तु प्रजहाति तदावृतिम् ॥ ६.७७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
vimucyate jāyamānam aśaikṣaṃ cittam āvṛteḥ |
nirudhyamāno mārgas tu prajahāti tad-āvṛtim || 6.77 ||
```

The verse distinguishes the presently operative final Path-moment from the Aśaikṣa consciousness arising immediately after it. Abandonment and liberated actuality are contiguous, causally ordered, and non-identical.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| vimucyate | third-person singular present passive of *vi-√muc* | is released or freed |
| jāyamānam | neuter nominative singular present middle participle of *√jan* | while arising |
| aśaikṣam cittam | neuter nominative singular | consciousness beyond training |
| āvṛteḥ | feminine ablative singular | from the obstruction |
| nirudhyamānaḥ | masculine nominative singular present passive participle of *ni-√rudh* | while ceasing; glossed by the Bhāṣya as “while present” |
| mārgaḥ | masculine nominative singular | the final Path-moment, vajropama-samādhi |
| tu | adversative particle | but, distinguishing the Path’s function |
| prajahāti | third-person singular present active of *pra-√hā* | abandons |
| tad-āvṛtim | feminine accusative singular | that obstruction |

The repeated obstruction is not affliction taken vaguely. The Bhāṣya identifies it precisely as **kleśa-prāpti**, acquisition or possession of the afflictions, insofar as it obstructs the arising of the Aśaikṣa consciousness.

## 4. Grammar

The first line predicates passive release of **aśaikṣaṃ cittam** during its arising. The ablative **āvṛteḥ** states what it is released from.

The adversative second line changes both subject and voice:

```text
Aśaikṣa consciousness
    vimucyate
    is released

Path
    prajahāti
    actively abandons
```

Although **nirudhyamāna** literally presents the Path as ceasing, Vasubandhu glosses it **vartamāna**, while present. The Path does not act after it has ceased; it abandons the obstruction in its presently operative final moment.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The Aśaikṣa consciousness, while arising, is released from the obstruction; but the Path, while ceasing, abandons that obstruction.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The consciousness beyond training, as it arises, is freed from the obstruction; but the Path, while present at its cessation, abandons that obstruction.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Path does not persist as a subject that first performs release and later possesses its result. One determinate moment removes the condition preventing freedom; the subsequent consciousness arises under the newly accomplished condition, free from that obstruction.

Abandonment belongs to the operative Path; liberation belongs to the actuality made possible by that operation. Their succession is the objective genesis of the consciousness beyond training.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kleśa-prāpti` — acquisition of the afflictions**

The Bhāṣya names it as the obstruction (*utpatti-āvaraṇa*) to the arising of the Aśaikṣa consciousness. The point is not that an affliction is present as a passing mental event: its acquisition holds the continuum under the determination in which the Aśaikṣa consciousness cannot yet arise.

**`Vajropama-samādhi` — diamond-like concentration**

The final Śaikṣa Path-moment, the maximally firm counterforce, in which the obstruction is abandoned. Its immediately following moment is the first Aśaikṣa consciousness.

**`Prahāṇa` / `vimukti` — abandonment and release**

The verbal distinction is exact: *mārgaḥ prajahāti*, the Path abandons; *cittaṃ vimucyate*, the consciousness is released. Prahāṇa is the operation removing the obstruction; vimukti is the unobstructed actuality resulting from the removal. This gives temporal precision to the fourfold Mārga of 6.65:

```text
ānantarya-mārga → abandons the obstruction
vimukti-mārga   → first arises free from it
```

**`Jāyamāna` — arising**

The treatise statement cited by the Bhāṣya speaks of the future Aśaikṣa consciousness “while arising.” The obstructing acquisition has been abandoned, so arising is no longer prevented and the new consciousness arises released. Once arisen, it is already liberated; the participle marks the transition, not an incomplete degree of freedom.

**`Nirudhyamāna` — presently operative**

Glossed by the Bhāṣya as *vartamāna*, “while present.” It blocks the reading that the Path has already ceased and only then performs abandonment: the Path abandons in its own present moment, which is also the moment passing into cessation.

**`Anāgata` / `laukika` — future and mundane consciousnesses**

The Bhāṣya notes that non-arising future consciousnesses and future mundane consciousnesses may also be called released from this obstruction, since what is removed is the acquisition of afflictions that would accompany and qualify them. This does not make every mundane consciousness of a Śaikṣa liberated: a mundane consciousness accompanied by kleśa-prāpti is not released in the relevant sense. The verse foregrounds the Aśaikṣa consciousness because its arising is the immediately determined consequence of vajropama-samādhi.

**`Kṣaṇika-krama` — momentary succession**

The analysis needs no enduring agent-substance linking the two functions. The present final Path-moment performs abandonment and ceases; the subsequent Aśaikṣa consciousness arises unobstructed. Continuity is causal and dharmic: the prior operation establishes the condition under which the subsequent actuality occurs.

## 8. Logical Determination

The transition is:

```text
KleśaPrāpti
    obstructsArising AśaikṣaConsciousness

VajropamaPathMoment
    abandons KleśaPrāpti

AbandonmentOf(KleśaPrāpti)
    removes ArisingObstruction

AśaikṣaConsciousness
    arisesImmediatelyAfter VajropamaPathMoment
    arisesFreeFrom ArisingObstruction
```

The functional non-identity is:

```text
AgentOfAbandonment = PathMoment
BearerOfReleasedActuality = AśaikṣaConsciousness

therefore

AbandonmentOperation ≠ LiberatedState
```

The temporal restriction is:

```text
Ceased(PathMoment)
    → no longer performs Abandonment

PresentAndCeasing(PathMoment)
    → performs Abandonment
```

Thus effectivity belongs to the present Path-moment, while freedom characterizes the consequent arising.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya identifies the obstruction as acquisition of the afflictions, which blocks the Aśaikṣa consciousness’s arising, and glosses “while ceasing” as “while present.” The Path abandons the obstruction in its operative moment; the consciousness is released as it arises and is already free once arisen.

This verse realizes in a single transition the fourfold form stated in 6.65. The Path’s immediate operation is not itself the liberated consciousness; it makes that consciousness possible by removing precisely what prevented its arising.

```text
ānantarya
    final Path actively abandons

vimukti
    Aśaikṣa consciousness arises unobstructed
```

This is an objective genesis without a persistent substrate migrating from bondage into freedom. The identity of the movement lies in determinate succession: the first moment’s entire meaning is to remove the condition whose absence constitutes the possibility of the second.

For the Organon reconstruction, the Path is the copula between obstruction and liberated actuality—not an inert “between,” but the effective negation that makes the successor possible. The Bhāṣya’s own claim remains exact and narrower: kleśa-prāpti obstructs arising, vajropama-samādhi abandons it while present, and the Aśaikṣa consciousness arises released.
