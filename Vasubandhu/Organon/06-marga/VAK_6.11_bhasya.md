# VAK_6.11 Bhāṣya — Attention Past the Support; Aśubha Classified

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> atikrānta-manaskāro bhrūmadhye citta-dhāraṇāt |
> alobho daśabhūḥ kāma-dṛśyālambā nṛjāśubhā || 6.11 ||

> Through holding consciousness between the eyebrows, one is called one whose
> attention has passed beyond. The unattractive is non-greed, has ten grounds,
> takes visible Form of the Desire Realm as its cognitive Condition, and
> arises among humans.

The Bhāṣya completes the third practitioner stage, then answers successive
questions about the unattractive practice's nature, grounds, Condition, and
place of arising.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Through holding consciousness (`citta`) between the eyebrows, a practitioner
is called one whose attention has passed beyond. Having released even the
remaining half of the skull, they hold consciousness between the brows. This,
it is said, is a practitioner whose attention has passed beyond *with respect
to the unattractive practice*.

The commentary then distinguishes limited and extensive cognitive Conditions
from lacking and having mastery, yielding four possible combinations. An
unmastered or mastered mode of attention can take one's own body or the field
as far as the ocean as its Condition. What is the intrinsic nature of the
unattractive practice? On how many grounds does it occur? What does it take as
its Condition? Where does it arise? The verse answers in order: non-greed is
its nature; it occurs on ten grounds; it takes visible form in the Desire Realm
as its Condition; and it arises among humans.

The prose specifies the four dhyānas together with their neighboring levels,
the intermediate dhyāna, and the Desire Realm in its ground count. A further
question concerns which visible forms the practice takes up; its transmitted
wording is damaged, so the precise qualification remains open. The practice
arises only among humans, in no other destiny, and even there not in
Uttarakuru. Its name indicates its unattractive aspect. Its temporal range
also varies with the temporal status of the practice; a practice that cannot
arise may take a Condition across times. It remains with influx because it
operates through resolutive attention. It may be obtained through detachment
or developed through application, according to prior habituation. Thus the
characteristics and divisions of the unattractive practice have been stated.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The first prose sentence removes the final half-skull and locates attention
between the brows. The stage-name is expressly scoped to *aśubha*. The
commentary then crosses two independent axes, Condition extent and mastery,
before posing four classificatory questions. After the verse answers them,
the prose specifies grounds, visible-form Condition, human production,
contamination status, temporal range, and two modes of acquisition.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Atikrānta-manaskāra` is a stage within the
unattractive practice; it does not name a general passage beyond attention or
consciousness. `Alobha` answers the question of intrinsic nature, while the
feminine compounds predicate grounds, Condition, and human origin of `aśubhā`.
The source says the practice is with influx because of resolutive attention;
that status belongs in the Bhāṣya analysis rather than being silently added
to the kārikā anchor. The damaged visible-form question remains visible in
the witness and is not used to invent a more precise classification.

**Organon determination.** A practice can be mastered and wholesome while
remaining with influx. Its name alone does not determine its ground,
Condition, origin, or effect. A future reflective Feature Grammar can carry
these independent predicates as typed features; a LogicFrame can then ask
which inferences they license. This is a project model of the source's
relations, not its own terminology.

## 6. Classification of the Practice

| Axis | Determination |
|---|---|
| Practitioner stage | Attention has passed beyond the skeletal support |
| Intrinsic nature | Non-greed (`alobha`) |
| Ground range | Ten grounds |
| Cognitive Condition | Visible form of the Desire Realm |
| Production | Human, excluding Uttarakuru |
| Influx status | With influx (`sāsrava`) |
| Provenance | Gained through detachment or developed through application |

Extent of the cognitive Condition and degree of mastery remain independent.
The earlier distinction between suppression and definitive abandonment still
applies.
