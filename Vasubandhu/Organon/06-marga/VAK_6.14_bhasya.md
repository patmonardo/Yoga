# VAK_6.14 Bhāṣya — Śamatha and Two-Characteristic Examination

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> niṣpanna-śamathaḥ kuryāt smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanām |
> kāya-vic-citta-dharmāṇāṃ dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇāt || 6.14 ||

> With śamatha accomplished, one should cultivate the establishments of
> recollection by examining body, feeling, consciousness, and dharmas through
> their two characteristics.

The Bhāṣya identifies the four fields, the two kinds of characteristic, and
the different ranges of the common characteristics.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The two entry practices have been stated. Having gained concentration through
them, the practitioner whose śamatha is accomplished should cultivate the
establishments of recollection in order to complete vipaśyanā. How? By
examining body, feeling, consciousness (`citta`), and dharmas according to two
kinds of characteristic.

One examines body through its own characteristic and its common
characteristics; likewise feeling, consciousness, and dharmas. Each field's
own nature is its own characteristic. The common characteristics are
impermanence of conditioned dharmas, suffering of dharmas with influx, and
emptiness and not-self of all dharmas. What is body's own nature? It consists
of the Great Elements and Form derived from them. “Dharmas” here
means those other than body, feeling, and consciousness. For a concentrated
practitioner who sees body atom by atom and moment by moment, it is said,
the establishment of recollection of the body becomes complete.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary closes the two entry practices by naming their result:
concentration and accomplished śamatha. It prescribes cultivation of the
establishments of recollection for the completion of vipaśyanā. The verse's
compressed four-field and two-characteristic expressions are unpacked by the
prose. Its three common-characteristic scopes are different. The final
reported formulation describes completion of examination of the body through
atom-by-atom and moment-by-moment seeing.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The compressed `vic` is resolved as feeling
(`vedanā`) by the prose. `Dvi-lakṣaṇa` is own and common characteristic, not
two additional fields. `Kuryāt` prescribes a further operation after
śamatha; vipaśyanā is its stated purpose. The anchor preserves the distinct
scope of each common mark: conditioned, with influx, and all dharmas. The
atom-and-moment completion claim is reported with `kila` and remains so in
the conventional translation.

**Organon determination.** Stabilization makes examination possible, while
each of the four fields receives its own determination and every common mark
retains its proper range. A future Feature Grammar can expose field,
own-characteristic, common-characteristic, and applicability as features;
a LogicFrame can then check which predications follow. This reflective model
is the project layer, grounded in the commentary's distribution.

## 6. The Four Fields and Scoped Marks

| Examined field | Own characteristic | Common characteristics when applicable |
|---|---|---|
| Body | Great Elements and derived Form | Impermanence if conditioned; suffering if with influx; emptiness and not-self |
| Feeling | Its own nature as feeling | Same scoped tests |
| Consciousness (`citta`) | Its own nature as consciousness | Same scoped tests |
| Remaining dharmas | Their respective own natures | Same scoped tests |

The Bhāṣya supplies body's own nature here. It does not separately specify
the other three fields' own natures in this unit. The next verse opens the
question about the nature of the establishments themselves.
