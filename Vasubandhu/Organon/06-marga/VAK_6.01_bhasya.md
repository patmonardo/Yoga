# VAK_6.01 Bhāṣya — Abandonment Already Taught; Status of the Two Paths

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kleśa-prahāṇam ākhyātaṃ satya-darśana-bhāvanāt |
>
> dvividho bhāvanā-mārgo darśanākhyas tv anāsravaḥ || 6.1 ||

> The abandonment of afflictions has been explained through seeing the truths
> and through cultivation. The Path of Cultivation is twofold, mundane and
> supramundane; the Path called Seeing, however, is without influx and solely
> supramundane.

The Bhāṣya supplies the two cultivation kinds and the exclusive status of
seeing. Its reasons for that status follow in the argument below.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

It has been stated how an abandonment receives the name *parijñā*. It has
also been explained that afflictions are abandoned through seeing the truths
and through cultivation: the afflictions to be abandoned through seeing and
those to be abandoned through cultivation were set out in detail. It must now
be said whether these two Paths, Seeing and Cultivation, are without influx
or with influx. Therefore the verse says that the Path of Cultivation is
twofold, mundane and supramundane. The Path of Seeing is solely supramundane,
because it is a counteragent to all three realms and because it abandons at
once the nine kinds to be abandoned through seeing. The mundane Path does not
have this power.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening recalls 5.68–5.70's account of when abandonment is designated
*parijñā*. It then recalls the seeing-abandonable and cultivation-abandonable
afflictions. These established classes become the premise for a new question:
what status must the Paths that remove them have?

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Dvividhaḥ` divides cultivation into `laukika`
and `lokottara`, not two forms of seeing. `Tu` makes seeing the contrasting
case. The kārikā predicates `anāsrava` of seeing; the prose calls it
`lokottara` and gives two reasons. The passage does not supply a universal
lexical equation of those two terms.

**Organon determination.** Anuśaya:Mārga terminates in the Path as a
conditioned Concept: the Understanding determines it through the kinds of
abandonment it can perform. Jñāna:Samāpatti will take up practical knowing
and truth as Idea. This is the project's transition, not a sentence in the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. The Two Paths and Their Powers

| Path | Status in this passage | Stated ground |
|---|---|
| Cultivation | Mundane or supramundane | The prose explicitly divides it in two. |
| Seeing | Solely supramundane; without influx in the kārikā | Counteracts all three realms and abandons the nine seeing-abandonable kinds at once. |

The two grounds are not interchangeable. `Traidhātuka-pratipakṣatva`
specifies the range of the counteragent; `sakṛt-prahāṇa` specifies the
operation it performs. `Śakti` names the capacity absent from a mundane
Path. The nine kinds are invoked here, not re-enumerated.
