# VAK 6.25 — From Liberative Orientation to Uncontaminated Acceptance

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
श्रुतचिन्तामयं त्रीणि कर्माण्याक्षिप्यते नृषु ।
लौकिकेभ्योऽग्रधर्मेभ्यो धर्मक्षान्तिरनास्रवा ॥ ६.२५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
śrutacintāmayaṃ trīṇi karmāṇy ākṣipyate nṛṣu |
laukikebhyo 'gradharmebhyo dharmakṣāntir anāsravā || 6.25 ||
```

The first half continues the understood **mokṣabhāgīyam** from 6.24. The second half resumes the sequence of direct realization and introduces a new subject, **dharmakṣāntiḥ**.

## 4. Grammar

**śrutacintāmayam** is a predicate of the unexpressed *mokṣabhāgīyam*: “[the liberation-conducive root] is formed through hearing and reflection.” It should not be carried forward as a modifier of the uncontaminated Acceptance in the second line.

**trīṇi karmāṇi** is an elliptical predication: all three kinds of action can constitute or project the liberation-conducive root. The singular passive **ākṣipyate** returns to the singular root as subject: “it is projected among human beings.”

The ablative **laukikebhyo ’gradharmebhyaḥ** establishes immediate succession from the Highest Mundane Dharmas. The Bhāṣya supplies **anantaram**, “immediately after”; no intermediate stage is inserted.

**dharmakṣāntiḥ** is the nominative subject of the implied verb “arises,” and **anāsravā** is its predicate adjective. The syntax marks both succession and a change of status:

```text
from the Highest Mundane Dharmas
    arises
uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The liberation-conducive root is formed through hearing and reflection,
> involves the three actions, and is projected among humans. From the
> Highest Mundane Dharmas arises uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The root conducive to liberation is hearing-born or reflection-born, involves
> the three actions, and is projected among humans. After the Highest Mundane
> Dharmas, uncontaminated Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance arises.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Liberative orientation begins within received and reflected Meaning and takes practical form through action governed by aspiration. This orientation is then cultivated through the penetration-conducive stages until mundane cognition reaches its highest possible determination.

Immediately there arises a cognition of another order: uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance. This is not one further degree of the mundane preparation. It is the first actual entry into the Noble Path, a transformation in the mode of knowing that simultaneously transforms the status of the practitioner.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Mokṣabhāgīya` — recapitulated**

The wholesome root conducive to liberation: formed through hearing and reflection, expressed in bodily, verbal and mental action (principally mental), and first projected among humans on the three continents.

**`Laukikāgradharma` — Highest Mundane Dharmas**

The final mundane preparation. Dharma-Acceptance follows immediately: the threshold from mundane to uncontaminated.

**`Dharmajñānakṣānti` — Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance**

The kārikā's *dharmakṣānti*, already uncontaminated but not identical with the Knowing that follows it. Related to it as flower to fruit. Its cognitive Condition is desire-realm suffering.

**`Anāsrava` — uncontaminated**

Without outflows. A change of order from the Highest Mundane Dharmas, not an increase of intensity.

**`Niyāmāvakrānti` — entry into determinate certainty**

Entry into the fixed Noble order: *samyaktva* (Nirvāṇa), *niyāma* (settled definitiveness), *avakramaṇa* (entry). Path-entry, not adoption of a correct opinion.

**`Āryapudgala` / `pṛthagjanatva` — Noble Person and ordinary-person status**

The practitioner is a Noble Person from this Acceptance; before it he is still a *pṛthagjana*. The Bhāṣya reports three views on what displaces that status: this Acceptance, the Highest Mundane Dharmas, or both.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse contains both a recapitulation and a threshold:

```text
LiberationConduciveRoot {
    genesis: hearing + reflection
    expression: bodily + verbal + mental action
    primaryMode: mental action
    firstSupport: human existence
}

HighestMundaneDharmas
    → immediately
DharmaKnowingAcceptance {
    status: uncontaminated
    pathFunction: entry into Noble order
}
```

The Bhāṣya coordinates four transformations in one arising:

```text
temporal:     immediately after the final mundane moment
cognitive:    Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance
purity:       uncontaminated
practitioner: ordinary person → Noble Person
```

This is a prepared discontinuity in its complete form. The long causal preparation is necessary, but its result is not homogeneous with its final mundane condition.

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, the **Ideal Copula becomes Path-entry**: the cultivated relation between Truth, cognitive Condition, aspiration, and practice no longer merely prepares Meaning; it becomes an uncontaminated operation whose arising determines both what is seen and who the practitioner now is within the Path.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya supplies mental-action primacy, the desire-
realm suffering Condition, the expanded name `dharmajñānakṣānti`, entry into
determinate rightness, and the resulting Noble status. It reports three
accounts of what displaces ordinary-person status; those remain attributed
separately in the continuous translation.


VAK 6.24 completed the architecture of preparation. VAK 6.25 crosses its boundary. The movement from the Highest Mundane Dharmas to Dharma-Acceptance is immediate, yet it is not a smooth identity. *Laukika* and *anāsrava* name a qualitative difference in Path-status.

This is the first place where the project's intuition that Meaning itself is Path becomes textually concrete. The decisive event is not the addition of a proposition about suffering. A determinate Truth is apprehended through an uncontaminated operation, and the arising of that operation is simultaneously entry into the Noble order.

Still, Acceptance must not be collapsed prematurely into Knowing. The Bhāṣya's flower–fruit analogy preserves a generated distinction between *kṣānti* and the *jñāna* that follows. VAK 6.26 will begin to articulate that sequence, and the present study stops before completing it.

The kārikā states the constitution and human projection of the liberation-conducive root, immediate succession from the Highest Mundane Dharmas, and uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance. Mental primacy, aspiration-governed action, the determinate cognitive Condition, the flower–fruit relation, *niyāmāvakrānti*, Noble status, and the three accounts of displacing ordinary-person status are Bhāṣya determinations. “Ideal Copula becomes Path-entry” is the Organon's reconstruction.
