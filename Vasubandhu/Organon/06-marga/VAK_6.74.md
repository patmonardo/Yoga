# VAK 6.74 — Four Verified Confidences, Two Operative Realities

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

लाभो मार्गाभिसमये बुद्धतत्संघयोरपि ।
धर्मः सत्यत्रयं बोधिसत्त्वप्रत्येकबुद्धयोः ॥ ६.७४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

lābho mārgābhisamaye buddha-tat-saṃghayor api |
dharmaḥ satya-trayaṃ bodhisattva-pratyekabuddhayoḥ || 6.74 ||

The Bhāṣya immediately continues the verse with **mārgaś ca**, “and the Path,” thereby including all four Truths in Dharma-confidence. The final compound **bodhisattva-pratyekabuddhayoḥ** is not separately explained in the surviving prose of this unit and must remain philologically open.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

lābhaḥ | mārga-abhisamaye | buddha-tat-saṃghayoḥ | api |
dharmaḥ | satya-trayam | bodhisattva-pratyekabuddhayoḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **lābhaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | acquisition, here of verified confidence |
| **mārga-abhisamaye** | masculine locative singular | at direct realization of the Path-truth |
| **buddha-tat-saṃghayoḥ** | masculine locative or genitive dual | concerning the Buddha and his Saṃgha |
| **api** | indeclinable | also; includes the Dharma and ethical confidences already acquired |
| **dharmaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | Dharma as an object of verified confidence |
| **satya-trayam** | neuter nominative singular | the triad of suffering, origin, and cessation |
| **bodhisattva-pratyekabuddhayoḥ** | masculine dual compound in genitive/locative form | compressed reference to Bodhisattva and Pratyekabuddha; immediate syntactic function uncertain |

The prose continuation **mārgaś ca** coordinates the Path with **satya-trayam**. Therefore “Dharma” in the completed Bhāṣya analysis includes all Four Truths rather than only the first three.

## 4. Grammar

The first half continues the ellipsis from 6.73:

```text
mārga-abhisamaye
    at direct realization of the Path

buddha-tat-saṃghayoḥ
    [verified confidence] concerning Buddha and his Saṃgha

lābhaḥ
    is acquired
```

The particle **api** indicates addition: at Path-realization the practitioner acquires the Buddha- and Saṃgha-confidences and also possesses Dharma- and ethical confidence.

The nominal statement **dharmaḥ satyatrayam** is completed by the prose **mārgaś ca**:

```text
Dharma-object here
    = first three Truths + Path-truth
```

No secure governing relation for **bodhisattvapratyekabuddhayoḥ** is supplied by the immediate prose. Its presence must be recorded without manufacturing a syntactic resolution.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

At direct realization of the Path there is also acquisition [of verified confidence] concerning the Buddha and his Saṃgha. Dharma is the triad of Truths—[and, according to the Bhāṣya, the Path also]. The Bodhisattva–Pratyekabuddha dual remains compressed in the transmitted verse.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Direct realization of the Path-truth produces verified confidence in the Buddha and his community of noble disciples, while also including confidence in Dharma and noble ethical discipline. Dharma-confidence is ultimately grounded in direct realization of all Four Truths. The four verified confidences differ according to their objects, but reduce to two operative dharmas: confidence for Buddha, Dharma, and Saṃgha, and ethical discipline for the fourth. All four are uncontaminated, being settled confidence generated after the Truths have been understood as they really are.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Path verifies more than itself as an abstract method. In realizing its efficacy, cognition becomes settled toward the awakened teacher, the realized teaching, the community constituted through that realization, and the ethical form appropriate to noble life.

These are four valid orientations because their objects differ, yet their operative constitution is twofold. Knowledge becomes confidence in relation to Buddha, Dharma, and Saṃgha; knowledge becomes stable comportment in noble ethical discipline.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Completion of the four avetyaprasādas**

The four named objects are:

```text
Buddha
Dharma
Saṃgha
āryakānta-śīla
    ethical discipline dear to the noble ones
```

Their acquisition is distributed across direct realization:

```text
seeing duḥkha, samudaya, and nirodha
    → Dharma-confidence
    → noble ethical confidence

seeing mārga
    → Buddha-confidence
    → Saṃgha-confidence
    → also Dharma- and ethical confidence
```

**Dharma includes all Four Truths**

The Kārikā says **dharmaḥ satyatrayam**, but the Bhāṣya immediately adds **mārgaś ca**. Its explicit conclusion is:

```text
realization of all Four Truths
    → Dharma-avetyaprasāda
```

The three-truth wording belongs to the distribution of acquisition begun in 6.73; it must not become a general reduction of Dharma to only three Truths.

**Four by name, two by dravya**

The Bhāṣya repeats the analytical form used for the thirty-seven factors:

```text
four names by difference of object

Buddha-confidence ┐
Dharma-confidence ├→ śraddhā
Saṃgha-confidence ┘

ethical confidence → śīla

two operative dravyas
```

The three confidences are one underlying **śraddhā**, differentiated by their supports or objects (**śraddhādhiṣṭhāna-bheda**). The fourth is ethical discipline itself.

This reduction does not erase the four objects. Object-difference is exactly what generates the four valid Path-determinations.

**All four are pure**

The Bhāṣya asks whether they can be contaminated or uncontaminated and answers:

```text
nirmalāḥ
    they are stainless or pure
```

Avetyaprasāda is therefore not ordinary confidence merely directed toward Buddhist objects. It belongs to noble realization and is exclusively **anāsrava**.

**Definition of avetyaprasāda**

The decisive sentence is:

```text
yathābhūtasatyāny avabudhya
saṃpratyayo ’vetyaprasādaḥ
```

Its order is:

```text
yathābhūta-avabodha
    understanding the Truths as they are
        →
saṃpratyaya
    settled assurance
        →
avetyaprasāda
    verified confidence
```

Knowledge is explanatory prior. Confidence is the stabilization generated by realization, not a substitute for it.

**Buddha and Saṃgha as Path-objects**

The preceding Bhāṣya explanation determines them through dharmas:

```text
Buddha
    aśaikṣa dharmas constituting perfect awakening

Saṃgha
    śaikṣa and aśaikṣa dharmas
    constituting the noble community
```

Confidence in Buddha and Saṃgha therefore arises internally from Path-realization. They are not unrelated devotional objects added after knowledge.

**The practical analogies**

In emergent reflection, the Bhāṣya orders the four confidences through two analogy-systems:

```text
physician     medicine     attendant     health
Buddha        Dharma       Saṃgha        ethical integrity

guide         path         caravan       vehicle
Buddha        Dharma       Saṃgha        ethical discipline
```

These analogies do not define the dravyas; they display the four objects as one practical system of orientation.

**The unresolved dual compound**

The transmitted **bodhisattvapratyekabuddhayoḥ** is textually present but receives no separate explanation in the preserved prose for this unit. It should therefore remain marked:

```text
secure
    lexical reference to Bodhisattva and Pratyekabuddha

not secure here
    precise syntactic attachment
    exact doctrinal distribution
```

Neither omission nor speculative expansion would preserve the witness. A later parallel or manuscript study may resolve it.

## 8. Logical Determination

The acquisition rules are:

```text
Realizes(FirstThreeTruths)
    → Acquires(DharmaConfidence)
    → Acquires(NobleEthicalConfidence)

Realizes(PathTruth)
    → Acquires(BuddhaConfidence)
    → Acquires(SaṃghaConfidence)
    → Includes(DharmaConfidence)
    → Includes(NobleEthicalConfidence)
```

The object/dravya mapping is:

```text
countBy(ObjectOfConfidence) = 4
countBy(OperativeDharma) = 2
```

And the epistemic order is:

```text
DirectKnowledgeOfTruth
    causes SettledConfidence

SettledConfidence
    does not cause or replace
DirectKnowledgeOfTruth
```

Purity follows from realization:

```text
Avetyaprasāda(x)
    → Anāsrava(x)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

This passage gives the Path a remarkable reflexive result. By realizing Mārga, the practitioner does not merely reach an object called “the Path.” The realization returns as a fourfold stable orientation toward the agent of awakening, its truth, its realized community, and its practical ethical form.

```text
Truth directly seen
    → knowledge settled
    → confidence differentiated by object
    → conduct stabilized as śīla
```

Thus knowledge passes into practical actuality without ceasing to be knowledge-grounded. The three śraddhā-confidences are Meaning differentiated by object; ethical confidence is that verified meaning embodied as comportment.

For the Organon reconstruction, this is Truth becoming Knowledge and then returning into Path as stable practical orientation. But the textual claim is more restrained and exact: *avetyaprasāda* is **saṃpratyaya** arising after **yathābhūta** comprehension, four by object and two by operative dharma.

The unresolved Bodhisattva–Pratyekabuddha compound also provides an important methodological limit: systematic pressure cannot authorize a translation where the immediate syntax and Bhāṣya do not securely determine one.
