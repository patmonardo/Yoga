# VAK_6.59 Bhāṣya — Three Senses of Falling Away

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> parihāṇis tridhā jñeyā prāptāprāptopabhogataḥ |
> antyā śāstur akopyasya madhyā cānyasya tu tridhā || 6.59 ||

> Falling away is to be understood in three ways: from what has been attained, from what remains unattained but attainable, and from the use of what is attained. The Teacher has the last; the immovable arhat has the middle as well; another arhat has all three.

The three meanings concern possession, further attainment, and the
present use of a quality. The closing response continues the dispute
about whether uncontaminated liberation itself can be lost.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The cited sūtra says that, of four higher pleasant abidings attained
in this life, a practitioner may fall away from one or another;
but it denies falling away in any way from unshakeable liberation of
consciousness directly realized. How, then, can the immovable arhat
fall away from the pleasant abidings? Falling away is to be understood
in three senses. First, there is loss of an attained quality if one
falls from a quality already acquired. Second, there is failure with
respect to an unattained quality if one does not acquire a quality
that could be acquired. Third, there is loss of present use if one
does not bring a quality already attained into immediate presence.

Among these, the Teacher has only the last kind: the Buddha may not
make an attained quality presently manifest, but does not lose it or
fail to gain a further one in this classification. The immovable
arhat may also have the middle kind, because a special quality of a
distinctive person-type may remain unattained. An arhat of another
type may also have the first kind, actual loss of an attained quality.

A closing objection answers the alleged sūtra conflict: the
reference to loss of present use explains how the immovable arhat
can be described as falling from a pleasant abiding without losing
unshakeable liberation. Every uncontaminated liberation is
unshakeable, the non-regression speaker says; the designation of a
special immovable arhat was explained in its own terms. That is
the speaker's answer to the objection, not an unqualified agreement
that arhatship can be lost.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The unit starts from the contrast in a sūtra between pleasant abiding
and unshakeable liberation, defines three senses of falling away,
and distributes them across Buddha, immovable arhat, and other arhat.
The final response marks a non-regression voice that keeps
uncontaminated liberation secure while allowing loss of present use.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Prāpta-parihāṇi` is loss of a possessed
quality; `aprāpta-parihāṇi` is failure to gain a further attainable
quality; `upabhoga-parihāṇi` is non-manifestation of a possessed
quality. `Antyā` is the third, `madhyā` the second. The last
non-regression claim is attributed to its speaker and does not erase
the received threefold distribution.

**Organon determination.** Possession, possible advance, and present
exercise are different relations to a Path-quality. This is project
synthesis grounded in the commentary's three definitions, not an
independent resolution of the arhat-regression dispute.

## 6. Distribution of the Three Senses

| Person | Loss of attained quality | Failure of further attainment | Failure of present use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buddha | No | No | Possible |
| Immovable arhat | No | Possible | Possible |
| Other arhat | Possible in received classification | Possible | Possible |

The closing non-regression speaker denies that the uncontaminated
liberation itself is a quality liable to loss.
