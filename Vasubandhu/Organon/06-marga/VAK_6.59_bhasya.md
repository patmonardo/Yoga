# VAK_6.59 Bhāṣya — Three Senses of Fall

After the Talk: name the three losses so the word *parihāṇi* stops lying.

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> parihāṇis tridhā jñeyā prāptāprāptopabhogataḥ |
> antyā śāstur akopyasya madhyā cānyasya tu tridhā || 6.59 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> Regression should be understood in three ways: with respect to what has been
> attained, what has not been attained, and enjoyment. The last belongs to the
> Teacher; the middle also belongs to the unshakeable one; but for another it is
> threefold.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_6.59.md`](./VAK_6.59.md). Next: 6.60.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> prāptaparihāṇir yadi pratilabdhād guṇāt parihīyate /
> aprāptaparihāṇir yadi prāpyaṃ gṇṇaṃ na prāpnoti /
> upabhogaparihāṇir yadi prāptaguṇaṃ na saṃmukhīkaroti /
> buddhasyopabhogaparihāṇir eva nānyā /
> akopyadharmaṇaḥ sā cāprāptaparihāṇiś ca /
> anyasyārhataḥ prāptaparihāṇir apy asti /

**Witness.** Pradhan 377|16–378|04. Voice: Vasubandhu distributing the three.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Fall is three. From what is already got: the quality leaves. From what is not yet
> got: a further quality she could still get, she does not get. From enjoyment: the
> quality stays hers, she does not bring it to the front.
>
> The Teacher: only the last. The unshakeable: the middle and the last — she may miss
> a special person-quality, and she may not put a held quality into use. Another
> Arhat: all three.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
have / get further / make present
Buddha: only make-present can lapse
akopya: further + make-present
other: also lose what she already had
```

## 6. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- *madhyā* = *aprāpta*;
- Teacher is not being docked of liberation.
