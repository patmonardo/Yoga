# VAK 6.35 — The Sixth Grade as the Second-Fruit Threshold

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

आपञ्चमप्रकारघ्नो द्वितीयप्रतिपन्नकः ।
क्षीणषष्ठप्रकारस्तु सकृदागाम्यसौ पुनः ॥ ६.३५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

ā-pañcama-prakāra-ghno dvitīya-pratipannakaḥ |
kṣīṇa-ṣaṣṭha-prakāras tu sakṛd-āgāmy asau punaḥ || 6.35 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

ā-pañcama-prakāra-ghnaḥ | dvitīya-pratipannakaḥ |
kṣīṇa-ṣaṣṭha-prakāraḥ | tu | sakṛt-āgāmī | asau | punaḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **ā-pañcama-prakāra-ghnaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | destroying up to and including the fifth grade |
| **dvitīya-pratipannakaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | one proceeding toward the second fruit |
| **kṣīṇa-ṣaṣṭha-prakāraḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | one in whom the sixth grade is exhausted |
| **tu** | indeclinable | but, whereas; marks the threshold contrast |
| **sakṛt-āgāmī** | masculine nominative singular | once-returner |
| **asau** | masculine nominative singular pronoun | that one; the practitioner under discussion |
| **punaḥ** | indeclinable | again, in turn; advances the sequential classification |

The first line predicates candidate-status throughout a range of abandonment. The second predicates fruit-status once its boundary is crossed.

## 4. Grammar

The preverb-like **ā** in **ā-pañcama** sets an inclusive terminus: abandonment extends “up to the fifth.” The agentive final member **-ghna** identifies the practitioner through the destruction accomplished. **dvitīya-pratipannakaḥ** supplies the resulting Path-designation: candidate for the second fruit.

In the contrasting clause, **kṣīṇa-ṣaṣṭha-prakāraḥ** is a possessive description: the sixth grade has become exhausted for that practitioner. **asau** resumes the same person, while **punaḥ** marks the next determination in the sequence. **sakṛdāgāmī** is then predicated: that candidate is now a once-returner.

The verse itself states the grade-boundary. Its restriction to cultivation-abandonable desire-realm afflictions and the causal explanation of once-returning are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

One who destroys up through the fifth grade is a candidate for the second [fruit]. But when the sixth grade is exhausted, that one in turn is a once-returner.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

A stream-enterer who, through renewed cultivation, has abandoned from one through five grades of the desire-realm cultivation-removable afflictions is proceeding toward the second fruit. With destruction of the sixth grade, that same practitioner attains the second fruit and becomes a once-returner.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The person established in the first fruit can become a candidate once more. Renewed cultivation progressively transforms the residual field, yet the first five removals remain determinations of transition toward the second fruit. The sixth is the decisive completion: the course reaches its End and candidate-status becomes possession of the new fruit.

This End is causally expressed in existence. With only mild afflictive grades remaining, the forces sustaining repeated return have been attenuated; the practitioner's possible future contracts to the condition named “once-returner.”

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**dvitīya-pratipannaka**

The **candidate for the second fruit** is not a separate personal kind. It is the stream-enterer, already established in the first fruit, whose renewed cultivation has abandoned one through five grades:

```text
first fruit possessed
    → renewed cultivation
    → one to five grades abandoned
    → second-fruit candidate
```

The Bhāṣya stresses the sequential context. The same number of prior abandonments can have a different classification when it occurs before the Path of Seeing; here the abandonments occur after stream-entry within the Path of Cultivation.

**pratipannaka and phalastha**

The verse instantiates the distinction established in VAK 6.32:

```text
pratipannaka
    Path toward a determinate fruit is active

phalastha
    that fruit has been attained and possessed
```

The same continuum can alternate between these determinations. Fruit-establishment is not a permanent personal identity, and candidacy is not merely the absence of a fruit; each names a present function in the Path.

**ṣaṣṭha-prakāra**

The sixth cultivation-removable grade is the threshold of the second fruit. Its destruction is not merely another increment after the fifth. It completes the determinate course toward once-returning and changes the practitioner's classification.

**sakṛdāgāmin**

The **once-returner** is one who has attained the second fruit. The Bhāṣya explains the name through one further coming to the human world after existence among the gods. This remaining range externally manifests an internal causal condition rather than an arbitrary itinerary.

**tanutva**

The Bhāṣya says that greed, hatred, and delusion have become attenuated because only their mild grades remain. Thus:

```text
six grades destroyed
    → only mild grades remain
    → greed, hatred, and delusion attenuated
    → rebirth-producing force reduced
    → once-return condition
```

The fruit-name and its cosmological range are grounded in the transformed afflictive structure.

## 8. Logical Determination

The kārikā defines a threshold transition:

```text
for abandonedGradeCount in {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}:
    status = CandidateForSecondFruit

if abandonedGradeCount = 6:
    status = OnceReturner
    fruit  = SecondFruit
```

The transition joins four determinations:

```text
grade of abandonment
    → residual affliction-profile
    → Path or fruit status
    → remaining existential range
```

It also demonstrates that classification depends upon sequence, not number alone:

```text
same abandonment count
    + different temporal relation to Seeing
    → different Path-significance
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, grades one through five articulate the internal moments of a single course. The sixth grade is its End because it exhausts the obstruction sufficient to prevent the second fruit. At that threshold, quantity does not merely cease; it is recollected as a new qualitative Path-position.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.34 anticipated that abandonment of the fifth grade cannot form a stable resting position: the sixth must follow. VAK 6.35 states the positive result of that necessity. Up through five, the practitioner is still *pratipannaka*; with six exhausted, the practitioner is *phalastha* in the second fruit.

This is the objective genesis of the next Path-position. The result is neither externally conferred nor merely contemplated in advance. It arises when the cultivation-process has removed the precise obstruction defining the boundary. The achieved End then constrains Loka because the remaining afflictive morphology can sustain only a sharply reduced range of return.

The verse's classifications and sixth-grade boundary belong to the kārikā. The renewed-cultivation context, the distinction from prior abandonment, attainment of the second fruit, etymology of once-returning, and attenuation of greed, hatred, and delusion belong to the Bhāṣya. The interpretation of the first five grades as moments of one course and the sixth as their qualitative recollection is Organon synthesis.
