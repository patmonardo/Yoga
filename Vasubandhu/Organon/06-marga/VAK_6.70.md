# VAK 6.70 — Developmental Predominance of the Seven Groups

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

आदिकर्मिकनिर्वेधभागीयेषु प्रभाविताः ।
भावने दर्शने चैव सप्त वर्गा यथाक्रमम् ॥ ६.७० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

ādikarmika-nirvedhabhāgīyeṣu prabhāvitāḥ |
bhāvane darśane caiva sapta vargā yathākramam || 6.70 ||

The seven Bodhi-aligned groups are not assigned as mutually exclusive possessions. The governing term is **prabhāvitāḥ**: each group is especially developed or brought to predominance at a corresponding developmental position.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

ādi-karmike | nirvedha-bhāgīyeṣu | prabhāvitāḥ |
bhāvane | darśane | ca | eva | sapta | vargāḥ | yathā-kramam

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **ādi-karmike** | masculine locative singular | in the beginner or beginning practitioner |
| **nirvedha-bhāgīyeṣu** | masculine locative plural | in the four wholesome roots conducive to penetration |
| **prabhāvitāḥ** | masculine nominative plural past passive participle | especially developed, made predominant |
| **bhāvane** | masculine locative singular | in the Path of Cultivation |
| **darśane** | masculine locative singular | in the Path of Seeing |
| **ca eva** | conjunction with emphasis | and also, indeed |
| **sapta** | numeral | seven |
| **vargāḥ** | masculine nominative plural | groups or classes |
| **yathā-kramam** | indeclinable | in corresponding order, according to sequence |

The plural **nirvedhabhāgīyeṣu** supplies four internal positions—Heat, Summits, Receptivity, and Highest Mundane Dharmas—so the four locative expressions yield seven stages altogether.

## 4. Grammar

The subject is **sapta vargāḥ**; **prabhāvitāḥ** is its predicate. The locatives enumerate the developmental fields in which the groups become predominant.

**Yathākramam** establishes a distributive correspondence:

```text
seven groups
    in their canonical order

correspond to

beginner
    + four nirvedhabhāgīya stages
    + Cultivation
    + Seeing
```

The grammar expresses ordered predominance, not exclusive presence. Other Bodhi-aligned qualities remain operative at every stage.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The seven groups are especially developed, in corresponding sequence, in the beginner, in the factors conducive to penetration, in Cultivation, and in Seeing.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The seven Bodhi-aligned groups are brought successively to predominance: the establishments of recollection in the beginner; the right abandonments at Heat; the bases of spiritual power at the Summits; the faculties at Receptivity; the powers at the Highest Mundane Dharmas; the factors of awakening in the Path of Cultivation; and the factors of the Noble Path in the Path of Seeing. This primary allocation marks characteristic predominance rather than exclusive occurrence; the Bhāṣya also preserves an alternative distribution of the final two groups.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The one Path develops by reorganizing which of its already cooperating determinations governs the whole. Examination first establishes its field; exertion intensifies the required work; samādhi stabilizes accomplishment; the faculties acquire governing efficacy; those faculties become powers resistant to opposition; and the noble Path reaches direct Seeing and continued cultivation through its awakening- and Path-factors.

Development is therefore neither replacement nor simple accumulation. Earlier functions persist while a new principality gives each stage its characteristic practical form.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The primary sevenfold distribution**

| Developmental position | Predominant group | Bhāṣya’s primary reason |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | four **smṛtyupasthānas** | body, feeling, mind, and dharmas must be discerned |
| Heat, **uṣmagata** | four **samyakprahāṇas** | exertion is bound to attaining further distinction |
| Summits, **mūrdhan** | four **ṛddhipādas** | non-declining wholesome roots establish a basis of accomplishment |
| Receptivity, **kṣānti** | five **indriyas** | the factors acquire governing efficacy and no further regression from that stage |
| Highest Mundane Dharmas, **laukikāgradharma** | five **balas** | the factors can no longer be overcome by affliction or mundane opposition |
| Path of Cultivation, **bhāvanāmārga** | seven **bodhyaṅgas** | Bodhi is near |
| Path of Seeing, **darśanamārga** | eight **mārgāṅgas** | the Path goes with exceptional swiftness there |

The seven are stated before the eight to maintain the ascending numerical order of the canonical groups. The order of enumeration is therefore not itself proof that the seven Bodhyaṅgas must precede the eight Mārgāṅgas temporally in every account.

**Prabhāvita: predominance, not exclusion**

The question opening the Bhāṣya is:

```text
At which stage are which Bodhi-aligned factors
especially developed?
```

This continues the rule of **pradhānagrahaṇa** from 6.69:

```text
predominant group
    gives the stage its characteristic form

associated groups
    remain operative within the Path-whole
```

No group simply vanishes when the next becomes principal.

**Beginner: the establishments of recollection**

Their immediate task is **kāyādyupalakṣaṇa**, discerning body and the remaining fields. The alternative masters add that recollection restrains cognition dispersed among many objects and removes conceptual positings connected with craving.

```text
scattered cognition
    → gathered and retained field
    → discriminative examination becomes possible
```

**Heat: the right abandonments**

Once a special attainment has begun, vīrya becomes decisive for the four tasks:

```text
prevent the unarisen unwholesome
abandon the arisen unwholesome
produce the unarisen wholesome
develop the arisen wholesome
```

**Summits: the bases of spiritual power**

The practitioner has entered wholesome roots characterized as non-declining (**aparihāṇīya-kuśalamūla**). Samādhi can now function as the stable basis of accomplishment.

**Receptivity: the faculties**

The factors acquire **ādhipatya**, governing efficacy, with respect to supramundane dharmas. This is the Path-form of Indriya: not an inert capacity but a rule capable of determining subsequent operation.

**Highest Mundane Dharmas: the powers**

The same five factors become **balas** once their contrary factors can no longer overcome them:

```text
faculty
    governing but still considered as capacity

power
    that capacity stabilized against its contrary
```

No new five dravyas are introduced; efficacy has reached a new degree.

**The primary final allocation**

The Kārikā’s direct sequential construal, as explained first by the Bhāṣya, is:

```text
bhāvanāmārga
    bodhyaṅgas predominate
    because Bodhi is near

darśanamārga
    mārgāṅgas predominate
    because the Path goes swiftly
```

The Vaibhāṣikas add that investigation of dharmas is both Bodhi and a factor of Bodhi, while right view is both Path and a factor of Path.

**Alternative masters on the final groups**

The Bhāṣya preserves another account (**apare**):

```text
darśanamārga
    bodhyaṅgas
    because dharma-reality is first seen

darśanamārga + bhāvanāmārga
    Noble Eightfold Path
    on scriptural grounds
```

This alternative agrees on the developmental sequence while modifying the placement of the last two groups. It should not be silently merged into the primary allocation.

**The alternative genetic explanation**

Its full movement is:

```text
smṛtyupasthāna
    restrains scattered cognition
        →
samyakprahāṇa
    intensifies exertion for the four tasks
        →
ṛddhipāda
    purifies and establishes samādhi
        →
indriya
    gains governing efficacy
        →
bala
    conquers its opposing factor
        →
bodhyaṅga
    accompanies first direct seeing of dharma-reality
        →
mārgāṅga
    operates through Seeing and Cultivation
```

This supplies an **ānupūrvī**, an ordered succession in which one achieved capacity conditions the next.

## 8. Logical Determination

The developmental model is:

```text
PathStage
    hasPredominantGroup BodhipakṣyaGroup

BodhipakṣyaGroup
    persistsBeyond its stage of predominance
```

Thus:

```text
PredominantAt(Group, Stage)
    does not entail
PresentOnlyAt(Group, Stage)
```

The relation from 6.67–6.70 can now be stated:

```text
10 operative dharmas
    organized into
37 named functions
    grouped as
7 practice-complexes
    distributed through
7 developmental positions
```

The same content moves in two analytical directions:

```text
reduction
    functions → operative dharmas

development
    operative dharmas → changing functional predominance
```

The preserved disagreement over the last groups means that the developmental invariant is stronger than any one stage table:

```text
ordered maturation is shared
exact terminal allocation is disputed
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse makes the Bodhi-aligned system genuinely dynamic. The Path maintains its identity not by remaining internally unchanged but by altering which of its own determinations governs the whole at each stage.

```text
same Path-content
    different principality
        → new stage-form
```

This is the Path “in itself” appearing as its own developmental morphology. Smṛti does not disappear when vīrya becomes principal; vīrya does not disappear when samādhi becomes principal. Each achieved organization is retained and redetermined within the next.

The user’s Dhātu–Indriya insight becomes especially clear at Receptivity. The examined field has been established, exertion activated, and samādhi stabilized; only then do the factors become **indriyas**, governing capacities with respect to a supramundane movement. At the Highest Mundane stage, those governing capacities become **balas** because opposition no longer determines them.

The Organon reconstruction can therefore speak of developmental predominance: a practical whole generates its course by reorganizing its internal hierarchy. The source-level restraint is equally important. Vasubandhu preserves more than one account of the final allocation, so the Path’s genetic unity must not be reduced to one rigid diagram.
