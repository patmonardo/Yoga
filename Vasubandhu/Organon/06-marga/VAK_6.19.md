# VAK_6.19 — Strong Acceptance and the Highest Mundane Dharmas

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कामाप्तदुःखविषया त्वधिमात्रा क्षणं च सा ।
तथाग्रधर्माः सर्वे तु पञ्चस्कन्धा विनाप्तिभिः ॥ ६.१९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kāmāptaduḥkhaviṣayā tv adhimātrā kṣaṇaṃ ca sā |
tathāgradharmāḥ sarve tu pañcaskandhā vināptibhiḥ || 6.19 ||
```

Strong Acceptance contracts to Desire Realm suffering for one moment. The
Highest Mundane Dharmas share this Condition and duration; the verse then
classifies all four preparatory stages by their associated constitution.

The Bhāṣya identifies `adhimātrā` as Acceptance and explains
`vināptibhiḥ` by excluding `prāpti`. The verse's `viṣaya` is Condition;
the prose specifies Desire Realm suffering as Support (`ālambana`).

## 4. Grammar

`Adhimātrā` qualifies understood `kṣāntiḥ`. Its feminine predicates give
both the contracted Condition and one-moment duration. `Tathā`
transfers those two predicates to `agradharmāḥ`, with the prose supplying
`laukika`, mundane. It does not assert identity of the two stages.

`Sarve tu pañcaskandhāḥ` shifts from intrinsic nature to constitution with
associates included. `Vināptibhiḥ` excludes acquisition from that count; the
Bhāṣya gives the reason. The contrastive `tu` marks this change in analytic
level.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Strong Acceptance has Desire Realm suffering as its Condition and
> lasts one moment. The Highest Dharmas do likewise. All the preparatory
> stages comprise five Bases when acquisitions are excluded.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Strong Acceptance has Desire Realm suffering as its Condition and Support
> and lasts one moment. The Highest Mundane Dharmas are likewise. With their
> associates, all these stages comprise five Bases, excluding acquisitions.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Strong Acceptance has narrowed its Condition and Support to Desire Realm
> suffering for one moment. The Highest Mundane Dharmas share that focus and
> duration, reaching the limit of mundane cultivation. Their causal efficacy
> draws in the Path without making them a homogeneous cause of that Path.
> Discriminative comprehension is the intrinsic nature of these preparatory
> stages, while their complete associated manifestation comprises five Bases.

```text
strong Acceptance: Desire Realm suffering as Condition and Support × one moment
    → Highest Mundane Dharmas: same focus, supreme mundane status
    → causal drawing-in of the Path
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Adhimātrā kṣānti` — strong Acceptance**

The highest grade of Acceptance, distinguished from weak and medium grades
by its contracted Condition. The Bhāṣya describes the prior narrowing until
only Desire Realm suffering remains. This grade lasts one moment and is not
an extended sequence.

**`Kāmāptaduḥkha-viṣaya` — Desire Realm suffering as Condition**

The verse's Condition predicate. The Bhāṣya expresses the same restriction as
`kāmāvacara-duḥkha-ālambana`, taking Desire Realm suffering as Support.
`Viṣaya` and `ālambana` describe different roles of the same
content; neither is the Organon's technical Object.

**`Kṣaṇa` — one moment**

The duration of strong Acceptance and the Highest Mundane Dharmas. The prose
says this Acceptance is momentary, not gradually extended. The comparison
`kṣaṇaṃ ca sā` is transferred by `tathā` to the following stage.

**`Laukikāgradharma` — Highest Mundane Dharmas**

The kārikā says `agradharmāḥ`; the Bhāṣya supplies `laukika`, mundane. They
are supreme among mundane dharmas, share strong Acceptance's Support and
one-moment duration, and stand at the threshold of the Path.

**`Ākarṣaṇa / puruṣakāra / sabhāgahetu` — drawing-in / causal efficacy / homogeneous cause**

The Highest Mundane Dharmas draw in the Path through their own efficacy,
without being its homogeneous cause. This determines a real causal relation
while preserving the difference between a mundane stage and the Path it
draws in.

**`Prajñātmaka` — having discriminative comprehension as intrinsic nature**

Heat and the following preparatory stages have discriminative comprehension as their intrinsic nature because they
are establishments of recollection in intrinsic nature. This answers what
the practice is by nature, not how many associated factors are included in
the complete event.

**`Saparivāra-grahaṇa / pañcaskandha` — including associates / five Bases**

With the associated complex counted, the preparatory stages comprise the
Form, Feeling, Reflection, Formations, and Cognition Bases. This full
constitution is compatible with discriminative comprehension as intrinsic
nature.

**`Prāpti / āpti / vināptibhiḥ` — acquisition / acquisition / excluding acquisitions**

The verse excludes acquisitions. The commentary explains that possession of
an acquisition by an ārya must not be counted as present manifestation of a
mundane preparatory stage. Acquired status and a presently occurring event
are distinct.

**Present and future cultivation matrix**

The closing prose classifies which establishment is present and which are
cultivated for the future in different truth Supports and stages. The paired
Bhāṣya translates each case, while several transmitted compounds and the
reasons given for the last cases remain uncertain.

| Case | Present establishment | Future establishments | Aspects stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heat formed with three Truths as Supports | Dharma | All four | Corresponding |
| Heat formed with cessation as Support | Dharma | Dharma | Corresponding |
| Heat strengthened apart from the cessation case | Any one of four | All four | — |
| Heat strengthened with cessation as Support | Dharma | All four | All |
| Summits formed with the Four Truths as Supports | Dharma | All four | All |
| Summits strengthened with cessation as Support | Dharma | All four | All |
| Summits strengthened with the other three Truths as Supports | Any one of four | All four | All |
| Acceptances, throughout | Dharma | All four | All |
| Highest Mundane Dharmas | Dharma | All four | Four only |

“Dharma” in the present column resolves the prose's “last establishment.”
The final limit is explained through absence of others and resemblance to
the Path of Seeing; the referent of “others” is not explicit in this witness.

## 9. Interpretive Note


**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya makes the Desire Realm restriction explicit, distinguishes medium from strong Acceptance by the transition from two moments to one, explains how the Highest Mundane Dharmas draw in the Path without being its homogeneous cause, and gives the reason acquisitions are excluded.

**The Condition and Support contract.** Strong Acceptance takes one truth
within the Desire Realm for one moment. The concentration of its Condition is a
specific stage determination, not a denial of the wider truth structure
cultivated earlier.

**Mundane cultivation has causal efficacy.** The Highest Mundane Dharmas are
supreme within that range. The Bhāṣya says they draw in the Path by their
own efficacy while excluding homogeneous causation.

**Intrinsic nature and complete event answer different questions.** The
preparatory stages are discriminative comprehension by nature; with associates they comprise five
Bases. Acquisition is excluded so a stage's possession is not mistaken
for its present occurrence.

**Organon interpretation.** The threshold is causally prepared by an exact
Condition, Support, and duration. A reflective Model can carry Condition,
Support, duration, status, causal role, intrinsic nature, and associated constitution
as separate features. This is a project formalization of the source's
mundane-to-Path relation.
