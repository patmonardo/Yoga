# VAK_6.09

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तत्रावतारोऽशुभया चानापानस्मृतेन च ।
अधिरागवितर्काणां शंकला सर्वरागिणाम् ॥ ६.९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
tatrāvatāro ’śubhayā cānāpānasmṛtena ca |
adhirāgavitarkāṇāṃ śaṃkalā sarvarāgiṇām || 6.9 ||
```

VAK 6.09 moves from the general preparation of the practitioner to diagnostic
entry into cultivation. Cultivation of the unattractive addresses a character
dominated by desire; recollection of inhalation and exhalation addresses one
dominated by discursive thought. The skeletal representation supplies a common
counter-object for all forms of bodily desire.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
tatra | avatāraḥ | aśubhayā | ca | ānāpāna-smṛtena | ca |
adhi-rāga-vitarkāṇām | śaṃkalā | sarva-rāgiṇām
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `tatra` | adverb | there; into that cultivation |
| `avatāraḥ` | nominative singular masculine | entry or introduction |
| `aśubhayā` | instrumental singular feminine | through cultivation of the unattractive |
| `ca ... ca` | paired conjunctions | both ... and; respectively in the Bhāṣya's distribution |
| `ānāpāna-smṛtena` | instrumental singular neuter compound | through recollection of inhalation and exhalation |
| `adhi-rāga-vitarkāṇām` | genitive plural compound | for those in whom desire or discursive thought predominates |
| `śaṃkalā` | nominative singular feminine | skeletal framework or skeleton |
| `sarva-rāgiṇām` | genitive plural masculine | for all those affected by desire |

The two instrumental forms state means of entry. The Bhāṣya distributes them
diagnostically rather than cumulatively: `aśubha` for the desire-dominant and
`ānāpānasmṛti` for the thought-dominant. The final genitive marks the skeleton
as the recommended common object for every subtype of bodily desire.

## 4. Grammar

The principal construction is:

```text
tatra avatāraḥ
    entry there [into cultivation]

aśubhayā ca
    through cultivation of the unattractive

ānāpānasmṛtena ca
    and through recollection of breathing
```

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed genitive distribution:

```text
adhirāgasya / rāgacaritasya
    aśubhayā avatāraḥ

for one dominated by desire
    entry is through aśubha
```

```text
adhivitarkasya / vitarkacaritasya
    ānāpānasmṛtena avatāraḥ

for one dominated by discursive thought
    entry is through recollection of breathing
```

The final nominal clause is equally compressed:

```text
śaṃkalā
    [ālambanam / upadiśyate]

sarvarāgiṇām
    for all those affected by desire
```

The supplied object relation and recommendation come from the Bhāṣya. It
explains that the skeleton jointly removes the attractive supports upon which
the differentiated forms of bodily desire depend.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Entry there is through the unattractive and through recollection of
> inhalation and exhalation, for those dominated by desire and discursive
> thought [respectively]. The skeleton is [the object] for all those affected
> by desire.

The respective distribution and the supplied object relation are fixed by the
Bhāṣya.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Once the practitioner has become a suitable vessel, entry into cultivation
> is selected according to the predominant obstruction. One whose character is
> dominated by desire enters through cultivation of the unattractive; one
> dominated by discursive thought enters through recollection of inhalation and
> exhalation. The several forms of bodily desire receive differentiated
> unattractive counter-objects, while the skeletal representation serves as a
> common object for them all. This practice suppresses active desire but does
> not yet accomplish its definitive abandonment.

Suitability of the practitioner, the diagnostic types, the differentiated
forms of desire, and the suppression/abandonment boundary are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The prepared Path does not begin with one uniform exercise. It diagnoses the
> relation in which cognition is obstructed and selects a counterforce adequate
> to it. Desire is addressed through transformation of the represented body;
> proliferating thought is addressed through recollection of a simple recurring
> process. These interventions can render an affliction non-operative, yet
> preparatory efficacy must not be mistaken for definitive liberation.

The basic architecture is:

```text
diagnosed predominance
    → corresponding object-support
    → typed counter-operation
    → suppression
    → preparation for deeper Path-capacity
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Avatāra` — entry**

The transition from general fitness for cultivation to an actual practice
selected for the practitioner's predominant obstruction. The Bhāṣya frames the
question in terms of one who has become a suitable vessel (`pātrībhūta`).

**`Aśubha-bhāvanā` — cultivation of the unattractive**

The entry-practice for the desire-dominant practitioner. It counters the
constructed attractiveness of the body by presenting an object-support from
which the relevant attractive feature is absent.

**`Ānāpānasmṛti` — recollection of inhalation and exhalation**

The entry-practice for the practitioner dominated by discursive thought. One
reported explanation emphasizes its non-variegated object-support: cognition
is constrained to the simple recurring process of breathing.

**`Rāgacarita` — desire-dominant character**

A practitioner-type defined by the predominance of desire. This diagnosis
selects `aśubha`, but the Bhāṣya further differentiates desire before assigning
particular counter-objects.

**`Vitarkacarita` — discursive-thought-dominant character**

A practitioner-type defined by excessive `vitarka`. Breath-recollection is
selected for its capacity to reduce proliferating cognitive movement.

**`Avicitrālambana` — non-variegated object-support**

One attributed explanation of why breath-recollection counteracts discursive
thought. Multiplicity supplies proliferation with material; a simple repeated
process constrains its range. The Bhāṣya records alternative explanations, so
this rationale should not be presented as its sole uncontested account.

**`Varṇa-rāga` — attachment to color or complexion**

Desire organized around bodily color. The Bhāṣya assigns it unattractive
representations involving discoloration and decomposition.

**`Saṃsthāna-rāga` — attachment to bodily configuration**

Desire organized around shape and arrangement. Dismemberment and dispersal
counter the synthetic bodily configuration upon which it depends.

**`Sparśa-rāga` — attachment to touch**

Desire organized around tactile qualities. Putrefaction and exposed bone
remove the tactile support represented as desirable.

**`Upacāra-rāga` — attachment to bodily behavior or presentation**

Desire organized around animation, bearing, movement, or personal display.
The inert corpse counters this practical presentation.

**`Śaṃkalā` — skeletal framework**

The common counter-object for all forms of desire because the attractive
features supporting color, configuration, touch, and animated presentation are
absent together. It is not merely one more special image but a general
analytical reduction of the attractive body.

**`Adhimukti-manaskāra` — resolutive or constructed attention**

The Bhāṣya's determination of how `aśubha` operates. Because it is a
constructed attention directed toward a partial field, its efficacy remains
preparatory rather than definitively abandoning the affliction.

**`Viṣkambhaṇa` — suppression or blocking**

The effect actually accomplished by `aśubha`: desire is inhibited and made
non-operative. This is genuine counteractive power but not yet
`kleśa-prahāṇa`.

**`Kleśa-prahāṇa` — definitive abandonment of affliction**

The stronger Path-operation denied to `aśubha` in this unit. Its distinction
from suppression reconnects 6.09 with the supramundane `śakti` of the Path of
Seeing established in 6.01.

## 8. Logical Determination

The diagnostic selection rules are:

```text
PredominantCharacter(person, DesireDominant)
    → SelectEntryPractice(person, AsubhaCultivation)

PredominantCharacter(person, DiscursiveThoughtDominant)
    → SelectEntryPractice(person, BreathRecollection)
```

The two intervention types are distinct:

```text
AsubhaCultivation
    transforms RepresentedObject
    counteracts Desire

BreathRecollection
    stabilizes CognitiveProcess
    counteracts DiscursiveProliferation
```

The desire subtypes require differentiated object-supports:

```text
ColorDesire
ConfigurationDesire
TouchDesire
BehaviorPresentationDesire
    → each receives AppropriateUnattractiveSupport
```

The skeleton provides a common rule:

```text
LacksTogether(
    SkeletonRepresentation,
    AttractiveColor,
    AttractiveConfiguration,
    AttractiveTouch,
    AnimatedPresentation
)
    → GeneralCounterObjectFor(SkeletonRepresentation, BodilyDesire)
```

The efficacy boundary is:

```text
OperatesThrough(AsubhaCultivation, ConstructedResolutiveAttention)
AND DirectedTo(AsubhaCultivation, PartialDomain)
    → Produces(AsubhaCultivation, SuppressionOfDesire)
    → NotYetProduces(AsubhaCultivation, DefinitiveAbandonment)
```

Therefore:

```text
CounteractiveEfficacy
    ≠ DefinitiveAbandonmentCapacity
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Path-entry is diagnostic.** A common liberating end does not entail one
identical first practice. The counterforce must correspond to the obstruction
actually governing the practitioner.

**Affliction can dominate object or process.** Desire depends upon how the body
is represented; discursive thought depends upon how cognition proliferates.
The two practices intervene at these different functional sites.

**Aśubha is a family of counter-representations.** The four types of desire
prevent “contemplation of the unattractive” from becoming an undifferentiated
generic technique. Its object-support is selected according to the feature
through which attraction operates.

**The skeleton is a common analytical object.** Its universality lies in the
joint absence of the several attractive supports. It dismantles the synthetic
presentation upon which bodily desire relies.

**Suppression is not abandonment.** `Viṣkambhaṇa` is a real Path-effect and a
necessary preparation, but it lacks the definitive `prahāṇa` power belonging
to the supramundane Path. Treating temporary non-operation as uprooting would
erase the graded architecture of Mārga.

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 6.09 turns Practical Meaning into a diagnostic
operation. This is project synthesis:

```text
Feature
    predominant afflictive relation

diagnosis
    determine whether distortion governs object or process

Plan
    select the corresponding counter-object and practice

effect
    record suppression without falsely asserting abandonment
```

The comparison with 6.04 is limited but exact: analysis there tests whether a
configured cognition survives decomposition; here the skeletal representation
decomposes bodily attractiveness so that desire loses its operative support.
Yet this constructed intervention remains within preparatory `saṃvṛti`; it
does not by itself constitute the definitive realization of `paramārtha` or
the abandonment of kleśa.
