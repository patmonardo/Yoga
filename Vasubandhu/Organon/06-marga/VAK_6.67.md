# VAK 6.67 — Bodhi and Its Thirty-Seven Path-Functions

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

अनुत्पादक्षयज्ञाने बोधिस्तदनुलोम्यतः ।
सप्तत्रिंशत्तु तत्पक्ष्याः नामतो द्रव्यतो दश ॥ ६.६७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

anutpādakṣayajñāne bodhis tad-anulomyataḥ |
saptatriṃśat tu tat-pakṣyā nāmato dravyato daśa || 6.67 ||

The Bhāṣya introduces this verse by saying that **this same Mārga** now receives the designation *bodhipakṣya*. The chapter has not left Path for a disconnected catalogue; it determines Path from the side of awakening.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

anutpāda-kṣaya-jñāne | bodhiḥ | tad-anulomyataḥ |
sapta-triṃśat | tu | tat-pakṣyāḥ | nāmataḥ | dravyataḥ | daśa

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **anutpāda-kṣaya-jñāne** | neuter nominative dual compound | knowledge of non-arising and knowledge of exhaustion |
| **bodhiḥ** | feminine nominative singular | awakening, technically constituted here by the two knowledges |
| **tad-anulomyataḥ** | ablatival adverbial compound | because of conformity with that Bodhi |
| **sapta-triṃśat** | numeral | thirty-seven |
| **tu** | adversative or resumptive particle | but, now, as for |
| **tat-pakṣyāḥ** | masculine nominative plural | belonging to its side; aligned with it |
| **nāmataḥ** | indeclinable ablatival adverb | according to designation or name |
| **dravyataḥ** | indeclinable ablatival adverb | according to underlying operative dharma-reality |
| **daśa** | numeral | ten |

The dual **jñāne** is predicated of singular **bodhiḥ**: one awakening is formally articulated through two consummate knowledges.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause is a nominal equation:

```text
anutpādajñāna + kṣayajñāna
    = bodhi
```

The compound **tad-anulomyataḥ** gives the reason for the following designation: because the factors conform to **that** Bodhi, they are **tat-pakṣyāḥ**, members of its side.

The final pāda supplies two contrasting standards of enumeration:

```text
nāmataḥ
    by functional designation: thirty-seven

dravyataḥ
    by underlying operative dharma: ten
```

The syntax does not dismiss the names as unreal. It counts the same system under two different relations.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Bodhi is knowledge of non-arising and knowledge of exhaustion. Because of conformity with that, those belonging to its side are thirty-seven by designation, but ten according to underlying dharma-reality.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Awakening is constituted by knowledge that exhaustion has been accomplished and knowledge that what has been exhausted will not arise again. The Path-factors are called Bodhi-aligned because their operation conforms to this awakening. They number thirty-seven as distinct named Path-functions, but reduce to ten when counted as underlying operative dharmas. According to differences of person, the Bhāṣya distinguishes Śrāvaka awakening, Pratyekabuddha awakening, and unsurpassed complete awakening without changing this formal definition of Bodhi.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Bodhi knows the End in its double completeness: the work of abandonment has been accomplished, and its accomplishment is irreversible. The thirty-seven are not external means attached to that End afterward. They belong to Bodhi’s side because their differentiated operations accord with and lead toward this consummate knowing.

Their plurality is functional rather than a multiplication of underlying realities. Ten operative dharmas assume thirty-seven valid Path-positions; identity of dharma and difference of function remain equally necessary.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Bodhi as two knowledges**

The Bhāṣya directly glosses:

```text
kṣayajñāna
    knowledge of exhaustion

anutpādajñāna
    knowledge of non-arising
```

Their articulated unity is:

```text
kṣayajñāna
    the required abandonment is complete

anutpādajñāna
    the abandoned affliction will not recur

together
    the task has been done
    and is not to be done again
```

The Bhāṣya grounds this in the abandonment of ignorance without remainder (**aśeṣāvidyāprahāṇa**). Bodhi is therefore knowledge of both completion and irreversibility.

**Three Bodhis according to person**

The Kārikā gives one formal definition. The Bhāṣya then distinguishes three realizations according to **pudgala-bheda**:

```text
śrāvakabodhi
    awakening of a disciple

pratyekabodhi
    awakening of a solitary Buddha

anuttarā samyaksaṃbodhi
    unsurpassed complete awakening
```

This is the decisive source-layer answer to whether the thirty-seven belong exclusively to a Bodhisattva path: they do not. The Path-system is common to the modes of awakening, although unsurpassed complete awakening supplies its distinct Buddha-determination.

The verse itself does not present a separate Bodhisattva curriculum. Later Mahāyāna can cultivate these factors under the vows, perfections, emptiness, compassion, and complete Buddhahood, but that is a further determination rather than their original restriction.

**The thirty-seven Bodhipakṣya dharmas**

The Bhāṣya enumerates seven groups:

```text
4 smṛtyupasthānas
    establishments of recollection

4 samyakprahāṇas
    right abandonments

4 ṛddhipādas
    bases of spiritual power

5 indriyas
    faculties

5 balas
    powers

7 bodhyaṅgas
    factors of awakening

8 āryamārgāṅgas
    factors of the Noble Path
```

Numerically:

```text
4 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 7 + 8 = 37
```

**Bodhipakṣya**

The governing relation is **anulomya**, conformity or accord:

```text
Bodhi
    consummate awakened knowing

bodhipakṣya
    dharmas whose operation conforms to Bodhi
```

“Adjuncts,” “aids,” and “wings” are possible traditional renderings, but each can sound external. Here “Bodhi-aligned” preserves the Bhāṣya’s explicit reason: they belong to Bodhi’s side because they are conformable to it.

Conformity is not identity:

```text
bodhipakṣya ≠ Bodhi itself

bodhipakṣya
    tends toward and accords with Bodhi
```

**Thirty-seven by name**

**Nāmataḥ** counts determinate functions. A single dharma can receive different names when it occupies different roles in cultivation.

```text
one operative capacity
    under several functional relations
        → several valid Path-designations
```

The thirty-seven names are therefore not merely dispensable words. They preserve distinctions in what the dharmas do.

**Ten by dravya**

**Dravyataḥ** asks how many underlying operative dharmas actually perform those functions. The answer is ten; VAK 6.68 will enumerate them.

“Substantially” is possible as a translation, but it risks suggesting a Western substance ontology. The present study uses “according to underlying dharma-reality” to preserve the Abhidharma counting operation:

```text
37 functional positions
    realized by
10 underlying dharmas
```

This is a many-to-fewer mapping, not a contrast between illusion and reality.

**Continuity of the same Mārga**

The sequence is now explicit:

```text
VAK 6.65
    Mārga by operative phase

VAK 6.66
    Mārga by course of progress

VAK 6.67
    Mārga by conformity with Bodhi
```

The governing content remains Path; the classificatory relation changes.

## 8. Logical Determination

The formal relations are:

```text
Bodhi
    hasConstituent Kṣayajñāna
    hasConstituent Anutpādajñāna

BodhipakṣyaDharma
    conformsTo Bodhi

NamedPathFunction
    isRealizedBy UnderlyingDharma
```

The counting rule is:

```text
countBy(FunctionName) = 37
countBy(UnderlyingDharma) = 10
```

Therefore:

```text
SameDravya
    can support
DifferentFunctionalNames

DifferentFunctionalNames
    do not entail
DifferentDravyas
```

Yet the reverse reduction is equally forbidden:

```text
SameUnderlyingDharma
    does not erase
DifferenceOfPathFunction
```

The system holds identity and difference together under explicitly distinct counting relations.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This is the promised LogoGenesis of Path from its End. Bodhi is not merely the final event waiting at the far side of practice. As the accomplished and irreversible End, it determines which operations belong to its side and gives the thirty-seven their systematic direction.

```text
Bodhi as End
    → determines conformity

conformity
    → gathers thirty-seven functions

thirty-seven functions
    → articulate ten operative dharmas
```

The relation is not merely one-way. Bodhi gives unity and direction to the factors, while the factors give practical articulation to Bodhi. But this reciprocal formulation belongs to the Organon reconstruction. The Bhāṣya’s direct claim is that the factors receive their name from conformity with Bodhi.

The distinction **nāmataḥ / dravyataḥ** is especially important for the NLP Pipeline of Practical Meaning. Meaning does not reside exclusively in the ten isolated dharmas or in the thirty-seven labels. It emerges from an underlying capacity occupying a determinate functional position within the Path-system:

```text
operative identity
    + relational function
    = determinate Path-meaning
```

VAK 6.68 must now test this interpretation materially by identifying the ten dharmas. Until that enumeration, the reduction is formally announced but not yet fully demonstrated.
