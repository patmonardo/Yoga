# VAK_6.75 Bhāṣya — The Trainee's Bondage and Twofold Liberation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> mārgaś ca dravyatas tu dve śraddhā śīlaṃ ca nirmalāḥ |
>
> noktā vimuktiḥ śaikṣāṅgaṃ baddhatvāt sā punar dvidhā || 6.75 ||

> Dharma includes the Path. The four verified confidences are two in substance—confidence and ethical discipline—and are stainless. Liberation is no factor of the trainee, who remains bound; for one beyond training, it is twofold: the unconditioned abandonment of afflictions and conditioned liberative determination.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The sūtra says that the trainee is endowed with eight factors and the one beyond training with ten. Why are right liberation and right Knowing not stated for the trainee? Liberation is not named a trainee factor because the trainee is still bound by afflictive bonds. How could liberation be established for one who remains bound? One freed from only a portion of bondage is not called liberated. And without liberation, how could Knowing of liberation be established? The one beyond training, having been wholly released from all afflictive bonds, is characterized by liberation and immediate Knowing of it; those designations are fitting for that person.

What is this liberation? It is twofold: conditioned and unconditioned. The unconditioned is abandonment of the afflictions; the conditioned is the liberative determination (`adhimokṣa`) of the one beyond training.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The scriptural eight-and-ten distinction frames the question. The commentary then argues that remaining afflictive bondage bars the unqualified designation “liberated” and, with it, immediate Knowing of completed liberation. It does not deny the trainee's partial abandonments. Once it establishes the one beyond training as the proper subject of liberation, it asks what liberation itself is and divides it into unconditioned abandonment and conditioned `adhimokṣa`.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The verse's `baddhatvāt` refers to residual afflictive bondage, not a mere unfinished curriculum. `Vimukti` is completed liberation in the passage's unqualified predication; the trainee's partial release is real but does not license that name. `Samyagjñāna` is right Knowing, and `vimuktijñāna` is immediate Knowing of accomplished liberation. The two forms of `vimukti` are `asaṃskṛta`, abandonment of afflictions, and `saṃskṛta`, the Aśaikṣa's `adhimokṣa`. The first pāda's confidence count belongs to the 6.74 source passage and is retained in this composite kārikā anchor.

**Organon determination.** The distinction between partial removal and completed freedom gives the project a rule for when an achieved state can bear an unqualified predicate. The commentary's own categorical division of liberation then keeps unconditioned abandonment distinct from conditioned liberative determination.
