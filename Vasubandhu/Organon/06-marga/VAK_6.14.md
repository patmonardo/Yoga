# VAK_6.14

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
निष्पन्नशमथः कुर्यात् स्मृत्युपस्थानभावनाम् ।
कायविच्चित्तधर्माणां द्विलक्षणपरीक्षणात् ॥ ६.१४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
niṣpanna-śamathaḥ kuryāt smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanām |
kāya-vic-citta-dharmāṇāṃ dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇāt || 6.14 ||
```

VAK 6.14 marks the transition from the two preparatory entrance-practices to
systematic examination. Having obtained samādhi and accomplished śamatha, the
practitioner cultivates the four establishments of recollection in order to
complete vipaśyanā, examining body, feeling, citta, and dharmas according to
their intrinsic and universal determinations.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
niṣpanna-śamathaḥ | kuryāt | smṛti-upasthāna-bhāvanām |
kāya-vit-citta-dharmāṇām | dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `niṣpanna-śamathaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | one whose śamatha has been accomplished |
| `kuryāt` | third-person singular optative active of `√kṛ` | should perform or cultivate |
| `smṛti-upasthāna-bhāvanām` | accusative singular feminine compound | cultivation of the establishments of recollection |
| `kāya-vit-citta-dharmāṇām` | genitive plural compound | of body, feeling, citta, and dharmas |
| `dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇāt` | ablative singular compound | through examination according to two determinations |

The compressed `vit` member is resolved by the Bhāṣya as `vedanā`, feeling.
The prose explicitly enumerates the four fields and identifies the two
`lakṣaṇas` as `svalakṣaṇa` and `sāmānyalakṣaṇa`.

## 4. Grammar

The agent is qualified before the prescribed operation:

```text
niṣpannaśamathaḥ
    one whose śamatha has been accomplished

kuryāt
    should cultivate

smṛtyupasthānabhāvanām
    the cultivation of the establishments of recollection
```

The optative gives a Path instruction, not a report that accomplished śamatha
is already vipaśyanā. The Bhāṣya supplies the telos:

```text
vipaśyanāyāḥ saṃpādanārtham
    for bringing vipaśyanā to completion
```

The ablative compound states the method:

```text
kāyasya
vedanāyāḥ
cittasya
dharmāṇām
    dvilakṣaṇaparīkṣaṇāt

through examining body, feeling, citta, and dharmas
according to two determinations
```

The prose resolves the two determinations:

```text
svalakṣaṇa
    the field's own nature

sāmānyalakṣaṇa
    the universal determination under which it falls
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> One whose śamatha has been accomplished should cultivate the establishments
> of recollection through examination of body, feeling, citta, and dharmas
> according to two determinations.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Having obtained samādhi through cultivation of the unattractive or
> recollection of breathing and thereby accomplished śamatha, the practitioner
> should cultivate the four establishments of recollection in order to complete
> vipaśyanā. Body, feeling, citta, and dharmas are each examined according to
> their intrinsic nature and their universal determination: impermanence for
> conditioned dharmas, suffering for contaminated dharmas, and emptiness and
> not-self for all dharmas.

The two entrance-practices, samādhi, vipaśyanā as telos, and the exact
distribution of universal determinations are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Stabilization establishes a field in which disciplined examination can
> occur; it does not complete that examination. Recollection now preserves
> body, feeling, citta, and dharmas as distinct fields while Prajñā determines
> each both in its own specificity and under the universal law appropriate to
> its range. The Path therefore neither dissolves particulars into abstract
> universality nor isolates them as unrelated data. Vipaśyanā is completed
> through their articulated unity.

The transition is:

```text
stable cognitive field
    → differentiated object-fields
    → intrinsic determination
    → scoped universal determination
    → completed vipaśyanā
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Avatāramukha` — entrance-method or entrance-gate**

The Bhāṣya's name for the two practices developed in 6.09–6.13:
`aśubha` and `ānāpānasmṛti`. They are entrances that prepare samādhi and
śamatha, not final Path states.

**`Niṣpanna-śamatha` — accomplished śamatha**

The stabilization achieved through the two entrance-methods. `Niṣpanna` marks
completion of śamatha's function, not completed liberation or completed
vipaśyanā.

**`Śamatha` — stabilization or calming**

The condition that renders the cognitive field stable enough for systematic
examination. Its relation to vipaśyanā is preparatory and enabling rather than
identical.

**`Smṛtyupasthāna` — establishment of recollection**

The disciplined establishment of recollection in relation to four fields:
body, feeling, citta, and dharmas. The intrinsic nature of these establishments
is taken up in 6.15; this verse determines their cultivation-method.

**`Smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanā` — cultivation of the establishments of recollection**

The mediation from accomplished śamatha toward completed vipaśyanā. It
subjects the stabilized field to two-level examination.

**`Vipaśyanā-saṃpādana` — completion of vipaśyanā**

The purpose explicitly supplied by the Bhāṣya. Vipaśyanā is not introduced as
an undefined heightened awareness but as the result of disciplined
`parīkṣaṇa` across the four fields.

**`Parīkṣaṇa` — examination**

Structured investigation rather than passive noticing. The stronger rendering
“examination” preserves the methodological force of the term better than a
generic “observation.”

**`Svalakṣaṇa` — intrinsic or own determination**

The nature specific to the field examined. The Bhāṣya gives body's own nature
as `bhūta-bhautikatva`, being constituted by the great-material bases and
derivative material form.

**`Sāmānyalakṣaṇa` — universal determination**

The general law under which the field falls. Its application is explicitly
scoped rather than indiscriminate.

**The scoped universal determinations**

```text
saṃskṛta-dharmas
    → anityatā

sāsrava-dharmas
    → duḥkhatā

all dharmas
    → śūnyatā
    → anātmatā
```

Impermanence applies to the conditioned, suffering to the contaminated, and
emptiness and not-self to all dharmas. The differing ranges must be preserved.

**`Kāya` — body**

The first field. Its intrinsic determination is material constitution through
the great-material bases and derivative material form. A reported formulation
holds its establishment of recollection complete when body is seen atom by
atom and moment by moment.

**`Vedanā` — feeling**

The second field, compressed in the kārikā compound as `vit` and explicitly
named in the Bhāṣya. It must be examined both as feeling in its specificity and
under the universal determinations appropriate to its status.

**`Citta` — cognition**

The third field. Citta is made an object of disciplined examination rather
than assumed as an unexamined observer standing outside the field.

**`Dharma` — dharmas or determinations**

The fourth field. The Bhāṣya defines it here as those dharmas other than the
preceding three fields. This is a residual technical scope, not a generic
synonym for every possible doctrine.

**`Bhūta-bhautikatva` — material-base and derivative-material constitution**

The own nature of body. It reconnects this examination to the earlier Dhātu
analysis without reducing body to a conventional unanalyzed whole.

**`Paramāṇuśaḥ` and `kṣaṇikataḥ` — atom by atom and moment by moment**

The spatial-material and temporal modes through which the stabilized
practitioner analyzes body in the reported Vaibhāṣika formulation marked by
`kila`.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Path transition is:

```text
Practice(person, AsubhaOrBreathRecollection)
    → Obtains(person, Samadhi)
    → Accomplishes(person, Shamatha)
    → Cultivates(person, FourSmrtyupasthanas)
    → Completes(person, Vipasyana)
```

The four fields are:

```text
SmrtyupasthanaField = {
    Body,
    Feeling,
    Citta,
    DharmasOtherThanFirstThree
}
```

Each field requires two modes of determination:

```text
Examines(person, field, IntrinsicDetermination)
AND Examines(person, field, ApplicableUniversalDetermination)
    → TwoDeterminationExamination(person, field)
```

Universal scope is typed:

```text
Conditioned(x)
    → Impermanent(x)

Contaminated(x)
    → Suffering(x)

Dharma(x)
    → Empty(x)
    AND NotSelf(x)
```

The body-field receives additional analysis:

```text
IntrinsicNature(Body)
    = GreatMaterialBasesAndDerivativeForm

Sees(person, Body, AtomByAtom)
AND Sees(person, Body, MomentByMoment)
    → CompletesReportedly(person, BodySmrtyupasthana)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Śamatha is accomplished yet not terminal.** Completion is function-relative.
Śamatha can be fully accomplished as stabilization while vipaśyanā remains to
be completed through another operation.

**The Path returns to the empirical field.** Having stabilized cognition, the
practitioner does not abandon body, feeling, citta, and dharmas. These become
the differentiated content of liberating examination.

**Intrinsic and universal determination are reciprocal requirements.** Without
`svalakṣaṇa`, universality lacks determinate content; without
`sāmānyalakṣaṇa`, particular data remain disconnected from the law governing
their Path-significance.

**Universal predicates have different ranges.** “All dharmas are suffering”
would erase the 6.03 distinction concerning the Path. The Bhāṣya instead says:
conditioned dharmas are impermanent, contaminated dharmas are suffering, and
all dharmas are empty and not-self.

**Citta belongs within the examined field.** The structure does not posit an
unexamined knower outside every determination. Citta itself is one of the four
objects whose intrinsic and universal characteristics must be discriminated.

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 6.14 gives the first explicit two-level
semantic examination of the stabilized Path-field. This is project synthesis:

```text
Feature
    the field's own determination
    svalakṣaṇa

Law
    the universal determination with the correct scope
    sāmānyalakṣaṇa

Meaning
    their articulated relation within Path-examination
```

Practical Meaning is not generated by attaching a universal slogan to an
isolated datum. The particular field must be preserved, the universal must be
correctly scoped, and their relation must be examined within accomplished
śamatha. This is the movement from field stability to vipaśyanā—not yet the
claim that examination alone has completed the supramundane Path.
