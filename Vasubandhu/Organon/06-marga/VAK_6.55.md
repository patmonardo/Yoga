# VAK 6.55 — Determinate Path-Beginning and Transdomain Completion

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

कामे त्रयाप्तिरन्त्यस्य त्रिषु नोर्ध्वं हि दृक्पथः ।
असंवेगादिह विधा तत्र निष्ठेति चागमात् ॥ ६.५५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

kāme trayāptir antyasya triṣu nordhvaṃ hi dṛkpathaḥ |
asaṃvegād iha vidhā tatra niṣṭheti cāgamāt || 6.55 ||

The kārikā compresses the reasons for the absence of the Path of Seeing above the desire domain. The Bhāṣya distributes them differently between the form and formless domains and explicitly defines **vidhā** as **mārgārambha**, commencement of the Path.

The three **dhātu** here are existential domains—desire, form, and formless existence—not the eighteen cognition-domains of Chapter 1.

## 4. Grammar

The locative **kāme** governs **trayāptiḥ**: attainment of the first three śrāmaṇya-fruits occurs in the desire domain. The ellipsis in **antyasya triṣu** supplies *āptiḥ*: attainment of the final fruit occurs in all three domains.

**na ūrdhvaṃ hi dṛkpathaḥ** gives the reason the third fruit cannot first be attained above: the Path of Seeing does not newly arise there. **asaṃvegāt** then supplies the kārikā's compressed reason, which the Bhāṣya applies to form-domain ordinary beings rather than indiscriminately to both higher domains.

The closing words cite an āgama in direct-speech form: **iha vidhā, tatra niṣṭhā**—“here is the commencement; there is the completion.” The opposition of the two locative adverbs correlates Path-origin and Path-completion without requiring identity of their domains.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

In the desire domain there is attainment of the three; [attainment] of the final [fruit] is in the three. For the Path of Seeing is not above, because of the absence of *saṃvega*, and because scripture says: “Here is the commencement; there is the completion.”

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The first three śrāmaṇya-fruits are attained only in the desire domain, whereas Arhatship can be attained in the desire, form, or formless domain. The Path of Seeing does not originate above the desire domain: in the formless domain there is no hearing of Dharma and cognition does not take the lower domains as the required objects; in the form domain ordinary beings lack *saṃvega* because they are attached to modes of meditative attainment and do not experience painful feeling. As scripture says of the relevant non-returners, “Here is the commencement of the Path; there is its completion.”

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Path has a determinate birthplace without being confined to it. Noble Seeing begins only where instruction can be received, its required field can become object, and the urgency for liberation can arise. Once begun, however, the Path can continue upon grounds incapable of originating it and can attain its final fruit in any of the three existential domains.

Beginning and End therefore need not share a location or set of enabling conditions. The higher ground can sustain and complete a movement whose initiating principle it could not itself generate.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Distribution of the four fruits**

The Bhāṣya gives the asymmetric distribution:

```text
stream-entry
once-returning
non-returning
    → first attained in kāmadhātu only

Arhatship
    → attainable in kāmadhātu
      or rūpadhātu
      or ārūpyadhātu
```

The first two fruits belong only to practitioners for whom desire-domain passion remains relevant. The third requires the Path of Seeing, which does not newly arise above the desire domain. Arhatship can complete a Path already begun below.

**The formless-domain exclusion**

The Bhāṣya gives two reasons that are not stated explicitly in the kārikā:

```text
śravaṇābhāva
    absence of hearing Dharma

adhodhātvanālambana
    failure to take the lower domains
    as the required objects

therefore
    no origination of Darśana-mārga
    in ārūpyadhātu
```

This directly connects Path-entry with both semantic transmission and determinate object-field.

**The form-domain exclusion**

Form-domain ordinary beings lack **saṃvega** because they are attached to the entrances or modes of *samāpatti* and painful feeling is absent. Profound meditative attainment therefore does not by itself generate noble Seeing:

```text
samāpatti
    ≠ by itself Mārga
```

The working prose witness has a questionable form **saṃgenā** where the immediate argument requires *saṃvega*. This textual issue is preserved as a Bhāṣya-witness caution rather than silently normalized.

**saṃvega**

Here **saṃvega** is the urgent disturbance or spiritual shock through which ordinary existence becomes practically oriented toward liberation. It is not sufficient by itself, but in the form-domain explanation its absence blocks initial acquisition of the noble Path.

**yukti and āgama**

Vasubandhu marks his causal explanation as **yukti**, reasoning, and then adds **āgama**, scriptural confirmation. These are two layers of warrant:

```text
yukti
    explains why Path-entry does not occur above

āgama
    confirms beginning here and completion there
```

**vidhā and niṣṭhā**

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed word **vidhā**:

```text
vidhā
    = mārgārambha
    = commencement of the Path

niṣṭhā
    = completion of the Path
```

The cited five kinds of non-returner begin the noble Path here and reach culmination above.

## 8. Logical Determination

The three existential domains have different Path-capacities:

```text
Originates(PathOfSeeing, DesireDomain)

not Originates(PathOfSeeing, FormDomain)
    because lacks(Samvega)

not Originates(PathOfSeeing, FormlessDomain)
    because lacks(HearingOfDharma)
    and lacks(RequiredLowerDomainObject)
```

But origin does not delimit completion:

```text
BeginsIn(NoblePath, DesireDomain)
    + continuesThrough(HigherDomain)
    → mayCompleteAs(Arhatship, HigherDomain)
```

Therefore:

```text
ground of genesis
    ≠ necessarily
ground of culmination
```

Nor can a higher contemplative ground infer its own capacity to originate the Path from its capacity to support completion:

```text
supports(PathCompletion, HigherDomain)
    does not entail
originates(PathOfSeeing, HigherDomain)
```

The Path is transdomain in range but not placeless in genesis.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.55 gives Beginning and End their most concrete relation. Beginning is not an abstract first point: it is the conjunction of a communicable Dharma, an available object-field, and the existential capacity for *saṃvega*. End is not simply what was already latent in a superior domain: that domain can complete the Path only because the decisive Seeing was generated elsewhere.

In the Organon reconstruction, this guards against reading the completed state as self-grounding. The End contains a history it could not have originated from its own local conditions. The Path is the mediation that preserves the force of its determinate beginning while transporting it into a different domain of completion.

The source account is more specific than a general theory of contradiction. Vasubandhu distinguishes lack of hearing and object in the formless domain from lack of *saṃvega* in the form domain. These precise conditions—not a vague claim that suffering automatically produces insight—govern the genesis of noble Seeing.
