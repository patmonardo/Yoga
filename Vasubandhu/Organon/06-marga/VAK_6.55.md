# VAK_6.55 — Determinate Path-Beginning and Transdomain Completion

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कामे त्रयाप्तिरन्त्यस्य त्रिषु नोर्ध्वं हि दृक्पथः ।
असंवेगादिह विधा तत्र निष्ठेति चागमात् ॥ ६.५५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kāme trayāptir antyasya triṣu nordhvaṃ hi dṛkpathaḥ |
asaṃvegād iha vidhā tatra niṣṭheti cāgamāt || 6.55 ||
```

The kārikā compresses the reasons for the absence of the Path of Seeing above the desire realm. The Bhāṣya distributes them differently between the form and formless realms and explicitly defines **vidhā** as **mārgārambha**, commencement of the Path.

The three **dhātu** here are existential domains—desire, form, and formless existence—not the eighteen cognition-domains of Chapter 1.

## 4. Grammar

The locative **kāme** governs **trayāptiḥ**: attainment of the first three śrāmaṇya-fruits occurs in the desire realm. The ellipsis in **antyasya triṣu** supplies *āptiḥ*: attainment of the final fruit occurs in all three domains.

**na ūrdhvaṃ hi dṛkpathaḥ** gives the reason the third fruit cannot first be attained above: the Path of Seeing does not newly arise there. **asaṃvegāt** then supplies the kārikā's compressed reason, which the Bhāṣya applies to form-realm ordinary beings rather than indiscriminately to both higher domains.

The closing words cite an āgama in direct-speech form: **iha vidhā, tatra niṣṭhā**—“here is the commencement; there is the completion.” The opposition of the two locative adverbs correlates Path-origin and Path-completion without requiring identity of their domains.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In the desire realm three fruits are attained; the final fruit
> is attained in all three realms. The Path of Seeing does not arise
> above the desire realm, because of lack of *saṃvega* and because
> scripture says, “Here is commencement; there is completion.”

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The first three śrāmaṇya-fruits are attained only in the desire realm; arhatship can be attained in any of the three realms. Seeing does not begin above the desire realm. In the formless realm there is no hearing of Dharma, and lower realms are not taken as cognitive Conditions. Form-realm ordinary beings, attached to meditative attainments and without painful feeling, lack the *saṃvega* needed for noble Path-entry. As scripture says of the relevant non-returners, “Here the Path begins; there it reaches completion.”

### Translation note

The first two fruits require continuing relation to desire-realm
passion; the third requires Seeing, which does not originate above
that realm. The Bhāṣya gives hearing and lower-realm cognitive
Conditions as the formless-realm constraints, and lack of *saṃvega*
as the form-realm constraint. It glosses `vidhā` as `mārgārambha`,
commencement of the Path. The source's `saṃgenā` is retained in the
Bhāṣya witness beside the prose discussion of *saṃvega*.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Path has a determinate birthplace without being confined to it. Noble Seeing begins only where instruction can be received, the required cognitive Conditions are available, and the urgency for liberation can arise. Once begun, however, the Path can continue upon grounds incapable of originating it and can attain its final fruit in any of the three existential domains.

Beginning and End therefore need not share a location or set of enabling conditions. The higher ground can sustain and complete a movement whose initiating principle it could not itself generate.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kāme trayāptiḥ / antyasya triṣu` — distribution of four fruits**

The Bhāṣya gives the asymmetric distribution:

```text
stream-entry
once-returning
non-returning
    → first attained in kāmadhātu only

Arhatship
    → attainable in kāmadhātu
      or rūpadhātu
      or ārūpyadhātu
```

The first two fruits belong only to practitioners for whom desire-realm passion remains relevant. The third requires the Path of Seeing, which does not newly arise above the desire realm. Arhatship can complete a Path already begun below.

**`Śravaṇābhāva / adhodhātvanālambana` — formless-realm constraints**

The Bhāṣya gives two reasons that are not stated explicitly in the kārikā:

```text
śravaṇābhāva
    absence of hearing Dharma

adhodhātvanālambana
    lower realms not taken
    as cognitive Conditions

therefore
    no origination of Darśana-mārga
    in ārūpyadhātu
```

This directly connects Path-entry with both semantic transmission and determinate cognitive field.

**`Asaṃvega / samāpatti-mukha-saṅga` — form-realm constraints**

Form-domain ordinary beings lack **`Saṃvega` — urgency for Path-entry** because they are attached to the entrances or modes of *samāpatti* and painful feeling is absent. Profound meditative attainment therefore does not by itself generate noble Seeing:

```text
samāpatti
    ≠ by itself Mārga
```

The working prose witness has a questionable form **saṃgenā** where the immediate argument requires *saṃvega*. This textual issue is preserved as a Bhāṣya-witness caution rather than silently normalized.

**`Saṃvega` — urgency for Path-entry**

Here **`Saṃvega` — urgency for Path-entry** is the urgent disturbance or spiritual shock through which ordinary existence becomes practically oriented toward liberation. It is not sufficient by itself, but in the form-realm explanation its absence blocks initial acquisition of the noble Path.

**`Yukti / āgama` — reason and scriptural witness**

Vasubandhu marks his causal explanation as **yukti**, reasoning, and then adds **āgama**, scriptural confirmation. These are two layers of warrant:

```text
yukti
    explains why Path-entry does not occur above

āgama
    confirms beginning here and completion there
```

**`Vidhā / niṣṭhā` — commencement and completion**

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed word **vidhā**:

```text
vidhā
    = mārgārambha
    = commencement of the Path

niṣṭhā
    = completion of the Path
```

The cited five kinds of non-returner begin the noble Path here and reach culmination above.

## 8. Logical Determination

The three existential domains have different Path-capacities:

```text
Originates(PathOfSeeing, DesireDomain)

not Originates(PathOfSeeing, FormDomain)
    because lacks(Samvega)

not Originates(PathOfSeeing, FormlessDomain)
    because lacks(HearingOfDharma)
    and lacks(RequiredLowerDomainCondition)
```

But origin does not delimit completion:

```text
BeginsIn(NoblePath, DesireDomain)
    + continuesThrough(HigherDomain)
    → mayCompleteAs(Arhatship, HigherDomain)
```

Therefore:

```text
ground of genesis
    ≠ necessarily
ground of culmination
```

Nor can a higher contemplative ground infer its own capacity to originate the Path from its capacity to support completion:

```text
supports(PathCompletion, HigherDomain)
    does not entail
originates(PathOfSeeing, HigherDomain)
```

The Path is transdomain in range but not placeless in genesis.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.55 gives Beginning and End their most concrete relation. Beginning is not an abstract first point: it is the conjunction of a communicable Dharma, an available cognitive field, and the existential capacity for *saṃvega*. End is not simply what was already latent in a superior domain: that domain can complete the Path only because the decisive Seeing was generated elsewhere.

In the Organon reconstruction, this guards against reading the completed state as self-grounding. The End contains a history it could not have originated from its own local conditions. The Path is the mediation that preserves the force of its determinate beginning while transporting it into a different domain of completion.

The source account is more specific than a general theory of contradiction. Vasubandhu distinguishes lack of hearing and object in the formless realm from lack of *saṃvega* in the form realm. These precise conditions—not a vague claim that suffering automatically produces insight—govern the genesis of noble Seeing.
