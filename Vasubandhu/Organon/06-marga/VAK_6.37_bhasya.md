# VAK_6.37 Bhāṣya — Five Non-Returners and the Akaniṣṭha Course

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> so 'ntarotpanna-saṃskārāsaṃskāra-parinirvṛtiḥ |
>
> ūrdhvasrotāś ca sa dhyāne vyavakīrṇo 'kaniṣṭhagaḥ || 6.37 ||

> The non-returner completes the course in one of five ways: in an
> intermediate existence, soon after rebirth, through renewed effort,
> without renewed effort, or by an upward-moving stream through higher
> existences. The upward-streamer whose dhyāna is intermingled goes to
> Akaniṣṭha and reaches final Nirvāṇa there; the commentary distinguishes
> this course by its meditative cause and its furthest destination.

The verse expands one attained fruit into five modes of final completion
and specifies the intermingled-dhyāna upward course.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The same non-returner is fivefold. The verse first names final
completion in an intermediate existence, after arising, with exertion,
and without exertion; it then adds the upward-streamer. The one who
completes in the intermediate existence reaches final Nirvāṇa there.
The one who completes after arising does so shortly after rebirth
because the carrying Path is strongly engaged. This is explained as
Nirvāṇa with a remainder; others say without a remainder. The latter
is rejected because this person lacks mastery over relinquishing the
lifespan.

The one who completes with exertion has not relaxed Path application
after rebirth and reaches final Nirvāṇa through effort and the operation
of the carrying Path. The one who completes without exertion does so
without that renewed effort, lacking the same engaged carrying Path.
Others distinguish these types by Paths taking conditioned and
unconditioned cognitive Conditions; the commentary rejects this as
overextending the distinction. Scripture lists the effortless type
first, and this ordering is fitting: one completion comes without
striving and the other through striving. The after-arising type has
an even stronger carrying Path and milder residual anuśayas.

An upward-streamer is one whose course rises higher and who does not
reach final Nirvāṇa in the first place of rebirth. “Stream” and
“course” have the same sense here. The verse says that one whose
dhyāna is intermingled goes to Akaniṣṭha. The upward-streamer is
twofold by cause, according as dhyāna is intermingled or not, and by
result, with Akaniṣṭha or the summit of existence as furthest point.
The person whose dhyāna is intermingled goes to Akaniṣṭha and reaches
final Nirvāṇa there.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary unfolds the four compressed completion designations,
adds the upward-streamer, and clarifies where and how the first four
complete. It reports and rejects alternative accounts of remainder
and of the effort distinction, then correlates Path strength and
residual anuśaya. For the upward-streamer it defines the ascending
course and gives cause and result as two bases of differentiation.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Antarā`, `utpanna`, `saṃskāra`, and
`asaṃskāra` are expanded as alternative completion modes; the compound
is not one person's sequence of four events. The effort distinction
is tied to `prayoga` and the carrying Path, whereas the rejected
alternative turns on the Path's cognitive Condition (`ālambana`).
`Vyavakīrṇa-dhyāna` is kept technical; the source here determines its
upward-course function without fully analyzing its composition.

**Organon determination.** Fruit status, locus of completion, Path
strength, residual anuśaya, effort, meditative cause, and endpoint can
vary independently within the non-returner class. This is project
synthesis.

## 6. Five Modes and the Upward Cause

| Mode | Where or how completion occurs | Commentary qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Intermediate completion | In the intermediate existence | Before a new birth is established |
| After-arising completion | Shortly after rebirth | Strong carrying Path, milder residue |
| With-exertion completion | Through continued Path application | Deliberate effort remains operative |
| Without-exertion completion | Without renewed effort | Object-based explanation rejected |
| Upward-streamer | After movement to higher loci | Intermingled dhyāna leads to Akaniṣṭha here |

The five are modes of a non-returner, not five separate fruits.
