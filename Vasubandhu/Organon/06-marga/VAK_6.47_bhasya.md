# VAK_6.47 Bhāṣya — Continued Non-Possession and the Range of Anāgamya

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> bhavāgrādhavimuktordhvajātavattvasamanvayaḥ |
> anāsraveṇa vairāgyam anāgāmyena sarvataḥ || 6.47 ||

> One partly freed from the summit does not reacquire its afflictions
> when the supramundane attainment of separation is relinquished;
> likewise, someone reborn above the first dhyāna does not reacquire
> desire-realm afflictions after losing a mundane attainment. An
> uncontaminated Path supported by the preliminary concentration
> *anāgamya* can bring dispassion from every ground through the summit.

The first half completes the answer to 6.46. The second half states
the range of dispassion obtainable from an uncontaminated Path based
on the preliminary concentration.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

As with a person partly freed from the summit of existence, or with
someone reborn above a lower ground, there is no renewed possession.
Even if no mundane attainment of separation exists, the afflictions
do not again become possessed. A person partly freed from the summit
has no mundane attainment for that separation, yet does not regain
those afflictions when the supramundane attainment is relinquished
through a change of faculties. Likewise, an ordinary person reborn
above the first dhyāna does not regain desire-realm afflictions when
a previous mundane attainment of separation is relinquished. Thus
the reason proposed in 6.46 does not establish its conclusion.

Then the commentary asks: from which ground, and from what range, is
dispassion gained? Through an uncontaminated Path based on the
preliminary concentration *anāgamya*, dispassion can be gained from
every ground, through the summit of existence. A further question
asks whether someone relying on a preliminary concentration for
dispassion from a lower ground also has every liberation Path arise
from that preliminary concentration, just as the uninterrupted Paths
do. The answer is no; the next verse specifies the alternatives.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The closing counterexamples defeat a proposed inference from lost
attainment to returned affliction. The new question distinguishes the
Path's supporting ground from the range of dispassion it can produce;
`ābhavāgrāt` supplies the upper bound. The final question prepares
6.48's discussion of the liberation Path's ground.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The source's compact first half is read
through its prose examples without silently reconstructing its
morphology. `Ardha prakāra` is left numerically open. `Anāgamya` is
the preliminary concentration; `anāsrava` qualifies the Path, and
`sarvataḥ` is glossed as extending through the summit. The question
about liberation Paths at the end is a transition, not yet 6.47's
answer to that question.

**Organon determination.** A changed acquisition need not undo
non-possession of an affliction. Likewise a Path's local meditative
support need not set the outer limit of its counteractive range.
These are two distinguishable source claims joined in the project's
interpretation of an operative relation.
