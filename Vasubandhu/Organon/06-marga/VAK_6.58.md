# VAK_6.58 — Bounded Regression and the Irreversibility of Seeing

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
गोत्राच्चतुर्णां पञ्चानां फलाद्धानिर्न पूर्वकात् ।
शैक्षानार्याश्च षड्गोत्राः संचारो नास्ति दर्शने ॥ ६.५८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
gotrāc caturṇāṃ pañcānāṃ phalād dhānir na pūrvakāt |
śaikṣānāryāś ca ṣaḍgotrāḥ saṃcāro nāsti darśane || 6.58 ||
```

The phrase **na pūrvakāt** means, according to the Bhāṣya, that neither one's first disposition nor one's first noble fruit is lost. The assertion that five regress from Arhat-fruit belongs to the Vaibhāṣika classification and is explicitly contested by the Sautrāntikas.

The kārikā uses **hāni** generally; the Bhāṣya distinguishes **gotra-parihāṇi** from **phala-parihāṇi** and explains **saṃcāra** as **indriya-saṃcāra**.

## 4. Grammar

The first line distributes two ablative objects of regression. **gotrāt** belongs with **caturṇām**: four types can regress from a subsequently acquired disposition. **phalāt** belongs with **pañcānām**: according to the received Vaibhāṣika classification, the first five can regress from the Arhat fruit.

**na pūrvakāt** limits both claims. The Bhāṣya supplies the omitted nouns in parallel: there is no regression from one's **first disposition** or one's **first fruit**.

In the second line, **śaikṣāḥ** and **anāryāḥ** are coordinated subjects of **ṣaḍgotrāḥ**: trainees and ordinary persons also possess the six dispositions. The impersonal assertion **saṃcāro nāsti darśane** excludes transition of faculties during Seeing, not from the Path generally.


The fruit-regression rule is explicitly school-dependent; the
Sautrāntika counterargument rejects it.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Four can decline from a disposition and five from a fruit,
> but none from what was first. Trainees and ordinary persons also
> have six dispositions. Faculty transition does not occur during
> the Path of Seeing.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Four arhat types can lose a stronger disposition gained by sharpening their faculties. The Vaibhāṣika account also allows the first five to fall from arhat fruit, while holding that no one loses an original disposition or a first noble fruit. The Sautrāntikas dispute loss of arhatship and place the instability in meditative attainment. Trainees and ordinary persons likewise have six dispositions; during Seeing, however, faculties do not change, because that transition requires preparatory practice.

### Translation note

`Na pūrvakāt` limits both kinds of loss: the first disposition and
the first noble fruit remain firm. The fruit-regression statement is
the Vaibhāṣika account; the Bhāṣya then records the Sautrāntika
objection and its distinction between arhatship and conditionally
manifest meditative attainment. `Saṃcāra` is faculty transition,
excluded during Seeing because the required preparatory practice
cannot occur in that immediate Path sequence.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Path admits real transformation and real loss, but neither without limit. A stronger morphology acquired through cultivation can be relinquished, while the disposition first stabilized by the Path and the first noble breakthrough remain a secured floor. Later intensification is defeasible; the constitutive achievement of Seeing is not.

Nor can every transformation occur in every Path-moment. Seeing is an uninterrupted disclosure whose work is not the preparatory reconstruction of faculties. Faculty-transition requires extended application and therefore belongs outside Seeing, especially to cultivation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Gotra-parihāṇi` — regression from acquired disposition**

The Bhāṣya assigns **gotra-parihāṇi** to four types:

```text
cetanādharman
anurakṣaṇādharman
sthitākampya
prativedhanābhavya
    → may lose an acquired stronger gotra
```

The *parihāṇadharman* has no lower disposition beneath its own from which to regress, while the *akopyadharman* is excluded by immovability.

**`Na pūrvakāt` — the first disposition and fruit remain firm**

The Bhāṣya interprets **na pūrvakāt** through a strict distinction:

```text
prathama-gotra
    original disposition
    made firm by the Paths
    → not lost

uttāpana-pratilabdha-gotra
    stronger disposition later acquired
    through faculty-sharpening
    → may be lost
```

The same principle applies to fruit. One does not regress from one's first noble fruit; consequently stream-entry is never lost. Regression cannot erase the entire noble history.

**`Darśanaheya / avastuka / vitathālambana` — Seeing and false apprehension**

The Bhāṣya describes afflictions abandoned by Seeing as **avastuka** in a technical sense. This does not mean that they have no cognitive Condition. They take real aggregates as cognitive Conditions but apprehend them falsely (**vitathālambana**) by superimposing agent, experiencer, controller, or self.

```text
real aggregates
    + false self-superimposition
    → darśana-heya error

noble Seeing destroys that error
    → it cannot simply reappear through inattention
```

Cultivation-afflictions such as attachment and aversion can arise toward actually given agreeable or disagreeable features and are termed **savastuka** in this contrast. A lapse of mindfulness can reactivate them, whereas the self-view destroyed by Seeing is not restored in the same way.

**`Vaibhāṣika phala-parihāṇi` — possible loss of arhat fruit**

The kārikā's **pañcānāṃ phalād dhāniḥ** presents the Vaibhāṣika thesis:

```text
first five Arhat-types
    → may regress from Arhatship

akopyadharman
    → cannot regress
```

This is a school determination, not an uncontested conclusion of the Bhāṣya.

**`Sautrāntika aparihāṇi` — no loss of arhatship**

The Sautrāntikas deny regression from Arhatship. They interpret condition-dependent liberation as a fundamental concentration brought into presence under suitable conditions for pleasant abiding in this life, not as defeasible destruction of the afflictions.

Their reasoning is a dilemma:

```text
complete antidote has arisen
    → afflictions cannot arise again
    → regression from Arhatship is incoherent

complete antidote has not arisen
    → influxes are not exhausted
    → the person is not yet an Arhat
```

The dispute must remain explicit:

```text
Vaibhāṣika
    Arhat-fruit of first five may be lost

Sautrāntika
    Arhatship is irreversible;
    only meditative ease or attainment is unstable
```

**`Ṣaḍ-gotra` — dispositions across trainee and ordinary stages**

The Bhāṣya extends the taxonomy genetically:

```text
pṛthagjana disposition
    → śaikṣa disposition
    → Arhat disposition
```

The final types continue capacities already present and cultivated before Arhatship; they are not invented only at the endpoint.

**`Darśane na saṃcāraḥ` — no faculty change within Seeing**

The Bhāṣya explains:

```text
indriya-saṃcāra
    requires prayoga
    requires cultivation over time

Darśana-mārga
    has no room for that preparatory effort
    → no faculty-transition within Seeing
```

Transition may occur in an ordinary person or a faith-resolved trainee, but not inside the immediate noble sequence itself.

## 8. Logical Determination

The possible losses must be typed by their object:

```text
Regression(x)
    requires targetOfRegression(x)

targetOfRegression
    = AcquiredDisposition
      or, for Vaibhāṣika, ArhatFruit
      or, for Sautrāntika, MeditativeAttainment
```

The secured floor is:

```text
OriginalDisposition(x)
    + stabilizedBy(x, Path)
    → irreversible(x)

FirstNobleFruit(y)
    + establishedBy(y, Seeing)
    → irreversible(y)
```

Later strengthening differs:

```text
AcquiredStrongerDisposition(z)
    + producedBy(z, FacultySharpening)
    → possiblyLost(z)
```

And the temporal rule is strict:

```text
FacultyTransition
    → requires(PreparatoryApplication)

PathOfSeeing
    → excludes(PreparatoryApplication)

therefore
PathOfSeeing
    → excludes(FacultyTransition)
```

The Path is consequently neither rigid nor indefinitely fluid: constitutive Seeing is irreversible, while later cultivated intensifications can remain modal and defeasible.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.58 determines the exact boundary between Perception and reflective reconstruction. Seeing does not pause to rebuild its faculty-morphology; it proceeds as one immediate truth-determined sequence. The heavier machinery of *prayoga* belongs before or after it, not within it. This gives textual precision to the rule: keep Seeing perceptual.

In the Organon reconstruction, the Path has memory. A later disposition may be lost without returning the continuum to an unmediated beginning, because its first noble determination remains incorporated as an irreversible floor. Development can reverse locally without making its entire genesis unreal.

The school dispute prevents a premature universal claim about the final boundary of reversibility. Vaibhāṣika allows regression from Arhatship for five types; Sautrāntika argues that genuine exhaustion must itself be irreversible and relocates the unstable element to meditative attainment. The study preserves both voices rather than deciding the controversy by project logic.
