# VAK_6.53 Bhāṣya — Eight Knowings and Mixed Fruit

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> jñānāṣṭakasya lābho ’tha ṣoḍaśākārabhāvanā |
> laukikāptaṃ tu miśratvād anāsravāptir dhṛteḥ phalam || 6.53 ||

> At each principal fruit, the eight Knowings are acquired together
> and the sixteen Truth-aspects are cultivated. An abandonment gained
> through a mundane Path can still belong to the once-returner or
> non-returner fruit: it is joined with abandonments of the Path of
> Seeing, gathered under one attainment of separation, and maintained
> by an uncontaminated attainment. Its mundane origin remains part
> of the fruit's history.

The first half completes the five criteria from 6.52. The second
explains how a mundanely attained abandonment can belong to a fruit
of the uncontaminated Path.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The next two criteria are acquisition of the eight Knowings and
cultivation of the sixteen aspects. The previous Path is relinquished
and a new Path attained when the candidate's Path gives way to the
fruit Path. The abandonments are gathered because the whole is
acquired through one attainment. The eight Knowings—four kinds of
Dharma-Knowing and four of Anvaya-Knowing—are acquired simultaneously.
The sixteen Truth-aspects, beginning with impermanence, are cultivated.
All five criteria occur at every principal fruit.

If the uncontaminated Path is śrāmaṇya, how can the two fruits reached
in part by a mundane Path count as fruits of śrāmaṇya? The verse
answers that what is mundanely attained is included because the fruit
is mixed and an uncontaminated attainment maintains it. The
once-returner or non-returner fruit is not merely the abandonment
produced by the mundane Path. Abandonments belonging to the Path of
Seeing are also mixed into it, and one attainment of separation is
acquired for the entire content included in that fruit.

The commentary cites the sūtra's descriptions: the once-returner
fruit includes abandonment of three fetters and attenuation of
passion, aversion, and delusion; the non-returner fruit includes
abandonment of five lower fetters. An uncontaminated attainment of
separation sustains the abandonment. The closing source lines link
that sustaining power to not falling away at death and to the
fruit's status as śrāmaṇya-fruit; their transmitted wording is
uneven and should be collated before a tighter translation.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary completes the five criteria and then poses a new
objection: how can a fruit involving mundane Path activity belong to
the uncontaminated Path? Its answer distinguishes the origin of one
abandonment from the mixed content and uncontaminated attainment that
gather and sustain the fruit.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Jñāna` is Knowing. The first half is
shared with 6.52; the second names `miśratva` and
`anāsrava-āpti` as grounds for fruit-inclusion. The prose's
`eka-visaṃyoga-prāpti` gives one attainment for the whole fruit.
The sūtra quotations specify the once-returner and non-returner
contents. The final death-related sentence is kept cautious because
the source form is uneven.

**Organon determination.** A fruit can integrate abandonments with
different Path histories without changing how each arose. The source
specifies mixture and uncontaminated maintenance; reading this as
preservation of distinct genesis in one attained whole is project
synthesis.
