# VAK 6.69 — Predominance within the Whole Practice

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
वीर्यं सम्यक्प्रहाणाख्यमृद्धिपादाः समाधयः ।
प्रधानग्रहणं सर्वे गुणाः प्रायोगिकास्तु ते ॥ ६.६९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
vīryaṃ samyakprahāṇākhyam ṛddhipādāḥ samādhayaḥ |
pradhānagrahaṇaṃ sarve guṇāḥ prāyogikās tu te || 6.69 ||
```

The verse completes the examples begun in 6.68 and then states their governing rule. A Bodhi-aligned practice is classified through its principal factor without being compositionally reduced to that factor.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **vīryam** | neuter nominative singular | energy as principal dharma of the right abandonments |
| **samyak-prahāṇa-ākhyam** | neuter nominative singular compound | bearing the designation “right abandonments” |
| **ṛddhi-pādāḥ** | masculine nominative plural | bases of spiritual power or accomplishment |
| **samādhayaḥ** | masculine nominative plural | samādhis; collectednesses as their principal nature |
| **pradhāna-grahaṇam** | neuter nominative singular compound | taking or designation according to the predominant factor |
| **sarve** | masculine nominative plural | all |
| **guṇāḥ** | masculine nominative plural | qualities or excellences |
| **prāyogikāḥ** | masculine nominative plural | belonging to application or cultivated practice |
| **tu** | adversative particle | but, however |
| **te** | masculine nominative plural pronoun | those practice-groups / those qualities in them |

```text
vīryam | samyak-prahāṇa-ākhyam | ṛddhi-pādāḥ | samādhayaḥ |
pradhāna-grahaṇam | sarve | guṇāḥ | prāyogikāḥ | tu | te
```

The final pronoun is compact, but the Bhāṣya makes the referent operationally clear: the three practice-groups contain all qualities belonging to application.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda gives a nominal identification:

```text
vīryam
    = samyakprahāṇākhyam
```

The second identifies plural **ṛddhipādāḥ** with plural **samādhayaḥ**. Neither equation is exhaustive identity in every respect; **pradhānagrahaṇam** supplies the syntactic and doctrinal restriction.

The final clause is adversative:

```text
principal factor is taken for classification

but

all applied qualities belong to the concrete practice
```

Thus **tu** prevents the nominal equations from becoming reductions of wholes to isolated parts.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Energy is what is called the right abandonments, and the bases of spiritual power are samādhis. This is taking the principal factor; but all the qualities belonging to application are in them.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Energy gives the right abandonments their operative character; samādhi does the same for the bases of spiritual power. Each name takes the predominant factor, while the complete practice still includes all the qualities active in its application.

### Translation note

The Bhāṣya explains the right abandonments through energy’s direction of body, speech, and mind, and the bases of power through samādhi as the support of accomplished qualities. **Pradhānagrahaṇa** governs both identifications and the preceding identification of recollection establishments with Prajñā. The verse’s **tu** preserves all the qualities active in each practice-complex. The further disputes over bases of power and the faculty/power distinction remain in the commentary.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

A practical whole receives its determinate name from the operation governing it, not from the exclusion of every other operation. Predominance organizes plurality: energy makes the right abandonments exertive, and samādhi makes the bases of accomplishment stable, while the full constellation of Bodhi-aligned qualities remains active within each.

The Path is consequently neither an aggregate of independent factors nor an abstract unity beneath them. It is an ordered whole whose principal and associated determinations cooperate without losing their difference.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Pradhānagrahaṇa` — naming by the predominant factor**

The Bhāṣya explicitly corrects an overly literal reading of 6.68:

```text
practice-complex
    contains many applied qualities

classification
    takes its principal factor

functional name
    expresses the whole under that predominance
```

Therefore:

```text
smṛtyupasthāna
    principally prajñā

samyakprahāṇa
    principally vīrya

ṛddhipāda
    principally samādhi
```

“Principally” is indispensable. None of these formulations states that the complete practice contains only the named dharma.

**`Sarve prāyogikā guṇāḥ` — all applied qualities**

The Bhāṣya names the three groups together:

```text
smṛtyupasthānas
samyakprahāṇas
ṛddhipādas

each contains
all prāyogika-guṇas
```

The associated qualities are not incidental decorations. They are constitutive conditions of the practice’s concrete operation, although one factor governs its classification.

**`Samyakprahāṇa / vīrya` — right abandonment governed by energy**

Vasubandhu derives the name functionally:

```text
through vīrya
    body, speech, and mind
    are correctly directed and exerted
```

The semantic force of **samyakprahāṇa** therefore includes correct exertion as well as abandonment. Vīrya predominates because it performs this directing operation.

**`Ṛddhipāda / samādhi` — bases of power governed by collectedness**

The Bhāṣya explains:

```text
samādhi
    is the ground upon which
    attainment of all qualities is established
```

Samādhi is principal without being solitary. Precisely as stable ground, it supports the coordinated qualities by which accomplishment becomes possible.

**`Ṛddhipāda / chanda / citta` — rejected alternative count**

An unattributed alternative proposes:

```text
samādhi itself = ṛddhi
chanda and the others = its separate bases
```

Vasubandhu rejects this because it would add aspiration (**chanda**) and mind (**citta**) as distinct dravyas, raising the count to thirteen, and because it conflicts with the sūtra’s separate description of ṛddhi.

The criticism establishes:

```text
ṛddhi
    accomplishment or supernormal power

ṛddhipāda
    samādhi-complex serving as its basis

these are related
    but not simply identical
```

The immediate passage does not identify the school holding the alternative, so no attribution should be imposed.

**`Indriya / bala` — faculty and power by degree**

The Bhāṣya then asks why the same five dharmas receive both names:

```text
śraddhā
vīrya
smṛti
samādhi
prajñā
```

The answer is difference of degree:

```text
mṛdu
    weaker, still capable of being overcome
        → indriya

adhimātra
    intensified, no longer capable of being overcome
        → bala
```

Thus the designation changes because operative stability changes, while the underlying five dharmas remain the same.

**`Śraddhā → vīrya → smṛti → samādhi → prajñā` — the faculty order**

Their sequence is practical rather than merely enumerative:

```text
śraddhā
    confidence in the fruit
        → initiates

vīrya
    exertion for that fruit
        → establishes

smṛti
    sustained recollection
        → prevents distraction and permits

samādhi
    collected mind
        → enables

prajñā
    knowledge of things as they are
```

Each determination becomes a condition for the next. The sequence therefore presents a genesis of Path-capacity, not five externally juxtaposed possessions.

## 8. Logical Determination

The governing model is:

```text
PracticeComplex
    hasPrincipalFactor PrincipalDharma
    hasAssociatedFactor AppliedQuality
    receivesNameFrom PrincipalDharma
```

This yields two complementary prohibitions:

```text
hasPrincipalFactor(x, y)
    does not entail
x contains only y

hasAssociatedFactor(x, z)
    does not entail
z governs the classification of x
```

The faculty–power distinction adds intensification:

```text
SameDharma(d)
    + OvercomeableDegree
        → FacultyFunction

SameDharma(d)
    + UnovercomeableDegree
        → PowerFunction
```

And the faculty order supplies genetic dependence:

```text
Confidence
    enables Energy
Energy
    enables Recollection
Recollection
    enables Collectedness
Collectedness
    enables Prajñā
```

The Path-system therefore combines composition, predominance, intensification, and ordered generation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse gives the precise rule required by the Semantic Copula. When the Kośa says:

```text
samyakprahāṇa is vīrya
ṛddhipāda is samādhi
```

the copula does not assert featureless identity. It states that one dharma is the principal determination through which a coordinated practice-whole performs and receives its name.

The reciprocal side is equally important for the Organon reconstruction:

```text
vīrya determines the right abandonments
as an exertive practice

the right-abandonment structure determines
what vīrya is doing in this Path-position
```

Likewise, samādhi organizes the bases of accomplishment, while their determinate practical relation gives samādhi its meaning here as the ground of attained qualities.

This avoids two errors:

```text
atomism
    principal factor isolated from its companions

indifferent holism
    all factors present but none governing the form
```

The Bhāṣya instead presents an internally differentiated whole. Moreover, the sequence from confidence to prajñā shows that its members do not merely coexist: each produces the practical condition under which the next becomes possible. That is the Path Meaning itself as ordered mediation, although this final formulation belongs to the project synthesis rather than Vasubandhu’s explicit terminology.
