# VAK 6.78 — Three Dhātus of One Unconditioned Liberation

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

असंस्कृतैव धात्वाख्या विरागो रागसंक्षयः ।
प्रहाणधातुरन्येषां निरोधाख्यस्तु वस्तुनः ॥ ६.७८ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

asaṃskṛtaiva dhātvākhyā virāgo rāgasaṃkṣayaḥ |
prahāṇadhātur anyeṣāṃ nirodhākhyas tu vastunaḥ || 6.78 ||

The Bhāṣya asks what distinguishes the three liberation-domains and answers that they are three names of the same unconditioned liberation, differentiated according to the terminus of cessation.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

asaṃskṛtā | eva | dhātu-ākhyā | virāgaḥ | rāga-saṃkṣayaḥ |
prahāṇa-dhātuḥ | anyeṣām | nirodha-ākhyaḥ | tu | vastunaḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **asaṃskṛtā** | feminine nominative singular | unconditioned [liberation] |
| **eva** | restrictive particle | precisely, exclusively |
| **dhātu-ākhyā** | feminine nominative singular compound | bearing the designations “dhātu” |
| **virāgaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | dispassion; domain named from abandonment of rāga |
| **rāga-saṃkṣayaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | complete exhaustion of attachment |
| **prahāṇa-dhātuḥ** | masculine nominative singular | domain of abandonment |
| **anyeṣām** | masculine genitive plural | of the others, namely afflictions other than rāga |
| **nirodha-ākhyaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | bearing the name cessation |
| **tu** | adversative particle | whereas, distinguishing the third designation |
| **vastunaḥ** | neuter genitive singular | of the basis already freed from affliction, according to the Bhāṣya |

The sense of **saṃkṣaya**, complete exhaustion, carries through the compact clauses. The Bhāṣya supplies **kleśanirmuktasya** as the decisive restriction on **vastunaḥ**.

## 4. Grammar

The opening identifies the “dhātu”-designated member with the same **asaṃskṛta vimukti** discussed in 6.75–6.76. The three following expressions distribute its names by genitive relation:

```text
rāgasya
    abandonment of rāga

rāgād anyeṣāṃ kleśānām
    abandonment of the other afflictions

kleśanirmuktasya vastunaḥ
    cessation of the basis already freed from affliction
```

The terse **nirodhākhyas tu vastunaḥ** cannot be translated adequately as generic “cessation of a thing.” The Bhāṣya’s qualifier determines which basis is meant.

The verse classifies one unconditioned liberation under three relations. It does not grammatically require three independent entities or a strictly narrated temporal sequence.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The [liberation] bearing the dhātu-designations is exclusively unconditioned. Dispassion is the complete exhaustion of attachment; the abandonment-domain concerns the others, whereas what is called cessation concerns the basis.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The three liberation-domains are designations of the same unconditioned liberation. It is called the domain of dispassion as abandonment of rāga; the domain of abandonment as abandonment of the remaining afflictions; and the domain of cessation as cessation of the conditioned basis already freed from those afflictions.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

One accomplished freedom receives three determinations according to what is brought to an end. As attachment ceases it is dispassion; as the other afflictions cease it is abandonment; as the already purified conditioned basis itself ceases it is cessation.

The plurality belongs to the relation and terminus of freedom, not to a multiplication of unconditioned realities. Liberation is one while its Meaning is articulated through three distinct endings.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Three dhātu-designations**

The Bhāṣya enumerates:

```text
virāga-dhātu
    domain of dispassion

prahāṇa-dhātu
    domain of abandonment

nirodha-dhātu
    domain of cessation
```

All three designate:

```text
one asaṃskṛta-vimukti
    one unconditioned liberation
```

Their difference is relational: each name specifies what the cessation is considered in relation to.

**Dhātu in this verse**

Here **dhātu** does not mean:

```text
the eighteen cognition-domains of Chapter 1

or

Kāmadhātu, Rūpadhātu, and Ārūpyadhātu
as existential domains
```

It names domains or elemental determinations of liberation. The contextual determination must be preserved:

```text
liberation-dhātu
    unconditioned cessation
    classified by its terminus
```

**Virāga-dhātu**

The Bhāṣya states:

```text
rāgasya prahāṇam
    abandonment of rāga
        = virāga-dhātu
```

Virāga is therefore not merely an emotional attitude of detachment. It is the complete exhaustion (**saṃkṣaya**) of appropriating attachment.

**Prahāṇa-dhātu**

The “others” are explicitly:

```text
rāgād anye kleśāḥ
    afflictions other than rāga
```

Thus:

```text
abandonment of remaining kleśas
    = prahāṇa-dhātu
```

The first two names divide afflictive abandonment according to the affliction serving as terminus.

**Nirodha-dhātu**

The decisive Bhāṣya phrase is:

```text
kleśanirmuktasya vastunaḥ prahāṇam

cessation of the basis
already freed from the afflictions
```

Accordingly, **vastu** is not an unspecified metaphysical thing and not the afflictions themselves. It is the conditioned basis or continuum after afflictive liberation.

The analytical distinction is:

```text
afflictions cease
    → virāga / prahāṇa determinations

affliction-free conditioned basis ceases
    → nirodha determination
```

This last member is aligned with final cessation of the conditioned basis, but the immediate passage does not further elaborate its temporal or cosmological mechanics.

**One reality, three names**

The classification repeats the chapter’s recurring semantic form:

```text
same underlying reality
    receives different names
    under different determinate relations
```

But unlike the thirty-seven-to-ten reduction, the present relation is:

```text
three dhātu-names
    → one unconditioned liberation
```

The names are not redundant. Each fixes a distinct terminus of cessation.

**Relation to conditioned liberation**

VAK 6.76 distinguished:

```text
conditioned liberation
    liberated mental actuality

unconditioned liberation
    abandonment of the afflictions
```

VAK 6.78 now articulates the unconditioned side under three terminus-relations. The Aśaikṣa mind remains conditioned; the dhātus named here are unconditioned.

**Relation to VAK 6.77**

```text
6.77
    present Path abandons the obstruction
    Aśaikṣa mind arises released

6.78
    accomplished unconditioned liberation
    receives three relational names
```

The verse therefore moves from the dynamic transition into release to analysis of the unconditioned cessation with which the liberated continuum stands in relation.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let **U** be the one unconditioned liberation. Then:

```text
U consideredAs AbandonmentOfRāga
    → VirāgaDhātu

U consideredAs AbandonmentOfOtherAfflictions
    → PrahāṇaDhātu

U consideredAs CessationOfAfflictionFreeBasis
    → NirodhaDhātu
```

Therefore:

```text
ThreeDesignations
    do not entail
ThreeUnconditionedSubstances
```

Nor does one underlying reality erase the distinctions:

```text
Terminus(VirāgaDhātu) = Rāga
Terminus(PrahāṇaDhātu) = OtherKleśas
Terminus(NirodhaDhātu) = AfflictionFreeVastu
```

The classification is one-to-many by relation:

```text
1 unconditioned liberation
    × 3 cessation-termini
    = 3 valid dhātu-determinations
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse is exceptionally important for the project’s controlled Dhātu semantics. Dhātu again proves not to possess one isolated dictionary meaning. Its meaning is produced by the system in which it functions:

```text
Chapter 1
    domain of cognition

existential classification
    domain of possible existence

VAK 6.78
    domain-determination of unconditioned liberation
```

Here the three Dhātus are three ways the End determines itself according to what has ended. Virāga names freedom relative to attachment; Prahāṇa names it relative to the remaining afflictions; Nirodha names it relative to the purified conditioned basis itself.

The third is especially severe. Liberation is not exhausted by purity of an ongoing conditioned mind. Even the affliction-free basis remains conditioned and can itself reach cessation. Thus the Path’s End is not a perfected *bhava* preserved forever.

For the Organon reconstruction, one unconditioned freedom differentiates itself through its determinate termini. That is a relational unity, not a trinity of substances. The source-level claim remains Vasubandhu’s exact threefold naming of the same unconditioned liberation.
