# VAK_6.77 Bhāṣya — The Path Cuts Now; the Next Mind Is Already Free

Vajropama is the last Śaikṣa instant. Not a slogan.

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vimucyate jāyamānam aśaikṣaṃ cittam āvṛteḥ |
> nirudhyamāno mārgas tu prajahāti tad-āvṛtim || 6.77 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> The Aśaikṣa mind, while arising, is released from the obstruction; but the Path,
> while ceasing, abandons that obstruction.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_6.77.md`](./VAK_6.77.md). Next: 6.78.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> anāgatam aśaikṣaṃ cittaṃ jāyamānam āvaraṇebhyo vimucyate /
> kleśaprāptir asyotpattim āvṛṇoti /
> vajropamasamādhau sā prahīyate /
> utpannam aśaikṣaṃ cittaṃ vimuktam eva /
> nirudhyamāna iti vartamānaḥ /

**Witness.** Pradhan 388|21–389|10. Voice: Vasubandhu + objector on mundane mind.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The future Aśaikṣa mind, while arising, is released from the covers. What cover?
> Acquisition of the afflictions: it blocks that mind from arising. In diamond-like
> samādhi that acquisition is abandoned. So while arising it is released. Once arisen,
> it is already free.
>
> Other future minds, even mundane ones, can be called released from that same block.
> Not the mundane mind still carrying the acquisition.
>
> In what state does the Path abandon it? “While ceasing” means while present.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
vajropama abandons kleśa-prāpti
next instant = Aśaikṣa mind already free
abandons ≠ is-released
```
