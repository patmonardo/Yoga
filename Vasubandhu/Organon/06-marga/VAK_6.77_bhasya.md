# VAK 6.77 Bhāṣya — The Path Abandons the Obstruction as Consciousness Arises Free

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vimucyate jāyamānam aśaikṣaṃ cittam āvṛteḥ |
> nirudhyamāno mārgas tu prajahāti tad-āvṛtim || 6.77 ||

> The Consciousness beyond training, as it arises, is freed from the obstruction; the Path, while ceasing—that is, while present—abandons that obstruction.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Which Consciousness is liberated: past, future, or present? “The Consciousness beyond training, as it arises, is released from the obstruction.” The treatise reads: “The future Consciousness beyond training, while arising, is released from obstructions.” What is its obstruction? Acquisition of the afflictions, because it prevents that Consciousness from arising. In diamond-like concentration that acquisition is abandoned. Thus the Consciousness beyond training, as it arises, is released; and that acquisition has been abandoned. The Consciousness beyond training, once arisen, is liberated.

But what of [future Consciousness] that does not arise, and mundane Consciousness? These too are released. The verse names what is certain to arise. From what is mundane Consciousness released? From that same obstruction to arising. But mundane Consciousness arises even for a trainee who is not liberated. Such [Consciousness] is not of that kind. What kind is it? It is accompanied by acquisition of the afflictions.

In what state does the Path abandon that obstruction to arising? “The Path, while ceasing, abandons that obstruction.” This means while present.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening question distinguishes past, future, and present Consciousness. The cited treatise supplies the arising future Consciousness beyond training as the primary case. The obstruction is specifically afflictive acquisition, which the diamond-like concentration abandons. The subsequent exchange extends release to the non-arising future and mundane cases, then limits the comparison with mundane Consciousness still accompanied by afflictive acquisition. The final gloss reads `nirudhyamāna` as `vartamāna`: the Path abandons while present.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Citta` is Consciousness, distinct from Cognition and Mind. `Kleśaprāpti` is acquisition of afflictions, not an affliction currently manifest as a mental episode. `Utpatti-āvaraṇa` is the obstruction to arising. The source's `vivandhatvāt` is retained as transmitted; its force in context is prevention of arising. `Nirudhyamāna` retains “while ceasing” in the verse, while the Bhāṣya explains its operative time as “while present.” The quoted `śāstrapāṭhaḥ` is a treatise reading cited by the Bhāṣya, not a second independent reading of the kārikā.

**Organon determination.** The present Path's abandonment and the arising Consciousness's liberation are distinct moments of the transition. This temporal account follows the Bhāṣya's stated sequence; describing that sequence as the Path's effective mediation belongs to the Organon study.

## 6. Scope of the Release

| Case | Bhāṣya's determination |
|---|---|
| Arising Consciousness beyond training | The verse's certain successor to diamond-like concentration; arises released from afflictive acquisition |
| Once-arisen Consciousness beyond training | Already liberated |
| Non-arising future or mundane Consciousness | Also released from that obstruction, though the verse names the certain arising case |
| Trainee's mundane Consciousness accompanied by afflictive acquisition | Not released in the relevant sense |

The broader cases do not erase the causal specificity of the verse's chosen case.
