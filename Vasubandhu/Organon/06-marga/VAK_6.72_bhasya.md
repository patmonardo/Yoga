# VAK_6.72 Bhāṣya — The Path Factors Across Contemplative Grounds

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dvitīye ’nyatra saṃkalpād dvayos tad-dvaya-varjitāḥ |
>
> dhyānāntare ca śīlāṅgais tābhyāṃ ca triṣv arūpiṣu || 6.72 ||

> In the second dhyāna the Path loses right resolve, since discursive thought is absent. In the third and fourth dhyānas, and in intermediate concentration, joy falls away with resolve. The three lower formless attainments lack those two and the ethical Path factors of speech, action, and livelihood.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

In the second dhyāna there are precisely thirty-six, with right resolve excluded because applied thought is absent there. In the third and fourth dhyānas there are thirty-five, deprived of joy and resolve. In the intermediate concentration too there are precisely thirty-five, deprived of those same two.

For the three formless attainments, “deprived” continues to govern the verse. There are thirty-two, deprived of right speech, right action, and right livelihood, as well as joy and resolve.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary takes the complete thirty-sevenfold configuration of the first dhyāna as its implicit comparison. It identifies the single missing factor in the second dhyāna and gives a reason: without `vitarka`, right resolve does not occur there. It then resolves “that pair” as joy and resolve for the third and fourth dhyānas and applies the same exclusion to intermediate concentration. Finally it carries `varjitāḥ` into the last pāda and names the three ethical Path factors excluded along with that pair in the lower formless attainments.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Saṃkalpa` is right resolve in this Path inventory; `vitarka` is the applied or discursive thought whose absence the Bhāṣya gives as the reason for its exclusion. `Tad-dvaya` means joy and resolve, not an unspecified second pair. `Śīlāṅga` is expanded by the Bhāṣya into right speech, right action, and right livelihood. The word “lower” in “three lower formless attainments” clarifies that Bhavāgra is treated in 6.73. The immediate source states the exclusions and counts, without separately arguing why the ethical factors cannot operate in the formless grounds.

**Organon determination.** A contemplative ground supports a determinate configuration of the one Path. The Bhāṣya's ground-by-ground exclusions give this project a model for conditional Path operation; that model is an interpretation of the source's distribution, not an additional claim made by its verse.
