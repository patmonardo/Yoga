# VAK_6.35 Bhāṣya — Candidate for the Second Fruit and Once-Return

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ā-pañcama-prakāra-ghno dvitīya-pratipannakaḥ |
> kṣīṇa-ṣaṣṭha-prakāras tu sakṛd-āgāmy asau punaḥ || 6.35 ||

> One who has destroyed up through the fifth grade is a candidate for the second
> fruit; when the sixth grade is exhausted, that one is again a once-returner.

The same practitioner moves from an established first fruit through
candidate status to attainment of the second fruit.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

That same person, again established in a fruit, becomes a candidate for
the second fruit by destroying up through the fifth grade. If any grade
from the first through the fifth has been abandoned by a person
established in the first fruit, that person should be understood as
proceeding toward the second fruit.

When the sixth grade is exhausted, that same person becomes a
once-returner: the second fruit has been attained. The name is explained
by one coming back to the human world after going among the gods. The
following compressed phrase concerning a further birth is uncertain
in this witness (`pareṇa janmāghāvāt`). The name is also explained by
the thinning of greed, hatred, and delusion: only mild grades remain.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The prose fixes the starting point as a person established in a fruit,
specifies the candidate range as one through five abandoned grades,
then identifies exhaustion of the sixth with attainment of the second
fruit. It explains “once-returner” by return from divine to human life
and by the attenuation of greed, hatred, and delusion.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Āpañcama` includes the fifth grade;
`pratipannaka` designates one proceeding toward a fruit, whereas
`dvitīyaphalaprāpta` marks its attainment. The study prints the
received `pratipannakaḥ` while preserving the source's
`pratīpannakaḥ` in §2. The unclear `janmāghāvāt` is not made to bear a
precise birth count beyond the explicit once-return explanation.

**Organon determination.** A fruit already attained and candidacy for
a subsequent fruit can coexist. Grade removal must be typed with the
current fruit, target fruit, and Path sequence; sixth-grade exhaustion
changes the status to second-fruit attainment. This is project
synthesis.

## 6. Second-Fruit Threshold

| Abandonment after stream-entry | Path status | Fruit status | Ground in the prose |
|---|---|---|---|
| One through five grades | Candidate for the second fruit | First fruit already possessed | `yasya phalasthasya ... yāvat pañcamaḥ` |
| Sixth grade exhausted | Once-returner | Second fruit attained | `dvitīyaphalaprāpto bhavati` |
| Mild grades remain | Afflictions attenuated | Second-fruit position | `rāgadveṣamohānāṃ ... tanutvāt` |

The five-grade case is a candidate position in this passage. The
previous unit's objection excludes it as a *family-to-family* fruit
classification; the two claims have different status scopes.
