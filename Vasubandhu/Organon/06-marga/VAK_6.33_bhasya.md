# VAK_6.33 Bhāṣya — Nine Grades of Fault and Opposing Path

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nava-prakārā doṣā hi bhūmau bhūmau tathā guṇāḥ |
> mṛdu-madhyādhimātrāṇāṃ punar mṛdv-ādi-bhedataḥ || 6.33 ||

> In each ground, afflictions are ninefold, and so too are the qualities; mild,
> medium, and intense are each further divided beginning with mild.

The commentary expands the recursive ninefold division and explains the
inverse order in which Path grades oppose afflictions.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The gradual sequence is now to be explained. First this classification:
as the desire-realm afflictions have been taught in nine grades, so in
every ground, up to the summit of existence, faults are ninefold. The
opposing qualities called uninterrupted and liberation Paths are likewise
ninefold in each ground.

How is the ninefold division obtained? Mild, medium, and strong are the
three primary grades; each is subdivided into mild, medium, and strong.
Thus the nine are mild-mild, mild-medium, mild-strong, medium-mild,
medium-medium, medium-strong, strong-mild, strong-medium, and
strong-strong.

The mild-mild Path abandons the strong-strong affliction, and so on until
the strong-strong Path abandons the mild-mild affliction. The strongest
Path cannot be present at the beginning; once it has arisen, the
strongest affliction cannot remain. Coarse dirt is removed from cloth
first, then subtle dirt. A slight light dispels coarse darkness, while
strong light dispels subtle darkness. This is the point of the analogy.
Bright dharmas are powerful and dark dharmas weak. Even a momentary mild
Noble Path can uproot strong afflictions nourished through the succession
of beginningless saṃsāra. The closing line adds two compressed images,
including a momentary small lamp dispelling great darkness; the precise
reading of the other image remains provisional.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya introduces the gradual case, applies the ninefold grading to
every ground and its opposing Path qualities, derives nine grades from a
three-by-three subdivision, and pairs Path power inversely with
remaining affliction. The images of cleaning cloth and dispersing
darkness explain why the subtler residue calls for a stronger Path.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Doṣa` names an afflictive fault;
`guṇa` here names its opposing uninterrupted and liberation Paths.
`Bhūmau bhūmau` distributes the grading over each ground through
`bhavāgra`. `Mṛdu`, `madhya`, and `adhimātra` are primary and then
internal grades. The inverse order of Path and affliction is established
by the prose, not directly by the kārikā. Damaged source readings
remain visible.

**Organon determination.** A reflective Model may record ground, fault
grade, opposing Path grade, and order of removal separately. The next
Path operation is determined by the remaining grade, while the full
series preserves the inverse relation. This is project synthesis.

## 6. Grading and Inverse Opposition

| Path grade | Affliction grade removed |
|---|---|
| Mild-mild | Strong-strong |
| Mild-medium | Strong-medium |
| Mild-strong | Strong-mild |
| Medium-mild | Medium-strong |
| Medium-medium | Medium-medium |
| Medium-strong | Medium-mild |
| Strong-mild | Mild-strong |
| Strong-medium | Mild-medium |
| Strong-strong | Mild-mild |

The ordered pair of grades is qualitative. The cloth and light analogies
explain the developmental order, not an equality of Path and fault power.
