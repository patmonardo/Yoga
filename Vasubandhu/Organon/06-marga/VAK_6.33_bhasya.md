# VAK_6.33 Bhāṣya — Nine Grades of Fault and Quality

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> navaprakārā doṣā hi bhūmau bhūmau tathā guṇāḥ |
> mṛdumadhyādhimātrāṇāṃ punar mṛdvādibhedataḥ || 6.33 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> For in each ground the faults are of nine kinds, and likewise the qualities,
> because the mild, medium, and strong are again differentiated beginning with mild.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The sequential case must be stated. First this: as desire-domain afflictions were
> taught in nine grades, so faults are ninefold in each ground, as far as the summit
> of existence. Likewise the qualities: the opposing uninterrupted and liberation
> paths are ninefold in each ground. How? Mild, medium, strong are the three root
> grades; each again threefold — nine: mild-mild through strong-strong.
>
> Mild-mild Path abandons strong-strong affliction; so on until strong-strong Path
> abandons mild-mild. Strongest Path cannot be there at the start; once it has
> arisen, strongest affliction cannot remain. Coarse dirt leaves the cloth first,
> fine dirt later. Coarse dark yields to slight light; subtle dark needs a strong
> light. Bright dharmas are strong, dark ones weak: even a momentary mild Noble Path
> can uproot intense afflictions fed through beginningless saṃsāra.
