# VAK 6.62 — Redetermination of Fruit and Faculty-Grounded Personhood

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सविशेषं फलं त्यक्त्वा फलमाप्नोति वर्धयन् ।
द्वौ बुद्धौ श्रावकाः सप्त नवैते नवधेन्द्रियाः ॥ ६.६२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
saviśeṣaṃ phalaṃ tyaktvā phalam āpnoti vardhayan |
dvau buddhau śrāvakāḥ sapta navaite navadhendriyāḥ || 6.62 ||
```

The Bhāṣya prevents two conflations. Faculty-strengthening replaces the weak-faculty fruit-and-Path complex with its sharp-faculty counterpart; it is not merely an unchanged fruit receiving an added quality. Also, the seven Śrāvakas counted here are not the separate sevenfold enumeration of all noble persons introduced after this verse.

The Bhāṣya makes **viśeṣa** concrete as the weak-faculty Path qualifying the fruit and identifies what is newly acquired as the corresponding fruit-and-Path of the sharp-faculty disposition.

## 4. Grammar

The participle **vardhayan** supplies the subject of **āpnoti**: one strengthening the faculties attains a fruit. The absolutive **tyaktvā** marks the prior operation, while **saviśeṣaṃ phalam** is its object: the fruit together with its former specification is relinquished.

The second half contains an enumerative equation:

```text
two Buddhas
    + seven Śrāvakas
    = nine persons
```

**navadhā indriyāḥ** gives the principle of division: these persons are nine because the faculties are graded ninefold. The plural **indriyāḥ** functions metonymically in the compressed verse; the Bhāṣya explicitly speaks of nine *pudgalas* generated through differences of faculty.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> One strengthening [the faculties], having relinquished the fruit together with its specific determination, attains [the] fruit. There are two Buddhas and seven Śrāvakas; these nine are [distinguished] through the ninefold faculties.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> One who strengthens the faculties relinquishes the fruit together with its weak-faculty Path and attains the fruit with a sharp-faculty Path. By their nine faculty-grades, these persons are two Buddhas and seven disciples.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Faculty-development is a replacement of concrete Path-form, not an attribute added to an inert result. The fruit as qualified by one operative capacity is relinquished, and the corresponding fruit is newly actualized through another. Continuity lies at the level of principal fruit; transformation reaches the Path-and-fruit complex through which that fruit is possessed.

The resulting persons are not independent substances ranked from outside. They are determinate forms of one Path-field, distinguished here by nine grades of faculty. Change the principle of division, and the same field yields a different valid enumeration.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Yataḥ / anāgāmi-phala / ārūpya-bhūmi` — the six-ground reason**

The first half resolves the causal **yataḥ** at the end of 6.61. A Śaikṣa cannot strengthen the faculties on a formless ground because the Anāgāmin fruit is not included there:

```text
faculty-transition
    requires an existing fruit/Path complex
    that can be relinquished and replaced

Anāgāmin fruit
    not included in ārūpya ground

therefore
    no Śaikṣa faculty-transition
    from those formless grounds
```

The limitation is relational and doctrinal, not merely a claim that the trainee possesses less contemplative power.

**`Saviśeṣaṃ phalam` — fruit with its Path specification**

The Bhāṣya explains the specific determination as **phala-viśiṣṭa mṛdvindriya-mārga**, the fruit together with its weak-faculty Path. What is given up is therefore a concrete complex:

```text
weak-faculty fruit
    + weak-faculty Path
    → relinquished

corresponding sharp-faculty fruit
    + sharp-faculty Path
    → acquired
```

The practitioner does not necessarily advance from one of the four principal fruits to the next. The same fruit-level is reconstituted under another faculty-disposition.

**`Ṣaḍ arhantaḥ / sapta śrāvakāḥ` — the Arhat subdivision**

The five condition-dependent Arhat-types remain five. The *akopyadharman* is subdivided by genesis:

```text
five types beginning with parihāṇadharman
    5

akopyadharman reached through sharpening
    1

akopyadharman original from the beginning
    1
    —
seven Śrāvakas
```

This subdivision does not add a seventh liberation-mode. It differentiates the sixth mode according to whether its disposition is original or acquired.

**`Dvau buddhau` — two Buddha types**

The Bhāṣya identifies:

```text
Pratyekabuddha
    + Buddha / Samyaksaṃbuddha
    = two Buddhas
```

Adding these to the seven Śrāvakas yields nine persons.

**`Navadhā indriyāḥ` — nine faculty grades**

The Bhāṣya grounds the enumeration in the ninefold scale beginning with mild-mild:

```text
mild-mild
mild-medium
mild-strong

medium-mild
medium-medium
medium-strong

strong-mild
strong-medium
strong-strong
```

The structural claim is that **indriya-bheda**, differentiation of faculties, generates the nine-person classification. The passage need not be forced into a simplistic one-label-to-one-grade table beyond what the Bhāṣya states.

**`Sapta āryapudgalāḥ` — the next classification**

Immediately after explaining this verse, Vasubandhu introduces seven noble persons—from *śraddhānusārin* through *ubhayatovimukta*. That is a new classification by mode and stage of noble realization, not the seven Śrāvakas inside the present faculty-based ninefold scheme. Its analysis belongs to 6.63.

The lesson is methodological:

```text
six Arhats
    principle: mode/disposition of liberation

nine persons
    principle: grade of faculty

seven noble persons
    principle: mode/stage of noble realization
```

Numerical difference is not contradiction when the basis of division differs.

## 8. Logical Determination

Faculty-transition is determinate replacement:

```text
Possesses(x, WeakFacultyFruitPath(fw))
    + Strengthens(x, Faculty)
    → Relinquishes(x, fw)
    → Acquires(x, SharpFacultyFruitPath(fs))

CorrespondingFruitLevel(fw, fs)
    = same principal fruit
```

Thus:

```text
identity of principal fruit-level
    does not entail
identity of determinate fruit/Path complex
```

The ninefold classification is generated as:

```text
FiveConditionDependentSravakas
    + TwoImmovableSravakaSubtypes
    + Pratyekabuddha
    + Samyaksambuddha
    = NineFacultyBasedPersons
```

More generally:

```text
same Path-person
    consideredBy(LiberationMode) → sixfold scheme
    consideredBy(FacultyGrade)   → ninefold scheme
    consideredBy(RealizationMode)→ sevenfold scheme
```

Classification is an explicit relation between a field and a principle of division, not a flat inventory of person-substances.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The opening answers the `yataḥ` of 6.61. The Bhāṣya glosses **saviśeṣaṃ phalam** through the fruit and weak-faculty Path that are relinquished, and the sharp-faculty fruit and Path then acquired. The same principal fruit rank need not be exchanged for a higher one. The seven Śrāvakas here are Arhat subtypes within a faculty-based ninefold count; the different sevenfold classification of all noble persons starts after this unit.

VAK 6.62 shows the Path altering not merely what a person has, but the concrete relation in which person, Path, and fruit stand. When *indriya* changes, the former fruit is not simply left untouched behind a stronger subject. The fruit-as-qualified and the Path that possesses it are replaced together.

In the Organon reconstruction, this is a Copular transformation. The relata do not remain externally fixed while only their connector changes: a new faculty-morphology redetermines the person as possessor and the fruit as possessed within one Path-relation. Yet the principal fruit-level supplies continuity across the conversion.

The second half provides an equally important discipline for systematic Meaning. Six, nine, and seven are not competing counts until their principles of division are shown to be identical. The Kośa's person-types are appearances of Path under determinate relations—liberation-mode, faculty-grade, or realization-mode—not isolated essences.
