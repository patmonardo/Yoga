# VAK 6.52 — Principal Fruit as Integrated Path-Position

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

चतुष्फलव्यवस्था तु पञ्चकारणसम्भवात् ।
पूर्वत्यागोऽन्यमार्गाप्तिः क्षयसंकलनं फले ॥ ६.५२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

catuṣphalavyavasthā tu pañcakāraṇasaṃbhavāt |
pūrvatyāgo ’nyamārgāptiḥ kṣayasaṃkalanaṃ phale || 6.52 ||

The verse announces five reasons but states three. The remaining two appear in the opening of 6.53. They are registered here only insofar as the Bhāṣya requires them to explain the announced fivefold criterion; their proper textual analysis belongs to the next kārikā.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

catuḥ-phala-vyavasthā | tu | pañca-kāraṇa-saṃbhavāt |
pūrva-tyāgaḥ | anya-mārga-āptiḥ | kṣaya-saṃkalanam | phale

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **catuḥ-phala-vyavasthā** | feminine nominative singular compound | establishment or systematic determination of four principal fruits |
| **tu** | adversative particle | but; contrasts the fourfold organization with the numerous fruits of 6.51 |
| **pañca-kāraṇa-saṃbhavāt** | masculine ablative singular compound | because of the occurrence of five reasons |
| **pūrva-tyāgaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | relinquishment of the preceding [Path] |
| **anya-mārga-āptiḥ** | feminine nominative singular compound | attainment of another Path |
| **kṣaya-saṃkalanam** | neuter nominative singular compound | gathering together of cessations or abandonments |
| **phale** | neuter locative singular | in or at the fruit |

The Bhāṣya sharpens **pūrva** to the previous candidate-for-fruit Path, **anya-mārga** to a previously unattained Path, and **kṣaya-saṃkalana** to the integration of abandonments through one acquisition of the whole.

## 4. Grammar

The abstract compound **catuṣphalavyavasthā** is the subject. The ablative **pañcakāraṇasaṃbhavāt** supplies its ground: the fourfold establishment occurs because five conditions concur.

The second line begins an appositional enumeration of those conditions. **pūrvatyāgaḥ**, **anyamārgāptiḥ**, and **kṣayasaṃkalanam** are coordinated nominatives, while **phale** indicates their occurrence at each principal fruit-position.

The Bhāṣya construes the first two relationally:

```text
relinquishment of candidate-path
    ↔
attainment of fruit-path
```

The third is not another transition alongside them, but the integration of relevant abandonments within the newly attained fruit. The remaining two members of the announced fivefold list are syntactically continued in 6.53.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

But the establishment of four fruits is due to the occurrence of five reasons: relinquishment of the preceding [Path], attainment of another Path, and the gathering of cessations in the fruit.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Although the Path produces many individual fruits, only four principal fruit-positions are established. At each, the practitioner relinquishes the preceding candidate-for-fruit Path, acquires a previously unattained fruit-Path, and gathers the relevant abandonments into a single attainment. These are the first three of the five criteria; the next verse supplies acquisition of the eight knowledges and cultivation of the sixteen truth-aspects.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

A principal fruit is not one more isolated result added to an inventory. It is a qualitative reorganization of the practitioner's attained Path. The preceding Path-form is relinquished, a genuinely new Path-form becomes actual, and the plurality of accomplished abandonments is possessed together as one fruit-position.

What came before is therefore neither simply retained nor simply erased. It is relinquished as the present governing form but preserved as accomplished content within the integrated result.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Eighty-nine fruits and four principal fruits**

VAK 6.51 enumerated eighty-nine liberation-paths and their corresponding cessations. VAK 6.52 answers why the canonical organization nevertheless has only four fruits:

```text
eighty-nine
    analytical enumeration of determinate Path-fruits

four
    major reorganizations of attained Path-status
```

These counts answer different questions. A fruit in the broad technical sense need not constitute a new principal fruit-position.

**pūrva-tyāga**

The Bhāṣya expands this as **pūrva-mārga-tyāga**, relinquishment of the previous Path. More exactly, the Path belonging to the candidate-for-fruit (*pratipannaka*) is relinquished when the fruit-Path is acquired.

This is not loss of the abandonments already accomplished. What is relinquished is the previous Path's status as the practitioner's presently governing determination.

**anya-mārga-āpti**

The Bhāṣya glosses “another Path” as **apūrva-mārga**, a Path not previously attained. A principal fruit therefore requires more than continuation within an unchanged series:

```text
previous candidate-status
    → relinquished

previously unattained fruit-status
    → acquired
```

**kṣaya-saṃkalana**

The Bhāṣya gives **prahāṇa-saṃkalana**, integration of abandonments, and explains it through **eka-prāpti**, one attainment of the whole. Multiple accomplished cessations are not left as externally juxtaposed items:

```text
many relevant abandonments
    → gathered through one acquisition
    → one principal fruit-position
```

The fruit does not create those cessations anew. It unifies their possession under a newly attained Path-status.

**The complete fivefold criterion**

The Bhāṣya necessarily reads 6.52 with the opening of 6.53. The five conditions occurring at each principal fruit are:

```text
1. relinquishment of the previous Path
2. attainment of a previously unattained Path
3. integration of abandonments in one attainment
4. acquisition of the eight knowledges
5. cultivation of the sixteen truth-aspects
```

Only the first three are kārikā-text in 6.52; the final two will be analyzed with 6.53.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let an individual Path-fruit be distinguished from a principal fruit-position:

```text
IndividualFruit(x)
    → one determinate liberation-path / cessation pair

PrincipalFruit(y)
    → satisfies(y, FivefoldCriterion)
```

The three conditions stated here form a genetic structure:

```text
PreviousCandidatePath(p)
    → relinquishedAsCurrentForm(p)

NewFruitPath(q)
    → previouslyUnattained(q)
    → acquired(q)

RelevantAbandonments(a1 ... an)
    → gatheredBy(EkaPrapti)
    → possessedTogetherIn(q)
```

Accordingly:

```text
principal fruit
    ≠ numerical addition of another result

principal fruit
    = transformed Path-position
      preserving prior accomplishments
      under a new integrated attainment
```

The preceding form is negated as present status, its achieved content is retained, and the new form organizes that content as a whole.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.52 shows why the Path cannot be represented adequately as a flat list of moments. Its many determinate results are organized into qualitative thresholds. At such a threshold, the prior course ceases to be the current form, a new course becomes actual, and the accomplished cessations are recollected into one possession.

In the Organon reconstruction, this is the Path's genuine self-articulation into moments. The new fruit is not externally imposed upon the earlier movement; it is the whole that results when that movement relinquishes its candidate-form and gathers its determinate achievements under a new rule. The End reorganizes the meaning of the path already traversed.

This is project synthesis. The source-layer claim is exact and narrower: the four principal fruits are selected from the many fruits by five jointly occurring criteria, of which this verse states Path-relinquishment, new Path-attainment, and integration of abandonments through one acquisition.
