# VAK_6.50 — Completion, Irreversibility, and Aśaikṣa Cognition

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
यद्यकोप्यः क्षयज्ञानादनुत्पादमतिः न चेत् ।
क्षयज्ञानमशैक्षी वा दृष्टिः सर्वस्य सार्हतः ॥ ६.५० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
yady akopyaḥ kṣayajñānād anutpādamatiḥ na cet |
kṣayajñānam aśaikṣī vā dṛṣṭiḥ sarvasya sā 'rhataḥ || 6.50 ||
```

The running verse gives `anutpādamatiḥ`; its prose specifies
`anutpādajñāna`, Knowing of non-arising. The study retains the verse
form and uses the prose to identify the cognition. The closing
`sā 'rhataḥ` is resolved by the prose as right view beyond training
for every arhat.


The Bhāṣya uses the fuller designation **akopyadharman**, “one whose nature is immovable,” and identifies the opposing condition as **parihāṇi-saṃbhava**, the possibility of falling away.

## 4. Grammar

The conditional **yadi akopyaḥ** governs the first sequence. The ablative **kṣayajñānāt** has a temporal-successive force: immediately after Knowing of exhaustion, Knowing of non-arising arises for an immovable Arhat. The verb “arises” is supplied by the Bhāṣya.

**na cet** introduces the contrary case. If the Arhat is not immovable, the ellipsed succession from **kṣayajñānāt** instead yields either **kṣayajñānam** again or **aśaikṣī dṛṣṭiḥ**. The final **sā** resumes the feminine **dṛṣṭiḥ**, while **sarvasya arhataḥ** gives its universal possession: this right view belongs to every Arhat, not only to the non-immovable type.

The syntax therefore combines a conditional distinction with a universal correction:

```text
if immovable
    → anutpādajñāna follows

if not immovable
    → kṣayajñāna or aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi follows

in every Arhat
    → aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi occurs
```

**Future Feature Grammar sketch (not executable):**

```text
ARHAT[TYPE=immovable, AFTER=Knowing-of-exhaustion]
  → NEXT[Knowing-of-non-arising]
ARHAT[TYPE=capable-of-falling-away, AFTER=Knowing-of-exhaustion]
  → NEXT[Knowing-of-exhaustion-or-right-view]
ARHAT[TYPE=any]
  → CAPACITY[right-view-beyond-training]
```

The final relation is universal; it does not assign right view as the
immediate successor in every case.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> If the arhat is immovable, Knowing of non-arising follows Knowing
> of exhaustion. If not, Knowing of exhaustion or right view beyond
> training follows. That right view belongs to every arhat.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> For an immovable arhat, Knowing of non-arising follows the first
> Knowing of exhaustion immediately. For an arhat still capable of
> falling away, another Knowing of exhaustion or right view beyond
> training follows instead; Knowing of non-arising does not arise.
> Right view beyond training belongs to every arhat, including the
> immovable one, whose later cognition may also be that right view.

### Translation note

The verse uses `anutpādamatiḥ`, while the Bhāṣya explains the successor
as `anutpādajñāna`. `Parihāṇi-saṃbhava`, the possibility of falling
away, blocks this Knowing for the non-immovable arhat. The phrase
`sarvasya sā 'rhataḥ` gives right view beyond training a universal scope; it does
not say that right view immediately follows in every case.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Completion and irreversibility are not the same determination. Knowing of exhaustion knows that the afflictive task has been completed. Knowing of non-arising knows, in addition, that what has been exhausted cannot arise again. The latter cognition is possible only where the liberated continuum itself has become immovable.

This Knowing does not manufacture stability by subjective assurance. It expresses the Path-status already achieved. Where falling away remains possible, exhaustion may be known as accomplished, but its permanent non-recurrence cannot yet be known.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kṣayajñānānantara` — what follows Knowing of exhaustion**

After the analysis of mundane and supramundane paths in 6.47–6.49, the Bhāṣya returns to the sequence opened in 6.45:

```text
vajropama-samādhi
    final uninterrupted path
        → first kṣayajñāna
          final liberation-path
        → what follows?
```

VAK 6.50 differentiates the subsequent cognition according to the kind of Arhat produced.

**`Kṣayajñāna` — Knowing of exhaustion**

Knowing of exhaustion cognizes the completed exhaustion of the relevant influxes and defilements. Its first occurrence is the liberation Path immediately following the final diamond-like uninterrupted Path. A subsequent *kṣayajñāna* may arise again for a non-immovable arhat.

**`Anutpādajñāna` — Knowing of non-arising**

Knowing of non-arising cognizes that the exhausted afflictions will not arise again:

```text
exhaustion accomplished
    + irreversibility objectively established
    → anutpādajñāna
```

It is not merely expectation, resolve, or confidence. The Bhāṣya excludes it when falling away remains possible.

**`Akopyadharman / parihāṇi` — immovability and possible falling away**

An **akopyadharman Arhat** is immovable: falling away cannot disturb the achieved liberation. A non-immovable Arhat is nevertheless genuinely an Arhat, but **parihāṇi-saṃbhava**, the possibility of falling away, prevents Knowing of non-arising.

Thus:

```text
aśaikṣa
    completion of liberating training

akopya
    impossibility of falling away

aśaikṣa ≠ necessarily akopya
```

**`Aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi` — right view beyond training**

Right view beyond training belongs to every Arhat. The verse's alternatives do not confine it to the movable type. The Bhāṣya further explains that, for an immovable Arhat, either another Knowing of non-arising or this right view may follow a Knowing of non-arising.

## 8. Logical Determination

The principal distinction is modal rather than merely chronological:

```text
Kṣayajñāna
    → knows ExhaustionAsAccomplished

Anutpādajñāna
    → knows ExhaustionAsIrreversible
```

Eligibility for the second cognition is constrained by the actual Path-continuum:

```text
Possible(FallingAway)
    → not Established(NonArising)
    → excludes(Anutpādajñāna)

Immovable(LiberatedContinuum)
    → Established(NonArising)
    → enables(Anutpādajñāna)
```

The distribution is therefore:

```text
FirstKṣayajñāna
    + ImmovableArhat
        → Anutpādajñāna

FirstKṣayajñāna
    + NonImmovableArhat
        → KṣayajñānaAgain
          or AśaikṣaRightView

EveryArhat
    → possesses AśaikṣaRightView
```

This Knowing expresses the stability of the attained Path-status; it
does not make a result irreversible merely by reflecting upon it.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse prevents “the End” from becoming an undifferentiated terminal state. The Path's result has internal articulation: the affliction is exhausted; this exhaustion is known; and, in the immovable case, it is known as incapable of reversal. Completion and the necessity of completion are distinct moments.

In Organon terms, the result becomes self-related only to the degree that its own objective constitution warrants. *Anutpādajñāna* is not a second action imposed upon liberation from outside, nor a subjective promise about the future. It is the cognition in which an actually irreversible result is present as irreversible.

This is project synthesis. Textually, the stricter point is that cognition follows Path-status: **parihāṇi-saṃbhava** blocks Knowing of non-arising, while immovability makes that Knowing possible. The verse thereby preserves the distinction between attained fruit and the determinate cognition appropriate to that fruit.
