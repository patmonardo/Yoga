# VAK 6.64 — Two Liberations and the Complete Trainee

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

निरोधलाभ्युभयतोविमुक्तः प्रज्ञयेतरः ।
समापत्तीन्द्रियफलैः पूर्णः शैक्षोऽभिधीयते ॥ ६.६४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

nirodhalābhy ubhayatovimuktaḥ prajñayetaraḥ |
samāpattīndriyaphalaiḥ pūrṇaḥ śaikṣo ’bhidhīyate || 6.64 ||

The first half defines the final two persons named in 6.63. The second half turns from the completed Arhat to the ways in which a trainee may properly be called complete.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

nirodha-lābhī | ubhayataḥ-vimuktaḥ | prajñayā | itaraḥ |
samāpatti-indriya-phalaiḥ | pūrṇaḥ | śaikṣaḥ | abhidhīyate

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **nirodha-lābhī** | masculine nominative singular possessive compound | one possessing the attainment of cessation |
| **ubhayataḥ-vimuktaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | liberated in both respects |
| **prajñayā** | feminine instrumental singular | through discriminative understanding |
| **itaraḥ** | masculine nominative singular | the other, namely the Arhat without cessation-attainment |
| **samāpatti** | feminine compound member | contemplative attainment |
| **indriya** | neuter compound member | spiritual faculty; here especially its sharpness |
| **phalaiḥ** | neuter instrumental plural | by or with respect to fruits |
| **pūrṇaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | complete under the relevant determination |
| **śaikṣaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | a trainee, one who still has training to perform |
| **abhidhīyate** | third-person singular present passive of *abhi-√dhā* | is designated, is called |

The Bhāṣya expands the compact instrumentals into axes of classification. It does not treat them as three possessions that every complete trainee must jointly have.

## 4. Grammar

In the first sentence, **nirodhalābhī** is the subject and **ubhayatovimuktaḥ** its predicate. The elliptical second clause supplies **vimuktaḥ** from context: “the other [is liberated] through prajñā.”

The compound **samāpattīndriyaphalaiḥ** is instrumental and gives the respects according to which **pūrṇaḥ śaikṣaḥ** is designated. The plural does not require conjunctive totality. The Bhāṣya explicitly distributes completeness across several possible configurations.

The syntax therefore compresses two classifications:

```text
Arhat
    classified by mode of liberation

trainee
    classified by respect of completeness
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

One who has attained cessation is liberated in both respects; the other is liberated through prajñā. A trainee is designated complete with respect to contemplative attainments, faculties, and fruits.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The Arhat who possesses the attainment of cessation is liberated in both respects—through the powers of prajñā and samādhi—whereas the other Arhat is liberated through prajñā. A trainee may be called complete under three determinations: fruit, faculty, and contemplative attainment, singly or in the combinations allowed by their dependencies; this need not mean completeness in all three at once.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Liberation from the afflictions and mastery of the contemplative liberations are distinct accomplishments. Prajñā supplies the decisive liberation from afflictive obstruction; when the attainment of cessation is also possessed, samādhi supplies liberation from the obstruction to the meditative releases. Their conjunction constitutes the doubly liberated Arhat without diminishing the genuine liberation of the prajñā-liberated Arhat.

A trainee’s completeness is likewise determinate rather than abstract. One may have fully realized the fruit-dimension, the faculty-dimension, or an admissible coordination of fruit, faculty, and attainment while training as such remains unfinished.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The doubly liberated Arhat**

The Bhāṣya defines the designation directly:

```text
attainment of nirodhasamāpatti
    → ubhayatobhāgavimukta
```

The two respects are not two rival salvations. They are two powers and two corresponding freedoms:

```text
prajñā-bala
    → freedom from kleśāvaraṇa

samādhi-bala
    → freedom from vimokṣāvaraṇa
```

**nirodhasamāpatti** is the attainment of cessation, specifically the cessation of perception and feeling. Its possession supplies the additional contemplative determination.

**The prajñā-liberated Arhat**

The **itara**, “the other,” is **prajñāvimukta**:

```text
prajñā alone
    → afflictive obstruction removed
```

This Arhat is not partially liberated from the afflictions. What is absent is the further samādhi-mastery associated with cessation-attainment. The distinction is therefore:

```text
essential liberation from kleśa

versus

that liberation together with
the complete meditative releases
```

**Three respects of trainee completeness**

Vasubandhu states:

```text
phala
    attained noble result

indriya
    maturity or sharpness of faculty

samāpatti
    contemplative attainment
```

These are irreducible but coordinated dimensions of one Path-person.

**Allowed configurations**

The Bhāṣya gives five forms:

```text
phala
indriya
phala + indriya
phala + samāpatti
phala + indriya + samāpatti
```

Examples include:

- a faith-resolved non-returner who is not a body-witness: complete by fruit;
- a seeing-attained person who is not yet dispassionate: complete by faculty;
- a seeing-attained non-returner: complete by fruit and faculty;
- a faith-resolved body-witness: complete by fruit and attainment;
- a seeing-attained body-witness: complete in all three respects.

The transmitted prose around the compound descriptions is highly compressed. The secure doctrinal result is the pattern of dimensions and dependencies, rather than an expansion of every compound beyond what the Bhāṣya licenses.

**Excluded configurations**

The Bhāṣya explicitly excludes:

```text
samāpatti alone

indriya + samāpatti
without phala
```

Thus samāpatti is not an autonomous measure of noble completion. Where attainment participates in a trainee’s completeness, fruit is presupposed.

**Relative completeness**

```text
paripūrṇa śaikṣa
    complete under a trainee determination

not

aśaikṣa
    one for whom training itself is complete
```

The predicate *pūrṇa* must therefore be read with its specified relation. Completion of a dimension does not erase the distinction between training and beyond-training.

## 8. Logical Determination

The two Arhat types exhibit a non-exclusive hierarchy:

```text
PrajñāLiberation
    is sufficient for
LiberationFromAffliction

PrajñāLiberation
    + CessationAttainment
    constitutes
DoubleLiberation
```

Hence:

```text
AbsenceOfCessationAttainment
    does not entail
IncompleteAfflictiveLiberation
```

The trainee classification is multidimensional:

```text
CompleteIn(x, Determination)
    does not entail
CompleteIn(x, EveryDetermination)
```

But its dimensions are not freely combinable:

```text
CompleteInSamāpatti(x)
    → CompleteInPhala(x)
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives more than a list. It gives the modal grammar of Path-completeness: independent determinations joined by definite conditions of compatibility.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse sharpens the user’s determination of Indriya as a proto-Path morphology. A Path-person does not move along only one scale. Faculty, attainment, fruit, and liberation establish different rules under which the same movement can be complete.

The result is a morphology rather than a ladder:

```text
indriya
    capacity under which movement operates

samāpatti
    contemplative field actualized by movement

phala
    End attained within training

vimukti
    release established by the completed movement
```

Most important is the asymmetry between samāpatti and prajñā. Cessation-attainment is a profound contemplative End, but it is not by itself the liberating principle. Prajñā removes the afflictive obstruction. Samādhi adds mastery of the meditative releases.

In the Organon reconstruction, this prevents the Path from being reduced either to cognition or to contemplative state. Its concrete actuality is their differentiated coordination. This is a project-level synthesis; the source claim remains the Bhāṣya’s exact distinction between the two powers, two obstructions, and three respects of trainee completeness.
