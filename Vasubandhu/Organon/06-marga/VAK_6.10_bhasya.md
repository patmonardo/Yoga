# VAK_6.10 Bhāṣya — Expansion, Contraction, and Mastery

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> āsamudrāsthi-vistāra-saṃkṣepād ādikarmikaḥ |
>
> pādāsthna ā kapālārdha-tyāgāt kṛtajayaḥ smṛtaḥ || 6.10 ||

> A beginner expands the contemplated skeleton across the world as far as the
> ocean, then contracts it back to the skeleton of their own body. A
> practitioner who has gained mastery progressively relinquishes the bones
> from the feet upward, until only half the skull remains in attention.

The Bhāṣya describes three stages of unattractive contemplation and gives the
operations defining the first two here.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The practitioner cultivating the unattractive is said to be of three kinds: a
beginner, one who has gained familiarity, and one whose attention has passed
beyond. The verse first designates a beginner through expansion and
contraction of the skeletal representation as far as the ocean.

One wishing to cultivate the unattractive initially binds consciousness
(`citta`) to a part of their own body: the big toe, forehead, or wherever the
mind rests readily. At that place, the practitioner imaginatively clears away
flesh and other coverings in stages and sees a complete skeleton. They then
resolve on a second skeleton and progressively extend the representation
through monastery, park, and field until the earth as far as the ocean is
filled with skeletons. The stated purpose is to increase determinate
resolution (`adhimokṣa`). Next they contract the field until only their own
skeleton remains, for the purpose of gathering consciousness. After some time,
it is said, cultivation of the unattractive becomes established in this way.
This is the beginner practitioner.

The verse then designates the practitioner who has gained mastery through
progressive relinquishment from the bones of the feet up to half the skull.
For a finer gathering of consciousness, they leave the foot bones out of the
skeletal representation and attend to what remains. They continue in order
until, having set aside half the skull, they attend to the other half. The
transmitted final designation is textually uneven, but the described operation
is clear.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary first names three practitioner stages, then explains the first
two. The beginner establishes a complete skeleton locally, extends it as far
as the ocean to increase resolution, and contracts it to their own body to
gather consciousness. The practitioner who has gained mastery further
contracts the representation by removing its parts in order, while keeping
attention on the remainder. The third stage is named here but its defining
operation begins in 6.11.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The first ablative compound gives the beginner's
expansion and contraction; the second gives the mastered practitioner's ordered
relinquishment. The source's `pādāsthna` is difficult, but the prose explicitly
begins with foot bones. The separate kārikā's `kapālārdha` agrees with the
prose's half-skull remainder; the running `kapālārtha` is left untouched in the
witness. The anchor follows these locally explicit operations without
importing the third stage.

**Organon determination.** The same constructed cognitive field is extended,
gathered, and partly released. These are distinct operations with distinct
functions: increasing resolution, gathering consciousness, and demonstrating
command of the counter-representation. A future Feature Grammar can carry
stage, scope, operation, and remainder as features; a LogicFrame could express
their ordered dependencies. This formalization belongs to the project layer.

## 6. Operations and Functions

| Stage | Operation | Stated function |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Extend the skeleton to the ocean | Increase `adhimokṣa` |
| Beginner | Contract to one's own skeleton | Gather consciousness (`citta`) |
| Gained mastery | Set aside bones from the feet until half the skull remains | Finer gathering of consciousness (`citta`) |

The earlier limit remains: mastery over this contemplative representation does
not by itself establish definitive abandonment of an affliction.
