# VAK 6.24 — Liberation-Conducive Roots and the Threefold Maturation

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आबोधेः सर्वमेकत्र ध्यानान्त्ये शास्तृखड्गयोः ।
प्राक्तेभ्यो मोक्षभागीयं क्षिप्रं मोक्षस्त्रिभिर्भवैः ॥ ६.२४ ॥
```

The Bhāṣya expands **ekatra** as **ekatra eva āsane**, “in one and the same seat.” It also identifies **khaḍga** as the rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha and **dhyānāntya** as the fourth principal dhyāna.

## 4. Grammar

The opening construction is elliptical. **śāstṛkhaḍgayoḥ** is a genitive of reference: “in the case of the Teacher and the Rhinoceros-like [Pratyekabuddha].” **sarvam** denotes the whole sequence beginning with the factors conducive to penetration, **ā bodheḥ** gives its terminus, and **ekatra dhyānāntye** specifies one uninterrupted sitting on the final dhyāna.

The second line contains two nominal statements. **prāk tebhyaḥ mokṣabhāgīyam [utpadyate]** means “prior to those, what is conducive to liberation [arises].” The ablative plural **tebhyaḥ** refers to the four *nirvedhabhāgīyas*.

**kṣipraṃ mokṣas tribhir bhavaiḥ** states the shortest ordinary sequence: “quickly, liberation [occurs] through three existences.” **kṣipram** qualifies the manner or limiting case, while **tribhir bhavaiḥ** supplies the temporal-causal span.

The grammar therefore juxtaposes an exceptional one-sitting completion with the fastest ordinary maturation across three lives.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For the Teacher and the rhinoceros-like solitary Buddha, the whole course
> up to Awakening takes place in one sitting on the final dhyāna. Before
> these roots comes one conducive to liberation; at the quickest, liberation
> takes three existences.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> For the Teacher and the rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha, the whole course up to
> Awakening unfolds in one sitting on the final dhyāna. Before these [roots],
> a root conducive to liberation [must arise]; in the quickest course,
> liberation occurs in three existences.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

For the two exceptional forms of awakening, the entire decisive Path becomes continuous within one stable and penetrating concentration. Yet even this concentrated ascent belongs to a causal architecture: prior orientation toward liberation, maturation into preparation for penetration, and culmination in supramundane release.

The ordinary three-life sequence makes this architecture visible. Liberation is not an isolated event added to an unchanged continuum. A liberative direction must first be established, then ripened as cultivated truth-capacity, and finally brought to fruit in the Noble Path.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Śāstṛ` / `khaḍga` — Teacher and rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha**

The Teacher is the fully awakened Buddha; *khaḍga* is the rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha. Both complete the sequence from the penetration-conducive roots to Awakening in one sitting.

**`Ekatrāsana` — one seat**

The uninterrupted sequence runs from the nirvedhabhāgīyas through the Paths of Seeing and Cultivation to Awakening. Others begin it already from contemplation of the impure.

**`Dhyānāntya` — the fourth dhyāna**

The supporting ground, for its unmoving and sharp samādhi (*aniñjya-paṭu-samādhi*).

**`Mokṣabhāgīya` — conducive to liberation**

A wholesome root that precedes the four penetration-conducive roots. It is born of hearing and reflection; the penetration-conducive roots are born of cultivation.

**`Mokṣābhilāṣa` — liberation-conducive action**

Bodily, verbal and mental action may all be liberation-conducive, mental action principally. Even a single gift or training rule qualifies when governed by the resolve for liberation.

**`Avatāra` / `paripāka` / `vimukti` — entry, maturation, liberation**

The three-life sequence: entry into the liberative Dharma-process, maturation of the continuum, liberation as fruit.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse contrasts two temporal forms of one causal architecture:

```text
Exceptional course:
    penetration-conducive roots → Awakening
    one sitting
    fourth-dhyāna support

Fastest ordinary course:
    life 1 → liberation-conducive root
    life 2 → penetration-conducive roots
    life 3 → Noble Path and liberation
```

The Bhāṣya expresses the ordinary sequence through an organic analogy:

```text
sowing the seed → growth of the crop → production of fruit
entry            → maturation         → liberation
```

The two preparatory classes must remain distinct:

```text
liberative orientation ≠ preparation for penetration
preparation for penetration ≠ supramundane penetration
```

Yet each earlier moment is determined by the later function it prepares.

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, this is a **genetic copula** of Practical Meaning: aspiration joins received and reflected Truth to a liberative telos; cultivation transforms that orientation into operative capacity; the Noble Path brings the matured determination to its proper efficacy.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya supplies the identity of the two
practitioners, the one-seat and fourth-dhyāna reading, the three-life
distribution, and the mode of the earlier root. It also reports an alternative
beginning the one-seat course with contemplation of the impure and adds a
qualification concerning other kinds of Pratyekabuddha; both remain in the
continuous translation.


VAK 6.24 closes the preparatory architecture before the exposition turns to actual realization. Its deepest addition is that Heat is not the absolute beginning. Before technical preparation for penetration, the continuum must already have acquired a direction toward liberation.

This direction is not merely an inward wish detached from action. The Bhāṣya treats aspiration as capable of appropriating bodily, verbal, and mental acts into a liberation-conducive causal series. Meaning here includes telos: what an act is within the Path depends on the end under which it is performed.

The three-life sequence then shows why Paramārtha Meaning is Path rather than possession. Truth is received and reflected, becomes the orientation of conduct, matures through cultivation, and culminates in liberating penetration. The stages are linked by transformation, not by the accumulation of propositions.

The kārikā states the one-sitting course of the two exceptional practitioners, fourth-dhyāna support, the priority of *mokṣabhāgīya*, and the rapid three-life course. Unmoving sharp samādhi, the alternative beginning from impurity contemplation, the discriminative comprehension classification of *mokṣabhāgīya*, aspiration-governed action, the organic analogy, and the entry–maturation–liberation triad are Bhāṣya determinations. “Genetic copula” is the Organon's reconstruction.
