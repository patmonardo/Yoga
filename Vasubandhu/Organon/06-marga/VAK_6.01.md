# VAK_6.01

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
क्लेशप्रहाणमाख्यातं सत्यदर्शनभावनात् ।
द्विविधो भावनामार्गो दर्शनाख्यस्त्वनास्रवः ॥ ६.१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kleśa-prahāṇam ākhyātaṃ satya-darśana-bhāvanāt |
dvividho bhāvanā-mārgo darśanākhyas tv anāsravaḥ || 6.1 ||
```

VAK 6.01 opens Mārganirdeśa by gathering the result of Chapter 5: afflictions
are abandoned through seeing the truths and through cultivation. It now asks
whether the Paths performing these two modes of abandonment are contaminated
or uncontaminated.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kleśa-prahāṇam | ākhyātam | satya-darśana-bhāvanāt |
dvi-vidhaḥ | bhāvanā-mārgaḥ | darśana-ākhyaḥ | tu | anāsravaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `kleśa-prahāṇam` | nominative singular neuter compound | abandonment of the afflictions |
| `ākhyātam` | nominative singular neuter past passive participle of `ā-√khyā` | has been declared or explained |
| `satya-darśana-bhāvanāt` | ablative singular compound | through seeing the truths and through cultivation |
| `dvividhaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | twofold |
| `bhāvanā-mārgaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | Path of Cultivation |
| `darśana-ākhyaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | the Path called Seeing |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but, whereas |
| `anāsravaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | without influx, uncontaminated |

The Bhāṣya resolves the two forms of cultivation as `laukika` and
`lokottara`, mundane and supramundane. The contrastive `tu` excludes the same
twofold division from the Path of Seeing.

## 4. Grammar

The opening statement is retrospective:

```text
kleśa-prahāṇam
    ākhyātam
    satya-darśana-bhāvanāt
```

The ablative compound gives two means of abandonment corresponding to the
seeing-abandonable and cultivation-abandonable classes analyzed in Chapter 5.

The second half contains two asymmetrical predicates:

```text
bhāvanā-mārgaḥ
    dvividhaḥ

darśana-ākhyaḥ [mārgaḥ] tu
    anāsravaḥ
```

The Bhāṣya expands:

```text
bhāvanā-mārga   = laukika or lokottara
darśana-mārga   = lokottara only
```

`Anāsrava` in the kārikā and `lokottara` in the prose converge in this
classification, but their lexical senses should not be treated as universally
interchangeable outside the present argument.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The abandonment of the afflictions has been explained as occurring through
> seeing the truths and through cultivation. The Path of Cultivation is
> twofold, whereas the Path called Seeing is uncontaminated.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Afflictions have been explained as abandoned either through seeing the
> truths or through cultivation. The Path of Cultivation is of two kinds,
> mundane and supramundane. The Path of Seeing, however, is exclusively
> supramundane and uncontaminated, because it functions as counteragent to all
> three domains and abandons at once the nine kinds of affliction abandoned
> through seeing—an efficacy no mundane Path possesses.

The two grounds of seeing's status—three-domain counteraction and its single
decisive abandonment—are supplied explicitly by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Having determined the field of bondage, the inquiry turns to the power
> adequate to remove it. Cultivation is formally twofold: it can remain within
> mundane efficacy or operate supramundanely. Seeing is not similarly divided,
> because its function is opposition to the entire three-domain afflictive
> field and its power is the decisive abandonment unavailable to a mundane
> Path. A Path is thus determined here not by the extraordinariness of an
> experience but by the scope and efficacy of its counteraction.

The chapter begins from an operational question: which cognition has the
power to abandon which afflictive structure?

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kleśa-prahāṇa` — abandonment of affliction**

The accomplished removal analyzed in Chapter 5 through seeing and cultivation.
Chapter 6 now determines the Paths capable of those operations.

**`Satya-darśana` — seeing the truths**

Direct discriminative seeing of what had not previously been seen. Its Path
does not merely represent truth but functions against the afflictions
abandoned through that seeing.

**`Bhāvanā` — cultivation**

Development or repeated actualization. The Bhāṣya prevents its automatic
identification with uncontaminated practice by dividing its Path into mundane
and supramundane forms.

**`Bhāvanā-mārga` — Path of Cultivation**

```text
laukika-bhāvanā-mārga
lokottara-bhāvanā-mārga
```

Its twofoldness concerns the status and efficacy of cultivation, not two
unrelated meanings of the word.

**`Darśana-mārga` — Path of Seeing**

Necessarily supramundane in this account. It counters the three-domain field
and performs the once-for-all abandonment of the nine seeing-abandonable
types.

**`Anāsrava` — without influx, uncontaminated**

The kārikā's predicate of the Path of Seeing. It indicates that the Path is
not governed by the influx-structure it opposes.

**`Laukika` — mundane**

A possible status of the cultivation Path. Its counteractive capacity is real
but does not include the distinctive operation assigned to seeing.

**`Lokottara` — supramundane**

Here functionally grounded rather than spatially imagined: the Path goes
beyond the world-order insofar as it counters the afflictive structure of all
three domains.

**`Traidhātuka-pratipakṣatva` — being counteragent to the three domains**

The first Bhāṣya reason that seeing must be supramundane. `Pratipakṣa` names
effective opposition, not simple difference from the world.

**`Sakṛt-prahāṇa` and `śakti` — abandonment at once and effective capacity**

The Path of Seeing abandons the nine seeing-abandonable kinds in one decisive
operation. The mundane Path lacks this `śakti`, power or capacity.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Path taxonomy is asymmetrical:

```text
BhāvanāMārga
    = {Laukika, Lokottara}

DarśanaMārga
    ⊆ Anāsrava
    AND DarśanaMārga ⊆ Lokottara
```

The functional grounds are:

```text
CounteragentTo(DarśanaMārga, AllThreeDomains)
AND AbandonsAtOnce(DarśanaMārga, NineSeeingAbandonableKinds)
    → NecessarilyLokottara(DarśanaMārga)
```

The negative capacity rule is:

```text
LaukikaMārga(x)
    → LacksCapacity(
          x,
          OnceForAllAbandonmentOfNineSeeingAbandonables
      )
```

The transition from Chapter 5 to Chapter 6 is:

```text
Classifies(Chapter5, ObjectsAndModesOfAbandonment)
    → RequiresInquiryInto(PathCapacity)
```

Thus:

```text
PathStatus
    is grounded by
CounteractiveRange + AbandonmentEfficacy
```

and not merely by phenomenological intensity or a spatial notion of being
outside the world.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Retrospective opening.** The first pāda does not repeat Chapter 5
redundantly. It converts the earlier classification of afflictions into the
new question of which Paths possess the power adequate to abandon them.

**Darśana and bhāvanā are not parallel species.** Cultivation crosses the
mundane/supramundane distinction; seeing does not. Their relation must
therefore be represented with this asymmetry intact.

**Supramundane means effective opposition here.** The Bhāṣya's primary ground
is `traidhātuka-pratipakṣatva`. Seeing is not made supramundane by an
unexplained elevation of consciousness but by its counteractive reach.

**Power distinguishes the Path.** `Na hi laukikasya eṣā śaktir asti`: the
mundane Path lacks the power to abandon the nine seeing-abandonable types at
once. The distinction is operational rather than honorific.

**Organon interpretation.** A later Dataset should model Path-status through
typed efficacy assertions:

```text
PathCapacityAssertion
    concernsPath
    hasMundaneStatus
    hasCounteractiveRange
    hasAbandonmentOperation
    hasEffectiveCapacity
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This project-level representation follows the Bhāṣya's functional grounds;
it does not attribute Organon architecture to Vasubandhu.
