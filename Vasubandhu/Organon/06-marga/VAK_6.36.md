# VAK 6.36 — The Final Interval and Crossing Beyond the Desire Realm

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

क्षीणसप्ताष्टदोषांश एकजन्मैकवीचिकः ।
तृतीयप्रतिपन्नश्च सोऽनागामि नवक्षयात् ॥ ६.३६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

kṣīṇa-saptāṣṭa-doṣāṃśa ekajanmaikavīcikaḥ |
tṛtīya-pratipannaś ca so 'nāgāmi nava-kṣayāt || 6.36 ||

The opening compounds jointly characterize the practitioner after seven or eight abandonments. The final ablative gives the causal threshold for non-returning.

## 4. Grammar

**kṣīṇa-saptāṣṭa-doṣāṃśaḥ** is a possessive compound: the practitioner is characterized by the exhaustion of seven or eight fault-portions. **eka-janma** and **eka-vīcikaḥ** stand in apposition, followed by the coordinated Path-status **tṛtīya-pratipannaḥ**.

The pronoun **saḥ** preserves continuity: it is that same practitioner who becomes **anāgāmī**. The ablative **nava-kṣayāt** supplies the ground of the new predicate—non-returning follows from destruction of the ninth grade.

The kārikā gives the grade-count, intermediate designations, candidate-status, and fruit-threshold. The two explanations of *vīci*, acquisition of the counter-faculty, domain-crossing, and the qualification concerning prior abandonment belong to the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

One in whom seven or eight portions of the faults have been exhausted is one-birth, one-interval, and proceeding toward the third [fruit]. From destruction of the ninth, that one is a non-returner.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

After abandoning seven or eight grades of the desire-realm cultivation-removable afflictions, the once-returner becomes a one-interval practitioner and candidate for the third fruit, with only one birth remaining. When the ninth and final grade is destroyed, that same practitioner attains non-returning and will not return to the desire realm.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The movement beyond once-returning begins when the superior Path becomes actual and removes the seventh or eighth grade. The practitioner then stands at one interval from the third fruit: almost the whole numerical series has been consumed, yet the final residue still functions as the boundary of an entire domain.

Destruction of the ninth grade is therefore more than the last addition to a sum. It removes the condition that still makes return to the desire realm possible. The Path completes its work within that domain by passing beyond the domain itself.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**ekavīcika**

The Bhāṣya glosses **vīci** as an interval or gap and preserves two related explanations:

```text
existential interval:
    one birth separates the practitioner from Nirvāṇa

Path-functional interval:
    one affliction-grade separates the practitioner from non-returning
```

Both express extreme proximity, but they should not be flattened into one identical count. The term relates remaining existence to remaining Path-work.

**Three determining conditions**

The Bhāṣya explains the one-interval position through three determinations:

```text
seven or eight grades abandoned
    + corresponding uncontaminated antidotal faculty acquired
    + only one birth remaining
    → ekavīcika
```

The Path-person is determined positively as well as negatively. Progress is not only subtraction of fault; it is acquisition of the uncontaminated capacity opposed to that fault.

**tṛtīya-pratipanna**

After destruction of seven or eight grades, the practitioner is proceeding toward the third fruit, non-returning. The seventh and eighth abandonments belong to a renewed superior Path beyond the fruit of once-returning.

**Actualized superior Path**

The Bhāṣya explicitly returns to the rule of VAK 6.32. If relevant grades had been abandoned before fruit-attainment, that stored condition does not immediately make the person *kulaṃkula* or *ekavīcika*. The Path superior to the attained fruit must be **made present** (*samukhīkṛta*).

```text
prior abandonment
    ≠ active higher Path-status

prior abandonment
    + superior Path made present
    → new candidate designation
```

**dhātvatikrama**

Why can one weak remaining grade obstruct the fruit? The Bhāṣya answers: because the transition is a **crossing beyond a domain**. The final grade is numerically small but structurally decisive. Until it is destroyed, the desire realm remains a possible domain of return.

Here **dhātu** is the existential desire realm, not the eighteen cognition-domains of Chapter 1.

**anāgāmin**

Upon destruction of all nine desire-realm grades, the practitioner becomes a **non-returner** because there is no return to *kāmadhātu*. The third fruit is thus defined by complete desire-realm afflictive abandonment.

**Five lower fetters**

The Bhāṣya also invokes abandonment of the five lower fetters. It warns that this is a grouped description: two or three had necessarily been abandoned earlier. The canonical formula summarizes a completed structure; it does not imply simultaneous destruction of all five at the final instant.

## 8. Logical Determination

The kārikā completes the desire-realm nine-grade transition:

```text
abandoned grades = 6
    → OnceReturner

abandoned grades ∈ {7, 8}
    → OneInterval
    → CandidateForThirdFruit

abandoned grades = 9
    → NonReturner
    → no return to DesireRealm
```

The Bhāṣya adds two necessary conditions:

```text
CandidateDesignation
    requires SuperiorPathMadePresent

DomainCrossing
    requires FinalObstructingGradeDestroyed
```

Consequently:

```text
small numerical residue
    may possess
decisive structural function

one final grade
    blocks
transcendence of one whole domain
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, the final fault is the domain's minimal sufficient condition of recurrence. Removing it does not transport an unchanged practitioner elsewhere; it eliminates the rule under which return to that domain remained possible.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.36 brings the relation between Path and Dhātu into full view. A domain is not merely a place containing action; it is the structured possibility of a kind of return. So long as even the final relevant afflictive grade remains, desire-realm recurrence is still permitted. When that grade is destroyed, the domain loses its determining hold upon the practitioner.

This also sharpens the role of *Indriya*. The one-interval practitioner is not defined only by what has disappeared but by acquisition of the corresponding uncontaminated faculty. Domain-transcendence therefore has two inseparable sides:

```text
obstructing rule loses its efficacy
    + counter-capacity becomes actual
    → Path crosses beyond the domain
```

The classifications, seven-or-eight range, and ninth-grade threshold belong to the kārikā. The three causes, two explanations of *vīci*, domain-crossing argument, need to actualize the superior Path, causal explanation of non-returning, and grouped lower-fetter account belong to the Bhāṣya. The final grade as the domain's minimal condition of recurrence and the explicit Dhātu–Indriya formulation are Organon synthesis.
