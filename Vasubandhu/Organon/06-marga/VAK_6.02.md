# VAK_6.02

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सत्यान्युक्तानि चत्वारि दुःखं समुदयस्तथा ।
निरोधमार्ग इत्येषां यथाभिसमयं क्रमः ॥ ६.२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
satyāny uktāni catvāri duḥkhaṃ samudayas tathā |
nirodho mārga ity eṣāṃ yathābhisamayaṃ kramaḥ || 6.2 ||
```

VAK 6.02 names the four truths and states the principle governing their order.
They are not placed in causal sequence. They are taught in the order in which
they are directly realized.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
satyāni | uktāni | catvāri | duḥkham | samudayaḥ | tathā |
nirodhaḥ | mārgaḥ | iti | eṣām | yathā-abhisamayam | kramaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `satyāni` | nominative plural neuter of `satya` | truths |
| `uktāni` | nominative plural neuter past passive participle of `√vac` | have been stated |
| `catvāri` | nominative plural neuter | four |
| `duḥkham` | nominative singular neuter | suffering; the afflicted appropriated aggregates as result |
| `samudayaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | arising or origin; the afflicted complex as cause |
| `tathā` | indeclinable connective | likewise, and |
| `nirodhaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | cessation |
| `mārgaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | Path |
| `iti` | quotative particle | thus; closes the enumeration |
| `eṣām` | genitive plural of `idam` | of these |
| `yathā-abhisamayam` | adverbial compound | according to direct realization |
| `kramaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | order, sequence |

`Yathābhisamayam` determines the kind of order asserted. The Bhāṣya glosses
`abhisamaya` with `abhisaṃbodha`, direct or complete awakening-knowing, and
further qualifies it as right knowing according to reality and oriented toward
nirvāṇa.

## 4. Grammar

The opening clause combines a retrospective assertion with an enumeration:

```text
satyāni uktāni catvāri
    four truths have been stated

[tāni]
    duḥkham
    samudayaḥ
    nirodhaḥ
    mārgaḥ
```

The four singular nouns stand in apposition to plural `satyāni`. `Iti` closes
the quoted enumeration.

The final construction is:

```text
eṣāṃ kramaḥ
    the order of these

yathā-abhisamayam
    according to direct realization
```

The Bhāṣya makes the relation explicit: whichever truth is directly realized
earlier is taught earlier. This excludes causal sequence as the intended
construal. Were cause the governing principle, `samudaya` would precede
`duḥkha`; the verse instead begins with the experienced result and then seeks
its cause.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Four truths have been stated: suffering, origin, cessation, and Path. The
> order of these accords with direct realization.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Four truths have been taught: suffering, its origin, cessation, and the Path.
> They are ordered according to the sequence of their direct realization: one
> first examines the suffering in which one is situated, then seeks its origin,
> then its cessation, and finally the Path to that cessation. This direct
> realization is right knowing according to reality and oriented toward
> nirvāṇa.

The diagnostic sequence, the definition of `abhisamaya`, and its orientation
toward nirvāṇa are supplied by the Bhāṣya rather than stated in the kārikā.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Truth becomes the content of a liberating Path in an order distinct from the
> causal production of the world. The practitioner begins with the actuality
> of bondage, penetrates its generating ground, determines cessation, and then
> determines the effective mediation of cessation. This is neither arbitrary
> enumeration nor detached knowledge: it is the sequence in which reality must
> be rightly realized for knowing to become oriented toward release.

The Bhāṣya preserves both sides of this determination:

```text
truth
    is not made true by the practitioner

abhisamaya-order
    determines how the practitioner becomes adequate to truth
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Satya` — truth**

Here truth is not merely a proposition. Each truth is a real determination
that can be examined and directly realized. The Bhāṣya says that the four are
true for noble and non-noble beings alike because they are non-inverted; the
difference lies in whether they are seen according to reality.

**`Duḥkha-satya` — truth of suffering**

The appropriated aggregates insofar as they are result. It comes first because
the practitioner initially examines that in which one is situated, by which
one is afflicted, and from which one seeks release.

**`Samudaya-satya` — truth of origin**

The causal determination of the afflicted conditioned complex. The Bhāṣya
holds that suffering and origin differ by designation rather than as two
distinct real entities: the same complex is determined as result and as cause.

**`Nirodha-satya` — truth of cessation**

The cessation sought after the cause of suffering has been determined. The
Bhāṣya treats cessation and Path as really distinct determinations, so
cessation must not be reduced to the activity that realizes it.

**`Mārga-satya` — truth of the Path**

The effective way to cessation. It is fourth in the order of realization, not
because it is an afterthought, but because a path can be adequately sought only
after the condition, its cause, and its cessation have been discriminated.

**`Krama` — order**

The relevant order is neither arbitrary textual arrangement nor causal order.
The Bhāṣya distinguishes teachings arranged in ways favorable to arising,
formulation, or direct realization. The Four Truths follow the third principle.

**`Abhisamaya` — direct realization**

Glossed by the Bhāṣya as `abhisaṃbodha`. Its full local determination is:

```text
samyak-bodha
    right knowing

tattvena
    according to reality

nirvāṇābhimukha
    oriented toward nirvāṇa
```

Its uncontaminated status depends not on immediacy alone but on truth,
orientation, and liberating direction together.

**`Vyavacāraṇā-avasthā` — stage of examination**

The preparatory discrimination of the truths. The Bhāṣya says examination
proceeds in the same sequence as their later direct realization.

**`Pūrvavedha` — prior penetration or preparatory orientation**

The earlier examination establishes the direction subsequently followed by
direct realization. Abhisamaya is therefore not an isolated flash without a
prepared trajectory.

**`Ārya-satya` — Noble Truth**

The truths do not become false for non-noble beings. Noble ones see their
non-inverted determination as it is; others apprehend them through inversion.
The title concerns adequacy of seeing, not a knower-relative truth.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes causal order from Path-order:

```text
CausalOrder:
    Samudaya → Duḥkha

AbhisamayaOrder:
    Duḥkha → Samudaya → Nirodha → Mārga
```

The order of realization can be reconstructed as:

```text
SituatedIn(person, Duḥkha)
AND SeeksReleaseFrom(person, Duḥkha)
    → FirstExamines(person, DuḥkhaSatya)

Realizes(person, DuḥkhaSatya)
    → SeeksGround(person, SamudayaSatya)

Realizes(person, SamudayaSatya)
    → SeeksCessation(person, NirodhaSatya)

Realizes(person, NirodhaSatya)
    → SeeksWayTo(person, MargaSatya)
```

The four truths are not four homogeneous substances:

```text
Duḥkha and Samudaya
    = one conditioned complex
      differently determined as result and cause
    → distinct by designation, not by real entity

Nirodha and Mārga
    = really distinct determinations
```

The adequacy condition for direct realization is:

```text
RightKnowing(x)
AND AccordingToReality(x)
AND OrientedToward(x, Nirvana)
    → AnasravaAbhisamaya(x)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The order is practical without being subjective.** The truths retain their
objective determination. Their sequence is governed by the transformation
required for a practitioner to see them adequately.

**Result precedes cause in the Path.** Causation runs from origin to suffering,
but inquiry begins from suffering and regresses to its origin. The Path does
not first reconstruct the world from a remote first cause; it begins from the
afflicted actuality requiring release.

**Examination prepares realization.** `Vyavacāraṇā` and `abhisamaya` must not
be collapsed, yet the former gives the latter its trajectory through
`pūrvavedha`. Preparatory discrimination is neither direct realization itself
nor external to its genesis.

**Truth and adequate seeing remain distinct.** The Four Truths are not true
only for the noble. The noble person is one whose seeing has become adequate
to their actual determination.

**Path is distinct from cessation.** The Path mediates release but is not
identical with cessation. The Bhāṣya expressly treats `nirodha` and `mārga` as
really distinct, a boundary that any later synthesis must preserve.

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 6.02 supplies a textual basis for Path as the
order of Practical Meaning. This is a project-level determination, not
Vasubandhu's own terminology:

```text
Truth
    what is non-inverted

Abhisamaya-order
    how the practitioner becomes adequate to Truth

Mārga
    the effective mediation by which realization is oriented toward release
```

On this reading, Meaning is not an additional fifth truth placed between truth
and knowledge. It is the relational and transformative order in which the
truths become Path-capacity. The textual controls are `yathābhisamayam`, the
diagnostic sequence, and the Bhāṣya's definition of realization as right
knowing according to reality and oriented toward nirvāṇa.
