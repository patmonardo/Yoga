# VAK 6.51 — Śrāmaṇya and the Twofold Fruit

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

श्रामण्यममलो मार्गः संस्कृतासंस्कृतं फलम् ।
एकोननवतिस्तानि मुक्तिमार्गाः सह क्षयैः ॥ ६.५१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

śrāmaṇyam amalo mārgaḥ |
saṃskṛtāsaṃskṛtaṃ phalam |
ekānnavatis tāni muktimārgāḥ saha kṣayaiḥ || 6.51 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

saṃskṛta-asaṃskṛtam | phalam |

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **śrāmaṇyam** | neuter nominative singular abstract noun | the defining efficacy or practice of a śramaṇa |
| **amalaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | stainless; glossed by the Bhāṣya as uncontaminated, *anāsrava* |
| **mārgaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | Path |
| **saṃskṛta-asaṃskṛtam** | neuter nominative singular coordinative compound | conditioned and unconditioned |
| **phalam** | neuter nominative singular | fruit, result |
| **ekānnavatiḥ** | numeral | eighty-nine |
| **tāni** | neuter nominative plural pronoun | those [fruits] |
| **mukti-mārgāḥ** | masculine nominative plural compound | liberation-paths; the Bhāṣya uses *vimukti-mārga* |
| **saha kṣayaiḥ** | indeclinable plus masculine instrumental plural | together with the corresponding exhaustions or cessations |

The kārikā states the paired total but does not display its arithmetic. The Bhāṣya derives eighty-nine from eight abandonments by seeing plus eighty-one abandonments by cultivation.

## 4. Grammar

The opening is an equational definition: **śrāmaṇyam** is identified with **amalaḥ mārgaḥ**, the stainless Path. The difference in grammatical gender reflects the predication of the masculine noun **mārgaḥ** of the neuter abstract **śrāmaṇyam**.

The next sentence predicates a coordinated character of **phalam**: it is both **saṃskṛta** and **asaṃskṛta**. The singular does not erase the distinction; it states that śrāmaṇya-fruit has conditioned and unconditioned determinations.

In the final statement, **tāni** resumes the fruits collectively and **ekānnavatiḥ** gives their enumeration. **mukti-mārgāḥ saha kṣayaiḥ** explains the pairing: the liberation-paths together with their corresponding cessations. The Bhāṣya counts eighty-nine paired cases, not 178 unrelated kinds of fruit.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The stainless Path is śrāmaṇya. Its fruit is conditioned and unconditioned. Those are eighty-nine: the liberation-paths together with the cessations.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Śrāmaṇya in the strict sense is the uncontaminated Path. Its conditioned fruits are the eighty-nine liberation-paths, and its unconditioned fruits are the corresponding eighty-nine abandonments of affliction. The total comprises eight liberation-paths associated with abandonment by seeing and eighty-one associated with abandonment by cultivation, each paired with its cessation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Authentic śramaṇa-practice is defined by the uncontaminated Path's effective pacification of affliction, not merely by an external status. Its operation and its fruit must then be distinguished. The uninterrupted Path is the abandoning activity; its fruit is actual both as conditioned liberation-cognition and as unconditioned cessation.

The conditioned does not manufacture the unconditioned. Rather, one liberating course has two irreducible fruit-determinations: an arisen path-moment in which separation is possessed, and the cessation or abandonment possessed through it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**śrāmaṇya**

The Bhāṣya gives the exact equivalence:

```text
śrāmaṇya
    = amala-mārga
    = anāsrava-mārga
```

The term does not primarily designate social membership in a renunciant order. The Path is *śrāmaṇya* because afflictive and unwholesome dharmas are pacified through it. The Bhāṣya therefore says that an ordinary person is not a **paramārtha-śramaṇa**, a śramaṇa in the ultimate or strict sense, because the pacification is not complete.

**Path and fruit**

The Bhāṣya makes a distinction more exact than the compressed kārikā alone:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    = śrāmaṇya itself
    = effective abandoning operation

vimukti-mārga
    = conditioned śrāmaṇya-fruit

kṣaya / kleśa-prahāṇa
    = unconditioned śrāmaṇya-fruit
```

The liberation-path is fruit as both **niṣyanda-phala**, an outflow-result, and **puruṣakāra-phala**, a result of the preceding activity. The corresponding abandonment is unconditioned and must not be identified with the cognition that possesses it.

**The eighty-nine pairs**

The Bhāṣya supplies the enumeration:

```text
abandonment by seeing
    8 uninterrupted paths
    → 8 liberation-paths

abandonment by cultivation
    9 grounds × 9 grades
    = 81 uninterrupted paths
    → 81 liberation-paths

8 + 81 = 89
```

Each liberation-path corresponds to one abandonment or cessation. The number eighty-nine therefore indexes one complete series under two fruit-aspects; it does not collapse the aspects or count them as 178 independent fruit-types.

**Conditioned possession and unconditioned cessation**

The liberation-path is an arisen cognition and is therefore conditioned. The affliction's abandonment is unconditioned. Their relation is not identity:

```text
conditioned vimukti-mārga
    possesses the result

unconditioned kṣaya
    is the cessation possessed
```

This also clarifies 6.50: *kṣaya*, *kṣayajñāna*, and *anutpādajñāna* are respectively cessation, knowledge of its accomplishment, and—in the immovable case—knowledge of its non-recurrence.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Path–fruit architecture is:

```text
UninterruptedPath(x)
    → directlyAbandons(x, AfflictionGrade)
    → instanceOf(x, Sramanya)

LiberationPath(y)
    → immediatelyFollows(y, x)
    → Conditioned(y)
    → fruitOf(y, x)

Cessation(z)
    → correspondsTo(z, AfflictionGrade)
    → Unconditioned(z)
    → fruitOf(z, x)
```

Thus one operative Path yields a paired fruit:

```text
śrāmaṇya as action
    ānantarya-mārga
        ↓
conditioned fruit
    vimukti-mārga
        +
unconditioned fruit
    kṣaya / prahāṇa
```

Two reductions are excluded:

```text
fruit ≠ cognition alone
fruit ≠ cessation abstracted from attained Path-relation
```

The two sides are functionally united as fruit while remaining ontologically distinct.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.51 makes action and fruit maximally precise. The action is not retrospectively called a fruit: the uninterrupted Path is *śrāmaṇya* itself, the effective course of abandonment. The following liberation-path is the conditioned result of that action, while cessation is its unconditioned result.

In the Organon reconstruction, this is a concrete articulation of Beginning, operation, and End. The End does not hover in advance as a merely imagined goal. Through the Path-operation it becomes actual in two inseparable but non-identical senses: as the practitioner's conditioned possession of freedom and as freedom-from the obstruction itself.

This is not a conversion of the unconditioned into a produced object. The Bhāṣya preserves the category boundary: causal Path-cognition is conditioned, abandonment is unconditioned, and their unity is the structured meaning of **śrāmaṇya-phala**. The Organon formulation names that relation; it does not replace the source's ontology.
