## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

At direct realization of the Path there is also acquisition of verified confidence concerning the Buddha and his Saṃgha. One directly realizing suffering, origin, and cessation acquires verified confidence in Dharma and the ethical disciplines dear to the noble ones. One directly realizing the Path acquires verified confidence in the Buddha and his community of disciples. Confidence in those two is confidence in the beyond-training dharmas constituting the Buddha and the training and beyond-training dharmas constituting the Saṃgha. The word “also” includes acquisition of the ethical and Dharma confidences.

Which Dharma is intended here? The verse says the three Truths, with Bodhisattvas and solitary Buddhas named in its final dual; the commentary adds “and the Path.” Thus Dharma-confidence is acquired through direct realization of all four Truths. These are called four verified confidences by name because their bases for confidence differ. By constituent dharma there are two: confidence and ethical discipline. Verified confidence concerning Buddha, Dharma, and Saṃgha is of the nature of `śraddhā`; the ethical disciplines dear to the noble ones are `śīla`.

Are these verified confidences contaminated or uncontaminated? They are stainless. What does “verified confidence” mean? It is settled assurance after understanding the Truths as they are. The commentary then asks how one brings them to mind after rising from realization and gives their order: the Blessed One is rightly awakened, his teaching and discipline are well proclaimed, and his community of disciples has practiced well. These are illustrated by physician, medicine, and attendant. Ethical confidence is called the fourth because it is made through confidence of consciousness. Such is the practice of one whose confidence is settled. Another comparison invokes health, a guide, a path, a caravan, and a vehicle.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The opening applies `api` to the two previously named confidences and relates Buddha and Saṃgha confidence to the dharmas constituting their objects. The commentary then completes Dharma's Truth-object by adding the Path to the first three. It distinguishes four names by bases of confidence from two constituent dharmas, marks the confidences stainless, and defines their genesis as assurance after understanding the Truths as they are. Finally it turns to the order in which these confidences become present after realization and offers practical comparisons.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Api` means that Path-realization brings Buddha and Saṃgha confidence together with Dharma and ethical confidence. `Mārgaś ca` adds the Path to `satyatrayam`, so Dharma-confidence takes all four Truths as its object. `Nāmataḥ` counts four designations by different confidence-bases; `dravyataḥ` counts two constituent dharmas, `śraddhā` and `śīla`. `Nirmalā` marks the verified confidences uncontaminated. `Avetyaprasāda` is settled assurance following comprehension of the Truths as they are. The source retains `bodhisattvapratyekabuddhayoḥ` without giving it a secure syntactic explanation here.

**Organon determination.** Direct realization can be represented as yielding settled confidence whose objects remain distinct while its operative constituents are counted in two ways. This is a project reconstruction of the Bhāṣya's acquisition and counting relations; it does not resolve the verse's unglossed dual compound.

## 6. Four Names and Two Constituents

| Verified confidence | Object or basis | Constituent dharma |
|---|---|---|
| Buddha | Beyond-training dharmas constituting the Buddha | `śraddhā` |
| Dharma | The four Truths directly realized | `śraddhā` |
| Saṃgha | Training and beyond-training dharmas constituting the community | `śraddhā` |
| Noble ethical discipline | Disciplines dear to the noble ones | `śīla` |

All four are `nirmala`, stainless or uncontaminated. The table records the commentary's four-by-name and two-by-constituent analysis; the verse translation stays with what the kārikā itself states and what its immediate gloss settles.
