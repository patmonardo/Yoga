# VAK 6.78 Bhāṣya — Three Principle Names of Unconditioned Liberation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> asaṃskṛtaiva dhātvākhyā virāgo rāgasaṃkṣayaḥ |
> prahāṇadhātur anyeṣāṃ nirodhākhyas tu vastunaḥ || 6.78 ||

> Only unconditioned [liberation] bears the name Principle. Dispassion is exhaustion of attachment; the Principle of abandonment [is exhaustion] of the other afflictions; the Principle called cessation [is exhaustion] of the affliction-free basis.

## 3. Continuous Translation

And what is the difference between the unconditioned liberation already stated and the three Principles spoken of as the Principle of abandonment, the Principle of dispassion, and the Principle of cessation? “Only unconditioned [liberation] bears the name Principle.” That same unconditioned liberation is the three Principles. Among these: “Dispassion is exhaustion of attachment.” Abandonment of attachment is the Principle of dispassion. “The Principle of abandonment [is exhaustion] of the others.” The word “exhaustion” continues here: abandonment of afflictions other than attachment is the Principle of abandonment. “The [Principle] called cessation [is exhaustion] of the basis.” “Exhaustion” continues here too: abandonment of the basis freed from afflictions is the Principle of cessation.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The question compares the previously stated unconditioned liberation with three named Principles. The answer identifies them, then differentiates the names by what is abandoned: attachment, other afflictions, or the basis freed from afflictions. The Bhāṣya twice carries `saṃkṣaya` forward into the abbreviated verse clauses. It does not present a temporal narrative of three successive liberations.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Dhātu` is Principle in this Organon reading; the source names three liberation Principles, not the eighteen cognitive Principles or three existential realms. `Virāga` here is exhaustion of attachment, specified by `rāgasya prahāṇam`. The referent of `anyeṣām` is other afflictions, supplied by the prose. `Vastu` is qualified by `kleśanirmuktasya`: the basis freed from afflictions. The source uses `prahāṇa` for all three explanations, while its continuation of `saṃkṣaya` supplies the kārikā's elliptical wording.

**Organon determination.** The same unconditioned liberation is named through three relations to what has ended. This relational account explains the passage's names; the source itself states their identity with unconditioned liberation and the three abandonment definitions.

## 6. Three Names and Their Referents

| Principle name | Bhāṣya's explanation |
|---|---|
| `virāgadhātu` | Abandonment of attachment (`rāga`) |
| `prahāṇadhātu` | Abandonment of the other afflictions |
| `nirodhadhātu` | Abandonment of the basis freed from afflictions |

The third referent is not another affliction. The passage identifies what is abandoned without explaining here the full process or timing of the basis's cessation.
