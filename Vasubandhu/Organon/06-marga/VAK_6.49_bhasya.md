# VAK_6.49 Bhāṣya — Mundane Paths and Comparative Aspects

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vimuktyānantaryapathā laukikās tu yathākramam |
>
> śāntādyudārādyākārā uttarādharagocarāḥ || 6.49 ||

> A mundane liberation Path takes the higher ground as its cognitive
> field, considering it peaceful, excellent, and an exit. A mundane
> uninterrupted Path takes the lower ground as its cognitive field,
> considering it coarse, painful, and wall-like: a ground that affords
> no exit from itself. The two Path functions thus proceed through
> opposed aspects of the higher and lower grounds.

The two mundane Path functions take different grounds as their
cognitive fields and determine them through opposed aspect triads.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Mundane liberation and uninterrupted Paths operate, respectively,
with aspects beginning with peacefulness and coarseness. Liberation
Paths have the higher ground as their cognitive field; uninterrupted
Paths have the lower. The commentary first assigns the aspect series:
liberation Paths take peacefulness and the rest, uninterrupted Paths
coarseness and the rest. It then names their respective fields.

A liberation Path considers the higher ground peaceful, excellent,
and an exit, as these aspects are applicable. An uninterrupted Path
considers the lower ground coarse, painful, and wall-like. The lower
is coarse because it is unpeaceful and involves greater formative
activity. It is painful because it is less excellent and opposed to
the practitioner through its greater heaviness or rigidity. It is
wall-like because there is no exit from that ground by remaining in
it, just as a wall does not provide an exit. The peaceful, excellent,
and exit aspects are the reverses of these lower-ground aspects.
The subsidiary discussion is complete.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

`Yathākramam` aligns liberation Path with the higher ground and its
positive aspect triad, and uninterrupted Path with the lower ground
and its negative triad. The Bhāṣya explains each negative aspect,
derives the positive aspects by reversal, and closes the subsidiary
discussion.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Gocara` is rendered cognitive field;
`ālambana` in the preceding unit remains cognitive Condition. The
compressed `śāntādyudārādyākārāḥ` is unpacked by the prose into two
triads. `Duḥkhila` is painful, and `sthūlabhittika` is wall-like. The
running verse's `uktarādhara` is not silently changed in §2.

**Organon determination.** A mundane Path evaluates a lower ground as
what must be left and a higher conditioned ground as its relative
exit. The interpretation of these opposed valuations as one practical
movement is project synthesis; the paired triads and fields are the
textual determinations.

## 6. Respective Paths, Fields, and Aspects

| Path | Cognitive field | Aspects |
|---|---|---|
| Mundane uninterrupted | Lower ground | Coarse, painful, wall-like |
| Mundane liberation | Higher ground | Peaceful, excellent, exit |

The higher ground is still conditioned; the comparative exit here is
not equated with unconditioned liberation.
