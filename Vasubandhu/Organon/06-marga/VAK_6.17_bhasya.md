# VAK_6.17 Bhāṣya — Heat, Sixteen Aspects, and the Summits

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tata ūṣmagatotpattis tac catuḥsatyagocaram |
> ṣoḍaśākāram ūṣmabhyo mūrdhānas te ’pi tādṛśāḥ || 6.17 ||

> From that arises Heat. The Four Truths are its cognitive range and Support,
> and it has sixteen aspects. From Heat arise the Summits; they share that
> Support and those aspects.

The Bhāṣya supplies the fire image, lists the sixteen aspects, and delimits
what the Summits share with Heat.

## 3. Continuous Translation

From the dharma establishment cultivated in this way there gradually arises
a wholesome root called Heat. It is Heat, as it were: it is the preliminary
sign of the Noble Path fire, which burns the fuel of the afflictions. Heat
ranges over the Four Truths. Because its course is extended
(*prākarṣikatvāt*), it takes the Four Truths as Support. It has sixteen
aspects. Suffering is seen as impermanent, suffering, empty, and not-self;
origin as cause, arising, production, and conditioning factor; cessation as
cessation, peace, excellence, and escape; and the Path as Path, right method,
practice, and leading out. The commentary says it will explain the
particulars of these aspects later.

From the Heats arise the Summits. Like Heat, they take the Four Truths as
Support and have sixteen aspects. They receive another name because they
are more developed and stand at the summit of the unstable wholesome roots.
From them there may be falling away or passing beyond.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary resolves the pronoun `tataḥ` as the dharma establishment
cultivated in 6.16 and names Heat as its gradually arising wholesome root.
It explains the fire image, gives the Four Truths as the cognitive range and
Support, and distributes sixteen aspects across them. It then explains
`tādṛśāḥ`: the Summits share Heat's truth Support and aspect structure, but are more
developed. The possibility of fall or passage beyond remains explicit.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Uṣmagata` is a wholesome root and preliminary
sign, distinct from the Noble Path fire itself. `Catuḥsatya-gocara` gives
Heat's cognitive range; the prose additionally calls the Four Truths its
Support (`ālambana`) because Heat's course is extended (`prākarṣikatvāt`).
The earlier 3.25 usage supports “extended”; the precise force of the reason
here remains open. `Ṣoḍaśākāra` distributes four aspects to each truth.
`Tādṛśāḥ` preserves that field and aspect structure in the
Summits, while the prose supplies greater development and their still
unstable status. The anchor includes the prose's contrast without assigning
Summits a new truth field.

**Organon determination.** The truth range, Support, and aspect structure can
remain constant while a wholesome root acquires greater practical force. A
future Feature Grammar can carry range, Support, aspects, development, and
reversibility separately; a LogicFrame can express which predicates are
inherited from Heat by the Summits. This is project formalization of the
source's distinctions.

## 6. Four Truths and Sixteen Aspects

| Truth | Four aspects |
|---|---|
| Suffering | Impermanent, suffering, empty, not-self |
| Origin | Cause, arising, production, conditioning factor |
| Cessation | Cessation, peace, excellent, escape |
| Path | Path, right method, practice, leading out |

The Summits retain the Four Truths as Support and the sixteen-aspect pattern.
Greater development does not make the stage irreversible.
