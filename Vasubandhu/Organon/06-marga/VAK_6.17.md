# VAK 6.17 — Heat, the Sixteen Modes, and the Summits

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

तत ऊष्मगतोत्पत्तिस्तच्चतुःसत्यगोचरम् ।
षोडशाकारमूष्मभ्यो मूर्धानस्तेऽपि तादृशाः ॥ ६.१७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

tata ūṣmagatotpattis tac catuḥsatyagocaram |
ṣoḍaśākāram ūṣmabhyo mūrdhānas te ’pi tādṛśāḥ || 6.17 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

tataḥ | uṣmagata-utpattiḥ | tat | catuḥ-satya-gocaram |
ṣoḍaśa-ākāram | uṣmabhyaḥ | mūrdhānaḥ | te | api | tādṛśāḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **tataḥ** | ablative singular used adverbially | from that; from the cultivated dharma-foundation of 6.16 |
| **uṣmagata-utpattiḥ** | feminine nominative singular compound | the arising of what has reached Heat, the stage called Heat |
| **tat** | neuter nominative singular pronoun | that Heat |
| **catuḥ-satya-gocaram** | neuter nominative singular compound agreeing with *tat* | having the Four Truths as its cognitive range |
| **ṣoḍaśa-ākāram** | neuter nominative singular compound agreeing with *tat* | possessing sixteen modes or aspects |
| **uṣmabhyaḥ** | ablative plural of *uṣman* | from the Heats; from the Heat-stage cognitions |
| **mūrdhānaḥ** | masculine nominative plural of *mūrdhan* | the Summits, the culminating unstable wholesome roots |
| **te api** | pronoun plus particle | they too; the Summits as well |
| **tādṛśāḥ** | masculine nominative plural | of that kind, having the same object and aspect-structure |

The sandhi resolves as **tataḥ + uṣmagatotpattiḥ → tata ūṣmagatotpattiḥ**, **tat + catuḥsatyagocaram → tac catuḥsatyagocaram**, and **mūrdhānaḥ + te → mūrdhānas te**.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda forms an elliptical statement: **tataḥ uṣmagata-utpattiḥ [bhavati]**, “from that there is the arising of Heat.” The omitted verb is supplied from the nominal construction. **tataḥ** reaches back to the dharma-foundation cultivated in the comprehensive manner described in 6.16; this antecedent is confirmed by the Bhāṣya.

The neuter pronoun **tat** refers to **uṣmagata**, treated as the name of the arisen wholesome root. Its two predicates are **catuḥsatyagocaram** and **ṣoḍaśākāram**: it ranges over the Four Truths and has sixteen modes.

The second half gives another genetic relation: **uṣmabhyaḥ mūrdhānaḥ [utpadyante]**, “from the Heats arise the Summits.” The plural forms reflect the series of cognitions constituting these stages.

**te ’pi tādṛśāḥ** is a nominal clause: “they too are of that kind.” The Bhāṣya determines the scope of the resemblance. The Summits, like Heat, take the Four Truths as object-support and possess sixteen modes. The kārikā does not say that the two stages are identical in every respect.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

From that arises Heat. It has the Four Truths as its range and possesses sixteen modes. From the Heats arise the Summits; they too are of that kind.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

From the dharma-foundation cultivated in that way there gradually arises the wholesome root called Heat. It takes the Four Truths as its object-support and examines them through sixteen modes. From Heat arise the Summits, which retain the same Four Truths and the same sixteen modes but are more developed.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

From sustained contemplation of the articulated total field, a new wholesome capacity arises: Heat, the precursor of the Noble Path. Its field is reorganized as the Four Truths, each grasped through four determinate modes.

When this same structured cognition increases in excellence, the Summits arise. Development here does not require a new truth-object or a new conceptual inventory. The same content and form acquire greater Path-force. Yet Summit remains a threshold: it is the highest point within the wholesome roots from which reversal is still possible.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**uṣmagata**

Literally “gone to Heat” or “reached Heat,” **uṣmagata** names the first of the wholesome roots conducive to penetration. The Bhāṣya says that it arises gradually from the dharma-foundation cultivated as in 6.16.

The metaphor is exact:

```text
afflictions = fuel
Noble Path  = fire that burns the fuel
Heat        = preliminary sign of the approaching fire
```

Heat is thus a causal precursor (*pūrvarūpa*) of the Noble Path, not yet the Noble Path itself.

**kuśalamūla**

The Bhāṣya calls Heat a **wholesome root**. “Root” marks an acquired causal capacity in the continuum. The stage is not only a correct proposition concerning the Truths; it is a developed cognition capable of generating a further Path-stage.

**catuḥsatya-gocara**

The **Four Truths** are the cognitive range of Heat and Summit:

1. **duḥkha** — suffering;
2. **samudaya** — origin;
3. **nirodha** — cessation;
4. **mārga** — Path.

Here **gocara** indicates their operative cognitive domain. The combined field of 6.16 is now organized according to these four truth-determinations.

**ṣoḍaśākāra**

Each Truth is seen through four modes, yielding sixteen:

| Truth | Four modes |
|---|---|
| **duḥkha** | impermanent (*anitya*), suffering (*duḥkha*), empty (*śūnya*), not-self (*anātman*) |
| **samudaya** | cause (*hetu*), arising (*samudaya*), production (*prabhava*), condition (*pratyaya*) |
| **nirodha** | cessation (*nirodha*), peace (*śānta*), excellent (*praṇīta*), release (*niḥsaraṇa*) |
| **mārga** | Path (*mārga*), right method (*nyāya*), practice (*pratipatti*), leading out (*nairyāṇika*) |

The list is given by the Bhāṣya. The kārikā states the number sixteen but does not itself enumerate the modes.

**mūrdhan**

**Mūrdhan**, “Summit,” names the more developed stage arising from Heat. According to the Bhāṣya, Summits receive another name because they are more excellent (*utkṛṣṭatara*) and stand at the summit of the **cala-kuśalamūlas**, the unstable wholesome roots.

Their instability is decisive: from the Summits there may be either falling away or passing beyond. Summit is consequently the maximum of a still-reversible development.

**tādṛśa**

“Of that kind” expresses structural continuity between Heat and Summit. Both take the Four Truths as support and both have sixteen modes. The Bhāṣya locates their difference in degree of development, not in object or formal articulation.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse articulates two genetic transitions and one invariant structure:

```text
cultivated DharmaFoundation
    → Heat

Heat
    → Summits

Heat and Summits
    share FourTruths as object-support
    share SixteenModes as cognitive form
```

The sixteen modes form a four-by-four determination:

```text
Four Truths × four modes per Truth = sixteen modes
```

The Bhāṣya adds the intensional difference:

```text
same object-field
+ same aspect-structure
+ greater excellence and force
= Summit
```

Accordingly, Path development is not reducible to accumulating new contents. A cognition may advance by becoming more stable and causally powerful while preserving its object and articulated form.

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, 6.17 distinguishes semantic structure from practical efficacy. The structure of Meaning—the Four Truths through sixteen modes—remains invariant; the capacity of that structure to transform the continuum intensifies. Summit is therefore the same determinate Meaning raised to a higher, though still reversible, Path-power.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.16 gathered the differentiated fields into a combined object-support and subjected them to universal seeing. VAK 6.17 shows that sustained cultivation of this synthesis produces a new root. The Path is now explicitly genetic: one determinate operation gives rise to another.

The name **Heat** prevents premature identification with realization. Heat announces the fire but does not yet burn the afflictive fuel definitively. The image preserves both continuity and difference between preparatory cultivation and the Noble Path.

The transition to Summit then isolates a second principle. Advancement may occur without changing what is cognized. Heat and Summit share the Four Truths and sixteen modes; Summit is distinguished by superior maturity. Thus the Truth is not merely “had” as Knowledge. Its determinations must acquire operative force within the Path.

The kārikā itself establishes the genesis, Four-Truth range, sixteen modes, and structural likeness of Heat and Summit. The fire metaphor, the complete list of modes, greater excellence, and the possibility of fall or passage beyond are Bhāṣya determinations. The account of invariant Meaning acquiring greater Path-power is the Organon's reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's terminology.
