# VAK_6.17 — Heat, Sixteen Aspects, and the Summits

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तत ऊष्मगतोत्पत्तिस्तच्चतुःसत्यगोचरम् ।
षोडशाकारमूष्मभ्यो मूर्धानस्तेऽपि तादृशाः ॥ ६.१७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
tata ūṣmagatotpattis tac catuḥsatyagocaram |
ṣoḍaśākāram ūṣmabhyo mūrdhānas te ’pi tādṛśāḥ || 6.17 ||
```

Heat arises from the repeatedly cultivated dharma establishment of 6.16.
It and the Summits share the Four Truths and sixteen aspects; the Bhāṣya
specifies how the latter surpass Heat while remaining vulnerable to falling.

The Bhāṣya resolves `tataḥ` as the repeatedly cultivated dharma

Condition and sixteen-aspect structure.

## 4. Grammar

`Tataḥ uṣmagatotpattiḥ` is a nominal statement of arising. The two neuter
predicates `catuḥsatyagocaram` and `ṣoḍaśākāram` qualify the Heat named by
`tat`. The second ablative, `uṣmabhyaḥ`, gives the source from which the
plural `mūrdhānaḥ` arises. `Te ’pi tādṛśāḥ` predicates likeness of the
Summits to Heat, with its range fixed by the commentary.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> From that arises Heat. It ranges over the Four Truths and has sixteen
> aspects. From the Heats arise the Summits; they too are of that kind.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Repeated cultivation of the dharma establishment gradually gives rise to
> Heat, a wholesome root signaling the approaching Noble Path. Heat takes the
> Four Truths as its cognitive field through sixteen aspects, four for each
> truth. From Heat arise the Summits: they retain the same Four Truths and
> sixteen aspects, yet surpass Heat in development and may still fall away.

### Translation note

The precursor image, four groups of aspects, greater development, and
possibility of falling away are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Continued examination of the combined field produces Heat, a new wholesome
> capacity directed to the Four Truths. The sixteen aspects give that
> capacity a determinate form. The Summits preserve the truth field and the
> aspects while developing greater force. Their possible fall shows that
> this development has not yet become irreversible.

```text
cultivated dharma establishment
    → Heat: Four Truths × four aspects
    → Summits: same structure, greater development, possible fall
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Uṣmagata` — Heat**

The wholesome root that gradually arises from cultivation of the combined
dharma establishment. It is the preliminary sign (`pūrvarūpa`) of the fire
of the Noble Path, which burns afflictions as fuel. The image marks its
prospective relation to the Path, not identity with that fire.

**`Kuśalamūla` — wholesome root**

The Bhāṣya calls Heat a root: an arising capacity in the practitioner that
supports further Path development. The name does not by itself state
irreversibility or definitive abandonment.

**`Catuḥsatya-gocara` — Four Truths as cognitive field**

Heat ranges over suffering, origin, cessation, and Path. The prose also calls
these truths its cognitive Condition (`catuḥsatya-ālambana`). The field is
newly specified in relation to 6.16's combined contemplation.

**`Ṣoḍaśākāra` — having sixteen aspects**

The Bhāṣya assigns four modes to each truth:

| Truth | Four aspects |
|---|---|
| Suffering | Impermanent (`anitya`), suffering (`duḥkha`), empty (`śūnya`), not-self (`anātman`) |
| Origin | Cause (`hetu`), arising (`samudaya`), production (`prabhava`), condition (`pratyaya`) |
| Cessation | Cessation (`nirodha`), peace (`śānta`), excellent (`praṇīta`), release (`niḥsaraṇa`) |
| Path | Path (`mārga`), right method (`nyāya`), practice (`pratipatti`), leading out (`nairyāṇika`) |

The kārikā gives the number sixteen; the prose gives the list and postpones
its fuller explanation.

**`Mūrdhan` — Summit**

The stage arising from Heat. The commentary explains its distinct name by
greater development (`utkṛṣṭatara`) and its position at the summit of the
unstable wholesome roots (`cala-kuśalamūla`). Falling away or passing beyond
remains possible from it.

**`Tādṛśa` — of the same kind**

The Summits share Heat's Four-Truth cognitive Condition and sixteen aspects.
The likeness does not erase their difference in development or guarantee
irreversibility.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Heat signals an approaching capacity.** The fire image explains why this
wholesome root is named Heat: the Noble Path is the fire that burns the
fuel of the afflictions, and Heat is its preliminary sign.

**Development need not change the cognitive field.** Heat and Summits take
the same Four Truths through the same sixteen aspects. The commentary
assigns the Summits greater excellence while also recording the possibility
of fall or passage beyond.

**Organon interpretation.** Practical Meaning has a determinate truth field
and aspect structure, while its Path-power can develop within the
practitioner. A future reflective Model can register that structural
continuity and the difference in operative capacity. This is a project
formalization of the source's stage relations.
