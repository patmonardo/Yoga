# VAK_6.65 Bhāṣya — Two Measures of Completion and Four Path Functions

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> aśaikṣaparipūrṇatvaṃ dvābhyāṃ mārgaḥ samāsataḥ |
> viśeṣamuktyānantaryaprayogākhyaś caturvidhaḥ || 6.65 ||

> One beyond training is complete in two respects. In summary, the Path is fourfold, called Further Distinction, Release, Immediacy, and Application.

The opening finishes the account of completeness begun in 6.64; the second clause asks for the Path's summary classification.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Completeness in one beyond training is determined in two respects: faculty and meditative attainment. Someone incomplete with respect to fruit would not be beyond training at all, so fruit is not established again as a variable measure of that person's completeness. The commentary gives three examples: one liberated independently of occasion but through Prajñā is complete by faculty; one liberated in both respects but dependent on occasion is complete by attainment; and one liberated in both respects independently of occasion is complete by faculty and attainment.

Many Path distinctions have been stated: mundane and supramundane, Seeing, cultivation, and beyond-training, and the Paths of Application, Immediacy, Release, and Further Distinction. How many kinds of Path are there in summary? Four, bearing the names Further Distinction, Release, Immediacy, and Application. The Path of Application is that immediately after which the Path of Immediacy arises. The Path of Immediacy is that by which an obstruction is abandoned. The Path of Release first arises free of the obstruction to be abandoned. The Path of Further Distinction is Path other than those three.

Why are they called Path? This is the road to Nirvāṇa, because by it one goes there; or by it one seeks Nirvāṇa. The commentary then asks how Release and Further Distinction can still be Path. They can be stronger instances of the same kind, bring about progressively higher attainment, or lead into Nirvāṇa without remainder.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The first movement closes the classification of complete persons: fruit is already implied by beyond-training status, while faculty and attainment admit differences. The second gathers the many Path divisions into four operative types. Its definitions separate preparation, removal of obstruction, first freedom from obstruction, and further Path. The closing question grounds even Release and Further Distinction in the movement toward Nirvāṇa.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Dvābhyām` is glossed as faculty and attainment. The four Path names appear in metrical order in the kārikā, while the Bhāṣya explains them most clearly in the operative order Application, Immediacy, Release, Further Distinction. `Viśeṣa` initially means Path other than the three named before it; the later reasons for its Path-status do not turn it into a single fixed fourth moment. The two formulations about going to or seeking Nirvāṇa are both retained.

**Organon determination.** The four modes distinguish a preparatory condition, the act that removes obstruction, the first released state, and development beyond it. This is an interpretation of the commentary's operational definitions. The source itself calls each a Path because of its relation to Nirvāṇa as destination, including modes that arise after the first liberation moment.

## 6. Four Path Functions

| Path | Bhāṣya definition | Operative relation |
|---|---|---|
| Application, `prayoga` | Immediately precedes Immediacy | Preparation |
| Immediacy, `ānantarya` | Abandons obstruction | Direct counteraction |
| Release, `vimukti` | First arises free of obstruction | First released actuality |
| Further Distinction, `viśeṣa` | Other Path beyond the three | Continued Path development |
