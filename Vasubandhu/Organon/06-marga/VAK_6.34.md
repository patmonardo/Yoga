# VAK 6.34 — First-Fruit Positions and Their Existential Bounds

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

अक्षीणभावनाहेयः फलस्थः सप्तकृत्परः ।
त्रिचतुर्विधमुक्तस्तु द्वित्रिजन्मा कुलंकुलः ॥ ६.३४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

akṣīṇa-bhāvanā-heyaḥ phalasthaḥ saptakṛt-paraḥ |
tri-catur-vidha-muktas tu dvi-tri-janmā kulaṃkulaḥ || 6.34 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

akṣīṇa-bhāvanā-heyaḥ | phala-sthaḥ | sapta-kṛt-paraḥ |
tri-catur-vidha-muktaḥ | tu | dvi-tri-janmā | kulam-kulaḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **akṣīṇa-bhāvanā-heyaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | one whose factors abandonable by cultivation have not been exhausted |
| **phala-sthaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | one established in the fruit |
| **sapta-kṛt-paraḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | one for whom seven occurrences are the maximum |
| **tri-catur-vidha-muktaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | freed from three or four grades |
| **tu** | indeclinable | but; marks the second classification |
| **dvi-tri-janmā** | masculine nominative singular compound | one having two or three births |
| **kulam-kulaḥ** | masculine nominative singular technical designation | one going from family to family |

Both halves classify persons established in stream-entry. The kārikā correlates remaining cultivation-work with a bounded number of births; the detailed rationale belongs to the Bhāṣya.

## 4. Grammar

In the first half, **phalasthaḥ** is the person qualified by **akṣīṇa-bhāvanā-heyaḥ** and identified as **sapta-kṛt-paraḥ**. The compound does not say that no cultivation-abandonable grade has been removed; it says that the cultivation-abandonable factors have not been exhausted. The Bhāṣya accordingly glosses the condition as even one such grade remaining unabandoned.

In the second half, **tu** contrasts a more advanced first-fruit position. **tri-catur-vidha-muktaḥ** states the amount of release, **dvi-tri-janmā** the remaining birth-range, and **kulaṃkulaḥ** the practitioner's technical name.

**para** in **sapta-kṛt-para** fixes an upper limit. It does not require exactly seven births. The compound is therefore construed as “seven at most.”

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

One established in the fruit whose factors abandonable by cultivation are not exhausted is a seven-at-most. But one freed from three or four grades, having two or three births, is a family-to-family practitioner.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

A stream-enterer established in the first fruit while any cultivation-removable affliction remains is classified as one whose further births are bounded by seven at most. A stream-enterer already freed from three or four such grades is family-to-family, with only two or three births remaining.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The first fruit is one real attainment, but it does not erase the internal differences of the work that remains. The practitioner's position is determined not only by the fruit attained but by the residual affliction, the antidotal capacity acquired, and the resulting range of possible existence.

Path-position therefore becomes existentially effective. As cultivation removes determinate grades, it contracts the range in which unresolved movement can continue. The fruit establishes the irreversible direction; cultivation determines how much of the course remains.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**phalastha and bhāvanā-heya**

The **phalastha** here is established in stream-entry, the first Noble fruit. This completion of the Path of Seeing is compatible with remaining **bhāvanā-heya**, afflictions abandonable through cultivation:

```text
Seeing completed
    → first fruit established
    → cultivation-residue may remain
```

Fruit and residual task are not contradictory determinations. They identify completion at one Path-level and incompletion at another.

**srota-āpanna**

The Bhāṣya analyzes **srota-āpanna** as one who has entered or attained the stream. The stream is the Path directed toward Nirvāṇa (*nirvāṇa-srotas*). The designation is thus Path-functional: the person's course has acquired an irreversible orientation.

Vasubandhu tests the precise boundary of this name against the initial “eighth person” and the already detached practitioner. His explanation associates full stream-entry with complete realization of the relevant stream, not merely first temporal contact with a Path moment.

**saptakṛtvaḥparama**

The **seven-at-most** practitioner is a stream-enterer with cultivation-removable affliction still remaining. Seven is a maximum causal range rather than a mandatory itinerary; liberation may occur sooner.

The Bhāṣya discusses alternative analyses of what the scriptural number counts among gods, humans, and intermediate existences. The stable determination needed here is the upper bound, not one harmonized sectarian arithmetic.

**The end of suffering**

The stream-enterer will make an end beyond which no new suffering connected with rebirth occurs. The Bhāṣya clarifies that Nirvāṇa is not thereby produced. What is removed is the obstruction to its attainment (*tat-prāpti-vibandha*):

```text
Nirvāṇa is not produced
obstruction to attainment is removed
    → the end of suffering is accomplished
```

**kulaṃkula**

The **family-to-family** practitioner has abandoned three or four cultivation-removable grades and has two or three births remaining. The Bhāṣya distinguishes divine and human forms according to the domain in which those remaining births occur.

The designation correlates three dimensions:

```text
affliction-grades abandoned
    ↔ uncontaminated antidotal faculties acquired
    ↔ remaining birth-range
```

**The five-six threshold**

The Bhāṣya asks why abandonment of five grades does not define another family-to-family position. Once the fifth is abandoned, the sixth must also be abandoned because that one remaining grade cannot obstruct the next fruit. Thus three or four grades constitute the family-to-family range; five cannot remain a stable classification.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 6.34 converts the ninefold scale into a classification of Path-persons:

```text
FirstFruitEstablished
    + CultivationAfflictionRemaining
    → SevenAtMost
    → remaining births ≤ 7

FirstFruitEstablished
    + GradesAbandoned ∈ {3, 4}
    → FamilyToFamily
    → remaining births ∈ {2, 3}
```

The Bhāṣya adds a threshold rule:

```text
Grade5Abandoned
    → Grade6Abandoned necessarily
    → next fruit threshold crossed
```

The classification is therefore not a simple continuous count. Some states are stable positions; another possible count triggers a necessary qualitative transition.

The full causal correlation is:

```text
fruit attained
    + residual affliction-profile
    + antidotal faculty acquired
    → practitioner-type
    → bounded existential range
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, the practitioner is not externally assigned a future. The transformed inner morphology determines which future courses remain possible. The Path's End operates as a real limit on Loka.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.33 established that each completed removal reveals a subtler determinate task. VAK 6.34 shows that these internal transformations are not confined to an inward psychological scale. They alter the practitioner's objective range of possible rebirth.

This gives a precise sense to the “stream.” Stream-entry does not mean that every remaining movement has ceased. It means that all remaining movement is now bounded by an irreversible direction toward Nirvāṇa. The practitioner may occupy distinguishable positions within that one directed course.

The fifth-sixth threshold is especially important. Quantitative advance accumulates until the remaining obstruction can no longer sustain the old fruit-position; a qualitative transition then follows necessarily. The next fruit is not arbitrarily awarded from outside but emerges when the residual structure can no longer block it.

The two practitioner-types and their numerical correlations belong to the kārikā. The etymology of stream-entry, the maximum rather than mandatory reading of seven, the debates over counting, the non-production of Nirvāṇa, the threefold determination by abandonment, faculty, and birth, and the fifth-sixth threshold belong to the Bhāṣya. The interpretation of fruit as an existential limit and of the threshold as transition from quantity to quality is Organon synthesis.
