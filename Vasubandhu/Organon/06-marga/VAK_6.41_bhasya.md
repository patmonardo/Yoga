# VAK_6.41 Bhāṣya — Another Birth, Faculty Maturity, and No Regression

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> na parāvṛtta-janmāryaḥ kāme dhātv-antaropagaḥ |
>
> sa cordhva-jaś ca naivākṣa-saṃcāra-parihāṇi-bhāk || 6.41 ||

> A Noble who has undergone another birth in the desire-realm reaches
> non-returning and final Nirvāṇa within that life, without moving to
> another realm. A Noble reborn in the form realm may still enter the
> formless realm. Yet neither the former practitioner nor a Noble born
> above changes faculty type or falls away: another birth has ripened
> the faculties and brought a superior basis.

The commentary scopes the realm restriction and explains why two
specified Noble positions no longer admit faculty transfer or falling
away.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Does the differentiation just given also apply to a non-returner who
has passed through another birth? The verse answers: a Noble who has
undergone another birth in the desire-realm does not go to another
realm. After attaining the fruit of non-returning, that person reaches
final Nirvāṇa within that same life. A Noble after another birth in
the form realm, however, may enter the formless realm; this is the
upward-streamer whose furthest point is the summit of existence.

But Śakra spoke of a future birth among the gods called Akaniṣṭha.
The Vaibhāṣikas reply that he was unfamiliar with the Abhidharma.
A further compressed reply in the witness appears to permit the
statement as encouragement; its exact wording remains uncertain.

Neither the Noble who has undergone another desire-realm birth nor
a Noble born in a higher realm transfers faculties or falls away in
any way. Why are these transitions excluded? Because they do not occur.
Why not? Residence through another birth has made the faculties more
mature, and the practitioner has acquired a superior basis.

Why does a trainee already free from desire not reach final Nirvāṇa
in the intermediate existence? The Vaibhāṣikas answer that the Path
has not been mastered or made present, latent tendencies are not yet
sufficiently weak, and the current of desire is difficult to cross.
Much remains to be done: abandon unwholesome and indeterminate
afflictions, attain two or three fruits of the ascetic life, and
cross the three realms. One situated in the intermediate existence
cannot accomplish all of that work there.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The passage restricts realm transition for a Noble after another
desire-realm birth, distinguishes the form-realm upward-streamer, and
responds to Śakra's statement. It then excludes faculty transfer and
regression for the two named positions through faculty maturation and
superior basis. A final Vaibhāṣika explanation denies that mere
desire-detachment suffices for intermediate-existence completion.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The first-line `kāme` scopes the rule
against realm transfer to another birth in the desire-realm. The
form-realm case is an explicit contrast. `Akṣa` in the verse is read
through the prose's `indriyāṇi`, the faculties. `Āśraya` is “basis.” The Vaibhāṣika
response to Śakra and its compressed follow-up are marked rather
than silently made into an agreed historical fact. The explanation
of the desire-detached trainee is likewise attributed.

**Organon determination.** Another Noble birth is a condition of
faculty maturation and a more adequate basis; this narrows the
available transitions. The restriction must be indexed to realm and
practitioner position rather than generalized to all Nobles. This is
project synthesis.
