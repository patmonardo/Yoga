# VAK_6.60 Bhāṣya — Fruit Regression and Change to Unshakeable Disposition

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> mriyate na phalabhraṣṭas tadakāryaṃ karoti na |
> vimuktyānantaryapathā navākopye ’tisevanāt || 6.60 ||

> One fallen from the fruit neither dies nor performs what is incompatible with it. Becoming unshakeable requires nine liberation Paths and nine uninterrupted Paths, through deep habituation.

The first claim continues the Vaibhāṣika account; the second turns to the technical process of changing faculties.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The commentary asks whether someone who falls from Arhat fruit will be born again and answers no. No one dies while fallen from that fruit. It cites a sūtra about a noble disciple's lapse of mindfulness quickly reaching its end; otherwise the holy life would offer no assurance. Moreover, one fallen from a fruit does not perform what someone established in that fruit ought not do. Even in the fallen state, the practitioner performs no action contrary to that fruit, like a hero who stumbles and falls.

The commentary then asks how many uninterrupted and liberation paths occur when faculties are changed. In penetrating the unshakeable disposition, the practitioner capable of that penetration has nine uninterrupted paths and nine liberation paths, as with someone attaining Arhatship. Why? Because of deep habituation: the former mild-faculty disposition cannot be turned around by a slight measure of practice. It has been made firm by both the trainee Path and the Path beyond training.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The passage first limits the practical consequences of fruit-regression in the Vaibhāṣika framework: no death while fallen and no action opposed to the fruit. It then turns from a bounded loss to a positive change of faculties. The full ninefold paired Path sequence answers to the firmness of the former disposition, which has been reinforced across both stages of noble practice.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Phalabhraṣṭa` is someone fallen from the fruit; `tadakārya` is specified in prose as action contrary to that fruit. `Nava` counts nine of each Path type. `Atisevanā` names deep habituation of the *prior mild-faculty disposition*, not simply the practitioner's future repetition of the new paths. The question of rebirth is answered through the narrower explicit claim that no one dies while fallen; the source does not spell out the timing or mechanics of recovery.

**Organon determination.** The attained Path continues to constrain action even in a fall, while repeated practice has given the existing faculty type resistance to change. This is a project reading of the two arguments together. The source does not call this “structural memory,” and the Sautrāntika objection to fruit-regression remains distinct.
