# VAK 6.42 — Mixed Dhyāna and the Proper Granularity of Practice

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

आकीर्यते चतुर्थं प्राक् निष्पत्तिक्षणमिश्रणात् ।
उपपत्तिविहारार्थं क्लेशभीरुतयापि च ॥ ६.४२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

ākīryate caturthaṃ prāk niṣpatti-kṣaṇa-miśraṇāt |
upapatti-vihārārthaṃ kleśa-bhīrutayāpi ca || 6.42 ||

The mounted research witness notes a reading issue around **caturthaṃ**; the kārikā and Bhāṣya construe the fourth dhyāna as the subject of mixing. The exact moment-mixing mechanism is not unqualified kārikā doctrine: the Bhāṣya attributes it to the Vaibhāṣikas and then restricts it.

## 4. Grammar

**caturtham** is the subject of the passive **ākīryate**: “the fourth [dhyāna] is mixed first.” **prāk** establishes its priority over mixing the other dhyānas.

The next phrase is best punctuated as **niṣpattiḥ kṣaṇa-miśraṇāt**: “completion is through the mixing of moments.” The ablative gives the means or ground of accomplished mixing.

**upapatti-vihārārtham** gives two purposes, rebirth and abiding. **kleśabhīrutayā api ca** adds another motivation, wariness of affliction. The Bhāṣya distributes these purposes according to practitioner-type and faculty strength.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The fourth [dhyāna] is mixed first; completion is through the mixing of moments—for the sake of rebirth and abiding, and also because of fear of the afflictions.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The fourth dhyāna is intermingled first because it is maximally serviceable. Mixed dhyāna is cultivated to condition rebirth in the Pure Abodes, to provide pleasant abiding in the present life, and, for practitioners vulnerable to regression, to protect against renewed afflictive influence. The Vaibhāṣikas analyze its completion through exact alternation of contaminated and uncontaminated moments; Vasubandhu limits such moment-level control to a Buddha and accepts voluntary mastery of three continuous streams.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The most serviceable meditative ground is formed first so that it can support the others. Its cultivation may project a future domain, sustain present abiding, or protect an unstable capacity against the return of affliction.

Yet formal precision must not be confused with realizable practice. A microscopic account may represent the structure of alternation, but the practitioner ordinarily masters whole streams rather than manufacturing isolated cognitive moments. The Path must be articulated at the resolution at which its faculty can actually operate.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Why the fourth dhyāna is first**

The Bhāṣya describes the fourth dhyāna as a samādhi capable of every function because it is supreme among pleasant modes of practice. Once it has been mixed, the other dhyānas can be mixed through its power.

```text
fourth dhyāna mixed first
    → enabling basis established
    → other dhyānas become mixable through its strength
```

**vyavakiraṇa**

Mixed dhyāna alternates uncontaminated and contaminated forms of the same attainment. Both the Vaibhāṣika account and Vasubandhu's preferred account preserve this ordered alternation:

```text
anāsrava
    → sāsrava
    → anāsrava
```

The disagreement concerns the controllable temporal resolution of that structure.

**The Vaibhāṣika procedure**

The reported preparation begins with continuous streams and progressively shortens them:

```text
continuous uncontaminated fourth dhyāna
    → continuous contaminated fourth dhyāna
    → continuous uncontaminated fourth dhyāna

then preparatory compression:
    2 uncontaminated moments
    → 2 contaminated moments
    → 2 uncontaminated moments

claimed completion:
    1 uncontaminated moment
    → 1 contaminated moment
    → 1 uncontaminated moment
```

The Vaibhāṣikas compare the two uncontaminated moments with uninterrupted Paths and the third moment with a liberation Path. This imports the severance–release form into the analysis of meditative construction.

**Vasubandhu's restriction**

Vasubandhu responds that moment-by-moment intermingling is impossible for anyone other than a Buddha. He understands completion instead as the ability to enter the three larger streams at will:

```text
formal microscopic analysis
    ≠ ordinarily realizable cognitive control

voluntary mastery of three streams
    = practicable completion
```

He does not reject alternation; he rejects attributing Buddha-level temporal control to an ordinary practitioner.

**upapatti**

For the sharp-faculty non-returner, mixed dhyāna conditions rebirth in the Pure Abodes. Meditative formation thereby becomes a causal preparation of a later existential domain.

**vihāra**

For sharp-faculty non-returners and arhats, the practice also serves pleasant abiding in the presently experienced life. The same operation can therefore have a present experiential function as well as a future rebirth function.

**kleśa-bhīrutā**

The mild-faculty non-returner or arhat additionally cultivates mixed dhyāna from wariness of affliction. It distances samādhi associated with savoring and protects against falling away. This is not generalized anxiety; it is technically directed caution appropriate to a less stable faculty.

**Practitioner-relative function**

The operation does not possess one purpose in isolation:

```text
same mixed dhyāna
    + non-returner or arhat status
    + sharp or mild faculty
    → rebirth function
    or present abiding
    or protection against regression
```

Thus its practical Meaning emerges from its relation to Path-person, Indriya, and End.

## 8. Logical Determination

The common transition-pattern is:

```text
State A: uncontaminated stream
    → State B: contaminated stream
    → State A': uncontaminated stream after controlled alternation
```

Two implementations must be distinguished:

```text
VaibhasikaModel:
    temporalGranularity = single cognitive moment
    controllerCapability = exact moment alternation

VasubandhuModel:
    temporalGranularity = continuous stream
    controllerCapability = voluntary entry into three streams
```

The purpose is parameterized:

```text
MixedDhyānaFunction =
    f(PathStatus, FacultyStrength, IntendedEnd)
```

where the Intended End may be:

```text
PureAbodeRebirth
PresentPleasantAbiding
ProtectionAgainstRegression
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, the correct descriptive resolution is constrained by Indriya. A finer theoretical syntax does not entail that the faculty can perform the corresponding operation. Practical Meaning requires executable granularity.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.42 is a remarkably exact warning against burdening direct cultivation with excessive reflective machinery. The Vaibhāṣika micro-model can be formally coherent and still exceed the practitioner's operative faculty. Vasubandhu preserves the experienced streams and refuses to pretend that analytical decomposition confers microscopic control.

This does not oppose analysis to Seeing absolutely. Analysis may describe the relation, but it must not replace the level at which the Path is actually present. For the non-Buddha practitioner, the operative object is the controlled transition among streams—not a reflective representation of every moment composing them.

The priority of fourth dhyāna, moment-mixing, and three stated purposes belong to the kārikā. The detailed Vaibhāṣika mechanism, Path analogy, Vasubandhu's objection, preferred stream-level account, and allocation of purposes by person and faculty belong to the Bhāṣya. The distinction between formal syntax and executable perceptual granularity is Organon synthesis.
