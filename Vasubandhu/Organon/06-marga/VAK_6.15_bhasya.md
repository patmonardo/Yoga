# VAK_6.15 Bhāṣya — Prajñā and the Four Counterforces

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> prajñā śrutādimayī anye saṃsargālambanāḥ kramaḥ |
> yathotpatti catuṣkaṃ tu viparyāsavipakṣataḥ || 6.15 ||

> The establishment of recollection by nature is discriminative comprehension
> formed from learning and the rest; the others are by association and by
> Support. Their order follows arising; the fourfold arrangement
> counters the inversions.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes three uses of “establishment of recollection,”
explains the name, and separates the reasons for the order and number four.

## 3. Continuous Translation

What is the intrinsic nature of the establishments of recollection? The name
has several applications: an establishment by intrinsic nature, by
association, and by Support. In intrinsic nature it is Prajñā. What kind of
Prajñā? That formed through learning, reflection, or cultivation. Thus the
establishments too are threefold by learning, reflection, and cultivation.
The other dharmas arising together with Prajñā receive the name through
association; what Prajñā takes as its Support receives it through Support.

Why identify the intrinsic establishment as Prajñā? A cited statement says,
“In desire (*kāme*), contemplation of body is an establishment of
recollection.” What is contemplation? Prajñā: through it one becomes a
contemplator. Another statement says, “One dwells contemplating body
inwardly.” The prose explains that one who has contemplation is a
contemplator, and that a contemplator with respect to body is a contemplator
of body. Why, then, did the Blessed One call Prajñā an establishment of
*recollection*? The Vaibhāṣikas say that recollection predominates: the
operation depends on its strength, as a wedge holds split wood. The
commentary prefers another explanation: Prajñā establishes recollection by
articulating what has been seen.

Thus Aniruddha says that, for one dwelling in contemplation of body in body,
recollection with body as Support stands and remains established, and so
forth. The Blessed One also says that, for one dwelling in contemplation of
body in body, recollection is established in the undeluded one. Yet another
teaching asks, “Monks, how do the four establishments of recollection arise
and pass away? From the arising of food comes the arising of body; from the
cessation of food comes the passing away of body.” Here the Support itself
is called an establishment of recollection, since recollection is established
there. The establishments are named according to Support; each is threefold
according to whether its Support belongs to one's own continuum, another's,
or both.

What determines their sequence? Their order of arising: the coarser is seen
first. The Vaibhāṣikas also give a causal sequence: body is the ground of
sensual desire; that desire comes from longing for feeling; that longing
comes from undisciplined Consciousness (*citta*); and that from afflictions
not yet abandoned. Why exactly four establishments? They counter the four
inversions of purity, pleasure, permanence, and self, respectively; there
are neither more nor fewer. The first three each take an unmixed Support.
The fourth may take dharmas alone as an unmixed Support, or a mixed Support
comprising two, three, or all four fields beginning with body.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary first determines Prajñā as intrinsic nature and gives its
three modes of formation. It extends the name to associated dharmas and the
Support. It then asks why a Prajñā practice bears a recollection
name, reporting the Vaibhāṣika answer before preferring its own. Finally it
distinguishes the arising-order from the fourfold number and explains the
latter through four paired counterforces.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Anye` refers to other dharmas arising with
Prajñā. `Saṃsarga` is association; `ālambana` is Support, distinct from the
Organon's technical Condition and Object. `Śrutādi` is unfolded by the prose as
learning, reflection, and cultivation. `Yathotpatti` explains sequence,
whereas `viparyāsavipakṣataḥ` explains number and function. The anchor names
the four fields and inversions in their Bhāṣya-given order. The preference
introduced by `evaṃ tu yujyate` belongs to the commentary's explanation of
the name and is retained in the continuous translation.

**Organon determination.** The same practice can be typed by intrinsic nature,
associated complex, and Support without making those three one
thing. The four practices are then ordered by arising and individuated by
the inversions each counters. A future reflective Feature Grammar can carry
these relations as separate features; a LogicFrame can test the four
counterforce assignments. These are project representations of source
relations.

## 6. Three Uses and Four Counterforces

| Use of *smṛtyupasthāna* | Referent |
|---|---|
| Intrinsic nature | Prajñā formed from learning, reflection, or cultivation |
| Association | Dharmas arising with Prajñā |
| Support | What Prajñā takes up |

| Establishment | Inversion countered |
|---|---|
| Body | Purity attributed to the impure |
| Feeling | Pleasure attributed to suffering |
| Consciousness (`citta`) | Permanence attributed to the impermanent |
| Dharmas | Self attributed to what is not-self |

The first three have unmixed Supports. The fourth may examine dharmas alone
or a mixture of the examined fields.
