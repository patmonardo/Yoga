# VAK_6.15 Bhāṣya — Prajñā and the Four Counterforces

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> prajñā śrutādimayī anye saṃsargālambanāḥ kramaḥ |
> yathotpatti catuṣkaṃ tu viparyāsavipakṣataḥ || 6.15 ||

> The establishment of recollection by nature is discriminative comprehension
> formed from learning and the rest; the others are by association and by
> cognitive Condition. Their order follows arising; the fourfold arrangement
> counters the inversions.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes three uses of “establishment of recollection,”
explains the name, and separates the reasons for the order and number four.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What is the intrinsic nature of the establishments of recollection? The name
has several applications: an establishment by intrinsic nature, by
association, and by cognitive Condition. In intrinsic nature it is Prajñā.
What kind of Prajñā? That formed through learning, reflection, or cultivation.
The other dharmas arising together with Prajñā are establishments by
association; what Prajñā takes as its Condition is an establishment by
cognitive Condition.

Why identify the intrinsic establishment as Prajñā? The commentary cites a
statement about observing body in body and asks what this observing is. It is
Prajñā: through it one becomes an observer. A further cited statement says
that one dwells observing body inwardly; the prose explains the grammatical
construction of “observer of body.” Why, then, did the Blessed One call Prajñā
an establishment of *recollection*? The Vaibhāṣikas say recollection
predominates and supports the operation, as a wedge holds split wood. The
commentary prefers another explanation: through Prajñā, recollection is
established by articulating what has been seen. Statements attributed to
Aniruddha and the Blessed One are offered in support. A further teaching on
the arising and cessation of the establishments uses the designation for
the cognitive Condition itself: recollection is established there. According
to the Condition, each establishment may concern one's own continuum,
another's, or both.

What determines their sequence? Their order of arising. The coarser is seen
first. The Vaibhāṣikas also give a causal sequence: body is a basis for
sensual desire; feeling is wanted in relation to the body; consciousness
(`citta`) is not disciplined because of this desire; and the afflictive
dharmas remain unabandoned. Why exactly four establishments? They counter the
four inversions of purity, pleasure, permanence, and self, respectively;
there are neither more nor fewer. The first three each take an unmixed
cognitive Condition. The fourth may take dharmas alone, or a mixed field of
two, three, or all four examined fields.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary first determines Prajñā as intrinsic nature and gives its
three modes of formation. It extends the name to associated dharmas and the
cognitive Condition. It then asks why a Prajñā practice bears a recollection
name, reporting the Vaibhāṣika answer before preferring its own. Finally it
distinguishes the arising-order from the fourfold number and explains the
latter through four paired counterforces.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Anye` refers to other dharmas arising with
Prajñā. `Saṃsarga` is association; `ālambana` is a cognitive Condition, not
the Organon's technical Object. `Śrutādi` is unfolded by the prose as
learning, reflection, and cultivation. `Yathotpatti` explains sequence,
whereas `viparyāsavipakṣataḥ` explains number and function. The anchor names
the four fields and inversions in their Bhāṣya-given order. The preference
introduced by `evaṃ tu yujyate` belongs to the commentary's explanation of
the name and is retained in the continuous translation.

**Organon determination.** The same practice can be typed by intrinsic nature,
associated complex, and cognitive Condition without making those three one
thing. The four practices are then ordered by arising and individuated by
the inversions each counters. A future reflective Feature Grammar can carry
these relations as separate features; a LogicFrame can test the four
counterforce assignments. These are project representations of source
relations.

## 6. Three Uses and Four Counterforces

| Use of *smṛtyupasthāna* | Referent |
|---|---|
| Intrinsic nature | Prajñā formed from learning, reflection, or cultivation |
| Association | Dharmas arising with Prajñā |
| Cognitive Condition | What Prajñā takes up |

| Establishment | Inversion countered |
|---|---|
| Body | Purity attributed to the impure |
| Feeling | Pleasure attributed to suffering |
| Consciousness (`citta`) | Permanence attributed to the impermanent |
| Dharmas | Self attributed to what is not-self |

The first three have unmixed Conditions. The fourth may examine dharmas alone
or a mixture of the examined fields.
