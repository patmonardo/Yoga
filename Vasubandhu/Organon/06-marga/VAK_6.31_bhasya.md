# VAK_6.31 Bhāṣya — The Sixteenth Moment and Fruit-Establishment

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ṣoḍaśe tu phalasthau tau yatra yaḥ pratipannakaḥ |
>
> śraddhādhimuktadṛṣṭyāptau mṛdutīkṣṇendriyau tadā || 6.31 ||

> When the sixteenth moment of consciousness arises, the Faith-Follower and
> Dharma-Follower cease to be candidates and stand in the fruit each had
> approached: stream-entry, once-return, or non-return. The gentle-faculty
> practitioner is then *śraddhādhimukta*, Faith-Resolved; the sharp-faculty
> practitioner is *dṛṣṭiprāpta*, one who has attained through Seeing.
> Arhatship is not reached at this first completion: Seeing leaves
> cultivation-abandonable afflictions, and prior mundane detachment cannot
> reach the summit of existence.

The commentary links the sixteenth moment to each practitioner's already
determined fruit and explains the new faculty-based names.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“At the sixteenth moment, those two stand in the fruit toward which each
had proceeded.” Once the sixteenth moment of consciousness arises, the
two are no longer called Faith-Follower and Dharma-Follower, nor are they
called candidates proceeding toward a fruit. They stand in the fruit.
Whichever fruit each approached, that is the fruit in which each now
stands: stream-entry, once-return, or non-return. Arhatship cannot be
attained at this initial completion because the Path of Seeing does not
abandon afflictions to be abandoned through cultivation and because
prior dispassion from the summit of existence is impossible.

“At that time the practitioners of gentle and sharp faculty are
Faith-Resolved and attained through Seeing.” The one of gentle faculty
who was previously a Faith-Follower is called `śraddhādhimukta`.
The one of sharp faculty who was previously a Dharma-Follower is called
`dṛṣṭiprāpta`. Their names reflect predominance of faith and Prajñā,
and corresponding influence of resolve and Seeing.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya first changes each practitioner's status from candidate to
fruit-established at the sixteenth moment, retaining the particular fruit
approached. It excludes immediate arhatship on two grounds. It then
renames the two practitioners while keeping their faculties and prior
follower histories visible.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Ṣoḍaśe` is the sixteenth moment of
consciousness. `Yatra yaḥ pratipannakaḥ` preserves the previously
determined target fruit for each practitioner. `Dṛṣṭyāptau` in the study
is explained by the prose's `dṛṣṭiprāpta`; the damaged source kārikā is
kept distinct. `Bhavāgra` is the summit of existence, the limit that
prior mundane dispassion cannot reach.

**Organon determination.** A reflective Model may retain faculty, prior
abandonment, target fruit, attained fruit, and path position as separate
features. The sixteenth moment actualizes the fruit toward which each
practitioner was already moving. This is project synthesis.
