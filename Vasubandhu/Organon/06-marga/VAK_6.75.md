# VAK 6.75 — Completed Liberation and Its Two Forms

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
मार्गश्च द्रव्यतस्तु द्वे श्रद्धा शीलं च निर्मलाः ।
नोक्ता विमुक्तिः शैक्षाङ्गं बद्धत्वात् सा पुनर्द्विधा ॥ ६.७५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
mārgaś ca dravyatas tu dve śraddhā śīlaṃ ca nirmalāḥ |
noktā vimuktiḥ śaikṣāṅgaṃ baddhatvāt sā punar dvidhā || 6.75 ||
```

The first pāda begins with the Bhāṣya continuation required by 6.74: Dharma includes the Path-truth. It then closes the verified-confidence analysis before opening a new question concerning liberation as a factor proper to the Aśaikṣa.

## 4. Grammar

**Mārgaś ca** completes the preceding nominal definition:

```text
Dharma in this confidence-analysis
    = three Truths + Path
```

The contrast **dravyatas tu dve** opposes four object-based designations to two operative realities. **Nirmalāḥ** then predicates purity of the four understood avetyaprasādas.

In the second line, **baddhatvāt** gives the causal ground for **noktā**: liberation is not counted as a Śaikṣa factor because the trainee remains bound. **Sā punar dvidhā** introduces the division supplied by the Bhāṣya:

```text
saṃskṛta-vimukti
asaṃskṛta-vimukti
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> And the Path [is Dharma]. But according to operative reality they are two, confidence and ethical discipline, and they are stainless. Liberation is not stated as a factor of the trainee because of being bound; that liberation, in turn, is twofold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Dharma includes the Path. The four verified confidences are two in substance—confidence and ethical discipline—and are stainless. Liberation is no factor of the trainee, who remains bound; for one beyond training, it is twofold: the unconditioned abandonment of afflictions and conditioned liberative determination.

### Translation note

The opening `mārgaś ca` closes 6.74's account of Dharma-confidence. The Bhāṣya counts four confidences by object but only `śraddhā` and `śīla` by constituent dharma. It explains `baddhatvāt` through the trainee's remaining afflictive bonds and identifies the two liberations as affliction-abandonment and the Aśaikṣa's `adhimokṣa`. The verse itself names the division; the prose supplies its two members.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Directly realized Truth becomes four stable orientations, but their practical substance is twofold: clarified confidence and stainless conduct. The Path thereby passes from knowing its objects to an uncontaminated disposition toward them.

Yet decisive realization is not identical with completed freedom. So long as any afflictive bond remains, the trainee’s real partial release does not warrant the unqualified predicate “liberated.” Complete liberation has two inseparable but non-identical aspects: the unconditioned fact of bondage abandoned and the conditioned determination of the person actualizing freedom.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Mārgaś ca` — Dharma includes the Path**

The opening **mārgaś ca** belongs syntactically with 6.74:

```text
satyatrayam
    suffering, origin, cessation

mārgaś ca
    and the Path

together
    the Four Truths as object of Dharma-confidence
```

This is why the previous verse’s “three Truths” cannot be treated as an exhaustive definition of Dharma.

**`Śraddhā / śīla` — four confidences, two operative dharmas**

```text
Buddha-confidence ┐
Dharma-confidence ├→ śraddhā
Saṃgha-confidence ┘

noble ethical confidence → śīla
```

The four remain differentiated by object. **Dravyataḥ** counts the capacities actualizing those relations; it does not invalidate the objectual distinctions.

**`Nirmala` — uncontaminated verified confidence**

All four avetyaprasādas are stainless:

```text
verified confidence
    generated by noble direct realization
    → exclusively anāsrava
```

Ordinary confidence in Buddha, Dharma, or Saṃgha may be wholesome, but it is not thereby the realization-grounded **avetyaprasāda** technically defined here.

**`Śaikṣa / aśaikṣa` — eight and ten factors**

The Bhāṣya invokes the scriptural contrast:

```text
Śaikṣa
    eight factors

Aśaikṣa
    ten factors
```

The additional two are:

```text
samyagvimukti
    right liberation

samyagjñāna
    right Knowing of that liberation
```

The question is why these are not attributed to the trainee.

**`Baddhatvāt` — the trainee is still bound**

The decisive reason is not merely that a curriculum remains unfinished:

```text
śaikṣa
    still bound by residual kleśa-bandhana

therefore
    not vimukta without qualification
```

Vasubandhu’s rule is exact:

```text
release from one portion of bondage
    ≠ being called liberated
```

This does not deny the trainee’s genuine abandonments or noble realization. It governs the technical predicate of completed liberation.

**`Vimukti-jñāna` — why Knowing of liberation is also absent**

```text
completed vimukti absent
    → completed vimukti-jñāna cannot arise
```

The Knowing in question is not general doctrinal knowledge about liberation. It is immediate Knowing of one’s own completed release.

**`Aśaikṣa` — one beyond training**

For one beyond training:

```text
all afflictive bonds removed
    → vimukti properly stated
    → immediate Knowing of liberation properly stated
```

This completes the movement first defined at 6.67 as knowledge that the task is exhausted and will not arise again.

**`Saṃskṛta / asaṃskṛta-vimukti` — twofold liberation**

The Bhāṣya divides:

```text
asaṃskṛta-vimukti
    unconditioned liberation
    = abandonment of the afflictions

saṃskṛta-vimukti
    conditioned liberation
    = the Aśaikṣa’s adhimokṣa
```

The unconditioned member is the attained absence or abandonment of afflictive bondage. The conditioned member is an operative dharma present in the liberated continuum.

**`Adhimokṣa` — conditioned liberative determination**

Here **adhimokṣa** is the decisive liberative determination belonging to the Aśaikṣa. It should not be weakened into a mere opinion or preference. VAK 6.76 will specify its relation to mind-liberation and prajñā-liberation.

**`Vimukti` — unconditioned abandonment and conditioned actuality**

The twofold division prevents either reduction:

```text
liberation
    not only an unconditioned absence

liberation
    not only a conditioned mental event
```

The Kośa analyzes the unconditioned abandonment and conditioned liberative actuality under different categories while retaining both within the complete account.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse gives a precise distinction between movement’s intermediate achievements and its completed End. A Śaikṣa has crossed real boundaries and abandoned real afflictions, but the remaining bond prevents partial success from being named the whole.

```text
determinate liberation from some bondage
    genuine Path-result

liberation without qualification
    total freedom from afflictive bondage
```

The twofold liberation then holds Beginning and End together in another way. Unconditioned liberation is the objective accomplished absence of bondage; conditioned liberation is that freedom active as a determination of the completed Path-person.

For the Organon reconstruction, the End must therefore be both achieved content and living actuality. Yet this formulation remains project-level. The Bhāṣya’s own secure distinction is categorical: abandonment of afflictions is unconditioned, whereas the Aśaikṣa’s *adhimokṣa* is conditioned.
