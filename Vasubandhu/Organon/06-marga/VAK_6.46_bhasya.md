# VAK_6.46 Bhāṣya — Two Separation-Attainments; the Reason Fails

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> laukikenāryavairāgye visaṃyogāptayo dvidhā |
> lokottareṇa cety eke tyakte kleśāsamanvayāt || 6.46 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> When an Ārya gains dispassion by the mundane [Path], the attainments of
> separation are twofold. Some say [they are twofold] also by the supramundane
> [Path], because, when [its attainment] is relinquished, possession of the
> afflictions [would otherwise follow].

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

**Witness.** Pradhan 366|16–367|10. Voices: Vasubandhu; *eke/apare*. 6.47 question at 367|11.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> For an Ārya who reaches dispassion by a mundane Path, two attainments of
> separation arise: mundane and supramundane. Some say the same when the Path is
> supramundane. Why? If no mundane attainment arose, one dispassionate from the
> Essence of nothingness who then shifts faculties on a dhyāna would drop the prior
> Path and, they fear, be possessed again by those afflictions.
>
> Vasubandhu: even without the mundane attainment, possession does not return. One
> partly freed at the summit: no mundane attainment available, supramundane one
> dropped in a faculty-shift — still not possessed again. An ordinary person born
> above first dhyāna drops a mundane desire-realm separation-attainment — still not
> possessed again. The reason does not prove it.

## 6. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- last pāda is *eke*'s reason, not Vasubandhu's doctrine;
