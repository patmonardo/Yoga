# VAK_6.46 Bhāṣya — Two Attainments of Separation and a Failed Reason

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> laukikenārya-vairāgye visaṃyogāptayo dvidhā |
> lokottareṇa cety eke tyakte kleśāsamanvayāt || 6.46 ||

> When an Ārya gains dispassion through a mundane Path, both mundane
> and supramundane attainments of separation arise. Some say the same
> happens through a supramundane Path: if no mundane attainment arose,
> they argue, relinquishing the supramundane one during a change of
> faculties would restore possession of the afflictions. The Bhāṣya
> answers with two counterexamples. Loss of an attainment does not by
> itself prove renewed possession of an abandoned affliction.

The first twofold acquisition is asserted. `Eke` introduces a
reported extension, and the Bhāṣya tests the argument for it.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Now, when an Ārya obtains dispassion by a mundane Path, two kinds of
attainment of separation arise: mundane and supramundane. Some say that
the same is true when dispassion is obtained by a supramundane Path.
Why? If a mundane attainment of separation did not arise for someone
who became dispassionate by the Noble Path, then consider a person
who has become dispassionate from the sphere of nothingness by that
Path. Relying on a dhyāna, that person changes faculties. Because
the previous Path is relinquished all at once and only the fruit Path
is acquired, the defender argues that the person would lack separation
from higher-ground afflictions. With that attainment relinquished, the
person would again possess those afflictions.

The answer denies this inference. Even without a mundane attainment
of separation, renewed possession of those afflictions need not
follow. Consider one partly freed from the summit of existence:
there is no mundane attainment of that separation, and even if the
supramundane attainment is relinquished in a change of faculties,
that person does not again possess the corresponding afflictions.
Likewise an ordinary person reborn above the first dhyāna may lose a
mundane attainment of separation from desire-realm afflictions without
again possessing those afflictions. Hence the proposed reason is
non-probative (`ajñāpaka`).

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary affirms two attainments after mundane dispassion by an
Ārya. It then reports another view that extends the same result to a
supramundane Path. The reason offered for that view equates loss of a
separation-attainment with renewed affliction-possession; two
counterexamples show that the inference fails.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Visaṃyogaprāpti` denotes an attainment of
separation, not the abandoning act itself. `Eke` and `apare` mark the
reported position. `Tyavate` is retained in the Sanskrit witness,
while the normalized study reading `tyakte` follows the prose's
scenario of relinquishment. The verdict `ajñāpaka` says this reason
does not establish the proposed extension; it does not by itself
settle whether the extension is false in every case.

**Organon determination.** Abandonment, attainment of separation, and
non-possession of an affliction must remain distinct. The project's
account of a Path result preserved through a change in its acquisition
form is an interpretation of the counterexamples, not a separate
claim made by the verse.

## 6. The Twofold Claim and Its Test

| Determination | Voice | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Mundane Path yields mundane and supramundane attainments | Commentary's initial assertion | Established case |
| Supramundane Path also yields both | `eke / apare` | Reported extension |
| Loss of one attainment would restore affliction | Defender's reason | Rejected as non-probative |
| Partly freed from the summit | Commentary's counterexample | No return of affliction |
| Ordinary person reborn above first dhyāna | Commentary's counterexample | No return of desire-realm affliction |

The counterexamples defeat the proposed inference from a lost
attainment to renewed possession; they do not erase the distinction
between the established case and the reported extension.
