# VAK_6.32 Bhāṣya — Fruit and the Further Path

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> phale phalaviśiṣṭasya lābho mārgasya nāsty ataḥ |
> nāprayukto viśeṣāya phalasthaḥ pratipannakaḥ || 6.32 ||

> When a fruit is attained, acquisition of the Path beyond that fruit does not
> occur. Therefore, one established in a fruit is not a candidate for further
> progress without applying toward further distinction.

The commentary asks why fruit-establishment does not automatically become
candidacy toward the next fruit and states the condition for renewed
movement.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Why is someone who had already abandoned five grades still called a
stream-enterer at the sixteenth moment, rather than a candidate for the
fruit of once-return? Because when a fruit is being attained, the Path
distinguished beyond that fruit is not acquired. This is a fixed rule.
Therefore one established in a fruit is not called a candidate for
another fruit until applying himself toward further distinction and
attainment of that other fruit. The same is to be understood for the
other fruits.

There is a specific qualification: someone detached from the third
dhyāna who enters the determinate order relying on a lower ground
necessarily brings the superior Path into present manifestation.
Otherwise, if reborn above that ground, the practitioner would not be
endowed with the pleasure faculty. The commentary concludes its account
of persons entering the determinate order with prior desire-realm
detachment. The gradual case follows in the next unit.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya answers the five-grade puzzle with a rule separating fruit
attainment from acquisition of a Path beyond that fruit. It extends the
rule to the other fruits, states a local supporting-ground qualification,
and closes the classification by prior detachment before turning to the
gradual case.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Phalaviśiṣṭasya mārgasya` names the Path
distinguished beyond the presently attained fruit. `Aprayuktaḥ
viśeṣāya` denies higher-fruit candidate status while application toward
further distinction is absent. The third-dhyāna case concerns necessary
present manifestation of the superior Path under specified supporting
conditions; it is not generalized to every fruit-establishment.
The source's `nāstyanaḥ` remains uncorrected in the witness.

**Organon determination.** A reflective Model can keep attained fruit,
application, superior Path manifestation, and candidate status as separate
features. A completed fruit establishes a real position; movement toward
a further fruit requires a further condition. This is project synthesis.

## 6. Fruit Position and Renewed Movement

| Condition | Status or effect |
|---|---|
| Present fruit attained | Established in that fruit |
| No application toward further distinction | Not a candidate for another fruit |
| Application toward further distinction | May become a candidate for another fruit |
| Specified third-dhyāna case | Superior Path must become presently manifest |

The local qualification does not turn attainment of every fruit into
automatic acquisition of its superior Path.
