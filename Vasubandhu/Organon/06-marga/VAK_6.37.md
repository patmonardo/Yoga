# VAK 6.37 — Five Modes of Non-Return and the Upward Stream

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

सोऽन्तरोत्पन्नसंस्कारासंस्कारपरिनिर्वृतिः ।
ऊर्ध्वस्रोताश्च स ध्याने व्यवकीर्णोऽकनिष्ठगः ॥ ६.३७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

so 'ntarotpanna-saṃskārāsaṃskāra-parinirvṛtiḥ |
ūrdhvasrotāś ca sa dhyāne vyavakīrṇo 'kaniṣṭhagaḥ || 6.37 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

saḥ | antara-utpanna-saṃskāra-asaṃskāra-parinirvṛtiḥ |
ūrdhva-srotāḥ | ca | saḥ | dhyāne | vyavakīrṇaḥ | akaniṣṭha-gaḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **saḥ** | masculine nominative singular pronoun | that same non-returner from the preceding verse |
| **antara-** | first member of compressed classificatory series | attaining final nirvāṇa in the intermediate existence |
| **utpanna-** | member of the same series | attaining final nirvāṇa after arising in a new existence |
| **saṃskāra-** | member of the same series | attaining final nirvāṇa with exertion |
| **asaṃskāra-** | member of the same series | attaining final nirvāṇa without renewed exertion |
| **parinirvṛtiḥ** | masculine nominative singular in technical compounds | one whose final nirvāṇa occurs in the specified manner |
| **ūrdhva-srotāḥ** | masculine nominative singular | one whose stream or course proceeds upward |
| **dhyāne** | neuter locative singular | with respect to dhyāna |
| **vyavakīrṇaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | intermingled or mixed |
| **akaniṣṭha-gaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | one who goes to Akaniṣṭha |

The first line compresses four technical person-designations. **ūrdhvasrotas** supplies the fifth; the remainder begins its subdivision by meditative cause and destination.

## 4. Grammar

The initial **saḥ** carries forward the **anāgāmin** of VAK 6.36. The long compound is coordinative in sense and must be expanded through the Bhāṣya as four alternative types, not construed as one person successively possessing every predicate.

**ūrdhvasrotāḥ ca** adds a fifth alternative. The second **saḥ** resumes this upward-streamer. **dhyāne vyavakīrṇaḥ** characterizes the relevant meditative condition, and **akaniṣṭhagaḥ** predicates the resulting destination.

The literal syntax says that the upward-streamer is mixed “in dhyāna.” The Bhāṣya supplies the causal construe: one whose dhyāna is intermingled goes to Akaniṣṭha and reaches final nirvāṇa there.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

That one attains final nirvāṇa in the intermediate state, after arising, with exertion, or without exertion; and [that one may be] an upward-streamer. When intermingled with respect to dhyāna, that one goes to Akaniṣṭha.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The non-returner is fivefold: one who attains final nirvāṇa in the intermediate existence, shortly after rebirth, with renewed exertion, without renewed exertion, or by an upward-moving course through higher existences. The upward-streamer whose dhyāna is intermingled proceeds to Akaniṣṭha and reaches final nirvāṇa there.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Complete transcendence of the desire realm does not make the remaining course uniform. The same fruit can terminate at different points and under different transition-conditions: before rebirth is established, shortly after arising, through continued application, without renewed exertion, or through an ascent across higher loci.

The Path-person is thus the determinate form of a remaining trajectory. What has been abandoned fixes non-return; the strength and configuration of the Path-current determine how and where the liberated course reaches completion.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The five non-returners**

The Bhāṣya unfolds the kārikā into five principal types:

```text
anāgāmin
    ├─ antarāparinirvāyin
    ├─ upapadyaparinirvāyin
    ├─ sābhisaṃskāraparinirvāyin
    ├─ anabhisaṃskāraparinirvāyin
    └─ ūrdhvasrotas
```

They are not five fruits. They are five modes in which the continuum already established in the third fruit proceeds to final nirvāṇa.

**antarāparinirvāyin**

This non-returner reaches final nirvāṇa during the intermediate existence, before a new rebirth is established. The classification is determined by the temporal locus at which the remaining course terminates.

**upapadyaparinirvāyin**

This type reaches final nirvāṇa shortly after rebirth. The Bhāṣya associates the rapid completion with a strongly developed carrying Path (*vāhi-mārga*) and weaker residual tendencies.

An alternative view concerning whether this completion is nirvāṇa with or without remainder is reported; the latter account is rejected here because the practitioner lacks mastery over relinquishing lifespan. The disagreement should remain attributed rather than silently harmonized.

**sābhisaṃskāra and anabhisaṃskāra**

The effortful type reaches completion through continued deliberate exertion; its *prayoga* has not relaxed. The effortless type reaches completion without such renewed exertion:

```text
sābhisaṃskāra
    → effort remains an operative condition

anabhisaṃskāra
    → completion occurs without renewed effort
```

The Bhāṣya rejects the alternative explanation that merely contrasts Paths taking conditioned and unconditioned objects, because that interpretation would overextend the classification. The operative distinction is attainment through effort or without effort.

**Path-current and residual anuśaya**

Time to completion is jointly determined by both sides of the process:

```text
greater carrying strength of Path
    + weaker residual anuśaya
    → more immediate completion
```

Thus an attained fruit underdetermines the manner of completion; the continuing relation between Path-power and residue remains decisive.

**ūrdhvasrotas**

The **upward-streamer** does not reach final nirvāṇa in the first place of higher rebirth. The course proceeds upward through successive loci. The Bhāṣya treats *srotas*, stream, and *gati*, course or movement, as equivalent in this context.

The upward-streamer is classified from two sides:

```text
cause:
    intermingled or non-intermingled dhyāna

result:
    Akaniṣṭha or summit-of-existence destination
```

This verse states the intermingled-dhyāna and Akaniṣṭha member; the contrasting case follows in VAK 6.38.

**vyavakīrṇa-dhyāna**

At this point the Bhāṣya establishes only its local Path-function: the technical history or configuration of dhyāna causally determines the upward trajectory and maximal destination. **Vyavakīrṇa** should not be reduced to ordinary psychological distraction or mixture.

Nor should this technical Kośa classification be identified with the project's dhyāna on OM-Artha. That practice concerns the semantic relation enacted through OM; this passage concerns a specific Abhidharma meditative condition governing post-desire-realm rebirth.

**Akaniṣṭha**

Akaniṣṭha is the maximal destination of the intermingled-dhyāna upward-streamer in this classification. The verse makes a meditative determination causally effective as an objective trajectory.

## 8. Logical Determination

The third fruit admits multiple completion functions:

```text
NonReturner
    → completesIn(IntermediateExistence)
    or completesSoonAfter(Rebirth)
    or completesThrough(RenewedExertion)
    or completesWithout(RenewedExertion)
    or follows(UpwardTrajectory)
```

For the final type:

```text
UpwardStreamer
    + IntermingledDhyāna
    → proceedsTo(Akaniṣṭha)
    → reaches(Parinirvāṇa)
```

The Bhāṣya supplies a multidimensional determination:

```text
attained fruit
    + remaining anuśaya strength
    + carrying power of Path
    + effort-condition
    + meditative configuration
    → mode, timing, and locus of completion
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, a completed domain-transcendence establishes the invariant direction, but it does not erase the morphology of the remaining movement. One End can ground several objectively distinct courses toward final completion.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.36 showed that destruction of the final desire-realm grade eliminates return to that domain. VAK 6.37 prevents us from mistaking that negative determination for an empty beyond. The non-returner's continuum remains positively differentiated by its Path-current, effort, meditative history, and destination.

The *ūrdhvasrotas* is especially important for the genesis of Path. A stream is not a substance transported through places; it is the ordered continuity of the movement itself. Its earlier cultivation determines which higher loci can become moments of that movement and where its completion can occur.

The fivefold classification and the mixed-dhyāna Akaniṣṭha relation belong to the kārikā. Their expansion, the effort analysis, rejected object-based explanation, comparative strength of Path and anuśaya, equivalence of stream and course, and cause/result subdivision belong to the Bhāṣya. The interpretation of the stream as the ordered continuity of movement and the explicit distinction from OM-Artha dhyāna are Organon editorial synthesis.
