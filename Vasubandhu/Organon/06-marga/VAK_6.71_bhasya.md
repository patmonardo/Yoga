# VAK_6.71 Bhāṣya — Purity Status and the First Two Grounds

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> anāsravāṇi bodhyaṅgamārgāṅgāni dvidhetare |
> sakalāḥ prathame dhyāne anāgamye prītivarjitāḥ || 6.71 ||

> The awakening factors and Path factors are uncontaminated; the others are twofold. All are present in the first dhyāna; in preliminary concentration, all but joy are present.

The passage first distinguishes purity-status, then counts factors by meditative ground.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The commentary now asks how many Bodhi-aligned dharmas have outflows and how many are without outflows. The awakening factors and the factors of the Path are uncontaminated, because these designations are established for the Paths of cultivation and Seeing. Mundane right view and similar qualities also exist, but they do not receive the name “Noble Path.” The other Bodhi-aligned factors are twofold, admitting both contaminated and uncontaminated forms.

It then asks how many factors occur in each ground. All thirty-seven are present in the first dhyāna. In preliminary concentration they are present except for joy. Why is joy absent there? The neighboring, preliminary attainments are carried through effort and remain apprehensive about the lower ground.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The passage distinguishes strict noble Path designations from mundane counterparts, while allowing the remaining groups both purity-statuses. It then changes the counting criterion to meditative ground: the first dhyāna supports all thirty-seven, whereas preliminary concentration lacks joy. The closing question supplies an operative reason for that absence and leads into 6.72's further ground map.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Anāsrava` is uncontaminated and `sāsrava` contaminated. The technical Bodhyaṅga and Mārgāṅga groups are uncontaminated in their noble Path determination; the existence of mundane right view does not make it a noble Path-factor. `Dvidhā itare` applies both purity-statuses to the other groups. `Anāgamya` is preliminary concentration, not the person-type Anāgāmin. The source's explanation of missing joy involves both effort and unease toward the lower ground.

**Organon determination.** A named function, its purity-status, and the ground that can support it are different relations of one Path-system. The first dhyāna and preliminary concentration make different configurations possible. This is a project synthesis of the two explicit questions and their answers.
