# VAK 6.79 — Object, Efficacy, and the Fourfold Path-Conclusion

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

निर्विद्यते दुःखहेतुक्षान्तिज्ञानैर्विरज्यते ।
सर्वैर्जहाति यैरेवं चतुष्कोटिकसम्भवः ॥ ६.७९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

nirvidyate duḥkha-hetu-kṣānti-jñānair virajyate |
sarvair jahāti yair evaṃ catuṣkoṭika-saṃbhavaḥ || 6.79 ||

The final verse distinguishes disenchantment from dispassion. Their relation cannot be inferred from truth-cognition alone; it depends jointly on the cognition’s object and whether that cognition actually abandons an affliction.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

nirvidyate | duḥkha-hetu-kṣānti-jñānaiḥ | virajyate |
sarvaiḥ | jahāti | yaiḥ | evam | catuṣkoṭika-saṃbhavaḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **nirvidyate** | third-person singular present middle of *nir-√vid* | becomes disenchanted, turns away through comprehension |
| **duḥkha-hetu** | compound | suffering and its cause, explained as duḥkha and samudaya |
| **kṣānti-jñānaiḥ** | neuter instrumental plural compound | through receptivities and knowledges |
| **virajyate** | third-person singular present middle of *vi-√rañj* | becomes dispassionate, freed from attachment |
| **sarvaiḥ** | instrumental plural | through any of the receptivities and knowledges of all Four Truths |
| **jahāti** | third-person singular present active of *√hā* | abandons |
| **yaiḥ** | relative instrumental plural | through those by which [one abandons afflictions] |
| **evam** | indeclinable | thus, in this manner |
| **catuṣkoṭika-saṃbhavaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | possibility of a four-cornered analysis |

The Kārikā’s **sarvaiḥ** is restricted by **yaiḥ [kleśān] jahāti**. The Bhāṣya makes explicit that not every cognition of every Truth automatically produces virāga.

## 4. Grammar

The first instrumental compound limits **nirvidyate**:

```text
duḥkha- and samudaya-kṣāntis and jñānas
    → disenchantment
```

The second construction combines a universal instrumental with a restrictive relative:

```text
sarvaiḥ
    through [cognitions of] all Four Truths

yaiḥ kleśān jahāti
    insofar as they actually abandon afflictions

    → virāga
```

Because object-class and abandoning efficacy vary independently, **evam** introduces the resulting **catuṣkoṭika**.

The verse does not use *nirveda*, *virāga*, and *prahāṇa* as synonyms. Its verbal relations make actual abandonment the additional condition for dispassion.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

One becomes disenchanted through the receptivities and knowledges concerning suffering and its cause; one becomes dispassionate through all those by which one abandons. Thus a fourfold alternative is possible.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Disenchantment arises only through the receptivities and knowledges whose objects are suffering and origination. Dispassion can arise through receptivities and knowledges concerning any of the Four Truths, but only when those cognitions actually abandon afflictions. Consequently there can be disenchantment without dispassion, dispassion without disenchantment, both, or neither, according to object and efficacy.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

What cognition means practically is not exhausted by the Truth it represents. Suffering and origination disclose an object appropriate to radical turning-away; cessation and Path disclose an object appropriate to gladness. Yet liberation depends upon whether cognition functions efficaciously to abandon an affliction.

The chapter therefore ends with exact discrimination rather than an undifferentiated state of awakening: object, affective-practical orientation, and abandoning power coincide in some Path-cognitions and separate in others.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Nirveda**

The Bhāṣya states:

```text
duḥkha-kṣānti and duḥkha-jñāna
samudaya-kṣānti and samudaya-jñāna
    → nirveda
```

The reason is objectual:

```text
duḥkha and samudaya
    are nirveda-vastu
    objects or bases appropriate to disenchantment
```

Nirveda is therefore not irrational disgust. It is the discriminatively grounded reversal produced by comprehending suffering and the causal ground of its arising.

**Hetu means samudaya here**

The Kārikā’s **duḥkha-hetu** is unpacked by the Bhāṣya as:

```text
duḥkha
    the Truth of suffering

samudaya
    the Truth of origination
```

“Cause” must therefore retain its Truth-structural determination rather than becoming any generic causal explanation.

**Virāga**

The Bhāṣya gives virāga a wider possible object-range but a stricter efficacy-condition:

```text
cognition of any of the Four Truths
    + actual abandonment of kleśa
        → virāga
```

Accordingly:

```text
seeing a Truth
    ≠ necessarily becoming dispassionate
```

The cognition must perform the Path-function of abandonment in that practitioner at that moment.

**Nirodha and Mārga as objects of gladness**

The receptivities and knowledges concerning cessation and Path take a **prāmodya-vastu**, an object of gladness or rejoicing:

```text
nirodha
    release realized as possible and actual

mārga
    the effective course to that release

together
    not objects of disenchantment
```

They can nevertheless produce virāga when they abandon kleśas.

**Prahāṇa as efficacy**

Prahāṇa is not another affective attitude:

```text
nirveda
    orientation of turning away

virāga
    dispassion where attachment is effectively removed

prahāṇa
    actual abandoning operation
```

Virāga in this passage is indexed to prahāṇa. Nirveda is indexed to the object.

**The catuṣkoṭi**

The two variables are:

```text
Object supports nirveda?
    yes: duḥkha / samudaya
    no: nirodha / mārga

Cognition abandons kleśa?
    yes: virāga
    no: no virāga
```

Their crossing yields:

| Corner | Object | Abandons kleśa? | Result |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Nirveda only | duḥkha or samudaya | no | disenchantment without dispassion |
| Virāga only | nirodha or mārga | yes | dispassion without disenchantment |
| Both | duḥkha or samudaya | yes | disenchantment and dispassion |
| Neither | nirodha or mārga | no | neither |

This is a logical matrix of Path-functions, not a metaphysical doctrine that every proposition must have four truth-values.

**Why the same cognition-type may differ in efficacy**

The Path-position matters. The Bhāṣya illustrates cognition without new abandonment through:

```text
one already dispassionate
    seeing the Truths
    without another kleśa being abandoned

knowledges included in
    prayoga-mārga
    vimukti-mārga
    viśeṣa-mārga
    where the present function is not abandonment
```

Thus a cognition’s propositional object does not fix its entire practical operation.

**Return to the fourfold Mārga**

The chapter’s final distinction reconnects with 6.65:

```text
prayoga
    prepares abandonment

ānantarya
    performs abandonment

vimukti
    first arises free

viśeṣa
    develops freedom further
```

Only the appropriate abandoning cognition performs prahāṇa. Other Path-cognitions may know the same Truths while functioning as application, release, or further development.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
N(x) = x takes duḥkha or samudaya
       as nirveda-object

V(x) = x actually abandons a kleśa
       and therefore produces virāga
```

Then:

```text
N(x) and not V(x)
    → nirveda only

not N(x) and V(x)
    → virāga only

N(x) and V(x)
    → both

not N(x) and not V(x)
    → neither
```

The general semantic rule is:

```text
SameTruthObject(x, y)
    does not entail
SamePathEfficacy(x, y)
```

And:

```text
TruthCognition
    + DeterminatePathPosition
    → PracticalFunction
```

The catuṣkoṭi is possible because object and efficacy are irreducible coordinates.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Mārga chapter closes by identifying the precise unit of Practical Meaning:

```text
what cognition knows
    object

what orientation it generates
    nirveda or prāmodya

what it actually accomplishes
    prahāṇa or non-abandonment

where it operates
    determinate Path-position
```

No isolated proposition about a Truth determines the whole. The same content can function differently depending on whether it is Receptivity, Knowledge, Application, Immediacy, Release, or Further Distinction. Meaning is the cognition’s systematic operation.

This is the final form of the Path as Ideal Copula: cognition mediates object and transformation only when its position gives it efficacy. Seeing suffering can turn away without yet liberating; seeing cessation can liberate without producing disenchantment; sometimes cognition does both, and sometimes neither.

That copular formulation is the Organon reconstruction. Vasubandhu’s own conclusion is the disciplined catuṣkoṭi: nirveda depends on the Truth-object, virāga on actual kleśa-abandonment, and their crossing yields four possible cases.
