# VAK_6.07

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
विपर्यासात्तद्विपक्षौ त्रिधात्वाप्तावमलौ च तौ ।
अलोभ आर्यवंशाश्च तेषां तुष्ट्यात्मकास्त्रयः ॥ ६.७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
viparyāsāt tad-vipakṣau tri-dhātv-āptāv amalau ca tau |
alobha āryavaṃśāś ca teṣāṃ tuṣṭy-ātmakās trayaḥ || 6.7 ||
```

VAK 6.07 positively determines the counterforces to the two obstacles analyzed
in 6.06. Contentment and fewness of desires oppose discontent and great desire.
They extend across all three domains, may also be uncontaminated, and share
non-greed as their intrinsic nature. The verse then introduces the noble
lineages, three of which have contentment as their nature.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

alobhaḥ | ārya-vaṃśāḥ | ca | teṣām | tuṣṭi-ātmakāḥ | trayaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |

| `viparyāsāt` | ablative singular masculine | by reversal or inversion |
| `tad-vipakṣau` | nominative dual masculine compound | the two counterforces to those |
| `tri-dhātu-āptau` | nominative dual masculine compound | belonging to the three domains |
| `amalau` | nominative dual masculine | stainless or uncontaminated |
| `ca` | conjunction | and, also |
| `tau` | nominative dual masculine demonstrative | those two |
| `alobhaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | non-greed |
| `ārya-vaṃśāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | noble lineages |

| `tuṣṭi-ātmakāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | having contentment as their nature |
| `trayaḥ` | nominative plural masculine | three |

`Tad` refers back to discontent and great desire. The Bhāṣya explicitly names
their counterforces as `saṃtuṣṭi` and `alpecchatā`. The dual forms
`vipakṣau`, `āptau`, `amalau`, and `tau` continue to govern these two.

## 4. Grammar

The opening derives the counterforces relationally:

```text
viparyāsāt
    by reversal

tad-vipakṣau
    the two counterforces to those [two defects]
```

The Bhāṣya resolves the pair:

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi
    ↔ saṃtuṣṭi

mahecchatā
    ↔ alpecchatā
```

The next predicates state their range and status:

```text
tri-dhātv-āptau
    belonging to all three domains

amalau ca
    and also uncontaminated
```

The prose clarifies the complete contrast: the two counterforces may be
contaminated or uncontaminated, whereas the two defects belong only to the
desire domain.

The nominal predication `alobhaḥ [anayoḥ svabhāvaḥ]` is completed by the
Bhāṣya's explicit question about intrinsic nature. The final clause begins a
new classification:

```text
āryavaṃśāḥ [catvāraḥ]
    the noble lineages [are four]

teṣāṃ trayaḥ
    three among them

tuṣṭyātmakāḥ
    have contentment as their nature
```

The number four and the particular objects of the three forms of contentment
are Bhāṣya expansions.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> By reversal, the two counterforces to those [two defects] belong to the three
> domains and are also uncontaminated. Those two are non-greed. There are also
> the noble lineages; among them, three have contentment as their nature.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The positive counterforces obtained by reversing discontent and great desire
> are contentment and fewness of desires. Unlike the defects, which are confined
> to the desire domain, these counterforces extend through all three domains and
> may be either contaminated or uncontaminated. Both have non-greed as their
> intrinsic nature. The same non-greed is articulated in four noble lineages:
> contentment with robes, alms-food, and lodging, followed by delight in the
> cultivation of abandonment.

The full domain contrast, the fourfold count, the objects of the first three,
and the name of the fourth lineage are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The Path does not answer appropriation with mere deprivation. It generates
> determinate counterforces. Contentment interrupts greed after possession;
> fewness of desires interrupts it before acquisition. These are two practical
> functions of one root, non-greed, whose range can extend beyond the domain in
> which the opposed defects arise and enter the uncontaminated Path itself.
> Organized as noble lineage, this counterforce becomes a reproducible form of
> life from which noble realization can arise.

The progression is:

```text
defect
    → determinate counterforce
    → common intrinsic nature
    → organized Path-practice
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Viparyāsa` — reversal**

Here the term gives the operation by which the two counterforces are derived
from the preceding defects. It should not be automatically read as the
standard doctrinal set of cognitive inversions.

**`Vipakṣa` / `pratipakṣa` — counterforce**

The kārikā uses `vipakṣa`; the Bhāṣya describes the same positive opposition
with `pratipakṣa`. The Path does not merely register that a defect is absent;
it develops the contrary determination capable of opposing it.

**`Saṃtuṣṭi` — contentment**

The counterforce to discontent. In the light of 6.06, it concerns the relation
to what is already possessed: the present object is not converted into a basis
for vexation and indefinite demand.

**`Alpecchatā` — fewness of desires**

The counterforce to great desire. It concerns the unpossessed field and limits
projection toward superior and abundant acquisitions. It is a transformation
of desire's range, not merely the accidental fact of owning little.

**`Tri-dhātv-āpta` — belonging to the three domains**

Contentment and fewness of desires may occur in the desire, form, and formless
domains. Their scope is wider than that of the defects, which the Bhāṣya
confines to the desire domain.

**`Amala` — stainless or uncontaminated**

The two counterforces may enter the uncontaminated Path. The Bhāṣya's complete
formulation permits both contaminated and uncontaminated instances; the virtue
name alone therefore does not determine Path-status without further context.

**`Alobha` — non-greed**

The one wholesome root that constitutes the intrinsic nature of both
contentment and fewness of desires. Their difference lies in practical
relation, not in two independent root natures:

```text
alobha in relation to what is possessed
    → saṃtuṣṭi

alobha in relation to what is unpossessed
    → alpecchatā
```

**`Āryavaṃśa` — noble lineage**

Not a biological descent. The Bhāṣya explains that they are called lineages of
the noble because noble persons arise from them. They are transmitted and
repeatable modes of life and practice with `alobha` as their nature.

**`Cīvara-saṃtuṣṭi` — contentment with robes**

The first noble lineage: non-greed articulated in relation to clothing.

**`Piṇḍapāta-saṃtuṣṭi` — contentment with alms-food**

The second noble lineage: non-greed articulated in relation to food.

**`Śayanāsana-saṃtuṣṭi` — contentment with lodging**

The third noble lineage: non-greed articulated in relation to a resting place
or lodging.

**`Prahāṇa-bhāvanā-rāmatā` — delight in cultivating abandonment**

The fourth noble lineage, named by the Bhāṣya here and developed in the next
verse. Its non-greed consists in turning away from craving for existence and
sensual desire. This study records only the determination supplied in the
present commentary and does not anticipate 6.08's fuller articulation.

## 8. Logical Determination

The counterforce relations are:

```text
Contentment
    counteracts Discontent

FewnessOfDesires
    counteracts GreatDesire
```

Their scopes are asymmetric:

```text
DomainRange(Discontent) = DesireDomain
DomainRange(GreatDesire) = DesireDomain

DomainRange(Contentment) ⊆ ThreeDomains
DomainRange(FewnessOfDesires) ⊆ ThreeDomains
```

Their contamination status is not fixed by their practical name:

```text
Contentment
    mayBe Sasrava
    mayBe Anasrava

FewnessOfDesires
    mayBe Sasrava
    mayBe Anasrava
```

Their common nature and functional differentiation are:

```text
IntrinsicNature(Contentment) = NonGreed
IntrinsicNature(FewnessOfDesires) = NonGreed

RelatesTo(Contentment, PossessedField)
RelatesTo(FewnessOfDesires, UnpossessedField)
```

The noble lineage structure is:

```text
NobleLineage
├── RobeContentment
├── AlmsFoodContentment
├── LodgingContentment
└── DelightInCultivationOfAbandonment

IntrinsicNature(each NobleLineage) = NonGreed
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Counterforce is positive determination.** The Path does not simply subtract
discontent and great desire. It generates contentment and fewness of desires as
operative contraries.

**One nature supports two functions.** `Alobha` becomes contentment or fewness
of desires according to whether it determines the possessed or unpossessed
field. Identity of intrinsic nature does not erase difference of relation.

**The counterforce exceeds the defect's range.** The defects are desire-domain
states, while their counterforces can belong throughout the three domains and
can become uncontaminated. Path-capacity is not a mirror image restricted to
the exact extension of what it opposes.

**Practical virtues do not automatically establish supramundane status.** Since
the same counterforces may be contaminated or uncontaminated, provenance and
Path-context remain necessary. This continues 6.01's demand to classify Path
by actual status and efficacy.

**Lineage is generated practice.** Noble persons arise from the
`āryavaṃśas`; lineage names an organized condition of noble genesis rather
than a hereditary essence.

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 6.07 gives Path Meaning the form of a positive
counter-determination. This is project synthesis:

```text
afflictive relation
    not merely deleted

counterforce
    generated with a determinate object-range

common root
    differentiated into practical functions

lineage
    stabilizes those functions as a form of life
```

The Ideal Copula here is not neutral linkage. It joins defect and Path through
the production of an adequate contrary: `lobha` is answered by `alobha`, and
`alobha` becomes concrete through contentment, fewness of desires, and noble
lineage. The Kośa thereby advances from the bhūmi of cultivation to its
positive practical organization.
