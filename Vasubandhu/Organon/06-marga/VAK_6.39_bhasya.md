# VAK_6.39 Bhāṣya — Nine Form-Going Types and Three Conditions

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> punas trīṃs trividhān kṛtvā nava rūpopagāḥ smṛtāḥ |
> tad-viśeṣaḥ punaḥ karma-kleśendriya-viśeṣataḥ || 6.39 ||

> Three form-going non-returner types—those completing in an
> intermediate existence, after rebirth, or along an upward course—are
> each divided three ways, yielding nine. Their different trajectories
> depend on when accumulated karma bears fruit, how strongly residual
> afflictions manifest, and the strength of the practitioner's faculty:
> milder affliction accompanies stronger faculty, and conversely.

The source supplies both halves of 6.39: a three-by-three count of
form-going non-returners and the grounds of their differentiation.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Having made the three types threefold again, nine form-going
non-returners are reckoned. Which three are divided? Those completing
in an intermediate existence, those completing after arising, and
upward-streamers. The first are differentiated by rapid, less rapid,
and delayed completion, with three illustrative comparisons. The
after-arising group comprises completion immediately after rebirth,
with exertion, and without exertion: all three complete after arising.
The upward-streamers are distinguished as leaper, half-leaper, and
traverser of every station. Alternatively, each of the three groups
may be distinguished by rapid, less rapid, and delayed completion.

Their difference depends on differences of karma, affliction, and
faculty. The three principal types and the nine subdivisions are
explained by the accumulation of action whose result is experienced
at coming-to-be, at rebirth, or in a later existence, respectively;
by mild, medium, and strong active manifestation of affliction; and
by strong, medium, and mild faculties. The inverse ordering of
manifest affliction and faculty is explicit.

The nine within these groups are differentiated as appropriate. For
the first two triads, affliction and faculty distinguish their members
as already described. For the final triad, the kind of karma bearing
fruit in a later existence is especially relevant. The commentary
concludes that, through the ninefold differentiation of affliction
and faculty, there are nine non-returners.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The commentary identifies three base form-going types and derives nine
by subdividing each three ways. It then names karma, active affliction,
and faculty as conditions of difference, gives their ordered ranges,
and allocates the variables across the first two and final triads.
An alternative speed-based subdivision is also preserved.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Karma` is differentiated by the timing of
its result; `kleśa` here is active manifestation (`samudācāra`);
`indriya` is operative faculty strength. The text correlates mild,
medium, and strong affliction with strong, medium, and mild faculty.
The second line's conditions do not erase the first line's count.
The first-half witness `rupopagāḥ` remains unaltered in §2, while the
study gives the normalized `rūpopagāḥ`.

**Organon determination.** The nine types arise from a structured
relation among karmic timing, afflictive activity, and faculty, within
three modes of form-realm completion. This is project synthesis; the
text's alternative subdivision should remain visible rather than
being collapsed into a single mandatory taxonomy.

## 6. Three Base Types and Nine Forms

| Base type | Threefold differentiation given in the prose | Relevant condition |
|---|---|---|
| Intermediate completion | Rapid, less rapid, delayed | Affliction and faculty |
| After-arising completion | Soon after arising, with exertion, without exertion | Affliction and faculty |
| Upward-streaming | Leaper, half-leaper, all-stations traverser | Later-result karma |

The Bhāṣya also allows each base type to be divided by speed of
completion. These are nine form-going non-returners, not nine fruits.
