# VAK_6.24 Bhāṣya — Prior Liberation-Root; Three Existences

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ābodheḥ sarvam ekatra dhyānāntye śāstṛkhaḍgayoḥ |
> prāk tebhyo mokṣabhāgīyaṃ kṣipraṃ mokṣas tribhir bhavaiḥ || 6.24 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> For the Teacher and the Rhinoceros-like [Pratyekabuddha], the whole [sequence]
> up to Awakening occurs in one [sitting], in the final dhyāna. Prior to those is
> what is conducive to liberation. In the rapid case, liberation occurs through
> three existences.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_6.24.md`](./VAK_6.24.md). Next: 6.25, anāsrava dharma-kṣānti.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The Teacher is the Buddha; the rhinoceros-like one, a Pratyekabuddha. For those
> two, from the penetration-roots through Awakening, the whole arises in one
> sitting, supported by the fourth dhyāna — unmoving, sharp samādhi. Awakening
> is knowledge of exhaustion and of non-arising; later. Others start the one
> sitting from the unattractive.
>
> Can one, in a first life of effort, produce the penetration-roots at once?
> No. What is conducive to liberation must arise before them. Fastest: three
> existences — first that root, second the penetration-roots, third the Noble
> Path. Seed, crop, fruit. Entry, ripening, release: the dharmatā of the series.
>
> That prior root they describe as hearing- and reflection-born, not
> cultivation-born. Three actions; mind is chief. Body and speech join when
> taken under that resolve. Even one alms-gift or one training-rule can throw
> such roots by the force of longing for liberation. First thrown among humans
> on the three continents.
>
> Digression closed. Direct-realization sequence was begun; Highest Dharmas
> stated; the rest next.

## 6. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `ekatra` = one sitting; `dhyānāntya` = fourth;
- cannot skip mokṣabhāgīya;
- three bhavas = fastest ordinary.
