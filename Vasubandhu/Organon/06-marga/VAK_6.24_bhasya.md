# VAK_6.24 Bhāṣya — One Sitting and the Prior Liberation Root

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ābodheḥ sarvam ekatra dhyānāntye śāstṛkhaḍgayoḥ |
> prāk tebhyo mokṣabhāgīyaṃ kṣipraṃ mokṣas tribhir bhavaiḥ || 6.24 ||

> For the Teacher and the rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha, the whole course up to
> Awakening unfolds in one sitting on the final dhyāna. Before these [roots],
> a root conducive to liberation [must arise]; in the quickest course,
> liberation occurs in three existences.

The commentary distinguishes the exceptional one-sitting course from the
prior root and the quickest three-life sequence.

## 3. Continuous Translation

The Teacher is the Buddha, and the rhinoceros-like one is a
Pratyekabuddha. For these two, the whole course from the roots conducive
to penetration through Awakening arises in a single sitting, relying on
the fourth dhyāna because its samādhi is steady and sharp. Awakening is
the knowings of exhaustion and non-arising; the commentary says it will
explain that later. Others say the single-sitting course begins already
with contemplation of the impure.

For those who admit a Pratyekabuddha other than the rhinoceros-like one,
redirection of such a lineage is not thereby excluded. The precise scope
of the transmitted compound at this point remains provisional.

Could someone who first applies effort in this life produce the roots
conducive to penetration immediately? No. A root conducive to liberation
must first be produced. At the quickest, liberation takes three lives:
in one, the liberation-conducive wholesome root is produced; in a second,
the penetration-conducive roots; in a third, the Noble Path. This is like
sowing seed, growing a crop, and producing fruit. The continuum enters
this Dharma process, matures, and is liberated in order.

The liberation-conducive root is described as hearing-born or
reflection-born, not cultivation-born. Of the three actions, mental action
is primary. Bodily and verbal action also become conducive to liberation
when embraced by that resolve. Even giving a single portion of alms or
undertaking one rule of training can project such roots through the power
of aspiration for liberation. They are first projected only among humans
of the three continents, where the necessary Prajñā and penetration are
available as appropriate; not elsewhere.

This has been a digression on what is conducive to liberation. The
commentary returns to the sequence of direct realization: it has reached
the Highest Mundane Dharmas and now proceeds to what remains.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya first explains the exceptional one-sitting course and reports
an alternative starting point. It then establishes the earlier
liberation-conducive root, unfolds the quickest three-life sequence,
specifies that root's mode of Prajñā and action, and returns to the
sequence of direct realization.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Śāstṛ` names the Buddha; `khaḍga` names a
rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha. `Ekatra` is explained by one sitting,
`dhyānāntya` by the fourth dhyāna, and `ābodheḥ` sets Awakening as the
terminus. `Mokṣabhāgīya` precedes `nirvedhabhāgīya`; its Prajñā is
hearing-born or reflection-born. `Kṣipram` bounds the quickest
three-existence course, not every practitioner's timetable. The reported
`apare` view and Pratyekabuddha qualification remain attributed.

**Organon determination.** A reflective Model may keep prior liberative
orientation, preparatory maturation, and realized Path operation as
separate stages, while representing a compressed one-sitting course without
erasing their dependency. Aspiration can give an outward act a liberative
causal direction. This is project synthesis.

## 6. Two Temporal Courses

| Course | Sequence | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher and rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha | Penetration-conducive roots through Awakening in one sitting | Fourth dhyāna's steady, sharp samādhi |
| Quickest three-life course | Liberation-conducive root; penetration-conducive roots; Noble Path | Entry, maturation, liberation in order |

The prior liberation-conducive root is not the same stage as the
cultivation-born penetration-conducive roots. The first may be produced
through hearing, reflection, and actions taken under liberative aspiration.
