# VAK_6.57 — Transformable Disposition and Modal Path-Capacity

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अतोऽसमयमुक्तोऽसौ दृष्टिप्राप्तान्वयश्च सः ।
तद्गोत्रा आदितः केचित्केचिदुत्तापनागताः ॥ ६.५७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ato ’samayamukto ’sau dṛṣṭiprāptānvayaś ca saḥ |
tadgotrā āditaḥ kecit kecid uttāpanāgatāḥ || 6.57 ||
```

The Bhāṣya makes two decisive clarifications. **uttāpana** is specifically the sharpening of the faculties, and **tadgotrāḥ** concerns the respective dispositions of all six Arhat-types rather than only the *akopyadharman*.


The running source presents the verse in three separate lines, with
`dṛṣṭipraptānvayaśca` and a compressed second half. The study
normalizes spacing without changing the expressed relations.
The Bhāṣya supplies the full phrase **indriyāṇām uttāpanā**,
sharpening of the faculties, and interprets the six type-names
through **saṃbhava**, capacity or possibility.

## 4. Grammar

**ataḥ** draws an inference from the preceding distinction: because the unshakeable Arhat does not depend upon suitable conditions for bringing concentration into presence, **asau** is **asamaya-muktaḥ**. The same subject is then predicated as **dṛṣṭiprāptānvayaḥ**, belonging to the developmental line of one formerly seeing-attained.

The paired construction **kecit ... kecit** divides the six Arhat dispositions by genesis. Some are **tadgotrāḥ āditaḥ**, of their respective type from the beginning; others are **uttāpanāgatāḥ**, having arrived at a stronger type through sharpening.

The second half does not assert that every Arhat begins weak and traverses all six types. It allows two routes:

```text
original disposition
    or
acquired stronger disposition through faculty-sharpening
```

The source's later realm comparison turns on this capacity reading;
it is not a claim that all six histories occur in one person.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Therefore this one is liberated without dependence on occasion
> and belongs to the line of the seeing-attained. Some have their
> respective disposition from the beginning; others reach it through
> sharpening.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The immovable arhat is liberated without waiting for a suitable
> occasion: concentration can become manifest at will. This arhat
> comes from the seeing-attained trainee line. Among the six arhat
> dispositions, some are present from the start; others are reached
> by sharpening the faculties, even up to immovability. The type
> names mark capacities—what can happen—not events that must occur.

### Translation note

The Bhāṣya explains `asamaya-mukta` by concentration becoming manifest
at will and `dṛṣṭiprāptānvaya` by prior seeing-attained training. It
glosses `uttāpana` as sharpening the faculties. The later prose
specifies six capacities and insists that a regression-susceptible
arhat need not regress and a penetration-capable one need not advance.
This modal reading admits all six types across the three realms.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The completed person bears both a lineage and a capacity. The unshakeable Arhat continues the seeing-attained line and can actualize concentration without waiting upon favorable conditions. Yet this stronger disposition need not have been fixed at the outset: the Path can sharpen the faculties and transform the very morphology through which liberation is possessed.

Classification therefore records a real modal power, not an immutable essence or a predetermined event. A person may be capable of regression without regressing, or capable of further penetration without making that advance.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Asamaya-vimukta` — liberated without occasion-dependence**

The term is the contrary of *samayavimukta*:

```text
samayavimukta
    concentration's presence depends
    upon suitable conditions

asamayavimukta
    no such occasion-dependence
    concentration becomes present at will
```

The Bhāṣya also records an alternative explanation concerning final liberation at death and the possibility or impossibility of regression. The governing determination remains independence from a suitable *samaya*, not an abstract timelessness.

**`Śraddhādhimukta / dṛṣṭiprāpta` — two prior trainee lines**

VAK 6.56–6.57 align the trainee-types and Arhat-types genetically:

```text
śraddhādhimukta
    → first five Arhat-types
    → condition-dependent liberation

dṛṣṭiprāpta
    → akopyadharman
    → liberation independent of occasion
```

This is a developmental derivation, not a claim that the final type is reducible to its earlier name.

**`Gotra` — transformable disposition**

Here **gotra** indicates a determinate spiritual disposition or capacity. Because the Bhāṣya explicitly permits movement among types through cultivation, it cannot be treated in this passage as an immutable personal essence:

```text
gotra
    = actual organization of Path-capacity

gotra
    ≠ necessarily fixed essence
```

**`Indriyāṇām uttāpana` — sharpening the faculties**

The mechanism of transformation is sharpening of the faculties. Vasubandhu gives the example of a *parihāṇadharman* becoming a *cetanādharman* and generalizes the possibility through the sequence up to *akopyadharmatā*.

```text
initial capacity
    → faculty-sharpening
    → stronger capacity
```

The six types are not six mandatory chronological stages. Some persons possess a stronger disposition originally.

**`Saṃbhava` — six type-names as capacities**

The Bhāṣya now defines the list introduced in 6.56:

- **parihāṇadharman** — one for whom regression is possible.
- **cetanādharman** — one capable of the operation expressed by *cetayitum*; the passage itself does not warrant a broader gloss.
- **anurakṣaṇādharman** — one capable of guarding the attainment.
- **sthitākampya** — one capable of remaining stable without active guarding unless a powerful regressive condition intervenes; without exertion, neither loss nor advance occurs.
- **prativedhanābhavya** — one capable of penetrating through to the unshakeable condition.
- **akopyadharman** — one for whom regression is impossible.

**`Sātatya / satkṛtya / indriya` — application and faculty**

The Bhāṣya traces these outcomes to continuity and intensity of application and to faculty-quality during training:

```text
first two
    deficient in continuous and intensive application

third
    continuous application

fourth
    intensive application

fifth
    both applications + dull faculties

sixth
    both applications + sharp faculties
```

The completed disposition thus retains the determinate history of how the Path was practiced.

**`Saṃbhava / avaśya` — possibility and necessity**

Vasubandhu explicitly says that the regression-susceptible person need not regress and the penetration-capable person need not advance. The names concern **saṃbhava**:

```text
type-name
    → what can occur

not
    → what must occur
```

On this modal interpretation, all six types can be admitted across all three existential domains.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse joins origin, transformation, and modal classification:

```text
ImmovableArhat(x)
    → formerly(x, SeeingAttained)
    → independentOf(x, SuitableOccasion)
    → canActualizeAtWill(x, Concentration)
```

Disposition has alternative genetic sources:

```text
Disposition(x, d)
    ← OriginalCapacity(x, d)

or

Disposition(x, d2)
    ← Disposition(x, d1)
      + Sharpens(x, Faculties)
      + StrongerThan(d2, d1)
```

But modality must be preserved:

```text
CanRegress(x)
    does not entail
ActuallyRegresses(x)

CanPenetrateFurther(y)
    does not entail
ActuallyPenetratesFurther(y)
```

Therefore the typology is neither an immutable ontology nor a deterministic event-sequence. It classifies the presently actual powers of a Path-formed continuum.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.57 makes *indriya* visibly morphological: faculty is not merely an instrument used by an otherwise finished subject. The organization and sharpening of faculties determine which Path-transformations are possible and how completed liberation can be actualized.

In the Organon reconstruction, the Path does not merely move an individual through externally fixed classes. It transforms the individual's capacity for movement itself. A stronger *gotra* is a new morphology of possible action, produced through prior continuity, intensity, and faculty-sharpening.

This synthesis remains controlled by Vasubandhu's modal caution. A capacity opens a course without necessitating its execution. The completed Path-person is therefore an actual determination of possibility: genetically formed, genuinely transformable, but never reducible to a prediction that the possible event must occur.
