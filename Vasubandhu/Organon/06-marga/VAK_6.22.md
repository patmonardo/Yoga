# VAK 6.22 — Regression, Reacquisition, and Non-Possession

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

आद्ये द्वे परिहाण्या च मौलेस्तत्रैव सत्यदृक् ।
अपूर्वाप्तिर्विहीनेषु हानी द्वे असमन्वितिः ॥ ६.२२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

ādye dve parihāṇyā ca maules tatraiva satyadṛk |
apūrvāptir vihīneṣu hānī dve asamanvitiḥ || 6.22 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **ādye dve** | neuter nominative dual | the first two preparatory roots: Heat and the Summits |
| **parihāṇyā** | feminine instrumental singular | through regression or decline |
| **ca** | connective particle | also; adds regression to the previously stated modes of loss |
| **mauleḥ** | genitive singular of *maula* | belonging to a principal dhyāna |

| **satya-dṛk** | masculine nominative singular compound | one who sees the Truths |
| **apūrva-āptiḥ** | feminine nominative singular compound | a new acquisition; acquisition of what did not exist immediately before |

| **hānī dve** | feminine nominative dual | the two losses: relinquishment and regression |
| **asamanvitiḥ** | feminine nominative singular | non-possession, no longer being endowed with the relevant dharmas |

The kārikā uses **asamanvitiḥ** for the common character of the two losses; the Bhāṣya formulates the same determination with the technical noun **asamanvāgama**.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause, **ādye dve parihāṇyā ca**, is elliptical: “the first two [are also lost] through regression.” The verb of relinquishment is carried forward from 6.21. The dual identifies Heat and the Summits, not Acceptance and the Highest Mundane Dharmas.

**maules tatraiva satyadṛk** is another compressed nominal statement. The understood subject is a practitioner whose factor conducive to penetration belongs to a principal dhyāna: “[one whose preparation is] from a principal [dhyāna] is a seer of the Truths there itself.” The Bhāṣya resolves **tatraiva** temporally as “in that very birth.”

In **apūrvāptir vihīneṣu**, the locative absolute-like expression supplies the condition: “when [the preparations] have been lost, there is a new acquisition.” The former dharmas do not return numerically; new dharmas are acquired.

The final equation **hānī dve asamanvitiḥ** has a dual subject and singular predicate: “the two losses are non-possession.” Their resultant status is common even though the Bhāṣya distinguishes their causes.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The first two [are lost] also through regression. [One whose preparation belongs] to a principal [dhyāna] sees the Truths there itself. When [the preparations] have been lost, there is a new acquisition. The two losses are non-possession.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Heat and the Summits may be lost through regression, whereas even an ordinary practitioner does not regress from Acceptance or the Highest Mundane Dharmas. Whoever produces the preparatory roots on a principal-dhyāna ground necessarily sees the Truths in that same life. If relinquished roots are later obtained again, the acquisition consists of numerically new dharmas. Relinquishment and regression both result in non-possession, although only regression is necessarily fault-conditioned.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The preparatory Path crosses a threshold of modal stability. Heat and Summit can still regress; Acceptance and the Highest Mundane Dharmas cannot. When the preparation is grounded in a principal dhyāna, its intensity determines a temporal consequence: seeing the Truths in that very life.

Yet stability does not imply the persistence of an identical cognitive entity. If a lost preparation is obtained again, a new dharma arises rather than the old one returning. Continuity belongs to the causal formation of the series, not to an enduring substance.

Finally, the cessation of possession must be read genetically. Regression and relinquishment have non-possession as their common result, but only regression is necessarily caused by a defect. A prior state may also be relinquished because a different or superior determination has superseded it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**parihāṇi**

**Parihāṇi** is regression or decline from an acquired attainment. The Bhāṣya states that it is produced by a fault or defect (*doṣakṛta*). Heat and Summit remain liable to it because they are unstable; Acceptance and the Highest Mundane Dharmas do not regress even when attained by an ordinary person.

**vihāni**

**Vihāni** is relinquishment or complete loss of possession. Unlike regression, it need not be fault-conditioned. The Bhāṣya allows it to occur through a different or superior quality-condition (*guṇaviśeṣa*), as when movement to another attainment entails leaving the prior ground.

The two forms therefore share an outcome but not necessarily an evaluation:

```text
parihāṇi → defect-conditioned → non-possession
vihāni   → may accompany transition or advancement → non-possession
```

**asamanviti and asamanvāgama**

Both terms express no longer possessing or being endowed with the relevant dharmas. This is the intrinsic characterization of both losses. It does not by itself state why possession ceased.

**principal dhyāna and same-life Truth-seeing**

**Maula** specifies a principal dhyāna, distinguishing it from preliminary and intermediate concentration. The Bhāṣya explains that preparation on such a ground leads necessarily to seeing the Truths in that birth because of intense impetus or urgency (*tīvra-saṃvega*).

The statement assigns the preparation a determinate temporal consequence within the doctrinal conditions of the passage; it should not be generalized beyond those conditions.

**apūrvāpti**

**Apūrvāpti** is new acquisition after previous loss. The reacquired preparation is numerically new. The Bhāṣya compares this to reacquiring prātimokṣa discipline through renewed effort.

Thus:

```text
former preparatory dharma → ceases
renewed conditions and effort → new preparatory dharma arises
```

**The residual consequence of Heat**

The Bhāṣya adds that even one who regresses after attaining Heat is determined toward final nirvāṇa and, provided no obstacle intervenes, is nearer to seeing the Truths than one possessing only a root conducive to liberation. Loss of present possession therefore does not erase every causal consequence of the earlier attainment.

This is not the survival of a permanent latent cognition. It is a changed causal orientation of the continuum.

## 8. Logical Determination

The four roots divide by regressibility:

| Preparatory root | Regressible? |
|---|---|
| Heat | yes |
| Summit | yes |
| Acceptance | no |
| Highest Mundane Dharmas | no |

Loss is then modeled by result and cause separately:

```text
common result:
    relinquishment → non-possession
    regression     → non-possession

different genesis:
    regression     → caused by defect
    relinquishment → may result from a superior transition
```

Reacquisition introduces numerical discontinuity within causal continuity:

```text
loss of Dharma₁
    → non-possession
    → renewed acquisition
    → arising of Dharma₂

Dharma₂ is not numerically identical with Dharma₁.
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, a Path-state is determined not only by present possession but by its **modal profile**: whether it can regress, what causes its loss, whether it can be reacquired, and which future possibilities remain after its cessation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.21 distinguished modes of relinquishment according to living continuum, meditative ground, and practitioner-status. VAK 6.22 adds a deeper distinction: not every loss is regression, and not every ceased state represents a reversal of the Path.

This prevents two opposed errors. One should not treat stable Path Meaning as a permanent entity surviving change, because reacquisition produces new dharmas. Nor should one treat every cessation as total erasure, because the prior attainment can alter the causal orientation and future possibilities of the continuum.

Practical Meaning is therefore historical without being substantial. Its actuality lies in a conditioned series whose acquisitions, losses, and reacquisitions transform what can follow. The Bhāṣya's final qualification concerning one who has regressed from Heat makes this especially clear: present non-possession and retained causal orientation are compatible.

The kārikā states the regressibility of the first two roots, same-life Truth-seeing from a principal-dhyāna preparation, new acquisition after loss, and non-possession as the nature of both losses. The stability of the higher two roots, intense impetus, the differing causes of the losses, and the residual orientation after regression from Heat are Bhāṣya determinations. “Modal profile” and “historical without being substantial” are Organon reconstructions.
