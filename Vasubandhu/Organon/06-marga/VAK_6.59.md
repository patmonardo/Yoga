# VAK 6.59 — Three Modes of Regression: Possession, Advance, and Actualization

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

परिहाणिस्त्रिधा ज्ञेया प्राप्ताप्राप्तोपभोगतः ।
अन्त्या शास्तुरकोप्यस्य मध्या चान्यस्य तु त्रिधा ॥ ६.५९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

parihāṇis tridhā jñeyā prāptāprāptopabhogataḥ |
antyā śāstur akopyasya madhyā cānyasya tu tridhā || 6.59 ||

The first-pass witness left the distribution of **antyā**, **madhyā**, and **anya** uncertain. The Bhāṣya resolves it: the Buddha is subject only to the final type, the unshakeable Arhat to the middle and final types, and another Arhat to all three.

The Bhāṣya expands the three members as **prāpta-parihāṇi**, **aprāpta-parihāṇi**, and **upabhoga-parihāṇi** and defines the last through failure to make an attained quality immediately present.

## 4. Grammar

The gerundive **jñeyā** governs the subject **parihāṇiḥ**: regression is to be understood threefold. The compound **prāptāprāptopabhogataḥ** distributes the three grounds or respects according to which the single general term is differentiated.

In the second line, **antyā** refers to the last of the ordered three and is predicated of **śāstuḥ**. For **akopyasya**, the Bhāṣya supplies both **madhyā** and the previously stated **antyā**. The adversative **tu** then introduces **anyasya ... tridhā**: for another Arhat, regression occurs in all three senses.

The correct distribution is therefore:

```text
Teacher
    → third only

akopyadharman
    → second + third

another Arhat
    → first + second + third
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Regression should be understood in three ways: with respect to what has been attained, what has not been attained, and enjoyment. The last belongs to the Teacher; the middle also belongs to the unshakeable one; but for another it is threefold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

“Regression” has three distinct meanings: actual loss of a quality already attained, failure to acquire a further attainable quality, and failure to bring an attained quality into present exercise. Only the third applies to the Buddha; the second and third apply to the unshakeable Arhat; another Arhat may undergo all three.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

A determination can fail in three non-equivalent respects. Its possession can be lost; its possible development can remain unrealized; or what remains securely possessed can fail to become presently manifest. Absence of exercise is not absence of possession, and failure of advance is neither.

The stronger Path-status is therefore not defined by uninterrupted empirical display. The Buddha may cease to manifest or enjoy a particular attained quality without losing it. The unshakeable Arhat may likewise fail to actualize a possession or to attain some further distinctive excellence without any regression from liberation itself.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The sūtra problem**

The Bhāṣya begins from scriptural statements allowing regression from superior pleasant abidings in the present life while denying regression from unshakeable liberation of mind. The apparent conflict arises only if **parihāṇi** is treated as one univocal event.

**prāpta-parihāṇi**

The strongest kind is actual loss of a quality already obtained:

```text
quality attained
    → possession ceases
```

This alone is regression from an achieved determination in the strict possessive sense.

**aprāpta-parihāṇi**

The middle kind is failure to obtain a further quality that remains capable of attainment:

```text
further attainable quality
    → not acquired

already possessed quality
    → need not be lost
```

For the unshakeable Arhat, the Bhāṣya explains this as failure to acquire a special quality belonging to a distinctive kind of person (**pudgala-viśeṣa-dharma**). Immovability does not imply possession of every possible excellence.

**upabhoga-parihāṇi**

The final kind occurs when an attained quality is not brought into immediate presence:

```text
quality remains possessed
    + not made presently manifest
    → upabhoga-parihāṇi
```

The Bhāṣya's verb is **saṃmukhīkaroti**, to make present or bring face-to-face. “Loss of enjoyment” must therefore not be mistaken for loss of acquisition.

**Distribution among persons**

```text
Buddha
    upabhoga-parihāṇi only

akopyadharman Arhat
    aprāpta-parihāṇi
    upabhoga-parihāṇi

another Arhat
    prāpta-parihāṇi
    aprāpta-parihāṇi
    upabhoga-parihāṇi
```

The first two rows are compatible with secure liberation because neither necessarily includes loss of that liberation.

**Uncontaminated liberation and the special person-type**

The Bhāṣya states in the concluding exchange that uncontaminated liberation is unshakeable, while the special establishment of the *akopyadharman* must be understood according to the prior classification. This compact remark continues the dispute of 6.58 and should not be expanded into a single uncontested theory.

It nevertheless requires a distinction between:

```text
anāsravā vimukti
    liberation considered in itself

akopyadharman
    a specially classified mode of the liberated person
```

The same adjective does not automatically erase the difference between a quality and a person-classification.

## 8. Logical Determination

The general predicate must be indexed to its object:

```text
Regression(x)
    → requiresKind(x)

kind(x) =
    LossOfPossession
    or FailureOfFurtherAcquisition
    or FailureOfPresentActualization
```

The three relations are non-equivalent:

```text
not Actualized(q, now)
    does not entail
not Possessed(q)

not Acquired(FurtherQuality)
    does not entail
lost(CurrentQuality)

lost(CurrentQuality)
    entails
not Possessed(CurrentQuality)
```

Accordingly, “regression” admits increasing scope:

```text
Buddha
    variation only in manifestation

ImmovableArhat
    variation in manifestation and further development

OtherArhat
    variation may reach possession itself
```

The Path-result must therefore be modeled across three axes: having, further becoming, and making-present.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.59 shows that actuality is not exhausted by present appearance. A Path-quality can be fully possessed without being manifest in the current moment. Likewise, a completed liberation can remain secure even where some further excellence is never acquired.

In the Organon reconstruction, these are three modes of Practical Meaning: a determination may belong to the agent's attained morphology, define a still-open course of possible development, or be enacted here and now. Conflating them would mistake absence of performance for absence of capacity, or blocked advance for loss of achieved form.

This synthesis remains subordinate to the Bhāṣya's immediate task: resolving apparently contradictory regression-statements by typing the object of loss. The passage does not erase the doctrinal dispute over Arhat-regression; it gives the distinctions without which that dispute cannot even be stated precisely.
