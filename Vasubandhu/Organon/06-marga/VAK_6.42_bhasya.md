# VAK_6.42 Bhāṣya — Intermingled Dhyāna and the Grain of Completion

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ākīryate caturthaṃ prāk niṣpatti-kṣaṇa-miśraṇāt |
>
> upapatti-vihārārthaṃ kleśa-bhīrutayāpi ca || 6.42 ||

> The fourth dhyāna is intermingled first because it is serviceable for every task. The practice can support rebirth in the Pure Abodes, pleasant abiding here, or protection from renewed affliction, according to the practitioner's faculty and fruit. The Vaibhāṣikas locate its completion in a contaminated moment between two uncontaminated ones; Vasubandhu holds that only a Buddha could mix individual moments, so for other practitioners completion is mastery of three continuous streams.

The commentary first describes intermingling the fourth dhyāna,
then distinguishes a Vaibhāṣika moment account from Vasubandhu's
stream-level account and allocates the purposes by practitioner.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Returning to the statement that a practitioner with intermingled
dhyāna goes to Akaniṣṭha: which dhyāna is intermingled first? The
fourth. It is a concentration serviceable for all work and foremost
among pleasant modes of practice. An arhat or non-returner enters the
uncontaminated fourth dhyāna in a continuous stream. On emerging,
that practitioner enters the same fourth dhyāna as a contaminated
stream and then enters the uncontaminated again. In preparation the
streams are shortened to two uncontaminated moments, two contaminated
moments, and two uncontaminated moments.

The verse says completion comes from mixing moments. According to the
Vaibhāṣikas, intermingling is complete when one contaminated moment
is made present immediately after an uncontaminated moment and
followed by another uncontaminated moment. The contaminated moment
is thus set between two uncontaminated ones. They compare the first
two with uninterrupted Paths and the third with a liberation Path.
After the fourth dhyāna has been intermingled, the others can be
intermingled through its power. It is first done in the three human
continents of the desire-realm; later, after a fall, it can be done
in the form realm.

But exact intermingling of moments is impossible for anyone other
than a Buddha. We understand completion instead as the ability to
enter the three continuous streams at will. Why intermix dhyāna?
For rebirth, present abiding, and wariness of affliction.

Sharp-faculty non-returners do so for rebirth in the Pure Abodes and
for pleasant abiding in this life. Mild-faculty non-returners also
do so through wariness of affliction, distancing the concentration
associated with savoring so as not to fall away. Sharp-faculty arhats
seek pleasant abiding in this life. Mild-faculty arhats also cultivate
it from wariness of affliction so as not to fall away.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The fourth dhyāna is chosen first for its functional range. A
Vaibhāṣika account moves from three continuous streams to a proposed
single-moment interleaving. Vasubandhu disputes the latter for
non-Buddhas and takes willing command of the three streams as the
attainable completion. The commentary then assigns rebirth, present
abiding, and protection against affliction according to fruit and
faculty.

## 5. Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Anāsrava` and `sāsrava` mark uncontaminated
and contaminated modes of the same fourth dhyāna. `Kṣaṇa-miśraṇa` is
the Vaibhāṣika completion claim, signaled by `iti vaibhāṣikāḥ`.
`Paśyāmaḥ` introduces Vasubandhu's alternative: completion through
voluntary entry into three streams. `Upapatti`, `vihāra`, and
`kleśabhīrutā` are allocated by practitioner and faculty in the prose.
The witness's damaged forms are not silently regularized.

**Organon determination.** A formal moment-level account can exceed
the faculty available to a practitioner. The reported practice has a
stream-level operation that may be mastered and different purposes
according to Path position. This is project synthesis.
