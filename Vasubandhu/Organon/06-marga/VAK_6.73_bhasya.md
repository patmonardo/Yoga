## 3. Continuous Translation

In the desire realm and at the summit of existence, the factors of awakening and the factors of the Path are absent. There are twenty-two Bodhi-aligned factors in each, because an uncontaminated Path is absent from both. At what stage should acquisition of verified confidence be understood for one proceeding among the Bodhi-aligned factors? At the Seeing of the three Truths there is acquisition of the verified confidences concerning ethical discipline and Dharma.

The next verse opens: at the direct realization of the Path, there is also acquisition concerning the Buddha and his Saṃgha. One directly realizing the Truths of suffering, origin, and cessation acquires verified confidence in Dharma and the ethical disciplines dear to the noble ones. One directly realizing the Path-truth acquires verified confidence in the Buddha and his community of disciples. Confidence in those two is confidence in the beyond-training dharmas constituting the Buddha and in the training and beyond-training dharmas constituting the Saṃgha. Because of “also,” one acquires the verified confidences concerning ethical discipline and Dharma as well.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The first sentence completes the classification by contemplative ground: both named grounds lack the two noble factor groups because the uncontaminated Path is absent there. The commentary then asks when verified confidence is acquired. Its answer begins with Seeing the first three Truths and the Dharma and ethical confidences. The opening of 6.74 supplies acquisition at the Path-truth and the remaining two confidence objects; its prose also explains the force of `api`.

## 5. Translation and Interpretation

**Translation decisions.** `Kāmadhātu` is the desire realm and `Bhavāgra` the summit of existence. `Bodhimārgāṅga` names the awakening and Noble Path factor groups, seven and eight respectively; their absence leaves twenty-two of the thirty-seven. `Avetyaprasāda` is verified confidence, acquired in relation to Truths directly seen. The verse's `trisatya` is suffering, origin, and cessation; the commentary supplies the names and the acquisition verb. Its account of Buddha and Saṃgha confidence and the force of `api` comments on the opening of 6.74.

**Organon determination.** The distribution by ground and the acquisition through Seeing are two distinct relations in one Path. A person's birth in the desire realm need not fix the contemplative ground to which a generated Path-dharma belongs. This distinction helps articulate the project interpretation; the immediate Sanskrit states the factor count and its reason, not a general account of practitioner birthplace.
