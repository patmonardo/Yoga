# VAK_3.69 Bhāṣya — Six Classes and Modes of Desire

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
tata ūrdhvaṃ vimāneṣu devāḥ kāmabhujas tu ṣaṭ /
dvandvāliṅganapāṇyāptihasitekṣitamaithunāḥ // 3.69 //
```

> Above that, devas dwell in celestial residences; but six classes enjoy
> sensual pleasures. Their sexual unions involve pairing, embrace,
> hand-contact, smile, and glance.

The Bhāṣya identifies the six kāmāvacara deva classes, distinguishes them from
higher devas, and distributes the five listed modes across the six classes.
It then adds a Vaibhāṣika reference to a measure of time, a relation between
object-intensity and passion, and a brief account of parentage. The ensuing
question about a divine child's apparent age begins the next unit.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Tata ūrdhvaṃ vimāneṣu devāḥ.

Tridaśebhya ūrdhvaṃ devā vimāneṣu pratiṣṭhitāḥ. Te punaḥ Yāmās Tuṣitā
Nirmāṇaratayaḥ Paranirmitavaśavartinaś ca, Brahmakāyikādayaś ca pūrvoktāḥ
ṣoḍaśasthānāntaragatāḥ. Ity ete dvāviṃśatidevanikāyāḥ samāsena yeṣāṃ
bhājanaṃ prajñāyate.

Kāmabhujas tu ṣaṭ.

Teṣāṃ tu ṣaṭ kāmāvacarā devanikāyāḥ kāmān paribhuñjate, na śeṣāḥ;
tadyathā Caturmahārājakāyikā yāvat Paranirmitavaśavartinaḥ.

Te punaḥ:

Dvandvāliṅganapāṇyāptihasitekṣitamaithunāḥ.

Dvandvena maithunaṃ bhūmisambaddhavāsinām, Cāturmahārājakāyikānāṃ
Trāyastriṃśānāṃ yathā manuṣyāṇām. Teṣāṃ tu vāyunirmokṣād dāhavigamaḥ,
śukrābhāvāt.

Āliṅganena maithunaṃ Yāmānām, āliṅganamātreṇa dāhavigamāt.
Pāṇisaṃprāptyā Tuṣitānām, hasitena Nirmāṇaratīnāṃ, prekṣitena
Paranirmitavaśavartinām.

Svavartināṃ sarveṣāṃ dvandvasamāpattyā.

Kālaparimāṇaṃ tu Prajñaptāv uktam iti Vaibhāṣikāḥ.

Yāvad yāvad viṣayāṇāṃ tīvrataratā, tāvat tāvad rāgo 'pi tīvrataraḥ.

Yasya devasya devyā vā utsaṅge devakumāro devakanyā vā jāyate, sa tayoḥ
putro bhavati, sā ca duhitā.
```

The witness contains routine spelling and joining irregularities, including
the form of `Trāyastriṃśa`, which are regularized for reading. The standalone
sentence `svavartināṃ sarveṣāṃ dvandvasamāpattyā` is retained, but its exact
referent is unclear in this excerpt; it is not folded into the preceding
class-by-class mapping. The reference to `kālaparimāṇa` is likewise preserved
without supplying a duration that this passage does not state.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Above the Thirty-Three, devas are established in celestial residences. These
are the Yāmas, Tuṣitas, Nirmāṇaratis, and Paranirmitavaśavartins, as well as
the previously described sixteen stations beginning with the Brahmakāyikas.
In brief, these are the twenty-two deva classes whose world-containers are
being described.

But six classes enjoy sensual pleasures. These six classes of devas who
belong to the kāma realm enjoy kāma; the remaining classes do not. They range
from the Cāturmahārājakāyikas through the Paranirmitavaśavartins.

Their sexual unions involve pairing, embrace, hand-contact, smile, and glance.
Among the earth-connected dwellers—the Cāturmahārājakāyikas and
Trāyastriṃśas—sexual union occurs by pairing, as among humans. For them,
however, sexual heat ceases through the release of wind, since there is no
semen.

Among the Yāmas, union occurs through an embrace, and an embrace alone
dispels the heat. Among the Tuṣitas it occurs through hand-contact; among the
Nirmāṇaratis, through a smile; and among the Paranirmitavaśavartins, through
a look.

The passage then adds that for all `svavartin` beings there is union through
pairing; the precise scope of this phrase is uncertain here. As for the
measure of time, the Vaibhāṣikas say that it is stated in the *Prajñapti*.

The more intense the objects become, the more intense passion becomes in
corresponding measure.

When a divine boy or girl arises in the lap of a god or goddess, the boy is
reckoned the son of those two, and the girl their daughter.

## 4. Commentary Movement and Voices

The Bhāṣya makes a distinction of scope before explaining the sexual modes:

1. It lists the devas established in residences above the Thirty-Three and
   summarizes twenty-two classes whose containers are under discussion.
2. It restricts enjoyment of kāma to six kāmāvacara classes.
3. It distributes the verse's modes of sexual union among those six.
4. It adds the temporal reference attributed to Vaibhāṣikas, the relation
   between object-intensity and passion, and a rule of parentage.

The class mapping and physical explanation are Vasubandhu's exposition. The
statement about temporal measure is specifically attributed to the
Vaibhāṣikas and their appeal to the *Prajñapti*. The phrase about
`svavartinām` is syntactically present in the witness, but this excerpt does
not establish its scope securely.

## 5. Six Classes and Five Modes

The Bhāṣya resolves the apparent mismatch between six classes and five modes:

| Deva class | Mode described |
|---|---|
| Cāturmahārājakāyika | Pairing, as among humans |
| Trāyastriṃśa | Pairing, as among humans |
| Yāma | Embrace |
| Tuṣita | Hand-contact |
| Nirmāṇarati | Smile |
| Paranirmitavaśavartin | Look or glance |

The first mode applies jointly to two classes; the remaining four apply one
by one. The list is thus distributive, not a one-to-one list of five classes.

The Bhāṣya calls the first two classes `bhūmisambaddhavāsin`, earth-connected
dwellers. Their mode resembles human sexual union, but the prose specifies
two differences: there is no semen, and sexual heat ceases with the release
of wind. The other four classes have the successively named modes, with heat
ceasing through the corresponding contact or perception.

## 6. Desire, Object, and Bodily Mediation

`Kāmabhujas tu ṣaṭ` restricts sensual enjoyment to six classes. The previous
statement about devas in celestial residences has a wider extension: some
higher classes dwell in residences without belonging to the six who enjoy
kāma.

The passage describes different modes through which sexual heat is dispelled.
It does not say that reduced bodily contact means diminished desire. Instead,
it adds that passion grows more intense as the objects of desire grow more
intense:

```text
greater intensity of objects → greater intensity of passion
```

Spatial rank, mode of contact, object-intensity, and passion are therefore
distinct determinations. The text's hierarchy of modes should not be
translated into a scale of liberation or moral progress.

## 7. Temporal Reference and Uncertain Clause

The Vaibhāṣikas say that the `kālaparimāṇa`, the temporal measure, is stated
in the *Prajñapti*. This excerpt does not give that measure, so the study
records the attribution without adding a duration.

The following line, `svavartināṃ sarveṣāṃ dvandvasamāpattyā`, literally
connects all `svavartins` with union through pairing. The compound's referent
is not made explicit here, and including it in the six-class table would
conflict with the immediately preceding differentiated mapping. It is
therefore kept visible as a textual clause and marked for review rather than
forced into a settled interpretation.

## 8. Parentage by Place of Arising

The final sentence says that a divine boy or girl arising in the lap of a god
or goddess is counted as their son or daughter. The commentary gives a
relational criterion of parentage tied to the locus of manifestation:

```text
child arises in the lap of a divine pair
    → boy is reckoned their son
    → girl is reckoned their daughter
```

This is a brief additional determination, not a separate account of
conception. The next question asks what age the child resembles; that question
belongs to VAK 3.70 and is not expanded here.

## 9. Organon Study

This unit distinguishes class membership from a particular capacity:

```text
deva class + residence
    does not by itself entail
enjoyment of kāma
```

Within the six classes that do enjoy kāma, the Bhāṣya further differentiates
the mode of contact, bodily response, object-intensity, and passion. For the
Organon, those should remain separate Features rather than be compressed into
a single rank field. The prose about the child's lap also shows how a
relational event can determine a social designation within the world being
described.

This is an Organon reconstruction of the passage's distinctions, not modern
terminology attributed to Vasubandhu. The uncertain `svavartin` clause remains
unresolved.
