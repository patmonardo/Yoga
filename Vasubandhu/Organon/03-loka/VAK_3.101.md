# VAK_3.101

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तदपक्षालसाधर्म्यात् न चतुर्थेऽस्त्यनिञ्जनात् ।
>
> न नित्यं सह सत्त्वेन तद्विमानोदयव्ययात् ॥ ३.१०१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tadapakṣālasādharmyāt na caturthe 'sty aniñjanāt /
>
> na nityaṃ saha sattvena tadvimānodayavyayāt // 3.101 //

The first pāda completes the explanation begun under VAK 3.100: a lower
dhyāna-world is vulnerable because its internal disturbance resembles the
external medium that destroys it. The second half blocks an invalid inference.
The fourth dhyāna's immunity from those three contractions is relative; its
abodes still arise and pass away with their beings.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
tat-apakṣāla-sādharmyāt     → tadapakṣālasādharmyāt
na                          → na
caturthe                    → caturthe
asti                        → 'sty (after -e)
aniñjanāt                   → aniñjanāt
na                          → na
nityam                      → nityaṃ (before s-)
saha                        → saha
sattvena                    → sattvena
tat-vimāna-udaya-vyayāt     → tadvimānodayavyayāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| tadapakṣālasādharmyāt | ablative neuter singular compound | because of similarity to that internal disturbance |
| caturthe | locative neuter singular | in the fourth dhyāna |
| asti | third-person singular present | there is |
| aniñjanāt | ablative neuter singular | because of non-movement or freedom from disturbance |
| nityam | nominative neuter singular | eternal; denied of the fourth-dhyāna receptacle |
| saha sattvena | instrumental construction | together with the being |
| tadvimānodayavyayāt | ablative masculine singular compound | because of the arising and disappearance of its celestial abode |

The Bhāṣya explains `aniñjana` through `āneñja`, “immovable” or
“unshaken,” and identifies the decisive absence as freedom from internal
`apakṣāla`. This is immunity from a specified failure-mode, not
unconditionedness.

## 4. Grammar

The first half joins the general explanation to its fourth-dhyāna exception:

```text
[saṃvartanī bhavati]
    tad-apakṣāla-sādharmyāt

na [saṃvartanī]
    caturthe asti
    aniñjanāt
```

> [The contractions occur] because of similarity to those disturbances; in
> the fourth [dhyāna] there is none, because it is unmoving.

The negative in the second half rejects permanence:

```text
[caturtha-dhyāna-bhājanam]
    na nityam

tat-vimāna-udaya-vyayāt
    saha sattvena
```

> [The fourth-dhyāna receptacle] is not eternal, because its abode arises and
> disappears together with the being.

The Bhāṣya supplies the subject `caturthadhyānabhājanam`. Its star-like image
also specifies that this is not one continuous plane: the abodes occupy
discontinuous locations and appear or disappear with their occupants.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> [The lower contractions occur] because of similarity to those disturbances;
> in the fourth [dhyāna] there is none, because it is unmoving. It is not
> eternal: its celestial abode arises and passes away together with the being.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Each of the lower dhyāna-worlds is vulnerable to an external contraction
> analogous to a disturbance internal to its meditative structure. The fourth
> dhyāna lacks such an internal disturbance and is therefore called immovable;
> no contraction by fire, water, or wind reaches it. This relative immunity
> does not make its receptacle eternal. Its abodes are discontinuously placed,
> like stars, and each celestial abode arises and disappears together with the
> being who inhabits it.

The internal/external correspondence, the Buddha's designation `āneñja`, and
the discontinuous star-like abodes are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Stability is always typed relative to a determinate mode of failure. The
> fourth dhyāna is stable because its organization lacks the internal
> disturbance corresponding to fire, water, or wind contraction. But absence
> of those failure-modes does not abolish conditioned arising and passing away.
> Its World is generated with the beings whose station it supports and
> vanishes with them. Relative invariance therefore remains inside produced
> Loka and never becomes an eternal ground.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| apakṣāla | disturbance / destabilizing factor | internal operation corresponding to external contraction |
| sādharmya | similarity of character | relation between internal instability and external destructive medium |
| āneñja / aniñjana | immovable / undisturbed | fourth dhyāna's freedom from the relevant internal disturbances |
| saṃvartanī | cosmic contraction | specified fire, water, or wind destruction absent at the fourth dhyāna |
| bhājana | receptacle-world | environmental support whose eternity is denied |
| vimāna | celestial abode | localized support arising and disappearing with its being |
| vicchinnasthānāntara | having discontinuous locations | Bhāṣya's denial of one continuous fourth-dhyāna plane |
| tārakāvat | like stars | image for spatially separated fourth-dhyāna abodes |
| Śuddhāvāsa | Pure Abode being | alternative explanation attributes fourth-dhyāna immunity to their power |

The Śuddhāvāsa explanation is introduced as another view (`ity apare`); it
must not replace Vasubandhu's main `apakṣāla` explanation.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Typed vulnerability

```text
InternalDisturbance(domain, type)
    → ExternalVulnerability(domain, type)

NoInternalDisturbance(fourth-dhyāna, fire/water/wind-type)
    → NoCorrespondingContraction(fourth-dhyāna)
```

The inference is restricted to the three named contraction-types. It does not
establish absolute indestructibility.

### Relative immunity does not entail eternity

```text
not destroyed by fire, water, or wind
    ↛ eternal

AbodeArisesWith(being)
    ∧ AbodePassesWith(being)
        → Conditioned(abode)
```

This is the logical center of the verse. A system can exclude one class of
failure while remaining generated and perishable through another operation.

### Discontinuous receptacle

```text
fourth dhyāna
    ≠ one continuous enduring plane

being arises at a location
    ↔ corresponding vimāna arises

being departs that location
    ↔ corresponding vimāna disappears
```

The receptacle is indexed to the beings it supports. Its apparent continuity
cannot be detached from the causal continuities occupying it.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.101 refuses the last easy escape from conditioned Loka. The fourth
dhyāna has a real and exact stability, but that predicate has a range:

```text
stable-with-respect-to
    fire / water / wind contraction

not stable-with-respect-to
    arising and passing with its being
```

The distinction matters methodically. To identify a domain's invariant is not
to convert that invariant into the Absolute. An invariant states what remains
unchanged under specified transformations; it says nothing by itself about
whether the domain is unproduced.

The star-like abodes also complete the chapter's account of enclosure. A
World-support need not be one homogeneous container laid out in advance. It
can arise locally with the station it supports:

```text
being's placement
    ↔ localized abode
        → determinate World-station
```

Thus Agent and World remain reciprocally determined even at the most stable
level of the form realm. The World does not subsist as eternal scenery after
the relevant continuity has departed.
