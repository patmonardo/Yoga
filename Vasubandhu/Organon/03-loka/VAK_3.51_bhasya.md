# VAK_3.51 Bhāṣya — Successive Halving and the Seven Śītā Seas

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ardhārdhahānir aṣṭāsu samocchrāyaghanāś ca te /
śītāḥ saptāntarāṇy eṣām ādyāśītisahasrikā // 3.51 //
```

> Across the eight outer heights there is successive diminution by halves,
> and each mountain's thickness equals its height. The seven intervals among
> the inner mountains are the Śītās; the first is eighty thousand yojanas
> wide.

The Bhāṣya generates the complete descending series from Sumeru through
Cakravāḍa, defines the equal-height-and-thickness rule, and identifies the
seven inner intervals as seas filled with water possessing eight qualities.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Ardhārdhahānir aṣṭāsu.

Jalād ūrdhvaṃ yāvān Sumerus, tato 'rdhena Yugandharaś catvāriṃśad
yojanasahasrāṇi. Tato 'rdhena Īśādhara ity evam anyeṣv apy ardhārdhahānir
veditavyā, yāvan Nimindharārdhenārdhatrayodaśottarāṇi trīṇi yojanaśatāni
Cakravāḍaḥ.

Samocchrāyaghanāś ca te.

Yāvān eva caiṣāṃ jalād ūrdhvaṃ samucchrāyas, tāvān eva ghano vistāra
ity arthaḥ.

Śītāḥ saptāntarāṇy eṣām.

Eṣāṃ ca Nimindharāntānāṃ parvatānāṃ saptāntarāṇi sapta Śītā ucyante,
pūrṇā aṣṭāṅgopetasya pānīyasya. Tad dhi pānīyaṃ śītalaṃ ca svādu ca laghu
ca mṛdu cācchaṃ ca niṣpratikaṃ ca, pibataś ca kaṇṭhaṃ na kṣiṇoti, pītaṃ ca
kukṣiṃ na vyābādhate.

Tāsāṃ ca punaḥ

ādyāśītisahasrikā.

Sumeruyugandharāntaraṃ prathamā Śītā, aśītir yojanasahasrāṇi vaipulyena.
```

The running witness is broadly intelligible but compresses the fractional
measure of Cakravāḍa. The continuous text regularizes punctuation and sandhi;
the exact numerical compound for 312½ yojanas remains provisional pending
comparison with critical witnesses.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Across the eight outer heights there is successive diminution by halves. The
height of Sumeru above the water is eighty thousand yojanas. Yugandhara is
half of that, forty thousand yojanas. Īśādhara is half of Yugandhara, and the
same successive halving is to be understood for the remaining mountains,
until Cakravāḍa, at half the height of Nimindhara, measures three hundred
twelve and one-half yojanas.

Their thickness is equal to their height. That is, for each mountain, its
horizontal thickness is equal to its elevation above the water.

The seven intervals among the mountains ending with Nimindhara are called the
seven Śītās. They are filled with water endowed with eight qualities. This
water is cool, sweet, light, soft, clear, and non-disagreeable; while being
drunk it does not injure the throat, and after being drunk it does not
distress the belly.

Of these seas, the first measures eighty thousand yojanas. The interval
between Sumeru and Yugandhara is the first Śītā, and its breadth is eighty
thousand yojanas.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

The commentary gives one quantitative system and then fills its intervals
qualitatively:

```text
Sumeru's emergent height
    → eight successive halvings outward
        → Cakravāḍa's height

each mountain
    → horizontal thickness equals emergent height

seven intervals through Nimindhara
    → seven Śītā seas
        → water possessing eight qualities

first interval
    → Sumeru–Yugandhara
        → width of 80,000 yojanas
```

Vasubandhu speaks directly throughout. No separate objector or alternative
school is introduced in this unit.

The prose distinguishes the complete mountain-height sequence, which reaches
Cakravāḍa, from the seven inner sea-intervals, which are counted only among
the mountains ending with Nimindhara.

## 5. The Successive-Halving Series

The generating rule begins with Sumeru's height above the water:

| Mountain | Height above water |
|---|---:|
| Sumeru | 80,000 yojanas |
| Yugandhara | 40,000 |
| Īśādhara | 20,000 |
| Khadiraka | 10,000 |
| Sudarśana | 5,000 |
| Aśvakarṇa | 2,500 |
| Vinitaka | 1,250 |
| Nimindhara | 625 |
| Cakravāḍa | 312½ |

The phrase `aṣṭāsu` refers to the eight successive reductions outward from
Sumeru, not to a sequence that stops with Nimindhara. This is confirmed by
the prose, which explicitly derives Cakravāḍa as half of Nimindhara.

Formally:

```text
H₀ = 80,000
Hₙ₊₁ = Hₙ / 2

after eight reductions:
H₈ = 312.5
```

The sequence is therefore centered upon an initial measure but extends
through the terminal enclosing mountain.

## 6. Equal Height and Thickness

The compound `samocchrāyaghana` is explained rather than left pictorially
vague:

```text
for each mountain:
    thickness or horizontal breadth
        = height above the water
```

`Ghana` here does not mean density in the modern physical sense. The Bhāṣya
glosses it by `vistāra`, extension or breadth. Each mountain thus preserves
one dimensional proportion even as its absolute scale is halved.

The two laws operate together:

```text
between successive mountains
    scale changes by one half

within each mountain
    breadth equals height
```

Variation across the series is governed by a fixed ratio, while the internal
height-to-thickness relation remains invariant.

## 7. The Seven Śītās and Their Eight Qualities

The relevant inner sequence runs from Sumeru through Nimindhara. Eight
mountains in that sequence create seven intervening regions:

```text
mountain / Śītā / mountain / Śītā ... / mountain
```

These intervals are not empty distances. Each is a positive water-domain
called a `Śītā`. Their water has eight determinations:

1. cool (`śītala`);
2. sweet (`svādu`);
3. light (`laghu`);
4. soft (`mṛdu`);
5. clear (`accha`);
6. non-disagreeable (`niṣpratika`, provisionally rendered);
7. it does not injure the throat while being drunk;
8. it does not distress the belly after being drunk.

The final two qualities are functional and temporally differentiated: one
concerns the act of drinking, the other its subsequent bodily effect. They
should not be collapsed into a generic statement that the water is
“healthy.”

`Aṣṭāṅgopeta pānīya` thus combines sensible qualities with effects upon an
embodied drinker.

## 8. The First Śītā and the Verse Boundary

The first Śītā is explicitly located and measured:

```text
inner boundary     Sumeru
outer boundary     Yugandhara
breadth             80,000 yojanas
```

Its width equals Sumeru's height above the water, but the passage states each
measure independently. Equality of value does not make height and breadth
the same dimension.

The term `vaipulya` fixes the 80,000 as the sea's breadth. VAK 3.52 will add a
different measure taken along its side, identify this first Śītā as the inner
ocean, and derive the widths of the remaining Śītās.

Those determinations must not be imported backward. VAK 3.51 establishes the
class of seven seas, their water-quality, and the first sea's cross-sectional
breadth; it does not yet give the complete ocean system.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The world-system is now governed by three coordinated forms of determination:

```text
recursive ratio
    each outer height is half the preceding one

structural invariant
    each mountain's thickness equals its height

positive interval
    each gap is a qualified water-domain
```

For the Organon, this distinguishes a generated series from a mere list. An
initial value, a transformation rule, and an invariant regenerate the
mountain measures; the intervals then require records of their own rather
than being represented as absences between objects.

An Ocular configuration would preserve:

```text
ordered members
successor relation
scale operation
per-member dimensional invariant
intervening domain
domain qualities and embodied effects
```

This is an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's formal vocabulary. It
also shows why a gap can be positively determined: the interval has
boundaries, measure, material content, sensible qualities, and practical
effects.

The resulting alternation is not the Fichtean Gap as a project concept, but
it supplies disciplined textual material for later comparison. The source's
own claim is concrete: seven water-domains articulate the spaces generated
by eight inner mountains.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete VAK 3.51 unit at 160.06–160.18: the eight
successive halvings from Sumeru through Cakravāḍa, the equality of each
mountain's height and thickness, the seven Śītā intervals through
Nimindhara, the eight qualities of their water, and the location and breadth
of the first Śītā.

This corrects the first-pass implication that the halving sequence ends at
Nimindhara. The running prose explicitly includes Cakravāḍa at half of
Nimindhara's height. No research witness was altered.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The fractional numerical
compound for Cakravāḍa, the local mountain-name forms, `ghana`, and especially
the precise sense of `niṣpratika` require comparison with critical Sanskrit,
Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations.

The Study stops before `ābhyantaraḥ samudro 'sau`, the opening of VAK 3.52.
It does not include the first Śītā's threefold lateral measure, the remaining
seas' successive widths, or the outer great ocean.
