# VAK_3.23 Bhāṣya — From Feeling to Acquisition-Oriented Pursuit

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vittiḥ prāk maithunāt tṛṣṇā bhogamaithunarāgiṇaḥ /
> upādānaṃ tu bhogānāṃ prāptaye paridhāvataḥ // 3.23 //

> Feeling extends until sexual passion becomes active. Craving is the state of
> one attached to sensual enjoyment and sexual union; appropriation is the
> state of one running about to obtain objects of enjoyment.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes three developmental functions: affective experience,
the active manifestation of passion, and practical pursuit organized around
acquisition.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> paricchedasāmarthye sati—
>
> vittiḥ prāk maithunāt /
>
> vedanāvasthā yāvan maithunarāgo na samudācarati /
>
> tṛṣṇā bhogamaithunarāgiṇaḥ /
>
> kāmaguṇamaithunarāgasamudācārāvasthā tṛṣṇety ucyate yāvan na
> tadviṣayaparyeṣṭim āpadyate /
>
> upādānaṃ tu bhogānāṃ prāptaye paridhāvataḥ // 3.23 //
>
> yasyām avasthāyāṃ viṣayaprāptaye paryeṣṭim āpannaḥ sarvato dhāvati,
> asāv avasthā upādānam ity ucyate /

This natural unit runs from the second statement at printed 132.11 through
the first statement at 132.18 in `Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The
inherited phrase `paricchedasāmarthye sati` links the unit to VAK 3.22: the
feeling-stage begins once the capacity for discrimination has arisen. The next
prose begins the definition of becoming in VAK 3.24 with `tathā ca
paridhāvan`, also at 132.18.

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The Sanskrit of this unit is
comparatively clear; no substantive conjectural repair has been introduced.
The running source remains unchanged.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Once the capacity for discrimination is present, “feeling extends until
sexual passion becomes active.” The stage of feeling continues for as long as
sexual passion has not become actively manifest.

“Craving is the state of one attached to sensual enjoyment and sexual union.”
The stage in which passion for sensual enjoyments and sexual union becomes
actively manifest is called craving. It continues until one enters into a
search for its object.

“Appropriation is the state of one running about to obtain objects of
enjoyment.” The stage in which one has entered upon a search to obtain the
object and runs everywhere for it is called appropriation.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

Vasubandhu speaks throughout. No objection or alternate school-position is
introduced. The unit differentiates three stages by the operation that has
become active:

```text
capacity for affective discrimination
    → feeling

passion actively manifests
    → craving

search for acquisition begins
    → appropriation
```

The boundaries are developmental and functional. They do not merely rank
three degrees of the same desire.

## 5. `Vittiḥ` as the Feeling-Stage

The Bhāṣya explicitly glosses `vittiḥ` as `vedanāvasthā`, the stage of
feeling. This settles the local sense of the less familiar verse-form.

Its lower boundary is inherited from VAK 3.22: the being has become capable
of discriminating the causes of the three kinds of feeling. Its upper boundary
is the active manifestation of sexual passion:

```text
affective discrimination is possible
    → feeling-stage
    → passion has not yet become active
```

Feeling is therefore not already craving. An object may be affectively
experienced without the motivational structure characteristic of `tṛṣṇā`
having become operative.

## 6. Craving as Manifest Passion

The decisive word is `samudācāra`: active occurrence or manifestation.
Craving is the state in which passion for sensual enjoyments and sexual union
has come into operation.

```text
vedanā
    the object is felt

rāgasamudācāra
    passion toward enjoyment becomes active
        → tṛṣṇā
```

The Bhāṣya also gives craving an upper boundary. It remains craving only until
the being enters into `paryeṣṭi`, a search for the desired object. Desire and
search are related but not identical operations.

## 7. Appropriation as Practical Pursuit

`Upādāna` begins when the desired object is posited as something to be
obtained and activity is reorganized around its acquisition:

```text
object desired
    → search for the object begins
    → pursuit extends in every direction
    → appropriation-stage
```

The vivid `sarvato dhāvati`, “runs everywhere,” makes the practical change
clear. Appropriation is not defined here as an invisible increment of craving;
it is craving that has entered acquisition-oriented activity.

This is a local stage-definition. It does not exhaust every doctrinal use of
`upādāna` elsewhere in the Kośa.

## 8. Bhāṣya Decisions for the Kārikā

The prose fixes three compressed expressions:

- `vitti` designates the `vedanā` stage.
- `prāk maithunāt` is explained through the non-manifestation and subsequent
  manifestation of sexual passion, rather than merely the first physical act.
- `tṛṣṇā` ends when active search for its object begins.
- `upādāna` is the stage of search directed toward obtaining the object.

The transition from craving to appropriation is consequently qualitative:
the object moves from being desired to functioning as the end of a practical
pursuit.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

This passage shows how a contacted world acquires practical force:

```text
object encountered
    → object felt
    → object desired
    → object pursued as something to obtain
```

For the Organon, the sequence distinguishes affective valuation,
motivational orientation, and goal-directed activity. These should not be
collapsed into one generic “preference” field. A rational-moral-agent dataset
would need to preserve at least the difference between what affects an agent,
what the agent desires, and what the agent is actively attempting to acquire.

Yet the teleology here is conditioned. The appearance of an end does not by
itself constitute freedom: the end has been supplied by craving, and the
field of action is organized as appropriation. This gives a precise source-
grounded contrast for a later Practical Reason study, where the determination
of an end by reason must be distinguished from pursuit generated by thirst.

The comparison is Organon reconstruction. Vasubandhu's own claim in this unit
is the narrower developmental analysis of feeling, craving, and
appropriation.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit from the second statement at
132.11 through the first statement at 132.18. It preserves the link from
discriminative capacity to feeling, the manifestation of passion as craving,
and the new practical operation—search for acquisition—
that defines appropriation.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional, especially the developmental
scope of `maithuna` and the relation of this stage-model to broader accounts
of the twelve members. These require comparison with critical Sanskrit,
Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations during the later learning
pass. The Study stops before the definition of becoming and does not enter
VAK 3.24.
