# VAK_3.55 Bhāṣya — Circular Godānīya, Square Kuru, and Human Form

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
godānīyaḥ sahasrāṇi sapta sārdhāni maṇḍalaḥ /
sārdhe dve madhyamasyāṣṭau caturasraḥ kuruḥ samaḥ // 3.55 //
```

> Godānīya measures seven and one-half thousand yojanas in total and is
> circular; its middle span is two and one-half thousand. Kuru measures eight
> thousand, is square, and is equal on every side.

The Bhāṣya locates Aparagodānīya west of Sumeru and Uttarakuru north of it,
resolves their compressed measurements, and adds that the faces of humans in
each continent correspond in shape to that continent.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Godānīyaḥ sahasrāṇi sapta sārdhāni maṇḍalaḥ.

Itaḥ paścimena Sumerupārśve 'paragodānīyo dvīpaḥ, sārdhāni sapta
yojanasahasrāṇi sākalyena. Maṇḍalaś cāsau pūrṇacandravat.

Sārdhe dve madhyamasya.

Madhyamasyārdhatṛtīye yojanasahasre.

Aṣṭau caturasraḥ Kuruḥ samaḥ.

Ihottareṇa Sumerupārśve Uttarakurudvīpaḥ. So 'ṣṭau yojanasahasrāṇi
sākalyena, caturasraḥ kṛtyāpīṭhikāvat. Sarveṣu ca pārśveṣu samo: yathaikaṃ
pārśvaṃ dviyojanasahasre, tathānyāni; stokam api nādhikam.

Yaś ca dvīpo yadākṛtis, tadākṛtīny eva tatra manuṣyāṇāṃ mukhāni.
```

The continuous text regularizes punctuation and evident sandhi. The simile
`kṛtyāpīṭhikāvat` is retained because its exact object and best English
rendering require critical comparison; it appears to compare Uttarakuru's
square form to a platform or seat used for a particular function.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Godānīya measures seven and one-half thousand yojanas and is circular. To the
west of here, on the western side of Sumeru, lies the continent
Aparagodānīya. Its total measure is seven and one-half thousand yojanas. It
is circular like the full moon.

Its middle span is two and one-half thousand yojanas.

Kuru measures eight thousand, is square, and is equal. To the north of here,
on the northern side of Sumeru, lies the continent Uttarakuru. Its total
measure is eight thousand yojanas. It is square, like the platform named in
the transmitted comparison. It is equal on all its sides: just as one side
measures two thousand yojanas, so do the others; not even a little is greater.

Whatever shape a continent has, the faces of the humans there have precisely
that shape.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

The unit completes the four-continent morphology in three movements:

```text
western Aparagodānīya
    → circular like the full moon
    → total measure 7,500
    → middle span 2,500

northern Uttarakuru
    → square
    → total measure 8,000
    → four exactly equal sides of 2,000

all four continents
    → continent shape corresponds to inhabitants' facial shape
```

Vasubandhu speaks directly throughout. There is no objector, alternative
school, or reported dissent in this unit.

The final sentence extends the scope beyond Godānīya and Kuru: `yaś ca dvīpo
yadākṛtiḥ` applies the correspondence to whatever form each continent has.

## 5. Aparagodānīya: Circle and Measure

The western continent is specified by direction, shape, and two coordinated
measures:

| Feature | Determination |
|---|---|
| position | west of Sumeru |
| name | Aparagodānīya |
| shape | circular, like the full moon |
| total measure | 7,500 yojanas |
| middle span | 2,500 yojanas |

The numbers preserve the threefold circular rule used earlier in the
cosmography:

```text
middle span 2,500 × 3
    = total encircling measure 7,500
```

`Sākalyena`, “in total,” does not by itself use the technical word
`parikṣepa`, circumference. The circular form and the 3:1 relation strongly
support reading the total as the encircling measure, but the translation
keeps “total measure” where the prose itself remains general.

`Pūrṇacandravat`, like the full moon, clarifies that `maṇḍala` describes a
complete circular form rather than merely an enclosing arrangement.

## 6. Uttarakuru: Square Equality

Uttarakuru is located north of Sumeru and measured through a different
geometrical operator:

| Feature | Determination |
|---|---|
| position | north of Sumeru |
| shape | square |
| total measure | 8,000 yojanas |
| each side | 2,000 yojanas |

Thus:

```text
4 equal sides × 2,000
    = total boundary measure 8,000
```

The Bhāṣya intensifies `sama`: all sides are equal, and none exceeds another
even slightly. Equality is not a loose resemblance but exact equality under
the stated side measure.

The comparison `kṛtyāpīṭhikāvat` may specify the familiar form of a square
platform. Because its precise cultural and lexical reference remains
uncertain in the working witness, the Study does not replace it with a more
specific ritual object without evidence.

## 7. Shape-Indexed Measurement

Godānīya and Kuru are not measured by one undifferentiated operation:

```text
circular Godānīya
    characteristic span: middle measure
    whole measure: three times that span

square Kuru
    characteristic span: side length
    whole measure: four times that side
```

The numerical meaning depends upon morphology. A value recorded only as
“total” loses the relation through which the whole is reconstructed.

Together with the preceding Studies, the four-continent system is:

| Relative position | Continent | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Jambūdvīpa's place in the sequence | Jambūdvīpa | cart-like |
| east | Prāgvideha | half-moon |
| west | Aparagodānīya | full-moon circle |
| north | Uttarakuru | equal square |

The exact southern assignment of Jambūdvīpa is standard in the broader
system but was not explicitly restated in the local 3.53–3.55 prose; this
table therefore avoids presenting it as new wording of the present unit.

## 8. Continent and Human Face

The Bhāṣya's closing statement establishes a formal correspondence:

```text
shape of continent
    ↔ shape of human faces in that continent
```

It does not say merely that people differ from continent to continent. Their
facial morphology corresponds specifically to the regional `ākṛti` already
described.

The grammatical formulation is correlational: whatever shape a given
continent has, the human faces there have that same shape. The passage does
not separately explain whether geography causes embodiment, whether both
share a karmic condition, or through what physical mechanism the
correspondence is produced.

Those causal possibilities must therefore remain open. The secure textual
determination is sameness of form across environmental region and embodied
appearance.

The claim belongs to the Bhāṣya's cosmological anthropology. It should be
reported as such rather than converted into modern biological or ethnic
classification.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

VAK 3.55 joins two kinds of configuration:

```text
shape ↔ appropriate measurement relation
shape ↔ corresponding embodied appearance
```

For the Organon Dataset, a numerical feature should retain the geometry that
makes it meaningful:

```text
entity
shape type
local generating measure
whole-measure operation
derived total
```

The final sentence then suggests a Domain–Agent correspondence, but only at
the level explicitly stated: regional form and facial form match. A stronger
claim that the Domain produces or constrains the Agent's form is an Organon
hypothesis, not Vasubandhu's local assertion.

This distinction is especially important for the project's concern with
Outflow and Projection. The text supplies a correspondence within the
manifest world; it does not yet identify whether the continent projects the
face, the inhabitants project the continent, or both arise from a shared
karmic configuration.

The disciplined result is nevertheless strong: `bhājanaloka` and
`sattvaloka` are not merely juxtaposed. At the level of form, the container
and its human inhabitants exhibit a systematic congruence.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete VAK 3.55 unit at 161.25–162.09:
Aparagodānīya's western position, full-moon circularity, total and middle
measures; Uttarakuru's northern position, exact square equality, total and
side measures; and the correspondence between continent shape and the faces
of its human inhabitants.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The precise geometrical
force of `sākalyena` and `madhyama`, the lexical reference of
`kṛtyāpīṭhikāvat`, and the doctrinal scope of the continent–face
correspondence require comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese,
and established translations.

The Study stops before the prose introduction to the intermediate islands at
162.10 and does not begin VAK 3.56.
