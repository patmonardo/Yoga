# VAK_3.48 Bhāṣya — Circumference and the First Mountains

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
samantatas tu triguṇaṃ tatra merur yugandharaḥ /
īśādhāraḥ khadirakaḥ sudarśanagiris tathā // 3.48 //
```

> Reckoned all around, its measure is threefold. There are Meru, Yugandhara,
> Īśādhāra, Khadiraka, and likewise Mount Sudarśana.

The first pāda completes the dimensional account begun in VAK 3.47. The
remaining pādas begin the enumeration of mountains established upon the
golden earth above the waters; the list continues in VAK 3.49.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Samantatas tu triguṇam.

Samantataḥ parikṣepeṇa tu parigaṇyamānaṃ triguṇaṃ jāyate:
ṣaṭtriṃśal lakṣā, daśa sahasrāṇi, sārdhāni ca trīṇi śatāni yojanānām.

Yac ca tat kāñcanamayaṃ mahīmaṇḍalam apām upariṣṭāt saṃniviṣṭam,

tatra merur yugandharaḥ /
īśādhāraḥ khadirakaḥ sudarśanagiris tathā.
```

The continuous text regularizes punctuation and numerical word division. The
local witness reads `īśādhāraḥ`; that form is retained rather than silently
replaced by the better-known `Īṣādhara` without critical comparison.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Reckoned all around, however, its measure is threefold. That is, when it is
calculated all around by circumference, the measure becomes three times as
great: thirty-six hundred-thousand, ten thousand, and three hundred fifty
yojanas.

Upon that golden earth-maṇḍala which is situated above the waters are Meru,
Yugandhara, Īśādhāra, Khadiraka, and likewise Mount Sudarśana.

The sentence and mountain enumeration continue into VAK 3.49. The present
translation stops with the five names transmitted in VAK 3.48 rather than
silently importing the remaining mountains or their spatial explanation.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

The compact unit makes two moves:

```text
transverse extent from 3.47
    → circumferential reckoning
        → threefold measure with exact total

golden earth above the waters
    → site of the mountain system
        → first five mountain names
```

Vasubandhu speaks in direct expository prose. No objector, alternative
school, or competing measurement is introduced here.

The movement from measurement to named geography is significant, but still
incomplete. The present passage locates the mountains upon the golden earth;
it does not yet state which mountain is central or how the others surround
it.

## 5. From Breadth to Circumference

The Bhāṣya glosses `samantataḥ` with `parikṣepeṇa`, “by circumference” or “by
the encircling measure.” It then treats `triguṇa` as a calculation performed
upon the transverse extent already established:

```text
transverse measure
    1,203,450 yojanas

circumferential rule
    × 3

circumference
    3,610,350 yojanas
```

The prose states this total in its own grouped notation:

```text
36 lakṣas       = 3,600,000
10 thousands    =    10,000
3½ hundreds     =       350
                   ---------
                     3,610,350 yojanas
```

The calculation employs the received approximation in which circumference
is three times the diameter or transverse measure. The text is specifying
the geometry internal to its cosmological model, not proposing a modern
value for π.

## 6. The Golden Earth as Mountain Support

The prose reidentifies the relevant field before naming its contents:

```text
remaining waters
    → support the golden earth-maṇḍala
        → upon it the mountains are situated
```

`Saṃniviṣṭa` marks the golden earth's placement above the waters. `Tatra`,
“there,” then refers to that earth-maṇḍala. This prevents the mountain list
from floating free of the layered construction developed through VAK
3.45–3.47.

The relation is one of support and placement. This verse does not repeat the
genesis of the golden earth, which 3.46 described as transformation of the
upper waters. It now treats that generated layer in its new function as the
ground of differentiated geography.

## 7. The Mountain Enumeration Begins

VAK 3.48 names:

1. Meru;
2. Yugandhara;
3. Īśādhāra in the local witness;
4. Khadiraka;
5. Sudarśana-giri.

The verse ends with `tathā`, “likewise” or “and also,” while the list
continues directly in VAK 3.49. Accordingly, these five should not be
mistaken for a complete mountain system.

The Bhāṣya's later statement that there are eight great mountains, with
Sumeru in the middle and the others positioned around it, follows only after
the next verse begins. Those determinations are important, but they are not
yet spoken within the exact 3.48 unit.

The spelling `Īśādhāra` is a witness fact here, not a settled emendation or
identification. The later learning pass should compare Sanskrit editions and
the Tibetan and Chinese witnesses before standardizing the mountain name.

## 8. Translation and Doctrinal Consequences

The pronoun implicit in “its measure” carries forward the water-and-gold
maṇḍala measured in 3.47. The first pāda therefore belongs grammatically to
the dimensional sequence even though the rest of the verse opens a new
geographical enumeration.

Two boundaries must be preserved:

```text
3.47
    equal transverse breadth of water and gold

3.48 first pāda
    threefold circumferential reckoning

3.48 remaining pādas
    first five mountain names on golden earth

3.49
    completion and explanation of the mountain system
```

“There” should be translated through its Bhāṣya-determined referent: upon the
golden earth above the waters. But the center-and-surrounding structure
should not be back-projected into the present translation from prose that
belongs after the opening of VAK 3.49.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The world-support undergoes a modest but exact transition:

```text
measured field
    → encircling measure
        → located material ground
            → named geographic contents
```

The circumference closes the horizontal field mathematically; the mountain
names begin to articulate it qualitatively. Yet an inventory of names does
not by itself constitute an oriented system. Center, enclosure, and relative
order will only be stated in the following unit.

For the Organon Dataset, VAK 3.48 requires separate records for:

```text
measurement operation
    circumference = 3 × transverse extent

support relation
    mountains are situated upon golden earth

enumeration status
    five members named, list still open
```

This is an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's formal terminology. The
“list still open” status matters: completing an inherited structure by
prediction would produce a counterfeit textual appearance, even if the
predicted names happened to be correct.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the exact VAK 3.48 unit at 158.19–158.24: the threefold
circumferential reckoning and its total, the golden earth above the waters as
the mountains' support, and the first five names of the continuing mountain
enumeration.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The numerical phrasing,
the relation between `samantataḥ` and `parikṣepa`, and especially the local
mountain name `īśādhāra` require comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan,
Chinese, and established translations.

The Study stops before `aśvakarṇo vinitako nimindharagiriḥ`, the opening of
VAK 3.49. It therefore does not yet assert the complete set of eight great
mountains, Sumeru's centrality, the surrounding ranges, the continents, or
Cakravāḍa.
