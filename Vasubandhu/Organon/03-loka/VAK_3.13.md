# VAK_3.13

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> एकाक्षेपादसावैष्यत्पूर्वकालभवाकृतिः ।
>
> स पुनर्मरणात्पूर्व उपपत्तिक्षणात्परः ॥ ३.१३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> ekākṣepād asāv aiṣyatpūrvakālabhavākṛtiḥ /
>
> sa punar maraṇāt pūrva upapattikṣaṇāt paraḥ // 3.13 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
eka-ākṣepāt                         → ekākṣepāt
asau                               → asau
aiṣyat-pūrva-kāla-bhava-ākṛtiḥ     → aiṣyatpūrvakālabhavākṛtiḥ
saḥ + punaḥ                        → sa punar
maraṇāt                            → maraṇāt
pūrvaḥ                             → pūrva
upapatti-kṣaṇāt                    → upapattikṣaṇāt
paraḥ                              → paraḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| ekākṣepāt | ablative singular compound | through one and the same projection |
| asau | nominative masculine singular pronoun | this intermediate existence |
| aiṣyat | future participial form in compound | going to be; coming |
| pūrvakālabhava | masculine compound | existence from the rebirth-moment until before death |
| ākṛtiḥ | nominative feminine singular | form; configuration |
| saḥ | nominative masculine singular pronoun | that `pūrvakālabhava` |
| punaḥ | indeclinable | in turn; now |
| maraṇāt pūrvaḥ | ablative with predicate adjective | before death |
| upapattikṣaṇāt paraḥ | ablative with predicate adjective | after the moment of rebirth |

The referent changes across the two halves. `Asau` points to `antarābhava`;
`saḥ` resumes the immediately preceding `pūrvakālabhava`. This distinction is
required by the Bhāṣya's definition and by the natural government of `pūrvaḥ`
and `paraḥ`.

## 4. Grammar

The first half determines the form of intermediate existence:

```text
asau [antarābhavaḥ]
    eka-ākṣepāt
    aiṣyat-pūrvakālabhava-ākṛtiḥ [asti]
```

> Through one and the same projection, this intermediate existence has the
> form of the coming prior-time existence.

The Bhāṣya identifies the single projecting principle as the same karma that
projects both the coming destiny and the intermediate existence directed
toward it.

The second half defines `pūrvakālabhava`:

```text
saḥ [pūrvakālabhavaḥ] punaḥ
    maraṇāt pūrvaḥ
    upapatti-kṣaṇāt paraḥ [asti]
```

> That prior-time existence, in turn, is before death and after the moment of
> rebirth.

It would invert the construction to take `saḥ` as intermediate existence and
read the phrase as “after death and before rebirth.” That temporal definition
belongs to VAK 3.10; it is not what the grammar of this second half states.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Through one and the same projection, this [intermediate existence] has the
> form of the coming prior-time existence. That [prior-time existence], in
> turn, is before death and after the moment of rebirth.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The same karma projects both a destiny and the intermediate existence that
> leads to it; consequently the intermediate being already bears the form of
> the life-phase that will arise in that destiny. This `pūrvakālabhava` extends
> from after the rebirth-junction until before the final death-moment.

The identification of karma as the one projection, and the precise extension
of the life-phase, are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Transition is already configured by its result without being identical with
> that result. One karmic projection determines both the destination and a
> corresponding intermediate form ordered toward it. The ensuing embodied
> life is then discriminated as a temporal-functional phase of the aggregates:
> after the rebirth-junction and before death.

## 8. Logical Determination

### One projection, two coordinated determinations

```text
one karma
    ├── projects future gati
    └── projects antarābhava for attaining that gati

therefore
    antarābhava.ākṛti
        corresponds to
    coming pūrvakālabhava.ākṛti
```

The intermediate form is anticipatory, not neutral:

```text
oriented toward future form
    ≠ already reborn

form-correspondence
    coordinates transition with its coming embodied phase
```

### Four temporal-functional modes of `bhava`

The Bhāṣya defines `bhava` generally as the five appropriated aggregates and
then discriminates four modes:

```text
upapattibhava
    the rebirth-junction moment
        ↓
pūrvakālabhava
    embodied aggregate-life after rebirth and before death
        ↓
maraṇabhava
    the final moment of that life
        ↓
antarābhava
    transition toward another rebirth among beings with form
```

These are functions and temporal positions within aggregate-continuity.

### The burning-womb objection

The Bhāṣya considers whether an intermediate being destined for hell would
burn a womb. Its answer restricts the crude physical picture:

```text
hell-beings are not invariably aflame
intermediate embodiment is subtle
ordinary visibility and tangibility do not apply
intermediate bodies do not physically adhere in the womb
karmic obstruction prevents burning
```

This is commentary-level defense, not content stated by the Kārikā. Its exact
physics remains a problem for direct Bhāṣya study.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.13 advances the World System from transition-continuity to
transition-configuration. The intermediate phase is not an empty corridor. It
is generated with a form already corresponding to the embodied course that
will follow.

```text
projected result
    determines destination
    and configures the transition toward it

transition-form
    prefigures successor embodiment
    without yet constituting rebirth
```

This is Transcendental Technē as project architecture. The World System does
not merely move an already finished Agent between locations; it configures the
mode through which the sentient continuity can arrive at its next embodiment.
The Rational Moral Agent here undergoes conditioned projection rather than
freely selecting its platform.

This comes close to the **Siva Trademark** problem of taking up bodies, but
only from below. The Kośa identifies a single projective law coordinating
destination and transitional embodiment. It does not yet disclose Rudra as a
free operator of manifestation and withdrawal. That higher reading remains a
declared Organon horizon.

The fourfold `bhava` analysis also gives us a cleaner state-machine:

```text
rebirth-junction
    → embodied life-course
    → death-moment
    → intermediate transition
    → rebirth-junction
```

But this is not merely a loop of locations. Each phase is a determination of
the five appropriated aggregates, and the coming embodied configuration is
already operative as the telos of the intermediate form.

The Bhāṣya has only been used here to establish those load-bearing relations.
Its subtle-body claims, karmic obstruction, and theory of projection still
require later direct study.
