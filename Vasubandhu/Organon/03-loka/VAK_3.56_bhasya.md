# VAK_3.56 Bhāṣya — The Eight Attendant Islands

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
dehā videhāḥ kuravaḥ kauravāś cāmarāvarāḥ /
aṣṭau tadantaradvīpā gāṭhā uttaramantriṇaḥ // 3.56 //
```

> Deha, Videha, Kuru, Kaurava, Cāmara, Avara, Gāṭha, and Uttaramantriṇa are
> the eight intermediate islands associated with the four great continents.

The Bhāṣya assigns the islands in pairs to the principal continents, states
that all are inhabited by humans, and records an alternative tradition
according to which one unspecified island is inhabited by rākṣasas.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Teṣāṃ khalu dvīpānām antarāle 'ntaradvīpā abhinirvṛttāḥ. Ke punas te,
kati cety āha:

dehā videhāḥ kuravaḥ kauravāś cāmarāvarāḥ /
aṣṭau tadantaradvīpā gāṭhā uttaramantriṇaḥ.

Tatra Dehavidehau Pūrvavidehaparivārau; Kurukauravāv Uttarakuroḥ;
Gāṭhottaramantriṇāv Aparagodānīyasya; Cāmarāvarau Jambūdvīpasya. Sarve
manuṣyair āvāsitāḥ.

Eko rākṣasair ity apare.
```

The running witness has the evident mechanical form `antaradbvīpā`, here
regularized to `antaradvīpā`. The eight proper names are otherwise retained
in their local witness forms pending critical comparison.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

In the spaces between those continents, intermediate islands have come into
formation. Which are they, and how many are there? The answer is: Deha,
Videha, Kuru, Kaurava, Cāmara, Avara, Gāṭha, and Uttaramantriṇa are their
eight intermediate islands.

Among them, Deha and Videha are the attendants of Pūrvavideha; Kuru and
Kaurava are the attendants of Uttarakuru; Gāṭha and Uttaramantriṇa are the
attendants of Aparagodānīya; and Cāmara and Avara are the attendants of
Jambūdvīpa. All are inhabited by humans.

Others say that one is inhabited by rākṣasas.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voices

The commentary performs three operations:

```text
interspaces among the great continents
    → formation of secondary islands

eight island names
    → reorganized as four attendant pairs

habitation
    → principal account: all human
    → alternative account: one rākṣasa island
```

Vasubandhu supplies the question, enumeration, pairings, and principal
habitation account. `Ity apare`, “others say,” explicitly marks the rākṣasa
claim as an alternative voice.

The target of the alternative is only `ekaḥ`, one island. Since it is not
named, no particular island should be assigned to rākṣasas by editorial
inference.

## 5. From Four Continents to Eight Islands

The verse names eight regions, while the prose supplies the relational
matrix:

| Principal continent | Attendant islands |
|---|---|
| Pūrvavideha | Deha and Videha |
| Uttarakuru | Kuru and Kaurava |
| Aparagodānīya | Gāṭha and Uttaramantriṇa |
| Jambūdvīpa | Cāmara and Avara |

Each principal continent therefore has exactly two attendants:

```text
4 principal continents × 2 attendant islands
    = 8 intermediate islands
```

The count is not an accidental total appended to the name-list. It is
generated by a uniform pairing relation across the already established
four-continent system.

## 6. `Antaradvīpa` and `Parivāra`

The two Bhāṣya terms describe different aspects of the same islands:

```text
antaradvīpa
    → intermediate island
    → spatial status in the interspaces

parivāra
    → attendant or satellite
    → relational status with respect to a great continent
```

The islands are not fifth through twelfth principal continents. They belong
to the same broad regional type, `dvīpa`, but occupy a subordinate role in
the organization.

Their identity therefore combines:

```text
proper name
spatial class
principal-continent association
```

The verb `abhinirvṛtta` also preserves their produced status. Like the larger
geographical structures, the intermediate islands have come into formation
within the container-world.

## 7. Habitation and the Reported Exception

The principal statement is universal:

```text
all eight intermediate islands
    → inhabited by humans
```

The alternative report changes exactly one part of that distribution:

```text
one unspecified island
    → inhabited by rākṣasas
```

The Bhāṣya does not say that humans and rākṣasas jointly inhabit that island,
nor that rākṣasas inhabit all eight. It also does not identify which member
of the list is meant.

The two accounts should remain separately attributed rather than flattened
into one harmonized statement. The principal rule and the dissenting variant
carry different provenance and scope.

`Rākṣasa` is retained as a class-name. Rendering it simply as “demon” would
import a broad comparative category and erase the specific cosmological
designation in the source.

## 8. Translation and Data Consequences

The surface order of the Kārikā does not display the pairings transparently.
In particular, Cāmara and Avara occur before the class-and-count phrase,
while Gāṭha and Uttaramantriṇa follow it. The Bhāṣya reorganizes all eight by
their attendant relation.

Thus a translation should first preserve the verse's complete enumeration
and then state the Bhāṣya's matrix. Reordering the names inside the quoted
Kārikā to match the prose would silently alter the witness.

The proper names also look morphologically plural in Sanskrit. In this
context they function as names of regional entities; an English translation
need not turn each into a population-name merely because of its ending.

Finally, the habitation variant requires three fields rather than one:

```text
claim          one island is rākṣasa-inhabited
authority      other teachers
target         unspecified member of the eight
```

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

VAK 3.56 moves from large-scale regional types toward greater particularity
without abandoning the organizing structure:

```text
principal continent
    → paired attendant regions
        → determinate habitation
```

For the Organon Dataset, a flat list of eight names would preserve syntax but
lose meaning. The Bhāṣya shows that the semantic record must include
association, role, inhabitant class, scope, and authority.

An Ocular representation would distinguish:

```text
island identity
principal continent
role as attendant
formation status
principal habitation account
reported alternative and its unresolved target
```

This is an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's formal vocabulary. It is
particularly useful for representing disagreement fairly: uncertainty about
which island is meant is itself positive information and should not be
“repaired” by guessing.

The passage is geographically derivative but methodologically exact. A
world-system becomes intelligible not by endlessly accumulating names, but
by preserving the relations that make each named region a member of one
configuration.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete VAK 3.56 unit at 162.10–162.16: formation
of the intermediate islands, their eight names, their four attendant pairs,
the principal human-habitation account, and the alternative report concerning
one unnamed rākṣasa-inhabited island.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The proper-name forms,
the exact spatial sense of `antarāla`, the relation between `antaradvīpa` and
`parivāra`, and the intended target of the alternative tradition require
comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established
translations. The final uncertainty should be preserved unless another
witness supplies the missing identification.

The Study stops before the northward Jambūdvīpa geography that begins VAK
3.57 with the nine Kīṭa mountains, Himavān, and Anavatapta lake. No material
from the next verse has been included.
