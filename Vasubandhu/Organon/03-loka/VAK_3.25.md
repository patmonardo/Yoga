# VAK_3.25 — Stage-Naming and the Removal of Temporal Confusion

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> आवस्थिकः किलेष्टोऽयं प्राधान्यात्त्वङ्गकीर्तनम् ।
>
> पूर्वापरान्तमध्येषु संमोहविनिवृत्तये ॥ ३.२५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> āvasthikaḥ kileṣṭo 'yaṃ prādhānyāt tv aṅgakīrtanam /
>
> pūrvāparāntamadhyeṣu saṃmohavinivṛttaye // 3.25 //

The compact sandhi resolves as:

```text
kila + iṣṭaḥ          → kileṣṭaḥ
prādhānyāt + tu       → prādhānyāt tv
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| āvasthikaḥ | nominative masculine singular adjective | stage-based; consisting in complex five-Base stages |
| kila | reportive particle | it is said; according to the received determination |
| iṣṭaḥ | nominative masculine singular past participle | intended; accepted |
| ayam | nominative masculine singular pronoun | this presentation of dependent origination |
| prādhānyāt | ablative neuter singular | because of predominance |
| tu | adversative/explanatory particle | but; however; introduces the naming rule |
| aṅgakīrtanam | nominative neuter singular compound | naming or designation of a member |
| pūrvāparāntamadhyeṣu | locative plural compound | concerning the prior limit, subsequent limit, and middle |
| saṃmoha | compound member | confusion; failure to comprehend temporal continuity correctly |
| vinivṛttaye | dative feminine singular action noun | for removal; gives the intended purpose |

`Kila` matters: the verse reports the stage-based presentation as the one
intended in the sūtra; it need not assert that this is the sole exhaustive
form of dependent origination.

## 4. Grammar

The first statement selects the model:

```text
ayam [pratītyasamutpādaḥ]
    āvasthikaḥ
    kila iṣṭaḥ
```

> This dependent origination is said to be intended as stage-based.

The second supplies the rule of denomination:

```text
aṅga-kīrtanam
    tu
    prādhānyāt
```

> But the naming of each member is due to predominance.

The final dative gives the purpose of the selected presentation:

```text
pūrva-apara-anta-madhyeṣu
    saṃmoha-vinivṛttaye
```

> For the removal of confusion concerning the prior limit, subsequent limit,
> and middle.

The Bhāṣya specifies that each member is a five-Base stage and that the
three limits are past, future, and present within the three-life articulation.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> This is said to be intended as stage-based; the naming of a member, however,
> is due to predominance—for the removal of confusion concerning the prior
> limit, subsequent limit, and middle.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The twelvefold sūtra presentation is intended as a sequence of complex
> five-Base stages. Each stage is named after the determination
> predominant within it. This sentient-life sequence is divided across past,
> future, and present in order to remove confusion about temporal continuity
> by rightly seeing conditioned arising through *prajñā*.

Five-Base composition, the sūtra's selective intention, the concrete
forms of temporal confusion, and the role of Prajñā are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The Base sequence now reflects into its governing principle. A stage
> is a complex whole; its name expresses the determination governing that
> whole, not an isolated component extracted from it. The twelve-member,
> three-section model is selected because its form answers a determinate
> cognitive need: it renders past inheritance, present mediation, and future
> result intelligible as one conditioned World System.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes several possible articulations of dependent
origination, including momentary, extended, connected, and stage-based forms.
The twelve-stage sequence is therefore a selected model, not the exhaustive
meaning of dependent origination in every context.

## 8. Logical Determination

### The principle of stage-designation

```text
five-Base stage
    contains multiple determinations

one determination governs the stage
    = prādhānya

the whole stage is named from that determination
    = aṅga-kīrtana
```

Therefore:

```text
avidyā-member
    = five-Base stage in which avidyā predominates

saṃskāra-member
    = five-Base stage in which saṃskāra predominates

...

jarāmaraṇa-member
    = five-Base stage in which aging-and-death predominates
```

The member-name neither isolates a substance nor denies the other factors
present in the stage. It expresses the stage's organizing determination.

### Characteristic analysis and intentional presentation

The Bhāṣya distinguishes two legitimate explanatory operations:

```text
Abhidharma, lākṣaṇika:
    What belongs to dependent origination by characteristic?
    → all conditioned dharmas

Sūtra, abhiprāyika:
    What presentation serves the intended cognitive purpose?
    → twelve sentient-life stages across three births
```

The narrower sūtra model is not defective because it is selective. Its
selection is rationally governed by the confusion it must remove:

```text
determinate confusion
    → determines pedagogical purpose
    → purpose selects explanatory form
```

### Three temporal confusions, one three-section response

The Bhāṣya illustrates the confusions through questions concerning existence
and identity in past, future, and present. The positive operation is causal
comprehension:

```text
confusion about the past
    → prior conditions: avidyā + saṃskāra

confusion about the future
    → projected result: jāti + jarāmaraṇa

confusion about the present
    → embodied mediation: vijñāna through bhava
```

Rather than answering temporal speculation with an invented biography, the
teaching reconstructs the lawful conditions of continuity.

**Project-level Atman-adhyāsa reading.** The imagined enduring Atman is
superimposed upon conditioned continuity: “Was I? What was I? Will I be?”
The Bhāṣya names these questions as forms of *saṃmoha* and gives the remedy
as seeing dependent arising and what arises dependently, correctly and as
they are, through *prajñā*. Calling this Atman-*adhyāsa* is the project's
comparative diagnosis; the passage does not use the term *adhyāsa* or present
an explicit refutation of every doctrine of Atman.

### The Base sequence enters its governing principle

VAK 3.20–3.24 gave the concrete articulation. VAK 3.25 determines how and why
that articulation is formed:

```text
Base-continuum
    → differentiated into stages
    → stages named by predominance
    → temporal model selected by purpose
    → confusion removed through Prajñā
```

This is a reflective advance from the presented Base sequence to the principle
of its intelligible presentation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Organon's project-level reading treats stage-designation as
purpose-governed but answerable to the conditioned field: the three-life
presentation is a valid instrument for removing temporal confusion, not an
exhaustive account of every possible articulation. This methodological
extension is distinct from Vasubandhu's direct explanation of the sūtra's
intended presentation.
