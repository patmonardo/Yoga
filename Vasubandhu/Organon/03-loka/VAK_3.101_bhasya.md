# VAK_3.101 Bhāṣya — The Fourth-Dhyāna Abodes Are Not Eternal

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
tadapakṣālasādharmyāt na caturthe 'sty aniñjanāt /
na nityaṃ saha sattvena tadvimānodayavyayāt // 3.101 //
```

> Because of the similarity of their disturbances; in the fourth there is
> none, because it is undisturbed. [The receptacle] is not eternal together
> with the beings, because their abodes arise and pass away.

The first pāda completes the explanation begun under 3.100. The Bhāṣya then
answers the question left there: the fourth-dhyāna receptacle is not thereby
eternal.

## Continuous Sanskrit

This study follows the commentary on 3.101 through its explanation of the
fourth-dhyāna abodes, stopping at the question that introduces 3.102. The
Sanskrit is lightly punctuated; sandhi and spacing are regularized.

```iast
Na nityaṃ saha sattvena tadvimānodayavyayāt.

Na hi caturthaṃ dhyānam ekabhūmisaṃbaddham. Kiṃ tarhi? Vicchinnasthānāntaraṃ,
tārakāvat. Tatrāpy ayaṃ sattva upapadyeta cyaveta vā, sa sārdhaṃ vimāneneti;
nāsty asya nityatvam.

Kena punaḥ krameṇaitāḥ saṃvartanyo bhavanti? Nirantaraṃ tāvat—
```

## Continuous Conventional Translation

It is not eternal together with the being, because that being's celestial
abode arises and passes away. The fourth dhyāna is not connected to one
continuous plane. What, then, is it? It consists of locations separated from
one another, like stars. A being may be reborn there or pass away from there;
it does so together with its celestial abode. Therefore that [receptacle] is
not eternal.

By what sequence, then, do these contractions occur? First, continuously—

The final question and its opening words lead into the Bhāṣya on 3.102; that
verse and its explanation are not included here.

## Natural Movement

The Bhāṣya distinguishes the fourth dhyāna's immunity to the three
contractions from permanence. Its receptacle is not one continuous plane:
the separated abodes arise and disappear together with their respective
beings. The brief final question turns from the permanence issue to the
sequence of cosmic contractions.

## Organon Contact Point

No additional reconstruction is needed here. The local distinction is
between immunity to specified modes of contraction and eternity: the Bhāṣya
denies that the former entails the latter.

## Review Status

- The commentary explains the 3.101 claim and stops at the transition into
  3.102.
- `vimāna` is rendered “celestial abode”; the source's spatial image
  `tārakāvat` (“like stars”) is retained in the translation.
- The translation preserves the Bhāṣya's referent `[that receptacle]` as
  supplied from context rather than explicit in the kārikā's second pāda.
