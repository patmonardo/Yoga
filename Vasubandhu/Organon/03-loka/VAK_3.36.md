# VAK_3.36 — Five Reproductive Functions of Affliction

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)


```sanskrit
उक्तं च वक्ष्यते चान्यदत्र तु क्लेश इष्यते ।
बीजवन्नागवन्मूलवृक्षवत्तुषवत्तथा ॥ ३.३६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)


```text
uktaṃ ca vakṣyate cānyad atra tu kleśa iṣyate /
bījavan nāgavan mūlavṛkṣavat tuṣavat tathā // 3.36 //
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |

| vakṣyate | third-person singular future passive | will be explained |
| anyat | neuter nominative/accusative singular | something else; another member or set of members |
| atra | adverb | here, in the present treatment |
| tu | adversative particle | but; isolates the present topic |
| kleśa | masculine singular stem-form in the transmitted verse | affliction as the class presently intended |
| iṣyate | third-person singular present passive | is intended, accepted, or understood |
| bījavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a seed |
| nāgavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a nāga sustaining its lake |
| mūlavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a root |
| vṛkṣavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a tree |
| tuṣavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a husk |
| tathā | adverb | likewise; completes the analogical series |

The transmitted Sanskrit has `kleśa iṣyate`; its singular sense is secured by
the Bhāṣya's `kleśo ... veditavyaḥ`. The five `-vat` forms predicate distinct
similarities of affliction rather than five names for one entity.

## 4. Grammar


The first hemistich explains the selective scope of the present discussion:

```text
kiñcit [aṅgam] uktam
    = some [limb] has been explained

anyat vakṣyate
    = another will be explained

atra tu kleśa iṣyate
    = here, however, affliction is intended
```

The explicit subjects in the prose are Bhāṣya determinations: Discriminative
Cognition and the six Essences were treated earlier; Formations and Becoming
will be treated in the Karma chapter; Craving and Appropriation in the
Affliction chapter.

The second hemistich is a coordinated series:

```text
kleśaḥ
    bījavat
    nāgavat
    mūlavat
    vṛkṣavat
    tuṣavat
    [veditavyaḥ]

affliction is to be understood
    like seed,
    like nāga,
    like root,
    like tree,
    and like husk
```

`Veditavyaḥ` is supplied by the Bhāṣya; the Kārikā uses `iṣyate` to govern the
topic and its analogical determinations.

## 5. Translation


### Literal Translation
> Some [members] have been explained, and others will be explained. Here,
> however, affliction is intended: like a seed, like a nāga, like a root, like
> a tree, and likewise like a husk.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Some [members] have been explained; others will be explained. Here, however,
> affliction is intended: like a seed, a nāga, a root, a tree, and likewise a
> husk.

## 6. Philosophical Translation


The five analogies articulate Affliction's different relations to the
Affliction–Karma–Thing complex. The husk makes the condition especially
clear: Karma joined with *kleśaprāpti* can germinate into another birth;
Karma alone cannot. The verse presents causal efficacy, not a permanent
bearer behind the sequence.

The project-level causal reading is limited to this verse's distinction:
Affliction conditions recurrence, but does not become a permanent bearer of
the sequence.

## 8. Logical Determination


### Why the Kośa changes method here

The twelve limbs are not being abandoned. Their analyses are distributed
across the architecture of the Kośa:

```text
already treated:
    Cognition, six Essences

treated later in Karma:
    Formations, Becoming

treated later in Kleśa:
    Craving, Appropriation

treated here by causal analogy:
    Affliction as a functional class
```

The present abstraction is therefore controlled by prior and future detailed
analyses.

### Five non-redundant causal functions

```text
seed:
    Affliction → <Affliction, Karma, Thing>

nāga:
    Affliction present → birth-series does not dry up

root:
    root not extracted → destinations arise again

tree:
    Affliction → repeatedly produces <Affliction, Karma, Thing>

husk:
    Karma + kleśaprāpti → capable of another-birth germination
    Karma alone         → not so capable
```

The seed and tree analogies are close but not duplicates. Seed marks originary
production of a differentiated series; tree marks periodic and recurrent
production from an established system.

### Karma is conditionally reproductive

The husk analogy yields the strongest dependency:

```text
productive Karma(k)
    ∧ joinedWith(k, kleśaprāpti)
        → capableOf(k, future-birth)

productive Karma(k)
    ∧ not joinedWith(k, kleśaprāpti)
        ↛ future-birth
```

Affliction does not replace Karma as the carrier of productive efficacy.
Rather, it conditions Karma's capacity to germinate into another birth.

### Recurrence depends upon retained conditions

```text
cutting an appearing result
    while retaining its root
        → recurrence

removing the reproductive condition
        → recurrence no longer follows
```

The wheel is not explained by the persistence of an unchanging Thing. It is
explained by the retention and reactivation of causal capacity.

## 9. Interpretive Note


**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya says some members have already been treated and
others belong to the later Karma and Affliction chapters. Affliction is like a seed
because it generates further affliction, action, and Vastu; like a nāga that keeps
the lake of birth from drying up; like an unextracted root from which destinies grow
again; like a tree that repeatedly produces flowers and fruit; and like a husk
enclosing action with the acquired capacity to germinate into another birth, unlike
action alone. The allocation of each precise causal function to its simile is
supplied by the Bhāṣya.
