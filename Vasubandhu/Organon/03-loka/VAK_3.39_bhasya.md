# VAK_3.39 Bhāṣya — Morsel Nutriment and Sustaining Efficacy

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
kavaḍīkāra āhāraḥ kāme tryāyatanātmakaḥ /
na rūpāyatanaṃ tena svākṣamuktānanugrahāt // 3.39 //
```

> Morsel nutriment in the desire realm is constituted by three spheres. The
> visible-form sphere is not nutriment, because it does not support its own
> faculty or the liberated.

The Bhāṣya first enumerates the four nutriments, then analyzes the first as
gross or subtle, restricts it to the desire realm, identifies its three
constituent spheres, and excludes visible form through a functional test of
sustaining support.

The running witness contains mechanical corruption in several compounds and
in the pleasure clause at 153.01. The continuous text regularizes these
provisionally. The examples of subtle nutriment, the scope of `niḥṣyanda`,
and the difficult final compound in the verse require critical comparison.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Morsel nutriment is the first nutriment. It is gross and subtle. It is subtle
for intermediate beings because they subsist upon odor; likewise for gods and
beings of the first cosmic age, because they have no excretive residue and it
penetrates their limbs as oil penetrates sand. Alternatively, subtle
nutriment belongs to subtle beings such as infants and sweat-born creatures.

Contact is the second nutriment, mental volition the third, and differentiated
cognition the fourth.

Of these, morsel nutriment occurs in the desire realm. It does not occur in
the form or formless realms because beings are born there after becoming
dispassionate toward it. It is constituted by three spheres: all the
desire-realm spheres of odor, taste, and tangible are morsel nutriment because
they are taken in after being formed into morsels, ordinarily delimited as
intake by mouth or nose.

How, then, can shadow, heat, flame, and radiance be nutriment? The description
is said to state the predominant case. Things that are not literally ingested
but nevertheless sustain continued existence also count as subtle nutriment,
as with bathing and anointing.

Why is the visible-form sphere not nutriment? It too is taken in when food is
formed into morsels. The answer is that visible form does not support its own
faculty or the liberated. Nutriment is that which conduces to the sustaining
support of the faculties and the great elements. At the time of ingestion,
visible form does not support its own faculty, the eye, or the great elements
belonging to it—still less the other faculties, since visible form is not
their object-field.

Even when something visible, while being seen, produces pleasure and
gladness, the nutriment is the pleasant-feeling-bearing contact that has that
visible form as its object-support, not the visible form itself. This is
confirmed by liberated persons, non-returners and arhats: although they may
see pleasing food, its appearance does not provide the sustaining benefit in
question.

## 4. Argument and Voices

The passage has a clear progression:

1. Vasubandhu enumerates the four nutriments and marks morsel nutriment as
   gross or subtle.
2. He restricts morsel nutriment to the desire realm and identifies it with
   odor, taste, and tangible.
3. A question about non-ingested supports qualifies the ordinary language of
   morsel-taking: functional sustenance, not chewing alone, controls the
   category.
4. An objector asks why visible form is excluded despite the visible meal
   being ingested.
5. The reply defines nutriment by `anugraha`, distinguishes object-support
   from nourishing factor, and uses liberated persons as a discrimination
   test.

The three other nutriments are enumerated but not analyzed here. Their
status, realms, and functions belong to VAK 3.40 and following.

## 5. Gross and Subtle Morsel Nutriment

`Kavaḍīkārāhāra` is broader than coarse food chewed in a mouthful. The
Bhāṣya recognizes:

```text
audārika
    gross nutriment

sūkṣma
    subtle nutriment
```

Intermediate beings subsisting on odor, gods and primordial beings whose
assimilation leaves no ordinary residue, and subtle beings provide examples.
The oil-and-sand image emphasizes pervasive penetration rather than discrete
coarse ingestion.

The later examples of bathing and anointing make the governing criterion
explicit. “Taken as a morsel” describes the predominant mode, not an
exceptionless mechanism. Something not swallowed can still count as subtle
material nutriment if it actually sustains continued existence.

## 6. Realm and Three Constituent Spheres

Morsel nutriment belongs only to the desire realm:

```text
kāmadhātu
    kavaḍīkārāhāra present

rūpadhātu and ārūpyadhātu
    kavaḍīkārāhāra absent
```

The reason given is dispassion toward this kind of nutriment among those born
in the higher realms. The claim concerns this material mode of sustenance,
not the absence of every condition that maintains higher-realm existence.

Its three constituents are:

```text
gandhāyatana
    odor sphere

rasāyatana
    taste sphere

spraṣṭavyāyatana
    tangible sphere
```

These are all desire-realm spheres. `Tryāyatanātmaka` therefore states a
technical decomposition of morsel nutriment, not three ordinary varieties of
food.

## 7. Why Visible Form Is Not Nutriment

The Bhāṣya defines nutriment functionally:

```text
āhāra
    = what conduces to anugraha
      of faculties and great elements
```

Visible form fails this criterion. Its relation to the eye is object-field to
faculty, not material support to what is sustained. It does not nourish the
eye merely by being visible, nor can it nourish the other faculties for which
it is not an object.

This distinguishes the apparent unity called “food” into different causal
roles:

```text
visible form
    displayed object-support

odor + taste + tangible
    material morsel nutriment

pleasant-feeling-bearing contact
    possible affective nutriment occasioned in seeing
```

When attractive appearance produces pleasure, the contact taking that form
as object is nutriment; the object-support is not thereby identical with the
nourishing operation.

## 8. The Liberated-Person Test

Non-returners and arhats may see an attractive meal without receiving the
relevant sustaining benefit from its appearance. This case separates three
facts that ordinary speech can conflate:

```text
appearance is pleasing
appearance is cognized
appearance sustains the embodied-affective system
```

The first two do not entail the third. Accordingly, pleasing presentation is
insufficient to establish technical nutriment.

The argument does not say that liberated persons need no material support or
cannot recognize attractiveness. It tests only the claim that visible form
itself nourishes by producing conditioned pleasure. The absence of that
affective support reveals that the form served as object, while contact had
performed the nourishing function in susceptible continuums.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

This unit offers an exact example of semantic discrimination. The ordinary
noun “food” groups visible appearance, odor, taste, texture, ingestion,
contact, pleasure, and bodily maintenance into one practical object. The
Bhāṣya separates these according to the work each performs.

For an Ocular Dataset, the source-side lesson is:

```text
surface label
    does not determine
functional role
```

An artifact may display beautifully without sustaining the process that uses
it. A displayed form can be an object-support while a distinct contact-event
produces the operational effect. Content, presentation, interaction, and
maintenance therefore need separate provenance and typing.

The inclusion of subtle nutriment also prevents narrow implementation by
stereotype. A definition based only on the familiar action of chewing would
miss bathing, anointing, and pervasive assimilation. The category must follow
sustaining efficacy across different modes of delivery.

This is the precise point at which the Semantic Copula becomes functional in
the project's reconstruction: “x is nutriment” is warranted not by resemblance
to prototypical food but by an evidenced sustaining relation. That Organon
formulation remains distinct from the Bhāṣya's own definition through
`anugraha`.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit at 152.10–153.03: enumeration
of the four nutriments, gross and subtle morsel nutriment, its desire-realm
restriction, its constitution by odor, taste, and tangible, the qualification
of literal ingestion, and the exclusion of visible form through sustaining
efficacy and the liberated-person test.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The examples of intermediate
and subtle beings, `niḥṣyanda`, the shadow–heat–flame–radiance sequence,
`anugraha`, the pleasure clause, and the final compound of the kārikā require
comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established
translations. The Study stops with the liberated-person argument and does not
begin VAK 3.40's analysis of contact, volition, and cognition as nutriments.
