# VAK_3.98 Bhāṣya — Ten-Year Lifespan and the Roots of Misfortune

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ālasyāt saṃnidhiṃ kṛtvā sāgrahaiḥ kṣetrapo bhṛtaḥ /
tataḥ karmapathādhikyād apahrāse daśāyuṣaḥ // 3.98 //
```

> Because of laziness, a store was made; through possessive appropriation a
> field-guardian was maintained. Then, as courses of action increased, human
> lifespan declined to ten years.

## Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Tataḥ karmapathādhikyād apahrāse daśāyuṣaḥ // 3.98 //

Tata evaṃ karmapathānāṃ vṛddhau satyāṃ krameṇa hrasatāṃ manuṣyāṇāṃ
daśavarṣāyuṣo manuṣyāḥ saṃbhavanti.
Ato 'sya kṛtsnasyānarthaughasya dvau dharmau mūlayoniḥ: rasarāga ālasyaṃ ca.
```

Punctuation and sandhi are lightly regularized.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

As these courses of action increased, human beings gradually declined in
lifespan until people with ten-year lifespans appeared. The two root causes
of this entire flood of misfortune are attachment to savor and laziness.

## Natural Movement

The Bhāṣya completes the social genealogy by stating the outcome of increasing
courses of action: human lifespans decline until people live for ten years.
It then gathers the sequence under two roots, attachment to savor and
laziness. The following account of the intermediate age's calamities belongs
to 3.99, whose first line is cited immediately after this statement in the
running source.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: the commentary names `rasarāga` and `ālasya` as the two roots
of the misfortune just narrated. This remains Vasubandhu's causal summary,
not an added Organon principle.
