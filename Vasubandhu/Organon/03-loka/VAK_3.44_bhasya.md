# VAK_3.44 Bhāṣya — The Three Determinations of the Sentient World

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
samyaṅmithyātvaniyatā āryānantaryakāriṇaḥ // 3.44 //
```

> The noble ones are fixed in rightness; performers of immediate-result
> actions are fixed in wrongness.

The compact line gives the paradigmatic members of two groups. The Bhāṣya's
framing prose supplies the complete threefold classification: those fixed in
rightness, those fixed in wrongness, and those not fixed.

The line `adhonṛsuragājānāṃ marmacchedas tv abādibhiḥ`, included with this
verse in an earlier Kārikā witness, belongs to the preceding account of death
and vital-point impairment. It is not part of VAK 3.44.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
So 'yaṃ sattvaloka evam utpadyamānas tiṣṭhañ cyavamānaś ca triṣu rāśiṣu
sthāpito bhagavatā.

Trayo rāśayaḥ: samyaktvaniyato rāśir, mithyātvaniyato rāśir, aniyato rāśir
iti. Tatra punaḥ:

samyaṅmithyātvaniyatā āryānantaryakāriṇaḥ.

“Samyaktvaṃ katamat? Yat tat paryādāya rāgaprahāṇaṃ, paryādāya
dveṣaprahāṇaṃ, paryādāya mohaprahāṇaṃ, paryādāya sarvakleśaprahāṇam—idam
ucyate samyaktvam” iti sūtram.

Āryāḥ katame? Yeṣām anāsravo mārga utpannaḥ. Ārād yātāḥ pāpakebhyo
dharmebhya ity āryāḥ, ātyantikavisaṃyogaprāptilābhāt. Ete hi kleśakṣaye
niyatatvāt samyaktvaniyatāḥ.

Mokṣabhāgīyalābhino 'py avaśyaṃ parinirvāṇadharmāṇa iti. Kasmān na
samyaktve niyatāḥ? Te hi mithyātve 'pi niyatā bhaveyuḥ. Na ca te
kālaniyamena samyaktve niyatā, yathā saptakṛtvaḥparamādayaḥ.

Mithyātvaṃ katamat? Narakāḥ pretās tiryañca—idam ucyate mithyātvam. Tatrānantaryakāriṇo narake niyatatvān mithyātvaniyatāḥ.

Niyatebhyo 'nye 'niyatā iti siddham. Pratyayāpekṣaṃ hi teṣām
ubhayabhāktvam anubhayabhāktvaṃ ca.

Uktaḥ sattvalokaḥ. Bhājanaloka idānīṃ vaktavyaḥ.
```

The continuous text regularizes spacing, punctuation, and evident sandhi in
the running witness. The transmitted etymological phrase explaining `ārya`
is retained but remains philologically uncertain; its exact form and force
require comparison with critical witnesses.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

This sentient world, arising, persisting, and passing away in this manner,
was placed by the Blessed One into three groups.

There are three groups: the group fixed in rightness, the group fixed in
wrongness, and the group not fixed. Concerning these it is said: the noble
ones are fixed in rightness; performers of immediate-result actions are fixed
in wrongness.

What is rightness? A sūtra says: “The complete abandonment of attachment, the
complete abandonment of hatred, the complete abandonment of delusion—the
complete abandonment of all afflictions—this is called rightness.”

Who are the noble ones? They are those in whom the path without outflows has
arisen. They are called noble because they have gone far from harmful
dharmas, through acquiring final disjunction. Because they are fixed with
respect to the exhaustion of the afflictions, they are fixed in rightness.

But even those who have acquired dharmas conducive to liberation are
necessarily of a nature to attain final nirvāṇa. Why are they not fixed in
rightness? Because they might still become fixed in wrongness. Nor is the
time at which they become fixed in rightness itself determined, as it is for
those such as the one who has at most seven returns.

What is wrongness? The hells, the preta condition, and the animal condition
are called wrongness. Among these, performers of an immediate-result action
are fixed in wrongness because they are fixed for hell.

Those other than the two fixed groups are thus established as not fixed. For
their participation in either group, or in neither, depends upon conditions.

The sentient world has been explained. The container-world is now to be
explained.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voices

The commentary completes the treatment of `sattvaloka` by adding a
classification of trajectories to its preceding account of arising,
persistence, and death:

```text
sattvaloka
    → fixed in rightness
    → fixed in wrongness
    → not fixed
```

It then proceeds in a strict order:

1. a sūtra defines rightness through complete abandonment;
2. Vasubandhu identifies the noble ones and explains their fixity;
3. he distinguishes eventual liberation from present fixity in rightness;
4. he defines wrongness through the three bad destinies;
5. he explains the immediate-result agent's fixity toward hell;
6. he derives the residual, condition-dependent class;
7. he closes `sattvaloka` and announces `bhājanaloka`.

The definition of `samyaktva` is explicitly introduced as a sūtra quotation.
The remaining questions function as internal exposition; the text does not
identify a separate objector or school here.

## 5. Rightness and the Noble Ones

`Samyaktva` does not mean merely correct belief in this passage. Its cited
definition is exhaustive:

```text
complete abandonment of attachment
complete abandonment of hatred
complete abandonment of delusion
    = complete abandonment of all afflictions
    = samyaktva
```

The noble ones are identified by an event in the path: the path without
outflows has arisen in them. Their designation and classification are then
grounded in acquisition:

```text
arising of anāsrava-mārga
    → acquisition of final disjunction
        → fixed orientation toward kleśakṣaya
            → samyaktva-niyata
```

The Bhāṣya's wording distinguishes the presently arisen path from its
terminus. Noble ones have not all already completed the abandonment of every
affliction, but their course is fixed with respect to that exhaustion. Thus
`niyata` determines a trajectory, not merely a currently manifest state.

The phrase rendered “gone far from harmful dharmas” is an etymological
explanation of `ārya`, not an independent definition replacing the arising
of the path without outflows.

## 6. Eventual Liberation Is Not Present Fixity

The qualification concerning `mokṣabhāgīya` dharmas prevents an overbroad
classification. Those who have acquired what is conducive to liberation
will necessarily attain final nirvāṇa, yet they are not for that reason
already `samyaktva-niyata`.

The Bhāṣya gives two related reasons:

```text
before fixity in rightness
    contrary fixity remains possible

and

eventual liberation may be certain
    while its time is not fixed
```

The “one with at most seven returns” supplies the contrast: in such a case
the remaining temporal range is delimited. Accordingly, three determinations
must not be collapsed:

| Determination | Question answered |
|---|---|
| possession of liberation-conducive dharmas | Will liberation eventually occur? |
| fixity in rightness | Has the course become irreversible toward exhaustion of affliction? |
| temporal fixity | Is the remaining time or number of births delimited? |

This distinction is one of the most important additions made by the Bhāṣya
to the terse verse.

## 7. Wrongness and Immediate-Result Action

`Mithyātva` is also more than an erroneous opinion here. The commentary
defines it by three bad destinies:

```text
hell
preta existence
animal existence
    = mithyātva in this classification
```

The verse names performers of an `ānantarya` action as the paradigmatic
members of the class because their next destination is determined:

```text
ānantarya action
    → fixity for hell
        → mithyātva-niyata
```

“Immediate-result” should be understood technically: the action bears its
result without another existence intervening. The present unit does not
enumerate the particular immediate-result actions; it uses their shared
destinational consequence to establish the classification.

The definition of wrongness names all three bad destinies, whereas the verse
selects a class fixed specifically for hell. It would therefore be too broad
to infer that every being presently in, or headed toward, any bad destiny is
already `mithyātva-niyata` in the same technical sense.

## 8. The Condition-Dependent Third Group

Those who belong to neither fixed group are `aniyata`, not fixed. This third
group is not merely an unexamined remainder. Its modal structure is stated
positively:

```text
participation in either fixed group
or participation in neither
    → depends upon conditions
```

`Pratyayāpekṣa` does not mean uncaused freedom or absence of determination.
It means that the relevant determination has not yet become fixed and that
the eventual classification depends upon operative conditions.

The three groups are therefore not three substances or permanent kinds of
persons. They classify the state of a conditioned course:

| Group | Status of the course |
|---|---|
| `samyaktva-niyata` | fixed toward exhaustion of affliction |
| `mithyātva-niyata` | fixed toward the specified bad result |
| `aniyata` | not yet fixed; outcome remains condition-dependent |

This also explains why the third group must be stated in the Bhāṣya even
though the Kārikā names only the exemplary agents of the first two.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The closing determination of the sentient world concerns configuration of a
course rather than description of an isolated present property. The same
agent can possess liberation-conducive dharmas, remain temporally
undetermined, and not yet belong to the rightness-fixed group. Object,
acquisition, present manifestation, future consequence, and irreversible
trajectory must therefore remain separate axes.

For the Organon Dataset, `niyata` should not be represented as a simple moral
label. A more faithful analysis requires at least:

```text
present path status
acquired capacity
destination fixed or open
terminus of the trajectory
temporal range fixed or open
conditions upon which reclassification depends
```

This is an Organon reconstruction of relations displayed by the Bhāṣya, not
Vasubandhu's own data vocabulary. It is especially important that
`aniyata` not be typed as “no configuration.” It is a conditioned
configuration whose decisive trajectory remains open.

The conclusion also gives Chapter 3 a major internal articulation:

```text
sentient world explained
    → container-world now to be explained
```

The transition does not discard the sentient world. It changes the object of
analysis from beings and their conditioned trajectories to the shared
material arrangement in which such lives occur. VAK 3.45 begins that new
scale of exposition.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete VAK 3.44 commentary unit at 157.11–157.25:
the three groups, the sūtra definition of rightness, the criterion for noble
status, fixity toward exhaustion of affliction, the qualification concerning
liberation-conducive dharmas, wrongness and immediate-result action, the
condition-dependent third group, and the explicit close of `sattvaloka`.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The etymological gloss of
`ārya`, the syntax of the sentence concerning those who possess
`mokṣabhāgīya` dharmas, the exact temporal contrast with the
`saptakṛtvaḥparama`, and the scope of `ubhayabhāktva` and `anubhayabhāktva`
require comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established
translations.

The deva death-sign material at 157.06–157.11 precedes this unit and belongs
to the preceding death discussion; it has not been reassigned to VAK 3.44.
The Study stops at the explicit announcement of the container-world and does
not begin VAK 3.45.
