# VAK_3.71

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कामोपपत्तयस्तिस्रः कामदेवाः समानुषाः ।
>
> सुखोपपत्तयस्तिस्रो नव त्रिध्यानभूमयः ॥ ३.७१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kāmopapattayas tisraḥ kāmadevāḥ samānuṣāḥ /
>
> sukhopapattayas tisro nava tridhyānabhūmayaḥ // 3.71 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
kāma-upapattayaḥ           → kāmopapattayas
tisraḥ                     → tisraḥ
kāma-devāḥ                 → kāmadevāḥ
sa-mānuṣāḥ                 → samānuṣāḥ
sukha-upapattayaḥ          → sukhopapattayas
tisraḥ                     → tisro
nava                       → nava
tri-dhyāna-bhūmayaḥ        → tridhyānabhūmayaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| kāmopapattayaḥ | nominative feminine plural compound | modes of birth characterized by sensual desire |
| tisraḥ | nominative feminine plural numeral | three |
| kāmadevāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | devas of the sensual domain |
| samānuṣāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | together with humans / including humans |
| sukhopapattayaḥ | nominative feminine plural compound | modes of birth characterized by happiness |
| tisraḥ | nominative feminine plural numeral | three |
| nava | numeral | nine |
| tridhyānabhūmayaḥ | nominative feminine plural compound | grounds belonging to the three dhyānas |

The verse gives the counts and broad domains. The Bhāṣya determines the three
`kāmopapatti` by the provenance of desired objects and groups the nine grounds
of the first three dhyānas into the three `sukhopapatti`.

## 4. Grammar

The first half presents a threefold classification within kāmadhātu:

```text
kāma-upapattayaḥ
    tisraḥ [santi]

[tāsu]
    kāma-devāḥ
    sa-mānuṣāḥ [antarbhavanti]
```

> There are three sensual-desire births, comprising the sensual devas together
> with humans.

The syntax does not enumerate the three kinds. The Bhāṣya supplies them:
enjoyment of already-present objects, self-produced objects, and objects
produced by others.

The second half establishes another threefold classification in rūpadhātu:

```text
sukha-upapattayaḥ
    tisraḥ [santi]

tri-dhyāna-bhūmayaḥ
    nava [tāḥ santi]
```

> There are three happiness-births: the nine grounds of the three dhyānas.

The Bhāṣya groups the nine grounds under three modes of prolonged pleasant
dwelling associated with the first, second, and third dhyānas.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> There are three sensual-desire births among the sensual devas together with
> humans. There are three happiness-births: the nine grounds of the three
> dhyānas.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the sensual domain there are three modes of birth. Humans and the four
> lower sensual-deva classes exercise mastery over objects of desire that are
> already present. Nirmāṇarati devas produce desired objects themselves and
> enjoy them at will. Paranirmitavaśavartin devas exercise mastery over desired
> objects produced by others and enjoy them at will. In the form domain, the
> nine grounds of the first three dhyānas form three happiness-births: prolonged
> happy dwelling through joy and pleasure born of seclusion, through joy and
> pleasure born of samādhi, and through pleasure without joy.

The object-provenance analysis, class distribution, mastery relation, three
forms of dhyāna happiness, long duration, and absence of suffering are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Birth into a World is classified here by the operative constitution of its
> experiential field. In kāmadhātu, the decisive variable is the provenance of
> the desired object: given, produced by the enjoying Agent, or produced by
> another Agent. In rūpadhātu, the classificatory axis changes from object-
> production to the conditioned source and quality of sustained happiness.
> `Upapatti` therefore binds an Agent not merely to a location, but to a
> characteristic relation among object, production, control, and enjoyment.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Three sensual object-fields

| Mode | Object provenance | Enjoying populations | Control relation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | already present | humans + four lower kāma-deva classes | mastery over given objects |
| 2 | self-produced | Nirmāṇarati | mastery over self-produced objects |
| 3 | produced by others | Paranirmitavaśavartin | mastery over other-produced objects |

Formally, let `A` be the enjoying Agent and `O` the desired object:

```text
mode 1:
    exists(O) prior to A's present production
    A controls and enjoys O

mode 2:
    A produces O at will
    A controls and enjoys O

mode 3:
    another Agent B produces O
    A controls and enjoys O
```

All three share enjoyment and mastery. They differ in the production relation
that supplies the object.

### Production and control are independent predicates

```text
produces(A, O)
    does not entail
controls(A, O)

controls(A, O)
    does not entail
produces(A, O)
```

The third birth-mode proves the second non-entailment: the enjoying Agent
controls an object produced by another. The first proves that control can also
apply to an already-given field.

### Three happiness-births

```text
first dhyāna grounds:
    happiness through seclusion-born joy and pleasure

second dhyāna grounds:
    happiness through samādhi-born joy and pleasure

third dhyāna grounds:
    happiness through pleasure without joy
```

The three dhyānas contain nine grounds in total. The Bhāṣya groups them by the
dominant pleasant mode rather than treating each ground as a separate kind of
`sukhopapatti`.

### Change of classificatory axis

```text
kāmopapatti:
    classify by object provenance + mode of enjoyment

sukhopapatti:
    classify by source and quality of sustained happiness
```

The common suffix `upapatti` does not make the two threefold classifications
semantically uniform. Each domain supplies its own discriminating principle.

### Taxonomic exception

```text
definition of happiness-birth:
    long-lasting happiness without suffering

intermediate-dhyāna birth:
    joy absent
    pleasure absent

therefore:
    inclusion as happiness-birth = requires examination
```

Vasubandhu preserves the recalcitrant case instead of forcing it into the
threefold schema.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.71 finally extracts the principle latent in the deva spectacle. A World
station is a structured relation between an Agent and the field it can enjoy:

```text
object provenance
    + production authority
    + control relation
    + mode of enjoyment
        → type of sensual World-birth
```

This is stronger than a hierarchy of possessions. The Nirmāṇarati Agent
constructs its desired object; the Paranirmitavaśavartin Agent controls an
object constructed by another. Creation and sovereignty are therefore
discriminated. The creator of an object and the Agent who commands its use
need not be identical.

That distinction is highly relevant to our Creator inquiry. Self-production
of an object is not yet Absolute creation here: it remains production under
the prior form of kāma and for the sake of enjoyment. Conversely, supremacy
over an object need not mean having originated it. The Kośa is mapping powers
internal to a conditioned World System, not granting one undifferentiated
predicate called creation.

The rūpa half performs a categorical shift. The operative field is no longer
classified by who manufactures the desirable object, but by how sustained
happiness arises:

```text
seclusion → joy and pleasure
samādhi   → joy and pleasure
third dhyāna → pleasure without joy
```

This makes the dhyāna worlds genuine experiential domains, but the correlation
must be stated carefully: the verse classifies modes of rebirth in dhyāna
grounds. It does not by itself identify that rebirth with the present practice
or attainment-event of an embodied meditator.

```ts
type DesireObjectField =
  | { provenance: "AlreadyPresent"; producer: null; controller: "EnjoyingAgent" }
  | { provenance: "SelfProduced"; producer: "EnjoyingAgent"; controller: "EnjoyingAgent" }
  | { provenance: "OtherProduced"; producer: "OtherAgent"; controller: "EnjoyingAgent" };

type HappinessBirth =
  | { source: "Seclusion"; joy: true; pleasure: true }
  | { source: "Samadhi"; joy: true; pleasure: true }
  | { source: "ThirdDhyana"; joy: false; pleasure: true };
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. The two
types intentionally use different fields because the Kāma and Rūpa
classifications operate through different semantic principles.

The intermediate-dhyāna qualification is methodologically decisive. A robust
World System retains an exception when its defining predicates fail; it does
not erase the case to preserve a beautiful threefold taxonomy.
