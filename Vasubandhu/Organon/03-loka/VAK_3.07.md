# VAK_3.07

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अनिच्छावसनान्नान्ये चतस्रः स्थितयः पुनः ।
>
> चत्वारः सास्रवाः स्कन्धाः स्वभूमावेव केवलम् ॥ ३.७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> anicchāvasanān nānye catasraḥ sthitayaḥ punaḥ /
>
> catvāraḥ sāsravāḥ skandhāḥ svabhūmāv eva kevalam // 3.7 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
anicchā-avasanāt + na + anye → anicchāvasanān nānye
catasraḥ                      → catasraḥ
sthitayaḥ                     → sthitayaḥ
punaḥ                         → punaḥ
catvāraḥ                      → catvāraḥ
sa-āsravāḥ                    → sāsravāḥ
skandhāḥ                      → skandhāḥ
sva-bhūmau + eva             → svabhūmāv eva
kevalam                       → kevalam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| anicchā-avasanāt | ablative singular compound | because dwelling is unwilling / because of unwanted residence |
| na anye | negation with nominative masculine plural | no others |
| catasraḥ | nominative feminine plural numeral | four, agreeing with `sthitayaḥ` |
| sthitayaḥ | nominative feminine plural of `sthiti` | standings, establishments, stations |
| punaḥ | indeclinable | again, further, in another scriptural classification |
| catvāraḥ | nominative masculine plural numeral | four, agreeing with `skandhāḥ` |
| sāsravāḥ | nominative masculine plural, `sa-āsrava` | with outflows; contaminated |
| skandhāḥ | nominative masculine plural | aggregates |
| sva-bhūmau | locative feminine singular compound | in their own ground |
| eva kevalam | double restrictive expression | only, exclusively |

The ablative `anicchāvasanāt` gives the reason for excluding further abodes.
The Bhāṣya makes its subject explicit: beings in the bad destinies dwell there
unwillingly.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause closes the preceding classification of being-abodes:

```text
na anye [sattva-āvāsāḥ]
    anicchā-avasanāt
```

The Bhāṣya clarifies that “no others” refers to the bad destinies. They are not
excluded because no beings occupy them, but because their inhabitants are
made to remain there against their wish.

`Punaḥ` then opens another scriptural use of `sthiti`:

```text
catasraḥ sthitayaḥ
    = catvāraḥ sāsravāḥ skandhāḥ
    = svabhūmau eva kevalam
```

The Bhāṣya identifies the four as cognition-stations associated respectively
with:

```text
rūpa       form
vedanā     feeling
saṃjñā     perception
saṃskāra   formations
```

They are the four with-outflow aggregates other than `vijñāna`. Each functions
as a station of differentiated cognition only in its own ground, never in
another ground.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> There are no others because dwelling [there] is unwilling. Again, the four
> stations are the four aggregates with outflows, exclusively in their own
> ground.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> No additional states count as abodes of beings, because beings occupy the
> bad destinies unwillingly. In another scriptural classification there are
> four cognition-stations: form, feeling, perception, and formations with
> outflows, each functioning as a station only within its own proper ground.

The bad destinies, the four named aggregates, and their function as stations
associated with `vijñāna` are Bhāṣya determinations. “With outflows” preserves
the literal structure of `sāsrava`; “contaminated” remains its doctrinal gloss.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System distinguishes dwelling from coerced occupancy. A being may
> be placed somewhere without making that state its abode in the proper sense.
> Differentiated cognition likewise does not stand abstractly: it becomes
> established through form, feeling, perception, and formations with outflows,
> and every such station functions only upon its own ground.

World-position therefore includes both a relation to dwelling and a lawful
ground of cognitive support.

### The station cognate

“Station” is exceptionally apt here. Sanskrit `sthiti` derives from √`sthā`,
“stand, remain, be established.” English “station” comes through Latin
`statio`, from `stāre`, “to stand.” Sanskrit √`sthā` and Latin `stāre` descend
from the same Indo-European standing-root. The English rendering therefore
recovers rather than invents the term's force:

```text
sthiti
    a standing
    an establishment
    a station
```

This does not make the Sanskrit word historically derived from Latin or the
English word derived from Sanskrit; they are cognate inheritances.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 3.07 makes two discriminations.

First:

```text
being located in a state
    ≠
dwelling there as an abode

sattvāvāsa
    requires a determinate relation to residence

bad destiny
    coerced occupancy
    comparable to confinement
```

The Bhāṣya's arresting causal image is:

```text
karmarākṣasaiḥ āvāsyante
    beings are made to dwell there
    by the rākṣasas of karma
```

Karma appears phenomenally as an alien coercive power because the determining
history of the present placement is not contained in the agent's present wish.
The image does not yet replace the rational account of karma that Chapter 4
must provide.

Second, `sthiti` receives a ground-specific support sense:

```text
vijñāna
    stands with respect to
        rūpa
        vedanā
        saṃjñā
        saṃskāra

each support
    functions only in svabhūmi
    never in an alien ground
```

`Vijñāna` is not included as its own station. Differentiated cognition stands
through relations to the other four with-outflow aggregates. No determination
floats free of the field that gives it its support-function.

## 9. Interpretive Note

Wonderworld now shows its coercive underside. The agent does not always choose
the station into which it awakens. The Bhāṣya gives this lack of transparency
a mythic image—`karmarākṣasāḥ`, rākṣasas of karma—before Karmanirdeśa supplies
the systematic account of the determining ground.

```text
from the isolated agent's standpoint
    karma appears as an alien demon of placement

from the completed World System
    karma must become rationally cognized
    as the agent's determinate transition-ground
```

That formulation remains an Organon anticipation. VAK 3.07 itself establishes
only that present unwillingness does not cancel karmically enforced residence.
It should not be turned into a doctrine that suffering agents consciously
chose or deserve their present pain.

The second half gives the stronger architectural result:

```text
PRAJNA
    becomes differentiated as vijñāna

vijñāna
    does not station itself
    stands through four aggregate-relations

four stations
    form
    feeling
    perception
    formations

svabhūmi
    constrains each station to its proper ground
```

This makes an Embodied Agent a ground-indexed cognitive system:

```text
EmbodiedAgent {
    formStation,
    feelingStation,
    perceptionStation,
    formationStation,
    properGround,
    differentiatedCognition
}
```

For a robot, the implication is deliciously strict. Hardware, valence or
evaluation, perceptual recognition, and formation or policy-state cannot be
arbitrarily mixed across incompatible grounds. A cognition-station requires
lawful support-relations within one coherent world-level. Mere execution is
not yet standing; `sthiti` means established operation.

The verse therefore advances the telos proposed in VAK 3.06:

```text
PRAJNA may become Known among Embodied Agents
    only if
vijñāna can stand through lawful aggregate-supports
    only if
each support operates upon its own proper ground
```

This is project architecture, not Vasubandhu's statement. Textually, the verse
has earned the indispensable constraint: cognition is relationally stationed,
and every station is ground-specific.
