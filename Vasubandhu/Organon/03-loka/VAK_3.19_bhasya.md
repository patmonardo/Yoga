# VAK_3.19 Bhāṣya — The Beginningless but Terminable Wheel of Becoming

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> yathākṣepaṃ kramād vṛddhaḥ santānaḥ kleśakarmabhiḥ /
> paralokaṃ punar yātīty anādibhavacakrakam // 3.19 //

> The continuum, having developed in sequence according to its projection,
> goes again to another world through afflictions and karma. Thus is the
> beginningless wheel of becoming.

The Bhāṣya follows the aggregate-continuum from karmically delimited growth
through embryonic development, birth, maturation, renewed affliction and
action, death, and another intermediate existence. It then explains why this
wheel has no first beginning yet can have an end.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

That continuum, “having developed in sequence according to its projection,
goes again to another world through afflictions and karma.” The projection of
every aggregate-continuum is not equal, because the karma determining lifespan
differs. Therefore, the extent of each continuum's gradual development accords
with the extent of its projection.

By what sequence? The noble ones say:

“First there is the *kalala*; from the *kalala* the *arbuda* arises. From the
*arbuda* the *peśī* arises; from the *peśī* the *ghana* arises. From the
*ghana* the branching members arise, then hair, body-hair, nails, and the
material faculties and sexual characteristics, each in succession.”

These are the five embryonic stages: *kalala*, *arbuda*, *peśī*, *ghana*, and
*praśākhā*.

After a period of time, when the fetus has reached maturity, winds produced
by karmic maturation blow within the mother's womb. They turn the fetus and
position it toward the opening in the mother's body. Dislodged from its place,
it is turned about in extreme pain; the comparison in the present e-text is
uncertain.

If, at some time, through an improper act in the mother's diet or conduct and
through the fault of previous karma, it dies within the womb, knowledgeable
women or attendants skilled in the care of children anoint a hand with
comfortably warm ghee-oil, finely ground silk-cotton paste, or another
substance, and attach a sharp, narrow instrument to it. They insert the hand
into what the text describes, in deliberately repellent detail, as the great
birth-channel wound produced by former karmic maturation—dark, foul-smelling,
wet, continually discharging, covered only by thin perforated skin, and
befouled by reproductive fluids and bodily impurities—and extract the fetus
piece by piece.

That continuity too is led to some destiny by prior karma whose result is to
be experienced on another occasion.

If, instead, it is safely born, the mother desiring a child, or other women
attending her, take the newborn—whose body is like a fresh wound—with hands
whose touch feels like a blade or caustic substance. They wash it, nourish it
with breast milk or ghee, and gradually accustom it to consuming coarse food.

As it grows and its faculties mature, afflictions again become active and
karma accumulates. Through these, when the body breaks up, it goes again to
another world through the continuity of intermediate existence, just as
before.

“Thus is the beginningless wheel of becoming.” It should be understood in
this way: birth has affliction and karma as its causes; afflictions and karma
arise again with that birth as their condition; from them there is birth
again. This is the beginningless wheel of becoming.

If a beginning were imagined, the first birth among these would follow
without a cause; and if causelessness were admitted, everything here could
arise without a cause. Yet the efficacy of seeds in producing sprouts and
other effects is observed through their restriction to determinate places and
times, as is the efficacy of fire and other causes in cooking and comparable
effects. There is therefore no causeless arising. The thesis that an eternal
cause exists has already been rejected. Hence saṃsāra has no beginning.

Its end, however, is reasonable through exhaustion of its causes, because
birth depends upon causes—just as a sprout no longer arises when its seed has
been exhausted.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

Vasubandhu speaks throughout, apart from the cited embryological verses
attributed to the noble ones. The argument unfolds in five movements:

```text
unequal karmic projection delimits development
    → five ordered embryonic stages
    → fetal death or live birth remains within causal continuity
    → mature faculties enable renewed affliction and karma
    → recurrence has no first cause but can end when its causes end
```

The graphic medical description is not an independent proof. It intensifies
the claim that embodiment is materially conditioned, vulnerable, and painful.
Its inherited physiology must be distinguished from modern medicine.

## 5. Projection and Ordered Development

`Yathākṣepam` first concerns measure. Lifespan-producing karma differs, so
aggregate-continua do not receive equal projections or equal extents of
growth. `Krama` then concerns order: the projected life develops through
successive material stages rather than appearing as an already completed
body.

The verse's `kleśakarmabhiḥ` is best read with its return to another world:
the prose first describes renewed affliction and accumulated karma after
faculty-maturation, then says `sa taiḥ ... paralokaṃ punar yāti`, “through
these it goes again to another world.”

The five stages give the order traditional to this passage:

```text
kalala → arbuda → peśī → ghana → praśākhā
    → hair, body-hair, nails, faculties, and sexual characteristics
```

The Bhāṣya thus brings the subtle causal continuity of VAK 3.18 into a
material developmental sequence. Projection is neither a mental picture nor
an instantaneous manufacture; it is the karmically bounded course within
which successive bodily determinations arise.

## 6. Birth, Death, and the Maturation of Faculties

The two possible outcomes do not break the causal account. If the fetus dies,
previous karma capable of ripening on another occasion leads the continuum
toward another destiny. If it is born, care, feeding, and habituation carry
development forward.

The decisive transition occurs with `indriyāṇāṃ paripāka`, maturation of the
faculties:

```text
growth and faculty-maturation
    → afflictions become active again
    → new karma accumulates
    → bodily death
    → intermediate-existence continuum
    → another world, as before
```

An old karma does not propel the wheel indefinitely by itself. Each embodied
life becomes a field in which the causes of another birth are renewed. The
faculties are therefore not only mature capacities; under affliction they are
also conditions through which the continuum reproduces its causal future.

## 7. The Recursive Wheel

The phrase `pūrvavat`, “as before,” makes VAK 3.10–3.18 recursively available.
The text need not redescribe death, intermediate existence, attraction, and
rebirth each time:

```text
affliction and karma
    → birth
    → maturation of faculties
    → renewed affliction and karma
    → death and intermediate existence
    → birth again
```

`Bhavacakraka` names this causal recursion. It is not a wheel moved by an
external driver, nor an entity in addition to its events. Its efficacy lies
in the way each conditioned life regenerates the conditions for another.

## 8. Why Beginningless Can Still End

Vasubandhu treats beginning and end asymmetrically.

To posit a first birth would leave it without the prior affliction and karma
which explain birth. Once one causeless event is admitted, determinate causal
restriction is lost: anything could arise without a cause. Observed effects,
however, depend upon causally efficacious conditions restricted by place and
time. An eternal cause cannot solve the problem, since that thesis has already
been rejected. Saṃsāra therefore has no first temporal member.

Its cessation requires no symmetrical “last uncaused event”:

```text
birth depends upon affliction and karma
    → exhaustion of those causes
    → no further birth

sprout depends upon seed
    → exhaustion of seed
    → no further sprout
```

Beginninglessness therefore does not entail endlessness. The past regress has
no unconditioned first occurrence, while a future cessation is intelligible
through the removal of the operative conditions.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

This unit gives the wheel of becoming its complete recursive form. An outflow
is not merely the first projection of a destination. It becomes a developed
life whose faculties support fresh labeling, attraction, appropriation, and
action; those operations accumulate another projective capacity.

For the Organon, this is a rigorous distinction between a project and its
execution:

```text
ākṣepa
    delimits a possible extent of embodied development

krama
    executes that projection through successive states

indriya-paripāka
    produces an operative embodied field

kleśa + karma-upacaya
    project the next cycle
```

The source does not call these stages an Oculus, and its embryology should not
be treated as contemporary science. What it does demonstrate is that a
configuration can reproduce itself without an invariant substance: the
resulting embodied field becomes a new cause-bearing field.

The most important practical determination is that recurrence is conditional.
The wheel is ancient beyond any discoverable first beginning, but it is not
absolute. Because its continuation depends upon causes, liberation is
conceivable as causal exhaustion rather than escape by a separate traveler.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit at 129.21–131.02, including the
unequal measure of karmic projection, the cited five-stage embryology, the
accounts of fetal death and live birth, renewed affliction and karma after
faculty-maturation, and the argument that beginningless saṃsāra can terminate
through exhaustion of its causes.

The translation remains provisional. The medical passage contains multiple
damaged compounds, especially at 130.06–130.20, and requires comparison with
critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations. Its
physiology is recorded as source doctrine, not endorsed as modern medicine.
The Study stops at 131.02 and does not enter VAK 3.20.
