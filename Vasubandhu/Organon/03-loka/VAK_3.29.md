# VAK_3.29 — Why Ignorance Is Not Defective Discriminative Comprehension

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)


```sanskrit
संयोजनादिवचनात् कुप्रज्ञा चेन्न दर्शनात् ।
दृष्टेस्तत्संप्रयुक्तत्वात् प्रज्ञोपक्लेशदेशनात् ॥ ३.२९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)


```text
saṃyojanādivacanāt kuprajñā cen na darśanāt /
dṛṣṭes tatsaṃprayuktatvāt prajñopakleśadeśanāt // 3.29 //
```

The first pāda closes the positive argument of 3.28 and then introduces a new
proposal:

```text
saṃyojana-ādi-vacanāt
[avidyā dharmāntaram]

ku-prajñā cet?
na
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| saṃyojanādivacanāt | ablative singular compound | because ignorance is called a fetter and by related functional names |
| kuprajñā | nominative feminine singular compound | faulty or afflicted discriminative comprehension |
| cet | conditional particle | if; suppose one identifies ignorance this way |
| na | negative response | no; rejects the proposed identity |
| darśanāt | ablative neuter singular | because of the case of views; wrong views are afflicted prajñā |
| dṛṣṭeḥ | genitive feminine singular | of view / wrong view |
| tatsaṃprayuktatvāt | ablative singular abstract compound | because of its being associated with that, namely avidyā |
| prajñopakleśadeśanāt | ablative singular compound | because ignorance is taught as a defilement of prajñā |

`Darśanāt` is elliptical. The Bhāṣya supplies `dṛṣṭayo hi kuprajñāḥ`: wrong
views are instances of faulty prajñā.

## 4. Grammar


The opening ablative completes the proof that ignorance is an operative
factor:

```text
saṃyojana-ādi-vacanāt
    [avidyā dharmāntaram]
```

> Because it is spoken of as a fetter and so forth, ignorance is a distinct
> operative factor.

The proposed reduction and rejection are:

```text
avidyā
    ku-prajñā cet?

na
    darśanāt
```

> If ignorance is faulty discriminative comprehension? No, because of the
> case of views.

Two ablatives give the reasons:

```text
dṛṣṭeḥ
    avidyā-saṃprayuktatvāt

avidyāyāḥ
    prajñā-upakleśatvena deśanāt
```

> Because views are associated with ignorance, and because ignorance is
> taught as a defilement of discriminative comprehension.

## 5. Translation


### Literal Translation
> [Ignorance is a distinct operative factor] because it is spoken of as a
> fetter and so forth. If it is faulty discriminative comprehension? No:
> views themselves are afflicted discriminative comprehension, and they are
> associated with ignorance; moreover, ignorance is taught as a defilement of
> discriminative comprehension.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Since ignorance is spoken of as a fetter and the like, [is it] faulty
> discriminative comprehension? No: views are associated with it, and
> ignorance is taught to defile discriminative comprehension.

## 6. Philosophical Translation


In the source's argument, wrong view is a determinate instance of faulty
discriminative comprehension; ignorance is distinct from that view, is
associated with it, and is taught as defiling *prajñā*. The Bhāṣya does not
settle ignorance's intrinsic nature: it questions the proposed definition as
non-disclosure and leaves Dharmatrāta's “I-am” account under discussion.

In the Organon's inner-instrument Techne, *prajñā* is Science of Principles.
This names the project-level function and is not a lexical gloss. The
passage's useful distinction is between a defective determination and the
associated factor that defiles the operation; it does not license treating
every error as the product of one fully specified hidden mechanism.

| darśana | seeing / view | used elliptically for the doctrinal case of wrong views |
| dṛṣṭi | view / wrong view | faulty prajñā associated with avidyā |
| saṃprayukta | associated / conjoined | technical relation requiring distinguishable factors |
| upakleśa | defilement / contaminating factor | role of avidyā relative to prajñā |
| deśanā | teaching / exposition | doctrinal statement used as evidence for the distinction |
| avidyā | ignorance | distinct afflictive condition associated with view and defiling prajñā |

VAK 3.28–3.29 jointly exclude:

```text
avidyā = everything other than vidyā
avidyā = absence of vidyā
avidyā = faulty prajñā
avidyā = wrong view
```

The positive working determination is:

```text
avidyā
    = operative afflictive dharma
    = contrary to vidyā
    = associated with wrong view
    = defiling prajñā
```

## 8. Logical Determination


### Non-identity through association

The first proof has the form:

```text
dṛṣṭi is ku-prajñā

dṛṣṭi is associated with avidyā

therefore:
avidyā is not identical with ku-prajñā
in the relevant functional analysis
```

The Bhāṣya's argument requires wrong view and ignorance to remain distinct
factors in the association it cites.

### Non-identity through defilement

The second proof turns on the relation of defiler and defiled:

```text
avidyā defiles prajñā

the defiling factor and what it defiles
are distinct in this relation

therefore:
avidyā ≠ prajñā
```

The Bhāṣya formulates the reductio: if ignorance were itself prajñā, how could
it be a defilement of prajñā?

### Two levels of error

```text
level 1:
    dṛṣṭi / ku-prajñā
    a determinate erroneous view

level 2:
    avidyā
    the distinct factor associated with wrong view
    and taught as defiling prajñā
```

These relations establish a functional distinction; they do not, by
themselves, provide a complete account of how every wrong view arises or
settle the intrinsic nature of ignorance.

## 9. Interpretive Note


**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya expands “fetter and the like” to bondage, latent
tendency, flood, and yoke. It argues that faulty views are forms of faulty
discriminative comprehension associated with Ignorance, while Ignorance is also said
to defile discriminative comprehension; these relations distinguish the factors
rather than identify them. The explicit identification of wrong views with
`ku-prajñā` and the two non-identity arguments are Bhāṣya determinations.
