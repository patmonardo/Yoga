# VAK_3.52 Bhāṣya — The Inner Seas and the Outer Great Ocean

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ābhyantaraḥ samudro 'sau triguṇaḥ sa tu pārśvataḥ /
ardhārdhenāparāḥ śītāḥ śeṣaṃ bāhyo mahodadheḥ // 3.52 //
```

> That first Śītā is the inner ocean; measured along its side, it is
> threefold. The other Śītās diminish by successive halves. The remainder is
> the outer great ocean.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes the inner and outer oceans, resolves the first
Śītā's two measurement axes, generates the widths of the remaining inner
seas, and identifies the residual interval between Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa
as the saline outer great ocean.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Ābhyantaraḥ samudro 'sau.

Dvau hi samudrāv, ābhyantaro bāhyaś ca. Tadāsau Śītābhyantaraḥ samudraḥ.

Triguṇaḥ sa tu pārśvataḥ.

Aśītir yojanasahasrāṇy asya vaipulyam uktam. Pārśvatas triguṇo bhavati,
Yugandharatīreṇa gaṇyamānaḥ: catvāriṃśat sahasrāṇi lakṣadvayaṃ ca.

Ardhārdhenāparāḥ Śītāḥ.

Yugandharasyeṣādhārasya cāntaraṃ dvitīyā Śītā, ardhena prathamāyāś
catvāriṃśad yojanasahasrāṇi. Tato 'rdhena punas tṛtīyety evam
ardhārdhenāparāḥ Śītā bhavanti, yāvad ardhatrayodaśaśatāni saptamī Śītā.
Dairghyaṃ tu tāsāṃ na parisaṃkhyātam atibahuprakarṣavisarpaṇāt.

Śeṣaṃ bāhyo mahodadheḥ.

Kiṃ śeṣam? Nimindharacakravāḍayor antaram. Tad dhi bāhyo mahāsamudro
lavaṇaḥ, pūrṇaḥ kṣārodakasya.
```

The running witness has minor mechanical damage, notably `pārśvatasriguṇo`
and `hṣārodakasya`. The continuous text provisionally restores evident word
division and reads the latter as `kṣārodakasya`; these are not presented as
critical-edition decisions.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

That is the inner ocean. For there are two oceans, an inner and an outer.
That Śītā—the first—is the inner ocean.

Measured along its side, however, it is threefold. Its breadth has been given
as eighty thousand yojanas. When calculated along the shore of Yugandhara,
its side-measure is three times that: two hundred forty thousand yojanas.

The other Śītās diminish by successive halves. The interval between
Yugandhara and Īśādhāra is the second Śītā; at half the width of the first, it
measures forty thousand yojanas. The third is again half of that, and in this
way the other Śītās diminish successively by halves, until the seventh Śītā,
which measures twelve hundred fifty yojanas. Their length is not enumerated,
because their extension spreads exceedingly far.

The remainder is the outer great ocean. What is the remainder? It is the
interval between Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa. That is the outer great ocean,
saline and filled with salt water.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

The commentary advances through four determinations:

```text
first Śītā
    → classified as the inner ocean

one sea, two axes
    → breadth: 80,000
    → shore-wise measure: 240,000

remaining six Śītās
    → widths generated by successive halving
    → length deliberately left unenumerated

residual outer interval
    → Nimindhara to Cakravāḍa
    → saline outer great ocean
```

Vasubandhu speaks directly throughout. The question `kiṃ śeṣam?`, “What is
the remainder?”, is internal exposition rather than a separately identified
opponent.

No rival school or alternative cosmological measurement is reported in this
unit.

## 5. The Inner Ocean and Its Two Measures

The first Śītā, already located between Sumeru and Yugandhara, is now named
the `ābhyantara-samudra`, inner ocean. Its two measures answer different
questions:

| Axis | Measure |
|---|---:|
| breadth across the interval, `vaipulya` | 80,000 yojanas |
| along the Yugandhara shore, `pārśvataḥ` | 240,000 yojanas |

The threefold statement therefore does not revise the 80,000-yojana breadth.
It applies a different operation along the sea's side or shore:

```text
80,000 × 3 = 240,000
```

The Bhāṣya uses grouped notation—two lakṣas plus forty thousand—to state the
same result.

`Pārśvataḥ` should remain “along the side” or “shore-wise” rather than being
silently assimilated to radial width. The comparison becomes coherent only
when the measurement axis is retained.

## 6. The Remaining Śītā Widths

The halving rule now transfers from mountain heights to sea widths:

| Śītā | Bounding mountains | Width |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Sumeru–Yugandhara | 80,000 yojanas |
| 2 | Yugandhara–Īśādhāra | 40,000 |
| 3 | Īśādhāra–Khadiraka | 20,000 |
| 4 | Khadiraka–Sudarśana | 10,000 |
| 5 | Sudarśana–Aśvakarṇa | 5,000 |
| 6 | Aśvakarṇa–Vinitaka | 2,500 |
| 7 | Vinitaka–Nimindhara | 1,250 |

Only the second interval is explicitly named and measured in the prose; the
remaining values follow from the stated repeated-halving rule and the known
mountain order.

The length (`dairghya`) of the seas is explicitly not calculated because of
their extensive spreading. This is not a claim that length is absent or
indeterminate in principle. It marks a dimension left unenumerated by the
present account.

## 7. Remainder as the Outer Great Ocean

The verse's `śeṣa`, remainder, receives a precise spatial determination:

```text
inner boundary    Nimindhara
outer boundary    Cakravāḍa
residual interval outer great ocean
```

This remainder is not empty space and not an unspecified excess. It is a
positive domain with boundaries and material quality. The prose calls it the
`bāhya-mahāsamudra`, outer great ocean, and distinguishes its saline water
from the eight-qualified water of the inner Śītās.

The contrast is therefore multidimensional:

| Domain | Position | Water quality |
|---|---|---|
| seven Śītās | within the inner mountain system | cool and eight-qualified |
| outer great ocean | between Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa | saline |

The precise width of the outer ocean is not given until VAK 3.53 and must not
be imported into this Study.

## 8. Translation and Boundary Consequences

The final pāda is elliptical. The Bhāṣya determines its sense:

```text
śeṣam
    = Nimindhara–Cakravāḍa interval

that interval
    = outer great ocean
```

Accordingly, “the remainder is the outer great ocean” is clearer than a
strictly possessive rendering such as “the remainder of the outer ocean.”
The residual interval itself constitutes the ocean.

Likewise, `ardhārdhena` gives an iterative rule, not one isolated halving.
Each sea after the first takes half the width of its immediate predecessor.

The running source begins the outer ocean's measure immediately after this
unit, but that numerical phrase is the opening of VAK 3.53. The exact
sequential boundary therefore falls after the definition of its saline
character.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

VAK 3.52 shows how the end of one configuration produces a new positive
domain:

```text
completed inner ring-system
    → bounded remainder
        → outer ocean
```

The remainder is relationally generated—it is what lies after the inner
sequence and before Cakravāḍa—but it is not merely negative. Once determined,
it has its own type, boundaries, and material quality.

For the Organon Dataset, this requires separate representation of:

```text
source operation       completion of the inner sequence
residual extent        Nimindhara–Cakravāḍa interval
positive domain type   outer great ocean
qualitative feature    saline water
pending determination  width supplied by the next verse
```

This is an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's own formal vocabulary.
It guards against treating “remainder” as missing data. A remainder may be
fully meaningful relative to the configuration that leaves it and may become
the ground of the next articulation.

The verse also confirms the importance of axis-indexed measure. One sea can
be 80,000 yojanas across and 240,000 along its shore without contradiction.
The numerical feature is incomplete unless the relation and axis are stored
with it.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete VAK 3.52 unit at 160.19–161.08: the first
Śītā as inner ocean, its breadth and shore-wise measure, the recursive widths
of the remaining Śītās, their unenumerated length, and the saline outer great
ocean constituted by the remainder between Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. `Pārśvataḥ`, the precise
geometrical force of calculation along the Yugandhara shore, the compound
for the seventh sea's 1,250-yojana width, and the damaged `kṣārodakasya`
reading require comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and
established translations.

The Study stops before the outer ocean's width, `lakṣatrayaṃ sahasrāṇi
viṃśatir dve ca`, which begins VAK 3.53. It does not yet begin the detailed
account of the four continents or Jambūdvīpa.
