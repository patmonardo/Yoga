# VAK_3.81 Bhāṣya — Lifespan and the Measure of a Kalpa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ārūpye viṃśatiḥ kalpasahasrāṇy adhikādhikam /
mahākalpaḥ parīttābhāt prabhṛty ardham adhas tataḥ // 3.81 //
```

> In the formless realm, lifespans increase by twenty thousand kalpas at each
> successive station. Beginning with Parīttābha, lifespan is measured in
> mahākalpas; below that, half a mahākalpa is reckoned as one kalpa.

The local kārikā witness compresses the second line. Its division here follows
the Bhāṣya's explicit prose: `parīttābhāt prabhṛti` (“beginning with
Parīttābha”) and `ardham adhas tataḥ` (“below that, half”).

## Continuous Conventional Translation

In the formless realm, lifespans increase successively by twenty thousand
kalpas. In the Sphere of Infinite Space, lifespan is twenty thousand
kalpas; in the Sphere of Infinite Cognition, twenty thousand more; in the
Sphere of Nothingness, twenty thousand more; and at the Summit of Existence,
another twenty thousand more. Their respective lifespans are therefore
twenty, forty, sixty, and eighty thousand kalpas.

What kind of kalpa is meant here: an intermediate kalpa, a contraction-kalpa,
an evolution-kalpa, or a great kalpa? From the Parīttābha class onward,
lifespan is to be understood in mahākalpas. Below that, half a mahākalpa is
reckoned as one kalpa for arranging the lifespans of Mahābrahmā and the
others.

How is this reckoning made? The world evolves for twenty intermediate
kalpas, remains in its evolved state for twenty intermediate kalpas, and
contracts for twenty intermediate kalpas. These sixty intermediate kalpas
are called one and a half kalpas for Mahābrahmā. Accordingly, half a
mahākalpa—forty intermediate kalpas—is reckoned as one kalpa for their
lifespan measure.

## Natural Movement

The Bhāṣya first gives the four formless-realm lifespans, adding twenty
thousand at each successive station. It then asks what unit the word
`kalpa` denotes. The answer divides the reckoning at Parīttābha: above that
boundary a kalpa is a full mahākalpa; below it, a half-mahākalpa is
conventionally counted as one kalpa. The final calculation explains this
lower unit through the world’s evolution, duration, and contraction.

## The Lifespans and Their Unit

| Formless station | Lifespan stated by the Bhāṣya |
|---|---:|
| Ākāśānantyāyatana | 20,000 kalpas |
| Vijñānānantyāyatana | 40,000 kalpas |
| Ākiñcanyāyatana | 60,000 kalpas |
| Bhavāgra | 80,000 kalpas |

These stations lie above the Parīttābha boundary, so these lifespan units are
mahākalpas. Below Parīttābha, the commentary explains the conventional
smaller unit through Mahābrahmā's lifespan:

```text
20 antarakalpas of evolution
+ 20 antarakalpas of remaining evolved
+ 20 antarakalpas of contraction
= 60 antarakalpas = 1½ lower kalpas

½ mahākalpa = 40 antarakalpas = 1 lower kalpa
```

The Bhāṣya's “half” applies below the boundary; it does not make the arūpya
lifespans half-mahākalpas.

## Doctrinal Note

The passage distinguishes a lifespan's number from the measure carried by
the unit `kalpa`. It preserves the ascending 20,000-step series, but clarifies
that the unit itself is realm-indexed: full mahākalpas from Parīttābha
upward, and half-mahākalpas counted as kalpas below. These remain measures of
conditioned existence, including at the Summit of Existence.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: a numerical quantity requires a specified unit, and here that
