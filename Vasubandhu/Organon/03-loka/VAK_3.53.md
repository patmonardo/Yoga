# VAK_3.53

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> लक्षत्रयं सहस्राणि विंशतिर्द्वे च तत्र तु ।
>
> जम्बूद्वीपो द्विसाहस्रस्त्रिपार्श्वः शकटाकृतिः ॥ ३.५३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> lakṣatrayaṃ sahasrāṇi viṃśatir dve ca tatra tu /
>
> jambūdvīpo dvisāhasras tripārśvaḥ śakaṭākṛtiḥ // 3.53 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text

viṃśatiḥ                    → viṃśatir

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| lakṣatrayam | neuter singular numerical compound | three hundred-thousands |
| sahasrāṇi | neuter plural | thousands |
| viṃśatiḥ | cardinal number | twenty |
| dve | neuter dual | two |
| tatra | indeclinable | there, within the outer ocean |
| tu | transitional particle | now, as to a particular continent |
| Jambūdvīpaḥ | nominative masculine singular | Jambūdvīpa, southern continent |
| dvisāhasraḥ | nominative masculine singular | measuring two thousand |
| tripārśvaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | having three sides [of that measure] |
| śakaṭākṛtiḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | having the form of a cart or wagon |

The first line gives 322,000 yojanas: three `lakṣas` plus twenty-two thousand.
The Bhāṣya identifies this as the width of the outer great ocean.

## 4. Grammar

The opening completes the measure begun in 3.52:

```text
saḥ [bāhya-mahāsamudraḥ]
    vistāreṇa
    lakṣa-trayam
    viṃśatiḥ sahasrāṇi
    dve sahasre ca

that [outer great ocean],
    in breadth,
    [measures] three lakṣas,
    twenty thousand,
    and two thousand
```

The verse then moves from field to contained region:

```text
tatra tu
    jambūdvīpaḥ
        dvi-sāhasraḥ
        tri-pārśvaḥ
        śakaṭa-ākṛtiḥ [asti]
```

`Dvisāhasraḥ` is distributed across `tripārśvaḥ`: Jambūdvīpa has three sides
of 2,000 yojanas each. Its fourth side, which explains the cart shape, is not
measured until the opening of 3.54.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> [The outer ocean measures] three hundred-thousands and twenty-two thousand
> [yojanas]. There, Jambūdvīpa has three sides of two thousand and is
> cart-shaped.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The outer great ocean is 322,000 yojanas wide. Within it, the four continents
> occupy the four sides of Sumeru. Jambūdvīpa lies to the south and is shaped
> like a cart; three of its sides measure 2,000 yojanas each. At its center,
> upon the golden earth, the diamond seat comes into formation. All
> Bodhisattvas sit there when they generate diamond-like samādhi, because no
> other support or locus is capable of bearing that event.

The width-axis, fourfold directional placement, southern location, diamond
seat, its unique support-capacity, and its relation to Bodhisattva samādhi are
Bhāṣya determinations. The fourth side's measure belongs textually to 3.54.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The outer field becomes inhabited through directional partition: four
> continents are assigned to four sides of the central world-axis. Within one
> continent, geometrical form yields a still more determinate middle, and that
> middle contains a singular locus whose capacity cannot be transferred to any
> other place. Spatial differentiation therefore becomes functional
> differentiation. A place is not exhausted by coordinates and shape; it may
> possess a unique capacity to support a specific Path-operation.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Nested spatial determination

```text
outer great ocean
    → four directional continent-regions
        → southern Jambūdvīpa
            → its middle
                → diamond seat
```

Each step narrows the address while increasing functional specificity. The
singular locus is intelligible only through the containing hierarchy that
locates it.

### From field to region through form

```text
oceanic field
    + directional assignment
        + side measures
            + shape relation
                → determinate continent
```

Jambūdvīpa is not identified merely by a name. Its identity within the model
is assembled from containment, direction, measure, and morphology.

### Locus-indexed capacity

```text
diamond seat
    → can bear diamond-like samādhi

any other support or locus
    → cannot bear diamond-like samādhi
```

The Bhāṣya predicates a capability, not merely a ceremonial association. The
place is selected by what it can support.

### Operation requires adequate support

```text
Path-operation
    + support below required capacity
        → operation cannot be sustained

Path-operation
    + uniquely adequate support
        → operation can arise
```

The relation generalizes cautiously: even a contemplative event has support
conditions. The Bhāṣya's specific claim remains restricted to Bodhisattvas,
the diamond seat, and diamond-like samādhi.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.53 advances from world-geometry to a topology of capacities. The
container-world is not homogeneous space populated by interchangeable
coordinates. Its regions differ in form and direction; within a region, one
locus differs from every other by the operation it alone can sustain.

This yields a methodical distinction:

```text
location
    answers: where is it?

capacity
    answers: what can occur there?
```

The diamond seat unites the two. Its address is nested within the produced
World System, while its identity is completed by a Path-capacity. The Kośa
therefore moves naturally from descriptive geography to practical function
without confusing them.

For the Organon projection:

```ts
interface CapabilityBearingLocus<Operation> {
  address: readonly string[];
  structuralSupport: string;
  supports: Operation;
  exclusivity: "no-other-locus-sufficient";
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It preserves a
useful rule for the Agent system: an operation should not be specified without
the support-capacity required for its execution. Logical possibility alone
does not guarantee operational realizability within a given domain.

The abstraction is particularly important because it reconnects Physical
World and Path without collapsing them. The produced container is not itself
the Path, but its differentiated loci can be necessary conditions for a Path-
event. Practical function is grounded in, yet not reducible to, spatial form.
