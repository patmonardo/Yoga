# VAK_3.10 Bhāṣya — Intermediate Existence and Directed Transition

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> mṛtyūpapattibhavayor antarā bhavatīha yaḥ /
> gamyadeśānupetatvān nopapanno 'ntarābhavaḥ // 3.10 //

> That which arises here between death-existence and rebirth-existence is
> intermediate existence. Because it has not reached its destination, it has
> not yet arrived in rebirth.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes something already arisen from something that has
reached the ensuing rebirth. It further defines the destination as the place
where a projected karmic result becomes manifest and reaches completion.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What is this so-called intermediate existence?

“That which arises here between death-existence and rebirth-existence” is
called intermediate existence. It is the sentient configuration that comes
forth between death-existence and rebirth-existence in order to reach rebirth
in another place. It is so called because it occupies the interval between
destinies.

How can it be said to have arisen while not yet having arrived in rebirth?

“Because it has not reached its destination, intermediate existence has not
yet arrived in rebirth.” One who has gone into rebirth is called `upapanna`,
because the verbal root `pad` has the sense of going. Intermediate existence
has not reached its destination; therefore it is not yet `upapanna`.

What, then, is the place to be reached? It is the place where the projected
karmic result becomes manifest and reaches completion.

The Nikāyāntarīyas say that rebirth-existence arises from death-existence only
after a break. This is not accepted. Why? On the basis of both reasoning and
authoritative teaching. First, the argument is stated by relying upon
reasoning.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The unit proceeds by definition, lexical objection, causal determination, and
doctrinal challenge:

```text
what is intermediate existence?
    ↓
how can it have arisen without yet being reborn?
    ↓
what defines the destination?
    ↓
can rebirth arise discontinuously from death?
    ↓
proof announced through reasoning and authoritative teaching
```

The opening questions function as an interlocutor's requests for
determination. The expository voice defines the intermediate phase and the
destination. The discontinuity thesis is explicitly attributed to the
Nikāyāntarīyas. The rejection belongs to the Bhāṣya's argument, but its actual
demonstration is deferred to the following verses.

The unit therefore ends in an announced task, not a completed proof. It would
be misleading to treat the existence of the intermediate phase as established
here solely because it has been defined.

## 5. Arisen Is Not Yet Arrived

The central distinction is:

```text
jāta
    an intermediate configuration has arisen

upapanna
    the continuity has gone into and reached the ensuing rebirth
```

The Bhāṣya derives the arrival-force of `upapanna` from the motion-sense of
the verbal root `pad`. Thus the interval is not an empty waiting time. A
conditioned configuration already exists, but it has not yet reached the
state in which the subsequent rebirth is realized.

This yields three distinguishable positions:

```text
death-existence
    terminal phase of the preceding course

intermediate existence
    arisen and destination-directed transition

rebirth-existence
    arrival into the subsequent course
```

The passage speaks of an `ātmabhāva`, a sentient or personal configuration,
not an eternal self travelling unchanged between bodies. Its identity is the
conditioned intermediate phase being defined.

## 6. The Destination as Manifested Result

`Gamya-deśa`, the place to be reached, is not specified by coordinates alone.
The Bhāṣya defines it functionally:

```text
projected karmic result
    becomes manifest there
    reaches completion there

therefore
    that place is the destination
```

The term `ākṣipta-vipāka` indicates a result already projected or impelled
toward realization. Intermediate existence is directed toward the locus at
which that determination can become actual as the new rebirth.

This preserves the distinctions established earlier. The karmic projection
is not the destination, the transition is not the rebirth, and the arisen
intermediate configuration is not yet the completed result. They form one
causal sequence without becoming one undifferentiated event.

## 7. The Discontinuity Thesis and the Proof Standard

The Nikāyāntarīya position denies the mediating phase:

```text
death-existence
    break
rebirth-existence
```

The Bhāṣya rejects this model but does not answer it merely by restating the
definition. It announces two controls:

```text
yukti
    reasoning

āgama
    authoritative teaching
```

This is methodologically important. A hidden transition within the world-
system must be supported inferentially and textually. VAK 3.11 begins the
reasoning with the continuity of a rice-seed series and then faces the proposed
counterexample of a mirror image. Those arguments belong to the next Study.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

`Mṛtyubhava` or `maraṇabhava` is death-existence, while `upapattibhava` is the
ensuing rebirth-existence. The intermediate phase is defined by its position
between them and its orientation toward the latter.

`Bhavati` and `upapanna` should not both be flattened into “is born.” The
intermediate configuration has come to be, but `upapanna` requires arrival at
the new rebirth-position.

`Gamya-deśa` is rendered “destination,” with the Bhāṣya's causal determination
kept visible: it is the locus where projected `vipāka` manifests and completes
itself.

The Nikāyāntarīya denial and the announcement `yuktitaś cāgamataś ca` are
Bhāṣya prose, not words compressed into the Kārikā. They establish the dispute
within which the verse's definition must be evaluated.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The restrained conceptual result is a mediated transition:

```text
prior terminal state
    → arisen intermediate configuration
        → destination-directed passage
            → manifestation and completion of result
                → subsequent rebirth
```

An Outflow or Projection cannot here mean an appearance produced without
mediation. The projected result has a causal direction, an intermediate
phase, a fitting locus of manifestation, and a point of completion. These
moments must remain distinguishable if the process is to be intelligible.

For the later Organon learning pass, the intermediate phase may become a
particularly important model of the apparent “gap” in the wheel: the prior
configuration has ended and the next has not yet been reached, yet continuity
has not simply vanished. That is a project comparison. The Bhāṣya's immediate
claim remains the more exact one: a conditioned `ātmabhāva` arises between
the two existences and proceeds toward its karmically determined destination.

The methodological ending is equally valuable. The unseen mechanism is not
accepted merely because it completes an attractive picture. It must survive
reasoning and authoritative testimony. The following studies will contain
that proof, one argument at a time.
