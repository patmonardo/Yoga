# VAK_3.36 Bhāṣya — Five Reproductive Functions of Affliction

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
uktaṃ ca vakṣyate cānyad atra tu kleśa iṣyate /
bījavan nāgavan mūlavṛkṣavat tuṣavat tathā // 3.36 //
```

> Some members have been explained and others will be explained later. Here,
> however, affliction is intended: like a seed, like a nāga, like a root,
> like a tree, and likewise like a husk.

The Bhāṣya explains why the remaining members of dependent co-arising are not
all repeated here, then differentiates five causal functions of affliction
through five similes.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The remaining limbs are not all explained again here: some have already
> been treated, while others will be treated in the Karma and Affliction
> chapters. The present topic is Affliction. It is like a seed because it
> generates further Affliction, Karma, and Thing; like a nāga because
> it keeps the lake of birth from drying up; like an unextracted root because
> the destinies grow again while it remains; like a tree because it repeatedly
> produces flowers and fruits; and like a husk because Karma enclosed by the
> acquisition of Affliction retains the capacity to germinate into another
> birth, whereas Karma alone does not.

The allocation of each precise causal function to its simile is supplied by
the Bhāṣya.

The running witness has `vīja` for `bīja`, `nāgādhiṣṭitaṃ` beside the later
`nāgādhiṣṭhitaṃ`, and the damaged `kālena kālalṃ`. At 151.03–06 it reads
`kleśaprāptatuṣāvanaddhaṃ`; the continuous text provisionally construes
this as `kleśaprāpti-tuṣāvanaddhaṃ`, “enclosed in the husk of affliction's
acquisition.” These are editorial readings, not established readings of a
critical edition. The cited earlier verses and chapter cross-references
remain Bhāṣya prose rather than Kārikā wording.

## 3. Scientific English Rendering

The remaining limbs are not stated again. Why? Because some have been
explained and others will be explained later. Some members have been explained
here, while others will be explained afterward. Discriminative Cognition has
already been stated: “Discriminative Cognition is specific presentation, and
that is also Mind Essence.” The six Essences have also been stated: “The clear
Form Faculties beginning with the Eye are the supports of those Cognitions.”
Formations and Becoming will be explained in the Karma chapter; Craving and
Appropriation in the Affliction chapter.

It was previously shown that dependent co-arising, in summary, consists of
Affliction, Karma, and Thing. Here, however, Affliction is intended:
it is like a seed, a nāga, a root, a tree, and a husk.

What similarity does Affliction have to a seed and the others? Just as sprout,
stalk, leaves, and so forth arise from a seed, so Affliction, Karma, and Thing
arise from Affliction.

Just as a lake occupied by a nāga does not dry up, so the lake of birth
occupied by the nāga of Affliction does not dry up.

Just as trees whose roots have not been extracted grow again even when cut
down repeatedly, so the destinations arise again while their root of
Affliction has not been extracted.

Just as trees produce flowers and fruits time after time, so Afflictions
repeatedly become the cause of Affliction, Karma, and Thing.

Just as rice grains enclosed in husk are capable of sprouting, while bare
grains are not, so Karma enclosed in the husk of the acquisition of
Affliction is capable of germinating into another birth, while Karma alone is
not. Thus Affliction is to be understood as like a seed and the rest.

## 4. Scope and Voice

Vasubandhu speaks throughout this unit; no independent objector or alternative
school position is introduced. The opening is an architectural cross-reference
within the Kośa:

```text
already explained
    Vijñāna
    six Essences

to be explained in Karmanirdeśa
    Formations
    Becoming

to be explained in Anuśayanirdeśa
    Craving
    Appropriation
```

The present compression does not replace the twelve members with three new
members. It groups dependent co-arising functionally as Affliction, Karma, and
Thing, then isolates Affliction for analysis by analogy.

## 5. Seed and Nāga: Generation and Maintenance

The seed analogy gives Affliction a differentiated generative output:

```text
bīja
    → sprout + stalk + leaves + ...

Affliction
    → Affliction + Karma + Thing
```

Affliction generates the Affliction–Karma–Thing complex, not only further
Affliction.

The nāga analogy shifts from origination to continued presence. A lake
occupied by a nāga does not dry up; likewise the “lake of birth” occupied by
the nāga of Affliction does not dry up. The comparison identifies a condition
of the continuing birth-series; it does not specify a separate mechanism of
maintenance.

## 6. Root and Tree: Regeneration and Repeated Production

The root analogy concerns recurrence after surface interruption:

```text
tree cut down
+ root not extracted
    → growth resumes

present existence interrupted
+ root of Affliction not extracted
    → destinations arise again
```

The relevant term is `anapoddhṛta`, not pulled out or extracted. Mere cutting
of manifest growth does not remove its regenerative ground.

The tree analogy adds repeated productivity. A tree produces flowers and
fruits over time; likewise Afflictions repeatedly cause Affliction, Karma,
and Thing. Seed and tree thus have different emphases here: the
seed comparison names what arises, while the tree comparison adds `asakṛt`,
“repeatedly.”

## 7. Husk: The Conditional Fertility of Karma

The final analogy makes the relation between Affliction and Karma unusually
precise:

```text
rice grain + husk
    → capable of sprouting

bare rice grain
    → not capable of sprouting

Karma + kleśaprāpti
    → capable of germinating another birth

Karma alone
    → not capable of germinating another birth
```

The Bhāṣya compares Karma enclosed by `kleśaprāpti` to a husked grain. It
says this Karma can germinate into another birth whereas Karma alone cannot.
The comparison does not itself describe how long that capacity persists.

This distinguishes Karma, the afflictive condition associated with it, and
the possible result of another birth. The precise mechanism of
`kleśaprāpti` is not worked out in this local comparison.

## 8. Five Non-Redundant Causal Profiles

The five similes can now be stated compactly:

| Simile | Function of Affliction |
|---|---|
| seed | generates Affliction, Karma, and Thing |
| nāga | keeps the lake of birth from drying up while present |
| root | permits regeneration until extracted |
| tree | produces Affliction, Karma, and Thing repeatedly |
| husk | marks Karma's capacity to germinate when enclosed by `kleśaprāpti` |

Together they yield a recursive structure:

```text
Affliction
    generates Karma and Thing
    accompanies the continuing birth-series
    survives surface interruption as root
    repeatedly renews the same causal complex
    conditions Karma's capacity to germinate into another birth
```

The five analogies distinguish the operations of the Affliction–Karma–Thing
sequence without reducing them to one generic causal relation.

## 9. Interpretation

The five analogies articulate Affliction's different relations to the
Affliction–Karma–Thing complex. The husk makes the condition especially clear:
Karma joined with *kleśaprāpti* can germinate into another birth; Karma alone
cannot. The verse presents causal efficacy, not a permanent bearer behind the
sequence.

This Loka–Karma analysis belongs to the Bhava Chakra, distinct from the Dharma
Chakra. BEP is the Abhidharma, with Base, Essence, and Principle as its three
levels of intuition. The further description of BEP as a Posited Process in
Buddhi, with Buddhi as Agent of the Matrix, is a working formulation still in
progress; BEP is not Mahat or the Matrix itself. The Dharma Chakra spans
*saṃvṛti* and *paramārtha* as two determinations of
the same reality, not two worlds or substances. The Absolute Base spans and
combines Form Base and Dharma Base; this unity is Absolute Dharma,
discriminating all Dharmas through Form and Law.

The Kośa presents Vijñāna as Discriminative Cognition joining and governing
Perception and Conception. Their unity is Inconceivable as a homogeneous
operation; its Idea is disclosed in Vijñāna Base. Vijñāna governs Manas and
guides the reading of Dharma Base, remaining distinct from *citta* and
*manas*. Form Base and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti* in distinct
ways, and Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it. Each Dharma has the Defined
Concept `<Base, Essence, Principle>`.
