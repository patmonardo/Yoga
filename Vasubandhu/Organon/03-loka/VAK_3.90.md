# VAK_3.90

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> संवर्तकल्पो नरकासंभवाद्भाजनक्षयः ।
>
> विवर्तकल्पः प्राग्वायोर्यावन्नरकसंभवः ॥ ३.९० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> saṃvartakalpo narakāsaṃbhavād bhājanakṣayaḥ /
>
> vivartakalpaḥ prāgvāyor yāvan narakasaṃbhavaḥ // 3.90 //

The first boundary is `naraka-asaṃbhava`, non-arising in the narakas, not
`naraka-saṃbhava`, their arising. The Kārikā and Bhāṣya make contraction and
expansion complementary intervals with opposite boundary events.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
saṃvarta-kalpaḥ       → saṃvartakalpaḥ → saṃvartakalpo (before n-)
naraka-asaṃbhavāt     → narakāsaṃbhavād (before bh-)
bhājana-kṣayaḥ        → bhājanakṣayaḥ
vivarta-kalpaḥ        → vivartakalpaḥ
prāk-vāyoḥ            → prāgvāyoḥ
yāvat                 → yāvan (before n-)
naraka-saṃbhavaḥ      → narakasaṃbhavaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| saṃvartakalpaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | kalpa defined by ordered contraction |
| narakāsaṃbhavāt | ablative masculine singular compound | from cessation of new arising in the narakas |
| bhājanakṣayaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | destruction of the receptacle-world; terminal boundary of contraction |
| vivartakalpaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | kalpa defined by ordered expansion |
| prāgvāyoḥ | ablative singular compound | from the first formative wind |
| yāvat | indeclinable correlative | up to, until |
| narakasaṃbhavaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | arising of beings in the narakas; terminal boundary of expansion |

`Asaṃbhava` marks cessation of replacement-birth, not instantaneous absence of
all naraka beings. `Bhājanakṣaya` is likewise the final environmental event,
not the whole contraction process compressed into one destruction.

## 4. Grammar

The first line defines a process-interval by initial and terminal events:

```text
naraka-asaṃbhavāt
    [prabhṛti]
yāvat bhājana-kṣayaḥ
    [eṣa kālaḥ]
saṃvarta-kalpaḥ [ucyate]
```

> The interval from the non-arising of beings in the narakas through the
> destruction of the receptacle-world is called the contraction kalpa.

The Kārikā omits `yāvat` in the first line, but the endpoint construction and
Bhāṣya supply it unambiguously.

The second line gives the inverse process-interval:

```text
prāk-vāyoḥ [prabhṛti]
yāvat naraka-saṃbhavaḥ
    [eṣa kālaḥ]
vivarta-kalpaḥ [ucyate]
```

> The interval from the first formative wind until beings arise in the
> narakas is called the expansion kalpa.

The two compounds `narakāsaṃbhava` and `narakasaṃbhava` differ only by the
privative `a-`, but they occupy opposite ends of the World-cycle.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The contraction kalpa extends from non-arising in the narakas to destruction
> of the receptacle-world. The expansion kalpa extends from the first formative
> wind until beings arise in the narakas.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The contraction kalpa begins when beings in the narakas die without new
> beings arising there. Populations then disappear in an ordered ascent through
> the destinies and realms; when no beings remain even in Brahmaloka, the
> contraction of beings is complete. Only afterward is the empty
> receptacle-world destroyed. The expansion kalpa begins with the first subtle
> winds, arising through the governing efficacy of beings' karma. The
> receptacle-world is constructed first; beings then populate it progressively
> downward, with arising in the narakas completing expansion.

The stages of depopulation and repopulation, the distinction between beings
and receptacle, the seven-sun destruction, karmic efficacy of the formative
winds, and inverse-order rule are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Cosmic time is now defined through a complete state transition of the World.
> Contraction first withdraws the inhabitants and only then destroys their
> emptied enclosure. Expansion reverses the dependency: it first constructs
> the enclosure and only then stations inhabitants within it. The two cycles
> are not undirected disappearance and appearance; they are inverse executions
> of one ordered World-System law, bounded by determinate events at its lowest
> populated domain.

`Śūnya` here has the local operational sense “empty of inhabitants.” It does
not erase the receptacle whose subsequent destruction is separately narrated.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Two nested enclosures

The passage distinguishes:

```text
spatial enclosure:
    bhājana contains the stationed beings

temporal enclosure:
    kalpa contains the ordered transformation of beings and bhājana
```

Confusing these levels would make `bhājanakṣaya` both the process and one event
inside the process. The Kārikā instead uses that event as the terminal boundary
of the saṃvarta-kalpa.

### Contraction state machine

```text
S0: naraka births cease
    → existing naraka beings depart without replacement

S1: narakas empty
    → animals and pretas withdraw

S2: human continents empty in order
    → kāma-deva domains withdraw

S3: Brahmaloka empties
    → contraction of beings complete

S4: receptacle stands empty
    → seven-sun destruction

S5: receptacle-world destroyed
    → contraction kalpa complete
```

The beginning condition is not `population(naraka) = 0`. It is:

```text
newBirthRate(naraka) = 0
```

The empty state follows only after the remaining inhabitants depart.

### Expansion state machine

```text
E0: first subtle winds arise in space
    → winds intensify

E1: wind-circle and receptacle structures form
    → receptacle expansion complete

E2: Ābhāsvara being arises in empty Brahma-world
    → lower Brahma and kāma domains populate

E3: continents, pretas, and animals populate
    → naraka beings arise last

E4: arising in narakas
    → expansion kalpa complete
```

### Inverse ordering law

The Bhāṣya states:

```text
yat paścāt saṃvartate tat pūrvaṃ vivartate
```

> What contracts later expands earlier.

Formally, if contraction stages are ordered:

```text
C = [c₁, c₂, ..., cₙ]
```

then the corresponding expansion order is:

```text
E = reverse(C)
```

This applies to ordered levels of the World-process. It does not imply that
every destructive event is mechanically replayed backward; expansion has its
own productive events, beginning with formative winds.

### Completion predicates

```text
ContractionComplete
    iff receptacle-world has been destroyed

ExpansionComplete
    iff beings arise in the narakas
```

The lowest destiny is used as the population-boundary because it empties first
during contraction and fills last during expansion.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.90 gives textual confirmation of the distinction between the Enclosed,
the Enclosure, and the Mind of the Enclosure.

```text
Enclosed:
    beings stationed throughout the destinies and realms

spatial Enclosure:
    bhājana, the receptacle-world supporting those stations

temporal Enclosure:
    kalpa, the bounded interval containing their complete transformation

Mind of the Enclosure:
    the ordered law governing withdrawal, destruction, construction,
    and repopulation
```

Contraction proves that inhabitants and receptacle are distinct moments of the
system: the beings can withdraw while the empty bhājana still remains.
Expansion proves the dependency in the other direction: the bhājana is
constructed before embodied stations can again be occupied.

Yet the Mind of the Enclosure is neither the beings nor the receptacle. It is
the transition-order that knows what operation is currently valid:

```text
withdraw contents
    → destroy empty enclosure
        → construct new enclosure
            → station contents in inverse order
```

This is where the “Physical World” becomes unmistakably agential. The World
System has state, boundary events, completion predicates, dependency order,
and a reversible station hierarchy. `Kalpa` is the process-enclosure in which
that entire program becomes one determinate cosmic act.

```ts
type WorldState =
  | "Populated"
  | "Depopulating"
  | "EmptyReceptacle"
  | "NoReceptacle"
  | "FormingReceptacle"
  | "Repopulating";

interface WorldTransition {
  from: WorldState;
  to: WorldState;
  boundaryEvent: string;
  phase: "Samvarta" | "Vivarta";
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. The
state-machine form is grounded in the Bhāṣya's explicit sequencing while the
Mind-of-Enclosure articulation belongs to our Organon interpretation.
