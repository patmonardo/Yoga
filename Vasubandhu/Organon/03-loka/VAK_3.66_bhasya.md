# VAK_3.66 Bhāṣya — Sudarśana, City at Meru's Center

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
madhye sārdhadvisāhasrapārśvam adhyardhayojanam /
puraṃ sudarśanaṃ nāma haimaṃ citratalaṃ mṛdu // 3.66 //
```

> At the center is the city Sudarśana, 2,500 yojanas on each side and one and
> one-half yojanas high: golden, variegated in its ground, and soft.

The Bhāṣya identifies Sudarśana as the city at the center of Sumeru's summit
and as Śakra's capital. It resolves the city's side and height measures,
assigns gold to its enclosing wall, describes the ground as composed of 101
substances, and explains its softness through the ground's response to a
footstep.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Tasya ca Merumūrdhnaḥ:

Madhye sārdhadvisāhasrapārśvam adhyardhayojanam /
puraṃ Sudarśanaṃ nāma haimaṃ citratalaṃ mṛdu.

Sumerutalasya madhye Sudarśanaṃ nāma nagaram. Dairghye sārdhatṛtīya-
yojanasahasre, ekaikaṃ pārśvam; ucchrāyeṇādhyardhayojanam.

Prākāraḥ sauvarṇaḥ; ekottareṇa dhātuśatenāsya bhūmiś citritā.

Tac ca bhūmitalaṃ tūlapicuvat mṛdusaṃsparśaṃ, pādakṣepotkṣepābhyāṃ
natonnatam. Śakrasya devānām Indrasya rājadhānī.
```

The prose witness has a difficult reading in the length clause:
`dairghye sārdhatṛtīyayojanasahasre`. The kārikā gives the clear compound
`sārdhadvisāhasra-pārśva`, and the prose then says `ekaikaṃ pārśvam`, each
side. I preserve the prose reading here and translate the side measure as
2,500 yojanas in accord with the kārikā; the prose's numerical expression
itself requires textual review. Other spacing and punctuation are
regularized. The wording `ekottareṇa dhātuśatena` is rendered as “one hundred
and one substances,” following the conventional parsing reflected in the
study witness.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

And concerning the summit of Meru:

At its center is a city named Sudarśana, 2,500 yojanas on each side and one
and one-half yojanas high.

In the middle of Sumeru's summit is a city called Sudarśana. Its length is
given in the prose by a difficult numerical expression; the kārikā specifies
2,500 yojanas for each side. Its height is one and one-half yojanas.

Its enclosing wall is golden, and its ground is variegated with one hundred
and one substances.

That ground is soft to the touch like cotton fluff. When a foot is placed
upon it, it sinks; when the foot is lifted, it rises again. It is the capital
of Śakra, Indra of the gods.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

The unit begins by carrying forward the summit of VAK 3.65, then moves from
location through architectural and sensory detail to civic function:

1. Sudarśana is at the center of Sumeru's summit.
2. Its side and height measures are given.
3. Its wall and ground are materially distinguished.
4. The ground's softness is described through touch and movement.
5. The city is identified as Śakra's capital.

The explanation is Vasubandhu's continuous prose. No opponent or competing
school is named. The prose sharpens the kārikā's compact qualities: “golden”
is assigned to the wall, while “variegated” and “soft” are explained of the
ground.

## 5. Dimensions and Textual Difficulty

The kārikā and Bhāṣya distinguish two dimensions:

| Feature | Measure |
|---|---:|
| Each side of Sudarśana | 2,500 yojanas |
| Height | 1½ yojanas |

The first follows the kārikā's `sārdhadvisāhasra-pārśva`, literally a
two-thousand-and-a-half side, and the prose's `ekaikaṃ pārśvam`, “each side.”
The second is directly assigned by `ucchrāyeṇa`, “in height.”

The prose's phrase `sārdhatṛtīyayojanasahasre` does not transparently match
the kārikā's 2,500. Since the source witness is not a critical edition, this
study does not emend the Sanskrit silently. The working translation follows
the kārikā's explicit measure, while the anomalous prose reading remains
visible for later review.

## 6. Wall, Ground, and Tactile Quality

The Bhāṣya assigns the material descriptions to different parts of the city:

```text
enclosing wall → golden
ground         → variegated with 101 substances
ground         → soft to the touch like cotton fluff
```

This prevents “the city is golden and variegated” from becoming a vague
description of one undifferentiated surface. The wall forms the enclosure;
the ground is the variegated, tactile field within it.

`Dhātu` here is used in the local sense of a material constituent or
substance. The passage says the ground is variegated with one hundred and one
such constituents. This architectural usage should not be confused with the
technical enumeration of dhātus elsewhere in the Kośa.

## 7. Ground as Responsive Support

The comparison to cotton fluff is expanded through a paired action:

```text
foot placed → ground sinks
foot lifted → ground rises
```

The ground's softness is not left as an isolated adjective. The commentary
shows how it is encountered through contact and how it responds to the
presence and removal of weight. `Pādakṣepa` and `pādotkṣepa` name the two
conditions under which the ground is respectively depressed and raised.

The text describes a quality of the built environment and its response to
inhabitants. It does not say that the walker creates the ground's disposition;
the action makes that disposition perceptible.

## 8. Sudarśana as Capital

The final phrase identifies Sudarśana as `rājadhānī`, the capital of Śakra,
who is specified as Indra of the gods. Thus its centrality has a civic
determination in addition to its spatial one:

```text
center of Sumeru's summit → spatial location
capital of Śakra         → political function
```

These descriptions converge on one city but answer different questions.
Sudarśana is not simply central by virtue of being a capital, nor is its
political role reducible to its position at the summit's center.

## 9. Organon Study

For the Organon, this passage models the progressive determination of a
habitable place:

```text
location → dimensions → enclosure → ground composition
         → tactile behavior → civic function
```

It also makes a useful distinction between a quality and its manifestation.
The ground is described as soft, and the Bhāṣya makes that quality legible
through its behavior under foot placement and release. The action provides
the condition for knowing the ground's disposition; it does not replace the
ground or its material determination.

This is an Organon reconstruction, not terminology attributed to Vasubandhu.
The unresolved length expression also matters methodologically: a coherent
interpretation should preserve the readable root verse and the damaged prose
reading together until the source can be critically reviewed.
