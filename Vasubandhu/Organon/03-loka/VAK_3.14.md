# VAK_3.14

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> सजातिशुद्धदिव्याक्षिदृश्यः कर्मर्द्धिवेगवान् ।
>
> सकलाक्षः अप्रतिघवान् अनिवर्त्यः स गन्धभुक् ॥ ३.१४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> sajātiśuddhadivyākṣidṛśyaḥ karmarddhivegavān /
>
> sakalākṣaḥ apratighavān anivartyaḥ sa gandhabhuk // 3.14 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
sajāti-śuddha-divya-akṣi-dṛśyaḥ → sajātiśuddhadivyākṣidṛśyaḥ
karma-ṛddhi-vega-vān             → karmarddhivegavān
sakala-akṣaḥ                     → sakalākṣaḥ
a-pratigha-vān                   → apratighavān
a-nivartyaḥ                      → anivartyaḥ
saḥ                              → sa
gandha-bhuk                      → gandhabhuk
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| sajāti | compound member | of the same class or kind |
| śuddha-divyākṣi | compound member | purified divine eye |
| dṛśyaḥ | nominative masculine singular gerundive/adjectival form | visible; capable of being seen |
| karmarddhi | compound member | extraordinary power produced by karma |
| vega-vān | nominative masculine singular possessive adjective | possessing speed or force |
| sakalākṣaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | complete in its faculties |
| apratighavān | nominative masculine singular possessive adjective | unobstructed; not subject to impact |
| anivartyaḥ | nominative masculine singular gerundive with privative prefix | unable to be turned back |
| saḥ | nominative masculine singular pronoun | that intermediate being |
| gandhabhuk | nominative masculine singular agent compound | scent-eater; one sustained by scent |

The Bhāṣya construes the first compound distributively: visible to
intermediate beings of the same class and visible to a purified divine eye.
It does not make `sajāti` qualify the eye.

## 4. Grammar

The verse is a sequence of predicates sharing one subject, `saḥ`, which
refers to the intermediate being established in the preceding verses:

```text
saḥ [antarābhavaḥ]
    sajāti-śuddha-divyākṣi-dṛśyaḥ
    karmarddhi-vega-vān
    sakala-akṣaḥ
    apratigha-vān
    anivartyaḥ
    gandha-bhuk [asti]
```

The Bhāṣya sharpens two compounds:

```text
karmarddhi-vega
    speed of extraordinary movement produced by karma

anivartya
    unable to turn away from the destiny
    toward which the transition was projected
```

Thus `anivartya` concerns destination-fixity, not merely the inability to turn
around spatially.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Visible to those of the same class and to the purified divine eye,
> possessing the speed of karmic power, complete in its faculties,
> unobstructed, and unable to be turned back—that intermediate being feeds on
> scent.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The intermediate being can be seen by intermediate beings of its own class
> and by a highly purified divine eye produced through `abhijñā`. It travels
> with karmically generated extraordinary speed, possesses all five faculties,
> passes through ordinary material obstructions, cannot change the destiny
> toward which it was projected, and in the desire domain takes subtle scent
> rather than gross morsel-food as nutriment.

The restriction to five faculties, production of the divine eye through
`abhijñā`, destination-fixity, desire-domain qualification, and contrast with
gross food are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System gives its transition-phase a determinate operating profile.
> Perception of it requires an appropriate class-position or purified faculty;
> its motion is driven by karmic power; its faculties are complete; ordinary
> material resistance does not stop it; and its course remains fixed upon the
> projected destiny until the conditions for rebirth are complete.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Visibility is access-controlled

```text
CanSee(observer, antarābhava)
    if SameIntermediateClass(observer, antarābhava)
    or HasPurifiedDivineEye(observer)
```

The Bhāṣya reports, under `apare` (“others say”), a further hierarchy in which
deva intermediate beings see all classes and other classes have successively
restricted visibility. That hierarchy is reported rather than adopted as the
unqualified voice of Vasubandhu.

### Transit is powered and constrained by one projection

```text
ākṣepa
    projects a determinate gati
        ↓
karmarddhi-vega
    rapidly conducts the intermediate configuration
        ↓
apratigha
    ordinary material resistance does not arrest transit
        ↓
anivartya
    the projected gati cannot be exchanged for another
        ↓
upapatti-sāmagrī
    completion of rebirth-conditions
        ↓
upapatti
```

The Bhāṣya says that even Buddhas cannot block the operative karmic movement
because that karma is stronger with respect to this particular result. This
is a local causal determination, not a general hierarchy placing karma above
Buddhahood.

### The transition configuration is faculty-complete

```text
sakalākṣa
    = samagra-pañcendriya
    = complete possession of the five sensory faculties
```

Intermediate existence is therefore specified as a functioning sentient
configuration, not merely an abstract interval.

### Nutriment and duration

For a desire-domain intermediate being:

```text
nutriment
    = subtle scent
    ≠ gross morsel-food
```

The Bhāṣya further reports that its duration has no fixed independent measure:

```text
remain in intermediate existence
    until upapatti-sāmagrī is obtained

no separately projected intermediate lifespan
    because transition and coming birth
    belong to one class-continuity
```

The attributed answer comes from unnamed `bhadantaḥ`; its exact authority
remains open for direct Bhāṣya study.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.14 is the World System at its most psychedelic and most technical. The
gandharva is not left as cinematic ornament. The Kārikā assigns it an access
policy, a propulsion system, a complete faculty-profile, a material-passage
rule, a fixed destination, and a subtle nutriment.

```text
visibility rule
motion rule
faculty schema
obstruction rule
destination constraint
support mode
termination condition
```

This is Transcendental Technē as our project characterization. The subtle
process hidden from ordinarily stationed Agents now appears as a disciplined
transition platform. What looks from ordinary perception like disappearance
and reappearance is internally articulated as karmically driven transit.

The reference to the purified divine eye is our first local contact with
`abhijñā`. We record only its textual function here: it produces the perceptual
capacity by which intermediate beings can be seen. The stronger Organon sense
of **Idea** belongs to the dedicated `abhijñā` investigation and is not
projected backward into this Kārikā.

The remarkable claim that a Buddha cannot interrupt this motion shows the
severity of manifestation once projection has become operative. Rudra does
not arbitrarily cancel the lawful determination from inside the Tāmasic World
System. In our higher synthesis, Rudra comprehends the appearance as `jagan
mithyā`; within the World System, however, the projected result executes until
its completion-conditions are met.

That is also why `anivartya` matters more than speed. The transition-form does
not browse possible embodiments. Its next platform is already selected by the
operative projection:

```text
projected destiny
    configures transition
    directs movement
    excludes destination-switching
    completes in rebirth
```

The Kārikā thus gives us conditioned body-deployment in remarkable detail,
while the **Siva Trademark** mastery of manifestation and withdrawal remains
the higher project horizon.
