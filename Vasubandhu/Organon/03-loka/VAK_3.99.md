# VAK_3.99

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कल्पस्य शस्त्ररोगाभ्यां दुर्भिक्षेण च निर्गमः ।
>
> दिवसान् सप्त मासांश्च वर्षाणि च यथाक्रमम् ॥ ३.९९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kalpasya śastrarogābhyāṃ durbhikṣeṇa ca nirgamaḥ /
>
> divasān sapta māsāṃś ca varṣāṇi ca yathākramam // 3.99 //

The Bhāṣya makes two indispensable determinations. `Kalpasya` here refers to
the terminal passage of an **intermediate kalpa**, not the destruction of a
mahākalpa. The repeated `ca` has cumulative force: disease lasts seven months
plus seven days, and famine lasts seven years plus seven months and seven
days.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
kalpasya                    → kalpasya
śastra-roga-ābhyām          → śastrarogābhyāṃ
durbhikṣeṇa                 → durbhikṣeṇa
ca                          → ca
nirgamaḥ                    → nirgamaḥ
divasān                     → divasān
sapta                       → sapta
māsān                       → māsāṃś (before c-)
ca                          → ca
varṣāṇi                     → varṣāṇi
ca                          → ca
yathā-kramam                → yathākramam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| kalpasya | genitive masculine singular | of the kalpa, resolved as antarakalpa by the Bhāṣya |
| śastrarogābhyām | instrumental dual | through weapon-calamity and disease-calamity |
| durbhikṣeṇa | instrumental neuter singular | through famine |
| nirgamaḥ | nominative masculine singular | exit, terminal passage, or conclusion |
| divasān | accusative masculine plural | duration measured in days |
| sapta | indeclinable numeral | seven, understood with each temporal unit |
| māsān | accusative masculine plural | duration measured in months |
| varṣāṇi | accusative neuter plural | duration measured in years |
| ca | coordinating particle | and; explicitly cumulative in the Bhāṣya |
| yathākramam | indeclinable | respectively, according to the stated order |

The instrumental expressions identify three alternative crisis-modes by
which the antarakalpa reaches its terminal passage. They do not describe three
stages that must all occur successively in one exit.

## 4. Grammar

The first half states the three alternatives:

```text
kalpasya nirgamaḥ
    śastreṇa
    rogeṇa
    durbhikṣeṇa ca
```

> The exit of the [intermediate] kalpa occurs through weapons, through
> disease, or through famine.

The transmitted dual compound `śastrarogābhyām` coordinates weapon and disease
under one instrumental ending; `durbhikṣeṇa` adds the third instrumental.

The second half assigns durations in the same order:

```text
śastrakam
    sapta divasān

rogaḥ
    sapta māsān ca [sapta divasān ca]

durbhikṣam
    sapta varṣāṇi ca [sapta māsān ca sapta divasān ca]
```

> Weapon-killing lasts seven days; disease lasts seven months together with
> seven days; famine lasts seven years together with seven months and seven
> days.

Only one `sapta` is overtly positioned in the Kārikā. The Bhāṣya distributes
it across days, months, and years and explicitly identifies each `ca` as
`samuccayārtha`, cumulative.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The kalpa's exit occurs through weapons and disease, and through famine:
> seven days, months, and years, respectively and cumulatively.

The cumulative duration is difficult to represent fully without the
commentary because the verse is radically compressed.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> At the ten-year human lifespan, an intermediate kalpa reaches its terminal
> passage through one of three calamities: weapon-killing, incurable disease,
> or famine. The weapon calamity lasts seven days; the disease calamity lasts
> seven months and seven days; and the famine calamity lasts seven years,
> seven months, and seven days.

The ten-year lifespan condition, alternative character of the crises,
cumulative durations, their ethical causes, and the detailed famine forms are
all Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The practical disorder developed through the Human World now becomes an
> objective crisis-field. Hostility becomes weaponized mutual perception;
> corruption of embodied and environmental relations becomes disease; and
> failed sustenance becomes famine. Each crisis has its own temporal
> enclosure, exactly measured according to the operation through which the
> World reaches its terminal condition. Yet an agent's placement within that
> field is not indifferent to action: even a minimal preserved restraint or
> gift can exclude birth in these terminal intervals.

The names `cañca`, `śvetāsthi`, and `śalākāvṛtti` should be retained as
technical famine-types. Their Bhāṣya explanations are more exact than any
single English replacement.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Three terminal crisis-forms

```text
terminal antarakalpa condition
    → weapon crisis
        → hostile agent-to-agent relation

    → disease crisis
        → collapse of embodied viability

    → famine crisis
        → collapse of the nourishment system
```

These are differentiated manifestations of one produced World-condition, not
three independent cosmic causes.

### Weapon calamity as transformed perception-action

```text
adharmarāga
    + viṣamalobha
    + mithyādharma
        → vyāpāda reaches maximum

Perceive(AgentA, AgentB)
    → StrikeIntention(AgentA)
    → KillIntention(AgentA)

Grasp(AgentA, stick-or-clod)
    → AppearsAs(thing, sharp-weapon)
    → mutual killing
```

The World-object is functionally redetermined by the agent's practical
station. A stick or clod does not enter merely as neutral formed content; in
the terminal hostile field, whatever is grasped becomes operative as weapon.

### Exact temporal typing

```text
WeaponCrisis.duration  = 7 days
DiseaseCrisis.duration = 7 months + 7 days
FamineCrisis.duration  = 7 years + 7 months + 7 days
```

Thus each terminal operation receives its own temporal enclosure. The
increasing duration corresponds to the different mode of crisis, not to a
simple repetition of “seven” at interchangeable scales.

### Practical routing by minimal wholesome action

The received statement introduced by `evaṃ varṇayanti` gives:

```text
one day of abstention from killing
    OR one harītakī respectfully given
    OR one alms-meal respectfully given to the Saṅgha
        → exclusion from birth in the three calamity-antarakalpas
```

This does not mean that one act externally cancels the World-crisis. It
determines the agent's causal routing relative to that already produced
field:

```text
WorldCondition
    ≠ identical for every possible karmic continuity

AgentKarma
    → determines whether that continuity is stationed within it
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.99 completes the conversion of the “Physical World” into Practical
Reason. The catastrophe is physical—weapon, disease, famine—but its genesis
is inseparable from the practical relations of embodied agents.

```text
corrupted caitta operations
    → repeated karmapathas
        → transformed shared relations
            → objective crisis-field
```

This is not the claim that a private thought magically manufactures weather
or disease. It is a systems determination: agents, institutions, bodies,
resources, and their environment have already been shown to form one
conditioned feedback structure. The terminal calamity is the achieved state
of that long process.

The weapon passage gives an especially sharp theory of appearance. Under
maximal hostility, perception immediately enters a lethal action-schema:

```text
another being appears
    → target

available thing appears
    → weapon
```

The object-field has been semantically reorganized by the practical station
of consciousness. Syntax may still name “being,” “stick,” and “clod,” but
their operative meaning inside the World is now target and killing-instrument.
This is exactly why the Kośa cannot be reduced to an inventory of physical
things.

Finally, the small wholesome-action statement introduces a crucial asymmetry.
The World-condition may be enormous, but the agent's relation to it remains
causally discriminated. A single preserved restraint can be a seed whose
result is not to be stationed in that terminal enclosure. This is the direct
hinge from Lokanirdeśa toward Karmanirdeśa.
