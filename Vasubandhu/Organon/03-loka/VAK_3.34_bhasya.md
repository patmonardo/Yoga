# VAK_3.34 Bhāṣya — Ranging across Three Realms

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> svo 'ṣṭālambanam ārūpyo dvayor dhyānadvaye tu ṣaṭ /
> kāmāḥ ṣaṇṇāṃ caturṇāṃ sva ekasyālambanaṃ paraḥ // 3.34 //

> Their own [Form realm] is object for eight, the Formless realm for two; in
> the [higher] pair of dhyānas, six [occur]. The desire realm is object for
> all six, their own realm for four, and the higher [realm] for one.

The Bhāṣya completes the distribution of mental rangings connected with the
form realm. The opening continues the first and second concentrations from
VAK 3.33; the remainder treats the third and fourth concentrations.

The reference of `sva`, the identification of the two dhyāna pairs, and the
affective and Condition membership of each count are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
svo 'ṣṭālambanam.

sa eva rūpadhātus teṣāṃ svaḥ. svo 'ṣṭānāṃ manopavicārāṇām ālambanaṃ,
gandharasopavicārāṃś caturo hitvā.

ārūpyo dvayoḥ.

dharmopavicārayor ālambanam.

dhyānadvaye tu ṣaṭ.

tṛtīyacaturthayor dhyānayoḥ ṣaḍ upekṣopavicārā eva santi, nānye. teṣāṃ
punar ālambanam:

kāmāḥ ṣaṇṇām.

kāmadhātuḥ ṣaṇṇām apy ālambanam.

caturṇāṃ svaḥ.

rūpadhātuś caturṇāṃ, tatra gandharasābhāvāt.

ekasyālambanaṃ paraḥ.

ārūpyadhātur ekasyaiva dharmopavicārasyālambanam. uktā
rūpapratisaṃyuktāḥ.
```

At 148.13 the running witness gives `sa eva rūpadhātus teṣaṃ svaḥ` and then
repeats it as `sa eva rūpadhātus teṣāṃ svaḥ`. The continuous text retains the
second, better-spelled occurrence once. Minor irregularities in compounds
and sandhi are regularized provisionally; the running source is unchanged.

## 3. Continuous Translation
Their own realm is the cognitive Condition for eight. That very Form realm
is “their own” realm. It is the Condition for eight Mental Rangings, with
the four Smell- and Taste-Rangings omitted.

The Formless realm is the Condition for two—the two Dharma-Rangings.

But in the other pair of dhyānas there are six. In the third and fourth
dhyānas only the six equanimity-Rangings exist, and no others. Their
cognitive Conditions are as follows.

The desire realm is the Condition for all six. Their own Form realm is the
Condition for four because Smell and Taste are absent there. The higher,
Formless realm is the Condition for only one, the Dharma-Ranging.

The Mental Rangings connected with the Form realm have now been stated.

## 4. Argument and Natural Boundary

This unit contains no extended objection. It completes one table in two
steps:

1. For the twelve Rangings of the first and second dhyānas established in
   VAK 3.33, it specifies eight with the Form realm and two with the Formless
   realm as cognitive Condition.
2. For the third and fourth dhyānas, it restricts the available affective
   class to six equanimity-Rangings and distributes their lower, own, and
   higher Conditions as six, four, and one.

The closure `uktā rūpapratisaṃyuktāḥ`—“the Form Realm-connected rangings have
been stated”—marks the natural endpoint. The next unit turns to rangings
connected with the formless realm and belongs to VAK 3.35.

## 5. First and Second Concentrations

VAK 3.33 established twelve Mental Rangings in the first two dhyānas:

```text
6 gladness-Rangings
+ 6 equanimity-Rangings
= 12
```

VAK 3.34 specifies their upward and own-realm reach:

```text
own Form realm
    4 available Conditions
    × 2 affective modes
    = 8 Rangings

higher Formless realm
    1 available Dharma Condition
    × 2 affective modes
    = 2 Rangings
```

The four exclusions from the own realm are Smell and Taste under each of the
two affective modes. The two rangings toward the formless realm are the
gladness-Dharma and equanimity-Dharma Rangings.

## 6. Third and Fourth Concentrations

The latter pair contains only:

```text
6 upekṣopavicāras
    equanimity-Rangings across six Conditions
```

Neither the six distress-Rangings nor the six gladness-Rangings occur there.
This is a restriction of affective mode, not yet a restriction to one
Condition. The six equanimity-Rangings retain differentiated range according
to the realm serving as cognitive Condition.

Thus higher meditative placement changes which affective mode can operate
across the Conditions.

## 7. The Six-Four-One Distribution

For the six equanimity-Rangings of the third and fourth dhyānas:

| Cognitive Condition | Count | Available Conditions |
|---|---:|---|
| lower desire realm | 6 | Form, Sound, Smell, Taste, Tangible, Dharma |
| own Form realm | 4 | Form, Sound, Tangible, Dharma |
| higher Formless realm | 1 | Dharma only |

The counts are not arbitrary permissions. They follow from the constitution
of each realm:

```text
desire realm
    all six Conditions present → 6

form realm
    Smell and Taste absent → 4

formless realm
    sensory Conditions absent → 1
```

`Sva` and `para` are relational terms centered on the Form Realm station.
The desire realm is lower, the Form realm is own, and the Formless realm is
higher. These relations do not change the Form Realm connection of the
Rangings.

## 8. Three Axes of Admissibility

An admissible ranging is determined by at least three coordinates:

```text
realm of belonging
    × affective mode
    × cognitive Condition
```

The unit shows why no coordinate can replace the others:

- Form Realm connection does not restrict the Condition to the Form realm;
- third- or fourth-dhyāna station removes gladness and distress but leaves
  six equanimity-Rangings with the desire realm as Condition;
- the target realm restricts which of those fields it can actually supply.

Consequently, ascent in concentration is not equivalent to the disappearance
of the lower realm. The lower field remains cognitively available, but it is
taken up under a transformed affective mode.

## 9. Interpretation

The distribution holds realm-connection, affective mode, and cognitive
Condition together. The first two dhyānas reach eight Conditions in the Form
realm and two in the Formless realm. In the third and fourth, six
equanimity-Rangings reach the desire realm, four the Form realm, and one the
Formless realm. The Condition's constitution and the Ranging's station
jointly determine this pattern.

BEP is the Abhidharma, with Base, Essence, and Principle as its three levels
of intuition. The further description of BEP as a Posited Process in Buddhi,
with Buddhi as Agent of the Matrix, is a working formulation still in
progress; BEP is not Mahat or the Matrix itself.

In the inner-instrument Techne, Vijñāna is Discriminative Cognition joining
and governing Perception and Conception, Inconceivable as a homogeneous
operation and disclosed in Cognition Base. It governs Manas and guides the
reading of Dharma Base, remaining distinct from consciousness (*citta*) and
Mind (*manas*). Form Base and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti*, and
Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it. Each Dharma has the Defined Concept
`<Base, Essence, Principle>`.

## 10. Review Status

This Study covers the complete compact unit at 148.12–148.24. It preserves
the continuation from VAK 3.33, the eight and two Condition-ranges of the
first two dhyānas, the restriction of the latter two dhyānas to six
equanimity-Rangings, and their six-four-one lower, own, and higher Condition
distribution.

The compact syntax of `svo 'ṣṭālambanam` depends on the continuation from
VAK 3.33. The Bhāṣya identifies `dhyānadvaya` here as the third and fourth
dhyānas. The Study stops at the explicit closure of the Form Realm-connected
Rangings and does not begin VAK 3.35.
