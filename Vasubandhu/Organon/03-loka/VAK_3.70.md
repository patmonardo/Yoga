# VAK_3.70

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> पञ्चवर्षोपमो यावद्दशवर्षोपमः शिशुः ।
>
> सम्भवत्येषु सम्पूर्णाः सवस्त्राश्चैव रूपिणः ॥ ३.७० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pañcavarṣopamo yāvad daśavarṣopamaḥ śiśuḥ /
>
> saṃbhavaty eṣu saṃpūrṇāḥ savastrāś caiva rūpiṇaḥ // 3.70 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
pañca-varṣa-upamaḥ         → pañcavarṣopamo
yāvat                      → yāvad
daśa-varṣa-upamaḥ          → daśavarṣopamaḥ
śiśuḥ                      → śiśuḥ
saṃbhavati                 → saṃbhavaty
eṣu                        → eṣu
saṃpūrṇāḥ                  → saṃpūrṇāḥ
sa-vastrāḥ                 → savastrāś
ca                         → ca
eva                        → eva
rūpiṇaḥ                    → rūpiṇaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| pañcavarṣopamaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | resembling a five-year-old |
| yāvat | indeclinable correlative | as far as / up to; marks the range endpoint |
| daśavarṣopamaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | resembling a ten-year-old |
| śiśuḥ | nominative masculine singular | child or newborn |
| saṃbhavati | present active third-person singular | arises, comes into being, is born |
| eṣu | locative masculine plural pronoun | among these, the six kāma-deva classes |
| saṃpūrṇāḥ | nominative masculine plural | complete, fully formed |
| savastrāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | clothed / accompanied by garments |
| ca eva | conjunction plus emphatic particle | and indeed |
| rūpiṇaḥ | nominative masculine plural | the form-possessing ones; Bhāṣya: rūpāvacara devas |

The surface sequence permits `saṃpūrṇāḥ savastrāḥ rūpiṇaḥ` to be heard as a
further description of the six kāma-deva children. The Bhāṣya explicitly
assigns those predicates to the rūpāvacara devas instead.

## 4. Grammar

The first sentence gives a range whose members are distributed across the six
classes of 3.69:

```text
eṣu [ṣaṭsu devanikāyeṣu]
    śiśuḥ
    pañca-varṣa-upamaḥ
    yāvat daśa-varṣa-upamaḥ
    saṃbhavati
```

> Among these [six deva classes], a child arises resembling one of five years,
> up through one resembling one of ten years.

The Bhāṣya supplies `yathāsaṃkhyam`, “respectively,” thereby converting the
range into an ordered sixfold mapping.

The latter construction changes the understood subject:

```text
rūpiṇaḥ [rūpāvacarā devāḥ]
    saṃpūrṇāḥ
    sa-vastrāḥ ca eva
    [upapadyante]
```

> The form-realm devas arise complete and indeed clothed.

This subject change is a Bhāṣya determination essential to the translation.
The two halves specify different populations and different initial forms of
embodiment.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Among these, a child arises resembling one of five years up through one
> resembling one of ten years. The form-possessing ones are complete and
> indeed clothed.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the six kāma-deva classes respectively, newborns appear like children of
> five, six, seven, eight, nine, and ten years of age, and then mature very
> quickly. When a divine boy or girl appears in the lap of a god or goddess,
> the child is reckoned their son or daughter. Rūpāvacara devas, by contrast,
> arise with bodies already complete and already clothed. All devas speak the
> ārya language.

The one-to-one age distribution, rapid maturation, parentage through the locus
of appearance, shift to rūpāvacara devas, complete-body determination,
clothing, and common language are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The destination-class determines the initial presentation of embodiment.
> Within the six sensual deva classes, a newborn's apparent age increases in
> ordered correspondence with its class and is followed by rapid maturation.
> In the form realm, embodiment begins from another initial condition: body
> and garment are complete at arising. The manifestation-event also establishes
> social relation, since the lap in which the child appears determines
> parentage. Birth here initializes bodily state, developmental state, and
> relational position together.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Ordered kāma-deva initialization

Let the six classes retain the order established in 3.69:

| Ordinal | Deva class | Apparent age at arising |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Cāturmahārājakāyika | 5 years |
| 2 | Trāyastriṃśa | 6 years |
| 3 | Yāma | 7 years |
| 4 | Tuṣita | 8 years |
| 5 | Nirmāṇarati | 9 years |
| 6 | Paranirmitavaśavartin | 10 years |

Formally:

```text
for class ordinal n ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}:
    apparentAgeAtArising(n) = n + 4
```

The Bhāṣya's `yathāsaṃkhyam` is what licenses this exact correspondence.

### Initial state and transition rate

```text
kāma-deva arising:
    initial state = class-indexed child-form
    next process  = rapid maturation
```

Initial apparent age and rate of subsequent growth are different variables.
A more developed starting form does not remove development as a process.

### Two embodiment constructors

```text
kāmāvacara rule:
    destination class
        → apparent child-age from 5 through 10
        → rapid maturation

rūpāvacara rule:
    arising
        → complete body
        + clothing already present
```

The second rule must not be appended as additional properties of the first
population. It has a different subject and domain.

### Manifestation and social relation

```text
child appears in lap L
    + L belongs to deva pair P
        → child is assigned as son or daughter of P
```

The site of manifestation functions as a relation-establishing condition.
Parentage is determined through the event's location, not described here as
the outcome of biological gestation.

### Cross-class invariant

```text
for every deva class D:
    language(D) = āryabhāṣā
```

This Bhāṣya addition introduces a shared communicative capacity across
otherwise different embodiment modes.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The central semantic discrimination in VAK 3.70 is between one grammatical
continuation and two doctrinal scopes:

```text
first scope:
    six kāma-deva classes
    → graded child-form
    → rapid maturation

second scope:
    rūpāvacara devas
    → complete body
    → already clothed
```

Without the Bhāṣya, the second line can be flattened into the first, producing
the inaccurate claim that the six kāma-deva children arise complete and
clothed. The commentary does more than add detail: it changes the subject of
the predication.

The useful abstraction is class-conditioned initialization. A being does not
first arise as an indeterminate occupant and only later receive the properties
of its domain. The destination class governs how embodiment initially appears:

```text
station
    → initialization rule
        → bodily presentation
        → developmental trajectory
        → available social relations
```

This continues the Agential character of the Physical World. Loka provides
not only places into which Agents are inserted, but domain-specific procedures
for the manifestation of their bodies and relations.

```ts
type DevaEmbodiment =
  | {
      domain: "Kama";
      classOrdinal: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6;
      apparentAgeAtArising: 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10;
      maturation: "Rapid";
    }
  | {
      domain: "Rupa";
      bodyAtArising: "Complete";
      clothingAtArising: "Present";
    };
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. The union
type is important: it preserves the Bhāṣya's subject change rather than
combining incompatible scope determinations into one record.

The verse also separates completion from cessation. Rūpāvacara embodiment is
complete at its initial appearance, but it is still `upapatti`, conditioned
arising into a determinate World domain. What changes is the initialization of
form, not the fact of manifestation.
