# VAK_3.64 Bhāṣya — Inhabitants of the Terraces and Seven Mountains

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
karoṭapāṇayas tāsu mālādhārāḥ sadāmadāḥ /
mahārājikadevāś ca parvateṣv api saptasu // 3.64 //
```

> On those terraces dwell the Karoṭapāṇis, Mālādhāras, and Sadāmadas; the
> Mahārājika devas also dwell on the seven mountains.

The Bhāṣya assigns the first three named populations to the first three
terraces, places the Four Great Kings and their attendants on the fourth, and
classifies all of them as Cāturmahārājakāyika devas. It then extends their
habitation to villages and towns on the seven mountains beginning with
Yugandhara.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Karoṭapāṇayas tāsu mālādhārāḥ sadāmadāḥ /
mahārājikadevāś ca

Prathamāyāṃ pariṣaṇḍāyāṃ Karoṭapāṇayo nāma yakṣāḥ prativasanti.
Dvitīyāyāṃ Mālādhārāḥ, tṛtīyāyāṃ Sadāmadaḥ, Sadāmattāḥ.
Sarva ete Caturmahārājakāyikāḥ.

Caturthyāṃ tu catvāro mahārājāḥ svayaṃ prativasanti, tatparicārāś ca.
Atas tasyāṃ Mahārājakāyikā devā ity uktam.

Yathā pariṣaṇḍāsu Caturmahārājakāyikā devā, evam

Parvateṣv api saptasu.

Yugandharādiṣu parvateṣu teṣāṃ grāmanigamāḥ. Ata evaiṣa devanikāyaḥ
sarveṣāṃ mahiṣṭhaḥ.
```

The running witness is clear apart from joined words, punctuation, and minor
orthographic variation. The continuous text regularizes spacing and sandhi.
The transmitted phrase `sarveṣāṃ mahiṣṭhaḥ` is retained as “the highest among
all,” while recognizing that the scope of this evaluative statement is not
further elaborated in this unit.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

On those terraces are the Karoṭapāṇis, the Mālādhāras, and the Sadāmadas; and
there are also Mahārājika devas on the seven mountains.

On the first terrace dwell yakṣas called Karoṭapāṇis. On the second dwell the
Mālādhāras; on the third, the Sadāmadas, also called Sadāmattas. All of these
belong to the host of the Four Great Kings.

On the fourth terrace dwell the Four Great Kings themselves and their
attendants. For this reason the devas there are called Mahārājikas.

Just as Cāturmahārājakāyika devas dwell on the terraces, so too do they dwell
on the seven mountains. On the mountains beginning with Yugandhara are their
villages and towns. For this reason this deva host is the highest among all.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

Vasubandhu's explanation proceeds from a compact list to an ordered account of
habitation:

1. Karoṭapāṇis occupy the first terrace;
2. Mālādhāras occupy the second;
3. Sadāmadas occupy the third;
4. the Four Great Kings and their attendants occupy the fourth;
5. the same deva host has settlements on the seven mountains.

The prose makes two classifications explicit. The Karoṭapāṇis are identified
as yakṣas, and all four terrace populations are grouped as
Cāturmahārājakāyikas. It also supplies `Sadāmattas` as another designation
for the Sadāmadas.

No opponent or alternate school position appears. The final phrase calls this
deva host `mahiṣṭha`, “highest” or “greatest among all,” without giving a
separate argument for the comparison.

## 5. Ordered Residence on the Terraces

| Terrace from VAK 3.63 | Residents determined here |
|---:|---|
| First | Karoṭapāṇi yakṣas |
| Second | Mālādhāras |
| Third | Sadāmadas, also called Sadāmattas |
| Fourth | The Four Great Kings themselves and their attendants |

This order depends on the four terraces already measured in VAK 3.63. The
Bhāṣya resolves the compressed first line of the kārikā level by level,
rather than treating all three named classes as co-residents at every terrace.

On the fourth terrace, the phrase “Mahārājika devas” is explained by the
presence of the Four Great Kings themselves and their retinue. The name
therefore identifies the class through its governing figures and their
association, while the earlier levels house other members of that same host.

## 6. Common Class and Local Difference

The Bhāṣya states `sarva ete caturmahārājakāyikāḥ`: all these populations
belong to the Cāturmahārājika host. This shared classification coexists with
distinct names, positions, and roles:

```text
one deva host
    ├── Karoṭapāṇis — first terrace
    ├── Mālādhāras — second terrace
    ├── Sadāmadas — third terrace
    └── Four Great Kings and attendants — fourth terrace
```

Class membership does not erase location. Nor does the distinction between
terraces imply that each population constitutes an unrelated deva order. The
commentary holds collective classification and differentiated residence
together.

## 7. Settlement Beyond Sumeru

The seven mountains are identified as those beginning with Yugandhara. On
them are the villages and towns of this deva host. The particle `api` extends
the habitation range: their domain includes both the measured terraces and
settlements distributed across the surrounding mountains.

The text names these settlement forms but does not give their number,
dimensions, or a separate population assignment for each mountain. The
translation therefore preserves the extension without inventing a finer
geography.

## 8. Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.63 constructed the terraces as spatial positions; VAK 3.64 binds named
populations to those positions and extends the host across further terrain:

```text
measured terrace structure
    → differentiated residents
        → common Cāturmahārājika classification
            → settlements on seven mountains
```

The final evaluation, `mahiṣṭha`, is retained as the Bhāṣya's statement about
the standing of this deva host. The passage itself does not explain whether
“highest” is based on rank, extent, power, or another comparison. It should
not be silently converted into a claim about liberation or ultimate
metaphysical status; the context is the ordering of deva populations in
Lokanirdeśa.

## 9. Organon Study

For the Organon, this passage keeps four relations distinct:

```text
population → residence
population → shared class
population → role or retinue relation
class      → territorial extension
```

The Four Great Kings' residence on the fourth terrace does not make the other
terrace populations identical with them; it places those populations within
the same larger host. Likewise, extension onto the seven mountains enlarges
the host's territory without changing the prior terrace assignments.

This is an Organon reconstruction of the passage's relational structure, not
a modern taxonomy attributed to Vasubandhu. It offers a concrete reminder
that a system's inhabitants need separate records for class, location, role,
and settlement if those relations are to remain intelligible.
