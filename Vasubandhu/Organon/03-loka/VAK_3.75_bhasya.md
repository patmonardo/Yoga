# VAK_3.75 Bhāṣya — Human Measures Across the Four Continents

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
jāmbūdvīpāḥ pramāṇena catuḥsārdhatrihastakāḥ /
dviguṇottaravṛddhayā tu pūrvagodottarāvhayāḥ // 3.75 //
```

> Humans of Jambūdvīpa have a measure of three and a half hastas; those called
> Pūrva, Goda, and Uttara increase successively by doubling.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Humans of Jambūdvīpa measure 3½ hastas in height according to the principal
> account, though some give 4 hastas. The humans of Pūrvavideha measure 8
> hastas, those of Godānīya 16, and those of Uttarakuru 32, each of these three
> successive populations being twice the measure of the preceding one.

The principal and alternative Jambūdvīpa values, exact measures of the other
three populations, and restricted scope of the doubling rule are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Kiṃ khalu yathā bhājanānāṃ pramāṇabheda evaṃ tadvāsinām api sattvānāṃ
pramāṇabhedo 'sti?

Asti, ity āha. Tatra tāvat:

Jāmbūdvīpāḥ pramāṇena catuḥsārdhatrihastakāḥ.

Jambūdvīpakā manuṣyāḥ pramāṇārdhacaturhastakāḥ; kecit tu caturhastakāḥ.

Dviguṇottaravṛddhyā tu pūrvagodottarāhvayāḥ.

Pūrvavidehakā Godānīkā Uttarakauravāś ca manuṣyā aṣṭaṣoḍaśadvātriṃśad-
hastakā yathākramam.
```

The witness has ordinary spacing and sandhi irregularities regularized here.
The commentary's first Jambūdvīpa measure is given as three and a half
`hasta`, while `kecit tu` explicitly preserves an alternative of four. The
prose identifies the successive measures for the other three populations.

## Scientific English Rendering
Do beings have differences in bodily measure just as world-containers have
differences in measure? They do, the Bhāṣya says.

First, the people of Jambūdvīpa measure three and a half hastas; some,
however, say four hastas. Those called Pūrva, Goda, and Uttara increase
successively by doubling. The humans of Pūrvavideha, Godānīya, and Uttarakuru
measure eight, sixteen, and thirty-two hastas, respectively.

## Natural Movement

The passage moves from the measure of the world-container (`bhājana`) to the
measure of its inhabitants (`tadvāsin sattva`). Vasubandhu answers the
question affirmatively, then gives the population measures: Jambūdvīpa has a
principal value and an attributed alternative; the remaining three form the
explicit sequence 8, 16, 32.

## Measure and Doubling

| Population | Measure | Status in the Bhāṣya |
|---|---:|---|
| Jambūdvīpa | 3½ hastas | Principal account |
| Jambūdvīpa | 4 hastas | Alternative attributed to “some” |
| Pūrvavideha | 8 hastas | First of the successive series |
| Godānīya | 16 hastas | Twice the preceding measure |
| Uttarakuru | 32 hastas | Twice the preceding measure |

The doubling phrase is resolved by the prose's ordered values for
Pūrvavideha, Godānīya, and Uttarakuru. It should not be made into a strict
doubling from Jambūdvīpa's principal value of 3½ hastas. The kārikā's
compressed first compound is clarified by the Bhāṣya, which also preserves
the four-hasta alternative.

## Doctrinal Note

The cosmological difference of the containers is accompanied by a difference
in the bodily measure of their inhabitants. The passage specifies a
continent-indexed correlation; it does not claim that a container's own
extent and an inhabitant's body-size are numerically the same kind of
measure.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: the world-domain is correlated with determinate bodily
conditions among its inhabitants. This is a project-level observation, not
Vasubandhu's technical terminology.
