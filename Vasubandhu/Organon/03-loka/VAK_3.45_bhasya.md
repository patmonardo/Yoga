# VAK_3.45 Bhāṣya — The Karmically Formed Wind-Maṇḍala

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
tatra bhājanalokasya saṃniveśam uśanty adhaḥ /
lakṣaṣoḍaśakodvedham asaṃkhyaṃ vāyumaṇḍalam // 3.45 //
```

> There they describe the arrangement of the container-world: below is a
> wind-maṇḍala sixteen hundred-thousand yojanas in vertical extent and
> incalculable in breadth.

The Bhāṣya identifies the scale as the great thousandfold world-system,
distinguishes vertical from lateral extent, and supplies the genesis of the
wind-maṇḍala through the governing efficacy of the karma of all beings.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Uktaḥ sattvaloko; bhājanaloka idānīṃ vaktavyaḥ.

Tatra bhājanalokasya saṃniveśam uśanty adhaḥ /
lakṣaṣoḍaśakodvedham asaṃkhyaṃ vāyumaṇḍalam.

Trisāhasramahāsāhasralokadhātor evaṃ saṃniveśam icchanti. Yad utākāśa-
pratiṣṭham adhastād vāyumaṇḍalam abhinirvṛttaṃ sarvasattvānāṃ
karmādhipatyena. Tasya yojanalakṣāṇāṃ ṣoḍaśakam udvedhaḥ, pariṇāhenāsaṃkhyam.
Tathā ca dṛḍhaṃ yan mahālagno 'pi vajreṇa bhettum aśaktaḥ. Tasyopariṣṭāt—
```

The running witness reads `ākāśaṣpratiṣṭham` and `dṛḍhaṃṃ`; spacing and the
evident duplicated nasal have been regularized provisionally. The expression
`mahālagno 'pi vajreṇa` is textually uncertain and has not been silently
resolved into a confident critical reading.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The sentient world has been explained; the container-world is now to be
explained.

There they describe the arrangement of the container-world: below is a
wind-maṇḍala sixteen hundred-thousand yojanas in vertical extent and
incalculable in breadth.

They maintain that the arrangement of the great thousandfold world-system is
as follows. Below, established in space, a wind-maṇḍala comes into formation
through the governing efficacy of the karma of all beings. Its vertical
extent is sixteen hundred-thousand yojanas, while its breadth is
incalculable. It is so firm that even the adamantine force described by the
damaged expression in the witness is unable to break it. Above it—

The final words open directly into VAK 3.46 and its account of the
water-maṇḍala. The present Study stops before that next structure is
introduced.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

This is a short foundational unit with four determinations:

```text
new object of exposition
    → bhājanaloka

scale
    → trisāhasramahāsāhasralokadhātu

first lower support
    → vāyumaṇḍala established in space

genesis and character
    → formed through the karmic efficacy of all beings
    → measured vertically, incalculable laterally, extraordinarily firm
```

The plural verbs `uśanti` and `icchanti`—“they describe,” “they
maintain”—mark the arrangement as a received cosmological account. No named
objector appears, but Vasubandhu does not cast the model as an unqualified
first-person assertion.

## 5. From Sentient World to Container-World

The transition at the beginning is architectonic:

```text
sattvaloka
    beings, their modes of existence, arising, persistence, death,
    rebirth, and fixed or open trajectories

bhājanaloka
    the shared receptacle or environmental arrangement
    in which those trajectories are embodied
```

`Bhājana` conveys a receptacle or container. The distinction changes the
object-range of the exposition; it does not yet assert that the two worlds
have independent causal histories. Indeed, the explanation immediately
relates the formation of the container to the karma of sentient beings.

The term `saṃniveśa` is equally important. Vasubandhu is not beginning with a
single undifferentiated physical substance but with an ordered disposition
of supports. VAK 3.45 gives the lowest member of the construction; subsequent
verses will place further maṇḍalas above it.

## 6. Measure, Position, and Firmness

The Bhāṣya resolves the verse's two measures:

| Dimension | Determination |
|---|---|
| vertical extent, `udvedha` | sixteen `lakṣas` of yojanas |
| lateral extent, `pariṇāha` | `asaṃkhya`, incalculable |

Since one `lakṣa` is one hundred thousand, the stated vertical extent is
1,600,000 yojanas. `Asaṃkhya` must not be allowed to cancel that determinate
measure: it applies to breadth, not to every dimension.

The wind-maṇḍala is `adhaḥ`, below, and `ākāśapratiṣṭha`, established in
space. The latter expression states its situation but does not provide a
general theory of space in this unit.

The concluding firmness claim gives the wind support objective stability
within the received model. Because the comparison involving `mahālagna` and
`vajra` is corrupt or uncertain in this witness, its general force—resistance
even to adamantine breaking—is clearer than its exact wording.

## 7. Formation Through Karmic Efficacy

The central prose addition is:

```iast
sarvasattvānāṃ karmādhipatyena
```

The wind-maṇḍala has `abhinirvṛtta`, come into formation, through the
`karmādhipatya` of all sentient beings. `Adhipatya` here names governing or
predominant efficacy: the common support is an effect within the karmic order
rather than an unexplained background merely presupposed by it.

The relation is collective in scope:

```text
karma of all beings
    → governing efficacy
        → formation of a common wind support
```

This does not make the wind-maṇḍala a private image in an individual mind.
The same passage calls it common in causal scope, spatially articulated, and
extraordinarily firm. Conversely, that firmness does not make it
self-grounding or causally independent.

“Collective karma” is a useful shorthand, but the literal wording should
remain visible: the text speaks of the governing efficacy of the karma “of
all beings,” not of a separately named collective substance.

## 8. Translation and Doctrinal Consequences

The Kārikā's syntax alone leaves the dimension qualified by `asaṃkhya`
compressed. The Bhāṣya determines the reading:

```text
lakṣaṣoḍaśakodvedham
    → sixteen lakṣas of yojanas vertically

asaṃkhyaṃ
    → incalculable in lateral extent
```

`Vāyumaṇḍala` is retained as “wind-maṇḍala.” “Wind-circle” is possible, but
“circle” can misleadingly suggest only a line or circumference, while the
prose attributes thickness, breadth, and supporting firmness to the domain.

The doctrinal sequence is genetic as well as spatial:

```text
not merely: below lies wind

but: wind comes into formation
     through karmic efficacy
     and functions as the first support
     in an ordered world-system
```

The passage should not be translated into modern physics. Its measurements
and causal explanation belong to the Kośa's received cosmological model.
Preserving that status is compatible with recognizing the model's exact
internal relations.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage joins the apparently opposed determinations of objectivity and
conditioned genesis:

```text
common and resistant world-support
    together with
formation through the karma of beings
```

For the Organon, this is a strong example of configuration preceding isolated
objecthood. The container-world first appears as `saṃniveśa`, an ordered
placement of supports, and the initial support is intelligible through its
position, dimensions, causal genesis, and capacity to bear what follows.

An Ocular dataset would therefore need to keep distinct:

```text
entity             vāyumaṇḍala
world role          lower support
spatial relations   below, established in space
dimensions          determinate height, incalculable breadth
genetic condition   karmic efficacy of all beings
functional quality  extraordinary firmness
source status       received cosmological account
```

That schema is an Organon reconstruction, not language attributed to
Vasubandhu. It guards against two counterfeit simplifications: reducing the
world to private projection, or treating its apparent solidity as evidence
of causal independence.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete compact VAK 3.45 unit at 157.25–158.04: the
transition to the container-world, the scale of the great world-system, the
wind-maṇḍala's position and dimensions, its karmically conditioned formation,
and its firmness.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. `Ākāśapratiṣṭha`, the
damaged `mahālagno 'pi vajreṇa` expression, the best rendering of `uśanti`,
and the technical scope of `karmādhipatya` require comparison with critical
Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations.

The terminal `tasyopariṣṭāt`, “above it,” is retained to show the natural
forward connection, but the water-maṇḍala and all determinations belonging to
VAK 3.46 are reserved for the next Study.
