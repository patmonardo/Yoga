# VAK_3.28

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> हेतुरत्र समुत्पादः समुत्पन्नः फलं मतम् ।
>
> विद्याविपक्षो धर्मोऽन्योऽविद्यामित्रानृतादिवत् ॥ ३.२८ ॥

The supplied Devanāgarī reads `समुत्पन्नः`. The Bhāṣya's Kārikā citation and
the syntax support neuter `समुत्पन्नं`, corresponding to `फलं मतम्`. The
supplied witness is preserved above; the IAST below follows the
Bhāṣya-supported form.

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

## 4. Grammar

The first half is a pair of nominal determinations:

```text
atra
    samutpādaḥ = hetuḥ

samutpannam
    phalam matam
```

> Here dependent arising is the cause; the dependently arisen is regarded as
> the result.

The Bhāṣya clarifies that these are relational roles:

```text
aṅgam
    hetubhūtam apekṣya
    pratītyasamutpādaḥ

aṅgam
    phalabhūtam apekṣya
    pratītyasamutpannam
```

The second half defines `avidyā`:

```text
avidyā
    anyaḥ dharmaḥ
    vidyā-vipakṣaḥ
    amitra-anṛta-ādi-vat
```

> Avidyā is another dharma opposed to vidyā, as an enemy is opposed to a
> friend and untruth to truth.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Here dependent arising is the cause, while the dependently arisen is
> regarded as the result. Avidyā is another dharma opposed to vidyā, as with
> an enemy, untruth, and similar expressions.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A member is called **dependent arising** insofar as it functions as the
> cause from which another member arises, and **dependently arisen** insofar
> as it functions as a result. The same member may bear both determinations
> under different relations. Ignorance is neither everything other than
> knowledge nor the mere absence of knowledge; it is a distinct operative
> factor standing contrary to knowledge, as an enemy stands contrary to a
> friend and untruth to truth.

Relational role, the rejection of two alternative definitions of ignorance,
and its status as an operative condition are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Cause and result are not intrinsic labels fixed permanently upon separate
> members. A determination is result relative to what conditions it and cause
> relative to what it conditions. Within this relational process, ignorance
> cannot be an empty absence. It is an operative contrary of knowledge whose
> causal efficacy allows distorted determination to enter the generative law
> of the World System.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Cause and result are relational functions

Given:

```text
A → B → C
```

then:

```text
B relative to A
    = samutpanna / result

B relative to C
    = samutpāda / cause
```

The Bhāṣya compares this with one person being a son relative to his father
and a father relative to his child. There is no disorder because the relation
changes:

```text
same member
    + different apekṣā
    → different causal role
```

This clarifies 3.26–3.27. `Vastu` can be `phala` relative to prior karma and
`āśraya` for renewed causal production without contradiction.

### Ignorance is an operative contrary

Three possibilities are distinguished:

```text
1. avidyā = anything other than vidyā
   Too broad: faculties such as the eye would become ignorance.

2. avidyā = sheer absence of vidyā
   Too weak: an absence cannot perform the conditioned functions assigned.

3. avidyā = a distinct dharma opposed to vidyā
   Accepted: an operative contrary capable of functioning as pratyaya.
```

Therefore:

```text
avidyā
    ≠ informational zero

avidyā
    = causally active opposition within the field of knowing
```

This explains how ignorance can enter the hypothetical syllogism:

```text
If avidyā is operative,
then saṃskāra can arise.
```

The antecedent is a positive causal condition, not the assertion of a blank.

### The two halves belong together

```text
first half:
    causal roles are relational

second half:
    the first afflictive condition must be operationally determinate
```

Together they establish that dependent origination requires both an exact
relation and factors capable of functioning within that relation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The massive Dharma discussion surrounding 3.28 should not obscure the basic
Kārikā. Its two principles are compact and decisive.

First:

```text
the same determination
    may be result of its ground
    and cause of its consequence
```

This is the conversion we identified in 3.26:

```text
phala
    → vastu / āśraya
    → renewed hetu
```

The Bhāṣya now supplies the exact reason: `apekṣābheda`, difference of
relation. Cause and result do not name two eternally separated worlds. They
are functions disclosed within the articulated Middle.

Second, avidyā is not eternal darkness and not a mere vacancy where knowledge
has failed to arrive. It is active opposition to knowledge. That is why more
information alone may not remove it: the contrary determination must be met
by a genuinely transformative `pratipakṣa`.

For our Jñāna-mārga, this matters profoundly. Logical-scientific meditation
does not merely fill gaps in an incomplete representation. It identifies the
operative contrary that bends the Ground–Condition sequence toward renewed
bondage and comprehends the exact conditional relation through which that
distortion becomes effective.

Within the Yogācāra assimilation, the opposition occurs in Me as a
determination of the World-field. `Vidyā` and `avidyā` are not two external
cosmic objects. They are opposed modes through which the conditional system
is articulated and enacted. The Kośa's primary claim remains narrower:
avidyā is a distinct causally effective dharma contrary to vidyā.
