# VAK_3.65 Bhāṣya — Meru's Summit and the Vajrapāṇi Peaks

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
merumūrdhni trayastriṃśāḥ sa cāśītisahasradik /
vidikṣu kūṭāś catvāra uṣitā vajrapāṇibhiḥ // 3.65 //
```

> The Thirty-Three dwell on Meru's summit, which has an eighty-thousand
> directional extent. In the intermediate directions are four peaks inhabited
> by Vajrapāṇis.

The Bhāṣya first gives the summit's dimension in two differently construed
accounts. It then identifies the intermediate directions as the summit's four
corners and describes a 500-yojana peak at each, inhabited by yakṣas called
Vajrapāṇis.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Merumūrdhni Trayastriṃśāḥ.

Atha kiyān Merumūrdhā?

Sa cāśītisahasradik.

Ekaikapārśvam aśītisahasrāṇi yathaivādhastāt.

Anye punar āhuḥ: sa viṃśatisahasradik. Catvāri pārśvāny asya, catasro
diśaḥ. Ekaikaṃ pārśvaṃ viṃśatir yojanasahasrāṇi; samantāt parikṣepeṇāśītir
iti.

Tasya ca:

Vidikṣu kūṭāś catvāra uṣitā vajrapāṇibhiḥ.

Sumerumūrdhno vidiśaḥ koṇāḥ. Tāsu pañcayojanaśatapramāṇāś catvāraḥ kūṭā
abhyudgatāḥ, yeṣu Vajrapāṇayo nāma yakṣāḥ prativasanti.
```

The witness is legible, with routine spacing and punctuation regularized.
The two dimensional accounts are retained as alternatives. In the concluding
sentence, `pañcayojanaśatapramāṇāḥ` states the measure of the four peaks but
does not specify a separate dimension such as height or width; the translation
therefore preserves “500-yojana measure.”

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The Thirty-Three dwell on Meru's summit.

Now, how large is Meru's summit?

It has an extent of eighty thousand in the directions. Each side measures
80,000 yojanas, just as below.

Others, however, say that it has an extent of twenty thousand in each
direction. It has four sides, corresponding to the four directions. Each side
measures 20,000 yojanas; all around, its circumference is therefore eighty
thousand.

And concerning the summit:

In the intermediate directions are four peaks inhabited by Vajrapāṇis.

The intermediate directions of Meru's summit are its corners. At these
corners four peaks rise, each with a measure of 500 yojanas. Yakṣas called
Vajrapāṇis dwell in them.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voices

The Bhāṣya moves through three steps:

1. It locates the Thirty-Three on the summit.
2. It asks for the summit's extent and preserves two interpretations of the
   eighty-thousand measure.
3. It construes the intermediate directions as corners and locates four
   peaks and their inhabitants there.

The second dimension is explicitly attributed to an alternative view:
`anye punar āhuḥ`, “others, however, say.” The first interpretation is stated
before it without a named authority; the alternative is not silently merged
with it. Both preserve the transmitted figure while assigning it different
scope.

The Bhāṣya identifies the Vajrapāṇis specifically as yakṣas. Here `Vajrapāṇi`
is functioning as a name for those inhabitants; the passage does not develop
the term as an epithet or symbolic doctrine.

## 5. The Summit's Measure

The disagreement concerns what the eighty-thousand figure measures:

| Account | Measure of one side | Total around four sides |
|---|---:|---:|
| First account | 80,000 yojanas | 320,000 yojanas, if treated as a four-sided perimeter |
| Alternative account | 20,000 yojanas | 80,000 yojanas |

The first account says each side is 80,000, “as below.” The comparison refers
to the corresponding lower measure already given in the cosmography. The
alternative explicitly counts four sides and assigns 20,000 yojanas to each,
so that the total circumference is 80,000.

The Sanskrit `aśītisahasradik` by itself compresses the relation between
number and measured feature. The prose disambiguates it by specifying either
the individual side or the total surrounding measure. The two accounts
therefore differ in metric scope, not in the numeral transmitted.

## 6. Directions, Sides, and Corners

The verse's `vidikṣu`, “in the intermediate directions,” is explained as the
corners of the summit. The Bhāṣya relates the four directional sides to their
four intersections:

```text
four sides / cardinal directions
    → four intermediate directions
        → four corners
            → one peak at each corner
```

This makes the corner a positional relation within the summit's outline. The
four peaks are located by that relation, not merely listed alongside the
directions. Each has a stated measure of 500 yojanas. Since no axis for that
measure is specified here, it should not be sharpened to “500 high” or “500
wide.”

## 7. Peak Inhabitants

The four peaks are inhabited by yakṣas called Vajrapāṇis. The sentence
connects each level of the description to the next:

```text
summit geometry → corner location → projecting peak → yakṣa inhabitants
```

The summit's extent, its corners, the peaks' measure, and their residents are
separate determinations. In particular, the text does not state that the
Thirty-Three and Vajrapāṇis share one residence: it places the former on the
summit and the latter in the corner peaks.

## 8. Doctrinal Determination

This passage extends the spatial account from the measured terraces in 3.63
and their inhabitants in 3.64 to the summit domain:

```text
Meru summit
    ├── Thirty-Three: summit residents
    ├── directional measure: two commentarial construals
    └── four corners
          └── four measured peaks
                └── Vajrapāṇi yakṣas
```

The disputed measurement demonstrates that a number alone is insufficient
for a determinate spatial claim. One must also know whether it applies to
each side or to the whole perimeter. The Bhāṣya keeps both readings visible,
while the location and population of the peaks are stated directly.

## 9. Organon Study

For the Organon, the passage presents a simple requirement for representing
measurements: preserve both the value and the Feature it measures.

```text
80,000 yojanas
    ├── first account: each summit side
    └── alternative: total perimeter
```

The same record should preserve provenance for the two readings. Otherwise a
later account could mistake a genuine interpretive difference for a numerical
error or collapse both into an unsupported single geometry.

The remaining spatial relations can be recorded independently: sides define
corners; corners locate peaks; peaks have inhabitants. This is an Organon
reconstruction of the passage's structure, not terminology attributed to
Vasubandhu.
