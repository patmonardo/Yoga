# VAK_3.90 Bhāṣya — Contraction and Expansion of the World

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
saṃvartakalpo narakāsaṃbhavād bhājanakṣayaḥ /
vivartakalpaḥ prāgvāyor yāvan narakasaṃbhavaḥ // 3.90 //
```

> The contraction kalpa runs from the non-arising [of beings] in the narakas
> to the destruction of the receptacle-world. The expansion kalpa runs from
> the first wind until the arising [of beings] in the narakas.

The paired boundaries are deliberately asymmetric: `narakāsaṃbhava` is
non-arising at the start of contraction; `narakasaṃbhava` is arising at the
end of expansion.

## Continuous Sanskrit

The following presents the Bhāṣya's continuous movement from the definition
of contraction through the inverse definition of expansion. Sandhi and
punctuation are lightly regularized; the source's compressed classifications
are retained.

```iast
Antarakalpaḥ, saṃvartakalpo, vivartakalpo, mahākalpaś ceti. Tatra tāvat:

Saṃvartakalpo narakāsaṃbhavād bhājanakṣayaḥ.

Narakeṣu hi sattvāsaṃbhavāt prabhṛti yāvad bhājanasaṃkṣayaḥ. Dve hi
saṃvartanī: gatisaṃvartanī, dhātusaṃvartanī ca. Punar dve saṃvartanī:
sattvasaṃvartanī, bhājanasaṃvartanī ca.

Bhavati sa kālo yan narakeṣu sattvāś cyavante, nopapadyante. Sa ārambhaḥ
saṃvartakalpasya. Yad ayaṃ loko viṃśatyantarakalpān vivṛtto 'sthāt, tan
niryātaṃ vaktavyam; yad viṃśatim antarikalpān saṃvartiṣyate, tat pratipannaṃ
vaktavyam.

Yadā narakeṣv eka-sattvo nāvaśiṣṭo bhavati, iyatāyaṃ lokaḥ saṃvṛtto bhavati,
yad uta narakasaṃvartanī. Yasya tadānīṃ niyataṃ narakavedanīyaṃ karma gṛhyate,
sa lokadhātvantaranarakeṣu kṣipyate. Evaṃ tiryaksaṃvartanī pretasaṃvartanī ca
vaktavyā. Mahāsamudragatās tiryañcaḥ pūrvaṃ saṃvartante; manuṣyasahacariṣṇavas
tu taiḥ sārdhaṃ bhavanti.

Sa kālo yan manuṣyeṣv anyatamaḥ sattvaḥ svayam anācāryakaṃ dharmatāprātilambhikaṃ
prathamaṃ dhyānaṃ samāpadyate. Sa tasmād vyutthāya vācaṃ bhāṣate: “Sukhaṃ vata
vivekajaṃ prītisukhaṃ, śāntaṃ vata vivekajaṃ prītisukham.” Taṃ ca śabdaṃ śrutvā
anye 'pi sattvāḥ samāpadyante, kālaṃ kṛtvā brahmaloka upapadyante.

Yadā Jambūdvīpa ekasattvo 'pi nāvaśiṣṭo bhavati, iyatāyaṃ lokaḥ saṃvṛtto
bhavati, yad uta Jambūdvīpasaṃvartanī. Evaṃ Pūrvavideha-Godānīya-Uttarakuru-
saṃvartanī vaktavyāḥ. Yadā manuṣyeṣv ekasattvo 'pi nāvaśiṣṭo bhavati,
iyatāyaṃ lokaḥ saṃvṛtto bhavati, yad uta manuṣyagatisaṃvartanī. Uttarakuravas
tu kālaṃ kṛtvā kāmāvacareṣu deveṣūpapadyante, tatra vairāgyābhāvāt.

Evaṃ Cāturmahārājakāyikeṣv api deveṣu prathamaṃ dhyānaṃ samāpadyā brahmaloka
upapadyante. Yadā tatraikasattvo 'pi nāvaśiṣṭo bhavati, iyatāyaṃ lokaḥ
saṃvṛtto bhavati, yad uta Cāturmahārājakāyikasaṃvartanī. Evaṃ yāvat
Paranirmitavaśavartisaṃvartanī vaktavyā. Yadāikasattvo 'pi kāmāvacareṣu deveṣu
nāvaśiṣṭo bhavati, iyatāyaṃ lokaḥ saṃvṛtto bhavati, yad uta kāmadhātusaṃvartanī.

Brahmaloke 'py anyatamaḥ sattvo dharmatāprātilambhikaṃ dvitīyaṃ dhyānaṃ
samāpadyotthāya vācaṃ bhāṣate: “Sukhaṃ vata samādhijaṃ prītisukhaṃ, śāntaṃ vata
samādhijaṃ prītisukham.” Taṃ śabdaṃ śrutvā 'nye 'pi sattvāḥ samāpadyante, kālaṃ
ca kṛtvā Ābhāsvareṣu deveṣūpapadyante. Yadā Brahmaloka ekasattvo 'pi nāvaśiṣṭo
bhavati, iyatāyaṃ lokaḥ saṃvṛtto bhavati, yad uta sattvasaṃvartanī.

Tataḥ śūnye bhājane, ita eva sāmantakāt sattvānāṃ tadākṣepake karmaṇi
parikṣīṇe, sapta sūryāḥ prādurbhūya krameṇa yāvat pṛthivīṃ Sumeruṃ ca niḥśeṣaṃ
dahanti. Tasmād evaṃ prajvalitād arcir vāyunā kṣiptaṃ śūnyaṃ brāhmaṃ vimānaṃ
nirdahat paraiti. Tac ca tadbhūmikam evārcir veditavyam; na hi visabhāgā
apakṣālāḥ kramante, tat-saṃbaddha-saṃbhūtatvāt “tat” ity uktam; kāmāvacaro hy
agni rūpāvacaram agniṃ saṃbadhnātīti. Evam anyasyām api saṃvartanyāṃ yathāyogaṃ
veditavyam. Narakeṣu sattvānāṃ cyutyanutpādāt prabhṛti yāvad bhājanānāṃ
saṃkṣaya eṣa kālaḥ.

Vivartakalpaḥ prāgvāyor yāvan narakasaṃbhavaḥ // 3.90 //

Vāyoḥ prabhṛti yāvan narakeṣu sattvasaṃbhavaḥ, eṣa kālo vivartakalpa ity ucyate.
Tathā saṃvṛte hi loke ākāśamātrāvaśeṣaś ciraṃ kālaṃ tiṣṭhati, yāvat punar api
sattvānāṃ karmādhipatyena bhājanānāṃ pūrvanimittabhūtā ākāśe mandamandā vāyavaḥ
syandante. Tadā yad ayaṃ loko viṃśatyantarakalpān saṃvṛto 'sthāt, tan niryātaṃ
vaktavyam; yad viṃśatim antarikalpān vivartiṣyate, tad upayātaṃ vaktavyam.

Tatas te vāyavo vardhamānā yathoktaṃ vāyumaṇḍalaṃ janayanti. Tataḥ śanaiḥ
yathoktakramavidhānaṃ sarvaṃ jāyate: apmaṇḍalaṃ, kāñcanamayī mahāpṛthivī,
dvīpāḥ, Sumeruprabhṛtayaś ca. Prathamaṃ tu brāhmaṃ vimānam utpadyate; tato
yāvat Yāmīyaṃ, tataḥ vāyumaṇḍalādīni. Iyatāyaṃ loko vivṛtto bhavati, yad uta
bhājanavivartanī.

Athānyataraḥ sattva Ābhāsvarebhyaś cyutvā śūnye brāhme vimāna utpadyate. Anye
'pi sattvās tataś cyutvā Brahmapurohiteṣūpapadyante; tato Brahmakāyikeṣu,
Paranirmitavaśavartiṣu, krameṇa yāvad Uttarakurau, Godānīye, Pūrvavidehe,
Jambūdvīpe, preteṣu, tiryakṣu, narakeṣūpapadyante. Dharmatā hy eṣā: yat paścāt
saṃvartate, tat pūrvaṃ vivartate.

Yadā narakeṣv ekaḥ sattvo 'pi prādurbhūto bhavati, tadā yad ayaṃ loko
viṃśatyantarakalpān vivartate, tan niryātaṃ bhavati; yad viṃśatim antarikalpān
vivṛtaḥ sthāsyati, tad upayātaṃ bhavati.
```

**Textual note.** The witness at `[179|11]` reads `thamādvāyoḥ`; the context
and Kārikā support `vāyoḥ prabhṛti` (“from the wind onward”), adopted here
without treating the damaged reading as certain. Several technical compounds
and a short fire-propagation explanation are difficult; their translation
below follows the clear sequence without pretending to settle every detail.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

The four kinds are the intermediate kalpa, contraction kalpa, expansion kalpa,
and great kalpa. First, the contraction kalpa runs from the non-arising of
beings in the narakas through the exhaustion of the receptacle-world. There are
two ways of classifying contraction: by destiny and by realm; and again by
beings and by the receptacle-world.

Contraction begins when beings die in the narakas and are no longer reborn
there. The world has completed its twenty intermediate kalpas of expansion
and has entered the period that will be twenty intermediate kalpas of
contraction. As each destination or realm empties, the text marks its
contraction: narakas, animals, pretas, human destinations and continents, then
the desire-realm gods. Beings with karma fixed for a naraka birth are said to
be cast into narakas in another world-system. The great-sea animals withdraw
first; those associated with human beings remain together with them.

Among human beings, one person spontaneously attains the first absorption,
without a teacher, through the natural acquisition of its characteristic. On
rising, that person voices the peace and happiness born of seclusion. Hearing
this, others attain the same absorption and, after death, are reborn in
Brahmaloka. The account then tracks the emptying of Jambūdvīpa and the other
continents, humanity, and the successive classes of desire-realm gods. The
Uttarakurus, lacking dispassion, are reborn among desire-realm gods. The gods
of the Four Great Kings and the higher desire-realm classes attain the first
absorption and are reborn in Brahmaloka.

In Brahmaloka, another being attains the second absorption and voices the
peace and happiness born of concentration. Others follow and are reborn among
the Ābhāsvara gods. When not even one being remains in Brahmaloka, the
contraction of beings is complete. Only then, with the receptacle empty and
the karma that sustained it exhausted, do seven suns appear and successively
burn the earth and Sumeru completely. The flames are carried onward by the
wind and consume the empty Brahmā-vimāna. The Bhāṣya adds that fire spreads
between connected levels; it says the same principle applies, as appropriate,
to other contractions. Thus the interval begins with the cessation of rebirth
in the narakas and ends only with the exhaustion of the receptacles.

The expansion kalpa runs from the first wind until beings arise in the
narakas. After contraction, the world remains for a long time with only space
left. Then, through the governing efficacy of beings' karma, subtle winds
begin to stir in space as the first sign of the receptacle-world. The period
of contraction has run its course, and the period of expansion is now
approaching. As the winds grow, the wind-circle forms; then, in the stated
sequence, come the water-circle, golden great earth, continents, and Sumeru.
The Brahmā-vimāna arises first among these structures, followed by the other
levels. This completes the expansion of the receptacle-world.

Beings then descend from the Ābhāsvara realm: one arises in the empty
Brahmā-vimāna, and others are reborn successively among Brahmapurohita,
Brahmakāyika, and the desire-realm gods, then in the northern, western,
eastern, and southern continents, and among pretas, animals, and finally in
the narakas. This is the natural order: what contracts later expands first.
When even one being has appeared in the narakas, the twenty intermediate
kalpas of expansion have run their course; the subsequent twenty-kalpa
duration of the expanded world is then approaching.

## Natural Movement

The Bhāṣya distinguishes contraction of beings from destruction of the
receptacle. Destinies and realms empty in sequence; beings' contraction ends
when Brahmaloka is empty, but the receptacle persists until its sustaining
karma is exhausted and the seven suns consume it. Expansion reverses the
sequence: karmically conditioned winds initiate the receptacle, its structures
arise, and beings repopulate the realms from above downward. The two lines of
the Kārikā therefore name opposite boundary events, not merely two names for
the same duration.

The opening's two pairs of `saṃvartanī` are reported as classifications—by
destiny/realm and by beings/receptacle. The ensuing account develops chiefly
the latter distinction; it need not be forced into a more elaborate taxonomy
than the Bhāṣya gives.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: the passage describes ordered withdrawal and re-emergence, with
the sequence reversed between contraction and expansion. Karma conditions the
arising of the environmental process in the Bhāṣya's account. These may be
compared with project concepts of configuration and outflow, but those are
comparative lenses, not Vasubandhu's terminology or claims.
