# VAK_3.37 — Karma and Maturation-result Thing

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)


```sanskrit
तुषितण्डुलवत् कर्म तथैवौषधिपुष्पवत् ।
सिद्धान्नपानवद्वस्तु तस्मिन् भवचतुष्टये ॥ ३.३७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)


```text
tuṣitaṇḍulavat karma tathaivauṣadhipuṣpavat /
siddhānnapānavad vastu tasmin bhavacatuṣṭaye // 3.37 //
```

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| tuṣitaṇḍulavat | indeclinable comparative compound | like a rice grain enclosed in its husk |
| karma | nominative neuter singular | Karma as productive activity and subject of the first analogies |
| tathaiva | adverbial combination | just so; likewise |
| auṣadhipuṣpavat | coordinated comparative compound | like a fruit-ripening plant and like a flower |
| siddhānnapānavat | indeclinable comparative compound | like prepared food and drink |
| vastu | nominative neuter singular | Thing (Vastu), here specifically the maturation-result |
| tasmin | locative neuter singular pronoun | in that |
| bhavacatuṣṭaye | locative neuter singular compound | in the fourfold Becoming |

`Tuṣitaṇḍula` is not a loose pair, “husk and grain.” The Bhāṣya parses it as
the grain that possesses or is enclosed by a husk: Karma joined with the
Affliction-enclosure established in 3.36.

## 4. Grammar


The first hemistich predicates three analogical determinations of `karma`:

```text
Karma
    tuṣi-taṇḍula-vat
    tathā eva auṣadhi-vat
    puṣpa-vat

Karma is
    like a husked grain,
    likewise like a plant,
    and like a flower
```

The compound `auṣadhipuṣpavat` is unpacked by the Bhāṣya into two comparisons
with different causal functions.

The second hemistich predicates the result analogy and locates the process:

```text
vastu
    siddha-anna-pāna-vat

Thing (Vastu) is
    like prepared food and drink

tasmin bhava-catuṣṭaye
    = in that fourfold Becoming
```

The Bhāṣya enumerates the four modes as Intermediate Becoming, Rebirth
Becoming, Life-course Becoming, and Death Becoming.

## 5. Translation


### Literal Translation
> Karma is like a grain enclosed in its husk, and likewise like a plant and a
> flower. Thing (Vastu) is like prepared food and drink, within that fourfold
> Becoming.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Karma is like a rice grain in its husk, and likewise like a medicinal plant
> and a flower. Vastu is like prepared food and drink, within those four modes
> of becoming.

## 6. Philosophical Translation


The verse separates three causal determinations: Karma enclosed by Affliction
retains germinative capacity; Karma ripens and is proximate to maturation;
the Thing called maturation-result is available for experience but does not,
merely as result, produce another maturation-result. This distinction
supports the Bhāṣya's argument that liberation is possible.

The prepared food and drink are similes for the experienced result. The next
unit's account of food as sustaining beings is a separate inquiry, not a
meaning to import into this comparison. The four modes—Intermediate,
Rebirth, Life-course, and Death Becoming—locate the causal process without
turning them into a self-driving bearer.

At the project level, the four modes locate a conditioned causal process;
they do not constitute a self-driving bearer. This distinction is essential
to the verse's account of Karma, maturation-result, and release.

The causal categories remain sharply divided:

```text
kleśa  → enables and renews productive capacity
Karma  → matures and produces the Thing called maturation-result
Vastu  → names the matured Thing available for experience
```

## 8. Logical Determination


### Three Karma analogies, three determinations

```text
husked grain:
    Karma + Affliction-enclosure
        → retains germinative capacity

plant:
    Karma ripens
        → reaches its terminus in fruit
        → does not repeatedly emit new vipākas after maturation

flower:
    Karma
        → proximate cause of vipāka-arising
```

The analogies distinguish retention of capacity, terminal maturation, and
causal proximity. They are not interchangeable images of generic growth.

### Maturation-result Thing is not recursively productive

```text
Karma
    → Thing called maturation-result

Thing called maturation-result
    ↛ another maturation-result merely by being a result
```

The Bhāṣya states the modal consequence:

```text
if every maturation-result necessarily produced another,
    then no terminal condition could occur;

if no terminal condition could occur,
    liberation would be impossible.
```

Therefore the distinction between productive cause and consumable result is
not taxonomic decoration. It is required for the intelligibility of release.

### Fourfold Becoming

```text
antarābhava
    → upapattibhava
        → pūrvakālabhava
            → maraṇabhava
                → [conditional transition]
```

The last arrow is not automatic. A renewed Intermediate Becoming depends on
the Affliction–Karma conditions established in 3.36–3.37. The four modes
classify the birth-continuum; they do not constitute a self-driving substance.

### Functional determination replaces bare inventory

```text
named factor       functional determination
------------       ------------------------
Affliction         enables recurrence
Karma              bears productive maturation
Vastu              names matured result
```

Each factor remains distinct through its causal role in the whole.

## 9. Interpretive Note


**Translation note.** Karma joined with Affliction is like grain in a husk,
retaining the capacity to germinate; it is also like a plant whose process ends in
ripened fruit, and like a flower near the arising of fruit. On the provisionally
supplied negative, Karma does not continue producing maturation-results after
ripening. Vastu is the maturation-result, available for experience but unable to
germinate another result. The four modes of Becoming are Intermediate, Rebirth,
Life-course, and Death. The distinct forces of the plant and flower comparisons, the
determination of `vastu` as maturation-result, and the enumeration of the four modes
of Becoming are Bhāṣya determinations.
