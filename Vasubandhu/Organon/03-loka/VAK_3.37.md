# VAK_3.37

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तुषितण्डुलवत् कर्म तथैवौषधिपुष्पवत् ।
>
> सिद्धान्नपानवद्वस्तु तस्मिन् भवचतुष्टये ॥ ३.३७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tuṣitaṇḍulavat karma tathaivauṣadhipuṣpavat /
>
> siddhānnapānavad vastu tasmin bhavacatuṣṭaye // 3.37 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
tuṣi-taṇḍula-vat            → tuṣitaṇḍulavat
karma                       → karma
tathā eva                   → tathaiva
auṣadhi-puṣpa-vat           → auṣadhipuṣpavat
siddha-anna-pāna-vat        → siddhānnapānavat
vastu                       → vastu
tasmin                      → tasmin
bhava-catuṣṭaye             → bhavacatuṣṭaye
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| tuṣitaṇḍulavat | indeclinable comparative compound | like a rice grain enclosed in its husk |
| karma | nominative neuter singular | karma as productive action and the subject of the first analogies |
| tathaiva | adverbial combination | just so; likewise |
| auṣadhipuṣpavat | coordinated comparative compound | like a fruit-ripening plant and like a flower |
| siddhānnapānavat | indeclinable comparative compound | like prepared food and drink |
| vastu | nominative neuter singular | resultant fact, here explicitly the maturation-result |
| tasmin | locative neuter singular pronoun | in that |
| bhavacatuṣṭaye | locative neuter singular compound | in the fourfold becoming |

`Tuṣitaṇḍula` is not a loose pair, “husk and grain.” The Bhāṣya parses it as
the grain that possesses or is enclosed by a husk: karma joined with the
afflictive enclosure established in 3.36.

## 4. Grammar

The first hemistich predicates three analogical determinations of `karma`:

```text
karma
    tuṣi-taṇḍula-vat
    tathā eva auṣadhi-vat
    puṣpa-vat

karma is
    like a husked grain,
    likewise like a plant,
    and like a flower
```

The compound `auṣadhipuṣpavat` is unpacked by the Bhāṣya into two comparisons
with different causal functions.

The second hemistich predicates the result analogy and locates the process:

```text
vastu
    siddha-anna-pāna-vat

resultant fact is
    like prepared food and drink

tasmin bhava-catuṣṭaye
    = in that fourfold becoming
```

The Bhāṣya enumerates the four modes as intermediate becoming, rebirth
becoming, life-course becoming, and death becoming.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Karma is like a grain enclosed in its husk, and likewise like a plant and a
> flower. Resultant fact is like prepared food and drink, within that fourfold
> becoming.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Karma joined with affliction is like a grain enclosed in its husk, retaining
> the capacity to germinate. It is like a plant whose process terminates in
> the ripening of fruit, because a karma that has matured does not continue
> producing further maturation-results; and it is like a flower because it is
> the proximate cause of the arising of its fruit. Resultant fact, insofar as
> it is maturation-result, is like prepared food and drink: available for
> experience, but not itself capable of germinating into another
> maturation-result. This causal process operates within the four modes of
> becoming: intermediate, rebirth, life-course, and death.

The distinct forces of the plant and flower comparisons, the determination of
`vastu` as maturation-result, and the enumeration of the four becomings are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Karma is productive capacity brought toward fruition: preserved by its
> afflictive enclosure, terminating when its maturation has occurred, and
> standing proximate to the result it produces. Resultant fact is already the
> prepared yield of that process—available to be undergone, not a new cause
> merely because it appears. Within intermediate existence, arising, lived
> duration, and death, the World System distinguishes what can generate from
> what can only be received. Because result does not automatically generate
> result, termination remains logically possible.

The causal categories remain sharply divided:

```text
kleśa  → enables and renews productive capacity
karma  → matures and produces result
vastu  → is the matured result available for experience
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### Three karma analogies, three determinations

```text
husked grain:
    karma + kleśa-enclosure
        → retains germinative capacity

plant:
    karma ripens
        → reaches its terminus in fruit
        → does not repeatedly emit new vipākas after maturation

flower:
    karma
        → proximate cause of vipāka-arising
```

The analogies distinguish retention of capacity, terminal maturation, and
causal proximity. They are not interchangeable images of generic growth.

### Result is not recursively productive

```text
karma
    → vipāka-vastu

vipāka-vastu
    ↛ vipāka-vastu₂ merely by being vipāka
```

The Bhāṣya states the modal consequence:

```text
if every vipāka necessarily produced another vipāka,
    then no terminal condition could occur;

if no terminal condition could occur,
    liberation would be impossible.
```

Therefore the distinction between productive cause and consumable result is
not taxonomic decoration. It is required for the intelligibility of release.

### Fourfold temporal articulation

```text
antarābhava
    → upapattibhava
        → pūrvakālabhava
            → maraṇabhava
                → [conditional transition]
```

The last arrow is not automatic. A renewed intermediate becoming depends on
the affliction–karma conditions established in 3.36–3.37. The four modes
classify the birth-continuum; they do not constitute a self-driving substance.

### Functional essence replaces bare inventory

```text
named factor       functional determination
------------       ------------------------
kleśa              enables recurrence
karma              bears productive maturation
vastu              presents matured result
```

The factor is comprehended through the causal work it performs in the whole.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.37 prevents a disastrous model of Learning: the assumption that every
output must automatically become another productive cause. Vasubandhu insists
that a maturation-result, merely as result, is prepared content for experience.
It does not germinate again by itself.

```text
productive lineage
    → formed result
        → consumption / experience
        ↛ automatic productive lineage
```

This gives the DataFrame an exact causal status. Formed content may carry
provenance from karma without itself inheriting karma's productive role. A
result row is not silently promoted into an executable rule. Reproduction
requires the appropriate causal ground—above all the afflictive enclosure
that keeps karma germinative.

The Learning architecture therefore needs typed lineage:

```ts
type CausalRole = "afflictive-enabler" | "productive-karma" | "mature-result";

interface WorldSystemRecord {
  causalRole: CausalRole;
  provenance: CausalLineage;
  canProduceFutureEmbodiment: boolean;
}
```

This is an Organon projection, not a translation of the Kārikā. Its logical
point is textual: provenance does not erase role-difference. A result can be
traced to its cause without thereby becoming the same type of cause.

The four modes of becoming now provide the lifecycle within which these roles
operate. Intermediate existence is not an ornamental afterlife doctrine
inserted into an unrelated argument. It is one phase in the precise lifecycle
of a causally generated embodiment:

```text
transition-state
    → pickup of embodiment
        → embodied life-course
            → disposal at death
```

That rendering is our Agent-side interpretation; the textual terms remain
intermediate, rebirth, life-course, and death becoming. The philosophical gain
is that embodiment appears as a staged operation inside the World System, not
as the migration of an unexplained object between two sealed worlds.

Most importantly, the sequence has an exit condition. If mature result itself
were necessarily reproductive, no amount of transformation could stop the
process. Because recurrence instead depends upon affliction-enabled karma,
the causal root can be removed and already-matured result can be undergone
without seeding another compulsory embodiment.

This is the clean culmination of 3.36–3.37:

```text
affliction makes karma germinative
karma produces maturation-result
result is experienced rather than self-replicated
removal of the enabling ground makes termination possible
```

The World System is learned recurrence, but it is not an eternal recurrence.
