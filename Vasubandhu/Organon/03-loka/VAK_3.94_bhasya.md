# VAK_3.94 Bhāṣya — Buddhas and Pratyekabuddhas

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
buddhatvam apakarṣe hi śatād yāvat tadudbhavaḥ /
dvayoḥ pratyekabuddhānāṃ khaḍgaḥ kalpaśatānvayaḥ // 3.94 //
```

> Buddhahood arises during decline, from [the eighty-thousand-year limit]
> down to one hundred. Pratyekabuddhas arise in both phases; the
> rhinoceros-horn-like solitary type has a continuity of one hundred kalpas.

The Bhāṣya makes the verse's temporal compressions explicit and clarifies
that the hundred kalpas of the solitary type are great kalpas spent in the
accumulations for awakening.

## Continuous Sanskrit

The natural unit begins with the question whether Buddhas arise during
increase or decrease. It continues through the Pratyekabuddha types and the
Bhāṣya's explanation of their limited teaching activity, stopping before the
discussion of Cakravartins.

```iast
Kiṃ punar utkarṣe buddhā utpadyante, āhosvid apakarṣe?

Apakarṣe hi śatād yāvat tadudbhavaḥ.

Aśītivarṣaśatāyuṣi prajāyām apakarṣe ārabdhe, yāvad varṣaśatāyuṣo manuṣyā
bhavanti, etasminn antare buddhā utpadyante.

Kasmān notkarṣakāle? Tadā hi duḥsamudvejāḥ sattvā bhavanti. Kasmān na śatāt?
Tadā hi pañca kaṣāyā abhyutsadā bhavanti, tadyathā: āyuṣkaṣāyaḥ,
kalpakaṣāyaḥ, kleśakaṣāyaḥ, dṛṣṭikaṣāyaḥ, sattvakaṣāyaś ca.

Apakarṣasyādhastāt pratyavarā āyurādayaḥ; kiṭabhūtatvāt kaṣāyā ucyante.
Dvābhyāṃ hi jīvitopakaraṇavipattī yathākramam; dvābhyāṃ kuśalapakṣavīpattiḥ,
kāmasukhallikātmaklamathānuyogādhikārād gṛhipravrajitapakṣayor vā;
ekenātmabhāvavīpattiḥ, pramāṇarūpārogyabalavṛddhismṛtivīryadhairyabhraṃsāt.

Atha pratyekabuddhānāṃ kasmin kāle utpādaḥ?

Dvayoḥ pratyekabuddhānām: utkarṣe 'py apakarṣe 'pi. Dvividhā hi
pratyekabuddhāḥ: vargacāriṇaḥ khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpāś ca.

Tatra vargacāriṇaḥ śrāvakapūrviṇaḥ pratyekajinā ucyante. Pṛthagjanapūrviṇo 'pi
santīty apare: ye 'nyatrotpāditanirvedhabhāgīyā iha svayaṃ mārgam abhisaṃbudhyante.
Tathā hi pūrvayogaṃ paṭhanti: “Parvate kila pañcaśatāni tāpasānāṃ kaṣṭāni
tapāṃsi tapyante sma, yāvat pratyekabuddhasahoṣitena markaṭenāgamya
tadīryāpathasaṃdarśanāt pratyekabodhim abhisaṃbaddhā” iti. Na cāryāḥ santaḥ
kaṣṭāni tapāṃsi tapyeran.

Khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpāḥ punar ekavihāriṇaḥ. Teṣāṃ pratyekabuddhānāṃ:

Khaḍgaḥ kalpaśatānvayaḥ // 3.94 //

Mahākalpānāṃ śataṃ bodhisaṃbhāreṣu caritaḥ khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpo bhavati.

Vinopadeśenātmānam ekaṃ pratibudhyanta iti pratyekabuddhāḥ. Te hy ekam ātmānaṃ
damayanti, nānyān. Kiṃ punar atra kāraṇam? Na hi tāvad aśaktā dharmaṃ deśayituṃ,
pratisaṃvitprāptatvāt. Śakyaṃ ca taiḥ pūrvabuddhānāṃ śāsanam anusmṛtyāpi dharmaṃ
deśayitum.

Nāpi niṣkaruṇāḥ: sattvānugrahārtham ṛddher āviṣkaraṇāt. Nāpi sattvānām abhavyatvāt;
tathā hi laukikamārge vītarāgāḥ saṃvidyante. Kiṃ tarhi? Pūrvābhyāsavaśād
alpotsukatādhimuktatvān notsahante gambhīradharmagrahaṇāya pareṣāṃ vyāpṛtum.

Anusrotogāminīnāṃ hi prajānāṃ duṣkaraṃ pratisrotaḥnayanam. Gaṇaparikarṣaṇa-
prasaṅgaparihārārthaṃ ca vyākṣepa-saṃsarga-bhīrutvāt.
```

**Textual notes.** The running witness contains spelling irregularities; obvious
spacing and sandhi are regularized, while the unusual `kiṭabhūta` comparison
is left untranslated below as uncertain. The story of the five hundred
ascetics is explicitly introduced as a prior account, not as an independently
verified historical report. In `pratisrotaḥnayanam`, the compound is spaced
for readability; the sense is leading beings against the current.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

At what time do Buddhas arise—during increase, or during decrease? During
decrease, from the period beginning at the eighty-thousand-year lifespan down
to the time when human beings have lifespans of one hundred years. They do
not arise during increase because beings are then difficult to rouse to
urgency. Why not once lifespans fall below one hundred years? Then the five
deteriorations are strongly prevalent: deterioration of lifespan, of the
age, of afflictions, of views, and of beings.

Below the lower point of decline, lifespan and the other conditions are
degraded; the text calls them deteriorations and uses the unclear comparison
`kiṭabhūta`. Two deteriorate life and its supports, respectively. Two
deteriorate the wholesome side, through the pursuit of sensual pleasure and
self-mortification in the household or renunciant ways of life. One
deteriorates the embodied person through loss of measure, appearance, health,
strength, growth, mindfulness, vigor, and steadiness.

When do Pratyekabuddhas arise? In both phases—in increase and in decrease.
There are two kinds: those who move in groups and those who are like a
rhinoceros horn.

The group-going Pratyekajinas are said to have previously been śrāvakas.
Others say that some were previously ordinary people: they had acquired the
practices conducive to penetrating insight elsewhere, and here they awaken
the path by themselves. For a prior account is recited: on a mountain, five
hundred ascetics were practicing difficult austerities. A monkey who had
lived with a Pratyekabuddha came and displayed that one's manner of comportment;
through seeing it, the ascetics attained Pratyekabodhi. The account adds that
noble ones would not undertake such difficult austerities.

The rhinoceros-horn-like Pratyekabuddhas, by contrast, dwell alone. Of these
Pratyekabuddhas, the solitary one has a continuity of one hundred kalpas:
one hundred great kalpas spent in the accumulations for awakening.

They are called Pratyekabuddhas because they awaken individually, without
instruction. They discipline themselves, not others. Why? It is not because
they are incapable of teaching Dharma: they have attained the analytical
knowledges, and they could teach Dharma by recalling the teaching of former
Buddhas.

Nor are they without compassion, since they display supernormal powers for
the benefit of beings. Nor are beings incapable of benefiting: people free
from passion through a worldly path are indeed found. Rather, through the
force of prior cultivation they incline toward limited involvement, and do
not undertake to engage others in grasping the profound Dharma. It is
difficult to lead people who go with the current against the current; and
they are wary of the distraction and entanglement that would come with
gathering a following.

## Natural Movement

The Bhāṣya first bounds the interval in which a Buddha appears and explains
both exclusions: beings are hard to rouse during lifespan-increase, while
the five deteriorations dominate below one hundred years. It then distinguishes
two Pratyekabuddha types. Both can arise in either phase, but the solitary
type's one-hundred-kalpa preparation is specifically an accumulation for
awakening.

Finally, the commentary rejects incapacity, lack of compassion, and the
unfitness of beings as explanations for Pratyekabuddhas' not teaching widely.
It attributes their limited engagement instead to prior habituation and
reluctance to gather and lead a community into profound teaching.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: the passage distinguishes awakening, teaching capacity,
compassion, and willingness to assume the work of guiding others. These are
kept as distinct determinations in Vasubandhu's argument, not collapsed into
a single measure of spiritual attainment.
