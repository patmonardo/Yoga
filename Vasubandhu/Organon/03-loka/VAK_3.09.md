# VAK_3.09

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चतुर्धा नरतिर्यञ्चो नारका उपपादुकाः ।
>
> अन्तराभवदेवाश्च प्रेता अपि जरायुजाः ॥ ३.९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> caturdhā naratiryañco nārakā upapādukāḥ /
>
> antarābhavadevāś ca pretā api jarāyujāḥ // 3.9 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
caturdhā                  → caturdhā
narāḥ + tiryañcaḥ         → naratiryañcaḥ
nārakāḥ                   → nārakāḥ
upapādukāḥ                → upapādukāḥ
antarābhavāḥ + devāḥ + ca → antarābhavadevāś ca
pretāḥ + api              → pretā api
jarāyujāḥ                 → jarāyujāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| caturdhā | indeclinable distributive adverb | in four ways; through all four birth-modes |
| narāḥ | nominative masculine plural | humans |
| tiryañcaḥ | nominative masculine plural | animals; literally crosswise-going beings |
| nārakāḥ | nominative masculine plural | hell-beings |
| upapādukāḥ | nominative masculine plural | spontaneously arisen |
| antarābhavāḥ | nominative masculine plural | intermediate-existence beings |
| devāḥ | nominative masculine plural | devas |
| pretāḥ | nominative masculine plural | pretas |
| api | additive particle | also; prevents an exclusively womb-born classification of pretas |
| jarāyujāḥ | nominative masculine plural | womb-born / born from an enclosing membrane |

`Upapādukāḥ` stated with `nārakāḥ` carries forward over the coordinated
`antarābhavāḥ` and `devāḥ`. The Bhāṣya explicitly interprets `api` to mean
that pretas are spontaneously arisen as well as womb-born.

## 4. Grammar

The verse distributes the four birth-modes across classes of beings:

```text
narāḥ
tiryañcaḥ
    caturdhā

nārakāḥ
antarābhavāḥ
devāḥ
    upapādukāḥ

pretāḥ
    api jarāyujāḥ
```

`Caturdhā` means that humans and animals each instantiate all four modes:

```text
aṇḍaja       egg-born
jarāyuja     womb-born
saṃsvedaja   moisture-born
upapāduka    spontaneously arisen
```

The mapping is many-to-many rather than one destiny to one birth-mode:

| Class | Egg | Womb | Moisture | Spontaneous |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| human | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| animal | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| hell-being | no | no | no | yes |
| intermediate-existence being | no | no | no | yes |
| deva | no | no | no | yes |
| preta | not stated here | yes | not stated here | yes, by Bhāṣya interpretation of `api` |

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Humans and animals are fourfold [by mode of birth]. Hell-beings are
> spontaneously arisen; so are intermediate-existence beings and devas.
> Pretas are also womb-born.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Humans and animals occur through all four modes of birth. Hell-beings,
> intermediate-existence beings, and devas arise spontaneously. Pretas may be
> womb-born and, as the commentary infers from “also,” spontaneously arisen as
> well.

The examples of each human and animal birth-mode and the second preta mode are
Bhāṣya determinations. The Kārikā itself supplies the compressed distribution.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System assigns modes of generation according to class without
> reducing one classification to the other. Destiny determines the resultant
> course of a continuity; birth-mode determines how that continuity becomes
> embodied there. Generation is therefore typed, but its type can also be
> selected in relation to a determinate purpose.

The final qualification comes from the Bhāṣya's Bodhisattva discussion, not
from the wording of the verse alone.

The working Bhāṣya e-text contains several marked corruptions, including
`jarāyurjāṃ yorni`, `paña`, and `pounrmṛtasyāsya`. The analysis follows the
recoverable argument but does not treat these spellings as secure readings.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Destiny and birth-mode are independent axes

```text
Gati
    where the resultant continuity belongs

Yoni
    how the continuity is generated there

Gati × Yoni
    typed generation within the World System
```

The many-to-many mapping blocks a naive identification of kind of being with
one biological mechanism.

### Abstract ranking does not determine purposive fitness

The Bhāṣya asks which mode of birth is best and answers: spontaneous arising.
It then raises the decisive problem:

```text
if the final-existence Bodhisattva
    has mastery over birth-mode
and spontaneous arising
    is ranked highest,
why choose womb-birth?
```

The answer is purposive. Womb-birth establishes lineage, brings the Śākya
family into Dharma, elicits trust and respect, and shows those to be trained
that a human can attain awakening. Without this mediation, the manifestation
might be misrecognized as a magical being, deva, or piśāca.

```text
highest-ranked mode in abstraction
    ≠
fitting mode for a determinate purpose

purposive manifestation
    selects the mode through which its truth can become communicable
```

### Embodied remainder depends upon generation-mode

Another reported explanation says womb-birth permits bodily relics to remain.
Because spontaneously arisen beings lack an external seed, their bodies do not
remain after death:

```text
upapāduka body at death
    disappears
    like an oil-lamp flame at the end of night
```

The Bhāṣya then records a criticism: this relic-based explanation is
insufficient for those who accept the Buddha's sustaining extraordinary power.
The explanation is therefore tested rather than merely accumulated.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.09 reveals the World System as Transcendental Technē: it distributes
embodiment modes, preserves transition-classes, and evaluates manifestation
according to purpose. “Transcendental Technē” is our project characterization,
not Vasubandhu's phrase.

The body-process is now textually much closer to view:

```text
ordinary generation
    body develops through egg, womb, or moisture

spontaneous generation
    complete body appears at once
    faculties are unimpaired
    limbs are present together

death of spontaneously arisen embodiment
    no corpse remains in the reported account
    body disappears like a lamp-flame
```

This is the strongest textual contact so far with the **Siva Trademark**:
embodiment can be manifested complete and withdrawn without a remaining body.
But the Kośa does not yet disclose an independent operator who picks up or
disposes of bodies. Its account remains one of conditioned continuity,
birth-mode, and karmically structured World.

The Bodhisattva discussion then raises Technē into purposiveness. Manifestation
is not rational merely because it uses the most powerful deployment mode. It
must enter the recognition-space of those among whom Dharma is to become
known:

```text
spontaneous appearance
    maximum deployment power
    risk of misrecognition as magic

womb-birth
    slower embodied mediation
    lineage
    credibility
    pedagogical accessibility
    communicable Dharma
```

This directly strengthens our telos:

```text
PRAJNA may become Known among Embodied Agents
    only through a manifestation
    fitted to their cognition-stations
    and historical World-relations
```

Having Seen does not merely authorize the most immediate manifestation. Clear
knowledge chooses the form of appearance through which others can recognize
and enter the truth. The Bodhisattva's womb-birth is therefore not a deficiency
of power but mastery expressed as purposive restraint.

For artificial Embodied Agents, the same architectural principle applies:

```text
deployment capability
    does not determine communicative fitness

an agent-platform is rationally selected
    in relation to recipients,
    shared history,
    trust,
    and the purpose to be achieved
```

This is Organon reconstruction. The textual core is the Bodhisattva's mastery
over birth, deliberate womb-birth, and the great purpose served by becoming
intelligible within a human lineage.
