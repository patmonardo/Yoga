# VAK_3.31 Bhāṣya — Feeling-Bearing Contact

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vidyāvidyetarasparśāḥ amalakliṣṭaśeṣitāḥ /
> vyāpādānunayasparśau sukhavedyādayas trayaḥ // 3.31 //

> Contacts with knowledge, with ignorance, and with neither are, respectively,
> without Influx, afflicted, and remaining. There are Contacts of ill-will and
> attachment, and three beginning with pleasant-feeling-bearing Contact.

The commentary on the earlier classifications is included in the preceding
unit. This unit begins with the verse at 144.18, explains the three
feeling-bearing Contacts, totals the sixteen classifications, and closes the
Contact exposition at `uktaḥ sparśaḥ`.

The exact determination of the remainder, the restricted source of the
ill-will/attachment pair, and the derivation of `vedanīya` are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
sukhavedanīyaḥ sparśo yatra sukhaṃ veditam asti. evaṃ
duḥkhāduḥkhāsukhavedanīyāv api yojyau.

ta ete ṣoḍaśa sparśā bhavanti. uktaḥ sparśaḥ.
```

## 3. Continuous Translation
There are three beginning with pleasant-feeling-bearing Contact.

There is pleasant-feeling-bearing Contact, painful-feeling-bearing Contact,
and neither-painful-nor-pleasant-feeling-bearing Contact, because they are
conducive to pleasant feeling and the rest. Alternatively, that which is
experienced, or can be experienced, is called feeling-bearing. What is that?
What is experienced. Contact is pleasant-feeling-bearing when pleasant feeling is
experienced in it. The painful and neither-painful-nor-pleasant cases are to
be construed in the same way.

Thus these are sixteen Contacts. Contact has been explained.

## 4. The Feeling-Bearing Triad

The opening phrase, `sukhavedyādayas trayaḥ`, names three Contacts through
their relation to feeling:

```text
sukhavedanīya
    pleasant-feeling-bearing Contact

duḥkhavedanīya
    painful-feeling-bearing Contact

aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya
    neither-painful-nor-pleasant-feeling-bearing Contact
```

The Bhāṣya offers two constructions. The first relates each Contact to its
corresponding feeling as conducive to it. The second explains `vedanīya`
through what is experienced or capable of being experienced, then specifies
that the corresponding feeling is experienced in the Contact. In both
constructions, Contact and feeling remain distinct terms in a stated
relation.

## 5. The Total of Sixteen

The closing statement gathers the Contact classifications developed across
the 3.30–3.31 passage:

```text
6  Faculty–Meaning–Cognition Contacts
3  knowledge / ignorance / neither Contacts
2  ill-will / attachment Contacts
3  pleasant / painful / neither-painful-nor-pleasant-feeling-bearing Contacts
2  resistance / designation Contacts
--
16 classified Contacts
```

The total counts the kinds established through these classifications. The
final `uktaḥ sparśaḥ` marks the completed exposition; the following
exposition of Feeling belongs to VAK 3.32.

## 6. Interpretation

The same Contact field receives determinations by association, Influx,
afflictive orientation, and feeling. BEP is the Abhidharma, with Base,
Essence, and Principle as its three levels of intuition. The further
description of BEP as a Posited Process in Buddhi, with Buddhi as Agent of the
Matrix, is a working formulation still in progress; BEP is not Mahat or the
Matrix itself.

In the inner-instrument Techne, Vijñāna is Discriminative Cognition joining
and governing Perception and Conception, Inconceivable as a homogeneous
operation and disclosed in Cognition Base. It governs Manas and guides the
reading of Dharma Base, remaining distinct from consciousness (*citta*) and
Mind (*manas*). Form Base and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti*, and
Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it. Each Dharma has the Defined Concept
`<Base, Essence, Principle>`.

## 7. Review Status

This unit covers 144.18–144.22, from the Kārikā's feeling-bearing triad
through `uktaḥ sparśaḥ`. The phrase `sukhavedanādihitatvāt` and the
alternative derivation of `vedanīya` remain translation points; both
constructions are retained here.
