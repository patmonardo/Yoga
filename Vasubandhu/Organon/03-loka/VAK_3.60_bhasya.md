# VAK_3.60 Bhāṣya — Celestial Support and Simultaneous Local Time

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ardhena meroś candrārkau pañcāśat-saika-yojanau /
ardharātro 'staṃgamanaṃ madhyāhna udayaḥ sakṛt // 3.60 //
```

> Halfway up Meru move the moon and sun, measuring respectively fifty and
> fifty-one yojanas. Midnight, sunset, midday, and sunrise occur
> simultaneously.

The Bhāṣya first identifies the support, elevation, dimensions, and functions
of the sun and moon. It then distributes the four simultaneous temporal states
across the four continents. The subsequent calculation of the seasonal growth
and diminution of night and day begins with VAK 3.61 and is not included here.

Kiyad viprakṛṣṭāv itaś candrārkau?

The running witness is clear about the principal determinations but contains
mechanical irregularities in spacing and compounds. The continuous text
regularizes those features. Its reading after the wind-circuit,
`candrārkatārāṇāṃ ... ūrdhvacārāḥ`, is grammatically uncertain and has been
rendered provisionally as describing winds that bear the upper courses of the
moon, sun, and stars. No external critical reading has been silently inserted.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Now, upon what are the moon and sun established? Upon wind.

Winds in the intermediate space, produced through the governing efficacy of
the karma common to all sentient beings, revolve around Sumeru like a whirl.
They also bear the upper courses of the moon, sun, and stars.

How far above this region are the moon and sun?

They are halfway up Meru. They travel level with the summit of Mount
Yugandhara. What are their dimensions? In their stated order, they measure
fifty and fifty-one yojanas.

The lunar disk measures fifty yojanas. The solar disk measures fifty plus one,
that is, fifty-one yojanas. Among the celestial residences of the stars, the
smallest measures one krośa.

Beneath and outside the sun's celestial residence there has been produced a
fiery crystalline disk, which heats and illuminates. Beneath the moon's
celestial residence is a watery disk, cool and luminous. Through the karma of
living beings, these act, as appropriate, for the benefit or harm of sight,
bodies, fruits, flowers, crops, and medicinal plants.

One moon and one sun perform their functions in a four-continent world.

Does the sun, then, perform its function simultaneously in all four
continents? No, says the Bhāṣya. What occurs instead? Midnight, sunset,
midday, and sunrise occur simultaneously among them.

When it is midnight in Uttarakuru, the sun is setting in Pūrvavideha, it is
midday in Jambūdvīpa, and the sun is rising in Godānīya. The same pattern is
to be applied to the other positions as the cycle proceeds. Here the increase
and decrease of night and day arise from differences in the sun's course.

## 4. Natural Movement and Translation Decisions

The unit develops one celestial process through five determinations:

1. the sun and moon are supported by karmically produced winds;
2. their course is fixed halfway up Meru, level with Yugandhara's summit;
3. the ordered dual expression gives the moon fifty yojanas and the sun
   fifty-one;
4. their fiery and watery disks perform differentiated physical functions;
5. one common solar course yields four distinct but simultaneous local times.

No named opponent or school appears. The question-and-answer movement belongs
to Vasubandhu's exposition. `Nety āha` rejects only the proposal that the sun
performs the same function simultaneously in all four continents; it does not
deny that one sun belongs to the complete four-continent system.

The numerical phrase must be resolved in order:

```text
candrārkau              pañcāśat-saika-yojanau
moon, sun               fifty, fifty-plus-one

moon  = 50 yojanas
sun   = 51 yojanas
```

The translation “halfway up Meru” is Bhāṣya-confirmed: the bodies move level
with Yugandhara's summit. The phrase does not say that their own bodies are
half the size of Meru.

## 5. Support, Motion, and Celestial Measure

The answer `vāyau` makes wind the immediate support of the celestial bodies.
But these winds are not an independent first cause. They are produced by
`sarvasattvasādhāraṇakarmādhipatya`, the governing efficacy of karma common to
all sentient beings. The explanatory sequence is therefore:

```text
karma common to beings
    → winds in intermediate space
        → circuit around Sumeru
            → supported celestial course
```

This continues the distinction made in 3.59 between common world-production
and particular karmic configurations. Here the emphasis falls upon a common
cosmic infrastructure: the moving support within which celestial functions
are possible.

The account also differentiates three measures:

| Object | Measure or level |
|---|---|
| moon | disk of 50 yojanas |
| sun | disk of 51 yojanas |
| smallest stellar residence | 1 krośa |

`Maṇḍala` identifies the measured disk; `vimāna` identifies the celestial
residence. The Bhāṣya's reference to a disk beneath the residence therefore
should not be flattened into a single undifferentiated celestial object.

## 6. Material Function and Karmic Efficacy

The solar disk is `taijasa`, fiery, and has the functions of heating and
illuminating. The lunar disk is `āpya`, watery, and is cool and luminous. The
passage thereby coordinates material constitution and function without making
them identical:

```text
fiery constitution → heating and illumination
watery constitution → cooling and luminosity
```

Their effects extend across sentient embodiment and organic life: sight,
bodies, fruit, flowers, crops, and medicines. `Yathāsaṃbhavam` prevents an
undifferentiated assignment of every effect to every recipient. Benefit and
harm occur according to the applicable case.

The appeal to karma does not erase heating, cooling, or illumination as
physical functions. It locates the production and consequence of those
functions within the common karmic world of living beings. Material process
and karmic efficacy are therefore concurrent explanatory determinations in
this cosmology.

## 7. One Process, Four Local Times

At the moment explicitly described, the relation is:

| Continent | Local determination |
|---|---|
| Uttarakuru | midnight |
| Pūrvavideha | sunset |
| Jambūdvīpa | midday |
| Godānīya | sunrise |

`Sakṛt` marks simultaneity. It does not mean that all four temporal conditions
occur in one place. Nor does the distribution require four independent suns.
One solar process has four functions relative to four positions in the same
world-system.

The instruction `evam anyeṣv api yojyam` makes the displayed assignment a
rotating relation, not an immutable privilege of the four named regions. As
the course proceeds, the other corresponding assignments are to be inferred.
Thus each continent successively occupies each phase.

The final statement about the growth and diminution of night and day announces
the next problem. This study includes it as the Bhāṣya's transition, but the
calendar calculation that explains it belongs to VAK 3.61.

## 8. Doctrinal Determination

The world-container is no longer merely a collection of spatial regions. It
is a coordinated field in motion. The identity of the sun is common, while
its currently performed function is regionally indexed:

```text
one sun
    + one position in its course
        + four continent-relations
            → four simultaneous local temporal states
```

This is stronger than a loose appeal to differing perspectives. The four
states are objectively coordinated within the cosmographic system. Each is
locally determinate; none alone states the condition of the whole system.

The reciprocal pairs are visible:

```text
midnight ↔ midday
sunset   ↔ sunrise
```

Yet the verse gives a fourfold rotation rather than two isolated oppositions.
Each moment is determined through the complete configuration of celestial
course and regional placement.

## 9. Organon Study

For the Organon, VAK 3.60 offers a disciplined example of a shared process
whose outputs are indexed by position. A statement such as “it is midday” is
incomplete for this world-model unless its regional context is supplied. The
same global instant supports several non-contradictory local predicates.

```text
common process + local relation → determinate appearance
```

This supplies a useful Dataset distinction between a common event and a
contextual feature-value. The event need not be copied into four unrelated
events, and the local values must not be collapsed into one supposedly
absolute value. This is an Organon reconstruction of the passage's logical
shape, not a claim that Vasubandhu proposes a modern data model.

The cosmology also prevents “projection” from meaning mere subjective
invention. The local temporal appearance is relationally produced, but it is
produced within a common, ordered, karmically constituted world. Difference
of appearance and unity of process are mutually determining features here.
