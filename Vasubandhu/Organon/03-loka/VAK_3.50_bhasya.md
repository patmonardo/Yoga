# VAK_3.50 Bhāṣya — Jeweled Meru, Conditioned Production, and Transformation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
catūratnamayo merur jale 'śītisahasrake /
magnā ūrdhvaṃ jalān merur bhūyo 'śītisahasrakaḥ // 3.50 //
```

> Meru is made of four precious substances. The mountains are submerged
> eighty thousand yojanas in the water; above the water Meru rises a further
> eighty thousand.

The Bhāṣya distributes the four jewels across Meru's four faces and explains
their directional coloring of the sky. Between the first and second halves
of the verse, it asks how the mountains, continents, and their differentiated
materials arise. This produces an explicit debate with a Sāṃkhya account of
`pariṇāma`, followed by Vasubandhu's conditioned-production account.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Catūratnamayo Meruḥ.

Suvarṇamayo rūpyamayo vaidūryamayaḥ sphaṭikamayaś ca yathāsaṃkhyaṃ caturṣu
pārśveṣu. Yac ca yanmayaṃ pārśvaṃ Sumeros tasyānubhāvena tadvarṇaṃ tasyāṃ
diśi nabho dṛśyate. Jāmbūdvīpakam asya pārśvaṃ vaidūryamayaṃ varṇayanti.
Tasyeha prabhānurāgeṇa vaidūryamayaṃ nabho dṛśyata iti.

Atha kathaṃ teṣāṃ saṃbhavaḥ? Kāñcanamayyāṃ pṛthivyāṃ punar vāridhārā
atipatanti. Tā hy āpo nānāvidhabījagarbhā bahuvidhaprabhāvabhinnair
vāyubhir mathyamānās tāṃ tāṃ jātiṃ pariṇamayanti. Evaṃ ca punaḥ
pariṇamayanti yad bhinnajātīyasya kāryaviśeṣasyotpattāv asamavadhānena
pratyayībhavanti, na tu khalu yathā Sāṃkhyānāṃ pariṇāmaḥ.

Kathaṃ ca Sāṃkhyānāṃ pariṇāmaḥ? Avasthitasya dravyasya
dharmāntaranivṛttau dharmāntaraprādurbhāva iti.

Kaś cātra doṣaḥ?

Sa eva hi dharmī na saṃvidyate, yasyāvasthitasya dharmāṇāṃ pariṇāmaḥ
kalpyeta.

Kaś caivam āha dharmebhyo 'nyo dharmīti? Tasyaiva tu dravyasyānyathībhāva-
mātraṃ pariṇāmaḥ.

Evam apy ayuktam.

Kim atrāyuktam?

Tad eva cedaṃ, na cedaṃ tatheti—apūrvaiṣā vā yuktir.

Evaṃ ca punaḥ saṃbhūtāḥ suvarṇādayaḥ karmaprabhāvāt preritair vāyubhiḥ
samāhṛtya rāśīkriyante. Ta ete parvatāś ca bhavanti dvīpāś ca.

Te punaḥ Sumervādayaś Cakravāḍaparyantāḥ parvatāḥ

jale 'śītisahasrake magnāḥ.

Kāñcanamayyāḥ pṛthivyā upary aśītiyojanasahasrāṇy udakam; tatra te magnāḥ.

Ūrdhvaṃ jalān Merur bhūyo 'śītisahasrakaḥ.

Iti hi Meroḥ ṣoḍaśa yojanaśatasahasraṃ samucchrayaḥ.
```

The running witness has several damaged forms, including `kaspyeta`,
`kaścaiṃvamāha`, `sumevadayaḥ`, and a corrupt-looking expression for Meru's
total height. The continuous text provisionally regularizes evident spelling
and sandhi. `Ṣoḍaśa yojanaśatasahasram`, sixteen hundred-thousands, is
supplied from the explicit 80,000 + 80,000 calculation; the witness's exact
reading must be checked rather than treated as critically settled.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Meru is made of four precious substances. Its four faces are made,
respectively, of gold, silver, vaidūrya, and crystal. Whatever substance a
face of Sumeru is made of, through that face's efficacy the sky in its
direction appears of the corresponding color. They describe the face toward
Jambūdvīpa as made of vaidūrya; because the radiance of that face colors this
region, the sky here appears vaidūrya-colored.

Now, how do these structures arise? Streams of water again fall upon the
golden earth. Those waters contain seeds of many kinds. Churned by winds
differentiated through powers of many kinds, they develop into various kinds.
They “transform” in this sense: without entering into one simultaneous
combination, the relevant factors become conditions for the arising of a
specific effect of a different kind. This is not transformation in the
Sāṃkhya manner.

And what is Sāṃkhya transformation? It is said to be the manifestation of one
property when another property ceases in a substance that remains established.

What fault is there in this? The very bearer of properties which would remain
established while its properties transform is not found.

Who says that the bearer is something other than the properties? Transformation
is merely that same substance's becoming otherwise.

Even so, it is not tenable.

What is untenable about it?

To say, “This is that very thing, and yet it is not such”—what an unprecedented
kind of reasoning this would be.

Gold and the other materials, having arisen in the conditioned manner just
described, are gathered and heaped up by winds impelled through the power of
karma. They become these mountains and continents.

The mountains beginning with Sumeru and extending through Cakravāḍa are
submerged in eighty thousand yojanas of water. Above the golden earth there
are eighty thousand yojanas of water, and the mountains are submerged in it.
Above the water Meru rises a further eighty thousand yojanas. Meru's total
height is therefore one hundred sixty thousand yojanas.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voices

The commentary unfolds in five movements:

```text
Meru's four material faces
    → their directional effect upon visible sky-color

question of genesis
    → seed-bearing waters and differentiated winds

meaning of Buddhist “transformation”
    → factors condition a distinct produced effect

Sāṃkhya debate
    → enduring substance and changing properties challenged

return to cosmography
    → materials gathered into mountains and continents
    → shared submerged depth and Meru's emergent height
```

The voices are distinct:

- Vasubandhu gives the principal exposition and critique;
- `varṇayanti`, “they describe,” marks the Jambūdvīpa-facing vaidūrya side as
  received cosmographical teaching;
- a Sāṃkhya position defines transformation through an enduring substance;
- a defender replies that no bearer separate from properties is being posited
  and that the same substance merely becomes otherwise;
- Vasubandhu rejects that reformulation as well.

The Sāṃkhya account here is a polemical representation inside the Bhāṣya. It
must not be presented as a complete neutral account of every Sāṃkhya theory
of `prakṛti`, `guṇa`, substance, and transformation.

## 5. Four Faces and Directional Appearance

`Catūratnamaya` is not explained as a mixture of four jewels. The Bhāṣya
distributes the materials respectively across four faces:

```text
four faces of Meru
    ↔ gold
    ↔ silver
    ↔ vaidūrya
    ↔ crystal
```

The exact directional assignment of all four faces is not enumerated here.
Only the face toward Jambūdvīpa is reported as vaidūrya.

The visible color of the sky is then relationally explained:

```text
material constitution of a face
    → its anubhāva, efficacy or influence
        → its radiance colors the corresponding direction
            → the sky there appears of that color
```

The claim is neither that the sky independently possesses the color nor that
an isolated observer fabricates it privately. Within this cosmology, a common
appearance is conditioned by material face, radiance, direction, and region.

`Vaidūrya` is retained rather than reduced without qualification to a modern
gemological term or color name. “Blue-green” may approximate the appearance
intended in some traditions, but the Sanskrit names the precious material
that is said to condition the appearance.

## 6. Conditioned Genesis of Differentiated Structures

The question `atha kathaṃ teṣāṃ saṃbhavaḥ?` asks how the differentiated
materials, mountains, and continents come to be. The answer supplies a causal
field rather than an already finished object:

```text
waters containing seeds of many kinds
    + winds differentiated by powers of many kinds
        → production of effects of different kinds

produced gold and other materials
    + winds impelled by karmic power
        → gathering and heaping
            → mountains and continents
```

Two operations remain distinct. First, heterogeneous products arise through
heterogeneous causal conditions. Second, those products are collected into
large-scale structures through karmically impelled winds.

The difficult phrase involving `asamavadhāna` is provisional. Its decisive
contrast is nevertheless visible: the factors serve as conditions for the
arising of a differentiated effect; they do not require one enduring bearer
in which exchanged properties successively reside.

Thus the Bhāṣya permits the verb `pariṇamayanti` in an ordinary explanatory
sense while immediately specifying that the intended transformation is not
the Sāṃkhya model described next.

## 7. The Sāṃkhya Transformation Debate

The Sāṃkhya position is formulated as:

```text
an established or enduring substance remains
    → one property ceases
    → another property becomes manifest
        = pariṇāma
```

Vasubandhu's first objection targets the bearer:

```text
the persisting dharmin
    in which the properties would transform
    is not found
```

The defender sharpens the position rather than simply positing an additional
entity: no bearer other than the properties need be asserted; transformation
is the same substance's becoming otherwise.

Vasubandhu replies that this remains incoherent if its explanatory form is:

```text
it is this very thing
    and
it is not such
```

The critique is therefore precise but limited. It does not show that no
Sāṃkhya response is possible, nor does it amount by itself to a comprehensive
refutation of Sāṃkhya metaphysics. It rejects the account as posed here:
numerical sameness of a substance is invoked while the determination by
which it is identified is said to have become otherwise.

Vasubandhu's positive contrast is causal continuity:

```text
not an invariant bearer exchanging properties

but conditioned factors
    → arising of a differentiated effect
        → further karmically mediated aggregation
```

This does not mean production from nothing. Prior waters, seeds, winds, and
karmic conditions do explanatory work, but they do so as causes and
conditions rather than as one self-identical substrate underlying every
result.

## 8. Submerged Depth and Meru's Height

The plural `magnāḥ` refers collectively to the mountains from Sumeru through
Cakravāḍa. All share one submerged relation:

```text
water above the golden earth
    = 80,000 yojanas deep

mountains from Sumeru through Cakravāḍa
    = submerged through that depth
```

The final clause returns to singular Meru:

```text
Meru below the water-line    80,000 yojanas
Meru above the water-line    80,000 yojanas
                             ----------------
total height                160,000 yojanas
```

The verse does not say that every mountain rises eighty thousand yojanas
above the water. Shared submerged depth and differentiated emergent height
are separate axes. The successive heights of the other mountains begin with
VAK 3.51.

The witness's prose expression for the total is corrupt or compressed, but
the arithmetic supplied by the two explicit measures is unambiguous. The
translation therefore states the total while labeling the Sanskrit repair as
provisional.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

This unit joins two problems that initially appear remote: how appearance is
conditioned and how substantial change is to be explained. In both cases,
Vasubandhu's commentary replaces an isolated predicate with a structured
relation.

```text
“the sky is vaidūrya-colored”
    → face + material + radiance + direction + region

“the world-material transforms”
    → seeds + differentiated winds + causal convergence + produced effect
```

For the Organon, this is a strong warning against confusing persistence of a
typed label with proof of an invariant bearer. A dataset may call both an
earlier and later configuration “the same object,” but that identity label
does not settle the metaphysics of the transition. Provenance, transformation
conditions, feature change, and criteria of identity must remain explicit.

An Ocular representation would need to separate:

```text
source conditions
produced determination
aggregation process
continuity relation asserted
identity criterion invoked
doctrinal voice making the assertion
```

This is an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's own data vocabulary. It
also prevents an unfair comparative move. The Bhāṣya establishes its own
causal preference and its objection to one formulation of Sāṃkhya
transformation; any wider comparison with Yoga's Absolute–Attribute–Mode
Concept must reconstruct both systems independently before identifying or
opposing them.

The cosmological result remains concrete: conditioned materials are gathered
into stable mountains and continents. Rejection of an enduring empirical
substrate does not entail rejection of structured continuity, common
appearance, or durable world-configuration.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the full VAK 3.50 unit at 159.12–160.05: Meru's four
jeweled faces, the directional coloration of the sky, the genesis of
differentiated materials and structures, the complete Sāṃkhya transformation
exchange, karmically impelled aggregation into mountains and continents, the
mountains' common submerged depth, and Meru's emergent and total height.

This repairs a substantial omission and boundary error in the first-pass
Bhāṣya Studies, which placed the genesis and Sāṃkhya passage under VAK 3.49
and omitted it from 3.50. No research witness was altered.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The `asamavadhāna` clause,
the exact wording of Vasubandhu's final retort, several mechanically damaged
forms, the distribution and directional order of Meru's four faces, and the
corrupt expression for total height require comparison with critical
Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations.

The Study stops before `ardhārdhahānir aṣṭāsu`, the opening of VAK 3.51. It
does not include the successive halving of the mountain heights, their equal
height and thickness, or the seven Śītā seas.
