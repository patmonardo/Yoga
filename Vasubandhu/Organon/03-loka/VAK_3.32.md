# VAK_3.32

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तज्जाः षड्वेदनाः पञ्च कायिकी चैतसी परा ।
>
> पुनश्चाष्टादशविधा सा मनोपविचारतः ॥ ३.३२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

The sixfold division follows the source in Contact. The five-and-one division
follows support. The eighteenfold division follows mental ranging across six
Conditions in three affective modes.

## 4. Grammar

The first hemistich states the production and then divides the result:

```text
tajjāḥ ṣaḍ vedanāḥ
    = from those [six contacts], six feelings

pañca kāyikī
    = five [constitute] bodily [feeling]

parā caitasī
    = the other [is] mental
```

`Kāyikī` is singular because it names one class containing the first five
Feelings. `Parā` identifies the sixth, Mind-Contact-born Feeling, as the other
class.

The second hemistich subjects that mental feeling to a further division:

```text
sā punaḥ aṣṭādaśavidhā
    manopavicārataḥ

that [mental feeling] is again eighteenfold
    according to mental ranging
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the matrix presupposed by `aṣṭādaśavidhā`: gladness,
distress, and equanimity, each ranging through the six Conditions.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Born from those [contacts] are six feelings. Five are bodily; the other is
> mental. That [mental feeling] is again eighteenfold according to mental
> ranging.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Six feelings arise in relation to the six contacts: feeling born from eye-,
> Ear-, Nose-, Tongue-, Body-, and Mind-Contact. The first five are called
> **bodily Feeling** because they are supported by Form Faculties; the
> remaining one is called **mental Feeling** because it is supported by
> consciousness (*citta*) alone. Mental Feeling is further divided into
> eighteen Mental Rangings: six of gladness, six of distress, and six of
> equanimity, each corresponding to one of the six Conditions—Form, Sound,
> Smell, Taste, Tangible, and Dharma.

The Form-Faculty and consciousness (*citta*) support relations, as well as
the internal `3 × 6` construction of the eighteen Mental Rangings, are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 7. Logical Determination

### Three successive grounds of articulation

```text
six Contacts
    → six Feelings                    [ground: causal relation]

five Form-Faculty-supported
    → bodily Feeling                  [ground: support]

one consciousness-supported
    → mental Feeling                  [ground: support]

three modes of mental Feeling
    × six Conditions
    → eighteen Mental Rangings        [ground: mode + Condition]
```

The eighteen are the cross-classification of two stated determinations:

| Feeling mode \ Condition | Form | Sound | Smell | Taste | Tangible | Dharma |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gladness | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| distress | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| equanimity | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Thus:

```text
by intrinsic character alone: 3
by association with Mind alone: 1
by cognitive Condition/support alone: 6
by all required grounds together: 3 × 6 = 18
```

### Feeling determines traversal

```text
Contact field
    → Feeling mode
        → repeated Mental Ranging through a Condition
```

The arrow here expresses the order of determination, not an uncontested
chronology of separate moments. Feeling is operational: it helps determine
how Mind ranges through Conditions again.

### The temporal relation remains disputed

```text
Vaibhāṣika:
    Contact ∥ Feeling
    relation: coexistent causality

rival account:
    Contact → Feeling
    relation: successive moments
```

Vasubandhu tests these models against sūtra rather than allowing the treatise
and sūtra formulations to decide by themselves. The closing argument
establishes Contact and Feeling with Cognition; the recorded debate preserves
the rival accounts of their temporal relation.

## 8. Interpretation

The six Contact-born Feelings are classified by their support into five
bodily Feelings grounded in Form Faculties and one mental Feeling grounded
in consciousness (*citta*) alone. The mental Feeling then ranges in three
modes across six Conditions. The Bhāṣya's two explanations of `manopavicāra`
meet in the repeated movement of Mind under the force of Feeling.

BEP is the indivisible Singular Matrix—vertical and horizontal, Mahat-fueled,
the OG Matrix of the Other Side, the House of God, Satya Loka, Paramārtha.
It does not itself enter appearances.

In the inner-instrument Techne, Vijñāna is Discriminative Cognition joining
and governing Perception and Conception, Inconceivable as a homogeneous
operation and disclosed in Cognition Base. It governs Manas and guides the
reading of Dharma Base, remaining distinct from consciousness (*citta*) and
Mind (*manas*). Form Base and Dharma Base classify the same *avijñapti*, and
Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it. Each Dharma has the Defined Concept
`<Base, Essence, Principle>`.
