# VAK_2.39 Bhāṣya — Form, then Non-Acquisition

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nivṛtasya ca rūpasya kāme rūpasya nāgrajā /
>
> akliṣṭāvyākṛtāprāptiḥ sātītājātayos tridhā // 2.39 //

> Acquisition of obscured-indeterminate manifest Form is co-arisen only.
> Acquisition of Desire-Realm Form is never prior to it.
> Non-acquisition is always unobscured-indeterminate; for past and
> future dharmas, non-acquisition may be past, present, or future.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Is acquisition co-arisen only for the unobscured-indeterminate?
The reply is: of obscured Form also. Acquisition of obscured-
indeterminate manifest Form is likewise co-arisen only, because
its weakness is established by its failure to produce nonmanifest
Form even when intense.

Just as indeterminate dharmas have this differentiation of acquisition,
is there also a differentiation for wholesome and unwholesome dharmas?
There is, it replies: of Form in the Desire Realm, acquisition is
not prior-arisen. Prior acquisition of Desire-Realm manifest and
nonmanifest Form never occurs; co-arisen and subsequent acquisition
do occur.

Does non-acquisition also have distinctions of kind like acquisition?
No, it replies. What, then? Non-acquisition is unafflicted and
indeterminate: all non-acquisition is unobscured-indeterminate only.
By temporal distinction, however, it is threefold for past and
not-yet-arisen dharmas. For a present dharma, non-acquisition is
present only [provisional reading of the damaged gloss]. For past
and future dharmas, it is of the three times.

## 4. Two Acquisition Restrictions

Obscured-indeterminate manifest Form has only co-arisen acquisition.
The weakness argument is specific: even its intense form does not
produce nonmanifest Form. This does not erase the special strength
cases discussed in 2.38.

The Desire-Realm Form rule concerns both manifest and nonmanifest
Form: prior acquisition is excluded, while co-arisen and subsequent
acquisition occur in this general range. It does not follow that
each Form subtype admits subsequent acquisition; the narrower
obscured-indeterminate rule remains in force where applicable.

## 5. The Negative Determination and Its Times

All non-acquisition has the unobscured-indeterminate classification,
not a classification inherited from the absent dharma. The question
about “distinctions like acquisition” is therefore answered negatively
before temporal distinctions are introduced; it is not a denial
of every possible distinction of non-acquisition.

Past and future dharmas each admit past, present, and future
non-acquisition: six class-level combinations. The damaged present
gloss is provisionally understood as present-only. It is not a
collated reading and is not included in the kārikā translation.
The model keeps it under a separately named provisional predicate.

## 6. Interpretation

In the Organon reading, Cognition joins and governs Perception and
Conception and guides the Dharma Base, while remaining distinct
from consciousness and Mind. A typed negative is not a blank:
non-acquisition has determinate ethical and temporal classification.
But classification does not establish a separate substance. The
distinct-real/designation dispute of 2.36 is not settled here,
and the negative side of the dyad cannot simply inherit the
positive side's rules.
