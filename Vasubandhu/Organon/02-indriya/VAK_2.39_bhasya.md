# VAK_2.39 Bhāṣya — Non-Acquisition and Ordinary-Person Status

## 1. Text and Scope

This study covers the Bhāṣya at 65.30–66.20. It asks whether non-acquisition
has the same varieties as acquisition, establishes its unobscured-
indeterminate status and temporal classification, then tests the definition
of ordinary-person status through non-acquisition of noble Dharmas.

## 3. Scientific English Rendering

> Does non-acquisition have varieties like acquisition? No. All
> non-acquisition is unobscured-indeterminate. With respect to time,
> non-acquisition of a present Dharma is present; for past and future
> Dharmas it is threefold.
>
> Non-acquisition of Dharmas in the three Principle-ranges and of
> uncontaminated Dharmas can belong to any of the three Principle-ranges;
> no non-acquisition is uncontaminated. Non-acquisition of the Path is
> held to be ordinary-person status.
>
> What is ordinary-person status? The non-obtaining of noble Dharmas.
> This cannot mean lacking every noble Dharma of every lineage, since even
> a Buddha does not possess the lineage-specific Dharmas of disciples and
> solitary buddhas. Others identify the relevant non-obtaining more narrowly
> with receptivity to Dharma-knowledge concerning suffering and its
> co-arising Dharmas. The text then commends the Sautrāntika account:
> ordinary-person status is a continuum in which noble Dharmas have not
> arisen.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The passage first rejects a symmetry between acquisition and non-acquisition,
then gives temporal and Principle-range classifications. Ordinary-person
status introduces the key case. The all-noble-Dharma reading, an implicit
restriction, and a narrower proposal are each tested. The last formulation
is explicitly attributed to the Sautrāntikas and is locally approved.

## 5. Ordinary-Person Status

The argument is quantificational. Not possessing some noble Dharma is
insufficient, because noble paths have lineage-specific determinations. The
proposed implicit restriction attempts to identify non-obtaining without
obtaining; the narrower account selects a particular receptivity and its
co-arising Dharmas. Each is challenged by the three-lineage problem.

The approved Sautrāntika definition avoids a separate real status-object:

```text
OrdinaryPersonStatus(S)
    := NobleDharmasHaveNotArisenInContinuum(S)
```

It is a genetic continuum determination. It does not declare an ordinary
person eternally deficient, and it does not erase the exact distinction
between non-acquisition and a Dharma that lies outside the relation's range.

## 6. Interpretation

This is the course threshold in exact form. A learner has not “failed” as a
person; rather, the relevant noble Dharma has not yet arisen in the
continuum. The next grade remains unavailable until the causal conditions
for its arising are made actual. Once those conditions arise, the status is
transformed, not merely administratively relabeled.

The Kośa-wide synthesis remains: Vijñāna is Discriminative Cognition joining
and governing Perception and Conception. Its unitary operation is
Inconceivable, and its Idea is disclosed in the Cognition Base. Vijñāna
governs Mind and guides Dharma Base. This context keeps the continuum
determination from being reduced to an isolated present mental factor.
