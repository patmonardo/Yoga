# VAK_2.16 Bhāṣya — Fruits Through Faculties

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kramamṛtyau tu catvāri śubhe sarvatra pañca ca /
>
> navāptir antyaphalayoḥ saptāṣṭanavabhir dvayoḥ // 2.16 //

> In gradual death, however, four; with wholesome consciousness,
> everywhere five in addition. The two extreme fruits, stream-entry and
> arhatship, are attained through nine Faculties; the two middle fruits,
> once-returning and non-returning, through seven, eight, or nine.

## 3. Continuous Translation

In gradual death, four faculties cease: body, life, Mind, and neutral feeling. These do not cease separately. This rule concerns death with an afflicted or indeterminate mind. If one dies with a wholesome mind, five more faculties cease in every case: those beginning with faith, which necessarily occur in a wholesome mind. Thus eight cease in the Formless realm and thirteen in the Form realm; the other totals are to be calculated in the same way.

In the section on faculties, all matters concerning faculties are examined. Which fruit of the path is attained through how many faculties? Attainment of the two extreme fruits is through nine. Which are the extremes? Stream-entry and arhatship. Which are the middle? Once-returning and non-returning.

Stream-entry through nine: those beginning with faith, excluding the faculty of one who has known, together with mind and neutral feeling. “I shall know” is on the uninterrupted path. The knowledge-faculty is on the path of liberation. Attainment is through both, since they respectively bring about and support the acquisition of disconnection. Arhatship through nine: those beginning with faith, excluding “I shall know,” together with mind and any one of pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling.

The two through seven, eight, or nine. Each of once-returning and non-returning is attained through seven, eight, or nine. If one proceeding in sequence attains once-returning by a mundane path, seven: the five beginning with faith, neutral feeling, and mind. By a supramundane path, eight, the knowledge-faculty as eighth. If one already more extensively free from desire attains it, nine, as stream-entry.

Non-returning, sequential and mundane, seven, as once-returning. Supramundane, eight, the same way. Already free from desire, nine, as stream-entry. This difference: any one of pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling, by the difference of support.

Even when one proceeding in sequence enters dhyāna on the ninth path of liberation, because of sharp faculties, by a mundane path, non-returning is attained through eight. On that ninth path of liberation, gladness is the eighth. On the uninterrupted path, neutral feeling alone is invariably present. Attainment is through both. By a supramundane path, nine, the knowledge-faculty as ninth.

The Abhidharma says: through how many faculties does one attain arhatship? Through eleven. How, then, through nine? One attains it through nine alone. Arhatship is nevertheless said through eleven, because that is possible for one person. One person, falling away repeatedly, may attain arhatship with pleasure, with gladness, and with neutral feeling. Therefore eleven. It is not possible for those feelings at one time.

Why does this not follow for a non-returner? Fallen away, that person never attains the fruit with the pleasure-faculty. One who had already become free from desire does not fall away, because that dispassion was attained through both paths.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

Extremes and middle, then each count by route. The sharp non-returning case qualifies a simple mundane-is-seven, supramundane-is-eight.

An objection cites eleven. The reply distinguishes one attainment from the possibilities across repeated attainments by one person. A last question tests non-returning. No school is named. Falling away is part of the reported argument.

## 5. The Two Extreme Fruits

Extreme is first and last: stream-entry and arhatship. Not the last two. Stream-entry: the five, mind, neutral feeling, and the first two realization-faculties. Arhatship: the five, mind, one feeling, and the last two.

The two realization-faculties of stream-entry are distributed. Uninterrupted path brings the acquisition of disconnection. Liberation supports it. Nine is not nine factors in one moment.

Arhatship says any one feeling, not three. That prepares the eleven.

## 6. The Middle Fruits

Sequential mundane is seven: faith, vigor, mindfulness, concentration,
discriminative comprehension, Mind, and neutral feeling. Supramundane
adds the knowledge-faculty. Prior dispassion permits nine, compared
with stream-entry. Non-returning varies the feeling by its Basis.
The Bases are not enumerated. None are supplied.

The sharp case: neutral feeling on the uninterrupted path, gladness on liberation. Both enter the count. Mundane eight. Supramundane nine.

The same total can arise for different reasons. Eight may be the knowledge-faculty, or an additional feeling across the path-moments. The number does not identify the set or the route.

## 7. Nine and Eleven

Nine is one attainment, one of the three feelings. Eleven is what may occur across repeated attainments by one person: the other eight, plus pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling at different times.

The three feelings are not simultaneous. Even the nine need not be nine designations in one moment.

A fallen non-returner does not regain the fruit with pleasure. One already free from desire does not fall away, because that dispassion was attained through both paths. No general theory of relapse is added.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Āpti and prāpti are attainment. Visaṃyogaprāpti is acquisition of disconnection. The instrumentals name the faculties through which. They do not identify the fruit with the set.

Ānantaryamārga is uninterrupted path. Vimuktimārga is path of liberation. Both stay visible where the count includes both. Niśrayaviśeṣa is difference of Basis. The particular Bases are not listed.

Bhūyovītarāga is more extensively free from desire. No degree is added. Through nine alone restricts the attainment. It does not deny the elevenfold range of one person. Ekasya matters as much as the number.

The death half retains five in addition, not merely five also present.
Wholesome consciousness is distinct from the Mind-Faculty counted
in the fruit sets. The eleven answer is 2.17's opening continuation,
not a numeral in 2.16's fruit clauses.

## 9. Interpretation

The result is not the route. The number is not the composition. The count is not the span. One fruit, several counts. One count, different routes.

Two feelings in succession are not three feelings across a history. A flat list erases that.

The Organon determination preserves both instrumental mediation and
temporal scope. A fruit is not identical to the Faculty-set through
which it is attained; a count may span successive Path-moments.

Mind is one Faculty in these instrumental sets, not a name for their
whole operation. The passage distinguishes faculties counted across
successive path moments from alternatives possible across one person's
repeated attainments. The wider Organon account of Cognition,
Perception, and Conception remains to be tested elsewhere.
