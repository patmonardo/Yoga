# VAK_2.41 Bhāṣya — Same-Class and the Non-Reflective Result

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sabhāgatā sattvasāmyam āsaṃjñikam asaṃjñiṣu /
>
> nirodhaś cittacaittānāṃ vipākaḥ te bṛhatphalāḥ // 2.41 //

> Same-class is sameness among beings. The non-reflective result among
> non-reflective beings is cessation of consciousness and the mental
> factors, a maturation. Those beings belong to the Great Fruit gods.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What is this same-class? Same-class is sameness among beings.
Same-class is an entity, on the account being presented: resemblance
among beings, called group same-class in the treatise. It is
undifferentiated and differentiated. Undifferentiated is the
being-same-class of all beings, since it occurs in each and all.
Differentiated same-class is restricted according to differences
of Realm, level, destiny, mode of birth, species, woman and man,
lay follower and monk, in training and beyond training, and so
forth. Dharma-same-class is further differentiated according to
Bases, Essences, and Principles. If being-same-class were not an
undifferentiated entity, there would be no undifferentiated recognition
or designation of “being” among beings differing from one another.
Apply this likewise to recognition and designation of Bases and
so forth.

Can someone die and be reborn without relinquishing or acquiring
being-same-class? There are four alternatives. First: being reborn
where one dies. Second: entering certainty, for one relinquishes
ordinary-person same-class and acquires noble same-class. Third:
transition of destiny. Fourth: leaving these cases aside.

If ordinary-person same-class is an entity, what further need is
there for ordinary-person status? One does not posit humanity
apart from human same-class. Moreover, people do not see same-class,
since it is without Form, nor delimit it through discriminative
comprehension; yet they recognize beings as of the same species.
Even if it exists, what work does it do in this recognition?
Why not also accept same-class of non-sentient things, since rice,
barley, mung beans, black gram, mangoes, jackfruit, iron, gold,
and so forth resemble their own kinds? And how are these mutually
different same-classes themselves designated without distinction
as same-class?

In this way the Vaiśeṣikas too are brought into view [force of
*dyotitā* provisional]. For they also hold that there is a universal
category from which similar cognitions arise even among unlike
particulars. Their difference is that one such universal occurs
in many. Whether or not they are thus brought into view, the
Vaibhāṣikas say: same-class exists because a sūtra mentions it.
The Blessed One says, “If he comes to this condition, to same-class
with human beings.” That was said, but it was not said to be a
distinct entity. What is it, then? Just those formations constituted
in such a way that “human” and so forth are designated upon them,
as with same-class among rice and so forth. But they do not
explain it thus [referent of the closing plural left implicit].

What is the non-reflective result? Among non-reflective beings,
it is cessation of consciousness and mental factors. For those
reborn among the non-reflective gods, that cessation is called the
non-reflective entity: by it consciousness and mental factors are
held back in the future for an interval and cannot arise, like
the damming of river water. It is exclusively maturation. Maturation
of what? Of the non-reflective attainment. Which beings are those
among whom the non-reflective beings occur? They are the Great
Fruit gods. Some among the Great Fruit gods are non-reflective
beings in a particular region, as with an intermediate division
of a dhyāna.

Are they never reflective at any time [contextual reading of the
damaged question]? They are reflective at rebirth and at death.
The sūtra reads: “After remaining even for a very long time, with
the arising of reflection those beings fall from that place.”
Like those waking from a long sleep, they fall from there and
are reborn in the Desire Realm, nowhere else. Because the formations
of the former attainment are exhausted and no new ones have been
accumulated, they fall like discharged arrows whose momentum is
spent. Whoever is to be reborn there necessarily has Desire-Realm
karma to be experienced in a later existence, just as inhabitants
of Uttarakuru have karma to be experienced in divine rebirth.

## 4. Same-Class: Claim, Classification, and Four Alternatives

The opening presents same-class as a real entity and argues from
undifferentiated recognition and designation. This is not already
the critic's designation account. The classificatory sequence
distinguishes being-same-class, differentiated classes of beings,
and Dharma-same-class by Bases, Essences, and Principles; it does
not reduce all three to one undifferentiated universal.

| Alternative | Death and rebirth | Relinquishing/acquiring same-class | Source example |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Yes | No | Rebirth in the same class |
| Second | No | Yes | Entering certainty: ordinary to noble |
| Third | Yes | Yes | Transition of destiny |
| Fourth | No | No | Cases apart from the preceding three |

The axes are distinct. The second example is not death followed
by rebirth, and the first does not imply that every same-class
instance is numerically one universal shared by all beings.

## 5. The Debate Must Remain a Debate

The critic first asks whether ordinary-person same-class duplicates
ordinary-person status. The next questions concern causal work
in recognition, non-sentient resemblance, and the common designation
of different same-classes. The Vaiśeṣika comparison includes its
stated difference: one universal occurs in many. It is not an
unqualified identification of the two accounts.

The Vaibhāṣikas answer from scriptural usage. The reply does not
deny the wording; it denies that the wording establishes a distinct
entity and offers appropriately constituted formations as the
basis of designation. The closing negative is preserved, rather
than turning the sequence into a consensual conclusion.

## 6. Result, Attainment, and Interval

The source calls the non-reflective cessation an entity and explains
its inhibiting activity through the river-water analogy. That
reported account is retained without settling the larger distinct-real
debate. The result is maturation **of** non-reflective attainment;
it is not the attainment whose properties are examined in 2.42.

Non-reflective beings occupy a region among the Great Fruit gods,
not all of that divine class. Reflection occurs at the birth and
death boundaries, with the intervening cessation between them.
Subsequent Desire-Realm rebirth is a different location and stage.
The sleep and spent-arrow comparisons, exhaustion of prior
attainment formations, absence of new accumulation, and necessary
later-experienced karma are all retained as steps in the account.

## 7. Interpretation

In the Organon reading, Cognition joins and governs Perception and
Conception and guides the Dharma Base, remaining distinct from
consciousness and Mind. Shared designation is a determinate relation
requiring explanation, not an automatic proof of another entity.
The non-reflective presentation likewise requires keeping an
attainment, its maturation, and the duration of that result distinct.
The cessation of consciousness and factors is the source's claim;
it should not be diluted to merely reduced attention, nor should
the result be silently turned into the practice that precedes it.
