# VAK_2.32 Bhāṣya — Moral Shame, Caution, Affection, and Respect

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ahrīr agurutā avadye bhayādarśitvam atrapā /
>
> prema śraddhā gurutvaṃ hrīḥ te punaḥ kāmarūpayoḥ // 2.32 //

> Shamelessness is lack of respect; lack of moral caution is failure to see
> danger in the blameworthy. Affection is faith, and respect is moral shame.
> These occur in the desire and form realms.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The distinctions established in the treatise among certain mental factors will now be stated. What distinguishes shamelessness from lack of moral caution? Shamelessness is lack of respect. Absence of reverence toward qualities and those possessing them, non-acknowledgment of authority, absence of fear, and refusal to be governed constitute shamelessness, a factor opposed to respect. Lack of moral caution is failure to see danger in the blameworthy. The blameworthy is what good persons condemn. Failure to see danger in it is lack of moral caution. Danger here means an undesirable result.

How should this be understood: seeing an absence of danger, or not seeing danger? If seeing an absence of danger, it would be prajñā. If not seeing danger, ignorance. The disposition so described is not simply seeing, nor simply not seeing. The secondary affliction that occasions either is lack of moral caution.

Others say shamelessness is being unashamed of faults with reference to oneself, and lack of moral caution with reference to others. How can the two references be established simultaneously? It is not said that one simultaneously considers oneself and another. There is a lack of shame that can operate even when one considers oneself: shamelessness. One that operates when one considers another: lack of moral caution. Moral shame and moral caution by reversing these. On the first account, moral shame is respect and acknowledgment of authority, and moral caution is seeing danger in the blameworthy. On the second, shame with reference to oneself and to others.

Affection is faith. Affection is twofold, afflicted and unafflicted. Afflicted is craving, as toward children and spouse. Unafflicted is faith toward the Teacher, preceptors, and those endowed with qualities. Faith without affection: directed toward suffering and its origin. Affection without faith: afflicted affection. Both: faith directed toward cessation and the path. Neither outside these modes. Among persons, affection without respect toward children, spouse, companions, and pupils. Respect without affection toward other teachers. Both toward one's own teacher. Neither outside these cases. Faith is esteem for qualities. Affection is fondness preceded by that esteem. Others say affection is not identical with faith. Respect is acknowledgment of authority. Moral shame is shame preceded by that acknowledgment. Others say respect is not identical with moral shame.

These, however, occur in the desire and form realms. Affection and respect do not occur in the formless realm. Faith and moral shame occur there too, being wholesome great-ground factors. Faith is twofold, toward dharmas and toward persons. Acknowledgment of authority likewise. The forms that have persons as support do not occur there. Those are what is intended here by affection and respect.

The reversal is damaged. Seeing danger, rather than the negative of the preceding definition, follows the announced reversal. It wants collation.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

Definitions deferred at the counts. The first account of the negative pair meets the collapse into prajñā or ignorance. The alternative meets simultaneity. Both are reversed. Affection and respect get compressed identifications, fourfold comparisons, and attributed alternatives. The realm objection tests the restriction against wholesome great-ground occurrence. The reply restricts by support.

## 5. Moral Caution

Judging that danger is absent would be Discrimination. Failing to recognize danger would be ignorance. The secondary affliction is the occasion of those expressions. Not a rename of either. The count that kept lack of moral caution apart from prajñā and delusion stays.

Danger is an undesirable result. Not fear indiscriminately. Not every absence of fear is unwholesome. The blameworthy is what good persons condemn.

## 6. Self and Other

The alternative is reference. The objection assumes two simultaneous considerations. The reply refuses that premise. Kinds of lack of shame that operate under the relevant consideration. Not a theory of unconscious self-reference.

The positive pair is the reversal under both accounts. Two approaches stay. Not one settled equation of shame, respect, fear, and social regard. The damaged reversal limits the wording.

## 7. Affection and Respect

The four alternatives keep affection is faith from being identity of the full ranges. Afflicted affection is craving. Faith toward suffering and its origin is not affection. Faith toward cessation and the path is the stated overlap.

The person examples show the relations can separate. Not judgments about every parent or teacher. Others distinguish affection from the esteem that precedes it, and moral shame from the acknowledgment that precedes it. Reported alternatives. Not a final rejection of identity.

## 8. The Formless

Faith and moral shame are wholesome great-ground factors, so they occur in wholesome consciousness in the formless realm. If affection and respect were those factors without restriction, they could not be absent there.

The reply narrows the reference. Person-directed forms are what the restriction means. Faith and moral shame are not withdrawn. The general factor, the directed form, and the realm stay apart.

## 9. Organon Reading

A relation can be mistaken for identity. A factor can occasion a presentation. An attitude can precede another. A general factor can have a restricted person-directed form. Compact formulas use the same names. The explanations differ.

Absence of the directed form is not absence of the factor. Distinguishing faith from the affection it precedes does not erase the relation. The voices stay. Objective Logic on the mind is the pass. Not the translation. Not the final pass.
