# VAK_2.63

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> निरुध्यमाने कारित्रं द्वौ हेतू कुरुतस्त्रयः ।
> जायमाने ततोऽन्यौ तु प्रत्ययौ तद्विपर्ययात् ॥ २.६३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nirudhyamāne kāritraṃ dvau hetū kurutas trayaḥ /
> jāyamāne tato 'nyau tu pratyayau tadviparyayāt // 2.63 //

The displayed text applies external sandhi in *kurutas trayaḥ* to the
research witness's *kurutaḥ trayaḥ*. The commentary runs from the question
about the state of the conditioned dharma at 100.19 through the explicit
close at 101.06. Its opening source label is anomalous; the argumentative
sequence is clear.

The commentary supplies *dharme* for the locative constructions.
The locatives describe the dharma toward which the causal activity
is directed, not simply the temporal status of the acting cause.

## 4. Grammar

The first sentence crosses the metrical line break:

```text
nirudhyamāne [dharme]
    dvau hetū kāritraṃ kurutaḥ

jāyamāne [dharme]
    trayaḥ [hetavaḥ kāritraṃ kurvanti]
```

“Three” must be joined to “while arising” in the second line. The
plural verb is supplied from the dual *kurutaḥ* with the appropriate
change of number. The first-pass translation stranded “three” in
the first line and thereby lost the division of the five causes.

The commentary identifies these five as the cause-condition already
established in 2.61. *Tataḥ anyau pratyayau* introduces two conditions
other than that cause-condition: immediately antecedent and object.
*Tadviparyayāt* reverses the order of the two states just given:
immediately antecedent operates with respect to arising, object with
respect to ceasing. It does not reverse cause and result.

Predominant condition is explained separately in the Bhāṣya. Its
all-state non-obstruction is not an additional literal clause in this verse.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Two causes perform their activity with respect to the ceasing
> [dharma]; three [do so] with respect to the arising [dharma].
> The other two conditions, however, [operate] in the reverse order.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Co-arisen and associated causes operate with respect to a present
> dharma directed toward cessation. Homogeneous, pervasive, and
> maturation causes operate with respect to a future dharma directed
> toward arising. Of the other two conditions, the immediately
> antecedent condition operates with respect to arising, and the
> object-condition with respect to the present, cessation-directed dharma.

The Bhāṣya adds: predominant condition stands in every state as
non-obstruction; this itself is its activity. It also explains that
immediate antecedence gives an opening, while object-conditioning
operates through apprehension by present mind and mental factors.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Knowing a condition includes knowing the state of the dharma
> in relation to which it operates. Co-arisen efficacy concerns
> the already present result; other causal relations concern the
> result approaching arising. Succession provides an opening for
> the next event, while object-conditioning operates in present
> apprehension. Predominance retains its wider non-obstructing role.

This is a commentary-informed study formulation. “Ceasing” here does
not make the two simultaneous causes agents that destroy their result.
Their result is present and directed toward cessation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Term | Controlled rendering | Determination |
|---|---|---|
| kāritra | activity / operative function | operation in relation to a specified state of a dharma |
| nirudhyamāna | ceasing | present, directed toward cessation |
| jāyamāna | arising | future, directed toward arising |
| nirodhābhimukha | directed toward cessation | Bhāṣya explanation of the present state |
| utpādābhimukha | directed toward arising | Bhāṣya explanation of the relevant future state |
| avakāśadāna | giving an opening | immediate antecedence's mode of activity |
| grahaṇa | apprehension | present mind and mental factors apprehending the object |
| anāvaraṇabhāva | non-obstruction | predominant condition's activity in every state |
| tadviparyaya | reversal of that order | arising then ceasing for the two further conditions |

*Grahaṇa* here is object-apprehension, not the taking of a result
examined in 2.59. The same broad English word “taking” would obscure
the difference between those constructions.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Causal or conditional relation | State of the target dharma | Local explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Co-arisen cause | Present, directed toward cessation | Activity concerning a co-arisen result |
| Associated cause | Present, directed toward cessation | Activity concerning a co-arisen result |
| Homogeneous cause | Future, directed toward arising | Cause-condition in its prospective application |
| Pervasive cause | Future, directed toward arising | Cause-condition in its prospective application |
| Maturation cause | Future, directed toward arising | Cause-condition in its prospective application |
| Immediately antecedent condition | Future, directed toward arising | Giving an opening |
| Object-condition | Present, directed toward cessation | Apprehension by present mind and factors |
| Predominant condition | Every state | Non-obstruction |

The first five rows divide one condition, *hetupratyaya*, into its
five constituent causes. They are not five further conditions in
addition to the established four.

**Target-state and cause-state remain distinct.** The claim that
homogeneous cause operates with respect to a future target does not
make the cause itself future. Earlier verses excluded future homogeneous
and pervasive causes. Similarly, maturation's prospective target is
compatible with maturation cause giving its result when past.

**Arising is technically qualified.** The commentary does not equate
all future dharmas indiscriminately with an actively impending result;
it specifies what is directed toward arising. Nor does “ceasing” mean
already past or absent. These are the account's technical temporal
determinations, not ordinary descriptions of a prolonged process.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya control of the reading.** The opening question asks in what
state of a dharma the conditions exercise their activity. That question
controls the locatives throughout. Without it, “two causes operate
while ceasing” could misleadingly describe the causes alone or turn
the passage into an account of destructive action.

The research Bhāṣya largely preserves the local exposition, but its
suggestion that the predecessor “vacates the place” is an explanatory
extension of *avakāśadāna*. The source says “giving an opening”; it
need not imply a spatial container vacated by one mind and occupied
by another. The running transcription includes *dvī hetū*,
*varktamānam*, and *samantarapratyaya*; their context is clear, but no
independent corrected edition is asserted here.

**Organon interpretation.** This verse sharpens our question about
grounds and conditions. It adds the state of the conditioned term to
the identification of the conditioning relation. Knowing that a cause
is homogeneous or that a dharma is an object is not yet the same as
knowing how that relation operates in the occurrence being explained.

The distinction between opening for succession and present apprehension
is especially useful: what allows the next cognitive event and what
that event apprehends are different determinations. Their articulation
can inform an inquiry into synthesis. The source does not itself
identify this articulation with Samādhi or divide the six causes and
four conditions into exclusively objective and subjective domains.
