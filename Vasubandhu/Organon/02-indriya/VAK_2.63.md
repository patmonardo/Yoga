# VAK_2.63 — Activity by State

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> निरुध्यमाने कारित्रं द्वौ हेतू कुरुतस्त्रयः ।
>
> जायमाने ततोऽन्यौ तु प्रत्ययौ तद्विपर्ययात् ॥ २.६३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nirudhyamāne kāritraṃ dvau hetū kurutas trayaḥ /
>
> jāyamāne tato 'nyau tu pratyayau tadviparyayāt // 2.63 //

Ceasing is the present, directed toward cessation. Not already ceased. Not a destruction. Three belongs with arising.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Two causes perform activity toward the ceasing. Three, toward the arising. The other two conditions, by the reverse of that.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

In what state of a dharma do the conditions act? Cause-condition is fivefold. Ceasing is the present, directed toward cessation. Co-arisen and associated act toward it. Their operation is on a fruit arisen together with them. Arising is the future, directed toward arising. Homogeneous, pervasive, and maturation act toward it.

The other two, in the reverse order. Immediately antecedent acts toward the arising, by giving an opening. Object-condition acts toward the ceasing, because present mind and factors apprehend. Predominant condition stands in every state by non-obstruction. That is its activity. The conditions have been explained, with their activities.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The locative is the target, not the cause. Two do not destroy the fruit. The fruit is present and directed toward cessation. Three do not become future by acting toward a future. Homogeneous cause was excluded as future. Maturation still gives when past.

Giving an opening is not vacating a place. Apprehension here is not the taking of a fruit. Predominance is not confined to either state.

Organon rendering:

Activity is the state toward which a relation operates. Naming the condition does not say that. Not the verse. Not the final pass.
