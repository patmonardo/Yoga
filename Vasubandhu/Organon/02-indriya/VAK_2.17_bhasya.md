# VAK_2.17 Bhāṣya — Necessary Possession of Neutral Feeling, Life, and Mind

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ekādaśabhir arhattvam uktaṃ tv ekasya saṃbhavāt /
> upekṣājīvitamanoyukto 'vaśyaṃ trayānvitaḥ // 2.17 //

> Arhatship is said through eleven, however, because of the possibility for one person. One endowed with neutral feeling, life, or mind is necessarily accompanied by the three.

The eleven was translated with 2.16. This study is possession. Any one is the commentary's anyatamena. Upekṣā follows the running witness.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

This is now examined: one possessing which faculty necessarily possesses how many? One possessing neutral feeling, life, or mind necessarily possesses the three. Whoever possesses any one of these, beginning with neutral feeling, necessarily possesses three faculties, these very three. For none of them is possessed without the others. With the remaining faculties there is no fixed rule. One may possess them or may not.

One born in the formless realm does not possess the eye, ear, nose, or tongue. In the desire realm, one also lacks them when they have not been acquired or have been lost. One born in the formless realm does not possess the body-faculty. One born in the form or formless realm does not possess the female faculty. In the desire realm, one lacks it when it has not been acquired or has been lost. The same applies to the male faculty.

An ordinary person born in the fourth dhyāna or the formless realm does not possess pleasure. An ordinary person born in the third or fourth dhyāna or the formless realm does not possess gladness. One born in the form or formless realm does not possess pain. One free from desire for the desire realm does not possess distress. One whose wholesome roots have been severed does not possess the faculties beginning with faith.

Ordinary persons and those established in the fruits do not possess “I shall know.” Ordinary persons and those on the paths of seeing and no-more-training do not possess the knowledge-faculty. Ordinary persons and trainees do not possess the faculty of one who has known. In conditions not excluded, possession is to be understood as stated.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The predicate changes from attainment through faculties to possession of faculties. A necessary relation among three, then why the rest are not fixed by possessing one of those three.

The exclusions are realm, not acquired or lost, ordinary person, dispassion, severed roots, and path-position. The last sentence is the complementary cases. No school. No opponent.

## 5. What the Triad Establishes

Reciprocal. Neutral feeling entails life and mind. Either of the latter entails the other two. Not a frequent association. Necessarily, and none without another.

Accompaniment, not exactly three and no others. No fixed rule is relative to this inference. It is not the denial of every further rule.

Possession is not an occurring neutral feeling at every moment. Samanvāgama is endowment. An event-count would erase the distinction of 2.07 and 2.08.

## 6. The Exclusions

Realm-absence and not-acquired or lost are different histories to the same negative. Neither is a faculty possessed but not operating.

Pleasure and gladness name an ordinary person. Not every person at those levels. Fourth dhyāna for pleasure. Third and fourth for gladness. Formless in both.

Distress is freedom from desire for the desire realm. The five are severed wholesome roots. Not realm alone, and not a mood.

## 7. The Realization Faculties

Provisional: ordinary persons and those established in fruits lack the first. Ordinary persons and those on seeing or no-more-training lack the second. Ordinary persons and trainees lack the third.

Established in fruits is not a claim that anyone who has attained a fruit can never stand in another path-relation. Lacking the knowledge-faculty is not lacking all knowledge. The damaged compounds stay recorded.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Samanvāgata is possessing, against attained and against arising. Yukta and anvita are resolved by that prose. No theory of the relation that establishes possession is supplied.

Apratilabdha is not acquired. Vihīna is lost. Aniyama is no fixed rule for the remainder, given the triad.

Born in a realm, ordinary person, free from desire, and established on a path are not one status.

## 9. Organon Reading

A necessary possession, and the rest variable. Absence has different grounds. A necessary possession is not a continuously occurring event.

The three are life, mind, and neutral feeling in possession. Not a minimum theory of mind. Non-consciousness is not the word, and non-reflective is the look-ahead, not this verse. Not the verse English.
