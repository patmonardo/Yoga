# VAK_2.29 Bhāṣya — Counting Unwholesome Mental Factors

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> āveṇike tv akuśale dṛṣṭiyukte ca viṃśatiḥ /
>
> kleśaiś caturbhiḥ krodhādyaiḥ kaukṛtyenaikaviṃśatiḥ // 2.29 //

> In the Desire Realm, isolated unwholesome consciousness and unwholesome
> consciousness associated with a view each have twenty mental factors.
> With one of the four afflictions—attachment, hostility, conceit, or doubt—
> or one secondary affliction beginning with anger, or regret, the local
> count is twenty-one.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

In isolated unwholesome consciousness, and in unwholesome consciousness associated with a view, there are twenty mental factors. The isolated case has ten universal great-ground factors, six affliction great-ground factors, two unwholesome great-ground factors, and initial and sustained examination. “Isolated” means that ignorance occurs without a further affliction such as attachment. The view-associated case has the same twenty. Should the view not make twenty-one? No: a particular form of discriminative comprehension, already counted among the universal great-ground factors, is called a view. The unwholesome views in question are wrong view, attachment to views, and attachment to rules and observances.

When unwholesome consciousness is associated instead with one of four afflictions—attachment, hostility, conceit, or doubt—the count is twenty-one: the twenty just stated, plus that affliction. The same count applies when one of the secondary afflictions beginning with anger is present. With regret, too, the count is twenty-one; regret itself is the additional factor. In brief, isolated unwholesome consciousness and the view-associated case have twenty, while consciousness associated with another affliction or secondary affliction has twenty-one.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

A base count. Isolated defined. The same count extended to view. An objection asks why view does not add. The reply identifies view as a form of a factor already counted. The remaining clauses add one. A summary. No school. Desire Realm is carried from 2.28, not restated in each clause.

## 5. Isolated

Ten universal, six affliction, two unwholesome, and the examination
pair. Isolated cannot mean isolated from all other factors. Ignorance
alone excludes another affliction such as attachment.

Read with the count. Heedlessness, laziness, lack of faith, sluggishness, and agitation are not removed because ignorance is called alone. The six stay. The passage distinguishes a further affliction. It does not retract the accompaniments already established.

Mental factors in a consciousness. Not faculties possessed by a continuum. Consciousness is not an additional factor.

## 6. Why View Does Not Add

The objection is numerical. The answer is identity. View is a special
form of discriminative comprehension, already counted among the
universal factors. The particular form is not a further member beside it.

Wrong view, attachment to views, attachment to rules and observances. Unwholesome is essential. Not every view is unwholesome. Obscured-indeterminate views are the next unit.

Discriminative comprehension here is not liberating knowledge. It is
already counted, and its determination can be an unwholesome view.
Occurrence is not adequacy.

## 7. Twenty-One

| Condition | Count |
|---|---|
| Isolated ignorance | 10 + 6 + 2 + 2 = 20 |
| A specified unwholesome view | the same twenty |
| Attachment, hostility, conceit, or doubt | twenty plus that one |
| A secondary beginning with anger | twenty plus that one |
| Regret | twenty plus regret |

Or distributes the four. Not twenty-four. The singular secondary controls the anger-group. Plurals name alternatives, not a joint list.

Regret continues the distinction of 2.28. This context is unwholesome, so the counted regret is the unwholesome case. Not all regret is unwholesome. Torpor is 2.30. These totals do not rule it out.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

The four and those beginning with anger are distinct groups. Attachment, hostility, conceit, doubt. Then secondaries beginning with anger. Four afflictions beginning with anger would conflate them.

Another affliction is an addition to the isolated count. Not a claim that the twenty are free of affliction, and not a claim that the six cease to be afflictions.

Mechanical defects stay in the source. Twenty, affliction great-ground, and twenty-one are contextual.

## 9. Interpretation

A particular determination is not a further associate. View is counted
through the discriminative comprehension it qualifies. The named
addition raises the total. Two descriptions can have different numbers
of names and the same number of factors.

Labels alone would count view twice. Collapsing every addition into
a qualification would lose the twenty-first. Identity before the count.

The view is a specialized form of discriminative comprehension, not
another factor beside it. That identity does not license collapsing
the additional afflictions into already-counted factors. A fine-grained
reading of a consciousness must test identity before adding members;
the count depends on which dharmas are actually distinct in the
episode. This passage does not identify a caitta with a Faculty.
