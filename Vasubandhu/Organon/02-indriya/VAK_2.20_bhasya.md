# VAK_2.20 Bhāṣya — The Fewest

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sarvālpair niḥśubho 'ṣṭābhir vinmanaḥkāyajīvitaiḥ /
>
> yukto bālas tathārūpye upekṣāyurmanaḥśubhaiḥ // 2.20 //

> At the minimum, one whose wholesome roots are severed possesses eight
> Faculties: the five feelings, Mind, body, and life. Likewise, an ordinary
> person in the Formless realm possesses eight: neutral feeling, life,
> Mind, and the five beginning with faith.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> samucchinnakuśamūlo niḥśubhaḥ /

The unit is printed 51.23–52.08 in
[the Sanskrit witness](../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt). Word division is
editorial. Srvālpaiḥ and niḥśāubhaḥ are read as sarvālpaiḥ and
niḥśubhaḥ. At 52.07–08 the witness reads aṣṭādhikārād
dvālādhikārācca; only the latter is normalized to bālādhikārāc ca.
The earlier note's Naṣṭādhikārād was not the witness reading.
Samucchinnakuśamūlo is construed as severed wholesome roots.
Vedanādibhiḥ supplies the five feelings. Vedayata is construed as
experiences. Contextual, not a collation. The greatest number at
52.09 opens 2.21.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Now, what is the smallest number of faculties with which someone is endowed? One “without the wholesome” possesses eight at the minimum: the five feelings, body, Mind, and life. “Without the wholesome” means that the person's wholesome roots have been severed. The word *vit* names feeling because it experiences; alternatively, it means the act of experiencing, as *saṃpat* means accomplishment.

An ordinary person in the Formless realm likewise possesses eight at the minimum, but a different eight. *Bāla* here means an ordinary person. Which faculties are counted? Neutral feeling, life, Mind, and the five beginning with faith. Those five are included under “the wholesome” because they are exclusively wholesome. Would that expression also include the three faculties beginning with “I shall know”? No: the count is eight, and the person in question is an ordinary person.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The question shifts from what one faculty entails to the fewest a possessor can have. Two eights. Each needs a lexical explanation: vit, then śubha.

An objection tests wholesome. If that quality takes the five, why not the three? The reply is the count and the ordinary person. No school is named.

## 5. Severed Roots

Without the wholesome is severed roots. Not a present unwholesome thought. Not no faculties.

The eight are pleasure, pain, gladness, distress, neutral feeling, body, mind, and life. Four expressions, five plus three. Vit is not a sixth feeling.

At the least is the minimum of the case. It does not say every such person lacks eye, ear, nose, tongue, or the sex faculties. Further faculties can raise the total. The prose does not list when each is absent.

## 6. The Formless Ordinary Person

The other eight are neutral feeling, life, Mind, faith, vigor,
mindfulness, concentration, and discriminative comprehension.
Bāla is ordinary person. Child would lose the status controlling the reply.

Formless and ordinary person both stay on this case. Not every ordinary person in every realm. Possession of the five is not possession of a realization-faculty.

The sets share neutral feeling, life, and mind. Body and the other four feelings complete the first. The five complete the second. Equal number, distinct membership.

## 7. Why Wholesome Does Not Take All

Exclusively wholesome does not uniquely name the five. The three would satisfy it.

Two restrictions. The three would exceed eight. The subject is an ordinary person. Numeral and bearer fix the local range.

The reply does not deny that the three are wholesome. It denies their inclusion here. General extension and local range need not coincide.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Both explanations of vit stay: that which experiences, and experiencing as an action noun. Saṃpat belongs to the second. A lexical derivation, not an experiencer beside the feeling.

Yathā … tathā is just as … likewise. The numerical comparison does not transfer severed roots to the formless ordinary person. That one possesses the five.

Possession is not simultaneous manifestation. Five feelings in the count are not five feelings in one moment.

The study translation resolves both collective expressions as five
and identifies severed wholesome roots explicitly. Likewise carries
the eight-count, not the privation of wholesome roots.

## 9. Interpretation

Eight can be severed roots, or a formless ordinary person with the five. The number cannot carry the determination. The shared triad is common possession, not a sufficient description of either case.

The Organon determination is a comparison of scoped minima. The shared
triad is not a complete account of either person, and the numeral
cannot replace the conditions identifying the five additional members.
This passage does not establish a controller, a telos of acquisition,
or a condition for entering the Path.

Mind is one member of both minima. Sharing it does not make the two
sets identical or turn possession into current cognitive operation.
The wider Organon account of Cognition, Perception, and Conception
remains to be tested elsewhere.

## 10. Review Status

Reviewed with the kārikā against printed 51.23–52.08. Both five-member
collectives and severed wholesome roots are explicit in the study
translation; the anchor matches exactly. The source note no longer
attributes a nonexistent initial na to aṣṭādhikārād.

Grammar–Logic distinguishes equal minima from equal membership or
every person's exact total. The wholesome objection and its two
restrictions remain intact. The interpretation's unsupported
controller and telos claims are removed; no global minimum proof
is claimed from a finite example model.

The maximum-possession question beginning 2.21 remains outside this review.
