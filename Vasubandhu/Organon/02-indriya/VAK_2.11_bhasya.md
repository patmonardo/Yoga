# VAK_2.11 Bhāṣya — Ethical Quality of the Faculties

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> mano 'nyavittiśraddhādīny aṣṭakaṃ kuśalaṃ dvidhā /
>
> daurmanasyaṃ mano 'nyā ca vittis tredhā anyad ekadhā // 2.11 //

> [The ten are] Mind, the other feelings, and those beginning with faith.
> The eight are exclusively wholesome. Distress is wholesome or
> unwholesome; Mind and the other feelings can be wholesome, unwholesome,
> or indeterminate. The remainder is exclusively indeterminate.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> kati kuśalāni katy akuśalāni katy avyākṛtāni /
> ekāntena tāvat / aṣṭakaṃ kuśalaṃ /
> pañca śraddhādīni trīṇi cājñāsyāmīndriyādīni /
> dvidhā / daurmanasyaṃ / kuśalaṃ cākuśalaṃ ca /
> mano 'nyā ca vittis tredhā / kuśalākuśalāvyākṛtāni /
> anyad ekadhā // 2.11 //
> kiñcid anyat / jīvitāṣṭamaṃ cakṣurādi /
> etad avyākṛtam eva //

The ethical unit is printed 46.07–16 in
[the Sanskrit witness](../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt). Word division is
editorial. No substantive repair. Kiñcid anyat asks what the remainder
is. The realm question at 46.17 opens 2.12. Material at 46.01–06 belongs
to 2.10: the opening words of this numbered kārikā complete that
preceding explanation. They remain in the verse and its translation,
but are not repeated as though they began the ethical Bhāṣya.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

How many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, and how many indeterminate? First, categorically, the eight are wholesome: the five beginning with faith and the three beginning with the faculty “I shall know.” Distress is twofold: wholesome and unwholesome. Mind and the other feelings are threefold: wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate. The remainder is onefold. What is that remainder? Those beginning with the eye, with life as the eighth. These are exclusively indeterminate.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

A new question, then the members the verse compresses. No objection. No quotation. No school. The work is distributive.

The verse's opening compound completes the previous ten: Mind, four
feelings other than distress, and five beginning with faith. The new
question begins at the wholesome eight. A colon between the opening
compound and the eight would falsely identify two different groups.

Twofold has just been used for maturation. Here it is wholesome or unwholesome. The count-word has no content apart from the question.

## 5. The Four Groups

| Group | Quality | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Five beginning with faith, and the three | wholesome only | 8 |
| Distress | wholesome or unwholesome | 1 |
| Mind and the four other feelings | three qualities | 5 |
| Seven of form, and life | indeterminate only | 8 |

Twenty-two. The four are pain, pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling. The final eight are eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, female, male, and life. Not the wholesome eight.

Threefold is the range of instances of each type. Not one quality assigned to mind and another to a feeling. Not three qualities in one instance.

## 6. Distress Can Be Wholesome

Kuśalaṃ cākuśalaṃ ca is explicit. Distress is not necessarily unwholesome. It is excluded from indeterminacy. The paragraph does not say when distress is wholesome. No example is added.

2.10 said distress is never maturation and always produces it. That does not make it unwholesome only. This paragraph does not revise that causal classification.

Pleasure is not necessarily wholesome. Pleasure and gladness are in the threefold group. Tone and ethical quality stay apart in both directions.

## 7. Two Neutralities

Upekṣā is neither painful nor pleasant. Avyākṛta is neither wholesome nor unwholesome. Neutral feeling is in the threefold group. Its tone does not settle its quality.

The seven of form and life are exclusively indeterminate. That is the faculties themselves. It does not make every act that uses them indeterminate, and it does not deny that they govern.

The wholesome eight still carry the 2.09 distinction. The five beginning with faith can be with or without *āsrava*; the three realization faculties are without *āsrava*. Their shared wholesome status does not erase that difference. Wholesome is not another name for being without *āsrava*.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Ekāntena makes the first group categorical. Eva makes the remainder exclusive. The prose supplies what twofold and threefold mean.

Anyā vittiḥ is read as the other feelings, the four. Jīvitāṣṭamam is the group with life as eighth, not eight kinds of life. Indeterminate is preferred to neutral, so avyākṛta stays off upekṣā.

## 9. Interpretation

Ethical quality is a further question about the faculties already established. The 2.09 question concerned *āsrava* status; 2.10 asked whether a faculty is maturation and whether it can produce maturation; 2.11 asks which ethical qualities each faculty type admits. These are different classification axes, not additional levels or newly enumerated faculties.

The Organon determination keeps the questions independent. Being
maturation is not an ethical quality; being without *āsrava* is not
another word for wholesome; neutral feeling is not ethical indeterminacy.
The source specifies permitted qualities, not a mechanism assigning a
quality to every concrete instance.

Mind's threefold ethical range does not make every cognitive power
identical to that Faculty or exhaust its systematic role. The wider
Organon account of Cognition, Perception, and Conception remains to be
tested against passages that address those relations.

## 10. Review Status

carried maturation enumeration from the ethical unit at 46.07–16.
The misleading colon is removed; the study translation resolves the
ethical ranges and the anchor matches exactly.

Wholesome distress, threefold neutral feeling, and the two distinct
eights remain explicit. Grammar–Logic preserves the carry boundary
and tests the 8 + 1 + 5 + 8 partition without inventing examples of
wholesome distress or treating permitted qualities as co-present.

The realm classification beginning 2.12 remains outside this review.
