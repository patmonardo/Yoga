# VAK_2.07 Bhāṣya — Bodily Feeling and Third-Dhyāna Pleasure

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> duḥkhendriyam aśātā yā kāyikī vedanā sukham /
> śātā dhyāne tṛtīye tu caitasī sā sukhendriyam // 2.07 //

> Unpleasant bodily feeling is the pain-faculty; pleasant bodily
> feeling is the pleasure-faculty. But in the third dhyāna, that
> pleasant feeling, when mental, is the pleasure-faculty.

The Bhāṣya supplies the bodily feeling understood with *sukham śātā*
and explains why the feeling in the third dhyāna is mental. The
introductory prose also locates these definitions within the chapter.

with this first definition of the feeling-faculties, as in the

Word division is editorial. The running witness's *karisyate* is
regularized to *kariṣyate*, *karttavyaḥ* to *kartavyaḥ*, and
*kāyikī śāta vedanā* to *kāyikī śātā vedanā*. The reading
*upaghātikā* follows the research Bhāṣya in place of the running
witness's *upadhātikā*. This repair gives the contrast with
*anugrāhikā* translated below; it is explicit rather than silently
treated as the transmitted form. No independent collation is claimed.

## 3. Scientific English Rendering

The Faculties beginning with the eye and extending through the Male
Faculty have been explained. Because the Life Faculty is dissociated
from mind, it will be explained among the factors dissociated from
Mind. Those beginning with confidence will be explained among the mental
factors. The Faculties beginning with pleasure and those beginning
with the faculty called “I shall know” remain to be explained.
That explanation is now given.

“Unpleasant bodily feeling is the pain-faculty.” “Unpleasant” means
afflictive: that is the meaning of painful. “Pleasure is pleasant”:
the pleasure-faculty is pleasant bodily feeling. “Pleasant” means
supportive: that is the meaning of pleasurable. “But in the third
dhyāna, that mental feeling is the pleasure-faculty.” In the third
dhyāna, that same pleasant feeling, being mental, is the pleasure-faculty.
For there is no bodily feeling there, because the five classes of
sensory cognition are absent.

## 4. The Transition to Definitions

The preceding units explained Faculty-status, its scope, and alternative
arrangements of the list. This passage turns to definitions while
distributing their treatment across the chapter. Life belongs in
The discussion of factors dissociated from consciousness; the five beginning
with confidence belong among the mental factors. Feeling and the three
realization faculties receive the immediate explanation.

“Beginning with the eye and extending through the male faculty”
refers to the previously treated range. It does not say “ten material
faculties”: that description in the research analysis is unsupported
by the sentence and would obscure the inclusion of mind in the
faculty sequence.

There is no opponent or named school in this unit. The commentary
introduces its next task and explains the verse's successive phrases.

## 5. Unpleasant and Pleasant Bodily Feeling

Both definitions identify a kind of *vedanā*, feeling. Unpleasant
bodily feeling receives the designation *duḥkhendriya*; pleasant
bodily feeling receives the designation *sukhendriya*. The adjectives
*aśātā*, *śātā*, and *kāyikī* qualify feeling, while the faculty-names
state its classification.

The commentary glosses unpleasantness as *upaghātikā*, afflictive
or injurious, and pleasantness as *anugrāhikā*, supportive or favorable.
These glosses characterize the experienced effect of feeling. They
do not identify pleasant feeling with ethical wholesomeness or
unpleasant feeling with ethical unwholesomeness. “Supportive” also
does not by itself establish a claim about long-term bodily benefit.

The terms “pain” and “pleasure” are technical Faculty designations
here. Their scope should not be expanded to every form of suffering
and happiness before the remaining feeling-faculties are defined.

## 6. Pleasant Mental Feeling in the Third Dhyāna

The verse's *tu*, “but,” introduces the qualification: in the third
dhyāna, pleasant mental feeling is the pleasure-faculty. The prose
repeats this and supplies the reason bodily feeling is unavailable:
the five sensory cognition-classes are absent there.

The explanation preserves distinct questions. The feeling is pleasant;
its mode is mental; its setting is the third dhyāna; and its designation
is the pleasure-faculty. None of these determinations should replace
the others. The pronoun *sā* and the prose's *saiva* resume pleasant
feeling as the relevant kind, not a numerically identical bodily
feeling that changes into a mental entity.

The absence of sensory cognition explains why the feeling cannot
be bodily. By itself, that absence does not provide the complete
reason for preferring the name *sukha* to *saumanasya*, gladness.
The next unit explicitly develops that distinction. The present
study preserves the assignment made here without importing the
next unit's additional explanation into this translation.

## 7. Bodily Feeling and Mental Factors

The introductory reference to *caitta*, mental factors, and the
verse's adjective *caitasī*, mental, occur in different classificatory
relations. The former locates a later discussion of the faculties
beginning with confidence. The latter distinguishes a mode of feeling
from bodily feeling in the present definition.

Consequently, “bodily feeling” should not be taken to mean that
feeling is a material organ. The commentary itself links its absence
to the absence of the five sensory cognition-classes. Likewise,
*vijñānakāya* here means a class or group of cognition; the word
*kāya* within that compound does not make cognition a physical body.

The life-faculty's being *viprayukta* is a reason for its placement
in another discussion, not an assertion that life has no relations
to mental activity. The full definition is expressly deferred.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

The prose explicitly resolves *sukham śātā* as pleasant bodily
feeling. Supplying “bodily feeling” in the anchor is therefore a
Bhāṣya-supported resolution of ellipsis. “Afflictive” and “supportive”
translate the commentary's glosses, not additional words of the verse.

*Caitasī* is rendered “mental” and *kāyikī* “bodily.” These retain
the local contrast without replacing it with a modern anatomical
or neurological account. *Pañcavijñānakāya* is rendered “the five
classes of sensory cognition,” preserving both the plurality and
the explanatory link to feeling.

“Dissociated from consciousness” makes explicit the technical context of
*viprayukta* in the opening survey. “Those beginning with confidence”
and “those beginning with ‘I shall know’” remain the text's abbreviated
references to groups already introduced.

## 9. Interpretation

The Bhāṣya distinguishes the Faculty's designation from the mode
of feeling: disagreeable bodily feeling is pain, and agreeable bodily
feeling is ordinarily pleasure. It then qualifies that ordinary
classification. Because bodily feeling is absent in the third dhyāna,
agreeable mental feeling there is assigned to the Pleasure Faculty.
The passage does not generalize this exception to every agreeable
mental feeling.

The commentary's glosses, *upaghātikā* and *anugrāhikā*, characterize
feeling as injurious or supportive; they do not establish moral
wholesomeness or long-term bodily benefit. Nor does the local contrast
turn feeling into an organ. The next unit's distinction between
*sukha* and *saumanasya* must be kept separate from the explanation
given here.

The project crosswalk with Dhātu 1.48 places this definition within
the Base–Essence–Principle architecture, while preserving the distinction
between location and feeling-mode. This is an Organon reading, not a
claim that the Bhāṣya states that framework.

Across the Kośa, Vijñāna is Discriminative Cognition joining Perception
and Conception without reducing their unity to one homogeneous
operation; it governs Mind and guides the reading of Dharma Base. The
same *avijñapti* remains one content in the Form Base and Dharma Base
classifications, and Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it. This
cross-chapter synthesis is an Organon framework, not a claim made by
this Bhāṣya.

In a Kant-informed Techne reading, affective character, feeling-mode,
and the stated meditative context jointly delimit the Faculty
designation. This is a project-level interpretation, not terminology
or philosophical attribution supplied by the source.
