# VAK_2.07 Bhāṣya — Bodily Feeling and Third-Dhyāna Pleasure

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> duḥkhendriyam aśātā yā kāyikī vedanā sukham /
>
> śātā dhyāne tṛtīye tu caitasī sā sukhendriyam // 2.07 //

> Pain-Faculty is whichever bodily feeling is disagreeable; Pleasure-Faculty
> is agreeable bodily feeling. But in the third dhyāna, that agreeable
> mental feeling is Pleasure-Faculty.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> tatra cakṣurādīnāṃ puruṣendriyaparyantānāṃ kṛto nirdeśaḥ /
> jīvitendriyasya viprayuktatvād viprayukteṣv eva kariṣyate nirdeśaḥ /
> śraddhādīnāṃ caitteṣu kariṣyate /
> sukhādīnām ājñāsyāmīndriyādīnāṃ ca kartavyaḥ /
> so 'yaṃ kriyate /
>
> duḥkhendriyam aśātā yā kāyikī vedanā /
> aśātety upaghātikā duḥkhety arthaḥ /
> sukham / śātā /
> sukhendriyaṃ kāyikī śātā vedanā /
> śātety anugrāhikā sukhety arthaḥ /
> dhyāne tṛtīye tu caitasī sā sukhendriyam // 2.07 //
> tṛtīye tu dhyāne saiva śātā vedanā caitasī sukhendriyam /
> na hi tatra kāyikī vedanā 'sti / pañcavijñānakāyābhāvāt /

The unit runs from printed 41.05 through 41.15 in
[the Sanskrit witness](../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt). The survey belongs
with this first definition. Anyatra sā saumanasyam begins 2.08 and is not
taken here.

Word division is editorial. Karisyate → kariṣyate, karttavyaḥ → kartavyaḥ, kāyikī śāta vedanā → kāyikī śātā vedanā. Upaghātikā follows the research bhāṣya against the running upadhātikā, for the contrast with anugrāhikā. No independent collation is claimed.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The faculties beginning with the eye and extending through the male faculty have been explained. Because the life-faculty is dissociated from mind, it will be explained among the factors dissociated from mind. Those beginning with faith will be explained among the mental factors. The faculties beginning with pleasure, and those beginning with the faculty called “I shall know,” remain to be explained. That explanation is now given.

Disagreeable bodily feeling is the pain-faculty. Disagreeable means injurious: that is the meaning of painful. Pleasure is agreeable: the pleasure-faculty is agreeable bodily feeling. Agreeable means supportive: that is the meaning of pleasurable. But in the third dhyāna, that mental feeling is the pleasure-faculty. In the third dhyāna, that same agreeable feeling, being mental, is the pleasure-faculty. For there is no bodily feeling there, because the five cognition-groups are absent.

## 4. The Transition to Definitions

The preceding units explained faculty-status, scope, and an alternative order. This passage turns to definitions and distributes the rest of the chapter. Life belongs among the dissociated. The five beginning with faith belong among the mental factors. Feeling and the three are explained now.

Eye through the male faculty is the range already treated. It does not say ten Form faculties. Mind is in that sequence.

No opponent. No named school.

## 5. Disagreeable and Agreeable Bodily Feeling

Both definitions are a kind of vedanā. Disagreeable bodily feeling is the pain-faculty. Agreeable bodily feeling is the pleasure-faculty. Aśātā, śātā, and kāyikī qualify feeling. The faculty-names classify it.

Upaghātikā is injurious. Anugrāhikā is supportive. These are the experienced effect. They are not ethical wholesomeness and unwholesomeness. Supportive is not a claim about long-term bodily benefit.

Pain and pleasure are faculty-names. They are not every suffering and every happiness. Gladness and distress are not yet defined.

## 6. Agreeable Mental Feeling in the Third Dhyāna

Tu introduces the qualification. In the third dhyāna, agreeable mental feeling is the pleasure-faculty. Bodily feeling is unavailable because the five cognition-groups are absent.

Pleasant, mental, third dhyāna, and pleasure-faculty stay distinct. Sā and saiva resume the agreeable feeling. They are not one bodily feeling becoming a mental entity.

Absence of sensory cognition explains why the feeling is not bodily. It does not yet say why the name is pleasure rather than gladness. That is the next unit. It is not imported here.

## 7. Bodily Feeling and Mental Factors

Caitta in the survey locates the later discussion of those beginning with faith. Caitasī in the verse is the mode of this feeling. Bodily feeling is not a material organ. Its absence is tied to the absence of the five cognition-groups. Kāya in vijñānakāya is the group, not a physical body.

Viprayukta places life in another discussion. It does not say life has no relation to mental activity. The definition is deferred.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

The prose resolves sukham śātā as agreeable bodily feeling. Bodily in that clause is the commentary's ellipsis. Injurious and supportive are the glosses, not words of the verse.

Caitasī is mental. Kāyikī is bodily. Pañcavijñānakāya is the five cognition-groups. Dissociated from mind is the survey's viprayukta.

## 9. Interpretation

Faculty-classification is constrained by tone, mode, and level. Agreeable
tone alone does not establish Pleasure-Faculty; the bodily case and the
third-dhyāna mental case must remain distinguishable. The same Faculty-name
does not posit one feeling persisting through a change of mode.

In the Organon synthesis, Cognition joins and governs Perception and
Conception, guides the reading of Dharma Base, and remains distinct from
Mind and consciousness. The local passage contributes a precise constraint:
where the five Cognition-groups are absent, bodily feeling is absent.
This does not establish the absence of all Cognition or all feeling. The
interpretive force is a rule of qualified classification, not an additional
causal mechanism attributed to the Sanskrit.

## 10. Review Status

Reviewed with the kārikā against printed 41.05–15. The verse translation
now explicitly carries the Bhāṣya's bodily-feeling ellipsis; the anchor
matches it exactly. Morphological agreement, the resumed agreeable
feeling, and the third-dhyāna restriction are distinguished in Grammar
and Logic. Finite-model theories include rejected unqualified mental
pleasure and rejected conversion of mental feeling into bodily feeling.

The upaghātikā repair remains provisional, not an independently collated
reading. The explanation of pleasure versus gladness remains with 2.08.
