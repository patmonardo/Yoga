# VAK_2.47

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> नामकायादयः संज्ञावाक्याक्षरसमुक्तयः ।
>
> कामरूपाप्तसत्त्वाख्या निःष्यन्दाव्याकृतास्तथा ॥ २.४७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nāmakāyādayaḥ saṃjñāvākyākṣarasamuktayaḥ /
>
> kāmarūpāptasattvākhyā niḥṣyandāvyākṛtās tathā // 2.47 //

The kārikā is cited in parts: its first half at 80.13, its
classification half at 82.03–04, and its final *tathā* at 82.08.
The intervening prose completes this unit; VAK 2.48 begins at 82.09.


| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| nāma-kāya-ādayaḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | name-groups and the remaining linguistic groups |
| saṃjñā-vākya-akṣara-samuktayaḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | collections of designations, sentences, and phonetic elements |
| kāma-rūpa-āpta | nominative masculine plural compound | belonging to the desire and form realms |
| sattva-ākhyāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | classified as associated with sentient beings |
| niḥṣyanda | nominative masculine plural in compound | homogeneous outflows |
| avyākṛtāḥ | nominative masculine plural adjective | indeterminate |
| tathā | indeclinable | likewise / also |

`Kāya` means a group or collection here, not an animate body. `Akṣara` is
determined by the Bhāṣya as a phonetic element such as `a` or `ā`; “letter” is
therefore misleading if it suggests that writing is primary.

## 4. Grammar

The first half defines the members denoted by `nāmakāyādayaḥ`:

```text
nāma-kāya-ādayaḥ
    = saṃjñā-vākya-akṣara-samuktayaḥ

name-groups and the rest
    = collections of designation, sentence, and phonetic element
```

The Bhāṣya unfolds the elliptical `ādayaḥ` as three parallel groups:

```text
nāma-kāya       name-group
vākya/pada-kāya sentence-group
akṣara-kāya     phonetic-element group
```

`Samukti` is explained from √`uc` in the sense of coming together and is
treated as equivalent to collection or aggregation. The second half predicates
four classifications of all three groups:

```text
kāma-rūpa-āpta     → realm classification
sattva-ākhyā       → association with a sentient continuum
niḥṣyanda          → result classification
avyākṛta           → ethical classification
```

The surface sandhi `avyākṛtās tathā` represents `avyākṛtāḥ tathā`.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Name-groups and the rest are collections of designations, sentences, and phonetic elements. They belong to the desire and form realms, are classified as associated with sentient beings, are homogeneous outflows, and are likewise indeterminate.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The Vaibhāṣikas classify the groups of names and the rest as formations dissociated from mind, belonging to the desire and form realms, possessed by the sentient being who signifies through them, homogeneous outflows, and unobscured-indeterminate. Others say that such entities also belong to the formless realm but are inexpressible. Vasubandhu challenges their substantial status, arguing that sound with a conventionally fixed application can reveal meaning without a distinct name-entity; the Vaibhāṣikas maintain their position.

## 6. Limited Organon Reading

Within the project's First Philosophy framing, this verse extends the
classification inquiry to linguistic formations: designation, sentence,
and phonetic-element groups, together with their reported status. The
Bhāṣya's dispute over whether these collections exist as separate
substances remains distinct from the conventional account of their
functions. The source raises the problem of successive sounds but does
not give a complete theory of retention or synthesis.

`Saṃjñā` must be controlled by its present function. Here it is designation;
its appearance in the analysis of non-reflective attainment does not license a
single undifferentiated English rendering across both discussions.

## 8. Logical Determination

The three linguistic functions are:

```text
Designation(sound, convention)
    → fixes a semantic application

Sentence(sequence)
    → articulates relations of action, quality, time, and the like
    → reaches completion of meaning

PhoneticElement(event)
    → supplies a minimal audible articulation
```

Their empirical genesis is successive:

```text
sound₁@t₁ → sound₂@t₂ → ... → soundₙ@tₙ

For i ≠ j:
    soundᵢ and soundⱼ need not coexist
```

The Bhāṣya uses this succession to question how speech could produce
one name as a whole. The proposal that the final sound alone produces
the name is challenged by the consequence that hearing only that sound
should then convey the meaning.

The Bhāṣya's production/manifestation dilemma is:

```text
If every sound produces or manifests NameEntity:
    mere noise should do so.

If only conventionally specific sound does so:
    that specific meaningful sound already performs the semantic work.

Therefore:
    no additional NameEntity is explanatorily required.
```

The final-sound hypothesis also fails:

```text
If finalSound alone produces the completed name:
    hearing finalSound alone should yield the meaning.

It does not.
```

And co-arising cannot ground reference:

```text
CoArises(Name, Referent)
    cannot explain past referents
    cannot explain future referents
    cannot explain freely assigned names
    cannot explain names of unconditioned dharmas
```

The general rule is:

```text
stable semantic function
    ⇏ separately existing semantic substance
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

The verse moves from the conditioned marks to the groups through which
designation, sentence, and phonetic elements are classified. The Bhāṣya
questions whether their semantic and collective functions require additional
substances, then preserves the Vaibhāṣika defense and the groups' stated
classifications. Its closing *tathā* is recited at 82.08 after the
concluding prose at 82.06–07; VAK 2.48 begins at 82.09.
