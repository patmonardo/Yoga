# VAK_2.47 Bhāṣya — Groups of Names

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nāmakāyādayaḥ saṃjñāvākyākṣarasamuktayaḥ /
>
> kāmarūpāptasattvākhyā niḥṣyandāvyākṛtās tathā // 2.47 //

> The groups of names and the rest are collections of designation, sentence,
> and syllable respectively. They belong to the Desire and Form Realms,
> are classed with sentient beings, are homogeneous outflows, and are
> indeterminate. Likewise [the classification continues in the next verse].

## 3. Continuous Translation

What are the groups of names and the rest? Collections of
designation, sentence, and syllable. “And the rest” includes
groups of sentences and syllables. A name makes a designation,
such as “Form” or “sound.” A sentence is a phrase sufficient
to complete a meaning, such as “Impermanent indeed are
formations,” through which distinctions of action, quality,
time, and relation are understood. A syllable is a sound-unit,
such as *a* or *ā*.

Are syllables not also names of parts of writing? Syllables
were not devised to convey written parts; written parts
were devised to convey syllables, so that what is not
heard might be understood through writing. Thus syllables
are not names of those written parts. The collections of
designations and the rest are the corresponding groups.
The root *uc* is taught in the sense of collection, and
*samukti* has that sense. A name-group is, for example,
“Form, sound, smell, taste, tangible.” A sentence-group
is “All formations are impermanent; all dharmas are
non-self; nirvāṇa is peaceful.” A syllable-group is
“ka, kha, ga, gha, ña” [the last item is the running
transcription's reading].

Since these have speech as their nature and therefore
consist of sound, would they not have Form as their
nature? Why call them dissociated from consciousness?
“They do not have speech as their nature. Speech is
vocal sound, and meanings are not understood through
mere sound.” What, then? “Speech operates upon a name,
and the name reveals meaning.” The reply is that speech
is not mere noise: the sound through which meaning is
understood is speech. Which sound conveys meaning?
That whose application to meanings speakers have fixed.
Thus *go* is conventionally applied to nine meanings:
speech, direction, earth, ray, thunderbolt, cattle,
eye, heaven, and water.

Even one who holds that names reveal meaning must accept
that convention determines which meaning is conveyed.
This is already accomplished by conventionally understood
sound; why posit another name-entity? How does speech
operate upon a name: does it produce or reveal it?
If it produces it, then either every vocal noise produces
a name, or the special sound said to produce a name
could itself reveal the meaning. If it reveals it,
the same alternatives apply: either every noise reveals
a name, or the special revealing sound could itself
reveal meaning.

Nor do the successive sounds come together in a single
moment, and one entity cannot properly be produced
piecemeal. How could speech produce a name? “How does
the final moment of manifest expression produce
nonmanifest expression in dependence on past moments?”
Then, if the name is produced by the final sound
[clause damaged], one hearing that sound alone should
understand the meaning. If speech produces syllables
and syllables produce the name, the same problem
returns: the syllables do not coexist. It also applies
if speech reveals the name. Even those with discriminative
comprehension of speech and undistracted consciousness
cannot delimit the proposed syllabic entity by its
characteristic [contextual rendering of a damaged
sentence]; speech is thus not established as producing
or revealing such an entity.

Suppose a name co-arises with its meaning, like arising
and the other marks. There could then be no present
name for a past or future meaning. Parents assign
children names at will; which name would co-arise
with the child? With what could a name for an
unconditioned dharma co-arise? This proposal too
is untenable.

What of the Blessed One's statement, “Verses depend
on names; the poet is the support of verses”?
Here a name is sound with conventionally fixed
application to meanings, and a verse is a particular
arrangement of such names. The arrangement is not
another entity, as with a row or a succession of
consciousnesses. Even if syllables alone were posited
as distinct entities, name-groups and the rest
would simply be their collections; positing those
as further entities would be pointless.

“Name-groups nevertheless exist as real formations
dissociated from consciousness,” maintain the
Vaibhāṣikas. “Not all dharmas are accessible to
reasoning.” Then how are these groups classified:
by Realm, with sentient or non-sentient things,
as maturation-born, accumulation-derived, or
homogeneous outflow, and as wholesome, unwholesome,
or indeterminate?

They are included in the Desire and Form Realms.
Others say there are also Formless ones, but
inexpressible. They are classed with sentient
beings: the one who signifies possesses them,
not what is signified. They are homogeneous
outflows and unobscured-indeterminate. Just as
these groups have the sentient classification,
homogeneous outflow, and unobscured-indeterminate
status, likewise [the same-class continuation
follows in 2.48].

## 4. Three Collections and Conventional Sound

Designation, sentence, and syllable correspond to
three collection-kinds. Written marks convey sounds;
the account does not identify audible syllables with
ink shapes. The critic's conventionally fixed sound
is not undifferentiated noise. The nine meanings of
*go* test conventional application, not a theory that
every occurrence has all nine meanings at once.

## 5. Production, Revelation, and Succession

Production and revelation are alternatives, each
tested by the noise/specific-sound distinction.
The non-coexistence objection then concerns the
successive sounds. The manifest/nonmanifest analogy
receives the last-sound-alone objection, followed
by the intermediate-syllable proposal. Co-arising
with meaning is a further proposal, tested against
past/future reference, arbitrary naming, and the
unconditioned. None should disappear into a generic
claim that language is conventional.

The verse/poet quotation, arrangement analogy,
and collection objection follow before the
Vaibhāṣika reaffirmation. The ontology remains
disputed; the shared classification is not a
proof that the critic accepted separate entities.

## 6. Classification and the Dangling “Likewise”

| Main classification | Determination |
|---|---|
| Realm | Desire or Form |
| Sentient classification | Possessed by the signifier, not the signified |
| Genesis | Homogeneous outflow |
| Ethical class | Unobscured-indeterminate |

The Formless/inexpressible extension is attributed
to other teachers. The final “likewise” transfers
sentient classification, outflow, and unobscured-
indeterminate status to 2.48's same-class discussion.
It does not transfer the two-Realm restriction:
same-class is there assigned to all three Realms
and also admits maturation.

## 7. Reading Control

The initial account classifies name, sentence, and syllable groups
as formations dissociated from consciousness. The critic argues
that conventionally applied sound and its arrangement already
explain intelligible speech, and presses the difficulty of producing
or revealing a separate name through successive sounds. The
Vaibhāṣikas reaffirm the groups as distinct entities before the
classification resumes. Their reaffirmation is an attributed
position, not a conclusion supplied by the critic's argument.
