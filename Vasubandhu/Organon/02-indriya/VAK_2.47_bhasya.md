# VAK 2.47 Bhāṣya — Linguistic Formations and Their Determination

## 1. Scope and Textual Position

The commentary runs from the question at printed 80.12 through the
closing *tathā* at 82.08. The verse is cited in parts: its definition
at 80.13, its classification at 82.03–04, and its closing *tathā*
at 82.08. The prose following that last citation begins the discussion
of commonality and belongs with VAK 2.48.

The translation follows the running text in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. Transcription uncertainties that
affect the argument are identified below; no independent collation is
claimed.

## 2. Continuous Scientific Translation

What are the groups of names and the rest?

> The groups of names and the rest are collections of designations,
> sentences, and phonetic elements.

“And the rest” includes groups of sentences and phonetic elements.
A name is the making of a designation, as in “Form,” “sound,” and
the like. A sentence is an expression extending as far as completion
of a meaning, as in “Impermanent indeed are conditioned formations.”
Through it, particular relations of action, quality, time, and the
like are understood. A phonetic element is an *akṣara*, such as *a*
or *ā*.

But are not phonetic elements names for the components of writing?
No. Phonetic elements were not devised so that components of writing
could be understood; rather, components of writing were devised so
that phonetic elements could be understood—“How might these be
understood through writing when they are not heard?” Therefore
phonetic elements are not names for those written components.

Collections of these designations and the rest are the groups of
names and the other groups. The root *uc* is taught in the sense of
coming together; *samukti* is a form derived from it. What “coming
together” means, “collection” means here.

A name-group is, for example, “Form, sound, smell, taste, and the
tangible.” A sentence-group is, for example, “All conditioned
formations are impermanent; all Dharmas are without self; Nirvāṇa
is peaceful.” A group of phonetic elements is, for example, “ka,
kha, ga, gha, ña,” and the like [the final member follows the
transmitted reading].

But since these have speech as their nature and consist of sound,
they would have Form as their nature. Why, then, are they called
formations dissociated from Mind? They do not have speech as their
nature. Speech is vocal sound, and meanings are not understood from
mere vocal sound.

How, then? Speech operates upon a name, and the name reveals a meaning.
But speech is not mere vocal sound. The sound through which a meaning
is understood is speech. Through what sound is meaning understood?
Through sound whose application to meanings has been fixed by speakers.
Thus the word *go* has a fixed application to nine meanings:

> A discerning person should recognize the word *go* in nine meanings:
> speech, direction, Earth, ray, thunderbolt, cattle, eye, heaven,
> and water.

Even someone who thinks that a name reveals meaning must admit that
the sound has an understood meaning. That is established from the
meaningful sound itself. Why posit another name?

Nor is it clear how speech operates upon a name. Does it produce the
name or reveal it? If it produces it, then, since speech has vocal
sound as its nature, every mere vocal sound would produce a name.
Or, if a particular kind of sound is said to produce the name, that
same sound can reveal the meaning. If speech reveals the name, then
every mere vocal sound would reveal it; or the particular sound said
to reveal the name would itself reveal the meaning.

Moreover, sounds do not form a totality that comes together in one
moment. Nor is it reasonable for one entity to be produced part by
part. How, then, could speech produce a name?

How, then, does the final moment of manifested communication, dependent
on what has passed, produce non-manifest communication? If that is so,
the name would be produced only at the final [sound]; then anyone who
heard that sound alone should understand the meaning.

Suppose instead that speech produces a phonetic element, and the
phonetic element produces a name. The same consequence follows, because
the phonetic elements do not form a simultaneous totality. The same
difficulty applies if speech reveals the name. Even people of
exceptional discernment in speech, with their minds attentive, cannot
delimit phonetic elements by their characteristics [the transmitted
wording is uncertain]. Therefore speech cannot reasonably be either
the producer or the revealer of phonetic elements.

Suppose a name arises together with its referent, like arising and the
other conditioned marks. Then there could be no present name for a past
or future referent. Parents assign names to children as they wish;
which name would arise together with the child? And with what would a
name of an unconditioned Dharma arise? This proposal is untenable.

The Blessed One said:

> A verse rests upon a name; the poet is the support of verses.

Here, a name is a sound whose application to meanings has been fixed;
a verse is a particular arrangement of names and thus rests upon them.
But a particular arrangement cannot reasonably be a separate substance,
just as a row or an ordered succession of minds is not a separate
substance. Or grant that phonetic elements alone are separate substances:
the groups of names and the rest would then simply be collections of
those elements, and positing further entities for the groups would
serve no purpose.

The Vaibhāṣikas, however, say that groups of names do exist as real
entities, as formations dissociated from Mind. Not all Dharmas are
accessible to reasoning.

It must now be stated to which realms the groups of names and the rest
belong; whether they are associated with sentient beings or not; whether
they arise from maturation, from increase, or as homogeneous outflows;
and whether they are wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate. The
answer is:

> They belong to the Desire and Form realms, are associated with
> sentient beings, are homogeneous outflows, and are indeterminate.

They belong to the Desire and Form realms. Others say that there are
also such entities belonging to the Formless realm, but that these
are inexpressible. They are associated with sentient beings: the one
who signifies possesses them, not the one who is signified. They are
homogeneous outflows and unobscured-indeterminate.

And just as these groups of names and the rest are associated with
sentient beings, homogeneous outflows, and unobscured-indeterminate,
likewise [commonality is classified in the following discussion].

## 3. The Argument's Distinct Questions

The commentary does not treat “language” as one undifferentiated object.
It distinguishes designation, sentence, and phonetic element; asks
whether the sound–meaning function requires an additional name-entity;
tests that proposal against temporal succession and reference; records
the Vaibhāṣika defense; and then classifies the formations.

The criticism of an additional entity proceeds through related but
distinct tests:

- **Convention:** speakers fix a sound's application; the word *go*
  can have multiple established meanings.
- **Production or revelation:** if mere sound is enough, any vocal
  noise would suffice; if only a meaningful specific sound suffices,
  the critic asks what an additional name contributes.
- **Succession:** the sounds of a name need not coexist in one moment.
  Making the final sound alone produce the whole name would imply that
  hearing that sound alone should convey the meaning.
- **Reference:** present names can refer to past and future things;
  names are freely assigned; unconditioned Dharmas do not arise with
  names.
- **Arrangement:** a verse is an arrangement of names, but arrangement
  is not thereby a separate substance. Even granting substantial
  phonetic elements does not establish additional substances for their
  collections.

These are arguments against the explanatory need for separate
linguistic entities. They do not deny the conventional functions of
designation, sentence, or sound. The closing Vaibhāṣika position is
not silently erased by the critique.

## 4. Classification and Possession

The verse's classification is unfolded on separate axes. The main
account places the linguistic groups in the Desire and Form realms;
“others” include a Formless-realm case but call it inexpressible.
The groups are associated with the sentient being who signifies, not
with the referent signified. The commentary calls them homogeneous
outflows and unobscured-indeterminate.

The relation of possessor to signified referent is not identity:
referring to someone does not make that person the possessor of the
speaker's linguistic formation. The classification of a linguistic
formation's ethical status likewise does not establish that every
intention or act of speaking is ethically indeterminate.

The final *tathā* carries selected classifications forward to
commonality. It does not transfer every predicate indiscriminately;
VAK 2.48 specifies which classifications carry over and which are
added.

## 5. Textual Decisions and Limits

- The source reads *ka kha ga gha ña* in the phonetic series. The
  expected member of the velar series would be *ṅa*; the translation
  retains the transmitted *ña* rather than silently correcting it.
- The exchange at 81.04–06 is textually damaged. The translation
  follows the argument about meaningful sound and the need, or lack
  of need, for a separate name-entity without claiming a critically
  established reading of every word.
- At 81.12–14 the example of the final manifested moment producing
  non-manifest communication is difficult. Its local force is kept
  as a challenge about dependence on preceding sound-moments; it is
  not expanded into a general theory of communication.
- The argument about delimiting phonetic elements at 81.16–18 is also
  insecure in the transmitted wording. Its conclusion is translated
  provisionally.
- The closing classifications preserve the Vaibhāṣika position and
  the “others” position separately. Neither is converted into a
  settled universal theory of language.

## Interpretation

This unit offers a sharp test for the Hub's relation to a determinate
Dharma. A linguistic formation can perform a conventional function
without that function proving an additional substance behind it. The
debate distinguishes the determinacy of a sign—its fixed application,
its arrangement, its possessor—from the claim that an independent
name-entity produces or reveals meaning. As an Organon reading, this
is an examination of what relations actually determine a Dharma, not
an equation of a Dharma with its name.

The Kośa-wide synthesis presents Vijñāna as Discriminative Cognition
joining and governing Perception and Conception; their unity is
Inconceivable as a homogeneous operation, with its Idea disclosed in
Cognition Base. Vijñāna governs Mind and guides the reading of Dharma
Base. *Avijñapti* is the same Dharma classified in Form Base and Dharma
Base, and Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it. This is the wider
systematic synthesis, not a claim that this linguistic debate states
every relation in the formula.

At the project level, the ten Samyama-bhūmis describe Path-related
operation of mental factors, and Buddha Dharma is the 11th Bhūmi.
That synthesis is not a literal translation or an enumeration made
in this passage.
