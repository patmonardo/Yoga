# VAK_2.56 Bhāṣya — Assigning Results to Causes

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vipākaphalam antyasya pūrvasyādhipataṃ phalam /
> sabhāgasarvatragayor niṣyandaḥ pauruṣaṃ dvayoḥ // 2.56 //

> The last has maturation-result; the first has predominant result.
> The homogeneous and pervasive causes have homogeneous outflow;
> the two have the result of efficacy.

The unit at 94.18–95.08 assigns results to the six causes and qualifies
how widely the result of efficacy applies. The definitions of the
individual results begin the next study.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Which of these results belongs to which cause? “The last has
maturation-result.” The maturation cause is “last” because it was
stated last. Its result is maturation-result. “The first has predominant
result.” The conditioning cause is “first” because it was stated first.
Its result is predominant result.

What predominance belongs to something that merely stands in a state
of non-obstruction? That very non-obstruction. Moreover, the conditioning
cause also has a contributory role: for example, the ten sense-bases
with respect to the five classes of consciousness, and karmic actions
with respect to the receptacle-world. The ear and the other faculties
also exercise predominance indirectly in the arising of eye-consciousness,
since hearing can give rise to the desire to see. The account should
be applied similarly to other cases.

“The homogeneous and pervasive causes have homogeneous outflow.”
Because these two have results similar to themselves, their result
is homogeneous outflow. “The two have the result of efficacy.” The
co-arisen and associated causes have the result of efficacy
(*puruṣakāraphala*). [The transmitted derivational sentence concerning
*pauruṣa* and *puruṣakāra* is damaged.] Its result is called *pauruṣa*.
What is this efficacy? Whatever activity a dharma performs. It is
called “a person's activity” by resemblance to a person's activity,
as a medicinal plant is called “crow's leg” or a person “a rutting
elephant.”

Do other causes also have a result of efficacy, or only these two?
The others have it too, except the maturation cause. For a result
of efficacy arises either together with its cause or immediately
afterward, and maturation does not arise in that way. Others say
that the maturation cause too has a distant result of efficacy,
like the crop of farmers.

## 4. The Assignment

| Cause | Result assigned here |
|---|---|
| Maturation | Maturation-result |
| Conditioning | Predominant result |
| Homogeneous | Homogeneous outflow |
| Pervasive | Homogeneous outflow |
| Co-arisen | Result of efficacy |
| Associated | Result of efficacy |

“First” and “last” refer to the order of enumeration in VAK 2.49,
not to temporal priority among causes. “The two” supplies the remaining
co-arisen and associated causes. The following question explicitly
prevents the table from becoming an exclusive one-result-per-cause
classification.

## 5. Non-obstruction and Positive Contribution

The response accepts non-obstruction itself as predominance, then
adds cases of positive contribution. The ten sense-bases are the
five sensory faculties and their corresponding objects in relation
to the five sensory consciousnesses. The example does not make all
ten equally direct supports of each consciousness.

The ear-to-eye example distinguishes mediated contribution from the
eye's own supporting role: hearing leads to wanting to see. The
karmic example concerns the receptacle-world. The detailed distinction
between that result and individual maturation is not yet given in
this unit and belongs to the following definitions.

## 6. Efficacy and the Figurative Expression

The Bhāṣya defines *puruṣakāra* through a dharma's *kāritra*, its
activity or function. The expression is therefore not restricted
to human agency. “Crow's leg” names a plant figuratively; “rutting
elephant” characterizes a person figuratively. These are two examples,
not three separate names, as the research translation suggests.

“Result of efficacy” keeps the functional determination visible.
The fact that associated and co-arisen causes receive that result
in the verse does not make their functions identical; their distinct
relations were explained in the preceding studies.

## 7. The Temporal Qualification and Alternative

The first reply allows results of efficacy for other causes but
excludes maturation on temporal grounds: the result is simultaneous
or immediately subsequent, whereas maturation is neither. “Others”
allow a distant result of efficacy as well, using the farmer's crop.
The second view broadens the temporal application of the result-term;
it does not claim that maturation suddenly becomes simultaneous.

The passage records the alternative without naming its school or
explicitly resolving the disagreement. Both accounts must remain
visible. The distinction is about the permitted scope of a result
classification, not whether farmers' activity has effects at all.

## 8. Textual and Translation Decisions

The Sanskrit ends at 95.07–08. The question at 95.09 introduces the
result definitions of VAK 2.57. The research 2.56 file continues into
those definitions and stops before their complete analysis; this
study follows the running-source boundary instead.

The transcription is preserved apart from source labels and surrounding
spacing. Forms such as *adhipajaṃ*, *niṣyandaḥ*, and *puruṣakāraphām*
remain visible. The sentence *puruṣabhāvavyatirekāt puruṣakāraḥ
puruṣaṃ eva* at 95.02 is syntactically and interpretively uncertain.
The translation marks it rather than silently replacing it with the
clear analogy at 95.04. The immediately following explanation of
activity and figurative naming is translated in full.

*Aṅgībhāva* is rendered “contributory role,” with its force determined
by the positive examples after non-obstruction. *Pāraṃparyeṇa* marks
the indirect succession in the hearing-to-seeing example. These
construals remain provisional pending independent collation.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, the mapping becomes explanatory when
we ask what relation a result has to its cause: maturation,
predominance, similarity, or efficacy. The same causal item may
admit more than one result-description, so learning the table
also requires learning the qualifications that follow it.

This continues our inquiry into mediation in a concrete way.
Hearing's contribution to seeing is intelligible through an
intervening desire; its causal relevance does not make hearing
identical with the eye's support. The exposition preserves the
terms while articulating the relation through which they matter
to one another.
