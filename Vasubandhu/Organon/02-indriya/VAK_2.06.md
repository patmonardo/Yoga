# VAK_2.06

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> प्रवृत्तेराश्रयोत्पत्तिस्थितिप्रत्युपभोगतः ।
>
> चतुर्दश तथान्यानि निवृत्तेरिन्द्रियाणि वा ॥ २.६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pravṛtter āśrayotpattisthitipratyupabhogataḥ /
>
> caturdaśa tathānyāni nivṛtter indriyāṇi vā // 2.6 //

## 3. Padaccheda

```text
pravṛtteḥ                       → pravṛtteḥ
āśrayotpattisthitipratyupabhogataḥ
                                → āśraya-utpatti-sthiti-pratyupabhogataḥ
tathānyāni                      → tathā anyāni
nivṛtteḥ                        → nivṛtteḥ
indriyāṇi vā                    → indriyāṇi vā
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| pravṛtteḥ | genitive feminine singular of `pravṛtti` | of engagement, ongoing activity, or the active course |
| āśraya | compound member | support or basis |
| utpatti | compound member | arising or origination |
| sthiti | compound member | persistence or continued standing |
| pratyupabhoga | compound member | experience or enjoyment |
| -taḥ | adverbial suffix | with respect to / on account of the enumerated functions |
| caturdaśa | nominative neuter plural numeral | fourteen; agreeing with understood `indriyāṇi` |
| tathā | indeclinable adverb | likewise, in the same manner |
| anyāni | nominative neuter plural | the others, the remaining eight faculties |
| nivṛtteḥ | genitive feminine singular of `nivṛtti` | of withdrawal or the cessation of the active course |
| indriyāṇi | nominative neuter plural | faculties |
| vā | alternative particle | or; according to the Bhāṣya, marks a difference of opinion |

The compound must be kept intact long enough for its syntax to become clear:

```text
pravṛtteḥ
    qualifies each member of
āśraya-utpatti-sthiti-pratyupabhoga
```

The same four members are then supplied after `tathā` for `nivṛtteḥ`.

## 4. Grammar

The first half gives the four respects in which fourteen determinations are
counted as faculties:

```text
pravṛtteḥ
    āśraya
    utpatti
    sthiti
    pratyupabhoga

āśraya-utpatti-sthiti-pratyupabhogataḥ
    → with respect to support, arising, persistence, and experience
```

`Caturdaśa` is nominative neuter plural with an understood `indriyāṇi`:

```text
caturdaśa [indriyāṇi]
    fourteen [are faculties]
```

The second half is elliptical:

```text
tathā anyāni nivṛtteḥ indriyāṇi
    likewise, the others are faculties of withdrawal
```

Here `tathā` carries the complete fourfold analysis forward:

```text
nivṛtteḥ
    āśraya
    utpatti
    sthiti
    pratyupabhoga
```

The final `vā` governs the proposal as an alternative. The Bhāṣya makes this
explicit with `matavikalpārtho vāśabdaḥ`: the word *or* indicates a difference
of opinion. The Kārikā is therefore reporting another justification for the
same twenty-two faculties.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Or, with respect to the support, arising, persistence, and experience of engagement, fourteen are faculties; likewise, the others are faculties of withdrawal.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Or, according to another account, fourteen faculties govern the support, arising, persistence, and experience of the active course of conditioned existence. In exactly the same four respects, the remaining eight govern withdrawal from that course.

The Bhāṣya distributes the fourteen as follows:

```text
support of pravṛtti
    → five sensory faculties + mind-faculty = 6

arising of pravṛtti
    → female-faculty + male-faculty = 2

persistence of pravṛtti
    → life-faculty = 1

experience of pravṛtti
    → five feeling-faculties = 5

total = 14
```

It distributes the remaining eight through the parallel structure:

```text
support of nivṛtti
    → confidence + energy + recollection + concentration + prajñā = 5

arising of nivṛtti
    → faculty of “I shall know what is not yet known” = 1

persistence of nivṛtti
    → knowledge-faculty = 1

experience of nivṛtti
    → faculty of one who has known = 1

total = 8
```

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The twenty-two faculties can be reconstructed as two complete courses of governed activity. Fourteen constitute the support, origination, continuation, and lived experience of `pravṛtti`; the remaining eight constitute the same four functions for `nivṛtti`. Withdrawal is therefore not bare inactivity or an instantaneous disappearance. It has conditions that support it, a faculty through which it arises, a faculty through which it persists, and a faculty through which its result is experienced.

Organon rendering:

> The Kośa presents a common operational form that can govern contrary courses. The conditioned continuum is supported, generated, sustained, and experienced; its withdrawal is likewise supported, generated, sustained, and experienced. The second course does not arrive from outside the faculty-system. It is an organized reconfiguration of governing powers already distinguished within that system.

This is a Bhāṣya-grounded reconstruction of the alternative account. It does
not yet claim that the Kośa derives both courses from one higher Principle.

`Pravṛtti` and `nivṛtti` should not be flattened into “world” and “salvation.”
The passage distinguishes two governed processes and gives each the same
fourfold functional articulation.

## 8. Logical Determination

The alternative account defines a common process-form:

```text
CourseForm(x)
    = Support(x)
    + Arising(x)
    + Persistence(x)
    + Experience(x)
```

The twenty-two faculties are divided exhaustively:

```text
PravrttiFaculties ∩ NivrttiFaculties = ∅

PravrttiFaculties ∪ NivrttiFaculties
    = TwentyTwoFaculties

Count(PravrttiFaculties) = 14
Count(NivrttiFaculties)  = 8
```

Their structures are parallel:

```text
CourseForm(Pravrtti)
    = <Support6, Arising2, Persistence1, Experience5>

CourseForm(Nivrtti)
    = <Support5, Arising1, Persistence1, Experience1>
```

The counts differ, but the functional positions correspond:

```text
For each role in {Support, Arising, Persistence, Experience}:
    HasGoverningFaculty(Pravrtti, role)
    ∧ HasGoverningFaculty(Nivrtti, role)
```

Therefore:

```text
Nivrtti
    ↛ MereAbsenceOf(Pravrtti)

Nivrtti
    → PositivelySupportedCourse
    ∧ HasArising
    ∧ HasPersistence
    ∧ HasExperience
```

This differs from VAK 2.05 without contradicting it:

```text
VAK 2.05
    → delimits faculty-status through six required system-functions

VAK 2.06
    → offers an alternative four-role ordering of the same faculties
```

The Bhāṣya's `matavikalpa` prevents the two arrangements from being silently
merged into one statement by Vasubandhu.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 2.06 does more than divide the faculties into worldly and liberating
groups. It discovers one functional form across two opposed directions. The
fourteen faculties do not merely cause isolated events; together they support,
originate, maintain, and provide the experience of `pravṛtti`. The final eight
do not merely negate that course; they give `nivṛtti` its own support,
origination, maintenance, and fruition.

The force of `tathā` is decisive. Withdrawal has the same ordered functional
completeness as engagement:

```text
support
    → arising
    → persistence
    → experience
```

This does not make the two courses identical. Their faculties and results are
different. The structural correspondence shows that cessation of the active
course is not achieved by removing every operation. A different organization
of powers must become effective and remain effective through completion.

The passage also refines the meaning of experience. In `pravṛtti`, the five
feelings supply `upabhoga`. In `nivṛtti`, experience belongs to the faculty of
one who has known. Feeling-mediated participation in the conditioned course
and the fruition of completed path-knowing occupy corresponding positions
without becoming the same kind of cognition.

For the Organon, this is the first explicit process-form of the Kośa Agent:

```text
an operative course requires
    support
    genesis
    persistence
    experienced completion
```

The Agent-reading remains a project reconstruction. Textually, the Kārikā
and Bhāṣya provide the two courses, the four functions, and their exhaustive
faculty-distribution. They do not use the language of software, task agents,
or a Logic–Model synthesis.
