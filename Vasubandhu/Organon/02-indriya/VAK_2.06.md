# VAK_2.06

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> प्रवृत्तेराश्रयोत्पत्तिस्थितिप्रत्युपभोगतः ।
>
> चतुर्दश तथान्यानि निवृत्तेरिन्द्रियाणि वा ॥ २.६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pravṛtter āśrayotpattisthitipratyupabhogataḥ /
>
> caturdaśa tathānyāni nivṛtter indriyāṇi vā // 2.6 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
pravṛtteḥ                       → pravṛtteḥ
āśrayotpattisthitipratyupabhogataḥ
                                → āśraya-utpatti-sthiti-pratyupabhogataḥ
tathānyāni                      → tathā anyāni
nivṛtteḥ                        → nivṛtteḥ
indriyāṇi vā                    → indriyāṇi vā
```

| Pada | Morphology | Force in this passage |
|---|---|---|
| pravṛtteḥ | genitive feminine singular of `pravṛtti` | of engagement, ongoing activity, or the active course |
| āśraya | compound member | support or basis |
| utpatti | compound member | arising or origination |
| sthiti | compound member | persistence or continued standing |
| pratyupabhoga | compound member | experience or enjoyment |
| -taḥ | adverbial suffix | with respect to / on account of the enumerated functions |
| caturdaśa | nominative neuter plural numeral | fourteen; agreeing with understood `indriyāṇi` |
| tathā | indeclinable adverb | likewise, in the same manner |
| anyāni | nominative neuter plural | the others, the remaining eight faculties |
| nivṛtteḥ | genitive feminine singular of `nivṛtti` | of withdrawal or the cessation of the active course |
| indriyāṇi | nominative neuter plural | faculties |
| vā | alternative particle | or; according to the Bhāṣya, marks a difference of opinion |

The compound applies the four coordinated functions to *pravṛtti*; *tathā*
carries the same fourfold analysis forward to *nivṛtti*.

## 4. Grammar

The first half gives the four respects in which fourteen determinations are
counted as faculties:

```text
pravṛtteḥ
    āśraya
    utpatti
    sthiti
    pratyupabhoga

āśraya-utpatti-sthiti-pratyupabhogataḥ
    → with respect to support, arising, persistence, and experience
```

`Caturdaśa` is nominative neuter plural with an understood `indriyāṇi`:

```text
caturdaśa [indriyāṇi]
    fourteen [are faculties]
```

The second half is elliptical:

```text
tathā anyāni nivṛtteḥ indriyāṇi
    likewise, the others are faculties of withdrawal
```

Here `tathā` carries the complete fourfold analysis forward:

```text
nivṛtteḥ
    āśraya
    utpatti
    sthiti
    pratyupabhoga
```

The final `vā` governs the proposal as an alternative. The Bhāṣya makes this
explicit with `matavikalpārtho vāśabdaḥ`: the word *or* indicates a difference
of opinion. The Kārikā is therefore reporting another justification for the
same twenty-two faculties.

## 5. Scientific English Rendering

### Kārikā

> Or, with respect to the support, arising, persistence, and experience of engagement, fourteen are Faculties; likewise, the others are Faculties of withdrawal.

### Bhāṣya-informed rendering

> Or, according to another account, fourteen Faculties govern the support, arising, persistence, and experience of the active course. In the same four respects, the remaining eight govern withdrawal.

The Bhāṣya distributes the fourteen as follows:

```text
support of pravṛtti
    → five sensory faculties + mind-faculty = 6

arising of pravṛtti
    → female-faculty + male-faculty = 2

persistence of pravṛtti
    → life-faculty = 1

experience of pravṛtti
    → five feeling-faculties = 5

total = 14
```

It distributes the remaining eight through the parallel structure:

```text
support of nivṛtti
    → confidence + energy + recollection + concentration + prajñā = 5

arising of nivṛtti
    → faculty of “I shall know what is not yet known” = 1

persistence of nivṛtti
    → knowledge-faculty = 1

experience of nivṛtti
    → faculty of one who has known = 1

total = 8
```

## 6. Interpretation

The Bhāṣya reports an alternative account: fourteen Faculties are assigned
to the support, arising, persistence, and experience of *pravṛtti*; the
remaining eight are assigned the same four roles in *nivṛtti*. The
distributions are parallel in function, but their members and the courses
they organize remain distinct. The Bhāṣya does not identify *nivṛtti* with
bare inactivity, nor does this account make nirvāṇa a conditioned product.

In the Kant-informed Techne, the four roles provide a determinate rule for
organizing this alternative Faculty-classification. The project crosswalk
with Dhātu 1.48 locates Faculty-status within the Base–Essence–Principle
relations; VAK 2.06 regroups those Faculties by the course and function
they serve. This is a project-level analysis, not terminology supplied by
the Bhāṣya.

Across the Kośa, Vijñāna is Discriminative Cognition joining Perception
and Conception without reducing their unity to one homogeneous operation;
it governs Mind and guides the reading of Dharma Base. The same
*avijñapti* remains one content in the Form Base and Dharma Base
classifications, and Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it. This wider
synthesis frames, but is not stated in full by, this verse.

| ājñendriya | knowledge-faculty | persistence of `nivṛtti` |
| ājñātāvīndriya | faculty of one who has known | experience of `nivṛtti` |

| indriya | faculty / governing efficacy | a determination governing one required function in either course |

`Pravṛtti` and `nivṛtti` should not be flattened into “world” and “salvation.”
The passage distinguishes two governed processes and gives each the same
fourfold functional articulation.

## 8. Logical Determination

The alternative account defines a common process-form:

```text
CourseForm(x)
    = Support(x)
    + Arising(x)
    + Persistence(x)
    + Experience(x)
```

The twenty-two faculties are divided exhaustively:

```text
PravrttiFaculties ∩ NivrttiFaculties = ∅

PravrttiFaculties ∪ NivrttiFaculties
    = TwentyTwoFaculties

Count(PravrttiFaculties) = 14
Count(NivrttiFaculties)  = 8
```

Their structures are parallel:

```text
CourseForm(Pravrtti)
    = <Support6, Arising2, Persistence1, Experience5>

CourseForm(Nivrtti)
    = <Support5, Arising1, Persistence1, Experience1>
```

The counts differ, but the functional positions correspond:

```text
For each role in {Support, Arising, Persistence, Experience}:
    HasGoverningFaculty(Pravrtti, role)
    ∧ HasGoverningFaculty(Nivrtti, role)
```

Therefore:

```text
Nivrtti
    ↛ MereAbsenceOf(Pravrtti)

Nivrtti
    → PositivelySupportedCourse
    ∧ HasArising
    ∧ HasPersistence
    ∧ HasExperience
```

This differs from VAK 2.05 without contradicting it:

```text
VAK 2.05
    → delimits faculty-status through six required system-functions

VAK 2.06
    → offers an alternative four-role ordering of the same faculties
```

The Bhāṣya's `matavikalpa` prevents the two arrangements from being silently
merged into one statement by Vasubandhu.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya marks this as an alternative account (*matavikalpa*), not a
replacement for VAK 2.05's six-function explanation. Its *tathā* carries
the four functions across to *nivṛtti*:

```text
support → arising → persistence → experience
```

The two distributions differ in their members and counts: fourteen
Faculties for *pravṛtti*, eight for *nivṛtti*. This correspondence does
not make the courses identical or turn their roles into stages measured
in time. The Bhāṣya's explanation of *vā* keeps the claim properly
qualified: this is one proposed rationale for the number and order of
the twenty-two Faculties.

**Reciprocal return to Dhātu.** Dhātu 1.48 maps Faculty-status within
the Base–Essence–Principle architecture; this account groups the same
Faculties by the functions assigned to two courses. The map and the
functional grouping answer different questions and should not be
collapsed. This classification does not imply that every effective
cause is a Faculty.

The distinction between the two experience-functions is also retained:
the five feelings mediate *pravṛtti*'s *upabhoga*, whereas *nivṛtti*'s
experience is assigned to the Faculty of one who has known. This
interpretation is limited to the Bhāṣya's stated allocation and does not
equate the two operations.
