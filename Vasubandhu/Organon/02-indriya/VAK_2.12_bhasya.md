# VAK_2.12 Bhāṣya — Realm-Connection

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāmāptam amalaṃ hitvā rūpāptaṃ strīpumindriye /
>
> duḥkhe ca hitvā ārūpyāptaṃ sukhe cāpohya rūpi ca // 2.12 //

> Except for the stainless triad, the Faculties are connected with the
> desire realm. Excluding the female and male Faculties and the two painful
> Faculties, the remainder are connected with the Form realm. Excluding in
> addition both pleasure and gladness and the Form Faculties, the remainder
> are connected with the Formless realm.

The unit runs from printed 46.17 through 47.03 in
[the Sanskrit witness](../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt). Abandonment at
47.04 opens 2.13.

Word division is editorial. Repairs: kāmachātau → kāmadhātau, akuśalā bhāvāccha → akuśalābhāvāc ca, avaśāiṣyate → avaśiṣyate, varttate → vartate. Anyaḥ puruṣabhāvo yaḥ kāmadhātau puruṣāṇāṃ bhavati is retained. No words are added to specify the other characteristic. No independent collation is claimed.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Which of these faculties is connected with which realm? Excluding the stainless triad, they are connected with the desire realm. More precisely, the faculties are connected with the desire realm apart from the triad beginning with the faculty “I shall know,” which is exclusively without *āsrava*. That triad is unconnected with the realms.

They are connected with the Form realm, excluding the female and male faculties and the two painful faculties. The exclusion of the stainless triad continues. The two painful faculties are pain and distress. The female and male faculties are absent in the Form realm because of dispassion toward sexual intercourse and because they would produce unattractiveness. How, then, are those beings called males? Where are they so called? In the sūtra: it is impossible for a woman to attain the status of Brahmā, but possible for a man. The male-being meant there differs from that of men in the desire realm. The Pain-Faculty is absent because the Basis is clear and the unwholesome is absent. The Distress-Faculty is absent because the continuum is softened by tranquillity and there is no basis for resentment.

They are connected with the formless realm, removing also the two pleasant faculties and what possesses form. The exclusion of female and male, the two painful faculties, and the stainless triad continues. What remains? Mind, life, neutral feeling, and the five beginning with faith. These, and no others, are found connected with the formless realm.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

A new axis, answered by cumulative exclusion. Within the Form Realm clause, a scriptural objection asks how male still applies. The reply distinguishes the senses of male-being.

How, then, and where, make the exchange visible. No objector is named. No school. The restriction and the aesthetic reason are claims of the account. The passage does not give a biological theory of sex.

## 5. Connection Is Not Occurrence

The triad is excluded because it is exclusively without outflows and apratisaṃyukta. That does not mean no being connected with a realm can develop the three. The predicate is the faculty's connection, not the place of the person who has it.

The nine that are twofold in outflow are included among connected types. That does not bind their without-outflow instances. Type, instance, and the bearer's circumstances stay apart.

Nineteen, fifteen, eight. The prose enumerates the eight. Possible connection, not a demand that every listed faculty occur together.

## 6. Form Realm Exclusions and the Male Name

Two reasons for female and male: dispassion toward intercourse, and unattractiveness in that setting. The second does not replace the first.

The sūtra calls a possible Brahmā male. The reply distinguishes Desire Realm puruṣabhāva. It does not retract the absence of the faculty, and it does not specify the other characteristic. Social, symbolic, or anatomical would exceed the sentence.

Pain and distress have separate reasons. Pain is excluded by clarity of the Basis and absence of the unwholesome; distress by a continuum softened by tranquillity and absence of a basis for resentment. The passage does not reduce these to one claim that a subtler realm is happier.

## 7. The Formless Remainder

Pleasure and gladness go, and what possesses Form. Earlier exclusions
stand. The eight are Mind, life, neutral feeling, faith, vigor,
mindfulness, concentration, and discriminative comprehension.

Female and male were already out of the Form Realm fifteen. Removing the remaining five of form, and the two pleasant faculties, leaves eight.

Not every dharma of that realm. Not a minimum theory of mind. Not liberation. The triad remains unconnected with all three.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Āpta is unfolded as pratisaṃyukta, connected. Belonging is not occurrence in a place.

Both duals are exclusions. The prose glosses duḥkhe. The remainder confirms sukhe is pleasure and gladness. Pleasure alone is not enough.

Amalaṃ ceti vartate keeps the earlier exclusion active. Rūpi collects the faculties that possess form. It is not the form realm being subtracted.

## 9. Interpretation

Realm-connection is another predicate. Faculty, realm-connection, *āsrava* status, ethical quality, and path-name stay distinct. The stainless triad is unconnected with all three realms; this does not mean a person connected with a realm cannot develop it.

The male name in the sūtra is not the male faculty. The unspecified characteristic is not invented.

The Formless remainder retains Mind as a Faculty; it is not an account
of liberation or of Cognition as a whole. The wider Organon account of
Cognition, Perception, and Conception remains to be tested elsewhere.
Here the text supplies a realm-connection filter, not another level
of the stainless Path.

## 10. Review Status

Reviewed with the kārikā against printed 46.17–47.03. The study
translation now explicitly excludes both pleasure and gladness;
the anchor matches exactly. Accusative dual exclusions and cumulative
scope are distinguished from realm predicates.

The objection concerning the male name and the separate reasons for
pain and distress remain intact. Grammar–Logic checks the 19–15–8
ranges and stainless exclusion without confusing possible connection
with the occurrence of a Faculty in a bearer.

The abandonment classification beginning 2.13 remains outside this review.
