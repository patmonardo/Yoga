# VAK_2.38_bhasya

## 1. Text and Scope

This study covers the Bhāṣya at 65.12–65.29. It announces an exception to
VAK 2.37's general temporal rule, differentiates weak and cultivated
indeterminate Dharmas, and carries the Form cases into the first half of
VAK 2.39.

## 3. Scientific English Rendering

> The rule that acquisition is threefold for Dharmas of the three times
> has an exception. Acquisition of unobscured-indeterminate Dharmas is
> co-arisen only, not prior or subsequent, because of their weakness.
> Thus their acquisition shares the time of the Dharma concerned.
>
> This does not apply to the two indeterminate superknowledges and
> magical-creation mind. Because these are strong and accomplished through
> special preparation, their acquisition can be prior, co-arisen, or
> subsequent. The text also reports this for certain craft-related and
> exceptionally practiced conduct-consciousness.
>
> Acquisition of obscured-indeterminate manifest Form is likewise
> co-arisen only. Desire-Principle manifest and non-manifest Form has no
> prior acquisition, while co-arisen and subsequent acquisition occur.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The commentary explicitly calls the first rule an exception. It asks whether
every unobscured-indeterminate Dharma falls under it, excludes cultivated
exceptions, then asks whether the restriction applies only there. It adds an
obscured-Form case and finally a distinct Desire-Principle Form rule.

“They accept” concerning certain craft and conduct cases has no specified
school attribution; no stronger attribution is supplied here.

## 5. Strength, Practice, and Temporal Acquisition

Weakness does not mean ethical deficiency. It explains why ordinary
unobscured-indeterminate acquisition is exhausted in its co-arising event.
The exceptions have special preparation and causal strength, allowing
availability before manifestation and retention after it.

The Form examples prevent the rule from becoming simple ethical inheritance.
Obscured-indeterminate manifest Form is co-arisen-only because it cannot
produce non-manifest Form even at high intensity. Desire-Principle Form
excludes prior acquisition but retains a separate subsequent mode.

## 6. Interpretation

The passage shows that *prāpti* is a rule of possible recurrence, not a
mere record of present occurrence. Special preparation can change a
Dharma's temporal availability, while other forms remain restricted to the
event in which they manifest. The relation is therefore determined by
capacity and transformed support, consistent with VAK 2.36's critical
account, without turning the passage into a modern skill theory.

Vijñāna is Discriminative Cognition joining and governing Perception and
Conception. Its governing relation preserves the distinction between a
Dharma's present manifestation and the continuum's prior or retained
capacity for it. This does not conflate Vijñāna, consciousness, and Mind.

## 7. Review Status

The kārikā and Bhāṣya have been compared with the source transcription at
65.12–65.29. The general-rule exception, the named cultivated exceptions,
the limited craft/conduct extension, the obscured manifest-Form case, and
the Desire-Principle Form rule are retained. The source witness is
unchanged.
