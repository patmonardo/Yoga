# VAK_2.38 Bhāṣya — Acquisition Status and Temporal Exceptions

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tridhā naśaikṣāśaikṣāṇām aheyānāṃ dvidhā matā /
>
> avyākṛtāptiḥ sahajābhijñānairmāṇikād ṛte // 2.38 //

> Acquisition of dharmas neither in training nor beyond training is
> threefold in status. Acquisition of dharmas not to be abandoned is held
> to be twofold. Acquisition of unobscured-indeterminate dharmas is
> co-arisen only, except for the two indeterminate superknowledges and
> magical-creation consciousness.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Acquisition of dharmas in training is itself in training; acquisition
of those beyond training is beyond training. For those neither in
training nor beyond training, there is a distinction. It is stated:
threefold for those neither in training nor beyond training.
Dharmas with *āsrava* and the unconditioned are called neither in
training nor beyond training. Their acquisition is threefold according
to the distinction of in training and so forth. Acquisition of
dharmas with *āsrava* is neither in training nor beyond training.
So too is acquisition of cessation not through Discrimination,
and of cessation through Discrimination acquired by an ordinary
person. Acquisition of that same cessation through a trainee path
is in training; through a path beyond training, it is beyond training.

Acquisition of dharmas abandonable through seeing and through
cultivation is respectively abandonable through seeing and through
cultivation. For those not to be abandoned, however, there is a
distinction. It is stated: acquisition of those not to be abandoned
is held to be twofold. Dharmas not to be abandoned are *anāsrava*.
Among these, acquisition of cessation not through Discrimination
is abandonable through cultivation, as is acquisition of cessation
through Discrimination acquired by an ordinary person. Acquisition
of that same cessation through a noble path is *anāsrava* and
not to be abandoned, as is acquisition of path-truth.

What was said—“threefold for dharmas of the three times”—is the
general rule; this is its exception: acquisition of the indeterminate
is co-arisen. Acquisition of unobscured-indeterminate dharmas is
co-arisen only, neither prior nor subsequent, because of their
weakness. Thus, for past dharmas of this kind, acquisition is past
only, and so on through present acquisition of present dharmas.
Does this apply to every unobscured-indeterminate dharma? Not every
one: except the superknowledges and magical-creation consciousness.
The two indeterminate superknowledges and creation-consciousness
are excluded. Because of their strength, resulting from distinctive
preparatory practice, their acquisition may be prior, subsequent,
or co-arisen. They also accept this for certain craft-based cases
and for greatly habituated deportment-based cases.

## 4. Three Statuses, With Different Cases

| Acquired dharma or acquisition context | Acquisition status |
|---|---|
| Dharmas with *āsrava* | Neither in training nor beyond training |
| Cessation not through Discrimination | Neither in training nor beyond training |
| Cessation through Discrimination, acquired by an ordinary person | Neither in training nor beyond training |
| The same cessation, acquired through a trainee path | In training |
| The same cessation, acquired through a path beyond training | Beyond training |

The threefold range is collective. It does not mean that every
dharma with *āsrava* can have trainee acquisition. The opening also
preserves the cases where status does correspond: trainee dharmas
and dharmas beyond training have acquisition of the respective status.
The unconditioned heading does not revoke 2.36's exclusion of space.

## 5. Two Abandonment Classes

| Non-abandonable dharma or acquisition context | Acquisition's abandonment class |
|---|---|
| Cessation not through Discrimination | Abandonable through cultivation |
| Cessation through Discrimination, acquired by an ordinary person | Abandonable through cultivation |
| The same cessation, acquired through a noble path | Not to be abandoned |
| Path-truth | Not to be abandoned |

Abandonable is a classification, not a claim that abandonment has
already occurred. The acquisition may be abandonable even where
the acquired dharma is not. Conversely, the preceding prose says
that acquisition of seeing-abandonable and cultivation-abandonable
dharmas respectively shares those classifications.

## 6. Exception and Further Attributed Extension

The ordinary unobscured-indeterminate case has acquisition only
simultaneously with the dharma. It therefore occupies the diagonal
of the general temporal matrix in 2.37. The two indeterminate
superknowledges and creation-consciousness allow acquisition before,
together with, or after the dharma. These are temporal relations,
not three simultaneous possession-events.

The final *icchanti* attributes a further acceptance for certain
craft-based and greatly habituated deportment-based cases. It does
not make every skill a superknowledge or every unobscured-indeterminate
dharma strong. Neither “co-arisen” nor the weakness explanation means
innate. The obscured Form continuation belongs to 2.39.

## 7. Reading Control

The threefold training-status count and twofold abandonment count
each cover several cases; neither says that every individual dharma
admits every acquisition class. The path by which cessation through
Discrimination is acquired changes the acquisition's class without
changing the cessation into a trainee dharma. The co-arisen rule
then restricts the earlier temporal classification; its explicit
exceptions are explained by strength derived from practice. These
are separate qualifications of acquisition, not definitions of
the listed dharmas' own natures.
