# VAK_2.13 Bhāṣya — What Is and Is Not to Be Abandoned

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> manovittitrayaṃ tredhā dviheyā durmanaskatā /
> nava bhāvanayā pañca tv aheyāny api na trayam // 2.13 //

> Mind and the three feelings are threefold; distress is to be
> abandoned in two ways. Nine are to be abandoned by cultivation;
> the five, however, may also be not to be abandoned. The triad
> is not to be abandoned.

The opening question supplies the three statuses: to be abandoned
by seeing, to be abandoned by cultivation, and not to be abandoned.
Mind together with three feelings counts as four faculties.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> katīndriyāṇi darśanaprahātavyāni kati bhāvanāprahātavyāni
> katy aprahātavyāni / manovittitrayaṃ tredhā /
> katamad vittitrayam / sukhasaumanasyopekṣāḥ /
> dviheyā durmanaskatā /
> daurmanasyaṃ dvābhyāṃ praheyaṃ darśanabhāvanābhyām /
> nava bhāvanayā / heyānīty adhikāraḥ /
> jīvitāṣṭamāni cakṣurādīni duḥkhendriyaṃ ca bhāvanāheyāny eva /
> pañca tv aheyāny api /
> śraddhādīni pañca bhāvanāheyāny apy aheyāny api /
> sāsravānāsravatvāt / na trayam // 2.13 //
> trayaṃ naiva praheyam ājñāsyāmīndriyādikam anāsravatvāt /
> na hi nirdoṣaṃ prahāṇārham / uktaḥ prakārabhedaḥ /

This is the complete unit at printed 47.04–15 in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. Word division is editorial;
the mechanical *dauarmanasyaṃ* is read as *daurmanasyaṃ*, following
the research Bhāṣya. The anchor's *bhāvanayā* follows the running
verse rather than the research kārikā's defective source-form
*bhavanayā*. No independent collation is claimed.

The explicit closure *uktaḥ prakārabhedaḥ* is included. The next
sentence, *lābha idānīṃ vaktavyaḥ*, introduces acquisition and is
reserved for VAK 2.14.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

How many faculties are to be abandoned by seeing, how many by
cultivation, and how many are not to be abandoned? “Mind and the
three feelings are threefold.” Which three feelings? Pleasure,
gladness, and neutral feeling. “Distress is to be abandoned in
two ways”: distress is to be abandoned by both seeing and cultivation.
“Nine by cultivation”: “to be abandoned” continues to apply.
Those beginning with the eye, with life as the eighth, together
with the pain-faculty, are to be abandoned only by cultivation.
“The five, however, may also be not to be abandoned”: the five
beginning with faith are both to be abandoned by cultivation and
not to be abandoned, because they can be with outflows or without
outflows. “The triad is not”: the three beginning with the faculty
“I shall know” are not to be abandoned at all, because they are
without outflows. For what is faultless is not fit to be abandoned.
The distinctions of kind have been explained.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The passage asks a classificatory question and distributes the
twenty-two faculties across its answers. It identifies the groups
left compressed in the verse, makes an implied predicate explicit,
and gives outflow-status as the reason for the last two assignments.
There is no opponent or named school in this unit.

The final sentence closes the distinctions of kind introduced in
VAK 2.09. Outflow-status, maturation, ethical quality, realm-affiliation,
and abandonment have been examined. Acquisition is the next question;
it is not another name for the abandonment-status just established.

## 5. The Five Groups of Faculties

| Faculties | Abandonment-status | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling | By seeing, by cultivation, or not to be abandoned | 4 |
| Distress | By seeing or by cultivation | 1 |
| Seven material faculties, life, pain | By cultivation only | 9 |
| Five beginning with faith | By cultivation or not to be abandoned | 5 |
| Three realization faculties | Not to be abandoned | 3 |

The counts sum to twenty-two. The threefold group includes three
feelings, whereas the ethically threefold group in VAK 2.11 included
four. Pain is now assigned separately to the cultivation-only group.
Repeating a previous grouping merely because both use “threefold”
would miss the change of question.

“Both seeing and cultivation” in the distress sentence distributes
possible instances between two classes. It does not require one
and the same instance to be abandoned twice. The same distinction
between type and instance governs the other mixed-status groups.

## 6. Cultivation-Abandonable Does Not Mean Unwholesome

The nine include material faculties and life, classified as ethically
indeterminate in VAK 2.11. The five beginning with faith are wholesome,
yet their with-outflow instances are also cultivation-abandonable.
The technical predicate therefore cannot simply mean “ethically bad.”

Likewise, distress was explicitly allowed to be wholesome or
unwholesome, but has no non-abandonable class here. Its affective
tone, ethical quality, and relation to abandonment are three
different determinations, even though they concern one faculty-type.

The paragraph does not explain the mechanism by which each of the
nine belongs to the cultivation-abandonable class. It should not
be expanded into an instruction to destroy bodily faculties or
terminate life, or into a claim that insight immediately removes
the body. Nor should ordinary momentary cessation be substituted
for the technical path-category of abandonment.

## 7. The Five and the Faultless Triad

The five beginning with faith receive their two statuses because
they may be *sāsrava* or *anāsrava*. The sentence assigns cultivation-
abandonability to the former and non-abandonability to the latter.
It does not declare every occurrence of faith, vigor, recollection,
concentration, or prajñā something to be discarded.

The realization triad differs: it is exclusively without outflows,
and therefore is not to be abandoned. The maxim *na hi nirdoṣaṃ
prahāṇārham* supplies the reason: what is faultless is not fit for
abandonment.

“Not to be abandoned” states this path-status. It does not assert
that each faculty-event is permanent, that it must operate without
interruption, or that its designation exempts it from further
analysis. Non-abandonability and temporal persistence are different
questions.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

*Prahātavya*, *praheya*, and *heya* are rendered “to be abandoned.”
The gerundive force matters: the classification is not a report
that abandonment has already occurred. *Aprahātavya* and *aheya*
are correspondingly “not to be abandoned.”

*Heyānīty adhikāraḥ* explicitly continues the governing expression
“to be abandoned” into the clause “nine by cultivation.” *Api*,
“also,” then adds non-abandonability for the five while retaining
their cultivation-abandonable class. The negation concerning the
triad is stronger: no abandonable class is admitted for it.

The verse's *durmanaskatā* is explained by *daurmanasya*, distress.
*Vittitraya* is expressly pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling;
*jīvitāṣṭama* is the seven material faculties with life as the eighth.
These identifications fix the counts without importing an additional
faculty or omitting pain from the nine.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage shows why practical classification must preserve the
qualified determination under discussion. A faculty-name alone
does not say whether seeing, cultivation, or non-abandonment applies.
For the five beginning with faith, the commentary expressly makes
outflow-status decisive.

For Organon reconstruction, this supports a disciplined distinction
between identifying a power and determining its path-relation.
One must not infer the rejection of a faculty-type from an abandonable
instance, or infer the permanence of an event from its faultlessness.
The classificatory relation has its own scope.

The closing maxim also gives a positive limit to abandonment:
faultlessness is a reason not to abandon. That is the local
principle established by the Bhāṣya. A broader account of preservation,
transformation, or the mechanisms of abandonment would require
further textual demonstration.
