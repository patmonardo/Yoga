# VAK_2.73

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> क्लिष्टे त्रैधातुके लाभः षण्णां षण्णां द्वयोः शुभे ।
> त्रयाणां रूपजे शैक्षे चतुर्णां तस्य शेषिते ॥ २.७३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kliṣṭe traidhātuke lābhaḥ ṣaṇṇāṃ ṣaṇṇāṃ dvayoḥ śubhe /
> trayāṇāṃ rūpaje śaikṣe caturṇāṃ tasya śeṣite // 2.73 //

The standalone kārikā and the running commentary agree on the verse.
Its syntax crosses the first line ending: *śubhe* belongs with
*trayāṇāṃ rūpaje*, not with the preceding *dvayoḥ*. The Bhāṣya opens
by explicitly returning to the twelve kinds of mind (109.04).

## 4. Grammar

The locatives supply the occasion of acquisition; the prose expands
them with *citte saṃmukhībhūte*, “when [that] mind has become manifest.”
The genitives specify the kinds acquired, with *cittānām* understood.
*Lābhaḥ* governs the entire sequence.

The three counts *ṣaṇṇāṃ ṣaṇṇāṃ dvayoḥ* distribute over desire, form,
and formless afflicted minds in that order. *Śubhe ... rūpaje* is one
locative phrase: “in wholesome form-realm [mind].” It takes the count
*trayāṇām*. *Śaikṣe* takes *caturṇām*.

The commentary explains *śeṣitam* as what has been left over. Where no
special acquisition has been specified, *tasya* means acquisition of
that mind itself, not an unspecified further list. *Rūpaje* qualifies
the mind's realm here; it is not a new class of matter-born cognition.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In afflicted [mind] belonging to the three realms, acquisition is
> of six, six, and two [kinds], respectively; in wholesome form-realm
> [mind], of three; in trainee [mind], of four; in the remaining
> [case], of that [mind itself].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Among the twelve kinds of mind, six kinds can be acquired when
> desire-realm afflicted mind is manifest, six when form-realm
> afflicted mind is manifest, and two when formless-realm afflicted
> mind is manifest. With wholesome form-realm mind, three kinds can
> be acquired; with trainee mind, four. In the remaining cases,
> acquisition concerns only the manifest kind itself.

These are context-dependent acquisition profiles. The commentary
specifies such occasions as return to a realm, decline from an
attainment, dispassion, and restoration of wholesome roots. The counts
do not assert that every manifestation newly acquires every listed kind.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> What is occurring and what one comes to possess are distinct
> determinations. A manifest mind can accompany the acquisition of
> another kind that is not then manifest. The account must therefore
> state both the acquisition's occasion and what is newly obtained.

This study interpretation follows the change of question from immediate
succession to acquisition. “Acquired” includes reacquisition following
loss; it need not mean obtained for the first time in a person's history.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Term | Controlled rendering | Local function |
|---|---|---|
| lābha | acquisition | obtaining a kind not previously possessed at the relevant occasion |
| saṃmukhībhāva | manifestation | actual occurrence, distinct from possession |
| samanvāgama | possession | status distinguished from the occurrence of a mind |
| kliṣṭa | afflicted | desire unwholesome or obscured-neutral; upper-realm obscured-neutral |
| dhātupratyāgamana | return to a realm | acquisition occasion in the prose |
| parihāṇi | decline or falling away | may occasion reacquisition of afflicted or trainee mind |
| kuśalamūlapratisaṃdhāna | restoration of wholesome roots | occurs through doubt or right view in the respective accounts |
| vairāgya | dispassion | acquisition occasion, especially for creation minds |
| śeṣita | remainder | cases without a separately specified cross-acquisition |

The acquisition relation belongs with the earlier discussion of *prāpti*,
but it is not an immediate-successor relation or an assertion that
several minds become simultaneously manifest.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The twelve-kind acquisition profiles.** “Neutral” in the following
lists means unobscured-neutral; obscured-neutral is named separately.

| Manifest kind | Kinds that can be acquired across the specified occasions | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Desire afflicted | desire wholesome, desire unwholesome, desire obscured-neutral, form afflicted, formless afflicted, trainee | 6 |
| Form afflicted | form wholesome, form afflicted, form neutral, desire neutral, formless afflicted, trainee | 6 |
| Formless afflicted | formless afflicted, trainee | 2 |
| Form wholesome | form wholesome, desire neutral, form neutral | 3 |
| Trainee | trainee, desire neutral, form neutral, formless wholesome | 4 |
| Other cases | the manifest kind itself | 1 |

**The occasions matter.** At 109.06–09, desire wholesome can be obtained
through restoration of wholesome roots by doubt or through return to
the realm. Desire unwholesome, desire obscured-neutral, and form afflicted
are listed with return or decline; formless afflicted and trainee with
decline. These alternatives explain why the six should not be represented
as a mandatory simultaneous bundle.

At 109.10–13, return explains acquisition of the three form kinds and
desire neutral in the form-afflicted profile; decline explains formless
afflicted and trainee. The formless-afflicted profile acquires its own
kind and trainee through decline. At 109.19–20, the trainee profile is
explained through the noble path's dispassion from desire and form.

**A competing summary.** Other teachers compress the account to nine
kinds acquired with afflicted mind, six with wholesome mind, and only
itself with neutral mind. The commentator corrects the wholesome count
to **seven**: desire wholesome; desire and form neutral; form and formless
wholesome; trainee and beyond-training. These are aggregate profiles
across occasions. This correction does not replace the verse's particular
three-kind form-wholesome or four-kind trainee profiles.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Corrections to the first pass.** The earlier kārikā translation attached
“of two” to wholesome mind and left the final rule undetermined. The
running prose resolves both: two belongs to formless afflicted mind;
three belongs to wholesome form-realm mind; the remainder acquires its
own kind. The objects counted are kinds of mind, not unspecified
“associated or relevant factors.”

The research Bhāṣya is more informative, but its account of desire neutral
under manifest form-afflicted mind needs care: *dhātupratyāgamana* does
not require return to the desire realm in that case. Desire-realm
creation mind can be acquired in connection with the form realm. The
realm classification of an acquired mind and the realm of rebirth must
not be identified automatically.

The transcription at 109.06–07 has *tairsamanvāgatasya*, an awkward
possession phrase in a discussion of new acquisition. It is retained
as a source issue rather than silently repaired here. The closing
summary explicitly states *atadvataḥ*, “for one not possessing that,”
which supports distinguishing new acquisition from continued possession.
A continuous Bhāṣya pass should register the problematic local wording.

**Organon interpretation.** Learning to distinguish a present act from
an acquired capacity prevents a classificatory shortcut. Neither the
name of the manifest kind nor its permissible successor list tells us
what has just been obtained. The chapter adds the circumstances that
make that further determination possible. This is a project interpretation,
not a claim that the source describes a modern computational system.

**Chapter close and review status.** The source closes the discussion
occasioned by conditions at 110.05 and Indriyanirdeśa at 110.06–08.
This completes the kārikā upgrades through 2.73. The study is provisional,
checked against the research witnesses, standalone verse, and running
source at 109.04–110.08. Continuous Bhāṣya work resumes separately at
2.61; the original witnesses remain unchanged.
