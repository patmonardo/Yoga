## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Among afflicted consciousnesses of the three realms, acquisition is of
> six, six, and two; among wholesome, of three; among trainee, of four;
> for the rest, of that consciousness itself.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> For afflicted consciousnesses across the three realms, acquisition is of
> six, six, and two; for wholesome consciousness, of three; for trainee,
> of four; for the remainder, of that consciousness itself.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The verse returns to the twelve kinds and states the acquisition (not succession) constraints. The Bhāṣya assigns the six/six/two to the three afflicted kinds, the three to Form wholesome, the four to trainee, and notes that for every remaining kind, acquisition is of that consciousness itself.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya assigns the first six acquisitions to desire-afflicted consciousness: desire-wholesome through restoration of wholesome roots by doubt and return; desire-unwholesome and desire-obscured, and Form-afflicted, through return or decline; and formless-afflicted and trainee through decline. Form-afflicted consciousness acquires six: its three Form kinds and desire-unobscured through return, plus formless-afflicted and trainee through decline. Formless-afflicted acquires two, itself and trainee, through decline. Form-wholesome acquires three: itself and desire- and form-unobscured. Trainee acquires four: itself, desire- and form-unobscured, and formless wholesome upon dispassion from desire and Form through the Path. For every remaining kind, acquisition is of that consciousness itself and no other. The prose also gives broader totals and summarizes acquisition at rebirth, attainment, dispassion, decline, and restoration of wholesome roots.

The verse states the acquisition constraints. The chapter closes with the summary verse on acquisition occasions.
