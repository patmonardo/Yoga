# VAK_2.40 Bhāṣya — Ordinary Status

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāmādyāptāmalānāṃ ca mārgasyāprāptir iṣyate /
>
> pṛthagjanatvaṃ tatprāptibhūsaṃcārād vihīyate // 2.40 //

> Non-acquisition of dharmas included in the Desire, Form, and Formless
> Realms, and of uncontaminated dharmas, may belong to any of the three
> Realms. Non-acquisition of the path is held to be ordinary-person
> status. Non-acquisition is relinquished through acquisition of the
> dharma concerned or through transition of level.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

For dharmas included in Desire and the other Realms, and for
uncontaminated dharmas also, “threefold” continues. Non-acquisition
of Desire-Realm dharmas belongs to the Desire, Form, or Formless
Realm; likewise for Form-Realm and Formless-Realm dharmas and
uncontaminated dharmas. There is no uncontaminated non-acquisition.
For non-acquisition of the path is held to be ordinary-person status.
The treatise reads: “What is ordinary-person status? Non-obtaining
of noble dharmas.” Non-obtaining means non-acquisition, and
ordinary-person status cannot be uncontaminated.

Non-obtaining of which noble dharmas? Of all, because the statement
does not distinguish. But it is non-obtaining without obtaining.
Otherwise even the Buddha would be non-noble because he does not
possess the dharmas belonging to disciple and solitary-buddha lineages.
Then the word “only” should be read in the text. It need not be:
even single expressions can delimit, as in “water-eater” and
“air-eater.”

Others say it is non-obtaining of receptivity to Dharma Knowledge
concerning suffering and its co-arising companions. This does not
entail non-nobility through that absence, since that non-obtaining
has been utterly destroyed. But those belong to three lineages:
non-obtaining of which is meant? Of all. Then the same fault
returns. The same answer returns as well. Then the effort is
pointless. The Sautrāntika account is preferable in this respect.
What is their account? “Ordinary-person status is a continuum
in which noble dharmas have not arisen.”

How is this non-acquisition relinquished? Whatever dharma a
non-acquisition concerns [contextual reading of the damaged
introductory clause], it is relinquished through acquisition of
that dharma or transition of level. For example, insofar as
non-obtaining of the noble path is ordinary-person status, it is
relinquished through obtaining that path and through transition
of level. Apply this likewise to the other cases. “Relinquished”
means that non-acquisition of that non-acquisition arises and
its acquisition is severed.

Do acquisition and non-acquisition themselves also have acquisition
and non-acquisition? They say that both have both. Does this not
entail an infinite regress of acquisitions? There is no regress,
because of mutual possession. A dharma arises as one of three,
counting itself: that dharma, its acquisition, and acquisition of
that acquisition. Through arising of acquisition, one possesses
the dharma and the acquisition-of-acquisition. Through arising
of acquisition-of-acquisition, one possesses the acquisition itself.
Thus there is no regress.

On this arrangement, for a wholesome or afflicted dharma with its
two companions [the class-list here contains a duplicated,
damaged reading], three acquisitions arise at the second moment,
and three further acquisitions of those acquisitions, making six.
At the third moment, nine acquisitions of the entities arisen
at the first and second moments, together with their further
acquisitions, make eighteen. By such successive increase,
acquisitions proliferate. For all past and future moments of
afflictions and subsidiary afflictions, and birth-acquired wholesome
moments with their associated and co-arising companions throughout
beginningless and endless saṃsāra, infinitely many arise for one
living being at every moment. Thus each moment of embodied
existence in a being's continuum comprises infinitely many entities.
What a festival of acquisitions! Fortunately they are non-resistant
and therefore find room in space. Otherwise there would not be
room even in space for a second living being.

## 4. Realm Range and Qualified Absence

Each of the three Realm-included dharma classes and the uncontaminated
class admits non-acquisition belonging to any of the three Realms.
These are twelve class-level combinations. Unlike acquisition of
Realm-included dharmas in 2.37, non-acquisition is not confined to
the acquired dharma's Realm. Uncontaminated is not a fourth Realm
or a possible classification of non-acquisition.

The Buddha counterexample distinguishes having attained none from
merely lacking some noble dharma. The narrower receptivity account
is reported as another position and tested against the same
three-lineage problem. It is not silently combined with the first
definition. The Sautrāntikas are explicitly named here; their
continuum account is not a generic, unattributed critical gloss.

## 5. Relinquishment Is General

The last clause concerns non-acquisition of any relevant dharma.
Ordinary-person status supplies the example, not the whole scope.
Transition of level relinquishes a level-specific non-acquisition;
it does not confer noble status or substitute for attaining the
path. Non-acquisition of the path in another level is not thereby
ruled out. The explanation in terms of non-acquisition of
non-acquisition and severance of acquisition belongs to the
distinct-real account, not a reconciliation with the Sautrāntikas.

## 6. Mutual Possession and Proliferation

| Arising member | What its acquisition secures |
|---|---|
| Primary acquisition | The base dharma and acquisition-of-acquisition |
| Acquisition-of-acquisition | Primary acquisition |

The mutual relation closes the proposed linear regress at that
moment. The critic then changes the question: later moments yield
six and eighteen new acquisitions, and the full temporal inventory
produces an infinite proliferation. The initial group has three
members including the base dharma; it is not three acquisitions.
The later six and eighteen are newly arising acquisition entities,
not total numbers of all entities ever counted. The closing irony
is preserved as criticism, not treated as a settled ontology.

## 7. Interpretation

In the Organon reading, Cognition joins and governs Perception and
Conception and guides the Dharma Base, remaining distinct from
consciousness and Mind. Ordinary status is a determinate relation
to attainment, not a person's immutable essence. The negative
side of the dyad must distinguish absence of every attainment from
absence of some attainment. A relational closure can answer regress
while leaving proliferation unanswered: those are distinct tests
of the acquisition account, not interchangeable objections.
