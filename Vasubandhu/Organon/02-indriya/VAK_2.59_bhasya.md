# VAK_2.59 Bhāṣya — Taking and Giving

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vartamānāḥ phalaṃ pañca gṛhṇanti dvau prayacchataḥ /
>
> vartamānātītau dvau eko 'tītaḥ prayacchati // 2.59 //

> Five causes take their fruit while present. Two give fruit while
> present, two while present or past, and one gives it only when past.

## 3. Conventional Argument Summary

Five causes take their fruit while present. They do not take it when past, since it has already been taken, or when future, since they then lack operative activity. The conditioning cause is treated similarly but is omitted because it need not have a fruit. The co-arisen and associated causes give fruit while present; for these, giving and taking occur at the same time. The homogeneous and pervasive causes give fruit while present or past. The Bhāṣya then examines four possibilities for a homogeneous cause: taking without giving, giving without taking, both, and neither. To take a fruit is to assume seed-status toward it. The maturation cause gives fruit only when past, since maturation is neither simultaneous with nor immediately subsequent to its cause.

## 4. Interpretation

Five take while present. Two give present. Two give present or past. Maturation gives past only. Taking is seed-status, not a store. Taking does not imply giving.

## 5. Review Status

The selected Sanskrit passages now support the named cause groups and the seed-status explanation. “Not a store” is an Organon caution against reifying that explanation, not a further claim of this Bhāṣya passage.
