# VAK_2.59 Bhāṣya — Taking and Giving

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vartamānāḥ phalaṃ pañca gṛhṇanti dvau prayacchataḥ /
>
> vartamānātītau dvau eko 'tītaḥ prayacchati // 2.59 //

> Five causes take their fruit while present. Two give fruit while
> present, two while present or past, and one gives it only when past.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

At what times do these causes take or give a fruit? Five take their
fruit while present. They do not take it while past, for it has
already been taken, or while future, for they then lack operative
activity. The conditioning cause works similarly, but is not named
among the five because it does not necessarily have a fruit.

Two causes give a fruit while present: the co-arisen and associated
causes. For them taking and giving occur at the same time. Two
others, homogeneous and pervasive cause, give their fruit while
present or past. Giving while past is intelligible; how can they
give a homogeneous-outflow fruit while present? Because the fruit
arises immediately afterward. By the time the fruit has arisen,
those causes too are past. A fruit already given is not given again.

For a wholesome homogeneous cause, can it take a fruit without
giving it? Four cases are distinguished. First, when wholesome
roots are being cut off, the acquisitions relinquished last take
but do not give. Second, when those roots are being reconnected,
the acquisitions first obtained give but do not take [the source
immediately qualifies how this should be stated]. The same
acquisitions during reconnection can form the third case, both
taking and giving; otherwise, one who has not cut off wholesome
roots in the remaining states supplies it. The fourth case is
neither, apart from those configurations.

For an unwholesome homogeneous cause, the parallel first case
concerns acquisitions relinquished last while gaining freedom from
Desire; the second concerns those first obtained while falling
from that freedom. The text again qualifies the second case by
referring to the same acquisitions during the fall. One who has
not become free from Desire in the other states supplies the
third; the fourth excludes these configurations. The same scheme
is to be applied, as appropriate, to obscured-indeterminate
dharmas in relation to attainment of and fall from arhatship.
The unobscured-indeterminate case is mentioned as subsequently
arising [the source phrase is terse].

What gives a fruit also takes it, but something may take without
giving: an arhat's final Bases are an example. If the analysis is
restricted to dharmas with a Support and considered moment by
moment, a further fourfold division is given for a wholesome
homogeneous cause. First, a defiled or indeterminate mind follows
a wholesome mind: taking without giving. Second, the reverse:
giving without taking. Third, a wholesome mind follows a wholesome
mind: both. Fourth, all cases outside those three: neither. Apply
this also to unwholesome and other kinds. How is a fruit “taken”?
By assuming the status of its seed.

One cause gives its fruit only when past: the maturation cause.
Its maturation is neither simultaneous with nor immediately after
it. Others also speak of four kinds of fruit. A supporting fruit
is like the water disc supporting the wind disc, down to grasses
supported by the earth. A fruit of application is illustrated by
the sequence from contemplation of the unattractive through
Knowledge of non-arising. A fruit of a conjunction of Conditions
is like eye Cognition arising from the eye and other factors. A
fruit of cultivation is like creation arising from a Form Realm
mind. These four, the text says, are included under activity-fruit
and predominance-fruit. The causes and fruits have been explained.

## 4. Reading Control

The temporal rules distinguish taking a fruit from giving one.
The fourfold analyses show that these operations need not coincide
for homogeneous cause. “Taking” is explained by acquiring
seed-status; the passage does not specify a store for that seed.
The additional four fruits are attributed to others and then
subsumed under activity and predominance in the closing reply.
