# VAK_2.14 Bhāṣya — First Acquired as Maturation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāmeṣv ādau vipāko dve labhyate nopapādukaiḥ /
> taiḥ ṣaḍ vā sapta vā aṣṭau vā ṣaḍ rūpeṣu ekam uttare // 2.14 //

> In the desire realm, two are first acquired as maturation, but not by the
> spontaneously born. By them, six, seven, or eight; in the Form realm, six;
> in the higher, Formless realm, one.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Acquisition must now be explained. How many faculties are first acquired as maturation, and in which realm? In the desire realm, two are initially acquired as maturation: the body-faculty and the life-faculty. This two-faculty case excludes spontaneous birth; it concerns the egg-born, womb-born, and moisture-born. Why are the Mind-Faculty and Neutral-Feeling Faculty not counted? Because both are necessarily afflicted at the time of relinking.

How many are acquired by the spontaneously born? By them, six, if they have no sexual characteristic, like the beings at the beginning of the cosmic age. Which six? Eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and life. Or seven, if they have one sexual characteristic, as among gods and others. Or eight, if they have both. Are there spontaneously born beings with both sexual characteristics? There are, in the unfortunate destinies.

So much for the desire realm. What about the Form and Formless realms? Six are acquired in the Form realm. The desire realm is called “desires” because desire predominates there; the Form realm is called “forms” because form predominates there. As a sūtra says: “Those peaceful liberations, monks, which, having transcended forms, are formless.” In the Form realm, six faculties are first acquired as maturation: the same six acquired in the desire realm by spontaneously born beings without sexual characteristics.

One in the higher. The formless realm is higher than the form realm, because it is beyond it with respect to meditative attainment and superior with respect to rebirth. There only one faculty, the life-faculty, is first acquired as maturation, and no other. Acquisition has been explained.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The question specifies acquisition, the initial time, maturation, and realm. Desire Realm birth is split, then the spontaneously born by sexual characteristic. Form and formless complete the distribution.

Two interruptions: why mind and neutral feeling are out of the first count, and whether spontaneous birth can have both marks. Both are answered. No opponent. No school. The sūtra supports the realm-language. It is not a proof of the counts.

## 5. Presence and Acquisition

Afflicted at relinking is why mind and neutral feeling are not maturation. Occurrence is not denied. That continues 2.10: identity is not the causal status of the instance.

Two does not mean no mind and no feeling. One in the formless realm is the maturation-count. It is not formless existence as life without mental activity. The eight of 2.12 were another question.

## 6. The Desire Realm Counts

| Condition | First acquired as maturation | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Egg-born, womb-born, moisture-born | body and life | 2 |
| Spontaneous, no characteristic | five and life | 6 |
| Spontaneous, one | the six plus one | 7 |
| Spontaneous, both | the six plus both | 8 |

The six include life, not Mind. The seventh and eighth are the female
and male Faculties as applicable, not the sexual characteristics counted
as separate entities. The birth-modes and the unfortunate destinies
stay the account. No embryonic theory is added.

## 7. The Two Higher Realms

Form receives the same six as the spontaneous Desire Realm case without marks. The shared list is the maturation-count. It is not identical sensory activity.

Formless receives life alone under this predicate. Higher is attainment and rebirth. Those are not the same event. Neither higher nor the smaller count is liberation. The triad stays unconnected.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Lābha is acquisition. Prathamataḥ explains ādau. Vipāka is carried through every number. Dropping it would turn the inventory into every faculty present.

Vyañjana is sexual characteristic: none, one, both. Not arbitrary marks. Upapāduka is spontaneously born. The negative assigns the two to the other three modes.

Desires and forms are realm-names by predominance. Uttara is higher, not a direction.

The running witness reads taiḥ, agreeing with Devanāgarī तैः; the
kārikā's earlier IAST teḥ is corrected. Labhyate remains singular with
dual dve; no grammatical regularization is silently imposed.

## 9. Interpretation

What can belong to a realm does not settle what is first acquired there as maturation. Presence, acquisition, time, and mode of arising stay apart. Mind and neutral feeling are the case: present at relinking, not in the count.

The Organon determination is a scoped inventory: realm, birth-mode,
time, and maturation qualify what is counted. Reducing the count to
bare presence would falsely exclude Mind and neutral feeling from
relinking or include them as maturation.

Mind's exclusion from the first two-faculty count does not mean Mind
is absent at relinking; the prose explicitly gives its afflicted
condition as the reason for exclusion. Likewise, the one-faculty
Formless count concerns initial acquisition as maturation, not the
whole inventory of faculties connected with that realm in 2.12.

## 10. Review Status

Reviewed with the kārikā against printed 47.16–48.14. Taiḥ is corrected
in the IAST witness and anchor. The study translation identifies the
higher realm as Formless and matches the anchor exactly.

Grammar–Logic preserves initial acquisition as maturation across all
counts, the resumed spontaneous-birth agent, and the prose's conditional
six/seven/eight sets. Sexual characteristics determine the additional
Faculties; they are not substituted for them. Presence at relinking
remains distinct from the count. The source's birth-mode claims are
preserved without independent biological validation.

The relinquishment question beginning 2.15 remains outside this review.
