# VAK_2.14 Bhāṣya — Initial Acquisition as Maturation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāmeṣv ādau vipāko dve labhyate nopapādukaiḥ /
> taiḥ ṣaḍ vā sapta vā aṣṭau vā ṣaḍ rūpeṣv ekam uttare // 2.14 //

> Initially in the desire realm, two faculties are acquired as
> maturation by those not spontaneously born. By the spontaneously
> born, six, seven, or eight are acquired; six in the form realm,
> and one in the higher realm.

The anchor retains the running witness's singular *labhyate* with
the dual *dve*, rather than silently adopting the research Bhāṣya's
*labhyete*. It reads *taiḥ*, “by them,” from the running commentary
in place of the research kārikā's defective *teḥ*. The question
concerns initial acquisition specifically as maturation, not every
faculty present at birth.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Acquisition must now be explained. How many faculties are first
acquired as maturation, and in which realm? “Initially in the
desire realm, two are acquired as maturation”: the body-faculty
and the life-faculty. These, however, are acquired “not by the
spontaneously born.” Since the spontaneously born are excluded,
the statement is to be understood of the egg-born, womb-born,
and moisture-born. Why not the faculties of mind and neutral
feeling? Because at the time of relinking both are necessarily
afflicted.

How many are acquired by the spontaneously born? “By them, six,”
if they have no sexual characteristic, like the beings at the
beginning of the cosmic age. Which six? Eye, ear, nose, tongue,
body, and life. “Or seven,” if they have one sexual characteristic,
as among gods and others. “Or eight,” if they have both sexual
characteristics. Are there spontaneously born beings with both
sexual characteristics? There are, in the unfortunate destinies.

So much for the desire realm. How is it in the form and formless
realms? The answer is: “Six in the forms.” The desire realm is
designated “desires” because desire predominates there; the form
realm is designated “forms” because form predominates there. It
is also said in a sūtra: “Those peaceful liberations, monks, which,
having transcended forms, are formless.” In the form realm, six
faculties are first acquired as maturation: the same ones acquired
in the desire realm by spontaneously born beings without sexual
characteristics.

“One in the higher.” The formless realm is higher than the form
realm because it is beyond it with respect to meditative attainment
and superior with respect to rebirth. There only one faculty,
the life-faculty, is first acquired as maturation, and no other.
Acquisition has been explained.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The new question specifies acquisition, its initial time, maturation-
status, and realm. The desire-realm answer first separates birth
modes and then distinguishes the spontaneously born by sexual
characteristics. The form and formless realms complete the distribution.

Two questions interrupt the enumeration: why mind and neutral
feeling are absent from the first count, and whether spontaneous
birth can include both sexual characteristics. The prose answers
both directly. No named opponent or school is introduced. The
scriptural quotation supports the realm-language rather than
supplying a separate proof of the faculty counts.

## 5. Presence and Acquisition as Maturation

The question about mind and neutral feeling makes the scope exact.
Their being afflicted at relinking explains why they are not
counted as maturation. Their occurrence is not denied. This
continues VAK 2.10's distinction between faculty identity and the
causal status of a particular occurrence.

Consequently, the answer “two” does not mean that a non-spontaneously
born being initially has no mind or feeling. Likewise, “one” in
the formless realm counts only the initially acquired maturation-
faculty; it does not reduce formless existence to life without
mental activity. The eight formless-affiliated faculty-types in
VAK 2.12 answered a different question.

## 6. The Desire-Realm Counts

| Mode and stated condition | Faculties initially acquired as maturation | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Egg-born, womb-born, or moisture-born | Body and life | 2 |
| Spontaneously born, without sexual characteristic | Five sensory faculties and life | 6 |
| Spontaneously born, with one sexual characteristic | The six plus one sexual faculty | 7 |
| Spontaneously born, with both sexual characteristics | The six plus both sexual faculties | 8 |

The basic six include life, not mind. Confusing them with the
familiar six sensory-and-mental faculties would alter the count's
meaning. The seventh and eighth members are supplied through the
specified sexual configuration.

Egg-, womb-, moisture-, and spontaneous birth are the transmitted
cosmological categories. The example of primordial beings and the
claim about unfortunate destinies remain part of that account.
The paragraph does not provide an account of subsequent embryonic
development or a contemporary biological theory.

## 7. The Two Higher-Realm Answers

The form realm receives the same six as the specified spontaneous
desire-realm case: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and life. The
comparison concerns which faculties are first acquired as maturation.
It does not establish identical sensory activity across the two
realms merely from the shared list.

The formless realm receives life alone under this predicate.
Its description as “higher” is explained in two respects, meditative
attainment and rebirth. These relations should remain distinct:
attaining a meditative state is not simply the same event as being
reborn in its corresponding realm.

Neither “higher” nor the smaller maturation count identifies
formless existence with liberation. The subject remains conditioned
realm and rebirth classification, while the stainless realization
triad was already distinguished from realm-affiliation.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

*Lābha* and *labhyante* are acquisition and being acquired.
*Prathamataḥ* explains *ādau*, initially. The qualification *vipāka*
is carried through every numerical answer, including the final one.
Omitting it would turn a restricted causal inventory into a claim
about every faculty present.

*Vyañjana* is rendered “sexual characteristic” in this context.
The three descriptions distinguish none, one, and both; they do
not count arbitrary bodily marks. *Upapāduka* is “spontaneously
born,” with the negative clause assigning the two-faculty case
to the other three birth modes.

The commentary explains the plurals “desires” and “forms” as realm-
designations through predominance. The conventional translation
retains that explanation in its transmitted position before the
form-realm enumeration. *Uttara*, higher, then receives its own
explicit explanation rather than being translated as a spatial
direction.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The change from classification to acquisition adds a temporal
question to the account of faculties. What can belong to a realm
does not settle what is first acquired there as maturation. The
commentary relates the initial count to realm, birth mode, and
the specified bodily configuration while keeping causal status
in view throughout.

For Organon reconstruction, the useful distinction is among
presence, acquisition, time, and mode of arising. Mind and neutral
feeling make that distinction concrete: their presence at relinking
does not put them into the maturation count. A faithful model must
retain the predicate under which an inventory has been formed.

The counts are therefore determinate answers within the Kośa's
account of rebirth. Their systematic precision does not require
turning that cosmology into modern developmental science or
importing a further theory of the origin of consciousness.
