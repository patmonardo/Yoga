## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Same-class is sameness among beings. The non-reflective, among the non-
> reflective, is cessation of consciousness and the mental factors, a
> maturation; those born there are the Great Fruit.
### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Same-class is sameness among beings; the non-reflective attainment is
> cessation of consciousness and the mental factors, a maturation. Those
> born among the non-reflective are the Great Fruit.
## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Same-class is the dispute about a shared designation. The universal is the comparison. The non-reflective is the interval, not the nirodha of virodha.


## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Sabhāgatā.** Same-class, explained as sameness among beings; the status of an independent entity remains disputed.

**Āsaṃjñika.** The non-reflective attainment, identified here with cessation of consciousness and the mental factors.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The commentary distinguishes undifferentiated sameness among beings from classifications by realm, ground, destiny, birth, species, sex, discipline, and path-status. It debates whether same-class is an entity, compares the Vaiśeṣika universal, and notes that the sūtra does not assert an independent entity. The non-reflective cessation is maturation of the attainment; inhabitants are reflective at birth and death, and renewed reflection ends their stay.
