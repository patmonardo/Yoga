# VAK_2.68 Bhāṣya — Neutral Desire-Realm and Wholesome Form-Realm Mind

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> pañcabhyo 'nivṛtaṃ tasmāt sapta cittāny anantaram /
> rūpe daśaikaṃ ca śubhān navabhyas tad anantaram // 2.68 //

> The unobscured [mind arises] from five; immediately after it,
> seven minds. In the form realm, eleven [arise] from wholesome
> [mind]; that wholesome mind arises immediately after nine.

The natural unit at 104.09–21 completes the desire-realm account
and begins the form-realm account. “Unobscured” is specified as
unobscured-indeterminate by the commentary. The counts refer to the
twelve kinds introduced in 2.66. Form-realm obscured-indeterminate
mind begins the next unit at 104.22.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“The unobscured [mind arises] from five.” “In the desire realm”
continues to apply. Unobscured-indeterminate mind arises immediately
after five kinds: the four of its own level, and form-realm wholesome
mind, from which the mind of supernormal creation arises. “Immediately
after it, seven minds.” From desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate
mind arise the four of its own level; two form-realm kinds—wholesome
immediately after creation-mind, and afflicted at rebirth-linking;
and formless-realm afflicted mind, likewise only at rebirth-linking.

“In the form realm, eleven [arise] from wholesome [mind].” Eleven
kinds arise immediately after form-realm wholesome mind, excluding
formless-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind. “That [mind arises]
immediately after nine.” Form-realm wholesome mind, in turn, arises
immediately after nine kinds, excluding the two desire-realm afflicted
kinds and formless-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind.

## 4. The Two Directed Profiles

| Focal kind | Predecessors | Successors |
|---|---|---|
| Desire unobscured-indeterminate | Four desire kinds and form wholesome: **5** | Four desire kinds, form wholesome, form obscured, formless obscured: **7** |
| Form wholesome | All twelve except desire unwholesome, desire obscured, and formless unobscured: **9** | All twelve except formless unobscured: **11** |

“Afflicted” in the higher realms identifies their obscured-indeterminate
kind: 2.66 excluded unwholesome mind from those realms. The “two
afflicted” desire-realm kinds are unwholesome and obscured-indeterminate.
These expansions establish the counted sets without adding new kinds.

Form wholesome's eleven successors include both afflicted desire-realm
kinds, although those two cannot immediately precede it. Its predecessor
and successor sets therefore differ in precisely those two members.
The exclusions must be applied in their stated direction.

## 5. Creation-Mind and Rebirth Are Different Occasions

*Nirmāṇacitta* identifies the desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind
in the connection with form-realm wholesome mind. The commentary states
both directions: form wholesome precedes creation-mind, and creation-mind
precedes form wholesome. “Mind of supernormal creation” makes its
technical sense explicit; the first pass's “constructive” could suggest
ordinary imagination or mental construction generally.

This particular member of the neutral class accounts for the cross-realm
connection. The text does not say that every kind of unobscured-indeterminate
desire-realm cognition can immediately follow or precede form wholesome
in every circumstance. The finer neutral subdivisions are developed
later in the chapter.

The higher-realm afflicted successors have another explanation:
rebirth-linking. That qualification must not be applied to the
creation-mind connection or dropped from the afflicted connections.
A mind's realm affiliation also need not mean that the person changes
realm of birth whenever such a mind becomes manifest.

The local prose gives the form-wholesome counts through exclusions.
It does not separately explain every one of the eleven outgoing or
nine incoming possibilities here. The continuous translation preserves
that brevity instead of supplying an unmarked account of each transition.

## 6. Translation and Source Decisions

The opening *kāma iti varttate* explicitly carries forward “in the
desire realm.” The final *navabhyas tad anantaram* makes *tat*, “that
mind,” the subject arising after nine; it is not a compound meaning
“after that.” The prose confirms the direction by naming form-realm
wholesome mind as what arises.

The anchor applies external sandhi in *śubhān navabhyaḥ* and separates
*tad anantaram* to display its syntax. The continuous Sanskrit retains
the running forms, including *svabhūmikebhya*, *avyākṛṭāt*,
*yatkukśalaṃ*, and *ārupyāvacaram*. The English follows their clear
contextual sense without presenting a corrected critical text.

“Own level” translates *svabhūmika*; in this enumeration it refers
to the four desire-realm kinds already established. “Neutral” in
the title abbreviates unobscured-indeterminate, while the translation
retains the full determination. Obscured-indeterminate mind is also
ethically indeterminate and must not disappear under a loose contrast
between “afflicted” and “neutral.”

The passage is a continuous exposition. It introduces no separate
objector or competing school account. Its statements concern permitted
relations between kinds under the appropriate circumstances, not
multiple simultaneous successors of one occurrence.

## 7. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, the commentary shows why a class-level
relation needs a more particular explanation. “Desire-realm
unobscured-indeterminate” identifies the broad kind; creation-mind
identifies the relevant member for one connection, while rebirth
explains different connections. The relation gains content through
those determinations.

The distinction between eleven successors and nine predecessors also
prevents an undirected notion of association from replacing succession.
Knowing that two kinds are connected in one direction does not establish
the reverse. The task of learning is to retain the direction and
the occasion that make the connection intelligible.

A future formal representation could record these distinctions, but
the present translation does not claim to supply a complete executable
rule system. The source's compressed exclusions and explicitly given
contexts remain the grounds for the study.
