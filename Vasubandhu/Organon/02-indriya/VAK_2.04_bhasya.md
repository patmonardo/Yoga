# VAK_2.04 Bhāṣya — The Three That Complete the Eight

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ājñāsyāmyākhyam ājñākhyam ājñātāvīndriyaṃ tathā /
>
> uttarottarasaṃprāptinirvāṇādyādhipatyataḥ // 2.04 //

> Likewise, each of the three is a Faculty: “I shall know” and “knowledge”
> govern attainment of their respective successors, and “one who has
> known” governs nirvāṇa; another account is also given.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> pratyekam indriyam ity upadarśanārthaṃ tathāśabdaḥ /
> tatrājñāsyāmīndriyasyājñendriyasya prāptāv ādhipatyam /
> ājñendriyasyājñātāvīndriyaprāptau ājñātāvīndriyasya parinirvāṇe /
> na hy avimuktacittasyāsti parinirvāṇam iti /
> ādiśabdo 'nyaparyāyadyotanārthaḥ / katamo 'nyaḥ paryāyaḥ /
> darśanaheyakleśaprahāṇaṃ praty ājñāsyāmīndriyasyādhipatyam /
> bhāvanāheyakleśaprahāṇaṃ praty ājñendriyasya /
> dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāraṃ praty ājñātāvīndriyasya /
> vimuktiprītisukhasaṃvedanād iti /

The prose is printed 40.03–08 in `Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`, after the verse at 40.01–02. The next objection, ādhipatyād indriyatve, belongs with 2.05. This unit stops before it.

Word division is editorial. Repairs follow the research bhāṣya: indrim → indriyam, ājñendraiyasya → ājñendriyasya, pāryāyaḥ → paryāyaḥ, kleāśa → kleśa. Darśnaheyakleśaparihāṇam is read as darśanaheyakleśaprahāṇam, with the parallel bhāvanāheyakleśaprahāṇam. That repair is more than orthography. No independent collation is claimed.

## 3. Continuous Translation

“Likewise” indicates that each of the three is a faculty in its own right. The faculty called “I shall know” governs attainment of the knowledge-faculty; the knowledge-faculty governs attainment of the faculty of one who has known; and the faculty of one who has known governs parinirvāṇa. For one whose mind is not liberated cannot attain parinirvāṇa.

“And so forth” indicates another way to explain their governing efficacy. What is it? The faculty called “I shall know” governs abandonment of afflictions to be abandoned by seeing; the knowledge-faculty governs abandonment of afflictions to be abandoned by cultivation; and the faculty of one who has known governs pleasant abiding in this life, through experiencing the joy and happiness of liberation.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

Two explanations, one account. First, successive attainment and parinirvāṇa. Then ādi opens abandonment by seeing, abandonment by cultivation, and pleasant abiding.

What is that other mode introduces the explanation. It is not an opponent. No school is named. The two accounts stay. No dispute is invented between them.

## 5. What Each Faculty Makes Attainable

| Faculty | First account |
|---|---|
| ājñāsyāmīndriya | attainment of the knowledge-faculty |
| ājñendriya | attainment of the faculty of one who has known |
| ājñātāvīndriya | parinirvāṇa |

The third does not introduce a fourth faculty. Parinirvāṇa is unavailable while mind is not liberated. That is a necessary condition. It is not the claim that liberated mind is already parinirvāṇa, and it is not a sufficient cause. The second account includes pleasant abiding in this life.

## 6. Abandonment by Seeing and by Cultivation

The first governs abandonment of afflictions abandoned by seeing. The second governs abandonment of afflictions abandoned by cultivation. Darśanaheya and bhāvanāheya qualify the afflictions. Prahāṇa names the abandonment.

Seeing removes ignorance, and practice removes habit, would replace the classes. The paragraph does not enumerate them.

This is not the checking of 2.03. The verbs stay apart.

## 7. The Accomplished Faculty and Present Abiding

The third governs pleasant abiding in this life, because of the experience of the joy and happiness of liberation. One who has known is accomplished knowing in this sequence. Pleasant abiding is not identified with parinirvāṇa. Ordinary pleasant feeling is not evidence of liberation.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Tathā carries pratyekam. It does not collapse the three into one faculty. The short “I shall know” belongs to the fuller name, I shall know what is not known.

Ājñā is knowledge as the faculty-name. The two relations place it. The three names are not future, present, and past.

The verse says nirvāṇa. The prose says parinirvāṇa. That difference stands. Ādi is the second account, not an open list. Dṛṣṭadharma is this life. Prīti and sukha both stay.

## 9. Interpretation

For the Organon, these three complete the eight associated with purification in 2.01. The first explanation orders their governing efficacy by successive attainment and parinirvāṇa; the second by abandonment and pleasant abiding in this life. The distinction between checking afflictions in 2.03 and abandoning them here matters for any account of the chapter's Hub. Neither explanation states the proposed “dual” Concept, though both can test it.

The first chapter located these faculties within a portion of the dharma-principle. Here the Bhāṣya explains what each governs. “Knowledge” is a faculty-name in this passage; identifying it with the Organon Idea would be a further interpretive step.

## 10. Review Status

The single kārikā anchor matches the Bhāṣya's distribution of the three faculties. The continuous translation covers both explanations through printed 40.08. The seeing-abandonment repair remains provisional. The objection beginning 2.05 is outside this review.
