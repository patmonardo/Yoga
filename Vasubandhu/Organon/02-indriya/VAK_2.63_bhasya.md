# VAK_2.63 Bhāṣya — Activity at Ceasing and Arising

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirudhyamāne kāritraṃ dvau hetū kurutaḥ trayaḥ /
>
> jāyamāne tato 'nyau tu pratyayau tadviparyayāt // 2.63 //

> Two causes act on a dharma that is ceasing, and three on one that is
> arising. The other two conditions act in the reverse temporal order.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

When these Conditions act, at what state of a dharma do they do so?
The cause-condition has been said to comprise five causes. Of these,
two act on a dharma that is ceasing. “Ceasing” means present, since
it faces cessation. The co-arisen and associated causes act then;
their activity concerns a fruit that arises together with them.
Three act on a dharma that is arising. “Arising” means future,
facing production. These are the homogeneous, pervasive, and
maturation causes. That is the cause-condition's division.

The other two Conditions are understood in the reverse order of
activity. The immediately antecedent Condition acts on what is
arising, by making room for it. The Support-condition acts on
what is ceasing, because present mind and mental factors apprehend
their Support. The predominant Condition stands in every state
without obstructing; this is its activity. The Conditions and
their activities have been explained.

## 4. Reading Control

The timing contrast locates a Condition's activity relative to the
state of a dharma. The source calls the present “ceasing” and the
future “arising”; these are directed states, not separate causal
entities. The immediately antecedent Condition gives an opening,
the Support-condition is apprehended by present mind and factors,
and predominance is non-obstruction in every state.
