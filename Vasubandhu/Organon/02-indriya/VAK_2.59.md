## 4. Grammar

The first four tokens state that five causes take fruit while present. The next two state that two give while present. The following two state that two give while present or past. The final three state that one gives only when past. The grammar keeps the four timing claims distinct; the identity of the five, the two, and the one, and the seed-status reading of taking, are prose.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Five, while present, take their fruit; two give it while present; two
> give it while present or past; one gives it only when past.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Five causes take their fruit while present. Two give fruit while
> present, two while present or past, and one gives it only when past.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The verse states four timing constraints on fruit-taking and fruit-giving. The Bhāṣya identifies the five, the two present-givers (co-arisen and associated), the two present-or-past givers (homogeneous and pervasive), and the past-only giver (maturation), and reads taking as the assumption of seed-status.

## 8. Logical Determination

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** A fruit is “taken” by assuming seed-status toward it. The Bhāṣya specifies the two present givers as co-arisen and associated, the two present-or-past givers as homogeneous and pervasive, and the past-only giver as maturation. The conditioning cause is not listed among the five because it need not have a fruit. Taking and giving do not always coincide; the prose supplies catuskoti examples.

The verse states the timing constraints. The next verses continue the fruit discussion with other classifications of fruit.
