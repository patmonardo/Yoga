# VAK_2.06 Bhāṣya — Engagement and Withdrawal

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> pravṛtter āśrayotpattisthitipratyupabhogataḥ /
>
> caturdaśa tathānyāni nivṛtter indriyāṇi vā // 2.06 //

> Or, for engagement, fourteen [Faculties] with respect to Basis, arising,
> persistence, and experience; likewise, the others are Faculties of
> withdrawal.

Word division is editorial. Repairs: śraddhadīni → śraddhādīni, caīṣām → caiṣām, uragaprabhṛtīṇām → uragaprabhṛtīnām, kariyāyām → kriyāyām, ayukataṃ → ayuktam. The split vikāśana kriyāsu is joined. Tad eva hi tad is retained. No independent collation is claimed.

## 3. Continuous Translation

The word “or” marks a difference of opinion. Others say: the six faculties are the Basis of engagement. The female and male faculties account for its arising, because it arises from them. The life-faculty accounts for its persistence, because it continues through that faculty. Experience occurs through the feelings. These are the fourteen faculties of engagement. The remaining faculties are assigned to withdrawal in the same way: those beginning with faith are its Bases; the faculty called “I shall know” is its arising; the knowledge-faculty is its persistence; and experience occurs through the faculty of one who has known. This explains both why there are just this many faculties and why they occur in this order.

Speech, however, is not a faculty with respect to speaking, because speaking depends on particular training. Nor are hand and foot faculties for taking hold and moving about: the account says they are not distinct from those activities. The very same thing, arising in another manner or another place, is called taking hold or moving about. Moreover, snakes and similar creatures take hold and move about without hands or feet. The anus is not a faculty for excretion, because heavy matter falls wherever there is space and wind propels it. Nor is the genital organ a faculty for pleasure, since that pleasure is produced by the female and male faculties. Otherwise the throat, teeth, eyelids, and joints would also count as faculties for swallowing, chewing, opening and closing the eyes, and contracting and extending. Indeed, anything that causes its own activity would then count as a faculty. It is therefore unreasonable to count speech and the others as faculties.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

Vā is matavikalpa. Others say introduces another justification of the number and the order. It follows the six functions of 2.05 and remains an alternative. The two are not merged.

Speech, however returns to the proposed additions. Particular reasons, then the generalization: every cause would be a faculty of its own act.

The transition is not a new named speaker. The reported alternative and the rebuttal are movements. Neither is assigned to a school.

## 5. One Fourfold Arrangement, Two Courses

| Function | Engagement: fourteen | Withdrawal: eight |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | the six | five beginning with faith |
| Arising | female and male | I shall know |
| Persistence | life | the knowledge-faculty |
| Experience | the five feelings | one who has known |

The conclusion is number and order. The order is the sequence of functions, not only a received list.

Arising from female and male is not a complete account of generation. Life is continued standing. Feeling supplies experience. On the other course the five are the Basis, and the three are arising, persistence, and experience.

Experience through one who has known can be read beside the pleasant abiding of 2.04. This unit does not repeat that explanation.

## 6. Why Speech, Hand, and Foot Are Not Added

Speech depends on particular training. That is the reason against faculty-status in speaking. It is not a rule that no cultivated capacity can be a faculty. This same unit includes the eight.

Hand and foot are not different from taking hold and moving about. The compressed sentence keeps that very thing. Material formation would be a supplement, not an expressed noun.

A second reason: snakes and the like do both without hands or feet. The example is the source's claim.

## 7. Excretion, Pleasure, and Every Cause

Excretion is the falling of heavy matter, and wind propelling it. That is the stated explanation. It is not a modern theory of gravity.

Pleasure of the genital organ is assigned to the female and male faculties already admitted. Excluding the proposal does not exclude those faculties.

If an activity were enough, throat, teeth, eyelids, and joints would enter. The argument ends with every cause and its own act. That is the threatened expansion, not a denial that the parts function.

The grounds differ: training, non-difference from the act, the act without the member, another explanation, a function already assigned. The generalization does not replace those grounds.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Pravṛtti is engagement, the ongoing course. Nivṛtti is withdrawal. The four functions give the range. *Āśraya* is Basis in both courses; it does not name *ālambana*, a cognitive Support. Withdrawal is not a new substance, and unconditioned nirvāṇa is not made a produced thing.

Tathā carries the four. Vā marks the alternative. Prabhava supplies arising for the first of the three, parallel to utpatti.

Upabhoga is experience. Enjoyment must not suggest that every feeling is pleasant.

Anukrama, the order, stays explicit. Ayuktam is unreasonable as faculty-status. It does not make speech or bodily action unreal.

## 9. Interpretation

Two courses, one form: Basis, arising, persistence, experience. Withdrawal is determinate powers, not the absence of engagement. The alternative marker stands. This is not the only account.

For the Organon, the parallel courses offer a stronger test of the proposed “dual” Concept: each has Basis, arising, persistence, and experience, while their faculty members differ. This is a reported alternative to 2.05, not the Bhāṣya's single settled account. The later rebuttal limits the extension of *indriya*: causal involvement in an activity does not by itself secure a place in the faculty enumeration. The Hub reading must account for both the parallel form and that limit.

## 10. Review Status

The single kārikā anchor matches the reported fourteen/eight division and its four parallel functions. The continuous translation covers the alternative explanation and the reply concerning speech and bodily organs through printed 41.03–04. The compressed hand-and-foot sentence remains difficult; the survey beginning 2.07 is outside this review.
