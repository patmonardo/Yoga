# VAK_2.58 Bhāṣya — Efficacy and Predominance

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> yadvalāj jāyate yat tatphalaṃ puruṣakārajam /
>
> apūrvaḥ saṃskṛtasyaiva saṃskṛto 'dhipateḥ phalam // 2.58 //

> Whatever arises through the force of a dharma is its efficacy-fruit. A
> conditioned dharma not arisen before is the predominance-fruit of
> conditioned dharmas alone.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Whatever arises through the force of a given dharma is its activity-
fruit. Thus a concentration at a higher level may arise through an
application-mind at a lower level; one without *āsrava* through one
with *āsrava*; and a mind of creation through a meditation-mind.
Cessation through Discrimination, however, must be said to be
attained through the force of a dharma, not to arise through it.

A conditioned dharma other than one previously arisen is the
predominance-fruit of any conditioned dharma. How do activity-fruit
and predominance-fruit differ? Activity-fruit belongs to an agent's
work; predominance-fruit may belong even to what did not do the
work. For a craftsman, the craft is both an activity-fruit and a
predominance-fruit. For others, it is a predominance-fruit alone.

## 4. Reading Control

The first clause defines an activity-fruit through arising by a
dharma's force, then qualifies the wording for cessation through
Discrimination: it is attained, not produced. The second clause
defines a predominance-fruit through conditioned succession. The
craft example distinguishes an agent's activity from the wider
range of predominance without denying that one fruit may satisfy
both descriptions.
