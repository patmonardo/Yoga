# VAK_2.58 Bhāṣya — Efficacy and Predominance

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> yadvalāj jāyate yat tatphalaṃ puruṣakārajam /
>
> apūrvaḥ saṃskṛtasyaiva saṃskṛto 'dhipateḥ phalam // 2.58 //

> Whatever arises through the force of a dharma is its efficacy-fruit. A
> conditioned dharma not arisen before is the predominance-fruit of
> conditioned dharmas alone.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Whatever arises through the force of a given dharma is its efficacy-fruit: for example, a higher-level concentration through a lower-level application-consciousness, an uncontaminated concentration through a contaminated one, and a transformation-consciousness through a concentration-consciousness. Cessation by Discrimination must instead be said to be attained through a force. A conditioned dharma other than one that has arisen earlier is the predominance-fruit of every conditioned dharma. Why distinguish efficacy-fruit from predominance-fruit? Efficacy-fruit belongs to the agent; predominance-fruit can belong even to a non-agent. For the craftsman, the craft is both an efficacy-fruit and a predominance-fruit; for others, it is a predominance-fruit alone.

## 4. Interpretation

Efficacy is arising through a force. Cessation by Discrimination is attained, not arisen. Predominance is not-earlier conditioned, of the conditioned alone. Agent gets both; non-agent predominance only.

## 5. Review Status

The full local witness now supports the agent/non-agent distinction and the craft example. The concentration example concerns a higher-level concentration arising through a lower-level application-consciousness; it does not establish an Organon ascent schema.
