# VAK_2.49 — The Six Causes and Non-obstruction

## Source

> kāraṇaṃ sahabhūś caiva sabhāgaḥ saṃprayuktakaḥ /
> sarvatrago vipākākhyaḥ ṣaḍvidho hetur iṣyate // 2.49 //

The commentary cites this enumeration at 82.21–24, then begins the
definition of the conditioning cause. Its discussion continues through
83.17; the co-arisen cause begins at 83.18.

## Scientific Translation

Cause is held to be sixfold: conditioning, co-arisen, homogeneous,
associated, pervasive, and maturation.

## Logical Movement

The verse names six types but does not define all six. In the
commentary attached to this section, the conditioning cause alone is
unfolded through its broad relation of non-obstruction, its principal
productive instances, objections, and an attributed alternative
involving causal succession. The remaining five should be left for
their own discussions.

## Interpretation

This opening enumeration poses the problem of causality correctly: a
determinate Dharma cannot be taken, by itself, as a complete explanation of
another Dharma's arising. Its causal role is determined only within a
specified relation. The six headings therefore do not multiply hidden
entities; they distinguish six ways in which a causal explanation may be
answerable. The first discussion immediately separates non-obstruction from
productive efficacy, showing why bare presence, absence, or classification
cannot yet settle the causal question.

The Kośa-wide synthesis presents Vijñāna as Discriminative Cognition
joining and governing Perception and Conception; their unity is
Inconceivable as a homogeneous operation, with its Idea disclosed in
Cognition Base. Vijñāna governs Mind and guides the reading of Dharma
Base. *Avijñapti* is the same Dharma classified in Form Base and Dharma
Base, and Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it. This larger
systematic frame is not a claim that the six-cause verse states each
of those relations.
