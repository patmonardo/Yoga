# VAK_2.31 Bhāṣya — What the Higher Levels Drop

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kaukṛtyamiddhākuśalāny ādye dhyāne na santy ataḥ /
>
> dhyānāntare vitarkaś ca vicāraś cāpy ataḥ param // 2.31 //

> Of the previously stated factors, regret, torpor, and all unwholesome
> factors are absent in the first dhyāna. In the intermediate dhyāna,
> initial examination is also absent. Beyond the intermediate—in the
> second and higher dhyānas and the Formless levels—sustained examination
> is also absent, as are deception and crookedness. Prior exclusions
> continue to apply.

## 3. Continuous Translation

The preceding rules for mental factors concerned the Desire Realm. In the first dhyāna, regret and torpor are entirely absent, as is anything unwholesome: hostility; the factors beginning with anger, apart from crookedness, intoxication, and deception; and shamelessness and lack of moral caution. The other factors remain as before.

The intermediate dhyāna retains those exclusions and also excludes initial examination. Beyond the intermediate dhyāna, in the second and higher dhyānas and in the Formless levels, sustained examination is also absent. The word “also” includes deception and crookedness among the further exclusions. Everything else remains as before.

Crookedness is described as occurring as high as Brahmā, but not above, because it is connected with an assembly. In his own assembly, the monk Aśvajit asked Brahmā where the four Great Elements cease without remainder. Brahmā did not know and evaded the question: “I am Brahmā, the Lord, the Maker, the Fashioner, the Creator, the Producer, the Father of beings.” [The distinction between “Creator” and “Producer” is uncertain in the damaged title sequence.] This completes the account of how many mental factors occur in each kind of consciousness at each level.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The Desire Realm distribution is the point of departure. Exclusions accumulate. The remainder as before keeps what is not altered. The Brahmā episode supports the upper limit of crookedness. The question is Aśvajit's. The titles are Brahmā's. The commentary calls the response an evasion made without knowing. Not an assertion of creative authority. The last sentence closes the distribution inquiry.

## 5. The First Dhyāna

Regret and torpor wholly excluded. Whatever is unwholesome. Hostility. The anger-group with three exceptions. Shamelessness and lack of moral caution.

Crookedness, intoxication, and deception are exceptions to the anger-group exclusion. Not a permission of unwholesome dharmas where the unwholesome has been excluded. Afflicted and unwholesome, distinguished in 2.30, stay necessary.

A level's possible consciousnesses are not one wholesome absorption. Crookedness in the Brahmā discussion prevents that collapse.

## 6. Intermediate and Beyond

| Level | Further exclusion |
|---|---|
| First, relative to the desire realm | regret, torpor, and the unwholesome specified |
| Intermediate | initial examination |
| Second and higher, and the Formless | sustained examination, and deception and crookedness |

The intermediate is between the first and the second. Initial and
sustained examination have different distributions. Definitions are
not this unit. Carrying exclusions forward does not make the levels
identical or make the intermediate a first-dhyāna consciousness.

Also includes deception and crookedness. It does not include intoxication. The three exceptions must not all be declared absent above the intermediate.

## 7. The Brahmā Example

Connected with an assembly. Asked before his assembly, he does not answer and recites titles. Lack of knowledge and evasion are supplied by the commentary. The question is where the four Great Elements cease without remainder. This unit does not answer it and does not complete the encounter.

Assembly-dependence is stated for crookedness. The joint exclusion of deception and crookedness is not expanded into a theory of both. The example stays in the distribution argument.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Ataḥ refers to the distribution already stated. Not a cause. As previously excluded carries what has been dropped. The remainder as before governs the unchanged.

Four and I am are contextual. The title sequence stays marked. Intoxication is mada, not conceit. Torpor is middha, not sluggishness.

## 9. Interpretation

Exclusion, not an increase of powers. Inherited classification and the exact change at each level. Removing a class is not qualifying a factor already counted.

Level, ethical character, and identity stay apart. Unwholesome excluded is not every affliction excluded. Absence at a higher level is not a description of every aspect of attainment there.

Status is not knowledge. The evasion is the commentary's evidence
for crookedness, not an endorsement of the asserted creative titles.

The cumulative exclusions constrain which mental episodes can occur
at each level. They do not make the level itself a caitta or prove
that all remaining factors are present in every episode. The Brahmā
story tests a real case of crookedness against an assumed upward
purification of every mental factor. It also distinguishes asserted
authority from knowledge: Brahmā evades the question he cannot answer.
The passage does not locate the caittas in a new Base, Essence, or
Principle merely because the episode depends on Form.

translation makes cumulative exclusions, the intermediate boundary,
and the prose's two further names explicit; the anchor matches.
