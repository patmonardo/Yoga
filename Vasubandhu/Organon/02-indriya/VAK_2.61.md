# VAK_2.61 — Opening the Four Conditions

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चित्तचैत्तास्तथान्येऽपि संप्रयुक्तकवर्जिताः ।
> चत्वारः प्रत्यया उक्ता हेत्वाख्यः पञ्च हेतवः ॥ २.६१ ॥

The displayed reading regularizes the opening to *cittacaittās tathā*,
following the running Bhāṣya's *cittacaittāḥ*. The standalone kārikā
transcription and research file instead have *cittacaitāḥ tathā*.
The Devanāgarī here is an editorial rendering of the stated IAST reading,
not an independently collated witness.

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> cittacaittās tathānye 'pi saṃprayuktakavarjitāḥ /
> catvāraḥ pratyayā uktā hetvākhyaḥ pañca hetavaḥ // 2.61 //

The full verse includes both the closing qualification of the cause
counts and the opening of the conditions. The research Bhāṣya focuses
on the second half as the beginning of its pratyaya commentary unit;
the standalone kārikā witness confirms the complete verse. A
commentary-unit boundary is not a verse boundary. The running
commentary treats the first half at 97.26–98.02 and the second at
98.03–08.

## 4. Grammar

The verse has three constructions, not one sentence identifying mental
factors as four conditions.

**Retrospective subject.** *Cittacaittāḥ* answers the commentary's
question about the four profiles in 2.60: they concern mind and mental
factors. The predicate “arise from the remaining causes” is inherited
from that verse.

**Extension with an exclusion.** *Tathā anye api* extends the four
profiles to other dharmas. *Saṃprayuktakavarjitāḥ* qualifies their causal
account: associated cause is additionally removed. The Bhāṣya identifies
these others as material dharmas and formations dissociated from mind.
It does not exclude the associated cause from the six-cause system itself.

**New topic.** *Catvāraḥ pratyayā uktāḥ* independently announces four
conditions. *Hetvākhyaḥ [pratyayaḥ] pañca hetavaḥ* is a nominal definition:
the condition called “cause” consists of five causes. Its singular subject
and plural predicate describe a grouping, not five additional conditions.
The Bhāṣya identifies the omitted sixth cause as *kāraṇahetu*.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> [The foregoing concern] mind and mental factors. The others also
> [arise] likewise, excluding associated cause. Four conditions are
> taught. The [condition] named “cause” is the five causes.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The preceding four causal profiles apply to mind and mental factors.
> Material dharmas and formations dissociated from mind follow the same
> pattern, with associated cause additionally excluded. Four kinds of
> condition are taught. Of these, the cause-condition comprises the five
> causes other than the conditioning cause (*kāraṇahetu*).

The names of the four conditions are supplied by the Bhāṣya's scriptural
citation: cause-condition, immediately antecedent condition, object-condition,
and predominant condition. The verse itself gives their number and
specifies only the cause-condition. The other definitions follow in 2.62.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The causal account must be adjusted to the kind of dharma being
> explained: the association proper to mind and its factors cannot
> simply be attributed to material and dissociated dharmas. The inquiry
> then distinguishes four kinds of condition, gathering five already
> explained causes under one of them.

This is a study interpretation of the transition. It keeps the
domain-specific cause profiles distinct from the classification of
conditions while showing that the five causes are carried into one of the
four conditions.

**Organon interpretation—not literal Bhāṣya doctrine.** In our project
reading, the completed account of Hetu and arising is not discarded when
the analysis turns to Loka; it is retained as an invariant within that
frame. The five causes enter the fourfold condition-system as
*hetu-pratyaya*. The movement from determining arising to dependent
origination within a Loka is what we call Absolute Idealism: the Loka is
articulated through relations, not treated as an external container or
reduced to a private mind. This verse opens that account; the fuller
condition-relations are explained in the following verses.

“Conditioning cause” remains the working English for *kāraṇahetu*;
its wording must not obscure its distinction from *hetupratyaya*.
Associated cause is retained within cause-condition despite being
excluded from the production profiles of material and dissociated dharmas.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Clause | Function | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| *cittacaittās tathā anye 'pi saṃprayuktakavarjitāḥ* | Carries the 2.60 cause profiles from mind and mental factors to other dharmas, with associated cause additionally excluded | Domain-specific arising profiles |
| *catvāraḥ pratyayā uktāḥ* | Announces the four kinds of condition | New conditional classification |
| *hetvākhyaḥ pañca hetavaḥ* | Defines the cause-condition as five causes, excluding *kāraṇahetu* | Membership within the four-condition system |

The two exclusions have different scopes. Associated cause is removed when
the 2.60 profiles apply to material and mind-dissociated dharmas; conditioning
cause is omitted from the five causes grouped as cause-condition. Neither
exclusion removes a cause-type from the overall six-cause account.

## 9. Interpretation

In the project's reading, the move from *hetu* to *pratyaya* is the
subsumption of the preceding account of arising within the Loka analysis:
the Hetu determination is not erased but retained as an invariant of Loka.
The textual hinge is explicit: five of the already explained causes are
grouped as one condition, the *hetu-pratyaya*, within a system of four
conditions.

This is the project's Absolute Idealism: dependent origination is
understood within a Loka articulated by its relations, not as an external
world added to isolated causes. The source itself names the four conditions
and defines the cause-condition; it does not state this Hegelian or Loka
synthesis. The fuller operation of the other conditions follows in 2.62 and
the subsequent verses.

The commentary boundary is also distinct from the kārikā boundary: the
first half of the complete verse closes the cause-count discussion with
2.60, while this study's Bhāṣya unit begins at 98.03 with the question
about conditions.

The basic learning move is to keep the classifications non-competitive.
The five causes collected as cause-condition do not replace the six-cause
analysis, and the four conditions are not four further causes added beside
it. This provides a foundation for later Loka–Karma study, where the
manifest movement of relations can deepen this account without canceling its
distinctions.
