## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Consciousness and mental factors, and the others likewise except the
> associated [cause]. Four conditions are taught; the cause-named
> condition is five causes.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Consciousness and mental factors, and the other [dharmas] likewise,
> except for the associated cause. Four conditions are taught; the
> cause-condition is the five causes.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The verse names the four conditions and states that the cause-condition consists of five causes. The Bhāṣya identifies the four as cause, immediately antecedent, object, and predominant, and excludes the conditioning cause from the five.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya names the four conditions as cause, immediately antecedent, object, and predominant. The “cause-condition” consists of the five causes other than the conditioning cause; these similarly named notions are distinct. The associated-cause exclusion continues the preceding discussion and does not redefine the four conditions.

The verse names the four conditions and specifies the cause-condition. The following verses define the remaining conditions.
