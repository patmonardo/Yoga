# VAK_2.61 — Four Conditions

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चित्तचैत्तास्तथान्येऽपि संप्रयुक्तकवर्जिताः ।
>
> चत्वारः प्रत्यया उक्ता हेत्वाख्यः पञ्च हेतवः ॥ २.६१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Mind and the mental factors, likewise. The others also, excluding the associated cause. Four conditions are taught. The one named cause is the five causes.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

What are the conditions? Four are taught. Where? In the sūtra. Four kinds of condition: cause-condition, immediately antecedent condition, object-condition, predominant condition. A kind of condition is what conditionality means. Of these, the one named cause is the five causes. The five apart from the conditioning cause are the cause-condition.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The associated cause was excluded from form and from the dissociated. That exclusion stands. It is not this one. This one excludes the conditioning cause from the group called cause-condition. The conditioning cause remains a cause. It is not a member of that condition.

The five are types. Not all five in every arising. The profiles of 2.60 stand. Not five cause-conditions beside the four.

The other three are named. Not defined.

Organon rendering:

Five causes are carried into one condition. The conditions are not a replacement of the causes. The special pratyaya is not this list. Not the verse. Not the final pass.
