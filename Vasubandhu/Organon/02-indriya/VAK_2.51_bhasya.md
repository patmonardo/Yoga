# VAK_2.51 Bhāṣya — Followers of Consciousness

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> caittā dvau saṃvarau teṣāṃ cetaso lakṣaṇāni ca /
>
> cittānuvartinaḥ kālaphalādiśubhatādibhiḥ // 2.51 //

> The mental factors, the two restraints, and their marks, together with
> the marks of consciousness, follow consciousness in time, result, and
> ethical status.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Which dharmas follow consciousness? The mental factors associated with it, the meditative and uncontaminated restraints, and the marks of these and of consciousness. They follow it in ten respects: together in arising, persistence, cessation, and period; together in fruit, maturation, and homogeneous outflow; and matching it in wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate status. The least consciousness is co-arisen cause for fifty-eight dharmas: ten major factors, forty of their marks, and eight of its own marks and secondary marks. Fifty-four dharmas are co-arisen causes for it, excluding its own secondary marks. Another count of fourteen is refused. Some dharmas are co-arisen without being co-arisen causes; the ensuing debate tests whether simultaneous arising establishes causal reciprocity and closes by retaining the co-arisen cause.

## 4. Interpretation

The ten respects define following; the 58 and 54 count opposite directions of co-arisen causality. Co-arising is wider than being a co-arisen cause. The objection from mere simultaneity remains visible beside the Bhāṣya's closing defense.
