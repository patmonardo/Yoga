# VAK_2.50 Bhāṣya — Co-Arisen Cause

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> svato 'nye kāraṇaṃ hetuḥ sahabhūr ye mithaḥphalāḥ /
>
> bhūtavac cittacittānuvartilakṣaṇalakṣyavat // 2.50 //

> What is other than itself is the conditioning cause. Co-arisen causes
> are mutually resultant: the four Great Elements; mind and the
> dharmas that follow it, each of the other; and the marks and what is
> marked, each of the other.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Co-arisen [causes] are those that are one another's results.” The
dharmas that are mutually results are co-arisen causes of one another.
How so? As with the Great Elements, mind and its followers,
and the marks and what they mark.

The four Great Elements are co-arisen causes of one another.
Mind is a co-arisen cause of the dharmas that follow it,
and they of mind. The conditioned marks are causes of what
is marked, and what is marked is a cause of them. In this qualified
sense, all conditioned dharmas are co-arisen causes as appropriate.

A further case must be counted: even without being one another's
results, a dharma is a co-arisen cause of its secondary marks; those
secondary marks are not co-arisen causes of that dharma.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The carried conditioning-cause definition closes in the preceding
Bhāṣya unit. This unit states mutual result as the co-arisen-cause
criterion and works through three examples. Its “as appropriate”
qualifies the scope of the generalization over conditioned dharmas.
The final secondary-mark case is expressly asymmetric and is supplied
by the Bhāṣya, not by the verse's reciprocal examples. The followers
of mind become the next question.
