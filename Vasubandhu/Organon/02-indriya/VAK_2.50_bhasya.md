# VAK_2.50 Bhāṣya — Co-arisen Cause

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> svato 'nye kāraṇaṃ hetuḥ sahabhūr ye mithaḥphalāḥ /
> bhūtavac cittacittānuvartilakṣaṇalakṣyavat // 2.50 //

> Other than itself, the conditioning cause. Co-arisen: those that are mutually results. As the great elements, as consciousness and its followers, as the marks and the marked.

The first clause was 2.49. This unit is 83.18–24. The followers are asked at 83.25. Not opened.

## 2. Continuous Conventional Translation

Co-arisen causes are those that are mutually results. Reciprocally one another's results, co-arisen causes of one another. The four great elements, of one another. Consciousness, of the dharmas that follow it, and they of it. The conditioned marks, of the marked, and it of them. Every conditioned dharma, as appropriate. A qualification: even without mutual result, a dharma is a co-arisen cause of its secondary marks. They are not of it.

## 3. Movement and Voice

Definition, three applications, then an added qualification. The return is stated. The extension is as appropriate. The secondary marks are the exception the text itself adds.

## 4. Organon Reading

Same moment is not the relation. Mutual result is. The asymmetric case keeps reciprocity from becoming the test of every admitted pair. Consciousness here is the produced. Not Citta the Universal. Not the translation. Not the final pass.
