# VAK_2.34 Bhāṣya — One Meaning, Fivefold Association

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> cittaṃ mano 'tha vijñānam ekārthaṃ cittacaitasāḥ /
>
> sāśrayālambanākārāḥ saṃprayuktāś ca pañcadhā // 2.34 //

> Consciousness, Mind, and Cognition have one meaning here. Consciousness
> and mental factors have a Basis, a Support, and a manner,
> and are associated through five equalities: Basis, Support,
> manner, time, and individual instance—one consciousness and one of each
> accompanying factor.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Mental factors together with consciousness have been explained by kind. Their technical designations must now be defined, since they are used in the teaching. “Consciousness,” “Mind,” and “Cognition” have one referent here. It is called consciousness (*citta*) because it gathers, Mind (*manas*) because it considers, and Cognition (*vijñāna*) because it cognizes. Others explain the names differently: it is *citta* because it is variegated by wholesome and unwholesome Principles; the same thing, when serving as Basis, is called Mind, and when based upon it, Cognition.

Likewise, “consciousness and mental factors,” “having Basis,” “having Support,” “having manner,” and “associated” refer to the same consciousness–factor pairing under different descriptions. They have a Basis because they depend on a Faculty; they have a Support because they apprehend a Condition; they have a manner because they take that very Support in a particular way; and they are associated because they are conjoined equally. In what respects are they equal? Five: Basis, Support, manner, time, and individual instance. What does equality of instance mean? As there is one consciousness, there is one instance of each accompanying mental factor. Thus consciousness and mental factors have been described with their detailed distinctions.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

From distributions and particular distinctions to the technical expressions. Three names, then the predicates of the pair, then five equalities, then the close. Others are not a named school. The questions introduce explanations. The next verse opens formations dissociated from consciousness.

## 5. Three Names

One meaning in this context. Gathering, considering, and cognizing do not make three counted entities. What is gathered is not specified. No accumulated-seed account is inserted.

Others: variegation, then Mind as Basis and Cognition as based upon
it. That same preserves common reference while distinguishing the
roles. These are the text's naming explanations, not independently
established etymologies or license for interchange in every context.

## 6. Basis, Support, Manner

Four descriptions, one pair, four explanations. Basis is the Faculty
depended on. Support is what is apprehended; the prose also calls it
the Condition (*viṣaya*) in explaining the apprehension. Manner is
how that same Support is taken. The Condition is not collapsed into
the Faculty merely because both enter the episode.

Manner is not established here as a separate image, and not as a theory of representation.

## 7. Fivefold Association

Basis, Support, manner, time, and individual instance. One consciousness, one of each factor. Not the claim that only one mental factor accompanies consciousness. Not the claim that the factors are one entity. Distributive: each has one instance, as consciousness has one.

Time supplies simultaneity. Simultaneity is not the whole definition. The counts said which occur. This says the respects of association.

## 8. Limits

Defective spacing stays in the source. The explanations secure the technical forms. The question what is this equality follows the list of five. The answer clarifies the last. Last is not inserted into the Sanskrit.

The five do not settle the deferred shared Support of
attention and equanimity. They do not refute the alternative on
initial and sustained examination. Those arguments concern how
particular factors satisfy the relation.

## 9. Interpretation

Contextual identity and relational distinction. Three names, one referent, different roles. Shared conditions, distinguishable factors, each counted once. Common reference, common conditions, and identity of factor stay apart.

The source's contextual one-referent claim concerns citta, manas,
and vijñāna as designations. The alternative explanation gives the
same referent different roles as Basis and what is based upon it.
Neither account identifies the accompanying caittas with that
referent. Any Organon distinction among Consciousness, Mind, and
Cognition must therefore be stated as a proposed functional analysis,
not attributed to this verse as three separate entities.

Fivefold association relates distinct factors without making their
tokens identical. Equality of instance is one consciousness and one
of each accompanying kind, not one factor total or one substance
behind the episode. The shared relations do not by themselves solve
every earlier dispute about particular factors.
