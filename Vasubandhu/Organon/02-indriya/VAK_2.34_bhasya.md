# VAK_2.34 Bhāṣya — Names of Consciousness and Fivefold Association

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> cittaṃ mano 'tha vijñānam ekārthaṃ cittacaitasāḥ /
>
> sāśrayālambanākārāḥ saṃprayuktāś ca pañcadhā // 2.34 //

> Consciousness, Mind, and Cognition have one meaning here. Consciousness
> and mental factors have a Basis, a Cognitive Condition, and a manner,
> and are associated through five equalities: Basis, Cognitive Condition,
> manner, time, and individual instance—one consciousness and one of each
> accompanying factor.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Mental factors, together with consciousness, have been explained according to their kinds. Their designations are now defined, because these expressions are current in the teaching. Consciousness, Mind, and Cognition have one meaning. Consciousness, because it gathers. Mind, because it considers. Cognition, because it cognizes. Others say: consciousness, because variegated by wholesome and unwholesome Principles. That same, serving as Basis, is Mind; as based upon it, it is Cognition.

Just as those three have one meaning, so the expressions concerning consciousness and the mental factors—having Basis, Cognitive Condition, and manner, and being associated—have one referent. They are called having Basis because they depend on the Faculties. Having Cognitive Condition, because they apprehend a Condition. Having manner, because they take that same Cognitive Condition in a particular way. Associated, because conjoined equally. In what manner? Fivefold: by equality of Basis, Cognitive Condition, manner, time, and individual instance. What is this equality? As there is one consciousness, so there is one of each mental factor. Consciousness and the mental factors, with their detailed distinctions, have been explained.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

From distributions and particular distinctions to the technical expressions. Three names, then the predicates of the pair, then five equalities, then the close. Others are not a named school. The questions introduce explanations. The next verse opens formations dissociated from consciousness.

## 5. Three Names

One meaning in this context. Gathering, considering, and cognizing do not make three counted entities. What is gathered is not specified. No accumulated-seed account is inserted.

Others: variegation, then Mind as Basis and Cognition as based upon
it. That same preserves common reference while distinguishing the
roles. These are the text's naming explanations, not independently
established etymologies or license for interchange in every context.

## 6. Basis, Cognitive Condition, Manner

Four descriptions, one pair, four explanations. Basis is the Faculty
depended on. Cognitive Condition is what is apprehended. Manner is
how that same Cognitive Condition is taken. Condition is not
collapsed into the Faculty or renamed Object.

Manner is not established here as a separate image, and not as a theory of representation.

## 7. Fivefold Association

Basis, Cognitive Condition, manner, time, and individual instance. One consciousness, one of each factor. Not the claim that only one mental factor accompanies consciousness. Not the claim that the factors are one entity. Distributive: each has one instance, as consciousness has one.

Time supplies simultaneity. Simultaneity is not the whole definition. The counts said which occur. This says the respects of association.

## 8. Limits

Defective spacing stays in the source. The explanations secure the technical forms. The question what is this equality follows the list of five. The answer clarifies the last. Last is not inserted into the Sanskrit.

The five do not settle the deferred shared Cognitive Condition of
attention and equanimity. They do not refute the alternative on
initial and sustained examination. Those arguments concern how
particular factors satisfy the relation.

## 9. Interpretation

Contextual identity and relational distinction. Three names, one referent, different roles. Shared conditions, distinguishable factors, each counted once. Common reference, common conditions, and identity of factor stay apart.

Cognition joins and governs Perception and Conception and guides
the reading of Dharma Base, remaining distinct in role from
consciousness and Mind. The source's contextual one-meaning
claim is not suppressed; it concerns these designations and their
common reference, not the erasure of their explanatory functions.

Fivefold association relates distinct factors without making their
tokens identical. Equality of instance is one consciousness and one
of each accompanying kind, not one factor total or one substance
behind the episode. The shared relations do not by themselves solve
every earlier dispute about particular factors.
