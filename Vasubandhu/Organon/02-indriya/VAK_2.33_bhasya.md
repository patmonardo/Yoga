# VAK_2.33 Bhāṣya — Coarse and Subtle

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vitarkacārāv audāryasūkṣmate māna unnatiḥ /
>
> madaḥ svadharme raktasya paryādānaṃ tu cetasaḥ // 2.33 //

> Initial and sustained examination are, respectively, coarseness and
> subtlety of consciousness. Conceit is elevation of consciousness through
> a constructed distinction relative to another. Intoxication, however,
> is the taking-over of consciousness in one attached to one's own qualities.

## 3. Continuous Translation

What distinguishes initial from sustained examination? They are coarseness and subtlety—but of what? Of consciousness, as the verse will say later. Initial examination is the coarseness of consciousness; sustained examination is its subtlety. How, then, can both occur in one consciousness? Some say that clarified butter placed in water [a provisional reading] and warmed by the sun's rays neither hardens nor melts excessively. Likewise, when both factors are present, consciousness is neither excessively coarse nor excessively subtle: each factor has an effect there.

But on that account the two factors would be conditions for coarseness and subtlety, just as water and heat condition the butter's hardening and melting. They would not themselves *be* those qualities. Moreover, coarse and subtle are relative to level and variety. If that relative difference defined the factors, both would be found even at the summit of existence. Mere degree does not establish a difference of kind.

Others appeal to a sūtra calling them formations of speech: one speaks after initial and sustained examination, not without them [the quotation is damaged]. On this account the coarse ones are initial examinations and the subtle ones sustained examinations. Why could a coarse dharma and a subtle dharma not occur in one consciousness? They could if they differed in kind, as feeling and reflection do. Lesser and greater intensity of *one* kind, however, cannot occur together. The reply asserts a difference of kind. Then specify it, comes the challenge. It is hard to state, so intensity is offered as its sign. But each kind can itself vary in intensity; that sign does not establish the difference.

Still others say the two do not occur in the same consciousness at all. How, then, does the first dhyāna have five factors? It has them with reference to the level, they answer, not to every single moment.

What distinguishes conceit from intoxication? Conceit is an elevation of consciousness through a constructed distinction from another. Intoxication is the taking-over of consciousness in someone attached to their own qualities. As there is intoxication from liquor, so there is intoxication from attachment. Others define it instead as a particular kind of exhilaration.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The inquiry into distinctions continues. Three positions: a balancing analogy, speech-formations, and denial of occurrence in one consciousness. Objections press condition against nature, degree against kind, and level against moment. No harmonization is appended. Conceit and intoxication follow, with another reported definition of intoxication.

## 5. Condition and State

The butter analogy says both operate to prevent excess. The reply makes them conditions of those states, not the states. The objection tests the definition. It does not merely deny that opposite influences could operate together.

Relativity by level and variety: if that alone established the factors, they would reach the summit. That conflicts with 2.31, where they are successively excluded. The image and the range do different work. An account would have to name the factors and keep the range.

## 6. Kind and Degree

The sūtra account allows coarse and subtle in one consciousness. Coexistence is allowed where kinds differ. Intensity alone is challenged as the mark of that difference. A difference of kind is asserted and admitted to be hard to state. Intensity is rejected as the indication, because each kind itself varies in intensity. The demand is a character beyond degree. Not a settled definition of coarse thought and subtle thought. The renderings stay labels while the referents are examined.

## 7. Level and Moment

The last position denies co-presence. Five factors of the first dhyāna are then answered by level, not moment. An alternative. Not a withdrawal of the earlier association. Both stay. No order of the factors is supplied under the alternative. A predicate of a level need not, on this account, distribute to every moment. The attribution stays with the debate.

## 8. Conceit and Intoxication

Elevation through a constructed distinction relative to another. Taking-over through attachment to one's own qualities. Comparison, and absorption in one's own. Pride for both would lose the distinction. Liquor clarifies attachment-born intoxication. It does not identify the factor with drinking. Exhilaration is others. Not a replacement.

## 9. Interpretation

Identity, conditioning, intensity, and scale do not substitute for one another. An influence is not the state. Strength is not kind. The disputed definitions stay disputed. What each proposal explains, and what it leaves, is the precision. Level and moment are useful because the role is explicit.

Comparison with another and attachment to one's own qualities
organize different explanations. Intoxication takes over consciousness;
the qualities are what the person is attached to, not what is taken
over. The text does not assign those qualities the technical role
of Condition or Support in this sentence.

The dispute over co-occurrence forces a model to distinguish a
factor's own nature, its causal effect on consciousness, its relative
intensity, and the level at which it occurs. These are four different
claims. The attributed positions cannot be combined into a single
settled mechanism merely because the same two factor-names recur.
