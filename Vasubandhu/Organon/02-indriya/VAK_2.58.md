## 4. Grammar

The first five tokens define efficacy-fruit as whatever arises through a force. The remaining tokens define predominance-fruit as a conditioned dharma not previously arisen, belonging to the conditioned alone. The grammar keeps the two fruits distinct; the agent/non-agent distinction and the attainment (not arising) of cessation by Discrimination are prose.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Whatever arises through the force of something is its fruit born of
> efficacy. A conditioned dharma not arisen earlier is the fruit of
> predominance, of the conditioned alone.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Whatever arises through the force of a dharma is its efficacy-fruit. A
> conditioned dharma not arisen before is the predominance-fruit of
> conditioned dharmas alone.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Efficacy is arising through a force. Predominance is a conditioned dharma not previously arisen, restricted to the conditioned. The Bhāṣya distinguishes the agent (who receives both) from the non-agent (who receives predominance only) and notes that cessation by Discrimination is attained, not arisen.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya gives examples of a higher-level concentration from a lower application-consciousness, an uncontaminated concentration from a contaminated one, and a transformation-consciousness from a concentration-consciousness. Cessation by Discrimination is attained through a force, not said to arise. Efficacy-fruit belongs to an agent; predominance-fruit can belong even to a non-agent (as with the craft for those who are not the craftsman).

The verse defines the two fruits by arising-through-force and by not-earlier conditioned restricted to the conditioned. The next verse turns to the timing of fruit-reception and fruit-giving by the causes.
