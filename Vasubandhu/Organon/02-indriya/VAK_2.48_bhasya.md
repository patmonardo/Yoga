# VAK_2.48 Bhāṣya — Same-Class and the Close

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sabhāgatā sā tu punar vipāko 'py āptayo dvidhā /
>
> lakṣaṇāni ca niḥṣyandāḥ samāpatty-asamanvayāḥ // 2.48 //

> Same-class is likewise classed with sentient beings, is a homogeneous
> outflow, and is unobscured-indeterminate; it is also maturation.
> Acquisitions and marks each admit two result-sorts: homogeneous outflow
> and maturation. The two attainments and non-acquisition are homogeneous
> outflows only.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Same-class is likewise [classed with sentient beings, a homogeneous
outflow, and unobscured-indeterminate, as stated immediately before].
That, however, is also maturation: it is not only a homogeneous
outflow. It belongs to the Desire, Form, and Formless Realms.

Acquisitions are twofold: acquisitions are homogeneous outflows and
maturation-born. And the marks: “twofold” continues, as with acquisitions.

The attainments and non-acquisition are homogeneous outflows. The two
attainments without consciousness and non-acquisition are homogeneous
outflows only.

The remaining things to be said about these have been stated, as have
those concerning the other two, the non-reflective result and life.
Therefore we do not say them again.

How has the sentient classification of acquisition and the rest been
stated? Through the statement of possession. How has the sentient
and non-sentient classification of the marks been stated? Because
they co-arise with all conditioned dharmas.

The dissociated formations have been explained.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

1. **Selective inheritance.** The final “likewise” of 2.47 transfers
   sentient classification, homogeneous outflow, and unobscured-indeterminate
   status to same-class. It does not transfer the two-Realm limit.
2. **Addition and range.** Same-class is also maturation and belongs to
   all three Realms.
3. **Twofold result-sort.** Acquisition and the marks admit homogeneous
   outflow and maturation-born cases. “Twofold” continues into the marks
   clause; it does not count two acquisitions or two marks.
4. **Outflow alone.** The two attainments without consciousness and
   non-acquisition receive the restrictive *eva* in the prose. The
   non-reflective result is not one of those attainments.
5. **No needless repetition.** Other classifications of these headings,
   and those of the non-reflective result and life, have already been
   explained.
6. **Two retrospective questions.** Possession-language establishes the
   sentient classification of acquisition and the rest. The marks have
   sentient and non-sentient cases because they co-arise with all
   conditioned dharmas.
7. **Section close.** The dissociated-formations discussion ends. The
   following prose at 82.19–20 recalls the earlier statement that arising
   generates nothing without causes and conditions and asks what those
   causes and conditions are. That transition leads into 2.49; it is not
   an answer supplied by 2.48.

## 5. Classification Controls

| Heading | Homogeneous outflow | Maturation | Qualification here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same-class | Admitted | Admitted | Sentient, unobscured-indeterminate; all three Realms |
| Acquisition | Admitted | Admitted | Twofold by result-sort; possession-language grounds sentient classification |
| Marks | Admitted | Admitted | Twofold is inherited; sentient and non-sentient cases |
| Non-reflective attainment | Only sort here | Excluded here | Not the maturation-result called non-reflective result |
| Cessation attainment | Only sort here | Excluded here | Second of the two attainments without consciousness |
| Non-acquisition | Only sort here | Excluded here | Not merged with acquisition's twofold range |

The columns record admitted result-sorts for kinds, not simultaneous
properties of every instance. The Realm range is not automatically
combined with every result-sort to generate a cross-product. Likewise,
the marks' two sentient-status options do not mean that each individual
mark is both. The closing classification does not reopen or settle
the preceding disputes about distinct real entities.

## 6. Interpretation

Cognition joins and governs Perception and Conception and guides the
Dharma Base, remaining distinct from consciousness and Mind. The
central hub's closure here is a determination of relations: possession,
same-class, marks, attainments, and non-acquisition do not acquire
one undifferentiated nature by sharing a catalogue.

The movement from this classification to causes and conditions asks
how the differentiated dharmas operate together. Classification is
not causal sufficiency. Nor does closing this discussion close the
Indriya chapter. The local result-sort model preserves the distinctions
needed for that next inquiry without answering it in advance.
