# VAK_2.49 Bhāṣya — Six Causes, and the Conditioning Cause

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāraṇaṃ sahabhūś caiva sabhāgaḥ saṃprayuktakaḥ /
> sarvatrago vipākākhyaḥ ṣaḍvidho hetur iṣyate // 2.49 //

> Conditioning, and co-arisen, homogeneous, associated, pervasive, and that called maturation: cause is held to be sixfold.

The question was asked at the close of 2.48. This unit answers with six names and defines the first. Printed through the close of the conditioning cause. Not a collation. The co-arisen cause is next.

## 2. Continuous Conventional Translation

Six causes. Other than itself is the conditioning cause. All dharmas except its own nature, by non-obstruction toward what is arising. Knowledge can obstruct influxes. The definition is of what arises, not of everything that might have arisen. A principal conditioning cause can also produce: eye and visible form, food, seed. Non-obstruction is not productive agency. Others give every conditioning cause a mediated efficacy, by a succession of causes. A direction. The conditioning cause has been explained.

## 3. Movement and Voice

Enumeration, then the broad definition, the knowledge objection, the powerless ruler, the principal examples, the agency objection, and an alternative. Voices stay. The alternative is not adopted as the definition.

## 4. Organon Reading

Typed cause is what an Agent can record. One edge called cause hides the schema. Permitting is not producing. Precedence is not power. Conditions remain the other list. Not the translation. Not the final pass.
