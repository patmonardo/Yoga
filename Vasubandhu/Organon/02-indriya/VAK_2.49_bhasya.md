# VAK_2.49 Bhāṣya — Six Causes

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāraṇaṃ sahabhūś caiva sabhāgaḥ saṃprayuktakaḥ /
>
> sarvatrago vipākākhyaḥ ṣaḍvidho hetur iṣyate // 2.49 //

> Cause is held to be sixfold: conditioning, co-arisen, homogeneous,
> associated, pervasive, and that called maturation.

## 3. Continuous Translation

It was said that arising generates what is to be generated only
with causes and Conditions. What are these causes, and what are
these Conditions? The verse now names six kinds of *hetu*.

These are the six causes: conditioning, co-arisen, homogeneous,
associated, pervasive, and maturation. First, the conditioning cause:
“Other [dharmas] than itself are the conditioning cause.” For a
conditioned dharma, all dharmas except its own nature are conditioning
causes insofar as they stand without obstructing it.

Objection: Influxes that would arise for someone without knowledge do
not arise for someone with knowledge. Knowledge obstructs their arising,
as sunlight obstructs the visibility of stars. How, then, can every
dharma except itself be a conditioning cause? The qualification is:
non-obstruction is said with respect to what is actually arising.

One might grant causehood through non-obstruction to something capable
of obstructing arising. People likewise say of a ruler who does not
harass them, “We live happily under our lord.” But how can something
with no power to obstruct be a cause? The objection cites nirvāṇa and
things that do not arise in relation to any arising, and inhabitants
of hell in relation to the aggregates of a Form-realm being. Even if
these were absent, they would still be incapable of obstruction. The
reply retains the analogy: people can speak that way even of a ruler
without such power. The designation is made generally.

A principal conditioning cause, however, is also capable of producing:
eye and visible Form in relation to eye-consciousness, food in relation
to the body, and seeds in relation to sprouts. Another objection asks
why everything does not arise at once if all dharmas are causes by
non-obstruction, and whether all then share responsibility for an
act of killing. The reply says they are asserted as causes through
non-obstruction, not as productive agents.

Others instead maintain that every conditioning cause has some efficacy
for every arising. Their example is that even nirvāṇa can mediately
condition eye-consciousness: as an object it gives rise to mental
consciousness, wholesome or unwholesome, and in a succession of causes
eye-consciousness later arises. This is offered as a direction for
understanding other cases. The conditioning cause has been explained.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya first recalls the causal restriction from 2.46 and asks
what the causes and Conditions are. The verse then lists six kinds
of *hetu*, and the commentary takes up the first definition using
the opening half-line of 2.50. The Conditions are not classified here.
The objection from knowledge and obstructed influxes narrows the
non-obstruction formula to what actually arises. A second objection
presses the status of what lacks any power to obstruct. The reply
retains a broad conventional designation, while distinguishing
principal cases that produce. The simultaneous-arising and moral
responsibility objection is answered by denying productive agency
to every conditioning cause. Finally, “others” propose mediated
efficacy through a succession of causes; that is an attributed
alternative, not the preceding reply's own premise.
