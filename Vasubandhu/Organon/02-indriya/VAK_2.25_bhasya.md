# VAK_2.25 Bhāṣya — Always in the Wholesome

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> śraddhāpramādaḥ praśrabdhir upekṣā hrīr apatrapā /
>
> mūladvayam ahiṃsā ca vīryaṃ ca kuśale sadā // 2.25 //

> Faith, heedfulness, pliancy, equanimity, moral shame and moral caution,
> the two roots—non-greed and non-hatred—non-harming, and vigor are
> always present together in wholesome consciousness.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The wholesome is the extensive ground of the wholesome great-ground factors: they always occur in wholesome consciousness. Which factors are these? Faith, heedfulness, pliancy, equanimity, moral shame, moral caution, the two roots, non-harming, and vigor. These ten dharmas are invariably present in wholesome consciousness.

Faith is clarity of consciousness. Others say conviction concerning the truths, the Jewels, and the results of action. Heedfulness is the cultivation of wholesome dharmas. What cultivation is there apart from those dharmas? Attentive care concerning them. Members of another school read in their sūtra, guarding of consciousness.

Pliancy is the workability of consciousness. But is bodily pliancy not also stated in the sūtra? It is stated. It should be understood as bodily feeling is. How, then, is it included among the factors of awakening? In that context, bodily pliancy is the workability of the body. How is it called a factor of awakening? Because it is favorable to the factors of awakening. That workability of the body brings about workability of consciousness, a factor of awakening. Is anything of this kind seen elsewhere? It is. The Blessed One called joy and the dharmas conducive to joy the awakening factor of joy. Aversion and the sign of aversion were called the hindrance of ill will. Right view, intention, and effort were called the wisdom collection (*prajñāskandha*). Intention and effort are not wisdom by nature. Because they are favorable to it, they receive its designation. In the same way, bodily pliancy receives the designation factor of awakening because it is favorable to a factor of awakening.

Equanimity is evenness of consciousness, non-application of consciousness. How can this be reconciled: in that very same consciousness, attention whose nature is application, and equanimity whose nature is non-application? Was it not said that their distinction is difficult to know? Even what is difficult to know can be known. This is exceedingly difficult: no opposition even where there is opposition. Application in one respect, non-application in another: what opposition is there? But then it would follow that the associated factors do not all have one Support (*ekālambana*). Other questions of this kind will also arise. The method applied to those should be understood to apply here too.

Moral shame and moral caution will be explained later. The two roots are the two wholesome roots, non-greed and non-hatred. Non-delusion also exists, but its nature is discriminative comprehension. And discriminative comprehension belongs to the great-ground factors, so it is not designated exclusively a wholesome great-ground factor. Non-harming is non-injury. Vigor is the energetic arousal of consciousness. The wholesome great-ground factors have been stated.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The wholesome ground, then ten factors. Faith has an alternative. Heedfulness has a question and another school's wording. Pliancy has the bodily and mental discussion. Equanimity is an unresolved exchange. Shame and caution are deferred. The roots, non-harming, and vigor are brief.

Others introduces the second faith. Another school introduces guarding. The school is not named. The pliancy and equanimity exchanges carry no local school. Roles are kept. Not every reply is assigned as the final position.

## 5. The Ten

Faith, heedfulness, pliancy, equanimity, moral shame, moral caution, non-greed, non-hatred, non-harming, vigor. Two roots are two members. The sandhi is faith and heedfulness, not negligence.

Every wholesome consciousness. This does not replace the ten of 2.24. That ground is consciousness generally. This ground is the wholesome. Necessary occurrence, not ten successive stages.

Non-delusion is explained, not omitted in silence. Its nature is
discriminative comprehension, already in the universal class. It is
not absent from wholesome consciousness. Nor does the two-root
enumeration say that there are only two wholesome roots in total.

## 6. Faith, Heedfulness, Pliancy

Clarity, and conviction with specified objects. Two reported definitions. Not fused.

Cultivation apart from the wholesome dharmas is attentive care toward them. Guarding is another formulation, not a substitute.

Bodily feeling is invoked without the analogy spelled out. When awakening factors are raised, bodily workability is distinguished from the mental workability it brings about. The examples defend a name through a favorable relation. Intention and effort are not discriminative comprehension by nature. The name does not abolish the difference.

## 7. Equanimity and the Shared Support

Evenness and non-application. Not the neutral-feeling faculty. The word is read by this definition and this class.

Attention is application. Every wholesome consciousness also has equanimity as non-application. Subtlety does not satisfy the objector. Difficulty of knowledge is not an apparent contradiction.

In one respect, in another, then meets the shared-Support objection:
associated factors are supposed to have one Support (*ekālambana*).
The exchange ends by referring to the treatment of similar questions.
That treatment is not supplied here.

A recorded difficulty and a deferral. Not a demonstrated solution.
A solution would still have to account for the shared Support.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Duśalānām is read as wholesome. Nikāyāntarīyāḥ as another school. The duplicated prajñā phrase is given once: favorable, they receive the name. Interpretive, not a collation.

Application keeps ābhoga and anābhoga in opposition. Peacefulness would hide the argument. In one respect is a construal of anyatra. Not a guarantee that the objection is answered.

Shame and caution are not filled from later passages. The deferral is part of this unit. Non-harming and vigor stay the local definitions.

## 9. Interpretation

Range is one question. Distinction inside the range is another.
Heedfulness is not simply the wholesome dharmas again. Bodily
pliancy is not the mental awakening factor it favors. Equanimity
is not attention. Nature, Support, and received
designation remain distinct.

The shared-Support objection concerns the relation of associated
factors to what they attend to; it does not define Support as a
combination of Bases. The universal wisdom factor does not alone
determine wholesomeness. This passage adds a narrower range and its
necessary accompanying factors. The wider Organon account of
Cognition, Perception, and Conception remains to be tested elsewhere.

## 10. Review Status

Reviewed with the kārikā against printed 55.02–56.01. The study
translation expands the two roots and makes co-presence explicit;
the English anchor matches. The Sanskrit note distinguishes
normalized transcription from preserved source defects.

The faith alternatives, another school's guarding formula, pliancy
examples, and equanimity exchange remain in sequence. The shared
Support remains an objection, not a solved reconciliation.
Grammar–Logic checks ten wholesome kinds alongside the universal
ten, without adding non-delusion again or asserting an exclusive total.
