# VAK_2.70 Bhāṣya — Closing the Twelve-Kind Succession Account

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nava cittāni tat ṣaṇṇāṃ nivṛtāt sapta tat tathā /
> caturbhyaḥ śaikṣam asmāt tu pañcāśaikṣaṃ tu pañcakāt // 2.70 //

> [From wholesome formless mind arise] nine minds; that mind arises
> from six. From obscured mind, seven; that mind likewise arises
> from seven. Trainee mind arises from four; from it, five.
> Beyond-training mind arises from that group of five.

The opening completes “from wholesome mind, however” at the end of
2.69. The natural unit here extends from 105.08 through 105.26,
including the opening clause of 2.71:

> tasmāc catvāri cittāni
>
> From that [beyond-training mind arise] four kinds of mind.

The commentary then expressly closes the twelve-kind account. The
refinement into twenty begins at 105.27 and will open 2.71_bhasya.
The verse boundary and the commentary-unit boundary remain distinct.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Nine minds.” Nine kinds of mind arise from wholesome formless-realm
mind, excluding desire-realm wholesome mind and the unobscured-indeterminate
minds of the desire and form realms. “That [arises] from six.”
Wholesome formless-realm mind arises from its own three kinds, from
form-realm wholesome mind, and from trainee and beyond-training mind.

“From the obscured, seven.” From formless-realm obscured mind arise
the three of its own level, form-realm wholesome and obscured mind,
and the two afflicted desire-realm kinds. “That likewise.” It too
arises only from seven, excluding the afflicted minds of the desire
and form realms and trainee and beyond-training mind.

“Trainee mind [arises] from four”: from the wholesome kinds belonging
to the three realms and from trainee mind. “From this, however, five”:
those same four and beyond-training mind. “Beyond-training mind,
however, [arises] from the group of five”: precisely from the group
just stated. “From that, four minds.” Immediately after beyond-training
mind, in turn, arise four kinds: the wholesome minds of the three
realms and beyond-training mind. The twelve kinds of mind are complete.

## 4. The Final Four Profiles

| Focal kind | Predecessors | Successors |
|---|---|---|
| Formless wholesome | Three formless kinds, form wholesome, trainee, beyond-training: **6** | All twelve except desire wholesome, desire unobscured, form unobscured: **9** |
| Formless obscured | Three formless kinds, desire wholesome and unobscured, form wholesome and unobscured: **7** | Three formless kinds, form wholesome and obscured, desire unwholesome and obscured: **7** |
| Trainee | Wholesome kinds of the three realms and trainee: **4** | The same four plus beyond-training: **5** |
| Beyond-training | Wholesome kinds of the three realms, trainee, beyond-training: **5** | Wholesome kinds of the three realms and beyond-training: **4** |

“Unobscured” and “obscured” in this table abbreviate their respective
indeterminate kinds. The five exclusions underlying the formless-obscured
predecessor count are desire unwholesome, desire obscured, form obscured,
trainee, and beyond-training. The twelve-kind inventory thus leaves
exactly seven.

The two seven-member sets are not identical. Desire and form unobscured
mind, together with desire wholesome, occur only in the incoming set;
desire unwholesome and obscured, together with form obscured, occur
only in the outgoing set. Their common members are the three formless
kinds and form wholesome. Equal counts do not establish reversibility.

## 5. Path-Mind and the Scope of the Asymmetry

Trainee mind can immediately precede beyond-training mind. Beyond-training
mind has no trainee mind among its immediate successors. The asymmetry
is explicit, but it concerns immediate succession of these kinds of
mind. It does not by itself settle every question of a person's later
decline from an attainment.

The research study's “irreversible transition” therefore exceeds what
this passage establishes. The chapter's later acquisition account
must be consulted for its own treatment of decline and reacquisition;
that distinct question should not be answered in advance by this
successor list.

Likewise, beyond-training mind does not mean only the arhat's final
mind. The final mental complex remains excluded from immediately
antecedent conditioning under 2.62. The present class-level list
cannot be used to supply a successor to that excluded final occurrence.

## 6. Translation and Source Decisions

The continuous translation restores the three explicit exclusions
from formless wholesome's nine successors, omitted from the research
Bhāṣya translation. It also unfolds *ata evānantaroktāt*, “precisely
from the group just stated,” so that the five predecessors of
beyond-training mind remain tied to the preceding list.

*Ṣaṇṇām* is genitive plural in the kārikā. The translation “from six”
follows the Bhāṣya's explicit ablative enumeration rather than silently
reclassifying the verse's morphology. The final *tat tathā* concerns
the formless obscured predecessor count; it repeats seven, not the
same seven members.

The local Sanskrit retains *ārūpyāvacarātṛ*, repeated *kukśala*,
and the compressed *kukśalācchaikṣāśaikṣābhyāṃ*. The English construes
them through the clear wholesome designation and enumeration. The
anchor applies sandhi in *pañcāśaikṣam*; syntactically these are
*pañca*, completing the trainee successor count, and *aśaikṣam*,
starting the beyond-training predecessor clause.

The four-successor clause belongs to kārikā 2.71, but its commentary
completes the natural unit begun here. Its location is explicitly
marked in the anchor. The following study will identify it as already
translated and begin with the new refinement question. No words of
2.71 are silently reassigned to the text of kārikā 2.70.

The passage supplies counts and lists without attributing an alternative
position or separately explaining each permitted transition. The
translation preserves that expository form and does not introduce
additional voices or unexpressed transition mechanisms.

## 7. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, these closing profiles make direction an
essential part of the relation. A kind's predecessors and successors
must be learned separately even when their counts agree. The formless
obscured profile supplies a precise example: seven on each side,
but different memberships.

The trainee and beyond-training distinction further shows how path
status enters the succession account without replacing its other
determinations. Both have connections with realm-wholesome cognition,
yet their mutual immediate relation is asymmetric. A broader account
of development must preserve that local result while distinguishing
it from acquisition, possession, and the status of the person.

The explicit closure matters for the sequence of study. The twelve
kinds have now been treated in their directed relations. The next
question refines the kinds themselves; the refined succession rules
will require their own account rather than automatic transfer of
every relation in the coarser scheme.
