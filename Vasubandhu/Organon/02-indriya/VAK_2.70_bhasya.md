# VAK_2.70 Bhāṣya — Formless and Uncontaminated Succession

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nava cittāni tat ṣaṇṇāṃ nivṛtāt sapta tat tathā /
>
> caturbhyaḥ śaikṣam asmāt tu pañca aśaikṣaṃ tu pañcakāt // 2.70 //

> Nine minds follow formless wholesome, which follows six. Seven
> follow formless obscured, which likewise follows seven. Trainee mind
> follows four and is followed by five; beyond-training follows that
> group of five.

## 3. Conventional Argument Summary

Nine kinds follow formless wholesome mind, excluding desire wholesome
and the unobscured-indeterminate kinds of desire and Form. That mind
itself follows six: its own three kinds, Form wholesome, trainee, and
beyond-training.

Seven kinds follow formless obscured: its own three, Form wholesome
and obscured, and the two afflicted desire kinds. It also arises
after seven kinds, with the afflicted kinds of desire and Form and
the two uncontaminated kinds excluded.

Trainee mind arises after four: wholesome mind of the three realms
and trainee mind itself. Five follow trainee: those four plus
beyond-training. Beyond-training arises after this five-member group.
The prose then says four follow beyond-training—the three
realm-wholesome kinds and beyond-training itself—and declares the
twelve-kind inventory complete. The last two claims continue the
exposition; they are not predications in the 2.70 verse.

## 4. Interpretation

The verse completes seven directed counts. *Tat ṣaṇṇām* supplies six
predecessors of formless wholesome, while *tat tathā* repeats seven
for the predecessors of formless obscured. *Pañcakāt* refers to the
group from which beyond-training can arise. The Bhāṣya's next line
provides beyond-training's successor count.

## 5. Review Status

The full verse, its antecedent in 2.69, and the seven directed counts
have been checked against the running witness. The four successors
of beyond-training are retained as a prose continuation.
