# VAK_2.35 Bhāṣya — Dissociated Formations

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> viprayuktās tu saṃskārāḥ prāptyaprāptī sabhāgatā /
>
> āsaṃjñikaṃ samāpattī jīvitaṃ lakṣaṇāni ca // 2.35 //

> Formations dissociated from the association of consciousness and its
> mental factors include acquisition and non-acquisition, commonality,
> āsaṃjñika, the two attainments, life, and the marks; the list continues
> with groups of names and related groups at the start of 2.36.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> viprayuktās tu saṃskārāḥ prāptyaprāptī sabhāgatā /
> āsaṃjñikaṃ samāpattī jīvitaṃ lakṣaṇāni ca // 2.35 //
> nāmakāyādayaś ceti /
> ime saṃskārā na cittena asaṃprayuktā na ca rūpasvabhāvā iti cittaviprayuktā ucyante /

The unit is printed 62.11–62.14 in
[the Sanskrit witness](../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt). Word division
is editorial. The opening nāmakāyādayaś ceti of 2.36 completes
this enumeration. The running na cittena asaṃprayuktā is retained:
its double negation conflicts with the class-name. Not associated
is the contextual English construal, not a literal resolution of
the double negative or an independently collated repair.
The acquisition inquiry begins at 62.15.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The formations dissociated from consciousness are acquisition and non-acquisition, commonality, *āsaṃjñika*, the two attainments, life, and the marks. The enumeration continues in the next verse with groups of names and related groups. They are called formations dissociated from consciousness because they are not associated with it [a contextual reading of the witness's defective double negative], yet they are not Form by nature either.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

A new class after consciousness and the mental factors. The continuation across the verse boundary belongs with the class-name. No objection. No school. The definitions follow separately.

## 5. The Enumeration

Acquisition and non-acquisition are a pair. Two attainments. Marks, and groups of names and so forth, are not single entities. The groups are not expanded.

Āsaṃjñika is named apart from the two attainments. The separation is kept. The later definitions are not imported. Non-consciousness is not the English. The look-ahead is not locked.

## 6. Dissociated

The kārikā says *viprayuktāḥ saṃskārāḥ*, without naming the term
from which they are dissociated. At printed 62.14 the Bhāṣya supplies
*citta*, ending its explanation with *cittaviprayuktā ucyante*.
This is a commentary determination of the verse's abbreviated class
name, not an extra word in the kārikā. The transmitted explanation
also has the conflicting *na cittena asaṃprayuktā*; the contextual
reading remains provisional at the level of that sentence.

The previous verse defined citta–caitta association by five equalities:
Basis, Support, manner, time, and individual instance. Dissociated here
means these formations are outside that *specific* association. It
does not say they lack every causal or temporal relation to a mental
continuum, or that they can never occur when consciousness occurs.

Not form. Still formations. Neither qualification makes them unconditioned.

## 7. The Introduction

A class, and its contrast with Form and with associated mental dharmas. Not an argument that each member is a distinct real. That question is the discussions that follow, and first the inquiry into acquisition.

## 8. Boundary

The double negation stays in the Sanskrit. The English follows the class-name. The acquisition inquiry, from among these, first, stays with 2.36.

## 9. Interpretation

The stronger question belongs to the account of *citta* preceding this
classification. At 2.34 the kārikā says *citta*, *manas*, and *vijñāna*
have one meaning. The Bhāṣya gives three verbal derivations and then
attributes another explanation to “others” (*apare*): the same item
is called *manas* as Basis and *vijñāna* as what is supported. These
are the text's naming and functional claims. Neither establishes that
gathering, thinking, and discriminating are one operation, or that
*citta* is an already complete subject prior to those operations.

**Organon correction to test.** Treat *citta* as the achieved
configuration of a cognitive process, with *manas*, *vijñāna*, and
*saṃjñā* requiring distinct functional accounts. On that reading,
2.35's exclusion concerns one specified mode of citta–caitta
conjunction; it cannot settle whether the listed formations condition
the process that yields *citta*. This is our proposed account, not a
translation of *viprayuktāḥ* or a position attributed to Vasubandhu.

Not classified as associated does not mean outside every relation
to an episode or a continuum. The list introduces entries for
examination; its names alone do not establish their distinct reality.
Acquisition is the first inquiry, not an already explained mechanism.

This classification depends on the preceding account of association:
first specify the fivefold relation, then identify formations that do
not enter it and are not Form. A process can include such formations
without making them caittas. The mention of name-groups also prevents
equating everything called a name with a momentary mental factor.
The verse itself does not explain how these formations arise or what
role each plays; those inquiries begin with acquisition.

## 10. Review Status

Reviewed with the kārikā against printed 62.11–62.14. The matching
anchor clarifies dissociation from consciousness. The continuation
from the next verse is identified separately. The conflicting double
negation remains in the Sanskrit, and the English construal is
explicitly provisional rather than presented as literal repair.

Grammar–Logic classifies the named entries without importing later
definitions, flattening duals or plural headings, or asserting an
exhaustive inventory of distinct reals. Dissociated does not mean
unconditioned, Form, or causally unrelated.

The enumeration's continuation and opening acquisition inquiry were
read to establish this unit's boundary.
