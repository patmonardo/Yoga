# VAK_2.36 Bhāṣya — Acquisition, Possession, and the Seed Account

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nāmakāyādayaś ceti prāptir lābhaḥ samanvayaḥ /
>
> prāptyaprāptī svasaṃtānapatitānāṃ nirodhayoḥ // 2.36 //

> And groups of names and so forth complete the preceding list.
> Acquisition is obtaining what was not obtained or had been relinquished,
> and possession of what has been obtained. Acquisition and non-acquisition
> concern conditioned dharmas belonging to one's own continuum and,
> among the unconditioned, the two cessations.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

And groups of names and the rest complete the preceding list. The intervening sentence calls those formations dissociated from consciousness and denies that they have the nature of Form; its conflicting double negative is discussed under 2.35. Among these formations, first: acquisition is obtaining and possession. Acquisition is twofold: obtaining what was not obtained or had been relinquished, and possessing what has been obtained. Non-acquisition is established by the reverse. Of what are acquisition and non-acquisition predicated? Of what belongs to one's own continuum, not another's; no one possesses another's dharmas. Nor are they predicated of what belongs to no sentient continuum; no one possesses what is classed as non-sentient. This is the rule for conditioned dharmas. Among the unconditioned, they concern the two cessations.

All sentient beings possess cessation not through Discrimination. Hence the higher Abhidharma asks, “Who possesses dharmas without *āsrava*?” and answers, “All sentient beings.” Cessation through Discrimination is possessed by all noble persons apart from those covered by the compressed *sakalabandhanādikṣaṇastha* exception [exact scope uncertain], and by some ordinary persons. No one possesses space, so acquisition does not apply to it. Where acquisition does not apply to a dharma, non-acquisition does not apply either: this is the established doctrine.

“Acquisition is some distinct entity.” On what basis? “On the basis of a sūtra: through the arising, obtaining, and possession of these ten dharmas of one beyond training, one becomes noble, with five factors abandoned,” and so forth. Then possession would also apply to non-sentient things and to other beings. Why? Because a sūtra says, “Monks, a wheel-turning king possesses seven treasures,” and so forth. “There the word possession means mastery: his mastery and freedom of action concerning those treasures.” Mastery here, but a distinct entity elsewhere—on what basis? “What is untenable about that?” This: neither its own nature is discerned, as with Form and sound or attachment and hatred, nor its activity, as with the eye and ear. Thus the critic finds it untenable as a real dharma.

If acquisition were a cause of dharmas arising, there would be none for the unconditioned. How would dharmas not acquired, or relinquished through a change of level or dispassion, arise? If their cause is acquisition arising together with them, what work is left for arising, or for arising-of-arising? Among those still fully bound, mild, middling, and intense afflictions would not differ in arising, since acquisition does not differ. Let whatever accounts for that difference also account for their arising. Therefore acquisition is not a cause of arising. “Who said it was? Rather, it is a cause of determining status. Without it, noble and ordinary persons whose consciousness is worldly could not be distinguished as noble and ordinary.” That distinction can follow from the difference between abandoned and unabandoned afflictions. “How will that difference itself be established? Given acquisition, it is established through acquisition's departure or non-departure.” It is established through a difference in the Basis. By the power of the paths of seeing and cultivation, the Basis of noble persons is so transformed that it can no longer produce the afflictions abandoned by those paths. When the Basis has thus ceased to be a seed for the afflictions, like rice burned by fire, the person is said to have abandoned afflictions. Or their seed-capacity has been impaired by a worldly path. The reverse is non-abandonment. Possessing the unabandoned, and not possessing the abandoned, is designated.

Wholesome dharmas are also of two kinds: those occurring without effort and those through effort, called birth-acquired and cultivated through application. One possesses the effortless through the Basis's unimpaired capacity to be their seed. When that capacity is impaired, one is said not to possess them and to have severed wholesome roots. The impairment is to be understood as due to wrong view; it is not a complete eradication of wholesome dharmas' seed-capacity from the continuum. Those occurring through effort, once arisen, are possessed through the continuum's unimpeded mastery of bringing them forth [contextual reading of the damaged mastery clause]. Thus it is the seed itself, not removed, not impaired, or developed at the time of mastery, that receives the name possession—not another entity.

What is the seed? Name-and-Form capable of producing a result, directly or through a succession, through a particular transformation of the continuum. What is transformation? The continuum's becoming otherwise. What is the continuum? Conditioned formations of the three times in a cause-and-result relation. As for the statement, “One possessing greed is incapable of cultivating the four establishments of recollection,” possession there is tolerating greed or not expelling it. For as long as one tolerates or does not expel it, one is [the running witness says *bhavyas*, “capable,” contrary to the quoted *abhavya*, “incapable”] of cultivating them. Thus, on this account, possession in every case is a dharma by designation, not a real dharma. Its negation is non-possession. The Vaibhāṣikas, however, declare both real. Why? “Because this is our established doctrine.”

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

Definition, then range, then a dispute. Scripture, causal work, and noble status are tested. The seed account is the alternative. The Vaibhāṣika reply closes the unit. Designation is the critical account, not a consensus. The seed account is not assigned a school in the local text.

## 5. Obtaining and Range

Not a new event alone. Own continuum for the conditioned. The two cessations for the unconditioned. The quotation says uncontaminated, not unconditioned. It supports universal possession of one cessation. Not every uncontaminated dharma, and not the noble path.

The *sakalabandhanādikṣaṇastha* exception is compressed and remains
unresolved here; the earlier identification of a fully bound person
at the path's initial moment was stronger than this witness secures.
Space is outside both. Non-acquisition is not the complement
of everything. “Where there is no acquisition” here concerns its
applicability to a dharma, not a person's present failure to possess
an otherwise eligible dharma. Otherwise the reverse defined at the
outset would be erased.

## 6. Scripture and Causal Work

The treasures test the inference from the word to a real. Mastery is allowed there. The challenge is what warrants a distinct entity in the other passage. Wording is not denied.

Production meets the unconditioned, the unacquired, co-arising, and degrees. It is disowned. Status remains, because worldly consciousness does not display the path. The critical account has to explain abandonment, not only deny an entity.

## 7. Basis and Seed

Abandonment is the Basis's altered capacity. Noble paths: cannot generate the relevant afflictions again. Worldly path: seed impaired. Burned rice belongs to the first. Effortless wholesome: unimpaired capacity. Wrong view impairs. Not eradicated. Effortful: mastery of arising. Not one claim that every possessed dharma is manifest.

The seed is capable name-and-form. Not a hidden extra beside the continuum. Transformation and continuum are the alternative's terms. Not an agreement with the Vaibhāṣikas.

## 8. Local Uses

The king's mastery, the seed, and tolerating greed are three uses. The critical argument attends to the difference before it concludes designation. The temporal classification opens at 2.37.

## 9. Interpretation

A determination that is not the present event. A real acquisition-dharma, or the Basis's capacity. Both address possession and abandonment. They differ on what must exist.

Attribution is not its ground. Rejecting the entity does not remove the burden. The seed account is the competing ground. The reply stands. Not settled.

**Organon question.** The passage requires a distinction between a
present manifestation and a continuum's capacity: a worldly mental
episode alone does not show whether afflictions can arise again.
The critical account locates that difference in a transformed Basis
and specifies a seed as capable Name-and-Form within a changing
cause-and-result continuum. This gives a process account to test
against the proposal that acquisition is an additional real dharma.
It does not establish that *nāmakāya* from the opening list is the
same as *nāmarūpa* in the seed definition, or settle the broader
architecture of *citta*, *manas*, and *vijñāna*.
