# VAK_2.66 Bhāṣya — Twelve Kinds of Mind

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kuśalākuśalaṃ kāme nivṛtānivṛtaṃ manaḥ /
>
> rūpārūpyeṣv akuśalād anyatra anāsravaṃ dvidhā // 2.66 //

> Mind is fourfold in the desire realm: wholesome, unwholesome,
> obscured-indeterminate, and unobscured-indeterminate. In Form and
> formless realms it has the other three kinds, without the
> unwholesome. Uncontaminated mind is twofold.

## 3. Conventional Argument Summary

Mind and mental factors were identified in general as the immediately
antecedent condition, but the rule for which mind can follow which
has not yet been stated. To prepare that rule, the text enumerates
twelve kinds of mind.

In the desire realm there are four: wholesome, unwholesome,
obscured-indeterminate, and unobscured-indeterminate. In the Form
realm there is no unwholesome kind, leaving three; the same holds in
the formless realm. These ten are contaminated. Uncontaminated mind
is twofold, trainee and beyond-training, making twelve in all.

## 4. Interpretation

The twelve-kind inventory is the substrate for the subsequent
succession rules. The realm exception applies to both higher realms;
it does not remove the wholesome kind. No particular successor
relation has yet been given in this passage.
