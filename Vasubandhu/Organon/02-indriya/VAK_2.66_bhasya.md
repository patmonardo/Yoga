# VAK_2.66 Bhāṣya — Twelve Kinds of Mind

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kuśalākuśalaṃ kāme nivṛtānivṛtaṃ manaḥ /
>
> rūpārūpyeṣv akuśalād anyatra anāsravaṃ dvidhā // 2.66 //

> Mind is fourfold in the Desire Realm: wholesome, unwholesome,
> obscured-indeterminate, and unobscured-indeterminate. In the Form
> and Formless Realms it has the other three kinds, without the
> unwholesome. Mind without *āsrava* is twofold.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Mind and mental factors were called immediately antecedent
Conditions without differentiating their kinds. The rule saying
which kind of mind can follow which has not yet been given; it
will now be stated. First, there are twelve kinds of mind in
summary. Why twelve?

In the Desire Realm mind is fourfold: wholesome, unwholesome,
obscured-indeterminate, and unobscured-indeterminate. The Form
Realm has no unwholesome mind, so it has three kinds; the same
holds for the Formless Realm. These ten kinds have *āsrava*.
Mind without *āsrava* is twofold: belonging to one in training
or to one beyond training. Thus there are twelve kinds of mind.
The text now turns to their succession.

## 4. Reading Control

This is an inventory for the immediately antecedent Condition's
later succession rules. Four Desire Realm kinds, three Form Realm
kinds, three Formless Realm kinds, and two without *āsrava* make
twelve. The absence of the unwholesome kind applies to both
higher Realms. No specific successor relation is yet asserted.
