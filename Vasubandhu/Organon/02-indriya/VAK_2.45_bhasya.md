# VAK_2.45 Bhāṣya — Life and the Marks

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> āyur jīvitam ādhāra ūṣmavijñānayor hi yaḥ /
>
> lakṣaṇāni punar jātir jarā sthitir anityatā // 2.45 //

> Life is lifespan, the support of warmth and Cognition. The marks, in turn,
> are arising, aging, persistence, and impermanence.

Here “support” renders *ādhāra*, the sustaining role argued over in
the Bhāṣya; it is not the technical Support (*ālambana*) or Basis
(*āśraya*).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What is life? Lifespan. For the Abhidharma says, “What is the
Life Faculty? Lifespan of the three Realms.” But this still does
not tell us what dharma lifespan is. It is that which supports
warmth and Cognition. The Blessed One said: “When lifespan,
warmth, and Cognition leave this body, it lies cast aside like
senseless wood.” The dharma supporting warmth and Cognition,
the cause of their persistence, is lifespan.

What now supports lifespan? If these operate in mutual dependence,
which will cease first, causing the other two to cease [contextual
reading of the damaged clause]? They would never cease.
Then karma supports lifespan, since it continues for as long
as karma has projected. Why not accept karma itself as the
support of warmth and Cognition too? Lest all Cognition until
death be maturation. Then let karma support warmth, and warmth
support Cognition. Even so, Cognition in the Formless would
lack support, because warmth is absent there. There karma
can support it. Such arbitrary choice is not allowed: warmth
supporting Cognition in one case and karma in another. And
the earlier objection remains—that all Cognition until death
would be maturation. Therefore lifespan exists as the support
of both.

We do not say it does not exist, but that it is not a distinct
entity. What, then? Projection by karma of the three Realms
of the duration of group same-class. For as long as karma
has projected that group same-class should persist, it persists;
that is called lifespan. It is like the projected ripening
time of crops and the projected duration of a discharged arrow.
If someone thinks a particular quality called an impetus
arises in the arrow and makes it travel until falling, its
being one and the additional condition stated here [clause
damaged] would not explain different times of reaching places
at successively greater speeds, or falling. If wind obstructs
it, it should fall earlier or never fall, since the wind does
not differ. Nevertheless, they maintain that lifespan is
indeed a distinct entity.

Does death occur only through exhaustion of lifespan, or
otherwise too? The *Prajñapti* says, “Death through exhaustion
of lifespan, not through exhaustion of merit.” There are four
alternatives: first, exhaustion of karma whose maturation is
lifespan; second, exhaustion of karma whose maturation is
resources; third, exhaustion of both; fourth, failure to
avoid harmful conditions. Should relinquishing lifespan
also be stated? It need not be: that death is through lifespan
exhaustion and falls under the first alternative. Once
lifespan is exhausted, exhaustion of merit has no further
power to cause death. Thus when both are exhausted, death
is said to be from exhaustion of lifespan.

The *Jñānaprasthāna* also asks whether lifespan should be
said to proceed bound to the continuum, or once arisen
to remain. It gives the first description for Desire-Realm
beings in the entry-status specified with the two attainments
[the quotation's opposed groups are defective], and the
second for the other stated group and Form- and Formless-Realm
beings. What does this mean? The first is damaged when
its Basis is damaged, since it depends on the continuum.
The second has no such damage to its Basis and remains
as arisen. The first is subject to interruption, the second
not, say the Kāśmīra teachers. Therefore untimely death exists.

A sūtra speaks of four acquisitions of embodied existence:
one in which one's own intention operates, but not another's.
There are four alternatives. Only one's own intention operates
for Desire-Realm gods heedless through play or corrupted
in Mind: exhilaration or mental hostility brings their fall
from that place, not another cause. The text then proposes
an additional case because of death by oneself [name damaged].
Only another's intention operates for beings in wombs or
eggs. Both operate for most Desire-Realm beings. Neither
operates for intermediate-state beings, Form- and Formless-Realm
beings, and various Desire-Realm cases: hell beings, inhabitants
of Uttarakuru, those entered into the path of seeing or the
attainments of loving-kindness, cessation, or non-reflection;
then a damaged series of named protected cases, final-existence
beings, a bodhisattva's mother while carrying him, a wheel-turning
king, and his mother while carrying him.

Why, then, does the sūtra answer Śāriputra's question about
beings for whom neither their own nor another's intention
operates by naming those who have reached the Essence of
neither reflection nor non-reflection? In other dhyānas
and Formless levels, it is said, one's own intention means
the noble path of one's level, another's the approach of
the level above; neither occurs there. But would not a
noble path of another level count as another's intention
there too? Then the endpoint is mentioned so that the
preceding cases are understood. Sometimes a beginning
indicates the endpoint too, and sometimes an endpoint
indicates the beginning, as with the Brahmā-group gods
called the first pleasant rebirth and the Radiant gods
called the second.

The reply is that “such as” there introduces an example,
from which the remaining cases are understood; even one
member of that kind can serve as an example. But “such
as” is absent here, so this is no valid extension. If
that expression were only example-introducing, it would
not fit “beings differing in body and reflection, such
as humans and certain gods.” Therefore understand it
as indicating the cases. Enough of this extended discussion.
Life has been explained.

The marks, in turn, are arising, aging, persistence, and
impermanence. These are the four marks of the conditioned.
A dharma in which these occur is marked as conditioned;
the reverse is unconditioned. Arising produces the dharma,
persistence maintains it, aging ages it, and impermanence
destroys it. But the sūtra speaks of three marks of the
conditioned. A fourth should also have been mentioned.
What was not mentioned? Persistence. What, then, of
“alteration of persistence”? That is a synonym for aging,
just as production is for arising and passing away for
impermanence.

The sūtra names as marks those dharmas operating to
move formations through the times, to arouse disquiet.
Arising moves formations from future to present. Aging
and impermanence move them from present to past by
weakening and destroying them. It is said to be like
three enemies confronting a man who entered a thicket:
one draws him out, and two deprive him of life.
Persistence, however, embraces the formations as if
unwilling to part from them. Hence it is not established
as a mark of the conditioned; the unconditioned also
persists in its own characteristic.

Others suppose that persistence and aging are combined
under the single scriptural mark “alteration of persistence.”
For what purpose? Persistence is a basis for attachment,
so it is shown like prosperity accompanied by misfortune,
to prevent attachment to it. Thus there are indeed four
marks of the conditioned.

## 4. Life: Support-Role and Disputed Entity

The support argument, karma objections, projected-duration
alternative, arrow analogy, and renewed distinct-entity
assertion are different stages. The alternative does not
deny lifespan as a designation; the final assertion does
not show that its objections have been answered. Neither
account is silently made the verse's settled ontology.

## 5. Two Fourfold Classifications

| Exhaustion of lifespan-producing karma | Exhaustion of resource-producing karma | Death classification |
|---|---|---|
| Yes | No | Lifespan exhaustion |
| No | Yes | Merit/resource exhaustion |
| Yes | Yes | Both |
| No | No | Harmful conditions not avoided |

The later fourfold own/other-intention classification is
a different matrix. It is not a second naming of these
four causes. The interrupted/uninterrupted explanation
addresses vulnerability of lifespan to damage of its
Basis and supports the Kāśmīra assertion of untimely
death. The protected-case list and quoted entry-status
division remain textually damaged.

## 6. Four Marks and Three Scriptural Designations

The verse lists arising, aging, persistence, impermanence.
The causal gloss orders them arising, persistence, aging,
impermanence. The first reconciliation excludes persistence
from the scriptural three because that presentation stresses
transition and disquiet. The alternative combines persistence
and aging under “alteration of persistence.” These are
different explanations, not one merged account.

The secondary marks and their regress problem begin 2.46.
The later Sautrāntika stream interpretation should not
be promoted into this verse's continuous translation.

## 7. Reading Control

The Bhāṣya first argues that lifespan sustains warmth and Cognition.
The critic accepts the sustaining role but explains lifespan as a
duration projected by karma, without a further entity. The reply
reasserts a distinct entity. The death classifications then test
how duration may end and whether interruption is possible; they do
not by themselves decide the entity dispute. In the final section,
the verse gives four marks while a cited sūtra gives three. Two
different reconciliations are reported and kept separate.
