# VAK_2.15 Bhāṣya — Cessation of Faculties at Death

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirodhayaty uparamān nārūpye jīvitaṃ manaḥ /
>
> upekṣāṃ caiva rūpe 'ṣṭau kāme daśa navāṣṭau vā // 2.15 //

> One dying in the formless realm brings to cessation life, mind, and neutral feeling. In the form realm, eight: those three and the five beginning with the eye. In the desire realm, ten, nine, or eight, according to sexual characteristic: ten with both sex faculties, nine with one, eight with neither.

The opening follows the commentary's question about dying. Uparamān nārūpye is kept as uncertain. The death unit continues into the next verse's first clauses: in gradual death, four; with a wholesome mind, everywhere five in addition. Those complete the counts. The fruit inquiry is not this unit.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Loss must now be explained. When dying in a given realm, how many faculties does one bring to cessation? At death in the formless realm, one brings life, mind, and neutral feeling to cessation; in the form realm, eight. One dying in the form realm brings eight to cessation: those three and the five beginning with the eye. For all beings there are spontaneously born. They arise and die with their faculties complete.

In the desire realm, ten, nine, or eight. One dying in the desire realm with both sexual characteristics brings ten faculties to cessation: those eight together with the female and male faculties. One with a single sexual characteristic brings nine. One with neither, eight. This is the rule for death occurring all at once. But in gradual death, four. One dying gradually brings four faculties to cessation: body, life, mind, and neutral feeling. For these do not cease separately. This rule is to be understood of dying with an afflicted or indeterminate mind.

When, however, one dies with a wholesome mind, then everywhere five in addition. In every case, one dying with a wholesome mind brings to cessation the faculties already stated, and the five beginning with faith in addition. For these necessarily occur in a wholesome mind. Thus in the formless realm one brings eight to cessation, and in the form realm thirteen. The remaining counts are to be worked out in detail in this way.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

Acquisition to loss. The prose explains the realm counts, then simultaneous against gradual, then the quality of the last mind. No opponent. No school.

Stopping at the numbered verse would leave the conditions unstated. Fruit-attainment is a new inquiry.

## 5. Realm and Mode of Dying

Formless: life, mind, neutral feeling. Form adds the five, eight. Desire, all at once: neither, one, or both marks, eight, nine, or ten.

Form-realm completeness is stated for that context. All beings there is not an account of every bodily history.

Gradual death ends on four. They do not cease separately, in that terminal context. Not one substance. Not every change in one a change in the others.

## 6. The Wholesome-Mind Addition

The earlier rule is afflicted or indeterminate mind. Wholesome mind adds faith, vigor, mindfulness, concentration, and Discrimination, because the text says they necessarily occur there.

| Case | Stated | With wholesome mind |
|---|---:|---:|
| Formless | 3 | 8 |
| Form | 8 | 13 |
| Desire, both marks | 10 | 15 |
| Desire, one | 9 | 14 |
| Desire, neither | 8 | 13 |

Eight and thirteen are recited. The other wholesome counts are the instruction to add five, not separately recited.

## 7. Cessation Here and Abandonment Earlier

The five cease at a wholesome death. That does not make them unwholesome, and it does not make them objects of abandonment. 2.13 classified what is to be abandoned. This counts cessation at death.

Not the reverse of 2.14. That count was initial acquisition as maturation. This includes mind and neutral feeling, and, in the wholesome case, the five. A shared realm does not make the inventories identical.

Nirodha here is the end of this life in faculty terms. It is not the cessation of every future continuation.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Tyāga is loss. Nirodhayati is brings to cessation. The subject is the dying person. The causative is not by itself a deliberate act.

Sakṛnmaraṇa is death all at once. Kramamṛtyu is gradual death. The second opens the next verse and completes this count. The cross-verse continuation is marked.

Śubhe is kuśale cetasi, with a wholesome mind. Pañca ca is five in addition. Avyākṛta is indeterminate. Upekṣā is neutral feeling. The two neutralities stay apart.

## 9. Organon Reading

The count depends on realm, marks, mode of dying, and the last mind. Detach those and the proposition is gone.

Initial maturation, possession, cessation at death, and abandonment are not interchangeable inventories. The five are the case: they add to the terminal count and remain wholesome.

Membership and consequence, at the boundary. Which possessed members cease. Not the inverse of first acquisition. Not the verse English.
