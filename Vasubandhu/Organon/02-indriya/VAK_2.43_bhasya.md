# VAK_2.43 Bhāṣya — The Attainment of Cessation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirodhākhyā tathaiveyaṃ vihārārthaṃ bhavāgrajā /
>
> śubhā dvivedyāniyatā cāryasyāpyā prayogataḥ // 2.43 //

> This attainment called cessation is likewise for abiding and is born of
> the summit of existence. It is wholesome, experienced at two times or
> unfixed, belongs to a noble person, and is acquired through application.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What is the attainment of cessation? This, called cessation, is likewise. Likewise the non-reflective attainment: likewise carries cessation of consciousness and the mental factors. The difference is for abiding. Entered by attention preceded by the conception of peaceful abiding. The other, by the conception of escape. That one is of the fourth dhyāna. This is born of the summit of existence, the essence of neither reflection nor non-reflection only. Wholesome. Not afflicted, not indeterminate. To be experienced in the next rebirth or a later life, and unfixed with respect to maturation. It may not mature, if one attains final nirvāṇa here. Four Bases at the summit.

Exclusively a noble person's. Ordinary persons cannot produce it, from fear of annihilation, from the power of the noble path, and from resolve toward nirvāṇa in this life. Not obtained by dispassion. Acquired through application. Past not acquired. Future not cultivated. Cultivation is through the power of consciousness.

Not so for the Blessed One. Acquired through awakening, together with the knowledge of exhaustion. Nothing of the Buddhas is called acquired through application. Their qualities manifest by the wish. Acquired, for them, through complete dispassion. Liberated in both respects by mastery, as if produced. Western teachers: produced earlier, as a trainee. Not beforehand, the Kāśmīra teachers. Thirty-four moments, it is said. No dissimilar consciousness in the interval. The outer regions keep the pledge as one course. The Kāśmīra teachers keep the former account.

## 4. Movement and Voice

Purpose, level, ethical class, maturation, eligibility, acquisition. Then the Buddha. Mastery, the western proposal, Upagupta, the Kāśmīra count, the outer-region pledge. The close records that the Kāśmīra teachers keep their account. Not an agreement.

## 5. Likewise

One feature carried. Not every property. Peaceful abiding, not escape. Summit, not the fourth dhyāna. Noble, not ordinary. Final nirvāṇa is invoked as the reason maturation may not occur. The attainment is not identified with it.

## 6. Maturation and Application

Two times are next rebirth and a later life. Unfixed is not the ethical indeterminate. The attainment is wholesome. Four Bases against the five of the other result. Application, not dispassion. The exclusion of past and future is the mode of obtaining. The power of consciousness explains cultivation. It does not say the preparation lacks consciousness.

## 7. The Dispute

Mastery is not prior production. The western account posits the earlier production. They answer one difficulty by different claims. Thirty-four is sixteen and eighteen. Kila stays. The pledge and emerging are not one action. The uncertain sentence is not repaired. Not settled.

## 8. Interpretation

Shared description is not shared purpose, path, result, or acquisition. Suspension of consciousness is not liberation. The verse's vihāra is abiding. The academy is not this gloss. This nirodha is the attainment. The nirodha of virodha is the look-ahead, not what the verse says is ceased. Not the translation. Not the final pass.
