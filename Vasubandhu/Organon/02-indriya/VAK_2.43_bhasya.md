# VAK_2.43 Bhāṣya — The Attainment of Cessation

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirodhākhyā tathaiveyaṃ vihārārthaṃ bhavāgrajā /
>
> śubhā dvivedyāniyatā cāryasyāpyā prayogataḥ // 2.43 //

> The attainment called cessation is likewise cessation of consciousness
> and the mental factors. It is entered for peaceful abiding and belongs
> to the summit of existence. It is wholesome; its maturation may be
> experienced in the next rebirth or a later existence, or may not occur.
> It is restricted to noble persons and is acquired through application.

## 3. Continuous Translation

### 2.43 explanation

What is the attainment of cessation? This, called cessation, is
likewise—like non-reflective attainment. What feature does “likewise”
carry? Cessation of consciousness and mental factors. Its difference
is this: it is for abiding. One enters it through attention preceded
by reflection on peaceful abiding; the other is entered through
reflection on escape. That one belongs to the fourth dhyāna;
this one is born of the summit of existence, belonging only to
the level of the Essence of neither reflection nor non-reflection.
It is wholesome only, neither afflicted nor indeterminate.
Although wholesome, its maturation is to be experienced at two
times, and is unfixed: in the next rebirth or a later existence,
and unfixed with respect to maturation, since sometimes it does
not mature if one attains final nirvāṇa here. For its maturation
at the summit of existence comprises four Bases.

It is exclusively for a noble person. Ordinary persons cannot
produce it because they fear annihilation, because its production
depends on the power of the noble path, and because it involves
resolve toward nirvāṇa in this life. Even for a noble person it
is not obtained by dispassion. How, then? It is acquired through
application only. A past instance is not acquired, nor is a future
instance cultivated, since that cultivation depends on the power
of consciousness.

### Inherited 2.44 look-ahead

Is it acquired through application even for the Blessed One? No:
for the Sage it is acquired through awakening. Buddhas obtain it
at the same time as Knowledge of exhaustion. Nothing of the
Buddhas is called application-acquired; manifestation of all their
excellent qualities depends only on their wish. Thus for them
it is acquired through complete dispassion.

How, then, is the Blessed One established as liberated in both
respects at Knowledge of exhaustion, without having produced
the attainment of cessation? Through mastery over it, as though
it had been produced. Western teachers say that the bodhisattva
produces it beforehand, while still in training. Why is that
not accepted? Then this guiding statement of the elder Upagupta
would also be authoritative: “Having produced the attainment of
cessation, he produces Knowledge of exhaustion”—this is to be
said of the Tathāgata [quotation's construction incomplete].

“Not beforehand,” say the Kāśmīra teachers, because earlier
production is untenable. Why? Because awakening is attained in
thirty-four moments. The bodhisattva, it is said, attained awakening
in thirty-four consciousness-moments: sixteen for direct realization
of the truths and eighteen for dispassion toward the summit of
existence, through production of nine uninterrupted and nine
liberation paths abandoning the nine grades of affliction. These
make thirty-four. Since he enters certainty already dispassionate
toward the Essence of nothingness, lower-level afflictions do not
remain to be abandoned. There is no possibility of dissimilar
consciousness in this interval, so the attainment of cessation
is excluded.

What would follow if dissimilar consciousness were made manifest
in between? There would be an intention to emerge, but bodhisattvas
intend not to emerge. True, they intend not to emerge, but this
does not mean non-emergence from the path [contextual rendering
of a damaged clause]. How, then, is the pledge understood:
“I will not break the cross-legged posture before attaining
exhaustion of the contaminants”? The teachers of the outer regions
say it means not departing from this resolve, completing the
whole purpose in one course. The Kāśmīra teachers maintain the
former account.

## 4. Movement and Voice

Purpose, level, ethical class, maturation, eligibility, acquisition. Then the Buddha. Mastery, the western proposal, Upagupta, the Kāśmīra count, the outer-region pledge. The close records that the Kāśmīra teachers keep their account. Not an agreement.

## 5. Likewise

One feature carried. Not every property. Peaceful abiding, not escape. Summit, not the fourth dhyāna. Noble, not ordinary. Final nirvāṇa is invoked as the reason maturation may not occur. The attainment is not identified with it.

## 6. Maturation and Application

Two times are next rebirth and a later life. Unfixed is not the ethical indeterminate. The attainment is wholesome. Four Bases against the five of the other result. Application, not dispassion. The exclusion of past and future is the mode of obtaining. The power of consciousness explains cultivation. It does not say the preparation lacks consciousness.

## 7. Cross-Verse Continuation: The Dispute

This inherited continuation belongs to the first half of 2.44,
not the words of 2.43. Mastery without prior production and
earlier production while in training are distinct replies.
The western proposal, Upagupta appeal, Kāśmīra objection, and
outer-region response remain in sequence.

The Kāśmīra count is 16 + 18 = 34; the eighteen are nine
uninterrupted/liberation-path pairs. *Kila* preserves the reportive
force. The argument also depends on prior dispassion toward
the Essence of nothingness and the absence of intervening
dissimilar consciousness, not arithmetic alone. The disputed
non-emergence clause is not a secure restored reading; the
pledge concerns resolve and posture as argued by the speakers,
not a settled permission to interrupt.

## 8. Reading Control

“Likewise” carries only cessation of consciousness and mental
factors from the other attainment. The Bhāṣya then differentiates
purpose, level, maturation, eligible practitioner, and manner of
acquisition. Final nirvāṇa is invoked to explain why maturation
may fail to occur; it is not equated with this attainment.
The Buddha-acquisition dispute displayed above is a 2.44
continuation and should be evaluated with that verse.
