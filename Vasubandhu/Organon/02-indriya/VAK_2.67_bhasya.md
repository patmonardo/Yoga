# VAK_2.67 Bhāṣya — Desire-Realm Predecessors and Successors

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kāme nava śubhāc cittāc cittāny aṣṭābhya eva tat /
> daśabhyo 'kuśalaṃ tasmāc catvāri nivṛtaṃ tathā // 2.67 //

> In the desire realm, nine kinds of mind arise from wholesome mind;
> that wholesome mind arises only from eight. Unwholesome mind arises
> from ten; from it, four. Obscured mind is likewise.

The natural unit is 103.19–104.08. The Bhāṣya specifies immediate
succession, supplies the members counted, and explains the relevant
cross-realm contexts. The unobscured-indeterminate account begins
with 2.68 at 104.09. The counts concern kinds within the twelvefold
classification, not several minds arising simultaneously.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“In the desire realm, nine minds [arise] from wholesome mind.”
“Immediately afterward” will be stated later. Nine kinds of mind
arise immediately after wholesome desire-realm mind: the four belonging
to its own level; two belonging to the form realm—wholesome at the
time of attainment, and obscured at rebirth-linking; only obscured
formless-realm mind, at rebirth-linking, but not wholesome, because
of its excessive remoteness. For the formless attainments are remote
from the desire realm through four kinds of remoteness: support,
aspect, object, and antidote. [The remaining two are] trainee and
beyond-training mind.

“That itself [arises] only from eight.” Wholesome desire-realm mind,
in turn, arises immediately after eight kinds: the four of its own
level; two form-realm kinds—from wholesome mind at emergence, and
from obscured mind when someone oppressed by an afflicted attainment
resorts to the wholesome supporting level; and from trainee and
beyond-training minds at emergence.

“Unwholesome [mind arises] from ten,” excluding trainee and beyond-training
mind. For, by way of rebirth-linking in the desire realm, unwholesome
mind arises immediately after all the form- and formless-realm minds.
“From it, four.” Immediately after unwholesome mind arise only the
minds belonging to its own level. As stated for unwholesome mind
in the desire realm, “obscured mind is likewise”: immediately after
ten kinds only, and from it, in turn, four only.

## 4. Resolving the Counts

| Desire-realm focal kind | Possible predecessor kinds | Possible successor kinds |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesome | Four desire kinds; form wholesome and obscured; trainee and beyond-training: **8** | The same eight kinds, plus formless obscured: **9** |
| Unwholesome | All ten contaminated kinds: **10** | Four desire kinds: **4** |
| Obscured-indeterminate | All ten contaminated kinds: **10** | Four desire kinds: **4** |

The ten predecessors of unwholesome mind comprise four desire, three
form, and three formless kinds. Excluding trainee and beyond-training
from the twelve establishes that membership. The prose singles out
rebirth-linking to explain the higher-realm predecessors; it does
not require every within-desire-realm transition in the list to be
a rebirth transition.

*Nivṛtaṃ tathā* transfers both counts to obscured-indeterminate mind.
It gives that kind the same predecessor and successor sets here,
without making it ethically identical to unwholesome mind. The fourth
desire-realm kind, unobscured-indeterminate, awaits the next verse.

## 5. The Contexts and the Asymmetry

Desire wholesome → form wholesome is explained through entry into
attainment; form wholesome → desire wholesome through emergence.
The form-obscured predecessor has a different explanation: a person
oppressed by an afflicted attainment resorts to a wholesome supporting
level. This should not be flattened into a rule that affliction
as such automatically produces wholesome cognition.

Higher-realm obscured successors of desire wholesome are explained
through rebirth-linking. Formless wholesome is excluded from that
successor list because the formless attainments are too remote from
the desire realm in support, aspect, object, and antidote. The local
prose names these four respects without separately explaining each;
the translation does not supply an unmarked theory of their distances.

Formless obscured occurs among the nine successors but not among the
eight predecessors of desire wholesome. This is the precise difference
between the two lists. Permitting one direction does not license its
reverse. Nor does a realm classification alone determine the transition:
the kind of mind and the occasion also matter.

## 6. Translation and Source Decisions

The commentary opens by supplying *anantaram*, “immediately afterward,”
from later in the verse sequence. That explicit syntactic note is
restored in the continuous translation. The immediate relation applies
to all the counts, even where the verse leaves it understood.

The source's *svabhūmika* is rendered “belonging to its own level”
in the continuous translation. In these counts it picks out the
four desire-realm kinds, rather than introducing four additional
meditative levels. *Śubha* in the verse is explicitly explained as
*kuśala*, wholesome. The local *nivṛta* spelling is retained for
obscured-indeterminate mind, as established in 2.66.

At 103.27–28, *ādhārakuśalabhūmisaṃśrayaṇa* is rendered “resorts to
the wholesome supporting level.” It does not itself say that the
person has been reborn in a lower realm. The phrase explains a
return from afflicted attainment; rebirth-linking is separately named
in other clauses. *Vyutthāna* is consistently rendered “emergence,”
including emergence after trainee or beyond-training cognition.

The numbers describe admissible relations between kinds across the
relevant circumstances. They do not promise that every particular
occurrence has every listed successor available regardless of the
person's attainment or situation. The continuous translation follows
the source's “arise”; the analysis makes this scope explicit.

## 7. Philosophical and Organon Study

The preceding classification made the kinds distinguishable. This
passage now supplies directed relations between them and identifies
occasions that explain particular connections. As Organon interpretation,
this is the further work that genus membership alone could not perform.

The difference between eight predecessors and nine successors makes
the point concrete. Knowing which kind of mind is present does not
yet give one undirected list of related kinds. We must distinguish
what can precede it from what can follow it, and then understand
why attainment, emergence, or rebirth permits the connection.

The text thereby advances from naming determinations to explaining
their succession. A comparison with mediation can use that movement
without treating these counts as a complete theory of rational
subjectivity or a modern executable model.
