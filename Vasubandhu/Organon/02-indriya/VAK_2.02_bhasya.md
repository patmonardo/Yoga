# VAK_2.02 Bhāṣya — Governing Apprehension, and the Body-Portion

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> svārthopalabdhyādhipatyāt sarvasya ca ṣaḍindriyam /
>
> strītvapuṃstvādhipatyāt tu kāyāt strīpuruṣendriye // 2.02 //

> The five are Faculties by governing apprehension of their own meanings;
> mind is a Faculty by governing apprehension of all meanings. The female
> and male Faculties are separately designated within the body-faculty
> because they govern female-being and male-being.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Others, however, say: it is not by the eye and ear themselves that embodied existence is preserved, since danger is avoided after it has been cognized. Their governing efficacy concerns cognition. Nor is seeing visible form or hearing sound something other than cognition, such that a separate governing efficacy could properly be assigned to them as specific causes. Therefore their status as faculties is not established in this way. How, then?

The six are faculties: the five govern apprehension of their own respective meanings, and mind governs apprehension of all meanings. The five beginning with the eye each have governing efficacy in apprehending their own meaning. Mind has governing efficacy in apprehending all meanings. Therefore each of these six is a faculty.

But do not the meanings also have governing efficacy here? No: governing efficacy is superior power. The eye has greater mastery in the apprehension of visible form, because it is a common cause in the apprehension of all visible forms, and because apprehension follows its sharpness, dullness, and so forth. Visible form lacks that superior power: the reverse applies to it. The same explanation extends through the series, up to mind in relation to dharmas.

The female and male faculties, however, are designated from the body because they govern female-being and male-being. They are established separately from the body-faculty itself, but are not different entities from it. A particular portion of the body-faculty, the genital region, receives the name of female or male faculty because it governs female-being or male-being, respectively. Female-being comprises a woman's form, voice, movement, and intentions; this is what the account calls her female-being. Male-being comprises a man's form, voice, movement, and intentions; this is what it calls his male-being.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

Others, however, say marks a challenge to the preceding explanation. It does not deny the eye and ear. The two reasons lead to how, then, and the kārikā answers by governance of apprehension.

A second objection tests that answer. If apprehension depends on meanings, why are the meanings not faculties? The reply refines ādhipatya as superior power. The last movement distinguishes two faculties inside the body-faculty by the function of a portion.

The opening alternative is unnamed. The objection about meanings is unattributed. Neither voice is assigned to a school here.

## 5. Why the Fourfold Account Is Challenged

The first criticism separates governing efficacy in cognition from the preservation that follows cognition of danger. The eye and ear do not receive a further governing function merely because avoidance helps preserve embodied existence.

The second criticism is duplication. Seeing and hearing are not operations added to the corresponding cognitions. Production of cognition and specific causation of seeing or hearing are not two functions unless that distinction stands.

The text does not here refute attractiveness point by point. The stated criticisms are not expanded into a refutation of all four matters.

## 6. Why the Faculty Governs Apprehension

The five govern apprehension of their own meaning. Mind governs apprehension of all meanings. All is the range. It does not say that every mental cognition apprehends all meanings at once, or that the apprehension is invariably correct.

Dependence alone does not settle faculty. Superior power is specified by two reasons. The eye is a common cause across the apprehension of visible form. Apprehension conforms to the eye's sharpness, dullness, and the rest. The first is not one permanent eye under every visual event. The second is not a denial that the meaning can affect apprehension.

Visible form lacks that superior mastery, though it remains a condition of apprehension. The same reasoning extends through the series, ending with mind in relation to dharmas. That application stays beside mind's all-meaning range. The two are not substituted for one another.

## 7. Separate Designation Within the Body-Faculty

The female and male faculties govern female-being and male-being. They are separately established, and they are not different entities from the body-faculty. The bearer is a portion, the genital region. The governed determination is wider: form, voice, movement, intention.

This is the transmitted account of sexed embodiment. It is not converted into a contemporary theory.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Svārtha is own meaning. The repeated svasya svasya makes the distribution explicit. Upalabdhi is apprehension. This passage does not rank it below cognition.

Ādhipatya remains governing efficacy. Adhikaṃ prabhutvam is superior power, not a second word for cause.

Kāyāt is read through kāyendriyād eva, from the body-faculty itself. Separately established supplies pṛthak vyavasthāpyete. Separate from the body would hide the denial of another entity.

Ceṣṭā is movement. Abhiprāya is intention. The repairs of the eye-mastery sentence and the compressed list remain in the witness.

## 9. Interpretation

For the Organon, this verse tests the **Principles Engine → Rules Engine** transition at a more exacting point. The unnamed alternative narrows sensory governance to apprehension; the reply then distinguishes a faculty's superior power from the meaning's role in the same event. The text does not deny that meanings condition apprehension. Mind's “all” specifies its range, while the female and male faculties are separately designated within a portion of the body-faculty. These are textual constraints on the Rules Engine reading, not expressions of that project vocabulary in the kārikā.

## 10. Review Status

The single kārikā anchor matches the Bhāṣya's two determinations: apprehension governed by the six, then female-being and male-being governed by faculties designated within the body-faculty. *Artha* remains “meaning”; “Cognition” is used where the objection says *vijñāna*. The conventional translation covers the objection and reply through printed 39.17.

The eye-mastery repair and the compressed sexed list remain provisional. The verse beginning 2.03 remains outside this review.
