# VAK_2.24 Bhāṣya — In Every Consciousness

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vedanā cetanā saṃjñā cchandaḥ sparśo matiḥ smṛtiḥ /
>
> manaskāro 'dhimokṣaś ca samādhiḥ sarvacetasi // 2.24 //

> Feeling, volition, reflection, wish to perform, contact, discriminative
> comprehension, mindfulness, attention, resolution, and concentration are
> all present together in every moment of consciousness.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Which factors occur in every consciousness? Feeling, volition, reflection, wish to perform, contact, discriminative comprehension, mindfulness, attention, resolution, and concentration. These ten dharmas, it is said, are present together in every moment of consciousness. Feeling is threefold experience: pleasant, painful, or neither painful nor pleasant. Volition is the formative activity of consciousness, mental action. Reflection is recognition, the taking up of the Condition's distinguishing mark [a provisional reading of the damaged gloss]. Wish to perform is the wish to act. Contact is the touching born from the conjunction of Faculty, Condition, and Cognition. Discriminative comprehension is the examination of dharmas. Mindfulness is not losing the Support. Attention is the application of consciousness; resolution is settling upon; concentration is the one-pointedness of consciousness.

The distinction between consciousness and mental factors is subtle. It is difficult to delimit even in their streams, all the more so in individual moments. Even among certain medicinal plants possessing Form and having many tastes, differences of taste apprehensible by the Faculties are difficult to determine. How much more so with dharmas that are without Form and apprehensible by understanding.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The question introduces the first class named in 2.23. Ten names. Complete co-presence. A definition of each. Then the subtlety.

Kila keeps the report. It may mark distance. The particle alone is not a rejection. No school is named. The analogy is not a rival list. It is the difficulty inside the account just given.

## 5. What the Universal Claim Requires

All ten together in every moment. Stronger than each occurring somewhere in a stream, and stronger than skills acquired later. Not ten successive operations.

Feeling's three are alternatives. Universal feeling is not three experiences in one moment. That is not the earlier possession of several feeling-faculties.

Occurrence, not equal strength, correctness, or ethical quality. Prajñā under mati is not liberating knowledge. Samādhi is not attainment of an absorption. No scale of intensity is given.

## 6. Distinctions within the Ten

Volition is formative activity and mental action. Wish to perform is the wish to act. One word for both would lose the difference. Wish to perform is not craving.

Reflection takes up the Condition's distinguishing mark, under the
provisional construal. Discriminative comprehension examines dharmas.
Related, not interchangeable. The damaged gloss limits the wording,
not the distinction of the two entries.

Contact is born of the conjunction of Faculty, Condition, and Cognition.
It is not confined to bodily touch. This sentence does not decide
whether the Condition is externally established.

Mindfulness is non-loss of the Support (*ālambana*). Attention is application. Resolution is settling upon, a short gloss, not a theory of decision. Concentration is one-pointedness. The four do not collapse into attention.

## 7. The Difficulty

Subtle even in streams. Harder in a moment. Naming ten does not make ten obvious appearances.

The plants are the more accessible case: possessing Form and
Faculty-apprehensible, yet still hard to distinguish. The factors
are without Form and apprehended by understanding. Difficulty,
not impossibility. The definitions are neither withdrawn nor proved
by the subtlety. Three claims stay apart: the universal assertion,
the definitions, and the difficulty of seeing the differences.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

The definitions stay one passage. The order follows the verse. No then is inserted.

Mindfulness is non-loss of the Support, not only memory of a past event. Concentration is one-pointedness in the reported universal context, not only perfected practice.

Ground and Support stay apart. Every consciousness is the operating
range. That does not make every consciousness a single common Support
for all ten.

## 9. Interpretation

A moment of consciousness is already qualified, formative, reflective, directed, and discriminative. The factors are distinguished inside the co-occurrence. Not successive additions to a bare consciousness.

Range, definition, and the ground of the distinction have to travel together. Universality does not say what makes mindfulness different from attention, or attention from concentration.

Presence in every consciousness is not adequacy. Nor does the list
say that no other mental factors can accompany a consciousness.
The numerical inventory is of ten universal factor-kinds, not a
maximum total for each episode.

Some of these factors are also named as Faculties elsewhere in this
chapter: feeling, mindfulness, concentration, and discriminative
comprehension. This ten-factor list classifies what accompanies every
consciousness; it does not turn all ten into Faculties. The contact
definition names a Faculty alongside Condition and Cognition, without
making that Faculty identical to the contact-factor itself. The wider
Organon account of Cognition, Perception, and Conception is not
established by this list.
