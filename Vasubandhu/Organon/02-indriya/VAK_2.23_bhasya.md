# VAK_2.23 Bhāṣya — Consciousness and the Factors

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> cittaṃ caittāḥ sahāvaśyaṃ sarvasaṃskṛtalakṣaṇaiḥ /
>
> prāptyā vā pañcadhā caittā mahābhūmyādibhedataḥ // 2.23 //

> Consciousness and mental factors necessarily arise together. All
> conditioned dharmas arise together with the marks of the conditioned.
> As for acquisition, only those designated as belonging to a sentient
> being arise together with it. Mental factors
> are fivefold by divisions beginning with the great ground.

Word division is editorial. Sahavaśyaṃ is read as sahāvaśyaṃ. Satvākhyam as sattvākhyam. Gativiśayaḥ as gativiṣayaḥ. Eśām as eṣām. The fifth class-name is kept. No independent collation is claimed.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The rule for the co-arising of Form dharmas has been stated. The rule for the remaining dharmas must now be given. First, consciousness and mental factors necessarily arise together: neither can occur without the other. Every conditioned dharma also arises together with the marks of the conditioned. This applies to whatever arises, whether Form, consciousness, mental factors, or formations dissociated from consciousness. The verse then says “or with acquisition.” Here “or” distinguishes a narrower case: only what is designated as belonging to a sentient being arises together with acquisition.

Mental factors have been mentioned. What are they? They fall into five classes: great-ground, wholesome great-ground, affliction great-ground, unwholesome great-ground, and limited-affliction great-ground factors. A “ground” is a range of operation: whatever is a factor's range of operation is called its ground. Great-ground factors have an extensive range, for they occur in every consciousness.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The Form rule is recalled. Three co-arising statements follow, different subjects, different scopes. Then the five classes, and the word ground.

No objection. No school. The question is expository. The first class is defined. Its members wait. The other four are named. Their distributions wait.

## 5. Consciousness and Mental Factors

Reciprocal. Consciousness does not occur without factors. Factors do not occur without consciousness. Not an independent consciousness, factors added later.

Not every factor in every consciousness. The general rule is consciousness and its accompanying factors. The classes distribute the particular factors. Only the great-ground class is said here to occur in every consciousness.

Inseparability is not a direction of production. Consciousness produces the factors, the factors produce consciousness, and neither without the other are different claims. This passage states the last.

## 6. Marks and Acquisition

The marks accompany whatever conditioned thing arises. Form, consciousness, factors, dissociated formations. The unconditioned, excluded from arising in 2.22, is not brought back.

The marks are not enumerated. No theory of their activity is inserted.

Acquisition is narrower. Only what is designated as belonging to a sentient being, nothing else. Acquisition is the term. Not a deliberate obtaining, and not personal ownership.

Or distinguishes the cases. It does not substitute acquisition for the marks, and it does not make acquisition optional where the condition holds. A dharma in the sentient range falls under both rules.

## 7. Ground and the Five Names

Ground is range of operation. The consciousnesses in which the factors occur. Not a place. Not automatically a meditative level or a path-stage.

Great is breadth: every consciousness. That does not make the class wholesome. The wholesome class stays distinct.

The five names are kept. Five names are not yet five exclusive sets. Members and ranges wait.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Everything with the marks governs the compact line. Not merely with all the marks as a further qualification of consciousness. Necessarily together supplies the predicate.

Caitta and caitasika are mental factor. Viprayukta is dissociated formation, from the five classes of 2.22. Not unrelated to every other dharma.

Vikalpa here is a distinction of cases, the explanation of or. Not imaginative construction. Ground follows the local definition.

## 9. Interpretation

Three relations. Shared language of together does not erase the scopes.

The movement into mental factors changes the question from the
Faculty census to the conditions of an episode of consciousness.
Reciprocal accompaniment is not an all-to-all relation between every
factor and every consciousness; universality belongs to the
great-ground kinds, not to arbitrary factor-tokens.

Faculty and mental factor are different classifications. The preceding
study locates the five beginning with faith among mental factors, while
the bodily Faculties and life have other placements. This co-arising
rule does not require every Faculty to be a mental factor, or make
every mental factor one of the twenty-two Faculties. It supplies no
separate act of confirmation by consciousness. The wider Organon
account of Cognition, Perception, and Conception remains to be tested
against later passages.
