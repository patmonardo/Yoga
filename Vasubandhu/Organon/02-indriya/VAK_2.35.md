# VAK_2.35

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विप्रयुक्तास्तु संस्काराः प्राप्त्यप्राप्ती सभागता ।
>
> आसंज्ञिकं समापत्ती जीवितं लक्षणानि च ॥ २.३५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> viprayuktās tu saṃskārāḥ prāptyaprāptī sabhāgatā /
>
> āsaṃjñikaṃ samāpattī jīvitaṃ lakṣaṇāni ca // 2.35 //

## 4. Grammar

The predicate noun is `saṃskārāḥ`, qualified by `viprayuktāḥ`. The
remaining nominatives enumerate members of that class:

```text
viprayuktāḥ saṃskārāḥ
    → formations [called] dissociated

prāpti-aprāptī ... lakṣaṇāni ca
    → acquisition and non-acquisition ... and the marks
```

The feminine dual `prāptyaprāptī` presents `prāpti` and `aprāpti` as
a pair. The dual `samāpattī` likewise presupposes two attainments, but the
kārikā does not identify them in this line.

`Viprayukta` is classificatory. The Bhāṣya explains that these formations
are so called because they are neither material by nature nor associated with
consciousness and mental factors. It does not mean unconditioned, causally
disconnected, or wholly unrelated to a cognitive continuum.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Now the dissociated formations are acquisition and non-acquisition, commonality, the non-reflective condition, the two attainments, life, and the marks.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The conditioned formations classified as dissociated from consciousness and its associated mental factors include acquisition-possession and non-acquisition, class-commonality, the non-reflective condition, the two attainments, life-continuity, and the conditioned marks.

The expansion of `prāpti` into “acquisition-possession” belongs to the
Bhāṣya's immediately following definition, not to the lexical content of the
kārikā alone.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A determination need not be a presently occurring mental factor in order to characterize a conditioned continuum. The Kośa therefore opens a class for statuses and formations not joined to the current cognition-event. At its head stands the polarity `prāpti/aprāpti`: whether a dharma has been obtained and remains possessed by a continuum, or lacks that relation to it.

This is initially a difference between **occurrence** and **status**:

```text
presently manifests(dharma, event)
    ≠
continuum is endowed with(dharma)
```

Calling the second determination a `viprayukta-saṃskāra` classifies it;
it does not yet settle whether it must be accepted as an additional real
entity. The ensuing discussion tests precisely that question.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| citta-viprayukta-saṃskāra | formation dissociated from consciousness | a conditioned determination not included in material form or the fivefold association of consciousness and mental factors |
| prāpti | acquisition-possession | according to the Bhāṣya, both obtaining what was not possessed and continuing to possess what was obtained |
| lābha | obtaining | Bhāṣya term for newly acquiring what was absent or had been relinquished |
| samanvāgama | possession / endowment | Bhāṣya term for continued possession of what has been acquired |
| aprāpti | non-acquisition | the contrary status inferred by reversing acquisition-possession |
| svasaṃtāna | one's own continuum | Bhāṣya restriction: the sentient series within which the conditioned dharma must belong |
| sabhāgatā | commonality | correspondence or shared class-status; merely listed here |
| āsaṃjñika | non-reflective condition | a formation connected with the absence or suspension of reflective recognition (`saṃjñā`), not necessarily the absence of every presentation or ideational content; merely listed here |
| samāpatti | attainment | two attainments are counted; merely listed here |
| jīvita | life / life-continuity | the life determination; merely listed here |
| lakṣaṇa | conditioned mark | marks of conditioned existence; merely listed here |

`Prāpti` must not be reduced to legal ownership. Its local range is an
indexed relation of acquisition or continued endowment between a dharma and
the sentient continuum to which it belongs.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let `S` be a sentient continuum and `D` a conditioned dharma belonging to
that continuum. The Bhāṣya's initial definition yields:

```text
Prapti(S, D)
    := NewlyObtained(S, D)
       OR ContinuesToPossess(S, D)

Aprapti(S, D)
    := NOT Prapti(S, D)
       within the admissible domain of the relation
```

The domain restriction is essential:

```text
Prapti(S, D) OR Aprapti(S, D)
    → BelongsToOwnContinuum(D, S)
```

Therefore neither another being's dharma nor a non-sentient item is simply
assigned `aprāpti` relative to `S`. Failure to satisfy the relation's domain
is not yet a negative value inside that domain:

```text
OutOfDomain(S, D) ≠ Aprapti(S, D)
```

The pair also distinguishes current manifestation from retained status:

```text
Prapti(S, D) ⇏ PresentlyManifest(D)
```

This makes `prāpti/aprāpti` suitable for tracking how a continuum may be
qualified by a dharma across moments. Whether the status requires a distinct
substantial dharma remains open at VAK 2.35.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 2.35 should first be allowed its modest textual force. It introduces a
catalogue. “Dissociated from consciousness” is the catalogue label, not an
explanation of an occult region outside mind. These items remain conditioned
formations and matter to the history of sentient continua.

`Prāpti/aprāpti` is the decisive opening pair because the Kośa must account
for a simple but difficult fact: what presently occurs does not exhaust what
a continuum has become. A wholesome capacity, an affliction, or a path-status
may characterize a continuum without appearing as a mental factor in the
current cognition-event.

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes two moments within `prāpti`:

```text
lābha
    transition into possession

samanvāgama
    continuation of possession
```

That distinction gives the pair a temporal structure:

```text
not possessed → obtained → retained
       aprāpti      prāpti
```

The arrow is a restrained reconstruction, not the kārikā's own diagram.
It shows why `prāpti` is more than a momentary event: it binds a determination
to the history of one continuum.

For the Kośa Technē, the immediate gain is state tracking:

```text
CognitionEvent
    records what is manifest now

ContinuumStatus
    records what has been obtained, retained, or not acquired
```

But we should not yet compile `prāpti` as a free-standing stored object. The
next discussion asks whether it is a real entity additional to continuum and
dharma, or whether possession-language can be grounded in the continuum's
transformed capacities. VAK 2.35 opens the problem; it does not decide it.
