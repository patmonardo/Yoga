## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In the desire realm, unobscured-indeterminate consciousness is fourfold:
> maturation-born, deportment, craft, and creation. In the Form realm it
> is without craft.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Unobscured-indeterminate consciousness is fourfold in the desire realm:
> maturation-born, deportment, craft, and creation; in the Form realm, it
> is without craft.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The verse completes the subdivision of the unobscured-indeterminate kinds. The Bhāṣya identifies avyākṛta as unobscured-indeterminate, notes that creation is supernormal, and states that these activities are absent in the formless.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Translation note.** The Bhāṣya calls these mental cognitions. The five sensory cognitions prepare deportment and craft; creation-consciousness is supernormal. In the formless realm these activities are absent. A deportment-produced mental cognition takes the twelve Essences. The succession analysis divides the twelve kinds into twenty: wholesome consciousness becomes sixfold; unobscured-indeterminate becomes sevenfold, or eight when the formless kind is left undivided. The resulting distribution is eight desire, six Form, four formless, and two uncontaminated. A coarse list cannot be assigned indiscriminately to each subtype. Creation-consciousness follows and is followed by its own kind and Form application-produced only. Application-produced consciousness is not followed in reverse by deportment, craft, or maturation-born consciousness, which are weak or prepare the activity; afflicted consciousness can precede it when one is wearied by affliction. Keenness explains some birth-acquired transitions. Attention before and after the Path remains disputed.

The verse completes the subdivision of the unobscured-indeterminate kinds. The next verse returns to the acquisition of consciousness.
