# VAK_2.72

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विपाकजैर्यापथिकशैल्पस्थानिकनैर्मितम् ।
> चतुर्धाव्याकृतं कामे रूपे शिल्पविवर्जितम् ॥ २.७२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vipākajairyāpathikaśailpasthānikanairmitam /
> caturdhāvyākṛtaṃ kāme rūpe śilpavivarjitam // 2.72 //

The standalone kārikā supports this reading. The running source's damaged
first compound at 106.05 is clarified by the prose enumeration at
106.07–08. The syntax continues the division begun in 2.71.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vipākajairyāpathikaśailpasthānikanairmitam
    → vipākaja-airyāpathika-śailpasthānika-nairmitam
caturdhāvyākṛtam → caturdhā avyākṛtam
kāme            → kāme
rūpe            → rūpe
śilpavivarjitam  → śilpa-vivarjitam
```

| Expression | Lexical sense |
|---|---|
| vipākaja | born from maturation |
| airyāpathika | pertaining to deportment, bodily postures and movement |
| śailpasthānika | pertaining to a craft or skilled occupation |
| nairmita | pertaining here to supernormal creation |
| caturdhā | fourfold |
| avyākṛta | indeterminate in ethical status |
| kāma / rūpa | desire / form, here the respective realms |
| śilpavivarjita | excluding craft |

The first expression enumerates four coordinate determinations. It does
not contain an instrumental plural *vipākajaiḥ*. Likewise, the second
line contains *avyākṛtam*, not *vyākṛtam*: the initial *a* is absorbed
in sandhi after *caturdhā*.

## 4. Grammar

*Bhittvā*, “having divided,” carries over from 2.71; the Bhāṣya explicitly
says so. *Avyākṛtam* is neuter singular, with *cittam* understood, and is
the object of that continued division. The opening compound supplies
the four resulting descriptions. *Kāme* and *rūpe* are locative singulars
restricting the divisions to their realms.

*Śilpavivarjitam* is a neuter singular qualification: the form-realm
classification omits the craft member. The prose makes its resulting
threefold division explicit. Although the verse says only *avyākṛta*,
the commentary specifies *anivṛtāvyākṛta*, unobscured-indeterminate.
Obscured-indeterminate mind is not being subdivided here.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> [Having divided] indeterminate [mind] in the desire [realm] fourfold
> into maturation-born, deportment-related, craft-related, and
> creation-related; in the form [realm], excluding craft.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Unobscured-indeterminate mind in the desire realm is divided into
> four kinds: maturation-born, deportment-related, craft-related, and
> the mind of supernormal creation. In the form realm it has three
> kinds, since the craft-related kind is absent.

Together with the wholesome divisions in 2.71, these distinctions
complete the twenty kinds announced there. The formless realm has
maturation-born neutral mind, but lacks the other three neutral kinds.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Ethical neutrality leaves further differences to be learned.
> A mind may be neutral as a maturation-result, in the conduct of bodily
> movement, in skilled work, or in supernormal creation. Which kinds
> occur depends on the realm; neutrality alone does not settle their
> function or their possible succession.

This is a study interpretation of the distinctions. The four terms do
not all answer an identical question about origin: maturation, ordinary
activity, and supernormal creation must retain their particular senses.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Term | Controlled rendering | Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|---|
| anivṛtāvyākṛta | unobscured-indeterminate | precise class divided here |
| vipākaja | maturation-born | retained in all three realms |
| airyāpathika | deportment-related | concerns bodily postures and movement |
| śailpasthānika | craft-related | absent in the form realm |
| nairmita / nirmāṇacitta | mind of supernormal creation | called an *abhijñāphala* in the succession account |
| abhijñāphala | result of supernormal knowledge | this particular neutral mind, not the whole category of higher knowledge |
| manovijñāna | mental consciousness | distinguished from sensory preparation in the prose |
| samanantara | immediately antecedent | relation reapplied to the twenty-kind scheme |

The spelling *airyāpathika* is supported by the local prose; it need
not be silently replaced with *īryāpathika*. “Creation-related” here
is not a general synonym for imagined, fabricated, or conditioned.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The completed classification.**

| Group | Wholesome | Unwholesome | Obscured-neutral | Unobscured-neutral | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Desire realm | 2 | 1 | 1 | 4 | 8 |
| Form realm | 2 | 0 | 1 | 3 | 6 |
| Formless realm | 2 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 4 |

The trainee and beyond-training kinds add two: **8 + 6 + 4 + 2 = 20**.
The phrase “sevenfold” for the neutral subdivision in the local prose
counts the four desire and three form kinds being divided; the
unchanged formless maturation-born kind makes eight neutral kinds
in the complete scheme.

**Refinement requires checking succession again.** The long Bhāṣya
at 106.17–109.03 specifies the refined transitions and their grounds.
For example, desire-realm creation mind can immediately precede and
follow only its own creation kind and form-realm application-produced
wholesome mind (107.01–02). Its coarse parent class's full transition
list therefore cannot be assigned indiscriminately to this subtype.

The commentary distinguishes the deportment- and craft-related mental
consciousnesses from the five sensory consciousnesses that prepare their
activity. It also records another teachers' view extending the object
range of deportment-produced mental consciousness. These qualifications
belong to the Bhāṣya; they are not additional words of the kārikā.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Corrections to the first pass.** The earlier lexical table separated
*vipākajaiḥ* and lost the negation in *avyākṛta*. Both distort the
construction. Its “constructed” also left the technical creation mind
unidentified. The commentary supplies *nirmāṇacitta* and the subsequent
*abhijñāphala* terminology, resolving that ambiguity.

The extended commentary includes the twenty-kind succession account,
explanations of directional asymmetries, and competing views concerning
attention before and after the noble path. This kārikā study establishes
the classification and its interpretive consequences; the planned
continuous Bhāṣya edition must preserve those arguments and voices in
full. No single unqualified attention rule is inferred here.

**Organon interpretation.** A genus begins the discrimination but does
not exhaust it. Knowing that a mind is unobscured-neutral does not yet
explain its work or its relations to other minds. The refinement teaches
which further distinctions matter, and the subsequent account tests
them through particular successions. This is the project's reading of
the exposition, kept separate from the literal translation.

**Review status.** Provisional study checked against both research
witnesses, the standalone kārikā, and the running commentary at
106.05–109.03. The originals remain unchanged.
