# VAK_2.60 Bhāṣya — Four Classes by Exclusion

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kliṣṭā vipākajāḥ śeṣāḥ prathamāryā yathākramam /
>
> vipākaṃ sarvagaṃ hitvā tau sabhāgaṃ ca śeṣajāḥ // 2.60 //

> Afflicted, maturation-born, remaining, and first noble dharmas arise,
> in that order, from the causes left after excluding the maturation
> cause, the pervasive cause, both, and both plus the homogeneous cause.

## 3. Continuous Translation

Which dharmas arise from how many causes? In summary they are of
four kinds: afflicted, maturation-born, first dharmas without
*āsrava*, and the remainder. What counts as the remainder?
Indeterminate dharmas other than those born of maturation, and
wholesome dharmas other than the first moment without *āsrava*.
These are the four kinds.

The verse orders them as afflicted, maturation-born, remaining,
and first noble dharmas. Afflicted dharmas arise from five causes,
all except maturation cause. Maturation-born dharmas also arise
from five, but exclude pervasive cause. The remaining dharmas
arise from four, excluding both maturation and pervasive cause.
The first dharmas without *āsrava* arise from three, excluding
those two and homogeneous cause. Thus the two five-cause counts
refer to different sets of causes.

## 4. Reading Control

*Yathākramam* fixes the order of the four exclusions; equal counts
do not mean identical cause profiles. The verse calls the fourth
class *prathamāryā*, while the prose explains it as the first
dharmas without *āsrava*. The following source passage specifies
mind and mental factors, subtracts associated cause for other
dharmas, and closes the six-cause account before listing the
*pratyaya*.
