# VAK_2.60 Bhāṣya — Four Classes by Exclusion

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kliṣṭā vipākajāḥ śeṣāḥ prathamāryā yathākramam /
>
> vipākaṃ sarvagaṃ hitvā tau sabhāgaṃ ca śeṣajāḥ // 2.60 //

> Afflicted, maturation-born, remaining, and first noble dharmas arise,
> in that order, from the causes left after excluding the maturation
> cause, the pervasive cause, both, and both plus the homogeneous cause.

## 3. Conventional Argument Summary

The question is how many causes produce each class of dharma. Four
classes are distinguished: afflicted, maturation-born, remaining, and
the first uncontaminated. The remaining class comprises indeterminate
dharmas other than maturation-born and wholesome dharmas other than
the first uncontaminated moment.

The verse orders these four classes against four cause exclusions. An
afflicted dharma arises from the five causes other than maturation;
a maturation-born dharma, from the five other than the pervasive
cause. A remaining dharma arises from the four other than those two.
The first uncontaminated dharmas arise from the three other than
maturation, pervasive, and homogeneous causes. The same count of five
therefore names two different cause profiles.

## 4. Interpretation

*Yathākramam* fixes the order of the mapping. The fourth class is
called *prathamāryā* in the verse and explained as first
uncontaminated dharmas in the prose. The citta and caitta scope, the
further subtraction of associated cause for other dharmas, and the
no-single-cause statement occur in the following source passage.
