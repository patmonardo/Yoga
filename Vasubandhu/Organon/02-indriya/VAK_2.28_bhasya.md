# VAK_2.28 Bhāṣya — Wholesome Consciousness and Regret

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> savitarkavicāratvāt kuśale kāmacetasi /
>
> dvāviṃśatiś caitasikāḥ kaukṛtyam adhikaṃ kvacit // 2.28 //

> In wholesome desire-realm consciousness, twenty-two mental factors occur
> with vitarka and vicāra; regret may be additional where present.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

It must now be stated how many mental factors necessarily occur in which consciousness. First, desire-realm consciousness is fivefold: wholesome and unwholesome, the unwholesome being twofold, isolated or associated with another affliction, and indeterminate, twofold, obscured-indeterminate and unobscured-indeterminate. Desire-realm consciousness is necessarily accompanied by vitarka and vicāra. Hence, because accompanied by them, in wholesome desire-realm consciousness there are twenty-two mental factors. They necessarily occur: ten universal great-ground factors, ten wholesome great-ground factors, vitarka, and vicāra. In some cases regret is additional. Regret is not present in every wholesome consciousness. Where it is, adding it makes twenty-three.

What is this called kaukṛtya? Kaukṛtya is the state of something badly done. Here, however, the dharma that has this as its support is called kaukṛtya: regret in consciousness. Similarly, the gateway to liberation that has emptiness as its support is called emptiness, and non-greed that has the unattractive as its support is called the unattractive. In ordinary usage too, the occupants are designated by the place: the whole village has arrived, the whole country has arrived. What is badly done is the basis of regret. Alternatively, this is the cause's name applied to the effect, as in the statement that these six bases of contact should be understood as former action.

How is that which has something not done as its support called kaukṛtya? Even what was not done can be called done: I did not do well in not doing it. Which regret is wholesome? That in which one is troubled after failing to do the wholesome, or after doing the unwholesome. By the reverse, regret is unwholesome. Both thus have a twofold basis.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The question opens the counts. Five desire-realm cases are prepared. This verse is the wholesome one. Twenty-two, then regret as a conditional addition, then the name. Two accounts of transferred designation. The second is marked alternatively. Wholesome and unwholesome regret close the unit. No school.

## 5. The Fivefold Setting and Twenty-Two

One wholesome, two unwholesome, two indeterminate. Isolated is āveṇika. Its explanation is 2.29. Not consciousness without factors. Obscured and unobscured stay apart.

Ten, ten, vitarka, vicāra. Consciousness is not a twenty-third factor. Not the twenty-two faculties. The objects of the count have changed.

Vitarka and vicāra are the two additions the realm supplies. Definitions are not in this unit. The renderings are provisional. Not two temporal stages.

## 6. Regret Added

In some cases. Not among the factors in every wholesome consciousness. Twenty-three when added. The local rule. Later conditions may add more. Not an exhaustive account of every possible addition.

Regret in consciousness, distinguished from the badly done that is its support. Confusing them would make the factor the deed.

This is the unfixed of 2.27 made local. Conditional, and not exclusively wholesome. Unfixed did not mean without conditions.

## 7. The Name

A factor named through its support. Emptiness and the unattractive keep object and the dharma directed toward it apart. Village and country name occupants by place. The badly done is the basis.

Alternatively, the cause's name on the effect. The six bases receive the name of prior action. The direction is from cause to effect, not the reverse.

Alternatives. Not one theory of all naming. The factor is not identified with its object or its cause.

## 8. Omission and Direction

Not-done can be spoken of as done badly. Omission is included. The omitted deed is not pretended to have occurred.

| Regret | Done | Not done |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesome | the unwholesome | the wholesome |
| Unwholesome | the wholesome | the unwholesome |

Twofold basis follows. Neither the mere presence of regret nor the ethical character of the act alone classifies it. Done or omitted, and what is regretted, stay explicit.

The naming examples are damaged in the source. Emptiness as support, gateway to liberation, and non-greed with the unattractive as support are contextual. Not a collation.

## 9. Interpretation

Classes supply members. Regret supplies a conditional addition. The factor, its support, its basis, and its ethical direction stay apart. Naming through a deed does not make the deed a factor. The cause's name on the effect does not collapse the relation.

Regret is not wholesome by self-reproach alone. Direction decides. Objective Logic on the mind is the pass. Not the translation. Not the final pass.
