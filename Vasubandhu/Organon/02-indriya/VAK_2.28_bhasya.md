# VAK_2.28 Bhāṣya — Wholesome Consciousness and Regret

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> savitarkavicāratvāt kuśale kāmacetasi /
>
> dvāviṃśatiś caitasikāḥ kaukṛtyam adhikaṃ kvacit // 2.28 //

> In wholesome Desire-Realm consciousness, twenty-two mental factors
> necessarily occur: the ten universal factors, the ten wholesome factors,
> and initial and sustained examination. In some cases regret is additional,
> making twenty-three where it is present.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

We must now say how many mental factors necessarily occur in each kind of consciousness. Desire Realm consciousness has five cases: wholesome; unwholesome, either isolated or associated with another affliction; and indeterminate, either obscured or unobscured. Desire Realm consciousness necessarily has initial and sustained examination. Thus wholesome Desire Realm consciousness has twenty-two mental factors: ten universal great-ground factors, ten wholesome great-ground factors, and those two kinds of examination. Regret occurs in some cases, adding a twenty-third factor where it is present.

What is called regret (*kaukṛtya*)? The word first means the condition of something badly done. Here it names remorse in consciousness, a dharma that takes what was badly done as its Support (*ālambana*). Likewise, a gateway to liberation that takes emptiness as its Support is called “emptiness,” and non-greed taking the unattractive as its Support is called “the unattractive.” Ordinary speech likewise names occupants by their place: “The whole village has arrived”; “the whole country has arrived.” What was badly done provides the occasion for remorse. Alternatively, the cause's name is applied to its effect, as in the statement that the six Contact Essences should be understood as former action.

How can remorse that takes an omission as its Support receive a name derived from what was badly done? Even an omission can be described this way: “I did badly in not doing it.” Which regret is wholesome? Remorse over failing to do what is wholesome, or over doing what is unwholesome. In the reverse cases regret is unwholesome. Each kind can thus arise in relation to something done or something left undone.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

The question opens the counts. Five Desire Realm cases are prepared. This verse is the wholesome one. Twenty-two, then regret as a conditional addition, then the name. Two accounts of transferred designation. The second is marked alternatively. Wholesome and unwholesome regret close the unit. No school.

## 5. The Fivefold Setting and Twenty-Two

One wholesome, two unwholesome, two indeterminate. Isolated is āveṇika. Its explanation is 2.29. Not consciousness without factors. Obscured and unobscured stay apart.

Ten, ten, initial examination, sustained examination. Consciousness is
not a twenty-third factor. This is not the twenty-two-Faculty census;
the counted entities have changed.

Initial and sustained examination are the two additions the realm
supplies to the inherited twenty, not additions beyond twenty-two.
Definitions are not in this unit. The renderings remain provisional,
not two temporal stages.

## 6. Regret Added

In some cases. Not among the factors in every wholesome consciousness. Twenty-three when added. The local rule. Later conditions may add more. Not an exhaustive account of every possible addition.

Regret in consciousness is distinguished from the badly done that
is its Support. Confusing them would make the present factor the deed.

This is the unfixed of 2.27 made local. Conditional, and not exclusively wholesome. Unfixed did not mean without conditions.

## 7. The Name

A factor can be named through its Support. Emptiness and the
unattractive keep the Support and the dharma directed toward it
apart. Village and country name occupants by place. What was badly
done provides the occasion for remorse; this is not the technical
*āśraya* relation.

Alternatively, the cause's name on the effect. The six Contact
Essences receive the name of prior action. The direction is from
cause to effect, not the reverse.

Alternatives, not one theory of all naming. The factor is not
identified with its Support or its cause.

## 8. Omission and Direction

Not-done can be spoken of as done badly. Omission is included. The omitted deed is not pretended to have occurred.

| Regret | Done | Not done |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesome | the unwholesome | the wholesome |
| Unwholesome | the wholesome | the unwholesome |

Twofold basis follows. Neither the mere presence of regret nor the ethical character of the act alone classifies it. Done or omitted, and what is regretted, stay explicit.

The naming examples are damaged in the source. Emptiness as
Support, gateway to liberation, and non-greed with
the unattractive as Support are contextual readings,
not a collation.

## 9. Interpretation

Classes supply members. Regret supplies a conditional addition.
The present factor, its Support, the deed or omission, and its ethical
direction remain distinct. Naming through a deed does not make
the deed a factor. The cause's name on the effect does not collapse
the relation.

Regret is not wholesome by self-reproach alone. Its direction matters,
including the difference between doing and omitting.

The counted twenty-two are mental factors accompanying consciousness,
not a new enumeration of Faculties. Neither the deed nor consciousness
itself is added to their count. The regret example shows how a factor
can be directed toward an absent act without making that act a present
factor. It does not define mental factors as constructions from
appearances or equate their Support with their Basis.
