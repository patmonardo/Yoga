# VAK_2.09 Bhāṣya — Path-Designations and Outflow Classification

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dṛgbhāvanāśaikṣapathe nava trīṇi amalaṃ trayam /
> rūpīṇi jīvitaṃ duḥkhe sāsravāṇi dvidhā nava // 2.09 //

> On the paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training, the nine are the three. The triad is stainless. Those possessing form, life, and the two painful faculties are with outflows. Nine are twofold.

The Bhāṣya names the nine, distributes the three designations, and reads duḥkhe as pain and distress. Aśaikṣa is confirmed by the prose.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

On the paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training, the nine are the three. The nine are mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, and the five beginning with faith. These nine faculties are called three faculties on the three paths: on the path of seeing, the faculty “I shall know what has not been known”; on the path of cultivation, the knowledge-faculty; on the path of no-more-training, the faculty of one who has known. Why? On the path of seeing, one is engaged in knowing what has not been known. On the path of cultivation, there is nothing unprecedented to be known. One knows that same thing for the sake of abandoning the remaining latent afflictions. On the path of no-more-training, the ascertainment “it has been known” is called ājñātāva. One who possesses this is ājñātāvin. Or the name means one whose disposition is to preserve what has been known, because of attaining the knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising. The faculty of one in such a condition — suffering has been fully known by me; it is not to be fully known again — is the faculty of one who has known.

Having explained their own nature, their distinctions of kind must be stated: how many faculties are with outflows, how many without outflows, and so forth. First, the group just mentioned, beginning with the faculty “I shall know,” is the stainless triad. This means without outflows, since stain is another designation for outflow. Those possessing form, life, and the two painful faculties are with outflows. The seven possessing form, the life-faculty, and the faculties of pain and distress are exclusively with outflows. The seven are eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, female, and male, because they are included in the form aggregate. Nine are twofold: mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, and the five beginning with faith are both with outflows and without outflows.

Some say that those beginning with faith are exclusively without outflows. For the Blessed One said: one in whom these five faculties are altogether absent I call an outsider, standing among ordinary persons. This does not establish the claim, because the statement was made with reference to the faculties without outflows. For he first set out the classification of noble persons and then said one in whom these. Or ordinary persons are of two kinds: an insider whose wholesome roots have not been severed, and an outsider whose wholesome roots have been severed. With reference to the latter he says: I call an outsider, standing among ordinary persons.

It is also said in a sūtra, before the Wheel of Dharma had been set in motion: there are beings born in the world and grown up in the world with sharp faculties, middling faculties, and weak faculties. Therefore those beginning with faith certainly do exist with outflows. Again it is said: so long as I had not known as they really are the arising, disappearance, gratification, danger, and escape concerning these five faculties, so long I, from this world with its gods — and so forth. But this is not the manner of examination of dharmas without outflows.

The final quotation is abbreviated in the Sanskrit. The unfinished clause is not completed from an unprovided sūtra. The argument is the five aspects of examination.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The first movement completes the three. A new phase is then announced: from own nature to distinctions of kind. The first axis is outflow.

Some restrict the five. The reply offers two readings of the citation, then two scriptural grounds. Vā marks an alternative reply, not a second step that must be joined to the first. No school is named.

## 5. Nine Types and Three Names

The nine are mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, faith, vigor, mindfulness, concentration, and Discrimination. The three names classify them on seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training.

Not three unrelated constituents. Not nine co-present in every path-event. The feeling-faculties have just been distributed. Nine types according to path is safer than nine factors in every instance.

Seeing is what was not known. Cultivation continues that knowing against remaining anuśaya. No-more-training ascertains that the task is done. Nothing unprecedented is this task, not omniscience. Suffering fully known is not the loss of the power to cognize it again.

## 6. The Threefold Partition

| Status | Faculties | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Exclusively without outflows | the three | 3 |
| Exclusively with outflows | seven of form, life, pain, distress | 10 |
| Either | mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, the five | 9 |

The two nines are the same list under two questions. Twofold types do not make the triad partly with outflows. The triad is without outflows.

Twofold is either status across instances, not both in one instance. With outflows is not unwholesome, and not personally afflicted. Material faculties and life are in the class, and bodily feeling in an arhat was already allowed. Outflow, ethical quality, and the bearer's attainment stay apart. This passage does not classify conditioned and unconditioned.

## 7. Why the Five Are Not Exclusively Without Outflows

The citation links absence of the five to the outsider. The first reply restricts the reference: after noble persons are set out, these can be the faculties without outflows. Their absence need not exclude faith with outflows.

The alternative distinguishes the insider whose wholesome roots are intact from the outsider whose roots are severed. The statement concerns the latter. Neither reply denies the quotation.

The next citation is faculties before the Wheel was set turning. The commentary takes it as with-outflow faith and the rest. The last citation is arising, disappearance, gratification, danger, and escape. Dharmas without outflows are not examined that way. The sūtras are not collated here.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

Amala is glossed anāsrava. The working words remain with outflows and without outflows. Without outflows is not without effect. Duḥkhe is the dual, pain and distress, not in pain.

Ājñātāvin has two stated derivations. Both are kept. Ājñātāva is not treated as an independently verified historical derivation.

The last extract ends with iti vistaraḥ. The ellipsis is marked. No awakening claim is supplied beyond the witness.

## 9. Organon Reading

A type receives a path-name through the task. The same type can have either outflow-status. The name does not settle the instance. The three are a unity through the path. The unity does not erase the nine, and it is not nine factors in every moment.

The net coordinates nine jewels into three names. The configuration is stainless. The jewels remain twofold. Faith and the rest are not stainless by being named. Pure is the Kant cut, always first. It is not amala. The principles remain deposited. Not the verse English.
