# VAK_2.09 Bhāṣya — Nine as Three, and the Partition

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dṛgbhāvanāśaikṣapathe nava trīṇi amalaṃ trayam /
>
> rūpīṇi jīvitaṃ duḥkhe sāsravāṇi dvidhā nava // 2.09 //

> On the Paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training, the nine
> Faculties are designated as the three; the triad is stainless, without
> *āsrava*. The Form Faculties, Life-Faculty, and the two painful Faculties
> are exclusively with *āsrava*; nine admit either status.

## 3. Continuous Translation

On the paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training, the nine are the three. The nine are mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, and the five beginning with faith. These nine faculties are called three faculties on the three paths: on the path of seeing, the faculty “I shall know what has not been known”; on the path of cultivation, the knowledge-faculty; on the path of no-more-training, the faculty of one who has known. Why? On the path of seeing, one is engaged in knowing what has not been known. On the path of cultivation, there is nothing unprecedented to be known. One knows that same thing for the sake of abandoning the remaining latent afflictions. On the path of no-more-training, the ascertainment “it has been known” is called ājñātāva. One who possesses this is ājñātāvin. Or the name means one whose disposition is to preserve what has been known, because of attaining the knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising. The faculty of one in such a condition — suffering has been fully known by me; it is not to be fully known again — is the faculty of one who has known.

Having explained their own nature, their distinctions of kind must be stated: how many faculties are with *āsrava*, how many without *āsrava*, and so forth. First, the group just mentioned, beginning with the faculty “I shall know,” is the stainless triad. This means without *āsrava*, since “stain” is another name for *āsrava*. Those possessing form, life, and the two painful faculties are with *āsrava*. The seven possessing form, the life-faculty, and the faculties of pain and distress are exclusively with *āsrava*. The seven are eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, female, and male, because they are included in the Base of Form. Nine are twofold: mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, and the five beginning with faith can occur either with or without *āsrava*.

Some say that those beginning with faith are exclusively without *āsrava*. For the Blessed One said: one in whom these five faculties are altogether absent I call an outsider, standing among ordinary persons. This does not establish the claim, because the statement was made with reference to faculties without *āsrava*. For he first set out the classification of noble persons and then said “one in whom these.” Alternatively, ordinary persons are of two kinds: an insider whose wholesome roots have not been severed, and an outsider whose wholesome roots have been severed. With reference to the latter he says: “I call an outsider, standing among ordinary persons.”

It is also said in a sūtra, before the Wheel of Dharma had been set in motion: there are beings born in the world and grown up in the world with sharp faculties, middling faculties, and weak faculties. Therefore those beginning with faith do exist with *āsrava*. Again it is said: “So long as I had not known as they really are the arising, disappearance, gratification, danger, and escape concerning these five faculties, so long I, from this world with its gods…” The Sanskrit abbreviates the rest of the citation. But this is not the manner of examination of dharmas without *āsrava*.

The final quotation is abbreviated in the Sanskrit. The unfinished clause is not completed from an unprovided sūtra. The argument is the five aspects of examination.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The first movement completes the three. A new phase is then announced: from own nature to distinctions of kind. The first axis is presence or absence of *āsrava*.

Some restrict the five. The reply offers two readings of the citation, then two scriptural grounds. Vā marks an alternative reply, not a second step that must be joined to the first. No school is named.

## 5. Nine Types and Three Names

The nine are Mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, faith, vigor,
mindfulness, concentration, and discriminative comprehension. The three
names classify them on seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training.

Not three unrelated constituents. Not nine co-present in every path-event. The feeling-faculties have just been distributed. Nine types according to path is safer than nine factors in every instance.

Seeing is what was not known. Cultivation continues that knowing against remaining anuśaya. No-more-training ascertains that the task is done. Nothing unprecedented is this task, not omniscience. Suffering fully known is not the loss of the power to cognize it again.

## 6. The Threefold Partition

| Status | Faculties | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Exclusively without *āsrava* | the three | 3 |
| Exclusively with *āsrava* | seven of form, life, pain, distress | 10 |
| Either | mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, the five | 9 |

The two nines are the same list under two questions. The partition is 3 + 10 + 9 = 22; the first mention of nine is not an additional group. Twofold types do not make the triad partly with *āsrava*. The triad is without *āsrava*.

Twofold means either status across instances, not both in one instance. With *āsrava* does not by itself mean unwholesome or personally afflicted. Form faculties and life are in that class, and bodily feeling in an arhat was already allowed. *Āsrava* status, ethical quality, and the bearer's attainment stay apart. This passage does not classify conditioned and unconditioned.

## 7. Why the Five Are Not Exclusively Without Āsrava

The citation links absence of the five to the outsider. The first reply restricts the reference: after noble persons are set out, “these” can mean the faculties without *āsrava*. Their absence need not exclude faith with *āsrava*.

The alternative distinguishes the insider whose wholesome roots are intact from the outsider whose roots are severed. The statement concerns the latter. Neither reply denies the quotation.

The next citation mentions faculties before the Wheel was set turning. The commentary takes it as evidence for faith and the rest with *āsrava*. The last citation names arising, disappearance, gratification, danger, and escape. The reply argues that dharmas without *āsrava* are not examined that way. The sūtras are not collated here.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

*Amala* is glossed *anāsrava*. The working expressions are “with *āsrava*” and “without *āsrava*”; the latter does not mean without effect. *Duḥkhe* is the dual, pain and distress, not “in pain.”

Ājñātāvin has two stated derivations. Both are kept. Ājñātāva is not treated as an independently verified historical derivation.

The last extract ends with iti vistaraḥ. The ellipsis is marked. No awakening claim is supplied beyond the witness.

## 9. Interpretation

A type receives a path-name through the task. The same type can have either *āsrava* status. The name does not settle the instance. The three are unified by their path roles. That does not erase the nine types or assert that all nine occur in every moment.

The Organon determination distinguishes type-level capacity from
Path-qualified operation. The nine types admit either *āsrava* status;
their realization designations apply to Path operation without *āsrava*.
Neither a name alone nor bare membership in the faith-group settles an
instance's status. “Stainless” expresses the source's determination of
being without *āsrava*, not a different classificatory axis.

The present nine include Mind and discriminative comprehension as
distinct Faculties. They are not interchangeable cognitive powers, and
this passage does not add Cognition as a tenth member. The wider Organon
account of Cognition, Perception, and Conception remains to be tested
against the text.

## 10. Review Status

Reviewed with the kārikā against printed 42.07–43.07. “Designated as
the three” replaces ambiguous “threefold,” and the *āsrava* classification
states exclusivity and either-status explicitly. The anchor matches
the study translation.

Grammar preserves the painful dual, group counts, and singular
stainless triad. Logic separates Path-designations from type-status,
with rejected swapped Path-names, twofold triad, and faith exclusively
without *āsrava*. The debate, alternative replies, and abbreviated
quotation are preserved. No independent source collation is claimed.
