# VAK_2.48

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> सभागता सा तु पुनर्विपाकोऽप्याप्तयो द्विधा ।
>
> लक्षणानि च निःष्यन्दाः समापत्त्यसमन्वयाः ॥ २.४८ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> sabhāgatā sā tu punar vipāko 'py āptayo dvidhā /
>
> lakṣaṇāni ca niḥṣyandāḥ samāpatty-asamanvayāḥ // 2.48 //

The commentary first completes the closing relation from VAK 2.47
(82.06–08); VAK 2.48 itself is cited at 82.09–15. Its commentary
closes at 82.18, and the question introducing VAK 2.49 follows at
82.19–20.

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| sabhāgatā | nominative feminine singular | commonality / membership in a shared class |
| sā | nominative feminine singular pronoun | that [commonality], carrying forward the preceding classification |
| tu | adversative particle | but / however |
| punaḥ | indeclinable | further / in addition |
| vipākaḥ api | nominative masculine singular plus particle | also a maturation-result |
| āptayaḥ | nominative feminine plural | acquisitions / possessions |
| dvidhā | indeclinable adverb | in two ways / twofold |
| lakṣaṇāni | nominative neuter plural | conditioned marks |
| ca | conjunction | and |
| niḥṣyandāḥ | nominative plural predicate | homogeneous outflows |
| samāpatti-asamanvayāḥ | compressed coordinative expression | the [two] attainments and non-acquisition |

The transmitted final compound is highly compressed. The Bhāṣya expands its
members as the two attainments and `asamanvāgama`, non-acquisition. It must not
be parsed as an equation of `samāpatti` with `samanvaya`.

## 4. Grammar

The opening pronoun carries forward the classifications of the linguistic
groups in VAK 2.47:

```text
sā sabhāgatā
    → that commonality [is likewise associated with beings,
      a homogeneous outflow, and unobscured-indeterminate]

tu punaḥ vipākaḥ api
    → but it is additionally also a maturation-result
```

The next construction distributes `dvidhā` across two subjects, as made
explicit by the Bhāṣya:

```text
āptayaḥ dvidhā
lakṣaṇāni ca dvidhā

acquisitions are twofold
and the conditioned marks are twofold
```

In each case the two result-types are:

```text
niḥṣyanda     homogeneous outflow
vipāka        maturation-result
```

The final compressed predicate has a narrower scope:

```text
samāpattayaḥ asamanvāgamaḥ ca
    → niḥṣyandāḥ eva

the two attainments and non-acquisition
    → homogeneous outflows only
```

Thus `niḥṣyandāḥ` does not erase the preceding twofold classification. Its
exclusive application to the final members is supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> That commonality, however, is further also a maturation-result. Acquisitions are twofold, and so are the conditioned marks. The attainments and non-acquisition are homogeneous outflows.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Class-commonality shares the preceding classifications of the linguistic groups, but it can arise not only as a homogeneous outflow but also as a karmic maturation-result. Acquisition and each of the conditioned marks likewise have these two possible result-statuses. The two attainments without mind and non-acquisition, by contrast, are homogeneous outflows only.

## 6. Limited Organon Reading

Within the project's First Philosophy framing, this verse extends the
classification of formations to their result-status. Locally, it
distinguishes homogeneous outflow from maturation-result, specifies which
items admit each classification, and leads to the question of causes and
conditions. The six-cause account belongs to the next kārikā.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| sabhāgatā | class-commonality | shared kind or mode of existence attributed to members of a continuum |
| vipāka | maturation-result | result arising through the maturation of prior karma |
| niḥṣyanda | homogeneous outflow | result continuous in kind with its conditioning series |
| āpti / prāpti | acquisition / possession | positive relation by which a continuum possesses a dharma |
| dvidhā | twofold | here: capable of being either `niḥṣyanda` or `vipāka` |
| lakṣaṇa | conditioned mark | birth, duration, aging, or impermanence accompanying a conditioned dharma |
| samāpatti | attainment | here the two attainments without mind previously analyzed |
| asamanvāgama / aprāpti | non-acquisition | absence of the possession-relation within a continuum |
| sattvākhyā | associated with sentient beings | predication grounded in possession by a sentient continuum |
| asattvākhyā | not associated with sentient beings | applicable to marks accompanying conditioned dharmas outside sentient continua |

`Niḥṣyanda` and `vipāka` are not generic synonyms for “effect.” They answer
different genetic questions: continuity in kind and karmic maturation.

The Bhāṣya calls them *acittasamāpatti*, “attainments without mind.” The
translation preserves that wording without resolving the operation of either
attainment here.

## 8. Logical Determination

The result matrix is:

| Formation | Homogeneous outflow | Maturation-result |
|---|---:|---:|
| class-commonality | yes | yes |
| acquisition | yes | yes |
| conditioned marks | yes | yes |
| two attainments without mind | yes | no |
| non-acquisition | yes | no |

The two causal forms are distinct:

```text
Niḥṣyanda(x, series)
    := x continues a homogeneous causal character of series

Vipāka(x, karma)
    := x arises through maturation of prior karma
```

Therefore category identity does not entail genetic identity:

```text
SameCatalogueClass(x, y)
    ⇏ SameResultType(x, y)
```

Nor does an identical surface determination entail one provenance:

```text
ClassCommonality(x)
    → Niḥṣyanda(x) OR Vipāka(x)

Acquisition(x)
    → Niḥṣyanda(x) OR Vipāka(x)

ConditionedMark(x)
    → Niḥṣyanda(x) OR Vipāka(x)
```

The final members are restricted:

```text
AttainmentWithoutMind(x) OR NonAcquisition(x)
    → Niḥṣyanda(x)
    AND NOT Vipāka(x)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

The verse assigns two result-statuses to class-commonality, acquisitions,
and conditioned marks, but restricts the two attainments without mind and
non-acquisition to homogeneous outflows. The Bhāṣya then closes the
dissociated-formations account and asks about causes and conditions. The
answer begins with VAK 2.49; it is not supplied here.
