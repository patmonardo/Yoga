# VAK_2.62 Bhāṣya — Antecedent, Object, Predominant

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> cittacaittā acaramā utpannāḥ samanantaraḥ /
>
> ālambanaṃ sarvadharmāḥ kāraṇākhyo 'dhipaḥ smṛtaḥ // 2.62 //

> Arisen consciousness and mental factors, except the final ones, are the
> immediately antecedent condition. All dharmas are object-conditions.
> What is called the conditioning cause is held to be the predominant
> condition.

These selected passages follow 98.09–100.13 in
[the running witness](../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt). The kārikā is
interleaved with a long discussion of immediate succession. The
witness reads *ālambanaṃ sarvadharmāḥ*, also found in the older VAK
study; this is not a collation.

## 3. Conventional Argument Summary

Arisen mind and mental factors, apart from an arhat's final mind and
factors, are immediately antecedent conditions. The discussion asks
why material form does not have this role and rejects limiting it to
succession of factors of the same kind. Its positive account makes
the whole preceding mental complex the condition of the whole next
complex. The status of a future event as immediately antecedent and
knowledge of future succession are debated in the intervening prose.

All dharmas can serve as object-conditions, each for the relevant
cognition: visible form for eye-consciousness and all dharmas for
mental consciousness. The qualifier “as appropriate” belongs to the
Bhāṣya, not the verse's bare universal statement. Finally, the
predominant condition is identified with what was called the
conditioning cause.

## 4. Interpretation

The three conditions are differentiated by their functions: immediate
mental succession, objecthood relative to cognition, and
predominance. The whole-complex analysis and the relevant-cognition
restriction specify the first two definitions without changing their
verse wording.

## 5. Review Status

The object-condition wording and the three verse predications have
been checked against the running witness. The selected Bhāṣya lines
support the whole-complex and relevant-object qualifications; the
long intervening debate is summarized rather than translated in full.
