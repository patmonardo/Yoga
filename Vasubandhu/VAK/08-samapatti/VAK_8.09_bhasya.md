# VAK_8.09 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**Corrected best translation:**  
By substance there are eleven. In the first two dhyānas, the factor called pleasure is pliancy; inner clarity is confidence; and rapture is pleasant-mindedness, as established from two scriptural statements.

## Source Caution

The working e-text is a reference text based principally on Pradhan with corrections from Shastri and is not a critical edition. The transmitted line marker `438|16-438|917` elsewhere in this unit is evidently defective and is not silently repaired here.

More importantly, the previous Kārikā pass misread `dravyato daśa caikaṃ ca` as a total of ten. The Bhāṣya removes the ambiguity completely:

```text
dravyata etāny ekādaśa bhavanti
    by substance these are eleven
```

The canonical count must therefore be corrected from ten to eleven.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The eighteen limbs enumerated across the four dhyānas are eighteen only **by name** (`nāmataḥ`). When substantially distinct dharmas are counted, they reduce to eleven.

The count proceeds genetically. The five factors of the first dhyāna supply the initial five. In the second, `adhyātmasaṃprasāda` adds one new factor. In the third, `upekṣā`, `smṛti`, `saṃprajñāna`, and a distinct kind of `sukha` add four. In the fourth, `aduḥkhāsukhā vedanā` adds one more. Thus:

```text
5 + 1 + 4 + 1 = 11
```

Vasubandhu then asks why the pleasure of the third dhyāna counts as a distinct substance. The answer is that third-dhyāna pleasure is pleasure as feeling (`vedanā-sukha`), whereas the pleasure named in the first two dhyānas is analyzed as the ease of pliancy (`prasrabdhi-sukha`).

A long dispute follows concerning whether the first two dhyānas instead contain bodily pleasure, whether mental pleasure should be admitted, and whether `prasrabdhi` itself can be identified with pleasure. The passage preserves competing analyses rather than presenting a simple uncontested taxonomy.

The discussion then turns to `adhyātmasaṃprasāda`. Vasubandhu first gives a functional description: when the disturbance of vitarka and vicāra is absent, the continuum flows peacefully. But if this were merely a state of the continuum rather than a distinct dharma, the eleven-substance count would fail. The Kārikā therefore identifies this `prasāda` with `śraddhā`, confidence.

Finally, the Bhāṣya tests the identification of `prīti`. It rejects the proposal that `sukha` in the dhyānas is `saumanasya`, and concludes instead that **prīti itself is saumanasya**, on the basis of two scriptural passages (`dvidhāgamāt`).

```text

    ↓

    ↓

    + upekṣā
    + smṛti

    + vedanā-sukha

fourth dhyāna
    + aduḥkhāsukhā vedanā

TOTAL = 11
```

The identifications then reduce apparent multiplicity:

```text
sukha in first two dhyānas
    → prasrabdhi-sukha

adhyātmasaṃprasāda
    → śraddhā

prīti
    → saumanasya
```

while the third-dhyāna `sukha` remains substantially distinct because it is a feeling (`vedanā`).

## Voice Structure

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — establishes the elevenfold substantial count and poses the problem of the third-dhyāna pleasure.

**Voice: Other masters / alternative position (`apare`)** — argues that there is no mental pleasure faculty in the three dhyānas and proposes bodily pleasure as the limb.

**Voice: Objector / disputant** — raises successive objections concerning bodily cognition during samāpatti, disturbance of samādhi, and the status of pliancy.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / text-governed resolution** — returns to the classificatory problem, identifies `prasāda` with `śraddhā`, and uses scripture to establish `prīti = saumanasya` rather than `sukha = saumanasya`.

The middle dialectic is dense enough that individual short objections should not be over-assigned to named schools without stronger textual evidence.

## New Technical Terms

- **nāmataḥ** — according to name or verbal enumeration
  - **status:** made technically operative by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** distinguishes the eighteen named limbs from the eleven substantially distinct dharmas

- **dravyataḥ** — according to substantial distinctness
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā and explicitly defined by the prose count
  - **function:** ontological re-counting of the named factors

- **vedanā-sukha** — pleasure that is itself a feeling
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** distinguishes third-dhyāna pleasure from the pleasure terminology of the first two dhyānas

- **prasrabdhi-sukha** — the ease or pleasure associated with pliancy
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** analysis of the `sukha` named in the first two dhyānas

- **praśāntavāhitā** — peaceful flow of the continuum
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** functional characterization of `adhyātmasaṃprasāda` once vitarka-vicāra disturbance is absent

- **saṃpratyaya** — confidence, assurance, settled conviction
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** explains why `śraddhā` can function as `adhyātmasaṃprasāda`

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive distinction is:

```text
named factor
    ≠ necessarily
substantially distinct dharma
```

VAK 8.07–8.08 gave the meditative constitution as experienced and taught under eighteen factor-names. VAK 8.09 now asks how many distinct dharmas those names actually designate.

The answer is **eleven**, not eighteen.

This is not a merely numerical reduction. The Bhāṣya has to discriminate several different relations:

```text
same name / different substance
    sukha in earlier vs third dhyāna

different names / same substance
    prasāda → śraddhā
    prīti → saumanasya

functional description / substantial classification
    peaceful flow of continuum
    versus a distinct caitasika dharma
```

The factor-analysis is therefore simultaneously phenomenological, functional, scriptural, and ontological.

## Translation Consequences

The previous Kārikā translation must be corrected:

```text
NOT:
    "By substance there are ten."

BUT:
    "By substance there are eleven."
```

`dvidhāgamāt` should not be rendered vaguely as "because of twofold occurrence." The Bhāṣya explicitly invokes **two scriptural statements** to establish the equation `prīti = saumanasya`. A safer translation is:

> "Rapture, however, is pleasant-mindedness, as established from two scriptural statements."

The first-pass statement that the named limbs simply reduce by treating several descriptions as one factor was directionally correct but incomplete. The Bhāṣya also shows the converse: the same ordinary term `sukha` can designate **different substances** at different dhyāna levels.

## Logical Determination

This verse introduces a crucial distinction between **enumeration by marks** and **identity by dharma**.

```text
nāmataḥ
    eighteen factor-designations

↓ discrimination

dravyataḥ
    eleven distinct dharmas
```

The mapping is not uniformly many-to-one. It is more subtle:

```text
one term
    may map to different dharmas by context

several terms
    may map to one dharma by function
```

Therefore neither lexical identity nor lexical difference is sufficient to determine dharma identity.

The Bhāṣya's reasoning requires:

```text
name
context
function
feeling-class
scriptural constraint
substantial identity
```

all to remain explicit.

## Light Organon Contact

This is especially useful for our terminology discipline, but no larger architecture needs to be imposed here.

The clean rule is:

> **Marks are not yet identities. A systematic analysis must determine when differing marks designate one factor and when one mark designates differing factors.**

That is precisely what happens with `sukha`:

```text
same Mark: sukha

first / second dhyāna
    prasrabdhi-sukha

third dhyāna
    vedanā-sukha
```

and with `prasāda` / `śraddhā` and `prīti` / `saumanasya` in the opposite direction.

This remains a local Abhidharma determination: the Bhāṣya is teaching us how to discriminate the factors constituting dhyāna, not yet supplying a general theory of Concept or Judgment.
