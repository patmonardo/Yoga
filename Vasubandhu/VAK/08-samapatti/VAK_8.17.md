# VAkK 8.17

## Sanskrit

चतुर्धा शुद्धकं हानभागीयादि यथाक्रमम् ।
क्लेशोत्पत्तिस्वभूम्यूर्ध्वानास्रवानुगुणं हि तत् ॥ VAkK_8.17 ॥

## IAST

caturdhā śuddhakaṃ hānabhāgīyādi yathākramam /

## Source Caution

The verse presupposes the standard fourfold division of pure attainment: decline-conducive, stability-conducive, distinction-conducive, and penetration-conducive.

## Grammar

- **caturdhā** — “in four ways.”
- **hānabhāgīyādi** — “beginning with that conducive to decline.”
- **yathākramam** — “respectively, in the stated order.”
- **anuguṇam** — “conducive, favorable, corresponding.”

## Literal Translation

Pure attainment is fourfold, beginning with that conducive to decline. Respectively, it is conducive to the arising of defilements, to its own level, to a higher level, and to the uncontaminated.

## Philosophical Translation

Worldly purity contains four distinct tendencies: relapse, maintenance, ascent, and breakthrough. A pure attainment must therefore be judged not only by its present quality, but by the direction toward which its inner disposition tends.

## Technical Vocabulary

- **hānabhāgīya** — conducive to decline.
- **sthitibhāgīya** — conducive to stability on its own level.
- **viśeṣabhāgīya** — conducive to a higher distinction or level.
- **nirvedhabhāgīya** — conducive to penetration and the uncontaminated.
- **anuguṇa** — tending toward; favorable to.

## Doctrinal Determination

The four pure modes are defined by their causal orientation:

1. toward renewed defilement;
2. toward stabilization on the same ground;
3. toward a higher ground;
4. toward uncontaminated attainment.

Thus “pure” names a genus containing qualitatively different developmental powers.

## Logical Determination

```text
PureAttainment
    ├── decline-conducive → defilement
    ├── stability-conducive → same level
    ├── distinction-conducive → higher level
    └── penetration-conducive → uncontaminated
```

The difference lies in immanent tendency, not merely external result.

## Organon Light

This is a precise qualitative division. The same apparent purity can contain contrary futures. Its truth is disclosed by its transition-potential: regression, persistence, elevation, or liberation.
