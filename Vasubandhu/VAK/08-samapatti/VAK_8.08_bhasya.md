# VAK_8.08 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> tṛtīye pañca tūpekṣā smṛtiḥ prajñā sukhaṃ sthitiḥ /
> catvāryante 'sukhāduḥkhopekṣāsmṛtisamādhayaḥ // VAK_8.08 //

**Best current translation:**  
In the third dhyāna there are five limbs: equanimity, recollection, discriminative knowing, ease, and stability. In the fourth there are four: neither-pain-nor-pleasure, purified equanimity, purified recollection, and samādhi.

## Source Caution

The Bhāṣya materially sharpens two expressions from the Kārikā.

First, the Kārikā's `prajñā` in the third dhyāna is glossed as `saṃprajñāna`. The Bhāṣya therefore supports reading this limb as active clear discrimination or full knowing within the meditative field, rather than importing a broader technical sense of `prajñā` without qualification.

Second, the fourth dhyāna does not merely contain bare `upekṣā` and `smṛti`; Vasubandhu specifies `upekṣāpariśuddhi` and `smṛtipariśuddhi`. The purificatory qualification belongs to the doctrinal determination of the fourth dhyāna.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The third dhyāna has five limbs:

```text
upekṣā
smṛti
saṃprajñāna
sukha
samādhi
```

The Kārikā uses `sthiti` for the fifth limb. Vasubandhu explicitly states that `sthiti` is a synonym (`paryāya`) of `samādhi`, and supports this with a sūtra definition of right samādhi as the stability (`sthiti`) of citta.

The fourth and final form-dhyāna has four limbs:

```text
aduḥkhāsukhā vedanā
upekṣāpariśuddhi
smṛtipariśuddhi
samādhi
```

Vasubandhu then totals the dhyāna limbs by name at eighteen:

```text
first dhyāna      5
second dhyāna     4
third dhyāna      5
fourth dhyāna     4
                  —
                 18
```

The qualification `nāmata evam` is important: this is the count **by name**. The next verse will ask how many distinct dharmas there are in substance (`dravyataḥ`).


Then:

```text

but only

```

This prepares the distinction between repeated names/functions across dhyānas and distinct dharmas counted in substance.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃprajñāna** — clear or complete discriminative knowing within the third dhyāna
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss of Kārikā `prajñā`
  - **function:** specifies the cognitive limb more precisely than the compressed verse

- **samādhiparyāya** — a synonym or alternate designation of samādhi
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation here
  - **function:** identifies `sthiti` as another name for the samādhi limb

- **upekṣāpariśuddhi** — purification of equanimity
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of Kārikā `upekṣā`
  - **function:** marks the refined quality of equanimity in the fourth dhyāna

- **smṛtipariśuddhi** — purification of recollection
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of Kārikā `smṛti`
  - **function:** marks the refined quality of recollection in the fourth dhyāna

- **nāmataḥ** — by name or designation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classificatory term
  - **function:** distinguishes the eighteen named limbs from the coming count by distinct dharma/substance

## Doctrinal Determination

### Third dhyāna

The Bhāṣya replaces any temptation to read the third dhyāna as merely a quieter pleasure-state. Its five-limb constitution includes both retention and discrimination:

```text
upekṣā
    balanced relation to the field

smṛti
    recollective retention

saṃprajñāna
    clear discrimination

sukha
    ease

samādhi
    stability / one-pointed collectedness
```

The pair `smṛti + saṃprajñāna` is especially significant: the state remains cognitively articulated.

### Fourth dhyāna

The fourth dhyāna removes `sukha` and has neutral feeling (`aduḥkhāsukhā vedanā`), but this is not cognitive blankness.

Its positive constitution is:

```text
neutral feeling
purified equanimity
purified recollection
samādhi
```

Thus the highest form-dhyāna is defined not merely by subtraction of affective disturbance, but by purification of the factors that maintain and regulate the field.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā-pass translation should be sharpened in two places.

For the third dhyāna:

```text
prajñā
    → in this passage, saṃprajñāna
    → clear discriminative knowing
```

For the fourth:

```text
upekṣā
    → purified equanimity

smṛti
    → purified recollection
```

The Bhāṣya therefore favors:

> In the third dhyāna there are five limbs: equanimity, recollection, clear discriminative knowing, ease, and stability. In the fourth there are four: neither-pain-nor-pleasure, purified equanimity, purified recollection, and samādhi.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.08 completes the four-dhyāna limb architecture but immediately introduces a new distinction:

```text
name-count
    ≠
substance-count
```

The same dharma may recur under several dhyānas and therefore be counted repeatedly by name while remaining one type of factor in the substantial classification.

The verse also shows that ascent is not simple subtraction:

```text
third → fourth

sukha removed
    but
upekṣā becomes purified
smṛti becomes purified
samādhi remains
```

The higher configuration is therefore determined by **qualitative purification of retained factors**, not only by absence of earlier factors.

## Light Organon Contact

The immediate Kośa result is sufficient:

```text
Dhyāna
    = structured whole

Aṅga
    = determinate factor within that whole

same factor-name
    may recur across levels

but
its qualification may change with the level
```

This is particularly clear in the fourth dhyāna:

```text
upekṣā
    → upekṣā-pariśuddhi

smṛti
    → smṛti-pariśuddhi
```

So the chapter is not merely tracking which marks are present. It is determining the **mode in which a factor functions inside a particular attained configuration**.

The stronger Organon interpretation can remain reserved until the complete factor analysis is in hand.
