# VAK_8.41_bhasya

## Kārikā

### IAST

`nimīlite śāstari lokacakṣuṣi kṣayaṃ gate sākṣijane ca bhūyasā /`
`adṛṣṭatattvair niravagrahaiḥ kṛtaṃ kutārkikaiḥ śāsanam etad ākulam //`

### Best Current Translation

When the Teacher, the Eye of the World, had closed his eyes, and when the community of direct witnesses had for the most part passed away, this Teaching was thrown into confusion by bad reasoners who had not seen reality and were without restraint.

Kārikā anchor: `VAK_8.41.md`

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 460|04–460|07:

```text
nimīlite śāstari lokacakṣuṣi kṣayaṃ gate sākṣijane ca bhūyasā /
adṛṣṭatattvairniravagrahaiḥ kṛtaṃ kutārkikaiḥ śāsanametadākulam // VAkK_8.41 //
```

No prose gloss follows this verse in the transmitted Bhāṣya text used for this project; the verse itself carries the argument.

## Bhāṣya Translation

When the Teacher, the Eye of the World, had closed his eyes, and when the community of witnesses had for the most part disappeared, this Teaching was made confused by bad reasoners who had not seen reality and who lacked restraint.

## Natural Argument

The previous verse distinguished school-method and authorial exposition from the final authority of realized sages. VAK 8.41 now supplies the historical danger that motivates that distinction.

Once the Buddha is gone and the population of direct witnesses (`sākṣijana`) has largely disappeared, reasoning no longer operates under the same living constraint. Those who have not seen the truth (`adṛṣṭatattva`) may nevertheless reason without restraint (`niravagraha`). The result is not simply disagreement; it is disorder within the Teaching itself (`śāsanam ... ākulam`).

The target is therefore not reasoning as such. Vasubandhu has just completed one of the most intensely analytical works in Indian philosophy. The target is reasoning that has become detached from realization and from any effective discipline governing its claims.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Teacher present
+ realized witnesses present
        ↓
reasoning remains answerable to living realization

Teacher absent
+ witnesses disappear
        ↓
unrestrained reasoning by the unrealized
        ↓
confusion of the Teaching
```

## New Technical Terms

- **lokacakṣus** — “Eye of the World,” epithet of the Buddha.
  - **status:** poetic term in the Kārikā.
  - **function:** identifies the Teacher as the world's organ of seeing.

- **sākṣijana** — community of witnesses; those who directly witnessed or realized the truth.
  - **status:** prominent in this closing passage.
  - **function:** supplies the living evidential/realizational constraint on doctrine.

- **adṛṣṭatattva** — one who has not seen reality/truth.
  - **status:** Kārikā term.
  - **function:** distinguishes discursive ability from realization.

- **niravagraha** — unrestrained, unchecked.
  - **status:** Kārikā term.
  - **function:** characterizes reasoning no longer governed by adequate limits.

- **kutārkika** — bad reasoner, sophist, false dialectician.
  - **status:** Kārikā term.
  - **function:** names the agent of doctrinal confusion.

- **ākula** — confused, disordered, disturbed.
  - **status:** Kārikā term.
  - **function:** describes the resulting condition of the Teaching.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, in the strongly personal and evaluative closing voice of the treatise.

## Doctrinal Determination

Vasubandhu closes neither by rejecting reason nor by privileging mute experience. His complaint presupposes the opposite: reasoning is indispensable, but it becomes dangerous when the distinction between **having an argument** and **having seen the truth** collapses.

The verse therefore protects the asymmetry:

```text
tarka
    can articulate and test

sākṣātkāra / tattvadarśana
    supplies a realization that tarka alone cannot manufacture
```

The precise positive relation between these must not be inferred beyond what this closing verse states.

## Translation Consequences

- `sākṣijana` should retain the sense of witnesses, not merely “disciples.”
- `adṛṣṭatattva` is literally those who have not seen reality/truth; this is stronger than “uninformed.”
- `kutārkika` is explicitly pejorative: bad or sophistical reasoners.
- `niravagraha` contributes the important qualification “unrestrained / unchecked.”

## Logical Determination

This is a theory of failure in transmission:

```text
formal reasoning
without realized constraint
        ↓
self-authorizing speculation
        ↓
systemic disorder
```

The verse thereby complements VAK 8.39. If the realized side of Saddharma disappears, the transmitted side does not simply remain intact. Its interpretation becomes increasingly vulnerable to distortion.

## Light Organon Contact

For our later work this is a useful warning: formal power is not self-validating. A technical system can become more elaborate precisely while losing contact with what originally constrained it.

That is a methodological warning worth carrying forward, but the present file leaves it at Vasubandhu's own distinction between witness and bad reasoning.
