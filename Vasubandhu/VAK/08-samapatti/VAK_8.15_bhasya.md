# VAK_8.15 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
tṛtīyādyāvad ūrdhvādho 'nāsravānantaraṃ śubham /
utpadyate tathā śuddhāt kliṣṭaṃ cāpi svabhūmikam // VAK_8.15 //
```

## Best Current Translation

Immediately after an uncontaminated attainment, a wholesome attainment may arise on the same level or above or below, within the permitted range extending to the third level. The same holds after a pure attainment, with the additional possibility that a defiled attainment of that same level may arise.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.15.md`](./VAK_8.15.md)

## Source Caution

The source prints `kliṭaṃ` in the verse; this is normalized to `kliṣṭaṃ`.

The compact phrase `tṛtīyādyāvat` is clarified by the prose. Vasubandhu is describing the range permitted in immediate meditative succession, especially in `vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti`, skip-attainment practice. The practitioner cannot leap beyond the third level in one such transition.

`Śubha` is also explicitly broader here than `śuddha`: the Bhāṣya says that the term includes both `śuddha` and `anāsrava`, because uncontaminated attainment is wholesome (`kuśala`).

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The discussion opens with a precise question: after each particular `samāpatti-dravya`, how many and which attainments can immediately arise?

Vasubandhu first gives examples rather than jumping directly to the rule.

After uncontaminated first dhyāna, six attainments can follow: pure and uncontaminated forms on the same level, and pure/uncontaminated forms of the second and third dhyānas.

After the sphere of nothingness, seven can arise: pure and uncontaminated forms on the same level, neighboring lower formless levels, and at the summit of existence only the pure mode, since there is no uncontaminated `bhavāgra`.

After second dhyāna, eight arise; after infinity of knowledge, nine; and analogous cases can reach ten.

The Kārikā then compresses these examples into a general law.

After an uncontaminated attainment:

```text
same level
    pure
    uncontaminated

higher or lower levels
    pure and/or uncontaminated
    within the permitted range
```

The limit is stated as `tṛtīyādyāvat`: practitioners of skip-attainment (`vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti`) cannot leap farther than the third level in a single transition.

Vasubandhu then adds a knowledge-condition for entering the formless attainments: they are entered immediately after `anvayajñāna`, not after `dharmajñāna`, because dharmajñāna has a lower support and object.

The second half then gives the rule after a pure attainment. Everything already stated for succession after anāsrava remains possible, but one additional transition appears:

```text
pure attainment
    → defiled attainment of the same level
```

This extra transition is explicitly impossible after an uncontaminated attainment.

## Argument Reconstruction

The transition structure is:

```text
ANĀSRAVA
    ↓
    same-level śuddha
    same-level anāsrava
    admissible higher śubha
    admissible lower śubha

but never
    ↓
    kliṣṭa
```

For pure attainment:

```text
ŚUDDHA
    ↓
    all admissible wholesome transitions
    +
    same-level kliṣṭa
```

Thus the difference between pure and uncontaminated attainment appears not merely in their intrinsic quality but in their **possible immediate successors**.

The rule is asymmetric:

```text
śuddha → kliṣṭa
    possible on same bhūmi

anāsrava → kliṣṭa
    impossible immediately
```

This is the central doctrinal determination of the verse.

## New Technical Terms

- **samanantara / anantara** — immediately successive, with no intervening attainment.
  - **status:** previously encountered in causal and cognitive contexts; now applied specifically to samāpatti succession.
  - **function:** determines legal immediate transitions among attainment-states.

- **vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti** — skip-attainment; a mode in which intermediate meditative levels are passed over within a bounded range.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific clarification in this immediate context.
  - **function:** explains the `tṛtīyādyāvat` range restriction.

- **anvayajñāna** — knowledge of conformity/sequence, here the knowledge after which formless attainments may immediately arise.
  - **status:** treated extensively in Chapter 7.
  - **function:** provides the appropriate cognitive predecessor for entry into ārūpya.

- **dharmajñāna** — knowledge of dharmas, here excluded as an immediate predecessor of ārūpya because of its lower support and object.
  - **status:** extensively treated in Chapter 7.
  - **function:** contrast-case defining the cognitive condition for transition into formless attainment.

- **svabhūmika** — belonging to the same ground or meditative level.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** restricts the pure-to-defiled transition to the same level.

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Samāpatti is governed by succession rules

The chapter has already classified samāpatti by:

```text
level
mode
factor-constitution
acquisition
```

VAK 8.15 adds:

```text
immediate successor
```

A meditative attainment is therefore not fully characterized merely by what it is. It must also be known by what can arise directly from it.

### 2. `Śubha` includes pure and uncontaminated

The Bhāṣya explicitly states:

```text
śubha
    includes śuddha
    includes anāsrava
```

because anāsrava is kuśala.

This corrects any reading in which `śubha` here would mean only worldly-pure attainment.

### 3. Uncontaminated attainment cannot immediately generate defilement

This is the strongest qualitative restriction:

```text
anāsrava
    ↛ kliṣṭa
```

The uncontaminated mode has no immediately succeeding defiled attainment.

### 4. Pure attainment can immediately decline in mode without changing level

By contrast:

```text
śuddha on bhūmi X
    → kliṣṭa on bhūmi X
```

The meditative level is preserved while its qualitative mode deteriorates.

### 5. Vertical movement is bounded

Higher and lower transitions are possible, but not arbitrarily. The skip-attainment practitioner cannot leap beyond the stated range.

Thus meditative succession is neither rigidly adjacent nor freely discontinuous.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation should be sharpened in three respects.

First, `śubham` should not simply be translated as a generic wholesome state without explanation. The Bhāṣya specifically includes both pure and uncontaminated samāpatti under it.

Second, `tṛtīyādyāvat` is not merely a vague reference to “the third and following.” It marks a bounded range of upward/downward meditative transition clarified through skip-attainment practice.

Third, the phrase `kliṣṭaṃ ... svabhūmikam` is restrictive: the defiled attainment arising after a pure one must belong to the **same bhūmi**.

## Logical Determination

The text has moved decisively from a taxonomy of states to a topology of admissible succession.

```text
State identity
    level
    mode
    factors

Transition identity
    permitted successor
    forbidden successor
    maximum displacement
```

The most important prohibition is:

```text
anāsrava
    cannot immediately become kliṣṭa
```

while pure attainment contains the possibility:

```text
same bhūmi
śuddha → kliṣṭa
```

So purity and uncontaminatedness differ not merely as predicates attached to an otherwise identical state. They differ in their **transition-powers**.

## Light Organon Contact

This verse gives strong textual support for a restrained principle already emerging from the chapter:

> A determination is partly defined by its lawful transitions.

But the Kośa itself supplies the content. We need not import a general Systems theory to see it.

The difference between `śuddha` and `anāsrava` is now operationally testable:

```text
ask what can immediately follow
```

Anāsrava excludes immediate defilement; worldly purity does not.

Likewise, ascent and descent are not arbitrary motion through an ordered list. They are bounded by explicit meditative capabilities and cognitive conditions.

The chapter is therefore giving a practical science of transition among determinate meditative configurations.
