# VAK_8.03 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> vibhūtarūpasaṃjñākhyāḥ saha sāmantakais tribhiḥ /
> nārūpye rūpasadbhāvaḥ rūpotpattis tu cittataḥ // VAK_8.03 //

**Best current translation:**  
The formless attainments are so designated through the disappearance of form-perception, together with three neighboring preparatory attainments. In the formless domain there is no presence of form; but the arising of form is from citta.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.03.md`](./VAK_8.03.md)

## Source Caution

The working e-text is a reference text based principally on Pradhan with corrections from Shastri and is not a critical edition. Several spellings in this passage are visibly unstable (`ārupya/ārūpya`, `rupotpatti/rūpotpatti`, etc.); the source excerpt preserves them rather than silently normalizing them.

The argument from 434|16 through 434|22 is textually compressed and partly omitted above only for readability. The natural argumentative sequence is nevertheless clear: an opponent offers several canonical relations between life, heat, name-and-form, and vijñāna as grounds for requiring rūpa in the formless realm; Vasubandhu rejects that inference by restricting the scope of those scriptural statements.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The designation `vibhūtarūpasaṃjña` applies to the formless attainments and three neighboring preparatory states. One exception is the preparatory state immediately neighboring the sphere of infinite space: because that state still takes the fourth dhyāna as object-support, form-perception has not yet disappeared there.

The Bhāṣya then returns to the previous verse's claim that the formless attainments consist of four aggregates. This requires proof that rūpa is genuinely absent in the formless domain.

**Voice: Objector**

Perhaps the term `ārūpya` does not mean absolute absence of rūpa. It might mean only that rūpa is exceedingly slight or subtle, just as a slightly tawny thing can receive a privative-looking designation.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

What such residual rūpa could be is unclear. If it is merely bodily and verbal restraint, those restraints would themselves require a material basis; a derivative material factor cannot coherently exist when the elemental basis is absent.

**Voice: Objector**

Canonical statements appear to require rūpa wherever citta or vijñāna exists: life and heat are spoken of as conjoined; nāma and rūpa are mutually dependent like two bundles of reeds; nāmarūpa is conditioned by vijñāna; and vijñāna is denied movement or establishment apart from rūpa and the remaining aggregates. Therefore rūpa must exist even in the formless realm.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Those statements cannot simply be universalized without determining their intended scope. If every unqualified relation were applied without restriction, absurd consequences would follow. The texts also explicitly describe the formless attainments as an escape from forms, as peaceful liberations that have passed beyond forms, and as the condition of beings who have completely transcended perceptions of form.

The decisive point is that beings in the formless domain have passed beyond the entire class (`kṛtsnā rūpajāti`) of rūpa, while they have not passed beyond the classes of feeling and the other nonmaterial aggregates. The canonical enumeration of what exists in the dhyānas includes rūpa through vijñāna; the corresponding enumeration for the ārūpyas begins with vedanā and extends through vijñāna, omitting rūpa. If rūpa genuinely existed there, that omission would be inexplicable.

Therefore:

```text
nārūpye rūpasadbhāvaḥ
```

There is no presence of rūpa in the formless realm.

This immediately creates a new problem.

**Voice: Objector / Question**

If rūpa has been cut off for many kalpas, how can rūpa arise again for beings who fall from a formless existence?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

```text
rūpotpattis tu cittataḥ
```

The arising of rūpa is from citta.

More precisely, rūpa arises from citta that has itself been conditioned or impressed (`paribhāvita`) by the karmic cause capable of producing that rūpa as result (`tad-vipāka-hetu`). When such citta obtains the relevant occasion or functioning (`labdha-vṛtti`), rūpa can arise again.

A final objection asks how citta can function without relying upon rūpa. Vasubandhu rejects the inference from ordinary observation: the fact that we do not observe citta without rūpa here does not establish universal impossibility, just as the absence here of citta sustained without gross nutriment does not establish that such functioning cannot occur in the rūpadhātu.

## New Technical Terms

- **vibhūtarūpasaṃjña** — designation characterized by the disappearance of perception of form
  - **status:** compressed in the Kārikā and explicitly delimited in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** naming condition for the ārūpyas and relevant neighboring attainments

- **rūpajāti** — the entire class or kind of rūpa
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** strengthens the claim from absence of a particular gross form to transcendence of the whole rūpa-class

- **analpakalpocchinna** — cut off for no small number of kalpas; absent through many kalpas
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** sharpens the regeneration problem by excluding simple short-term continuity of rūpa

- **tadvipākahetu** — the cause whose maturation/result is the relevant rūpa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only causal specification here
  - **function:** identifies the karmic ground through which citta can later produce renewed form

- **paribhāvita** — conditioned, impregnated, or dispositionalized by a cause
  - **status:** previously encountered in broader Abhidharma usage
  - **function:** expresses retained causal determination without requiring the later effect to remain presently instantiated

- **labdhavṛtti** — having obtained operative activity or an occasion for functioning
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** marks activation of the latent causal determination in the citta-series

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes the Kārikā much stronger than a simple classification of realms.

First, Vasubandhu explicitly denies a concealed-materialist reading of ārūpya. The formless domain does not contain an extremely subtle remainder of rūpa that merely escapes ordinary classification. The entire rūpa-class has been passed beyond.

Second, the reappearance of rūpa therefore cannot be explained through numerical persistence of the previous form. The relevant continuity lies instead in the causal determination of citta:

```text
prior karma capable of rūpa-vipāka
        ↓
conditions citta
        ↓
rūpa absent during ārūpya existence
        ↓
citta-series continues with causal determination
        ↓
appropriate operation / occasion
        ↓
renewed rūpa
```

The Bhāṣya thus separates three things that must not be conflated:

```text
present constituent
    rūpa may be absent

causal determination
    rūpa-producing condition may remain operative through citta

future result
    rūpa may arise again
```

This is not the claim that citta creates matter arbitrarily. `cittataḥ` is immediately qualified by `tad-vipāka-hetu-paribhāvita`: the citta from which rūpa arises is itself causally formed by the relevant maturation-ground.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation "the arising of form is from mind" is philologically sound but too easy to read as unrestricted mental creation.

The Bhāṣya requires the fuller determination:

> There is no presence of rūpa in the formless realm; nevertheless, rūpa can arise again from citta that has been conditioned by the karmic cause producing that rūpa as maturation-result.

Accordingly, `cittataḥ` should remain "from citta" in the canonical verse translation, while the Bhāṣya note must preserve its causal qualification.

The first half is also clarified: the designation based on disappearance of form-perception does not apply to the preliminary state immediately neighboring the sphere of infinite space, because that preliminary state still takes the fourth dhyāna as object-support.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.03 distinguishes **persistence of a form** from **persistence of its ground of regeneration**.

```text
rūpa(t1)
    present

ārūpya interval
    rūpa = absent

citta-continuity
    causally determined by rūpa-producing karma

rūpa(t2)
    newly arisen
```

Therefore:

> **Identity of result across a temporal interruption does not require continuous existence of the result-form.**

What must persist is not the presently instantiated rūpa but the determinate causal capacity by which a corresponding rūpa can again arise.

A second rule follows:

> **Absence from constitution does not entail absence from the space of lawful genesis.**

This is exactly why the two halves of the Kārikā belong together:

```text
nārūpye rūpasadbhāvaḥ
    denial at the level of present constitution

rūpotpattis tu cittataḥ
    affirmation at the level of genesis
```

The Bhāṣya refuses both opposite reductions:

```text
future regeneration
    does not prove hidden present persistence

present absence
    does not prove future impossibility
```

## Light Organon Contact

This passage gives a particularly strong systems distinction.

A system cannot identify Meaning with the persistence of an instantiated Form if it can lawfully regenerate that Form after the Form has ceased to be a constituent of the current state.

```text
Form
    instantiated configuration

Formless interval
    Form genuinely absent

retained determination
    not hidden Form
    but generative / causal structure

regenerated Form
    new instantiation grounded by that retained determination
```

The disciplined Organon contact is therefore:

> **Meaning is not demonstrated by merely preserving Form; a stronger test is whether the system retains sufficient lawful determination to regenerate an appropriate Form after that Form has genuinely disappeared.**

Vasubandhu's own mechanism must remain distinct from our systems reconstruction. His claim is specifically karmic and Abhidharmic:

```text
citta
    paribhāvita by tad-vipāka-hetu
        ↓
labdha-vṛtti
        ↓
rūpotpatti
```

But formally this is already a genesis architecture rather than a storage architecture.

That gives Chapter 8 a significant new progression:

```text
8.01
samāpatti = wholesome one-pointedness

8.02
higher configuration through viveka from lower ground

8.03
constituent Form can disappear entirely
while its lawful possibility of regeneration remains grounded
```

The next question is therefore not merely what a formless state lacks, but how the four ārūpya attainments are positively determined by their distinct modes of `manasikāra`.
