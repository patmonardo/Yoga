# VAK_8.20 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> āryākiṃcanyasāṃmukhyād bhavāgre tv āsravakṣayaḥ /
> satṛṣṇāḥ svabhavālambāḥ dhyānaṃ sadviṣayaṃ śubham // VAK_8.20 //

**Best current translation:**  
At the summit of existence, the destruction of the outflows occurs through making the noble attainment of nothingness directly present. Craving-associated attainments take their own existence as object-support; wholesome dhyāna has unrestricted object-range.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.20.md`](./VAK_8.20.md)

## Source Caution

The Kārikā e-text reads `sadviṣayaṃ`, not an unambiguous `ṣaḍviṣayam`. The Bhāṣya settles the sense by glossing wholesome dhyāna as `sarvālambana`, taking as object-support whatever exists, conditioned or unconditioned. The Bhāṣya therefore does **not** support the first-pass interpretation “six object-fields” as the primary meaning here.

The translation should consequently be revised toward **unrestricted / all-object-support** rather than “sixfold object-range.”

## Natural Argument

### 1. An exception to the lower-attainment rule

Vasubandhu explicitly announces an exception:

```text
utsarga kṛtvā 'pavādaṃ karoti
having stated the general rule, he makes an exception
```

VAK 8.19 had said that one born on a higher ground does not ordinarily make a lower attainment present because it would be useless. The exception is the summit of existence (`bhavāgra`).

A being born there can make present the **uncontaminated sphere of nothingness** (`anāsrava-ākiṃcanyāyatana`). Through that directly present noble attainment, destruction of the outflows occurs.

The prose gives two grounds:

```text
svasyābhāvāt
    because an uncontaminated attainment of its own ground is absent

tasya cābhyāsāt
    and because that lower noble attainment has been cultivated
```

Thus a lower attainment becomes necessary precisely because the highest conditioned ground lacks its own uncontaminated form.

### 2. From support to object-support

The Bhāṣya then asks a new question:

```text
athaiṣāṃ dhyānārūpyāṇāṃ kimālambanam /
What is the object-support of these dhyānas and formless attainments?
```

This marks a clean shift from:

```text
āśraya
    who / what existential level supports the attainment
```

to:

```text
ālambana
    what the attainment takes as object-support
```

### 3. Craving-associated attainments are confined to their own existence

The phrase `satṛṣṇāḥ svabhavālambāḥ` is explained through the already established `āsvādanāsaṃprayukta` mode.

Such attainments take as object-support the conditioned existence belonging to their own ground:

```text
svabhūmikaṃ bhavam ālambante
```

Here `bhava` is explicitly explained as **sāsrava-vastu**, contaminated conditioned reality.

Their limits are then stated negatively:

```text
not lower
    because dispassion from the lower has already been achieved

not higher
    because the grounds are delimited by craving

not uncontaminated
    because that would entail wholesomeness
```

So craving itself delimits the cognitive field.

### 4. Wholesome dhyāna is all-object-support

The final pāda is glossed:

```text
kuśalaṃ dhyānaṃ śubhakamanāsravaṃ ca /
```

Here wholesome dhyāna includes both:

```text
śuddhaka
    worldly-pure

anāsrava
    uncontaminated
```

Vasubandhu then states:

```text
tat sarvālambanam
```

It can take as object-support whatever exists, whether:

```text
saṃskṛta
    conditioned

asaṃskṛta
    unconditioned
```

This is a much stronger object-range claim than the first-pass “six objects” reading.

## Argument Reconstruction

The verse brings together two distinct kinds of freedom.

First, **freedom of attainment-support**:

```text
bhavāgra
    highest conditioned ground

but no native anāsrava there
    ↓
make lower noble ākiṃcanyāyatana present
    ↓
āsravakṣaya
```

Second, **freedom of object-support**:

```text
craving-associated samāpatti
    → own-ground contaminated existence only

wholesome dhyāna
    → all conditioned and unconditioned objects
```

The highest conditioned location therefore does not imply the widest liberative power, while wholesome dhyāna is distinguished from savoring precisely by the freedom of its object-range.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the exception at bhavāgra and the object-support ranges of the samāpattis.

No separate opponent is required in this passage; the questions function as internal transitions in the exposition.

## New Technical Terms

- **utsarga / apavāda** — general rule / exception
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās; operative in the Bhāṣya prose
  - **function:** logical distinction structuring the relation between VAK 8.19 and 8.20

- **sarvālambana** — taking everything as object-support; unrestricted object-support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the compressed Kārikā phrase `sadviṣayam`
  - **function:** defines the object-range of wholesome dhyāna

- **tṛṣṇāparicchinna** — delimited by craving
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical explanation in this passage
  - **function:** explains why a craving-associated attainment cannot range to higher grounds

- **abhyāsa** — prior cultivation / habituation
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptually
  - **function:** causal explanation for making the lower noble attainment present at bhavāgra

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Cosmological height and liberative efficacy diverge

Bhavāgra is the highest conditioned ground but has no uncontaminated attainment of its own. Liberation there therefore depends upon making a lower noble attainment present.

```text
highest bhūmi
    ≠
highest liberative resource
```

### 2. The exception is principled

The lower ākiṃcanyāyatana is not manifested arbitrarily. Two necessary conditions are named:

```text
absence of a native uncontaminated option
    +
prior cultivation of the lower noble attainment
```

### 3. Craving constrains object-range

The savoring-associated attainment is reflexively bound to its own contaminated existence.

Its object-range is limited because:

```text
lower is already abandoned
higher lies beyond the craving-delimited ground
anāsrava would contradict its craving-bound character
```

### 4. Wholesome dhyāna has universal object-support

The Bhāṣya's `sarvālambana` means that wholesome dhyāna may take conditioned or unconditioned dharmas as object-support.

This applies to both worldly-pure and uncontaminated dhyāna.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation:

> wholesome dhyāna has six object-fields

should be revised.

The source text writes `sadviṣayam`, and the Bhāṣya glosses it as:

```text
sarvālambanam
```

A better canonical rendering is therefore:

> **wholesome dhyāna has unrestricted object-support**

or more literally:

> **wholesome dhyāna takes all as object-support.**

This is a substantive Bhāṣya correction to the Kārikā-pass interpretation.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.20 distinguishes **location**, **operative resource**, and **object-range**.

```text
location
    bhavāgra

operative liberative resource
    noble ākiṃcanyāyatana

result
    āsravakṣaya
```

The second half gives another relation:

```text
mode of attainment
    determines
range of admissible object-supports
```

More precisely:

```text
tṛṣṇā
    → pariccheda
    → restricted field

śubha / kuśala dhyāna
    → sarvālambana
    → unrestricted field
```

The text therefore makes cognitive range itself a qualitative determination of samāpatti.

## Light Organon Contact

The strongest contact point is not “higher versus lower,” but **systematic freedom versus determination by a local condition**.

The craving-associated attainment is not false because it lacks an object. It has a perfectly determinate object-field—but that field is bounded by the very craving that constitutes its mode.

```text
craving
    fixes the admissible horizon
```

Wholesome dhyāna, by contrast, is defined by a widened freedom of object-support:

```text
conditioned
    +
unconditioned
    ↓
sarvālambana
```

This is close to the project's emerging thought that knowing is not adequately defined by an isolated cognitive act. One must specify the **system of admissible objects** within which that knowing can operate.

That comparison remains provisional; the Bhāṣya's own claim is simply that different samāpatti-modes are distinguished by different object-ranges.
