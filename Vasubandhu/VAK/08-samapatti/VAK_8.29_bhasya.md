# VAK_8.29_bhasya

## Kārikā

**IAST**

`apramāṇāni catvāri vyāpādādivipakṣataḥ /`
`maitry adveṣo 'pi karuṇā muditā sumanaskatā // VAK_8.29 //`

## Best Current Translation

There are four Immeasurables because they stand as counterforces to ill will and the remaining afflictive tendencies. Friendliness is non-hatred, and compassion likewise; sympathetic joy is gladness.

See: [VAK_8.29.md](./VAK_8.29.md)

## Natural Argument

Having completed the exposition of samādhi itself, Vasubandhu now turns to qualities that depend upon samādhi. The first group is the four `apramāṇas`: maitrī, karuṇā, muditā, and upekṣā.

They are called immeasurable because their object-support is an immeasurable multitude of living beings (`apramāṇasattvālambanatvāt`). Their fourfold number is not arbitrary. Each functions as a counterforce to a determinate affective distortion.

Vasubandhu lists the opposed tendencies in sequence: ill will (`vyāpāda`), harmfulness (`viṃhisā`), discontent (`arati`), and the predominance of sensual desire and ill will (`kāmarāgavyāpāda`). The four immeasurables are cultivated, correspondingly, for the abandonment of these tendencies.

An objection arises because both impurity contemplation (`aśubha`) and equanimity (`upekṣā`) are said to oppose sensual desire. The Vaibhāṣikas distinguish them by saying that impurity opposes attachment to visible form while equanimity opposes sexual desire. Vasubandhu proposes a different distinction: impurity opposes sexual desire, whereas equanimity opposes attachment rooted in familial relations—mother, father, child, and kin.

The verse then classifies the mental nature of the first three immeasurables. Maitrī is by nature non-hatred (`adveṣa`). Compassion is likewise (`karuṇāpy evam`). Sympathetic joy, by contrast, is by nature gladness (`saumanasya`).

## Argument Reconstruction

1. The exposition has shifted from samādhi itself to qualities supported by samādhi.
2. The first such qualities are the four immeasurables.
3. They are called immeasurable because they take an immeasurable multitude of beings as object-support.
4. Their number is four because four distinct affective distortions require four corresponding counterforces.
5. These counterforces are not merely sentiments; they are functionally specified antidotes.
6. Maitrī and karuṇā share the mental nature of non-hatred.
7. Muditā has the distinct mental nature of gladness.
8. Upekṣā is introduced but its nature is completed in VAK 8.30.

## New Technical Terms

- **apramāṇa** — immeasurable; so called because its object-support is an immeasurable multitude of beings.
  - **status:** prominent here as a new group dependent upon samādhi.
  - **function:** unrestricted relational cultivation directed toward living beings.
- **viṃhisā** — harmfulness, injurious intention.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit in the antidotal sequence.
  - **function:** distortion opposed by karuṇā.
- **arati** — discontent, aversion, inability to rejoice.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** distortion opposed by muditā.
- **adveṣa** — non-hatred.
  - **status:** previously established mental factor.
  - **function:** intrinsic nature (`svabhāva`) of both maitrī and karuṇā here.
- **saumanasya** — gladness, positive mental feeling.
  - **status:** previously encountered affective term.
  - **function:** intrinsic nature of muditā.

## Voice

- **Vasubandhu:** introduces the four apramāṇas, explains their name, gives their antidotal sequence, and classifies their mental natures.
- **Vaibhāṣika position:** impurity opposes attachment to visible form, while equanimity opposes sexual desire.
- **Vasubandhu's preferred determination:** impurity more properly opposes sexual desire, while equanimity opposes familial attachment.

## Doctrinal Determination

The four immeasurables are not defined first by mystical expansiveness but by a precise twofold structure:

1. **scope:** immeasurable living beings as object-support;
2. **function:** removal of determinate affective distortions.

This gives the group both extension and function. They universalize the yogin's relation to beings by transforming the affective conditions under which beings are encountered.

A significant correction to the first-pass Kārikā analysis is required. The phrase `maitry adveṣo 'pi karuṇā` is explained by the Bhāṣya as:

```text
maitrī = adveṣa
karuṇā = adveṣa likewise
muditā = saumanasya
```

Compassion is therefore not classified as gladness in this passage.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā should be translated:

> Friendliness is non-hatred, and compassion likewise; sympathetic joy is gladness.

rather than grouping both compassion and sympathetic joy under `sumanaskatā`.

`apramāṇa` should retain “Immeasurable,” but the Bhāṣya prevents this from becoming vague metaphysical language: its immediate reason is `apramāṇasattvālambanatva`, taking immeasurable living beings as object-support.

## Logical Determination

The four immeasurables display a striking technical form:

```text
pathological relation
    ↓ opposed by
cultivated counter-relation
    ↓ universalized across
immeasurable living beings
```

The universal is therefore not produced by abstracting from beings. It is produced by removing the determinate distortions that restrict the relation to beings.

This is especially clear with maitrī and karuṇā. Their common basis is `adveṣa`: the relation becomes positive not by adding a sentimental predicate, but by removing hatred as a constitutive determination of the relation.

The distinction between maitrī and karuṇā must therefore arise not from different underlying mental factors here, but from their different intentional forms and objects; VAK 8.30 will specify those forms.

## Light Organon Contact

This passage is particularly valuable for a Rational-Practical architecture because it treats affective universality as **technically constructed**.

The apramāṇas are not transcendental principles. They are concrete relational disciplines dependent upon samādhi. Yet their construction has a recognizably systematic form:

```text
restricted relation
    → determine its distortion
    → cultivate its counterforce
    → remove restriction on scope
    → relation becomes immeasurable
```

That is close to a practical technē of system-building: universality is achieved through explicit determination and correction of the conditions that deform relation.
