# VAK_8.18 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
dve trīṇi trīṇi caikaṃ ca hānabhāgādyanantaram /
gatvāgamya dvidhā bhūmīr aṣṭau śliṣṭaikalaṅghitāḥ // VAK_8.18 //
```

## Best Current Translation

Immediately after the four pure attainments beginning with the decline-conducive one, there arise respectively two, three, three, and one. Having traversed and returned through the eight grounds in two modes, first contiguously and then by skipping one ground, one undertakes the training for crossed attainment.

See also: [VAK_8.18.md](./VAK_8.18.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 445|11–446|06.

```text
athaiṣāṃ caturṇā kati kasmādanantaramutpadyante /

    dve trīṇi trīṇi caikaṃ ca hāna bhāgādyanantaram /

hānabhāgīyaṃ hānabhāk /
tasmādanantaraṃ dve utpadyete /
hānisthitibhāgīye /

sthitibhāgīyānantaraṃ trīṇyanyatra nirvedhabhāgīyāt /

viśeṣabhāgīyādanantaraṃ trīṇyanyatra hānabhāgīyāt /
nirvedhabhāgīyādanantaraṃ tadevaikamiti /

kathaṃ vyutkrāntakasamāpattirutpadyate /

    gatvāgamya dvidhā bhūmīraṣṭau śliṣṭaikalaṅghitāḥ // VAkK_8.18 //

    vyutkrāntakasamāpattirvisabhāgatṛtīyagā /

gatvetyanulomaṃ samāpadya /
āgamyeti pratilomaṃ samāpadya /

dvidheti sāsravānāsravā bhūmīḥ /

śliṣṭā ityanukrameṇa /

sāsravā aṣṭau bhūmiranulomapratilomasamāpattito nirjitya anāsravāśca sapta /

paścātsāsravāt prathamāddhyānāt sāsravaṃ tṛtīyaṃ samāpadyate /
tasmādākāśānantyāyatanaṃ tasmādākiṃcanyāyatanam /

evaṃ punaḥ pratilomaṃ nirjitya anāsravā apyekalaṅghitā anulomapratilomaṃ ca samāpadyate /

```

## Source Caution

The Bhāṣya quotes the opening pāda of VAK 8.19 — `vyutkrāntakasamāpattir visabhāgatṛtīyagā` — while explaining the training begun in VAK 8.18. The natural argument therefore crosses the verse boundary.

This file stops with Vasubandhu's explicit conclusion:

```text
ayaṃ prayogo vyutkrāntakasamāpatteḥ
this is the preparation for crossed attainment
```

The perfected form signaled by `visabhāgatṛtīyagā` belongs to the VAK 8.19 unit.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first completes the fourfold analysis of pure (`śuddhaka`) attainment introduced in VAK 8.17. The question is no longer merely what each pure class tends toward, but how many of the four classes can arise immediately after each one.

The answer is a determinate successor matrix:

```text
hānabhāgīya      → 2
sthitibhāgīya    → 3
viśeṣabhāgīya    → 3
nirvedhabhāgīya  → 1
```

The prose then identifies the actual successors.

After `hānabhāgīya`, two arise:

```text
hānabhāgīya
sthitibhāgīya
```

After `sthitibhāgīya`, three arise, excluding `nirvedhabhāgīya`:

```text
hānabhāgīya
sthitibhāgīya
viśeṣabhāgīya
```

After `viśeṣabhāgīya`, three arise, excluding `hānabhāgīya`:

```text
sthitibhāgīya
viśeṣabhāgīya
nirvedhabhāgīya
```

After `nirvedhabhāgīya`, only itself immediately follows:

```text
nirvedhabhāgīya
    → nirvedhabhāgīya
```

Vasubandhu then opens a second problem:

```text
kathaṃ vyutkrāntakasamāpattir utpadyate /
How does crossed attainment arise?
```

The answer begins with training across the entire field of the eight principal attainments.

`gatvā` means entering them in forward order (`anuloma`).

`āgamya` means entering them in reverse order (`pratiloma`).

`dvidhā` means in two modes:

```text
sāsrava
anāsrava
```

`aṣṭau bhūmīḥ` means the eight grounds constituted by the four dhyānas and four ārūpyas.

`śliṣṭa` means traversing them contiguously, in sequence.

`ekalaṅghita` means skipping one intervening ground at a time.

The order of training is decisive. The practitioner first masters ordinary forward and reverse traversal. Only afterward is one-level skipping trained.

For example, in the worldly series:

```text
first dhyāna
    ↓ skip second
third dhyāna
    ↓ skip fourth
ākāśānantyāyatana
    ↓ skip vijñānānantyāyatana
ākiṃcanyāyatana
```

The same is then mastered in reverse, and likewise for the uncontaminated series.

Vasubandhu calls this entire procedure the `prayoga` — the preparatory training — for `vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti`.

## Argument Reconstruction

The verse contains two levels of transition logic.

### 1. Local successor law

Each pure class has a determinate set of immediately admissible successors:

```text
H = hānabhāgīya
S = sthitibhāgīya
V = viśeṣabhāgīya
N = nirvedhabhāgīya

H → {H, S}
S → {H, S, V}
V → {S, V, N}
N → {N}
```

The numbers `2–3–3–1` therefore encode an adjacency structure, not mere arithmetic.

### 2. Global traversal training

Crossed attainment requires mastery of the complete ordered field:

```text
8 grounds
×
2 modes
×
2 directions
```

but the modes are not perfectly symmetrical: the Bhāṣya speaks of eight worldly grounds and seven uncontaminated grounds, since bhavāgra has no uncontaminated attainment.

The sequence of mastery is:

```text
contiguous forward traversal
contiguous reverse traversal
        ↓
one-level skipping forward
one-level skipping reverse
        ↓
preparation for vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti
```

Thus the capacity to skip arises only after the ordinary sequence has been conquered.

## New Technical Terms

- **vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti** — crossed / skip attainment.
  - **status:** important new technical expression in this chapter.
  - **function:** names the trained capacity to pass across non-adjacent meditative grounds.

- **anuloma** — forward, in regular order.
  - **status:** previously encountered elsewhere in the Kośa; newly functional here for samāpatti traversal.
  - **function:** forward entry through the ordered grounds.

- **pratiloma** — reverse, against the ordinary order.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** reverse traversal of the same attainment series.

- **śliṣṭa** — contiguous, joined, immediately adjacent.
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikā analysis.
  - **function:** marks ordinary sequential traversal before skipping is attempted.

- **utkrama / ekalaṅghana** — crossing over one ground.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of `ekalaṅghita`.
  - **function:** defines the controlled skip used in preparation for crossed attainment.

- **prayoga** — preparation, preparatory practice.
  - **status:** previously encountered technical term.
  - **function:** explicitly distinguishes training for crossed attainment from its perfected accomplishment.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Successor counts among the four pure attainments

| Present pure class | Immediate successors | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| hānabhāgīya | hāna, sthiti | 2 |
| sthitibhāgīya | hāna, sthiti, viśeṣa | 3 |
| viśeṣabhāgīya | sthiti, viśeṣa, nirvedha | 3 |
| nirvedhabhāgīya | nirvedha | 1 |

This reveals a directed structure among the four classes. Their definitions in VAK 8.17 already indicated their tendencies; VAK 8.18 now specifies their actual immediate adjacency relations.

### Crossed attainment presupposes ordinary mastery

The Bhāṣya does not permit arbitrary movement between meditative levels.

The practitioner first conquers the full sequence by neighboring transitions:

```text
adjacent mastery
    before
skip mastery
```

Only after forward and reverse command of the ordinary order is one-level crossing trained.

### Worldly and uncontaminated series

The training is performed in both `sāsrava` and `anāsrava` modes. But Vasubandhu's prose specifies:

```text
8 sāsrava grounds
7 anāsrava grounds
```

because the summit of existence does not possess an uncontaminated mode.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā's second half should not be translated as though `śliṣṭa` and `ekalaṅghita` were two equivalent descriptions of the same traversal.

The Bhāṣya gives a temporal training order:

```text
first contiguous
then one-level skipping
```

A better philosophical rendering is therefore:

> Having traversed and returned through the eight grounds in both modes, first contiguously and then by skipping one ground, one undertakes the training for crossed attainment.

Likewise, `dvidhā` is explicitly:

```text
sāsrava + anāsrava
```

not merely “in two directions”; direction is already expressed through `gatvā / āgamya` and clarified as `anuloma / pratiloma`.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.18 transforms the attainment hierarchy into an explicit transition system.

First comes a local graph:

```text
H ↔ S ↔ V ↔ N
```

but with asymmetric restrictions encoded by the exact successor sets.

Then comes mastery of the global graph:

```text
ordered adjacency
    ↓ complete mastery
controlled non-adjacency
```

The important point is that skipping does not abolish mediation. It presupposes mastery of mediation.

```text
skip
    ≠ ignorance of the middle

skip
    = power to omit the middle
      after the middle has been mastered
```

That is a precise distinction between an accidental gap and a controlled transition.

## Light Organon Contact

This is one of the clearest places in the chapter where samāpatti behaves like a formally constrained state-space.

A state is determined not just by its own content but by:

```text
its lawful neighbors
its admissible successors
its place in the whole traversal
its accessibility under different modes
its controlled skip-relations
```

Yet Vasubandhu's procedure prevents us from identifying systematic freedom with arbitrary jumping. Freedom of transition is acquired through prior command of the ordered sequence.

In project terms, this is suggestive for any later account of a System of cognition:

```text
complete mediation
    → controlled abbreviation of mediation
```

But the stronger Organon reconstruction should wait. Here the Kośa's own result is already sufficient: `vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti` is a trained power grounded in exhaustive traversal.

## Transition to VAK 8.19

The Bhāṣya has already quoted the next defining pāda:

```text
vyutkrāntakasamāpattir visabhāgatṛtīyagā
```

VAK 8.18 establishes the preparation. VAK 8.19 will determine when crossed attainment is actually perfected and who can accomplish it.
