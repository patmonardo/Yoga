# VAK_8.07 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> pañcādye tarkacārau ca prītisaukhyasamādhayaḥ /
> prītyādayaḥ prasādaś ca dvitīye 'ṅgacatuṣṭayam // VAK_8.07 //

**Best current translation:**  
In the first dhyāna there are five factors: vitarka, vicāra, prīti, sukha, and samādhi. In the second there are four factors: prīti, sukha, samādhi, and inner clarity (`prasāda`).

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.07.md`](./VAK_8.07.md)

## Source Caution

The prose explicitly restores the compressed kārikā pair `tarka-cāra` to `vitarka-vicāra`. It also glosses `samādhi` here as `cittaikāgratā`, linking the factor directly back to the chapter-opening definition of samāpatti.

The phrase `samādhiḥ kilāṅgaṃ dhyānaṃ ca` should not be flattened. Vasubandhu is marking an unusual whole/part relation: samādhi is counted as one factor within dhyāna, while dhyāna as a whole is also characterized through samādhi.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu first restricts the coming factor analysis to the dhyānas proper, not the formless attainments.

The first dhyāna has five factors:

```text
vitarka
vicāra
prīti
sukha
cittaikāgratā
```

The prose makes explicit that the last factor is samādhi understood as one-pointedness of mind.

Vasubandhu then notes that samādhi is both a factor and, in a broader sense, the dhyāna itself. The remaining items are the other factors.

To explain this, he gives an analogy: just as an army is called four-limbed while being constituted by its members, so the first dhyāna is five-limbed.

The second dhyāna has four factors:

```text
adhyātmasaṃprasāda
prīti
sukha
cittaikāgratā
```

Thus vitarka and vicāra are absent, while an inward clarification or serene settling (`adhyātmasaṃprasāda`) appears.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
factor analysis applies here to dhyāna
    not ārūpya

first dhyāna
    vitarka
    vicāra
    prīti
    sukha
    cittaikāgratā

second dhyāna
    adhyātmasaṃprasāda
    prīti
    sukha
    cittaikāgratā
```

The transition is therefore not simple subtraction:

```text
first dhyāna
    - vitarka
    - vicāra
    + adhyātmasaṃprasāda
    = second dhyāna
```

## New Technical Terms

- **adhyātmasaṃprasāda** — inward clarity, serene confidence, or inner settling proper to the second dhyāna
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā in this expanded form
  - **function:** positive factor replacing the discursive pair at the second dhyāna

- **caturaṅgā senā** — four-limbed army
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** analogy clarifying how a whole may be named through a structured set of factors

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives the first explicit factor matrix of the chapter.

```text
Dhyāna 1
    vitarka
    vicāra
    prīti
    sukha
    samādhi / cittaikāgratā

Dhyāna 2
    adhyātmasaṃprasāda
    prīti
    sukha
    samādhi / cittaikāgratā
```

Two points matter.

First, `samādhi` is not identical to the total list of factors. It is one determinant among others, even though dhyāna as a whole is also a samādhi-state.

Second, higher dhyāna is not constituted merely by removing factors. The loss of vitarka and vicāra is accompanied by the emergence of a new positive determination: `adhyātmasaṃprasāda`.

## Translation Consequences

The kārikā's `tarka-cāra` should be translated in light of the prose as `vitarka and vicāra`, not as two unrelated technical nouns.

`samādhi` in this verse should be glossed locally as `cittaikāgratā`, because Vasubandhu explicitly equates them in the factor list.

`prasāda` should be strengthened to `adhyātmasaṃprasāda` in explanatory prose: an inward clarification or serene confidence, not merely generic clarity.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.07 gives a whole constituted through differentiable factors.

```text
whole
    dhyāna

factors
    aṅgas

one factor
    samādhi / cittaikāgratā
```

The army analogy prevents two opposite reductions:

```text
whole ≠ one isolated factor
whole ≠ unstructured aggregate
```

The whole exists as an ordered factor-configuration.

The transition from first to second dhyāna is also genetically determinate:

```text
remove discursive pair
    vitarka + vicāra

retain
    prīti + sukha + samādhi

introduce
    adhyātmasaṃprasāda
```

So a higher attainment can simplify one dimension while positively differentiating another.

## Light Organon Contact

The text itself supplies enough structure without further reconstruction:

```text
samāpatti
    whole attained configuration

samādhi
    one-pointed factor within that configuration

other aṅgas
    co-determining factors
```

This is useful for the larger project because it keeps `samādhi`, `dhyāna`, and `samāpatti` distinct rather than treating them as synonyms.

The important immediate rule is:

> A meditative whole is specified by its factor-constitution, and ascent changes that constitution by both subtraction and positive replacement.
