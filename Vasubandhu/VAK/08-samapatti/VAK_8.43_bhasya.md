# VAK_8.43_bhasya

## Kārikā

### IAST

`iti kaṇṭhagataprāṇaṃ viditvā śāsanaṃ muneḥ /`
`balakālaṃ malānāṃ ca na pramādyaṃ mumukṣubhiḥ //`

### Best Current Translation

Knowing thus that the Sage's Teaching has its breath lodged in its throat, and that this is the season of strength for the defilements, those who seek liberation must not be negligent.

Kārikā anchor: `VAK_8.43.md`

No prose gloss follows the verse. The chapter colophon immediately closes the eighth Kośasthāna, the Samāpattinirdeśa.

## Bhāṣya Translation

Knowing in this way that the Sage's Teaching has its life-breath already at the throat, and that this is the time when the defilements are strong, those who seek liberation must not be negligent.

Thus ends the eighth Kośasthāna, called the Exposition of Attainments, in the Abhidharmakośabhāṣya.

## Natural Argument

The final verse converts the preceding historical lament into an imperative.

The Teaching is figured as a dying body whose breath has risen to the throat (`kaṇṭhagataprāṇa`): it is still alive, but precariously so. At exactly the same time, the defilements (`mala`) are said to be in their season of strength (`balakāla`).

The conclusion is not despair and not speculation about decline. It is practical vigilance: those who desire liberation (`mumukṣu`) must not fall into negligence (`pramāda`).

This is a fitting end to the Kośa proper for the present project phase. After eight chapters of exhaustive classification, causal analysis, path theory, knowledge, and attainment, the last instruction is simply that the practitioner must not become careless.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Teaching is precarious
        +
defilements are strong
        ↓
conditions are unfavorable
        ↓
therefore
        ↓
no pramāda for the mumukṣu
```

The conclusion is practical, not theoretical.

## New Technical Terms

- **kaṇṭhagataprāṇa** — literally “with the life-breath gone to the throat”; an idiom for being at the point of death.
  - **status:** poetic expression in the final Kārikā.
  - **function:** dramatizes the precarious condition of the Teaching.

- **balakāla** — time or season of strength.
  - **status:** Kārikā expression.
  - **function:** characterizes the present condition as favorable to the growth or force of defilements.

- **mala** — stain, impurity, defilement.
  - **status:** established Buddhist term, used here in the plural.
  - **function:** collective designation for the forces opposed to liberation.

- **pramāda** — negligence, heedlessness, carelessness.
  - **status:** major ethical-practical term.
  - **function:** the final danger explicitly prohibited.

- **mumukṣu** — one who desires liberation.
  - **status:** Kārikā term, especially striking in the final injunction.
  - **function:** identifies the intended subject of the closing command.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, directly exhortative in the final verse.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Kośa closes by subordinating doctrinal mastery to practical vigilance.

The final criterion is not possession of the taxonomy, mastery of debate, or even familiarity with the meditative technologies catalogued in Chapter 8. The addressee is the `mumukṣu`, the one who seeks liberation, and the command is negative but decisive:

```text
na pramādyam
```

Do not be negligent.

The closing sequence of the chapter can now be seen as one argument:

```text
Saddharma is transmission + realization          (8.39)
        ↓
the exposition is fallible and method-bound      (8.40)
        ↓
reasoning without realization produces confusion (8.41)
        ↓
loss of custodians permits doctrinal disorder     (8.42)
        ↓
therefore the liberation-seeker must be vigilant  (8.43)
```

## Translation Consequences

- `kaṇṭhagataprāṇa` should be translated idiomatically while retaining the vivid bodily image.
- `mala` is broader than a single named kleśa; “defilements” or “stains” suits the collective force here.
- `mumukṣu` should remain “one who seeks/desires liberation,” preserving the intentional orientation of the subject.
- `na pramādyam` is an injunction: “one must not be negligent / heedless.”

## Logical Determination

The final verse converts system into practice:

```text
knowledge of conditions
        ↓
recognition of danger
        ↓
practical obligation
```

The immense analytical apparatus of the Kośa ends not in encyclopedic closure but in a rule for conduct under conditions of uncertainty and decline.

## Light Organon Contact

For our later reconstruction, this is a useful ending to preserve intact. The system does not terminate in a claim that classification itself liberates. It terminates in the demand that knowledge become vigilance in practice.

That relation—between a completed science and the agent who must still enact it—is a problem for the next stage of our study, not something to solve inside this closing Bhāṣya file.

## Chapter Closure

`abhidharmakośabhāṣye samāpattīnirdeśo nāmāṣṭamakośasthānam iti`

Thus ends the eighth Kośasthāna, the **Samāpattinirdeśa**, in the Abhidharmakośabhāṣya.
