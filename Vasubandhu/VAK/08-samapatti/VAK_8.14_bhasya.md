# VAK_8.14 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
atadvān labhate śuddhaṃ vairāgyeṇopapattitaḥ /
anāsravaṃ tu vairāgyāt kliṣṭaṃ hānyupapattitaḥ // VAK_8.14 //
```

## Best Current Translation

One who does not yet possess the relevant attainment acquires a pure attainment through dispassion or through rebirth. An uncontaminated attainment is acquired through dispassion; a defiled attainment through decline or through rebirth.

See the existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_8.14.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan, pp. 442–443:

```text
atha śuddhakādīnāṃ dhyānārupyāṇāṃ kathaṃ lābhaḥ /

atadvān labhate śuddhaṃ vairāgyeṇopapattitaḥ /

asamanvāgatastena śuddhakaṃ dhyānam ārūpyaṃ vā pratilabhate /
adhobhūmivairāgyād vā /
adhobhūmyupapattito vā /
anyatra bhavāgrāt /
na hi tasyopapattito lābhaḥ /

atadvān iti kimartha samanvāgataḥ /
śuddhakena prayogato 'pi nirvedhabhāgīyaṃ śuddhakaṃ pratilabhate
parihāṇito vā hānabhāgīyam /

... 

anāsravaṃ tu vairāgyāt /

atadvān labhate iti vartate /
tadvāṃs tu kṣayajñānato 'py aśaikṣaṃ labhate
indriyasaṃcārato 'pi śaikṣāśaikṣam /

nanu ca niyāmāvakrāntito 'py anāsravaṃ prathamato labhate /
nāvaśyam ānupūrvikeṇālābhāt /
yathā tv avaśyaṃ labhate tathoktam /

kliṣṭaṃ hānyupapattitaḥ //

atadvān labhata ity evānuvartate /
parihāṇito yadi tadvairāgyāt parihīyate /
upapattito yady uparibhūmer adharāyām upapadyate /
```

## Source Caution

The kārikā gives a compact acquisition rule, but the Bhāṣya distinguishes two cases that should not be collapsed:

```text
atadvān
    one who does not yet possess the attainment

tadvān
    one who already possesses a corresponding attainment
```

The simple rule in the verse primarily concerns the first case. Vasubandhu then supplements it with additional acquisition routes for one already endowed with the relevant pure or uncontaminated attainment.

A second caution concerns `upapatti`. In the pure case Vasubandhu explicitly excludes `bhavāgra`: the summit of existence is not acquired merely by rebirth into it.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The preceding section has completed the analysis of dhyāna as such. Vasubandhu now asks:

```text
atha śuddhakādīnāṃ dhyānārupyāṇāṃ kathaṃ lābhaḥ /
```

How are the pure and other dhyānas and ārūpyas acquired?

### 1. Acquisition of a pure attainment

For one who does not already possess it, a pure dhyāna or ārūpya is acquired in two principal ways:

```text
adhobhūmi-vairāgya
    dispassion toward the lower level

or

upapatti
    rebirth into the corresponding higher level
```

The first is genetic through cultivation: by becoming dispassionate toward a lower bhūmi, the practitioner acquires the pure attainment belonging above it.

The second is cosmological: one born into a corresponding higher sphere obtains its pure meditative configuration.

But Vasubandhu immediately restricts the second rule:

```text
anyatra bhavāgrāt
```

except for the summit of existence. `Bhavāgra` is not acquired merely through rebirth.

### 2. Why does the verse say `atadvān`?

Vasubandhu explicitly asks why the kārikā specifies one who does not already possess the attainment.

Because someone already endowed with a pure attainment may acquire another pure attainment by additional routes:

```text
prayoga
    deliberate practice
        → nirvedhabhāgīya pure attainment

parihāṇi
    decline
        → hānabhāgīya pure attainment
```

Thus the kārikā's rule is not an exhaustive list of every acquisition-event. It gives the principal rule for first acquisition by one previously not endowed with that attainment.

Vasubandhu illustrates the point with the first dhyāna: it may be gained through dispassion from kāmadhātu, lost through dispassion from Brahmaloka, regained through decline from that higher dispassion, lost through loss of desire-realm dispassion, gained through birth above Brahmaloka, and abandoned again through rebirth in kāmadhātu.

The important point is that acquisition and loss depend on determinate causal relations among levels, not merely on possession of a nameable meditative state.

### 3. Acquisition of uncontaminated attainment

For one who does not already possess it:

```text
anāsrava
    is acquired through vairāgya
```

But once again Vasubandhu supplements the rule for someone already possessing uncontaminated attainment.

Such a person may also acquire:

```text
kṣayajñāna
    → an aśaikṣa uncontaminated attainment

indriyasaṃcāra
    transformation of faculties
    → śaikṣa or aśaikṣa uncontaminated attainment
```

**Voice: Objector**

An objection follows: is an uncontaminated attainment not also first acquired upon entry into `niyāma`, the fixed course?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Yes, but that route is not universal for someone proceeding in strict sequence. The kārikā states the mode by which it is necessarily acquired, not every possible first occurrence.

### 4. Acquisition of defiled attainment

For one who does not possess it, a defiled attainment is acquired through:

```text
hāni / parihāṇi
    decline from the relevant dispassion

or

upapatti
    rebirth from a higher level into a lower one
```

The Bhāṣya therefore defines defiled acquisition relationally: it appears when a previously overcome lower relation is restored through decline, or when rebirth places the being back within a lower conditioned field.

## New Technical Terms

- **atadvān** — one not yet endowed with the relevant attainment
  - **status** — important qualification newly foregrounded in this passage
  - **function** — restricts the kārikā's acquisition rule to first acquisition by a non-possessor

- **tadvān / samanvāgata** — one already endowed with the relevant attainment
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-only qualification of the kārikā rule
  - **function** — opens supplementary acquisition routes not stated in the verse

- **nirvedhabhāgīya** — conducive to penetration / belonging to the factors leading toward decisive penetration
  - **status** — previously encountered in Path material; newly applied here to a pure meditative attainment
  - **function** — names a pure attainment gained through practice by one already possessing a pure basis

- **hānabhāgīya** — tending toward decline or belonging to the class associated with falling away
  - **status** — previously encountered doctrinal classification
  - **function** — names a pure attainment obtained through decline from a superior condition

- **kṣayajñāna** — knowledge of destruction
  - **status** — previously established in Chapter 7
  - **function** — acquisition-ground for an aśaikṣa uncontaminated attainment

- **indriyasaṃcāra** — transformation/change of faculties
  - **status** — Bhāṣya technical term
  - **function** — additional acquisition-route for śaikṣa/aśaikṣa uncontaminated attainments

- **niyāmāvakrānti** — entry into the fixed course / certainty
  - **status** — Path vocabulary recalled here
  - **function** — objector's counterexample to the simplified acquisition rule

- **parihāṇi** — decline, falling away
  - **status** — previously encountered
  - **function** — acquisition condition for defiled attainment and for a hānabhāgīya pure attainment in the possessor-case

## Doctrinal Determination

### Acquisition is not one-dimensional

VAK 8.14 distinguishes at least three independent determinations:

```text
what level?
    dhyāna / ārūpya

what mode?
    pure / uncontaminated / defiled

how acquired?
    dispassion / rebirth / practice / decline / knowledge / faculty-change
```

The same meditative level can therefore have different causal histories and different soteriological status.

### Pure does not mean uncontaminated

The pure attainment may be obtained through rebirth. The uncontaminated attainment cannot be reduced to cosmological placement; its necessary acquisition is tied to `vairāgya`.

This reinforces the distinction already established in VAK 8.05–8.06:

```text
śuddhaka
    wholesome, but worldly

anāsrava
    supramundane
```

### Bhavāgra is exceptional

The summit of existence is explicitly excluded from the rule that a pure attainment may be acquired by rebirth.

Thus even at the highest conditioned level, possession of the corresponding meditative configuration cannot simply be inferred from location.

### Acquisition and loss are relational

Vasubandhu's first-dhyāna example shows that an attainment may be gained, abandoned, regained, and abandoned again according to movement among levels of dispassion and rebirth.

The object is therefore not a permanently possessed inner entity. Its possession is dependent on a precise network of conditions.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā translation should preserve `atadvān` explicitly:

> One who does not possess it acquires the pure attainment through dispassion or rebirth; the uncontaminated through dispassion; the defiled through decline or rebirth.

This is better than an impersonal formulation because the Bhāṣya makes the non-possession condition essential and then deliberately contrasts it with the case of one who already possesses the attainment.

`Vairāgya` should remain **dispassion / detachment**, with the local force of release from a lower bhūmi.

`Hāni` should be translated **decline / falling away**, not mere destruction or cessation.

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya distinguish **state identity** from **genesis of possession**.

```text
same attainment-level
    can be acquired through different routes

same acquisition-route
    does not imply the same modal quality
```

The important logical structure is conditional:

```text
non-possessor + lower-level dispassion
    → pure attainment

non-possessor + rebirth
    → pure attainment
    except bhavāgra

non-possessor + dispassion
    → uncontaminated attainment

loss of dispassion
    → defiled attainment

higher-to-lower rebirth
    → defiled attainment
```

The Bhāṣya then adds further rules when the subject is already endowed with the relevant attainment.

Thus possession is not a static predicate. It has a genealogy.

## Light Organon Contact

The text itself gives enough here without importing a larger reconstruction.

A meditative determination is not fully understood merely by saying what it is. One must also know **how it came to be possessed** and **under what conditions it can be lost or regained**.

```text
classification
    what is it?

genesis
    how was it acquired?

retention / loss
    under what conditions does possession persist?
```

For the present Bhāṣya pass, that is sufficient: VAK 8.14 turns the static taxonomy of samāpatti into a causal account of acquisition.
