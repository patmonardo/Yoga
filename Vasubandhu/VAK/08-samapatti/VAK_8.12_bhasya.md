# VAK_8.12 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> saumanasyasukhopekṣā upekṣāsumanaskate /
> sukhopekṣe upekṣā pravido dhyānopapattiṣu // VAK_8.12 //

**Best current translation:**  
In the rebirths corresponding to the dhyānas there are, respectively: mental gladness, pleasure, and equanimity; equanimity and mental gladness; pleasure and equanimity; and equanimity.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.12.md`](./VAK_8.12.md)

## Source Caution

The reference e-text is not a critical edition and explicitly warns that errors remain. The kārikā itself contains difficult compressed forms (`sumanaskate`, `pravido`) whose exact philological normalization should remain provisional. The prose explanation, however, makes the doctrinal distribution of the feelings unambiguous.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The preceding discussion established the feeling-tone occurring in the four dhyāna attainments themselves:

```text
first and second dhyānas
    saumanasya, indicated through prīti

third dhyāna
    sukha

fourth dhyāna
    upekṣā
```

Vasubandhu now raises a new question:

```text
Are the feelings occurring in dhyāna-samāpatti
identical with those occurring in rebirth
into the corresponding dhyāna worlds?
```

His answer is explicitly negative:

```text
na
    no
```

The reason is that rebirth in a dhyāna-world has its own distribution of sensory and mental knowledge-bodies (`vijñānakāya`). The feeling-structure of meditative attainment therefore cannot simply be transferred unchanged to the corresponding mode of existence.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. First-dhyāna rebirth

Three feelings occur:

```text
sukha
    connected with three vijñānakāyas

saumanasya
    manobhūmika
    belonging to the mental level

upekṣā
    connected with four vijñānakāyas
```

This is the richest affective configuration among the four form-realm rebirths.

### 2. Second-dhyāna rebirth

Two feelings occur:

```text
saumanasya
upekṣā
```

Both are `manobhūmika`, belonging to the mental level.

The Bhāṣya gives the ground for the absence of sukha:

```text
sukhaṃ nāsty asya vijñānakāyābhāvāt
```

There is no such sukha because the relevant knowledge-body is absent.

### 3. Third-dhyāna rebirth

Two feelings occur:

```text
sukha
upekṣā
```

Again both are mental-level (`manobhūmika`) feelings.

### 4. Fourth-dhyāna rebirth

Only one feeling occurs:

```text
upekṣā eva
    equanimity alone
```

The fourth-dhyāna rebirth therefore completes the affective simplification already associated with the immovability of the fourth dhyāna.

## New Technical Terms

- **dhyānopapatti** — rebirth corresponding to a dhyāna; **status:** previously encountered as the rebirth-side of the twofold dhyāna distinction; **function:** distinguishes cosmological rebirth from meditative attainment.
- **manobhūmika** — belonging to the mental level; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicative vocabulary; **function:** identifies saumanasya, and at higher levels sukha/upekṣā, as mental rather than sensory feeling.
- **vijñānakāyika** — connected with a knowledge-body; **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical specification in this passage; **function:** grounds the presence or absence of particular feelings according to available modes of knowledge.
- **trivijñānakāyika** — connected with three knowledge-bodies; **status:** Bhāṣya-only local specification; **function:** determines the sukha of first-dhyāna rebirth.
- **caturvijñānakāyikī** — connected with four knowledge-bodies; **status:** Bhāṣya-only local specification; **function:** determines the upekṣā of first-dhyāna rebirth.

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive distinction is:

```text
dhyāna-samāpatti
    meditative attainment

≠

dhyāna-upapatti
    rebirth corresponding to that attainment
```

Their feeling-structures are related but not identical.

| Dhyāna rebirth | Feelings | Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|---|
| First | sukha, saumanasya, upekṣā | sensory/mental distribution across vijñānakāyas |
| Second | saumanasya, upekṣā | both mental; sukha absent because relevant vijñānakāya absent |
| Third | sukha, upekṣā | both mental |
| Fourth | upekṣā alone | final affective simplification |

This prevents a simple projection from meditative phenomenology onto cosmological rebirth.

## Translation Consequences

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā translation correctly gives the fourfold distribution. The Bhāṣya adds the causal and functional explanation: the distribution depends on which sensory and mental knowledge-bodies are available at each level.

The project translation `vijñāna = knowledge` remains in force. `vijñānakāya` is therefore rendered here as `knowledge-body`, not `consciousness-body`.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.12 introduces a distinction between two instantiations of the same dhyāna-level:

```text
same dhyāna level
    ├── attained as samāpatti
    └── instantiated as rebirth (upapatti)
```

The shared level does not entail identical internal constitution.

The Bhāṣya therefore determines affect not merely from the name of the level but from the operative cognitive supports available within that mode of existence:

```text
available vijñānakāyas
    ↓
possible feeling-types
```

Thus absence is grounded rather than merely enumerated:

```text
no relevant vijñānakāya
    ↓
no corresponding sukha
```

## Light Organon Contact

The text itself gives the important distinction: one and the same dhyāna determination can occur as **attainment** and as **rebirth**, yet its internal factor-distribution differs according to the conditions of each mode.

```text
shared determination
    does not erase
mode-specific constitution
```

This is enough for the present pass. The stronger Yoga or Systems comparison should remain deferred until the Kośa has completed its own analysis of the relation between samāpatti, upapatti, and the operative vijñānakāyas.
