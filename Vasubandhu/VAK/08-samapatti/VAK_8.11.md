# VAK_8.11 — The Immovability of the Fourth Dhyāna

## Sanskrit

### Devanāgarī

```text
अष्टापक्षालमुक्तत्वादानिञ्जं तु चतुर्थकम् ।
वितर्कचारौ श्वासौ च सुखादि च चतुष्टयम् ॥ ८.११ ॥
```

### IAST

```text
aṣṭāpakṣālamuktatvād āniñjaṃ tu caturthakam /
vitarkacārau śvāsau ca sukhādi ca catuṣṭayam // VAK_8.11 //
```

## Source Caution

The transmitted compound `aṣṭāpakṣāla` is compressed and should be read doctrinally as referring to the eight disturbances or faults from which the fourth dhyāna is free.

The eight are enumerated in the second half:

```text
vitarka
vicāra
inhalation
exhalation
and the four beginning with sukha
```

The final group is understood as the four affective disturbances:

```text
sukha
-duḥkha
saumanasya
daurmanasya
```

This first pass preserves that established doctrinal count without forcing a philological reconstruction beyond what the kārikā requires.

## Grammar

The first half is causal:

```text
aṣṭa-apakṣāla-muktatvāt
    because it is free from eight disturbances

āniñjaṃ tu caturthakam
    the fourth is immovable
```

The predicate `āniñjam` characterizes the fourth dhyāna by its freedom from perturbation.

The second half supplies the eightfold enumeration:

```text
vitarka-cārau
    vitarka and vicāra

śvāsau
    inhalation and exhalation

sukhādi catuṣṭayam
    the four beginning with pleasure
```

Thus:

```text
2 + 2 + 4 = 8
```

The verse is therefore both definitional and analytic:

```text
immovability
    is defined through
freedom from eight specific disturbances
```

## Literal Translation

Because it is free from eight disturbances, the fourth is immovable: vitarka and vicāra, the two breaths, and the four beginning with pleasure.

## Philosophical Translation

The fourth dhyāna is called immovable because the mental field is no longer disturbed by eight factors: discursive thought and examination, inhalation and exhalation, and the four affective movements beginning with pleasure. Its stillness is therefore not an abstract absence but a determinate freedom from specific modes of agitation.

## Technical Vocabulary

### āniñja

```text
ā-niñj
    not moving
    not trembling
    not shaken
```

`Āniñja` does not mean mere inactivity. It signifies a field whose stability has been achieved through the cessation of determinate disturbances.

```text
immovable
    not because nothing is present
    but because the causes of shaking are absent
```

### vitarka and vicāra

These are the first pair of disturbances counted here.

```text
vitarka
    initial application
    discursive placement

vicāra
    sustained examination
    continued analysis
```

They have already fallen away by the second dhyāna, but they remain part of the complete contrast by which the fourth is defined as fully unshaken.

### śvāsau

The dual refers to the two breaths:

```text
inhalation
exhalation
```

Their cessation here is doctrinally linked to the extreme refinement of the bodily process in the fourth dhyāna.

The verse therefore includes both mental and bodily disturbances within the same purity-analysis.

### sukhādi catuṣṭaya

The four beginning with pleasure are:

```text
sukha
    bodily pleasure

duḥkha
    bodily pain

saumanasya
    mental gladness

daurmanasya
    mental distress
```

The fourth dhyāna is beyond all four affective oscillations.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.11 explains why the fourth dhyāna is called immovable.

### Eight disturbances

| Group | Members |
|---|---|
| Discursive cognition | vitarka, vicāra |
| Vital movement | inhalation, exhalation |
| Affective movement | sukha, duḥkha, saumanasya, daurmanasya |

The fourth dhyāna is free from all eight.

### Staged purity

The verse confirms that purity in the dhyāna system is progressive and determinate.

```text
first dhyāna
    discursive activity remains

second dhyāna
    discursive activity ceases

third dhyāna
    rapture ceases

fourth dhyāna
    affective polarity and breath-motion cease
```

The fourth is therefore not merely higher by position. It is qualitatively purified of the principal modes of movement that characterize the lower stages.

### Mental and bodily stilling

The inclusion of the breaths is doctrinally important.

```text
mental perturbation
    vitarka, vicāra

bodily perturbation
    inhalation, exhalation

affective perturbation
    pleasure, pain, gladness, distress
```

The attained whole is stable across all three dimensions.

## Logical Determination

The kārikā gives a negative definition with positive content.

```text
fourth dhyāna
    = freedom from eight disturbances
```

This is not an empty negation. Each excluded factor is named.

```text
abstract negation
    merely says: not disturbed

determinate negation
    identifies precisely what no longer disturbs
```

The fourth dhyāna is therefore a qualitative totality defined by a structured absence.

Its immovability is not imposed from outside. It is the result of the progressive removal of lower-stage determinations.

```text
stability
    = completed purification of the field
```

## Organon Light

Modern science often analyzes disturbances, variables, and sources of error. What it commonly lacks is the Samāpatti moment: the attained whole in which those analyses are gathered into a realized qualitative state.

VAK 8.11 does not merely count eight excluded factors. It shows that their coordinated cessation constitutes a new whole:

```text
analysis of disturbances
    vitarka
    vicāra
    breath
    affect

Samāpatti
    the immovable fourth dhyāna
```

The whole is not reducible to the list, yet it is not independent of the list.

```text
qualitative logic
    determines the factors

Samāpatti
    realizes their purified configuration
```

This remains a light Organon observation. The immediate kārikā determination is simply that the fourth dhyāna is immovable because it is free from eight specific disturbances.
