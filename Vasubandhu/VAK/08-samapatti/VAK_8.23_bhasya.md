# VAK_8.23 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
tridhā aduḥkhāsukhaṃ tac ca mahābrahmaphalaṃ ca tat /
savitarkavicāro 'dhaḥsamādhiḥ parato 'dvayaḥ // VAK_8.23 //
```

## Best Current Translation

The intermediate dhyāna is threefold; it is associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, and its result is the state of Mahābrahmā. The samādhi below it is accompanied by vitarka and vicāra; beyond it, samādhi is without both.

See the existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_8.23.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 448|11–449|07.

```text
tat punar dhyānāntaraṃ
    tridhā
āsvādanāsaṃprayuktaṃ śuddhakam anāsravaṃ ca /

    aduḥkhāsukhaṃ tac ca
nātra sukhaṃ duḥkham ity aduḥkhāsukham upekṣendriyasaṃprayuktam ity arthaḥ /
na prītisaṃprayuktaṃ sābhisaṃskāravāhitvāt /
ata eva duḥkhā pratipat /

tasya tarhi dhyānāntarasya kaḥ phalaviśeṣaḥ /
tad dhi
    mahābrahmaphalaṃ ca tat /
tāṃ hi dhyānāntarikāṃ bhāvayitvā mahābrahmā bhavati /

punaḥ sarvasamādhīn saṃkalayya trayaḥ samādhayaḥ uktāḥ sūtre /
savitarkaḥ savicāraḥ samādhiḥ /
avitarko vicāramātraḥ /
avitarko 'vicāra iti /

tatra dhyānāntaraṃ tāvad avitarko vicāramātraḥ samādhir iti jñāpitam /
vitarkamātrapratiṣedhāt /

tataḥ
    savitarkavicāro 'dhaḥsamādhiḥ

tasmād dhyānāntarād adhaḥ samādhiḥ savitarkaḥ savicāraḥ /
prathamaṃ dhyānam anāgamyaṃ ca /

    parato 'dvayaḥ // VAK_8.23 //

nātra dvayam astīty advayaḥ /
pareṇa tu dhyānāntarāt samādhir avitarko 'vicāraḥ /
dvitīyadhyānasāmantakādyāvat bhavāgram /
```

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya completes the account of `dhyānāntara` introduced at VAK 8.22.

First, the intermediate dhyāna itself is said to be **threefold**:

```text
āsvādanāsaṃprayukta
    savoring-associated

śuddhaka
    pure

anāsrava
    uncontaminated
```

Second, its feeling-tone is clarified. `Aduḥkhāsukha` is not a vague absence of feeling; Vasubandhu explicitly glosses it as association with the **upekṣā faculty** (`upekṣendriya`). It is also not joined with `prīti`, because its flow still proceeds with effort or active construction (`sābhisaṃskāravāhitva`). This is why it belongs to the difficult mode of practice (`duḥkhā pratipat`).

Third, Vasubandhu asks what distinctive result this intermediate dhyāna has. The answer is cosmological: cultivating it leads to birth as **Mahābrahmā**.

Finally, he gathers all samādhis under a threefold sūtra classification according to vitarka and vicāra:

```text
1. savitarka + savicāra
2. avitarka + vicāramātra
3. avitarka + avicāra
```

The intermediate dhyāna is the middle member: vitarka has ceased, while vicāra remains.

Below it are:

```text
first dhyāna
anāgamya
```

Both are accompanied by vitarka and vicāra.

Above it, from the sāmantaka of the second dhyāna through bhavāgra, samādhi is without both vitarka and vicāra.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
first dhyāna + anāgamya
    vitarka + vicāra
        ↓ removal of vitarka

dhyānāntara
    no vitarka + vicāra
        ↓ removal of vicāra

second-dhyāna sāmantaka → bhavāgra
    no vitarka + no vicāra
```

This is a determinate progression rather than a loose taxonomy. Each higher configuration is defined through the specific factor removed from the prior one.

## New Technical Terms

- **sābhisaṃskāravāhitva** — proceeding with active construction/effort; **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical phrase in this local unit; **function:** explains why dhyānāntara is not joined with prīti and why its path is difficult.
- **duḥkhā pratipat** — difficult mode of practice/path; **status:** previously encountered doctrinal category; **function:** classifies the intermediate dhyāna's mode of progress.
- **mahābrahmaphala** — the Mahābrahmā state as result; **status:** uncommon in the Kārikā sequence; **function:** gives the distinctive cosmological result of dhyānāntara.
- **vicāramātra** — vicāra alone; **status:** technical sūtra wording; **function:** defines the middle member of the threefold samādhi classification.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the threefold constitution, feeling-tone, result, and the sūtra's threefold classification of samādhi.

## Doctrinal Determination

The intermediate dhyāna is not merely a gap between the first and second dhyānas. It is a determinate meditative configuration with its own:

- three qualitative modes;
- feeling-tone;
- relation to prīti;
- path-character;
- cosmological result;
- position in the vitarka/vicāra system.

The complete classification is therefore:

| Samādhi range | Vitarka | Vicāra |
|---|---:|---:|
| First dhyāna + anāgamya | present | present |
| Dhyānāntara | absent | present |
| Second-dhyāna sāmantaka through bhavāgra | absent | absent |

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation is substantially confirmed, but the Bhāṣya adds two important clarifications:

1. `aduḥkhāsukha` should be understood specifically as **association with the upekṣā faculty**, not merely affective neutrality in an abstract sense.
2. `advaya` in the final pāda means explicitly **without the two factors vitarka and vicāra**.

A tightened translation is therefore:

> The intermediate dhyāna is threefold; it is associated with the neither-painful-nor-pleasant, that is, the upekṣā faculty, and its result is Mahābrahmā. The samādhi below it is accompanied by vitarka and vicāra; beyond it, samādhi is without both.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya supplies a three-state factor logic:

```text
(vitarka, vicāra)
    → (¬vitarka, vicāra)
    → (¬vitarka, ¬vicāra)
```

The middle term is indispensable. Without `dhyānāntara`, the passage from both-present to both-absent would be merely asserted. With it, the transformation is genetically articulated.

This is one of the cleanest examples in Chapter 8 of a transition being determined by **selective negation** rather than by undifferentiated ascent.

## Light Organon Contact

The text itself already gives enough structure to justify a light systems observation:

```text
state 1
    retain A + B

state 2
    remove A, retain B

state 3
    remove A + B
```

The significance is not that vitarka and vicāra are themselves the Concept, but that the chapter closes this block by making the objective field of samādhi **explicitly transition-complete** with respect to these two factors.

That prepares the next local section, where Vasubandhu immediately turns to a different threefold classification:

```text
śūnyatā-samādhi
apraṇihita-samādhi
ānimitta-samādhi
```

So VAK 8.23 closes the first half of Chapter 8 by completing the structural taxonomy of dhyāna/ārūpya samāpatti before the liberative samādhis begin.
