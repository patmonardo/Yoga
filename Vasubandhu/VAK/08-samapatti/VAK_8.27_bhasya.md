# VAK_8.27 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
sāsravāḥ nṛṣu akopyasya saptasāmantavarjitāḥ /
samādhibhāvanā dhyānaṃ śubham ādyaṃ sukhāya hi // VAK_8.27 //
```

## Best Current Translation

The further reflexive samādhis are contaminated; they arise only among human beings, belong only to the Unshakeable Arhat, and occur on levels other than the seven sāmantakas. The first wholesome dhyāna is the cultivation of samādhi for pleasant abiding in the present life.

See the existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_8.27.md`.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first completes the account of the three `parasamādhayaḥ` introduced in VAK 8.25–8.26.

They are said to be **exclusively contaminated** (`ekāntena ... sāsravāḥ`). Vasubandhu gives a specific reason:

```text
āryamārgadveṣitvāt
    because of hostility/opposition toward the noble path
```

This is stronger and more determinate than merely saying that they are conditioned. Their reflexive operation turns upon previously attained noble samādhis in a way that criticizes, relinquishes, or negates them as objects; for that reason they are not themselves `anāsrava`.

Their occurrence is then tightly restricted by person and locus.

They arise:

```text
only among human beings
    nṛṣu / manuṣyeṣu eva

only in the Unshakeable Arhat
    akopyasya
    not in another type of Arhat
```

Their level-range is also specified. They exclude the **seven sāmantakas**, but occur on the remaining **eleven levels**:

```text
kāmadhātu
anāgamya
dhyānāntara
the dhyānas
the ārūpyas
```

The verse then changes topic. Vasubandhu announces **four cultivations of samādhi** (`catasraḥ samādhibhāvanāḥ`). VAK 8.27 gives the first; VAK 8.28 supplies the remaining three.

The first is based on wholesome first dhyāna and is cultivated for:

```text
dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāra
    pleasant abiding in the present life
```

The relevant first dhyāna may be either:

```text
śuddhaka
    pure/worldly

anāsrava
    uncontaminated
```

Vasubandhu then adds an important restriction: the formula concerns **present-life** pleasant abiding. It does not necessarily entail pleasant abiding in a later existence (`saṃparāya`). He points to practitioners who do not fall back and who are reborn above and attain parinirvāṇa there; the corresponding later-life pleasant abiding is not universally present.

## Argument Reconstruction

The verse has two distinct but connected movements:

```text
further reflexive samādhis
    ↓ determine their status
sāsrava
    ↓ determine their bearer
human + akopya Arhat
    ↓ determine their level-range
11 levels, excluding 7 sāmantakas

then

four samādhibhāvanās
    ↓
first cultivation
    wholesome first dhyāna
    ↓
dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāra
    present-life pleasant abiding
```

The first half closes the second-order samādhi section by specifying **where, in whom, and with what status** these practices actually occur. The second half begins a new functional classification: not what samādhi is, but **what cultivation of samādhi is for**.

## New Technical Terms

- **āryamārgadveṣitva** — hostility/opposition toward the noble path; **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory phrase in this unit; **function:** gives the reason the three further samādhis are exclusively contaminated.
- **akopya** — the Unshakeable Arhat; **status:** previously established technical person-type; **function:** identifies the only practitioner in whom these further samādhis arise.
- **saptasāmantavarjita** — excluding the seven neighboring/preliminary attainments; **status:** Kārikā expression expanded by the Bhāṣya; **function:** determines the level-range of the further samādhis.
- **samādhibhāvanā** — cultivation of samādhi; **status:** major new organizing category at this point of Chapter 8; **function:** opens a fourfold functional classification continued in VAK 8.28.
- **āsevita / bhāvita / bahulīkṛta** — practiced, cultivated, made abundant; **status:** sūtra formula quoted by Vasubandhu; **function:** characterizes mature cultivation of samādhi.
- **dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāra** — pleasant abiding in the present life; **status:** sūtra-derived technical phrase; **function:** defines the purpose of the first samādhi-cultivation.
- **saṃparāyasukhavihāra** — pleasant abiding in a later existence; **status:** Bhāṣya contrast-term; **function:** marks what is not necessarily implied by the first cultivation.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — determination of the status, locus, bearer, and levels of the three further samādhis; introduction and explanation of the first of four samādhi-cultivations.

The quoted formula `asti samādhibhāvanā āsevitā bhāvitā vahulīkṛtā...` is presented as inherited sūtra teaching and then analytically distributed by Vasubandhu across VAK 8.27–8.28.

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. The further samādhis are not supramundane merely because their objects are supramundane

This is a decisive clarification. The object-status of a cognition does not automatically transfer to the cognition itself.

The three further samādhis may take aśaikṣa samādhis or their non-arising as object, yet Vasubandhu classifies the further acts themselves as `sāsrava`.

Thus:

```text
supramundane object
    ≠
supramundane cognitive act
```

The nature of the act must be determined independently.

### 2. Reflexive samādhi is a highly restricted attainment

These practices are not general contemplative techniques. They require:

```text
human embodiment
+
akopya Arhatship
+
a non-sāmantaka supporting level
```

The restriction confirms that the second-order samādhis belong to a very advanced and stable condition of the path.

### 3. Samādhi is now classified by function

With `catasraḥ samādhibhāvanāḥ`, the chapter turns from structural and object-based classifications toward **teleological use**.

The first function is not liberation itself but:

```text
present-life pleasant abiding
```

The meditative system therefore recognizes a legitimate practical function of samādhi that is neither merely preparatory nor directly eliminative.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation should be tightened in two places.

First, `sāsrava` should remain **contaminated / accompanied by outflows** rather than being paraphrased simply as “conditioned.” The Bhāṣya's reason, `āryamārgadveṣitvāt`, is specifically soteriological.

Second, `sukhāya` is not generic “for happiness.” The Bhāṣya explicitly supplies:

```text
dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihārāya
```

Therefore the preferred translation is:

> The further samādhis are contaminated; they arise among human beings, belong to the Unshakeable Arhat, and occur on levels other than the seven sāmantakas. The first wholesome dhyāna is the cultivation of samādhi for pleasant abiding in the present life.

This also corrects the earlier tendency to gloss `saptasāmantavarjitāḥ` as requiring simply “completed rather than preliminary cognitive ground.” Vasubandhu gives an exact doctrinal enumeration: **eleven levels remain after excluding the seven sāmantakas**.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.27 makes two distinctions that should remain separate.

First:

```text
object of an act
    ≠
logical/soteriological status of the act
```

A cognition may take an uncontaminated attainment as object while itself remaining contaminated. Reflection does not inherit the status of what it reflects upon.

Second:

```text
formal constitution of samādhi
    ≠
function of samādhi-cultivation
```

The chapter has spent many verses determining the constitution, level, object, and mode of samādhi. Here the question becomes:

```text
What does cultivating this samādhi accomplish?
```

The first answer is immediate and practical: it establishes a pleasant mode of abiding here and now.

## Light Organon Contact

A useful systems distinction emerges directly from the text:

```text
State type
    what the samādhi is

Object relation
    what the samādhi takes as object-support

Agent constraint
    who can instantiate it

Level constraint
    where it can occur

Functional cultivation
    what its repeated cultivation accomplishes
```

Vasubandhu does not collapse these dimensions into one another. That is especially important for an Organon reconstruction: a feature's object, status, admissible agent, admissible level, and practical function must remain separately typed even when they belong to one living meditative process.

The genetic continuity is therefore reciprocal rather than merely hierarchical. The achieved samādhi becomes object for further reflection, but that reflection receives its own independent determination from the path, the practitioner, and the supporting level.

## Relation to VAK 8.28

VAK 8.27 introduces the four `samādhibhāvanā`s but explains only the first:

```text
1. dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāra
   present-life pleasant abiding
```

VAK 8.28 continues the same natural argument with the remaining three:

```text
2. jñānadarśana
3. prajñāprabheda
4. āsravakṣaya
```

So the natural Bhāṣya unit continues directly into the next verse, even though the repository remains verse-anchored.
