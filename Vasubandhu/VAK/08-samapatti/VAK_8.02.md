# VAK_8.02 — Progressive Separation and the Formless Attainments

## Sanskrit

### Devanāgarī

```text
विचारप्रीतिसुखवत् पूर्वपूर्वाङ्गवर्जितम् ।
तथारूप्याश् चतुःस्कन्धा अधोभूमिविवेकजाः ॥ ८.२ ॥
```

### IAST

```text
vicāraprītisukhavat pūrvapūrvāṅgavarjitam /
tathārūpyāś catuḥskandhā adhobhūmivivekajāḥ // VAK_8.02 //
```

## Source Caution

The source gives the compressed sequence:

```text
vicāra-prīti-sukha-vat
pūrva-pūrva-aṅga-varjitam
```

The verse presupposes the progressive constitution of the four dhyānas. Its exact distribution of factors is stated more fully in VAK 8.07–8.08. This first pass therefore preserves the secure structural claim:

```text
successive higher dhyānas
    are constituted by relinquishing
    factors belonging to the preceding level
```

The phrase `tathārūpyāḥ` introduces the formless attainments by analogy. They are not declared to be the same as the dhyānas; they follow the same principle of emergence through separation from a lower ground.

## Grammar

The first half is a compressed characterization of the four dhyānas:

```text
vicāra-prīti-sukha-vat
    possessing vicāra, prīti, and sukha
    according to the successive level

pūrva-pūrva-aṅga-varjitam
    each higher determination
    is without the preceding factor
```

The singular neuter expression gathers the dhyāna-series as one ordered structure.

The second half is a nominal statement:

```text
ārūpyāḥ
    the formless attainments

catuḥ-skandhāḥ
    are four-aggregate configurations

adho-bhūmi-viveka-jāḥ
    born from separation from the lower ground
```

`Viveka` governs the ablative relation embedded in the compound:

```text
adho-bhūmeḥ vivekaḥ
    separation from the lower ground

vivekāt jāḥ
    born from that separation
```

## Literal Translation

Possessing vicāra, joy, and ease in succession, each is deprived of the factor belonging to the preceding stage. Likewise, the formless attainments consist of four aggregates and are born from separation from the lower ground.

## Philosophical Translation

The dhyānas form a progressive sequence in which a higher level is constituted by relinquishing a factor that belonged to the level beneath it. The formless attainments arise according to the same law of separation: because material form is absent, they are four-aggregate configurations generated through detachment from the lower meditative ground.

## Technical Vocabulary

### pūrva-pūrvāṅga-varjita

```text
pūrva-pūrva
    each preceding stage

aṅga
    constituent factor

varjita
    deprived of
    released from
    without
```

The higher state is not produced merely by adding a new property. It is determined through the removal of a preceding constituent.

```text
lower level
    possesses a determinate factor

higher level
    arises through release from that factor
```

The dhyāna-series is therefore genetic rather than merely enumerative.

### viveka

`Viveka` here has a determinate technical sense:

```text
viveka
    separation
    disengagement
    distinction from a lower ground
```

It should not yet be inflated into the complete Yoga doctrine of discriminative knowledge. The verse uses it to state the condition from which a higher attainment arises.

Nevertheless, the structure is important:

```text
higher determination
    is born through discrimination from
    the lower determination
```

### ārūpya

The formless attainments are called `ārūpya` because rūpa is absent from their aggregate constitution.

```text
form-realm attainment
    five aggregates

formless attainment
    four aggregates
        vedanā
        saṃjñā
        saṃskāra
        vijñāna
```

The rūpa aggregate is excluded.

### adho-bhūmi

```text
adhas
    below

bhūmi
    ground; level; meditative domain
```

The higher attainment is defined relationally. It is higher because it is separated from a determinate lower ground.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.02 establishes two parallel sequences.

### 1. The dhyāna sequence

The four dhyānas are ordered through progressive relinquishment.

```text
first dhyāna
    vitarka + vicāra + prīti + sukha

second dhyāna
    released from vitarka and vicāra

third dhyāna
    released from prīti

fourth dhyāna
    released from sukha
```

The precise constituent tables are supplied later in VAK 8.07–8.08. The present verse gives their formal law:

> A higher dhyāna is constituted through the exclusion of a factor belonging to the preceding level.

### 2. The formless sequence

The formless attainments are:

```text
four-aggregate configurations
    because rūpa is absent

born from separation from lower grounds
    because each arises through detachment
    from the domain beneath it
```

Thus the form and formless attainments share a common architecture:

```text
meditative progression
    = determinate separation
      from a lower configuration
```

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.02 presents **determinate negation as ascent**.

```text
higher stage
    is not an unrelated new state

higher stage
    is the preceding field
    transformed through the removal
    of one of its constitutive factors
```

The sequence is therefore neither simple addition nor external succession.

```text
A
    contains factor x

B
    arises through release from x

B
    preserves the ordered relation to A
    while no longer possessing x
```

This gives a lawful transition between meditative worlds.

The same logic governs the formless attainments:

```text
lower bhūmi
    determinate meditative world

viveka
    separation from that world

higher ārūpya
    new aggregate-configuration
    born from that separation
```

The verse therefore introduces a directed transformation structure:

```text
state
    → relinquishment of factor
    → higher state
```

The relation is asymmetric. The higher state presupposes the lower as that from which it is separated, while the lower does not presuppose attainment of the higher.

## Organon Light

VAK 8.01 supplied the first dataset structure of Samāpatti:

```text
samāpatti
    wholesome one-pointedness
    with an associated aggregate-field
```

VAK 8.02 now supplies its first transformation rule:

```text
Samāpatti level A
    -- release from constituent -->
Samāpatti level B
```

In platform language:

```text
nodes
    meditative grounds

properties
    constituent factors

morphisms
    lawful transitions produced by viveka
```

This is why Samāpatti is more than a catalogue of states. It is the Idea of Samādhi articulated as a traversable field of determinate transformations.

The governing distinction remains:

```text
Samādhi
    = Concept of Samāpatti

Samāpatti
    = Idea of Samādhi
```

Here the Idea begins to reveal its internal law:

```text
one meditative configuration
    transforms into another
    through determinate separation
```

No claim about Supreme Samādhi is imposed on the verse. The immediate result is the formal architecture that discriminative knowledge must eventually comprehend.
