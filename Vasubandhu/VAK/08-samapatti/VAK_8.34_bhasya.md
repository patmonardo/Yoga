## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first classifies the eight Deliverances by their object-supports. The initial form-oriented Deliverances take visible form belonging to the desire realm as object. According to the particular Deliverance, the visible object may be unpleasant or pleasant.

The formless Deliverances have a different cognitive range. They take suffering belonging to their own and higher planes, together with its ground and cessation, and the path on the side of *anvayajñāna*. Vasubandhu adds that non-analytical cessation (*apratisaṃkhyānirodha*) must also be included and, for one formless Deliverance, space.

He then asks why there is no corresponding Deliverance in the third Dhyāna. The reason is that attachment to color/form belonging to the second Dhyāna is absent there, while the third Dhyāna is disturbed by pleasure. Therefore the pure Deliverance is produced instead.

The pure Deliverance has two practical functions. It can brighten a continuum made depressed or contracted by sustained impurity contemplation, and it can test whether the first two impurity-based Deliverances have actually been mastered. Their completion is demonstrated when the yogin can deliberately attend to the beautiful without affliction arising.

Vasubandhu finally states two general purposes for cultivating the Deliverances and related attainments: to distance the afflictions and to obtain mastery over meditative attainment (*samāpattivaśitva*). Such mastery supports the manifestation of further qualities such as non-conflict and noble supernormal power. The third and eighth Deliverances receive special language of direct realization because they are principal and occupy terminal positions within their respective domain-level sequences.

## Argument Reconstruction

1. Deliverance must be classified not only by state but by **object-field**.
2. The first Deliverances remain within visible desire-realm form.
3. The formless Deliverances operate over a truth-structured field belonging to their own and higher planes.
4. Freedom cannot be established merely by replacing an attractive field with an unattractive one.
5. Therefore the practitioner must test whether the supposedly mastered relation remains free when the attractive field is deliberately restored.
6. If beautiful form can be attended to without affliction arising, the impurity-based Deliverances have genuinely accomplished their function.
7. The general goal is twofold: distancing affliction and acquiring mastery over samāpatti.

The decisive practical criterion is therefore not the disappearance of the tempting object but the disappearance of its power to generate affliction.

## New Technical Terms

- **samāpattivaśitva** — mastery over meditative attainment. **Status:** Bhāṣya emphasis. **Function:** states the second general purpose for cultivating the Deliverances and related practices.
- **varṇarāga** — attachment to color/form. **Status:** local technical expression. **Function:** explains the distribution of Deliverances across Dhyāna levels.
- **līna-saṃtati** — a depressed, contracted, or sunken continuum. **Status:** Bhāṣya-only local expression. **Function:** explains why pure/beautiful contemplation may follow sustained impurity contemplation.
- **jijñāsā** — desire to test or ascertain. **Status:** ordinary term with technical function here. **Function:** introduces the mastery-test of the first two Deliverances.
- **āryā ṛddhi** — noble supernormal power. **Status:** previously encountered doctrine. **Function:** one result supported by mastery of these attainments.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting the received Abhidharma classification and supplying practical reasons and tests.

The passage contains question-and-answer transitions, but there is no compelling reason here to assign the questions to a distinct doctrinal school voice.

## Doctrinal Determination

The first Deliverances take desire-realm visible form as object. The formless Deliverances take truth-structured objects belonging to their own and higher planes, together with additional unconditioned or spatial objects where applicable.

More important than the classification itself is Vasubandhu's criterion of completion. The first two Deliverances are not perfected merely because the practitioner can sustain an unattractive aspect. They are perfected when the practitioner can intentionally attend to the beautiful and no affliction arises.

Thus counter-practice is preparatory to mastery but is not identical with mastery.

The general purposes of the Deliverances are:

```text
kleśadūrīkaraṇa
    distancing the afflictions

samāpattivaśitva
    mastery over attainment
```

## Translation Consequences

- **viṣaya / ālambana / gocara** should remain differentiated where possible: object, object-support, and cognitive range/object-field are related but not interchangeable.
- **śubha** in this passage must retain the positive sense of the beautiful/pure, because Vasubandhu contrasts it directly with *aśubha* and uses it as a test of freedom from attachment.
- **vaśitva** is stronger than mere proficiency: it indicates mastery or command over the attainment.
- **anvayajñānapakṣa** should remain technical. The Bhāṣya clarifies its extension to the path but does not justify replacing it with a loose generic expression such as “inferential cognition.”

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.34 distinguishes two very different operations:

```text
change the appearing content
    ≠
change the governing relation to that content
```

Impurity contemplation initially changes the aspect under which form appears. But this alone does not prove freedom. The stronger test restores positive appearance while checking whether attachment returns.

Hence the genetic sequence is:

```text
binding appearance
    → counter-aspect
    → stabilization
    → restoration of positive appearance
    → no return of affliction
    → mastery
```

This is a particularly clear example of practical abstraction. A difference in the object-field is first deliberately manipulated so that the practitioner can isolate the binding relation itself. The completed practice no longer depends upon retaining the counter-object.

## Light Organon Contact

This passage provides a useful model for a later transcendental theory of appearance. Freedom is not established by destroying or permanently negating the phenomenal field. The field may return in positive form while its former power of determination does not.

That suggests a disciplined distinction between:

```text
field-content
and
governing relation
```

The first may vary while the second is the real target of transformation. This is close to the project's emerging idea of a regulated field of cognition and relation, but the Bhāṣya itself should remain the controlling evidence: here Vasubandhu's explicit criterion is simply that beautiful form may be contemplated without the re-arising of affliction.
