# VAK_8.04 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ākāśānantya-vijñānānantyākiṃcanya-saṃjñakāḥ /
> tathāprayogān māndyāt tu naivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñakaḥ // VAK_8.04 //

**Best current translation:**  
The first three are designated as infinity of space, infinity of knowledge, and nothingness because practice is undertaken in those respective ways. The fourth is called neither-perception-nor-non-perception because perception there is attenuated.

## Source Caution

The working e-text contains minor orthographic irregularities (`kimepām`, `nasaṃjñanāpyasaṃjñakaḥ`, etc.). The doctrinal syntax is nevertheless clear. The Bhāṣya's central correction is important: the names of the first three ārūpyas do **not** prove that space, knowledge, and nothingness are simply their exclusive object-supports. Vasubandhu explicitly asks that question and answers `na hi` — no.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu begins with an interpretive question. Are the formless attainments called infinity of space, infinity of knowledge, and nothingness because those are simply their respective objects?

His answer is no.

The first three receive those names because the practitioner enters them through a corresponding mode of practice (`tathā-prayoga`). One attends respectively:

```text
anantam ākāśam
    space is infinite

anantaṃ vijñānam
    knowledge is infinite

nāsti kiñcit
    there is nothing
```

The names arise from these modes of directed mental practice.

The fourth attainment requires a different account. It is called `naivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñāyatana` because `saṃjñā` has become exceedingly weak or subtle (`mṛdu`, `māndya`). It is not a clear or forceful perception (`paṭvī saṃjñā`), yet perception is not altogether absent.

Vasubandhu notes that the practitioner may also contemplate perception negatively — as disease, swelling, thorn — and non-perception as delusion, while regarding neither-perception-nor-non-perception as peaceful and excellent. But he does not make that evaluative practice the decisive definition. The determining explanation remains the actual attenuation of `saṃjñā`.

## New Technical Terms

- **tathā-prayoga** — practice undertaken in precisely the manner expressed by the corresponding formulation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā
  - **function:** explains the naming of the first three formless attainments

- **manas-kṛ / manasikurvāṇa** — to attend to, bring before the mind, or mentally determine
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** specifies the operative practice by which the first three attainments are approached

- **mṛdu-saṃjñā** — weak, subtle, attenuated perception
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** grounds the designation of the fourth formless attainment

- **paṭvī saṃjñā** — sharp, forceful, clearly operative perception
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** contrast-class for the attenuated perception of the fourth attainment

- **saṃjñā-roga / saṃjñā-gaṇḍa / saṃjñā-śalya** — perception regarded as disease, swelling, or thorn
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only evaluative vocabulary
  - **function:** describes a contemplative valuation used in approaching the fourth attainment

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya corrects an overly simple object-theory of the ārūpyas.

The names of the first three are not merely labels for three independently given objects. They are generated through **specific modes of practice**:

```text
practice-form
    determines designation
```

Thus:

```text
ākāśānantya
    is entered through attending to boundless space

vijñānānantya
    is entered through attending to boundless knowledge

ākiñcanya
    is entered through attending “there is nothing”
```

The fourth differs structurally. It cannot be defined by a comparable new object-support. Its determination concerns the degree of operation of `saṃjñā` itself.

```text
ordinary saṃjñā
    too strong to describe the state

absence of saṃjñā
    also false

therefore
    neither-perception-nor-non-perception
```

This is not indeterminacy. It is a determinate limit-condition of a still-operative factor.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā's `tathāprayogāt` should be allowed to govern the naming of the first three attainments explicitly:

> They are so called because one practices by attending respectively: “space is infinite,” “knowledge is infinite,” and “there is nothing.”

The term `vijñāna` remains **knowledge** in this project vocabulary. The phrase `anantaṃ vijñānam` therefore means “knowledge is infinite” or “boundless knowledge,” not automatically “infinite consciousness.”

For the fourth attainment, `māndya` should be rendered as attenuation, weakness, or extreme subtlety of perception rather than ordinary dullness.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.04 gives two distinct modes of specification.

### 1. Specification through practice

```text
possible field
    ↓
mode of attending
    ↓
determinate attainment-name
```

The important rule is:

> **A systematic determination may be generated from the mode of operation by which the field is entered, not merely from a static object already present.**

The first three ārūpyas therefore have a genetic structure:

```text
practice
    → determination
    → named attainment
```

### 2. Specification through finite limit

The fourth attainment gives a different logical form:

```text
saṃjñā is present
    but below the threshold of ordinary robust perception

therefore
    not ordinary perception

saṃjñā is not absent

therefore
    not non-perception
```

The double negation is determinate because the field is already finite and specified by the status of one factor, `saṃjñā`.

```text
na saṃjñā
    does not mean everything outside perception

na asaṃjñā
    does not mean an unlimited complement

both operate inside
    a fixed meditative configuration
```

This is a strong example of an apparently “infinite” judgment becoming meaningful only within a determinate systematic domain.

## Light Organon Contact

The immediately useful result is methodological rather than metaphysical.

The Kośa shows how a System converts apparently unbounded terms into finite specifications.

```text
“infinite space”
“infinite knowledge”
“nothing”
“neither perception nor non-perception”
```

These expressions would be dangerously indefinite outside a System. Within the ordered field of Samāpatti they become controlled determinations because the System specifies:

```text
which practice generates them
which lower state they presuppose
which factor is operative
which factor has been attenuated or excluded
```

Thus `ārūpya` is not an infinite outside of Form. It is a finite family of positively specifiable meditative configurations.

This supports a restrained systems principle:

> **Negation becomes scientifically meaningful only when the System fixes the domain, the operative determination, and the admissible transition.**

That is particularly clear in the fourth attainment: “neither perception nor non-perception” is not a vague mystical contradiction. It is the exact classification of a state in which perception remains, but only in an extremely attenuated mode.

## Relation to VAK 8.01–8.03

The opening sequence now has a coherent progression:

```text
8.01
    samāpatti = wholesome one-pointedness
    with associated aggregate-field

8.02
    higher fields arise through viveka
    and ārūpya is four-skandha

8.03
    rūpa is genuinely absent
    yet rūpa can arise again from conditioned citta

8.04
    the formless field is positively differentiated
    through practice-form and factor-intensity
```

This is important: the chapter does not leave `ārūpya` as mere privation. Once rūpa is excluded, the remaining field receives its own internal determinations.
