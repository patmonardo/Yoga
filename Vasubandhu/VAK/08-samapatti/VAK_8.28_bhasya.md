# VAK_8.28 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
darśanāyākṣyabhijñeṣṭā dhībhedāya prayogajāḥ /
vajropamo 'ntye yo dhyāne sāsravakṣayabhāvanā // VAK_8.28 //
```

## Best Current Translation

For knowledge-and-seeing, the divine-eye superknowledge is intended. Application-born qualities are the cultivation of samādhi for differentiation of prajñā. The Vajra-like samādhi in the final dhyāna is the cultivation for exhaustion of the outflows.

See the existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_8.28.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 451|14–452|03.

```text
    darśanāyākṣyabhijñeṣṭā

divyacakṣurabhijñā jñānadarśanāya samādhibhāvanā /

    dhībhedāya prayogajāḥ /

prayogajāḥ sarve guṇāstraidhātukā anāsravāḥ prajñāprabhedāya samādhibhāvanā /

    vajropamo'ntye yo dhyāne sāsravakṣayabhāvanā // VAkK_8.28 //

yaścaturthadhyāne vajropamaḥ samādhiḥ sa āsravakṣayāya samādhibhāvanā /
ātmopanāyikī kilaiṣā bhagavato dharmadeśanā /
ataścaturbhya evāha /
kṛtaḥ samādhīnāṃ kālagato nirdeśaḥ /
idānīṃ samādhisaṃniśritānāṃ guṇānāṃ nirdeśakāla iti vyākhyāyante /
```

## Natural Argument

VAK 8.28 completes the four `samādhibhāvanās` introduced at VAK 8.27.

The second cultivation is for `jñānadarśana`, knowledge-and-seeing. Vasubandhu identifies its operative attainment as the divine-eye superknowledge (`divyacakṣurabhijñā`). The point is direct disclosure rather than merely pleasant abiding.

The third cultivation is for `prajñāprabheda`, differentiation of prajñā. The Bhāṣya is broader than the Kārikā's compressed wording: all qualities born from deliberate application (`prayogajāḥ sarve guṇāḥ`), throughout the three domains and including uncontaminated qualities, belong here.

The fourth cultivation is the Vajra-like samādhi (`vajropamaḥ samādhiḥ`) in the fourth dhyāna. Its function is `āsravakṣaya`, exhaustion of the outflows.

Vasubandhu then closes the discussion of samādhis as such and explicitly announces a transition: the exposition of samādhis is complete; what follows concerns the qualities that depend upon samādhi (`samādhisaṃniśritā guṇāḥ`). This is the natural bridge to VAK 8.29 and the four immeasurables.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
1. dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāra
   pleasant abiding in the present life
   → first wholesome dhyāna

2. jñānadarśana
   knowledge-and-seeing
   → divine-eye superknowledge

3. prajñāprabheda
   differentiation of prajñā
   → all application-born qualities

4. āsravakṣaya
   exhaustion of the outflows
   → Vajra-like samādhi in fourth dhyāna
```

The sequence is functional rather than merely scalar. Four cultivations are distinguished by four different ends.

## New Technical Terms

- **jñānadarśana** — knowledge-and-seeing; **status:** previously encountered doctrinal phrase; **function:** names the aim of the second cultivation.
- **prajñāprabheda** — differentiation/articulation of prajñā; **status:** Bhāṣya gloss sharpening Kārikā `dhībheda`; **function:** defines the aim of the third cultivation.
- **prayogajāḥ sarve guṇāḥ** — all qualities born from deliberate application; **status:** Bhāṣya expansion; **function:** broadens the basis of the third cultivation.
- **vajropamaḥ samādhiḥ** — Vajra-like samādhi; **status:** previously encountered path term; **function:** identifies the fourth cultivation's immediate liberating instrument.
- **samādhisaṃniśritā guṇāḥ** — qualities dependent upon samādhi; **status:** Bhāṣya transition phrase; **function:** marks the move from the classification of samādhis to the qualities supported by them.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the remaining three cultivations and the transition to the next section.

The phrase `ātmopanāyikī kilaiṣā bhagavato dharmadeśanā` is reported as an explanatory remark about the Buddha's teaching; no separate opponent is introduced here.

## Doctrinal Determination

Together VAK 8.27–8.28 establish four distinct purposes of samādhi cultivation:

| Cultivation | Operative basis | Aim |
|---|---|---|
| First | first wholesome dhyāna | pleasant abiding here and now |
| Second | divine-eye superknowledge | knowledge-and-seeing |
| Third | all application-born qualities | differentiation of prajñā |
| Fourth | Vajra-like samādhi in fourth dhyāna | exhaustion of outflows |

The third member is especially important. `dhībheda` should not be reduced to generic "differentiated understanding." Vasubandhu glosses it specifically as `prajñāprabheda`: differentiation or articulation of prajñā.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation should be tightened in two places.

First, `darśanāya` is governed by Vasubandhu's gloss `jñānadarśanāya`; the second cultivation is therefore better rendered **for knowledge-and-seeing** than simply "for direct vision."

Second, `dhībheda` should be rendered in light of `prajñāprabheda` as **for differentiation of prajñā**. This preserves the technical cognitive force of `dhī` and avoids weakening the passage into ordinary understanding.

A tightened translation is therefore:

> For knowledge-and-seeing, the divine-eye superknowledge is intended. Application-born qualities are for differentiation of prajñā. The Vajra-like samādhi in the fourth dhyāna is the cultivation for exhaustion of the outflows.

## Logical Determination

The four cultivations distinguish four functions of a developed samādhi-system:

```text
abiding
    → disclosure
    → differentiation
    → exhaustion
```

This is not a ladder of increasing concentration. Each member changes the function under which samādhi operates.

The third cultivation is the mediating term between seeing and liberation. Direct disclosure by itself is insufficient; the disclosed field must become differentiated through prajñā before the terminal Vajra-like act can exhaust the remaining outflows.

## Light Organon Contact

A restrained systems contact is warranted:

```text
present coherent actuality
    → direct manifestation
    → articulated discrimination
    → terminal liberating efficacy
```

The Bhāṣya does not itself identify these with a transcendental triad or with Hegelian moments. What it does establish textually is a genetically ordered functional progression: seeing is not yet differentiated knowing, and differentiated knowing is not yet final exhaustion.

The closing prose also provides a structural hinge for Chapter 8:

```text
samādhis themselves
    ↓
qualities dependent upon samādhi
```

That transition begins with VAK 8.29 and the four immeasurables.
