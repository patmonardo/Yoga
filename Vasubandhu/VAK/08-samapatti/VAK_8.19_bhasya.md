# VAK_8.19 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> vyutkrāntakasamāpattir visabhāgatṛtīyagā /
> svādhobhūmyāśrayā eva dhyānārūpyāḥ vṛthādharam // VAK_8.19 //

**Best current translation:**  
Crossed attainment goes to the third attainment in a dissimilar mode. The dhyānas and formless attainments are manifested on the support of their own or lower grounds; for one established above, the lower is useless.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.19.md`](./VAK_8.19.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

**Reference:** Pradhan 446|06–446|21.

```text
ayaṃ prayogo vyutkrāntakasamāpatteḥ /
yadā tu prathamātsāsravāt tṛtīyamanāsravaṃ dhyānaṃ samāpadyate tasmātsāsravamākāśānantyāyatanaṃ tasmādanāsravamākiñcanyāyatanam /
evaṃ punaḥ pratilomam /
tadā visabhāgatṛtīyadravyagamanādabhiniṣpannā bhavati /
ativiprakṛṣṭatvānna caturthī samāpadyate /
tāṃ ca triṣu dvīpeṣu asamayavimukta evārhannutpādayati /
niḥkleśatvātsamādhivaśitvācca /
dṛṣṭiprāptasya yadyapi tīkṣṇendriyatvāt samādhau vaśitvaṃ na tu niṣkleśaḥ /
samayavimukto yadyapi niḥkleśo natvasya samādhau vaśitvamiti /
kenāśrayeṇa kati dhyānārupyāḥ saṃmukhīkriyante /
svādhobhūmyāśrayā eva /
dhyānāśrayā eva dhyānārūpyāḥ /
bhavāgraṃ bhavāgre ca saṃmukhīkriyate adhaśca yāvatkāmadhātau /
śeṣāṇi svasyāṃ bhūmāvadhaśceti /
kiṃ kāraṇamūrdhvopapanno nādharāṃ samāpatti saṃmukhīkaroti /
tasmāttasya vṛthā'dharam // VAkK_8.19 //
nahi tasyādhareṇa samāpattidravyeṇa kiñcit prayojanaṃ vidhīyate /
nihīnatvāt /
```

## Source Caution

The e-text is a reference text rather than a critical edition. The phrase `visabhāgatṛtīyadravyagamana` is doctrinally decisive and should not be reduced to mere spatial skipping: the perfected attainment combines a move to the third attainment with a change of mode.

The prose immediately before this verse belongs to VAK 8.18 and defines the contiguous and one-skipped practice as `prayoga`, preparation. VAK 8.19 gives the criterion for completion (`abhiniṣpannā`).

## Natural Argument

### 1. From preparation to perfected crossed attainment

VAK 8.18 established the preliminary discipline:

```text
master all principal grounds
    forward and backward
    worldly and uncontaminated

then

practice one-level skipping
    forward and backward
```

Vasubandhu now says that this is only the `prayoga`, the preparation for crossed attainment.

The actual perfected form is more exact:

```text
worldly first dhyāna
    ↓ skip second + change mode
uncontaminated third dhyāna
    ↓ skip fourth + change mode
worldly ākāśānantyāyatana
    ↓ skip vijñānānantyāyatana + change mode
uncontaminated ākiñcanyāyatana
```

The same sequence is then mastered in reverse (`pratilomam`).

The attainment is called perfected because there is:

```text
tṛtīya-dravya-gamana
    going to the third attainment

+

visabhāga
    entering a dissimilar mode
```

Thus the crossing is not merely ordinal. It is simultaneously a controlled discontinuity of level and mode.

### 2. Why not jump to the fourth?

Vasubandhu explicitly blocks an unlimited interpretation:

```text
ativiprakṛṣṭatvān na caturthī samāpadyate
```

One does not enter the fourth [removed attainment] because it is too remote.

So `vyutkrānti` is bounded mastery, not unrestricted jumping.

### 3. Who can produce this attainment?

The perfected crossed attainment is produced, in the three continents, only by an `asamayavimukta arhat`.

The reason is twofold:

```text
niḥkleśatva
    freedom from defilement

samādhi-vaśitva
    mastery over samādhi
```

Vasubandhu then contrasts two incomplete cases:

- the `dṛṣṭiprāpta` has mastery in samādhi owing to sharp faculties, but is not yet free from kleśa;
- the `samayavimukta` is free from kleśa, but lacks the required mastery in samādhi.

The crossed attainment therefore requires both liberation from affliction and operational mastery of meditative transition.

### 4. What support can manifest which attainments?

Vasubandhu next asks:

```text
kenāśrayeṇa kati dhyānārūpyāḥ saṃmukhīkriyante?
```

On what support are how many dhyānas and ārūpyas made present?

The answer is:

```text
svādhobhūmyāśrayā eva
    only on their own ground or a lower ground
```

Bhavāgra can be manifested by one born at bhavāgra itself and by beings on lower grounds all the way down to kāmadhātu. The remaining attainments are likewise manifested on their own ground or below it.

Thus a lower existential support can generate a higher meditative attainment.

### 5. Why does one born above not manifest a lower attainment?

The answer is terse:

```text
vṛthā'dharam
    the lower is useless
```

Vasubandhu explains that a lower samāpatti-dravya provides no relevant purpose (`prayojana`) for one already born above it, because it is inferior (`nihīnatvāt`).

The claim is therefore practical, not a denial that the lower state exists. The lower attainment has ceased to be a useful goal for that higher support.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
ordinary ordered mastery
    ↓
one-level skipping
    ↓
mode-changing one-level skipping
    ↓
perfected vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti
```

Completion requires two differences at once:

```text
change of bhūmi-position
    +
change of sāsrava / anāsrava mode
```

And the capacity itself requires:

```text
niḥkleśatva
    +
samādhi-vaśitva
```

The second half adds an independent support-rule:

```text
attainment X
    can be manifested from
    own ground or lower ground

but

higher rebirth-support
    does not manifest lower attainment
    because the lower has no use there
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose throughout the passage.

No distinct opponent is required for the main argument, although the questions function dialectically to open successive determinations.

## New Technical Terms

- **abhiniṣpanna** — fully accomplished, perfected
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative term in this passage
  - **function:** marks the transition from preparation to completed crossed attainment

- **visabhāga** — dissimilar in mode or class
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly unpacked by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** specifies that perfected crossing changes qualitative mode as well as level

- **samādhi-vaśitva** — mastery or command over samādhi
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** necessary condition for perfected crossed attainment

- **niḥkleśatva** — freedom from defilement
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** second necessary condition for perfected crossed attainment

- **saṃmukhīkṛ / saṃmukhībhāva** — to make present, bring into direct manifestation
  - **status:** previously encountered vocabulary made technically operative here
  - **function:** distinguishes existential support from the attainment made present upon it

- **prayojana** — use, purpose, practical function
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative term here
  - **function:** explains why a higher-born being does not manifest an inferior attainment

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.19 completes the doctrine of crossed attainment by distinguishing **preparation** from **accomplishment**.

A mere ability to skip one intervening ground is not yet the perfected form. Full accomplishment requires simultaneous heterogeneity of mode:

```text
worldly → uncontaminated → worldly → uncontaminated
```

while also moving through every third attainment.

The procedure is therefore a disciplined control of two independent dimensions of samāpatti:

```text
vertical position in the bhūmi-series
qualitative mode of the attainment
```

The verse also establishes that meditative attainment and rebirth-support are not identical. Lower supports can manifest higher attainments, but a higher support has no practical reason to re-enter an inferior attainment.

## Translation Consequences

**Clarification and revision recommended.**

The earlier philosophical translation, “passing over the intervening level and changing the mode of attainment,” is substantially correct, but the Bhāṣya makes `visabhāgatṛtīyagā` more precise:

> Crossed attainment is the perfected entry into every third attainment in a dissimilar mode.

For the second half, `vṛthādharam` should be read practically:

> for one born above, the lower attainment serves no purpose.

This is preferable to an unqualified “the lower is useless.”

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes **freedom from sequence** from **ignorance of sequence**.

```text
untrained discontinuity
    skips because the middle is not possessed

vyutkrāntaka mastery
    skips because the middle has already been mastered
```

But 8.19 adds another condition:

```text
mere ordinal skip
    is still preparation

perfected crossing
    = ordinal skip
      + qualitative alternation
```

The attained power is therefore not arbitrary mobility. It is controlled transformation across a known state-space.

The support doctrine likewise separates:

```text
āśraya
    where the practitioner is established

samāpatti
    what state can be made present

prayojana
    whether making it present has a function
```

These are three distinct determinations.

## Light Organon Contact

This passage gives a particularly strong example of systematic freedom.

Freedom is not represented as the ability to ignore mediation. It appears only after the mediated order is completely possessed, at which point the practitioner can deliberately abbreviate that order without losing control of it.

```text
mastered order
    ↓
controlled omission
    ↓
controlled qualitative alternation
    ↓
freedom of transition
```

The comparison should remain subordinate to the Kośa: Vasubandhu's immediate concern is meditative mastery, not a general theory of systems. But the formal structure is unusually explicit.
