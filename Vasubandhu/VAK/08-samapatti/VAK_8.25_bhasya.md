# VAK_8.25 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
śuddhāmalāḥ nirmalās tu te vimokṣamukhatrayam /
śūnyatāśūnyatādyākhyās trayo 'parasamādhayaḥ // VAK_8.25 //
```

## Best Current Translation

The three Recognitions may be pure or immaculate; the immaculate forms are the three gates of liberation. There are also three further Recognitions, called Emptiness-of-Emptiness and so forth.

See the existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_8.25.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 450|01–450|11.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first completes the explanation of `apraṇihita` from VAK 8.24. Impermanence, suffering, and their causes provoke `udvega`, while the path itself is ultimately to be relinquished like a raft. Emptiness and non-self do not produce the same reaction because they are common, in the relevant respect, to nirvāṇa and point toward passing beyond conditioned existence.

He then classifies the three samādhis of VAK 8.24 in a new way.

They are **twofold**:

```text
śuddhaka
    pure
    laukika
    worldly

anāsrava
    uncontaminated / immaculate
    lokottara
    supramundane
```

The worldly forms occur on eleven levels (`ekādaśasu bhūmiṣu`). The supramundane forms occur wherever the path is present (`yatra mārgaḥ`).

This yields the decisive qualification of the first half of the verse:

```text
three samādhis
    ↓ two possible modes

worldly / pure
    not yet vimokṣamukha

supramundane / anāsrava
    = three gates of liberation
```

The three uncontaminated samādhis are called:

```text
śūnyatā-vimokṣamukha
apraṇihita-vimokṣamukha
ānimitta-vimokṣamukha
```

They are called `vimokṣamukha` because they are **doors of liberation** (`mokṣadvāratvāt`).

The Bhāṣya then introduces three further samādhis:

```text
śūnyatāśūnyatā
apraṇihitāpraṇihita
ānimittānimitta
```

Their names are not rhetorical doubling. Vasubandhu states the principle explicitly:

```text
śūnyatādyālambanatvāt tannāma
```

They receive these names because they take śūnyatā and the corresponding preceding samādhis as their object-supports.

Thus the second triad is structurally dependent on the first:

```text
first-order samādhi
    śūnyatā
    apraṇihita
    ānimitta
        ↓ becomes ālambana

further samādhi
    śūnyatāśūnyatā
    apraṇihitāpraṇihita
    ānimittānimitta
```

VAK 8.26 will specify exactly how each of these further samādhis takes its object.

## Argument Reconstruction

The passage contains two successive determinations.

### 1. Purity is not yet liberation

```text
same formal samādhi
    ↓
worldly manifestation
    śuddhaka
    ↓
not yet a gate of liberation
```

versus:

```text
same formal samādhi
    ↓
supramundane manifestation
    anāsrava
    ↓
vimokṣamukha
```

The truth-aspect structure alone therefore does not determine liberative status. The mode of the knowing activity matters.

### 2. Liberative samādhi becomes itself an object

```text
samādhi₁
    determines its truth-object
        ↓

samādhi₁ itself becomes ālambana
        ↓

samādhi₂
    reflexively determines samādhi₁
```

This is a genuine new level of mediation. The second triad does not abandon the first; it turns the accomplished liberative mode itself into determinable content.

## New Technical Terms

- **vimokṣamukha** — gate/opening of liberation; **status:** introduced here as the technical designation for the uncontaminated forms of the three samādhis; **function:** distinguishes genuinely liberative samādhi from worldly pure samādhi.
- **mokṣadvāra** — door of liberation; **status:** Bhāṣya gloss; **function:** gives the derivational explanation of `vimokṣamukha`.
- **laukika / lokottara** — worldly / supramundane; **status:** established doctrinal pair; **function:** explains the twofold division expressed by `śuddha` and `amala`.
- **śūnyatāśūnyatā** — emptiness-of-emptiness; **status:** new technical samādhi; **function:** takes the preceding śūnyatā-samādhi as object-support.
- **apraṇihitāpraṇihita** — non-directedness-of-non-directedness; **status:** new technical samādhi; **function:** takes the preceding apraṇihita-samādhi as object-support.
- **ānimittānimitta** — signlessness-of-signlessness; **status:** new technical samādhi; **function:** takes the preceding ānimitta-samādhi as object-support.
- **ālambanatva** — being an object-support; **status:** familiar technical relation, newly decisive here; **function:** grounds the naming and reflexive structure of the further three samādhis.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of the twofold worldly/supramundane status of the three samādhis, definition of the liberation gates, and introduction of the three further samādhis.

No explicit opponent appears in this unit.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes an important distinction that the compressed Kārikā only signals.

`Śūnyatā`, `apraṇihita`, and `ānimitta` are not intrinsically identical with the gates of liberation merely by possessing their characteristic cognitive forms. Each can be either:

```text
śuddhaka / laukika
or
anāsrava / lokottara
```

Only the second mode is called a `vimokṣamukha`.

This means that **content and liberative status are distinct determinations**. A cognition may have the proper formal object-structure and still remain worldly. The liberation gate arises only when that structure is instantiated as uncontaminated supramundane samādhi.

The three further samādhis then introduce a second-order structure. The attained liberative samādhi is not left as an untouchable final object. It can itself become an `ālambana` for a further samādhi.

This prevents the first liberative determination from becoming doctrinally reified.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation is substantially confirmed.

Two refinements are now secure:

1. `śuddha` and `amala/nirmala` are explained through the doctrinal opposition **worldly / supramundane**, not merely through degrees of psychological purity.
2. The doubled names `śūnyatāśūnyatā`, `apraṇihitāpraṇihita`, and `ānimittānimitta` designate samādhis whose **object-support is the corresponding preceding samādhi**.

A tightened translation is therefore:

> The three samādhis are either pure or immaculate; the immaculate, supramundane forms are the three gates of liberation. There are also three further samādhis, called emptiness-of-emptiness, non-directedness-of-non-directedness, and signlessness-of-signlessness, because they take the corresponding preceding samādhis as their object-supports.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.25 introduces two logical distinctions.

First:

```text
form of cognition
    ≠
liberative actuality of cognition
```

The same determinate structure may occur in a worldly or supramundane mode. Liberative status therefore cannot be inferred merely from formal resemblance.

Second:

```text
Recognition
    ↓
Recognition as object-support
    ↓
Recognition of Recognition
```

The second triad is thus not another parallel classification. It is generated from the first by a change in logical role:

```text
what first functioned as determining activity
    ↓
becomes determinate object-support
    ↓
for a further determining activity
```

This is genuine reflection in the strict structural sense: the achieved determination returns into the field as an object of further determination.

## Light Organon Contact

The passage gives unusually strong textual support for the Kārikā-pass phrase **reflexive Recognition**, provided we keep it disciplined by Vasubandhu's explicit `ālambana` relation.

The movement is:

```text
truth-aspects
    ↓ determined by
first samādhi
    ↓ itself becomes ālambana of
further samādhi
```

The significance is not that the first samādhi was false or incomplete. Rather, liberation itself must not be converted into an inert final representation.

A determination capable of liberation remains living precisely because it can enter a further relation of determination without losing the truth already achieved.

This is a particularly clean instance of the project's governing requirement of genetic continuity: the second form is generated through the first, not imposed beside it as an analogy or static duplicate.
