# VAK_8.24 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
ānimittaḥ śamākāraiḥ śūnyatānātmaśūnyataḥ /
pravartate 'praṇihitaḥ satyākārair ataḥ paraiḥ // VAK_8.24 //
```

## Best Current Translation

Signless Recognition proceeds through the aspects of peace. Emptiness Recognition proceeds through emptiness and non-self. Non-directed Recognition proceeds through the remaining aspects of the truths.

See the existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_8.24.md`.

## Source Note

The supplied source text reads `samākāraiḥ` at the first pāda, while the existing Kārikā file provisionally reads `śamākāraiḥ`, “through the aspects of peace.” The Bhāṣya immediately glosses the expression as `nirodhasatyākāraiḥ`, “through the aspects of the truth of cessation,” which strongly supports the semantic determination of peaceful cessation. The source reading itself is preserved below rather than silently emended.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu opens a new threefold classification of samādhi:

```text
śūnyatā-samādhi
apraṇihita-samādhi
ānimitta-samādhi
```

The important movement of the prose is that these are not three arbitrary names placed over one undifferentiated meditative state. Each is generated from a determinate range of the sixteen aspects (`ākāra`) of the four noble truths.

### 1. Ānimitta-samādhi

The signless samādhi is associated with the **four aspects of the truth of cessation** (`nirodhasatyākāra`). Vasubandhu then supplies the ground of the name:

```text
nirvāṇa
    is free from ten nimittas
        ↓
therefore nirvāṇa is animitta
        ↓
a samādhi taking that as object-support
    is ānimitta-samādhi
```

The ten signs are enumerated as:

```text
five sense objects
    rūpa
    śabda
    gandha
    rasa
    spraṣṭavya

female
male

three determinations of the conditioned
    arising
    duration
    impermanence
```

Thus `ānimitta` is not an indeterminate blankness. It is determined by its object-support: nirvāṇa precisely insofar as nirvāṇa is free from the signs characterizing conditioned presentation.

### 2. Śūnyatā-samādhi

The emptiness samādhi is associated with exactly **two aspects**:

```text
anātman
śūnyatā
```

Vasubandhu therefore calls it `dvyākāra`, two-aspected. Its name is generated immediately from the two truth-aspects through which it proceeds.

### 3. Apraṇihita-samādhi

The non-directed samādhi is associated with the **remaining ten truth-aspects**.

The Bhāṣya does more than count them. It explains the practical ground of non-direction:

- impermanence (`anitya`), suffering (`duḥkha`), and their causes produce revulsion or alarm (`udvega`);
- the path too is ultimately to be abandoned, like a raft (`kolopamatā`);
- samādhi associated with these aspects therefore does not project itself toward them as a final object of aspiration.

It is consequently called `apraṇihita`: non-directed, non-projective, not fixed upon a conditioned terminus as something finally to be attained and retained.

Vasubandhu then guards the distinction. There is no corresponding `udvega` toward emptiness and non-self because they share something with nirvāṇa (`nirvāṇasāmānya`): they face toward passing beyond the conditioned rather than furnishing another conditioned goal.

## Aspect Distribution

The Bhāṣya gives an exact partition of the sixteen truth-aspects:

| Samādhi | Truth-aspects | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Ānimitta | four aspects of cessation | 4 |
| Śūnyatā | śūnyatā, anātman | 2 |
| Apraṇihita | all remaining truth-aspects | 10 |
| **Total** | | **16** |

So the three samādhis constitute a complete redistribution of the already established truth-field.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
sixteen aspects of the four truths
    ↓ differentiated according to liberative function

cessation as free from conditioned signs
    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

```

The names are therefore consequences of the relation between samādhi and its determinate object-aspects.

## New Technical Terms

- **daśa-nimitta** — the ten signs from which nirvāṇa is free; **status:** introduced explicitly in this Bhāṣya unit; **function:** grounds the name `animitta` rather than leaving “signless” merely negative.
- **nirodhasatyākāra** — aspect of the truth of cessation; **status:** familiar truth-aspect vocabulary, here used as the complete object-range of ānimitta-samādhi; **function:** determines the four aspects assigned to ānimitta.
- **dvyākāra** — two-aspected; **status:** local classificatory expression; **function:** fixes śūnyatā-samādhi to the two aspects śūnyatā and anātman.
- **udvega** — revulsion, alarm, recoil before what is impermanent, painful, and causally productive of suffering; **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary; **function:** supplies the practical reason for the designation `apraṇihita`.
- **kolopamatā** — the condition of being like a raft; **status:** sūtra-based analogy invoked in the Bhāṣya; **function:** establishes that even the path is instrumental and must ultimately be relinquished.
- **nirvāṇasāmānya** — commonality with nirvāṇa; **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression in this unit; **function:** explains why śūnyatā and anātman do not generate the same `udvega` as the remaining aspects.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduction of the three samādhis, distribution of the sixteen truth-aspects, explanation of the ten signs, and the practical rationale for `apraṇihita`.

No independent opponent is introduced in this unit.

## Doctrinal Determination

The three samādhis are best understood here as **three liberative orientations of the same articulated truth-field**, not as three unrelated techniques.

The underlying field has already been constituted by the sixteen aspects of the truths. VAK 8.24 now redistributes that field according to how liberation stands toward it:

```text
conditioned presentation
    → relinquished as sign and projected end

substantial bearer
    → denied through emptiness and non-self

cessation
    → taken precisely as free from conditioned signs
```

The decisive point is that liberation does not arise by deleting determination. It arises through a determinate re-relation to the field of truth-aspects.

`Ānimitta` is therefore not absence of all cognition; its four aspects are specified. `Śūnyatā` is not a generalized void; it has exactly two aspects. `Apraṇihita` is not apathy or lack of purposiveness; it is the refusal to absolutize what is conditioned or merely instrumental.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only, with one source-critical caution.**

The Bhāṣya confirms the existing philosophical distribution:

- ānimitta → cessation aspects;
- śūnyatā → emptiness and non-self;
- apraṇihita → the remaining truth-aspects.

It also materially strengthens the translation of `apraṇihita` as **non-directed** rather than merely “wishless”: the reason is not absence of agency but the refusal to direct final aspiration toward an impermanent, painful, causally conditioned, or merely instrumental object.

For the first pāda, the transmitted `samākāraiḥ` remains textually uncertain. The Bhāṣya's gloss `nirodhasatyākāraiḥ` supports the existing semantic interpretation “through the aspects of peaceful cessation,” but the source should not be silently normalized.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.24 performs a **complete functional partition** of an already determined universal field:

```text
16 truth-aspects
    = 4 + 2 + 10
```

But the logical significance is deeper than arithmetic. The partition is generated by three distinct relations to determinacy:

```text
ānimitta
    conditioned sign is negated
    through cessation as object-support

śūnyatā
    substantial bearer is negated
    through emptiness/non-self

apraṇihita
    projected conditioned end is negated
    through relinquishment
```

These are not three empty negations. Each negation preserves and reorganizes determinate content. The resulting samādhi is therefore not achieved by abstraction away from the truth-field but through its liberative determination.

The Bhāṣya's strongest logical contribution is the mediation of `apraṇihita` through the raft analogy. The path is necessary as path precisely because it is **for passage beyond itself**. If the means were retained as final end, its own function would be contradicted.

## Light Organon Contact

This verse gives a particularly clean example of the distinction between a determinate field and the free relation that comprehends it.

The sixteen truth-aspects are not erased in samādhi. They are recollected under three relations that disclose what they mean for liberation. The higher unity therefore does not suppress the determinate content it comprehends; it returns that content as an internally ordered whole.

That is the only Organon contact required here. We should resist prematurely identifying the three samādhis with an external logical triad. The textual genesis is already sufficient:

```text
truth-field
    ↓
determinate liberative relation
    ↓
three samādhis
```

The living middle is the **relation of samādhi to its object-aspects**. Each name is generated through that relation rather than imposed as a static label.

## Transition to VAK 8.25

The prose immediately continues by stating that these three samādhis are each twofold, pure and uncontaminated, and that in their uncontaminated form they are the three gates of liberation (`vimokṣamukha`). It then introduces a second, reflexive set:

```text
śūnyatā-śūnyatā
apraṇihita-apraṇihita
ānimitta-ānimitta
```

VAK 8.24 therefore establishes the first three samādhis in their object-structure; VAK 8.25 will turn them back upon themselves as further samādhis and explicitly determine their status as gates of liberation.
