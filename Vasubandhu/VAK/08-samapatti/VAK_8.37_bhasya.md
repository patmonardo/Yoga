# VAK_8.37_bhasya

## Kārikā

### IAST

`nirodha uktaḥ vairāgya-prayogāptaṃ tu śeṣitam /`
`tridhātv-āśrayam ārūpya-saṃjñaṃ śeṣaṃ manuṣya-jam // VAK_8.37 //`

### Best Current Translation

Cessation has already been explained. The remaining Deliverances and related attainments may be acquired either through dispassion or through deliberate application. Those designated as formless may have support in any of the three realms; the rest are generated only on a human support.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu closes the preceding classification by adding two kinds of conditions: **how these attainments are acquired** and **what sort of support can first produce them**.

The cessation Deliverance is set aside because it has already been explained in all relevant respects. The other Deliverances and the related mastery and totality practices can arise in two ways. They may be obtained through **vairāgya**, dispassion from a lower field, or through **prayoga**, deliberate cultivation. Vasubandhu explains the variation through `ucitānucitatva`: whether the practitioner is already habituated or not habituated to the attainment.

The verse then classifies support. The formless Deliverances and the two formless totalities can occur with support in any of the three realms. The remaining practices are produced only with a human support. Vasubandhu gives a reason: their production depends upon the power of instruction.

Thus the verse does not merely locate practices cosmologically. It connects attainment with prior formation, deliberate training, and the availability of teaching.

## Argument Reconstruction

1. The cessation Deliverance has already received its complete explanation.
2. The remaining Deliverances and related practices admit more than one mode of acquisition.
3. A practitioner already habituated to a practice may acquire it through dispassion; one not so habituated may require deliberate application.
4. Therefore acquisition depends upon the practitioner's prior formation as well as the attainment itself.
5. The formless Deliverances and formless totalities are not restricted to human support; they may have support across the three realms.
6. The remaining practices require human support for their initial production.
7. Their dependence on instruction explains this restriction.

## New Technical Terms

- **ucita / anucita** — habituated / not habituated to a practice or attainment.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya clarification not explicit in the Kārikā.
  - **Function:** explains why an attainment may be acquired through dispassion in one case and deliberate application in another.

- **vairāgyalābhika** — obtained through dispassion.
  - **Status:** extension of Kārikā `vairāgya`.
  - **Function:** mode of acquisition.

- **prāyogika** — produced through deliberate application.
  - **Status:** extension of Kārikā `prayoga`.
  - **Function:** mode of acquisition.

- **upadeśasāmarthya** — efficacy or power of instruction.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
  - **Function:** explains why the non-formless practices are initially produced only with human support.

- **āśraya** — support, here the type of embodied/existential basis in which an attainment is produced.
  - **Status:** present implicitly in the Kārikā compound.
  - **Function:** support-classification rather than object-classification.

## Voice

- **Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the preserved passage.

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage draws three distinctions that should not be collapsed:

```text
attainment
    ≠
mode of acquiring attainment
    ≠
support in which attainment is produced
```

The same type of attainment may be reached through different genetic routes. `Vairāgya` and `prayoga` therefore describe acquisition, not two different species of attainment.

The support rule is also narrower than a loose reading of the Kārikā suggests:

```text
ārūpya-vimokṣas + two ārūpya-kṛtsnas
    → support possible in all three realms

remaining vimokṣa / abhibhvāyatana / kṛtsna practices
    → initially human-supported
```

The stated reason for the latter restriction is pedagogical: they are generated through the efficacy of instruction.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass rendering of `tridhātvāśrayam ārūpyasaṃjñam` as though all the remaining higher practices simply had support in the three realms was too broad. The Bhāṣya restricts `traidhātukāśraya` specifically to the formless Deliverances and the two formless totalities.

Likewise, `vairāgya-prayogāptam` should not be read as a rigid partition into one class acquired by dispassion and another by application. Vasubandhu says the same remaining complex can be `vairāgyalābhika` or `prāyogika` according to habituation.

## Logical Determination

The verse shifts from classification of practiced fields to **genesis of capacities**.

```text
prior habituation
        ↓
mode of acquisition
        ↓
stable attainment
```

And at another level:

```text
instruction
    → human production of practice
    → stabilized capacity
```

This is important because the ability to regulate or master an appearing field is not treated as an immediate property of a subject. It has conditions of genesis: prior habituation, deliberate application, dispassion, support, and instruction.

## Light Organon Contact

This passage is useful for a later theory of transcendental training because it refuses to treat cognitive mastery as an unexplained faculty. A capacity has a **history of formation**.

The stronger lesson is therefore not simply that a field can be regulated. It is that the agent capable of such regulation must itself be genetically produced through disciplined mediation.

That remains a practical and pedagogical determination in the Kośa. It should not yet be inflated into a general constitutive theory of cognition.
