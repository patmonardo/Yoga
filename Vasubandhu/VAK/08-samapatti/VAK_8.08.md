# VAK_8.08 — The Limbs of the Third and Fourth Dhyānas

## Sanskrit

### Devanāgarī

```text
तृतीये पञ्च तूपेक्षा स्मृतिः प्रज्ञा सुखं स्थितिः ।
चत्वार्यन्तेऽसुखादुःखोपेक्षास्मृतिसमाधयः ॥ ८.८ ॥
```

### IAST

```text
tṛtīye pañca tūpekṣā smṛtiḥ prajñā sukhaṃ sthitiḥ /
catvāry ante 'sukhāduḥkhopekṣāsmṛtisamādhayaḥ // VAK_8.08 //
```

## Source Caution

The second half is highly compact. The sequence:

```text
asukhāduḥkha-upekṣā-smṛti-samādhayaḥ
```

is to be resolved as four distinct limbs:

```text
aduhkhāsukha vedanā
upekṣā
smṛti
samādhi
```

The transmitted sandhi may visually suggest a single compound, but the doctrinal count requires four members.

Likewise, `sthitiḥ` in the first half is taken as mental stability or collected abiding and functions here as the concentration-limb of the third dhyāna.

## Grammar

The first half lists five limbs of the third dhyāna:

```text
upekṣā
smṛti
prajñā
sukha
sthiti
```

The construction is elliptical:

```text
tṛtīye pañca
    in the third, there are five
```

The second half gives four limbs of the final form dhyāna:

```text
ante catvāri
    in the last, there are four

asukha-aduḥkha
upekṣā
smṛti
samādhi
```

Thus VAK 8.07 and 8.08 together complete the limb-counts of all four form dhyānas.

## Literal Translation

In the third there are five: equanimity, mindfulness, prajñā, pleasure, and stability. In the last there are four: neither-pleasure-nor-pain, equanimity, mindfulness, and samādhi.

## Philosophical Translation

The third dhyāna is constituted by equanimity, mindfulness, discriminative understanding, pleasure, and stable collectedness. In the fourth dhyāna, pleasure has ceased; the field is constituted instead by neither-pleasure-nor-pain, equanimity, mindfulness, and samādhi.

## Technical Vocabulary

### upekṣā

`Upekṣā` appears in both the third and fourth dhyānas.

```text
upekṣā
    non-reactive balance
    evenness toward the meditative field
```

Its repetition shows continuity across the transition from the third to the fourth dhyāna.

### smṛti

`Smṛti` likewise belongs to both levels.

```text
smṛti
    retention of the meditative determination
    non-forgetfulness of the object-support
```

It prevents the state from dissolving into vagueness.

### prajñā

In the third dhyāna, `prajñā` is explicitly listed as a limb.

```text
prajñā
    discriminative knowing
    clear discernment of the meditative state
```

This is not yet identical with uncontaminated wisdom. It is a factor within the cultivated form-dhyāna field.

### sukha

The third dhyāna retains `sukha`, but not `prīti`.

```text
second dhyāna
    prīti + sukha

third dhyāna
    sukha without prīti
```

The affective tone becomes quieter and more stable.

### asukhāduḥkha

```text
asukha-aduḥkha
    neither pleasure nor pain
```

This is not blankness or unconsciousness. It is a neutral feeling-tone present together with equanimity, mindfulness, and samādhi.

### sthiti and samādhi

The third dhyāna uses `sthiti`, while the fourth explicitly names `samādhi`.

```text
sthiti
    stable abiding

samādhi
    complete collectedness
```

The distinction should be preserved without over-systematizing it at this first pass.

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Third dhyāna: five limbs

| Limb | Function |
|---|---|
| upekṣā | equanimity |
| smṛti | mindfulness |
| prajñā | discriminative understanding |
| sukha | pleasure or ease |
| sthiti | stable collectedness |

The third dhyāna is therefore neither merely pleasant nor merely concentrated. It is a balanced and discerning meditative field.

### 2. Fourth dhyāna: four limbs

| Limb | Function |
|---|---|
| asukhāduḥkha | neutral feeling-tone |
| upekṣā | equanimity |
| smṛti | mindfulness |
| samādhi | collectedness |

The fourth dhyāna removes pleasure while preserving and intensifying stability.

### 3. The transition from third to fourth

```text
third dhyāna
    sukha remains

fourth dhyāna
    sukha ceases
    neutral feeling remains
```

The transition is not the loss of consciousness or structure. It is a purification of the feeling-tone.

## Logical Determination

The four dhyānas now form a determinate sequence of subtraction and replacement.

```text
First
    vitarka
    vicāra
    prīti
    sukha
    samādhi

Second
    prīti
    sukha
    samādhi
    prasāda

Third
    upekṣā
    smṛti
    prajñā
    sukha
    sthiti

Fourth
    asukhāduḥkha
    upekṣā
    smṛti
    samādhi
```

The movement is not a simple numerical decrease. New dominant determinations emerge as earlier ones cease.

```text
cessation of discursive factors
    → inner clarity

cessation of rapture
    → equanimity and discernment

cessation of pleasure
    → neutral feeling and complete stability
```

Thus each higher dhyāna is a qualitative reconstitution of the field.

## Organon Light

The immediate significance is internal to the Kośa's meditative science.

The form-dhyānas do not merely represent increasing intensity. They form a sequence in which the mode of apprehension is progressively purified:

```text
coarse directing and examination
    ↓
inner confidence
    ↓
equanimous discernment
    ↓
neutral and stable collectedness
```

This supports the idea that Rūpa-dhātu is the domain of refined apprehension, but the present kārikā should remain at the level of its own factor-analysis.

The third and fourth dhyānas show that higher form meditation is not richer through accumulation. It is richer through the disciplined removal of instability.
