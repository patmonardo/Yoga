# VAK_8.26 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
ālambete aśaikṣaṃ dvau śūnyataścāpyanityataḥ /
ānimittānimittastu śāntato 'saṃkhyayā kṣayam // VAK_8.26 //
```

## Best Current Translation

Two take an aśaikṣa samādhi as object-support: Emptiness-of-Emptiness under the aspect of emptiness, and Non-Direction-of-Non-Direction under the aspect of impermanence. Signlessness-of-Signlessness, however, takes the non-analytical cessation of an aśaikṣa Signless samādhi as object under the aspect of peace.

See the existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_8.26.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 450|12–450|20.

```text
    ālambete akṣaikṣaṃ dvau śūnyataścāpyanityataḥ /

aśaikṣaṃ samādhiṃ dvāvaparasamādhī ālambete /
śūnyatāśūnyatā aśaikṣaṃ śūnyatāsamādhimālambate śūnyatākāreṇa /
apraṇihitāpraṇihito 'pyaśaikṣamapraṇihitamanityākāreṇa /
na duḥkhato na hetvādito 'nāsravasyātallakṣaṇatvānna mārgākāraiḥ dūṣaṇīyatvāt /

    ānimittānimittastu śāntato'saṃkhyayā kṣayam // VAK_8.26 //

ānimittānimittastu samādhiraśaikṣasyānimittasyāpratisaṃkhyānirodhamālambate /
śāntākāreṇa /
anāsravasya pratisaṃkhayānirodhābhāvāt /
na nirodhapraṇītaniḥsaraṇākārairanityatānirodhasādhāraṇatvādavyākṛtatvā
davisaṃyogācca /
```

### Source Caution

The e-text reads `akṣaikṣaṃ` in the repeated kārikā at 450|12. The Kārikā source and the immediately following prose require `aśaikṣaṃ`; this is treated as an evident e-text corruption, but the source reading is preserved here rather than silently normalized.

The compact kārikā expression `asaṃkhyayā kṣayam` is substantially clarified by the Bhāṣya: the object of `ānimittānimitta` is specifically `apratisaṃkhyānirodha`, not an unspecified "cessation through non-discrimination."

## Natural Argument

VAK 8.25 established the three further samādhis and explained that they receive their names because the corresponding first-order samādhis become their object-supports. VAK 8.26 now determines exactly **which object** and **which aspect** belongs to each.

The first two take an aśaikṣa samādhi directly as object-support:

```text
śūnyatāśūnyatā
    object: aśaikṣa śūnyatā-samādhi
    aspect: śūnyatā

apraṇihitāpraṇihita
    object: aśaikṣa apraṇihita-samādhi
    aspect: anityatā
```

The aspect-selection is restrictive, not arbitrary.

For `apraṇihitāpraṇihita`, Vasubandhu explicitly excludes:

```text
duḥkha-aspect
hetu / samudaya aspects
```

because an uncontaminated (`anāsrava`) samādhi does not possess those determinations.

He also excludes the path-aspects (`mārgākāra`) because the object is here to be depreciated or relinquished (`dūṣaṇīyatva`), whereas the path-aspects would positively characterize it as path.

The third member cannot operate in exactly the same way. `Ānimittānimitta` takes not the aśaikṣa Signless samādhi itself, but its **apratisaṃkhyānirodha** as object-support:

```text
ānimittānimitta
    object: apratisaṃkhyānirodha
            of an aśaikṣa ānimitta-samādhi
    aspect: śānta
```

Why not `pratisaṃkhyānirodha`? Vasubandhu answers directly:

```text
anāsravasya pratisaṃkhyānirodhābhāvāt
```

An uncontaminated dharma does not have analytical cessation in the relevant Abhidharma sense, since `pratisaṃkhyānirodha` is disjunction from contaminated dharmas. Thus the cessation available here is `apratisaṃkhyānirodha`.

Even among the four cessation-aspects, only `śānta` applies. `Nirodha`, `praṇīta`, and `niḥsaraṇa` are excluded because this object is not the liberating disjunction characterized by those determinations: it is impermanence-linked cessation, morally indeterminate (`avyākṛta`), and not disjunction (`avisaṃyoga`).

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya generates a highly constrained second-order structure:

```text
first-order liberated samādhi
    ↓ becomes object-support
second-order samādhi
    ↓ must select an aspect adequate to that object
```

But the three cases are not symmetrical.

```text
A. śūnyatāśūnyatā
   aśaikṣa śūnyatā-samādhi
       ↓ śūnyatākāra
   emptiness applied to Emptiness-samādhi

B. apraṇihitāpraṇihita
   aśaikṣa apraṇihita-samādhi
       ↓ anityākāra
   impermanence applied to Non-directed samādhi

C. ānimittānimitta
   aśaikṣa ānimitta-samādhi
       ↓ its apratisaṃkhyānirodha
       ↓ śāntākāra
   peace applied to the non-analytical cessation
   of Signless samādhi
```

The third case therefore reflects not simply upon a prior samādhi but upon the determinate mode in which that samādhi is absent.

## New Technical Terms

- **dūṣaṇīyatva** — being fit to be depreciated, criticized, or relinquished.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative term in this local unit.
  - **function:** explains why the positive path-aspects are excluded from `apraṇihitāpraṇihita`.
- **apratisaṃkhyānirodha** — non-analytical cessation/non-arising through deficiency of conditions.
  - **status:** foundational term from Chapter 1, newly operative here in the samādhi analysis.
  - **function:** supplies the object of `ānimittānimitta` where `pratisaṃkhyānirodha` cannot apply.
- **atallakṣaṇatva** — not having those characteristics.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory formulation.
  - **function:** excludes suffering and causal aspects from an uncontaminated samādhi.
- **avisaṃyoga** — absence of disjunction.
  - **status:** doctrinally familiar relation, locally important here.
  - **function:** explains why `niḥsaraṇa` and related cessation-aspects do not characterize the relevant `apratisaṃkhyānirodha`.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — direct doctrinal exposition of the object-support and permissible aspect of each further samādhi.

No explicit opponent appears in this unit.

## Doctrinal Determination

The further samādhis are not merely repetitions of the first triad at a higher intensity. They are second-order discriminations whose object-supports and aspects are rigorously controlled by the ontological status of what they determine.

Especially important is the difference between the two kinds of cessation:

```text
pratisaṃkhyānirodha
    disjunction from contaminated dharmas
    attained through discriminative knowing

apratisaṃkhyānirodha
    non-arising through deficiency of conditions
```

Because an aśaikṣa ānimitta-samādhi is uncontaminated, its absence cannot be treated as liberating disjunction from a contaminant. Hence `ānimittānimitta` must take its `apratisaṃkhyānirodha`, not `pratisaṃkhyānirodha`, as object.

This prevents the reflexive samādhi from falsely converting the disappearance of an uncontaminated path-state into nirvāṇa itself.

## Translation Consequences

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass translation rendered the last pāda approximately as:

> Signlessness-of-Signlessness takes cessation through non-discrimination as object under peace.

The Bhāṣya establishes the more exact determination:

> Signlessness-of-Signlessness takes the **non-analytical cessation (`apratisaṃkhyānirodha`) of an aśaikṣa Signless samādhi** as object, under the aspect of peace.

Likewise the opening should make explicit that the two object-supports are not generic "aśaikṣa Recognition" but specifically the corresponding perfected samādhis.

A tightened translation is therefore:

> Two take an aśaikṣa samādhi as object-support: Emptiness-of-Emptiness takes the perfected Emptiness samādhi under the aspect of emptiness, and Non-Direction-of-Non-Direction takes the perfected Non-Directed samādhi under the aspect of impermanence. Signlessness-of-Signlessness, however, takes the non-analytical cessation of the perfected Signless samādhi as object under the aspect of peace.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.26 gives the strongest evidence yet that the second triad is genuinely reflexive but not self-reifying.

The prior samādhi changes logical role:

```text
first movement
    samādhi = determining act

second movement
    attained samādhi = determined object-support
```

Yet the second movement does not simply assert the negation of the first. The Bhāṣya asks what predicates are actually valid of the new object.

Thus reflexivity is controlled by **adequacy of aspect to object**:

```text
object-status
    determines
admissible aspect
```

This matters particularly in the third case. The disappearance of an uncontaminated samādhi cannot be represented under the determinations proper to liberating cessation. The text therefore distinguishes:

```text
cessation of contamination
    ≠
absence of an uncontaminated state
```

That is a precise logical safeguard against turning the negation of a path-state into the Absolute result.

## Light Organon Contact

The generated relation is more interesting than a simple "Recognition recognizing Recognition" formula.

The Bhāṣya shows:

```text
Determination A
    becomes object for Determination B
        but
B may predicate of A only what A's actual status permits.
```

Reflection is therefore not unrestricted self-application. It is **reciprocal determination**: the higher reflective act determines the prior act, while the prior act's determinate nature constrains what the reflection may truthfully say of it.

That is exactly the genetic continuity worth preserving. The second-order act does not abolish its first-order content or convert it into a static identity; the prior determination survives as the governing content of its own reflection.
