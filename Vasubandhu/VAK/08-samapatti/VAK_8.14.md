# VAkK 8.14

## Sanskrit

अतद्वान् लभते शुद्धं वैराग्येणोपपत्तितः ।
अनास्रवं तु वैराग्यात् क्लिष्टं हान्युपपत्तितः ॥ VAkK 8.14 ॥

## IAST

atadvān labhate śuddhaṃ vairāgyeṇopapattitaḥ /
anāsravaṃ tu vairāgyāt kliṣṭaṃ hānyupapattitaḥ // VAkK_8.14 //

## Source Caution

The transmitted compound **atadvān** is compact and should be read cautiously. In context, it indicates one who does not yet possess the relevant attainment. The verse classifies the distinct modes by which pure, uncontaminated, and defiled attainments are acquired.

## Grammar

- **atadvān** — nominative singular masculine; one not possessing that, one without the relevant attainment
- **labhate** — third-person singular middle; obtains, acquires
- **śuddham** — accusative singular neuter; the pure attainment
- **vairāgyeṇa** — instrumental singular; through detachment or dispassion
- **upapattitaḥ** — ablatival adverb; through rebirth, by reason of birth into the relevant sphere
- **anāsravam** — accusative singular neuter; the uncontaminated attainment
- **tu** — but, however
- **vairāgyāt** — ablative singular; from detachment
- **kliṣṭam** — accusative singular neuter; the defiled attainment
- **hāni-upapattitaḥ** — through decline and through rebirth

## Literal Translation

One who does not possess it acquires the pure attainment through detachment or through rebirth. But the uncontaminated attainment is acquired through detachment; the defiled attainment is acquired through decline or through rebirth.

## Philosophical Translation

The same meditative level may be acquired under different qualitative determinations. A pure attainment may arise either through cultivated detachment or through birth into its corresponding sphere. The uncontaminated mode cannot be inherited merely by location or birth: it must arise through liberating detachment. The defiled mode appears through decline from a higher condition or by birth within a conditioned sphere.

## Technical Vocabulary

- **śuddha** — pure; wholesome but still worldly
- **anāsrava** — uncontaminated; supramundane and free from outflows
- **kliṣṭa** — defiled; connected with affliction
- **vairāgya** — detachment, dispassion
- **upapatti** — rebirth, arising in a corresponding sphere
- **hāni** — decline, loss, falling away from a superior attainment

## Doctrinal Determination

This verse distinguishes meditative level from mode of acquisition. Birth into a sphere may provide access to its pure or defiled configuration, but rebirth alone does not produce an uncontaminated attainment. The uncontaminated requires a liberating causal transformation, here named vairāgya.

The verse therefore blocks any simple identification of spiritual rank with cosmological location. One may inhabit a higher realm without possessing the uncontaminated mode of its attainment.

## Logical Determination

The classification is modal and causal:

- pure attainment: detachment or rebirth
- uncontaminated attainment: detachment only
- defiled attainment: decline or rebirth

Thus identical level does not imply identical genesis, and identical genesis does not imply identical purity.

## Organon Light

A stage is not defined merely by where consciousness is located. It must also be determined by the manner in which the stage has been acquired and by the quality governing it.

This is a logic of staged purity rather than a quantitative ladder. The same formal level can be worldly-pure, uncontaminated, or defiled. The decisive determination lies in the causal and qualitative constitution of the attained whole.
