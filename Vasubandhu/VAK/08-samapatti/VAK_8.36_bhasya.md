# VAK_8.36_bhasya

## Kārikā

**IAST**

`daśa kṛtsnāni alobhāṣṭau dhyāne 'ntye gocaraḥ punaḥ /`
`kāmāḥ dve śuddhākārūpye svacatuḥskandhagocare // VAK_8.36 //`

**Best Current Translation**

There are ten totalities. The first eight are constituted by non-greed and belong to the final Dhyāna; their object-field is again the desire realm. The remaining two are pure formless attainments and have as their object the four aggregates of their own level.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu introduces ten **kṛtsnāyatanas**, totality-spheres, and explains the name by `nirantarakṛtsnaspharaṇa`: uninterrupted complete pervasion. The ten are earth, water, fire, wind, blue, yellow, red, white, infinite space, and infinite knowledge.

The first eight are constituted by **alobha**, non-greed. They belong exclusively to the fourth Dhyāna. Their ordinary object-support is visible form belonging to the desire realm. Vasubandhu records an alternative opinion for the wind-totality: some take its support to be the tangible sphere rather than visible form.

The final two totalities are pure formless attainments. Their respective object-support is the four aggregates belonging to their own level.

The commentary then gives the structural relation among the three practice-complexes just discussed. The mastery-spheres are entrances into the Deliverances' further development, and the totality-spheres are entered through the mastery-spheres. The reason is explicit: each subsequent practice is superior to the previous one (`uttarottaraviśiṣṭatvāt`).

Finally, all these practices from the Deliverances onward can occur in the continuities of both ordinary persons and noble persons, with the exception of the cessation-Deliverance.

## Argument Reconstruction

1. A `kṛtsna` is defined not merely by size but by uninterrupted complete pervasion of the selected aspect.
2. Ten such totalities are distinguished:
   - earth,
   - water,
   - fire,
   - wind,
   - blue,
   - yellow,
   - red,
   - white,
   - infinite space,
   - infinite knowledge.
3. The first eight are `alobha` in nature.
4. They belong to the fourth Dhyāna.
5. Their normal support is desire-realm visible form, with an alternative view assigning tangible form to the wind-totality.
6. The last two are pure formless attainments.
7. Their support consists of the four aggregates of their own level.
8. The three complexes form a graded practical sequence:

```text
vimokṣa
    → abhibhvāyatana
        → kṛtsnāyatana
```

9. This ordering is genetic, because each later stage is described as superior to the previous one.
10. Except for cessation-Deliverance, these practices may belong to either ordinary or noble continuities.

## New Technical Terms

- **kṛtsnāyatana** — totality-sphere; a practice in which a selected aspect pervades the field without interruption.
  - **status:** introduced here as a new practice-complex.
  - **function:** establishes totalized presentation after mastery of an object-field.
- **nirantarakṛtsnaspharaṇa** — uninterrupted complete pervasion.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory compound in this passage.
  - **function:** gives the reason for the name `kṛtsna`.
- **prāveśika** — serving as an entrance or access to a subsequent practice.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** establishes the genetic ordering among Deliverances, mastery-spheres, and totality-spheres.
- **uttarottaraviśiṣṭatva** — successive superiority of each later stage.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory formulation here.
  - **function:** justifies the graded practical sequence.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu — principal exposition and graded ordering.

**Voice:** alternative authority (`eke`) — wind-totality takes the tangible sphere as object-support.

## Doctrinal Determination

The ten totalities are not simply ten large meditation objects. Their defining feature is complete and uninterrupted pervasion by a selected determination.

The first eight remain connected with form and are supported by non-greed. Their placement in the fourth Dhyāna matters: the totalization of the visible field is performed from a highly stabilized meditative basis.

The two formless totalities differ structurally. They are pure formless attainments and take the four aggregates of their own respective level as support.

Most importantly, Vasubandhu explicitly links the three practice systems genetically:

- `vimokṣa` provides entry into `abhibhvāyatana`;
- `abhibhvāyatana` provides entry into `kṛtsnāyatana`.

This is therefore a training progression, not a loose aggregation of lists.

## Translation Consequences

**kṛtsna** should retain the sense of **totality** rather than being flattened into merely “whole” or “universal.” The Bhāṣya's `nirantarakṛtsnaspharaṇa` shows that totality here is an enacted pervasion of the entire meditative field.

**āyatana** remains **sphere** in the project vocabulary. `kṛtsnāyatana` is therefore best rendered **totality-sphere** when the full compound matters.

**vijñānānantyāyatana** should follow the project convention that `vijñāna` is **knowledge**, hence the totality associated with the sphere of infinite knowledge rather than “infinite consciousness.”

## Logical Determination

The progression from Deliverance through mastery to totality can now be stated with textual support:

```text
vimokṣa
    freedom from domination by a field

abhibhvāyatana
    mastery of the object-support

kṛtsnāyatana
    uninterrupted total pervasion of the field
```

The crucial point is that totality does not abolish determination. One determination is intentionally made coextensive with the field.

This is therefore not abstraction from all difference. It is a controlled transformation of the scope of a selected determination.

The text also prevents us from identifying this practical universality directly with liberative knowledge. Except for cessation-Deliverance, these practices may belong to both ordinary persons and noble persons. Their technical perfection and their soteriological status are distinct dimensions.

## Light Organon Contact

This passage is especially useful for a later theory of field regulation, but the Bhāṣya itself gives the governing structure.

The sequence is not primarily epistemic extraction from a field. It is training in the relation between cognition and its object-support:

```text
release
    → mastery
        → totalized presentation
```

A `kṛtsna` demonstrates that a field can be systematically reorganized under one selected determination without that determination thereby becoming a constitutive Principle of Knowledge.

The important restraint is supplied by Vasubandhu himself: these highly developed practices can belong to ordinary persons as well as noble ones. Mastery of appearance therefore cannot simply be equated with liberative cognition.
