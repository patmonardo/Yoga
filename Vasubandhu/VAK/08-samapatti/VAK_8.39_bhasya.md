# VAK_8.39_bhasya

## Kārikā

### IAST

`saddharmo dvividhaḥ śāstur āgamādhigamātmakaḥ /`
`dhātāras tasya vaktāraḥ pratipattāra eva ca //`

### Best Current Translation

The Teacher's True Dharma is twofold, consisting of transmitted teaching and realization. Its bearers are those who preserve and expound it, and those who put it into practice and realize it.

Kārikā anchor: `VAK_8.39.md`

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 459|07–459|14:

```text
kiyacciraṃ punarayaṃ saddharmaḥ sthāsyati /

saddharmo dvividhaḥ śāsturāgamādhigamātmakaḥ /

dhātārastasya vaktāraḥ pratipattāra eva ca // VAkK_8.39 //

āgamasya hi dhārayitāro vaktāraḥ /
adhigamasya pratipattāraḥ /
ato yāvadete sthāsyanti tāvatsaddharma iti veditavyam /
teṣāṃ tu varṣasahasramavasthānamāhuḥ /
adhigamasyaivam /
āgamasya tu bhūyāṃsaṃ kālamityapare /
```

## Bhāṣya Translation

How long, then, will this True Dharma remain—the Dharma in which such varieties of dharmas as these are discerned?

The Teacher's True Dharma is twofold: it consists of transmission and realization.

Here, transmission is the Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma; realization is the factors belonging to awakening. Such is the twofold True Dharma.

Its bearers are those who preserve and speak it, and those who put it into practice.

For the preservers and speakers are the bearers of the transmitted teaching, while practitioners are the bearers of realization. Therefore, for as long as these remain, the True Dharma is to be understood as remaining.

Some say that these remain for one thousand years—this applies to realization. Others hold that the transmitted teaching remains for a longer time.

## Natural Argument

The closing movement begins with a question of duration: how long can a teaching of this complexity remain alive?

Vasubandhu refuses to identify the Dharma with a text-corpus alone. `Saddharma` has two constitutive modes:

1. **āgama** — the teaching as transmitted, preserved, and articulated: Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma;
2. **adhigama** — the teaching as actually realized: the factors conducive to awakening (`bodhipakṣya`).

These two modes require corresponding bearers. Transmission persists through those who retain and expound it; realization persists through practitioners. The existence of the True Dharma is therefore indexed to living functions, not merely to the survival of manuscripts or propositions.

The chronological remark is secondary to this structural definition. Different traditions may assign different durations, but the criterion itself is clear: the Dharma remains as long as transmission and realization remain embodied.

The teaching remains fully alive only where its articulated form and realized practice continue together.

## New Technical Terms

- **saddharma** — the authentic Dharma of the Teacher in its transmitted and realized modes.
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā; here formally defined by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** names the teaching whose historical persistence is under discussion.

- **āgama** — transmitted teaching; here explicitly Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma.
  - **status:** major technical term, here specified extensionally.
  - **function:** objective/transmissible mode of Saddharma.

- **adhigama** — realization or achieved acquisition of the teaching.
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā; Bhāṣya glosses it through the `bodhipakṣya` dharmas.
  - **function:** realized/practical mode of Saddharma.

- **bodhipakṣya** — dharmas belonging to or conducive to awakening.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification of `adhigama`.
  - **function:** gives the realized content of the second mode of Saddharma.

- **dhārayitṛ / vaktṛ** — preserver and speaker.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanation of the Kārikā's `dhātāraḥ`.
  - **function:** bearer-functions of `āgama`.

- **pratipattṛ** — practitioner, one who undertakes and realizes the teaching.
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā.
  - **function:** bearer of `adhigama`.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting a received Abhidharma classification and reporting variant chronological opinions.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a strict twofold definition of the True Dharma:

```text
Saddharma = Āgama + Adhigama
```

The two are irreducible. `Āgama` without realization preserves articulation but not the achieved truth of the path. `Adhigama` without living transmission would lack the publicly articulated form by which the teaching is preserved and taught.

The closing question of decline is therefore not first a question about institutional continuity. It is a question about whether these two functions still have living bearers.

## Translation Consequences

- `āgama` should here be translated more concretely than merely “tradition”: the Bhāṣya explicitly identifies it with Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma.
- `adhigama` is not generic “understanding.” It is realized acquisition, here specified through the `bodhipakṣya` dharmas.
- `dhātāraḥ` should be rendered in a way that preserves the active sense of bearers/preservers rather than passive possession.
- `pratipattāraḥ` means practitioners or those who undertake realization, not merely believers or adherents.

## Logical Determination

The verse closes the technical body of Chapter 8 by giving a theory of **system persistence**.

A body of knowledge persists only if both its articulated form and its realized competence continue to be reproduced:

```text
articulated transmission
        +
realized competence
        ↓
continuity of the living system
```

This is not merely a historical claim. It gives a structural criterion for when a teaching is still operative as a teaching.

This is a useful restraint for the later Organon comparison. A rational system cannot be identified only with its corpus, ontology, or formal specification. Nor can it be reduced to an unarticulated private realization.

The text itself requires both:
