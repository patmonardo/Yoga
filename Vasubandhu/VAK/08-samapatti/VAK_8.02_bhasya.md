# VAK_8.02 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> vicāraprītisukhavat pūrvapūrvāṅgavarjitam /
> tathārūpyāś catuḥskandhā adhobhūmivivekajāḥ // VAK_8.02 //

**Best current translation:**  
The first dhyāna possesses vicāra, joy, and ease; each of the remaining dhyānas is deprived successively of factors belonging to the preceding stage. Likewise, the formless attainments consist of four aggregates and arise through separation from the lower ground.

## Source Caution

The working e-text is based principally on Pradhan with corrections from Shastri and contains minor orthographic defects. The important doctrinal sequence is secure.

The Kārikā's compact `vicāraprītisukhavat` does not mean that every dhyāna possesses all three factors. The Bhāṣya explicitly distributes them across the sequence and additionally states that `vitarka` is implied together with `vicāra` in the first dhyāna.

Likewise, `viveka` here should not be imported wholesale from Yoga as discriminative knowledge. Vasubandhu defines the local sense himself: it is the path by which one is released from the lower level through proceeding to dispassion (`vairāgyagamana`).

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu first asks what kind of wholesome one-pointedness constitutes each of the four dhyānas.

The first dhyāna is wholesome one-pointedness associated with `vicāra`, `prīti`, and `sukha`. Although `vitarka` is not named in the pāda, Vasubandhu says it is included through the mention of `vicāra`, because in this configuration vicāra does not occur without vitarka. He illustrates their concomitance by the relation of smoke and fire.

The remaining three dhyānas are understood under the continuing predicate `śubhaikāgrya`, wholesome one-pointedness, but are progressively deprived of prior factors:

```text
first dhyāna
    vitarka + vicāra + prīti + sukha

second dhyāna
    prīti + sukha
    without vicāra (and therefore without vitarka)

third dhyāna
    sukha
    without vicāra and prīti

fourth dhyāna
    without vicāra, prīti, and sukha
```

The point is not that the later dhyānas cease to be one-pointed. `śubhaikāgrya` continues throughout. What changes is the associated factor-structure.

Vasubandhu then says the four formless attainments are analogous to the dhyānas in two respects. They too are considered both as meditative attainments (`samāpatti`) and as corresponding rebirths (`upapatti`), and as attainments they too have wholesome one-pointedness as their defining nature.

Their associated constitution, however, is four aggregates rather than five because there is no rūpa accompanying them.

Each formless attainment is generated through `viveka` from the lower ground:

```text
fourth dhyāna
    ↓ viveka
ākāśānantyāyatana
    ↓ viveka
vijñānānantyāyatana
    ↓ viveka
ākiñcanyāyatana
    ↓ viveka
naivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñāyatana
```

Vasubandhu then asks directly what `viveka` means. His answer is operational rather than abstract: it is **that path by which one is released from what lies below**, through proceeding into dispassion.

The remainder of the unit answers an objection: perhaps the formless domain contains a very subtle kind of form and therefore should not literally be called formless.

**Voice: Objector / alternative position**

The objector attempts to establish subtle rūpa in the formless states by appealing to doctrines concerning bodily and verbal restraint, the mutual dependence of name-and-form, the relation between life and heat, and canonical statements about vijñāna not proceeding independently of the remaining aggregates.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu rejects these arguments by showing that their scriptural formulations cannot simply be universalized without qualification. If every apparently unrestricted statement were applied to every domain, absurd consequences would follow.

He then adduces positive scriptural evidence describing the formless attainments as departure from form, transcendence of forms, and states of formless beings who have completely passed beyond perceptions of form.

His final textual argument is especially clean. In descriptions of dhyāna, the Buddha speaks of whatever belongs there beginning with `rūpagata` and continuing through `vijñānagata`. In descriptions of the formless attainments, the enumeration begins instead with `vedanāgata` and continues through `vijñānagata`. If rūpa existed there, Vasubandhu argues, there would be no reason to omit it.

The conclusion is explicit:

> `nārupye rūpasadbhāvaḥ` — there is no existence of rūpa in the formless domain.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
samāpatti
    remains kuśala-ekāgratā
        ↓
but its associated factor-structure changes by level

first dhyāna
    vitarka + vicāra + prīti + sukha
        ↓ remove prior factors
second dhyāna
    prīti + sukha
        ↓ remove prīti
third dhyāna
    sukha
        ↓ remove sukha
fourth dhyāna
    higher one-pointed configuration
```

The same formal principle is then extended beyond the rūpa-dhyānas:

```text
lower bhūmi
    ↓ viveka / release through dispassion
higher ārūpya
```

And the aggregate constitution changes categorically:

```text
dhyāna-samāpatti
    five aggregates

ārūpya-samāpatti
    four aggregates
    rūpa absent
```

## New Technical Terms

- **pūrvapūrvāṅgavarjita** — deprived successively of constituent factors belonging to the preceding stage
  - **status:** Kārikā compound clarified decisively by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** gives the genetic rule governing progression through the four dhyānas

- **sāhacarya** — concomitance; occurring together
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary in this passage
  - **function:** explains why `vitarka` is understood when `vicāra` is stated for the first dhyāna

- **viveka** — release or separation from a lower ground by the path leading into dispassion
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly defined by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** genetic condition for emergence of each higher formless attainment

- **vairāgyagamana** — proceeding into dispassion / going toward freedom from attachment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** specifies how `viveka` from the lower ground is effected

- **anuparivartirūpa** — rūpa that accompanies or follows along with a state
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation
  - **function:** explains why the formless attainment has only four aggregates

- **rūpagata / vedanāgata / vijñānagata** — what belongs to or falls under rūpa, feeling, knowledge, etc.
  - **status:** scriptural terminology cited in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** supplies Vasubandhu's final textual proof that rūpa is absent in ārūpya

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.02 confirms and sharpens the definition of samāpatti given at 8.01.

The defining nature does not change:

```text
samāpatti
    = kuśala-ekāgratā
```

But one-pointedness is not an internally featureless state. It can be instantiated in distinct configurations differentiated by their associated factors.

This yields an important distinction:

```text
identity of defining nature
    kuśala-ekāgratā

≠

identity of concrete constitution
    vitarka / vicāra / prīti / sukha etc.
```

The meditative states therefore form a structured species-system under one common determination.

The second doctrinal result is equally important. `Viveka` is productive in a precise relational sense:

```text
higher attainment
    is generated through release
    from a determinate lower ground
```

The higher is therefore neither simply added to the lower nor merely numerically subsequent to it. Its possibility is defined through the overcoming of the lower level.

Finally, the Bhāṣya insists that the difference between rūpa and ārūpya is not merely quantitative subtlety. The transition to ārūpya changes the aggregate constitution itself:

```text
five-skandha configuration
    →
four-skandha configuration
```

The rūpa aggregate is genuinely absent.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation should be made more explicit in two respects.

First, `pūrvapūrvāṅgavarjitam` describes progressive subtraction of the associated dhyāna factors while `śubhaikāgrya` remains understood throughout.

Second, `adhobhūmivivekajāḥ` is best rendered here not merely as "born from discrimination from the lower ground" but as:

> **arising through separation or release from the lower ground**

because Vasubandhu himself glosses `viveka` as the path by which one is released from below through proceeding into dispassion.

A Bhāṣya-controlled rendering is therefore:

> The first dhyāna is wholesome one-pointedness associated with vitarka, vicāra, joy, and ease; the remaining three are wholesome one-pointedness successively deprived of the preceding factors. The formless attainments are analogous: as attainments they are wholesome one-pointedness, but with their accompaniments they consist of four aggregates, and each arises through release from the lower ground.

## Logical Determination

The verse supplies the first explicit transformation law of Chapter 8:

```text
common determination
    ekāgratā

concrete configuration A
    ekāgratā + factor-set A

transition
    determinate relinquishment

concrete configuration B
    ekāgratā + reduced factor-set B
```

Thus the identity of the meditative genus is preserved while the constitutive feature-set changes.

This is stronger than a simple ladder:

```text
higher ≠ more predicates
higher = more determinate through release
```

The formless series gives the same structure at the level of grounds:

```text
Ground_n
    ↓ viveka
Ground_n+1
```

where the transition is defined by liberation from the lower domain rather than by an arbitrary change of object.

The categorical removal of rūpa also shows that `viveka` can alter not merely the value of a feature but the ontology of the instantiated configuration:

```text
five-aggregate state
    ↓ release beyond rūpa
four-aggregate state
```

## Light Organon Contact

This is compatible with the emerging Judgment interpretation, but the text itself gives us a useful restraint.

If `ekāgratā` is compared with Judgment, then Judgment cannot mean a bare act of predication detached from its field. VAK 8.02 makes the one-pointed operation invariant while its determinate factors and admissible domain change.

A controlled Organon rendering would therefore be:

```text
Judging operation
    invariant orientation to one object-support

Concrete judgment-state
    operation
    + associated determining factors
    + level / bhūmi
    + admissible aggregate constitution
```

This also clarifies why an immediate equation

```text
samāpatti = third eye
```

would be premature. The Kośa is constructing something more formal: an ordered field in which cognition becomes capable of increasingly purified configurations through lawful release from lower determining conditions.

The Yoga comparison may become very strong later, especially if Yoga's `viveka`, `prajñā`, and samāpatti-series explicitly supply the rational determination of this field. But VAK 8.02 itself licenses only the more modest claim:

> **Samāpatti is structurally differentiated one-pointed knowing whose higher configurations arise through determinate release from lower conditions.**

That is already enough to make the relation to Judgment and Pure Reason philosophically serious without collapsing the Buddhist and Yoga systems into one another.
