# VAK_8.17 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> caturdhā śuddhakaṃ hānabhāgīyādi yathākramam /
> kleśotpatti-svabhūmy-ūrdhvānāsravānuguṇaṃ hi tat // VAK_8.17 //

**Best translation:**  
Pure attainment is fourfold, beginning with that conducive to decline. Respectively, it is conducive to the arising of defilements, to its own level, to a higher level, and to the uncontaminated.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.17.md`](./VAK_8.17.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

**Reference:** Pradhan 445|04–445|10.

```text
caturdhā śuddhakaṃ hānabhāgīyādi

hānabhāgīyaṃ viśeṣabhāgīyaṃ sthitibhāgīyaṃ nirvedhabhāgīyamiti caturvidhaṃ śuddhakam /

bhavāgraṃ tu trividhamanyatra viśeṣabhāgīyāt /
kimasya lakṣaṇam /

yathākramam /
kleśotpattisvabhūmyūrdhvānāsravānuguṇaṃ hi tat // VAkK_8.17 //

kleśotpattyanuguṇaṃ hānabhāgīyaṃ svabhūmyanuguṇaṃ sthitibhāgīyamūrdhvabhūmyanuguṇaṃ viśeṣabhāgīyamanāsravānuguṇaṃ nirvedhabhāgīyam /

tasmādanāsravamutpadyate /
```

## Source Caution

The prose at 445|05 enumerates the four names in the sequence:

```text
hānabhāgīya
viśeṣabhāgīya
sthitibhāgīya
nirvedhabhāgīya
```

but the following functional mapping at 445|09–10 gives the expected order:

```text
hānabhāgīya      → kleśotpatti
sthitibhāgīya    → svabhūmi
viśeṣabhāgīya    → ūrdhvabhūmi
nirvedhabhāgīya  → anāsrava
```

The source wording is therefore preserved rather than silently normalized. The functional gloss governs the doctrinal interpretation.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu now subdivides the previously defined `śuddhaka` samāpatti. “Pure” is not a single developmental condition. It has four distinct orientations.

The first question is simple: what are the four?

```text
hānabhāgīya
sthitibhāgīya
viśeṣabhāgīya
nirvedhabhāgīya
```

He then inserts one restriction:

```text
bhavāgra
    has only three
    because viśeṣabhāgīya is absent
```

There is no still higher bhūmi toward which the summit of existence could be oriented.

The next question is decisive:

```text
kim asya lakṣaṇam?
what is the mark of each?
```

The answer does not define the four by static intrinsic descriptions. It defines each through what it is **conducive toward** (`anuguṇa`).

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Hānabhāgīya

```text
kleśotpatti-anuguṇa
    conducive to the arising of defilements
```

This pure attainment tends toward decline. It is pure in its present condition, but its causal orientation is toward renewed kleśa.

### 2. Sthitibhāgīya

```text
svabhūmi-anuguṇa
    conducive to its own level
```

This tends toward maintenance of the present bhūmi rather than ascent or liberation.

### 3. Viśeṣabhāgīya

```text
ūrdhvabhūmi-anuguṇa
    conducive to a higher level
```

This is the pure attainment whose developmental tendency is upward differentiation or superiority.

The summit of existence lacks this member because no higher conditioned bhūmi remains.

### 4. Nirvedhabhāgīya

```text
anāsrava-anuguṇa
    conducive to the uncontaminated
```

This is the pure attainment oriented toward supramundane breakthrough.

Vasubandhu makes the causal consequence explicit:

```text
tasmād anāsravam utpadyate
from that, the uncontaminated arises
```

So `nirvedhabhāgīya` is not merely associated conceptually with the uncontaminated. It is the worldly-pure condition from which the uncontaminated attainment can arise.

## New Technical Terms

- **hānabhāgīya** — belonging to or conducive to decline
  - **status:** introduced as a technical subdivision of `śuddhaka`
  - **function:** identifies pure attainment oriented toward renewed defilement

- **sthitibhāgīya** — belonging to or conducive to stability
  - **status:** introduced as a technical subdivision of `śuddhaka`
  - **function:** identifies pure attainment oriented toward persistence on its own level

- **viśeṣabhāgīya** — belonging to or conducive to distinction
  - **status:** introduced as a technical subdivision of `śuddhaka`
  - **function:** identifies pure attainment oriented toward a higher bhūmi

- **nirvedhabhāgīya** — belonging to or conducive to penetration
  - **status:** previously encountered elsewhere in the Kośa; newly operative here as a samāpatti-class
  - **function:** identifies pure attainment oriented toward the uncontaminated

- **anuguṇa** — favorable to, tending toward, conducive to
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-operational term in this passage
  - **function:** gives the defining relation of all four subdivisions

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive result is that `śuddhaka` does not name one homogeneous meditative condition.

```text
śuddhaka
    ├── hānabhāgīya
    │      → kleśa
    │
    ├── sthitibhāgīya
    │      → same bhūmi
    │
    ├── viśeṣabhāgīya
    │      → higher bhūmi
    │
    └── nirvedhabhāgīya
           → anāsrava
```

Thus two attainments may both be presently pure while differing radically in what they are capable of generating next.

This also sharpens VAK 8.15–8.16. Those verses gave external succession rules among attained states; VAK 8.17 now distinguishes **internal developmental tendencies within the pure class itself**.

The exception of `bhavāgra` is structurally exact:

```text
bhavāgra
    no higher conditioned bhūmi
    therefore no viśeṣabhāgīya
```

But it may still have decline-, stability-, and penetration-conducive pure forms.

## Translation Consequences

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā translation is basically correct. The Bhāṣya strongly supports translating `anuguṇa` as “conducive to” or “favorable to,” rather than merely “corresponding to.” The four classes are causal-developmental orientations.

`Nirvedhabhāgīya` should continue to be rendered provisionally as “conducive to penetration,” with the important local clarification that here its defining result is the arising of `anāsrava`.

## Logical Determination

This verse distinguishes a state from its **vector**.

```text
present determination
    śuddhaka

future-directed tendency
    hāna / sthiti / viśeṣa / nirvedha
```

The same genus therefore contains four internally different powers:

```text
regression
maintenance
ascent
breakthrough
```

This is stronger than saying that four different results sometimes happen after pure attainment. Vasubandhu defines each subtype through its lawful orientation toward such a result.

So the truth of a pure attainment is not exhausted by what it presently is:

```text
what it is now
    +
what it is disposed to generate
```

Both belong to its determination.

## Light Organon Contact

The immediate contact point is modest but powerful: a determination can be classified by its **immanent transition-potential**.

```text
same present genus: śuddhaka

contrary developmental meanings:
    fall
    persist
    rise
    break through
```

That is exactly why “pure” cannot be treated as a final spiritual judgment. The Kośa demands specification of the direction internal to the state.

No larger Systems reconstruction is needed here. Vasubandhu has already supplied the essential form: a state is known partly by the transformation for which it is fit.
