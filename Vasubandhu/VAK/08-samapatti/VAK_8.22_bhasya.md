# VAK_8.22 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
aṣṭau sāmantakāny eṣāṃ śuddhāduḥkhāsukhāni hi /
āryaṃ cādyaṃ tridhā kecid atarkaṃ dhyānam antaram // VAK_8.22 //
```

## Best Current Translation

There are eight neighboring attainments belonging to these. They are pure and accompanied by neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. The first is also noble. Some hold [the first] to be threefold. The intermediate dhyāna is without vitarka.

See the established Kārikā analysis in `VAK_8.22.md`.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The preceding verse established that neighboring attainments (`sāmantaka`) can function as antidotal paths against the lower ground. Vasubandhu now asks how many such neighboring attainments there are.

There are eight: one neighboring attainment corresponding to each of the eight principal (`maula`) attainments. Their function is expressed genetically: by each neighboring attainment there is entry into its corresponding principal attainment.

The next question is whether these neighboring attainments have the same threefold classification as the principal attainments—savoring-associated, pure, and uncontaminated—and whether their feeling-tone is likewise distributed in the same way.

Vasubandhu answers no.

The neighboring attainments are pure and associated with the faculty of equanimity (`upekṣendriya`). They are not ordinarily savoring-associated. The prose grounds this in their transitional function: they stand at the boundary toward another level, the disturbance produced by the lower ground has not yet simply vanished into settled enjoyment, and above all they are `vairāgyamārga`, paths of dispassion. Their very function opposes savoring.

The first neighboring attainment is exceptional. This is `anāgamya`, the preliminary attainment before the first dhyāna. It can be both pure and uncontaminated.

Vasubandhu then adds an important qualification. A consciousness belonging to a neighboring attainment can be defiled when it functions in rebirth-linking (`pratisaṃdhibandha`), but this does not establish a defiled meditative attainment: defiledness is denied of the collected (`samāhita`) state itself.

**Voice: Other teachers**

Some nevertheless hold that `anāgamya` can also be savoring-associated, thereby making it threefold.

The discussion then distinguishes `sāmantaka` from `dhyānāntara`, the intermediate dhyāna. They are not synonymous. A sāmantaka is a path of dispassion; `dhyānāntara` is a distinct variety of dhyāna characterized by the absence of vitarka.

The detailed threefold constitution and feeling-tone of `dhyānāntara` continue into VAK 8.23 and should not be pulled backward into the present unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **anāgamya** — the first neighboring/preparatory attainment before the first dhyāna.  
  **status:** previously encountered indirectly; newly decisive here.  
  **function:** exceptional first sāmantaka capable of pure and uncontaminated forms.

- **upekṣendriya** — faculty of equanimity / neutral feeling.  
  **status:** previously encountered.  
  **function:** feeling-factor accompanying the neighboring attainments.

- **vairāgyamārga** — path of dispassion.  
  **status:** important Bhāṣya formulation.  
  **function:** explains why sāmantaka is transitional and not ordinarily savoring-associated.

- **pratisaṃdhibandha** — rebirth-linking, binding to a new existence.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya technical expression.  
  **function:** distinguishes a defiled rebirth-linking use of neighboring-level cognition from a defiled meditative attainment.

- **dhyānāntara** — intermediate dhyāna.  
  **status:** newly introduced here.  
  **function:** distinct dhyāna variety defined by absence of vitarka; completed in the following verse.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.22 prevents the `sāmantaka` from being treated as merely a weaker copy of its principal attainment.

Its identity comes from function:

```text
principal attainment
    attained configuration

sāmantaka
    entry-path into that configuration
```

Because the sāmantaka is a path of dispassion, its ordinary constitution cannot simply reproduce the savoring-mode available in principal attainment.

This gives a precise distinction between **state-type** and **transition-type**.

The same chapter now contains both:

```text
maula
    what the established attainment is

sāmantaka
    how entry into it is effected
```

The first sāmantaka, `anāgamya`, is exceptional because it can belong directly to the noble path and therefore admits an uncontaminated form.

The caveat about rebirth-linking is also important. A cognition associated with a level can be defiled in one function while the corresponding collected meditative state is denied that classification. Function and meditative constitution must therefore remain distinct.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation is broadly correct, but two points should be sharpened.

First, `āryaṃ cādyam` means that the **first neighboring attainment** has a noble/uncontaminated form, not merely that it is generically spiritually elevated.

Second, `tridhā kecit` records a school disagreement: some teachers allow even a savoring-associated `anāgamya`, making the first sāmantaka threefold. This is not Vasubandhu's unqualified base classification.

A stronger translation is therefore:

> There are eight neighboring attainments, one for each [principal attainment]. They are pure and accompanied by neutral feeling. The first is also noble. Some hold [the first] to be threefold. The intermediate dhyāna is a dhyāna without vitarka.

## Logical Determination

This verse makes the distinction between a **node** and its **entry relation** explicit.

```text
maula
    state attained

sāmantaka
    transition enabling entry
```

The transition is not neutral with respect to the state-space. It has its own qualitative determination because it performs antidotal work against the lower ground.

Hence:

```text
transition-function
    determines
qualitative constitution
```

This is why the sāmantaka cannot simply inherit every mode available to the principal attainment.

The introduction of `dhyānāntara` adds a second kind of intermediate determination. It is not merely an access-path but a genuine meditative configuration in which one discursive factor has disappeared while another remains.

## Light Organon Contact

The useful contact is restrained but strong.

A systematic transition cannot be represented as an empty arrow between two finished states. The Kośa gives the transition its own determination:

```text
lower ground
    ↓
sāmantaka
    antidotal / dispassion-path
    ↓
principal attainment
```

The middle is therefore real and functionally specified.

At the same time, `dhyānāntara` warns us that not every intermediate is of the same logical kind. One intermediate is a **path of entry**; another is a **distinct internal configuration** produced by the progressive removal of factors.

That distinction should be preserved for the later Organon reconstruction.
