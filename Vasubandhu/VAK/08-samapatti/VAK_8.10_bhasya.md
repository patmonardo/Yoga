# VAK_8.10 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> kliṣṭeṣv asatprītisukhaṃ prasādaḥ saṃpradhīḥ smṛtiḥ /
> upekṣāsmṛtiśuddhiś ca kecit prasrabdhyupekṣaṇe // VAK_8.10 //

**Best current translation:**  
In the defiled dhyānas, rapture-and-ease, inner clarity, clear awareness and recollection, and the purity of equanimity and recollection are absent. Some hold instead that pliancy and equanimity are absent.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.10.md`](./VAK_8.10.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 441|01–441|12.

```text
kiṃ punaḥ kliṣṭeṣvapi dhyāneṣu yathāvihitānyaṅgāni bhavanti /
na hi kiṃ teṣu na vidyate /

kliṣṭeṣvasatprītisukhaṃ prasādaḥ saṃpradhīḥ smṛṭiḥ /
upekṣāsmṛtiśuddhiśca

prathame dhyāne vivekajaṃ prītisukhaṃ nāsti /
kleśāviviktatvāt /

dvitīye dhyāne 'dhyātmasaṃprasādo nāsti /
kleśāvilatvāt /

tṛtīye smṛtisaṃprajanyaṃ nāsti /
kliṣṭasukhasaṃbrhamitatvāt /

ccaturthe upekṣāsmṛtipariśuddhirnāsti /
kleśamalinatvāt /

evaṃ tāvat kecidāhuḥ /

kecit prasrabdhyupekṣaṇe // VAkK_8.10 //

kecitpunaḥ prathamadvitīyayoḥ kliṣṭayoḥ prasrabdhirnāsti /
tṛtīyacaturthayorupekṣā nāsti /
kuśalalmahābhūmikatvādanayoriti /
```

## Source Caution

The e-text contains several evident orthographic irregularities (`smṛṭiḥ`, `ccaturthe`, `kuśalalmahābhūmikatvāt`). They are preserved here as source witnesses rather than silently normalized.

The verse distributes absent factors across successive defiled dhyānas. It does not mean that every listed factor is absent from every defiled dhyāna.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The discussion begins with a direct question: do the limbs prescribed for the dhyānas also occur in defiled dhyānas?

The answer is negative, and the absence is determined level by level.

### First defiled dhyāna

```text
vivekajaṃ prītisukham
    rapture-and-ease born of separation

is absent

because:
kleśāviviktatvāt
    there is no separation from the defilements
```

### Second defiled dhyāna

```text
adhyātmasaṃprasāda
    inner clarity / inward serene confidence

is absent

because:
kleśāvilatvāt
    the state is turbid because of the defilements
```

### Third defiled dhyāna

```text
smṛti-saṃprajanya
    recollection and clear awareness

are absent

because:
kliṣṭa-sukha-saṃbhramitatvāt
    one is bewildered by defiled pleasure
```

### Fourth defiled dhyāna

```text
upekṣā-smṛti-pariśuddhi
    purification of equanimity and recollection

is absent

because:
kleśa-malinatvāt
    the state is stained by defilement
```

Vasubandhu then records an alternative position:

```text
first and second defiled dhyānas
    lack prasrabdhi

third and fourth defiled dhyānas
    lack upekṣā
```

The reason supplied is that these two factors belong to the `kuśala-mahābhūmika` class.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya is not merely removing arbitrary factors from a list. Each absence follows from the defining condition of the defiled state.

```text
first dhyāna
    requires separation-born joy/ease
    but defilement means non-separation
    therefore that factor is absent

second dhyāna
    requires inward clarity
    but defilement produces turbidity
    therefore clarity is absent

third dhyāna
    requires recollection + clear awareness
    but defiled pleasure bewilders
    therefore those factors fail

fourth dhyāna
    requires purified equanimity + recollection
    but defilement stains
    therefore their purification is absent
```

Thus the Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
belonging to a dhyāna-level
    from
possessing the pure factor-configuration of that level
```

A meditative state can retain enough structural determination to count as a dhyāna while failing to instantiate the wholesome quality that characterizes the pure form of that dhyāna.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames the question and gives the primary explanation.

**Voice: uncertain / alternative authorities** — `evaṃ tāvat kecidāhuḥ`, “thus some say,” followed by the alternative account concerning prasrabdhi and upekṣā.

The Bhāṣya does not here force a final adjudication between the two accounts; it records the alternative taxonomy.

## New Technical Terms

- **kleśāviviktatva** — non-separation from the defilements
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only compound in this passage
  - **function:** ground explaining why separation-born rapture and ease are absent in the defiled first dhyāna

- **kleśāvilatva** — turbidity through defilement
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expression
  - **function:** causal explanation for absence of `adhyātmasaṃprasāda`

- **kliṣṭasukhasaṃbhramitatva** — bewilderment through defiled pleasure
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only compound
  - **function:** causal explanation for loss of recollection and clear awareness in the third defiled dhyāna

- **kleśamalinatva** — stain or impurity through defilement
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expression
  - **function:** causal explanation for absence of purified equanimity and recollection in the fourth defiled dhyāna

- **kuśala-mahābhūmika** — factor belonging to the great wholesome ground/class
  - **status:** previously encountered Abhidharma category
  - **function:** doctrinal ground for the alternative account that defiled dhyānas lack prasrabdhi and upekṣā

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.10 establishes that the dhyāna taxonomy has at least two independent determinations:

```text
level
    first / second / third / fourth dhyāna

quality
    pure / defiled
```

A defiled dhyāna is therefore not a contradiction in terms. It belongs to a meditative level while lacking the factor or factor-purity that would characterize that level in its wholesome realization.

The sequence is especially instructive because the absent factor becomes progressively more cognitive:

```text
first
    separation-born rapture and ease

second
    inward clarity

third
    recollection + clear awareness

fourth
    purified equanimity + purified recollection
```

The higher the dhyāna, the more its purity is expressed through the quality of the cognitive field rather than through gross affective factors.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā's `saṃpradhīḥ smṛtiḥ` should be read in light of the prose as `smṛti-saṃprajanya`, recollection together with clear awareness. This sharpens the first-pass rendering of `saṃpradhī`.

Likewise, `upekṣā-smṛti-śuddhi` is not mere presence of equanimity and recollection, but specifically their purification.

The phrase `prasrabdhyupekṣaṇe` belongs to an alternative doctrinal position and should not be merged into Vasubandhu's first distributed list.

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes formal stage-membership from qualitative adequacy:

```text
Dhyāna-level
    can remain identifiable

while

its determining pure factor
    is absent or corrupted
```

The causal logic is internal:

```text
non-separation
    negates separation-born joy

turbidity
    negates inward clarity

bewilderment
    negates recollection and clear awareness

stain
    negates purification
```

This is therefore not merely a list of absences. Each negation is grounded in the positive character of `kliṣṭatva`.

## Light Organon Contact

The strongest light contact is qualitative rather than structural.

```text
same nominal form
    ≠ same cognitive actuality
```

The Kośa is showing that a meditative configuration is determined not only by which factors can be named, but by the manner in which those factors actually function.

That is enough for the present pass. The stronger comparative question—whether this resembles Yoga's progressive purification of the Samāpatti field—should remain bracketed until the chapter gives more evidence.
