# VAK_8.21 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

```text
na maulāḥ kuśalārūpyāḥ sāsravādharagocarāḥ /
anāsraveṇa hīyante kleśāḥ sāmantakena ca // VAK_8.21 //
```

## Best Current Translation

The principal wholesome formless attainments do not take contaminated lower-ground states as their object. Defilements are abandoned by the uncontaminated attainment and also by the neighboring preparatory attainment.

See the established Kārikā analysis in `VAK_8.21.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 447|08–447|17.

```text
na maulāḥ kuśalārūpyāḥ sāsravādharagocarāḥ /

maulānāṃ kuśalārūpyāṇām adhobhūmikaṃ sāsravaṃ vastu nālambanaṃ
svordhvabhūmyālambanatvāt /

anāsravaṃ tv ālambanam /
sarvānvayajñānapakṣyo na dharmajñānapakṣo nādhobhūminirodhaḥ /

sāmantakānantaryamārgāṇāṃ tv adharā bhūmir ālambanam /

eṣāṃ ca punas trividhānāṃ dhyānānāṃ rūpyārūpyāṇām

anāsraveṇa hīyante kleśāḥ

na śuddhakena /
kuta eva kliṣṭena /
vītarāgatvān nādhaḥ /
tasyaiva tadapratipakṣatvān na svabhūmau /
viśiṣṭataratvān nordhvam iti /

sāmantakena ca //

dhyānārūpyasāmantakena ca kleśāḥ prahīyante śuddhakenāpi /
adhobhūmipratipakṣatvāt /
```

## Source Caution

The first half is more specific than a simple statement that higher formless attainments avoid lower objects. Vasubandhu distinguishes the object-range of the **principal** (`maula`) wholesome ārūpyas from that of the **neighboring/preparatory** (`sāmantaka`) paths.

Likewise, the second half distinguishes the principal pure attainment from a pure sāmantaka. A principal `śuddhaka` does not abandon kleśas, whereas a pure sāmantaka can, because it functions as the antidote to the lower ground.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first asks what the wholesome formless attainments can take as object.

The principal wholesome ārūpyas do not take contaminated (`sāsrava`) objects belonging to a lower ground. Their object-support is restricted because they take their own or higher grounds as object.

They can, however, take uncontaminated objects. Vasubandhu further specifies these as belonging to the `anvayajñāna` side, not to the `dharmajñāna` side, and not to cessation belonging to a lower ground.

The neighboring paths are different. The `sāmantaka-anantaryamārga` takes the lower ground as its object precisely because it operates against that lower ground.

The discussion then turns from object-support to abandonment of kleśa. Among the three modes of dhyāna and ārūpya—pure, uncontaminated, and defiled—the principal uncontaminated mode abandons kleśa. The principal pure mode does not; still less does the defiled mode.

Vasubandhu gives the reasons why the principal pure attainment cannot function as the antidote:

```text
lower-ground kleśa
    already abandoned through dispassion

same-ground kleśa
    pure attainment is not its antidote

higher-ground kleśa
    higher ground is too superior to be opposed by it
```

But the sāmantaka is an exception. Even when pure, it abandons the kleśas of the lower ground because it is precisely the antidote (`pratipakṣa`) to that ground.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
principal wholesome ārūpya
    ↓
does not take lower contaminated ground as object
    ↓
takes own/higher ground and uncontaminated object-support

but

sāmantaka path
    ↓
takes lower ground as object
    ↓
because it functions against that lower ground
```

The same structural contrast appears in abandonment:

```text
principal śuddhaka
    does not abandon kleśa

principal anāsrava
    abandons kleśa

pure sāmantaka
    also abandons lower-ground kleśa
    because it is its pratipakṣa
```

So the decisive issue is not merely whether a state is pure. It is **what function that state performs in relation to a ground**.

## New Technical Terms

- **maula** — principal, fundamental attainment
  - **status** — previously encountered
  - **function** — distinguishes fully established attainment from neighboring preparation

- **sāmantaka** — neighboring, access, preparatory attainment
  - **status** — previously encountered locally; now structurally central
  - **function** — mediating attainment whose object is the lower ground and which acts as its antidote

- **anantaryamārga** — immediate path
  - **status** — previously encountered in Path materials
  - **function** — path immediately effecting abandonment

- **pratipakṣa** — antidote, counteragent
  - **status** — previously encountered
  - **function** — explains why the sāmantaka can abandon lower-ground kleśa

- **anvayajñānapakṣya** — belonging to the anvayajñāna side
  - **status** — previously encountered in Chapter 7
  - **function** — restricts the uncontaminated object-range of the ārūpyas

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.21 distinguishes **attainment-level**, **object-range**, and **antidotal function**.

### Principal wholesome ārūpya

```text
lower contaminated object      ✗
own/higher appropriate object   ✓
uncontaminated object           ✓
```

### Neighboring attainment

```text
lower ground as object          ✓
```

because the lower ground is what it must overcome.

### Abandonment of kleśa

```text
principal anāsrava              ✓
principal śuddhaka              ✗
kliṣṭa                          ✗
pure sāmantaka                  ✓ lower-ground kleśa
```

Thus `śuddha` by itself is not sufficient to determine liberative efficacy.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation remains broadly correct but should now be read with two qualifications.

First, `na maulāḥ ... sāsravādharagocarāḥ` concerns specifically the **principal** wholesome ārūpyas. It does not apply indiscriminately to their neighboring preparations.

Second, `sāmantakena ca` does not merely add another attainment that happens to abandon kleśa. The Bhāṣya explains why it can do so: the sāmantaka is the **antidote to the lower ground** (`adhobhūmipratipakṣatvāt`).

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a clean functional distinction:

```text
what a cognition may take as object
    depends on
what role it performs in the system
```

A principal attainment and its preparatory neighbor may be close in the hierarchy while having opposite intentional orientations.

```text
maula
    established in achieved ground
    → does not turn toward lower contaminated ground

sāmantaka
    transition out of lower ground
    → must take lower ground as object
```

That is an important genetic point. The mediating state is determined by the very determination it is overcoming.

Likewise:

```text
purity
    ≠ antidotal efficacy
```

The principal pure attainment is wholesome but not therefore a counteragent to the kleśas of its own or higher ground. Antidotal power belongs to a specifically relational function.

## Light Organon Contact

This is one of the clearest passages so far for distinguishing **state-description from transition-function**.

```text
same broad quality: śuddha

principal state
    maintains achieved determination

sāmantaka state
    negates a lower determination
```

The difference is not captured by the adjective `pure` alone. One must know the state's place in a transition relation.

This fits the emerging Systems insight without forcing it onto Vasubandhu:

```text
node properties
    do not determine the whole behavior

transition-role
    can change object-range
    and causal efficacy
```

The Kośa remains concrete: the sāmantaka looks downward because what it is doing is abandoning the lower ground.
