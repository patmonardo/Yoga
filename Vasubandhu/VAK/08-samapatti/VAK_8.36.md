# VAK_8.36

## Sanskrit

दश कृत्स्नानि अलोभाष्टौ ध्यानेऽन्त्ये गोचरः पुनः ।
कामाः द्वे शुद्धकारूप्ये स्वचतुःस्कन्धगोचरे ॥

## IAST

daśa kṛtsnāni alobhāṣṭau dhyāne 'ntye gocaraḥ punaḥ /
kāmāḥ dve śuddhākārūpye svacatuḥskandhagocare //

## Padaccheda

daśa | kṛtsnāni | alobhāḥ | aṣṭau | dhyāne | antye | gocaraḥ | punaḥ | kāmāḥ | dve | śuddhaka-ārūpye | sva-catuḥ-skandha-gocare

## Literal Translation

There are ten totalities. Eight are constituted by non-greed and belong to the final Dhyāna; their object-field is again the desire realm. The remaining two are pure formless attainments and have as their object the four aggregates of their own level.

## Philosophical Translation

Ten totality-practices are distinguished. The first eight expand a selected field until it is apprehended without remainder; they are grounded in non-appropriation, belong to the final Dhyāna, and still operate upon objects of the desire realm. The last two are pure formless practices whose field is the fourfold aggregate-complex belonging to their own plane.

## Technical Vocabulary

- **kṛtsna** — totality; an all-encompassing meditative field
- **alobha** — non-greed or non-appropriation
- **antya-dhyāna** — the final, fourth Dhyāna
- **gocara** — object-range or operative field
- **kāma** — the desire realm
- **śuddhaka-ārūpya** — pure formless attainment
- **sva-catuḥ-skandha** — the four aggregates belonging to the attainment's own level

## Doctrinal Determination

The ten totalities continue the progression from Deliverance to mastery. A totality-practice does not merely resist or dominate an object-field; it expands one selected aspect until the field is apprehended as complete.

The first eight concern form and color totalities and are constituted by non-greed. Their dependence on non-appropriation is essential: the field is made total without being seized as an object of craving.

The final two correspond to the pure formless totalities. Their object is no longer a visible field in the desire realm but the four aggregates belonging to their own level.

## Logical Determination

The practical sequence now appears as:

1. **vimokṣa** — release from domination by an object-field
2. **abhibhū-āyatana** — mastery within that field
3. **kṛtsna** — comprehension of the field under the form of totality

A kṛtsna is therefore not merely a larger object. It is a practice in which limitation by local appearance is overcome and a selected determination becomes the governing form of the whole field.

## Organon Interpretation

The kṛtsna-practice is a discipline of universality at the level of appearance. A finite object is no longer treated as this isolated patch of earth, color, space, or consciousness; its aspect is extended as the form of the entire field.

This is still practice rather than speculative theory. The yogin does not infer the universal but constructs and inhabits a field in which the selected determination is presented without remainder.

The condition **alobha** prevents totalization from becoming appropriation. The practitioner masters the field precisely by not converting it into possession.
