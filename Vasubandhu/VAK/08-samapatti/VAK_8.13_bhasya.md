# VAK_8.13 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> kāyākṣiśrotravijñānaṃ vijñaptyutthāpakaṃ ca yat /
> dvitīyādau tadādyāptaṃ akliṣṭāvyākṛtaṃ ca tat // VAK_8.13 //

**Best current translation:**  
The body-, eye-, and ear-knowledges, and whatever gives rise to manifest action—in the second dhyāna and above, these are obtained from the first level, and they are undefiled-neutral.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_8.13.md`](./VAK_8.13.md)

## Source Caution

The reference e-text is not a critical edition and warns that errors remain. The transition into VAK 8.13 is nevertheless unusually clear. The prose question begins before the verse and the explanation closes explicitly with `avasitaṃ dhyānakāryam` — “the work concerning dhyāna is concluded.”

The prose asks how beings in the second dhyāna and above can see, hear, touch, and produce manifest action if the corresponding sensory knowledge-bodies are not native to those higher levels. The answer is not that such functions disappear, but that first-dhyāna-level knowledges are made present when required.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Objector**

If the second and higher dhyānas do not contain the three sensory knowledge-bodies, and if vitarka and vicāra are absent there, how can beings born in those levels see, hear, touch, and produce manifest action?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

It is not that eye-knowledge and the others are altogether absent in beings born there. They are simply not native to that level (`na tu svabhūmikāḥ`).

What occurs instead is that beings in the second and higher dhyānas make present (`saṃmukhīkurvanti`) eye-, ear-, and body-knowledge belonging to the first-dhyāna level, together with the cognition that produces manifest action.

Vasubandhu compares this to a constructed mind (`nirmāṇacittavat`): through that lower-level operative cognition they see, hear, touch, and produce manifestation.

The cognition made present in this way is `anivṛtāvyākṛta` / `akliṣṭāvyākṛta`: unobstructed or undefiled and karmically neutral. It is not defiled because beings at those higher levels are separated from passion; it is not wholesome because the borrowed cognition is inferior (`hīnatvāt`).

The prose then declares the dhyāna discussion complete.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya resolves an apparent contradiction generated by the previous verse.

```text
Premise 1
    higher dhyāna rebirths lack
    native first-dhyāna sensory knowledge-bodies

Premise 2
    beings there nevertheless
    see, hear, touch, and act manifestly

Therefore
    operative sensory cognition must occur
    without becoming native to the higher level
```

Vasubandhu's solution is:

```text
higher-level being
    ↓ requires sensory / manifest function
makes present
    ↓
first-dhyāna-level vijñāna
    ↓
seeing / hearing / touching / vijñapti
```

The decisive distinction is therefore between:

```text
possession or availability of a function
    and
native level of that function
```

The higher rebirth does not regenerate a higher-level sensory knowledge. It temporarily actualizes a lower-level operative cognition.

## New Technical Terms

- **svabhūmika** — belonging to one's own level; native to the current bhūmi
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative distinction in this passage
  - **function:** distinguishes availability of cognition from the level to which that cognition properly belongs

- **saṃmukhīkaroti / saṃmukhīkurvanti** — to make present, bring into immediate operation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya vocabulary
  - **function:** explains how a lower-level cognition becomes functionally operative in a higher-level being

- **nirmāṇacittavat** — like a constructed mind
  - **status:** Bhāṣya comparison
  - **function:** analogy for temporarily operating through a cognition that is not native to the current level

- **anivṛtāvyākṛta** — unobstructed-neutral / undefiled-neutral
  - **status:** technical Abhidharma classification, made explicit in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** ethical classification of the borrowed first-dhyāna cognition

- **hīnatva** — inferiority, lower status
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** explains why the borrowed cognition is not classified as wholesome

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.13 establishes that a higher dhyāna rebirth need not contain every operative cognition as a native constituent of its own level.

```text
higher dhyāna
    establishes the being's level

first-dhyāna vijñāna
    supplies selected sensory and manifest functions
```

The Bhāṣya therefore rejects a simple identity between:

```text
where a being is established
    and
where every cognition it employs belongs
```

This also prevents the absence of sensory knowledge-bodies in the higher levels from being interpreted as total sensory incapacity.

The borrowed cognition has a specific ethical status:

```text
not kliṣṭa
    because passion has been abandoned

not kuśala
    because the operative cognition is lower / inferior

therefore
    akliṣṭa-avyākṛta
```

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass rendering `associated with the first dhyāna` is too weak for `tadādyāptam` when read through the Bhāṣya. The prose explicitly says the relevant cognition is `prathamadhyānabhūmika` and is made present by beings in the higher dhyānas.

A stronger philosophical translation is therefore:

> In the second dhyāna and above, body-, eye-, and ear-knowledge and the cognition that produces manifest action are drawn from the first-dhyāna level; when made present, they are undefiled-neutral.

The project vocabulary should continue to translate `vijñāna` as **knowledge**, not consciousness.

## Logical Determination

The passage distinguishes level from operation.

```text
level-determination
    where the being belongs

operative determination
    what cognition is activated for a function
```

The relation is asymmetric:

```text
higher level
    may activate lower cognition

lower cognition
    does not redefine the higher level
```

This is not a simple inclusion relation. The cognition is explicitly `na svabhūmika` — not native to the higher field — yet it can be made present there.

The Bhāṣya thus gives a precise account of functional availability across qualitative levels without collapsing those levels into one another.

## Light Organon Contact

The strongest restrained contact point is the difference between **constitutive level** and **operative capacity**.

```text
higher determination
    need not internalize every lower function
    as one of its own defining factors

instead
    it can make a lower operation present
    when the task requires it
```

That is enough for the present pass. The text itself supplies the important distinction: qualitative ascent does not imply loss of every lower capacity, but neither does functional use erase the hierarchy of levels.

The closing sentence is significant for chapter structure:

```text
avasitaṃ dhyānakāryam
    the treatment of dhyāna is concluded
```

VAK 8.14 therefore begins a new local problem: how the pure, uncontaminated, and defiled dhyānas and ārūpyas are acquired.
