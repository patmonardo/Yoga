# VAK_8.33

## Sanskrit

निरोधस्तु समापत्तिः सूक्ष्मसूक्ष्मादनन्तरम् ।
स्वशुद्धकाधरार्येण व्युत्थानं चेतसा ततः ॥

## IAST

nirodhas tu samāpattiḥ sūkṣma-sūkṣmād anantaram /
sva-śuddhakādharāryeṇa vyutthānaṃ cetasā tataḥ //

## Padaccheda

nirodhaḥ | tu | samāpattiḥ | sūkṣma-sūkṣmāt | anantaram | sva-śuddhaka-adhara-āryeṇa | vyutthānam | cetasā | tataḥ

## Literal Translation

But cessation is an attainment, immediately following the most subtle of the subtle. Emergence from it occurs by a mind belonging to oneself, to a pure one, to a lower [level], or to a noble one.

## Philosophical Translation

The eighth Deliverance is the attainment of cessation. It follows immediately upon the most subtle of the subtle formless attainments. From this cessation one emerges through an appropriate mind: one’s own, a purified mind, a mind belonging to a lower sphere, or a noble mind, according to the precise doctrinal classification to be confirmed in the bhāṣya.

## Technical Vocabulary

- **nirodha** — cessation; here the cessation-attainment associated with the eighth Deliverance
- **samāpatti** — meditative attainment or entry into a stabilized field
- **sūkṣma-sūkṣma** — the most subtle of the subtle; the summit of the formless sequence
- **anantaram** — immediately following, without an intervening state
- **vyutthāna** — emergence or rising out of an attainment
- **cetas** — mind or mental event by which emergence occurs
- **ārya** — noble; connected with supramundane realization

## Doctrinal Determination

This verse makes the classification explicit: the eighth Deliverance is **nirodha-samāpatti**, the attainment of cessation. The text therefore gives us direct warrant for calling this Deliverance a Samāpatti.

The sequence is practical rather than merely theoretical:

1. the practitioner traverses increasingly subtle formless attainments;
2. cessation follows the most subtle of these without an intervening state;
3. emergence occurs through a determinate mental condition.

The compressed second half belongs to a technical account of the possible minds connected with emergence. Its exact distribution should remain provisional until the bhāṣya pass.

## Logical Determination

The eighth Deliverance differs from the earlier deliverances because release no longer concerns mastery over a presented object-field. The field of active cognition itself is suspended.

This does not mean that Samādhi has been forced into Samāpatti. The verse says only and precisely:

> cessation is a Samāpatti.

Thus the logical movement is:

- earlier Deliverances: freedom within transformed presentation;
- eighth Deliverance: entry into cessation of the active cognitive field;
- emergence: restoration of determinate mental activity.

## Organon Interpretation

The attainment of cessation is an extreme form of practical release from Māyic absorption. In ordinary absorption, an appearing field captures consciousness. In the eighth Deliverance, the operation of the field is not replaced by another appearance; it is brought to cessation.

Yet this is still a practice and an attainment, not by itself the Concept of cessation. The verse describes entry, succession, and emergence. It therefore belongs first to the architecture of meditative performance.

The important distinction is:

- **Samāpatti** names the achieved condition of entry;
- **Samādhi** should not be inferred here merely because the attainment is exalted;
- **nirodha** names what is attained: cessation.
