# VAK_8.32_bhasya

## Kārikā

**IAST**

`aṣṭau vimokṣāḥ prathamāv aśubhā dhyānayor dvayoḥ /`
`tṛtīyo 'ntye sa cālobhaḥ śubhārūpyāḥ samāhitāḥ // VAK_8.32 //`

**Best Current Translation**

There are eight Deliverances. The first two are impurity-practices in the first two Dhyānas. The third is in the final Dhyāna, and it is non-greed. The wholesome formless states, when collected, are Deliverances.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first enumerates the eight Deliverances by their canonical formulas. The first is seeing forms while oneself possessed of form. The second is seeing external forms while retaining an internal representation of form. The third is dwelling after directly realizing the pure Deliverance. Four further Deliverances are the four formless attainments, and the eighth is the attainment of cessation, treated in the following verse.

He then classifies the first three. The first two are `aśubha` in nature because their cognitive aspects are the blue-black and related unattractive forms familiar from impurity contemplation. Their method is therefore to be understood on the model of `aśubhā-bhāvanā`. They occur only in the first and second Dhyānas and respectively counter attachment to color/form belonging to the desire realm and to the first-Dhyāna level.

The third Deliverance is different. It belongs to the fourth Dhyāna and is `alobha` in nature. Vasubandhu explicitly denies that it is itself an impurity-practice: its mode is `śubha`, pure or beautiful. With their associated factors, these first three are complexes having the five aggregates as their nature.

The four formless states receive the name `vimokṣa` only when wholesome and collected. Afflicted or uncollected formless states are excluded. Vasubandhu further allows the liberation-paths of the neighboring attainments to be called Deliverances, but not their uninterrupted paths, because those take a lower level as object. The semantic rule governing the classification is explicit: the meaning of `vimokṣa` is `vaimukhya`, turning away or release from domination by the former field.

## Argument Reconstruction

1. There are eight Deliverances.
2. The first two manipulate the appearance of form through unattractive aspects.
3. Their liberative function therefore proceeds by counter-aspect and is restricted to the first two Dhyānas.
4. The third Deliverance no longer requires the unattractive aspect.
5. It encounters the pure/beautiful while remaining constituted by non-greed.
6. Hence release from form is stronger than mere negation of attractive form: the field can appear positively without reactivating appropriation.
7. The formless attainments count as Deliverances only when they are wholesome and genuinely collected.
8. Therefore `vimokṣa` names a functional relation of turning-away/release (`vaimukhya`), not merely residence in a particular meditative level.

## New Technical Terms

- **vimokṣa** — Deliverance; release characterized by `vaimukhya`, turning away from domination by a prior field.
  - **status:** encountered earlier as liberation terminology, here given a specific meditative taxonomy.
  - **function:** classification of trained releases from form and increasingly subtle fields.

- **vaimukhya** — turning away, non-facing, release from dominance.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** explicit semantic explanation of `vimokṣa`.

- **vinīlaka** — blue-black aspect of corpse/impurity contemplation.
  - **status:** previously encountered in aśubha contexts.
  - **function:** explains why the first two Deliverances are `aśubha` in nature.

- **śubhākāra** — pure/beautiful mode or aspect.
  - **status:** important here.
  - **function:** distinguishes the third Deliverance from impurity-practice.

- **sāmantakavimuktimārga** — liberation-path belonging to a neighboring/preliminary attainment.
  - **status:** specialized path taxonomy.
  - **function:** marks which neighboring attainments may receive the name `vimokṣa`.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting the standard classification and supplying doctrinal reasons.

**Voice:** alternate opinion (`apare`) at 455|17 concerning uncollected formless states.

## Doctrinal Determination

The eight Deliverances are not homogeneous absorptions. They are grouped under a common functional notion: release from domination by a given object-field.

The first two achieve release through a counter-aspect: attractive form is re-presented under the unattractive. The third is more advanced in a precise sense. It is `alobha` while retaining a positive `śubha` mode. Thus the practitioner no longer needs to negate beauty in order to avoid attachment.

The four formless attainments count as Deliverances only under normative conditions: they must be wholesome and collected. Mere occupancy of a formless level is insufficient.

## Translation Consequences

`vimokṣa` is best translated here as **Deliverance** or **Release**, not as an already-final metaphysical liberation. The Bhāṣya's gloss `vaimukhya` emphasizes a practical turning-away from the binding authority of a field.

`śubha` in the third Deliverance should retain its positive force—pure, beautiful, agreeable—because Vasubandhu explicitly contrasts it with `aśubha`. Translating both merely as technical meditation labels would conceal the argument.

`samāhita` should remain **collected / stabilized**, not automatically be rendered as `samādhi`, since the text is specifying the condition under which a formless attainment receives the title `vimokṣa`.

## Logical Determination

The sequence of the first three Deliverances is logically important:

```text
aśubha counter-aspect
    → freedom by negating the authority of attraction

śubha + alobha
    → freedom within positive appearance
```

The second is the stronger form. A field is not fully mastered merely because cognition can replace its attractive aspect with an unattractive one. Greater freedom is demonstrated when the positive appearance itself can remain while appropriation does not arise.

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes **content-transformation** from **relation-transformation**. The first two alter the governing appearance; the third transforms the subject's relation to a positive appearance.

## Light Organon Contact

This is useful for the emerging idea of a regulated field of cognition and relation. The first two Deliverances regulate the field by changing its dominant predicate. The third regulates the relation itself: beauty need not be denied because greed no longer governs the relation to it.

So the progression is not abstraction from all difference. It is training cognition to identify which difference is binding, neutralize that determination, and eventually preserve determinate appearance without subjection to it.

That is a valuable model for transcendental systems-building, but the Bhāṣya itself remains more modest: it is giving a practical taxonomy of release.
