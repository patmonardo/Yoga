# VAK_8.13 — Higher Dhyāna Function Through First-Dhyāna Consciousness

## Sanskrit

### Devanāgarī

```text
कायाक्षिश्रोत्रविज्ञानं विज्ञप्त्युत्थापकं च यत् ।
द्वितीयादौ तदाद्याप्तम् अक्लिष्टाव्याकृतं च तत् ॥ ८.१३ ॥
```

### IAST

```text
kāyākṣiśrotravijñānaṃ vijñaptyutthāpakaṃ ca yat /
dvitīyādau tadādyāptam akliṣṭāvyākṛtaṃ ca tat // VAK_8.13 //
```

## Source Caution

The compound cluster in the first half is compressed. This pass reads:

```text
kāya-vijñāna
    body-knowledge

akṣi-vijñāna
    eye-knowledge

śrotra-vijñāna
    ear-knowledge

vijñapti-utthāpaka
    that which gives rise to informative bodily or verbal action
```

The verse concerns beings or attainments of the second dhyāna and above. It states that these operative consciousnesses are not native to those higher levels. They are associated with the first dhyāna and are undefiled-neutral.

The exact scope of `vijñapti` and the detailed bhāṣya account of bodily and verbal manifestation should be reserved for the commentary pass.

## Grammar

The first half identifies a group of operative consciousnesses:

```text
kāyākṣiśrotravijñānam
    body-, eye-, and ear-knowledge

vijñaptyutthāpakaṃ ca yat
    and whatever gives rise to informative action
```

The second half locates and qualifies them:

```text
dvitīyādau
    in the second dhyāna and above

tad ādyāptam
    that belongs to the first level

akliṣṭāvyākṛtaṃ ca tat
    and it is undefiled-neutral
```

The relative-correlative structure is:

```text
yat ... tat
    whatever ... that
```

The verse therefore states that certain functions occurring in higher dhyāna existence are executed through a consciousness associated with the first dhyāna.

## Literal Translation

The body-, eye-, and ear-knowledges, and whatever gives rise to informative action—in the second dhyāna and above, that belongs to the first level, and it is undefiled-neutral.

## Philosophical Translation

In the second dhyāna and the higher form-realm levels, bodily sensation, seeing, hearing, and the consciousness that initiates communicative manifestation are not generated from the defining consciousness of those higher levels. Their operative basis belongs to the first dhyāna and is neither defiled nor karmically determinate, but neutral.

## Technical Vocabulary

### kāya-, akṣi-, and śrotra-vijñāna

These are functional sensory knowledges:

```text
kāya-vijñāna
    bodily contact and sensation

akṣi-vijñāna
    visual apprehension

śrotra-vijñāna
    auditory apprehension
```

Their presence in higher dhyāna beings does not mean that every higher level intrinsically contains all lower-level cognitive functions.

### vijñaptyutthāpaka

```text
vijñapti
    manifest or informative action

utthāpaka
    that which brings forth
    or initiates
```

The term indicates the consciousness capable of producing an outwardly manifest bodily or verbal sign.

The verse therefore concerns not only reception through the senses but also expressive operation.

### ādyāpta

```text
ādi
    the first

āpta
    obtained
    belonging to
    associated with
```

Here it means:

```text
associated with the first dhyāna
```

The higher level borrows a lower-level operational basis.

### akliṣṭa-avyākṛta

```text
akliṣṭa
    not defiled

avyākṛta
    karmically indeterminate
    neutral
```

This is not a liberating or uncontaminated cognition. It is a neutral functional consciousness.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.13 establishes three points.

### 1. Higher levels do not contain every operative function natively

The second dhyāna and higher levels are free from the discursive and sensory structure proper to the first.

Yet beings born there still see, hear, experience bodily contact, and communicate.

The solution is:

```text
higher-level existence
    uses
first-dhyāna-associated operative consciousness
```

### 2. Functional descent does not equal spiritual regression

A being established in a higher dhyāna may activate a lower-level consciousness for a determinate function.

```text
higher attainment
    remains higher

lower functional consciousness
    is temporarily employed
```

The level of the agent and the level of the instrument are not identical.

### 3. The borrowed consciousness is neutral

It is:

```text
not defiled
not wholesome path-consciousness
not uncontaminated
but neutral-functioning
```

This prevents communication and sensory operation from being mistaken for either affliction or liberating wisdom.

## Logical Determination

The verse introduces a distinction between **system-level determination** and **functional module**.

```text
system-level determination
    second dhyāna or above

functional module
    first-dhyāna-associated consciousness
```

A higher system can therefore employ a lower function without being reduced to that lower system.

```text
higher whole
    contains access to
lower operation

but

higher whole
    is not identical with
lower operation
```

This is a relation of controlled subordination.

The logical form is:

```text
location of the being
    higher dhyāna

origin of the function
    first dhyāna

ethical quality of the function
    undefiled-neutral
```

Thus the Kośa distinguishes three independent axes:

```text
where the being is established
what consciousness performs the act
what ethical quality that consciousness has
```

## Organon Light

The immediate point should remain doctrinal. Still, the verse strongly supports a system architecture in which higher wholes retain access to lower operational powers.

```text
higher Samāpatti
    does not erase lower capacities

it governs their use
    through a subordinate functional layer
```

This is not yet the grahya–grahaṇa–grahītṛ reconstruction. It is a more basic law:

```text
qualitative ascent
    reorganizes functions
    rather than merely deleting them
```

The higher state is therefore not an abstract purity incapable of action. It can descend functionally without losing its attained determination.
