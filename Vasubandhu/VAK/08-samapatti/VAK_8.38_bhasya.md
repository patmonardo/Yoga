# VAK_8.38_bhasya

## Kārikā

**IAST**

`hetu-karma-balād dhātvor ārūpyotpādanaṃ dvayoḥ /`
`dhyānānāṃ rūpadhātau tu tābhyāṃ dharmatayāpi ca // VAK_8.38 //`

**Best Current Translation**

The production of the formless attainments in the two realms occurs through the power of causal preparation and karma. In the form realm, however, the Dhyānas arise through those two and also through dharmatā.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu asks how distinctive formless attainments and Dhyānas arise in the form and formless realms. He answers by identifying three powers under which Dhyāna and formless Samāpatti can arise: **cause (`hetu`)**, **karma**, and **dharmatā**.

The formless Samāpattis arise in the form and formless realms through the first two powers. **Causal power** means recent and repeated cultivation: `āsanna-abhīkṣṇa-abhyāsa`. **Karmic power** means the maturation of karma belonging to a higher level whose result has become present. Vasubandhu adds that one cannot arise in a higher level while still retaining attachment to a lower one.

Dhyāna in the form realm can arise through those same two powers and also through **dharmatā**. Vasubandhu locates this third mode specifically at the time of cosmic re-formation (`saṃvartanīkāla`): beings of lower levels then produce the relevant Dhyāna because the determinations of the dharmic order as a whole have entered into manifested activity.

The verse therefore distinguishes cultivated genesis, karmically conditioned genesis, and genesis belonging to the lawful configuration of the world-process itself.

## Argument Reconstruction

1. Higher meditative states require an account of how they arise in the realms where they occur.
2. Three explanatory powers are available: `hetu`, `karma`, and `dharmatā`.
3. `hetu-bala` is not an abstract cause; it is recent and repeated habituation.
4. `karma-bala` is the present maturation of karma belonging to a higher level.
5. Formless Samāpattis in the form and formless realms can arise through those two powers.
6. Dhyāna in the form realm can arise through those powers and, at cosmic re-formation, through `dharmatā` as well.
7. Therefore the production of a meditative state is not reducible to deliberate practice alone: personal cultivation, karmic history, and the lawful configuration of the domain can each function as determinants of its arising.

## New Technical Terms

- **āsanna-abhīkṣṇa-abhyāsa** — recent and repeated practice; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit clarification; **function:** explains `hetu-bala`.
- **apara-paryāya-vedanīya karma** — karma whose result is experienced in a subsequent existence; **status:** Bhāṣya technical specification; **function:** explains the karmic ground for higher-level rebirth and attainment.
- **saṃvartanīkāla** — period of cosmic re-formation/contraction; **status:** Bhāṣya-specific contextual term here; **function:** fixes the context in which `dharmatā` itself contributes to the production of Dhyāna.
- **udbhūta-vṛtti** — manifested or arisen mode of functioning; **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory phrase; **function:** describes the dharmic order becoming operative as a whole.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes the three causes considerably more precise than the compressed Kārikā permits.

### 1. `hetu-bala`

This is the efficacy of **recent and repeated cultivation**. It is a directly acquired capacity produced by practice.

### 2. `karma-bala`

This is the efficacy of prior karma belonging to a higher level when its maturation becomes present. The practitioner cannot simply appear in a higher level while attachment to the lower remains intact.

### 3. `dharmatā`

This is not invoked indiscriminately as a metaphysical explanation for every meditative event. Vasubandhu uses it in a determinate cosmological case: during cosmic re-formation, beings of lower levels produce the relevant Dhyāna because the total configuration of dharmas has entered that mode of manifestation.

Thus the three powers are different forms of genesis, not three names for one generic cause.

## Translation Consequences

- **hetu** should not be rendered here as a merely abstract “cause”; the Bhāṣya specifies recent and repeated cultivation.
- **karma-bala** is not just accumulated moral force in general; it is tied to a higher-level karmic result becoming present.
- **dharmatā** should be translated cautiously as dharmic regularity / the lawful configuration of dharmas, while preserving the specific cosmological context supplied by the Bhāṣya.
- The first-pass phrase “intrinsic law of the sphere” was directionally useful but too general. Vasubandhu gives a concrete case rather than an unrestricted metaphysical principle.

## Logical Determination

VAK 8.38 distinguishes three modes of determination of an attained state:

```text
practice-history
    → hetu-bala

karmic-history
    → karma-bala

lawful configuration of the domain
    → dharmatā
```

This is a useful constraint on any later theory of field-regulation. The appearing field is not simply a passive object manipulated by an already-complete agent. The agent's own capacities arise through determinate histories, and in the `dharmatā` case the larger configuration of the domain itself participates in the genesis of what can appear and be attained.

So the relation is more reciprocal than a simple subject regulating an external field:

```text
agent-formation
    ↔ conditions of domain
    ↔ attainable field
```

The Bhāṣya does not turn this into a transcendental doctrine; it remains a technical account of meditative production.

## Light Organon Contact

This verse helps refine the provisional “regulative field” language. A purely regulative picture could suggest that a ready-made rational subject simply imposes order upon appearances. Vasubandhu's causal analysis is more genetic: the subject capable of regulation has itself been formed through cultivation and karma, while the lawful configuration of the domain can also condition what mode of cognition becomes possible.

Accordingly, a later transcendental reconstruction should distinguish at least:

```text
Principle of validity
≠
genesis of cognitive capacity
≠
regulated field of appearance
```

The present passage concerns the latter two and does not itself determine the Principle of validity.
