# VAK_8.06 — Savoring, Pure, and Uncontaminated Samāpatti

## Sanskrit

### Devanāgarī

```text
आस्वादनासंप्रयुक्तं सतृष्णं लौकिकं शुभम् ।
शुद्धकं तत्तदास्वाद्यं लोकोत्तरमनास्रवम् ॥ ८.६ ॥
```

### IAST

```text
āsvādanāsaṃprayuktaṃ satṛṣṇaṃ laukikaṃ śubham /
śuddhakaṃ tattadāsvādyaṃ lokottaramanāsravam // VAK_8.06 //
```

## Source Caution

The compact expression `āsvādanāsaṃprayuktam` is read here as:

```text
āsvādanā-saṃprayuktam
    associated with savoring
```

The source joins the elements tightly. The doctrinal contrast with `śuddhakam` and `anāsravam` confirms the analysis.

The phrase `tattadāsvādyam` is likewise compressed:

```text
tat-tat-āsvādyam
    that which is savored in each respective case
```

The verse is not merely listing three labels. It defines each mode by its relation to craving, worldliness, purity, and supramundane release.

## Grammar

The verse presents three elliptical definitions whose understood subject is the mode of samāpatti introduced in VAK 8.05.

```text
āsvādana-saṃprayuktam
    [that which is] associated with savoring

sa-tṛṣṇam
    [is] accompanied by craving
```

```text
laukikaṃ śubham
    the wholesome worldly [mode]

śuddhakam
    [is called] pure
```

```text
lokottaram anāsravam
    the supramundane [mode]

[is called] uncontaminated
```

The phrase `tattadāsvādyam` reconnects the pure attainment to the savoring mode:

```text
the pure attainment at each level
    is the respective object
    that savoring appropriates
```

Thus savoring does not require a different attainment-object. It appropriates the corresponding pure attainment through craving.

## Literal Translation

That which is associated with savoring is accompanied by craving. The wholesome worldly mode is pure; each respective pure attainment is what is savored. The supramundane mode is uncontaminated.

## Philosophical Translation

A meditative attainment becomes a savoring attainment when it is joined with craving. The wholesome attainment that remains within the worldly order is called pure, and it is precisely this pure attainment at each level that can become the object of savoring. When the attainment is supramundane, it is uncontaminated.

## Technical Vocabulary

### āsvādana

```text
āsvādana
    tasting
    savoring
    relishing
```

Here it signifies appropriation of a meditative state through craving.

```text
attainment occurs
    ↓
pleasure or excellence is tasted
    ↓
craving adheres to the attainment
```

The defect is therefore not necessarily impurity of the meditative level itself. It is the practitioner's relation to it.

### sa-tṛṣṇa

```text
sa-tṛṣṇa
    with craving
```

This gives the defining criterion of the savoring-associated mode:

```text
savoring-associated samāpatti
    = samāpatti joined with craving
```

Even a highly refined attainment remains contaminated when it becomes an object of thirst.

### laukika śubha

```text
laukika
    belonging to the world
    remaining within conditioned existence

śubha
    wholesome
```

The pure mode is therefore not morally or meditatively defective. It is wholesome but still worldly.

```text
pure
    ≠ liberating by definition
```

This is the distinction the previous discussion of Bhavāgra requires: refinement and purity can reach the summit of conditioned existence without becoming uncontaminated.

### śuddhaka

`Śuddhaka` names the pure mode of the attainment.

```text
śuddhaka
    wholesome
    free from the immediate defilement of savoring
    yet still laukika
```

It is the basis that can be appropriated by savoring:

```text
pure attainment
    + craving
    = savoring-associated attainment
```

### lokottara

```text
loka
    world

uttara
    beyond; higher than

lokottara
    supramundane
```

This does not mean merely cosmologically higher. The form and formless realms are already higher than the desire realm while remaining worldly.

`Lokottara` means beyond the conditioned worldly order in the path-oriented sense.

### anāsrava

```text
an-āsrava
    without outflows
    uncontaminated
```

The uncontaminated mode is defined by its supramundane status.

```text
higher realm
    does not imply anāsrava

supramundane realization
    is anāsrava
```

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 8.06 defines the three modes introduced in VAK 8.05.

| Mode | Defining determination |
|---|---|
| savoring-associated | joined with craving |
| pure | wholesome and worldly |
| uncontaminated | supramundane and without outflows |

### 1. Savoring is relational contamination

The attainment is savored because craving takes hold of it.

```text
pure attainment
    becomes object of relish

craving
    appropriates its excellence
```

Thus one can be attached not only to coarse desire but also to meditative purity, luminosity, peace, or formlessness.

### 2. Purity remains within the world

The pure mode is `laukika śubha`.

```text
wholesome
    yes

world-transcending
    not yet
```

This prevents the equation:

```text
purity = liberation
```

### 3. The uncontaminated is supramundane

The uncontaminated mode is not defined merely by intensity or elevation.

```text
anāsrava
    requires lokottara status
```

The decisive distinction is therefore not lower versus higher realm, but worldly versus supramundane realization.

## Logical Determination

The verse supplies a threefold modal classification of one and the same attainment-level.

```text
same meditative level
    ├── appropriated through craving
    │       savoring-associated
    │
    ├── wholesome within conditioned existence
    │       pure
    │
    └── supramundane and outflow-free
            uncontaminated
```

The classification is not primarily spatial. It concerns the logical and causal status of the attainment.

### Pure and savoring are internally related

```text
pure attainment
    is what savoring savors
```

The savoring mode therefore presupposes a pure attainment as its object, but alters its practical status through attachment.

### Uncontaminated is not a stronger degree of purity

```text
pure
    worldly wholesomeness

uncontaminated
    supramundane freedom from outflows
```

The transition is not simply quantitative intensification. It is a change of order.

## Organon Light

The immediate lesson is Eastern and practical:

```text
one meditative form
    can stand in three different relations
    to bondage and release
```

The chapter therefore refuses to infer spiritual status from phenomenological grandeur alone.

```text
sublime appearance
    may be savored

pure appearance
    may remain worldly

only the supramundane mode
    is uncontaminated
```

This sharpens the developing distinction:

```text
Bhavāgra
    summit of conditioned existence

Dharma-dhātu access
    requires uncontaminated realization
```

No stronger Hegelian reconstruction is required at this stage. The kārikā already gives the essential practical logic: attachment, worldly purity, and supramundane release are distinct determinations of meditative attainment.
