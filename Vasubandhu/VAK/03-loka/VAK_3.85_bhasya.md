## Natural Argument

The discussion begins by closing the lifespan section. Beings in Uttarakuru have a fixed lifespan and necessarily live out that entire lifespan. Elsewhere this is not generally guaranteed: `antarāmṛtyu`, death before the full lifespan, can occur.

Vasubandhu immediately qualifies the general rule. There are many persons for whom death does not occur before the completion of the relevant life-term. The transmitted list includes especially bodhisattva and Buddha-related cases, persons following faith or Dharma, and mothers pregnant with certain extraordinary persons such as a bodhisattva or cakravartin. The point is structural: outside Uttarakuru premature death is possible, but it is not universal.

The text then changes scale. Having previously measured places and bodies in yojanas and lifespans in years, it asks for the limiting measures themselves. This yields three minima:

```text
rūpa  → paramāṇu
nāma  → akṣara
adhvan / kāla → kṣaṇa
```

The `paramāṇu` is the limit reached when form is continually diminished. The `akṣara` is the limit of a name or linguistic expression. The `kṣaṇa` is the limit of time.

Vasubandhu then asks what measures a moment. He gives an operational definition: when the causal conditions are complete, a kṣaṇa is the time required for a dharma to attain its own occurrence (`ātmalābha`); alternatively, for a moving dharma, it is the time required to traverse the interval from one ultimate atom to the next. The Abhidharmikas add a conventional comparative measure: sixty-five moments pass during a single snap by a strong man.

The final pāda, `paramāṇur aṇus tathā`, begins the ascending scale of material measure continued in VAK 3.86.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting the Abhidharma measurement scheme.

The statement attributing sixty-five moments to a finger-snap is explicitly introduced as the view of the `ābhidhārmikāḥ`.

## New Technical Terms

- **antarāmṛtyu** — death before completion of the normal lifespan
  - **status:** newly prominent in this passage
  - **function:** qualification of realm-specific lifespan

- **niyatāyuṣ** — having a fixed lifespan
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kuru exception
  - **function:** explains why Uttarakuru beings necessarily live out their full lifespan

- **paryanta** — limiting endpoint / minimum
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya term controlling the second half
  - **function:** defines the terminal unit of form, name, and time

- **paramāṇu** — ultimate atom
  - **status:** Kārikā term expanded in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** minimal limit of rūpa

- **akṣara** — letter / syllabic unit
  - **status:** Kārikā term expanded in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** minimal limit of nāma

- **kṣaṇa** — moment
  - **status:** Kārikā term expanded in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** minimal limit of time

- **ātmalābha** — attainment of its own occurrence / becoming actual as that dharma
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary here
  - **function:** operational criterion for the duration of a kṣaṇa

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes two distinct determinations.

First, lifespan is not identical with guaranteed duration. Uttarakuru is exceptional because its inhabitants have fixed lifespans and necessarily complete them; elsewhere a being may die before reaching the maximum or normal lifespan, although special classes are exempt from premature death.

Second, the cosmological measurements are grounded in limiting units. The Kośa moves from vast world and lifespan measures to the smallest analyzable limits of three domains:

```text
form     → ultimate atom
name     → akṣara
time     → moment
```

This is not a transcendental triad. It is an Abhidharma analytic classification of the produced world-field.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā translation is substantially sound, but `antarāmṛtyu` should be understood specifically as death before completion of the lifespan, not merely as death occurring “in between” in an unspecified sense.

The Bhāṣya also confirms that `rūpa-nāma-adhva-paryantāḥ` gives three respective minima:

- `paramāṇu` — limit of form,
- `akṣara` — limit of name,
- `kṣaṇa` — limit of time.

## Logical Determination

The verse shifts from maximal duration to minimal measure:

```text
lifespan
    → possible interruption

measurement
    → search for a terminal unit
        → rūpa: paramāṇu
        → nāma: akṣara
        → kāla: kṣaṇa
```

The definition of `kṣaṇa` is especially precise: time is not introduced as an independent substance but through the minimal duration of a conditioned dharma's occurrence or movement.

## Organon Contact Point

The useful contact point is methodological rather than metaphysical:

```text
A field of measurement requires a limiting unit.
```

The Kośa has moved from cosmological magnitude to analytic granularity. The world remains produced and conditioned; the present task is to determine the smallest units by which its form, naming, and temporal course can be measured.
