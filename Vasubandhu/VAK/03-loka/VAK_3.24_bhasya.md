# VAK_3.24_bhasya — Abhidharmakośabhāṣya on VAkK 3.24

## Kārikā

```iast
sa bhaviṣyat bhavaphalaṃ kurute karma tat bhavaḥ /
pratisaṃdhiḥ punarjātiḥ jarāmaraṇamā vidaḥ // VAkK_3.24 //
```

**Best current translation:**

> The one so engaged produces karma whose fruit is future existence; that karma is becoming. Reconnection, in turn, is birth; thereafter aging-and-death extends until the feeling-stage.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_3.24.md`](./VAK_3.24.md)

### Bhāṣya correction to the first-pass reading

The Bhāṣya makes two important clarifications.

First, `bhava` is not primarily the future existence-result itself. It is the **renewal-producing karma** (`paunarbhavikaṃ karma`) accumulated while one runs after objects for acquisition.

Second, the difficult final expression is explained by the prose as extending `jarāmaraṇa` from birth **up to the feeling-stage** (`yāvad vedanāvasthā`).

### Source note

The supplied e-text reads `jarāmaraṇamā vidaḥ`. The prose immediately glosses this as `jāteḥ pareṇa yāvad vedanāvasthā jarāmaraṇam`: aging-and-death extends after birth up to the feeling-stage. The source reading is therefore preserved, while the Bhāṣya rather than conjectural emendation governs the interpretation.

---

## Natural Argument

This unit closes the first developmental exposition of the twelve members and then immediately reflects on what kind of structure that twelvefold scheme actually is.

### 1. Upādāna becomes bhava through karma accumulated for renewed existence

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The subject continues directly from 3.23: the one who runs about seeking acquisition of desired objects.

```iast
sa viṣayāṇāṃ prāptihetoḥ paridhāvan paunarbhavikaṃ karmopacinoti so 'sya bhavaḥ
```

> Running about for the sake of obtaining objects, he accumulates karma productive of renewed existence; that is his `bhava`.

This is more precise than taking `bhava` as a static state called “existence.”

Here:

```text
upādāna
    ↓
acquisitive pursuit
    ↓
karma-upacaya
    ↓
paunarbhavika karma
    ↓
bhava
```

`Bhava` is the karmic production that makes another existence possible.

The future result belongs to the karma, but the limb is named from the productive karmic determination itself.

### 2. That karma produces future pratisaṃdhi

The Bhāṣya continues:

```iast
tena hi karmaṇā punaritaḥ pracyutasyāyatyāṃ pratisaṃdhirbhavati
```

> By that karma, when one has fallen away from here, a future reconnection occurs.

The transition is therefore explicitly practical-causal:

```text
present pursuit
    ↓
renewal-producing karma
    ↓
death from this life
    ↓
future pratisaṃdhi
```

This is the point where the present middle produces the future section.

### 3. Jāti is the same structural position that was called vijñāna in the present life

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

```iast
yadeveha vijñānāṅgaṃ tadevāsyānyatra janmani jātiḥ
```

> What here is the `vijñāna` member is, for this same continuum in another birth, `jāti`.

This is a major clarification.

`Jāti` and `vijñāna` are not two completely different ontic items. They are two names applied to structurally corresponding portions of the continuum depending on the explanatory cut.

```text
this birth:
    pratisaṃdhi-stage = vijñāna

future birth viewed from here:
    same structural event = jāti
```

The twelve-member system is therefore **indexical and relational**: the name depends partly on where the stage stands within the three-birth articulation.

### 4. Jarāmaraṇa gathers the next four stages of that future life

The prose explains:

```iast
jāteḥ pareṇa yāvad vedanāvasthā jarāmaraṇam
```

> After birth, up to the feeling-stage, is aging-and-death.

And then makes the equivalence explicit:

```text
here / present birth:
    nāmarūpa
    ṣaḍāyatana
    sparśa
    vedanā

there / future birth viewed from the present:
    jarāmaraṇa
```

So again the limb-name is not an isolated substance or event. `Jarāmaraṇa` can summarize a complex five-aggregate developmental stretch when that stretch is viewed as the future result-section of the present causal series.

This completes the first twelvefold articulation.

## The Four Modes of Dependent Co-Arising

After completing the twelve members, Vasubandhu immediately says:

```iast
sa caiṣa pratītyasamutpādaś caturvidha ucyate
```

> This dependent co-arising is said to be fourfold.

He names:

```text
kṣaṇika
prakarṣika
sāṃbandhika
āvasthika
```

Provisional working translations:

```text
kṣaṇika      momentary
prakarṣika   extended / progressive
sāṃbandhika  connected / relational
āvasthika    stage-based
```

These labels should remain provisional until their use across the Bhāṣya is consolidated.

### 5. Kṣaṇika: all twelve members can be present in a single moment

Vasubandhu gives the example of killing a living being under the power of greed.

Within that single moment:

```text
moha
    = avidyā

cetanā
    = saṃskāra

vastu-prativijñapti
    = vijñāna

four aggregates co-arising with vijñāna
    = nāmarūpa

faculties established there
    = ṣaḍāyatana

impact/convergence of the spheres
    = sparśa

experience of contact
    = vedanā

lobha
    = tṛṣṇā

associated paryavasthāna
    = upādāna

body-and-speech karma arisen from it
    = bhava

emergence/release of those dharmas
    = jāti

maturation
    = jarā

breakup
    = maraṇa
```

Thus the twelvefold articulation can function at a radically different temporal scale from the three-birth presentation.

This is crucial: the twelve members are **not intrinsically twelve chronological epochs**.

They are determinations capable of being articulated across different scales.

### 6. Āvasthika: the twelve are twelve five-aggregate stages across three continuous births

The Bhāṣya defines the stage-based form explicitly:

```iast
āvasthiko dvādaśa pañcaskandhikā avasthā nirantarajanmatrayasaṃbaddhāḥ
```

> The stage-based form consists of twelve five-aggregate stages connected across three uninterrupted births.

This confirms the interpretation developing since 3.21.

Each limb is a **five-skandha stage**.

That immediately raises a question: if each member consists of the five aggregates, why call a stage `avidyā`, another `saṃskāra`, and so forth?

### 7. The members are named by predominance

The answer is explicit:

```iast
prādhānyattv aṅgakīrtanam
```

> The naming of the member is according to predominance.

Thus:

```text
stage in which avidyā predominates
    → called avidyā

stage in which saṃskāra predominates
    → called saṃskāra

...

stage in which jarāmaraṇa predominates
    → called jarāmaraṇa
```

This may be the single most important interpretive result of VAK 3.20–3.24.

The twelve limbs are not twelve independently existing substances. Nor are they merely twelve isolated mental events.

They are names assigned to complex five-aggregate stages according to the **dominant determination** in each stage.

```text
five-aggregate continuum
    ↓
stage differentiation
    ↓
predominant dharma
    ↓
member-name
```

### 8. Sūtra and Abhidharma operate at different explanatory intentions

The Bhāṣya then contrasts two formulations:

```text
Sūtra:
    twelve-member dependent co-arising

Abhidharma treatises:
    all conditioned dharmas are dependent co-arising
```

Vasubandhu states:

```iast
abhiprāyikaḥ sūtre lākṣaṇiko 'bhidharmaḥ
```

A cautious working rendering is:

> In the sūtra the teaching is according to a specific intention; in Abhidharma it is according to characteristic determination.

This distinction should not yet be over-systematized, but it clearly shows that different doctrinal presentations can be valid because they answer different explanatory purposes.

The next verse, 3.25, begins by asking why the sūtra specifically uses the sentient-being presentation.

---

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

While running about for the sake of obtaining desired objects, one accumulates karma productive of renewed existence. This renewal-producing karma is the member called `bhava`. Through that karma, after falling away from the present life, future reconnection occurs.

That reconnection is called birth. What is called the `vijñāna` member when the present life is being analyzed is called `jāti` when the corresponding event is viewed as belonging to another, future birth. What follows birth through the feeling-stage is called aging-and-death; thus the stages called name-and-form, the six spheres, contact, and feeling in the present life are gathered under aging-and-death when viewed as the future result-section.

Dependent co-arising is also taught in four modes: momentary, extended, connected, and stage-based. In the momentary mode, all twelve determinations can be articulated within a single moment. In the stage-based mode, the twelve members are twelve five-aggregate stages continuously connected across three births.

Although every such stage consists of five aggregates, each member is named for the dharma predominant in that stage. Hence an avidyā-dominant stage is called avidyā, a saṃskāra-dominant stage is called saṃskāra, and so forth through aging-and-death.

The sūtra and Abhidharma may therefore present dependent co-arising differently: the sūtra according to its pedagogical or doctrinal intention, Abhidharma according to characteristic analysis.

## The Crucial Result: The Twelvefold Wheel Is a View-Dependent Articulation of One Continuum

The Bhāṣya has now given enough information to reject a simplistic reading of the twelve links as twelve self-standing causes arranged in one fixed timeline.

Three results converge.

### A. The same structural event receives different names according to temporal position

```text
vijñāna here
    =
jāti there
```

### B. Several present-life members can be gathered into one future-life member

```text
nāmarūpa
ṣaḍāyatana
sparśa
vedanā
    ↓
viewed prospectively as future result
    ↓
jarāmaraṇa
```

### C. The entire twelvefold articulation can occur at different temporal scales

```text
one moment
    → kṣaṇika articulation

three births
    → āvasthika articulation
```

The invariant is therefore not a rigid sequence of twelve ontic objects.

The invariant is the **conditioned structure of the continuum**, articulated differently according to scale, position, predominance, and explanatory intention.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — defines bhava as renewal-producing karma, identifies jāti with the corresponding vijñāna-stage in another birth, defines jarāmaraṇa as the subsequent future-life stretch, and introduces the fourfold modes of dependent co-arising.

**Voice: Abhidharma doctrinal tradition / `punar āhuḥ`** — supplies an alternate formulation of the four modes and the treatise statement that all conditioned dharmas are dependent co-arising.

**Voice: exegetical questioner** — asks which mode the Bhagavan intends and why member-names such as avidyā are used if every member is a five-aggregate stage. The answers are incorporated into Vasubandhu's exposition.

## New Technical Terms

- **paunarbhavika karma** — karma productive of renewed existence.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific precision of `bhava` in this passage.
  - **function:** explains how present acquisitive activity produces future reconnection.

- **karma-upacaya / upacinoti** — accumulation of karma.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary, newly decisive here.
  - **function:** mediates upādāna into bhava.

- **kṣaṇika pratītyasamutpāda** — momentary dependent co-arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classification in this unit.
  - **function:** shows all twelve determinations articulated within a single moment.

- **prakarṣika pratītyasamutpāda** — extended/progressive dependent co-arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classification; translation provisional.
  - **function:** one of the four modes; further consolidation required.

- **sāṃbandhika pratītyasamutpāda** — connected/relational dependent co-arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classification; translation provisional.
  - **function:** one of the four modes; tied to connected conditioned dharmas.

- **āvasthika pratītyasamutpāda** — stage-based dependent co-arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classification, crucial here.
  - **function:** names the twelve five-aggregate stages connected through three births.

- **pañcaskandhikā avasthā** — a five-aggregate stage.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit formulation.
  - **function:** ontological/doctrinal basis of each member in the stage-based model.

- **prādhānya** — predominance, principal determination.
  - **status:** major new Bhāṣya reflective term.
  - **function:** explains why a complex five-aggregate stage is named after one dharma.

- **aṅga-kīrtana** — naming/designation of a member.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reflective vocabulary.
  - **function:** connects doctrinal names to the predominant determination of a stage.

- **abhiprāyika** — according to intention/purport.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya hermeneutic term.
  - **function:** characterizes the sūtra presentation.

- **lākṣaṇika** — according to characteristic determination.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya hermeneutic/Abhidharma term.
  - **function:** characterizes the Abhidharma presentation.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Substantive refinement required.**

The earlier line:

> “The one driven by appropriation produces karma that has future becoming as its fruit; this karmic production is the limb called becoming.”

was close, but the Bhāṣya lets us be sharper:

> **Running about to obtain desired objects, one accumulates karma productive of renewed existence; that renewal-producing karma is `bhava`.**

Likewise the final pāda should no longer be left unresolved. The Bhāṣya interprets it as:

> **Aging-and-death extends after birth up to the feeling-stage.**

A Bhāṣya-informed translation of the verse is therefore:

> Running after acquisition, one produces karma whose fruit is future existence; that renewal-producing karma is becoming. Reconnection is, in turn, birth; thereafter aging-and-death extends up to the feeling-stage.

## Logical Determination

The first half of the verse completes the practical causal movement:

```text
feeling
    ↓
craving
    ↓
appropriation
    ↓
search / pursuit
    ↓
karma accumulation
    ↓
bhava
    ↓
future reconnection
```

The second half then reveals a more sophisticated logic of classification.

The same continuum can be recoded according to standpoint:

```text
present analytic standpoint:
    vijñāna → nāmarūpa → ṣaḍāyatana → sparśa → vedanā

future-result standpoint:
    jāti → jarāmaraṇa
```

The member is therefore not simply an atom in a chain. It is a **determinate view of a structured stage**.

And `prādhānya` supplies the principle of designation:

```text
complex stage
    ↓
dominant determination
    ↓
member-name
```

This is a genuine reflective theory of classification internal to the Bhāṣya itself.

## Organon Light

The Organon should notice three things here, without importing them into Vasubandhu's doctrine.

### 1. Conditioned teleology becomes reproductive action

VAK 3.23 showed desire becoming practical pursuit. VAK 3.24 shows what that pursuit **does**:

```text
object sought as end
    ↓
action organized toward acquisition
    ↓
karma accumulated
    ↓
future state projected
```

This is a complete practical arc, but one governed by conditioned desire rather than Freedom.

The distinction for a future Science of Practical Reason is sharp:

```text
conditioned teleology:
    end supplied by tṛṣṇā / upādāna
    → reproduces bondage

rational teleology:
    end supplied by Knowledge / Freedom
    → must transform the conditions of agency
```

The Kośa is giving us a technically exact negative model of agency: **how an Agent reproduces the very world that conditions it**.

### 2. A member is a View, not merely a thing

The equivalence

```text
vijñāna here = jāti there
```

is striking from an Organon standpoint.

The object has not simply changed. The **systematic role under which the same conditioned occurrence is known has changed**.

Likewise:

```text
nāmarūpa + ṣaḍāyatana + sparśa + vedanā
    = jarāmaraṇa
```

when the same future course is viewed from the present causal standpoint.

That is extremely close to what an Agential View system needs: a single real process can receive distinct rational determinations according to **position, range, purpose, and relation to the knower's explanatory task**.

This is not yet our Organon doctrine. But Vasubandhu has now supplied the kind of machinery that makes such a doctrine plausible.

### 3. `Prādhānya` looks like a principle of abstraction

Every āvasthika member is a five-aggregate stage, but the stage receives one name because one determination predominates.

```text
rich concrete state
    ↓
principal determination selected
    ↓
logical/doctrinal name
```

An Organon reading should remember this.

A concept need not pretend that its object contains only the feature named by the concept. It may name a concrete structured whole **through the determination that is principal for the current science**.

That is a much stronger account of classification than simple feature-labeling.

And it is already Vasubandhu's own move.
