# VAK_3.57_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ihottareṇa kīṭādri navakād dhimavān tataḥ /
pañcāśadvistṛtāyāmaṃ saro 'rvāg gandhamādanāt // VAkK_3.57 //
```

**Working translation:**

Here, to the north, beyond the nine Kīṭa mountains, is Himavān; beyond it lies a lake fifty yojanas in breadth and length, on this side of Gandhamādana.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya turns the compressed geographical sequence of the Kārikā into an ordered regional description within Jambūdvīpa.

First, Vasubandhu specifies what lies northward within Jambūdvīpa. He describes three black mountains, then another three, then yet another three. After passing these nine mountain barriers lies Himavān.

Beyond Himavān, but before Gandhamādana, lies a lake named **Anavatapta**. Four rivers flow from it:

```text
Anavatapta
    → Gaṅgā
    → Sindhu
    → Śītā
    → Vakṣu
```

The lake is fifty yojanas in breadth and fifty in length. Its water is said to possess eight qualities. It is difficult of access for human beings who do not possess ṛddhi.

Vasubandhu then adds an etymological-geographical determination absent from the Kārikā: near this lake grows the **Jambū** tree, whose fruits are sweet. Jambūdvīpa is said to receive its name from this tree, or alternatively from its fruit.

The argument therefore moves:

```text
northward within Jambūdvīpa
    → nine mountain barriers
        → Himavān
            → Anavatapta lake
                → four rivers
                    → Jambū tree
                        → name Jambūdvīpa
```

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — the geographical explanation, naming of Anavatapta, river list, dimensions, accessibility, and derivation of the name Jambūdvīpa.
- **Voice: alternative explanation** — `tatphalādhikāreṇa vā`, the alternative that the continent is named with reference to the fruit of the Jambū tree rather than simply the tree itself.

## New Technical Terms

- **kṛṣṇaparvata** — black mountain
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage
  - **function:** expands the compressed mountain-series of the Kārikā

- **atikramya** — having passed beyond
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory verb
  - **function:** establishes serial spatial ordering

- **Anavatapta** — Anavatapta
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** proper name of the lake between Himavān and Gandhamādana

- **nadī** — river
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** introduces the four rivers issuing from Anavatapta

- **aṣṭāṅga / aṣṭāṅgopeta ambhas** — water endowed with eight qualities
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only descriptive determination here
  - **function:** qualifies the lake water

- **ṛddhimat** — possessing ṛddhi / extraordinary power
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** distinguishes those capable of reaching the lake from ordinary humans

- **Jambū** — Jambū tree
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** supplies the naming-ground of Jambūdvīpa

- **adhikāra** — reference / predominating association
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** explains the derivation of the continent-name from the tree or its fruit

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya confirms that this passage is not merely a list of isolated names. It produces a determinate regional order within Jambūdvīpa by means of sequence, relative location, measure, hydrology, accessibility, and naming.

The Kārikā gave:

```text
nine mountains
    → Himavān
        → lake
            → Gandhamādana
```

The Bhāṣya unfolds this into:

```text
Jambūdvīpa
    → northern mountain barriers
        → Himavān
            → Anavatapta
                → four river systems
                    → Jambū tree
                        → denomination of Jambūdvīpa
```

Thus the prose does more than clarify a difficult compound. It shows how the named world-region is internally articulated and how one of its principal names is grounded in a determinate feature of that region.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Substantive clarification.**

The first-pass caution around `kīṭādri navakāt` can now be reduced. The Bhāṣya clearly interprets the expression as a sequence of nine mountains northward in Jambūdvīpa, arranged as three groups of three, beyond which lies Himavān.

The anonymous `saraḥ` of the Kārikā is identified as **Anavatapta**, situated beyond Himavān and on this side of Gandhamādana. Its dimensions are confirmed as fifty yojanas by fifty yojanas.

A revised philosophical translation may therefore read:

> Here in Jambūdvīpa, to the north beyond the nine Kīṭa mountains, stands Himavān; beyond it and before Gandhamādana lies Lake Anavatapta, fifty yojanas in breadth and fifty in length.

## Logical Determination

```text
regional orientation
    → serial boundary
        → named mountain
            → named water-source
                → generated river-directions
                    → regional denomination
```

The important reflective gain is the passage from **placement** to **relational articulation**. The Kārikā gives coordinates in compressed form; the Bhāṣya expresses how those coordinates belong together as a local world-system.

The Jambū tree adds a further reflective determination:

```text
regional thing
    → characteristic feature
        → name of the whole region
```

The whole receives a designation through one of its salient internal determinations.

## Organon Contact Point

This remains a derivative determination of produced Loka. We should not elevate the mountain-lake-river sequence into a hidden transcendental architecture. Its philosophical value is more restrained and more exact: the Bhāṣya shows a produced world becoming intelligible through reciprocal spatial relations, measurable extension, generated flows, accessibility, and naming.

That is fully consistent with the current distinction between the Principle and its later determinate expansion. The living mediation is preserved by tracing how each local determination receives its meaning from its relation to the others, without confusing any one of them with the Principle itself.
