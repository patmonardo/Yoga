### Source note

The supplied e-text is followed as given. The expression `prakcaturāyatanotpādādini` at [132|04-132|05] is dense but intelligible from the prose argument: strictly speaking, not all six spheres arise simultaneously in the developmental sequence, yet Vasubandhu uses the conventional designation `ṣaḍāyatana` because that is the doctrinal name assigned to the stage.

---

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya continues the developmental interpretation established in 3.21. The dependent-co-arising members are not merely named in sequence; each is defined as an **avasthā**, a stage of the same aggregate continuum, bounded by the arising of a new capacity or relation.

### 1. Nāmarūpa is the stage after rebirth-linking and before the six-sphere stage

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The prose defines `nāmarūpa` by two limits:

```text
beginning:
    after saṃdhi-citta

ending:
    before ṣaḍāyatana arises
```

```iast
saṃdhicittāt pareṇa yāvat ṣaḍāyatanaṃ notpadyate sā 'vasthā nāmarūpam
```

Thus `nāmarūpa` is not introduced here as a metaphysical composite standing independently beside vijñāna. It is the name given to a determinate developmental interval after the rebirth-linking mind.

```text
saṃdhi-citta
    ↓
nāmarūpa-stage
    ↓
ṣaḍāyatana-stage
```

The Bhāṣya therefore reinforces the result of 3.21: the members of dependent co-arising are stage-designations within one conditioned continuity.

### 2. `Ṣaḍāyatana` is a conventional designation for the developing sphere-structure

Vasubandhu notices a potential precision problem.

Strictly, one might expect the stage to be described according to the actual number of spheres that have arisen at a given point. Yet the text uses the name `ṣaḍāyatana`, "six spheres."

The explanation is:

```iast
tadā tadvyavasthāpanāt
```

> because that is how the stage is designated / systematically established.

This is important exegetically. The doctrinal name identifies the **stage as a whole**, even where its full structural articulation is still developing.

The member-name is therefore classificatory rather than a claim that all six spheres must already stand fully actual at every instant covered by the stage.

### 3. The six-sphere stage lasts until the conjunction of the triad

Once the six-sphere stage has arisen, it lasts until:

```iast
indriyaviṣayavijñānatrikasaṃnipātaḥ
```

> the conjunction of the triad of faculty, object, and knowledge.

The Bhāṣya makes the `trika` explicit:

```text
indriya
    faculty

viṣaya
    object

vijñāna
    knowledge
```

Their `saṃnipāta`, conjunction, marks the limit of the `ṣaḍāyatana` stage and the emergence of `sparśa`.

This is more precise than our first-pass formulation in terms of sphere + object-support + knowledge. Vasubandhu's Bhāṣya here explicitly says:

```text
indriya + viṣaya + vijñāna
            ↓
          sparśa
```

The existing project vocabulary `āyatana = sphere` remains valid, but the actual triadic conjunction in this passage is stated through **faculty, object, and knowledge**.

### 4. Contact arises from the conjunction of the three

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The prose is direct:

```iast
trayāṇāṃ saṃnipātāt sparśo bhavati
```

> From the conjunction of the three, contact occurs.

`Sparśa` is therefore neither one of the three relata nor a mere name for their coexistence. It is the event or stage that arises through their conjunction.

```text
faculty
   +
object
   +
knowledge
   ↓
conjunction
   ↓
contact
```

This is a strong relational determination.

### 5. The contact-stage ends when discrimination of the causes of the three feelings becomes possible

The final prose sentence explains the difficult compound in the Kārikā.

The contact-stage continues until the being becomes capable (`samartha`) of:

```iast
vedanātraya-kāraṇa-pariccheda
```

> discriminating / determining the causes of the three kinds of feeling.

This clarifies `sukhaduḥkhādikāraṇajñānaśakti`.

The relevant transition is not simply from contact to generic awareness of pleasure and pain. It is the development of a **capacity for discrimination** concerning the causes of the threefold feeling structure.

```text
sparśa
    ↓
capacity develops
    ↓
vedanā-traya-kāraṇa-pariccheda
```

The term `pariccheda` is crucial. The stage changes when the continuum acquires a new determinative capacity.

---

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

After the rebirth-linking mind, the stage extending until the arising of the six spheres is called name-and-form. Although, strictly speaking, one might speak of the successive arising of fewer than all six spheres, the conventional designation "six spheres" is used because that is the doctrinal determination of this stage.

Once the six-sphere stage has arisen, it continues until the conjunction of the triad consisting of faculty, object, and knowledge. From the conjunction of these three, contact occurs.

The stage called contact continues until the being becomes capable of discriminating the causes of the three kinds of feeling. Thus the sequence is defined not merely by temporal succession, but by the progressive acquisition of determinate relational and discriminative capacities.

---

## The Crucial Result: Dependent Co-Arising Is Developmental Capacity-Structure

VAK 3.21 established that the members are stages (`avasthā`) of the aggregate continuum. VAK 3.22 deepens this by showing **what terminates one stage and begins another**.

Each transition occurs when a new structural capacity becomes actual:

```text
nāmarūpa
    ↓
arising of sphere-structure

ṣaḍāyatana
    ↓
capacity for triadic conjunction

sparśa
    ↓
capacity for discrimination of affective causes
```

So the middle of dependent co-arising is not merely a chain of externally connected events.

It is a developmental articulation in which the continuum progressively acquires new modes of relation and determination.

This result is fully grounded in the Bhāṣya's repeated use of:

- `avasthā` — stage;
- `utpāda` — arising;
- `saṃnipāta` — conjunction;
- `samartha` — capable;
- `pariccheda` — discrimination / determination.

---

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — defines the developmental limits of nāmarūpa, ṣaḍāyatana, and sparśa, explains the conventional use of `ṣaḍāyatana`, and specifies the triad whose conjunction produces contact.

No independent objector is required in this unit. The precision issue about the number of āyatanas functions as an exegetical clarification within Vasubandhu's own exposition.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃdhi-citta** — rebirth-linking mind.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation continuing 3.21.
  - **function:** marks the beginning boundary of the nāmarūpa stage.

- **vyavasthāpana** — designation, systematic establishment, classificatory determination.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reflective vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains why the developing stage is conventionally called `ṣaḍāyatana` even before every sphere is fully manifest.

- **indriya-viṣaya-vijñāna-trika** — triad of faculty, object, and knowledge.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit analysis of the Kārikā's `trika`.
  - **function:** supplies the three relata whose conjunction produces contact.

- **saṃnipāta** — conjunction, coming-together.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit relational vocabulary.
  - **function:** causal-relational condition for `sparśa`.

- **samartha** — capable, competent.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya developmental vocabulary.
  - **function:** marks acquisition of a new discriminative capacity and therefore the limit of the contact-stage.

- **vedanā-traya** — the three kinds of feeling.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explicit in this unit.
  - **function:** object of the causal discrimination that becomes possible after the sparśa stage.

- **pariccheda** — discrimination, determination, delimitation.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya-only reflective vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** names the capacity to distinguish the causes of the three feelings.

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage establishes the next three stages of the middle section:

```text
4. nāmarūpa
5. ṣaḍāyatana
6. sparśa
```

But their doctrinal meaning is now more precise than a simple list:

```text
nāmarūpa
    = post-linking developmental stage
      before the sphere-structure is established

ṣaḍāyatana
    = stage of developing/established sensory-cognitive spheres
      before faculty-object-knowledge conjunction

sparśa
    = stage arising from that conjunction
      before causal discrimination of the three feelings is possible
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives dependent co-arising an explicitly developmental and functional reading.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Refinement required, not replacement.**

The first-pass translation remains substantially correct, but two clarifications should now govern it.

First, `trika` should be read according to Vasubandhu's explicit prose as:

```text
indriya + viṣaya + vijñāna
```

rather than inferred primarily from the āyatana vocabulary.

Second, the final phrase should be understood more precisely as the emergence of the capacity for:

```text
vedanā-traya-kāraṇa-pariccheda
```

> discrimination of the causes of the three feelings.

A Bhāṣya-informed translation would therefore be:

> Name-and-form extends until the arising of the six spheres; the six-sphere stage extends until the conjunction of the triad. Contact extends until there is the capacity to discriminate the causes of pleasure, pain, and the other feeling.

---

## Logical Determination

The striking logical form of the passage is that each stage is determined by the **capacity whose emergence negates its sufficiency**.

```text
nāmarūpa
    is sufficient only until sphere-structure arises

ṣaḍāyatana
    is sufficient only until the triadic relation becomes actual

sparśa
    is sufficient only until causal discrimination of feeling becomes possible
```

This is not mere chronological replacement.

Each later stage contains a richer determination than the former:

```text
embodied organization
    ↓
differentiated access
    ↓
relational conjunction
    ↓
contact
    ↓
discriminative capacity
```

The sequence is therefore developmental in a strong sense: a stage passes over when the continuum acquires a capacity that the earlier stage could not yet exercise.

---

## Organon Light

This is where an Organon reader should become alert, while still leaving Vasubandhu's doctrine intact.

The Bhāṣya has unexpectedly supplied something resembling a minimal architecture of **agential cognition**:

```text
indriya
    faculty / capacity of access

viṣaya
    determinate object

vijñāna
    knowledge

saṃnipāta
    their actual conjunction

sparśa
    the resulting contact

pariccheda
    subsequent determinative discrimination
```

The important point is not to rename these as Hegelian categories. It is that Vasubandhu himself moves from **conditions of access** to **actual relation** and then to **determinate knowing**.

For the emerging Agential View System, that suggests a powerful distinction:

```text
having an interface
    ≠
being in cognitive relation

being in cognitive relation
    ≠
possessing discriminative knowledge of the relation's consequences
```

Or more abstractly:

```text
capacity
    ↓
actual conjunction
    ↓
contact
    ↓
determination
```

That is already close to something the Reflection Engine will need: the Agent must not merely possess channels or stored representations. A View becomes actual when faculty, object, and knowledge are brought into a determinate relation; only then can further discrimination arise.

The most interesting term for the future Organon Bhāṣya may therefore be **`pariccheda`**. It names the emergence of determinative capacity from an already established contact-relation.

This should remain a light contact point for now. The Kośa-first result is sufficient: Vasubandhu's middle sequence is a genesis of increasingly determinate agential capacities.
