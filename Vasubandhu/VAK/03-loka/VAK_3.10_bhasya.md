# VAK_3.10_bhasya — Antarābhava as the Intermediate Transition

## Kārikā

### IAST

```iast
mṛtyupapattibhavayor antarā bhavatīha yaḥ /
gamyadeśānupetatvān nopapanno 'ntarābhavaḥ // VAkK_3.10 //
```

### Best Translation

That which comes to be between death-existence and rebirth-existence is, because it has not yet reached the destination-place, not yet reborn: it is the intermediate existence.

## Segmentation by Natural Argument

The unit has five movements.

### 1. Definition Requested

```iast
ko 'yam antarābhāvo nāma /
```

**Voice: Objector / interlocutor**

What is this so-called intermediate existence?

The Bhāṣya does not assume that `antarābhava` is self-explanatory. It asks for its exact determination.

### 2. Positive Definition

```iast
maraṇabhavasyopapattibhavasya cāntarā ya ātmabhāvo 'bhinirvarttate
deśāntaropapattisamprāptaye so 'ntarābhava ityucyate /
gatyantarālatvāt /
```

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

An `ātmabhāva` comes forth between death-existence and rebirth-existence for the purpose of reaching rebirth in another place. This is called `antarābhava`, because it occupies the interval between destinies.

The prose makes the Kārikā more precise. `antarābhava` is not merely temporal “between-ness.” It is oriented toward a determinate subsequent arising.

```text
death-existence
    ↓
intermediate existence
    ↓
destination-directed transition
    ↓
rebirth-existence
```

### 3. Why Is It Born but Not Yet Reborn?

```iast
kathamayaṃ jātaśca nāma nacopapanno bhavati /
```

**Voice: Objector**

How can it be called arisen and yet not be `upapanna`, reborn?

The answer turns on the semantic force of the verbal root in `upapanna`.

```iast
upapattigato hyupapanna ityucyate /
pader gatyarthatvāt /
```

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

One is called `upapanna` when one has gone into rebirth, because the verbal root `pad` carries the sense of going.

Thus birth in the intermediate phase and arrival at the new rebirth-position are distinct determinations.

### 4. Destination Defined Through Vipāka

The Bhāṣya asks:

```iast
kaḥ punar asau deśo gantavyaḥ /
```

What is that destination-place which is to be reached?

Answer:

```iast
yatrākṣiptasya vipākasyābhivyaktiḥ samāptiśca /
```

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

It is the place where the projected karmic result (`ākṣipta-vipāka`) becomes manifest and reaches completion.

This is the decisive development of the Kārikā. `gamya-deśa` is not an arbitrary geographical endpoint. It is defined through the manifestation and completion of a determinate `vipāka`.

```text
gamya-deśa
    =
place of manifestation and completion
of projected karmic result
```

The destination is therefore already internally related to karma, though the full doctrine of karma remains deferred to Chapter 4.

### 5. The Discontinuity Objection

A doctrinal opponent now denies the intermediate state:

```iast
vicchinna evopapattibhavo maraṇabhavāt saṃbhavatīti nikāyāntarīyāḥ /
```

**Voice: uncertain — attributed in the text to the Nikāyāntarīyas**

The rebirth-existence arises from the death-existence with a break; there is no continuous intermediate existence.

Vasubandhu rejects this:

```iast
tad etan neṣyate /
```

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

This is not accepted.

He then announces the two standards by which the claim will be tested:

```iast
yuktitaś ca āgamataś ca /
```

```text
yukti
    reasoning

āgama
    authoritative textual testimony
```

The actual proof begins with VAkK 3.11. Thus 3.10 closes naturally at the announcement of the twofold proof rather than absorbing the next Kārikā's argument.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Prose Translation

“What is this thing called intermediate existence?

That personal configuration which comes forth between death-existence and rebirth-existence for the attainment of rebirth in another place is called intermediate existence, because it occupies the interval between destinies.

How, then, can this be said to have arisen but not yet to be reborn?

One who has gone into rebirth is called ‘reborn,’ because the verbal root *pad* has the sense of going. But this intermediate existence has not reached the destination-place; therefore it is not yet reborn.

What, then, is that place which is to be reached?

It is that in which the projected karmic result becomes manifest and reaches completion.

The Nikāyāntarīyas say that rebirth-existence arises directly from death-existence with a break. This is not accepted. Why? On the basis both of reasoning and of authoritative teaching.”

### Philosophical Translation

The intermediate existence is the determinate transitional configuration produced between death and rebirth in order to attain a new rebirth-position. It is already arisen, but it is not yet `upapanna`, because rebirth in the strict sense means arrival at the destination. That destination is itself defined as the locus in which the karmic result already projected becomes manifest and completes its realization.

An opposing school denies such continuity and claims that rebirth follows death discontinuously. Vasubandhu rejects this position and announces that the existence of an intermediate phase must be established by two independent controls: rational argument and authoritative testimony.

## Course of the Argument

### Question

What exactly is `antarābhava`, and how can it be real without already being the subsequent rebirth?

### Initial Determination

It is an arisen intermediate configuration situated between `maraṇabhava` and `upapattibhava` and directed toward another rebirth-position.

### Distinction

```text
jāta
    arisen

upapanna
    arrived into the subsequent rebirth
```

These are not equivalent.

### Ground of the Distinction

`Upapanna` contains the sense of movement into the new rebirth-position. The intermediate being has arisen but has not yet reached that position.

### Further Determination

The destination is not defined merely spatially. It is where an already projected `vipāka` manifests and reaches completion.

### Objection

The Nikāyāntarīya position claims a discontinuous transition directly from death to rebirth.

### Resolution Status

Not yet fully resolved in this unit. Vasubandhu rejects the discontinuity claim and announces a twofold proof:

```text
yukti
+
āgama
```

The reasoning begins in VAkK 3.11.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya converts a cosmological representation into a transition theory.

The naive image would be:

```text
someone dies
someone travels
someone is reborn somewhere else
```

But the Bhāṣya's actual determinations are:

```text
maraṇabhava
    prior terminal state

antarābhava
    arisen transition-state

ākṣipta-vipāka
    already projected result

gamya-deśa
    locus of its manifestation/completion

upapattibhava
    realized subsequent birth-state
```

The important relation is therefore not primarily a travelling individual moving through mythic space. It is the continuity of a conditioned series toward the manifestation of an already determined result.

The opponent's discontinuity thesis makes the underlying logical problem explicit:

```text
Can a later determinate state arise
from the prior terminal state
without a continuous mediating series?
```

Vasubandhu answers: no—not merely by assertion, but by a proof to be supplied through both `yukti` and `āgama`.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing translation is structurally sound, but the Bhāṣya sharpens two points:

1. `gamya-deśa` is not merely a destination-place in a geographical sense; it is the locus where projected karmic result manifests and completes itself.
2. `nopapannaḥ` means not yet arrived into the subsequent rebirth-position, not simply “not born.”

A slightly improved philosophical translation is therefore:

> That which arises between death-existence and rebirth-existence is the intermediate existence. Since it has not yet reached the destination in which the projected result is to become manifest, it has not yet entered the subsequent rebirth.

## Organon Contact Point

The “Logic of Bollywood” is becoming unusually precise.

Representation gives us:

```text
demons
heavens
hells
miraculous births
intermediate beings
cosmic destinations
```

But Abhidharma asks what relations make those representations intelligible.

Here the apparent travelling being is reconstructed as:

```text
terminal state
    ↓
mediating continuity
    ↓
projected determination
    ↓
manifestation
    ↓
completed result
```

The spectacle is therefore subordinated to the Dharma-relation.

The important point is not that a fantastic creature flies from one world to another. The important point is that **a determinate result cannot simply appear from nowhere**. The transition requires mediation, and the mediation must itself be rationally defensible.

That is why Vasubandhu's final move in this unit matters so much:

```text
yukti + āgama
```

The image is permitted to remain only after Reason has tested its structure.

## Summary

VAkK 3.10 defines `antarābhava` as the arisen but not-yet-arrived phase between death and subsequent rebirth. The Bhāṣya deepens the definition by identifying the destination as the place where an already projected karmic result becomes manifest and reaches completion. An opposing school denies any mediating continuity and posits a discontinuous leap from death to rebirth. Vasubandhu rejects that claim and announces that the matter must be established through both reasoning (`yukti`) and authoritative teaching (`āgama`).

The formal advance is decisive:

```text
mythic transition
    ↓
causal continuity
    ↓
rational proof
```
