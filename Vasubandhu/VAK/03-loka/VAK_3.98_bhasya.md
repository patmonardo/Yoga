# VAK_3.98 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast

tataḥ karmapathādhikyād apahrāse daśāyuṣaḥ // VAkK_3.98 //
```

**Working translation:** Because of laziness, storage was made; with possessive appropriation, a protector of fields was maintained. Thereafter, through the increase of courses of action, lifespan declined to ten years.

## Natural Argument

The genealogy begun in VAK 3.97 now reaches social differentiation.

At first the naturally arising rice is taken only as needed for each meal. A lazy being begins to store it. Others imitate him. Once storage exists, `mamakāra`—the making of something as "mine"—arises. The rice then no longer regenerates automatically after harvesting.

Fields are divided. Beings establish claims over their own portions (`sveṣv āgrahaṃ kṛtvā`) and begin taking what belongs to others. Vasubandhu marks this explicitly as the first beginning of theft (`prathama ārambhaś cauryasya`).

To restrain this disorder, the community appoints a distinguished person to guard the fields and supports him with a sixth share. Because he is lord over the fields, the designation `kṣatriya` arises. Because he is approved by the great mass of people and pleases the populace, the designation `Mahāsammata Rāja` arises. Vasubandhu calls this the beginning of the succession of kings.

The Bhāṣya then continues the degeneration: greed in a ruler, failure of distribution, increased theft, punishment by weapons, and false speech. As these karmapathas increase, human lifespan progressively declines until beings with a ten-year lifespan appear.

Vasubandhu closes the passage by naming two root sources of the whole flood of misfortune: `rasarāga`, attachment to savor, and `ālasya`, laziness.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is explanatory narrative and doctrinal genealogy. No distinct objector intervenes.

## New Technical Terms

- **mamakāra** — making something "mine"; possessive appropriation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this immediate sequence
  - **function:** marks the emergence of property-possession from storage

- **āgraha** — taking possession, laying claim, appropriative holding
  - **status:** clarifies the Kārikā's compressed `sāgrahaiḥ`
  - **function:** mediates the transition from divided fields to theft

- **kṣetrapa / kṣetrāṇām adhipati** — protector / lord of the fields
  - **status:** central to this Bhāṣya explanation
  - **function:** origin of political authority

- **ṣaṣṭhabhāga** — sixth share
  - **status:** Bhāṣya detail absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** material support given to the field-protector

- **Mahāsammata Rāja** — king approved by the great multitude
  - **status:** Bhāṣya genealogy of kingship
  - **function:** names the first king as socially authorized ruler

- **rājaparaṃparā** — succession of kings
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation here
  - **function:** marks the beginning of royal lineage/order

- **anarthaugha** — flood/mass of misfortune
  - **status:** Bhāṣya summary term
  - **function:** gathers the entire degenerative sequence under one result

## Doctrinal Determination

The Kārikā compresses a long genealogy into a few terms. The Bhāṣya makes the sequence explicit:

```text
ālasya
    → storage
    → mamakāra
    → division of fields
    → āgraha / possessive claim
    → theft
    → field-protector
    → kṣatriya / Mahāsammata king
    → greed and failed distribution
    → theft increases
    → punishment
    → false speech
    → karmapathas multiply
    → lifespan declines to ten years
```

The political order is therefore not primordial. It arises inside produced Loka as a response to possessive conflict.

Vasubandhu's final diagnosis is especially important:

```text
mūlayoni of the entire anarthaugha:
    rasarāga
    ālasya
```

Attachment to savor begins the material descent; laziness initiates storage and the social descent into possession and conflict.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required.**

The first-pass rendering of `sāgrahaiḥ` as merely "with claims" was too weak. The Bhāṣya shows that it belongs to a concrete sequence of `mamakāra`, division of fields, taking possession of one's own portion, and stealing another's. A stronger translation such as **"with possessive claims"** or **"through appropriation"** better reflects the argument.

Likewise `kṣetrapa` is not an abstract field-guardian only; the Bhāṣya identifies this office as the origin of `kṣatriya` and kingship.

## Logical Determination

The immediate logic is genealogical:

```text
need
    → storage
    → possession
    → exclusion
    → transgression
    → coercive authority
```

Then ethical degeneration feeds back into biological/cosmic decline through the increase of karmapathas.

This remains a genealogy within conditioned Loka. It is not a transcendental derivation of political authority from a higher Principle.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
private possession generates the need for public authority.
```

The significant feature for the larger project is that social institutions appear here as derivative formations inside a produced world-field. The Bhāṣya's causal sequence is concrete and historical-cosmological rather than transcendental.
