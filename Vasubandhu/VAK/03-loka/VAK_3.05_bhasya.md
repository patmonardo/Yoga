# VAK_3.05 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> nānātvakāyasaṃjñāś ca nānākāyaikasaṃjñinaḥ /
> viparyayāc caikakāyasaṃjñāś cārūpiṇas trayaḥ // VAkK_3.5 //

**Best current translation:**  
Those with diverse bodies and diverse perceptions; those with diverse bodies and one perception; by reversal, those with one body and diverse or one perception; together with the three formless classes.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_3.05.md`](./VAK_3.05.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Principal source range: Pradhan [115|17] through [117|04], continuing naturally into the naming of the seven `vijñānasthitayaḥ` in VAkK 3.6.

The Bhāṣya begins:

```iast
atraiva pañcagatike dhātutraye yathākramaṃ veditavyāḥ

nānātvakāyasaṃjñāśca nānākāyaikasaṃjñinaḥ /
viparyayāccaikakāyasaṃjñāścārūpiṇasrayaḥ // VAkK_3.5 //

vijñānasthitayaḥ sapta
```

The transmitted e-text reads `cārūpiṇasrayaḥ`; as in the Kārikā file, this is treated cautiously as the compressed/corrupt reading corresponding to `ca ārūpiṇaḥ trayaḥ`.

The prose then cites the sūtra classification of the first four embodied knowledge-stations and concludes with the three formless ones.

## Continuous Translation

Within these very three domains and five destinies, the following are to be understood in order.

There are form-possessing beings whose bodies are diverse and whose perceptions are diverse, such as human beings and certain devas. This is the first `vijñānasthiti`, or station of knowledge. Their bodies are diverse because their color, sex-characteristics, and bodily configurations are manifold. Their perceptions are diverse because they possess perceptions of pleasure, pain, and neither-pain-nor-pleasure.

There are form-possessing beings whose bodies are diverse but whose perception is one, namely the Brahmakāyika devas who are first produced there. This is the second station of knowledge.

There are form-possessing beings whose bodies are one but whose perceptions are diverse, namely the Ābhāsvara devas. This is the third station of knowledge. The Bhāṣya explains the diversity of their perception through the differing responses of recently born and long-established Ābhāsvaras to the cosmic fire: some become frightened, while others, knowing the cycle of contraction and expansion, reassure them. Thus their diversity of perception is grounded in the differing apprehension of the coming and going of the flame and in fear versus non-fear, rather than simply in pleasure and neutral feeling.

There are form-possessing beings whose bodies are one and whose perception is one, namely the Śubhakṛtsna devas. This is the fourth station of knowledge. Their bodily unity is explained through non-difference of color, configuration, and sex-characteristics; their unity of perception through the common perception of pleasure.

The Bhāṣya then summarizes the embodied sequence: in the first dhyāna, unity of perception is associated with an afflicted perception; in the second, diversity of perception with a wholesome perception; in the third, unity of perception with a resultant perception. The three formless stations are then taken as established according to the sūtra. These together are the seven `vijñānasthitayaḥ`.

## Argument Reconstruction

The prose makes explicit that VAkK 3.5 is not merely a list of beings. It constructs a formal matrix inside the already established world-field.

```text
given:
    three dhātus
    five gatis

introduce two discriminating features:
    kāya
        diverse / one

    saṃjñā
        diverse / one

result:
    four embodied configurations

then add:
    three formless configurations

result:
    seven vijñānasthitis
```

The four embodied possibilities are therefore:

```text
1. nānākāya + nānāsaṃjñā
2. nānākāya + ekasaṃjñā
3. ekakāya  + nānāsaṃjñā
4. ekakāya  + ekasaṃjñā
```

This is a genuine feature matrix. The text is no longer asking simply *where* beings are, but by what determinate structure embodiment and perception are configured within the world.

## Voices

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma voice** — introduces the sevenfold structure within the previously established three domains and five destinies.
- **Voice: sūtra citation** — supplies the first four `vijñānasthiti` formulas and their representative classes.
- **Voice: Sautrāntika position** — appears in the explanation of the differing perception of the Ābhāsvaras, especially the distinction between tranquil pleasure and the mentally agitating character of `saumanasya`; attribution is explicit in the prose immediately before the cosmic-fire example.

## New Technical Terms

- **vijñānasthiti** — station or establishment of knowledge.
  - **status:** introduced as the name of the classification completed by VAkK 3.5–3.6.
  - **function:** names a determinate configuration in which knowledge is established within the conditioned world-field.

- **nānātvakāya / nānākāya** — bodily diversity.
  - **status:** made technically explicit by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** classificatory feature grounded in differences of color, bodily form, and sex-characteristics.

- **nānātvasaṃjñā / nānāsaṃjñā** — diversity of perception.
  - **status:** made technically explicit by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** classificatory feature grounded in differing felt or apprehended modes.

- **ekatvakāya / ekakāya** — bodily unity.
  - **status:** made technically explicit by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** classificatory feature where bodily characteristics are non-different.

- **ekatvasaṃjñā / ekasaṃjñā** — unity of perception.
  - **status:** made technically explicit by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** classificatory feature where a class shares one dominant perceptual mode.

- **abhinirvṛtta / prathamābhinirvṛtta** — newly or initially produced in a given realm.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary here.
  - **function:** distinguishes newly arisen members of a deva-class from those long established there.

- **saṃvartanīkuśala / vivartanīkuśala** — skilled or knowledgeable regarding cosmic contraction / expansion.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** explains why members of the same bodily class can nevertheless differ in perception.

## Translation Consequences

The Bhāṣya confirms that `saṃjñā` here should not be translated as an abstract “concept.” It functions as the perceptual-recognitional determination by which beings apprehend pleasure, pain, neutrality, danger, safety, and the meaning of events in their world.

The first-pass translation of `kāya` as “body-configuration” is also strengthened. The prose explicitly grounds bodily diversity in `varṇa`, `liṅga`, and `saṃsthāna`: color, sex-characteristics, and configuration.

The first-pass treatment of `viparyaya` should be kept restrained. In the Kārikā it primarily instructs us to reverse the preceding feature combination. The Bhāṣya supplies the actual reversed cases; it does not here develop `viparyaya` as a doctrine of epistemic error. Any stronger Yoga connection belongs to the later Organon Comment.

## Doctrinal / Logical Determination

The decisive formal result is:

```text
gati
    does not exhaust the determination of a being.

Within a gati and dhātu,
a being-continuity is further determinable by:
    bodily configuration
    perceptual configuration
```

Thus the hierarchy of analysis becomes:

```text
dharma
    ↓
dhātu
    ↓
gati
    ↓
kāya / saṃjñā configuration
    ↓
vijñānasthiti
```

This is a Dharma-first construction. A `vijñānasthiti` is not inferred from an ordinary zoological or mythological grouping. The class is generated by formally specified determinations already operating within the Dharma-field.

The Ābhāsvara example is especially important because it shows that:

```text
same bodily class
    does not imply
same perceptual determination
```

Perception differs according to the operative relation to an event and to the knowledge available within the continuum. Newly arisen beings encounter the flame as danger; those who know the cosmic cycle apprehend the same event differently.

So `saṃjñā` is not merely a passive sensory label. It is a determinate mode under which a world-event is recognized.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This gives a useful refinement of the “Principled Gati Theory.”

```text
Naive gati theory:
    there are humans, devas, hell-beings, etc.

Dharma-first gati theory:
    establish the domain
    distinguish the destiny
    determine the bodily configuration
    determine the perceptual configuration
    only then identify the resulting station of knowledge
```

The crucial point is methodological:

```text
Formal cognition
    does not begin from the image of a creature.

It begins from determinations.
```

And the reciprocal relation discovered earlier now becomes finer:

```text
world-field
    determines possible configurations of embodiment and perception

while

embodied-perceptual configurations
    articulate the world-field as distinct vijñānasthitis
```

So the `vijñānasthiti` is neither merely subjective nor merely objective. It is a structured relation of a sentient continuity to a determinate world-mode.

This is an important development for the later Organon Comment: **Loka becomes formally cognizable only through Dharma-determinations that mediate world and being reciprocally.** Gati is therefore principled rather than naive, and `vijñānasthiti` is the next refinement of that principle.

## Summary

VAkK 3.5 constructs the first four embodied `vijñānasthitis` through a two-feature matrix of `kāya` and `saṃjñā`, then adds the three formless stations. The Bhāṣya gives concrete criteria for bodily sameness/difference and perceptual sameness/difference and shows, through the Ābhāsvara example, that identical bodily configuration does not entail identical apprehension of the world.

The formal movement is:

```text
dhātu
    → gati
        → kāya/saṃjñā configuration
            → vijñānasthiti
```

This is a Dharma-first, principled analysis of world-being rather than a naive taxonomy of beings.
