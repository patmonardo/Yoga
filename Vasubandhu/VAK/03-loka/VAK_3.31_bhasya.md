## Natural Argument

VAK 3.30 established six contacts according to their cognitive support and mode: five `pratigha-saṃsparśa` and one `adhivacana-saṃsparśa`. VAK 3.31 now reclassifies those same six contacts along three different axes.

The important point is that these are **cross-classifications of sparśa**, not a new temporal sequence.

### 1. The six contacts become three according to vidyā, avidyā, or neither

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Abhidharma exposition**

```iast
punasta eva ṣaṭ sparśāstrayo bhavanti
```

> Again, those very six contacts become three.

The three are:

```text
vidyā-saṃsparśa
avidyā-saṃsparśa
naiva-vidyā-nāvidyā-saṃsparśa
```

That is:

```text
contact associated with vidyā
contact associated with avidyā
contact associated with neither vidyā nor avidyā
```

The first-pass term `itara` is therefore made completely explicit by the Bhāṣya: it means **neither-vidyā-nor-avidyā contact**.

### 2. Their doctrinal correlates are anāsrava, kliṣṭa, and the remainder

The Kārikā's compact:

```iast
amalakliṣṭaśeṣitāḥ
```

is expanded as follows.

#### Vidyā-contact

```iast
anāsravaḥ sparśo vidyāsaṃsparśaḥ
```

> An uncontaminated contact is contact associated with vidyā.

So `amala` is not merely a poetic “pure.” In this context it is concretely identified with **anāsrava**, uncontaminated.

#### Avidyā-contact

```iast
kliṣṭo 'vidyāsaṃsparśo vidyā 'vidyābhyāṃ saṃprayuktatvāt
```

> Defiled contact is contact associated with avidyā, by association with avidyā.

The e-text wording is compressed, but the doctrinal function is clear: `avidyā-saṃsparśa` is the **kliṣṭa** class.

#### Neither-vidyā-nor-avidyā contact

```iast
śeṣo naivavidyānāvidyāsaṃsparśa ubhābhyāmasaṃprayogāt
```

> The remainder is neither-vidyā-nor-avidyā contact because it is not associated with either.

Vasubandhu then asks what the remainder is:

```iast
kuśalasāsravo 'nivṛtāvyākṛtaśca
```

The remainder includes:

```text
kuśala-sāsrava
    wholesome but contaminated

anivṛta-avyākṛta
    unobstructed-indeterminate
```

This is a major correction to any simplistic binary model.

```text
vidyā / avidyā
```

does **not** exhaust all contact.

There is a third field containing contact that is neither associated with liberating knowledge nor presently defiled by avidyā.

### 3. A recurrent portion of avidyā-contact divides into aversion and attraction

Vasubandhu next takes a particular portion (`ekadeśa`) of avidyā-contact characterized by recurrent manifestation:

```iast
avidyāsaṃsparśasyābhīkṣṇasamudācāriṇa ekadeśasya grahaṇāt
```

and derives two contacts:

```iast
vyāpādānunayasparśau
```

These are produced by association with:

```text
vyāpāda
    aversion / hostility

anunaya
    attraction / following-after / inclination
```

The important point is not simply a pleasant/unpleasant binary. These are **afflictive orientations of contact** within the avidyā-conditioned field.

```text
avidyā-contact
    ↓
frequently manifest portion
    ↓
vyāpāda | anunaya
```

### 4. All contacts can finally be gathered according to three feeling consequences

Vasubandhu then says:

```iast
punaḥ sarvasaṃgraheṇa
```

> Again, by a comprehensive collection...

and gives:

```text
sukha-vedanīya
    contact associated with / conducive to pleasant feeling

duḥkha-vedanīya
    contact associated with / conducive to painful feeling

aduḥkhāsukha-vedanīya
    contact associated with / conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
```

The Bhāṣya provides two ways to understand `vedanīya`.

First:

```iast
sukhavedanādihitatvāt
```

The contact is so called because of its relation to pleasant feeling and so forth.

Second, through a more explicit derivation:

```iast
vedyate tadvedayituṃ vā śakyamiti vedanīyam
```

> That which is experienced, or can be made the object of experiencing, is `vedanīya`.

Hence:

```iast
sukhaṃ vedanīyamasminniti sukhavedanīyaḥ sparśaḥ
```

> It is pleasant-feeling-bearing contact where pleasant experience is present.

The same construction applies to painful and neutral feeling.

### 5. The full contact taxonomy now totals sixteen

Vasubandhu closes the section:

```iast
ta ete ṣoḍaśa sparśā bhavanti /
uktaḥ sparśaḥ //
```

> Thus these are sixteen contacts. Contact has been explained.

This is a significant structural marker. The Bhāṣya has finished its detailed analysis of `sparśa` and is ready to turn to `vedanā`.

---

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

The six contacts previously described may again be classified in three ways: as contact associated with knowledge, contact associated with ignorance, and contact associated with neither knowledge nor ignorance. Respectively, these are uncontaminated contact, defiled contact, and the remainder. The remaining class consists of wholesome contaminated contact and unobstructed-indeterminate contact.

From a frequently manifest portion of ignorance-contact, two further types are distinguished according to their association with aversion and attraction. Finally, gathering all contacts comprehensively according to feeling, there are three: contact bearing pleasant feeling, contact bearing painful feeling, and contact bearing neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling.

Thus the analysis yields sixteen classified contacts in all, and the exposition of contact is complete.

---

## Doctrinal Determination

The crucial result is that `sparśa` is not a bare, homogeneous event.

The same contact-field can be classified according to different principles:

```text
1. cognitive-afflictive condition
   vidyā | avidyā | neither

2. contamination status
   anāsrava | kliṣṭa | remainder

3. afflictive orientation
   vyāpāda | anunaya

4. feeling-bearing character
   sukha | duḥkha | aduḥkhāsukha
```

These classifications are not interchangeable.

In particular:

```text
vidyā-contact
    = anāsrava

avidyā-contact
    = kliṣṭa

neither-contact
    = kuśala-sāsrava
      or anivṛta-avyākṛta
```

This third category is doctrinally important because it prevents us from flattening experience into a simple opposition between wisdom and ignorance.

---

## Logical Determination

VAK 3.31 demonstrates **multiple determination of one field according to distinct classificatory grounds**.

```text
same six contacts
    ↓ viewed under one determination
vidyā / avidyā / neither

same contact-field
    ↓ viewed under another determination
aversion / attraction

same contact-field
    ↓ viewed under another determination
pleasant / painful / neutral
```

The relation is therefore not:

```text
one contact = one fixed label
```

but:

```text
one contact-event
    can bear several determinations
    according to distinct principles of classification
```

This continues the methodological lesson of 3.25: doctrinal classification is not ontological atomization.

## New Technical Terms

- **naiva-vidyā-nāvidyā-saṃsparśa** — contact associated with neither vidyā nor avidyā.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit expansion of the Kārikā's `itara`.
  - **function:** third term preventing an exhaustive vidyā/avidyā binary.

- **anāsrava** — uncontaminated.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal term; newly decisive for this contact taxonomy.
  - **function:** defines the class of `vidyā-saṃsparśa`.

- **kuśala-sāsrava** — wholesome but contaminated.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit in this unit.
  - **function:** one component of neither-vidyā-nor-avidyā contact.

- **anivṛta-avyākṛta** — unobstructed-indeterminate.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit in this unit.
  - **function:** second component of neither-vidyā-nor-avidyā contact.

- **abhīkṣṇa-samudācārin** — frequently or repeatedly manifesting.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific descriptive vocabulary.
  - **function:** qualifies the portion of avidyā-contact from which the aversion/attraction pair is abstracted.

- **vyāpāda** — aversion, hostility.
  - **status:** present in Kārikā, clarified by Bhāṣya association.
  - **function:** afflictive orientation qualifying a contact.

- **anunaya** — attraction, inclination, following-after.
  - **status:** present in Kārikā, clarified by Bhāṣya association.
  - **function:** afflictive orientation qualifying a contact.

- **sarva-saṃgraha** — comprehensive gathering/classification.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reflective vocabulary.
  - **function:** signals regrouping of all contacts under the three feeling-bearing classes.

- **vedanīya** — that which is to be experienced / bears or admits a determinate feeling.
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly derived in Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** relation between contact and the three modes of feeling.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Refinement required.**

The first-pass translation was broadly correct, but the Bhāṣya specifies two points more precisely.

First, `amala` should here be read through the explicit gloss `anāsrava`, **uncontaminated**, rather than merely “pure.”

Second, `itara` is not an undefined residue. It is explicitly:

```text
naiva-vidyā-nāvidyā-saṃsparśa
```

and contains:

```text
kuśala-sāsrava
anivṛta-avyākṛta
```

A Bhāṣya-informed translation is therefore:

> Contacts are associated with knowledge, ignorance, or neither; these are respectively uncontaminated, defiled, and the remaining contacts. There are also contacts of aversion and attraction, and three contacts characterized by pleasant, painful, or neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling.

## Organon Light

The Organon should notice here that Vasubandhu is already doing something much more sophisticated than assigning one type to one event.

The same event-domain is subjected to **orthogonal determinations**.

```text
Contact
    │
    ├── epistemic-afflictive determination
    │      vidyā | avidyā | neither
    │
    ├── contamination determination
    │      anāsrava | kliṣṭa | remainder
    │
    ├── motivational polarity
    │      vyāpāda | anunaya
    │
    └── affective consequence
           sukha | duḥkha | neutral
```

That is very close to what an Agential View would require: **the event is one, but its rational significance depends upon the determination under which it is viewed**.

The most important restraint is equally clear. These are not arbitrary analyst-selected tags. Each classification has a doctrinal ground (`saṃprayoga`, contamination status, affective association, feeling relation).

So the Organon lesson is not merely “use multiple labels.” It is:

```text
A valid View
    requires a principle of determination.
```

And the third class is especially important:

```text
neither vidyā nor avidyā
```

because a rational classifier must permit **determinate remainder**, rather than forcing every case into a false binary.

That is an extremely useful lesson for the future Organon Bhāṣya, while remaining completely subordinate here to Vasubandhu's own contact taxonomy.

## Transition to VAK 3.32

The Bhāṣya closes this unit explicitly:

```iast
uktaḥ sparśaḥ
```

> Contact has been explained.

Then:

```iast
vedanā vaktavyā
```

> Feeling must now be explained.

VAK 3.32 therefore begins a new natural sub-unit: the six feelings born from the six contacts, divided into five bodily and one mental feeling.
