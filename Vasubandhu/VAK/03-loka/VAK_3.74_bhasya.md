## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya explains the two further stages of recursive cosmological aggregation.

```text
1,000 sāhasra-cūḍika world-domains
    → dvisāhasra madhyama lokadhātu

1,000 dvisāhasra world-domains
    → trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra lokadhātu
```

The final aggregate is then characterized by a shared cosmic process:

```text
samaṃ saṃvartate
    → contracts / dissolves together

samaṃ vivartate
    → expands / evolves together
```

Vasubandhu explicitly states that `saṃbhava` in the verse means `vivarta` and postpones the fuller explanation to the later cosmological-cycle discussion.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — direct explanation of both aggregation levels and of `samasaṃvartasaṃbhava`.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

A thousand of those small thousandfold world-domains is the middle, second-order thousandfold world-domain.

A thousand of those second-order thousandfold world-domains is the third-order great thousandfold world-domain.

This whole [system] contracts together and expands together. For `saṃbhava` here means `vivarta`; this will be explained later.

### Philosophical Translation

The previously established thousandfold world-domain becomes the unit of a second recursion, and that second-order aggregate becomes the unit of a third. The resulting great world-domain is not merely numerically grouped: its constituent worlds participate in one common cycle of contraction and expansion.

## Course of the Argument

### Initial Determination

`tat-sahasram` is explicitly iterative: one thousand of the immediately preceding cosmological aggregate forms the next level.

### Result

```text
world-unit
    × 1,000
        → sāhasra-cūḍika
            × 1,000
                → dvisāhasra madhyama lokadhātu
                    × 1,000
                        → trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra lokadhātu
```

The highest aggregate in this sequence has a common `saṃvarta / vivarta` cycle.

## New Technical Terms

- **dvisāhasra madhyama lokadhātu** — middle second-order thousandfold world-domain
  - **status:** Kārikā term clarified by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** names the aggregation of one thousand sāhasra-cūḍika world-domains

- **trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra lokadhātu** — third-order great thousandfold world-domain
  - **status:** expanded Bhāṣya terminology
  - **function:** names the aggregation of one thousand dvisāhasra world-domains

- **saṃvarta** — contraction / dissolution
  - **status:** important cosmological-cycle term
  - **function:** contraction phase shared by the great world-domain

- **vivarta** — expansion / evolution
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term for `saṃbhava` here
  - **function:** expansion phase opposed to `saṃvarta`

- **saṃbhava** — arising
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly glossed
  - **function:** in this passage, identified with `vivarta`

## Doctrinal Determination

The world-system is presented as recursively scalable and dynamically unified. The highest aggregate is not merely a collection of independent world-units; it shares one process of contraction and expansion.

```text
aggregation
    → common cosmic process
```

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and refinement.**

`samasaṃvartasaṃbhavaḥ` should be translated dynamically as:

> “contracting and expanding together”

rather than more loosely as “sharing common dissolution and arising.” The Bhāṣya explicitly glosses `saṃbhava` as `vivarta`.

## Logical Determination

```text
recursive quantity
    → aggregate totality
        → common process
```

The important shift is from repeated numerical aggregation to coordinated becoming. The many world-units form a determinate totality insofar as they undergo contraction and expansion together.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only: this remains produced Loka. The structurally interesting point is that quantitative recursion terminates not in a merely larger heap, but in a domain defined by a shared process of arising and dissolution.
