## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya identifies the center of Sumeru's summit as the city Sudarśana and expands the compressed measures and qualities of the verse.

```text
center of Sumeru summit
    → Sudarśana
        → 2,500 yojanas on each side
        → 1½ yojanas in height
        → golden wall
        → ground variegated with 101 substances
        → soft, responsive surface
        → capital of Śakra
```

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose expanding the verse.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

In the middle of the surface of Sumeru is the city named Sudarśana. In length it is two thousand five hundred yojanas, each side being so measured, and in height it is one and a half yojanas.

Its enclosing wall is golden. Its ground is variegated with one hundred and one substances.

That ground is soft to the touch like cotton fluff; with the placing and lifting of the foot it sinks and rises. It is the capital of Śakra, the Indra of the gods.

### Philosophical Translation

At the center of Sumeru's summit stands Sudarśana, the capital of Śakra. The Bhāṣya fixes its dimensions precisely and presents it as a sensuously refined constructed environment: enclosed by gold, patterned through a multiplicity of substances, and possessing a ground that responds elastically to the movements of its inhabitants.

## Course of the Argument

### Initial Determination

The city occupies the center of Sumeru's summit.

### Measurement

The difficult numerical compounds of the Kārikā are resolved as:

```text
side / length: 2,500 yojanas
height: 1½ yojanas
```

### Material Determination

The wall is golden, and the ground is variegated with 101 substances.

### Sensuous Determination

The ground is compared to cotton fluff in softness and responds to walking by sinking beneath the placed foot and rising again when the foot is lifted.

### Result

Sudarśana is identified as the `rājadhānī`, the capital, of Śakra, Indra of the gods.

## New Technical Terms

- **rājadhānī** — capital city
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** identifies Sudarśana as the political center of Śakra's realm

- **prākāra** — enclosing wall / rampart
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** architectural specification

- **ucchrāya** — height / vertical extent
  - **status:** previously encountered as a measure-type
  - **function:** resolves the vertical measure in the Kārikā

- **dhātu** — substance / constituent in this local architectural context
  - **status:** previously encountered, but with a different local function
  - **function:** specifies the variegated composition of the city's ground

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya turns the compressed description of Sudarśana into a complete habitation-form. The summit-world is not merely occupied by devas; it contains a central capital with measure, enclosure, material differentiation, tactile qualities, and political function.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The Bhāṣya resolves the previously provisional numerical reading:

```text
sārdhadvisāhasrapārśva
    = 2,500 yojanas on each side

adhyardhayojana
    = 1½ yojanas in height
```

The Kārikā file's phrase “with sides of two-and-a-half thousand and a measure of one-and-a-half yojanas” should therefore eventually be sharpened to make the height explicit.

## Logical Determination

```text
summit field
    → center
        → city
            → measured enclosure
                → differentiated material surface
                    → responsive habitation
```

The Bhāṣya's work is one of progressive specification: the city becomes determinate through exact measure, material, tactile quality, and civic function.

## Organon Contact Point

The relevant point is modest: produced Loka becomes increasingly concrete through determinate relations and qualities. Sudarśana is not a transcendent principle but a highly articulated product-world whose form is specified spatially, materially, sensuously, and politically.
