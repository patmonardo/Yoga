## Natural Argument

### 1. Vasubandhu asks two different questions

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya first distinguishes:

```text
Which manopavicāras belong to a realm?

and

Which realms can serve as their ālambana?
```

This distinction is decisive. `pratisaṃyukta` concerns the realm to which the mental approach belongs; `ālambana` concerns what it ranges over.

So the same manopavicāra can be:

```text
located in one realm
    yet
have another realm as object-support
```

The verse therefore describes both **location** and **range**.

### 2. In kāmadhātu, all eighteen manopavicāras are present

```iast
kāmadhātau sarve 'ṣṭādaśa santi
```

> In the desire realm, all eighteen exist.

And:

```iast
sa ca teṣāṃ sarveṣām ālambanam
```

> It is also the object-support of all of them.

Thus kāmadhātu has the fullest articulation of the eighteenfold field:

```text
6 saumanasya-upavicāras
6 daurmanasya-upavicāras
6 upekṣā-upavicāras
= 18
```

All six object-fields are available there.

### 3. Rūpadhātu is the object-range of only twelve

The Bhāṣya explains the restriction explicitly:

```iast
rūpadhātur dvādaśānām ālambanaṃ
ṣaṭ gandharasopavicārān apāsya /
tatra tayor abhāvāt /
```

> The form realm is the object-support of twelve, excluding the six approaches concerned with odor and taste, because those two are absent there.

So:

```text
18 total
− 3 odor approaches
− 3 taste approaches
= 12
```

The restriction is not arbitrary; it follows from the constitution of the realm.

### 4. Ārūpyadhātu is the object-range of only three

The word `gocara` carries forward:

```iast
ārupyadhātus trayāṇāṃ dharmopavicārāṇām ālambanam
```

> The formless realm is the object-support of the three dharma-approaches.

Why only three?

```iast
tatra rūpādyabhāvāt
```

> Because form and the other sensory objects are absent there.

Therefore only:

```text
saumanasya-dharmopavicāra

daurmanasya-dharmopavicāra

upekṣā-dharmopavicāra
```

can take the formless realm as their object-support.

### 5. In the first two dhyānas there are twelve manopavicāras

The Bhāṣya next shifts from object-range to the manopavicāras belonging to rūpadhātu.

For the first and second dhyānas:

```iast
dhyānadvaye dvādaśa
```

Why twelve rather than eighteen?

```iast
daurmanasyopavicārān apāsya
```

> excluding the approaches of mental displeasure.

Thus:

```text
6 saumanasya
+ 6 upekṣā
= 12
```

The six daurmanasya-approaches do not occur there.

### 6. Those twelve can take kāmadhātu as their object-support

```iast
te ca dvādaśa kāmagāḥ
```

Vasubandhu glosses:

```iast
kāmān gacchantīti kāmagāḥ /
kāmadhātum ālabanta ity arthaḥ /
```

> They are called `kāmagāḥ` because they go toward desire; that is, they take the desire realm as their object-support.

He then explains that verbs of going can be used in the sense of apprehending an object-field:

```iast
dṛṣṭo hi viṣayāṇāṃ grahaṇārthe gamiprayogaḥ
```

So `kāmagāḥ` should not be translated as literal spatial movement. It expresses **object-directedness**.

---

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

Among the eighteen mental approaches, all eighteen occur in the desire realm, and the desire realm can serve as the object-support of all eighteen. The form realm, however, can serve as the object-support of only twelve, because the six approaches concerned with odor and taste are excluded, those two object-fields being absent there. The formless realm can serve as the object-support only of the three approaches concerned with dharmas, because form and the other sensory objects are absent there.

With regard to the approaches belonging to the form realm itself, twelve occur in the first two dhyānas, the six approaches of mental displeasure being absent. These twelve may nevertheless take the desire realm as their object-support; this is the meaning of calling them `kāmagāḥ`.

## Doctrinal Determination

The important Bhāṣya distinction is:

```text
realm-membership
    ≠
object-range
```

A mental approach belongs to a determinate realm, but its `ālambana` may lie elsewhere.

The mapping in this verse is:

```text
KĀMADHĀTU
    18 manopavicāras present
    kāmadhātu can be ālambana of all 18

RŪPADHĀTU as object-support
    range of 12
    excludes odor and taste approaches

ĀRŪPYADHĀTU as object-support
    range of 3
    only dharma-upavicāras

FIRST TWO DHYĀNAS
    12 manopavicāras present
    excludes all daurmanasya-upavicāras
    all 12 can take kāmadhātu as ālambana
```

This makes the eighteenfold system a genuine **realm × affect × object-field** architecture rather than a simple list.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratisaṃyukta** — connected with / belonging to a realm.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary, newly central here.
  - **function:** distinguishes the realm-membership of a mental approach from its object-support.

- **kāmaga** — going toward the desire realm; desire-directed.
  - **status:** verse-specific formation.
  - **function:** marks a manopavicāra whose object-support is kāmadhātu.

- **gami-prayoga** — use of a verb of going.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya exegetical vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains how `gacchati` can mean reaching/apprehending an object-field rather than literal locomotion.

- **gocara** — field / range.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** marks the range within which a mental approach can operate.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The Bhāṣya substantially clarifies the first-pass translation.

The most important correction is that `rūpī dvādaśagocaraḥ / trayāṇām uttaraḥ` concerns **what can be taken as object-support**, not simply how many approaches exist in those realms.

A Bhāṣya-informed translation is therefore:

> In the desire realm all eighteen occur, and that realm is the object-support of all of them. The form realm is the range of twelve, and the higher, formless realm is the range of three. In the first two dhyānas twelve occur; those twelve can take the desire realm as their object-support.

---

## Logical Determination

The logical structure is not merely hierarchical inclusion. It is a distinction between **where an operation is constituted** and **what field it can determine**.

```text
operation-state
    has a realm of belonging

operation-state
    has an object-support

these need not coincide
```

That matters because the Abhidharma model allows cross-realm directedness while preserving the determination of the knowing state itself.

The restrictions on range are immanent to the constitution of each realm:

```text
absence of gandha/rasa
    → six approaches impossible as rūpadhātu-object ranges

absence of rūpa etc.
    → only dharma-upavicāras remain for ārūpyadhātu
```

So the field determines the admissible operations.

## Organon Light

This is a particularly clean Agential-View contact point, but it should remain separate from Vasubandhu's doctrine.

An Organon would immediately distinguish:

```text
where a View is constituted
    from
what the View is about
```

In platform language, the passage suggests a primitive distinction like:

```text
View {
    supportRealm
    objectSupport
    gocara
}
```

The crucial point is not the software analogy. It is the logical separation:

```text
Determination of the knower-state
    ≠
Determination of the known field
```

And the permitted `gocara` is constrained by what the field actually contains.

This is Hegel-clean enough to retain as a light marker: determination is not externally assigned; the admissible range follows from the determinate constitution of the field.

## Bridge to VAK 3.34

The argument is not finished. VAK 3.34 continues the distribution for the form realm:

```text
first two dhyānas
    → 12

third and fourth dhyānas
    → 6 upekṣā-upavicāras only
```

and then specifies which lower, own, and higher realms may serve as their object-supports.
