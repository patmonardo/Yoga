# VAK_3.51_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ardhārdhahāniraṣṭāsu samocchrāyaghanāśca te /
śītāḥ saptāntarāṇyeṣāṃ ādyāśītisahasrikā // VAkK_3.51 //
```

**Working translation:**

Across the mountain sequence there is successive halving; their height and thickness are equal. The seven intervals between them are the Śītās; the first is eighty thousand yojanas wide.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.51.md`.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya converts the compressed proportional rule into a descending series: from Sumeru's height above the water, Yugandhara is half, Īśādhara half again, and so on outward. It also defines `samocchrāyaghana`: each mountain's horizontal thickness equals its height above the water.

The seven intervals between the inner mountain-rings are called Śītās. Their water has eight qualities: cool, sweet, light, soft, clear, non-disagreeable, non-irritating to the throat, and non-distressing to the belly. The first Śītā, between Sumeru and Yugandhara, is eighty thousand yojanas wide.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — descriptive and numerical explanation.
- No independent opponent appears.

## New Technical Terms

- **ardhārdhahāni** — successive diminution by halves
  - **function:** proportional law of the mountain sequence

- **ghana** — thickness / breadth
  - **function:** horizontal dimension equal to height

- **śītā** — inner sea / cool-water interval
  - **function:** names the seven waters between mountain-rings

- **aṣṭāṅgopeta pānīya** — water endowed with eight qualities
  - **function:** qualitative determination of the inner seas

- **vaipulya** — breadth / width
  - **function:** measure of the first Śītā

## Doctrinal Determination

The produced world is articulated by a repeated proportional rule and by alternating mountain and water regions. The inner waters are not neutral extension; they possess a determinate sensible quality.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Bhāṣya confirms the halving rule, equality of height and thickness, and the qualitative meaning of the Śītās.
