# VAK_3.15 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 3.15**

```iast
viparyastamatir yāti gatideśaṃ riraṃsayā /
gandhasthānābhikāmo 'nyaḥ ūrdhvapādas tu nārakaḥ //
```

**Best current translation**

> With inverted understanding, it goes toward the place of its destiny through longing. Another is drawn toward a scent-place; but the hell-bound intermediate being goes with its feet upward.

## Bhāṣya Source

Primary passage: Pradhan [126|19–127|25].

The e-text is non-critical; exact readings are preserved below where they matter.

```iast
sa khalveṣa gatideśasaṃprāptyarthaṃ prādurbhūto 'ntarābhavaḥ
```

```iast
viparyastamatir yāti gatideśam riraṃsayā /
```

```iast
sa hi karmaprabhāvasaṃbhūtena cakṣuṣā sudūrastho 'pi svamupapattideśaṃ prekṣate /
```

The prose then explains the distorted apprehension associated with entry into womb-birth, before turning to the other birth-modes.

For moisture-birth:

```iast
gandhasthānābhikāmo 'nyaḥ
```

```iast
saṃsvedajāṃ yoniṃ pratipadyamāna upapattisthānaṃ gatvābhilāṣāt gacchatyamedhyaṃ medhyaṃ vā yathākramam /
```

For spontaneous birth:

```iast
upapādukāṃ tu yoniṃ prapadyamānaḥ sthānābhilāṣāt /
```

The Bhāṣya then asks:

```iast
kathaṃ narakeṣu sthānābhilāṣaḥ /
```

and answers:

```iast
viparyastabuddhitvāt /
```

It explains that a being tormented by cold may see fire in the hells and run toward it seeking warmth; one tormented by heat may see cold there and run toward it seeking relief.

The older teachers add:

```iast
yad avasthas tad upapattisaṃvarttanīyaṃ karmākārṣīt tadavasthātmānaṃ tāśca sattvān paśyan dhāvatīti pūrvācāryāḥ /
```

Finally the orientation of the intermediate being is distinguished:

```iast
tatra punardevāntarābhava ūrdhvaṃ gacchatyā sanādivottiṣṭhan manuṣyatiryakpretānāṃ manuṣyādivat /
```

```iast
ūrdhvapādastu nārakaḥ // VAkK_3.15 //
```

followed by a supporting verse in which hell-bound beings fall feet-upward and face-downward.

The natural unit closes at [127|25]. At [127|26] the Bhāṣya begins the next question: whether every intermediate being enters the mother's womb in this same manner.

## Segmentation by Natural Argument

### 1. Antarābhava arises for attainment of its destiny-place

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The prose explicitly states the purpose of the intermediate existence:

```text
antarābhava
    arises for the sake of attaining gati-deśa
```

This is important. The transition-being is not an autonomous entity with an open itinerary. Its function is already determined by the destination projected in the preceding analysis.

### 2. It sees its future rebirth-place through karmically produced vision

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The intermediate being sees its own future `upapatti-deśa` even from far away by an eye produced through the power of karma:

```text
karma-prabhāva-saṃbhūta cakṣus
    ↓
perception of one's own future rebirth-place
```

The perceptual field is therefore itself karmically conditioned.

### 3. Womb-birth is approached under inverted cognition and desire

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The key phrase is:

```iast
viparyasta-matiḥ ... riraṃsayā
```

The transition does not occur through neutral cognition. The destination is apprehended under distortion and approached through longing.

The Bhāṣya's role is not to introduce a free decision-maker. It shows how attraction functions inside an already projected causal course.

### 4. Other birth-modes are attracted by other supports

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

For moisture-born beings the attraction is toward a place associated with smell; for spontaneously arisen beings the attraction is directly toward a place.

Thus the prose differentiates the mode of attraction according to `yoni`.

```text
yoni
    determines the relevant mode of approach
```

### 5. Why would anyone desire a hell-place?

**Voice: Objector**

```iast
kathaṃ narakeṣu sthānābhilāṣaḥ /
```

How can there be desire for a place in the hells?

### 6. The answer: inverted understanding

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

```iast
viparyastabuddhitvāt /
```

Because the understanding is inverted.

The Bhāṣya gives two symmetric cases:

```text
experienced cold
    + appearance of fire
    → desire for warmth
    → rush toward destination

experienced heat
    + appearance of cold
    → desire for coolness
    → rush toward destination
```

The hell-destination is therefore not desired *as hell*. It appears under a distorted presentation as the sought remedy for present distress.

### 7. Older teachers: karmic re-presentation of the state in which the deed was performed

**Voice: uncertain** — explicitly attributed to `pūrvācāryāḥ`.

The older teachers say that the being sees itself and other beings in the very condition in which it previously performed the karma that matures into that rebirth.

This is a stronger representational thesis:

```text
past karmic condition
    ↓
re-presented appearance
    ↓
transition toward corresponding rebirth
```

### 8. Orientation differs by destination

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The deva intermediate being moves upward; human, animal, and preta transition-beings move in modes corresponding to their destinations; the hell-bound intermediate being is specifically described as `ūrdhvapāda`, feet-upward.

The supporting verse reinforces the directional image.

## Literal Prose Translation

“This intermediate existence arises for the purpose of reaching the place of its destiny. With inverted understanding it goes toward the place of its destiny through longing. For, even when very far away, it sees its own place of rebirth by means of an eye produced through the power of karma.

Another, desiring a place of scent: one entering a moisture-born mode of birth, having reached the place of rebirth, goes toward what is impure or pure respectively through desire. One entering a spontaneous mode of birth does so through desire for the place.

How can there be desire for a place in the hells? Because its understanding is inverted. Seeing itself afflicted by cold wind and rain, and seeing fire blazing in the hells, it runs there from desire for warmth. Again, seeing itself afflicted by hot wind, sun, and fire, and seeing coolness in the hells, it runs there from desire for cold.

The former teachers say that it runs while seeing itself in the state in which it performed the karma productive of that rebirth, and seeing those beings likewise.

The intermediate existence of a deva goes upward; those of humans, animals, and pretas move correspondingly. But the hell-being goes with its feet upward.”

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya converts the vivid rebirth imagery into a determinate causal structure:

```text
prior karmic projection
    ↓
fixed future gati
    ↓
karmically conditioned perception
    ↓
distorted presentation of destination
    ↓
longing / attraction
    ↓
motion toward gati-deśa
```

This is crucial: desire does **not** create an otherwise undetermined destination. The destination has already been projected. Desire and distorted apprehension are the proximate mode through which the transition is carried toward it.

So:

```text
ākṣepa
    determines the course

viparyasta-mati + riraṃsā
    mediate its experiential pursuit
```

The hell example is especially clear. The being does not positively know “this is hell” and choose it. The destined place appears under a false aspect as relief from present suffering.

This makes `viparyaya` genuinely operative in the machinery of Loka:

```text
false appearance
    becomes transition-effective
```

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — primary explanation of the function, vision, attraction, and orientations of antarābhava.
- **Voice: Objector** — “How can there be desire for a place in the hells?”
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — answer through `viparyastabuddhitva` and the hot/cold examples.
- **Voice: uncertain** — the alternative explanation explicitly attributed to `pūrvācāryāḥ`.

No final doctrinal commitment beyond the presentation should be inferred from the older-teacher attribution alone.

## New Technical Terms

- **karma-prabhāva-saṃbhūta-cakṣus** — an eye produced through the efficacy/power of karma
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** causal explanation of how the intermediate being perceives its distant rebirth-place

- **upapatti-deśa** — place of rebirth
  - **status:** prose sharpening of the Kārikā's `gati-deśa`
  - **function:** destination specification

- **viparyasta-buddhitva** — condition of having inverted/distorted understanding
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā in this explicit nominal form
  - **function:** resolution of the objection concerning attraction toward hell

- **upapatti-saṃvartanīya-karma** — karma conducing to / productive of a particular rebirth
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** older-teacher causal explanation

- **pūrvācārya** — former/earlier teacher
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only speaker attribution
  - **function:** doctrinal attribution

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 3.15 is not simply a mythology of spirits attracted to smells, sex, heat, or cold. The Bhāṣya provides a layered determination:

```text
gati
    fixed result-direction

yoni
    mode of entry

karma
    projection and perceptual conditioning

viparyaya
    false presentation

riraṃsā / abhilāṣa
    affective attraction

gamana
    transition toward completion
```

The important logical distinction is:

```text
destination-determination
    ≠
conscious recognition of destination
```

A being can be objectively oriented toward one result while subjectively apprehending the path under distortion.

That is a powerful Dharma-first result. The truth of the process lies in its determining relations, not in the transition-being's representation of what is happening.

---

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation should be sharpened in two respects.

1. `riraṃsayā` is not merely generic “desire.” In context it is the longing through which the karmically projected destination is approached.
2. `viparyasta-mati` should remain strong: **inverted / distorted understanding**, not a neutral reversal.

Recommended translation:

> With inverted understanding, it goes toward the place of its destiny through longing. Another is drawn toward a scent-place; but the hell-bound intermediate being goes with its feet upward.

The Bhāṣya clarifies that the destination is already karmically fixed; longing explains the proximate mode of approach, not sovereign selection.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage gives a clean distinction between **objective determination** and **subjective appearance**:

```text
objective:
    karmically projected gati

subjective:
    viparyasta appearance
    + attraction toward what appears desirable
```

Thus ignorance does not suspend Law. It is one of the forms under which Law operates within appearance.

A compact formulation for the later Organon Comment:

```text
The worldling does not need to know the determining ground
for the determining ground to be effective.
```

Or, in the present Loka sequence:

```text
Karma fixes the course.
Viparyaya makes the course appear otherwise.
Desire carries the continuity into the already determined result.
```

That is enough for the second pass; the larger synthesis belongs to the later Comment.
