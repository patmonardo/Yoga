### Source Caution

The compressed Kārikā wording cannot be safely parsed from the verse alone. The Bhāṣya supplies the arithmetic explicitly. In particular, `sonarātraḥ` must be read through Vasubandhu's explanation in terms of `ūnarātra`, a periodically omitted or deficient night, rather than as a generic reference to day-and-night.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya completes the scale of ordinary temporal measure begun in VAK 3.88.

First, the smaller units are aggregated:

```text
30 lavas
    = 1 muhūrta

30 muhūrtas
    = 1 ahorātra

30 ahorātras
    = 1 māsa
```

Vasubandhu notes that the night is not always equal in length: sometimes it is longer, sometimes shorter, sometimes equal. The unit `ahorātra` therefore names the complete day-night cycle without requiring a permanently equal division between daylight and night.

Twelve months make the year:

```text
4 months of hemanta
+ 4 months of grīṣma
+ 4 months of varṣā
= 12 months
= 1 saṃvatsara
```

But the year is not simply twelve idealized thirty-day months. Six `ūnarātra`s are dropped in the course of the year. Vasubandhu gives a mnemonic verse specifying their calendrical placement within the three four-month seasonal divisions.

Having completed the measure of the year (`varṣapramāṇa`), the Bhāṣya turns to kalpa. The phrase `kalpo bahuvidhaḥ smṛtaḥ` is immediately unpacked into four kinds:

1. `antarakalpa`
2. `saṃvartakalpa`
3. `vivartakalpa`
4. `mahākalpa`

Thus VAK 3.89 closes the ordinary calendar ladder and opens the technical classification of cosmic time.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is straightforward exposition: numerical definition, calendrical clarification, then explicit classification of kalpas.

## New Technical Terms

- **ūnarātra** — deficient or omitted night used in calendrical adjustment
  - **status:** introduced here in the Bhāṣya as explanation of `sonarātraḥ`
  - **function:** calendar correction within the annual measure

- **varṣapramāṇa** — measure of the year
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expository term
  - **function:** marks completion of the ordinary calendar discussion

- **antarakalpa** — intermediate kalpa
  - **status:** previously encountered as a lifespan measure, now explicitly classified as a kind of kalpa
  - **function:** first member of the fourfold kalpa classification

- **saṃvartakalpa** — kalpa of contraction/dissolution
  - **status:** introduced here as a formal kalpa class
  - **function:** cosmic contraction phase

- **vivartakalpa** — kalpa of expansion/formation
  - **status:** introduced here as a formal kalpa class
  - **function:** cosmic expansion phase

- **mahākalpa** — great kalpa
  - **status:** previously encountered as a duration-unit; here formally classified
  - **function:** comprehensive cosmic cycle

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya establishes two distinct but continuous levels of temporal determination.

```text
ordinary measured time
    kṣaṇa → lava → muhūrta → ahorātra → māsa → saṃvatsara

cosmic measured time
    kalpa
        → antarakalpa
        → saṃvartakalpa
        → vivartakalpa
        → mahākalpa
```

The kalpa is therefore not a vague expression for an immense duration. It is a classified temporal structure whose kinds will be defined through the production, persistence, and destruction of Loka.

The calendrical `ūnarātra` also shows that conventional temporal measure is adjusted to observed periodicity rather than treated as a perfectly uniform arithmetic abstraction.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required.**

The first-pass Kārikā translation was too uncertain around both `trayo` and `sonarātraḥ`. The Bhāṣya supplies the intended structure:

```text
30 lavas = 1 muhūrta
30 muhūrtas = 1 day-night
30 day-nights = 1 month
12 months = 1 year
6 ūnarātras are omitted in the year
```

A better working translation is therefore:

> Thirty [lavas make] a muhūrta; thirty muhūrtas a day-and-night; thirty day-and-nights a month. A year consists of twelve months, with deficient nights; the kalpa is remembered as manyfold.

The final phrase is then explicitly fourfold in the Bhāṣya.

## Logical Determination

The temporal ladder now reaches a transition in kind:

```text
minimal temporal unit
    → repeated aggregation
        → daily cycle
            → month
                → year
                    → classified cosmic cycle
```

The significant point is not merely increasing magnitude. At `kalpa`, measure becomes phase-structure: duration is determined by what happens to the world and its beings during that duration.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
Measure becomes cycle when duration is determined by a complete process of world-change.
```

This remains entirely within produced Loka. The four kalpa-types are determinations of conditioned cosmic process, not genetic principles standing above that process.
