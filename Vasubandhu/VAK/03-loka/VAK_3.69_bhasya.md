# VAK_3.69 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
tata ūrdhva vimāneṣu devāḥ kāmabhujastu ṣaṭ /
dvaṃdvāliṃṅganapāṇyāptivasitekṣitamaithunāḥ // VAkK_3.69 //
```

**Working translation:**

Above that, in aerial mansions, are gods; six classes are enjoyers of desire. Their modes of sexual union are bodily pairing, embrace, hand-contact, smile, and glance.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.69.md`.

## Natural Argument

The prose first distinguishes the six kāmāvacara deva classes from the higher gods: only these six are `kāmabhujaḥ`, enjoyers of sensual desire. It then distributes a progressively attenuated mode of sexual satisfaction across the six classes.

```text
Cāturmahārājakāyika + Trāyastriṃśa
    → bodily intercourse, as among humans

Yāma
    → embrace

Tuṣita
    → hand-contact

Nirmāṇarati
    → smile

Paranirmitavaśavartin
    → glance
```

For the two earth-connected classes bodily intercourse occurs, but no semen is emitted; sexual heat is extinguished through release of wind. Among the higher kāma-devas the corresponding act alone extinguishes the heat.

The Bhāṣya then reports a Vaibhāṣika appeal to the Prajñapti for temporal measure and adds a general relation: the more intense the objects of desire, the more intense the passion. Divine children arise in the lap of a god or goddess and are thereby reckoned their son or daughter.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of the six kāma-deva classes and their differentiated modes of sexual satisfaction.
- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — `kālaparimānaṃ tu prajñaptāvuktam iti vaibhāṣikāḥ`, referring the temporal measure to the Prajñapti.

## New Technical Terms

- **kāmabhuj** — enjoyer of sensual desire
  - **status:** Kārikā term explained by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** restricts sensual enjoyment to the six kāmāvacara deva classes

- **bhūmisambaddhavāsin** — earth-connected dweller
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** distinguishes Cāturmahārājakāyikas and Trāyastriṃśas from higher aerial kāma-devas

- **dāha** — sexual heat / burning
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** physiological-affective condition terminated by the appropriate mode of contact

- **śukrābhāva** — absence of semen
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification here
  - **function:** differentiates divine bodily intercourse from the human case

- **dvandvasamāpatti** — coming together as a pair
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression
  - **function:** general description of pairing among these gods

## Doctrinal Determination

The ascent within kāmadhātu is not an ascent out of desire. Rather, desire itself becomes progressively less dependent upon gross bodily contact.

```text
gross bodily union
    → embrace
        → touch
            → smile
                → glance
```

The Bhāṣya therefore confirms the Kārikā's central distinction: refinement of the mode of enjoyment does not abolish `kāma`.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and correction.**

The second pāda should be understood distributively across the six kāma-deva classes. `dvandva` applies jointly to the first two classes; the remaining four modes correspond respectively to Yāma, Tuṣita, Nirmāṇarati, and Paranirmitavaśavartin. The source's apparent `vasita` is confirmed by the prose as **hasita**, “smile/laughter.”

## Logical Determination

```text
same genus: kāma-enjoyment
    → progressively refined mode of satisfaction
```

The relevant distinction is not desire versus non-desire, but differences internal to desire itself.

## Organon Contact Point

Elevation of form does not entail liberation from its determining ground. Here the form of sensual satisfaction becomes increasingly subtle while the beings remain explicitly `kāmabhujaḥ`.
