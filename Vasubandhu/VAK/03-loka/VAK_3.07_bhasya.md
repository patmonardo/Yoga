# VAK_3.07_bhasya — Undesired Abodes and the Four Stations

## Kārikā

> There are no others, because residence there is undesired. Again, there are four stations: the four contaminated aggregates, only in their own ground.

## Bhāṣya — Continuous Translation

In these [nine abodes], beings dwell by their own wish.

Why are there no others? The bad destinies (`apāyāḥ`). In them beings, against their will, are made to dwell by the **rākṣasas of karma** (`karmarākṣasaiḥ`). They do not dwell there by choice. Therefore those states are not counted among the `sattvāvāsas`, just as a place of imprisonment is not properly called an abode.

Elsewhere in the sūtra seven `vijñānasthitis` are stated; elsewhere, again, four stations are stated. What are these four? A station associated with form, one associated with feeling, one associated with perception, and one associated with formations.

What is their own-nature? Respectively, they are the four contaminated aggregates. And these function only in their own ground, not in another ground.

---

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya makes two distinct moves.

### 1. Why the bad destinies are not `sattvāvāsas`

The criterion for an `āvāsa` is not bare occupancy.

```text
mere being-there
    ≠
āvāsa

āvāsa
    requires dwelling in the relevant sense
```

Vasubandhu sharpens this through `svecchayā`: beings in the nine recognized abodes dwell there "by their own wish." The bad destinies fail this criterion because their inhabitants are `akāmakāḥ`, unwilling.

The image is deliberately severe:

```text
karmarākṣasaiḥ āvāsyante
    they are made to dwell there
    by the rākṣasas of karma
```

The concluding comparison is equally important:

```text
bandhanasthānavat
    like a place of confinement / imprisonment
```

Thus a forced place of confinement is not an `āvāsa` in the same sense as a dwelling.

### 2. A second doctrine of four `vijñānasthitis`

The Bhāṣya then distinguishes another scriptural classification from the seven cosmological knowledge-stations:

```text
rūpopagā vijñānasthitiḥ
vedanopagā vijñānasthitiḥ
saṃjñopagā vijñānasthitiḥ
saṃskāropagā vijñānasthitiḥ
```

These correspond respectively to the four contaminated aggregates other than vijñāna itself:

```text
rūpa
vedanā
saṃjñā
saṃskāra
```

Finally, the Bhāṣya restricts them emphatically:

```text
svabhūmāv eva
nānyatra bhūmau
```

They function only in their own ground, not in another ground.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explains why the bad destinies are excluded from the nine `sattvāvāsas` and introduces the separate fourfold scriptural classification of `vijñānasthiti`.

**Voice: scriptural doctrine as presented by Vasubandhu** — seven `vijñānasthitis` are taught in one context; four are taught in another.

No explicit objector is required in this unit; the interrogative transitions are expository.

## New Technical Terms

- **svecchayā** — by one's own wish; willingly
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative term in this passage
  - **function:** criterion distinguishing an abode from coerced confinement

- **akāmaka** — unwilling; against one's wish
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative term in this passage
  - **function:** exclusion criterion for the bad destinies

- **karmarākṣasa** — rākṣasa of karma; karmic rākṣasa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only striking expression
  - **function:** figurative-causal characterization of coercive karmic placement

- **bandhanasthāna** — place of confinement / imprisonment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative comparison
  - **function:** analogy explaining why forced occupancy is not an `āvāsa`

- **rūpopaga / vedanopaga / saṃjñopaga / saṃskāropaga** — associated with form / feeling / perception / formations
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-introduced specification of the four stations
  - **function:** doctrinal classification of the four aggregate-based `vijñānasthitis`

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya materially sharpens `anicchāvasanāt`.

The Kārikā's compressed phrase should not merely be understood as "because residence is undesirable." The prose makes the subject explicit:

```text
beings do not dwell there willingly
```

and then gives a causal image:

```text
they are made to dwell there by karmarākṣasas
```

A stronger philosophical translation of the first pāda is therefore:

> No other states are counted as abodes, because beings dwell there unwillingly.

The Bhāṣya also confirms that the second half introduces a genuinely different fourfold sense of `vijñānasthiti`, namely the four contaminated aggregates associated with vijñāna, each restricted to its own `bhūmi`.

## Doctrinal / Logical Determination

### Abode is not mere location

The most important distinction is:

```text
location / occupancy
    ≠
āvāsa / dwelling
```

A being can be objectively located in a state while that state fails to count as its `āvāsa` because the residence is coerced.

This means the classification is not naive geography. It contains a modal determination:

```text
where one is
+
how one is related to being there
```

### Karma appears as coercive determining ground

The phrase `karmarākṣasaiḥ` is especially significant because karma is no longer merely implied as the remote explanation of a destiny. It appears as the determining power that **places** beings in a world-state against their present wish.

The text does not yet provide Chapter 4's analysis of karma, so we should not import that doctrine backward. But the transition is now textually visible:

```text
gati / loka
    resultant placement

karma
    determining force of placement
```

### Ground-specific support

The second half retains the Dharma-first discipline:

```text
vijñāna does not stand abstractly

its four stations are:
    rūpa
    vedanā
    saṃjñā
    saṃskāra

and each is restricted to:
    svabhūmi
```

No support-function floats free of its determinate field.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage gives an unusually clean distinction between **being placed** and **dwelling**.

```text
external determination:
    I am there

relation to the determination:
    I dwell there willingly / unwillingly
```

The bad destinies reveal a negative form of individuality. The being experiences the result as "my situation," yet the determining ground of that situation is not contained in the isolated present individual. The field has a history and a ground that exceed the individual's immediate self-representation.

That is why the rākṣasa image is philosophically effective rather than decorative:

```text
karma appears to the isolated individual
as an alien coercive power
```

From the Dharma-first standpoint, however, this "alien" power is precisely what Chapter 4 must rationally determine as karma rather than leave in demonic representation.

So the movement is suggestive:

```text
rākṣasa-image
    negative representation of coercive ground

Karmanirdeśa
    principled cognition of that ground
```

This should remain provisional until the karma chapter earns the stronger formulation.

## Summary

VAK 3.07 makes two major distinctions.

First, bad destinies are not counted among the nine `sattvāvāsas` because beings do not dwell there willingly. Vasubandhu says they are made to dwell there by the **rākṣasas of karma**, like prisoners confined in a place of bondage.

Second, the text introduces another fourfold doctrine of `vijñānasthiti`: form, feeling, perception, and formations as contaminated aggregate-stations associated with vijñāna. These supports function only in their own proper ground.

The resulting formal structure is:

```text
mere location
    ≠ dwelling

being-abode
    requires a determinate relation to residence

karma
    appears as the coercive ground of unwanted placement

vijñāna
    remains dependent upon ground-specific aggregate supports
```

This is another step away from naive cosmology and toward a principled account of the world-field.
