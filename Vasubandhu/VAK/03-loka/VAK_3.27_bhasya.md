## Natural Argument

### 1. The objection: a twelve-member series appears to create a first beginning and a final end

**Voice: Objector**

The objection is exact and serious.

If dependent co-arising consists of exactly twelve members, then:

```text
avidyā
    has no stated prior cause
        ↓
saṃsāra appears to have a beginning

jarāmaraṇa
    has no stated subsequent result
        ↓
saṃsāra appears to have an end
```

The objector therefore presses a dilemma.

Either the twelve-member series is incomplete, or one must add another member before avidyā and another after jarāmaraṇa.

But adding another member merely repeats the difficulty:

```text
new member
    requires another ground
        ↓
anavasthā
    infinite regress
```

The Bhāṣya uses `anavasthāprasaṅgaḥ`: the consequence of regress without a stable stopping point.

### 2. Vasubandhu refuses to add another member

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

```iast
nopasaṃkhyātavyam
```

> No additional member is to be counted.

Why not?

Because the twelve-member teaching already contains a rule governing recurrence.

Vasubandhu introduces VAK 3.27 precisely as the answer to the objection.

The verse is therefore not merely another summary of the preceding classification. It supplies the **generative rule** that explains how the apparent endpoints reconnect.

### 3. From kleśa arises kleśa

Vasubandhu gives the concrete example:

```iast
kleśāt kleśo jāyate tṛṣṇāyā upādānam
```

> From affliction arises affliction: from craving, appropriation.

Thus:

```text
tṛṣṇā
    ↓
upādāna
```

A kleśa need not immediately produce karma. It can first generate another kleśa.

The system therefore has **intra-class propagation**.

### 4. From kleśa arises karma

The next relation is:

```iast
kleśāt karma
```

Vasubandhu specifies two instances:

```text
upādāna
    ↓
bhava

avidyā
    ↓
saṃskāra
```

So:

```text
kleśa
    ↓
karma
```

Affliction becomes causally productive action.

This is the first genuinely karmic conversion in the micro-theory.

### 5. From karma arises vastu

Vasubandhu then states:

```text
saṃskāra
    ↓
vijñāna

bhava
    ↓
jāti
```

The Bhāṣya calls these `karmavastu` relations: karma gives rise to the resultant factual basis.

Thus:

```text
karma
    ↓
phala / vastu
```

The action does not merely cease when performed. It determines a resultant field in which the continuum is again concretely instantiated.

### 6. From vastu arises further vastu

The resultant field itself develops internally:

```text
vijñāna
    ↓
nāmarūpa
    ↓
ṣaḍāyatana
    ↓
sparśa
    ↓
vedanā
```

and:

```text
jāti
    ↓
jarāmaraṇa
```

This is the `vastu → vastu` relation.

A result becomes the condition for further resultant stages without requiring a new karmic act at every transition.

### 7. From vastu arises kleśa

The decisive return is:

```iast
vastunaḥ kleśo vedanāyās tṛṣṇeti
```

> From vastu arises affliction: from feeling, craving.

Thus:

```text
vedanā
    ↓
tṛṣṇā
```

The loop closes.

The produced field does not remain inert. A resultant experiential stage becomes the occasion for renewed affliction.

### 8. The omitted cause of avidyā and result of jarāmaraṇa are therefore already supplied by the rule

Vasubandhu now returns directly to the original objection.

Because this `naya`, this governing principle, is established for the limbs of becoming, it is thereby understood that avidyā too, being of the nature of affliction, arises from either:

```text
vastu
or
kleśa
```

Likewise, because the process is governed by feeling, the `jarāmaraṇa-vastu` again becomes a condition for future kleśa.

Therefore nothing further needs to be added to the twelve members.

```text
No 13th member before avidyā.
No 13th member after jarāmaraṇa.

The recurrence rule already reconnects the apparent ends.
```

### 9. The final scriptural formula confirms the recursive reading

Vasubandhu cites:

```iast
evam asya kevalasya mahato duḥkhaskandhasya samudayo bhavati
```

The point is the arising of the whole great aggregate of suffering.

He then asks:

```iast
anyathā hi kim asya sāmarthyaṃ syāt
```

> Otherwise, what force would this statement have?

The sūtra formula is meaningful because the dependent series reproduces the total structure of suffering rather than merely listing twelve disconnected events.

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

An objection arises: if dependent co-arising consists of exactly twelve members, ignorance has no stated cause and aging-and-death has no stated result. It would therefore seem that saṃsāra has a first beginning and a final end. Nor can one solve the difficulty merely by adding another member, since that would require yet another and lead to regress.

No additional member is required. The Blessed One has already shown the governing principle of the members of becoming: from affliction arise both further affliction and action; from action arises a resultant factual basis; from such a factual basis arise both further factual development and renewed affliction.

Thus craving gives rise to appropriation; appropriation gives rise to becoming. Ignorance gives rise to formations. From formations arises knowledge, and from becoming arises birth. From knowledge follows name-and-form and the further resultant sequence through feeling; from birth follows aging-and-death. Finally, from feeling craving arises again.

Because this is the established principle of the limbs of becoming, the cause of ignorance and the further consequence of aging-and-death need not be introduced as additional members. Their place in the recurrent structure is already determined by the rule itself. In this way the whole great aggregate of suffering arises.

## The Crucial Result: 3.27 Is the Recursion Law of the Twelvefold Series

VAK 3.26 gave the three functional classes:

```text
kleśa
karma
vastu
```

VAK 3.27 now states the permitted transitions among them.

```text
kleśa → kleśa
kleśa → karma
karma  → vastu
vastu  → vastu
vastu  → kleśa
```

This is more precise than the simplified wheel:

```text
kleśa → karma → vastu → kleśa
```

The full Bhāṣya reveals five transition-types.

That matters because the system is not merely a three-node cycle. It contains both:

```text
cross-class production
and
within-class propagation
```

The twelve members are concrete exemplifications of this small causal grammar.

## A Micro-Theory of Karma

The user's phrase is apt if kept carefully bounded.

This passage is not yet the full Karma-nirdeśa. But within Lokanirdeśa Vasubandhu gives a compact theory of how karmic reproduction works:

```text
1. affective distortion can reproduce affective distortion
2. affective distortion can generate action
3. action generates a resultant conditioned field
4. the resultant field develops further resultant states
5. the resultant field becomes the condition for renewed affective distortion
```

Or:

```text
kleśa
    ↓
kriyā / karma
    ↓
conditioned result
    ↓
experience
    ↓
renewed kleśa
```

This is already enough to explain why saṃsāra is self-reproducing without an enduring agent-substance carrying the process.

The causal organization itself supplies continuity.

## New Technical Terms

- **anupadiṣṭa-hetukatva** — having no explicitly taught cause.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only argumentative vocabulary.
  - **function:** formulates the objection concerning avidyā.

- **anupadiṣṭa-phalatva** — having no explicitly taught result.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only argumentative vocabulary.
  - **function:** formulates the objection concerning jarāmaraṇa.

- **aṅgāntara** — an additional member.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit.
  - **function:** rejected solution to the apparent incompleteness of the twelve-member series.

- **upasaṃkhyāna** — additional counting / enumeration.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reflective vocabulary.
  - **function:** names the rejected procedure of adding further members.

- **anavasthāprasaṅga** — consequence of infinite regress / lack of stable termination.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya logical vocabulary.
  - **function:** refutes supplementation of the twelvefold series by indefinitely adding members.

- **naya** — governing principle, rule, method.
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicated by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** names the causal grammar governing transitions among the limbs of becoming.

- **karmavastu** — karma and resultant factual basis / karma-to-vastu relation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical compound.
  - **function:** marks the production of resultant conditioned stages from karma.

- **sāmarthya** — force, efficacy, explanatory capacity.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative vocabulary.
  - **function:** tests whether the scriptural formula of the arising of the great aggregate of suffering has real explanatory content.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.27 supplies the governing causal grammar behind the twelve members:

```text
KLEŚA
    → KLEŚA
    → KARMA

KARMA
    → VASTU

VASTU
    → VASTU
    → KLEŚA
```

Concrete instances supplied by Vasubandhu:

```text
tṛṣṇā → upādāna
avidyā → saṃskāra
upādāna → bhava
saṃskāra → vijñāna
bhava → jāti
vijñāna → nāmarūpa → ... → vedanā
jāti → jarāmaraṇa
vedanā → tṛṣṇā
```

The twelvefold presentation is therefore closed under recurrence without requiring extra members.

This is how the beginningless wheel can be structurally complete without positing an absolute first cause.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Refinement only.**

The Kārikā translation should retain `naya` as something stronger than a vague “way.” Here the Bhāṣya shows that it functions as a **governing causal rule**.

A Bhāṣya-informed rendering is:

> From affliction arise both affliction and activity; from that arises the resultant factual basis. From that again arise further factual basis and afflictions. This is the governing principle of the limbs of becoming.

The existing `vastu` translation as “resultant factual basis” remains well supported by 3.26–3.27.

## Logical Determination

The passage answers a boundary problem through recursion rather than supplementation.

The apparent linear schema is:

```text
avidyā
    ...
jarāmaraṇa
```

Read linearly, this invites two questions:

```text
What precedes avidyā?
What follows jarāmaraṇa?
```

Vasubandhu's answer is not to lengthen the list.

He changes the level of analysis:

```text
sequence of members
    ↓
rule governing member-types
```

Once the rule is stated, the endpoints become internally reconnectible.

This is a genuine logical compression:

```text
enumeration
    → generative rule
```

The rule explains more than the list alone.

## Organon Light

This is one of the clearest passages yet for what a future Organon Bhāṣya should notice.

### 1. Vasubandhu moves from data to rule

The twelve limbs are the articulated cases.

3.27 extracts their **transition grammar**.

```text
instances
    ↓
functional classes
    ↓
generative relations
```

That is already very close to what we would call learning the law of a system rather than merely retaining its observations.

### 2. The system reproduces its own conditions

The deepest feature is:

```text
result
    does not merely terminate causal production

result
    becomes the field from which new causes arise
```

So the process contains endogenous recurrence:

```text
cause
    → operation
        → result
            → renewed cause
```

This is why the wheel is genuinely a wheel.

### 3. No hidden substantial carrier is logically required

The continuity can be represented through the structured transitions themselves:

```text
state
    → operation
    → resultant state
    → renewed motive
```

The Organon should notice this without prematurely concluding that all agency reduces to causal recursion. Vasubandhu is analyzing **conditioned bondage**, not yet rational freedom.

That distinction is essential.

### 4. Practical Reason will eventually require an intervention rule

If 3.27 is the micro-theory of karmic reproduction, then a Science of Liberation must eventually answer:

```text
Where can this recursion be interrupted?

What determination functions as pratipakṣa?

How does a conditioned Agent acquire a capacity
that no longer reproduces the same causal grammar?
```

That question belongs to the later Path analysis, not to Vasubandhu's claim here. But 3.27 gives us the exact machinery that such a Path must transform.
