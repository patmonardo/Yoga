## Bhāṣya — Continuous Translation

The question is raised: **in which destiny are how many birth-modes found?**

Humans are fourfold. There are egg-born humans, for example the elders Śaila and Upaśaila, who emerged from the Krauñcī; also the thirty-two sons of Mṛgāramātṛ and the five hundred sons of the king of Pañcāla. Womb-born humans are such as we see now. Moisture-born humans include Māndhātṛ, Cāru, Upacāru, Kapotamālinī, Āmrapālī, and others. The spontaneously arisen humans are those at the beginning of a cosmic age.

Animals likewise are fourfold. Three kinds are directly observed. The spontaneously arisen include nāgas, suparṇins, and the like.

Hell-beings are spontaneously arisen. Intermediate-existence beings and devas are likewise all spontaneously arisen.

Pretas are also womb-born. The word **api**, “also,” indicates that they may also be spontaneously arisen. A female preta says to the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana that she gives birth to five sons at night and five more by day, devours them after giving birth, and still is not satisfied.

Which birth-mode is best of all? **Spontaneous arising.**

Why, then, does the Bodhisattva in his final existence, although he has mastery over his mode of birth, adopt womb-birth? Because by doing so he sees a great purpose.

Through connection with relatives, the great Śākya lineage can be brought into the Dharma. Others are moved to esteem and respect him as one descended from a lineage of universal monarchs. And those to be trained are encouraged by seeing that even one who has become human can attain such accomplishment.

Otherwise, if his family and lineage were unknown, people might imagine: “Who is this magical person?” They might suppose that he were a deva or a piśāca. Other sectarians might malign him as a magician who appears in the world after hundreds of cosmic ages and devours the world through magic.

Others say that womb-birth is chosen so that bodily relics may remain; through recognizing those relics, humans and other beings have by the thousands reached heaven and liberation. For spontaneously arisen beings, because there is no external seed, the body does not remain after death; it disappears like the flame of an oil-lamp at the end of the night.

But for those who accept the Lord's sustaining supernatural power, this explanation is not satisfactory.

One question gives rise to another. Although the corpse of a spontaneously arisen being is not perceived, why does a sūtra say that a spontaneously arisen suparṇin carries off a spontaneously arisen nāga for food? The text does not say that it eats the corpse; it carries it away for food out of ignorance. Or it may eat it while the nāga is still alive; after death it is no longer available to be eaten.

Which birth-mode is the most numerous? **Spontaneous arising.** It comprises two destinies completely, portions of three others, and all intermediate-existence beings.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya does substantially more than map four yonis onto five gatis.

### 1. Cross-classification

The question itself is formal:

```text
For each gati,
how many yonis are instantiated?
```

The resulting relation is many-to-many rather than one destiny = one birth-mode:

| Class | Birth-mode determination |
|---|---|
| Humans | all four |
| Animals | all four |
| Hell-beings | spontaneously arisen only |
| Intermediate existence | spontaneously arisen only |
| Devas | spontaneously arisen only |
| Pretas | womb-born also; `api` implies spontaneous arising as well |

Thus `gati` and `yoni` are independent axes of determination.

### 2. Observation and testimony

The Bhāṣya distinguishes what is presently observable from what belongs to transmitted examples. For animals, three birth-modes are said to be directly seen, while spontaneous animal birth is exemplified by nāgas and suparṇins.

The classification therefore does not simply collapse all its evidence into one epistemic register.

### 3. A new evaluative question

After the classification is established, Vasubandhu asks:

```iast
katamā yoniḥ sarvasādhvī /
```

Which yoni is best?

The answer is **upapāduka**, spontaneous arising.

This immediately creates a problem: if spontaneous arising is best, why does the final-birth Bodhisattva choose womb-birth despite mastery over birth?

### 4. Purpose overrides abstract superiority

The answer is teleological rather than classificatory. Womb-birth has a **great purpose** (`mahāntam artham`): it establishes lineage, social intelligibility, confidence, pedagogical accessibility, and the possibility that human beings recognize Buddhahood as attainable from human existence.

Thus:

```text
best mode in abstraction
    ≠
best mode for a determinate purpose
```

This is the decisive philosophical feature of the passage.

### 5. Manifestation must be intelligible within the field

If the Bodhisattva simply appeared spontaneously, ordinary beings might classify him as a magical being, deva, or piśāca. The mode of manifestation therefore matters because it conditions how the manifestation can be recognized.

The Bodhisattva does not merely appear; he appears **under conditions that make the Dharma communicable**.

### 6. Alternative rationale and criticism

Another explanation says womb-birth permits bodily relics to remain, whereas spontaneously arisen beings leave no corpse because they lack an external seed.

Vasubandhu then records a criticism: this is inadequate for those who accept the Buddha's sustaining supernatural power (`ādhiṣṭhānikī ṛddhi`).

The text therefore does not simply accumulate explanations; it tests their sufficiency.

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expository narrator** — poses the distribution question and explains the four human and animal birth-modes.
- **Voice: doctrinal exposition** — all hell-beings, intermediate beings, and devas are spontaneously arisen; pretas can also be womb-born.
- **Voice: scriptural testimony** — the female preta's statement to Mahāmaudgalyāyana.
- **Voice: Objector / questioner** — asks which yoni is best and why the final-birth Bodhisattva does not choose it.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu / received explanation** — womb-birth serves lineage, respect, intelligibility, and pedagogical encouragement.
- **Voice: others (`apare tv āhuḥ`)** — Bodhisattva womb-birth allows relics to remain.
- **Voice: critical response** — rejects that rationale as insufficient for those who accept the Buddha's sustaining supernatural power.

## New Technical Terms

- **prasavasāmya** — similarity in mode of generation
  - **status:** introduced in the preceding yoni discussion and operative here
  - **function:** classificatory principle grounding the four yonis

- **upapattivaśitva** — mastery over mode of birth/arising
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this passage
  - **function:** establishes that the Bodhisattva's womb-birth is chosen rather than compelled

- **caramabhavika** — one in the final existence
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical designation
  - **function:** marks the Bodhisattva whose present existence culminates in Buddhahood

- **jñātisaṃbandha** — connection through kinship
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific here
  - **function:** explains one practical purpose of womb-birth

- **vineya** — one to be trained
  - **status:** technical Buddhist pedagogical term
  - **function:** identifies the recipients whose confidence is shaped by the Bodhisattva's human birth

- **bāhyabīja** — external seed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific here
  - **function:** explains, in one proposed account, why spontaneously arisen bodies leave no corpse

- **ādhiṣṭhānikī ṛddhi** — sustaining supernatural power
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific doctrinal term
  - **function:** grounds criticism of the relic-based explanation

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Kārikā translation should preserve several points sharpened by the Bhāṣya.

First, `caturdhā` means that humans and animals each instantiate **all four** yonis, not merely that together they form four classes.

Second, `upapādukāḥ` governs hell-beings, intermediate beings, and devas.

Third, `pretā api jarāyujāḥ` should not be translated as though pretas were exclusively womb-born. The Bhāṣya explicitly states:

```iast
apiśabdād apy upapādukā iti
```

Because of the word `api`, they are **also spontaneously arisen**.

Recommended translation:

> Humans and animals occur in all four birth-modes. Hell-beings, intermediate-existence beings, and devas are spontaneously arisen; pretas are also womb-born.

The final clause deliberately leaves open the additional spontaneous mode explained by the Bhāṣya.

## Doctrinal / Logical Determination

VAkK 3.09 crosses two previously independent determinations:

```text
gati
    destiny / resultant course

×

yoni
    mode of generation
```

The result is not a naive biological taxonomy. It is a **relational matrix of generation within the world-field**.

More importantly, the Bhāṣya then moves beyond classification into practical determination:

```text
abstract superiority of a mode
    ↓
concrete purpose of manifestation
```

Spontaneous arising is declared the best yoni, but the Bodhisattva chooses womb-birth because manifestation must be appropriate to the beings who are to receive the Dharma.

Thus the passage introduces a distinction between:

```text
what is superior in itself
and
what is fitting for a determinate end
```

That is a genuine advance beyond simple enumeration.

## Organon Interpretation

The light Organon contact point is unusually clear.

The Dharma-first analysis now has three levels:

```text
Gati
    where the resultant continuity belongs

Yoni
    how that continuity is generated

Artha / purpose
    why a particular mode of manifestation is fitting
```

The Bodhisattva episode is crucial because **formal superiority does not decide the concrete act**. A mode of manifestation is rational only in relation to its purpose and the field in which it must become intelligible.

```text
Upapāduka
    formally best yoni

Jarāyuja
    concretely chosen by the Bodhisattva
    because it mediates Dharma to human beings
```

So manifestation itself has a communicative condition:

```text
Truth appearing without mediation
    can be misrecognized as magic

Truth entering an intelligible lineage
    can become teachable Dharma
```

This is not yet the full Organon Comment, but it is a strong seed for it.

## Summary

VAkK 3.09 distributes the four yonis across the destinies and thereby demonstrates that `gati` and `yoni` are independent principles of classification. Humans and animals possess all four birth-modes; hell-beings, intermediate beings, and devas are spontaneously arisen; pretas may be womb-born and, as the Bhāṣya clarifies, spontaneously arisen as well.

The Bhāṣya then asks which yoni is best and answers: spontaneous arising. Yet the final-birth Bodhisattva deliberately chooses womb-birth because it serves a greater purpose: lineage, intelligibility, respect, pedagogical encouragement, and the successful entrance of beings into Dharma.

The decisive result is therefore not merely the fourfold taxonomy of generation but the distinction between **abstract superiority and purposive fitness**. The world-field is not only structured by where beings arise and how they arise, but also by the conditions under which a manifestation can become intelligible and effective.
