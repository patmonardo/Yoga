# VAK_3.02_bhasya — Rūpadhātu as Dhyāna-Structured World

## Segmentation and Grammar

Only forms decisive for the Bhāṣya argument are noted here.

### `etasmāc ca kāmadhātoḥ ūrdhvam`

```text
etasmāt kāmadhātoḥ
    from / above this desire-domain

ūrdhvam
    above, upward
```

The Bhāṣya makes the reference of `ūrdhvam` explicit: the rūpadhātu is determined relative to the kāmadhātu just analyzed.

### `pratyekaṃ tribhūmikāni`

```text
pratyekam
    each separately

tri-bhūmika
    having three bhūmis / levels
```

This resolves `pṛthak pṛthak`: the first, second, and third dhyānas are each independently threefold.

### `sthānam utkṛṣṭataram`

```text
sthānam
    station, place, locus

utkṛṣṭataram
    more elevated, more excellent
```

This expression becomes decisive in the Kāśmīra objection: a more exalted `sthāna` need not constitute another `bhūmi`.

### `na tu bhūmyantaram`

```text
na tu
    but not

bhūmi-antaram
    another / distinct ground or level
```

This is the conceptual hinge of the passage.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

Above this desire-domain is the form-domain, having seventeen stations. How so? He says: separately, each dhyāna there is three-levelled; but the fourth is eight-levelled.

The first, second, and third dhyānas are each three-levelled. In this scheme, the first dhyāna consists of the Brahmakāyikas, the Brahmapurohitas, and the Mahābrahmās. The second consists of the Parīttābhas, Apramāṇābhas, and Ābhāsvaras. The third consists of the Parīttaśubhas, Apramāṇaśubhas, and Śubhakṛtsnas. The fourth consists of the Anabhrakas, Puṇyaprasavas, Bṛhatphalas, Abṛhas, Atapas, Sudṛśas, Sudarśanas, and Akaniṣṭhas. These are the seventeen stations constituting the form-domain.

The Kāśmīras, however, say that together with the beings inhabiting them there are sixteen. They hold that Mahābrahmā has merely a more exalted station among the Brahmapurohitas, produced like an enclosure for a single leader, and not another distinct ground.

### Philosophical Translation

Above the desire-domain, the form-domain is articulated through the hierarchy of dhyāna. The first three dhyānas each differentiate into three levels, while the fourth differentiates into eight. On the seventeenfold reckoning these levels constitute the stations of the rūpadhātu.

The Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣikas object to treating every distinguished station as an independent ground. Mahābrahmā occupies an exceptionally elevated position within the Brahmapurohita field, but this superiority of station does not generate a distinct `bhūmi`. Hence they count sixteen rather than seventeen.

## Course of the Argument

### Question or Problem

After determining the extent and internal articulation of kāmadhātu, what is the structure of the domain above it?

### Initial Determination

```text
rūpadhātu
    = 17 sthānas
    organized through 4 dhyānas

Dhyāna 1 = 3 bhūmis
Dhyāna 2 = 3 bhūmis
Dhyāna 3 = 3 bhūmis
Dhyāna 4 = 8 bhūmis
```

The Bhāṣya then names all seventeen stations explicitly.

### Objection / Opponent

**Voice: Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣika position**

The Kāśmīras count only sixteen.

Their reason is not a denial of Mahābrahmā's superiority, but a distinction between two kinds of determination:

```text
higher sthāna
    ≠
new bhūmi
```

Mahābrahmā has an exceptionally elevated station among the Brahmapurohitas, comparable to a specially formed enclosure for a single leader, but does not thereby constitute another ground.

### Reply / Resolution

No explicit refutation of the Kāśmīra position occurs in this passage. Vasubandhu records the seventeenfold presentation and then reports the sixteenfold Kāśmīra alternative.

**Voice: uncertain** as to whether the seventeenfold count should here be taken as Vasubandhu's own final preference rather than the transmitted kārikā scheme he is explicating.

### Result

The crucial doctrinal result is not merely the numerical disagreement. The Bhāṣya introduces a formal distinction between:

```text
sthāna
    determinate station or position within a domain

bhūmi
    distinct structural ground / level
```

A difference of rank or location does not automatically entail a difference of ground.

## Voices

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma voice** — explains the seventeenfold rūpadhātu scheme and enumerates its stations.
- **Voice: Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣika position** — proposes a sixteenfold count by denying that Mahābrahmā constitutes a separate `bhūmi`.
- **Voice: uncertain** — the passage does not explicitly adjudicate the disagreement beyond recording it.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratyekam** — each separately, individually.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of Kārikā `pṛthak pṛthak`.
  - **function:** distributive clarification; fixes the three-level count independently for each of the first three dhyānas.

- **utkṛṣṭatara** — more elevated, more excellent.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā.
  - **function:** marks superiority of station without requiring a distinct ground.

- **bhūmyantara** — another or distinct ground / level.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā as a compound.
  - **function:** doctrinal criterion for whether a distinguished locus counts as a separate level.

- **parigaṇa** — enclosure, bounded precinct, entourage-like enclosed place; exact nuance remains provisional here.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit.
  - **function:** analogy explaining Mahābrahmā's special station within an already existing level.

- **ekanāyaka** — single leader.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā.
  - **function:** supports the analogy for Mahābrahmā's exceptional but non-independent placement.

## Doctrinal Determination

The rūpadhātu is not merely a region containing refined beings. Its articulation is correlated with dhyāna:

```text
rūpadhātu
    ↓
dhyāna-order
    ↓
bhūmi differentiation
    ↓
sthāna / inhabitant configuration
```

This is an important strengthening of the first-pass reading. Dhyāna functions here not merely as a psychological or meditative state but as a principle according to which a world-domain itself is stratified.

At the same time the Kāśmīra objection prevents us from identifying every cosmological distinction with a distinct ontological level. The text explicitly permits:

```text
difference of station
without
difference of ground
```

That is a significant control on any later formal ontology of Lokanirdeśa.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing translation is structurally sound, but `pṛthak pṛthak` is now securely determined by the prose as `pratyekam`: each of the first three dhyānas is separately three-levelled.

A slightly sharper translation is therefore:

```text
Above [the desire-domain], the form-domain has seventeen stations. There, each of the first three dhyānas has three levels, while the fourth has eight.
```

The Bhāṣya also establishes that `sthāna` and `bhūmi` must not be collapsed into one term.

## Logical Determination

VAK 3.01 gave the first decisive reciprocity:

```text
beings determine the domain as inhabited
        ↕
the domain determines beings as placed
```

VAK 3.02 adds a second determination:

```text
placement
    is not flat
    but internally grounded by dhyāna
```

The form-domain therefore exhibits a hierarchy of determinations:

```text
Dhātu
    ↓
Dhyāna
    ↓
Bhūmi
    ↓
Sthāna
    ↓
Sattva as inhabitant
```

But the Kāśmīra intervention introduces a necessary logical restraint:

```text
Sthāna-difference
    does not necessarily generate
Bhūmi-difference.
```

Thus the Bhāṣya is already doing more than enumeration. It asks what degree of differentiation is sufficient to constitute a genuinely new structural level.

---

## Organon Contact Point

This passage supports the emerging movement from the first two chapters into Loka without requiring us to impose the later system onto the text.

Chapters 1–2 classify determinations and their capacities. Chapter 3 now presents those determinations as a structured field of placement. In VAK 3.02, that field is no longer governed primarily by desire-destinies but by formal meditative differentiation:

```text
kāmadhātu
    beings ↔ inhabited locations

rūpadhātu
    dhyāna ↔ grounds ↔ stations ↔ inhabitants
```

This gives a more precise sense in which Loka begins a reflective movement: what was previously a classification `by dhātu` is now displayed as the internal articulation `of dhātu`.

The especially useful formal lesson is:

```text
rank ≠ ground
place ≠ level
```

A higher position inside a structured field does not by itself create a new structural principle. That discrimination will matter when the Kośa's cosmological classifications are eventually reconstructed formally.
