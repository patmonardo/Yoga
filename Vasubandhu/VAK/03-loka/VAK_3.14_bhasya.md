# VAK_3.14 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> sajātiśuddhadivyākṣidṛśyaḥ karmarddhivegavān /
> sakalākṣaḥ apratighavān anivartyaḥ sa gandhabhuk //

**Best translation:**  
The intermediate being is visible to others of its own class and to the purified divine eye; it moves with the speed of karmic power, possesses all five faculties, is unobstructed and unable to turn back from its destined course, and feeds on scent.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_3.14.md`](./VAK_3.14.md)

## Segmentation by Natural Argument

### 1. Visibility is class- and faculty-dependent

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The intermediate being is not universally visible. It is seen by:

```text
samānajātīya antarābhavikas
    intermediate beings of the same class

and

those possessing
    suviśuddha divya-cakṣus
    a highly purified divine eye produced through abhijñā
```

The prose therefore converts the Kārikā's compact compound into an explicit visibility rule.

A further reported hierarchy is then added:

```text
deva-antarābhavika
    sees all

other intermediate classes
    may see successively lower classes
```

The latter is introduced as `apare`, “others [say],” and should not be silently treated as Vasubandhu's own unqualified commitment.

### 2. `karmarddhivega` is karmic supernormal speed

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya parses the compound step by step:

```text
ṛddhi
    movement through space

karmaṇā ṛddhiḥ
    power produced by karma

karmarddhi-vega
    the speed of that karmic power
```

The consequence is striking:

```text
na śakyo buddhair api pratibanddhum
```

Even Buddhas cannot block this movement, because the karma determining it is stronger in this respect.

The point is not that karma is metaphysically superior to Buddhahood. The local claim is narrower: once the relevant karmic projection has become operative as this transition, it is not externally interruptible.

### 3. The transition-form retains all five faculties

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

`sakalākṣaḥ` is glossed directly as:

```iast
samagra-pañcendriyaḥ
```

“complete in the five faculties.”

This confirms that the antarābhava is not presented as an abstract point of continuity. It remains a determinate sentient configuration.

### 4. Non-obstruction

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

`apratighavān` is derived by contrasting `pratigha`, obstruction or impact. The intermediate being is not stopped even by hard material obstacles such as vajra.

The Bhāṣya adds the report of a worm found inside a split mass of heated iron as an illustration of this non-obstructibility.

The example is evidential rhetoric, not the definition itself. The definition remains:

```text
apratigha
    not subject to ordinary obstruction
```

### 5. `anivartya` means destination-fixity

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

This is the most important clarification in the passage.

The intermediate being projected toward one destiny does not disappear and reappear as an intermediate being of another destiny:

```text
human antarābhava
    does not become
    deva antarābhava
```

Rather:

```text
yāmeva gatim adhikṛtya abhinirvṛttaḥ
    whichever destiny it has arisen oriented toward

tasyām eva upapattavyam
    in that very destiny it must be reborn
```

Thus `anivartya` is not merely spatial irreversibility. It is **karmically fixed destination-orientation**.

### 6. `gandhabhuk` and the name `gandharva`

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya asks whether a desire-domain intermediate being takes gross morsel-food (`kavaḍīkāra-āhāra`). The answer is qualified:

```text
yes,
but not gross food
```

What then?

```text
sa gandhabhuk
    it feeds on scent
```

From this, the prose explains the designation `gandharva`.

A further distinction is given:

```text
alpeśākhya
    consumes foul scent

maheśākhya
    consumes fragrant scent
```

The cosmological image is retained, but the prose integrates it into the technical doctrine of nutriment.

### 7. Duration is not independently fixed

**Voice: uncertain**

The question follows naturally: how long does the intermediate state remain?

The answer attributed simply to `bhadantaḥ` is:

```text
nāsti niyamaḥ
    there is no fixed rule
```

It remains until the complete conditions for rebirth (`upapatti-sāmagrī`) are obtained.

The reason is especially important:

```text
na hi tasya āyuṣaḥ pṛthag eva ākṣepaḥ
```

There is no separate projection of a distinct lifespan for the intermediate existence, because it belongs to the same `nikāyasabhāga` as the projected rebirth-series.

If it possessed an independently projected lifespan, exhaustion of that lifespan would generate another `maraṇabhava`, which would distort the structure already established.

## Padaccheda / Grammar Where Needed

### `sajātiśuddhadivyākṣidṛśyaḥ`

Analytically:

```iast
sajāti + śuddha-divya-akṣi + dṛśya
```

The Bhāṣya disambiguates the compound distributively:

```text
visible to its own kind
and
visible to the purified divine eye
```

It should not be read as though `sajāti` qualified the divine eye.

### `karmarddhivegavān`

```iast
karma + ṛddhi + vega + vat
```

Bhāṣya derivation:

```text
karmaṇā ṛddhiḥ = karmarddhi
karmarddheḥ vegaḥ = karmarddhivega
```

### `anivartyaḥ`

The prose shows that the relevant negation is not primarily:

```text
cannot physically turn around
```

but:

```text
cannot reverse the gati for which it has been projected
```

This materially sharpens the Kārikā translation.

## Literal Prose Translation

This intermediate existence is visible only to intermediate beings of the same kind. Those whose divine eye is extremely purified and produced by superknowledge also see it. It is not seen by the ordinary eye of rebirth, because of the subtlety associated with its mode of birth. An intermediate deva sees all [intermediate beings]; others say that human, preta, animal, and hell intermediate beings see the successively preceding classes.

“Possessing the speed of karmic power”: supernormal power is movement through space. Power produced by karma is karmic power, and the speed of that power is the rapidity called the speed of karmic power. Possessing this, the intermediate being cannot be obstructed even by Buddhas, because this karma is stronger [with respect to this result].

“Having all faculties”: it possesses the complete five faculties.

“Unobstructed”: obstruction is impact; one possessing it is obstructible, while this one is not. It cannot be stopped even by vajra and the like.

And with respect to whichever destiny it is going to arise in, it is entirely “irreversible.” A human intermediate being never disappears and becomes a deva intermediate being or another kind. Whatever destiny it has arisen directed toward, it must be reborn in that very destiny and not another.

Does a desire-domain intermediate being also consume morsel-food? Yes, but not gross food. What then? “It feeds on scent.” For this very reason it is called a gandharva. One of little status takes foul scent as food; one of great status takes fragrant scent as food.

How long does it remain? The venerable teachers say there is no fixed rule: until it obtains the complete conditions for rebirth. There is no separate projection of its lifespan, because it belongs to one and the same class-continuity. Otherwise, when that lifespan were exhausted, another death-existence would follow.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya turns the apparently fantastic intermediate being into a tightly constrained transition-structure.

```text
antarābhava
    is not universally visible
    ↓
visibility depends on class and refined faculty

antarābhava
    does not wander freely
    ↓
motion is generated by karmic power

antarābhava
    is not incomplete residue
    ↓
it possesses all five faculties

antarābhava
    is not stopped by ordinary material resistance
    ↓
it is apratigha

antarābhava
    cannot change its projected destination
    ↓
it is anivartya

antarābhava
    is not nutritionally undefined
    ↓
it has a subtle scent-based nutriment

antarābhava
    does not receive an independent lifespan
    ↓
it persists until rebirth-conditions are complete
```

The deepest formal determination is therefore not any single extraordinary property. It is this:

```text
The transition is constrained by the same karmic projection
that determines its destination.
```

That makes the intermediate existence a function of the causal series rather than an autonomous individual wandering between worlds.

## New Technical Terms

- **abhijñāmaya** — produced through superknowledge; here qualifying the purified divine eye
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās
  - **function:** definition / qualification of perceptual capacity

- **ākāśagamana** — movement through space; used here to gloss `ṛddhi`
  - **status:** absent from this Kārikā
  - **function:** definition

- **pratibandha** — obstruction, blocking
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** consequence / logical distinction

- **samagra-pañcendriya** — complete possession of the five faculties
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā as a phrase
  - **function:** definition of `sakalākṣa`

- **upapatti-sāmagrī** — the complete set of conditions for rebirth
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** definition of the stopping condition of antarābhava

- **pṛthag-ākṣepa** — a separate projection
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** rejected causal alternative

- **ekanikāyasabhāgatva** — belonging to one and the same class-continuity
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** ground for denying a separately projected lifespan

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAkK 3.14 is not merely a list of marvels. The Bhāṣya systematically specifies the **constraints on transition**.

The key relation is:

```text
ākṣepa
    projects the destination
    ↓
karmarddhi
    drives the transition
    ↓
anivartya
    prevents destination-switching
    ↓
upapatti-sāmagrī
    supplies the completion conditions
    ↓
upapatti
    realizes the projected destination
```

This gives us a stronger form of the Dharma-first interpretation:

```text
The individual transition-being does not determine its course.
Its course determines the individual transition-form.
```

The `gandharva` is therefore not an autonomous agent inserted between two lives. It is a determinate phase of a karmically projected `santāna`.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya materially sharpens two expressions.

### `karmarddhivegavān`

Retain:

```text
possessing the speed of karmic power
```

but understand `ṛddhi` here specifically as an extraordinary movement through space generated by karma.

### `anivartyaḥ`

The first-pass rendering “irreversible” is defensible but underspecified.

Better contextual rendering:

```text
unable to turn back from its destined course
```

or:

```text
fixed to its projected destiny
```

The former is preferable in the running translation because it preserves the verbal force while the note supplies the technical meaning.

### Best revised translation

> The intermediate being is visible to others of its own class and to the purified divine eye; it moves with the speed of karmic power, possesses all five faculties, is unobstructed and unable to turn back from its destined course, and feeds on scent.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The most useful contact point is not the supernatural imagery but the structure of **constrained transition**:

```text
State_t
    terminates

TransitionState
    is projected toward one determinate successor
    has no independent destination-selection
    persists until successor-conditions are complete

State_t+1
    actualizes that projected successor
```

This strengthens the distinction already emerging between naive individualization and Dharma-first determination.

The apparent individual `gandharva` contributes no independent principle of truth. Its intelligibility comes from:

```text
class
faculty
karma
projection
destination
conditions of completion
```

So even at its most “Bollywood,” the Bhāṣya keeps forcing the image back under Law.
