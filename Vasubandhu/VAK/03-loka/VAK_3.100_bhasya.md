# VAK_3.100 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
saṃvartanyaḥ punas tisro bhavanty agny-ambu-vāyubhiḥ /
dhyānatrayaṃ dvitīyādi śīrṣa tāsāṃ yathākramam // VAkK_3.100 //
```

**Working translation:** Moreover, there are three cosmic contractions, by fire, water, and wind. The second, third, and fourth dhyānas are respectively their upper limits.

### Source caution

The two pādas of VAkK 3.100 are separated by a long polemical excursus against a doctrine of eternal atoms. That excursus belongs to the natural Bhāṣya argument around this verse and should not be omitted merely because it interrupts the metrical unit.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first defines three `saṃvartanī` events: contraction by fire, water, and wind. A `saṃvartanī` is so called because beings belonging to one dhyāna are together involved in that contraction. Fire-contraction occurs through seven suns, water-contraction through torrential water, and wind-contraction through violent wind. Nothing subtle of the receptacle-world remains after these destructions.

This claim provokes a polemic against outsiders who maintain that eternal atoms survive cosmic destruction because gross things could not arise again without seed-atoms. Vasubandhu rejects that necessity. The power of beings' karma and the formative winds already provide a causal basis for renewed world-formation; Mahīśāsaka scripture even speaks of winds carrying seeds from other world-systems.

The opponents then insist that gross things arise only from homogeneous constituent substances. Vasubandhu attacks this by denying that a mat is a substance distinct from reeds or a cloth a substance distinct from threads. The same constituents, differently arranged, receive different designations, just as a row of ants is nothing over and above the ants arranged in a row. A supposed whole distinct from its parts cannot be established perceptually. Thus the idea of an independently existing gross substance over and above its constituents fails.

He then turns the argument directly against eternal atoms. The designation `paramāṇu` is established only with respect to rūpa and the other sensible determinations. When these perish, the atom is thereby destroyed; there is no separately ascertainable eternal atomic substance beyond them.

Returning to the verse, Vasubandhu defines `śīrṣa` as the level immediately above the reach of a given destruction. Thus fire destroys below the second dhyāna, water below the third, and wind below the fourth.

He then asks why precisely these three lower dhyānas are destroyed by fire, water, and wind. His answer is `tad-apakṣāla-sādharmya`: similarity between an internal defect/disturbance and the corresponding external destructive medium.

- In the first dhyāna, `vitarka` and `vicāra` are the internal `apakṣāla`; because they scorch the mind, they are fire-like.
- In the second dhyāna, `prīti` is the internal `apakṣāla`; because together with pliancy it softens the support, it is water-like.
- In the third dhyāna, inhalation and exhalation are themselves winds.

Accordingly, where a given internal disturbance exists in meditative attainment, a corresponding external disturbance manifests at that dhyāna-world level.

There is no earth-destruction because the receptacle itself is earth; earth is opposed by fire, water, and wind, not by earth itself. The fourth dhyāna has no such destruction because it lacks internal `apakṣāla` and is designated `āneñja`, immovable. Hence no corresponding external disturbance operates there.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — definition of the three contractions; critique of eternal atoms; account of the dhyāna limits and internal/external correspondence.

**Voice: Objector** — the position that eternal atoms must survive because gross things cannot arise without atomic seeds, together with the claim that substances arise only from homogeneous substances.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — the general cosmological mapping of fire, water, and wind contractions and their dhyāna limits.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃvartanī** — a cosmic contraction/destruction in which beings associated with a dhyāna-level are together implicated
  - **status:** developed beyond the Kārikā
  - **function:** cosmological classification

- **śīrṣa** — the level immediately above the reach of a destruction
  - **status:** glossed explicitly in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** defines the upper boundary of each saṃvartanī

- **apakṣāla** — internal disturbance, defect, or destabilizing factor
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical term in this passage
  - **function:** explains why a given dhyāna-world is vulnerable to a corresponding external destruction

- **āneñja** — immovable, unshaken
  - **status:** scriptural/doctrinal term
  - **function:** explains the absence of a cosmic destruction at the fourth dhyāna

- **paramāṇu-nityatva** — permanence of atoms
  - **status:** opponent doctrine
  - **function:** target of Vasubandhu's anti-substantialist polemic

## Doctrinal Determination

The three destructions are not merely three physical catastrophes. They are ordered according to dhyāna structure:

```text
fire-contraction
    → destroys below second dhyāna
    → first-dhyāna internal analogue: vitarka-vicāra

water-contraction
    → destroys below third dhyāna
    → second-dhyāna internal analogue: prīti

wind-contraction
    → destroys below fourth dhyāna
    → third-dhyāna internal analogue: breathing

fourth dhyāna
    → no internal apakṣāla
    → āneñja
    → no corresponding external saṃvartanī
```

The cosmology therefore correlates the instability of world-levels with determinations already present within their corresponding meditative structures.

The atom excursus is equally important: Vasubandhu refuses to posit an indestructible material substrate surviving world-dissolution. Re-formation is explained through causal efficacy, karma, wind, and conditions—not through an eternal atomic remainder.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and expansion required.**

The basic translation of the two pādas is correct, but `śīrṣa` should be understood specifically as **the level immediately above the region destroyed**, not simply a summit within the destruction itself.

The Kārikā also conceals a major doctrinal argument: Vasubandhu inserts a sustained critique of eternal atoms between its two halves.

## Logical Determination

The argument has two distinct but connected forms.

First:

```text
world-destruction
    → leaves no subtle receptacle remainder
    → no need for eternal atomic seed
    → renewed formation follows causal conditions and karma
```

Second:

```text
internal dhyāna instability
    ↔ analogous external world-instability
```

This is a correspondence within conditioned Loka. It should not be elevated into a transcendental genetic principle. The dhyāna-worlds and their destructions remain produced, conditioned determinations.

## Organon Contact Point

The most interesting contact point is Vasubandhu's rejection of a whole as an independently existing substance over and above its constituents:

```text
threads arranged as cloth
    → designation of cloth
    ≠ second substance added to threads
```

This is useful logical material because it distinguishes **determinate organization and designation** from the hypothesis of an additional substrate. But the Bhāṣya remains engaged in Abhidharma ontology, not in a theory of Absolute genesis.
