# VAK_3.94_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor


**Working translation:** Buddhahood arises during decline, from the period below the eighty-thousand-year lifespan down to the hundred-year lifespan. Pratyekabuddhas arise in both [increase and decline]; the rhinoceros-like solitary type has a hundred-kalpa preparatory continuity.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first asks whether Buddhas arise during the upward or downward phase of the lifespan cycle. The answer is precise: they arise only during `apakarṣa`, the declining phase. More exactly, once decline has begun from the eighty-thousand-year lifespan, Buddhas arise during the interval ending when human lifespan reaches one hundred years.

The Bhāṣya then explains both boundaries.

Buddhas do not arise during `utkarṣa`, the increasing phase, because beings are then `duḥsamudvejāḥ`: difficult to stir or arouse to urgency regarding suffering. The issue is therefore not merely the amount of lifespan but the practical receptivity of beings to the Dharma.

Nor do Buddhas arise once lifespan has fallen below one hundred years. At that point the five `kaṣāya` become predominant:

1. `āyuṣkaṣāya` — corruption of lifespan,
2. `kalpakaṣāya` — corruption of the age,
3. `kleśakaṣāya` — corruption of afflictions,
4. `dṛṣṭikaṣāya` — corruption of views,
5. `sattvakaṣāya` — corruption of beings.

Vasubandhu then asks when Pratyekabuddhas arise. Unlike samyaksaṃbuddhas, they may arise in both directions of the lifespan cycle: increase and decline.

Two types are distinguished:

- `vargacārin` — those who move in groups; generally explained as having formerly belonged to the śrāvaka path,
- `khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpa` — the rhinoceros-horn-like solitary type, an `ekavihārin`, one who lives alone.

The solitary type requires a preparation extending through one hundred mahākalpas of `bodhisaṃbhāra`, accumulation for awakening.

The Bhāṣya finally asks why Pratyekabuddhas do not teach others extensively. Vasubandhu rejects three explanations: they are not incapable of teaching, not without compassion, and the beings around them are not intrinsically incapable of instruction. Rather, through previous cultivation they are disposed toward `alpotsukatā`, little concern or involvement; they do not undertake the demanding task of leading others into profound Dharma.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu.

The passage is exegetical and argumentative throughout, with a sequence of explicit questions and answers. The remarks on alternative origins of `vargacārin` Pratyekabuddhas preserve other opinions, but the principal explanatory voice remains Vasubandhu.

## New Technical Terms

- **kaṣāya** — corruption, deterioration, or turbidity characteristic of a declining age
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-developed doctrinal term in this passage
  - **function:** explains why Buddhas no longer arise below the hundred-year lifespan

- **āyuṣkaṣāya** — corruption of lifespan
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya list of five corruptions
  - **function:** deterioration of life-duration

- **kalpakaṣāya** — corruption of the age
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya list
  - **function:** deterioration of the temporal/world condition

- **kleśakaṣāya** — corruption of afflictions
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya list
  - **function:** deterioration of the wholesome side through dominance of affliction

- **dṛṣṭikaṣāya** — corruption of views
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya list
  - **function:** deterioration through wrong views

- **sattvakaṣāya** — corruption of beings
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya list
  - **function:** deterioration of embodied capacities, including measure, beauty, health, strength, recollection, vigor, and steadiness

- **duḥsamudveja** — difficult to arouse to urgency
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here
  - **function:** explains why Buddhas do not arise during lifespan increase

- **vargacārin** — group-going Pratyekabuddha
  - **status:** Bhāṣya classification
  - **function:** one of two Pratyekabuddha types

- **khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpa** — rhinoceros-horn-like solitary Pratyekabuddha
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā's `khaḍga`
  - **function:** solitary Pratyekabuddha type

- **ekavihārin** — one who dwells alone
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss
  - **function:** defines the mode of life of the `khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpa`

- **bodhisaṃbhāra** — accumulation/equipment for awakening
  - **status:** Bhāṣya doctrinal term
  - **function:** names the preparatory accumulation cultivated for one hundred mahākalpas

- **alpotsukatā** — little concern, low involvement
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** explains why Pratyekabuddhas do not undertake extensive teaching of others

## Doctrinal Determination

The temporal placement of Buddhahood is not arbitrary:

```text
utkarṣa
    → beings difficult to arouse to urgency
    → no Buddha arising

apakarṣa
    → below 80,000-year lifespan
    → down to 100-year lifespan
    → Buddha arising possible

below 100 years
    → five kaṣāyas dominant
    → no Buddha arising
```

Thus the world-age supplies a practical condition for the appearance of a Buddha: suffering must be sufficiently manifest to make beings teachable, but the world must not yet be so corrupted that the Dharma cannot function effectively.

Pratyekabuddhas have a wider temporal range:

```text
utkarṣa
    → possible

apakarṣa
    → possible
```

Their distinction from Buddhas is therefore not merely quantitative preparation. Their historical function differs: Buddhas establish a teaching for others; Pratyekabuddhas awaken independently, and the solitary type is characteristically disinclined toward extensive instruction.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required.**

The first-pass Kārikā analysis treated `śatād yāvat` too vaguely as a “hundred-year marker.” The Bhāṣya establishes the full interval:

```text
from the beginning of decline below 80,000 years
    → until lifespan reaches 100 years
```

Thus a better translation is:

> Buddhahood arises during decline, from the period below the eighty-thousand-year lifespan down to the hundred-year lifespan. Pratyekabuddhas arise in both [increase and decline]; the rhinoceros-like solitary one follows from a hundred kalpas.

The final phrase is further clarified by the Bhāṣya: the `kalpaśata` are specifically **one hundred mahākalpas** spent in the accumulations for awakening.

## Logical Determination

This passage distinguishes a temporal condition from a sufficient cause.

```text
world-phase
    → determines teachability conditions
    → permits or excludes Buddha appearance
```

The decline itself does not produce Buddhahood. Rather, a certain interval of decline provides the conditioned world-field in which a Buddha can effectively appear and teach.

The upper and lower bounds are therefore practical rather than merely numerical:

```text
too little felt urgency
    → no Buddha

teachable suffering-condition
    → Buddha possible

too much corruption
    → no Buddha
```

## Organon Contact Point

The important point is restrained but strong: **Practical Reason requires a determinate field of receptivity.** The Dharma does not appear historically at an arbitrary point in Loka. Yet this temporal conditioning remains a produced condition of appearance, not a genetic ground of Buddhahood itself.
