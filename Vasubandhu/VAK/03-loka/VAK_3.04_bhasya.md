# VAK_3.04_bhasya — The Five Destinies within the Three Domains

## Kārikā

**Best translation:**  
Among those domains are the five destinies, named by their own names beginning with naraka. They are only unafflicted and indeterminate, designated as beings; intermediate existence is not [a destiny].

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

The three domains having been stated, the five destinies within them are called by their own names: hell-beings, animals, pretas, devas, and humans.

In the desire-domain there are four complete destinies and a portion of the fifth; in the form and formless domains there is a portion of only one, the deva destiny.

Are there then dharmas belonging to the domains that are free from destiny, since the text says that the destinies are *in* the domains? Yes. Wholesome, afflicted, container-world, and intermediate-existence dharmas also belong to domains, but they are not themselves the five destinies.

The five destinies are only non-obscured indeterminate and are designated as sentient beings; intermediate existence is not itself a destiny. Otherwise the destinies would become mixed together.

The Prajñapti text states that the five destinies are included within the four modes of birth, but the four modes of birth are not exhausted by the five destinies, because intermediate existence remains outside them. The Dharmaskandha likewise distinguishes intermediate existence from the forms belonging to the established destinies.

A sūtra speaks of seven existences: hell existence, animal existence, preta existence, deva existence, human existence, karma-existence, and intermediate existence. Here the five destinies are stated together with their cause and with that which accompanies passage to them. Precisely because their cause, karma-existence, is excluded from the destinies themselves, the destinies are established as non-obscured indeterminate.

The Kāśmīras also cite a sūtra attributed to Śāriputra: when karmas to be experienced in hell are accumulated, the five aggregates ripen there as result; once that result has arisen, one receives the designation “hell-being.” Yet no hell-being is found apart from those dharmas.

### Philosophical Translation

The Bhāṣya sharply separates three levels that the compressed Kārikā could easily allow us to conflate:

```text
dhātu
    domain in which dharmas occur

gati
    a resultant course or destiny of sentient existence

karmabhava
    the causal activity that produces such a destiny

antarābhava
    the transitional existence between established destinies
```

A destiny is therefore not the whole domain, not its karmic cause, and not the interval through which one passes toward it. It is a determinate resultant configuration of sentient existence.

## Course of the Argument

### 1. The five destinies are placed inside the three domains

The three domains are broader than the five destinies. The Bhāṣya makes this explicit by asking why the verse says the destinies are *within* the domains rather than simply identifying them with the domains.

```text
dhātu > gati
```

A domain also contains wholesome and afflicted dharmas, the container-world, and intermediate existence.

### 2. Gati must be separated from moral causation

The destinies themselves are `anivṛtāvyākṛta`—non-obscured indeterminate. If the karmic cause of a destiny were included in the destiny itself, the categories would collapse into one another.

```text
cause of destiny
    ≠
destiny as resultant mode of existence
```

The sūtra's separate `karmabhava` supplies the decisive control.

### 3. Gati must also be separated from transition

`antarābhava` is explicitly excluded from the five destinies. It belongs to becoming, but not to one of the settled destination classes.

```text
bhava
    broader than gati

antarābhava
    bhava
    not gati
```

### 4. “Being” is a designation upon resultant dharmas

The Śāriputra citation gives the deepest ontological clarification in the passage. When the relevant aggregates arise as karmic result, the conventional designation “hell-being” is applied; no separate hell-being is found apart from those dharmas.

```text
vipāka-dharmas
    ↓
resultant configuration
    ↓
saṃkhyā / designation
    “nāraka”
```

This prevents `sattva` from becoming an independent substance behind the aggregates.

## Voices

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma voice** — explains the relation among `dhātu`, `gati`, `antarābhava`, and `karmabhava`.
- **Voice: textual authorities** — Prajñapti, Dharmaskandha, and sūtra citations are used as doctrinal controls.
- **Voice: Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣika citation** — introduces the Śāriputra passage to reinforce the resultant and merely designative character of `gati` and `sattva`.

## New Technical Terms

- **gati** — destiny, course of sentient rebirth.
  - **status** — present in the Kārikā, substantially delimited only by the Bhāṣya here.
  - **function** — resultant classification inside a broader domain.
- **gatinirmukta** — free from / outside a destiny classification.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-only in this local discussion.
  - **function** — proves that `dhātu` is broader than `gati`.
- **anivṛtāvyākṛta** — non-obscured indeterminate.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya precision for the Kārikā's `akliṣṭāvyākṛta`.
  - **function** — distinguishes resultant destiny from afflicted or karmically productive causes.
- **gatisaṃbheda** — confusion or intermixture of destinies.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-only.
  - **function** — reductio controlling the exclusion of karmic causes from gati.
- **karmabhava** — karma-existence / karmic becoming.
  - **status** — introduced through sūtra citation.
  - **function** — causal source of destiny, explicitly outside the destiny itself.
- **sahetuka** — accompanied by / presented together with its cause.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-only here.
  - **function** — explains the sevenfold bhava formula.
- **vipāka** — result, maturation.
  - **status** — previously encountered; crucial here.
  - **function** — gives the resultant dharmas upon which the conventional designation of a destiny-being is imposed.
- **saṃkhyāṃ gacchati** — comes to be designated / receives the name.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-only phrase in this passage.
  - **function** — establishes the conventional, non-substantial status of `sattva` such as “hell-being.”

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.04 establishes a layered taxonomy of conditioned existence:

```text
dhātu
    broad domain
    includes many dharmas not themselves gatis

        ↓

gati
    resultant sentient destiny
    akliṣṭa / anivṛtāvyākṛta
    sattvākhyā

        ← caused by karmabhava
        ← reached through antarābhava
```

The destiny is thus a karmically generated result without being identical to the karma that generates it.

The final citation adds an important anti-substantialist determination:

```text
sattva
    = designation upon a resultant aggregate-complex
    ≠ independent entity behind the dharmas
```

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation correctly treats `gati` as destiny and excludes `antarābhava`. The Bhāṣya now makes explicit that:

1. `dhātu` is strictly broader than `gati`;
2. `gati` is a resultant, non-obscured indeterminate classification;
3. `karmabhava` is the causal ground of a destiny and therefore excluded from it;
4. `sattva` here is a designation upon resultant dharmas, not a separate substance.

## Logical Determination

The central formal operation is **discrimination of field, result, cause, and transition**.

```text
Field:
    dhātu

Result-position:
    gati

Cause:
    karmabhava

Transition:
    antarābhava

Designation of the resultant complex:
    sattva
```

This is considerably more precise than a simple taxonomy of beings.

The decisive relation is:

```text
karma produces placement
without being identical with placement
```

This is the first explicit internal preparation for the Chapter 4 thesis:

```text
karmajaṃ lokavaicitryam
```

## Organon Contact Point

VAK 3.04 gives us a methodological correction before any full Organon synthesis is attempted.

The formal cognition of World requires that we not collapse:

```text
world-field
course within the field
cause of the course
transition between courses
empirical designation of the bearer
```

So the sequence now looks more rigorous:

```text
Dhātu
    field of determination

Gati
    determinate resultant placement

Karmabhava
    determining activity generating placement
```

The transition from Loka to Karma is therefore not an imported analogy. The Bhāṣya has already begun generating it from within the analysis of `gati` itself.

For the later Organon Comment, this will be important: the Tamasic or supersensible problem cannot be solved merely by subtracting sensible form. Formal cognition must recover the **ground of determinate placement**. VAK 3.04 starts that recovery by separating the result from its generating cause.
