# VAK_3.55_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
godānīyaḥ sahasrāṇi sapta sārdhāni maṇḍalaḥ /
sārdhe dve madhyamasya aṣṭau caturasraḥ kuruḥ samaḥ // VAkK_3.55 //
```

**Working translation:**

Aparagodānīya measures seven and a half thousand yojanas in total and is circular. Its middle measure is two and a half thousand. Uttarakuru measures eight thousand yojanas, is square, and equal on all sides.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.55.md`.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya locates Aparagodānīya west of Sumeru and gives it a total measure of seven and a half thousand yojanas. It is circular like the full moon. The middle measure is two and a half thousand yojanas.

Uttarakuru lies to the north. It is square, eight thousand yojanas in total, and strictly equal on all four sides, each side measuring two thousand yojanas.

The prose then adds a striking correspondence: the faces of the humans inhabiting each continent have the same shape as their continent.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — descriptive exposition.
- No separate opponent appears.

## New Technical Terms

- **maṇḍala** — circular / disk-shaped
  - **function:** shape of Aparagodānīya

- **pūrṇacandravat** — like the full moon
  - **function:** image clarifying its circular form

- **caturasra** — square / four-sided
  - **function:** shape of Uttarakuru

- **sama** — equal
  - **function:** equality of Uttarakuru's four sides

- **ākṛti** — form / shape
  - **function:** relation between continental shape and inhabitants' faces

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes the container-world and its inhabitants formally correspondent: regional shape is reflected in the bodily appearance of the humans who inhabit that region. This reinforces that `bhājanaloka` is not presented as an indifferent container detached from the forms appearing within it.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Bhāṣya specifies the full-moon analogy for Aparagodānīya and strict equality of Uttarakuru's sides.

## Logical Determination

```text
regional form
    → determinate habitation
        → corresponding bodily appearance
```

## Organon Contact Point

For later synthesis, this provides a native image for the thought that a Domain conditions the form in which appearance is given. The stronger formulation that a Domain is a “Light of Substance” remains our own conceptual development, not Vasubandhu's explicit doctrine.
