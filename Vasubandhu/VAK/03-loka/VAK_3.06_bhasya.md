# VAK_3.06_bhasya — Seven Knowledge-Stations and Nine Being-Abodes

## Kārikā

> vijñānasthitayaḥ sapta śeṣaṃ tatparibhedavat /
> bhavāgrāsaṃjñisattvāś ca sattvāvāsā nava smṛtāḥ // VAkK_3.6 //

**Best translation:**

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Continuous Translation

The three formless states are as stated in the sūtra; thus these are the seven knowledge-stations.

What, here, constitutes a knowledge-station? According to the case, it consists of the five aggregates or the four aggregates connected with that state.

Why is the remainder not a knowledge-station? Because:

> the remainder possesses what breaks it apart.

What is the remainder? The bad destinies, the fourth dhyāna, and the peak of existence. In these there are conditions that break apart vijñāna, and precisely for that reason they are not knowledge-stations.

What is the breaking-apart by which vijñāna is disrupted? In the bad destinies, painful feeling is the disruptive condition because of its injurious character. In the fourth dhyāna, the non-percipient state and the attainment of non-perception are disruptive. At the peak of existence, the attainment of cessation breaks the continuity of citta.

Another explanation is given: a knowledge-station is a state to which those here wish to go and from which those already there do not wish to depart. In the bad destinies neither condition is present. In the fourth dhyāna beings have unsettled intentions: ordinary persons wish to enter the non-percipient state, while noble ones wish to enter the Pure Abodes; and at the peak of existence the activity of knowing is weak. Therefore these are not knowledge-stations.

These, then, are the seven knowledge-stations already described. Together with the peak of existence and the non-percipient beings, nine abodes of beings are remembered, because beings dwell in these according to their own inclination.

## Course of the Argument

### 1. Naming the seven

The four embodied configurations of VAkK 3.5 together with the three formless states are formally named the **seven vijñānasthitis**.

### 2. What actually counts as a vijñānasthiti?

The Bhāṣya immediately refuses a naive reading. A knowledge-station is not simply a location populated by beings or even a location in which vijñāna happens to occur.

It asks:

```text
kā 'tra vijñānasthitiḥ?
What here is the knowledge-station?
```

The answer is dharma-first: the relevant four or five aggregates connected with that mode of existence constitute the station.

```text
vijñānasthiti
    ≠ substantial population
    ≠ individual soul-location

vijñānasthiti
    = determinate aggregate-complex
      capable of supporting the establishment of vijñāna
```

### 3. The criterion of exclusion: paribheda

The excluded cases are not excluded arbitrarily. They contain a **paribheda**, a condition under which vijñāna is broken apart or destabilized.

```text
bad destinies
    painful feeling disrupts vijñāna

fourth dhyāna
    non-percipient state / attainment interrupts vijñāna

bhavāgra
    cessation-attainment cuts the citta-continuity
```

Thus the classification is functional and principled.

### 4. A second criterion: inclination to enter and remain

A second explanation supplies a practical criterion:

```text
those here wish to go there
+
those there do not wish to leave
=
knowledge-station
```

The bad destinies fail both conditions. The fourth dhyāna does not provide stable satisfaction because beings there aim beyond their present state. The peak of existence likewise lacks sufficiently vigorous cognitive activity.

This second explanation gives `sthiti` an especially strong meaning: **establishment**, not mere presence.

### 5. Sattvāvāsa is broader than vijñānasthiti

The seven knowledge-stations plus `bhavāgra` and the non-percipient beings yield nine `sattvāvāsas`.

```text
vijñānasthiti
    narrower criterion:
    stable establishment of conditioned knowing

sattvāvāsa
    broader criterion:
    a mode in which beings dwell
```

So an abode of beings need not be a station of knowledge.

## Voices

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma voice** — identifies the seven and asks for the criterion of a knowledge-station.
- **Voice: doctrinal explanation** — explains exclusion through `vijñānaparibheda`.
- **Voice: uncertain** — `punar āha` introduces the alternative desire-to-enter / desire-to-remain criterion; the source does not identify the school in this passage.

## New Technical Terms

- **vijñānaparibheda** — disruption, breaking-apart, or destabilization of vijñāna.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā as a full compound; generated by the Bhāṣya from `tatparibhedavat`.
  - **function:** exclusion criterion explaining why some modes of existence are not knowledge-stations.

- **upaghātitatva** — injurious or damaging character.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this local argument.
  - **function:** explains why painful feeling in the bad destinies disrupts vijñāna.

- **cittasantaticcheda** — cutting or interruption of the continuity of citta.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion.
  - **function:** causal explanation for exclusion of bhavāgra as a knowledge-station.

- **gantukāmatā / avyuccalitukāmatā** — desire to go to a state / desire not to depart from it.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only criterion here.
  - **function:** alternative functional definition of `vijñānasthiti`.

- **apaṭupracāratva** — weak, non-vigorous, or dull operation/activity.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific.
  - **function:** explains the weakness of cognitive activity at bhavāgra in the alternative account.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAkK 3.06 turns the classification of beings into a theory of **conditions under which conditioned knowing can be established**.

The decisive relation is:

```text
aggregate-field
    ↓
condition of vijñāna
    ↓
stable establishment or disruption
    ↓
qualification as vijñānasthiti / non-vijñānasthiti
```

This confirms the Dharma-first character of the analysis. The unit of truth is not an isolated individual but the determinate complex of conditions through which a mode of knowing is possible.

That also clarifies individualization:

```text
individual taken in isolation
    adds no positive determination

individual as determined through the field
    expresses the field's lawful result
```

The singular can therefore carry **negative truth** when treated abstractly: it reveals precisely what is missing when the determining field is ignored.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised slightly.**

`śeṣaṃ tatparibhedavat` is stronger than a neutral “the remainder is according to its subdivision.” The Bhāṣya explicitly takes `paribheda` as what **breaks apart or disrupts vijñāna**.

Preferred translation:

> The knowledge-stations are seven; the remainder possesses conditions that break them apart. Together with the peak of existence and the non-percipient beings, nine abodes of beings are remembered.

This is a genuine Bhāṣya-driven correction to the first-pass understanding.

## Organon Contact Point

The significant result is not a cosmological inventory but a formal distinction between **being somewhere** and **knowing being stably established there**.

```text
Sattvāvāsa
    Being can abide.

Vijñānasthiti
    conditioned knowing can stand.
```

This is precisely where naive individualization loses truth. The isolated being does not explain its own cognitive state. The truth lies in the field of dharmic conditions that makes stable knowing possible or impossible.

So the movement is:

```text
individual appearance
    ↓ reflection into conditions
Dharma-field
    ↓
conditions of establishment
    ↓
knowledge-station
```

The Bhāṣya has therefore converted what looked like a taxonomy of inhabitants into a principled analysis of the **standing conditions of knowing within Loka**.
