## Segmentation by Natural Argument

The prose forms one compact opening argument:

```text
previous chapter classified citta and related dharmas by the three domains
    ↓
what are those three domains themselves?
    ↓
first determine kāmadhātu by its inhabitants / destinies
    ↓
include the six kāma-deva classes and the container-world
    ↓
ask how many locations this domain contains
    ↓
resolve the count as twenty
    ↓
state the vertical extent of the kāma-domain
```

The Bhāṣya therefore does more than gloss the names in the verse. It changes the level of analysis from classification **by domain** to determination **of the domain itself**.

## Continuous Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma voice**

This must now be explained. Citta and the other dharmas have been analyzed according to their restriction to the desire-, form-, and formless-domains. What, then, are these desire-, form-, and formless-domains?

“Hell-beings, pretas, animals, humans, and the six heaven-dwellers: the desire-domain.”

There are four destinies, together with six classes of devas, namely the Cāturmahārājakāyikas, Trāyastriṃśas, Yāmas, Tuṣitas, Nirmāṇaratis, and Paranirmitavaśavartins. This is the desire-domain, together with its container-world.

How many locations does this comprise? He says: “Through the differentiation of hells and continents, it is twentyfold.” The word “locations” is to be supplied to complete the sentence.

There are eight great hells: Saṃjīva, Kālasūtra, Saṃghāta, Raurava, Mahāraurava, Tapana, Pratāpana, and Avīci. There are four continents: Jambūdvīpa, Pūrvavideha, Avaragodānīya, and Uttarakuru. Together with the six classes of devas just mentioned, the animals, and the pretas, these make twenty locations.

The desire-domain extends from the Paranirmitavaśavartin devas down to Avīci; when the container-world is included, it extends down as far as the wind-circle (`vāyumaṇḍala`).

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Chapter 3 begins from a dependency created by Chapter 2

The opening sentence is decisive:

```text
kāmarūpārūpyanaiyamyena cittādīnāṃ kṛto nirdeśaḥ
```

Chapter 2 repeatedly classified cittas and other dharmas according to whether they belong to kāma-, rūpa-, or ārūpya-dhātu. The Bhāṣya now asks for the determination presupposed by those classifications:

```text
We have classified dharmas by domain.
What are the domains themselves?
```

So Lokanirdeśa does not begin as an unrelated cosmological excursus. It explicates a structural presupposition already operating in Indriyanirdeśa.

### 2. Kāma-domain is first specified through modes of sentient existence

The verse names:

```text
naraka
preta
tiryañc
manuṣya
six deva classes
```

The prose gathers the first four under:

```text
catasro gatayaḥ
four destinies
```

and then separately enumerates the six deva classes.

This is an important distinction. The first line is not yet the twentyfold count. It gives the principal sentient articulation of the kāma-domain.

### 3. `saha bhājanalokena` prevents reduction of dhātu to a class of beings

Vasubandhu then says explicitly:

```text
eṣa kāmadhātuḥ saha bhājanalokena
this is the kāma-domain together with the container-world
```

The kāma-domain therefore includes both:

```text
sattva-side
    beings / destinies / deva classes

bhājana-side
    the supporting world-container
```

This materially sharpens the first-pass determination of `dhātu = domain`. A domain is not merely a taxonomic class of inhabitants. It is the articulated field in which those beings have determinate placement.

### 4. The twentyfold determination is a count of `sthāna`

The Bhāṣya asks:

```text
sa eṣa kati sthānāni?
how many locations is this?
```

and explicitly supplies `sthānāni` as the understood noun after `viṃśatiḥ`.

The twenty are:

```text
8 great hells
4 continents
6 deva classes
1 animal location/class
1 preta location/class
-------------------------
20 sthānas
```

Humans are not added as a twenty-first location because human existence is distributed across the four continents. The verse's inhabitant-list and the prose's location-count therefore operate at two related but non-identical classificatory levels.

### 5. The domain receives a vertical limit

The final sentence determines the extent of kāmadhātu:

```text
sentient-world extent:
    Paranirmitavaśavartin
        ↓
    Avīci

including bhājanaloka:
    Paranirmitavaśavartin
        ↓
    vāyumaṇḍala
```

Thus `dhātu` is not merely a conceptual grouping. It has ordered extension and boundaries.

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma voice** — introduces Chapter 3 as an explication of the three domains presupposed by the preceding classifications, enumerates the kāma-domain, resolves the twentyfold count, and states its extent.
- No explicit objector or competing school position appears in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **naiyamya** — restriction, determination, or fixed belonging to a specified domain.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical operation in this opening passage.
  - **function:** retrospective bridge from Chapter 2; explains why Chapter 3 must now determine the domains themselves.

- **gati** — destiny / course of rebirth.
  - **status:** not present in VAkK 3.1 itself; introduced by the prose to classify the first four sentient modes.
  - **function:** doctrinal classification of sentient existence within kāmadhātu.

- **devanikāya** — class or group of devas.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā, which says only `ṣaḍ divaukasaḥ`.
  - **function:** explicit enumeration of the six kāma-deva classes.

- **bhājanaloka** — container-world; the world considered as the supporting field or receptacle of sentient existence.
  - **status:** absent from VAkK 3.1; technically decisive in the prose.
  - **function:** prevents identification of kāmadhātu with beings alone and introduces the sattva-world / container-world distinction developed later in the chapter.

- **sthāna** — location, station, determinate place.
  - **status:** supplied by the Bhāṣya as the elided noun governed by `viṃśatiḥ`.
  - **function:** determines what exactly the twentyfold count counts.

- **mahānaraka** — great hell.
  - **status:** prose expansion of the Kārikā's `naraka-bheda`.
  - **function:** supplies the eightfold internal differentiation required for the total of twenty.

- **sabhājanagrahaṇa** — inclusion/taking-in of the container-world.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expression.
  - **function:** distinguishes the lower boundary of the sentient articulation from the lower boundary obtained when the supporting world-container is included.

- **vāyumaṇḍala** — wind-circle / wind-disc.
  - **status:** absent from VAkK 3.1; anticipated here before its detailed cosmological treatment later in Chapter 3.
  - **function:** lower boundary of kāmadhātu when bhājanaloka is included.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation is basically sound, but the Bhāṣya permits one important refinement.

`viṃśatiḥ` should not be understood vaguely as “a twentyfold set” of beings or modes. Vasubandhu explicitly interprets it as:

```text
viṃśatiḥ sthānāni
20 locations
```

A more text-controlled translation is therefore:

> Hell-beings, pretas, animals, humans, and the six classes of heaven-dwellers constitute the desire-domain. Through the differentiation of its hells and continents, it comprises twenty locations.

The Bhāṣya also confirms that **domain** remains the right project rendering of `dhātu`: the prose gives kāmadhātu inhabitants, internal locations, a container-world, and upper/lower extent. “Element” would obscure precisely the articulated field Vasubandhu is constructing.

## Doctrinal / Logical Determination

The decisive logical development is:

```text
Chapter 2:
    dharmas classified by domain

Chapter 3:
    domain itself becomes object of determination
```

This is a reflective turn. A distinction previously used as a classifier is now itself explicated.

Within VAK 3.01:

```text
kāmadhātu
    ↓
sentient articulation
    gati + devanikāya
    ↓
container articulation
    bhājanaloka
    ↓
internal differentiation
    sthāna
    ↓
quantified totality
    viṃśatiḥ
    ↓
bounded extent
    Paranirmitavaśavartin → Avīci / vāyumaṇḍala
```

The world is therefore not merely “counted.” More precisely:

```text
World-domain
    = inhabitants
    + places of placement
    + supporting container
    + ordered limits
```

This is the Bhāṣya's first substantial enrichment of the Kārikā.

## Organon Contact Point

The earlier Kārikā analysis correctly identified VAK 3.01 as the opening appearance of world as a differentiated domain. The Bhāṣya now makes that idea more exact.

The strongest Organon contact point is not yet a metaphysics of “worldling consciousness.” It is the logical operation by which a previously presupposed classifier becomes an explicit structured field:

```text
use(domain)
    ↓
reflect on domain
    ↓
articulate domain
```

And the articulation is reciprocal:

```text
beings determine the domain as inhabited
    ↕
the domain determines beings as placed
```

The `saha bhājanalokena` clause is especially important. The world is neither merely the beings nor merely an external container. The determinate kāma-world is the articulated unity of sentient placements and their world-support.

This should remain a light contact point for now. Later verses will determine `bhājanaloka` in its own right, and Chapter 4 will supply the genetic question announced by `karmajaṃ lokavaicitryam`—how this differentiated world comes to be karma-born.

---

## Summary

VAK 3.01 opens Lokanirdeśa by making explicit a structure already presupposed throughout Chapter 2. Cittas and dharmas had been classified according to the three domains; now the domains themselves must be determined.

The Bhāṣya defines the kāma-domain through four destinies and six deva classes, explicitly includes the `bhājanaloka`, interprets the verse's twentyfold count as **twenty locations**, and fixes the vertical extent of the domain. The decisive refinement is therefore:

```text
kāmadhātu
    is not merely a class of desire-realm beings
    and not merely an abstract region

kāmadhātu
    = an articulated field of sentient existence,
      determinate placement,
      supporting world-container,
      and ordered extent
```
