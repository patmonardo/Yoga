# VAK_3.79_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
nṛṇāṃ varṣāṇi pañcāśad ahorātro divaukasām /
kāme 'dharāṇāṃ tenāyuḥ pañcavarṣaśatāni tu // VAkK_3.79 //
```

**Working translation:** Fifty human years are one day-and-night for the lower kāma-devas; by that measure their lifespan is five hundred divine years.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.79.md`.

## Natural Argument

After giving human lifespans, Vasubandhu turns to divine lifespan. A deva lifespan cannot be stated until the corresponding divine day-and-night has first been established.

The lower kāma-devas are identified explicitly as the Cāturmahārājakāyikas. For them:

```text
50 human years
    = 1 divine day-and-night

30 divine day-nights
    = 1 divine month

12 divine months
    = 1 divine year

500 divine years
    = lifespan of a Cāturmahārājakāyika deva
```

The Bhāṣya then immediately states the rule that will govern the higher kāma-devas: both the day-and-night measure and the lifespan increase successively by doubling.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Straightforward exposition and calendrical specification.

## New Technical Terms

- **rātrindiva / ahorātra** — day-and-night as the basic divine temporal unit
  - **status:** operative here as a formal comparative unit
  - **function:** establishes the conversion from human to divine time

- **vyavasthāpya** — having established / fixed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** marks the methodological need to establish the day-night unit before lifespan can be calculated

- **āyuḥ-pramāṇa** — measure of lifespan
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** identifies lifespan as a quantified temporal measure

- **dviguṇottara** — successively doubled
  - **status:** introduced at the transition to the following sequence
  - **function:** gives the rule for higher kāma-deva temporal measures

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse does not merely state that gods live longer. It establishes a realm-specific temporal conversion.

The Cāturmahārājakāyika lifespan is calculated internally from a divine calendar whose basic day-night already equals fifty human years. Divine duration is therefore expressed through a nested proportional system rather than by direct comparison alone.

The following rule, `dviguṇottaram`, shows that the higher kāma-deva realms form an ordered temporal series in which both the basic clock-unit and total lifespan increase together.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

`kāme 'dharāṇām` should be rendered more precisely as referring to the **lowest kāma-devas, the Cāturmahārājakāyikas**, rather than vaguely to lower heavenly dwellers.

The phrase `tenāyuḥ pañcavarṣaśatāni` means five hundred **divine** years calculated by that divine day-night and calendar.

## Logical Determination

```text
human temporal measure
    → conversion into divine day-night
        → divine month
            → divine year
                → divine lifespan
```

The determination is recursive but exact: first establish the unit, then calculate the duration.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
a temporal field is determined by its unit of measure
```

The produced Loka has not only different durations but different internally articulated temporal scales.
