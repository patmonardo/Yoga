### Source Caution

The Kārikā is split across the Bhāṣya: the first half is cited at [187|25], while `VAkK_3.99` is printed after the second half at [188|21]. The source prints `ythākramam`; this is preserved above rather than silently normalized.

The Bhāṣya explicitly states that `ca` is cumulative (`samuccayārthaḥ`). Therefore the durations are not simply 7 days / 7 months / 7 years.

## Natural Argument

At the minimum human lifespan of ten years, an intermediate kalpa reaches its terminal crisis (`niryāṇa`). Vasubandhu gives three possible modes: weapons, disease, and famine.

The weapon calamity arises when beings are saturated with unlawful attachment, distorted greed, false dharma, and hostility at its maximum. Merely seeing another being occasions an intense intention to strike and kill. Whatever is picked up—a stick or clod—appears as a sharp weapon, and beings kill one another.

The disease calamity is explained through the same defects: nonhuman beings release destructive affliction, incurable diseases arise, and beings die.

The famine calamity occurs when, because of those same defects, the gods do not release rain. Vasubandhu names three forms of famine:

- `cañca`
- `śvetāsthi`
- `śalākāvṛtti`

`Cañca` is associated both with collective gatherings of starving people and with containers in which seed is preserved for future generations. `Śvetāsthi`, “white bone,” is explained through bodies drying so severely that bones quickly whiten, and through starving people boiling gathered white bones for nourishment. `Śalākāvṛtti` is a regime of rationing by tally-stick: households allocate turns for eating, while tiny remnants of grain are extracted with sticks and boiled in much water.

A traditional statement then adds a practical karmic determination: one who has preserved abstention from killing even for a single day, or respectfully given even one harītakī or one alms-meal to the Saṅgha, does not arise in these weapon-, disease-, or famine-antarakalpas.

Finally Vasubandhu explains the durations. The weapon calamity lasts seven days. Disease lasts seven months **and seven days**. Famine lasts seven years **and seven months and seven days**. The repeated `ca` is explicitly cumulative.

The other two continents experience corresponding analogues rather than the full Jambūdvīpa calamities: hostility reaches excess, together with ugliness/weakness and hunger/thirst.

## Translation

For humans whose lifespan is ten years, the intermediate kalpa reaches its exit. That exit occurs in three ways: through weapons, disease, and famine.

At that time humans are inflamed by unlawful attachment, overwhelmed by distorted greed, and encompassed by false dharma. Their hostility reaches its extreme. On seeing another being, an intense intention to strike and kill immediately arises. Whatever they take up—a stick or even a clod of earth—becomes for them a sharp weapon, and they deprive one another of life.

At another such terminal period, because of those same faults, nonhuman beings release affliction and incurable diseases arise, from which humans die. Again, because of those same faults, the gods do not send rain, and famine arises in the forms called `cañca`, `śvetāsthi`, and `śalākāvṛtti`.

It is traditionally said that one who has guarded abstention from killing even for a single day, or has respectfully given even one harītakī or one alms-meal to the Saṅgha, is not born in those intermediate kalpas of weapons, disease, or famine.

How long do these calamities last? The weapon-killing lasts seven days. Disease lasts seven months and seven days. Famine lasts seven years, seven months, and seven days. The particle `ca` has cumulative force.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The exposition is largely direct doctrinal explanation. At [188|17] `evaṃ varṇayanti` marks a received traditional statement concerning minimal wholesome conduct and avoidance of birth in the calamity-periods.

## New Technical Terms

- **niryāṇa / nirgama** — exit, terminal passage of an intermediate kalpa
  - **status:** newly prominent in this passage
  - **function:** names the terminal crisis-condition of the ten-year lifespan phase

- **adharmarāga** — unlawful / non-Dharmic attachment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical term
  - **function:** ethical-affective condition of the weapon calamity

- **viṣamalobha** — distorted / perverse greed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical term
  - **function:** intensifies the terminal moral degeneration

- **mithyādharma** — false dharma / wrong normative order
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical term
  - **function:** marks corruption of the social-normative field

- **cañca** — one form of terminal famine
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanation here
  - **function:** names collective starvation and emergency preservation practices

- **śvetāsthi** — “white bone,” one form of terminal famine
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical term
  - **function:** names extreme bodily desiccation and bone-based subsistence

- **śalākāvṛtti** — “tally-stick subsistence,” one form of terminal famine
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical term
  - **function:** names rationing and extraction of minute grain remnants by stick

- **samuccayārtha** — having cumulative force
  - **status:** grammatical-exegetical term
  - **function:** determines the correct interpretation of repeated `ca` in the duration statement

## Doctrinal Determination

The three terminal calamities belong to the ten-year lifespan phase of an **antarakalpa**:

```text
ethical degeneration
    → ten-year lifespan
        → antarakalpa niryāṇa
            → weapon
            → disease
            → famine
```

Their durations are cumulative:

```text
weapon
    → 7 days

disease
    → 7 months + 7 days

famine
    → 7 years + 7 months + 7 days
```

The passage also directly links even minimal wholesome action to exclusion from these terminal birth-conditions. This prepares the transition from Loka to Karma: the world-condition is inseparable from the accumulated courses of action of beings.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required.**

The repository Kārikā translation reads the durations as simply seven days, seven months, and seven years respectively. Vasubandhu explicitly says the repeated `ca` is cumulative (`samuccayārthaḥ`). A more accurate translation is:

> The exit of the intermediate kalpa occurs through weapons, disease, and famine: respectively for seven days; seven months together with seven days; and seven years together with seven months and seven days.

The Bhāṣya also specifies that `kalpasya` here means the exit of the **intermediate kalpa** (`antarakalpasya niryāṇam`), not the destruction of the entire mahākalpa.

## Logical Determination

The passage turns the prior genealogy of moral decline into determinate world-crisis:

```text
rāga / lobha / mithyādharma
    → vyāpāda at maximum
        → social and bodily catastrophe
            → measured terminal interval
```

The calamities are differentiated expressions of the same produced Loka-condition: violence, disease, and scarcity. They are not independent cosmic principles.

## Organon Contact Point

A light contact point is the conversion of qualitative ethical degeneration into determinate objective conditions of a world:

```text
degenerated practical relations
    → produced social-natural crisis
```

The important guardrail remains: this is a genealogy **within produced Loka**. The text is explaining how a determinate world-condition is generated through the actions and dispositions of beings, not deriving Loka from a transcendental principle.
