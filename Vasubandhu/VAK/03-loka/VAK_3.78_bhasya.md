# VAK_3.78_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
sahasrāmāyuḥ kuruṣu dvayorardhārdhavarjitam /
ihāniyatam ante tu daśābdāḥ ādito 'mitam // VAkK_3.78 //
```

**Working translation:** In the Kurus the lifespan is one thousand years. In the other two continents it is reduced by half and again by half. Here, in Jambūdvīpa, lifespan is not fixed: at the end it is ten years, while at the beginning it is immeasurable.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.78.md`.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya explicitly shifts from differences of bodily measure to differences of lifespan.

The four human continents are then assigned distinct temporal measures:

```text
Uttarakuru
    → 1,000 years

Godānīya
    → 500 years

Pūrvavideha
    → 250 years

Jambūdvīpa
    → no fixed lifespan
```

The expression `dvayor ardhārdha-varjitam` is therefore concretely interpreted as the progressive reduction that yields 500 years for Godānīya and 250 years for Pūrvavideha.

Jambūdvīpa is different in kind. Vasubandhu says there is no `āyuṣo niyama`, no fixed determination of lifespan: sometimes it is greater, sometimes less. At the terminal point of decline it reaches ten years.

At the beginning of the kalpa, however, the lifespan of the first humans (`prāthamakalpikāḥ manuṣyāḥ`) is `aparimāṇa`, immeasurable. It cannot be measured even by numerical series beginning with thousands.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is direct explanatory prose without a competing doctrinal voice.

## New Technical Terms

- **āyuṣo niyama** — fixed determination of lifespan
  - **status:** made technically operative in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** defines the contrast between fixed continental lifespans and Jambūdvīpa variability

- **hīyamāna** — declining, diminishing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** explains how Jambūdvīpa lifespan reaches the ten-year minimum

- **prāthamakalpika** — belonging to the first period of the kalpa; the first humans of the kalpa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** specifies the subjects whose lifespan is immeasurable

- **aparimāṇa** — immeasurable
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss on `amita`
  - **function:** explains that the initial lifespan cannot be captured by ordinary numerical measure

## Doctrinal Determination

The crucial distinction is not merely four different numerical lifespans.

Three continents have determinate measures:

```text
Uttarakuru → 1000
Godānīya   → 500
Pūrvavideha→ 250
```

Jambūdvīpa instead has a variable temporal condition. Its lifespan belongs to a process of increase and decline rather than to one fixed number.

This introduces temporal variability into the produced world-field without turning time into a genetic Principle. The Loka remains conditioned and produced; the temporal measures are determinations within that produced order.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The earlier provisional wording “the two other continents are successively reduced by halves” is acceptable only if the explicit values are supplied:

```text
Godānīya    → 500 years
Pūrvavideha → 250 years
```

`iha aniyatam` should be rendered strongly as:

> “Here, in Jambūdvīpa, lifespan is not fixed.”

`ādito 'mitam` is not merely “very long.” The Bhāṣya glosses it as `aparimāṇa`: immeasurable and not numerically expressible even through thousand-based reckoning.

## Logical Determination

```text
spatial measure of bodies
    → temporal measure of lives

fixed lifespan
    → Uttarakuru / Godānīya / Pūrvavideha

variable lifespan
    → Jambūdvīpa
        → decline to 10 years
        → initial immeasurability
```

The verse therefore introduces a new kind of measure: not magnitude of body but duration of embodied existence.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
measure of embodiment
    → measure of duration
```

Jambūdvīpa is distinguished by variability rather than a single fixed temporal quantity. The Bhāṣya presents this as a property of the conditioned world-order, not as a transcendental principle of time.
