# VAK_3.75_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
jāmbūdvīpāḥ pramāṇena catuḥsārdhatrihastakāḥ /
dviguṇottaravṛddhayā tu purvagodottarāvhayāḥ // VAkK_3.75 //
```

**Working translation:** The humans of Jambūdvīpa have the stated measure in hastas; those called Pūrva, Goda, and Uttara increase successively by doubling.

## Natural Argument

The discussion turns from differences in the measures of world-containers (`bhājana`) to differences in the bodily measures of the beings who inhabit them. Vasubandhu asks whether inhabitants vary in magnitude just as their environments do, and answers affirmatively.

For Jambūdvīpa humans, the principal reading gives a bodily height of three and a half `hasta`; an alternative says four `hasta`.

The remaining three human populations are then stated exactly:

```text
Pūrvavideha   → 8 hastas
Godānīya      → 16 hastas
Uttarakuru    → 32 hastas
```

Thus `dviguṇottaravṛddhi` governs the successive series among these three: each following population is twice the preceding one.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is straightforward exposition, with a brief alternative measure introduced by `kecit tu` (“some, however”).

## New Technical Terms

- **pramāṇabheda** — difference in measure or magnitude
  - **status:** locally explicit in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** connects differences among cosmological containers with differences among their inhabitants

- **bhājana** — container, environmental world-support
  - **status:** previously encountered cosmological term
  - **function:** names the environmental side of the inhabited world whose measures are being compared

- **tadvāsin** — inhabitant of that [world/container]
  - **status:** local descriptive term
  - **function:** shifts the analysis from world-structure to embodied beings

- **ardhacaturhasta** — three-and-a-half hastas
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** gives the principal bodily measure for Jambūdvīpa humans

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes clear that cosmological differentiation includes the bodies of inhabitants, not only the dimensions of continents, mountains, and world-systems.

```text
measured world-container
    → measured inhabitant
```

But the numerical relation must be kept exact. Jambūdvīpa is given as three and a half hastas in the principal account, with four hastas as an alternative. The clean doubling series begins with Pūrvavideha at eight hastas and proceeds through Godānīya at sixteen to Uttarakuru at thirty-two.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Material clarification.**

The earlier Kārikā analysis correctly recognized successive doubling but left the Jambūdvīpa measure unresolved. The Bhāṣya now fixes it:

- principal account: 3½ hastas;
- alternative account: 4 hastas;
- Pūrvavideha, Godānīya, Uttarakuru: 8, 16, 32 hastas respectively.

Accordingly, `dviguṇottaravṛddhyā` should not be read as requiring a strict doubling from the principal 3½-hasta Jambūdvīpa measure. It describes the ordered increase of the three subsequently named populations.

## Logical Determination

```text
difference of containers
    → question of difference among inhabitants
    → affirmative answer
    → determinate bodily measures by continent
```

The reasoning is classificatory and quantitative: world-position correlates with a determinate bodily magnitude.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only: produced Loka determines not merely spatial supports but the quantitative embodiment of beings within those supports. This remains a determination inside the conditioned world-field, not a genetic Principle.
