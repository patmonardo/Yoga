# VAK_3.16 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> saṃprajānan viśaty ekaḥ tiṣṭhaty apy aparaḥ aparaḥ /
> niṣkrāmaty api sarvāṇi mūḍho 'nyaḥ nityam aṇḍajaḥ //

**Best translation:**  
One enters with clear awareness; another also remains with awareness; a further one also exits with awareness. Another is confused in all these stages, and the egg-born is always confused.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_3.16.md`](./VAK_3.16.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Primary working source: Pradhan-based e-text, with the standing caution that this is not a critical edition.

The natural unit begins with the question carried over from the explanation of `viparyasta-mati` in VAK 3.15:

```iast
kim avaśyaṃ sarvo 'ntarābhavas tathā mātuḥ kukṣim avakrāmati /
na ity āha /
kiṃ tarhi /
catasro garbhāvakrāntayaḥ sūtra uktāḥ /
katamāś catasraḥ /
```

Then the Kārikā is unfolded phrase by phrase:

```iast
saṃprajānan viśaty ekaḥ
```

```iast
tiṣṭhaty apy aparaḥ /
saṃprajānann iti varttate /
praviśaty apīty apikṣabdāt /
```

```iast
aparaḥ /
niṣkrāmaty api /
saṃprajānan praviśati tiṣṭhaty api /
```

```iast
sarvāṇi mūḍho 'nyaḥ
```

```iast
kaścit punaḥ sarvāṇy evāsaṃprajānan karoti /
praviśaty asaṃprajānan tiṣṭhati niṣkrāmaty api /
```

Vasubandhu then notes that the four womb-descents are stated in reverse order for metrical convenience:

```iast
etāś catasro garbhāvakrāntayaḥ pratilomaṃ nirdiṣṭāḥ /
ślokabandhānuguṇyataḥ /
```

The final pāda is explained:

```iast
nityam aṇḍajaḥ
```

```iast
aṇḍajaḥ sattvo nityaṃ mūḍha eva sarvāṇi karoti /
```

An objection follows:

```iast
katham aṇḍāj jāto garbhaṃ praviśati /
```

The answer is that one who will be egg-born may already be designated by the future designation:

```iast
yo 'pi janiṣyate so 'py aṇḍajaḥ /
athavā bhāvinyāpi saṃjñayā nirdeśāḥ kriyante /
```

Vasubandhu supports this with ordinary and scriptural examples in which a thing is named by the state it is becoming.

He then asks how unclear and clear womb-entry are to be understood:

```iast
kathaṃ punar asaṃprajānan mātuḥ kukṣiṃ praviśati yāvan niṣkrāmati /
kathaṃ vā saṃprajānan /
```

For a being of little status or power:

```iast
alpeśākhyasya tāvat sattvasya mātuḥ kukṣiṃ praviśata evaṃ viparītau saṃjñādhimokṣau pravarttete /
```

It may apprehend the situation as wind, rain, cold, darkness, tumult, or as entering grass, forest, a hut, the root of a tree, or similar shelter. While remaining there it thinks it remains in such a place; while emerging it thinks it is leaving such a place.

For a being of great status or power:

```iast
maheśākhyasya tu sattvasyārāmaṃ vā praviśāmy udyānaṃ vā prāsādaṃ vā 'bhirohāmi kūṭāgāraṃ vā paryaṅkaṃ veti /
```

It may apprehend itself as entering a park or garden, ascending a palace or pavilion, or approaching a couch; the corresponding representations continue while remaining and exiting.

The prose then closes the deluded case:

```iast
evaṃ tāvad asaṃprajānan praviśati yāvan niṣkrāmati /
```

And defines the clear case negatively:

```iast
nāsya viparītau saṃjñādhimokṣau pravarttete /
```

For the clearly aware being, inverted `saṃjñā` and `adhimokṣa` do not arise.

The next line, `atra punar apadiśyate`, introduces the special three womb-descents of VAK 3.17 and therefore marks the natural stopping point for this unit.

## Segmentation by Natural Argument

### 1. The general rule is denied

**Voice: Objector**

Must every `antarābhava` enter the mother's womb in the manner just described under `viparyasta-mati`?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

No. Scripture teaches four `garbhāvakrāntis`, four modes of descent into the womb.

The first important move is therefore a restriction:

```text
viparyasta-mati
    is not universal across every womb-entry
```

### 2. Four cumulative awareness profiles

The Bhāṣya makes the syntax explicitly cumulative.

```text
Type 1
    enters with clear awareness

Type 2
    enters and remains with clear awareness

Type 3
    enters, remains, and exits with clear awareness

Type 4
    enters, remains, and exits without clear awareness
```

The repeated `api` is not ornamental. Vasubandhu uses it to carry `saṃprajānan` forward through the successive stages.

### 3. The order of statement is metrical, not doctrinal

The four are said to be presented `pratiloma`, in reverse order, simply because that arrangement suits the verse (`ślokabandhānuguṇyataḥ`).

This is a useful exegetical caution: surface order in the Kārikā need not itself encode hierarchy.

### 4. Why the egg-born can be called egg-born before egg-birth

**Voice: Objector**

How can one who is born from an egg be said to enter a womb?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The designation may be made according to a future state (`bhāvinyā saṃjñayā`). One who is going to be egg-born can already be called `aṇḍaja`.

The answer is semantic rather than biological. The text is explaining the temporal scope of designation.

### 5. Deluded womb-entry as inverted representation and resolve

The key prose formulation is:

```iast
viparītau saṃjñādhimokṣau pravarttete
```

Two things arise inverted:

```text
saṃjñā
    recognitional representation

adhimokṣa
    determination / resolve / taking-to-be
```

The being does not merely lack information. It positively takes the process under an erroneous appearance.

For a being of lesser status, the appearance may be:

```text
storm
cold
bad weather
crowd-noise
thicket
forest
hut
root of a tree
```

For a being of greater status:

```text
park
garden
palace
pavilion
couch
```

The content varies with the being, but the formal structure is the same:

```text
actual womb-entry
    ↓
represented under another appearance
    ↓
that appearance is taken as the situation
```

### 6. Clear awareness is absence of inversion

The clearly aware case is defined economically:

```iast
nāsya viparītau saṃjñādhimokṣau pravarttete
```

The prose does not define `saṃprajānan` through some additional positive faculty here. It defines it by the non-arising of inverted representation and determination.

That is an important textual restraint.

## Literal Prose Translation

Must every intermediate being necessarily enter the mother's womb in that manner? No, he says. What then? Four descents into the womb are taught in the sūtra. What four?

One enters knowing clearly. Another also remains [knowing clearly]; the expression “knowing clearly” continues, and from the word “also” it is understood that this one also enters [knowingly]. Another also exits [knowingly]; he enters knowing clearly and also remains [knowingly]. Another performs all of these without clear awareness: he enters without clear awareness, remains, and also exits [without clear awareness]. These four womb-descents are stated in reverse order to conform to the construction of the verse.

“The egg-born is always [deluded].” An egg-born being performs all of these always in a deluded state. How does one born from an egg enter a womb? One who will be born [from an egg] is also called egg-born; or designations are made according to a future state as well. Therefore there is no fault.

How, then, does one enter the mother's womb without clear awareness and continue so until emerging, and how does one do so with clear awareness? For a being of little status, while entering the mother's womb, inverted perception and determination arise in this way: “The wind is blowing; the sky is raining; it is cold and gloomy; there is the uproar of a great crowd. Ah, I am entering a dense thicket, or a dense forest, or a grass hut, or a leaf hut, or I am creeping beneath the root of a tree,” and so on. While remaining, it thinks, “I remain in these”; while emerging, “I am coming out from these.”

For a being of great status, [the apprehension is], “I am entering a park or garden, or ascending a palace, pavilion, or couch.” In the same way it thinks that it remains there and comes out from there. Thus one enters without clear awareness and remains so until emerging.

For the clearly aware being, inverted perception and determination do not arise.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 3.15 had connected transition with `viparyasta-mati`. VAK 3.16 now prevents that description from being universalized.

The argument proceeds:

```text
1. Womb-entry has three phases:
       entry
       residence
       exit

2. Awareness can persist through progressively more of those phases.

3. Ordinary/deluded entry is not mere absence of awareness.

4. It contains positive inversion:
       viparīta-saṃjñā
       viparīta-adhimokṣa

5. Clear awareness is marked by the absence of those inversions.
```

This is more precise than the first-pass formula “clarity versus confusion.” The Bhāṣya says what the confusion consists in.

```text
mūḍhatā
    is instantiated as
        false recognitional presentation
        +
        false taking-to-be / determination
```

The being undergoes one process while representing it under another form.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the four `garbhāvakrāntis`, explains the cumulative force of the Kārikā, the reversed metrical order, future designation of `aṇḍaja`, and the phenomenology of clear/deluded entry.

**Voice: Objector** — asks whether every intermediate being enters the womb under inversion and later how an egg-born being can be said to enter a womb.

No school attribution is explicit in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **garbhāvakrānti** — descent/entry into the womb; the whole transition into gestation considered as a classified process
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā as a full compound here, though implied by its verbs
  - **function:** definition / classificatory framework

- **pratiloma** — in reverse order
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** exegetical clarification

- **ślokabandhānuguṇyatā** — conformity to the construction/metre of the verse
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** exegetical explanation

- **bhāvinī saṃjñā** — designation according to a future state
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** semantic resolution of objection

- **viparīta-saṃjñā** — inverted recognitional perception/representation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the earlier `viparyasta-mati`
  - **function:** definition of deluded womb-entry

- **viparīta-adhimokṣa** — inverted determination, resolve, or taking-to-be
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** definition of deluded womb-entry

- **alpeśākhya / maheśākhya** — being of lesser / greater status or power
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** differentiation of the representational content of delusion

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya changes the center of gravity of the verse.

The Kārikā appears to classify beings by degrees of awareness at entry, residence, and exit. The prose shows that the real doctrinal contrast is:

```text
saṃprajānan
    transition without inverted saṃjñā/adhimokṣa

mūḍha
    transition under inverted saṃjñā/adhimokṣa
```

So the issue is not “how much consciousness is present.” It is whether the transition is apprehended **as what it is**.

This produces a very strong continuity with 3.15:

```text
3.15
    viparyasta-mati directs the transition under false appearance

3.16
    viparīta-saṃjñā + viparīta-adhimokṣa specify the structure of that false appearance
```

The worldling's error is therefore not a mere missing datum. It is a structured substitution:

```text
real conditioned process
    ↓
misrepresented as another field of objects
    ↓
taken as that field
```

That is a much more exact form of “ignorance of the field.”

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The current translation remains basically sound, but `mūḍha` should be heard more strongly than generic “confused.”

Recommended translation:

> One enters with clear awareness; another also remains [with clear awareness]; a further one also exits [with clear awareness]. Another is deluded in all [three stages]; the egg-born is always [deluded].

The Bhāṣya supports **deluded** over merely **confused**, because it explicitly analyzes the state through inverted `saṃjñā` and `adhimokṣa`.

`saṃprajānan` should remain “with clear awareness” rather than be inflated into higher `prajñā` or liberating knowledge.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage gives a particularly clean distinction between **objective process** and **the form under which that process appears to the subject**.

```text
objective transition
    womb-entry

subjective presentation
    forest / hut / palace / garden / couch

subjective determination
    “this is where I am going / staying / leaving”
```

The error is therefore not generated by individualization as such. It is generated when the individualized standpoint takes its own appearance for the determining field.

A provisional Organon formula:

```text
Field-truth
    determines the process

Individual appearance
    may misrepresent the process

Ignorance
    = taking the appearance
      as the truth of the field
```

That is exactly the kind of “negative truth” we were discussing earlier: the individual standpoint becomes philosophically useful when its failure reveals the field it had ignored.

But the Bhāṣya itself remains more modest: clear awareness here means simply that the inverted `saṃjñā` and `adhimokṣa` do not arise.

## Working Summary

VAK 3.16 distinguishes four modes of womb-descent according to how long clear awareness persists through entry, residence, and exit. The Bhāṣya then specifies the ordinary deluded case: the being does not merely fail to know what is happening; it experiences inverted `saṃjñā` and `adhimokṣa`, taking womb-entry under the appearance of entering a forest, hut, park, palace, pavilion, and so forth. Clear awareness is defined by the absence of these inversions.

The decisive result is:

```text
mūḍhatā
    = structured misapprehension of transition

saṃprajānatā
    = transition without that inversion
```

The next unit, VAK 3.17, asks which exceptional beings instantiate the first three modes and grounds them through clarity of karma, knowledge, or both.
