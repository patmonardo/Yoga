# VAK_3.87 — Bhāṣya


**Working translation:** Twenty-four aṅgulis make one hasta; four hastas make one dhanuḥ. Five hundred of these make one krośa, and the region at about that distance is regarded as wilderness.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya continues the material measure-series of the preceding verse into ordinary linear measurement.

Twenty-four aṅgulis make one hasta. Four hastas make one dhanuḥ; Vasubandhu adds `vyāsena`, clarifying the measure as an extent or span. Five hundred such dhanuḥ make one krośa.

The final expression involving `araṇya` does not establish a new independent unit. Vasubandhu explains that the region beginning at approximately one krośa from a village is regarded as wilderness (`araṇya`).

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is straightforward explanatory prose without objection or competing school attribution.

## New Technical Terms

- **vyāsa** — span / extent in measurement
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** clarification of the dhanuḥ measure

- **grāmādi** — beginning from / outside a village
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** defines the conventional use of `araṇya`

## Doctrinal Determination

The measure-chain is:

```text
24 aṅgulis
    = 1 hasta

4 hastas
    = 1 dhanuḥ

500 dhanuḥ
    = 1 krośa
```

`araṇya` belongs to conventional spatial designation rather than to the arithmetic ladder itself:

```text
approximately one krośa from a village
    → regarded as wilderness
```

The Kośa therefore moves continuously from minute material measure into ordinary human spatial measure without introducing a discontinuity between atomic and macroscopic space.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required.**

The earlier Kārikā file treated `araṇya` provisionally as an associated distance measure. The Bhāṣya shows instead that it denotes the wilderness-region beginning at about a krośa from a village.

A better translation is:

> Twenty-four aṅgulis make one hasta; four hastas make one dhanuḥ. Five hundred of these make one krośa; a region at about that distance from a village is regarded as wilderness.

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a continuous scale conversion:

```text
bodily unit
    → constructed linear unit
        → traversable distance
            → conventional territorial designation
```

The final step is not another numerical multiplication but the application of an established measure to a worldly distinction.

## Organon Contact Point

The useful contact point is restrained: quantitative measure becomes sufficient to support a qualitative spatial designation. Once the krośa is established, `araṇya` can be determined conventionally relative to the village.

This remains entirely within produced Loka: a measured world-field and its conventional articulation.
