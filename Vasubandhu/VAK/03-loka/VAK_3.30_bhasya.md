# VAK_3.30_bhasya — Abhidharmakośabhāṣya on VAkK 3.30

### Source note

The source passage surrounding VAK 3.30 is substantially larger than the verse itself. The Bhāṣya first defines `nāma`, then enters a real doctrinal controversy concerning the status of `sparśa`, and only after that returns to the Kārikā's division of the six contacts. The argument is therefore preserved as a natural unit rather than reduced to the verse's four pādas.

---

## Natural Argument

### 1. `Nāma` is the non-material aggregates

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The question is direct:

```iast
atha nāmarūpamiti ko 'rthaḥ
```

> What, then, is meant by name-and-form?

`Rūpa` has already been treated extensively. The present task is to determine `nāma`.

The Kārikā answers:

```iast
nāma tv arūpiṇaḥ skandhāḥ
```

> Name, however, is the non-material aggregates.

Thus `nāma` is not simply a linguistic label. In this doctrinal context it designates the non-material side of the five-aggregate complex.

The four non-material aggregates are therefore implicated:

```text
vedanā
saṃjñā
saṃskāra
vijñāna
```

The first-pass caution was correct: `nāma` here cannot be collapsed into language.

### 2. Why call the non-material aggregates `nāma`?

Vasubandhu gives an etymological-functional account centered on `nam`, bending or inclining.

```iast
nāmendriyārthavaśenārtheṣu namatīti nāma
```

The interpretation is difficult but the governing thought is clear: `nāma` is so called because it **inclines toward / bends toward meanings or objects under determinate conditions**.

He then invokes ordinary names that indicate meanings:

```text
gauḥ   cow
aśvaḥ  horse
rūpa   form
rasa   taste
```

The name functions as `pratyāyaka`, something that makes a meaning known or indicates it.

A second explanation is attributed to **others**:

**Voice: other teachers**

The non-material aggregates are called `nāma` because, when this body has been laid aside, they incline toward another rebirth (`upapattyantara`).

This alternative should not be conflated with Vasubandhu's first explanation.

### 3. Sparśa is sixfold

After noting that the six spheres have been explained, Vasubandhu turns to contact:

```iast
sparśāḥ ṣaṭ
```

The six are:

```text
cakṣuḥ-saṃsparśa
śrotra-saṃsparśa
ghrāṇa-saṃsparśa
jihvā-saṃsparśa
kāya-saṃsparśa
manaḥ-saṃsparśa
```

The division corresponds to the sixfold sensory-mental architecture already established.

### 4. Contact is said to arise from conjunction

The Bhāṣya makes the triad explicit again:

```iast
indriya + artha + vijñāna
```

and says:

```iast
trayāṇāṃ saṃnipātājjātā
```

> [The contacts are] born from the conjunction of the three.

For the five sensory contacts this is comparatively straightforward because the factors are co-arisen (`sahaja`).

The mental case is harder.

The `manas` serving as faculty may already be ceased, while its dharma-object and mental knowledge may be future or present. How can these be said to "come together"?

Vasubandhu offers two ways of understanding `saṃnipāta`:

```text
1. kārya-kāraṇa-bhāva
   causal relation

2. eka-kāryārtha
   orientation toward one common effect
```

The essential point is functional rather than spatial:

```text
The three need not literally collide.
They form a conjunction insofar as they stand in the relevant
causal relation and are jointly competent for the arising of contact.
```

The term `praguṇa` is important here: all three are suitably disposed / competent for the production of `sparśa`.

### 5. A real dispute: is sparśa identical with conjunction?

**Voice: competing Abhidharma teachers**

Vasubandhu explicitly says:

```iast
atra punarācāryāṇāṃ bhedaṃ gatā buddhayaḥ
```

> Here the understandings of the teachers have diverged.

Two positions follow.

#### Position A — sparśa is the conjunction itself

Some explain:

```text
sparśa = saṃnipāta
```

They cite a sūtra equating the meeting, conjunction, or combination of the three dharmas with contact.

On this reading:

```text
indriya + artha + vijñāna
        in relevant conjunction
                = sparśa
```

#### Position B — sparśa is a distinct dharma associated with mind

Others explain contact as:

```iast
citta-saṃprayuktaṃ dharmāntaram
```

> another dharma associated with mind.

They cite the six-by-six teaching, which separately enumerates:

```text
6 internal spheres
6 external spheres
6 knowledge-bodies
6 contact-bodies
6 feeling-bodies
6 thirst-bodies
```

The separate enumeration of contact after faculty, object, and knowledge is used as evidence that contact is distinct from their conjunction.

### 6. The debate turns on the relation between enumeration and ontological distinction

The defenders of identity reply:

```iast
na vai pṛthignirdeśāt pṛthagbhāvo bhavati
```

> Separate mention does not necessarily entail separate existence.

That is a significant logical principle.

The opposing side answers that in this case, once faculty, object, and knowledge have already been specified, an additional mention of contact would become pointless unless contact contributed something distinct.

A further compromise-style explanation is reported: the specific members among faculty, object, and knowledge that stand in the relevant **cause-effect relation** are what are established as contact.

Thus the debate is not merely lexical. It concerns the ontology of relation:

```text
Is contact identical with the structured relation?

or

Is contact a further dharma produced through the relation?
```

Vasubandhu finally cuts the excursus short:

```iast
ativahuvistaraprasāriṇī tveṣā kathetyalaṃ prasaṅgena
```

The discussion is capable of spreading into excessive detail; enough of the digression.

This restraint is itself useful: the Bhāṣya records the dispute without forcing a premature universal resolution here.

### 7. Five contacts are `pratigha-saṃsparśa`

The five sensory contacts are called contacts involving `pratigha` because their supporting faculties are `sapratigha`, resistant / materially obstructive.

```text
eye
 ear
 nose
 tongue
 body
    ↓
materially resistant faculties
    ↓
pratigha-saṃsparśa
```

The classification is therefore **āśraya-prabhāvita** — determined by the character of the support.

### 8. The sixth is `adhivacana-saṃsparśa`

The sixth, mental contact, is called designation-contact.

The first explanation rests on its object-support.

Vasubandhu asks what `adhivacana` is and answers:

```iast
adhivacanamucyate nāma
```

> `adhivacana` is called name / designation.

Name is said to be an additional object-support of mental knowledge.

The quoted contrast is extremely important:

```text
Eye-knowledge knows blue.

Mental knowledge knows blue
and knows: "blue."
```

That is:

```text
cakṣurvijñāna
    vijānāti nīlam

manovijñāna
    vijānāti nīlam
    +
    vijānāti "nīlam" iti
```

The mental mode can take not only the presented blue but also its **designation**.

This yields the Bhāṣya's compact contrast:

```text
five sensory contacts
    āśraya-prabhāvita
    determined by support

mental designation-contact
    ālambana-prabhāvita
    determined by object-support
```

### 9. An alternative explanation of `adhivacana`

**Voice: other teachers**

Others say that mental knowledge proceeds toward meanings **with reference to words**, whereas the five sensory knowledges do not.

On that explanation, designation-contact is so called because it is associated with (`saṃprayukta`) that word-mediated mental activity.

This produces a second contrast:

```text
first five
    āśraya-prabhāvita

sixth
    saṃprayoga-prabhāvita
```

Thus the Bhāṣya preserves two explanations for what determines the sixth contact:

```text
object-support
or
association with word-mediated mental cognition
```

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

What is meant by `nāmarūpa`? Form has already been explained. `Nāma` consists of the non-material aggregates. It is called "name" because it inclines toward meanings or objects under the determining force of names, which in ordinary usage indicate such meanings as cow, horse, form, taste, and so forth. Others explain that the non-material aggregates are called `nāma` because, when this body is laid aside, they incline toward another rebirth.

Contact is sixfold, from eye-contact through mental contact, and is born from the conjunction of faculty, object, and knowledge. For the first five this conjunction is straightforward because the relevant factors arise together. In the mental case, conjunction should not be understood as literal simultaneity alone: it may consist in a cause-effect relation or in the three factors' common orientation toward one effect, all three being suitably disposed for the arising of contact.

Teachers disagree over whether contact is identical with this conjunction or is a distinct mind-associated dharma arising through it. Scripture is cited on both sides. Vasubandhu records the dispute but declines to let the excursus expand indefinitely.

Of the six contacts, the five sensory contacts are called `pratigha-saṃsparśa` because they depend on materially resistant faculties. The sixth, mental contact, is called `adhivacana-saṃsparśa`, designation-contact. Mental knowledge can apprehend not only blue, for example, but also "blue" as designated; hence this contact is determined either by its additional object-support, namely designation, or, according to another explanation, by its association with word-mediated mental activity.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames and explains `nāma`, sixfold contact, the causal interpretation of conjunction, the five resistant contacts, and the sixth designation-contact.

**Voice: other teachers** — alternative explanation of why the non-material aggregates are called `nāma`, namely their inclination toward another rebirth.

**Voice: one Abhidharma position** — `sparśa` is the conjunction itself.

**Voice: another Abhidharma position** — `sparśa` is a distinct mind-associated dharma arising from the conjunction.

**Voice: other teachers** — designation-contact is explained through the word-mediated activity of mental knowledge rather than primarily through its object-support.

Where the Bhāṣya only reports competing interpretations, no stronger attribution is forced.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratyāyaka** — that which makes a meaning known or indicates it.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory vocabulary here.
  - **function:** explains ordinary naming and the proposed derivation of `nāma`.

- **nam / naman** — bending, inclining, tending toward.
  - **status:** etymological vocabulary in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** supports the derivation of `nāma`.

- **upapattyantara** — another rebirth / another arising-existence.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this explanation.
  - **function:** alternative explanation of `nāma` through continuity toward another birth.

- **saṃnipāta** — conjunction, convergence.
  - **status:** previously encountered; now technically analyzed.
  - **function:** condition from which contact arises, or according to one school the contact itself.

- **kārya-kāraṇa-bhāva** — cause-effect relation.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya logical vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains how conjunction can obtain in the mental case without simple simultaneity.

- **eka-kāryārtha** — oriented toward one common effect.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya logical vocabulary.
  - **function:** alternative account of `saṃnipāta`.

- **praguṇa** — suitably disposed, competent, ready.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific functional vocabulary.
  - **function:** marks the joint capacity of faculty, object, and knowledge to produce contact.

- **pṛthag-nirdeśa** — separate mention / separate enumeration.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative vocabulary.
  - **function:** raises the question whether separate enumeration entails ontological distinctness.

- **pṛthag-bhāva** — separate existence / distinct status.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative vocabulary.
  - **function:** ontological consequence denied by one side of the sparśa debate.

- **pratigha-saṃsparśa** — resistant contact / contact conditioned by materially resistant faculties.
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly explained in Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** classifies the five sensory contacts by their support.

- **adhivacana** — designation, name-expression.
  - **status:** Kārikā term substantially clarified in Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** characterizes the additional semantic object available to mental knowledge.

- **āśraya-prabhāvita** — determined by the support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya classificatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains the naming of the five resistant contacts.

- **ālambana-prabhāvita** — determined by the object-support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya classificatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** one explanation for the naming of mental designation-contact.

- **saṃprayoga-prabhāvita** — determined by association/conjunction.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya classificatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** alternative explanation for designation-contact.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.30 gives a detailed analysis of two members already encountered in the twelvefold series:

```text
nāmarūpa
    ↓
nāma = four non-material aggregates

sparśa
    ↓
sixfold contact
```

But the Bhāṣya substantially deepens both.

### Nāma is functional, not merely lexical

```text
nāma
    ≠ merely a word

nāma
    = non-material aggregate structure
```

The use of linguistic examples does not reduce `nāma` to language. Rather, it attempts to explain why the non-material aggregates receive this designation.

### Sparśa is relationally generated

The stable doctrinal minimum across the reported dispute is:

```text
indriya
+ artha
+ vijñāna
    ↓
relevant conjunction
    ↓
sparśa
```

Whether `sparśa` is identical with that conjunction or a further dharma arising from it remains disputed in the passage.

### The six contacts divide by mode of determination

```text
five sensory contacts
    determined through materially resistant support

mental contact
    determined through designation
```

The sixth is therefore not merely "another sense contact." It has a specifically semantic or designative dimension.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation is substantially correct, but the Bhāṣya requires three refinements.

First, `saṃnipāta` should not be pictured as literal physical meeting. In the mental case Vasubandhu explicitly allows:

```text
causal relation
or
common orientation toward one effect
```

Second, `pratigha` is explained through the **supporting faculties** being `sapratigha`; hence "resistant contact" means contact classified by materially resistant support.

Third, `adhivacana` is much richer than simple verbal naming. Mental knowledge can know both:

```text
blue
and
"blue"
```

A Bhāṣya-informed rendering remains:

> Name is the non-material aggregates. The six contacts arise from conjunction. Five are contacts based on resistant faculties; the sixth is called designation-contact.

## Logical Determination

This passage contains several unusually explicit logical distinctions.

### 1. Relation need not mean simultaneity

The mental-contact problem forces a distinction:

```text
conjunction
    ≠ mere co-location or co-temporality
```

It may instead be constituted by:

```text
cause-effect relation
or
common functional orientation
```

That is a genuinely relational conception of conjunction.

### 2. Separate mention does not automatically entail separate being

One side states:

```text
pṛthag-nirdeśa
    does not by itself entail
pṛthag-bhāva
```

This is an important rule of interpretation. A taxonomy cannot automatically be converted into an ontology.

### 3. Yet explanatory redundancy becomes evidence

The opposing side replies that if all constitutive factors have already been exhaustively enumerated, a further independent doctrinal mention demands explanation.

Thus the debate is structurally:

```text
semantic distinction
    ?
ontological distinction
```

The Bhāṣya does not permit us to collapse those two levels.

### 4. Classification can be determined by different relational axes

The first five and sixth contacts are classified according to different determinants:

```text
support
object-support
association
```

This anticipates a more general Abhidharma method: a dharma can be determined through its position in a network of relations rather than through a single isolated intrinsic predicate.

## Organon Light

This passage is especially fertile for an Organon reading, but the contact must remain visibly downstream from Vasubandhu's analysis.

### 1. Contact is a relation becoming effective

The sequence can be rendered abstractly as:

```text
faculty-capacity
    +
object-domain
    +
knowledge-function
        ↓
functional conjunction
        ↓
contact
```

The important point is not the vocabulary of "interface." It is that a relation becomes **effective** when its terms stand in the relevant causal-functional configuration.

That is more precise than saying merely that three things are present.

### 2. Mental cognition has a second-order semantic reach

The blue example is remarkable:

```text
sense knowledge:
    blue

mental knowledge:
    blue
    +
    "blue"
```

An Organon will immediately notice that the second mode can take the first-order content **and its designation** as object-support.

This is the beginning of a distinction between:

```text
presentation
and
presentation-as-determined
```

We should not yet call this reflection in the Hegelian technical sense. But it is plainly a second-order semantic capacity.

### 3. A View may be determined by support, object-support, or association

The Bhāṣya's own classificatory vocabulary gives us something potentially more valuable than an imposed modern schema:

```text
āśraya-prabhāvita
ālambana-prabhāvita
saṃprayoga-prabhāvita
```

An eventual Agential View could learn from this directly:

```text
A cognitive event is not fully specified merely by content.
It may also require:
    what supports it,
    what it takes as object-support,
    and what it is associated with.
```

That is already very close to the emerging Organon architecture, but here it comes from the Kośa's own relational analysis.

### 4. Do not infer ontology directly from schema

The `pṛthag-nirdeśa / pṛthag-bhāva` dispute deserves to survive into the future Organon Bhāṣya almost unchanged:

```text
Separate field in a schema
    ≠ automatically
separate ontological entity
```

That is an exceptionally useful methodological restraint for any formal ontology.

The Kośa is effectively warning us not to reify the data model.

---

## Provisional Result

VAK 3.30 transforms what looked like a simple taxonomy into a theory of relational cognition.

```text
nāma
    = non-material aggregate organization

sparśa
    = effective relational conjunction

five sensory contacts
    = classified through resistant support

mental contact
    = distinguished by semantic/designative reach
```

The strongest forward-looking result is not that "mind is language." The text does not say that.

It is this:

```text
mental knowledge can take
both a presented meaning
and its designation
as object-support.
```

That is a much cleaner and more powerful result.
