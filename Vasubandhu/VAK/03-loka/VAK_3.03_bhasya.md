## Bhāṣya Translation

### Continuous Translation

The formless-domain is without a station. For formless dharmas there is no spatial station. It is a rule that past and future dharmas, avijñapti, and formless dharmas are not spatially situated.

Nevertheless, the formless-domain is fourfold according to rebirth: the sphere of infinite space, the sphere of infinite knowledge, the sphere of nothingness, and the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. These are not differentiated as higher and lower through spatial location. Wherever one who has attained the corresponding samāpatti dies, there that being is reborn; and when dying from that existence, the intermediate existence also arises there.

A question then arises. In beings possessing form, the continuity of citta proceeds in dependence upon form. On what does it proceed among beings of the formless-domain?

**Voice: Ābhidhārmika position** — It proceeds in dependence upon `nikāyasabhāga` and the `jīvitendriya`, the common class-character and the life-faculty.

**Voice: Objector** — Then why does the citta-continuity of beings possessing form not proceed in dependence upon just these same two?

**Voice: Ābhidhārmika position** — Because they are weak there.

**Voice: Objector** — What makes them strong in the formless-domain?

**Voice: Ābhidhārmika position** — Their arising from a distinctive samāpatti. That samāpatti has eliminated the representation of form.

**Voice: Objector** — If they are strong for precisely that reason, then they should continue by that strength alone. What further support is required?

And another difficulty must be stated. Just as in beings possessing form the common class-character and the life-faculty proceed in dependence upon form, on what do those two proceed among formless beings?

**Voice: Ābhidhārmika position** — Those same two support one another.

**Voice: Objector** — Then why do those two not mutually support one another among beings possessing form?

**Voice: Ābhidhārmika position** — Because they are weak there.

**Voice: Objector** — What makes them strong in the formless-domain?

**Voice: Ābhidhārmika position** — Their arising from a distinctive samāpatti.

**Voice: Sautrāntika position** — But exactly the same explanation applies to the citta-continuity, or indeed to citta and the mental factors themselves. Therefore there is no mutually supporting basis of the sort proposed for the citta-continuity of formless beings. Rather, when the projecting cause of a citta-continuity has not abandoned craving for form, that continuity arises together with form and proceeds dependent upon form. When its cause has abandoned craving for form, it proceeds without depending upon form, because its cause has turned away from form.

## Course of the Argument

### 1. `asthāna` is literal doctrinal pressure

The Bhāṣya does not merely call the formless-domain “subtle.” It denies `sthāna` to formless dharmas:

```text
rūpadhātu
    differentiated through stations

ārūpyadhātu
    no station
```

The fourfold structure therefore cannot be a fourfold spatial geography.

### 2. Fourfoldness shifts from place to rebirth-mode

The four ārūpya attainments determine four corresponding modes of rebirth:

```text
ākāśānantyāyatana
vijñānānantyāyatana
ākiñcanyāyatana
naivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñāyatana
```

Their hierarchy is not `deśakṛta` — not produced by spatial placement. The Bhāṣya therefore sharply separates:

```text
logical / meditative differentiation
    ≠
spatial differentiation
```

### 3. The support problem becomes unavoidable

Once form is removed, the text has to explain continuity:

```text
If not rūpa,
what supports citta-santati?
```

The Abhidharma answer is:

```text
nikāyasabhāga
+
jīvitendriya
```

This is not merely descriptive. It is a proposed dependency structure.

### 4. The proposed support relation generates a regress-like difficulty

If `nikāyasabhāga` and `jīvitendriya` support the citta-continuity, what supports them when form has disappeared? The reply that they support one another invites the further question why this does not suffice in rūpadhātu as well.

The answer — greater strength because of a distinctive samāpatti — is then challenged because the same strength could seemingly be assigned directly to the citta-continuity itself.

### 5. Sautrāntika causal reformulation

The Sautrāntika reply replaces a static support architecture with a genetic account:

```text
projecting cause not free from craving for form
    → citta-continuity arises together with form
    → proceeds dependent upon form

projecting cause free from craving for form
    → citta-continuity arises without form
    → proceeds without depending upon form
```

The decisive explanatory term becomes not a hidden mutual support but the orientation of the causal series itself.

## Voices

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expository voice** — explains `asthāna` and the four rebirths and frames the support problem.
- **Voice: Ābhidhārmika position** — citta-continuity in ārūpya depends on `nikāyasabhāga` and `jīvitendriya`; these may mutually support one another.
- **Voice: Objector** — presses the symmetry and regress problems generated by that support theory.
- **Voice: Sautrāntika position** — rejects the mutual-support account and gives a causal explanation through the projecting cause and freedom or non-freedom from craving for form.

The passage explicitly names the final position as Sautrāntika. The intermediate dialectic is best treated as a critique of the Ābhidhārmika account without over-identifying every objection with Vasubandhu personally.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **adeśastha** — not spatially situated / without a location in space
  - **status:** introduced explicitly by the Bhāṣya in explaining `asthāna`
  - **function:** doctrinal clarification of the non-spatial status of formless dharmas

- **upapattibheda** — differentiation by rebirth
  - **status:** prose expansion of `upapattyā caturvidhaḥ`
  - **function:** replaces spatial differentiation as the principle of fourfoldness

- **deśakṛta-auttarādharya** — superiority and inferiority produced by spatial location
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** explicitly denied as the basis of hierarchy among the four ārūpya rebirths

- **nikāyasabhāga** — common class-character / commonality of a class of beings
  - **status:** previously encountered in Chapter 2; made causally operative here
  - **function:** proposed support of citta-continuity in the Ābhidhārmika account

- **jīvitendriya** — life-faculty
  - **status:** previously established in Chapter 2
  - **function:** second proposed support of citta-continuity

- **samāpattiviśeṣaja** — arisen from a distinctive samāpatti
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory compound in this passage
  - **function:** explains the alleged strength of the non-material supports

- **vibhūtarūpasaṃjñā** — having removed / cleared away the representation of form
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only characterization here
  - **function:** explains why the relevant samāpatti is capable of generating formless rebirth

- **ākṣepahetu** — projecting cause
  - **status:** decisive Sautrāntika explanatory term
  - **function:** genetic replacement for the static mutual-support theory

- **avītatṛṣṇa / vītatṛṣṇa rūpe** — not freed / freed from craving for form
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only causal distinction in this unit
  - **function:** determines whether the arising continuity requires form

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya confirms and deepens the Kārikā's central claim:

```text
ārūpyadhātu
    is not a spatial region above rūpadhātu.
```

It is fourfold because four kinds of rebirth correspond to four formless attainments. Their differentiation is therefore generated by meditative and causal conditions, not by separate spatial places.

The second half of the passage then exposes a doctrinal disagreement about how continuity without form is possible.

### Ābhidhārmika model

```text
citta-santati
    depends on
        nikāyasabhāga
        jīvitendriya

nikāyasabhāga ↔ jīvitendriya
    mutual support
```

### Sautrāntika model

```text
prior causal orientation
    determines
whether the continuity is generated with or without form
```

The Sautrāntika position therefore explains formless continuity genetically rather than by positing a special pair of mutually sustaining non-material supports.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and one vocabulary revision.**

The first-pass translation's “class-continuity and life-continuity” should be made more technical. The Bhāṣya explicitly supplies:

```text
nikāya
    → nikāyasabhāga

jīvita
    → jīvitendriya
```

So the preferred translation is now:

> The formless-domain has no station; it is fourfold according to rebirth. Here, the continuity of citta rests upon the common class-character and the life-faculty.

This records the Ābhidhārmika interpretation of the kārikā while the commentary must also preserve the subsequent Sautrāntika objection to that support theory.

## Logical Determination

VAK 3.03 introduces a major logical transformation of `loka`:

```text
place is negated
but differentiation remains.
```

Therefore:

```text
World
    ≠ merely spatial container.
```

The sequence now reads:

```text
kāmadhātu
    differentiation through inhabited locations and destinies

rūpadhātu
    differentiation through stations and dhyāna-grounds

ārūpyadhātu
    station negated
    differentiation retained through mode of rebirth
```

The Bhāṣya then asks what sustains continuity once the spatial-material support has been removed. That question forces a movement from simple classification to explanation of dependence.

The Sautrāntika answer is especially important:

```text
continuity is not explained by adding an invisible substrate;
it is explained from the causal determination that generates the continuity.
```

This is a genuine shift from support to genesis.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The first-pass analysis said that ārūpyadhātu is “World at the limit of spatiality.” The Bhāṣya confirms this, but makes the result sharper.

```text
Loka survives the negation of Location.
```

That is important. If `loka` were simply place, the negation of `sthāna` would terminate world. It does not. What remains is an ordered continuum generated according to determinate conditions of rebirth.

The stronger result is therefore:

```text
Loka
    is not fundamentally extension.

Loka
    is the determinate field in which a conditioned continuity occurs.
```

And the Sautrāntika intervention adds:

```text
Field-dependence
    is grounded genetically
    in the causal orientation that produces the continuum.
```

This is highly relevant to the emerging Loka → Karma movement, but we should not yet turn it into the final Organon Comment. For now the textual result is enough: once material station disappears, the Bhāṣya is compelled to ask for the ground of continuity, and one major answer is explicitly causal rather than substantial.
