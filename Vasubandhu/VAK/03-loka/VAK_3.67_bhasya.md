## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first identifies Vaijayanta precisely: it is Śakra's palace in the middle of Sudarśana. Its magnificence surpasses other divine residences through the arrangement and abundance of many jewels.

The prose then turns from the city's inner magnificence to its exterior. On the four sides outside the city are four pleasure-grounds of the gods: Caitraratha, Pāruṣyaka, Miśraka, and Nandana.

```text
Sudarśana
    → central palace: Vaijayanta
        → 250 yojanas on each side

outside the city
    → four sides
        → four divine playgrounds
```

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — identification and measurement of Vaijayanta and explanation of the four gardens.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

Here is Vaijayanta, having sides of two hundred and fifty yojanas.

Vaijayanta is the palace of Śakra, Indra of the gods, in the middle of the city. Through the excellence of the arrangement of many kinds of jewels, it puts to shame the splendor and greatness of all other residences. In length, each side is two hundred and fifty yojanas.

So much for the city's beauty. Outside, however, it is adorned by Caitraratha, Pāruṣyaka, Miśraka, and Nandana.

Outside that city, on its four sides, are four gardens and related places, playgrounds of the gods: the Caitraratha garden, the Pāruṣyaka, the Miśraka grove, and the Nandana grove. By these the city is adorned externally.

### Philosophical Translation

The divine city is internally centered by Śakra's palace, Vaijayanta, and externally surrounded by four spaces of divine recreation. The Bhāṣya thus converts the terse Kārikā into a determinate civic order: ruler, palace, center, boundary, exterior, and pleasure-ground.

## New Technical Terms

- **prāsāda** — palace
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** identifies Vaijayanta as Śakra's palace

- **krīḍābhūmi** — playground / recreation-ground
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** defines the function of the four gardens

- **udyāna** — garden
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** classifies Caitraratha and related exterior spaces

- **vana** — grove
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** identifies Miśraka and Nandana as wooded pleasure-spaces

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes the Trāyastriṃśa summit into an explicitly organized divine polity. Sudarśana is not merely a beautiful city: its center is occupied by the palace of Śakra, and its four sides open into divine recreation grounds.

The relevant sequence is:

```text
city
    → political center
        → Śakra's palace
            → exterior pleasure grounds
```

This remains a determinate kāmadhātu world of refined enjoyment.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and correction.**

The Bhāṣya confirms that `sārdhadviśata` means **250 yojanas on each side** and identifies Vaijayanta specifically as **Śakra's palace**. It also clarifies that Caitraratha, Pāruṣyaka, Miśraka, and Nandana are four exterior divine recreation grounds distributed on the four sides of the city.

## Logical Determination

```text
central city
    → ruler's palace
        → exterior recreation field
```

The world-description proceeds from location to institutional organization and then to the differentiated uses of space.

## Organon Contact Point

The passage remains within produced Loka. Its significance is simply that a world becomes increasingly concrete through relations of center, ruler, residence, boundary, and use. No transcendental principle needs to be imported into the cosmography.
