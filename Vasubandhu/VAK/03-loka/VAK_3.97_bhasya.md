# VAK_3.97 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor


**Working translation:** The Muni's marks are superior through their proper placement, vividness, and completeness. Formerly beings were like rūpa-realm beings; then, through attachment to savor, they gradually declined.

### Source Caution

The transmitted prose line before the second half reads `prāgāsannupivat sattvāḥ`, while the Kārikā file has `prāg āsan rūpivat sattvāḥ`. The Bhāṣya immediately glosses the intended sense as `prathamakalpikā manuṣyā rūpāvacarā ivāsan`, so the doctrinal meaning is secure: first-kalpa humans were like beings of the rūpa realm. The source form should not be silently normalized.

## Natural Argument

The passage first asks whether the only distinction between cakravartins and ordinary kings is their wheel-sovereignty. Vasubandhu answers that there is another distinction: cakravartins possess the thirty-two marks of a great person, as Buddhas do. Yet the Buddha's marks are superior. The Bhāṣya explains the three terms comparatively: the Buddha's marks are more exactly situated (`deśasthatarāṇi`), more intensely manifest (`uttaptatarāṇi`), and more complete (`saṃpūrṇatarāṇi`).

The text then asks whether the first humans of the kalpa already had kings. They did not. Their original condition was instead analogous to rūpāvacara beings. A cited sūtra describes them as formed, mind-made, complete in limbs and faculties, beautiful, self-luminous, moving through the sky, feeding on joy, and long-lived.

The decline begins when `bhūmirasa`, an earth-savor sweet like honey, appears. One greed-prone being smells, tastes, and eats it; others imitate him. Vasubandhu explicitly identifies this as the beginning of `kavaḍīkārāhāra`, gross morsel-food.

Repeated consumption changes the body: hardness and heaviness enter it, while self-luminosity disappears. Darkness therefore appears, and sun and moon become manifest. Attachment then successively consumes the available food-forms: earth-savor disappears, followed by earth-film, forest-creeper, and finally naturally arising rice.

With the increasing grossness of food, bodily excretory channels and male/female sexual organs arise; bodily form differentiates. Seeing one another, beings fall under unwise attention conditioned by previous habit, sexual passion intensifies, and misconduct begins.

The Bhāṣya therefore gives `rasarāga` as the initiating affective condition in a long produced sequence of bodily, environmental, and social differentiation.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — question-and-answer framing, gloss of the Buddha's marks, and reconstruction of primordial human decline.
- **Voice: scriptural citation** — the description of first-kalpa humans as mind-made, self-luminous, joy-fed, and long-lived.

## New Technical Terms

- **mahāpuruṣalakṣaṇa** — mark of a great person
  - **status:** previously present in Buddhist doctrinal background; newly important in this local comparison
  - **function:** distinguishes Buddha/cakravartin bodies from ordinary royal bodies

- **deśastha** — properly situated
  - **status:** local Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** one axis of superiority of the Buddha's marks

- **uttapta** — intensely manifest / vivid / prominent
  - **status:** difficult term clarified by comparative `uttaptatara`
  - **function:** second axis of superiority of the Buddha's marks

- **saṃpūrṇa** — complete / fully developed
  - **status:** local Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** third axis of superiority of the Buddha's marks

- **prāthamakalpika** — belonging to the first age of the kalpa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific historical designation
  - **function:** identifies primordial humans

- **manomaya** — mind-made
  - **status:** introduced through scriptural citation
  - **function:** characterizes the subtle body of first-kalpa humans

- **bhūmirasa** — earth-savor
  - **status:** newly introduced in this genealogy
  - **function:** first material food-object whose appropriation begins decline

- **kavaḍīkārāhāra** — morsel-food / gross edible nourishment
  - **status:** newly foregrounded here
  - **function:** marks the transition from joy-feeding to gross material alimentation

- **āsvādagṛddha** — greedy for savor
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expression in this passage
  - **function:** names the attachment that drives progressive exhaustion of food-forms

- **ayoniśomanaskāra** — unwise attention
  - **status:** major technical term, here appearing in the genealogy of sexual passion
  - **function:** mediates perception of differentiated bodies into the arising of kāmarāga

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes the descent a produced sequence rather than an unexplained fall:

```text
rūpāvacara-like primordial humans
    → mind-made, self-luminous, joy-fed
    → bhūmirasa appears
    → rasa-rāga / greed for savor
    → morsel-food begins
    → bodily hardness and heaviness
    → luminosity disappears
    → sun and moon become manifest
    → progressively grosser food
    → excretion + sexual differentiation
    → ayoniśomanaskāra
    → kāmarāga
    → sexual misconduct
```

The cosmology remains fully conditioned. No stage here is a genetic Principle. `Rasarāga` is an affective condition operating within produced Loka, and the succeeding bodily and environmental determinations arise as dependent world-process.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required in nuance.**

The first-pass rendering of `uttapta` as merely "clear" is too weak. The prose gloss `uttaptatarāṇi` indicates greater intensity or prominence of the marks. A better translation is:

> The Muni's marks are superior through being more properly situated, more vividly manifest, and more complete. Formerly beings were like rūpa-realm beings; then, through attachment to savor, they gradually declined.

The Bhāṣya also makes `rūpivat` precise: not merely "form-like," but **like rūpāvacara beings**.

## Logical Determination

The genealogy is an ordered dependency chain:

```text
new object of enjoyment
    → appropriation through rāga
    → altered mode of nourishment
    → altered body
    → altered environment
    → differentiated embodiment
    → new affective and social relations
```

The important logical point is that the later determinations are not simply juxtaposed. Each becomes intelligible through the preceding change in the conditions of embodied life.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
A produced world differentiates through the consequences of appropriation.
```

The useful contact is not to turn `rasa` into a transcendental principle, but to notice the Bhāṣya's strongly genetic exposition inside the conditioned field: desire changes nourishment; nourishment changes embodiment; embodiment changes the experienced world and social relation.
