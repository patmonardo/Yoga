# VAK_3.83 — Bhāṣya


**Working translation:** In Pratāpana the lifespan is half an intermediate kalpa; in Avīci, one intermediate kalpa. For animals, again, the maximum is a kalpa. For pretas, a human month is a day-and-night, and their lifespan is five hundred years by that measure.

### Source caution

The Kārikā compresses several distinct lifespan rules into one verse. The Bhāṣya is necessary to determine the referent of `ardham`, the force of `kalpam` for animals, and the exact preta time-conversion.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu continues the lifespan taxonomy beyond the six hot hells treated in the preceding verse.

First, the remaining two principal hot hells are measured directly in antarakalpas:

```text
Pratāpana
    → 1/2 antarakalpa

Avīci
    → 1 antarakalpa
```

The phrase `ardhaṃ pratāpane` therefore does not mean half of the preceding Tāpana year-count. The Bhāṣya explicitly supplies `antarakalpasya`: half of an intermediate kalpa.

The animal realm differs. Vasubandhu states first:

```text
tiraścāṃ tu niyamo nāsti
    → for animals there is no fixed rule
```

He then explains the Kārikā's `kalpaṃ tiraścām` as a statement of maximum lifespan, not a universal lifespan:

```text
paramāyus tiraścām antarakalpam
    → the maximum lifespan of animals is one antarakalpa
```

This extreme duration belongs to exceptional great nāgas such as Nanda and Upananda. A scriptural citation concerning eight great nāgas who remain for a kalpa supports the claim.

Finally, preta duration is given by a realm-specific temporal conversion:

```text
1 human month
    = 1 preta day-and-night

500 preta years
    = preta lifespan
```

The verse thus distinguishes fixed hell-duration, variable animal lifespan with an extreme upper limit, and a converted preta calendar.

## Translation

In the Pratāpana great hell, the lifespan is half an intermediate kalpa; in Avīci, one intermediate kalpa. Animals have no fixed lifespan, but their maximum lifespan is one intermediate kalpa, as in the case of great nāgas such as Nanda and Upananda. For pretas, one human month constitutes a day-and-night, and by that measure their lifespan is five hundred years.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is straightforward exegetical determination of the compressed lifespan taxonomy, with a scriptural citation supporting the exceptional animal maximum.

## New Technical Terms

- **paramāyus** — maximum lifespan
  - **status:** important clarification introduced by the Bhāṣya for the animal case
  - **function:** prevents `kalpaṃ tiraścām` from being read as the universal lifespan of all animals

- **niyama** — fixed rule, determinate uniformity
  - **status:** ordinary technical term, decisive here
  - **function:** marks the absence of one fixed lifespan for animals

- **kalpastha** — remaining for a kalpa
  - **status:** introduced in the scriptural citation concerning great nāgas
  - **function:** supports the exceptional antarakalpa maximum among animals

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya differentiates three kinds of lifespan determination:

```text
fixed duration
    → Pratāpana: 1/2 antarakalpa
    → Avīci: 1 antarakalpa

variable duration with maximum
    → animals: no niyama
    → paramāyus = 1 antarakalpa

realm-relative calendar
    → pretas
    → 1 human month = 1 preta day-night
    → lifespan = 500 preta years
```

The animal case is especially important because it blocks a simple one-realm/one-lifespan model. Loka remains determinate, but the determination can be a range with a maximum rather than a single fixed quantity.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required.**

The first-pass Kārikā analysis left `ardham` and the animal `kalpam` too open. The Bhāṣya establishes:

1. `ardhaṃ pratāpane` = **half an antarakalpa**.
2. `avīcāv antaḥkalpam` = **one antarakalpa**.
3. `kalpaṃ tiraścām` = **the maximum animal lifespan is one antarakalpa**, not the lifespan of all animals.
4. `pretānāṃ māsāhnā` = **one human month is one preta day-and-night**.
5. `śatapañcakam` = **five hundred preta years** by that converted measure.

## Logical Determination

The important distinction is between fixed measure and bounded variability:

```text
fixed lifespan
    ≠
maximum lifespan
```

The Kośa therefore does not impose numerical uniformity where the doctrinal classification does not support it. `niyamo nāsti` explicitly preserves variability while `paramāyus` supplies the upper bound.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
A determinate world-field need not assign a single value;
it may assign a rule, a range, or a maximum.
```

This remains entirely within produced Loka. The temporal distinctions are determinations of conditioned birth-fields, not genetic principles.
