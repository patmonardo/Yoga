# VAK_3.59_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
kukūlaṃ kuṇapaṃ cātha kṣuramārgādikaṃ nadī /
teṣāṃ caturdiśaṃ śītā anye 'ṣṭāvarvudādayaḥ // VAkK_3.59 //
```

**Working translation:**

Embers, putrid mire, then the razor-road group, and a river — these lie in the four directions [of each great hell]. There are also eight cold hells, beginning with Arvuda.

## Natural Argument

The prose first answers the question left by 3.58: what are the sixteen `utsadas` of each great hot hell?

Each of the four gates has four subsidiary regions:

```text
1. kukūla
   knee-deep burning embers

2. kuṇapa
   putrid excrement-mire inhabited by piercing creatures

3. kṣuramārgādika
   a grouped weapon-region consisting of:
       razor road
       sword-leaf forest
       iron śālmalī forest

4. Vaitaraṇī river
   boiling caustic water
```

Thus:

```text
4 gates × 4 utsadas = 16 utsadas
```

The Bhāṣya then pauses over the word `utsada`, gives competing explanations of its name, and opens a doctrinal question about the hell-guards (`narakapāla`): are they sentient beings or not?

One position says they are not sentient beings; their apparent activity is produced by the karma of the hell-beings, like wind operative in cosmic evolution. Another position says that they are sentient beings. The prose tests the latter position by asking where the guards' own karmic result occurs and why they are not burned by the fires of hell.

The passage then returns to the verse and distinguishes the eight cold hells:

```text
Arvuda
Nirarvuda
Aṭata
Hahava
Huhuva
Utpala
Padma
Mahāpadma
```

Their names are said to correspond to bodily and vocal transformations produced in beings struck by extreme cold.

Finally, Vasubandhu locates the cold hells laterally beneath Jambūdvīpa, relative to the great hot hells, and closes with a causal determination: all sixteen principal hells are produced under the predominance of the karma of sentient beings, while individual hells are produced by their own particular karmas.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of the four utsadas, the cold-hell list, their location, and the final karmic determination.
- **Voice: uncertain / transmitted etymology** — explanations of the name `utsada`.
- **Voice: doctrinal position** — `nety eke`, one group denies that hell-guards are sentient beings.
- **Voice: doctrinal position** — `sattvasaṃkhyātā ity eke`, another group affirms that they are sentient beings.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu / dialectical examiner** — objections concerning the guards' karmic result and their immunity to fire; the passage tests the competing positions without a simple explicit final endorsement.

## New Technical Terms

- **utsada** — subsidiary hell-region / annex
  - **status:** Kārikā term technically unpacked by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** structural subdivision of each great hot hell

- **śastrābhinipāta** — assault/fall of weapons
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** common genus under which razor-road, sword-leaf forest, and iron-śālmalī forest are grouped

- **Vaitaraṇī** — the river Vaitaraṇī
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā's compressed `nadī`
  - **function:** names the fourth utsada

- **narakapāla** — hell-guard
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only controversy in this passage
  - **function:** object of a sentience / karmic-production dispute

- **sattvasaṃkhyāta** — counted as a sentient being
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical classification
  - **function:** criterion in the hell-guard dispute

- **sattvakarmādhipatya** — predominance / governing efficacy of sentient beings' karma
  - **status:** Bhāṣya causal determination
  - **function:** explains the production of the hell-world as a shared world-container

- **abhinirvṛtta** — specifically produced / brought into determinate existence
  - **status:** previously encountered cosmological-production vocabulary
  - **function:** links world-structure to karma

## Doctrinal Determination

This passage is much more than a gruesome inventory. It makes two doctrinal moves.

First, the sixteen `utsadas` are generated systematically from the four gates of each great hell:

```text
four gates
    × four subsidiary regions
        = sixteen utsadas
```

Second, the discussion of the hell-guards forces a question about how apparently agentive features of a karmically produced world are to be understood. One view treats the guards as non-sentient productions generated by the karma of the sufferers; another treats them as sentient beings with their own karmic continuity.

The closing causal distinction is especially important:

```text
shared hell-world
    ← sattvakarmādhipatya
       predominance of beings' karma

particular hells
    ← svaiḥ svaiḥ karmabhiḥ
       particular karmas
```

Thus the Bhāṣya distinguishes the production of a common container from the more particular karmic production of specific hell-configurations.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised / repository correction required.**

The present repository `VAK_3.59.md` is misaligned with the primary source. The verified VAkK 3.59 is:

```iast
kukūlaṃ kuṇapaṃ cātha kṣuramārgādikaṃ nadī /
teṣāṃ caturdiśaṃ śītā anye 'ṣṭāvarvudādayaḥ //
```

The Bhāṣya also clarifies:

- `nadī` = Vaitaraṇī;
- `kṣuramārgādikam` is a collective expression for three weapon-regions;
- `teṣāṃ caturdiśam` means the four utsadas occur at each of the four gates/directions;
- `arvudādayaḥ` opens the complete eightfold cold-hell list.

## Logical Determination

The verse begins with spatial subdivision, but the Bhāṣya drives it into causal reflection:

```text
great hell
    → four gates
        → four utsadas at each gate
            → sixteen subsidiary regions
                → question of apparent agents within the region
                    → karmic production of the shared world
```

The important generated relation is therefore:

```text
world-structure
    ↔ karmic production
```

The hell is not merely a place in which karma later bears fruit. Its determinate environment itself is explained as karmically produced.

## Organon Contact Point

This is still produced Loka, not a subtle genetic Principle. But the Bhāṣya now gives us a precise reason not to treat Loka as inert background. The container is itself a result of karmic determination.

The distinction to preserve is:

```text
shared produced field
    ≠ hidden transcendental power
```

Yet within the produced field, causal mediation is real: beings' karma determines a world in which further determinate experiences become possible.
