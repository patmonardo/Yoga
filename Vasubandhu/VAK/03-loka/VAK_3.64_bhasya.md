# VAK_3.64_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
karoṭapāṇayas tāsu mālādhārāḥ sadāmadāḥ /
mahārājikadevāś ca parvateṣv api saptasu // VAkK_3.64 //
```

**Working translation:**

On those terraces dwell the Karoṭapāṇis, Mālādhāras, and Sadāmadas; the Mahārājika devas also dwell on the seven mountains.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.64.md`.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya distributes the inhabitants terrace by terrace:

```text
first terrace
    → Karoṭapāṇi yakṣas

second terrace
    → Mālādhāras

third terrace
    → Sadāmadas / Sadāmattas

fourth terrace
    → the Four Great Kings themselves
       and their attendants
```

Vasubandhu then states that all of these belong to the `caturmahārājakāyika` class. Their settlements are not limited to the terraces: towns and villages belonging to them also occur on the seven mountains beginning with Yugandhara.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory distribution of the deva/yakṣa classes and their settlements.

## New Technical Terms

- **caturmahārājakāyika** — belonging to the host/body of the Four Great Kings
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory classification
  - **function:** unifies the terrace-dwellers and Great Kings under one deva class

- **yakṣa** — yakṣa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification here
  - **function:** identifies the Karoṭapāṇis specifically as yakṣas

- **paricāra** — attendant / retinue
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** identifies those dwelling with the Four Great Kings

- **grāma / nigama** — village / town
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory terms
  - **function:** extends deva habitation from terraces to settlements on the seven mountains

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya turns a compressed list of divine names into a determinate social-spatial order. The four terraces are not merely inhabited; each level has a distinct class, culminating in the Four Great Kings themselves on the fourth terrace.

The seven surrounding mountains are likewise inhabited through their towns and villages.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Substantial clarification.**

The Kārikā can sound as though the named classes are simply co-present on the terraces. The Bhāṣya fixes the distribution level by level and identifies all of them as Cāturmahārājakāyikas.

## Logical Determination

```text
terrace hierarchy
    → distinct inhabitant class at each level
        → culmination in the Four Great Kings
            → extension into mountain settlements
```

The spatial hierarchy becomes a hierarchy of habitation.

## Organon Contact Point

This remains produced Loka. The relevant point is not to elevate the deva hierarchy into a transcendental architecture, but to observe how an already-produced world is increasingly articulated through determinate relations of level, habitation, class, and settlement.
