# VAK_3.101 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
tadapakṣālasādharmyāt na caturthe 'sty aniñjanāt /
na nityaṃ saha sattvena tadvimānodayavyayāt // VAkK_3.101 //
```

**Working translation:** Because of similarity with those internal defects, the lower destructions occur; but in the fourth dhyāna there is no such destruction, because it is unmoving. Yet it is not eternal: its celestial abodes arise and perish together with beings.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first completes the principle opened in the preceding verse. A destruction affecting a dhyāna-world corresponds to an internal `apakṣāla` present in the meditative attainment belonging to that dhyāna. Where the internal disturbance exists, an analogous external disturbance can affect the corresponding realm.

He then asks why there is no destruction by earth. The answer is that the receptacle-world itself is designated earth; fire, water, and wind stand in opposition to it, whereas earth does not destroy earth in the same way.

The fourth dhyāna presents the more important problem. Why does no saṃvartanī reach it? Vasubandhu answers that the fourth dhyāna is free from internal `apakṣāla` and is therefore called `āneñja`, unmoving or undisturbed, by the Buddha. Since there is no corresponding inner disturbance, no analogous external destructive disturbance operates there.

An alternative explanation attributes this immunity to the power of the Śuddhāvāsa beings, who cannot enter the ārūpya realms and cannot go elsewhere.

This immediately raises an objection: if the fourth-dhyāna receptacle is never destroyed by cosmic contraction, must it then be eternal?

Vasubandhu explicitly denies this. The fourth dhyāna is not one continuous plane or single connected ground. Its abodes are spatially discontinuous, "like stars" (`tārakāvat`). When a being is born in one such abode, that vimāna arises with the being; when the being dies there, the abode disappears with it. Thus the fourth dhyāna's exemption from the three cosmic destructions does not entail permanence.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu — the main account linking internal `apakṣāla` to external saṃvartanī and rejecting eternity of the fourth-dhyāna abodes.

**Voice:** uncertain / alternative school position — `śuddhāvāsaprabhāvād ity apare`, the explanation that immunity derives from the power of the Śuddhāvāsa beings.

**Voice:** Objector — `nityaṃ tarhi caturthadhyānabhājanaṃ prāpnoti`, the inference that the fourth-dhyāna receptacle would then be eternal.

## New Technical Terms

- **āneñja** — unmoving, undisturbed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of `aniñjana`
  - **function:** explains why no corresponding external destruction reaches the fourth dhyāna

- **ādhyātmika-apakṣāla** — internal disturbance / internal corrupting factor
  - **status:** developed in the Bhāṣya across VAK 3.100–3.101
  - **function:** grounds the correspondence between meditative structure and external cosmic destruction

- **bāhya-apakṣāla** — external disturbance
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression in this passage
  - **function:** names the external analogue of an internal dhyāna disturbance

- **vicchinnasthānāntara** — having discontinuous spatial locations
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only
  - **function:** denies that the fourth dhyāna is one continuous world-plane

- **tārakāvat** — like stars
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only simile
  - **function:** characterizes the discontinuous placement of fourth-dhyāna abodes

## Doctrinal Determination

The doctrinal distinction is exact:

```text
fourth dhyāna
    → no corresponding internal apakṣāla
    → āneñja
    → no fire/water/wind saṃvartanī

but

no saṃvartanī
    ≠ nitya

fourth-dhyāna vimāna
    → spatially discontinuous
    → arises with the being
    → disappears with the being
    → conditioned
```

The fourth dhyāna therefore has relative immunity from a specified class of cosmic destruction, not ontological permanence.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

`aniñjanāt` is best understood through the Bhāṣya's `āneñja`: the fourth dhyāna is free from the internal disturbance corresponding to the lower destructive media.

The second half should not be read merely as a generic statement that "mansions arise and perish." Vasubandhu's point is more specific: fourth-dhyāna abodes are not one continuous eternal receptacle; they are discontinuous, star-like locations whose vimānas arise and disappear together with the beings occupying them.

## Logical Determination

The decisive inference is blocked:

```text
immunity from destruction-type X
    ≠ unconditional permanence
```

The predicate "not destroyed by fire, water, or wind" is strictly relative. It establishes only the absence of those determinate modes of destruction. It does not remove arising and passing away.

## Organon Contact Point

This is a particularly clean instance of the project's governing Loka guardrail:

```text
relative stability
    remains a determination of produced Loka
```

The fourth dhyāna is more stable than lower rūpa-worlds, but that stability never becomes an unconditioned Principle. Vasubandhu explicitly keeps even its highest receptacles within arising and passing away.
