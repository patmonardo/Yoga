# VAK_3.72 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
sthānāt sthānād adho yāvat tāvad ūrdhvaṃ tatas tataḥ /
nordhvaṃ darśanam asty eṣām anyatrarddhi-parāśrayāt // VAkK_3.72 //
```

**Working translation:**

From each station, the next station above lies as far upward as Jambūdvīpa lies below the former. These beings do not normally see the higher stations, except through ṛddhi or reliance on another.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.72.md`.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first states a rule governing the vertical spacing of heavenly stations. For any station above Jambūdvīpa, the next station above is separated from it by an interval equal to that station's own distance downward to Jambūdvīpa.

Thus the hierarchy expands by repeated proportional spacing rather than by a fixed interval.

Vasubandhu illustrates the sequence with the fourth terrace of the Four Great Kings, the Trāyastriṃśas, Yāmas, Tuṣitas, and finally Akaniṣṭha. Above Akaniṣṭha there is no further station.

The prose then asks whether beings born in a lower station can travel to and see the higher vimānas. The answer is normally no. Two exceptions are distinguished:

```text
own ṛddhi
    → ascent by one's own supernormal power

parāśraya
    → ascent through reliance on another
       especially a more powerful deva or a deva belonging to that higher station
```

The Bhāṣya further distinguishes the visibility of a higher-born visitor from perception of a genuinely higher realm or higher bhūmi. A lower being may see a higher-born being who has come down, but does not thereby acquire the higher realm itself as an object-domain.

A Nikāyāntarīya view is then reported: the higher being does not descend in its own body, but manifests a constructed lower-level body suited to the perceptual range of beings here.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — principal spacing rule, question concerning upward vision, and explanation of the two exceptions.
- **Voice: alternative opinion** — `aghaṇiṣṭhā ity apare`, alternative etymology/name of Akaniṣṭha.
- **Voice: Nikāyāntarīya position** — higher beings appear through a constructed body of a lower bhūmi.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

Beginning from Jambūdvīpa, for whatever station is above it, as far as Jambūdvīpa lies below that station, so far again above that station is the next station.

For example, the fourth terrace, the principal station of the Four Great Kings, is forty thousand yojanas from here. As far as Jambūdvīpa lies below it, so far above it is the station of the Thirty-Three; and as far as Jambūdvīpa lies below that, so far above is the station of the Yāmas; likewise above that is the station of the Tuṣitas. Proceeding through the whole sequence in this manner, as far as Jambūdvīpa lies below Sudarśana, so far above is the station of the Akaniṣṭhas. Above that there is no further station.

Can beings born in a lower station go to higher vimānas and see them? For these there is no upward vision except through ṛddhi or reliance upon another. Thus Trāyastriṃśas might go to the Yāmas through ṛddhi, or through reliance upon another if they were led by a deva possessing greater ṛddhi or by a deva belonging there. The remaining cases are similar.

A being born above who has come down may be seen, but neither a being of a higher dhātu nor one of a higher bhūmi is thereby seen as such, just as a tangible object outside the range of touch would not be touched because it is not an object of that faculty. For that reason such beings do not come in their own bodies. The Nikāyāntarīyas say that, according to their intention, they appear through a constructed body belonging to the lower bhūmi, as if native to this world.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse joins cosmological position to perceptual capacity.

```text
station
    → determinate vertical position
    → determinate perceptual range
```

The critical distinction is that physical or supernormal access does not erase the difference of bhūmi. A lower being's perceptual domain remains constrained by the level to which its faculties belong.

The Nikāyāntarīya proposal is especially revealing: communication across levels requires a form constructed at the recipient's level. The higher body itself remains outside the lower being's proper object-range.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first pāda is more precise than the first-pass rendering suggested. It does not primarily describe a "visibility range." It establishes the spacing rule:

```text
For each station,
its distance down to Jambūdvīpa
    =
the distance from it to the next station above.
```

`ṛddhi-parāśrayāt` should also be understood as two alternatives:

```text
ṛddhi
or
para-āśraya
```

rather than as one compound notion.

## Logical Determination

```text
hierarchical position
    → proportional interval
        → level-specific object-domain
            → restricted upward perception
                → exceptional mediation
```

The important logical point is not simply that a higher world is hidden. A bhūmi determines what can function as an object for beings belonging to it.

## New Technical Terms

- **mūlasthāna** — principal station / primary seat
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** identifies the principal location of a deva class

- **parāśraya** — reliance upon another
  - **status:** Kārikā term clarified by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** mediated access to a higher station through a more powerful or higher-station deva

- **aviṣayatva** — being outside an object's proper range / not being an object-domain
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term
  - **function:** explains why higher-level bodies cannot simply become objects of lower-level faculties

- **nirmita** — constructed / manifested form
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary
  - **function:** Nikāyāntarīya explanation of how a higher being can appear at a lower bhūmi

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
A standpoint does not merely occupy a location;
it determines an object-domain.
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives a stronger result than a simple horizon metaphor. The restriction is categorical to the level: a genuinely higher bhūmi is not automatically available as an object for a lower one. Mediation must occur in a form adequate to the lower field.
