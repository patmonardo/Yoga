## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first locates the Trāyastriṃśa gods on the summit of Meru, then asks for the summit's measure.

The principal account takes each side of the summit to be eighty thousand yojanas, matching the corresponding measure below. An alternative account makes each side twenty thousand yojanas, yielding eighty thousand in total circumference.

The four intermediate directions are then identified with the four corners of Sumeru's summit. At those corners rise four peaks, each five hundred yojanas in measure, inhabited by yakṣas called Vajrapāṇis.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — principal explanation of the summit, corners, peaks, and Vajrapāṇi yakṣas.
- **Voice: alternative opinion** — `anye punar āhuḥ`, giving twenty thousand yojanas per side and eighty thousand in circumference.

## New Technical Terms

- **parikṣepa** — circumference / surrounding measure
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** distinguishes total perimeter from the length of an individual side

- **koṇa** — corner
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** explains `vidik` as the corners of Sumeru's summit

- **abhyudgata** — risen / projecting upward
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** describes the four corner peaks

- **yakṣa** — yakṣa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** identifies the beings called Vajrapāṇis

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya turns the compressed summit description into a measured habitation-field:

```text
Meru summit
    → Trāyastriṃśa habitation
    → measured sides / circumference
    → four corners
    → four projecting peaks
    → Vajrapāṇi yakṣas
```

The passage also preserves a real disagreement over how the eighty-thousand measure is to be understood.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and correction of precision.**

`aśītisahasradik` should not simply be rendered as an undifferentiated “eighty-thousand extent.” The Bhāṣya's principal reading is eighty thousand for each side, while an alternative construes the total circumference as eighty thousand.

`vajrapāṇi` is also clarified here as the name of a class of yakṣas, not merely a generic epithet.

## Logical Determination

```text
summit
    → directional measure
        → corner determination
            → projecting peak
                → inhabitant
```

The world-field becomes concrete through the reciprocal determination of place, measure, and occupant.

## Organon Contact Point

This remains produced Loka. The logical interest is the increasingly explicit relational articulation of the summit-field: side, circumference, corner, height, and inhabitant are mutually determining features of one constructed cosmological region.
