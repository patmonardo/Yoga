# VAK_3.12_bhasya — Scriptural proof of antarābhava

## Kārikā

### IAST

```iast
sahaikatra dvayābhāvāt asantānād dvayodayāt /
kaṇṭhokteścāsti gandharvāt pañcokteḥ gatisūtrataḥ //
```

### Best Translation

Because two cannot coexist in one place, because [a reflection] is not a continuity, and because it arises from two [causes], it is not a valid example. Further, [intermediate existence] exists because it is expressly stated, because of the gandharva, because five are stated, and because of the sūtra on courses.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal / Continuous Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Further, intermediate existence exists because it is expressly stated. A sūtra says: “There are seven existences: hell-existence, animal-existence, preta-existence, deva-existence, human-existence, karma-existence, and intermediate existence.”

**Voice: Objector**

That sūtra is not recited in our transmission.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Then consider another [proof]: because of the gandharva. It is said: “When three conditions are present, descent into the mother’s womb occurs: the mother is fertile and in season, the mother and father are sexually impassioned and united, and the gandharva is present.” Apart from an intermediate existence, what other gandharva could this be?

**Voice: Objector**

We do not recite that passage either. We read instead: “the dissolution of the aggregates is present.”

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

If so, how is the Āśvalāyana Sūtra to be interpreted? It asks whether the gandharva who has become present is a kṣatriya, brāhmaṇa, vaiśya, or śūdra, and whether he has come from the east, south, west, or north. Coming from a direction cannot reasonably be predicated of mere dissolution of aggregates.

If that too is not admitted, then consider another [proof]: because five [non-returners] are stated. The Blessed One teaches five kinds of non-returner: one who attains parinirvāṇa in the intermediate state, one who attains it upon rebirth, one who attains it without effort, one who attains it with effort, and one who goes upstream. If there were no intermediate existence, how could there be one called an `antarāparinirvāyin`?

**Voice: Objector**

Some say there are devas named “Antarā.”

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Then by the same reasoning the other terms, beginning with “upapadya,” would also have to be names of devas. That construction is therefore not sound.

Further, there is the sūtra on the seven courses of good persons. There three kinds of `antarāparinirvāyin` are taught according to differences of time, place, and degree. They are illustrated by three kinds of fire that go out at progressively different points before reaching their destination. Such distinctions are not plausibly explained by inventing three classes of devas named “Antarā.”

## Course of the Argument

### Problem

VAkK 3.11 established the case for `antarābhava` by `yukti`, reasoning. VAkK 3.12 now supplies the second announced standard: `āgama`, authoritative teaching.

### Structure of the proof

The proof is cumulative rather than dependent upon one isolated citation:

```text
1. Explicit enumeration:
   seven bhavas include antarābhava.

2. Conception passage:
   gandharva must be present for garbhāvakrānti.

3. Āśvalāyana clarification:
   gandharva is spoken of as coming from a social class and direction.

4. Five non-returners:
   antarāparinirvāyin requires an intermediate state.

5. Seven courses of good persons:
   three antarāparinirvāyins differentiated by time/place/degree.
```

The opponent repeatedly attempts a textual escape:

```text
“We do not recite that sūtra.”
“We read skandhabheda instead.”
“Antarā is merely a name of devas.”
```

Vasubandhu answers by shifting to another textual witness or by showing that the proposed reinterpretation produces implausible consequences.

### Result

The argument for `antarābhava` has now passed through both declared standards:

```text
yukti
    causal continuity requires mediation

āgama
    multiple teachings presuppose or explicitly name that mediation
```

The Bhāṣya therefore does not ask the reader merely to imagine an intermediate being. It tests whether the category is required both by reasoning and by the received doctrinal corpus.

## New Technical Terms

- **kaṇṭhokti** — explicit or direct statement
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās
  - **function:** principle of textual proof

- **āmnāya** — received recitation / textual transmission
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā here
  - **function:** objection concerning scriptural authority

- **garbhāvakrānti** — descent/entry into the womb
  - **status:** developed explicitly in the prose and upcoming verses
  - **function:** doctrinal transition from intermediate existence to rebirth

- **gandharva** — the being present at conception, interpreted here as the intermediate existence
  - **status:** Kārikā term
  - **function:** scriptural evidence

- **antarāparinirvāyin** — one who attains parinirvāṇa in the intermediate state
  - **status:** absent from this Kārikā as a full term
  - **function:** scriptural consequence supporting antarābhava

- **satpuruṣagati** — course of a good person
  - **status:** Bhāṣya citation
  - **function:** supplementary scriptural proof

## Doctrinal / Logical Determination

VAkK 3.12 is not fundamentally a cosmological inventory. It is a **proof-theory verse**.

The issue is whether a proposed transition-state has sufficient warrant. Vasubandhu uses two distinct forms of control:

```text
Reasoning:
    continuity of the dharma-series

Textual authority:
    convergent doctrinal testimony
```

Most importantly, he does not treat textual authority naively. The opponent disputes recensions and proposes alternate readings; Vasubandhu then tests those readings against other passages and against their consequences.

Thus `āgama` itself is submitted to rational discrimination.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The compact second half should be heard as a sequence of proof-markers:

```text
kaṇṭhokteḥ
    because it is expressly stated

gandharvāt
    because of the gandharva passage

pañcokteḥ
    because the five non-returners are taught

gatisūtrataḥ
    because of the sūtra on the courses of good persons
```

The first half belongs to the completion of the reflection-analogy refutation begun under VAkK 3.11; the second half begins the independent `āgama` proof.

## Organon Contact Point

The important generated relation is methodological:

```text
Representation
    proposes a picture

Yukti
    tests whether the relation is rationally necessary

Āgama
    tests whether the doctrinal corpus consistently bears that relation

Abhidharma
    discriminates both
```

So even “scripture” is not allowed to remain an opaque authority. Competing recitations and interpretations are compared by coherence and consequence.

The Rational Kośa point is therefore modest but strong:

```text
Authority without discrimination is insufficient.
Reason without textual control is insufficient.
The category is stabilized through their reciprocal test.
```

## Summary

VAkK 3.12 completes the defense of `antarābhava` by turning from reasoning to authoritative teaching. Vasubandhu accumulates several independent witnesses and answers attempts to reinterpret or reject them. The result is not simply “the scriptures say there is an intermediate being,” but a more rigorous conclusion: the category survives both causal analysis and adversarial textual criticism.
