### Source Caution

The source prints `arvuda`; the normalized form is `arbuda`. The Bhāṣya itself presents the cold-hell durations by analogy rather than by reducing them to ordinary calendar years.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first asks for the measure of lifespan in the cold hells. The answer is not given as a direct numerical total. Instead, the Buddha's comparison is cited.

Imagine a Māgadha sesame-vāha holding twenty khārīs, completely filled with sesame seeds and tied shut. If one sesame seed were removed only once every hundred years, that whole store would reach exhaustion sooner than the lifespan of a being born in Arbuda would reach its end.

The comparison therefore does not identify the Arbuda lifespan with the sesame-depletion duration. It says that even this enormously prolonged depletion would finish first.

The later cold-hell lifespans then increase by a fixed ratio. Twenty Arbuda lifespans make one Nirarbuda lifespan. The same twentyfold relation continues through the sequence; the source explicitly gives the later example that twenty Padmas make one Mahāpadma.

## Translation

“What is the measure of lifespan in the cold hells?

The lifespan [of those in Arbuda] is illustrated by [the time required for] the exhaustion of a vāha through the removal of one sesame seed every hundred years; after Arbuda, the lifespans increase successively twentyfold.

The Blessed One described their lifespan only by comparison: ‘Suppose, monks, there were here a Māgadha sesame-vāha of twenty khārīs, filled with sesame seeds and tied shut. Suppose someone removed from it a single sesame seed at the end of every hundred years. More quickly would that twenty-khārī Māgadha sesame-vāha be exhausted and come to an end by this procedure; but I do not say that the lifespan of beings born in Arbuda would thereby have reached its limit. Just as twenty Arbudas make one Nirarbuda … and just as twenty Padmas make one Mahāpadma.’”

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the question and frames the cold-hell lifespan measure.

**Voice: Buddha, cited by Vasubandhu** — supplies the sesame-vāha comparison and the twentyfold progression.

## New Technical Terms

- **tilavāha** — a vāha-measure filled with sesame seeds
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory measure in this passage
  - **function:** supplies a finite comparison for an otherwise inexpressibly vast lifespan

- **khārī** — capacity measure
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya explanation
  - **function:** specifies the size of the Māgadha vāha as twenty khārīs

- **upamānamātra** — comparison alone / merely by analogy
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya qualification
  - **function:** prevents the sesame-depletion procedure from being mistaken for an exact equality

- **parikṣaya / paryādāna** — exhaustion / complete depletion
  - **status:** explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** marks the endpoint of the comparison procedure

- **Nirarbuda** — second cold hell in the lifespan sequence
  - **status:** named explicitly in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** establishes the first twentyfold multiplication beyond Arbuda

## Doctrinal Determination

The key doctrinal point is that the Arbuda lifespan is not given as an ordinary chronological number.

```text
20-khārī Māgadha sesame-vāha
    → remove 1 sesame seed every 100 years
    → entire store is eventually exhausted

but

that exhaustion occurs
    before
Arbuda lifespan reaches its limit
```

The analogy therefore gives a lower comparison for the magnitude of Arbuda duration, not an equation.

The higher cold hells are then ordered through a determinate multiplication rule:

```text
20 Arbuda
    = 1 Nirarbuda

...

20 Padma
    = 1 Mahāpadma
```

This is produced saṃsāric duration: enormously extended, but still articulated through determinate succession and ratio.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required.**

The first-pass Kārikā translation made the Arbuda lifespan equal to the time required to exhaust the sesame vāha. The Bhāṣya shows that this is too strong. The Buddha says the sesame store would be exhausted **more quickly**; even then he would not declare the Arbuda lifespan exhausted.

A better working translation is therefore:

> The lifespan in Arbuda is illustrated by a vāha of sesame seeds from which one seed is removed every hundred years; even that store is exhausted before Arbuda life reaches its limit. The later [cold hells] have lifespans increasing successively twentyfold.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya distinguishes **measure by equation** from **measure by comparison**.

```text
upamāna
    ≠ exact numerical identity

upamāna
    = finite procedure used to indicate magnitude
```

The cold-hell sequence itself is then exact in a different sense:

```text
one level
    × 20
        → next level
```

Thus the passage combines an intentionally non-exhaustive analogy for the base magnitude with an exact ratio governing the ordered series.

## Organon Contact Point

A useful light contact point is the distinction between an **illustrative bound** and an **exact determination**. The sesame procedure makes the magnitude thinkable without pretending to calculate it exactly; the twentyfold progression then supplies the determinate relation internal to the sequence.
