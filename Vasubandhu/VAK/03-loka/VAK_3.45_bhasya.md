### Source caution

The working e-text is not a critical edition and contains evident irregularities in this passage, including `ākāśaṣpratiṣṭham` and `dṛḍhaṃṃ`. These are preserved above rather than silently normalized. The sense of the prose is nevertheless clear enough for the present analysis.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Explicit transition from `sattvaloka` to `bhājanaloka`

The Bhāṣya announces the new section directly:

```iast
uktaḥ sattvaloko bhājanaloka idānīṃ vaktavyaḥ
```

“The sentient world has been explained; now the container-world is to be explained.”

This is a major structural boundary in Chapter 3. The preceding discussion followed beings through conditioned existence, death, rebirth, and orientation. The present section turns to the shared receptacle in which those beings exist.

### 2. The object is the great world-system

Vasubandhu states that this is the arrangement (`saṃniveśa`) of the `trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra-lokadhātu`.

Thus the verse is not merely describing a local landscape. It begins the construction of the large-scale world-system.

### 3. The first support is the wind-maṇḍala

The Bhāṣya explains the first cosmological support as:

```iast
ākāśa-pratiṣṭham adhastād vāyumaṇḍalam abhinirvṛttam
```

The wind-maṇḍala is below and is established in space. It is not yet the visible inhabited surface but the first support in the arrangement of the container-world.

### 4. Its genesis is karmic

The decisive addition of the Bhāṣya is:

```iast
sarvasattvānāṃ karmādhipatyena
```

The wind-maṇḍala comes into formation through the governing efficacy of the karma of all beings.

The Bhāṣya therefore does not introduce the container-world as an independently self-grounding object standing outside the causal order of beings. Its first support is itself generated within the karmic field.

```text
sarvasattva
    → karma
        → karmādhipatya
            → vāyumaṇḍala-abhinirvṛtti
```

This does **not** by itself authorize a modern physical theory of karma, spacetime, or gravitation. Textually, the claim is that the world-support arises through karmic predominance or governing efficacy.

### 5. Measure and firmness

The vertical extent is given as sixteen `lakṣas` of yojanas. Its lateral extent (`pariṇāha`) is said to be `asaṃkhya`, incalculable.

The prose also emphasizes extraordinary firmness: even a massive adamantine force is said to be unable to break it. The karmically produced world-support is therefore not presented as merely subjective or imaginary; it functions as a stable common condition of the world-system.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — structural transition and explanatory prose.
- **Voice: received cosmological doctrine / “they maintain”** — `icchanti` marks the cosmological arrangement as a maintained doctrinal account rather than an unmarked first-person assertion.
- No independent objector appears in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **bhājanaloka** — container-world / receptacle-world
  - **status:** Kārikā term, newly foregrounded as the major section heading
  - **function:** names the shared world-container as distinct from `sattvaloka`

- **saṃniveśa** — arrangement / disposition / configuration
  - **status:** Kārikā term
  - **function:** specifies that the present topic is the ordered structure of the world-system

- **trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra-lokadhātu** — great trichiliocosm / great thousandfold world-system
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** fixes the scale of the arrangement being described

- **ākāśa-pratiṣṭha** — established in space
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation here
  - **function:** determines the support relation of the wind-maṇḍala

- **abhinirvṛtta** — fully arisen / come into formation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reflective vocabulary
  - **function:** expresses the genesis of the cosmological support

- **karmādhipatya** — governing efficacy / predominance of karma
  - **status:** decisive Bhāṣya term
  - **function:** gives the causal ground for formation of the shared world-support

- **pariṇāha** — breadth / lateral extent
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification
  - **function:** distinguishes lateral measure from vertical `udvedha`

## Doctrinal Determination

The opening of `bhājanaloka` establishes three points at once:

```text
world-container
    = ordered (`saṃniveśa`)
    = generated (`abhinirvṛtta`)
    = karmically conditioned (`karmādhipatya`)
```

The transition from `sattvaloka` to `bhājanaloka` is therefore not a transition from causal life to causally independent matter. The first objective support of the shared world is introduced as a result within the same karmic order.

The text supports the cautious formulation:

> The common container-world is not merely where karmically conditioned beings happen to reside; its first cosmological support is itself said to arise through the governing efficacy of the karma of all beings.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya confirms that `lakṣaṣoḍaśakodvedham` means a vertical extent of sixteen `lakṣas` of yojanas and that `asaṃkhya` concerns the lateral extent.

A stronger translation than the first-pass version is therefore:

> There, they describe the arrangement of the container-world: below is a wind-maṇḍala sixteen hundred-thousand yojanas in vertical extent and incalculable in breadth.

The Bhāṣya also makes clear that the Kārikā's static description has a genetic background: the wind-maṇḍala has `abhinirvṛtta` through `sarvasattvānāṃ karmādhipatyena`.

## Provisional Logical Determination

```text
sattvaloka
    → bhājanaloka

karma of beings
    → governing efficacy
        → formation of shared support
```

The important logical gain is not a simple subject/object opposition. The world inhabited by beings is presented as a common objective structure whose genesis is mediated by their karma.

This is a legitimate Organon contact point, but it should remain restrained in the Bhāṣya pass. The text has not yet supplied a theory of space itself; it has supplied a karmic genesis of a world-support that is `ākāśa-pratiṣṭha`, established in space.
