# VAK_3.93_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
vivartate 'tha saṃvṛtta āste saṃvartate samam /
te hy aśītir mahākalpaḥ tad asaṃkhya-trayodbhavam // VAkK_3.93 //
```

**Working translation:** It expands; then, having expanded, it stands; it contracts, for an equal duration. These eighty intermediate kalpas constitute a mahākalpa; from three asaṃkhyeyas of these arises [buddhahood].

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.93.md`.

### Source Caution

The Bhāṣya's list between the beginning and end of the sixty-place numerical sequence is very long; the excerpt preserves its opening, closing, and Vasubandhu's explicit conclusion. The point is not that `asaṃkhyeya` is absolutely beyond enumeration. It is the conventional name assigned when calculation reaches the sixtieth place in this numerical series.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first completes the temporal architecture established in the preceding verses. The four great phases are equal in duration:

```text
vivarta                 20 antarakalpas
saṃvṛtta-sthiti         20 antarakalpas
saṃvarta                20 antarakalpas
saṃvṛtta-sthiti         20 antarakalpas
                       ----------------
                        80 antarakalpas
                        = 1 mahākalpa
```

Even where the human lifespan increase and decrease (`utkarṣa`, `apakarṣa`) do not operate, the duration is nevertheless reckoned as equal.

The Bhāṣya also retains the internal asymmetry of the active phases:

```text
vivarta
    1 antarakalpa  → receptacle-world forms
    19             → beings populate it

saṃvarta
    19             → beings disappear
    1              → receptacle-world is destroyed
```

The resulting four groups of twenty antarakalpas make eighty, and this is the measure (`pramāṇa`) of a `mahākalpa`.

Vasubandhu then asks a striking ontological question: **What is a kalpa by nature (`kiṃsvabhāva`)?** His answer is concise: `pañcaskandhasvabhāvaḥ` — it has the nature of the five aggregates. A kalpa is therefore not introduced as an independently existing cosmic substance or container of time. Its reality is that of the conditioned aggregate-complex through which the world-process occurs.

The discussion then turns to the claim that buddhahood is attained through three `asaṃkhyeyas` of kalpas. The relevant kalpa is precisely the `mahākalpa` just defined.

An objection follows: if `asaṃkhyeya` means "incalculable," how can there be three of them? Vasubandhu rejects the assumption behind the objection. He cites a `muktakasūtra` according to which `asaṃkhyeya` is reached through **sixty successive numerical places (`ṣaṣṭiḥ sthānāntarāṇi`)**. The series begins with one, ten, hundred, thousand, and continues through increasingly named decimal magnitudes until the sixtieth position, `asaṃkhya`.

Once that numerical magnitude is reached, counting begins again. Reaching it three times is what is meant by **three asaṃkhyeyas**.

Thus:

```text
asaṃkhyeya
    ≠ absolutely impossible to count

asaṃkhyeya
    = conventional name of an enormous determinate number
    = the sixtieth numerical place in the cited system
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly concludes:

```iast
na tu naiva parisaṃkhyātuṃ śakyanta iti
```

It does **not** mean that they simply cannot be enumerated.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the four twenty-antarakalpa phases, definition of the mahākalpa, and the five-aggregate nature of kalpa.

**Voice: Objector** — asks how `asaṃkhyeya` can be spoken of as three if it has no calculable terminus.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — rejects that interpretation and supplies the sixty-place numerical convention.

## New Technical Terms

- **mahākalpa-pramāṇa** — the measure of a great kalpa: eighty antarakalpas
  - **status:** developed from the preceding kalpa analysis
  - **function:** fixes the complete duration of the world-cycle

- **pañcaskandha-svabhāva** — having the five aggregates as its nature
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only determination in this passage
  - **function:** answers what a kalpa is by nature and prevents reification of time as an independent substance

- **sthānāntara** — numerical place / successive place-value
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical term here
  - **function:** defines the large-number system used to explain `asaṃkhyeya`

- **asaṃkhyeya** — an enormous named numerical magnitude
  - **status:** familiar doctrinal term, technically redefined here
  - **function:** denotes the magnitude reached at the sixtieth numerical place, not absolute innumerability

- **muktakasūtra** — an independent / detached sūtra cited as authority
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only source designation
  - **function:** textual authority for the sixty-place numerical system

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage brings three levels of temporal analysis together:

```text
antarakalpa
    → local lifespan-cycle measure

mahākalpa
    → 80 antarakalpas
    → complete world-cycle measure

asaṃkhyeya mahākalpas
    → vast numerical accumulation used for the bodhisattva path
```

But the Bhāṣya refuses to turn any of these into an independent metaphysical Time. Even `kalpa` is said to be `pañcaskandhasvabhāva`.

This is fully consistent with the chapter's governing character: **Loka is produced and conditioned.** Its vast temporal structures are measures of conditioned processes, not hidden productive principles behind them.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Revision required.**

The existing Kārikā translation's phrase "three incalculables" risks suggesting quantities beyond all counting. The Bhāṣya instead requires something like:

> These eighty [intermediate kalpas] are a mahākalpa; from three `asaṃkhyeyas` of these [mahākalpas] arises buddhahood.

Here `asaṃkhyeya` should preferably remain untranslated or be glossed as **the conventionally named enormous number reached at the sixtieth numerical place**.

The first half is also clarified: the four phases are equal **in duration**, even though their internal processes differ.

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes equality of quantitative duration from difference of internal constitution:

```text
same temporal magnitude
    ≠ same process-content
```

This was already visible in VAK 3.91–3.92 and is now generalized to the complete mahākalpa.

The `asaṃkhyeya` discussion adds another important distinction:

```text
practically enormous
    ≠ logically innumerable
```

Vasubandhu preserves determinacy even at the limit of ordinary calculation.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
Measure remains determinate even when magnitude exceeds ordinary representation.
```

The philosophically strongest Bhāṣya sentence is perhaps the simplest:

```iast
kalpaḥ kiṃsvabhāvaḥ /
pañcaskandhasvabhāvaḥ /
```

The enormous world-cycle is still only conditioned aggregate-being. The Kośa does not promote cosmic duration into an Absolute.
