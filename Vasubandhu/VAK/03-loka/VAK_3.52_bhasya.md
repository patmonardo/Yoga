# VAK_3.52_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ābhyantaraḥ samudro 'sau triguṇaḥ sa tu pārśvataḥ /
ardhārdhenāparāḥ śītāḥ śeṣaṃ bāhyo mahodadheḥ // VAkK_3.52 //
```

**Working translation:**

That first Śītā is the inner ocean; along its side its measure is threefold. The remaining Śītās diminish successively by halves. What remains is the outer great ocean.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya explicitly distinguishes two oceans: an inner and an outer ocean. The first Śītā between Sumeru and Yugandhara is the inner ocean. Its width has already been given as eighty thousand yojanas; reckoned along the Yugandhara shore, its measure is three times that.

The six remaining Śītās decrease successively by halves. Their longitudinal extent is left unenumerated because of their great spreading.

The verse then asks what `śeṣam`, the remainder, means. Vasubandhu answers: the interval between Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa. This is the outer great ocean, filled with salty or alkaline water. The four continents will be situated in this outer ocean.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — direct exposition, including the rhetorical question `kiṃ śeṣam?`.
- No separate opponent appears.

## New Technical Terms

- **ābhyantara-samudra** — inner ocean
  - **function:** designation of the first Śītā

- **bāhya-mahāsamudra** — outer great ocean
  - **function:** interval between Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa

- **kṣārodaka** — saline / alkaline water
  - **function:** qualitative distinction of the outer ocean from the inner Śītās

- **dairdhya** — length
  - **function:** dimension left unenumerated for the Śītās

- **śeṣa** — remainder
  - **function:** the residual spatial interval constituting the outer ocean

## Doctrinal Determination

This verse closes the foundational container-world sequence. The concentric mountain-and-sea system terminates in an outer great ocean bounded by Cakravāḍa, within which the four great continents are located.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Bhāṣya identifies `śeṣam` precisely as the Nimindhara–Cakravāḍa interval and distinguishes its saline water from the inner seas.

## Logical Determination

```text
inner concentric articulation
    → progressive diminution
        → residual exterior interval
            → outer ocean
                → field for inhabited continents
```

VAkK 3.52 therefore completes the immediate support architecture and prepares the transition from world-foundation to world-habitation.

## Organon Contact Point
