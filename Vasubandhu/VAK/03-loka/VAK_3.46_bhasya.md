### Source caution

The e-text contains evident irregularities, including `laklṣāḥ`, and should not be silently normalized. The numerical explanation is nevertheless explicit enough to correct the ambiguity left in the first-pass Kārikā file.

## Natural Argument

### 1. The water-maṇḍala forms above the wind-maṇḍala

The prose continues directly from 3.45: above the wind-maṇḍala, clouds arise through the karma of beings and rain down streams described as axle-sized.

The resulting mass of water forms a maṇḍala.

```text
vāyumaṇḍala
    → karmically arisen clouds
        → rain
            → water-maṇḍala
```

The Bhāṣya thereby adds a process of formation that the compressed Kārikā merely measures.

### 2. The numerical expression is clarified

The prose states that the water-maṇḍala measures:

```text
11 lakṣas + 20,000 yojanas
```

in vertical extent.

Thus the first-pass uncertainty can be removed. The phrase is not “eleven and twenty thousand” in isolation; it is eleven hundred-thousand plus twenty thousand yojanas.

### 3. Objection: why does the water not flow away sideways?

Vasubandhu introduces a physical-coherence question:

```iast
kathaṃ tā āpo na tiryaṅg visravanti
```

“Why do those waters not flow away laterally?”

The first answer is again:

```iast
sattvānāṃ karmādhipatyena
```

“Through the governing efficacy of the karma of beings.”

This confirms that `karmādhipatya` is not incidental to the wind-disc alone. It governs the stability of the common world-structure as well.

### 4. Analogy from digestion

One view illustrates karmic containment by analogy with consumed food and drink, which do not simply pass unprocessed into the lower digestive tract.

The analogy is not a second causal principle; it is offered to make intelligible how a mass can remain ordered rather than dispersing despite an apparently natural tendency to do so.

### 5. Nikāyāntarīya alternative

The Bhāṣya then identifies another position explicitly:

```iast
kusūlanyāyena vāyunā saṃdhāryanta iti nikāyāntarīyāḥ
```

The Nikāyāntarīyas say that the waters are held together by wind, by analogy with a granary or enclosing receptacle.

This is an important voice distinction:

```text
primary account
    → karmādhipatya

Nikāyāntarīya account
    → wind physically contains/supports the waters
```

The Bhāṣya therefore preserves competing explanatory models inside the cosmology rather than merely reciting a fixed picture.

### 6. Water is transformed into the golden earth

The waters are then turned or churned by winds that themselves arise through the power of the karma of beings:

```iast
sattvānāṃ karmaprabhāvasaṃbhūtair vāyubhiḥ
```

Their upper portion becomes golden (`kāñcanībhavanti`). The text compares this process to the formation of a skin or film on cooked milk.

Thus the golden world-support is not merely placed on top of the water. It is generated through transformation of the water under karmically generated winds.

```text
karma-prabhāva
    → winds
        → turning of waters
            → kāñcanībhāva
```

### 7. What remains?

The Bhāṣya asks explicitly:

```iast
kiṃ ca śeṣam
```

“What remains?”

It answers: three `lakṣas` plus twenty thousand yojanas of water remain. Since the original water depth was eleven `lakṣas` plus twenty thousand, the transformed golden portion is eight `lakṣas` high.

Thus:

```text
water originally
    11 lakṣas + 20,000

becomes gold
    8 lakṣas

water remaining
    3 lakṣas + 20,000
```

The resulting golden earth lies above the remaining waters.

## Literal Prose Translation

Above it, the water-maṇḍala has a vertical extent of eleven hundred-thousand plus twenty thousand yojanas; the word “maṇḍala” is understood from the preceding context.

On that wind-maṇḍala, clouds arise through the karma of beings and rain down in streams the size of an axle. From these there comes to be their maṇḍala. Its vertical extent is eleven hundred-thousand plus twenty thousand yojanas.

Why do those waters not flow away sideways? Through the governing efficacy of the karma of beings. Some say that, just as food and drink that have been eaten and drunk do not pass uncooked into the lower digestive tract, so it is here. The Nikāyāntarīyas say that the waters are held together by wind according to the analogy of a granary.

These waters, moreover, being turned by winds produced through the power of the karma of beings, become golden on their upper surface, by analogy with the formation of a skin on cooked milk. From these there comes to be their maṇḍala.

Afterwards, the remainder becomes gold to a height of eight hundred-thousand yojanas. What remains? Three hundred-thousand plus twenty thousand yojanas. This becomes the golden earth above the waters. Thus the vertical extent of the water-and-gold maṇḍala has been stated.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — main exposition, questions, numerical clarification, and report of positions.
- **Voice: some teachers (`eke`)** — digestive analogy explaining why the waters do not disperse.
- **Voice: Nikāyāntarīya position** — wind contains the waters according to the granary analogy.
- The Bhāṣya does not clearly adjudicate between the two explanations in this local unit; it records them within the construction of the model.

## New Technical Terms

- **jala-/ap-maṇḍala** — water-maṇḍala
  - **status:** Kārikā content clarified by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** second large-scale support of the container-world

- **karmādhipatya** — governing efficacy of karma
  - **status:** carried forward from 3.45
  - **function:** explains why the waters remain ordered rather than dispersing

- **karmaprabhāva** — power / efficacy of karma
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** generates the winds that transform the water into the golden earth

- **saṃdhāryante** — are held together / supported
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reflective vocabulary
  - **function:** expresses the Nikāyāntarīya causal account

- **kusūla-nyāya** — analogy of a granary / enclosing receptacle
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only analogy
  - **function:** explains containment of the waters by wind

- **kāñcanībhavati** — becomes golden
  - **status:** Bhāṣya genetic formulation
  - **function:** gives the transformation hidden behind the Kārikā's `bhavati kāñcanam`

- **śeṣa** — remainder
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly interrogated by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** distinguishes the water remaining from the portion transformed into gold

- **kāñcanamayī mahī** — golden earth
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification
  - **function:** names the stable world-support produced above the waters

## Doctrinal Determination

VAkK 3.46 is not merely a list of three substances. The Bhāṣya supplies a genetic sequence:

```text
karma of beings
    → clouds and rain
        → water-maṇḍala

karmic efficacy
    → stability of water

karma-produced winds
    → transformation of upper water
        → golden earth
```

This significantly sharpens the first-pass analysis. The relation is not simply:

```text
wind
    → water
        → gold
```

but rather a repeatedly karmically mediated genesis of the common container-world.

The passage also demonstrates that the Bhāṣya is willing to ask how the cosmological account is causally coherent and to preserve rival explanations. The cosmology therefore contains reflective reasoning, not only inherited spatial description.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya resolves both numerical questions left provisional in the Kārikā file.

A clearer translation is:

> The water-maṇḍala is eleven hundred-thousand plus twenty thousand yojanas in vertical extent. Afterwards, eight hundred-thousand yojanas of it become gold, leaving three hundred-thousand plus twenty thousand yojanas of water.

This is preferable to reading `śeṣa` as though the eight-lakṣa portion itself were simply “the remainder.” The prose explicitly asks what remains and answers that the remaining water is three lakṣas plus twenty thousand; the eight-lakṣa portion is what has become the golden earth.

The existing Kārikā file should eventually be corrected accordingly.

## Provisional Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya now gives a genuine genesis of determinate world-support:

```text
formation
    → containment
        → transformation
            → stable earth
```

The especially important relation is:

```text
sattva-karma
    → bhājanaloka formation
        → objective conditions for sattvas
```

This reciprocal structure is textually stronger after 3.46 than it was at 3.45, because both the formation and the persistence/transformation of the shared container are referred back to karmic efficacy.

The question whether this amounts to a theory of the origin or determination of space itself remains open. The text still describes supports as `ākāśa-pratiṣṭha`; it has not derived `ākāśa` from karma. That distinction should be preserved as we continue through Lokanirdeśa.
