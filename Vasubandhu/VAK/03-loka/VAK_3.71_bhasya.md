# VAK_3.71 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
kāmopapattayas tisraḥ kāmadevāḥ samānuṣāḥ /
sukhopapattayas tisro nava tridhyānabhūmayaḥ // VAkK_3.71 //
```

**Working translation:**

There are three desire-births among humans and the desire-realm gods; there are three pleasure-births, constituted by the nine grounds of the three dhyānas.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.71.md`.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first asks how the three `kāmopapatti` are constituted. They are distinguished not simply by realm or location, but by the mode in which desire-objects are available and enjoyed.

```text
1. pratyupasthita-kāma
   already-present desire objects
   → humans and four deva classes

2. nirmita-kāma
   self-created desire objects
   → Nirmāṇarati devas

3. paranirmita-kāma
   desire objects created by others
   → Paranirmitavaśavartin devas
```

The first group includes humans and four deva classes who exercise mastery over already-present objects of desire. The second creates its own objects and enjoys them at will. The third enjoys objects created by others and exercises mastery over those.

The Bhāṣya then turns to the form realm. The nine grounds belonging to the first three dhyānas constitute three `sukhopapatti` classes because beings there remain for long periods dwelling happily through forms of pleasure associated with those dhyānas.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

Here in the desire realm three desire-births are to be understood.

How so?

There are beings whose desires are already present; they exercise mastery over already-present objects of desire. Such are humans and certain gods, namely four classes of gods.

There are beings whose desires are constructed: having constructed objects of desire, they exercise mastery over them. Such are the Nirmāṇarati gods.

There are beings whose desires are constructed by others: they exercise mastery over objects of desire constructed by others. Such are the Paranirmitavaśavartin gods.

Because they enjoy, respectively, what has arisen as given, what they themselves construct at will, and what others construct for them at will, these are called the three desire-births.

In the form realm, the nine grounds of the three dhyānas are three pleasure-births. The gods there remain for a long time dwelling happily through pleasure and joy born of seclusion, through that born of samādhi, and through pleasure without joy. Because these births are without suffering and possess long-lasting happiness, they are called pleasure-births.

In the case of birth in the intermediate dhyāna, however, since joy and pleasure are absent, whether it is properly a pleasure-birth requires consideration.

### Philosophical Translation

The three desire-births classify not merely where beings are born but how their field of enjoyment is constituted. Desire can be directed toward objects already given, toward objects produced by oneself, or toward objects produced by another. Higher kāmadhātu therefore refines the production and control of desire-objects without leaving desire itself.

The three pleasure-births classify the nine grounds of the first three dhyānas according to the forms of sustained pleasure through which beings remain there for long periods. Here the classification shifts from desire-object production to the experiential quality of meditative world-birth.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — question, threefold analysis of `kāmopapatti`, and explanation of `sukhopapatti`.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu, cautious qualification** — `dhyānāntarotpattau ... vicāryam`, noting that the intermediate-dhyāna case requires examination because joy and pleasure are absent.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratyupasthita-kāma** — desire whose objects are already present
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** first mode of desire-birth

- **nirmita-kāma** — constructed desire / desire for self-produced objects
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** second mode of desire-birth

- **paranirmita-kāma** — desire for objects constructed by others
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** third mode of desire-birth

- **aiśvarya** — mastery / dominion
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** expresses control over the relevant desire-field

- **paribhoga** — enjoyment / use
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** explains the threefold distinction by mode of enjoyment

- **vivekaja-prītisukha** — joy and pleasure born of seclusion
  - **status:** doctrinal dhyāna vocabulary
  - **function:** basis of the first pleasure-birth

- **samādhija-prītisukha** — joy and pleasure born of samādhi
  - **status:** doctrinal dhyāna vocabulary
  - **function:** basis of the second pleasure-birth

- **niṣprītika-sukha** — pleasure without joy
  - **status:** doctrinal dhyāna vocabulary
  - **function:** basis of the third pleasure-birth

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya shows that `upapatti` here is classificatory rather than merely locative.

```text
kāmopapatti
    → classified by mode of desire-object enjoyment

sukhopapatti
    → classified by mode of sustained dhyāna pleasure
```

The three desire-births are:

```text
given objects
    → humans + four lower kāma-deva classes

self-constructed objects
    → Nirmāṇarati

other-constructed objects
    → Paranirmitavaśavartin
```

The three pleasure-births are the nine grounds of the first three dhyānas, grouped according to long-lasting pleasant dwelling.

The closing qualification is important: the intermediate-dhyāna case does not fit the definition without remainder, because the relevant joy and pleasure are absent there. Vasubandhu explicitly leaves that point for consideration rather than forcing the taxonomy.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be clarified.**

The first half should not suggest merely three geographical classes of rebirth. The Bhāṣya defines the three `kāmopapatti` by three modes of enjoying desire-objects.

The second half should likewise be read as three classes of pleasure-birth constituted by the nine grounds of the first three dhyānas.

## Logical Determination

```text
desire-field
    → given object
    → self-produced object
    → other-produced object

form-realm field
    → seclusion-born pleasure
    → samādhi-born pleasure
    → pleasure without joy
```

The ascent is therefore not simply spatial. The mode by which the experiential field is constituted changes.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

The verse distinguishes world-birth by the manner in which its object-field is constituted and enjoyed. In the kāma realm the decisive variation concerns the production of the desired object; in the first three dhyānas it concerns the quality of sustained pleasant dwelling. These remain determinations of conditioned world-experience, not liberation.
