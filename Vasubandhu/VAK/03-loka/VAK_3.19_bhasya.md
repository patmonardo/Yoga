# VAK_3.19_bhasya — Abhidharmakośabhāṣya on VAkK 3.19

## Kārikā

```iast
yathākṣepaṃ kramād vṛddhaḥ santānaḥ kleśakarmabhiḥ /
paralokaṃ punar yāti ity anādibhavacakrakam // VAkK_3.19 //
```

**Best translation:**

> According to its projection, the continuum gradually develops through afflictions and karmas; it goes again to another world. Thus is the beginningless wheel of becoming.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya completes the argument begun at VAkK 3.10.

### 1. The continuum develops according to its projection

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The subject remains the `santāna`, not an ātman. It develops `yathākṣepam`—according to the projection already discussed in the preceding verses.

```text
ākṣepa
    ↓
ordered development of santāna
```

The important point is that the continuum does not merely appear fully formed. It undergoes an ordered developmental process (`krama`).

### 2. The Bhāṣya explicitly supplies the developmental sequence

Vasubandhu asks:

```iast
kena krameṇa /
```

> By what sequence?

He then cites the traditional embryological series:

```text
kalala
    ↓
arbuda
    ↓
peśī
    ↓
ghana
    ↓
praśākhā
```

These are called the five `garbhāvasthāḥ`, the five embryonic stages.

The description is deliberately material and sequential. The rebirth process that had looked like a dramatic passage between worlds is now traced into ordered bodily development.

### 3. Birth does not terminate causal determination

When development reaches maturity, karmically resultant winds (`karmavipākajā vāyavaḥ`) bring about the orientation associated with birth.

The Bhāṣya then gives an unusually graphic account of fetal suffering, miscarriage, extraction, birth, handling of the newborn, feeding, and gradual habituation to coarse food.

The doctrinal purpose is not the graphic detail itself. The point is that the projected continuum is carried through a determinate embodied process.

```text
projection
    ↓
embryonic development
    ↓
birth or fetal death
    ↓
further karmically determined continuity
```

Even fetal death does not introduce a break in the causal order: the being is led toward another destiny through previously produced karma whose result is experienced in another occasion.

### 4. Mature embodiment regenerates the very causes of recurrence

The decisive sentence is:

```iast
tasya vṛddher anvayād indriyāṇāṃ paripākāt punar api kleśāḥ samudācaranti karmaṇi copacayaṃ gacchanti /
```

As the being grows and the faculties mature:

```text
indriya-paripāka
    ↓
kleśa again becomes active
    ↓
karma is accumulated
```

This is the internal engine of the wheel.

The cycle does not continue merely because an earlier karma keeps pushing forever. The matured life itself becomes a new field in which afflictions arise and new karma accumulates.

### 5. Death restarts the transition already analyzed

After bodily dissolution:

```iast
antarābhavasaṃtatyā pūrvavat paralokaṃ punar yāti
```

> Through the continuity of intermediate existence, as before, it goes again to another world.

The phrase `pūrvavat` is important: **as before**. VAkK 3.10–3.18 have already supplied the mechanism.

The whole block is therefore recursive:

```text
birth
    ↓
growth
    ↓
faculty maturation
    ↓
kleśa
    ↓
karma accumulation
    ↓
death
    ↓
antarābhava-saṃtati
    ↓
paraloka
    ↓
rebirth
```

This is what Vasubandhu names:

```iast
anādibhavacakrakam
```

> the beginningless wheel of becoming.

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

The continuum projected by karma develops gradually in an ordered sequence. In womb-birth it passes through the successive embryonic stages, matures, and is brought to birth through conditions themselves belonging to karmic maturation. Whether it dies before birth or is born successfully, the continuity remains causally determined.

As the embodied being develops and its faculties mature, afflictions arise again and karma is accumulated. At the breaking-up of the body, the continuum passes, as already explained, through intermediate existence and goes again to another world.

Thus birth produced by affliction and karma gives rise again to affliction and karma, and from these there is birth again. This is the beginningless wheel of becoming.

If one were to posit an absolute first beginning, the first occurrence would have to be without a cause. But causeless arising is inadmissible: observable arising is governed by determinate causal capacities and restrictions of place and time, and the theory of an eternal cause has already been rejected. Therefore saṃsāra has no beginning.

Its **end**, however, is intelligible, because birth depends upon causes. When its causes are exhausted, birth ceases, just as a sprout ceases to arise when the seed is exhausted.

---

## The Crucial Argument: Beginningless Does Not Mean Endless

This is the strongest philosophical result of the passage.

Vasubandhu distinguishes two questions:

```text
Does saṃsāra have a first beginning?
    No.

Can saṃsāra have an end?
    Yes.
```

Why no beginning?

Because a first member would lack the causal conditions which explain every member of the series.

```text
first birth
    with no prior kleśa-karma ground
        ↓
causeless arising
```

That is rejected.

But why can there be an end?

Because each birth is **dependent upon causes**:

```iast
hetvadhīnatvāj janmanaḥ
```

> because birth depends upon a cause.

Therefore:

```text
cause exhausted
    ↓
effect no longer arises
```

The seed/sprout analogy gives the model:

```text
seed exhausted
    → no sprout

kleśa-karma causes exhausted
    → no further birth
```

This is not symmetrical with the question of a beginning.

```text
Beginning:
    would require an uncaused first event.

End:
    requires only cessation of the causes.
```

That is a remarkably compact sufficient-ground argument.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — defines the recursive development of the continuum and argues for beginningless saṃsāra with a possible end.

**Voice: Āryas / cited tradition** — embryological verses giving the ordered stages of fetal development.

No independent Self-theorist voice appears in this unit; the no-self result of 3.18 remains the controlling background.

## New Technical Terms

- **garbhāvasthā** — embryonic stage.
  - **status:** newly explicit in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** divides womb-development into an ordered developmental sequence.

- **kalala / arbuda / peśī / ghana / praśākhā** — the five embryonic stages named by the cited tradition.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only terms in the present block.
  - **function:** material specification of `krama`, ordered development.

- **karmavipākaja-vāyu** — wind produced from karmic maturation.
  - **status:** newly explicit.
  - **function:** explains a bodily process of birth through karmic maturation rather than an external soul-agent.

- **indriya-paripāka** — maturation of the faculties.
  - **status:** familiar components, newly important as a compound process here.
  - **function:** condition under which kleśas again become active.

- **karma-upacaya** — accumulation/increase of karma.
  - **status:** newly explicit in this passage.
  - **function:** explains regeneration of karmic causal capacity during an embodied life.

- **anādi-bhava-cakraka** — beginningless wheel of becoming.
  - **status:** Kārikā term, fully explained here.
  - **function:** names the recursive causal structure `birth → kleśa/karma → birth`.

- **nirhetuka-prādurbhāva** — causeless arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative terminology.
  - **function:** rejected consequence of positing an absolute first beginning.

- **hetu-kṣaya** — exhaustion/destruction of the cause.
  - **status:** newly decisive in this passage.
  - **function:** explains how beginningless saṃsāra can nevertheless terminate.

- **hetvadhīnatva** — dependence upon a cause.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative terminology.
  - **function:** grounds the possibility of cessation.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAkK 3.19 closes the first major analysis of sentient continuity in Lokanirdeśa.

The complete structure is now:

```text
kleśa + karma
    ↓
ākṣepa
    ↓
antarābhava
    ↓
upapatti
    ↓
embryonic / embodied development
    ↓
indriya maturation
    ↓
renewed kleśa
    ↓
new karma accumulation
    ↓
death
    ↓
antarābhava again
```

The wheel is **self-reproducing causally**, but not self-subsistent metaphysically.

That distinction matters:

```text
self-reproducing causal process
    ≠
self-existing substance
```

The ātman remains unnecessary.

---

## Adhyāsa Control

The temptation after 3.19 is to turn the beginningless continuity into a metaphysical entity:

```text
beginningless continuity
    ↓
there must be some eternal bearer
```

That repeats the Self-theorist move rejected in 3.18.

The Bhāṣya gives sufficient grounds without such a bearer:

```text
birth has causes
those causes generate further causes
those generate another birth
```

The beginninglessness belongs to the **causal regress of conditioned events**, not to an eternal Self hidden behind them.

So the Dharma-clean rule remains:

```text
anādi
    does not imply nitya-ātman

continuity
    does not imply substance

recurrence
    does not imply a recurring identical subject
```

---

## Organon Contact

Kept deliberately light:

3.19 gives a remarkably clean model of a recursively reproduced conditioned system.

```text
Condition_t
    produces State_t

State_t
    regenerates Condition_t+1

Condition_t+1
    produces State_t+1
```

The system has no need for an external bearer to preserve identity across iterations.

But the decisive point is the asymmetry:

```text
No first beginning:
    because every arising requires sufficient ground.

Possible ending:
    because conditioned arising ceases when its ground is exhausted.
```

This is the point where the apparently Tamasic machinery becomes philosophically important. The wheel is not eternal by necessity. It is **beginningless because causality excludes an ungrounded first event, yet terminable because causality also makes every conditioned event dependent upon exhaustible grounds**.

That is the logical opening for the Path.

---

## Translation Consequence

The Kārikā's `anādibhavacakrakam` should not be rendered in a way suggesting a metaphysically eternal entity.

Preferred:

> **the beginningless wheel of becoming**

Avoid:

> eternal cycle of existence

because the Bhāṣya immediately insists that the cycle has no beginning but **can have an end** through `hetukṣaya`.

Likewise `santāna` remains **continuum / continuity-stream**, not an enduring subject.

---

## Working Summary

VAkK 3.19 closes the first Bhāṣya block by making the whole transition process recursive.

```text
ākṣepa
    → ordered growth
    → embodied maturation
    → renewed kleśa
    → karma accumulation
    → death
    → antarābhava
    → another world
    → rebirth
```

This is the `anādi-bhava-cakraka`.

Its beginninglessness follows from the rejection of causeless arising: a first birth without prior grounds would violate the causal structure used everywhere else.

Its cessation is nevertheless possible because birth is cause-dependent:

```text
hetukṣaya
    → janma-kṣaya
```

Just as exhaustion of seed prevents the sprout, exhaustion of the causes prevents another birth.

So the final result of `VAK_3a_bhasya` is not merely cosmological:

> **Saṃsāra is beginningless, but it is not necessary.**

Its sufficient grounds reproduce it; remove those grounds, and the wheel ends.
