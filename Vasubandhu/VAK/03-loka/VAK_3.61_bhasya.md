# VAK_3.61_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
prāvṛṇmāse dvitīye 'ntyanavamyāṃ vardhate niśā /
hemantānāṃ caturthe tu hīyate aharviparyayāt // VAkK_3.61 //
```

**Working translation:**

In the second month of the rains, on the ninth day of the latter fortnight, night begins to increase. In the fourth month of winter, on the corresponding ninth day, it decreases; day changes inversely.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.61.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

yadā rātrirhīyate tadā divaso vardhate /

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya removes the calendrical ambiguity left by the compressed Kārikā.

`prāvṛṇmāse dvitīye` is identified as the second month of the rains, **Bhādrapada**. `antyanavamyām` is then unpacked as the ninth day of the second fortnight.

The corresponding point of reversal occurs in the fourth winter month, **Phālguna**, again on the ninth day of the second fortnight.

Vasubandhu then makes `aharviparyayāt` fully explicit:

```text
when night increases
    day decreases

when night decreases
    day increases
```

The verse therefore describes reciprocal temporal magnitudes rather than two independent seasonal changes.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — calendrical specification and explanation of the reciprocal relation between night and day.

No opponent or alternative school position appears in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **Bhādrapada** — the specified second month of the rainy season
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** calendrical specification of `prāvṛṇmāse dvitīye`

- **pakṣa** — fortnight
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** resolves `antyanavamī` as the ninth day of the second fortnight

- **Phālguna** — the specified fourth month of winter
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** calendrical specification of the point at which night begins to decrease

- **viparyaya** — inverse relation / reversal
  - **status:** Kārikā term made explicit by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** expresses reciprocal increase and decrease of day and night

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya turns an abbreviated seasonal statement into a determinate calendrical rule. Temporal variation is fixed by named months, fortnight, and lunar day, then expressed as reciprocal alteration of two opposed magnitudes.

```text
Bhādrapada, second fortnight, ninth day
    → night increases
        → day decreases

Phālguna, second fortnight, ninth day
    → night decreases
        → day increases
```

This continues the bhājanaloka analysis of time as an articulated order belonging to the produced world.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The existing Kārikā file provisionally understood `antyanavamī` as a “final ninth day.” The Bhāṣya is more precise: it means the **ninth day of the second fortnight**.

The prose also identifies the months explicitly as Bhādrapada and Phālguna.

## Logical Determination

The central relation is reciprocal measure:

```text
niśā ↑  → ahaḥ ↓
niśā ↓  → ahaḥ ↑
```

`viparyaya` is therefore not merely chronological reversal. It expresses the inverse determination of two magnitudes within one day-night whole.

The Bhāṣya makes explicit what the Kārikā compresses:

```text
one temporal whole
    → opposed magnitudes
        → reciprocal variation
```

## Organon Contact Point

This remains a determination internal to produced Loka. The value for the Rational-Empirical field is that temporal appearances are not isolated facts: each is determined through its reciprocal relation to the other within a single measurable cycle.

The logical interest lies in relation, not in projecting a higher Principle into the calendrical material.
