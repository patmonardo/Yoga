# VAK_3.13 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ekākṣepād asāv aiṣyatpūrvakālabhavākṛtiḥ /
> sa punar maraṇāt pūrva upapattikṣaṇāt paraḥ //

**Best translation:**  
By a single projection, this intermediate being has the form of the coming prior-time existence. It is after death and before the moment of rebirth.

## Segmentation and Grammar

The Bhāṣya explains the two halves separately.

First, it asks what form (`ākṛti`) the intermediate existence takes when headed toward a given destiny. The governing explanation is:

```iast
yenaiva karmaṇā gatir ākṣipyate tenaivāntarābhavas tatprāptaye
```

The very karma by which the destiny is projected also projects the intermediate existence for attaining that destiny.

Second, Vasubandhu asks what `pūrvakālabhava` means. Here `bhava` is defined generically as the five appropriated aggregates (`pañcopādānaskandhāḥ`) and divided into four temporal modes.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

What form does the intermediate existence assume for one who is going to a particular destiny?

By the very karma by which the destiny is projected, the intermediate existence is also projected for the attainment of that destiny. Therefore, in whatever destiny one is to go, the intermediate existence has the form of the prior-time existence that is going to arise in that destiny.

An objection follows: if intermediate beings of the five destinies can arise in one womb, would a hell-intermediate being burn the womb? Vasubandhu replies that hell beings are not always aflame even in their prior-time existence; and even if the intermediate being were aflame, its subtle body is neither ordinarily visible nor tangible. Moreover, intermediate beings do not physically adhere to one another in the womb, and karmic obstruction prevents burning.

What, then, is `pūrvakālabhava`? `Bhava` in general is the five appropriated aggregates. It is divided fourfold. The intermediate existence is as already explained. Rebirth-existence is the moment of junction (`pratisandhikṣaṇa`) in the destinies. Excluding the death-moment, all existence after that is prior-time existence. The final moment is death-existence, after which the intermediate existence arises when rebirth is to occur among beings possessing form.

### Philosophical Translation

The intermediate existence is not an indeterminate residue between two lives. Its form is already governed by the same karmic projection that determines the coming destiny. It therefore bears, in advance, the configuration of the future life toward which the continuum is moving.

Vasubandhu then removes any temptation to posit a separate bearer of these phases: `bhava` is analyzed as the five appropriated aggregates and divided according to temporal function—rebirth, prior-time life, death, and intermediate existence.

## Course of the Argument

### Question or Problem

If `antarābhava` is a real transition-state, what determines its form?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

### Initial Determination

The same karma that projects the destiny projects the intermediate existence for reaching that destiny.

```text
karma
  ↓ one ākṣepa
future gati
  +
antarābhava shaped for that gati
```

The transition-state is therefore not neutral. It is already future-oriented.

### Objection

**Voice: Objector**

If intermediate beings of different destinies may be associated with a womb, would a hell-intermediate being not burn it?

### Reply / Resolution

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The objection assumes too crude a material model. Hell beings are not perpetually aflame; the intermediate body is subtle, and karmic obstruction prevents destructive contact. The doctrinal point is not abandoned merely because a literalized spatial picture produces an absurdity.

### Definition of `pūrvakālabhava`

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

`Bhava` is the five appropriated aggregates. The prose then discriminates four modes:

```text
upapattibhava
    moment of rebirth / junction

pūrvakālabhava
    subsequent life before the terminal death-moment

maraṇabhava
    final moment of that life

antarābhava
    transition after death toward another rebirth
```

### Result

The Bhāṣya converts a seemingly pictorial doctrine into a temporal and causal analysis of aggregate-continuity. The future form of the intermediate existence is grounded in karmic projection; the four `bhavas` are phases of the appropriated aggregates, not states of an enduring self.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratisandhikṣaṇa** — the junction-moment constituting `upapattibhava`
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** definition
- **karmapratibandha** — karmic obstruction preventing the imagined destructive interaction of intermediate bodies
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** resolution
- **pañcopādānaskandha** — the five appropriated aggregates used here to define `bhava` generally
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** definition

## Doctrinal Determination

The key doctrinal relation is explicit:

```text
one karma
    projects the gati
    and
    projects the antarābhava leading to it
```

Thus `ekākṣepa` should not be weakened into mere temporal succession. It is a causal projection linking transition-form and destination-form.

At the same time, `bhava` is not introduced as a substance. Its general basis is the five appropriated aggregates. The fourfold division is temporal-functional rather than substantial.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation correctly recognized future-oriented form, but the Bhāṣya makes `ekākṣepa` more precise: the one projection is specifically the same karma that projects both the destination and the intermediate state required to reach it.

A slightly sharper translation is therefore:

> Through one and the same projection, this [intermediate existence] has the form of the coming prior-time existence; it is after death and before the moment of rebirth.

## Logical Determination

```text
World as causally projected transition-form.
```

The sequence is now:

```text
karma
    ↓
projects destination
    ↓
projects matching intermediate form
    ↓
rebirth-junction
    ↓
prior-time aggregate existence
    ↓
death
```

The intermediate state is determined by the future without being identical with the future rebirth.

## Organon Contact Point

This is a strong Dharma-first result. The transition is intelligible because its form is generated through a determining relation, not because an individualized bearer persists unchanged.

```text
not:
    Self carries its identity into the future

but:
    one determining projection
        generates a transition-form
        ordered toward a resultant form
```

The later Karmanirdeśa will have to explain this `ākṣepa` genetically. Here Lokanirdeśa establishes that such a principle is already required by the structure of world-transition.
