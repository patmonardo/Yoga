### Source cautions

The source prints several compressed sandhi forms, including `saptāgninā`, `gate'dbhiḥ`, and `ṣaṭpañcāśattejaḥ`. They are preserved above rather than silently normalized.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first asks for the order in which the cosmic destructions occur.

The sequence begins with seven uninterrupted destructions by fire. After those seven fire-destructions comes one destruction by water.

This pattern continues until seven water-destructions have occurred. Since each water-destruction is preceded by seven fire-destructions, those seven completed groups account for:

```text
49 fire destructions
7 water destructions
```

After the seventh water-destruction, another complete set of seven fire-destructions occurs. One destruction by wind then follows.

Thus the full sequence is:

```text
56 fire destructions
7 water destructions
1 wind destruction
--------------------
64 destructions
```

Vasubandhu then asks why the sequence has this structure. His answer connects the durability of the receptacle-world with the condition of the beings inhabiting the corresponding realms. Just as the stability of beings' modes of embodiment differs according to differences in their samāpatti, so the stability of the receptacle-worlds differs correspondingly.

The count also explains the Prajñaptibhāṣya statement that the lifespan of the Śubhakṛtsna gods is sixty-four kalpas.

The chapter then closes with the well-known causal formula: dharmas arise from causes; the Tathāgata teaches those causes and also their cessation.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks the sequence and causal rationale, computes the totals, and reconciles the count with the Prajñaptibhāṣya.

**Voice: cited canonical/formulaic verse** — the final `ye dharmā hetuprabhavā...` stanza closes the chapter by restating conditioned arising and cessation.

## New Technical Terms

- **ṣaṭpañcāśat-tejaḥ-saṃvartanī** — fifty-six fire destructions
  - **status:** Bhāṣya arithmetic made explicit here
  - **function:** total count within the sixty-four-destruction sequence

- **sapta-ap-saṃvartanī** — seven water destructions
  - **status:** Bhāṣya arithmetic made explicit here
  - **function:** intermediate destruction count

- **ekā vāyu-saṃvartanī** — one wind destruction
  - **status:** Kārikā term numerically fixed by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** terminal destruction in the sixty-fourfold sequence

- **samāpatti-viśeṣa** — differentiation of samāpatti
  - **status:** important explanatory term in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** explains differential stability of beings and, correspondingly, receptacle-worlds

- **sthiti-viśeṣa** — differentiation in stability/duration
  - **status:** explanatory term
  - **function:** links differences in embodied existence to differences in world-container durability

## Doctrinal Determination

The Kārikā's compressed recurrence is resolved into an exact count:

```text
(7 fire + 1 water) × 7
    = 49 fire + 7 water

+ 7 fire
+ 1 wind

= 56 fire + 7 water + 1 wind
= 64 cosmic destructions
```

The sequence is not arbitrary. Vasubandhu correlates world-container durability with the differentiated stability of beings arising from differentiated samāpatti.

This remains entirely within conditioned Loka. Neither beings nor their receptacle-worlds possess independent permanence; their differing durations are conditioned determinations.

The final causal stanza is therefore an apt closure to the chapter: the immense cosmological machinery is still governed by conditioned arising and cessation.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The repository's provisional translation correctly recognized the general pattern, but the Bhāṣya fixes the arithmetic explicitly. A sharper translation is:

> Seven destructions occur by fire, then one by water. When this has proceeded until seven water-destructions have occurred, there is again a sevenfold series by fire; after that comes one destruction by wind.

The resulting total is exactly sixty-four destructions: fifty-six by fire, seven by water, and one by wind.

## Logical Determination

The closing structure of Loka-nirdeśa is counted conditioned recurrence:

```text
conditioned beings
    → differentiated samāpatti
    → differentiated stability of embodiment
    → corresponding stability of receptacle-worlds
    → ordered destruction
    → cessation
```

The decisive point is that differences in duration do not introduce an eternal substrate. They are differences within conditioned existence.

## Organon Contact Point

The chapter closes exactly where its governing discipline requires: **produced Loka is a lawful conditioned field**.

Its vast cycles, relative stabilities, and repeated destructions never acquire the status of self-subsisting Principles. The closing causal verse returns the whole cosmology to dependence upon causes and cessation.
