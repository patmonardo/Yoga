# VAK_3.11_bhasya — Uninterrupted Continuity and the Rejection of the Reflection Analogy

## Kārikā

> vrīhisantānasādharmyād avicchinnabhavodbhavaḥ /
> pratibimbam asiddhatvād asāmyāc cānidarśanam //

**Best translation:**  
Because of similarity to the continuity of rice, the arising of existence is uninterrupted. A reflected image is not a valid example, because it is unestablished and dissimilar.

## Segmentation by Natural Argument

### 1. Vasubandhu explicitly begins the proof from reasoning

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> `tatra tāvat yuktiṃ niśrityocyate`

“First, relying on reasoning, it is said.”

This is the promised **yukti** branch of the proof announced after VAK 3.10.

### 2. The positive analogy: rice-continuity

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Dharmas belonging to a continuity are observed to appear in another place **without interruption**, as in the continuity of rice. Therefore the same should hold for the continuity of a sentient being.

The decisive relation is not identity of substance but continuity of series:

```text
prior member
    → subsequent member
    → subsequent member

without:
    permanent bearer

without:
    absolute break
```

### 3. The objector proposes a discontinuous model

**Voice: Objector**

A discontinuous arising is also observed: a reflected image appears from an original image in a mirror. Therefore rebirth could arise from death without any intermediate continuity.

The proposal is structurally important:

```text
maraṇabhava
    -- gap -->
upapattibhava
```

with the mirror-image relation offered as its model.

### 4. Vasubandhu rejects the reflection analogy as unestablished

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The claim that a reflected image is a separately arisen `dharmāntara`, a distinct new dharma, is itself not established.

He then argues through the spatial and causal oddities of reflection: mirror-surface and reflected appearance, reflections in water, shadow and illumination, and the dependence of the appearance on a complex conjunction of conditions.

His striking conclusion is:

> `sāmagryāstu ... tathā darśanaṃ bhavati`

It is because of the efficacy of the causal complex that such an appearance occurs.

And:

> `acintyo hi dharmāṇāṃ śaktibhedaḥ`

“The differentiation of the powers of dharmas is indeed inconceivable.”

The point is not magical surrender. It is that the reflection does not establish the independent arising required by the opponent's analogy.

### 5. Even if granted, reflection is dissimilar

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The reflection is not a member of the same `santāna` as the original image. It is connected with the mirror-continuum and coexists with the original.

This differs from the proposed relation between death and rebirth.

The second defect is causal:

```text
reflection
    arises from two principal conditions:
        original image
        mirror

rebirth
    cannot be modeled as arising
    from death + an independent second principal cause
```

Thus the analogy fails both as a continuity-model and as a causal model.

### 6. Conclusion of the yukti proof

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

A rebirth-existence arising discontinuously from death-existence is rejected on rational grounds. Therefore intermediate existence must be admitted.

The Bhāṣya closes this unit before turning to the second branch of proof, **āgama**, authoritative teaching.

## Literal Prose Translation

First, relying on reasoning: dharmas that proceed in a continuity are seen to appear in other places without interruption, as in the continuity of rice. Therefore this continuity of a sentient being likewise should appear in other places without interruption.

An opponent may say that an appearance arising after a break is also observed, as when a reflection appears from an image in a mirror; rebirth could arise from death in the same way.

But a reflected image is not a valid example, because its supposed independent arising as another dharma is not established. Even if that were granted, the comparison would still fail through dissimilarity. The reflected image is not a continuation of the original image, and it arises through a different causal structure. Therefore discontinuous arising of rebirth from death is not established by reasoning. Hence an intermediate existence is admitted.

---

## Argument Reconstruction

The argument can be reconstructed as follows:

```text
1. A sentient sequence is a santāna.

2. Where a santāna manifests at another place,
   an uninterrupted serial relation is the appropriate model.

3. Rice-continuity exhibits this structure:
   later members arise from earlier members
   without a permanent substance
   and without an absolute break.

4. The opponent proposes reflection as a counterexample
   to uninterrupted serial manifestation.

5. Reflection fails:
   a. its status as a separately arisen dharma is unestablished;
   b. it is not in the same santāna as the original;
   c. it coexists with the original;
   d. it has a different causal structure.

6. Therefore reflection cannot prove discontinuous rebirth.

7. Thus rebirth must be connected to death by uninterrupted continuity.

8. Hence antarābhava remains rationally required.
```

The Bhāṣya is therefore not defending a traveling entity. It is defending **serial causal continuity against discontinuous appearance**.

## Voices

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the `yukti` proof and the rice-continuity analogy.
- **Voice: Objector** — proposes the reflection analogy as evidence for discontinuous arising.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — rejects the analogy as `asiddha` and `asama`, then concludes that `antarābhava` is required.

## New Technical Terms

- **yukti** — reasoning; rational proof
  - **status:** previously encountered in the broader tradition, newly made technically operative in this local Bhāṣya argument
  - **function:** logical distinction / proof method

- **santānavartin** — belonging to or proceeding within a continuity-series
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `santāna`
  - **function:** definition / argument

- **dharmāntara** — another or distinct dharma
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this argument
  - **function:** objection / ontological distinction

- **sāmagrī** — causal conjunction, complex of conditions
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical term
  - **function:** causal explanation

- **śaktibheda** — differentiation of powers or capacities of dharmas
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only emphasis here
  - **function:** causal consequence / explanatory caution

- **asantāna** — not belonging to the same continuity-series
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** refutation

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā's `avicchinnabhavodbhavaḥ` should be heard more strongly than merely “continuous rebirth.” The Bhāṣya specifically means:

```text
arising within an uninterrupted santāna
```

Likewise, `anidarśanam` is not simply “a bad metaphor.” It means that reflection fails as a **probative example** in the argument.

Recommended translation remains:

> “Because of similarity to the continuity of rice, the arising of existence is uninterrupted. A reflected image is not a valid example, because it is unestablished and dissimilar.”

---

## Doctrinal / Logical Determination

The important distinction is:

```text
continuity
    ≠ identity

continuity
    ≠ discontinuity
```

The Kośa needs a third structure:

```text
serial dependence without substantial persistence
```

That is what `santāna` supplies.

The rice analogy is therefore not merely biological imagery. It performs a formal task: it models how one determinate state can condition another without requiring one numerically identical bearer to pass through the whole series.

The reflection analogy fails because it models **appearance at a distance through another support**, not **generation within one causal series**.

---

## Organon Interpretation

This is an especially clean Dharma-first result.

The Bhāṣya refuses two naive models:

```text
Naive substantialism:
    the same self travels onward

Naive discontinuity:
    one life vanishes and another simply appears
```

Instead:

```text
Dharma-series
    → lawful continuity
    → transformed successor-state
```

So the truth does not belong to an individualized bearer standing behind the process. It belongs to the **lawful relation of the determinations themselves**.

This also sharpens the movement from Loka toward Karma. Once rebirth is understood as a continuous causal series rather than magical relocation, the question becomes unavoidable:

```text
What determines the successor-state of the series?
```

Chapter 3 is not yet answering that fully, but it is clearing the logical ground on which Chapter 4 can answer it.

---

## Summary

VAK 3.11 supplies the rational proof for continuity across death and rebirth.

Its structure is:

```text
rice-series
    valid model of serial continuity

reflection
    invalid model of discontinuous arising

therefore:
    bhava arises through uninterrupted santāna
    and antarābhava remains required
```

The significant philosophical result is **continuity without identity-substance**. Vasubandhu's rationalization of the rebirth spectacle begins by replacing the picture of a traveler with the logic of a causal Dharma-series.
