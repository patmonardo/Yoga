### Source Caution

The source prints several compounds continuously and has minor orthographic compression (`cakravarttin`, `cakrkāṇi`). These are preserved above rather than silently normalized.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu asks when a cakravartin arises and answers by fixing a temporal threshold. Cakravartins appear while human lifespan ranges from immeasurable down to eighty thousand years, but not below eighty thousand.

The Bhāṣya does not leave this as a bare calendrical rule. It gives a causal condition: beings whose lifespan has fallen below that threshold are no longer a suitable basis (`abhājana`) for the extraordinary abundance of crops and resources (`sasyasaṃpad`) associated with cakravartin sovereignty.

He then explains the name `cakravartin`: such kings characteristically make sovereignty proceed by means of the wheel (`rājyaṃ cakreṇa vartayitum`). The wheel is therefore constitutive of their mode of kingship, not merely an ornamental emblem.

Finally the class is divided fourfold according to wheel-material:

```text
suvarṇa  → gold
rūpya    → silver
tāmra    → copper
ayaḥ     → iron
```

These four are explicitly ranked from highest to lowest:

```text
gold   → uttama
silver → upottama
copper → madhyama
iron   → adhama
```

## Translation

> When do wheel-turning kings arise? The arising of a cakravartin does not occur below an eighty-thousand-year lifespan. Cakravartins arise while human lifespan ranges from immeasurable down to eighty thousand years, but not below that, because those whose lifespan is less are not a suitable basis for that abundance of crops. They are called cakravartins because it is their characteristic to make sovereignty proceed by means of the wheel. They are fourfold: possessors of wheels of gold, silver, copper, and iron. Of these, the first is highest, the second next-highest, the third middling, and the fourth lowest; thus they are arranged in descending order.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The passage is straightforward exposition. No distinct objection or rival doctrinal voice appears within the unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **cakravartin** — wheel-turning sovereign; a king whose rule proceeds by means of the wheel
  - **status:** previously encountered as a cosmological-historical figure; here explicitly defined
  - **function:** defines a class of universal monarch and explains its name

- **sasyasaṃpad** — abundance or prosperity of crops
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory term in this passage
  - **function:** gives the environmental/material condition correlated with cakravartin emergence

- **abhājana** — not a suitable receptacle, basis, or qualified support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** explains why beings below the eighty-thousand-year lifespan threshold cannot support the conditions of cakravartin rule

- **uttama / upottama / madhyama / adhama** — highest / next-highest / middling / lowest
  - **status:** classificatory vocabulary
  - **function:** ranks the four cakravartin types

## Doctrinal Determination

The important doctrinal point is not merely that cakravartins belong to very long-lived humanity. Their appearance depends upon a conjunction of temporal and environmental conditions.

```text
human lifespan ≥ 80,000 years
    + suitable material abundance
        → possible cakravartin arising

human lifespan < 80,000 years
    → beings are abhājana for that sasyasaṃpad
    → no cakravartin arising
```

The fourfold wheel hierarchy is then a hierarchy of sovereign types rather than four unrelated royal symbols.

```text
gold-wheel cakravartin
    → highest

silver-wheel
    → next-highest

copper-wheel
    → middling

iron-wheel
    → lowest
```

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā translation is substantially correct, but the Bhāṣya adds two points that should remain attached to it:

1. the eighty-thousand-year threshold is explained by the loss of suitability for the extraordinary `sasyasaṃpad` required by cakravartin conditions;
2. `adhara-kramāt` is an explicit rank-order: gold highest, silver next-highest, copper middling, iron lowest.

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a conditioned classification:

```text
temporal condition
    → material/environmental suitability
        → possibility of sovereign appearance
            → four ranked forms
```

The cakravartin is therefore not produced merely by the passage of cosmic time. A determinate world-condition must be present. This remains entirely within produced Loka: a historically conditioned sovereignty arising within a suitable world-state.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
A world-historical form appears only when its determinate conditions are adequate to it.
```

The useful point is conditionality, not an attempt to turn the cakravartin into a transcendental principle. The Bhāṣya remains a description of a produced world-order.
