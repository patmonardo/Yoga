# VAK_3.81_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ārūpye viṃśatiḥ kalpasahasrāṇy adhikādhikam /
mahākalpaḥ parīttābhāt prabhṛty ardham adhas tataḥ // VAkK_3.81 //
```

**Working translation:** In the formless realm, lifespans are twenty thousand kalpas and successively twenty thousand more. Beginning with Parīttābha, the kalpa is a mahākalpa; below that, it is half [a mahākalpa].

### Source Caution

The repository Kārikā file records a difficult transmitted reading around `prabhṛtyadharmadhastataḥ`. The Bhāṣya removes the ambiguity: `parīttābhāt prabhṛti` means beginning with the Parīttābha deva class, and `ardham adhas tataḥ` means that below that level half a mahākalpa is conventionally treated as one kalpa for lifespan calculation.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first makes the arūpya sequence explicit:

```text
Ākāśānantyāyatana   → 20,000 kalpas
Vijñānānantyāyatana → 40,000 kalpas
Ākiñcanyāyatana     → 60,000 kalpas
Bhavāgra             → 80,000 kalpas
```

The phrase `adhikādhikam` therefore means a successive addition of twenty thousand kalpas at each higher arūpya station.

Vasubandhu then asks what kind of `kalpa` is intended in these lifespan calculations: an antarakalpa, a saṃvartakalpa, a vivartakalpa, or a mahākalpa.

The answer introduces a boundary at Parīttābha:

```text
Parīttābha and above
    → lifespan counted in full mahākalpas

below Parīttābha
    → half a mahākalpa is conventionally counted as one kalpa
```

He demonstrates the lower convention through Mahābrahmā. The world:

- expands (`vivartate`) for twenty antarakalpas,
- remains expanded (`vivarta āste`) for twenty antarakalpas,
- contracts (`saṃvartate`) for twenty antarakalpas.

Those sixty antarakalpas are called Mahābrahmā's one-and-a-half kalpas. Hence one such lower-realm `kalpa` equals forty antarakalpas, i.e. half a mahākalpa.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is direct explanatory analysis, including a question raised and immediately resolved by the commentator.

## New Technical Terms

- **ākāśānantyāyatana** — sphere of infinite space
  - **status:** arūpya realm-name newly operative in this lifespan sequence
  - **function:** first arūpya lifespan station; 20,000 kalpas

- **vijñānānantyāyatana** — sphere of infinite knowledge
  - **status:** arūpya realm-name
  - **function:** second arūpya lifespan station; 40,000 kalpas

- **ākiñcanyāyatana** — sphere of nothingness
  - **status:** arūpya realm-name
  - **function:** third arūpya lifespan station; 60,000 kalpas

- **bhavāgra** — summit of existence
  - **status:** arūpya technical designation
  - **function:** highest arūpya lifespan station; 80,000 kalpas

- **antarakalpa** — intermediate kalpa
  - **status:** newly required temporal subdivision
  - **function:** unit used to explain the conventional lower-realm kalpa

- **mahākalpa** — great kalpa
  - **status:** introduced in the Kārikā and now defined operationally
  - **function:** full lifespan unit from Parīttābha upward; twice the lower conventional kalpa

- **kalpīkṛtya** — making/counting something as a kalpa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory verb here
  - **function:** signals that the lower `kalpa` is a stipulated measure equal to half a mahākalpa

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive point is that `kalpa` is not univocal across the lifespan table. The same word is used with two magnitudes according to cosmological level.

```text
Parīttābha boundary
    → full mahākalpa measure above
    → half-mahākalpa convention below
```

The arūpya lifespans are consequently immense but still strictly quantified. Even `bhavāgra`, the summit of existence, remains a conditioned birth-field with a determinate lifespan of 80,000 kalpas.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Substantive clarification.**

The repository's provisional second-half translation should not be read merely as introducing a vague mahākalpa measure “from Parīttābha and below.” The Bhāṣya gives the exact contrast:

> Beginning with Parīttābha, [lifespan] is to be understood in mahākalpas; below that, half a mahākalpa is made into the kalpa-unit.

The first half is likewise fully distributive: 20,000, 40,000, 60,000, and 80,000 kalpas for the four arūpya stations respectively.

## Logical Determination

The passage distinguishes **numerical series** from **unit determination**.

```text
first:
realm sequence
    → 20k → 40k → 60k → 80k

then:
what counts as "one kalpa"?
    → full mahākalpa above the Parīttābha boundary
    → half mahākalpa below it
```

Thus a quantity cannot be interpreted without determining the unit in which it is counted.

## Organon Contact Point

Very light contact only: the Kośa here is unusually precise about **measure as quantity together with its unit**. The numerical predicate alone is insufficient until the relevant `kalpa` is determined. This remains entirely within produced Loka and its temporal articulation.
