# VAK_3.08_bhasya — Catuṣkoṭi of Station and the Four Yonis

## Kārikā

> catasro yonayas tatra sattvānām aṇḍajādayaḥ //

**Best translation:**  
Knowledge itself is not declared to be a station. But when their inclusion is examined, the relation is fourfold. Within the three-domain world, beings have four modes of birth, beginning with the egg-born.

## Segmentation by Natural Argument

### 1. Why vijñāna is not itself a sthiti

The Bhāṣya invokes the field-and-seed relation. The Buddha taught the four `vijñānasthitis` under the aspect of **field (`kṣetra`)**, whereas vijñāna together with appropriation is taken under the aspect of **seed (`bīja`)**. A seed is not established as the field for itself.

The decisive relation is therefore:

```text
four contaminated aggregates
    = field/support for vijñāna

vijñāna
    = seed relative to those supports

therefore:
    vijñāna is not counted as its own sthiti
```

The prose sharpens this further: the dharmas that coexist with vijñāna are especially suitable to function as its `kṣetra`; therefore these, rather than vijñāna itself, are called its stations.

### 2. The seven and the four do not simply contain one another

A question then arises: how are the seven `vijñānasthitis` related to the four aggregate-stations?

The Bhāṣya rejects simple equivalence in either direction:

```text
seven do not simply include the four
four do not simply include the seven
```

The relation must instead be determined by a `catuṣkoṭi`.

### 3. Catuṣkoṭi as formal inclusion analysis

The first three corners are stated explicitly:

1. **Included in the seven but not in the four:** vijñāna occurring in the seven vijñānasthitis.
2. **Included in the four but not in the seven:** the non-vijñāna aggregates in the bad destinies, fourth dhyāna, and bhavāgra.
3. **Included in both:** the four aggregates occurring in the seven.
4. **Included in neither:** the remaining cases after excluding the preceding forms (`etān ākārān sthāpayitvā`).

Thus the `catuṣkoṭi` here is not a metaphysical tetralemma. It is a rigorously controlled four-way relation of extension between two classificatory sets.

### 4. Transition from station to generation

Only after the inclusion problem is settled does Vasubandhu return to the three-domain world already differentiated by gati and other distinctions, and introduce the four `yoni`.

### 5. Definition of yoni

The four are:

```text
aṇḍajā       egg-born
jarāyujā      womb-born
saṃsvedajā    moisture-born
upapādukā     spontaneously arisen
```

The Bhāṣya gives the compact definition:

```iast
yonir nāma jātiḥ
```

`Yoni` is a **mode/kind of birth (`jāti`)**.

It then explains the term through commonality of generation: beings are grouped together in a yoni because of similarity in the manner of birth (`prasavasāmya`).

### 6. The four birth modes

- `aṇḍaja`: beings born from eggs.
- `jarāyuja`: beings born from a womb/chorionic enclosure.
- `saṃsvedaja`: beings born from moisture.
- `upapāduka`: beings who arise at once complete, with unimpaired faculties and all major and minor limbs.

The last category includes devas, hell-beings, and intermediate-existence beings.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

Knowledge is not declared to be a station.

Moreover, the Blessed One taught four stations of knowledge under the aspect of field, while knowledge together with appropriation, taken as a whole, was taught under the aspect of seed. He therefore did not establish the seed itself as the field of the seed. The dharmas that coexist with knowledge are more suitable to function as its field; therefore precisely these are said to be its stations.

How, then, are the four stations of knowledge included by the seven stations of knowledge, or the seven by the four? The four are not simply included by the seven, nor the seven by the four.

Rather, with respect to inclusion there is a four-cornered analysis. When inclusion is examined, it should be understood fourfold: something may be included by the seven but not by the four, and so forth. The first corner is whatever vijñāna occurs among the seven. The second is the aggregates excluding vijñāna in the bad destinies, the fourth dhyāna, and the peak of existence. The third is the four aggregates among the seven. The fourth is what remains after setting aside these forms.

This three-domain world, differentiated by destiny and the other distinctions already stated, is also to be understood through four modes of birth of beings, beginning with the egg-born.

The modes of birth are egg-born, womb-born, moisture-born, and spontaneously arisen. `Yoni` means a kind of birth. Beings are grouped in it because of similarity in generation.

Egg-born beings are those born from eggs, such as geese, cranes, cakravāka birds, peacocks, parrots, mynahs, and others. Womb-born beings are those born from a womb, such as elephants, horses, cattle, buffalo, donkeys, boars, and others. Moisture-born beings are those born from the moisture of the earth, such as worms, insects, moths, mosquitoes, and others. Spontaneously arisen beings are those who arise all at once, complete and unimpaired in their faculties and furnished with all major and minor limbs. Devas, hell-beings, beings in intermediate existence, and others are examples.

### Philosophical Translation

Vijñāna is not one more support alongside the aggregates that condition it. The Bhāṣya instead distinguishes the conditioned knowing from the field in which it becomes established: the coexisting aggregates function as its field, while vijñāna functions as seed. The two classificatory systems—the seven knowledge-stations and four aggregate-stations—therefore overlap without coinciding, and their relation must be determined through a fourfold inclusion analysis.

Once that formal relation is settled, the analysis advances from **where conditioned knowing can stand** to **how sentient continuity arises into the world-field**. The four yonis are not four substances or four species of self. They are four lawful forms of generation distinguished by similarity in the manner of birth.

## Course of the Argument

### Question or Problem

Why is vijñāna itself not counted as a `sthiti`, and how do the seven vijñānasthitis relate to the four aggregate-stations?

### Initial Determination

The four aggregates are treated as `kṣetra`, field, while vijñāna is treated as `bīja`, seed. Therefore vijñāna is not its own station.

### Formal Problem

The seven and four classifications overlap but neither contains the other without remainder.

### Resolution

A fourfold inclusion analysis (`catuṣkoṭi`) determines their extensions.

### Transition

With the station/support relation completed, the text reopens the three-domain world under another determination: mode of generation (`yoni`).

### Result

```text
sthiti
    determines support/establishment

saṃgraha-catuṣkoṭi
    determines formal extension between classifications

yoni
    determines mode of arising
```

## New Technical Terms

- **kṣetrabhāva** — functioning as a field for vijñāna
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** logical distinction / explanation

- **bījabhāva** — functioning as seed
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** logical distinction / explanation

- **sahavartin** — coexisting/concurrent with vijñāna
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** definition of the dharmas suited to function as its field

- **saṃgraha** — inclusion or classificatory containment
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā but technically unfolded only in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** logical distinction

- **prasavasāmya** — similarity/commonality in the manner of generation
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** definition of yoni

- **avikala / ahīnendriya / sarvāṅgapratyaṅgopeta** — complete, with unimpaired faculties and all major/minor limbs
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** definition of spontaneous arising

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya prevents two simplifications.

First, `vijñānasthiti` cannot mean that vijñāna is a self-sufficient standing entity. Vijñāna is established relative to other dharmas that function as its field. The classification is relational from the outset.

Second, `yoni` is not an ontology of four kinds of substantial beings. Vasubandhu defines it through `jāti` and `prasavasāmya`: it is a classification according to the mode in which a sentient continuum is generated.

The formal order is therefore:

```text
conditioned knowing
    requires field/support

classifications of support
    require exact inclusion relations

sentient existence
    requires determinate mode of generation
```

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass rendering of `catuṣkoṭi tu saṃgrahe` as a generic “fourfold classification” is too weak. The Bhāṣya explicitly makes `saṃgraha` an **inclusion relation between the seven and four sets**, and `catuṣkoṭi` the four possible extensional relations.

Recommended translation:

> Knowledge itself is not declared to be a station. But when their inclusion is examined, the relation is fourfold. Within that three-domain world, beings have four modes of birth, beginning with the egg-born.

For `yoni`, “mode of birth” is now preferable to the looser “generative matrix” in the primary translation because the Bhāṣya explicitly glosses `yoni` as `jāti` and explains it through similarity of generation.

## Logical Determination

VAK 3.08 gives two linked formal determinations:

```text
1. World as relational field of knowing
2. World as determinate generation
```

The first is especially important. Vijñāna does not individualize itself into truth merely by being “this vijñāna.” Its determination comes through relation to the coexisting dharmas that provide its field.

Thus:

```text
individual vijñāna abstracted from field
    = incomplete determination

vijñāna-in-relation-to-kṣetra
    = doctrinally intelligible determination
```

The `catuṣkoṭi` then formalizes how two different lawful descriptions of this field intersect without being reducible to one another.

After that, `yoni` introduces genesis:

```text
being somewhere
    ↓
knowing standing somewhere
    ↓
arising into that field in a determinate way
```

## Organon Contact Point

This is an unusually clean Dharma-first passage.

The individual is not the first truth of the analysis. The first truth is the **relation that determines it**:

```text
bīja ↔ kṣetra
vijñāna ↔ coexisting aggregates
classification ↔ inclusion relation
sattva ↔ mode of arising
```

So the negative truth of isolated individualization can now be stated more precisely: abstraction of the individual from the determining field removes the very relations through which it is intelligible.

Positive determination begins only when the singular is returned to its Dharma-relations.

The move to `yoni` then adds genesis without introducing a substantial bearer:

```text
not:
    a self enters a world

but:
    a conditioned continuum arises
    through a determinate mode
    within an already articulated Dharma-field
```

That is a principled theory of generation rather than a naive theory of creature-types.
