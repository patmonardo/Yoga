# VAK_3.62_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
lavaśo rātryaharvṛddhī dakṣiṇottarage ravau /
svacchāyayārkasāmīpyādvikalendusamīkṣaṇam // VAkK_3.62 //
```

**Working translation:**

The increases of night and day occur lava by lava as the sun moves south and north. The moon is seen as incomplete because of shadow arising in proximity to the sun.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first asks by how much night and day increase. The answer is `lavaśaḥ`: increment by increment, one lava at a time.

The direction of solar motion then determines which magnitude increases:

```text
sun moves toward the southern side of Jambūdvīpa
    → night increases

sun moves toward the northern side
    → day increases
```

The prose then turns to the moon. At the beginning of the bright fortnight the lunar disk is seen as incomplete, and the text asks for the cause.

The principal explanation is attributed to the Prajñapti tradition: when the lunar vimāna moves near the solar vimāna, solar rays fall upon it; a shadow then falls on the opposite side and makes the lunar disk appear incomplete.

A second explanation is attributed to the earlier teachers: the configuration of motion itself is such that at times only half of the lunar vimāna is visible.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — questions concerning the amount of day/night increase and the cause of the moon's incomplete appearance.
- **Voice: Prajñapti position** — `prājñaptiko nirdeśaḥ`, the explanation by solar rays and shadow.
- **Voice: earlier teachers** — `pūrvācāryāḥ`, the alternative explanation by the configuration of the lunar vehicle's motion.

## New Technical Terms

- **lava** — a minute temporal increment
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly explained in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** measure of gradual increase of night or day

- **bhāskara** — sun
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory synonym
  - **function:** subject whose north/south motion conditions temporal variation

- **śuklapakṣa** — bright fortnight
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** calendrical location of the moon's initially incomplete appearance

- **maṇḍala** — disk
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** the visible lunar disk whose completeness is at issue

- **prājñaptika nirdeśa** — Prajñapti explanation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya school/source attribution
  - **function:** attributes the lunar-phase explanation to a specific doctrinal tradition

- **vāhayoga** — configuration/arrangement of motion or conveyance
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here
  - **function:** basis of the alternative account attributed to earlier teachers

- **pūrvācārya** — earlier teacher
  - **status:** Bhāṣya voice marker
  - **function:** source attribution for an alternative explanation

## Doctrinal Determination

The first half of the passage makes the temporal relation fully determinate: day and night do not change abruptly but by measured increments, and the direction of the sun's course specifies which increases.

The second half is explicitly doxographical. The Bhāṣya does not simply give one uncontested explanation of lunar phases. It records:

```text
Prajñapti account
    solar proximity
        → solar rays
            → shadow on opposite side
                → incomplete lunar disk

Earlier-teacher account
    configuration of lunar motion
        → only part of the disk visible
```

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised slightly.**

The existing Kārikā file renders `svacchāyayā` as "by its own shadow." The Bhāṣya's explanation is more specific: solar rays strike the lunar vimāna and a shadow falls on the opposite side. The prose therefore does not strongly support reading `sva-chāyā` as a simple intrinsic shadow belonging independently to the moon.

A better working translation is:

> Night and day increase lava by lava as the sun moves south and north; because of shadow in proximity to the sun, the moon is seen as incomplete.

The Bhāṣya also removes any ambiguity about the directional relation: southward solar motion increases night; northward motion increases day.

## Logical Determination

```text
measured motion
    → incremental temporal differentiation

relational celestial position
    → conditioned visible appearance
```

The lunar discussion is especially reflective because Vasubandhu presents competing explanations for one appearance rather than merely repeating the appearance itself.

## Organon Contact Point

The useful point is modest but precise: a visible determination is not self-explanatory. The same appearance can be mediated through competing causal accounts, and the Bhāṣya preserves that difference rather than collapsing appearance into its first explanation.
