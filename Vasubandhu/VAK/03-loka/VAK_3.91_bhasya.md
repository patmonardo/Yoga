# VAK_3.91_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
antaḥkalpo 'mitād yāvad daśavarṣāyuṣas tataḥ /
utkarṣā apakarṣāś ca kalpā aṣṭādaśāpare // VAkK_3.91 //
```

**Working translation:** An intermediate kalpa runs from immeasurable lifespan down to a ten-year lifespan. Thereafter, the other eighteen intermediate kalpas consist of increase and decrease.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_3.91.md`.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya supplies the complete structure only compressed in the Kārikā.

During the formation of the world, nineteen intermediate kalpas pass while humans still possess immeasurable lifespan. Once the formed world is standing, the first intermediate kalpa is the gradual decline of that human lifespan from immeasurable down to ten years.

Thereafter come eighteen intermediate kalpas, each containing both movements:

```text
10 years
    → utkarṣa
    → 80,000 years
    → apakarṣa
    → 10 years
```

This pattern repeats through the eighteenth such increase-and-decrease cycle.

A twentieth intermediate kalpa then consists only of increase:

```text
10 years
    → 80,000 years
```

The upper limit is fixed: human lifespan does not increase beyond eighty thousand years in these cycles.

Vasubandhu finally removes a possible numerical misunderstanding. The first and twentieth intermediate kalpas contain only one directional movement, whereas the middle eighteen contain both increase and decline, yet all twenty intermediate kalpas are equal in duration. The slower first decline and final increase occupy the same time as an entire middle increase-and-decrease cycle.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is straightforward exposition of the Vaibhāṣika kalpa chronology, with no explicit objector introduced.

## New Technical Terms

- **utkarṣa** — increase of human lifespan from ten years toward eighty thousand years
  - **status:** introduced in the Kārikā and precisely specified in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** defines the ascending phase of an intermediate kalpa

- **apakarṣa** — decrease of human lifespan from eighty thousand years toward ten years
  - **status:** introduced in the Kārikā and precisely specified in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** defines the descending phase of an intermediate kalpa

- **samānakāla** — equal in duration
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification in this passage
  - **function:** establishes temporal equivalence among structurally different intermediate kalpas

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a twentyfold structure for the standing world:

```text
1st antarakalpa
    immeasurable → 10

2nd–19th antarakalpas
    10 → 80,000 → 10
    repeated eighteen times

20th antarakalpa
    10 → 80,000
```

The extrema are therefore asymmetrical in form but equal in duration to the middle cycles.

The number 80,000 is the upper bound of the ordinary utkarṣa phases. Human lifespan does not rise beyond it in these antarakalpas.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The Kārikā's `aṣṭādaśāpare` refers specifically to eighteen intermediate kalpas each containing both increase and decrease. The Bhāṣya further establishes a twentieth kalpa consisting only of increase, which the Kārikā itself does not fully state until the continuation immediately following it.

A more precise working sense is:

> The first intermediate kalpa is the decline from immeasurable lifespan to ten years. The next eighteen each contain an increase and a decline.

The complete twentyfold structure requires the Bhāṣya continuation.

## Logical Determination

The important formal distinction is between **shape** and **duration**.

```text
first cycle
    one-way decline

middle cycles
    increase + decline

last cycle
    one-way increase
```

Yet:

```text
all twenty
    → samānakāla
    → equal duration
```

Thus equal temporal measure does not require identical internal articulation.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
A determinate temporal whole can preserve equality of measure through differently articulated internal processes.
```

This remains entirely within produced Loka. The oscillation of lifespan is a conditioned world-process, not a genetic principle.
