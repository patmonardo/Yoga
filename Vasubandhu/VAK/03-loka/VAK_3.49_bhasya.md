## Natural Argument

The first part completes the concentric mountain system. Nimindhara is the outermost of the seven mountains surrounding Sumeru. Outside it lie the four continents, and beyond those the iron Cakravāḍa that encloses the four-continent world.

The Bhāṣya then asks a genuinely genetic question: how do these mountains and continents arise? Waters containing many kinds of seeds are churned by winds of differing powers. These conditions produce heterogeneous effects.

This leads to an explicit polemic against Sāṃkhya `pariṇāma`. Vasubandhu rejects transformation understood as the disappearance of one determination and appearance of another in an enduring substance. His objection is that no such abiding `dharmī` is found. Even the reply that transformation is merely the same substance becoming otherwise is rejected as incoherent if it requires saying that the same thing both is and is not that thing in the relevant respect.

Vasubandhu's positive model is causal production through conditions: differentiated products arise when the relevant causes and conditions converge. Gold and the other materials are gathered by winds impelled through karmic efficacy and become mountains and continents.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — framing and principal critique.
- **Voice: Sāṃkhya position** — enduring substance undergoing change of determinations.
- **Voice: defender of the Sāṃkhya formulation** — `kaścaivamāha dharmebhyo'nyo dharmī` and `tasyaiva tu dravyasya...`.

## New Technical Terms

- **prākāra** — enclosing wall / rampart
  - **function:** describes the surrounding mountain structure

- **pariṇāma** — transformation
  - **status:** central polemical term
  - **function:** distinguishes Buddhist causal production from Sāṃkhya transformation

- **avasthita-dravya** — enduring substance
  - **function:** substrate presupposed in the Sāṃkhya account as presented here

- **dharmī** — bearer of determinations
  - **function:** target of Vasubandhu's critique

- **pratyayībhavati** — becomes a condition
  - **function:** states the alternative causal logic of heterogeneous production

- **karmaprabhāva** — efficacy / power of karma
  - **function:** explains the winds that gather produced materials into mountains and continents

## Doctrinal Determination

This is the philosophically decisive verse of the immediate cosmological sequence. The Bhāṣya does not deny production or transformation in an ordinary sense; it denies that world-genesis requires an enduring substrate bearing successive determinations.

```text
Sāṃkhya model as presented
    abiding dravya/dharmī
        → one determination ceases
        → another appears

Vasubandhu's preferred model
    heterogeneous causes and conditions
        → differentiated effects arise
        → no separate enduring bearer required
```

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The verse itself remains geographical, but its Bhāṣya opens a major doctrinal dispute about the nature of production.

## Logical Determination

The world-system is produced through conditioned genesis rather than through alteration of an invariant empirical substrate.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage should be preserved for later comparison with Sāṃkhya. The immediate lesson is negative and precise: Loka is produced and may develop, but it is not itself a subtle genetic Power, nor does its production require treating it as an enduring substance whose accidents change.
