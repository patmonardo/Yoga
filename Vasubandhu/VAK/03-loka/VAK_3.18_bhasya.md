# VAK_3.18_bhasya — Abhidharmakośabhāṣya on VAkK 3.18

## Kārikā

```iast
nātmāsti skandhamātraṃ tu kleśakarmābhisaṃskṛtam /
antarābhavasaṃtatyā kukṣim eti pradīpavat //
```

**Best translation:**

> There is no self. Rather, there are only the aggregates, conditioned by affliction and karma. Through the continuity of intermediate existence, they enter the womb like a flame.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_3.18.md`](./VAK_3.18.md)

---

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya takes up an externalist inference from the preceding account of an intermediate being moving from one existence to another. The opponent concludes that such continuity requires an ātman. Vasubandhu rejects that inference and explains rebirth entirely through conditioned aggregate-continuity.

Central Bhāṣya determination:

```iast
nātmāsti / skandhamātraṃ tu kleśakarmābhisaṃskṛtam /
```

The argument then explains how the aggregate-series continues through `antarābhava-saṃtati` and enters a new womb without positing a numerically identical self-substance.

---

## Segmentation by Natural Argument

### 1. The Self-theorist inference

The preceding discussion described a being that:

- dies,
- enters intermediate existence,
- moves toward a fixed destination,
- and enters a new birth.

An externalist now infers:

```text
continuity across births
    therefore
there must be a persisting ātman
```

**Voice:** Objector

This inference treats the continuity-function as requiring a separate identical bearer.

### 2. Vasubandhu's categorical rejection

```iast
nātmāsti
```

> There is no ātman.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is not merely a denial appended to the cosmology. It is the explicit logical control on the whole `antarābhava` analysis.

### 3. Positive replacement: `skandhamātra`

```iast
skandhamātraṃ tu
```

> Rather, there are only the aggregates.

The `tu` is decisive. Vasubandhu does not replace the ātman with nothing. He replaces it with the conditioned aggregate-series already analyzed throughout the chapter.

```text
not:
    ātman + aggregates

but:
    aggregates only
```

### 4. The aggregate-series is itself determined

```iast
kleśakarmābhisaṃskṛtam
```

The skandha-complex is shaped by:

- `kleśa` — affliction,
- `karma` — karmic action/conditioning.

So the continuity is not an unexplained stream. It is a causally formed series.

### 5. Transition through intermediate continuity

```iast
antarābhavasaṃtatyā kukṣim eti
```

The conditioned aggregate-series reaches the womb through the continuity of the intermediate existence.

No extra entity is inserted at the transition locus.

### 6. Flame analogy

```iast
pradīpavat
```

The flame analogy preserves:

```text
causal continuity
without numerical identity
```

The later flame depends on the earlier flame, but a permanent identical substance does not travel from one locus to another.

This is exactly the structure required for rebirth without ātman.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

An externalist might object: if a being leaves one existence, traverses an intermediate existence, and enters another birth, then surely there must be a self that goes from here to there.

That does not follow. There is no ātman. There are only the aggregates, formed and conditioned by affliction and karma. Through the continuity of intermediate existence, this aggregate-series reaches the womb, like the continuity of a flame.

---

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Opponent:
    A persisting transition requires a persisting bearer.

Vasubandhu:
    No.

    What persists is not a substance,
    but causal continuity.

    skandha-series
        conditioned by kleśa + karma
        ↓
    antarābhava-saṃtati
        ↓
    new birth

Therefore:
    continuity does not entail ātman.
```

This directly answers the continuity problem developed in 3.10–3.17.

The whole sequence can now be read retrospectively:

```text
gati
    resultant destination

yoni
    mode of generation

antarābhava
    transition-state

ākṣepa
    projection toward destination

viparyaya + desire
    subjective orientation

garbhāvakrānti
    entry into birth

skandhamātra
    what actually continues
```

The final term excludes the false surplus:

```text
ātman
    explanatory addition not required by the field
```

---

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Objector**
- continuity from death through intermediate existence to rebirth appears to require a self.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**
- `nātmāsti`: no ātman exists at this locus.
- the continuity is `skandhamātra`, only the aggregate-series.
- that series is shaped by kleśa and karma and continues through intermediate existence.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **skandhamātra** — aggregate-only; nothing over and above the conditioned aggregate-series
  - **status:** technically sharpened here
  - **function:** doctrinal replacement for the posited ātman

- **kleśakarmābhisaṃskṛta** — formed/conditioned by affliction and karma
  - **status:** prose-theoretical determination made explicit by the verse and Bhāṣya
  - **function:** causal explanation of the aggregate-series

- **antarābhava-saṃtati** — continuity of intermediate existence
  - **status:** developed across the preceding mini-block
  - **function:** continuity relation connecting death and new birth

- **pradīpavat** — like a flame
  - **status:** analogy introduced here
  - **function:** illustrates continuity without a numerically identical substance

---

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The key doctrinal point is not merely `anātman` in abstraction. It is a more exact claim:

```text
The transition-function has already been fully analyzed
without requiring an ātman.
```

The Self-theorist commits an explanatory overreach:

```text
observed / inferred continuity
    ↓
adds
    hidden identical bearer
```

But the Bhāṣya gives a sufficient determination:

```text
kleśa
    +
karma
    ↓
skandha-series
    ↓
antarābhava continuity
    ↓
new birth
```

Thus the ātman contributes no additional explanatory determination.

---

## Translation Consequences

### 1. `skandhamātra` must remain strong

Do not weaken it into “a collection of aggregates.” The point is exclusionary:

```text
aggregates only
```

The `mātra` rules out a further substantial bearer.

### 2. `abhisaṃskṛta` is formative

The aggregate-series is not merely accompanied by kleśa and karma. It is formed, prepared, and conditioned by them.

### 3. `pradīpavat` is not identity-transfer

The analogy should not be translated in a way that suggests one enduring flame-substance migrating. The point is serial dependence.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

This is another precise case of what we have been calling **Self-theorist adhyāsa**.

The field already gives:

```text
Dharma-relations
    → conditioned aggregates
    → causal continuity
    → transition
    → new determination
```

The Self-theorist then projects:

```text
an independent identical subject
behind the whole process
```

That added subject does not increase the truth of the determination. It mistakes a function of the field—continuity—for a separately existing thing.

So the error can be stated very cleanly:

```text
continuity
    is a relation within the Dharma-field

ātman
    is the reification of that relation
```

Or in the language of the current discussion:

```text
Self-theorist adhyāsa
    = individualizing the continuity-function
      into a supposed self-subsistent bearer
```

The Bhāṣya's answer is not nihilism. It is **more determination, not less**:

```text
not Self
but
skandha + kleśa + karma + saṃtati
```

This is substantially richer than the Self-theory because every term names an operative determination.

---

## Working Summary

VAkK 3.18 is the explicit no-self payoff of the entire `antarābhava` discussion.

```text
There is continuity.
There is rebirth.
There is karmic projection.
There is intermediate existence.
There is womb-entry.

But:
    there is no ātman.

What continues:
    skandhamātra

What forms it:
    kleśa + karma

How it transitions:
    antarābhava-saṃtati

Model:
    pradīpa, flame-continuity
```

The Self-theorist's ātman is therefore not an explanatory necessity but an added reification of the already sufficient continuity of the Dharma-field.
