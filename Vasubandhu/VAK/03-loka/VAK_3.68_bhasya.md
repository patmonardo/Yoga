## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first fixes the four `subhūmi` pleasure-grounds as spaces twenty yojanas from the four gardens. It then turns to the two special sites named in the verse.

Pārijāta is identified as a `kovidāra` tree and as the support of the Trāyastriṃśa gods' most intense sensual enjoyment. Its dimensions and fragrance are then specified. The claim that its scent travels even against the wind generates an objection and several explanations concerning the causal transmission of fragrance.

Finally Sudharmā is identified as the divine assembly hall in the south-west, where the gods deliberate concerning what is to be done and what is not to be done.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

In the four directions from these gardens are four pleasant grounds, each separated by twenty yojanas. They are themselves play-places of the gods and spread beauty through mutual rivalry.

Outside the city itself, in the north-east, is Pārijāta; in the south-west is Sudharmā.

Pārijāta is a kovidāra tree, the support of the highest sensual delight of the Trāyastriṃśa gods. Its root-base extends five yojanas; its height is one hundred yojanas; its branches and foliage spread fifty yojanas. When it is fully in bloom, its fragrance travels one hundred yojanas downwind and fifty yojanas against the wind.

Downwind is reasonable; but how against the wind?

Some say that this statement is made with reference to not passing beyond the tree. But wind does not literally blow against the wind. Rather, such power must be attributed to the fragrance itself: although resisted by gentle divine winds, it produces a further continuity of fragrance. Because that continuation begins only weakly, its series soon ceases, and therefore it does not spread equally far.

Does such a continuum of flower-fragrance occur supported by its own material constituents, or does the wind itself become perfumed? There is no fixed rule here; the teachers accept both possibilities.

Sudharmā is the divine assembly hall in the south-western quarter. Seated there, the gods deliberate what is to be done and what is not to be done.

### Philosophical Translation

The Trāyastriṃśa summit is articulated not only as a beautiful environment but as a field of enjoyment, causal interaction, and communal decision. Pārijāta marks the intensification of kāma within the deva-world, while Sudharmā introduces an explicitly deliberative institution within that same conditioned world.

### Objection

If fragrance is carried by wind, how can it travel against the wind?

### Replies

The text records more than one explanatory strategy. One view restricts the claim by reference to the tree's own range. Another treats the fragrance as possessing a causal efficacy capable of continuing even while opposed by gentle divine winds.

A further question asks whether the fragrance-continuum depends upon its own material constituents or upon perfumed wind. Vasubandhu does not impose one exclusive mechanism: both are accepted by teachers.

## New Technical Terms

- **kāmaratiprakarṣāśraya** — support of the highest degree of sensual enjoyment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression
  - **function:** doctrinal determination of Pārijāta within kāmadhātu

- **gandhasaṃtāna** — continuum / series of fragrance
  - **status:** Bhāṣya analytical term
  - **function:** causal explanation of how scent propagates

- **prabhāvasaṃpad** — efficacy / power
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** accounts for fragrance transmission under resistance

- **devasabhā** — divine assembly hall
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** identifies Sudharmā institutionally

- **kṛtyākṛtya** — what is to be done and not to be done
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this passage
  - **function:** practical deliberation of the gods

## Doctrinal Determination

Pārijāta belongs wholly to the refined pleasure-structure of kāmadhātu. Its importance is not symbolic transcendence but intensified conditioned enjoyment.

The fragrance discussion is characteristic Abhidharma analysis: a cosmological description is immediately tested for causal coherence. The text distinguishes observable motion, causal continuity, supporting material conditions, and alternative explanatory models.

Sudharmā adds another determination of deva-life: the gods possess an assembly in which they deliberate concerning action. Even divine habitation therefore contains practical differentiation rather than undifferentiated bliss.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and correction.**

- `subhūmīni` are explicitly divine play-grounds associated with the four gardens.
- `viṃśatyantaritāni` means separated by twenty yojanas.
- `dakṣiṇāvare` is explicitly glossed as `dakṣiṇapaścime digbhāge`, the south-western quarter.
- Sudharmā is specifically a divine assembly hall.

## Logical Determination

```text
pleasure-field
    → intensified enjoyment
        → causal analysis of appearance and transmission

civic field
    → assembly
        → practical deliberation
```

The produced deva-world is not merely spatially ornamented. It contains causal processes and organized collective activity.

## Organon Contact Point

The phrase `kṛtyākṛtyaṃ samarthayanti` is a notable practical-reason marker: the gods deliberate what is to be done and not done. But this remains an institution within conditioned Loka, not Pure Practical Reason itself.
