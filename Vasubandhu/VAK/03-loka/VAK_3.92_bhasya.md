## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first closes the lifespan-cycle analysis from the preceding verse. Human lifespan does not rise beyond eighty thousand years. The first decline, the eighteen complete rise-and-fall cycles, and the final increase are all equal in duration. Thus the expanded world remains in its standing condition for twenty intermediate kalpas.

Vasubandhu then widens the analysis from the internal lifespan oscillations to the four large phases of the world-cycle. The time during which the world stands expanded is equal to each of the other three phases:

```text
vivarta       → expansion / formation
vivṛtta-sthiti → standing while expanded
saṃvarta      → contraction / dissolution
saṃvṛtta-sthiti → standing while contracted
```

Each phase lasts twenty intermediate kalpas.

The internal distribution is asymmetric even though the total duration is equal:

```text
formation phase
    1 antarakalpa  → receptacle-world forms
    19 antarakalpas → beings populate it

contraction phase
    19 antarakalpas → beings disappear / world empties
    1 antarakalpa  → receptacle-world is destroyed
```

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes equality of total duration from equality of internal process.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is explanatory and classificatory, with no marked objection or rival-school intervention.

## New Technical Terms

- **vivṛtta** — expanded, unfolded; the world after formation
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā and clarified by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** names the world-state during the standing phase after expansion

- **saṃvṛtta** — contracted, dissolved
  - **status:** introduced explicitly in the Bhāṣya continuation
  - **function:** names the world-state during the standing phase after contraction

- **āvāsyante** — become inhabited / are populated
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this passage
  - **function:** marks the nineteen-antarakalpa population phase of vivarta

- **śūnyī bhavanti** — become empty
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this passage
  - **function:** marks the nineteen-antarakalpa depopulation phase of saṃvarta

## Doctrinal Determination

The twenty intermediate kalpas of the opened world are only one quarter of the larger cyclic structure.

```text
vivarta                 20 antarakalpas
vivṛtta-sthiti           20 antarakalpas
saṃvarta                20 antarakalpas
saṃvṛtta-sthiti          20 antarakalpas
                        ----------------
                         80 antarakalpas
```

This prepares the definition of the mahākalpa in the next verse.

The important doctrinal point is that cosmic equality is equality of duration, not identity of content. During the standing phases there are no lifespan `utkarṣa` and `apakarṣa` processes of the same kind, yet their duration is still reckoned as equal.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā's claim that the expanded world stands for twenty kalpas is correct, but the Bhāṣya establishes that these are specifically twenty **antarakalpas**, and that the same twentyfold duration applies to expansion, contraction, and contracted-standing as well.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya introduces a striking distinction between quantitative equality and qualitative difference:

```text
equal duration
    ≠ identical internal process
```

All four world-phases are temporally commensurate, even though their internal event-structure differs. The world-cycle is therefore proportionally ordered rather than merely repetitive.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

```text
Equality of measure does not imply identity of determination.
```

This is exactly the sort of distinction the Kośa is now forcing: the same temporal magnitude can contain very different world-processes.
