# VAK_2.02 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> svārthopalabdhyādhipatyāt sarvasya ca ṣaḍindriyam |
> strītvapuṃstvādhipatyāt tu kāyāt strīpuruṣendriye || VAK 2.2 ||

**Best current translation:**

> The six are faculties because of their governing efficacy in apprehending their respective objects—and, in the case of mind, all objects. The female and male faculties are separately distinguished within the body-faculty because they govern female-being and male-being.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_2.02.md`](./VAK_2.02.md)

## Bhāṣya Source: Objection to the Fourfold Account

```text
apare punarāhuḥ /
naiva cakṣuḥśrotrābhyāmātmabhāvaparikarṣaṇaṃ vijñāya viṣamaparihārāt /
vijñāne tu tayorādhipatyam /
nāpi vijñānādanyadrūpadarśanaṃ śabdaśravaṇaṃ vā 'sti /
yatastayorasādhāraṇakāraṇatve pṛthagādhipatyaṃ yujyeta /
tasmānnaivameṣāmindriyatvam /
kathaṃ tarhi /
```

## Translation

> Others, however, say: It is not the eye and ear themselves that preserve the embodied individual through the avoidance of dangers after those dangers have been recognized. Rather, the eye and ear possess governing efficacy with respect to knowledge. Nor is seeing visible form or hearing sound something different from knowledge, such that a separate governing efficacy could properly be assigned to them as the specific causes of seeing and hearing. Therefore, their status as faculties cannot be explained in the preceding manner. How, then, is it to be explained?

**Voice:** alternative doctrinal position

## The Force of the Objection

The first account assigned four functions to the sensory faculties. The objector argues that two of them are improperly counted:

1. avoiding danger is a remote consequence of knowledge, not the direct work of the eye or ear;
2. seeing and visual knowledge, or hearing and auditory knowledge, are not two separate events.

The causal sequence must therefore be distinguished:

```text
faculty
    ↓
knowledge-event
    ↓
recognition
    ↓
response
    ↓
avoidance
```

A faculty should be defined by its proximate and characteristic governing function, not by every later consequence in the causal series.

## Revised Definition of the Six Faculties

```text
cakṣurādīnāṃ pañcānāṃ svasyasvasyārthasyopalabdhāvādhipatyam /
manasaḥ punaḥ sarvārthopalabdhāvādhipatyam /
ata etāni ṣaṭ pratyekamindriyam /
```

> The five beginning with the eye possess governing efficacy in the apprehension of their respective objects. Mind, however, possesses governing efficacy in the apprehension of all objects. Therefore, each of these six is a faculty.

The five sensory faculties are domain-specific:

| Faculty | Proper object-domain |
|---|---|
| cakṣus | visible form |
| śrotra | sound |
| ghrāṇa | odor |
| jihvā | flavor |
| kāya | the tangible |

Mind differs structurally from the five. They each govern one proper object-domain; mind governs apprehension across all object-domains.

```text
five particular faculties
    ↓
one generally extensible faculty
```

Mind does not abolish the particular faculties. It integrates their domains and also apprehends objects not immediately present to the senses.

## Objection: Do Objects Also Govern Apprehension?

```text
nanu cārthānāmapyatrādhipatyam /
```

> But do not the objects also possess governing efficacy here?

A visual event requires both eye and visible form. Mere causal participation therefore cannot distinguish a faculty from its object.

## Ādhipatya as Predominant Causal Command

```text
nādhipatyam /
adhikaṃ hi prabhutvamādhipatyam /
```

> No. The objects do not possess governing efficacy, for governing efficacy is a superior power of command.

The Bhāṣya therefore refines the definition:

```text
ādhipatya
    ≠ merely being a cause

ādhipatya
    = predominant or superior causal command
```

The eye possesses greater governing power in visual apprehension for two reasons:

1. it is the common cause throughout the entire domain of visible apprehension;
2. the quality of apprehension conforms to the condition of the eye—sharp, dull, impaired, and so forth.

```text
variation in faculty
    → corresponding variation in apprehension
```

The object contributes the particular content apprehended, but the faculty governs the operative capacity and form of apprehension. The same reasoning applies through mind in relation to dharmas.

## Female and Male Faculties Within the Body-Faculty

```text
kāyendriyādeva strīpuruṣendriye pṛthag vyavasthāpyete /
nārthāntarabhūte /
```

> The female and male faculties are separately established from the body-faculty itself. They are not entities substantially different from it.

A certain portion of the body-faculty, the genital region, receives the designation “female faculty” or “male faculty” because of its governing efficacy over female-being or male-being.

Thus the distinction is functional rather than substantial:

```text
body-faculty
    ↓
determinate bodily region
    ↓
specific governing function
    ↓
separate faculty-name
```

## Female-Being and Male-Being

The Bhāṣya describes each as a distributed configuration:

```text
ākṛti      bodily form
svara      voice
ceṣṭā      behavior or expressive movement
abhiprāya  intention or orientation
```

The ancient account therefore does not restrict *strītva* and *puṃstva* to anatomy. It understands a local bodily determination as governing a wider embodied and psychological configuration. This should be translated historically and accurately, not silently converted into a modern medical or ethical claim.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
The fourfold sensory account is challenged.
    ↓
Avoidance of danger is too remote to define sensory facultyhood.
    ↓
Seeing and visual knowledge are not separate functions.
    ↓
Each sensory faculty governs apprehension of its own object.
    ↓
Mind governs apprehension of all objects.
    ↓
Objects also participate causally in apprehension.
    ↓
Mere causality cannot define a faculty.
    ↓
Ādhipatya means predominant causal command.
    ↓
The faculty is common across its domain,
and cognition varies according to its condition.
    ↓
Female and male faculties are functional distinctions
within the body-faculty, not separate substances.
```

## Logical Determination

VAK 2.2 determines the strict sense of governing efficacy:

```text
A governs B when:

1. A is a common condition throughout the domain of B;
2. changes in A produce corresponding changes in B;
3. A determines the capacity or form of B,
   not merely one particular content within it.
```

The faculty is therefore not merely one cause among others. It is the condition whose own determination is reflected in the resulting cognition.

```text
object
    determines the particular content apprehended

faculty
    determines the operative form and capacity of apprehension
```

## Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya reveals the Kārikā as the compression of a dispute. The final formula—facultyhood through apprehensive governance—is reached only after rejecting remote causal attribution, eliminating duplicated functions, and distinguishing ordinary causality from predominant causal command.

This is an ancient Dharma Science at work: classification is tested by causal role, functional range, counterexample, and explanatory economy. It does not yet present a complex formal logic, but it clearly practices disciplined rational determination.

## New Technical Terms

- **svārthopalabdhi** — apprehension of a faculty’s proper object; Kārikā term unfolded by the Bhāṣya; direct cognitive function.
- **sarvārthopalabdhi** — apprehension of all objects; Bhāṣya expansion of *sarvasya*; distinctive function of mind.
- **adhika-prabhutva** — superior or predominant power of command; Bhāṣya-only; distinguishes governance from ordinary causality.
- **adhika-aiśvarya** — greater governing power; Bhāṣya-only; explains why the faculty, rather than the object, is an *indriya*.
- **sāmānya-kāraṇatva** — being a common cause throughout a domain; Bhāṣya-only; first criterion of governance.
- **anuvidhāna** — conformity or variation in accordance with another; reflective vocabulary; cognition conforms to the faculty’s condition.
- **kāyendriya-bhāga** — a portion of the body-faculty; Bhāṣya-only; denies separate substantiality of the sexual faculties.
- **strībhāva / puṃbhāva** — female configuration / male configuration; Bhāṣya expansion; distributed embodied determination.
- **ākṛti** — bodily form or configuration; Bhāṣya-only in this unit.
- **abhiprāya** — intention, orientation, or disposition; Bhāṣya-only in this unit.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā translation should read “separately distinguished within the body-faculty,” not simply “distinguished from the body.” The Bhāṣya explicitly denies that the female and male faculties are substantially different entities.

The source also confirms:

```text
indriya = faculty
ādhipatya = governing efficacy
svārthopalabdhi = apprehension of its proper object
```
