# VAK 2.52 Bhāṣya — Homogeneous Cause, Temporal Priority, and Ordered Development

## Kārikā

```text
sabhāgahetuḥ sadṛśāḥ svanikāyabhuvo 'grajāḥ /
anyo 'nyaṃ navabhūmis tu mārgaḥ samaviśiṣṭayoḥ // VAkK_2.52 //
```

## Continuous Translation

The homogeneous cause consists of dharmas that are similar, that belong to their own class and level, and that have arisen previously.

The path, however, in its nine levels, is reciprocally a homogeneous cause across those levels, but only in relation to what is equal or superior.

Why does the path differ from ordinary conditioned dharmas? In the preliminary concentration, the intermediate concentration, the four concentrations, and the three formless attainments, the truth of the path may serve as homogeneous cause across levels. The path is adventitious within those levels: it does not belong intrinsically to their realm because it has not been appropriated by the cravings belonging to those levels. Therefore a path belonging to one level may serve as homogeneous cause for a path of the same kind belonging to another level.

Yet it serves as cause only for what is equal or superior, not for what is inferior. Thus the receptivity to the knowledge of dharmas concerning suffering is homogeneous cause for a future receptivity of the same kind and for superior path moments extending as far as the knowledge of non-arising. The knowledge of non-arising, because nothing superior to it remains, is homogeneous cause only for another knowledge of non-arising.

The paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-learning are respectively causes for three, two, and one of these path divisions. Within them, a path belonging to weak faculties is cause for paths belonging both to weak and sharp faculties, whereas a path belonging to sharp faculties is cause only for another sharp-faculty path.

Thus the paths of the follower through faith, one liberated through faith, and one liberated in due time are respectively causes for six, four, and two path-types. The paths of the follower of Dharma, the one who has attained right view, and the one liberated out of due time are respectively causes for three, two, and one.

How can a path belonging to a lower level be equal or superior to one belonging to a higher level? Equality and superiority are determined by the faculties and by the accumulation of causes. Among the paths beginning with the path of seeing, and among the nine grades beginning with weak-weak, each later member is more strongly accumulated through causes than the preceding one. Even where two such paths cannot arise in the same continuum, an arisen path may serve as cause for an unarisen future path.

Is it only the path that is homogeneous cause for what is equal or superior? No. Worldly qualities produced through application also serve as homogeneous causes only for equal or superior qualities, never for inferior ones. These include qualities born of hearing, reflection, and cultivation.

In the desire realm, qualities born of hearing are causes for qualities born of hearing and reflection; qualities born of reflection are causes only for qualities born of reflection, because qualities born of cultivation are absent there. In the form realm, qualities born of hearing are causes for qualities born of hearing and cultivation, because qualities born of reflection are absent there; qualities born of cultivation are causes only for cultivated qualities. In the formless realm, cultivated qualities are causes only for cultivated qualities.

Since these qualities are each divided into nine grades, the weak-weak grade is cause for all nine, the weak-middle for eight, and so on according to this rule.

Wholesome dharmas obtained by birth, however, in all nine grades, may be homogeneous causes for one another. Defiled dharmas are treated similarly.

Unobscured-neutral dharmas are fourfold: maturation-born, conduct-related, craft-related, and those arising together with a mind of transformation. In this order they are homogeneous causes for four, three, two, and one classes respectively. A mind of transformation in the desire realm may be a result of the four concentrations, but a result produced by a higher concentration is not homogeneous cause for a result produced by a lower concentration.

For a homogeneous cause produced through deliberate application does not yield an inferior result. Otherwise effort would become fruitless, just as cultivation of rice would be pointless if it yielded barley.

Therefore it is said that an arisen uncontaminated dharma may fail to be cause for an unarisen uncontaminated dharma—for example, an arisen knowledge of dharmas concerning suffering is not cause for the unarisen receptivities to that knowledge. More generally, what is superior is not cause for what is inferior.

A previously acquired uncontaminated dharma fixed within one continuum may also fail to be cause for a later-arisen uncontaminated dharma. Future receptivities to the knowledge of dharmas concerning suffering are not causes for the knowledge itself, because a result cannot precede its cause and because there is no future homogeneous cause in the required sense.

Likewise, a previously arisen uncontaminated dharma may not be cause for a later uncontaminated dharma where the latter is inferior—for example, when someone falls away from a higher fruit and manifests a lower fruit.

Thus the homogeneous cause has been explained.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Ordinary homogeneous causality

For ordinary contaminated dharmas, the rule is:

```text
similarity
+ same class
+ same level
+ temporal priority
= homogeneous cause
```

The requirement of prior arising is essential. Homogeneous causality is not merely resemblance. It is resemblance functioning genetically through a sequence.

### 2. The Path as an exception to same-level restriction

The Path crosses the nine meditative levels because it is not appropriated by the craving belonging to any one of them.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika exposition**

```text
ordinary dharma:
    causally restricted to its own level

path-dharma:
    adventitious to the levels
    therefore causally mobile across them
```

This is not yet transcendence of conditioned existence. The Path remains conditioned, but its causal membership is not determined by the craving that defines an ordinary realm-bound continuum.

### 3. Equal-or-superior direction

A cultivated cause does not yield a deliberately inferior homogeneous result.

```text
weak → weak or stronger
strong → strong, not weaker
```

This gives homogeneous causality a directional structure. It is not circular repetition but ordered development.

### 4. Hearing, reflection, and cultivation

The Bhāṣya carefully distinguishes the available modes of produced knowledge by realm:

```text
desire realm:
    hearing
    reflection

form realm:
    hearing
    cultivation

formless realm:
    cultivation
```

The causal rule is governed not merely by semantic similarity but by what modes of cognition can actually occur in each level.

### 5. Effort must not be fruitless

The rice-and-barley analogy states a strong principle:

> Deliberate cultivation must preserve the qualitative direction of what is cultivated.

The causal series is therefore not only temporally ordered but normatively graded.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository narrator** — presents the definitions, exceptions, and ordered examples.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — treats homogeneous cause as a real causal relation structured by similarity, prior arising, class, level, and grade.

**Voice: Objector** — asks how lower-level paths can be equal or superior to higher-level paths, and whether the special rule applies only to the path.

**Voice: uncertain** — the closing scholastic examples preserve inherited Abhidharma matrices whose exact school attribution is not always marked in the passage.

## New Technical Terms

- **sabhāga-hetu** — homogeneous cause; a prior similar dharma functioning as cause for a later similar dharma.
  - **status:** introduced in the Kārikā, extensively developed in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** defines serial causality through similarity and temporal priority.

- **svanikāya** — one’s own class or group.
  - **status:** technical restriction developed here.
  - **function:** limits ordinary homogeneous causality to the relevant dharma-class.

- **svabhūmi** — one’s own level or plane.
  - **status:** technical restriction developed here.
  - **function:** limits realm-bound causal continuity.

- **āgantuka** — adventitious, not native to a level.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanation in this passage.
  - **function:** explains why the Path crosses level-bound causal restrictions.

- **samaviśiṣṭa** — equal or superior.
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā and heavily specified in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** determines the permitted qualitative direction of homogeneous causality.

- **hetūpacaya** — accumulation or strengthening of causes.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical expression.
  - **function:** explains how a lower-level path may nevertheless be equal or superior through greater causal development.

- **prayogaja** — produced through application or deliberate practice.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit.
  - **function:** distinguishes cultivated qualities from those obtained merely by birth.

- **śrutamayī / cintāmayī / bhāvanāmayī** — born of hearing, reflection, and cultivation.
  - **status:** major Bhāṣya terminology.
  - **function:** classifies modes of produced knowledge and their causal reach.

- **upapatti-pratilambhika** — obtained through birth.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit.
  - **function:** contrasts naturally acquired qualities with qualities produced through deliberate application.

## Translation Consequences

### Sabhāga is more than “similar”

“Homogeneous” should preserve three linked senses:

1. qualitative resemblance,
2. membership in an ordered class,
3. causal continuity through prior and later moments.

A merely static translation such as “similar cause” risks obscuring the serial and developmental character.

### Agraja means prior-born

Temporal priority belongs to the definition itself. The cause is not simply logically prior; it must occupy the earlier position in the relevant stream.

### Mārga is adventitious, not external

The Path functions within the meditative levels but is not possessed by their craving. “Adventitious” therefore means causally present without being ontologically owned by the level’s defiled structure.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 2.52 introduces a causal form combining:

```text
temporal priority
qualitative similarity
class membership
level membership
directional development
```

This is not yet the associated cause of simultaneous citta and caittas. It is diachronic: an earlier determination grounds a later determination of the same type.

The principal rule is:

```text
prior similar cause
    → equal or superior homogeneous result
```

The principal prohibition is:

```text
cultivated superior cause
    ↛ inferior homogeneous result
```

The Path modifies the spatial or level-bound side of the rule without abolishing its temporal and qualitative order.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This passage supports the developing Logic of Experience with unusual precision.

Empirical Being-there supplies the ordered domain:

```text
class
level
continuum
prior and later moments
```

Rational illumination determines the genetic connection within that domain:

```text
this earlier determination
is ground for
that equal or superior later determination
```

Thus causal judgment contains both a spatial-like restriction and a temporal connection:

- **Being-there / level:** where the causal determination belongs;
- **prior-later sequence:** how the determination is genetically transmitted;
- **qualitative grade:** what kind of result can follow.

The Path is especially important because it is conditioned and operative within Nature, yet not enclosed by the craving that ordinarily fixes a dharma to its level. It therefore shows how Rational Nature can function within empirical-relative reality in a dual manner: immanent to the causal field, but not reducible to its ordinary realm-membership.

A problematic assertion becomes self-certain here only when all three determinations are supplied:

```text
where it belongs
what precedes it
what grade of result it can generate
```

This is genetic judgment in a strict form.

## Summary

VAK 2.52 defines homogeneous cause as prior similarity operating within an ordered continuum. Ordinary causality is restricted by class and level. The Path crosses levels because it is not appropriated by their cravings, yet it still produces only equal or superior results. Cultivated cognition therefore has a directional causal form: hearing, reflection, and cultivation do not merely repeat themselves but transmit and intensify determinate capacities through time.
