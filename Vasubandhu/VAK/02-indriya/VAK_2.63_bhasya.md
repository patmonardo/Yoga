# VAK_2.63 — Bhāṣya: Temporal Activity of Hetu and Pratyaya

## Kārikā

### Sanskrit — Devanāgarī

```text
निरुध्यमाने कारित्रं द्वौ हेतू कुरुतः त्रयः ।
जायमाने ततोऽन्यौ तु प्रत्ययौ तद्विपर्ययात् ॥ VAkK_2.63 ॥
```

### Sanskrit — IAST

```text
nirudhyamāne kāritraṃ dvau hetū kurutaḥ trayaḥ /
jāyamāne tato 'nyau tu pratyayau tadviparyayāt // VAkK_2.63 //
```

### Working Translation

> While [a dharma] is ceasing, two causes perform their activity; three [do so] while [a dharma] is arising. The other two conditions are to be understood in the reverse manner.

---

## Bhāṣya — Continuous Translation

The conditions have now been stated. When they perform their activity, in what state of the dharma do they perform it?

The cause-condition has already been stated to be fivefold. Among these:

> **While [the dharma] is ceasing, two causes perform their activity.**

What is called “ceasing” here is the present [dharma], because it is directed toward cessation. In this phase the **co-arisen cause** (*sahabhū-hetu*) and the **associated cause** (*saṃprayuktaka-hetu*) perform their activity. Their operation is with respect to a result that has arisen together with them.

> **Three [perform their activity] while [the dharma] is arising.**

What is called “arising” here is the future [dharma], insofar as it is directed toward arising. In this phase the **homogeneous cause** (*sabhāga-hetu*), **pervasive cause** (*sarvatraga-hetu*), and **maturation cause** (*vipāka-hetu*) perform their activity.

So much, then, for the cause-condition.

> **The other two conditions are to be understood by the reverse of this.**

According to the principle by which the cause-condition has been divided in two and explained, the **immediately antecedent condition** (*samanantara-pratyaya*) and the **object-condition** (*ālambana-pratyaya*) are to be understood in the reverse manner.

The immediately antecedent condition performs its activity with respect to what is **arising**, by giving it an opening or opportunity (*avakāśadāna*).

The object-condition performs its activity with respect to what is **ceasing**, because present citta and caittas apprehend their object.

The **dominant condition** (*adhipati-pratyaya*), however, is present in every state through its character of non-obstruction (*anāvaraṇabhāva*). This itself is its activity.

Thus the conditions have been stated together with their activities.

---

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory exposition of the Vaibhāṣika causal-condition system.

No explicit objector is introduced in this unit. The passage is taxonomic and analytic rather than polemical.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **kāritra** — operative activity or causal function in a determinate temporal phase.
  - **status:** previously encountered, but here explicitly time-indexed.
  - **function:** defines when a cause or condition is actually operative.

- **nirudhyamāna** — “ceasing”; technically the present dharma insofar as it is oriented toward cessation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of Kārikā language.
  - **function:** marks the present-phase locus of causal activity.

- **jāyamāna** — “arising”; technically the future dharma insofar as it is oriented toward arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of Kārikā language.
  - **function:** marks the future-phase locus of causal activity.

- **avakāśadāna** — “giving an opening / opportunity.”
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
  - **function:** explains how *samanantara-pratyaya* conditions the next citta-caitta event: the preceding mental event vacates the place for the next.

- **anāvaraṇabhāva** — non-obstruction.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
  - **function:** defines the universal mode of activity of *adhipati-pratyaya*.

---

## Translation Consequences

The temporal vocabulary is technical. “Ceasing” does not mean that the dharma is already absent; it designates the **present** dharma as directed toward cessation. Likewise, “arising” designates the **future** dharma as directed toward production.

The verse therefore does not merely contrast production and destruction. It distributes causal operation over two temporal orientations:

```text
present / cessation-directed
    sahabhū-hetu
    saṃprayuktaka-hetu
    ālambana-pratyaya

future / arising-directed
    sabhāga-hetu
    sarvatraga-hetu
    vipāka-hetu
    samanantara-pratyaya

all phases
    adhipati-pratyaya as non-obstruction
```

The Bhāṣya thus makes *kāritra* phase-sensitive.

---

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 2.63 turns the causal taxonomy into an **operator system**. A cause or condition is not fully specified merely by naming its type; its valid temporal phase must also be known.

Three distinct modes appear:

1. **Simultaneous efficacy** — co-arisen and associated causes operate upon results arising together with them.
2. **Prospective efficacy** — homogeneous, pervasive, and maturation causes operate with respect to what is coming into being.
3. **Conditional presentation** — immediately antecedent condition opens the place for the next cognitive event, while the object-condition is operative in the present act of apprehension.

The dominant condition differs from all of them because its causal role is the broadest possible one: it operates through **non-obstruction**, and therefore is not restricted to a single temporal phase.

This shows why *pratyaya* cannot be reduced to a loose notion of “circumstance.” Each condition has a determinate logical function in the genesis of an event.

---

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This passage strongly supports a phase-sensitive Rational–Empirical reading, provided we keep it secondary to the Abhidharma account.

```text
Hetu
    determines productive ground
    according to a temporal mode of efficacy

Pratyaya
    determines the relational form
    under which arising or apprehension becomes possible
```

The contrast between *samanantara* and *ālambana* is particularly important:

```text
samanantara
    condition of the next event's arising
    succession / opening

ālambana
    condition of the present event's apprehension
    object-reference / presentation
```

This gives the “Rational Subjectivity” side a precise structure. Subjectivity is not merely a stream of states; it has both a law of succession and a law of object-reference.

The dominant condition then supplies the widest field of possibility through non-obstruction.

So a cognitive event is intelligible only when we can say not merely **what** conditioned it, but **when and how** that condition was operative.

---

## Relation to the Hetu–Pratyaya Dyad

The previous verses established the productive objectivity of *hetu*. VAK 2.63 now shows that *pratyaya* articulates the phase and relational form of manifestation.

```text
Hetu
    productive determination

Pratyaya
    lawful condition of manifestation
```

In the comparative Yoga reconstruction, this gives a strong basis for treating their unity as a Samādhi structure: productive ground and lawful presentation comprehended together as one determinate actuality. This is an Organon/Yoga interpretation, not an explicit claim of the Bhāṣya.
