# VAK 2.51 Bhāṣya — Citta-Followers and Shared Determination

## Kārikā

```text
caittā dvau saṃvarau teṣāṃ cetaso lakṣaṇāni ca /
cittānuvartinaḥ kālaphalādiśubhatādibhiḥ // VAkK_2.51 //
```

## Continuous Translation

What, then, are these dharmas that follow citta?

The mental factors, the two restraints, and the marks belonging to them and to citta are called followers of citta. All dharmas associated with citta are included here. The two restraints are the restraint belonging to concentration and the uncontaminated restraint. The marks are birth and the other conditioned marks belonging to those restraints and to citta.

How do these dharmas follow citta? In brief, they do so with respect to time, result, and such matters as ethical quality.

First, with respect to time: they have one arising, duration, and cessation together with citta, and they fall within the same temporal period.

With respect to result and the rest: they have one result, one maturation-result, and one homogeneous outflow together with citta. Here the word “one” is to be understood in the sense of “together with.”

With respect to wholesomeness and the like: when citta is wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate, these are likewise wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate.

Thus they are called followers of citta by reason of ten conditions.

Even the smallest citta is a co-arisen cause of fifty-eight dharmas: the ten universal mental factors, the forty marks belonging to them, and the eight own-marks and secondary marks. In turn, fifty-four dharmas are co-arisen causes of that citta, once its own secondary marks are excluded.

Some say that only fourteen dharmas are its co-arisen causes: the ten universal mental factors and citta’s own four marks. This position is not accepted, because it conflicts with the *Prakaraṇagrantha*.

The text states that there may be a truth of suffering caused by the view of a real personality which is not itself a cause of that view. Apart from the birth, aging, duration, and impermanence of the view of a real personality and of the dharmas associated with it, the remaining afflicted truth of suffering is caused by that view but is not itself a cause of that view. What was excepted, however, is a cause of the view.

Those who do not read the phrase “and of the dharmas associated with it” must nevertheless supply it or understand the passage in that sense. Such is the position of the Kāśmīras.

Whatever is a cause through the co-arisen cause is itself co-arisen. But something may be co-arisen without being a cause through the co-arisen cause. The secondary marks of a dharma are an example: the dharma is their co-arisen cause, but they are not its cause. The secondary marks are also merely co-arisen with one another.

Likewise, the secondary marks that follow citta, resistant derived matter in relation to other such matter, some non-resistant derived matter, all derived matter in relation to the great elements, and co-arisen acquisitions in relation to the dharma acquired are co-arisen but are not co-arisen causes. They do not share one result, one maturation-result, and one homogeneous outflow. Nor do acquisitions invariably accompany the acquired dharma: they may arise before it or after it.

## Restrained Reconstruction

VAK 2.51 clarifies what “following citta” means. It does not mean mere adjacency or psychological association. A *cittānuvartin* shares a determinate profile with citta across several dimensions:

- one arising, duration, and cessation;
- one temporal location;
- one result;
- one maturation-result;
- one homogeneous outflow;
- one ethical status.

The Bhāṣya says these amount to ten conditions because arising, duration, cessation, and temporal period give four temporal conditions; result, maturation, and outflow give three causal-result conditions; and wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate give three ethical conditions.

The important point is that co-arising is still not enough. A dharma may coexist with another and yet fail to count as its co-arisen cause. Shared result-structure is required. Thus the Kośa sharpens the distinction already opened in VAK 2.50:

```text
co-presence
    is broader than
co-arisen causality
```

The numerical analysis of fifty-eight and fifty-four dharmas is not decorative scholasticism. It expresses a strict event ontology. A citta-event is never an isolated point; it is a structured complex whose members are mutually coordinated, though not always symmetrically causal.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository voice** — defines the citta-followers and explains their ten shared conditions.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — treats the coordinated citta-complex through exact co-arisen causal relations and numerical enumeration.

**Voice: alternative Abhidharma opinion** — claims that only fourteen dharmas are co-arisen causes of citta.

**Voice: Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣika** — rejects that narrower count by appeal to the *Prakaraṇagrantha* and insists that an omitted phrase must be supplied in interpretation.

## New Technical Terms

- **cittānuvartin** — a dharma that follows citta by sharing its temporal, causal-result, and ethical profile.
  - **status:** introduced here as an explicit technical class.
  - **function:** defines coordinated membership in the citta-event.

- **dhyānasaṃvara** — restraint belonging to meditative concentration.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā’s explicit wording but supplied by the Bhāṣya as one of the two restraints.
  - **function:** a non-mental dharma that nevertheless follows citta under the stated conditions.

- **anāsravasaṃvara** — uncontaminated restraint.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification of the second restraint.
  - **function:** path-related restraint coordinated with uncontaminated citta.

- **ekotpāda-sthiti-nirodhatā** — having one arising, duration, and cessation.
  - **status:** dense Bhāṣya formulation.
  - **function:** temporal criterion of citta-followership.

- **ekādhvapatitatva** — falling within one temporal period.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical determination in this unit.
  - **function:** locates citta and its followers in the same past, present, or future phase.

- **satkāyadṛṣṭi** — view of a real personality or existing body-complex.
  - **status:** previously important doctrinal term, here used in a textual proof.
  - **function:** tests the scope of co-arisen causality through the *Prakaraṇagrantha*.

## Translation Consequences

“Follower” is too weak if heard as something merely subsequent. The Sanskrit *anuvartin* here means a factor that conforms to and accompanies citta through a common structural profile.

Likewise, “one result” should not suggest numerical identity of a separate object possessed by several causes. The Bhāṣya explicitly says that “one” means “together with”: the members participate in the same result-series.

The passage also warns against translating *sahabhū* simply as “simultaneous.” All co-arisen causes are co-arisen, but not all co-arisen dharmas are causes of one another.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The verse gives a criterion for judging whether a factor belongs internally to a citta-event:

```text
CittaFollower(x, citta)
    only if x shares with citta:
        temporal genesis
        temporal phase
        result
        maturation
        homogeneous continuation
        ethical determination
```

This is a genetic judgment, not a judgment from bare observation. Empirical simultaneity supplies a problematic assertion: “these factors belong together.” Rational analysis determines whether that assertion is valid by disclosing the shared temporal and causal form.

The relation is also asymmetrical in certain cases. A primary dharma may cause its secondary marks without those marks causing it. Thus reciprocity must be established, not presumed.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This passage provides an exact model of the genetic form of judgment discussed in our reading.

The empirical field presents a coordinated manifold: citta, mental factors, restraints, and marks arise together. But self-certainty does not come from their visible or introspectively given conjunction. It comes from rationally determining the invariants of their genesis:

```text
same arising
same temporal order
same causal result
same ethical determination
```

A problematic assertion becomes a determined judgment when the conditioning structure is illuminated. In this sense, the Kośa’s causal analysis is *prakāśa*: it makes the internal form of the event intelligible without replacing the event by a separate absolute substance.

The event is therefore both empirical and rational:

```text
empirical nature
    the manifold appears together

rational nature
    the manifold is judged as one event
    through shared genetic determinations
```

This is not yet the final Organon subsumption, but it is a strong local correspondence. VAK 2.51 shows that the unity of an event is neither a bare datum nor a separately reified universal. It is a determinable unity established through its genesis.
