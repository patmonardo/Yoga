# VAK 2.22 — Fixed Co-Arising of Material Dharmas

## Kārikā

```text
kāme 'ṣṭadravyako 'śabdaḥ paramāṇur anindriyaḥ /
kāyendriyī navadravyaḥ daśadravyo 'parendriyaḥ // VAkK_2.22 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

> The detailed distinctions of the faculties, introduced in connection with their division by realm, have now been explained.
>
> The following question is now considered: are these conditioned dharmas, just as they possess distinct characteristics, also separately produced, or are there some that necessarily arise together?
>
> There are such dharmas that necessarily arise together.
>
> All dharmas fall into five classes: material form, mind, mental factors, conditioned forces dissociated from mind, and the unconditioned.
>
> The unconditioned does not arise at all. For material dharmas, however, the following rule applies.
>
> In the desire realm, the smallest material aggregate, when it is without sound and without a faculty, arises with eight substances and never with fewer.
>
> The smallest material aggregate is called a *paramāṇu*: it is the most subtle aggregate of material form, one within which no further distinct constituent can be discerned.
>
> In the desire realm, such a soundless and facultyless aggregate arises with eight substances. These eight are the four great elements and the four forms derived from them: visible form, odor, taste, and the tangible.
>
> If the aggregate includes a faculty, then, while remaining without sound, it arises with nine or ten substances.
>
> An aggregate possessing the body faculty has nine substances: the preceding eight together with the body faculty.
>
> An aggregate possessing another sensory faculty has ten substances: those same nine together with one among the eye, ear, nose, or tongue faculties.
>
> If these aggregates arise together with sound, they respectively arise with nine, ten, or eleven substances. For sound too may be inseparable from a faculty-bearing aggregate when it is produced by appropriated great elements.

## Reconstruction of the Count

```text
facultyless, soundless cluster:
    four great elements
    visible form
    odor
    taste
    tangible
    = eight

body-faculty cluster:
    preceding eight
    body faculty
    = nine

other sensory-faculty cluster:
    preceding nine
    eye, ear, nose, or tongue
    = ten

with sound added:
    nine, ten, or eleven respectively
```

## Main Determination

This passage opens a new problem:

```text
distinct characteristic
    does not imply
separate arising
```

The dharmas remain analytically distinct, yet some arise only in fixed material bundles.

The *paramāṇu* here is therefore not a solitary indivisible atom in the modern sense. Vasubandhu defines it as the most subtle **aggregate** within which no further constituent is perceptually distinguished. Even the minimum material unit is internally structured.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu — transition from faculty analysis to the problem of necessary co-arising.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika doctrinal exposition — fivefold classification of dharmas and the fixed counts of material clusters.

## New Technical Terms

- **niyata-sahotpāda** — necessary co-arising; absent from the Kārikā; introduces the governing problem of the unit.
- **rūpa-saṃghāta** — material aggregate; Bhāṣya-only expression here; defines the paramāṇu as a cluster rather than an isolated simple.
- **aṣṭa-dravyaka** — composed of eight substances; technical count-classification.
- **upādāya-rūpa** — derived material form; previously encountered in the Kośa, here used to identify visible form, odor, taste, and tangible form.
- **upātta-mahābhūta-hetuka** — produced from appropriated great elements; explains sound inseparable from a living faculty-bearing aggregate.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā should not be rendered as though it said that a single atom literally contains eight independent atoms. The Bhāṣya's own definition is:

```text
paramāṇu
    = the most subtle material aggregate
      below which no distinct constituent is discerned
```

Thus **“eight-substance minimum cluster”** is safer than **“eightfold atom.”**

## Doctrinal Determination

The minimum material event is already composite:

```text
material existence
    = great elements
    + derived sensible determinations
```

A sensory faculty does not arise by itself. It presupposes the common material base and, except for the body faculty, also presupposes the body faculty within the same aggregate.

This is a clear case in which the Abhidharma's classificatory distinctions are constrained by a doctrine of necessary co-production. The system is not merely multiplying classes; it is also specifying which classes can never occur independently.

## Compact Formula

```text
VAK 2.22 begins the doctrine of fixed co-arising:
the smallest material unit is an internally structured aggregate,
and sensory faculties arise only within determinate material bundles.
```
