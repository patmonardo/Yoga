# VAK 2.17 — Arhatship Counts and the Necessary Triad

## Kārikā

```text
ekādaśabhir arhattvam uktaṃ tv ekasya saṃbhavāt /
upekṣājīvitamanoyukto 'vaśyaṃ trayānvitaḥ // VAkK_2.17 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

> It is said in the Abhidharma: “By how many faculties does one attain arhatship?” The answer is: “By eleven.” How, then, has it just been said that arhatship is attained by nine?
>
> In fact, one attains it by nine faculties alone.
>
> Arhatship is nevertheless said to be attained by eleven because all eleven are possible for one person. It is possible that one and the same person, after repeatedly falling away, may attain arhatship with the pleasure faculty, with the gladness faculty, and with the neutral-feeling faculty. For this reason the total is stated as eleven.
>
> It is not, however, possible for pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling to occur at one and the same moment.
>
> Why does the same possibility not apply to a non-returner?
>
> Because a fallen non-returner never attains the fruit with the pleasure faculty, and one who had previously become free from desire does not fall away. This is because that freedom from desire was attained by both paths.
>
> We must now investigate the following question: when one is endowed with a given faculty, with how many faculties is one necessarily endowed?
>
> One who is endowed with any one among neutral feeling, life, and mind is necessarily endowed with three faculties—namely, with these very three.
>
> None of these can be possessed without the other two. With respect to the remaining faculties, however, there is no fixed rule: one may possess them or may not possess them.
>
> Thus one born in the formless realm is not endowed with the eye, ear, nose, or tongue faculties. In the desire realm, one may also lack faculties that were never acquired or that have been lost.
>
> One born in the formless realm is not endowed with the body faculty.
>
> One born in the form or formless realm is not endowed with the female faculty; and in the desire realm one may lack it because it was never acquired or has been lost. The same applies to the male faculty.
>
> An ordinary person born in the fourth dhyāna or in the formless realm is not endowed with the pleasure faculty.
>
> An ordinary person born in the third or fourth dhyāna or in the formless realm is not endowed with the gladness faculty.
>
> One born in the form or formless realm is not endowed with the pain faculty.
>
> One who is free from desire for the desire realm is not endowed with distress.
>
> One whose wholesome roots have been severed is not endowed with the faculties beginning with faith.
>
> Ordinary persons and those established in the fruits are not endowed with the faculty called “I shall know.”
>
> Ordinary persons, those on the path of seeing, and those on the path of no-more-training are not endowed with the faculty of knowledge.
>
> Ordinary persons and trainees are not endowed with the faculty of one who has known.
>
> In conditions not excluded by these rules, one should understand a person to be endowed with the relevant faculty.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, reporting Abhidharma and explaining its counts.

## Reconstruction

The statement that arhatship is attained by eleven faculties is cumulative, not simultaneous.

```text
one attainment-event
    = nine faculties

one person across repeated attainments
    may employ sukha, saumanasya, or upekṣā

therefore the total possible set
    = eleven faculties
```

The second half begins a new analysis of necessary co-possession.

```text
upekṣā
jīvita
manas
```

Possession of any one entails possession of all three.

This is not merely a frequently observed association. Vasubandhu states that none of the three can be possessed without the other two.

## Doctrinal Determination

The necessary triad expresses a minimum living mental continuum:

```text
jīvita
    persistence of the continuum

manas
    mental coordination

upekṣā
    unavoidable neutral feeling at the minimum level
```

The remaining faculties are contingent upon realm, path-stage, sex, meditative level, moral condition, and prior acquisition or loss.

The analysis therefore separates:

```text
necessary structural conditions
    from
variable functional equipment
```

## Organon Interpretation

This is one of the clearest passages for treating the indriyas as a functional architecture.

The minimum conditioned system is not bare cognition alone. It requires:

```text
continuity
    jīvita

coordination
    manas

affective registration
    upekṣā
```

The triad is one Idea expressed through three irreducible functions. The other faculties are optional modules conditioned by the operating domain and developmental state.

Still, the Bhāṣya does not present this as a universal theory of planning. Its immediate purpose is to calculate which faculties necessarily accompany possession of another faculty.

## New Technical Terms

- **samanvāgata** — endowed with, possessed, or presently connected with a faculty
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya classificatory term
  - **function:** marks present possession rather than mere possible existence
- **parihāya** — after falling away or losing an attainment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya path terminology
  - **function:** explains cumulative attainment of arhatship through different feeling faculties
- **aniyama** — absence of a fixed rule
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only logical qualifier in this passage
  - **function:** distinguishes contingent faculties from the necessary triad

## Compact Formula

```text
VAK 2.17 distinguishes cumulative possibility from simultaneous attainment and identifies neutral feeling, life, and mind as an inseparable minimum triad of the living mental continuum.
```
