# VAK 2.43 Bhāṣya — Nirodha-samāpatti

## Kārikā

```text
nirodhākhyā tathaiveyaṃ vihārārthaṃ bhavāgrajā /
śubhā dvivedyāniyatā cāryasya āpyā prayogataḥ // VAK 2.43 //
```

## Continuous Translation

What now is the attainment of cessation?

This attainment, called cessation, is likewise a cessation of citta and caittas. The word “likewise” carries forward that common feature from the attainment of non-ideation.

Its difference is this. The attainment of cessation is entered for the sake of abiding. Practitioners enter it through an act of attention preceded by the conception of peaceful abiding. The attainment of non-ideation, by contrast, is entered through an act of attention preceded by the conception of release.

The attainment of non-ideation belongs to the fourth dhyāna. This attainment belongs to the summit of existence: it is situated only on the level of the sphere of neither-ideation-nor-non-ideation.

It is wholesome—neither afflicted nor indeterminate. Because it is wholesome, its result can be experienced at two times and can also remain unfixed. It may be experienced in the immediately following life or in a later life. Its maturation is not necessary in every case, since one may attain final nirvāṇa in the present life. When it matures at the summit of existence, its result consists of the four immaterial aggregates.

This attainment belongs exclusively to a noble person. Ordinary persons cannot produce it because they fear annihilation; because it is produced through the power of the noble path; and because those who enter it are resolved upon nirvāṇa visible in this very life.

Even for a noble person it is not obtained merely through dispassion. How, then, is it obtained? It is acquired through deliberate practice. Neither a past instance is obtained nor is a future instance cultivated, because its cultivation depends upon the power of citta.

Is it also acquired by the Blessed One through deliberate practice? No. For the Sage it is acquired together with awakening. Buddhas acquire it at the same time as the knowledge of exhaustion. Nothing in the Buddhas is properly called the result of preparatory practice, for the manifestation of their complete qualities depends only upon their wish. Therefore all such qualities are obtained by them through the acquisition of complete dispassion.

An objection follows. If the Buddha had not previously produced the attainment of cessation, how can he be established as liberated in both respects at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion?

One answer says that mastery over the attainment is equivalent to its having been produced. Another, associated with western teachers, holds that the bodhisattva produced it earlier while still a trainee. This would also preserve the authoritative instruction attributed to the elder Upagupta: one should say that the Tathāgata produced the attainment of cessation and then produced the knowledge of exhaustion.

The Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣikas reject this. The bodhisattva does not produce it beforehand, because awakening is acquired in thirty-four moments of citta: sixteen moments of direct realization of the truths and eighteen moments connected with dispassion from the summit of existence—the nine uninterrupted paths and nine paths of liberation by which its nine grades of affliction are abandoned.

Because the bodhisattva enters certainty already dispassionate toward the sphere of nothingness, the lower levels no longer have to be abandoned. Within these thirty-four moments no dissimilar citta can intervene; therefore there is no occasion for entering the attainment of cessation.

If such a dissimilar citta did intervene, one might say that the bodhisattva intended to rise from his seat. But bodhisattvas are resolved not to rise. The teachers outside Kashmir reply that this means they do not rise from the path destroying the contaminants, not that no other attainment can intervene. The Kāśmīra teachers retain their former account.

## Argument Reconstruction

The two cessative attainments share one outward feature:

```text
asaṃjñi-samāpatti
nirodha-samāpatti
    cessation of citta and caittas
```

But their inner determinations differ completely:

```text
non-ideational attainment
    motive: imagined escape
    level: fourth dhyāna
    subject: ordinary person
    result: necessary rebirth among non-ideational beings

cessation attainment
    motive: peaceful abiding
    level: summit of existence
    subject: noble person
    result: two-times or unfixed
    basis: power of the noble path
```

The decisive difference is therefore not the bare absence of citta, but the path, intention, level, and knowledge that determine the cessation.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — organizes the comparison and reports the controversy.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — restricts prior production by the bodhisattva and gives the thirty-four-moment account.

**Voice: teachers of the western regions** — permit prior production by the bodhisattva as a trainee.

**Voice: Objector** — asks how the Buddha can be liberated in both respects without prior entry.

## New Technical Terms

- **śānta-vihāra** — peaceful abiding.
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** motive and purpose of nirodha-samāpatti
- **bhavāgra** — summit of existence.
  - **status:** previously encountered as the highest conditioned level
  - **function:** level supporting the attainment
- **dṛṣṭadharma-nirvāṇa** — nirvāṇa visible in this very life.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific here
  - **function:** explains noble eligibility
- **kṣaya-jñāna** — knowledge of exhaustion.
  - **status:** major path term
  - **function:** marks the Buddha's acquisition of the attainment
- **ubhayato-bhāga-vimukta** — liberated in both respects.
  - **status:** new in this passage
  - **function:** raises the Buddha-specific objection
- **catustriṃśat-kṣaṇa** — thirty-four moments.
  - **status:** technical path chronology
  - **function:** supports the Kāśmīra denial of prior entry

## Translation Consequences

“Cessation” alone is insufficient to distinguish this attainment from non-ideation. The translation must preserve its determination by noble path, peaceful abiding, and the summit of existence.

“Acquired through practice” applies to noble practitioners generally, but the Buddha is treated exceptionally: he acquires it with awakening, and all qualities are immediately available through mastery.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 2.43 establishes that formally identical cessation can have opposite soteriological value.

```text
same extensional description:
    no active citta or caittas

different intensional determination:
    mistaken release
    versus noble peaceful abiding
```

This is a sophisticated Dharma distinction: a state is not defined only by what is absent, but by its cause, path-basis, object of resolve, temporal result, and relation to knowledge.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

The Science of Knowing comparison becomes precise here. The path may be intensely ideational and rational even when it culminates in a state where ideational operation is suspended. What determines the truth of the cessation is the genesis through knowledge, not non-ideation taken abstractly.

```text
abstract absence of thought
    does not establish liberation

genetic mastery through the path
    determines the meaning of cessation
```
