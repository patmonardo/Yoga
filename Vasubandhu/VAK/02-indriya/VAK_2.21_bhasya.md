# VAK 2.21 Bhāṣya — The Greatest Faculty-Configurations

## Kārikā

```text
bahubhir yukta ekānnaviṃśatyāmalavarjitaiḥ /
dviliṅga āryo rāgī ekaliṅgadvayāmalavarjitaiḥ // VAK 2.21 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

> Now, one who is endowed with the greatest number of faculties—how many faculties does that person possess?
>
> One endowed with many possesses nineteen, the stainless faculties being excluded.
>
> This is a person possessing both sexual characteristics, whose faculties are complete. Such a person is endowed with nineteen faculties, because the three uncontaminated faculties are excluded.
>
> Who else possesses the greatest number?
>
> A noble disciple who still possesses attachment.
>
> Even a trainee who has not yet become free from attachment is endowed with the greatest number—nineteen faculties.
>
> In this case one sexual faculty and two stainless faculties are excluded. Of the two sexual faculties, one is excluded; and of the three uncontaminated faculties, either the faculty of one who has known or one of the other two is excluded.
>
> The detailed analysis of the faculties, introduced incidentally through their division according to the realms, has now been explained.

## Reconstruction of the Two Nineteen-Faculty Cases

The verse does not describe one single maximal being. It gives two structurally different configurations that happen to have the same total.

### 1. Complete worldly configuration

```text
all twenty-two faculties
minus the three uncontaminated realization-faculties
= nineteen
```

The subject is:

```text
dviliṅga
    possessing both sexual characteristics

samagrendriya
    having complete faculties
```

This is a maximal worldly configuration. It has extensive conditioned equipment but lacks the three uncontaminated faculties.

### 2. Noble disciple still possessing attachment

```text
all twenty-two faculties
minus one sexual faculty
minus two of the three uncontaminated faculties
= nineteen
```

The noble trainee possesses only one sexual faculty and only the realization-faculty appropriate to the present path-stage. Thus the same numerical total is reached through a different organization.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — question, explanation of both nineteen-faculty cases, and closure of the extended faculty analysis.
- No explicit objector appears in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **bahubhiḥ samanvāgata** — endowed with the greatest number of faculties.
  - **Status:** introduced in this configuration analysis.
  - **Function:** identifies a maximum co-possession profile.

- **samagrendriya** — possessing complete faculties.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya clarification absent from the compressed Kārikā.
  - **Function:** qualifies the worldly two-sexed case.

- **avītarāga śaikṣa** — a trainee not yet free from attachment.
  - **Status:** path-status clarification.
  - **Function:** identifies the noble nineteen-faculty case.

- **ekaliṅga** — possessing one sexual characteristic.
  - **Status:** explicit contrast with `dviliṅga`.
  - **Function:** supplies one exclusion in the noble configuration.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā’s second half should not be read as describing a “two-sexed noble one.” The Bhāṣya separates the clauses:

```text
dviliṅgaḥ
    belongs to the first case

āryo rāgī
    begins the second case
```

A clearer translation is therefore:

> One possessing the greatest number has nineteen, the stainless faculties being excluded: one possessing both sexual characteristics. A noble one who still has attachment also has nineteen, with one sexual faculty and two of the stainless faculties excluded.

## Doctrinal Determination

The number nineteen does not identify one essential type of person. It is produced by two different exclusion rules:

```text
worldly maximum:
    excludes awakening-faculties

noble trainee maximum:
    excludes excess sexual differentiation
    and realization-faculties inappropriate to the stage
```

Thus:

```text
same quantity
    ≠
same qualitative organization
```

The worldly person possesses more conditioned differentiation. The noble trainee possesses a path faculty but lacks other mutually incompatible realization-faculties.

## Living Buddha and the Maximum

This passage helps refine the idea of a “living Buddha.” The awakened living system should not be identified simply with the greatest numerical inventory of faculties.

The worldly two-sexed being can possess nineteen while lacking every uncontaminated realization-faculty. The noble trainee can also possess nineteen, but through a radically different structure. Numerical abundance therefore fails to measure spiritual completion.

```text
mere maximum
    = greatest conditioned co-possession

awakening
    = correct purification and organization
      of the faculties appropriate to realization
```

A living Buddha is not the being with the most faculties in an additive sense. The Buddha is the living organization in which cognition, affect, embodiment, path-capacity, and completed knowledge operate without the faults that make abandonment necessary.

## Organon Interpretation

This is an unusually clean distinction between **cardinality** and **system-form**:

```text
configuration A = nineteen
configuration B = nineteen

but

A and B differ in governing principle,
internal exclusions,
and developmental status.
```

The faculty model therefore cannot be reduced to a feature count. Its meaning lies in the relations among its components and in the principle governing the whole.

In the user’s formulation:

```text
One Idea over Many Concepts
```

must be supplemented here by:

```text
one numerical extension
may instantiate different Ideas
```

## Compact Formula

```text
VAK 2.21 presents two nineteen-faculty maxima:
a fully equipped worldly being lacking all uncontaminated faculties,
and a noble trainee who still has attachment but possesses the realization-faculty proper to the path-stage.
The same count conceals opposite forms of organization.
```
