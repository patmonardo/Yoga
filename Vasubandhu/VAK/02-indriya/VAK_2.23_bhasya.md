# VAK 2.23 Bhāṣya — Necessary Co-Arising and the Five Classes of Mental Factors

## Kārikā

```text
cittaṃ caittāḥ sahāvaśyaṃ sarvasaṃskṛtalakṣaṇaiḥ /
prāptyā vā pañcadhā caittā mahābhūmyādibhedataḥ // VAK 2.23 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

The fixed rule governing the simultaneous arising of material dharmas has been explained. The rule for the remaining dharmas must now be stated.

First of all:

**Citta and the mental factors necessarily arise together.**

For they are not capable of existing without one another.

**Everything arises together with the marks of the conditioned.**

The expression “necessarily together” is to be carried forward here. Whatever conditioned thing arises—whether material form, citta, a mental factor, or a dissociated formation—arises together with the marks of the conditioned.

**Or together with acquisition.**

Only what is designated as belonging to a sentient being arises together with acquisition; other dharmas do not. The word *vā*, “or,” marks this alternative restriction.

The term “mental factors” has now been used. What are these mental factors?

**The mental factors are fivefold according to the distinction beginning with the great-ground factors.**

There are five classes of mental factors:

1. great-ground factors,
2. wholesome great-ground factors,
3. defiled great-ground factors,
4. unwholesome great-ground factors,
5. limited-defilement great-ground factors.

A “ground” is a sphere of operation. Whatever serves as the sphere of operation of a given dharma is called that dharma’s ground.

These factors are called “great-ground factors” because their ground is extensive: they occur in every citta.

Which factors occur in every citta? That will be stated next.

## Argument Structure

### 1. Citta and caittas are mutually inseparable

The decisive statement is:

```text
nahy ete vinā anyonyaṃ bhavitum utsahante
```

They are not capable of existing without one another.

This is stronger than saying that mental factors commonly accompany citta. The Bhāṣya asserts a necessary relation:

```text
no citta without caittas
no caittas without citta
```

The mental event is therefore composite from its very arising. The text does not posit a bare citta that subsequently acquires qualities.

### 2. The conditioned marks accompany every conditioned dharma

The phrase *sarvaṃ saṃskṛtalakṣaṇaiḥ* applies universally to:

```text
rūpa
citta
caitasika
cittaviprayukta-saṃskāra
```

Whatever arises within these conditioned classes arises together with the conditioned marks. The verse is giving a co-arising rule, not merely adding another classification label.

The Bhāṣya does not enumerate the marks in this unit; it only states their universal conjunction with conditioned dharmas.

### 3. Prāpti has a narrower range

The phrase *prāptyā vā* does not mean that every conditioned dharma necessarily arises with acquisition.

The Bhāṣya restricts the rule:

```text
sattvākhyam eva
```

Only what is designated as belonging to a sentient being arises together with *prāpti*. Dharmas not belonging to a sentient continuum do not.

Thus:

```text
conditioned marks:
    universal among conditioned dharmas

prāpti:
    restricted to dharmas belonging to a sentient continuum
```

The particle *vā* signals this alternative qualification.

### 4. The fivefold division begins, but is not yet populated

The five classes are:

```text
mahābhūmika
kuśala-mahābhūmika
kleśa-mahābhūmika
akuśala-mahābhūmika
parīttakleśa-mahābhūmika
```

VAK 2.23 names the classes and defines the organizing notion of *bhūmi*. The individual factors belonging to the first class are listed only in VAK 2.24.

## Voice Analysis

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — transition from material co-arising to the co-arising of the remaining conditioned dharmas.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — assertion that citta and caittas cannot exist separately.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — restriction of *prāpti* to dharmas designated as belonging to sentient beings.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / inherited Abhidharma taxonomy** — fivefold classification of mental factors.

There is no explicit objector in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **sahotpādaniyama** — fixed rule of simultaneous arising
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya discussion surrounding VAK 2.22–2.23
  - **function:** names the necessary co-arising structure of dharmas

- **caitta / caitasika** — mental factor
  - **status:** central new term in this section
  - **function:** dharma necessarily associated with citta

- **saṃskṛtalakṣaṇa** — mark of the conditioned
  - **status:** invoked here but not yet enumerated
  - **function:** universal accompaniment of every conditioned dharma

- **prāpti** — acquisition or possession
  - **status:** previously encountered as a technical dissociated formation
  - **function:** connects a dharma with a sentient continuum; not universal to all conditioned dharmas

- **sattvākhya** — designated as belonging to a sentient being
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification in this passage
  - **function:** limits the range of *prāpti*

- **bhūmi** — ground or sphere of operation
  - **status:** explicitly defined in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** classificatory basis for the five classes of mental factors

- **gativiṣaya** — range or sphere of operation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here
  - **function:** defines what “ground” means

- **mahābhūmika** — great-ground mental factor
  - **status:** class named in the Kārikā and defined in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** factor occurring in every citta

- **kuśalamahābhūmika** — wholesome great-ground factor
  - **status:** first named in this Bhāṣya unit
  - **function:** class of factors pervasive in wholesome citta

- **kleśamahābhūmika** — defiled great-ground factor
  - **status:** first named in this Bhāṣya unit
  - **function:** class associated broadly with defiled citta

- **akuśalamahābhūmika** — unwholesome great-ground factor
  - **status:** first named in this Bhāṣya unit
  - **function:** class pervasive in unwholesome citta

- **parīttakleśamahābhūmika** — limited-defilement great-ground factor
  - **status:** first named in this Bhāṣya unit
  - **function:** class with a narrower range among defiled states

## Translation Consequences

### Citta should not be rendered as an isolated substance

The Bhāṣya explicitly denies that citta can exist without mental factors. A translation that suggests an independently existing mind subsequently modified by accidental properties would misrepresent the passage.

### “Ground” is functional, not spatial

*Bhūmi* is defined as *gativiṣaya*, a range or sphere of operation. Here it means the class of cittas across which a factor operates, not a physical location.

### “Great” means broad in range

The factors are *mahābhūmika* because they occur in every citta. “Great” refers to the extent of their operating ground.

### Prāpti is not a universal conditioned mark

The grammar could easily suggest one undifferentiated list of necessary accompaniments. The Bhāṣya prevents that reading:

```text
all conditioned dharmas arise with conditioned marks
only sentient-continuum dharmas arise with prāpti
```

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 2.23 replaces any model of a simple mental substance with a model of a necessarily articulated mental event.

```text
citta-event
    = citta
    + associated mental factors
    + conditioned marks
    + prāpti when belonging to a sentient continuum
```

The passage also opens a second level of analysis. Mental factors are not merely enumerated individually; they are grouped according to the breadth and ethical character of the cittas in which they operate.

The immediate doctrinal movement is therefore:

```text
necessary association
    → classification by operating range
```

## Restrained Analytical Note

This section is more structurally successful than the preceding faculty-count tables. It does not merely multiply classifications. It establishes dependencies:

```text
citta depends on caittas
caittas depend on citta
conditioned dharmas depend on conditioned marks
prāpti depends on belonging to a sentient continuum
mental-factor classes depend on operating range
```

The taxonomy that follows is grounded in these relations. VAK 2.24 will identify the ten factors claimed to occur in every citta.
