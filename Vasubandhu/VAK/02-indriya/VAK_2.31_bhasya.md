# VAK_2.31_bhasya — Caitta Exclusions Across the Dhyānas

## Kārikā

```text
kaukṛtyamiddhākuśalāny ādye dhyāne na santy ataḥ /
dhyānāntare vitarkaś ca vicāraś cāpy ataḥ param // VAkK_2.31 //
```

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
ya eva kāmadhātau caittānāṃ niyama uktaḥ

kaukṛtyamiddhākuśalānyādye dhyāne na santyataḥ /

ato yathoktāt kaukṛtyaṃ middhaṃ ca sarvathā nāsti prathame dhyāne yat kiñcidakuśalam /

pratighaḥ śāṭhyamadamāyāvarjyāśca krodhādaya āhrīkyānapatrāpye ca /
anyatsarva tathaiva /

ya eva prathame dhyāne na santi ta eva

dhyānāntare vitarkaśca

nāsti /
śeṣaṃ tathaiva /

vicāraścāpyataḥ param // VAkK_2.31 //

dhyānāntarāt pareṇa dvitīyādiṣu dhyāneṣvārūpyeṣu ca yathāpratiṣiddhaṃ nāsti vicāraśca /

māyā śāṭhyaṃ cetyapiśabdāt /
śeṣaṃ tathaiva /

brahmaṇo hi yāvacchāṭhyaṃ paṭhyate parṣatsambandhatvānnordhvam /

sa hi svasyāṃ parṣadyaśvajitā bhikṣuṇā praśnaṃ pṛṣṭaḥ "kutremāni brahman catavāri mahābhūtānyapariśeṣaṃ nirudhyante" iti /

aprajānan kṣepamakārṣīt /

"ahamasmin brahmā īśvaraḥ kartā nirmātā sraṣṭā sṛjaḥ pitṛbhūto bhūtānāmi"ti /

uktametadyasyāṃ bhūmau yatra citte yāvantaścaittāḥ //
```

## Continuous Translation

The fixed distribution of mental factors in the desire realm has already been stated.

Remorse, torpor, and unwholesome factors are not present in the first dhyāna.

Accordingly, among the factors already described, remorse and torpor are entirely absent from the first dhyāna, as is whatever is unwholesome: hostility; the minor afflictions beginning with anger, except crookedness, intoxication, and deception; and also shamelessness and lack of moral concern. Everything else remains as previously stated.

Those factors that are absent from the first dhyāna are likewise absent in the intermediate dhyāna, and vitarka is absent there as well. The remainder is unchanged.

Beyond this, vicāra too is absent.

Above the intermediate dhyāna—that is, in the second and subsequent dhyānas and in the formless attainments—everything previously excluded remains absent, and vicāra is absent as well. By the word “also,” deception and crookedness are also excluded. The remainder is unchanged.

Crookedness is taught only as far as Brahmā’s level, because it is connected with an assembly; it does not occur above that level. For when the monk Aśvajit asked Brahmā in his own assembly, “Where, Brahmā, do these four great elements cease without remainder?”, Brahmā did not know and made an evasive display:

“I am Brahmā, the Lord, the Maker, the Creator, the Fashioner, the Father of beings.”

Thus it has now been stated how many mental factors occur in a given citta on each bhūmi.

## Structural Analysis

VAK 2.31 completes the count-profile discussion by replacing numerical addition with progressive exclusion.

```text
Desire realm:
    vitarka and vicāra ordinarily present
    remorse, torpor, and unwholesome factors possible

First dhyāna:
    no remorse
    no torpor
    no unwholesome factors generally
    vitarka and vicāra remain

Intermediate dhyāna:
    first-dhyāna exclusions remain
    vitarka is additionally absent
    vicāra remains

Second dhyāna and above:
    previous exclusions remain
    vicāra is additionally absent
```

The Bhāṣya therefore treats contemplative ascent as a lawfully changing caitta configuration.

```text
higher bhūmi
    does not merely add superior factors

higher bhūmi
    excludes factors incompatible with its mode of collectedness
```

## Important Qualification: Unwholesome Factors in the First Dhyāna

The statement that unwholesome factors are absent is not followed mechanically. Vasubandhu names exceptions among factors that may still occur on the first-dhyāna level:

```text
śāṭhya    crookedness
mada      intoxication or conceit-like exhilaration
māyā      deception
```

These are not retained above Brahmā’s level. Their restricted range is explained socially: crookedness and deception require an audience before whom one can maintain appearances.

The Brahmā narrative is therefore doctrinal evidence, not an ornamental anecdote. Brahmā’s grand declaration avoids answering a question he cannot answer.

```text
ignorance of the answer
    +
public claim to supreme authority
    =
assembly-dependent crookedness and deception
```

The passage quietly denies that exalted cosmological rank guarantees knowledge.

## Vitarka and Vicāra

Within this Bhāṣya unit, vitarka and vicāra are treated through presence and absence rather than fully defined.

```text
first dhyāna:
    vitarka + vicāra

intermediate dhyāna:
    vicāra without vitarka

second dhyāna and above:
    neither vitarka nor vicāra
```

The text therefore distinguishes them as separable functions. Vitarka can cease while vicāra remains; vicāra ceases only at the next elevation.

This supports a graded rather than synonymous reading:

```text
vitarka
    coarser initiating engagement

vicāra
    subtler sustained examination
```

These descriptions remain provisional here because their explicit definitional dispute follows in later verses.

## Bhūmi as Operating Level

The closing sentence gathers the entire preceding inquiry:

```text
yasyāṃ bhūmau
    on whichever bhūmi

yatra citte
    in whichever citta

yāvantaś caittāḥ
    however many mental factors occur
```

Bhūmi here is an operative level that determines which configurations of citta and caitta are possible. It is not merely a spatial world and not merely an object of attention.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explains the exclusions governing the first dhyāna, intermediate dhyāna, higher dhyānas, and formless attainments.

**Voice: scriptural narrative quoted by Vasubandhu** — Aśvajit’s question and Brahmā’s evasive declaration.

**Voice: Brahmā within the narrative** — claims universal lordship instead of admitting ignorance.

## New Technical Terms

- **dhyānāntara** — the intermediate dhyāna between the first and second dhyānas; **status:** newly decisive in this passage; **function:** marks the level where vitarka has ceased but vicāra remains.
- **parṣat-sambandha** — connection with an assembly; **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanation; **function:** explains why crookedness and deception extend only as far as Brahmā’s social sphere.
- **kṣepa** — evasion, diversion, or ostentatious deflection; **status:** Bhāṣya narrative term; **function:** characterizes Brahmā’s response when he lacks knowledge.
- **yathāpratiṣiddham** — according to what has already been excluded; **status:** procedural expression; **function:** carries earlier exclusions upward through subsequent levels.

## Translation Consequences

1. `dhyānāntara` should not be translated vaguely as merely “another dhyāna.” It denotes the recognized intermediate concentration between the first and second dhyānas.
2. The verse presents a cumulative exclusion rule: each higher stage inherits the exclusions of the preceding stage unless the text says otherwise.
3. `api` in the final quarter is interpreted by the Bhāṣya to include the additional disappearance of deception and crookedness above the relevant Brahmā level.
4. Brahmā’s declaration should be translated as an evasive assertion of status, not as Vasubandhu’s endorsement of a creator deity.

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage closes the inquiry begun with the fivefold caitta classification:

```text
class of factor
    → range of occurrence

ethical type of citta
    → required count-profile

contemplative bhūmi
    → progressive exclusions
```

The result is a stage-sensitive psychology in which a citta is determined not only by what it possesses, but by what can no longer occur within it.
