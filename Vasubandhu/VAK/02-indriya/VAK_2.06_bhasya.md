# VAK_2.06 — Bhāṣya: Pravṛtti and Nivṛtti

## Kārikā Anchor

```text
pravṛtter āśrayotpattisthitipratyupabhogataḥ /
caturdaśa tathānyāni nivṛtter indriyāṇi vā // VAK 2.6 //
```

**Best current translation:**

> Or, the fourteen are faculties because they govern the support, arising, persistence, and experience of conditioned activity; in the same manner, the remaining faculties govern withdrawal from it.

## Continuous Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu introducing an alternative opinion**

> The word *vā*, “or,” indicates a difference of opinion. Others say the following.
>
> The six faculties are the support of *pravṛtti*, the active continuation of conditioned existence. The female and male faculties account for its arising, because birth proceeds from them. The life faculty accounts for its persistence, because through it conditioned existence remains in its present state. Its experience or enjoyment occurs through the feelings. These, therefore, are the fourteen faculties associated with *pravṛtti*.
>
> In precisely the same manner, the remaining faculties are associated with *nivṛtti*, withdrawal from conditioned existence. The faculties beginning with faith are the support of withdrawal. The faculty called “I shall know” is its arising. The faculty of knowledge is its persistence. Its experience is through the faculty of one who has known. For this reason there are exactly this many faculties, and for the same reason they are presented in this order.

**Voice: Vasubandhu answering the proposal that speech and the motor organs should also be faculties**

> Speech, however, is not a faculty, because speaking depends upon a particular training.
>
> Nor are the hand and foot faculties, because grasping and moving are not intrinsically different from the hand and foot. The very same material formation, when it has arisen in a different manner or in a different place, is called “grasping” or “moving.” Moreover, there is grasping and locomotion even without hands and feet, as in snakes and similar creatures.
>
> The anus is also not a faculty with respect to excretion. A heavy substance falls everywhere through space, and wind propels it.
>
> Nor is the sexual organ a faculty with respect to pleasure, because that pleasure is produced by the female and male faculties.
>
> Otherwise the throat, teeth, eyelids, and joints would also have to be regarded as faculties with respect to swallowing, chewing, opening and closing the eyes, contracting, and extending. Or indeed every cause would have to be called a faculty with respect to its own activity. It is therefore unreasonable to count speech and the other motor organs as faculties.

## Translation Notes

### 1. *Vā* marks a real alternative

The final *vā* of the Kārikā is not ornamental. Vasubandhu states:

```text
matavikalpārtho vāśabdaḥ
```

> The word *vā* indicates a difference of opinion.

VAK 2.6 therefore does not merely restate VAK 2.5. It reports another systematic justification of the same twenty-two faculties.

### 2. *Pravṛtti* is the active course of conditioned existence

The passage does not define *pravṛtti* abstractly. It analyzes it through four functions:

```text
āśraya      support
utpatti     arising
sthiti      persistence
upabhoga    experience or enjoyment
```

The fourteen faculties are distributed accordingly:

```text
support:
    five sensory faculties + mind = 6

arising:
    female and male faculties = 2

persistence:
    life faculty = 1

experience:
    five feeling faculties = 5

Total = 14
```

The six faculties support the cognitive constitution of the living being. The two sexual faculties are assigned to arising because this ancient account connects them with generation. Life sustains the resulting continuum. The five feelings provide its lived experience.

### 3. *Nivṛtti* reproduces the same fourfold structure

The remaining eight faculties do not merely oppose the first fourteen as “good” against “worldly.” They form a corresponding architecture:

```text
support of nivṛtti:
    śraddhā, vīrya, smṛti, samādhi, prajñā = 5

arising of nivṛtti:
    anājñātam-ājñāsyāmīndriya = 1

persistence of nivṛtti:
    ājñendriya = 1

experience of nivṛtti:
    ājñātāvīndriya = 1

Total = 8
```

The remarkable claim is that withdrawal from conditioned existence has its own support, arising, persistence, and experience. Liberation is not treated as a single event or simple disappearance.

### 4. *Upabhoga* means lived participation

For *pravṛtti*, experience occurs through the five feelings. For *nivṛtti*, experience occurs through the faculty of one who has known.

Thus *upabhoga* is more than passive reception. It is the lived appropriation of a mode of existence:

```text
conditioned existence is experienced through feeling
liberated withdrawal is experienced through completed knowledge
```

The previous Bhāṣya passage identified this latter experience as the joy and happiness of liberation in the present life.

## Argument Reconstruction

The alternative position argues:

```text
A complete account of conditioned existence requires:
    support
    arising
    persistence
    experience

The first fourteen faculties exhaust these functions.

A complete account of release likewise requires:
    support
    arising
    persistence
    experience

The final eight faculties exhaust these functions.

Therefore the list contains exactly twenty-two faculties,
and their sequence follows the order of these functions.
```

The second half then tests whether speech, hands, feet, anus, and sexual organ should also be counted.

The answer is negative for several different reasons:

1. **Speech depends on acquired training.** It is not a primary governing power simply by virtue of its physical basis.
2. **Grasping and locomotion are modes of bodily formation, not independent faculty-substances.**
3. **Their functions can occur without the proposed organs.** Snakes move without feet; grasping-like activity can occur without hands.
4. **Excretion is explained through gravity and wind rather than a distinct governing organ.**
5. **Sexual pleasure has already been assigned to the female and male faculties.**
6. **Unlimited inclusion would follow.** Throat, teeth, eyelids, joints, and ultimately every cause would become faculties.

The category *indriya* must therefore remain narrower than “cause,” “organ,” or “capacity for action.”

---

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya presents two symmetrical processes:

```text
pravṛtti:
    conditioned existence becomes supported,
    arises,
    persists,
    and is experienced

nivṛtti:
    withdrawal becomes supported,
    arises,
    persists,
    and is experienced
```

This is a work of Practical Reason in a precise sense: the path is not added from outside to an already complete theoretical description of existence. It has its own causal and experiential organization.

But it can also become a principle of practice:

```text
faith, energy, recollection, samādhi, and prajñā
    are the support of withdrawal

seeing what was not known
    is its arising

cultivated knowledge
    is its persistence

completed knowledge
    is its lived fruition
```

The Rational-Empirical character is strong. The structure is rationally ordered, but each stage must become operative and experienced in the continuum.

---

## Minimal Organon Contact

The important distinction is not simply activity versus inactivity.

```text
pravṛtti
    = the organized continuation of conditioned experience

nivṛtti
    = the organized practical reversal of that continuation
```

Nivṛtti is not bare negation. It possesses support, genesis, persistence, and fruition. The path is therefore a positive counter-organization of the same living continuum.

The darker Kantian question may later be whether this parallelism establishes the unity of the two movements or merely classifies them. For the present passage, however, the practical claim is enough: withdrawal must be generated, sustained, and lived.

## New Technical Terms

- **matavikalpa** — difference or variation of opinion
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** explains the force of *vā* and marks an alternative doctrinal arrangement

- **pravṛtti** — active continuation of conditioned existence
  - **status:** Kārikā term
  - **function:** names the process supported, generated, sustained, and experienced by fourteen faculties

- **nivṛtti** — withdrawal from conditioned existence
  - **status:** Kārikā term
  - **function:** names the corresponding process governed by the remaining eight faculties

- **pratyupabhoga / upabhoga** — experience, enjoyment, or lived participation
  - **status:** Kārikā term expanded in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** feeling experiences conditioned existence; completed knowledge experiences liberation

- **prabhava** — arising or origination
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** characterizes the first realization faculty as the arising of nivṛtti

- **śikṣāviśeṣāpekṣa** — dependent on a particular training
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only
  - **function:** reason speech is not counted as an independent faculty

- **ananyatva** — non-difference
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only
  - **function:** grasping and locomotion are not independent from the bodily formations called hand and foot

---

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā file translates *nivṛtti* as “withdrawal / cessation / turning back.” The Bhāṣya supports **withdrawal** or **reversal** more strongly than simple cessation, because nivṛtti itself has support, arising, persistence, and experience.

The phrase *tathānyāni* means that the remaining eight are organized **in the same fourfold manner** as the fourteen. This structural parallel should be explicit in the best translation.
