# VAK 2.53 Bhāṣya — Associated Cause and Pervasive Cause

## Kārikā

```text
saṃprayuktakahetus tu cittacaittāḥ samāśrayāḥ // VAkK_2.53 //
```

## Continuous Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu reporting the doctrinal definition**

The associated cause consists of citta and the mental factors.

If this were stated without further qualification, then citta and mental factors belonging to different times and different continua would also have to count as associated causes of one another.

Then perhaps they must have the same mode of apprehension and the same object.

But even so, the same difficulty remains.

Then perhaps they must occur at the same time.

In that case, citta and mental factors arising in different continua would still have to count as associated causes of one another—for example, in persons simultaneously looking at the new moon.

Therefore they must have:

> **the same support.**

Citta and mental factors having one and the same support are associated causes of one another.

“Same” means numerically non-different. Thus, the very moment of the eye faculty that serves as support for visual consciousness also serves as support for the feeling and the other mental factors associated with that visual consciousness. The same should be understood, up through the mental faculty, for the factors associated with mental consciousness.

Whatever is an associated cause is also a co-arisen cause.

What, then, is the distinction between being a co-arisen cause and being an associated cause?

They are co-arisen causes insofar as they are mutually one another’s results—like fellow travelers on a road who advance through one another’s strength.

They are associated causes insofar as they are conjoined through the five equalities—like those same travelers jointly engaging in the use of the same food, drink, and other provisions.

For if even one of these conditions is absent, they are not fully associated. This is what constitutes their causal character.

Thus the associated cause has been explained.

What is the pervasive cause?

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika doctrinal statement**

The dharmas called pervasive, previously arisen on their own level, are pervasive causes of later afflicted dharmas on that same level.

These pervasive dharmas will be explained later in the chapter on the latent tendencies.

The pervasive cause is distinguished from the homogeneous cause because it functions as a common cause of afflicted dharmas generally. It can also cause dharmas belonging to a different class, since through its power afflictions of another class may arise.

Does this apply even to the afflicted dharmas of a noble person?

**Voice: Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣika position**

All afflicted dharmas have as their cause dharmas to be abandoned by seeing.

The doctrinal compendia accordingly state: “Which dharmas have causes to be abandoned by seeing? Afflicted dharmas, and the maturation-result of the dharmas to be abandoned by seeing.” They also state that conditioned neutral dharmas and unwholesome dharmas have neutral causes.

The compendia further explain at length that the truth of suffering may be caused by the view of a real personality without itself causing that view, excluding from this statement the arising, aging, duration, and impermanence of those dharmas and referring to the remaining afflicted truth of suffering.

**Voice: Objector**

How, then, should one understand the statement of the *Prajñapti-bhāṣya*: “There may be an unwholesome dharma caused only by an unwholesome cause—for example, the first afflicted volition manifested by a noble person falling back from dispassion toward the desire realm”?

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika response**

That statement refers to an unabandoned cause. Its cause to be abandoned by seeing is not mentioned because it has already been abandoned.

Thus the pervasive cause has been explained.

## Argumentative Reconstruction

VAK 2.53 completes the definition of **associated cause** and begins the definition of **pervasive cause**.

The associated-cause analysis proceeds by eliminating insufficient criteria:

```text
same object alone
    is insufficient

same mode of apprehension alone
    is insufficient

same time alone
    is insufficient

same support
    is decisive
```

Two cognitive events may be simultaneous and directed toward the same object without belonging to one causal complex. The example of several persons looking at the new moon proves that empirical co-presence does not yet constitute one cognition.

The same support is therefore not merely spatial proximity. It is the numerically identical faculty-basis grounding citta and its associated mental factors as one event.

The passage also separates two causal descriptions of the same cognitive complex:

```text
co-arisen cause
    mutual efficacy / mutual result

associated cause
    full conjunction through the five equalities
```

The first concerns reciprocal causal power. The second concerns the structured identity of the cognitive event.

The pervasive cause then introduces a different causal pattern. It is not merely like producing like. A previously arisen pervasive affliction can generate later afflicted dharmas broadly, including afflictions of another class. It is therefore isolated from homogeneous cause as the common causal basis of contamination.

## The Five Equalities

The phrase “five equalities” is presupposed rather than enumerated in this local passage. In the established citta-caitta analysis, association requires equality of:

- support,
- object,
- mode of apprehension,
- time,
- and the relevant event or substantial occurrence.

The decisive addition in VAK 2.53 is **numerical identity of support**. Similarity of support is insufficient.

## New Technical Terms

- **samāśraya** — having one and the same support
  - **status:** decisive specification added in this passage
  - **function:** excludes merely simultaneous cognitions in different continua from association

- **pañca-samatā** — five equalities
  - **status:** presupposed technical doctrine
  - **function:** defines full citta-caitta association

- **sarvatraga-hetu** — pervasive cause
  - **status:** newly defined cause-type
  - **function:** common cause of later afflicted dharmas on the same level

- **nikāyāntarīya** — belonging to another class
  - **status:** technical extension of pervasive causality
  - **function:** shows that pervasive cause is broader than homogeneous cause

- **aprahīṇa-hetu** — unabandoned cause
  - **status:** clarification introduced in resolving the textual objection
  - **function:** distinguishes an operative present cause from a cause already abandoned by seeing

## Translation Consequences

### 1. “Same support” must mean numerical identity

The Bhāṣya explicitly rejects a merely qualitative sameness. Citta and its mental factors form one associated complex because they depend upon the very same moment of the faculty.

### 2. Association is stronger than simultaneity

Several subjects may perceive the same object at the same time, but their cognitions are not associated with one another. Association is intra-event, not merely inter-event resemblance.

### 3. One complex admits multiple causal descriptions

Citta and caittas are co-arisen causes insofar as they mutually sustain one another, and associated causes insofar as they satisfy the full identity-profile of one cognitive event.

### 4. Pervasive causality is contamination-wide

The pervasive cause explains why affliction reproduces more than its exact likeness. It supplies the general causal atmosphere from which later contaminated states arise.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 2.53 gives a genetic criterion for the unity of cognition:

```text
one cognitive event
    requires
one numerically identical support
    plus
shared object, mode, time, and event-profile
```

This is stronger than a bundle account. The event is not assembled from independent factors afterward; its factors are originally co-grounded through one support and one association-structure.

The pervasive-cause doctrine adds a second form of rational determination:

```text
homogeneous cause
    reproduces similarity

pervasive cause
    reproduces contamination across classes
```

Thus the causal system distinguishes exact genetic continuity from the broader transmissibility of affliction.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This passage is exceptionally useful for the Rational–Empirical distinction.

Empirically, several cognitions can be simultaneous, share an object, and even share an apparent mode. Rational judgment nevertheless distinguishes whether they belong to one event by identifying the prior immediate connection of a numerically identical support.

```text
empirical conjunction
    same object + same time

rational unity
    same numerically identical support
    + fivefold equality
```

The judgment becomes self-certain when the appearance is traced to the ground that makes it one event rather than several resembling events.

The pervasive cause then shows the darker side of genetic form: a rational ground need not reproduce only an identical product. A pervasive affliction can determine a whole field of heterogeneous contaminated appearances. This is the causal logic of avidyā operating through relative reality.

The Organon consequence should remain restrained:

> The unity of experience is not inferred from resemblance alone. It is genetically judged from the identical support and the structured equalities that constitute one cognitive event.
