# VAK_2.65_bhasya — Material Causal Matrix of Bhūta and Bhautika

## Kārikā

### Devanāgarī

```text
द्विधा भूतानि तद्धेतुः भौतिकस्य तु पञ्चधा ।
त्रिधा भौतिकमन्योन्यं भूतानामेकधैव तत् ॥ VAkK_2.65 ॥
```

### IAST

```text
dvidhā bhūtāni taddhetuḥ bhautikasya tu pañcadhā /
tridhā bhautikam anyonyaṃ bhūtānām ekadhaiva tat // VAkK_2.65 //
```

### Working Translation

The great elements are causes of the great elements in two ways; of derived matter, in five ways.
Derived matter is cause of other derived matter in three ways; of the great elements, in one way only.

---

## Bhāṣya Translation

The question is raised: **In what way are the great elements the cause-condition of the great elements?**

They are twofold as causes of the great elements: by the homogeneous cause (*sabhāga-hetu*) and the co-arisen cause (*sahabhū-hetu*).

But the great elements are causes of derived matter in five ways.

How so?

> By generation, by serving as support, by serving as basis, by sustaining, and by nourishing.

This is the conditioning cause (*kāraṇa-hetu*) itself, differentiated fivefold.

They are **generative causes** because derived matter arises from them.

They are **supporting causes** because, once derived matter has arisen, it conforms to the great elements, just as a result produced through activity depends upon such supports as a teacher and the like.

They are **basis-causes** because they function as the foundation or locus, like the surface upon which a picture is made.

They are **sustaining causes** because they prevent interruption or destruction.

Thus the great elements are declared causes with respect to the generation, modification, basis, persistence, and increase of derived matter.

Derived matter, in turn, is cause of other derived matter in three ways: by the co-arisen cause, the homogeneous cause, and the maturation cause. The conditioning cause is not separately counted because of its non-specific character.

Among these, derived bodily and verbal action that follows citta is mutually a co-arisen cause; other forms of appropriated matter are not so.

All previously arisen derived matter is a homogeneous cause of derived matter of the same kind.

Derived matter is a maturation cause when bodily or verbal action has as its maturation the eye faculty and the other faculties.

But derived matter is a cause of the great elements in only one way: it is a maturation cause when the great elements themselves are the maturation-result of bodily or verbal action.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma analysis**

The passage is primarily classificatory and explanatory. No substantial objection-answer exchange occurs inside this short unit; the argumentative work lies in distinguishing the exact modes of material causation rather than treating “material cause” as a single relation.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **janana-hetu** — generative cause
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification in this passage
  - **function:** explains arising of derived matter from the great elements

- **niśraya-hetu** — supporting/dependent-support cause
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification in this passage
  - **function:** explains the conformity of already arisen derived matter to the great elements

- **pratiṣṭhā-hetu** — basis/foundation cause
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification in this passage
  - **function:** explains the role of the great elements as the locus or foundation of derived matter

- **upastambha-hetu** — sustaining cause
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification in this passage
  - **function:** explains non-interruption and persistence of derived matter

- **bṛṃhaṇa** — nourishing / increasing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only functional term in this passage
  - **function:** marks increase or augmentation of derived matter

- **bhūtānuvidhāyin** — conforming to / following the great elements
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expression in this passage
  - **function:** characterizes dependence of derived matter upon its elemental support

## Translation Consequences

The verse is not merely a numerical table. The Bhāṣya shows that the same broad causal category can be internally differentiated according to **function**.

The fivefold causality of the great elements toward derived matter is especially important:

```text
bhūta → bhautika
    generation
    support
    basis
    sustaining
    nourishment
```

These are not five independent substances or five additional ultimate causes. They are five determinate functions of the broader *kāraṇa-hetu* relation.

The verse therefore gives a genuinely structured material ontology:

```text
bhūta → bhūta
    sahabhū
    sabhāga

bhūta → bhautika
    kāraṇa, differentiated fivefold

bhautika → bhautika
    sahabhū
    sabhāga
    vipāka

bhautika → bhūta
    vipāka only
```

---

## Doctrinal Determination

The striking point is that **materiality is not explained by one undifferentiated material relation**.

The great elements are productive not merely because they stand “underneath” derived matter, but because they perform several irreducible functions in its genesis and persistence:

1. they generate it;
2. they provide the support upon which its character depends;
3. they provide its basis or locus;
4. they maintain its continuity;
5. they permit its increase.

Derived matter is also not inert. In limited ways it becomes causally productive in its own right, especially through homogeneous continuation, co-arising, and karmic maturation.

Thus a produced material determination can itself become a productive ground.

```text

```

## Limited Organon Interpretation

For the Organon, this is best read as a **metaphysical derivation of elemental materiality prior to the full constitution of a loka**.

The previous verse rejected explanation by a single invariant world-cause. VAK 2.65 now replaces that rejected abstraction with a differentiated causal architecture internal to material manifestation itself.

```text
no single undifferentiated world-cause
        ↓
differentiated causal functions
        ↓
material constitution
        ↓
only later: organized loka
```

This fits the emerging Rational–Empirical distinction without attributing that vocabulary directly to Vasubandhu:

```text
Rational determination
    = the specific lawful relation by which a material factor functions

Empirical manifestation
    = the arisen elemental or derived-material configuration
```

The material world is therefore intelligible not because an external metaphysical principle is appended to it, but because its own production is articulated through determinate causal roles.

A useful formulation for the present project is:

> **The elements are not merely the stuff of a world; they are already a system of productive relations from which a world can later be constituted.**

This is why VAK 2.65 belongs logically before a full theory of loka.

## Relation to the Previous Pratyaya Discussion

VAK 2.64 established that conditioned arising requires differentiated conditions rather than a single creator-principle.

VAK 2.65 supplies a concrete material example of that doctrine:

```text
Pratyaya logic
    differentiated conditions of arising

Material logic
    differentiated causal functions of bhūta and bhautika
```

The transition is therefore from the **formal condition-profile of arising** to the **material causal articulation of what arises**.
