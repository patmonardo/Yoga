# VAK_2.01 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> caturṣv artheṣu pañcānām ādhipatyaṃ dvayoḥ kila |
> caturṇāṃ pañcakāṣṭānāṃ saṃkleśavyavadānayoḥ || VAK 2.1 ||

**Best current translation:**

> The first five possess governing efficacy with respect to four functions; the two, according to one position, and the following four each possess it with respect to two. The fivefold and eightfold groups govern defilement and purification respectively.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_2.01.md`](./VAK_2.01.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
uktānīndriyāṇi /
kaḥ punarindriyārthaḥ /
"idi paramaiśvarye" /
tasya indantīti indriyāṇi /
ata ādhipatyārtha indriyārthaḥ /
kasya caiṣāṃ kvādhipatyam /
```

## Translation

> The faculties have already been stated. But what is the meaning of *indriya*? The verbal root *id* has the meaning of supreme governance. Those which exercise that governance are called *indriyas*. Therefore, the meaning of *indriya* is governing efficacy. Of which among these is there governing efficacy, and with respect to what?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

## The First Five Faculties

The eye and ear each possess governing efficacy with respect to four functions:

1. the attractiveness or integrity of embodied existence, because blindness and deafness affect bodily appearance;
2. the protection of embodied existence, because dangers are avoided after they have been seen or heard;
3. the arising of visual and auditory knowledge together with their associated mental factors;
4. being the specific causes of seeing visible forms and hearing sounds.

The nose, tongue, and body possess the same fourfold efficacy:

1. attractiveness of embodied existence;
2. maintenance of embodied existence through the consumption of material nourishment;
3. the arising of olfactory, gustatory, and bodily knowledge together with associated mental factors;
4. being the specific causes of smelling, tasting, and touching.

## The Four Following Faculties

The four are the female faculty, male faculty, life faculty, and mind faculty. Each is assigned governing efficacy with respect to two functions.

### Female and Male Faculties

The female and male faculties govern:

- **sattva-bheda** — differentiation of beings as female and male;
- **sattva-vikalpa** — differentiated configuration in bodily form, voice, and conduct.

**Voice:** reported doctrinal position

Others say that these two govern defilement and purification, since beings lacking these determinations are excluded, according to the ancient classification, from certain forms of non-restraint, immediate-result action, severance of wholesome roots, restraint, attainment of fruits, and dispassion.

**Voice:** alternative doctrinal position

### Life Faculty

> jīvitendriyasya nikāyasabhāgasaṃbandhasādhāraṇayoḥ /

The life faculty governs connection with, and maintenance within, the homogeneous class of embodied existence. The compact source is textually difficult, so this translation remains provisional.

### Mind Faculty

> manaindriyasya punarbhavasaṃbandhavaśibhāvānuvarttanayoḥ /

The mind faculty governs:

1. connection with renewed existence;
2. the continuation of directive mastery.

The first is illustrated by the mental orientation of the *gandharva* toward renewed existence; the second by the scriptural statement that “this world is led by mind.”

## The Five and the Eight

The five feeling faculties beginning with pleasure possess governing efficacy with respect to defilement because attachment and the other afflictions lie latent in dependence upon them.

The eight faculties beginning with faith possess governing efficacy with respect to purification because through them purification occurs.

Others maintain that the feeling faculties also govern purification, since “the mind of one who is happy becomes concentrated.” The Vaibhāṣika response points to a conditioned sequence in which suffering can become the proximate condition for faith and for factors associated with renunciation.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
The term indriya requires a common definition.
    ↓
Its meaning is ādhipatya: governing efficacy.
    ↓
Governing efficacy always exists with respect to a function.
    ↓
The first five govern embodied and cognitive functions.
    ↓
The following four govern differentiation, continuity, renewed existence,
and directive mastery.
    ↓
The five feelings principally govern defilement.
    ↓
The eight beginning with faith govern purification.
    ↓
Yet feeling can also enter the genesis of purification.
```

## Logical Determination

```text
indriya
    =
a dharma insofar as it exercises ādhipatya
with respect to a determinate function
```

Facultyhood is relational rather than substantial. A dharma is not first an independently complete entity and only afterward assigned a function. It is determined as a faculty through the governing relation in which it operates.

## Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya turns the compressed classification of the Kārikā into a living argument. It does not merely list faculties but asks what unifies them and why different powers deserve the same name. The governing relation mediates between a dharma and the field it organizes.

This should not yet be identified directly with Sāṃkhya’s guṇas, but it supplies the correct form of the later question: how can an objective determination govern the manifestation and knowledge of its field?

## New Technical Terms

- **paramaiśvarya** — supreme governance; introduced in the etymology of *indriya*; definition.
- **ādhipatya** — governing efficacy; Kārikā term explicitly defined by the Bhāṣya; common principle of facultyhood.
- **ātmabhāva-śobhā** — attractiveness or integrity of embodied existence; Bhāṣya-only; first sensory function.
- **ātmabhāva-parikarṣaṇa** — protection or maintenance of embodied existence; Bhāṣya-only; second sensory function.
- **asādhāraṇa-kāraṇatva** — being a specific or non-common cause; Bhāṣya-only; fourth sensory function.
- **sattva-bheda** — differentiation of living beings; Bhāṣya-only; sexual differentiation.
- **sattva-vikalpa** — differentiated configuration of living beings; Bhāṣya-only; bodily, vocal, and behavioral articulation.
- **punarbhava-saṃbandha** — connection with renewed existence; Bhāṣya-only; function of mind.
- **vaśibhāva-anuvartana** — continuation of directive mastery; Bhāṣya-only; function of mind.
- **anuśāyitva** — lying latent in dependence upon something; reflective vocabulary explaining the relation of feeling to affliction.

## Translation Consequences

The Bhāṣya confirms the working translation:

```text
ādhipatya = governing efficacy
indriya = faculty
```

It also shows that the division between defilement and purification is functional and principal rather than an absolute separation of two unrelated classes.
