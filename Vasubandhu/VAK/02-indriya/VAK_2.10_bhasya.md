# VAK_2.10_bhasya — Faculties as Maturation and as Producers of Maturation

## Kārikā

```text
vipāko jīvitaṃ dvedhā dvādaśāntyāṣṭakād ṛte /
daurmanasyāc ca tat tv ekaṃ savipākaṃ daśa dvidhā // VAK 2.10 //
```

## Working Translation of the Kārikā

```text
The life faculty is maturation. Twelve are twofold, excluding the final eight
and distress. Distress alone invariably has maturation as its result.
Ten are twofold with respect to producing maturation.
```

The verse uses two distinct questions:

```text
Is a faculty itself vipāka, a matured result?

Does a faculty have vipāka, that is, can it produce future maturation?
```

These must not be confused.

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu presenting the Vaibhāṣika classification**

> The life faculty is maturation-result.
>
> Twelve faculties are twofold: they may be maturation or not maturation. Which twelve? Excluding the final eight faculties and the faculty of distress, the twelve other than the life faculty are twofold.
>
> Among the seven faculties beginning with the eye, those produced by development or nourishment are not maturation; the remainder are maturation.
>
> Mind, pain, pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling, when wholesome or afflicted, are not maturation. Those connected, as appropriate, with deportment, craftsmanship, magical creation, and similar neutral activities are likewise not maturation. Their remaining instances are maturation.
>
> It follows that, apart from the life faculty and these twelve, the remaining faculties are not maturation.
>
> But if the faculty of distress is not maturation, how is the sūtra to be understood which speaks of three kinds of action: action to be experienced as gladness, action to be experienced as distress, and action to be experienced as neutral feeling?
>
> That statement concerns the feeling associated with the action. An action associated with distress is called “to be experienced as distress,” just as contact associated with pleasure is called “to be experienced as pleasure.”
>
> Then the actions to be experienced as gladness and neutral feeling must be understood in the same way.
>
> Let it be so, if you wish. There is no fault in understanding the statement through association, nor is there a fault in understanding it through maturation where that is possible. Necessity, however, requires the former interpretation in the case of distress.
>
> What reasoning establishes that distress is not maturation?
>
> Distress is produced and calmed by particular conceptual constructions. Maturation-result does not behave in this way.
>
> Would it not follow that gladness is likewise not maturation?
>
> If distress were maturation, then when one who has committed an immediate offense produced distress on account of that deed, the deed would already have matured. The same objection may be made concerning gladness: if gladness were invariably maturation, then whenever one who had performed meritorious action felt gladness because of it, that action would already have matured.
>
> But those free from attachment cannot produce distress, whereas maturation-result is not excluded from them merely for that reason. Their gladness, moreover, may be indeterminate. What sort of maturation would that be?
>
> Let it be of whatever sort it is.
>
> Nevertheless, because gladness can occur in forms that leave room for maturation, it may be maturation. Distress has no such possibility, because it never occurs in those free from attachment. Therefore distress is never maturation. This is the Vaibhāṣika position.
>
> Among these faculties, the group of eight ending with the life faculty is the result of wholesome action in a favorable destiny and of unwholesome action in an unfavorable destiny. The mind faculty may be the result of either wholesome or unwholesome action in either kind of destiny. Pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling are results of wholesome action. Pain is a result of unwholesome action.
>
> In a favorable destiny, the acquisition of the bodily basis of one possessing both sexual characteristics is caused by unwholesome action. The faculty itself, however, is not therefore unwholesome maturation, because the embodied existence as a whole was projected by wholesome action. This matter is concluded.
>
> It must now be stated how many faculties possess maturation as a future result and how many do not.
>
> The faculty of distress just discussed alone invariably possesses maturation as its result. The word “but” has the force of “only” and is to be construed out of its metrical order. Distress is neither indeterminate nor without outflows, because it is never concentrated. Therefore there is no instance of distress that does not possess maturation as its result.
>
> Ten faculties are twofold: they may possess maturation as their result or may not. Which ten? Mind, the other four feelings apart from distress, and the five beginning with faith.
>
> Mind, pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling, when unwholesome or wholesome with outflows, possess maturation as their result. When without outflows or indeterminate, they do not.
>
> Pain, when wholesome or unwholesome, possesses maturation as its result; when indeterminate, it does not.
>
> The faculties beginning with faith, when with outflows, possess maturation as their result; when without outflows, they do not.
>
> It follows that all the remaining faculties do not possess maturation as their result.

## 1. Two Different Uses of Vipāka

The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
vipāka
    a dharma that is itself a matured karmic result

savipāka
    a dharma capable of producing a future matured result
```

A faculty can therefore be:

```text
maturation without producing new maturation
not maturation but productive of maturation
neither
or, according to its mode, capable of either classification
```

This is not a single linear causal label. It is a two-axis model.

## 2. Why Life Is Always Maturation

The life faculty is not a voluntary act and not a path-practice. It is the matured support of a presently existing continuum.

```text
past karma
    -> matured embodied series
        -> jīvita maintains its duration
```

Thus life is classified categorically as `vipāka`.

## 3. The Twelve Twofold Faculties

Excluding:

```text
the final eight:
    śraddhā
    vīrya
    smṛti
    samādhi
    prajñā
    the three realization faculties

and distress

and treating life separately
```

the remaining twelve may be either maturation or non-maturation:

```text
seven material faculties:
    eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, female, male

mind
pain
pleasure
gladness
neutral feeling
```

The same named faculty is therefore not assigned one fixed karmic status. Its status depends on its mode of production and operation.

For example, an eye-faculty produced as part of the karmically projected organism is maturation; an eye strengthened through present nourishment or development is not itself maturation.

## 4. Why Distress Is Never Maturation

The crucial argument is:

```text
distress arises and subsides through parikalpa-viśeṣa
particular constructions or interpretations

vipāka does not arise and cease merely through such conceptual alteration
```

Distress is therefore too dependent on present interpretive activity to be a passively matured result.

This does not make it causally harmless. Quite the reverse: because distress is always afflicted and never uncontaminated or indeterminate, it invariably produces future maturation.

```text
daurmanasya:
    never vipāka
    always savipāka
```

That is the sharpest distinction in the passage.

## 5. One Faculty, Several Causal Roles

The faculty-model now becomes explicitly multidimensional:

```text
faculty identity
    + ethical quality
    + contaminated/uncontaminated status
    + mode of production
    + present-result status
    + future-result productivity
```

This supports reading the indriya as a higher functional model rather than a simple substance. One faculty-Idea can govern many concrete instances whose causal statuses differ.

## 6. Practical Significance

The passage does not treat feeling merely as something suffered. Mental feeling can itself become karma-producing activity.

```text
feeling as matured result
    what the continuum receives

feeling as savipāka
    what the continuum actively carries forward
```

Distress is especially revealing: although it is not received as karmic maturation, the way the mind constructs and sustains distress becomes productive of future consequence.

This is a direct point of practice without requiring an external moralizer. The present organization of experience is itself causally efficacious.

## New Technical Terms

- **vipāka** — maturation-result; a presently arisen dharma produced by past karma
  - **status:** central classification term
  - **function:** identifies a faculty as received karmic result

- **savipāka** — possessing maturation as a future result
  - **status:** distinct from `vipāka`
  - **function:** identifies a faculty as karmically productive

- **avipāka** — not maturation-result
  - **status:** Bhāṣya classification
  - **function:** denies present result-status

- **upacayika** — produced or augmented through nourishment/development
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** explains non-maturation instances of material faculties

- **airyāpathika** — connected with bodily deportment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** indeterminate activity, hence not maturation

- **śailpasthānika** — connected with craft or skilled activity
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** indeterminate operational mode

- **nairmāṇika** — connected with magical creation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** another indeterminate mode of mind and feeling

- **saṃprayoga-vedanīya** — to be experienced through association
  - **status:** interpretive distinction used in resolving a sūtra
  - **function:** explains why action may be called “distress-experiencing” without distress itself being maturation

- **parikalpa-viśeṣa** — particular conceptual construction or interpretation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** explains the arising and calming of distress

## Compact Determination

```text
jīvita:
    always vipāka

 twelve faculties:
    either vipāka or avipāka

daurmanasya:
    never vipāka
    always savipāka

mind + four other feelings + five pure faculties:
    either savipāka or avipāka
```
