# VAK_2.61_bhasya — Opening the Four Pratyayas

## Kārikā

### IAST

```text
catvāraḥ pratyayā uktāḥ /
hetvākhyaḥ pañca hetavaḥ // VAkK_2.61 //
```

### Working Translation

> Four conditions (*pratyaya*) are taught. Five causes are designated the cause-condition (*hetu-pratyaya*).

> **Textual note:** the existing `VAK_2.61.md` combines material belonging to the close of VAK 2.60 with VAK 2.61. The Bhāṣya source marks the actual opening of 2.61 at `catvāraḥ pratyayā uktāḥ / hetvākhyaḥ pañca hetavaḥ`.

## Bhāṣya Translation

**What are the conditions (*pratyaya*)?**

Four conditions are taught.

Where are they taught? In the sūtra:

> “There are four kinds of conditionality: cause-condition (*hetu-pratyayatā*), immediately antecedent condition (*samanantara-pratyayatā*), object-condition (*ālambana-pratyayatā*), and dominant condition (*adhipati-pratyayatā*).”

“Conditionality” (*pratyayatā*) means a class or kind of condition.

Of these, five causes—excluding the conditioning cause (*kāraṇa-hetu*)—constitute the **cause-condition** (*hetu-pratyaya*).

All arisen minds and mental factors, except the final ones of an Arhat, are the **immediately antecedent condition** (*samanantara-pratyaya*).

It is called *samanantara-pratyaya* because it is both equal/continuous (*sama*) and immediately successive (*anantara*).

For this very reason, material form is not an immediately antecedent condition, because its arising is irregular. After form belonging to the desire realm, for example, there may arise desire-realm form together with form-realm non-informative form, or desire-realm form together with uncontaminated non-informative form. The manifestation of form is therefore disordered in this respect.

**Venerable Vasumitra:** The immediately antecedent condition is not disordered, because before one moment of accumulated material form has ceased, another arises in the same continuum.

**Another elder:** Material form also varies quantitatively. Sometimes a small amount arises from a large amount, as ashes from a heap of straw; sometimes a large amount from a small amount, as the great banyan tree develops gradually from a tiny seed.

**Objector:** But mental factors too can arise in greater or lesser numbers—for example in wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate minds, and in the three concentrations with or without applied and sustained thought.

**Response:** Such variation occurs between different kinds, not within one and the same kind. There is never a plurality of feelings arising as one moment of feeling, nor likewise of perception and the other factors. The whole mental complex is the immediately antecedent condition for the whole succeeding complex; it is not that a smaller number of units of feeling gives rise to a greater number of units of feeling.

Some who maintain continuity by homogeneous series hold that each factor is immediately antecedent only to another of its own kind—mind to mind, feeling to feeling, and so forth. But this will not work. If an afflicted state arises after a non-afflicted state, one would then have to posit some previously ceased affliction as the immediately antecedent condition of the later affliction, just as one might posit the mind preceding the attainment of cessation as condition for the mind that emerges from it. This position is rejected, among other reasons because it would make the first uncontaminated mind impossible.

Nor are dissociated formations immediately antecedent conditions, because their manifestation is likewise irregular and multiple.

Why is the future not accepted as an immediately antecedent condition? Because the future series is unordered: prior and posterior are not yet fixed there.

An objection is raised: how then does the Buddha know that one future event will follow another? Several explanations are reported. One appeals to inference from past and present causal patterns; another posits a special formation in the continuum serving as a sign of the future. The Sautrāntikas reject the need for such constructions and hold that the Buddha knows whatever he wills to know; the sphere of a Buddha is declared inconceivable.

The fixed succession of path-moments does not require a pre-existing future order. Whatever has its arising dependent upon a given condition arises immediately after that condition, just as a sprout follows from a seed without requiring an immediately antecedent mental condition.

Why are the final mind and mental factors of an Arhat not immediately antecedent conditions? Because no further mind is connected with them.

They still count as mind-base (*manas*) because that status is defined by being a support, not by actually exercising the function of immediately producing another consciousness. Another consciousness does not arise simply because the other required conditions are absent.

By contrast, the immediately antecedent condition is defined by operative efficacy. Once a dharma has taken its fruit under this condition, no dharma and no living being can prevent that fruit from arising.

The Bhāṣya then distinguishes being “immediately after mind” from being “without another mind intervening,” and similarly analyzes emergence from a non-cognitive attainment. A mind arising after such an attainment can still be called immediately successive to the earlier mind because **no other mind has intervened**, even though a long interval of non-mental factors has occurred.

Thus the immediately antecedent condition is explained.

Next:

> **All dharmas are object-condition (*ālambana-pratyaya*).**

According to the relevant consciousness: visible form is the object of eye-consciousness and its associated factors; sound of ear-consciousness; smell of nose-consciousness; taste of tongue-consciousness; tangibles of body-consciousness; and **all dharmas** are objects of mental consciousness.

A dharma that is the object of another dharma does not cease to count as its object merely because it is not presently being apprehended. It retains that object-character by its nature, just as wood and similar material may still be called fuel even when it is not presently burning.

Mind and mental factors are therefore fixed with respect to their objects according to the determinate character of their sense-field, substance, and defining mark. They are also fixed with respect to their supports when arisen; unarisen future events have not yet joined a support, whereas past events have become separated from it. Some, however, maintain that even past events remain connected with their support.

Thus the object-condition is explained.

## Voice Map

- **Vasubandhu:** principal exposition and criticism of inadequate accounts of immediate succession.
- **Vaibhāṣika framework:** fourfold pratyaya classification and much of the three-times causal discussion.
- **Venerable Vasumitra:** defense of material continuity against the exclusion of rūpa from immediate antecedence.
- **Other elder(s):** quantitative argument about material arising.
- **Continuity-by-homogeneous-series school:** own-kind-only theory of immediate succession; rejected.
- **Sautrāntika position:** future cognition of the Buddha does not require reified future ordering; Buddha’s cognitive domain is inconceivable.
- **Objector:** several dialectical challenges concerning variation, futurity, and Arhat final cognition.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratyayatā** — “conditionality,” the class-character of being a condition.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-explicit formulation.  
  **Function:** defines the fourfold condition-system at the level of kinds.

- **hetu-pratyaya** — cause-condition.  
  **Status:** newly explicit in the pratyaya section.  
  **Function:** gathers five of the six hetus under one conditional relation; excludes *kāraṇa-hetu* here because that becomes *adhipati-pratyaya*.

- **samanantara-pratyaya** — immediately antecedent / equal-and-immediately-successive condition.  
  **Status:** new central term.  
  **Function:** determines lawful mental succession; excludes irregularly arising material formations.

- **ālambana-pratyaya** — object-condition.  
  **Status:** new central term.  
  **Function:** determines the object-side of cognition according to the relevant consciousness.

- **adhipati-pratyaya** — dominant condition.  
  **Status:** named here; defined fully in VAK 2.62.  
  **Function:** fourth condition-type and the pratyaya-form corresponding to *kāraṇa-hetu*.

- **kāritra** — operative efficacy / causal function.  
  **Status:** important Bhāṣya term in the succession discussion.  
  **Function:** distinguishes merely being a support from actually functioning as an immediately antecedent condition.

- **cittāntarāvyavahita** — not interrupted by another mind.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation.  
  **Function:** explains how emergence-cognition after a non-cognitive attainment can still be immediately successive to the earlier cognition.

## Doctrinal Determination

The move from *hetu* to *pratyaya* is not repetition.

The six hetus classified productive grounds according to **how a result is generated**. The four pratyayas classify the **relational conditions under which arising is determinate**.

The opening mapping is:

```text
five hetus except kāraṇa-hetu
    -> hetu-pratyaya

preceding citta-caitta complex
    -> samanantara-pratyaya

object appropriate to cognition
    -> ālambana-pratyaya

kāraṇa-hetu
    -> adhipati-pratyaya (defined next)
```

The decisive advance is especially clear in the mental case. Mere chronological proximity is insufficient for *samanantara*. The relation must possess lawful succession: the preceding complex must stand to the succeeding complex as the immediately operative condition of its arising.

Likewise, *ālambana* is not a private psychological image. It is an objective relation specified by the type of cognition: visible form for eye-consciousness, sound for ear-consciousness, and ultimately all dharmas for mental consciousness.

---

## Translation Consequences

1. **Pratyaya should remain “condition” rather than be collapsed into “cause.”** The section exists precisely because conditionality and causal ground are not identical classifications.
2. **Samanantara is stronger than “previous.”** It means a specifically lawful and uninterrupted succession relation.
3. **Ālambana should be translated “object-condition” rather than merely “object.”** The dharma is being classified by the role it plays in cognition.
4. **The mental complex is the unit of succession.** Vasubandhu resists atomizing the succession into feeling-to-feeling, perception-to-perception, etc.
5. **Future order is not simply presupposed.** The Bhāṣya explicitly asks how determinacy of succession is possible without reifying an already ordered future.

## Organon Interpretation

This is the first genuinely **qualitative condition-logic** after the quantitative/genetic analysis of hetu-phala.

```text
Hetu
    asks: from what productive ground?

Pratyaya
    asks: under what determinate relation can this arise or appear?
```

The first two developed conditions reveal two poles of rational subjectivity:

```text
samanantara-pratyaya
    temporal-formal continuity of the cognitive subject

ālambana-pratyaya
    determinate object-relation of that cognitive subject
```

This gives a disciplined version of the proposed Yoga parallel:

```text
hetu
    empirical-quantitative objectivity
    productive determination

pratyaya
    rational-empirical qualitative subjectivity
    conditioned mode of manifestation
```

The striking point is that *pratyaya* is not subjective arbitrariness. It is **lawful subjectivity**. A cognition has a determinate predecessor, a determinate support, and a determinate object-domain. The Bhāṣya spends most of this unit ruling out merely accidental succession.

That makes *samanantara-pratyaya* a particularly strong model of what we have called a **Qualitative Syllogism**: a later cognitive determination is warranted as belonging to the same rational continuum only when the transition itself possesses lawful form.

In the language developed for the project:

> Hetu gives the productive ground; pratyaya gives the lawful form under which that ground can become manifest for a rational-empirical subject.

This is why the hetu–pratyaya dyad is a plausible structural analogue for Samādhi in the Organon reconstruction: not because the Kośa itself equates them, but because their unity comprehends productive objectivity and conditioned qualitative subjectivity in one determinate cognitive event.
