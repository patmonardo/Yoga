# VAK_2.24 Bhāṣya — The Ten Universal Mental Factors

## Kārikā

```text
vedanā cetanā saṃjñā cchandaḥ sparśo matiḥ smṛtiḥ /
manaskāro 'dhimokṣaś ca samādhiḥ sarvacetasi // VAkK_2.24 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

Which factors occur in every citta?

Feeling, volition, recognition, desire-to-act, contact, discernment, recollection, attention, resolution, and concentration occur in every citta.

These ten dharmas, it is said, are present together in every moment of citta.

Feeling is threefold experience: pleasant, painful, or neither-painful-nor-pleasant.

Volition is the formative activity of citta; it is mental action.

Recognition is recognition itself: the apprehension of the distinguishing mark of an object.

Desire-to-act is the wish to perform an action.

Contact is the touching born from the conjunction of faculty, object, and cognition.

Discernment is prajñā: discrimination among dharmas.

Recollection is the non-loss of the object-support.

Attention is the directing or application of citta.

Resolution is decisive commitment.

Concentration is the one-pointedness of citta.

The distinctions among citta and the mental factors are subtle. They are difficult to determine even across streams, all the more so within individual moments.

Even among material medicinal plants having many tastes, certain differences of taste that are perceptible to the senses are difficult to determine. How much more difficult, then, are the differences among immaterial dharmas that are apprehended only by understanding?

## Voice

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika classification reported by Vasubandhu.

The phrase `ime kila daśa dharmāḥ` — “these ten dharmas, it is said” — marks some distance from the inherited assertion that all ten are present in every citta-moment.

## The Ten Factors

| Sanskrit | Translation in this passage | Bhāṣya definition |
|---|---|---|
| vedanā | feeling | threefold experience: pleasant, painful, or neutral |
| cetanā | volition | formative activity of citta; mental karma |
| saṃjñā | recognition | apprehension of the distinguishing mark of the object |
| chanda | desire-to-act | wish to perform an action |
| sparśa | contact | touching born from faculty-object-cognition conjunction |
| mati | discernment | prajñā; discrimination among dharmas |
| smṛti | recollection | non-loss of the object-support |
| manaskāra | attention | directing or application of citta |
| adhimokṣa | resolution | decisive commitment |
| samādhi | concentration | one-pointedness of citta |

## Analytical Reconstruction

The universal claim is:

```text
for every citta-moment:
    all ten mahābhūmika factors are jointly present
```

The Bhāṣya does not treat them as ten successive stages. They are ten distinguishable functions attributed to every actual mental occurrence.

Several definitions establish a functional sequence without converting the list into a temporal series:

```text
contact
    = encounter produced by faculty + object + cognition

feeling
    = affective experience of that encounter

recognition
    = apprehension of the object's distinguishing mark

volition / desire-to-act
    = formative and prospective orientation

recollection
    = retention of the object-support

attention
    = direction of citta toward it

resolution
    = decisive settling upon it

concentration
    = one-pointed organization of citta

discernment
    = discrimination among dharmas
```

This reconstruction is analytical only. The Bhāṣya's explicit claim remains simultaneous co-presence.

## Bhūmi

From the preceding passage:

```text
bhūmi = gativiṣaya
      = range or sphere of operation
```

The ten are `mahābhūmika` because their sphere is maximally extensive: they are said to occur in every citta.

Thus `bhūmi` here does not name the object attended to. It names the range within which a mental factor operates.

A later comparison with samyama-bhūmis may therefore be fruitful, but it should preserve the distinction:

```text
Abhidharma bhūmi here:
    operational range of a caitta

samyama-bhūmi:
    field or object upon which coordinated attention is established
```

## Translation Consequences

- `matiḥ prajñā dharmapravicayaḥ` identifies **mati** here directly with prajñā as discrimination among dharmas.
- `smṛti` is not merely recollection of a past event; it is the non-loss of the present object-support.
- `manaskāra` is orientation or application, not the whole of attention in a modern psychological sense.
- `adhimokṣa` is decisive settling or commitment.
- `samādhi` is present in every citta as some degree of one-pointedness; the term therefore has a broader range here than perfected meditative absorption.

## New Technical Terms

- **cittābhisaṃskāra** — formative activity of citta
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya definition of cetanā
  - **function:** defines volition as mental construction and mental karma

- **viṣayanimittodgraha** — apprehension of the distinguishing mark of an object
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression in this unit
  - **function:** defines recognition

- **kartṛkāmatā** — wish to act or perform
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss
  - **function:** defines chanda

- **dharmapravicaya** — discrimination or examination of dharmas
  - **status:** important technical expression
  - **function:** defines mati as prajñā

- **ālambanāsaṃpramoṣa** — non-loss of the object-support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definition
  - **function:** defines smṛti

- **cittasyaikāgratā** — one-pointedness of citta
  - **status:** established meditative expression
  - **function:** defines samādhi

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage asserts that a citta-moment is never psychologically bare. Every moment is already affective, formative, recognitional, contactual, retentive, directed, resolved, discriminative, and minimally unified.

But Vasubandhu immediately emphasizes an epistemic limitation: the distinctions are extremely difficult to isolate. The taxonomy is therefore a doctrinal analysis of jointly occurring functions, not a claim that ordinary introspection can clearly observe ten separate components in each instant.

## Compact Formula

```text
Every citta-moment is said to contain ten universal functions,
but their distinction is subtler than sensory discrimination
and difficult to determine even across a stream of experience.
```
