# VAK 2.59 — Taking and Giving Fruit Across Time

## Kārikā

```text
vartamānāḥ phalaṃ pañca gṛhṇanti dvau prayacchataḥ /
vartamānābhyatītau dvau eko 'tītaḥ prayacchati // VAkK_2.59 //
```

> Five, when present, take fruit; two give it.  
> Two give fruit when present or past; one gives only when past.

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu presenting the causal system**

Among these causes, which cause takes fruit at which time, and which gives it?

Five causes take fruit when they are present. They do not take it when past, because they have already taken it; nor when future, because they are without operative activity. The conditioning cause is also like this, but because it does not necessarily possess a fruit, it is not counted in this statement.

Two causes give fruit while present: the co-arisen cause and the associated cause. For these two, the taking and giving of fruit occur at the same time.

Two causes—the homogeneous cause and the pervasive cause—give fruit both while present and while past. It is readily understood that they give fruit when past. But how do they give homogeneous-outflow fruit while present? Because their fruit arises immediately after them. Once the cause has ceased and the fruit has arisen, both cause and fruit are past; nevertheless, the fruit has been given, and that same fruit is not given again.

With respect to a wholesome homogeneous cause, a fourfold analysis can be made concerning whether it takes fruit and whether it gives fruit. The detailed cases are illustrated through the cutting off and restoration of wholesome roots. Parallel analyses apply to unwholesome dharmas, defiled-indeterminate dharmas in relation to attainment and loss of arhatship, and nondefiled-indeterminate dharmas.

Whatever gives fruit also takes it. There can, however, be something that takes fruit but does not give it—for example, the final aggregates of an arhat.

When considered moment by moment according to a fixed object, a further fourfold analysis may be made. One case is a wholesome mind followed by a defiled or indeterminate mind; the converse gives the second case; a wholesome mind followed by another wholesome mind gives the third; and the remaining possibilities give the fourth.

How, then, is a fruit said to have been taken?

It is taken when the cause **assumes seed-status** with respect to that fruit.

One cause gives fruit only when past: the maturation cause. A maturation cause gives its fruit only after it has become past, because a maturation-result is neither simultaneous with its cause nor immediately subsequent to it.

Some other teachers speak of four additional kinds of fruit: a support-result, as water supports the wind-circle and the earth supports grasses and so forth; a result of application, extending from the contemplation of impurity up to knowledge of non-arising; a result of causal assemblage, as visual consciousness arises from the eye and the other required factors; and a result of cultivation.

## Structural Reconstruction

The passage distinguishes two moments in causal efficacy:

```text
taking fruit
    = the cause becomes a seed determinately ordered toward a result

giving fruit
    = the result actually becomes manifest
```

This is not merely a verbal difference. It explains how a present cause can be genetically related to a result that has not yet appeared.

The six causes divide temporally as follows:

```text
sahabhū-hetu
saṃprayuktaka-hetu
    take and give fruit in the present

sabhāga-hetu
sarvatraga-hetu
    take fruit in the present
    give fruit in the present or past

vipāka-hetu
    takes fruit in the present
    gives fruit only when past

kāraṇa-hetu
    takes fruit in the present when it has a determinate fruit,
    but is omitted from the numerical statement because
    it does not invariably possess one
```

Thus causal relation has an internal temporal articulation:

```text
seed-determination
    → persistence of causal capacity
    → manifestation of fruit
```

## Voices

- **Vasubandhu** — asks and explains the temporal distinction between taking and giving fruit.
- **Vaibhāṣika causal classification** — the six-cause framework and its temporal rules.
- **Other teachers** — introduce four additional kinds of fruit at the end of the unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **phalaṃ pratigṛhṇāti / phalaṃ gṛhṇāti** — “takes” or appropriates a fruit; the cause acquires a determinate relation to a future or present result.
  - **Status:** developed explicitly here.
  - **Function:** distinguishes seed-determination from actual result-manifestation.

- **phalaṃ prayacchati** — gives fruit; the result actually arises from the relevant cause.
  - **Status:** developed explicitly here.
  - **Function:** marks fulfilled causal efficacy.

- **bījabhāvopagama** — assumption of seed-status.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
  - **Function:** defines what it means for a cause to take a fruit.

- **niṣpuruṣakāratva** — absence of operative activity.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya terminology.
  - **Function:** explains why a future cause cannot yet take fruit.

- **caramāḥ skandhāḥ** — final aggregates, here those of an arhat.
  - **Status:** doctrinal example.
  - **Function:** shows that a cause may take fruit without later giving it when the continuum terminates.

- **pratiṣṭhāphala** — support-result.
  - **Status:** doctrine of “other teachers.”
  - **Function:** result consisting in support or maintenance.

- **prayogaphala** — result of application or practice.
  - **Status:** doctrine of “other teachers.”
  - **Function:** result produced through sustained cultivation.

- **sāmagrīphala** — result of causal assemblage.
  - **Status:** doctrine of “other teachers.”
  - **Function:** result arising from a complete conjunction of conditions.

## Translation Consequences

“Taking fruit” should not be translated as passively receiving an already existent object. The Bhāṣya defines it genetically: the cause becomes a seed ordered toward the fruit.

Likewise, “giving fruit” should not suggest the transfer of a substance. It is the actual manifestation of the result when the relevant temporal and causal conditions are complete.

The passage therefore supports a dynamic rather than substantial understanding of *bīja*:

```text
seed
    = determinate causal capacity within a continuum
```

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 2.59 concludes the analysis of the six causes by showing that causal truth is inseparable from temporal form.

The cause is not exhausted by the empirical moment in which it appears. As present, it can assume seed-status; as past, its causal determination can become manifest in a later result. Yet different causes possess different temporal structures. Simultaneous causes, immediately productive causes, homogeneous continuities, and karmic maturation cannot be collapsed into one abstract relation.

The verse therefore completes the *hetu* analysis by specifying:

```text
what kind of ground?
when does it become seed?
when does its result appear?
```

The next verse begins the analysis of the four conditions (*pratyaya*).

## Limited Organon Interpretation

The decisive formula is:

> **A cause takes its fruit when it assumes seed-status; it gives its fruit when the result becomes manifest.**

This is a rigorous expression of genetic judgment. The result is present rationally in the cause as determinate causal capacity before it is present empirically as an arisen fact.

```text
Rational moment
    cause as bīja
    lawful determination of a possible result

Empirical moment
    fruit as manifest Being-there
```

This resembles a qualified *satkārya* principle: not necessarily the numerical pre-existence of a finished effect, but the pre-existence of its lawful form and productive determination in the cause.

The transition from *hetu* to *pratyaya* can therefore be read carefully as a dyad:

```text
hetu
    intrinsic ground-character of the genesis

pratyaya
    complete relational conditions under which that genesis occurs
```

For the Yoga comparison, this gives a promising but still provisional determination of Samādhi: not one member of the dyad alone, but the comprehension of ground and conditional manifestation as one genetic act. The Kośa distinction itself should remain intact as we enter VAK 2.60.
