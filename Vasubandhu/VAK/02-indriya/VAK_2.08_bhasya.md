# VAK_2.08 — Mental Pleasure, Mental Pain, and Neutral Feeling

## Kārikā Anchor

```text
anyatra sā saumanasyam aśātā caitasī punaḥ /
daurmanasyam upekṣā tu madhyā ubhayī avikalpanāt // VAK 2.8 //
```

**Best current translation:**

> Elsewhere, that pleasant mental feeling is gladness; unpleasant mental feeling is distress. Neutral feeling is the middle feeling and is both bodily and mental, because there is no differentiation.

Related Kārikā file: [VAK_2.08.md](./VAK_2.08.md)

## Bhāṣya Source Passage

```text
anyatra sā saumanasyaṃ

tṛtīyāddhyānād anyatra kāmadhātau prathame dvitīye ca dhyāne sā caitasikī śātā vedanā saumanasyendriyam /

tṛtīye tu dhyāne prītivītarāgatvāt sukhendriyam eva sā na saumanasyendriyam /

prītir hi saumanasyam /

aśātā caitasī punaḥ /

daurmanasyam

upekṣā tu madhyā

naiva śātā nāśātā aduḥkhāsukhā vedanā madhyety ucyate /

sopekṣendriyam /

kiṃ kāyikī caitasikīty āha /

ubhayī

kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇam iyam abhisamasyaikam indriyaṃ kriyate /

avikalpanāt /

caitasikaṃ hi sukhaduḥkhaṃ prāyeṇa vikalpanād utpadyate na tu kāyikam /

viṣayavaśād arhatām apy utpatteḥ /

atas tayor indriyatvena bhedaḥ /

upekṣā tu svarasenāvikalpayata evotpadyate kāyikī caitasikī cety ekam indriyaṃ kriyate /

anyathā ca kāyikaṃ sukham anugṛhṇāty anyathā caitasikam /

evaṃ duḥkham anyathā kāyikam upahanty anyathā caitasikam /

upekṣāyāṃ tv eṣa vikalpo nāsty ata upekṣaṇaṃ praty avikalpanād abhedaḥ /
```

## Continuous Translation

> Outside the third dhyāna—that is, in the desire realm and in the first and second dhyānas—pleasant mental feeling is the faculty of gladness.
>
> In the third dhyāna, however, because one has become free from rapture, that same pleasant mental feeling is the pleasure faculty and not the faculty of gladness. For rapture is gladness.
>
> Unpleasant mental feeling, in turn, is distress.
>
> Neutral feeling is the middle feeling. Feeling that is neither pleasant nor unpleasant, neither painful nor pleasurable, is called “middle.” That is the faculty of neutral feeling.
>
> Is it bodily or mental? It is both.
>
> Why, then, are these two combined and made into a single faculty? Because there is no differentiation.
>
> Mental pleasure and pain arise for the most part through differentiation, but bodily pleasure and pain do not. Bodily pleasure and pain arise under the power of their objects even in arhats. Therefore bodily and mental pleasure and pain are distinguished as separate faculties.
>
> Neutral feeling, however, whether bodily or mental, arises naturally in one who is not differentiating. Therefore the two are made into a single faculty.
>
> Bodily pleasure supports the continuum in one way, and mental pleasure in another. Likewise bodily pain injures it in one way, and mental pain in another. But no such differentiation exists in neutral feeling. Therefore, with respect to the act of neutral attending, bodily and mental neutral feeling are not divided.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is explanatory throughout. No explicit opponent is introduced.

## Argument Reconstruction

The first half completes the fivefold classification:

```text
duḥkha
    = unpleasant bodily feeling

sukha
    = pleasant bodily feeling
      and pleasant mental feeling in the third dhyāna

saumanasya
    = pleasant mental feeling outside the third dhyāna

daurmanasya
    = unpleasant mental feeling

upekṣā
    = neutral feeling, bodily or mental
```

The third-dhyāna exception is explained through **prīti**:

```text
prīti
    = saumanasya
```

Because the third dhyāna is free from rapture, its pleasant mental feeling is not classified as gladness. It is classified as *sukha*.

The second half asks why bodily and mental neutral feeling form one faculty, whereas bodily and mental pleasure and pain are divided.

The answer is not merely that neutral feeling lacks a pleasant or unpleasant tone. Vasubandhu gives a functional criterion.

Mental pleasure and pain generally involve *vikalpana*, differentiation. Bodily pleasure and pain can arise simply through the force of an object, even in an arhat. They therefore arise and function differently.

```text
mental pleasure / pain
    generally arise through differentiation

bodily pleasure / pain
    can arise directly through object-contact
```

Their effects also differ:

```text
bodily and mental pleasure support differently
bodily and mental pain injure differently
```

Neutral feeling does not display this difference. In both its bodily and mental forms it is characterized by non-differentiating neutral attendance. Therefore it is counted as one faculty.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā phrase **avikalpanāt** should not be translated only as “because neutral feeling is undifferentiated.” The Bhāṣya gives a more exact meaning:

> because bodily and mental neutral feeling do not differ with respect to the function of neutral attending.

Likewise, **ubhayī** means that the single neutral-feeling faculty includes both bodily and mental instances.

The term **saumanasya** should retain “gladness” rather than being flattened into generic mental pleasure, because Vasubandhu explicitly equates it with *prīti*, rapture or joyful elevation.

## Provisional Doctrinal Determination

Feeling-faculties are classified not by one feature alone but by the conjunction of:

```text
hedonic tone
locus or mode
manner of arising
functional effect on the continuum
meditative level
```

This is a functional taxonomy rather than a merely introspective description.

The passage also establishes that an arhat can experience bodily pleasure and pain. Liberation does not eliminate all feeling. It removes the afflicted differentiation and appropriation that ordinarily attach to feeling.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The faculty is again a higher functional category rather than a single empirical event.

```text
one indriya
    can gather bodily and mental instances
    when their governing function is one
```

Conversely, similar hedonic tones can be divided into different faculties when their mode of arising and their effect differ.

This supports the interpretation of *indriya* as a planning or governing model:

```text
Idea-level function
    organizes
multiple empirical realizations
```

The category follows functional unity, not anatomical identity alone.

## New Technical Terms

- **prīti** — rapture, joyful elevation
  - **status:** introduced explicitly in the Bhāṣya explanation
  - **function:** explains why pleasant mental feeling outside the third dhyāna is *saumanasya*

- **avikalpana** — absence of differentiating activity
  - **status:** Kārikā term developed in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** explains the unity of bodily and mental neutral feeling

- **viṣayavaśa** — under the force or power of the object
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** explains the arising of bodily pleasure and pain without mental differentiation

- **svarasena** — naturally, by its own mode
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** describes the arising of neutral feeling in the absence of differentiation

- **anugṛhṇāti** — supports, benefits, sustains
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptually
  - **function:** distinguishes bodily from mental pleasure by effect

- **upahanti** — injures, harms, impairs
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptually
  - **function:** distinguishes bodily from mental pain by effect

- **upekṣaṇa** — neutral attending
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** common functional character of bodily and mental neutral feeling
