# VAK_2.64_bhasya — Condition-Profiles, Sequence, and the Rejection of a Single World-Cause

## Kārikā

### Devanāgarī

```text
चतुर्भिश्चित्तचैत्ता हि समापत्तिद्वयं त्रिभिः ।
द्वाभ्यामन्ये तु जायन्ते नेश्वरादेः क्रमादिभिः ॥ VAkK_2.64 ॥
```

### IAST

```text
caturbhiś cittacaittā hi samāpattidvayaṃ tribhiḥ /
dvābhyām anye tu jāyante neśvarādeḥ kramādibhiḥ // VAkK_2.64 //
```

### Working Translation

Citta and caittas arise through four conditions; the two attainments through three. Other formations arise through two. They do not arise from Īśvara and the like, as is shown by sequence and related considerations.

---

## Bhāṣya Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Which dharma arises through how many conditions?

> **Citta and caittas, indeed, through four.**

For them, the cause-condition (*hetu-pratyaya*) consists of all five causes. The immediately antecedent condition (*samanantara-pratyaya*) consists of the preceding cittas and caittas, when no other citta-caitta complex intervenes. The object-condition (*ālambana-pratyaya*) consists, as appropriate, of the five sense-objects and also of all dharmas. The dominant condition (*adhipati-pratyaya*) consists of all dharmas except the dharma itself.

> **The two attainments through three.**

For the attainment of cessation and the attainment of non-perception there is no object-condition, because these attainments do not apprehend an object.

Their cause-condition is twofold: the co-arisen cause, namely their arising and the other conditioned marks, and the homogeneous cause, namely previously arisen wholesome dharmas belonging to the same level.

Their immediately antecedent condition is the attainment-citta together with its associated mental factors. Their dominant condition is as before.

Because these two attainments are produced by an intentional formation of mind, they occur immediately after citta; but because they obstruct the arising of citta, they themselves are not immediately antecedent conditions for a subsequent citta.

> **Other formations arise through two.**

Other dharmas—dissociated formations and material dharmas—arise through the cause-condition and dominant condition, exactly as already explained.

**Voice: Objector**

You say that existents arise from conditions, rather than that Īśvara, Puruṣa, Pradhāna, or something of that sort is the cause of the entire world. What is the reason for this? If you think that the existence of effects proves a cause, should not that very argument establish one cause such as Īśvara for everything?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Moreover:

> **Not from Īśvara and the like, because of sequence and related considerations.**

If Īśvara, or anything else, were the single cause, the whole world would have to arise simultaneously. But beings and things are observed to arise in sequence.

**Voice: Objector**

Then perhaps the sequence depends upon the will of Īśvara: “Let this arise now, let this cease now, let that arise afterward.”

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

If the differences in sequence depend upon differences in will, then a plurality of causal differentiations has already been admitted. Moreover, since the supposed cause, Īśvara, is itself undifferentiated, the differences of will should themselves occur simultaneously.

If the different acts of will depend upon other differentiated causes, then Īśvara is no longer the sole cause. And if those causes in turn require further differentiating causes in order to arise successively, an infinite regress follows. Once one admits an beginningless series of differentiated causes, the advocate of Īśvara has not gone beyond the Buddhist account of causal succession.

**Voice: Objector**

Even if the divine intentions occur simultaneously, the world need not arise simultaneously; Īśvara could produce things according to those intentions in the appropriate order.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

No. There would be no later differentiating factor that could explain why one result occurs now and another later.

And what benefit does Īśvara obtain from the labor of creating the world? If the purpose is pleasure, then if he cannot obtain that pleasure without this means, he is not sovereign with respect to pleasure; and the same difficulty applies elsewhere.

If, after creating beings tormented by many sufferings in hell and elsewhere, he takes pleasure in that, homage indeed to such an Īśvara.

A verse is aptly sung concerning him:

> “Because he burns, because he is fierce, terrible, blazing,
> an eater of flesh, blood, and marrow—therefore he is called Rudra.”

To accept a single cause of the world would also suppress the directly observable efficacy (*puruṣakāra*) of other factors.

If one imagines that Īśvara acts together with other causes, that becomes only a statement of devotion, because no activity of anything separate from those causes is observed in producing the result. And if other causes are genuinely co-operating, Īśvara is no longer sovereign.

**Voice: Objector**

Perhaps only the first creation has Īśvara as its cause.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

If that first creation depended upon nothing else, then, like Īśvara himself, it too would have to be beginningless.

The same argument, with appropriate changes, applies to Pradhāna.

Therefore there is no single cause of the world.

It is beings’ own actions that generate them in their respective births. Yet unfortunate people, failing to recognize what they themselves have done, experience their own maturation-results and falsely imagine another Īśvara.

## Argument Structure

The verse contains two linked analyses.

### 1. Condition-Profiles

```text
citta-caitta
    = 4 conditions
    hetu + samanantara + ālambana + adhipati

nirodha-samāpatti / asaṃjñi-samāpatti
    = 3 conditions
    no ālambana

material dharmas / dissociated formations
    = 2 conditions
    hetu + adhipati
```

The count is functional, not merely classificatory. Different kinds of conditioned beings require different relational structures in order to arise.

### 2. Sequence Against a Single Cause

The central argument is:

```text
one invariant cause
    → no principle of differentiated timing
    → simultaneous production expected

observed world
    → ordered sequential production
    → differentiated causal conditions required
```

Appealing to differentiated divine intentions does not solve the problem. Either those differences are unexplained, or they depend upon further conditions. In the latter case the supposed single cause has ceased to be explanatory.

---

## Voices

- **Vasubandhu** — explains the condition-profile of each class of dharmas and argues against a single creator-cause.
- **Objector** — proposes Īśvara/Puruṣa/Pradhāna as a single cause and introduces appeals to divine will and first creation.
- **Vaibhāṣika position** — the four-pratyaya framework itself remains the Abhidharma causal framework being explained, though Vasubandhu’s anti-creator argument is presented in his own argumentative voice.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **cittābhisaṃskāra** — intentional mental formation or preparation producing the two attainments.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory expression in this passage.
  - **function:** explains why the two attainments occur immediately after citta.

- **cittotpatti-vibandhaka** — obstructing the arising of citta.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific functional description.
  - **function:** explains why the attainments, although following citta, are not themselves samanantara-pratyaya for the next citta.

- **krama-saṃbhava** — arising in sequence.
  - **status:** central Bhāṣya argument associated with the Kārikā’s *krama*.
  - **function:** evidence against a single invariant creator-cause.

- **puruṣakāra** — efficacious activity / observable causal operation.
  - **status:** previously encountered in *puruṣakāraja-phala*; here used polemically.
  - **function:** marks the directly observable efficacy of plural causal factors that a single-cause theory would obscure.

- **kāraṇa-paramparā** — series or succession of causes.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this unit.
  - **function:** replaces appeal to a single creator with a beginningless differentiated causal series.

- **bhakti-vāda** — merely a statement of devotion.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only polemical expression.
  - **function:** criticizes adding Īśvara to an already sufficient plurality of co-operating causes.

## Translation Consequences

The final pāda should not be rendered as though *krama* were itself a separate cause replacing Īśvara. The point is argumentative: orderly succession and other observed features count against the hypothesis of one universal creator-cause.

Likewise, the first three pādas are not a detachable census. They prepare the anti-creator argument by showing that arising is already explained through differentiated condition-profiles.

---

## Doctrinal Determination

The Kośa here refuses both extremes:

```text
not:
    an unconditioned external agent produces every conditioned event

and not:
    events arise without determinate conditions

but:
    differentiated dharmas arise through differentiated condition-profiles
    in an ordered causal succession
```

The world-order therefore does not require a transcendent efficient agent. Its intelligibility lies in the articulated relations of conditioned dharmas themselves.

The two samāpattis are especially instructive. They are produced from citta and depend upon prior wholesome formations, but because they suppress citta they alter the normal cognitive succession. The condition-system can therefore explain not only continuation but structured interruption.

---

## Organon Interpretation

The philosophically important point is not simply atheistic polemic. It is a demand for **genetic sufficiency**.

```text
Explanatory principle:
    a cause must explain
        difference
        sequence
        timing
        specific result-profile
```

A formally universal cause that remains identical while the effects differ does not yet explain their differences.

This gives a striking formulation of the Rational–Empirical relation developed in this study:

```text
Rational law
    = differentiated condition-form

Empirical world
    = sequential manifestation of that form
```

The lawful form of truth is therefore not imposed upon loka from outside. It is disclosed as the determinate causal and conditional organization through which loka arises.

For the Samādhi reconstruction, this verse makes the *hetu–pratyaya* unity especially clear:

```text
hetu
    productive determination

pratyaya
    relational condition-profile

krama
    ordered manifestation
```

Samādhi, in our Yoga comparison, can therefore be treated as comprehension of productive ground and qualitative condition as one lawful actuality—without attributing this comparative formulation to Vasubandhu himself.

---

## Relation to the Existing Kārikā Analysis

The existing Kārikā file correctly identifies the 4/3/2 condition-count and the rejection of Īśvara. The Bhāṣya adds three decisive refinements:

1. the exact membership of each condition-profile;
2. the special asymmetry of the two samāpattis with respect to samanantara-pratyaya;
3. the full logical critique of a single invariant world-cause from differentiated temporal sequence.
