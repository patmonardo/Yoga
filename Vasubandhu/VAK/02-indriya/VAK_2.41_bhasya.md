# VAK 2.41 — Sabhāgatā and the Non-Ideational Result

## Kārikā

```text
sabhāgatā sattvasāmyaṃ āsaṃjñikam asaṃjñiṣu /
nirodhaś cittacaittānāṃ vipākaḥ te bṛhatphalāḥ // VAK 2.41 //
```

## Continuous Translation of the Bhāṣya

### Sabhāgatā

What is *sabhāgatā*?

**Class-commonality is the sameness of beings.**

*Sabhāgatā* is said to be a real entity. In the treatises it is called the common membership of a group: the resemblance among beings by which they belong to one class.

It is both undivided and divided. The undivided form is the commonality of all beings simply as beings, because it is present in every individual being. The divided form is the determinate commonality of those same beings according to realm, level, destiny, mode of birth, species, female or male status, lay follower or monastic status, trainee or one beyond training, and so forth.

The Vaibhāṣikas argue that if this commonality were not a distinct real entity, then among beings who differ individually there could be no undivided cognition or designation, “being.” The same reasoning is applied to cognitions and designations such as “aggregate,” “sense-field,” and “element.”

A fourfold analysis is possible concerning one who dies and is reborn while either abandoning or acquiring a particular commonality. First, one may die and be reborn in the same class. Second, on entering the certainty of the noble path, one abandons the commonality of an ordinary person and acquires noble commonality. Third, through transition from one destiny to another, one both abandons and acquires a class-commonality. Fourth, apart from these cases, neither occurs.

**Objection:** If ordinary-person commonality is a real entity, what further need is there for a separate ordinary-person status? One does not posit a humanity distinct from human commonality.

Moreover, ordinary people do not perceive this alleged entity, since it is nonmaterial, nor do they delimit it by insight; yet they still understand differences of kind among beings. If the entity is not apprehended, how can it perform the function assigned to it?

Further, why should there not also be a class-commonality among nonsentient things? Rice, barley, mung beans, mango trees, breadfruit trees, iron, gold, and so forth each resemble others of their own type. If many distinct commonalities are admitted, by what higher commonality are they all designated together as “commonality”?

This position therefore approaches the Vaiśeṣika doctrine of universals.

### Āsaṃjñika

What is the *āsaṃjñika*?

Among the gods called non-ideational beings, the cessation of citta and caittas is called the non-ideational state. It is a real entity, according to the Vaibhāṣikas, by which citta and caittas are held in cessation for a long interval in the future and are prevented from arising, like the blocking of the flow of river water.

This state is exclusively a maturation-result.

Maturation-result of what? Of the non-ideational attainment.

Who are the beings in whom it occurs?

They are the Bṛhatphala gods. Some among the gods called Bṛhatphala are non-ideational beings located in a distinct region, as in the case of the intermediate-dhyāna gods.

Do they ever possess ideation?

They do at the moment of rebirth and at the moment of death. The sūtra says that after remaining for an exceedingly long time, when ideation arises again, those beings fall from that place.

Having fallen from there, they are reborn only in the desire realm, like persons awakening from a long sleep. Because the formative force of their previous attainment has been exhausted and no new force has been accumulated, they descend like arrows whose momentum has been spent.

Anyone destined to be reborn there must necessarily possess desire-realm karma whose maturation is to be experienced in a later life, just as the inhabitants of Uttarakuru possess karma whose maturation is rebirth among the gods.

## Doctrinal Reconstruction

VAK 2.41 introduces two very different dissociated formations.

```text
sabhāgatā
    class-commonality
    the alleged real basis of shared classification

āsaṃjñika
    a maturation-result
    suspending citta and caittas for a long interval
```

The discussion of *sabhāgatā* is fundamentally a debate over the ontological status of universals.

```text
Vaibhāṣika:
    shared classification requires a real commonality-entity

Vasubandhu's criticism:
    classification may occur without perceiving such an entity
    the theory duplicates ordinary-person status
    it should extend equally to nonsentient kinds
    it risks an infinite hierarchy of commonalities
```

The Bhāṣya therefore again pressures a Vaibhāṣika *dravya* into the status of a conceptual designation grounded in resemblance and classification.

The second half explains why *āsaṃjñika* belongs among dissociated formations. It is neither an active citta nor a caitta. It is the structural condition in which their arising is prevented for a long karmically determined interval.

## Voices

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — *sabhāgatā* and *āsaṃjñika* are real dissociated formations.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — raises objections against treating class-commonality as a separate substance.

**Voice: Objector** — questions the duplication of ordinary-person commonality and ordinary-person status, and the failure to extend commonality to nonsentient things.

## New Technical Terms

- **nikāya-sabhāga** — common membership in a group or class
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific expression in this passage
  - **function:** Vaibhāṣika definition of *sabhāgatā*

- **sattva-sabhāgatā** — commonality of beings simply as beings
  - **status:** elaboration beyond the Kārikā
  - **function:** undivided universal commonality

- **dharma-sabhāgatā** — determinate commonality according to realm, destiny, species, status, and so forth
  - **status:** elaboration beyond the Kārikā
  - **function:** divided class-membership

- **asaṃjñi-sattva** — non-ideational being
  - **status:** explicit doctrinal class
  - **function:** bearer of the *āsaṃjñika* result

- **Bṛhatphala** — “Great Fruit,” the form-realm divine level in which non-ideational beings occur
  - **status:** named in the Kārikā and explained in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** cosmological location of the result

## Translation Consequences

*Sabhāgatā* should not be reduced to “similarity.” The Vaibhāṣika claim is stronger: it is an allegedly real class-membership principle grounding common cognition and designation.

*Āsaṃjñika* is not mere unconsciousness. It is a karmically matured state in which the arising of citta and caittas is structurally prevented for a long period, while ideation still occurs at entry into and departure from that existence.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This passage exposes both the power and danger of classification.

```text
classification requires:
    recognition of common form across individuals

but does not necessarily require:
    a separately existing universal substance
```

For the future Dharma dataset, *sabhāgatā* should initially be modeled as a classificatory relation or common-type determination, not automatically as an independent entity.

The *āsaṃjñika* case adds a different structural operator:

```text
state = citta-caitta suspension
cause = prior non-ideational attainment
result-type = vipāka
location = Bṛhatphala
exit = renewed ideation followed by death and desire-realm rebirth
```

The chapter has therefore moved far beyond a faculty catalog. It is now specifying the formal structures by which a continuum is classified, suspended, maintained, and transformed.
