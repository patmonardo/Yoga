# VAK 2.20 — The Two Minimum Eight-Faculty Configurations

## Kārikā

```text
sarvālpair niḥśubho 'ṣṭābhir vinmanaḥkāyajīvitaiḥ /
yuktaḥ bālas tathārūpye upekṣāyurmanaḥśubhaiḥ // VAkK_2.20 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

> Now, how many faculties does the person possessing the fewest faculties possess?
>
> One who is without wholesome faculties possesses the fewest of all: eight faculties—feeling, mind, body, and life.
>
> “Without wholesome faculties” means one whose roots of wholesome action have been severed. Such a person possesses no fewer than eight faculties: the five feelings, together with the body, mind, and life faculties.
>
> Feeling is called *vit* because it experiences (*vedayate*); or *vit* may mean the act of experiencing, just as *saṃpat* means accomplishment.
>
> Just as one without wholesome faculties possesses the fewest faculties—eight—so too does an ordinary being in the formless realm.
>
> “Ordinary being” (*bāla*) is a designation for a common person (*pṛthagjana*).
>
> With which eight faculties is that being endowed?
>
> With neutral feeling, life, mind, and the wholesome faculties.
>
> That is: neutral feeling, life, mind, and the five beginning with faith.
>
> Because the five beginning with faith are exclusively wholesome, they are included by the expression “the wholesome faculties.”
>
> **Objection:** Then the three beginning with the faculty called “I shall know” should also be included.
>
> **Reply:** No, because the present topic concerns a total of eight and because it concerns an ordinary being.

## Reconstruction of the Two Minimum Configurations

### 1. One whose wholesome roots are severed

```text
five feeling faculties:
    pleasure
    pain
    gladness
    distress
    neutral feeling

plus:
    body
    mind
    life

= eight faculties
```

The person is called **niḥśubha**, “without wholesome faculties,” because the wholesome roots have been severed. This does not mean that every functional or affective faculty has disappeared. The continuum still possesses the full fivefold range of feeling, bodily support, mental coordination, and life.

### 2. An ordinary being in the formless realm

```text
neutral feeling
life
mind
faith
energy
mindfulness
concentration
wisdom

= eight faculties
```

Here material and sensory faculties are absent, as are the other four feelings. Yet the five faculties beginning with faith remain possible because an ordinary being in the formless realm may possess contaminated wholesome faculties.

## Voice Analysis

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the question of the smallest possible faculty-set and explains both eightfold configurations.
- **Voice: Objector** — argues that “the wholesome faculties” might also include the three uncontaminated realization-faculties.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — excludes them because the count is eight and the subject is explicitly an ordinary person.

## New Technical Terms

- **niḥśubha** — one without wholesome faculties; here, a person whose wholesome roots have been severed.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā.
  - **function:** identifies the first minimum-possession case.

- **samucchinna-kuśala-mūla** — one whose wholesome roots have been completely cut off.
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** doctrinal definition of *niḥśubha*.

- **vit** — feeling or experiencing; explained from *vedayate*, “experiences.”
  - **status:** lexical explanation of the Kārikā’s compressed expression.
  - **function:** collective designation for the five feeling faculties.

- **bāla** — ordinary or immature being.
  - **status:** clarified in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** synonym here for *pṛthagjana*, excluding an ārya.

- **śubha** — wholesome; in this count, specifically the five faculties beginning with faith.
  - **status:** Kārikā term narrowed by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** collective designation fixed by count and subject-status.

## Translation Consequences

The compressed phrase *vin-manaḥ-kāya-jīvitaiḥ* must not be read as only four faculties. *Vit* collectively denotes the **five faculties of feeling**. Thus:

```text
vit = five feelings
manas = one
kāya = one
jīvita = one

total = eight
```

Likewise, *śubhaiḥ* in the second half does not mean every wholesome or uncontaminated faculty. The syntax is constrained by two contextual rules:

```text
total count = eight
subject = pṛthagjana
```

Therefore it denotes only the five faculties beginning with faith.

## Doctrinal Determination

The same numerical minimum can represent radically different modes of existence.

```text
minimum A:
    five feelings + body + mind + life
    morally depleted but fully embodied

minimum B:
    neutral feeling + mind + life + five path faculties
    disembodied but wholesomely equipped
```

The count alone therefore does not reveal the quality of the system. Eight faculties can constitute either:

- a continuum deprived of wholesome roots, or
- a refined formless continuum retaining the basic faculties of cultivation.

The difference lies not in quantity but in **configuration**.

## Living-System Interpretation

This sharpens the insight into a “living Buddha” by first defining its contrary limits. A living continuum never reduces to bare mind alone. Even at the minimum it remains an organized complex.

But the organization can take opposed forms:

```text
embodied affective survival
    without wholesome direction

or

formless mental persistence
    with wholesome path-capacities
```

Thus life is not defined merely by the number of faculties present. It is defined by which powers organize the continuum and toward what end.

The awakened living system will therefore not be understood by maximal faculty-count alone. It must be understood through the purified relation among:

```text
continuity
cognition
affect
embodiment
path-capacity
realization
```

## Compact Formula

```text
VAK 2.20 identifies two minimum eight-faculty systems:
one retains embodied feeling after wholesome roots are severed;
the other retains mind, life, neutral feeling, and the five wholesome faculties in the formless realm.
Equal count does not mean equal organization.
```
