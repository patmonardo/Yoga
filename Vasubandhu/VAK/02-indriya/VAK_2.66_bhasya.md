# VAK_2.66_bhasya — Twelvefold Citta Matrix

## Kārikā

```text
kuśalākuśalaṃ kāme nivṛtānivṛtaṃ manaḥ /
rūpārūpyeṣv akuśalād anyatra anāsravaṃ dvidhā // VAkK_2.66 //
```

## Translation

In the desire realm, mind is fourfold: wholesome, unwholesome, obscured-indeterminate, and unobscured-indeterminate. In the form and formless realms there is no unwholesome mind; each therefore has the other three. These make ten minds accompanied by outflows. The outflow-free mind is twofold: that of the trainee and that of the one beyond training. Thus there are twelve minds in all.

## Bhāṣya

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The immediately antecedent condition has already been stated in general for citta and caittas, but its rule has not yet been specified: after which citta does which citta arise? That determination must now be given.

For this purpose, first, in summary, there are twelve cittas.

Why twelve?

In the desire realm there are four:

- wholesome (`kuśala`),
- unwholesome (`akuśala`),
- obscured-indeterminate (`nivṛtāvyākṛta`),
- unobscured-indeterminate (`anivṛtāvyākṛta`).

In the form realm there is no unwholesome citta; therefore three remain. The same is true in the formless realm.

These ten are `sānusrava`, accompanied by outflows.

The outflow-free (`anāsrava`) citta is twofold:

- `śaikṣa` — the citta of one still in training,
- `aśaikṣa` — the citta of one beyond training.

Thus there are twelve cittas altogether.

## Doctrinal Determination

The purpose of this classification is not taxonomic for its own sake. It is introduced because `samanantara-pratyaya` requires a determinate law of succession.

The Bhāṣya has already established:

```text
citta/caitta
    -> immediately antecedent condition
```

but that statement is too general. The present verse now supplies the finite state-space within which lawful succession can be specified:

```text
Which citta
    may be followed by
which citta?
```

The twelvefold matrix is therefore the classificatory precondition for the transition-rules that follow.

## Translation Consequences

`nivṛta` and `anivṛta` should not be rendered simply as moral opposites. Both belong here under `avyākṛta`, the indeterminate. The contrast is whether the indeterminate citta is obscured/afflicted or unobscured.

Likewise, `anāsrava` is not merely another realm. It cuts across the realm-bound ten cittas as a distinct purity-status and is divided according to the Path: training and beyond-training.

## New Technical Terms

- **sānusrava** — accompanied by outflows; **status:** previously encountered in related form; **function:** gathers the ten realm-bound cittas into one contaminated class.
- **nivṛtāvyākṛta** — obscured-indeterminate; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit compound; **function:** distinguishes an afflicted but ethically indeterminate citta.
- **anivṛtāvyākṛta** — unobscured-indeterminate; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit compound; **function:** distinguishes a non-afflicted indeterminate citta.
- **śaikṣa** — trainee; **status:** previously encountered doctrinal category; **function:** first division of outflow-free citta.
- **aśaikṣa** — beyond training; **status:** previously encountered doctrinal category; **function:** second division of outflow-free citta.

## Logical Determination

VAK 2.66 transforms the pratyaya discussion from a general relation into a finite transition-system.

```text
General rule:
    prior citta -> later citta

Required refinement:
    classify citta-types
    then determine allowed transitions
```

This is a decisive shift from mere temporal adjacency to typed lawful succession.

## Organon Interpretation

In our comparative reconstruction, this is a particularly clear instance of Rational Subjectivity becoming determinate.

The subject is not treated as an undifferentiated stream. Its possible states are classified according to:

- realm,
- ethical status,
- obscuration,
- outflow-status,
- path-status.

Only after that classification can the law of succession be stated.

```text
Rational Subjectivity
    = not bare sequence
    = typed state-space + lawful transitions
```

This should remain secondary to the Abhidharma claim itself. Vasubandhu's immediate task is precise: to determine the rules of `samanantara-pratyaya` by first establishing the twelve classes of citta.

## Relation to the Previous Verse

VAK 2.65 closed with the causal relations among elements and derived materiality. VAK 2.66 now turns from material dependency to cognitive succession:

```text
material causal matrix
    -> citta classification matrix
    -> rules governing succession of citta
```

The inquiry has moved from the constitution of material arising to the constitution of a lawful cognitive continuum.
