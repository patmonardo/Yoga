# VAK_2.40 Bhāṣya — Abandoning Aprāpti and the Proliferation of Prāpti

## Kārikā

```text
kāmādyāptāmalānāṃ ca mārgasyāprāptir iṣyate /
pṛthagjanatvaṃ tatprāptibhūsaṃcārād vihīyate // VAK 2.40 //
```

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

**Voice: Objector**

How, then, is this non-acquisition abandoned?

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

Whatever acquisition there is of a given dharma, that non-acquisition is abandoned through the arising of that acquisition or through transition to another ground.

For example, ordinary-person status is the non-acquisition of the noble path. It is abandoned through acquisition of that path and also through transition to another ground. The same analysis should be applied to the other cases.

When it is said that non-acquisition is abandoned, this means that a non-acquisition of that non-acquisition arises and that its acquisition is cut off.

**Voice: Objector**

But do acquisition and non-acquisition themselves also possess acquisition and non-acquisition?

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

They say that each of the two possesses both.

**Voice: Objector**

Would this not lead to an infinite regress of acquisitions?

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

It does not lead to infinite regress, because they mutually possess one another.

A dharma arises as a third together with itself: the dharma, its acquisition, and the acquisition of that acquisition. Through the arising of the acquisition, one possesses both the dharma and the acquisition-of-acquisition. Through the arising of the acquisition-of-acquisition, one possesses the acquisition itself. Therefore there is no infinite regress.

On this account, when a wholesome or afflicted dharma arises together with its acquisition and acquisition-of-acquisition, three acquisitions arise in the second moment. Three subsequent acquisitions of these also arise, making six.

In the third moment, nine acquisitions arise in relation to the entities produced in the first and second moments; together with their subsequent acquisitions, there are eighteen.

Proceeding in this way, acquisitions spread by progressive increase. For every past and future moment of affliction and secondary affliction, and for every wholesome moment obtained at birth, together with the associated and co-arisen dharmas belonging to beginningless and endless saṃsāra, infinitely many acquisition-substances arise in every moment of a single living being.

What a festival of acquisitions this becomes!

Only because these acquisitions offer no resistance can they find room in space. Otherwise there would not be enough room even in space for a second living being.

## Argument Structure

The Bhāṣya begins with a practical question:

```text
How is aprāpti abandoned?
```

The Vaibhāṣika answer is relational:

```text
aprāpti of dharma D
    is abandoned when
prāpti of D arises

or when
continuum S changes ground
```

But the explanation immediately generates a second-order problem:

```text
If prāpti is a real dharma,
does prāpti itself require prāpti?
```

The Vaibhāṣika accepts second-order acquisition:

```text
dharma
prāpti of dharma
prāpti of prāpti
```

and claims that mutual possession stops the regress.

Vasubandhu’s criticism is not merely that the explanation is formally circular. It is that the ontology explodes quantitatively. Every dharma brings acquisitions, acquisitions-of-acquisitions, and subsequent acquisitions across successive moments.

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage exposes the cost of reifying a status-relation.

```text
ordinary statement:
    a continuum possesses a dharma

Vaibhāṣika ontology:
    dharma
    + prāpti
    + prāpti of prāpti
    + temporally subsequent acquisitions
```

The theory can block an explicit infinite regress only by producing an indefinitely expanding population of real entities.

The sarcastic line — “What a festival of acquisitions!” — makes Vasubandhu’s critical stance unmistakable.

## Relation to VAK 2.36–2.39

The preceding verses already introduced the Sautrāntika alternative:

```text
prāpti
    need not be a separate substance

it can designate
    the transformed capacity of the continuum
```

VAK 2.40 now supplies the negative argument against the Vaibhāṣika reification.

```text
status-language
    should not automatically generate
new ontological substances
```

This strengthens the Bhāṣya’s preference for causal transformation, seed-capacity, and developmental condition over separately existing possession-relations.

## New Technical Terms

- **tatprāpti** — acquisition of that dharma
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-level clarification
  - **function:** explains abandonment of non-acquisition

- **bhūsaṃcāra** — transition to another ground
  - **status:** previously implicit in realm and stage classification
  - **function:** alternative condition for abandoning a status

- **prāptiprāpti** — acquisition of an acquisition
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical development
  - **function:** second-order entity invoked to defend prāpti as real

- **anuprāpti** — subsequent acquisition
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** tracks continuing possession across later moments

- **anavasthā** — infinite regress / non-termination
  - **status:** logical term in the debate
  - **function:** objection to second-order acquisition

- **parasparasamanvāgama** — mutual possession
  - **status:** Vaibhāṣika technical reply
  - **function:** proposed regress-blocking relation

## Translation Consequences

`vihīyate` should not be rendered merely as psychological “letting go.” Here it is a formal change in status:

```text
aprāpti ceases to qualify the continuum
because the opposed acquisition arises
or because the continuum changes ground
```

Likewise, `prāpti` is not simply “attainment” in the ordinary meditative sense. It is the technical relation of being endowed with a dharma.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

The passage gives a powerful warning for theoretical Dharma design:

```text
Do not convert every relation,
status,
or classification
into an independent entity.
```

A semantic system may represent:

```text
continuum S has dharma D
```

without requiring:

```text
entity: possession-of-D
entity: possession-of-possession-of-D
entity: later-possession-of-possession-of-D
```

The better model may be a state-transition or capacity relation grounded in the changing continuum itself.

This is especially relevant to the idea of Principle as Seed:

```text
Principle present as operative capacity
    need not require
an additional possession-substance
```

But this remains an Organon comparison; the Bhāṣya’s own target is specifically the Vaibhāṣika ontology of prāpti.
