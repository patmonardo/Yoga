# VAK 2.38 Bhāṣya — Path Status and Modes of Acquisition

## Kārikā

```text
tridhā na śaikṣāśaikṣāṇām aheyānāṃ dvidhā matā /
avyākṛtāptiḥ sahajā abhijñānairmāṇikād ṛte // VAK 2.38 //
```

## Continuous Translation

The dharmas that are neither those of a trainee nor those of one beyond training are contaminated dharmas and the unconditioned. Their acquisition is threefold according to the distinction between trainee and the other two statuses.

Thus, first, the acquisition of contaminated dharmas is neither that of a trainee nor that of one beyond training. The same is true of the cessation not produced through discernment, and of the cessation produced through discernment when it is acquired by one who is not noble. When that same cessation is acquired through the path of a trainee, its acquisition belongs to a trainee; when it is acquired through the path of one beyond training, its acquisition belongs to one beyond training.

The acquisition of dharmas abandoned through seeing and cultivation is itself, respectively, abandoned through seeing and cultivation. But there is a distinction concerning dharmas that are not to be abandoned. This is stated:

> **For those not to be abandoned, it is held to be twofold.**

Dharmas that are not to be abandoned are uncontaminated. The acquisition of the cessation not produced through discernment is nevertheless abandoned through cultivation. The same is true of the cessation produced through discernment when it is acquired by one who is not noble. But when that same cessation is acquired through the noble path, its acquisition is uncontaminated and not to be abandoned; the same applies to the truth of the path.

What was previously stated—that the acquisition of dharmas belonging to the three times is threefold—was a general rule. The following is an exception:

> **The acquisition of the indeterminate is co-arisen.**

For unobscured-indeterminate dharmas, acquisition is only co-arisen. It does not precede them and does not arise afterward, because of their weakness. Consequently, for past unobscured-indeterminate dharmas there is only a past acquisition, and so on; for present ones there is only a present acquisition.

Does this apply to every unobscured-indeterminate dharma?

No. The verse says:

> **Except for the superknowledges and the mind of magical creation.**

The two indeterminate superknowledges and the mind that produces magical creations are excluded. Because they are powerful and arise from the accomplishment of a special preparatory practice, their acquisition may precede them, arise together with them, and follow them.

Some also accept this threefold acquisition for certain highly cultivated skills and for forms of bodily conduct that have been practiced to an exceptional degree.

Is co-arisen acquisition found only in unobscured-indeterminate dharmas? No. The Kārikā continues:

> **And for obscured-indeterminate material form.**

The acquisition of obscured-indeterminate manifest material action is also only co-arisen. Its weakness is established by the fact that even when intense it does not generate non-manifest material form.

Is there also a distinction in modes of acquisition among wholesome and unwholesome dharmas, as there is among indeterminate dharmas? There is. The verse states:

> **In the desire realm, material form has no prior acquisition.**

For manifest and non-manifest material form belonging to the desire realm, prior acquisition does not exist at all. Co-arisen and subsequent acquisition do exist.

## Reconstruction of the Argument

VAK 2.38 refines the acquisition relation by introducing exceptions to apparently general classification rules.

```text
General rule:
    dharmas across three times
    may have past, present, and future acquisition

Exception:
    weak unobscured-indeterminate dharmas
    have only co-arisen acquisition
```

But even the exception has exceptions:

```text
powerful cultivated neutral states:
    two indeterminate superknowledges
    magical-creation mind
    some highly trained skills
    some exceptionally practiced bodily activities

may have:
    prior acquisition
    co-arisen acquisition
    subsequent acquisition
```

Thus temporal extension of acquisition depends upon causal strength and cultivation.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expositor** — unfolds the Vaibhāṣika classification and marks the general rules and exceptions.

**Voice: Objector** — asks whether the rule applies to every unobscured-indeterminate dharma and whether co-arisen acquisition belongs only to that class.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — treats acquisition as a real dissociated formation and classifies it by path status, abandonability, ethical class, temporal relation, and causal strength.

## New Technical Terms

- **naivaśaikṣa-nāśaikṣa** — neither trainee nor beyond-training; status applying to contaminated dharmas and some unconditioned acquisitions.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the Kārikā's compressed path classification.
  - **function:** distinguishes dharma-status from the status of the person acquiring it.

- **apraheya** — not to be abandoned.
  - **status:** already implicit in the Kārikā as *aheya*.
  - **function:** identifies uncontaminated dharmas while allowing their acquisition to have a different abandonment-status.

- **agrajā prāpti** — acquisition arising before the acquired dharma.
  - **status:** technical specification developed in this prāpti block.
  - **function:** marks dispositional possession prior to manifestation.

- **paścātkālajā prāpti** — acquisition arising after the acquired dharma.
  - **status:** technical specification developed in this block.
  - **function:** marks continued possession after manifestation has ceased.

- **prayogaviśeṣa** — special preparatory practice.
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** explains how some ethically neutral states become sufficiently powerful to possess temporally extended acquisition.

- **śailpasthānika** — belonging to a craft or trained skill.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only example here.
  - **function:** illustrates how repeated cultivation can strengthen an otherwise neutral capacity.

- **īryāpathika** — belonging to bodily posture or conduct.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only example here.
  - **function:** illustrates practiced bodily capacities whose acquisition may persist beyond the present act.

## Translation Consequences

The phrase *avyākṛtāptiḥ sahajā* should not be read as saying that every neutral dharma is simply innate. Here *sahajā* means that its acquisition occurs only together with the dharma itself. The issue is temporal relation, not birth or essential nature.

Likewise, the exception *abhijñānairmāṇikād ṛte* concerns powerful but ethically indeterminate cognitive accomplishments. Their neutrality does not entail weakness. Because they are products of specialized cultivation, their acquisition may extend before and after their actual manifestation.

## Doctrinal Determination

This passage separates at least four dimensions that might otherwise be conflated:

```text
status of the dharma
status of the practitioner
abandonment-status of the acquisition
strength and temporal reach of the acquisition
```

An uncontaminated dharma may have an acquisition that is still abandoned through cultivation when possessed by an ordinary person. The relation does not simply inherit every determination of its object.

Likewise, an ethically neutral state may be causally weak or highly developed. Ethical neutrality and functional strength are independent axes.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This is directly useful for a Theoretical Dharma model because it shows that possession of a determination is indexed by more than categorical membership.

```text
DharmaPossession:
    dharmaStatus
    practitionerStatus
    abandonmentStatus
    temporalMode
    causalStrength
    cultivationHistory
```

A Principle used in classification may therefore be present in more than one sense:

```text
presently manifest
available through prior acquisition
retained through subsequent acquisition
strengthened by repeated cultivation
```

The Kośa does not formulate this as a theory of classificatory Principles, but it supplies a rigorous structure for distinguishing current occurrence from durable capacity.

## Summary

VAK 2.38 demonstrates that acquisition is governed by exceptions based upon path-status, abandonment-status, ethical neutrality, strength, and cultivation. Weak neutral dharmas possess only a simultaneous acquisition. Powerful cultivated neutral states can be acquired before they arise and remain acquired afterward. Desire-realm material action, however, has no prior acquisition.
