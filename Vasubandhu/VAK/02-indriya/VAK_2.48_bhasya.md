# VAK 2.48 Bhāṣya — Result-Status of the Dissociated Formations

## Kārikā

> **sabhāgatā sā tu punar vipāko 'pi āptayo dvidhā /**  
> **lakṣaṇāni ca niḥṣyandāḥ samāpatty-asamanvayāḥ // VAK 2.48 //**

Working translation:

> Class-commonality has the same classifications, but it is also a maturation-result. Acquisitions and the conditioned marks are twofold. The two attainments and non-acquisition are homogeneous outflows only.

The transmitted source is compressed, and the final compound should be read with the Bhāṣya: **samāpattayaḥ asamanvāgamaś ca**—the two mindless attainments and non-acquisition.

## Continuous Translation of the Bhāṣya

Just as the groups of names and the rest are connected with sentient beings, are homogeneous outflows, and are unobscured-neutral, so too is class-commonality. But class-commonality is not merely a homogeneous outflow: it can also be a maturation-result, and it occurs in the desire, form, and formless realms.

Acquisitions are of two kinds: some are homogeneous outflows and some are maturation-results.

The conditioned marks are likewise twofold, as are acquisitions.

The two mindless attainments and non-acquisition are homogeneous outflows only.

What remains to be said about these formations has already been stated. The remaining two—non-ideational existence and the life faculty—have also already been explained, and therefore are not discussed again here.

How was it established that acquisition and the other relevant formations are connected with sentient beings? By speaking of their possession or endowment by a continuum.

How was it established that the conditioned marks may be connected either with sentient beings or with what is not so designated? Because they accompany every conditioned dharma.

The dissociated formations have now been explained.

Earlier it was said: “Birth generates what is to be generated, but not without causes and conditions.” What, then, are these causes, and what are the conditions?

[VAK 2.49 begins the causal analysis.]

## Argumentative Reconstruction

VAK 2.48 is not primarily introducing a new entity. It closes the account of the **citta-viprayukta-saṃskāras** by assigning the remaining members of the class their causal-result status.

The classification is exact:

| Formation | Result-status |
|---|---|
| name-, sentence-, and syllable-groups | homogeneous outflow; unobscured-neutral |
| class-commonality | homogeneous outflow and maturation-result |
| acquisition | homogeneous outflow or maturation-result |
| conditioned marks | homogeneous outflow or maturation-result |
| non-ideational attainment | homogeneous outflow only |
| cessation-attainment | homogeneous outflow only |
| non-acquisition | homogeneous outflow only |
| non-ideational existence | previously determined |
| life faculty | previously determined |

This matters because the formations are not merely gathered into one taxonomic list. Each must be located within the genetic order of conditioning. The question is not only **what the formation is**, but **how it arises as a result**.

### Homogeneous outflow and maturation-result

A **niḥṣyanda** is a result continuous in kind with its causal series. It is an outflow of a prior homogeneous condition. A **vipāka**, by contrast, is the matured result of karma.

Thus class-commonality can have two distinct geneses:

1. it may continue homogeneously within an already constituted class-continuum;
2. it may arise as the matured result that places a being within a determinate mode of existence.

Acquisition and the conditioned marks receive the same twofold classification. The attainments and non-acquisition, however, are not karmic maturations in this scheme; they arise as homogeneous consequences of the relevant continuum and practice.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu**  
Presents and closes the classificatory account, then turns immediately toward the analysis of causes and conditions.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**  
Presupposed throughout the classification of the dissociated formations as real dharmas possessing determinate result-status.

**Voice: uncertain**  
The brief questions concerning how sentient-being designation was established may be read as scholastic clarification rather than as a hostile objection.

## New Technical Terms

- **naiḥṣyandika / niḥṣyanda** — a homogeneous outflow or result continuous with its causal series  
  **status:** previously encountered, now used systematically  
  **function:** result-classification

- **vipākaja** — born as a maturation-result of karma  
  **status:** previously encountered  
  **function:** distinguishes karmic maturation from homogeneous continuation

- **sattvākhyā** — designated as connected with a sentient being  
  **status:** previously encountered  
  **function:** locates a formation within a living continuum

- **asattvākhyā** — not designated as connected with a sentient being  
  **status:** clarified here through the conditioned marks  
  **function:** allows marks to apply to all conditioned dharmas, not only living continua

- **asamanvāgama** — non-acquisition or non-endowment  
  **status:** previously encountered  
  **function:** negative structural relation within a continuum

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā should not be translated as though *samāpatti* were itself equivalent to *samanvaya*. The Bhāṣya makes the construction clear: the two mindless attainments and non-acquisition are **niḥṣyanda only**.

Likewise, *dvidhā* governs both acquisitions and the conditioned marks: each may be either a homogeneous outflow or a maturation-result.

The verse is therefore a compact result-matrix, not a fresh definition of samāpatti.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

This verse completes one block and opens another:

```text
structural formation
    → result-status
    → causal provenance
    → analysis of hetu and pratyaya
```

The transition is philosophically important. A dharma has not been fully explained when it has merely been named and classified. Its **genesis** must be determined.

The Bhāṣya therefore moves from the inventory of dissociated formations to the six causes in VAK 2.49. This is the point where the *Indriya-nirdeśa* becomes explicitly a science of conditioned intelligibility.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

The Organon reading should remain modest here.

VAK 2.48 teaches that structural determinations possess different modes of causal derivation. Some are continuous outflows of an already active form; others are matured results of a deeper karmic conditioning.

In the language we have developed:

```text
prakāśa
    illuminates a determination
    by displaying its genesis

niḥṣyanda
    determination as homogeneous continuation

vipāka
    determination as matured consequence
```

Thus the “Light of Substance” is **prakāśa** precisely because it does not absorb the determinations into one substance. It illuminates how each determination belongs to its own causal order.

The verse also prevents the Organon from treating all structural relations as having one genesis. Acquisition, class-commonality, attainment, and the conditioned marks may occupy the same broad category while differing in causal form.

## Summary

VAK 2.48 closes the account of the dissociated formations by assigning their result-status. Class-commonality, acquisition, and the conditioned marks may arise either as homogeneous outflows or as maturation-results, while the two mindless attainments and non-acquisition are homogeneous outflows only. The closing question—what are causes and conditions?—then carries the chapter directly into the causal theory beginning with VAK 2.49.
