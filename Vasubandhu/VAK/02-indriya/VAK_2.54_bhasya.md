# VAK 2.54 Bhāṣya — Pervasive and Maturation Causes

## Kārikā

```text
sarvatragākhyaḥ kliṣṭānāṃ svabhūmau pūrvasarvagāḥ /
vipākahetur aśubhāḥ kuśalāś caiva sāsravāḥ // VAkK_2.54 //
```

## Continuous Translation

The causes called pervasive are the previously arisen pervasive afflictions within their own level, functioning as causes of later afflicted dharmas there.

What is the maturation cause?

Unwholesome dharmas and wholesome dharmas associated with outflows are maturation causes, because they possess the nature of producing maturation.

Why do ethically indeterminate dharmas not bring about maturation?

Because they are weak, like rotten seeds.

Why do uncontaminated dharmas not bring about maturation?

Because they have not been moistened by craving, like sound seeds that have not been moistened. Since they are not connected with any realm, what realm-bound maturation could they produce?

The remaining dharmas produce maturation because they possess both required features, like sound seeds that have been moistened.

How is the expression “maturation cause” to be understood? Does it mean the cause of maturation, or a cause that is itself maturation?

What follows from this question?

If it means only “cause of maturation,” the expression “the eye born of maturation” would become problematic. If it means only “maturation which is a cause,” the expression “the maturation of karma” would become problematic.

There is no fault. As stated earlier, the compound can be connected in both ways.

What, then, is the meaning of maturation?

Maturation is a ripening dissimilar from its cause. The ripening of the other causes is similar to them. The Vaibhāṣikas say that in one case both modes can occur.

Yet strictly speaking, “ripening” is not appropriate for those other causes. Ripening is a result arising from a specific transformation of a continuum and reaching a terminal result. The co-arisen and associated causes do not have a result born from such transformation of a continuum. Nor do homogeneous and related causes terminate in a final result, because wholesome and other dharmas can continue to produce results repeatedly throughout saṃsāra.

In the desire realm, a one-aggregate maturation cause may have one result: for example, a condition together with its arising and other marks. A two-aggregate cause may have one result: bodily or verbal action together with its marks. A four-aggregate cause may have one result: wholesome or unwholesome citta and mental factors together with their marks.

In the form realm, a one-aggregate maturation cause may consist of acquisition or the non-ideational attainment together with their marks. A two-aggregate cause may consist of manifestation in the first dhyāna together with its marks. A four-aggregate cause occurs in wholesome non-concentrated mind, and a five-aggregate cause in concentrated mind.

In the formless realm, a one-aggregate maturation cause may consist of acquisition or cessation-attainment together with its marks. A four-aggregate cause consists of wholesome citta and mental factors together with their marks.

There is karma whose maturation is only one sense-base, namely the life faculty. When the mental base matures, two bases mature: mind and dharma. When the tangible base matures, or the body base, three bases mature: body, tangible, and dharma. When the eye base matures, four bases mature: eye, body, tangible, and dharma. The same applies correspondingly to ear, nose, and tongue.

There is also karma whose maturation consists of five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, or eleven bases. Karma has varied or unvaried results, like external seeds: some external seeds yield diverse results, while others yield less diverse ones.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Pervasive cause

The pervasive cause explains why affliction is not reproduced only through strict similarity. A prior affliction can condition many later afflicted dharmas within the same level.

```text
homogeneous cause:
    like produces like

pervasive cause:
    an afflictive field conditions diverse later afflictions
```

This is the causal architecture of contamination.

### 2. Maturation requires potency and appropriation

The Bhāṣya gives two exclusions:

```text
indeterminate dharmas:
    no maturation because too weak

uncontaminated dharmas:
    no maturation because not moistened by craving
    and not bound to a realm
```

A maturation cause therefore requires both effective potency and appropriation by craving.

### 3. Maturation is not mere succession

Vipāka is defined by three features:

```text
dissimilarity from its cause
transformation of a continuum
arrival at a terminal result
```

This separates karmic maturation from simple homogeneous continuation.

### 4. Karma determines structured result-complexes

The long classification by aggregates and sense-bases shows that karma does not merely produce an isolated event. It can project an organized result-complex of varying breadth.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks why indeterminate and uncontaminated dharmas do not mature, clarifies the compound, and restricts the proper meaning of ripening to continuum-transformation ending in a terminal result.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — identifies unwholesome and contaminated wholesome dharmas as maturation causes and allows a broader application of the language of ripening.

**Voice: Objector** — raises the ambiguity in “maturation cause” and the linguistic consequences of choosing only one interpretation.

## New Technical Terms

- **vipākadharmatva** — the capacity or nature of producing maturation
  - **status:** new in the Bhāṣya discussion
  - **function:** defines why certain dharmas count as maturation causes

- **tṛṣṇābhiṣyandita** — moistened or suffused by craving
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only image in this passage
  - **function:** explains karmic appropriation and realm-bound maturation

- **santati-pariṇāma-viśeṣaja** — born from a specific transformation of a continuum
  - **status:** major Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** gives the genetic definition of maturation

- **phalaparyanta** — reaching a terminal result
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this unit
  - **function:** distinguishes maturation from indefinitely recurrent homogeneous causation

## Translation Consequences

“Ripening” is better than a generic “result” for *vipāka* because the Bhāṣya explicitly treats it as a transformation of a continuum culminating in a determinate terminal result. Yet “maturation” remains preferable as the stable technical translation because it can cover both process and result.

The seed imagery is not decorative. It identifies the two conditions of karmic efficacy:

```text
soundness
+ moistening
```

These correspond to causal potency and craving-based appropriation.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 2.54 completes the sixfold cause system by distinguishing two broad modes of causal rationality:

```text
pervasive cause:
    field-like propagation of affliction

maturation cause:
    delayed transformation of a continuum into a dissimilar terminal result
```

This gives a precise Rational–Empirical structure.

Empirically, one encounters a resulting body, faculty, cognition, or life-span. Rationally, that result is illuminated as the terminal phase of a prior karmic continuum-transformation.

```text
empirical appearance:
    this result is present

rational prakāśa:
    this result is the matured outcome
    of a prior appropriated causal series
```

The rational determination does not replace the empirical result. It discloses its genesis.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This passage is especially important for the Logic of Experience.

A maturation judgment does not merely connect an earlier event and a later event externally. It judges the later Being-there as the completed transformation of a prior causal continuum.

```text
prior causal series
    → internal transformation
    → terminal manifestation
```

Thus the causal relation is neither an invisible substance behind appearance nor a mere regular sequence. It is the intelligible form by which the empirical result is known as the completion of its ground.

The distinction between homogeneous result and maturation result will be crucial later:

```text
homogeneous result:
    repetition and continuation

maturation result:
    transformation and completion
```

This is a strong Kośa contribution to the Organon’s genetic theory of judgment.
