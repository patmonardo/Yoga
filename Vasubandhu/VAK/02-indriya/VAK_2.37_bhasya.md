# VAK 2.37 Bhāṣya — The Classification Matrix of Prāpti

## Kārikā

```text
traiyadhvikānāṃ trividhā śubhādīnāṃ śubhādikā /
svadhātukā tadāptānām anāptānāṃ caturvidhā // VAK 2.37 //
```

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

This acquisition is threefold with respect to dharmas of the three times.

For past dharmas there may be a past acquisition, a future acquisition, and a present acquisition. In the same way, the acquisitions of future and present dharmas are each threefold.

For wholesome and the other classes, acquisition is correspondingly wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate.

For dharmas belonging to a realm, acquisition belongs to that same realm. Thus the acquisition of desire-realm, form-realm, and formless-realm dharmas belongs respectively to the desire, form, and formless realms.

For dharmas not belonging to a realm, acquisition is fourfold.

The acquisition of uncontaminated dharmas is, in summary, of four kinds: it may belong to any of the three realms, or it may itself be uncontaminated.

The acquisition of cessation not produced by discernment belongs to the three realms. The acquisition of cessation produced by discernment may belong to the form realm, the formless realm, or be uncontaminated. The acquisition of the truth of the path is exclusively uncontaminated. In this way, taken together, the acquisition of dharmas not belonging to a realm is fourfold.

The acquisition of the dharmas of trainees is itself of the trainee class, and the acquisition of the dharmas of those beyond training is of the beyond-training class. For dharmas that are neither trainee nor beyond training, however, there is a further distinction.

The acquisition of these dharmas is threefold: trainee, beyond training, or neither trainee nor beyond training.

Dharmas that are neither trainee nor beyond training are contaminated dharmas and the unconditioned. The acquisition of contaminated dharmas is itself neither trainee nor beyond training. The same applies to cessation not produced by discernment and to cessation produced by discernment when acquired by an ordinary person. When that same cessation is acquired through a trainee path, its acquisition is of the trainee class; when acquired through a beyond-training path, its acquisition is of the beyond-training class.

The acquisition of dharmas abandoned through seeing and dharmas abandoned through cultivation is, respectively, abandoned through seeing and abandoned through cultivation. For dharmas not subject to abandonment, there is again a distinction.

Their acquisition is held to be twofold.

The dharmas not subject to abandonment are uncontaminated. The acquisition of cessation not produced by discernment is abandoned through cultivation, as is the acquisition of cessation produced by discernment when attained by an ordinary person. The acquisition of that same cessation when attained through the noble path is uncontaminated and not subject to abandonment, as is the acquisition of the truth of the path.

## Doctrinal Reconstruction

VAK 2.37 does not merely list kinds of prāpti. It demonstrates that acquisition is indexed across several independent axes:

```text
time:
    past
    future
    present

ethical status:
    wholesome
    unwholesome
    indeterminate

realm:
    desire
    form
    formless
    uncontaminated

path status:
    trainee
    beyond training
    neither

abandonment status:
    abandoned through seeing
    abandoned through cultivation
    not subject to abandonment
```

The same dharma may therefore stand in different acquisition-relations depending on who acquires it, by what path, and at what stage.

## Voices

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — Prāpti is accepted as a real dissociated formation and classified across time, ethical status, realm, path status, and abandonment status.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — The passage reports and organizes the Vaibhāṣika system after the critique of prāpti as a separate substance in the preceding unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **traiyadhvika** — belonging to the three times: past, future, and present.
  - **status:** newly foregrounded in the Bhāṣya analysis of prāpti.
  - **function:** temporal classification.

- **anāpta** — not belonging to a realm; here applied to uncontaminated dharmas.
  - **status:** clarified beyond the compressed Kārikā.
  - **function:** realm-transcending classification.

- **śaikṣa** — trainee.
  - **status:** introduced here as an acquisition-status class.
  - **function:** path-stage classification.

- **aśaikṣa** — beyond training.
  - **status:** introduced here as an acquisition-status class.
  - **function:** path-completion classification.

- **naivaśaikṣanāśaikṣa** — neither trainee nor beyond training.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expansion in this unit.
  - **function:** residual path-status class for contaminated and unconditioned dharmas.

- **darśanaheya** — abandoned through seeing.
  - **status:** newly applied to acquisition itself.
  - **function:** abandonment classification.

- **bhāvanāheya** — abandoned through cultivation.
  - **status:** newly applied to acquisition itself.
  - **function:** abandonment classification.

- **aheya** — not subject to abandonment.
  - **status:** newly applied to acquisition itself.
  - **function:** uncontaminated status classification.

## Translation Consequences

Prāpti should not be translated merely as “possession.” The Bhāṣya treats it as a highly indexed relation whose own status mirrors or depends upon the dharma, the continuum, the path, and the mode of attainment.

A useful working sense is therefore:

```text
prāpti
    = acquisition-status of a dharma in a continuum
```

This preserves the technical structure better than a simple property of ownership.

## Logical Determination

The governing form is a multi-axis classificatory matrix:

```text
Prāpti(dharma, continuum)
    hasTimeStatus
    hasEthicalStatus
    hasRealmStatus
    hasPathStatus
    hasAbandonmentStatus
```

The Bhāṣya therefore converts the simple statement “this continuum has this dharma” into a fully determined relation.

## Limited Organon Note

The passage is relevant to classificatory cognition because no principle is applied without indices. Recognition of a dharma is not exhausted by naming its kind; its temporal, ethical, modal, and developmental determination must also be known.

That makes prāpti less like a bare label and more like a stateful relation in a structured system.
