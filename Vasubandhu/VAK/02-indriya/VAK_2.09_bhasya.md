# VAK_2.09_bhasya — The Three Path-Faculties and the Outflow Classification

## Kārikā Anchor

```text
dṛgbhāvanāśaikṣapathe nava trīṇi amalaṃ trayam /
rūpīṇi jīvitaṃ duḥkhe sāsravāṇi dvidhā nava // VAK 2.9 //
```

Working translation:

> On the paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training, nine faculties constitute each of the three realization-faculties. The triad itself is stainless. The seven material faculties, the life faculty, pain, and distress are exclusively with outflows; nine faculties are twofold.

## Continuous Translation

> On the path of seeing, on the path of cultivation, and on the path of no-more-training, nine faculties are called the three realization-faculties.
>
> The nine are mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, and the five beginning with faith. These same nine faculties, when present on the three paths, receive three different collective names. On the path of seeing they are called the faculty named “I shall know what has not yet been known.” On the path of cultivation they are called the faculty of knowledge. On the path of no-more-training they are called the faculty of one who has known.
>
> Why is this so?
>
> On the path of seeing, one has set out to know what has not previously been known. On the path of cultivation, there is nothing wholly new still to be known; rather, one continues to know that same truth for the purpose of abandoning the remaining latent afflictions. On the path of no-more-training there is the ascertainment, “It has been known.” This ascertainment is called *ājñātāva*. One who possesses it is called *ājñātāvin*, “one who has known.” Or the word may mean one whose character is to preserve what has been known. This is because such a person has obtained the knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising.
>
> For one who has reached the determination, “Suffering has been completely known by me; it is not to be completely known again,” the corresponding faculty is the faculty of one who has known.
>
> Having explained the intrinsic nature of the faculties, their different classifications must now be stated: how many are with outflows, how many are without outflows, and so forth.
>
> First, the triad just mentioned—the faculty called “I shall know,” the faculty of knowledge, and the faculty of one who has known—is stainless. This means that it is without outflows, because “stains” is another designation for the outflows.
>
> The seven material faculties, the life faculty, and the faculties of pain and distress are exclusively with outflows. The seven material faculties are the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, female, and male faculties, because they are included in the aggregate of form.
>
> Nine faculties are twofold, that is, they may be either with outflows or without outflows. These are mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, and the five beginning with faith.
>
> Some maintain that the five beginning with faith are exclusively without outflows. For the Blessed One said: “A person in whom these five faculties are altogether and entirely absent I call an outsider, one who stands among ordinary persons.”
>
> But this statement does not establish that they are exclusively without outflows, because it was spoken with the uncontaminated faculties specifically in view. The Blessed One first established the classification of noble persons and then said, “one in whom these are absent.” Moreover, ordinary persons are of two kinds: an inward ordinary person whose wholesome roots have not been cut off, and an outward ordinary person whose wholesome roots have been cut off. It is with the latter in view that he says, “I call him an outsider, one who stands among ordinary persons.”
>
> The sūtra also says, before the Wheel of Dharma had even been set in motion: “There are beings born in the world and grown up in the world whose faculties are sharp, middling, or weak.” Therefore the faculties beginning with faith certainly can exist with outflows.
>
> Again it is said: “So long as I had not known, as they truly are, the arising, disappearance, gratification, danger, and escape with respect to these five faculties, for so long I did not claim to have awakened beyond this world with its gods,” and so forth. But uncontaminated dharmas are not investigated in this fivefold manner. Therefore the five faculties beginning with faith are not exclusively without outflows.

## Argument Reconstruction

The first half of the passage solves a problem left compressed in the Kārikā. The three realization-faculties are not three new substances added to the nine path-factors. They are three **collective functional determinations** of the same nine faculties according to the path on which they operate.

```text
same nine faculties
    + path of seeing
        = faculty called “I shall know”

same nine faculties
    + path of cultivation
        = faculty of knowledge

same nine faculties
    + path of no-more-training
        = faculty of one who has known
```

This is precisely a case of one higher functional determination gathering many subordinate faculties into a single path-operation.

The three names mark distinct practical tasks:

```text
seeing:
    knowing what had not yet been known

cultivation:
    repeatedly knowing the same truth
    in order to abandon remaining anuśayas

no-more-training:
    knowing is completed
    and its result is secured
```

The second half introduces a new classificatory axis:

```text
anāsrava
    without outflows

sāsrava
    with outflows

dvidhā
    capable of either mode
```

The three realization-faculties are necessarily without outflows. The seven material faculties, life, pain, and distress are necessarily with outflows. The remaining nine can operate in either an ordinary or a purified mode.

## Voice Map

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the nine faculties constituting the three path-faculties.
- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — primary classification of faculties as with outflows, without outflows, or twofold.
- **Voice: alternative position** — the five faculties beginning with faith are exclusively without outflows.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu / Vaibhāṣika response** — scriptural argument that faith and the others can also exist in contaminated ordinary experience.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā translation should not suggest that “nine, three, and the triad” are three unrelated numerical groups. The syntax is better unfolded as:

> The same nine faculties, on the paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training, constitute respectively the three realization-faculties; that triad is stainless.

The second line should also include *daurmanasya*, which is syntactically paired with *duḥkha* in the Bhāṣya:

> The seven material faculties, life, pain, and distress are exclusively with outflows; nine faculties are twofold.

## Practical Determination

The path-faculty is not a single isolated psychological event. It is a coordinated system consisting of:

```text
mind
feeling appropriate to the path-stage
faith
energy
recollection
samādhi
prajñā
```

The Idea-level unity lies in the task being performed. The subordinate faculties retain their differences, but the path gives them one governing direction.

This is not merely a theoretical classification of practice. It is a model of how practice becomes one operation:

```text
many faculties
    organized by one path-task
        produce one realization-faculty
```

## New Technical Terms

- **dṛg-patha / darśana-mārga** — path of seeing
  - **status:** previously encountered in the Kārikās; explicitly explained here
  - **function:** knowing what had not previously been known

- **bhāvanā-mārga** — path of cultivation
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** repeated knowing for abandonment of remaining latent afflictions

- **aśaikṣa-mārga** — path of no-more-training
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** completed knowing beyond further training

- **ājñātāva** — ascertainment that something has been known
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanation
  - **function:** basis for the name *ājñātāvin*

- **kṣaya-jñāna** — knowledge of exhaustion
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** confirms that the afflictions have been exhausted

- **anutpāda-jñāna** — knowledge of non-arising
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** confirms that what has been exhausted will not arise again

- **amala** — stainless
  - **status:** Kārikā term explained in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** synonym here for without outflows

- **sāsrava** — with outflows
  - **status:** technical classificatory term
  - **function:** faculty operating within contaminated experience

- **anāsrava** — without outflows
  - **status:** technical classificatory term
  - **function:** faculty belonging to uncontaminated path-operation

- **dvidhā** — twofold
  - **status:** Kārikā term
  - **function:** capable of contaminated or uncontaminated operation

## Provisional Organon Contact Point

The most useful contact point is structural rather than doctrinal:

```text
Concepts:
    the nine distinct faculties

Idea:
    their unified operation as one path-faculty
```

The Idea does not erase the Concepts. It organizes them under one practical end. The same components can therefore acquire a different higher identity according to the path-system in which they function.

This supports reading *indriya* as an ancient functional category and planning architecture rather than merely as an organ or isolated capacity.
