# VAK_2.12_bhasya — Realm Classification of the Faculties

## Kārikā

```text
kāmāptam amalaṃ hitvā rūpāptaṃ strīpumindriye /
duḥkhe ca hitvā ārūpyāptaṃ sukhe cāpohya rūpi ca // VAK 2.12 //
```

## Translation of the Kārikā

> Excluding the stainless faculties, the remaining faculties may belong to the desire realm. Excluding also the female and male faculties and the two painful faculties, the remainder may belong to the form realm. Excluding in addition pleasure and all material faculties, the remainder may belong to the formless realm.

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

> First, a faculty should be understood as connected with the desire realm when the exclusively uncontaminated triad beginning with the faculty called “I shall know” is excluded, for that triad is not connected with any realm.
>
> A faculty may be connected with the form realm when the female and male faculties and the two painful faculties are excluded, and when the stainless triad remains excluded as before. By “the two painful faculties” are meant pain and distress.
>
> The female and male faculties do not exist in the form realm because beings there are free from the practice of sexual intercourse and because those faculties produce bodily unattractiveness.
>
> How, then, are beings in the form realm called males?
>
> Where are they called males?
>
> In the sūtra, where it is said: “It is impossible and cannot occur that a woman should become Brahmā. Such a possibility does not exist. But it is possible that a man should become Brahmā.”
>
> The word “male” there refers to a different male characteristic from the male characteristic possessed by men in the desire realm.
>
> The pain faculty does not exist in the form realm because the bodily basis there is clear and refined, and because there is no unwholesome activity there.
>
> The distress faculty does not exist because the continuum is softened by tranquillity and because there is no object capable of provoking resentment.
>
> A faculty may be connected with the formless realm when, in addition to the female and male faculties, the two painful faculties, and the stainless triad already excluded, pleasure and all material faculties are also removed.
>
> What remains?
>
> The faculties of mind, life, and neutral feeling, together with the five beginning with faith. These, and no others, may be connected with the formless realm.

## Voice

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — classificatory explanation and objections.
- **Voice: Objector** — “How, then, are they called males?”
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — distinction between ordinary sexual faculty and a different conventional male characteristic.

## Realm Distribution

### Desire Realm

The three realization-faculties are excluded because they are exclusively uncontaminated and therefore not bound to any realm.

```text
22 faculties
- 3 stainless realization-faculties
= 19 desire-realm-connected faculties
```

The phrase *kāmāpta* therefore does not mean that every one of the nineteen must occur in every desire-realm being. It means that each of them can belong to that realm.

### Form Realm

The form realm excludes:

```text
three stainless realization-faculties
female faculty
male faculty
pain faculty
distress faculty
```

The two sexual faculties are absent because sexual reproduction and intercourse are absent. The Bhāṣya also gives an aesthetic-cosmological reason: they would impair the refined form characteristic of that realm.

Pain is excluded for two reasons:

```text
refinement of the bodily basis
absence of unwholesome activity
```

Distress is excluded because tranquillity has softened the continuum and because the realm supplies no basis for resentment.

### Formless Realm

The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies the surviving eight:

```text
manas
jīvita
upekṣā
śraddhā
vīrya
smṛti
samādhi
prajñā
```

The formless realm therefore preserves:

```text
cognitive coordination
continuity of the living series
neutral feeling
path-equipment
```

It excludes every material faculty and every feeling whose classification depends upon bodily or lower meditative conditions.

## The Important Scientific Point

The same faculty-system is filtered by the ontological conditions of each realm.

```text
faculty-Idea
    remains identifiable across realms

available implementation
    changes according to the realm
```

The chapter is therefore not treating an *indriya* as an isolated organ. It is treating it as a functional determination whose possible realization depends upon the architecture of the world in which the continuum operates.

This agrees closely with the planning-system interpretation:

```text
one governing functional model
    constrained by
realm-specific conditions of execution
```

The form realm does not merely possess fewer organs. Its whole mode of embodiment excludes sexual reproduction, coarse bodily injury, unwholesome action, and resentment-based mental distress.

The formless realm does not merely lack visible bodies. Its remaining faculty-system is reduced to the minimal organization required for mental continuity, neutral experience, and meditative-path functioning.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā-only translation should make two points explicit:

1. *duḥkhe* is interpreted by the Bhāṣya as both **pain and distress**.
2. The “stainless” faculties are excluded from all three realm classifications because they are **unconnected with any realm**, not merely absent from the desire realm.

A better philosophical translation is therefore:

> The faculties other than the three uncontaminated realization-faculties may belong to the desire realm. Of these, those other than the female and male faculties and the faculties of pain and distress may belong to the form realm. Of these again, when pleasure and every material faculty are excluded, only mind, life, neutral feeling, and the five path faculties may belong to the formless realm.

## New Technical Terms

- **apratisaṃyukta** — unconnected with a realm
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** explains why the uncontaminated realization-faculties are excluded from all three realms

- **maithuna-dharma-vairāgya** — freedom from sexual activity
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only
  - **function:** explains the absence of female and male faculties in the form realm

- **aśobhākara** — producing unattractiveness
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only
  - **function:** secondary explanation for the absence of sexual faculties in refined form-realm embodiment

- **acchatva** — clarity or refinement
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only
  - **function:** characteristic of the form-realm bodily basis that excludes pain

- **śamatha-snigdha-santāna** — a continuum softened by tranquillity
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only
  - **function:** explains the absence of distress in the form realm

- **āghāta-vastu** — basis or object of resentment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only
  - **function:** absent in the form realm, preventing distress

## Compact Determination

```text
desire realm:
    all realm-connected faculties

form realm:
    refined embodiment without sex, pain, or distress

formless realm:
    mind, continuity, neutrality, and path faculties only

uncontaminated realization:
    not connected with any realm
```
