# VAK_2.29 — Unwholesome Citta Count-Profiles

## Kārikā

```text
āveṇike tv akuśale dṛṣṭiyukte ca viṃśatiḥ /
kleśaiś caturbhiḥ krodhādyaiḥ kaukṛtyenaikaviṃśatiḥ // VAkK_2.29 //
```

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

In an unwholesome citta that is isolated, there are twenty mental factors:

the ten great-ground factors, the six great-ground factors of affliction, the two great-ground factors of the unwholesome, and initial and sustained thought.

An “isolated” citta is one in which ignorance alone is present and no other affliction, such as greed, occurs.

There are likewise twenty in an unwholesome citta associated with a view—the same twenty as in the isolated case.

**Voice: Objector**

But because view is additional, should there not be twenty-one?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

No. What is called “view” is a particular form of discernment belonging to the great-ground factors themselves.

An unwholesome citta associated with view is one in which there is wrong view, attachment to views, or attachment to rules and observances.

When an unwholesome citta is associated with one of the four afflictions—greed, hostility, conceit, or doubt—there are twenty-one factors: that affliction together with the twenty stated for the isolated case.

Likewise, when citta is associated with one of the secondary afflictions beginning with anger, there are twenty-one: the same twenty plus that secondary affliction.

There are also twenty-one when remorse is present. Remorse itself is the twenty-first.

In summary: in an isolated unwholesome citta and in an unwholesome citta associated with view, there are twenty factors; when it is associated with another affliction or secondary affliction, there are twenty-one.

## Reconstruction of the Counts

### Isolated unwholesome citta

```text
10 mahābhūmikas
6 kleśa-mahābhūmikas
2 akuśala-mahābhūmikas
vitarka
vicāra
= 20
```

Here ignorance is present without greed, hostility, conceit, doubt, or another distinct affliction.

### Unwholesome citta associated with view

```text
same base profile
= 20
```

View does not increase the count because it is not an additional mental factor alongside discernment. It is a particular afflicted form of discernment.

The three views named here are:

```text
mithyādṛṣṭi
    wrong view

dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
    attachment to views

śīlavrataparāmarśa
    attachment to rules and observances
```

### Citta associated with another root affliction

```text
base twenty
+ greed, hostility, conceit, or doubt
= 21
```

### Citta associated with a secondary affliction

```text
base twenty
+ one secondary affliction beginning with anger
= 21
```

### Citta associated with remorse

```text
base twenty
+ remorse
= 21
```

## Analytical Determination

The passage distinguishes two relations between a named mental condition and the universal factor-set.

First, a named condition may be a **special determination of a factor already counted**:

```text
view
    = a special form of prajñā
    = no numerical addition
```

Second, a condition may be a **distinct associated factor**:

```text
greed / hostility / conceit / doubt
secondary affliction
remorse
    = additional factor
    = count rises from twenty to twenty-one
```

The count therefore depends on ontological and functional identity, not merely on the number of names used in description.

## New Technical Terms

- **āveṇika citta** — an isolated citta in which ignorance alone is present without another distinct affliction
  - **status:** newly defined in this passage
  - **function:** establishes the twenty-factor unwholesome base profile

- **prajñāviśeṣa** — a special form of discernment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** explains why view does not add another factor

- **mithyādṛṣṭi** — wrong view
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal term
  - **function:** one form of unwholesome view-associated citta

- **dṛṣṭiparāmarśa** — attachment to views
  - **status:** newly specified here in this sequence
  - **function:** one form of unwholesome view-associated citta

- **śīlavrataparāmarśa** — attachment to rules and observances
  - **status:** newly specified here in this sequence
  - **function:** one form of unwholesome view-associated citta

## Translation Consequences

`āveṇika` should not be translated merely as “specific.” The Bhāṣya explicitly defines it as a citta in which ignorance occurs alone, without another affliction such as greed.

`dṛṣṭi` should not be counted as a mental factor separate from `prajñā` in this context. It is an afflicted specialization of discernment.

## Compact Result

```text
isolated unwholesome citta              = 20
unwholesome citta associated with view  = 20
with greed, hostility, conceit, or doubt = 21
with a secondary affliction             = 21
with remorse                            = 21
```

The governing rule is:

```text
specialization of an already-counted factor
    does not increase the count

a genuinely distinct associated factor
    increases the count by one
```
