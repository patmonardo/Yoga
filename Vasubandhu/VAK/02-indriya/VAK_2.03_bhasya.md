# VAK_2.03 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> nikāyasthitisaṃkleśavyavadānādhipatyataḥ /
> jīvitaṃ vedanāḥ pañca śraddhādyāś cendriyaṃ matāḥ // VAkK_2.3 //

## Best Current Translation

> Life, the five feelings, and the five beginning with faith are regarded as faculties because they exercise governing efficacy, respectively, over the persistence of the living class, defilement, and purification.

The verse continues the defining principle established in VAK 2.1–2.2:

```text
indriya
    =
a determination insofar as it exercises ādhipatya
```

Here three new forms of governing efficacy are introduced:

```text
jīvita
    → persistence

five feelings
    → defilement

faith and the following four
    → purification
```

---

## Bhāṣya Source

> nikāyasabhāgasthitau jīvitendriyasyādhipatyaṃ saṃkleśe vedanānām /
>
> tathā hi sukhāyāṃ vedanāyāṃ rāgo 'nuśete duḥkhāyāṃ pratigho 'duḥkhāsukhāyām avidyety uktaṃ sūtre /

---

# 1. The Life Faculty

## Source

> nikāyasabhāgasthitau jīvitendriyasyādhipatyam /

## Translation

> The life faculty possesses governing efficacy with respect to the persistence of the homogeneous class of living beings.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya expands the Kārikā’s compact `nikāya-sthiti` as:

```text
nikāyasabhāga-sthiti
```

This is not simply biological survival and not the persistence of an ego-substance. It is the continuation of the present living series within its corresponding class of existence.

The important terms are:

```text
nikāya
    group, collection, embodied class

sabhāga
    sharing a common kind or likeness

sthiti
    persistence, maintenance, continued standing
```

Thus the life faculty governs:

```text
the continued standing
of the present living continuum
as a member of its determinate class
```

The doctrine does not claim that `jīvita` creates the aggregates. Nor is life a separate self inhabiting them. Its function is narrower and more exact:

```text
jīvita-indriya
    governs the continuation
    of an already constituted living series
```

The faculty is therefore defined through persistence, not through substance.

---

# 2. The Five Feeling Faculties

## Source

> saṃkleśe vedanānām /

## Translation

> The feelings possess governing efficacy with respect to defilement.

The five feeling faculties are:

```text
sukha
    bodily pleasure

duḥkha
    bodily pain

saumanasya
    mental gladness

daurmanasya
    mental distress

upekṣā
    neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
```

The Bhāṣya does not say that feeling is itself identical with defilement. Feeling governs defilement because it is the affective field in dependence upon which particular afflictions remain latent and become active.

This is explained through a scriptural citation.

---

# 3. The Distribution of Latent Affliction

## Source

> tathā hi sukhāyāṃ vedanāyāṃ rāgo 'nuśete duḥkhāyāṃ pratigho 'duḥkhāsukhāyām avidyety uktaṃ sūtre /

## Translation

> Thus it is said in the sūtra: attachment lies latent in pleasant feeling, aversion lies latent in painful feeling, and ignorance lies latent in neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling.

## Voice

**Voice: scriptural citation introduced by Vasubandhu**

The relation is:

| Feeling-tone | Latent affliction |
|---|---|
| pleasant feeling | rāga — attachment |
| painful feeling | pratigha — aversion |
| neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling | avidyā — ignorance |

The key verb is:

```text
anuśete
    lies latent in
    rests upon
    adheres as an underlying tendency
```

This is more precise than saying:

```text
pleasure causes attachment
pain causes aversion
neutrality causes ignorance
```

The Bhāṣya describes a relation of latent dependence. The feeling supplies the affective basis upon which the corresponding affliction can settle and persist.

Thus:

```text
vedanā
    ≠ kleśa

vedanā
    = affective condition
      in dependence upon which
      a kleśa can lie latent
```

This is an important feature of the Kośa’s Dharma Science. It distinguishes the experiential condition from the affliction that appropriates it.

Pleasure is not intrinsically attachment. Pain is not intrinsically aversion. Neutral feeling is not intrinsically ignorance. But each provides a characteristic field in which a corresponding defilement can become established.

---

# 4. Why Feeling Has Governing Efficacy

The five feelings are faculties because changes in the affective field govern the form taken by defilement.

```text
pleasant feeling
    opens the field of attachment

painful feeling
    opens the field of resistance and aversion

neutral feeling
    opens the field of unexamined indifference
    in which ignorance remains concealed
```

The neutral case is especially important.

Ignorance does not always appear as an active distortion. It can persist precisely where experience seems affectively uneventful. Neither pleasure nor pain forces the continuum to react decisively, and the absence of affective disturbance allows non-knowing to remain unnoticed.

Therefore the five feelings govern `saṃkleśa` not because they are morally impure substances but because they organize the affective conditions under which the continuum becomes entangled.

---

# 5. The Five Faculties Beginning with Faith

## Source

> vyavadāne śraddhādīnāṃ pañcānām /

## Translation

> The five beginning with faith possess governing efficacy with respect to purification.

The five are:

```text
śraddhā
vīrya
smṛti
samādhi
prajñā
```

Working translations:

```text
śraddhā
    confidence / faith

vīrya
    energy / disciplined exertion

smṛti
    recollection

samādhi
    collectedness / concentration

prajñā
    discriminative knowing / principle
```

These are not merely desirable mental qualities. They are faculties because purification depends upon their governing efficacy.

---

# 6. The Twofold Work of the Path Faculties

## Source

> tathā hi taiḥ kleśāś ca viṣkabhyante mārgaś cāvāhyate /

## Translation

> For through them the afflictions are suppressed, and the path is drawn forward.

This sentence gives the internal logic of `vyavadāna`.

Purification has two inseparable sides:

```text
negative
    the afflictions are held down

positive
    the path is brought forward
```

The first verb is:

```text
viṣkabhyante
    are checked
    held down
    restrained
    prevented from operating freely
```

The second is:

```text
āvāhyate
    is drawn onward
    brought forward
    conducted into actuality
```

Purification is therefore not merely the absence of impurity.

```text
vyavadāna
    ≠ only removal of kleśa
```

It is:

```text
restraint of the contrary
    together with
positive advancement of the path
```

This is one of the most important Bhāṣya clarifications so far.

The path faculties perform a double action:

```text
śraddhā–prajñā
    suppress the forces that obstruct liberation
    and
    generate the forward movement of liberation itself
```

They do not merely clear a space in which the path might later appear. Their operation is already the path becoming active.

---

# 7. Each Is Individually a Faculty

## Source

> ata ete 'pi pratyekam indriyam iṣṭāḥ /

## Translation

> Therefore each of these also is individually accepted as a faculty.

The word `pratyekam` matters.

The five feelings are not one collective faculty called “feeling.” Each has its own governing efficacy.

Likewise, the five beginning with faith are not merely components of one undifferentiated purifying force. Each is separately an `indriya`.

This means:

```text
śraddhā governs in its own way
vīrya governs in its own way
smṛti governs in its own way
samādhi governs in its own way
prajñā governs in its own way
```

The present passage does not yet give the exact differentiated function of each. It establishes only that each makes an irreducible contribution to purification.

We should therefore resist collapsing them prematurely into one transcendental faculty or one generalized “mindfulness” process.

---

# 8. Argument Reconstruction

The full argument of VAK 2.3 is:

```text
An indriya is a determination possessing governing efficacy.
    ↓
The life faculty governs the continued standing
of the living series within its homogeneous class.
    ↓
The five feelings govern defilement.
    ↓
They govern it because attachment, aversion,
and ignorance lie latent in dependence upon
pleasant, painful, and neutral feeling.
    ↓
The five beginning with faith govern purification.
    ↓
They govern purification in two ways:
they suppress the afflictions
and draw the path forward.
    ↓
Because each makes its own governing contribution,
each is individually counted as a faculty.
```

The Kārikā gives three classifications.

The Bhāṣya reveals three distinct causal structures:

```text
continuity
latent affective dependence
path advancement
```

---

# 9. Logical Determination

VAK 2.3 expands the concept of governing efficacy beyond sensory apprehension.

## Persistence

```text
jīvita
    governs the continued standing
    of the living continuum
```

## Affective Entanglement

```text
vedanā
    governs the field
    in which affliction lies latent
```

## Purification

```text
śraddhā–prajñā
    govern both inhibition of affliction
    and advancement of the path
```

Thus `ādhipatya` now has at least three forms:

```text
sustaining governance
    jīvita

conditioning governance
    vedanā

transformative governance
    śraddhā and the following faculties
```

The chapter is developing a general science of operative powers.

A faculty need not be a sense organ. It may govern:

```text
whether a continuum persists
how it becomes affectively entangled
how it is practically transformed
```

---

# 10. Doctrinal Determination

This passage shows why the Kośa is a Dharma Science.

It does not merely state that pleasure is bad and faith is good. It asks:

```text
What exact functional relation
makes feeling relevant to defilement?

What exact functional relation
makes faith and the other four
relevant to purification?
```

The answers are:

```text
feeling supports the latency of affliction

path faculties suppress affliction
and positively conduct the path
```

The distinctions are functional and causal, not merely moral.

This is scientific even without a developed formal Logic because it:

```text
distinguishes condition from conditioned result
assigns different functions to different dharmas
uses scriptural claims as propositions to be analyzed
explains classification through operative efficacy
```

---

# 11. Restrained Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya gives us a very useful distinction between the negative and positive sides of transformation:

```text
viṣkambhana
    restraint of the opposed determination

mārgāvāhana
    positive bringing-forward of the path
```

This prevents purification from being reduced to mere negation.

```text
not-kleśa
    is not yet the complete path
```

The path is present only when the powers of purification positively generate its movement.

That resembles the distinction between:

```text
removing a contradiction
    and
producing the positive determination
that resolves it
```

But the primary Bhāṣya point remains practical and local. These five faculties operate within the conditioned continuum. They are not yet an Absolute Subject and should not be elevated into one.

---

# 12. New Technical Terms

- **nikāyasabhāga-sthiti** — persistence of the homogeneous living class or continuum
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the Kārikā's `nikāya-sthiti`
  - **function:** defines the governing domain of the life faculty

- **anuśete** — lies latent in; rests upon as an underlying tendency
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya vocabulary
  - **function:** explains the dependence of affliction upon feeling

- **aduhkhāsukha** — neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
  - **status:** doctrinal specification of neutral feeling
  - **function:** affective basis in which ignorance lies latent

- **viṣkambhana / viṣkabhyante** — checking, suppressing, or holding down
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** negative side of purification

- **mārgāvāhana / mārga āvāhyate** — drawing or conducting the path forward
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** positive side of purification

- **pratyekam** — individually, each separately
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya determination
  - **function:** establishes that each item is separately a faculty

---

# 13. Translation Consequences

The existing Kārikā file translates `nikāya-sthiti` as the “synthetic persistence of the living series.” The Bhāṣya supports “persistence of the living series,” but the adjective “synthetic” belongs to Organon interpretation rather than the primary translation.

The primary translation should therefore remain:

```text
nikāyasabhāga-sthiti
    =
persistence of the homogeneous living class / series
```

The existing statement that the path faculties govern purification is confirmed and made more exact:

```text
purification
    =
suppression of affliction
    +
positive advancement of the path
```
