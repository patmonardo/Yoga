# VAK 2.58 Bhāṣya — Puruṣakāraja and Adhipati Results

## Kārikā

```text
yadvalāj jāyate yat tat phalaṃ puruṣakārajam /
apūrvaḥ saṃskṛtasyaiva saṃskṛto 'dhipateḥ phalam // VAkK_2.58 //
```

## Continuous Translation

Whatever arises through the force of something is called a result produced by operative activity (*puruṣakāraja-phala*).

For example, the concentration belonging to a higher level arises through the force of an applied mind belonging to a lower level; an uncontaminated state arises through the force of a contaminated one; and a mind of magical creation arises through the force of a meditative mind. Other cases are to be understood in the same way.

Analytical cessation, however, should be described as that which is attained through the force of the Path.

A conditioned dharma, different from one that has already arisen, is the dominant result (*adhipati-phala*) of every conditioned dharma.

What distinguishes an operative result from a dominant result?

A result belonging to an agent is an operative result. A result may be a dominant result even for what is not its agent.

For example, in relation to an artisan, the artifact is both an operative result and a dominant result. In relation to others, it is only a dominant result.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expositor** — defines the two result-types and gives examples.

**Voice: Objector** — asks what differentiates the operative result from the dominant result.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expositor** — answers through the distinction between agent and non-agent.

## Doctrinal Reconstruction

### 1. Puruṣakāra does not mean a metaphysical person

The word *puruṣakāra* can mislead if read as the action of a permanent self. Here it designates operative efficacy: a dharma functions as an agent when a result arises through its force.

```text
operative force
    → determinate arising
    → puruṣakāraja result
```

The examples are transformations within conditioned series:

- an applied mind of a lower level enables concentration of a higher level;
- a contaminated state gives rise to an uncontaminated one;
- meditative consciousness produces a consciousness capable of magical creation.

The relevant “agent” is therefore a causal function, not a substance or enduring self.

### 2. The Path and analytical cessation

The Bhāṣya preserves the distinction developed in the preceding verses. Analytical cessation is attained through the force of the Path, but it is not thereby reduced to an ordinary conditioned product.

```text
Path-force
    → transformation and acquisition
    → attainment of cessation
```

The Path is operative. Cessation is what becomes attainable when that operation has destroyed the relevant bond.

### 3. Adhipati-phala is broader than direct production

A dominant result is any new conditioned dharma standing under the dominance of another conditioned dharma. The relation is wider than direct agency.

```text
puruṣakāraja:
    the source actively performs a producing function

adhipati-phala:
    the source conditions or permits the result,
    whether or not it is the direct agent
```

Thus the same artifact is both kinds of result relative to its artisan, but only a dominant result relative to other conditions and persons.

### 4. Result is relational, not intrinsic

A result does not bear one result-status absolutely. Its classification depends on the precise relation in which it stands to a given cause.

```text
one result
    relative to direct agent       = puruṣakāraja
    relative to broader conditions = adhipati-phala
```

This prevents the causal system from being flattened into a single generic dependence relation.

## Translation Consequences

- **puruṣakāraja-phala** is best rendered as “result produced by operative activity” or “operative result,” not as evidence for a permanent person.
- **yad-valāt** emphasizes force or efficacy: the result arises through the causal power of something.
- **apūrva** marks a conditioned outcome other than what has already arisen; it need not imply creation from absolute nothing.
- **adhipati-phala** is the result of dominance, governance, or non-obstructive conditioning, and is broader than direct production.

## New Technical Terms

- **puruṣakāraja-phala** — result produced through operative efficacy
  - **status:** newly defined in this passage
  - **function:** distinguishes direct causal agency from wider dominance

- **adhipati-phala** — dominant result
  - **status:** previously named, now precisely distinguished
  - **function:** classifies a new conditioned outcome relative to a dominant condition

- **kartṛ** — agent, performer
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-level explanatory term
  - **function:** marks the source that directly performs the productive operation

- **akartṛ** — non-agent
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-level explanatory term
  - **function:** shows that a source may still stand in a dominance relation without directly producing the result

## Logical Determination

VAK 2.58 differentiates two judgments of causal relation:

```text
If Y arises through the operative force of X,
then Y is X's puruṣakāraja result.

If Y is a new conditioned outcome standing under X's dominance,
then Y is X's adhipati result.
```

The first determines direct efficacy. The second determines the broader field of conditioning.

## Organon Interpretation

This passage is especially important for the genetic form of judgment.

A merely empirical judgment says:

```text
Y followed X.
```

A rationally illuminated judgment asks:

```text
Did X actively produce Y?
Or did X belong only to the dominant field
within which Y arose?
```

The result is therefore the lawful form of the ground only when the mode of manifestation is determined.

In the language of *satkārya*:

```text
ground as productive power
    → operative manifestation

ground as dominant condition
    → permitted or governed manifestation
```

The effect is not absolutely new, because its arising is already rationally determined by the causal field. Yet it is empirically new as a conditioned event. This preserves the dual structure:

```text
Rational Nature
    lawful causal determination

Empirical Nature
    newly arisen conditioned result
```

The two together form the complete judgment of experience.
