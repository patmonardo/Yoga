## Continuous Translation

In gradual death, four faculties cease: the body faculty, the life faculty, the mind faculty, and the neutral-feeling faculty. These do not cease separately. This rule is to be understood when death occurs with an afflicted or neutral mind.

But when one dies while established in a wholesome mind, then in every realm the faculties already stated cease, together with five additional faculties—the faculties beginning with faith. This is because those five are necessarily present in a wholesome mind. Thus, in the formless realm eight faculties cease, and in the form realm thirteen; the remaining cases are to be calculated in the same manner.

Since all matters concerning faculties are examined in the section on faculties, the following question is now asked: which fruit of religious practice is attained through how many faculties?

The two final fruits are attained through nine faculties. Which are the two final fruits? The fruit of stream-entry and arhatship. Which are the two middle fruits? The fruit of once-returning and the fruit of non-returning.

The fruit of stream-entry is attained through nine faculties: the five beginning with faith, the two realization-faculties other than the faculty of one who has known, and the faculties of mind and neutral feeling. The faculty called “I shall know” is to be understood as present on the uninterrupted path, and the faculty of knowledge on the path of liberation. The fruit is attained through both, because respectively they draw forth and provide the supporting basis for the acquisition of disconnection.

Arhatship is likewise attained through nine faculties: the five beginning with faith, the two realization-faculties other than the faculty called “I shall know,” the mind faculty, and one among the faculties of pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling.

Each of the fruits of once-returning and non-returning may be attained through seven, eight, or nine faculties. How so?

If a person proceeding in regular sequence attains the fruit of once-returning through a mundane path, it is attained through seven faculties: the five beginning with faith, together with neutral feeling and mind. If it is attained through a supramundane path, it is attained through eight faculties, the faculty of knowledge being the eighth. But if one who is already more extensively free from desire attains it directly, it is attained through nine faculties, in the same manner as the fruit of stream-entry.

The fruit of non-returning is likewise attained through seven faculties when one proceeding in regular sequence reaches it by a mundane path, just as with the fruit of once-returning. If reached through a supramundane path, it is attained through eight in the same way. If one already free from desire attains it directly, it is attained through nine, as with stream-entry. The difference is that one among pleasure, gladness, and neutral feeling is present.

## Argument Reconstruction

The unit contains two distinct calculations.

### 1. Death with a wholesome mind

The ordinary terminal set is increased by the five faculties beginning with faith:

```text
ordinary cessation profile
    + śraddhā
    + vīrya
    + smṛti
    + samādhi
    + prajñā
```

These five cease because they are necessarily present whenever the terminal mind is wholesome.

### 2. Attainment of the fruits

The count is determined not merely by the fruit attained, but by the route of attainment:

```text
final fruits:
    stream-entry = nine
    arhatship = nine

middle fruits:
    mundane sequential route = seven
    supramundane sequential route = eight
    direct attainment by one already free from desire = nine
```

Thus the count does not identify a simple essence of each fruit. It records the operative faculty-configuration through which the fruit is attained.

## Voice

- **Voice:** Vasubandhu — transition from cessation analysis to fruit-attainment analysis.
- **Voice:** Vaibhāṣika classificatory doctrine — counts of faculties and path stages.
- **Voice:** Questioner — asks which fruits are final or middle and how the variable counts arise.

## New Technical Terms

- **śrāmaṇya-phala** — fruit of religious practice or ascetic discipline.
  - **Status:** introduced in this Bhāṣya unit as the new topic.
  - **Function:** names the four fruits whose attainment is analyzed through faculty-counts.

- **ānantarya-mārga** — uninterrupted path.
  - **Status:** not stated in the Kārikā.
  - **Function:** path-moment associated here with the faculty called “I shall know.”

- **vimukti-mārga** — path of liberation.
  - **Status:** not stated in the Kārikā.
  - **Function:** path-moment associated here with the faculty of knowledge.

- **visaṃyoga-prāpti** — acquisition of disconnection.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this unit.
  - **Function:** the result for which the uninterrupted path is the producer and the liberation path the supporting basis.

- **ānupūrvika** — one proceeding in regular sequence.
  - **Status:** introduced in the explanation of the middle fruits.
  - **Function:** distinguishes sequential attainment from direct attainment by one already free from desire.

- **bhūyovītarāga / vītarāga** — one already more extensively free from desire / free from desire.
  - **Status:** introduced in the variable-count explanation.
  - **Function:** explains direct attainment of a middle fruit through nine faculties.

## Translation Consequences

The phrase **antya-phalayoḥ** does not mean the chronologically last two fruits. The Bhāṣya explicitly defines the “two final fruits” as stream-entry and arhatship—the two extremes of the fourfold series. Once-returning and non-returning are the two middle fruits.

The word **āpti** is best translated here as “attainment,” not mere possession. The issue is the active faculty-complex through which a fruit is reached.

## Doctrinal Determination

The same fruit may have more than one operational realization. Especially for the middle fruits, the faculty-count varies according to whether attainment is mundane, supramundane, sequential, or direct.

The doctrine therefore distinguishes:

```text
fruit as attained result
    from
route and faculty-complex of attainment
```

## Limited Organon Contact

This passage is compatible with reading an indriya-complex as a planning configuration:

```text
one practical objective
    = attainment of a fruit

multiple coordinated faculties
    = operative realization of that objective

alternative routes
    = different valid configurations reaching the same result
```

The text itself, however, remains a technical path-calculus. Its immediate claim is not a general systems theory but that achievement of one fruit can be mediated by different coordinated sets of faculties.
