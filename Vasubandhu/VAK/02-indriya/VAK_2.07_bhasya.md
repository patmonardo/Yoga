# VAK_2.07_bhasya — Bodily Pain, Bodily Pleasure, and Third-Dhyāna Pleasure

## Kārikā Anchor

```text
duḥkhendriyam aśātā yā kāyikī vedanā sukham /
śātā dhyāne tṛtīye tu caitasī sā sukhendriyam // VAkK_2.7 //
```

**Best current translation**

> The pain faculty is unpleasant bodily feeling. The pleasure faculty is pleasant bodily feeling; but in the third dhyāna, pleasant mental feeling is the pleasure faculty.

Kārikā file: [VAK_2.07.md](./VAK_2.07.md)

## Bhāṣya Source Passage

```text
tatra cakṣurādīnāṃ puruṣendriyaparyantānāṃ kṛto nirdeśaḥ /
jīvitendriyasya viprayuktatvād viprayukteṣv eva kariṣyate nirdeśaḥ /
śraddhādīnāṃ caitteṣu kariṣyate /
sukhādīnām ājñāsyāmīndriyādīnāṃ ca kartavyaḥ /
so 'yaṃ kriyate /

duḥkhendriyam aśātā yā kāyikī vedanā /
aśātety upaghātikā duḥkhety arthaḥ /

sukham śātā /
sukhendriyaṃ kāyikī śātā vedanā /
śātety anugrāhikā sukhety arthaḥ /

dhyāne tṛtīye tu caitasī sā sukhendriyam /
tṛtīye tu dhyāne saiva śātā vedanā caitasī sukhendriyam /
na hi tatra kāyikī vedanā 'sti /
pañcavijñānakāyābhāvāt /
```

## Continuous Translation

> The faculties beginning with the eye and extending through the male faculty have now been explained. Because the life faculty is dissociated from mind, it will be explained among the factors dissociated from mind. The faculties beginning with faith will be explained among the mental factors. What remains to be explained are the faculties beginning with pleasure and those beginning with the faculty called “I shall know.” That explanation now follows.
>
> The pain faculty is unpleasant bodily feeling. “Unpleasant” means injurious or afflictive; this is the meaning of “pain.”
>
> Pleasure is pleasant. The pleasure faculty is pleasant bodily feeling. “Pleasant” means beneficial or supportive; this is the meaning of “pleasure.”
>
> But in the third dhyāna, that pleasant mental feeling is the pleasure faculty. In the third dhyāna, the same pleasant feeling, although mental, is called the pleasure faculty, because no bodily feeling exists there, since the five groups of sensory consciousness are absent.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is expository rather than polemical. Vasubandhu first locates the remaining faculties within the larger architecture of the chapter and then defines the first two feeling-faculties.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya begins by explaining why the remaining faculties are not treated together in one place.

```text
first ten material faculties
    already explained

jīvita
    will be explained among cittaviprayukta factors

śraddhā through prajñā
    will be explained among mental factors

five feelings and three realization faculties
    must now be defined
```

The feeling-classification ordinarily uses two coordinates:

```text
hedonic tone:
    pleasant / unpleasant

locus or mode:
    bodily / mental
```

Thus:

```text
unpleasant bodily feeling
    = duḥkhendriya

pleasant bodily feeling
    = sukhendriya
```

The third dhyāna creates an exception. There the pleasant feeling is mental, yet it is still classified as `sukha`, not `saumanasya`.

The reason is not that bodily feeling has somehow become mental. Rather, the five sensory consciousness-groups are absent, so bodily feeling cannot occur. The pleasant mental feeling characteristic of that meditative level therefore receives the name `sukhendriya`.

## Translation Consequences

The Bhāṣya sharpens three terms.

### aśātā

```text
aśātā
    = upaghātikā
    = injurious, afflictive, working against the continuum
```

“Unpleasant” is therefore not merely a report of preference. It names a feeling-tone experienced as injurious.

### śātā

```text
śātā
    = anugrāhikā
    = beneficial, supportive, favorable to the continuum
```

Likewise, “pleasant” carries an operative sense. It supports or favors the living continuum.

### sukhendriya in the third dhyāna

The Kārikā’s exception must be preserved exactly:

```text
ordinary sukha
    pleasant bodily feeling

third-dhyāna sukha
    pleasant mental feeling
```

The same name does not imply the same bodily locus in every sphere.

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage does not define pleasure and pain as abstract qualities existing independently of cognition. They are determinate modes of feeling.

```text
vedanā
    is classified by
        hedonic function
        and cognitive locus
```

This is important for the doctrine of `indriya`. A feeling becomes a faculty not because it is a separate planning agent, but because it occupies a governing functional position within the organization of experience.

Your proposed interpretation can be stated cautiously as follows:

```text
indriya
    = a system-level functional determination
      gathering many subordinate processes
      beneath one governing Idea
```

On that reading, the Kośa and Sāṃkhya may disagree over which bodily operations deserve independent faculty status, while still sharing the deeper intuition that a faculty is not merely an anatomical part. It is an organized functional category.

That comparative interpretation goes beyond the literal Bhāṣya, but it fits the structural evidence accumulated in VAK 2.1–2.7.

## Minimal Organon Contact

The third-dhyāna exception shows that the classification is not tied mechanically to anatomy.

```text
same functional Idea:
    pleasure-faculty

variable empirical realization:
    bodily in ordinary contexts
    mental in the third dhyāna
```

This is precisely the kind of Rational-Empirical relation the chapter repeatedly exhibits: a stable functional determination can be instantiated differently according to the level and organization of experience.

## New Technical Terms

- **upaghātika** — injurious, afflictive, working against the continuum
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** explains `aśātā` and therefore `duḥkha`

- **anugrāhika** — beneficial, supportive, favoring the continuum
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** explains `śātā` and therefore `sukha`

- **pañca-vijñāna-kāya** — the five groups of sensory consciousness
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary
  - **function:** explains why bodily feeling is absent in the third dhyāna

- **caitasī vedanā** — mental feeling
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā and explained in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** identifies the special mode of pleasure in the third dhyāna
