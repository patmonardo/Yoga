# VAK 2.36 Bhāṣya — Prāpti, the Two Cessations, and the Seed-Theory Critique

## Kārikā

```text
nāmakāyādayaś ceti prāptir lābhaḥ samanvayaḥ /
prāptyaprāptī svasaṃtāna-patitānāṃ nirodhayoḥ // VAK 2.36 //
```

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

With respect to conditioned dharmas, acquisition and non-acquisition apply only to dharmas that belong to one’s own continuum, not to dharmas belonging to another continuum and not to dharmas that do not belong to any continuum.

With respect to the unconditioned, acquisition and non-acquisition apply to the two cessations.

All sentient beings possess cessation not produced through discriminative knowledge (*apratisaṃkhyānirodha*). Thus the higher Abhidharma asks, “Who possesses unconditioned dharmas?” and answers, “All sentient beings.”

All noble persons, except those at the first moment of the bondage-sequence, possess cessation produced through discriminative knowledge (*pratisaṃkhyānirodha*); some ordinary persons possess it as well. But no one possesses space. Therefore there is no acquisition of space. Where there is no acquisition, there is also no non-acquisition: this is the established doctrine.

Is acquisition, then, some distinct real entity?

It is said that this follows from scripture. For scripture says that, through the arising, obtaining, and possession of the ten dharmas of one beyond training, a noble person becomes one who has abandoned the five factors.

But then one would also have to possess non-sentient things and the belongings of other persons, because scripture says that a wheel-turning king is “possessed of” the seven treasures.

In that passage, however, “possession” means mastery: the king has authority and freedom of action regarding those treasures.

Why, then, should possession mean mastery there but a distinct substance elsewhere? What incompatibility is there in rejecting such a substance?

The incompatibility is this: acquisition has neither an identifiable intrinsic nature, as visible form, sound, greed, or hatred do, nor an identifiable function, as the eye and ear faculties do. Therefore it is inappropriate to regard it as a real substantial dharma.

Suppose one says that acquisition is the cause of the arising of dharmas. Then there would be no acquisition of the unconditioned. Moreover, how would dharmas arise that have not yet been acquired, or dharmas relinquished through movement between levels or through dispassion?

If their arising is caused by a co-arisen acquisition, what work remains for arising itself, or for the arising of arising?

Furthermore, among persons still bound by all the fetters, distinctions between weak, medium, and strong afflictions could not arise if acquisition were undifferentiated. Whatever explains those differences should itself be accepted as the cause of their arising. Therefore acquisition is not the cause of arising.

No one says that acquisition is the cause of arising.

What, then, is it?

It is the cause of classificatory determination. Without acquisition, there could be no determination, among persons whose minds are worldly, that “these are noble persons” and “these are ordinary persons.”

But this determination can be made through the difference between afflictions that have been abandoned and those that have not been abandoned.

How, then, is it established that an affliction has been abandoned in one person and not abandoned in another?

According to the Vaibhāṣikas, this is established when acquisition is present or absent.

But it is established through a difference in the support. Through the power of the paths of seeing and cultivation, the support of noble persons is transformed in such a way that it is no longer capable of producing the afflictions abandoned by those paths. When the support has become like rice burned by fire and incapable of functioning as seed, the person is said to have abandoned the affliction. When the seed-state has merely been damaged by a mundane path, the same expression is used provisionally. The opposite condition is called “not having abandoned the affliction.”

Thus one is conventionally said to possess an affliction that has not been abandoned and not to possess an affliction that has been abandoned.

Wholesome dharmas are also of two kinds: those that arise without effort and those that arise through effort, called respectively innate and practice-produced.

A person is said to possess the effortless wholesome dharmas when the seed-state of the support has not been damaged, and not to possess them when it has been damaged, as in one whose wholesome roots have been severed. Such damage should be understood as caused by wrong view. Yet the seed-state of wholesome dharmas is never completely eradicated from the continuum.

As for dharmas produced through effort, a person is said to possess them when, through their prior arising, the continuum has acquired a capacity for their renewed arising, and not to possess them when that capacity has been obstructed.

Therefore what receives the name “possession” is nothing other than the seed itself when it has not been removed or damaged, together with the developed capacity present at the time of mastery. There is no additional distinct entity.

What is this “seed”?

It is name-and-form capable of producing a result, either immediately or through a succession, because of a particular transformation of the continuum.

What is transformation?

It is the continuum’s becoming otherwise.

What is the continuum?

It is the conditioned dharmas of the three times standing in the relation of cause and result.

Scripture also says that one who “possesses greed” is incapable of cultivating the four establishments of mindfulness. Here possession means tolerating greed or failing to expel it. So long as one tolerates it and does not expel it, one remains incapable of cultivating those practices.

Accordingly, possession in every case is a merely designated dharma, not a substantially real dharma. Non-possession is simply the negation of that designation.

The Vaibhāṣikas, however, declare both acquisition and non-acquisition to be real substances.

Why?

Because, they say, this is our established doctrine.

## Argument Structure

### 1. Scope of prāpti and aprāpti

```text
conditioned dharmas:
    only those belonging to one’s own continuum

unconditioned dharmas:
    the two cessations

space:
    neither acquisition nor non-acquisition
```

### 2. Scriptural argument and reply

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

Scripture speaks of persons as possessing noble dharmas; therefore possession must be real.

**Voice: Objector / Vasubandhu’s critical line**

Scripture also says that a king possesses seven treasures. There the word means mastery, not an additional entity. Scriptural usage alone cannot establish a separate substance.

### 3. Ontological test

A proposed real dharma should have either:

```text
an identifiable intrinsic nature
or
an identifiable function
```

Acquisition displays neither. Hence its substantial reality is doubtful.

### 4. Causal argument rejected

If acquisition caused the arising of dharmas:

- acquisition of the unconditioned would be impossible;
- unacquired or relinquished dharmas could never arise;
- arising itself would become redundant;
- differences in strength among afflictions would remain unexplained.

The Bhāṣya therefore rejects acquisition as a production-cause.

### 5. Vaibhāṣika classificatory defense

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

Acquisition is required to distinguish:

```text
noble / ordinary
abandoned / unabandoned
possessed / unpossessed
```

### 6. Continuum-and-seed alternative

**Voice: Vasubandhu / critical reconstruction**

No distinct acquisition-dharma is required. The distinction is grounded in transformation of the support and the condition of the seed.

```text
support transformed by the path
    → no longer capable of producing an abandoned affliction

seed undamaged
    → effortless wholesome dharma remains available

practice creates renewed capacity
    → effort-produced dharma can arise again
```

Thus “possession” names a real capacity or incapacity of the continuum without naming an additional substance.

## Doctrinal Determination

This is one of the sharpest ontological disputes encountered so far.

The Vaibhāṣika model reifies possession as an additional dissociated formation:

```text
continuum
+ dharma
+ acquisition-relation as separate real entity
```

Vasubandhu’s critical model reduces it to the transformed causal organization of the continuum:

```text
continuum transformed
    → seed preserved, damaged, or rendered incapable
    → possession-language applied conventionally
```

The decisive move is from a **relation-substance** to a **dispositional continuity**.

## Relation to Yoga’s Vāsanā–Saṃskāra Architecture

The present Bhāṣya does not introduce *vāsanā*. Its own technical sequence is:

```text
bīja
santati
pariṇāma
sāmarthya / vaśitva
```

Nevertheless, the comparison with Yoga is important.

Yoga often explains the continuity of latent determination through the relation of *saṃskāra* and *vāsanā*. Here Vasubandhu explains possession without a substantial *prāpti* by appeal to:

```text
seed
transformation of the continuum
capacity for renewed arising
```

The two systems are therefore addressing a similar structural problem:

> How can a determination remain effective when it is not presently manifest?

The Kośa’s answer here is seed-capacity within a transformed continuum. The Yogic extension through *vāsanā* gives that latent continuity a more explicit formative architecture.

This comparison should remain provisional until the later chapters develop the Kośa’s own seed, latent-affliction, karma, and path theories more fully.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratisaṃkhyānirodha** — cessation attained through discriminative knowledge.
  - **Status:** previously encountered as one of the unconditioned dharmas.
  - **Function:** object of acquisition for noble persons and some ordinary persons.

- **apratisaṃkhyānirodha** — cessation not produced through discriminative knowledge.
  - **Status:** previously encountered.
  - **Function:** universally possessed by sentient beings according to the Abhidharma citation.

- **vaśitva** — mastery, control, or capacity for free exercise.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this argument.
  - **Function:** alternative non-substantial meaning of “possession.”

- **āśrayaparāvṛtti / support-transformation** — the support becoming altered through the power of the path.
  - **Status:** introduced here in explanatory form.
  - **Function:** explains abandonment without a separate acquisition-dharma.

- **bījabhāva** — seed-state or capacity to generate a future result.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-only in the present local sequence.
  - **Function:** grounds continued possession and non-possession dispositionally.

- **santatipariṇāmaviśeṣa** — a particular transformation of the continuum.
  - **Status:** newly explicit.
  - **Function:** explains how name-and-form becomes capable of producing a future result.

- **prajñaptidharma** — a dharma existing by designation.
  - **Status:** important critical term.
  - **Function:** Vasubandhu’s classification of possession rather than a substantially real entity.

- **dravyadharma** — a substantially real dharma.
  - **Status:** doctrinal contrast term.
  - **Function:** Vaibhāṣika status assigned to acquisition and non-acquisition.

## Translation Consequences

1. **Prāpti should not be translated simply as “ownership.”** It includes obtaining, continued possession, status-classification, and latent capacity.
2. **Samanvāgama is context-sensitive.** It may mean mastery, tolerance, continued association, or technical possession.
3. **Bīja is not a miniature substance.** It is name-and-form rendered capable of producing a result through transformation of the continuum.
4. **Santati is causal continuity, not personal identity.** It is the three-time series of conditioned dharmas related as cause and result.
5. **Abandonment is structural incapacity.** An affliction is abandoned when the transformed support can no longer generate it.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

The passage distinguishes two models of state attribution.

```text
Reified state model:
    state relation is an additional entity

Dispositional state model:
    state attribution records the transformed capacities
    of the underlying continuum
```

Vasubandhu’s critical alternative is much closer to a stateful system in which status is inferred from causal organization rather than stored as an unexplained extra object.

```text
Possesses(Dharma, Continuum)
    iff
Continuum retains the seed-capacity or acquired mastery
for the relevant dharma
```

The Organon significance is substantial, but the present pass should retain the Bhāṣya’s own conclusion: *prāpti* is a designation grounded in continuity, transformation, seed, and capacity—not an additional real substance.
