# VAK 2.32 Bhāṣya — Shame, Moral Concern, Affection, and Respect

## Kārikā

```text
ahrīr agurutā avadye bhayādarśitvam atrapā /
prema śraddhā gurutvaṃ hrīḥ te punaḥ kāmarūpayoḥ // VAkK_2.32 //
```

## Continuous Translation

The text will now explain the distinctions, established in the treatises, among certain mental factors.

What is the distinction between shamelessness and lack of moral concern?

**Shamelessness is lack of respect.**

Shamelessness is absence of reverence toward qualities and toward persons possessing qualities; it is refusal of subordination, fearlessness, and unwillingness to be governed. It is the dharma opposed to respect.

**Lack of moral concern is failure to see danger in what is blameworthy.**

What is called blameworthy is that which good persons condemn. Lack of moral concern is failure to recognize danger in it. Here “danger” means the undesirable result because of which one is afraid.

How should “failure to see danger” be understood? Does it mean seeing the absence of danger, or failing to see danger?

What follows from either alternative?

If it is seeing the absence of danger, it would be discernment. If it is failing to see danger, it would be ignorance.

But neither seeing is itself the state of being one who sees, nor is not-seeing itself the state of being one who does not see. Rather, the secondary affliction that serves as the cause of either is what is called lack of moral concern.

Others say: shamelessness is failure to be ashamed of faults with reference to oneself; lack of moral concern is failure to be ashamed with reference to others.

But how can these two stand together at the same time if they depend upon two different references?

It is not said that one simultaneously considers oneself and another. Rather, there is a kind of lack of shame that operates even when one is considering oneself; that is shamelessness. There is another that operates when one is considering others; that is lack of moral concern.

By reversing these, shame and moral concern should be understood.

According to the first account, shame is respect, submission, and not being governed by fearlessness; moral concern is recognition of danger in blameworthy acts.

According to the second account, they are the two forms of shame arising with reference to oneself and to others.

What is the distinction between affection and respect?

**Affection is faith.**

Affection is twofold: afflicted and unafflicted. Afflicted affection is craving, as toward children, spouse, and so forth. Unafflicted affection is faith toward a teacher, a preceptor, or one endowed with qualities.

There can be faith without affection: faith whose objects are suffering and its origin.

There can be affection without faith: afflicted affection.

There can be both: faith whose objects are cessation and the path.

There can be neither, apart from these modes.

Among persons, there can be affection without respect, as toward children, spouse, companions in the religious life, or pupils. There can be respect without affection, as toward teachers other than one’s own. There can be both toward one’s own teacher. There can be neither apart from these cases.

Faith is confidence in qualities. Affection is the fondness that follows upon it. Therefore, others say that affection is not identical with faith.

**Respect is shame.**

Respect is submission to authority. Shame is the embarrassment that follows upon it. Therefore, others say that respect is not identical with shame.

**These occur again in the desire and form realms.**

Affection and respect do not occur in the formless realm.

Objection: But faith and shame, being wholesome great-ground factors, are also present there.

Response: Faith is twofold: directed toward dharmas and directed toward persons. Submission to authority is likewise twofold. In the formless realm, the forms of faith and shame having persons as their objects do not occur. It is these person-directed forms that are intended here by affection and respect.

## Reconstruction of the Argument

The Bhāṣya gives two alternative analyses of the negative ethical pair:

```text
first account:
    shamelessness
        = lack of respect toward qualities and qualified persons

    lack of moral concern
        = failure to recognize danger in blameworthy action

second account:
    shamelessness
        = absence of shame with reference to oneself

    lack of moral concern
        = absence of shame with reference to others
```

Vasubandhu does not force a final reduction of one account into the other. Instead, he shows how both preserve a real distinction.

The same method is then applied to two positive relational pairs:

```text
faith and affection
respect and shame
```

The Kārikā uses compressed identity-formulas:

```text
prema = śraddhā
gurutva = hrī
```

The Bhāṣya immediately qualifies them:

```text
faith is confidence in qualities
    affection follows from it

respect is submission to authority
    shame follows from it
```

Thus the verse states a close doctrinal identification, while the prose preserves causal and functional distinction.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the distinctions and develops the objections and replies.

**Voice: Objector** — asks how lack of moral concern can be distinguished from discernment or ignorance; later asks how the self-regarding and other-regarding analyses can coexist; finally objects that faith and shame must also exist in the formless realm.

**Voice: Other teachers** — distinguish shamelessness and lack of moral concern by reference to self and others; distinguish affection from faith and respect from shame as consequents rather than identities.

## Doctrinal Determinations

### 1. Ethical restraint is not a single undifferentiated faculty

The text distinguishes at least two modes:

```text
respect for value and authority
recognition of danger in wrongdoing
```

Their opposites are therefore also distinct:

```text
shamelessness
    = refusal of normative authority

lack of moral concern
    = failure of consequence-sensitive restraint
```

### 2. A mental factor is not reducible to its cognitive content

Failure to see danger cannot simply be identified with ignorance, nor can seeing no danger simply be identified with discernment. The technical factor is the affliction that disposes the cognition in that direction.

```text
cognitive presentation
    ≠
underlying caitta disposition
```

### 3. Faith and affection overlap without being identical

The Bhāṣya explicitly constructs all four logical possibilities:

```text
faith without affection
affection without faith
both
neither
```

This demonstrates that doctrinal equivalence in the Kārikā is not strict extensional identity.

### 4. Respect and shame have the same structure

Respect is the governing orientation; shame is the affective restraint that follows from it.

### 5. Realm-location depends on object-range

Affection and respect are absent from the formless realm because the person-directed forms are absent there. Faith and shame as universal wholesome factors remain possible when directed toward dharmas rather than persons.

## New Technical Terms

- **āhrīkya** — shamelessness; lack of reverence toward qualities and those who possess them
  - **status:** previously encountered as an unwholesome great-ground factor
  - **function:** ethical opposition to respect and normative submission

- **anapatrāpya** — lack of moral concern; failure to recognize danger in blameworthy conduct
  - **status:** previously encountered as an unwholesome great-ground factor
  - **function:** absence of consequence-sensitive restraint

- **avadya** — the blameworthy; what good persons condemn
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory term in this passage
  - **function:** object-domain of moral concern

- **sapratīśatā** — submission to authority; acknowledgment of governance or normative superiority
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only terminology here
  - **function:** definition of respect and a basis of shame

- **prema** — affection or fondness, either afflicted or unafflicted
  - **status:** introduced in this local discussion
  - **function:** relational affect following faith, or afflicted attachment when corrupted

- **gurutva / gaurava** — weight, respect, reverence
  - **status:** introduced in this local discussion
  - **function:** person-directed normative orientation underlying shame

- **guṇasaṃbhāvanā** — confidence in or esteem for qualities
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only phrase
  - **function:** definition of faith in this argument

## Translation Consequences

The verse’s identity-statements should not be translated so rigidly that the Bhāṣya’s distinctions disappear.

```text
prema śraddhā
```

is best understood as:

> Unafflicted affection has faith as its basis and may therefore be identified with it in compressed classification.

Likewise:

```text
gurutvaṃ hrīḥ
```

means:

> Shame is grounded in respect and may therefore be classified through it.

The prose rejects strict identity in both cases.

## Analytical Result

VAK 2.32 shows the Bhāṣya correcting the compression of the Kārikā without rejecting it.

```text
Kārikā:
    doctrinal identity

Bhāṣya:
    causal, functional, and object-relative distinction
```

The passage also completes a precise ethical architecture:

```text
shamelessness
    refusal of normative weight

lack of moral concern
    refusal of danger-recognition

faith
    confidence in qualities

affection
    fondness arising from such confidence

respect
    submission to normative authority

shame
    restraint arising from such respect
```

The distinctions are relational, not merely emotional. Their presence depends on what is regarded, toward whom the factor is directed, and which realm permits that object-range.
