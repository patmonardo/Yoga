# VAK_2.11 — Ethical Classification of the Faculties

## Kārikā

```text
mano 'nyavittiśraddhādīny aṣṭakaṃ kuśalaṃ dvidhā /
daurmanasyaṃ mano 'nyā ca vittis tredhānyad ekadhā // VAK 2.11 //
```

## Working Translation of the Kārikā

```text
The eight are exclusively wholesome. Distress is twofold.
Mind and the other feelings are threefold. The rest is of one kind.
```

The Bhāṣya shows that the syntax is distributive rather than a single continuous list.

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

How many of the faculties are wholesome, how many unwholesome, and how many neutral?

First, the eight are exclusively wholesome: the five beginning with faith and the three beginning with the faculty called “I shall know.”

Distress is twofold: it may be wholesome or unwholesome.

Mind and the other feelings are threefold: wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral.

The rest are of one kind. What are these remaining faculties? The seven beginning with the eye, together with the life faculty, making eight. These are exclusively neutral.

## Classification

### Exclusively wholesome

```text
śraddhā
vīrya
smṛti
samādhi
prajñā
ājñāsyāmīndriya
ājñendriya
ājñātāvīndriya
```

The first five are the faculties that equip the path. The final three are the composite realization-faculties operating on the paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training.

### Wholesome or unwholesome

```text
daurmanasya
```

Distress is never neutral. It is ethically determined in one direction or the other.

### Wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral

```text
manas
sukha
saumanasya
duḥkha
upekṣā
```

The phrase **anyā vitti**, “the other feelings,” means the four feeling-faculties other than distress. Together with mind, these can occur in all three ethical modes.

### Exclusively neutral

```text
cakṣus
śrotra
ghrāṇa
jihvā
kāya
strī-indriya
puruṣa-indriya
jīvita
```

These seven material faculties and the life faculty are not themselves wholesome or unwholesome. They are ethically neutral supports and results within which wholesome and unwholesome mental activity may occur.

## Argument and Significance

This classification distinguishes the ethical status of a faculty from its functional importance.

```text
powerful function
    ≠
wholesome or unwholesome in itself
```

The eye, body, sexual faculties, and life faculty govern indispensable parts of embodied existence, yet they are ethically neutral. Ethical determination belongs principally to the mental and path systems operating through them.

The path faculties are different. Faith, energy, recollection, concentration, and wisdom are not neutral capacities waiting to be directed. As faculties, they are intrinsically constructive and therefore exclusively wholesome.

The three realization-faculties are likewise exclusively wholesome because they are constituted by uncontaminated path-operation.

Distress occupies an unusual middle position. Unlike the other feelings, it cannot be neutral. The Bhāṣya therefore treats it as an affective state that always carries ethical determination.

## Functional-Model Note

The same faculty-Idea can include events of different ethical kinds when its defining function is broader than ethical quality.

```text
manas as one faculty
    → wholesome mental events
    → unwholesome mental events
    → neutral mental events
```

Likewise, pleasure, pain, gladness, and neutral feeling remain the same functional faculty-types across different ethical modes.

By contrast, the five path faculties are defined by a function that is already normatively directed toward purification. Their functional identity and wholesome status coincide.

This gives two types of faculty-model:

```text
general functional faculty
    one Idea over ethically diverse instances

path-directed faculty
    one Idea whose function is intrinsically wholesome
```

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu**, presenting the standard Abhidharma classification without an explicit objection in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **kuśala** — wholesome; constructive with respect to favorable result and the path
  - **status:** central classification term
  - **function:** marks eight faculties as exclusively wholesome and other faculties as optionally wholesome

- **akuśala** — unwholesome
  - **status:** supplied by the Bhāṣya’s question and explanation
  - **function:** one possible mode of distress, mind, and four feelings

- **avyākṛta** — neutral or ethically indeterminate
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit
  - **function:** one possible mode of mind and four feelings; exclusive status of the seven material faculties and life

- **anyā vitti** — the other feelings
  - **status:** compressed Kārikā expression clarified by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** the four feeling-faculties other than distress

- **jīvitāṣṭama** — the group ending with life as the eighth
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only compact count
  - **function:** identifies the seven material faculties plus life as exclusively neutral

## Compact Determination

```text
8 exclusively wholesome:
    five path faculties + three realization faculties

1 twofold:
    distress = wholesome or unwholesome

5 threefold:
    mind + four other feelings
    = wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral

8 onefold:
    seven material faculties + life
    = neutral only
```
