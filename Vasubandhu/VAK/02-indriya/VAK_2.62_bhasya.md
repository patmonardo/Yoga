# VAK 2.62 — Bhāṣya: Samanantara, Ālambana, and Adhipati Conditions

## Kārikā

### Sanskrit — IAST

```text
cittacaittā acaramā utpannāḥ samanantaraḥ /
ālambanaṃ sarvadharmāḥ kāraṇākhyo 'dhipaḥ smṛtaḥ // VAkK_2.62 //
```

### Working Translation

> Arisen citta and caittas, except the final ones, are the immediately antecedent condition. All dharmas are object-condition. What is called the conditioning cause is remembered as the dominant condition.

The verse now defines three of the four pratyayas introduced at VAK 2.61. The first, hetu-pratyaya, was identified there with five of the six hetus, excluding kāraṇa-hetu. Here the remaining condition-types are specified.

---

## Bhāṣya Translation

### 1. Samanantara-pratyaya: ordered succession of citta and caittas

**Voice: Vasubandhu / exposition of the Abhidharma definition**

> Arisen citta and caittas, with the exception of the final ones, are the immediately antecedent condition.

That is, all arisen citta and caittas except the final citta and caittas of an Arhat function as samanantara-pratyaya.

The compound *samanantara-pratyaya* is explained as a condition that is both *sama* and *anantara*: equal/continuous and immediately successive.

This is why material form (*rūpa*) is not an immediately antecedent condition: its arising is irregular or non-uniform (*viṣamotpatteḥ*).

For example, immediately after material form belonging to the desire realm there may arise, on different occasions, material form belonging to the desire realm together with non-informative form belonging to the form realm; or desire-realm material form together with uncontaminated non-informative form. Thus the manifestation of material form is not governed by a single unconfused succession.

**Voice: Bhhadanta Vasumitra**

> But the immediately antecedent condition is not confused in this way, because within one uninterrupted continuum of accumulated material form a second moment arises while the first has not yet ceased.

The discussion then asks why future dharmas are not admitted as immediately antecedent conditions.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Vaibhāṣika problem-setting**

Because the future is disordered: it possesses no established relation of earlier and later.

An objection follows: if the future does not possess a fixed order, how can the Blessed One know that, immediately after a certain future event, another particular future event will occur?

One answer appeals to inference from past and present. The Buddha sees that from a karma of a certain type a maturation of a certain type has arisen in the past, or that one dharma has arisen from another; seeing a present karma of the same type, he knows that a corresponding future maturation or dharma will arise.

But this raises a difficulty: such knowledge would appear inferential rather than direct.

A further account proposes that within the continuum of beings there exists a special dissociated formation functioning as a sign of the future result. By observing that sign, the Buddha knows the future even without directly entering the relevant concentration or superknowledge.

**Voice: Objector / Vasubandhu's criticism**

If so, the Buddha would be merely an interpreter of signs rather than one who directly realizes the object.

**Voice: Sautrāntika position**

Therefore the Sautrāntikas say that the Buddha knows whatever he wishes to know merely by directing cognition toward it. As the Blessed One said: "The sphere of the Buddhas is inconceivable."

The discussion returns to causal order. If no fixed order is intrinsically established among future dharmas, why does the patience of dharma-knowledge concerning suffering arise immediately after the highest mundane dharmas, and why, in the later sequence, does knowledge of exhaustion arise immediately after the adamantine-like concentration rather than some other dharma?

The answer is genetic rather than merely positional:

> Whatever has its arising bound to a particular dharma arises immediately after that dharma.

The analogy is seed and sprout: a sprout follows from its seed even without a separate samanantara-pratyaya governing material succession.

The final citta and caittas of an Arhat do not function as immediately antecedent condition because no further citta is connected with them.

An objection asks whether the final citta can still count as *manas*, since a ceased citta becomes the mental faculty only in relation to a subsequent cognition.

The answer distinguishes two functions. Its status as support (*āśrayabhāva*) does not depend upon the same efficacy as samanantara-pratyaya. The final citta retains the character of mental support, but because other necessary conditions are lacking no subsequent consciousness arises.

The Bhāṣya then makes a decisive statement about causal efficacy:

> The immediately antecedent condition is defined by efficacy (*kāritra*). Once a dharma has taken its fruit through this condition, neither all dharmas together nor all living beings together can prevent that fruit from arising.

Thus samanantara is not mere chronological adjacency. It is a determinate causal ordering of the cognitive continuum.

The text next distinguishes "immediately after citta" from "without an intervening citta" by a fourfold analysis, using emergence from and entry into non-cognitive attainment as examples. A citta arising after a long interval of cessation can still be called immediately subsequent to the preceding citta because no other citta intervened.

This completes the explanation of samanantara-pratyaya.

---

### 2. Ālambana-pratyaya: the object-condition

**Voice: Vasubandhu / exposition**

> All dharmas are object-condition.

According to the appropriate consciousness:

- visible form is the object of eye-consciousness together with its associated mental factors;
- sound is the object of ear-consciousness;
- odor of nose-consciousness;
- taste of tongue-consciousness;
- the tangible of body-consciousness;
- all dharmas are objects of mental consciousness.

A dharma that is the object of a given dharma does not at one time cease to possess the character of being its possible object. Even when it is not actually being apprehended, it retains the relevant object-character.

The Bhāṣya compares this with fuel: wood and similar things are called fuel even when they are not presently burning, because they possess the defining character of fuel.

Citta and caittas are therefore determinate with respect to their objects according to the fixed structures of sense-field, individual entity, and defining characteristic (*āyatana-dravya-lakṣaṇa-niyama*).

The text asks whether they are likewise determinate with respect to their support (*āśraya*).

The answer given is yes: arisen cognitive events occur together with their support. Unarisen future events do not yet possess such an actual support relation, while past events are separated from their support.

**Voice: alternative teachers**

Others hold that past cognitive events too remain related to their support.

This completes the account of ālambana-pratyaya.

---

### 3. Adhipati-pratyaya: dominant condition

**Voice: Vasubandhu / exposition**

> What is called the conditioning cause (*kāraṇa-hetu*) is remembered as the dominant condition.

The same factor that was called kāraṇa-hetu in the six-cause analysis is here called adhipati-pratyaya.

Why "dominant" (*adhipati*)? Because this condition is especially extensive or pre-eminent.

An objection notes that ālambana-pratyaya also includes all dharmas. In what sense, then, is adhipati-pratyaya more extensive?

The answer is that simultaneously arisen dharmas can never serve as one another's object-condition in the relevant sense, whereas they can serve as dominant conditions for one another. This gives adhipati-pratyaya a wider causal scope.

Alternatively, it may be called the condition of what is dominant or powerful.

In general, every dharma can function as dominant condition for every conditioned dharma other than itself.

The Bhāṣya then considers whether there can be a dharma that is related to another through all four conditions or through none. The combinations depend upon whether the relation is self-to-self, other-to-other, conditioned-to-unconditioned, or unconditioned-to-unconditioned. The point is that pratyaya is relational and cannot be assigned merely by listing entities in isolation.

The text then asks at what temporal stage the conditions exercise their efficacy. This prepares VAK 2.63.

## Doctrinal Reconstruction

VAK 2.62 gives three distinct forms of conditioning that should not be collapsed into generic "causation."

### Samanantara-pratyaya

This is the condition of ordered cognitive succession.

```text
prior citta-caitta
    -> determinate succession
    -> subsequent citta-caitta
```

The key is not merely that one mental event occurs before another. The earlier event must stand in a lawful sequence such that the later event is genetically bound to arise after it. This is why the future, considered merely as an unordered domain, cannot itself function as samanantara-pratyaya.

### Ālambana-pratyaya

This is the condition of objective presentation.

```text
cognitive event
    -> requires determinate object-domain
    -> object retains object-capacity even when not presently apprehended
```

The distinction between being an object-condition and being presently cognized is important. Objecthood here is a structural capacity, not an occurrent psychological fact.

### Adhipati-pratyaya

This is the widest enabling or governing condition.

```text
conditioned arising
    -> depends on the absence of obstruction and/or positive enabling conditions
```

Adhipati-pratyaya therefore captures a relation broader than direct production. A dharma can condition another by governing the field within which its arising is possible.

## Logical Determination

The three pratyayas define three irreducible questions:

```text
Samanantara:
    What must precede this cognition in the cognitive series?

Ālambana:
    What can this cognition be of?

Adhipati:
    What must enable or at least not obstruct its arising?
```

This makes pratyaya a qualitative relational system. Hetu specifies productive ground; pratyaya specifies the determinate relational form under which arising becomes possible and intelligible.

The Bhāṣya's strongest genetic formulation is:

```text
yasya yat-pratibaddha utpādaḥ
sa tasyānantaram utpadyate
```

> Whatever has its arising bound to a particular dharma arises immediately after that dharma.

Thus succession is grounded in dependence, not dependence inferred merely from succession.

## Organon Interpretation

For the Organon, this passage strongly supports treating pratyaya as a form of rational subjectivity rather than as merely empirical circumstance.

### 1. Qualitative subjectivity

Samanantara determines whether the next cognitive event genuinely belongs to the same ordered cognitive process. Ālambana determines what counts as the presented object of that process. Adhipati determines the wider enabling field.

```text
Rational Subjectivity
    = ordered continuity
    + determinate object-reference
    + enabling relational field
```

This is close to the qualitative syllogistic function we have been identifying: cognition is not validated merely because a representation occurs. Its place in an ordered continuum, its object-domain, and its enabling conditions must be determinable.

### 2. Hetu–Pratyaya as a Samādhi dyad

The distinction can now be stated more exactly:

```text
Hetu
    productive determination of the object-side
    ground from which a result is generated

Pratyaya
    relational determination of the subject-side
    lawful conditions under which manifestation occurs

Samādhi
    comprehension of productive ground and conditioned manifestation
    as one determinate actuality
```

This is an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's explicit terminology. But it preserves the Kośa's own distinction between productive causal ground and the several relational modes of conditioning.

### 3. Rational–Empirical discrimination

The passage gives an especially useful model for the distinction between mere empirical sequence and rationally determined succession:

```text
Empirical:
    A occurred, then B occurred.

Rational:
    B's arising is bound to A under a determinate condition.
```

The second is the genetic judgment. It identifies the lawful form of manifestation rather than merely registering temporal order.

## New Technical Terms

- **samanantara-pratyaya** — immediately antecedent condition.
  - **status:** introduced in the pratyaya block; absent as a developed causal category in the preceding Kārikā analysis.
  - **function:** determines ordered succession within citta-caitta continua.

- **acarama** — non-final; excluding the last citta and caittas of an Arhat.
  - **status:** locally important qualification.
  - **function:** marks the limit at which cognitive succession terminates and therefore samanantara efficacy ceases.

- **viṣamotpattti / viṣamotpatteḥ** — irregular or non-uniform arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory terminology in this passage.
  - **function:** explains why material form is not samanantara-pratyaya.

- **kāritra** — efficacy, operative function.
  - **status:** recurring Bhāṣya causal term.
  - **function:** distinguishes a merely classificatory relation from an actually efficacious condition.

- **pratibaddha-utpāda** — arising bound or tied to another dharma.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only genetic formulation.
  - **function:** grounds determinate succession without requiring a pre-fixed substantial future order.

- **ālambana-pratyaya** — object-condition.
  - **status:** formally defined here.
  - **function:** determines the object-domain available to a cognitive event.

- **āyatana-dravya-lakṣaṇa-niyama** — determination by sense-field, individual entity, and defining characteristic.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical clarification.
  - **function:** explains the structured determination of cognitive object-domains.

- **adhipati-pratyaya** — dominant condition.
  - **status:** formally defined here.
  - **function:** recasts kāraṇa-hetu as the broad enabling/non-obstructing condition of conditioned arising.

---

## Translation Consequences

1. **samanantara** should not be weakened to simple temporal adjacency. "Immediately antecedent" is acceptable only if understood as ordered, efficacious succession.
2. **ālambana** is best translated as "object-condition" or "object-support," not merely "object," because its role is explicitly conditional.
3. **adhipati** is broader than direct efficient causation. "Dominant condition" preserves its enabling and non-obstructive scope.
4. **kāritra** is crucial throughout the passage: the system distinguishes what a dharma is from what causal work it is presently capable of performing.

---

## Summary

VAK 2.62 turns the pratyaya system into a theory of lawful manifestation.

A cognitive event requires:

```text
ordered succession      — samanantara
object determination    — ālambana
enabling causal field   — adhipati
```

The decisive principle is genetic: a later event follows an earlier one not because succession itself creates necessity, but because its arising is bound to that prior condition. The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes empirical sequence from rationally determined continuity with unusual clarity.

For the Organon, this is the strongest basis yet for construing pratyaya as rational-empirical qualitative subjectivity: it determines the form under which a cognition can arise, possess an object, and belong to an ordered continuum.
