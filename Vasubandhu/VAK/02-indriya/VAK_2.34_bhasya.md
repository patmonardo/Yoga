# VAK 2.34 Bhāṣya — Citta, Manas, Vijñāna, and Fivefold Association

## Kārikā

```text
cittaṃ mano 'tha vijñānam ekārthaṃ cittacaitasāḥ /
sāśrayālambanākārāḥ saṃprayuktāś ca pañcadhā // VAK 2.34 //
```

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

The caittas that occur together with citta have now been explained according to their different kinds. The following designations applied to them are now defined, because these expressions are conventionally employed in the teaching:

> **Citta, manas, and vijñāna have one meaning.**

It is called *citta* because it accumulates or gathers (*cinoti*). It is called *manas* because it thinks or considers (*manute*). It is called *vijñāna* because it discriminatively knows (*vijānāti*).

Alternatively, it is called *citta* because it is variegated by wholesome and unwholesome elements. That same occurrence, insofar as it serves as a support, is called *manas*; insofar as it is supported, it is called *vijñāna*.

Just as *citta*, *manas*, and *vijñāna* have one meaning, so too the expressions applied to

> **citta and the caittas—possessing a support, possessing an object, possessing an aspect, and being associated—**

have one meaning.

Those very citta and caittas are said to possess a support because they depend upon a faculty. They are said to possess an object because they apprehend an object. They are said to possess an aspect because they take that same object in a particular manner. They are called associated because they are conjoined together.

In what manner are they conjoined together? The Kārikā answers:

> **Fivefold.**

They are associated through five forms of equality: equality of support, object, aspect, time, and substance or numerical instance.

What is meant by the last equality? Just as there is one citta in the event, so too there is one instance of each associated caitta.

Citta and the caittas, together with their detailed divisions, have now been explained.

## Reconstruction of the Argument

The passage first establishes that three major words do not name three separate entities here:

```text
citta
manas
vijñāna
    = one mental occurrence described under different functions
```

The Bhāṣya offers two explanations.

### Etymological explanation

```text
citta
    gathers or accumulates

manas
    thinks or considers

vijñāna
    discriminatively knows
```

### Relational explanation

```text
citta
    the occurrence variegated by wholesome and unwholesome factors

manas
    that same occurrence functioning as support

vijñāna
    that same occurrence functioning as supported cognition
```

The second explanation is important because identity of referent does not erase functional distinction. One occurrence can be denominated differently according to the relation under consideration.

The passage then defines the technical unity of citta and caittas. They are not associated merely because they happen to be nearby or simultaneous. Association requires five equalities:

```text
1. same support
2. same object
3. same aspect
4. same time
5. corresponding numerical instance
```

The fifth equality means that one citta is accompanied by one instance of each caitta belonging to that event. It prevents the association relation from being treated as an indefinite heap.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces and explains the conventional equivalence of *citta*, *manas*, and *vijñāna*.

**Voice: alternative teachers** — explain the three terms as the same occurrence viewed as variegated citta, supporting manas, and supported vijñāna.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — defines the fivefold association of citta and caittas.

## New Technical Terms

- **ekārtha** — having one meaning or one referent in this context.
  - **status:** important explicit formulation in the Bhāṣya and Kārikā.
  - **function:** establishes contextual equivalence without requiring that every use of the three words be interchangeable.

- **sāśraya** — possessing a support; dependent upon a faculty.
  - **status:** newly foregrounded association term.
  - **function:** identifies the common basis of citta and caittas.

- **sālambana** — possessing an object.
  - **status:** newly foregrounded association term.
  - **function:** identifies their common object-directedness.

- **sākāra** — possessing an aspect or mode.
  - **status:** newly foregrounded association term.
  - **function:** indicates the particular manner in which the object is taken.

- **saṃprayukta** — associated or conjoined.
  - **status:** central technical term for the citta-caitta relation.
  - **function:** states event-unity through five equalities.

- **āśraya-samatā** — equality of support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification.
  - **function:** first condition of association.

- **ālambana-samatā** — equality of object.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification.
  - **function:** second condition of association.

- **ākāra-samatā** — equality of aspect.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification.
  - **function:** third condition of association.

- **kāla-samatā** — equality of time.
  - **status:** supplied explicitly by the Bhāṣya beyond the compressed verse.
  - **function:** ensures simultaneity.

- **dravya-samatā** — equality of substance or numerical instance.
  - **status:** supplied explicitly by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** establishes one corresponding instance of each associated factor in the event.

## Translation Consequences

### Citta, manas, and vijñāna

“Synonyms” is usable but slightly too loose. The text says *ekārtha*: they have one meaning or referent here. The Bhāṣya still permits different functional explanations for the three names.

### Ākāra

“Aspect” is preferable to “form” in this passage. It means the manner in which the shared object is cognitively taken.

### Dravya-samatā

“Equality of substance” can mislead if heard as material substance. Here it is best understood as equality of numerical instance:

```text
one citta
one instance of each associated caitta
```

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The central determination is **organic event-unity through structured equality**.

```text
Citta-Caitta Association
    is not aggregation
    is not mere simultaneity
    is fivefold co-determination
```

The factors belong to one event because they have the same support, object, aspect, and time, while remaining numerically distinct factors.

This gives a precise answer to a problem raised throughout the preceding caitta catalog: how can many distinct factors constitute one cognition?

```text
unity
    does not erase distinct factors

distinction
    does not break event-unity
```

The five equalities mediate the two.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This passage supplies a particularly strong model of Organic Unity.

The citta is not an underlying container into which independent features are inserted. Nor are the caittas detachable predicates added from outside. Each factor belongs to the event only through the same determinate relational structure.

```text
one cognitive event
    one support
    one object
    one aspect
    one time
    one coordinated instance of each factor
```

The multiplicity is therefore internally organized. It is a many whose members are possible only within one event, while the event exists only through its differentiated members.

That is a much stricter unity than the raw claim that mental factors “occur together.”
