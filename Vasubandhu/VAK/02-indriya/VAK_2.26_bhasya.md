# VAK 2.26 Bhāṣya — The Afflicted and Unwholesome Great-Ground Factors

## Kārikā

```text
mohaḥ pramādaḥ kauśīdyam āśraddhyaṃ styānam auddhatyam /
kliṣṭe sadaiva akuśale tv āhrīkyam anapatrapā // VAK 2.26 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

The wholesome great-ground factors have been explained.

A “great ground” is an extensive sphere. The afflicted factors whose ground is extensive are called the afflicted great-ground factors: they are the dharmas that are always present in an afflicted citta.

Which factors are always present in an afflicted citta?

Delusion, negligence, laziness, lack of faith, sluggishness, and agitation are always present in the afflicted.

Here delusion means ignorance: not knowing and not clearly comprehending.

Negligence is the non-cultivation of wholesome dharmas; it is the factor opposed to heedfulness.

Laziness is the absence of energetic exertion in citta; it is opposed to energy.

Lack of faith is the absence of clarity or confidence in citta; it is opposed to faith.

What is sluggishness? It is heaviness of body and heaviness of citta, unworkability of body and unworkability of citta. The Abhidharma speaks of bodily sluggishness and mental sluggishness.

How can a mental factor be called bodily? In the same way that one speaks of bodily feeling.

Agitation is the non-pacification of citta.

These, then, are the six afflicted great-ground factors.

**Objector:** But the Abhidharma lists ten afflicted great-ground factors: lack of faith, laziness, lost mindfulness, distraction of citta, ignorance, lack of clear comprehension, improper attention, false resolution, agitation, and negligence.

**Vasubandhu:** You know the recitation, dear to the gods, but not its intention.

What, then, is its intention?

Lost mindfulness, distraction, lack of clear comprehension, improper attention, and false resolution are not restricted exclusively to the afflicted great-ground factors, because they are already universal great-ground factors in afflicted form. This resembles the case of non-delusion: it is not separately fixed as a wholesome great-ground factor because its nature is prajñā.

Lost mindfulness is simply mindfulness when afflicted. Distraction is concentration when afflicted, and so on.

For this reason it is said: some factors that are universal great-ground factors are also afflicted great-ground factors.

This produces a fourfold analysis:

1. Universal great-ground factors but not afflicted great-ground factors: feeling, volition, recognition, desire-to-act, and contact.
2. Afflicted great-ground factors but not universal great-ground factors: lack of faith, laziness, ignorance, agitation, and negligence.
3. Both: the five beginning with lost mindfulness, when afflicted.
4. Neither: the remaining factors.

Some, however, regard distraction of citta as distinct from wrong concentration. For them the fourfold analysis must be arranged differently.

Sluggishness, furthermore, is held to be associated with every affliction. Why, then, is it not listed among the afflicted great-ground factors?

They answer that it ought to be listed, but that it is omitted because it is favorable to concentration. A person dominated by sluggishness is said to produce concentration more quickly than one dominated by agitation.

But who is dominated by sluggishness without being dominated by agitation, and who is dominated by agitation without sluggishness? These two never abandon their coexistence.

Nevertheless, a person is designated according to whichever of the two is stronger.

Thus six afflicted great-ground factors are established. These are always found only in afflicted citta and nowhere else.

But in an unwholesome citta, shamelessness and lack of moral concern are also always present. These two dharmas are called the unwholesome great-ground factors. Their characteristics will be explained later.

## Reconstruction of the Argument

The Bhāṣya distinguishes three different claims that the compressed list can otherwise blur:

```text
present in every citta
present in every afflicted citta
present in every unwholesome citta
```

The six afflicted great-ground factors are:

```text
moha          delusion / ignorance
pramāda       negligence
kauśīdya      laziness
āśraddhya     lack of faith
styāna        sluggishness
uddhatya      agitation
```

The two specifically unwholesome great-ground factors are:

```text
āhrīkya       shamelessness
anapatrāpya   lack of moral concern
```

An afflicted citta is therefore not automatically being classified as unwholesome in precisely the same sense. The second pair marks the specifically ethical deterioration of an unwholesome citta.

## The Apparent List of Ten

The most important issue in this unit is the conflict between two enumerations.

The Abhidharma recitation gives ten afflicted great-ground factors, whereas the Kārikā gives six. Vasubandhu does not reject the recitation as false. He interprets its intention.

Five members of the longer list are afflicted forms of factors already counted among the universal great-ground factors:

```text
muṣitasmṛtitā          afflicted mindfulness
vikṣepa                 afflicted concentration
asaṃprajanya            afflicted discernment
ayoniśomanaskāra        afflicted attention
mithyādhimokṣa          afflicted resolution
```

Thus the longer enumeration concerns factors occurring in afflicted citta, while the list of six concerns factors whose classificatory identity is specifically that of afflicted great-ground factors.

This is a distinction between:

```text
occurring as afflicted
and
being classified essentially in the afflicted class
```

## Fourfold Analysis

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The four alternatives are used to prevent the two taxonomies from collapsing into one another:

| Class | Members |
|---|---|
| Universal, not specifically afflicted | feeling, volition, recognition, desire-to-act, contact |
| Specifically afflicted, not universal | lack of faith, laziness, ignorance, agitation, negligence |
| Both universal and afflicted in form | lost mindfulness and the other four corrupted universal factors |
| Neither | all remaining factors |

The argument is therefore classificatory but not merely numerical. A factor may belong to one class by its general range and to another by its present qualitative condition.

## Sluggishness and Agitation

The Bhāṣya preserves a striking tension:

```text
sluggishness and agitation always coexist
but a person is named by whichever predominates
```

This means that the labels “sluggish type” and “agitated type” do not indicate mutually exclusive mental constitutions. They indicate dominance within a co-present pair.

The claim that sluggishness can be favorable to concentration is not praise of sluggishness. It explains why one tradition hesitated to count it among factors defined exclusively by affliction: its functional relation to concentration is not entirely straightforward.

## Translation Consequences

- `uddhatya` should be translated as **agitation** or **excitation**, not by the source-file form “uddhava.”
- `āśraddhya` is **lack of faith**, **non-confidence**, or **absence of clarity**, as the explicit opposite of `śraddhā`.
- `styāna` is broader than ordinary sleepiness. It includes heaviness and unworkability of body and citta.
- `anapatrāpya` is not simple embarrassment. It is absence of moral concern regarding others or the consequences of misconduct.
- `kliṣṭa` and `akuśala` should not be treated as exact synonyms in this passage.

## New Technical Terms

- **asaṃprajanya** — lack of clear comprehension
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya’s longer Abhidharma list
  - **function:** afflicted form of discernment

- **ayoniśomanaskāra** — improper or non-principled attention
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** afflicted form of attention

- **mithyādhimokṣa** — false resolution
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** afflicted form of resolution

- **muṣitasmṛtitā** — lost or corrupted mindfulness
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** afflicted form of mindfulness

- **vikṣepa** — distraction
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** afflicted form of concentration; disputed by some as distinct from wrong concentration

- **catuṣkoṭika** — fourfold alternatives
  - **status:** Bhāṣya analytical term
  - **function:** classification by four possible relations between two predicates

## Voice Map

- **Vasubandhu:** defines the six afflicted great-ground factors and the two unwholesome factors.
- **Objector:** cites the Abhidharma list of ten.
- **Vasubandhu:** distinguishes the recited list from its intended classification.
- **Alternative opinion:** distraction is distinct from wrong concentration.
- **Traditional explanation:** sluggishness is omitted because of its compatibility with concentration.
- **Vasubandhu / critical question:** challenges any simple separation of sluggish and agitated personality types.

## Compact Determination

```text
Every afflicted citta contains six specifically afflicted factors.
Every unwholesome citta additionally contains shamelessness and lack of moral concern.
Universal factors can themselves occur in corrupted form without thereby becoming exclusively afflicted factors.
```
