# VAK 2.33 Bhāṣya — Vitarka, Vicāra, Māna, and Mada

## Kārikā

```text
vitarkacārāv audāryasūkṣmate māna unnatiḥ /
madaḥ svadharme raktasya paryādānaṃ tu cetasaḥ // VAK 2.33 //
```

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

What is the distinction between *vitarka* and *vicāra*?

> Vitarka and vicāra are respectively the coarseness and subtlety [of citta].

Of what are they the coarseness and subtlety? The word *cetas* will be stated later in the verse. Vitarka is the coarseness of citta; vicāra is the subtlety of citta.

But how can these two be associated with one and the same citta?

Some say: clarified butter that has not been fully solidified, when touched from above by the rays of the sun, becomes neither excessively solid nor excessively melted. In the same way, through association with both vitarka and vicāra, citta becomes neither excessively subtle nor excessively coarse. Thus both perform an operation there.

But on this account vitarka and vicāra would be causes of the coarseness and subtlety rather than being those very states—just as cold and heat are causes of the solidification and melting of clarified butter, but are not themselves solidification and melting.

Moreover, coarseness and subtlety are relative, varying according to differences of level and type. On this interpretation, vitarka and vicāra would occur even as far as the summit of existence. Nor is it reasonable to establish a difference of kind merely from coarseness and subtlety.

Others say: in the sūtra, vitarka and vicāra are called formations of speech, for one speaks only after having applied and examined thought. Of these, the coarse formations are vitarka and the subtle formations are vicāra.

What contradiction would there be if, in one citta, one dharma were coarse and another subtle?

There would be no contradiction if they differed in kind, as feeling and recognition do. But lesser and greater degrees of one and the same kind cannot occur simultaneously.

There is nevertheless a difference of kind.

Then that difference should be stated. Yet it is difficult to express, because it appears only as lesser and greater intensity.

But it does not appear in that way, since each distinct kind can itself possess lesser and greater degrees.

Still others say that vitarka and vicāra do not occur together in one citta at all.

How, then, is the first dhyāna said to possess five factors?

It possesses five factors with reference to the level as a whole, not with reference to one single moment.

What is the distinction between *māna* and *mada*?

> Māna is elevation. Mada is the complete taking-over of the citta of one attached to one’s own qualities.

Māna is the elevation of citta produced by constructing some superiority over others.

Mada is the complete occupation of the citta of one attached precisely to one’s own qualities. Just as there is intoxication produced by liquor, so there is intoxication produced by attachment.

Others define mada as a particular kind of exhilaration.

The caittas have now been explained together with citta according to their several classes. The technical designations applied to them in the teaching will next be defined.

## Reconstruction of the Argument

The passage contains two distinct inquiries.

### 1. Vitarka and vicāra

The Kārikā gives a compressed definition:

```text
vitarka = coarseness of citta
vicāra  = subtlety of citta
```

The Bhāṣya immediately tests whether this definition can sustain simultaneous association.

The debate develops through four positions:

1. **Joint modulation:** vitarka and vicāra jointly prevent citta from becoming too coarse or too subtle.
2. **Causal objection:** this would make them causes of coarseness and subtlety, not those states themselves.
3. **Distinct speech-formations:** both are formations of speech, coarse and subtle respectively.
4. **Non-simultaneity:** they do not occur in one moment; the first dhyāna has both only at the level of the whole stage.

The passage does not force a final harmonization. It records the difficulty of explaining how two factors can be distinct in kind while appearing primarily as stronger and weaker modes.

### 2. Māna and mada

The second distinction is cleaner:

```text
māna
    = elevation through comparison with others

mada
    = saturation by attachment to one’s own qualities
```

Māna is comparative and relational. Mada is absorptive and self-enclosed.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames the questions, raises objections, and clarifies the definitions.

**Voice: uncertain doctrinal position** — the clarified-butter analogy explaining the coexistence of vitarka and vicāra.

**Voice: other teachers** — identify vitarka and vicāra as coarse and subtle formations of speech.

**Voice: other teachers** — deny their coexistence in a single citta-moment and interpret the five factors of first dhyāna level-wise rather than moment-wise.

**Voice: other teachers** — define mada as a special exhilaration.

## New Technical Terms

- **audārikatā** — coarseness or grossness of citta.
  - **status:** introduced here as the defining character of vitarka.
  - **function:** differentiates a mode of mental operation.

- **sūkṣmatā** — subtlety or fineness of citta.
  - **status:** introduced here as the defining character of vicāra.
  - **function:** differentiates a finer mode of mental operation.

- **vāksaṃskāra** — formation or preparation of speech.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this unit.
  - **function:** gives an alternative doctrinal account of vitarka and vicāra.

- **jātibheda** — difference of kind.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only logical vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** tests whether vitarka and vicāra are genuinely distinct dharmas rather than degrees of one factor.

- **mṛdvadhimātratā** — lesser and greater intensity.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only analytical vocabulary.
  - **function:** distinguishes degree-difference from kind-difference.

- **bhūmitaḥ** — with reference to the level as a whole.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya qualification.
  - **function:** permits a dhyāna to possess several factors without requiring all of them in one instant.

- **kṣaṇataḥ** — with reference to a single moment.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only contrast here.
  - **function:** marks moment-level analysis.

- **unnati** — elevation or raising of citta.
  - **status:** defining term for māna.
  - **function:** identifies comparative self-inflation.

- **paryādāna** — complete occupation, exhaustion, or taking-over.
  - **status:** defining term for mada.
  - **function:** describes attachment saturating the citta.

- **saṃpraharṣaṇa** — exhilaration or heightened elation.
  - **status:** alternative definition supplied by other teachers.
  - **function:** specifies mada affectively.

## Translation Consequences

Vitarka and vicāra should not be flattened into ordinary “thinking” and “reflection.” The Bhāṣya treats them as contested structural modes of citta whose distinction may concern:

- coarseness and subtlety,
- stages in the formation of speech,
- difference of kind,
- difference of intensity,
- or distribution across a meditative level rather than coexistence in one instant.

Likewise, māna and mada should not both be translated simply as “pride.”

```text
māna
    comparative elevation

mada
    intoxicated absorption in one’s own condition
```

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

This passage introduces an important distinction between two scales of analysis:

```text
bhūmi-level attribution
    what belongs to a stage as an organized whole

kṣaṇa-level attribution
    what is present in one citta-moment
```

The same dhyāna may therefore possess a structured profile whose factors need not all be simultaneously instantiated.

The vitarka-vicāra controversy also reveals a strict demand of Abhidharma classification:

```text
difference of degree
    does not by itself establish
    difference of dharma-kind
```

A genuine classification requires a determinate functional difference, not merely a stronger and weaker appearance.

## Limited Organon Comment

The most useful result for the present project is methodological rather than speculative.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
system-level possession
from
moment-level co-presence
```

This is a powerful rule for the later Dataset. A feature may belong to an organized state without being instantiated in every event that constitutes that state.

The passage also shows why the Kośa cannot remain a mere list of caittas. Each factor must have:

```text
its own function
its own scale of attribution
its own conditions of coexistence
its own difference from neighboring factors
```

That is precisely what turns the technical catalog into a systematic science of citta.
