# VAK_2.27 — The Ground of Minor Afflictions

## Kārikā

```text
krodhopanāhaśāṭhyerṣyāpradāsamrakṣamatsarāḥ /
māyāmadavihiṃsāś ca parīttakleśabhūmikāḥ // VAkK_2.27 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

> Anger, resentment, deceitfulness, envy, spite, concealment, stinginess, deception, intoxication, and harmfulness belong to the ground of minor afflictions.
>
> Their ground is the minor afflictions; therefore they are called factors belonging to the ground of minor afflictions.
>
> This is because they are associated only with ignorance, only with what is abandoned through cultivation, and only with the mental sphere.
>
> Their detailed explanation will be given later among the secondary afflictions.
>
> These five classes of mental factors have now been stated.
>
> There are also other indeterminate factors, such as initial thought, sustained thought, regret, and torpor.
>
> It must therefore now be explained how many mental factors necessarily occur in each kind of citta.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The prose explains the class-name, states its three restrictions, postpones detailed definitions, closes the fivefold taxonomy, and announces the next inquiry.

## The Ten Minor-Affliction Factors

```text
krodha       anger
upanāha      resentment / grudge
śāṭhya       deceitfulness / crookedness
īrṣyā        envy
pradāsa      spite / hostile obstinacy
mrakṣa       concealment of faults
matsara      stinginess
māyā         deception
mada         intoxication / prideful elation
vihiṃsā      harmfulness
```

The source passage itself does not yet define these individually. Vasubandhu explicitly postpones that work until the discussion of the secondary afflictions.

## Why Their Bhūmi Is “Minor”

The Bhāṣya gives three restrictions:

```text
1. avidyāmātreṇa saṃprayoga
   associated only with ignorance

2. bhāvanāheya
   abandoned only through cultivation

3. manobhūmika
   belonging only to the mental sphere
```

Their field is therefore narrower than that of the universal, wholesome-great-ground, or general afflicted-great-ground factors.

`parītta` does not mean morally insignificant. It marks restricted range.

## Structural Determination

These factors are not said to occur together in every afflicted citta.

The class is defined by common restrictions, not by universal co-presence:

```text
minor-affliction factor
    = associated only with ignorance
    + cultivation-abandonable
    + mental-sphere only
```

This distinguishes a **class-ground** from a **necessary bundle**.

## Closure of the Fivefold Caitta Taxonomy

With this verse, Vasubandhu closes the five classes introduced at VAK 2.23:

```text
1. mahābhūmika
2. kuśala-mahābhūmika
3. kleśa-mahābhūmika
4. akuśala-mahābhūmika
5. parīttakleśa-bhūmika
```

He then notes that further factors remain outside these fixed classes:

```text
vitarka     initial thought
vicāra      sustained thought
kaukṛtya    regret
middha      torpor
```

These are called **aniyata**, indeterminate or not fixed to one of the five universal ranges.

## New Technical Terms

- **parīttakleśabhūmika** — belonging to the restricted ground of minor afflictions
  - **status:** introduced as the fifth caitta class
  - **function:** classifies afflictions by restricted range of association

- **avidyāmātreṇa saṃprayoga** — association only with ignorance
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification in this unit
  - **function:** limits the class by its accompanying root-affliction

- **bhāvanāheya** — abandonable through cultivation
  - **status:** previously encountered as an abandonment category
  - **function:** gives the path-stage at which these factors are removed

- **manobhūmika** — belonging to the mental sphere
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification here
  - **function:** excludes sensory-consciousness fields

- **aniyata** — indeterminate / not fixed
  - **status:** introduced at the transition beyond the fivefold taxonomy
  - **function:** names factors not universally assigned to one of the five grounds

## Compact Determination

```text
VAK 2.27 completes the fivefold taxonomy of caittas.

The minor-affliction factors form a restricted class:
they are mental-only, cultivation-abandonable,
and associated only with ignorance.

The next problem is no longer which classes exist,
but how many factors actually accompany each kind of citta.
```
