# VAK 2.49 Bhāṣya — The Six Causes and the General Conditioning Cause

## Kārikā

```text
kāraṇaṃ sahabhūś caiva sabhāgaḥ saṃprayuktakaḥ /
sarvatrago vipākākhyaḥ ṣaḍvidho hetur iṣyate // VAkK_2.49 //
```

> Cause-as-condition, co-arisen cause, homogeneous cause, associated cause, pervasive cause, and the cause called maturation: cause is held to be sixfold.

## Continuous Translation

It was said above: “Birth produces what is to be produced, but not without causes and conditions.” What, then, are these causes, and what are the conditions?

There are six causes: the conditioning cause, the co-arisen cause, the homogeneous cause, the associated cause, the pervasive cause, and the maturation cause.

Among these, the conditioning cause is expressed as follows:

> Apart from itself, all [dharmas] are conditioning causes.

For a conditioned dharma, all dharmas other than its own nature are conditioning causes, because they remain in a state of non-obstruction with respect to its arising.

Objection: Yet the defilements that would have arisen in one who did not know do not arise in one who knows. Thus knowledge obstructs their arising, just as sunlight obstructs the visibility of the stars. How, then, can all dharmas other than the dharma’s own nature be conditioning causes of a conditioned dharma?

Reply: The statement must be understood as referring to non-obstruction with respect to a dharma that is actually arising.

It is reasonable that something capable of obstructing an arising should be called a cause through its non-obstruction. For example, villagers may say, with reference to a governor who does not oppress them, “Through our lord we are happy.”

But how can something entirely incapable of obstruction possess the status of cause? Nirvāṇa, for example, and dharmas incapable of arising are unable to obstruct the arising of anything. Nor can hell-beings and the like obstruct the arising of the aggregates of a being in the immaterial realm. Even if such things did not exist, they would still be equally incapable of producing an obstruction.

Reply: People may speak in the same way even with reference to a governor who has no power to oppress them. The same example therefore applies. The definition is given only in a general sense.

A principal conditioning cause, however, is also capable of production—for example, the eye and visible form with respect to visual consciousness, food with respect to the body, and seeds with respect to sprouts and similar results.

Someone may object: If all dharmas are causes merely through being non-obstructive, why do all things not arise simultaneously? And why, in the case of killing, do all who are non-obstructive not share the deed as the killer does?

This is no objection. All dharmas are asserted to be causes through non-obstruction, not through productive agency.

Others maintain that every conditioning cause has some efficacy with respect to every arising. Even nirvāṇa, they say, has efficacy with respect to visual consciousness. How so? By taking nirvāṇa as its object, a wholesome or unwholesome mental consciousness arises; from that, in due sequence, visual consciousness later arises. Thus, because nirvāṇa too becomes a condition through a succession of causes, it possesses causal efficacy. The same principle should be understood in other cases. This indicates the direction of the doctrine.

The conditioning cause has now been explained.

## Argumentative Reconstruction

### 1. The sixfold causal field

VAK 2.49 does not offer one undifferentiated notion of cause. It opens a differentiated causal system:

```text
kāraṇa-hetu        general conditioning / non-obstruction
sahabhū-hetu       mutual co-arising
sabhāga-hetu       homogeneous continuity
saṃprayukta-hetu   associated mental co-operation
sarvatraga-hetu    pervasive afflicted causality
vipāka-hetu        karmic maturation
```

The verse therefore begins a rational analysis of conditioned arising. “Cause” is not a single relation repeated everywhere; distinct forms of dependence must be distinguished.

### 2. Kāraṇa-hetu has a broad and a principal sense

The Bhāṣya gives the conditioning cause two levels.

In its broad sense:

```text
for a conditioned dharma x,
every dharma other than x itself
is a conditioning cause of x
insofar as it does not obstruct x’s arising
```

In its principal sense, the conditioning cause is positively productive:

```text
eye + visible form → visual consciousness
food → bodily support
seed → sprout
```

Thus the category includes both a universal background of non-obstruction and determinate productive causes.

### 3. Non-obstruction is not productive agency

The objection about simultaneous arising forces a crucial distinction:

```text
non-obstructive causality ≠ direct production
```

All other dharmas may be called conditioning causes in the broad sense, but this does not mean that each actively produces every result. The Bhāṣya refuses to confuse permissive conditions with productive grounds.

This distinction also answers the moral objection concerning killing. Mere failure to prevent an action does not make every non-obstructing being its agent.

### 4. The debate over universal efficacy

A more expansive interpretation claims that every conditioning cause has some mediated efficacy in every arising. Nirvāṇa itself may indirectly condition visual consciousness through a causal sequence beginning with mental consciousness that takes nirvāṇa as its object.

This is presented as the view of “others,” not as the uncontested conclusion of Vasubandhu.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position — the formal definition of kāraṇa-hetu through non-obstruction.

**Voice:** Objector — challenges concerning knowledge as obstruction, powerless non-obstructors, simultaneous arising, and shared responsibility.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / Bhāṣya narrator — distinguishes non-obstruction from production and presents the principal causal examples.

**Voice:** uncertain / another school position — universal mediated efficacy of every conditioning cause.

## Translation Consequences

### Hetu

Here **hetu** should not be reduced to “cause” in the loose modern sense. It names an intelligible mode of grounding or conditioning. The sixfold division shows that causal explanation requires qualitative distinctions.

### Kāraṇa

**Kāraṇa-hetu** is broader than an efficient cause. Its defining feature is first stated negatively: non-obstruction. Only its principal instances are directly productive.

A useful working translation is therefore **conditioning cause**, while retaining “general cause” as an alternative in doctrinal summaries.

### Avighna-bhāva

**Avighna-bhāva** means the state of not obstructing. The concept is relational and event-indexed: something is non-obstructive with respect to a dharma that is arising.

## New Technical Terms

- **kāraṇa-hetu** — conditioning cause; the broad causal category defined by non-obstruction, with principal instances also exercising productive efficacy
  - **status:** introduced explicitly here
  - **function:** first member of the six-cause system

- **avighna-bhāva** — state of non-obstruction
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory term in this passage
  - **function:** defines the broad scope of kāraṇa-hetu

- **vighna** — obstruction
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argument term
  - **function:** distinguishes what blocks arising from what permits it

- **utpādana-sāmarthya** — capacity for production
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this unit
  - **function:** distinguishes principal productive conditioning causes from merely non-obstructive causes

- **kāraṇa-paraṃparā** — succession or series of causes
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only causal expression here
  - **function:** explains mediated causal efficacy

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The sixfold hetu system is a theory of **Rational Nature** because it reconstructs the intelligible forms by which empirical events arise. Yet it remains entirely within conditioned reality.

The twofold function is:

```text
empirical event:
    this consciousness, body, sprout, action, or result appears

rational determination:
    the event is comprehended through its specific causal form
```

The rational does not create a second world behind appearance. It illuminates the causal structure immanent in appearance.

For the present verse, the first rational form is especially broad:

```text
field of non-obstruction
    + principal productive causes
    + ordered causal succession
    = kāraṇa-hetu
```

This is why Rational Nature can function “dually” within relative reality. The same conditioned event exists:

1. as empirically present;
2. as rationally intelligible through its causal genesis.

The difference is epistemic and structural, not a division into two unrelated substances.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

VAK 2.49 belongs naturally to the Organon’s analysis of Ground and Relative Necessity.

Each causal type expresses a different form of the proposition:

```text
Given this determinate causal relation,
this result can or must arise accordingly.
```

Kāraṇa-hetu begins with the most extensive field: all that does not obstruct the event. But the Bhāṣya immediately differentiates this permissive field from the principal causes that positively produce the event.

That gives a valuable Organon distinction:

```text
condition of possibility
    ≠
productive ground
```

The illumination—**prakāśa**—of substance is therefore not a consuming Absolute Substance. It is the disclosure of how a determinate event stands within a structured causal whole.

The Kośa’s own form must remain primary: it is classifying causes within conditioned dharma theory. The Organon reading comes afterward and recognizes this as a highly refined analysis of rational conditioning operating within empirical reality.
