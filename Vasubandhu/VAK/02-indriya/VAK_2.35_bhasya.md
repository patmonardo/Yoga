# VAK 2.35 Bhāṣya — Dissociated Formations and the Opening Definition of Prāpti

## Kārikā

```text
viprayuktās tu saṃskārāḥ prāptyaprāptī sabhāgatā /
āsaṃjñikaṃ samāpattī jīvitaṃ lakṣaṇāni ca // VAK 2.35 //
```

The formations dissociated from citta are acquisition and non-acquisition, class-commonality, the non-ideational state, the two attainments, life, and the marks.

The prose immediately continues the list with the groups of names and related verbal formations, then begins the analysis with acquisition.

## Continuous Translation of the Bhāṣya

The citta and caittas, together with their detailed divisions, have now been explained.

The formations dissociated from citta are acquisition and non-acquisition, class-commonality, the non-ideational state, the two attainments, life, the conditioned marks, the groups of names, and the other verbal formations.

These formations are called “dissociated from citta” because they are not associated with citta and are not material by nature.

Among these, first:

**Acquisition is obtaining and possession.**

Acquisition is of two kinds. It is the obtaining of what was not previously obtained or of what had been relinquished, and it is possession of what has already been obtained.

From the reverse of this, non-acquisition is established.

Of what, then, do acquisition and non-acquisition occur?

They occur with respect to dharmas belonging to one’s own continuum.

They do not occur with respect to dharmas belonging to another continuum, for no one possesses what belongs to another. Nor do they occur with respect to what does not belong to any continuum, for no one possesses what is classified as non-sentient.

This is the rule with respect to conditioned dharmas.

## Restrained Reconstruction

VAK 2.35 begins a new category after the citta-caitta analysis.

```text
material dharmas
    are rūpa by nature

citta and caittas
    are mutually associated mental occurrences

citta-viprayukta-saṃskāras
    are conditioned structures
    that are neither material
    nor associated mental factors
```

The category exists because the system requires real conditioned relations that cannot be reduced either to matter or to a moment of cognition.

The Bhāṣya begins with *prāpti* because it is the basic relation through which a dharma counts as belonging to a living continuum.

```text
prāpti
    = newly obtaining a dharma
      or continuing to possess it

aprāpti
    = the corresponding absence of that relation
```

The distinction between acquisition and possession is important:

```text
lābha
    obtaining what was absent or lost

samanvāgama
    continuing possession of what has been obtained
```

Thus *prāpti* is not merely a momentary event. It covers both transition into possession and the maintained relation of possession.

## Scope Rule

The Bhāṣya imposes a strict continuum condition:

```text
prāpti / aprāpti apply only to dharmas
belonging to one’s own continuum
```

They do not apply:

```text
to another being’s continuum
or
to non-sentient entities outside any continuum
```

This makes *prāpti* a formally indexed relation:

```text
continuum S
    possesses dharma D

only when D belongs to S
```

The relation is therefore neither universal ownership nor metaphysical inclusion in general. It is a conditioned belonging-relation internal to a sentient series.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Introduces the class of citta-dissociated formations and defines why they are called dissociated.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

Treats acquisition and non-acquisition as real conditioned dharmas distinct from both matter and mental association.

**Voice: Objector**

The question “Of what do acquisition and non-acquisition occur?” opens the scope problem, though the sustained objection to the substantial reality of acquisition develops in the following passage.

## New Technical Terms

- **citta-viprayukta-saṃskāra** — conditioned formation dissociated from citta
  - **status:** introduced as a new major class
  - **function:** accounts for structural, relational, and continuity conditions not reducible to rūpa or associated mental factors

- **prāpti** — acquisition or possession
  - **status:** previously encountered briefly; now formally defined
  - **function:** establishes that a dharma is obtained by and belongs to a continuum

- **lābha** — obtaining
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only refinement in this passage
  - **function:** acquisition of what was previously absent or had been relinquished

- **samanvāgama** — possession / being endowed with
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only refinement in this passage
  - **function:** maintained possession of what has already been acquired

- **aprāpti** — non-acquisition
  - **status:** formally introduced with prāpti
  - **function:** inverse condition of not possessing a dharma

- **svasaṃtāna-patita** — belonging to one’s own continuum
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** defines the domain within which acquisition and non-acquisition operate

## Translation Consequences

“Dissociated” must not be taken to mean unconditioned, inactive, or unrelated. These dharmas are fully conditioned. They are called dissociated only because they do not enter the fivefold association of citta and caittas.

“Acquisition” is also too narrow unless paired with “possession.” The Bhāṣya explicitly includes both initial obtaining and continued endowment.

Accordingly:

```text
prāpti
    = acquisition-possession
```

is the fullest provisional rendering.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The passage introduces a third conditioned layer:

```text
rūpa
    material determination

citta-caitta
    associated cognitive determination

viprayukta-saṃskāra
    dissociated structural determination
```

Prāpti is a relation of determinate belonging. Its logical form is neither simple inherence nor mere temporal succession.

```text
S has D
```

means that a real acquisition-relation connects dharma D to continuum S.

The immediate strength of this doctrine is that karmic, ethical, and path-status distinctions can persist even when the relevant mental factor is not presently manifest.

Its immediate difficulty is equally clear: what kind of entity is this possession-relation, and what work does it perform beyond redescribing the fact that a continuum has a dharma? The Bhāṣya will now press exactly this issue.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

This is a genuine structural-operator layer.

```text
citta-caitta
    describes the active event

prāpti
    describes the indexed status of a dharma
    relative to a continuum
```

In dataset terms, this is not simply a feature occurring in the present frame. It is a state relation preserved across frames:

```text
ContinuumState:
    subject = continuum
    dharma = acquired dharma
    relation = obtained | possessed | not-acquired
```

The Kośa is therefore distinguishing occurrence from entitlement or possession. A dharma need not be presently active in order for the continuum to be classified as possessing it.

That is the central advance of VAK 2.35.
