# VAK_2.25 — The Ten Wholesome Great-Ground Factors

## Kārikā

```text
śraddhā 'pramādaḥ praśrabdhir upekṣā hrīr apatrapā /
mūladvayam ahiṃsā ca vīryaṃ ca kuśale sadā // VAkK_2.25 //
```

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

The wholesome great ground is the sphere of those factors called the wholesome great-ground factors, which are present at all times in a wholesome citta.

What, then, are they?

Faith, heedfulness, pliancy, equanimity, moral shame, moral caution, the two wholesome roots, non-harming, and energy are always present in a wholesome citta.

These ten dharmas are constantly present in a wholesome citta.

Faith is clarity or serenity of citta. Others define it as conviction concerning truth, the Three Jewels, karma, and the results of karma.

Heedfulness is the cultivation of wholesome dharmas.

What cultivation is there apart from those wholesome dharmas themselves?

It is careful attentiveness with regard to them. Members of another school recite in the sūtra that heedfulness is the guarding of citta.

Pliancy is the workability of citta.

But is bodily pliancy not also mentioned in the sūtra?

It certainly is mentioned. It should be understood in the same manner as bodily feeling.

How, then, is it to be connected with the factors of awakening?

There bodily pliancy should be understood as the workability of the body.

How can it be called a factor of awakening?

Because it is favorable to the factors of awakening. Bodily workability brings forth mental workability, which is itself a factor of awakening.

Does this kind of transferred designation occur elsewhere?

It does. For example, the Blessed One calls joy and the dharmas serving as bases for joy the awakening factor of joy. He calls aversion and the signs of aversion the hindrance of ill will. Right view, right intention, and right effort are called the aggregate of wisdom, although intention and effort are not themselves wisdom by nature; because they are favorable to wisdom, they receive that designation. In the same way, bodily pliancy receives the name “factor of awakening” because it is favorable to a factor of awakening.

Equanimity is evenness of citta and non-exertion of citta.

How is that compatible with the statement that, in the same citta, attention has the nature of exertion while equanimity has the nature of non-exertion?

Was it not already said that the distinction among these factors is difficult to know?

Yet even something difficult to know can still be known. What is exceedingly difficult to know is how there can be non-contradiction despite opposition.

There is no contradiction if exertion concerns one aspect and non-exertion another.

But then all associated factors would not have one and the same object-support.

Other difficulties of this kind will also arise here; the same interpretive method should be applied to them.

Moral shame and moral caution will be explained later.

The two roots are the two wholesome roots: non-greed and non-hatred.

Non-delusion also exists, but it has the nature of prajñā. Since prajñā is already a universal great-ground factor, non-delusion is not counted again as exclusively a wholesome great-ground factor.

Non-harming is non-injury.

Energy is the energetic arousal of citta.

The wholesome great-ground factors have now been explained.

## Voice Analysis

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — opening classification and definitions.
- **Voice: Other teachers** — alternative definition of faith as conviction concerning truth, the Three Jewels, karma, and karmic result.
- **Voice: Another school** — citation defining heedfulness as guarding the citta.
- **Voice: Objector** — challenges concerning bodily pliancy, its status as an awakening factor, and the compatibility of attention with equanimity.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — replies through functional relation, transferred designation, and aspect-distinction.

## The Ten Factors

```text
faith
heedfulness
pliancy
equanimity
moral shame
moral caution
non-greed
non-hatred
non-harming
energy
```

These are not merely wholesome factors that may occur. They are said to be present **always in every wholesome citta**.

## Main Determinations

### 1. Faith

The Bhāṣya gives two definitions:

```text
faith
    = clarity or serenity of citta
```

and, according to another view:

```text
faith
    = conviction regarding truth,
      the Three Jewels,
      karma,
      and karmic result
```

The first is phenomenological and affective; the second is doctrinal and intentional.

### 2. Heedfulness

Heedfulness is not an additional wholesome object alongside wholesome dharmas. It is the mode in which they are sustained:

```text
heedfulness
    = careful attentiveness toward wholesome dharmas
    = guarding of citta
```

### 3. Pliancy

Mental pliancy is defined as:

```text
cittakarmaṇyatā
    = workability of citta
```

The discussion of bodily pliancy introduces a rule of transferred designation:

```text
what directly supports an awakening factor
may itself receive the name of that awakening factor
```

Thus bodily pliancy is not itself identical with the mental awakening factor, but it is named in relation to the factor it supports.

### 4. Equanimity

Equanimity is:

```text
cittasamatā
    = evenness of citta

cittānābhogatā
    = non-exertion of citta
```

The objection is serious because attention was previously defined as exertion or application of citta. The reply is not that exertion and non-exertion are identical, but that they can concern different aspects.

This preserves co-presence without collapsing functional difference.

### 5. The Two Wholesome Roots

The two counted here are:

```text
alobha
    = non-greed

adveṣa
    = non-hatred
```

Non-delusion is not omitted doctrinally. It is excluded from this count because it is identical in nature with prajñā, already counted among the universal great-ground factors.

### 6. Non-harming and Energy

```text
ahiṃsā
    = non-injury

vīrya
    = energetic arousal of citta
```

## New Technical Terms

- **kuśalamahābhūmika** — a factor whose sphere is every wholesome citta; absent from the Kārikā sequence before this local taxonomy; function: class-definition.
- **cittaprasāda** — clarity or serenity of citta; introduced as a definition of faith; function: phenomenological definition.
- **abhisaṃpratyaya** — conviction or confident acceptance; introduced in the alternative account of faith; function: doctrinal definition.
- **cittārakṣā** — guarding of citta; introduced as another school’s definition of heedfulness; function: school doctrine.
- **cittakarmaṇyatā** — workability of citta; introduced as the definition of pliancy; function: functional definition.
- **kāyakarmaṇyatā** — workability of the body; introduced in the bodily-pliancy debate; function: distinction between bodily and mental support.
- **bodhyaṅgānukūlya** — favorability toward an awakening factor; introduced to explain transferred designation; function: relational naming rule.
- **cittasamatā** — evenness of citta; introduced as a definition of equanimity; function: functional definition.
- **cittānābhogatā** — non-exertion of citta; introduced as a second definition of equanimity; function: contrast with attention.
- **tācchabdya** — receiving a name by relation or conformity; implicit in the explanation of transferred designation; function: semantic rule.

## Translation Consequences

1. **Praśrabdhi** should not be reduced to relaxation. “Pliancy” or “workability” preserves the practical sense of a citta or body made serviceable.
2. **Upekṣā** here is not merely neutral feeling. It is evenness and non-exertion of citta, a wholesome mental factor distinct from the feeling-faculty called upekṣā.
3. **Apramāda** is not merely caution. It is active maintenance of wholesome dharmas and guarding of citta.
4. **Śraddhā** is broader than belief. The Bhāṣya preserves both clarity of citta and conviction regarding doctrinal truths.
5. **Amoha** must not be treated as absent from the wholesome field. It is present through prajñā but not counted twice.

## Structural Result

The wholesome bhūmi is a coordinated field with four major dimensions:

```text
orientation:
    faith
    heedfulness

workability and balance:
    pliancy
    equanimity

ethical restraint:
    shame
    caution
    non-greed
    non-hatred
    non-harming

active power:
    energy
```

The Bhāṣya does not yet present these as objects of samyama. It presents them as factors whose operating range is every wholesome citta.

## Compact Formula

```text
wholesome citta
    necessarily includes
    confidence + vigilance + workability + balance
    + ethical restraint + energetic power
```
