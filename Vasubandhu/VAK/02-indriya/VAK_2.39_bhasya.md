# VAK 2.39 Bhāṣya — Aprāpti and the Status of the Ordinary Person

## Corresponding Kārikā

```text
akliṣṭāvyākṛtā 'prāptiḥ
sā 'tītājātayos tridhā /
kāmādyāptāmalānāṃ ca
mārgasyāprāptir iṣyate // VAK 2.39 //
```

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

Is non-acquisition also divided into varieties in the same way as acquisition?

No.

What, then, is it?

> Non-acquisition is non-afflicted and indeterminate.

Every non-acquisition is exclusively unobscured-indeterminate.

With respect to temporal division:

> For past and future dharmas, it is threefold.

For a present dharma, the non-acquisition is present. For past and future dharmas, however, non-acquisition may belong to all three times.

> For dharmas belonging to the desire realm and the other realms, and for uncontaminated dharmas, it is also threefold.

For dharmas belonging to the desire realm, non-acquisition may belong to the desire, form, or formless realm. The same applies respectively to dharmas belonging to the form and formless realms, and also to uncontaminated dharmas. There is, however, no uncontaminated non-acquisition.

For this reason it is held that:

> Non-acquisition of the Path is ordinary-person status.

The treatise says:

> What is ordinary-person status? It is non-acquisition of the noble dharmas.

“Non-acquisition” here means aprāpti.

Ordinary-person status cannot itself be uncontaminated.

Of which noble dharmas is there non-acquisition?

Of all of them, because the text makes no distinction.

But this means the non-acquisition that exists without acquisition. Otherwise, even a Buddha would be an ordinary person because he does not possess the dharmas belonging specifically to the lineages of disciples and solitary buddhas.

**Objector:** Then the restrictive particle “only” should be included.

**Reply:** It need not be explicitly stated. A single word can itself express restriction, as in “water-eater” or “air-eater.”

Others say that ordinary-person status is non-acquisition of the receptivity accompanying knowledge of dharmas with respect to suffering.

**Objection:** Would this not entail that someone associated with that non-acquisition is not noble?

**Reply:** No, because once that receptivity has been acquired, its non-acquisition has been permanently destroyed.

**Objection:** What, then, of those whose disposition admits three lineages? Of which noble dharmas do they have non-acquisition?

**Reply:** Of all of them.

But then the former fault returns.

And the former reply must again be given.

In that case the entire effort is pointless.

The Sautrāntika account is better:

> Ordinary-person status is a continuum in which noble dharmas have not arisen.

How, then, is this non-acquisition abandoned?

That will be explained in the next verse: it is abandoned through the arising of the acquisition of that dharma or through a change of realm.

## Natural Argument of the Passage

The passage has two movements.

First, it classifies *aprāpti*:

```text
ethical status:
    always unobscured-indeterminate

temporal status:
    present dharma → present non-acquisition
    past/future dharma → three-time non-acquisition

realm status:
    desire/form/formless profiles are possible
    uncontaminated non-acquisition is impossible
```

Second, it tests the doctrine against *pṛthagjanatva*, ordinary-person status.

The Vaibhāṣika formula is:

```text
ordinary person
    = one possessing non-acquisition
      of the noble dharmas
```

Vasubandhu exposes the difficulty: if this means non-acquisition of every noble dharma without qualification, even a Buddha would fail to possess the lineage-specific dharmas of disciples and solitary buddhas. The proposed restrictions become increasingly artificial.

The Sautrāntika replacement is structurally simpler:

```text
ordinary-person status
    = a continuum in which noble dharmas have not arisen
```

This eliminates the need to posit a separate real entity called non-acquisition.

## Voices

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

- Aprāpti is a real dissociated formation.
- Ordinary-person status is non-acquisition of noble dharmas.

**Voice: Objector**

- Presses the overbreadth of “non-acquisition of all noble dharmas.”
- Notes that Buddhas do not possess lineage-specific dharmas of all vehicles.
- Challenges proposed repairs as circular or pointless.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

- Favors the Sautrāntika account.
- Replaces a substantialized non-acquisition with the actual condition of the continuum.

## New Technical Terms

- **pṛthagjanatva** — ordinary-person status; the condition of not yet being noble.
  - **status:** newly central in this Bhāṣya passage.
  - **function:** tests whether aprāpti must be a real entity.

- **āryadharma** — noble dharma, especially Path-dharmas distinguishing the noble person.
  - **status:** doctrinally familiar, newly used here as the object of non-acquisition.
  - **function:** marks transformation from ordinary to noble status.

- **dharmajñānakṣānti** — receptivity accompanying knowledge of dharmas, here with respect to suffering.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific technical refinement in this unit.
  - **function:** proposed narrower criterion for ordinary-person status.

- **anutpannāryadharmasantati** — a continuum in which noble dharmas have not arisen.
  - **status:** Sautrāntika formulation introduced here.
  - **function:** replaces aprāpti as a separate substance with a causal description of the continuum.

## Translation Consequences

### Aprāpti is not mere logical negation

Within the Vaibhāṣika framework, *aprāpti* is a positive conditioned status of non-possession. Yet Vasubandhu's criticism steadily reduces it to a designation based on the continuum's actual capacities and history.

Therefore the safest translation remains:

```text
aprāpti
    = non-acquisition / non-possession
```

but never simply “absence.”

### Pṛthagjana is genetically defined

The Sautrāntika formula does not define an ordinary person by an additional negative entity attached to the stream. It defines the stream by what has not yet arisen within it.

```text
not:
    continuum + real aprāpti entity

but:
    continuum lacking the arising
    of noble dharmas
```

This is a genetic and developmental definition.

## Doctrinal Determination

This verse continues Vasubandhu's critique of reified relational operators.

VAK 2.36 replaced *prāpti* with seed-capacity and transformation of the continuum. VAK 2.39 now does the same for *aprāpti*:

```text
prāpti
    redescribed as effective capacity
    within a transformed continuum

aprāpti
    redescribed as the non-arising
    of a relevant capacity or dharma
    within that continuum
```

The underlying issue is whether relation-status requires an additional substance.

Vasubandhu's answer is increasingly negative.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

For the later Theoretical Dharma Path, this passage suggests that a status should be modeled through the developmental condition of a series rather than by multiplying hidden entities.

```text
OrdinarySeries:
    nobleDharmaArisen = false

NobleSeries:
    nobleDharmaArisen = true
```

The conceptual gain is considerable:

```text
classification
    grounded in genesis of the series

not
    classification grounded in
    an independently reified negative token
```

This is especially relevant to the idea of Principle as Seed. The absence of a Principle is not necessarily another entity called “non-possession of the Principle.” It may simply mean that the relevant classificatory or transformative Principle has not yet arisen—or has not yet become operative—in the continuum.

## Summary

VAK 2.39 gives a technical classification of *aprāpti*, but the Bhāṣya's philosophical center is the dispute over ordinary-person status.

The Vaibhāṣika account:

```text
pṛthagjanatva
    = real non-acquisition
      of noble dharmas
```

The preferred Sautrāntika account:

```text
pṛthagjanatva
    = a continuum in which
      noble dharmas have not arisen
```

The second account is more economical and more genetic. It defines the person through the developmental structure of the continuum rather than through a separately existing negative formation.
