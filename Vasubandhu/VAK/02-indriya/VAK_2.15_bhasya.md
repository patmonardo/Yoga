# VAK_2.15_bhasya — Cessation of Faculties at Death

## Kārikā

```text
nirodhayaty uparamān nārūpye jīvitaṃ manaḥ /
upekṣāṃ caiva rūpe 'ṣṭau kāme daśa navāṣṭau vā // VAkK_2.15 //
```

## Continuous Translation

The acquisition of faculties has been explained. Their loss must now be explained: when one dies in a given realm, how many faculties cease?

In the formless realm, at death, the life faculty, the mind faculty, and the neutral-feeling faculty cease.

In the form realm, one who dies causes eight faculties to cease: those three, together with the five beginning with the eye. For all beings there are spontaneously born; they arise with complete faculties and die with them complete.

In the desire realm, one possessing both sexual characteristics causes ten faculties to cease: the eight just mentioned together with the female and male faculties. One possessing a single sexual characteristic causes nine to cease. One possessing neither causes eight to cease.

This is the rule when death occurs all at once.

But in gradual death, four faculties cease together: the body faculty, the life faculty, the mind faculty, and the neutral-feeling faculty. These do not cease separately.

This rule is to be understood for death occurring with an afflicted or neutral mind.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting the standard Abhidharma classification.

No sustained objection appears in this unit.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 2.14 asked which resultant faculties are first acquired when a continuum begins. VAK 2.15 asks the inverse question: which faculties cease when the continuum ends?

The minimum terminal set is:

```text
life
mind
neutral feeling
```

In the form realm, the five sensory faculties are added:

```text
life
mind
neutral feeling
five sensory faculties
    = eight
```

In the desire realm, sexual differentiation determines the final count:

```text
without sexual faculty
    = eight

one sexual faculty
    = nine

both sexual faculties
    = ten
```

The Bhāṣya then distinguishes two modes of death:

```text
sakṛn-maraṇa
    death occurring all at once

krama-mṛtyu
    gradual death
```

In gradual death, only four faculties form an inseparable terminal cluster:

```text
body
life
mind
neutral feeling
```

The statement `nahy eṣāṃ pṛthag-nirodhaḥ` is decisive: these four do not cease separately.

## Translation Consequences

### 1. `uparama`

Here `uparama` means the stopping that occurs at death, not liberative cessation in general.

### 2. `nirodha`

`Nirodha` in this passage is the cessation of a presently possessed faculty. It should not be imported without qualification into the higher doctrine of cessation.

### 3. `samagrendriya`

Spontaneously born beings arise and die with their faculties complete. The form-realm count therefore assumes no progressive loss of the five sensory faculties before death.

### 4. Simultaneous and gradual death

The Kārikā itself gives realm-based counts. The Bhāṣya adds the operational distinction between complete simultaneous cessation and gradual bodily dissolution.

## New Technical Terms

- **tyāga** — loss or relinquishment of faculties; here the inverse of initial acquisition
  - **status:** newly foregrounded in this passage
  - **function:** introduces the cessation-side classification

- **sakṛn-maraṇa** — death occurring all at once
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** defines the mode presupposed by the Kārikā's realm counts

- **krama-mṛtyu** — gradual death
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** distinguishes progressive dissolution from simultaneous terminal cessation

- **samagrendriya** — possessing complete faculties
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only terminology
  - **function:** explains the fixed form-realm count

- **pṛthag-nirodha** — separate cessation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expression
  - **function:** denied of the body, life, mind, and neutral-feeling faculties in gradual death

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage does not treat death as the departure of a self. It analyzes the ending of a continuum through the cessation of coordinated faculties.

The terminal event is therefore expressed as a structured dependency:

```text
no substantial bearer departs

rather:
    faculty-supports cease
    according to realm
    embodiment
    and mode of dying
```

The fourfold terminal cluster in gradual death is especially important. Body, life, mind, and neutral feeling are treated as mutually implicated at the final stage and do not terminate independently.

## Limited Organon Contact

Within the planning-system analogy, VAK 2.14 described initialization and VAK 2.15 describes shutdown.

The analogy is useful only at the functional level:

```text
realm
    determines available faculty architecture

mode of embodiment
    determines optional components

mode of death
    determines shutdown sequence
```

The Bhāṣya itself, however, remains a technical analysis of death and faculty cessation, not a general systems theory.
