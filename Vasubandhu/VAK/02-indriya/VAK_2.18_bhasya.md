# VAK_2.18 — Necessary Faculty Co-Presence

## Kārikā

```text
caturbhiḥ sukhakāyābhyāṃ pañcabhiś cakṣurādimān /
saumanasyī ca duḥkhī tu saptabhiḥ strīndriyādimān // VAkK_2.18 //
```

The final expression `strīndriyādimān` is completed syntactically by `aṣṭābhiḥ`, the opening word of VAK 2.19. The Bhāṣya makes this carryover explicit.

## Continuous Bhāṣya Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> One who possesses the pleasure faculty is necessarily endowed with four faculties: the three already mentioned—neutral feeling, life, and mind—together with the pleasure faculty.
>
> One who possesses the body faculty is likewise endowed with four: those same three together with the body faculty.
>
> One who possesses the eye faculty is necessarily endowed with five: neutral feeling, life, mind, body, and eye. The same should be understood for the ear, nose, and tongue faculties.
>
> One who possesses the gladness faculty is also necessarily endowed with five: neutral feeling, life, mind, pleasure, and gladness.
>
> Suppose someone is born in the second dhyāna and has not attained the third dhyāna. With what pleasure faculty is that person endowed? With an afflicted pleasure faculty belonging to the level of the third dhyāna.
>
> One who possesses the pain faculty is necessarily endowed with seven: body, life, and mind, together with four feeling faculties.
>
> One who possesses the female faculty—and likewise the faculties indicated by “and so on”—is necessarily endowed with eight. The number eight is supplied by the opening of the following verse.

## Reconstruction of the Counts

The irreducible triad established in VAK 2.17 is:

```text
upekṣā
jīvita
manas
```

From that base, possession of further faculties entails additional dependencies.

### Pleasure or Body

```text
sukha-holder:
    upekṣā
    jīvita
    manas
    sukha
    = four

body-holder:
    upekṣā
    jīvita
    manas
    body
    = four
```

### Eye and the Other External Cognitive Faculties

```text
eye-holder:
    upekṣā
    jīvita
    manas
    body
    eye
    = five
```

The same structure applies separately to ear, nose, and tongue.

The body faculty is required because the eye, ear, nose, and tongue faculties occur as differentiated material bases within an embodied continuum.

### Gladness

```text
gladness-holder:
    upekṣā
    jīvita
    manas
    sukha
    saumanasya
    = five
```

The surprising inclusion of `sukha` reflects the Abhidharma distribution of feeling across meditative levels. The Bhāṣya's question about a being born in the second dhyāna but lacking attainment of the third shows that co-possession does not always mean simultaneous manifestation at one level. A latent or afflicted faculty belonging to another meditative level can still count as possessed.

### Pain

```text
pain-holder:
    body
    life
    mind
    four feeling faculties
    = seven
```

The four feelings are:

```text
duḥkha
sukha
saumanasya
upekṣā
```

Distress is not included as necessary merely from possession of bodily pain.

### Female Faculty and the “Et Cetera” Group

The Kārikā's `ādi` is explained in the continuation as gathering:

```text
female faculty
male faculty
distress
five faculties beginning with faith
```

Each case entails a minimum of eight faculties, although the composition differs. The detailed eightfold counts belong to the opening explanation of VAK 2.19.

## Doctrinal Determination

This is no longer a classification of faculties by realm, ethical status, maturation, or abandonment. It is a theory of **necessary co-possession**:

```text
possession of faculty X
    entails
possession of a determinate supporting configuration
```

The chapter is therefore moving from a list of powers toward a dependency structure.

Yet `samanvāgama`, possession or endowment, must not be reduced to present execution. A person may possess a faculty that is not presently manifest, as shown by the afflicted third-dhyāna pleasure faculty attributed to one born in the second dhyāna.

```text
possession
    ≠ present activation
```

It means that the faculty belongs to the continuum under the Abhidharma rules of acquisition and non-loss.

## New Technical Terms

- **samanvāgata** — endowed with or possessing a dharma; broader than presently manifesting it.
  - **status:** already implicit in the preceding configuration discussion, now structurally central.
  - **function:** states lawful co-possession of faculties within one continuum.

- **dvitīyadhyānaja** — one born in the second dhyāna.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification in this passage.
  - **function:** tests whether possession is restricted to faculties manifest at one's present meditative level.

- **tṛtīyadhyānālābhin** — one who has not attained the third dhyāna.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification.
  - **function:** sharpens the distinction between meditative attainment and possession of an afflicted faculty belonging to that level.

- **kliṣṭa** — afflicted or contaminated.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinally; newly applied here to a pleasure faculty of another dhyāna level.
  - **function:** explains how the pleasure faculty can be possessed without purified attainment of the third dhyāna.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā-first file treated `strīndriyādimān` as though it belonged to the preceding seven-count. The Bhāṣya shows that this is incorrect. The syntax crosses the verse boundary:

```text
strīndriyādimān
    +
aṣṭābhiḥ
```

Therefore:

```text
one possessing the female faculty and so on
    = endowed with eight
```

The Bhāṣya also corrects a possible simplification of the configuration model. The counts concern possession across a continuum, not merely faculties active in one present cognitive event.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

The strongest structural result is:

```text
faculty system
    = dependency graph
    + acquisition state
    + latent possession
    + current activation
```

The distinction between possession and manifestation is essential for any planning-system analogy. A system can possess a capacity without executing it at the present moment. But the Bhāṣya's own model remains karmic and meditative: the continuity belongs to a sentient series structured by realm, dhyāna, acquisition, and affliction.

## Compact Formula

```text
VAK 2.18 maps minimum co-possession rules:
pleasure or body entails four faculties;
eye and the other external cognitive faculties entail five;
gladness entails five;
pain entails seven;
and the female faculty and the group indicated by “and so on” entail eight, as completed by VAK 2.19.
```
