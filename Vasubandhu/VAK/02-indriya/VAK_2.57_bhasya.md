# VAK 2.57 — Bhāṣya: Maturation, Homogeneous Outflow, and Disconnection

## Kārikā

```text
vipāko 'vyākṛto dharmaḥ sattvākhyo vyākṛtodbhavaḥ /
niḥṣyando hetusadṛśo visaṃyogaḥ kṣayo dhiyā // VAK 2.57 //
```

## Translation

**Maturation-result is an indeterminate dharma, designated as belonging to a sentient continuum, and arising from what is morally determinate. Homogeneous outflow is similar to its cause. Disconnection is destruction through insight.**

## Bhāṣya Translation

What, then, is called a maturation-result, and what are the other results through the dominant result?

### Maturation-result

**Maturation-result is an indeterminate dharma.**

A maturation-result is an unobscured-indeterminate dharma.

It might also be something not designated as belonging to a sentient being; therefore the verse says:

**It is designated as belonging to a sentient continuum.**

It might also be something produced through nourishment or a homogeneous outflow; therefore it says:

**It arises from what is determinate.**

Wholesome and unwholesome dharmas are called determinate because they are determinable with respect to maturation. Therefore a result that arises at a later time—not simultaneously and not immediately afterward—is a maturation-result. This is the defining characteristic of maturation.

Why is something produced by karma but not designated as belonging to a sentient continuum not a maturation-result?

Because it is shared. Another person is also capable of enjoying it in the same way.

Maturation-result, however, is unshared. One person does not experience the maturation of karma performed by another.

Why, then, can one experience a dominant result produced by others?

Because it arises from common karma.

### Homogeneous-outflow result

**Homogeneous outflow is similar to its cause.**

A dharma similar to its cause is called a homogeneous-outflow result, as in the case of homogeneous cause and pervasive cause.

If the result of a pervasive cause is also similar to its cause, why is it not simply accepted as a result of homogeneous cause?

Because its similarity lies in belonging to the same level and in being afflicted, but not necessarily in its specific type.

Where similarity also exists in specific type, the cause is indeed admitted as a homogeneous cause.

For this reason a fourfold analysis is made concerning what is a homogeneous cause and what is a pervasive cause:

1. a homogeneous cause that is not pervasive;
2. a pervasive cause belonging to another afflictive class;
3. a pervasive cause belonging to the same afflictive class;
4. the remaining cases that are neither in the relevant respect.

### Disconnection-result

**Disconnection is destruction through insight.**

Destruction means cessation. Insight means wisdom. Thus analytical cessation is called the result of disconnection.

## Voice Map

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces and organizes the definitions of the result-types.
- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — supplies the technical definitions of maturation, homogeneous outflow, and disconnection as distinct real result-classes.
- **Voice: Objector** — asks why shared karmically produced environments are not maturation-results and why pervasive results are not simply homogeneous results.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu / exegetical reply** — answers through the distinctions shared/unshared and generic/specific similarity.

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Maturation is not mere succession

Maturation requires a precise profile:

```text
moral determination of the cause
+ temporal distance
+ appropriation by one sentient continuum
+ ethically neutral result
```

A result does not count as maturation merely because karma contributed to it. Shared environments may be karmically produced, yet because they are commonly enjoyed, they belong to dominant result rather than to the strictly individual maturation-result.

### 2. The result may differ ethically from its cause

Wholesome or unwholesome karma produces an indeterminate result. Thus causal continuity does not require identity of ethical status.

```text
determinate karma
    → transformation of the continuum
    → indeterminate maturation-result
```

The effect preserves the lawful efficacy of the cause without merely copying its predicate.

### 3. Homogeneous outflow is continuity through similarity

The homogeneous-outflow result resembles its cause. But resemblance has degrees:

- same level;
- same afflicted status;
- same specific type.

The pervasive cause need only reproduce the wider afflicted field. Homogeneous cause requires the more exact likeness of type.

### 4. Disconnection is effected through knowing

The verse defines disconnection as destruction through **dhī**, insight or wisdom. The result is therefore inseparable from cognitive efficacy:

```text
wisdom
    → destruction of the bond
    → analytical cessation / disconnection
```

The Path does not manufacture cessation as an ordinary conditioned product. Through knowing, it destroys the causal capacity of bondage and thereby realizes disconnection.

## Satkārya and the Lawful Form of Truth

The passage strongly supports a genetic reading, but the comparison with **satkārya** must remain analogical rather than being attributed directly to Vasubandhu.

The result is not an arbitrary addition to its cause. Each fruit manifests a determinate causal law:

```text
maturation:
    the cause becomes manifest through transformed, delayed, individual consequence

homogeneous outflow:
    the cause becomes manifest through likeness and continuation

disconnection:
    insight becomes manifest through the destruction of bondage
```

Thus the effect is the **lawful form of the truth of its ground**. Yet the Kośa does not say that a permanent substance remains numerically identical beneath cause and effect. What persists is causal determination within the continuum.

## Rational–Empirical Determination

The empirical result is encountered as a present dharma. Rational *prakāśa* determines its genetic form:

```text
Empirical:
    this body, faculty, cognition, environment, or cessation of bondage

Rational:
    maturation, homogeneous continuation, common predominance,
    or destruction through insight
```

The same appearance can therefore be judged differently according to its genesis. A shared environment and an individual faculty may both arise through karma, but their result-types differ because their causal relations differ.

This is self-certain genetic judgment: not merely recognizing that a result is present, but determining **how its ground is present in it**.

## Translation Consequences

- **vipāka** — “maturation-result,” not merely “effect”; it includes delay, individual appropriation, and ethical neutrality.
- **sattvākhyā** — “designated as belonging to a sentient continuum”; this avoids treating *sattva* as a permanent person.
- **vyākṛtodbhava** — “arising from what is determinate,” specifically wholesome or unwholesome karma determinable as to maturation.
- **niḥṣyanda** — “homogeneous outflow” or “homogeneous continuation”; resemblance may be generic or specific.
- **dhī** — “insight” or “wisdom”; here it names the discriminative knowing that destroys the bond.
- **visaṃyoga** — “disconnection”; the result is freedom from conjunction with the afflictive bond.

## New Technical Terms

- **asādhāraṇa** — unshared; **status:** important refinement introduced in the Bhāṣya; **function:** distinguishes individual maturation from commonly experienced karmic results.
- **sādhāraṇa-karma** — common karma; **status:** Bhāṣya clarification; **function:** explains shared dominant results such as common environments.
- **vyākṛtodbhava** — arisen from the determinate; **status:** explicit explanation of the Kārikā; **function:** identifies wholesome and unwholesome karma as the source of maturation.
- **prakārataḥ sādṛśya** — similarity in specific type; **status:** Bhāṣya-only analytical distinction; **function:** separates homogeneous cause from the broader pervasive cause.
- **bhūmitaḥ sādṛśya** — similarity by level; **status:** Bhāṣya-only analytical distinction; **function:** explains how pervasive cause yields a homogeneous-outflow result without exact type-identity.
- **pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha** — analytical cessation; **status:** previously encountered, here newly defined as disconnection-result; **function:** cessation realized through wisdom.

## Organon Comment

VAK 2.57 supplies a result-semantics for genetic judgment:

```text
ResultJudgment:
    identify present determination
    trace its causal provenance
    determine its mode of manifestation
    distinguish individual, common, homogeneous, and liberative result
```

The crucial principle is:

> The truth of the cause is not merely repeated in the result; it is manifested under the lawful form proper to that causal relation.

This is why a produced dharma can itself become productive. As result it expresses its ground; as a newly present dharma it enters further causal relations. The continuum is therefore neither a static substance nor an aggregate of disconnected facts, but an articulated genetic order.
