# VAK 2.42 Bhāṣya — Asaṃjñi-samāpatti

## Kārikā

```text
tathāsaṃjñisamāpattiḥ dhyāne 'ntye niḥsṛtīcchayā /
śubhā upapadyavedyaiva nāryasya ekādhvikāpyate // VAK 2.42 //
```

## Continuous Translation

What are the two attainments previously mentioned? They are the attainment of non-ideation and the attainment of cessation.

What is the attainment of non-ideation? Just as the non-ideational state was described as “the cessation of citta and caittas,” so likewise is the attainment of non-ideation. It is called the attainment of the non-ideational beings, or the attainment consisting in non-ideation. It too is a cessation of citta and caittas; this much is carried forward by the word “likewise.”

This attainment belongs to the final dhyāna, namely the fourth. It is included in that level and in no other.

Why do practitioners enter it? Through the desire for release. They regard this state as an escape and therefore enter it out of a wish for liberation.

The non-ideational result itself is indeterminate because it is a maturation-result. The attainment, however, is wholesome. Its maturation is the five-aggregate existence among the non-ideational beings.

Because it is wholesome, it is exclusively to be experienced in the immediately following life. It is not to be experienced in the present life, nor is its result unfixed. Even one who produces this attainment and later falls away from it must, it is said, produce it again and be born among the non-ideational beings. For this very reason, one who has acquired it does not enter the certainty of the noble path.

This attainment is held to belong only to an ordinary person, not to a noble one. Noble persons do not enter it because they see it as a place of downfall. Those who take it to be release enter it because they conceive it as liberation.

Do noble persons who obtain the fourth dhyāna acquire its past and future instances, as they do the dhyāna itself? No. Nor do others acquire it merely through obtaining that level. Why? Even when habituated, it is accomplished only through powerful preparation, and because it is a state without citta.

It is acquired in only one temporal mode: only a present instance is obtained, like the restraint of individual liberation. Once obtained, however, from the second moment onward one is also endowed with the past instance for as long as it has not been relinquished. Because the attainment is without citta, no future instance is cultivated.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya distinguishes sharply between the attainment and its result:

```text
asaṃjñi-samāpatti
    wholesome intentional cultivation

āsaṃjñika existence
    indeterminate maturation-result
```

The practitioner mistakes suspension of citta and caittas for liberation. The state is therefore ethically wholesome as disciplined practice, but doctrinally misdirected.

Its causal profile is unusually strict:

```text
cause
    powerful preparation in the fourth dhyāna

motive
    desire for release

result
    rebirth among non-ideational beings

eligibility
    ordinary person only
```

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames and explains the distinctions.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — the detailed temporal and karmic classification of the attainment.

**Voice: Objector** — asks whether noble persons acquire past and future instances with the fourth dhyāna.

## New Technical Terms

- **asaṃjñi-samāpatti** — attainment of non-ideation; a cultivated cessation of citta and caittas.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā analysis before this block
  - **function:** special meditative attainment
- **niḥsṛti-icchā** — desire for release.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory motive
  - **function:** explains why the attainment is entered
- **upapadya-vedanīya** — to be experienced in the immediately following rebirth.
  - **status:** technical karmic result term
  - **function:** fixes maturation timing
- **ekādhvika** — belonging to one temporal mode.
  - **status:** rare
  - **function:** restricts acquisition to the present instance

## Translation Consequences

“Non-ideation” should not be translated as liberation. The Bhāṣya explicitly presents it as a state mistaken for release by ordinary practitioners.

The phrase “wholesome” qualifies the attainment as intentional cultivation, not the resultant existence, which is neutral maturation.

## Doctrinal Determination

This passage prevents a simple identification of cessation with freedom:

```text
cessation of citta-caittas
    can be cultivated
    can be wholesome
    can produce exalted rebirth

and still
    not be noble liberation
```

The decisive criterion is not mere absence of mental activity, but whether the attainment belongs to the noble path and correct insight.

## Limited Organon Interpretation

The state suppresses the operation of the cognitive system without transforming its principle. It is therefore suspension rather than completed rational liberation.

```text
operation removed
    ≠ principle comprehended
```

This helps preserve the distinction between a non-ideational goal and an intensely ideational path of knowing.
