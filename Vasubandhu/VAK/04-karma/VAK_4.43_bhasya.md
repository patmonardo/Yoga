# VAK_4.43_bhasya — Eligibility for Restraint and Non-Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.43**

```iast
nṛṇām asaṃvaro hitvā śaṇḍhapaṇḍadvidhākṛtīn |
kurūṃś ca saṃvaro 'py evaṃ devānāṃ ca nṛṇāṃ trayaḥ ||
```

> Non-restraint occurs among humans, excluding the śaṇḍhas, paṇḍakas, those of twofold constitution, and the Kurus; restraint is likewise. Among humans are the three restraints, and restraint also occurs among gods as further specified.

See: [VAK_4.43.md](./VAK_4.43.md)

## Source Cautions

The passage contains inherited Abhidharma/Vinaya classifications of bodies and modes of birth. They should be reported as the text's technical categories, not silently mapped onto modern identities.

The argument is specifically about the acquisition of the standing karmic forms `saṃvara` and `asaṃvara`, not about an absolute incapacity for wholesome or unwholesome action.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu asks explicitly: **for which beings does non-restraint occur, and for which does restraint occur?**

The first answer restricts `asaṃvara` to the human mode of existence, with specific exclusions. Restraint occurs in humans under the corresponding exclusions and also among gods.

The Bhāṣya then asks why the excluded human configurations do not possess restraint. The answer is not merely juridical. Vasubandhu gives psychological and support-based reasons: the support is dominated by strong affliction, is said to lack capacity for the required reflective discrimination (`pratisaṃkhyāna`), and lacks the intense shame and moral apprehension (`hrī`, `vyapatrāpya`) required by this theory of acquisition.

Why then do these same supports not acquire non-restraint? Because, according to the text, their intention toward evil is also unstable (`pāpe 'py asthirāśayatva`). Restraint and non-restraint are treated as genuine contraries (`pratidvandva`), arising in the same general field of eligible supports.

Uttarakuru is excluded for a different reason: there is neither undertaking/meditative discipline (`samādāna`, `samādhi`) nor the intention toward harmful activity that would generate non-restraint.

Vasubandhu finally summarizes these exclusions through a striking image: the support itself is like a **barren field (`ūṣarakṣetra`)** in which neither restraint nor non-restraint grows properly.

An objector then cites the sūtra in which a nāga observes the eight-limbed upavāsa. Vasubandhu replies that this is only `sucaritamātra`, mere good conduct, **not** technically acquired restraint.

The verse closes by specifying that humans can possess all three forms of restraint: prātimokṣa, dhyāna-born, and uncontaminated restraint.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — framing questions, explanations, and final distinction.

**Voice: Objector** — the appeal to the sūtra concerning the nāga observing upavāsa.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratisaṃkhyānākṣamatva** — incapacity for the required discriminative reflection
  - **status:** newly operative in this passage
  - **function:** explanation of ineligibility for restraint

- **tīvra-hrī-vyapatrāpya** — intense shame and moral apprehension
  - **status:** existing Abhidharma vocabulary, newly operative here
  - **function:** causal condition invoked for acquisition

- **asthirāśayatva** — instability of practical intention or disposition
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here
  - **function:** explanation of failure to acquire non-restraint

- **pratidvandva** — direct contrary or opponent
  - **status:** rare / locally important
  - **function:** establishes the contrary relation of saṃvara and asaṃvara

- **ūṣarakṣetra** — barren or saline field
  - **status:** Bhāṣya analogy
  - **function:** image for a support in which neither standing form takes root

- **sucaritamātra** — mere good conduct
  - **status:** previously encountered distinction
  - **function:** distinguishes good action from technically acquired saṃvara

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage requires several distinctions:

```text
good action
    ≠ technical saṃvara

bad action
    ≠ technical asaṃvara
```

Both restraint and non-restraint are **acquired standing forms** requiring an eligible support and sufficiently stable causal conditions.

The structure is therefore:

```text
eligible āśraya
    + appropriate āśaya / discipline
    → standing karmic determination
```

The nāga example is decisive: even behavior matching the outward form of upavāsa can be classified as `sucarita` without the technical `saṃvara` being acquired.

The final distribution within the human domain is:

```text
humans, where eligible:
    prātimokṣa-saṃvara
    dhyānaja-saṃvara
    anāsrava-saṃvara
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation should avoid making the last phrase sound as if all three restraints occur indiscriminately among both gods and humans. The Bhāṣya clarifies:

> **Among eligible humans there are all three restraints. Restraint also occurs among gods, with the higher forms distributed as specified in 4.44.**

This preserves the enjambment into the next verse.

## Logical Determination

The main distinction is between an act and the **capacity of a support to bear a standing determination**:

```text
act-token
    may occur

but

standing saṃvara / asaṃvara
    requires a suitable āśraya
```

The barren-field analogy expresses this precisely:

```text
seed alone is insufficient
field alone is insufficient

standing form arises only
where causal seed and suitable field coincide
```

Eligibility is therefore part of the ontology of acquisition, not an external administrative afterthought.

## Organon Contact Point

Restrained formulation:

```text
universal form
    requires a determinate bearer
    capable of sustaining it
```

The Bhāṣya is analyzing conditions under which a practical determination can become stable in a continuum. This is structurally useful for our model of generated practical form, but the inherited anthropological classifications remain Vasubandhu's doctrinal framework and should not be generalized beyond it.
