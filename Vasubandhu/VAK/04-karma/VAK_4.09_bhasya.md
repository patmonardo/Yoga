# VAK_4.09 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
saṃprayogeṇa tadyuktāḥ samutthānāt kriyādayaḥ |
viparyayeṇākuśalaṃ paramāvyākṛte dhruve ||
```

**Working translation:** Those associated with them are wholesome through association; actions and the rest are wholesome through their originating condition. The unwholesome is constituted conversely. The two permanent dharmas are ultimately indeterminate.

### Source cautions

The e-text contains obvious spacing and typographic irregularities such as `samutthā nāt`, `kāyavāvakkarmaṇi`, and `hriścāpatrapyaṃ`-type sequences. These are preserved here rather than silently normalized. The passage is interpreted through its clear argumentative structure.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.08 introduced four distinct senses in which something may be called wholesome. VAK 4.09 completes the scheme.

Dharmas associated with the intrinsically wholesome roots, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya` are wholesome `saṃprayogataḥ`, through association. Vasubandhu compares them to water mixed with medicine: the water is not itself medicine by nature, but acquires medicinal character through mixture.

Bodily and verbal karma, along with dissociated formations such as arising and other formations named in the passage, are wholesome `samutthānataḥ`, through the wholesome factors that produce them. Vasubandhu compares this to the milk of a wet nurse being affected by medicine she has consumed.

The Bhāṣya then notes a difficulty: some `prāptis` may arise through a mind of a different ethical type. Vasubandhu flags this as requiring explanation rather than forcing the simple rule without qualification.

The same fourfold structure is then inverted for the unwholesome. Saṃsāra is unwholesome in the ultimate sense because its nature is the continuation of suffering and thus the opposite of supreme security. The unwholesome roots, shamelessness, and lack of moral concern are intrinsically unwholesome; associated dharmas are unwholesome through association; and bodily/verbal action and related products are unwholesome through their originating condition.

An objection follows: if saṃsāra is ultimately unwholesome, would not every contaminated dharma become unwholesome and leave no room for the indeterminate? Vasubandhu answers that the preceding claim was made only in the ultimate sense. Relative to karmic maturation, contaminated dharmas that are not classified as yielding a determinate result are called `avyākṛta`, while those producing desirable maturation are called wholesome.

If one nevertheless asks for what is indeterminate in the ultimate sense, the answer is the two permanent unconditioned dharmas: space and non-analytical cessation.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of associative and originative goodness, analogies, the inverse structure of unwholesomeness, and clarification of ultimate versus conventional ethical classification.

**Voice: Objector** — asks whether the ultimate unwholesomeness of saṃsāra would eliminate the category of contaminated indeterminate dharmas.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — answers by distinguishing ultimate predication from classification relative to karmic maturation.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃprayogataḥ** — wholesome or unwholesome through association; **status:** explicit Bhāṣya elaboration of the Kārikā; **function:** mode of ethical predication.
- **samutthānataḥ** — wholesome or unwholesome through originating condition; **status:** previously encountered, now systematically classified; **function:** causal transmission of ethical determination.
- **paramārthataḥ** — in the ultimate sense; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** distinguishes ultimate ethical status from karmic classification.
- **svabhāvataḥ** — intrinsically / by own nature; **status:** explicit in the surrounding Bhāṣya; **function:** intrinsic ethical predication.
- **prāpti** — acquisition / possession; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** introduces a complication for simple origin-based ethical classification.
- **asaṃskṛta** — unconditioned; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** identifies the two ultimately indeterminate permanent dharmas.

## Doctrinal Determination

The completed fourfold structure is:

```text
kuśala
├── paramārthataḥ
│   └── mokṣa / nirvāṇa
├── svabhāvataḥ
│   └── kuśalamūla + hrī + apatrāpya
├── saṃprayogataḥ
│   └── dharmas associated with those factors
└── samutthānataḥ
    └── bodily/verbal karma and related products generated by them
```

The unwholesome is constituted by the contrary arrangement.

The important qualification is that `avyākṛta` remains meaningful at the level of karmic maturation even though saṃsāra is called unwholesome in the ultimate sense.

Ultimately indeterminate:

```text
ākāśa
apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
```

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation is substantially confirmed.

`kriyādayaḥ` is broader than bodily and verbal activity alone: the Bhāṣya includes bodily/verbal karma and several dissociated formations produced by wholesome associated dharmas.

`paramāvyākṛte dhruve` is now secure: the two permanent ultimately indeterminate dharmas are `ākāśa` and `apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha`.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya develops a grammar of predication according to the ground by which a predicate belongs:

```text
by ultimate status
by intrinsic nature
by association
by origination
```

Thus `kuśala` and `akuśala` are not homogeneous predicates applied in one way. Their mode of attribution matters.

A second distinction is equally important:

```text
ultimate determination
    ≠
classification relative to vipāka
```

This prevents the ultimate characterization of saṃsāra from collapsing the operative threefold ethical classification.

## Organon Contact Point

The passage is especially useful for later special study because it distinguishes several modes by which one determination belongs to another without reducing them to identity.

```text
intrinsic determination
association
causal origination
ultimate status
```

For now, the textual result is sufficient: ethical predicates are mediated by different relations, and Vasubandhu explicitly preserves the distinction between ultimate and functional classifications.
