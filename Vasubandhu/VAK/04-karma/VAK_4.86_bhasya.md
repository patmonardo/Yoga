# VAK 4.86 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā

**lobhajaṃ kāya-vāk-karma mithyājīvaḥ pṛthak kṛtaḥ |**  
**duḥśodhatvāt pariṣkāra-lobhotthaṃ cen na sūtrataḥ || 4.86 ||**

> Bodily or verbal karma born of greed is wrong livelihood. It is treated separately because it is difficult to purify. If one says that it is only what arises from greed for requisites, no—according to the sūtra.

[See the Kārikā study](./VAK_4.86.md).

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya opens from the Buddha's threefold expression:

> `mithyāvāk mithyākarmānto mithyājīvaḥ`

and asks:

> `ko 'yam anyas tābhyāṃ mithyājīvaḥ`
>
> What is this wrong livelihood that would be other than those two?

The answer is immediate:

> `nāyam anyo 'sti`
>
> It is not something other.

The Kārikā then specifies:

> `lobhajaṃ kāyavākkarma mithyājīvaḥ`
>
> Bodily and verbal karma born from greed is wrong livelihood.

The Bhāṣya contrasts:

> `dveṣamohajau tu kāyavākkarmāntau`
>
> Bodily and verbal wrong action arising from hatred and delusion are the other wrong bodily/verbal courses.

Why, then, is wrong livelihood separately named?

> `duḥśodhatvāt`
>
> Because it is difficult to purify.

Vasubandhu explains:

> `hārī hi lobhadharmaḥ / tatsamutthāt karmaṇaś cittaṃ na surakṣyam /`
>
> Greed has a carrying or dragging character; when action arises from it, the mind is not easily guarded.

Therefore:

> `ata ājīvo duḥkhaśodha ity ādarotpādanārthaṃ tatrāsau pṛthag nirdiṣṭaḥ`
>
> Because livelihood is difficult to purify, it is separately stated in order to generate careful attention toward it.

The Bhāṣya then quotes a verse contrasting the habitual dangers of householders and renunciants: householders have difficulty purifying view because of their exposure to diverse views, while a monk's livelihood is difficult to purify because he looks to others for support.

An objection restricts wrong livelihood to bodily/verbal activity arising specifically from greed for the requisites of life:

> `jīvitapariṣkāralobhottham eva kāyavākkarma mithyājīvo nānyat`

The example offered is that dancing, singing, and similar activity done merely for one's own amusement would then not concern livelihood.

The Kārikā rejects this:

> `na sūtrataḥ`
>
> Not according to the sūtra.

The Bhāṣya explains that in the Śīlaskandhika material the Buddha places even such activities as going to watch elephant fights under wrong livelihood.

Asked why, it answers:

> `mithyāviṣayaparibhogāt`
>
> Because of improper enjoyment/use of an object-field.

## Natural Segmentation

The argument has four stages:

```text
1. Is wrong livelihood a third bodily/verbal action-type?
       ↓
   No.

2. What is it?
       ↓
   bodily or verbal karma born from greed

3. Why name it separately?
       ↓
   because greed makes livelihood especially difficult to purify

4. Is it limited to greed for material requisites?
       ↓
   No; sūtra gives a wider extension
```

## Literal Prose Translation

The Blessed One has spoken of wrong speech, wrong action, and wrong livelihood. What is this wrong livelihood that is different from those two? It is not something different. Rather, bodily and verbal karma born from greed is wrong livelihood; bodily and verbal actions born from hatred and delusion are the other wrong actions.

Yet wrong livelihood is distinguished separately from them because it is difficult to purify. Greed has a carrying, dragging nature, and the mind is not easily protected with regard to action that arises from greed. Therefore wrong livelihood is separately taught so that careful concern may be generated toward what is difficult to purify.

If one says that only bodily and verbal karma arising from greed for the requisites of life is wrong livelihood, and not other activity—for dancing, singing, and the like undertaken merely for one's own enjoyment would not concern livelihood—this is not so according to the sūtra. In the Śīlaskandhika teaching the Blessed One places even such things as going to see elephant fights under wrong livelihood. Why? Because of improper enjoyment or use of the object-field.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Wrong livelihood adds no new expressive medium

The first question is classificatory:

```text
wrong speech
wrong bodily action
wrong livelihood
```

might appear to imply three independent action-types.

Vasubandhu blocks that inference immediately:

```text
mithyājīva
    ≠ third expressive medium
```

There remain only bodily and verbal karma here. Wrong livelihood is a **subclassification by causal root and practical function**.

### 2. The defining root is greed

```text
body/speech + greed
    → wrong livelihood

body/speech + hatred or delusion
    → wrong bodily/verbal action in the ordinary classification
```

The distinction is therefore not phenomenological. The same outwardly similar bodily or verbal form can receive a different practical classification according to the root from which it proceeds.

### 3. Separate naming is pedagogical, not ontological

This is perhaps the central point of the verse.

Wrong livelihood is separately named because:

```text
lobha
    has a dragging/carrying character
        ↓
action arising from it
    is hard to guard
        ↓
livelihood becomes difficult to purify
        ↓
separate doctrinal warning is useful
```

`pṛthak kṛtaḥ` therefore does **not** mark a new dharma-substrate. It marks a practical field requiring special vigilance.

### 4. The monk's structural vulnerability

The quoted verse is important because the Bhāṣya does not explain wrong livelihood merely as private greed.

A renunciant depends materially on others:

```text
livelihood
    depends on social support
        ↓
greed can enter the relation
        ↓
body and speech become instruments
for maintaining or improving support
```

Wrong livelihood is therefore structurally connected with dependence, presentation, persuasion, and the acquisition or enjoyment of support.

### 5. The category is wider than greed for necessities

The objector proposes:

```text
wrong livelihood
    = greed-driven acquisition of food, robes,
      dwelling, medicine, etc.
```

But the sūtra prevents this narrow equivalence.

Vasubandhu's example of going to watch elephant fights shows that the category includes more than fraudulent acquisition of requisites.

The Bhāṣya gives the reason:

```text
mithyā-viṣaya-paribhoga
    improper enjoyment/use of an object-field
```

Thus livelihood is tied not only to **how support is obtained**, but also to the practical economy within which objects are sought, used, and enjoyed.

## Voice Identification

- **Vasubandhu:** `nāyam anyo 'sti` — wrong livelihood is not a distinct third action-medium.
- **Vasubandhu / explanatory voice:** greed-born bodily/verbal karma is wrong livelihood; separate naming is due to difficulty of purification.
- **Objector:** wrong livelihood should be restricted to greed for life-requisites.
- **Vasubandhu:** `na sūtrataḥ` — the restriction fails on scriptural grounds.
- **Scriptural attribution:** activities such as viewing elephant fights are included under wrong livelihood in the Śīlaskandhika teaching.

## New Technical Terms

### `mithyājīva`

- **Meaning:** wrong livelihood; greed-born bodily or verbal karma in the practical field of sustaining and enjoying life.
- **Status:** Kārikā term, now fully determined by the Bhāṣya.
- **Function:** doctrinal subclassification of bodily/verbal karma.

### `hārī`

- **Meaning:** carrying, dragging, captivating.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this unit.
- **Function:** explains why greed makes livelihood especially difficult to purify.

### `duḥśodhatva`

- **Meaning:** difficulty of purification.
- **Status:** explicit in Kārikā and expanded in Bhāṣya.
- **Function:** explains separate pedagogical treatment.

### `pariṣkāra`

- **Meaning:** requisite, material support, equipment of life.
- **Status:** Kārikā term.
- **Function:** object of the rejected narrow definition of wrong livelihood.

### `mithyāviṣayaparibhoga`

- **Meaning:** improper enjoyment/use of an object-field.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-only in this passage.
- **Function:** grounds the sūtra's wider extension of wrong livelihood beyond simple acquisition of requisites.

### `ādarotpādana`

- **Meaning:** generating careful attention, concern, or regard.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-only.
- **Function:** states the pedagogical purpose of separately naming wrong livelihood.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse should not be reduced to:

```text
wrong livelihood = bad job
```

The Abhidharma determination is finer:

```text
bodily or verbal karma
    ↓
produced through greed
    ↓
within the practical economy
of sustaining/enjoying life
    ↓
mithyājīva
```

Its separate place in the path taxonomy is **functional and corrective** rather than ontological.

This is also why the category is difficult to delimit by outward act alone. The relevant classification depends upon:

```text
medium
root
practical purpose
relation to support/enjoyment
```

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.86 gives a clean example of **cross-classification**.

An action can already belong to:

```text
bodily karma
or
verbal karma
```

and simultaneously be classified as:

```text
wrong livelihood
```

because the second classification cuts across the first according to root and practical function.

Formally:

```text
Medium(A) ∈ {Body, Speech}
Root(A) = Greed
PracticalField(A) = Livelihood / Support / Enjoyment
    ↓
WrongLivelihood(A)
```

This means:

```text
separate doctrinal category
    ≠
separate ontological substrate
```

The reason for separate indexing may instead be:

```text
difficulty of detection
difficulty of purification
importance for practice
```

The sūtra objection also introduces a hermeneutic constraint familiar from 4.75:

```text
conceptual proposal
    must not narrow
scripturally attested extension
```

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation is broadly correct, but two points should be retained in the later Super Kārikās pass:

1. `mithyājīva` is not merely activity "functioning as livelihood" in a narrow occupational sense; the Bhāṣya's sūtra argument gives it a wider field of improper support/enjoyment.
2. `pṛthak kṛtaḥ` should not suggest an independent third category of bodily/verbal action. It is separately **treated** because purification is difficult.

A Bhāṣya-informed concise rendering would be:

> Greed-born bodily or verbal karma is wrong livelihood; it is treated separately because it is difficult to purify. Nor is it restricted merely to greed for requisites, according to the sūtra.

## Light Organon Contact

The logical lesson is strong but should remain restrained.

A system of practical classification cannot rely only on the visible operation:

```text
same action-medium
    may instantiate
multiple practical classifications
```

The classification may depend on:

```text
causal ground
practical function
context of support
correction difficulty
```

And a category may deserve first-class representation not because it adds a new primitive, but because it marks a **high-risk mode of an existing primitive**.

That is exactly what wrong livelihood is here:

```text
not a new kind of action
but a difficult-to-purify mode
of bodily/verbal action under greed
```
