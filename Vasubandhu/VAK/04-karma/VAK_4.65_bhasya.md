# VAK_4.65_bhasya — Bad Conduct Is Broader Than Karma

## Kārikā Anchor

**IAST**

> *aśubhaṃ kāyakarmādi mataṃ duścaritatrayam /*  
> *akarmāpi tvabhidhyādi manoduścaritaṃ tridhā // VAK_4.65 //*

**Best working translation**

> Unwholesome bodily karma and the rest are considered the threefold bad conduct. But covetousness and the others, though not karma, are the threefold mental bad conduct.

See: [`VAK_4.65.md`](./VAK_4.65.md).

## Natural Argument

The prose begins by unpacking the first half of the Kārikā.

Unwholesome karma of body, speech, and mind is respectively:

```text
akuśala kāya-karma
    → kāya-duścarita

akuśala vāk-karma
    → vāk-duścarita

akuśala mano-karma
    → mano-duścarita
```

This is straightforward classification.

The second half introduces the important asymmetry.

There is also threefold mental bad conduct whose intrinsic nature is **not karma**:

```text
abhidhyā       covetousness
vyāpāda        hostility / ill will
mithyādṛṣṭi    false view
```

The Bhāṣya states explicitly that these are:

> *akarmasvabhāvam api*

“even though their own nature is not karma,”

and further:

> *cetanārthāntarabhūtam*

“being something distinct in meaning/nature from intention (`cetanā`).”

This is the decisive doctrinal point:

```text
mental bad conduct
    >
mental karma
```

`duścarita` is therefore not coextensive with `karma`.

## The Dārṣṭāntika Objection

**Voice: Dārṣṭāntika position**

The Dārṣṭāntikas maintain:

> *abhidhyādaya eva manaskarmeti dārṣṭāntikāḥ*

Covetousness and the others themselves are mental karma.

Their textual ground is the `Saṃcetanīya-sūtra`.

This produces an immediate problem.

## The Category-Collapse Problem

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika response / argumentative reconstruction**

If covetousness, hostility, and false view are themselves karma, then:

> *karmakleśayor aikyaṃ syāt*

there would be an identity of karma and kleśa.

The objection is then pressed:

> What would be wrong if some kleśa were also karma?

The answer is categorical:

> *naitad asti / sūtraṃ viruddhaṃ syāt /*

This cannot stand, because it would conflict with scripture.

The governing distinction is therefore preserved:

```text
karma
    ≠ kleśa
```

while still allowing certain kleśas to count as `duścarita`.

That gives us a three-level distinction:

```text
kleśa
    may function as
        duścarita

without therefore becoming
        karma
```

## Vaibhāṣika Resolution of the Sūtra

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

The Vaibhāṣikas explain the apparent scriptural identification by saying that the sūtra indicates `cetanā` through those kleśas because intention proceeds by way of them:

> *cetanāyās tanmukhena pravṛttes tais tāṃ darśayati*

The mental defilements are therefore the **modes or avenues through which intention proceeds**, not intention itself.

The structure is:

```text
abhidhyā / vyāpāda / mithyādṛṣṭi
        ↓
provide the kleśic orientation
        ↓
cetanā proceeds through them
        ↓
mental karma
```

This preserves both scriptural usage and doctrinal differentiation.

The Bhāṣya thereby distinguishes:

```text
orientation / affliction
        from
intention / karma
```

without isolating them from one another.

## Definition of Duścarita

The Bhāṣya closes with an etymological-functional definition:

> *aniṣṭaphalatvāt kuṭsitaḥ kāyavāṅmanaścāro duścaritam /*

Because it has an undesirable result, the conduct of body, speech, and mind is blameworthy (`kutsita`); hence it is called `duścarita`.

The definition is consequential:

```text
aniṣṭa-phala
    undesirable result
        ↓
kuṭsita-cāra
    blameworthy conduct
        ↓
duścarita
```

So `duścarita` is not defined merely by an external act-form. Its determination includes the character of the result toward which the conduct tends.

## Argument Reconstruction

The full movement is:

```text
akuśala karma
    ↓
three bodily/verbal/mental bad conducts

but mental bad conduct also includes
    ↓
abhidhyā / vyāpāda / mithyādṛṣṭi

these are kleśas
    ≠ karma

Dārṣṭāntika:
    these themselves are mental karma

problem:
    karma = kleśa

Vaibhāṣika resolution:
    cetanā proceeds through these kleśas
    therefore scripture can indicate cetanā by them
    without identifying them with karma
```

This is a genuine boundary-maintenance argument.

## New Technical Terms

- **cetanārthāntarabhūta** — being distinct from intention in nature or doctrinal meaning.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit formulation.
  - **function:** categorical distinction between kleśa and karma.

- **aikya** — identity, oneness.
  - **status:** operative in the objection/resolution.
  - **function:** names the unacceptable collapse of `karma` and `kleśa`.

- **tanmukhena pravṛtti** — proceeding by way of / through the face or avenue of something.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains how cetanā can be scripturally indicated through associated kleśas without being identical with them.

- **kuṭsita** — blameworthy, censurable.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definitional vocabulary.
  - **function:** etymological-functional explanation of `duścarita`.

- **aniṣṭaphalatva** — having an undesirable result.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definitional ground.
  - **function:** grounds the designation `duścarita`.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.65 prevents three categories from being flattened:

```text
karma
kleśa
duścarita
```

Their relation is overlapping but non-identical.

```text
some duścarita = karma
some duścarita = kleśa

but
karma ≠ kleśa
```

In particular:

```text
abhidhyā
vyāpāda
mithyādṛṣṭi
    = mental duścarita
    = kleśic determinations
    ≠ karma strictly speaking
```

Mental karma remains fundamentally tied to `cetanā`.

This is consistent with the opening thesis of Chapter 4:

```text
cetanā
    = mental karma
```

The Bhāṣya here protects that opening determination against a later classificatory collapse.

## Translation Consequences

The existing Kārikā translation is confirmed.

The phrase `akarma api` must be retained strongly. It does real doctrinal work and should not be softened into something like “not actions in the ordinary sense.”

Likewise, `mano-duścarita` should remain “mental bad conduct” rather than “bad mental karma,” because the Bhāṣya explicitly denies that all three members are karma.

The safest working distinction is:

```text
duścarita    bad conduct
karma         karma / action in the strict technical sense
kleśa         affliction
```

## Logical Determination

The logical form is one of extension without identity.

```text
karma
    ⊂ some instances of duścarita

kleśa
    ⊂ some instances of duścarita

karma
    ≠ kleśa
```

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes a wider practical predicate from the narrower ontological/doctrinal kinds falling under it.

More importantly, the Vaibhāṣika resolution supplies a mediated relation rather than a static exclusion:

```text
kleśa
    determines the avenue of cetanā

cetanā
    proceeds through that determination

karma
    is generated as intentional activity
```

The categories remain distinct precisely through their relation.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage is valuable because it refuses a crude identity between motive and act.

The kleśa is not simply the karma, yet karma does not arise independently of the kleśic orientation through which `cetanā` proceeds.

```text
kleśic determination
        ↓
mediates cetanā
        ↓
karma
```

This gives a more genetic account than either of two reductions:

```text
kleśa = karma
```

or

```text
kleśa | karma
```

as externally unrelated classes.

The Bhāṣya instead gives reciprocal doctrinal determination while maintaining difference.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 4.65 establishes the crucial rule:

```text
duścarita is broader than karma.
```

The three mental bad conducts `abhidhyā`, `vyāpāda`, and `mithyādṛṣṭi` are not karma in their own nature; they are distinct from `cetanā`. Yet `cetanā` can proceed through them, which explains why scriptural language may appear to identify them with mental action.

This distinction prepares directly for VAK 4.66, where Vasubandhu selects the ten **karmapathas** from the broader fields of good and bad conduct.
