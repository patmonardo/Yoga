# VAK_4.13 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
avijñaptis tridhā jñeyā saṃvarāsaṃvaretarā /
saṃvaraḥ prātimokṣākhyo dhyānajo 'nāsravastathā // VAkK_4.13 //
```

**Working translation:** Non-manifest action is to be known as threefold: restraint, non-restraint, and the other. Restraint is that called prātimokṣa, that born from meditation, and likewise the uncontaminated.

### Source cautions

The source e-text is a corrected working transcription based principally on Pradhan (1975), with corrections from later editorial work. It is not a critical edition and should not be silently normalized.

The phrase `saṃvarāsaṃvaretarā` is unpacked explicitly by the Bhāṣya as three classes: `saṃvara`, `asaṃvara`, and that which is neither. The prose therefore resolves the compact compound without requiring conjectural segmentation.

## Natural Argument

The prose resumes the previously introduced `avijñapti` and now classifies it according to practical orientation rather than material support or causal mode.

```text
avijñapti
├── saṃvara
├── asaṃvara
└── neither saṃvara nor asaṃvara
```

The third category is not a weakened form of either pole. Vasubandhu states explicitly that it is neither restraint nor non-restraint.

He then defines `saṃvara` positively:

```text
dauḥśīlya-prasara
    spread / extension of bad conduct

saṃvaraṇa / saṃrodha
    checking, blocking, containing

therefore:
saṃvara
    restraint of the spread of bad conduct
```

The definition is functional. Restraint is not merely the absence of wrongdoing, nor merely a verbal commitment. It is a continuing determination that prevents misconduct from extending into bodily or verbal activity.

The Bhāṣya then divides restraint itself into three:

```text
saṃvara
├── prātimokṣa-saṃvara
├── dhyāna-saṃvara
└── anāsrava-saṃvara
```

These are identified through three corresponding forms of `śīla`:

```text
prātimokṣa-saṃvara
    = kāmāvacara śīla
    discipline of the desire domain

dhyāna-saṃvara
    = rūpāvacara śīla
    discipline of the form domain

anāsrava-saṃvara
    = anāsrava śīla
    uncontaminated discipline
```

The natural argument closes at this point and immediately opens the next question: the internal subdivision of prātimokṣa restraint, treated in VAK 4.14.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the threefold classification of avijñapti and explicitly defines the neutral remainder as neither restraint nor non-restraint.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — defines `saṃvara` through `saṃvaraṇa` / `saṃrodha` of the spread of `dauḥśīlya`.

**Voice: Vasubandhu reporting the doctrinal classification** — maps the three forms of restraint onto desire-domain, form-domain, and uncontaminated `śīla`. No explicit school attribution occurs in this short unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **dauḥśīlya-prasara** — spread or extension of bad conduct
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** definition of what restraint blocks

- **saṃvaraṇa** — containing, checking, restraining
  - **status:** prose expansion of `saṃvara`
  - **function:** etymological-functional definition

- **saṃrodha** — blocking, checking, confinement
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** synonym used to sharpen the definition of restraint

- **kāmāvacara śīla** — desire-domain discipline
  - **status:** absent from this Kārikā's wording
  - **function:** doctrinal identification of prātimokṣa restraint

- **rūpāvacara śīla** — form-domain discipline
  - **status:** absent from this Kārikā's wording
  - **function:** doctrinal identification of meditation-born restraint

- **anāsrava śīla** — uncontaminated discipline
  - **status:** absent from this Kārikā's wording
  - **function:** doctrinal identification of uncontaminated restraint

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya confirms that `asaṃvara` is not simply logical negation of restraint, because a third field remains:

```text
saṃvara
    positive restraint

asaṃvara
    positive non-restraint

itarā
    neither
```

Thus the opposition is not exhaustive.

More importantly, `saṃvara` is defined dynamically rather than statically. Its object is the `prasara`, the outward spread or extension, of `dauḥśīlya`. Restraint therefore functions as a blocking determination within an action-continuum.

This also clarifies why the classification belongs under `avijñapti`: the relevant ethical determination can remain operative even when no manifest act of abstention is presently occurring.

The three restraints are not merely three institutional labels. They are three modes of ethical discipline distinguished by their domain and mode of arising:

```text
formal prātimokṣa discipline
    → desire-domain restraint

meditative attainment
    → form-domain restraint

uncontaminated Path
    → uncontaminated restraint
```

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation remains basically sound, but two points should be sharpened.

First, `itarā` should be understood explicitly as:

> that which is neither restraint nor non-restraint

rather than as an undefined neutral remainder.

Second, `saṃvara` should retain the strong sense of active containment or checking. “Restraint” is adequate, provided it is not heard as mere passive abstention.

A refined philosophical translation is therefore:

> Non-manifest action is threefold: restraint, non-restraint, and that which is neither. Restraint itself is threefold: prātimokṣa restraint, meditation-born restraint, and uncontaminated restraint.

## Logical Determination

The verse establishes a practical triad that is not reducible to simple opposition:

```text
positive determination
    saṃvara

contrary positive determination
    asaṃvara

uncommitted remainder
    neither
```

Within the positive determination of restraint, a second threefold articulation appears according to the mode under which discipline is constituted:

```text
institutional / vow-based
    prātimokṣa

meditative
    dhyānaja

Path-born / uncontaminated
    anāsrava
```

The important logical point is that stable ethical determination is being classified by **how it is generated and what field it belongs to**, not merely by the visible deed that happens to accompany it.

## Organon Contact Point

The light contact point is now clearer than in the Kārikā pass:

```text
manifest abstention
    is an event

saṃvara
    is a continuing inhibitory determination
    against the spread of misconduct
```

That is enough for the present pass. Whether such restraint is best reconstructed as disposition, rule, practical ground, or another logical form should remain deferred until the full Karma chapter and Path material are available.
