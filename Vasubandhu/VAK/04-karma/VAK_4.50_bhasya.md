# VAK_4.50_bhasya — Determinate and Indeterminate Karma

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.50**

```iast
niyatāniyataṃ tac ca niyataṃ trividhaṃ punaḥ |
dṛṣṭadharmādi-vedyatvāt pañcadhā karma kecana ||
```

> That karma is determinate or indeterminate. Determinate karma is again threefold, according to whether it is to be experienced in the present life, the next life, or a later life. Some hold karma to be fivefold.

See: [VAK_4.50.md](./VAK_4.50.md)

## Source Cautions

The key Bhāṣya gloss is `nāvaśyavedanīyam aniyatam`: indeterminate karma is **not necessarily to be experienced**. This is stronger than simply saying that its time of maturation is unknown or unfixed.

The fivefold alternative is produced by dividing `aniyatavedanīya` into two according to `vipāka`: one form in which maturation is determinate and another in which maturation itself is indeterminate. The precise scholastic terminology should be preserved rather than assimilated too quickly to ordinary modal language.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.45–4.49 established the qualitative forms of karmic maturation and the five senses in which something can be called `vedanīya`. VAK 4.50 now adds a modal-temporal determination.

The three kinds of karma previously called pleasant-, painful-, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant-to-be-experienced may each be either:

```text
niyata
    determinate

aniyata
    indeterminate
```

But Vasubandhu immediately defines the latter negatively:

```text
aniyata
    = na avaśya vedanīya
    = not necessarily to be experienced
```

Thus the distinction is not merely:

```text
known time / unknown time
```

but more fundamentally:

```text
maturation is necessary
    /
maturation is not necessary
```

Determinate karma is then divided according to the life in which its maturation occurs:

```text
dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya
    produced and matured in this very life

upapadyavedanīya
    matured in the second life, the immediately following rebirth

aparaparyāyavedanīya
    matured after that, in a later life
```

Together with `aniyatavedanīya`, this yields four classes.

Some authorities instead count five. They divide indeterminate karma itself into two:

```text
aniyatavedanīya
    vipākena niyata
        maturation determinate

    vipākena aniyata
        maturation indeterminate
```

The Bhāṣya then records a further controversy concerning `dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya`. Some hold that karma bearing this name can still mature in another life, the name being assigned from the circumstance of its initial undertaking (`ārambhavaśāt`). The Vaibhāṣikas reject this extension.

Their counter-model is botanical: some seeds characteristically bear fruit near in time, others only at a distance. Likewise karmas may possess intrinsically different temporal ranges of fruition.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory framing and report of positions.

**Voice: some authorities (`apare`)** — fivefold classification and the proposal that `dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya` may mature in another life.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — rejection of the latter proposal, supported by the analogy of seeds with near and remote fruiting times.

## New Technical Terms

- **niyata** — determinate, fixed
  - **status:** important karmic modal term
  - **function:** marks karma whose maturation is necessarily to be experienced in a determinate temporal class

- **aniyata** — indeterminate
  - **status:** important karmic modal term
  - **function:** glossed as `nāvaśyavedanīya`, not necessarily to be experienced

- **avaśya-vedanīya** — necessarily to be experienced
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit modal gloss
  - **function:** reveals that the niyata/aniyata distinction concerns necessity of fruition, not merely temporal uncertainty

- **dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya** — to be experienced in the present life
  - **function:** first temporal form of determinate karma

- **upapadyavedanīya** — to be experienced in the immediately following rebirth
  - **function:** second temporal form of determinate karma

- **aparaparyāyavedanīya** — to be experienced in a later life
  - **function:** third temporal form of determinate karma

- **ārambhavaśāt** — by reason of the undertaking or initiation
  - **status:** introduced in an alternative interpretation
  - **function:** proposed basis for naming present-life karma even when its result occurs later; rejected by the Vaibhāṣikas

- **saṃnikṛṣṭaphala / viprakṛṣṭaphala** — near-fruiting / remote-fruiting
  - **function:** Vaibhāṣika distinction used to defend real temporal differentiation among karmas

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya requires a correction to the first-pass formulation.

The primary opposition is:

```text
niyata
    fruition necessarily occurs

aniyata
    fruition does not necessarily occur
```

Only after necessity is established does determinate karma receive a temporal index:

```text
NIYATA KARMA
    ↓
when must it mature?

present life
next life
later life
```

Therefore:

```text
necessity of fruition
    ≠
time of fruition
```

They are distinct determinations.

The standard fourfold scheme is:

```text
1. dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya
2. upapadyavedanīya
3. aparaparyāyavedanīya
4. aniyatavedanīya
```

The alternative fivefold scheme refines the fourth member by asking whether the maturation (`vipāka`) of indeterminate karma is itself determinate or indeterminate.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation remains basically sound, but `aniyata` should not be explained merely as "unfixed maturation." The Bhāṣya forces a stronger formulation:

> **That karma is determinate or indeterminate; indeterminate karma is that whose experience of maturation is not necessary. Determinate karma is again threefold: to be experienced in this life, in the immediately following rebirth, or in a later life. Some authorities count five forms by dividing indeterminate karma according to whether its maturation is itself determinate or indeterminate.**

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.50 introduces modality into the karmic relation.

```text
KARMA
    ↓
FRUITION-MODALITY
    necessary / not-necessary
```

For necessary fruition:

```text
necessary fruition
    ↓
TEMPORAL INDEX
    present / next / later
```

This produces a two-stage determination:

```text
Does fruition have to occur?
        ↓
If yes, when must it occur?
```

That is logically stronger than treating `niyata` as merely a timestamp.

The Vaibhāṣika seed analogy adds another principle:

```text
different causal formations
    may possess
intrinsically different temporal ranges of fruition
```

Near-fruiting and remote-fruiting are therefore not merely accidental delays imposed from outside; temporal efficacy belongs to the differentiated causal structure itself.

## Organon Contact Point

This verse adds a genuine modal dimension to the rational-empirical field of karma:

```text
causal determination
    ≠
necessary actualization
```

A cause may be karmically effective without its fruit being necessary in the same sense as determinate karma. Where fruition is necessary, its temporal position may itself be further determined.

The restrained upgrade is therefore:

```text
karma carries
    qualitative determination
    experiential determination
    modal determination
    temporal determination
```

These must remain distinct dimensions of one causal field.

## Commit Note

Bhāṣya pass for VAK 4.50. Corrects the first-pass tendency to read `aniyata` as merely temporally unfixed by preserving Vasubandhu's gloss `nāvaśyavedanīya`: indeterminate karma is not necessarily to be experienced. Distinguishes necessity of fruition from temporal placement, records the fourfold and alternative fivefold classifications, and preserves the Vaibhāṣika near-fruit/remote-fruit seed analogy.
