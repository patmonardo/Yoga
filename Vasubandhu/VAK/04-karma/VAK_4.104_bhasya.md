# VAK 4.104 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `buddhe na tāḍanecchasya prahārān nordhvam arhati | nānantaryaprayuktasya vairāgyaphalasaṃbhavaḥ || 4.104 ||`

**Best current translation:** With respect to a Buddha, merely wishing to strike does not constitute the ānantarya; nor does an ānantarya arise when one strikes a non-arhat who only afterward becomes an arhat. For one engaged in the preparation for an ānantarya and not turned back from it, there is no possibility of dispassion or a Path-fruit.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.104.md](./VAK_4.104.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
kimavaśyaṃ tathāgatasyāsti kuṭuṣṭacittarudhirotpādanādānantaryam /
vadhābhiprāyasya syāt /

buddhe na tāḍanecchasya

yadi tāḍanābhiprāya utpādayenna syāt /
yadyanarhati praharetsa ca prahārādūrdhvamarhan syāt

prahārānnordhvamarhati /

syādānantaryamiti vartate /
nahi tena tatra prayogaḥ kṛtaḥ /

kimānantarye prayogaṃ kṛtvā tasminnavyāvartite vairāgyaṃ phalaṃ vā na prāpnuyāt /

nānantaryaprayuktasya vairāgyaphalasaṃbhavaḥ // VAkK_4.104 //

anyakarmaphalapathaprayogaṃ tu kṛtvāryamārgotpattau na punaḥ karmapathotpattirāśrayasyātyantaṃ tadviruddhatvāt /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya addresses three tightly connected problems:

1. Is causing a Buddha's blood to flow always an ānantarya?
2. Does striking someone who later becomes an arhat count as killing an arhat?
3. Can a person who has already undertaken the preparation for an ānantarya attain dispassion or a noble fruit before the act reaches completion?

All three questions test the same principle: the technical karmic determination depends not upon the external result alone but upon the intentional preparation directed toward a qualified field.

## Literal Prose Translation

> Must there necessarily be an ānantarya whenever blood is caused to arise in a Tathāgata with a hostile mind? It would be so for one whose intention is to kill. But not in the case of one whose intention regarding the Buddha is merely to strike him. If he causes blood to arise with the intention of striking, there would be no ānantarya.
>
> And if one strikes a person who is not an arhat, and that person becomes an arhat only after the blow, would there be an ānantarya if he subsequently dies from that blow? No. For the assailant did not undertake the preparation with respect to an arhat.
>
> Suppose one has undertaken the preparation for an ānantarya and has not turned back from it: could one then attain dispassion or a fruit? No. For one engaged in an ānantarya there is no possibility of dispassion or fruit.
>
> In the case of preparation for some other karmapatha, however, if the Noble Path arises, that karmapatha can no longer arise, because the transformed basis is utterly opposed to it.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Physical injury is not sufficient

The first question concerns the ānantarya traditionally described as maliciously causing a Buddha's blood to flow.

The Bhāṣya refuses to classify the act from the bodily result alone:

```text
blood is caused to flow
    ≠ by itself
ānantarya
```

The decisive distinction is intentional:

```text
vadhābhiprāya
    intention to kill
    → ānantarya may be constituted

tāḍanābhiprāya
    intention merely to strike
    → no ānantarya of this kind
```

Thus the qualified field and the agent's projected end must correspond.

### 2. The arhat must be the field of the preparation

The second case tests the same rule temporally.

Suppose:

```text
t₁: victim is not an arhat
    assailant strikes victim

t₂: victim becomes an arhat

t₃: victim dies from the earlier wound
```

The physical blow contributes to the death of an arhat, but the act is not technically the ānantarya of killing an arhat.

Why?

```text
nahi tena tatra prayogaḥ kṛtaḥ
he did not undertake the preparation toward that [arhat]
```

The relevant karmic object is therefore not retroactively redefined by a later change in the victim's status.

### 3. Preparation can itself close Path-possibility

The final question concerns `prayoga`, preparation or operative engagement.

If preparation for an ānantarya has been undertaken and remains `avyāvartita`, not reversed, then:

```text
ānantarya-prayoga
    ↓
no vairāgya
no noble fruit
```

This is stronger than saying that the completed act produces a future painful maturation. The karmic trajectory, once deliberately entered and not abandoned, is already incompatible with the emergence of the liberating transformation.

### 4. Why ordinary karmapatha preparations differ

Vasubandhu immediately contrasts other karmapathas.

One may undertake preparation for another karmapatha and then generate the Noble Path before completion. In that case:

```text
Noble Path arises
    ↓
āśraya becomes utterly opposed to that karmapatha
    ↓
its completion can no longer arise
```

Thus the ānantarya case is exceptional precisely because its unreversed preparation itself blocks the attainment that could otherwise interrupt an unwholesome causal sequence.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Objector / questioner** — asks whether causing a Buddha's blood to flow necessarily constitutes ānantarya.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — distinguishes intention to kill from intention merely to strike.
- **Voice: Objector / questioner** — raises the case of a victim becoming an arhat only after being struck.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — denies ānantarya because no preparation was undertaken toward an arhat.
- **Voice: Objector / questioner** — asks whether dispassion or fruit can arise after unreversed preparation for ānantarya.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — denies that possibility and contrasts ordinary karmapatha preparations.

## New Technical Terms

- **vadhābhiprāya** — intention to kill.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification in this passage.
  - **function:** specifies the intention required for the Buddha-bloodshed ānantarya.

- **tāḍanābhiprāya** — intention merely to strike or injure.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification.
  - **function:** exclusion condition; bodily injury under this intention does not constitute the relevant ānantarya.

- **prayoga** — preparation, undertaking, operative engagement toward completion of a karma.
  - **status:** previously encountered but doctrinally sharpened here.
  - **function:** links intention to the qualified object and projected completion.

- **avyāvartita** — not turned back, unreversed.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** condition under which ānantarya preparation blocks dispassion and fruit.

- **āśraya** — basis or continuum as transformed by the Noble Path.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** explains why ordinary karmapatha completion becomes impossible after Path-arising.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya establishes that technical ānantarya classification is neither result-only nor intention-only.

It requires a determinate relation among:

```text
agent
intention
qualified field
preparation
completion
```

The first two cases show that later facts do not retroactively alter the karmic object intended at the moment of preparation.

Accordingly:

```text
injury to Buddha + strike-intention
    ≠ Buddha-bloodshed ānantarya

death of later-arhat from earlier blow
    ≠ arhat-killing ānantarya
```

The decisive question is what act was intentionally undertaken against what field.

## Logical Determination

Let:

```text
I = intention
F = qualified karmic field
P = preparation directed toward F
R = eventual physical result
K = technical karmic classification
```

Then the Bhāṣya rejects:

```text
R alone → K
```

and instead requires something like:

```text
I + F + P + appropriate completion → K
```

A later transformation of the field does not rewrite the original preparation:

```text
F(t₁) ≠ F(t₂)
P directed toward F(t₁)

therefore
P is not retroactively directed toward F(t₂)
```

The final section adds a practical incompatibility relation:

```text
UnreversedAnantaryaPreparation(x)
    → excludes PathFruit(x)
```

while for ordinary karmapathas:

```text
NoblePathArises(x)
    → transformed basis incompatible with completion of that karmapatha
```

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation:

> "With respect to a Buddha, the act of one wishing to strike him cannot proceed beyond a blow."

is not supported by the Bhāṣya and should not be retained.

The first half is better understood as two exclusions:

> **With respect to a Buddha, causing blood to flow does not constitute the ānantarya for one whose intention was merely to strike; nor does it become arhat-killing when the victim becomes an arhat only after the blow.**

The second half is likewise more precise when `prayukta` is understood through the Bhāṣya as operative preparation that has not been reversed:

> **For one engaged in unreversed preparation for an ānantarya, dispassion and noble fruit cannot arise.**

## Light Organon Contact

The passage supplies a remarkably exact model of practical determination:

```text
projected end
    +
qualified object
    +
operative preparation
    →
ethical identity of the act
```

Neither the mere external event nor a later reinterpretation of its object is sufficient.

The final contrast is especially important for Practical Reason. In an ordinary unwholesome trajectory, the emergence of the Path transforms the `āśraya` so radically that completion of the incompatible karmapatha becomes impossible. But unreversed ānantarya preparation is itself specified as preventing that liberating transformation from arising.

Thus 4.104 defines two opposed practical trajectories:

```text
ordinary unwholesome preparation
    → can be interrupted by Path-transformation

unreversed ānantarya preparation
    → blocks Path-transformation itself
```
