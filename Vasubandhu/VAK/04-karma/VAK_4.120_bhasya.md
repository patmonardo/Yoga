## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya begins from a technical distinction:

```text
kṛta
    karma that has been performed

upacita
    karma that has been accumulated / causally consolidated
```

It then states explicitly that karma becomes `upacita` through **five causes (`pañcabhiḥ karaṇaiḥ`)**:

1. `saṃcetanā` — deliberate performance;
2. `samāpti` — completion of the karmically sufficient set;
3. `niṣkaukṛtya-vipakṣa` — absence of remorse and absence of a counteracting factor;
4. `parivāra` — accompaniment by factors of the same ethical species;
5. `vipāka` — determination toward maturation.

The Bhāṣya then explains each in turn and concludes that the same structure applies to wholesome karma.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

Karma is spoken of as both performed and accumulated. How does karma become accumulated? Through five causes.

Through deliberate intention and completion, through being without remorse and without an opposing factor, through accompaniment, and through maturation, karma is called accumulated.

How through deliberate intention? It is performed after having been thought out; it is not done without prior awareness and not done suddenly or impulsively.

How through completion? One person goes to the bad destinies through one misconduct, another through as many as three. One does so through one karmapatha, another through as many as ten. In each case, so long as that through which the person reaches the result has not been completed, the karma is performed but not accumulated; when it has been completed, it is accumulated.

How through absence of remorse and absence of an opposing factor? That karma is without regret and without a counteragent.

How through accompaniment? An unwholesome act is accompanied by an unwholesome surrounding complex.

How through maturation? It is fixed to give maturation.

The same is to be applied to wholesome karma. Otherwise, karma is performed but not accumulated.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Performed does not entail accumulated

The opening distinction is decisive:

```text
kṛta(k)
    does not entail
upacita(k)
```

An action can genuinely have occurred and yet fail to acquire the full causal consolidation designated by `upacita`.

`Upacita` therefore does not merely repeat the fact that an action happened. It marks a stronger status of karmic establishment.

### 2. `Saṃcetanā`: deliberate adoption

Vasubandhu glosses `saṃcetanā` by contrasting it with two forms of deficient agency:

```text
saṃcintya kṛta
    done after reflection / deliberately

na abuddhipūrva
    not done without prior awareness

na sahasā kṛta
    not done suddenly or rashly
```

The relevant act has been cognitively and volitionally adopted before execution.

Thus:

```text
mere occurrence
    ≠
deliberately appropriated deed
```

### 3. `Samāpti`: completion of the sufficient karmic configuration

The Bhāṣya does not define `samāpti` as simply “the external deed has reached its endpoint.” Instead, it asks how much misconduct is sufficient for the relevant result.

For some agents:

```text
one duścarita
    is sufficient
```

For others:

```text
up to three duścaritas
```

Likewise:

```text
one karmapatha
    may suffice

or

up to all ten karmapathas
```

The criterion is therefore relative to the causal threshold:

```text
requisite set incomplete
    → kṛta but not upacita

requisite set complete
    → upacita
```

This is much more precise than treating `samāpti` as merely objective completion of one isolated deed.

### 4. `Niṣkaukṛtya-vipakṣa`: no remorse and no antidote

The Kārikā compresses what the Bhāṣya unfolds as a paired condition:

```text
nirvipratisāra
    without remorse / regret

niṣpratipakṣa
    without an opposing counteragent
```

These are not two separately counted causes. The commentary has already said there are five causes, so this is one combined condition.

The deed remains unretracted from within and unopposed by a contrary practical force.

### 5. `Parivāra`: ethical reinforcement by accompaniment

For an unwholesome act:

```text
akuśala karma
    +
akuśala parivāra
```

The principal karma is surrounded by factors of the same ethical species.

`Parivāra` is therefore not merely any contextual accompaniment. It is a reinforcing environment congruent with the principal act.

The same logic is to be applied positively to wholesome karma.

### 6. `Vipāka`: fixed maturation

The final condition is stated with unusual precision:

```text
vipākadāna-niyata
    fixed / determined to give maturation
```

This is stronger than saying merely that the act is capable of maturation.

Accumulated karma has reached a condition in which its maturation is determined.

## New Technical Terms

- **kṛta** — performed, actually done.
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptually, here technically opposed to `upacita`.
  - **function:** classification of karma by occurrence.

- **upacita** — accumulated, causally consolidated.
  - **status:** important technical distinction in this passage.
  - **function:** classification of karma by established causal efficacy.

- **saṃcetanā** — deliberate, reflected intention preceding action.
  - **status:** related to prior `cetanā`, but more specifically determined here.
  - **function:** first condition of accumulation.

- **samāpti** — completion of whatever karmic configuration is sufficient for the relevant result.
  - **status:** refined by Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** second condition of accumulation.

- **nirvipratisāra** — absence of remorse or regret.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification.
  - **function:** one side of the third condition.

- **pratipakṣa** — counteragent, opposing or antidotal factor.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinally.
  - **function:** practical opposition capable of preventing accumulation.

- **parivāra** — surrounding factors of the same ethical quality as the principal act.
  - **status:** refined here.
  - **function:** reinforcement of karmic accumulation.

- **vipākadāna-niyata** — fixed to give maturation.
  - **status:** strong Bhāṣya formulation.
  - **function:** fifth condition of accumulation.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a strict distinction:

```text
performed karma
    = historical occurrence

accumulated karma
    = historically performed karma
      that has crossed a threshold of causal consolidation
```

The five determinants can be represented as:

```text
Upacita(k) requires:

1. deliberate adoption
2. sufficient completion
3. no remorse / no counteragent
4. congruent reinforcement
5. fixed maturation
```

The central insight is that karmic efficacy is **threshold-sensitive**.

A deed may already exist as a fact while not yet possessing the full determination required to structure a definite maturation.

This also prevents a simplistic equation:

```text
action occurred
    therefore
full karmic accumulation occurred
```

That inference is explicitly rejected.

---

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation should be tightened in three places.

First, `niṣkaukṛtyavipakṣataḥ` should not be split into two independently counted conditions. The Bhāṣya states five causes and glosses the expression jointly as:

> without remorse and without a counteracting factor.

Second, `samāpti` should not be rendered simply as “objective accomplishment.” It is completion of the **requisite karmic set** sufficient for the result in that agent's case.

Third, `vipākāt` is stronger than a generic relation to maturation: the act is `vipākadāna-niyata`, **fixed to give maturation**.

A better translation is:

> Karma is called accumulated through deliberate intention, through completion of the requisite karmic configuration, through absence of remorse and of a counteragent, through congruent accompaniment, and through being fixed to give maturation.

## Light Organon Contact

This passage distinguishes **fact from causal incorporation**.

```text
deed as occurrence
    → kṛta

deed as integrated determination
of the continuing causal system
    → upacita
```

The distinction is especially useful because the Kośa does not treat causal efficacy as automatically attached to every event in equal degree. The act must become integrated through deliberation, completion, lack of reversal, reinforcement, and determinate futurity.

The best light formulation is:

```text
occurrence
    ≠
incorporation into the causal architecture
```

`Upacita` names the second.
