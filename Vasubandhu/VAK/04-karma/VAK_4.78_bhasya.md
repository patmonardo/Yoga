# VAK 4.78 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**VAK 4.78**

`vyāpādaḥ sattva-vidveṣaḥ nāsti-dṛṣṭiḥ śubhāśubhe /`
`mithyādṛṣṭiḥ trayo hy atra panthānaḥ sapta karma ca // 4.78 //`

Working translation:

> Ill will is hostility toward living beings. The view that wholesome and unwholesome [karma] do not exist is false view. Here, three are paths [of karma], while seven are both karma and [paths of karma].

Kārikā source: [VAK_4.78.md](./VAK_4.78.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

`vyāpādaḥ sattvavidveṣaḥ`

`sattveṣu vidveṣo vyāpādaḥ parapīḍākārapravṛttaḥ /`

`nāstidṛṣṭiḥ śubhāśubhe / mithyādṛṣṭiḥ`

`śubhe cāśubhe ca karmaṇi yā nāstīti dṛṣṭiḥ sā mithyādṛṣṭiḥ /`

`tadyathā "nāsti dattaṃ nāstīṣṭaṃ nāsti hutaṃ nāsti sucaritaṃ nāsti duścaritam" ...`

`saiṣā sākalyena karmaphalāryāpavādikā mithyādṛṣṭirbhavati /`

`ādimātraṃ tu śloke darśitam / evaṃlakṣaṇā ete daśa karmapathāḥ /`

`karmapathā iti ko'rthaḥ /`

`trayo hyatra panthānaḥ sapta karma ca //`

`abhidhyādayo hi trayaḥ karmaṇaḥ panthāna iti karmapathāḥ /`

`tatsaṃprayogiṇī hi cetanā teṣāṃ vāhena vahati teṣāṃ gatyā gacchati / tadvaśena tathābhisaṃskaraṇāt /`

`sapta tu prāṇātipātādayaḥ karma ca kāyavākkarmatvāt karmaṇaśca panthāna iti karmapathāḥ ...`

## Literal Prose Translation

Ill will is hostility toward living beings, proceeding in the form of harming another.

False view is the view, with regard to wholesome and unwholesome karma, that it does not exist. Examples are: “There is no giving, no offering, no sacrifice; there is no good conduct and no bad conduct,” and so forth, extending to denial of Arhats in the world. Taken comprehensively, this false view repudiates karma, its result, and the noble ones. The verse shows only the beginning of this range.

These ten are the karmapathas characterized in this way.

What does “karmapatha” mean? Here three are paths, while seven are both karma and paths.

Covetousness, ill will, and false view are called karmapathas because they are paths of karma. The volition associated with them moves under their conveyance, goes according to their course, and is formed accordingly under their influence.

The seven beginning with killing are themselves karma, since they are bodily or verbal karma, and they are also paths of karma because the volition that gives rise to them proceeds by taking them as its basis.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. `vyāpāda` is more determinate than `dveṣa`

The Bhāṣya does not define `vyāpāda` as hatred in general. It is:

```text
dveṣa
    general unwholesome root

vyāpāda
    hostility toward a living being
    proceeding in the form of harming another
```

Thus `parapīḍākāra-pravṛtta` is decisive. Ill will is hatred specified as a directed harmful orientation toward another being.

### 2. `mithyādṛṣṭi` is denial of the moral-causal order

The Kārikā gives only the opening example: the view that wholesome and unwholesome karma do not exist. The Bhāṣya expands this into a broader denial:

```text
no giving
no offering
no sacrifice
no good conduct
no bad conduct
...
no Arhats
```

Vasubandhu summarizes this as a false view that repudiates:

```text
karma
karma-phala
ārya
```

The verse therefore gives only `ādimātra`, the beginning or first sample of the full class.

### 3. Why are the ten called `karmapatha`?

The answer is asymmetrical.

#### Three mental karmapathas

```text
abhidhyā
vyāpāda
mithyādṛṣṭi
```

These are not karma in the strict Vaibhāṣika sense established earlier. They are **paths of karma**:

```text
mental karmapatha
    ↓ governs / channels
cetanā
    ↓
karma proceeds according to that path
```

The text says the associated volition is carried by their conveyance and goes by their course because it is formed under their influence.

#### Seven bodily/verbal karmapathas

```text
3 bodily
4 verbal
```

These are:

```text
karma
    because they are bodily/verbal karma

and

path of karma
    because the originating cetanā proceeds upon them / through them
```

So:

```text
3 mental courses
    = path of karma

7 bodily/verbal courses
    = karma + path of karma
```

This is the central doctrinal determination of the verse.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu explaining the Vaibhāṣika karmapatha analysis.

The definitions of `vyāpāda`, `mithyādṛṣṭi`, and the primary explanation of `karmapatha` are presented as the operative doctrinal account.

Later in the same natural unit Vasubandhu mentions the Dārṣṭāntika view that the three mental courses themselves are mental karma; that alternative generates a different etymological explanation of `karmapatha`, but the main 4.78 structure remains the Vaibhāṣika asymmetry.

## New Technical Terms

- **parapīḍākāra** — the form or orientation of harming another.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific specification of `vyāpāda`.
  - **function:** distinguishes directed ill will from hatred in general.

- **karmaphalāryāpavāda** — repudiation of karma, karmic result, and the noble ones.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion beyond the concise Kārikā example.
  - **function:** defines the full doctrinal extension of false view.

- **ādimātra** — merely the beginning / an initial sample.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya metacomment on the Kārikā's compression.
  - **function:** warns that the verse gives only one representative form of false view.

- **vāha** — conveyance, carrying, channel.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory metaphor.
  - **function:** explains how the associated volition is conducted by the three mental courses.

## Doctrinal Determination

The preceding verses have now fully differentiated:

```text
root
    lobha / dveṣa / moha

specific mental karmapatha
    abhidhyā / vyāpāda / mithyādṛṣṭi

cetanā
    karma in the strict mental sense
```

VAK 4.78 explains their relation rather than identifying them.

For the three mental courses:

```text
karmapatha
    is not itself karma

karmapatha
    directs the volition that is karma
```

This answers the problem raised already at 4.65:

```text
kleśa-like mental determination
    ≠
cetanā
```

but it can still structure the path along which `cetanā` becomes karmically determinate.

For the seven bodily/verbal courses, the relation is reflexive:

```text
course is itself karma
and
course is the path of the karma that gives rise to it
```

## Logical Determination

The verse gives `path` a genuinely genetic meaning.

A path is not merely a static class label. It is that through which another determination proceeds.

```text
mental course
    ↓
channels cetanā
    ↓
karma
```

This is especially clear in:

`tatsaṃprayogiṇī hi cetanā teṣāṃ vāhena vahati teṣāṃ gatyā gacchati`

The volition does not merely coexist with the mental karmapatha; it moves according to its direction.

Thus:

```text
karmapatha
    = determinate route of karmic formation
```

For the bodily/verbal seven the completed act becomes both product and pathway:

```text
cetanā
    ↓ produces
bodily/verbal karma
    ↓ serves as
karmic path
```

The term therefore contains an internally differentiated relation, not an accidental compound of two nouns.

## Light Organon Contact

The most important conceptual structure here is **determination as channeling**.

```text
root
    supplies general unwholesome ground

mental karmapatha
    specifies practical orientation

cetanā
    moves according to that orientation

bodily/verbal karmapatha
    externally realizes the process
```

This is much stronger than an analogy between categories. The Bhāṣya itself supplies a genetic relation:

```text
orientation
    → formation of volition
    → realized course
```

The three mental karmapathas therefore function as determinate practical forms through which karma proceeds, while the seven external courses are simultaneously realized karma and the determinate route of karmic activity.

## Translation Consequences

1. `vyāpāda` should be translated as **ill will** or **hostile intention**, not simply as the general root “hatred.”
2. `mithyādṛṣṭi` here is stronger than ordinary factual error: it is **false view denying the moral-causal order**.
3. `karmapatha` should retain the dynamic force of **course/path of karma**, because the Bhāṣya explicitly explains it through movement, conveyance, and direction.
4. The Kārikā's `trayo ... panthānaḥ sapta karma ca` must preserve the asymmetry:
   - three = paths of karma;
   - seven = karma and paths of karma.

## Carry-Forward

VAK 4.78 closes the definitional sequence of the ten unwholesome karmapathas and explains their name. It also establishes the immediate basis for the next topic: how these karmapathas oppose wholesome action and, beginning with 4.79, how false view can sever the wholesome roots.
