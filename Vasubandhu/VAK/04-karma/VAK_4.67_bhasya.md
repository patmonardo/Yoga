# VAK_4.67_bhasya — Vijñapti and Avijñapti in the Seven Bodily-Verbal Karmapathas

## Kārikā Anchor

**IAST**

> *aśubhāḥ ṣaḍ avijñaptir dvidhaikaḥ te ’pi kurvataḥ /*  
> *dvividhāḥ sapta kuśalāḥ avijñaptiḥ samādhijāḥ // 4.67 //*

Best working translation:

> Of the unwholesome bodily-verbal courses, six are necessarily avijñapti; one is twofold. Those six also become twofold when one performs them oneself. The seven wholesome bodily-verbal courses are twofold, while those born from samādhi are avijñapti alone.

See: `VAK_4.67.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya first resolves the compact reference of the numeral six:

> *akuśalāḥ ṣaṭ karmapathā avaśyam avijñaptisvabhāvāḥ prāṇātipātādattādānamṛṣāvādapaiśunyapāruṣyasaṃbhinnapralāpāḥ /*

These six are:

```text
prāṇātipāta        killing
adattādāna         taking what is not given
mṛṣāvāda           false speech
paiśunya           divisive speech
pāruṣya            harsh speech
saṃbhinnapralāpa   disordered / idle speech
```

They are necessarily avijñapti because:

> *pareṇa kārayato maulavijñaptyabhāvāt /*

When one causes another to perform them, the instigator has no principal manifest action (`maula-vijñapti`) of his own.

The one exceptional bodily-verbal bad course is sexual misconduct:

> *kāmamithyācāro nityaṃ vijñaptyavijñaptisvabhāvas tasya svayaiva niṣṭhāpanāt /*

It is always both vijñapti and avijñapti because its completion is necessarily by oneself.

The Bhāṣya adds:

> *na hi taṃ pareṇa kārayatas tādṛśī prītir bhavatīti /*

The relevant completion cannot be obtained merely by causing another to act.

The Kārikā then says `te 'pi kurvataḥ`:

> *te 'pi ṣaṭ karmapathāḥ svayaṃ kurvato dvividhā bhavanti vijñaptir avijñaptiś ca tatkālamaraṇe paścānmaraṇe tv avijñaptir eva /*

Thus those same six, when performed personally, can be both vijñapti and avijñapti. In the paradigmatic case of killing, if death coincides with the manifest act, both are present at principal completion; if death occurs only later, the completed course is avijñapti alone.

On the wholesome side:

> *kuśalāḥ sapta rūpiṇaḥ karmapathā avaśyaṃ dviprakārā vijñaptir avijñaptiś ca /*

The seven wholesome bodily-verbal courses are necessarily twofold, because undertaken discipline depends upon manifest enactment:

> *vijñaptyadhīnatvāt samādānaśīlasya /*

But the samādhi-born courses differ:

> *dhyānānāsravasaṃvarasaṃgṛhītāḥ samādhijā ucyante /*

and:

> *te hy avijñaptir eva cittamātrādhīnatvāt /*

Those comprised by dhyāna-restraint and anāsrava-restraint are avijñapti alone because they depend only upon mind (`cittamātra`).

## Source Cautions

The e-text is based on Pradhan/Shastri and is not treated as a critical edition. The doctrinal structure of the passage is clear. The phrase concerning death at the time of action versus later death should be read together with the subsequent discussion of `prayoga`, `maula`, and `pṛṣṭha`; VAK 4.68 and following will refine the temporal structure of completion.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.66 selected ten karmapathas from the broader field of conduct. VAK 4.67 now asks a different question:

```text
not:
    which karmapatha is this?

but:
    in what mode does the bodily-verbal karmapatha exist?
```

The governing distinction is:

```text
vijñapti
    manifest bodily or verbal action

avijñapti
    non-manifest karmic determination
```

### Six unwholesome courses

The six listed bodily-verbal bad courses are necessarily capable of existing as avijñapti because they may be completed through an agent acting on one's instruction.

```text
instigator
    intention / command
        ↓
other agent performs act
        ↓
principal course completed

instigator:
    no maula-vijñapti of his own
    but avijñapti is present
```

This shows that karmic completion is not reducible to visible bodily motion performed by the karmically responsible continuum.

### The exceptional seventh — sexual misconduct

Sexual misconduct differs because the principal completion cannot be delegated in the relevant sense.

```text
sexual misconduct
    completion necessarily personal
        ↓
manifest performance present
        +
non-manifest karmic determination
```

Therefore it is always twofold.

### Personal performance of the six

When one performs the other six oneself, they too may contain both modes.

The killing example makes the temporal issue explicit:

```text
manifest blow
    + death at that time
        → vijñapti + avijñapti at completion

manifest blow
    + death later
        → completion occurs after manifest act has ceased
        → avijñapti alone at that principal completion
```

This is an important preparation for the next verse's distinction among preparatory, principal, and subsequent phases.

### Seven wholesome bodily-verbal courses

Wholesome undertaken discipline (`samādānaśīla`) is dependent on vijñapti. Hence the seven wholesome bodily-verbal karmapathas associated with undertaken restraint are both:

```text
vijñapti
    explicit undertaking / manifest discipline

avijñapti
    continuing restraint-determination
```

### Samādhi-born restraint

The dhyāna-born and anāsrava restraints are different:

```text
samādhija saṃvara
    depends on citta alone
        ↓
avijñapti only
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly grounds this in `cittamātrādhīnatva` — dependence upon mind alone.

## Translation Refinement

The first-pass file correctly recognized that the verse distinguishes modes of realization, but the Bhāṣya now resolves the previously provisional counting.

The exact structure is:

```text
UNWHOLESOME BODILY-VERBAL COURSES

six delegable courses
    necessarily avijñapti
    if personally performed:
        may be vijñapti + avijñapti

sexual misconduct
    always personally completed
    always vijñapti + avijñapti

WHOLESOME BODILY-VERBAL COURSES

seven undertaken courses
    vijñapti + avijñapti

samādhi-born restraint
    avijñapti alone
```

So `dvidhaikaḥ` is not merely an abstract "one is twofold." It refers specifically to sexual misconduct as the single unwholesome bodily-verbal course necessarily possessing both modes.

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage makes several important distinctions.

First:

```text
karmic responsibility
    ≠ bodily motion personally executed
```

A course can be karmically complete for the instigator even when another performs the manifest act.

Second:

```text
manifestation
    ≠ completion
```

The temporal moment at which the course becomes principal may occur after the manifest action has ceased, as in a killing whose victim dies later.

Third:

```text
restraint through undertaking
    depends on vijñapti

restraint born from samādhi
    depends on citta alone
```

The same wholesome content can therefore possess different modes of karmic existence according to its genetic source.

## Logical Determination

The most important logical gain is the separation of three dimensions that might otherwise be conflated:

```text
1. agent of visible execution
2. bearer of karmic determination
3. temporal point of karmapatha completion
```

They need not coincide.

For the delegated six:

```text
visible executor
    ≠
karmically determining instigator
```

For delayed killing:

```text
moment of visible action
    ≠
moment of principal completion
```

For samādhi-born restraint:

```text
karmic determination
    without present external manifestation
```

Thus `avijñapti` functions precisely where karmic determination exceeds immediate appearance.

## Light Organon Interpretation

This passage provides a very clean distinction between manifestation and determination without reducing either to the other.

```text
vijñapti
    determination appearing outwardly

avijñapti
    determination persisting or completing
    independently of present outward appearance
```

But the relation remains genetic. Avijñapti does not float free of its ground: it arises according to action, undertaking, or samādhi. Conversely, visible action is not by itself sufficient to determine the complete karmic structure.

The particularly useful pattern is:

```text
source / ground
    ↓
mode of generation
    ↓
manifest or non-manifest karmic determination
```

The verse therefore deepens the chapter's opening question—how `cetanā` becomes objectively effective—by showing that objective karmic efficacy is not exhausted by what is presently displayed.

## New Technical Terms

- **maula-vijñapti** — principal manifest action; the manifest component at the principal completion of a karmapatha.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the karmapatha analysis.
  - **function:** explains why delegated bad courses may lack principal vijñapti in the instigator.

- **niṣṭhāpana** — bringing to completion, completing.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** distinguishes the act that finishes a karmic course from mere preparatory activity.

- **samādānaśīla** — discipline or morality undertaken by explicit assumption.
  - **status:** previously encountered field, sharpened here.
  - **function:** explains why the seven wholesome bodily-verbal courses depend on vijñapti.

- **cittamātrādhīna** — dependent on mind alone.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this local passage.
  - **function:** grounds the avijñapti-only status of samādhi-born restraint.

## Canonical Translation Decision

Retain:

```text
vijñapti
    manifest action / manifestation

avijñapti
    non-manifest karmic determination
```

Do not reduce `avijñapti` to "unexpressed intention." The passage requires it to account for completed karmic determination where principal outward manifestation is absent, has ceased, or is generated from samādhi.
