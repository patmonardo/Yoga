# VAK 4.96 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ānantaryāṇi karmāṇi tīvrakleśo 'tha durgatiḥ |
> kauravāsaṃjñisattvāśca matamāvaraṇatrayam || 4.96 ||

**Best current translation:** Three obstructions are recognized: karmic obstruction, consisting in the immediate-result karmas; afflictive obstruction, consisting in continual affliction; and resultant obstruction, consisting in the three bad destinies, Uttarakuru existence, and existence among non-percipient beings.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.96.md](./VAK_4.96.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan [259|04–259|24]. The working e-text is explicitly non-critical and contains several evident orthographic corruptions; they are preserved here rather than silently repaired.

```text

teṣāṃ kaḥ svabhāvaḥ /

ānantaryāṇi karmāṇi tīvrakleśo 'tha durgatiḥ /
kauravāsaṃjñisattvāśca matamāvaraṇatrayam // VAkK_4.96 //
```

## Natural Segmentation

The passage has five movements:

1. It identifies the three obstructions taught by the Buddha: karma, affliction, and maturation.
2. It specifies the five `ānantarya` acts as karmic obstruction.
3. It distinguishes **continual** affliction (`tīvra`) from merely **very strong** affliction (`tīkṣṇa`) and explains why only the former is intrinsically obstructive.
4. It defines resultant obstruction and states exactly what is obstructed: the noble path and the wholesome roots preparatory to it.
5. It ranks the obstructions and then explains the meaning of `ānantarya`.

The third movement is the decisive Bhāṣya correction to the first-pass Kārikā translation.

## Literal Prose Translation

Three obstructions were taught by the Blessed One: karmic obstruction, afflictive obstruction, and resultant obstruction. What is their nature?

The five `ānantarya` acts are karmic obstruction: killing one's mother, killing one's father, killing an arhat, causing a schism in the Saṅgha, and, with a malicious mind, causing blood to flow from the body of a Tathāgata.

Continuality of affliction is afflictive obstruction. Affliction is of two kinds: `tīvra`, which is frequent or recurring, and `tīkṣṇa`, which is of very great degree. Of these, that which is `tīvra` is an obstruction. Even an affliction of extremely strong force, if only occasional, can be destroyed; but not even a collection of afflictions that is incessant can readily be destroyed. When affliction is continuous, one gets no time for exertion toward its removal. Depending upon a mild affliction, a medium one arises; depending upon a medium one, an intense one arises.

Therefore the three bad destinies are resultant obstruction; so also, within good destiny, the Uttarakurus and the non-percipient beings.

To what are these obstructions? To the noble path and to the wholesome roots preparatory to the noble path. There are other fixed conditions that would also have to be called obstructive, but the text names these because they are especially evident under five headings: basis, fruit, destiny, mode of birth, and person.

Among these, afflictive obstruction is the worst; after it comes karmic obstruction. The Vaibhāṣikas explain that through these two one can remain resistant to cure even in a subsequent birth because they successively bring forth further consequences.

What does `ānantarya` mean? These karmas cannot be interrupted with respect to their maturation by the fruit of another birth or by another karma; they cannot be displaced. Alternatively, for the person who has performed them, after death there is no intervening state with respect to proceeding toward hell: there is immediacy, and the condition by which one becomes thus immediate is called `ānantarya`.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Three forms of obstruction

The Kārikā compresses three different mechanisms under the common function `āvaraṇa`:

```text
karmāvaraṇa
    = obstruction already constituted as a decisive act

kleśāvaraṇa
    = obstruction operating as the present afflictive stream

vipākāvaraṇa
    = obstruction constituted by the resultant mode of existence
```

The common denominator is not moral badness in the abstract. It is **incompatibility with the arising of the noble path and its preparatory wholesome roots**.

### 2. The five ānantarya karmas

The Bhāṣya identifies karmic obstruction with five acts:

```text
mātṛvadha
pitṛvadha
arhadvadha
saṃghabheda
tathāgataśarīre duṣṭacittarudhirotpādana
```

Their special status will govern the following verses.

### 3. Tīvra is not tīkṣṇa

This is the most important lexical result of the passage.

```text
tīvra
    = abhīkṣṇika
    = frequent, continual, recurrent

tīkṣṇa
    = adhimātra
    = very strong, intense
```

The obstruction is `tīvra`, not merely `tīkṣṇa`.

Vasubandhu's reason is temporal and practical:

```text
very strong but occasional affliction
    → leaves an interval
    → effort against it is possible

continual affliction
    → no interval for counter-effort
    → Path-preparation is blocked
```

Thus the obstructive force lies not simply in magnitude but in **continuity**.

### 4. Resultant obstruction

The Bhāṣya explicitly groups together:

```text
three durgatis
Uttarakuru
non-percipient beings
```

The last two are important because they show that `vipākāvaraṇa` is not equivalent to suffering or bad destiny. A resultant state can be comparatively fortunate and still be structurally unsuitable for the noble path.

### 5. What is obstructed?

The answer is exact:

```text
āryamārga
    noble path

āryamārga-prāyogika kuśalamūla
    wholesome roots preparatory to the noble path
```

`Āvaraṇa` therefore names a functional relation to liberation, not merely an undesirable condition.

### 6. Why these examples?

The prose acknowledges that other conditions can also obstruct the path. The listed cases are selected as especially evident under five perspectives:

```text
adhiṣṭhāna — basis
phala       — fruit
gati        — destiny
upapatti    — mode of birth
pudgala     — person
```

The e-text phrase `sudarśakāni pajñakāni` is textually suspect, so no stronger claim should be built upon its exact wording.

### 7. Ranking the obstructions

The prose states:

```text
kleśāvaraṇa
    = worst

then

karmāvaraṇa
```

The Vaibhāṣika explanation is that these can continue to render the continuum difficult to cure even beyond the immediate life by generating further consequences. The exact syntax of this sentence in the working e-text is not fully secure, so this ranking should be retained without over-elaborating the mechanism.

### 8. Meaning of ānantarya

The Bhāṣya offers two related explanations:

```text
1. no other karmic result can intervene before its maturation

2. after death there is no intervening birth before the hell-destiny toward which it leads
```

Thus `ānantarya` is not simply “immediate” in ordinary clock-time. It means **without an intervening karmic or rebirth result capable of displacing the maturation**.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces and explains the three obstructions, distinguishes `tīvra` from `tīkṣṇa`, and defines what they obstruct.
- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — explicitly marked in the explanation of why afflictive and karmic obstruction are graver than resultant obstruction.
- **Voice: uncertain** — the two alternative etymological explanations of `ānantarya` are presented without an explicit school label in this passage.

## New Technical Terms

- **karmāvaraṇa** — obstruction constituted by karma.
  - **status:** made technically operative here.
  - **function:** doctrinal classification.

- **kleśāvaraṇa** — obstruction constituted by continual affliction.
  - **status:** made technically operative here.
  - **function:** doctrinal classification.

- **vipākāvaraṇa** — obstruction constituted by a resultant mode of existence.
  - **status:** made technically operative here.
  - **function:** doctrinal classification.

- **abhīkṣṇika** — frequent, recurrent, continual.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this unit.
  - **function:** definition of `tīvra`.

- **tīkṣṇa** — very strong, intense.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only contrast term in this unit.
  - **function:** logical distinction from `tīvra`.

- **ājasrika** — continuous, incessant.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term.
  - **function:** explains why continual affliction obstructs effort.

- **parākramakāla** — opportunity/time for exertion.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expression.
  - **function:** practical explanation of obstruction.

- **āryamārga-prāyogika kuśalamūla** — wholesome roots preparatory to the noble path.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of what `āvaraṇa` blocks.
  - **function:** specifies the object of obstruction.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya changes the interpretation of the verse in two decisive ways.

First, `tīvra-kleśa` must not be translated simply as **intense affliction**. The prose explicitly reserves intensity for `tīkṣṇa` and defines `tīvra` through recurrence or continuity. The better rendering here is **continual affliction** or **persistently recurring affliction**.

Second, the three obstructions are not merely three morally bad categories. They are three ways in which a continuum can become unable to generate the path:

```text
past determining act
    → karmāvaraṇa

present uninterrupted affliction
    → kleśāvaraṇa

resultant existential placement
    → vipākāvaraṇa
```

The criterion is path-capacity.

## Logical Determination

Let `P` be the arising of the noble path or its preparatory wholesome roots.

Then:

```text
Obstructs(KarmaState, P)
Obstructs(AfflictiveContinuity, P)
Obstructs(ResultantExistence, P)
```

But the mechanism differs in each case.

For affliction specifically:

```text
Intensity(K) alone
    ≠ sufficient for obstruction

Continuity(K)
    → removes interval for counter-practice
    → obstructs P
```

This is a temporal logic of practical possibility:

```text
NoIntervalForCounterEffort
    → NoEffectivePrayoga
    → PathPreparationBlocked
```

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation should be corrected from:

> afflictive obstruction, consisting in intense affliction

to:

> afflictive obstruction, consisting in continual or persistently recurring affliction.

A stronger philosophical translation of the whole verse is:

> Three obstructions are recognized: the five karmas whose maturation admits no intervening result; continual affliction; and resultant existence in the three bad destinies, among the Uttarakurus, or among non-percipient beings.

The Bhāṣya then supplies the three formal labels: karmic, afflictive, and resultant obstruction.

## Light Organon Contact

The passage gives an unusually precise Practical-Reason criterion for obstruction:

```text
āvaraṇa
    = a determination that closes the practical possibility of Path-generation
```

Most strikingly, afflictive obstruction is not defined by maximal force but by **loss of an interval for negation**. An occasional powerful affliction can still become an object of counter-action. A continuous afflictive stream suppresses the temporal opening in which practical opposition could arise.

So the structure is not simply:

```text
stronger kleśa → greater obstruction
```

but:

```text
continuous kleśa
    → no practical interval
    → no effective counter-determination
    → obstruction of Path
```

This is textually grounded and should be retained for the later Practical Reason reconstruction.
