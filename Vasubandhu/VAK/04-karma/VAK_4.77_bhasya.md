# VAK 4.77 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā

**IAST**

ato ’nyat kliṣṭam ity anye lapanā-gīta-nāṭyavat /
kuśāstravac cābhidhyā tu para-sva-viṣamā spṛhā // 4.77 //

**Working translation**

Others say that afflicted speech other than these is disordered chatter, as in flattery, song, drama, and corrupt treatises. Covetousness, however, is an improper longing for another’s property.

Kārikā study: [VAK_4.77.md](./VAK_4.77.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya closes the preceding discussion of `saṃbhinnapralāpa` and then explains the definition of `abhidhyā`:

> evaṃ paridevasaṃgaṇikādikamapi yad anyan mṛṣāvādādibhyaḥ kliṣṭaṃ vākkarma tat saṃbhinnapralāpaḥ /
>
> kathaṃ cakravartikāle gītaṃ cāsti na ca saṃbhinnapralāpaḥ /
>
> naiṣkramyopasaṃhitāni hi tadānīṃ gīyante sma na grāmyarasopasaṃhitāni /
>
> tadāpy āvāhavivāhādyabhilāpasadbhāvād asti saṃbhinnapralāpo na karmapatha ity apare /
>
> abhidhyā tu parasvaviṣamaspṛhā //
>
> aho vata yat pareṣāṃ tan mama syād iti parasvebhyo yā viṣameṇānyāyena spṛhā svīkaraṇecchā balād vā copāyād vā sā ’bhidhyā karmapathaḥ /
>
> sarvaiva kāmāvacarī tṛṣṇābhidhyety apare /
>
> tathā hi nivaraṇādhikāre kāmacchandam adhikṛtyoktaṃ sūtre “so ’bhidhyāṃ loke prahāya” ity evamādi /
>
> yady api sarvābhidhyā na tu sarvā karmapatha audārikaduścaritasaṃgrahād ity apare /
>
> mā bhūc cakravartinām apy uttarakuravāṇāṃ cābhidhyā karmapatha iti /

## Literal Prose Translation

Likewise lamentation, social chatter, and other afflicted verbal action distinct from false speech and the other specifically defined verbal courses is `saṃbhinnapralāpa`.

How, then, can there be song in the age of a wheel-turning king without there being `saṃbhinnapralāpa`? Because at that time songs connected with renunciation are sung, not songs connected with vulgar or sensual flavor.

Others say that even then `saṃbhinnapralāpa` exists, because there is talk concerning marriage and similar matters, but it does not constitute a karmapatha.

Covetousness is an improper longing for another’s property. The wish concerning another’s possession, “Ah, may what belongs to them become mine,” when it is a wrongful and unjust longing—an intention to make it one’s own either by force or by some device—is the karmapatha of covetousness.

Others hold that all craving belonging to the desire realm is called `abhidhyā`, citing the sūtra treatment of sensual desire: “Having abandoned covetousness regarding the world ...”

Others distinguish further: although all such craving may be called `abhidhyā`, not all of it is the karmapatha of covetousness, because the karmapathas collect the gross forms of bad conduct. Otherwise even wheel-turning kings and inhabitants of Uttarakuru would possess the karmapatha of covetousness.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The residual verbal category is still contested

The Bhāṣya preserves the distinction already emerging in 4.76:

```text
afflicted verbal action
    minus false speech
    minus divisive speech
    minus harsh speech
        ↓
saṃbhinnapralāpa
```

Examples include lamentation and social chatter. But the cakravartin objection shows that the same **surface form**—for example song—does not by itself determine the karmic type.

```text
song connected with sensual/vulgar taste
    → afflicted verbal conduct

song connected with renunciation
    → not afflicted in that way
```

A second opinion goes further: ordinary chatter may exist even in the cakravartin age, yet fail to reach the status of a **karmapatha**.

Thus the text distinguishes:

```text
verbal act
    ≠ necessarily
verbal bad conduct
    ≠ necessarily
karmapatha
```

### 2. `abhidhyā` is not merely desire

The principal definition is relational and appropriative:

```text
another possesses X
    ↓
“may X become mine”
    ↓
wrongful / unjust appropriation-wish
    ↓
abhidhyā-karmapatha
```

The Bhāṣya glosses `viṣamā spṛhā` with `anyāyena spṛhā`—a longing proceeding wrongly or unjustly—and specifies `svīkaraṇecchā`, the wish to make another’s property one’s own.

The means contemplated may be:

```text
balāt
    by force

upāyāt
    by some device / stratagem
```

This creates a precise relation to VAK 4.73:

```text
abhidhyā
    wrongful projected appropriation

adattādāna
    completed bodily appropriation
```

The mental karmapatha is therefore not yet theft, but it already has theft’s practical form as a projected relation.

### 3. Three levels of extension are distinguished

The Bhāṣya records competing uses of `abhidhyā`:

**View A:** all desire-realm craving (`kāmāvacarī tṛṣṇā`) is `abhidhyā`.

**View B:** all such craving may be called `abhidhyā`, but only its gross, ethically decisive form is `abhidhyā` as a karmapatha.

The second distinction invokes the principle already established in 4.66:

```text
broader bad conduct
    ↓ audārika-saṃgraha
selected gross / decisive forms
    ↓
karmapatha
```

This prevents ordinary desire from being indiscriminately identified with the karmapatha of covetousness.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / main exposition** — definition of `abhidhyā` as wrongful longing for another’s possession and the explanatory gloss through appropriation.
- **Voice: alternate view** — all desire-realm craving is `abhidhyā`, supported by sūtra language concerning the abandonment of covetousness.
- **Voice: alternate view** — all such craving may be `abhidhyā` in a broad sense, but not all is the karmapatha because karmapathas select the gross forms of bad conduct.
- **Voice: alternate view** — even in the cakravartin age some `saṃbhinnapralāpa` remains, though it is not a karmapatha.

The text does not force all these extensions into a single univocal definition.

## New Technical Terms

### `viṣamā spṛhā`
- **Meaning:** improper, distorted, or wrongful longing.
- **Status:** explicit Kārikā term, strongly specified by the Bhāṣya.
- **Function:** differentiates karmically decisive covetousness from desire in general.

### `svīkaraṇecchā`
- **Meaning:** wish to make something one’s own; appropriative intention.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
- **Function:** gives the practical form of `abhidhyā`.

### `anyāya`
- **Meaning:** injustice, improper order, what is not rightfully grounded.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
- **Function:** specifies the normative defect in `viṣamā spṛhā`.

### `grāmyarasa`
- **Meaning:** vulgar, worldly, or sensual flavor.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local discussion.
- **Function:** distinguishes afflicted song from song connected with renunciation.

### `naiṣkramyopasaṃhita`
- **Meaning:** connected with renunciation.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-only here.
- **Function:** explains why song as such is not intrinsically `saṃbhinnapralāpa`.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.77 gives the first exact mental karmapatha definition:

```text
abhidhyā
    is not bare wanting
    is not every desire
    is not yet physical theft

abhidhyā-karmapatha
    = wrongful appropriative longing
      directed toward another’s possession
```

The decisive practical content is:

> “May what belongs to another become mine.”

This is why `abhidhyā` and `adattādāna` share the characteristic completion-root of greed while remaining different karmapathas.

The verse also confirms once again that `karmapatha` is a **selected decisive determination**, not coextensive with every ethically defective occurrence.

---

## Logical Determination

The mental karmapatha has the form of a projected relational transformation:

```text
actual relation:
    belongsTo(X, Other)

projected wrongful relation:
    belongsTo(X, Self)
```

What makes the projection karmically determinate is not desire as such but the practical negation of the existing rightful relation.

Thus:

```text
lobha
    general root

abhidhyā
    determinate wrongful appropriation-project

adattādāna
    bodily realization of wrongful appropriation
```

This preserves the distinction between **root**, **mental course**, and **bodily course**.

---

## Translation Consequences

1. `abhidhyā` should remain **covetousness** or **wrongful acquisitive longing**, not generic “desire.”
2. `viṣamā spṛhā` is better rendered “improper/wrongful longing” than merely “unequal desire.”
3. `saṃbhinnapralāpa` should remain broad enough to include intelligible but afflicted speech; “nonsense” is too narrow.
4. The verse itself does not define `vyāpāda`; that begins VAK 4.78.

---

## Light Organon Contact

The strongest text-grounded structure is not psychological but practical:

```text
existing relation
    ↓
projected negation of that relation
    ↓
appropriative practical determination
```

`abhidhyā` is therefore a mental karmapatha because the relation has already been **practically posited otherwise**, even before bodily appropriation occurs.

This gives a clean genetic sequence:

```text
lobha
    ↓
abhidhyā
    ↓
adattādāna
```

The Bhāṣya does not collapse these moments into one another; it distinguishes root, projected practical form, and completed bodily action.
