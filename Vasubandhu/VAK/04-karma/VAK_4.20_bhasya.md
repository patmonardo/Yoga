# VAK_4.20_bhasya — Temporal Possession of Non-Restraint, Meditation Restraint, and Noble Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.20**

```iast
tathaivāsaṃvarastho ’pi dhyānasaṃvaravān sadā |
atītājātayā āryas tu prathame nābhyatītayā ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> One established in non-restraint is likewise [endowed with present avijñapti, and after the first moment with past avijñapti]. One who possesses meditation-born restraint is continually endowed with past and future avijñapti. The noble person is likewise with respect to uncontaminated restraint, except that in the first moment he is not endowed with past avijñapti.

Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.20.md`.

## Source Cautions

The electronic source reads `ātyāgāt` at [209|18-209|19], while the immediately preceding governing statement gives `atyāgāt`. The exact e-text reading is preserved here.

The phrase `atītājātayā` in the Kārikā is explained by the prose as possession of **past and future** (`atītānāgatābhyām`) avijñaptis. The Bhāṣya therefore controls the interpretation of the compressed compound.

The next verse begins immediately afterward at [210|01], so the natural unit for VAK 4.20 ends with the explanation at [209|21-209|22].

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first repeats the rule carried forward from VAK 4.19: `atyāgāt`, “because it has not been abandoned,” is to be understood throughout the temporal-possession analysis.

He then extends the prātimokṣa pattern to `asaṃvara`. So long as a person established in non-restraint has not abandoned that non-restraint, the person is continually endowed with present avijñapti; after the first moment, the person is also endowed with past avijñapti.

Meditation-born restraint differs. A person who has obtained `dhyānasaṃvara` is continually endowed with past and future avijñapti as long as the restraint has not been abandoned. Vasubandhu gives a specific reason why past restraint is possessed even in the first moment of the present attainment: the practitioner obtains a meditation restraint that had been abandoned in a previous life (`janmāntaratyaktaṃ dhyānasaṃvaram atītaṃ labhate`). Thus past meditation-restraint can already belong to the continuum at the first present moment.

The noble person is similarly endowed with uncontaminated restraint, but with one decisive exception. At the first noble Path moment, there is no possession of past uncontaminated avijñapti because the Path had never arisen before in that continuum (`mārgasya pūrvam anutpāditatvāt`).

The temporal distinction therefore depends upon the history of acquisition in the continuum, not merely upon an abstract classification into past, present, and future.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — the entire explanatory unit. No explicit objection is introduced in this passage.

## New Technical Terms

- **lābhin** — possessor / one who has obtained; **status:** previously familiar doctrinal usage; **function:** identifies the bearer of meditation-born restraint.
- **janmāntaratyakta** — abandoned in another birth / previous life; **status:** new in this immediate sequence; **function:** explains why past meditation restraint can already be possessed in the first present moment.
- **pūrvam anutpādita** — not previously produced; **status:** explanatory phrase; **function:** explains why the first noble Path moment has no past uncontaminated restraint.
- **samanvāgata** — endowed with / possessed of; **status:** recurring Abhidharma technical term; **function:** expresses possession across temporal modes.

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Non-restraint follows the prātimokṣa temporal pattern

```text
asaṃvara

while not abandoned:
    present avijñapti

from the second moment onward:
    present avijñapti
    + past avijñapti
```

The same governing condition applies:

```text
atyāga
    non-abandonment
```

### 2. Meditation-born restraint spans past and future

```text
dhyānasaṃvara-lābhin
    → past avijñapti
    → future avijñapti
```

This is true even in the first present moment of attainment because an earlier meditation restraint abandoned in a previous life is again obtained.

Thus:

```text
first present moment of dhyānasaṃvara
    does not imply
absence of past dhyānasaṃvara
```

### 3. The first noble Path moment has no past uncontaminated restraint

```text
first ārya Path moment
    → present uncontaminated restraint
    → future uncontaminated restraint
    → no past uncontaminated restraint
```

Reason:

```text
mārgasya pūrvam anutpāditatvāt
    the Path had not previously arisen
```

This is the decisive contrast with meditation-born restraint.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā file's basic interpretation is confirmed but sharpened.

The phrase `atītājātayā` should be understood from the Bhāṣya as **past and future avijñapti**, not as an undifferentiated temporal expression.

The reason for past possession in the first moment of meditation-restraint should also be added to the interpretation: the practitioner recovers a meditation restraint abandoned in a previous birth.

The noble person's exception is equally precise: absence of past uncontaminated restraint applies specifically in the **first Path moment**, because no such Path had previously arisen.

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes temporal possession according to the history of a determination in the continuum:

```text
present acquisition
    does not by itself determine
whether a past instance is possessed
```

Rather:

```text
if the same class of determination
has existed earlier in the continuum
    → past possession is possible immediately

if it has never arisen before
    → no past possession at the first moment
```

Thus temporal possession depends on both present status and prior causal history.

## Organon Contact Point

A restrained contact point is enough:

```text
current actuality
    ≠ complete temporal determination

current possession
    must be read together with
    the history of the continuum
```

No stronger reconstruction is needed in the present pass.
