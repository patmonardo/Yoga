# VAK 4.62 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

> **navame cetanā yā sā kṛṣṇaśuklakṣayāya ca |**  
> **śuklasya dhyānavairāgyeṣv antyānantaryamārgajā || 4.62 ||**

Provisional translation:

> The volition in the ninth [uninterrupted path] serves for the exhaustion of black-and-white karma; the volition arising in the final uninterrupted paths of detachment from the Dhyānas serves for the exhaustion of white karma.

## Bhāṣya Source

Vasubandhu explains:

```text
navame kāmavairāgyānantaryamārge yā cetanā
    sā kṛṣṇaśuklasya kuśalasya karmaṇaḥ
    kṛṣṇasya cākuśalasya navamasya prakārasya
    prahāṇāya
```

That is, the volition in the ninth uninterrupted path of detachment from the desire-domain serves for the abandonment of:

```text
1. kṛṣṇaśukla kuśala karma
   desire-domain wholesome karma

2. the ninth and final type of kṛṣṇa akuśala karma
   desire-domain unwholesome karma
```

For white karma:

```text
dhyānād dhyānād vairāgyaṃ kurvataḥ
yo 'ntyo navama ānantaryamārgaḥ
tatra yā cetanā
iyaṃ caturvidhā cetanā
śuklasya karmaṇaḥ prahāṇāya
```

The final, ninth uninterrupted path in detachment from each of the four Dhyānas supplies four volitions for the abandonment of white karma.

The Bhāṣya then asks why wholesome karma is abandoned only by the final uninterrupted path:

```text
kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇam antyenaivānantaryamārgeṇa
kuśalasya karmaṇaḥ prahāṇaṃ nānyena /
```

Its answer is decisive:

```text
na hi tasya svabhāvaprahāṇam
prahīṇasyāpi saṃmukhībhāvāt /

kiṃ tarhi /
tadālambanakleśaprahāṇāt /
```

Wholesome karma is not abandoned by an intrinsic destruction of its own nature, since even after being abandoned it can become present again. Its abandonment consists instead in the abandonment of the afflictions that take it as object.

Therefore:

```text
yāvad eko 'pi tadālambanakleśaprakāra āste
tāvad asya prahāṇaṃ nopapadyate
```

So long as even one type of affliction taking that karma as object remains, its abandonment is not complete.

## Source Cautions

The first-pass Kārikā analysis correctly assigned the ninth desire-domain uninterrupted path to black-and-white karma and the final Dhyāna paths to white karma, but it did not capture two Bhāṣya refinements:

1. the ninth desire-domain path also abandons the ninth and final type of black unwholesome karma;
2. white karma is not abandoned through destruction of its own intrinsic nature, but through elimination of all afflictions that take it as object.

The second point changes the logical interpretation substantially.

## Natural Argument

The argument proceeds in three stages.

### 1. Completion of desire-domain abandonment

VAkK 4.61 assigned the first eight uninterrupted paths to black karma. VAkK 4.62 now gives the ninth and final path a double completion-function:

```text
first eight uninterrupted paths
    → abandon first eight types of black karma

ninth uninterrupted path
    → abandon ninth type of black karma
    → abandon desire-domain wholesome black-and-white karma
```

The ninth path therefore closes the entire karmic field belonging to desire-domain detachment.

### 2. White karma is exhausted domain by domain

White karma belongs to the form-domain. For each of the four Dhyānas, the final ninth uninterrupted path produces a volition serving for its abandonment:

```text
Dhyāna 1 final uninterrupted path
Dhyāna 2 final uninterrupted path
Dhyāna 3 final uninterrupted path
Dhyāna 4 final uninterrupted path

= four volitions
```

### 3. Why only the final path?

The Bhāṣya rejects an intrinsic-destruction model:

```text
wholesome karma
    is not annihilated in svabhāva
```

Even after it is said to be abandoned, the wholesome dharma can still become present. Hence `prahāṇa` here must mean something relational:

```text
abandonment of karma
    = abandonment of every kleśa
      that can take that karma as ālambana
```

Until the last such affliction has been removed, abandonment is incomplete.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of the ninth desire-domain path, the four final Dhyāna paths, and the argument distinguishing `svabhāvaprahāṇa` from abandonment through removal of object-taking afflictions.

## New Technical Terms

- **svabhāvaprahāṇa** — abandonment or destruction in intrinsic nature; **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this local argument; **function:** rejected explanation of how wholesome karma is abandoned.
- **tadālambanakleśa** — affliction taking that karma as its object-support; **status:** Bhāṣya-only articulation here; **function:** supplies the actual condition whose complete removal constitutes abandonment of wholesome karma.
- **navama prakāra** — ninth/final type; **status:** implicit in staged detachment, explicit here; **function:** identifies the final black unwholesome type abandoned by the ninth desire-domain path.

## Doctrinal Determination

The distribution is:

```text
BLACK KARMA
    first 8 types
        → first 8 desire-domain uninterrupted paths

    9th type
        → 9th desire-domain uninterrupted path

BLACK-AND-WHITE KARMA
    desire-domain wholesome karma
        → 9th desire-domain uninterrupted path

WHITE KARMA
    form-domain wholesome karma
        → final (9th) uninterrupted path
          for each of the 4 Dhyānas
```

The decisive doctrinal refinement is:

```text
prahāṇa of wholesome karma
    ≠ annihilation of wholesome karma's svabhāva

prahāṇa of wholesome karma
    = complete elimination of the kleśas
      that take it as object
```

Thus liberation does not require erasing wholesome dharmas from possible manifestation. It removes the afflicted relation to them.

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya yield a precise target-sensitive operation:

```text
PATH-STAGE
    → TARGET-KARMA
    → MODE-OF-ABANDONMENT
```

But the most important result is the distinction:

```text
INTRINSIC NEGATION
    destroy X itself

RELATIONAL NEGATION
    eliminate every afflictive relation R to X
```

For wholesome karma, Vasubandhu chooses the second.

Formally:

```text
ABANDONED(X)
    does not imply
NONMANIFESTABLE(X)
```

Instead:

```text
ABANDONED(X)
    iff
no remaining kleśa can take X as ālambana
```

The final uninterrupted path is required because abandonment becomes complete only when the last relevant object-taking affliction has disappeared.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation remains basically sound, but the Bhāṣya requires us to hear `kṣaya` as doctrinally articulated through `prahāṇa`, not as simple ontological destruction.

A stronger philosophical translation is:

> The volition of the ninth uninterrupted path of desire-domain detachment completes the abandonment of both black-and-white wholesome karma and the final type of black unwholesome karma. The volitions arising in the final uninterrupted paths of detachment from each Dhyāna complete the abandonment of white karma—not by destroying wholesome karma in its own nature, but by eliminating every remaining affliction that can take it as its object.

## Organon Contact Point

This is an unusually strong case where **negation is relational rather than annihilative**.

```text
X remains capable of appearing

but

the afflicted relation to X
is completely removed
```

So the Path does not simply negate its positive contents. It transforms the system of relations in which those contents function.

This gives a sharper form of karmic exhaustion:

```text
ordinary conditioned relation
    → progressively dismantled
    → last supporting opposition removed
    → karmic determination counts as abandoned
```

The final path is final because the relation-system is now exhausted, not because a positive dharma has been metaphysically annihilated.
