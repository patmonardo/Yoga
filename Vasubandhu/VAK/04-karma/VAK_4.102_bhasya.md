# VAK 4.102 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `ādāvante 'rbudāt pūrvaṃ yugāccoparate munau | sīmāyāṃ cāpyabaddhāyāṃ cakrabhedo na jāyate || 4.102 ||`

**Best current translation:** Division of the Dharma-wheel does not arise at the beginning or end, before the corruptions have arisen, before the foremost pair of disciples has appeared, after the Sage has passed away, or while a common monastic boundary remains unestablished.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.102.md](./VAK_4.102.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
cakrabhedastu ṣaṭsu kāleṣu na bhavati /
katameṣu /

ādāvante 'rbudāt pūrvaṃ yugāccoparate munau /
sīmāyāṃ cāpyabaddhāyāṃ cakrabhedo na jāyate // VAkK_4.102 //

ādāvacirapravartite dharmacakre /
ante parinirvāṇakāle bhagavataḥ /
etayorhi dvayoravasthayoḥ saṃgha ekaraso bhavati /

madhye 'pyarbudāt pūrvaṃ na bhidyate yāvacchāsane śīlārbudaṃ dṛṣṭyarbudaṃ ca notpannaṃ bhavati /

yugācca pūrvaṃ na bhidyate yāvacchrāvakāgrayugaṃ notpannaṃ bhavati /
bhinnanyāyaparivāsāttena ca pratisaṃdhānīyatvāt /

uparate munau śāstari parinirvṛte pratidvandvābhāvāt /

sīmāyāmabaddhāyāṃ yāvat sīmā na baddhā bhavati /
ekasīmāyāṃ hi pakṣadvayāvasthānātsaṃghabheda ityeṣu kālesu cakrabhedona bhavati /

na ca punaḥ sarveṣāṃ baddhānāṃ cakrabhedaḥ /
karmādhīnatvāt /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya identifies six temporal or institutional conditions under which `cakrabheda` cannot arise:

1. at the beginning of the Buddha's dispensation;
2. at its end;
3. before the internal corruptions (`arbuda`) of discipline and view have arisen;
4. before the foremost pair of disciples has appeared;
5. after the Buddha has entered parinirvāṇa;
6. before a common monastic boundary (`sīmā`) has been formally established.

The governing problem is not merely chronological. Vasubandhu is identifying the conditions under which a genuine rupture of an already constituted Dharma-wheel is structurally possible.

## Literal Prose Translation

> Division of the Dharma-wheel does not occur at six times. Which six?
>
> At the beginning, when the Dharma-wheel has only recently begun to turn; at the end, at the time of the Blessed One's parinirvāṇa. In these two conditions the Saṅgha is of one flavor.
>
> Even in the middle period it cannot be divided before an `arbuda` has arisen—that is, before a corruption of discipline and a corruption of view have appeared in the dispensation.
>
> Nor can it be divided before the pair has appeared, that is, before the foremost pair of disciples has arisen, because the divided Saṅgha is to be reunited by that pair according to the established procedure of reconciliation.
>
> Nor after the Sage has ceased, that is, after the Teacher has entered parinirvāṇa, because there is then no rival [who can set himself up against the living Teacher].
>
> Nor while the boundary is unestablished, that is, before a `sīmā` has been formally bound. For Saṅgha division consists in the two factions taking their stand within one boundary.
>
> Yet it does not follow that every community possessing a bound boundary undergoes division, since this depends upon karma.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Beginning and end: the Saṅgha is `ekarasa`

At the very beginning of the dispensation the Dharma-wheel has only just begun to turn. At the very end, when the Buddha is approaching parinirvāṇa, the community is likewise not available for this special rupture.

Vasubandhu's reason is concise:

```text
saṃgha ekarasaḥ
    the Saṅgha is "of one flavor"
```

The determinate opposition required for `cakrabheda` has not yet developed at the beginning, and is no longer structurally available at the end.

### 2. `Arbuda`: corruption must already have appeared

The Kārikā's obscure `arbuda` is explicitly explained as two internal corruptions:

```text
śīlārbuda
    corruption / excrescence of discipline

dṛṣṭyarbuda
    corruption / excrescence of view
```

Thus schism presupposes that contradiction has already emerged within the dispensation. A perfectly undifferentiated community cannot yet divide along the doctrinal lines analyzed in 4.98–4.101.

### 3. `Yuga`: the foremost pair of disciples

`Yuga` is not an era but the `śrāvakāgrayuga`, the foremost pair of disciples.

The Bhāṣya gives a teleological-seeming institutional reason: the extraordinary schism is one that can subsequently be repaired by this pair according to the procedure for reconciliation.

Thus the very definition of the historical possibility of schism includes the institutional possibility of reunion.

### 4. The Buddha must still be alive

After the Teacher has entered parinirvāṇa, `cakrabheda` cannot occur because there is no longer a `pratidvandvin`, a rival who can set himself up against the living Buddha as an alternative teacher.

This confirms that `cakrabheda` is specifically the structure analyzed in 4.100:

```text
living Buddha as Teacher
        versus
false rival teacher

Buddha's Path
        versus
alternative path
```

Without the living Teacher, that exact opposition cannot be constituted.

### 5. A common `sīmā` is constitutive

The Bhāṣya gives the strongest institutional condition:

```text
ekasīmāyāṃ pakṣadvayāvasthānam
    two factions taking their stand within one boundary
        =
    saṃghabheda
```

If there is no common formally established boundary, there may be distinct groups, but not division of one formally constituted Saṅgha in the technical sense.

This is an important refinement:

```text
mere plurality
    ≠
division
```

Division presupposes prior unity within a determinate common domain.

### 6. Conditions are necessary, not sufficient

Vasubandhu immediately adds that not every Saṅgha with a formally bound `sīmā` undergoes `cakrabheda`:

```text
karmādhīnatvāt
    because it depends upon karma
```

The six conditions therefore define possibility, not inevitability.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / Vaibhāṣika doctrinal exposition** — identifies the six conditions excluding `cakrabheda` and interprets the technical terms.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — adds the important qualification that an established boundary is necessary but not sufficient because actual schism remains karma-dependent.

## New Technical Terms

- **śīlārbuda** — an internal corruption or "tumor" of discipline within the dispensation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of obscure Kārikā term.
  - **function:** historical condition enabling schism.

- **dṛṣṭyarbuda** — an internal corruption or "tumor" of doctrinal view.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of obscure Kārikā term.
  - **function:** doctrinal condition enabling schism.

- **śrāvakāgrayuga** — the foremost pair of disciples.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of `yuga`.
  - **function:** agents capable of restoring a divided Saṅgha.

- **ekarasa** — literally "of one flavor"; here the undivided concord of the Saṅgha.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
  - **function:** characterizes states in which `cakrabheda` cannot arise.

- **pratidvandvin** — rival or counter-claimant.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only term here.
  - **function:** explains why the special schism cannot arise after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa.

- **sīmā-bandha** — formal establishment/binding of a monastic boundary.
  - **status:** previously encountered institutional vocabulary.
  - **function:** constitutive condition of technical Saṅgha division.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 4.102 defines `cakrabheda` through a set of necessary background conditions:

```text
Established Dharma-wheel
+ developed internal contradiction
+ foremost disciple-pair
+ living Buddha
+ common formally bounded Saṅgha
+ appropriate karma
        ↓
possibility of cakrabheda
```

The crucial logical principle is:

```text
Division(x)
    presupposes
PriorUnity(x)
```

and, more strongly:

```text
FormalDivision(x)
    presupposes
DeterminateCommonDomain(x)
```

Without an established common `sīmā`, two groups are simply two groups; they are not yet two opposed factions of one formally constituted Saṅgha.

The Bhāṣya also makes internal contradiction a condition of the historical process. `Śīlārbuda` and `dṛṣṭyarbuda` must first appear before the unity becomes susceptible to doctrinal schism.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation must now resolve both obscure terms:

- `arbuda` = the arising of corruptions of discipline and view;
- `yuga` = the foremost pair of disciples.

A more precise translation is:

> Division of the Dharma-wheel does not arise at the beginning or end of the dispensation, before corruptions of discipline and view have appeared, before the foremost pair of disciples has arisen, after the Sage has passed away, or before a common monastic boundary has been formally established.

## Light Organon Contact

The Bhāṣya gives an unusually exact account of **determinate negation at the institutional level**.

A genuine division is not externally imposed upon an indifferent aggregate. It requires:

```text
prior unity
    → internal differentiation
    → developed opposition
    → actual division
```

`Arbuda` is especially revealing: the contradiction must first become immanent to the institution before a full rupture of its normative wheel is possible.

But Vasubandhu prevents this from becoming a purely structural determinism with his final qualification:

```text
necessary structural conditions
    ≠
sufficient cause

actual schism
    remains karmādhīna
```

Thus structure defines the field of possibility; karma determines whether that possibility becomes actual.
