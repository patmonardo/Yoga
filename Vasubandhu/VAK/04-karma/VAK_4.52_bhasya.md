# VAK_4.52_bhasya — Detachment, Stability, and the Contraction of Karmic Possibility

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.52**

```iast
yadviraktaḥ sthiro bālas tatra notpadyavedyakṛt |
nānyavedyakṛd apy āryaḥ kāme 'gre vā 'sthiro 'pi na ||
```

> A stable ordinary person, when detached from a given level, does not produce karma there to be experienced in the next rebirth. A stable noble person does not produce the other future-experience karma either; and even an unstable noble person does not do so with respect to the desire domain or the summit of existence.

See: [VAK_4.52.md](./VAK_4.52.md)

## Source Cautions

The e-text contains several orthographic irregularities in this passage; the quoted source has been minimally spaced for readability without changing the argument.

The term `sthira` continues into the second pāda concerning the Ārya. The Bhāṣya explicitly says `sthira iti vartate` — “the word stable continues to apply.”

The final pāda concerns an Ārya who is `parihāṇadharmā`, liable to fall back. Its restriction is special to detachment from the two boundary levels: `kāmadhātu` and `bhavāgra`.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.50–4.51 classified karma according to whether fruition is necessary and, if determinate, when it occurs. VAK 4.52 now asks a new question:

```text
Which temporally determinate karmas
can a given kind of person still produce?
```

The answer depends on three variables:

```text
1. the bhūmi from which the person is detached
2. whether that detachment is stable
3. whether the person is ordinary or noble
```

### 1. Stable ordinary person

If an ordinary person (`pṛthagjana`) is detached from a given bhūmi and is stable — that is, not liable to fall back (`aparihāṇadharmā`) — that person does not produce karma to be experienced in the immediately following rebirth in that bhūmi.

```text
stable ordinary person
    detached from bhūmi X
        → no upapadyavedanīya karma for X
```

The Bhāṣya does not yet deny every future karmic relation to that level. The restriction stated here is specifically against next-birth fruition there.

### 2. Stable Ārya

For the stable Ārya, the restriction is stronger.

If an Ārya is detached from a bhūmi and is not liable to fall back, that person produces neither:

```text
upapadyavedanīya
    next-birth karma for that bhūmi

nor

aparaparyāyavedanīya
    later-life karma for that bhūmi
```

The Bhāṣya gives the ground explicitly:

```text
na hy asau bhavyaḥ
punar ādhastanīṃ bhūmim āyātum
```

Such a person is no longer capable of returning to the lower bhūmi.

This is the key doctrinal advance of the verse. The Path changes not merely which karmas are likely, but which karmic futures remain genuinely possible.

The remaining possibilities are:

```text
aniyata karma
    because its fruition is not necessary

and

dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya karma
    if the person is presently born in that bhūmi
```

### 3. Ārya liable to fall back

The final pāda introduces an apparent exception. Even an Ārya who is `parihāṇadharmā` — liable to fall back — cannot produce next-birth or later-life karma toward the two limiting levels from which they are detached:

```text
kāmadhātu
bhavāgra
```

Why?

The Bhāṣya says that such an Ārya falls back **from the fruit** (`phalāt parihīṇaḥ`). It then invokes a doctrine to be explained later: one who has fallen from the fruit does not die in that fallen condition.

Thus the temporal opening that would be required for such future fruition does not arise.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose and statement of the doctrinal grounds.

The final explanation about falling from the fruit anticipates a later doctrinal discussion and is stated here without extended debate.

## New Technical Terms

- **vītarāga** — one detached or dispassionate with respect to a bhūmi
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** identifies the level from which karmic projection is being restricted

- **sthira** — stable, firm
  - **status:** important local technical qualifier
  - **function:** means not liable to fall back from the attained detachment

- **aparihāṇadharmā** — not of a nature liable to fall back
  - **status:** technical path term
  - **function:** glosses the stability relevant to the restriction on future karma

- **parihāṇadharmā** — of a nature liable to fall back
  - **status:** technical path term
  - **function:** marks the unstable Ārya addressed in the final pāda

- **upapadyavedanīya** — karma to be experienced in the immediately following rebirth
  - **status:** established in VAK 4.50
  - **function:** temporally determinate future karma prohibited under the relevant conditions

- **aparaparyāyavedanīya** — karma to be experienced in a later existence
  - **status:** established in VAK 4.50
  - **function:** additional future karma excluded for the stable Ārya

- **bhavāgra** — summit of existence, highest conditioned level
  - **status:** previously encountered cosmological term
  - **function:** upper boundary level in the final exception

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse establishes that the ability to produce karma is **agent-relative**.

```text
same karmic category
    may be producible by one continuum
    and impossible for another
```

The decisive variables are:

```text
agent-status
    ordinary / noble

detachment
    from which bhūmi?

stability
    liable to fall back / not liable
```

This yields:

```text
stable ordinary person
    detached from X
        → cannot produce next-birth karma for X

stable Ārya
    detached from X
        → cannot produce next-birth karma for X
        → cannot produce later-life karma for X

unstable Ārya
    detached from kāmadhātu or bhavāgra
        → still cannot produce those two future karmas
```

The reason for the stronger Ārya restriction is not moral superiority in the abstract. It is structural:

```text
the relevant future return
is no longer possible
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation was substantially correct but left the scope of `anya-vedya` and the final pāda provisional.

The Bhāṣya fixes both.

Preferred translation:

> **A stable ordinary person who is detached from a given level does not produce karma to be experienced there in the next rebirth. A stable noble person does not produce either next-birth or later-life karma for that level. Even a noble person liable to fall back does not produce these with respect to the desire domain or the summit of existence.**

The Bhāṣya also confirms that `anya-vedya` refers here to `aparaparyāyavedanīya` in addition to `upapadyavedanīya`.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.52 introduces a powerful modal relation:

```text
POSSIBLE-KARMA(A, K)
    depends on
POSSIBLE-FUTURE(A)
```

For the stable Ārya:

```text
RETURN(A, bhūmi-X) = impossible

therefore

PRODUCE(A, next-birth-karma-for-X) = impossible
PRODUCE(A, later-life-karma-for-X) = impossible
```

This is stronger than saying that certain karmas are absent.

The Bhāṣya is determining a contraction of the **space of possible causal continuations**.

```text
path transformation
    → contraction of possible return
    → contraction of producible karma
    → contraction of possible rebirth futures
```

The verse therefore links the modal analysis of 4.50–4.51 directly to the status of the practitioner.

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained Organon contact point is that a transformed subject no longer has the same field of possible operations.

```text
change in achieved determination
    → change in possible action
    → change in possible future world
```

The Path is therefore not merely an additional predicate attached to an otherwise unchanged agent. In Vasubandhu's analysis, attainment changes the **modal range of what the continuum can still produce**.

This is an important practical-reason structure, but it should remain grounded here in the technical Abhidharma doctrine of detachment, non-retrogression, and karmic fruition.
