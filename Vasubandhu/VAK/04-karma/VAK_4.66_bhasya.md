# VAK_4.66_bhasya — The Ten Karmapathas as a Selection from Conduct

## Kārikā Anchor

**IAST**

> *viparyayāt sucaritaṃ tad-audārika-saṃgrahāt /*  
> *daśa karmapathā uktā yathāyogaṃ śubhāśubhāḥ // 4.66 //* 

**Best working translation:**

> Good conduct is determined by reversal. Because the gross or principal forms of the wider conduct-field are comprehended, ten courses of karma are taught, wholesome and unwholesome as appropriate.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_4.66.md`](./VAK_4.66.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Primary working source: `kosabhasaya.txt`, Pradhan [237|23–238|12]. The e-text is a reference text based principally on Pradhan with comparison to Shastri; it is not treated as a critical edition.

The prose begins by completing the positive counterpart to VAK 4.65:

> *viparyayāt sucaritam*

and explains:

> *iṣṭaṃ kuśalaṃ kāyavāṅmanaskarmānabhidhyā 'vyāpādasamyagdṛṣṭayaś ca /*

Thus good conduct comprises wholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma together with non-covetousness, non-hostility, and right view.

The Bhāṣya then raises a question about the last pair:

> *parānugrahopaghātābhisaṃdhyabhāve kathaṃ samyagdṛṣṭimithyādṛṣṭyoḥ kuśalākuśalatvam /*

How can right view and false view be wholesome and unwholesome when they do not themselves contain an intention either to benefit or to injure another?

The answer is concise:

> *tanmūlatvāt /*

Because they are roots of such conduct.

The Kārikā then gives the transition:

> *tad-audārika-saṃgrahāt /*  
> *daśa karmapathā uktā yathāyogaṃ śubhāśubhāḥ //*

The Bhāṣya explains:

> *teṣām eva sucaritaduścaritānāṃ caudārikasaṃgraheṇa daśa karmapathāḥ sūtra uktāḥ yathāyogaṃ kuśalāḥ sucaritebhyo 'kuśalā duścaritebhyaḥ /*

The ten karmapathas taught in the sūtra are obtained by comprehending the gross or principal members of precisely those good and bad conducts: the wholesome ones from good conduct and the unwholesome ones from bad conduct, as applicable.

The prose then asks what has *not* been included:

> *kim atra duścaritam asaṃgṛhītaṃ kiṃ vā sucaritam /*

### What bad conduct remains outside the ten?

For bodily bad conduct the Bhāṣya excludes:

> *prayogapṛṣṭhabhūtaḥ kliṣṭaś cānyo 'pi bandhānādiḥ /*

that is, conduct belonging to preparatory and subsequent phases, and other afflicted bodily conduct such as binding and the like.

The reason is:

> *tasya nātyaudārikatvāt /*

because such conduct is not sufficiently gross or decisive.

What is selected as karmapatha is the bodily misconduct which dislodges another from:

> *jīvitabhogadārebhyaḥ*

life, possessions, or sexual partner.

The same principle applies to verbal misconduct: only the sufficiently decisive verbal forms are selected as karmapathas.

For mental bad conduct the Bhāṣya states that a portion consisting in `cetanā` is not included among the ten.

### What good conduct remains outside the ten?

The same selective principle applies positively. The Bhāṣya excludes portions of bodily good conduct such as:

- preparatory and subsequent phases,
- abstention from intoxicants and similar disciplines,
- giving,
- sacrificial or reverential wholesome activity,

and portions of verbal good conduct such as pleasant or affectionate speech. Mental `cetanā` likewise belongs to the wider field of mental good conduct without thereby becoming one of the ten karmapathas.

## Literal Prose Translation

By reversal there is good conduct: wholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma, together with non-covetousness, non-hostility, and right view.

How are right view and false view wholesome and unwholesome when there is no intention in them to benefit or harm another? Because they are roots of such conduct.

Of these very good and bad conducts, through comprehension of their gross forms, ten courses of karma are taught in the sūtra: as appropriate, the wholesome courses are taken from good conduct and the unwholesome courses from bad conduct.

What bad conduct is not included here, and what good conduct is not included? Among the unwholesome karmapathas, a portion of bodily bad conduct is not included: what belongs to the preparatory and subsequent phases, and other afflicted bodily conduct such as binding and so forth, because it is not sufficiently gross. Bodily bad conduct which deprives others of life, possessions, or partners is taught as a karmapatha in order to distinguish these principal forms. The corresponding restriction applies to verbal bad conduct. A portion of mental bad conduct consisting in intention is likewise not included.

Among wholesome conduct also, portions of bodily good conduct—such as preparatory and subsequent activity, abstention from intoxicants, giving, and related wholesome actions—are not included; likewise pleasant speech and similar verbal good conduct, and mental intention.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.65 established that the field of conduct is wider than karma narrowly considered. VAK 4.66 now performs a second restriction:

```text
sucarita / duścarita
    broad field of good and bad conduct
            ↓
audārika-saṃgraha
    selection of principal / gross determinations
            ↓
daśa karmapathāḥ
    ten courses of karma
```

Thus:

```text
conduct
    > karmapatha
```

just as the previous verse established:

```text
duścarita
    > karma
```

The ten karmapathas are therefore not a complete inventory of morally qualified bodily, verbal, and mental activity. They are a **selected operative set**.

## Why `audārika` Matters

The Bhāṣya makes the criterion concrete. Mere afflicted bodily activity is not enough. Binding someone, preparatory activity, or subsequent activity can be `duścarita`, but it does not therefore count as one of the ten principal karmapathas.

The bodily karmapathas are selected because they bear on decisive domains of another being:

```text
jīvita     life
bhoga      possessions / enjoyment
 dāra       sexual partner
```

In other words, the distinction is one of **ethical determination and completion**, not merely visible size.

So `audārika` should not be flattened into “physically coarse.” Here it functions more nearly as:

```text
audārika
    gross
    manifest
    principal
    ethically decisive
```

The later verses on `prayoga`, `maula`, and `pṛṣṭha` will make this selection principle even more precise.

## The Important Question about Right and False View

The Bhāṣya pauses before introducing the ten to address an asymmetry.

Right and false view do not themselves contain an immediate intention:

```text
to benefit another
or
to injure another
```

Yet they are still classified as wholesome and unwholesome.

The answer:

> *tanmūlatvāt* — because they are roots.

This is important for the genetic structure of the chapter. Ethical qualification does not attach only to completed outward actions. A cognitive determination can be ethically decisive because it functions as a **root from which action proceeds**.

Hence:

```text
view
    ↓ root
orientation of cetanā
    ↓
action
```

This does not identify view with karma. It determines the ground through which karma becomes qualified.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / expository voice

The initial explanation of `sucarita`, the question regarding right and false view, and the selection of the ten karmapathas are presented without explicit school attribution in this passage.

No separate opponent school is named here. The question-and-answer form should therefore not be over-dramatized into a doctrinal controversy.

## New Technical Terms

- **audārika-saṃgraha** — comprehension or selection of the gross/principal determinations from a wider conduct-field
  - **status:** made technically operative here by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** classificatory restriction / selection criterion

- **aty-audārika** — sufficiently gross, principal, or decisive
  - **status:** Bhāṣya prose refinement
  - **function:** explains why some conduct becomes karmapatha and other conduct does not

- **jīvitabhogadāra** — life, possessions/enjoyment, and sexual partner
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory grouping in this passage
  - **function:** specifies the decisive objects affected by the three bodily bad karmapathas

- **tanmūlatva** — being the root of that
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory determination here
  - **function:** grounds the wholesome/unwholesome status of right and false view

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse and prose establish three nested extensions:

```text
bodily / verbal / mental conduct
        ↓
sucarita / duścarita
        ↓ selective restriction
karmapatha
```

Not every act of good conduct is one of the ten wholesome karmapathas, and not every act of bad conduct is one of the ten unwholesome karmapathas.

Examples outside the selected ten include:

```text
preparatory phases
subsequent phases
lesser afflicted bodily conduct
abstention from intoxicants
acts of giving
pleasant speech
mental cetanā as such
```

This distinction must be retained throughout VAK 4.67 onward.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.66 does not merely enumerate ten items. It defines an operation of abstraction from a richer field:

```text
whole conduct-field
        ↓ discrimination
principal determinations
        ↓ comprehension
finite operative classification
```

The ten are therefore generated by **selective comprehension**, not by asserting that only ten morally significant acts exist.

This is a useful safeguard against reading the karmapathas as an exhaustive moral ontology.

There is also a second genetic movement:

```text
samyagdṛṣṭi / mithyādṛṣṭi
        ↓ tanmūlatvāt
root-condition
        ↓
qualified action
```

A determination can thus belong to the practical field not only by being an action but by functioning genetically as the root of action.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā translation is basically confirmed, but `audārika` should receive a richer gloss than simply “gross.” In this passage:

> **gross / principal / ethically decisive forms**

best captures the Bhāṣya's use.

`karmapatha` should remain **course of karma** for now. The Bhāṣya is beginning to show why `patha` is not ornamental: these are selected determinate routes through which karma takes practical form. The explicit etymological analysis comes later at VAK 4.78 and should govern any final canonical decision.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest contact point is the relation between a complete field and its operative discrimination:

```text
ConductField
    contains many determinations
            ↓
DiscriminativeSelection
            ↓
KarmicCourse
    principal actionable determination
```

This is not yet an ontological reduction. The selected ten remain intelligible only against the broader field from which they are drawn.

The same reciprocal structure holds between cognitive root and practical manifestation:

```text
root determination
        → action

action
        → reveals operative root
```

The Bhāṣya therefore preserves rather than erases the mediation between inward orientation and outward course.

## Working Synthesis

VAK 4.64–4.66 now gives a precise progression:

```text
4.64

        ↓
4.65
sucarita / duścarita

        ↓
4.66

```

The chapter is progressively narrowing from the whole ethical-practical field to the determinate courses that will now be analyzed in their material form, phases, roots, completion, and results.
