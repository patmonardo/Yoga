# VAK 4.103 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `upakāriguṇakṣetranirākṛtivipādanāt | vyañjanāntarito 'pi syāt mātā yacchoṇitodbhavaḥ || 4.103 ||`

**Best current translation:** Because of the repudiation or destruction of a benefactor and of a field of qualities, these acts have immediate consequence. Even if the sexual characteristic has changed, the parental relation remains; in the exceptional case, the mother is the one from whose maternal generative substance one arose.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.103.md](./VAK_4.103.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
kasmāt mātṛvadhādiṣvānantaryaṃ nānyatra /

upakāriyuṇakṣetranirākṛtivipādanāt /

mātāpitṛvadhe tāvadupakāriṇo nirākaraṇāt /
kathaṃ tāvupakāriṇau /
ātmabhāvasya tatprabhavatvāt /
kiṃ tayornirākaraṇam /
parityāgaḥ /
guṇakṣetratvādarhadvadhād ānantaryam /

yadi punarmāturvyañjanaṃ parivṛttaṃ syāt piturvā tatraikasminnāmnāyo

vyañjanāntarito 'pi syāt

ata evocyate "syāt puruṣaṃ jīvitādvacyaparopayenna pitaraṃ nārhantam /
ānantaryāvadyena ca spṛśyeta /
syānmāturvyañjanaṃ parivṛttaṃ syāditi /
syāt strīṃ jīvitādvyaparopayenna mātaraṃ nārhantīm ānantaryāvadyena ca spṛśyeta /
syāt piturvyañjanaṃ parivṛttaṃ syādi"ti /

anyasyāḥ striyāḥ kalalaṃ prasrutamanyayā yonyā pītam /
katarā tasya mātā yāṃ ghnataḥ syādānantaryam /

mātā yacchoṇitodbhavaḥ // VAkK_4.103 //

yasyāḥ śoṇitādasāvudbhūtaḥ sattvaḥ sā 'sya mātā dvitīyā tu sarvakṛtyeṣvavalokyā /
sā hyāpāyikā poṣikā saṃvardhikā ca /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya develops four connected determinations:

1. why killing mother or father is ānantarya;
2. why killing an arhat is ānantarya;
3. why a later change of sexual characteristic does not alter the parental relation;
4. how maternity is determined in an exceptional case involving genetic and gestational mothers.

The governing distinction is between an exceptional **benefactor-field** and an exceptional **field of qualities**.

## Literal Prose Translation

Why is there ānantarya in killing one's mother and the others, but not elsewhere? Because of repudiating a benefactor and destroying a field of qualities.

In the killing of mother or father, first of all, there is repudiation of one who has rendered benefit. How are they benefactors? Because this embodied existence arises from them. What is their repudiation? Abandonment. Killing an arhat is ānantarya because the arhat is a field of qualities.

But suppose the sexual characteristic of the mother or father were transformed. In such a case the scriptural determination remains: even though the sexual characteristic has changed, the relevant parental status persists. Thus one might kill a man who is neither one's father nor an arhat and nevertheless incur ānantarya if that person was one's mother whose sexual characteristic had changed; and conversely with the father.

Suppose an embryonic mass issued from one woman and was received into the womb of another. Which woman is the mother such that killing her constitutes ānantarya? The mother is the one from whose maternal generative substance the being arose. The second woman, however, is to be regarded as mother in all ordinary practical respects, because she carried, nourished, and raised the child.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Parental killing: destruction of a constitutive benefactor

The Bhāṣya asks why killing parents has a special karmic status.

Its answer is not simply that parents deserve gratitude. Their beneficence is constitutive:

```text
mother + father
    → source of this ātmabhāva
    → exceptional benefactor relation
```

The text says:

```text
ātmabhāvasya tatprabhavatvāt
because this embodied existence arises from them
```

Thus the karmic field is determined by the history through which this very continuum obtained embodiment.

### 2. `nirākṛti` as repudiation

The Bhāṣya glosses `nirākṛti` with:

```text
parityāga
abandonment / repudiation
```

The ānantarya character of parental killing therefore includes a relational negation: the agent destroys one whose prior activity stands constitutively behind the agent's own embodied existence.

### 3. Arhat killing: destruction of a field of qualities

The explanation changes with the arhat:

```text
guṇakṣetratvāt
because the arhat is a field of qualities
```

Thus two distinct grounds of exceptional karmic gravity are present:

```text
parent
    → upakārin
    → exceptional benefactor-field

arhat
    → guṇakṣetra
    → exceptional field of spiritual qualities
```

The verse compresses these two principles into one causal account of ānantarya.

## Changed Sexual Characteristic

The Bhāṣya explicitly separates parental identity from present sexual appearance.

If the mother's sexual characteristic changes and she is presently male, killing that person can still constitute matricide. Likewise, if the father's sexual characteristic changes and he is presently female, killing that person can still constitute patricide.

The underlying rule is:

```text
present bodily characteristic
    does not erase
causally constituted parental relation
```

The karmic field is therefore historically determined rather than exhausted by present visible predicates.

## The Exceptional Case of Two Mothers

The Bhāṣya considers a striking embryological case:

```text
woman A
    → supplies the kalala / maternal generative substance

woman B
    → receives it into her womb
    → carries, nourishes, raises the child
```

Which one is the `mātā` whose killing constitutes ānantarya?

Vasubandhu's answer is:

```text
mātā yacchoṇitodbhavaḥ
mother = she from whose śoṇita the being arose
```

Thus woman A is technically the mother for the ānantarya classification.

But Vasubandhu immediately preserves the practical maternal status of woman B:

```text
āpāyikā
poṣikā
saṃvardhikā
```

She bears, nourishes, and raises the child, and therefore is to be regarded as mother in ordinary practical relations.

This is a remarkably precise differentiation between **causal-genetic maternity** and **gestational/nurturing maternity**.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks why these killings have ānantarya status and gives the benefactor/quality-field distinction.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — glosses parental beneficence through the origination of the embodied existence and `nirākṛti` through `parityāga`.
- **Voice: scriptural citation / doctrinal precedent** — the examples concerning changed sexual characteristics.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — resolves the exceptional two-mother case by maternal generative origin while preserving the ordinary nurturing status of the gestational mother.

## New Technical Terms

- **upakārin** — benefactor; here specifically mother and father as constitutive sources of the present embodied existence.
  - **status:** previously encountered, newly decisive here.
  - **function:** explains the special field-character of parental killing.

- **guṇakṣetra** — field of qualities; here the arhat as a spiritually exceptional karmic field.
  - **status:** explicit doctrinal term in this passage.
  - **function:** explains the ānantarya character of arhat-killing.

- **nirākṛti** — repudiation or rejection.
  - **status:** newly clarified by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** relational determination of the offense against parents.

- **parityāga** — abandonment; the Bhāṣya's gloss of `nirākṛti`.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** defines the relational negation involved.

- **vyañjana-parivṛtti** — transformation of sexual characteristic.
  - **status:** doctrinal case introduced here.
  - **function:** tests whether parental identity depends on present bodily form.

- **śoṇita** — maternal generative substance in the ancient embryological model.
  - **status:** doctrinally decisive in the maternity case.
  - **function:** determines technical maternity for ānantarya classification.

- **āpāyikā / poṣikā / saṃvardhikā** — bearer, nourisher, and rearer.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only practical differentiation.
  - **function:** preserves the ordinary maternal status of the gestational/nurturing mother.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse should not be reduced to a generic claim that certain persons are especially sacred.

The Bhāṣya supplies two distinct structures:

```text
benefactor relation
    → parent
    → present embodiment arises through them

quality-field relation
    → arhat
    → extraordinary spiritual qualities
```

Karmic gravity is therefore field-relative and relation-sensitive.

The maternity discussion strengthens this point. The karmic identity of the field is not exhausted by present bodily appearance or even by present social function. For this technical classification, the determining relation is the causal history of origination.

## Logical Determination

Let:

```text
A = agent
P = parent
R = arhat
E = present embodied existence of A
```

Then:

```text
OriginatesFrom(E, P)
    → BenefactorRelation(P, A)
```

and:

```text
PossessesExceptionalQualities(R)
    → QualityField(R)
```

The karmic gravity of killing is therefore not determined solely by:

```text
Kills(A, x)
```

but by:

```text
Kills(A, x)
+ ConstitutiveRelation(A, x)
+ FieldCharacter(x)
```

For parental identity:

```text
ChangeOfSexCharacteristic(P)
    does not imply
LossOfParentalRelation(P, A)
```

And in the exceptional maternity case:

```text
GeneticMaternalOrigin(A, M1)
    → TechnicalMotherForAnantarya(M1)

GestatesAndRaises(A, M2)
    → PracticalMaternalRelation(M2)
```

The Bhāṣya thus distinguishes kinds of relation instead of collapsing them into a single predicate `Mother`.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation was broadly correct, but the Bhāṣya requires three refinements:

1. `upakāri` and `guṇakṣetra` are two different grounds of gravity rather than one vague class of exceptional objects;
2. `nirākṛti` is explicitly glossed as `parityāga`, abandonment or repudiation;
3. the final maternity statement is specifically a rule for determining the technical ānantarya relation in an exceptional embryological case.

A more precise translation is:

> Ānantarya arises through repudiating those who are one's constitutive benefactors and through destroying a field of exceptional qualities. A parental relation persists even when sexual characteristics change; and in the exceptional case of divided genetic and gestational maternity, the technical mother is the woman from whose maternal generative substance the being arose.

## Light Organon Contact

The text gives us a disciplined case of identity through genesis:

```text
present predicate
    ≠ complete determination of relation
```

A later change in visible bodily determination does not cancel a relation constituted through prior genesis.

Likewise, the two-mother case shows that one ordinary name can conceal several distinct relations:

```text
Mother
    → genetic / causal relation
    → gestational relation
    → nurturing relation
```

The Bhāṣya does not confuse them. It selects the causal-genetic relation for the technical ānantarya determination while retaining the practical truth of the nurturing relation.

That is a strong example of the Kośa's preference for precise relational determination over merely nominal classification.
