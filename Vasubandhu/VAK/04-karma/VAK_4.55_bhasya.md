# VAK_4.55_bhasya — Present-Life Fruition and Closure of a Karmic Field

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.55**

```iast
dṛṣṭadharmaphalaṃ karma kṣetrāśayaviśeṣataḥ |
tadbhūmyatyantavairāgyād vipāke niyataṃ hi yat ||
```

> Karma may bear fruit in the present life because of an exceptional field or an exceptional intention; and, through complete detachment from its corresponding level, karma fixed with respect to maturation may also become present-life-result karma.

See: [VAK_4.55.md](./VAK_4.55.md)

## Source Cautions

The Bhāṣya distinguishes three different routes to present-life fruition:

```text
exceptional field
exceptional intention
complete detachment from the karma's bhūmi
```

The third route is narrowly qualified. The rule applies to karma that is **fixed with respect to maturation (`vipāke niyata`) but not already fixed with respect to another temporal stage (`avasthā`)**.

Therefore complete detachment does not simply accelerate every outstanding karma.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first gives two comparatively direct causes of `dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya` karma.

### 1. Exceptional field

An action may ripen in the present life because the karmic field is exceptional (`kṣetraviśeṣa`). The Bhāṣya cites the reported transformation of sexual characteristics resulting from conduct directed toward the Saṃgha.

The important doctrinal point is not the anecdote itself but the causal form:

```text
exceptional recipient-field
    → exceptional karmic efficacy
    → present-life result
```

### 2. Exceptional intention

Present-life fruition may also occur because of an exceptional underlying intention (`āśayaviśeṣa`). Vasubandhu cites the case of a eunuch who releases bulls from castration and thereby acquires male characteristics.

Again the general structure is:

```text
exceptional āśaya
    → intensified causal efficacy
    → present-life result
```

### 3. Complete detachment from the karmic bhūmi

The most important case is `tadbhūmyatyantavairāgya`: complete detachment from the level to which a wholesome or unwholesome karma belongs.

If the karma is fixed in maturation but its temporal stage is not fixed, the impending elimination of that bhūmi as a possible future field forces the determinate maturation to occur in the present existence.

```text
karma belongs to bhūmi X
karma is vipāka-niyata
future time of maturation is not independently fixed
complete detachment from X occurs

therefore
    maturation becomes dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya
```

Vasubandhu then explicitly excludes two other cases.

If a karma is already fixed to another temporal stage, it must ripen there; complete detachment from that bhūmi cannot occur beforehand.

If karma is indeterminate, complete detachment does not force maturation at all. Rather:

```text
aniyata karma
    + complete detachment
        → non-maturation may occur
```

Thus the Path does not merely accelerate karma. It discriminates among pending causal determinations according to their modal status.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory argument throughout.

## New Technical Terms

- **kṣetraviśeṣa** — exceptional or specially powerful karmic field
  - **status:** developed from the `guṇakṣetra` analysis of VAK 4.54
  - **function:** explains one route to present-life fruition

- **āśayaviśeṣa** — exceptional underlying intention, disposition, or purposive orientation
  - **status:** newly decisive here
  - **function:** agent-side condition for accelerated maturation

- **atyantavairāgya** — complete detachment from a bhūmi
  - **status:** previously encountered as a path/domain relation
  - **function:** closes the relevant bhūmi as a future maturation-field

- **vipāke niyata** — fixed specifically with respect to maturation
  - **status:** developed from VAK 4.50–4.51
  - **function:** identifies the class of karma that must still mature when its future field is closed

- **avasthā-niyata** — fixed with respect to temporal stage
  - **status:** developed explicitly in VAK 4.51
  - **function:** excludes karma already assigned to a later temporal stage from the present acceleration rule

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.55 distinguishes **fixity of maturation** from **immediacy of maturation**.

```text
niyata
    → maturation is necessary

dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya
    → maturation occurs in the present existence
```

These are not identical.

Present-life fruition can be generated by exceptional causal intensity:

```text
field-side
    kṣetraviśeṣa

agent-side
    āśayaviśeṣa
```

or by the closure of a future karmic field:

```text
complete detachment from bhūmi X
    ↓
X no longer available as a future field
    ↓
if maturation is necessary
and its later temporal stage is not fixed
    ↓
result must occur now
```

But if maturation itself is not necessary:

```text
aniyata
    + closure of future field
        → result may simply never occur
```

This is the crucial Bhāṣya refinement.

## Logical Determination

The verse now requires three independent variables:

```text
MATURATION-NECESSITY
    fixed | unfixed

TEMPORAL-STAGE
    fixed | unfixed

FIELD-AVAILABILITY
    open | permanently closed
```

The central rule is:

```text
IF
    vipāka = fixed
    temporal-stage = unfixed
    maturation-field X = permanently closing
THEN
    maturation-time = present-life
```

But:

```text
IF vipāka = unfixed
AND field X closes
THEN maturation may be cancelled
```

and:

```text
IF temporal-stage = later-stage-fixed
THEN field X cannot be completely abandoned before that maturation
```

This is a genuine modal system rather than a simple chronology.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation is basically sound, but the final clause should make the modal restriction explicit.

Preferred translation:

> **Karma may bear fruit in the present life because of an exceptional field or an exceptional intention. It may also do so through complete detachment from the level to which it belongs—provided that its maturation is fixed, while its temporal stage is not independently fixed.**

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is the relation between **determinate content** and **closure of its future field of actualization**.

```text
necessary result
    + future placement still open
        → deferred actuality possible

necessary result
    + future placement permanently closed
        → present actuality
```

But the Bhāṣya prevents overgeneralization:

```text
mere possibility
    + closure of future field
        → cancellation is possible
```

So the Path does not indiscriminately actualize every karmic tendency. It separates what **must** be resolved from what can cease without fruition.

Bhāṣya pass for VAK 4.55. Clarifies three causes of present-life fruition—exceptional field, exceptional intention, and complete detachment from the relevant bhūmi—and sharpens the last rule by distinguishing karma fixed in maturation from karma fixed in temporal stage and from indeterminate karma.
