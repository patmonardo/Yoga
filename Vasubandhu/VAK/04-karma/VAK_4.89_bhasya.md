# VAK 4.89 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**aśubhasya śubhādyā dve trīṇi catvāry anukramam |**  
**avyākṛtasya dve trīṇi trīṇi caite śubhādayaḥ || 4.89 ||**

> For unwholesome karma, the results beginning with the wholesome are respectively two, three, and four. For neutral karma, these results beginning with the wholesome are two, three, and three.

[Kārikā analysis](./VAK_4.89.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
aśubhasya śubhādyā dve trīṇi catvāryanukramam /

yathākramamityarthaḥ /
akuśalasya karmaṇaḥ kuśalā dharmā dve puruṣakārādhipatiphale /
akuśalāstrīṇi vipākavisaṃyogaphale hitvā /
avyākṛtāścatvāri visaṃyogaphalaṃ hitvā /
avyākṛte hi satkāyāntagrāhadṛṣṭī akuśalānāṃ sarvatragāṇāṃ duḥkhadarśanaheyānāṃ ca niḥṣyandaphalam /

avyākṛtasya dve trīṇi trīṇi caite śubhādayaḥ // 4.89 //

avyākṛtasya karmaṇaḥ kuśalā dharmā dve puruṣakārādhipatiphale /
akuśalāḥ trīṇi vipākavisaṃyogaphale hitvā /
akuśalā hi duḥkhadarśanādiheyā avyākṛtayordṛṣṭyoniḥṣyandaphalam /
avyākṛtā dharmā etānyeva trīṇi /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya divides the verse into two causal rows:

1. unwholesome karma as source;
2. neutral karma as source.

Within each row, results are considered in the same order:

```text
wholesome
unwholesome
neutral
```

The Bhāṣya then supplies the exact fruit-types corresponding to the numerical counts.

## Literal Prose Translation

“For unwholesome karma, [the results] beginning with wholesome are two, three, and four respectively.”

That is, for unwholesome karma, wholesome dharmas are two results: the activity-produced result and the dominant result. Unwholesome dharmas are three results, excluding maturation and disconnection. Neutral dharmas are four results, excluding disconnection. For the neutral [dharmas], the personality-view and extreme-view are an outflow-result of the unwholesome universally pervasive dharmas and of those abandoned by seeing suffering.

“For neutral karma, these [results] beginning with wholesome are two, three, and three.”

For neutral karma, wholesome dharmas are two results: the activity-produced result and the dominant result. Unwholesome dharmas are three results, excluding maturation and disconnection. For unwholesome dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering and the like are an outflow-result of the two neutral views. Neutral dharmas are these same three results.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Unwholesome karma → wholesome results

The two admissible relations are:

```text
puruṣakāra-phala
adhipati-phala
```

Thus unwholesome karma can stand in a productive or dominant causal relation to wholesome dharmas without thereby producing a wholesome maturation.

### 2. Unwholesome karma → unwholesome results

The Bhāṣya says:

```text
three fruits
excluding:
    vipāka
    visaṃyoga
```

Therefore:

```text
niṣyanda
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

are the three admissible relations.

### 3. Unwholesome karma → neutral results

The Bhāṣya gives four fruits, excluding only `visaṃyoga`:

```text
vipāka
niṣyanda
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

This is the broadest result-set in the unwholesome row.

The Bhāṣya supplies a special justification for `niṣyanda`: the neutral `satkāya-dṛṣṭi` and `antagrāha-dṛṣṭi` can be outflow-results of unwholesome universally pervasive dharmas and of dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering.

So ethical difference between source and result does not block an outflow-relation where the doctrinal causal lineage permits it.

### 4. Neutral karma → wholesome results

Again the two are:

```text
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

No maturation, outflow, or disconnection relation is admitted here.

### 5. Neutral karma → unwholesome results

The Bhāṣya again gives three fruits, excluding:

```text
vipāka
visaṃyoga
```

Hence:

```text
niṣyanda
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

The reason for `niṣyanda` is important: unwholesome dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering and the other truths can be outflow-results of the two neutral views, `satkāya-dṛṣṭi` and `antagrāha-dṛṣṭi`.

Thus causal resemblance is not reducible to ethical identity.

### 6. Neutral karma → neutral results

The Bhāṣya says these are the same three:

```text
niṣyanda
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

Again `vipāka` and `visaṃyoga` are excluded.

## Completed 4.88–4.89 Matrix

The numerical table is now fully determined by the Bhāṣya:

| Source karma | Wholesome result-dharmas | Unwholesome result-dharmas | Neutral result-dharmas |
|---|---|---|---|
| wholesome | niṣyanda, visaṃyoga, puruṣakāra, adhipati | puruṣakāra, adhipati | vipāka, puruṣakāra, adhipati |
| unwholesome | puruṣakāra, adhipati | niṣyanda, puruṣakāra, adhipati | vipāka, niṣyanda, puruṣakāra, adhipati |
| neutral | puruṣakāra, adhipati | niṣyanda, puruṣakāra, adhipati | niṣyanda, puruṣakāra, adhipati |

The visible counts are therefore projections of a deeper three-place relation:

```text
source quality
    ×
fruit-type
    ×
result quality
```

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, reporting the Abhidharma causal classification.

No explicit objection is introduced in this passage. The prose is explanatory and justificatory.

## New Technical Terms

- **satkāya-dṛṣṭi** — view of a real self/personality in the aggregates; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** supplies a neutral dharma capable of serving as an outflow-result in the matrix.
- **antagrāha-dṛṣṭi** — extreme view; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** paired with `satkāya-dṛṣṭi` as a neutral view in explaining cross-quality `niṣyanda` relations.
- **sarvatraga** — universally pervasive; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** identifies the unwholesome causal class from which the neutral views may arise as outflow-results.
- **duḥkha-darśana-heya** — to be abandoned by seeing suffering; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** specifies another causal class relevant to the `niṣyanda` explanation.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.89 confirms that ethical quality and causal relation are independent axes.

An unwholesome cause may have a wholesome dharma as `puruṣakāra-phala` or `adhipati-phala`. A neutral cause may have an unwholesome dharma as `niṣyanda-phala`. Therefore one must never infer the quality of a result-dharma simply from the quality of its source karma.

The matrix also clarifies the special restrictions of the five fruits:

```text
vipāka
    appears only where the source can produce maturation

visaṃyoga
    appears only in specifically liberative abandoning relations

niṣyanda
    requires a doctrinal relation of causal continuity or resemblance,
    not necessarily identical ethical quality

puruṣakāra + adhipati
    are the most broadly distributed relations
```

## Logical Determination

The causal system is not adequately represented by:

```text
CauseQuality → ResultQuality
```

It requires:

```text
CauseQuality
    ×
FruitRelation
    ×
ResultQuality
```

This explains why two cells with the same number can contain different fruit-relations and why different ethical qualities can still participate in an outflow relation.

The strongest result is:

```text
qualitative identity
    is not the principle of karmic causality
```

Causal classification is relational and mediated.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation and numerical table are correct, but the Bhāṣya now fixes the exact content of every cell. The Super Kārikās pass should preserve the concise matrix while replacing the earlier “await Bhāṣya” notes with these exact memberships.
