# VAK_4.68_bhasya — Preparation, Principal Completion, and Aftermath

## Kārikā

**VAK 4.68**

**IAST**

*sāmantakās tu vijñaptir avijñaptir bhaven na vā /*  
*viparyayeṇa pṛṣṭhāni prayogas tu trimūlajaḥ // 4.68 //*

**Translation**

The proximate preparatory actions are necessarily manifest action; non-manifest action may or may not accompany them. The subsequent actions are the converse. Preparation may arise from any of the three unwholesome roots.

## Bhāṣya Translation

The proximate actions surrounding a karmic course are necessarily of the nature of manifest action. Non-manifest action may or may not occur. If one begins the preparation under an intense afflictive domination, or with heedlessness of especially concentrated force, non-manifest action arises; otherwise it does not.

The subsequent actions of karmic courses are to be understood conversely to the proximate actions. They are necessarily of the nature of non-manifest action, while manifest action may or may not occur. If, after completing the karmic course, one again behaves in a manner conforming to it, manifest action occurs; otherwise it does not.

As for the extent of preparation, principal action, and aftermath: suppose someone wants to kill an animal. From the time he rises, takes money, goes out, handles the animal, buys it, brings it back, fattens it, places it where it will be killed, takes up the weapon, and strikes one or two blows—so long as he has not deprived it of life, all this is preparation.

The manifest action associated with the blow by which he deprives it of life, together with the non-manifest determination arising at that moment, constitutes the principal karmic course.

After that, the succeeding moments of non-manifest determination are aftermath. And while he cuts up the animal, cleans it, sells it, cooks it, eats it, or recounts the deed approvingly, those moments of manifest action are also aftermath.

This model is to be applied to the other courses as appropriate.

Covetousness, hostility, and false view, however, have neither preparation nor aftermath. Their mere coming into presence constitutes the karmic course.

Preparation for the unwholesome karmic courses may arise from any of the three unwholesome roots: greed, hatred, or delusion.

## Argument Segmentation

### 1. The preparatory phase is necessarily manifest

**Voice: Vasubandhu / doctrinal exposition**

The Bhāṣya first fixes the modal structure of the `sāmantaka`, the action adjacent to the principal karmapatha:

```text
sāmantaka / prayoga
    necessarily vijñapti
    optionally avijñapti
```

Preparation is action already underway. It therefore has an outward bodily or verbal manifestation. But it does not automatically generate a corresponding non-manifest determination. That depends upon the intensity and settled force of the afflictive condition.

The Kārikā's `bhaven na vā` is therefore not uncertainty in the doctrine. It is a genuine conditional distinction.

---

### 2. The aftermath has the converse structure

**Voice: Vasubandhu / doctrinal exposition**

The `pṛṣṭha`, what comes after the principal karmapatha, is explicitly said to be the converse:

```text
pṛṣṭha
    necessarily avijñapti
    optionally vijñapti
```

Once the principal karmic course has been completed, its non-manifest continuation persists. New outward manifestation occurs only if the agent continues to act in conformity with what was done.

Thus `viparyayeṇa` is exact:

```text
preparation
    vijñapti necessary
    avijñapti optional

principal course
    completion point

aftermath
    avijñapti necessary
    vijñapti optional
```

This is a major correction to any first-pass reading that treats preparation and aftermath merely as temporal labels. They possess different modal structures.

---

### 3. The Bhāṣya gives a genetic model of killing

**Voice: Vasubandhu / explanatory example**

The killing example is unusually detailed because it identifies a continuous sequence whose ethical status changes at a determinate boundary.

```text
intention to kill
    ↓
rising / acquiring means / going / buying / bringing / preparing
    ↓
taking up weapon / striking before death
    = prayoga

life actually destroyed
    = maula karmapatha

continuing non-manifest determination
    + cutting / cooking / eating / praising, when present
    = pṛṣṭha
```

The decisive distinction is therefore not simply chronological:

```text
before / during / after
```

but teleological in the limited technical sense of relation to completion:

```text
oriented toward completion
    → prayoga

that which fulfills the karmic course
    → maula

that which continues after fulfillment
    → pṛṣṭha
```

The `maula` is the point at which the course attains its defining result.

---

### 4. Manifest act and karmic completion need not be the same analytical object

**Voice: Objector / Vaibhāṣika response / Vasubandhu's critical presentation**

The Bhāṣya then presses a difficult question: in killing, is the principal karmapatha present while the victim is in the process of dying, or only once the victim is dead?

If it is already principal while the victim is still dying, unacceptable consequences seem to follow. If it exists only after death, the earlier statement identifying the fatal blow's manifest action as principal becomes difficult.

The text reports a Vaibhāṣika attempt to reconcile the terminology by allowing `prayoga` in a cited śāstra passage to designate the principal act itself. Vasubandhu exposes the resulting pressure on the account of `vijñapti` and `avijñapti`.

The safest reconstruction is:

```text
physical execution
    may precede
result-completion

result-completion
    fixes the principal karmic status
```

The discussion therefore reinforces the distinction already encountered in 4.67:

```text
moment of outward performance
    ≠ necessarily
moment at which karmic completion is analytically fixed
```

The Bhāṣya does not let a visible bodily movement alone define the completed karmapatha.

---

### 5. One karmapatha may function as preparation or aftermath for another

**Voice: Vasubandhu / doctrinal exposition**

The Bhāṣya states that a karmapatha may itself become another karmapatha's preparation or aftermath.

For killing, even all ten karmapathas can function as preparation. Vasubandhu gives a constructed example in which an enemy's destruction is prepared through sacrifice, theft, sexual misconduct, lying, divisive speech, harsh speech, frivolous speech, covetousness, hostility, and false view.

This prevents us from treating the ten karmapathas as ten isolated boxes.

```text
karmapatha A
    can become a functional phase of
karmapatha B
```

Their identity and their role in a larger karmic process must be distinguished.

---

### 6. The three mental courses are structurally exceptional

**Voice: Vasubandhu / doctrinal exposition**

`abhidhyā`, `vyāpāda`, and `mithyādṛṣṭi` do not possess `prayoga` or `pṛṣṭha` in the same sense.

> *saṃmukhībhāvamātrāt karmapathaḥ*

Their **mere coming into presence** is the karmapatha.

Thus:

```text
bodily / verbal karmapatha
    can be temporally articulated
    prayoga → maula → pṛṣṭha

mental karmapatha
    direct presence itself
    = karmapatha
```

This is another important asymmetry. The three-phase structure is not a universal template imposed indiscriminately on all ten.

---

### 7. Preparation may arise from all three unwholesome roots

**Voice: Vasubandhu / doctrinal exposition**

The final pāda gives the central thesis:

> *prayogas tu trimūlajaḥ*

Preparation may arise from:

```text
lobha   greed

dveṣa  hatred

moha    delusion
```

The Bhāṣya explains that the sūtra's statement that the karmic courses are greed-born, hatred-born, or delusion-born should not be read as saying that the **completion of every karmapatha** can occur through all three roots. The statement applies particularly to their arising or preparatory genesis.

This distinction between genesis and completion becomes crucial for the verses that follow.

---

## Examples of the Three Roots in Preparation

The Bhāṣya begins illustrating killing:

### Greed-born killing

One kills for bodily parts, money, payment, personal advantage, or the protection of oneself or one's associates.

### Hatred-born killing

One kills in order to injure or destroy an enemy.

### Delusion-born killing

One kills because one believes the act religiously or morally justified—for example, through sacrificial doctrine, authoritative teaching, a theory of punitive righteousness, or a false view that killing certain beings is meritorious or proper.

This third category is philosophically especially important:

```text
moha
    does not merely mean absence of information

moha
    can organize a normative worldview
    in which harmful action appears righteous
```

Thus false principle can generate perfectly deliberate action.

## New Technical Terms

### `sāmantaka`

- **Provisional meaning:** proximate or adjoining action surrounding the principal karmapatha; here substantially the preparatory phase.
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā but clarified substantially by the Bhāṣya.
- **Function:** distinguishes action oriented toward completion from the completed karmapatha itself.

### `prayoga`

- **Provisional meaning:** preparatory undertaking or execution prior to fulfillment of the karmapatha.
- **Status:** established term, now given a detailed temporal-functional analysis.
- **Function:** names the genetic phase leading toward principal completion.

### `maula karmapatha`

- **Provisional meaning:** the principal or root karmic course at the point its defining result is fulfilled.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-explicit and central to this passage.
- **Function:** fixes the completion boundary of karmic action.

### `pṛṣṭha`

- **Provisional meaning:** posterior action, aftermath, or continuation following principal completion.
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā; now precisely defined.
- **Function:** distinguishes the continuing karmic determination and any conforming subsequent manifestation from the principal act.

### `anudharma`

- **Provisional meaning:** behavior conforming to or continuing in the character of a completed karmapatha.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-only in this immediate analysis.
- **Function:** explains when aftermath again includes manifest action.

### `paryavasthāna`

- **Provisional meaning:** dominating or enveloping afflictive condition.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya terminology.
- **Function:** helps explain when preparation generates avijñapti as well as vijñapti.

### `saṃmukhībhāva`

- **Provisional meaning:** direct presence, immediate occurrence, coming face-to-face in the mental continuum.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-only in this immediate unit.
- **Function:** explains why the three mental karmapathas have no separate preparation or aftermath.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.68 establishes that karmic action cannot be analyzed adequately as a single punctual event.

For bodily and verbal courses there is a determinate process:

```text
prayoga
    preparation toward fulfillment
        ↓
maula
    principal completion
        ↓
pṛṣṭha
    posterior continuation
```

But these phases are not merely successive temporal regions. They differ in mode of realization:

```text
prayoga
    vijñapti necessary
    avijñapti contingent

pṛṣṭha
    avijñapti necessary
    vijñapti contingent
```

The principal course is defined through fulfillment of the action's decisive result.

The mental courses introduce a limit to this model:

```text
abhidhyā / vyāpāda / mithyādṛṣṭi
    no distinct prayoga
    no distinct pṛṣṭha
    direct occurrence = karmapatha
```

Finally, causal genesis and final completion must be distinguished. The preparatory process may be generated by greed, hatred, or delusion even where the completed karmapatha has a more restricted relation to its root.

---

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation of `sāmantaka` as "proximate or preliminary actions" is substantially confirmed, but the Bhāṣya shows that these are not merely nearby acts. They are acts **functionally ordered toward completion**.

`pṛṣṭha` is best rendered "aftermath" or "subsequent phase," provided we preserve its doctrinal content: the non-manifest karmic determination necessarily continues after completion, while fresh manifestation occurs only when subsequent behavior conforms to the completed action.

`trimūlaja` should be read genetically:

> **born from any of the three roots**

rather than as saying that all three roots equally characterize the final completed nature of every karmapatha.

---

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya introduces a robust distinction between **genesis, completion, and persistence**.

```text
Ground / root
    ↓
Preparation
    ↓
Manifest execution
    ↓
Result-fulfillment
    ↓
Principal karmic determination
    ↓
Persistent aftermath
```

Three dimensions therefore have to be kept distinct:

```text
temporal phase
    before / completion / after

mode of realization
    vijñapti / avijñapti

causal root
    lobha / dveṣa / moha
```

No one of these dimensions can simply substitute for the others.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya gives us a genuinely genetic model rather than a static moral predicate.

An action is not philosophically exhausted by saying:

```text
"this event is killing"
```

The complete determination requires:

```text
what generated the course
what prepared it
what outwardly manifested it
what fulfilled it
what persisted after fulfillment
```

This is especially clear in the technical dispute over killing: **appearance and completion are analytically separable**. The visible blow is not by itself sufficient to settle the full karmic determination; the course is fixed through the relation between action and realized result.

The Organon contact point is therefore restrained but strong:

```text
Genesis
    ≠
Appearance
    ≠
Completion
    ≠
Persistence
```

A determination becomes intelligible only through the mediated process connecting these moments.

---

## Carry Forward

VAK 4.68 leaves us with four distinctions needed for the next verses:

1. `prayoga`, `maula`, and `pṛṣṭha` are functionally distinct phases.
2. `vijñapti` and `avijñapti` distribute differently across those phases.
3. the three mental karmapathas do not share the same temporal articulation.
4. the root that generates preparation must be distinguished from the root associated with completion.

VAK 4.69 will now turn to the corresponding genesis of the **wholesome** karmapathas.
