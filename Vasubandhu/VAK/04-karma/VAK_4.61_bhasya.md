# VAK 4.61 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā Anchor

```text
dharmakṣāntiṣu vairāgye cānantaryapathāṣṭake /
yā cetanā dvādaśadhā karma kṛṣṇakṣayāya tat // VAK_4.61 //
```

> The volition occurring in the dharma-patiences and in the eight uninterrupted paths of detachment is twelvefold; that karma is for the exhaustion of black karma.

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya first asks whether every anāsrava karma serves to exhaust all three preceding karmic classes:

```text
kiṃ punaḥ sarvam anāsravaṃ karma sarvasyāsya trividhasya karmaṇaḥ kṣayāya saṃvartate /
nety ucyate /
kiṃ tarhi /
```

No. Vasubandhu then specifies the twelvefold karma of VAK 4.61:

```text
darśanamārge catasṛṣu dharmajñānakṣāntiṣu
kāmavairāgye cāṣṭāsv ānantaryamārgeṣu
yā cetanā seyaṃ dvādaśavidhā cetanā
kṛṣṇasya karmaṇaḥ prahāṇāya karma /
```

## Source Cautions

The verse is highly compressed. The Bhāṣya fixes three points that should not be supplied from the Kārikā alone:

1. `dharmakṣānti` means the four `dharmajñānakṣānti` of the Path of Seeing.
2. The eight uninterrupted paths are specifically the first eight `ānantaryamārga` involved in detachment from the desire-domain (`kāmavairāgya`).
3. The twelvefold item is `cetanā` itself, functioning as karma for the abandonment (`prahāṇa`) of black karma.

The text does not say here that all anāsrava karma indiscriminately destroys all prior karma. It explicitly rejects that generalization.

## Natural Argument

The preceding verse established:

```text
anāsrava karma
    → karmakṣayāya
```

VAK 4.61 now asks for the exact extension of that claim.

The Bhāṣya proceeds:

```text
Is every anāsrava karma
for the exhaustion of every one
of the three ordinary karmic classes?

No.

For black karma specifically:

4 dharma-knowledge patiences
    on the Path of Seeing
+
8 uninterrupted paths
    of desire-domain detachment
=
12 volitions
```

Thus the fourth karmic class of VAK 4.60 is not a homogeneous undifferentiated negation of ordinary karma. Its karmic function is internally articulated according to the particular karma being abandoned.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Objector / questioner**

> Is every anāsrava karma for the exhaustion of all three kinds of karma?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> No. Rather, the twelvefold volition specified here is karma for the abandonment of black karma.

## New Technical Terms

- **dharmajñānakṣānti** — dharma-knowledge patience; **status:** previously encountered in Path material; **function:** four Path-of-Seeing loci whose associated volitions participate in abandoning black karma.
- **ānantaryamārga** — uninterrupted path; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** active abandoning phase in the graded detachment sequence.
- **kāmavairāgya** — detachment from the desire-domain; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** domain in which the first eight uninterrupted paths contribute to the twelvefold black-karma-exhausting karma.
- **prahāṇa** — abandonment; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** the Bhāṣya's explicit gloss on `kṣaya` in this passage.

## Doctrinal Determination

The twelve are generated exactly:

```text
Path of Seeing
    4 dharmajñānakṣānti

Desire-domain detachment
    first 8 ānantaryamārga

TOTAL
    12 cetanās
```

These twelve volitions are anāsrava karma whose specific function is:

```text
kṛṣṇasya karmaṇaḥ prahāṇāya
    for the abandonment of black karma
```

This is a path-specific functional classification. The relevant karma is not classified by a future pleasant or painful vipāka but by what it removes.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.60 introduced a new causal orientation:

```text
ordinary karma
    → produces vipāka

anāsrava karma
    → conduces to karmakṣaya
```

VAK 4.61 now differentiates that second relation:

```text
not:
ANĀSRAVA(K) → exhausts every ordinary karma indiscriminately

but:
PATH-STAGE(K)
    determines
TARGET-OF-ABANDONMENT(K)
```

For this verse:

```text
4 seeing-path volitions
+
8 desire-detachment volitions
    ↓
black-karma abandonment
```

The path therefore has a typed negative efficacy. Its activity is determinate precisely by the determination it removes.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation is substantially confirmed, with one strengthening:

> **The volition present in the four dharma-knowledge patiences and in the first eight uninterrupted paths of desire-domain detachment is twelvefold; this is karma for the exhaustion—more precisely, the abandonment—of black karma.**

The Bhāṣya confirms that `kṣaya` here is to be understood through `prahāṇa`, abandonment.

## Organon Contact Point

The important structural result is that liberating activity is not an abstract cancellation of activity.

```text
ordinary karmic activity
    produces determinate continuation

path activity
    is itself determinate activity
    whose object is abandonment
```

And that abandonment is articulated:

```text
specific path-determination
    ↔ specific karmic determination removed
```

So the negation is not empty. It is a positive, stage-specific operation of the Path.

## Cross-References

- **VAK 4.60** — anāsrava karma as neither black nor white and as conducing to karmakṣaya.
- **VAK 4.62** — continues the distribution by identifying karma that exhausts black-and-white karma and white karma.
- **Path of Seeing** — four `dharmajñānakṣānti`.
- **kāmavairāgya** — nine uninterrupted-path moments, of which the first eight are assigned here and the ninth is treated in VAK 4.62.

## Commit Note

Bhāṣya pass for VAK 4.61: restricts the general karmakṣaya claim, derives the twelve volitions as four dharma-knowledge patiences plus eight desire-domain uninterrupted paths, and identifies their specific function as abandonment of black karma.
