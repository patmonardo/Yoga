## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya proceeds in three steps:

1. define the distinguished giver by internal qualities;
2. identify four concrete manners in which such a giver gives;
3. correlate those four manners, in order, with four determinate features of the fruit.

## Literal Prose Translation

A giver endowed with qualities such as faith, discipline, and learning is a distinguished giver. Because of that distinction of the giver in the act of giving, the production of the fruit is correspondingly distinguished.

Such a giver gives respectfully and in the other ways: respectfully, with his own hand, at the proper time, and without harming others.

Consequently, after giving in such ways, four distinctions arise for the giver respectively: he receives honor; he acquires delight in excellent enjoyments; he obtains enjoyments at the proper time and not after the proper time has passed; and he obtains enjoyments that are not liable to being cut off.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 4.114 announced three determinants of giving's distinction: giver, gift-object, and field. VAK 4.115 develops the first, `dātṛ-viśeṣa`.

The giver's distinction is first ethical-cognitive:

```text
śraddhā
+ śīla
+ śruta
+ related qualities
    ↓
dātṛ-viśeṣa
```

That internal distinction then becomes visible in the manner of giving:

```text
satkṛtya       respectfully
svahastam      personally / with one's own hand
kāle           at the proper time
parānupahatya  without injuring others
```

The result reproduces these practical determinations in ordered correspondence:

```text
respectful giving
    → receipt of honor

personal giving
    → delight in excellent enjoyments

timely giving
    → timely acquisition of enjoyments

giving without harming others
    → enjoyments not liable to interruption
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly says `yathākramaṃ`: the four fruits correspond in sequence to the four manners.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose defining the giver's qualities, the four manners of giving, and the four resultant distinctions.

## New Technical Terms

- **dātṛviśeṣa** — distinction or excellence of the giver.  
  **status:** previously encountered in 4.114.  
  **function:** one of the three determinants of the excellence of giving.

- **svahasta** — one's own hand; giving personally rather than merely delegating the act.  
  **status:** new in this local analysis.  
  **function:** manner of giving.

- **parānupahatya** — without injuring or harming others.  
  **status:** new in this local analysis.  
  **function:** ethical condition on the manner of giving.

- **anācchedya-bhoga** — enjoyments or possessions not liable to being cut off.  
  **status:** rare.  
  **function:** corresponding karmic result.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The verse gives a precise form of karmic correspondence. The fruit is not merely greater in undifferentiated quantity. Its *mode* reflects the mode of the deed.

```text
inner quality of agent
    ↓
mode of practical execution
    ↓
correspondingly structured maturation
```

This strengthens the broader doctrine already emerging from the giving sequence: karma preserves determinate relations. A deed is not evaluated only by the material thing transferred. The ethical constitution of the giver and the precise way in which the transfer is enacted alter the character of the maturation.

One important correction to the first-pass Kārikā analysis is needed. `udārarucitā` is not best treated as two independent results, “abundance” and “agreeableness.” The Bhāṣya glosses it as:

> `udāreṣu ca bhogeṣu ruciṃ labhate`

He acquires delight or taste **for excellent / abundant enjoyments**. The four fruits therefore remain four, not five.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

A tighter translation is:

> **A giver is distinguished by faith and the other qualities; therefore he gives respectfully and in the other proper ways. From this come, respectively, receiving honor, delight in excellent enjoyments, timely acquisition, and acquisition that cannot be cut off.**

The implied `ādi` after `satkṛtya` is explicitly unpacked by the Bhāṣya as:

- personally;
- at the proper time;
- without harming others.

## Light Organon Contact

The noteworthy structure is not a simple reward calculus but preservation of form:

```text
manner of relinquishment
    → manner of future availability
```

Respect returns as recognition; timeliness returns as timely availability; non-injury returns as stability against interruption. Karma here behaves as a practical rule preserving the qualitative structure of activity across temporal mediation.
