# VAK_4.29_bhasya — Functional Structure of the Eight Upavāsa Limbs

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.29**

```iast
śīlāṅgāny apramādāṅgaṃ vratāṅgāni yathākramam |
catvāry ekaṃ tathā trīṇi smṛtināśo madaś ca taiḥ ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> Respectively, four are limbs of discipline, one is a limb of non-negligence, and three are limbs of observance. Without the latter supporting limbs there would be destruction of recollection and intoxicated self-indulgence.

## Source Cautions

The e-text reads `aṣṭāṅganyupādīyante` at [214|03]; an expected normalized form would be `aṣṭāṅgāny upādīyante`. The transmitted form is preserved.

At [214|15-214|16] the source reads `nṛtyagotavāditraṃ`; context strongly suggests the familiar `nṛtyagītavāditraṃ`, but no silent correction is imposed.

At [214|21-214|22], the source has `bodhirvodhyaṅgaṃ`; this appears textually irregular. The exact e-text is retained.

The expression `vṛtāṅga` in the prose corresponds to the Kārikā's `vratāṅga`; the source wording is preserved rather than normalized.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu begins with a direct question: why are eight limbs undertaken in the upavāsa observance?

The answer is functional rather than merely enumerative. The eight are not a flat list. They divide into three groups according to what they do.

The first four—abstention from killing, taking what is not given, sexual activity, and false speech—are `śīlāṅga`, limbs of discipline, because they are abstentions from acts that are naturally blameworthy (`prakṛtisāvadya`). Their role is direct regulation of conduct.

The fifth, abstention from intoxicating drink, is the single `apramādāṅga`, limb of non-negligence. Vasubandhu's reason is concrete: even one who has undertaken discipline may become negligent if intoxicated. This limb therefore protects the operative presence of the discipline already undertaken.

The final three are `vṛtāṅga` / `vratāṅga`, limbs of the special observance, because they accord with `saṃvega`, the urgent or sober orientation that supports religious discipline.

Vasubandhu then asks what would happen if the non-negligence limb and the observance-limbs were omitted.

Drinking destroys recollection of what ought and ought not to be done. Participation in high or luxurious beds, dancing, singing, and related indulgences generates `mada`, a state of intoxicated self-inflation or exhilaration. Once `mada` arises, `dauḥśīlya`, breach of discipline, is near at hand.

The untimely-eating rule functions somewhat differently. By departing from one's customary meal-time, recollection of the upavāsa itself and `saṃvega` are brought to presence. If that departure were absent, both recollection of the observance and the supporting urgency could fail to arise.

The Bhāṣya therefore presents the supporting limbs as practical safeguards around the four directly ethical abstentions:

```text
śīla
    direct abstention from naturally blameworthy conduct

apramāda
    preserves recollection and guards against negligence

vrata
    supports saṃvega and blocks indulgent mada
```

The argument then turns to an alternative analysis. Some (`kecit`) hold that abstention from untimely eating is itself the upavāsa proper and that the remaining practices are its eight limbs, counting entertainment and adornment as two separate items.

Vasubandhu rejects this because the sūtra wording would no longer fit. The formula explicitly calls abstention from untimely eating the eighth limb. It cannot therefore be separated out as a distinct whole standing outside the eight.

An obvious question follows: if all eight are limbs, what is the upavāsa itself whose limbs they are?

The reply introduces an important whole–part determination: `samudāyasya avayavā aṅgāni` — the limbs are the parts of the aggregate. The upavāsa is the eightfold aggregate itself, just as a four-member army and a five-member musical ensemble are wholes articulated through their members.

Vasubandhu then accepts that one determination may be both the whole in one respect and a limb in another. Abstention from untimely eating can be called upavāsa and also an upavāsa-limb, just as right view can be called Path and also Path-limb.

The Vaibhāṣikas extend the analogy: investigation of dharmas can be both awakening and awakening-factor; samādhi can be both dhyāna and dhyāna-limb. The point is not that a thing becomes its own constituent by bare self-identity. Rather, earlier instances such as right view may function as constituent conditions of the later articulated whole. If this temporal-functional differentiation were denied, the noble Path arising in its first moment could not already be eightfold.

The passage then closes the internal analysis of the eight limbs and opens the next problem: whether upavāsa belongs only to one already established as an upāsaka or may also be acquired by another person.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks why the eight limbs are undertaken and explains their threefold functional classification.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explains the dangers of `smṛtināśa`, `mada`, and proximity to `dauḥśīlya`, and the positive function of meal-time restriction in sustaining `upavāsasmṛti` and `saṃvega`.

**Voice: uncertain / alternative interpreters (`kecit`)** — claim that abstention from untimely eating is itself the upavāsa and the remaining items are its limbs.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — rejects that proposal as incompatible with the sūtra wording and introduces the whole–part analysis.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — extends the whole/limb relation through the examples of right view, dharma-investigation, and samādhi.

**Voice: Vasubandhu or shared explanatory voice** — gives the temporal-functional reason why the same determination need not be its own limb in the same respect: earlier instances can be limbs of later ones; otherwise the noble Path could not be eightfold at its first arising.

## New Technical Terms

- **prakṛtisāvadya** — naturally blameworthy; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit classification term; **function:** grounds why the first four are specifically `śīlāṅga`.
- **apramādāṅga** — limb of non-negligence; **status:** present in the Kārikā but functionally explained only in the prose; **function:** protects undertaken discipline from intoxication-generated negligence.
- **saṃvega** — urgent, sober, religiously activating orientation; **status:** newly operative here; **function:** explains the purpose of the special observance-limbs.
- **kāryākāryasmṛti** — recollection of what should and should not be done; **status:** new compound in this sequence; **function:** specifies what intoxication destroys.
- **mada** — intoxicated self-inflation / exhilarated indulgence; **status:** recurring kleśa-related term; **function:** mediates from luxury/entertainment toward breach of discipline.
- **upavāsasmṛti** — recollection of the upavāsa observance; **status:** new local expression; **function:** positive cognitive support generated by departure from ordinary meal-time.
- **samudāya / avayava** — aggregate / constituent part; **status:** general logical vocabulary made technically operative here; **function:** resolves how the eight items are limbs of upavāsa without positing a ninth entity outside them.
- **aṅga** — limb / constituent; **status:** central Kārikā term; **function:** expresses functional membership in an articulated whole.

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Four limbs of discipline

```text
abstention from killing
abstention from taking what is not given
abstention from sexual activity
abstention from false speech
        ↓
śīlāṅga
```

Their common ground is:

```text
prakṛtisāvadya-vairati
    abstention from what is naturally blameworthy
```

### 2. One limb of non-negligence

```text
abstention from intoxicants
        ↓
prevents pramāda
        ↓
preserves operative śīla
```

The Bhāṣya makes the dependence explicit:

```text
śīla undertaken
    + intoxication
        → pramāda remains possible
```

Therefore discipline requires a safeguarding condition in addition to direct ethical prohibitions.

### 3. Three observance-limbs

```text
untimely eating
luxury / entertainment / adornment
high or luxurious beds and seats
        ↓
vratāṅga
        ↓
saṃvegānuguṇa
```

They do not merely add further moral prohibitions of the same type as the first four. They shape the temporary mode of life appropriate to the observance.

### 4. Functional dangers they prevent

```text
intoxicants
    → smṛtināśa
    → loss of what should / should not be done

luxury and entertainment
    → mada
    → dauḥśīlya becomes near
```

Conversely:

```text
departure from ordinary meal-time
    → upavāsasmṛti
    + saṃvega
```

Thus the eightfold structure includes both direct restraint and conditions for sustaining restraint.

### 5. Upavāsa as articulated whole

The alternative view would make:

```text
abstention from untimely eating
    = upavāsa proper

remaining practices
    = eight limbs
```

But Vasubandhu rejects this on scriptural grounds.

Instead:

```text
upavāsa
    = eightfold samudāya

its eight observances
    = avayava / aṅga
```

There is no need to posit a ninth upavāsa-entity beyond the eight limbs.

### 6. Whole and limb can overlap by function

```text
samyagdṛṣṭi
    Path
    and Path-limb

akālabhojana-prativirati
    upavāsa
    and upavāsa-limb
```

This is not a flat identity relation. The Bhāṣya's later qualification introduces temporal-functional articulation:

```text
earlier determination
    → constituent of
later articulated whole
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and slight revision.**

The Kārikā file translated the final pāda as though `smṛtināśa` and `mada` were both simply "prevented through these." The Bhāṣya is more precise.

`smṛtināśa` is specifically tied to intoxication: drinking causes recollection of what should and should not be done to perish.

`mada` is specifically tied to luxury, entertainment, high beds, and related indulgence. Untimely eating is explained positively: its restriction sustains `upavāsasmṛti` and `saṃvega`.

A better philosophical translation is therefore:

> Respectively, four are limbs of discipline, one is a limb of non-negligence, and three are limbs of observance. Without these supporting limbs, recollection would be destroyed and intoxicated self-indulgence would arise.

This preserves the compressed Kārikā while the Bhāṣya supplies the differentiated causal assignments.

The Kārikā pass was correct that the eight form a functional rather than flat list, but the Bhāṣya gives the exact grounds of the three groups.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.29 reveals that an ethical-practical whole contains determinations with different logical functions.

```text
primary prohibition
    ≠
condition preserving the prohibition
    ≠
form of life supporting the prohibition
```

Yet all three belong internally to one observance.

The strongest logical clarification arises in the `samudāya / avayava` discussion:

```text
whole
    is not an entity added to its limbs

whole
    = articulated unity of its limbs
```

At the same time, functional membership prevents reduction to mere extensional aggregation:

```text
aṅga
    = constituent insofar as it functions within the whole
```

The right-view example adds a temporal determination:

```text
same determination-type
    can be whole-character in one relation
    and limb-character in another
```

without requiring crude self-membership.

## Organon Contact Point

The safe contact point is the distinction between **constitutive**, **preservative**, and **supporting** moments of a practical form:

```text
śīla
    determines the prohibited conduct directly

apramāda
    preserves the determination against breakdown

vrata
    organizes the surrounding life-context
    in which the determination can remain effective
```

This is already generated by Vasubandhu's own reasoning and does not require an imported analogy.

The whole–part passage is especially valuable:

```text
practical whole
    does not stand behind its members
    as an additional ninth thing

it exists as
    their articulated functional unity
```

No stronger Organon reconstruction is necessary in this pass.
