# VAK_4.53_bhasya — Twenty-Twofold Projection in the Intermediate Existence

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.53**

```iast
dvāviṃśatividhaṃ kāmeṣv ākṣipaty antarābhavaḥ |
dṛṣṭadharmaphalaṃ tac ca nikāyo hy eka eva saḥ ||
```

> In the desire domain the intermediate existence projects a twenty-twofold karmic range. Its determinate fruit is still classed as belonging to the present existence, because that continuum is one and the same.

## Source Cautions

The source's `dvāviṃśatividham` is not a loose rhetorical number. The Bhāṣya derives it exactly.

The first-pass Kārikā file correctly identified the importance of continuity between `antarābhava` and rebirth existence, but it left the internal derivation of twenty-two for the Bhāṣya pass. That derivation can now be made explicit.

The e-text's `gāmāvacara` is an orthographic form for the desire-domain (`kāmāvacara`) intermediate existence.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu begins with a question:

```text
Can karma also be projected in the intermediate existence?
```

The answer is yes.

The desire-domain intermediate existence can project karma relative to eleven experiential stages.

First come five embryonic stages (`garbhāvasthā`):

```text
1. kalala
2. arbuda
3. peśī
4. ghana
5. praśākhā
```

Then five post-birth stages (`jātāvasthā`):

```text
6. bāla
7. kumāra
8. yuvan
9. madhya
10. vṛddha
```

And the intermediate existence itself supplies the eleventh:

```text
11. antarābhava
```

For each of these eleven targets, the karma can be either:

```text
niyata
    determinate

aniyata
    indeterminate
```

Hence:

```text
11 target-stages × 2 modal statuses = 22
```

Thus the twenty-twofold classification is generated systematically rather than merely enumerated.

The Bhāṣya then asks how the determinate half of this set is temporally classified. The answer is striking: the eleven determinate karmas are all `dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya` — to be experienced in the present existence.

Why?

Because the intermediate existence and the ten following developmental stages belong to **one and the same `nikāyasabhāga`**, projected by one karma.

```text
antarābhava
    + five embryonic stages
    + five post-birth stages
        = one nikāyasabhāga
```

Therefore a karma produced in the intermediate existence and maturing only in childhood, youth, maturity, or old age still matures within the same karmically projected continuum.

The criterion for `dṛṣṭadharma` is therefore not merely a crude count of visible bodily phases. It is indexed to continuity of the same projected `nikāyasabhāga`.

Vasubandhu closes by explaining why no separate temporal class called `antarābhavavedanīya` is required: the intermediate existence itself is projected by `upapadyavedanīya` karma, while karmas produced within it and maturing within its associated continuum fall under `dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya`.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — question, enumeration, and explanation throughout.

## New Technical Terms

- **garbhāvasthā** — embryonic or intrauterine developmental stage
  - **status:** cosmological/embryological terminology previously encountered in the Kośa
  - **function:** supplies five of the eleven target-stages for karmic maturation

- **jātāvasthā** — post-birth stage of life
  - **status:** developmental terminology
  - **function:** supplies five further target-stages

- **kalala / arbuda / peśī / ghana / praśākhā** — five embryonic stages
  - **status:** technical embryological vocabulary
  - **function:** differentiates possible maturation-targets within one projected continuum

- **bāla / kumāra / yuvan / madhya / vṛddha** — child, juvenile, youth, middle age, old age
  - **status:** ordinary developmental vocabulary used technically here
  - **function:** differentiates post-birth maturation-targets

- **nikāyasabhāga** — homogeneous aggregate-continuum or class-continuity projected in rebirth
  - **status:** central Abhidharma continuity term
  - **function:** grounds the classification of the eleven determinate karmas as present-existence-result karma

- **ākṣepa / ākṣipati** — projection, casting forth
  - **status:** already active in the preceding karmic discussion
  - **function:** describes karma as projecting a continuum and its developmental field

## Doctrinal Determination

The twenty-twofold range is:

```text
TARGET OF MATURATION
    antarābhava
    kalala
    arbuda
    peśī
    ghana
    praśākhā
    bāla
    kumāra
    yuvan
    madhya
    vṛddha

×

MODAL STATUS
    niyata
    aniyata

=

22 karmic possibilities
```

The eleven determinate cases are all `dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya` because they mature within one projected `nikāyasabhāga`.

This gives a precise doctrinal relation:

```text
same projected continuum
    → same dṛṣṭadharma temporal class

even when
    developmental stage differs
```

The verse therefore adds a criterion of **continuum identity** to the preceding analysis of karmic temporal determination.

## Logical Determination

The twenty-two classes are generated by a product of two independent dimensions:

```text
11 maturation-targets
    ×
2 modal statuses
    =
22
```

More importantly, VAK 4.53 shows that temporal classification is relative to a persisting causal unit:

```text
PHASE(A) ≠ PHASE(B)

but

NIKĀYASABHĀGA(A) = NIKĀYASABHĀGA(B)

therefore

karma produced in A
and maturing in B
may still be
DṚṢṬADHARMAVEDANĪYA
```

Thus:

```text
chronological difference
    does not entail
karmic-life difference
```

The relevant identity is not identity of body, developmental form, or moment. It is continuity of the karmically projected aggregate-series.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation was substantially correct but can now be made more exact by incorporating the Bhāṣya's derivation and the restriction to the determinate eleven.

Preferred translation:

> **In the desire domain, the intermediate existence projects twenty-two kinds of karma: karma directed toward each of eleven stages, each either determinate or indeterminate. The eleven determinate kinds have their fruit in the present existence, because the intermediate existence and the ten stages that follow it form one and the same projected continuum.**

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is **identity through developmental transformation**.

The Bhāṣya does not posit a substance that persists unchanged across the stages. Instead:

```text
one projected causal organization
    differentiates itself through
multiple successive phases
```

Hence:

```text
identity of continuum
    ≠ identity of momentary constitution
```

For our modeling purposes, this is a strong example of an invariant process-identity persisting through a sequence of determinate state-transformations. But here it should remain grounded in Vasubandhu's technical `nikāyasabhāga` doctrine.
