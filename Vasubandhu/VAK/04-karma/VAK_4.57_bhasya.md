# VAK_4.57_bhasya — Mental and Bodily Loci of Karmic Feeling-Result

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.57**

```iast
kuśalasyāvitarkasya karmaṇo vedanā matā |
vipākaś caitasiky eva kāyiky evāśubhasya tu ||
```

> The maturation-feeling of wholesome karma without vitarka is held to be exclusively mental; but that of unwholesome karma is exclusively bodily.

See: [VAK_4.57.md](./VAK_4.57.md)

## Source Cautions

The e-text has `kukśalasya` at the verse line in one place; the Kārikā and sense clearly require `kuśalasya`.

`Avitarka` is not a general psychological adjective here. Vasubandhu explicitly fixes its range from **dhyānāntara through bhavāgra**.

The distinction is about the **feeling that is karmic maturation (`vedanā-vipāka`)**. It does not deny that mental distress can occur in beings undergoing unwholesome results; it denies that `daurmanasya` itself is karmic vipāka.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu introduces the verse by saying that karmic maturation is principally considered through feeling (`vipākaḥ punar vedanā-pradhānaḥ`). He then asks whether karma can have exclusively mental feeling as maturation, or exclusively bodily feeling.

The first case is wholesome karma without vitarka:

```text
avitarka-kuśala-karma
    range: dhyānāntara → bhavāgra
    vipāka-vedanā: mental only
```

Why can its result not be bodily feeling?

Because bodily feeling necessarily belongs to a state possessing vitarka and vicāra:

```text
kāyikī vedanā
    → necessarily savitarka-savicāra

avitarka karma
    → cannot mature as kāyikī vedanā
```

The second case is unwholesome karma:

```text
akuśala-karma
    vipāka-vedanā: bodily only
```

Why can its painful result not be mental feeling?

Because the mental painful feeling is `daurmanasya`, and Vasubandhu recalls the already established rule:

```text
daurmanasya
    ≠ vipāka
```

Therefore the painful feeling that serves as direct maturation of unwholesome karma must be bodily.

The verse is thus not a loose psychological observation. It gives two exclusion arguments:

```text
wholesome avitarka karma
    excludes bodily vipāka
    because bodily feeling requires vitarka-vicāra

unwholesome karma
    excludes mental painful vipāka
    because daurmanasya is not vipāka
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose and two explicit why-questions with answers.

## New Technical Terms

- **avitarka** — without vitarka; here specifically karma from the intermediate dhyāna through the summit of existence
  - **status:** previously encountered meditative term; newly decisive as a karmic classifier
  - **function:** excludes bodily feeling from the maturation of higher wholesome karma

- **caitasikī vedanā** — mental feeling
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** exclusive feeling-locus for the maturation of wholesome avitarka karma

- **kāyikī vedanā** — bodily feeling
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** exclusive feeling-locus for the maturation of unwholesome karma

- **daurmanasya** — mental distress, displeasure
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** candidate mental painful feeling explicitly excluded from the category of karmic vipāka

- **savitarka-vicāratva** — condition of possessing vitarka and vicāra
  - **status:** familiar meditative structure, newly used as an exclusion-ground
  - **function:** explains why bodily feeling cannot be the maturation of avitarka karma

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse distinguishes the locus of feeling-result by the structure of the karma that produces it.

```text
KARMA
    wholesome + avitarka
        → mental feeling as vipāka only

    unwholesome
        → bodily painful feeling as vipāka only
```

The range of the first is explicit:

```text
dhyānāntara
    ↓
form-domain higher attainments
    ↓
formless attainments
    ↓
bhavāgra
```

The governing doctrinal constraints are:

```text
bodily feeling
    requires vitarka-vicāra structure

mental painful feeling = daurmanasya
    is not karmic vipāka
```

Thus the result-locus is constrained by the cognitive and affective architecture of the domain in which karma operates.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.57 adds a new axis to the preceding karmic analysis:

```text
4.46   maturation-domain
4.47   feeling-quality
4.50   maturation-necessity and time
4.57   feeling-locus
```

Formally:

```text
IF
    karma.quality = wholesome
    AND karma.mode = avitarka
THEN
    vipāka.feeling-locus = mental
    vipāka.feeling-locus ≠ bodily
```

because:

```text
bodily-feeling → vitarka-vicāra
avitarka-karma → not(vitarka-vicāra)
```

And:

```text
IF
    karma.quality = unwholesome
THEN
    vipāka.pain-locus = bodily
```

because:

```text
mental-pain = daurmanasya
daurmanasya ≠ vipāka
```

This is a typed causal restriction. The same generic predicate `painful maturation` does not license arbitrary assignment to bodily and mental loci.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation is substantially correct but should be sharpened to preserve the technical force of `avitarka` and the exclusion structure.

Preferred translation:

> **For wholesome karma belonging to the avitarka range, from the intermediate dhyāna through the summit of existence, the feeling that constitutes its maturation is exclusively mental; for unwholesome karma, the maturation-feeling is exclusively bodily.**

The Bhāṣya supplies the grounds:

- bodily feeling necessarily belongs to a vitarka-vicāra mode;
- mental painful feeling (`daurmanasya`) is not itself karmic maturation.

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is that a result is not determined only by its qualitative value but also by its **admissible locus**.

```text
cause-structure
    constrains
result-type
    constrains
result-locus
```

So two results both describable as feeling are not interchangeable merely because they share an affective predicate. Their admissibility depends on the structural conditions of their causal domain.

This is useful as a typed-result principle, but the primary point remains Vasubandhu's Abhidharma distinction between mental and bodily `vedanā-vipāka`.
