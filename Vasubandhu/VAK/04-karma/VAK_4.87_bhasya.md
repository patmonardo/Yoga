# VAK 4.87 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā

**prahāṇa-mārge samale sa-phalaṃ karma pañcabhiḥ |**  
**caturbhir amale ’nyac ca sāsravaṃ yac chubhāśubham || 4.87 ||**

Working translation:

> Karma in the contaminated path of abandoning possesses five fruits. In the uncontaminated path it possesses four; other contaminated karma, wholesome or unwholesome, likewise possesses four.

Kārikā file: `VAK_4.87.md`

---

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya begins by recalling the five fruits previously established and asks:

> Of the five fruits explained earlier, which karma has how many fruits?

It then defines the `prahāṇa-mārga`:

> `prahāṇārthaṃ mārgaḥ prahīyante vā 'nena kleśā iti prahāṇamārgaḥ ānantaryamārgaḥ`

The path is for abandoning, or the afflictions are abandoned by it; hence it is the path of abandoning — the **immediate path** (`ānantarya-mārga`).

For contaminated karma in that path:

> `tasmin sāsrave yat karma tat pañcabhiḥ phalaiḥ saphalam`

It possesses all five fruits.

The Bhāṣya then names them one by one:

1. **vipāka-phala** — the maturation belonging to its own domain.
2. **niḥṣyanda-phala** — similar dharmas arising later from samādhi.
3. **visaṃyoga-phala** — disconnection itself, namely the abandoning achieved.
4. **puruṣakāra-phala** — dharmas drawn forth by it, such as the path of liberation (`adhimukti-mārga`) and its co-arising dharmas; also future cultivation and the abandoning.
5. **adhipati-phala** — all conditioned dharmas other than itself, excluding those already arisen before it.

For karma in the uncontaminated abandoning path:

> `anāsrave prahāṇamārge yat karma tac caturbhiḥ phalaiḥ saphalaṃ vipākaphalaṃ hitvā`

It possesses four fruits, **excluding maturation fruit**.

For other contaminated wholesome or unwholesome karma:

> `yaccānyat prahāṇamārgāt sāsravaṃ kuśalaṃ karma yaccākuśalaṃ tadapi caturbhir visaṃyogaphalaṃ hitvā`

It likewise possesses four fruits, but here the excluded fruit is **disconnection fruit**.

## Natural Segmentation

### 1. What does `prahāṇa-mārga` mean?

The Bhāṣya identifies it with the **immediate path** (`ānantarya-mārga`), the path that directly performs the abandonment of afflictions.

This matters because the five-fruit claim is not about every path-state indiscriminately. It belongs to a very specific causal moment:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    directly abandons kleśa
```

### 2. Contaminated immediate path: all five fruits

Because the karma is still contaminated, it can mature (`vipāka`); because it produces similar later dharmas, it has `niḥṣyanda`; because it directly effects abandoning, it has `visaṃyoga`; because it generates associated and consequent path-dharmas, it has `puruṣakāra`; and because it conditions other conditioned dharmas, it has `adhipati`.

### 3. Uncontaminated immediate path: four fruits

The only absent fruit is:

```text
vipāka-phala
```

The uncontaminated path does not produce maturation in the karmic sense.

### 4. Other contaminated wholesome/unwholesome karma: four fruits

Here the missing fruit is different:

```text
visaṃyoga-phala
```

Such karma may mature, correspondingly flow out, produce activity-results, and condition dominantly, but it does not itself accomplish the disconnection from affliction that defines the abandoning path.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

> The path whose purpose is abandoning, or by which the afflictions are abandoned, is called the path of abandoning, namely the immediate path. Karma in that path, when contaminated, possesses five fruits. Its maturation fruit is the maturation situated in its own domain. Its outflow fruit is similar dharmas of a higher kind born from samādhi. Its disconnection fruit is disconnection itself, that is, the abandoning. Its activity-produced fruit consists in dharmas drawn forth by it, such as the path of liberation and the dharmas co-arising with it; also what is cultivated in the future and the abandoning itself. Its dominant fruit consists in all conditioned dharmas other than itself, excluding those already arisen before it.
>
> Karma in an uncontaminated abandoning path possesses four fruits, excluding maturation fruit. Other contaminated wholesome karma and unwholesome karma outside the abandoning path also possess four fruits, excluding disconnection fruit.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / Abhidharma exposition.

No major adversarial dialogue occurs in this unit. The Bhāṣya is primarily classificatory and explanatory.

## New Technical Terms

### ānantarya-mārga

- **provisional meaning:** immediate path; the phase that directly abandons affliction.
- **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of `prahāṇa-mārga`.
- **function:** identifies the exact path-stage to which the five-fruit classification applies.

### adhimukti-mārga

- **provisional meaning:** path of liberation/release immediately following the abandoning moment.
- **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya as an example.
- **function:** example of a `puruṣakāra-phala` drawn forth by the immediate path.

### visaṃyoga-phala

- **provisional meaning:** disconnection-result; the actual separation from an affliction.
- **status:** part of the pre-existing five-fruit taxonomy, here made concrete.
- **function:** distinguishes karma that directly abandons affliction from ordinary contaminated karma.

### puruṣakāra-phala

- **provisional meaning:** activity-produced result; dharmas actively brought forth by a causal operation.
- **status:** part of the five-fruit taxonomy.
- **function:** includes consequent path-factors, co-arising dharmas, and future cultivation generated by the path.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes clear that the verse is about **set membership**, not merely cardinality.

### Contaminated immediate path

```text
{
    vipāka,
    niḥṣyanda,
    visaṃyoga,
    puruṣakāra,
    adhipati
}
```

### Uncontaminated immediate path

```text
{
    niḥṣyanda,
    visaṃyoga,
    puruṣakāra,
    adhipati
}
```

Excluded:

```text
vipāka
```

### Other contaminated wholesome/unwholesome karma

```text
{
    vipāka,
    niḥṣyanda,
    puruṣakāra,
    adhipati
}
```

Excluded:

```text
visaṃyoga
```

Thus two classes may each have four fruits while differing essentially in which causal relation is absent.

A crucial distinction is therefore:

```text
same cardinality
    ≠
same causal structure
```

---

## Argument Reconstruction

The logic of the classification is functional.

### Why all five for contaminated immediate-path karma?

It can:

```text
mature
produce similar later dharmas
disconnect affliction
produce consequent path-dharmas
condition other dharmas
```

Hence all five fruit-relations are present.

### Why no `vipāka` for uncontaminated path karma?

Because uncontaminated karma does not generate karmic maturation.

### Why no `visaṃyoga` for ordinary contaminated karma?

Because disconnection is not a generic result of karma. It belongs specifically to a path-event that **actually abandons** an affliction.

So the five fruits are not interchangeable output labels. Each marks a distinct causal relation.

---

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation is sound at the level of cardinality, but the Bhāṣya requires a stronger presentation:

> In the contaminated immediate path of abandoning, karma has all five fruits. In the uncontaminated immediate path it has four, lacking maturation fruit. Other contaminated wholesome or unwholesome karma also has four, but lacks disconnection fruit.

The word `prahāṇa-mārga` should not be left as a generic “path of abandoning” without noting the Bhāṣya identification:

```text
prahāṇa-mārga = ānantarya-mārga
```

## Logical Determination

This verse introduces a useful distinction among three independent dimensions:

```text
path-function
contamination status
fruit-set membership
```

Formally:

```text
ContaminatedImmediatePath(K)
    → Results(K) = {V, N, D, P, A}
```

```text
UncontaminatedImmediatePath(K)
    → Results(K) = {N, D, P, A}
```

```text
OtherContaminatedKarma(K)
    → Results(K) = {V, N, P, A}
```

where:

```text
V = vipāka
N = niḥṣyanda
D = visaṃyoga
P = puruṣakāra
A = adhipati
```

The key logical rule is:

```text
|Results(K1)| = |Results(K2)|
    does not imply
Results(K1) = Results(K2)
```

## Light Organon Contact

This is a clean case where a rational system must preserve **typed causal relations**, not merely count outputs.

Two processes may each have four consequences while differing in kind:

```text
Process A:
    lacks maturation

Process B:
    lacks disconnection
```

The distinction is not numerical but structural.

For the Kernel:

```text
ResultProfile {
    hasMaturation
    hasOutflow
    hasDisconnection
    hasActivityProduced
    hasDominant
}
```

is superior to a single integer field such as:

```text
resultCount = 4
```

The Bhāṣya is therefore already performing something like a typed causal ontology.
