# VAK_4.124_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.124**

```text
svargāya śīlaṃ prādhānyāt visaṃyogāya bhāvanā |
caturṇāṃ brāhmapuṇyatvaṃ kalpaṃ svargeṣu modanāt || 4.124 ||
```

> Moral discipline is principally conducive to heaven; cultivation is principally conducive to disconnection. Four have the status of Brahmā-merit, because one delights in the heavens for a kalpa.

See: `VAK_4.124.md`.

---

---

## Natural Segmentation

The passage has two movements:

1. the principal fruits of `śīla` and `bhāvanā`, completing the three `puṇyakriyāvastu`;
2. an exegetical disagreement over the measure of `brāhmapuṇya`.

The Bhāṣya begins by recalling the result of giving:

```text
dāna
    → mahābhogatā
       abundant enjoyment/resources
```

It then places discipline and cultivation beside it.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

It has already been said that giving conduces to abundant enjoyment. What, then, of discipline and cultivation?

> Discipline is principally for heaven; cultivation is for disconnection.

Giving also conduces to heaven, but discipline does so principally. Discipline also conduces to disconnection, but cultivation does so principally.

A sūtra says: “Four persons generate Brahmā-merit.” What is this Brahmā-merit?

The Vaibhāṣikas say: it is that measure of merit which was stated for the purpose of indicating the measure of the karma whose maturation produces the [Buddha’s] marks.

The former teachers, however, explain:

> The four have the status of Brahmā-merit because one delights in the heavens for a kalpa.

Whatever amount of merit causes one to delight in the heavens for a kalpa is Brahmā-merit, because the Brahmā ministers have a lifespan of a kalpa.

Another school recites the verse:

> “One generates Brahmā-merit and delights in the heavens for a kalpa.”

---

## Course of Argument

### 1. Completing the fruit-map of the three merit-bases

The Bhāṣya recalls:

```text
dāna
    → mahābhogatā
```

Then adds:

```text
śīla
    → svarga, principally

bhāvanā
    → visaṃyoga, principally
```

The crucial qualifier is `prādhānyāt`.

Vasubandhu immediately prevents an exclusive reading:

```text
dānam api svargāya
    giving also conduces to heaven

śīlam api visaṃyogāya
    discipline also conduces to disconnection

bhāvanā tu prādhānyāt
    but cultivation does so principally
```

Thus the classification is one of **predominant causal orientation**, not mutually exclusive outcome.

### 2. `Śīla` and heaven

Discipline principally produces a favorable destination because it restrains the bodily and verbal causes of bad rebirth.

It can also support liberation, but its characteristic worldly result is the construction of a purified rebirth-condition.

### 3. `Bhāvanā` and `visaṃyoga`

Cultivation principally conduces to `visaṃyoga`, disconnection.

This follows directly from VAK 4.123. There, `bhāvanā` was concentrated wholesome activity that perfumes the mind and makes the continuum of the nature of wholesome qualities.

Here the causal function of that transformation becomes explicit:

```text
conditioning of the continuum
    →
weakening/removal of binding factors
    →
visaṃyoga
```

The point is not merely improvement of future existence but severance of the relevant conjunction with affliction.

---

## The Three Merit-Bases as Dominant Functions

The sequence can now be summarized:

```text
dāna
    dominant fruit:
    abundant enjoyment/resources

śīla
    dominant fruit:
    favorable heavenly rebirth

bhāvanā
    dominant fruit:
    disconnection
```

But:

```text
none of these is exclusive
```

Giving can contribute to heaven.
Discipline can contribute to liberation.
Cultivation can also have worldly results.

The verse classifies by the **chief causal tendency** of each practice.

---

## `Visaṃyoga`

`Visaṃyoga` should be kept technically distinct from a vague translation such as “liberation.”

It names liberation under the aspect of:

```text
separation from a binding conjunction
```

That makes it especially appropriate in this context:

```text
bhāvanā
    transforms the mental continuum

transformed continuum
    becomes capable of abandoning binding factors

abandonment
    = visaṃyoga
```

Thus the fruit is described structurally rather than rhetorically.

---

## `Brāhmapuṇya`: the Question of Measure

The Bhāṣya now quotes a sūtra:

```text
catvāraḥ pudgalā brāhmaṇyaṃ puṇyaṃ prasavanti
```

> Four persons generate Brahmā-merit.

The present passage does **not** enumerate those four persons. Their identities should therefore not be supplied here from general Buddhist tradition without an external-source comparison.

The question is instead:

```text
katamat tad brāhmapuṇyam
    What is that Brahmā-merit?
```

Two answers follow.

---

## Voice: Vaibhāṣika Position

```text
yattallakṣaṇavipākasya karmaṇaḥ parimāṇajñāpanāyoktam
```

The Vaibhāṣikas identify the measure of Brahmā-merit with the quantity of merit previously invoked to explain the karma whose maturation produces a Buddha’s marks.

This recalls the discussion around VAK 4.110, where the measure of a single “merit” involved in producing the Buddha-marks was itself disputed and treated as cosmically large.

So on this view:

```text
brāhmapuṇya
    = a previously established immense karmic measure
```

The definition is quantitative by reference to another doctrinal measure.

---

## Voice: Former Teachers (`pūrvācāryāḥ`)

The former teachers instead define Brahmā-merit through its maturation-result:

```text
yāvatā puṇyena kalpaṃ svargeṣu modate
idaṃ brāhmaṃ puṇyam
```

> Whatever amount of merit causes one to delight in the heavens for a kalpa is Brahmā-merit.

The reason is:

```text
brahmapurohitānāṃ kalpāyuṣkatvāt
```

> because the Brahmā ministers have a lifespan of a kalpa.

Thus:

```text
brāhmapuṇya
    = merit sufficient for kalpa-long celestial enjoyment
```

This definition is not based on the visible size of the act but on the magnitude and duration of its maturation.

---

## Voice: Other School

The Bhāṣya records a variant recitation:

```text
brāhmaṃ puṇyaṃ prasavati kalpaṃ svargeṣu modata
```

> One generates Brahmā-merit and delights in the heavens for a kalpa.

This supports the former teachers’ result-based explanation.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **prādhānya** — predominance, principal causal tendency
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptually
  - **function:** prevents an exclusive mapping between practice and result

- **visaṃyoga** — disconnection from a binding conjunction
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** characteristic liberative result of cultivation

- **brāhmapuṇya** — Brahmā-merit, an exceptionally large class of conditioned merit
  - **status:** new in this local section
  - **function:** quantitative/resultative classification of merit

- **brahmapurohita** — Brahmā minister
  - **status:** rare
  - **function:** supplies the kalpa-long lifespan used to define Brahmā-merit

- **parimāṇajñāpana** — indication of measure
  - **status:** rare
  - **function:** Vaibhāṣika explanation of how Brahmā-merit is quantified

---

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The passage establishes:

1. giving characteristically conduces to abundant enjoyment;
2. discipline principally conduces to heaven;
3. cultivation principally conduces to disconnection;
4. these are dominant tendencies, not exclusive causal channels;
5. giving can also conduce to heaven;
6. discipline can also conduce to disconnection;
7. Brahmā-merit is disputed in measure;
8. Vaibhāṣikas identify it with the very large measure used in the Buddha-mark discussion;
9. former teachers define it by kalpa-long heavenly enjoyment;
10. the present Bhāṣya passage does not identify the four persons mentioned in the quoted sūtra.

---

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation was substantially correct, but `prādhānyāt` should be made explicit in both clauses:

> **Moral discipline is principally conducive to heaven; cultivation is principally conducive to disconnection. Four have the status of Brahmā-merit because one delights in the heavens for a kalpa.**

The Bhāṣya specifically excludes the interpretation:

```text
śīla → only heaven
bhāvanā → only liberation
```

The relation is one of predominance.

---

## Light Organon Contact

This passage distinguishes two different practical accomplishments:

```text
śīla
    improves the organization of conditioned existence

bhāvanā
    can transform the continuum so that a binding relation is severed
```

That distinction should not be exaggerated into an opposition. Vasubandhu explicitly makes the causes overlap.

The logical form is therefore:

```text
practice
    → multiple possible results

but

practice
    → one characteristic dominant tendency
```

The second half then sharply separates **quantity within conditioned existence** from **liberative type**:

```text
kalpa-long heavenly enjoyment
    = immense conditioned merit

visaṃyoga
    = severance of binding conjunction
```

A longer conditioned result does not thereby become disconnection.
