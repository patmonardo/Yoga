# VAK_4.06 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
naiḥṣyandikī ca sattvākhyā niṣyandopātta-bhūtajā |
samādhijā aupacayikānupāttābhinna-bhūtajā ||
```

**Working translation:** Ordinary non-manifest action is outflow-derived, designated as belonging to a sentient being, and born from outflow-derived appropriated great material determinations. Non-manifest action born from concentration is development-produced, non-appropriated, and born from non-distinct great material determinations.

### Source cautions

The e-text reads `anupātā` in the compound at [200|09], while the Kārikā file has normalized `anupāttā`. The prose explanation at [200|10-200|12] also reads `anupātāni`; this may reflect a transmission or normalization issue in the source. The doctrinal force is nevertheless explicit from the surrounding discussion: samādhi-born avijñapti is treated as non-appropriated.

The first-pass Kārikā analysis left `abhinna` provisional. The Bhāṣya now determines it through the explicit contrast with prātimokṣa restraint.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya first distinguishes the ordinary, non-samāhita mode of avijñapti from avijñapti born from samādhi.

For the ordinary case, avijñapti is `naiḥṣyandikī`: it belongs to a homogeneous causal continuation. It arises taking as support both outflow-derived great material determinations and mental factors (`citta-caitta`). Vasubandhu explicitly marks this as the mode belonging to the non-concentrated level (`asamāhitabhūmikā`).

The samādhi-born case is different. The avijñapti of dhyāna-restraint and uncontaminated restraint is `samādhijā`; it arises from great material determinations produced through samādhi. These are classified as `aupacayika` and non-appropriated.

The most important clarification concerns `abhinna`. In samādhi-born restraint, the seven avijñaptis corresponding to abstention from killing through frivolous or incoherent speech arise dependent on the very same great material determinations. The Bhāṣya explains this by saying that the material basis is not divided, just as the mind itself is not divided: `cittavat bhūtābhedāt`.

This is contrasted directly with prātimokṣa restraint. There, the seven avijñaptis arise dependent on distinct sets of great material determinations.

Thus `abhinna` is not merely a vague quality of "undivided matter." It designates the non-differentiation of the material support across the seven restraints in samādhi-born avijñapti.

The final sentence begins the next topic by noting that vijñapti itself is naiḥṣyandika, and bodily vijñapti is appropriated. That transition should be carried into the next unit rather than allowed to obscure the present distinction.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position, presented and explained by Vasubandhu.

The passage is expository rather than polemical. No explicit Sautrāntika objection is introduced in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **asamāhitabhūmikā** — non-concentrated level; **status:** new local specification; **function:** marks the domain in which ordinary naiḥṣyandika avijñapti is classified.
- **dhyānasaṃvara** — restraint associated with dhyāna; **status:** previously anticipated through samādhi-born avijñapti; **function:** identifies one class of samādhi-born non-manifest action.
- **anāsravasaṃvara** — uncontaminated restraint; **status:** previously encountered in relation to anāsrava karma; **function:** identifies the path-related form of samādhi-born avijñapti.
- **cittavat bhūtābheda** — non-difference of material support analogous to the non-division of mind; **status:** new explanatory formula; **function:** explains why the seven samādhi-born restraints share one material basis.
- **prātimokṣasaṃvara** — restraint of individual liberation; **status:** previously encountered in the avijñapti dossier; **function:** contrast-case in which seven avijñaptis depend on distinct material bases.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya now gives a twofold causal-material architecture of avijñapti:

```text
ordinary / non-concentrated avijñapti
    → naiḥṣyandika
    → supported by naiḥṣyandika mahābhūtas
    → also dependent on citta-caittas

samādhi-born avijñapti
    → samādhijā
    → aupacayika
    → non-appropriated
    → supported by samādhi-produced mahābhūtas
```

The decisive internal distinction is:

```text
samādhi-born restraint
    seven avijñaptis
    one undivided material support

prātimokṣa restraint
    seven avijñaptis
    distinct material supports
```

This shows that avijñapti is not merely divided by moral content. Its mode of material support varies according to the mode in which restraint is generated.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation should be tightened around `abhinna`.

Instead of the vague:

> born from non-distinct great material determinations

prefer:

> born from great material determinations that are not differentiated among the several restraints

or more compactly:

> born from a common, undivided material basis.

The Bhāṣya also confirms that `samādhijā` specifically covers avijñapti belonging to dhyāna-restraint and uncontaminated restraint.

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes two modes of continuity:

```text
niṣyanda
    homogeneous causal continuation

upacaya
    cultivated development through samādhi
```

But the Bhāṣya adds a second axis:

```text
shared basis
    samādhi-born restraint

plural differentiated bases
    prātimokṣa restraint
```

So the logical structure is not simply:

```text
ordinary continuity
vs
cultivated continuity
```

It is:

```text
mode of generation
    ×
mode of material support
```

This is important because one and the same normative content—abstention from the seven bodily/verbal wrongs—can have different ontological articulation depending on how the restraint is generated.

## Organon Contact Point

The lightest useful contact point is that cultivated determination can unify several normative determinations under one generated basis.

```text
samādhi
    → one cultivated determination
    → seven restraints
       sharing one material support
```

This is more than mere coexistence. The Bhāṣya presents a common generated basis through which multiple abstentions are simultaneously sustained.

No stronger identification with Principle or Path should yet be imposed; the text is still specifying the Abhidharma mechanics of restraint.
