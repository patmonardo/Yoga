# VAK_4.03 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
na kasyacid ahetoḥ syāt hetuḥ syāc ca vināśakaḥ /
dvigrāhyaṃ syāt na cāṇau tat vāgvijñaptis tu vāgdhvaniḥ // VAK_4.03 //
```

**Working translation:** Destruction would not require a cause for anything; otherwise the very cause of arising would also become a destroyer. If configuration were a real distinct thing, it would be apprehended in two ways; moreover, it does not exist in a single atom. Verbal manifestation, however, is vocal sound.

### Source cautions

The e-text contains several typographical irregularities in this passage, especially around page/line numbering near `[195|06]` and forms such as `kāyavijñarpti`. They are not silently normalized beyond obvious spacing in the quotations above.

The natural argument crosses the formal boundary between VAK_4.02 and VAK_4.03. VAK_4.03 therefore cannot be understood without the preceding proof of `vyaya` and `kṣaṇikatva`.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Destruction is not an effect requiring a destroying cause

Vasubandhu first insists that the claim that destruction is spontaneous must be established inferentially. The key starting point is that destruction is `abhāva`, non-being or absence. A cause produces an effect, but absence is not something to be produced. Therefore destruction, as such, does not require a productive cause.

The Kārikā's compressed `na kasyacid ahetoḥ syāt` is not denying causality in general. It is arguing that if destruction required a cause merely because arising does, then destruction would have to resemble production. Yet momentary cognitions, sounds, and flames are observed to cease without any separate destroying entity.

Vasubandhu rejects attempts to make one succeeding cognition destroy the prior cognition, or one sound destroy another. Opposed cognitions cannot coexist so as to destroy one another, and a weaker succeeding cognition cannot intelligibly destroy a stronger one merely because it follows it.

### 2. A supposed destroying cause becomes self-contradictory

The second pāda makes the problem sharper. Suppose fire-contact destroys wood or cooked qualities. The same causal complex that generates later, more intensely cooked qualities would then also have to destroy the earlier ones.

Thus:

```text
productive cause
    = destroying cause
```

But if the same or equivalent ground both produces a determination and destroys that very determination, causal explanation becomes unstable.

The example of boiling water clarifies Vasubandhu's alternative. Fire does not positively produce the destruction of water. Rather, fire strengthens the fire-domain; the watery aggregate becomes progressively diminished until a further corresponding water-continuum is no longer produced.

The decisive result is:

```text
bhāvas
    do not need a vināśa-hetu

because they are bhaṅgura
    intrinsically perishable

utpannamātra
    having arisen, they perish
```

Hence `kṣaṇabhaṅga` is established. From momentary destruction follows the absence of real motion.

### 3. Motion becomes an appearance generated by serial production

What appears as motion is `gatyabhimāna`, the conviction or appearance of motion produced when momentary determinations arise without interruption in neighboring places, like a moving line of flame.

Thus:

```text
real enduring motion
    rejected

serial arising at successive places
    retained

appearance of motion
    explained
```

On the Vaibhāṣika line, once motion is removed, bodily manifestation is established as `saṃsthāna`, configuration.

### 4. The Sautrāntika attacks `saṃsthāna` as a separate real entity

The prose then explicitly marks the Sautrāntika position:

```text
nāsti saṃsthānaṃ dravyata iti sautrāntikāḥ
```

Configuration does not exist as a distinct real substance/dharma.

Long, short, square, round, and similar configurations are designated when color-material is arranged in particular distributions. The example of a firebrand is decisive: rapidly successive appearances in one direction are cognized as a line; when seen around a circle they are cognized as circular. There need not be an additional entity called configuration over and above the ordered plurality.

### 5. `dvigrāhyam`: the double-apprehension argument

If configuration were a distinct material entity, it would become apprehensible by two senses.

One determines something as long by seeing it with the eye. But one may also determine something as long by touching it with the bodily faculty. A single rūpa-dharma should not be directly apprehended by two separate sensory domains in this way.

The reply that touch merely recalls configuration through association is examined and rejected. Such associative recollection works where there is reliable co-occurrence, as when seeing fire recalls heat or smelling a flower recalls its color. But no determinate tactile quality is invariably associated with a particular configuration.

The conclusion is again:

```text
nāsti dravyataḥ saṃsthānam
```

configuration is not a separate real entity.

### 6. `na cāṇau tat`: the atomic argument

The second Sautrāntika argument is even more direct. Whatever is real material form with resistance must be present at the atomic level. But longness, shortness, and the other configurations are not present in a single atom.

Therefore configuration arises only as designation upon many atoms/material determinations arranged in a certain manner:

```text
bahuṣu tathā saṃniviṣṭeṣu
    → dīrghādi-prajñapti
```

The proposal that there are special 'configuration-atoms' merely shifts the problem, because no independent self-characteristic of such atoms has been established.

### 7. Sautrāntika reconstruction of bodily karma

Vasubandhu then asks the Sautrāntikas directly: if both real motion and real configuration have been rejected, what do they call bodily manifestation?

Their answer is subtle:

```text
saṃsthāna
    may still be designated as bodily manifestation

but not dravyataḥ
    not as an independently real entity
```

The deeper determination of bodily karma is then shifted to volition:

```text
kāyādhiṣṭhānaṃ karma kāyakarma
```

Bodily karma is karma having the body as its basis/domain, namely the volition that directs the body here and there (`yā cetanā kāyasya tatra tatra praṇetrī`).

This gives a two-stage account of volition:

```text
saṃkalpa-cetanā
    prior volition:
    "I will do thus and thus"

kriyā-cetanā
    subsequent operative volition
    by which the body is impelled
```

The latter is what the sūtra's `cetayitvā karma` can mean on this reading.

### 8. Vaibhāṣika reassertion and verbal manifestation

The Vaibhāṣikas, however, maintain that configuration itself is a real entity and that bodily manifestation has configuration as its nature.

The verse then turns to the less controversial case of speech:

```text
vāgvijñapti
    = vāgdhvani
    = vocal sound
```

The sound whose nature is speech is verbal manifestation.

This closes the bodily-vijñapti controversy but immediately prepares the larger dispute over whether `avijñapti` itself exists substantially.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu / argumentative exposition** — develops the inference for uncaused destruction, rejects destructive-cause theories, and derives momentariness and the unreality of enduring motion.

**Voice: Sautrāntika position** — explicitly introduced at `nāsti saṃsthānaṃ dravyata iti sautrāntikāḥ`; denies configuration as an independent real entity, develops the double-apprehension and atomic arguments, and reconstructs bodily karma through volition.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — explicitly restored at `[196|01]`: configuration is real as a substance/dharma and bodily manifestation is configuration in nature.

**Voice: Objector / interlocutor** — several short challenges occur within the momentariness and configuration discussions; individual attributions are not always textually marked, so they should not all be assigned to one school.

## New Technical Terms

- **akāryatva** — the status of not being an effect/product
  - **status:** made technically operative in the Bhāṣya argument
  - **function:** ground for denying a productive cause of destruction

- **bhaṅgura** — intrinsically fragile/perishable
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** characterizes conditioned entities as perishing from their own momentary nature rather than through a destroying cause

- **kṣaṇabhaṅga** — momentary breaking/perishing
  - **status:** major doctrinal term made explicit in the prose
  - **function:** result of the destruction argument and ground for rejecting real motion

- **gatyabhimāna** — appearance/conviction of motion
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this unit
  - **function:** explains phenomenal motion without admitting an enduring mover

- **saṃniveśa** — arrangement / disposition
  - **status:** technically operative in the Sautrāntika argument
  - **function:** supplies the real plurality upon which configuration is designated

- **prajñapti** — designation
  - **status:** previously encountered; decisive here
  - **function:** ontological alternative to treating configuration as a separate `dravya`

- **saṃkalpa-cetanā** — projective/planning volition
  - **status:** Bhāṣya distinction absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** prior determination, "I will do thus"

- **kriyā-cetanā** — operative volition
  - **status:** Bhāṣya distinction absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** volition that actually impels the body

- **praṇetrī** — that which directs or leads
  - **status:** Bhāṣya characterization of cetanā
  - **function:** determines bodily karma as volitional direction of the body

## Doctrinal Determination

The first-pass Kārikā analysis correctly recognized three separate pressure points but left their argumentative ownership open. The Bhāṣya now resolves them.

### Momentariness

`na kasyacid ahetoḥ syāt` belongs to the proof that destruction is not a produced effect requiring a special destroying cause.

The doctrine is not:

```text
every change needs an external destroyer
```

but:

```text
arising requires productive grounds
perishing does not require a second productive event
```

Conditioned existence is therefore genetically asymmetric:

```text
production
    causally conditioned

perishing
    intrinsic to the produced moment
```

### Configuration

The Kārikā's `saṃsthānaṃ kāyavijñaptiḥ` expresses the Vaibhāṣika determination.

The Sautrāntika accepts configuration only conventionally/designationally:

```text
saṃsthāna
    not independent dravya
    but prajñapti on arranged plurality
```

Thus the Bhāṣya reveals a genuine school controversy concealed by the terse Kārikā.

### Bodily karma

The Sautrāntika reconstruction is especially important:

```text
bodily karma
    is not fundamentally a persisting physical motion
    nor a separately real configuration

bodily karma
    = cetanā directing the body
```

Manifest configuration remains the appearance/designation of bodily action, while karmic agency is relocated to volitional determination.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass rendering of the first pāda was too cautious because the Bhāṣya now supplies the missing subject and argumentative force.

A better working translation is:

> Destruction does not require a cause for anything; otherwise a productive cause would itself become a destroyer. If configuration were a distinct real entity, it would be apprehended by two senses, and moreover it is not found in a single atom. Verbal manifestation, however, is vocal sound.

The term `dvigrāhya` is now secure: it means apprehensible by two sensory faculties, eye and body/touch, not merely 'apprehensible twice' in an unspecified sense.

`na cāṇau tat` is also secure: `tat` is `saṃsthāna`, configuration.

## Logical Determination

The natural movement is now explicit:

```text
conditioned production
    → momentary arising
    → intrinsic perishing
    → no enduring mover
    → motion becomes serial appearance

therefore Vaibhāṣika:
    bodily vijñapti = configuration

but Sautrāntika critique:
    configuration is doubly apprehensible
    configuration absent in atom
    configuration therefore not separate dravya

so:
    arrangement of plurality
        → designation of configuration

and karmic determination moves inward:
    saṃkalpa-cetanā
        → kriyā-cetanā
        → body directed here and there
        → manifest configuration designated
```

The crucial conceptual result is that the Sautrāntika does not simply abolish bodily manifestation. It separates:

```text
what is karmically determining
    cetanā

from

what is manifestly designated
    saṃsthāna
```

This prepares the avijñapti dispute perfectly, because the question becomes whether karmic efficacy requires a further real material determination after manifest action has ceased.

## Organon Contact Point

This is a particularly strong reflective passage because the Bhāṣya transforms the ontological location of action through criticism.

The Vaibhāṣika line seeks the action in a real material form:

```text
cetanā
    → real saṃsthāna
    → kāyavijñapti
```

The Sautrāntika line distinguishes the determining activity from its appearance:

```text
cetanā
    = operative determination

saṃsthāna
    = designated manifestation of ordered plurality
```

The important point for the Rational Kośa is not to choose sides prematurely, but to preserve the generated distinction:

```text
determining activity
    ≠
its manifest configuration
```

That distinction is exactly what the next controversy over `avijñapti` will test.
