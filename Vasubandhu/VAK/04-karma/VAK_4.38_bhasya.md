# VAK_4.38_bhasya — Abandonment of Prātimokṣa Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
prātimokṣadamatyāgaḥ śikṣānikṣepaṇāc cyuteḥ |
ubhayavyañjanotpatter mūlacchedān niśātyayāt || VAK_4.38 ||
```

Working translation:

> Prātimokṣa restraint is abandoned through laying down the training, through death, through the arising of both sexual characteristics, and through severance of the wholesome roots; upavāsa restraint is also abandoned through the passing of the night.

## Source Cautions

The Kārikā compresses five causes into one list, but the Bhāṣya explicitly distributes them between two forms of prātimokṣa restraint.

```text
ordinary prātimokṣa restraint
    four causes of abandonment

upavāsa restraint
    those same four
    + passing of the night
```

Accordingly, `niśātyaya` should not be presented as an additional universal cause applying identically to every prātimokṣa category.

The source has `prātimokṣadamatyāgaḥ`, where `dama` is immediately glossed by Vasubandhu as `saṃvara`. The term should therefore be translated as restraint/discipline rather than introducing an independent technical category.

---

## Natural Argument

### 1. Transition from acquisition to abandonment

Vasubandhu explicitly closes the preceding investigation:

```text
pratilambha
    acquisition
        ↓
completed

now:

tyāga
    abandonment / relinquishment
```

The Karma chapter therefore studies a karmic determination through both directions of its lifecycle:

```text
how it is acquired
how it is lost
```

### 2. `dama` means restraint

Vasubandhu explains:

```text
dāmyanty anena iti damaḥ
```

“It is called `dama` because by it [the faculties] are restrained.”

He then specifies that `saṃvara` is intended, because there is restraint of the faculties (`indriyadamana`).

Thus the opening compound does not introduce a new kind of dharma:

```text
dama
    = saṃvara in this context
```

### 3. Four causes for ordinary prātimokṣa restraint

Leaving aside upavāsa, Vasubandhu gives four causes.

#### a. `śikṣānikṣepaṇa` — laying down the training

The Bhāṣya expands this as rejection or renunciation of the training rules before a person capable of understanding the declaration:

```text
śikṣāpadānāṃ
vijñapuruṣasyāntike
pratyākhyāna
```

The loss therefore requires a determinate communicative act, not merely private discouragement or a lapse in observance.

#### b. `cyuti` — death

The prose explains this as:

```text
nikāyasabhāgatyāga
```

abandonment of the homogeneous class / present life-basis.

Death terminates the living support upon which this particular prātimokṣa status depends.

#### c. `ubhayavyañjanotpatti` — arising of both sexual characteristics

The source says:

```text
yugapad ubhayavyañjanaprādurbhāva
```

simultaneous manifestation of both sexual characteristics.

This is treated as a transformation of the qualifying bodily support relevant to the disciplinary form.

#### d. `kuśalamūlasamuccheda` — severance of the wholesome roots

The fourth cause is the cutting off of the wholesome roots.

This is stronger than committing a particular offense. The sustaining causal basis of wholesome discipline itself is severed.

### 4. Upavāsa adds temporal expiration

Upavāsa restraint is lost by those same four causes and additionally:

```text
ā rātrikṣayāt
    at the exhaustion/end of the night
```

This is not failure of the observance. The restraint simply reaches the terminus built into its undertaking.

Thus the Bhāṣya counts five causes only after combining the general four with the special upavāsa condition.

### 5. Why do these causes produce abandonment?

Vasubandhu does not leave the list as an unexplained Vinaya taxonomy. He asks explicitly:

```text
kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇam ebhiḥ kāraṇais tyāgo bhavati /
```

Why, precisely, does abandonment occur through these causes?

He then gives a corresponding ground for each one.

```text
1. śikṣānikṣepaṇa
   → samādānaviruddhavijñaptyutpāda

2. cyuti
   → āśrayatyāga

3. ubhayavyañjanotpatti
   → āśrayavikopana

4. kuśalamūlasamuccheda
   → nidānaccheda

5. niśātyaya
   → tāvadevākṣepa
```

This is the philosophical center of the passage.

---

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — transition from acquisition to abandonment; gloss of `dama`; enumeration of the four ordinary causes and the fifth upavāsa cause; question asking why these causes effect abandonment; causal explanation of each.

No explicit rival school is introduced within the natural 4.38 unit. Alternative theories of loss begin immediately with VAK 4.39.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **tyāga** — abandonment, relinquishment, loss of an acquired karmic determination.  
  **Status:** important new organizing term for the present block.  
  **Function:** counterpart to `pratilambha`; introduces the conditions under which restraint ceases to be possessed.

- **dama** — restraint, discipline; explicitly glossed here as `saṃvara`.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya gloss of the Kārikā wording.  
  **Function:** explains the term `prātimokṣadama` through restraint of the faculties.

- **śikṣānikṣepaṇa** — laying down the training.  
  **Status:** technical disciplinary expression.  
  **Function:** voluntary abandonment of prātimokṣa status.

- **pratyākhyāna** — rejection, renunciation, formal repudiation.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya expansion.  
  **Function:** specifies how laying down the training is enacted.

- **nikāyasabhāgatyāga** — abandonment of the homogeneous class / present life-basis.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory phrase in this passage.  
  **Function:** explains why death terminates prātimokṣa restraint.

- **āśrayatyāga** — abandonment of the support.  
  **Status:** reflective explanatory vocabulary.  
  **Function:** causal ground corresponding to death.

- **āśrayavikopana** — disruption or impairment of the support.  
  **Status:** reflective explanatory vocabulary.  
  **Function:** causal ground corresponding to the arising of both sexual characteristics.

- **nidānaccheda** — severance of the causal basis.  
  **Status:** reflective explanatory vocabulary.  
  **Function:** causal ground corresponding to severance of the wholesome roots.

- **samādānaviruddhavijñapti** — a manifestation/declaration contrary to the undertaking.  
  **Status:** important Bhāṣya compound.  
  **Function:** explains why formally laying down the training cancels the previously undertaken restraint.

- **ākṣepa** — projection, carrying-forward, determination of a temporal extent.  
  **Status:** previously important Abhidharma causal vocabulary; here applied to vow duration.  
  **Function:** explains why upavāsa ends when the period projected by its undertaking is exhausted.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya distinguishes five mechanisms of loss, but they belong to different dimensions of the restraint.

```text
1. declaration
   the undertaking is explicitly contradicted

2. support
   the living basis is abandoned

3. support-qualification
   the basis is disrupted

4. causal root
   the wholesome sustaining condition is cut

5. temporal projection
   the undertaken duration is exhausted
```

This means that `tyāga` is not a single homogeneous process.

A restraint may cease because its bearer rejects it, because the bearer dies, because the relevant bodily basis changes, because its wholesome causal ground is destroyed, or because the restraint has simply completed the duration for which it was projected.

### Breach is not yet identified with abandonment

Nothing in this verse says that every violation automatically destroys prātimokṣa restraint.

That question is precisely what the next verse opens through alternative doctrines concerning `patanīya` offenses.

Thus 4.38 should not be expanded into the stronger claim:

```text
any transgression
    → complete loss of restraint
```

The present list is more specific.

---

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation should expose the distribution that the Bhāṣya makes explicit:

> Prātimokṣa restraint is abandoned through laying down the training, death, the arising of both sexual characteristics, and severance of the wholesome roots; upavāsa restraint is abandoned through these four and, additionally, through the passing of the night.

This is preferable to presenting all five as though they applied uniformly to every subtype.

`mūlaccheda` should be rendered specifically as **severance of the wholesome roots**, following the Bhāṣya's `kuśalamūlasamuccheda`.

## Logical Determination

The passage gives a highly structured account of cessation.

```text
acquisition
    establishes a relation

abandonment
    requires a determinate negation
    of some constitutive condition
```

Each surface event is paired with its underlying reason:

```text
formal renunciation
    → contradiction of undertaking

death
    → loss of support

basis transformation
    → disruption of support

severance of wholesome roots
    → severance of causal ground

expiration of upavāsa
    → exhaustion of projected duration
```

So the Bhāṣya is not satisfied with an extensional list of events. It asks for the **ground through which each event has the power to terminate the restraint**.

That is a characteristic Bhāṣya move:

```text
condition stated
    → why is it effective?
    → underlying relation disclosed
```

---

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is the distinction between **an event of cessation** and the **ground of cessation**.

```text
event
    death

ground
    loss of āśraya
```

or:

```text
event
    verbal laying-down of training

ground
    manifestation contrary to the original undertaking
```

The passage therefore models a determination through its dependencies rather than treating its disappearance as brute negation.

Especially significant is the upavāsa case:

```text
samādāna
    projects a determinate temporal measure

measure exhausted
    → restraint ends
```

The same undertaking that generates the restraint also determines the internal limit of its persistence.

No larger metaphysical claim is required at this stage.
