# VAK_4.11 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
pravartakaṃ dṛṣṭiheyaṃ vijñānam ubhayaṃ punaḥ |
mānasaṃ bhāvanāheyaṃ pañcakaṃ tv anuvartakam ||
```

**Working translation:** Knowledge abandoned by seeing functions as an initiator. Mental knowledge abandoned by cultivation functions as both initiator and continuator; the fivefold group of sensory knowledges functions only as continuator.

Existing Kārikā analysis: [`VAK_4.11.md`](./VAK_4.11.md).

### Source cautions

The working e-text is a reference text based principally on Pradhan with corrections from Shastri and explicitly warns that it is not a critical edition. The source alternates `pravartaka` / `pravarttaka`; this file preserves the transmitted spellings in the quoted excerpt and uses the Kārikā spelling `pravartaka` analytically.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu now distributes the two functions defined in VAK_4.10 across different kinds of knowledge.

First, a mind abandoned by seeing (`darśanaprahātavya citta`) can initiate manifest action. It is called `pravartaka` because it serves as the ground (`nidāna`) for the `vitarka` and `vicāra` that generate the manifestation.

It is not, however, an `anuvartaka`. At the time of outward bodily or verbal performance, that particular mind is absent: action is then occurring with an outward-facing mind (`bahirmukha-citta`).

An objection follows. If form is generated by a mind abandoned by seeing, should that generated form itself also be abandoned by seeing? Vasubandhu presents the Abhidharma restriction that no form is classified as abandoned by seeing, since form as an undefiled determination is not opposed to either knowledge or ignorance (`vidyā` / `avidyā`). The issue is resolved by restricting the scriptural statement to `hetu-samutthāna`: the mind abandoned by seeing is the initiating ground, not a reason for transferring its Path-classification to the produced form.

The remaining functions are then assigned. Mental knowledge abandoned by cultivation (`bhāvanā-heya manovijñāna`) can both initiate and accompany action. The five sensory knowledge-bodies (`pañca vijñānakāyāḥ`) can accompany action but cannot initiate it.

Finally Vasubandhu explicitly formulates the result as a fourfold alternative (`catuṣkoṭika`):

```text
pravartaka only
    darśana-prahātavya citta

anuvartaka only
    five sensory vijñāna-kāyas

both
    bhāvanā-heya manovijñāna

neither
    anāsrava knowledge
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explains why knowledge abandoned by seeing is initiating but not continuing.

**Voice: Objector** — presses the consequence that form generated by such a mind should itself be abandoned by seeing.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — invokes the Abhidharma classification that form is not abandoned by seeing and resolves the apparent conflict by limiting the scriptural statement to causal origination.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — completes the functional distribution and gives the `catuṣkoṭika`.

## New Technical Terms

- **bahirmukha-citta** — outward-facing mind during bodily or verbal performance
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** explains why the initiating mind need not continue during execution

- **nidāna-bhūta** — functioning as a causal basis or ground
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** explains how the initiating mind gives rise to `vitarka` and `vicāra`

- **catuṣkoṭika** — fourfold alternative
  - **status:** previously encountered in Abhidharma argumentation
  - **function:** exhaustive logical classification of the four possible functional relations

- **akliṣṭa dharma** — undefiled determination
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** blocks transfer of the Path-classification of the initiating mind to generated material form

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya shows that `pravartaka` and `anuvartaka` are functional roles, not fixed names for two substances or two independently existing volitions.

```text
knowledge-type
    ×
Path-mode of abandonment
    ×
action-function
```

The same general category of knowledge can occupy different causal roles depending on its type and Path-status.

The sensory knowledges are especially restricted: they participate in ongoing performance but do not project the deed. Mental knowledge alone can occupy the initiating role.

The Path-classification of a causal mind also does not automatically transfer to its produced bodily or verbal form.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation is substantially confirmed, with one important terminological discipline:

- `vijñāna` should remain **knowledge**, not “consciousness.”
- `mānasaṃ` specifies **mental knowledge / manovijñāna**.
- `pañcakam` is securely the five sensory knowledge-bodies.

A refined translation is:

> Knowledge abandoned by seeing functions as initiator. Mental knowledge abandoned by cultivation functions as both initiator and continuator; the five sensory knowledge-bodies function only as continuators.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives an explicit fourfold logic:

```text
                    pravartaka    anuvartaka

darśana-heya            yes            no
five sensory             no            yes
bhāvanā-heya manas       yes           yes
anāsrava                  no             no
```

This is stronger than a simple temporal sequence. It distinguishes four possible relations of a cognition to action:

```text
initiation without continuation
continuation without initiation
both
neither
```

The `catuṣkoṭika` therefore functions as a complete logical distribution of causal roles.

## Organon Contact Point

The safest contact point is functional rather than metaphysical:

```text
pravartaka
    projection / initiation of activity

anuvartaka
    accompaniment / continuation of activity
```

Vasubandhu's analysis prevents us from treating an action as the expression of one numerically continuous inner state. Different determinations can initiate and sustain the same action-process.

That is already enough for the present pass. A deeper reconstruction of projection, execution, and the Path-classification of cognition should wait for the later special study.
