# VAK 4.116 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.116**

**varṇādisampadā vastu surūpatvaṃ yaśasvi vā |  
priyatā sukumārartusukhasparśāṅgatā tataḥ || 4.116 ||**

Best current translation:

> The gift-object is distinguished through excellence of color and the other sensible qualities. From this arise beauty, fame, being beloved, delicate limbs, and bodily members whose touch is pleasant according to the seasons.

## Natural Segmentation

1. The gift-object is distinguished by excellence in sensible qualities.
2. Four sensible qualities are named: color, smell, taste, and touch.
3. The Bhāṣya maps them in order to five aspects of maturation.
4. Smell and taste receive explicit analogical explanations.
5. Touch yields two related bodily results.

## Literal Prose Translation

The qualification “distinguished” continues here. If what is given is endowed with excellence in even one among color, smell, taste, and touch, then the gift-object is distinguished.

What happens when such an object is given? In order: by giving something excellent in color, one becomes beautiful; by giving something excellent in smell, one becomes renowned, because reputation spreads through the directions just as fragrance does. By giving something excellent in taste, one becomes beloved, just as flavor is sweet or agreeable. By giving something excellent in touch, one comes to possess delicate limbs, and one’s limbs have a touch that is pleasant according to the seasons, like that of the jewel-woman.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 4.114 announced three determinants of the excellence of giving:

- giver (`dātṛ`),
- gift-object (`vastu`),
- recipient-field (`kṣetra`).

VAK 4.115 treated the giver. VAK 4.116 now treats the second factor: `vastu-viśeṣa`.

The Bhāṣya defines an excellent gift-object not through monetary value but through sensible quality:

```text
varṇa   color / appearance
gandha  smell / fragrance
rasa    taste
sparśa  touch
```

The resulting correspondence is explicit:

```text
excellent color
    → surūpatva
      beauty of form

excellent smell
    → yaśas
      fame / renown

excellent taste
    → priyatā
      being beloved / agreeable

excellent touch
    → sukumārāṅgatā
      delicate limbs
    + ṛtusukhasparśāṅgatā
      limbs whose touch is pleasant according to the seasons
```

This removes the uncertainty of the first-pass Kārikā analysis around `yaśasvi vā`: the Bhāṣya clearly reads `yaśasvī` as the fruit corresponding to a fragrant gift.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The passage is explanatory and gives the authoritative sequence `yathākramam` together with explicit glosses for the correspondences.

## New Technical Terms

- **varṇasampanna** — endowed with excellence of color or appearance  
  **status:** previously encountered vocabulary, newly functionalized here  
  **function:** gift-object qualification

- **gandhasampanna** — endowed with excellent smell or fragrance  
  **status:** rare in this karmic context  
  **function:** gift-object qualification and causal analogy

- **rasasampanna** — endowed with excellent taste  
  **status:** rare in this karmic context  
  **function:** gift-object qualification and causal analogy

- **sparśasampanna** — endowed with excellent tactile quality  
  **status:** rare in this karmic context  
  **function:** gift-object qualification

- **ṛtusukhasparśa** — touch experienced as pleasant according to seasonal conditions  
  **status:** rare  
  **function:** maturation-result

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The verse develops a principle of **formal correspondence between the quality bestowed and the quality matured**.

The act does not simply yield “more merit” in an undifferentiated way. Different sensible excellences of the gift yield differently qualified future embodiment and social appearance.

The clearest cases are analogical:

```text
fragrance spreads in all directions
    → reputation spreads in all directions

good taste is agreeable
    → the giver becomes agreeable / beloved
```

Thus karmic maturation preserves not numerical quantity alone but a recognizable **form of relation**.

The tactile case is especially rich because one cause yields a twofold bodily completion:

```text
excellent touch given
    → refined bodily constitution
    → bodily touch appropriately pleasant under changing seasonal conditions
```

The gift-object is therefore a real determinant of karma, not a neutral carrier of the giver’s intention.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass file correctly identified the general sensible correspondence but left the pairing around `yaśasvi vā` provisional. The Bhāṣya settles it completely.

Recommended translation:

> **The gift-object is distinguished through excellence of color and the other sensible qualities. From this arise beauty, fame, being beloved, delicate limbs, and limbs whose touch is pleasant according to the seasons.**

The exact correspondences are:

- color → beauty;
- smell → fame;
- taste → being beloved;
- touch → delicacy and seasonally pleasant touch.

## Light Organon Contact

The gift-object is the objective middle through which intention becomes actual benefit. VAK 4.116 now shows that the **form of the middle matters**.

```text
subjective wholesome intention
    ↓
qualitatively formed gift-object
    ↓
recipient receives a determinate sensible benefit
    ↓
that form returns in maturation
```

The object is therefore not inert material. Its determinate sensible quality participates in the karmic specification of the act.

This gives a precise form to the principle:

```text
form bestowed
    → form matured
```
