# VAK 4.59 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.59**

> **vaṅkadoṣakaṣāyoktiḥ śāṭhyadveṣajarāgaje |**  
> **kṛṣṇaśuklādibhedena punaḥ karma caturvidham || 4.59 ||**

Best current translation:

> “Crookedness,” “fault,” and “stain” are expressions for what is born respectively from deceit, hatred, and attachment. Again, karma is fourfold according to the division beginning with black and white.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.59.md`

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya first cites a sūtra teaching three bodily, verbal, and mental crookednesses, three faults, and three stains. Vasubandhu then explains the three labels by their afflictive origin:

```text
śāṭhya-ja
    → vaṅka

dveṣa-ja
    → doṣa

rāga-ja
    → kaṣāya
```

He applies each relation across body, speech, and mind.

The prose then turns to the second half of the verse and introduces a fourfold karma classification by citing the sūtra:

```text
asti karma kṛṣṇaṃ kṛṣṇavipākam
asti karma śuklaṃ śuklavipākam
asti karma kṛṣṇaśuklaṃ kṛṣṇaśuklavipākam
asti karmākṛṣṇamaśuklam avipākam
    yat tat karma karmakṣayāya saṃvartate
```

The detailed identifications of these four belong to the following verse, VAK 4.60.

## Source Cautions

This verse is a hinge between two distinct topics:

1. the sūtra vocabulary of crookedness, fault, and stain;
2. the fourfold black/white classification of karma.

The Bhāṣya does not yet fully define the four karmas under VAK 4.59. Those definitions are supplied under VAK 4.60. The present file therefore records the quoted fourfold schema but avoids importing the later analysis prematurely.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Three sūtra labels require causal interpretation

The sūtra speaks of:

```text
three vaṅkas
three doṣas
three kaṣāyas
```

in bodily, verbal, and mental action.

Vasubandhu explains that these are not nine unrelated moral labels. Each triad is generated from a specific afflictive ground.

### 2. Crookedness is generated by deceit

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Bodily karma born from **śāṭhya**, deceit or duplicity, is called bodily **vaṅka**, crookedness, because it follows a crooked or devious course. The same explanation applies to verbal and mental crookedness.

```text
śāṭhya
    → kuṭila-anvaya
    → vaṅka
```

### 3. Fault is generated by hatred

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Bodily karma born from **dveṣa**, hatred, is called bodily **doṣa**, fault/corruption, because it follows the corruption of mind (`cittapradoṣa`). The same applies to speech and mind.

```text
dveṣa
    → cittapradoṣa-anvaya
    → doṣa
```

### 4. Stain is generated by attachment

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Bodily karma born from **rāga**, attachment, is called bodily **kaṣāya**, stain, because it follows the coloring (`rañjana`) produced by attachment. The same applies to speech and mind.

```text
rāga
    → rañjana-anvaya
    → kaṣāya
```

### 5. A new fourfold karma classification is introduced

The second half of the kārikā opens a new sūtra classification:

```text
black karma with black maturation
white karma with white maturation
black-and-white karma with black-and-white maturation
neither-black-nor-white karma without maturation,
    conducive to the exhaustion of karma
```

At VAK 4.59 this is an announced schema. VAK 4.60 supplies its doctrinal allocation.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the sūtra terms vaṅka, doṣa, and kaṣāya through their respective afflictive origins.

**Voice: Scripture cited by Vasubandhu** — the fourfold black/white karma formula.

No significant opponent exchange occurs in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **kuṭilānvayatva** — following or participating in crookedness/deviousness
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary in this passage
  - **function:** causal-semantic explanation of `vaṅka`

- **cittapradoṣa** — corruption or damaging disturbance of mind
  - **status:** prose clarification beyond the Kārikā wording
  - **function:** causal-semantic explanation of `doṣa`

- **rañjana** — coloring, dyeing, affective coloration
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary in this passage
  - **function:** causal-semantic explanation of `kaṣāya`

- **karmakṣaya** — exhaustion/destruction of karma
  - **status:** introduced through the quoted sūtra; becomes technically operative in VAK 4.60–4.62
  - **function:** defines the orientation of the fourth, neither-black-nor-white karma

## Doctrinal Determination

The first half of VAK 4.59 is not merely a synonym list. Vasubandhu gives a **genetic account of moral predicates**:

```text
crookedness
    is named from deceit as its afflictive ground

fault
    is named from hatred as its afflictive ground

stain
    is named from attachment as its afflictive ground
```

These predicates can characterize bodily, verbal, or mental karma because the same afflictive source can generate action in any of the three channels.

The second half then shifts from this affliction-generated vocabulary to a fourfold classification of karma by the paired determination of karma and maturation:

```text
karma-quality
    +
vipāka-quality
```

The fourth class is already structurally distinct because the quoted sūtra says it is `avipāka` and tends toward `karmakṣaya`. Its exact doctrinal identification must wait for VAK 4.60.

## Logical Determination

The first movement has the form:

```text
GROUND
    śāṭhya | dveṣa | rāga

GENERATED ACTION
    bodily | verbal | mental karma

DERIVED PREDICATE
    vaṅka | doṣa | kaṣāya
```

Thus the predicate is not attached externally. It is licensed by the **genetic relation to its afflictive source**.

The second movement introduces a new classification whose principle is relational:

```text
KARMA
    classified together with
ITS MATURATION
```

This prepares a distinction between ordinary karmic production and a fourth kind of activity whose function is not to generate another maturation but to bring karma toward exhaustion.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The existing translation remains sound.

The Bhāṣya strengthens two points:

1. `vaṅka`, `doṣa`, and `kaṣāya` should be understood as predicates generated from specific afflictive origins, not loose moral synonyms;
2. `kṛṣṇaśuklādibhedena` announces a scripturally given fourfold schema whose detailed assignment belongs to VAK 4.60.

No major translation correction is required.

## Organon Contact Point

The first half gives a clean example of **predicate from ground**:

```text
a determination is what it is
because of the generating relation
from which it arises
```

So:

```text
śāṭhya
    → crooked action

dveṣa
    → corrupting action

rāga
    → staining/coloring action
```

The second half then raises the level of classification. Karma is no longer described merely by its immediate afflictive source but by its **relation to its own result**:

```text
ACTION
    ↔
MATURATION
```

VAK 4.60 will determine the content of that relation.
