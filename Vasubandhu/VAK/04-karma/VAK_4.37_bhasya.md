# VAK_4.37_bhasya — Acquisition of Non-Restraint and the Remaining Avijñapti

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
asaṃvarasya kriyayā lābho 'bhyupagamena vā |
śeṣāvijñaptilābhas tu kṣetrādānādarehanāt || 4.37 ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya:

> Non-restraint is acquired either through performance of the action or through undertaking it. The remaining avijñapti, however, is acquired through an appropriate field, through undertaking, or through sufficiently earnest effort.

See: `VAK_4.37.md`.

The Bhāṣya shows that the second half should not be treated as a simple four-item list without qualification. Its prose distributes the causes as: an especially efficacious field; a deliberate undertaking (`samādāna`); or an action pursued with such earnestness (`ādara`) that avijñapti arises.

## 3. Source Cautions

The compact compound `kṣetrādānādarehanāt` is easy to over-segment mechanically.

The Bhāṣya itself gives the controlling interpretation:

```text
kṣetra
    an especially efficacious field

samādāna
    a deliberate undertaking

ādara + īhā
    earnest performance or striving
```

Thus the prose should control the translation rather than forcing four completely independent technical causes from the surface compound.

`śeṣāvijñapti` means the avijñapti remaining after the two principal standing forms already discussed:

```text
saṃvara
asaṃvara
śeṣa-avijñapti
```

It therefore concerns avijñapti that is neither complete restraint nor complete non-restraint.

## 4. Natural Argument

Vasubandhu begins by marking the exact gap left by VAK 4.36:

```text
already explained:
    with respect to what objects asaṃvara is acquired

not yet explained:
    how asaṃvara is acquired
```

The answer is that there are two causes.

### A. Acquisition through action — `kriyā`

For those born into the relevant occupational lineage, non-restraint is acquired through actually carrying out the operation of killing:

```text
vadhaprayogakriyā
    → asaṃvaralābha
```

The Bhāṣya therefore does not present inherited social identity by itself as sufficient. The practical form becomes operative through the occupational action.

### B. Acquisition through undertaking — `abhyupagama`

For persons not born into such a lineage, non-restraint can arise through deliberate adoption of that livelihood:

```text
vayam api anayā jīvikayā jīviṣyāmaḥ

“we too shall live by this livelihood”
```

Here the standing harmful form precedes repeated performance in the sense that the person has explicitly made the occupation his practical determination.

```text
adoption of livelihood
    → asaṃvara
    → future corresponding activity
```

### C. Acquisition of the remaining avijñapti

The Bhāṣya then turns from full `asaṃvara` to other avijñapti.

#### 1. Through the field — `kṣetra`

A field may possess such karmic significance that the mere giving of a monastery or similar support generates avijñapti.

The example is the class of meritorious actions based on material support (`aupadhika puṇyakriyāvastu`).

```text
specially qualified field
    + donation
        → avijñapti
```

The field is therefore not merely the physical recipient. It is the relational field in virtue of which the act has this karmic efficacy.

#### 2. Through undertaking — `samādāna`

A person may establish a determinate practice by undertaking something such as:

```text
“I will not eat before paying homage to the Buddha.”

“I will regularly provide meals on the lunar day,
monthly, or fortnightly.”
```

The undertaking gives a continuing form to conduct beyond the isolated moment of speech.

#### 3. Through earnest performance — `ādara` / `īhā`

Finally, one may perform a wholesome or unwholesome action with such earnestness and intensity that avijñapti arises from it.

```text
kuśala or akuśala action
    + strong earnest engagement
        → avijñapti
```

Bare occurrence is therefore insufficient as the general model. The manner in which the action is pursued matters to whether a persistent unmanifest determination is generated.

The section then closes the whole acquisition discussion:

```text
saṃvara
asaṃvara
saṃvarāsaṃvaretara avijñapti

    acquisition explained
```

Vasubandhu immediately announces the next topic: abandonment (`tyāga`).

## 5. Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu / main exposition**

The passage is predominantly straightforward exposition.

No new explicit school dispute is introduced inside VAK 4.37 itself. The preceding sentence still carries the Vaibhāṣika/Sautrāntika dispute from 4.36, but 4.37 proceeds by explaining modes of acquisition rather than reopening that dispute.

The occupational examples should therefore not automatically be assigned to a named school unless later textual evidence requires it.

## 6. New Technical Terms

- **`vadhaprayogakriyā`** — performance of the operation/preparation of killing; **status:** Bhāṣya specification; **function:** explains acquisition of asaṃvara through enacted occupational practice.
- **`tatkulīna`** — one belonging to that occupational lineage; **status:** Bhāṣya-only contextual term here; **function:** distinguishes action-based acquisition from deliberate adoption by outsiders.
- **`jīvikā`** — livelihood; **status:** important practical term; **function:** identifies asaṃvara as a standing occupational determination rather than an isolated harmful deed.
- **`śeṣāvijñapti`** — remaining avijñapti; **status:** technical classification; **function:** covers avijñapti other than complete saṃvara and asaṃvara.
- **`kṣetra`** — karmically efficacious field; **status:** established Buddhist technical term with a specific function here; **function:** explains how relation to a qualified field can generate avijñapti.
- **`aupadhika puṇyakriyāvastu`** — basis of meritorious action involving material support; **status:** Bhāṣya technical example; **function:** illustrates acquisition through field.
- **`samādāna`** — deliberate undertaking; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** generates continuing practical form through an adopted rule or practice.
- **`ādara`** — earnestness, seriousness, intensity; **status:** important here; **function:** marks the qualitative strength of engagement capable of producing avijñapti.
- **`īhā` / `īhate`** — striving, active effort, performance; **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary; **function:** links earnest intention to enacted wholesome or unwholesome practice.

## 7. Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.37 completes the acquisition side of the avijñapti taxonomy.

```text
asaṃvara
    acquired by:
        kriyā
        or abhyupagama

remaining avijñapti
    acquired by:
        qualified kṣetra
        samādāna
        earnest īhā
```

The important doctrinal point is that persistent karmic form does not have one uniform genesis.

Different forms of avijñapti arise through different structures:

```text
repeated or constitutive action
formal adoption of a livelihood
relation to an efficacious field
undertaken rule
intensely pursued wholesome/unwholesome action
```

This is more precise than saying simply that avijñapti is “produced by intention.”

## 8. Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā file's first half remains sound:

> Non-restraint is acquired through action or through undertaking.

The second half should be made more cautious.

Rather than treating `kṣetra-ādāna-ādara-īhana` as four wholly independent factors, the Bhāṣya supports:

> The remaining avijñapti is acquired through an appropriate field, through undertaking, or through sufficiently earnest performance.

This preserves the prose distribution:

```text
kṣetraṃ vā ...
athavā samādānam ādatte ...
ādareṇa ... kriyām īhate ...
```

The existing Kārikā analysis should therefore be read as structurally suggestive but refined by this Bhāṣya determination.

## 9. Logical Determination

The passage distinguishes two directions of genesis for `asaṃvara`:

```text
actual performance
    → standing harmful form

standing practical adoption
    → future corresponding performance
```

These are not contradictory.

They show that a practical determination can be generated either through **actualized practice** or through a sufficiently determinate **undertaking of a mode of life**.

The remaining avijñapti adds a second lesson:

```text
same visible class of act
    ≠ necessarily same persistent karmic result
```

Generation depends on relational and qualitative conditions:

```text
field
undertaking
earnestness in performance
```

Thus Abhidharma is discriminating the genesis of persistent karmic determination, not merely classifying visible movements.

## 10. Organon Contact Point

A restrained contact point is the distinction between **event** and **generated practical form**.

```text
action as occurrence
    ≠ automatically
persistent determination
```

What matters is how the occurrence is mediated:

```text
through a standing livelihood
through explicit undertaking
through a qualified field
through earnest execution
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives a genuinely genetic analysis: it asks under what determinate conditions an action ceases to be merely episodic and generates a continuing practical determination.

No stronger metaphysical identification is needed at this stage.
