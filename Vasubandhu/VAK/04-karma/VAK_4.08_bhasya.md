# VAK_4.08 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
kāme ’pi nivṛtā nāsti samutthānam asad yataḥ |
paramārthaśubho mokṣaḥ svato mūla-hrī-apatrapāḥ ||
```

**Working translation:** Even in the desire domain there is no obscured-indeterminate manifestation, because its originating condition is unwholesome. Liberation is wholesome in the ultimate sense; the wholesome roots, hrī, and apatrāpya are wholesome by their own nature.

### Source cautions

The e-text reads `nivṛktāvyākṛtaṃ` at [202|04], evidently for `nivṛtāvyākṛtaṃ`; this file preserves the transmitted reading in the source block and treats the normalized form only as an editorial hypothesis.

The compound transmitted in the kārikā as `mūlahyrapatrapāḥ` is explained unambiguously by the prose as `trīṇi kuśalamūlāni`, `hrī`, and `apatrāpya`.

The initial `nivṛtā` concerns `vijñapti`, not `avijñapti`. The Kārikā-pass translation should therefore be corrected accordingly.

## Natural Argument

The verse closes the domain-distribution discussion of manifest action and then opens a new taxonomy of goodness.

First, Vasubandhu states that obscured-indeterminate (`nivṛtāvyākṛta`) **manifest action** does not occur in the desire domain. Such manifest action is attested only in Brahmaloka; the example is Mahābrahmā's deceit-motivated bodily act directed toward Āyuṣmat Aśvajit.

He then asks why manifest action does not exist above Brahmaloka and why obscured-indeterminate manifest action does not exist in the desire domain. The answer is given through `samutthāna`: manifest action is produced by a mind accompanied by `vitarka` and `vicāra`. Such a mind is absent from the second dhyāna upward. Moreover, the mind producing this manifestation is of the class abandoned by cultivation (`bhāvanāprahātavya`), whereas what is abandoned by seeing (`darśanaprahātavya`) proceeds inwardly and therefore does not generate this outward manifestation. In the desire domain there is no `nivṛtāvyākṛta` mind of the relevant producing class.

This immediately provokes a broader question: are dharmas to be classified as wholesome and unwholesome solely from their originating condition? Vasubandhu answers no. Their ethical determination is known in four distinct ways:

```text
paramārthataḥ
    ultimately

svabhāvataḥ
    by intrinsic nature

saṃprayogataḥ
    by association

samutthānataḥ
    by originating condition
```

VAK 4.08 itself gives the first two.

`Mokṣa`, identified here with nirvāṇa, is wholesome in the ultimate sense because it is the complete pacification of suffering and therefore supreme security, compared with health.

The three wholesome roots, together with `hrī` and `apatrāpya`, are wholesome by intrinsic nature because their goodness does not depend upon association with another wholesome factor or upon being produced by another wholesome originating condition. Vasubandhu compares them with beneficial medicine.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of the distribution of `nivṛtā vijñapti`, explanation through `samutthāna`, and introduction of the fourfold classification of goodness.

**Voice: reported doctrinal tradition** — the example of Mahābrahmā's deceit-produced bodily action is introduced as something heard or transmitted (`śrūyate`).

**Voice: Objector / interlocutor** — the questions asking why manifestation is absent above Brahmaloka and whether wholesome/unwholesome status is determined only through `samutthāna`.

## New Technical Terms

- **nivṛtāvyākṛta** — obscured-indeterminate; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** ethical classification of a defiled but indeterminate mental/action state.
- **samutthāna** — originating condition / activating source; **status:** now becoming central; **function:** explains how an action is generated and one of four modes by which ethical quality may be predicated.
- **paramārthataḥ kuśala** — wholesome ultimately; **status:** new explicit classification; **function:** goodness predicated from final liberative status.
- **svabhāvataḥ kuśala** — wholesome by intrinsic nature; **status:** new explicit classification; **function:** goodness belonging without dependence on association or originating condition.
- **saṃprayogataḥ kuśala** — wholesome by association; **status:** introduced here, completed in VAK 4.09; **function:** derivative ethical determination through conjunction with intrinsically wholesome factors.
- **samutthānataḥ kuśala** — wholesome by originating condition; **status:** introduced here, completed in VAK 4.09; **function:** derivative ethical determination through production by wholesome factors.
- **hrī** — ethical self-respect / moral shame; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** intrinsically wholesome mental factor.
- **apatrāpya** — moral circumspection / regard; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** intrinsically wholesome mental factor.
- **kuśalamūla** — wholesome root; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** intrinsically wholesome causal root.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya corrects an important first-pass assumption:

```text
kāme 'pi nivṛtā nāsti
```

refers to `vijñapti`, not `avijñapti`.

The domain structure is therefore:

```text
nivṛtāvyākṛta vijñapti
    not in kāmadhātu
    attested in Brahmaloka

vijñapti in general
    requires a savitarka-savicāra mind
    therefore absent above first dhyāna
```

The second half establishes that `kuśala` is not a single univocal predication. Vasubandhu explicitly distinguishes four grounds of ethical attribution:

```text
1. paramārtha
2. svabhāva
3. saṃprayoga
4. samutthāna
```

VAK 4.08 determines:

```text
mokṣa / nirvāṇa
    → paramārthataḥ kuśala

three kuśalamūlas + hrī + apatrāpya
    → svabhāvataḥ kuśala
```

The remaining two modes are developed immediately in VAK 4.09.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation must be corrected. The understood subject of `kāme 'pi nivṛtā nāsti` is **vijñapti**, not avijñapti.

A revised translation is:

> Even in the desire domain there is no obscured-indeterminate manifestation, because its originating condition is unwholesome. Liberation is wholesome in the ultimate sense; the wholesome roots, hrī, and apatrāpya are wholesome by their own nature.

The second pāda `samutthānam asad yataḥ` should not be read as a universal assertion that the origin of all non-manifest action is unwholesome. It explains the restriction on the relevant type of `vijñapti`.

## Logical Determination

The first movement is causal:

```text
vijñapti
    requires a determinate samutthāna

samutthāna
    must be a savitarka-savicāra mind

where such a mind is absent
    vijñapti cannot arise
```

But Vasubandhu immediately blocks the inference that ethical predicates reduce entirely to causal origin.

```text
ethical determination
    ≠ samutthāna alone
```

Instead the predicate `kuśala` is differentiated according to four relations by which it belongs to a dharma.

```text
ultimate goodness
    relation to final security / cessation of suffering

intrinsic goodness
    belongs from the dharma's own nature

associative goodness
    belongs through conjunction

originative goodness
    belongs through production
```

This is a classification of **grounds of predication**, not merely a list of wholesome entities.

## Organon Contact Point

The text gives a particularly clean logical contact point without requiring a deeper reconstruction yet.

`Kuśala` is not treated as a flat quality attached identically to all wholesome dharmas. Vasubandhu distinguishes the mediating relation through which the predicate belongs:

```text
in itself
    svabhāvataḥ

through relation
    saṃprayogataḥ

through genesis
    samutthānataḥ

through final truth / end-state
    paramārthataḥ
```

For the present pass, this should remain a textual observation rather than a forced correspondence to a later logical system. It is exactly the kind of structure worth returning to in a dedicated study after the chapter corpus is complete.
