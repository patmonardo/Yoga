# VAK 4.100 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `bhikṣur dṛkcarito vṛttī bhinatty anyatra bāliśān | śāstṛmārgāntarakṣāntau bhinno na vivasaty asau || 4.100 ||`

**Best current translation:** A monk, governed by doctrinal view and of conduct capable of commanding acceptance, divides the spiritually immature away from the Teacher's presence. The Saṅgha is divided when they assent to another teacher and another path; that division does not last beyond the same night.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.100.md](./VAK_4.100.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
kaḥ punareṣa saṃghaṃ bhanatti /

bhikṣur ... dṛkcarito ... bhinatti /

bhikṣurbhinatti na gṛhī na bhikṣuṇyādayaḥ /
sa ca dṛṣṭicarita eva na tṛṣṇācaritaḥ /
ṛtastho na bhinnavṛttastasyānādeyavākyatvāt /

kva bhinatti /
atra bhagavān saṃnihitastataḥ /

anyatra

nahi śāstureva saṃnidhau śakyo bhettum /
tathāgatānāṃ duṣprasahatvādādeyavākyatvācca /

kān bhinatti /

bāliśān /

pṛthagjanāneva nāryān pratyakṣadharmatvāt /
tānapi na kṣāntilābhina ityapare /

kiyatā bhinnaḥ saṃgho bhavati /

śāstṛmārgāntarakṣāntau bhinnaḥ

yadā tebhyastathāgatādanyaḥ śāstā kṣamate tadupadiṣṭācca mārgādanyo mārga iyatā bhinnaḥ saṃgho vaktavyaḥ /

kiyantaṃ kālaṃ bhinna āste /
tāmeva rātriṃ

na vivasatyasau // VAkK_4.100 //

aparyuṣita eva hi saṃghabhede saṃgho 'vaśyaṃ pratisaṃdhīyate /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya answers five successive questions:

1. Who can split the Saṅgha?
2. What kind of motivation and conduct must the divider have?
3. Where can the schism be effected?
4. Whom can he split, and what counts as completed division?
5. How long can such a schism persist?

The argument therefore moves from **qualified agent**, through **qualified audience and locus**, to the **constitutive criterion of completed communal division**.

## Literal Prose Translation

Who is it that splits the Saṅgha? A monk splits it, not a layman, nor a nun and the like. He is one whose conduct is governed by a view, not one whose conduct is governed merely by craving. He must also be a person whose manner of life permits his speech to be accepted, not one of visibly broken conduct, because the words of such a person would not be received.

Where does he split it? Elsewhere than where the Blessed One himself is present. The Saṅgha cannot be split in the Teacher's immediate presence, because Tathāgatas are difficult to overcome and their words command acceptance.

Whom does he split? The spiritually immature: ordinary beings only, not noble persons, because the latter directly realize Dharma. Some say that even ordinary beings who have attained `kṣānti` cannot be split.

When is the Saṅgha to be called divided? When those followers accept someone other than the Tathāgata as teacher and accept a path other than the path taught by him. At that point the Saṅgha is divided.

How long does it remain divided? Only that same night. It does not pass the night in division, for the Saṅgha must necessarily be reunited before the schism has endured overnight.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The divider must be an internal religious agent

The Bhāṣya excludes laypersons, nuns, and others from the technical role of divider.

```text
qualified bhikṣu
    → possible formal divider

outsider / non-qualified agent
    → cannot constitute this technical saṃghabheda
```

Saṅgha-schism is therefore not simply hostility directed at a Buddhist community from outside. It is an internally mediated rupture of the monastic body.

### 2. Doctrinal view, not mere craving

Vasubandhu specifies:

```text
dṛṣṭicarita
    governed by view

not merely

tṛṣṇācarita
    governed by craving
```

The schism at issue is consequently doctrinal and normative. The divider attempts to reorganize the community around another account of teacher and path.

### 3. The divider must retain social credibility

The Bhāṣya's next qualification is easily missed. One of manifestly broken conduct cannot effectively split the community because his speech is `anādeyavākya`—not something others will accept.

Thus successful schism requires not only false doctrine but **credible authority**.

```text
false doctrine
    + socially receivable speaker
    → possible communal uptake
```

### 4. The Buddha's immediate presence blocks the schism

`anyatra` is explained concretely: the split must occur elsewhere than where the Blessed One is present.

The reason is not magical proximity. The Bhāṣya appeals to the Tathāgata's unsurpassability and the authoritative force of his speech.

Thus the false teacher requires a field in which rival normative recognition can actually be established.

### 5. Only the spiritually immature are susceptible

`bāliśa` is glossed as `pṛthagjana`, ordinary beings.

Noble persons cannot be split because:

```text
pratyakṣadharmatva
    direct realization of Dharma
```

Their relation to the Path no longer depends merely upon second-hand acceptance of a claimant's representation.

A reported alternative position (`apare`) further excludes ordinary beings who have attained `kṣānti`.

### 6. Assent completes the schism

The decisive criterion is not the divider's utterance alone.

The community is divided when followers **accept**:

```text
another teacher
    +
another path
```

Hence the complete mediation is:

```text
mṛṣāvāda
    → reception / assent
    → rival teacher-and-path recognition
    → asāmagrī
```

The previous verses distinguished causal false speech from the resulting non-concord. VAK 4.100 supplies the missing middle term: **acceptance by members of the community**.

### 7. The division cannot survive the night

`na vivasati` is literal and strong. This special schism does not endure overnight. The Bhāṣya says the community is necessarily reunited before the divided state becomes an overnight condition.

This prepares the next distinction between this special **wheel-schism (`cakrabheda`)** and the more ordinary **procedural/action schism (`karmabheda`)** of VAK 4.101.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / Vaibhāṣika doctrinal exposition** — restricts the divider to a monk governed by view, with socially credible conduct.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explains why division cannot occur in the Buddha's immediate presence.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — identifies `bāliśa` with ordinary beings and excludes nobles through direct realization of Dharma.
- **Voice: reported teachers (`apare`)** — further exclude ordinary beings who have attained `kṣānti`.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — defines completed schism through assent to another teacher and another path, and states that the schism cannot remain overnight.

## New Technical Terms

- **dṛṣṭicarita** — one whose conduct is governed by doctrinal view.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** specifies the motivational type capable of formal schism.

- **tṛṣṇācarita** — one whose conduct is governed by craving.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya contrast term.
  - **function:** excluded motivational type.

- **ādeyavākya / anādeyavākya** — speech that is / is not accepted as authoritative or credible.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary here.
  - **function:** explains why outward credibility is required for successful division.

- **bāliśa** — spiritually immature person, glossed here as `pṛthagjana`.
  - **status:** explicit Kārikā term, doctrinally specified by Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** identifies susceptible recipients.

- **pratyakṣadharmatva** — direct realization or direct presence of Dharma.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term.
  - **function:** reason noble persons cannot be doctrinally split away from the authentic Path.

- **śāstrantara-kṣānti** — assent to another teacher.
  - **status:** explicit structural meaning of the Kārikā compound.
  - **function:** one constitutive condition of completed schism.

- **mārgāntara-kṣānti** — assent to another path.
  - **status:** explicit structural meaning of the Kārikā compound.
  - **function:** second constitutive condition of completed schism.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 4.98–4.100 now form one complete causal analysis:

```text
divider's false speech
    ↓
followers' assent
    ↓
alternative teacher + alternative path
    ↓
Saṅgha's asāmagrī
```

The bearer relations remain distinct:

```text
divider
    bears the culpable speech-karma

followers
    bear the assent

Saṅgha
    bears the resulting non-concord
```

Thus successful division requires both **causal agency** and **recognitive uptake**.

The Bhāṣya also yields a precise epistemic asymmetry:

```text
pṛthagjana
    can be redirected through authoritative representation

ārya
    cannot, because Dharma is directly realized
```

This is the text's own reason; it should not be generalized beyond the doctrinal context.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation correctly saw doctrinal assent as constitutive, but the Bhāṣya sharpens several points:

1. `anyatra` means **away from the Buddha's immediate presence**.
2. `bāliśān` means **ordinary beings**, contrasted explicitly with nobles.
3. The ambiguous conduct qualification is explained through the requirement that the divider's words remain socially receivable.
4. `na vivasati` is literal: **the special schism does not endure overnight**.

A more exact translation is therefore:

> A monk governed by doctrinal view, whose conduct leaves his speech capable of acceptance, splits spiritually immature ordinary beings away from the Teacher's presence. The Saṅgha is divided when they assent to another teacher and another path; such a division does not last beyond that night.

## Light Organon Contact

The Bhāṣya provides a remarkably exact model of institutional rupture:

```text
representation
    → recognition
    → normative reorganization
    → collective division
```

False speech is causally effective only when it becomes **accepted speech**. The collective transformation is therefore mediated neither by utterance alone nor by private belief alone, but by socially organized recognition of a rival teacher and path.

That is the strongest logical gain of VAK 4.100: **recognition is the middle term between false representation and institutional actuality.**
