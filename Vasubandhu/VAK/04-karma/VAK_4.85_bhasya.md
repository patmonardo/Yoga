# VAK 4.85 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā

**sarve ’dhipati-niṣyanda-vipāka-phaladā matāḥ |**  
**duḥkhanān māraṇād ojo-nāśanāt trividhaṃ phalam || 4.85 ||**

> All the karmic courses are held to give dominant, outflow, and maturation results. Because they cause suffering, death, and destruction of vitality, the result is threefold.

See: [VAK_4.85.md](./VAK_4.85.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya first states the general rule for the ten unwholesome and ten wholesome karmic courses:

> `ta ete daśākuśalāḥ karmapathāḥ kuśalāś ca sarve 'dhipatiniṣyandavipākaphaladā matāḥ`

All are held to produce three kinds of result:

- `vipāka-phala` — maturation result
- `niṣyanda-phala` — outflow / corresponding result
- `adhipati-phala` — dominant result

The prose then gives the three levels concretely.

### 1. Maturation result

For the unwholesome courses, when cultivated, developed, and repeatedly practiced:

> `akuśalais tāvat sarvair evāsevitabhāvitabahulīkṛtaiḥ narakeṣūpapadyate / tad eṣāṃ vipākaphalam /`

One is reborn in hell. This is their `vipāka-phala`.

### 2. Outflow-result

If afterward one reaches human similarity, each course has a corresponding result:

- killing → short life
- theft → loss of possessions
- sexual misconduct → an unfaithful / unsatisfactory spouse relation
- false speech → frequent false accusation
- divisive speech → rupture of friendships
- harsh speech → hearing unpleasant sounds
- disordered speech → speech that lacks authority / is not accepted
- covetousness → intense greed
- ill will → intense hatred
- false view → intense delusion

The Bhāṣya explicitly calls this:

> `idam eṣāṃ niḥṣyandaphalam`

### 3. Dominant result

Repeated cultivation of the same courses deforms the external field:

- killing → external things have little vitality
- theft → lightning, dust, and destructive environmental disturbances become abundant
- sexual misconduct → dusty / polluted surroundings
- false speech → foul-smelling conditions
- divisive speech → uneven, broken terrain
- harsh speech → barren, rough, hostile ground
- disordered speech → irregular seasons
- covetousness → dried-up fruits
- ill will → bitter fruits
- false view → meager or fruitless yields

The Bhāṣya says:

> `idam eṣām adhipatiphalam`

## Natural Segmentation

The passage has four argumentative movements:

1. establish three result-types for all karmapathas;
2. enumerate the unwholesome `vipāka`, `niṣyanda`, and `adhipati` results;
3. answer an objection concerning whether a corresponding result is numerically the same result produced by the original karma;
4. explain the threefold result through the killing example and then reverse the pattern for wholesome courses.

## Literal Prose Translation

All ten unwholesome karmic courses and the wholesome ones are regarded as productive of dominant, outflow, and maturation results.

First, through all unwholesome courses that have been practiced, cultivated, and repeatedly developed, one is reborn among the hells. That is their maturation result.

If afterward one comes into the human condition: through killing one has short life; through taking what is not given one suffers loss of possessions; through sexual misconduct one has an unsatisfactory spouse relation; through false speech one is frequently falsely accused; through divisive speech friendships are broken; through harsh speech one hears disagreeable sounds; through disordered speech one’s words are not accepted; through covetousness one has intense greed; through ill will intense hatred; through false view intense delusion. This is their outflow-result.

Through repeated killing, external things have little vitality; through theft there are destructive atmospheric and environmental conditions; through sexual misconduct, polluted conditions; through false speech, foul smells; through divisive speech, uneven terrain; through harsh speech, barren and hostile ground; through disordered speech, irregular seasons; through covetousness, dried-up fruits; through ill will, bitter fruits; through false view, little fruit or none. This is their dominant result.

## The Objection About the Outflow-Result

An objection arises because even a short human lifespan is still a wholesome result:

> How then can short life be called the outflow-result of killing?

The answer is important:

> `nocyate tad evāyus tasya phalam`

It is **not** said that that very lifespan itself is the fruit of killing.

Rather:

> `tenālpāyur bhavatīti`

Because of that karma, one becomes short-lived.

The Bhāṣya then gives the precise relation:

> `ato 'ntarāyahetuḥ prāṇātipātas tasyāyuṣo bhavatīti veditavyam`

Killing becomes a **ground of obstruction** (`antarāya-hetu`) with respect to that lifespan.

This prevents a crude identity model:

```text
past killing
    ≠
this later lifespan itself
```

Instead:

```text
past killing
    ↓
obstruction-ground
    ↓
later shortness of life
```

The Bhāṣya also notes a disagreement over whether the karma producing the human life itself is numerically the same karma responsible for the corresponding outflow effect. One opinion says yes; another distinguishes preparatory and principal phases. A further response explains that `niṣyanda-phala` is called such because of a special relation of similarity, even though doctrinally it does not fall outside the more general classes of maturation and dominant result.

> `yad apy etan niḥṣyandaphalam uktaṃ naitad dvayam ativartate vipākaphalam adhipatiphalaṃ ca / sādṛśyaviśeṣāt tu tathoktam /`

Thus `niṣyanda` is not an ontologically independent third universe of fruit. It is named by a distinctive **correspondence or similarity**.

## Why Three Results?

The Bhāṣya asks:

> Why do these karmic courses produce this threefold result?

It uses killing as the model.

In killing another:

1. one produces suffering in the victim;
2. one kills the victim;
3. one destroys the victim’s vitality (`ojas`).

The three results correspond:

```text
causing suffering
    → vipāka-phala
    → one suffers in hell

causing death
    → niṣyanda-phala
    → one becomes short-lived

destroying vitality
    → adhipati-phala
    → external medicines / supports have little vitality
```

The Bhāṣya says the same pattern should be applied to the other courses:

> `evam anyeṣv api yojyam`

## Wholesome Reversal

The same threefold structure applies to wholesome karmapathas.

For example, when abstention from killing is cultivated, developed, and repeatedly practiced:

- one is born among the gods;
- if one later attains human similarity, one is long-lived;
- the remaining corresponding results are to be understood by reversal of the unwholesome cases.

The Bhāṣya therefore directs:

> `akuśalaviparyayeṇa sarvaṃ yojayitavyam`

Everything is to be applied by reversal of the unwholesome case.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — main exposition and explanation of the three result-types.

**Voice: Objector** — asks how short human life can be an outflow-result of killing when even short human life is itself a wholesome maturation.

**Voice: attributed doctrinal positions** — competing explanations about whether the same karma accounts for the later outflow effect, and whether preparatory or principal karma should be assigned that role.

## New Technical Terms

- **vipāka-phala** — maturation result. **Status:** previously encountered conceptually, here systematically coordinated with the karmapathas. **Function:** specifies rebirth-level fruition.
- **niṣyanda-phala** — outflow / corresponding result. **Status:** developed here in detail. **Function:** marks a later result resembling or corresponding to the form of the karmic course.
- **adhipati-phala** — dominant result. **Status:** developed here in detail. **Function:** marks the broader environmental field conditioned by karma.
- **āsevita** — practiced. **Status:** Bhāṣya emphasis. **Function:** indicates repeated enactment required for strong fruition.
- **bhāvita** — cultivated / developed. **Status:** Bhāṣya emphasis. **Function:** indicates stabilization of the karmic determination.
- **bahulīkṛta** — made abundant / repeatedly intensified. **Status:** Bhāṣya emphasis. **Function:** marks repeated reinforcement.
- **antarāya-hetu** — ground of obstruction. **Status:** important Bhāṣya-only clarification here. **Function:** prevents identifying the later corresponding result with the very same resultant lifespan or object.
- **sādṛśya-viśeṣa** — special relation of similarity. **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term. **Function:** explains why a result is conventionally called `niṣyanda`.
- **ojas** — vitality / vigor. **Status:** rare in this passage. **Function:** mediates the explanation of `adhipati-phala` through the killing example.

## Doctrinal Determination

The three fruits are not three arbitrary rewards attached to one act. They correspond to three distinct dimensions of karmic efficacy:

```text
vipāka
    transformation of the agent’s mode of rebirth

niṣyanda
    corresponding determination within a later existence

adhipati
    conditioning of the broader external field
```

The killing example reveals the internal rationale:

```text
injury to sentient experience
    → later suffering

interruption of life
    → later shortened life

destruction of vitality
    → weakened environmental vitality
```

The Bhāṣya therefore treats karmic result as a structured propagation of the form of the deed through different causal registers.

## Logical Determination

The first-pass model:

```text
one karmic course
    → three fruits
```

is correct but incomplete.

The Bhāṣya supplies the mediation:

```text
one completed karmic determination
    ↓
multiple causal relations
    ↓
agent-level maturation
corresponding later determination
field-level conditioning
```

The decisive warning is:

```text
similar result
    ≠
numerically identical result
```

A later short lifespan is not itself the unwholesome fruit in the crude sense that killing directly manufactures that lifespan. Rather, killing functions as an obstruction-ground relative to a lifespan produced under other karmic conditions.

This is a much more sophisticated causal model:

```text
resulting existence
    has its own productive grounds

past karma
    can constrain, obstruct,
    or qualify that resulting existence
```

The `niṣyanda` relation is therefore one of structured correspondence, not simple substance-transfer.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation of the second half should be read with the Bhāṣya’s exact mapping:

```text
duḥkhana
    causing suffering
    → maturation result

māraṇa
    killing
    → outflow-result

ojo-nāśana
    destroying vitality
    → dominant result
```

So the verse does not merely say that suffering, death, and vitality-loss somehow explain three fruits collectively. It establishes a one-to-one causal correspondence through the killing example.

`Niṣyanda-phala` should remain **outflow-result / corresponding result**, with the Bhāṣya’s `sādṛśya-viśeṣa` kept in mind. “Similar result” alone is too weak if it suggests mere resemblance without causal mediation.

## Light Organon Contact

The useful logical distinction is not “one operation returns three values.” It is stronger:

```text
one determination
    enters several causal relations
    according to what aspect of the determination is taken
```

Thus the same practical act can be considered with respect to:

```text
its effect on the agent’s future mode
its corresponding later limitation
its conditioning of the surrounding field
```

The Bhāṣya also gives us a strong anti-reduction principle:

```text
causal contribution
    ≠
sole production of the resultant state
```

An `antarāya-hetu` constrains a result without being identical with the complete productive ground of that result.

That is a genuinely reusable form of causal reasoning and is much more valuable than the first-pass software analogy.

## Bhāṣya Upgrade to the Kārikā

The Super Kārikās pass should retain at least these clarifications:

1. all karmapathas give `vipāka`, `niṣyanda`, and `adhipati` results;
2. for killing specifically:
   - causing suffering → maturation result;
   - causing death → outflow-result;
   - destroying vitality → dominant result;
3. `niṣyanda` is named because of a special correspondence (`sādṛśya-viśeṣa`), not because the later result is numerically identical with the prior karma’s immediate fruit;
4. wholesome fruits are understood by reversal of the unwholesome structure.
