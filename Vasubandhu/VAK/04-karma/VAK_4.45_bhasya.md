# VAK_4.45_bhasya — The Fundamental Classifications of Karma

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.45**

```iast
kṣemākṣemetarat karma kuśalākuśaletarat |
puṇyāpuṇyam aniñjaṃ ca sukhavedyādi ca trayam ||
```

> Action is secure, insecure, or other—that is, wholesome, unwholesome, or other. It is also meritorious, demeritorious, or immovable, and threefold according to whether its maturation is experienced as pleasure, pain, or neither.

## Source Cautions

The source e-text explicitly begins a new major section at [227|04]: Vasubandhu now turns from restraint and non-restraint to the karmas enumerated in the sūtras.

The e-text reads `puṇyāpuṇyamaviñjaṃ` at [227|11], whereas the Kārikā source gives `aniñjaṃ`, and the prose immediately uses `āneñjam`. The quoted source is preserved without silent correction.

The Bhāṣya materially sharpens the first-pass Kārikā analysis: `kṣema / akṣema / itarat` is not presented as a separate classification parallel to `kuśala / akuśala / itarat`. Vasubandhu says explicitly:

```text
idaṃ kuśalādīnāṃ lakṣaṇam
    this is the defining characteristic of kuśala and the others
```

Thus the first pāda supplies the criterion by which the second triad is understood.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first announces the standard threefold division:

```text
karma
    kuśala
    akuśala
    avyākṛta
```

The Kārikā then gives their defining marks:

```text
kṣema
    = kuśala

akṣema
    = akuśala

neither kṣema nor akṣema
    = avyākṛta
```

But `kṣema` is not left as an unexplained synonym for "good." Vasubandhu determines wholesome karma through two consequences:

```text
kuśala / kṣema karma
    → iṣṭa-vipāka
       desirable maturation

    → nirvāṇa-prāpaka
       conducive to the attainment of nirvāṇa

because:
    → duḥkha-paritrāṇa
       it protects / rescues from suffering
```

This is the most important determination in the verse. `Kuśala` is not characterized merely by social approval, intention, or an abstract moral predicate. It is **secure (`kṣema`) because of the kind of result and direction it establishes**: desirable maturation and, ultimately, liberation from suffering.

The opposite is `akuśala`:

```text
akuśala / akṣema karma
    → aniṣṭa-vipāka
       undesirable maturation

    → stands as the contrary of kṣema
```

Vasubandhu adds `tatkālam atyantaṃ ca`: the opposition concerns insecurity both in the relevant temporal result and ultimately. The precise scope of this phrase should remain close to the text; the basic point is that akuśala is determined as the contrary of the security produced by kuśala.

The third member is then generated negatively:

```text
neither kṣema
nor akṣema
    → neither kuśala nor akuśala
    → avyākṛta
```

So `avyākṛta` is not a vague middle state. Here it is exactly the residual class that falls under neither side of the kṣema/akṣema opposition.

After establishing this primary ethical-soteriological determination, Vasubandhu announces two further threefold divisions:

```text
puṇya
apuṇya
āneñja
```

and

```text
sukhavedanīya
    karma whose maturation is to be experienced pleasantly

duḥkhavedanīya
    karma whose maturation is to be experienced painfully

aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya
    karma whose maturation is to be experienced as neither pain nor pleasure
```

The first of these is only enumerated here. Its decisive explanation begins immediately with VAK 4.46. The second is likewise announced here and elaborated in the following verses.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose throughout this unit.

There is no developed objection in VAK 4.45 itself. The dialectical testing of `āneñja` begins with the following verse.

## New Technical Terms

- **lakṣaṇa** — defining mark or determining characteristic
  - **status:** previously encountered; newly decisive in this local classification
  - **function:** tells us that `kṣema / akṣema / itarat` defines `kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta`

- **iṣṭavipāka** — desirable maturation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the Kārikā
  - **function:** one defining consequence of wholesome karma

- **aniṣṭavipāka** — undesirable maturation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the Kārikā
  - **function:** defining consequence of unwholesome karma

- **nirvāṇaprāpaka** — leading to / conducive to the attainment of nirvāṇa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only determination in this verse
  - **function:** supplies the explicitly soteriological criterion of wholesome karma

- **duḥkhaparitrāṇa** — protection or rescue from suffering
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only determination in this verse
  - **function:** explains why wholesome karma is `kṣema`

- **kṣemapratidvandva** — contrary or opposed counterpart of security
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only compound
  - **function:** defines `akṣema / akuśala` relationally as the contrary of `kṣema / kuśala`

- **vedanīya** — to be experienced, to be felt
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā and made explicit in prose
  - **function:** classifies karma according to the feeling-character of its maturation

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya reveals that the opening two apparent triads in the Kārikā are structurally one determination:

```text
KARMA

kṣema
    = kuśala
    = desirable maturation
    + conducive to nirvāṇa
    + protection from suffering

akṣema
    = akuśala
    = undesirable maturation
    + contrary of kṣema

itarat
    = avyākṛta
    = neither kṣema nor akṣema
```

This is considerably stronger than translating `kuśala` simply as "morally good."

The criterion has both a karmic and a soteriological horizon:

```text
immanent consequence
    iṣṭa / aniṣṭa vipāka

ultimate direction
    nirvāṇa / non-security
```

Wholesomeness is therefore determined through the relation of action to the welfare and liberation of the continuum.

The verse then opens two new axes that will be developed in the following verses:

```text
realm/result stability
    → puṇya / apuṇya / āneñja

felt maturation
    → sukha / duḥkha / aduḥkhāsukha vedanīya
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation treated `kṣema / akṣema / other` and `kuśala / akuśala / other` as two distinct classifications. The Bhāṣya corrects that reading.

Preferred Bhāṣya-informed translation:

> **Karma is secure, insecure, or other—that is, wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate. It is further meritorious, demeritorious, or immovable, and threefold as karma whose maturation is experienced as pleasure, pain, or neither.**

An explanatory rendering can make the first relation still clearer:

> **Wholesome karma is secure, unwholesome karma insecure, and indeterminate karma neither; karma is also classified as meritorious, demeritorious, or immovable, and according to whether its maturation is felt as pleasure, pain, or neither.**

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.45 is not merely a list of predicates. Its first movement is a definition by ground and consequence:

```text
KUŚALA(K)
    because
KṢEMA(K)

KṢEMA(K)
    because
IṢṬA_VIPĀKA(K)
    and
NIRVĀṆA_PRĀPAKA(K)

NIRVĀṆA_PRĀPAKA(K)
    because
DUḤKHA_PARITRĀṆA(K)
```

The contrary is generated relationally:

```text
AKUŚALA(K)
    = AKṢEMA(K)
    = contrary-of KṢEMA
    → ANIṢṬA_VIPĀKA
```

And the third member is determined through exclusion:

```text
¬KṢEMA(K) ∧ ¬AKṢEMA(K)
    → AVYĀKṚTA(K)
```

Thus the triad is not three unrelated labels. The middle opposition generates a determinate remainder.

The larger architecture opened by the verse is:

```text
karma
    ethical-soteriological determination
        kṣema / akṣema / neither
        = kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta

    karmic mode
        puṇya / apuṇya / āneñja

    experienced maturation
        sukha / duḥkha / neither
```

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is the way a practical determination is known through **what it generates and where it leads**.

Vasubandhu does not begin here with an isolated intrinsic moral quality. He gives a relational determination:

```text
action
    → maturation
    → protection or injury
    → orientation toward or away from liberation
```

This is important for the larger Rational Path project: practical reason cannot be reduced to an abstract label attached to an act. The determination of karma includes its generated consequence and its place within the path from suffering toward liberation.

But this remains a contact point only. The primary textual result is Vasubandhu's own definition of `kuśala` as `kṣema` through desirable maturation and nirvāṇa-directed protection from suffering.

## Determinate Result

The decisive Bhāṣya result is:

```text
kṣema is not a second classification beside kuśala

kṣema is the lakṣaṇa of kuśala
```

And `kṣema` is itself generated through a concrete practical relation:

```text
wholesome action
    → desirable maturation
    → protection from suffering
    → conduciveness to nirvāṇa
```

VAK 4.45 therefore opens the new karmic classification block by grounding ethical determination in **experienced consequence and soteriological direction**.
