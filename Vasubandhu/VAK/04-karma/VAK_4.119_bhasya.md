# VAK_4.119_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.119**

> *pṛṣṭhaṃ kṣetram adhiṣṭhānaṃ prayogaś cetanāśayaḥ |*  
> *eṣāṃ mṛdvadhimātratvāt karma mṛdvadhimātratā || 4.119 ||*

> Subsequent reinforcement, field, karmic basis, undertaking, volition, and underlying resolve: because these are mild or intense, karma is mild or intense.

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya proceeds in four steps:

1. announce six causes by which karmic heaviness or lightness is known;
2. define each of the six technical factors;
3. explain that a karma may become heavy because of one factor even when the others are not maximal;
4. state the limit-cases: all six intense gives extremely heavy karma; all six mild gives extremely mild karma.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

For one who wishes to know the heaviness and lightness of karmas, six causes should be known in summary: subsequent reinforcement, field, karmic basis, undertaking, volition, and underlying resolve. According as these are mild or intense, karma is mild or intense.

`Pṛṣṭha` is the repetition or renewal after something has been done. `Kṣetra` is that in relation to which benefit or harm is performed. `Adhiṣṭhāna` is the karmapatha itself. `Prayoga` is bodily or verbal karma performed for the sake of that karmapatha. `Cetanā` is that volition by which the karmapatha is brought to completion. `Āśaya` is the underlying intention or resolve: “I shall do such and such; I shall not do such and such.”

For some acts, the karma becomes heavy merely through being taken up again afterward, because it thereby becomes fixed with certainty of maturation. For some, heaviness arises merely through the field. Within that same field, however, it may again become heavy through the particular karmapatha and not otherwise—for example, through killing one’s mother or father, but not in the same way through stealing and the like from them. Other cases should be understood analogously.

When all six factors are intense, the karma should be known as extremely intense and heavy. When they are mild, it should be known as extremely mild.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Kārikā gives a general theory of karmic weight. The Bhāṣya prevents reduction of karmic gravity to the visible deed alone.

```text
karmic gravity
    = degree of determination
      across six distinct factors
```

The six factors are not interchangeable:

```text
pṛṣṭha
    subsequent renewal of the deed

kṣetra
    the qualified field toward which benefit or harm is directed

adhiṣṭhāna
    the karmapatha itself

prayoga
    the bodily/verbal undertaking performed for that karmapatha

cetanā
    the volition that brings the karmapatha to completion

āśaya
    the deeper purposive resolve governing what one will or will not do
```

This yields a temporally and structurally extended deed:

```text
āśaya
    ↓
cetanā
    ↓
prayoga
    ↓
adhiṣṭhāna / karmapatha
    ↓
pṛṣṭha

all occurring in relation to a kṣetra
```

But Vasubandhu explicitly refuses the idea that every heavy karma must be maximal in all six factors. A single factor can sometimes suffice to make an act heavy.

## New Technical Terms

- **pṛṣṭha** — subsequent repetition or renewal of an already performed deed.  
  **status:** rare / newly clarified in this context.  
  **function:** determinant of karmic gravity.

- **kṣetra** — the field in relation to which benefit or harm is performed.  
  **status:** previously encountered.  
  **function:** relational determinant of karmic gravity.

- **adhiṣṭhāna** — here explicitly the **karmapatha itself**.  
  **status:** previously encountered, but now technically fixed.  
  **function:** completed deed-type / basis of karmic gravity.

- **prayoga** — bodily or verbal karma undertaken for the sake of the karmapatha.  
  **status:** previously encountered.  
  **function:** practical preparation/execution leading to completion.

- **cetanā** — volition by which the karmapatha is brought to completion.  
  **status:** fundamental recurring term.  
  **function:** immediate volitional completion of the deed.

- **āśaya** — deeper purposive resolve, expressed as “I will do this / I will not do that.”  
  **status:** important Bhāṣya-level technical term.  
  **function:** underlying orientation or policy of action.

- **vipākanaimya / vipākaniyama** — certainty/fixity of maturation.  
  **status:** technical.  
  **function:** explains why subsequent reinforcement can make karma heavy.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

### 1. `Pṛṣṭha` is not merely emotional reaction after the deed

The Bhāṣya defines it narrowly:

```text
pṛṣṭha = yat kṛtasya punar anukriyā
         repetition / renewed enactment of what has been done
```

Thus repeated endorsement can stabilize the karma toward determinate maturation.

### 2. `Adhiṣṭhāna` is the karmapatha

This corrects a possible broad reading of `adhiṣṭhāna` as an external object-support.

```text
adhiṣṭhāna = karmapatha
```

The determinant is therefore not simply “what thing was affected,” but **which completed karmic path the act constitutes**.

### 3. `Prayoga` is the bodily/verbal means toward completion

```text
prayoga = tadarthaṃ kāyavākkarma
```

It is the embodied or verbal undertaking directed toward producing the karmapatha.

### 4. `Cetanā` and `āśaya` must be distinguished

```text
āśaya
    deeper purposive resolve

cetanā
    volition completing the karmapatha
```

A useful structural reading is:

```text
āśaya = standing practical orientation
cetanā = determinate act-volition
```

### 5. Heaviness is multi-factorial but not mechanically additive

Vasubandhu gives three possibilities:

```text
heavy through pṛṣṭha alone
heavy through kṣetra alone
heavy through adhiṣṭhāna within the same kṣetra
```

The parental example is decisive:

```text
same field: mother/father

killing
    → exceptionally heavy

stealing etc.
    → not heavy in the same way
```

So field alone does not erase deed-type.

### 6. Maximum convergence gives maximum gravity

```text
all six adhimātra
    → extremely heavy karma

all six mṛdu
    → extremely mild karma
```

Karmic gravity is therefore best represented as a structured convergence rather than one scalar attached to an act-name.

---

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

A more precise translation is:

> **Subsequent reinforcement, field, karmapatha, undertaking, volition, and underlying resolve: according as these are mild or intense, karma is mild or intense.**

This improves the first-pass rendering by translating `adhiṣṭhāna` in the Bhāṣya-defined sense as **karmapatha**, not as a vague “basis or object.”

Likewise, `pṛṣṭha` should be understood as **renewal/repetition after performance**, not merely any later mental reaction.

---

## Light Organon Contact

This verse gives one of the Kośa’s clearest analyses of a deed as a complete practical determination rather than an isolated event.

```text
orientation
    → volition
    → embodied undertaking
    → completed karmapatha
    → subsequent reinforcement
```

all of it standing in relation to a qualified field.

The important point is not simply that “intention matters.” The deed has an internal architecture, and its force depends upon the intensity of each moment and on how those moments converge.

The parental example also prevents formalism: the same field does not make every act equivalent. **Relation and deed-type reciprocally determine karmic gravity.**
