# VAK_4.79 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**Best translation:**  
The severance of the wholesome roots occurs through false view, for those possessing desire-domain wholesome roots acquired by birth, through a view denying result and ground; completely, successively, among humans.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### 1. What cuts the wholesome roots?

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository framework**

The severance of the wholesome roots occurs through false view when that false view is **fully developed in an intense degree** (`adhimātra-paripūrṇā`).

A scriptural formulation attributes the cutting to the unwholesome roots. The Bhāṣya explains this by causal attribution: false view is brought forward by the unwholesome roots, so the action of false view may be ascribed to them, just as one may say that thieves burned a village when in strict physical terms the fire did the burning and the thieves supplied it.

Thus:

```text
unwholesome roots
    bring forth / support
false view
    directly performs
root-severance
```

### 2. Which wholesome roots are cut?

The wholesome roots of the desire domain are severed.

The Bhāṣya explains the apparently broader statement that a person has severed the wholesome roots of all three domains as referring to the fact that the continuum has been made incapable of acquiring those higher wholesome roots: their attainment has been placed far away because the continuum has been rendered an unsuitable vessel for them.

Only wholesome roots **acquired by birth** (`upapatti-pratilābhika`) are directly severed. Wholesome roots produced through deliberate practice (`prāyogika`) have already fallen away before the final severance.

So the operative distinction is:

```text
upapatti-pratilābhika
    birth-acquired wholesome roots
    → directly severed

prāyogika
    cultivated / practice-produced wholesome roots
    → already lost beforehand
```

### 3. What kind of false view cuts them?

The relevant false view denies either the **ground** or the **result** of karma:

```text
hetu-apavāda
    “there is no good conduct,
     there is no bad conduct”

phala-apavāda
    “good and bad karmas
     have no fruit or maturation”
```

The Bhāṣya records the view that these two forms stand respectively like an immediate path and a liberation path in the process of severance.

It further restricts the effective false view to one taking contaminated objects and objects of the same domain, rather than uncontaminated or different-domain objects; some explain this by the weakness of merely associated latent tendencies in the latter cases.

### 4. `sarvathā` and `kramaśaḥ`

A disagreement is preserved.

**Voice: attributed alternative position**

One group says all nine grades of wholesome roots are severed at once, like what is abandoned by the path of seeing.

```text
sarvathā
    = all nine grades at once
```

**Voice: preferred explanation in the Bhāṣya**

The stronger explanation is sequential:

```text
nine grades of false view
    cut
nine grades of wholesome roots
```

by analogy with afflictions abandoned through cultivation.

The sequence runs until the strongest false view cuts the weakest wholesome roots:

```text
stronger false view
    progressively reaches
subtler / weaker wholesome roots
```

This preserves the textual force of `kramaśaḥ` rather than reducing the process to a single instantaneous collapse.

The Bhāṣya supports this reading with Abhidharma prose concerning the final relinquishment of the residual wholesome roots and the point at which one is designated “one whose wholesome roots are severed.” Even one remaining grade would be a ground for the re-arising of the whole set, so complete severance requires no residual grade to remain.

### 5. Where can this happen?

Root-severance occurs among humans.

It does not occur in the lower destinies because neither afflicted nor unafflicted principle/discriminative knowing (`prajñā`) is sufficiently firm there.

It does not occur among gods because karmic result is directly evident to them.

The Bhāṣya also records disagreement over geographical restriction among the human continents, but rejects an overly narrow Jambūdvīpa-only claim by citing Abhidharma material that also applies the relevant minimum-faculty condition to Pūrvavideha and Godānīya.

## Course of the Argument

### Problem

VAK 4.78 defined false view as a mental karmapatha denying the reality of wholesome and unwholesome karma. VAK 4.79 asks what happens when this denial reaches its most intense form.

### Determination

The effect is not merely another unwholesome act. Fully intensified false view can sever the operative wholesome roots of the continuum.

### Distinctions introduced by the Bhāṣya

```text
false view in general
    ≠
root-severing false view
```

```text
root-severing false view
    = adhimātra-paripūrṇā
```

```text
desire-domain birth-acquired roots
    are directly severed

practice-produced roots
    have already fallen away
```

```text
complete scope
    sarvathā

successive mechanism
    kramaśaḥ
```

### Result

The phrase `sarvathā kramaśaḥ` should be read as **complete severance through an ordered graded process** according to the explanation preferred in the Bhāṣya.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expository framework** — root severance is performed by fully intensified false view; desire-domain birth-acquired wholesome roots are the direct object; humans are the locus.
- **Voice: attributed alternative** — all nine grades are cut at once, like what is abandoned by the path of seeing.
- **Voice: preferred explanation** — nine grades of false view successively cut nine grades of wholesome roots.
- **Voice: attributed alternatives** — different explanations of whether restraint is lost before root-severance and how the relevant scriptural formulations should be reconciled.

## New Technical Terms

- **adhimātra-paripūrṇā mithyādṛṣṭi** — fully intensified false view
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** condition delimiting which false view can sever wholesome roots

- **upapatti-pratilābhika** — acquired by birth
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical specification here
  - **function:** identifies the wholesome roots directly subject to severance

- **prāyogika** — produced through deliberate practice or application
  - **status:** previously encountered family of `prayoga`
  - **function:** contrasts cultivated wholesome roots with birth-acquired roots; these have fallen away beforehand

- **abhājana / bhājana** — unsuitable / suitable vessel or basis for acquisition
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local argument
  - **function:** explains how severance in the desire domain also blocks acquisition of higher-domain wholesome roots

- **navaprakāra** — nine grades or modes
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `sarvathā kramaśaḥ`
  - **function:** supplies the graded mechanism of severance

## Doctrinal Determination

The most important doctrinal clarification is that **root-severance is not the annihilation of an eternal entity**. It is a transformation of the continuum such that the birth-acquired wholesome roots are no longer possessed and the continuum is rendered incapable of the corresponding higher wholesome acquisitions.

The mechanism is epistemic-practical in a strong sense:

```text
mithyādṛṣṭi
    denies karmic ground
    denies karmic result
        ↓
becomes fully intensified
        ↓
progressively excludes
wholesome-root acquisition and possession
```

The Bhāṣya therefore ties the most radical ethical collapse to a **determinate view about the structure of action and consequence**.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and partial revision required.**

The first-pass rendering “among human beings who have obtained a birth belonging to the desire domain” is too loose for `kāmāptotpatti-lābhinām` in context.

The Bhāṣya shows that the phrase concerns specifically:

> **the desire-domain wholesome roots acquired by birth**

A better working translation is:

> **The wholesome roots acquired by birth in the desire domain are severed by fully intensified false view that denies karmic result and ground; the severance is complete and successive, and occurs among humans.**

This should guide the later Super Kārikās cleanup.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.79 distinguishes **scope** from **mode of realization**:

```text
sarvathā
    complete extension

kramaśaḥ
    successive realization
```

The result can therefore be total without being immediate.

The deeper structure is:

```text
false view
    first determines the practical field incorrectly
        ↓
its intensification becomes generative
        ↓
it removes increasingly subtle wholesome capacities
        ↓
no residual grade remains
        ↓
root-severance is complete
```

This is a stronger conception than mere “loss of belief.” A view becomes causally formative for the continuum.

## Organon Contact Point

The text itself generates a useful distinction between **error within a practical system** and **a determination that destroys the system's own capacity for wholesome generation**.

```text
ordinary unwholesome course
    violates a determinate practical relation

root-severing false view
    attacks the ground-result structure
    by which practical distinctions are generated
```

The Organon contact should remain subordinate to that textual fact: the important point is not an external analogy, but the generated relation

```text
view
    → orientation of the continuum
    → capacity / incapacity for future wholesome determination
```
