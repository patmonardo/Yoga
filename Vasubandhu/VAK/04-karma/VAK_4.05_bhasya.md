# VAK_4.05 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
svāni bhūtānyupādāya kāyavākkarma sāsravam /
anāsravaṃ yatra jātaḥ avijñaptiranupāttikā // VAkK_4.5 //
```

**Working translation:** Contaminated bodily and verbal karma depends upon the great material determinations belonging to its own level. Uncontaminated karma depends upon those of the level in which the practitioner is born. Avijñapti is non-appropriated.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.05.md`.

### Source cautions

The source reading at `[199|27-200|01]` gives `anāsravaṃ tu kāyavākkarma`, which resolves the compressed Kārikā syntax by supplying the neuter `karma` as the subject governed by `yasyāṃ bhūmau jātaḥ ... utpādayati`.

The phrase `tadbalena cotpatteḥ` is important: uncontaminated bodily/verbal karma arises through the force of the corresponding level, not from a distinct class of intrinsically uncontaminated mahābhūtas.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu asks from what sort of great material determinants bodily and verbal karma arise.

For contaminated (`sāsrava`) bodily and verbal karma, the answer is level-specific. Desire-domain bodily and verbal karma depends upon desire-domain mahābhūtas. Bodily and verbal karma belonging to the first dhyāna depends upon mahābhūtas of the first dhyāna, and this continues level by level through the fourth dhyāna.

Thus `svāni bhūtāni` means not simply “its own material elements” in an individual bodily sense, but the mahābhūtas proper to the same domain or meditative level as the karma in question.

The case of uncontaminated (`anāsrava`) bodily and verbal karma is different. Such karma is not assigned to an independent uncontaminated material domain. Rather, when a practitioner born in a particular level produces uncontaminated bodily or verbal karma, that karma depends upon the mahābhūtas belonging to that level.

Vasubandhu gives two reasons. First, uncontaminated dharmas do not fall within the three ordinary domains (`dhātu-apratitatva`). Second, there are no intrinsically uncontaminated mahābhūtas. Therefore the material basis must be supplied by the level in which the uncontaminated karma arises; the karma arises through the force of that level.

He then reminds the reader that both types of karma under discussion are to be understood through the categories already established:

```text
vijñapti
avijñapti
```

Finally, the verse fixes one further determination: avijñapti is `anupāttikā`, non-appropriated.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks which mahābhūtas support bodily and verbal karma, explains the same-level rule for contaminated karma, clarifies the special case of uncontaminated karma, and states the non-appropriated status of avijñapti.

No distinct objector or school voice is marked in this short unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **svāni bhūtāni** — the great material determinations proper to the same level
  - **status:** Kārikā expression clarified by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** fixes the material basis of contaminated bodily and verbal karma by domain/level

- **tadbhūmikāni** — belonging to that level
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** makes explicit that the relevant material basis is determined by the bhūmi of the karma or practitioner

- **dhātvapatitatva** — not falling within a domain
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term
  - **function:** explains why anāsrava dharmas cannot be assigned their own ordinary domain-specific material class

- **anāsrava-bhūta-abhāva** — absence of uncontaminated mahābhūtas
  - **status:** doctrinal clarification
  - **function:** blocks the inference that uncontaminated karma requires a distinct pure material substrate

- **tadbalena utpatti** — arising through the force of that level
  - **status:** Bhāṣya causal formulation
  - **function:** explains how uncontaminated bodily/verbal karma can depend on ordinary level-specific mahābhūtas without itself becoming domain-bound in the same way

- **anupāttika** — non-appropriated
  - **status:** Kārikā term
  - **function:** determines the relation of avijñapti to the living appropriated body

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a precise two-part rule.

### Sāsrava bodily and verbal karma

```text
kāmāvacara karma
    → kāmāvacara mahābhūtas

first-dhyāna karma
    → first-dhyāna mahābhūtas

...

fourth-dhyāna karma
    → fourth-dhyāna mahābhūtas
```

The karma and its material basis belong to the same level.

### Anāsrava bodily and verbal karma

```text
anāsrava karma
    ≠ karma supported by “anāsrava matter”

because:
    anāsrava dharmas are not domain-bound
    and there are no anāsrava mahābhūtas

therefore:
    the karma takes as basis
    the mahābhūtas of the level
    in which the practitioner produces it
```

This prevents a misleading dualism between contaminated matter and a hypothetical pure matter.

The distinction is instead between:

```text
status of the karma
    sāsrava / anāsrava

and

level of the material support
    kāma / rūpa-dhyāna bhūmi
```

These two determinations intersect without being identical.

The final statement adds:

```text
avijñapti
    is rūpa in the Vaibhāṣika system
    yet it is anupātta
```

Thus material classification does not entail appropriation as living bodily matter.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The first-pass rendering “its own great material determinations” is too ambiguous if it suggests merely the individual organism’s own matter. The Bhāṣya shows that `svāni` is governed by bhūmi correspondence.

A sharper translation is:

> Contaminated bodily and verbal karma depends upon the great material determinations proper to its own level. Uncontaminated karma depends upon those of the level in which the practitioner who produces it is born. Avijñapti is non-appropriated.

The Bhāṣya also removes the earlier grammatical uncertainty around `yatra jātaḥ`: the referent is the person who, being born in a given level, produces uncontaminated bodily/verbal karma.

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes two independent axes:

```text
axiological / soteriological status
    sāsrava
    anāsrava

material localization
    bhūmi-specific mahābhūtas
```

For contaminated karma these coincide straightforwardly:

```text
karma of level X
    → matter of level X
```

For uncontaminated karma they do not:

```text
anāsrava determination
    is not itself domain-bound

but its manifestation as bodily/verbal karma
    requires a material basis

therefore:
    it borrows the basis supplied by
    the practitioner’s present bhūmi
```

The relation is therefore one of mediation rather than identity.

## Organon Contact Point

This unit is useful because it blocks a crude equation:

```text
pure action
    ≠ pure matter
```

An uncontaminated determination can operate through a conditioned material basis without being reducible to the status of that basis.

So the structure is:

```text
anāsrava determination
    ↓
level-specific material support
    ↓
vijñapti / avijñapti
```

The higher determination does not require a second ontological substance corresponding to it at the material level.

This remains textually controlled and should not yet be generalized into a complete theory of Path embodiment.
