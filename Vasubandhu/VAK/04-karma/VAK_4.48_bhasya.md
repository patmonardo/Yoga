# VAK_4.48_bhasya — Neutral Maturation Below the Fourth Dhyāna

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.48**

```iast
adho 'pi madhyam asty eke dhyānāntaravipākataḥ |
apūrvācaramaḥ pākas trayāṇāṃ ceṣyate yataḥ ||
```

> Some hold that the middle kind, karma to be experienced as neither pain nor pleasure, exists even below the fourth dhyāna, because it is the maturation of the intermediate dhyāna; and because a maturation of the three is accepted in which none is earlier or later than the others.

## Source Cautions

The first-pass Kārikā file correctly marked `apūrvācaramaḥ pākaḥ` as unresolved. The Bhāṣya now supplies its technical force through the cited sūtra: the issue is not position in the meditative sequence, but a case in which the maturations of all three karmas occur together, with no one maturation standing as temporally first or last.

The e-text at `[229|01-229|02]` reads only `durbalaṃ tu tat` after asking whether the neutral-result karma in question is wholesome or unwholesome. The transmitted snippet is too compressed or possibly textually defective to force a secure reconstruction of the omitted doctrinal predicate. We therefore preserve the uncertainty rather than silently normalize it.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.47 gave the principal rule:

```text
pleasant maturation
    kāmadhātu + first three dhyānas

neutral maturation
    above the third dhyāna

painful maturation
    unwholesome karma in kāmadhātu
```

VAK 4.48 introduces a qualification attributed to `eke`, "some authorities." They maintain that `aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya` karma also exists below the fourth dhyāna.

### First argument: intermediate dhyāna

The intermediate dhyāna contains neither pleasant nor painful feeling. Therefore, if karma belonging to that level matures there, its maturation cannot be classified as pleasant or painful.

```text
dhyānāntara
    no sukha
    no duḥkha
        ↓
its karmic maturation
    requires a third experiential classification
        ↓
aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya
```

The Bhāṣya records two attempted alternatives:

```text
View 1
    intermediate-dhyāna karma
        → sukha-indriya as maturation in dhyāna

View 2
    its maturation is not feeling at all
```

The second is rejected as contrary to the śāstra, which explicitly allows a mental feeling to mature as the result of wholesome karma without vitarka.

### Second argument: simultaneous maturation of the three karmas

The second half of the verse invokes a sūtra describing a maturation of all three classes in which none is prior or posterior to the others.

The sūtra distributes the simultaneous fruits as follows:

```text
sukhavedanīya karma
    → rūpa

duḥkhavedanīya karma
    → citta-caitta dharmas

aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya karma
    → citta-viprayukta dharmas
```

Vasubandhu then states the decisive locality condition:

```text
only in kāmadhātu
    can the maturations of all three karmas
    occur together (yugapat)
```

Therefore neutral-result karma must also exist in the desire domain.

This gives a much stronger reading of:

```text
apūrvācaramaḥ pākaḥ
```

not:

```text
neither early nor late in the dhyāna series
```

but:

```text
no one of the three maturations
is temporally first or last
because they mature together
```

The Bhāṣya then confronts an apparent contradiction with the broad rule that wholesome karma through the third dhyāna is pleasantly experienced and that wholesome karma has desirable maturation. Its answer is methodological: `bāhulika eṣa nirdeśaḥ` — the earlier statement is to be understood as a **predominant / general-case description**, not an exceptionless universal.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: some authorities (`eke`, `anye`)** — neutral-result karma exists below the fourth dhyāna.

**Voice: alternative doctrinal positions** — one assigns pleasant maturation to intermediate-dhyāna karma; another denies feeling as its maturation.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / śāstra-controlled resolution** — the denial of feeling as maturation is rejected as `ucchāstra`, contrary to the treatise; the sūtra evidence establishes neutral-result karma below, especially in kāmadhātu.

**Voice: uncertain** — the exact force of `durbalaṃ tu tat` cannot be securely reconstructed from the present e-text alone.

## New Technical Terms

- **apūrvācarama** — neither first nor last; here, a maturation relation in which the three results occur together rather than successively
  - **status:** clarified decisively only by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** sūtra-based argument for neutral-result karma below the fourth dhyāna

- **yugapat** — simultaneously
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** supplies the temporal structure implied by `apūrvācarama`

- **vipāka-saṃyoga** — conjunction of maturations
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific phrase in this passage
  - **function:** explains coexistence of the three karmic fruit-types in kāmadhātu

- **ucchāstra** — contrary to the authoritative treatise / outside the śāstra
  - **status:** argumentative vocabulary
  - **function:** rejects the claim that intermediate-dhyāna karma has no feeling as maturation

- **bāhulika** — predominant, applying in the majority/general case
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification here
  - **function:** converts an apparently universal earlier rule into a general rule admitting exceptions

## Doctrinal Determination

The central doctrinal result is not merely that the extension of neutral maturation is disputed. The Bhāṣya gives two positive grounds for extending it downward:

```text
1. structural ground
   intermediate dhyāna lacks sukha and duḥkha

2. scriptural ground
   all three karmas can mature simultaneously in kāmadhātu
```

Therefore:

```text
aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya karma
    is not restricted absolutely
    to the fourth dhyāna and above
```

The earlier distribution of 4.47 must be understood as predominant rather than exceptionless.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation of the second half should be corrected. `apūrvācarama` does not refer to the meditative sequence.

Preferred translation:

> **Some hold that the middle kind exists even below, because it is the maturation of the intermediate dhyāna; and because a maturation of the three is admitted in which none is earlier or later than the others.**

Expanded Bhāṣya-informed sense:

> **Some maintain that karma to be experienced as neither pain nor pleasure also occurs below the fourth dhyāna: the intermediate dhyāna requires such a maturation, and scripture moreover recognizes a case in which pleasant-, painful-, and neutral-result karmas mature simultaneously—something possible only in the desire domain.**

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya distinguish a **general distribution rule** from its **exception structure**.

```text
GENERAL RULE
    level L
        → characteristic maturation M

BOUNDARY CASE
    dhyānāntara
        lacks the feelings predicted by neighboring classes
        → forces reclassification

SCRIPTURAL COUNTERINSTANCE
    kāmadhātu
        supports simultaneous maturation of all three classes
        → neutral-result karma exists below
```

The important logical advance is:

```text
classification
    ≠ rigid one-to-one partition by level
```

A karmic class is determined by the structure of its maturation, and the actual field can contain exceptional overlaps that force refinement of the first distribution rule.

The Bhāṣya explicitly marks the status of the rule:

```text
bāhulika
    = true predominantly
    ≠ exceptionless universal
```

This is a sophisticated piece of model revision: the original law is retained but its quantificational force is weakened in light of a counterexample.

## Organon Contact Point

The strongest contact point is the relation between **law and exception**.

```text
first determination
    universal-looking distribution

reflection
    boundary case + counterexample

revised determination
    predominant law with explicit exceptions
```

The Concept is not destroyed by the counterexample. Its extension is made more adequate to the concrete field.

There is also an important temporal structure:

```text
three distinct causal histories
    ↓
three distinct result-types
    ↓
simultaneous manifestation
```

Difference of causal determination therefore does not require temporal succession of effects.
