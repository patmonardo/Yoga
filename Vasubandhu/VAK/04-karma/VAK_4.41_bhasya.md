# VAK_4.41_bhasya — Loss of Non-Restraint and Intermediate Avijñapti

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.41**

```iast
asaṃvaraḥ saṃvarāptimṛtyudvivyañjanodayaiḥ |
vegādānakriyārthāyurmūlacchedais tu madhyamā ||
```

**Best current translation:**

> Non-restraint is relinquished through acquisition of restraint, death, or the arising of both sexual characteristics. Intermediate avijñapti is relinquished through the severance of its force, undertaking, activity, object-purpose, lifespan, or root.

## Source Cautions

The source e-text is explicitly non-critical. The compound in the second half of the Kārikā is clarified by the prose as six distinct causes of relinquishment:

```text
vega-cheda
ādāna-tyāga
kriyā-viccheda
artha-viccheda
āyuś-cheda
kuśalamūla-cheda
```

`artha` here should not be generalized as abstract “Meaning.” The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies concrete enduring objects or projects—such as a caitya, monastery, dwelling, bed-seat, mechanism, or net—as the relevant `vastu` whose destruction terminates the associated avijñapti.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Three causes terminate asaṃvara

Vasubandhu states that non-restraint is cut off through three causes:

```text
saṃvaraprāpti
maraṇa
dvivyañjanotpāda
```

The first is the most philosophically important. If one undertakes prātimokṣa restraint or acquires dhyāna-born restraint through attainment of samādhi, the non-restraint is cut off because the opposing determination is stronger:

```text
pratidvandva-balīyastva
    the contrary is stronger
```

Thus complete non-restraint is not removed merely by ceasing the external occupation.

### 2. Mere abandonment of instruments does not suffice

Vasubandhu explicitly says that even if one abandons weapons and nets with an intention not to act, non-restraint is not thereby cut off in the absence of restraint.

The analogy is medical:

```text
avoiding the cause of illness
    ≠
curing an already developed illness
```

Just as a developed disease does not disappear merely because its cause is avoided, established asaṃvara does not disappear merely through stopping its external conditions.

This is a strong distinction between:

```text
cessation of present harmful activity
    and
termination of the standing non-restraint
```

### 3. Temporary upavāsa creates a problem

Vasubandhu then asks about an `āsaṃvarika`, one possessing non-restraint, who undertakes the one-day upavāsa restraint.

When that temporary restraint ends, what happens?

Two positions are recorded.

**Position 1:** the person returns to asaṃvara because the intention to abandon it was never ultimate or permanent (`tyāgāśayasya anātyantikatvāt`).

**Position 2:** asaṃvara does not automatically return. The analogy is a heated iron ball that becomes dark again when no longer acted upon; reacquisition of asaṃvara nevertheless depends on a new `vijñapti`.

The second position therefore insists:

```text
prior asaṃvara history
    ≠ automatic reacquisition

new acquisition
    depends on vijñapti
```

The prose does not explicitly name the schools in this short exchange, so the voices should remain cautious.

### 4. The intermediate avijñapti

The text then turns to avijñapti that is free from both complete restraint and complete non-restraint:

```text
saṃvarāsaṃvaravinirmukta avijñapti
```

This is the `madhyamā`, intermediate avijñapti.

Its persistence is conditional and project-specific rather than comprehensive.

### 5. Severance of force — `vegaccheda`

An avijñapti may have been projected by a force of faith/purity (`prasāda`) or affliction (`kleśa`). When that force is cut, the avijñapti is cut as well.

Vasubandhu's analogy is the potter's wheel:

```text
initial impulse
    → wheel continues moving

impulse exhausted
    → motion ceases
```

The avijñapti persists after the initiating moment because of projected force, but not indefinitely without that sustaining momentum.

### 6. Relinquishing the undertaking — `ādānatyāga`

If one explicitly abandons the undertaking—“enough of this undertaking”—the associated avijñapti ceases.

This is the intermediate-form analogue of laying down a formal discipline.

### 7. Cessation of activity — `kriyāviccheda`

If someone who has undertaken an action no longer performs what was undertaken, the associated avijñapti may cease.

Here persistence depends on the continuation of the project as enacted practice.

### 8. Destruction of the object-purpose — `arthaviccheda`

The Bhāṣya specifies concrete objects:

```text
caitya
ārāma
vihāra
śayanāsana
yantra
jāla
etc.
```

If the enduring object or institutional/project basis is destroyed, the associated avijñapti ceases.

This is especially important for meritorious constructions and harmful devices alike: avijñapti may persist because an enduring object continues to function as the practical result of the initiating act.

### 9. Death and severance of wholesome roots

The intermediate avijñapti also ceases when the bearer's lifespan ends.

Finally, wholesome intermediate avijñapti ceases when the wholesome roots are being severed.

The prose sums these as six causes.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — framing the taxonomy of loss, explaining why established asaṃvara requires a genuine contrary restraint for termination, and unpacking the six causes of intermediate avijñapti loss.

**Voice: uncertain** — `asaṃvaram ity eke`, the view that an āsaṃvarika returns to asaṃvara after the temporary upavāsa ends.

**Voice: uncertain** — `ity apare`, the alternative that asaṃvara does not automatically return and must be reacquired through vijñapti.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratidvandvabalīyastva** — superior strength of an opposing determination
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** causal explanation of why acquisition of restraint cuts off non-restraint

- **āsaṃvarika** — one who possesses or is established in asaṃvara
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** designation of the bearer in the upavāsa thought-experiment

- **tyāgāśaya** — intention or disposition to relinquish
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** explains one view of why non-restraint returns after temporary restraint

- **saṃvarāsaṃvaravinirmukta** — free from both restraint and non-restraint
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** definition of the intermediate avijñapti class

- **prasādakleśavega** — projected force arising from faith/purity or affliction
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** causal ground of persistence for intermediate avijñapti

- **kumbhakāracakragati** — motion of a potter's wheel
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** analogy for persistence through residual/projected force

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.41 distinguishes two different kinds of persistence and therefore two different kinds of cessation.

### Asaṃvara

Asaṃvara is a comprehensive standing harmful determination. Its cessation requires either:

```text
1. acquisition of a stronger contrary restraint
2. cessation of the bearer at death
3. corruption/transformation of the qualifying bodily support
```

Mere occupational inactivity is insufficient.

### Intermediate avijñapti

Intermediate avijñapti is narrower and conditional. It persists through a determinate causal complex and ceases when that complex is severed:

```text
force
undertaking
activity
enduring object/project
life
wholesome root
```

The category is therefore not merely “weak restraint.” It is a distinct type of persistent karmic form outside the comprehensive saṃvara/asaṃvara opposition.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation should preserve the sixfold structure of the second half rather than flattening the compound into generic “conditions.”

Recommended translation:

> Non-restraint is relinquished through acquisition of restraint, death, or the arising of both sexual characteristics. Intermediate avijñapti is relinquished through the severance of its force, undertaking, activity, object-purpose, lifespan, or root.

For `artha`, “object-purpose” is preferable here to the project's usual canonical “Meaning,” because the Bhāṣya explicitly glosses the term through concrete enduring `vastu` such as monasteries, seats, devices, and nets.

## Logical Determination

The passage gives two distinct logics of cessation.

For asaṃvara:

```text
established standing determination
    + stronger contrary determination
    → prior determination cut off
```

For intermediate avijñapti:

```text
persistent form
    depends on sustaining relation R

R severed
    → persistent form ceases
```

This yields an important distinction:

```text
stopping an action
    ≠
removing the persistent determination generated by it
```

and another:

```text
comprehensive practical form
    terminates by contrary acquisition

project-specific persistent form
    terminates by dependency severance
```

## Organon Contact Point

The potter's-wheel analogy is particularly useful. A determination may continue after its initiating event because the event has projected a continuing force. Persistence therefore need not mean an independently self-subsisting substance; it can mean a generated continuation whose duration is internally conditioned by its ground.

```text
genesis
    → projected continuation
    → exhaustion or severance
    → cessation
```

This is a clean local model of how Karma becomes persistent form without requiring a permanent agent behind the sequence.
