# VAK_4.36_bhasya — Scope of Restraint and Non-Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
saṃvaraḥ sarvasattvebhyo vibhāṣā tv aṅgakāraṇaiḥ |
asaṃvaras tu sarvebhyaḥ sarvāṅgebhyo na kāraṇaiḥ || 4.36 ||
```

Best current translation:

> Restraint is acquired with respect to all beings, while there is variation with respect to its limbs and causes. Non-restraint, however, is acquired with respect to all beings and all limbs, though not with respect to all causes.

See: `VAK_4.36.md`.

## Source Cautions

The source contains several obvious transmission or typing irregularities, especially around compounds and sandhi. These are preserved rather than silently normalized.

The verse's `kāraṇa` receives two different analyses in the Bhāṣya:

1. `alobha`, `adveṣa`, `amoha`;
2. weak, medium, and strong mind (`mṛdu`, `madhya`, `adhimātra`).

The detailed fourfold classification that follows adopts the second interpretation.

## Natural Argument

The question is whether restraint and non-restraint are acquired universally with respect to three dimensions at once:

```text
sattva
    beings

aṅga
    karmic limbs

kāraṇa
    causal or motivational condition
```

Vasubandhu's answer is asymmetrical.

For restraint:

```text
beings
    always all

limbs
    variable

causes
    variable
```

The limb-variation is straightforward. Bhikṣu restraint includes all relevant karmapatha-limbs; other restraints may include only four.

The causal variation is more subtle. If `kāraṇa` means the wholesome roots `alobha-adveṣa-amoha`, restraint may be said to arise from all three. If `kāraṇa` means the degree of the undertaking mind, then any single undertaking is weak, medium, or strong, not all three simultaneously.

The Bhāṣya then explicitly generates four combinations:

```text
1. all beings
   not all limbs
   not all causal strengths

2. all beings
   all limbs
   not all causal strengths

3. all beings
   all limbs
   all causal strengths

4. all beings
   all causal strengths
   not all limbs
```

What is impossible is a restraint that does not extend to all beings.

Why? Because prātimokṣa restraint requires a wholesome intention extending to all beings. A person who limits abstention by saying, for example, “I abstain only from this being,” “only in this place,” “only for this month,” or “except in war,” acquires only good conduct (`sucarita`), not prātimokṣa restraint.

Vasubandhu identifies five possible limitations:

```text
sattva-niyama
    limitation by being

aṅga-niyama
    limitation by limb

deśa-niyama
    limitation by place

kāla-niyama
    limitation by time

samaya-niyama
    limitation by occasion
```

The passage then asks how restraint can be acquired with respect to beings whom one could never actually harm. The answer is that the undertaking is generated by the intention not to injure the life of any being. Restriction to only presently “possible” victims would make the restraint increase and decrease merely as beings moved between possible and impossible relation to the agent.

The second half turns to non-restraint.

The Vaibhāṣika position is stronger:

```text
asaṃvara
    all beings
    all karmapathas
    one causal strength at a time
```

There is, for them, no `asaṃvarika` possessing only a mutilated or partial non-restraint.

But the Sautrāntikas reject this final restriction. In accordance with the actual undertaking (`yathābhyupagamam`), they allow both partial restraint and partial non-restraint outside the fixed eightfold restraint systems.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames the multidimensional question, explains the alternatives, develops objections about possible and impossible beings, and preserves the school dispute.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — prātimokṣa requires universality toward all beings; non-restraint likewise extends to all beings and all karmapathas; no partial `asaṃvarika` exists.

**Voice: Sautrāntika position** — partial restraint and partial non-restraint can exist according to the actual content of the undertaking.

**Voice: Objector** — presses the difficulties concerning impossible objects, extinct beings, and partial training rules.

## New Technical Terms

- **sattvaniyama** — limitation of the undertaking to specified beings.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-level specification.  
  **Function:** marks a restriction incompatible with full prātimokṣa restraint.

- **aṅganiyama** — limitation to specified karmic limbs.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-level specification.  
  **Function:** determines the content-range of an undertaking.

- **deśaniyama** — limitation by place.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-level specification.  
  **Function:** example of a restriction yielding mere good conduct rather than universal restraint.

- **kālaniyama** — limitation by duration.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-level specification.  
  **Function:** distinguishes bounded behavioral abstention from the universal form intended here.

- **samayaniyama** — limitation by special occasion or circumstance.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-level specification.  
  **Function:** e.g. “except in war”; another restriction incompatible with full prātimokṣa universality.

- **śakya / aśakya** — possible / impossible as an actual object of one's action.  
  **Status:** argumentative vocabulary.  
  **Function:** tests whether the extension of restraint depends on empirical possibility.

- **prādeśika asaṃvara** — partial or restricted non-restraint.  
  **Status:** school-disputed category.  
  **Function:** denied by Vaibhāṣikas and allowed by Sautrāntikas.

## Doctrinal Determination

1. Prātimokṣa restraint must extend to all beings.
2. Its limb-range can vary according to the type of restraint.
3. Its causal strength can vary as weak, medium, or strong.
4. Restriction by being, place, time, or occasion yields only good conduct, not full prātimokṣa restraint.
5. Universality does not depend on whether a being is presently a possible object of action.
6. Vaibhāṣikas require asaṃvara to extend to all beings and all karmapathas.
7. Asaṃvara does not simultaneously arise from all three strengths of mind.
8. Sautrāntikas allow restricted restraint and restricted non-restraint according to the actual undertaking.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The existing translation is broadly correct, but `vibhāṣā` should be read more technically:

> Restraint is acquired with respect to all beings; with respect to its limbs and causes there are differentiated alternatives. Non-restraint, however, is acquired with respect to all beings and all limbs, though not with respect to all causes.

The Bhāṣya shows that `kāraṇa` is not simply “kleśic motive.” In the operative classification it refers especially to the strength of the undertaking mind: weak, medium, or strong.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.36 separates three independent dimensions of a practical determination:

```text
extension
    toward whom?

content
    which karmic limbs?

strength / ground
    under what causal determination?
```

The key asymmetry is:

```text
saṃvara
    universal extension
    variable content
    variable strength

asaṃvara (Vaibhāṣika)
    universal extension
    complete harmful content
    variable strength
```

The strongest result is the refusal to identify universality with empirical reachability.

```text
universal practical determination
    ≠ inventory of currently possible encounters
```

The vow is acquired from the universal intention not to injure beings, not by summing presently available objects.

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained comparison here is with quantified practical determination.

The universal is not generated by enumerating all possible objects one by one. Rather, the undertaking establishes a rule whose extension is all beings:

```text
kalyāṇāśaya
    universal intention
        ↓
saṃvara
    universal practical relation
```

This is useful for the Rational Kośa because it shows that a karmic form has separable dimensions of extension, content, and causal strength. But the primary result remains Vasubandhu's own: prātimokṣa universality is grounded in the scope of the undertaking intention, not in empirical opportunity.
