# VAK 4.60 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAkK 4.60**

> *aśubhaṃ rūpakāmāptaṃ śubhaṃ caiva yathākramam |*  
> *kṛṣṇaśuklobhayaṃ karma tatkṣayāya nirāsravam || 4.60 ||*

> Unwholesome karma, wholesome karma belonging respectively to the form-domain and the desire-domain, are, in order, black, white, and both; karma without influxes is for the exhaustion of those.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.60.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya identifies the four classes introduced in 4.59:

```text
aśubha karma
    → kṛṣṇa

rūpāpta śubha karma
    → śukla

kāmāpta śubha karma
    → kṛṣṇaśukla

anāsrava karma
    → akṛṣṇa-aśukla, avipāka,
      directed toward exhaustion of the preceding three
```

Vasubandhu then raises two important problems:

1. Why is wholesome ārūpya-domain karma not included in the white class here?
2. How can desire-domain wholesome karma be called both black and white if black and white are mutually opposed?

The second question produces the decisive interpretive rule:

> *saṃtānata etad vyavasthāpitaṃ na svabhāvataḥ* — this is established with respect to the continuum, not with respect to intrinsic nature.

## Source Cautions

The source e-text has minor typographical instability, but the argument is clear.

The Kārikā uses **nirāsrava**; the prose uses the standard **anāsrava**. They refer here to karma without influxes.

The Bhāṣya's explanation of the omission of ārūpya karma is tied to the particular sūtra formulation being interpreted: the cited classification is stated where both intermediate existence and rebirth existence, and bodily, verbal, and mental karma, are available. Vasubandhu immediately notes that ārūpya karma is nevertheless mentioned in another sūtra. The omission therefore does not imply that ārūpya wholesome karma is intrinsically non-white.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Black karma

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Unwholesome karma is entirely black because it is defiled (`kliṣṭa`), and its maturation is black because the maturation is undesirable (`amanojña`).

```text
akuśala / aśubha
    ↓
kliṣṭa
    ↓
kṛṣṇa

its vipāka
    ↓
amanojña
    ↓
kṛṣṇavipāka
```

The color of the karma and the color of its maturation are therefore grounded separately but correspondingly.

### 2. White karma

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Wholesome karma belonging to the form-domain is entirely white because it is not interpenetrated with unwholesome karma there (`akuśalena avyatibheda`), and its maturation is white because it is desirable (`manojña`).

```text
rūpāpta kuśala
    → śukla

manojña vipāka
    → śuklavipāka
```

### 3. Why not ārūpya karma here?

**Voice: Objector**

Why is wholesome karma belonging to the formless domain not mentioned?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The cited sūtra formulation concerns the field in which two kinds of maturation-existence are available—intermediate existence and rebirth existence—and in which the three kinds of bodily, verbal, and mental karma occur. The formless domain does not satisfy that whole formulation. Vasubandhu adds that ārūpya karma is included in another sūtra.

The classification therefore follows the scope of the cited teaching, not an ontological denial of wholesome formless karma.

### 4. Black-and-white karma

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Wholesome karma belonging to the desire-domain is called black-and-white because it occurs in a continuum interspersed with unwholesome karma, and its maturation likewise occurs in a mixed resultant continuum.

But Vasubandhu immediately blocks an incorrect interpretation:

```text
saṃtānataḥ
    with respect to the continuum

na svabhāvataḥ
    not with respect to intrinsic nature
```

One single karma cannot be both black and white, and one single maturation cannot be both black and white, because black and white are mutually opposed (`anyonyavirodha`).

Thus:

```text
NOT:
    one karma
        = black + white intrinsically

BUT:
    one desire-domain continuum
        contains wholesome and unwholesome karmas
        and their correspondingly mixed maturations
```

### 5. Why is unwholesome karma not also called mixed?

**Voice: Objector**

If mixture in a continuum is sufficient, should not unwholesome karma also be called black-and-white, since wholesome karma can coexist in the same desire-domain continuum?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

No. Unwholesome karma need not be mixed with wholesome karma, because in the desire-domain it is strong (`balavat`). Wholesome karma, being weaker (`durbala`), is characteristically interspersed with unwholesome karma.

The designation is therefore asymmetrical, not merely the result of bare coexistence.

### 6. Neither black nor white

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Anāsrava karma conduces to the exhaustion and abandonment of the preceding three karmas.

It is:

```text
akṛṣṇa
    because it is not defiled

aśukla
    because it lacks white vipāka

avipāka
    because it does not belong to a dhātu
    and opposes continued pravṛtti
```

Vasubandhu adds an important semantic caution. The word **aśukla** here is intentional or context-specific (`ābhiprāyika`). In other scriptural and Abhidharma contexts, wholesome and unobstructed-neutral dharmas can indeed be called white. Here "not white" means specifically that anāsrava karma does not possess the white karmic maturation that defines the second member of this fourfold classification.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — definitions of black, white, mixed, and anāsrava karma; continuum-based interpretation; explanation of ārūpya omission.
- **Voice: Objector** — asks why ārūpya karma is omitted and why unwholesome karma is not likewise mixed.
- No independent Vaibhāṣika attribution is explicit in this immediate unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃtānataḥ** — with respect to the continuum
  - **status:** technically operative in the Bhāṣya explanation, not stated in the Kārikā
  - **function:** logical distinction; prevents intrinsic contradiction in the mixed category

- **na svabhāvataḥ** — not by intrinsic nature
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification here
  - **function:** logical distinction between individual-dharma constitution and continuum-level classification

- **avyatibheda** — non-intermixture / non-interpenetration
  - **status:** Bhāṣya prose
  - **function:** ground for the pure-white classification of form-domain wholesome karma

- **anyonyavirodha** — mutual opposition
  - **status:** Bhāṣya prose
  - **function:** reason why one karma cannot intrinsically be both black and white

- **ābhiprāyika** — intentional, context-governed in usage
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only semantic qualification
  - **function:** explains why anāsrava karma is called `aśukla` here although wholesome dharmas can elsewhere be called white

## Doctrinal Determination

The four classes are not four homogeneous moral colors.

```text
1. black
    unwholesome conditioned production

2. white
    wholesome higher-domain conditioned production

3. black-and-white
    desire-domain wholesome karma
    classified at continuum level

4. neither black nor white
    anāsrava activity
    that does not generate samsaric vipāka
    but conduces to exhaustion of karma
```

The third class requires two levels of predication:

```text
individual karma
    has one ethical nature

continuum
    may contain opposed karmic kinds
```

The fourth class changes the function of karma itself. The first three belong to karmic production; the fourth belongs to karmic exhaustion.

## Logical Determination

The most important result is the explicit distinction:

```text
SVABHĀVA-LEVEL
    black and white are mutually exclusive

SAṂTĀNA-LEVEL
    black and white karmic processes may be interspersed
```

Therefore:

```text
MIXED(continuum)
    does not imply
MIXED(intrinsic nature of each member)
```

This is a precise protection against a category error between member-properties and system-properties.

The fourth karma introduces a second logical contrast:

```text
productive karma
    → generates vipāka

anāsrava karma
    → opposes continued production
    → conduces to karmakṣaya
```

Thus "karma" is not univocally reproductive. Activity can also function as the internal process by which the karmic system is brought to exhaustion.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation remains broadly correct, but two points should now be read more strictly:

1. **`ubhaya` / black-and-white is continuum-relative, not an intrinsic hybrid nature of one karma.**
2. **`nirāsravam` is not merely a fourth neutral color.** It is without ordinary vipāka and functions toward exhaustion of the preceding karmic system.

A Bhāṣya-informed rendering is:

> Unwholesome karma is black; wholesome form-domain karma is white; wholesome desire-domain karma is black-and-white at the level of the continuum. Karma without influxes is neither black nor white in this classificatory sense and conduces to the exhaustion of those three.

## Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya gives an unusually clean distinction between **determination of the member** and **determination of the whole**:

```text
member
    intrinsically black OR white

continuum
    can contain black AND white determinations
```

Contradictory predicates need not be forced into one substrate merely because they characterize one articulated totality.

Even more important, the fourth class changes the orientation of activity:

```text
karma as production
    → further conditioned result

anāsrava karma
    → exhaustion of the productive karmic circuit
```

The Path therefore appears here not as inactivity but as a determinate activity whose result is the termination of karmic reproduction.
