# VAK_4.10 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
samutthānaṃ dvidhā hetu-tat-kṣaṇotthāna-saṃjñitam |
pravartakaṃ tayor ādyaṃ dvitīyam anuvartakam ||
```

**Working translation:** The originating condition of action is twofold, designated as causal origination and origination in that very moment. Of the two, the first is the initiator; the second is the continuator.

### Source cautions

The source is the project Bhāṣya e-text based principally on Pradhan 1975, with corrections from later editorial work. It is not a critical edition. The compounds around `hetu-tat-kṣaṇa-utthāna` are lightly separated here for readability; no silent doctrinal normalization is intended.

## Natural Argument

The verse arises from a difficulty generated by the preceding fourfold account of ethical determination. If bodily and verbal karma are wholesome or unwholesome because of the `samutthāna` from which they arise, why does the same ethical quality not belong equally to the great material determinations that support them?

Vasubandhu first answers that the agent's intention (`kartur abhiprāya`) is directed toward the action, not toward the great material determinations. But this immediately produces a further difficulty: in samādhi-born avijñapti there is no explicit intention directed toward the avijñapti itself, and a non-concentrated mind cannot originate it because it belongs to a different level. How, then, is its wholesomeness to be explained?

The text then recalls another apparent problem. A mind abandoned by seeing was said not to be an immediate originator of manifestation. Yet scripture states a sequence beginning from wrong view and continuing through wrong conceptual positing into wrong speech and wrong bodily action. Vasubandhu answers that there is no contradiction because `samutthāna` is not univocal.

It is twofold:

```text
hetu-samutthāna
    causal origination

 tatkṣaṇa-samutthāna
    origination in the very moment of performance
```

The first is `pravartaka`: it sets or projects the action into operation because it has an impelling function (`ākṣepakatva`). The second is `anuvartaka`: it accompanies and sustains the action while the action is actually occurring (`kriyākālānuvartana`).

Vasubandhu then asks what causal efficacy the contemporaneous condition really adds. The answer is that without such accompaniment, even an action already initiated would not be fully operative; it would resemble the activity of a corpse. The objection from action or restraint occurring without an active mind does not eliminate the distinction. Rather, where a mind is present, the action is more explicit or forceful (`sphuṭatara`).

The natural unit therefore does not merely define two names. It distinguishes two causal functions required for the analysis of action:

```text
prior projection
    ≠
contemporaneous continuation
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Objector** — asks why ethical character derived from `samutthāna` does not also characterize the material basis; raises the samādhi-born avijñapti difficulty; cites the scriptural sequence from wrong view to wrong action; asks what efficacy the contemporaneous condition has.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — distinguishes intention toward karma from intention toward material support; states that the scriptural passage is compatible with the Abhidharma account; introduces the twofold `samutthāna`; explains `pravartaka` through `ākṣepakatva` and `anuvartaka` through contemporaneous continuation.

**Voice: uncertain / dialectical interlocutor** — the objection concerning restraint arising in an unconscious state and the brief response emphasizing the greater explicitness of action when accompanied by mind are presented in rapid dialogue; the argumentative function is clear even if the exact persona is not independently marked.

## New Technical Terms

- **hetu-samutthāna** — causal origination; **status:** newly explicit in the Kārikā/Bhāṣya sequence; **function:** the causal condition that projects or initiates bodily/verbal action.
- **tatkṣaṇa-samutthāna** — contemporaneous origination; **status:** newly explicit; **function:** the condition present in the very moment of performance that accompanies and sustains action.
- **pravartaka** — initiator / setting-into-operation; **status:** newly explicit; **function:** name for the first causal role.
- **anuvartaka** — continuator / accompanying sustainer; **status:** newly explicit; **function:** name for the second causal role.
- **ākṣepakatva** — projecting or impelling capacity; **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage; **function:** explains why hetu-samutthāna is called `pravartaka`.
- **kriyākālānuvartana** — continuation during the time of performance; **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory phrase; **function:** explains why tatkṣaṇa-samutthāna is called `anuvartaka`.
- **abhiprāya** — intention / purposive orientation; **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term; **function:** distinguishes ethical intention directed toward action from the merely material basis of action.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse prevents `samutthāna` from being understood as one undifferentiated cause. A bodily or verbal act can be causally related to mind in at least two distinct ways:

```text
hetu-samutthāna
    prior causal determination
    projects the act
    = pravartaka

 tatkṣaṇa-samutthāna
    contemporaneous determination
    accompanies the act during execution
    = anuvartaka
```

This distinction also solves the apparent conflict between Abhidharma classification and scriptural causal sequences. A cognition can function as a causal origin without being the cognition present at the moment of bodily or verbal manifestation.

The Bhāṣya therefore differentiates:

```text
what makes an action begin
    from
what accompanies it while it is being done
```

The two functions may coincide in some cases but are not conceptually identical.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation remains substantially correct, but `hetu-samutthāna` should be heard more strongly than merely "causal origination." The Bhāṣya gloss `ākṣepakatvāt` gives it the sense of a cause that **projects or impels** the action.

Likewise, `anuvartaka` is not merely temporal persistence in the abstract. It is specifically the condition that **continues along with the action during its performance**.

A refined philosophical translation is therefore:

> The originating condition of action is twofold: causal origination and origination in the very moment of performance. Of these, the first is the initiator because it projects the action; the second is the continuator because it accompanies the action throughout its execution.

## Logical Determination

The distinction has a clear functional structure:

```text
projection
    hetu-samutthāna
    pravartaka
    ākṣepaka function

execution
    tatkṣaṇa-samutthāna
    anuvartaka
    contemporaneous continuation
```

The causal account of action is therefore not reducible to a single antecedent event. It contains at least two moments:

```text
condition that determines transition into action
    ↓
condition that accompanies realized performance
```

This is a distinction between **genetic initiation** and **operative continuation**, not between two independent substances.

## Organon Contact Point

A light contact point is now textually justified:

```text
pravartaka
    determination of transition into activity

anuvartaka
    determination preserving activity in its execution
```

This is close to a distinction between genesis and maintenance, but no stronger identification with a specific Hegelian or Fichtean category is required at this stage. The Bhāṣya itself is already performing the essential logical discrimination.
