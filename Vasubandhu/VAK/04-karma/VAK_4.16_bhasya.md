# VAK_4.16_bhasya — Names of Restraint and the First Moment of Prātimokṣa

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.16**

```iast
śīlaṃ sucaritaṃ karma saṃvaraś cocyate punaḥ |
ādye vijñaptyavijñaptī prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> Prātimokṣa restraint is called discipline, good conduct, action, and restraint. In the first moment [of undertaking restraint], both manifestation and non-manifest action are called prātimokṣa; prātimokṣa restraint is also called a karmapatha insofar as it belongs to the fundamental course of action.

## Source Cautions

The electronic text reads `prātimokṣakiyāpathaḥ` at [207|17], while the prose immediately glosses the expression with `karmapatha` at [207|19-207|20]. The e-text may therefore contain a transmission or OCR irregularity. The exact source form is preserved here and no silent normalization is imposed.

The Kārikā file's earlier interpretation of `ādye` as “the first two [terms]” is corrected by the Bhāṣya. Vasubandhu explicitly glosses it as `saṃvarasamādānasya prathame`: “in the first [moment] of undertaking restraint.”

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first explains why prātimokṣa restraint receives four names: `śīla`, `sucarita`, `karma`, and `saṃvara`.

It is called `śīla` because it is the undertaking of abstention from irregular or improper actions (`viṣamakarmaṇāṃ viratisamādāna`). He adds an etymological association with coolness (`śītalatva`) and cites a verse in which the body does not burn when wholesome discipline has been undertaken.

It is called `sucarita` because it is praised by the learned (`vidvatpraśastatvāt`).

It is called `karma` because it has the nature of activity (`kriyāsvabhāvatvāt`). This produces an objection: avijñapti has already been called `akriyā`, “non-activity,” so how can it count as activity? Vasubandhu answers that it is called `akriyā` because one who has undertaken it does not commit evil through it. Yet the avijñapti itself is produced by `vijñapti` and `citta`, and therefore is still an activity in the causal sense. Another view explains this by saying it is both a cause and a result of activity.

It is called `saṃvara` because it restrains body and speech.

Vasubandhu then gives a more exact use of `prātimokṣa`. In the first moment of undertaking restraint, both the manifest and non-manifest factors are called `prātimokṣa`, because through them evil is released or cast off (`pāpasya ... prātimokṣaṇād utsarjanāt`). From the second moment onward, there is only `prātimokṣasaṃvara`, not `prātimokṣa` in this strict first-moment sense.

Finally, prātimokṣa restraint is also called a `karmapatha` because it belongs to the fundamental (`maula`) course of action. What follows afterward (`pṛṣṭha`) is not the fundamental karmapatha.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanations of `śīla`, `sucarita`, `karma`, `saṃvara`, and the first-moment sense of `prātimokṣa`.

**Voice: Objector** — `nanu cāvijñaptir akriyety ucyate / sā kathaṃ kriyā bhāti /`: if avijñapti is called non-activity, how can it be activity?

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — avijñapti is called non-activity because the restrained person does not perform evil through it, but it is itself causally produced by manifestation and mind.

**Voice: uncertain / alternative doctrinal explanation** — `kriyāhetutvāt kriyāphalatvāccetyapare`: others explain avijñapti as activity because it is a cause and result of activity.

## New Technical Terms

- **viṣamakarman** — irregular / improper action; **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this immediate sequence; **function:** object of abstention used to explain `śīla`.
- **śītalatva** — coolness; **status:** explanatory etymology; **function:** nirukti for `śīla`.
- **vidvatpraśasta** — praised by the learned; **status:** explanatory phrase; **function:** account of why restraint is called `sucarita`.
- **akriyā** — non-activity; **status:** doctrinal predicate applied to avijñapti; **function:** objection requiring distinction between not doing evil and causal production of avijñapti.
- **prātimokṣaṇa / utsarjana** — release / casting off; **status:** explanatory derivation; **function:** explains the strict first-moment sense of `prātimokṣa`.
- **maula** — fundamental / principal; **status:** new technical distinction in this chapter; **function:** distinguishes the basic karmapatha from what follows afterward.
- **pṛṣṭha** — subsequent / following phase; **status:** new technical distinction; **function:** contrasts with `maula` and prepares later karmapatha analysis.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Four names of prātimokṣa restraint

```text
prātimokṣasaṃvara
├── śīla
│   abstention from improper action
├── sucarita
│   praised good conduct
├── karma
│   causally produced activity
└── saṃvara
    restraint of body and speech
```

The four terms are not simply interchangeable definitions. Each selects a different determination of the same restraint-complex.

### Why avijñapti can be both `akriyā` and `karma`

```text
akriyā
    because the restrained person
    does not perform evil through it

karma / kriyā
    because avijñapti itself
    is causally produced
    by vijñapti and citta
```

Thus the opposition is only apparent. “Non-activity” concerns the evil deed prevented; “activity” concerns the causal production and efficacy of avijñapti itself.

An alternative explanation adds:

```text
avijñapti
    → cause of activity
    → result of activity
```

### Strict and extended prātimokṣa

```text
first moment of restraint-taking
    vijñapti + avijñapti
    → prātimokṣa

second and later moments
    continuing restraint
    → prātimokṣasaṃvara
    → not prātimokṣa in the strict first-moment sense
```

This is the major correction supplied by the Bhāṣya.

### Karmapatha

Prātimokṣa restraint is called a `karmapatha` insofar as it is included in the `maula`, the fundamental action-course. The subsequent phase (`pṛṣṭha`) is not itself the fundamental karmapatha.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā file's provisional reading of `ādye` as “the first two [names]” must be rejected.

A better translation is:

> It is called discipline, good conduct, action, and restraint. In the first [moment of undertaking restraint], manifestation and non-manifest action are both prātimokṣa; prātimokṣa restraint is also called a karmapatha insofar as it belongs to the fundamental course of action.

The exact printed form of the final compound remains textually cautious because the e-text gives `prātimokṣakiyāpathaḥ` while the prose explanation gives `karmapatha`.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.16 distinguishes several relations that should not be collapsed:

```text
one restraint-complex
    may receive several predicates
    according to distinct grounds
```

and:

```text
non-performance of evil
    ≠
absence of causal activity
```

Most importantly, it introduces a temporal distinction inside restraint itself:

```text
production / acquisition moment
    → prātimokṣa

continuation after acquisition
    → prātimokṣasaṃvara
```

The chapter therefore continues its recurring discipline of distinguishing an initiating determination from its subsequent continuation.

## Organon Contact Point

A restrained contact point is enough here:

```text
acquisition
    ≠
continuation

preventive determination
    can itself be positively produced
```

This is important for later reconstruction of practical determination, but no stronger identification with Ground, habit, or freedom is required in the present pass.
