# VAK_4.46_bhasya — Āneñja as Fixed Karmic Maturation

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.46**

```iast
kāmadhātau śubhaṃ karma puṇyam āneñjam ūrdhvajam |
tadbhūmiṣu yataḥ karma vipākaṃ prati neñjati ||
```

> Wholesome karma in the desire-domain is meritorious. Karma produced above is immovable, because with respect to maturation it does not move away from those levels.

## Source Cautions

The e-text varies between forms such as `āneñja`, `āniñja`, and `aviñja` in this local region. The kārikā file uses **āneñja**, and the Bhāṣya's explanatory conclusion `vyavasthitavipākatvād āneñjam` fixes the intended term here.

The quotation concerning the first three dhyānas is used to create an explicit objection: if they have elsewhere been called `señjita`, "moved" or "perturbed," how can karma belonging to them be called `āneñja`, "immovable"? The resolution depends on preserving the different respects in which the predicates are applied.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.45 announced the threefold division:

```text
puṇya
apuṇya
āneñja
```

VAK 4.46 now determines the first and third members.

First:

```text
kāmadhātu + kuśala karma
    → puṇya
```

The word `śubha` carries forward into the second pāda. Thus wholesome karma belonging to the form and formless domains is called `āneñja`.

```text
rūpāvacara kuśala karma
ārūpyāvacara kuśala karma
    → āneñja
```

But Vasubandhu immediately raises a difficulty. The Buddha has elsewhere described the first three dhyānas as `señjita`: there is still "movement" in them, including what is associated with vitarka and vicāra. How then can karma connected with these same meditative levels be called immovable?

The answer is a precise determination of **respect**.

The dhyānas are called moved when one is speaking about defects or perturbations **within samādhi**:

```text
samādau sāpakṣālatā
    → señjita
```

But they are called immovable when one is speaking about **karmic maturation**:

```text
vipākaṃ prati
    → āneñja
```

The same dharma-complex may therefore receive contrary-looking predicates without contradiction because the grounds of predication differ.

Vasubandhu then explains what "movement" of maturation means. Desire-domain karma is `kampate` with respect to maturation because of `avyavasthāna`, lack of fixed placement.

A karma associated with one destiny may mature in another destiny. Karma associated with one class of gods may mature in another divine class. A karma capable of producing such features as measure, strength, color, happiness, and enjoyment among gods may, under the power of other conditions (`anyapratyayavaśāt`), mature instead among humans, animals, or pretas.

Thus desire-domain karma has a comparatively mobile relation between its originating karmic specification and the precise locus of its maturation.

Higher-domain karma is different:

```text
rūpārūpyāvacara karma
    produced with respect to bhūmi X
        → cannot mature in bhūmi Y
```

It never succeeds in ripening in another level (`anyasyāṃ bhūmau`). Therefore Vasubandhu concludes:

```text
vyavasthita-vipākatvāt
    because its maturation is fixed / stationed
        → āneñja
```

The definition of immovability is therefore not absence of change in general. It is **invariance of the bhūmi-relation of karmic maturation**.

The prose closes by observing that `apuṇya` is simply akuśala karma, a usage already established in ordinary discourse.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — principal explanation and resolution.

**Voice: Objector** — `nanu ca trīṇi dhyānāni señjitānyuktāni bhagavatā`: the objection that the Buddha calls the three dhyānas moved.

**Voice: Scriptural citation** — the statement identifying vitarka and vicāra as movement, and the reference to the Āniñja Sūtra.

## New Technical Terms

- **āneñja** — immovable, non-shifting
  - **status:** announced in VAK 4.45; technically defined here
  - **function:** wholesome karma of the form and formless domains whose maturation does not shift to another bhūmi

- **señjita** — moved, perturbed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya contrast term
  - **function:** characterizes meditative states under the aspect of perturbation or defect in samādhi

- **iñjita / iñjati** — movement, to move or shift
  - **status:** lexical root controlling the argument
  - **function:** permits Vasubandhu to distinguish movement in samādhi from movement in karmic maturation

- **kampate** — moves, trembles, shifts
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory verb
  - **function:** describes desire-domain karma's instability with respect to the locus of maturation

- **avyavasthāna** — non-fixity, lack of determinate placement
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only local explanatory term
  - **function:** direct explanation of how desire-domain karmic maturation "moves"

- **vyavasthita-vipākatva** — having fixed or determinate maturation
  - **status:** decisive Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** gives the positive definition of `āneñja`

- **anyapratyayavaśāt** — through the power of other conditions
  - **status:** important causal qualification
  - **function:** explains how desire-domain karma can mature in a locus different from the one initially expected

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya yields a sharper threefold schema than the Kārikā alone:

```text
puṇya
    = kuśala karma of kāmadhātu

apuṇya
    = akuśala karma

āneñja
    = kuśala karma of rūpa / ārūpya
      whose maturation is fixed by bhūmi
```

The crucial distinction is not simply:

```text
lower karma / higher karma
```

but:

```text
DESIRE-DOMAIN KARMA
    vipāka-location relatively mobile
    because other conditions can redirect
    the precise destiny or class of maturation

HIGHER-DOMAIN KARMA
    vipāka-location fixed by bhūmi
    does not mature in another level
```

This also clarifies that `āneñja` is a relational determination:

```text
not "immovable in every respect"

but

"immovable with respect to vipāka-bhūmi"
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation was substantially correct but can now be made more exact.

Preferred translation:

> **Wholesome karma in the desire domain is meritorious; wholesome karma produced above is immovable, because with respect to maturation it does not shift away from its corresponding level.**

The phrase `tadbhūmiṣu ... vipākaṃ prati neñjati` should be understood through the Bhāṣya's `vyavasthita-vipākatva`: the relevant immovability is fixedness of the maturation-level.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya makes an unusually clean logical distinction of predicative respect:

```text
Dhyāna D
    is MOVED
        with respect to internal samādhi-perturbation

Dhyāna-associated karma K
    is IMMOVABLE
        with respect to maturation-level
```

Thus:

```text
MOVED(x, respect A)
    does not contradict
IMMOVABLE(x, respect B)
```

More importantly, `āneñja` expresses an invariant mapping:

```text
karma generated at higher bhūmi X
        ↓
fixed maturation relation
        ↓
vipāka at bhūmi X
```

Whereas desire-domain karma permits conditional redistribution:

```text
karma K
    + other conditions
        ↓
multiple admissible maturation loci
```

The Bhāṣya itself names the contrast:

```text
avyavasthāna
    non-fixity
        ↔
vyavasthita-vipākatva
    fixed maturation
```

This is stronger than saying merely that higher karma is "stable." The stability is generated by a determinate relation between karma and the level of its result.

## Upgraded Organon Contact Point

This verse merits a stronger Organon contact because the logical form is supplied explicitly by Vasubandhu rather than imposed from outside.

`Āneñja` is **relational invariance**:

```text
not
    absolute immobility

but
    invariance under a specified mapping
```

The same determination can vary under one relation while remaining invariant under another:

```text
meditative constitution
    may contain movement

karmic maturation mapping
    remains fixed
```

That distinction is philosophically powerful because it prevents a static interpretation of stability. What remains invariant is not an inert substance but a **determinate causal relation**.

In the present project language:

```text
activity
    does not cease to be activity
        in order to be necessary

rather

the activity acquires a determinate law
    linking its originating bhūmi
    to its maturation bhūmi
```

This remains a restrained upgrade: the text itself gives us movement, conditional displacement, fixed placement, and the relation under which invariance holds.
