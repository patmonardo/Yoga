## 3. Natural Segmentation

The passage has five movements:

1. Vasubandhu announces that he is now giving several **synonymous designations (`paryāya`)** for classes of dharmas.
2. `sāvadyā`, `nivṛtā`, and `hīnā` are synonyms for afflicted (`kliṣṭa`) dharmas.
3. `śubha`, `amala`, and `praṇīta` are applied to wholesome uncontaminated (`kuśala-anāsrava`) dharmas, with the intermediate (`madhya`) inferred between inferior and excellent.
4. Conditioned wholesome dharmas are `sevitavya`, to be cultivated; the remaining dharmas are not.
5. All other dharmas have something superior to them, but liberation alone is unsurpassed because nothing exceeds nirvāṇa.

---

## 4. Literal Prose Translation

> Now some alternative expressions for dharmas are stated.
>
> “Blameworthy, obscured, inferior—afflicted dharmas.” `Sāvadyā`, `nivṛtā`, and `hīnā` are alternative expressions for afflicted dharmas.
>
> “Auspicious, stainless, excellent.” `Praṇīta` is an alternative expression for wholesome uncontaminated dharmas. From inferior and excellent, it follows that the others are intermediate.
>
> “Conditioned wholesome dharmas are to be cultivated.” `Sevitavya` is an alternative expression for wholesome conditioned dharmas. It follows that the remainder are not to be cultivated.
>
> Why is the unconditioned not to be cultivated? Because it cannot be repeatedly practiced and because it is without fruit. For cultivation is undertaken for the sake of a fruit.
>
> All other dharmas have something superior to them.
>
> “But liberation is unsurpassed.” For there is nothing more excellent than nirvāṇa. It is superior to all because it is wholesome and permanent.

## 5. Course of Argument

### 5.1 The verse begins as a lexicon of evaluative synonyms

The Bhāṣya explicitly introduces the section with:

```text
dharmāṇām idānīṃ kecit paryāyā ucyante
```

> Now certain alternative expressions for dharmas are stated.

This is important. The verse is not primarily introducing four newly independent ontological classes. It is consolidating the evaluative vocabulary used for already established classes of dharmas.

### 5.2 Afflicted dharmas

```text
kliṣṭa-dharmāḥ
    = sāvadyāḥ
    = nivṛtāḥ
    = hīnāḥ
```

The Bhāṣya treats the three predicates as `paryāya`, synonymous designations of afflicted dharmas.

A restrained translation is therefore:

```text
sāvadyā
    blameworthy / faulty

nivṛta
    obscured / covered

hīna
    inferior
```

The verse is not claiming three separately generated defects. It says that these are three valid evaluative ways of characterizing what is afflicted.

### 5.3 Wholesome uncontaminated dharmas

The commentary's decisive phrase is:

```text
kuśalānāsravāṇāṃ praṇītā iti paryāyaḥ
```

Thus the positive series belongs specifically to:

```text
kuśala + anāsrava
```

not simply to all wholesome dharmas.

The verse's:

```text
śubhāḥ
amalāḥ
praṇītāḥ
```

therefore characterizes wholesome uncontaminated dharmas as auspicious, stainless, and excellent.

This corrects any reading that would equate every conditioned wholesome karma with `amala` or `praṇīta` in the strongest sense.

### 5.4 The middle is inferred

Vasubandhu adds:

```text
hīnapraṇītebhyo 'nye madhyā iti siddhaṃ bhavati
```

> From the inferior and the excellent, the others are established as intermediate.

So the evaluative scale is:

```text
hīna
    inferior

madhya
    intermediate

praṇīta
    excellent
```

The middle term is not stated in the kārikā but is inferred by the commentary.

### 5.5 Which dharmas are to be cultivated?

```text
saṃskṛta-kuśala
    → sevitavya
```

Conditioned wholesome dharmas are to be practiced, attended to, repeatedly cultivated.

The remaining dharmas are not `sevitavya`.

This does not mean that the unconditioned is rejected. The reason it is not cultivated is precisely that it is not something produced or strengthened through repetition.

### 5.6 Why the unconditioned is not cultivated

The Bhāṣya gives two reasons:

```text
anabhyasanīyatvāt
    because it cannot be repeatedly practiced / habituated

aphalatvāt
    because it does not yield a further fruit
```

And then the general principle:

```text
phalārthaṃ hi sevā bhavati
```

> Cultivation is for the sake of fruit.

This produces a sharp distinction between **means** and **end**:

```text
conditioned wholesome dharmas
    are cultivated as causes

unconditioned liberation
    is not cultivated as another productive cause
```

### 5.7 Liberation alone is unsurpassed

The commentary states:

```text
sottarā anye sarvadharmāḥ
```

> All other dharmas have something superior to them.

Then:

```text
mokṣas tv anuttaraḥ
```

> Liberation, however, is unsurpassed.

Why?

```text
na hi nirvāṇād viśiṣṭataram asti
```

> There is nothing more excellent than nirvāṇa.

And:

```text
tac ca sarvebhyo viśiṣṭaṃ kuśalanityatvāt
```

> It is superior to all because it is wholesome and permanent.

So the final chapter judgment is not simply that nirvāṇa is the highest product. It is precisely **not another conditioned product** within the karmic sequence.

## 6. Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduction of the synonym taxonomy and explanatory glosses.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation that the unconditioned is not cultivated because it cannot be habituated and has no further fruit.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — final assertion that nothing is more excellent than nirvāṇa.

No explicit objection-response dialogue occurs in this closing passage.

---

## 7. New Technical Terms

- **paryāya** — alternative expression, synonymous designation.
  - **status:** previously encountered vocabulary, but important here as the formal key to the verse.
  - **function:** metalinguistic classification.

- **madhya** — intermediate, middle in rank between `hīna` and `praṇīta`.
  - **status:** not stated in the kārikā; supplied by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** inferred evaluative category.

- **sevitavya** — to be practiced, attended upon, cultivated repeatedly.
  - **status:** develops `sevya` in the kārikā.
  - **function:** practical prescription for conditioned wholesome dharmas.

- **anabhyasanīyatva** — incapacity for repeated practice or habituation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
  - **function:** reason the unconditioned is not an object of cultivation.

- **aphalatva** — absence of a further fruit.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
  - **function:** second reason the unconditioned is not cultivated.

- **sottara** — having something higher or superior.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this passage.
  - **function:** contrast class to `anuttara`.

## 8. Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The closing taxonomy is best represented as three different questions.

### Ethical-afflictive status

```text
kliṣṭa
    → sāvadyā
    → nivṛta
    → hīna
```

### Purity and rank

```text
kuśala-anāsrava
    → śubha
    → amala
    → praṇīta
```

with:

```text
hīna < madhya < praṇīta
```

### Practical relation

```text
saṃskṛta-kuśala
    → sevitavya

asaṃskṛta
    → not sevitavya
    because not habituated into existence
    and not productive of a further fruit
```

Finally:

```text
all other dharmas
    → sottara

mokṣa / nirvāṇa
    → anuttara
```

This gives the Karma chapter a precise ending:

```text
karma can be purified
wholesome causes can be cultivated
cultivation can be intensified
but the final end is not another karma
```

The chapter therefore ends by placing the entire causal technology of karma beneath a goal that is not reducible to karmic production.

## 9. Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation needs three refinements.

### 9.1 `śubhāmalāḥ praṇītāḥ`

This should not be generalized to all wholesome dharmas. The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies the referent as:

```text
kuśalānāsravāḥ
```

Therefore:

> **Wholesome uncontaminated dharmas are auspicious, stainless, and excellent.**

### 9.2 `saṃskṛtaśubhāḥ sevyāḥ`

This means specifically:

> **Conditioned wholesome dharmas are to be cultivated.**

The unconditioned is excluded not because it is inferior but because it is not a practice-produced cause directed toward another fruit.

### 9.3 `mokṣas tv anuttaraḥ`

The Bhāṣya identifies `mokṣa` here with nirvāṇa and gives the explicit criterion:

```text
kuśalanityatvāt
    because it is wholesome and permanent
```

Thus a strong final translation is:

> **Afflicted dharmas are called blameworthy, obscured, and inferior; wholesome uncontaminated dharmas are auspicious, stainless, and excellent. Conditioned wholesome dharmas are to be cultivated; liberation, however, is unsurpassed.**

---

## 10. Light Organon Contact

The final verse gives a disciplined relation between **practice and end**.

```text
conditioned wholesome determinations
    are means
    therefore cultivated

mokṣa
    is not another means
    and not another karmic product
```

This prevents the practical system from confusing ever-more-refined conditioning with freedom itself.

The chapter has spent enormous effort analyzing how activity is generated, intensified, accumulated, purified, and cultivated. It closes by stating that the highest term is not the perfection of that machinery as machinery.

```text
refine the causal instrument
    → necessary

absolutize the causal instrument
    → mistake

mokṣa
    → anuttara
```

That is a fitting closure for the Karma chapter: Practical Reason must master conditioned causality, but liberation is not merely its most successful conditioned product.
