# VAK 4.117 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.117**  
**gatiduḥkhopakāritvaguṇaiḥ kṣetraṃ viśiṣyate |**  
**agraṃ muktasya muktāya bodhisattvasya cāṣṭamam || 4.117 ||**

Best current translation:

> The recipient-field is distinguished by destiny, suffering, beneficence, and qualities. Foremost is the gift of a liberated person to one who is liberated, and also [the gift] to a Bodhisattva; among the other eight gifts taught by the Blessed One, the eighth is foremost.

See the established Kārikā analysis: [`VAK_4.117.md`](./VAK_4.117.md).

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 270.04–270.26.

The commentary opens by asking how the recipient-field is distinguished, then analyzes the four determinants named in the Kārikā. It next identifies the highest material gift, adds the special case of giving to a Bodhisattva, and finally explains the reference to the eighth of another canonical list of eight gifts.

Key source sequence:

- `atha kṣetraṃ kathaṃ viśiṣyate /`
- `gatiduḥkhopakāritvaguṇaiḥ kṣetraṃ viśiṣyate /`
- examples under `gati`, `duḥkha`, `upakāritva`, and `guṇa`
- `sarveṣāṃ tu dānānām agraṃ muktasya muktāya`
- `bodhisattvasya ca`
- `... anyānyaṣṭau dānānyuktāni teṣām aṣṭamam ... agram`

## Natural Segmentation

The passage falls into five movements:

1. **Four grounds of field-distinction** — destiny, suffering, prior beneficence, qualities.
2. **Canonical examples** — each ground is illustrated by scripture or narrative.
3. **Foremost material gift** — a liberated giver giving to a liberated recipient.
4. **Special Bodhisattva case** — giving to a Bodhisattva is also supreme because the Bodhisattva is the cause of benefit to all beings.
5. **The eighth of eight gifts** — among another canonical series, the eighth is best because it is directed toward the highest aim.

## Literal Prose Translation

How, then, is the field distinguished?

The field is distinguished through destiny, suffering, beneficence, and qualities.

First, it is distinguished through difference of destiny. Thus the Blessed One said: “Having given a gift to one born in an animal destiny, a hundredfold maturation is to be expected; having given to an immoral human being, a thousandfold maturation.”

It is distinguished through difference of suffering. Thus, among the meritorious activities based on material support, gifts to the sick, gifts to attendants upon the sick, and the other such cases are taught; and it is said that for a faithful son or daughter of good family endowed with those seven material-support bases of meritorious activity, the measure of the merits cannot be grasped.

It is distinguished through difference of beneficence, as in the case of mother and father and other benefactors, illustrated by such narratives as the Ṛṣyamṛga Jātaka.

It is distinguished through difference of qualities, as in such statements as: “Having given to one endowed with discipline, the maturation is a hundred-thousandfold.”

But among all gifts, foremost is that of one liberated to one liberated. The Blessed One has said: “When one free from passion gives to one free from passion, this gift is exceedingly great; among material gifts this is the best gift.”

And [a gift] to a Bodhisattva [is also foremost]. Or, when one gives to a Bodhisattva, because he is the cause of benefit to all beings, that gift—although given by one not liberated to one not liberated—is supreme.

Setting this aside, among the other eight gifts taught by the Blessed One, the eighth is foremost. What are the eight? Giving to one who has approached; giving from fear; giving because ‘he gave to me’; giving because ‘he will give to me’; giving because ‘my father and grandfather gave before’; giving for heaven; giving for fame; and giving for the adornment of mind, purification of mind, as equipment for yoga, for attainment of the highest goal.

The ancient teachers explain `āsādya-dāna` as giving to those who have come near and approached. `Bhayadāna` is giving when one sees oneself facing destruction and thinks, “Better that I give.” The rest are easy to understand and are not separately explained.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Field-quality is not one-dimensional

The recipient is not ranked by one simple scale. The Bhāṣya identifies four distinct grounds:

```text
kṣetra-viśeṣa
│
├── gati
│   └── existential mode / rebirth condition
├── duḥkha
│   └── present suffering or need
├── upakāritva
│   └── history of benefit toward the giver
└── guṇa
    └── realized ethical or spiritual qualities
```

The same recipient can instantiate several grounds simultaneously.

### 2. `Gati` — existential condition

The scriptural comparison is striking:

```text
gift to animal
    → hundredfold maturation

gift to immoral human
    → thousandfold maturation
```

The point is not that the immoral human is ethically superior. The field differs because the recipient occupies a different existential mode. `Gati` therefore belongs to the objective constitution of the field independently of the recipient’s moral excellence.

### 3. `Duḥkha` — need itself can distinguish a field

The Bhāṣya cites giving to the sick and to those who care for the sick. Here the field is distinguished not by attainment but by suffering.

```text
spiritual excellence
    is one source of field-power

acute need
    is another source of field-power
```

This is an important correction to any simple idea that a “better field” must always mean a more spiritually advanced recipient.

### 4. `Upakāritva` — the field contains relational history

Parents and other benefactors are distinguished because of what they have previously done for the giver.

Thus the field is historical:

```text
past benefit received
    → persists as present practical determination
```

The same relation that earlier made injury to parents especially grave now makes benefaction toward them especially weighty.

### 5. `Guṇa` — realized excellence

Giving to one endowed with discipline is said to produce vastly greater maturation. This is the familiar `guṇakṣetra`, the field constituted by qualities.

Here giving most naturally takes the form of `pūjā`: honor directed toward realized excellence.

## Foremost Giving

The Bhāṣya presents two different supreme cases.

### A. Liberated giver → liberated recipient

```text
vītarāga giver
    → vītarāga recipient
```

The Blessed One is quoted as calling this the best among material gifts.

Both relational poles are free from passion. The gift is therefore purified both in the agent and in the field.

This does not mean that the gift produces liberation. Rather, the act itself is performed within a relation already purified of ordinary acquisitive attachment.

### B. Gift to a Bodhisattva

The Bhāṣya then adds:

```text
amukta giver
    → amukta Bodhisattva
```

This too may be supreme, despite neither party yet being liberated, because the Bodhisattva is:

` sarvasattvahitahetu `

> the cause of benefit for all beings.

The field is therefore evaluated not only by present attainment but by its **causal reach**.

A Bodhisattva is an extraordinary field because support given there is related to a trajectory whose purpose is universal benefit.

## The Eighth of Eight Gifts

The phrase `aṣṭamam` does **not** refer to an eightfold hierarchy completed by VAK 4.118. The Bhāṣya explicitly introduces a separate canonical list of eight gifts.

The series culminates in giving:

- for adornment of mind,
- purification of mind,
- equipment for yoga,
- attainment of the highest goal.

That eighth is called `agra`, foremost.

This substantially corrects the uncertainty of the Kārikā pass.

The sequence can be read as a purification of motive:

```text
proximity
fear
reciprocity
anticipated reciprocity
family custom
heaven
fame
highest spiritual purpose
```

The eighth is best because the purpose of giving has become ordered toward transformation of mind and the supreme end rather than worldly exchange or reward.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — framing question, organization of the four field-distinctions, interpretive transitions.
- **Voice: scriptural citation** — numerical examples for animal/human recipients; the superiority of liberated-to-liberated giving; the eightfold gift series.
- **Voice: ancient teachers (`paurāṇāḥ`)** — explanation of `āsādya-dāna`.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — gloss of `bhaya-dāna` and decision not to explain the remaining transparent cases.

## New Technical Terms

- **`kṣetra-viśeṣa`** — distinction or excellence of the recipient-field.  
  **Status:** previously encountered in related form.  
  **Function:** general classificatory principle.

- **`gativiśeṣa`** — distinction through mode of rebirth.  
  **Status:** new in this local analysis.  
  **Function:** field-determination by existential condition.

- **`duḥkhaviśeṣa`** — distinction through suffering.  
  **Status:** new in this local analysis.  
  **Function:** field-determination through need.

- **`upakāritvaviśeṣa`** — distinction through prior beneficence.  
  **Status:** conceptually prepared earlier.  
  **Function:** relational/historical determination of field.

- **`guṇaviśeṣa`** — distinction through realized qualities.  
  **Status:** previously encountered as `guṇakṣetra`.  
  **Function:** determination through ethical/spiritual excellence.

- **`āsādya-dāna`** — gift made to one who has approached / come near.  
  **Status:** new.  
  **Function:** first item in canonical eightfold gift series.

- **`bhaya-dāna`** — gift made from fear.  
  **Status:** new.  
  **Function:** second item in canonical eightfold gift series.

- **`yogasaṃbhāra`** — equipment or accumulation for yoga.  
  **Status:** rare/new in this passage.  
  **Function:** part of the motive defining the highest of the eight gifts.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The complete giving relation developed in 4.114–4.117 is now explicit:

```text
giver
    +
gift-object
    +
recipient-field
    =
determinate giving-event
```

But `recipient-field` itself is internally articulated:

```text
FieldPower = f(
    destiny,
    suffering,
    priorBeneficence,
    realizedQualities,
    causalReach
)
```

The Bodhisattva introduces the last factor especially clearly. Present qualities are not the whole truth of a field; a field can also be extraordinary because of the universal benefit it is causally ordered to produce.

The passage therefore resists a flat merit calculus. Different fields are powerful for different reasons.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

Two points in the first-pass file require correction.

First, `bodhisattvasya cāṣṭamam` should **not** be read as saying that the Bodhisattva is the eighth member of a hierarchy completed in VAK 4.118.

The Bhāṣya separates:

```text
bodhisattvasya ca
    → gift to a Bodhisattva is also supreme

...
anyānyaṣṭau dānāni
    → another canonical set of eight gifts

aṣṭamam agram
    → the eighth of those is foremost
```

Second, the highest gift is not reducible to one linear ranking. The commentary explicitly allows both:

- liberated → liberated;
- gift to a Bodhisattva because of universal-benefit causality.

A more precise translation is therefore:

> **The recipient-field is distinguished by destiny, suffering, beneficence, and qualities. Foremost is the gift of a liberated person to one who is liberated, and also [a gift] to a Bodhisattva; among the [other] eight gifts, the eighth is foremost.**

## Light Organon Contact

The field is not a passive endpoint. It determines the Concept of the act.

```text
to the suffering
    giving = relief

to the benefactor
    giving = gratitude

to the excellent
    giving = honor

to the Bodhisattva
    giving = support of universal future benefit
```

The same transferred object changes its practical determination according to the relation into which it enters.

This is a particularly clean case of determinate relation: **the deed is what it is only within the whole relational field**.

The Bodhisattva case adds a genetic dimension. The field is not exhausted by present predicates; it is also determined by the future universal activity whose cause is already present in the Bodhisattva trajectory.
