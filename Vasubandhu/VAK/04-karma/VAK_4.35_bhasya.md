# VAK_4.35_bhasya — The Objective and Temporal Scope of the Three Restraints

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
sarvobhayebhyaḥ kāmāpto vartamānebhya āpyate |
maulebhyaḥ sarvakālebhyo dhyānānāsravasaṃvarau || 4.35 ||
```

Working translation:

> Desire-realm restraint is acquired with respect to all [three stages] and both [classes], from presently existing bases. Meditation-born and uncontaminated restraint are acquired with respect to the root karmapathas, from bases belonging to all times.

The Bhāṣya is required to unpack the compressed expressions `sarva`, `ubhaya`, `vartamāna`, `maula`, and `sarvakāla`.

See: `VAK_4.35.md`.

## 3. Source Cautions

- The source prints `skandhāyatana dhātubhyaḥ`; this is retained without silent normalization.
- `sarvobhayebhyaḥ` cannot be translated adequately without the Bhāṣya. `sarva` and `ubhaya` each cover specific technical contrasts.
- The expression `kāmāpta` is explicitly glossed as `prātimokṣasaṃvara`, the restraint belonging to the desire realm.
- `maulebhyaḥ` means the root or principal karmapathas, not simply “fundamental offenses” in a vague sense.
- The four-cornered analysis belongs to the natural argument of 4.35 and is therefore retained in this file.

## 4. Natural Argument

The passage begins with a structural question about the three restraints:

```text
prātimokṣa-saṃvara
dhyāna-saṃvara
anāsrava-saṃvara

Does acquisition of one
from a given objective basis
imply acquisition of the other two
from that same basis?
```

The answer is no. Their objective and temporal scopes differ.

### A. Scope of prātimokṣa restraint

The desire-realm restraint is acquired only with respect to **present** `skandha`, `āyatana`, and `dhātu` bases.

The Bhāṣya then decomposes `sarva`:

```text
sarva
    = maula
      prayoga
      pṛṣṭha
```

That is, prātimokṣa restraint covers:

1. the root karmic act,
2. its preparatory stage,
3. its aftermath or continuation.

It then decomposes `ubhaya` in two ways:

```text
ubhaya
    = sattva + asattva
```

and

```text
ubhaya
    = prakṛtisāvadya
      + pratikṣepaṇasāvadya
```

Thus the ordinary vow extends across both sentient and non-sentient objective bases, and across both naturally blameworthy acts and acts blameworthy by prohibition.

But it is acquired from present bases only.

The reason given is decisive:

```text
sattvādhiṣṭhāna-pravṛtti
```

Prātimokṣa operates with a living being as its support or objective basis. Past and future beings are not presently counted as living beings in that relevant sense.

### B. Scope of dhyāna-born and uncontaminated restraint

The two higher restraints differ in both dimensions.

They are acquired only with respect to:

```text
maula karmapatha
```

not with respect to the preparatory and aftermath stages, and certainly not merely conventionally blameworthy prohibitions.

But temporally they are broader:

```text
past
present
future
```

They are acquired with respect to `skandha`, `āyatana`, and `dhātu` belonging to all three times.

### C. The catuṣkoṭika

Vasubandhu then uses a four-cornered analysis to make the non-coincidence precise.

#### First corner — prātimokṣa only

```text
present
+ preparatory / aftermath stages
+ pratikṣepaṇasāvadya
```

These ground prātimokṣa restraint but not dhyāna-born or uncontaminated restraint.

#### Second corner — higher restraints only

```text
past or future
+ root karmapathas
```

These ground dhyāna-born and uncontaminated restraint but not prātimokṣa restraint.

#### Third corner — both

```text
present
+ root karmapathas
```

These ground both prātimokṣa and the two higher restraints.

#### Fourth corner — neither

```text
past or future
+ preparatory / aftermath stages
```

These ground neither class in the relevant way.

### D. Final precision about “present”

The Bhāṣya finally warns that one should not say that presently occurring karmapathas themselves exist at the exact moment of restraint acquisition.

Rather, one should speak of:

```text
vartamānādhiṣṭhāna
    present objective/support bases
```

Strict restraint applies to what is future:

```text
anāgata
    can be restrained

atīta / vartamāna
    cannot literally be restrained
```

Past action is finished, and present action is already occurring. The restraint is acquired **from present bases**, but its prohibitive force bears on future misconduct.

## 5. Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma framework** — the opening question and decomposition of the scopes of the three restraints.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika doctrinal framework, likely** — the real acquisition of restraints from determinate `skandha-āyatana-dhātu` bases and the temporal analysis presuppose the Sarvāstivāda architecture used throughout this section.

**Voice: Objector or corrective interlocutor** — the final objection that presently occurring karmapathas cannot themselves be what is restrained.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / response** — the correction to `vartamānādhiṣṭhāna`: acquisition is based on present supports, while literal restraint concerns future action.

## 6. New Technical Terms

- **`maula`** — the root or principal karmic act / karmapatha. **Status:** previously encountered conceptually, newly decisive here. **Function:** determines the narrow objective scope of dhyāna-born and uncontaminated restraint.
- **`prayoga`** — preparatory activity leading toward a root karmic act. **Status:** important Bhāṣya technical term. **Function:** one of the three stages covered by prātimokṣa restraint.
- **`pṛṣṭha`** — aftermath, continuation, or subsequent stage of an act. **Status:** important Bhāṣya technical term. **Function:** third stage included in ordinary restraint.
- **`sattvākhyā / asattvākhyā`** — designated as sentient / non-sentient. **Status:** Bhāṣya specification. **Function:** first decomposition of `ubhaya`.
- **`prakṛtisāvadya`** — naturally blameworthy. **Status:** encountered directly in 4.34. **Function:** one class of conduct covered by prātimokṣa restraint.
- **`pratikṣepaṇasāvadya`** — blameworthy because prohibited. **Status:** encountered directly in 4.34. **Function:** second class covered by prātimokṣa but excluded from the two higher restraints.
- **`sattvādhiṣṭhānapravṛtta`** — operating with a living being as objective/support basis. **Status:** new formulation. **Function:** explains why prātimokṣa acquisition is restricted to present bases.
- **`catuṣkoṭika`** — four-cornered analysis. **Status:** standard Abhidharma logical device. **Function:** differentiates the overlapping scopes of the restraints without collapsing them.
- **`vartamānādhiṣṭhāna`** — a presently existing support or objective basis. **Status:** new precision. **Function:** resolves the apparent contradiction between present acquisition-bases and future restraint.

## 7. Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya yields a much more exact model than “ordinary restraint is present, higher restraint is universal.”

### Prātimokṣa

```text
realm:
    desire realm

time of acquisition-basis:
    present only

act-stage scope:
    root
    preparation
    aftermath

object-class scope:
    sentient
    non-sentient

fault-class scope:
    naturally blameworthy
    prohibition-based blameworthy
```

### Dhyāna-born and uncontaminated restraint

```text
time of acquisition-basis:
    past
    present
    future

act-stage scope:
    root karmapatha only

not acquired from:
    preparation
    aftermath
    merely prohibition-based faults
```

Thus the higher restraints are temporally broader but structurally narrower with respect to the phase and type of misconduct.

## 8. Effect on Kārikā Translation

The earlier translation:

> “The restraint belonging to the desire realm is acquired with respect to all relevant classes and to presently existing beings. The meditation-born and uncontaminated restraints are acquired with respect to the fundamental offenses and throughout all times.”

is directionally correct but too vague.

A better Bhāṣya-informed translation is:

> **Desire-realm restraint is acquired, from present bases, with respect to all three stages [root act, preparation, and aftermath] and both classes [sentient and non-sentient; naturally blameworthy and prohibition-based]. Meditation-born and uncontaminated restraint are acquired with respect to the root karmapathas, from bases belonging to all three times.**

The compressed `sarvobhayebhyaḥ` should therefore never be flattened to “all relevant classes” without retaining the Bhāṣya’s twofold decomposition.

## 9. Logical Determination

The three restraints cannot be ordered on a single scale from “less universal” to “more universal.” Their scopes cross-cut one another.

```text
prātimokṣa
    temporally narrower
    structurally broader

higher restraints
    temporally broader
    structurally narrower
```

The catuṣkoṭika makes this irreducibility explicit.

A second distinction is equally important:

```text
basis from which restraint is acquired
    ≠
action temporally restrained
```

Prātimokṣa is acquired from **present** living/objective supports, but restraint strictly concerns **future** misconduct.

This avoids the absurdity of imagining that an already completed past act, or an act already presently occurring, can be prevented retroactively.

## 10. Organon Contact Point

The clean contact point is the multidimensional character of determination.

```text
scope
    is not one scalar quantity
```

A determination may be broader in one dimension and narrower in another.

```text
prātimokṣa:
    broad across stages and fault-types
    narrow across time

higher restraint:
    narrow across stages
    broad across time
```

The Bhāṣya therefore resists any simplistic hierarchy in which “higher” means “contains everything lower plus more.” The higher restraint has a different **principle of determination**.

This is especially useful for the Rational Kośa because the doctrine is genetic rather than merely taxonomic: the **mode of acquisition determines the field over which the restraint has validity**.
