# VAK_4.51_bhasya — Four-Cornered Fixity and Projection of a Rebirth Continuum

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.51**

```iast
catuṣkoṭikam ity anye nikāyākṣepaṇaṃ tribhiḥ |
sarvatra catur ākṣepaḥ śubhasya narake tridhā ||
```

> Others analyze it through four alternatives. A rebirth-continuum is projected by three. In general all four kinds of karma may be accumulated throughout the realms and destinies; but in hell wholesome karma is threefold.

## Source Cautions

The first-pass Kārikā file correctly anticipated that the `catuṣkoṭika` concerns two dimensions of fixity, but the Bhāṣya makes their names exact: `avasthā-niyata` and `vipāka-niyata`.

The first pass also treated `nikāyākṣepaṇa` as opening the later projecting/completing distinction. That is too anticipatory here. In this passage Vasubandhu's precise claim is narrower: of the four temporal kinds of karma, **three can project a `nikāyasabhāga`; `dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya` cannot**.

`ākṣepa` in the final half must also be read carefully. The Bhāṣya speaks of the occurrence/accumulation of the four karmic types in the realms and destinies, then specifically of projection of `nikāyasabhāga`. These functions should not be collapsed.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.50 distinguished four principal temporal classes:

```text
dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya
upapadyavedanīya
aparaparyāyavedanīya
aniyatavedanīya
```

VAK 4.51 first records a Dārṣṭāntika analysis that crosses two independent questions:

```text
Is the temporal stage fixed?
    avasthā-niyata

Is maturation itself fixed?
    vipāka-niyata
```

This generates a genuine four-cornered analysis:

```text
1. stage-fixed / maturation-unfixed
2. maturation-fixed / stage-unfixed
3. both fixed
4. neither fixed
```

The Dārṣṭāntikas consequently obtain eight karmic classes by allowing each temporal class to be either fixed or unfixed with respect to maturation.

The opposed account is stricter:

```text
dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya
upapadyavedanīya
aparaparyāyavedanīya
    → necessarily fixed

aniyatavedanīya
    → fourth class
```

The Bhāṣya then asks whether all four kinds can be generated in a single moment. It says yes: if one causes three others to act and oneself engages in sexual misconduct, and the four acts reach completion simultaneously, four karmic types may be projected in one moment.

The argument then changes from classification to rebirth-function:

```text
nikāyākṣepaṇaṃ tribhiḥ
```

Only three of the four temporal types project a new `nikāyasabhāga`. Present-life-result karma cannot do so, because its result belongs to the already existing life rather than establishing the next rebirth-continuum.

Finally Vasubandhu gives the general distribution rule:

```text
all three dhātus
all destinies
    → four kinds may occur as appropriate
```

and then an exception:

```text
hell
    wholesome karma only threefold
    not dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya
```

Why? Because in hell there is no desirable maturation (`iṣṭavipāka`) available in that very life.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Dārṣṭāntika position** — the explicit `catuṣkoṭika` analysis.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / received doctrinal exposition** — the explanation of the four temporal types, `nikāyākṣepaṇa`, realm distribution, and the hell exception.

The sentence `niyatameva tu...` records the contrasting standard interpretation rather than the Dārṣṭāntika multiplication.

## New Technical Terms

- **avasthā-niyata** — fixed with respect to temporal stage
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification of the four-cornered analysis
  - **function:** specifies whether fruition is tied to this life, next life, or a later life

- **vipāka-niyata** — fixed with respect to maturation
  - **status:** newly decisive in this local controversy
  - **function:** specifies whether maturation itself must occur

- **nikāyasabhāga** — homogeneous rebirth-continuum / class-continuity
  - **status:** previously encountered technical Abhidharma term
  - **function:** names the rebirth-series projected by karma

- **nikāyākṣepaṇa** — projection of a rebirth-continuum
  - **status:** important new karmic function in this block
  - **function:** distinguishes karma establishing a future rebirth-series from karma fructifying within the present life

- **utsarga / apavāda** — general rule / exception
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya reasoning vocabulary
  - **function:** controls the distribution statement `sarvatra` and its hell exception

## Doctrinal Determination

The Dārṣṭāntika `catuṣkoṭi` is not a vague logical flourish. It is a two-axis modal analysis:

```text
                 VIPĀKA
              fixed   unfixed

AVASTHĀ fixed    III      I
        unfixed  II       IV
```

Thus:

```text
necessity of maturation
    ≠
necessity of temporal placement
```

That is the principal doctrinal gain from the first half of the verse.

The second half introduces another independent determination:

```text
karma that bears fruit in this life
    cannot project the future nikāyasabhāga

future-directed karmas
    can project a new rebirth-continuum
```

And the hell exception shows that karmic classification is constrained by the experiential possibilities of the destination:

```text
hell continuum
    no desirable present-life maturation
        → no wholesome dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya karma
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation was too compressed in its final half and too quick to introduce the projecting/completing distinction.

Preferred translation:

> **Others analyze karma through four alternatives. A rebirth-continuum is projected by three of the four temporal kinds, not by karma whose fruit is experienced in the present life. In general all four kinds may occur throughout the realms and destinies; but in hell wholesome karma is only threefold, since desirable present-life maturation does not occur there.**

The opening `catuṣkoṭika` should be understood through the Bhāṣya as crossing temporal-stage fixity with maturation fixity.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.50 gave a simple modal-temporal classification. VAK 4.51 reflects on it and discovers two independent modal axes:

```text
AVASTHĀ-FIXITY
    when?

VIPĀKA-FIXITY
    whether necessarily?
```

Hence:

```text
WHEN fixed
    does not entail
WHETHER fixed

WHETHER fixed
    does not entail
WHEN fixed
```

This is a much stronger model than a single `niyata / aniyata` flag.

The verse then adds a distinct causal role:

```text
FRUIT-BEARING-IN-CURRENT-LIFE
    ≠
PROJECTING-A-FUTURE-CONTINUUM
```

So karma now carries at least three separable dimensions:

```text
modal necessity
    whether fruit must occur

temporal determination
    when fruit occurs

projective function
    whether it establishes a future rebirth-continuum
```

## Organon Contact Point

The strongest restrained contact point is **orthogonal determination**.

A single causal determination can be fixed under one relation and unfixed under another:

```text
same karma
    temporally fixed
    maturation-modally unfixed
```

or the reverse.

This is precisely the kind of structure that disappears when a complex determination is represented by one undifferentiated property such as `necessary`.

The second contact point is equally important:

```text
producing a result within a field
    ≠
projecting the field itself
```

But the later technical distinction between `ākṣepaka` and `paripūraka` should not yet be imported here beyond this textual result.
