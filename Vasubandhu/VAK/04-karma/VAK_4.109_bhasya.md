# VAK_4.109_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.109**

**jambūdvīpe pumān eva saṃmukhaṃ buddhacetanaḥ |**  
**cintāmayaṃ kalpaśate śeṣam ākṣipate hi tat || 4.109 ||**

> Only in Jambūdvīpa, as a male, face-to-face with the Teacher, and with volition taking a Buddha as its object, does the Bodhisattva produce that reflection-born karma; over a hundred kalpas he projects the remainder of the mark-producing accumulation.

See: [VAK_4.109.md](./VAK_4.109.md)

## Natural Segmentation

1. The mark-producing karma is projected only in Jambūdvīpa.
2. The traditional bodily qualification is male embodiment.
3. The Teacher must be immediately present.
4. The volition takes a Buddha as its object.
5. The karma is produced through reflection (`cintāmaya`), not merely through hearing or meditative cultivation.
6. The standard accumulation lasts one hundred kalpas.
7. Śākyamuni, through exceptional vigor, is said to have shortened this by nine kalpas and completed it in ninety-one.
8. The previous teachers add that certain defects have already been abandoned and certain qualities acquired from an earlier point in the Bodhisattva career.

## Literal Prose Translation

Only in Jambūdvīpa does the Bodhisattva project the karma whose maturation is the [Great Person’s] marks, and not elsewhere, because those born in Jambūdvīpa possess sharp intelligence. He does so only as a man, not as a woman.

This karma is performed only when the Teacher is immediately present, through a volition whose object is a Buddha. It is produced through reflection; it is not produced merely from hearing and is not produced through meditative cultivation.

He projects the remainder of this karma over one hundred kalpas, not over many unrelated existences. The Blessed Śākyamuni, however, because of the intensity of his vigor, turned back nine kalpas and completed the projection in ninety-one kalpas.

Accordingly a saying is cited in which he declares that, looking back over ninety-one kalpas, he does not remember even a single family having been harmed because of giving him alms. The previous teachers further maintain that from an earlier point in the Bodhisattva career he has already abandoned four defects and acquired two qualities.

## Course of Argument

VAK 4.108 established the beginning of the Bodhisattva in the strict technical sense: once the being begins producing `lakṣaṇa-vipāka-karman`, he has entered certainty (`niyatipatita`). VAK 4.109 now determines the conditions under which that karma can actually be produced.

The Bhāṣya does not treat the future Buddha-body as an accidental ornament added at the end of the career. Its marks are the maturation of a highly specific karmic practice. That practice is constrained by locus, embodiment, relation to a living Buddha, cognitive mode, and temporal accumulation.

The most important clarification concerns `buddhacetanaḥ`. The first-pass reading “volition directed toward Buddhahood” is too teleological. Vasubandhu says explicitly:

`buddhālambanayaiva cetanayā`

The volition has **a Buddha as its object-support**. The present Buddha is thus not merely an image of the Bodhisattva’s future state; the Bodhisattva acts in direct relation to an actual Buddha as field and object.

The second clarification concerns `cintāmaya`. The Bhāṣya uses the standard threefold distinction:

- `śrutamaya` — produced from hearing/study;
- `cintāmaya` — produced from reflection;
- `bhāvanāmaya` — produced from cultivation/meditation.

The mark-producing karma belongs specifically to the middle term, `cintāmaya`. It is therefore a consciously reflected and discriminatively formed karmic activity, not merely inherited from instruction and not generated in meditative absorption.

Finally, the hundred-kalpa period is presented as a standard measure rather than an absolutely fixed duration. Śākyamuni’s exceptional vigor (`uttaptavīryatā`) contracts the process by nine kalpas.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of the conditions of `lakṣaṇavipāka-karman`.

**Voice: received Abhidharma doctrine** — restriction to Jambūdvīpa, male embodiment, direct presence of the Teacher, and the hundred-kalpa norm.

**Voice: scriptural citation** — the ninety-one-kalpa recollection concerning families that gave alms.

**Voice: previous teachers (`pūrvācāryāḥ`)** — the final supplementary claim about four abandoned defects and two acquired qualities.

## New Technical Terms

- **lakṣaṇa-vipāka-karman** — karma whose maturation is the marks of a Great Person.  
  **Status:** previously encountered in 4.108.  
  **Function:** defines the causal production of Buddha-form.

- **buddhālambana-cetanā** — volition taking a Buddha as its object-support.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā’s `buddhacetanaḥ`.  
  **Function:** specifies the intentional field of the mark-producing karma.

- **cintāmaya** — produced through reflection.  
  **Status:** doctrinally familiar, newly decisive here.  
  **Function:** distinguishes the cognitive mode of this karma from hearing and meditation.

- **śrutamaya** — produced from hearing/study.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only contrast in this passage.  
  **Function:** excluded cognitive mode.

- **bhāvanāmaya** — produced through meditative cultivation.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only contrast in this passage.  
  **Function:** excluded cognitive mode.

- **uttaptavīryatā** — exceptionally intense or ardent vigor.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit.  
  **Function:** explains Śākyamuni’s contraction of the hundred-kalpa norm to ninety-one.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The mark-producing karma is conditioned by five determinations:

```text
locus
    Jambūdvīpa

embodiment
    traditionally qualified male human birth

field
    a Buddha immediately present

intentional object
    Buddha as ālambana

cognitive mode
    cintāmaya — reflection-produced
```

These are not merely external circumstances. Together they determine the species of karma being produced.

The doctrinally most significant relation is:

```text
present Buddha
    ↓ serves as intentional field
reflective Bodhisattva volition
    ↓ produces
mark-producing karma
    ↓ matures as
future Buddha-form
```

The future Buddha-body is therefore genetically mediated through repeated practical relation to actual Buddhas.

The temporal point is equally important. A standard hundred-kalpa accumulation can be shortened by intensified vigor. Thus karmic genesis is ordered but not mechanically uniform:

```text
fixed end
    ≠ fixed rate of acquisition
```

Śākyamuni’s ninety-one-kalpa case shows that the causal sequence is determinate while its duration remains responsive to the force of practice.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The existing Kārikā translation should be tightened in two places.

First, `buddhacetanaḥ` should not be rendered simply as “with volition directed toward Buddhahood.” The Bhāṣya explicitly gives `buddhālambanayaiva cetanayā`: **with volition taking a Buddha as its object**.

Second, `cintāmaya` should retain its technical contrast with `śrutamaya` and `bhāvanāmaya`. “Formed through reflection” is preferable to a vague “deliberate” or “conscious” activity.

Recommended translation:

> **Only in Jambūdvīpa, as a male, face-to-face with the Teacher, and with volition taking a Buddha as its object, does he produce that reflection-born karma; over one hundred kalpas he projects the remaining accumulation.**

The Bhāṣya further records the exceptional Śākyamuni case of ninety-one kalpas.

## Light Organon Contact

The mark-karma gives an unusually concrete model of practical genesis. The future determination does not arise by simply representing oneself as a future Buddha. The Bodhisattva encounters an actual Buddha as object-support, reflects upon that field, and through repeated volitional activity produces the causal form that will later appear as Buddha-form.

The structure is therefore not merely:

```text
idea of Buddha
    → imitation
```

but:

```text
actual Buddha as field
    → reflective practical determination
    → accumulated karma
    → future Buddha-form
```

The end is generated through mediation. It is already operative as a determinate object of practical reason, yet it becomes actual only through an immense history of conditioned activity.
