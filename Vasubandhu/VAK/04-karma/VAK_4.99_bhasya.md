# VAK 4.99 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> tadavadyaṃ mṛṣāvādas tena bhettā samanvitaḥ |  
> avīcau pacyate kalpam adhikair adhikā rujaḥ || 4.99 ||

**Best current translation:** The culpable karma associated with that schism is false speech, and the divider is endowed with it. He undergoes maturation in Avīci for an intermediate kalpa; with additional ānantarya karmas, the suffering is correspondingly greater.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.99.md](./VAK_4.99.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

**Reference:** Pradhan [260|22–261|07]

sa hi bhūyobhirānantaryaistasminnavīcau ghanataraṃ sukumārataraṃ cāśrayaṃ labhate kāraṇāśca bahutarāstīvratarāśca yena dvitricatuṣpañcaguṇāṃ vedanāṃ vedayate /

**Source caution:** the e-text contains minor orthographic irregularities (`sadavadya...`, `kalpan`, `adhhikair`). They are preserved here as source readings rather than silently normalized.

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya develops 4.99 in three steps:

1. If the schism belongs to the divided Saṅgha rather than the divider, what does the divider possess?
2. What exactly is the culpable karma of schism?
3. How does that karma mature, and what happens if several ānantarya karmas have been committed?

The entire argument depends on the distinction established in 4.98 between the **bearer of the schism** and the **bearer of the culpable karma that produces it**.

## Literal Prose Translation

> The division belongs to that which has been divided, not to the divider. Then with what is the divider endowed? The divider is endowed with the culpable false speech associated with it. The divider is endowed with the culpability belonging to Saṅgha-division, and that is false speech. More precisely, it is the verbal manifestation and non-manifestation that arise together with the Saṅgha-division. The divider is endowed with that culpable karma.
>
> He undergoes maturation in Avīci for a kalpa. He ripens in the great Avīci hell for an intermediate kalpa. With the other ānantarya karmas, however, maturation is not necessarily in Avīci.
>
> But if someone has committed several ānantarya karmas, do they all ripen immediately one after another? What then occurs through them? With additional ānantarya karmas, there is additional suffering. Through a greater number of ānantarya karmas, in that same Avīci he obtains a denser and more sensitive support, and the causes of torment are more numerous and more intense, so that he experiences feeling twofold, threefold, fourfold, or fivefold.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The divider does not possess the schism itself

VAK 4.98 established:

```text
Saṅgha
    → bears saṃghabheda / asāmagrī

divider
    → does not bear that collective state
```

VAK 4.99 therefore asks what the divider actually possesses.

### 2. The divider possesses the culpable false speech

The answer is:

```text
saṃghabheda-avadya
    = mṛṣāvāda
```

The Bhāṣya further specifies this as the **verbal manifestation and non-manifestation** (`vāgvijñapti` and `avijñapti`) co-arising with the schism.

Thus the doctrinal circuit is:

```text
false speech
    → culpable verbal karma of the divider
    → Saṅgha non-concord as collective result
```

The unwholesome cause and the indeterminate collective state remain categorically distinct.

### 3. Avīci maturation is specific and measured

The Kārikā says `kalpam`; the Bhāṣya glosses this as:

```text
antarakalpam
    one intermediate kalpa
```

The special claim is that the karma of Saṅgha schism matures in the great Avīci hell for that duration. Vasubandhu explicitly adds that the other ānantarya karmas do **not necessarily** mature in Avīci.

This is a substantive precision for the Kārikā translation.

### 4. Several ānantarya karmas intensify one maturation

An objection arises naturally: if ānantarya means non-intervening maturation, what happens when several such karmas coexist?

The reply is not a sequence of separate intervening rebirths. Rather:

```text
more ānantarya karmas
    → more intense resultant basis
    → more numerous and stronger tormenting causes
    → multiplied painful feeling
```

The Bhāṣya gives a graded scale of two-, three-, four-, and fivefold feeling.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — carries forward the distinction from 4.98 and asks what the divider possesses.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — identifies the divider's culpable factor as false speech and specifies its verbal manifestation/non-manifestation structure.
- **Voice: Objector** — asks how several ānantarya karmas can all ripen immediately.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — resolves the problem through intensification of one Avīci maturation rather than a sequence of separate immediate rebirths.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃghabhedāvadyam** — the culpable karma associated with Saṅgha division.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā compound.
  - **function:** identifies the divider's own karmic determination.

- **saṃghabhedasahaja** — co-arising with the Saṅgha division.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here.
  - **function:** links the culpable verbal karma temporally and causally to the produced collective state.

- **antarakalpa** — intermediate kalpa.
  - **status:** introduced by the Bhāṣya to gloss `kalpam`.
  - **function:** specifies the duration of Avīci maturation.

- **ghanatara āśraya** — denser support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only consequence language.
  - **function:** explains intensified maturation under multiple ānantarya karmas.

- **sukumāratara āśraya** — more sensitive/delicate support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only consequence language.
  - **function:** explains increased susceptibility to painful feeling.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.99 completes the causal distribution begun in 4.98:

```text
divider
    bears culpable mṛṣāvāda

Saṅgha
    bears asāmagrī / saṃghabheda

later continuum of divider
    bears vipāka in Avīci
```

The three determinations must not be collapsed:

```text
cause
    ≠ collective resultant state
    ≠ later maturation
```

The verse also shows that multiplicity of grave causes need not entail multiplicity of rebirth-results. Several ānantarya karmas can converge by intensifying one resultant field.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

Two refinements are required:

1. `kalpam` should be rendered here as **an intermediate kalpa**, following the Bhāṣya's explicit `antarakalpam`.
2. `adhikair adhikā rujaḥ` refers specifically to **additional ānantarya karmas producing greater suffering**, not merely unspecified aggravating circumstances.

Revised philosophical translation:

> The culpable karma of that schism is false speech, and the divider is endowed with it. He undergoes maturation in Avīci for an intermediate kalpa; when additional ānantarya karmas are present, his suffering there is correspondingly intensified.

## Logical Determination

Let:

```text
A = divider
K = culpable false-speech karma
S = Saṅgha
D = collective division
V = later Avīci maturation
```

Then:

```text
Bears(A, K)
Produces(K, D)
Bears(S, D)
MaturesAs(K, V)
```

and, for several ānantarya karmas `K1 ... Kn`:

```text
K1 ... Kn
    do not require
n successive immediate rebirth-results

rather

K1 ... Kn
    → Intensifies(V)
```

The Bhāṣya thus introduces **convergent causal intensification** rather than simple one-cause/one-result seriality.

## Light Organon Contact

The generated relation is more precise than a generic model of punishment:

```text
representational act
    → objective communal rupture
    → later intensified experiential actuality
```

The same causal event therefore has determinations distributed across different bearers and times. The divider's false representation is not itself the collective rupture, and the rupture is not itself the later painful maturation. The unity lies in the causal mediation among them.
