# VAK 4.90 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**sarve ’tītasya catvāri madhyamasyāpy anāgatāḥ |**  
**madhyamā dve ’jātasya phalāni trīṇy anāgatāḥ || 4.90 ||**

See: [VAK_4.90.md](./VAK_4.90.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
sarve 'tītasya catvāri

sarva iti traiyadhvikāḥ /
atītasya karmaṇastraiyadvikāḥ dharmāścatvāri phalāni /
visaṃyogaphalaṃ hitvā /

madhyamasyāpyanāgatāḥ /

pratyutpannasyāpi karmaṇo 'nāgatā dharmāścatvāri phalānyetānyeva /

madhyamā dve

pratyutpannā dharmāḥ pratyupannasya dve puruṣakārādhipatiphale /

ajātasya phalāni trīṇyanāgatāḥ // VAkK_4.90 //

anāgatasyānāgatāni trīṇi phalāni /
niḥṣyandavisaṃyogaphale hitvā /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya resolves the Kārikā as four temporal relations:

1. **past karma → dharmas of all three times:** four fruits;
2. **present karma → future dharmas:** the same four fruits;
3. **present karma → present dharmas:** two fruits;
4. **future karma → future dharmas:** three fruits.

Thus the verse is not merely assigning one fruit-count to each temporal class of karma. It is indexing the fruit relation by **both source-time and result-time**.

## Literal Prose Translation

> “All” means belonging to the three times. Dharmas belonging to the three times are four fruits of past karma, excluding the fruit of disconnection.
>
> Future dharmas are likewise four fruits of present karma—the same four.
>
> Present dharmas are two fruits of present karma: the activity-produced fruit and the dominant fruit.
>
> For future karma, future dharmas are three fruits, excluding the outflow fruit and the fruit of disconnection.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Past karma

The phrase `sarve 'tītasya` is glossed:

```text
sarva iti traiyadhvikāḥ
```

Here `sarve` means **dharmas of all three times**:

```text
past
present
future
```

For past karma, such dharmas may stand as four fruits:

```text
vipāka
niḥṣyanda
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

The excluded fruit is:

```text
visaṃyoga
```

Thus the first-pass reading “past karma has all five fruits” was too strong. The Bhāṣya gives **four**, while `sarve` modifies the temporal range of the result-dharmas, not the five fruit-types.

### 2. Present karma and future dharmas

For present karma, future dharmas can likewise be those same four fruits:

```text
vipāka
niḥṣyanda
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

Again:

```text
visaṃyoga
    excluded
```

### 3. Present karma and present dharmas

The compressed phrase:

```text
madhyamā dve
```

is resolved by the Bhāṣya as:

```text
pratyutpannā dharmāḥ pratyupannasya dve
puruṣakārādhipatiphale
```

So `madhyamā` means **present dharmas**, not “the middle two fruit-types.”

Present dharmas can be two fruits of present karma:

```text
puruṣakāra-phala
adhipati-phala
```

This is the strongest correction to the provisional Kārikā analysis.

### 4. Future karma and future dharmas

For future karma, future dharmas can be three fruits:

```text
vipāka
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly excludes:

```text
niḥṣyanda
visaṃyoga
```

Hence:

```text
future source + future result
    → 3 admissible fruit-relations
```

## Temporal Matrix

The Bhāṣya gives the following relations explicitly:

| Source karma | Result dharma time | Fruit relations |
|---|---|---|
| Past | Past / Present / Future | vipāka, niḥṣyanda, puruṣakāra, adhipati |
| Present | Future | vipāka, niḥṣyanda, puruṣakāra, adhipati |
| Present | Present | puruṣakāra, adhipati |
| Future | Future | vipāka, puruṣakāra, adhipati |

The verse therefore encodes a temporally indexed causal relation rather than a simple function from karma-time to fruit-count.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, reporting the Abhidharma classificatory determination.

No objection-response sequence appears in this unit; the Bhāṣya is explanatory and distributive.

## New Technical Terms

- **traiyadhvika** — belonging to the three times; **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of `sarve`; **function:** determines the temporal extension of result-dharmas.
- **pratyutpanna** — present; **status:** explicit Bhāṣya technical term; **function:** resolves the Kārikā's `madhyama`.
- **anāgata** — future; **status:** already present in Kārikā; **function:** marks both future source-karma and future result-dharmas.
- **puruṣakāra-phala** — activity-produced result; **status:** previously encountered in five-fruit taxonomy; **function:** one of the two fruit-relations possible between present karma and present dharmas.
- **adhipati-phala** — dominant result; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** second present-to-present fruit relation.

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive result is that fruit-relation is **temporally relational**.

It is not enough to ask:

```text
What time is the karma?
```

One must ask:

```text
What time is the karma?
What time is the candidate result-dharma?
Which fruit-relation is being asserted?
```

Thus:

```text
KarmaTime × ResultTime × FruitType
```

is the minimal structure.

### `sarve` does not mean all five fruits

This is the central textual correction:

```text
sarve
    = traiyadhvikāḥ dharmāḥ
    = dharmas of all three times
```

not:

```text
sarve
    = all five fruit-types
```

Past karma therefore has **four** relevant fruit-relations with dharmas of all three times, not five.

### Present-to-present causation is especially restricted

Only:

```text
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

can relate present karma to present dharmas.

This reflects the restriction imposed by simultaneity: maturation and outflow require a different causal-temporal structure, while disconnection is excluded in this classification.

### Future-to-future relation

Future karma can already stand in three fruit-relations to future dharmas within the Sarvāstivāda temporal system:

```text
vipāka
puruṣakāra
adhipati
```

This should be understood as a doctrinal temporal classification, not imported into a common-sense model in which future entities are simply nonexistent.

## Translation Consequences

A better translation than the first-pass rendering is:

> For past karma, dharmas of all three times are four fruits; future dharmas are likewise four fruits of present karma. Present dharmas are two fruits of present karma. For future karma, future dharmas are three fruits.

Expanded with the Bhāṣya:

> Past karma can have past, present, and future dharmas as four kinds of fruit, excluding disconnection. Present karma can have future dharmas through those same four fruit-relations, while present dharmas can be only its activity-produced and dominant fruits. Future karma can have future dharmas as three fruits, excluding outflow and disconnection.

## Logical Determination

The appropriate relation is ternary:

```text
FruitRelation(
    sourceKarma,
    resultDharma,
    fruitType
)
```

with temporal predicates on both relata:

```text
Time(sourceKarma)
Time(resultDharma)
```

The verse blocks the simpler representation:

```text
KarmaTime → ResultCount
```

because one source-time may support different fruit sets depending on result-time.

In particular:

```text
PresentKarma × PresentDharma
    → {puruṣakāra, adhipati}

PresentKarma × FutureDharma
    → {vipāka, niḥṣyanda, puruṣakāra, adhipati}
```

So temporal distance is an active parameter of causal form.

## Light Organon Contact

This is a clean case where **relation requires indexed context**.

The same source determination cannot be assigned one timeless causal signature. Its available relations depend on the temporal determination of the target.

At a restrained structural level:

```text
cause
    is not fully determined
    until its target relation is specified
```

The Bhāṣya therefore strengthens the emerging Kośa pattern:

```text
universal type
    ≠ complete relational determination
```

Quality, domain, path, and now time all mediate causal applicability.

## First-Pass Correction

The existing Kārikā analysis should later be revised at two points:

1. `sarve 'tītasya` does **not** mean that past karma has all five fruits; it means that dharmas of all three times are four fruits of past karma, excluding `visaṃyoga`.
2. `madhyamā dve` means **present dharmas are two fruits of present karma**, namely `puruṣakāra` and `adhipati`; it does not denote an ambiguous pair of “middle” fruit-types.

These are exactly the sorts of obscurities the later Super Kārikās pass should replace with concise Bhāṣya-derived clarification.
