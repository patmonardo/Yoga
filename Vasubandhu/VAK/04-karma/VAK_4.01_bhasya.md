# VAK_4.01 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
karmajaṃ lokavaicitryaṃ cetanā tatkṛtaṃ ca tat /
cetanā mānasaṃ karma tajjaṃ vākkāyakarmaṇī // VAkK_4.1 //
```

**Working translation:** The differentiation of the world is born from karma. That karma is volition and what is produced by it. Volition is mental action; the action born from it is verbal and bodily action.

### Source cautions

The e-text reads `cetanākṛṭaṃ` at [192|11-192|12]; this is preserved above rather than silently normalized.

The phrase around [192|07-192|08] is compressed and appears textually rough (`tānyevañjātīyāti vyābhiśrakāriṇāṃ`). The doctrinal contrast is nevertheless clear from the continuation: beings whose karmic activity is mixed have disagreeable bodily supports together with agreeable enjoyments, while for gods whose activity is described as unmixed both are agreeable. No silent emendation is imposed here.

## Natural Argument

The chapter begins by explicitly taking up the result established in Lokanirdeśa: the manifold differentiation of both the world of beings (`sattva-loka`) and the receptacle-world (`bhājana-loka`). Vasubandhu asks what produced this differentiation.

His first answer is negative. The world was not made by someone through prior intelligence or deliberate design (`buddhi-pūrvaka`). He then gives the positive answer: the differentiation of the world is born from the karmas of beings.

An objection immediately tests the claim. If the world is karma-born, why do beings sometimes obtain beautiful enjoyments — saffron, sandalwood, and the like — while their own bodies are not correspondingly beautiful?

The reply preserves the karmic thesis by distinguishing mixed from unmixed activity. In beings whose karmic activity is mixed, the bodily support may arise as something wound-like or disagreeable while the enjoyments arise as attractive countermeasures to it. In gods, whose activity is characterized here as unmixed, both body and enjoyments are attractive.

The discussion then asks what karma itself is. Vasubandhu cites the sūtra distinction between two karmas:

```text
cetanā karma
    volition as karma

cetayitvā karma
    karma after having willed
```

The Kārikā expresses the same distinction as:

```text
cetanā
    + tat-kṛta
```

These two forms of karma are then articulated into the familiar threefold classification:

```text
kāya-karma
vāk-karma
manas-karma
```

But Vasubandhu does not allow this threefold division to remain a flat taxonomy. He asks what principle determines the classification: support (`āśraya`), intrinsic nature (`svabhāva`), or origination (`samutthāna`)? Each criterion taken universally would collapse all three karmas into only one class:

```text
by support
    all would be bodily karma
    because all depend upon the body

by intrinsic nature
    all would be verbal karma
    because speech itself is karmic in nature

by origination
    all would be mental karma
    because all arise from mind
```

The Vaibhāṣika solution is distributive rather than exclusive: the three karmas are classified respectively by these three different reasons.

The verse then fixes the decisive internal relation:

```text
cetanā
    = mental karma

cetanā-janita / cetayitvā karma
    = bodily and verbal karma
```

Thus bodily and verbal action are not merely parallel members beside mental action. They are generated from volition.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — opens the chapter by asking for the producer of the differentiated world, rejects deliberate world-construction, introduces the definition of karma, cites the sūtra, and develops the classification problem.

**Voice: Objector** — `yadi karmajaṃ kasmāt...`: if the world is born from karma, why are beings' enjoyments sometimes more attractive than their bodies?

**Voice: Vasubandhu / doctrinal reply** — answers through the contrast between mixed and unmixed karmic activity.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — explicitly attributed at [192|16]: the three karmas are classified respectively through three different grounds — support, intrinsic nature, and origination.

## New Technical Terms

- **sattva-bhājana-loka** — world of beings and receptacle-world
  - **status:** the two poles developed throughout Chapter 3 are now named together in the Bhāṣya opening
  - **function:** identifies the total differentiated field whose ground Chapter 4 now seeks

- **buddhi-pūrvaka** — preceded by intelligence; deliberately designed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this opening argument
  - **function:** negative determination; excludes intentional world-construction by a designing maker

- **vyāmiśra-kārin** — one whose activity is mixed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** explains divergence between the quality of bodily support and the quality of enjoyments

- **avyāmiśra-kārin** — one whose activity is unmixed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** accounts here for the correspondence of agreeable embodiment and agreeable enjoyments among gods

- **cetayitvā karma** — karma after having willed
  - **status:** sūtra terminology cited by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** scriptural counterpart to `tat-kṛta`; action produced from volition

- **āśraya** — support
  - **status:** previously encountered; newly operative as a classificatory criterion
  - **function:** one possible ground for distinguishing bodily, verbal, and mental karma

- **svabhāva** — intrinsic nature
  - **status:** previously encountered; newly operative as a classificatory criterion
  - **function:** second possible ground of karmic classification

- **samutthāna** — origination / arising source
  - **status:** important Chapter 4 technical term introduced here in the prose before its later systematic treatment
  - **function:** third possible ground of karmic classification; all three karmas arise from mind, so this criterion cannot universally distinguish them

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes the transition from cosmology to karma fully explicit:

```text
Chapter 3
    sattva-loka + bhājana-loka
    manifold differentiated world

Chapter 4
    asks for its producing ground

answer
    karma of beings
```

The negative clause is doctrinally important:

```text
na khalu kenacid buddhi-pūrvakaṃ kṛtam
```

The world's differentiation is not explained as the product of a deliberating world-maker. The positive explanation is immanent to conditioned existence: beings' karmas generate differentiation.

The objection concerning beautiful enjoyments and unattractive bodies also shows that karma is not treated as a simplistic one-action/one-visible-result mechanism. Different aspects of lived existence can reflect mixed karmic determinations.

The definition of karma is then grounded in a sūtra distinction:

```text
dve karmaṇī

1. cetanā karma
2. cetayitvā karma
```

The Kārikā condenses this as:

```text
karma
    = cetanā
    + cetanā-kṛta
```

The three modes of action are consequently internally ordered:

```text
mental karma
    cetanā

bodily + verbal karma
    generated by cetanā
```

The Bhāṣya therefore confirms the asymmetry already visible in the Kārikā: mental, verbal, and bodily karma are not simply three coordinate species.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**No correction required, but the Bhāṣya substantially sharpens the translation.**

The existing rendering of `tat-kṛta` as "what is produced by it" is confirmed by the Bhāṣya's equation with both `cetayitvā karma` and `cetanā-janita`.

The strongest clarified rendering remains:

> The differentiation of the world is born from karma. That karma is volition and what is produced by it. Volition is mental action; the action born from it is verbal and bodily action.

The Bhāṣya shows that "born from it" should be heard genetically, not merely classificatorily.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya opens Chapter 4 with a movement from produced actuality back to its immanent determining activity:

```text
manifold world
    ↓ question of ground

not deliberate external construction
    ↓

karma of beings
    ↓

cetanā
    + action generated from cetanā
```

This gives two distinct orders.

The explanatory order runs backward:

```text
world-differentiation
    ← karma
    ← volitional determination
```

The genetic order runs forward:

```text
cetanā
    → bodily/verbal activity
    → karmic efficacy
    → differentiated world
```

The classification discussion then introduces a second logical problem: a determination cannot be classified by an undifferentiated criterion that applies equally to every member. If support alone were decisive, everything would collapse into bodily karma; if origination alone were decisive, everything would collapse into mental karma.

The Vaibhāṣika answer uses differentiated grounds for differentiated members:

```text
one articulated field
    requires distinct principles of classification
```

This is already characteristic of the Bhāṣya as Reflection: the terse Kārikā gives the determinations, while the prose tests the grounds under which those determinations can actually be maintained.

## Organon Contact Point

The first Bhāṣya passage strengthens the working hypothesis that Karmanirdeśa studies **determining activity as the ground of produced Loka**.

The crucial point, however, is that the ground is not placed outside the field as a designing intelligence. Vasubandhu explicitly refuses that move. The differentiated world is referred back to the activity of the beings within conditioned existence itself.

So the opening structure is not:

```text
external intelligence
    → designed world
```

but:

```text
conditioned beings
    → karmic determination
    → differentiated conditioned world
```

This remains firmly within the Kośa's Practical field. The chapter is beginning to explain how volitional determination becomes effective actuality, not to introduce a metaphysical creator-principle.

A second important contact point appears in the classification argument. The distinction among bodily, verbal, and mental action survives only when the principle of determination is itself differentiated. Reflection asks not merely *what are the three?* but *under what ground can these three genuinely be distinguished?*
