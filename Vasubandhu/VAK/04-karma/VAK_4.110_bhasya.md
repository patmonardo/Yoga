# VAK_4.110_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.110**

**ekaikaṃ puṇyaśatajam asaṃkhyeyatrayāntyajāḥ |**  
**vipaśyī dīpakṛd ratnaśikhī śākyamuniḥ purā || 4.110 ||**

> Each individual mark is generated by a hundred merits. The Buddhas standing at the ends of the three incalculable periods are Vipaśyin, Dīpaṃkara, and Ratnaśikhin; before them was Śākyamuni.

See: `VAK_4.110.md`.

## Natural Segmentation

1. Each Buddha-mark is produced from a hundred merits.
2. The Bhāṣya asks what quantity of merit one such unit represents.
3. Three different traditional estimates are reported, ending in an explicit refusal to reduce the measure to a securely knowable worldly quantity.
4. Vasubandhu then gives the immense number of Buddhas served during each of the three incalculable periods.
5. Ratnaśikhin, Dīpaṃkara, and Vipaśyin mark the ends of the first, second, and third incalculables respectively.
6. Before these stands an earlier Buddha named Śākyamuni, before whom the Bodhisattva who would become the present Śākyamuni first made his vow for Buddhahood.

## Literal Prose Translation

Concerning those marks, each individual one is born from a hundred merits.

What is the measure of one merit? Some say: leaving aside the Bodhisattva who is near to awakening, it is equal to the fruition of enjoyment belonging to all sentient beings. Others say: it is equal to the karmic predominance by which the entire three-thousand-great-thousand world-system is produced. Others say that only Buddhas know its measure.

How many Buddhas did the Blessed One serve while he was a Bodhisattva? In the first incalculable kalpa, seventy-five thousand; in the second, seventy-six thousand; in the third, seventy-seven thousand.

Which Buddha stood at the end of each incalculable period? In reverse order relative to the verse: the first incalculable ended with the perfectly awakened Ratnaśikhin, the second with the Blessed Dīpaṃkara, and the third with the Tathāgata Vipaśyin.

Before all these there was a perfectly awakened Buddha named Śākyamuni. In his presence the Blessed One, while still a Bodhisattva, made his first vow: “May I become a Buddha of precisely this kind.”

## Argument Reconstruction

The first half of the verse looks quantitative, but the Bhāṣya deliberately prevents the number from becoming trivial arithmetic.

```text
1 Buddha-mark
    = 100 puṇya-units
```

But when asked what one `puṇya` amounts to, the tradition gives increasingly expansive answers:

```text
all beings' enjoyment-result
        ↓
world-producing karmic predominance
        ↓
only Buddhas know the true measure
```

The point is therefore not that Buddha-form is cheaply reducible to a calculable score. The “hundred” expresses an ordered accumulation of extraordinary karmic magnitude whose exact measure exceeds ordinary cognition.

The second half places that accumulation inside a still larger temporal architecture:

```text
first asaṃkhyeya  → Ratnaśikhin
second asaṃkhyeya → Dīpaṃkara
third asaṃkhyeya  → Vipaśyin
```

And prior to these three termini lies an earlier Buddha Śākyamuni, before whom the Bodhisattva first makes the explicit vow for Buddhahood.

Thus the trajectory is:

```text
first vow
    ↓
three incalculable periods of cultivation
    ↓
hundred-kalpa mark-producing phase
    ↓
manifest Buddha-form
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — framing questions, organizing the chronology, and transmitting the account.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika / earlier scholastic traditions** — the three proposed measurements of a unit of merit.

The Bhāṣya does not settle the first two calculations as Vasubandhu's own final arithmetic. The third report, that only Buddhas know the true measure, functions as an explicit limit on ordinary quantification.

## New Technical Terms

- **puṇyaśataja** — produced from a hundred merits.
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās.
  - **function:** quantitative characterization of the causal production of each Buddha-mark.

- **puṇyaparimāṇa** — measure of merit.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** raises the problem of whether Buddha-producing merit can be numerically measured.

- **karmādhipatya** — karmic predominance or governing efficacy.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains one proposed measure of merit through the karma capable of producing an entire world-system.

- **kalpāsaṃkhyeya** — incalculable kalpic period.
  - **status:** established Buddhist cosmological term.
  - **function:** measures the macro-temporal architecture of the Bodhisattva career.

- **praṇidhāna** — vow or resolve.
  - **status:** important Bodhisattva-career term.
  - **function:** marks the explicit beginning of the directed project of Buddhahood.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

### 1. Quantification is real but not reductive

The verse says each mark is produced from a hundred merits, but the Bhāṣya immediately asks what one such merit is. Its answer destabilizes any naive numerical reading.

The hundred is therefore an internally ordered causal multiplicity, not a simple tally of ordinary good deeds.

### 2. Buddha-form is accumulated karma made manifest

The marks are not arbitrary signs attached to an already complete Buddha. Each mark has a distinct causal history.

```text
multiplicity of merit
    → integrated karmic disposition
    → singular bodily mark
```

A qualitative feature of Buddha-form is the maturation of a quantitatively immense causal history.

### 3. The Bodhisattva career has nested temporal scales

The hundred-kalpa production of marks discussed in 4.109 belongs within a much larger career of three incalculable periods. These are not competing chronologies but nested stages.

### 4. The vow precedes the completed accumulation

The earlier Buddha Śākyamuni functions as the field before whom the Bodhisattva makes the first explicit `praṇidhāna`. The future Buddha therefore first exists as a consciously adopted end before appearing as completed form.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation is broadly sound, but the chronology should be made explicit rather than saying only that the career is “articulated by” the three Buddhas.

Recommended translation:

> **Each individual mark is born from a hundred merits. Ratnaśikhin, Dīpaṃkara, and Vipaśyin stand respectively at the ends of the three incalculable periods; before them was a Buddha named Śākyamuni.**

Because the verse lists Vipaśyin, Dīpaṃkara, Ratnaśikhin in reverse order, the Bhāṣya is essential for reconstructing the actual first-second-third chronology.

## Light Organon Contact

The passage gives a particularly clean example of **quantity passing over into qualitative form**.

```text
100 merits
    → one mark

many incalculable acts
    → one determinate Buddha-career
```

Yet the Bhāṣya also places a limit on external calculation: the causal magnitude of the Buddha-form cannot simply be reconstructed from ordinary worldly units. The quantitative determination is real, but its adequate measure belongs only to a cognition proportionate to the whole causal field.

The first vow adds the practical side:

```text
Buddha as represented end
    → sustained karmic production
    → Buddha as actual form
```

The end is not merely predicted; it becomes genetically active through the Bodhisattva's own continuing activity.
