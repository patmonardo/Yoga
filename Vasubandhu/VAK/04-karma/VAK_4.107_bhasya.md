# VAK 4.107 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `ānantaryasabhāgāni pañcamaṃ stūpabhedanam | kṣāntyanāgāmitārhattvaprāptau karmātivighnakṛt || 4.107 ||`

**Best current translation:** These are analogues of the immediate-consequence karmas; destruction of a stūpa is the fifth. Karma becomes an extreme obstruction at the attainment of kṣānti, non-returnership, and arhatship.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.107.md](./VAK_4.107.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
kimānantaryairevāvaśyaṃ narakeṣūpapadyate /
ānantaryasabhāgairapyavaśyamupapadyate /

natvanantaramevetyapare /
katamāni tānītyāha

dūṣaṇaṃ māturarhantyā niyatisthasya māraṇam /
bodhisattvasya śaikṣasya saṃghāyadvārahārikā // VAkK_4.106 //

ānantaryasabhāgāni pañcamaṃ stūpabhedanam /

etāni pañca pañcānāmānatnaryāṇāṃ yathākramam sabhāgāni /

yadi mātaramarhantīṃ dūṣayatyabrahmacaryakaraṇāt niyatipatītaṃ bodhisattvaṃ mārayati śaikṣaṃ mārayati saṃghasya sukhāyadvārikaṃ harati stūpabhedaṃ karoti /

anyadapi tu karma savipākaṃ triṣu kāleṣvatyarthaṃ vighnayati /

katameṣu trisu /

kṣāntyanāgāmitārhattvaprāptau karmātivighnakṛt // VAkK_4.107 //

mūrdhabhyaḥ kṣāntimākirata āpāyikāni karmāṇi vighnāyopatiṣṭhante /

tadvipākabhūmyatikramāt /

yathā puruṣasya deśatyāgaṃ kurvato dhanikā upatiṣṭhante /

anāgāmiphalaṃ prāpnuvataḥ kāmāvacarāṇi vighnāyopatiṣṭhante tathaiva sthāpayitvā dṛṣṭadharmavedanīyaṃ karma /

arhattvaṃ prāpnuvato rūpārūpyāvacarāṇi tathaiva /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The passage contains two connected but distinct arguments:

1. completion of the five `ānantarya-sabhāga` acts by naming destruction of a stūpa as the fifth;
2. a broader doctrine of karmic obstruction at three decisive transitions of the Path.

The second argument is not limited to the five analogues. Vasubandhu explicitly broadens the scope: `anyad api tu karma savipākam` — other karma possessing maturation can also become an especially strong obstruction at these three moments.

## Literal Prose Translation

> Is it only through the immediate-consequence karmas that one necessarily takes birth in the hells? One also necessarily takes birth there through acts analogous to the immediate-consequence karmas. According to others, however, not necessarily immediately in the next existence.
>
> What are these acts? The preceding four — sexual violation of one’s mother when she is an arhat, killing a bodhisattva established in certainty, killing a trainee, and taking away the Saṅgha’s means of comfort — are analogues of the immediate-consequence karmas; destruction of a stūpa is the fifth. These five correspond in order to the five immediate-consequence karmas.
>
> Other karma possessing maturation also becomes an extreme obstruction at three times. At which three? At the attainment of kṣānti, non-returnership, and arhatship.
>
> When one proceeds from the `mūrdhan` stage toward kṣānti, karmas whose maturation belongs to the bad destinies arise as obstacles, because one is about to pass beyond the realm in which their maturation could occur. It is like creditors confronting a man who is about to leave the country.
>
> When one is about to attain the fruit of non-returning, desire-realm karmas similarly arise as obstacles, except for karma to be experienced in this very life. When one is about to attain arhatship, form-realm and formless-realm karmas similarly arise as obstacles.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The fifth ānantarya analogue

The list is completed by:

```text
stūpabhedana
    destruction of a stūpa
```

Together with the four acts of 4.106, these five are said to be `sabhāga` — structurally analogous — to the five strict ānantarya karmas.

The distinction remains important:

```text
ānantarya
    → strict technical identity
    → result without intervening rebirth

ānantarya-sabhāga
    → analogous grave structure
    → hell rebirth certain
    → according to some, not necessarily immediately next
```

### 2. The argument broadens beyond the five analogues

Vasubandhu then says:

```text
anyad api tu karma savipākam
other karma possessing maturation also ...
```

Thus `karmātivighnakṛt` should not be restricted to the five `ānantarya-sabhāga` acts. The doctrine concerns karmas whose maturation-realm is about to become inaccessible through spiritual advancement.

### 3. First threshold: kṣānti

When the practitioner advances from `mūrdhan` toward `kṣānti`, karmas destined to mature in the bad destinies become especially obstructive.

Reason:

```text
kṣānti attained
    → bad-destiny maturation-domain passed beyond
```

The karmic result therefore presses for realization before its possible field disappears.

### 4. Second threshold: non-returnership

At attainment of `anāgāmiphala`, desire-realm karmas become obstructive, because non-returning marks departure from future rebirth in the desire realm.

Exception:

```text
dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya karma
    karma to be experienced in this very life
```

This karma does not require a later desire-realm birth and therefore need not obstruct the transition.

### 5. Third threshold: arhatship

At attainment of arhatship, form- and formless-realm karmas become obstructive, because arhatship terminates further rebirth even in those higher realms.

The three thresholds therefore form a progressive exhaustion of possible karmic maturation-domains.

## The Creditor Analogy

Vasubandhu gives a compact simile:

```text
debtor prepares to leave the country
    → creditors appear and stop him

practitioner prepares to leave a maturation-domain
    → karma belonging to that domain becomes obstructive
```

This is more precise than saying that past karma simply “punishes” the practitioner. The problem is one of unresolved causal claims whose field of possible maturation is about to disappear.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — states that ānantarya analogues also certainly lead to hell.
- **Voice: reported others (`apare`)** — deny that the hell rebirth from these analogues must necessarily be the immediately following birth.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu / doctrinal exposition** — defines the three thresholds at which mature karmas become exceptionally obstructive and explains each by transcendence of its maturation-domain.

## New Technical Terms

- **ānantarya-sabhāga** — act analogous in structure and gravity to an immediate-consequence karma without strict technical identity.
  - **status:** introduced across 4.106–4.107.
  - **function:** graded karmic classification.

- **stūpabhedana** — destruction of a stūpa.
  - **status:** new in this passage.
  - **function:** fifth ānantarya analogue.

- **ati-vighnakṛt** — producing an extreme obstruction.
  - **status:** Kārikā term clarified by Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** describes karma becoming especially obstructive at a threshold of Path-transition.

- **āpāyika-karma** — karma whose maturation belongs to the bad destinies.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** obstruction at the transition to kṣānti.

- **dṛṣṭadharmavedanīya-karma** — karma to be experienced in this very life.
  - **status:** previously encountered karmic category.
  - **function:** explicit exception at the transition to non-returnership.

- **vipākabhūmi** — domain or level in which a karma can mature.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term.
  - **function:** supplies the causal principle behind the three obstructions.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a much more exact doctrine than the first-pass formula “grave karma blocks attainment.” The obstruction is indexed to the realm of maturation that the practitioner is about to transcend:

```text
toward kṣānti
    bad-destiny karma obstructs

toward non-returning
    desire-realm karma obstructs
    except present-life karma

toward arhatship
    form- and formless-realm karma obstructs
```

The operative principle is:

```text
spiritual transition
    → closes a future maturation-domain
    → unresolved karma belonging to that domain becomes pressing
```

## Logical Determination

Let:

```text
K = karma with maturation-domain D
T = Path-transition that permanently transcends D
```

Then immediately before T:

```text
PossibleMaturation(K, D)
+
Transcends(T, D)
    →
StrongObstruction(K, T)
```

The three cases instantiate the same relation at progressively higher domains.

This gives karma the form of an outstanding causal claim constrained by the remaining space of possible realization.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first half requires no major correction, but the second half should not suggest that only the ānantarya analogues obstruct these attainments.

A more exact translation is:

> These are analogues of the immediate-consequence karmas; destruction of a stūpa is the fifth. Karma whose maturation would otherwise remain outstanding becomes especially obstructive at the attainment of kṣānti, non-returnership, and arhatship.

The Bhāṣya supplies the three different classes of obstructing karma and the reason for their appearance.

## Light Organon Contact

The creditor analogy gives the strongest contact point without imposing an external system:

```text
karma
    = unresolved conditioned claim

Path-transition
    = transcendence of the domain in which that claim could be realized
```

Thus advancement does not merely add a higher state. It progressively removes whole domains of possible becoming.

The three thresholds are therefore irreversible contractions of the space in which conditioned karmic possibilities can still become actual.
