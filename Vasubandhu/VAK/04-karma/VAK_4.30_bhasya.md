# VAK_4.30_bhasya — Upavāsa, Refuge, and Acquisition of Upāsaka Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.30**

```iast
anyasyāpy upavāso 'sti śaraṇaṃ tv agatasya na |
upāsakatvopagamāt saṃvṛt uktis tu bhikṣuvat ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> Upavāsa restraint may also arise for one who was not previously an upavāsaka, but not for one who has not gone for refuge. According to the Kāśmīra position, lay restraint arises from undertaking upāsaka status; the subsequent statement of the training rules serves instruction, as in the case of a monk.

Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.30.md`.

## Source Cautions

The e-text reads `upāvāsako` in the quoted sūtra at [215|01-215|04]; the exact transmitted form is preserved.

The Kārikā source has the compressed expression `saṃvṛt`, while the Bhāṣya unpacks it explicitly as `upāsakasaṃvaro jāyate`: the lay restraint arises from undertaking upāsaka status. This materially clarifies the first-pass reading.

The phrase `yāvajjīvaṃ prāṇāpetam` is itself glossed by Vasubandhu as elliptical: `prāṇātipātādyapetam`, “free from killing and the other [transgressions],” through omission of the middle member of the compound.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first asks whether the upavāsa restraint belongs only to someone already established as an `upavāsaka`, or whether another person may acquire it.

The answer is that someone who was not previously an upavāsaka may acquire the day-and-night restraint, provided that person first goes for refuge to Buddha, Dharma, and Saṃgha and then undertakes the upavāsa. Without this, the restraint does not arise, except in a case of ignorance (`ajñāna`) that Vasubandhu leaves as a qualification rather than developing here.

A sūtra is then cited in which a white-clothed householder goes for refuge to the Three Jewels and declares, “Hold me as an upavāsaka.” This raises a new question: does refuge by itself make one an upavāsaka?

Here the schools divide.

**Bahirdeśakas:** yes. Going for refuge is sufficient.

**Kāśmīras:** no. There is no upāsaka without restraint.

The Kāśmīra reply explains the sūtra by taking the declaration `upāsakaṃ māṃ dhāraya` as an undertaking of upāsaka status. From that undertaking itself, lay restraint (`upāsakasaṃvara`) arises.

The later explicit statement of training rules does not therefore generate the restraint for the first time. It serves `vyutpādana`, instruction or clarification of the already acquired discipline. Vasubandhu compares this with monks and novices, who may already have acquired restraint and are nevertheless subsequently taught the training rules in order to understand what the restraint requires.

Thus the passage separates:

```text
acquisition of practical status
    from
instruction in the articulated rules belonging to that status
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks whether upavāsa can arise for someone not already an upavāsaka and explains the refuge requirement.

**Voice: Sūtra** — quoted formula defining the householder who goes for refuge and asks to be regarded as an upavāsaka.

**Voice: Bahirdeśaka position** — refuge alone suffices for upāsaka status.

**Voice: Kāśmīra position** — there is no upāsaka without restraint; the restraint arises through undertaking upāsaka status.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Kāśmīra explanatory line** — the later recitation of training rules serves instruction, analogous to monastic discipline. The exact boundary between Vasubandhu's own endorsement and presentation of the Kāśmīra explanation should remain cautious.

## New Technical Terms

- **anupavāsaka** — one not previously established as an upavāsaka; **status:** new in this local discussion; **function:** tests whether prior observance-status is required for acquisition.
- **śaraṇagamana** — going for refuge; **status:** familiar Buddhist term but newly operative here; **function:** prerequisite orientation for upavāsa acquisition.
- **upāsakatvābhyupagama** — undertaking / accepting the status of an upāsaka; **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the compressed Kārikā; **function:** proposed acquisition-ground of lay restraint.
- **upāsakasaṃvara** — lay restraint; **status:** explicit prose determination; **function:** identifies what arises from undertaking upāsaka status.
- **vyutpādana** — instruction, clarification, making someone understand; **status:** new here; **function:** explains why training rules may be stated after restraint has already been acquired.
- **bahirdeśaka** — teachers of the outer regions; **status:** named doctrinal group; **function:** attributes the view that refuge alone establishes upāsaka status.
- **kāśmīra** — Kāśmīra Abhidharmikas; **status:** recurring doctrinal group; **function:** attributes the position that upāsaka status requires restraint.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Upavāsa eligibility

```text
not previously an upavāsaka
    + refuge in Buddha, Dharma, Saṃgha
    + undertaking upavāsa
        ↓
upavāsasaṃvara arises
```

Prior possession of temporary observance-status is therefore unnecessary.

### Refuge and upāsaka status

The Bhāṣya records a doctrinal disagreement:

```text
Bahirdeśaka:
    refuge
        → upāsaka status

Kāśmīra:
    refuge alone is insufficient
    upāsakatvābhyupagama
        → upāsakasaṃvara
        → upāsaka status
```

The Kāśmīra account therefore distinguishes religious orientation from determinate disciplinary acquisition.

### Acquisition versus instruction

```text
undertaking upāsaka status
        ↓
restraint acquired

later statement of śikṣāpadas
        ↓
vyutpādana
instruction in what the restraint entails
```

The same pattern is said to occur with monks and novices.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass translation made `saṃvṛt uktiḥ` sound like a verbal declaration that itself creates restraint. The Bhāṣya gives a more precise sequence.

A better translation is:

> Upavāsa restraint may also arise for one who was not previously an upavāsaka, but not for one who has not gone for refuge. From undertaking upāsaka status the lay restraint arises; the subsequent statement of the training rules is for instruction, as in the case of a monk.

The final `bhikṣuvat` does not primarily establish an analogy of social status. It explains why explicit rules may be taught after restraint has already been obtained.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.30 distinguishes three moments that the Kārikā pass had partially compressed:

```text
orientation
    śaraṇagamana

undertaking / acquisition
    upāsakatvābhyupagama
        → saṃvara

explication
    śikṣāpada-ukti
        → vyutpādana
```

The relation is not:

```text
rule recitation
    → first production of restraint
```

but rather:

```text
undertaking
    → status-bearing restraint
    → subsequent articulation of its determinate content
```

This also explains the doctrinal dispute. The Bahirdeśaka view lets orientation itself establish the status; the Kāśmīra view requires a further practical determination, restraint.

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is the distinction between **genesis of a practical determination** and **subsequent explicit knowledge of its determinate rules**.

```text
practical form may be acquired
    before
its content has been fully explicated to the bearer
```

The Bhāṣya therefore prevents us from reducing practical status either to private orientation or to mere verbal enumeration of rules. A mediating act of undertaking establishes the restraint; instruction subsequently makes its content explicit.
