# VAK_4.49_bhasya — Five Modes of Vedanīyatā

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.49**


> The condition of being “to be experienced” is fivefold: by intrinsic nature, by association, by object-support, by maturation, and by becoming immediately present.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya begins with a semantic problem generated by the preceding verses:

```text
karma is not itself vedanā

so how can karma be called
sukha-vedanīya / duḥkha-vedanīya / aduḥkhāsukha-vedanīya?
```

Vasubandhu's first answer is explicitly relational. Karma may be called `sukhavedanīya` because it is conducive to pleasant feeling, or because pleasure is what is to be experienced as its maturation. He asks what exactly is `vedanīya` and answers: **the maturation (`vipāka`)**.

Thus the expression does not predicate the intrinsic nature of feeling directly of karma. It predicates karma through the kind of maturation it generates.

VAK 4.49 then generalizes the point. The same language of `vedanīyatā` is licensed in five different ways:

```text
1. svabhāva
   feeling itself is vedanīya by being feeling

2. saṃprayoga
   contact is vedanīya through association with feeling

3. ālambana
   an object is vedanīya as the object-support of feeling

4. vipāka
   karma is vedanīya through the maturation it produces

5. saṃmukhībhāva
   a feeling is said to be experienced through its present manifestation
```

The fifth is especially subtle. When pleasant feeling is presently occurring, the other two feelings are absent. One does not literally experience a second feeling over and above the pleasant feeling itself. Rather, by **making that feeling present (`saṃmukhīkurvan`)**, one is said to experience it.

The conclusion returns to karma: because the maturation generated by karma is `vedanīya`, karma itself is conventionally designated `sukhavedanīya`, and likewise for the other two.

## New Technical Terms

- **svabhāvavedanīyatā** — being experienced by intrinsic nature
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya technical formation
  - **function:** identifies the case where the bearer of the predicate is feeling itself

- **saṃprayogavedanīyatā** — being experienced through association
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya technical formation
  - **function:** explains the designation of contact (`sparśa`) through its conjunction with feeling

- **ālambanavedanīyatā** — being experienced through object-support
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya technical formation
  - **function:** explains how objects are designated through their role as supports of feeling

- **vipākavedanīyatā** — being experienced through maturation
  - **status:** decisive for Chapter 4
  - **function:** specifies the exact sense in which karma is called pleasant-, painful-, or neutral-to-be-experienced

- **saṃmukhībhāvavedanīyatā** — being experienced through present manifestation
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya technical formation
  - **function:** distinguishes present actuality of feeling from the other relational senses of `vedanīyatā`

- **saṃmukhīkurvan** — making present, bringing into present manifestation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya prose clarification
  - **function:** explains why present feeling is said to be experienced without positing a second feeling that experiences the first

## Doctrinal Determination

The fivefold distinction prevents a major category error:

```text
vedanīya
    does not name one ontological relation
```

Rather:

```text
vedanā
    vedanīya by svabhāva

sparśa
    vedanīya by saṃprayoga

viṣaya
    vedanīya by ālambana

karma
    vedanīya by vipāka

present feeling
    vedanīya by saṃmukhībhāva
```

The karmic use is therefore specifically causal-resultant:

```text
karma
    ≠ vedanā

karma
    → vipāka
    → vedanā-character

therefore
karma is called vedanīya
by vipāka
```

This preserves the distinction already established in 4.47: feeling characterizes the resultant experience without constituting the entire resultant complex.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The existing Kārikā translation is broadly correct, but `saṃmukhībhāva` should be sharpened from merely “becoming immediately present” to **present manifestation / being made presently actual**.

Preferred translation:

> **Vedanīyatā is fivefold: through intrinsic nature, association, object-support, maturation, and present manifestation.**

The Bhāṣya makes clear that these are five **grounds licensing the designation**, not five species of feeling.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.49 is a remarkably explicit theory of typed predication.

The apparent form:

```text
X is vedanīya
```

is semantically incomplete until the mediating relation is specified:

```text
VEDANIYA(X, by-svabhāva)
VEDANIYA(X, by-saṃprayoga)
VEDANIYA(X, by-ālambana)
VEDANIYA(X, by-vipāka)
VEDANIYA(X, by-saṃmukhībhāva)
```

Thus the same predicate can arise through:

```text
identity
association
object-relation
causal maturation
present actuality
```

These must not be collapsed.

The strongest local result is:

```text
predication
    is mediated by a determinate relation
```

In the karmic case:

```text
KARMA
    --produces-vipāka→ RESULT
    --has-feeling-character→ VEDANĀ

therefore
"karma is pleasant-to-be-experienced"
```

The predicate belongs to karma only through this mediating causal relation.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage deserves a stronger-than-usual Organon contact because Vasubandhu himself is analyzing the logic of predication.

A surface proposition can conceal distinct genetic relations:

```text
"X is P"
```

may mean:

```text
X is intrinsically P
X is associated with P
X has P as object-support relation
X produces P as result
X presently manifests P
```

The copular surface therefore does not erase mediation. The `is` is intelligible only through the determinate relation that makes the predication true.

For this project, that is a significant result:

```text
immediate predication
    ← mediated determination
```

But the Bhāṣya remains the control: the five relations are Vasubandhu's own five determinations of `vedanīyatā`, not an imposed external scheme.

## Commit Note

Bhāṣya pass for VAK 4.49. Resolves how karma can be called `vedanīya` despite not being feeling by distinguishing five grounds of experiential designation: intrinsic nature, association, object-support, maturation, and present manifestation. Identifies `vipākavedanīyatā` as the precise relation governing karmic usage and records the broader logical principle that one surface predicate may be licensed by several irreducibly different mediating relations.
