# VAK_4.42_bhasya — Loss of Desire-Realm Wholesome and Defiled Non-Material Dharmas

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.42**

```iast
kāmāptaṃ kuśalārūpaṃ mūlacchedordhvajanmataḥ |
pratipakṣodayāt kliṣṭam arūpaṃ tu vihīyate ||
```

> Wholesome non-material dharma belonging to the desire realm is abandoned through severance of the wholesome roots or through birth above; defiled non-material dharma is abandoned through the arising of its counteragent.

## Source Cautions

The Bhāṣya decisively corrects the first-pass tendency to associate `arūpa` here with avijñapti. Vasubandhu explicitly glosses both classes as `arūpasvabhāva`, **non-material in nature**. Since avijñapti is classified as rūpa in the Sarvāstivāda system, it should not be imported into this verse.

Likewise, `ūrdhvajanman` is concretized as rebirth in either the rūpadhātu or ārūpyadhātu.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu completes the current account of abandonment by turning from the previously classified restraints and avijñapti formations to two broad classes of **non-material dharmas**.

First, a wholesome non-material dharma belonging to kāmadhātu is lost in either of two ways:

```text
kuśalamūlasamuccheda
    severance of the wholesome roots

or

rūpa/ārūpyadhātūpapatti
    rebirth in the form or formless domain
```

The first destroys its wholesome causal basis. The second removes the continuum from the domain to which that wholesome dharma belongs.

Second, every defiled non-material dharma is abandoned only through the arising of its appropriate counteragent. Vasubandhu makes this highly determinate: for each type of secondary defilement (`upakleśaprakāra`), there is a corresponding path of abandonment (`prahāṇamārga`). That defilement, together with its associated complex (`saparivāra`), is abandoned by that path and **not otherwise**.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose throughout this short unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **arūpasvabhāva** — non-material in intrinsic character
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the compressed Kārikā expression
  - **function:** terminological correction; excludes avijñapti from the referent here

- **upakleśaprakāra** — a determinate kind or species of secondary defilement
  - **status:** newly operative in this passage
  - **function:** specifies what is abandoned

- **prahāṇamārga** — the path specifically effecting abandonment
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary, newly decisive here
  - **function:** counteragent / means of abandonment

- **saparivāra** — together with its associated complex or entourage
  - **status:** previously encountered construction
  - **function:** determines the scope of what is abandoned with a defilement

## Doctrinal Determination

The two classes cease asymmetrically:

```text
kāmāvacara-kuśala-arūpa
    depends on:
        wholesome roots
        + desire-realm possession

    lost by:
        root-severance
        or higher-realm rebirth

kliṣṭa-arūpa
    lost by:
        determinate pratipakṣa
        embodied in the corresponding prahāṇamārga
```

The second statement is especially important. Absence, suppression, distraction, or external prevention is not equivalent to abandonment. A defilement is abandoned by the Path specifically opposed to it.

```text
defilement D
    ↔ corresponding abandonment-path P

P arises
    → D + its associated complex abandoned

no P
    → no abandonment in the strict sense
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation must be corrected so that `arūpa` is not glossed as a nonmanifest material karmic form.

Preferred translation:

> **Wholesome non-material dharma belonging to the desire realm is abandoned through severance of the wholesome roots or through birth in a higher realm. Defiled non-material dharma, however, is abandoned through the arising of its counteragent.**

The Bhāṣya supplies the stricter meaning of the second half:

> each type of defilement is abandoned by its corresponding path of abandonment, together with its associated factors, and not otherwise.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.42 distinguishes three different logics of cessation:

```text
loss of generative root
    → mūlaccheda

loss of domain-compatibility
    → ūrdhvajanman

active contrary determination
    → pratipakṣodaya
```

The most important result is the one-to-one structure of genuine abandonment:

```text
specific afflicted determination
    → specific counter-path
    → determinate abandonment
```

Abandonment is therefore not generic negation. It is mediated opposition.

## Organon Contact Point

Restrained formulation only:

```text
mere non-appearance of a determination
    ≠
its mediated negation
```

The Bhāṣya requires a determinate counteragent whose arising actually removes the afflicted form. This is a useful formal model of **negation through a specified contrary ground**, but the primary doctrinal content remains the Abhidharma theory of `pratipakṣa` and `prahāṇamārga`.
