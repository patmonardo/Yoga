# VAK_4.63_bhasya — Alternative Grounds for Black and Black-and-White Karma

## Kārikā Anchor

**IAST**

> *anye narakavedyānyakāmavedyaṃ dvayaṃ viduḥ /*  
> *dṛgdheyaṃ kṛṣṇam anye ’nyat kṛṣṇaśuklaṃ tu kāmajam // 4.63 //* 

Best working translation:

> Other teachers understand the pair as, respectively, karma to be experienced in hell and karma to be experienced elsewhere in the desire-domain. Others call karma abandoned by seeing “black,” while the remaining desire-domain karma is black-and-white.

See: `VAK_4.63.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya records two alternative interpretations.

First:

> *anye punar ācāryāḥ paśyanti narakavedanīyaṃ narakāc cānyakāmadhātuvedanīyaṃ karma yathākrama kṛṣṇaṃ kṛṣṇaśuklaṃ ceti /*

It then explains:

> *narake hy akuśalasyaiva karmaṇo vipākas tena tadvedanīyaṃ kṛṣṇam ity ucyate / ato ’nyakāmadhātau kuśalākuśalasya karmaṇo vipākas tena tadvedanīyaṃ kṛṣṇaśuklam ity uktam /*

Second:

> *anye punar āhur darśanaprahātavyaṃ karma kṛṣṇaṃ kuśalenāmiśratvāt /*

and:

> *anyat tu kāmāvacaraṃ karma kṛṣṇaśuklam / kiṃ tad anyat / bhāvanāprahātavyam / tad dhi kuśalaṃ cākuśalaṃ ceti /*

## Source Cautions

The e-text is based on Pradhan/Shastri and is not being treated as a critical edition. The wording is sufficiently clear for the doctrinal alternatives here, but punctuation and sandhi should remain provisional.

The Bhāṣya is explicitly reporting **other teachers** (`anye ācāryāḥ`, `anye punar āhuḥ`). These alternatives should not be silently promoted over the principal classification established in VAK 4.60.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.60 gave the principal fourfold scheme:

```text
unwholesome karma
    → black

form-domain wholesome karma
    → white

desire-domain wholesome karma
    → black-and-white at the continuum level

anāsrava karma
    → neither black nor white
    → conducive to karmic exhaustion
```

VAK 4.63 now preserves two alternative ways of distinguishing the two desire-related categories, black and black-and-white.

### First alternative — classification by place and composition of maturation

Some teachers say:

```text
karma to be experienced in hell
    → black

karma to be experienced elsewhere in kāmadhātu
    → black-and-white
```

The Bhāṣya gives the ground.

In hell:

```text
only unwholesome karma has maturation
```

Therefore karma whose maturation is to be experienced there is called black.

Elsewhere in the desire-domain:

```text
wholesome karma matures
and
unwholesome karma matures
```

Therefore karma associated with that field of maturation is termed black-and-white.

The classificatory ground is therefore not merely the intrinsic ethical quality of a single karma but the **maturation-field and the kinds of vipāka realized there**.

### Second alternative — classification by mode of abandonment

Other teachers say:

```text
darśanaprahātavya karma
    → black

remaining kāmāvacara karma
    → black-and-white
```

The Bhāṣya explains why karma abandoned by seeing is called black:

```text
kuśalenāmiśratvāt
    because it is unmixed with wholesome karma
```

The remainder is identified as:

```text
bhāvanāprahātavya
    what is abandoned by cultivation
```

This remainder is called black-and-white because it includes both:

```text
kuśala
and
akuśala
```

Thus the second alternative uses **mode of abandonment** as the ground of division.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames and reports the alternatives.

**Voice: other teachers (`anye ācāryāḥ`)** — classify hell-result karma as black and other desire-domain-result karma as black-and-white.

**Voice: other teachers (`anye punar āhuḥ`)** — classify karma abandoned by seeing as black and the remaining desire-domain karma as black-and-white.

No reason appears here to identify either alternative as Vasubandhu’s own preferred replacement for the principal 4.60 classification.

## New Technical Terms

- **narakavedanīya** — karma whose maturation is to be experienced in hell  
  **status:** new in this local classification  
  **function:** alternative criterion based on maturation-field

- **anyakāmadhātuvedanīya** — karma whose maturation is experienced elsewhere in the desire-domain  
  **status:** new in this local classification  
  **function:** counterpart to hell-result karma in the first alternative

- **darśanaprahātavya** — to be abandoned by seeing  
  **status:** previously encountered doctrinally, newly decisive here  
  **function:** alternative classificatory ground for black karma

- **bhāvanāprahātavya** — to be abandoned by cultivation  
  **status:** previously encountered doctrinally, newly decisive here  
  **function:** identifies the remainder classed as black-and-white

- **amiśra** — unmixed  
  **status:** locally important  
  **function:** explains why what is abandoned by seeing is called black

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse does not merely add exceptions to VAK 4.60. It shows that Abhidharma classifications may depend on different **grounds of division**.

First ground:

```text
maturation-field
    +
kinds of karma capable of ripening there
```

Second ground:

```text
mode of abandonment
    seeing
    versus
    cultivation
```

This is important because identical surface labels do not guarantee identical intensions.

```text
“black”
    may be determined from ethical quality
    or maturation-field
    or mode of abandonment
```

The context supplies the operative criterion.

The first alternative is relational and field-based:

```text
hell
    admits only unwholesome vipāka
        → black

other kāmadhātu destinations
    admit wholesome + unwholesome vipāka
        → black-and-white
```

The second is path-based:

```text
abandoned by seeing
    unmixed with wholesome
        → black

abandoned by cultivation
    includes wholesome + unwholesome
        → black-and-white
```

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.63 is a compact study in the logic of classification.

```text
same predicate
    “black”

can be licensed by
    different determining relations
```

Formally:

```text
Classification₁(Karma)
    depends on maturation-location

Classification₂(Karma)
    depends on abandonment-mode
```

Therefore:

```text
TERM
    ≠ complete definition

TERM + classificatory ground
    → determinate meaning
```

This continues the lesson of VAK 4.60, where `kṛṣṇaśukla` was shown to be established at the level of the continuum rather than intrinsic nature. Here the Bhāṣya goes further: even the *ground from which a taxonomic predicate is assigned* can vary between doctrinal presentations.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation was substantially correct, but the Bhāṣya allows two refinements.

First, `anyākāmavedya` should be understood concretely as:

> karma to be experienced elsewhere in the desire-domain than hell.

Second, the second alternative should explicitly identify `anyat` as:

> the remaining desire-domain karma, namely what is abandoned by cultivation.

Thus a strengthened translation is:

> Other teachers understand the pair as karma to be experienced in hell and karma to be experienced elsewhere in the desire-domain, respectively. Still others call karma abandoned by seeing “black”; the remaining desire-domain karma—what is abandoned by cultivation—is black-and-white.

## Organon Contact Point

The strongest contact point is methodological rather than metaphysical.

A determination is intelligible only relative to its **ground of predication**.

```text
predicate
    black / black-and-white

requires
    rule of classification
```

The same vocabulary may organize the field differently depending on whether the determining relation is:

```text
ethical nature
maturation-field
path-abandonment
continuum structure
```

This is a useful warning against treating Abhidharma terminology as a flat ontology of labels. Its concepts often function inside explicit systems of discrimination.
