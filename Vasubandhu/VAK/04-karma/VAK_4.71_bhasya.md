# VAK_4.71_bhasya — Completion Roots and the Objective Bases of Karmapathas

## Kārikā

**VAK 4.71**

**IAST**

> mithyādṛṣṭes tu mohena śeṣāṇāṃ tribhir iṣyate /
> sattvabhogāv adhiṣṭhānaṃ nāmarūpaṃ ca nāma ca // 4.71 //

**Working translation**

> False view reaches completion through delusion; the remaining courses are held to reach completion through all three roots. Their respective bases are living beings, possessions, name-and-form, and name.

See: `VAK_4.71.md`

---

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya continues directly from the completion-root classification of VAK 4.70:

> mithyādṛṣṭes tu mohena

> adhimātrabhūto hi tāṃ niṣṭhāpayati /

> śeṣāṇāṃ tribhir iṣyate /

It then asks:

> ke punaḥ śeṣāḥ /

and answers:

> mṛṣāvādapaiśunyasaṃbhinnapralāpāḥ /

These are completed through any of the three roots:

> eṣāṃ tribhir api niṣṭhā lobhena dveṣeṇa mohena vā /

The Bhāṣya then reorganizes the ten karmapathas according to their objective bases:

> ya ete karmapathāś caturbhiḥ kāṇḍair uktās trayas traya ekaḥ trayaś ca eṣāṃ yathākramaṃ /

> sattvabhogāv adhiṣṭhānaṃ nāmarūpaṃ ca nāma ca //

and gives the exact distribution:

> sattvādhiṣṭhānā vadhādayaḥ /

> bhogādhiṣṭhānāḥ parastrīgamanādayaḥ /

> nāmarūpādhiṣṭhā mithyādṛṣṭiḥ /

> nāmakāyādhiṣṭhānā mṛṣāvādādayaḥ /

---

## Translation of the Bhāṣya Unit

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

False view reaches completion through delusion. For delusion, when it becomes intense, brings it to completion.

The remaining courses are held to be completed through the three roots.

Which are the remaining ones? False speech, divisive speech, and disordered or idle speech. These reach completion through any of the three: greed, hatred, or delusion.

These karmapathas have been stated in four groups: three, three, one, and three. Their bases, respectively, are living beings, possessions, name-and-form, and name.

The group beginning with killing has living beings as its basis. The group beginning with sexual misconduct has possessions or objects of enjoyment as its basis. False view has name-and-form as its basis. The group beginning with false speech has the name-complex as its basis.

---

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 4.71 completes two classifications at once.

First, it finishes the analysis of the roots that bring the unwholesome karmapathas to completion:

```text
Dveṣa
    killing
    ill will
    harsh speech

Lobha
    sexual misconduct
    covetousness
    taking what is not given

Moha
    false view

Lobha / Dveṣa / Moha
    false speech
    divisive speech
    disordered / idle speech
```

The Bhāṣya explains false view especially sharply:

```text
moha
    ↓ becomes intense
adhimātra
    ↓
mithyādṛṣṭi reaches niṣṭhā
```

Thus false view is not simply synonymous with delusion. Delusion is the root; false view is its determinate culmination in a specific karmapatha.

Second, the same ten karmapathas are regrouped according to `adhiṣṭhāna`, the objective basis upon which they operate.

The Bhāṣya says explicitly that the ten were presented in four groups:

```text
3 + 3 + 1 + 3
```

and maps them as follows:

```text
1. sattva — living being
   killing
   ill will
   harsh speech

2. bhoga — possession / object of enjoyment
   sexual misconduct
   covetousness
   taking what is not given

3. nāmarūpa — name-and-form
   false view

4. nāmakāya — name-complex
   false speech
   divisive speech
   disordered / idle speech
```

This is significantly more exact than the Kārikā alone.

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. `adhiṣṭhāna` is not a generic object

The Bhāṣya does not merely say that actions need objects. It classifies the ten courses by the kind of objective basis upon which each reaches completion.

The structure is therefore:

```text
root
    internal generative ground

karmapatha
    determinate ethical course

adhiṣṭhāna
    objective basis upon which it operates
```

The karmapatha is relational. It is neither merely an inner motive nor merely an outward event.

### 2. The four bases mirror the four karmapatha groups

The Bhāṣya's phrase `trayas traya ekaḥ trayaś ca` is important. The grouping is systematic:

```text
three — living beings
three — possessions
one   — name-and-form
three — name-complexes
```

This is not an incidental list of possible objects.

### 3. False view is distinguished from the speech group

`mithyādṛṣṭi` has `nāmarūpa` as its basis, not merely `nāma`.

This is philosophically significant. False view concerns the determination of experienced reality as name-and-form, whereas the three variable verbal karmapathas operate through the linguistic name-complex itself.

We should not over-interpret this distinction beyond what the Bhāṣya states, but the text itself clearly keeps the two objective domains apart.

### 4. Speech is structurally multi-rooted

False speech, divisive speech, and idle/disordered speech may reach completion through greed, hatred, or delusion.

Thus speech functions as a flexible medium of karmic execution:

```text
speech can serve acquisition
speech can serve hostility
speech can express delusion
```

This is why these three cannot be assigned to one characteristic completion-root in the way the other seven can.

## New Technical Terms

### **adhiṣṭhāna**
- **provisional meaning:** objective basis; that upon which a karmapatha is established or operates
- **status:** newly decisive in this passage
- **function:** classifies karmapathas from their objective side

### **nāmakāya**
- **provisional meaning:** name-complex; aggregate or structured body of linguistic designation
- **status:** Bhāṣya specification of the Kārikā's `nāma`
- **function:** objective basis of false speech, divisive speech, and disordered/idle speech

### **adhimātra**
- **provisional meaning:** intense; predominant; of heightened degree
- **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here
- **function:** explains how delusion brings false view to completion

### **niṣṭhā / niṣṭhāpayati**
- **provisional meaning:** completion; bringing to culmination
- **status:** continuing technical vocabulary from the preceding verses
- **function:** distinguishes the completed karmapatha from preparation or merely present affliction

---

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass rendering of `adhiṣṭhāna` as “objective basis” is confirmed, but the Bhāṣya now fixes the distribution precisely.

The Kārikā's `nāma` should also be read in light of the prose specification `nāmakāya`. “Name” alone is too thin if it suggests an isolated word. The Bhāṣya points to a structured linguistic complex serving as the support of the three speech courses.

For `bhoga`, “possession” or “object of enjoyment” remains preferable to the abstract “enjoyment,” because the three associated courses concern appropriation or access to what is treated as another's good.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.71 adds an objective moment to the analysis already developed through root and completion:

```text
Subjective ground
    root
        ↓
Determinate realization
    karmapatha
        ↓
Objective field
    adhiṣṭhāna
```

This yields a more complete relational structure:

```text
Moral act
    = rooted course upon a determinate basis
```

The same external type of speech can be generated by different roots, while the same root can become determinate through different courses. Adequate analysis therefore requires all three dimensions:

```text
which root?
which karmapatha?
upon which basis?
```

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest Organon contact point is textually earned by the introduction of `adhiṣṭhāna`.

The system now refuses two reductions:

```text
karma ≠ inward motive alone
karma ≠ outward event alone
```

Instead:

```text
Ground
    becomes determinate as Course

Course
    realizes itself upon an Objective Basis
```

The striking case is false view:

```text
moha
    subjective root
        ↓
mithyādṛṣṭi
    determinate course
        ↓
nāmarūpa
    objective basis
```

while the speech courses have:

```text
variable root
        ↓
determinate speech-course
        ↓
nāmakāya
```

That is a genuine advance from an internal psychology of karma to a relational structure of ethical determination.

---

## Summary

VAK 4.71 completes the completion-root classification and introduces the objective bases of the ten unwholesome karmapathas.

Established:

```text
false view
    completed through delusion

false speech / divisive speech / idle speech
    completed through greed, hatred, or delusion
```

and:

```text
3 karmapathas → sattva
3 karmapathas → bhoga
1 karmapatha  → nāmarūpa
3 karmapathas → nāmakāya
```

The decisive structural result is:

```text
root
    ≠ course
    ≠ objective basis
```

A completed karmapatha is a determinate relation among all three.
