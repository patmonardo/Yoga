**Working translation:** Prātimokṣa restraint is eightfold by designation, but fourfold in terms of distinct real type. Because designation changes according to sex-characteristic, the corresponding male and female classes do not constitute new restraint-natures. The successively acquired restraints arise separately, yet they are mutually compatible.

- The working e-text is based principally on Pradhan with corrections from Shastri and explicitly warns that it is not a critical edition.
- The transmitted `śramaṇerasaṃvara` at [205|24] is retained as printed; elsewhere the source uses `śrāmaṇera`.
- `liṅga` is explicitly glossed here through `vyañjana`, the sex-characteristic by which female and male are distinguished. The prose therefore controls the meaning more narrowly than the Kārikā alone.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya proceeds through three distinct questions.

### 1. Why eight by name but four in real type?

Eight disciplinary classes are named:

```text
bhikṣu
bhikṣuṇī
śikṣamāṇā
śrāmaṇera
śrāmaṇerī
upāsaka
upāsikā
upavāsa
```

But only four restraint-types are distinct `dravyataḥ`:

```text
bhikṣu-saṃvara
śrāmaṇera-saṃvara
upāsaka-saṃvara
upavāsa-saṃvara
```

The female counterparts do not constitute additional restraint-natures. Bhikṣuṇī restraint is not other than bhikṣu restraint; śikṣamāṇā and śrāmaṇerī restraint are not other than śrāmaṇera restraint; upāsikā restraint is not other than upāsaka restraint.

### 2. How is that known?

The proof is `liṅgato nāmasaṃcārāt`.

The Bhāṣya defines `liṅga` here as `vyañjana`, the bodily sex-characteristic by which female and male are marked. If that characteristic changes, the disciplinary name changes accordingly, but the text says there is no cause either for abandoning the previous restraint or for acquiring a new restraint merely because of that change.

Therefore:

```text
change of sex-characteristic
    → change of designation
    ≠ abandonment of old restraint
    ≠ acquisition of new restraint
```

The difference between the corresponding male and female designations is thus nominal rather than a difference in the restraint's `svabhāva`.

### 3. Are successively higher restraints merely increments of one restraint?

A second issue now arises. Someone may first undertake lay restraint, then novice restraint, then monastic restraint. Are these only larger quantities of one and the same restraint, like fifteen versus twenty coins, or do distinct complete restraints arise?

The answer is:

```text
pṛthak
```

They arise separately, unmixed, each with its own characteristic.

The Bhāṣya explains the difference by `nidāna-viśeṣa`. As more training rules are undertaken, the practitioner withdraws from more occasions and causal conditions of heedlessness and misconduct, while entering into more conditions supporting abstention. The restraints therefore differ because their causal bases differ.

Yet they are `avirodhin`: mutually compatible. Taking a higher restraint does not cancel the earlier one. Lay, novice, and monastic restraints can coexist.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — presents the eightfold nominal and fourfold real classification, gives the `liṅga` argument, poses the question about successively acquired restraints, and gives the resolution `pṛthak ... avirodhinaḥ`.

No explicit school attribution is marked in this passage.

## New Technical Terms

- **nāmataḥ** — by name or designation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya prose determinant of the Kārikā's eightfold division
  - **function:** distinguishes nominal enumeration from real-type classification

- **pratiniyata-lakṣaṇa** — having a specifically fixed characteristic
  - **status:** introduced in the prose
  - **function:** grounds the fourfold `dravyataḥ` classification

- **vyañjana** — sex-characteristic by which female and male are marked
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss on `liṅga`
  - **function:** explains why disciplinary names can change without a new restraint being produced

- **vyañjana-parivṛtti** — alteration of sex-characteristic
  - **status:** introduced in the prose
  - **function:** test case separating change of designation from change of restraint-nature

- **avyāmiśra** — unmixed, distinct
  - **status:** introduced in the prose
  - **function:** characterizes successively acquired restraints as separately arising wholes

- **pṛthag-lakṣaṇa** — separately characterized
  - **status:** introduced in the prose
  - **function:** prevents reduction of higher restraints to mere quantitative enlargement

- **nidāna-viśeṣa** — difference of causal occasion/basis
  - **status:** introduced as the decisive explanatory principle
  - **function:** explains the difference among lay, novice, and monastic abstentions

- **avirodhin** — mutually non-conflicting / compatible
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly glossed in prose
  - **function:** states that the distinct restraints can coexist

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage requires two distinctions that must not be collapsed.

First:

```text
nominal multiplicity
    ≠
real-type multiplicity
```

Eight institutional designations correspond to four restraint-natures.

Second:

```text
successive disciplinary enlargement
    ≠
mere quantitative increase of one restraint
```

Lay, novice, and monastic restraints arise as distinct complete determinations because their causal bases differ.

Thus the Bhāṣya simultaneously denies one kind of over-differentiation and one kind of over-identification:

```text
sex-based naming difference
    does not create a new restraint-nature

higher disciplinary undertaking
    does create a distinct restraint
```

This is the conceptual center of the verse.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The existing Kārikā translation requires refinement in the second half.

`liṅgato nāmasaṃcārāt` does not merely mean that the eight classes are differentiated "by gender." The prose argument is more exact:

```text
because a change in sex-characteristic
produces a transfer of disciplinary designation
without loss or fresh acquisition of restraint,
the corresponding male/female classes are not new restraint-natures.
```

Also, `pṛthak te cāvirodhinaḥ` refers not to the male/female nominal classes but to successively acquired lay, novice, and monastic restraints: they arise separately and are mutually compatible.

A stronger working translation is therefore:

> The restraint called prātimokṣa is eightfold by designation, but fourfold in terms of distinct real type. Because designation transfers with a change of sex-characteristic, the corresponding male and female classes do not constitute new restraint-natures. The successively acquired restraints arise separately, yet are mutually compatible.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya distinguishes three relations:

```text
name-change
    without real-type change

real-type difference
    without mutual exclusion

causal-basis difference
    as ground of distinct restraint
```

The important inference is:

```text
mere difference of name
    is insufficient for real difference

mere inclusion of fewer rules in more rules
    is insufficient for identity

real differentiation is established
    through difference of determining causal basis
```

The verse is therefore not simply classificatory. It asks what kind of difference counts as a difference in the dharma itself.

## Organon Contact Point

A light contact point only:

```text
designation
    may vary while determination remains the same

causal articulation
    may differ while determinations remain compatible
```

The Bhāṣya is carefully separating nominal difference, real difference, and opposition. This is worth preserving for later systematic study without importing a stronger logical framework now.
