# VAK_4.40_bhasya — Loss of Meditative and Noble Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> bhūmisaṃcārahānibhyāṃ dhyānāptaṃ tyajyate śubham |
> tathārūpyāptam āryaṃ tu phalāptyuttaptihānibhiḥ ||

### Best Current Translation

> Wholesome restraint acquired through dhyāna is relinquished through transition of ground or falling away; the same applies to that acquired through the formless attainments. Noble uncontaminated restraint, however, is relinquished through attainment of a fruit, strengthening of the faculties, or falling away.

## Source Cautions

The Bhāṣya resolves a problem in the existing Kārikā file. The verse reads:

```text
phalāptyuttaptihānibhiḥ
```

The middle member is not `utpatti` (“arising”) but `uttapti`, reflected explicitly by the prose explanation:

```text
indriyottāpana
    strengthening / sharpening of the faculties
```

The Kārikā translation should therefore be revised accordingly.

## Natural Argument

The preceding verses treated the loss of prātimokṣa restraint. Vasubandhu now asks how the other two restraints are relinquished:

```text
dhyānasaṃvara
anāsravasaṃvara
```

### 1. Dhyāna-acquired wholesome dharmas

All wholesome dharmas acquired through dhyāna are relinquished in two ways:

```text
bhūmisaṃcāra
    transition from one ground to another

hāni / parihāṇi
    falling away from the attainment
```

The prose specifies that transition may occur through rebirth into another level, while falling away concerns loss of the attainment itself. Some portion may also cease through loss of membership in the relevant class of beings (`nikāyasabhāgatva`).

The same rule applies to wholesome dharmas belonging to the formless attainments.

### 2. Uncontaminated wholesome dharmas

Uncontaminated (`anāsrava`) wholesome dharmas are relinquished in three ways.

#### a. Fruit attainment — `phalaprāpti`

When a fruit is attained, the preceding Path is relinquished:

```text
pūrvaka mārga
    → fruit attained
    → prior Path no longer possessed in that former mode
```

This is not simple failure. The Path has completed the function by which the fruit becomes attained.

#### b. Strengthening the faculties — `indriyottāpana`

When the faculties are sharpened or strengthened, the Path belonging to weaker faculties is relinquished:

```text
mṛdvindriya-mārga
    → indriyottāpana
    → stronger-faculty Path
```

The former Path ceases because its determining basis has been transformed.

#### c. Falling away — `parihāṇi`

Through falling away, a superior Path is relinquished, or a fruit / fruit-distinguished attainment is lost where such regression is possible.

```text
uttara mārga
or
phala / phalaviśiṣṭa
    → parihāṇi
    → relinquishment
```

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

How are the restraint belonging to dhyāna and the uncontaminated restraint relinquished?

All wholesome dharmas acquired through dhyāna are relinquished for two reasons: either through transition of ground by rebirth upward, or through falling away from the attainment; and some also through change in belonging to a class of beings.

Just as wholesome dharmas belonging to the Form Realm are relinquished through transition of ground and falling away, so too are those belonging to the formless attainments.

Uncontaminated wholesome dharmas, however, are relinquished for three reasons. Through attainment of a fruit, the preceding Path is relinquished. Through strengthening of the faculties, the Path belonging to weak faculties is relinquished. Through falling away, a higher Path is relinquished, or a fruit or an attainment distinguished by fruit.

### Philosophical Translation

Meditative wholesome determinations persist only while the ground and attainment that sustain them remain operative. They cease either because the practitioner passes into another ground or because the attainment itself is lost.

Noble uncontaminated determinations possess a different developmental structure. A prior Path is relinquished when its fruit is attained; a weaker-faculty Path is relinquished when the faculties are strengthened; and a superior Path or fruit may be relinquished through regression. Thus loss may occur not only through deterioration but also through successful completion or qualitative transformation.

## New Technical Terms

- **indriyottāpana** — strengthening, sharpening, or intensification of the faculties
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā as an expanded prose form; reflected by `uttapti`
  - **function:** explains why a weaker-faculty noble Path is relinquished

- **mṛdvindriyamārga** — Path associated with weak faculties
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific expression in this unit
  - **function:** identifies the Path-form superseded when faculties are strengthened

- **pūrvaka mārga** — preceding Path
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory phrase
  - **function:** identifies what is relinquished upon fruit-attainment

- **uttara mārga** — higher / subsequent Path
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory phrase
  - **function:** identifies what may be lost through regression

- **phalaviśiṣṭa** — an attainment distinguished by or superior through a fruit
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** specifies an advanced attainment subject to loss through falling away

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse differentiates two fundamentally different lifecycle structures.

### Meditative wholesome restraint

```text
ground-dependent

loss through:
    bhūmisaṃcāra
    parihāṇi
```

Its persistence is tied to the meditative ground from which it is acquired.

### Noble uncontaminated restraint

```text
Path-stage-dependent

relinquishment through:
    fruit-attainment
    faculty-strengthening
    falling-away
```

The important point is that `tyāga` is broader than mere collapse. A dharma may be relinquished because:

```text
1. its ground is left behind
2. its attainment is lost
3. its function is fulfilled
4. its basis is qualitatively upgraded
```

Thus cessation can mark either regression or development.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The existing Kārikā file provisionally read the middle member of the final compound as `utpatti`, “arising.” The Bhāṣya establishes:

```text
uttapti
    → indriyottāpana
    → strengthening of the faculties
```

Recommended translation:

> Wholesome restraint acquired through dhyāna is relinquished through transition of ground or falling away; the same applies to that acquired through the formless attainments. Noble restraint, however, is relinquished through attainment of a fruit, strengthening of the faculties, or falling away.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.40 distinguishes **destructive loss** from **determinate supersession**.

```text
parihāṇi
    loss by regression

bhūmisaṃcāra
    loss by ground-transition

phalaprāpti
    relinquishment through fulfillment

indriyottāpana
    relinquishment through qualitative upgrade
```

The same predicate `tyāga` therefore covers changes with very different logical forms.

A prior dharma can cease because it fails, but also because the continuum has moved beyond the condition under which that dharma was the appropriate operative form.

```text
former ground
    → former dharma

new ground / stronger faculty / attained fruit
    → former dharma relinquished
```

The cessation of the earlier determination is therefore sometimes generated internally by its successful development.

## Organon Contact Point

The strongest contact is not a general analogy but the distinction between two forms of negation:

```text
abstract loss
    parihāṇi

superseding transition
    bhūmisaṃcāra
    phalaprāpti
    indriyottāpana
```

A Path-determination can disappear because it has failed, or because its own function has generated a successor determination. In the latter case, cessation belongs to the Path's development itself.
