# VAK_4.19_bhasya — Temporal Possession of Prātimokṣa Avijñapti

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.19**

```iast
prātimokṣasthito nityam atyāgād vartamānayā |
avijñaptyānvitaḥ pūrvāt kṣaṇād ūrdhvam atītayā ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> One established in prātimokṣa restraint is continually endowed with present avijñapti so long as it has not been abandoned; from the moment after the first, he is also endowed with past avijñapti.

## Source Cautions

The electronic text reads `prātimmokṣasthito` at [209|06] and `prātimokṣāsaṃvarasthaḥ` at [209|08]. These are preserved exactly as transmitted here; the expected forms are evident from context, but no silent normalization is imposed.

The Kārikā file's `atyāgāt` must be understood as governing not merely the first clause but the entire temporal possession discussion. Vasubandhu explicitly states: `atyāgāditi sarvatrādhikṛtaṃ veditavyam` — “the phrase ‘because it has not been abandoned’ is to be understood as governing throughout.”

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu introduces a new question: which person is endowed with which manifestation or non-manifest action, and for how long?

The prātimokṣa bearer is the first case. A person established in prātimokṣa restraint is continuously endowed with **present avijñapti** for as long as that avijñapti has not been abandoned.

The temporal structure then expands after the first moment. From the moment following the first, the person is also endowed with **past avijñapti**: each prior moment of restraint has passed, while a new present moment of avijñapti continues to arise.

Vasubandhu closes the unit by making the governing condition explicit. `Atyāga`, non-abandonment, applies throughout this entire analysis. The temporal predicates do not describe possession unconditionally; they describe possession so long as the restraint has not been relinquished.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the temporal-possession question, explains present and past possession of prātimokṣa avijñapti, and explicitly extends the condition of non-abandonment throughout the discussion.

## New Technical Terms

- **samanvāgata** — endowed with / possessed of
  - **status:** previously encountered, now made central to temporal possession analysis
  - **function:** technical predicate for possession of a dharma across temporal modes
- **atyāga** — non-abandonment / non-relinquishment
  - **status:** technically operative here
  - **function:** governing condition for continued possession
- **vartamāna** — present
  - **status:** standard temporal category
  - **function:** marks the currently arising avijñapti
- **atīta** — past
  - **status:** standard temporal category
  - **function:** marks avijñapti from earlier moments still possessed as past

## Doctrinal Determination

### The governing condition

```text
prātimokṣa possession
    holds only while
    restraint has not been abandoned
```

Vasubandhu explicitly generalizes this condition:

```text
atyāgāt
    governs throughout
```

### First moment

```text
first moment of prātimokṣa restraint
    → present avijñapti
```

There is not yet any past avijñapti of this restraint, because no earlier moment of it has occurred.

### From the second moment onward

```text
current moment
    → present avijñapti

preceding moments
    → past avijñapti
```

Thus:

```text
from second moment onward
    present + past possession
```

The bearer is not described as possessing one numerically enduring avijñapti. Rather, the current avijñapti is present and earlier occurrences are possessed as past.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The existing translation is substantially confirmed, but the scope of `atyāgāt` should be made explicit:

> One established in prātimokṣa restraint is continually endowed with present avijñapti so long as it has not been abandoned; from the moment after the first, he is also endowed with past avijñapti.

The phrase “so long as it has not been abandoned” governs both present and past possession.

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes continuity from identity:

```text
continuity of restraint
    ≠
one numerically identical avijñapti enduring through time
```

Instead:

```text
moment 1
    present avijñapti

moment 2
    new present avijñapti
    + moment 1 as past

moment 3
    new present avijñapti
    + earlier moments as past
```

The persistence of restraint is therefore temporally articulated possession under the continuing condition of non-abandonment.

## Organon Contact Point

A restrained contact point is enough:

```text
persistence
    can be constituted by ordered succession
    rather than numerical endurance

continuation
    remains conditional on non-abandonment
```

This will matter later when the project studies how a practical determination remains efficacious without requiring an unchanging substrate.
