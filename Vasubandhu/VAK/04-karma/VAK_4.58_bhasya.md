# VAK_4.58_bhasya — Mental Derangement, Karmic Maturation, and Immediate Conditions

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.58**

> Mental derangement occurs in mental cognition. It may arise from karmic maturation, and also through fear, injury, bodily imbalance, and grief, among desire-domain beings other than the Kurus.

## Source Cautions

The Bhāṣya distinguishes **cittakṣepa** from **vikṣepa**. The former is a derangement or displacement of mental functioning; the latter is defilement/distraction. They can coincide but are not identical.

The text also distinguishes a karmically produced **mediate cause** from the presently manifest mental derangement. Vasubandhu explicitly denies that the disturbed mind itself is the karmic maturation.

`dhātu` in the physiological passage is best read here as bodily constitutional factor/domain, while `mahābhūta` refers to the great material factors. The text uses the classical humoral vocabulary of wind, bile, and phlegm as proximate bodily disturbances.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first asks two questions:

```text
In which cognition does mental derangement occur?
By what causes does it arise?
```

The first answer is precise:

```text
cittakṣepa
    occurs in manovijñāna
    not in the five sensory vijñāna-series
```

The reason is that the five sensory knowledge-series are `avikalpaka`, non-conceptual. Mental derangement in the relevant sense therefore belongs to the mental knowledge-series where recollection, purposive discrimination, and practical orientation can be disrupted.

The second answer initially says that derangement can be `karmavipākaja`, born from karmic maturation. Vasubandhu illustrates the causal ancestry through acts such as deliberately deranging others, intoxicating them against their will, frightening them, causing dangerous injuries, or otherwise making them lose recollection. Such acts can mature in a future state as one's own mental derangement.

But this creates an objection. If mental derangement is karmically produced, why should mental feeling not count as karmic maturation, contrary to the rule of VAK 4.57?

Vasubandhu's answer is decisive:

```text
mental derangement itself
    ≠ the vipāka

karmically produced disturbance of mahābhūtas
    = vipāka

mental derangement
    arises from that disturbed bodily basis
    and is therefore called vipākaja
```

Thus the causal sequence is layered:

```text
past karma
    ↓
karmic maturation in bodily/elemental constitution
    ↓
vaiṣamya — disequilibrium
    ↓
mental cognition becomes disturbed,
helpless, and deprived of recollection
    ↓
cittakṣepa
```

This preserves the result of VAK 4.57: mental painful feeling does not thereby become direct karmic maturation.

The kārikā also lists present proximate causes:

```text
bhaya
    fear

upaghāta
    physical injury / blows to vital points

vaiṣamya
    bodily constitutional imbalance

śoka
    grief
```

So a manifest derangement may have both a remote karmic ground and a proximate present condition.

Finally Vasubandhu asks in whom such derangement occurs. It occurs among desire-domain beings other than the Kurus: gods can become deranged, and certainly humans, animals, and pretas. Hell beings are said to be continuously deranged because extreme pain prevents them from clearly apprehending even themselves, much less what should or should not be done.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — main exposition and resolution of the objection.

**Voice: Objector** — the question asking why mental feeling would not then have to count as vipāka if mental derangement is karmically produced.

## New Technical Terms

- **cittakṣepa** — derangement or displacement of mental functioning
  - **status:** newly central in this passage
  - **function:** denotes loss of stable mental orientation and recollection

- **manovijñāna** — mental knowledge-series
  - **status:** established Abhidharma term
  - **function:** locus in which cittakṣepa occurs

- **avikalpaka** — non-conceptual, lacking imaginative differentiation
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** explains why the five sensory knowledge-series are not loci of cittakṣepa

- **mahābhūta-prakopa** — disturbance/aggravation of the great material factors
  - **status:** local physiological causal term
  - **function:** the direct karmic maturation from which mental derangement may arise

- **vaiṣamya** — disequilibrium, imbalance
  - **status:** previously encountered physiologically
  - **function:** proximate causal disturbance of the bodily basis

- **bhraṣṭasmṛtika** — deprived of recollection, having lost memory/orientation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific descriptive term here
  - **function:** marks the cognitive condition produced by derangement

## Doctrinal Determination

The doctrinal point is a strict distinction between levels of causation:

```text
karma
    does not have to mature directly
    as the manifest mental state

karma
    may mature as a bodily condition
    from which a mental state arises
```

Therefore:

```text
vipāka
    ≠ every downstream consequence of vipāka
```

A phenomenon can be called `vipākaja`, born from maturation, without itself being the `vipāka`.

This is precisely how the text preserves the prior rule:

```text
daurmanasya
    not vipāka

and yet

mental derangement
    can be vipākaja
```

The verse also distinguishes remote and proximate causes:

```text
REMOTE GROUND
    karmic maturation

PROXIMATE CONDITIONS
    fear
    injury
    constitutional imbalance
    grief
```

## Logical Determination

The passage requires a typed causal graph:

```text
PAST_KARMA
    → VIPĀKA(BODILY-CONSTITUTION)
    → IMBALANCE
    → MENTAL-DERANGEMENT
```

not:

```text
PAST_KARMA
    → MENTAL-DERANGEMENT AS VIPĀKA
```

So:

```text
CAUSES_VIA_MATURATION(X, Y)
    does not entail
IS_MATURATION(Y)
```

This distinction is essential for causal modeling. A downstream state can be karmically conditioned without being the immediate matured result.

The passage also distinguishes two mental predicates:

```text
kṣipta
    mentally deranged

vikṣipta
    defiled/distracted
```

Vasubandhu gives a fourfold analysis showing that either, both, or neither may obtain. This confirms that mental pathology and moral/afflictive defilement are logically distinct determinations.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation should be sharpened to preserve the mediate causation:

> **Mental derangement occurs in mental knowledge. It can be born from karmic maturation, and also from fear, injury, bodily imbalance, and grief, among desire-domain beings other than the Kurus. When it is karmically produced, the deranged mind itself is not the maturation; rather, karmic maturation disturbs the bodily basis, from which the derangement arises.**

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is the distinction between **ground**, **mediate actualization**, and **manifest effect**:

```text
ground
    past karma

mediate actualization
    bodily vipāka

manifest consequence
    mental derangement
```

This is stronger than a flat causal assertion. It shows how one determination is realized through a transformed substrate before appearing as a later state.

The passage also guards against reducing mental disturbance to moral fault:

```text
cittakṣepa
    ≠ vikṣepa
```

The causal and ethical predicates belong to different dimensions even when they coincide in a concrete case.
