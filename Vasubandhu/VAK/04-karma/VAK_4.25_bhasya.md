# VAK_4.25_bhasya — Vijñapti without Avijñapti and Avijñapti without Vijñapti

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.25**

```iast
vijñaptyaivānvitaḥ kurvan madhyastho mṛducetanaḥ |
tyaktānutpannavijñaptir avijñapty āryapudgalaḥ ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> A person standing in the middle, acting with weak intention, is endowed only with manifestation. A noble person whose manifestation has either not yet arisen or has been abandoned is endowed with non-manifest action.

Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.25.md`.

## Source Cautions

The e-text reads `akukśalaṃ` at [211|15-211|17], evidently where `akuśalaṃ` is expected. The exact source form is preserved above; any normalization remains editorial.

The opening `catuṣkoṭikam` belongs naturally with the transition from VAK 4.24 into VAK 4.25. The present verse supplies two explicitly stated crossed cases: `vijñapti` without `avijñapti`, and `avijñapti` without `vijñapti`. The Bhāṣya then declares the possession-arrangement for restraint, non-restraint, and the middle position stated and moves on to acquisition of restraints.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu raises a four-cornered question concerning possession: can someone be endowed with `vijñapti` but not `avijñapti`?

The first explicitly stated case is a `madhyastha`, one established in neither restraint nor non-restraint, who acts with weak intention (`mṛdvyā cetanayā`). Such a person may perform wholesome or unwholesome action yet possess only `vijñapti`, not `avijñapti`. In indeterminate action the absence of avijñapti is even more straightforward, apart from specifically excepted action-courses connected with material merit-making (`aupadika-puṇyakriyā-vastu-karmapatha`).

The second explicitly stated case reverses the relation. A noble person may possess `avijñapti` alone and not `vijñapti`. Vasubandhu gives the case of an `āryapudgala` who, after transition to another birth, has not yet produced the relevant manifestation, or whose manifestation has subsequently been abandoned.

The point is therefore not merely that vijñapti and avijñapti are conceptually different. Their possession can actually come apart in both directions.

Vasubandhu then closes this local possession analysis: the arrangement of possession of vijñapti and avijñapti among those established in restraint, non-restraint, and the middle state has now been stated. The next question is how these restraints are acquired.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — opens the `catuṣkoṭika` concerning possession of vijñapti and avijñapti and supplies the two explicit cases in VAK 4.25.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explains the weak-intention `madhyastha` case and the noble-person case.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — closes the possession classification and introduces the next topic: acquisition of restraints.

## New Technical Terms

- **mṛdu-cetanā** — weak intention; **status:** previously anticipated but now explicitly decisive in this possession analysis; **function:** explains why a manifest act may fail to generate avijñapti.
- **samanvāgama** — possession / being endowed with; **status:** recurring Abhidharma technical relation; **function:** specifies whether vijñapti or avijñapti belongs to the continuum.
- **janmāntara-parivṛtti** — transition into another birth; **status:** new in this immediate sequence; **function:** explains how an āryapudgala may possess avijñapti before any new vijñapti has arisen.
- **aupadika-puṇyakriyā-vastu-karmapatha** — action-course belonging to material merit-making bases; **status:** compressed technical exception; **function:** limits the blanket statement about indeterminate action and avijñapti.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Case 1 — Vijñapti without avijñapti

```text
madhyastha
    + weak cetanā
    + wholesome or unwholesome action
        → vijñapti
        → no avijñapti
```

For indeterminate action, the absence of avijñapti is even more evident, subject to the stated exception.

The decisive point is that mere manifestation does not guarantee production of a continuing non-manifest determination.

### Case 2 — Avijñapti without vijñapti

```text
āryapudgala
    after transition to another birth
    before relevant vijñapti has arisen

or

āryapudgala
    whose vijñapti has been abandoned

        → avijñapti remains possessed
        → no vijñapti presently possessed
```

Thus non-manifest restraint is not dependent on simultaneous manifestation.

### Separation of the two

```text
vijñapti
    can occur without avijñapti

avijñapti
    can be possessed without vijñapti
```

Their relation is therefore not one of simple inseparability.

### Closure of the possession analysis

Vasubandhu states that the arrangement of `vijñapti-avijñapti-samanvāgama` among:

```text
saṃvara-stha
asaṃvara-stha
madhyastha
```

has now been given.

The next topic is acquisition (`lābha`) of the restraints.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā pass correctly saw the crossed relation, but the Bhāṣya sharpens both cases.

A better translation is:

> A middle-position person who acts with weak intention is endowed only with manifestation. A noble person whose manifestation has either not yet arisen after transition to another birth, or has been abandoned, is endowed with non-manifest action alone.

The second half should not be generalized merely as “a noble person without present outward action”; Vasubandhu supplies specific temporal circumstances.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.25 establishes a genuine independence relation:

```text
manifest occurrence
    does not entail
continuing non-manifest determination
```

and:

```text
continuing non-manifest determination
    does not entail
present manifest occurrence
```

The role of `cetanā` is also sharpened:

```text
weak cetanā
    → action may remain at the level of vijñapti
    → no avijñapti need be established
```

But the verse itself does not yet supply a general theory reducing avijñapti to cetanā. It only gives a condition under which the two modes of action come apart.

## Organon Contact Point

A restrained contact point is sufficient:

```text
occurrence
    ≠
persistent practical determination
```

and conversely:

```text
persistent determination
    ≠
current outward occurrence
```

The Bhāṣya thereby preserves distinct logical roles for manifestation and continuing karmic possession.
