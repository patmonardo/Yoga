# VAK_4.123_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.123**

*samāhitaṃ tu kuśalaṃ bhāvanā cittavāsanāt || 4.123 ||*

> Concentrated wholesome activity is cultivation, because it conditions the mind.

See: `VAK_4.123.md`.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 273|23–274|03:

> samāhitaṃ tu kuśalaṃ bhāvanā
>
> kimidaṃ samāhitaṃ nāma /
> samādhisvabhāvaṃ sahabhū yat /
> kimarthametat bhāvanetyucyate /
>
> cittavāsanāt // VAkK_4.123 //
>
> taddhi samāhitaṃ kuśalamatyarthaṃ cittaṃ vāsayati /
> guṇaistanmayīkaraṇātsaṃtateḥ /
>
> puṣpaistilabhāvanāvat /

Immediately afterward the Bhāṣya turns to the characteristic fruits of dāna, śīla, and bhāvanā.

## Natural Segmentation

1. What does `samāhita` mean here?
2. Definition: wholesome factors conjoined with samādhi.
3. Why is this called `bhāvanā`?
4. Because it intensely conditions or perfumes the mind.
5. The continuum is made of the nature of those qualities.
6. Analogy: sesame seeds perfumed by flowers.

## Literal Prose Translation

“Concentrated wholesome [activity] is cultivation.”

What is this which is called `samāhita`?

It is that which is conjoined with what has the nature of samādhi.

Why is this called cultivation?

“Because it conditions the mind.”

For concentrated wholesomeness intensely perfumes the mind, because it makes the continuum of the nature of those qualities—just as sesame seeds are perfumed by flowers.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya defines `bhāvanā` by function rather than by posture, ritual, or isolated mental occurrence.

```text
wholesome factors
    +
conjunction with samādhi
    ↓
samāhitaṃ kuśalam
    ↓
intense conditioning of citta
    ↓
continuum becomes of the nature of those qualities
    ↓
bhāvanā
```

The decisive phrase is:

`guṇais tanmayīkaraṇāt saṃtateḥ`

The continuum is made `tanmayī`, “of the nature of that,” through the qualities repeatedly and concentratively impressed upon it.

Thus bhāvanā is not merely the occurrence of a wholesome mental state. It is a concentrated wholesome activity whose causal effect is to reshape the continuing series itself.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is straightforward explanatory prose without an objection-response exchange.

## New Technical Terms

- **samādhisvabhāva** — that whose own-nature is samādhi; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** technical definition of what makes a wholesome state `samāhita`.
- **sahabhū** — co-arising, conjoined; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** specifies the relation of wholesome factors to samādhi.
- **cittavāsanā** — perfuming/conditioning of mind; **status:** rare in the Kārikās; **function:** definition of why concentrated wholesome activity is called bhāvanā.
- **tanmayīkaraṇa** — making something of the nature of that; **status:** Bhāṣya-specific/rare; **function:** explains the transformative effect of cultivation on the continuum.
- **saṃtati** — causal continuum/series; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** locus of transformation through cultivation.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

### 1. `Samāhita` is relational

Vasubandhu does not define `samāhita` merely as “concentrated” in a loose psychological sense.

He says:

`samādhisvabhāvaṃ sahabhū yat`

That which is co-arising with what has samādhi as its nature.

Thus the third merit-basis consists in wholesome mental factors integrated with samādhi.

### 2. Bhāvanā is defined by its effect on citta

The reason for the name is:

`cittavāsanāt`

because it conditions, impregnates, or perfumes the mind.

The metaphor is not ornamental. It supplies a causal model:

```text
present concentrated wholesomeness
    → leaves qualitative determination
      in the continuing series
```

### 3. The continuum becomes `tanmaya`

The strongest Bhāṣya phrase is:

`guṇais tanmayīkaraṇāt saṃtateḥ`

Cultivation makes the continuum “of the nature of” the wholesome qualities.

This is stronger than saying that the mind temporarily contains them.

```text
state occurs
    ≠ cultivation in the full sense

state repeatedly/concentratively conditions continuum
    → continuum acquires that quality
```

### 4. Sesame and flowers

`puṣpais tilabhāvanāvat`

Sesame seeds acquire the scent of flowers through sustained contact.

Likewise:

```text
flowers : sesame
=
wholesome qualities : mental continuum
```

The wholesome quality does not remain an external episode. It modifies the character of the series that receives it.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass Kārikā file incorrectly treats the first half of 4.123 as continuing the four qualities of purified discipline. The Bhāṣya source places those four qualities entirely under VAK 4.122 and begins 4.123 at:

`samāhitaṃ tu kuśalaṃ bhāvanā`

A more text-controlled translation is therefore:

> **Wholesome factors conjoined with concentration constitute cultivation, because they condition the mind.**

The Bhāṣya further specifies:

> They intensely perfume the mind, making the continuum of the nature of those qualities, as sesame seeds are perfumed by flowers.

## Relation to the Three Puṇyakriyāvastu

The sequence is now very clear:

```text
dāna
    transforms the relation to possession

śīla
    institutes and purifies restraint

bhāvanā
    transforms the mental continuum itself
```

The text then immediately assigns their characteristic dominant fruits:

```text
dāna
    → great enjoyment/resources

śīla
    → heaven, principally

bhāvanā
    → disconnection/liberation, principally
```

This latter doctrine belongs to the following verse-unit rather than VAK 4.123 itself.

## Light Organon Contact

Bhāvanā is a particularly exact case of recursive practical formation:

```text
citta produces concentrated wholesome activity
    ↓
that activity conditions citta
    ↓
future citta is more fully determined by those qualities
```

The instrument acts upon its own future mode of operation.

The Bhāṣya therefore gives a causal account of acquired practical form without invoking a permanent self: transformation belongs to the `saṃtati`, the continuing causal series.

The sesame analogy is especially apt for this: identity is not static substance but continuity capable of receiving and retaining qualitative determination.
