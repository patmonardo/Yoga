# VAK_4.122_bhasya

## Kārikā anchor

**VAK 4.122**  
**dauḥśīlyam aśubhaṃ rūpaṃ śīlaṃ tadviratir dvidhā |**

> Misconduct is unwholesome bodily-verbal form. Discipline is abstention from it, twofold; and also from what has been prohibited by the Buddha. Purified discipline, however, is fourfold in quality.

## Natural Segmentation

The passage has five movements:

1. define `dauḥśīlya` and `śīla`;
2. explain the twofold structure of restraint as `vijñapti` and `avijñapti`;
3. extend restraint beyond intrinsically unwholesome conduct to acts prohibited by the Buddha for one who has undertaken the training;
4. define the four qualities of purified discipline;
5. record two alternative analyses of purified discipline.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

The basis of meritorious activity consisting in giving, together with its connected discussion, has now been completed. The basis consisting in discipline must be explained.

Unwholesome bodily-verbal form is called misconduct. Abstention from that is discipline.

That abstention is twofold: the manifest action (`vijñapti`) by which one refrains, and the unmanifest (`avijñapti`) which is that continuing abstention.

Nor is discipline merely abstention from misconduct. It is also abstention from what has been prohibited by the Buddha. Thus even something that is not misconduct by its own nature—such as eating at the wrong time—becomes an object of twofold restraint when prohibited by the Blessed Buddha. For one who has undertaken the training, transgressing such a rule becomes misconduct.

Purified discipline has four qualities. It is not damaged by misconduct; it is not damaged by the causes of misconduct, namely greed and the other afflictions and secondary afflictions; it rests on the antidote to misconduct, because it is grounded in the establishments of mindfulness; and it does not rest upon a superior rebirth, because it is transformed toward nirvāṇa.

Some say purification has five causes: purity regarding the principal karmapathas, purity regarding their neighboring acts, freedom from damaging thoughts, being guarded by mindfulness, and being transformed toward nirvāṇa.

Others distinguish four kinds of discipline: discipline from fear, maintained from fear of livelihood, bad reputation, punishment, or bad destiny; discipline from expectation, motivated by craving for existence, enjoyment, or honor; discipline conforming to the factors of awakening, maintained by those with right view for the sake of liberation; and completely purified discipline, namely uncontaminated discipline, because it is stainless.

## New Technical Terms

- **dauḥśīlya** — misconduct; here unwholesome bodily-verbal form.  
  **Status:** previously encountered.  
  **Function:** definition of the opposed state.

- **virati** — abstention/restraint.  
  **Status:** previously encountered.  
  **Function:** definition of `śīla`.

- **vijñapti** — manifest bodily or verbal activity by which abstention is enacted.  
  **Status:** previously central in Chapter 4.  
  **Function:** manifest side of restraint.

- **avijñapti** — unmanifest continuing restraint.  
  **Status:** previously central in Chapter 4.  
  **Function:** continuing karmic form of abstention.

- **pratikṣipta** — prohibited, ruled out.  
  **Status:** rare in this technical role.  
  **Function:** marks training rules not intrinsically unwholesome.

- **akālabhojana** — eating at an improper time.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya example.  
  **Function:** example of an act not intrinsically misconduct but prohibited within adopted discipline.

- **samāttaśikṣa** — one who has undertaken the training.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya technical qualification.  
  **Function:** identifies the agent for whom violation of a Buddha-prohibited rule becomes misconduct.

- **dauḥśīlyahetu** — cause of misconduct.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation here.  
  **Function:** identifies greed and other afflictions as causal corruption of discipline.

- **smṛtyupasthāna** — establishment of mindfulness.  
  **Status:** previously encountered.  
  **Function:** antidotal basis supporting purified discipline.

- **nirvāṇapariṇāmita** — transformed/directed toward nirvāṇa.  
  **Status:** rare.  
  **Function:** distinguishes liberative discipline from discipline aimed at superior rebirth.

- **bhayaśīla** — discipline motivated by fear.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only classification here.  
  **Function:** alternative taxonomy.

- **āśāstiśīla** — discipline motivated by expectation/desire for future benefit.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only classification here.  
  **Function:** alternative taxonomy.

- **bodhyaṅgānulomaśīla** — discipline conforming to the factors of awakening.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only classification here.  
  **Function:** liberative but still conditioned discipline.

- **anāsravaśīla** — uncontaminated discipline.  
  **Status:** previously doctrinally familiar.  
  **Function:** completely purified discipline.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

### 1. `Śīla` is active restraint, not mere absence

The definition is:

```text
dauḥśīlya
    unwholesome bodily/verbal form

virati
    determinate abstention from it

śīla
    that abstention
```

The absence of a bad deed by accident is not yet the same thing as discipline. `Virati` is a positively constituted restraint.

### 2. Restraint has manifest and unmanifest form

The Bhāṣya explicitly interprets `dvidhā` through the Chapter 4 distinction:

```text
vijñapti
    the manifest bodily/verbal act by which one refrains

avijñapti
    the continuing unmanifest abstention
```

Thus the old `vijñapti / avijñapti` problem now appears in its most practical form: discipline is not only the visible refusal to transgress but also the continuing karmic determination established by that refusal.

### 3. Intrinsic wrong and training prohibition must be distinguished

The Bhāṣya separates:

```text
prakṛtyā dauḥśīlya
    misconduct by its nature

buddhena pratikṣipta
    conduct prohibited by the Buddha
```

The example is `akālabhojana`, eating at an improper time. It is not intrinsically misconduct, but for one who has undertaken the training, violation becomes misconduct relative to that adopted discipline.

This is not a confusion of convention with intrinsic evil. It is a distinction between:

```text
intrinsic ethical prohibition
and
rule-governed disciplinary prohibition
```

### 4. Purified discipline has four determinations

The four are best read as a progression:

```text
1. act-level purity
   not damaged by misconduct

2. causal purity
   not damaged by greed and other afflictions

3. antidotal grounding
   supported by the establishments of mindfulness

4. teleological transformation
   directed toward nirvāṇa, not superior rebirth
```

This moves from the visible deed inward to its causes and then forward to its end.

### 5. Purity is not merely external compliance

A person may refrain outwardly and yet remain driven by greed, fear, reputation, or desire for heavenly reward. The Bhāṣya therefore refuses to identify purified discipline with behavioral compliance alone.

The alternative fourfold classification makes this explicit:

```text
fear
    → restraint from fear of loss, blame, punishment, bad destiny

expectation
    → restraint for existence, enjoyment, honor

awakening-conformity
    → restraint for liberation

uncontaminated purity
    → stainless discipline itself
```

The same outward restraint can therefore belong to very different practical structures according to its motive and end.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation was broadly correct, but two points require revision.

First, `dvidhā` does not primarily mean two different classes of forbidden conduct. The Bhāṣya first explains it as the **two modes of restraint**:

```text
vijñapti + avijñapti
```

The verse then separately extends that twofold restraint from intrinsically unwholesome conduct to what the Buddha has prohibited.

Second, `viśuddhaṃ tu caturguṇam` is completed in this very Bhāṣya unit, not deferred to VAK 4.123.

A more exact translation is therefore:

> Misconduct is unwholesome bodily-verbal form; discipline is abstention from it, and that abstention is twofold. It is also abstention from what the Buddha has prohibited. Purified discipline, however, possesses four qualities.

## Light Organon Contact

This passage gives perhaps the cleanest Chapter 4 distinction between **rule, execution, disposition, and end**:

```text
visible restraint
    vijñapti

continuing practical determination
    avijñapti

causal purification
    freedom from kleśa/upakleśa

counter-principle
    smṛtyupasthāna

final orientation
    nirvāṇa
```

Purified discipline is therefore not merely inhibition. It is an action-system whose output, generator, counter-principle, and end have all been transformed.

The distinction between intrinsic misconduct and adopted training-rule is equally important. The Kośa does not collapse ethics into convention: some acts are unwholesome by nature; others become binding determinations because a training has been deliberately undertaken.
