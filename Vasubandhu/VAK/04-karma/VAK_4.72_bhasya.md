# VAK_4.72_bhasya — Completion Across Death and Collective Agency

## Kārikā

**VAK 4.72**

```text
samaṃ prāk ca mṛtasyāsti na maulo 'nyāśrayodayāt /
senādiṣv ekakāryatvāt sarve kartṛvad anvitāḥ // 4.72 //
```

Corrected translation in light of the Bhāṣya:

> For one who has died simultaneously [with the victim] or before [the victim], there is no principal karmic course, because another support arises. In armies and similar collectives, because there is one common undertaking, all are connected with the act like the immediate doer.

## Literal Prose Translation

Suppose someone has arranged for another person to be killed and then dies either at the same time as that person or before that person. Does the principal karmic course arise for him or not?

For one who has died simultaneously or beforehand, there is no principal karmic course.

Hence it is said that there can be a case in which one performs the preparation and the result is brought to completion, yet one is not touched by the fault of killing. This occurs when the person who arranged the destruction dies before the victim or at the same time as the victim.

Why is this so? As long as the victim is alive, the killer is not yet connected with the fault of killing; nor is a killer who has died simultaneously connected with it.

This is because another support has arisen. The support through which the preparation was performed has ceased, and an entirely different support, belonging to another membership-series (`nikāyasabhāga`), has arisen. Since that later support was not the one that undertook the preparation, it is not appropriate for it to be connected with the karmic course.

Now suppose that in an army, a hunting party, a raiding force, or similar group, many people jointly set out in order to kill others, while only one of them actually performs the killing. Who becomes connected with that killing?

In armies and similar collectives, because there is one common undertaking, all are connected just like the immediate doer.

Just as the immediate performer is connected with the act, so all are connected because they share one undertaking. In effect, they are mutual instigators of one another.

What about someone compelled by force to go along? Such a person too is ordinarily connected, except for one who goes having firmly resolved: “Even for the sake of my life I will not kill a living being.”

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The principal killing-course occurs only at the death of the victim

The Bhāṣya presupposes the distinction already developed in 4.67–4.68:

```text
preparation / assault
    ≠
principal completion
```

The `maula-karmapatha` of killing arises only when the victim actually dies.

Therefore, while the victim remains alive:

```text
preparation may exist
assault may exist
causal efficacy may exist

but

maula-prāṇātipāta
    has not yet arisen
```

### 2. Death of the killer before completion blocks principal attribution

If the killer dies before the victim, the support that performed the preparation has ceased before the principal course arises.

The Bhāṣya does not transfer that principal course to the new support after rebirth:

```text
support A
    performs prayoga
    ↓
    dies
    ↓
support B
    arises in another nikāyasabhāga
    ↓
victim dies

result:
    no maula-karmapatha for support B
```

The decisive argument is:

```text
prayoktṛtva
    belongs to support A

support B
    was not the preparatory agent

therefore
    the principal karmapatha is not attributed to B
```

### 3. Simultaneous death also fails to establish the principal course

The Bhāṣya explicitly adds:

```text
nāpi sahamṛtasya hantuḥ
```

Nor does the killer who dies simultaneously with the victim become connected with the completed fault.

This corrects the first-pass interpretation of the Kārikā. The phrase:

```text
samaṃ prāk ca mṛtasyāsti na maulaḥ
```

must be construed as:

> For one who dies simultaneously or beforehand, **there is no principal [karmic course]**.

It must not be read as saying that the principal course “exists” in those cases.

### 4. Temporal discontinuity restricts attribution

The rule is therefore:

```text
same causal project
    does not guarantee
same bearer of principal karmic completion
```

Causal influence can survive the death of the initiating person, while the completed `maula-karmapatha` does not simply migrate to a later support.

This is a precise Sarvāstivāda account of karmic attribution without appeal to a permanent agent-substance.

### 5. Collective practical unity expands attribution

The second half reverses the direction of the problem.

Instead of one project extending across two supports, Vasubandhu considers many supports participating simultaneously in one project:

```text
many participants
    +
one killing-project
    +
one actual executor
```

The answer is:

```text
sarve kartṛvad anvitāḥ
```

All are connected like the immediate doer.

The ground is:

```text
ekakāryatva
    unity of undertaking / one common operation
```

Thus physical execution is not the sole criterion of agency.

### 6. The participants are mutual instigators

The explanation is especially strong:

```text
arthato hi te 'nyonyaṃ prayoktāro bhavanti
```

“In effect, they are mutual instigators of one another.”

The collective is therefore not treated as a mysterious super-agent. Responsibility is distributed because each participant practically contributes to and authorizes the same undertaking.

### 7. Coercion does not automatically remove participation

The Bhāṣya considers someone forcibly taken along with the group. Even such a person is ordinarily connected with the collective act.

The exception is ethically stringent: one who has firmly resolved beforehand that even to save his own life he will not kill a living being.

Thus the decisive distinction is not mere physical presence but practical commitment:

```text
forced presence alone
    does not necessarily remove implication

firm refusal of the killing-project
    blocks participation in its practical unity
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu — exposition and reconstruction of the Vaibhāṣika karmic rule.

The cited formula concerning preparation, completion of the result, and absence of the killing-fault is presented as an authoritative doctrinal statement and then explained by Vasubandhu.

No independent objector is developed in this unit; the questions function primarily as scholastic prompts.

## New Technical Terms

### `anyāśraya`

- **Meaning:** another support; the psycho-physical support arising after the prior life-support has ceased.
- **Status:** important local refinement of `āśraya`.
- **Function:** explains why principal karmic completion does not transfer after death of the initiating support.

### `nikāyasabhāga`

- **Meaning:** membership-series / class-continuity associated with a particular mode of rebirth.
- **Status:** previously encountered in the Kośa system; newly operative here in the argument about postmortem support-difference.
- **Function:** marks the difference between the support that undertook the preparation and the later support that arises after death.

### `ekakāryatva`

- **Meaning:** having one common undertaking or one practical operation.
- **Status:** newly decisive in this karmapatha analysis.
- **Function:** criterion for distributed karmic attribution in collective action.

### `prayoktṛ`

- **Meaning:** instigator, one who sets an action in motion or causes it to be undertaken.
- **Status:** operative refinement of the prior `prayoga` vocabulary.
- **Function:** explains both the lack of transfer across supports and the reciprocal implication of members of a collective undertaking.

### `samanvāgata`

- **Meaning:** connected with, endowed with, karmically possessing the determination.
- **Status:** established Abhidharma term, newly important in the collective-agency argument.
- **Function:** states who bears the completed karmic course.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.72 establishes two apparently opposite attribution rules.

### Diachronic restriction

```text
one causal project
    +
support discontinuity before completion
    →
no transfer of the principal karmapatha
```

### Synchronic extension

```text
many participants
    +
one common undertaking
    →
all implicated like the immediate doer
```

The common principle is not substance-identity but **practical relation to the completing process**.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass Kārikā translation requires correction.

Earlier:

> “The principal course exists when the death occurs beforehand or simultaneously...”

Bhāṣya-controlled correction:

> **For one who has died simultaneously with the victim or before the victim, there is no principal karmic course, because another support arises.**

The Bhāṣya is explicit enough that this should be treated as a genuine correction rather than an optional interpretive alternative.

The existing Kārikā file should therefore be flagged for the later Super Kārikās / cleanup pass.

## Logical Determination

This verse gives us an unusually precise pair of relations:

```text
diachronic identity:
    causal continuity
    ≠ bearer continuity

synchronic agency:
    bodily execution
    ≠ whole practical agency
```

The first prevents karmic attribution from simply following a causal chain into a new support.

The second prevents karmic attribution from collapsing into a purely physical “whose hand did it?” criterion.

So agency is located neither in a permanent substance nor in an isolated bodily event. It is determined through the articulated practical process.

## Light Organon Contact Point

A modest but textually earned formulation is:

```text
Attribution requires
    continuity of the relevant practical bearer
        OR
    participation in one practical undertaking
```

The verse therefore distinguishes two forms of practical unity:

```text
unity through time
    limited by support-transition

unity across persons
    established by common undertaking
```

This is a strong model of distributed agency without a substantial collective self.

The most important result is the reciprocal contrast:

```text
support difference can break attribution

while

practical unity can extend attribution
```

That is Vasubandhu's own argumentative structure before any stronger Organon interpretation is added.
