# VAK 4.118 — Bhāṣya Analysis

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.118**

*mātṛpitṛglānadhārmakathikebhyo 'ntyajanmane |*  
*bodhisattvāya cāmeyā anāryebhyo 'pi dakṣiṇā || 4.118 ||*

> An offering to mother, father, a sick person, a Dharma speaker, or the Bodhisattva in his final birth is immeasurable in fruit, even when these recipients are not noble ones.

See: [VAK_4.118.md](./VAK_4.118.md)

## Natural Segmentation

The passage has three movements:

1. five non-noble fields can nevertheless make an offering immeasurable in fruit;
2. `antyajanman` is defined as the Bodhisattva in the final existence (`caramabhavika`);
3. the Dharma speaker is assigned to the `upakārin` field and his beneficent function is explained at length.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

An offering to mother, father, a sick person, a Dharma speaker, and a Bodhisattva in the final birth is immeasurable, even when they are non-noble persons.

An offering to these five, even when they are in the state of ordinary persons (`pṛthagjana`), is immeasurable with respect to its fruit.

Here, the Bodhisattva in the final birth is one who is in his final existence (`caramabhavika`).

Into which of the four kinds of distinguished field should the Dharma speaker be placed?

Into the class of benefactors (`upakārin`).

For he is a great good friend (`mahākalyāṇamitra`): for beings blinded by ignorance he is a giver of the eye of prajñā; he reveals what is even and uneven; he turns beings toward the uncontaminated Dharma-body; in brief, he performs the work of a Buddha.

---

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 4.117 established four ways a recipient-field can be distinguished:

```text
gati          — mode of rebirth
duḥkha        — suffering
upakāritva    — beneficence
guṇa          — qualities
```

VAK 4.118 now demonstrates that **noble status is not required for immeasurable field-value**.

The five concrete fields are:

```text
mother
father
sick person
Dharma speaker
final-birth Bodhisattva
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly states:

```text
ebhyaḥ pañcabhyaḥ pṛthagjanabhūtebhyo 'pi
    even when these five are ordinary persons

aprameyā phalato dakṣiṇā
    the offering is immeasurable in fruit
```

Thus:

```text
ārya-status
    is one possible source of field excellence

but

field excellence
    is not reducible to ārya-status
```

The recipient may be exceptional because of prior benefit, present suffering, Dharma-function, or assured future Buddhahood.

---

## The Five Fields

### 1. Mother

The mother is paradigmatically an `upakārin`, a prior benefactor. The karmic significance of the gift includes the historical relation by which the giver's life and support were made possible.

### 2. Father

The father is likewise a benefactor-field. As in the earlier ānantarya discussion, the relation is not exhausted by the recipient's present qualities; past constitutive benefit remains practically active.

### 3. Sick Person

The sick person is distinguished through `duḥkha`. Here the field is powerful because actual suffering creates an urgent capacity to be benefited.

```text
present deprivation
    → increased significance of assistance
```

### 4. Dharma Speaker

The Bhāṣya explicitly asks which of the four field-types contains the Dharma speaker and answers:

```text
upakāripakṣe
    in the benefactor class
```

This is the major doctrinal determination of the passage.

The Dharma speaker is a benefactor because he gives beings something more fundamental than material support: the practical conditions of seeing and traversing the Path.

### 5. Final-birth Bodhisattva

`antyajanman` is glossed:

```text
caramabhavika
    one in the final existence
```

The phrase therefore refers to the Bodhisattva whose present life will culminate directly in Buddhahood.

His field-value is future-oriented but not indeterminate: the future Buddha-function is already fixed within the present continuum.

---

## The Dharma Speaker as Benefactor

The praise of the Dharma speaker is unusually strong.

### `mahākalyāṇamitra`

The speaker is a **great good friend**.

The relation is not merely informative. It is practical assistance directed toward liberation.

### `avidyāndhāyāṃ prajāyāṃ prajñācakṣuṣo dātā`

> giver of the eye of prajñā to beings blinded by ignorance

This is an exact inversion of ordinary blindness:

```text
avidyā
    → blindness

Dharma teaching
    → prajñā-cakṣus
    → eye of discriminative insight
```

The speaker gives not an external possession but a principle of seeing.

### `samaviṣamasya prakāśayitā`

The speaker reveals what is even and uneven: the passable and impassable, correct and incorrect, wholesome and unwholesome directions of practice.

The Dharma speaker therefore performs a discriminative function:

```text
Path / non-Path
right / wrong
favorable / obstructive
```

### `anāsravasya dharmakāyasyātinivartayitā`

The speaker turns beings toward the uncontaminated Dharma-body.

Whatever textual difficulty remains in the electronic form, the doctrinal function is clear: Dharma teaching redirects the continuum toward the uncontaminated Dharma-order rather than merely improving worldly conditions.

### `samāsato buddhakṛtyasya kartā`

> in brief, he performs the work of a Buddha

This is the climax of the passage.

The Dharma speaker is an exceptional benefactor-field because the activity of teaching extends beyond the immediate dyad of donor and recipient. Supporting the teacher supports an activity whose object is the liberation of many beings.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — question concerning the fourfold field classification and the explanatory answer.

No competing school attribution is required in this passage.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **caramabhavika** — one in the final existence; gloss of `antyajanman`.  
  **Status:** rare / Bhāṣya clarification.  
  **Function:** defines the final-birth Bodhisattva.

- **mahākalyāṇamitra** — great good friend; one whose relation to beings is profoundly beneficial for the Path.  
  **Status:** rare in the present Karma discussion.  
  **Function:** characterizes the Dharma speaker as `upakārin`.

- **prajñācakṣus** — eye of prajñā; capacity for discriminative seeing given through Dharma instruction.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local passage.  
  **Function:** explains the beneficent function of Dharma teaching.

- **buddhakṛtya** — work/function of a Buddha.  
  **Status:** rare / Bhāṣya emphasis.  
  **Function:** identifies Dharma exposition as participation in Buddha-function.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The central result is:

```text
immeasurable field-value
    ≠ noble status alone
```

A recipient can possess immeasurable field-value through different relational determinations:

```text
parents
    → historical beneficence

sick person
    → present suffering

Dharma speaker
    → liberating beneficence

final-birth Bodhisattva
    → assured future Buddha-function
```

The Dharma speaker is especially important because field-value can derive from **what the recipient does for others**, not merely from what the recipient intrinsically is.

Thus:

```text
recipient quality
    +
recipient relation
    +
recipient function
    +
recipient causal reach
        ↓
field determination
```

This considerably enriches the meaning of `kṣetra`.

---

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass grouping of the final expression is confirmed:

```text
antyajanmane bodhisattvāya
    to the Bodhisattva in the final birth
```

It is one recipient, not two.

The Bhāṣya also confirms that `anāryebhyo 'pi` should be understood technically:

> even when these five recipients are still ordinary persons / non-noble beings.

Recommended translation:

> **An offering to mother, father, a sick person, a Dharma speaker, or the Bodhisattva in his final birth is immeasurable in fruit, even when these recipients are non-noble persons.**

---

## Light Organon Contact

The recipient-field is now fully temporal and functional.

```text
parents
    → past benefit

sick person
    → present negation requiring relief

Dharma speaker
    → present transmission with distributed future effects

final-birth Bodhisattva
    → future Buddhahood already determining the present field
```

Especially in the Dharma speaker, the recipient is not merely the terminus of a gift. The recipient becomes a **middle through which benefit propagates**:

```text
donor
    → supports Dharma speaker
        → Dharma is taught
            → many beings acquire prajñā and Path-direction
```

This is why the speaker belongs to `upakāritva`: the field is determined by causal beneficence.

The strongest phrase is therefore also the simplest:

```text
samāsato buddhakṛtyasya kartā
    in brief, one who performs Buddha-work
```
