# VAK_4.12 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
pravartake śubhādau hi syāt tridhāpy anuvartakam |
tulyaṃ muneḥ śubhaṃ yāvan nobhayaṃ tu vipākajam ||
```

**Working translation:** When the initiating cognition is wholesome and so forth, the continuing cognition may nevertheless be any of the three ethical kinds. In the Sage, the continuator corresponds to the initiator, with a special restriction concerning wholesome action. Neither function is performed by maturation-born cognition.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.12.md`.

### Source cautions

The e-text reads `vipāpkajam` at the verse line; the sense and following prose clearly require `vipākajam`. The prose also reads `kuślaṃ` once for expected `kuśalaṃ`. These are retained in the source excerpt and treated as obvious transmission/typing errors rather than silently normalized.

## Natural Argument

The passage begins from the distinction established in VAK_4.10–4.11 between an initiating cognition (`pravartaka`) and a contemporaneous continuing cognition (`anuvartaka`). Vasubandhu asks whether the ethical quality of the second must always match that of the first. The answer is no: there is no invariant correspondence.

If the initiator is wholesome, the continuator may be wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate. The same threefold possibility holds when the initiator is unwholesome or indeterminate. The action-series therefore need not preserve one ethical quality throughout its performance.

The Buddha is then introduced as a special case. For the Blessed One, the continuing cognition generally corresponds to the initiating cognition: wholesome follows wholesome, and indeterminate follows indeterminate. Yet the relation is asymmetric. Even with an indeterminate initiator, a wholesome continuator may occur; but a wholesome initiator is never followed by an indeterminate continuator.

A school disagreement follows. Some other schools claim that Buddhas have no indeterminate cognition at all because their continua are exclusively wholesome and always concentrated. The Vaibhāṣika answer is more qualified: the cited description of the Buddha as always concentrated means that his mind does not involuntarily scatter, not that every cognition is wholesome. Buddhas still possess indeterminate maturation-born cognitions and certain magical-creation cognitions.

Finally, the Bhāṣya returns to the general classification. Mental cognition abandoned by cultivation may be wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate and may function as either initiator or continuator. Maturation-born cognition, however, is excluded from both roles because it proceeds without deliberate constructive activity (`nirabhisaṃskāravāhitva`).

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks whether initiator and continuator must match, rejects an absolute rule, and explains the general threefold variation.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / exegetical presentation** — states the special relation in the Buddha and introduces the doctrinal problem concerning indeterminate cognition.

**Voice: Other school (`nikāyāntarīyāḥ`)** — Buddhas have no indeterminate cognition because their continua are exclusively wholesome and always concentrated.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — the scriptural description of continuous concentration excludes involuntary distraction, not all indeterminate cognition; Buddhas retain maturation-born and magical-creation indeterminate cognitions.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — maturation-born cognition is neither initiating nor continuing because it flows without deliberate construction.

## New Technical Terms

- **ekānta** — invariance / necessity without exception
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit in this argument
  - **function:** rejection of a universal identity between initiator and continuator

- **kuśalaikatāna** — a continuum exclusively oriented toward the wholesome
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only doctrinal characterization in this passage
  - **function:** other-school description of the Buddha

- **avīsaraṇa / avisaraṇa** — non-scattering, non-diffusion of mind
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanation of cited scripture
  - **function:** Vaibhāṣika reinterpretation of “always concentrated”

- **nirmāṇa-citta** — cognition connected with magical creation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only example here
  - **function:** counterexample to the claim that all Buddha-cognition is wholesome

- **nirabhisaṃskāravāhitva** — proceeding without deliberate constructive activity
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only causal explanation
  - **function:** reason maturation-born cognition cannot initiate or continue action

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse does not state that one action-series has one fixed ethical quality. Rather:

```text
pravartaka
    kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta

anuvartaka
    independently:
    kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta
```

The initiating condition determines the onset of the deed; it does not mechanically determine every cognition that accompanies its execution.

For the Buddha, ordinary variation is restricted:

```text
kuśala pravartaka
    → kuśala anuvartaka

avyākṛta pravartaka
    → avyākṛta or kuśala anuvartaka

never:
kuśala pravartaka
    → avyākṛta anuvartaka
```

The Bhāṣya also preserves a doctrinal dispute concerning whether Buddhas possess any indeterminate cognition. Vasubandhu does not erase the disagreement; he reports the other-school view and the Vaibhāṣika response.

The final exclusion of `vipākaja` is functional, not merely ethical. Maturation-born cognition is a resultant flow. Because it lacks deliberate constructive activation, it cannot play either causal role within present action-production.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā-pass translation requires refinement in two places.

First, `tulyaṃ muneḥ` is not merely a vague correspondence “where wholesome action is concerned.” It introduces a special Buddha-case in which continuator and initiator generally correspond, followed by an explicit asymmetry concerning wholesome and indeterminate cognition.

Second, `nobhayaṃ tu vipākajam` is best read causally rather than only classificatorily:

> Maturation-born cognition functions as neither initiator nor continuator, because it flows without deliberate constructive activity.

## Logical Determination

VAK_4.12 differentiates causal continuity from qualitative identity.

```text
one action-series
    requires causal connection

but does not require
    invariant ethical identity
    across every cognitive moment
```

The Buddha-case then introduces constrained variation rather than simple identity.

The final exclusion of `vipākaja` establishes a further logical distinction:

```text
resultant cognition
    receives / manifests prior karmic result

productive cognition
    initiates or sustains present action
```

Thus receiving a causal result and actively participating in present production are distinct functions.

## Organon Contact Point

The passage suggests a clean distinction between determination of a process and the qualities of its successive moments:

```text
pravartaka
    sets the course in motion

anuvartaka
    maintains the course in execution

but:
maintenance ≠ qualitative identity
```

The `vipākaja` exclusion is particularly useful: a determination may belong to a causal series as result without thereby functioning as an active ground of a new action. Deeper reconstruction should wait for the later special study.
