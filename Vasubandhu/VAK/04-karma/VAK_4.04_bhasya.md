# VAK_4.04 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
trividhāmalarūpoktivṛddhyakurvatpathādibhiḥ /
kṣaṇādūrdhvamavijñaptiḥ kāmāptātītabhūtajā // VAK_4.04 //
```

**Working translation:** Non-manifest action is maintained on such grounds as the teaching of threefold form and pure form, the increase of merit, and the completion of an action-path when one is not oneself presently acting. Beyond the first moment, desire-domain avijñapti arises in dependence upon past great material determinations.

Existing Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.04.md`.

### Source cautions

The e-text is not a critical edition. Several compounds and quoted sūtra passages are compressed, and the source contains minor orthographic irregularities. They are retained rather than silently normalized.

The first pāda does not itself state the Sautrāntika objections. It is the compressed heading for the Vaibhāṣika proof-dossier; the Bhāṣya then preserves the Sautrāntika counter-interpretation at considerable length.

## Natural Argument

The discussion begins from the Sautrāntika denial that avijñapti exists as a separate real entity (`dravyataḥ`). The Vaibhāṣika responds by asking how its existence is known and gives a cumulative argument from scripture and doctrine.

First, the sūtras speak of three kinds of form: visible-resistant form, invisible-resistant form, and invisible-non-resistant form. The Vaibhāṣika identifies avijñapti as the indispensable instance of the third kind. The sūtras also speak of non-contaminated form; again, the Vaibhāṣika argues that without avijñapti there is no suitable form that is both non-contaminated and invisible/non-resistant.

Second comes `vṛddhi`, increase. A donor's merit is said to increase continuously while walking, sleeping, standing, or waking, even when the donor's present mind is elsewhere. The Vaibhāṣika concludes that some non-manifest karmic factor must continue beyond the initial act.

Third comes `akurvat-patha`: a person who causes another to perform an act can complete a karmapatha even though the instigator is not personally performing the final bodily deed. The command itself cannot be the principal action-path before the commissioned act has occurred. Avijñapti is therefore invoked to account for the completed path.

Further arguments follow. The dharma-sphere is described as invisible and non-resistant without simply being called immaterial. The noble eightfold path contains right speech, right action, and right livelihood even while the practitioner is absorbed and not speaking, acting, or seeking livelihood. Prātimokṣa restraint continues after the moment of undertaking even when the monk's mind is elsewhere. Restraint is also called a `setu`, a bridge or barrier against misconduct. The Vaibhāṣika reads all these as requiring a real avijñapti.

The Sautrāntika then answers the entire dossier rather than merely denying the conclusion.

For threefold and non-contaminated form, Yogācāra interpretations are introduced: meditative concentration itself can generate a subtle form that is invisible because it is not an object of the eye and non-resistant because it does not obstruct space. In non-contaminated samādhi such form can be called non-contaminated. Thus these scriptural categories need not refer to avijñapti.

The answer to the increase of merit is more important. The Sautrāntika invokes the teaching of earlier teachers (`pūrvācāryāḥ`). The donor's continuum has been cultivated by the volition of giving. As the recipient actually enjoys the gift, the recipient's qualities and the benefit received condition a subtle transformation in the donor's continuum even when the donor's present mind is elsewhere. This transformation makes that continuum increasingly capable of producing a larger future result.

Thus:

```text
dāna-cetanā
    cultivates the continuum
        ↓
recipient actually benefits
        ↓
subtle transformation of donor-continuum
    saṃtati-pariṇāma-viśeṣa
        ↓
greater capacity for future fruit
```

The same architecture explains karmapatha through another person. When the commissioned action is completed, a subtle transformation arises in the instigator's continuum. That transformation is capable of producing a greater future result and is designated `karmapatha` by transferring the name of the cause to its effect (`kārye kāraṇopacāra`).

The Vaibhāṣika presses the obvious objection: if the Sautrāntika accepts an imperceptible transformation of the continuum, why object to imperceptible avijñapti? The Sautrāntika's answer is precise. The objection is not to invisibility. It is to introducing a completely separate dharma, distinct from mind and body, at the completion of action. A transformation of the same causal continuum is intelligible because future fruit arises from the continuum of citta and caittas; a separately generated material dharma is not required.

The eightfold path and prātimokṣa are then reconstructed in the same way. A practitioner acquires an appropriate disposition and basis through the path such that, after emerging from samādhi, wrong speech/action/livelihood no longer occur and right forms occur. The names of the corresponding path factors can be applied by causal or sign-based designation. Likewise prātimokṣa restraint can consist in the volitional undertaking that restrains body and speech, together with recollection and habituation of that undertaking. Vasubandhu preserves a sharp Sautrāntika counterexample: if avijñapti itself automatically blocked misconduct, a person whose recollection had been lost could never break a training rule.

The Vaibhāṣika closes the controversy abruptly: enough elaboration; avijñapti is indeed another real entity (`dravyāntaram avijñaptirūpam`). The discussion then shifts from proving its existence to determining its material basis.

Avijñapti does not arise from the very same material aggregate that produces manifest action. The same causal complex cannot appropriately produce both a coarse and a subtle result. After the first moment, desire-domain avijñapti arises dependent on past great material determinations. Those past material determinations function as its `āśraya`; the presently existing great elements of the body function as `saṃniśraya`. Vasubandhu compares this to a wheel: the initial hand-impulse sets it in motion, while the ground provides the continuing support for its rolling.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Sautrāntika position** — denies avijñapti as a real separate entity at the opening and later supplies the extended alternative account through continuum-transformation.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — presents the cumulative proofs for real avijñapti and closes by insisting that it exists as a distinct real entity.

**Voice: Yogācāra position** — introduced within the Sautrāntika reply to reinterpret invisible/non-resistant and non-contaminated form as form generated through samādhi.

**Voice: earlier teachers (`pūrvācāryāḥ`)** — cited for the theory that giving-volition cultivates a continuum which subsequently undergoes subtle transformation as the gift benefits the recipient.

**Voice: uncertain / interlocutor** — several objections and questions within the debate are not securely assignable beyond their dialectical function.

## New Technical Terms

- **dravyataḥ** — as a real entity, substantially
  - **status:** technically operative in the Bhāṣya controversy
  - **function:** distinguishes Vaibhāṣika real existence from Sautrāntika denial

- **saṃtati-pariṇāma-viśeṣa** — specific transformation of the continuum
  - **status:** major Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression
  - **function:** Sautrāntika alternative to a separate avijñapti entity for karmic persistence and maturation

- **bhāvita-saṃtati** — cultivated / conditioned continuum
  - **status:** introduced in the explanation of merit growth
  - **function:** identifies the continuum as modified by prior volition

- **kārye kāraṇopacāra** — figurative application of the cause-name to the effect
  - **status:** explicit logical-semantic principle
  - **function:** explains why the transformed continuum can be called `karmapatha`

- **dravyāntara** — a distinct real entity
  - **status:** central ontological term
  - **function:** marks precisely what the Sautrāntika refuses and the Vaibhāṣika affirms

- **āśraya** — supporting basis
  - **status:** sharpened here for avijñapti
  - **function:** past great material determinations function as the producing basis

- **saṃniśraya** — continuing support / sustaining basis
  - **status:** new distinction in this passage
  - **function:** present bodily great elements sustain the already-produced avijñapti

- **pravṛtti / anuvṛtti** — initiation / continuation
  - **status:** explanatory pair
  - **function:** distinguishes the causal roles of past producing material factors and present sustaining material factors

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK_4.04 is not merely a list of reasons for avijñapti. It exposes two rival theories of karmic continuity.

The Vaibhāṣika theory is:

```text
manifest or meditative action
    ↓
produces avijñapti-rūpa
    ↓
avijñapti exists as a distinct real dharma
    ↓
it continues beyond the manifest moment
    ↓
it supports restraint, karmapatha, merit-growth, and path factors
```

The Sautrāntika theory is structurally different:

```text
cetanā / completed action
    ↓
conditions the same psycho-physical continuum
    ↓
saṃtati-pariṇāma-viśeṣa
    ↓
continuum acquires a new causal capacity
    ↓
future fruit
```

The dispute is therefore not between continuity and no continuity. Both sides require a real causal connection extending beyond the momentary manifest deed. The dispute concerns **what kind of determination carries that connection**.

```text
Vaibhāṣika:
    distinct non-manifest material dharma

Sautrāntika:
    transformation of the causal continuum itself
```

This is the decisive clarification of the first-pāda compound. `vṛddhi` and `akurvat-patha` are not simply descriptive properties of avijñapti; they are major evidential battlegrounds in the dispute over whether a distinct avijñapti entity must be admitted.

The second half of the verse then specifies the Vaibhāṣika material mechanics. Desire-domain avijñapti beyond the first moment is generated from past great elements. The producing basis and the sustaining support are not identical:

```text
past mahābhūtas
    = āśraya
    = cause of initiation / pravṛtti

present bodily mahābhūtas
    = saṃniśraya
    = condition of continuation / anuvṛtti
```

The wheel analogy prevents us from flattening this into simple material persistence. The initial impulse and the continuing support have different causal functions.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Substantial clarification required.**

The first-pass translation treated the first pāda as if the grounds simply established avijñapti. The Bhāṣya shows that it is specifically a compressed Vaibhāṣika proof-list inside a controversy.

A sharper translation is:

> [Its existence is argued] from the teaching of threefold and non-contaminated form, from increase [of merit], from the action-path of one not personally acting, and from related grounds. Beyond the first moment, desire-domain avijñapti is generated from past great material determinations.

The bracketed phrase should be understood as doctrinally supplied by the Bhāṣya rather than grammatically explicit in the verse.

`kāmāptātītabhūtajā` is now secure in scope: after the first moment, **desire-domain** avijñapti arises by taking past great material determinations as its producing basis.

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya generate a complete opposition of explanations:

```text
Problem:
How can a momentary act remain causally effective?

Vaibhāṣika answer:
manifest act
    → distinct avijñapti-rūpa
    → persistence of karmic determination

Sautrāntika answer:
manifest/volitional act
    → transformation of the same continuum
    → altered future-producing capacity
```

The Sautrāntika does not deny mediation beyond the moment. It denies the need to posit a **separate bearer** of that mediation.

This is why `saṃtati-pariṇāma-viśeṣa` is philosophically important. It gives a genetic account:

```text
prior action
    does not remain as an enduring thing

rather:
prior action changes what the continuum is capable of producing next
```

The causal identity lies in the transformed sequence, not in the persistence of a self-identical material token.

The Vaibhāṣika answer, however, is not simply crude substantialism. Its own model distinguishes genesis from maintenance:

```text
āśraya
    produces

saṃniśraya
    sustains
```

Thus even the realist account is dynamically articulated rather than being a single inert substrate.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage gives us one of the clearest examples yet of the Bhāṣya as **Reflection** rather than mere expansion.

The Kārikā gives the determination `avijñapti`. The Bhāṣya forces that determination through its opposite explanations until the real question becomes visible:

```text
What must remain
when nothing numerically identical remains?
```

The Sautrāntika answer is especially significant for the Rational Kośa because it places continuity in **genetic transformation of a continuum**, not in a static hidden thing. But the textual discipline matters: that is the Sautrāntika alternative preserved by Vasubandhu here, while the Vaibhāṣika explicitly retains avijñapti as `dravyāntara`.

The living opposition is therefore:

```text
separate non-manifest determination
        ↔
continuum transformed by its own prior activity
```

Neither side permits the manifest moment to stand alone. The controversy concerns the middle through which action becomes future efficacy.
