# VAK 4.101 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `cakrabhedaḥ sa ca mato jambūdvīpe navādibhiḥ | karmabhedas triṣu dvīpeṣv aṣṭabhir adhikaiś ca saḥ || 4.101 ||`

**Best current translation:** That schism is held to be a division of the Dharma-wheel; it occurs in Jambūdvīpa through nine or more. Division of communal action occurs in the three continents through eight or more.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.101.md](./VAK_4.101.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
yo 'yaṃ saṃghabheda uktaḥ

cakrabhedaḥ sa ca mataḥ

dharmacakraṃ hi tadā bhagavato bhinnaṃ bhavati /
mārgapravṛttiviṣṭhāpanāt /
ata eva cakrādaścocyate saṃghabhedaśca /

kva punaścakrabhedo bhavati /

jambūdvīpe

nānyeṣu dvīpeṣu /
katibhirbhikṣubhiḥ /

navādibhiḥ /

navādiṃ kṛtvā /
pareṇāniyamaḥ /
aṣṭau bhikṣayaḥ saṃgho bhavati /
navamo bhettā /
avaśyaṃ hi saṃghena dvayoḥ pakṣayoḥ sthātavyam /
evaṃ bhinno bhavati /

anyastu saṃghabhedaḥ karmabhedād bhavati /
yadyekasīmāyāṃ vyagrāḥ karma kurvanti /
sa caiṣa

karmabhedas triṣu dvīpeṣu

yeṣveva śāsanam /
katibhirbhikṣubhiḥ

aṣṭābhir adhikaiś ca saḥ // VAkK_4.101 //
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya distinguishes two structurally different forms of Saṅgha division:

1. `cakrabheda` — rupture of the Dharma-wheel itself through obstruction of the Path's functioning;
2. `karmabheda` — rupture of communal action when factions within one boundary perform Saṅgha acts separately.

The verse then specifies the locus and minimum numerical constitution of each.

## Literal Prose Translation

The Saṅgha division just described is held to be a division of the wheel. At that time the Blessed One's Dharma-wheel is divided, because the functioning of the Path is obstructed. For that very reason it is called both a division of the wheel and a division of the Saṅgha.

Where does this division of the wheel occur? In Jambūdvīpa, not in the other continents.

By how many monks? By nine or more. Beginning with nine, there is no fixed upper limit. Eight monks constitute the two Saṅghas, and the ninth is the divider. For a Saṅgha must necessarily stand on each of the two sides. In this way division occurs.

There is another kind of Saṅgha division, produced through division of communal action. This occurs when, within a single boundary, factions perform the communal act separately. This division of action occurs in the three continents in which the Teaching exists. By how many monks? By eight or more.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Why this is called `cakrabheda`

The decisive gloss is:

```text
mārgapravṛtti-viṣṭhāpana
obstruction of the functioning of the Path
```

The division is therefore not merely institutional disagreement. The Dharma-wheel is said to be divided because the Saṅgha's normative rupture prevents the Path from functioning as one transmitted and practiced order.

Thus:

```text
acceptance of another teacher and another path
    → normative rupture of the Saṅgha
    → obstruction of Path-functioning
    → cakrabheda
```

### 2. Why nine are required

The Bhāṣya derives the minimum formally:

```text
4 monks = one Saṅgha faction
4 monks = opposing Saṅgha faction
1 monk  = divider
-------------------
9 minimum
```

The important point is not numerology. Each side must itself satisfy the minimum constitution of a Saṅgha. The ninth is the agent who effects the division.

Hence `navādibhiḥ` means nine as the lower bound, with no fixed upper limit.

### 3. `karmabheda` as a distinct division

A second kind of division occurs when factions remain within one ecclesiastical boundary (`ekasīmā`) but perform a formal communal act (`karma`) separately.

Its structure is:

```text
one boundary
    +
two opposed groups
    +
separate Saṅgha acts
    → karmabheda
```

Here no ninth divider is required because the division consists directly in the two groups of four acting separately.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — identifies the previously described schism as `cakrabheda` and explains its relation to obstruction of the Path.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — states its restriction to Jambūdvīpa and derives the minimum of nine.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu / Vaibhāṣika disciplinary framework** — distinguishes `karmabheda` and specifies its occurrence in the three continents with eight or more monks.

## New Technical Terms

- **cakrabheda** — division of the Dharma-wheel.
  - **status:** explicit in Kārikā, defined more precisely in Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** identifies the strongest doctrinal form of Saṅgha schism.

- **mārgapravṛtti-viṣṭhāpana** — obstruction or displacement of the functioning of the Path.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory phrase.
  - **function:** explains why Saṅgha schism is called division of the Dharma-wheel.

- **karmabheda** — division of communal action.
  - **status:** explicit in Kārikā, operationally defined in Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** distinguishes procedural schism from doctrinal/path schism.

- **ekasīmā** — one common ecclesiastical boundary.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** specifies the field within which separate communal acts constitute `karmabheda`.

- **vyagra** — separated, disunited, factionalized.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya term.
  - **function:** describes the condition of the factions performing separate communal acts.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya recognizes at least two dimensions of Saṅgha unity:

```text
normative/path unity
    → one teacher
    → one path
    → one functioning Dharma-wheel

operative/ritual unity
    → one ecclesiastical boundary
    → one communal act
```

Their negations are correspondingly different:

```text
cakrabheda
    = rupture of teacher/path unity
    = obstruction of Path-functioning

karmabheda
    = rupture of communal-action unity
```

This distinction should be preserved. Not every procedural division is the exceptional ānantarya-producing `cakrabheda` discussed in the preceding verses.

## Logical Determination

Let:

```text
N = normative unity of teacher and Path
O = operative unity of communal action
```

Then:

```text
Negation(N) → Cakrabheda
Negation(O) → Karmabheda
```

but:

```text
Karmabheda ≠ necessarily Cakrabheda
```

The numerical conditions follow from the relational structure rather than being accidental external thresholds:

```text
Cakrabheda:
Saṅgha(A) ∧ Saṅgha(B) ∧ Divider(D)
minimum = 4 + 4 + 1 = 9

Karmabheda:
Saṅgha(A) ∧ Saṅgha(B)
minimum = 4 + 4 = 8
```

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation is substantially confirmed, but the Bhāṣya allows a stronger rendering of `cakrabheda`:

> The previously described schism is regarded as division of the Dharma-wheel, because it obstructs the functioning of the Path. It occurs only in Jambūdvīpa and requires at least nine monks: two groups of four and the divider. Division of communal action, by contrast, occurs in the three continents where the Teaching exists and requires at least eight monks.

The phrase “division of the wheel” should therefore not be left metaphorical. The Bhāṣya defines its functional content as obstruction of `mārgapravṛtti`.

## Light Organon Contact

This verse yields a precise distinction between two failures of a collective rational order:

```text
principle-level rupture
    → competing teacher/path
    → Path itself ceases to function as one order

operation-level rupture
    → shared framework remains
    → common action fractures procedurally
```

The first attacks the governing principle of the collective; the second attacks its coordinated execution.

The distinction is useful precisely because the Bhāṣya does not collapse all disagreement into one kind of division. The level at which unity is negated determines the kind of schism produced.
