# VAK_4.33_bhasya — Why the Lay Precept Is Sexual Restraint, Not Celibacy

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
mithyācārātigarhyatvāt saukaryād akriyāptitaḥ |
yathābhyupagamaṃ lābhaḥ saṃvarasya na saṃtateḥ ||
```

**Working translation:**

> Because sexual misconduct is especially blameworthy, because abstention from it is practicable, and because the restraint of non-performance is acquired in that form, the restraint is acquired according to what has been undertaken, not with respect to an unrestricted extension through the continuum.

The Bhāṣya shows that the second half must be read in the concrete context of the lay sexual precept: an upāsaka undertakes abstention from sexual misconduct, not lifelong celibacy as such.

## Source Cautions

The verse is highly compressed. The Bhāṣya supplies the missing subject matter: the contrast is between `kāmamithyācāra` and `abrahmacarya`, not between an arbitrary partial vow and a complete vow in general.

The final `na saṃtateḥ` should therefore not be expanded into a general doctrine that restraint never extends beyond any explicitly named item. Vasubandhu's own prose explains the point through the practitioner’s continuum (`saṃtāna`) and the exact content of the undertaking.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu begins with a concrete Vinaya-Abhidharma question:

```text
other restraints:
    abstention from abrahmacarya
    = complete celibacy

upāsaka restraint:
    abstention from kāmamithyācāra
    = sexual misconduct

why the difference?
```

He gives three reasons.

### 1. Sexual misconduct is especially blameworthy

`kāmamithyācāra` is strongly condemned in ordinary social life because it injures the spouses of others and leads toward unfortunate rebirth.

```text
kāmamithyācāra
    → injury to others' marital relations
    → strong blameworthiness
    → apāyika consequence
```

Complete celibacy is not blameworthy in the opposite sense; rather, it is simply much more demanding.

### 2. The lay rule is practicable

For householders, abstention from sexual misconduct is comparatively easy; complete celibacy is difficult.

If lay entry required the more difficult form, many householders would not be able to undertake it.

```text
householder
    + prohibition of sexual misconduct
        → practicable restraint

householder
    + compulsory complete celibacy
        → difficult undertaking
```

The lay discipline is therefore shaped as a practicable form of restraint appropriate to household life.

### 3. Noble persons acquire `akaraṇasaṃvara` in this form

Vasubandhu then appeals to the restraint of non-performance (`akaraṇasaṃvara`). Noble persons acquire such restraint with respect to sexual misconduct because they do not engage in it even across other births; the same is not asserted of complete celibacy.

He glosses:

```text
akriyāniyama
    = akaraṇasaṃvara
```

that is, a fixed non-performance constitutes the restraint of non-doing.

This explains why the lay training rule is formulated specifically as abstention from sexual misconduct.

## The Marriage Objection

A further difficulty now arises.

Upāsakas may marry. If restraint has already been acquired with respect to women generally, would marrying a woman disturb or break the restraint?

Vasubandhu first blocks one easy escape: we should not say that no restraint had been acquired with respect to the future wife, because that would make the restraint merely regional or partial (`prādeśika`).

The real answer is instead given by the second half of the verse:

```text
yathābhyupagamaṃ lābhaḥ
    acquisition is according to the undertaking
```

What did the upāsaka undertake?

```text
kāmamithyācārād viramāmi
    "I abstain from sexual misconduct."
```

He did **not** undertake:

```text
mayā brahmacaryaṃ kartavyam
    "I must practice complete celibacy."
```

Therefore the acquired restraint is grounded in the sexual-misconduct precept, not in the broader determination of total celibacy.

Marriage consequently does not disturb the restraint.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames the question why the upāsaka precept differs from celibate restraints and gives the threefold explanation.

**Voice: Objector** — raises the difficulty concerning upāsakas who marry: if restraint has been acquired, why does marriage not disturb it?

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — resolves the difficulty through the exact content of `abhyupagama`.

No separate school attribution is explicit in this immediate unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **`kāmamithyācāra`** — sexual misconduct; **status:** established Buddhist ethical term; **function:** object of the lay sexual restraint.
- **`abrahmacarya`** — non-celibacy; by contrast, the total sexual abstinence required in stricter restraints; **status:** previously relevant in prātimokṣa contexts; **function:** contrast class.
- **`saukarya`** — practicability, ease of performance; **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative vocabulary; **function:** practical ground for differentiation of rules.
- **`akaraṇasaṃvara`** — restraint consisting in fixed non-performance; **status:** technical Bhāṣya term; **function:** explains why the prohibition is established in this form.
- **`akriyāniyama`** — fixed determination not to perform an act; **status:** explanatory gloss; **function:** definition of `akaraṇasaṃvara`.
- **`prādeśikasaṃvara`** — regionally or partially limited restraint; **status:** technical term; **function:** rejected explanation of why marriage remains possible.
- **`abhyupagama`** — undertaking, acceptance; **status:** already encountered; **function:** determines the precise content under which restraint is acquired.
- **`saṃvarakṣobha`** — disturbance of restraint; **status:** Bhāṣya-only/rare; **function:** names the feared contradiction generated by marriage.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya establishes a precise asymmetry between lay and celibate discipline.

```text
upāsaka restraint
    sexual misconduct is prohibited
    lawful sexual relation is not thereby prohibited

celibate restraint
    sexual activity as such is prohibited
```

The difference is not a defect in the lay restraint. It is built into the rule's intended scope.

The key principle is:

```text
content of abhyupagama
    → content of saṃvaralābha
```

But this should be stated narrowly. Vasubandhu is not yet giving a universal theory that every unspoken implication is excluded from restraint. He is showing that one cannot silently convert an undertaking of abstention from sexual misconduct into an undertaking of absolute celibacy.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā-pass translation was too general when it suggested that the verse chiefly establishes that restraint "does not automatically extend to unaccepted rules."

A better contextual translation is:

> Because sexual misconduct is especially blameworthy, because abstention from it is practicable, and because the restraint of non-performance is acquired in that form, restraint is acquired according to the undertaking; it does not extend through the continuum as an undertaking of complete celibacy.

The final clause remains compact in Sanskrit, but the Bhāṣya makes its practical target unambiguous.

## Logical Determination

The distinction is between **determinate prohibition** and **indefinite enlargement of the prohibition**.

```text
undertaking:
    abstain from sexual misconduct

acquired determination:
    sexual-misconduct restraint

not licensed inference:
    therefore complete celibacy was undertaken
```

Thus the standing practical form is generated according to its determining content.

This gives us a useful refinement of the preceding discussion of practical status:

```text
status alone
    does not determine every possible prohibition

undertaking
    supplies determinate content

restraint
    persists according to that content
```

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is a principle of **determinate practical judgment**.

A practical determination is not made more universal merely by removing its stated limit.

```text
"I abstain from X"
    ≠
"I abstain from every broader class containing X"
```

The universal force of the vow lies in the stability of the determination actually undertaken, not in an unlimited semantic expansion of its predicate.

This is especially important here because the bearer remains a householder. The Path-form reorganizes household life without simply converting the householder into a monastic subject.
