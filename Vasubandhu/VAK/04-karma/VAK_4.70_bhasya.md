# VAK_4.70_bhasya — The Roots That Complete the Bad Karmapathas

## Kārikā

**VAK 4.70**

> *vadha-vyāpāda-pāruṣya-niṣṭhā dveṣeṇa lobhataḥ /*  
> *para-strī-gamanābhidhyādattādāna-samāpanam // 4.70 //*

**Best current translation**

The completion of killing, ill will, and harsh speech is through hatred. Through greed comes the completion of sexual misconduct, covetousness, and taking what is not given.

See: [VAK_4.70.md](./VAK_4.70.md)

---

## Bhāṣya Source

The prose opens by recalling the unresolved question from the preceding analysis:

> *yad uktaṃ “na sarveṣāṃ karmapathānāṃ lobhādibhir niṣṭhe” ty atha kasya kena niṣṭhā /*

“What was said was that not all karmapathas reach completion through greed and the other roots [indifferently]. Through which [root], then, does each reach completion?”

The answer begins:

> *vadha-vyāpāda-pāruṣya-niṣṭhā dveṣeṇa*

> *eṣāṃ prāṇātipāta-vyāpāda-pāruṣyāṇāṃ dveṣeṇa samāptiḥ /*

“The completion of killing, ill will, and harsh speech is through hatred.”

Vasubandhu adds the ground:

> *parityāga-paruṣa-citta-saṃmukhībhāvāt /*

The text then gives the greed-group:

> *lobhataḥ /*

> *para-strī-gamanābhidhyādattādāna-samāpanam // 4.70 //*

> *lobhena kāma-mithyācārābhidhyādattādānānāṃ samāptiḥ /*

“Through greed comes the completion of sexual misconduct, covetousness, and taking what is not given.”

The prose immediately completes the root-map beyond the two triads explicitly named in the kārikā:

> *mithyādṛṣṭes tu mohena /*

> *adhimātrabhūto hi tāṃ niṣṭhāpayati /*

“But false view [is completed] through delusion, for delusion become intense brings it to completion.”

Finally:

> *śeṣāṇāṃ tribhir iṣyate /*

“What of the remainder? It is held that [their completion] is through all three.”

The remainder is then specified:

> *ke punaḥ śeṣāḥ / mṛṣāvāda-paiśunya-saṃbhinnapralāpāḥ /*

> *eṣāṃ tribhir api niṣṭhā lobhena dveṣeṇa mohena vā /*

“The remainder are false speech, divisive speech, and idle/disordered speech. These can reach completion through any of the three: greed, hatred, or delusion.”

---

## Literal Prose Translation

It was said that not all karmapathas reach completion through greed and the other roots [indifferently]. Through which root, then, does each reach completion?

The completion of killing, ill will, and harsh speech is through hatred. The completion of these—killing, ill will, and harsh speech—is through hatred, because of the coming into presence of a harsh, rejecting mind.

Through greed comes the completion of sexual misconduct, covetousness, and taking what is not given. Sexual misconduct, covetousness, and taking what is not given reach completion through greed.

False view, however, reaches completion through delusion, for delusion when intensified brings it to completion.

The remainder are held to reach completion through all three roots. What are the remainder? False speech, divisive speech, and idle/disordered speech. These can reach completion through any of the three: greed, hatred, or delusion.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 4.68 established that a preparatory undertaking may arise from any of the three unwholesome roots. VAK 4.69 then clarified the relation between immediately antecedent root and the arising of the mental courses. VAK 4.70 now asks a narrower question:

```text
not:
    What roots may occur somewhere in the history of the act?

but:
    Which root brings this karmapatha to niṣṭhā / samāpti?
```

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes **genesis** from **completion**.

### 1. Hatred-completed courses

```text
prāṇātipāta    killing
vyāpāda        ill will
pāruṣya        harsh speech
        ↓
dveṣa
        ↓
niṣṭhā / samāpti
```

The explanatory phrase `parityāga-paruṣa-citta-saṃmukhībhāva` points to the defining presence of a rejecting, harsh mind. The completion is classified by the afflictive form that is immediately operative in the completed course.

### 2. Greed-completed courses

```text
kāma-mithyācāra    sexual misconduct
abhidhyā            covetousness
adattādāna          taking what is not given
        ↓
lobha
        ↓
samāpana
```

These are completed through an appropriative movement: acquisition, possession, or enjoyment directed toward what is not properly one’s own.

### 3. Delusion-completed course

```text
mithyādṛṣṭi
    ↓
moha intensified
    ↓
niṣṭhā
```

This is especially important. False view is not merely accompanied by delusion. Vasubandhu says that **delusion become intense** (`adhimātrabhūta`) brings false view to completion.

### 4. The three variably completed speech-courses

```text
mṛṣāvāda             false speech
paiśunya              divisive speech
saṃbhinnapralāpa      idle / disordered speech
        ↓
may be completed through
        lobha
        dveṣa
        moha
```

These lack a single exclusive completion-root. The same formal speech-course can be generated to completion by different afflictive grounds.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting the Abhidharma completion-root classification.

There is no explicit objection or competing school voice in this short unit. The prose is classificatory and explanatory.

---

## New Technical Terms

### niṣṭhā

- **Provisional meaning:** culmination, decisive completion of a karmapatha.
- **Status:** previously encountered in the immediately preceding karmapatha analysis.
- **Function:** marks the threshold at which a karmic process counts as a completed course.

### samāpti / samāpana

- **Provisional meaning:** completion, bringing-to-completion.
- **Status:** operative in this passage as parallel terminology to `niṣṭhā`.
- **Function:** identifies the completion relation between a karmapatha and its characteristic root.

### adhimātrabhūta

- **Provisional meaning:** become excessive, intense, or predominant.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific determination in this unit.
- **Function:** explains how `moha` completes `mithyādṛṣṭi`: not by mere presence, but by becoming sufficiently intense.

### saṃmukhībhāva

- **Provisional meaning:** coming into presence, becoming immediately operative.
- **Status:** encountered elsewhere in Abhidharma analysis; important here in the explanation of hatred-completed courses.
- **Function:** indicates the immediately present mental configuration that constitutes completion.

### parityāga-paruṣa-citta

- **Provisional meaning:** rejecting / abandoning and harsh mind.
- **Status:** local explanatory phrase.
- **Function:** gives the mental morphology by which killing, ill will, and harsh speech are completed through hatred.

## Doctrinal Determination

The complete bad-karmapatha root map supplied by the kārikā plus its immediate Bhāṣya is:

```text
DVEṢA
    prāṇātipāta        killing
    vyāpāda            ill will
    pāruṣya            harsh speech

LOBHA
    kāma-mithyācāra    sexual misconduct
    abhidhyā            covetousness
    adattādāna          taking what is not given

MOHA
    mithyādṛṣṭi         false view

LOBHA / DVEṢA / MOHA
    mṛṣāvāda            false speech
    paiśunya            divisive speech
    saṃbhinnapralāpa    idle / disordered speech
```

This gives all ten bad karmapathas.

The classification must not be mistaken for a claim that each course has only one affliction anywhere in its causal history. VAK 4.68 already allows the preparatory phase to arise from different roots. The present question is specifically **completion**.

Thus:

```text
preparatory root
    may vary

completion root
    is the afflictive determination
    under which the karmapatha reaches niṣṭhā
```

The three speech-courses at the end show that even completion-root classification is not always one-to-one.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation of the kārikā is substantially confirmed:

> The completion of killing, ill will, and harsh speech is through hatred. Through greed comes the completion of sexual misconduct, covetousness, and taking what is not given.

But the Bhāṣya requires the surrounding prose to be retained in any future “Super Kārikā” condensation, because the verse by itself omits four members of the tenfold map:

```text
mithyādṛṣṭi
    → moha

mṛṣāvāda
paiśunya
saṃbhinnapralāpa
    → any of the three roots
```

Without this Bhāṣya continuation, VAK 4.70 appears to classify only six courses rather than opening the complete root classification.

---

## Logical Determination

The passage gives us three distinct logical relations that should not be collapsed:

```text
root as prior generator
        ≠
root immediately antecedent to arising
        ≠
root under which the course reaches completion
```

VAK 4.68–4.70 progressively separate these.

The completion relation is therefore not merely historical causation. It determines the **formal afflictive character of the accomplished course**.

```text
process history
    may be heterogeneous

completed determination
    has a determinate afflictive form
```

But the last three speech-courses prevent this from becoming an over-simple one-root/one-course theory. A single objective form of wrongdoing can be completed under different subjective grounds.

That means ethical classification requires both:

```text
WHAT course was completed?

and

THROUGH WHAT root was it completed?
```

The two axes intersect but are not identical.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage is unusually clean for distinguishing **process genesis** from **determinate completion**.

```text
Ground
    ↓
Process
    ↓
Completion
```

The completed determination cannot always be read backward as a complete history of its genesis. A speech act of falsehood, for example, may be formally the same karmapatha while being completed through greed, hatred, or delusion.

Thus the Bhāṣya gives us a two-dimensional determination:

```text
objective karmic form
    ×
subjective completion-ground
```

The most important case is `mithyādṛṣṭi`:

```text
moha
    becomes adhimātra
        ↓
false view reaches niṣṭhā
```

Here delusion is not merely a background defect. It becomes sufficiently determinate to produce a false principial orientation. That is a genuinely genetic claim and should be preserved without reducing `mithyādṛṣṭi` simply to “ignorance.”

## Study Summary

VAK 4.70 begins the exact completion-root classification and the Bhāṣya finishes the whole tenfold map:

```text
hatred completes:
    killing
    ill will
    harsh speech

greed completes:
    sexual misconduct
    covetousness
    taking what is not given

delusion completes:
    false view

any of the three may complete:
    false speech
    divisive speech
    idle / disordered speech
```

The decisive conceptual distinction is:

```text
causal history of a karmapatha
    ≠
root of its completion
```

VAK 4.71 will now reorganize these same ten courses according to their **adhiṣṭhāna**, their objective support or basis.
