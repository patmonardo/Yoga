# VAK 4.113 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.113**  
**dīyate yena tad dānaṃ pūjānugrahakāmyayā |**  
**kāyavākkarma sotthānaṃ mahābhogyaphalaṃ ca tat || 4.113 ||**

> That through which something is given, from the wish to honor or benefit, is giving. It is bodily or verbal karma together with what brings it into operation, and it has abundant enjoyment as its fruit.

See: `VAK_4.113.md`.

> bhavati sma /

> ato viśeṣaṇārthamāha  
> **pūjānugrahakāmyayā /**  
> pareṣāṃ pūjānugrahakāmatābhyāṃ yena dīyate /  
> kiṃ punastatsyādyena dīyate /  
> **kāyavākkarma sotthānaṃ**  
> kiṃ punastadutthānam /  
> yena kalāpena tadutthāpyate /  
> āha cātra  
> **śubhena manasā dravyaṃ svaṃ dadāti yadā pumān /  
> tat kṣaṇaṃ kuśalāḥ skandhāḥ dānamityabhidhīyate //**  
> **mahābhogyaphalaṃ ca tat //**

## Natural Segmentation

1. What is giving?
2. Giving is not simply the thing transferred.
3. Not every transfer is meritorious giving: transfer can also occur through passion and other afflictions.
4. Therefore the verse adds the motive: desire to honor or benefit others.
5. The effective giving is bodily or verbal karma together with its activating aggregate.
6. A cited verse identifies the wholesome aggregates present at the moment of giving as `dāna`.
7. Its characteristic maturation is abundant enjoyment.

## Literal Prose Translation

What is this thing called giving? Even that which is given could be called giving; but here, giving is that through which something is given.

Yet something can also be given through passion and the other afflictions, and that is not what is intended here. Therefore, by way of qualification, the text says: **“from the wish to honor or benefit.”** Giving is that through which something is given because of the wish to honor or benefit others.

What, then, is that through which it is given? **Bodily or verbal karma together with its activation.** What is that activation? The aggregate by which that bodily or verbal karma is brought into operation.

And it is said:

> When a person, with a wholesome mind, gives his own property, the wholesome aggregates present at that moment are designated “giving.”

And that giving has abundant enjoyment as its fruit.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya moves through three successive exclusions.

### 1. Gift-object is not identical with giving

The thing transferred may conventionally be called `dāna`, but the technical sense required here is:

```text
what is given
    ≠
that through which it is given
```

The latter is the karmic act.

### 2. Transfer alone is insufficient

Vasubandhu explicitly notes:

`rāgādibhir api dīyate`

Something may be transferred because of passion or other afflictions. Therefore external relinquishment alone does not constitute the meritorious giving under analysis.

### 3. Motive determines the wholesome species

The verse therefore adds:

`pūjānugrahakāmyayā`

The transfer must be undertaken from the wish either:

- to honor (`pūjā`), or
- to benefit (`anugraha`)

others.

Thus the act becomes meritorious giving only when the bodily or verbal transfer is generated within a wholesome practical complex.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — the initial definition and exclusion of afflictively motivated transfer.

**Voice: cited authoritative verse / traditional stanza** — `śubhena manasā dravyaṃ...`; exact authorship uncertain.

## New Technical Terms

- **pūjānugrahakāmatā** — wish to honor and wish to benefit.  
  **Status:** previously encountered notions, newly compounded here.  
  **Function:** specifies the motive that distinguishes meritorious giving from afflictively motivated transfer.

- **utthāna** — activation; that by which bodily or verbal karma is brought into operation.  
  **Status:** rare / Bhāṣya clarification required by the Kārikā.  
  **Function:** explains `sotthānam` as the activating causal complex accompanying the manifest deed.

- **kalāpa** — aggregate, complex, cluster.  
  **Status:** previously encountered.  
  **Function:** identifies the causal complex that activates bodily or verbal giving.

- **mahābhogyaphala** — having abundant enjoyment/resources as its fruit.  
  **Status:** Kārikā term.  
  **Function:** states the characteristic maturation of giving.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya rejects two reductions:

```text
external object
    ≠ dāna by itself

external transfer
    ≠ meritorious dāna by itself
```

The complete structure is:

```text
wholesome intention
+ wish to honor or benefit
+ activating aggregate
+ bodily/verbal execution
    ↓
dāna
```

The cited stanza goes even further: at the moment when a person gives his own property with a wholesome mind, the **wholesome aggregates (`kuśalāḥ skandhāḥ`) present in that event** are collectively designated giving.

Thus `dāna` is not one isolated physical motion. It is a coordinated wholesome event.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass reading correctly distinguished the gift-object from the act of giving, but two refinements are required.

First, `pūjānugrahakāmyayā` is not an optional description of two common motives. It is a **restrictive qualification** introduced precisely because transfer can also be generated by affliction.

Second, `sotthānam` should not be rendered merely as “together with motivation.” The Bhāṣya defines the `utthāna` as:

> `yena kalāpena tadutthāpyate` — the aggregate by which that bodily or verbal karma is brought into operation.

A tighter translation is therefore:

> **That through which something is given, from the wish to honor or benefit others, is giving. It is bodily or verbal karma together with the activating complex that brings it into operation, and it has abundant enjoyment as its fruit.**

## Light Organon Contact

The Bhāṣya gives a clean practical structure:

```text
purpose
    ↓
activation
    ↓
embodied/verbal execution
    ↓
objective transfer
```

But the decisive point is that the same external transfer can belong to different ethical species depending on its determining ground.

Therefore:

```text
same external result
    ≠
same karma
```

Merit belongs to the internally determined practical whole, not to mere redistribution of an object.

This also continues the distinction established in 4.104: karmic identity is determined by intention, field, activation, and completion together rather than by external result alone.

## Continuity

The Bhāṣya immediately proceeds to classify giving according to whether it serves:

- oneself,
- others,
- both,
- or neither,

before VAK 4.114 introduces the further differentiation of giving by **giver, gift-object, and field**.
