# VAK 4.108 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.108**  
**bodhisattvaḥ kuto yāvat yato lakṣaṇakarmakṛt |**  
**sugatiḥ kulajo ’vyakṣaḥ pumān jātismaro ’nivṛt || 4.108 ||**

> From what point onward is he [technically] a Bodhisattva? From the time when he begins performing karma whose maturation is the [Great Person’s] marks. ## Natural Segmentation

1. Why the previous verse can speak technically of “killing a Bodhisattva.”
2. The threshold of the strict Bodhisattva designation: beginning the karmas whose maturation is the Buddha marks.
3. This threshold is described as entry into certainty (`niyatipatita`).
4. Six recurrent characteristics thereafter: good destiny, eminent family, unimpaired faculties, male embodiment according to this historical doctrinal scheme, recollection of former births, and non-retrogression.
5. `anivṛt` is explained ethically and practically: suffering and the misconduct of beings cannot exhaust the Bodhisattva’s labor for their welfare.
6. The “unbought slave” image presents compassion as a freely assumed dependence upon the needs of beings.

## Literal Prose Translation

Regarding the statement, “killing a Bodhisattva”: from what point onward is one to be called a Bodhisattva?

From the time when he begins to perform karmas whose maturation consists in the marks [of a Great Person]. At that time he has entered certainty. From then onward he is continually of good destiny, born into an eminent family, with unimpaired faculties, male, one who remembers former births, and non-retrogressing.

He is called “of good destiny” because he is born among gods and humans. Within such a good destiny he is born in an eminent kṣatriya, brāhmaṇa, or wealthy householder family, not in another kind of family. `Avyakṣa` means that his faculties are not defective. The text states, within its inherited bodily classification, that he is male rather than female or included among the other listed sexual categories. He remembers former births.

`Anivṛt` means `anivartaka`, one who does not turn back. In laboring for the welfare of beings he is not wearied by any form of suffering or by any misconduct on the part of sentient beings. Hence the worldly expression “an unbought slave” is applied: although Bodhisattvas are great beings endowed with extraordinary excellence and prosperity, because they are governed by causeless compassion they establish themselves humbly in relation to all beings, endure every injury inflicted by beings, and bear every burden of toil and torment.

## Course of Argument

The Bhāṣya answers a classificatory problem generated by 4.106. If killing a Bodhisattva “established in certainty” is an analogue of ānantarya, the category must have a determinate beginning.

Vasubandhu locates that beginning in **karma**, not merely in aspiration or self-description:

```text
beginning of lakṣaṇa-vipāka karma
    ↓
niyatipatita — entry into certainty
    ↓
strict Bodhisattva trajectory
```

`Lakṣaṇa-karman` therefore marks a change in modal status. The future is not yet actual Buddhahood, but the trajectory has become sufficiently determined that characteristic constraints now persist through subsequent births.

The six listed determinations describe that stabilized trajectory at several levels:

```text
sugati       → rebirth-domain
kulaja       → social/familial condition
avyakṣa      → embodied capacities
pumān        → inherited bodily qualification
jātismara    → diachronic recollection
anivṛt       → irreversible practical orientation
```

The last is the culmination. Non-retrogression is not explained abstractly as mere inevitability; it is shown as **inexhaustibility of practical commitment**. No suffering received and no misconduct encountered from other beings makes the Bodhisattva abandon their welfare.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Abhidharma exposition** — the principal classificatory explanation.

The bodily and social restrictions are reported as part of the inherited doctrinal scheme. They should be preserved as historical Abhidharma claims rather than generalized into contemporary anthropology.

The concluding praise of Bodhisattva compassion has a markedly elevated rhetorical register but remains integrated into Vasubandhu’s explanation of `anivṛt`.

## New Technical Terms

- **lakṣaṇa-vipāka-karman** — karma whose maturation produces the marks of a Great Person.  
  **Status:** newly important in this sequence.  
  **Function:** defines the beginning of the strict Bodhisattva stage.

- **niyatipatita** — entered into certainty or fixed determination.  
  **Status:** rare/new in this immediate discussion.  
  **Function:** modal classification of the Bodhisattva trajectory after mark-producing karma begins.

- **avikalendriya** — having unimpaired faculties.  
  **Status:** gloss of the difficult `avyakṣa`.  
  **Function:** bodily/capacity determination.

- **jātismara** — one who remembers former births.  
  **Status:** previously encountered concept.  
  **Function:** diachronic continuity through recollection.

- **anivartaka** — non-retrogressing.  
  **Status:** explicit Bhāṣya gloss of `anivṛt`.  
  **Function:** practical and modal determination of the Bodhisattva career.

- **akheditatva** — not becoming exhausted or wearied.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here.  
  **Function:** explains non-retrogression through perseverance for beings’ welfare.

- **niṣkāraṇa-karuṇā** — compassion without an interested cause or return.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only terminology in this passage.  
  **Function:** explains why the Bodhisattva voluntarily bears the burdens of beings.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The strict Bodhisattva is defined by an **irreversible causal trajectory**.

This is stronger than saying that certain future events are probable. `Niyatipatita` indicates that, from this stage, a corridor of becoming has been established whose contrary possibilities are progressively excluded.

```text
aspiration
    ≠ yet strict technical Bodhisattva status

lakṣaṇa-vipāka karma begins
    ↓
entry into certainty
    ↓
regulated future sequence
```

The most important philosophical feature is the relation between **future form and present activity**. The marks of the Buddha are not simply bestowed at the end; their causal production has already begun in the Bodhisattva’s karma. Future Buddha-form is therefore accumulated activity becoming manifest.

The passage also links irreversible becoming to practical universality. `Anivṛt` means that the project of benefiting beings survives every adverse encounter:

```text
suffering inflicted on Bodhisattva
hostility or misconduct of beings
        ↓
no cancellation of sattva-hita
```

Non-retrogression is thus not merely persistence of an internal state. It is **persistence of relation to all beings**.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation is substantially confirmed, with two refinements:

1. `yato lakṣaṇakarmakṛt` should explicitly indicate **the beginning of karmas whose maturation produces the marks**, not merely generic “mark-producing karma.”
2. `anivṛt` should be translated **non-retrogressing**, since the Bhāṣya explicitly glosses it with `anivartaka` and explains it through unwavering labor for beings.

A refined translation is:

> From what point onward is he [technically] a Bodhisattva? From the time when he begins producing karma whose maturation is the [Great Person’s] marks. From then onward he is continually in a good destiny, born into an eminent family, with unimpaired faculties, male, remembering former births, and non-retrogressing.

## Light Organon Contact

The verse offers a particularly clear example of **genetic determination**.

The Bodhisattva is not defined by possession of an already completed Buddha-form. Rather, the future form becomes intelligible from the activity already producing it:

```text
present karmic genesis
    → determinate trajectory
    → future Buddha-form
```

The higher form is therefore not externally appended to the series. Its principle is already active within the series as the law of its becoming.

The Bhāṣya’s account of `anivṛt` adds a practical dimension: certainty is demonstrated as the inability of contingent opposition to negate the universal end. The Bodhisattva remains determined toward the welfare of beings through every particular contradiction encountered along the Path.
