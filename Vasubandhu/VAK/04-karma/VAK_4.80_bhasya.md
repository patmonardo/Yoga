# VAK 4.80 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā

**chinatti strī pumān dṛṣṭi-caritaḥ so ’samanvayaḥ /**  
**saṃdhiḥ kāṅkṣāsti-dṛṣṭibhyāṃ nehānantarya-kāriṇaḥ // 4.80 //**

> A woman or a man, when governed by view, severs them; that condition is non-endowment. Reconnection occurs through doubt or through the view that they exist; but not here, in the present life, for one who has committed an ānantarya action.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### 1. Who can sever the roots?

“A woman severs them, and a man severs them.”

The Bhāṣya immediately records a disagreement. One position accepts both women and men as capable of severance. Another says that a woman does not sever them, appealing to comparatively weaker desire, energy, and discriminative capacity. Vasubandhu replies that this would conflict with an established doctrinal statement concerning one endowed with the female faculty.

The important point is therefore not biological sex as such but the type of practical disposition capable of becoming fixed in radical false view.

### 2. Not one governed by craving

Even among such persons, one whose conduct is governed by craving (`tṛṣṇā-carita`) does not sever the wholesome roots, because the disposition is unstable (`calāśayatvāt`).

Who does sever them?

> **dṛṣṭi-caritaḥ** — one whose conduct is governed by view.

Why?

Because such a person possesses a firm and deeply concealed evil disposition (`dṛḍha-gūḍha-pāpāśaya`).

Thus the operative contrast is:

```text
tṛṣṇā-carita
    affectively driven
    unstable orientation
    → does not complete root-severance

 dṛṣṭi-carita
    principially fixed
    deeply established orientation
    → capable of root-severance
```

### 3. What is root-severance in its own nature?

The Bhāṣya asks:

> What is the intrinsic character of the severance of wholesome roots?

Answer:

> **so ’samanvayaḥ** — it is non-endowment.

More precisely, when the `prāpti` of the wholesome roots no longer arises again and `aprāpti` arises, that state of non-endowment is called the severance of the wholesome roots.

This is technically important. Severance is not destruction of a permanent substance. It is a change in the continuum’s relation to the wholesome roots:

```text
prāpti ceases to arise
    +
aprāpti arises
    =
asamanvāgama
    =
root-severance
```

### 4. How are the roots reconnected?

The Bhāṣya asks how roots that have been severed become connected again.

Answer:

> **saṃdhiḥ kāṅkṣāstidṛṣṭibhyām**

Reconnection occurs through either:

- `kāṅkṣā` / `vicikitsā` — doubt concerning ground and result, or
- `astidṛṣṭi` — the view that they exist, which the Bhāṣya explicitly glosses as `samyagdṛṣṭi`, right view.

Thus the closed certainty of denial need not first be replaced by complete knowledge. The first opening may simply be doubt:

```text
“There is no ground and no result.”
    ↓
“Perhaps there is.”
    ↓
“There is ground and result.”
```

When either doubt or right view arises, the relevant `prāpti` arises again, and the wholesome roots are said to have been reconnected.

### 5. All nine reconnect at once; manifestation is sequential

The Bhāṣya makes an important distinction:

> The reconnection of all nine grades occurs simultaneously, but their coming into manifestation occurs successively, like the recovery of strength after illness.

So:

```text
pratisaṃdhāna
    all nine grades reconnect together

saṃmukhībhāva
    their actual manifestation occurs gradually
```

This corrects any simple assumption that restoration must reverse the ninefold severance one grade at a time.

The structure is asymmetric:

```text
severance
    sequential

reconnection
    simultaneous at the level of endowment

manifest recovery
    sequential
```

### 6. The ānantarya exception

The reconnection may occur in the present life for another person, but not for one who has committed an `ānantarya` action.

The Bhāṣya cites the statement that such a person is incapable of reconnecting the wholesome roots in this very life; the reconnection will necessarily occur either while dying or while arising into the next existence.

It then distinguishes different causal patterns:

- one who severed through the force of a ground (`hetu-bala`) reconnects while dying,
- one who severed through the force of conditions (`pratyaya-bala`) reconnects while arising,
- similarly for severance through one’s own force versus another’s force.

The key point for 4.80 is that present-life restoration is blocked for the `ānantarya-kārin`, though restoration is not denied absolutely.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 4.79 established how fully intensified false view severs the birth-acquired wholesome roots. VAK 4.80 now analyzes the resulting state and its reversibility.

The sequence is:

```text
fixed false view
    ↓
severance of wholesome roots
    ↓
cessation of prāpti
    + arising of aprāpti
    ↓
asamanvaya
    ↓
possible doubt or right view
    ↓
renewed prāpti
    ↓
saṃdhi / reconnection
```

The decisive distinction is between **severance as loss of endowment** and **reconnection as restoration of endowment**.

This is not annihilation and recreation of a substance. It is a change in the practical status of the continuum.

## New Technical Terms

- **dṛṣṭi-carita** — one whose conduct or disposition is governed by view.  
  **Status:** important new specification in this passage.  
  **Function:** identifies the type capable of radical root-severance.

- **tṛṣṇā-carita** — one whose conduct is governed by craving.  
  **Status:** introduced contrastively here.  
  **Function:** excluded from full root-severance because of unstable disposition.

- **calāśaya** — unstable disposition or orientation.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory term.  
  **Function:** explains why craving-governed persons do not complete severance.

- **dṛḍha-gūḍha-pāpāśaya** — firm, deeply concealed evil disposition.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific.  
  **Function:** explains the capacity of view-governed persons to complete severance.

- **asamanvaya / asamanvāgama** — non-endowment, non-possession.  
  **Status:** central technical term of the verse and Bhāṣya.  
  **Function:** defines the intrinsic state produced by severance.

- **prāpti** — acquisition/endowment relation.  
  **Status:** previously encountered Sarvāstivāda technical category.  
  **Function:** its cessation of arising marks loss of wholesome-root endowment.

- **aprāpti** — non-acquisition / non-endowment relation.  
  **Status:** paired technical category.  
  **Function:** its arising constitutes the state of non-endowment.

- **saṃdhi / pratisaṃdhi** — reconnection, re-establishment of continuity.  
  **Status:** new local use.  
  **Function:** restoration of wholesome-root endowment.

- **saṃmukhībhāva** — coming into manifestation or direct presence.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific distinction here.  
  **Function:** distinguishes gradual manifestation from simultaneous reconnection.

- **ānantarya-kārin** — one who has committed an immediate-retribution action.  
  **Status:** introduced here in this block; developed later.  
  **Function:** marks the exception to present-life reconnection.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

### Severance is not annihilation

The most important clarification is:

```text
mūlaccheda
    ≠ destruction of a permanent entity

mūlaccheda
    = transition from endowment to non-endowment
```

The Bhāṣya expresses this with `prāpti` and `aprāpti`.

The subject-continuum changes status:

```text
endowed
    →
non-endowed
```

### View is structurally stronger than craving

The contrast between `tṛṣṇā-carita` and `dṛṣṭi-carita` is philosophically important.

Craving may be intense, but it remains unstable. Radical false view can become a fixed governing determination.

```text
craving
    fluctuating practical attraction

view
    principial organization of the field
```

Thus the severance of wholesome roots is not merely the maximal quantity of desire. It requires a qualitative change in the governing standpoint.

### Doubt has a positive transitional function

In most contexts `vicikitsā` is an affliction. Here, however, relative to dogmatic nihilistic certainty, doubt functions as the first break in closure.

```text
false certainty
    ↓ negation of certainty
 doubt
    ↓
possible right view
```

This does not make doubt wholesome in itself. It means that, genetically, doubt can condition restoration by breaking the completed false determination.

### Restoration separates possession from manifestation

The distinction between simultaneous reconnection and sequential manifestation is especially valuable:

```text
capacity restored
    all at once

capacity exercised
    progressively
```

The illness analogy makes this explicit. One may again possess the capacity for health before full strength appears in every activity.

---

## Translation Consequences

1. `asamanvaya` should be translated **non-endowment** or **non-possession**, not merely “absence.”
2. `saṃdhi` is best rendered **reconnection** or **restoration of continuity**, because the Bhāṣya explains it by renewed `prāpti`.
3. `asti-dṛṣṭi` is not a generic metaphysical “belief in existence”; in context it means the view that karmic ground and result exist, and the Bhāṣya explicitly equates it with `samyagdṛṣṭi`.
4. `kāṅkṣā` is best rendered here as **doubt** concerning ground and result.
5. The ānantarya clause means **no reconnection in this present life**, not absolute impossibility of future restoration.

## Light Organon Contact

The text gives us a clean genetic structure of practical corruption and recovery:

```text
principle becomes fixed as false
    ↓
generative capacity is lost
    ↓
continuum becomes non-endowed
    ↓
certainty is destabilized
    ↓
correct principle becomes possible again
    ↓
capacity is restored
    ↓
manifest exercise returns gradually
```

The strongest point is that **restoration begins before full truth is possessed**. The first mediation can be the negation of false certainty itself.

But the Bhāṣya remains equally clear that restoration of principle does not erase every consequence already fixed by prior action. The `ānantarya` case prevents any simple equation:

```text
corrected view
    =
reversal of all completed consequences
```

Practical genesis has memory.

## Carry-Forward

VAK 4.79–4.80 now give a complete local cycle:

```text
false view intensified
    → severance
    → non-endowment
    → doubt / right view
    → reconnection
    → gradual manifestation
```

The next verses can therefore treat possession and co-presence of karmapathas against a much more precise background: ethical determinations belong to a continuum through specific relations of acquisition, loss, and restored endowment.
