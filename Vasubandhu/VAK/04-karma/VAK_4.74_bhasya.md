# VAK_4.74_bhasya — Sexual Misconduct and False Speech

## Kārikā

**VAK 4.74**

```text
agamyagamanaṃ kāmamithyācāraś caturvidhaḥ /
anyathāsaṃjñino vākyam arthābhijñe mṛṣāvacaḥ // 4.74 //
```

**Best current translation**

Going to one who should not be approached is sexual misconduct, and it is fourfold. The statement of one whose recognition is otherwise, addressed to one who understands its meaning, is false speech.

## 1. Literal Prose Translation

### Sexual misconduct

Sexual misconduct is fourfold going to one or somewhere that should not be approached.

One goes to an impermissible person: one possessed or protected by another, or one's mother, daughter, or a female relative connected through mother or father.

Or one goes through an impermissible bodily locus: even with one's own wife, through the anus or mouth.

Or one goes in an impermissible place: in public, at a caitya, or in a monastery.

Or one goes at an impermissible time. What is an impermissible time? With a pregnant woman, one nursing, or one under an observance. Some say that an observance undertaken with the husband's permission is treated differently.

The qualification `abhrānti`, non-mistake, continues to apply. If one approached another man's wife while taking her to be one's own wife, the completed karmapatha would not arise. There is disagreement concerning the converse case in which one approaches one woman while taking her to be another man's wife: some hold that the karmapatha arises because there is the preparation toward another's wife and actual enjoyment of the basis; others deny it except at the level of preparation, comparing it to killing.

### False speech

If, concerning the meaning one states, one's own recognition is otherwise, and the person to whom one speaks understands the meaning of the statement, that statement is false speech.

If the hearer does not understand the meaning, what is it? It is `saṃbhinnapralāpa`, disordered or incoherent speech.

The text says `vākya`, a statement. Since a statement may consist of many phonetic units, which one constitutes the karmapatha? The final unit, together with the avijñapti, at the point where the hearer understands the meaning; the preceding units are preparation.

## 2. Argument Reconstruction

Vasubandhu does not define either karmapatha merely by outward behavior.

For sexual misconduct, the act becomes determinate through a violated practical relation. Four distinct dimensions can render the sexual act wrongful:

```text
1. wrong person / relation
2. wrong bodily locus
3. wrong place
4. wrong time
```

The first category is relational rather than intrinsic. The person is `agamya` because the relevant relation makes the approach impermissible: another's protected partner, certain kin relations, or another protected person.

The second category shows that even an otherwise permitted partner does not make every bodily mode permitted.

The third and fourth categories add spatial and temporal conditions. The same general activity changes ethical determination according to place and time.

The inherited `abhrānti` condition from 4.73 is then applied to sexual misconduct. This produces a difficult issue of object-reference:

```text
actual forbidden object
    + mistaken recognition as permitted
        → no completed karmapatha

actual permitted/different object
    + recognition as forbidden object
        → disputed
```

One position emphasizes intention plus use of the objective basis; another emphasizes actual object identity and therefore treats the mistaken case only as preparation.

For false speech, the structure is explicitly semantic:

```text
speaker's recognition
    ≠
meaning asserted

and

hearer understands asserted meaning
    →
completed false speech
```

If the hearer does not understand the meaning, the utterance does not reach the completion-condition of `mṛṣāvāda`; it falls instead under `saṃbhinnapralāpa`.

The completion point of a multi-syllabic statement is therefore not the first sound emitted. The earlier phonetic units belong to `prayoga`; completion occurs with the final unit that establishes the understood statement, together with avijñapti.

---

## 3. Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu / explanatory Bhāṣya voice** — gives the fourfold analysis and the semantic definition of false speech.

**Voice: doctrinal alternatives** — the text reports disagreement over mistaken object-recognition in sexual misconduct:

- one view allows completion through forbidden-object intention plus actual enjoyment of the basis;
- another limits this to preparation, by analogy with killing.

The Bhāṣya does not here force these reported alternatives into a single simplified rule.

## 4. New Technical Terms

### `agamya`

- **provisional meaning:** one who / that which should not be sexually approached under the governing relation
- **status:** operative in this Kārikā/Bhāṣya unit
- **function:** marks relational impermissibility rather than intrinsic impurity

### `adeśa`

- **provisional meaning:** impermissible place
- **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the fourfold classification
- **function:** spatial condition of sexual misconduct

### `akāla`

- **provisional meaning:** impermissible time
- **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
- **function:** temporal condition of sexual misconduct

### `abhrānti`

- **provisional meaning:** non-mistaken identification / correct object-linkage
- **status:** carried forward from VAK 4.73
- **function:** determines whether intended and actual objective basis coincide

### `arthābhijña`

- **provisional meaning:** one who understands the meaning
- **status:** central in the Kārikā and Bhāṣya
- **function:** completion-condition for false speech

### `saṃbhinnapralāpa`

- **provisional meaning:** disordered, incoherent, or disconnected speech
- **status:** newly decisive in the Bhāṣya
- **function:** classification of speech that fails the hearer's meaning-comprehension condition

### `varṇa`

- **provisional meaning:** phonetic unit / sound-element of a statement
- **status:** Bhāṣya technical clarification
- **function:** distinguishes preparatory speech-elements from the final completion-bearing unit

## 5. Doctrinal Determination

### Sexual misconduct is fourfold

The Bhāṣya determines `caturvidha` as:

```text
agamya
    impermissible person / relation

anaṅga
    impermissible bodily locus

adeśa
    impermissible place

akāla
    impermissible time
```

This corrects any overly narrow reading in which sexual misconduct is simply equated with adultery. The doctrine is broader: it concerns violation of the determinate conditions governing sexual conduct.

The object-recognition issue is structurally continuous with killing and theft. Completed karmic identity depends not merely on physical occurrence but on the relation among:

```text
intended object
recognized object
actual object
```

### False speech requires successful semantic transmission

The Bhāṣya's definition is stricter than:

```text
false proposition uttered
```

It requires:

```text
speaker cognizes one way
speaker states otherwise
hearer understands the asserted meaning
```

Thus simple factual error is not automatically `mṛṣāvāda`. The speaker must have an inward recognition contrary to the statement.

And failed communication is not completed false speech. If the hearer does not understand, the utterance is assigned elsewhere.

### The statement has a completion-point

A `vākya` may consist of many `varṇa`. The Bhāṣya therefore maps verbal karma onto the same temporal process discovered earlier:

```text
earlier phonetic units
    = prayoga

final meaning-completing unit
    + avijñapti
    = maula karmapatha
```

The principal verbal course therefore exists at the point of successful semantic completion, not merely at the onset of vocalization.

## 6. Translation Consequences

The first-pass philosophical translation remains broadly sound but can now be made more exact.

A stronger rendering is:

> Sexual misconduct is sexual activity violating one of four governing conditions: the person or relation, bodily locus, place, or time. False speech is a statement made contrary to the speaker's own recognition when the hearer understands the meaning asserted.

`agamya` should not be flattened into an intrinsic category of a person. The Bhāṣya's fourfold analysis shows that permissibility is relational, bodily, spatial, and temporal.

`arthābhijña` should remain visibly semantic: **understanding the meaning**, not merely hearing the sounds.

`mṛṣāvacaḥ` / `mṛṣāvāda` is therefore best translated as **false speech** or **lying speech** depending context, with the Bhāṣya preserving the intentional contradiction between recognition and assertion.

## 7. Logical Determination

VAK 4.74 gives two highly structured practical relations.

### Sexual misconduct

```text
activity
    + governing relation
    + bodily condition
    + place
    + time
    + object-recognition
        → determinate practical status
```

The same generic activity is not morally self-classifying. Its determination depends upon the full relational context.

### False speech

```text
Recognition₁
    inwardly held meaning

Statement₂
    contrary asserted meaning

Understanding₂
    recipient grasps Statement₂

Recognition₁ ≠ Statement₂
    + Understanding₂
        → mṛṣāvāda
```

This is not merely a contradiction inside the speaker. It is a **completed semantic act** only when the contrary representation is successfully transmitted.

That gives a precise distinction:

```text
semantic production
    ≠
semantic completion
```

The utterance begins as preparation and becomes a karmapatha only when its meaning is constituted for the recipient.

---

## 8. Light Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya now gives us a remarkably precise model of verbal practical action:

```text
inward determination
    ↓
linguistic manifestation
    ↓
recipient comprehension
    ↓
completed practical-semantic act
```

This is stronger than treating speech as mere outward behavior. `vijñapti` is functioning as manifestation, but manifestation becomes a completed karmic course only through its semantic relation to another knower.

The falsehood itself is a relation of divergence:

```text
what I determine
    ≠
what I make another understand
```

The practical defect therefore lies in the deliberate production of a false semantic relation between agents.

The sexual-misconduct analysis makes the complementary point on the bodily side: practical determination arises only inside a structured field of relations, not from an isolated physical motion.

Thus both halves of 4.74 converge on one principle:

```text
an act is morally determinate
only through the relations it actually realizes
```

That principle is directly earned from Vasubandhu's analysis and does not require an external analogy.

---

## 9. Summary

VAK 4.74 defines two karmapathas by their constitutive relational conditions.

Sexual misconduct is fourfold:

```text
wrong relation/person
wrong bodily locus
wrong place
wrong time
```

and the inherited `abhrānti` requirement raises a further issue of correct object-recognition.

False speech requires:

```text
speaker's recognition contrary to statement
    +
hearer understanding the statement's meaning
```

If comprehension fails, the speech is not completed `mṛṣāvāda` but `saṃbhinnapralāpa`.

For a statement composed of many sound-units, the earlier units are preparation; the final unit at which meaning is understood, together with avijñapti, bears the principal karmapatha.

The main result is therefore:

```text
bodily karma is relationally conditioned action
verbal karma is semantically completed action
```
