# VAK_4.47_bhasya — Karma According to the Feeling of Maturation

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.47**

```iast
sukhavedyaṃ śubhaṃ dhyānād ā tṛtīyād ataḥ param |
aduḥkhāsukhavedyaṃ tu duḥkhavedyam ihāśubham ||
```

> Wholesome karma from the first through the third dhyāna is to be experienced as pleasure. Beyond this, wholesome karma is to be experienced as neither pain nor pleasure. Here, unwholesome karma is to be experienced as pain.

See: [VAK_4.47.md](./VAK_4.47.md)

## Source Cautions

The Bhāṣya makes explicit that the domain of pleasant feeling includes the desire domain together with the first three dhyānas. The compact Kārikā wording should therefore not be read as restricting pleasant maturation to dhyāna alone.

The final statement, `na caiṣāṃ vedanaiva phalam / ... sasaṃbhārā /`, is decisive: the three labels `sukhavedanīya`, `duḥkhavedanīya`, and `aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya` name karma through the feeling characteristic of maturation, but **feeling alone is not the whole fruit**. The fruit occurs together with its associated assemblage.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.45 announced a threefold classification according to experienced maturation. VAK 4.47 now distributes the classes.

First:

```text
kuśala karma
    in kāmadhātu
    and through the first three dhyānas
        → sukhavedanīya
```

Vasubandhu grounds this directly in the structure of the relevant `bhūmi`: these are levels in which pleasant feeling occurs.

Second:

```text
kuśala karma
    above the third dhyāna
        → aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya
```

The ground is equally explicit: above the third dhyāna there is no pleasant or painful feeling. The mode of karmic maturation therefore changes with the affective constitution of the level in which maturation occurs.

Third:

```text
akuśala karma
    → duḥkhavedanīya
```

The word `iha`, “here,” restricts this to kāmadhātu, because unwholesome karma occurs only there.

But Vasubandhu immediately blocks an overly simple reading. The result of these karmas is **not feeling alone**:

```text
vedanā eva phalam
    rejected

sasaṃbhārā
    feeling together with its associated complex
```

Thus the classification names the maturation by its dominant experiential determination without reducing the total karmic result to an isolated feeling-event.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose throughout.

## New Technical Terms

- **sukhavedanīya** — karma whose maturation is characterized by pleasant feeling
  - **status:** announced in the Kārikā, technically explicated here
  - **function:** result-classification by experiential character

- **aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya** — karma whose maturation is characterized by neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
  - **status:** announced in the Kārikā, technically explicated here
  - **function:** higher-level result-classification

- **duḥkhavedanīya** — karma whose maturation is characterized by painful feeling
  - **status:** announced in the Kārikā, technically explicated here
  - **function:** result-classification of akuśala karma

- **sasaṃbhāra** — together with its associated assemblage / accompanying complex
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification in this unit
  - **function:** prevents reduction of karmic fruit to vedanā alone

- **bhūmi** — level or ground
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** determines which forms of feeling can constitute maturation

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya sharpens the relation between karma and experience:

```text
karma
    → vipāka
        → characteristic vedanā
        + associated result-complex
```

The classification is therefore neither merely ethical nor merely psychological. It is a mapping from the quality and level of karma to the experiential form under which its maturation is undergone.

The complete distribution given here is:

```text
kāmadhātu + dhyāna 1–3
    kuśala karma
        → pleasant maturation

above dhyāna 3
    kuśala karma
        → neither-painful-nor-pleasant maturation

kāmadhātu
    akuśala karma
        → painful maturation
```

The decisive qualification is:

```text
felt character of maturation
    ≠ totality of maturation
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The existing Kārikā analysis correctly distinguishes the feeling at the time of action from the feeling belonging to maturation. The Bhāṣya adds two refinements.

First, `sukhavedanīya` includes wholesome desire-domain karma as well as wholesome karma through the third dhyāna.

Second, the translation should not imply that the karmic fruit consists solely of feeling.

Preferred translation:

> **Wholesome karma in the desire domain and through the third dhyāna is karma whose maturation is experienced pleasantly. Beyond the third dhyāna, wholesome karma is experienced as neither painful nor pleasant. Here in the desire domain, unwholesome karma is experienced painfully. These designations characterize the maturation by feeling, though feeling is not its entire fruit: the associated complex matures with it.**

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.46 established a fixed relation between the level of production and the level of maturation. VAK 4.47 now adds a second determination:

```text
maturation-level
    → possible feeling-form
```

Together:

```text
karma-quality
    + karmic level
        ↓
fixed maturation-context
        ↓
characteristic experiential determination
```

But `sasaṃbhāra` prevents reductionism:

```text
EXPERIENTIAL_MARK(result)
    = vedanā-class

RESULT
    ≠ vedanā alone
```

Thus `vedanīya` functions as a determination of the whole through a characteristic moment of the whole.

## Organon Contact Point

The important contact point is that **experience is not an accidental after-effect added to karma**. Once the productive ground and maturation-level are fixed, the form in which the result is experienced is itself systematically determined.

```text
determining activity
    → determinate field of fruition
    → determinate mode of experience
```

At the same time, Vasubandhu refuses to identify the whole result with that experiential predicate. This gives a clean distinction between:

```text
result as concrete complex
    and
felt determination of that result
```

The practical field is therefore already richer than a simple pleasure/pain calculus.
