## Source-Boundary Note

The repository Kārikā file joins the Tiṣya/vīrya and dhī/samādhi half-verse to the `puṇyaṃ kriyā...` half-verse as VAK 4.112. The electronic Bhāṣya source, however, places the former material before the explicit `VAkK_4.112` marker and marks only:

> *puṇyaṃ kriyā 'tha tadvastu trayaṃ karmapathā yathā*

as 4.112.

This should be preserved as a source-segmentation issue rather than silently normalized. The natural argument is nevertheless continuous: the Bodhisattva pāramitā discussion closes, and a new general analysis of meritorious activity begins.

## Natural Segmentation

The passage has two distinct movements.

1. **Closure of the six pāramitās**
   - vīrya is perfected through the Tiṣya praise;
   - dhyāna and prajñā are perfected immediately before awakening in the vajropama-samādhi.

2. **Opening of the doctrine of puṇyakriyāvastu**
   - scripture gives three: giving-made, discipline-made, cultivation-made;
   - Vasubandhu asks how these can each be called `puṇya-kriyā-vastu`;
   - the answer is functional rather than a simple identity: according to circumstance, something can be merit, activity, basis, or several of these at once.

## Literal Prose Translation

Through the praise of Tiṣya, vigor was perfected. The Bhagavat, while still a Bodhisattva, saw the Tathāgata Tiṣya in a jewel-cave absorbed in the fire-element attainment. Standing on one foot, he praised him continuously for seven days with a single verse. At that time, it is said, the perfection of vigor was completed and nine kalpas were turned back.

As for concentration and wisdom: their perfection occurred immediately before awakening, in the vajropama-samādhi. Because each reached the far shore of its own excellence, they are called perfections.

A sūtra says: “There are three bases of meritorious activity: that made of giving, that made of discipline, and that made of cultivation.” How are these three `puṇyakriyāvastu`?

They are, according to the appropriate case, merit, activity, and basis—just as with the karmapathas. Just as some are both karma and paths, some are paths of karma, and so are called karmapathas, so too these three are called bases of meritorious activity in different functional respects.

In the giving-made basis of meritorious activity, bodily and verbal karma can bear the threefold designation. The volition that gives rise to it is both merit and activity. The co-arising dharmas are merit only. In the discipline-made case, bodily and verbal karma itself bears the threefold designation. In the cultivation-made case, loving-kindness is both merit and the basis of meritorious activity. The associated volition, because it actively forms through loving-kindness, is merit and activity; accompanying discipline likewise participates in merit and activity. Other co-arising factors are merit only.

On another analysis, wholesome volition is, in the ultimate sense, meritorious activity; the three are the bases of that activity.

## Argument Reconstruction

The passage rejects a crude reading in which `puṇya`, `kriyā`, and `vastu` are three fixed substances corresponding one-to-one with three practices.

Instead:

```text
one event or factor
    may function as
        merit (puṇya)
        activity (kriyā)
        basis (vastu)
        or more than one of these
```

The analogy with `karmapatha` is exact. A compound term need not assert simple identity between its members. The relation can vary according to function.

Thus:

```text
dānamaya / śīlamaya / bhāvanāmaya
    are not merely three piles of merit

rather

three practical structures
    within which
    merit, activity, and basis
    are differently distributed
```

The final alternative view radicalizes the analysis:

```text
paramārthena puṇyakriyā
    = kuśalacetanā
    = wholesome volition
```

On that account, giving, discipline, and cultivation are principally the **bases** through which wholesome volition operates.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — narrative explanation of Tiṣya praise; placement of dhyāna and prajñā perfection; principal functional analysis of `puṇyakriyāvastu`.
- **Voice: Sūtra citation** — the three `puṇyakriyāvastu`: giving, discipline, cultivation.
- **Voice: alternative masters / uncertain school attribution** — `kuśalacetanā paramārthena puṇyakriyā`; wholesome volition alone is meritorious activity in the strictest sense.

## New Technical Terms

- **puṇyakriyāvastu** — basis of meritorious activity; a functional compound whose members are distributed differently according to case.  
  **Status:** newly central in this passage.  
  **Function:** classificatory analysis.

- **dānamaya** — made of giving; the giving-based form of meritorious activity.  
  **Status:** new in this form.  
  **Function:** first member of the threefold scriptural classification.

- **śīlamaya** — made of discipline.  
  **Status:** new in this form.  
  **Function:** second member of the classification.

- **bhāvanāmaya** — made of cultivation.  
  **Status:** previously encountered root vocabulary, new as merit-classification.  
  **Function:** third member of the classification.

- **vajropamasamādhi** — diamond-like concentration immediately preceding awakening/arhatship, here the locus of perfection of dhyāna and prajñā.  
  **Status:** previously encountered doctrinally.  
  **Function:** terminal Path attainment.

- **kuśalacetanā** — wholesome volition.  
  **Status:** previously encountered components, newly privileged definition.  
  **Function:** alternative strict definition of meritorious activity.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

### 1. Completion of vīrya is a deed of sustained invariance

The Tiṣya episode defines vigor through an extraordinary uninterrupted act: seven days, standing on one foot, one praise, one supreme field. Its doctrinal function is not merely devotional. It exemplifies effort that does not break under duration.

The Bhāṣya also explicitly links this to the previously noted shortening of the Bodhisattva career by nine kalpas.

### 2. Dhyāna and prajñā are perfected at the threshold of awakening

The first-pass file tentatively read `anantaram` as “immediately afterward” after the Tiṣya episode. The Bhāṣya corrects this.

It means:

> **immediately before awakening** (`bodheḥ pūrvasamanantaram`)

and locates the completion in `vajropamasamādhi`.

Thus the correct sequence is not:

```text
Tiṣya praise
→ immediately afterward concentration + wisdom
```

but:

```text
Tiṣya praise
→ vīrya perfected
...
long Bodhisattva career
...
vajropama-samādhi immediately before bodhi
→ dhyāna + prajñā perfected
```

### 3. Pāramitā means reaching the far shore of one’s own excellence

The Bhāṣya glosses the name through:

> *svasyāḥ saṃpadaḥ pāragamanāt pāramitāḥ*

They are perfections because each has gone to the far shore of its own excellence.

This is a stronger definition than merely “a virtue practiced very greatly.” A pāramitā is a capacity brought to its terminal completeness.

### 4. Merit is functionally articulated

The second half introduces a new analytic register. `Puṇya` is not treated as a single hidden substance accumulated behind actions. The Bhāṣya distributes merit across volition, bodily/verbal karma, loving-kindness, discipline, and associated dharmas according to their role.

The key is **functional predication**:

```text
x may be puṇya
x may be kriyā
x may be vastu
x may satisfy two or three of these descriptions
```

### 5. Volition reappears as the practical center

The alternative formulation—`kuśalacetanā paramārthena puṇyakriyā`—fits the wider Karma chapter remarkably well. The deepest practical unity of meritorious action is wholesome volition; the external forms are the determinate bases in which it becomes actual.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The existing Kārikā pass requires two corrections.

First, `dhīsamādhyor anantaram` should not be translated “wisdom and concentration were completed immediately afterward” after the Tiṣya praise. The Bhāṣya states explicitly that they are completed **immediately before awakening**, in the vajropama-samādhi.

Second, `puṇyaṃ kriyātha tadvastu trayaṃ` should not be flattened into “merit is activity, and its basis is threefold.” The Bhāṣya gives a subtler construction:

> **The three are, as appropriate, merit, activity, and basis—just as with the paths of karma.**

This preserves the variable functional relations inside the compound `puṇyakriyāvastu`.

## Light Organon Contact

The passage gives an unusually clean distinction between **activity and its field of actualization**.

```text
kuśalacetanā
    practical determining activity

vastu
    determinate field/basis

puṇya
    wholesome value and causal efficacy of that determination
```

The Bhāṣya does not let these collapse into one another. Yet neither are they externally independent. A single concrete event may bear several determinations at once depending on the relation under consideration.

This is exactly the sort of analysis in which the Kośa is strongest: not abstract moral praise, but the decomposition of a practical act into **volition, manifestation, field, accompaniment, and causal value**.
