## Source Cautions

The verse's repeated `utthita / vyutthita` is doctrinally decisive. The relevant field is not simply a person who possesses a certain attainment or rank, but one **freshly emerged** from an exceptional attainment or path transformation.

The Bhāṣya first states more generally that the Saṅgha headed by the Buddha is an exceptional karmic field, then singles out five types of persons for the special rule of immediate present-life fruition.

`āśrayaparivṛtti` should here be read cautiously as a transformation or alteration of the psychophysical basis/continuum. The passage does not yet warrant importing later Yogācāra technical meanings wholesale.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.55 identified `kṣetraviśeṣa`, an exceptional field, as one reason karma may become present-life-result karma. VAK 4.56 answers the natural next question:

```text
What kind of field is so powerful
that action done toward it
necessarily matures in this very life?
```

Vasubandhu first names the Saṅgha headed by the Buddha, and then identifies five particularly powerful personal fields.

### 1. One emerged from cessation attainment

A person emerging from `nirodhasamāpatti` obtains extraordinary peace of mind because that attainment resembles nirvāṇa.

Vasubandhu's striking formulation is:

```text
as though having gone to nirvāṇa
and just returned from it
```

The force of the field lies in the exceptional calm of the continuum immediately after emergence.

### 2. One emerged from araṇā

The continuum of one emerging from `araṇā` is pervaded by an extraordinarily elevated orientation toward preventing conflict or harm among immeasurable beings and by immeasurable cultivation of merit.

The field is therefore determined by the present configuration of the continuum:

```text
immeasurable beings
    + benevolent non-contention orientation
    + immeasurable merit-cultivation
        → exceptionally powerful field
```

### 3. One emerged from maitrī

Similarly, the continuum of one emerging from loving-kindness is pervaded by an exceptionally elevated wish for the happiness of immeasurable beings and by immeasurable cultivation of merit.

Here again, field-power is not an abstract status. It is a concrete transformed orientation of the continuum.

### 4. One emerged from the Path of Seeing

After emergence from `darśanamārga`, all afflictions abandoned by seeing have just been abandoned.

The Bhāṣya describes the resulting continuum as:

```text
pratyagra-āśraya-parivṛtti-nirmala
    freshly purified through transformation of the basis
```

The recipient is karmically exceptional because the continuum has just undergone a decisive purification.

### 5. One emerged from the fruit of arhatship

After arhat-fruition, all factors to be abandoned by cultivation have been abandoned. The continuum is freshly purified through a still more complete transformation of its basis.

This is the maximal case in the list.

The conclusion follows:

```text
benefit or injury
    done toward any of these five
        → fruit necessarily obtained
          in this very life
```

Vasubandhu then gives an important negative boundary. Those emerging from the remaining stages of the Path of Cultivation are **not** equally powerful fields, because their fruit-nature is not yet complete and their continuum is not freshly purified in the same way.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose and the derivation of the five fields.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratyagra** — fresh, newly arisen, newly transformed
  - **status:** rare/newly decisive in this local discussion
  - **function:** marks the immediacy of the transformed state after a decisive attainment

- **āśrayaparivṛtti** — transformation of the basis or supporting continuum
  - **status:** important technical expression; not to be overloaded with later Yogācāra meaning here
  - **function:** explains why Path of Seeing and arhat-fruition create exceptionally powerful karmic fields

- **araṇā** — non-contention, non-conflict attainment/practice
  - **status:** specialized meditative attainment
  - **function:** produces an immeasurably benevolent orientation of the continuum

- **puṇyakṣetra** — field of merit
  - **status:** standard Buddhist karmic term, locally sharpened
  - **function:** identifies a recipient whose condition greatly amplifies karmic efficacy

- **kāra / apakāra** — benefit / injury
  - **status:** ordinary vocabulary used technically here
  - **function:** symmetrical positive and negative action toward the exceptional field

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse concretizes `kṣetraviśeṣa` from VAK 4.55.

Its deepest doctrinal result is that the karmic efficacy of a recipient-field depends not merely on who the person is in abstraction, but on the **actual state of the continuum at the time of the action**.

```text
recipient-status alone
    is not the whole explanation

freshly transformed continuum
    → exceptional field-power
```

The five cases are unified by a common structure:

```text
exceptional attainment or path event
    ↓
freshly transformed continuum
    ↓
heightened field-power
    ↓
immediate present-life maturation
of benefit or injury directed toward it
```

The rule is symmetric with respect to ethical polarity:

```text
benefit
    → immediate favorable fruit

injury
    → immediate unfavorable fruit
```

What determines immediacy here is the extraordinary field, not merely the positive or negative character of the act.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.54 identified several sufficient causes of karmic determinacy. VAK 4.55 identified exceptional field as a cause of present-life maturation. VAK 4.56 now specifies the internal structure of such a field.

```text
FIELD-POWER
    = function of
      current transformed state
      of recipient continuum
```

More explicitly:

```text
attainment/path transformation
    → new continuum-state

new continuum-state
    → exceptional recipient relation

act toward exceptional recipient
    → temporally accelerated fruition
```

Thus:

```text
same external act
    + ordinary recipient-state
        → ordinary karmic schedule

same external act
    + freshly transformed recipient-state
        → immediate present-life result
```

This is relational causality in a strong form. The causal determination of an act is partly constituted by the state of the field into which it enters.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation remains basically correct, but the Bhāṣya requires the force of `utthita` to be preserved more strongly.

Preferred translation:

> **For those freshly emerged from cessation, non-contention, loving-kindness, the Path of Seeing, or the fruit of arhatship, the result of benefit or injury done toward them is experienced immediately.**

The word **freshly** is interpretive but justified by the Bhāṣya's repeated emphasis on `pratyagra`, newly transformed purity of the continuum.

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained Organon contact is unusually strong:

```text
relation R
    does not depend only on
    agent A and action X

it also depends on
    the determinate state of field F
```

The recipient is not a passive endpoint. A transformed field changes the causal force and temporal realization of the action entering it.

```text
ACTION
    + FIELD-STATE
        → RESULT-FORCE
        → RESULT-TIME
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives us a practical causal relation in which **the state of the object-field participates in determining the efficacy of the action itself**.

This remains Vasubandhu's karmic doctrine first; the broader logical use should be developed only after the Bhāṣya pass.
