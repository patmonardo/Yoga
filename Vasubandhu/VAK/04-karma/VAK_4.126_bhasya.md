# VAK_4.126_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.126**

> `yogapravartitaṃ karma sasamutthāpakaṃ tridhā |`
> `lipimudre sagaṇanaṃ kāvyaṃ saṃkhyā yathākramam || 4.126 ||`

Best current translation:

> Activity set in motion through a specific applied method, together with what gives rise to it, is threefold. Writing and sealing, calculation and poetry, and mental reckoning correspond respectively to bodily, verbal, and mental karma.

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya proceeds in five short determinations:

1. asks the nature (`svabhāva`) of five familiar worldly activities: writing, sealing, calculation, poetry, and reckoning;
2. glosses `yogapravartita` as activity initiated by a **specific method or means** (`upāyaviśeṣa`);
3. identifies the threefold karma as body, speech, and mind;
4. assigns writing/sealing to body and calculation/poetry to speech;
5. identifies `saṃkhyā` as mental karma: the mental collecting or reckoning of dharmas.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

“What is the nature of what in the world is called writing, sealing, calculation, poetry, and reckoning?

‘Activity set in motion through application, together with what produces it, is threefold; writing and sealing, calculation and poetry, and reckoning respectively.’

‘Set in motion through application’ means set in motion by a specific method or means. ‘Threefold karma’ means bodily, verbal, and mental karma.

Of these, writing and sealing are bodily karma set in motion through such application, together with what gives rise to them. Calculation and poetry are verbal karma. These [first four] are of the nature of the five aggregates. Reckoning is mental karma: the mental collecting/reckoning of dharmas.”

---

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** throughout the explanatory prose.

The opening question is expository rather than a hostile objection.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **`upāyaviśeṣa`** — a specific method, means, or technique by which an activity is initiated.
  - **status:** significant Bhāṣya gloss of `yoga` in this passage.
  - **function:** definition.

- **`samutthāpaka`** — that which gives rise to or sets an activity in motion.
  - **status:** previously encountered family of terms.
  - **function:** causal specification of an activity together with its initiating factor.

- **`saṃkalana`** — collecting, reckoning, putting together mentally.
  - **status:** important local gloss.
  - **function:** definition of `saṃkhyā`.

- **`saṃkhyā`** — here specifically mental reckoning/collecting of dharmas.
  - **status:** clarified against broader meanings such as mere numerical result.
  - **function:** classification as mental karma.

---

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya does not offer a general theory of “higher Art” or creative agency here. Its task is much narrower and taxonomic.

It asks how familiar skilled activities should be classified within the already established threefold system of karma.

The decisive gloss is:

```text
yogapravartita
    = upāyaviśeṣapravartita
    = set in motion by a specific method/means
```

Thus `yoga` here means **applied technique or method**, not contemplative Yoga in the later specialized sense.

The exact distribution is:

```text
kāyakarma
    lipi      writing
    mudrā     sealing / stamping

vākkarma
    gaṇanā    calculation
    kāvya     poetry

manaskarma
    saṃkhyā   mental reckoning
```

And `saṃkhyā` is explicitly defined:

```text
yan manasā saṃkalanaṃ dharmāṇām
    the mental collecting/reckoning of dharmas
```

This makes the taxonomy one of **operative channel**, not one of subject matter.

---

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The main result is that technical or learned activities remain analyzable through the ordinary karma triad:

```text
specific method
    ↓
activity initiated
    ↓
body / speech / mind
```

The Bhāṣya does not treat writing, calculation, poetry, and reckoning as a separate ontological class of action. They are ordinary karmic activities differentiated by the channel through which the learned method becomes actual.

The first four are said to be:

```text
pañcaskandhasvabhāva
    of the nature of the five aggregates
```

That prevents us from treating them as merely abstract symbolic objects. They are embodied events belonging to the aggregate-complex.

The distinction between `gaṇanā` and `saṃkhyā` is especially useful:

```text
gaṇanā
    verbal calculation / reckoning as expressed operation

saṃkhyā
    mental collecting/reckoning of dharmas
```

So two activities can concern number or enumeration while differing by karmic channel.

---

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass uncertainty around `yathākramam` is fully resolved.

The correct grouping is:

```text
lipi-mudre
    bodily

sa-gaṇanaṃ kāvyam
    verbal

saṃkhyā
    mental
```

`Yogapravartita` should be translated concretely as:

> set in motion by a specific method or technique

rather than importing a broad philosophical sense of Yoga.

`Saṃkhyā` should be rendered here as:

> mental reckoning / mental collecting

not merely “number” or “enumeration” as an objective product.

Recommended translation:

> Activity set in motion by a specific method, together with what gives rise to it, is threefold. Writing and sealing are bodily; calculation and poetry are verbal; mental reckoning is mental karma, respectively.

---

## Light Organon Contact

This passage gives a restrained but useful model of technical execution:

```text
method
    → initiating condition
    → operative channel
    → determinate activity
```

The strongest point for our purposes is not that the Kośa already contains a theory of programming or higher Art. It is that **learned method does not float free of embodied execution**.

A method becomes actual only as body, speech, or mind.

And mental reckoning is itself karma:

```text
manasā saṃkalanaṃ dharmāṇām
```

The internal organization of determinations is therefore counted as genuine activity, not as an inert representation preceding action.
