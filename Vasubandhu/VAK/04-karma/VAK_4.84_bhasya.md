# VAK_4.84_bhasya — Endowment and Manifestation of the Seven Wholesome Bodily-Verbal Courses

## Kārikā

**ārūpyāsaṃjñi-sattveṣu lābhataḥ sapta śeṣite /**  
**saṃmukhībhāvataś cāpi hitvā sa-narakān kurūn // 4.84 //**

> In the formless domain and among non-percipient beings, the seven occur by acquisition alone. In the remaining domains and destinies they also occur through present manifestation, except among hell beings and the Kurus.

[Kārikā study: VAK_4.84.md](./VAK_4.84.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
ārupyāsaṃjñisattveṣu lābhataḥ sapta

kāyikavācikāḥ sapta kuśalā ārūpyeṣvasaṃjñisattveṣu samanvāgamata eva /

ārūpyopapannānāmāryāṇāmatītānāgatānāsravasaṃvarasamanvāgamāt /

asaṃjñisattvānāṃ ca dhyānasaṃvarasamanvāgamāt /

yadbhūmyāśrayamāryeṇānāsravaṃ śīlamutpāditaṃ nirodhitaṃ bhavati tenārūpyeṣvatītena samanvāgato bhavati /

pañca bhūmyāśrayeṇa tvanāgatena /

śeṣite /

saṃmukhībhāvataścāpi hitvā sanarakān kurun

śeṣaḥ kṛtaḥ śeṣitaḥ /
yo 'nyo dhātuḥ śeṣito gatirvā /

tatraite sapta kuśalāḥ karmapathāḥ saṃmukhībhāvato 'pi saṃvidyante 'nyatra narakottarakurubhyaḥ /

te punastiryakpreteṣu saṃvaranirmuktā rūpadhātau tu saṃvarasaṃgṛhītā anyatrobhayathā /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya proceeds in four steps:

1. Identify the seven as the wholesome bodily and verbal karmapathas.
2. Explain why they can be possessed in the formless domain and among non-percipient beings even when they cannot presently manifest there.
3. Explain `śeṣita`: every remaining domain or destiny not covered by those special cases.
4. Distinguish whether the seven occur as formal restraint (`saṃvara`) or independently of such restraint in different destinations.

## Literal Prose Translation

“The seven wholesome bodily and verbal karmic courses exist in the formless attainments and among non-percipient beings only by way of endowment.

For noble ones born in the formless domain this is because they are endowed with past and future non-outflow restraint. And for non-percipient beings it is because they are endowed with dhyāna-restraint.

When a noble one has produced and then ceased non-outflow morality based upon a certain level, while in the formless attainments he is endowed with it as past. But with future restraint he is endowed on the basis of five levels.

‘Remaining’ means: what has been left over — whatever other domain or destiny remains.

There these seven wholesome karmic courses also exist by way of present manifestation, except among hell beings and the inhabitants of Uttarakuru.

Among animals and pretas these seven are independent of formal restraint; in the form domain they are included within restraint; elsewhere they occur in both ways.”

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The seven are bodily-verbal wholesome courses

The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies:

```text
sapta kuśalāḥ
    = seven wholesome bodily and verbal karmapathas
```

These are the wholesome counterparts of the three bodily and four verbal unwholesome courses.

### 2. Formless existence does not imply loss of the seven

The formless domain lacks the ordinary bodily and verbal bases for present enactment. Yet Vasubandhu does not infer:

```text
no bodily/verbal manifestation
    → no possession of bodily/verbal wholesome dharmas
```

Instead, noble beings born there may remain endowed with **past and future non-outflow restraint** (`atītānāgatānāsrava-saṃvara`).

Thus the seven can belong to the continuum through `samanvāgama` even though they cannot be presently enacted through body or speech.

This is more precise than saying simply that “restraint persists without body.” The Bhāṣya grounds the endowment in a previously generated and now past non-outflow discipline, together with future non-outflow restraint.

### 3. Non-percipient beings possess them through dhyāna-restraint

For `asaṃjñi-sattva`, the ground differs:

```text
asaṃjñi-sattva
    → dhyāna-saṃvara-samanvāgama
```

The seven are possessed because the continuum is endowed with restraint born from dhyāna.

So two superficially similar cases have different grounds:

```text
formless noble being
    → non-outflow restraint

non-percipient being
    → dhyāna restraint
```

### 4. `śeṣita` is a residual domain/destiny operator

Vasubandhu glosses the compact form explicitly:

```text
śeṣaḥ kṛtaḥ śeṣitaḥ
```

“what has been made the remainder is the remaining [case].”

It includes whatever other domain (`dhātu`) or destiny (`gati`) remains after the exceptional cases have been specified.

### 5. Present manifestation is broadly possible, but not in hell or Uttarakuru

In the remaining relevant domains and destinies, the seven wholesome karmapathas may also occur through `saṃmukhībhāva`, present manifestation.

But two destinations are excluded:

```text
naraka
uttarakuru
```

The preceding verses have already supplied the structural reasons why these environments do not support the relevant active wholesome bodily-verbal restraint in the ordinary way.

### 6. Formal restraint and wholesome karmapatha are not extensionally identical

The final sentence is especially important:

```text
tiryak + preta
    → saṃvara-nirmukta

rūpa-dhātu
    → saṃvara-saṃgṛhīta

elsewhere
    → ubhayathā
```

Among animals and pretas, the seven wholesome bodily-verbal courses are **independent of formal restraint**.

In the form domain, they are **included within restraint**.

Elsewhere, they may occur **either as restraint or independently of it**.

Therefore:

```text
wholesome bodily/verbal karmapatha
    ≠ necessarily formal vow/restraint
```

A wholesome abstention can be genuinely present without belonging to an undertaken discipline of restraint.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / Vaibhāṣika doctrinal exposition

No major objection-response dialectic is introduced in this unit. The prose explains the compact distribution stated by the Kārikā.

## New Technical Terms

### `anāsrava-saṃvara`

- **Meaning:** non-outflow restraint; restraint belonging to the undefiled path.
- **Status:** previously encountered conceptually; technically decisive here.
- **Function:** explains endowment with the seven bodily-verbal wholesome courses among noble beings born in the formless domain.

### `dhyāna-saṃvara`

- **Meaning:** restraint generated through dhyāna.
- **Status:** previously encountered in the discipline taxonomy.
- **Function:** explains endowment with the seven among non-percipient beings.

### `saṃvara-saṃgṛhīta`

- **Meaning:** included or comprised within formal restraint.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-level refinement of the Kārikā distribution.
- **Function:** distinguishes the form-domain mode of the seven wholesome bodily-verbal courses.

### `saṃvara-nirmukta`

- **Meaning:** independent of / not included within formal restraint.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-level refinement.
- **Function:** characterizes the seven among animals and pretas.

### `śeṣita`

- **Meaning:** the residual case; whatever domain or destiny remains after specified exceptions.
- **Status:** explicit Kārikā term clarified by Bhāṣya.
- **Function:** scope operator for the distribution rule.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.84 completes the realm-distribution sequence by forcing three distinctions that should not be collapsed:

```text
1. course-type
2. continuum endowment
3. present manifestation
```

It now adds a fourth:

```text
4. inclusion within formal restraint
```

Thus the seven bodily-verbal wholesome courses can be represented along two independent axes:

```text
endowed / manifest

and

restraint-included / restraint-independent
```

This is why simple behavioral absence is insufficient to classify wholesome conduct.

A being may:

- possess restraint without presently manifesting it;
- presently abstain without belonging to formal restraint;
- possess and manifest restraint;
- lack the bodily/verbal conditions for manifestation while retaining endowment.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass Kārikā interpretation correctly identified the endowment/manifestation distinction, but the Bhāṣya requires two refinements.

First, the formless case should not be generalized as though any formless being possesses the seven. The prose specifically grounds the case in **noble beings born there who are endowed with past and future non-outflow restraint**.

Second, `virati` should not automatically be equated with formal `saṃvara`. The final sentence expressly distinguishes wholesome karmapathas that are `saṃvara-nirmukta` from those `saṃvara-saṃgṛhīta`.

## Logical Determination

The structure can be represented as:

```text
WholesomeCourse(C)
    ↓
may be Endowed(C)
    independently of
PresentManifestation(C)

and

may be
    IncludedInRestraint(C)
or
    IndependentOfRestraint(C)
```

The important non-equivalences are:

```text
Endowed
    ≠ Manifest

Wholesome Abstention
    ≠ Formal Restraint

No Present Violation
    ≠ Present Wholesome Restraint
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives a multi-dimensional rather than binary practical ontology.

## Light Organon Contact

The strong reusable point is not the cosmological geography but the distinction among **capacity, actualization, and normative registration**.

A system may possess a valid capacity that is not executable in its current environment; conversely, a valid local action may occur without having been generated by a globally registered discipline or policy.

```text
Capability
    ≠ current execution

Current valid execution
    ≠ formal policy membership
```

This is a useful constraint for any practical reasoning system, but it should remain secondary to Vasubandhu’s own doctrinal distinction.

## Compact Result

```text
Formless noble beings:
    seven bodily/verbal wholesome courses
    → endowment through past/future non-outflow restraint

Non-percipient beings:
    seven
    → endowment through dhyāna-restraint

Remaining domains/destinies:
    seven may also manifest
    except hell and Uttarakuru

Animals + pretas:
    seven are restraint-independent

Form domain:
    seven are included in restraint

Elsewhere:
    both modes occur
```

VAK 4.84 therefore closes the distribution problem by showing that **possession, manifestation, and formal restraint are distinct determinations**.
