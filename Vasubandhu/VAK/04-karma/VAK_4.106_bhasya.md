# VAK 4.106 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `dūṣaṇaṃ mātur arhantyā niyatisthasya māraṇam | bodhisattvasya śaikṣasya saṃghāya dvārahārikā || 4.106 ||`

**Best current translation:** Sexual violation of one’s mother when she is an arhat; killing a bodhisattva established in certainty; killing a trainee; and taking away the Saṅgha’s access to comfort/support—

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.106.md](./VAK_4.106.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
kimānantaryairevāvaśyaṃ narakeṣūpapadyate /
ānantaryasabhāgairapyavaśyamupapadyate /

natvanantaramevetyapare /
katamāni tānītyāha

dūṣaṇaṃ māturarhantyā niyatisthasya māraṇam /
bodhisattvasya śaikṣasya saṃghāyadvārahārikā // VAkK_4.106 //

ānantaryasabhāgāni pañcamaṃ stūpabhedanam /

etāni pañca pañcānāmānatnaryāṇāṃ yathākramam sabhāgāni /

yadi mātaramarhantīṃ dūṣayatyabrahmacaryakaraṇāt niyatipatītaṃ bodhisattvaṃ mārayati śaikṣaṃ mārayati saṃghasya sukhāyadvārikaṃ harati stūpabhedaṃ karoti /
```

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya first asks whether only the five ānantaryas necessarily lead to hell. It answers that the acts analogous to ānantarya also necessarily produce hell rebirth. A reported alternative view (`apare`) qualifies this: the hell rebirth need not follow immediately in the next existence.

The Kārikā then begins the fivefold list of these analogues, completed in 4.107.

## Literal Prose Translation

> Is it only through the ānantarya acts that one necessarily takes rebirth in the hells? One necessarily takes rebirth there also through acts analogous to the ānantaryas.
>
> Others say, however, that this rebirth does not necessarily occur immediately in the next existence.
>
> What are these acts? Sexual violation of one’s mother when she is an arhat; killing a bodhisattva who has entered certainty; killing a trainee; taking away the Saṅgha’s access to comfort or support; and, as the fifth, destroying a stūpa.
>
> These five are, respectively, analogous to the five ānantaryas.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Technical ānantarya and ānantarya-analogue are distinct

The Bhāṣya does not expand the canonical five. Instead it introduces a second class:

```text
ānantarya
    strict technical category

ānantarya-sabhāga
    act structurally analogous in gravity
```

The analogue resembles an ānantarya through the exceptional field or function that it attacks, without thereby becoming one of the five technical ānantaryas.

### 2. The five analogues

The five acts are:

1. sexual violation of one’s mother when she is an arhat;
2. killing a bodhisattva established in certainty;
3. killing a noble trainee (`śaikṣa`);
4. depriving the Saṅgha of access to its means of comfort/support;
5. destroying a stūpa.

The fifth item is supplied by 4.107, but the Bhāṣya explicitly says that all five correspond in order to the five ānantaryas.

### 3. Necessary hell result, but disputed immediacy

The primary statement is strong:

```text
ānantarya-sabhāga
    → necessarily hell rebirth
```

But `apare`, “others,” distinguish this from strict ānantarya by denying that the hell rebirth must be temporally immediate:

```text
strict ānantarya
    → immediate next-life maturation

analogue
    → hell maturation certain
      but not necessarily immediately next
```

This is an important doctrinal distinction between certainty of destination and immediacy of maturation.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / received doctrine** — ānantarya-analogues also necessarily lead to hell rebirth.
- **Voice: reported teachers (`apare`)** — their hell rebirth need not occur immediately in the next existence.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — enumerates the five analogues and states their ordered correspondence to the five ānantaryas.

## New Technical Terms

- **ānantarya-sabhāga** — an act analogous to an ānantarya in karmic gravity without being one of the canonical five.
  - **status:** newly explicit in this passage.
  - **function:** secondary classification of exceptionally grave karma.

- **niyati-patita bodhisattva** — a bodhisattva who has entered certainty or fixed destiny toward Buddhahood.
  - **status:** newly explicit in this section.
  - **function:** exceptional karmic field.

- **śaikṣa** — noble trainee whose Path has not yet reached arhatship.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** exceptional karmic field.

- **saṃghasya sukhāya-dvārikā** — access or means by which the Saṅgha obtains comfort/support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the compact Kārikā expression.
  - **function:** material condition of communal life.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The verse extends the analysis of exceptional karmic fields beyond the strict five ānantaryas.

The field can be exceptional because of:

```text
combined status
    mother + arhat

assured future
    fixed bodhisattva

active Path-development
    śaikṣa

communal function
    Saṅgha support

symbolic/religious locus
    stūpa
```

Thus karmic gravity is sensitive not only to what a being presently is, but also to relation, attained quality, assured trajectory, and institutional function.

The distinction between strict identity and analogy is essential:

```text
same structural gravity
    does not imply
same technical category
```

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass phrase “violation of one’s mother when she is an arhat” should be sharpened to **sexual violation**, because the Bhāṣya glosses `dūṣaṇa` as `abrahmacaryakaraṇa`.

Likewise, `saṃghāya dvārahārikā` should not be rendered vaguely as “depriving the Saṅgha of its means of support.” The Bhāṣya specifies **taking away the access or means by which the Saṅgha obtains comfort/support**.

A more precise translation is:

> Sexual violation of one’s mother when she is an arhat; killing a bodhisattva established in certainty; killing a noble trainee; and taking away the Saṅgha’s access to comfort or support—

The fivefold list is completed in 4.107.

## Light Organon Contact

The strongest structural point is the distinction between **identity** and **analogy**.

```text
ānantarya
    = exact doctrinal species

ānantarya-sabhāga
    = another act sharing its decisive structure
```

This is not mere resemblance by surface feature. The analogue reproduces the kind of exceptional negation that makes the strict ānantarya grave: negation of benefactor, quality-field, developing Path, communal condition, or sacred locus.

The classification therefore shows a mature practical logic: two acts may differ in species while sharing a higher-order structure of karmic gravity.
