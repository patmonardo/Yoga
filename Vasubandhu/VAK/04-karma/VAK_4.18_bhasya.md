# VAK_4.18_bhasya — Abandonment Restraint and Cognitive Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.18**

```iast
anāgamye prahāṇākhyau tāv ānantaryamārgajau |
saṃprajñānasmṛtī dve tu manaindriyasaṃvarau ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> In the preliminary concentration, those two restraints born in the uninterrupted paths are called abandonment restraints. Mind-restraint and faculty-restraint, however, each have both clear comprehension and recollection as their nature.

## Source Cautions

The working e-text is based principally on Pradhan 1975 with corrections and is explicitly not a critical edition. Exact transmitted forms and Pradhan page-line references are preserved.

The Kārikā file provisionally read `saṃprajñāna` and `smṛti` distributively, assigning the former to mind-restraint and the latter to faculty-restraint. The Bhāṣya explicitly rejects that reading. Vasubandhu explains that the repeated dual expression indicates that **each** restraint has a twofold nature: both `smṛti` and `saṃprajñāna`.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu resumes the two mind-following restraints of VAK 4.17: meditation-born restraint (`dhyānasaṃvara`) and uncontaminated restraint (`anāsravasaṃvara`). He asks under what circumstances these receive the additional designation `prahāṇasaṃvara`, “abandonment restraint.”

The answer is restricted to the nine uninterrupted paths (`navasv ānantaryamārgeṣu`) of the preliminary concentration (`anāgamya`). In those moments the two restraints are called abandonment restraints because through them there is abandonment both of bad conduct (`dauḥśīlya`) and of the defilements that generate that bad conduct (`tatsamutthāpakāḥ kleśāḥ`).

Vasubandhu immediately prevents identification of the classes. He constructs a four-cornered analysis (`catuṣkoṭika`) asking whether something may be meditation restraint without being abandonment restraint, abandonment restraint without being meditation restraint, both, or neither.

The four cases are:

1. **Meditation restraint but not abandonment restraint:** contaminated meditation restraint outside the uninterrupted paths of the anāgamya.
2. **Abandonment restraint but not contaminated meditation restraint:** uncontaminated restraint within those uninterrupted paths.
3. **Both:** contaminated meditation restraint within the uninterrupted paths of the anāgamya.
4. **Neither:** uncontaminated restraint outside those uninterrupted paths, relative to the first formulation.

Vasubandhu says an analogous fourfold analysis must be made when beginning instead from uncontaminated restraint. The point is that `dhyānasaṃvara`, `anāsravasaṃvara`, and `prahāṇasaṃvara` cross-cut one another; none is simply reducible to another.

The discussion then turns to scriptural statements praising restraint of body, speech, and mind and to the formula of dwelling restrained through the eye faculty. This raises a new question: what is the nature of mind-restraint (`manaḥsaṃvara`) and faculty-restraint (`indriyasaṃvara`)?

Vasubandhu states explicitly that these are **not** avijñapti-discipline (`nāitāv avijñaptiśīlasvabhāvau`). Instead, both are constituted by recollection (`smṛti`) and clear comprehension (`saṃprajñāna`). The repeated dual expression is deliberate: it prevents a distributive `yathāsaṃkhya` reading in which one factor would correspond only to one restraint.

Thus:

```text
manaḥsaṃvara
    = smṛti + saṃprajñāna

indriyasaṃvara
    = smṛti + saṃprajñāna
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the special designation `prahāṇasaṃvara` for meditation-born and uncontaminated restraints under specified path conditions.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — constructs the `catuṣkoṭika` to show that meditation restraint and abandonment restraint are overlapping but non-identical classes.

**Voice: canonical citation** — verses praising restraint of body, speech, and mind, and the formula concerning restraint of the eye faculty.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks and answers the nature of mind-restraint and faculty-restraint, explicitly denying that they are avijñapti-discipline.

## New Technical Terms

- **prahāṇasaṃvara** — abandonment restraint.
  - **status:** introduced as a special designation in the Bhāṣya/Kārikā sequence.
  - **function:** names restraint specifically insofar as it participates in abandoning bad conduct and its generating defilements.
- **ānantaryamārga** — uninterrupted / immediate path.
  - **status:** technical Path term, newly operative in this Karma discussion.
  - **function:** specifies the path-moments in which restraint receives the abandonment designation.
- **anāgamya** — preliminary concentration / threshold attainment.
  - **status:** previously doctrinally known, newly operative here.
  - **function:** level in which the relevant nine uninterrupted paths occur.
- **tatsamutthāpaka kleśa** — defilement that gives rise to that [bad conduct].
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory phrase.
  - **function:** shows that abandonment restraint targets not only misconduct but its afflictive source.
- **catuṣkoṭika** — four-cornered analysis.
  - **status:** previously encountered methodological form.
  - **function:** demonstrates partial overlap and non-identity between doctrinal classes.
- **manaḥsaṃvara** — restraint of mind.
  - **status:** canonical restraint category made technically explicit here.
  - **function:** cognitive restraint whose nature is both recollection and clear comprehension.
- **indriyasaṃvara** — restraint of the faculties.
  - **status:** canonical restraint category made technically explicit here.
  - **function:** faculty restraint whose nature is likewise both recollection and clear comprehension.
- **saṃprajñāna** — clear comprehension / discriminative awareness.
  - **status:** new technical factor in this immediate sequence.
  - **function:** one of the two factors constituting both cognitive restraints.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Abandonment restraint is a functional designation

```text
dhyānasaṃvara
anāsravasaṃvara
    ↓
when occurring in the nine ānantaryamārgas
of the anāgamya
    ↓
prahāṇasaṃvara
```

The reason is explicitly causal and practical:

```text
prahāṇa of dauḥśīlya
    +
prahāṇa of the kleśas
that generate dauḥśīlya
```

Therefore `prahāṇasaṃvara` is not simply a third substance added to the earlier two restraints. It is a designation they acquire under a determinate Path-function.

### The classes cross-cut

The `catuṣkoṭika` blocks identity:

```text
dhyānasaṃvara
    ≠ prahāṇasaṃvara

anāsravasaṃvara
    ≠ prahāṇasaṃvara
```

Rather, the extension of the predicates varies according to level, contamination-status, and path-moment.

### Mind and faculty restraint are not avijñapti

This is the major categorical distinction of the second half:

```text
prātimokṣa / dhyāna / anāsrava restraint
    discussed as avijñapti

manaḥsaṃvara / indriyasaṃvara
    not avijñaptiśīla
    = smṛti + saṃprajñāna
```

The chapter therefore uses `saṃvara` across ontologically different kinds of dharma. The common term does not imply one common substantial nature.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

Two corrections are required.

First, `prahāṇākhyau` should be read narrowly:

> In the preliminary concentration, those two restraints born in the uninterrupted paths are called abandonment restraints.

The designation is not universal for all instances of meditation-born and uncontaminated restraint.

Second, the final pāda must not be translated distributively as:

```text
saṃprajñāna → mind-restraint
smṛti → faculty-restraint
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly says the dual is repeated to prevent exactly that `yathāsaṃkhya` reading. A better translation is:

> Mind-restraint and faculty-restraint each have two natures: recollection and clear comprehension.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.18 adds a strong classificatory discipline:

```text
same designation
    does not imply
same substantial nature
```

and conversely:

```text
same restraint-dharma
    may acquire a new designation
    through a specific function
```

The first half is a case of **functional predication**:

```text
restraint
    considered in its abandoning function
    → prahāṇasaṃvara
```

The second half is a case of **shared functional name across distinct ontology**:

```text
saṃvara
    may name avijñapti-based restraint
    or cognitive restraint
```

Thus the Bhāṣya repeatedly refuses to infer identity of dharma from identity of vocabulary.

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is methodological:

```text
predicate identity
    ≠
substantial identity
```

A determination may receive a name because of what it does in a particular relation, while another determination may receive the same general name from a different ground. This is highly relevant to later systematic reconstruction, but the present pass should retain Vasubandhu's own classificatory distinctions without forcing them into a single metaphysical substrate.
