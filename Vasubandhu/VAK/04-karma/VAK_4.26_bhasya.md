# VAK_4.26_bhasya — Acquisition of the Three Restraints

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.26**

```iast
dhyānajo dhyānabhūmyaiva labhyate 'nāsravas tayā |
āryayā prātimokṣākhyaḥ paravijñāpanādibhiḥ ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> Meditation-born restraint is acquired together with a dhyāna-level mind; uncontaminated restraint is acquired through that same meditative ground in its noble, uncontaminated form. Prātimokṣa restraint is acquired through notification by another and the other recognized modes of ordination.

Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.26.md`.

## Source Cautions

The electronic source reads `dhyanabhūmyaiva` at [211|23], while the expected form is `dhyānabhūmyaiva`. The exact source reading is preserved.

At [212|03], `saṃdhād` appears where the context concerns acquisition from the `saṃgha`; this may be an e-text irregularity. No silent correction is imposed in the quoted source.

The source also has `ūpasaṃgrahaṇārtham` at [212|04]. The long Vinaya list at [212|04-212|09] is preserved as transmitted and may contain minor e-text irregularities.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu turns from possession of restraint to acquisition: `athaite saṃvarāḥ kathaṃ labhyante` — “How, then, are these restraints acquired?”

Meditation-born restraint is acquired when a dhyāna-level mind is acquired. Vasubandhu explicitly includes both a fundamental (`maulīya`) and a neighboring or preparatory (`sāmantakīya`) dhyāna-level mind, provided it is contaminated (`sāsrava`). The restraint is acquired at the same time because it is `sahabhū`, co-arising with that mind.

Uncontaminated restraint is acquired through that same dhyāna-ground when the ground is itself uncontaminated (`anāsrava`) and noble (`ārya`). Vasubandhu notes six uncontaminated dhyāna grounds and postpones the fuller account.

Prātimokṣa restraint is ordinarily acquired through `paravijñapti`, formal notification by another. Vasubandhu then distinguishes acquisition from the Saṃgha and from an individual. The `ādi` in `paravijñāpanādibhiḥ` is crucial: according to the Vinayavibhāṣikas there are ten recognized modes of ordination/acquisition, including exceptional cases where prātimokṣa is not dependent on another person's formal manifestation. He therefore concludes that prātimokṣa restraint is not necessarily `vijñaptyadhīna`.

The passage then asks how long prātimokṣa restraint is to be undertaken, opening the material of VAK 4.27.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — general account of the acquisition of the three restraints.

**Voice: Vinayavibhāṣikāḥ** — the doctrine that ordination is tenfold.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — uses the Vinaya enumeration to explain `ādi` and reject the claim that prātimokṣa is universally dependent on another person's manifestation.

## New Technical Terms

- **maulīya** — fundamental, belonging to the principal dhyāna; **status:** related to earlier `maula`; **function:** specifies one kind of dhyāna-level citta.
- **sāmantakīya** — neighboring / preparatory; **status:** new here; **function:** specifies an adjacent meditative level.
- **sahabhū** — co-arising; **status:** important causal term; **function:** explains simultaneous acquisition of dhyānasaṃvara and dhyāna-level citta.
- **paravijñapti / paravijñāpana** — notification by another; **status:** new acquisition term; **function:** ordinary mode of prātimokṣa acquisition.
- **upasaṃpad** — ordination; **status:** Vinaya technical term; **function:** general name for recognized modes of monastic acquisition.

## Doctrinal Determination

```text
acquisition of sāsrava dhyāna-level citta
    maulīya or sāmantakīya
        ↓
co-arising dhyānasaṃvara
```

```text
acquisition of the same dhyāna-ground
as anāsrava / ārya
        ↓
anāsrava-saṃvara
```

For prātimokṣa:

```text
ordinary case
    paravijñapti
        ↓
prātimokṣasaṃvara
```

but:

```text
paravijñāpanādibhiḥ
    = notification by another
      plus recognized alternative Vinaya procedures
```

Therefore:

```text
prātimokṣasaṃvara
    is not necessarily dependent
    on another person's vijñapti
```

Vasubandhu also distinguishes acquisition from the Saṃgha from acquisition from an individual, without requiring a full reconstruction of Vinaya procedure here.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā pass was broadly correct but too strong in making interpersonal declaration appear universally necessary. The Bhāṣya requires:

> Prātimokṣa restraint is ordinarily acquired through notification by another, but `ādi` includes recognized alternative modes, so such restraint is not universally dependent upon another person's manifestation.

The first half is also sharpened: dhyānasaṃvara is acquired together with a dhyāna-level citta because it co-arises with that citta.

## Logical Determination

```text
same genus: saṃvara

acquisition differs by adequate ground:
    dhyāna-level citta
    noble uncontaminated meditative ground
    valid disciplinary procedure
```

The institutional case is procedurally plural and cannot be reduced to a single outward speech event.

## Organon Contact Point

The safe point is simply:

```text
form of acquisition
    is determined by
    the kind of ground capable of generating the restraint
```

No stronger reconstruction is needed in this pass.
