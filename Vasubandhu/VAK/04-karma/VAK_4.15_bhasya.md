## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya begins from the question generated by VAK 4.14:

```text
How does one become
    an upāsaka,
    an upavāsa observer,
    a śrāmaṇera,
    or a bhikṣu?
```

The answer is governed by one repeated construction:

```text
virati-samādāna
    undertaking abstention
```

The four statuses correspond numerically (`yathāsaṃkhyam`) to four scopes of undertaken restraint:

```text
five
    → upāsaka

eight
    → upavāsastha

ten
    → śrāmaṇera

all prohibited bodily and verbal actions
    → bhikṣu
```

The status is therefore not established merely by social designation or by accidental non-performance. It is established through the undertaking of abstention with a determinate scope.

---

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

How does this person become an upāsaka, how one established in the upavāsa, and so on up to a bhikṣu?

The enumeration is to be understood respectively.

By undertaking abstention from five dharmas that are to be avoided, one becomes established in the restraint of an upāsaka: from killing, taking what is not given, sexual misconduct, false speech, and drinking surā, maireya, or intoxicating liquor.

By undertaking abstention from eight, one becomes established in the upavāsa: from killing, taking what is not given, sexual activity, false speech, intoxicating drink, perfumes-garlands-anointing together with dancing-singing-instrumental music, high and luxurious beds, and eating at an improper time.

By undertaking abstention from ten, one becomes a śrāmaṇera. These same are observed together with abstention from accepting gold and silver; here dancing-singing-instrumental music and perfumes-garlands-anointing are divided into two, making ten.

By undertaking abstention from all prohibited bodily and verbal actions, one is called a bhikṣu.

This is the prātimokṣa restraint.

### Philosophical Translation

The four substantive forms of prātimokṣa restraint are determined by the scope of abstention formally undertaken. Lay restraint comprises five prohibited fields; the upavāsa observance expands this to eight; novice discipline comprises ten; and full monastic restraint extends to the complete field of prohibited bodily and verbal action. The operative principle throughout is not mere omission but `virati-samādāna`, the deliberate undertaking of restraint.

## New Technical Terms

- **virati-samādāna** — undertaking or acceptance of abstention
  - **status:** Bhāṣya makes the compound technically operative beyond the Kārikā's compressed `virati-graha`
  - **function:** definition of the condition by which a restraint-status is established

- **varjanīya-dharma** — a dharma/action to be avoided
  - **status:** explanatory prose terminology
  - **function:** specifies the field toward which restraint is directed

- **jātarūpa-rajata-pratigraha** — acceptance of gold and silver
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya enumeration
  - **function:** distinguishes novice tenfold restraint from the upavāsa eightfold observance

- **yathāsaṃkhyam** — respectively, according to numerical order
  - **status:** prose clarification
  - **function:** fixes the correspondence between 5/8/10/all and the four disciplinary statuses

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Fivefold upāsaka restraint

The five are explicitly enumerated:

```text
1. prāṇātipāta
   killing

2. adattādāna
   taking what is not given

3. kāmamithyācāra
   sexual misconduct

4. mṛṣāvāda
   false speech

5. surā-maireya-madya-pāna
   drinking intoxicating liquor
```

The Bhāṣya therefore confirms that the lay five are not simply five arbitrary disciplinary rules; they are the specific field whose abstention constitutes `upāsaka-saṃvara`.

### 2. Eightfold upavāsa restraint

The eightfold observance substitutes complete celibacy (`abrahmacarya`) for the lay rule against sexual misconduct and adds restrictions concerning adornment/entertainment, beds, and untimely eating.

The counting is doctrinally important: in the eightfold observance the entertainment and adornment materials are treated together as one field.

### 3. Tenfold novice restraint

The novice ten are generated from the same expanded discipline, but:

```text
entertainment
    and
adornment
```

are counted separately, and abstention from accepting gold and silver is added.

Thus the numerical increase is not produced merely by adding two wholly new behavioral fields; part of it results from a different internal articulation of the same disciplinary material.

### 4. Complete bhikṣu restraint

The bhikṣu is defined not by a final finite numeral but by extension over:

```text
sarve varjanīyāḥ kāya-vāk-karmāṇi
    all prohibited bodily and verbal actions
```

This is the qualitative transition from enumerated restraint to complete disciplinary scope.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing Kārikā translation is structurally correct, but the Bhāṣya strengthens two points:

1. `virati-graha` should be heard technically as **undertaking abstention**, closely reflected by the prose `virati-samādāna`.
2. `sarvebhyaḥ varjyebhyaḥ` is specifically glossed as all prohibited **bodily and verbal actions**.

The best translation may therefore be sharpened to:

> Through undertaking abstention from five, eight, ten, or all prohibited bodily and verbal actions, there is respectively the restraint-status of an upāsaka, one established in the upavāsa, a novice, or a bhikṣu.

---

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.15 is not merely a quantitative ladder.

```text
scope of undertaken abstention
    → determines
restraint-type
```

But the numerical progression is internally heterogeneous:

```text
5 → 8
    expanded field

8 → 10
    expanded field
    + finer differentiation of prior material

10 → all
    transition from finite enumeration
    to complete disciplinary extension
```

This means that substantial restraint-type is determined by an articulated field of prohibition, not by number alone.

A second distinction is now secure:

```text
mere absence of misconduct
    ≠
virati-samādāna
```

Prātimokṣa restraint is a positively undertaken practical determination.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained logical contact is the relation between **determinate extension** and **practical status**:

```text
a field of prohibited actions
    ↓
undertaken abstention over that field
    ↓
corresponding restraint-status
```

The Bhāṣya prevents us from reducing the discipline either to an inner intention alone or to a list of externally omitted acts. The determination is the undertaken relation of the agent to a specified field of possible action.

No stronger systematic identification is required at this stage.
