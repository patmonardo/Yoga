# VAK_4.32_bhasya — The Objects and Meaning of the Three Refuges

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
buddhasaṃghakarān dharmān aśaikṣān ubhayāṃś ca saḥ |
nirvāṇaṃ ceti śaraṇaṃ yo yāti śaraṇatrayam ||
```

**Working translation**

> Whoever goes to the Three Refuges takes refuge in the aśaikṣa dharmas that make a Buddha, in both the śaikṣa and aśaikṣa dharmas that make the Saṅgha, and in nirvāṇa.

See: `VAK_4.32.md`.

## 3. Source Cautions

The source contains several obvious orthographic irregularities (`budha`, `harman`, `klelśa`, etc.). They are preserved in the quoted source rather than silently normalized.

The Kārikā must not be read as asserting:

```text
Buddha = aśaikṣa dharmas only
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly rejects that formulation. The verse says `buddhakara` — **Buddha-making / Buddha-constituting dharmas** — and Vasubandhu uses this distinction to preserve the status of the living support (`āśraya`).

---

## 4. Natural Argument

### A. What is the object when one goes to the Buddha for refuge?

The object is the set of **aśaikṣa dharmas that make one a Buddha**.

Vasubandhu explains them as those dharmas by whose predominance that personal continuum is called “Buddha,” or by whose acquisition one becomes capable of complete awakening (`sarvāvabodhasāmarthya`).

He then specifies:

```text
kṣayajñāna and the associated dharmas
```

The physical body cannot itself be the decisive refuge-object, because the `rūpakāya` does not differ in the relevant way before and after the attainment of Buddhahood.

### B. One Buddha or all Buddhas?

Refuge is directed, by characteristic (`lakṣaṇataḥ`), toward **all Buddhas**, because the Path does not differ in defining characteristic (`mārgasyāvilakṣaṇatvāt`).

The object is therefore not restricted to one historical bearer considered as an isolated individual.

### C. What is the Saṅgha-refuge?

One takes refuge in the **śaikṣa and aśaikṣa dharmas that make the Saṅgha**.

These are the dharmas by whose acquisition the eight noble persons (`aṣṭau pudgalāḥ`) constitute the noble Saṅgha.

Again, one goes by characteristic to all Saṅghas because the Path is without difference in characteristic.

The sūtra statement that refuge also extends to the Saṅgha that will exist in the future is explained as showing the protective efficacy of the refuge even before that future Saṅgha is directly present.

### D. What is the Dharma-refuge?

The Dharma-refuge is **nirvāṇa**, specifically `pratisaṃkhyānirodha`.

Why nirvāṇa?

Because the pacification of kleśas and suffering, in one's own continuum and in others', has one defining characteristic:

```text
śānti
```

The refuge-object here is therefore cessation itself, not merely the verbal teaching that indicates it.

### E. Does this mean that the Buddha is nothing but a collection of aśaikṣa dharmas?

An objection follows immediately.

If the Buddha were simply identical with aśaikṣa dharmas, why would maliciously causing the Tathāgata to bleed constitute an ānantarya offense?

The Vaibhāṣikas answer that harming the support (`āśraya`) also harms the aśaikṣa dharmas dependent upon it.

Vasubandhu then makes the more important textual clarification:

```text
The treatise does not say:
    “the aśaikṣa dharmas alone are the Buddha.”

It says:
    “the Buddha-making dharmas.”
```

Therefore the objection presupposes an identity claim the text itself does not make.

If one collapsed bearer and constituting determination completely, absurd consequences would follow: a Buddha abiding in an ordinary (`laukika`) mind would cease to be Buddha; a noble person would cease to belong to the Saṅgha whenever the relevant Path-state was not presently manifest; and, by analogy, because morality makes a monk, morality itself would have to be the monk.

The correct relation is constitutive rather than simply identical.

### F. Alternative view

Some say that one who takes refuge in the Buddha takes refuge specifically in the **eighteen distinctive Buddha-dharmas** (`aṣṭādaśāveṇikā buddhadharmāḥ`).

Vasubandhu records this as an alternative position without making it the main determination of the passage.

### G. What is the intrinsic nature of going for refuge?

Vasubandhu asks:

```text
kiṃsvabhāvāni śaraṇagamanāni?
```

The answer is strikingly concrete:

```text
vāgvijñapti-svabhāvāni
```

The acts of going for refuge have **verbal manifestation as their intrinsic nature**.

Thus refuge is not treated merely as an inward belief-state. It is an enacted verbal determination.

### H. What does “refuge” mean?

```text
trāṇārthaḥ śaraṇārthaḥ
```

“Refuge” means **protection**.

Why?

Because through reliance on these refuges there is ultimate release from all suffering (`sarvaduḥkhātyantanirmokṣa`).

The cited verses then contrast ordinary fear-driven refuges in mountains, forests, parks, trees, and shrines with the supreme refuge in Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha, together with insight into the Four Noble Truths and the noble Path leading to nirvāṇa.

Finally:

```text
eta eva śaraṇagamanāni
sarvasaṃvarasamādāneṣu dvārabhūtāni
```

These very acts of going for refuge function as the **gateway to undertaking all restraints**.

This closes the refuge discussion and returns directly to the analysis of restraint.

## 5. Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames the questions, identifies the principal refuge-objects, distinguishes `buddhakara` from identity with the Buddha, defines refuge as verbal manifestation and as protection.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — [216|29-217|01], answers the objection about harming the Tathāgata by saying that damage to the support damages the aśaikṣa dharmas as well.

**Voice: Other interpreters (`apare`)** — [217|05-217|06], identify the Buddha-refuge specifically with the eighteen distinctive Buddha-dharmas.

**Voice: Objector** — asks whether the view that refuge concerns aśaikṣa dharmas entails that the Buddha is simply identical with them.

## 6. New Technical Terms

- **buddhakara-dharma** — a dharma that makes or constitutes one as Buddha.
  - **status:** central clarification introduced by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** prevents collapse of the Buddha into either the empirical body or a bare identity with aśaikṣa dharmas.

- **saṃghakara-dharma** — a śaikṣa or aśaikṣa dharma whose acquisition makes the noble persons constitute the Saṅgha.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the Kārikā compound.
  - **function:** determines the strict object of Saṅgha-refuge.

- **kṣayajñāna** — knowledge of exhaustion/destruction.
  - **status:** previously important Path terminology.
  - **function:** principal example of the aśaikṣa Buddha-making dharmas.

- **sarvāvabodhasāmarthya** — capacity for complete awakening/comprehension.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains why acquisition of these dharmas makes one a Buddha.

- **pratisaṃkhyānirodha** — cessation through discriminative comprehension.
  - **status:** established Abhidharma technical term.
  - **function:** strict identification of the Dharma-refuge with nirvāṇa.

- **āśraya** — support; here the embodied/personal support upon which the constituting dharmas depend.
  - **status:** recurrent technical term.
  - **function:** preserves the bearer/constituting-dharma distinction.

- **vāgvijñapti** — verbal manifestation.
  - **status:** already central in Chapter 4.
  - **function:** intrinsic nature (`svabhāva`) of the acts of going for refuge.

- **trāṇa** — protection.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definition.
  - **function:** gives the meaning (`artha`) of `śaraṇa`.

- **dvārabhūta** — functioning as a gateway.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local sequence.
  - **function:** locates refuge as prerequisite entrance to the undertaking of restraints.

## 7. Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a precise threefold structure:

```text
Buddha-refuge
    → aśaikṣa Buddha-making dharmas
    → especially kṣayajñāna and its associates

Saṅgha-refuge
    → śaikṣa + aśaikṣa Saṅgha-making dharmas
    → those by which the eight noble persons constitute Saṅgha

Dharma-refuge
    → nirvāṇa
    → pratisaṃkhyānirodha
```

But the first two must be read as **constitutive relations**, not identity claims:

```text
Buddha-making dharmas
    constitute the Buddha as Buddha

but

Buddha ≠ merely those dharmas considered apart from their support
```

This distinction is explicit in Vasubandhu's rebuttal.

The passage then determines refuge itself on two levels:

```text
svabhāva:
    vāgvijñapti

artha:
    trāṇa
```

Thus the act is verbal manifestation; its Meaning is protection culminating in complete release from suffering.

## 8. Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation should preserve the force of `-kara` and avoid formulations such as “the Buddha is the aśaikṣa dharmas.”

Preferred translation:

> Whoever goes to the Three Refuges takes refuge in the aśaikṣa dharmas that make a Buddha, in both the śaikṣa and aśaikṣa dharmas that make the Saṅgha, and in nirvāṇa.

This is superior to a translation that converts the constituting relation into strict identity.

The Bhāṣya also clarifies that “Dharma” here, as refuge-object, terminates specifically in `pratisaṃkhyānirodha`, not indiscriminately in every dharma or merely in scripture as verbal teaching.

## 9. Logical Determination

The most important logical distinction is:

```text
bearer
    ≠
constituting determination

but

bearer is what it is
    through constituting determination
```

This is why Vasubandhu can say that one who takes refuge in the Buddha takes refuge in the Buddha-making dharmas without saying that the Buddha is nothing but those dharmas.

The relation is therefore neither accidental predication nor bare numerical identity.

The same pattern applies to Saṅgha:

```text
pudgala continuum
    + acquisition of Path-dharmas
        → noble Saṅgha member
```

A second determination concerns practical institution:

```text
refuge as inner orientation alone
    is insufficient as technical definition

śaraṇagamana
    has vāgvijñapti as svabhāva
```

The practical orientation becomes an instituted Path relation through manifest speech.

Finally:

```text
refuge
    → gateway
    → undertaking restraint
```

Thus the refuge sequence of 4.30–4.32 is not an excursus. It supplies the practical ground from which the restraint-system is entered.

## 10. Organon Contact Point

The passage offers a restrained but important distinction between a **bearer** and the **determinations through which the bearer is what it is**.

```text
Buddha
    is not reduced to rūpakāya

Buddha
    is not reduced to an abstract bundle

Buddha-making dharmas
    are constitutive determinations
    through which this support is Buddha
```

This is closer to genetic constitution than to static classification. The determination is neither externally attached nor separately identical with the bearer.

A second useful point is that refuge has both an enacted form and a rational end:

```text
vāgvijñapti
    enacted taking of refuge

trāṇa
    Meaning of refuge

atyantanirmokṣa
    final result
```

The practical form therefore mediates orientation toward liberation; it is not merely descriptive speech.

No stronger Organon identification should yet be imposed.
