# VAK_4.125_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.125**

```text
dharmadānaṃ yathābhūta sūtrādyakliṣṭadeśanā |
puṇyanirvāṇanirvedhabhāgīyaṃ kuśalaṃ tridhā || 4.125 ||
```

> The gift of Dharma is the unafflicted exposition of sūtra and the other teachings as they really are. Wholesome activity is threefold: belonging to merit, belonging to nirvāṇa, and belonging to penetration.

## Natural Segmentation

The passage has two arguments:

1. define `dharmadāna`, Dharma-giving;
2. classify wholesome activity by three developmental orientations.

The transition is explicit: material giving (`āmiṣadāna`) has been explained; now Dharma-giving must be defined.

## Literal Prose Translation

Material giving has been explained. Dharma-giving must now be stated. It is as follows:

> The gift of Dharma is the unafflicted exposition of sūtra and the other teachings as they really are.

The exposition of sūtras and the other teachings as they really are, when unafflicted, is Dharma-giving.

Therefore those who expound Dharma perversely cause a great loss of merit for themselves; likewise those who teach with an afflicted mind, desiring gain, honor, or fame.

Wholesome activity was previously stated as threefold according to the bases of meritorious action. It is again threefold:

> belonging to merit, belonging to nirvāṇa, and belonging to penetration.

`Puṇyabhāgīya` is that which has a desirable maturation. `Mokṣabhāgīya` is that upon whose arising one becomes certain to be of a nature destined for final nirvāṇa.

If, on hearing discourse illuminating the dangers of saṃsāra, selflessness, and the qualities of nirvāṇa, a person experiences horripilation and tears, it should be known that a wholesome root belonging to liberation exists in that person—just as, in the rainy season, the sprouting of shoots in the hollows of a threshing floor reveals that seeds are present there.

The fourfold `nirvedhabhāgīya`, beginning with `uṣman`, will be explained later.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Dharma-giving requires two conditions

```text
true exposition
    yathābhūta
+
unafflicted exposition
    akliṣṭa
=
dharmadāna
```

Correct doctrine alone is insufficient if the teaching act is governed by affliction. Conversely, sincere intention does not turn distorted doctrine into Dharma-giving.

The act therefore requires both:

```text
semantic fidelity
+
agential purity
```

### 2. Two ways of corrupting Dharma-giving

Vasubandhu explicitly names two failures:

```text
viparītadharma-deśanā
    teaching Dharma perversely / in inverted form

kliṣṭacitta-deśanā
    teaching with an afflicted mind
```

The latter is specified by seeking:

```text
lābha
    gain

satkāra
    honor / respectful treatment

yaśas
    fame / reputation
```

Such teaching does not merely fail to generate the highest merit; Vasubandhu says it causes a **great loss of merit** (`mahatī puṇyajyāni`).

### 3. The threefold wholesome is teleological

The division is not by external deed-form but by causal orientation:

```text
puṇyabhāgīya
    → desirable maturation

mokṣabhāgīya / nirvāṇabhāgīya
    → certainty of eventual final nirvāṇa

nirvedhabhāgīya
    → fourfold penetration-stage beginning with uṣman
```

This moves from improvement of conditioned existence to liberation and then to the immediate breakthrough sequence.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — transition from material giving to Dharma-giving and the definitions.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — warning against distorted or afflicted teaching.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — definition of `puṇyabhāgīya` and `mokṣabhāgīya`.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — experiential sign of a liberation-oriented wholesome root.

## New Technical Terms

- **āmiṣadāna** — material giving; **status:** previously implicit in the giving section; **function:** contrast with Dharma-giving.
- **dharmadāna** — gift of Dharma; **status:** new in this passage; **function:** definition of teaching as giving.
- **yathābhūta** — according to actuality, as it really is; **status:** recurring Buddhist technical term; **function:** truth-condition of Dharma transmission.
- **akliṣṭadeśanā** — unafflicted exposition; **status:** new compound here; **function:** purity-condition of Dharma transmission.
- **puṇyajyāni** — loss or diminishment of merit; **status:** rare; **function:** consequence of distorted or afflicted teaching.
- **mokṣabhāgīya** — belonging to liberation; **status:** Bhāṣya gloss of Kārikā `nirvāṇabhāgīya`; **function:** causal orientation toward final nirvāṇa.
- **parinirvāṇadharman** — one whose nature is destined for final nirvāṇa; **status:** technical; **function:** defines the certainty produced by a liberation-oriented wholesome root.
- **nirvedhabhāgīya** — belonging to penetration; **status:** previously anticipated; **function:** category for the four preparatory breakthrough stages beginning with `uṣman`.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The passage establishes that teaching is itself a karmic act whose moral and causal quality cannot be judged by propositional content alone.

```text
correct content
but afflicted motive
    ≠
full dharmadāna
```

Likewise:

```text
pure motive
but distorted Dharma
    ≠
full dharmadāna
```

Dharma-giving therefore requires the unity of truth and purity in the act of communication.

The threefold wholesome can be represented:

```text
kuśala
├── puṇyabhāgīya
│   └── iṣṭavipāka
├── mokṣabhāgīya
│   └── certainty of final nirvāṇa
└── nirvedhabhāgīya
    └── uṣman etc., fourfold
```

The crucial distinction is qualitative:

```text
better conditioned result
    ≠
liberative orientation
    ≠
penetrative breakthrough
```

## The Diagnostic of Mokṣabhāgīya Wholesomeness

Vasubandhu gives an unusual phenomenological sign. When discourse on:

```text
saṃsārādīnava
    the dangers / defects of saṃsāra

nairātmya
    selflessness

nirvāṇaguṇa
    the qualities of nirvāṇa
```

produces:

```text
romaharṣa
    horripilation / hair standing on end

āśrupāta
    tears
```

this indicates that a liberation-oriented wholesome root exists.

The analogy is agricultural:

```text
hidden seed
    → revealed by sprouting after rain

hidden mokṣabhāgīya-kuśalamūla
    → revealed by transformative response to Dharma
```

The reaction is not itself liberation. It is evidence that the relevant causal seed is present.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation is basically sound, but two refinements are important:

1. `akliṣṭa` qualifies the **act of exposition**, not merely the doctrinal content.
2. `nirvāṇabhāgīya` is glossed in the Bhāṣya as `mokṣabhāgīya`, so “belonging to liberation” is a useful explanatory rendering.

Recommended translation:

> **The gift of Dharma is the unafflicted exposition of sūtra and the other teachings as they really are. Wholesome activity is threefold: belonging to merit, belonging to liberation, and belonging to penetration.**

## Light Organon Contact

This passage gives a strict model of transmission:

```text
truth of content
+
purity of purpose
+
actual exposition
    →
valid Dharma-giving
```

The teacher is not a neutral channel. The mode of presentation and the practical end governing it belong to the truth of the deed as karma.

The second half then distinguishes three developmental directions of wholesome activity:

```text
world-improving
    → puṇya

liberation-orienting
    → mokṣa / nirvāṇa

breakthrough-producing
    → nirvedha
```

The important point is genetic continuity: meritorious activity, liberation-oriented roots, and penetrative stages are not simply three labels for the same wholesome state. They designate different positions in the development of the continuum.
