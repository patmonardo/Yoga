# VAK_4.07 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
nāvyākṛtāsty avijñaptiḥ tridhānyat aśubhaṃ punaḥ |
kāme rūpe ’py avijñaptiḥ vijñaptiḥ savicārayoḥ ||
```

**Working translation:** Non-manifest action is never ethically indeterminate; the other karma is threefold. Unwholesome karma exists only in the desire domain. Non-manifest action exists also in the form domain, while manifest action exists only in the two levels possessing vicāra.

### Source cautions

The e-text itself warns that it is a best reading based principally on Pradhan rather than a critical edition. The line `kuśalāvyākṛtaṃ tu sarvatra prativedhāt` is semantically awkward in the transmitted form; no silent normalization is made here.

The compound beginning `ārūpyāśca kāmadhātor āśrayākārālambanapratipakṣadūratābhiḥ` is dense and should be taken as listing dimensions of remoteness rather than over-translated word by word.

## Natural Argument

The passage first completes the classification generated by the preceding discussion of bodily, verbal, and mental karma. Within that field, `avijñapti` receives a striking restriction: it can be wholesome or unwholesome, but never ethically indeterminate.

Vasubandhu explains the restriction causally. An `avyākṛta` mind is weak. It cannot project (`ākṣip`) a sufficiently forceful karma capable of continuing after the originating mind has ceased. Thus the exclusion of neutral avijñapti is not merely classificatory; it follows from a theory of karmic force and continuation.

The expression `tridhā anyat` is then explicitly unpacked. The “other karma” is `vijñapti` and `cetanā`, each of which may be wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate.

The argument next crosses ethical quality with cosmological domain. Unwholesome substantial karma is confined to the desire domain. Above it, the unwholesome roots and the factors `āhrīkya` and `anapatrāpya` have been abandoned.

`Avijñapti`, however, is not confined to the desire domain; it also exists in the form domain. The Bhāṣya then raises the natural question whether it might occur in the formless domains as well. The Vaibhāṣika answer is no. The immediate reason is the absence of great material determinants there. The fuller explanation appeals to the structure of bodily and verbal restraint, to the practitioner's turning away from all form in formless attainments, and to the fact that ethical discipline is the antidote to misconduct, while misconduct itself is desire-domain in scope.

Finally, the Bhāṣya resolves `savicārayoḥ`: manifest action exists only in the two levels where `vicāra` remains operative—the desire domain and the first dhyāna. Above the first dhyāna, `vijñapti` in this sense does not arise.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation that indeterminate mind is too weak to project a continuing avijñapti; unpacking `tridhā anyat`; restriction of unwholesome karma to the desire domain.

**Voice: Objector** — asks whether, by analogy with uncontaminated manifestation, an avijñapti might arise for one absorbed in a formless attainment.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — denies avijñapti in the formless domains and supplies the cluster of reasons concerning absence of material support, turning away from form, and the relation of discipline to desire-domain misconduct.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — identifies the two `savicāra` levels as the desire domain and first dhyāna.

## New Technical Terms

- **durbala-citta** — weak mind
  - **status:** prose determination, not stated in the Kārikā
  - **function:** causal explanation for why ethically indeterminate mind cannot generate avijñapti

- **balavat-karma** — forceful karma
  - **status:** prose determination
  - **function:** distinguishes karma capable of continuing after the originating mind has ceased

- **ākṣip / karmākṣepa** — to project or establish karmic efficacy
  - **status:** previously encountered elsewhere in the Kośa, newly operative here
  - **function:** causal relation between originating mind and continuing karmic determination

- **āhrīkya** — shamelessness
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** marks the unwholesome field that is absent above the desire domain

- **anapatrāpya** — lack of moral concern / lack of restraint before blame
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** paired with `āhrīkya` in explaining the restriction of unwholesome karma

- **sarvarūpavaimukhya** — complete turning away from form
  - **status:** prose determination
  - **function:** explains why formless attainment is not capable of producing material avijñapti

- **dauḥśīlya-pratipakṣa** — antidote to misconduct
  - **status:** prose determination
  - **function:** explains discipline (`śīla`) through its opposition to desire-domain misconduct

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya establishes a three-axis classification:

```text
1. mode
   vijñapti / avijñapti / cetanā

2. ethical quality
   kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta

3. domain
   kāma / rūpa / ārūpya
```

But the axes are asymmetrical.

```text
avijñapti
    kuśala or akuśala
    never avyākṛta

vijñapti + cetanā
    kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta

akuśala karma
    desire domain only

avijñapti
    desire + form domains
    absent from formless domains

vijñapti
    desire domain + first dhyāna
```

The reason avijñapti excludes neutrality is particularly important. Avijñapti is not simply a hidden counterpart of every action. It presupposes sufficient karmic force to establish a continuation after the originating mental event has ceased.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā translation remains substantially correct, but two points can now be made canonical.

First, `tridhā anyat` explicitly means the other two karmas, `vijñapti` and `cetanā`, and not merely `vijñapti` alone.

Second, `savicārayoḥ` is no longer provisional. It designates exactly:

```text
kāmadhātu
prathama-dhyāna
```

A refined translation is therefore:

> There is no ethically indeterminate avijñapti; the other two karmas are threefold. Unwholesome karma exists only in the desire domain. Avijñapti exists also in the form domain, while vijñapti exists only in the desire domain and the first dhyāna, the two levels possessing vicāra.

## Logical Determination

The striking inference of this verse is:

```text
avyākṛta citta
    = durbala

therefore it cannot project
    balavat karma

therefore no karmic determination
    can continue after that citta has ceased

therefore
    no avyākṛta avijñapti
```

This gives `avijñapti` a functional criterion:

```text
avijñapti
    requires a determination strong enough
    to outlast the originating mental event
```

The domain argument adds another constraint:

```text
avijñapti is rūpa
    → requires material support
    → therefore absent where bhūtas are absent
```

Yet the anāsrava status of the path does not itself force avijñapti into a separate uncontaminated material domain. As already established in 4.05, the ethical/path status of karma and its material basis are distinct determinations.

## Organon Contact Point

The text now gives a useful distinction between mere occurrence and effective determination.

```text
an event may occur
without establishing a continuing determination

only a sufficiently forceful determination
projects beyond its originating moment
```

That is a stronger conception of karma than simple succession. The Bhāṣya is asking not merely whether an act happened, but whether the act acquired enough determination to become causally transmissible through the continuum.

The Organon contact point should remain light: avijñapti is beginning to look less like a concealed object and more like the objective persistence of a determination. The Sautrāntika alternative from 4.04 remains an important control against reifying that persistence prematurely.
