# VAK_4.44_bhasya — Realm Distribution of Dhyāna-Born and Uncontaminated Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.44**

```iast
kāmarūpajadevānāṃ dhyānajaḥ anāsravaḥ punaḥ |
dhyānāntarāsaṃjñisattvavarjyānām apy arūpiṇām ||
```

> Dhyāna-born restraint occurs among gods born in the desire and form domains. Uncontaminated restraint also occurs among beings of the formless domain, with the specified exclusions and qualifications.

## Source Cautions

The verse is syntactically compressed across the boundary with VAK 4.43. The Bhāṣya supplies the actual distribution.

The final statement about formless beings is especially important: anāsrava restraint is present for them **by possession (`samanvāgama`)**, not by present manifestation (`saṃmukhībhāva`)**.** That distinction must be retained.

The source e-text has minor orthographic irregularities (`dhyānāntarikāsaṃjñisatva-` etc.); these are preserved in the quoted source rather than silently normalized.

## Natural Argument

VAK 4.43 concluded that eligible humans can possess all three restraints. VAK 4.44 completes the distribution for gods and formless beings.

First, `dhyānasaṃvara` occurs among gods born in the desire and form domains. Vasubandhu immediately sets a limit: **above the form domain it does not occur**.

```text
kāma-born gods
    → dhyānaja-saṃvara possible

rūpa-born gods
    → dhyānaja-saṃvara possible

ārūpya-born beings
    → dhyānaja-saṃvara not present
```

Second, `anāsravasaṃvara` has a wider distribution. It occurs among beings born in the desire and form domains, excluding those born in `dhyānāntara` and among `asaṃjñisattvas`.

It is also possessed by beings born in the formless domain. But Vasubandhu immediately qualifies the sense of this possession:

```text
ārūpyopapanna
    anāsravasaṃvara exists by samanvāgama
    not by saṃmukhībhāva
```

Thus a formless being may continue to **possess** the uncontaminated restraint without its being presently manifest as an operative state.

The prose then explicitly closes this entire topic. From the next verse onward Vasubandhu begins the systematic exposition of the karmas enumerated in the sūtras.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose throughout.

## New Technical Terms

- **samanvāgama** — possession, endowment, continued belonging-to a continuum
  - **status:** central Abhidharma term, newly decisive in this local distinction
  - **function:** explains how anāsrava restraint can remain possessed without present manifestation

- **saṃmukhībhāva** — present manifestation, immediate actuality, being presently operative
  - **status:** previously encountered but technically contrasted here with `samanvāgama`
  - **function:** distinguishes possession from current manifestation

- **dhyānāntarika** — beings associated with the intermediate-dhyāna state/domain
  - **status:** existing cosmological/meditative category
  - **function:** exclusion from the present distribution of anāsrava restraint

- **asaṃjñisattva** — non-percipient beings
  - **status:** previously encountered in the Kośa
  - **function:** exclusion based on mode of existence/cognitive condition

## Doctrinal Determination

The three restraints now have a complete differentiated distribution:

```text
prātimokṣa-saṃvara
    eligible humans


dhyānaja-saṃvara
    eligible humans
    + gods born in kāmadhātu
    + gods born in rūpadhātu
    not above rūpadhātu

anāsrava-saṃvara
    eligible humans
    + eligible kāma/rūpa-born gods
    - dhyānāntarika births
    - asaṃjñisattva births
    + formless-born beings by possession
      but not by present manifestation
```

The last distinction is doctrinally important:

```text
possession
    ≠ present occurrence

samanvāgama
    ≠ saṃmukhībhāva
```

A dharma can belong to a continuum without being manifest at that moment.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation should be sharpened. In particular, the final pāda should not imply that uncontaminated restraint is presently manifest in formless existence.

Preferred translation:

> **Dhyāna-born restraint occurs among gods born in the desire and form domains. Uncontaminated restraint occurs among beings born in the desire and form domains, except those of the intermediate dhyāna and the non-percipient beings; it is also possessed by beings born in the formless domain, though there only by possession and not by present manifestation.**

## Logical Determination

This verse distinguishes two relations that must not be collapsed:

```text
D belongs to continuum C
    = samanvāgama

D is presently actual in C
    = saṃmukhībhāva
```

So:

```text
POSSESSED(D, C)
    does not entail
MANIFEST(D, C, now)
```

This is a major Abhidharma modeling principle. A continuum has both currently manifest states and non-manifestly possessed determinations.

The range of each restraint is also controlled by its generative basis:

```text
formal undertaking
    → prātimokṣa distribution

meditative ground
    → dhyānaja distribution

noble-path acquisition
    → anāsrava distribution
```

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained contact point is the distinction between **possession of a determination** and **its current actuality**:

```text
being-determined-by
    ≠
currently manifesting
```

That is structurally useful for any theory of persistent rational or practical form. But here it is first and foremost Vasubandhu's technical distinction between `samanvāgama` and `saṃmukhībhāva`.
