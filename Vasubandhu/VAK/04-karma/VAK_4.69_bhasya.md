# VAK_4.69_bhasya — Roots of Mental and Wholesome Karmic Courses

## Kārikā

**VAK 4.69**

`tad-anantara-saṃbhūter abhidhyādyās trimūlajāḥ /`
`kuśalāḥ sa-prayogāntā alobha-dveṣa-moha-jāḥ // 4.69 //`

**Translation**

Because they arise immediately after those roots, covetousness and the others are born from the three roots. The wholesome karmic courses, together with their preparatory and subsequent phases, arise from non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion.

See: `VAK_4.69.md`

---

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya asks:

`abhidhyādayaḥ kathaṃ lobhajāḥ /`

How are covetousness and the others said to be born from greed?

It answers by quoting the first half of the verse:

`tad-anantara-saṃbhūter abhidhyādyās trimūlajāḥ /`

and then explains:

`lobhād anantaram utpannā lobhajāḥ dveṣād anantaraṃ dveṣajā mohād anantaraṃ mohajāḥ /`

Those arising immediately after greed are greed-born; those arising immediately after hatred are hatred-born; those arising immediately after delusion are delusion-born.

The Bhāṣya then turns to the wholesome karmic courses:

`kuśalāḥ katham ity āha`

How are the wholesome ones [rooted]?

`kuśalāḥ sa-prayogāntā alobha-dveṣa-moha-jāḥ //`

The wholesome karmic courses, together with their preparatory and subsequent phases, are born from non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion.

Vasubandhu explains:

`sa-prayoga-pṛṣṭhāḥ kuśalāḥ karmapathā alobha-dveṣa-moha-jāḥ /`

The wholesome karmic courses, together with preparation and aftermath, arise from non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion.

`kuśala-citta-samutthitatvāt /`

Because they arise from a wholesome mind.

`tasya cāvaśyam alobhādibhiḥ saṃprayogāt /`

And because that wholesome mind is necessarily associated with non-greed and the other wholesome roots.

The Bhāṣya then defines the three phases on the wholesome side by reversal of the corresponding unwholesome phases:

`akuśala-karmapatha-prayogād viratiḥ kuśala-karmapatha-prayogaḥ /`

Abstention from the preparation for an unwholesome karmic course is the preparation for a wholesome karmic course.

`maulād viratiḥ maulaḥ /`

Abstention from the principal [unwholesome course] is the principal wholesome course.

`pṛṣṭhād viratiḥ pṛṣṭhaḥ /`

Abstention from the aftermath is the wholesome aftermath.

The Bhāṣya illustrates this with the ordination of a novice. Entering the residence, venerating the monks, requesting the preceptor, and the first two formal acts are preparation. At the third formal act, the manifest action and the momentary non-manifest determination constitute the principal karmic course. What follows, including declaration of the supports and the continuing non-manifest determination, is the aftermath.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The first half is more exact than the Kārikā alone suggests

The first-pass Kārikā analysis left open whether `abhidhyā`, `vyāpāda`, and `mithyādṛṣṭi` arose from a general three-root field. The Bhāṣya resolves this more strictly.

```text
immediately after lobha
    → lobhaja

immediately after dveṣa
    → dveṣaja

immediately after moha
    → mohaja
```

The point is not that every mental karmapatha is equally generated by all three roots. Rather, the causal designation follows the immediately antecedent root.

Thus `tad-anantara-saṃbhūti` is a rule of proximate causal classification.

### 2. Root and specific mental course remain distinct

The Bhāṣya preserves the distinction established in 4.65:

```text
root
    ≠
specific mental bad conduct
```

Greed, hatred, and delusion are general unwholesome roots. Covetousness, hostility, and false view are determinate mental courses that may arise immediately after those roots.

The relation is therefore genetic rather than one of simple identity.

### 3. Wholesome action is grounded in a wholesome mind

The wholesome karmapathas arise from:

```text
alobha
adveṣa
amoha
```

The Bhāṣya gives the reason:

```text
wholesome karmapatha
    ↓ arises from
kuśala-citta
    ↓ necessarily associated with
alobha + adveṣa + amoha
```

This is stronger than mere external conformity. The wholesome act is wholesome because its generating mind is wholesome and necessarily associated with the wholesome roots.

### 4. The wholesome three-phase process is defined by abstention

The Bhāṣya now supplies a striking symmetry with 4.68:

```text
unwholesome preparation
    ↓ abstention from it
wholesome preparation

unwholesome principal act
    ↓ abstention from it
wholesome principal course

unwholesome aftermath
    ↓ abstention from it
wholesome aftermath
```

Thus wholesome karma is not merely a separate positive inventory placed alongside bad karma. In this context it is genetically determined as **virati**, abstention from the corresponding unwholesome process.

This makes the wholesome karmic course itself temporally articulated:

```text
prayoga
    preparation for restraint

maula
    principal restraint

pṛṣṭha
    continued restraint / aftermath
```

### 5. The ordination example makes this temporal structure concrete

The novice ordination example shows that even a wholesome karmapatha possesses a processual morphology.

```text
entering the residence
venerating monks
requesting the preceptor
first and second formal acts
    = prayoga

third formal act
manifest action + momentary avijñapti
    = maula

subsequent declarations
continuing avijñapti
    = pṛṣṭha
```

The example is especially important because it confirms that the three-phase model is not restricted to wrongdoing. It belongs to karmic determination as such.

## New Technical Terms

- **tad-anantara-saṃbhūti** — arising immediately after a given root; **status:** newly decisive in this passage; **function:** proximate causal classification of mental courses.
- **kuśala-citta-samutthita** — arisen from a wholesome mind; **status:** explanatory Bhāṣya formulation; **function:** grounds wholesome karmapathas genetically rather than behaviorally.
- **saṃprayoga** — association/conjunction; **status:** familiar Abhidharma relation, newly operative here; **function:** explains why a wholesome mind necessarily carries the wholesome roots.
- **virati** — abstention/restraint; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** defines the wholesome phases through abstention from corresponding unwholesome phases.
- **karmavācana** — formal ecclesiastical act/formula; **status:** Bhāṣya-specific example vocabulary here; **function:** illustrates preparation, principal act, and aftermath in ordination.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 4.69 introduces two distinct causal determinations.

First, the mental bad courses are classified according to the root immediately antecedent to their arising:

```text
proximate root
    → classification of mental course
```

Second, wholesome bodily and verbal karmapathas, including their preparation and aftermath, arise from a wholesome mind necessarily associated with the three wholesome roots.

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes:

```text
proximate causal antecedence
    mental bad courses

necessary wholesome association
    wholesome karmic process
```

These are not the same explanatory relation.

The first-pass suggestion that the three mental courses simply emerge from a loose three-root field should therefore be replaced by the Bhāṣya's more exact formulation.

---

## Translation Consequences

The phrase `tad-anantara-saṃbhūteḥ` should be rendered so that immediate antecedence remains visible. A good translation is:

> "Because they arise immediately after those roots, covetousness and the others are born from the three roots."

The second half should preserve both process phases. `sa-prayogāntāḥ` is clarified by the Bhāṣya as `sa-prayoga-pṛṣṭhāḥ`, so "together with preparation and aftermath" is preferable to the vaguer "together with beginning and end."

---

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya distinguish three levels:

```text
Root
    ↓
Mind / causal orientation
    ↓
Karmic process
    ↓
Determinate course
```

But the causal relation varies.

For unwholesome mental courses:

```text
immediate antecedence
    determines causal classification
```

For wholesome karmapathas:

```text
wholesome mind
    necessarily associated with wholesome roots
        ↓
generates the entire karmic process
```

The decisive philosophical point is therefore not merely that good and bad actions have opposite roots. It is that Vasubandhu analyzes **how a root becomes operative in a determinate process**.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest text-grounded contact point is the distinction between **ground** and **determinate realization**.

```text
root
    is not yet the karmic course

root
    becomes operative through a generated mind/process

process
    becomes determinate as karmapatha
```

On the wholesome side the structure is especially clear:

```text
alobha / adveṣa / amoha
        ↓
kuśala-citta
        ↓
prayoga
        ↓
maula
        ↓
pṛṣṭha
```

This is a genuinely genetic sequence supplied by the Bhāṣya itself. The ethical determination is not inferred only from an isolated visible act; it is reconstructed from its ground through its temporal realization.
