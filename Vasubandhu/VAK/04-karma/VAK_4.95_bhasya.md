# VAK 4.95 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `ekaṃ janmākṣipaty ekam anekaṃ paripūrakam | nākṣepike samāpattī acitte prāptayo na ca || 4.95 ||`

**Best current translation:** One karma projects one birth; completing karma is multiple. The two non-mental attainments are not projecting, nor are acquisitions (`prāpti`).

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.95.md](./VAK_4.95.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya moves through four questions:

1. Does one karma project one birth or many births?
2. Can many karmas jointly project one birth?
3. What is the difference between projecting and completing karma?
4. Can the two non-mental attainments or `prāpti` perform the projecting function?

The argumentative center is `nikāyasabhāga`: projection concerns the common class-character by whose acquisition one is said to have been born in a determinate rebirth-class.

## Literal Prose Translation

Does one karma project only one birth, or not even one? And does one karma project one birth, or many? The established doctrine is this: one karma projects one birth. One karma projects only one birth, and one birth is projected by one karma, not many. “Birth” here is the designation of the `nikāyasabhāga`; when that has been obtained, one is said to have been born.

But what of the statement of the elder Aniruddha that, through the maturation of a single alms-food offering, he was born seven times among the Thirty-three Gods and eventually here in the Śākya clan? Having obtained prosperity through that act, he remembered former births and subsequently performed further merit; he was indicating the originating occasion. It is like a person who, after beginning with one dinar and later gaining a thousand, says, “Through that one dinar I obtained this wealth.”

Others say instead: taking that alms-food as a support, a great continuum of intentions of giving arose, and one result was appropriated by one intention, another by another.

Nor should several karmas jointly project one birth, for then the projection of `nikāyasabhāga` would occur in fragments. One karma is accepted as projecting the afflicted `nikāyasabhāga`.

Completing karma, however, is multiple. Just as a painter sketches a figure with one stroke and fills it out with many, so among human beings of the same class one person possesses complete faculties, limbs, color, form, proportion, and strength, while another is deficient in one or another of these.

Nor is karma alone said to be projecting; another factor possessing maturation may also be so. Nevertheless, the two non-mental attainments are not projecting, nor are `prāptis`. The Bhāṣya explicitly glosses the former as the two `acitta-samāpattis`, then gives a separate reason for excluding `prāpti`.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. One projecting karma, one projected birth

```text
one projecting karma
    → one nikāyasabhāga
    → one determinate rebirth-class
```

“Birth” here is not the total content of an embodied life. The one-to-one relation concerns the projection of `nikāyasabhāga`, the class-determination by which the continuum is born as this kind of being.

### 2. Why many karmas do not jointly project one birth

Joint projection would imply a fragmented projection (`khaṇḍaśaḥ ākṣepa`) of the `nikāyasabhāga`. But the class-determination functions as one projected unity. Hence one projecting karma is assigned to it.

### 3. The Aniruddha objection

A counterexample appears to show one gift producing many births. The first reply treats the original gift as the originating occasion of a subsequent sequence of merit. The second, introduced by `apare`, treats the gift as the support for a stream of giving-intentions, different intentions appropriating different results.

Both explanations preserve the rule that one projecting karma does not literally project several distinct rebirth-classes.

### 4. Projecting and completing karma

The decisive distinction is:

```text
ākṣepaka
    establishes the unitary rebirth-class

paripūraka
    fills out the concrete determinations of that birth
```

The painter analogy makes the distinction exact:

```text
one stroke
    → establishes the figure

many strokes
    → complete its determinate features
```

Thus one causal function determines what kind of rebirth-instance there is, while multiple causal functions determine how that instance is concretely constituted.

## The Two Non-Mental Attainments and Prāpti

The syntax is clarified by the prose immediately following the kārikā:

```text
avipākābhyām api acitta-samāpattibhyāṃ ...
prāptibhiś ca ...
```

Accordingly:

```text
samāpattī acitte
    = the two non-mental attainments

prāptayaḥ
    = acquisitions / possession-relations, separately excluded
```

`acitte` should therefore not be carried over to qualify `prāptayaḥ` in translation.

The two non-mental attainments do not project `nikāyasabhāga` because they are not coexistent with karma (`karmāsahabhūtatvāt`). `Prāptis` are excluded separately; the Bhāṣya gives the reason `karmaṇo 'nekaphalatvāt`, “because karma has multiple fruits.” Whatever the compressed logic of this last clause, the text clearly distinguishes the possession-relation from the projecting causal function.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — states the `siddhānta`: one karma projects one birth.
- **Voice: Objector** — raises the Aniruddha example.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — answers by distinguishing originating occasion from the later causal series.
- **Voice: uncertain / reported teachers (`apare`)** — alternative account through a continuum of giving-intentions.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu within the Abhidharma framework** — distinguishes projecting from completing karma and excludes the two non-mental attainments and `prāpti` from projection.

## New Technical Terms

- **ākṣepaka-karma** — projecting karma; karma that determines a rebirth through projection of its `nikāyasabhāga`.
  - **status:** central clarification of the Kārikā.
  - **function:** causal classification.

- **paripūraka-karma** — completing karma; the multiple karmas that fill out the concrete determinations of an already projected birth.
  - **status:** explicit in Kārikā and unfolded in Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** causal differentiation.

- **nikāyasabhāga** — common class-character / class-membership of a determinate rebirth-group.
  - **status:** crucial explanatory term in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** specifies what projection determines.

- **khaṇḍaśaḥ ākṣepa** — fragmented projection.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argument term.
  - **function:** reductio against several karmas jointly projecting one birth.

- **adhiṣṭhāna** — support or basis; here the original gift as support for a stream of giving-intentions.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** causal support in a reported alternative account.

- **acitta-samāpatti** — non-mental attainment; the dual refers to the two attainments without active mind.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal category.
  - **function:** excluded from birth-projection.

- **prāpti** — acquisition / possession relation.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** separately distinguished from projecting causation.

## Doctrinal Determination

The doctrine is not:

```text
one karma causes one whole life
```

It is:

```text
one projecting karma
    → one nikāyasabhāga
    → one rebirth-class

many completing karmas
    → differentiated concrete features
      within that projected birth
```

Projection answers: **why is this continuum born as this kind of being?**

Completion answers: **why does this instance possess these particular conditioned features?**

## Logical Determination

Let `N` be one projected `nikāyasabhāga`, `Kp` its projecting karma, and `Kc₁ ... Kcₙ` its completing karmas.

```text
Projects(Kp, N)
ExactlyOneProjector(N)

Completes(Kc₁, J)
Completes(Kc₂, J)
...
Completes(Kcₙ, J)
```

Therefore:

```text
projection ≠ exhaustive completion
completion ≠ projection
prāpti ≠ projection
```

The verse differentiates causal roles rather than accumulating them into a single undifferentiated notion of karma.

## Translation Consequences

**Translation should be revised.**

The first half remains secure. The second half should read:

> The two non-mental attainments are not projecting, nor are acquisitions (`prāpti`).

The previous wording “nor are acquisitions in the non-mental state” incorrectly allowed `acitte` to qualify `prāptayaḥ`. The Bhāṣya resolves the syntax by explicitly speaking first of the two `acitta-samāpattis` and then separately of `prāptis`.

A fuller philosophical translation is:

> One projecting karma determines one rebirth-class; the concrete completion of that birth depends upon many completing karmas. The two non-mental attainments and acquisition-relations do not themselves perform the projecting function.

## Light Organon Contact

The text itself generates a distinction between **constitutive placement** and **concrete completion**:

```text
projecting determination
    → establishes the unitary class-instance

completing determinations
    → articulate the concrete instance
```

This remains an internal development of the karmic theory rather than an imported analogy. The painter simile already gives the logical form: outline and completion are reciprocally necessary for the concrete result but are not the same causal determination.
