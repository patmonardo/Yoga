# VAK 4.95 — Bhāṣya

**Kārikā:** `ekaṃ janmākṣipaty ekam anekaṃ paripūrakam | nākṣepike samāpattī acitte prāptayo na ca || 4.95 ||`

**Best current translation:** One karma projects one birth; completing karma is multiple. The two non-mental attainments are not projecting, nor are acquisitions in the non-mental state.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_4.95.md](./VAK_4.95.md)

## Natural Segmentation

The Bhāṣya moves through four questions:

1. Does one karma project one birth or many births?
2. Can many karmas jointly project one birth?
3. What is the difference between projecting and completing karma?
4. Are the two non-mental attainments or `prāpti` capable of projecting birth?

The argumentative center is `nikāyasabhāga`: projection concerns the acquisition of the common class-character by which one is said to be born in a determinate rebirth-class.

## Literal Prose Translation

> Does one karma project only one birth, or not even one? And does even one karma project one birth, or many? The established doctrine is this: one karma projects one birth. One karma projects only one birth, and one birth is projected by one karma, not many. “Birth” here is the designation of the acquisition of the `nikāyasabhāga`; when that has been obtained, one is said to have been born.
>
> But what of the statement of the elder Aniruddha that, through the maturation of a single alms-food offering, he was born seven times among the Thirty-three Gods and eventually here in the Śākya clan? Having obtained prosperity through that act, he remembered former births and subsequently performed further merit; he was indicating the originating occasion. It is like a person who, after beginning with one dinar and later gaining a thousand, says, “Through that one dinar I obtained this wealth.”
>
> Others say instead: taking that alms-food as objective support, a great continuum of intentions of giving arose, and one result was appropriated by one intention, another by another.
>
> Nor should one say that several karmas project one birth, for then the projection of the `nikāyasabhāga` would occur in fragments. Rather, one karma is accepted as projecting the afflicted `nikāyasabhāga`.
>
> Completing karma, however, is multiple. Just as a painter sketches a figure with one stroke and fills it out with many, so among human beings of the same class one person has all sense faculties and limbs complete and shines with excellence of color, form, proportion, and strength, while another is deficient in one or another of these.
>
> Nor is karma alone capable of projecting birth; other factors that possess maturation may also do so. Nevertheless, the two non-mental attainments are not projecting, nor are acquisitions in a non-mental condition. Even though the two non-mental attainments lack maturation, a `nikāyasabhāga` is not projected by them because they do not coexist with karma. Nor is it projected by acquisitions, because karma has multiple fruits.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. One projecting karma, one projected birth

The Bhāṣya states the thesis strongly:

```text
one karma → one birth
one birth → one projecting karma
```

But “birth” is not the total phenomenological content of a life. It is tied to acquisition of `nikāyasabhāga`, the common class-character of that rebirth.

Thus:

```text
ākṣepaka-karma
    → acquisition of nikāyasabhāga
    → determinate rebirth-class
```

The one-to-one structure therefore belongs to the projection of the rebirth-class, not to every feature that later belongs to that life.

### 2. Why many karmas do not jointly project one birth

The Bhāṣya rejects joint projection because this would imply:

```text
fragmented causes
    → fragmented projection
    → khaṇḍaśaḥ nikāyasabhāga
```

But `nikāyasabhāga` functions as a unitary class-determination. The projected birth must therefore be grounded by one projecting karma.

### 3. The Aniruddha objection

A scriptural-looking counterexample is raised: one alms offering appears to have produced many births.

Vasubandhu gives one explanation:

```text
one initial meritorious act
    → prosperity
    → recollection of former births
    → further meritorious acts
```

The original gift is then spoken of as the originating occasion, just as a person may attribute later wealth to the first coin with which the process began.

A second reported explanation says that the alms offering became an objective support (`adhiṣṭhāna`) for a great continuum of giving-intentions; different intentions then appropriated different fruits.

Both interpretations preserve the rule that one projecting karma does not literally project a series of distinct births.

## Projecting and Completing Karma

The sharp distinction is:

```text
ākṣepaka
    projects the birth as one rebirth-class

paripūraka
    fills out the concrete determinations of that birth
```

The painter analogy is exact:

```text
one stroke
    → outlines the figure

many strokes
    → complete its details
```

Likewise:

```text
one projecting karma
    → human birth, etc.

many completing karmas
    → faculties
      limbs
      color
      form
      proportion
      strength
      and other differential features
```

Thus unity of existential placement and multiplicity of concrete determination belong to different causal functions.

## The Two Non-Mental Attainments and Prāpti

The Kārikā excludes:

```text
two acitta-samāpattis
    → not projecting

prāptis in an acitta condition
    → not projecting
```

The Bhāṣya gives distinct reasons.

### Non-mental attainments

They do not project `nikāyasabhāga` because:

```text
karmāsahabhūtatvāt
because they are not coexistent with karma
```

### Prāpti

Acquisitions do not project birth because:

```text
karmaṇo 'nekaphalatvāt
because karma has multiple fruits
```

A possession-relation must therefore not be confused with the causal function that projects a rebirth-class.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — states the siddhānta that one karma projects one birth and one birth has one projector.
- **Voice: Objector** — raises the Aniruddha example of one gift apparently yielding many births.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — answers by treating the original act as originating occasion for a later meritorious series.
- **Voice: uncertain / reported teachers (`apare`)** — alternative account via a continuum of giving-intentions grounded on the original gift.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu / Vaibhāṣika doctrinal framework** — distinguishes projecting and completing karma and excludes the two non-mental attainments and `prāpti` from projection.

## New Technical Terms

- **ākṣepaka-karma** — projecting karma; karma that determines one rebirth by projecting its `nikāyasabhāga`.
  - **status:** central Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā.
  - **function:** causal classification.

- **paripūraka-karma** — completing karma; the many karmas that fill out the concrete qualities of an already projected birth.
  - **status:** explicit in Kārikā, richly unfolded in Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** causal differentiation.

- **nikāyasabhāga** — common class-character or class-membership of a determinate rebirth-group.
  - **status:** crucial Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage.
  - **function:** specifies what exactly is projected by projecting karma.

- **khaṇḍaśaḥ ākṣepa** — fragmented projection.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argument term.
  - **function:** reductio against many karmas jointly projecting one birth.

- **adhiṣṭhāna** — objective basis/support; here the original alms offering functioning as support for a stream of giving-intentions.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** causal support in a reported alternative account.

- **acitta-samāpatti** — non-mental attainment; the dual refers to the two attainments without active mind.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal category.
  - **function:** excluded from birth-projection.

- **prāpti** — acquisition/possession relation.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** distinguished from projecting causation.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse should not be reduced to:

```text
one karma causes one whole life
```

The Bhāṣya gives the more exact doctrine:

```text
one projecting karma
    → one nikāyasabhāga
    → one rebirth-class

many completing karmas
    → differentiated concrete features
      within that one projected birth
```

The theory therefore separates two questions:

1. Why is this continuum born as this kind of being?
2. Why does this individual instance have these particular qualities?

Projection answers the first; completion answers the second.

## Logical Determination

Let:

```text
J = one rebirth-instance
N = its nikāyasabhāga
Kp = projecting karma
Kc₁ ... Kcₙ = completing karmas
```

Then:

```text
Projects(Kp, N)
N determines BirthClass(J)
```

with:

```text
ExactlyOneProjector(N)
```

while:

```text
Completes(Kc₁, J)
Completes(Kc₂, J)
...
Completes(Kcₙ, J)
```

The relations are non-equivalent:

```text
Projects(K, J)
    does not imply
CompletesAllFeatures(K, J)
```

and:

```text
Completes(K, J)
    does not imply
ProjectsBirthClass(K, J)
```

Similarly:

```text
PossessesViaPrāpti(X, D)
    does not imply
Projects(X, Birth)
```

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass phrase “one karma projects one birth” is correct but incomplete without `nikāyasabhāga`.

A more precise philosophical translation is:

> One projecting karma determines one rebirth-class; the concrete completion of that birth depends upon many completing karmas. The two non-mental attainments and acquisition-relations do not themselves perform the projecting function.

This avoids making `ākṣepa` sound as though one single karma exhaustively generates every feature of an entire life.

## Light Organon Contact

The Bhāṣya itself already supplies the architecture:

```text
projecting determination
    → establishes the unitary class-instance

completing determinations
    → populate and differentiate the instance
```

The painter simile makes this almost formal:

```text
outline
    ≠
completion
```

One determination establishes what kind of instance exists; many further determinations establish how that instance is concretely filled out.

This is a stronger and more textually earned model than treating karma as a single undifferentiated causal accumulator.
