## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first unpacks `te tu vijñaptyavijñaptī`: the two actions generated by volition, bodily and verbal karma, are each to be understood as having the nature of `vijñapti` and `avijñapti`.

He then turns specifically to bodily manifestation. The maintained position is that bodily `vijñapti` is `saṃsthāna`: the body is configured in one way or another under the power of mind (`cittavaśena`). The manifestation is therefore not an independently subsisting motion but a determinate bodily configuration produced under mental direction.

A rival position identifies bodily karma with `gati`, motion. Its intuitive basis is straightforward: bodily action seems to belong to a body while it is moving, not while it is motionless.

The reply is ontological. Motion understood as one conditioned entity passing from one place to another would require that the same entity persist across locations. But conditioned things are momentary.

Vasubandhu defines the momentary (`kṣaṇika`) through the moment (`kṣaṇa`): what acquires its own being and is destroyed immediately thereafter is momentary. Whatever is conditioned does not remain after acquiring its existence; it perishes exactly where it arises. Therefore spatial transference (`deśāntara-saṃkrānti`) of one and the same conditioned entity is impossible.

The objector then presses the hidden premise: this proves that bodily karma is not motion only if universal momentariness has first been established.

The answer appeals to `vyaya`, perishing. The prose following the verse begins to explain why destruction is not a produced effect requiring a destroying cause. A cause produces a result; destruction is absence (`abhāva`), and there is nothing to be produced in an absence. Thus destruction is not a second positive event imposed upon an enduring entity.

The apparent counterexample is ordinary destruction: wood seems to be destroyed through contact with fire. Vasubandhu replies that mere subsequent non-perception does not decide whether fire destroyed an enduring piece of wood or whether the previous wood-moment ceased by itself and no similar succeeding moment was produced. This issue must be settled inferentially, not simply by appeal to perception.

The argument therefore moves from a theory of bodily action into a general theory of conditioned existence:

```text
bodily manifestation
    → configuration rather than transit
    → because no conditioned entity persists through locations
    → because conditioned entities are momentary
    → because destruction is not an independently produced effect
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository voice** — explains that bodily and verbal karma are each `vijñapti` and `avijñapti`, and presents bodily manifestation as `saṃsthāna`.

**Voice: rival position** — `gatir ity apare`: others maintain that bodily action is motion, because bodily karma occurs in a moving body rather than a non-moving one.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / reply** — denies `gati` on the ground of momentariness and rejects transference of one enduring conditioned entity from place to place.

**Voice: Objector** — grants the conclusion conditionally but asks whether universal momentariness has actually been established; later appeals to perceived destruction of wood by fire.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / reply** — argues from `vyaya`, distinguishes destruction from a produced positive effect, and reframes the alleged perceptual evidence as an inferential question.

## New Technical Terms

- **cittavaśena** — under the power / determination of mind
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification operative here
  - **function:** explains how bodily configuration counts as expressive action rather than mere static shape

- **tathā tathā saṃsthāna** — configuration in this or that determinate manner
  - **status:** expansion of Kārikā `saṃsthāna`
  - **function:** specifies bodily `vijñapti` as determinate bodily articulation

- **prasyandamāna** — moving / flowing forth
  - **status:** introduced in rival account
  - **function:** phenomenological ground for identifying bodily karma with motion

- **ātmalābha** — acquisition of its own existence / coming into its own being
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical expression
  - **function:** defines the beginning of a momentary conditioned entity

- **anantaravināśin** — immediately perishing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definition of the momentary
  - **function:** fixes `kṣaṇika` as arising followed immediately by destruction

- **deśāntara-saṃkrānti** — transference to another place
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this argument
  - **function:** states what genuine enduring motion would require and what momentariness excludes

- **akasmika-vināśa** — destruction not produced by a separate destroying cause
  - **status:** major reflective term introduced by the prose
  - **function:** begins the proof that perishing belongs intrinsically to the conditioned sequence

- **abhāva** — absence / non-being
  - **status:** previously encountered general term, made argumentative here
  - **function:** grounds the claim that destruction is not itself a produced positive effect

- **anumāna-sādhya** — to be established by inference
  - **status:** Bhāṣya methodological term
  - **function:** distinguishes the ontological issue from what mere perception can settle

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes `vijñapti` substantially more precise than the Kārikā alone.

```text
cetanā
    mental determination
        ↓
body under the power of mind
        ↓
tathā tathā saṃsthāna
    determinate bodily configuration
        ↓
kāya-vijñapti
```

Thus bodily manifestation is not merely visible body-form. It is bodily configuration insofar as the configuration is generated under mental determination and thereby expresses action.

The rejection of `gati` does not deny apparent locomotion. It denies a metaphysical account in which one numerically identical conditioned thing endures while traversing successive places.

```text
not:
A-at-place-1 → same A-at-place-2

but:
moment A₁ ceases
    → conditioned production of A₂
    → conditioned production of A₃
```

The exact causal account of the succeeding moments continues into VAK 4.03.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required, not reversal.**

The existing translation of `saṃsthāna` as **configuration** remains strong. The Bhāṣya adds the essential qualification:

```text
cittavaśena
    under the power of mind
```

A sharper philosophical translation is therefore:

> Verbal and bodily action are each manifest and non-manifest. Bodily manifestation is held to be the body's determinate configuration under the power of mind, not motion; for conditioned existence is momentary because it perishes.

The phrase “under the power of mind” belongs to the Bhāṣya rather than the Kārikā itself, so it should be treated as explanatory expansion rather than inserted silently into a strictly literal verse translation.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a regress from an apparent empirical phenomenon to its ontological condition:

```text
body appears to move
    ↓
rival: bodily karma = motion
    ↓
what would motion require?
    persistence of one entity across places
    ↓
conditioned entity is momentary
    ↓
no deśāntara-saṃkrānti of one identical entity
    ↓
therefore bodily vijñapti ≠ enduring motion
    ↓
bodily vijñapti = momentary determinate configuration
```

The proof of momentariness itself turns on a second reflective movement:

```text
destruction
    is not a positive product
    ↓
absence has no product to be made
    ↓
a special destroying cause is not required to produce destruction
    ↓
what requires causal explanation is the production of the succeeding moment
```

This is the decisive conceptual reversal. Continuity is not explained by an enduring substrate to which destruction later happens. The burden shifts to the conditioned production of the next determination.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage is especially important for the Chapter 4 research question:

```text
How does cetanā become objective activity?
```

The first answer is now textually stronger:

```text
cetanā
    → mind determines body
    → determinate bodily configuration
    → vijñapti
```

Manifest action is therefore not an enduring thing migrating through spacetime. It is a **determinate expression generated anew within conditioned succession**.

That is already a useful discipline for our later treatment of `avijñapti`: whatever continuity `avijñapti` supplies cannot simply reintroduce the enduring moving substrate just rejected here.

No stronger Organon identification is required yet. The next verse must complete the argument concerning destruction, causal succession, and the status of configuration.
