# VAK 4.88 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā

**anāsravaṃ punaḥ śeṣāṃ tribhir avyākṛtaṃ ca yat |**  
**catvāri dve tathā trīṇi kuśalasya śubhādayaḥ || 4.88 ||**

> Other uncontaminated karma, and neutral karma, have three fruits. For wholesome karma, wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral dharmas are its fruits in counts of four, two, and three respectively.

[Return to Kārikā](./VAK_4.88.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
anāsravaṃ punaḥ śeṣāṃ tribhiravyākṛtaṃ ca yat /

prahāṇamārgādanyadanāsravaṃ karma avyākṛtaṃ ca karma tribhirvipākavisaṃyogaphale hitvā /

catvāri dve tathā trīṇi kuśalasya śubhādayaḥ // VAkK_4.88 //

anukramamiti paścādvakṣyati /

kuśalsya karmaṇaḥ kuśalā dharmāścatvāri phalāni vipākaphalaṃ hitvā /

akuśalā dve puruṣakārādhipatiphale /

avyākṛtā strīṇi niḥṣyandavisaṃyogaphale hitvā /
```

## Natural Segmentation

### 1. Other uncontaminated karma and neutral karma

`prahāṇamārgād anyad anāsravaṃ karma` — uncontaminated karma other than that belonging to the immediate path of abandoning — has three fruits.

Neutral (`avyākṛta`) karma likewise has three fruits.

In both cases the excluded fruits are:

```text
vipāka-phala
visaṃyoga-phala
```

Therefore the three retained fruits are:

```text
niḥṣyanda-phala
puruṣakāra-phala
adhipati-phala
```

### 2. Fruits of wholesome karma according to the ethical quality of the resulting dharmas

The second half begins a new classification. The Bhāṣya notes that the understood order is `anukramam` / `yathākramam`: respectively.

For wholesome karma as cause:

```text
wholesome dharmas
    → four fruits

unwholesome dharmas
    → two fruits

neutral dharmas
    → three fruits
```

The exact membership is:

```text
wholesome effects:
    niḥṣyanda
    visaṃyoga
    puruṣakāra
    adhipati
    [vipāka excluded]

unwholesome effects:
    puruṣakāra
    adhipati

neutral effects:
    vipāka
    puruṣakāra
    adhipati
    [niḥṣyanda and visaṃyoga excluded]
```

## Literal Prose Translation

> Uncontaminated karma other than that of the path of abandoning, and neutral karma, each possess three fruits, maturation and disconnection being excluded. As for wholesome karma: wholesome dharmas are its fruits in four ways, maturation being excluded; unwholesome dharmas in two ways, as activity-produced and dominant fruits; and neutral dharmas in three ways, outflow and disconnection being excluded.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 4.87 classified karma according to the number of fruit-relations available to major causal classes. VAK 4.88 now introduces two further distinctions.

First:

```text
same fruit-count
    does not imply
same causal class
```

Other uncontaminated karma and neutral karma both have three fruits, but they reach this count because neither gives maturation or disconnection as fruit.

Second, and more importantly, the Bhāṣya now changes the question from:

```text
How many fruits does this karma have?
```

to:

```text
Given karma of ethical quality Q,
what kinds of dharmas can stand to it
in each fruit-relation?
```

Thus the causal analysis becomes relational in two dimensions:

```text
cause-quality
    ×
result-quality
    ×
fruit-relation
```

For wholesome karma the row is:

| Resulting dharmas | Number of fruit-relations | Included fruits | Excluded fruits |
|---|---:|---|---|
| wholesome | 4 | niḥṣyanda, visaṃyoga, puruṣakāra, adhipati | vipāka |
| unwholesome | 2 | puruṣakāra, adhipati | vipāka, niḥṣyanda, visaṃyoga |
| neutral | 3 | vipāka, puruṣakāra, adhipati | niḥṣyanda, visaṃyoga |

This prepares the symmetrical treatment of unwholesome karma in the continuation and neutral karma in VAK 4.89.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, explanatory Abhidharma analysis.

The passage is largely classificatory rather than polemical. The phrase `anukramam iti paścād vakṣyati` is a compact authorial clarification that the numerical sequence is to be read respectively.

## New Technical Terms

### `śeṣa-anāsrava-karma`

- **Meaning:** uncontaminated karma other than the immediate path of abandoning.
- **Status:** Bhāṣya-level clarification of the Kārikā's `śeṣam anāsravam`.
- **Function:** separates non-path uncontaminated activity from the special abandoning-path case treated in 4.87.

### `anukramam / yathākramam`

- **Meaning:** respectively, in the stated order.
- **Status:** explanatory connective.
- **Function:** binds the counts 4–2–3 to wholesome–unwholesome–neutral resulting dharmas without ambiguity.

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Ethical quality and causal role are independent axes

A wholesome cause does not produce only wholesome dharmas under every fruit-relation.

The Bhāṣya explicitly permits:

```text
wholesome karma
    → unwholesome dharmas
        as puruṣakāra or adhipati fruit
```

and:

```text
wholesome karma
    → neutral dharmas
        as vipāka, puruṣakāra, or adhipati fruit
```

Therefore:

```text
ethical quality of cause
    ≠
ethical quality of every causally related result
```

The kind of fruit-relation mediates the connection.

### 2. Maturation has a special ethical structure

Wholesome dharmas are not `vipāka-phala` of wholesome karma in this analysis. Neutral dharmas can be.

This preserves the familiar Abhidharma principle that maturation-result itself is ethically neutral even when its causal karma is wholesome or unwholesome.

Thus:

```text
wholesome karma
    ↓ vipāka
neutral maturation dharma
```

not:

```text
wholesome karma
    ↓ vipāka
wholesome dharma
```

### 3. Disconnection is restricted

`visaṃyoga-phala` can stand among wholesome dharmas but not among unwholesome or neutral dharmas in this row.

Disconnection is therefore not a generic effect-class. It belongs to a very specific liberative causal structure.

### 4. Puruṣakāra and adhipati are the broadest cross-quality relations

All three result-quality columns retain `puruṣakāra` and `adhipati`.

That suggests a broad causal reach:

```text
wholesome → wholesome
wholesome → unwholesome
wholesome → neutral
```

can all be connected through activity-produced or dominant-result relations.

## Logical Determination

The verse requires a matrix, not a scalar function.

A simple representation such as:

```text
FruitCount(WholesomeKarma) = 4
```

is inadequate.

The correct form is closer to:

```text
FruitRelations(
    causeQuality,
    resultQuality
)
```

For wholesome karma:

```text
FruitRelations(Wholesome, Wholesome)
    = {Niṣyanda, Visaṃyoga, Puruṣakāra, Adhipati}

FruitRelations(Wholesome, Unwholesome)
    = {Puruṣakāra, Adhipati}

FruitRelations(Wholesome, Neutral)
    = {Vipāka, Puruṣakāra, Adhipati}
```

This yields an important general law:

```text
same causal source
    can stand in different result-relations
    to differently qualified effects
```

The fruit-name therefore specifies a **mode of causal dependence**, not merely a label attached to a later event.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass Kārikā file should eventually make clearer that the second half is not simply another list of absolute fruit-counts. The counts 4–2–3 are indexed by the ethical quality of the dharmas counted as fruits of wholesome karma.

A stronger working translation is:

> Other uncontaminated karma, and neutral karma, each have three fruits. As for wholesome karma, wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral dharmas are its fruits in four, two, and three ways respectively.

## Light Organon Contact

The useful formal lesson is relational typing.

A causal system should not encode only:

```text
Cause → Effect
```

but:

```text
Cause
    --relationType-->
Effect
```

and both cause and effect retain their own independent type-properties.

Thus a rational causal graph needs at least:

```text
CausalRelation {
    cause
    causeQuality
    relationType
    effect
    effectQuality
}
```

The Kośa's five-fruit scheme is therefore already behaving like a typed causal relation algebra: causal source, result quality, and relation-type must all be represented independently.
