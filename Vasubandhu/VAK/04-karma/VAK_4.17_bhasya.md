# VAK_4.17_bhasya — Bearers of Restraint and Mind-Following Restraint

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.17**

```iast
prātimokṣānvitā aṣṭau dhyānajena tadanvitaḥ |
anāsraveṇāryasattvāḥ antyau cittānuvartinau ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> The eight disciplinary classes are endowed with prātimokṣa restraint. Whoever is endowed with dhyāna is necessarily endowed with the restraint born from dhyāna. Noble beings are endowed with uncontaminated restraint. Of these three restraints, the latter two follow the mind.

## Source Cautions

The electronic text separates the opening as `prātimokṣānvitā aṣṭau`; sandhi would permit `prātimokṣānvitāṣṭau`. The exact source reading is preserved.

The expression `dhyānāddhyāne vā jāto dhyānajaḥ` explicitly allows two explanatory relations: born **from** dhyāna or born **in** dhyāna. The translation should therefore avoid reducing `dhyānaja` to only one causal nuance.

`Sāmantaka` is included under `dhyāna` by extended designation. Vasubandhu compares this with calling the outskirts of a village part of the village itself.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu asks who is endowed (`samanvāgata`) with each of the three restraints introduced earlier.

First, the eight disciplinary classes are endowed with prātimokṣa restraint: from bhikṣu and bhikṣuṇī through the eighth class, the upavāsa observer.

A question is then raised about outsiders (`bāhyaka`). Do they possess no undertaken discipline at all? Vasubandhu answers that they do possess `samādānaśīla`, undertaken discipline, but not `prātimokṣasaṃvara`. The reason is teleological and doctrinal: their discipline does not conduce to complete release from evil (`atyantaṃ pāpasya pratimokṣaṇa`), because it remains dependent upon continued existence or becoming (`bhavasaṃniśrita`).

Second, meditation-born restraint belongs necessarily to one endowed with dhyāna. Vasubandhu explains `dhyānaja` as born from dhyāna or in dhyāna. For this purpose the neighboring or preliminary attainment (`sāmantaka`) is also called dhyāna by extension, just as the outskirts of a village may be called the village.

Third, noble persons (`āryapudgala`) are endowed with uncontaminated restraint. Vasubandhu divides these noble persons into trainees (`śaikṣa`) and non-trainees (`aśaikṣa`).

Finally, he returns to an earlier statement concerning coexistent cause (`sahabhūhetu`): two restraints are said to follow the mind. Which two? Of the three restraints, the latter two — meditation-born restraint and uncontaminated restraint — are `cittānuvartin`, mind-following. Prātimokṣa restraint is not.

The reason is decisive: prātimokṣa restraint continues even when another kind of mind is present and even in a mindless state (`anyacittācittakasyāpy anuvṛtti`). Its persistence is therefore not conditioned by moment-to-moment presence of the specific mind from which meditation-born or uncontaminated restraint arises.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks which beings possess which restraints and gives the classifications.

**Voice: Objector / interlocutor** — `kiṃ khalu bāhyakānāṃ samādānaśīlaṃ nāsti`: do outsiders really possess no undertaken discipline?

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — outsiders possess undertaken discipline but not prātimokṣa restraint, because their discipline remains dependent upon `bhava` and does not tend toward complete release from evil.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explains `dhyānaja`, the inclusion of `sāmantaka`, the noble bearers of uncontaminated restraint, and the distinction between mind-following and non-mind-following restraint.

## New Technical Terms

- **samanvāgata** — endowed with / possessed of; **status:** previously encountered doctrinal relation; **function:** states the relation between a continuum and a restraint-dharma.
- **bāhyaka** — outsider, one outside the Buddhist discipline; **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this immediate sequence; **function:** introduces comparison between non-Buddhist undertaken discipline and prātimokṣa restraint.
- **samādānaśīla** — undertaken discipline; **status:** new compound in this sequence; **function:** acknowledges genuine discipline outside prātimokṣa while distinguishing its soteriological ground.
- **bhavasaṃniśrita** — dependent on / based upon continued existence or becoming; **status:** doctrinal characterization; **function:** explains why outsider discipline is not prātimokṣa restraint.
- **sāmantaka** — neighboring / preliminary attainment; **status:** new technical term here; **function:** included under `dhyāna` by extended designation.
- **āryapudgala** — noble person; **status:** established Buddhist technical term; **function:** bearer of uncontaminated restraint.
- **śaikṣa** — trainee; **status:** established Path term; **function:** one class of noble person endowed with uncontaminated restraint.
- **aśaikṣa** — non-trainee; **status:** established Path term; **function:** second class of noble person endowed with uncontaminated restraint.
- **cittānuvartin** — following the mind; **status:** explicit technical predicate; **function:** distinguishes restraints whose presence depends upon the corresponding mind from prātimokṣa restraint.
- **acittaka** — mindless / without mind; **status:** established Abhidharma condition; **function:** proves that prātimokṣa restraint can continue independently of present mind.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Bearers of the three restraints

```text
prātimokṣa-saṃvara
    → eight disciplinary classes

Dhyāna-saṃvara
    → one endowed with dhyāna
    → including sāmantaka by extended designation

anāsrava-saṃvara
    → āryapudgalas
    → śaikṣa and aśaikṣa
```

The verse is therefore not merely listing restraint-types. It specifies the conditions under which a continuum is `samanvāgata`, endowed with each restraint.

### Outsider discipline and prātimokṣa

The Bhāṣya explicitly refuses the crude claim that outsiders possess no discipline:

```text
bāhyaka
    → may possess samādānaśīla

but

bāhyaka discipline
    ≠ prātimokṣa-saṃvara
```

The distinction is not mere institutional membership. Vasubandhu gives a soteriological ground:

```text
prātimokṣa
    → tends toward complete release from evil

outsider undertaken discipline
    → bhavasaṃniśrita
    → remains dependent upon continued existence / becoming
```

This should be preserved as Vasubandhu's doctrinal claim rather than generalized beyond the passage.

### The latter two are mind-following

```text
three restraints
├── prātimokṣa-saṃvara
│   └── not cittānuvartin
├── dhyāna-saṃvara
│   └── cittānuvartin
└── anāsrava-saṃvara
    └── cittānuvartin
```

The criterion is temporal dependence:

```text
dhyāna / anāsrava restraint
    present with corresponding mind
    → mind-following

prātimokṣa restraint
    continues with another mind
    and even without mind
    → not mind-following
```

This is stronger than the Kārikā file's provisional formulation that the latter two merely “arise and persist in dependence upon the corresponding state of mind.” The Bhāṣya supplies the contrastive test explicitly.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation can be sharpened to:

> The eight disciplinary classes are endowed with prātimokṣa restraint. Whoever is endowed with dhyāna is necessarily endowed with the restraint born from dhyāna. Noble beings are endowed with uncontaminated restraint. Of these three restraints, the latter two follow the mind.

The Bhāṣya resolves `tadanvitaḥ`: it refers to the person endowed with dhyāna, who is necessarily endowed with dhyāna restraint.

It also fixes the practical force of `cittānuvartin`: the latter two restraints depend on the corresponding mind, whereas prātimokṣa restraint persists through other mental states and even through mindlessness.

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya distinguish two different modes of persistence:

```text
mind-dependent persistence
    dhyāna-saṃvara
    anāsrava-saṃvara

mind-independent continuity
    prātimokṣa-saṃvara
```

The second should not be misunderstood as absolutely independent of all causal conditions. The point is narrower: prātimokṣa restraint does not require the continued presence of a corresponding present mind.

A further distinction appears in the outsider discussion:

```text
formal or undertaken discipline
    ≠
prātimokṣa restraint
```

The difference is stated in terms of the end toward which the restraint tends, not merely its outward behavioral form.

## Organon Contact Point

A restrained contact point is sufficient:

```text
one practical determination
    may persist through changing mental states

another practical determination
    exists only with a determinate mental condition
```

This distinction between mind-following and non-mind-following practical determination is significant for later reconstruction, but the present pass should leave the Sarvāstivāda classifications intact.
