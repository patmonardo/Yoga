# VAK_4.22_bhasya — Cross-Current Avijñapti and the Force of Prasāda or Kleśa

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.22**

```iast
asaṃvarasthaḥ śubhayāśubhayā saṃvare sthitaḥ |
avijñaptyānvito yāvat prasādakleśavegavān ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> One established in non-restraint may be endowed with wholesome avijñapti, and one established in restraint with unwholesome avijñapti, for as long as the force of faith or of defilement continues.

## Source Cautions

The introductory prose at [210|11-210|12] reads `vijñaptyā`, while the verse and its direct explanation concern `avijñapti`. Since the discussion concludes with `avijñaptyadhikāraḥ samāptaḥ`, the e-text may contain an omission or irregularity in the introductory sentence. The exact source reading is preserved rather than silently corrected.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu asks whether ethical orientation and occasional avijñapti must always agree.

Can a person established in `asaṃvara`, non-restraint, ever be endowed with a wholesome determination? Conversely, can a person established in `saṃvara`, restraint, ever become endowed with an unwholesome determination?

The answer is yes in both cases.

A person established in non-restraint can produce wholesome avijñapti when acting under a strong force of `prasāda`, for example while engaging in praise, reverence, or similar acts (`stava-vandanādi-kriyā`).

Conversely, a person established in restraint can produce unwholesome avijñapti when acting under a strong force of `kleśa`, for example while killing, binding, striking, or performing similar actions (`vadha-bandhana-tāḍanādi-kriyā`).

The resulting avijñapti lasts for as long as the corresponding motivating force continues. Thus its duration is not determined merely by the person's standing ethical status; it is determined by the continuing force that generated the contrary action.

Vasubandhu then applies the same temporal rule used earlier: in the first moment one possesses only present avijñapti; in later moments one also possesses its past instances.

With this, he explicitly closes the present section on avijñapti: `avijñaptyadhikāraḥ samāptaḥ`.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu / framing question** — asks whether a non-restrained person can possess wholesome determination and a restrained person unwholesome determination, and for how long.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explains the two contrary cases through the force of `prasāda` and `kleśa` and gives concrete examples.

## New Technical Terms

- **prasādavega** — force or intensity of `prasāda`; **status:** newly operative in this passage; **function:** explains how wholesome avijñapti can arise in one established in non-restraint and how long it continues.
- **kleśavega** — force or intensity of defilement; **status:** newly operative in this passage; **function:** explains how unwholesome avijñapti can arise in one established in restraint and how long it continues.
- **stava-vandana** — praise and reverence; **status:** Bhāṣya example; **function:** example of wholesome action under strong `prasāda`.
- **vadha-bandhana-tāḍana** — killing, binding, striking; **status:** Bhāṣya example; **function:** examples of unwholesome action under strong `kleśa`.
- **avijñaptyadhikāra** — section or topic concerning avijñapti; **status:** structural prose marker; **function:** explicitly closes the present avijñapti discussion.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse prevents us from identifying a person's established ethical status with every action that can arise in that continuum.

```text
asaṃvara-stha
    can generate wholesome avijñapti

saṃvara-stha
    can generate unwholesome avijñapti
```

The decisive condition is the force of the immediate motivational state:

```text
prasādavega
    → wholesome contrary avijñapti

kleśavega
    → unwholesome contrary avijñapti
```

And the duration rule is:

```text
motivating force continues
    → corresponding avijñapti continues

motivating force ceases
    → that occasional contrary avijñapti ceases
```

This means stable restraint-status and occasional karmic determination belong to different levels of classification.

### Temporal possession

The ordinary rule remains:

```text
first moment
    → present avijñapti only

later moments
    → present + past avijñapti
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Bhāṣya confirms that `prasāda-kleśa-vega-vān` governs the duration of the contrary avijñapti.

A clear translation is:

> One established in non-restraint may be endowed with wholesome avijñapti, and one established in restraint with unwholesome avijñapti, for as long as the force of faith or of defilement continues.

`Prasāda` is left close to the Sanskrit rather than reduced simply to belief; here it is a wholesome affective-cognitive force capable of generating action.

## Logical Determination

The passage separates:

```text
established ethical status
    ≠
ethical determination of every occurrent act
```

and:

```text
structural standing
    ≠
local contrary production
```

A stable practical determination can admit contrary episodic action without thereby becoming identical with that action.

The duration of the episodic determination is proportional not to the standing status but to the persistence of its generating force.

## Organon Contact Point

A restrained contact point is sufficient:

```text
standing determination
    and
occurrent determination

must be distinguished
```

The passage is valuable precisely because it blocks a simplistic model in which one ethical status mechanically fixes every subsequent act.
