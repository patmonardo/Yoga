## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu begins from the previously used expression `asaṃvarastha`, “one established in non-restraint,” and asks what `asaṃvara` itself is.

The verse gives a set of alternative designations. Vasubandhu says explicitly that these are `paryāyaśabdāḥ`, synonymous or alternative terms for the same non-restraint complex.

Each name, however, is justified from a different determination:

- `asaṃvara` because body and speech are not restrained (`kāyavācor asaṃvaraṇa`).
- `duścarita` because it is censured by good people and produces undesirable fruit (`sadbhiḥ kutsitatvāt`, `aniṣṭaphalatvāt`).
- `dauḥśīlya` because it is opposed to `śīla` (`śīlavipakṣāt`).
- `karma` because it is bodily and verbal action (`kāyavākkarmatvāt`).
- `karmapatha` because it is included in the fundamental or principal phase (`maulasaṃgṛhītatvāt`).

Thus Vasubandhu is not merely accumulating moral labels. He is showing that the same practical determination can be named from its restrictive status, ethical valuation, opposition to discipline, action-nature, and place within the fundamental action-course.

The passage then changes question. Having defined non-restraint, Vasubandhu asks whether one can be endowed with `vijñapti` but not `avijñapti`, introducing a four-cornered classification (`catuṣkoṭika`). The actual cases begin with VAK 4.25.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks `ko 'yam asaṃvaro nāma`, gives the explanatory grounds for each synonym, and introduces the following `catuṣkoṭika`.

No distinct objector voice is required in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **paryāyaśabda** — alternative / synonymous designation; **status:** explanatory Bhāṣya terminology; **function:** marks the five expressions as different names for the same non-restraint complex.
- **duścarita** — bad conduct; **status:** technical ethical designation; **function:** names non-restraint from blameworthiness and undesirable fruit.
- **dauḥśīlya** — bad discipline / misconduct opposed to `śīla`; **status:** technical ethical designation; **function:** names non-restraint as the contrary of discipline.
- **maulasaṃgṛhīta** — included in the fundamental phase; **status:** previously prepared by `maula`; **function:** explains why non-restraint is called a `karmapatha`.
- **catuṣkoṭika** — four-cornered classification; **status:** previously encountered method; **function:** opens the next analysis of possible possession-relations between `vijñapti` and `avijñapti`.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives five aspectual determinations of one non-restraint complex:

```text
asaṃvara
    lack of restraint of body and speech

duścarita
    censured conduct with undesirable fruit

dauḥśīlya
    contrary of śīla

karma
    bodily and verbal action

karmapatha
    included in the fundamental action-course
```

The names are co-referential in this context, but their explanatory grounds are distinct.

This also confirms that `asaṃvara` is not mere absence of a vow. It is treated positively as bodily-verbal action and as a fundamental karmapatha.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā can be translated straightforwardly:

> Non-restraint is also called bad conduct, bad discipline, action, and the corresponding path of action.

`Dauḥśīlya` is best kept close to its explicit Bhāṣya ground, opposition to `śīla`; “bad discipline” or “misconduct opposed to discipline” is therefore preferable to importing a more general moral vocabulary.

## Logical Determination

VAK 4.24 again shows one determination under several predicates:

```text
one practical complex
    → named from absence of restraint
    → named from ethical defect
    → named from opposition to discipline
    → named from action-nature
    → named from structural place in karmapatha
```

The important restraint on interpretation is:

```text
several names
    ≠ several independent dharmas
```

The difference lies in the explanatory ground of predication.
