# VAK_4.21_bhasya — Present Mind-Following Restraint and the Middle Position

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 4.21**

```iast
samāhītāryamārgasthau tau yuktau vartamānayā |
madhyasthasyāsti ced ādau madhyayā ūrdhvaṃ dvikālayā ||
```

Working translation after the Bhāṣya pass:

> The concentrated practitioner and the one established in the noble Path are respectively endowed with present meditation-born and uncontaminated avijñapti. If a person established in neither restraint nor non-restraint possesses avijñapti, then at first it is possessed only as present, and afterward as both past and present.

Kārikā analysis: `VAK_4.21.md`.

## Source Cautions

The e-text reads `samāhitāryamārgasthau`; the prose gloss resolves the intended pair as one entered into concentration and one entered into the noble Path (`samāhita-āryamārga-samāpannau`). The source form is preserved without silent emendation.

The term `madhyastha` is explicitly defined by the Bhāṣya as one established in neither `saṃvara` nor `asaṃvara`; this confirms the Kārikā file's provisional identification.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first completes the temporal account of the two mind-following restraints. The practitioner endowed with meditation-born restraint and the noble practitioner endowed with uncontaminated restraint each possess a present avijñapti while actually entered into the corresponding concentration or noble Path. The qualification `na tu vyutthitau` is decisive: when emerged from those states, they do not possess the corresponding avijñapti as present.

This closes the temporal analysis of those established in restraint and non-restraint.

Vasubandhu then turns to the `madhyastha`, literally the one standing in the middle. He defines this person as one established in neither restraint nor non-restraint. Such a person does not necessarily possess avijñapti at all. But if avijñapti does arise—for example in connection with misconduct, components of discipline, and related cases—then in its first moment the person is endowed with it only as present.

The verse calls the present the “middle” temporal mode because it stands between past and future. From the second moment onward, the same avijñapti-series is possessed in two temporal modes: as past and as present.

Finally, Vasubandhu explicitly carries forward the condition `atyāgāt` from the preceding discussion: possession continues only so long as the avijñapti has not been abandoned.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — throughout the passage. The prose is explanatory and classificatory; no explicit objection is introduced in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **vyutthita** — emerged from / no longer entered into a meditative or Path state; **status:** new in this immediate temporal analysis; **function:** marks the absence of present mind-following restraint outside the corresponding state.
- **madhyastha** — one established in neither restraint nor non-restraint; **status:** explicit Bhāṣya definition; **function:** identifies the third practical position corresponding to avijñapti that is neither `saṃvara` nor `asaṃvara`.
- **madhyā** — the middle temporal mode, i.e. the present between past and future; **status:** explanatory gloss; **function:** resolves the compressed Kārikā expression `ādau madhyayā`.
- **dauḥśīlyaśīlāṅgādi-saṃgṛhīta** — included under misconduct, components of discipline, and related cases; **status:** local explanatory phrase; **function:** gives examples of occasional avijñapti in a `madhyastha`.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Present possession of mind-following restraints

```text
samāhita practitioner
    + actual meditative attainment
    → present dhyānaja avijñapti

ārya practitioner
    + actual noble Path
    → present anāsrava avijñapti
```

But:

```text
vyutthita
    emerged from the corresponding state
    → no present mind-following avijñapti
```

This sharpens VAK 4.17's statement that the last two restraints are `cittānuvartin`: their present existence follows the corresponding mind-state itself.

### The middle position

```text
madhyastha
    neither saṃvara-stha
    nor asaṃvara-stha
```

The third class is therefore not an established ethical system parallel to the other two. The person may possess no avijñapti at all.

If occasional avijñapti arises:

```text
first moment
    → present only

from second moment onward
    → past + present
```

### Governing condition

```text
atyāga
    non-abandonment
```

continues to govern possession throughout this temporal discussion.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The Kārikā file's first-pass interpretation is substantially confirmed, but two points should be made explicit.

First, `samāhita` and `āryamārgastha` possess present avijñapti only while actually entered into the corresponding state, not while emerged from it.

Second, `madhyastha` is explicitly defined as one established in neither restraint nor non-restraint, and such a person does not necessarily possess avijñapti.

A refined translation is:

> The concentrated practitioner and the one established in the noble Path are respectively endowed with present avijñapti while actually in those states. If one established in neither restraint nor non-restraint possesses avijñapti, then at first it is possessed only as present, and afterward as both past and present.

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes two forms of practical continuity:

```text
state-dependent determination
    dhyānaja / anāsrava restraint
    exists presently only with its corresponding citta

occasional determination
    madhyastha avijñapti
    need not exist at all,
    but once produced acquires a past temporal accumulation
```

Thus:

```text
absence of established restraint
    ≠
absence of all karmic determination
```

and:

```text
present dependence on a state
    ≠
serial possession once an occasional determination has arisen
```

## Organon Contact Point

A restrained contact point is sufficient:

```text
a determination may be
    structurally state-dependent
or
    contingently produced
```

The `madhyastha` is especially useful because it prevents the opposition `saṃvara / asaṃvara` from exhausting the practical field.
