# VAK 6.24 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**ābodheḥ sarvam ekatra dhyānāntye śāstṛkhaḍgayoḥ |**  
**prāk tebhyo mokṣabhāgīyaṃ kṣipraṃ mokṣas tribhir bhavaiḥ || 6.24 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 348|21–349|22.

## 1. One Sitting from the Preparations to Awakening

Vasubandhu identifies the two exceptional practitioners:

- **śāstṛ** — the Buddha, the Teacher;
- **khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpa pratyekabuddha** — the rhinoceros-like solitary Buddha.

For these two, the sequence from the **nirvedhabhāgīyas** through Awakening occurs **in one and the same sitting**:

> **tayor ekatraivāsane ... nirvedhabhāgīyāny ārabhya yāvad bodhir utpadyate**

The support is the **fourth dhyāna**, because of its unmoving and sharp samādhi:

> **aniñjya-paṭu-samādhitvāt**

Thus the decisive Path sequence can, in these exceptional cases, become completely continuous:

```text
nirvedhabhāgīya
    ↓
Path of Seeing
    ↓
Path of Cultivation
    ↓
final Knowledges
    ↓
Awakening
```

Vasubandhu notes that Awakening is constituted by **kṣayajñāna** and **anutpādajñāna**, knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge of non-arising, to be explained later.

An alternative view begins the uninterrupted sequence already from aśubha practice, but Vasubandhu reports this as **apare**, “others.”

## 2. The Nirvedhabhāgīyas Require an Earlier Root

Vasubandhu asks whether a practitioner can simply begin in one life and immediately produce the nirvedhabhāgīyas.

His answer is categorical:

> **naitad asti** — this does not occur.

Before them there must first arise:

> **mokṣabhāgīyaṃ kuśalamūlam** — a wholesome root conducive to liberation.

So the causal architecture is now explicitly three-tiered:

```text
mokṣabhāgīya
    orientation toward liberation

nirvedhabhāgīya
    preparation for decisive penetration

āryamārga
    supramundane Noble Path
```

This is crucial for the developing account of operative Path Knowledge. The Truth-capacity does not begin with Heat. Before penetration can be prepared, the continuum must first acquire a **direction toward liberation**.

## 3. The Three-Life Minimum

Vasubandhu gives the quickest ordinary sequence:

```text
first life:
    mokṣabhāgīya wholesome root

second life:
    nirvedhabhāgīyas

third life:
    Noble Path
```

He compares this to:

> **bījaviropaṇa-sasyābhivṛddhi-phalotpatti-krama**

```text
sowing the seed
    ↓
growth of the crop
    ↓
production of the fruit
```

The comparison is not decorative. It states the causal structure of the Path.

Vasubandhu then gives the abstract formulation:

> **avatāra-paripāka-vimuktayaḥ**

```text
avatāra
    entry into the Dharma-structure

paripāka
    maturation / ripening of the continuum

vimukti
    liberation
```

These arise in sequence because this is the **dharmatā**, the lawful nature, of the continuum.

This is one of the strongest genetic statements in the whole preparatory section:

> liberation is not an isolated event but the fruit of a continuum that has entered, ripened, and become capable of release.

## 4. What Kind of Prajñā Is Mokṣabhāgīya?

The Bhāṣya adds an important qualification not stated directly in the Kārikā:

> **śrutacintāmayaṃ na bhāvanāmayam**

The mokṣabhāgīya root is produced through:

- **śruta** — learning/hearing;
- **cintā** — reflection;

but not through bhāvanā in the strict technical sense.

So the threefold Prajñā sequence is now distributed across the Path architecture:

```text
mokṣabhāgīya
    śruta + cintā

nirvedhabhāgīya
    bhāvanā

āryamārga
    supramundane penetration
```

This clarifies the transformation from Truth as meaningful orientation into Truth as cultivated causal power.

## 5. Liberation-Conducive Action Is Principally Mental

Vasubandhu asks what kind of karma produces the mokṣabhāgīya root.

All three forms of action can participate:

```text
body
speech
mind
```

But principally:

> **prādhānyena tu manaskarma** — primarily mental action.

Bodily and verbal action become liberation-conducive when gathered under a liberative resolve:

> **tat-praṇidhāna-parigrahāt**

Thus even apparently small acts can acquire a radically different causal function when appropriated by the intention for liberation.

Vasubandhu gives striking examples:

- giving even a single alms offering;
- undertaking even a single training rule;

can project liberation-conducive roots when empowered by:

> **mokṣābhilāṣa-balādhāna** — the strengthening power of aspiration for liberation.

So the causal value of action is not exhausted by its external form. Its orientation within the continuum matters.

## 6. Human Acquisition

The mokṣabhāgīya root is initially projected among humans on the three inhabited continents:

> **manuṣyeṣv eva triṣu dvīpeṣu**

Vasubandhu excludes other destinies according to the absence, as appropriate, of sufficient Prajñā and penetration-capacity.

The important point for this block is that liberation-orientation first requires a particular kind of human cognitive-practical support before the nirvedhabhāgīyas can mature.

## 7. Closing the Preparatory Architecture

Vasubandhu explicitly closes the digression:

> **uktaṃ prasaṅgena mokṣabhāgīyam**

and returns to the sequence of abhisamaya:

> **abhisamayakramas tu vaktum ārabdhaḥ**

Up through the Highest Mundane Dharmas has now been explained; what remains is the actual breakthrough.

Thus 6.24 functions naturally as the end of this preparatory block.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

The complete structure developed in 6a can now be expressed genetically:

```text
liberative aspiration
    ↓
mokṣabhāgīya
    Truth becomes the governing direction

śruta + cintā
    ↓
orientation becomes determinate

bhāvanā
    ↓
nirvedhabhāgīyas
    Truth becomes cultivated capacity

Heat → Summits → Acceptance → Highest Mundane Dharmas
    ↓
capacity becomes increasingly stable and irreversible

āvāhana / ākarṣaṇa
    ↓
Path is brought on / drawn in

āryamārga
    ↓
Truth becomes supramundane penetration
```

The Bhāṣya therefore presents liberation as a lawful transformation of the continuum rather than as an inexplicable leap.

## New Technical Terms

- **avatāra** — entry; here the first effective entry of the continuum into the liberative Dharma-process.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya explanatory term.
  - **function:** first moment of the entry–ripening–liberation triad.

- **paripāka** — ripening, maturation.
  - **status:** central causal-development term.
  - **function:** names the transformation by which liberative orientation becomes mature capacity.

- **vimukti** — liberation.
  - **status:** established term, given here as the fruit of the genetic sequence.
  - **function:** culmination of entry and ripening.

- **mokṣābhilāṣa** — aspiration/desire for liberation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** gives bodily, verbal, and especially mental acts a liberation-conducive orientation.

- **balādhāna** — strengthening, imparting force.
  - **status:** appears in the compound mokṣābhilāṣa-balādhāna.
  - **function:** explains how aspiration changes the causal potency of an act.

- **aniñjya-paṭu-samādhi** — unmoving, sharp samādhi.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanation of fourth-dhyāna support.
  - **function:** supports the uninterrupted path sequence of Buddha and rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, with one explicitly reported alternative position (`apare`) concerning beginning the one-sitting sequence from aśubha.

The three-life sequence is stated as the fastest ordinary maturation, not as an absolute timetable for every practitioner.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.24 closes the preparatory architecture:

- Buddha and rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha can complete the decisive sequence from the nirvedhabhāgīyas to Awakening in one sitting on the fourth dhyāna.
- Ordinary practitioners require an earlier mokṣabhāgīya wholesome root before the nirvedhabhāgīyas can arise.
- The fastest ordinary sequence is modeled across three lives: liberation-orientation, penetration-preparation, Noble Path.
- Vasubandhu summarizes the genetic law as **avatāra → paripāka → vimukti**.
- Mokṣabhāgīya is based on hearing and reflection, not yet cultivation-born Prajñā.
- Its action is principally mental, though bodily and verbal acts can become liberation-conducive when governed by aspiration for liberation.

The preparatory block therefore ends with a complete causal theory of how a continuum becomes capable of supramundane Path Knowledge.
