# VAK 6.12 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ānāpānasmṛtiḥ prajñā pañcabhūr vāyurgocarā |
> kāmāśrayā na bāhyānāṃ ṣaḍvidhā gaṇanādibhiḥ || 6.12 ||

**Best translation:** Recollection of inhalation and exhalation is Prajñā; it belongs to five levels and has wind as its cognitive range. It has its support in the desire-domain, does not belong to outsiders, and is sixfold through counting and the other methods.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.12.md`](./VAK_6.12.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 339|07–340|11.

The Bhāṣya first defines inhalation and exhalation as winds entering and leaving the body, then states that recollection directed to these is ānāpānasmṛti. Crucially, Vasubandhu says: `saiva prajñāsvabhāvā` — its intrinsic nature is Prajñā. The designation `smṛti` is used because the practice operates by the force or support of recollection, as in the smṛtyupasthānas.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Name and intrinsic nature are distinct

The practice is called ānāpānasmṛti, but its svabhāva is Prajñā.

```text
name:
    smṛti

intrinsic nature:
    prajñā
```

This is not bare retention of a sensation. Recollection stabilizes the continuity of the object-support; Prajñā discriminates the process.

### 2. Five levels

Vasubandhu places the practice on five levels because it is associated with equanimity: the desire-domain, the three preliminary concentrations, and the intermediate concentration. He also notes an alternative view according to which, if equanimity is admitted in the principal dhyānas, the practice would extend to eight levels.

### 3. Object-support and support

Its object-support is vāyu, the breath as a wind-process. Its support is the desire-domain embodiment.

Vasubandhu also states that it arises among gods and humans, may be produced by practice or obtained through dispassion, and is a form of `tattva-manaskāra`.

### 4. Why outsiders do not possess it

The text states `na bāhyānām`: it does not belong to outsiders. The Bhāṣya gives two reasons:

```text
absence of instruction
    +
inability to awaken to subtle dharmas by themselves
```

The point is not mere sectarian possession but dependence upon a specific instruction capable of disclosing a subtle process.

### 5. The sixfold completion of the practice

Vasubandhu says the practice becomes complete when endowed with six causes or operations:

```text
1. gaṇanā       counting
2. anugama      following
3. sthāpanā     establishing
4. upalakṣaṇā   discerning
5. vivarta      turning
6. pariśuddhi   purification
```

These are not six unrelated exercises. They form a progressive transformation of the practice.

#### gaṇanā — counting

The practitioner places cognition on inhalation and exhalation and counts from one to ten through recollection alone, without forcing body or mind.

Three faults are distinguished:

```text
ūnagaṇanā
    under-counting

adhikagaṇanā
    over-counting

saṃkara
    confusing inhalation and exhalation
```

If distraction intervenes, counting begins again from the start until samādhi is obtained.

#### anugama — following

The practitioner follows the movement of inhalation and exhalation without artificial construction, examining how far they enter or leave and how their course proceeds.

The Bhāṣya reports expansive descriptions of the breath's travel, but Vasubandhu rejects overly literal cosmological extensions of this account as inconsistent with `tattva-manaskāra`.

#### sthāpanā — establishing

The breath is apprehended as stationed at a selected point, from the tip of the nose down to the toes, like a thread of jewels. The operation establishes a stable cognitive location.

#### upalakṣaṇā — discerning

Here the practice becomes explicitly analytical. The breaths are not apprehended merely as winds. The practitioner discerns:

```text
four great elements
    ↓
derivative material form
    ↓
cognition and mental factors dependent on that material field
    ↓
five aggregates
```

So the breath-process opens into discrimination of the whole aggregate-complex.

#### vivarta — turning

The practitioner turns away from mere growth of cognition centered on the wind-object and connects the practice with the higher wholesome roots, up through the Highest Mundane Dharmas.

The practice thus ceases to be merely a stabilizing exercise and is redirected into the preparatory Path.

#### pariśuddhi — purification

Vasubandhu defines purification as entry into the Path of Seeing and the subsequent Paths.

```text
breath recollection
    ↓
stabilization
    ↓
discrimination
    ↓
connection with higher wholesome roots
    ↓
entry into darśana-mārga and beyond
```

This is the decisive point of the verse: the six operations describe how an apparently elementary practice becomes continuous with the liberating Path.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — principal exposition and definitions.

**Voice: reported doctrinal position** — alternative enumeration of levels and some expansive descriptions of the course of the breath.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — rejection of an overextended breath-description as incompatible with `tattva-manaskāra`.

## New Technical Terms

- **tadvalādhāna-vṛtti** — functioning through the force/support of recollection
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** explains why a Prajñā-nature practice is named smṛti

- **tattva-manaskāra** — attention oriented according to actuality/reality
  - **status:** operative Bhāṣya term
  - **function:** doctrinal control on how the breath is apprehended

- **ūnagaṇanā** — under-counting
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical fault
  - **function:** error diagnosis

- **adhikagaṇanā** — over-counting
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical fault
  - **function:** error diagnosis

- **saṃkara** — confusion or mixing of inhalation and exhalation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical fault
  - **function:** error diagnosis

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The main correction to the Kārikā-only reading is that the sixfold sequence is not merely a graduated concentration technology. It culminates in the Path itself.

```text
gaṇanā
    order

anugama
    continuity

sthāpanā
    stability

upalakṣaṇā
    discrimination of dharmas

vivarta
    redirection into higher wholesome roots

pariśuddhi
    entry into Seeing and subsequent Paths
```

Thus ānāpānasmṛti is a model case of how Prajñā is generated from a disciplined object-support.

The progression is especially important for the chapter-wide question of Path-capacity:

```text
simple embodied process
    ↓
recollected process
    ↓
discriminated process
    ↓
aggregate-field
    ↓
preparatory wholesome roots
    ↓
darśana-mārga
```

The practice does not produce liberation merely because breath is special. Its efficacy lies in the ordered transformation of cognition through which a subtle conditioned process becomes the basis for discriminative knowing and finally for entry into the supramundane Path.

## Translation Consequence

The canonical line `ānāpānasmṛtiḥ prajñā` should be read strongly:

> Breath-recollection is Prajñā by intrinsic nature.

`smṛti` names the stabilizing mode by which it functions; `prajñā` names what the practice actually is.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya gives a precise genetic sequence from retention to discrimination and from discrimination to Path-entry. This is not yet an external philosophical mapping; it is Vasubandhu's own demonstration that disciplined cognition can transform a minimal empirical process into a liberating structure of knowing.
