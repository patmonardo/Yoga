# VAK 6.19 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**kāmāpta-duḥkha-viṣayā tv adhimātrā kṣaṇaṃ ca sā |**  
**tathāgradharmāḥ sarve tu pañcaskandhāḥ vināptibhiḥ || 6.19 ||**

**Working translation:** But the highest Acceptance has suffering belonging to the desire-domain as its object, and it lasts for one moment. The Highest Mundane Dharmas are likewise. All these, however, comprise the five aggregates, excluding acquisitions.

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
kāmāptaduḥkhaviṣayā tvadhimātrā

kṣāntiriti varttate /
apradharmasaṃśleṣādasau kāmāvacaraduḥkhālambanaiva /

ata evoṣmagatādīnāṃ traidhātukaduḥkhadyālambanatvasiddhirniyamāvacanāt /

yadā kila rūpārupyapratipakṣādyekaikasatyālambanāpahlāsena yāvat kāvāvacarameva dukhaṃ dvābhyāṃ kṣaṇābhyāṃ manasi karotyeṣā sarvaiva madhyā kṣāntiryadaikameva kṣaṇaṃ tadadhimātreti /

kṣaṇaṃ ca sā /

kṣaṇikā cāsau na prākarṣikī /

tathāgradharmāḥ

yathaivādhimātrā kṣāntiḥ /

te 'pi hi kāmāvacaraduḥkhālambanāḥ kṣaṇikāśca laukikāścaite 'grāśca dharmāḥ /

sarvalaukikaśreṣṭhatvāditi laukikāgradharmāḥ /

vinā sabhāgahetunā mārgasya tatpuruṣakāreṇākarṣaṇāt /

ta eta ūṣmagatādayaḥ smṛtyupasthānasvabhāvatvāt prajñātmakā ucyante /

sarve tu pañcaskandhāḥ

saparivāragrahaṇāt /

vināptibhiḥ

prāptayo noṣmagatādibhiḥ saṃgṛhyante /

mā bhūdāryasya tatsaṃmukhībhāvādūṣmagatādīnāṃ saṃmukhībhāva iti /
```

## Natural Segmentation

1. Highest Acceptance narrows its object to desire-domain suffering.
2. The broader Truth-field is progressively contracted until only this final object remains.
3. Highest Acceptance lasts for one moment only.
4. The Highest Mundane Dharmas have the same object and the same momentary duration.
5. They are called "highest" because they are supreme among all mundane dharmas.
6. They draw in the Path through their own causal efficacy, without functioning as a homogeneous cause of the Path.
7. Heat, Summits, Acceptance, and Highest Mundane Dharmas are intrinsically Prajñā because they are of the nature of smṛtyupasthāna.
8. When their entire associated complex is counted they comprise the five aggregates, excluding prāpti.

## Translation and Argument

### 1. Highest Acceptance contracts the field

The subject remains **adhimātrā kṣāntiḥ**, the strongest grade of Acceptance. Unlike the lower and middle grades, its object is now restricted to **kāmāvacara-duḥkha**, suffering belonging to the desire-domain.

The Bhāṣya describes this as the endpoint of a progressive elimination of the wider objects of the Four Truths. The practitioner ceases successively to take the higher-domain objects and other Truths as the present object-support until only desire-domain suffering remains.

Thus the movement is not from less knowledge to more knowledge. The Truth-structure has already been acquired. The field is now **contracted** toward the exact object with which the Path of Seeing will begin.

```text
Four Truths across three domains
    ↓
progressive contraction of object-range
    ↓
desire-domain suffering
```

### 2. One moment

The strongest Acceptance is **kṣaṇikā** — momentary.

Vasubandhu adds that it is not extended through a succession of increasingly intense moments. Its very definition is this single culminating moment.

This is therefore not merely "very strong Acceptance." It is a structurally terminal state within the Acceptance sequence.

```text
mṛdu-kṣānti
    ↓
madhya-kṣānti
    ↓
adhimātra-kṣānti
    one moment
```

### 3. Highest Mundane Dharmas

The **laukikāgradharmas** are said to be just like highest Acceptance in these respects:

- their object is desire-domain suffering;
- they last for one moment;
- they remain mundane.

Yet they are **agra**, highest or supreme, because they are the best of all mundane dharmas:

> `sarvalaukikaśreṣṭhatvāt`

They are therefore the terminal point of mundane cultivation.

```text
mundane cultivation
    ↓
its maximal possible determination
    ↓
laukikāgradharma
```

### 4. They draw in the Path

The most important causal statement is:

> `vinā sabhāgahetunā mārgasya tatpuruṣakāreṇākarṣaṇāt`

The Highest Mundane Dharmas draw in the Path through their own **puruṣakāra**, while not serving as a **sabhāgahetu**, a homogeneous cause, of that Path.

This is a highly precise threshold relation.

The supramundane Path does not arise because mundane cognition simply reproduces something of the same class. The preceding mundane state nevertheless has genuine causal efficacy: it **attracts / draws in** the Path.

```text
Highest Mundane Dharma
    not homogeneous with supramundane Path

but

Highest Mundane Dharma
    causally draws in Path
```

This gives an important answer to the developing śakti problem.

The transition to supramundane Seeing is neither:

- an arbitrary discontinuity with no preparation,
- nor a simple continuation of the same mundane cognition.

It is a **causally prepared discontinuity**.

### 5. Prajñā by intrinsic nature

Vasubandhu explicitly returns to the intrinsic nature of the entire sequence:

> `ta eta ūṣmagatādayaḥ smṛtyupasthānasvabhāvatvāt prajñātmakā ucyante`

Heat and the subsequent stages are called **Prajñā-natured** because they are of the nature of smṛtyupasthāna.

Thus:

```text
Heat
Summits
Acceptance
Highest Mundane Dharmas

svabhāva:
    Prajñā
```

This confirms that the preparatory penetration sequence is fundamentally a transformation of discriminative knowing.

### 6. Yet the complete event comprises five aggregates

The Kārikā then says:

> `sarve tu pañcaskandhāḥ`

Vasubandhu explains this by **saparivāragrahaṇa**: when the entire associated complex or entourage is included, all five aggregates are present.

So we must distinguish:

```text
intrinsic nature
    Prajñā

complete associated event
    five aggregates
```

This is another important protection against reducing the Path to an isolated mental factor. The discriminative principle is Prajñā, but the realized cognitive event involves an entire psycho-physical complex.

### 7. Why prāpti is excluded

The final phrase excludes **prāpti**, acquisition.

Vasubandhu's reason is technical: if the acquisitions of Heat and the other stages were themselves included in those stages, then when an ārya possessed such acquisitions it would follow that those preparatory mundane states themselves were presently manifest in the ārya.

The distinction prevents possession of a causal acquisition from being confused with present manifestation of the acquired state.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / Ābhidhārmika exposition, with no major explicit opponent in this unit.

The explanation is technical and classificatory, but the statement about the Highest Mundane Dharmas drawing in the Path is philosophically decisive.

## New Technical Terms

- **adhimātrā kṣānti** — strongest Acceptance; final one-moment grade of kṣānti.
  - **status:** developed from the preceding verse.
  - **function:** terminal Acceptance immediately before the Highest Mundane Dharmas.

- **laukikāgradharma** — Highest Mundane Dharma.
  - **status:** central new stage.
  - **function:** supreme mundane cognition immediately preceding the Path of Seeing.

- **sabhāgahetu** — homogeneous cause.
  - **status:** previously encountered causal category.
  - **function:** explicitly denied as the mode by which the Highest Mundane Dharmas produce the supramundane Path.

- **puruṣakāra** — causal efficacy / operative causal contribution.
  - **status:** technical causal vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains how the Highest Mundane Dharmas actively draw in the Path despite not being homogeneous with it.

- **ākarṣaṇa** — drawing in, attracting.
  - **status:** especially important in this passage.
  - **function:** names the causal relation from the highest mundane state to the supramundane Path.

- **saparivāragrahaṇa** — inclusion of the associated complex or entourage.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** explains why the full preparatory state comprises five aggregates although its intrinsic nature is Prajñā.

## Doctrinal / Logical Determination

The sequence now has an exact threshold architecture:

```text
Heat
    ↓
Summits
    ↓
Acceptance
    ↓
Highest Acceptance
    object-field maximally contracted
    one moment
    ↓
Highest Mundane Dharmas
    supreme mundane cognition
    one moment
    ↓
causal drawing-in of the Path
    ↓
Path of Seeing
```

The decisive relation is:

```text
continuity of preparation
    +
non-identity of causal type
        ↓
threshold transition
```

The mundane does not turn into the supramundane merely by quantitative increase. Yet the supramundane is not causally unprepared.

This is precisely the kind of distinction needed to explain **how Path-power can arise without treating liberation as either mechanical continuation or inexplicable miracle**.

## Relation to the śakti Question

VAK 6.01 said mundane cognition lacks the śakti required for the Path of Seeing.

VAK 6.19 now supplies a crucial intermediate determination:

```text
mundane cognition
    cannot itself perform supramundane abandonment

but

maximally cultivated mundane Prajñā
    can draw in the supramundane Path
```

So the developmental problem is no longer simply:

> Where does the supramundane power come from?

It has become:

> How does cultivated mundane Prajñā become causally adequate to condition the arising of a cognition whose efficacy is of a different order?

That is a much sharper form of the question.

## Light Organon Note

The passage distinguishes **content identity**, **causal continuity**, and **functional discontinuity**.

The same Truth-field is progressively stabilized and contracted, yet the final mundane cognition does not simply remain the same cognition at higher intensity. It becomes the immediate causal condition for a qualitatively different Path-function.

That is enough for now. The later Organon pass can ask whether this threshold should be modeled as a transition from conditioned determination to a new functional power rather than as the addition of new propositional content.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.19 brings mundane cultivation to its absolute limit.

- Highest Acceptance takes desire-domain suffering alone as object and lasts one moment.
- The Highest Mundane Dharmas are likewise momentary and take the same object.
- They are the supreme mundane dharmas.
- They do not produce the supramundane Path as a homogeneous cause.
- Nevertheless, through their own causal efficacy, they **draw in the Path**.
- The four preparatory stages are intrinsically Prajñā.
- Their full associated manifestation comprises the five aggregates, excluding prāpti.

The central result is:

> **The supramundane breakthrough is a causally prepared discontinuity: the highest possible mundane Prajñā does not itself possess the Path's power, but it becomes the immediate condition that draws that power into manifestation.**
