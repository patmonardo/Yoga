# VAK 6.20 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**iti nirvedhabhāgīyaṃ caturdhā bhāvanāmayam |**  
**anāgamyāntaradhyānabhūmikaṃ dve tv adho ’pi vā || 6.20 ||**

Thus the factors conducive to penetration are fourfold and cultivation-born. They belong to the levels of the preliminary concentration, the intermediate concentration, and the dhyānas; according to another position, the first two may also occur below.

## Bhāṣya Source

```text
ityetāni catvāri nirvedhabhāgīyāni kuśalamūlāni yadutoṣmagataṃ mūrdhānaḥ kṣāntayo 'gradharmāśca /

eṣāṃ dve mṛdūnī calatvāt parihāṇitaḥ kṣāntayo madhyamagradharmā adhimātram /

nirvedhabhāgīyānīti ko 'rthaḥ /
vidha vibhāge /

niścito vedhā nirvedhaḥ āryamārgastena vicikitsā prahāṇāt satyānāṃ ca vibhajanādidaṃ duḥkhamayaṃ yāvat mārga iti /

tasya bhāgo darśanamārgaikadeśaḥ /

tasyāvāhakatvena hitatvānnirvedhabhāgīyāni /

taccaitaccaturvidhamapi nirvedhabhāgīyam

bhāvanāmayam /

na śrutacintāmayam /

anāgamyāntaradhyānabhūmikam

anāgamyaṃ dhyānāntaraṃ catvāri ca dhyānānyasya bhūmistatsaṃgṛhītatvāt /

nordhva darśanamārgaparivāratvāt /
tadabhāvaḥ kāmadhātvālambanatvāt /

 dve tvadho 'pi vā // VAkK_6.20 //

bhadantaghoṣakasya tu dve prathame nirvedhabhāgīye saptabhūmike kāmāvacare api taḥ /
```

## Literal Prose Translation

These four wholesome roots are called factors conducive to penetration: Heat, the Summits, the Acceptances, and the Highest Mundane Dharmas.

Of these, the first two are weak because they are unstable and liable to decline; the Acceptances are medium, while the Highest Mundane Dharmas are strongest.

What does *nirvedhabhāgīya* mean? The root *vidh* has the sense of division or discrimination. A determinate penetration is *nirvedha*: this is the Noble Path, because by it doubt is abandoned and the Truths are discriminated—“this is suffering,” and so on through “this is Path.” A part of that penetration is a portion of the Path of Seeing. These four are called conducive to penetration because they are beneficial as what brings it on.

All four are cultivation-born, not hearing-born or reflection-born.

Their grounds are the preliminary concentration, the intermediate concentration, and the four dhyānas. They are not found above these because they form the retinue of the Path of Seeing, and such a Path is absent there insofar as it takes the desire-domain as object and that domain must first be fully known and abandoned.

Their maturation in the form-domain consists of the five aggregates. These states are completing rather than projecting karmic causes, because they are opposed to continued existence.

According to another opinion, the first two may also occur below. The word *vā* marks an alternative doctrine. According to Bhadanta Ghoṣaka, the first two factors conducive to penetration occur on seven grounds, including the desire-domain.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The Four Stages Are One Functional Class

Vasubandhu now formally closes the sequence:

```text
uṣmagata
    Heat

mūrdhan
    Summits

kṣānti
    Acceptance

laukikāgradharma
    Highest Mundane Dharmas
```

These are not four disconnected enumerated attainments. They are four grades of one function: **preparation for decisive penetration**.

### 2. The Difference Between Them Is Degree of Stability

The Bhāṣya ranks them:

```text
Heat + Summits
    weak
    unstable
    liable to decline

Acceptance
    medium

Highest Mundane Dharmas
    strongest
```

The development from 6.17–6.19 is therefore explicitly a strengthening of the same Truth-oriented cognitive root.

### 3. Nirvedha Means Determinate Penetration

Vasubandhu derives the term through the sense of **division/discrimination**.

*Nirvedha* is not mere intensity of experience. It is **determinate penetration by the Noble Path**.

Why?

Because it performs two operations:

```text
abandons doubt
    +
properly differentiates the Truths
```

The practitioner can determine:

```text
this is suffering
this is origin
this is cessation
this is Path
```

So penetration is intrinsically discriminative.

### 4. Why the Preparatory Stages Are Called Nirvedhabhāgīya

They are not themselves yet the full Noble Path.

They are called *nirvedhabhāgīya* because they are:

```text
āvāhaka
    what brings on / draws in penetration

hita
    beneficial or conducive to it
```

This confirms the causal threshold already exposed in 6.19.

The preparatory root does not become supramundane merely by quantitative increase. It becomes causally adequate to bring about the supramundane event.

### 5. They Are Bhāvanāmayī Prajñā

Vasubandhu states categorically:

```text
bhāvanāmayam
na śrutacintāmayam
```

They are cultivation-born, not hearing-born or reflection-born.

This completes the genetic structure introduced in 6.05:

```text
śruta
    truth received

cintā
    truth rationally determined

bhāvanā
    truth made operative as cognitive power
```

The nirvedhabhāgīyas belong only to the third.

### 6. Their Meditative Grounds

The principal account places them on six grounds:

```text
anāgamya
    preliminary concentration

dhyānāntara
    intermediate concentration

four dhyānas
```

They are not assigned above these because they are the immediate entourage of the Path of Seeing and retain an operative relation to desire-domain suffering, which must be known and abandoned first.

### 7. They Do Not Project Further Existence

A subtle but important line says their karmic maturation is **pūripūraka**, completing, rather than **ākṣepaka**, projecting.

Why?

Because they are marked by **bhava-dveṣitva**, opposition to continued existence.

So these roots may still be mundane, but their causal orientation has already reversed:

```text
ordinary wholesome karma
    may support continued becoming

nirvedhabhāgīya roots
    complete the existing continuum
    but do not function to project new becoming
```

This is a major practical determination.

## Voice

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / Ābhidhārmika exposition** — main definition and classification.
- **Voice: Bhadanta Ghoṣaka** — alternative view allowing Heat and Summits also in the desire-domain.

## New Technical Terms

- **nirvedha** — determinate penetration; the Noble Path insofar as it abandons doubt and discriminates the Four Truths.
  - **status:** central new technical definition in this block.
  - **function:** defines the breakthrough for which the four preparatory roots are causes.

- **nirvedhabhāgīya** — conducive to, participating in, or bringing on penetration.
  - **status:** already named in the Kārikā, fully defined here.
  - **function:** functional class containing Heat, Summits, Acceptance, and Highest Mundane Dharmas.

- **āvāhaka** — bringing on, drawing in, ushering in.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term.
  - **function:** explains the causal relation between mundane preparation and supramundane penetration.

- **pūripūraka** — completing.
  - **status:** technical karmic description.
  - **function:** says these roots complete the existing maturation rather than project a new existence.

- **ākṣepaka** — projecting.
  - **status:** technical karmic contrast.
  - **function:** type of karma these roots do not perform.

- **bhava-dveṣitva** — opposition or aversion to continued becoming.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya doctrinal explanation.
  - **function:** explains why the nirvedhabhāgīyas do not project renewed existence.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The whole block VAK 6.16–6.20 can now be stated as a genetic architecture:

```text
synthetic Dharma-field
    ↓
Heat
    first sign
    ↓
Summits
    intensified but unstable
    ↓
Acceptance
    increasingly stable Truth-receptivity
    ↓
Highest Mundane Dharmas
    maximal mundane Prajñā
    ↓
āvāhana / ākarṣaṇa
    drawing in penetration
    ↓
nirvedha
    Noble Path
    doubt abandoned
    Truths decisively discriminated
```

The transition is therefore neither mere accumulation nor an arbitrary rupture.

It is a **prepared discontinuity**:

- continuity of object-field and cultivated Prajñā;
- discontinuity of path-status and liberating efficacy.

## Translation Consequence

*Nirvedha* should not be flattened into generic “insight.” In this passage it carries at least three determinations:

1. decisive penetration,
2. abandonment of doubt,
3. discriminative partition of the Four Truths.

Likewise *bhāgīya* should preserve causal orientation: these roots are **conducive to / bringing on penetration**, not merely thematically related to it.

## Light Organon Note

What looks taxonomic is actually a theory of **threshold formation**:

```text
truth as content
    → truth as cultivated determination
    → truth as stable cognitive root
    → truth as causal power
    → supramundane penetration
```

The Bhāṣya therefore provides a precise account of how knowledge can become practically efficacious without simply identifying mundane cognition with liberation itself.
