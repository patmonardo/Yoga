# VAK 6.08 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> karmāntena tribhir vṛttis tṛṣṇotpāda-vipakṣataḥ |
> mamāhaṃkāra-vastv-icchā tat-kālātyanta-śāntaye || 6.8 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Livelihood is maintained by the three and the work by the fourth, because they oppose the arising of thirst. Desire regarding the objects of “mine” and “I-making” is thereby pacified respectively for the present and ultimately.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.08.md`](./VAK_6.08.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Primary passage: Pradhan [336|11–337|06].

The natural argument begins with the question why the fourth noble lineage, delight in abandonment and cultivation, can still have **alobha** as its nature. Vasubandhu answers that it is opposed to desire for existence and sensual desire. He then asks what the Buddha intended to show through the four noble lineages.

Key source movement:

```text
prahāṇa-bhāvanā-rāmatā
    ↓
vaimukhya toward bhava-kāma-rāga
    ↓
four āryavaṃśas
    ↓
vṛtti + karma
    ↓
pratipakṣa to four arisings of tṛṣṇā
    ↓
temporary pacification of mine-making desire
    ↓
final pacification of both mine-making and I-making desire
```

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

How is the fourth noble lineage, delight in abandonment and cultivation, of the intrinsic nature of non-greed? Because it turns away from desire for existence and sensual desire.

What, then, did the Blessed One show by the four noble lineages?

> **karmāntena tribhir vṛttiḥ** — “Livelihood is by the three; work is by the [fourth].”

The Blessed One, lord of Dharma, prescribed two things for disciples who had abandoned their former means of livelihood and occupations and had undertaken the path for the sake of liberation: **livelihood and work**.

Livelihood is through the three noble lineages; work is through the fourth.

The meaning is: maintaining yourselves by this livelihood and doing this work, you will before long attain liberation.

Why was precisely such a livelihood and such a work prescribed?

> **tṛṣṇotpāda-vipakṣataḥ** — because they are opponents of the arising of thirst.

Four arisings of thirst are stated in the Sūtra: thirst arising on account of robes, alms-food, lodging, and existence/non-existence. The four noble lineages were taught as their respective counterforces.

The same meaning is then stated by remainder:

> **mamāhaṃkāra-vastv-icchā tat-kālātyanta-śāntaye**

The objects of mine-making are robes and the like; the object of I-making is one's own existence or personal continuum. Desire toward these is thirst. The first three noble lineages are for the present pacification of desire toward the objects of mine-making; the fourth is for the final pacification of both kinds of desire.

The Bhāṣya then closes this unit by saying that it has now explained how cultivation becomes successful for such a practitioner and turns next to the question of entry into cultivation.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 6.07 established that the first three noble lineages are forms of contentment and that the fourth is delight in abandonment and cultivation. VAK 6.08 explains their systematic unity.

The Buddha prescribes a complete practical economy:

```text
three noble lineages
    ↓
vṛtti
    livelihood / maintenance

fourth noble lineage
    ↓
karma
    the work to be done
```

This distinction is decisive. The renunciant does not merely reduce possessions. Life is maintained in a restricted form **so that a determinate work can be performed**.

```text
minimal maintenance
    is not the end

minimal maintenance
    enables
prahāṇa + bhāvanā
```

The four noble lineages are then explained as **pratipakṣas** to four productions of thirst:

```text
robes        ↔ contentment with robes
food         ↔ contentment with food
lodging      ↔ contentment with lodging
bhava/vibhava ↔ delight in abandonment and cultivation
```

The fourth member therefore moves beyond regulation of possession. It confronts craving directed toward the very structure of continued existence and its negation.

## Mine-Making and I-Making

The most important distinction in the Bhāṣya is:

```text
mamakāra-vastu
    robes, food, lodging, possessions

ahaṃkāra-vastu
    ātmabhāva
    one's own existence / embodied personal continuum
```

The first three noble lineages restrain desire toward the first field. Their pacification is **tat-kāla**, for the present.

The fourth noble lineage addresses both fields and aims at **atyanta-śānti**, final pacification.

Thus the progression is:

```text
appropriation of things
    ↓
regulated now

appropriation of self-existence
    ↓
requires prahāṇa-bhāvanā
    ↓
final pacification
```

This is more precise than treating renunciation as a single undifferentiated rejection of possessions.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the fourth lineage as alobha, the vṛtti/karma distinction, and the final mamakāra/ahaṃkāra interpretation.
- **Voice: Sūtra citation** — four arisings of thirst associated with robes, alms-food, lodging, and bhava/vibhava.
- No major unresolved doctrinal objection occurs in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **dharmasvāmin** — “lord/master of Dharma,” epithet of the Buddha in this passage
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only expression here
  - **function:** establishes the Buddha as prescriber of the practical economy of the path

- **vṛtti** — livelihood, maintenance, mode by which life is sustained
  - **status:** made technically operative by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** distinguishes material maintenance from the actual work of liberation

- **karmānta / karma** — occupation, work to be done
  - **status:** Kārikā term expanded by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** identifies prahāṇa-bhāvanā as the practitioner's proper work

- **tṛṣṇotpāda** — arising or production of thirst
  - **status:** Kārikā compound expanded through Sūtra citation
  - **function:** provides the fourfold field to which the āryavaṃśas function as counterforces

- **mamakāra-vastu** — object of “mine-making”
  - **status:** made explicit in prose
  - **function:** identifies robes, food, lodging, and similar supports as objects of appropriation

- **ahaṃkāra-vastu** — object of “I-making”
  - **status:** made explicit in prose
  - **function:** identified with **ātmabhāva**, one's own existence/personal continuum

- **tat-kāla-śānti** — pacification for the present
  - **status:** prose clarification of the Kārikā
  - **function:** marks the limited practical effect of the first three noble lineages

- **atyanta-śānti** — final/ultimate pacification
  - **status:** prose clarification of the Kārikā
  - **function:** marks the aim of the fourth noble lineage

## Doctrinal Determination

The four āryavaṃśas form a complete practical structure:

```text
conditions of subsistence
    first three

purpose of subsistence
    fourth
```

The first three do not themselves finish the problem of appropriation. They stabilize the practitioner's relation to material necessity. The fourth turns that stabilized life toward the actual work of liberation.

The deeper movement is therefore:

```text
mamakāra
    appropriation of supports
        ↓
contentment

ahaṃkāra
    appropriation of own existence
        ↓
prahāṇa + bhāvanā
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly reserves **final pacification** for the fourth.

## Logical Determination

The fourfold noble lineage is organized as a **three-plus-one** structure:

```text
Three
    establish a bounded economy of maintenance

One
    gives that economy its liberating end
```

The relation is not merely additive. The fourth determines the meaning of the first three:

```text
vṛtti
    is maintained
for
karma

maintenance
    is subordinated to
liberating work
```

The passage also deepens **pratipakṣa**. A counterforce is here a positive practice precisely specified against a determinate production of thirst.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā's **karmānta** should not be flattened into generic “activity.” The Bhāṣya explicitly contrasts it with **vṛtti** and identifies it as the work assigned to one who has entered the path for liberation.

Likewise, **mama-ahaṃkāra-vastu-icchā** should preserve the distinction between:

```text
desire for objects constituted as “mine”
    and
desire centered on one's own existence as “I”
```

The Bhāṣya's identification of **ahaṃkāra-vastu** with **ātmabhāva** is especially important and should govern later interpretation.

## Light Organon Contact Point

Without importing an external doctrine, the internal structure can be stated simply:

```text
Path-life
    = maintenance + task

maintenance
    is finitized by contentment

task
    transforms the deeper relation to existence
```

This gives a more concrete sense to the developing doctrine of Path-capacity: the practitioner is not merely deprived of ordinary aims; the whole life-process is reorganized so that its maintenance serves a single determinate work.

## Determinate Summary

```text
VAK 6.08 Bhāṣya

first three āryavaṃśas
    = vṛtti
    bounded livelihood
    present pacification of desire for “mine”

fourth āryavaṃśa
    = karma
    abandonment + cultivation
    final pacification of desire for both “mine” and “I”

four noble lineages
    = four pratipakṣas
      to four productions of tṛṣṇā
```

The central result is that renunciant simplicity is not liberation itself. It establishes a bounded mode of maintenance whose purpose is to make the **work of abandonment and cultivation** possible.
