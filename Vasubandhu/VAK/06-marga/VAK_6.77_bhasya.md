# VAK 6.77 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> **vimucyate jāyamānam aśaikṣaṃ cittam āvṛteḥ /**  
> **nirudhyamāno mārgas tu prajahāti tad-āvṛtim // VAK_6.77 //**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 388|21–389|10.

The natural Bhāṣya unit begins with the śāstra citation explaining the first half of the verse and concludes with the explanation of `nirudhyamāna` as `vartamāna`.

## Literal Prose Translation

The treatise reading is:

> “The future Aśaikṣa mind, while arising, is released from the obstructions.”

What, then, is its obstruction? The acquisition of the afflictions, because it obstructs the arising of that mind.

In the vajropama-samādhi that acquisition is abandoned. Therefore, while the Aśaikṣa mind is arising, it is released; the acquisition of the afflictions has been abandoned. Once the Aśaikṣa mind has arisen, it is already liberated.

But what about a mind that is not presently arising, and what about a mundane mind? These too are released. Why is the mundane mind released? From that very obstruction to arising.

One might object: does not a mundane mind arise even for a Śaikṣa who has not yet been liberated? Not a mundane mind of this kind. What kind is that? One accompanied by the acquisition of the afflictions.

In what state does the Path abandon the obstruction to the arising of that Aśaikṣa mind?

> **nirudhyamāno mārgas tu prajahāti tad-āvṛtim**

The meaning is: while present.

## Course of Argument

The Bhāṣya separates three things that could easily be conflated:

```text
kleśa-prāpti
    acquisition of the afflictions
    functions as obstruction to arising

vajropama-samādhi / Path
    abandons that obstruction while present

Aśaikṣa-citta
    arises released from that obstruction
```

The key term is not simply `kleśa` but **`kleśa-prāpti`**. The obstruction is the acquisition or possession of the afflictions insofar as it prevents the arising of the Aśaikṣa mind.

Thus the transition is:

```text
Path present
    ↓
abandons kleśa-prāpti
    ↓
obstruction to arising removed
    ↓
Aśaikṣa mind arises liberated
```

The Bhāṣya also adds that future mundane minds can be called released from the same obstruction. The verse singles out the arising Aśaikṣa mind because its arising is determinate immediately after the vajropama-samādhi.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose and reconstruction of the śāstra statement.

**Voice: Objector** — questions concerning the mundane mind and the apparent fact that mundane minds can arise in a Śaikṣa.

## New Technical Terms

- **utpatti-āvaraṇa** — obstruction to arising
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory term in this unit
  - **function:** identifies precisely what is removed so that the Aśaikṣa mind can arise

- **kleśa-prāpti** — acquisition/possession of the afflictions
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal machinery
  - **function:** here identified as the obstruction to the arising of the Aśaikṣa mind

- **vajropama-samādhi** — diamond-like samādhi
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** final Path-moment in which the obstructing acquisition is abandoned

## Doctrinal Determination

The Kārikā is exact in distinguishing **prahāṇa** from **vimukti**.

```text
mārga
    prajahāti
    abandons the obstruction

Aśaikṣa-citta
    vimucyate
    is released from the obstruction
```

The Path is the operative abandoning condition. The Aśaikṣa mind is the newly arising actuality in which the obstruction is absent.

The Bhāṣya therefore does not posit a persistent subject that first performs abandonment and later enjoys liberation. It gives a momentary functional succession:

```text
present Path-moment
    performs abandonment

subsequent Aśaikṣa mind
    arises unobstructed
```

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

`nirudhyamāna` should not be rendered in a way that suggests the Path acts only after it has ceased. Vasubandhu glosses it explicitly as:

> **vartamāna** — “while present.”

The sense is that the Path, in the very moment that is coming to cessation, is still presently operative and abandons the obstruction.

A precise translation is therefore:

> **The Aśaikṣa mind, while arising, is released from the obstruction; but the Path, while present in its cessation, abandons that obstruction.**

## Light Organon Contact

For the first pass, the useful distinction is simply:

```text
condition-removal
    ≠
resulting liberated actuality
```

The Path does not merely state freedom; it performs the determinate removal of the obstruction whose absence makes the liberated state possible.

This preserves the scientific structure of the Kośa: operation, condition, temporal position, and resulting state are separately determined.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.77 explains the exact transition into Aśaikṣa status. The acquisition of the afflictions functions as an obstruction to the arising of the Aśaikṣa mind. In the vajropama-samādhi the Path, while presently operative, abandons this obstruction. The Aśaikṣa mind then arises released from it. The Bhāṣya further notes that other future minds may also be released from this obstruction, but the verse names the immediately determined Aśaikṣa mind.
