# VAK 6.44 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**ābhavāgrāṣṭabhāgakṣidarhattve pratipannakaḥ /**  
**navamasyāpyānantaryapathe vajropamaśca saḥ // VAkK_6.44 //**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 364|10–365|10.

## 1. From the Non-Returner to the Candidate for Arhatship

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The subject remains the **anāgāmin**.

Vasubandhu states:

> `sa khalvayamanāgāmī prathamadhyānaikaprakāravairāgyāt prabṛti yāvat bhavāgrāṣṭaprakāraprahāṇādarhattvapratipannako bhavanti`

From the abandonment of one grade belonging to the first dhyāna onward, up through the abandonment of eight grades belonging to the summit of existence, the non-returner is a **practitioner entered upon arhatship** (*arhattva-pratipannaka*).

The point is therefore broader than the bare kārikā might first suggest. The candidate-status is not produced only at the very last eight-grade threshold. Rather, once the practitioner has crossed beyond the desire-realm work and is proceeding through the remaining higher-realm cultivation-abandonables, the trajectory is already specifically directed toward arhatship.

```text
anāgāmin
    ↓
first higher-realm cultivation-grade abandoned
    ↓
...
    ↓
eight grades at bhavāgra abandoned
    ↓
throughout this span:
    arhattva-pratipannaka
```

## 2. The Final Ninth Grade at Bhavāgra

The kārikā continues:

> `navamasyāpyānantaryapathe`

Vasubandhu explains that even on the uninterrupted path directed toward abandoning the **ninth bhavāgra grade**, the practitioner remains an `arhattva-pratipannaka`.

This is the final abandonment-operation before the arhat fruit itself.

```text
bhavāgra grades 1–8 abandoned
    ↓
ninth grade remains
    ↓
final ānantarya-mārga
    ↓
still candidate for arhatship
```

The actual fruit-position comes only with the immediately following liberation-path, explained in VAK 6.45.

## 3. Vajropama-Samādhi

The kārikā names this final uninterrupted path:

> `vajropamaśca saḥ`

The Bhāṣya states:

> `sa cānantaryamārgo vajropamaḥ samādhirityucyeta`

That uninterrupted path is called **vajropama-samādhi**, diamond-like concentration.

The reason is:

> `sarvānuśayabheditvāt`

because it is capable of breaking all latent afflictive tendencies (*anuśaya*).

Vasubandhu immediately qualifies the wording. The path does not, at this moment, literally break every anuśaya at once, because many have already been broken. Rather:

> `sarvāstu bhettuṃ samarthaḥ`

it is **capable of breaking them all**.

This capacity follows because it is:

> `sarvānantaryamārgāṇāmadhimātratamatvāt`

—the most intense / strongest of all uninterrupted paths.

Thus `vajropama` names maximal counteractive capacity, not merely temporal finality.

```text
all prior ānantarya-mārgas
    <
final bhavāgra ānantarya-mārga

because:
    strongest counteractive path
    capable of breaking every anuśaya

therefore:
    vajropama-samādhi
```

## 4. A Crucial Distinction: Actual Destruction vs Capacity for Destruction

The Bhāṣya carefully distinguishes:

```text
what this path actually destroys now
    ≠
what this path is intrinsically capable of destroying
```

At this final moment, only the last remaining grade is the immediate target. Yet the path is called diamond-like because its force is universally adequate to the whole afflictive field.

This distinction prevents a crude reading in which all afflictions are imagined as simultaneously present and shattered in one final event.

The continuum has already undergone a long graded purification. The final path is supreme because of its **power**, not because all earlier work is somehow repeated.

## 5. The Multiplicity of Vajropama Paths

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika scholastic analysis, reported by Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu then records a detailed Abhidharma enumeration of multiple forms of vajropama-samādhi.

The variants are distinguished according to:

- object-support (*ālambana*),
- knowledge-mode (*jñāna-ākāra*),
- meditative level (*bhūmi* / dhyāna / ārūpya),
- lineage (*gotra*),
- faculty (*indriya*).

For anāgamya-associated vajropama paths, the scholastic count given first is **52**, differentiated by knowledge-form and object-support.

The same pattern extends through the fourth dhyāna. In the formless attainments the count decreases according to which Dharma-knowledge and lower-level cessation objects are unavailable there.

A further doctrinal option allows path-conformity knowledge to take the counteractive path of each individual level as object; on that interpretation the counts increase—for example to **80** for anāgamya-associated vajropama paths.

Vasubandhu then notes that further multiplication results from differences of lineage and faculty.

The doctrinal point is not merely arithmetic. `vajropama-samādhi` is not one psychologically featureless final state. It is a functional class whose members can differ by cognitive object, knowledge-aspect, meditative support, lineage, and faculty while sharing the same final counteractive function.

## 6. Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.44 completes the logic of the ninefold abandonment model introduced in VAK 6.33.

The final segment now reads:

```text
higher-realm residual afflictions
    ↓
graded abandonment through cultivation
    ↓
progressive arhatship-candidacy
    ↓
bhavāgra grade 9 remains
    ↓
final ānantarya-mārga
    ↓
vajropama-samādhi
    ↓
capacity adequate to every anuśaya
```

The decisive point is that **candidate-status and fruit-status remain distinct right to the end**.

The final uninterrupted path destroys the last obstruction. It does not yet itself constitute the arhat fruit. The immediately subsequent liberation-path does that.

Thus the same transition-rule found earlier remains operative:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    removes obstruction

vimukti-mārga
    establishes freedom from it
```

VAK 6.44 is the maximal instance of that rule.

## 7. New Technical Terms

- **arhattva-pratipannaka** — practitioner entered upon / progressing toward arhatship.
  - **status:** previously encountered structurally through other fruit-candidates; specific arhatship designation foregrounded here.
  - **function:** path-person classification for the final cultivation trajectory.

- **vajropama-samādhi** — diamond-like concentration; the final uninterrupted path against the last cultivation-abandonable grade.
  - **status:** new technical designation in this passage.
  - **function:** names the strongest uninterrupted path, capable of breaking every anuśaya.

- **sarvānuśayabheditva** — capacity to break all latent afflictive tendencies.
  - **status:** new explanatory formulation.
  - **function:** gives the reason for the name `vajropama`.

- **adhimātratama** — strongest / most intense.
  - **status:** related graded terminology previously encountered; superlative force is central here.
  - **function:** ranks the final uninterrupted path above all earlier uninterrupted paths.

## 8. Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu for the basic explanation of arhatship-candidacy and vajropama-samādhi.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika scholastic analysis, reported by Vasubandhu, for the extensive numerical differentiation of vajropama paths by object, knowledge-mode, level, lineage, and faculty.

The arithmetic should therefore not be conflated with the minimal doctrinal core of the verse.

The central determination is stable:

```text
final ninth bhavāgra grade
    → final uninterrupted path
    → vajropama-samādhi
```

while the exact enumeration of its many variants belongs to the finer Abhidharma systematics.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.44 places the non-returner on the final trajectory toward arhatship. From the first higher-realm cultivation-abandonment onward, and up through the destruction of eight grades at the summit of existence, the practitioner is an **arhattva-pratipannaka**.

When only the ninth bhavāgra grade remains, the uninterrupted path directed against it is called **vajropama-samādhi**. It is diamond-like because it is the strongest of all uninterrupted paths and is capable of breaking every latent affliction, although at this final stage it actually destroys only the last remaining one.

The Bhāṣya therefore preserves the same fundamental structure seen throughout the chapter:

```text
remaining obstruction
    ↓
precisely matched uninterrupted path
    ↓
obstruction destroyed
    ↓
next liberation-path establishes the fruit
```

At VAK 6.44 that structure has reached its maximum intensity. The next moment will be the final liberation-path and the arising of `kṣayajñāna`.
