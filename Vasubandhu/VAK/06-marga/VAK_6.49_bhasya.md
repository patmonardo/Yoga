# VAK 6.49 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**vimuktyānantaryapathā laukikās tu yathākramam /**  
**śāntādyudārādyākārā uttarādharagocarāḥ // VAkK_6.49 //**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 368|08–368|17.

This verse contrasts the structure of **mundane** (`laukika`) liberation and uninterrupted paths with the supramundane paths discussed at the end of VAK 6.48.

## 1. Mundane Paths Are Not Satya-Ākāra Paths

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

VAK 6.48 concluded that supramundane uninterrupted and liberation paths operate in truth-forms because the truths are their object-supports.

VAK 6.49 now turns to the mundane case:

> `vimuktyānantaryapathā laukikāstu yathākramam`

The mundane liberation-paths and uninterrupted paths are differentiated by their respective cognitive forms.

Vasubandhu states:

> `vimuktimārgāḥ śāntādyākārā ānantaryamārgā audārikādyākārāḥ`

Thus:

```text
mundane vimukti-mārga
    → śānta-ādi ākāras

mundane ānantarya-mārga
    → audārika-ādi ākāras
```

The distinction is therefore cognitive and evaluative, not merely temporal.

## 2. The Liberation-Path Takes the Higher Ground

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `vimuktimārgā uttarāṃ bhūmiṃ śāntataḥ praṇītato niḥsaraṇataścākārayanti saṃbhavataḥ`

The liberation-path determines the **higher ground** under the aspects:

- `śānta` — peaceful / calm,
- `praṇīta` — excellent / superior,
- `niḥsaraṇa` — escape / exit.

The exact applicability is `saṃbhavataḥ` — as appropriate to the case.

The higher ground is therefore not apprehended neutrally. It is positively determined as the relatively preferable field toward which mundane dispassion moves.

```text
higher ground
    ↓
śānta
praṇīta
niḥsaraṇa
    ↓
vimukti-mārga
```

## 3. The Uninterrupted Path Takes the Lower Ground

The corresponding mundane uninterrupted path takes the **lower ground**:

> `ānantaryamārgā adharāṃ bhūmim audārikato duḥkhilataḥ sthūlabhittikataśca`

under three negative aspects:

- `audārika` — coarse,
- `duḥkhila` — painful / defective,
- `sthūlabhittika` — like a thick wall / gross barrier.

Thus:

```text
lower ground
    ↓
audārika
duḥkhila
sthūlabhittika
    ↓
ānantarya-mārga
```

The path of abandonment works through a negative determination of the lower field, while the liberation-path positively determines the higher field.

## 4. Why the Lower Ground Is Audārika

Vasubandhu explains:

> `aśāntatvād audārikato mahābhisaṃskārataratvāt`

The lower ground is regarded as coarse because it is **not peaceful** and because it requires / involves greater formative activity (`mahābhisaṃskārataratva`).

This gives `audārika` a functional meaning. The lower ground is not merely physically or psychologically “gross.” It is comparatively less settled and more heavily constructed.

```text
greater abhisaṃskāra
    +
less śānti
    ↓
audārika
```

## 5. Why the Lower Ground Is Duḥkhila

Vasubandhu continues:

> `apraṇītatvāt duḥkhilato bahudauṣṭhulyataratvena pratikūlabhāvāt`

It is regarded as `duḥkhila` because it is **not excellent** (`apraṇīta`) and is opposed / disagreeable due to its greater `dauṣṭhulya`—heaviness, rigidity, or maladaptive coarseness.

The negative judgment is therefore comparative:

```text
lower ground
    = more dauṣṭhulya
    = less praṇīta
    = pratikūla
```

`duḥkhila` here is not simply a generic declaration that conditioned existence is suffering. It is the specific evaluative form by which one lower meditative level is rejected in favor of a higher one.

## 6. Why the Lower Ground Is Like a Thick Wall

The third negative aspect is:

> `sthūlabhittikataḥ`

Vasubandhu explains it through the failure of the lower ground itself to provide escape from its own level:

> `tayaiva tadbhūmyaniḥsaraṇāt bhittyaniḥsaraṇavat`

The image is of a wall that does not itself provide the exit through it.

The lower ground is thus determined as a **barrier** relative to the transition sought.

```text
lower ground
    cannot itself be its own exit
    ↓
sthūlabhittika
    thick-wall aspect
```

This is closely related to the counteraction principle already encountered in VAK 6.45: a field in which the relevant afflictive structure remains operative cannot simply function as its own adequate liberation.

## 7. The Positive Aspects Are the Contraries

Vasubandhu concludes:

> `eṣāṃ viparyayeṇa śāntapraṇītaniḥsaraṇākārāḥ`

The positive aspects are obtained by reversal of these negative determinations:

```text
audārika
    ↔ śānta

duḥkhila
    ↔ praṇīta

sthūlabhittika
    ↔ niḥsaraṇa
```

This is the heart of mundane dispassion.

The practitioner comparatively determines:

```text
lower
    coarse
    defective
    obstructive

higher
    peaceful
    excellent
    escape
```

The movement remains entirely within the conditioned hierarchy.

## 8. Path-Knowledge Determination

The distinction from the supramundane path is now precise.

### Supramundane path

From VAK 6.48:

```text
satya as object-support
    ↓
satya-ākāra path
```

### Mundane path

From VAK 6.49:

```text
lower conditioned ground
    negatively evaluated

higher conditioned ground
    positively evaluated

comparative determination
    ↓
vairāgya
```

Mundane dispassion therefore does not negate conditioned existence as such. It produces detachment from one conditioned level by judging another conditioned level superior.

## 9. A Strong Practical-Scientific Distinction

This verse gives us a useful distinction between two modes of practical transformation:

```text
relative counteraction
    lower conditioned level
        → higher conditioned level

absolute path-counteraction
    conditioned field
        → satya-determined anāsrava path
```

The mundane path is perfectly real and effective within its range. But its structure is comparative and hierarchical.

It says, in effect:

```text
this level is coarse, defective, obstructive
that level is peaceful, superior, an escape
```

The supramundane path has a different cognitive ground altogether: the truths themselves.

## 10. Organon Contact

The strongest restrained Organon contact is that VAK 6.49 exhibits a **relational judgment of grounds**.

The lower ground is not rejected in isolation. It is negatively determined only in relation to a higher ground, and the higher ground is positively determined only relative to the lower.

```text
lower as inadequate
    ↔
higher as relative resolution
```

The two determinations therefore reciprocally articulate the mundane path-field.

But the result remains relative:

```text
higher conditioned ground
    ≠
unconditioned liberation
```

This is why the distinction introduced in VAK 6.48 matters so much. The supramundane path is not simply the same comparative movement carried farther upward. Its principle is different because its object-support and cognitive forms are truths.

## 11. New Technical Terms

- **śānta-ākāra** — aspect of peacefulness / calm.
  - **status:** explicitly specified in this Bhāṣya passage.
  - **function:** positive determination of the higher ground in mundane liberation-paths.

- **praṇīta-ākāra** — aspect of excellence / superiority.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya expansion.
  - **function:** positive determination opposed to the lower ground's defectiveness.

- **niḥsaraṇa-ākāra** — aspect of escape / exit.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya expansion.
  - **function:** determines the higher ground as a way out relative to the lower.

- **audārika-ākāra** — aspect of coarseness.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya expansion.
  - **function:** negative determination of the lower ground as less peaceful and more heavily constructed.

- **duḥkhila-ākāra** — aspect of painfulness / defectiveness.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya expansion.
  - **function:** negative determination of the lower ground as less excellent and marked by greater dauṣṭhulya.

- **sthūlabhittika-ākāra** — aspect of being like a thick wall or gross barrier.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya expansion.
  - **function:** determines the lower ground as unable to furnish its own exit.

- **dauṣṭhulya** — heaviness, rigidity, maladaptive coarseness.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term.
  - **function:** helps explain why the lower ground is judged `duḥkhila`.

- **abhisaṃskāra** — formative / constructing activity.
  - **status:** previously encountered generally; comparative force foregrounded here.
  - **function:** greater formative activity helps explain the coarseness of the lower ground.

## 12. Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the explanation.

There is no major school dispute in this short passage. The central task is analytic clarification of the mundane path's six evaluative aspects and their respective object-fields.

The inherited translation “coarse / peaceful” can easily sound merely psychological. The Bhāṣya shows instead that these are **structured comparative determinations of meditative grounds**.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.49 distinguishes mundane liberation-paths from mundane uninterrupted paths by their object-fields and cognitive aspects. The uninterrupted path takes the **lower ground** under the negative aspects `audārika`, `duḥkhila`, and `sthūlabhittika`: coarse because it is less peaceful and more heavily constructed, defective because it is less excellent and marked by greater rigidity, and wall-like because it cannot itself provide the exit from its own level.

The liberation-path takes the **higher ground** under the contrary positive aspects `śānta`, `praṇīta`, and `niḥsaraṇa`: peaceful, superior, and an escape.

The mundane path is therefore a comparative practical judgment within conditioned existence:

```text
lower ground
    coarse
    defective
    obstructive
        ↓
        vairāgya
        ↓
higher ground
    peaceful
    superior
    escape
```

This differs structurally from the supramundane path of VAK 6.48, whose object-supports are the truths themselves and whose operation therefore cannot be reduced to comparative ascent within the conditioned hierarchy.
