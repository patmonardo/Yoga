# VAK 6.26 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**kāmaduḥkhe tato 'traiva dharmajñānaṃ tathā punaḥ |**  
**śeṣe duḥkhe 'nvayakṣāntijñāne satyatraye tathā || 6.26 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 350|12–351|06.

VAK 6.25 introduced the first uncontaminated moment, **duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti**, whose object is suffering in the desire realm. VAK 6.26 continues immediately from that moment and lays out the remaining members of the fourfold pattern, then repeats that pattern for the other three truths.

## 1. Dharma-Knowledge Follows Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance

Vasubandhu begins:

> **tataḥ punar duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānter anantaram atraiva kāmāvacare duḥkhe dharmajñānam utpadyate**

Immediately after the Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance concerning suffering, there arises, with regard to this very same suffering in the desire realm, **Dharma-Knowledge**.

He names it:

> **tad duḥkhe dharmajñānam ity ucyate**

Thus the first pair is now complete:

```text
kāmāvacara-duḥkha
    suffering in the desire realm

1. duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti
       Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance concerning suffering

       ↓ immediately

2. duḥkhe dharmajñāna
       Dharma-Knowledge concerning suffering
```

The object does not change between the two moments. What changes is the mode of the path-cognition.

Vasubandhu adds an important general rule:

> **anāsravādhikāraḥ sarvatra veditavyaḥ**

The qualification **anāsrava**, uncontaminated, is to be understood throughout.

So although the Kārikā explicitly marked only the first kṣānti as uncontaminated in VAK 6.25, the Bhāṣya makes clear that this determination governs the entire sequence now being described.

## 2. From Dharma to Anvaya

Vasubandhu then extends the realization beyond the desire realm:

> **yathā ca kāmāvacare duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣāntir dharmajñānaṃ cotpadyate tathā punaḥ śeṣe duḥkhe 'nvayakṣāntijñāne**

Just as Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance and Dharma-Knowledge arise with regard to suffering in the desire realm, so with regard to the remaining suffering there arise **Anvaya-Knowledge-Acceptance** and **Anvaya-Knowledge**.

The Bhāṣya specifies the remaining field:

> **rūpārūpyāvacare duḥkhe**

suffering belonging to the form and formless realms.

But these are not treated as two separate realization sequences. Vasubandhu says:

> **samastālambanānvayajñānakṣāntir utpadyate**

The Anvaya-Knowledge-Acceptance arises with the higher-realm suffering **collectively as its object-support**.

This gives the exact structure:

```text
Desire realm
    kāmāvacara-duḥkha
        ↓
    dharmajñānakṣānti
        ↓
    dharmajñāna

Form + formless realms collectively
    rūpārūpyāvacara-duḥkha
        ↓
    anvayajñānakṣānti
        ↓
    anvayajñāna
```

The higher realms therefore form one combined object-field for the **anvaya** pair.

## 3. The Four Moments Concerning Suffering

The complete first tetrad is therefore:

```text
1. duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti
   object: desire-realm suffering

2. duḥkhe dharmajñāna
   object: desire-realm suffering

3. duḥkhe anvayajñānakṣānti
   object: form- and formless-realm suffering collectively

4. duḥkhe anvayajñāna
   object: form- and formless-realm suffering collectively
```

The Bhāṣya states the third moment:

> **sā duḥkhe 'nvayajñānakṣāntir ity ucyate**

and then:

> **tato 'nvayajñānam utpadyate**

From it Anvaya-Knowledge arises.

This is called:

> **duḥkhe 'nvayajñānam**

Thus each truth is traversed through two ordered pairs:

```text
dharma pair
    kṣānti → jñāna

anvaya pair
    kṣānti → jñāna
```

The detailed functional distinction between kṣānti and jñāna will be made explicit in VAK 6.28. Here the text establishes their succession and their respective object-domains.

## 4. Why Dharma-Jñāna and Anvaya-Jñāna?

Vasubandhu gives a brief explanation of the names:

> **prathamato dharmatattvajñānād dharmajñānam**

The first knowledge is called **Dharma-Knowledge** because it is, first, knowledge of the reality/truth of the dharma.

He then explains the second:

> **tadanvayād ūrdhvaṃ duḥkhālambanam anvayajñānam**

The knowledge having higher-realm suffering as its object is called **Anvaya-Knowledge** because it follows in accordance with that prior realization.

And he adds:

> **tathaivānugamanāt**

because it follows in just that manner.

The terminology should therefore be handled carefully.

**Anvaya** is not merely a spatial extension from one realm to another. It names a relation of ordered following or conformity:

```text
dharmajñāna
    first realization of the truth in the desire-realm field
        ↓

anvaya
    following according to that realization
        ↓

anvayajñāna
    corresponding realization with the higher-realm field as object
```

The identity of the truth is preserved while its object-domain changes.

## 5. The Same Fourfold Pattern for Origin

The Kārikā says:

> **satyatraye tathā** — likewise with regard to the other three truths.

The Bhāṣya begins the explicit expansion:

> **duḥkhānvayajñānād anantaraṃ kāmāvacare samudaye dharmajñānakṣāntir utpadyate**

Immediately after Anvaya-Knowledge concerning suffering, there arises Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance concerning **origin** in the desire realm.

Then:

> **tataḥ samudaye dharmajñānam**

there arises Dharma-Knowledge concerning origin.

The sequence then continues in the same uninterrupted order:

```text
samudaya — origin

5.  samudaye dharmajñānakṣānti
6.  samudaye dharmajñāna
7.  samudaye anvayajñānakṣānti
8.  samudaye anvayajñāna
```

Again:

- the **dharma** pair concerns the desire-realm field;
- the **anvaya** pair concerns the corresponding higher-realm field.

## 6. Cessation and Path

Vasubandhu then expands the remaining truths without further interruption.

### Cessation

```text
9.  nirodhe dharmajñānakṣānti
10. nirodhe dharmajñāna
11. nirodhe anvayajñānakṣānti
12. nirodhe anvayajñāna
```

The Dharma pair concerns cessation of desire-realm suffering; the Anvaya pair concerns cessation in the remaining higher-realm field.

### Path

```text
13. mārge dharmajñānakṣānti
14. mārge dharmajñāna
15. mārge anvayajñānakṣānti
16. mārge anvayajñāna
```

The Bhāṣya specifies the desire-realm object here as:

> **kāmāvacaraduḥkhapratipakṣamārga**

— the Path that is the counteragent to desire-realm suffering.

The corresponding higher-realm Path becomes the object of the Anvaya pair.

Thus the sixteen moments are not inferred loosely from the Kārikā. Vasubandhu explicitly enumerates their sequential generation.

## 7. A Strictly Successive Realization

A significant Bhāṣya expression is:

> **samanantarotpattikrameṇa**

— according to the sequence of immediately successive arising.

The sixteen moments therefore form a determinate temporal-cognitive order:

```text
Suffering
    Dharma-Acceptance
    Dharma-Knowledge
    Anvaya-Acceptance
    Anvaya-Knowledge
        ↓
Origin
    Dharma-Acceptance
    Dharma-Knowledge
    Anvaya-Acceptance
    Anvaya-Knowledge
        ↓
Cessation
    Dharma-Acceptance
    Dharma-Knowledge
    Anvaya-Acceptance
    Anvaya-Knowledge
        ↓
Path
    Dharma-Acceptance
    Dharma-Knowledge
    Anvaya-Acceptance
    Anvaya-Knowledge
```

The Path of Seeing is therefore not presented as an undifferentiated simultaneous intuition of the Four Truths. It is a generated sequence in which each determinate cognition arises immediately after its predecessor.

VAK 6.27 will gather this ordered multiplicity under the single designation **satyābhisamaya**.

## 8. What Has Become Clear About Kṣānti and Jñāna

VAK 6.25 already showed that kṣānti and jñāna are distinct but genetically connected, using the flower–fruit analogy.

VAK 6.26 now adds a second determination:

```text
kṣānti
    immediately precedes its corresponding jñāna

jñāna
    immediately arises after its corresponding kṣānti
```

For every truth and every domain-pair:

```text
kṣānti → jñāna
```

This relation is invariant across the whole realization sequence.

But we should still resist importing the later distinction prematurely. VAK 6.26 itself does not yet say:

```text
kṣānti = ānantaryamārga
jñāna  = muktimārga
```

That determination belongs explicitly to VAK 6.28.

For the present verse, the secure result is succession, common uncontaminated status, distinct object-fields, and ordered generation.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

The Bhāṣya now transforms the single breakthrough of VAK 6.25 into a complete articulated cognition of Truth.

```text
first uncontaminated breakthrough
    ↓

Truth of suffering
    desire-realm determination
        kṣānti → jñāna
    higher-realm determination
        kṣānti → jñāna
        ↓

Truth of origin
    same fourfold articulation
        ↓

Truth of cessation
    same fourfold articulation
        ↓

Truth of path
    same fourfold articulation
```

Several structural determinations are now secure.

### 1. Seeing has determinate content

The Path does not merely "see reality." It sees:

```text
duḥkha
samudaya
nirodha
mārga
```

under explicitly differentiated object-domains.

### 2. Seeing is internally mediated

For each truth:

```text
dharma
    ↓
anvaya
```

and within each:

```text
kṣānti
    ↓
jñāna
```

The directness of Noble realization therefore does not mean absence of articulation.

### 3. The same Truth is carried across domains

The movement from **dharma** to **anvaya** preserves the truth while extending its realization from the desire-realm field to the collectively apprehended higher-realm field.

This is especially important for understanding **anvaya**: it is not a second independent truth but the corresponding following-through of the same truth.

### 4. The Path is generated as a sequence

The Bhāṣya's **samanantarotpattikrama** gives us a particularly strong formulation:

> Path Knowledge is not a static possession but a rigorously ordered arising of determinate uncontaminated cognitions.

That is the principal result of VAK 6.26.

## New Technical Terms

- **duḥkhe dharmajñāna** — Dharma-Knowledge concerning suffering.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya completion of the first pair introduced in VAK 6.25.
  - **function:** second uncontaminated moment; immediately follows Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance with the same desire-realm suffering as object.

- **anvayajñānakṣānti** — Anvaya-Knowledge-Acceptance.
  - **status:** first explicit occurrence in the realization sequence.
  - **function:** uncontaminated acceptance concerning the corresponding truth in the form and formless realms collectively.

- **anvayajñāna** — Anvaya-Knowledge.
  - **status:** first explicit occurrence in this sequence.
  - **function:** immediately follows Anvaya-Knowledge-Acceptance and completes the higher-realm pair for a truth.

- **samastālambana** — having a collective object-support.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya clarification absent from the compressed Kārikā.
  - **function:** establishes that form- and formless-realm suffering are taken together as the object of the Anvaya pair.

- **dharmatattva** — the reality/truth of the dharma.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya term used in explaining the name Dharma-Knowledge.
  - **function:** indicates what is first known in the dharma mode; the passage is terse and should not yet bear a wider metaphysical interpretation.

- **anugamana** — following, proceeding in accordance with.
  - **status:** explanatory term for **anvaya**.
  - **function:** expresses the ordered conformity of higher-realm realization to the preceding Dharma-Knowledge.

- **samanantarotpattikrama** — sequence of immediately successive arising.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya structural term.
  - **function:** characterizes the generation of the sixteen realization moments as a strict succession.

- **kāmāvacaraduḥkhapratipakṣamārga** — the Path counteracting desire-realm suffering.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification of the object of the Dharma pair concerning the Path truth.
  - **function:** prevents "Path" from remaining abstract by identifying it as a determinate counteragent.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the standard realization sequence.

No competing school position is introduced within the passage assigned to VAK 6.26.

Two cautions should be retained:

1. **anvaya** is explained here through following/conformity to the preceding realization and should not yet be overloaded with a broader logical meaning.
2. The functional identification of kṣānti with the uninterrupted path and jñāna with the liberation path belongs explicitly to VAK 6.28, not to the present verse.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.26 articulates the first uncontaminated breakthrough into the complete sixteen-moment content of realization.

For suffering:

```text
desire realm:
    dharmajñānakṣānti
        ↓
    dharmajñāna

form + formless realms collectively:
    anvayajñānakṣānti
        ↓
    anvayajñāna
```

The same fourfold pattern is then repeated for:

```text
origin
cessation
path
```

All of these moments are to be understood as **anāsrava**.

The higher realms constitute a collective object for the Anvaya pair, and **anvaya** is explained as following in accordance with the preceding Dharma realization.

Most importantly, Vasubandhu describes the series as a **samanantarotpattikrama**: a sequence of immediately successive arising. The Path of Seeing is therefore direct realization without being undifferentiated realization; it is a rigorously articulated and generated order of uncontaminated cognition.
