# VAK 6.74 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

> *lābho mārgābhisamaye buddha-tat-saṃghayor api /*  
> *dharmaḥ satya-trayaṃ bodhisattva-pratyekabuddhayoḥ //*

The Bhāṣya immediately continues the compressed verse with:

> *mārgaś ca*

The unit therefore belongs to the analysis of the four **avetyaprasādas** begun in VAK 6.73.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 386|18–387|14.

The natural argumentative sequence is:

1. What is meant here by **Dharma**?
2. Dharma includes the truths, and the Path must also be included.
3. Therefore direct realization of all four truths yields Dharma-avetyaprasāda.
4. The four avetyaprasādas are four by their objects or bases of confidence, but only two by dravya: **śraddhā** and **śīla**.
5. All four are exclusively **anāsrava**.
6. Vasubandhu then defines avetyaprasāda itself as confidence arising after knowing the truths as they really are.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Question:** What Dharma is intended here?

**Answer:** Dharma is the three truths; and the Path also [is Dharma]. Therefore, through direct realization of all four truths, there is acquisition of verified confidence in Dharma.

These are called four verified confidences by name because of the difference in the supports or objects of confidence.

But substantially there are two: confidence and ethical discipline.

Verified confidence in Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha has **śraddhā** as its nature; the ethical disciplines dear to the noble ones are **śīla**. Thus there are two dravyas.

Are these verified confidences either with outflows or without outflows?

They are pure.

What does “verified confidence” mean?

Having understood the truths as they really are, settled confidence is **avetyaprasāda**.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Objector / interlocutor** — asks what Dharma means here and whether the avetyaprasādas can be sāsrava or anāsrava.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — answers by extending Dharma to all four truths, reducing the four confidences to two operative dravyas, and defining avetyaprasāda through realized truth.

The compact phrase *bodhisattva-pratyekabuddhayoḥ* is not separately unpacked in the surviving prose of this unit. It should therefore remain textually marked rather than assigned a speculative doctrinal explanation in this pass.

## Four by Name, Two by Dravya

The Bhāṣya gives another instance of the distinction already developed in VAK 6.67–6.69:

```text
four named avetyaprasādas
    Buddha
    Dharma
    Saṅgha
    śīla

reduce substantially to two operative dharmas
    śraddhā
    śīla
```

Specifically:

```text
Buddha-confidence ┐
Dharma-confidence ├→ śraddhā
Saṅgha-confidence ┘

ethical confidence → śīla
```

The fourfold distinction is therefore valid at the level of determinate object, while the operative constitution is twofold.

## Nirmala — Exclusively Pure

The Bhāṣya asks whether these confidences may be either contaminated or uncontaminated. The answer is concise:

> *nirmalāḥ*

They are pure.

Thus **avetyaprasāda** is not ordinary confidence that happens to have a Buddhist object. It is a determination belonging to realized noble knowledge.

## Definition of Avetyaprasāda

The most important prose sentence is:

> *yathābhūtasatyāny avabudhya saṃpratyayo ’vetyaprasādaḥ*

“Having understood the truths as they really are, settled confidence is verified confidence.”

The order is decisive:

```text
yathābhūta-avabodha
    knowing the truths as they really are
        ↓
saṃpratyaya
    settled confidence
        ↓
avetyaprasāda
```

The confidence is **generated by realization**. It does not function as a substitute for realization.

## The Fourfold Order in Emergent Reflection

Vasubandhu then explains how the four confidences are brought explicitly before the mind after emerging from contemplative realization. The practitioner recognizes:

- the Blessed One as perfectly awakened,
- his Dharma-Vinaya as well proclaimed,
- his Śrāvaka Saṅgha as well practiced,
- and the ethical discipline as the practical comportment of one whose mind has become clarified.

The Bhāṣya gives analogies of physician, medicine, attendant, and health, and also guide, path, caravan, and vehicle. These analogies organize the four confidences as a practical system rather than four isolated beliefs.

## New Technical Terms

- **śraddhādhiṣṭhāna-bheda** — difference in the support/object on which confidence is established  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation in this unit  
  **function:** explains why four names arise from fewer operative dharmas

- **saṃpratyaya** — settled assurance or confidence  
  **status:** Bhāṣya terminology  
  **function:** definition of avetyaprasāda after realized comprehension

- **yathābhūta-avabodha** — understanding things/truths as they really are  
  **status:** established Buddhist epistemic formulation  
  **function:** ground of verified confidence

- **nirmala** — pure, stainless  
  **status:** previously encountered doctrinal category  
  **function:** fixes avetyaprasāda as exclusively anāsrava

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The movement is:

```text
four truths directly realized
        ↓
knowledge becomes settled assurance
        ↓
four determinate objects of confidence
        ↓
two operative realities
    śraddhā + śīla
```

This is another case where the Kośa preserves both differentiation and operative unity. The named objects are not abolished by the reduction, and the reduction does not create an inert substrate beneath them.

More importantly for the first-pass study, **knowledge is prior in the explanatory order**: avetyaprasāda is a consequence of seeing the truths as they are.

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

The earlier Kārikā file correctly resisted translating *avetyaprasāda* as ordinary “faith.” The Bhāṣya now confirms that restraint explicitly.

A stronger translation is:

**verified confidence**, **realization-grounded confidence**, or **settled confidence born of direct comprehension**.

The Bhāṣya also corrects any reading that would restrict Dharma-confidence to only the first three truths. Its explicit conclusion is:

> realization of **all four truths** yields Dharma-avetyaprasāda.

The compressed *bodhisattva-pratyekabuddhayoḥ* clause remains textually unresolved in this immediate prose and should be reopened only if a later passage clarifies it.

## Light Organon Contact

For this first pass, one restrained result is enough:

```text
knowledge
    is not one belief among others

knowledge
    generates a stable practical determination
```

The Kośa is therefore explaining a transformation of rational-practical comportment through realized truth, not prescribing assent prior to knowledge.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.74 completes the analysis of the four **avetyaprasādas**. Dharma-confidence is ultimately grounded in direct realization of all four truths. The four confidences are four according to their determinate objects, but only two by operative dravya: **śraddhā** and **śīla**. All are exclusively pure. Most importantly, Vasubandhu defines avetyaprasāda as settled confidence arising **after the truths have been understood as they really are**.
