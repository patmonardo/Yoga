# VAK 6.17 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

`tata ūṣmagatotpattis tac catuḥsatya-gocaram |`
`ṣoḍaśākāram ūṣmabhyo mūrdhānas te ’pi tādṛśāḥ || 6.17 ||`

## Bhāṣya Source

`tasmāddharmasmṛtyupasthānādevamabhyastāt krameṇoṣmagataṃ nāma kuśalamūlamutpadyate /`

`ūṣmagatamivoṣmagatam / kleśendhanadahanasyāryamārgāgneḥ pūrvarūpatvāt /`

`tadūṣmagataṃ prākarṣikatvāccatuḥsatyālambanam /`

`duḥkhaṃ caturbhirākāraiḥ paśyatyanityato duḥkhataḥ śūnyato 'nātmataśca /`

`samudayaṃ caturbhirhetutaḥ samudayataḥ prabhavataḥ pratyayataśca /`

`nirodhaṃ caturbhiḥ nirodhataḥ śāntataḥ praṇītato niḥsaraṇataśca /`

`mārgaṃ caturbhirmārgato nyāyataḥ pratipattito nairyāṇikataśca /`

`eṣāṃ tu viśeṣaṇam paścādvakṣyāmaḥ /`

`yādṛśā ūṣmāṇaścatuḥsatyālambanāḥ ṣoḍaśākārāśca /`

`utkṛṣṭataratvāttu nāmāntaraṃ cala kuśalamūlamūrdhatvāt mūrdhānaḥ /`

`ebhyo hi pāto 'tikramo vā /`

## Literal Translation

From that Dharma-Foundation of Recollection, cultivated in this way, there gradually arises a wholesome root called Heat.

It is called Heat because it is like heat: it is the preliminary sign of the fire of the Noble Path which burns the fuel of the afflictions.

Because of its developed excellence, this Heat takes the Four Truths as its object-support. It sees suffering through four aspects: impermanent, suffering, empty, and not-self; origin through cause, arising, production, and condition; cessation through cessation, peace, excellence, and release; and the Path through Path, right method, practice, and leading outward.

From Heat arise the Summits. They too have the Four Truths as object and sixteen aspects. Because they are more excellent they receive another name: Summits, since they are the summit of the unstable wholesome roots. From them there is either falling or passing beyond.

## Natural Argument

VAK 6.16 established a synthetic Dharma-Foundation whose object-support is the total articulated field. VAK 6.17 explains what arises from repeated cultivation of that field.

The first result is not yet the Noble Path itself. It is a new wholesome root called **uṣmagata**, Heat. Vasubandhu explains the metaphor causally:

```text
kleśa
    fuel

āryamārga
    fire

uṣmagata
    preliminary heat
```

Heat is therefore significant because it is the first experiential-cognitive sign that the fire capable of definitive abandonment is approaching.

Its object-field is no longer the total field only under four general universal characteristics. It is reorganized as the Four Truths, each articulated by four modes.

```text
Duḥkha
    anitya
    duḥkha
    śūnya
    anātman

Samudaya
    hetu
    samudaya
    prabhava
    pratyaya

Nirodha
    nirodha
    śānta
    praṇīta
    niḥsaraṇa

Mārga
    mārga
    nyāya
    pratipatti
    nairyāṇika
```

Thus the synthetic field does not become less determinate. It becomes more strongly organized.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, explaining the Vaibhāṣika path taxonomy without objection in this passage.

## Heat as a Genetic Stage

The key relation is:

```text
repeated Dharma-Foundation cultivation
    ↓
Heat
```

This means Heat is not an arbitrary named station. It is generated from prior practice.

The Bhāṣya calls it a **kuśalamūla**, a wholesome root. The language is important: a new causal capacity has arisen in the continuum.

But it remains preparatory. Heat is the sign of Noble Path fire, not yet the fire itself.

## Sixteen Aspects

The Four Truths are each viewed under four ākāras, producing a sixteenfold cognitive architecture.

This is not merely enumeration. Each Truth is approached through a structured set of determinations sufficient to prevent reduction to a single vague idea.

The practitioner therefore no longer merely knows:

```text
There are Four Truths.
```

Rather, the field of each Truth is internally articulated.

## The Summits

From Heat arise the **mūrdhānaḥ**, Summits.

Vasubandhu explicitly says they are `tādṛśāḥ`: of the same kind as Heat with respect to object and aspect-structure.

```text
Heat
    Four Truths
    sixteen aspects

Summits
    Four Truths
    sixteen aspects
```

The difference is strength:

`utkṛṣṭataratvāt` — because they are more excellent or more developed.

So development here is not necessarily acquisition of new doctrinal content. It is increase in the power and stability of the same cognition.

## Why “Summits”?

They are called Summits because they stand at the top of the **cala-kuśalamūlas**, the unstable wholesome roots.

Vasubandhu gives the decisive reason:

```text
from here
    either fall
    or pass beyond
```

This stage is therefore a threshold.

The practitioner has climbed to the highest point that still admits reversal.

## Relation to Śakti

This verse gives an important Kośa-internal distinction between content and force.

```text
same Four Truths
same sixteen aspects
    ↓
stronger wholesome root
```

So the growth of Path-capacity is not always the acquisition of a new object. The same truth-field can become more causally effective as the cognitive root matures.

This gives a more precise route toward the śakti of VAK 6.01:

```text
Prajñā
    ↓
synthetic Dharma-field
    ↓
Heat
    ↓
Summits
    ↓
still stronger penetration stages
    ↓
darśana-mārga
```

The special power of supramundane Seeing is being genetically prepared through increasingly powerful roots.

## New Technical Terms

- **uṣmagata** — Heat; wholesome root that is the preliminary sign of Noble Path fire.
- **pūrvarūpa** — preliminary sign or precursor.
- **āryamārgāgni** — fire of the Noble Path.
- **kleśendhana** — fuel of the afflictions.
- **mūrdhan** — Summit; the highest point of the unstable wholesome roots.
- **cala-kuśalamūla** — unstable wholesome root, still subject to falling away.
- **utkṛṣṭatara** — more excellent, more developed, stronger.
- **atikrama** — passing beyond, advancing past the present stage.

## Determinate Summary

```text
Repeated cultivation of the synthetic Dharma-field
    produces Heat.

Heat:
    wholesome root
    precursor of Noble Path fire
    Four Truths as object
    sixteen aspects

From Heat arise Summits.

Summits:
    same Four Truths
    same sixteen aspects
    greater strength
    highest unstable wholesome roots
    threshold of fall or advance
```

The central result is:

> Path development is not merely accumulation of new doctrines. The same truth-field becomes progressively more powerful as a causal and cognitive root.
