# VAK 6.62 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> saviśeṣaṃ phalaṃ tyaktvā phalam āpnoti vardhayan /  
> dvau buddhau śrāvakāḥ sapta navaite navadhendriyāḥ // VAK_6.62 //

**Best current translation:**  
When one strengthens the faculties, one relinquishes the fruit together with its [former] specific determination and attains the fruit [with the sharper-faculty determination]. There are two Buddhas and seven Śrāvakas; these nine [persons] correspond to the nine grades of faculties.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.62.md`](./VAK_6.62.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 379|13–380|05.

The first half of VAK 6.62 completes the explanation begun at the end of VAK 6.61: why the śaikṣa can strengthen the faculties on only six grounds rather than the nine available to the aśaikṣa. The second half then reorganizes the preceding sixfold Arhat scheme into nine persons according to the nine grades of faculties. Immediately afterward Vasubandhu introduces a distinct sevenfold enumeration of all noble persons, which prepares VAK 6.63.

## 1. What Is Relinquished When the Faculties Are Strengthened?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The kārikā states:

> `saviśeṣaṃ phalaṃ tyaktvā phalam āpnoti vardhayan`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `indriyāṇi hi saṃcaran phalaṃ phalaviśiṣṭaṃ ca mṛdvindriyamārgaṃ tyaktvā tīkṣṇendriyagotrakaṃ phalamārgameva pratilabhate`

When a practitioner changes or strengthens the faculties, he relinquishes both the fruit and the path specifically qualified by weak faculties and acquires the fruit and path belonging to the sharp-faculty disposition.

The point is therefore more exact than the first-pass expression “the same fruit is simply possessed more strongly.” In Abhidharma analysis, the weak-faculty fruit/path complex is relinquished and the corresponding sharp-faculty fruit/path complex is acquired.

```text
weak-faculty fruit/path
    ↓ relinquished
faculty-transition
    ↓ acquired
sharp-faculty fruit/path
```

The fruit-level does not necessarily advance from one of the four fruits to the next. What changes is the determinate path-and-fruit complex according to the faculty-disposition.

## 2. Why the Śaikṣa Has Only Six Grounds

The Bhāṣya now supplies the reason anticipated by VAK 6.61:

> `na cānāgāmiphalamārupyabhūmisaṃgṛhītam astīty etat kāraṇam`

The fruit of the non-returner is not included in a formless ground.

This is why the śaikṣa cannot use the three lower formless attainments as bases for the faculty-transition under discussion.

Thus:

```text
śaikṣa faculty-transition
    anāgamya
    dhyānāntara
    four dhyānas
    = 6 grounds

not the ārūpyas
    because Anāgāmin fruit is not included there
```

By contrast, the aśaikṣa can undertake the corresponding transition on nine grounds, as stated in VAK 6.61.

The distinction is therefore doctrinally grounded in the distribution of the relevant fruit, not merely in a generic claim that the Arhat has “more contemplative power.”

## 3. From Six Arhats to Nine Persons

Vasubandhu then returns to the six Arhats already established:

> `ta ete ṣaḍ evārhanto nava bhavanti / indriyabhedāt / kathaṃ kṛtvā`

These same six Arhats become nine [persons] because of differences in the faculties. How so?

The kārikā answers:

> `dvau buddhau śrāvakāḥ sapta navaite navadhendriyāḥ`

The Bhāṣya therefore shifts the principle of classification:

```text
six Arhats
    classified by mode/disposition of liberation

nine persons
    classified by nine grades of faculties
```

The ninefold classification is not simply a second name for the sixfold one. It is obtained by differentiating some members more finely according to the faculties.

## 4. The Seven Śrāvakas of the Ninefold Faculty Classification

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `katame sapta śrāvakāḥ`

Which are the seven Śrāvakas?

It answers:

> `parihāṇadharmādayaḥ pañca`

First come the five beginning with the *parihāṇadharman*—that is, the five condition-dependent Arhat types discussed in VAK 6.56–6.60.

Then:

> `akopyadharmā ca dvividha uttāpanāgata āditaś ca tadgotraḥ`

The *akopyadharman* is twofold:

1. one who has arrived at the unshakeable disposition through sharpening (*uttāpanāgata*)
2. one who belonged to that disposition from the beginning (*āditaḥ tadgotra*)

Vasubandhu adds:

> `akopyabheda eva`

This is simply a subdivision of the unshakeable type.

So the seven Śrāvakas counted here are:

```text
1. parihāṇadharman
2. cetanādharman
3. anurakṣaṇādharman
4. sthitākampya / sthitākuśala-type [the relevant fourth in the sixfold sequence]
5. prativedhanādharman
6. akopyadharman attained through sharpening
7. akopyadharman of original disposition
```

The exact Sanskrit labels of the middle members belong to the sixfold taxonomy already established in the preceding Bhāṣya; the important point here is the arithmetic:

```text
five condition-dependent types
+ two akopya subtypes
= seven Śrāvakas
```

## 5. The Two Buddhas

The Bhāṣya then states:

> `dvau buddhau pratyekabuddho buddhaś ca`

The two Buddhas are:

```text
Pratyekabuddha
Buddha (Samyaksaṃbuddha)
```

Thus the complete ninefold scheme is:

```text
7 Śrāvakas
+ Pratyekabuddha
+ Buddha
= 9 persons
```

This confirms the first-pass inference, but now directly from the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Nine Persons Because the Faculties Are Ninefold

Vasubandhu summarizes:

> `ity ete mṛdumṛdvādinavaprakārendriyabhedān nava pudgalā bhavanti`

These become nine persons because of the ninefold differentiation of faculties beginning with mild-mild (*mṛdu-mṛdu*).

The familiar nine grades are:

```text
mild-mild
mild-medium
mild-strong

medium-mild
medium-medium
medium-strong

strong-mild
strong-medium
strong-strong
```

The important point is not that each name is mechanically equated with one grade in isolation, but that the nine-person architecture is generated by the ninefold differentiation of the faculties.

```text
nine grades of indriya
    ↓
nine determinate person-types
```

The classification is therefore grounded in operative capacity.

## 7. A Different Classification: All Ārya Persons Are Seven

Immediately after explaining the nine-person scheme, Vasubandhu introduces a new statement:

> `sarva eva tv āryapudgalāḥ sapta bhavanti`

But all noble persons are seven.

He enumerates them:

> `śraddhānusārī dharmānusārī śraddhādhimukto dṛṣṭiprāptaḥ kāyasākṣī prajñāvimukta ubhayatovimuktaś ca`

The seven are:

1. **śraddhānusārin** — follower through faith
2. **dharmānusārin** — follower through Dharma
3. **śraddhādhimukta** — faith-resolved
4. **dṛṣṭiprāpta** — one who has attained through seeing
5. **kāyasākṣin** — bodily witness
6. **prajñāvimukta** — liberated through wisdom
7. **ubhayatovimukta** — liberated in both ways

This sevenfold enumeration must not be conflated with the “seven Śrāvakas” in the ninefold faculty classification immediately preceding it.

They arise from different principles of division.

```text
seven Śrāvakas in the ninefold scheme
    = five Arhat dispositions + two akopya subtypes
    principle: faculty grade

seven Ārya persons
    = śraddhānusārin ... ubhayatovimukta
    principle: mode/stage of noble realization
```

The second sevenfold classification is the one that VAK 6.63 will analyze.

## 8. A Classification Can Be Reorganized by Its Principle of Division

This passage is a useful warning against reading the Kośa's person-taxonomies as a single flat list.

The same Path-field can be divided according to different determinants:

```text
mode of liberation
    → six Arhats

faculty grade
    → nine persons

mode/stage of noble realization
    → seven Ārya persons
```

No contradiction follows from the changing numbers because the principle of classification has changed.

This is especially important in Chapter 6. The *pudgala* names are not independent substances. They are determinations generated by Path-structure, faculty, attainment, concentration, liberation, and developmental history.

## 9. Restrained Organon Contact

The strongest conceptual result is classificatory rather than metaphysical.

A determinate individual can fall under different systematic descriptions depending upon the relation being made explicit:

```text
same Path-person
    ↓ considered by liberation-mode
sixfold system

same Path-person
    ↓ considered by faculty-grade
ninefold system

same Path-person
    ↓ considered by mode of noble realization
sevenfold system
```

The Kośa therefore does not treat classification as mere naming. Each division is generated by a different explanatory ground.

This is a useful Organon discipline: before comparing two enumerations, first identify the **principle of division**. Numerical mismatch is not yet doctrinal disagreement.

## New Technical Terms

- **phala-viśiṣṭa mṛdvindriya-mārga** — path/fruit specifically determined by weak faculties  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression  
  **function:** identifies what is relinquished in faculty-transition

- **tīkṣṇendriya-gotraka** — belonging to the sharp-faculty disposition  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression  
  **function:** identifies the stronger determination acquired through faculty-transition

- **indriya-bheda** — differentiation of faculties  
  **status:** recurrent technical principle  
  **function:** generates the nine-person classification

- **uttāpanāgata akopyadharman** — unshakeable Arhat who has arrived at that disposition through sharpening  
  **status:** Bhāṣya specification  
  **function:** one of two subdivisions of the akopya type

- **āditaḥ tadgotra akopyadharman** — unshakeable Arhat whose disposition is such from the beginning  
  **status:** Bhāṣya specification  
  **function:** second subdivision of the akopya type

- **mṛdu-mṛdu** — mild-mild  
  **status:** standard grade of faculties  
  **function:** first member of the ninefold faculty scale

- **āryapudgala** — noble person  
  **status:** recurrent  
  **function:** heading for the separate sevenfold classification introduced after the nine-person scheme

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / standard Abhidharma exposition.

No major inter-school dispute is developed in this particular passage. The chief caution is structural: the seven Śrāvakas counted within the ninefold faculty classification are **not** the same enumeration as the seven Ārya persons introduced immediately afterward.

The first-pass Kārikā file correctly anticipated the two Buddhas but left the seven Śrāvakas provisional. The Bhāṣya now fixes them as the five beginning with *parihāṇadharman* plus the two subdivisions of the *akopyadharman*.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.62 completes the mechanics of faculty-strengthening and then reorganizes the Arhat taxonomy according to faculty grade. In faculty-transition, the weak-faculty path and fruit are relinquished and the corresponding sharp-faculty path and fruit are acquired. The śaikṣa cannot perform this transition on formless grounds because the Anāgāmin fruit is not included there. The six Arhat types become seven Śrāvaka types when the unshakeable Arhat is divided into one who attains that disposition through sharpening and one who possesses it from the beginning; adding Pratyekabuddha and Buddha yields nine persons corresponding to the nine grades of faculties. Vasubandhu then introduces a separate sevenfold classification of all Ārya persons, which leads directly into VAK 6.63.
