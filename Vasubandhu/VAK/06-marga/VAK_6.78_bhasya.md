# VAK 6.78 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> **asaṃskṛtaiva dhātvākhyā virāgo rāgasaṃkṣayaḥ |**  
> **prahāṇadhātur anyeṣāṃ nirodhākhyas tu vastunaḥ ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 389|12–389|20.

## Literal Prose Translation

The unconditioned liberation previously stated and the three domains now spoken of—*prahāṇa-dhātu*, *virāga-dhātu*, and *nirodha-dhātu*—are the same unconditioned liberation under three designations.

Among these:

- the abandonment of rāga is the **virāga-dhātu**;
- the abandonment of the other afflictions is the **prahāṇa-dhātu**;
- the abandonment of the basis (*vastu*) already freed from the afflictions is the **nirodha-dhātu**.

## Course of the Argument

Vasubandhu begins with a question of difference:

> *ka eṣāṃ viśeṣaḥ* — what is the distinction among these?

His answer is not that three different unconditioned realities have been introduced.

```text
unconditioned liberation
    = one operative reality

three names
    according to what is brought to an end
```

The three determinations are:

```text
rāga
    → virāga-dhātu

other kleśas
    → prahāṇa-dhātu

kleśa-freed vastu itself
    → nirodha-dhātu
```

The phrase *saṃkṣaya iti vartate* is carried through the verse: in each case the point is complete exhaustion or cessation.

## The Three Domains

### Virāga-dhātu

> *rāgasya prahāṇaṃ virāgadhātuḥ*

The domain of dispassion is the abandonment of rāga.

This makes *virāga* a precise liberative determination, not merely a psychological mood of detachment.

### Prahāṇa-dhātu

> *rāgād anyeṣāṃ kleśānāṃ prahāṇaṃ prahāṇadhātuḥ*

The domain of abandonment is the abandonment of the afflictions other than rāga.

Thus the first two names divide afflictive exhaustion according to the kleśa abandoned.

### Nirodha-dhātu

> *kleśanirmuktasya vastunaḥ prahāṇaṃ nirodhadhātuḥ*

This is the most important clarification supplied by the Bhāṣya.

The domain of cessation is the abandonment or cessation of the **basis already freed from the afflictions**.

So *vastu* here is not merely “conditioned actuality” in the abstract. The prose specifies a conditioned basis from which the kleśas have already been removed.

A cautious first-pass structure is therefore:

```text
first
    rāga exhausted

then
    remaining kleśas exhausted

finally
    the liberated conditioned basis itself ceases
```

## Doctrinal Determination

The three domains are not three independent unconditioned substances.

```text
asaṃskṛta-vimukti
    one unconditioned liberation

named as
    virāga-dhātu
    prahāṇa-dhātu
    nirodha-dhātu

according to
    the determination brought to cessation
```

This preserves the distinction between:

```text
afflictive cessation
and
the final cessation of the liberated conditioned basis
```

The Bhāṣya therefore sharpens the Kārikā considerably: *nirodha-dhātu* is specifically tied to *kleśa-nirmukta-vastu*.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is explanatory and classificatory. No explicit school dispute is introduced here.

## New Technical Terms

- **virāga-dhātu** — domain of dispassion; the abandonment of rāga
  - **status** — introduced in this closing liberation analysis
  - **function** — definition

- **prahāṇa-dhātu** — domain of abandonment; the abandonment of afflictions other than rāga
  - **status** — introduced in this closing liberation analysis
  - **function** — definition

- **nirodha-dhātu** — domain of cessation; the cessation of the basis already freed from the afflictions
  - **status** — introduced in this closing liberation analysis
  - **function** — definition

- **kleśa-nirmukta-vastu** — the conditioned basis already freed from affliction
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-only clarification
  - **function** — logical distinction

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The earlier provisional gloss of *nirodhākhyas tu vastunaḥ* as generic “cessation of conditioned actuality” is too broad.

The Bhāṣya requires:

> **nirodha-dhātu is the cessation of the basis already freed from affliction.**

This keeps the threefold distinction exact and avoids turning *vastu* into an unspecified metaphysical substrate.

## Light Organon Contact

For this first pass, the useful structural point is simply:

```text
one liberation
    receives three determinate names
    according to three distinct termini of cessation
```

The distinction is relational and functional, not a multiplication of substances.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.78 identifies the three liberation-domains as three designations of the same unconditioned liberation. *Virāga-dhātu* is the abandonment of rāga; *prahāṇa-dhātu* is the abandonment of the other afflictions; and *nirodha-dhātu* is the cessation of the conditioned basis already freed from those afflictions. The Bhāṣya therefore gives *nirodha* a more precise meaning than a generic cessation of conditioned existence.
