# VAK 6.75 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

> **mārgaś ca dravyatas tu dve śraddhā śīlaṃ ca nirmalāḥ |**  
> **noktā vimuktiḥ śaikṣāṅgaṃ baddhatvāt sā punar dvidhā ||**

The first half closes the *avetyaprasāda* discussion: the four verified confidences reduce substantially to two operative dharmas, **śraddhā** and **nirmala śīla**. The second half opens a new question: why are right liberation and right knowledge not counted among the factors of the trainee (*śaikṣa*)?

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 387|14–388|05.

The Bhāṣya cites the sūtra statement that the trainee is endowed with eight factors, whereas the one beyond training is endowed with ten. Vasubandhu then asks why **samyagvimukti** and **samyagjñāna** are omitted from the trainee.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Question:** Why are right liberation and right knowledge not stated for the trainee?

**Answer:**

> *noktā vimuktiḥ śaikṣāṅga baddhatvāt*  
> Liberation is not stated as a factor of the trainee, because the trainee is still bound.

For the trainee is still bound by the bonds of the afflictions. How could liberation be established for one who is still bound? One who is released from only one portion of bondage is not called “liberated.” And without liberation, how could knowledge of liberation be established?

The one beyond training, however, is completely released from all bonds of affliction. Therefore it is appropriate that liberation and the immediate knowledge of that liberation be stated only of the Aśaikṣa.

Vasubandhu then asks:

> *keyaṃ vimuktir nāma*  
> What, then, is this liberation?

The Kārikā answers:

> *sā punar dvidhā*  
> It is twofold.

The Bhāṣya specifies:

> *saṃskṛtā cāsaṃskṛtā ca*  
> conditioned and unconditioned.

And then:

> *asaṃskṛtā kleśahānam adhimuktas tu saṃskṛtā*  
> The unconditioned liberation is the abandonment of the afflictions; the conditioned liberation is the Aśaikṣa’s *adhimokṣa*.

## Course of Argument

The reasoning is exact:

```text
śaikṣa
    residual kleśa-bondage remains
        ↓
partial release only
        ↓
not vimukti simpliciter
        ↓
no completed vimukti-jñāna

Aśaikṣa
    total removal of kleśa-bondage
        ↓
vimukti properly so called
        ↓
knowledge of that liberation
```

The key sentence is:

> *na hi bandhanaikadeśān mukto mukta ity ucyate*  
> One released from only one portion of bondage is not called liberated.

This is not a denial of partial progress. It is a rule governing the technical designation **vimukti**.

## Twofold Liberation

Vasubandhu then distinguishes:

```text
asaṃskṛta-vimukti
    kleśa-prahāṇa
    abandonment of the afflictions

saṃskṛta-vimukti
    aśaikṣa-adhimokṣa
    the conditioned liberative determination of the Aśaikṣa
```

The first is the unconditioned fact of the afflictions having been abandoned. The second is a conditioned dharma belonging to the liberated person.

The next verse will further relate this conditioned liberation to the scriptural pair **ceto-vimukti** and **prajñā-vimukti**.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — framing the question, giving the argument from residual bondage, and defining the twofold liberation.

No competing school position is introduced in this immediate unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **samyagvimukti** — right liberation; completed liberation counted among the Aśaikṣa factors.  
  **Status:** new in this closing sequence.  
  **Function:** doctrinal distinction.

- **samyagjñāna** — right knowledge; here knowledge of completed liberation.  
  **Status:** new in this immediate sense.  
  **Function:** consequence of completed liberation.

- **bandhana** — bondage, specifically the bonds of the afflictions.  
  **Status:** previously encountered conceptually.  
  **Function:** explanatory ground for excluding vimukti from the Śaikṣa.

- **saṃskṛta-vimukti** — conditioned liberation.  
  **Status:** introduced here.  
  **Function:** definition.

- **asaṃskṛta-vimukti** — unconditioned liberation, identified with abandonment of the afflictions.  
  **Status:** introduced here.  
  **Function:** definition.

- **adhimokṣa** — decisive determination / resolve; here the conditioned liberative determination of the Aśaikṣa.  
  **Status:** previously encountered term, new function here.  
  **Function:** definition of conditioned liberation.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The crucial distinction is between **partial release** and **completed liberation**.

```text
removal of some bondage
    ≠
liberation without qualification
```

The trainee can possess genuine Path-realization and genuine abandonment while still remaining within a structure of residual bondage. Thus the Kośa refuses to identify a decisive breakthrough with final liberation.

The twofold definition of liberation also prevents a simple collapse of liberation into either:

```text
only an unconditioned absence
or
only a conditioned mental state
```

Instead, the Bhāṣya distinguishes the unconditioned abandonment itself from the conditioned liberative determination of the person who has completed the Path.

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

The Kārikā’s *baddhatvāt* should remain strong: **“because of still being bound.”** It is not merely “because training remains.” Vasubandhu’s explanation explicitly concerns residual **kleśa-bandhana**.

Likewise *sā punar dvidhā* must be read as introducing a real doctrinal division of vimukti into conditioned and unconditioned forms.

## Light Organon Contact

For this first pass, the main structural point is enough:

```text
partial negation of bondage
    does not yet constitute
completed freedom
```

The Path therefore distinguishes intermediate victories from the completed actuality of liberation. The stronger reconstruction of this distinction belongs to the later Organon pass.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.75 explains why liberation and knowledge of liberation are not counted among the factors of the trainee: the Śaikṣa remains bound by residual afflictions, and release from only part of bondage is not called liberation simpliciter. Complete vimukti properly belongs to the Aśaikṣa. Vasubandhu then divides liberation into **unconditioned liberation**, the abandonment of the afflictions, and **conditioned liberation**, the Aśaikṣa’s liberative *adhimokṣa*.
