# VAK 6.29 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**mṛdutīkṣṇendriyau teṣu śraddhādharmānusāriṇau |**  
**ahīnabhāvanāheyau phalādyupapratipannakau || 6.29 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 353|12–353|22.

Vasubandhu has completed the initial explanation of how the Path of Seeing and Path of Cultivation arise. He now announces a new task:

> **yathedānīm utpannāryamārgāṇāṃ pudgalānāṃ vyavasthānaṃ tathā vakṣyāmaḥ**

He will explain the classification of persons in whom the Noble Path has now arisen.

The discussion therefore turns from the internal structure of the sixteen-moment realization to the determinate **path-status of the practitioner**.

## 1. The Fifteen Moments Generate Two Path-Persons

Vasubandhu explicitly refers back to the fifteen moments whose nature is the Path of Seeing:

> **ya ete darśanamārgasvabhāvāḥ pañcadaśa kṣaṇā uktāḥ**

Within these fifteen moments there are two kinds of practitioner:

```text
mṛdu-indriya
    gentle / weaker faculty
        → śraddhānusārin

 tīkṣṇa-indriya
    sharp faculty
        → dharmānusārin
```

The distinction does not alter the truths seen or the ordered Path itself. It classifies the **person traversing that invariant path** according to faculty.

Thus 6.29 begins a new determination:

```text
same Path of Seeing
same fifteen moments
same four truths

but two modes of path-person
    according to indriya
```

## 2. Śraddhānusārin — The Faith-Follower

Vasubandhu states:

> **mṛdvindriyas teṣu vartamānaḥ śraddhānusārīty ucyate**

The practitioner of gentle faculty who is proceeding through those moments is called a **śraddhānusārin**.

He gives two grammatical explanations:

> **śraddhayānusāraḥ śraddhānusāraḥ | so 'syāstīti śraddhānusārī**

or:

> **śraddhayānusartuṃ śīlam asyeti vā**

The person follows by means of **śraddhā**, or has the disposition to follow through śraddhā.

But the Bhāṣya then supplies the important historical determination:

> **pūrvaṃ parasaṃpratyayenārthānusaraṇāt**

Before entering the Noble Path, this practitioner followed the **meaning (artha)** through reliance on another's assurance or testimony.

Thus śraddhā here should not be reduced to irrational belief. It names the predominating mode by which this practitioner's prior approach to meaning was mediated:

```text
another's testimony / assurance
    ↓
confidence
    ↓
following the meaning
    ↓
Noble Path arises
    ↓
śraddhānusārin
```

Once within the Path of Seeing, the cognition itself is already uncontaminated. The name records the practitioner's faculty and prior mode of access, not a lesser objective truth.

## 3. Dharmānusārin — The Dharma-Follower

The practitioner of sharp faculty is:

> **tīkṣṇendriyo dharmānusārī**

Vasubandhu explains the contrast:

> **pūrvaṃ svayam eva sūtrādibhir dharmair arthānusaraṇāt**

Before Noble Path-entry, this practitioner followed the meaning **by oneself**, through dharmas such as the sūtras.

The contrast is therefore precise:

```text
śraddhānusārin
    previously follows meaning
    through reliance on another

 dharmānusārin
    previously follows meaning
    by oneself
    through Dharma sources such as sūtra
```

The distinction is not faith versus reason in a modern abstract sense. Both enter the same uncontaminated Seeing. Rather, Vasubandhu differentiates the **genesis of their access to meaning** and connects that difference with gentle versus sharp faculty.

This is especially important because **artha** remains constant:

```text
what is followed:
    artha — meaning

mode of following:
    through another's assurance
        or
    through Dharma by oneself
```

The objective content does not divide; the cognitive route does.

## 4. From Path-Structure to Pudgala-Classification

The opening sentence of the Bhāṣya is programmatic. Vasubandhu is now explaining:

> **pudgalānāṃ vyavasthānam** — the determination/classification of persons.

This does not introduce a substantial self. It introduces a systematic classification of a continuum according to:

```text
Path moment
faculty
prior abandonment
fruit toward which one is proceeding
```

So the machinery now acquires a new coordinate system.

Earlier verses specified:

```text
object
    truth

cognitive operation
    kṣānti / jñāna

path-function
    ānantarya / mukti
```

VAK 6.29 adds:

```text
subject-position
    śraddhānusārin / dharmānusārin

faculty
    mṛdu / tīkṣṇa

prior karmic-path history
    degree of bhāvanā-heya already abandoned

prospective fruit
    pratipannaka toward a determinate result
```

This is why the following verses become numerically intricate: the Path is invariant, but practitioners arrive at Seeing with different prior histories of abandonment.

## 5. If the Cultivation-Abandonable Defilements Have Not Been Abandoned

The second half states:

> **ahīnabhāvanāheyau**

Vasubandhu expands this condition:

> **yadi pūrvaṃ laukikena mārgeṇāprahīṇabhāvanāheyau bhavataḥ**

If, **before** the Noble Path, the defilements abandonable through cultivation have not been abandoned by the mundane path, the two practitioners still possess the complete set of relevant bonds:

> **sakalabandhanau**

That qualification is crucial.

The Path of Seeing itself abandons the defilements that are **darśana-heya**. But some **bhāvanā-heya** defilements can already have been weakened or abandoned beforehand through a mundane path of detachment.

Therefore the practitioner's destination during the fifteen moments depends partly on what has already happened before supramundane Seeing begins.

For the present case:

```text
before Path of Seeing:
    no bhāvanā-heya grades abandoned
    complete binding remains

within Path of Seeing:
    śraddhānusārin or dharmānusārin

status:
    proceeding toward first fruit
```

## 6. The First Fruit Is Stream-Entry

Vasubandhu glosses **phalānām ādyam**:

> **srota āpattiphalaṃ sarvaphalaprāptau tasya prathamatvāt**

The first among the fruits is the fruit of **stream-entry**, because it is first in the attainment of all the fruits.

Hence, if the practitioner entered Seeing without prior mundane abandonment of cultivation-removable defilements:

> **srota āpattiphalapratipannakāv ucyete**

both the Faith-Follower and Dharma-Follower are called persons **proceeding toward the fruit of stream-entry**.

The status distinction is exact:

```text
moments 1–15
    pratipannaka
    one proceeding toward the fruit

not yet:
    phalastha
    one established in the fruit
```

The sixteenth moment will change that status, as VAK 6.31 later explains.

## 7. Seeing Does Not Erase the Practitioner's Prior History

This verse adds an important complication to the apparent automatism of the sixteen moments.

The path-process is fixed, but its **result-position is conditional upon the incoming state of the continuum**.

```text
fixed supramundane process:
    fifteen moments of Seeing

variable input-condition:
    how much bhāvanā-heya
    was already abandoned
    by the mundane path

therefore variable prospective fruit
```

In the simplest case of 6.29:

```text
zero prior cultivation-abandonment
    ↓
first fruit is next
    ↓
stream-entry candidate
```

VAK 6.30 will vary this input-condition and thereby vary the fruit toward which the same Path-of-Seeing practitioner is proceeding.

This is a highly systematic move: the Noble Path is not treated in isolation from the causal history of the continuum in which it arises.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.25–6.28 described the internal engine of Seeing:

```text
truth as determinate object
    ↓
kṣānti
    obstruction severed
    ↓
jñāna
    liberation secured
    ↓
next determinate truth-moment
```

VAK 6.29 now adds the path-subject as a stateful participant:

```text
PathSubject {
    faculty:
        gentle | sharp

    priorModeOfFollowingMeaning:
        reliance-on-another | Dharma-by-oneself

    priorAbandonmentState:
        determinate quantity of bhāvanā-heya removed

    currentPathState:
        one of fifteen Seeing moments

    targetFruit:
        determined from prior abandonment state
}
```

For the present verse:

```text
mṛdu faculty
    → śraddhānusārin

 tīkṣṇa faculty
    → dharmānusārin

no prior bhāvanā-heya abandoned
    → both proceed toward stream-entry
```

This gives us a more exact formulation of the emerging method:

> **The Path operates according to a fixed cognitive sequence, but it operates upon a continuum with a determinate prior state.**

The result is therefore neither arbitrary nor uniform. It is lawfully determined by the conjunction of Path-function and practitioner-condition.

## New Technical Terms

- **śraddhānusārin** — Faith-Follower.
  - **status:** major path-person classification introduced here.
  - **function:** gentle-faculty practitioner traversing the fifteen moments of Seeing; previously followed meaning through reliance upon another.

- **dharmānusārin** — Dharma-Follower.
  - **status:** major path-person classification introduced here.
  - **function:** sharp-faculty practitioner traversing the fifteen moments of Seeing; previously followed meaning by oneself through dharmas such as the sūtras.

- **parasaṃpratyaya** — reliance upon another's assurance/testimony.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** characterizes the prior mode by which the Faith-Follower followed meaning.

- **arthānusaraṇa** — following the meaning.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya phrase.
  - **function:** common objective orientation underlying both Faith-Follower and Dharma-Follower; their difference concerns how meaning was followed.

- **mṛdvindriya** — gentle/weak faculty.
  - **status:** established faculty terminology, here determinative of path-person type.
  - **function:** condition for classification as Faith-Follower.

- **tīkṣṇendriya** — sharp faculty.
  - **status:** established faculty terminology, here determinative of path-person type.
  - **function:** condition for classification as Dharma-Follower.

- **bhāvanā-heya** — abandonable through cultivation.
  - **status:** crucial path-classification term.
  - **function:** its degree of prior abandonment determines which fruit the Seeing practitioner approaches.

- **sakalabandhana** — having the complete binding/bonds intact.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya characterization of the case in which no cultivation-abandonable grades have previously been removed.
  - **function:** establishes the initial condition leading to candidacy for the first fruit.

- **pratipannaka** — one who has entered upon/proceeds toward a fruit.
  - **status:** central practitioner-status term for the following sequence.
  - **function:** distinguishes active transition toward a fruit from being established in that fruit.

- **srotaāpattiphala** — fruit of stream-entry.
  - **status:** first Noble fruit.
  - **function:** target fruit for a Seeing practitioner with no prior mundane abandonment of bhāvanā-heya defilements.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the standard path-person classification.

No explicit rival-school objection occurs in this immediate unit.

Two cautions are important:

1. **śraddhānusārin** should not be treated simply as a person of irrational belief. Vasubandhu defines the distinction historically and cognitively: prior reliance on another's assurance versus following meaning independently through Dharma sources.
2. The distinction between the two persons does not imply two different Paths or two different truth-fields. It concerns faculty and prior mode of access within the same Noble Path.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.29 shifts from the structure of Seeing to the classification of the person undergoing Seeing.

Within the fifteen moments:

```text
gentle faculty
    → Faith-Follower

sharp faculty
    → Dharma-Follower
```

The Bhāṣya explains their difference through their prior relation to **artha**:

```text
Faith-Follower:
    followed meaning
    through reliance on another

Dharma-Follower:
    followed meaning
    by oneself
    through sūtra and other Dharma sources
```

If no defilements abandonable through cultivation had previously been removed by a mundane path, both remain **sakalabandhana** and are therefore **pratipannakas toward the first fruit**, stream-entry.

The emerging system can now be stated more precisely:

```text
fixed Path operation
    +
faculty of practitioner
    +
prior abandonment state
    =
determinate path-person and determinate target fruit
```

The Path of Seeing therefore transforms a continuum without erasing the continuum's prior causal configuration. VAK 6.30 will vary that prior configuration and show how the target fruit changes accordingly.
