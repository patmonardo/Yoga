# VAK 6.50 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> yady akopyaḥ kṣayajñānād anutpādamatiḥ /  
> na cet kṣayajñānam aśaikṣī vā dṛṣṭiḥ /  
> sarvasya sā 'rhataḥ // VAkK_6.50 //

**Best current translation:**  
If the Arhat is of immovable nature, immediately after knowledge of exhaustion there arises knowledge of non-arising. If he is not immovable, there arises either another knowledge of exhaustion or the right view beyond training. That right view belongs to every Arhat.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.50.md`](./VAK_6.50.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 368|18–369|07.

The Bhāṣya opens by explicitly returning from the preceding excursus on mundane and supramundane paths:

> `idaṃ tu vaktavyam / atha kṣayajñānānantaraṃ kim utpadyate`

“What, then, arises immediately after knowledge of exhaustion?”

The passage therefore resumes the Aśaikṣa sequence established at VAK 6.45.

---

## 1. The Question: What Follows Kṣayajñāna?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / exposition

VAK 6.45 established that immediately after the final diamond-like uninterrupted path (`vajropama-samādhi`) there arises the final liberation-path, namely the first `kṣayajñāna`.

VAK 6.50 now asks what follows that first knowledge of exhaustion.

```text
vajropama-samādhi
    final ānantarya-mārga
        ↓
first kṣayajñāna
    final vimukti-mārga
        ↓
what cognition follows?
```

The answer depends upon the kind of Arhat who has been produced.

---

## 2. The Immovable Arhat: Kṣayajñāna → Anutpādajñāna

The kārikā states:

> `yady akopyaḥ kṣayajñānād anutpādamatiḥ`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `akopyadharmā ced arhan bhavati kṣayajñānāt samanantaram anutpādajñānam asyotpadyate`

If the Arhat is **akopyadharman**, of an immovable nature, then immediately after knowledge of exhaustion there arises **knowledge of non-arising** (`anutpādajñāna`).

```text
akopyadharman Arhat
    ↓
kṣayajñāna
    ↓
anutpādajñāna
```

The distinction is precise.

`kṣayajñāna` knows exhaustion as accomplished.

`anutpādajñāna` knows that what has been exhausted will not arise again.

The second knowledge therefore adds a determination unavailable where falling away remains possible.

---

## 3. The Non-Immovable Arhat

The verse continues:

> `na cet / kṣayajñānam aśaikṣī vā dṛṣṭiḥ`

The Bhāṣya:

> `na ced akopyadharmā bhavati kṣayajñānāt kṣayajñānam evotpadyate aśaikṣī vā samyagdṛṣṭiḥ`

If the Arhat is not immovable, then after the first knowledge of exhaustion there arises either:

1. another `kṣayajñāna`, or
2. `aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi`, right view belonging to one beyond training.

```text
non-akopyadharman Arhat
        ↓
first kṣayajñāna
        ↓
   ┌───────────────┐
   │               │
kṣayajñāna     aśaikṣī
again           samyagdṛṣṭi
```

The Bhāṣya then gives the decisive reason:

> `na tv anutpādajñānaṃ parihāṇisaṃbhavāt`

But **knowledge of non-arising does not arise**, because falling away (`parihāṇi`) remains possible.

This is the doctrinal center of the verse.

```text
possibility of falling away
    ↓
non-arising cannot yet be known
```

`anutpādajñāna` is therefore not merely confidence or expectation. It requires a path-status in which renewed loss is excluded.

---

## 4. Does Aśaikṣī Samyagdṛṣṭi Fail to Arise in the Immovable Arhat?

The Bhāṣya immediately raises an objection:

> `kiṃ punar akopyadharmaṇaḥ sā naivotpadyate /`

Does that aśaikṣa right view, then, not arise at all for one of immovable nature?

**Voice:** Objector / questioner

The kārikā answers:

> `sarvasya sā 'rhataḥ`

It belongs to **every Arhat**.

This corrects any overly exclusive reading of the preceding alternatives.

The point is not:

```text
movable Arhat    → aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi
immovable Arhat  → no aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi
```

Rather:

```text
aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi
    belongs to every Arhat
```

The differentiating cognition is `anutpādajñāna`, not aśaikṣa right view as such.

---

## 5. What Can Follow Anutpādajñāna?

The Bhāṣya further clarifies:

> `akopyadharmaṇo 'py anutpādajñānāt kadācid anutpādajñānam evotpadyate kadācid aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭiḥ`

For the immovable Arhat, after a knowledge of non-arising there may arise:

1. another knowledge of non-arising, or
2. aśaikṣa right view.

```text
akopyadharman Arhat

kṣayajñāna
    ↓
anutpādajñāna
    ↓
┌──────────────────────┐
│                      │
anutpādajñāna       aśaikṣī
again                samyagdṛṣṭi
```

Thus the verse does not describe one rigid terminal cognition. It classifies the possible Aśaikṣa cognitions according to whether the liberated continuum is still capable of falling away.

---

## 6. Three Aśaikṣa Cognitions Distinguished

The passage forces a precise distinction among three kinds of cognition.

### Kṣayajñāna — Knowledge of Exhaustion

Knowledge that the relevant afflictive task has been completed.

```text
āsravas exhausted
    ↓
exhaustion known
```

### Anutpādajñāna — Knowledge of Non-Arising

Knowledge that the exhausted afflictions will not arise again.

```text
exhaustion accomplished
    +
irreversibility established
        ↓
anutpādajñāna
```

### Aśaikṣī Samyagdṛṣṭi — Right View Beyond Training

Right view belonging to the Arhat as one for whom no further liberating training remains.

This belongs to every Arhat, movable or immovable.

The classes therefore overlap rather than forming three successive universal stages.

```text
Every Arhat
    → kṣayajñāna
    → aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi possible

Only akopyadharman Arhat
    → anutpādajñāna
```

---

## 7. Completion and Irreversibility Are Not the Same Determination

This is the strongest conceptual result of the passage.

An Arhat may have genuinely attained exhaustion while still belonging to a class for whom falling away is possible.

Therefore:

```text
kṣaya
    ≠
anutpāda
```

and correspondingly:

```text
kṣayajñāna
    ≠
anutpādajñāna
```

The first determines **completion**.

The second determines **irreversibility**.

The Kośa therefore refuses to collapse achieved liberation, cognition of achieved liberation, and cognition of irreversible liberation into one undifferentiated state.

---

## 8. The Ground: Parihāṇi-Saṃbhava

The explanatory ground is exceptionally concise:

> `parihāṇisaṃbhavāt`

“because falling away is possible.”

This is a genuine inferential restriction on what can count as knowledge.

```text
If relapse remains possible,
then non-arising is not established.

If non-arising is not established,
there can be no anutpādajñāna.
```

The Bhāṣya does not define `anutpādajñāna` psychologically, as certainty or conviction. Its possibility is controlled by the objective path-status of the practitioner.

That is important for the Kośa's epistemology: **what one can know is constrained by what has actually been accomplished in the path-continuum.**

---

## 9. Relation to VAK 6.45

VAK 6.45 established:

```text
final bhavāgra obstruction
    ↓
vajropama-samādhi
    ↓
first kṣayajñāna
    ↓
Arhat / Aśaikṣa
```

VAK 6.50 now differentiates what happens *within* that completed Aśaikṣa condition:

```text
Aśaikṣa established
    ↓
what kind of Arhat?

non-immovable
    → kṣayajñāna / aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi

immovable
    → anutpādajñāna
      → anutpādajñāna / aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi
```

So `aśaikṣa` names completion of training, while `akopyadharman` adds the determination of immovability.

These must not be conflated.

---

## 10. Restrained Organon Contact

The Bhāṣya gives a particularly clean reflected structure:

```text
exhaustion
    ↓
knowledge of exhaustion
    ↓
knowledge of non-arising
```

But the third determination is not universal merely because the second has occurred. It requires an additional objective condition: impossibility of falling away.

Hence the mediation is not simply conceptual embellishment.

```text
accomplished result
    → known result
    → known irreversible result
```

The last transition occurs only when the result itself has acquired the corresponding stability.

This is exactly the kind of distinction the Rational Kośa must preserve: cognition follows determinate accomplishment rather than manufacturing its object through subjective assurance.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **akopyadharman** — “of immovable nature”; an Arhat whose liberation cannot be shaken by falling away.
  - **status:** introduced here as the decisive classification governing the post-`kṣayajñāna` sequence; elaborated later in the chapter.
  - **function:** determines eligibility for `anutpādajñāna`.

- **anutpādajñāna** — knowledge of non-arising.
  - **status:** central Aśaikṣa cognition introduced explicitly here.
  - **function:** knows that the exhausted afflictions will not arise again.

- **parihāṇi** — falling away, loss, relapse from an attained state.
  - **status:** doctrinal control on the distinction between Arhat types.
  - **function:** its possibility excludes `anutpādajñāna`.

- **aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi** — right view belonging to one beyond training.
  - **status:** differentiated here from both `kṣayajñāna` and `anutpādajñāna`.
  - **function:** Aśaikṣa right view common to every Arhat.

- **samanantara** — immediately following, without an intervening cognition.
  - **status:** familiar path-sequence vocabulary.
  - **function:** marks the precise temporal/genetic succession of Aśaikṣa cognitions.

---

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — principal exposition of which cognition follows `kṣayajñāna` and why `anutpādajñāna` is restricted to the immovable Arhat.

**Voice: Objector / questioner** — `kiṃ punar akopyadharmaṇaḥ sā naivotpadyate?`, asking whether aśaikṣa right view therefore fails to occur in the immovable Arhat.

**Doctrinal caution:** VAK 6.50 does **not** say that only movable Arhats possess aśaikṣa right view. The Bhāṣya explicitly says it belongs to every Arhat. Nor does `aśaikṣa` itself imply `akopya`: completion of training and impossibility of falling away are distinct determinations.

---

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.50 resumes the Aśaikṣa sequence after the excursus on mundane and supramundane paths. Immediately after the first `kṣayajñāna`, the ensuing cognition depends upon the Arhat's stability.

```text
first kṣayajñāna
    ↓
Is the Arhat akopyadharman?

YES
    → anutpādajñāna
    → thereafter anutpādajñāna
       or aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi

NO
    → kṣayajñāna again
       or aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi
    → no anutpādajñāna
      because parihāṇi remains possible
```

The fundamental distinction is therefore:

```text
completion
    kṣayajñāna

irreversibility
    anutpādajñāna
```

Every Arhat is Aśaikṣa, but not every Arhat is immovable. The Kośa consequently distinguishes having completed the path from knowing that the completed liberation is incapable of reversal.
