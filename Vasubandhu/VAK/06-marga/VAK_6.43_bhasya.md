# VAK 6.43 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**tad-pāñcavidhyāt pañcaiva śuddhāvāsopapattayaḥ |**  
**nirodha-lābhy anāgāmī kāya-sākṣī punar mataḥ || 6.43 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 363|09–364|03.

## 1. Fivefold Mixed Cultivation and the Five Pure Abodes

Vasubandhu first completes the explanation of the mixed cultivation discussed in the preceding verse.

> sā hi vyavakīrṇabhāvanā pañcaprakārā mṛdumadhyādhimātrataratamabhedāt /

The mixed cultivation is fivefold according to progressively differentiated degrees beginning from mild and proceeding through stronger grades.

The Bhāṣya then gives the moment-count for each grade:

```text
first grade:
    3 cittas
    anāsrava → sāsrava → anāsrava

second grade:
    6 cittas

third grade:
    9 cittas

fourth grade:
    12 cittas

fifth grade:
    15 cittas
```

The key point is not merely numerical multiplication. The five grades preserve the same mixed architecture while extending its cultivated scope.

## 2. Five Pure-Abode Results

The corresponding fruits are the five Pure Abodes:

> tāsāṃ yathāsaṃkhyaṃ pañca śuddhāvāsāḥ phalam /

Thus the fivefold differentiation of mixed cultivation maps in order onto the five Pure-Abode rebirths.

Vasubandhu then specifies the causal component responsible for rebirth there:

> yat tatra sāsravaṃ tad-vaśāt teṣūpapattiḥ /

It is through the force of the contaminated component within the mixed cultivation that rebirth in those Pure Abodes occurs.

This is important. The anāsrava component belongs to liberative cultivation, but rebirth as such still requires a sāsrava causal basis.

A reported alternative explains the five Pure Abodes through predominance of the faculties beginning with faith:

> śraddhādīndriyādhikyāt pañcety apare /

**Voice:** reported alternative.

The Bhāṣya does not develop this alternative here.

## 3. The Non-Returner Who Has Attained Cessation

The kārikā then introduces another designation:

> nirodhalābhy anāgāmī kāyasākṣī punar mataḥ

Vasubandhu glosses:

> nirodhalābho 'syāstīti nirodhalābhī /

A **nirodhalābhin** is one who possesses the attainment of cessation.

More precisely:

> yo hi kaścid anāgāmī nirodhasamāpattilābhī sa kāyasākṣīty ucyate /

Any non-returner who has attained the **nirodha-samāpatti** is called a **kāyasākṣin**, a bodily witness.

So the definition is:

```text
anāgāmin
    +
attainment of nirodha-samāpatti
    =
kāyasākṣin
```

## 4. Why “Bodily Witness”?

The initial explanation is:

> nirvāṇasadṛśasya dharmasya kāyena sākṣātkaraṇāt /

The practitioner directly realizes, through the body, a dharma resembling nirvāṇa.

But the Bhāṣya immediately raises the problem:

> kathaṃ punaḥ kāyena sākṣātkaroti /

How can one directly realize it through the body?

The first answer appeals to the absence of mind and the arising supported by the body:

> cittābhāvāt kāyāśrayotpatteḥ /

Because mind is absent and the occurrence is supported by the body.

Yet Vasubandhu does not leave the matter there. He says:

> evaṃ tu bhavitavyam /

“It should rather be understood in this way.”

This marks a corrective clarification.

## 5. Vasubandhu’s Retrospective Explanation

Upon emerging from the cessation attainment, the practitioner obtains a previously unexperienced bodily tranquility accompanied by cognition:

> sa hi tasmād vyutthāyāpratilabdhapūrvāṃ savijñānakāṃ kāyaśāntiṃ pratilabhate /

The practitioner then recognizes:

> śāntā vata nirodhasamāpattir nirvāṇasadṛśī vata nirodhasamāpattir iti /

“How peaceful indeed is the attainment of cessation; how like nirvāṇa indeed is the attainment of cessation.”

Thus the peacefulness of cessation is said to have been bodily realized:

> evam anena tasyāḥ śāntatvaṃ kāyena sākṣātkṛtaṃ bhavati /

The crucial point is that the witnessing is not ordinary cognition occurring inside nirodha-samāpatti. Cognition is absent there. The realization is established through the transformed bodily condition and the cognition that occurs upon emergence.

The sequence is therefore:

```text
entry into nirodha-samāpatti
    ↓
cognition absent
    ↓
cessation supported by bodily continuum
    ↓
emergence
    ↓
previously unattained bodily tranquility with cognition
    ↓
recognition of cessation as peaceful and nirvāṇa-like
    ↓
“bodily witnessing”
```

This avoids turning kāyasākṣātkriyā into a hidden cognitive event during cessation itself.

## 6. What Counts as Sākṣātkriyā?

Vasubandhu gives a useful general formulation:

> prāptijñānasākṣātkriyābhyāṃ pratyakṣīkāro hi sākṣātkriyā /

Direct realization (*sākṣātkriyā*) is making something directly present through attainment and knowledge.

This is important for the local epistemology of the passage. Direct realization need not mean a single homogeneous act of conscious perception. It can involve:

```text
attainment
    +
knowledge
    =
pratyakṣīkāra / sākṣātkriyā
```

In the present case the attainment itself occurs without cognition, while the knowledge belongs to the emergence from it.

## 7. Why Kāyasākṣin Is Not a Separate Śaikṣa Class

The Bhāṣya asks why the kāyasākṣin is not listed separately in the sūtra that enumerates eighteen trainees.

The answer is that those trainee classifications are based on the three uncontaminated trainings and their fruits:

> anāsravās tisraḥ śaikṣās tatphalaṃ ca /

The nirodha-samāpatti, however, is neither specifically a training nor non-training fruit:

> nirodhasamāpattiś ca naiva śaikṣānāśaikṣā phalam /

Therefore possession of it does not establish another independent śaikṣa class.

So **kāyasākṣin** is a cross-cutting qualification of a non-returner, not another basic fruit-position.

## 8. Coarse and Fine Classification

Vasubandhu closes this section by saying that the preceding account gives only the coarse distinctions among non-returners:

> eṣa tāvad anāgāmināṃ yathāsthūlaṃ bhedaḥ /

When differentiated finely, they divide into thousands:

> sūkṣmaṃ tu bhidyamānāḥ sahasraśo bhidyante /

He gives examples of variables that multiply the classification:

- mild / medium / strong faculties,
- level differences,
- six lineage-types beginning with *parihāṇadharman*,
- sixteen station-differences,
- thirty-six differences of level-detachment.

This confirms that the named path-persons are not a closed set of essences. They are coarse projections over a much more highly parameterized causal field.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.43 adds two distinct determinations.

First:

```text
five grades of mixed cultivation
    ↓
five Pure-Abode results

with:
    sāsrava component
        → rebirth-causal function
```

Second:

```text
anāgāmin
    + nirodha-samāpatti attainment
        ↓
kāyasākṣin
```

The second determination is particularly important because realization is distributed across a sequence rather than confined to a single conscious instant:

```text
attainment without cognition
    → emergence
    → bodily tranquility
    → knowing recognition
    → sākṣātkriyā
```

The cognitive connection is therefore not erased by nirodha; it is articulated across entry, absence, emergence, and retrospective knowledge.

## New Technical Terms

- **nirodhalābhin** — one possessing the attainment of cessation.
  - **status:** newly foregrounded here.
  - **function:** qualification defining the bodily witness.

- **nirodhasamāpatti** — attainment of cessation in which cognition is absent.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinally, newly central to path-person classification here.
  - **function:** meditative attainment defining kāyasākṣin.

- **kāyasākṣin** — bodily witness; a non-returner who has attained cessation.
  - **status:** new path-person designation in this sequence.
  - **function:** cross-cutting qualification of the anāgāmin.

- **sākṣātkriyā** — direct realization or making-present through attainment and knowledge.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** epistemic definition explaining bodily witness.

- **savijñānaka kāyaśānti** — bodily tranquility accompanied by cognition after emergence from cessation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory phrase.
  - **function:** mediates between noncognitive cessation and subsequent recognition.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu for the main explanation of kāyasākṣin and the retrospective account of bodily witnessing.

**Voice:** reported alternative for the view that the five Pure Abodes derive from predominance of the faculties beginning with faith.

The phrase *cittābhāvāt kāyāśrayotpatteḥ* gives an initial explanation of bodily witnessing, but Vasubandhu immediately refines it with *evaṃ tu bhavitavyam*. The stronger reading is therefore the retrospective one: cessation itself is noncognitive; the bodily witness is established through the attained bodily tranquility and subsequent cognition upon emergence.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.43 completes the fivefold mixed-cultivation schema and introduces the bodily witness.

```text
mixed cultivation:
    five grades
        → five Pure Abodes

within each mixed cultivation:
    sāsrava component
        → rebirth in corresponding Pure Abode
```

For the second half:

```text
anāgāmin
    + nirodha-samāpatti
        → kāyasākṣin
```

The most important Bhāṣya clarification is:

```text
nirodha-samāpatti
    is not conscious witnessing during cessation

rather:
    noncognitive cessation
        → emergence
        → unprecedented bodily tranquility
        → cognitive recognition
        → realization of cessation as peaceful and nirvāṇa-like
```

Thus *sākṣātkriyā* can be temporally articulated through attainment and knowledge rather than compressed into one simultaneous cognitive event.
