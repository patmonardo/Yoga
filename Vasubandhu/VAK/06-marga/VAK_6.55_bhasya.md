# VAK 6.55 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> kāme trayāptir antyasya triṣu nordhvaṃ hi dṛkpathaḥ /  
> asaṃvegād iha vidhā tatra niṣṭheti cāgamāt // VAK_6.55 //

**Best current translation:**  
The first three [fruits] are attained in the desire domain; the final one is attained in all three domains. For the path of seeing does not arise above the desire domain. In the form domain this is because there is no *saṃvega*, and because scripture says: “Here is the commencement; there is the completion.”

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.55.md`](./VAK_6.55.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 372|01–372|15.

The Bhāṣya turns from the Dharma-wheel discussion of VAK 6.54 to a precise question about the domains in which the fruits of śrāmaṇya can be attained. The prose importantly distinguishes the reasons why the path of seeing does not arise in the formless and form domains. It also settles the compressed expression *iha vidhā tatra niṣṭhā* by explicitly defining *vidhā* as the commencement of the Path.

## 1. In Which Domains Are the Fruits Attained?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `atha kasmin dhātau kati śrāmaṇyaphalāni prāpyante`

In which domain are how many fruits of śrāmaṇya attained?

The first answer is:

> `kāme trayāptiḥ`

and the prose explains:

> `kāmadhātāv eva trayāṇāṃ śrāmaṇyaphalānāṃ prāptir nānyatra`

The first three fruits are attained only in the desire domain, not elsewhere.

The second determination is:

> `antyasya triṣu`

The final fruit is Arhatship:

> `antyaṃ śrāmaṇyaphalam arhatvaṃ tasya triṣu dhātuṣu prāptiḥ`

and it can be attained in all three domains.

Thus the distribution is asymmetrical:

```text
first three fruits
    desire domain only

Arhatship
    desire domain
    form domain
    formless domain
```

This already shows that the domain in which the Path begins need not be the domain in which its final completion occurs.

## 2. Why the First Two Fruits Are Not Attained Above

For the first two fruits Vasubandhu says the explanation is straightforward:

> `phaladvayasya tāvad avītarāgaprāpyatvād ūrdhvam aprāptir yuktā`

The first two fruits are attained by persons who have not yet become dispassionate with respect to the desire domain. Hence their non-attainment above the desire domain is intelligible.

The issue is therefore not yet difficult.

```text
first two fruits
    ↓
attained while desire-domain passion remains relevant
    ↓
not attained above the desire domain
```

The difficulty concerns the third fruit.

## 3. Why Is the Third Fruit Also Not Attained Above?

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `tṛtīyasya tu kasmād aprāptiḥ`

Why is the third fruit, the fruit of non-returning, also not attained above?

The kārikā answers:

> `nordhvaṃ hi dṛkpathaḥ`

The prose makes the meaning explicit:

> `ūrdhvaṃ hi kāmadhātor darśanamārgo nāsti`

Above the desire domain there is no path of seeing.

And:

> `na ca tena vinā 'sti vītarāgasyānāgāmiphalaprāptir ity etat kāraṇam`

Without the path of seeing, one who is already dispassionate cannot attain the fruit of non-returning.

The third fruit therefore cannot newly arise above the desire domain because its attainment presupposes the path of seeing, and that path does not originate there.

```text
Anāgāmin fruit
    requires
path of seeing

path of seeing
    does not arise above desire domain

therefore
    Anāgāmin fruit is not first attained there
```

## 4. The Bhāṣya Separates the Formless and Form Domains

The next question is decisive:

> `kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇaṃ tatra darśanamārgo nāsti`

Why, then, is there no path of seeing there?

Vasubandhu does **not** give one undifferentiated answer for all higher existence. He first treats the formless domain, then the form domain.

This corrects the overly compressed impression one might receive from the kārikā alone.

## 5. Why There Is No Path of Seeing in the Formless Domain

For the formless attainments the Bhāṣya says:

> `ārupyeṣu tāvat śravaṇābhāvād adhodhātvanālambanāc ca`

Two reasons are given:

1. absence of hearing — `śravaṇābhāva`
2. failure to take the lower domains as object — `adhodhātvanālambana`

The first point ties path-entry to instruction. In the formless domain there is no auditory reception of Dharma teaching.

The second is cognitive and objective: cognition at that level does not take the lower domains as its object in the way required for the path of seeing.

Thus the exclusion of path-entry from the formless domain is not explained by lack of suffering alone.

```text
formless domain
    ↓
no hearing of Dharma
    +
no required taking of lower domains as object
    ↓
no new arising of path of seeing
```

This is an important refinement over the first-pass formulation.

## 6. Why There Is No Path of Seeing in the Form Domain

The form domain requires a different explanation. The kārikā gives:

> `asaṃvegāt`

The Bhāṣya expands:

> `rūpāvacarā hi pṛthagjanāḥ samāpattimukhasaṅgā duḥkhavedanābhāvāc ca na saṃvijante`

Ordinary beings of the form domain do not experience *saṃvega* because they are attached to the modes or gateways of meditative attainment and because painful feeling is absent.

The phrase `samāpattimukhasaṅgā` is dense. It should be kept close to the text: these beings are attached to the entrances, modes, or avenues of *samāpatti*. The basic point is that their contemplative condition itself does not produce the urgent disturbance required for entry into the noble Path.

The prose continues:

> `na ca vinā saṃgenāryamārgaḥ śakto labdhum`

The e-text reads `saṃgenā`, but the argumentative context clearly concerns *saṃvega*. This should not be silently normalized; the intended sense is that without the relevant urgency or spiritual shock the noble Path cannot be obtained.

So the structure is:

```text
form-domain ordinary beings
    ↓
attachment to samāpatti modes
    +
absence of painful feeling
    ↓
no saṃvega
    ↓
no initial acquisition of noble Path
```

This is more precise than simply saying that the higher realm is “too calm.” The issue is a determinate lack of the motivating and disclosive condition required for path-entry.

## 7. Saṃvega Is a Condition of Path-Origination

The Bhāṣya's reasoning makes *saṃvega* function as a condition for the acquisition of the noble Path.

This is not yet a complete theory of *saṃvega*, but its role is clear:

```text
ordinary existence
    confronted by suffering / instability
        ↓
saṃvega
        ↓
orientation toward liberation
        ↓
acquisition of noble Path
```

The Path is therefore not generated merely by contemplative refinement. A realm can possess profound meditative attainment and still fail to provide the conditions for the initial breakthrough of noble seeing.

This distinction is especially important for Chapter 6 as Mārga:

```text
samāpatti
    ≠ by itself
mārga
```

Meditative attainment can provide a basis or field for further development, but it does not by itself constitute the genetic beginning of the noble Path.

## 8. Vasubandhu Marks the Reasoning as Yukti

After explaining the form domain, the Bhāṣya says:

> `iyaṃ tāvad yuktiḥ`

“This, for the moment, is the reasoning.”

Vasubandhu explicitly distinguishes rational explanation from scriptural confirmation.

```text
yukti
    rational explanation

āgama
    scriptural confirmation
```

This is methodologically significant. The doctrinal claim is not supported only by citation; Vasubandhu first supplies a causal-rational account and then adds āgama.

## 9. Scriptural Confirmation: “Here the Commencement, There the Completion”

The Bhāṣya then cites an āgama:

> `pañcānāṃ pudgalānām iha vidhā tatra niṣṭhā antarāparinirvāyiṇo yāvad ūrdhvaṃsrotasa iti`

For five persons, from the intermediate-state attainer of nirvāṇa through the one who goes upstream, “here is the *vidhā*, there is the *niṣṭhā*.”

The five are types of non-returners whose path begins here and whose final completion occurs above.

This quotation is the textual control on the kārikā's compressed phrase:

> `iha vidhā tatra niṣṭhā`

The decisive point is that the Bhāṣya itself explains *vidhā*.

## 10. Vidhā Means the Commencement of the Path

The prose states:

> `vidhā hi mārgārambho nirvāṇopāyatvāt`

*Vidhā* is the **commencement of the Path**, because the Path is the means to nirvāṇa.

This resolves the principal lexical uncertainty in the Kārikā file.

The translation should therefore not leave *vidhā* as merely “institution,” “prescription,” or an unspecified “undertaking.” In this passage its operative meaning is explicitly:

```text
vidhā
    = mārgārambha
    = commencement of the Path
```

And *niṣṭhā* stands opposed to it as completion or culmination.

```text
iha
    here, in the desire domain
        ↓
vidhā
    Path begins
        ↓
movement through higher existence
        ↓
tatra
    there
        ↓
niṣṭhā
    completion
```

This is the strongest determination supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 11. Beginning and Completion Belong to Different Grounds

VAK 6.55 therefore establishes a very precise distinction:

```text
ground of origination
    desire domain

ground of continuation / completion
    may be higher
```

The Path of seeing is locally conditioned in its origination, while Arhatship is not restricted to the same domain.

The universal efficacy of the Path does not mean that its genesis is placeless.

This gives Chapter 6 a genuine Mārga determination:

```text
Path
    has a determinate beginning
    can continue beyond that beginning
    can reach completion on another ground
```

The distinction is not accidental geography. It belongs to the structure of liberation itself.

## 12. Translation Consequence

The first-pass translation read:

> “The attainment of the first three occurs in the desire domain; that of the final one occurs in all three. For the path of seeing does not exist above, because of the absence of spiritual urgency, and because scripture says: the undertaking is here, the completion there.”

The Bhāṣya requires two refinements.

First, the reasons must be distributed correctly:

```text
formless domain
    no hearing
    no taking lower domains as required object

form domain
    no saṃvega
```

Second, *vidhā* is explicitly defined as `mārgārambha`.

Therefore the stabilized translation is:

> **The first three [fruits] are attained in the desire domain; the final one is attained in all three domains. For the path of seeing does not arise above the desire domain. In the form domain this is because there is no *saṃvega*, and because scripture says: “Here is the commencement [of the Path]; there is the completion.”**

The kārikā alone compresses the distinction between the two higher domains; the prose supplies the full causal differentiation.

## 13. Restrained Organon Contact

The strongest conceptual result is not simply “contradiction produces enlightenment.” Vasubandhu's structure is more disciplined.

The noble Path has conditions of genesis that are not identical with the conditions of its completion.

```text
hearing / objective orientation / saṃvega
    ↓
Path can begin

once begun
    ↓
Path can continue on grounds
that could not themselves originate it
```

This gives a clean distinction between **genetic ground** and **ground of fulfillment**.

A higher contemplative state may preserve and complete a movement whose principle it could not originate. In Organon terms, this is a useful example of mediation retaining its dependence on its generated beginning rather than retrospectively treating the completed state as self-grounding.

But the Bhāṣya's own categories should remain primary here: hearing, object, *saṃvega*, path-beginning, and completion.

## New Technical Terms

- **śravaṇābhāva** — absence of hearing  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term  
  **function:** one reason the path of seeing does not newly arise in the formless domain

- **adhodhātvanālambana** — not taking the lower domains as object  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory compound  
  **function:** second reason for exclusion of the path of seeing from the formless domain

- **saṃvega** — urgency, spiritual shock, or existential alarm that impels entry into the noble Path  
  **status:** made technically operative here by the Bhāṣya  
  **function:** necessary condition of initial path-acquisition in the explanation of the form domain

- **samāpattimukhasaṅga** — attachment to the entrances, modes, or avenues of meditative attainment  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression in this passage  
  **function:** helps explain why form-domain ordinary beings do not experience *saṃvega*

- **yukti** — reasoning / rational explanation  
  **status:** recurrent Bhāṣya methodological term  
  **function:** marks Vasubandhu's rational explanation before appeal to scripture

- **āgama** — transmitted scriptural authority  
  **status:** recurrent  
  **function:** confirms the doctrinal conclusion after the rational explanation

- **vidhā** — commencement of the Path  
  **status:** obscure in the Kārikā, explicitly defined in the Bhāṣya  
  **function:** names the beginning of the liberating procedure

- **mārgārambha** — commencement / beginning of the Path  
  **status:** Bhāṣya gloss on *vidhā*  
  **function:** resolves the kārikā's compressed terminology

- **niṣṭhā** — completion, culmination  
  **status:** present in the Kārikā and scriptural citation  
  **function:** opposed to *vidhā* as the completion of a Path begun elsewhere

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

- **Vasubandhu:** asks the domain-distribution questions, supplies the rational reasons, distinguishes form from formless existence, and explains *vidhā* as *mārgārambha*.
- **Āgama citation:** supplies the formula “here the commencement, there the completion” for five types of non-returner.
- **Source caution:** the e-text at 372|12–13 reads `na ca vinā saṃgenāryamārgaḥ śakto labdhum`. The immediate argument is about *saṃvega*, so the reading should be treated cautiously rather than silently emended.

No school controversy is foregrounded in this unit. The structure is principally explanatory: question, rational reason, scriptural confirmation, lexical clarification.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.55 distinguishes the **place where the noble Path can begin** from the broader range in which it can be completed. The first three fruits are attained only in the desire domain, while Arhatship can be attained in all three domains. The path of seeing does not newly arise above the desire domain, but the reasons differ: in the formless domain there is no hearing and the requisite lower-domain object is absent; in the form domain ordinary beings lack *saṃvega*, owing to their attachment to meditative attainment and absence of painful feeling.

The Bhāṣya then settles the compressed scriptural formula:

```text
iha vidhā
    here: commencement of the Path

tatra niṣṭhā
    there: completion
```

The decisive Mārga structure is therefore:

```text
determinate conditions of Path-entry
    ↓
Path begins in desire domain
    ↓
Path can continue beyond its originating ground
    ↓
final completion can occur in any of the three domains
```

The higher ground can complete the Path without being capable of originating the path of seeing itself.
