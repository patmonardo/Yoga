# VAK 6.47 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> bhavāgrādhavimuktordhvajātavattvasamanvayaḥ /  
> anāsraveṇa vairāgyam anāgāmyena sarvataḥ // VAkK_6.47 //

**Best current translation:**  
As in the case of one liberated from half the grades of bhavāgra, or one born above [a lower level], there is no renewed possession [of the abandoned afflictions]. By the uncontaminated path based on anāgamya, dispassion can be attained from every level, up through bhavāgra.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.47.md`](./VAK_6.47.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 367|05–367|14.

The natural argument is divided across two functions. The first half of the kārikā completes the objection-resolution begun under VAK 6.46. The second half opens a new question: from which levels can dispassion be attained when the uncontaminated path is based on `anāgamya`?

### Source caution

The reference e-text gives the compressed kārikā reading `bhavāgrādhavimukta...`, but the Bhāṣya prose explicitly explains the example as:

> `bhavāgrād ardha-prakāra-vimuktasya`

—“of one liberated from half the grades of bhavāgra.”

This strongly suggests that the kārikā text should be understood with `ardha` in the opening compound, or that the transmitted e-text has suffered compression/corruption. This is recorded as an editorial hypothesis, not silently normalized.

## 1. The First Half Completes VAK 6.46

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The preceding position had argued that if supramundane dispassion did not also generate a mundane attainment of separation, then loss of the supramundane attainment during change of faculties would entail renewed possession of the previously abandoned kleśas.

Vasubandhu rejects that inference by counterexample.

The kārikā gives:

> `bhavāgrādhavimuktordhvajātavattvasamanvayaḥ`

The prose explains the first case:

> `bhavāgrād ardha prakāravimuktasya asatyām api tu laukikyāṃ tadvisaṃyogaprāptau tyaktāyām api cendriyasaṃcāreṇa lokottarāyāṃ na punastaiḥ kleśaiḥ samanvāgamo bhavati`

For one liberated from **half the grades of bhavāgra**, there is no mundane attainment of separation. Even if the supramundane attainment is relinquished through change of faculties, the person does **not** thereby come once again to possess those afflictions.

This is decisive because bhavāgra, as established in VAK 6.45, cannot be abandoned by a mundane path at all.

```text
partial bhavāgra abandonment
    ↓
no mundane visaṃyoga-prāpti exists
    ↓
supramundane acquisition can later be relinquished in faculty-change
    ↓
NO renewed possession of the abandoned kleśas
```

The supposed necessity of a duplicate mundane attainment is therefore disproved.

## 2. The Second Counterexample: One Born Above

Vasubandhu then gives the comparison indicated by `ūrdhvajātavat`:

> `yathā ca pṛthagjanasya prathamadhyānabhūmer ūrdhvaṃ jātasya kāmāvacarakleśavisaṃyogaprāptityāgān na punastaiḥ samanvāgamo bhavati`

An ordinary person born above the level of the first dhyāna can relinquish the attainment of separation from desire-realm afflictions without thereby reacquiring those desire-realm afflictions.

Again:

```text
loss of visaṃyoga-prāpti
    ≠
renewed kleśa-samanvāgama
```

Vasubandhu concludes the argument from VAK 6.46:

> `ajñāpakam etat`

“This does not establish [the claim].”

The proposed proof for the necessity of two attainments in every supramundane abandonment fails.

## 3. Translation Consequence: A Significant Correction to the Kārikā Seed

The existing kārikā file cautiously read the first half as referring to one “liberated from what lies below the summit of becoming.” The Bhāṣya does not support that reading.

The prose instead gives a highly specific technical example:

```text
bhavāgrād ardha-prakāra-vimukta
    one liberated from half the grades of bhavāgra
```

This matters doctrinally. The example is chosen precisely because **no mundane abandonment of bhavāgra exists**. It therefore supplies the cleanest counterexample to the thesis that a mundane separation-acquisition must stand behind continued non-possession whenever a supramundane acquisition is lost.

The first-pass translation should therefore eventually be revised to reflect this Bhāṣya-controlled reading.

## 4. A New Question: From Which Level Can Dispassion Be Produced?

After closing the controversy, Vasubandhu changes topics:

> `katapayā punar bhūmyā kuto vairāgyaṃ bhavati`

“By how many / by which levels, and from what [levels], does dispassion occur?”

The kārikā answers:

> `anāsraveṇa vairāgyam anāgāmyena sarvataḥ`

Vasubandhu glosses the range simply:

> `ābhavāgrāt`

—up through **bhavāgra**.

Thus the uncontaminated path based on **anāgamya** can produce dispassion with respect to every relevant level all the way to the summit of existence.

```text
support:
    anāgamya

path:
    anāsrava

range of abandonment:
    every level
    ↓
    up through bhavāgra
```

## 5. Anāgamya Is a Local Basis with a Universal Path-Range

This is the central new determination of 6.47.

The practitioner does not need to be situated in a fundamental meditative level corresponding to each affliction that is abandoned. The `anāgamya` concentration can serve as the basis of an uncontaminated path whose counteractive range extends through the entire vertical hierarchy of conditioned existence.

This sharply distinguishes:

```text
path-support
    ≠
object / counteractive range of the path
```

The meditative basis is local. The liberating operation is not confined to that locality.

This also reinforces the lesson of 6.45. Mundane paths remain level-bound by their relation to the conditioned hierarchy; the supramundane path is capable of functioning against levels above its immediate meditative support, including bhavāgra.

## 6. The Path Is Not a Mere Cosmological Ascent

The verse therefore blocks a simple spatial or hierarchical interpretation of liberation.

One might imagine:

```text
first dhyāna
    ↓
second dhyāna
    ↓
third dhyāna
    ↓
...
    ↓
bhavāgra
    ↓
liberation
```

But this is not Vasubandhu's path-logic.

The path is organized by **counteractive capacity**, not merely by ascending residence or meditative location.

Anāgamya can ground a supramundane path whose operation reaches beyond the level in which it is based.

```text
meditative basis
    anāgamya
        ↓
 supramundane counteraction
        ↓
 all levels through bhavāgra
```

This is a precise example of the Kośa distinguishing the **condition of operation** from the **scope of operation**.

## 7. Transition to VAK 6.48

The Bhāṣya immediately asks a further question.

If a practitioner relies on a `sāmantaka` to gain dispassion from a lower level, do all liberation-paths arise from that same preliminary concentration just as the uninterrupted paths do?

Vasubandhu answers no, and VAK 6.48 begins the finer determination of when the final liberation-path may arise from either the preliminary concentration or the fundamental dhyāna.

Thus the sequence is:

```text
6.47
which meditative basis can abandon which levels?
    ↓
6.48
from which basis does the final liberation-path arise?
```

## 8. Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.47 has two logically distinct achievements.

First, it completes the argument of 6.46:

```text
technical loss of an attainment
    ≠
reversal of the accomplished abandonment
```

Second, it establishes the reach of anāgamya-based supramundane cultivation:

```text
anāgamya as support
    ↓
anāsrava-mārga
    ↓
vairāgya from any level
    ↓
up through bhavāgra
```

Together these show that the path cannot be understood by simply mapping one acquisition, one meditative level, and one liberated result into a one-to-one correspondence.

The accomplished continuum has a history, and the causal/path relations within that history are more articulated than a simple possession model suggests.

## 9. New Technical Terms

- **ardha-prakāra-vimukta** — liberated from half the grades of a level; here, half the grades of bhavāgra.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification not securely visible in the transmitted kārikā e-text.
  - **function:** counterexample showing that loss of a supramundane attainment does not imply renewed possession of abandoned kleśas.

- **ūrdhvajāta** — born above a given level.
  - **status:** present in compressed form in the kārikā and explicated by the prose.
  - **function:** second counterexample to the inference rejected under VAK 6.46.

- **anāgamya** — the preliminary concentration immediately accessible before the first dhyāna, capable here of supporting an uncontaminated path with very broad counteractive range.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** meditative support for supramundane dispassion through all levels up to bhavāgra.

- **ābhavāgrāt** — up through / as far as bhavāgra.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya prose gloss of `sarvataḥ`.
  - **function:** fixes the maximal range of the anāgamya-based uncontaminated path.

## 10. Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu in the counterexamples, the conclusion `ajñāpakam etat`, and the subsequent explanation of anāgamya-based dispassion.

The first half of the verse should not be detached from the controversy of 6.46. Its doctrinal function is argumentative, not an independent general definition of liberation.

The second half begins a new technical enumeration that continues directly into VAK 6.48.

## 11. Light Organon Contact

The strongest local contact is the distinction between **basis** and **range**.

```text
local determinate support
    ≠
limit of rational counteractive efficacy
```

The anāgamya is not universal because it is spatially or hierarchically highest. It is capable of grounding a supramundane operation whose efficacy exceeds the immediate level of its support.

Equally important, the first half shows that an accomplished negation cannot be reduced to the continued presence of one detachable acquisition-token. The path has generated a transformed relation whose persistence is not exhausted by the persistence of one particular `prāpti`.

That is enough for the Bhāṣya pass; a fuller Organon reconstruction should wait for the complete Aśaikṣa block.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.47 first completes Vasubandhu's rejection of the argument introduced in 6.46. The Bhāṣya clarifies the difficult opening compound through the example of one **liberated from half the grades of bhavāgra**: even though no mundane attainment of separation exists for bhavāgra, relinquishment of the supramundane attainment through change of faculties does not restore the abandoned afflictions. The analogous case of an ordinary person born above the first dhyāna confirms that loss of a separation-attainment does not by itself entail renewed possession of the kleśas.

The verse then begins a new determination. An uncontaminated path based on **anāgamya** can produce dispassion from every level **up through bhavāgra**. The support of the path and the range of its counteractive operation are therefore not identical.

The principal correction to the first-pass kārikā interpretation is substantial:

```text
not:
    liberated from below bhavāgra

but, according to the Bhāṣya:
    liberated from half the grades of bhavāgra
```

This should be carried into the later Super-Kārikā / clarification pass.
