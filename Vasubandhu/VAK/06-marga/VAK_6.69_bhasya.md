# VAK 6.69 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

वीर्यं सम्यक्प्रहाणाख्यमृद्धिपादाः समाधयः ।
प्रधानग्रहणं सर्वे गुणाः प्रायोगिकास्तु ते ॥

*vīryaṃ samyakprahāṇākhyam ṛddhipādāḥ samādhayaḥ /
pradhānagrahaṇaṃ sarve guṇāḥ prāyogikās tu te // VAK 6.69 //*

**Translation:** Vīrya is what is called the correct abandonments, and the bases of accomplishment are samādhis. This is designation by taking the principal factor; all the qualities, however, belong to the application.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 384|01–384|14.

The passage begins by returning to the statement from 6.68 that the smṛtyupasthānas, samyakprahāṇas, and ṛddhipādas have respectively prajñā, vīrya, and samādhi as their operative nature.

## Segmentation and Translation

### 1. The previous reductions are by predominance

*yat tv etad uktaṃ “prajñā-vīrya-samādhi-svabhāvāḥ smṛtyupasthānādaya” iti / atra veditavyam /*

As for what was said—that the establishments of recollection and the others have prajñā, vīrya, and samādhi as their operative nature—this should be understood as follows.

*pradhānagrahaṇenaivam uktam /*

It has been stated in this way by taking the principal factor.

*sarve tu prāyogikā guṇāḥ smṛtyupasthāna-samyakprahāṇarddhipādāḥ /*

But the establishments of recollection, the correct abandonments, and the bases of accomplishment contain all the qualities belonging to application.

This is the decisive clarification of the 37-to-10 reduction. The named practice-complex is not exhausted by the dharma identified as its principal operative nature.

```text
practice-complex
    contains many applied qualities

classification
    takes the principal factor

principal factor
    does not abolish associated factors
```

### 2. Why are the correct abandonments called vīrya?

*kasmād vīryaṃ samyakpradhānam uktam /*

Why is vīrya called the correct exertion/abandonment?

*tena samyak kāya-vāṅ-manāṃsi pradhīyante /*

Because through it body, speech, and mind are correctly directed and exerted.

The Bhāṣya therefore grounds the designation in function. Vīrya is principal because it performs the directing and exertive work characteristic of the samyakprahāṇas.

### 3. Why are the ṛddhipādas samādhis?

*samādhiḥ kasmād ṛddhipāda uktaḥ /*

Why is samādhi called a base of accomplishment?

*tatpratiṣṭhatvāt sarvaguṇasaṃpatteḥ /*

Because the attainment of all qualities is established upon it.

Samādhi is not isolated from the other qualities. It is principal because it is the grounding condition upon which the full attainment of the qualities is established.

### 4. Alternative interpretation rejected

**Voice: Objector / alternative doctrinal position**

*ye tv āhuḥ “samādhir evarddhiḥ pādāś chandādayaḥ” iti /*

Those who say, “Samādhi itself is accomplishment, while aspiration and the others are its bases ...”

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

*teṣāṃ dravyatas trayodaśa bodhipakṣyāḥ prāpnuvanti /*

For them, the Bodhi-aligned factors would become thirteen substantially.

*cchanda-cittayor ādhikyāt sūtraṃ ca virudhyate /*

Because aspiration and mind would have to be added, and this also conflicts with the sūtra.

Vasubandhu then cites the sūtra in which ṛddhi itself is separately described through the exercise of supernormal accomplishment—becoming many after being one, and so forth. This distinguishes ṛddhi from the ṛddhipādas and blocks the proposal that samādhi itself simply is ṛddhi while chanda and the others are separate substantial bases.

The logical consequence of the rejected interpretation would be:

```text
10 underlying dharmas
+ chanda
+ citta
+ additional differentiation implied by the account
= 13 substantial bodhipakṣya factors
```

The reduction is therefore constrained by both functional analysis and scriptural usage.

### 5. Why are the same five called both faculties and powers?

*kasmād indriyāṇy eva balāny uktāni /*

Why are precisely the faculties themselves also called powers?

*mṛdv-adhimātra-bhedād avamardanīyānavamardanīyatvāt /*

Because of the difference between weak and intensified degrees: in the former they can be overcome; in the latter they cannot be overcome.

Thus the same five operative dharmas receive two different functional designations according to degree:

```text
weaker degree
    indriya
    liable to being overcome

intensified degree
    bala
    no longer liable to being overcome
```

This is another exact example of one operative dharma receiving different valid names according to its determinate mode of functioning.

### 6. Order of the five faculties

*indriyāṇāṃ kiṅkṛto 'nukramaḥ /*

What determines the order of the faculties?

Vasubandhu gives a genetic sequence:

*śraddadhāno hi phalārthaṃ vīryam ārabhate /*

One who has confidence undertakes exertion for the sake of the fruit.

*ārabdhavīryasya smṛtir upatiṣṭhate /*

For one whose exertion has been undertaken, recollection becomes established.

*upasthitasmṛter avikṣepāc cittaṃ samādhīyate /*

Because one whose recollection is established is not distracted, the mind becomes collected.

*samāhitacitto yathābhūtaṃ prajānāti /*

One whose mind is collected knows according to how things are.

Hence:

```text
śraddhā
    → vīrya
        → smṛti
            → samādhi
                → prajñā
```

This is not merely an enumerative ordering. Each factor supplies the practical condition for the next.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of `pradhānagrahaṇa`, the functional justification of vīrya and samādhi, rejection of the alternative ṛddhipāda analysis, distinction of indriya and bala, and genetic ordering of the five faculties.
- **Voice: Objector / alternative doctrinal position** — `samādhir evarddhiḥ pādāś chandādayaḥ`.
- **Voice: uncertain** — the exact school attribution of this alternative is not specified in the immediate passage and should not be forced.

## New Technical Terms

- **pradhānagrahaṇa** — taking/designating according to the principal factor
  - **status:** introduced explicitly here as the rule governing the preceding reductions
  - **function:** logical distinction between principal operative determination and full practice-complex

- **prāyogika-guṇa** — quality belonging to application/cultivation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā's `sarve guṇāḥ prāyogikāḥ`
  - **function:** prevents reduction of a practice-complex to its principal factor alone

- **avamardanīya / anavamardanīya** — capable / incapable of being overcome
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary in this unit
  - **function:** distinguishes faculty from power by degree of stability

- **anukrama** — ordered sequence
  - **status:** previously common structural term, explicitly queried here
  - **function:** genetic ordering of śraddhā → vīrya → smṛti → samādhi → prajñā

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 6.69 prevents an atomistic reading of 6.67–6.68.

The reduction from thirty-seven named factors to ten operative dharmas is not:

```text
many appearances
    → one hidden substrate
```

It is instead:

```text
concrete practice-complex
    contains a plurality of cooperating qualities
        ↓
classification identifies
    the principal operative determination
        ↓
the whole receives
    a determinate functional designation
```

`pradhānagrahaṇa` therefore expresses **predominance within an internally articulated whole**.

The same principle explains why identical operative dharmas can receive distinct names at different functional intensities:

```text
same five dharmas
    as indriya when weaker
    as bala when intensified
```

The Bhāṣya also gives an explicitly genetic order among the five faculties. Confidence does not merely stand beside exertion; it motivates it. Exertion supports recollection; established recollection prevents distraction and allows samādhi; samādhi enables prajñā to know things as they are.

Thus the system is not simply a taxonomy of coexisting factors. It contains **ordered practical mediation**.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

`pradhānagrahaṇa` should not be rendered as if Vasubandhu were identifying each practice exclusively with one dharma. The force is:

**the practice is named according to its principal factor while all the associated applied qualities remain operative.**

Likewise `sarve guṇāḥ prāyogikās tu te` is philosophically essential, not an afterthought. It explicitly preserves the concrete multiplicity of the practice against reductive identification.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage gives a much stronger logical structure than mere substance-and-mode reduction.

```text
whole practice
    internally differentiated

principal factor
    organizing determination

associated factors
    retained as constitutive conditions

functional name
    expression of the organized whole
```

The unity is therefore not an indifferent substrate beneath predicates. It exists through the organization of determinate functions.

Even more importantly, the sequence

```text
śraddhā → vīrya → smṛti → samādhi → prajñā
```

shows that the determinations do not merely coexist: they **generate the conditions for one another in an ordered Path-process**.

This strongly supports the emerging Organon reading identified in 6.68: the Kośa's unity of Practical Reason is articulated through operative differentiation and genetic mediation rather than through substance-monist reduction. The historical text does not formulate a critique of Spinoza; that comparison belongs strictly to our later reconstruction.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.69 explains the rule underlying the reduction of the thirty-seven Bodhi-aligned factors: the identification of a group with prajñā, vīrya, or samādhi is a `pradhānagrahaṇa`, a designation according to the principal operative factor. All the relevant qualities nevertheless participate in the concrete application. Vasubandhu defends this structure against an alternative account of the ṛddhipādas, distinguishes faculties from powers by degree, and concludes with a genetic ordering of confidence, exertion, recollection, samādhi, and prajñā. The Path is therefore disclosed not as an aggregate of isolated factors but as an internally differentiated and ordered practical whole.
