# VAK 6.04 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> yatra bhinnena tadbuddhir anyāpohe dhiyā ca tat |
> ghaṭārthavat saṃvṛtisat paramārthasad anyathā || 6.4 ||

**Working translation:**  
That in the case of which cognition of it no longer occurs when it is broken,
or when other dharmas are excluded by cognition, is conventionally real, like
the object “pot.” Ultimate reality is otherwise.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. The question of two truths

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

All four truths have been taught by the Blessed One. There are also two truths:
conventional truth and ultimate truth. What are their defining marks?

The two-truth distinction does not replace the Four Truths. It asks under what
mode of reality the objects spoken of in the teaching are established.

### 2. Conventional reality under physical division

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

That in the case of which cognition of it no longer occurs when it is divided
part by part is conventionally real. A pot is the example. Once the pot is
broken into shards, pot-cognition no longer occurs.

```text
pot
    physically divided into shards
        → cognition “pot” ceases
        → conventionally real
```

The pot is not thereby declared sheer non-being. The test concerns whether the
same object-cognition survives the decomposition of the composite.

### 3. Conventional reality under cognitive exclusion

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

That in the case of which cognition of it no longer occurs when other dharmas
are excluded by cognition must likewise be known as conventionally real. Water
is the example. When dharmas such as visible form are cognitively excluded,
water-cognition no longer occurs.

```text
water
    analyzed by excluding its supporting dharmas
        → cognition “water” ceases
        → conventionally real
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives two different tests and examples: physical division
of the pot and cognitive exclusion in the analysis of water.

### 4. Conventional truth remains truth

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The conventional designation is imposed upon those very dharmas. Therefore,
when people say through the force of convention that a pot or water exists,
they speak truth, not falsehood. This is conventional truth.

The passage explicitly blocks the inference:

```text
not ultimately real
    → false or nonexistent
```

Conventional truth is true discourse grounded in dependently configured
dharmas, although its named unity does not survive analysis.

### 5. Ultimate reality

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Ultimate truth is otherwise. Cognition of the object continues even when it is
divided, and also when other dharmas are excluded by cognition. Visible form
is the example. Even when the thing is divided atom by atom, and when other
associated dharmas are cognitively excluded, cognition of visible form's own
nature still occurs. Feeling and the other dharmas are to be understood in the
same way.

```text
rūpa
    divided atom by atom
    or discriminated from associated dharmas
        → cognition of rūpa's svabhāva remains
        → ultimately real
```

It is called ultimate truth because it exists ultimately (`paramārthena
bhāvāt`). This is the Vaibhāṣika analytic criterion presented in the passage;
it should not be generalized into every Buddhist school's account of the two
truths.

### 6. The former teachers' cognitive criterion

**Voice: former teachers, reported by Vasubandhu**

That which is apprehended in the same way by supramundane knowledge, or by the
mundane cognition subsequently acquired from it, is ultimate truth. What is
apprehended otherwise is conventional truth.

This alternative is attributed to `pūrvācāryāḥ`, the former teachers. The
passage reports it; it must not be silently merged with Vasubandhu's preceding
analytic test or presented as unattributed kārikā doctrine.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Four Truths have been taught
    ↓
question of their mode of reality
    ↓
two truths
    saṃvṛti-satya
    paramārtha-satya

named composite loses its cognition under decomposition
    → conventionally real

own-nature cognition survives decomposition and exclusion
    → ultimately real

conventional designation rests on dharmas
    → conventional assertion can still be true
```

## Doctrinal Determination

The criterion is cognitive but not merely subjective. It tests whether a
specific object-cognition remains when the basis is physically or analytically
decomposed.

```text
saṃvṛti-sat
    cognition depends on an assembled configuration

paramārtha-sat
    own-nature cognition survives the relevant analysis
```

The distinction is not equivalent to false versus true. Both are called
truths. Nor does the passage identify paramārtha with one universal Absolute:
its examples are dharmas such as rūpa and vedanā.

## Translation Consequences

`Ghaṭārthavat` is best heard as “like the object or referent ‘pot.’” The
Bhāṣya's water example shows that `anyāpohe dhiyā` is a second criterion, not
merely a restatement of physically smashing the pot.

`Paramārthasad anyathā` is compressed: the ultimately real is “otherwise,”
namely, cognition of its own nature remains under the relevant division and
analytical exclusion.
