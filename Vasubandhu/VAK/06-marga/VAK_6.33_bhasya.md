# VAK 6.33 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**nava-prakārā doṣā hi bhūmau bhūmau tathā guṇāḥ |**  
**mṛdu-madhyādhimātrāṇāṃ punar mṛdv-ādi-bhedataḥ || 6.33 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 355|04–355|22.

Vasubandhu has just closed the classification of practitioners who enter the Noble Path with prior mundane detachment and announces:

> **ānupūrvikaṃ tu vaktavyam** — the sequential case must now be explained.

VAK 6.33 supplies the grading machinery required for that gradual account.

## 1. Nine Grades in Every Level

Vasubandhu begins by recalling that the afflictions of the desire realm have already been taught as ninefold and generalizes the same structure:

> **navaprakārā doṣā hi bhūmau bhūmau**

The faults are of nine kinds **in each level**, extending:

> **yāvad bhavāgre** — as far as the summit of existence.

The same recurrence applies to the qualities that oppose them:

> **tathā guṇāḥ**

These qualities are specifically the path-factors called:

> **ānantarya-vimukti-mārgākhyā guṇāḥ**

— the qualities named uninterrupted paths and liberation paths.

Thus each relevant level contains two ordered series:

```text
nine grades of doṣa
    afflictive factors to be abandoned

nine grades of guṇa
    opposing path-powers
    ānantarya + vimukti paths
```

This is not merely a scale of moral intensity. It is a paired structure of what is to be removed and the path-power by which removal and release are accomplished.

## 2. How Three Becomes Nine

Vasubandhu asks:

> **kathaṃ kṛtvā** — how is this constituted?

There are first three fundamental grades:

> **mṛdu-madhyādhimātrā hi trayo mūlaprakārāḥ**

```text
mṛdu
    mild

madhya
    medium

adhimātra
    strong / intense
```

Each of those three is then itself divided threefold:

> **teṣāṃ punaḥ pratyekaṃ mṛdu-madhyādhimātratvena trividhatvāt nava vyavasthāpyante**

Hence the nine:

```text
1. mṛdu-mṛdu
2. mṛdu-madhya
3. mṛdu-adhimātra

4. madhya-mṛdu
5. madhya-madhya
6. madhya-adhimātra

7. adhimātra-mṛdu
8. adhimātra-madhya
9. adhimātra-adhimātra
```

The important point is recursive qualitative determination:

```text
three primary strengths
    ↓
each internally differentiated threefold
    ↓
nine operative grades
```

## 3. The Surprising Inverse Matching

The Bhāṣya then gives the rule governing path and affliction:

> **mṛdumṛdunā mārgeṇādhimātrādhimātrasya kleśasya prahāṇam**

The **mild-mild path** abandons the **strong-strong affliction**.

And conversely the sequence continues until:

> **adhimātrādhimātreṇa mṛdumṛdoḥ**

The **strong-strong path** abandons the **mild-mild affliction**.

So the two ninefold series run in opposite directions:

```text
Path strength              Affliction removed

mild-mild             →    strong-strong
mild-medium           →    strong-medium
mild-strong           →    strong-mild

medium-mild           →    medium-strong
medium-medium         →    medium-medium
medium-strong         →    medium-mild

strong-mild           →    mild-strong
strong-medium         →    mild-medium
strong-strong         →    mild-mild
```

This is one of the most important technical determinations of the gradual path.

The sequence is therefore **not**:

```text
strongest path first
    destroys strongest affliction first
```

It is instead:

```text
coarsest / strongest affliction first
    removed by the earliest, weakest path

subtlest / weakest affliction last
    removed only by the strongest developed path
```

## 4. Why the Order Must Be Inverse

Vasubandhu gives two connected reasons.

First:

> **ādita evādhimātramārgasaṃbhavāt**

A maximally strong path cannot exist from the very beginning.

Second:

> **utpannādhimātramārgasya cādhimātrakleśāsaṃbhavāt**

Once a maximally strong path has arisen, a maximally strong affliction can no longer coexist with it.

Thus a direct strong-to-strong matching would violate the temporal development of the process.

The path itself has to develop:

```text
early path
    relatively mild
        ↓

repeated cultivation
        ↓

later path
    increasingly strong
```

Meanwhile the afflictions are removed in the opposite order:

```text
coarse / powerful affliction
    removed first
        ↓

progressively subtler residue
        ↓

most subtle affliction
    removed last
```

This makes the ninefold system dynamic rather than merely classificatory.

## 5. Dirt on Cloth

Vasubandhu's first analogy is:

> **audāriko hi malaś celāt pūrvaṃ nirdūyate paścāt sūkṣmaḥ**

From a cloth, coarse dirt is removed first and subtle dirt afterward.

```text
cloth with dirt

coarse stain
    visible and gross
    removed first

subtle stain
    remains after coarse removal
    requires finer cleansing
```

The difficulty of the later task does not arise because the remaining affliction is stronger in magnitude. It arises precisely because it is **subtler**.

This is an important distinction:

```text
strength of affliction
    ≠
difficulty of final removal
```

The path must become stronger as its object of removal becomes subtler.

## 6. Darkness and Light

The second analogy makes the inverse relation still clearer:

> **audārikaṃ ca tamaḥ sūkṣmeṇālokena hanyate sūkṣmaṃ cādhimātreṇa**

Coarse darkness is destroyed by a slight light; subtle darkness is destroyed by a stronger light.

The point is not that weak light is intrinsically more powerful than strong darkness. Rather:

```text
coarse darkness
    is easily exposed
    even by slight illumination

subtle darkness
    persists within increasing illumination
    and requires stronger light for complete removal
```

The analogy converts the nine grades from a static ranking into a theory of progressive discrimination.

## 7. Why Even a Mild Noble Path Can Uproot Ancient Affliction

Vasubandhu then states the governing asymmetry:

> **śuklā hi dharmā balavanto durbalāstu kṛṣṇāḥ**

Wholesome/bright dharmas are strong; dark/unwholesome dharmas are weak.

This explains something otherwise surprising. The afflictions have been nourished through beginningless saṃsāra:

> **anādi-saṃsāra-paraṃparāpyāyita**

Yet even a momentary and mild Noble Path:

> **kṣaṇika-mṛdukenāpy āryamārgeṇa**

can uproot even very strong afflictions:

> **adhimātrāṇāṃ kleśānām unmūlatvāt**

The contrast is striking:

```text
kleśa
    reinforced through beginningless continuity

āryamārga
    may be momentary
    may initially be mild

nevertheless:
    Noble Path uproots the affliction
```

Vasubandhu's claim is therefore not a quantitative competition between accumulated duration and momentary force. The two sides differ in kind and causal power.

He reinforces the point with further analogies: long-accumulated faults can be rapidly removed, just as a small momentary lamp destroys a great mass of darkness.

## 8. A Graded Path Is a Progressive Refinement Process

The Bhāṣya now lets us see why the ninefold machinery is needed.

The gradual practitioner does not simply repeat one identical act nine times.

Instead:

```text
Stage 1
    coarse affliction removed
    by relatively mild path

Stage 2
    remaining field is subtler
    path has become stronger

...

Stage 9
    subtlest residual affliction removed
    by strongest path-grade
```

Thus every successful abandonment transforms the field for the next abandonment.

The sequence has a built-in refinement:

```text
abandonment
    ↓
less afflictive residue
    ↓
more subtle remaining fault
    ↓
stronger and more discriminating path required
    ↓
next abandonment
```

This is the operative method behind the ninefold scale.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.28 gave the basic dyadic operation:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    sever afflictive acquisition
        ↓

vimukti-mārga
    possess the resulting disconnection
```

VAK 6.33 now grades that operation across a whole level:

```text
nine affliction-grades
    paired inversely with
nine path-grades
```

The result is a progressive transformation:

```text
coarse obstruction
    ↓ removed

field becomes more refined
    ↓

subtler obstruction appears as the remaining task
    ↓

path-strength increases
    ↓

final subtle obstruction removed
```

The most important point for the present pass is therefore:

> **The ninefold scheme is not merely a taxonomy of stronger and weaker states. It is a rule for the ordered consumption of affliction by a progressively strengthened path.**

This does not yet establish our later formal reconstruction, but the Bhāṣya itself clearly presents a recursive path-process in which each completed removal determines the next field of work.

## New Technical Terms

- **mūlaprakāra** — fundamental grade or root grade.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term.
  - **function:** names the primary threefold division into mild, medium, and strong.

- **prakāra** — grade, mode, kind.
  - **status:** established classificatory vocabulary, technically intensified here.
  - **function:** marks the nine ordered degrees of both affliction and opposing path-quality.

- **doṣa** — fault, defect; here afflictive factor.
  - **status:** established term.
  - **function:** what is graded ninefold and progressively abandoned.

- **guṇa** — quality, positive factor.
  - **status:** established term, specified here as the opposing path-series.
  - **function:** includes the ninefold uninterrupted and liberation paths that counter the faults.

- **audārika** — coarse, gross.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** characterizes what is removed first despite its greater apparent strength.

- **sūkṣma** — subtle, fine.
  - **status:** established contrast-term.
  - **function:** characterizes the residual affliction removed only by later, stronger path-grades.

- **unmūlana / unmūlatva** — uprooting, eradication from the root.
  - **status:** strong Bhāṣya causal vocabulary.
  - **function:** expresses the power of even a mild momentary Noble Path against deeply reinforced affliction.

- **śukla / kṛṣṇa dharma** — bright/wholesome and dark/unwholesome dharmas.
  - **status:** established evaluative vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains the asymmetry of causal strength between Noble Path and affliction.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the standard Abhidharma grading of the gradual path.

No explicit competing school voice is introduced in this passage.

The statement that bright dharmas are strong and dark dharmas weak should be retained in its local causal context. It explains how a newly arisen Noble Path can uproot afflictions reinforced through beginningless saṃsāra; it should not yet be generalized into a metaphysical doctrine of good and evil.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.33 establishes the machinery for the gradual path:

- in every relevant level, as far as the summit of existence, there are nine grades of affliction;
- the opposing uninterrupted and liberation paths are likewise graded ninefold;
- the nine arise by subdividing mild, medium, and strong each into mild, medium, and strong;
- the two series are paired inversely: the mildest path removes the strongest affliction, while the strongest path removes the mildest and subtlest remaining affliction;
- this order is necessary because the strongest path cannot exist initially, while strong affliction cannot remain once such a path has developed;
- coarse dirt is cleaned before subtle dirt, and coarse darkness is dispelled by slight light while subtle darkness requires stronger illumination;
- even a mild momentary Noble Path can uproot afflictions nourished through beginningless saṃsāra because the bright Dharma-series is causally stronger than the dark afflictive series.

The ninefold scale is therefore a **progressive refinement procedure**: each removal changes the remaining field, and the path grows stronger as the residual obstruction becomes subtler.
