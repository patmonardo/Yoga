# VAK 6.57 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ato 'samayamukto 'sau dṛṣṭiprāptānvayaś ca saḥ /  
> tadgotrā āditaḥ kecit kecid uttāpanāgatāḥ // VAK_6.57 //

**Best current translation:**  
Therefore this [unshakeable Arhat] is liberated independently of occasion, and he belongs to the succession of the one attained through seeing. Some are of their respective disposition from the beginning; others have reached it through the sharpening of the faculties.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.57.md`](./VAK_6.57.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 373|09–374|08.

VAK 6.57 continues the sixfold differentiation of Arhats introduced in 6.56. The first half completes the characterization of the *akopyadharman*: because his liberation does not depend upon a suitable occasion, he is called *asamayavimukta*, and genetically he continues the earlier *dṛṣṭiprāpta* type. The second half then asks whether the six Arhat dispositions are fixed from the beginning. Vasubandhu's answer is no: some are originally of a given disposition, while others arrive at a stronger disposition through the sharpening of the faculties (*indriyāṇām uttāpanā*).

## 1. Why the Unshakeable Arhat Is Asamayavimukta

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The verse begins:

> `ato 'samayamukto 'sau`

The prose explains:

> `ata evāsamayavimukti ucyate`

For precisely this reason he is called one whose liberation is independent of occasion.

The reason comes directly from VAK 6.56. The first five Arhats possess a *sāmayikī vimukti* because the manifestation of concentration depends upon suitable conditions. The unshakeable Arhat does not:

> `sa hy asamayāpekṣāvimuktiś ca`

and:

> `icchātaḥ samādhisaṃmukhībhāvāt`

His concentration can be brought into presence at will.

Thus *asamayavimukta* should not be translated merely as “timelessly liberated.” The contrast is functional:

```text
samayavimukta
    concentration/liberation depends upon suitable occasion

asamayavimukta
    no such dependence
    concentration can be made present at will
```

The Bhāṣya also offers an alternative explanation. The distinction can be understood in relation to final liberation at the end of life: the one susceptible to regression and the unshakeable one are respectively liberated “at a time” and “not at a time,” because regression is possible in the former and impossible in the latter.

The central determination remains non-dependence upon a favorable occasion.

## 2. The Akopyadharman Continues the Dṛṣṭiprāpta Line

The verse adds:

> `dṛṣṭiprāptānvayaś ca saḥ`

The Bhāṣya states:

> `sa cākopyadharmā dṛṣṭiprāptapūrvako veditavyaḥ`

The *akopyadharman* should be understood as one who was formerly a *dṛṣṭiprāpta*.

This completes the genetic pairing established in VAK 6.56:

```text
śraddhādhimukta
    ↓
five condition-dependent Arhat types

dṛṣṭiprāpta
    ↓
akopyadharman
    ↓
asamayavimukta
```

The completed type is therefore not detached from the history of training. The mode of the trainee persists genetically into the form of Arhatship.

## 3. Are the Six Dispositions Fixed from the Beginning?

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `kiṃ punarete ṣaḍarhanta ādita eva tadgotrā bhavanty atha paścāt`

Are these six Arhats of their respective dispositions from the very beginning, or do they become so later?

The kārikā answers:

> `tadgotrā āditaḥ kecit kecid uttāpanāgatāḥ`

Some possess the relevant disposition from the beginning; others reach it through *uttāpana*.

The prose makes the latter term precise.

A person may from the outset possess the disposition of the *cetanādharman*:

> `kaścit prathamata eva cetanādharmagotrako bhavati`

But another may first be a *parihāṇadharman* and then become a *cetanādharman*:

> `kaścit punaḥ parihāṇadharmā bhūtvendriyāṇām uttāpanayā cetanādharmatāṃ gataḥ`

The mechanism is:

> `indriyāṇām uttāpanā`

**the sharpening or intensification of the faculties.**

Vasubandhu then generalizes:

> `evaṃ yāvad akopyadharmatāṃ gato veditavyaḥ`

The same principle is to be understood all the way up to attaining the state of the *akopyadharman*.

Thus the sixfold typology is not a rigid sixfold essence of persons.

```text
initial disposition
    ↓
sharpening of faculties
    ↓
stronger disposition
    ↓
possibly all the way to akopyadharmatā
```

This is the principal clarification supplied by the Bhāṣya to VAK 6.57.

## 4. Gotra Here Is Developmental, Not an Immutable Essence

Because movement among these types is possible, *gotra* in this passage must be handled carefully.

It denotes a disposition, capacity, or spiritual type, but the Bhāṣya explicitly permits transformation through cultivation of the faculties.

The structure is therefore:

```text
gotra
    determinate capacity/disposition

but not necessarily
    immutable personal essence
```

Some possess a given disposition from the beginning; others acquire it by strengthening the faculties.

This is important for the Mārga architecture. Classification records an attained organization of capacities, while the Path can alter that organization.

## 5. Vasubandhu Defines the Six Arhat Types

The prose now explains the six types introduced in VAK 6.56.

### 5.1 Parihāṇadharman

> `parihāṇadharmā yaḥ parihātuṃ bhavyo na cetanādidharmā`

The *parihāṇadharman* is one for whom regression is possible, and who does not possess the stronger capacities beginning with the *cetanādharman* type.

The term therefore marks **susceptibility to regression**.

### 5.2 Cetanādharman

> `cetanādharmā yaś cetayituṃ bhavyaḥ`

The Bhāṣya defines this type through the capacity expressed by *cetayitum*. The immediate Sanskrit supports “one capable of willing/intending” but the precise technical application of this capacity is not further unfolded in this sentence. It is therefore better not to force a broader doctrinal gloss here.

What is certain from the surrounding structure is that this is a stronger disposition than *parihāṇadharman* and one into which the latter may develop through sharpening the faculties.

### 5.3 Anurakṣaṇādharman

> `anurakṣaṇādharmā yo 'nurakṣituṃ bhavyaḥ`

The *anurakṣaṇādharman* is capable of guarding or protecting the attainment.

This connects directly with VAK 6.56, where the condition-dependent liberation of the first five Arhats was said to require continual guarding.

### 5.4 Sthitākampya

> `sthitākampyo yaḥ parihāṇipratyayaṃ balavantam antareṇānurakṣann api sthātuṃ bhavyo na hātuṃ nāpi vardhayituṃ vinābhiyogena`

The *sthitākampya* is capable of remaining stable even without active guarding, unless a powerful condition of regression intervenes. Without deliberate exertion he neither loses the attainment nor advances beyond it.

His defining determination is therefore **stability**.

```text
no special exertion
    ↓
remains where he is

but
    ↓
strong regressive condition may still disturb him
```

### 5.5 Prativedhanābhavya

> `prativedhanābhavyo yo 'kopyaḥ prativeddhaṃ bhavyaḥ`

The fifth type is capable of penetrating through to the unshakeable condition. His distinctive capacity is therefore developmental: he can attain *akopyatā*.

### 5.6 Akopyadharman

> `akopyadharmā yo naiva parihātuṃ bhavyaḥ`

The *akopyadharman* is one for whom regression is no longer possible.

This is the endpoint of the series:

```text
parihāṇadharman
    possibility of regression
        ↓
cetanādharman
        ↓
anurakṣaṇādharman
        ↓
sthitākampya
        ↓
prativedhanābhavya
        ↓
akopyadharman
    impossibility of regression
```

The sequence should not be mistaken for six mandatory chronological stages for every Arhat. Some possess a stronger disposition from the beginning; others reach it through *uttāpana*.

## 6. The Six Differences Are Already Prepared During Training

Vasubandhu then traces the distinctions backward into the *śaikṣa* stage.

The first two types were deficient in two features of practice:

> `prathamau dvau pūrvam eva śaikṣāvasthāyāṃ sātatyasatkṛtyaprayogavikalau`

They lacked both continuous application and intensive/respectful application during training.

The third:

> `tṛtīyaḥ sātatyaprayogī`

practiced continuously.

The fourth:

> `caturthaḥ satkṛtyaprayogī`

practiced intensively / with full application.

The fifth:

> `pañcama ubhayathāprayogī mṛdvindriyas tu`

possessed both forms of application, but had dull faculties.

The sixth:

> `ṣaṣṭha ubhayathāprayogī tīkṣṇendriyaś ca`

possessed both forms of application and sharp faculties.

The completed sixfold classification therefore has a determinate genesis:

```text
                continuity       intensity       faculties

first two          deficient        deficient       —
third              present          —              —
fourth             —                present        —
fifth              present          present        dull
sixth              present          present        sharp
```

The table is schematic rather than exhaustive, but it captures the explicit causal distinctions of the prose.

This is one of the strongest Mārga results in the passage: **the form of completion bears the history of practice within it.**

## 7. Capacity Is Not Necessity

Vasubandhu immediately adds an important qualification:

> `na cāvaśyaṃ parihāṇadharmā parihīyate nāpi yāvat prativedhanābhavyaḥ pratividhyati`

It is not necessary that the *parihāṇadharman* actually regress, nor that the *prativedhanābhavya* actually penetrate to the stronger state.

Why then are the names used?

> `saṃbhavaṃ tu praty evam ucyate`

They are named in this way **with reference to possibility or capacity**.

This is a crucial logical caution:

```text
dharmatā/type
    = what can occur given this capacity

not
    = what must occur
```

The taxonomy is modal, not fatalistic.

A person classified as liable to regression need not regress. A person capable of further penetration need not actually make that transition.

## 8. The Six Types Across the Three Domains

On this interpretation Vasubandhu says:

> `evaṃ kṛtvā dhātutraye 'pi ṣaḍarhanto yujyante`

Understood in terms of capacity, all six kinds of Arhat can be admitted in all three domains.

But if one interprets the names as implying that the stated event must actually occur, the distribution becomes narrower. Then all six occur in the desire domain, whereas in the form and formless domains only the *sthitākampya* and *akopyadharman* would occur, because there is no regression, intentional transition of the relevant sort, or change of faculties there.

Vasubandhu's preferred explanatory move here is therefore again modal:

```text
capacity
    permits six types across all three domains

necessitated event
    would artificially restrict the taxonomy
```

This prepares directly for VAK 6.58, which asks from what exactly these persons can regress: from disposition (*gotra*), from fruit, or both.

## 9. Translation Consequence

The first-pass translation read:

> “Therefore, this one is liberated independently of occasion, and he belongs to the succession of the one who has attained through seeing. Some are of that disposition from the beginning; others have arrived at it through intensification.”

The Bhāṣya allows a more precise rendering of the final term:

> **Therefore this [unshakeable Arhat] is liberated independently of occasion, and he belongs to the succession of the one attained through seeing. Some are of their respective disposition from the beginning; others have reached it through the sharpening of the faculties.**

The phrase “their respective disposition” is important because the question concerns all six Arhat types, not only the *akopyadharman*.

And *uttāpana* is no longer vague “intensification”: the prose specifies **`indriyāṇām uttāpanā`**, sharpening of the faculties.

## 10. Restrained Organon Contact

The strongest conceptual result is developmental rather than taxonomic.

Vasubandhu refuses to let the final classifications become static essences:

```text
initial capacity
    +
form of practice
    +
quality of faculties
        ↓
mode of completed attainment
```

And even after classification, a type expresses **possibility**, not mechanical necessity.

This gives the Mārga a genuinely genetic structure. The completed determination contains the history through which it arose, yet that history is not destiny: faculties can be sharpened and dispositions can change.

The useful Organon contact is therefore modest but exact: a determination is intelligible through its genesis, and its modal power must not be confused with a necessary event.

## New Technical Terms

- **asamayavimukta** — one liberated independently of a suitable occasion  
  **status:** Bhāṣya/Kārikā technical designation continuing VAK 6.56  
  **function:** names the *akopyadharman* through non-dependence upon conditions for bringing concentration into presence

- **icchātaḥ samādhisaṃmukhībhāva** — bringing concentration into presence at will  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory phrase  
  **function:** explains why the unshakeable Arhat is *asamayavimukta*

- **dṛṣṭiprāptapūrvaka** — formerly a *dṛṣṭiprāpta*  
  **status:** Bhāṣya genetic explanation  
  **function:** identifies the training lineage of the *akopyadharman*

- **gotra** — disposition, spiritual capacity, type  
  **status:** recurrent technical term, sharpened here  
  **function:** classifies capacities while remaining open to transformation through cultivation

- **uttāpana** — sharpening, intensification  
  **status:** ambiguous in Kārikā, explicitly specified by Bhāṣya  
  **function:** process by which a person may move into a stronger Arhat disposition

- **indriyāṇām uttāpanā** — sharpening of the faculties  
  **status:** Bhāṣya gloss on the kārikā's *uttāpana*  
  **function:** causal mechanism of change between dispositions

- **parihāṇadharman** — one susceptible to regression  
  **status:** one of the six Arhat types  
  **function:** marks the weakest modal stability among the six

- **cetanādharman** — one possessing the capacity expressed by *cetayitum*  
  **status:** one of the six Arhat types  
  **function:** second type; precise broader doctrinal force should remain cautious in this passage

- **anurakṣaṇādharman** — one capable of guarding the attainment  
  **status:** one of the six Arhat types  
  **function:** third type, defined by protective maintenance

- **sthitākampya** — one capable of remaining stable without continual guarding unless a powerful regressive condition intervenes  
  **status:** one of the six Arhat types  
  **function:** fourth type, defined by stability

- **prativedhanābhavya** — one capable of penetrating through to the unshakeable condition  
  **status:** one of the six Arhat types  
  **function:** fifth type, defined by capacity for further penetration

- **sātatyaprayoga** — continuous application/practice  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term  
  **function:** one training factor explaining later Arhat differentiation

- **satkṛtyaprayoga** — intensive, earnest, or fully applied practice  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term  
  **function:** second training factor explaining later Arhat differentiation

- **mṛdvindriya** — dull or weak faculties  
  **status:** recurrent path term  
  **function:** distinguishes the fifth Arhat type from the sixth despite both forms of application

- **tīkṣṇendriya** — sharp faculties  
  **status:** recurrent path term  
  **function:** together with both forms of application, explains the sixth and unshakeable type

- **saṃbhava** — possibility, capacity for occurrence  
  **status:** ordinary term used with technical force here  
  **function:** prevents the sixfold taxonomy from being interpreted as necessary destiny

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the principal explanatory sequence.

The sixfold list is introduced as scripturally given in the discussion attached to VAK 6.56. VAK 6.57 explains its genetic and modal interpretation.

Two cautions are important:

1. *gotra* must not be treated here as an immutable essence, because Vasubandhu explicitly allows transition by sharpening the faculties.
2. The terse definition of *cetanādharman* (`yaś cetayituṃ bhavyaḥ`) should not be expanded beyond what this passage itself establishes without consulting parallel doctrinal explanations.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.57 completes the characterization of the unshakeable Arhat as *asamayavimukta*: his liberation is independent of favorable occasions, and concentration can be brought into presence at will. Genetically he continues the earlier *dṛṣṭiprāpta* type. The Bhāṣya then rejects a static reading of the six Arhat dispositions. Some persons possess a given *gotra* from the beginning, while others arrive at a stronger type through **the sharpening of the faculties** (*indriyāṇām uttāpanā*), potentially progressing all the way to *akopyadharmatā*. Vasubandhu traces the six differences back into the training stage through continuous practice, intensive practice, and the sharpness of the faculties. Finally, the classifications name capacities and possibilities, not necessary outcomes: the one liable to regression need not actually regress, and one capable of further penetration need not necessarily advance. The completed Path thus preserves its developmental history while remaining genuinely transformable.
