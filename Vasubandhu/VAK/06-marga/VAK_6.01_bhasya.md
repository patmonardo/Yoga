# VAK 6.01 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> kleśa-prahāṇam ākhyātaṃ satya-darśana-bhāvanāt |
> dvividho bhāvanā-mārgo darśanākhyas tv anāsravaḥ || 6.1 ||

**Best translation:**  
The abandonment of afflictive determinations has already been established as proceeding through the seeing of truth and through cultivated realization. Cultivation may take two forms; the Path named Seeing, however, is intrinsically free from influx.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.01.md`](./VAK_6.01.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan [327|03–327|10].

> uktaṃ yathā prahāṇaṃ parijñākhyāṃ labhate /  
> tadapi ca  
> kleśaprahāṇamākhyātaṃ satyadarśanabhāvanāt /  
> darśanaheyā bhāvanāheyāśca kleśā iti vistareṇākhyātam /  
> tāvidānīṃ darśanabhāvanāmārgau kimanāsravau sāsravāviti vaktavyam /  
> ata idamucyate  
> dvividho bhāvanāmārgo darśanākhyastvanāsravaḥ // VAkK_6.1 //  
> dvidho bhāvanāmārgo laukiko lokottaraśca /  
> darśanamārgastu lokottara eva traidhātukapratipakṣatvāt /  
> navaprakārāṇāṃ darśanaheyānāṃ sakṛtprahāṇācca /  
> na hi laukikasya eṣā śaktirasti /

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. Retrospective transition from Chapter 5

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> uktaṃ yathā prahāṇaṃ parijñākhyāṃ labhate /

It has been explained how an abandonment acquires the designation **parijñā**.

> tadapi ca kleśaprahāṇamākhyātaṃ satyadarśanabhāvanāt /

And the abandonment of the afflictions has likewise been explained as occurring through seeing the truths and through cultivation.

> darśanaheyā bhāvanāheyāśca kleśā iti vistareṇākhyātam /

It has been explained in detail that the afflictions are of two kinds: those to be abandoned through Seeing and those to be abandoned through Cultivation.

The chapter therefore begins by gathering the conclusion of the Anuśayanirdeśa into the question that now has to be answered: **what kind of Path performs these two modes of abandonment?**

### 2. The governing question

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> tāv idānīṃ darśana-bhāvanā-mārgau kim anāsravau sāsravāv iti vaktavyam /

It must now be stated whether these two Paths, the Path of Seeing and the Path of Cultivation, are uncontaminated or contaminated.

This is the first genuinely reflective move of Chapter 6. The prior chapter distinguished the objects to be abandoned; this chapter must determine the **Path-capacity** adequate to that abandonment.

### 3. Cultivation is twofold

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> dvidho bhāvanāmārgo laukiko lokottaraśca /

The Path of Cultivation is twofold: mundane and supramundane.

This directly explains the Kārikā's **dvividhaḥ**. Bhāvanā is not intrinsically identical with the noble uncontaminated Path. There exists a mundane cultivation as well as a supramundane cultivation.

Thus the Bhāṣya prevents an overly spiritualized reading of **bhāvanā**. Cultivation is a formal Path-function that can occur on either side of the mundane/supramundane distinction.

### 4. Seeing is necessarily supramundane

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> darśanamārgas tu lokottara eva traidhātukapratipakṣatvāt /

The Path of Seeing, however, is supramundane only, because it is the counterforce to the three domains.

The decisive reason is **traidhātuka-pratipakṣatva**. Seeing is not supramundane merely because its experience is extraordinary or because it belongs to an ārya. Its supramundane character is functionally grounded: it stands as **pratipakṣa** to the entire three-domain conditioned field.

This is a strong definition of transcendence internal to the Kośa. The Path of Seeing is not somewhere outside the field spatially; it is the determination whose function is opposition to the whole field insofar as that field is governed by affliction.

### 5. Its distinctive power

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> navaprakārāṇāṃ darśanaheyānāṃ sakṛtprahāṇācca /

And because it abandons at once the nine kinds of those afflictions that are to be abandoned through Seeing.

> na hi laukikasya eṣā śaktir asti /

For a mundane path does not possess this power.

The second ground is therefore not merely the object-range of Seeing but its **mode of efficacy**. The Path of Seeing performs a single decisive abandonment that no mundane path can perform.

The Bhāṣya gives two mutually reinforcing determinations:

```text
Path of Seeing
    ↓
pratipakṣa to the three domains
    ↓
capable of abandoning the nine Seeing-abandonables at once
    ↓
therefore necessarily lokottara / anāsrava
```

## Argument Reconstruction

The passage has a compact but important logical form:

```text
Chapter 5:
kleśas are distinguished by mode of abandonment
    ↓
Seeing-abandonable
Cultivation-abandonable

Chapter 6 asks:
what is the status of the Paths that perform these abandonments?
    ↓
Bhāvanā-mārga is twofold
    laukika
    lokottara

Darśana-mārga is not twofold
    ↓
it opposes the entire three-domain field
    +
it abandons the nine Seeing-abandonables at once
    ↓
no mundane path has that śakti
    ↓
Darśana-mārga is necessarily lokottara / anāsrava
```

The crucial point is that **anāsrava is here demonstrated through function**. Vasubandhu does not merely repeat a classification. He gives the reason why the Path of Seeing cannot be mundane.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Darśana and Bhāvanā are not parallel species

The Kārikā can superficially look as if there were simply two Paths of equal logical status:

```text
darśana-mārga
bhāvanā-mārga
```

The Bhāṣya shows that the relation is asymmetrical.

```text
bhāvanā-mārga
    can be laukika or lokottara

darśana-mārga
    can only be lokottara
```

This matters for the whole chapter. **Seeing is the irreversible breach in the ordinary field; cultivation is the continued work that may exist both before and after that breach in different forms.**

### Pratipakṣa is the decisive functional category

The Path of Seeing is defined as **traidhātuka-pratipakṣa**: the counterforce to the three domains.

This gives us a disciplined sense of the Path as practical reason. A Path is not merely a representation of liberation. It is a determination that actually functions against the causal-afflictive structure of the conditioned field.

### Śakti marks effective Path-capacity

The closing sentence is especially important:

> na hi laukikasya eṣā śaktir asti

A mundane path simply **does not have this power**.

This prevents the distinction between mundane and supramundane from becoming merely classificatory. The distinction is one of effective capacity.

## New Technical Terms

- **laukika** — mundane; belonging to the conditioned world-order
  - **status:** technically operative here in the Bhāṣya's explanation of `dvividhaḥ`
  - **function:** logical distinction within the Path of Cultivation

- **lokottara** — supramundane; literally going beyond the world
  - **status:** technically operative here as the Bhāṣya's explanation of the Path's uncontaminated status
  - **function:** classification grounded in Path-function

- **traidhātuka-pratipakṣatva** — being the counterforce to the three domains
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion absent from the Kārikā wording
  - **function:** principal ground for why the Path of Seeing is necessarily supramundane

- **sakṛt-prahāṇa** — abandonment at once, in a single decisive operation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** second ground distinguishing the Path of Seeing from mundane cultivation

- **śakti** — effective power or capacity
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative term in this passage
  - **function:** states what mundane cultivation lacks

## Translation Consequences

The Bhāṣya confirms the Kārikā translation but sharpens two points.

First, **dvividho bhāvanāmārgaḥ** should be understood explicitly as:

```text
Path of Cultivation
    mundane
    supramundane
```

Second, **darśanākhyas tv anāsravaḥ** is not merely a stipulative classification. Its uncontaminated status follows from its function as the counterforce to all three domains and from its unique power of simultaneous abandonment.

No revision of the current philosophical translation is required, but the explanatory note should retain this functional grounding.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya opens the Mārga chapter by moving from **classification of what must be removed** to **determination of the power adequate to remove it**.

```text
object of abandonment
    ↓
mode of abandonment
    ↓
Path capable of that mode
```

This is already more than taxonomy. The Path is being determined through its **efficacy**.

The central opposition is:

```text
laukika cultivation
    limited counterforce

lokottara Seeing
    universal counterforce within the three-domain field
```

The difference is therefore not merely higher/lower experience but a difference in logical reach and practical power.

## Organon Contact Point

This verse gives a very strong entry into our determination of the Kośa as a Science of Practical Reason.

The Path is not first defined by inward state but by **what it can do**:

```text
pratipakṣa
    opposition to the afflictive field

prahāṇa
    actual removal

śakti
    effective capacity for removal
```

This is exactly the discipline we want to preserve. We should not prematurely identify `lokottara` with a metaphysical elsewhere. It is first the Path's capacity to negate the determining power of the three-domain field.

That makes the opening of Chapter 6 structurally powerful:

```text
Truth is seen
    not as passive cognition
    but as a Path-power

Path-power
    is measured by effective opposition

Seeing
    is the first intrinsically supramundane efficacy
```

The later Organon comparison with Pure Practical Reason can therefore be developed from a textual basis: **freedom must appear as an effective counter-determination of the field of bondage, not merely as a different description of that field.**

## Working Synthesis

VAK 6.01 establishes the first governing law of the Mārga chapter:

> **Cultivation can remain within the world; genuine Seeing cannot.**

Not because Seeing escapes the world spatially, but because it possesses a power no mundane path has: it opposes the whole three-domain field and performs the decisive abandonment of the Seeing-abandonable afflictions.

The chapter therefore begins with an exact transition from knowledge of bondage to the effective power of liberation.
