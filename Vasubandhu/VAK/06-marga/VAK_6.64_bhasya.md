# VAK 6.64 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> nirodhalābhy ubhayatovimuktaḥ prajñayetaraḥ /  
> samāpattīndriyaphalaiḥ pūrṇaḥ śaikṣo 'bhidhīyate // VAK_6.64 //

**Best current translation:**  
The one who has attained cessation is liberated in both respects; the other is liberated through prajñā. A trainee is called complete according to contemplative attainment, faculties, and fruit.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.64.md`](./VAK_6.64.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 381|01–381|13.

The Bhāṣya first defines the two Arhat types named at the close of the seven-person classification: the *ubhayatobhāgavimukta* and the *prajñāvimukta*. It then turns to the scriptural expression “complete trainee” (*paripūrṇa śaikṣa*) and makes an important refinement: completeness is threefold in principle, but a particular trainee need not be complete in all three respects at once.

## 1. Who Is Liberated in Both Respects?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `ko 'yam ubhayatobhāgavimukta ity ucyate kaś ca prajñāvimuktaḥ`

Who is called “liberated in both respects,” and who is “liberated through prajñā”?

The kārikā answers:

> `nirodhalābhy ubhayatovimuktaḥ prajñayetaraḥ`

The prose immediately fixes the first definition:

> `yo nirodhasamāpattilābhī sa ubhayatobhāgavimuktaḥ`

The one who has attained the attainment of cessation (*nirodhasamāpatti*) is liberated in both respects.

Thus the classification is not inferred merely from a general superiority of meditation. It is tied to a specific attainment:

```text
nirodhasamāpatti attained
    ↓
ubhayatobhāgavimukta
```

## 2. The Two Liberating Powers

Vasubandhu explains why this Arhat is called liberated “in both respects”:

> `prajñāsamādhibalābhyāṃ kleśavimokṣāvaraṇavimuktatvāt`

He is liberated through the powers of **prajñā** and **samādhi**, being freed from:

```text
kleśāvaraṇa
    obstruction constituted by the afflictions

vimokṣāvaraṇa
    obstruction to the liberations / meditative releases
```

The twofold structure is therefore exact:

```text
prajñā-bala
    ↓
freedom from kleśāvaraṇa

samādhi-bala
    ↓
freedom from vimokṣāvaraṇa
```

The Bhāṣya does not treat these as two competing paths to the same result. In the doubly liberated Arhat they are coordinated powers within one completed Path.

## 3. The Prajñā-Liberated Arhat

The prose continues:

> `itaraḥ prajñāvimuktaḥ`

The other is the one liberated through prajñā.

And:

> `prajñābalena kevalaṃ kleśāvaraṇavimuktatvāt`

He is liberated from the obstruction of the afflictions **through the power of prajñā alone**.

This is a decisive qualification. The *prajñāvimukta* is not imperfectly liberated from the kleśas. The difference concerns the additional meditative liberation associated with *nirodhasamāpatti*.

```text
prajñāvimukta
    prajñā-bala
    ↓
kleśāvaraṇa removed

ubhayatobhāgavimukta
    prajñā-bala + samādhi-bala
    ↓
kleśāvaraṇa + vimokṣāvaraṇa removed
```

Thus liberation from affliction and mastery of the full meditative releases must not be collapsed into one axis.

## 4. Scriptural Transition to the Complete Trainee

The Bhāṣya then cites the Bhagavat:

> `kleśān prahāyeha hi yastu pañca ahāryadharmā paripūrṇaḥ śaikṣa iti`

The transmitted wording is compressed, but the operative expression is clear:

> `paripūrṇaḥ śaikṣaḥ`

“complete trainee.”

Vasubandhu asks:

> `kiyatā paripūrṇaḥ śaikṣo bhavati`

By what is a trainee complete?

The kārikā answers:

> `samāpattīndriyaphalaiḥ pūrṇaḥ śaikṣo 'bhidhīyate`

The prose then gives the governing rule:

> `trividhā śaikṣasya paripūriḥ / phalata indriyataḥ samāpattitaś ca`

The completeness of a trainee is threefold:

1. with respect to **fruit** (*phala*)
2. with respect to **faculty** (*indriya*)
3. with respect to **contemplative attainment** (*samāpatti*)

This is the central classificatory principle of the second half of the verse.

## 5. Completeness Does Not Require All Three at Once

The Kārikā by itself can easily suggest:

```text
complete trainee
    = complete in samāpatti
      + complete in faculty
      + complete in fruit
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly shows that this is too simple.

There are several valid combinations of completeness.

### A. Complete by Fruit Alone

> `phalata eva śraddhādhimuktasyākāyasākṣiṇo 'nāgāminaḥ`

A *śraddhādhimukta* Anāgāmin who is **not** a *kāyasākṣin* is complete with respect to fruit alone.

```text
śraddhādhimukta
+ Anāgāmin
+ not kāyasākṣin
    ↓
complete by phala
```

He has reached the highest trainee fruit, but does not possess the sharper faculty determination or the relevant samāpatti determination.

### B. Complete by Faculty Alone

> `indriyata eva dṛṣṭiprāptasyāvītarāgasya`

A *dṛṣṭiprāpta* who is not yet dispassionate is complete with respect to faculty alone.

```text
dṛṣṭiprāpta
+ avītarāga
    ↓
complete by indriya
```

This is especially important: “complete trainee” can name completeness of one dimension even while another Path-dimension remains unfinished.

### C. Complete by Fruit and Faculty

> `phalendriyato dṛṣṭiprāptasya kāyasākṣiṇo 'nāgāminaḥ`

A *dṛṣṭiprāpta* *kāyasākṣin* Anāgāmin is complete with respect to fruit and faculty.

The wording as transmitted associates *kāyasākṣin* with this person, while the subsequent line separately identifies the fruit-plus-samāpatti case. The basic classificatory point is that two axes may be complete together without all three being the basis of the designation.

```text
phala complete
+ indriya complete
    ↓
twofold completeness
```

### D. Complete by Fruit and Samāpatti

> `phalasamāpattitaḥ śraddhādhimuktasya kāyasākṣiṇaḥ`

A *śraddhādhimukta* *kāyasākṣin* is complete with respect to fruit and samāpatti.

```text
phala complete
+ samāpatti complete
    ↓
twofold completeness
```

The weaker-faculty lineage does not prevent completeness in these other two dimensions.

### E. Complete by Fruit, Faculty, and Samāpatti

> `phalendriyasamāpattito dṛṣṭiprāptasya kāyasākṣiṇaḥ`

A *dṛṣṭiprāpta* *kāyasākṣin* is complete with respect to all three:

```text
phala
+ indriya
+ samāpatti
    ↓
threefold completeness
```

This is the maximal form of trainee completeness, but it is **not the only form** that earns the designation *paripūrṇa śaikṣa*.

## 6. Why There Is No Samāpatti-Only Completeness

Vasubandhu adds an explicit restriction:

> `samāpattita eva samāpattīndriyaś ca paripūrṇatvaṃ nāsti vinā phalena`

There is no completeness:

```text
by samāpatti alone
```

nor:

```text
by samāpatti + faculty
```

without fruit.

This establishes an asymmetry among the three axes.

The possible patterns are not all combinations of three independent predicates.

```text
possible:
    phala
    indriya
    phala + indriya
    phala + samāpatti
    phala + indriya + samāpatti

not established:
    samāpatti alone
    indriya + samāpatti without phala
```

The structure therefore contains dependency relations.

Samāpatti can enrich trainee completeness, but it cannot by itself substitute for attainment of the noble fruit.

## 7. A Better Reading of `Pūrṇa`

The Bhāṣya prevents us from reading *pūrṇa* as an undifferentiated absolute predicate.

There are several senses in which a trainee may be complete:

```text
complete qua fruit
complete qua faculty
complete qua fruit + faculty
complete qua fruit + samāpatti
complete qua all three
```

So:

```text
paripūrṇatā
    = completeness under a specified Path-determination
```

This is a relational and classificatory notion of completeness, not simply “having everything possible.”

The trainee remains a *śaikṣa* in every case. Completeness of a trainee-determination does not convert the practitioner into an *aśaikṣa*.

## 8. Relation to the Seven-Person Classification

VAK 6.63 generated persons through several axes:

```text
prayoga
indriya
samāpatti
vimukti
```

VAK 6.64 now gives the technical content of two of those axes.

For Arhats:

```text
vimukti
    ↓
prajñāvimukta
ubhayatobhāgavimukta
```

For trainees:

```text
phala
indriya
samāpatti
    ↓
several forms of paripūrṇatā
```

The same individual can therefore be named differently depending on which determination is under consideration.

This is exactly why the Kośa's person-taxonomies cannot be read as a flat list of substantial types.

## 9. Mārga Determination

The strongest result for Chapter 6 is the independence-with-coordination of Path dimensions.

```text
prajñā
    liberating discrimination

samādhi / samāpatti
    contemplative capacity

indriya
    operative sharpness

phala
    attained Path-result
```

These are coordinated, but not reducible to one another.

A practitioner may be advanced or complete under one determination while remaining incomplete under another.

Thus Mārga is not a single scalar ladder.

It is a structured field in which several developmental dimensions intersect.

## 10. Restrained Organon Contact

The Bhāṣya supplies a useful logical lesson without requiring us to import a larger external system.

A predicate such as “complete” has determinate content only relative to the respect under which it is asserted.

```text
pūrṇa
    not abstract totality

pūrṇa with respect to X
    determinate completion
```

The passage also prevents a simple additive model:

```text
phala
indriya
samāpatti
```

are not three freely combinable attributes. The admissible combinations are constrained by the Path itself.

This is enough Organon contact for the present pass: **classification follows the internal dependencies of the Path rather than an externally imposed combinatorial grid.**

## New Technical Terms

- **ubhayatobhāgavimukta** — one liberated in both respects  
  **status:** previously named, explicitly defined here  
  **function:** Arhat possessing *nirodhasamāpatti*, liberated through both prajñā and samādhi powers

- **prajñāvimukta** — one liberated through prajñā  
  **status:** previously named, explicitly defined here  
  **function:** Arhat freed from the afflictive obstruction through prajñā without the additional cessation-attainment determination

- **kleśāvaraṇa** — obstruction constituted by the afflictions  
  **status:** Bhāṣya technical term  
  **function:** obstruction removed through prajñā in both Arhat types

- **vimokṣāvaraṇa** — obstruction to the liberations / meditative releases  
  **status:** Bhāṣya technical term  
  **function:** additional obstruction from which the doubly liberated Arhat is freed through samādhi-power

- **paripūri / paripūrṇatā** — completeness, fulfillment under a specified Path-determination  
  **status:** made technically explicit here  
  **function:** classifies the trainee according to fruit, faculty, and contemplative attainment

- **phalataḥ** — with respect to fruit  
  **status:** classificatory adverb  
  **function:** first axis of trainee completeness

- **indriyataḥ** — with respect to faculty  
  **status:** classificatory adverb  
  **function:** second axis of trainee completeness

- **samāpattitaḥ** — with respect to contemplative attainment  
  **status:** classificatory adverb  
  **function:** third axis of trainee completeness

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting the Abhidharma classification.

Two cautions matter here.

First, *prajñāvimukta* should not be translated as an Arhat whose liberation is somehow defective. The Bhāṣya explicitly grounds that person's liberation from *kleśāvaraṇa* in the power of prajñā.

Second, the Kārikā's compact compound `samāpattīndriyaphalaiḥ` should not be read as requiring all three conditions simultaneously in every *paripūrṇa śaikṣa*. The prose gives multiple forms of partial and combined completeness and explicitly excludes certain combinations.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.64 defines the Arhat who has attained *nirodhasamāpatti* as *ubhayatobhāgavimukta*: liberated through the coordinated powers of prajñā and samādhi from the afflictive obstruction and the obstruction to the liberations. The other Arhat is *prajñāvimukta*, liberated from the afflictive obstruction through prajñā alone.

The verse then defines trainee completeness through three axes—fruit, faculty, and contemplative attainment—but the Bhāṣya shows that “complete trainee” is not one uniform configuration. Completeness may be by fruit alone, faculty alone, fruit plus faculty, fruit plus samāpatti, or all three together; samāpatti alone, or samāpatti plus faculty without fruit, does not establish completeness.

The decisive Mārga result is therefore that Path-development is **multidimensional but internally constrained**. Fruit, faculty, contemplative attainment, and liberation form coordinated but non-identical determinations, and the Kośa's person-classifications record their lawful intersections rather than substantial kinds of self.
