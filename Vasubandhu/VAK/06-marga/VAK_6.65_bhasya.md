# VAK 6.65 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> aśaikṣaparipūrṇatvaṃ dvābhyām mārgaḥ samāsataḥ /  
> viśeṣamuktyānantaryaprayogākhyaś caturvidhaḥ // VAK_6.65 //

**Best current translation:**  
The completeness of the one beyond training is determined in two respects. In summary, the Path is fourfold: the Path of Further Distinction, the Path of Release, the Path of Immediacy, and the Path of Application.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.65.md`](./VAK_6.65.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 381|14–382|06.

This verse has two movements. First, it completes the discussion of relative completeness begun in VAK 6.64 by defining the completeness proper to the Aśaikṣa. Second, Vasubandhu deliberately steps back from the many preceding classifications of persons, faculties, fruits, grounds, and forms of liberation and asks for the Path itself *in summary*.

That shift makes VAK 6.65 a natural closure for the present block.

## 1. Aśaikṣa Completeness Is Twofold

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The kārikā states:

> `aśaikṣaparipūrṇatvaṃ dvābhyām`

The Bhāṣya glosses:

> `indriyataḥ samāpattiś ca`

The Aśaikṣa is complete in two respects:

```text
indriya
    faculty

samāpatti
    contemplative attainment
```

Fruit is not counted as a third independent respect.

## 2. Why Fruit Is Not Counted for the Aśaikṣa

The prose makes the reason explicit:

> `phalena tv aparipūrṇasyāśaikṣatvam eva nāsti`

If one were incomplete with respect to the fruit, one would not be an Aśaikṣa at all.

Therefore:

> `nāsya punaḥ phalena paripūrṇāparipūrṇatvaṃ vyavasthāpyate`

No further distinction of completeness or incompleteness is established for the Aśaikṣa with respect to fruit.

This is an important correction to any attempt to parallel the trainee mechanically.

For the trainee, fruit can still function as an axis of relative completeness. For the one beyond training, possession of the final fruit is presupposed by the very designation.

```text
śaikṣa
    fruit can still distinguish degrees of completeness

aśaikṣa
    final fruit already presupposed
    therefore completeness is measured otherwise
```

## 3. Three Forms of Aśaikṣa Completeness

The Bhāṣya then gives concrete cases.

### Completeness by faculty alone

> `indriyata evāsamayavimuktasya prajñāvimuktasya`

The *asamayavimukta* who is *prajñāvimukta* is complete by faculty alone.

### Completeness by samāpatti alone

> `samāpattita eva samayavimuktasyobhayabhāgavimuktasya`

The *samayavimukta* who is *ubhayabhāgavimukta* is complete by samāpatti alone.

### Completeness by both faculty and samāpatti

> `indriyasamāpattibhyām asamayavimuktasyobhayatobhāgavimuktasya`

The *asamayavimukta* who is *ubhayatobhāgavimukta* is complete in both respects.

So the structure is:

```text
Aśaikṣa completeness
    faculty only
    samāpatti only
    faculty + samāpatti
```

The Bhāṣya therefore preserves the multidimensional structure of the completed Path without introducing any incompleteness in liberation itself.

## 4. Vasubandhu Steps Back from the Proliferation of Classifications

The prose now changes level:

> `bahava ime mārgabhedā uktā`

Many divisions of the Path have been stated.

He lists:

```text
laukika
lokottara
darśana
bhāvanā
aśaikṣa
prayoga
ānantarya
vimukti
viśeṣa
```

This sentence is methodologically important. Vasubandhu recognizes that the chapter has accumulated many valid divisions of Mārga according to different principles.

He therefore asks:

> `katividha eṣa samāsato mārga iti`

How manyfold is this Path **in summary**?

The kārikā answers:

> `mārgaḥ samāsataḥ ... caturvidhaḥ`

In summary, the Path is fourfold.

## 5. Prayoga-mārga — Path of Application

The Bhāṣya defines it:

> `prayogamārgo yasmād anantaram ānantaryamārgotpattiḥ`

The Path of Application is that from which the Path of Immediacy arises immediately afterward.

Its definition is therefore relational and preparatory:

```text
prayoga-mārga
    prepares the immediate condition
        ↓
ānantarya-mārga arises next
```

It is not merely generic effort. It is the Path-phase immediately preparing the effective abandoning path.

## 6. Ānantarya-mārga — Path of Immediacy

The Bhāṣya states:

> `ānantaryamārgo yenāvaraṇaṃ prajahāti`

The Path of Immediacy is that by which the obstruction is abandoned.

This is the decisive negative operation:

```text
āvaraṇa
    obstruction
        ↓
ānantarya-mārga
        ↓
prahāṇa
    abandonment
```

The term therefore marks the path-moment that directly effects the removal of the relevant obstruction.

## 7. Vimukti-mārga — Path of Release

The Bhāṣya defines:

> `vimuktimārgo yas tatpraheyāvaraṇavinirmuktas tat prathamata utpadyate`

The Path of Release is that which first arises free from the obstruction that was to be abandoned.

The distinction between Ānantarya and Vimukti is therefore exact:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    abandons the obstruction

vimukti-mārga
    first arises free from that obstruction
```

Removal and released actuality are not collapsed into one moment.

## 8. Viśeṣa-mārga — Path of Further Distinction

The Bhāṣya says simply:

> `viśeṣamārgo ya ebhyo 'nyo mārgaḥ`

The Path of Further Distinction is whatever Path is other than these [three].

This is deliberately broad. It gathers the Path that proceeds beyond the immediate application–abandonment–release sequence.

The prose then explains why such a path still deserves to be called Mārga.

## 9. Why Is It Called “Path”?

Vasubandhu asks:

> `kasmāt mārga ity ucyate`

Why is it called Path?

He answers:

> `eṣa hi nirvāṇasya panthā etena tadgamanāt`

It is the road to nirvāṇa because one goes to nirvāṇa by it.

He adds an etymological explanation:

> `nirvāṇaṃ mārgayanty aneneti vā`

Or: nirvāṇa is sought/reached by means of it.

So Mārga is defined teleologically but concretely:

```text
Mārga
    that by which nirvāṇa is reached
```

## 10. Why Are Vimukti and Viśeṣa Still “Path”?

The Bhāṣya raises a subtle objection:

> `vimuktiviśeṣamārgayoḥ kathaṃ mārgatvam`

How can the Paths of Release and Further Distinction still count as Path?

If liberation has already arisen, one might think the Path is over.

Vasubandhu gives three reasons:

> `tajjātīyād adhimātrataratvād`

because they can be more intense than something of the same class;

> `uttarottaraprāpaṇāt`

because they lead to progressively higher attainments;

> `nirūpadhiśeṣapraveśād vā`

or because they lead into nirvāṇa without remainder.

This is the decisive closure of the block.

```text
release
    is not merely terminus

release
    can itself become basis
    for further Path-development
```

Thus the Path includes not only the production of liberation but the progressive realization and consummation of that liberation.

## 11. The Fourfold Operational Form of Mārga

The conceptual order is:

```text
prayoga
    preparation
        ↓
ānantarya
    abandonment of obstruction
        ↓
vimukti
    first unobstructed actuality
        ↓
viśeṣa
    further development toward higher attainment
    and final nirvāṇa
```

This fourfold summary does not replace the many earlier divisions. It abstracts their operative form.

The Chapter can therefore sustain many classifications without becoming merely taxonomic:

```text
many distinctions of persons, fruits, faculties, grounds
        ↓
all remain determinations of one Mārga
        ↓
whose operative summary is fourfold
```

## 12. Closure of VAK 6.55–6.65

The present block began at VAK 6.55 by asking where the Path can begin and where it can be completed.

It then moved through:

```text
6.55
    genesis and completion across domains

6.56–6.60
    six Arhat types, stability, regression, transformation

6.61–6.62
    faculty-transition and graded persons

6.63–6.64
    Path-person classifications and relative completeness

6.65
    Aśaikṣa completeness
    and the fourfold Path in summary
```

The block therefore closes not on a special privilege of the Aśaikṣa, but on **Mārga itself**.

That validates the editorial correction made at the start of this thread: the governing subject is Path, while Aśaikṣa is one determinate form within it.

## Restrained Organon Contact

The strongest conceptual result is structural and can be stated without forcing an external schema onto the text:

```text
preparation
    ≠
abandonment

abandonment
    ≠
released actuality

released actuality
    ≠
its further development
```

The Bhāṣya thereby refuses to reduce liberation to a single instantaneous negation.

The Path contains:

```text
condition of transformation
act of removal
first actuality of freedom
positive development of freedom
```

This is especially important for the later Organon pass. The positive development of achieved freedom is already explicit in Vasubandhu's own fourfold Mārga and should not be imported from outside as though the Kośa knew only cessation or negation.

## New Technical Terms

- **aśaikṣaparipūrṇatva** — completeness proper to one beyond training  
  **status:** explicitly developed here from VAK 6.64  
  **function:** determined through faculty and contemplative attainment, not fruit

- **prayogamārga** — Path of Application  
  **status:** recurrent technical Path term, summarized here  
  **function:** immediate preparatory condition for the Path of Immediacy

- **ānantaryamārga** — Path of Immediacy  
  **status:** recurrent technical Path term  
  **function:** directly abandons the relevant obstruction

- **vimuktimārga** — Path of Release  
  **status:** recurrent technical Path term  
  **function:** first path-moment arising free from the abandoned obstruction

- **viśeṣamārga** — Path of Further Distinction  
  **status:** recurrent technical Path term  
  **function:** Path beyond the first three moments, leading to stronger and higher attainment

- **āvaraṇa** — obstruction  
  **status:** recurrent  
  **function:** that which is abandoned by the Path of Immediacy

- **nirūpadhiśeṣapraveśa** — entry into nirvāṇa without remainder  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression  
  **function:** one reason Vimukti and Viśeṣa continue to count as Path

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the preserved passage.

The Bhāṣya here is not presenting an explicit school controversy. Its main work is classificatory and definitional.

A translation caution remains with *viśeṣamārga*: the Bhāṣya defines it broadly as the Path other than the first three and then explains its Path-status through greater intensity, progressively higher attainment, and final entry into nirvāṇa without remainder. “Path of Further Distinction” therefore remains preferable to a weakly nominal “special path.”

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.65 completes the present Mārga block. Aśaikṣa completeness is measured through faculty and contemplative attainment because possession of the final fruit is already presupposed by being beyond training. Vasubandhu then gathers the many preceding divisions of the Path into a fourfold operational summary: Application prepares; Immediacy abandons the obstruction; Release first arises free from it; Further Distinction carries the liberated actuality into stronger, higher, and ultimately final realization. The block therefore ends by returning from the taxonomy of completed persons to the universal structure of Mārga itself.
