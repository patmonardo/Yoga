# VAK 6.79 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> निर्विद्यते दुःखहेतुक्षान्तिज्ञानैर्विरज्यते ।  
> सर्वैर्जहाति यैरेवं चतुष्कोटिकसम्भवः ॥

**nirvidyate duḥkha-hetu-kṣānti-jñānair virajyate |**  
**sarvair jahāti yair evaṃ catuṣkoṭika-saṃbhavaḥ ||**

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu closes the Mārga chapter by distinguishing two related transformations:

- **nirveda** — disenchantment or radical turning-away;
- **virāga** — dispassion, freedom from attachment.

The question is whether these always coincide.

They do not. Their relation admits a **catuṣkoṭi**, a fourfold alternative.

The determining conditions are:

1. the truth serving as object;
2. whether the cognition actually abandons a kleśa.

## Nirveda

Vasubandhu states:

> **duḥkhe samudayakṣāntijñānaireva nirvidyate nānyaiḥ**

One becomes disenchanted only through the receptivities and knowledges concerning **suffering** and **origination**, not through the others.

The reason given later is their object:

> **nirvedavastvālambanatvāt**

They take as object a basis appropriate to disenchantment.

Thus nirveda is not merely an affective recoil. It is a Path-function grounded in cognition of suffering and its ground.

## Virāga

Virāga has a broader possible range:

> **sarvair api duḥkha-samudaya-nirodha-mārga-kṣānti-jñānair virajyate / yaiḥ kleśān prajahāti**

One becomes dispassionate through receptivities and knowledges concerning all four truths **when those cognitions actually abandon afflictions**.

The qualification is essential:

```text
truth-cognition
    does not automatically imply abandonment

virāga here
    depends on actual kleśa-abandonment
```

## The Fourfold Alternative

Vasubandhu then gives the four corners explicitly.

### 1. Nirveda without virāga

The receptivities and knowledges concerning suffering and origination can produce disenchantment while not, in the relevant case, abandoning a kleśa.

```text
object
    duḥkha / samudaya

function
    nirveda

kleśa-abandonment
    absent
```

### 2. Virāga without nirveda

The receptivities and knowledges concerning cessation and Path can abandon kleśas and so produce virāga, while their objects are not objects of disenchantment.

Vasubandhu gives the reason:

> **prāmodyavastvālambanatvāt**

They take as object a basis of gladness or rejoicing.

```text
object
    nirodha / mārga

function
    virāga

nirveda
    absent
```

### 3. Both nirveda and virāga

The suffering- and origination-cognitions that actually abandon kleśas perform both functions.

```text
duḥkha / samudaya cognition
    + kleśa-abandonment
        ↓
    nirveda + virāga
```

### 4. Neither

Cognitions that neither take an object producing nirveda nor perform kleśa-abandonment yield neither.

The Bhāṣya illustrates this with one already dispassionate who sees the truths without abandoning further kleśas, and with knowledges included in preparatory, liberation, special, and related paths that do not perform abandonment.

## Argument Reconstruction

The chapter ends with a threefold distinction that must not be collapsed:

```text
ālambana
    what the cognition takes as object

path-function
    nirveda or virāga

efficacy
    whether kleśa-abandonment actually occurs
```

From these relations the catuṣkoṭi follows.

The final analysis is therefore not a loose psychological description. It is a functional classification of cognition according to object and efficacy.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — question, fourfold classification, and examples.

No significant school dispute is developed in this final unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **nirveda** — discriminatively grounded disenchantment or turning-away
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptually, here technically delimited
  - **function:** Path-function linked specifically to objects of suffering and origination

- **prāmodya-vastu** — basis/object of gladness or rejoicing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** explains why cessation and Path cognitions can produce virāga without nirveda

- **nirveda-vastu** — basis/object of disenchantment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** explains why suffering and origination cognitions are specifically related to nirveda

- **catuṣkoṭika** — fourfold alternative
  - **status:** explicit in Kārikā and Bhāṣya
  - **function:** distinguishes possible relations between nirveda and virāga

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive distinction is:

```text
seeing a truth
    ≠
necessarily abandoning a kleśa
```

The same class of truth-cognition can have different practical efficacy depending on the Path-position of the practitioner.

Thus Vasubandhu closes the chapter by preserving the difference between:

```text
knowledge-object
practical function
actual abandonment
```

Liberation is not explained by cognition in abstraction, but by cognition functioning efficaciously within a determinate Path-position.

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

The Kārikā's **sarvaiḥ** should not be read as saying that every cognition of all four truths invariably produces virāga. The Bhāṣya supplies the restriction:

> **yaiḥ kleśān prajahāti** — through those by which the afflictions are abandoned.

A clearer first-pass translation is therefore:

> One becomes disenchanted through the receptivities and knowledges concerning suffering and its origin; one becomes dispassionate through any of the receptivities and knowledges concerning the four truths **insofar as they abandon afflictions**. Thus a fourfold alternative is possible.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The chapter concludes with a relation among:

```text
Object
Function
Efficacy
```

The same cognition-type is not defined solely by its propositional content. Its practical determination depends upon what it does within the Path.

That is enough for the Bhāṣya pass. The stronger reconstruction of Mārga as Practical Concept belongs to the later Organon synthesis.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.79 closes Chapter 6 by distinguishing **nirveda** and **virāga** through a fourfold analysis. Nirveda is specifically tied to cognition of suffering and origination; virāga can arise through cognitions of any of the four truths when those cognitions actually abandon kleśas. Hence one may have nirveda without virāga, virāga without nirveda, both, or neither. The final criterion is not merely what is known, but the practical efficacy of that knowledge within the Path.
