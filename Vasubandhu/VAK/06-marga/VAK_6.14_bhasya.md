# VAK 6.14 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**IAST**

niṣpanna-śamathaḥ kuryāt smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanām |
kāya-vic-citta-dharmāṇāṃ dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇāt || 6.14 ||

**Working translation**

One whose śamatha has been accomplished should cultivate the Foundations of Recollection through examination of body, feeling, cognition, and dharmas according to their two determinations.

See: `VAK_6.14.md`

---

## Bhāṣya Source

The Bhāṣya transitions directly from the preceding two entry-practices:

> ukte dve avatāramukhe / tābhyāṃ tu samādhilabdhā niṣpannaśamathaḥ kuryāt smṛtyupasthānabhāvanām / vipaśyanāyāḥ saṃpādanārtham / kathaṃ ca punaḥ kuryāt / kāyaviccittadharmāṇāṃ dvilakṣaṇaparīkṣaṇāt //

It then explains:

> kāyaṃ svasāmānyalakṣaṇābhyāṃ parīkṣate / vedanāṃ cittaṃ dharmāśca / svabhāva evaiṣāṃ svalakṣaṇam / sāmānyalakṣaṇaṃ tu anityatā saṃskṛtānāṃ duḥkhatā sāsravāṇāṃ śūnyatā 'nātmate sarvadharmāṇām /

The prose further specifies:

> kāyasya punaḥ kaḥ svabhāvaḥ / bhūtabhautikatvam / dharmāstribhyo 'nye /

and notes:

> sāmāhitasya kila kāyaṃ paramāṇuśaḥ kṣaṇikataśca paśyataḥ kāyasmṛtyupasthānaṃ niṣpannaṃ bhavati /

The subsequent question concerning the intrinsic nature of the smṛtyupasthānas opens the next unit and is developed under VAK 6.15.

---

## Literal Prose Translation

Two entrances have now been stated. Having obtained samādhi through them, one whose śamatha has been accomplished should cultivate the Foundations of Recollection for the completion of vipaśyanā.

How should one cultivate them?

One examines body according to its self-determination and universal determination; likewise feeling, cognition, and dharmas.

Their own nature is their self-determination. Their universal determination is: impermanence for conditioned dharmas; suffering for contaminated dharmas; emptiness and not-self for all dharmas.

What then is the own nature of body? Its being composed of the great-material bases and derivative material form. Dharmas are those determinations other than the preceding three fields.

For one established in samādhi, the Foundation of Recollection directed to body is said to be completed when body is seen atom-by-atom and moment-by-moment.

## Argument Reconstruction

The sequence is explicit:

```text
Two entrance-practices
    aśubha
    ānāpānasmṛti
        ↓
samādhi obtained
        ↓
śamatha accomplished
        ↓
smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanā
        ↓
vipaśyanā completed
```

This is a major transition. The earlier practices stabilize and discipline cognition; the smṛtyupasthānas now turn that stabilized cognition into systematic examination.

Vasubandhu does not define vipaśyanā here as a vague heightened awareness. Its preparation is expressed through **parīkṣaṇa**, examination.

The four fields are:

```text
kāya
vedanā
citta
dharma
```

Each must be examined under two determinations:

```text
svalakṣaṇa
    the determination belonging to the field itself

sāmānyalakṣaṇa
    the universal determination under which it falls
```

The universal determinations are not applied indiscriminately:

```text
all conditioned dharmas
    → anitya

all contaminated dharmas
    → duḥkha

all dharmas
    → śūnya
    → anātman
```

Thus `dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇa` means that the practitioner must know both what a determination is in its own specificity and what universal law governs it.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The transition from the two entrance-practices to accomplished śamatha and then to smṛtyupasthāna cultivation is presented directly as explanatory prose.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position / inherited doctrinal formulation**

The sentence beginning `sāmāhitasya kila` is marked by `kila`, suggesting reported doctrinal formulation: the body-foundation is complete for one in samādhi who sees body atomically and momentarily.

No adversarial objection is developed in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **avatāramukha** — entrance-method, entrance-gate
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit term summarizing the practices of 6.09–6.13
  - **function:** doctrinal transition; identifies aśubha and ānāpānasmṛti as preparatory entrances rather than final Path states

- **saṃpādana** — bringing to completion, accomplishment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit
  - **function:** establishes that smṛtyupasthāna cultivation serves the completion of vipaśyanā

- **parīkṣaṇa** — examination
  - **status:** made technically operative here
  - **function:** defines the cognitive procedure by which the four fields are investigated

- **svalakṣaṇa** — self-determination / intrinsic determination
  - **status:** previously encountered globally, decisive here
  - **function:** first of the two modes under which each field is examined

- **sāmānyalakṣaṇa** — universal determination
  - **status:** technically operative here
  - **function:** second mode of examination; connects the particular field to universal conditioned structure

- **bhūta-bhautikatva** — being constituted by the great-material bases and derivative material form
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification relative to the Kārikā
  - **function:** gives the self-determination of body

- **paramāṇuśaḥ** — atom-by-atom
  - **status:** prose specification
  - **function:** analytic mode of body-examination

- **kṣaṇikataḥ** — moment-by-moment / according to momentariness
  - **status:** prose specification
  - **function:** temporal mode of body-examination

## Doctrinal Determination

The most important result is that **śamatha is preparatory to, but not identical with, vipaśyanā**.

```text
śamatha
    stabilizes the field

vipaśyanā
    examines the field adequately
```

The smṛtyupasthānas mediate between them.

The practitioner does not abandon the empirical field once samādhi is obtained. The stabilized field is now subjected to disciplined discrimination.

The two-determination method prevents two opposite errors:

```text
only svalakṣaṇa
    → disconnected particulars

only sāmānyalakṣaṇa
    → abstract universality without determinate content
```

The Path requires both:

```text
what this dharma is
    +
what universal structure it instantiates
```

The body example is especially concrete. Body is not merely contemplated as "body" in a general sense. Its own nature is material constitution, and its examination proceeds atomically and momentarily.

---

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā phrase `dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇāt` should remain strong in translation.

Preferred:

> through examination according to the two determinations

rather than a weaker rendering such as "by observing two characteristics."

`parīkṣaṇa` is doing methodological work: this is structured investigation.

Likewise `niṣpanna-śamatha` should be understood as **accomplished śamatha**, not final liberation or a completed Path.

## Relation to 6.12–6.13

The transition is now explicit:

```text
ānāpānasmṛti
    Prajñā applied to breath-process
        ↓
samādhi
        ↓
accomplished śamatha
        ↓
smṛtyupasthāna examination
        ↓
vipaśyanā
```

The breath practice already began to transform a bodily process into an analyzed dharma-field. VAK 6.14 universalizes that procedure across body, feeling, cognition, and dharmas.

This is the point where preparatory stabilization becomes systematic Path-analysis.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The text itself warrants a minimal logical formulation:

```text
Determinate object
    examined as itself
        = svalakṣaṇa

Determinate object
    examined under universal law
        = sāmānyalakṣaṇa
```

The conjunction is the important point. Liberating analysis does not dissolve singular determinations into universals, nor isolate them from universal law.

No stronger Kantian, Fichtean, or Hegelian identification is required at this stage.

---

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.14 marks the transition from stabilization to examination.

```text
aśubha / ānāpānasmṛti
    → samādhi
    → accomplished śamatha

accomplished śamatha
    → smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanā
    → completion of vipaśyanā
```

The four fields—body, feeling, cognition, and dharmas—are examined according to two determinations:

```text
svalakṣaṇa
sāmānyalakṣaṇa
```

The universal determinations are:

```text
conditioned → impermanent
contaminated → duḥkha
all dharmas → empty and not-self
```

The central result is that **vipaśyanā is introduced as disciplined two-level examination of a field already stabilized by śamatha**.
