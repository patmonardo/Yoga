# VAK 6.36 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**kṣīṇa-saptāṣṭa-doṣāṃśa ekajanmaikavīcikaḥ |**  
**tṛtīya-pratipannaś ca so 'nāgāmi nava-kṣayāt || 6.36 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 358|04–358|18.

## 1. The Once-Returner Becomes an Ekavīcika

Vasubandhu continues directly from VAK 6.35. The same practitioner who has become a **sakṛdāgāmin** is now further determined according to the destruction of the seventh and eighth grades:

> **sa eva sakṛdāgāmy ekavīcido veditavyaḥ**

The once-returner is to be understood as **ekavīcika** when the relevant threshold is reached.

The determining condition is:

> **saptāṣṭaprakāraprahāṇāt**

because seven or eight grades have been abandoned.

Thus the sequence continues:

```text
6 grades abandoned
    ↓
sakṛdāgāmin
    once-returner

7–8 grades abandoned
    ↓
ekavīcika
    one-interval practitioner
    ↓
tṛtīyaphalapratipannaka
    candidate for the third fruit
```

The same path-person is therefore renamed as the residual structure of the desire-realm afflictions changes.

## 2. Three Reasons for the Ekavīcika Classification

The Bhāṣya says that the designation is understood through **three causes**:

> **tribhiḥ kāraṇaiḥ**

and then gives the relevant determinations.

### 2.1 Seven or Eight Grades Have Been Abandoned

The first is the explicit grading condition:

> **saptāṣṭaprakāraprahāṇāt**

Only one or two of the nine desire-realm cultivation-abandonable grades remain.

### 2.2 The Opposing Uncontaminated Faculty Has Been Acquired

Vasubandhu adds:

> **tatpratipakṣānāsravendriyalābhāt**

because the uncontaminated faculty that is the antidote to those afflictions has been acquired.

This is important. The classification does not depend only on subtraction:

```text
less affliction
```

but also on acquisition:

```text
corresponding uncontaminated counter-power acquired
```

So the path-state is determined by both sides:

```text
abandonment of obstruction
    +
acquisition of its opposing Noble capacity
```

This continues the operative structure already visible in the earlier **ānantarya / mukti** pairing.

### 2.3 Only One Birth Remains

The third condition is:

> **ekajanmāvaśeṣatvāt**

because only one birth remains.

Hence the Kārikā's paired designations:

```text
ekajanma
    one birth remaining

ekavīcika
    one interval remaining
```

are not independent labels. Both express the extreme proximity of the practitioner to the third fruit.

## 3. Can One Remaining Grade Really Obstruct the Fruit?

Vasubandhu raises the obvious objection:

> **katham asyaikaḥ prakāraḥ phalaṃ vidhnayituṃ śaknoti**

How can a single remaining grade be capable of obstructing the fruit?

The answer is:

> **dhātvatikramāt**

because what is at issue is the **crossing beyond a domain**.

The ninth grade is not merely one small residue among many. Its abandonment marks the complete transcendence of the desire realm as an afflictive domain.

Thus:

```text
one grade numerically
    ≠
minor functionally

final desire-realm grade
    =
last condition preventing domain-transcendence
```

This is another clear example of the Kośa using threshold structure rather than simple accumulation.

## 4. The Analogy with Karma

Vasubandhu refers back to an earlier principle:

> **avasthātraye hi karmāṇi vidhnāyopatiṣṭhanta ity uktaṃ prāk**

It was said earlier that karmas present obstacles at three critical stages.

He then generalizes:

> **yathā karmāṇy evaṃ kleśā api veditavyāḥ**

Just as with karmas, so too the afflictions are to be understood.

The point is structural: a factor need not be quantitatively large to function as a decisive obstacle when a transition of state or domain is at stake.

Vasubandhu specifies the transitions as involving:

> **vipāka-niḥṣyanda-phala-bhūmyatikramāt**

crossing beyond the relevant maturation, outflow/result, and level.

The precise inherited doctrinal distribution is compressed here, but the operative principle is clear:

```text
transition threshold
    ↓
small remaining condition
    can still block
    ↓
complete change of level
```

## 5. What Does Vīci Mean?

Vasubandhu explicitly glosses the technical term:

> **vīcir nāmāntaram**

**vīci** means an interval, a gap.

He then gives two possible explanations of **ekavīcika**.

### First explanation: one birth intervenes

> **tasya caikajanmāvyavahitatvān nirvāṇasya**

Nirvāṇa is separated from him by only one birth.

Thus:

```text
present state
    ↓
one remaining birth
    ↓
Nirvāṇa
```

### Second explanation: one affliction-grade intervenes

> **ekakleśaprakāravyavahitatvād vā 'nāgāmiphalasya**

Or the fruit of non-returning is separated from him by only one grade of affliction.

Thus:

```text
present state
    ↓
one remaining kleśa-grade
    ↓
anāgāmiphala
```

Vasubandhu therefore preserves two closely related senses of the “one interval”:

```text
existential interval:
    one birth

path-functional interval:
    one affliction-grade
```

Both mark nearness to completion of desire-realm abandonment.

## 6. Candidate for the Third Fruit

The Bhāṣya states explicitly:

> **tṛtīyaphalapratipannakaś caikavīciko veditavyaḥ prahīṇasaptāṣṭaprakāraḥ**

The ekavīcika, having abandoned seven or eight grades, is to be understood as a candidate for the third fruit.

So the classification is now exact:

```text
6 grades abandoned
    fruit-established once-returner

7–8 grades abandoned
    ekavīcika
    candidate for non-returning

9 grades abandoned
    fruit-established non-returner
```

The seventh and eighth grades therefore belong to a new directed transition, not merely to a stronger version of once-returning.

## 7. Prior Abandonment Does Not Automatically Produce the New Path-Person

Vasubandhu adds an important qualification:

> **pūrvaprahīṇaprakāras tu phalaprāptau na tāvat kulaṅkulo bhavaty ekavīciko vā yāvat phalaviśiṣṭo mārgo na samukhīkṛtaḥ**

If the grades had already been abandoned beforehand, then on attaining the fruit the practitioner does **not immediately** become a **kulaṅkula** or **ekavīcika** until the path superior to that fruit has actually been brought face-to-face / made present.

This strongly confirms the transition rule established in VAK 6.32:

```text
prior condition
    ≠
active superior path-status

fruit attained
    ↓
renewed superior path made present
    ↓
new pratipannaka / intermediate designation
```

So classification follows actual path-operation, not merely a stored numerical state.

This is one of the most methodologically important statements in this sub-section.

## 8. Destruction of the Ninth Grade: Anāgāmin

The final threshold is:

> **so 'nāgāmī navakṣayāt**

With destruction of the ninth grade, that same practitioner becomes an **anāgāmin**, a non-returner.

The Bhāṣya explains:

> **navaprakāraprahāṇād anāgāmī upadiṣṭaḥ**

because all nine grades have been abandoned.

And the name is causal:

> **kāmadhātv anāgamanāt**

because there is no return to the desire realm.

Thus:

```text
9th grade destroyed
    ↓
complete desire-realm afflictive abandonment
    ↓
no return to kāmadhātu
    ↓
anāgāmin
```

The third fruit therefore marks a domain-level transformation, not simply the ninth item in a list.

## 9. Relation to the Five Lower Fetters

Vasubandhu also cites the standard formulation:

> **pañcānām avarabhāgīyānāṃ saṃyojanānāṃ prahāṇāt**

The non-returner is described through abandonment of the **five lower fetters**.

But he immediately cautions that this formula is an aggregate description:

> **prahāṇasaṃkulanāt**

because the abandonments are grouped together.

He notes:

> **avaśyaṃ dve trīṇi vā pūrvaṃ prahīṇāni bhavanti**

necessarily two or three of them had already been abandoned earlier.

So the canonical five-fetter formula should not be read as though all five are first destroyed simultaneously at the moment of non-returning.

Again the Bhāṣya protects the temporal and causal differentiation of the process.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.36 makes the threshold structure fully explicit:

```text
six grades abandoned
    ↓
once-return fruit

superior path made present
    ↓
seventh/eighth grades abandoned
    ↓
ekavīcika
    third-fruit candidate

final remaining grade
    still functionally blocks
    domain-transcendence
    ↓
ninth grade abandoned
    ↓
complete kāmadhātu detachment
    ↓
anāgāmin
```

Two principles are especially important.

First, **numerical residue and functional importance are not identical**. One final weak grade can still block the crossing of an entire domain.

Second, **stored attainment and operative transition are not identical**. Even if grades were abandoned earlier, a new path-person designation requires the superior path beyond the attained fruit to become actually present.

Thus the Kośa's classification is increasingly revealed as a state-transition system:

```text
current fruit-state
    +
actualized superior path
    +
remaining obstruction
    +
new abandonment
        ↓
new path-person
        ↓
new fruit-threshold
```

## New Technical Terms

- **ekavīcika** — one-interval practitioner.
  - **status:** technical noble-person classification developed explicitly here.
  - **function:** names the practitioner separated from Nirvāṇa by one birth or from non-returning by one remaining affliction-grade.

- **vīci** — interval, gap.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya lexical explanation of **ekavīcika**.
  - **function:** expresses the minimal remaining separation from the next decisive attainment.

- **tatpratipakṣānāsravendriyalābha** — acquisition of the uncontaminated faculty opposed to the remaining affliction.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya causal explanation.
  - **function:** shows that progress involves acquisition of a counter-capacity as well as abandonment of faults.

- **dhātvatikrama** — crossing beyond a domain.
  - **status:** important explanatory term in the objection-response.
  - **function:** explains why even one remaining affliction-grade can still obstruct a major fruit-transition.

- **samukhīkṛta** — made present, brought face-to-face.
  - **status:** significant operative vocabulary in this passage.
  - **function:** marks actual activation of the path superior to an attained fruit; prior abandonment alone does not constitute the new path-status.

- **avarabhāgīya-saṃyojana** — lower fetter.
  - **status:** established canonical classification invoked here.
  - **function:** supplies an alternative aggregate description of the abandonment underlying non-returning.

- **prahāṇasaṃkulana** — grouping/collective presentation of abandonments.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** warns that a canonical grouped formula need not describe simultaneous abandonment.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the main explanation, with alternative etymological explanations of **ekavīcika** presented without forcing a single exclusive reading.

The five-lower-fetters formula is explicitly treated as a grouped account of abandonments, not as evidence that all five fetters are destroyed for the first time simultaneously at the third fruit.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.36 completes the desire-realm cultivation sequence:

- after destruction of seven or eight grades, the once-returner becomes an **ekavīcika**, a candidate for the third fruit;
- the designation is grounded in the abandonment already accomplished, acquisition of the corresponding uncontaminated counter-faculty, and the fact that only one birth remains;
- **ekavīcika** may be explained as separation from Nirvāṇa by one birth or from the non-return fruit by one remaining affliction-grade;
- even one final weak grade can still obstruct the fruit because the transition involves crossing beyond an entire domain;
- prior abandonment alone does not automatically confer the intermediate path-person designation: the superior path beyond the attained fruit must actually be made present;
- destruction of the ninth grade completes desire-realm abandonment and yields **anāgāmin**, the non-returner;
- the standard five-lower-fetters formula is a grouped description of abandonments whose components have already begun to fall earlier.

The verse therefore presents non-returning as the completion of a genuine domain-transition governed by actual path-operation rather than by numerical accumulation alone.
