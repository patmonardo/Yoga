# VAK 6.63 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> prayogākṣasamāpattivimuktyubhayataḥ kṛtāḥ /  
> pudgalāḥ sapta ṣaṭ tv ete dvau dvau mārgatraye yataḥ // VAK_6.63 //

**Best current translation:**  
Seven persons are distinguished according to application, faculty, meditative attainment, liberation, and both [attainment and liberation]. Yet these are six in actual distinctness, because there are two in each of the three Paths.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.63.md`](./VAK_6.63.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 380|04–380|22.

The preceding passage has just distinguished nine persons according to the nine grades of faculties. Vasubandhu now introduces another classification: all noble persons (*āryapudgala*) can be named in seven ways. The Bhāṣya then explains exactly which principle of differentiation generates each name and, crucially, distinguishes **seven names** from **six actual person-types**.

## 1. The Seven Noble Persons

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The prose first states:

> `sarva eva tv āryapudgalāḥ sapta bhavanti`

All noble persons are seven.

It immediately enumerates them:

> `śraddhānusārī dharmānusārī śraddhādhimukto dṛṣṭiprāptaḥ kāyasākṣī prajñāvimukta ubhayatovimuktaś ca`

Thus:

```text
1. śraddhānusārin
2. dharmānusārin
3. śraddhādhimukta
4. dṛṣṭiprāpta
5. kāyasākṣin
6. prajñāvimukta
7. ubhayatovimukta
```

This confirms the provisional sevenfold list in the Kārikā file.

But the Bhāṣya does much more than enumerate them. It derives the names from different principles of division.

## 2. `Prayoga`: The Two Followers

The kārikā begins:

> `prayogākṣasamāpattivimuktyubhayataḥ kṛtāḥ`

The first differentiating principle is *prayoga*.

The prose says:

> `prayogataḥ śraddhādharmānusāriṇau`

The *śraddhānusārin* and *dharmānusārin* are distinguished according to application or mode of practice.

Vasubandhu explains:

> `pūrvam eva parapratyayadharmānusārābhyām artheṣu prayogāt`

Their distinction lies in the way they had already applied themselves to meanings: one through dependence on another and the other through following Dharma.

The point is genetic. Their names retain the character of the practice by which they entered noble seeing.

```text
prayoga
    ↓
mode of entering / applying oneself to meaning
    ↓
śraddhānusārin  |  dharmānusārin
```

## 3. `Akṣa` / `Indriya`: Faith-Resolved and Seeing-Attained

The second principle is faculty:

> `indriyataḥ śraddhādhimuktadṛṣṭiprāptau`

The *śraddhādhimukta* and *dṛṣṭiprāpta* are differentiated according to faculties.

The explanation is explicit:

> `mṛdutīkṣṇendriyatvāt śraddhādhimokṣaprajñādhikyataḥ`

The distinction is between mild and sharp faculties, correlated respectively with predominance of faith/resolution and predominance of wisdom.

```text
mild faculties
    + predominance of śraddhā / adhimokṣa
    → śraddhādhimukta

sharp faculties
    + predominance of prajñā
    → dṛṣṭiprāpta
```

Thus `akṣa` in the kārikā is not an abstract reference to sense faculties. The Bhāṣya interprets it through the technical Path-category of *indriya*.

## 4. `Samāpatti`: The Body-Witness

The third principle is meditative attainment:

> `samāpattitaḥ kāyasākṣī nirodhasamāpattisākṣātkaraṇāt`

The *kāyasākṣin* is distinguished according to *samāpatti*, because of the direct realization of the attainment of cessation (*nirodhasamāpatti*).

This sharply determines the category:

```text
nirodhasamāpatti
    directly realized
        ↓
kāyasākṣin
```

The body-witness is therefore not simply a metaphorical “embodied knower.” It is a technical designation generated by possession and realization of a specific meditative attainment.

## 5. `Vimukti`: The Wisdom-Liberated

The fourth principle is liberation:

> `vimuktitaḥ prajñāvimuktaḥ`

The *prajñāvimukta* is distinguished according to liberation.

This category will be sharpened immediately after 6.63, but already the classificatory principle is clear: the decisive determination here is the mode of liberation rather than possession of cessation attainment.

## 6. `Ubhaya`: The Doubly Liberated

The final principle combines meditative attainment and liberation:

> `samāpattivimuktita ubhayatobhāgavimuktaḥ`

The *ubhayatobhāgavimukta* is distinguished from both sides: meditative attainment and liberation.

Thus the sevenfold taxonomy is generated through heterogeneous but explicit principles:

```text
prayoga
    → śraddhānusārin
    → dharmānusārin

indriya
    → śraddhādhimukta
    → dṛṣṭiprāpta

samāpatti
    → kāyasākṣin

vimukti
    → prajñāvimukta

samāpatti + vimukti
    → ubhayatobhāgavimukta
```

This confirms that the list is not a single linear hierarchy.

## 7. Seven by Name, Six in Actual Distinctness

The Bhāṣya now makes the crucial qualification:

> `nāmata ete sapta pudgalāḥ`

By name, these are seven persons.

Then, glossing `ṣaṭ tv ete`, it states:

> `dravyatas tv ete ṣaṭ bhavanti`

But in actual distinctness they are six.

Here *dravya* should not be read as introducing seven or six substantial selves. The point is classificatory: seven designations do not correspond to seven mutually exclusive underlying person-types.

Why not?

The kārikā answers:

> `dvau dvau mārgatraye yataḥ`

Because there are two and two in the three Paths.

## 8. The Same Two Persons Across the Three Paths

Vasubandhu explains the developmental identity explicitly.

### Path of Seeing

> `darśanamārge hi dvau pudgalau śraddhādharmānusāriṇau`

In the Path of Seeing there are two:

```text
śraddhānusārin
dharmānusārin
```

### Path of Cultivation

> `tāv eva bhāvanāmārge dvau bhavataḥ śraddhādhimuktadṛṣṭiprāptau`

Those very same two become, in the Path of Cultivation:

```text
śraddhādhimukta
dṛṣṭiprāpta
```

### Path Beyond Training

> `tau punar aśaikṣamārge dvau bhavataḥ samayāsamayavimuktāv iti`

And those two again become, in the Path Beyond Training:

```text
samayavimukta
asamayavimukta
```

This is the central genetic structure of the verse:

```text
DARŚANAMĀRGA
śraddhānusārin      dharmānusārin
      ↓                    ↓

BHĀVANĀMĀRGA
śraddhādhimukta     dṛṣṭiprāpta
      ↓                    ↓

AŚAIKṢAMĀRGA
samayavimukta       asamayavimukta
```

The names change because the operative Path-determination changes. The continuity of the practitioner is preserved through the transformation.

This is why seven names need not imply seven distinct persons.

## 9. The Kāyasākṣin Cross-Cuts the Paired Development

The seventh name, *kāyasākṣin*, does not form an additional fourth developmental line parallel to the others.

It is generated from another axis entirely: possession and realization of *nirodhasamāpatti*.

Thus the taxonomy has a crossing structure:

```text
longitudinal axis
    Path-stage + faculty/history

cross-cutting axis
    meditative attainment
```

This explains why the sevenfold list cannot be understood as seven serial stations on one ladder.

## 10. Vasubandhu Immediately Multiplies the Classification

After establishing the seven/six distinction, the Bhāṣya demonstrates how numerous the possible determinations become once additional variables are introduced.

For the *śraddhānusārin*, Vasubandhu says there are:

- three according to faculties (`indriyatas trayaḥ`)
- five according to disposition (`gotrataḥ pañca`)
- fifteen according to Path-position (`mārgataḥ pañcadaśa`)
- further differentiations according to dispassion (`vairāgyataḥ`)
- nine according to support or basis (`āśrayato nava`)

He then gives an enormous combinatorial total and adds:

> `evam anye 'pi pudgalāḥ saṃbhavataḥ saṃkhyeyāḥ`

Other persons too can be enumerated in this way according to the possible combinations.

This is methodologically important. The named person-types are not exhaustive atoms. They are convenient determinations within a multidimensional classificatory field.

```text
pudgala determination
    =
faculty
+ gotra
+ path-position
+ degree of dispassion
+ existential basis
+ relevant attainment
+ mode of liberation
```

The Kośa can therefore generate very large numbers of “persons” without multiplying metaphysical selves.

## 11. The Meaning of `Pudgala` Here

The Bhāṣya is especially useful for restraining an overly substantial reading of *pudgala*.

The same practitioner can receive different technical names as the Path develops:

```text
same developmental continuum
    ↓
new Path-position
    ↓
new technical designation
```

And one designation such as *kāyasākṣin* may arise from a different classificatory axis than another such as *dṛṣṭiprāpta*.

Thus a Kośa *pudgala*-class is fundamentally a **Path determination**: a way of identifying a continuum according to relevant capacities, attainments, and stage.

## 12. Relation to VAK 6.62

VAK 6.62 had just produced nine persons through the nine grades of faculties:

```text
7 Śrāvaka forms
+ Pratyekabuddha
+ Buddha
= 9
```

VAK 6.63 now changes the principle of division.

It asks not for nine grades of faculty but for the principal ways in which noble disciples are designated through:

```text
practice
faculty
attainment
liberation
combined attainment + liberation
```

Therefore the numbers are not rivals:

```text
9
    classification by graded faculty

7 names / 6 actual types
    classification by Path-function and attainment
```

The Bhāṣya makes it impossible to compare these enumerations without first identifying the relevant principle of division.

## 13. Restrained Organon Contact

The strongest conceptual result is the identity-through-development structure:

```text
one practitioner-line
    ↓
new determination of Path
    ↓
new valid name
```

The later determination does not merely replace an arbitrary label. It expresses an objective change in the practitioner's relation to the Path.

Yet neither does the new name posit a new substantial subject.

This gives a useful distinction between:

```text
numerical proliferation of descriptions
    and
actual multiplication of entities
```

Vasubandhu's own `nāmataḥ / dravyataḥ` distinction performs exactly this work here.

The enormous combinatorial enumeration that follows reinforces the point: a rational taxonomy can generate very many determinate positions from a limited number of underlying continuities by crossing independent dimensions of determination.

For the Organon pass, this will be highly useful. For now, the Bhāṣya's own architecture should remain primary.

## New Technical Terms

- **prayoga** — application, preparatory mode, or manner of undertaking the Path  
  **status:** recurrent, made classificatorily explicit here  
  **function:** distinguishes the *śraddhānusārin* and *dharmānusārin*

- **akṣa / indriya** — faculty or operative spiritual capacity  
  **status:** recurrent  
  **function:** distinguishes mild- and sharp-faculty noble persons

- **nirodhasamāpattisākṣātkaraṇa** — direct realization of the attainment of cessation  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression  
  **function:** defines the basis for the designation *kāyasākṣin*

- **kāyasākṣin** — body-witness  
  **status:** established noble-person category  
  **function:** person distinguished through direct realization of cessation attainment

- **prajñāvimukta** — wisdom-liberated  
  **status:** established noble-person category  
  **function:** person distinguished according to liberation through wisdom

- **ubhayatobhāgavimukta** — liberated in both respects / doubly liberated  
  **status:** established technical category  
  **function:** person distinguished through both meditative attainment and liberation

- **nāmataḥ** — by name or designation  
  **status:** Bhāṣya methodological term in this passage  
  **function:** marks the sevenfold nominal classification

- **dravyataḥ** — in actual distinctness  
  **status:** technically important here  
  **function:** reduces the seven designations to six non-duplicated person-types; should not be read as asserting substantial selves

- **mārgatraya** — the three Paths: seeing, cultivation, and beyond-training  
  **status:** recurrent structural category  
  **function:** explains the paired developmental transformation of the principal persons

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the principal exposition.

The kārikā file's provisional statement that “six form three corresponding pairs across the three principal stages of the Path” is confirmed, but the Bhāṣya adds the decisive distinction between **seven names** and **six in actual distinctness**.

The term `dravyataḥ` should be handled carefully. In this local argument it contrasts actual non-duplicated classification with mere multiplicity of names. It does not authorize reading these *pudgalas* as permanent substantial selves.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.63 derives seven noble-person designations from five classificatory principles: application, faculty, meditative attainment, liberation, and the combination of attainment with liberation. The seven are *śraddhānusārin*, *dharmānusārin*, *śraddhādhimukta*, *dṛṣṭiprāpta*, *kāyasākṣin*, *prajñāvimukta*, and *ubhayatovimukta*. Yet Vasubandhu says they are only six in actual distinctness, because the two persons of the Path of Seeing become the corresponding two persons of the Path of Cultivation and then the two persons of the Path Beyond Training. The names therefore track real transformations of one developmental continuum rather than multiplying substantial subjects. The enormous combinatorial enumeration that follows confirms that Kośa person-taxonomy is a multidimensional classification of Path-configurations rather than a flat list of selves.
