# VAK 6.22 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**ādye dve parihāṇyā ca maules tatraiva satyadṛk |**  
**apūrvāptir vihīneṣu hānī dve asamanvitiḥ || 6.22 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 347|08–347|23.

The passage distinguishes ordinary loss through death from regression, identifies the special stability of Acceptance and the Highest Mundane Dharmas, explains reacquisition after loss, and defines the two modes of loss through non-possession.

## 1. Heat and Summits Can Still Regress

Vasubandhu first qualifies the previous verse:

> **ādye dve parihāṇyā ca**

The first two nirvedhabhāgīyas—**uṣmagata** (Heat) and **mūrdhan** (Summits)—may be lost by an ordinary practitioner not only through death but also through **parihāṇi**, regression or decline.

By contrast:

> **kṣāntyagradharmābhyāṃ tu pṛthagjanasyāpi nāsti parihāṇiḥ**

Even an ordinary person does not regress from **kṣānti** or the **laukikāgradharmas**.

This produces a decisive stability threshold:

```text
Heat / Summits
    attainment exists
    but may regress

Acceptance / Highest Mundane Dharmas
    attainment has become non-regressive
```

The Path therefore changes not only in content and strength but in its **modal stability**.

## 2. Principal-Dhyāna Support Guarantees Truth-Seeing in This Life

The verse continues:

> **maules tatraiva satyadṛk**

Vasubandhu explains that whoever produces the nirvedhabhāgīyas on the level of a **principal dhyāna** necessarily sees the Truths **in that very birth**:

> **satatraiva janmāti satyāny avaśyaṃ paśyati**

The reason is:

> **tīvrasaṃvegatvāt** — because of the intensity of the impetus / urgency.

This is an especially important point for the genesis of operative Path Knowledge. A sufficiently powerful concentration-ground does not merely make Truth cognition more vivid; it makes the transition to Truth-seeing causally inevitable within that life, barring the relevant framework of conditions.

```text
principal-dhyāna nirvedhabhāgīya
    + intense saṃvega
        ↓
Truth-seeing in this very life
```

The preparatory cognition has therefore acquired a determinate temporal consequence.

## 3. Reacquisition Is Not Recovery of the Same Dharmas

Vasubandhu then asks what happens if an attainment has been lost and later obtained again:

> **apūrvāptir vihīneṣu**

When there is acquisition again after loss, what is acquired is **new**; the previously abandoned dharmas do not return.

He compares this to the prātimokṣa discipline: its reacquisition requires renewed effort rather than numerical restoration of what ceased.

So continuity of the Path does not require substantial persistence of one identical cognitive entity:

```text
former Path-dharma
    ceases

later renewed effort
    ↓
new Path-dharma arises
```

This is an important Abhidharma control on our language of “stable knowledge.” Stability means stability of causal structure and capacity, not survival of a permanent cognitive substance.

## 4. Vihāni and Parihāṇi

Vasubandhu explicitly asks:

> What are **vihāni** and **parihāṇi** by intrinsic nature?

The Kārikā answers:

> **hānī dve asamanvitiḥ**

Both are forms of **asamanvāgama**—non-possession or no longer being endowed with the relevant dharmas.

But their causal structures differ.

### Parihāṇi

> **parihāṇis tu doṣakṛtā**

Regression is produced through a defect or fault.

### Vihāni

Complete loss need not be caused by such a defect. Vasubandhu adds that it can also result from a **guṇaviśeṣa**, a superior or different quality-condition—for example, when a prior level is relinquished through movement to another attainment.

Thus:

```text
same resultant status:
    non-possession

but different genesis:
    regression → defect-conditioned
    relinquishment → not necessarily defect-conditioned
```

This distinction matters enormously. Not every cessation of a former Path-state is spiritual failure. A state may cease because its function has been superseded.

## 5. Even Regression Does Not Erase the Entire Path-Causal Transformation

The Bhāṣya closes with a remarkable qualification:

> Even one who has regressed after obtaining Heat is **niyataṃ parinirvāṇadharmā**—determined toward final nirvāṇa.

Vasubandhu asks how this differs from an ordinary **mokṣabhāgīya**, a root conducive to liberation.

His answer:

> **satyadarśanāsannataratvam asaty antarāye**

The Heat-attainer is **nearer to seeing the Truths**, provided no obstacle intervenes.

So regression does not mean returning to the exact previous practical condition.

A causal transformation has already occurred:

```text
before Heat
    liberation-oriented capacity

Heat acquired
    nearer to Truth-seeing

Heat regressed from
    immediate attainment lost
    but causal proximity to liberation remains altered
```

This gives a particularly subtle model of Path Knowledge becoming operative: an acquired Path-capacity may cease as a presently possessed dharma while leaving the continuum **irreversibly reoriented**.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

The distinction now becomes:

```text
Truth as content
    can be forgotten or contradicted

Path Knowledge as cultivated root
    can become causally embedded

advanced Path Knowledge
    changes what regression itself can mean
```

The crucial point is that operative Truth is measured not merely by whether a cognition is presently occurring but by what **causal possibilities remain open or have become closed** in the continuum.

## New Technical Terms

- **parihāṇi** — regression or decline from an acquired attainment; here specifically defect-conditioned.
  - **status:** newly central in this passage.
  - **function:** distinguishes unstable attainment from irreversible stages.

- **vihāni** — relinquishment / complete loss of an attainment.
  - **status:** contrasted explicitly with parihāṇi.
  - **function:** identifies loss not necessarily caused by defect.

- **asamanvāgama** — non-possession, no longer being endowed with a dharma.
  - **status:** technical Abhidharma possession vocabulary.
  - **function:** common intrinsic characterization of both forms of loss.

- **tīvrasaṃvega** — intense impetus, urgency, or spiritual shock/energy.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory ground here.
  - **function:** explains inevitable Truth-seeing in the same birth for principal-dhyāna preparations.

- **guṇaviśeṣa** — superior/different qualitative condition.
  - **status:** introduced in explaining non-defective loss.
  - **function:** shows that relinquishment can result from advancement rather than failure.

- **parinirvāṇadharman** — having final nirvāṇa as a determinate future orientation.
  - **status:** important consequence attached even to one who has regressed from Heat.
  - **function:** marks retained causal orientation after loss of present attainment.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu explaining the Vaibhāṣika path taxonomy.

The passage should not be read as positing a permanent latent entity that survives regression. Its own doctrine explicitly says reacquired preparatory dharmas are numerically new. What persists is better understood as a transformed causal orientation of the continuum.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.22 deepens the theory of Path stability:

- Heat and Summits remain regressible.
- Acceptance and Highest Mundane Dharmas are non-regressive even for an ordinary practitioner.
- Preparations grounded in a principal dhyāna necessarily lead to Truth-seeing in that same life because of intense saṃvega.
- Reacquired preparations are newly arisen dharmas, not recovered identical entities.
- Vihāni and parihāṇi both terminate present possession, but only regression is necessarily defect-conditioned.
- Even regression from Heat leaves the practitioner nearer to Truth-seeing and determined toward final nirvāṇa.

The Path is therefore becoming operative precisely by changing the **space of possible futures** of the practitioner.
