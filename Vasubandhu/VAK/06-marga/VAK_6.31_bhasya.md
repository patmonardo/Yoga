# VAK 6.31 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**ṣoḍaśe tu phalasthau tau yatra yaḥ pratipannakaḥ |**  
**śraddhādhimukta-dṛṣṭyāptau mṛdu-tīkṣṇendriyau tadā || 6.31 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 354|09–354|19.

## 1. The Sixteenth Moment Changes the Practitioner’s Status

Vasubandhu begins with the direct consequence of the sixteenth cognitive moment:

> **ṣoḍaśe tu citta utpanne tau na punaḥ śraddhādharmānusāriṇāv ucyete**

When the sixteenth citta has arisen, the two practitioners are no longer called:

- **śraddhānusārin** — Faith-Follower;
- **dharmānusārin** — Dharma-Follower.

Nor are they any longer:

> **pratipannakau** — candidates progressing toward a fruit.

Vasubandhu asks:

> **kiṃ tarhi?** — What, then?

The answer is concise:

> **phalasthau** — established in the fruit.

Thus the transition introduced structurally in VAK 6.28 is now determined on the side of the practitioner:

```text
moments 1–15
    pratipannaka
    progressing toward fruit

sixteenth moment
    phalastha
    established in fruit
```

The sixteenth moment therefore changes not merely the cognitive classification of the path but the formal status of the path-person.

## 2. Each Becomes Established in the Fruit Already Approached

Vasubandhu glosses:

> **yatra phale yaḥ pratipannako bhūtaḥ sa tadānīṃ tatra phalasthito bhavati**

Whichever fruit each practitioner had been progressing toward, at that time that practitioner becomes established in that very fruit.

The possible fruits are explicitly listed:

> **srota-āpatti-phale sakṛdāgāmi-phale vā anāgāmi-phale vā**

```text
stream-entry fruit
or
once-return fruit
or
non-return fruit
```

The sixteenth moment therefore does not erase the distinctions produced by prior abandonment. It actualizes the determinate trajectory already established during the fifteen moments of Seeing.

```text
prior abandonment-state
    ↓
fruit-candidacy during Seeing
    ↓
sixteenth moment
    ↓
establishment in that corresponding fruit
```

The process is path-dependent: the result is generated from the practitioner’s already determinate position.

## 3. Why Arhatship Cannot Be Reached Immediately

Vasubandhu inserts an important exclusion:

> **arhattvaṃ tu na śakyam āditaḥ prāptum**

Arhatship cannot be attained immediately at the outset.

He gives two reasons.

### First reason: cultivation-removable afflictions remain

> **darśanamārgeṇa bhāvanāheyānām aprahāṇāt**

The Path of Seeing does not abandon the afflictions that are removable through cultivation.

This maintains the distinction established in VAK 6.28:

```text
darśanamārga
    abandons darśana-heya
    what is removable through Seeing

bhāvanāmārga
    abandons bhāvanā-heya
    what is removable through Cultivation
```

Seeing is therefore decisive but not universally exhaustive.

### Second reason: prior detachment cannot extend through the summit of existence

> **pūrvaṃ ca bhavāgravairāgyāsaṃbhavāt**

Prior detachment from **bhavāgra**, the summit of existence, is impossible.

A practitioner may have entered the Path of Seeing with substantial prior mundane detachment, even extending through the Sphere of Nothingness, as VAK 6.30 explained. But the final conditioned summit cannot already have been transcended by that mundane procedure.

Hence no configuration exists in which the first completion of Seeing can directly yield arhatship.

This is an important methodological constraint:

```text
Seeing
    has a determinate causal scope

prior mundane detachment
    has a determinate upper limit

therefore
    immediate arhatship is excluded
```

The classificatory scheme is thus controlled by causal conditions rather than by free assignment of labels.

## 4. New Names at the Sixteenth Moment

The second half of the verse renames the two practitioners.

### Gentle faculty

Vasubandhu states:

> **yo mṛdvindriyaḥ śraddhānusāripūrvī sa śraddhādhimukta ity ucyate**

The practitioner of gentle faculty who was formerly a Faith-Follower is now called:

> **śraddhādhimukta**

The designation marks the same continuum after attainment of fruit.

```text
mṛdu-indriya
    ↓
śraddhānusārin
    during Seeing
    ↓
śraddhādhimukta
    once established in fruit
```

### Sharp faculty

Similarly:

> **yas tīkṣṇendriyo dharmānusāripūrvī sa dṛṣṭiprāpta ity ucyate**

The sharp-faculty practitioner formerly called Dharma-Follower is now:

> **dṛṣṭiprāpta** — one who has attained through seeing/view.

```text
tīkṣṇa-indriya
    ↓
dharmānusārin
    during Seeing
    ↓
dṛṣṭiprāpta
    once established in fruit
```

The practitioner’s faculty remains the same; the name changes because the path-position has changed.

## 5. Why These Two Names?

Vasubandhu gives the reason:

> **śraddhā-prajñā-adhikatvena adhimokṣa-dṛṣṭi-prabhāvitatvāt**

The distinction is grounded in the predominance of **śraddhā** and **prajñā**, and consequently in the predominance of **adhimokṣa** and **dṛṣṭi**.

The structure is:

```text
gentle faculty
    predominance of śraddhā
    ↓
    adhimokṣa predominates
    ↓
    śraddhādhimukta

sharp faculty
    predominance of prajñā
    ↓
    dṛṣṭi predominates
    ↓
    dṛṣṭiprāpta
```

This is more precise than treating the two names as merely devotional versus intellectual personality types.

The Bhāṣya ties them to a specific distribution of faculties within the attained noble continuum.

## 6. Path-Names Are Functional, Not Personal Essences

The same continuum passes through a sequence of names:

```text
before fruit:
    śraddhānusārin / dharmānusārin

at the sixteenth moment:
    śraddhādhimukta / dṛṣṭiprāpta
```

Nothing in the passage suggests a new substantial person appearing at the sixteenth moment.

Rather:

```text
same continuum
same faculty-type
same causal history
new path-position
    ↓
new technical designation
```

The nomenclature therefore functions as a scientific classification of the practitioner according to:

- faculty;
- causal history;
- current path-stage;
- attained fruit.

This is exactly the kind of compressed technical presentation that makes the chapter initially appear taxonomic while actually encoding a transformation-process.

## 7. The Sixteenth Moment as Result-Position

The previous verses established:

```text
Seeing
    converts the unseen into seen

Acceptance
    functions as uninterrupted path

Knowledge
    functions as liberation path
```

VAK 6.31 now adds the practitioner-side result:

```text
fifteen moments
    fruit is still approached

sixteenth moment
    fruit becomes occupied position
```

This is a clean distinction between:

```text
process-position
    pratipannaka

result-position
    phalastha
```

The sixteenth moment therefore marks a genuine phase transition in the path-person.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

The Path of Seeing sequence can now be expressed with both process and person included:

```text
first uncontaminated kṣānti
    ↓
entry into Noble status
    ↓

fifteen Seeing moments
    practitioner = pratipannaka
    fruit = determinate end approached
    ↓

sixteenth moment
    complete truth-field already seen
    Path of Cultivation begins
    ↓

practitioner = phalastha
    fruit = attained position
```

The corresponding subjective names transform simultaneously:

```text
Faith-Follower
    → Faith-Resolved / śraddhādhimukta

Dharma-Follower
    → attained through Seeing / dṛṣṭiprāpta
```

The crucial restriction is equally important:

```text
Seeing does not remove every possible affliction
and mundane detachment cannot pre-remove bhavāgra

therefore:
    completion of Seeing
    cannot immediately equal arhatship
```

The method is thus not merely a sequence of labels. Each transition is constrained by what the preceding path has actually accomplished.

## New Technical Terms

- **phalastha** — one established in a fruit.
  - **status:** central path-person classification, contrasted here with **pratipannaka**.
  - **function:** identifies the practitioner after the sixteenth moment as possessing the fruit previously approached.

- **śraddhādhimukta** — one resolved/liberated through faith; the post-Seing designation of the former Faith-Follower.
  - **status:** new path-person designation in this sequence.
  - **function:** names the gentle-faculty practitioner established in fruit, with predominance of faith and adhimokṣa.

- **dṛṣṭiprāpta** — one who has attained through seeing/view.
  - **status:** new path-person designation in this sequence.
  - **function:** names the sharp-faculty practitioner established in fruit, with predominance of prajñā and dṛṣṭi.

- **adhimokṣa** — resolve, decisive inclination, determination.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term used to account for the name śraddhādhimukta.
  - **function:** faculty-expression predominating in the faith-dominant practitioner.

- **bhavāgra** — summit of existence, the highest conditioned level.
  - **status:** established cosmological/path term, crucial here as an upper limit of prior mundane detachment.
  - **function:** explains why arhatship cannot be obtained immediately through the first completion of Seeing.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the standard path classification.

No competing doctrinal voices are introduced in this short passage.

Translation caution: **śraddhādhimukta** is difficult to capture with one English expression. “Faith-Liberated” is traditional but may suggest a stronger completed liberation than the technical classification intends. For this pass the Sanskrit should remain visible and its functional definition should govern interpretation.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.31 determines the sixteenth moment from the side of the path-person:

- the Faith-Follower and Dharma-Follower cease to be **pratipannakas** and become **phalasthas**;
- each becomes established in exactly the fruit toward which prior abandonment had made that practitioner a candidate: stream-entry, once-return, or non-return;
- immediate arhatship is impossible because Seeing does not abandon cultivation-removable afflictions and prior mundane detachment cannot extend through the summit of existence;
- the gentle-faculty practitioner is now called **śraddhādhimukta**;
- the sharp-faculty practitioner is now called **dṛṣṭiprāpta**;
- these names arise from the relative predominance of faith/adhimokṣa and prajñā/dṛṣṭi.

The sixteenth moment therefore converts a directed path-process into a determinate attained position without exceeding the causal work actually accomplished by the Path.
