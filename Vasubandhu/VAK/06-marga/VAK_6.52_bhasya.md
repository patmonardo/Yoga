# VAK 6.52 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> catuṣphalavyavasthā tu pañcakāraṇasaṃbhavāt /  
> pūrvatyāgo 'nyamārgāptiḥ kṣayasaṃkalanaṃ phale // VAkK_6.52 //

**Best current translation:**  
The establishment of the four fruits is due to the occurrence of five reasons: relinquishment of the preceding [path], attainment of another path, and the gathering of cessations into the fruit.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.52.md`](./VAK_6.52.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 370|02–370|12.

The Bhāṣya explains the five reasons as one unit. The first three are stated in VAK 6.52; the remaining two are supplied by the opening half-line of VAK 6.53 (`jñānāṣṭakasya lābho 'tha ṣoḍaśākārabhāvanā`). This file keeps the principal analysis centered on the three reasons stated in 6.52 while recording the full fivefold structure needed to understand why only four principal fruits are established.

## 1. Why Only Four Fruits if There Are Eighty-Nine?

**Voice:** Objector / questioner

VAK 6.51 has just established eighty-nine conditioned śrāmaṇya-fruits together with eighty-nine unconditioned abandonments. This creates an obvious problem:

```text
many śrāmaṇya-fruits
    ↓
why does the Buddha establish
only four principal fruits?
```

The Bhāṣya raises precisely this issue:

> `evaṃ tarhi buddhasyopasaṃkhyānaṃ kartavyaṃ jāyate`

Would this not require a further enumeration by the Buddha?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

No. Although the fruits are many, the fourfold establishment is governed by a stricter criterion.

> `yadyapi bhūyāṃsi phalāni`
>
> `catuṣphalavyavasthā tu pañcakāraṇasaṃbhavāt`

A path-stage is established as one of the **four principal fruits** only where five reasons occur together.

This is the key distinction:

```text
fruit in the broad technical sense
    ≠
principal fruit-position
```

The eighty-nine count concerns individual conditioned and unconditioned fruits. The fourfold system identifies major reorganizations of the practitioner's attained path-status.

## 2. First Reason — Relinquishment of the Previous Path

The first criterion is:

> `pūrvatyāgaḥ`

The Bhāṣya expands this as:

> `pūrvamārgatyāgaḥ`

— relinquishment of the previous path.

Vasubandhu explains this together with the second criterion:

> `pratipannakaphalamārgatyāgalābhāt`

The practitioner relinquishes the path belonging to the candidate-for-fruit (`pratipannaka`) and acquires the path belonging to the fruit-state.

The transition is therefore not merely additive:

```text
old path-status
    + new attainment
```

but transformational:

```text
candidate path relinquished
    ↓
fruit path attained
```

A principal fruit marks a real change of path-position.

## 3. Second Reason — Attainment of Another / Previously Unattained Path

The second criterion is:

> `anyamārgāptiḥ`

The Bhāṣya glosses it more sharply:

> `apūrvamārgāptiḥ`

— attainment of a path not previously possessed.

Thus `anya` is not merely numerical otherness. The point is that the practitioner enters a **new operative path-status**.

```text
previous candidate-path
    relinquished
        ↓
previously unattained fruit-path
    acquired
```

This is why a principal fruit cannot be identified simply with one more liberation-path in an already continuous series. It requires a qualitative transition in the practitioner's relation to the Path.

## 4. Third Reason — Gathering the Abandonments into One Fruit

The third criterion is:

> `kṣayasaṃkalanaṃ phale`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `prahāṇasaṃkalanaṃ sarvasyaikaprāptilābhāt`

— the gathering together of the abandonments, because a **single attainment** of the whole is acquired.

This is especially important after the eighty-ninefold enumeration of 6.51.

The various abandonments are not merely left as an aggregate of isolated accomplishments:

```text
abandonment 1
abandonment 2
abandonment 3
...
```

At a principal fruit they are gathered under one attainment:

```text
multiple accomplished abandonments
        ↓
   eka-prāpti
        ↓
one fruit-position
```

`saṃkalana` therefore expresses integration or collection into a new attained whole.

The fruit is not another cessation added to earlier cessations. It is a path-position in which the relevant abandonments are **possessed together**.

## 5. The Complete Fivefold Criterion

The Bhāṣya immediately continues with the remaining two reasons, stated in the opening half of VAK 6.53:

> `jñānāṣṭakasya lābho 'tha ṣoḍaśākārabhāvanā`

These are:

4. simultaneous acquisition of the **eight knowledges**;
5. cultivation of the **sixteen aspects** beginning with impermanence.

Vasubandhu concludes:

> `imāni hi pañca kāraṇāni phale phale bhavanti`

These five reasons occur at each [principal] fruit.

So the complete criterion is:

```text
1. relinquishment of previous path
2. attainment of a new path
3. integration of abandonments into one attainment
4. acquisition of eight knowledges
5. cultivation of sixteen truth-aspects
        ↓
principal śrāmaṇya-fruit
```

The last two will be analyzed fully under VAK 6.53, where they belong textually.

## 6. Fruit as Reorganization Rather Than Isolated Reward

The Bhāṣya confirms the strongest point already visible in the Kārikā seed: a principal fruit is not an isolated reward occurring after some piece of practice.

It is a **reorganization of the attained Path**.

Three determinations already stated in 6.52 make this clear:

```text
previous determination
    relinquished

new determination
    attained

previous abandonments
    gathered into one possession
```

The old path is not simply annihilated. Its accomplished content persists in the abandonments that are now gathered into the new fruit-attainment. What is relinquished is its status as the practitioner's present governing path.

This yields a restrained genetic reading:

```text
prior path
    ↓ relinquished as current form
accomplished abandonments
    ↓ preserved as attained content
new path
    ↓ acquired
integrated fruit-position
```

The Kośa's taxonomy is therefore historical in the strict practical sense: the present status contains the determinate results of previous path-work while no longer being identical with the previous path-form.

## 7. Relation to VAK 6.51

VAK 6.51 distinguished:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    = śrāmaṇya

vimukti-mārga
    = conditioned fruit

prahāṇa / kṣaya
    = unconditioned fruit
```

VAK 6.52 now explains why these numerous fruits do not generate an equally numerous list of principal fruit-persons.

The eighty-ninefold analysis answers:

> How many determinate fruits arise through the abandoning process?

The fourfold analysis answers a different question:

> At which transitions is the whole attained path-status reorganized strongly enough to constitute a principal fruit?

Thus:

```text
89
    analytical enumeration of fruit-events / attainments

4
    structural organization of major path-positions
```

There is no contradiction between the two numbers because they classify at different levels.

## 8. New Technical Terms

- **catuṣphalavyavasthā** — establishment or determination of the four principal fruits.  
  **Status:** clarified in the Bhāṣya.  
  **Function:** distinguishes principal fruit-positions from the larger analytical enumeration of fruits.

- **pūrvamārgatyāga** — relinquishment of the previous path.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `pūrvatyāga`.  
  **Function:** first criterion marking transition from candidate-path to fruit-path.

- **apūrvamārgāpti** — attainment of a previously unattained path.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya gloss of `anyamārgāpti`.  
  **Function:** second criterion establishing qualitative path-transition.

- **prahāṇasaṃkalana** — gathering or integration of abandonments.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya gloss of `kṣayasaṃkalana`.  
  **Function:** third criterion; collects previously accomplished abandonments into one fruit-attainment.

- **eka-prāpti** — single attainment.  
  **Status:** important Bhāṣya specification.  
  **Function:** explains how a plurality of abandonments is possessed as one fruit-position.

## 9. Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Objector / questioner — raises the numerical problem generated by the eighty-nine fruits of 6.51.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu — answers that the Buddha's fourfold fruit-system is determined by the concurrence of five criteria.

Textual boundary caution: the final two criteria are stated in the first half of VAK 6.53 but are syntactically and doctrinally required to complete the fivefold explanation announced in 6.52. They are recorded here only in outline and will receive their detailed analysis with 6.53.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.52 explains why the many fruits enumerated in 6.51 are nevertheless organized into only four principal śrāmaṇya-fruits. A principal fruit is established where a complete transformation of path-status occurs.

The three criteria stated directly in this verse are:

```text
previous path relinquished
        ↓
new path attained
        ↓
multiple abandonments gathered
into one attainment
        ↓
principal fruit-position
```

The opening of 6.53 adds acquisition of the eight knowledges and cultivation of the sixteen truth-aspects, completing the fivefold criterion.

The central distinction is therefore:

```text
many determinate fruits
    ≠
four major fruit-positions
```

The former enumerate the results produced throughout the Path; the latter mark the moments at which those results are reorganized into a new integrated practical status.
