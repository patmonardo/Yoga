# VAK 6.32 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**phale phalaviśiṣṭasya lābho mārgasya nāsty ataḥ |**  
**nāprayukto viśeṣāya phalasthaḥ pratipannakaḥ || 6.32 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 354|18–355|03.

## 1. The Question: Why No Immediate Promotion?

Vasubandhu asks why a practitioner who had already abandoned five grades before the sixteenth moment is still called a **srotaāpanna**, rather than immediately being classified as a candidate for the once-returner fruit:

> **kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇaṃ prahīṇapañcaprakāro 'pi ṣoḍaśe citte srotaāpanna evocyate na sakṛdāgāmiphalapratipannakaḥ**

This is not a merely terminological puzzle. It tests the causal rules governing transition from one fruit-position to the superior path beyond that fruit.

## 2. Attaining a Fruit Does Not Simultaneously Give the Superior Path

Vasubandhu states the governing rule:

> **phale hi labhyamāne phalaviśiṣṭo mārgo na labhyata ityeṣa niyamaḥ**

When a fruit is being attained, the path superior to that fruit is not simultaneously attained. This is a fixed rule (**niyama**).

The distinction is therefore:

```text
fruit-attainment
    ≠
attainment of the superior path beyond that fruit
```

The sixteenth moment establishes the practitioner in the fruit toward which the Path of Seeing had been directed. It does not at the same instant initiate the next superior path.

## 3. A Fruit-Established Person Is Not Yet a New Candidate

Vasubandhu continues:

> **ataḥ phalastho yāvanna viśeṣāya prayujyate phalāntaraprāptau tāvat pratipannako nocyate**

Therefore, so long as one who is established in a fruit has not applied oneself toward a further distinction (**viśeṣa**) for attainment of another fruit, that person is not called a **pratipannaka** toward that higher fruit.

The sequence is exact:

```text
phalastha
    established in a fruit
        ↓

prayoga toward viśeṣa
    renewed application toward superiority
        ↓

pratipannaka
    candidate toward another fruit
```

A new candidacy requires a new orientation of practice.

## 4. Position and Transition Are Distinct

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes two kinds of path determination:

```text
phalastha
    attained position

pratipannaka
    active transition toward a further position
```

One cannot infer transition merely from position.

This is why prior abandonment does not automatically propel the practitioner into the next fruit-path at the sixteenth moment. The prior condition constrains what is possible, but a further **prayoga** is still required to constitute the next directed path-process.

## 5. The Rule Applies to the Other Fruits as Well

Vasubandhu adds:

> **evam anyatrāpi phale veditavyam**

The same rule is to be understood for the other fruits.

So this is a general path-law, not an exception specific to stream-entry:

```text
attain fruit N
    ↓
stand in fruit N
    ↓
renewed application toward superiority
    ↓
become candidate toward fruit N+1
```

The path does not automatically telescope one fruit into the next.

## 6. A Qualified Exception Concerning the Supporting Level

Vasubandhu then gives a special case:

> **yas tu tṛtīyadhyānavītarāgo 'dharāṃ bhūmi niśritya niyāmam avakrāmati so 'vaśyaṃ phalaviśiṣṭaṃ mārgaṃ saṃmukhīkaroti**

Someone already detached from the third dhyāna who enters **niyāma** while relying on a lower level must necessarily bring the superior path face-to-face.

Why?

> **anyathā hi sa tasmād ūrdhvopapannaḥ sukhendriyeṇāsamanvāgataḥ syāt**

Otherwise, if reborn above that level, the practitioner would lack possession of the pleasure faculty.

This is a highly technical qualification tied to the relation between prior detachment, supporting level, and the faculties available in later rebirth. It should not be generalized into the ordinary fruit-transition rule.

## 7. Completion of the Prior-Detachment Classification

Vasubandhu closes the discussion:

> **evaṃ tāvad bhūyaḥ kāmavītarāgāṇāṃ niyāmāvakrāntau pudgalavyavasthānam**

Thus far has been explained the classification of persons who enter **niyāma** with prior detachment from the desire realm to various degrees.

He then turns to the sequential case:

> **ānupūrvikaṃ tu vaktavyam**

The gradual or sequential case must now be explained.

This prepares VAK 6.33 and following.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.32 introduces a crucial rule of path-transition:

```text
Seeing completes
    ↓
fruit is attained
    ↓
practitioner becomes phalastha
    ↓
no automatic superior-path acquisition
    ↓
renewed prayoga toward viśeṣa
    ↓
new pratipannaka-status
```

This sharply distinguishes:

```text
attainment
from
further-directed activity
```

The practitioner is not a continuously advancing label. Each transition requires the causal conditions appropriate to that transition.

The path-person classification is therefore dynamic but not automatic.

## New Technical Terms

- **phalaviśiṣṭa-mārga** — path superior to a given fruit.
  - **status:** central Bhāṣya explanatory term in this passage.
  - **function:** names the path leading beyond an already attained fruit.

- **viśeṣa** — superiority, further distinction, higher attainment.
  - **status:** important path-development term.
  - **function:** object of renewed application after fruit-attainment.

- **prayujyate / prayoga** — applies oneself, renewed application.
  - **status:** established practice vocabulary, functionally decisive here.
  - **function:** converts a fruit-established person into one actively proceeding toward a further fruit.

- **niyama** — fixed rule, determination.
  - **status:** previously encountered in **niyāmāvakrānti**, here used as a rule-governing term.
  - **function:** states that fruit-attainment and acquisition of the superior path do not occur as one event.

- **ānupūrvika** — gradual, sequential.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya transition term at the close of the passage.
  - **function:** opens the next analysis of practitioners progressing through the fruits in order.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the governing path-rule and one technical qualification concerning a practitioner detached from the third dhyāna.

The special case tied to rebirth and the pleasure faculty should remain local. It does not cancel the general rule that a fruit-established practitioner requires renewed application before becoming a candidate toward a further fruit.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.32 distinguishes attained position from renewed transition.

- Attainment of a fruit does not simultaneously give the path superior to that fruit.
- A **phalastha** is not yet a **pratipannaka** toward another fruit.
- New candidate-status arises only when there is renewed **prayoga** toward further **viśeṣa**.
- This rule applies generally across the fruits.
- Vasubandhu then closes the classification of practitioners entering the Noble order with prior detachment and prepares the sequential analysis beginning in VAK 6.33.

The operative structure is therefore:

```text
fruit attained
    → position established
    → renewed application
    → superior path
    → new fruit-candidacy
```

The Path proceeds by determinate transitions, not by automatic promotion.
