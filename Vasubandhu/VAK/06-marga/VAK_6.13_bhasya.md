# VAK 6.13 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 6.13**

> ānāpānau yataḥ kāyaḥ sattvākhyāv anupāttakau |
> naiḥṣyandikau nādhareṇa lakṣyete manasā ca tau || 6.13 ||

Working translation:

> Inhalation and exhalation belong to the same level as the body; they are designated within the sentient continuum, yet are unappropriated. They are homogeneous-flow products, and are apprehended by mind of their own or a higher level, not by a lower one.

## Bhāṣya Source and Natural Argument

Vasubandhu now turns from the classification of **ānāpānasmṛti** to the classification of the breaths themselves.

He first explains:

> **yadbhūmiko hi kāyaḥ tadbhūmikāvetau / kāyaikadeśatvāt /**
>
> Whatever level the body belongs to, the inhalation and exhalation belong to that same level, because they are a part of the body.

He then adds an important condition:

> **kāyacittaviśeṣasaṃniśritā āśvāsapraśvāsā vartante /**
>
> Inhalations and exhalations occur in dependence upon a particular condition of body and cognition.

They are absent among beings of the formless domain, absent in the embryonic kalala state, absent in the attainment of non-cognition, and absent in the fourth dhyāna. If the body is sufficiently open and cognition of a breathing-level is present, the breaths occur.

Vasubandhu further describes their entrance and exit: they enter at birth and when one emerges from the fourth dhyāna; they cease at death and when one enters the fourth dhyāna.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 6.12 established ānāpānasmṛti as **Prajñā** taking vāyu as its object-range. 6.13 now prevents that object from being treated as a vague or merely subjective sensation.

The breath-process receives a full Abhidharma determination:

```text
same level as the body
    because it is a bodily part

conditioned by a specific body-cognition configuration
    not universally present in every embodied state

sattvākhyā
    counted within the sentient continuum

anupāttaka
    not appropriated as an indriya

naiḥṣyandika
    a homogeneous-flow product

known by cognition of its own or a higher level
    not by a lower-level cognition
```

The crucial point is that breath belongs to the living continuum without being reducible to an appropriated faculty.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu explaining the Vaibhāṣika-Abhidharma classification.

No major objection-response structure occurs in this short unit; the passage is primarily definitional and classificatory.

## New Technical Terms

- **kāyacittaviśeṣasaṃniśrita** — dependent upon a specific configuration of body and cognition.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion not explicit in the Kārikā.
  - **function:** states the conditions under which breathing occurs.

- **sattvākhyā** — designated/countable within the sentient continuum.
  - **status:** present in Kārikā, explained in Bhāṣya by `nāsattvasaṃkhyātau`.
  - **function:** continuum-classification.

- **anupāttaka** — unappropriated.
  - **status:** present in Kārikā.
  - **function:** distinguishes breath from appropriated indriya-related bodily material.

- **indriyavinirbhāgitva** — separability from the faculties.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term.
  - **function:** reason breath is unappropriated.

- **naiḥṣyandika** — homogeneous-flow product.
  - **status:** present in Kārikā.
  - **function:** causal classification; explicitly excludes both accumulation-produced and maturation-born status.

## The Meaning of `anupāttaka`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> **indriyavinirbhāgitvāt**
>
> because [the breaths] are separable from the faculties.

This is a very precise point. Breath is part of embodied life, but it is not appropriated in the same way as the sensory faculties.

So:

```text
living continuum
    includes breath

but

appropriated bodily basis
    does not simply equal breath
```

This preserves an important middle position between “external object” and “organically appropriated faculty.”

## The Meaning of `naiḥṣyandika`

Vasubandhu says that inhalation and exhalation are **naiḥṣyandika**, not **aupacayika** and not **vipākaja**.

His reasons are concrete:

- bodily increase does not entail a proportional increase in breath;
- when breathing has been interrupted, it can reconnect;
- this is not characteristic of maturation-born material form.

Thus breath is not treated as a fixed substance or a direct karmic maturation-product. It is a conditioned ongoing flow.

This is especially important after 6.12: the object of Prajñā is a process whose continuity has to be understood causally, not reified.

## Epistemic Determination

The final line says the breaths are not apprehended by a lower-level cognition.

The Bhāṣya glosses:

> **svabhūmyuparibhūmikena ca tayościttenopalakṣaṇam / nāvareṇa ...**
>
> They are apprehended by cognition of their own level or a higher level, not by a lower one.

So the object-range of cognition is itself level-sensitive.

This is not simply “mind notices breath.” Rather:

```text
object-level
    constrains
adequate cognitive level
```

That is an important Abhidharma principle: an object is not adequately knowable from just any cognitive standpoint.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The breath-process is therefore determined across five axes:

```text
1. level
    same as body

2. continuum-status
    sattvākhyā

3. appropriation-status
    anupāttaka

4. causal type
    naiḥṣyandika

5. cognitive accessibility
    own-level or higher cognition
```

This makes 6.13 the analytic complement to 6.12.

6.12:

```text
what kind of cognition is ānāpānasmṛti?
    Prajñā
```

6.13:

```text
what kind of dharma is the breath it knows?
    precisely classified bodily process
```

The Path therefore requires both:

```text
adequate cognition
    +
adequately determined object
```

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass wording “become objects only for an adequate cognition” should be kept, but with a stricter Abhidharma qualification:

- **nādhareṇa** does not merely mean “not by an inferior standpoint” in a broad philosophical sense;
- the Bhāṣya specifically means cognition of a lower **bhūmi** cannot apprehend them, whereas cognition of the same or a higher level can.

Likewise, **anupāttaka** should remain “unappropriated,” with the explanation that breath is separable from the indriyas.

## Light Organon Contact Point

A useful but restrained structural contact is:

```text
Prajñā does not create its object arbitrarily.

It requires a dharma whose
level,
causal status,
appropriation status,
and cognitive accessibility
are themselves determined.
```

So the practical science of breath here is not introspective immediacy. It is **disciplined determination of a conditioned process**.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.13 clarifies the object of ānāpānasmṛti:

```text
breath belongs to the body's level
is part of the sentient continuum
is nevertheless unappropriated
is a homogeneous-flow product
and is apprehensible only by cognition
of its own or a higher level
```

The main result is that breath becomes a genuine object of Prajñā only after its bodily, causal, appropriative, and epistemic determinations are explicitly separated.
