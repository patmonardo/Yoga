# VAK 6.67 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> anutpādakṣayajñāne bodhis tad-anulomyataḥ /  
> saptatriṃśat tu tat-pakṣyā nāmato dravyato daśa // VAK_6.67 //

**Best current translation:**  
Awakening is knowledge of non-arising and knowledge of exhaustion. Because they conform to that awakening, the factors belonging to its side are thirty-seven by designation, but ten according to underlying dharma-reality.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.67.md`](./VAK_6.67.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 382|20–383|08.

The prose begins from the transition already announced at the close of VAK 6.66:

> `punarapyeṣa mārgo bodhipakṣyākhyāṃ labhate`

Again, this same Path receives the designation **bodhipakṣya**.

The point is therefore not that Vasubandhu has left Mārga behind and begun a disconnected list. He is redescribing the same Path according to its conformity with Bodhi.

## 1. The Thirty-Seven Bodhipakṣya Dharmas

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya first enumerates the familiar seven groups:

> `saptatriṃśad bodhipakṣā dharmāḥ`

```text
4 smṛtyupasthāna
    establishments of mindfulness

4 samyakprahāṇa
    right abandonments / right efforts

4 ṛddhipāda
    bases of spiritual power

5 indriya
    faculties

5 bala
    powers

7 bodhyaṅga
    factors of awakening

8 āryamārgāṅga
    factors of the Noble Eightfold Path
```

Numerically:

```text
4 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 7 + 8 = 37
```

This list supplies the **nāman** side of the coming distinction: thirty-seven determinate Path-functions or classifications.

## 2. What Is Bodhi?

The kārikā states:

> `anutpādakṣayajñāne bodhiḥ`

The Bhāṣya glosses directly:

> `kṣayajñānam anutpādajñānaṃ ca`

Bodhi consists in:

```text
kṣayajñāna
    knowledge of exhaustion

anutpādajñāna
    knowledge of non-arising
```

These are not two arbitrary knowledges placed side by side. Together they articulate completion in two respects:

```text
what had to be exhausted
    is exhausted

what was exhausted
    will not arise again
```

Thus awakening is not merely awareness that an obstruction has presently disappeared. It knows both **completion** and **irreversibility**.

## 3. Why Bodhi Is Threefold

The Bhāṣya then adds something not explicit in the kārikā:

> `pudgalabhedena tisro bodhaya utpadyante`

According to differences of person, three awakenings arise:

> `śrāvakabodhiḥ pratyekabodhiranuttarā samyaksaṃbodhir iti`

```text
śrāvakabodhi
    awakening of a disciple

pratyekabodhi
    awakening of a solitary Buddha

anuttarā samyaksaṃbodhi
    unsurpassed complete awakening
```

This is a classification by **pudgala**, not a multiplication of the formal essence of Bodhi.

The Bhāṣya has first defined Bodhi through the two knowledges; it then distinguishes the forms in which Bodhi is realized according to the awakened person.

So we should preserve the order:

```text
Bodhi in definition
    kṣayajñāna + anutpādajñāna

Bodhi by person
    śrāvaka
    pratyekabuddha
    samyaksaṃbuddha
```

The second distinction does not replace the first.

## 4. Why These Two Knowledges Constitute Bodhi

Vasubandhu gives the ground:

> `aśeṣāvidyāprahāṇāt`

because ignorance has been abandoned without remainder;

and:

> `tābhyāṃ svarthasya yathābhūta kṛtāpunaḥkartavyatāvavodhācca`

because through those two knowledges there is correct comprehension that one's own task has been done and is no longer to be done.

The structure is therefore:

```text
complete abandonment of avidyā
        ↓
kṣayajñāna
    knows the task as accomplished

anutpādajñāna
    knows there is no further recurrence requiring repetition
        ↓
Bodhi
```

This supplies the Bhāṣya's explanation of why the two knowledges are not merely consequences of awakening. They articulate awakened completion itself.

The expression concerning the task being done and no longer to be done also fits the preceding Aśaikṣa discussion: one beyond training is precisely one for whom no further training remains necessary for the final fruit.

## 5. Why the Thirty-Seven Are Bodhipakṣya

The kārikā continues:

> `tad-anulomyataḥ`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `bodher anulomatvād bodhipakṣyāḥ saptatriṃśad utpadyante`

The thirty-seven are called **bodhipakṣya** because they are in conformity with Bodhi.

The crucial relation is therefore **anulomya / anulomatva**:

```text
Bodhi
    consummate awakened knowing

bodhipakṣya dharmas
    factors whose operation accords with
    and tends in the direction of Bodhi
```

The word *pakṣya* should therefore not be weakened into a merely external collection of "aids." These dharmas belong to the side of Bodhi because their operation is conformable to it.

At the same time, the text does not identify the thirty-seven with Bodhi itself. Their identity is relational:

```text
Bodhi
    governing consummation

bodhipakṣya
    that which conforms to this consummation
```

## 6. Thirty-Seven by Name, Ten by Dravya

The final line introduces the major analytical problem developed immediately in VAK 6.68:

> `nāmato dravyato daśa`

The Bhāṣya restates:

> `daśa dravyāṇi sarve bodhipakṣyāḥ`

All the bodhipakṣya factors are **ten dravyas**.

It then asks:

> `katamāni daśa`

What are the ten?

VAK 6.68 answers.

The distinction is exact:

```text
nāman
    37
    distinct classifications / functional designations

dravya
    10
    underlying operative dharmas
```

Thus the thirty-seven are not thirty-seven utterly different dharma-substances. The same operative dharma can occur under several Path-descriptions because it performs several determinate functions in the system.

This is why the prose must not be translated as though Vasubandhu were dismissing the thirty-seven as merely verbal fiction. The nāman distinctions are valid; they simply do not imply thirty-seven distinct dravyas.

## 7. The Genetic Movement from VAK 6.65 to 6.67

The chapter-ending movement is now visible:

```text
6.65
    one Mārga
    summarized through four operative phases

6.66
    one Mārga
    distinguished as four courses of progress

6.67
    one Mārga
    redescribed as bodhipakṣya
    through conformity with Bodhi
```

Vasubandhu is therefore not accumulating unrelated lists.

He repeatedly takes **the same Path** and determines it under a new principle:

```text
operation
    → fourfold mārga

mode of progress
    → fourfold pratipad

relation to awakening
    → thirty-seven bodhipakṣya dharmas
```

This is a strong confirmation that the governing subject of the chapter remains **Mārga**.

## 8. Translation Note

The repository Kārikā translation says:

> “Bodhi consists in knowledge of non-arising and knowledge of exhaustion.”

The Bhāṣya supports this strongly through:

> `kṣayajñānam anutpādajñānaṃ ca`

No major correction is needed.

For the last pāda, however, the Bhāṣya makes it useful to keep the distinction between **designation** and **underlying dharma-reality** explicit:

```text
nāmataḥ
    by designation / by named classification

drav yataḥ
    according to dravya, underlying operative dharma
```

“Substantially” is possible, but it can misleadingly suggest metaphysical substance. In this project, “according to underlying dharma-reality” better preserves the Abhidharma function of the distinction without importing an alien substance ontology.

## Restrained Organon Contact

The most important structural result is already internal to Vasubandhu:

```text
one operative dharma
    can receive several valid determinations
    according to several Path-functions
```

Hence:

```text
identity of dravya
    does not erase
plurality of functional determination
```

Nor does plurality of names require plurality of underlying realities.

This will be important later for the Organon pass because it supplies a disciplined example of how one content becomes multiply determined without becoming numerically many in the same respect.

The stronger point, however, should still be deferred: VAK 6.68 must show exactly how the thirty-seven determinations reduce to the ten dravyas before we formalize the relation further.

## Dream of Philosophy Contact

This passage gives a particularly clean instance of **unity through differentiated determination**.

Bodhi is not set beside the thirty-seven as an external final object. The thirty-seven are what they are through their **conformity to Bodhi**, while Bodhi becomes practically articulated through these determinate Path-functions.

That is compatible with the governing insight of reciprocal mediation, but the text should not yet be forced into the Brahman Chakra. The genetic continuity test is satisfied insofar as unity is not reduced to static identity:

```text
Bodhi
    one consummation

bodhipakṣya
    differentiated determinations conforming to it
```

The stronger reciprocal claim will require the following verses, where the apparently thirty-seven factors are shown to be ten operative dharmas distributed through many functional positions.

This is therefore **not yet a new development of the Dream of Philosophy**, but it is a strong textual preparation for one.

## New Technical Terms

- **bodhipakṣya** — belonging to the side of / conforming to awakening  
  **status:** introduced here as the new designation of Mārga  
  **function:** names the thirty-seven Path-factors through their conformity with Bodhi

- **bodhi** — awakening  
  **status:** central Buddhist term, technically defined here  
  **function:** constituted by knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge of non-arising

- **kṣayajñāna** — knowledge of exhaustion  
  **status:** previously encountered, explicitly constitutive of Bodhi here  
  **function:** knows the relevant work of abandonment as completed

- **anutpādajñāna** — knowledge of non-arising  
  **status:** previously encountered, explicitly constitutive of Bodhi here  
  **function:** knows that what has been exhausted will not arise again

- **anulomya / anulomatva** — conformity, accordance, moving in the same direction  
  **status:** important Bhāṣya explanatory relation here  
  **function:** explains why the thirty-seven are called bodhipakṣya

- **nāmataḥ** — according to name/designation  
  **status:** explicit in the kārikā  
  **function:** counts the thirty-seven distinct functional classifications

- **dravyataḥ** — according to dravya  
  **status:** explicit in the kārikā  
  **function:** reduces the thirty-seven classifications to ten underlying operative dharmas

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the preserved passage.

No explicit school controversy occurs in this unit.

A doctrinal caution is needed with the word *dravya*. The text clearly contrasts thirty-seven names with ten dravyas, but this should not automatically be translated into the categories of Western substance metaphysics. Here *dravya* functions as the underlying dharma counted as operative reality beneath several valid functional designations.

A second caution concerns the three Bodhis. The Bhāṣya says that three awakenings arise according to differences of person; it does not thereby give three different formal definitions of Bodhi. The formal definition remains the two knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.67 redescribes the same Mārga as the thirty-seven bodhipakṣya dharmas. Bodhi is technically defined as knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge of non-arising: awakened knowing comprehends both that the work has been completed and that the eliminated obstruction will not recur. According to differences of person, this Bodhi is realized as Śrāvaka awakening, Pratyekabuddha awakening, or unsurpassed complete awakening. The thirty-seven Path-factors are called bodhipakṣya because they conform to Bodhi. Yet the thirty-seven are thirty-seven only according to their named Path-functions; according to underlying operative dharmas they are ten. VAK 6.68 will now perform that reduction explicitly.
