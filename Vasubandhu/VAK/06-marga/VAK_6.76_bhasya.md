# VAK 6.76 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> **asaṃskṛtā kleśahānam adhimuktas tu saṃskṛtā |**  
> **sāṅgaḥ saiva vimuktī dve jñānaṃ bodhir yathoditā ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 388.03–388.19.

The preceding verse asked what liberation (*vimukti*) is and answered that it is twofold. The Bhāṣya now specifies the distinction.

## Literal Prose Translation

Liberation is conditioned and unconditioned.

The unconditioned liberation is the abandonment of the afflictions. The conditioned liberation is the Aśaikṣa’s *adhimokṣa*.

The unconditioned liberation itself, when joined with the Aśaikṣa factors, is called an Aśaikṣa factor, because the factors are conditioned.

The same conditioned liberation is the two liberations spoken of in the sūtra: liberation of mind (*ceto-vimukti*) and liberation through prajñā (*prajñā-vimukti*). The liberation aggregate is to be understood in the same way.

An objection is then raised from a sūtra describing the purification and completion of liberation. This suggests that liberation may not simply be *adhimokṣa*. Another interpretation is therefore recorded: after desire, hatred, and delusion have been removed by knowledge of reality, the purity of mind is liberation.

Right knowledge is the Bodhi already stated earlier: knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge of non-arising.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Two kinds of liberation

```text
vimukti
    ├── asaṃskṛta
    │     abandonment of kleśas
    │
    └── saṃskṛta
          Aśaikṣa adhimokṣa
```

The distinction is between accomplished unconditioned cessation and a conditioned liberated mental determination.

### 2. `sāṅgaḥ` resolves the apparent contradiction

The Kārikā’s compressed `sāṅgaḥ` is explained by the prose:

```text
asaṃskṛta vimukti
    itself unconditioned

but counted as an Aśaikṣa factor
    when joined with Aśaikṣa factors

because the factors themselves
    are conditioned
```

Thus the unconditioned liberation does not itself become conditioned. Rather, it is included in the Aśaikṣa complex through association with conditioned factors.

### 3. The two scriptural liberations

The Bhāṣya identifies the conditioned liberation with:

```text
ceto-vimukti
prajñā-vimukti
```

and says the *vimukti-skandha* is to be understood in the same way.

### 4. An alternative interpretation of conditioned liberation

A sūtra passage concerning the purification and completion of liberation generates an objection to identifying liberation simply with *adhimokṣa*.

**Voice: Objector / alternative masters**

> After rāga, dveṣa, and moha have been removed by knowledge of reality, the purity of mind is liberation.

So the prose preserves two explanations of conditioned liberation:

```text
Aśaikṣa adhimokṣa

or

purity of mind after the kleśas
have been removed by tattva-jñāna
```

The passage does not fully adjudicate the disagreement here.

### 5. Right knowledge is Bodhi

Vasubandhu then asks what distinct factor is meant by *samyagjñāna* and answers:

```text
samyagjñāna
    = Bodhi previously defined
    = kṣayajñāna + anutpādajñāna
```

So Chapter 6 returns explicitly to VAK 6.67.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / exposition** — conditioned and unconditioned liberation; Aśaikṣa adhimokṣa; two scriptural liberations; Bodhi as the two knowledges.
- **Voice: Objector** — appeal to the sūtra on the purification and completion of liberation.
- **Voice: uncertain / alternative masters** — liberation as purity of mind after kleśas are removed by knowledge of reality.

## New Technical Terms

- **asaṃskṛta-vimukti** — unconditioned liberation; abandonment of the afflictions  
  **status:** newly explicit here  
  **function:** doctrinal distinction

- **saṃskṛta-vimukti** — conditioned liberation; initially identified with Aśaikṣa *adhimokṣa*  
  **status:** newly explicit here  
  **function:** doctrinal distinction

- **ceto-vimukti** — liberation of mind  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this immediate discussion  
  **function:** scriptural classification

- **prajñā-vimukti** — liberation through prajñā  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this immediate discussion  
  **function:** scriptural classification

- **vimukti-skandha** — aggregate of liberation  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-specific here  
  **function:** scriptural/doctrinal classification

- **tattva-jñāna** — knowledge of reality; that by which rāga, dveṣa, and moha are removed  
  **status:** Bhāṣya terminology  
  **function:** alternative explanation of liberation

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes four things that must not be collapsed:

```text
abandonment of kleśa
    unconditioned liberation

liberated mental actuality
    conditioned liberation

knowledge of exhaustion
knowledge of non-arising
    right knowledge / Bodhi
```

The Bhāṣya therefore does not reduce liberation to either cessation alone or knowledge alone.

At the same time, it preserves an important dependency:

```text
tattva-jñāna
    removes rāga, dveṣa, moha
        ↓
purity of mind
    can be called vimukti
```

This alternative reading makes the cognitive ground of liberation explicit without identifying liberation simply with cognition.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya resolves two compressed points in the Kārikā:

1. **`adhimuktas tu saṃskṛtā`** should be understood through the prose as *aśaikṣādhimokṣaḥ saṃskṛtā vimuktiḥ* — the Aśaikṣa’s adhimokṣa is conditioned liberation.
2. **`sāṅgaḥ`** refers to the unconditioned liberation being taken together with conditioned Aśaikṣa factors; it does not mean that the unconditioned liberation itself is conditioned.

A careful translation is therefore:

> The unconditioned liberation is the abandonment of the afflictions; conditioned liberation is the Aśaikṣa’s adhimokṣa. The former is [counted] together with the factors. The conditioned liberation is itself the two liberations; knowledge is Bodhi, as previously stated.

## Light Organon Contact

For the first pass, one structural point is enough:

```text
knowledge
    is not identical with liberation

abandonment
    is not identical with liberated mental actuality

but the completed Path
    coordinates all three
```

The Kośa is preserving differentiated moments of one liberating science rather than reducing them to a single undifferentiated state.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.76 distinguishes unconditioned liberation as abandonment of the afflictions from conditioned liberation as the Aśaikṣa’s liberated mental determination. The Bhāṣya explains that the unconditioned liberation is counted with the Aśaikṣa factors through association, identifies the two scriptural liberations with conditioned liberation, records an alternative interpretation of liberation as purity of mind produced after the kleśas are removed by knowledge of reality, and identifies right knowledge with the Bodhi already defined as knowledge of exhaustion and non-arising.
