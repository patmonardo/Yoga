# VAK 6.56 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ṣaḍarhanto matās teṣāṃ pañca śraddhādhimuktijāḥ /  
> vimuktiḥ sāmayiky eṣām akopyākopyadharmaṇaḥ // VAK_6.56 //

**Best current translation:**  
Six kinds of Arhats are recognized. Of these, five arise from one who was formerly *śraddhādhimukta*. Their liberation is condition-dependent (*sāmayikī*); the liberation of the *akopyadharman* is unshakeable.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.56.md`](./VAK_6.56.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 372|16–373|08.

VAK 6.55 had distinguished the grounds on which the Path begins and reaches completion. VAK 6.56 now turns inward, asking whether Arhatship itself admits differentiation. Vasubandhu answers yes: six kinds of Arhats are recognized. The verse then gives the governing five-plus-one division, while the prose explains the developmental origin of the five and, crucially, what *sāmayikī vimukti* means.

The central result is that completed Arhatship is not doctrinally undifferentiated. Five kinds possess a liberation whose manifestation in concentration depends upon suitable conditions and therefore must be continually guarded; the sixth possesses an unshakeable liberation from which regression is impossible.

## 1. Does Arhatship Itself Admit Distinctions?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya explicitly links the new question back to the earlier discussion of the unshakeable Arhat:

> `idam uktaṃ "yady akopyaḥ kṣayajñānād anutpādamati" riti`

It had been said that, in the case of one who is unshakeable, the cognition of non-arising follows the cognition of destruction.

Vasubandhu then asks:

> `tatra kim arhatām apy asti bhedaḥ`

Is there, then, a distinction even among Arhats?

The answer is direct:

> `astīty ucyate`

Yes, there is.

This question is important for the architecture of Mārga. The attainment of the final fruit does not erase every distinction in the manner in which that fruit is possessed.

```text
Arhatship
    one final fruit
        ↓
    nevertheless
        ↓
several modes of stability / susceptibility
```

The present passage therefore does not introduce a goal beyond Arhatship. It discriminates forms of the completed state.

## 2. The Six Arhats

The kārikā states:

> `ṣaḍarhanto matāḥ`

Six Arhats are recognized.

The Bhāṣya cites a sūtra listing them:

> `sūtra uktaṃ ṣaḍarhantaḥ parihāṇadharmā cetanādharmā anurakṣaṇādharmā sthitākampyaḥ prativedhanābhavyo 'kopyadharmā ceti`

The six are:

1. **parihāṇadharman** — one whose disposition admits regression
2. **cetanādharman** — one characterized by the possibility of intentional self-release / decisive volition
3. **anurakṣaṇādharman** — one whose attainment requires guarding
4. **sthitākampya** — one capable of remaining stable without being shaken from the attained state
5. **prativedhanābhavya** — one capable of penetrating through to the higher, unshakeable condition
6. **akopyadharman** — one whose liberation is unshakeable

The following verses explain these types more precisely, so the English labels here should remain provisional. VAK 6.56 itself is concerned principally with the structural division of the six into five and one.

```text
six Arhats
    ├── first five
    │     genetically connected with śraddhādhimukti
    │     sāmayikī vimukti
    │
    └── sixth: akopyadharman
          akopyā vimukti
```

## 3. Five Arise from the Faith-Resolved Type

The kārikā continues:

> `teṣāṃ pañca śraddhādhimuktijāḥ`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `akopyadharmāṇaṃ varjayitvā 'nye pañca śraddhādhimukti pūrvakāḥ`

Excluding the *akopyadharman*, the other five were formerly *śraddhādhimukta*.

This makes `śraddhādhimuktijāḥ` a statement of **path-history** or developmental derivation. The five are not being called merely “faithful Arhats.” Their present Arhat-types are traced back to an earlier mode of training.

```text
śraddhādhimukta
    ↓
training and attainment
    ↓
first five Arhat types
```

The distinction is therefore genetic. A mode of entry and development within the training path leaves a determinate differentiation in the way final liberation is possessed.

This is one reason the verse belongs naturally to Chapter 6 as Mārga rather than to an isolated taxonomy of perfected persons. The perfected person still bears a determinate relation to the path by which perfection was reached.

## 4. Their Liberation Is Sāmayikī

The kārikā says:

> `vimuktiḥ sāmayiky eṣām`

The Bhāṣya expands:

> `eṣāṃ ca pañcānāṃ sāmayikī kāntā cetovimuktir veditavyā`

For these five, the relevant liberation of mind is to be understood as *sāmayikī*.

The exact expression `kāntā cetovimukti` should be kept close to the source. The decisive term for the present classification is *sāmayikī*: the liberation is dependent upon an appropriate occasion or conjunction of conditions.

The Bhāṣya immediately gives the reason:

> `nityānurakṣyatvāt`

because it must be continually guarded.

Thus *sāmayikī* does not merely mean that liberation happens “sometimes.” It indicates a mode of possession whose secure manifestation still depends upon guarding and favorable circumstances.

```text
sāmayikī vimukti
    ↓
requires continual guarding
    ↓
its manifestation depends upon suitable conditions
```

This is the first major clarification supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Why They Are Called Samayavimukta

The prose continues:

> `ata evaite samayavimuktā ucyante`

For precisely this reason they are called *samayavimukta* — “occasion-/condition-dependent liberated ones.”

Vasubandhu then analyzes the expression:

> `samayāpekṣāś caite 'dhimuktāś ceti samayavimuktā madhyapadalopāt dhṛtaghaṭavat`

They are dependent upon a *samaya* and they are liberated/resolved; hence they are called *samayavimukta*, through omission of the middle member of the compound, like the cited grammatical example `dhṛtaghaṭa`.

The grammatical detail matters less than the doctrinal result: *samayavimukta* encodes **dependence upon a suitable occasion or set of conditions**.

The term should therefore not be flattened into simply “temporarily liberated.” The Bhāṣya itself explains it through `samayāpekṣā` — dependence upon *samaya*.

## 6. What Conditions Are Required?

The Bhāṣya then makes the notion concrete:

> `eṣāṃ hi samayāpekṣā samādhisaṃmukhībhāva upakaraṇārogyadeśaviśeṣāpekṣatvāt`

For these persons, the bringing of concentration into immediate presence depends upon suitable conditions because it depends upon particulars of:

- **upakaraṇa** — requisite equipment, supports, or material circumstances
- **ārogya** — health
- **deśaviśeṣa** — a suitable or particular place

This is an important correction to any overly abstract reading of the verse.

The five are not said to possess a metaphysically incomplete liberation. They are Arhats. Rather, the relevant *samādhisaṃmukhībhāva* — the actual bringing of concentration into presence — depends upon favorable supporting conditions.

```text
supporting requisites
health
suitable place
      ↓
suitable samaya
      ↓
samādhi-saṃmukhībhāva
      ↓
condition-dependent manifestation of liberation
```

The Bhāṣya therefore locates the distinction in a very concrete modal structure of practice and attainment.

## 7. Completion Does Not Mean Independence from Every Condition

The doctrinal subtlety is now clearer.

All six are Arhats:

```text
kleśa-destruction
    attained

final fruit
    attained
```

Yet among Arhats there remains a difference concerning the stability and accessibility of liberation:

```text
first five
    favorable conditions remain relevant
    continual guarding remains relevant
    regression is therefore doctrinally possible

akopyadharman
    liberation cannot be shaken
    regression is impossible
```

Thus “final fruit” and “unshakeability” are not simply interchangeable predicates in this taxonomy.

This is precisely why Vasubandhu can first affirm six Arhats and only afterward distinguish their modes of liberation.

## 8. The Akopyadharman

The last pāda states:

> `akopyākopyadharmaṇaḥ`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `akopyadharmaṇas tv akopyā vimuktiḥ`

For the *akopyadharman*, liberation is *akopyā* — unshakeable.

And the reason is explicit:

> `kopayitum aśakyatvād aparihāṇitaḥ`

because it cannot be disturbed or shaken, owing to the impossibility of regression.

This determines *akopya* by negating *parihāṇi*:

```text
akopyā vimukti
    ↓
cannot be shaken
    ↓
no parihāṇi
    ↓
no falling away from the attained liberation
```

The contrast with the first five is therefore not merely psychological confidence versus doubt. It concerns the objective modal possibility or impossibility of regression.

## 9. A Five-plus-One Architecture

The Bhāṣya gives VAK 6.56 a very clean internal architecture:

```text
                       six Arhats
                           │
             ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
             │                           │
         first five                 akopyadharman
             │                           │
 śraddhādhimukta-pūrvaka             distinct line
             │                           │
     sāmayikī vimukti               akopyā vimukti
             │                           │
 depends on suitable conditions       cannot be shaken
             │                           │
 continual guarding relevant          no regression
```

The verse therefore shifts the inquiry from **whether liberation has been attained** to **the mode under which attained liberation is possessed**.

That is the decisive doctrinal move.

## 10. Relation to the Earlier Śaikṣa Classification

The phrase `śraddhādhimukti-pūrvaka` confirms continuity between the earlier classification of trainees and the present classification of Arhats.

The perfected types do not appear without genealogy.

```text
mode of training
    ↓
mode of maturation
    ↓
mode of completed attainment
```

The Bhāṣya does not yet give the full transformation rules among the six dispositions; those belong to the following verses. But VAK 6.56 already establishes that the distinction among Arhats is intelligible only through the history of the Path.

This is an especially useful reason not to isolate the present discussion under an excessively narrow heading such as “Aśaikṣa taxonomy.” It is still the analysis of **Mārga**, now seen from the side of the persons produced by its completion.

## 11. Translation Consequence

The first-pass Kārikā translation rendered:

> “Six Arhats are recognized. Of these, five arise from the faith-resolved type. Their liberation is occasion-dependent; that of the one whose determination is unshakeable is unshakeable.”

The Bhāṣya confirms the general structure but makes `sāmayikī` more exact.

Because Vasubandhu glosses the term through `samayāpekṣā` and then specifies requisites, health, and place, the most informative translation is:

> **Six kinds of Arhats are recognized. Of these, five arise from one who was formerly faith-resolved. Their liberation is condition-dependent; the liberation of the one whose disposition is unshakeable is unshakeable.**

“Occasion-dependent” remains philologically possible, but “condition-dependent” better carries the Bhāṣya's explicit explanation so long as the underlying `samaya` is kept visible in commentary.

## 12. Restrained Organon Contact

VAK 6.56 introduces an important distinction between **attainment** and **modal stability of attainment**.

```text
same determinate result:
    Arhatship

but different modality:
    condition-dependent possession
    unshakeable possession
```

The result is philosophically useful because completion does not erase the genesis or mode of possession of the result. The first five Arhats are genuinely liberated, yet the manner in which liberation becomes present remains related to determinate supports. In the sixth, that dependence no longer takes the form of possible regression.

The restrained lesson is therefore not that the first five possess only “relative liberation.” The Bhāṣya does not say that. It says that the **mode of liberation of an Arhat can still be distinguished according to dependence, guarding, and possibility of regression**.

That is enough conceptual structure for this pass.

## New Technical Terms

- **parihāṇadharman** — an Arhat-type whose disposition admits regression  
  **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya's sixfold sūtra list; explained more fully in following verses  
  **function:** first member of the sixfold Arhat classification

- **cetanādharman** — a second Arhat-type characterized by a determinate capacity associated with volition  
  **status:** introduced here; exact functional definition follows later  
  **function:** second member of the sixfold classification

- **anurakṣaṇādharman** — an Arhat-type characterized by guarding or preservation  
  **status:** introduced here; developed later  
  **function:** third member of the sixfold classification

- **sthitākampya** — an Arhat capable of remaining stable / not being shaken from the attained state  
  **status:** introduced here; later Bhāṣya supplies a more exact definition  
  **function:** fourth member of the sixfold classification

- **prativedhanābhavya** — an Arhat capable of further penetration  
  **status:** introduced here; exact relation to *akopyadharman* clarified later  
  **function:** fifth member of the sixfold classification

- **akopyadharman** — one whose disposition is unshakeable  
  **status:** already encountered in the preceding discussion  
  **function:** sixth Arhat-type and the one excluded from the five *śraddhādhimukta*-derived types

- **sāmayikī vimukti** — liberation whose manifestation is dependent upon suitable occasion/conditions  
  **status:** Kārikā term explicitly explained by the Bhāṣya  
  **function:** common liberation-mode of the first five Arhat types

- **samayavimukta** — condition-/occasion-dependent liberated person  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory designation  
  **function:** names the first five Arhats from their dependence on suitable *samaya*

- **samayāpekṣā** — dependence upon a suitable occasion or conjunction of conditions  
  **status:** Bhāṣya gloss  
  **function:** explains the force of *sāmayikī* / *samayavimukta*

- **samādhisaṃmukhībhāva** — bringing concentration into immediate presence / actualization of concentration  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression in this passage  
  **function:** identifies what, for the first five Arhats, depends upon favorable conditions

- **upakaraṇa** — requisite equipment, supports, or practical resources  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term  
  **function:** one class of condition required for the relevant manifestation of concentration

- **ārogya** — health  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term  
  **function:** second supporting condition explicitly named

- **deśaviśeṣa** — a suitable or particular place  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term  
  **function:** third supporting condition explicitly named

- **akopyā vimukti** — unshakeable liberation  
  **status:** Kārikā/Bhāṣya term  
  **function:** liberation of the *akopyadharman*, defined by impossibility of disturbance through regression

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu frames the question whether Arhats themselves admit distinctions and supplies the explanatory prose.

**Voice:** The sixfold list is explicitly attributed to a **sūtra**.

**Caution:** The exact functional definitions of the first five Arhat-types are not all supplied in VAK 6.56. The Bhāṣya develops them in the following verses. Their English glosses here are therefore provisional and should not import later distinctions prematurely.

**Caution:** `sāmayikī vimukti` should not be translated as though the first five were only intermittently or incompletely liberated. They are Arhats. The Bhāṣya explains the term specifically through continual guarding and dependence of *samādhi-saṃmukhībhāva* upon requisites, health, and place.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.56 begins the sixfold analysis of Arhatship. Vasubandhu asks whether differentiation remains even among Arhats and answers that six kinds are recognized. Five are genetically traced to the earlier *śraddhādhimukta* type and possess *sāmayikī vimukti*: their relevant manifestation of concentration depends upon suitable conditions and therefore requires continual guarding. The sixth, the *akopyadharman*, possesses *akopyā vimukti*, liberation that cannot be shaken because regression is impossible.

The decisive Mārga determination is therefore:

```text
final fruit
    does not erase path-history

Arhatship
    admits different modes of stability

first five
    condition-dependent / guarded

sixth
    unshakeable / non-regressive
```

The Path is now being analyzed from the side of its completed persons, without ceasing to be an analysis of the Path itself.
