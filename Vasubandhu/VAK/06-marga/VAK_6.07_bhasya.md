# VAK 6.07 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā

### Devanāgarī

विपर्यासात्तद्विपक्षौ त्रिधात्वाप्तावमलौ च तौ ।
अलोभ आर्यवंशाश्च तेषां तुष्ट्यात्मकास्त्रयः ॥ ६.७ ॥

### IAST

viparyāsāt tad-vipakṣau tri-dhātv-āptāv amalau ca tau |
alobha āryavaṃśāś ca teṣāṃ tuṣṭy-ātmakās trayaḥ || 6.7 ||

## Bhāṣya Argument

VAK 6.06 defined the two defects that obstruct the practical conditions of cultivation:

- **asaṃtuṣṭi** — discontent with what has been obtained,
- **mahecchatā** — desire for superior or abundant things not obtained.

The present verse determines their counterforces by reversal:

> **asaṃtuṣṭi-mahecchatā-viparyayeṇa tat-pratipakṣau veditavyau / saṃtuṣṭiś cālpecchatā ceti /**

The two counterforces are to be known through reversal of discontent and great desire: **contentment** and **fewness of desires**.

The structure is therefore:

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi
    ↔
saṃtuṣṭi

mahecchatā
    ↔
alpecchatā
```

This is a direct **pratipakṣa** relation. The Path does not merely remove an undesirable state by absence; it develops a positively contrary determination.

## Scope of the Two Counterforces

The Kārikā states:

> **tri-dhātv-āptāv amalau ca tau**

The Bhāṣya explains that contentment and fewness of desires may belong to all three domains and may also be uncontaminated:

> **tridhātukau ca ... sāsravānāsravatvāt /**

Thus they are not restricted to the desire domain. By contrast, the two defects they oppose—discontent and great desire—belong only to the desire domain.

This asymmetry is important:

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi / mahecchatā
    desire-domain only

saṃtuṣṭi / alpecchatā
    three domains
    and may be uncontaminated
```

The counterforce therefore has a wider possible Path-extension than the defect it counters.

## What Is Their Intrinsic Nature?

Vasubandhu asks:

> **kaḥ punar anayor alpecchatā-saṃtuṣṭyoḥ svabhāvaḥ?**

What is the intrinsic nature of fewness of desires and contentment?

The answer is extremely concise:

> **alobhaḥ**

They have **non-greed** as their intrinsic nature:

> **alobha-svabhāve hy ete /**

So the two virtues are not two independent substances or roots. They are two functional specifications of one wholesome root.

```text
alobha
    ↓
    ├── saṃtuṣṭi
    │      non-greed in relation to what is possessed
    │
    └── alpecchatā
           non-greed in relation to what is not possessed
```

This gives a precise answer to the practical cycle diagnosed in 6.06.

```text
before acquisition:
    alpecchatā
    blocks expansive desire

after acquisition:
    saṃtuṣṭi
    blocks dissatisfaction and increase
```

The same root—**alobha**—interrupts appropriation at both temporal positions.

## The Noble Lineages — āryavaṃśa

The verse then introduces the **āryavaṃśas**.

The Bhāṣya states that there are four:

> **āryāṇām ebhyaḥ prasavād āryavaṃśāś catvāraḥ /**

They are called the lineages of the noble ones because noble persons arise from them.

The Bhāṣya explicitly carries forward the previous determination:

> **te 'py alobha-svabhāvāḥ /**

They too have **alobha** as their intrinsic nature.

Three of the four are forms of contentment:

1. **cīvara-saṃtuṣṭi** — contentment with robes,
2. **piṇḍapāta-saṃtuṣṭi** — contentment with alms-food,
3. **śayanāsana-saṃtuṣṭi** — contentment with lodging / resting place.

Thus:

```text
āryavaṃśa
    ├── contentment with robes
    ├── contentment with food
    ├── contentment with lodging
    └── fourth noble lineage
```

The fourth is named here by the Bhāṣya as **prahāṇa-bhāvanā-rāmatā**—delight in the cultivation of abandonment. Its fuller function is developed with VAK 6.08.

## Why Is the Fourth Noble Lineage Also Alobha?

An objection naturally arises. The first three concern material requirements and are easily intelligible as non-greed. But how can delight in the cultivation of abandonment also have non-greed as its intrinsic nature?

The Bhāṣya answers:

> **bhava-kāma-rāga-vaimukhyāt /**

Because it turns away from craving for existence and sensual desire.

This extends **alobha** beyond restraint concerning possessions. Its deepest form is a turning away from the craving that sustains continued existence itself.

The progression is therefore:

```text
alobha toward possessions
    robes
    food
    lodging
        ↓
alobha toward bhava and kāma
    delight in abandonment
```

The fourth noble lineage internalizes and radicalizes the same principle expressed empirically in the first three.

## Voice

**Voice: Vasubandhu / received Abhidharma exposition.**

The passage is primarily expository. Unlike VAK 6.06, where Vasubandhu explicitly corrected an inherited definition with **evaṃ tu yujyate**, this section presents the counterforces, their intrinsic nature, and the noble lineages without a developed polemical dispute.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃtuṣṭi** — contentment; counterforce to discontent.
  - **status:** introduced in this local Path sequence.
  - **function:** practical condition of cultivation and specification of alobha.

- **alpecchatā** — fewness of desires.
  - **status:** introduced in this local Path sequence.
  - **function:** counterforce to great desire; restriction of acquisitive projection.

- **alobha** — non-greed, non-appropriative wholesome root.
  - **status:** established Abhidharma category, here given a new Path-function.
  - **function:** intrinsic nature of contentment, fewness of desires, and the four noble lineages.

- **āryavaṃśa** — noble lineage.
  - **status:** new major Path term.
  - **function:** four modes of life/practice from which noble persons arise.

- **prahāṇa-bhāvanā-rāmatā** — delight in cultivation of abandonment.
  - **status:** introduced here and developed with 6.08.
  - **function:** fourth noble lineage.

## Reflective Structure

VAK 6.06 gave the defects as two movements of appropriation:

```text
what I have is insufficient
    asaṃtuṣṭi

what I do not have must be acquired
    mahecchatā
```

VAK 6.07 does not merely negate them. It generates their determinate counterforces:

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi
    → pratipakṣa → saṃtuṣṭi

mahecchatā
    → pratipakṣa → alpecchatā
```

Then reflection discovers that both counterforces have one intrinsic nature:

```text
saṃtuṣṭi
        ↘
          alobha
        ↗
alpecchatā
```

Finally this one root is articulated into the four **āryavaṃśas**.

Thus the Bhāṣya moves:

```text
defect
    ↓
counterforce
    ↓
common intrinsic nature
    ↓
organized Path-practice
```

## Path-of-Knowing Interpretation

This passage gives an important clarification of how the Path acquires effective capacity.

The Path does not merely issue a prohibition:

```text
do not desire
```

It establishes an opposing positive determination:

```text
lobha
    opposed by
alobha
```

and then differentiates that counterforce according to the field in which appropriation occurs.

This is already a concrete instance of the **pratipakṣa** logic introduced in the opening of Chapter 6. A Path has efficacy insofar as a determination is generated that can function as the actual contrary of the afflictive determination.

For that reason, 6.07 is more than ascetic advice. It is a small model of Path causality:

```text
afflictive determination
    ↓
adequate counter-determination
    ↓
new mode of conduct
    ↓
fitness for noble realization
```

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.07 positively determines the practical conditions introduced negatively in 6.06.

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi
    countered by saṃtuṣṭi

mahecchatā
    countered by alpecchatā

saṃtuṣṭi + alpecchatā
    intrinsic nature: alobha

alobha
    further articulated as the four āryavaṃśas
```

Three noble lineages are contentment with robes, food, and lodging. The fourth is delight in cultivation of abandonment, whose relation to the destruction of craving is developed in the next verse.

The key result is:

> **The Path counters appropriation not by mere deprivation, but by generating a positive contrary whose intrinsic nature is alobha.**
