# VAK 6.42 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**ākīryate caturthaṃ prāk niṣpatti-kṣaṇa-miśraṇāt |**  
**upapatti-vihārārthaṃ kleśa-bhīrutayāpi ca || 6.42 ||**

The fourth meditation is mixed first; its completion is through the mixing of moments. It is cultivated for the sake of rebirth and abiding, and also from fear of the afflictions.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 362|10–363|05.

## 1. Return to the Mixed Dhyāna

Vasubandhu resumes the phrase from VAK 6.37:

> **sa dhyāne vyavakīrṇe 'kaniṣṭhagaḥ**

The upward-streamer whose meditation is mixed proceeds to Akaniṣṭha.

The immediate question is therefore:

> **atha katamad dhyānaṃ prathamato vyavakīryate**

Which meditation is mixed first?

The answer is:

> **ākīryate caturthaṃ prāk**

The fourth dhyāna is mixed first.

This is not arbitrary. Vasubandhu gives the reason:

> **sa hi sarvakarmaṇyaḥ samādhiḥ sukhapratipadām agryatvāt**

It is a samādhi capable of every function because it is supreme among the pleasant modes of practice.

Thus the fourth dhyāna serves as the initial operative basis from which the other dhyānas can subsequently be mixed.

## 2. The Vaibhāṣika Mixing Procedure

The Bhāṣya then gives the Vaibhāṣika account of how this mixing occurs.

An arhat or non-returner first enters a continuous uncontaminated fourth dhyāna:

> **arhannanāgāmī vā pravāhayuktam anāsravaṃ caturthaṃ dhyānaṃ samāpadyate**

Having emerged from it, the practitioner enters the same fourth dhyāna in contaminated form, again as a continuous stream, and then once more enters the uncontaminated form.

The preliminary exercise is therefore:

```text
anāsrava stream
    → sāsrava stream
        → anāsrava stream
```

The practitioner progressively shortens these streams without breaking the intended alternation.

The Bhāṣya says that when two uncontaminated moments, two contaminated moments, and again two uncontaminated moments are entered, this constitutes the preparatory exercise (*prayoga*) for mixing.

```text
2 anāsrava kṣaṇas
    → 2 sāsrava kṣaṇas
        → 2 anāsrava kṣaṇas
```

This is already an explicitly moment-structured cognitive operation.

## 3. Completion by Moment-Mixing

The kārikā continues:

> **niṣpatti kṣaṇa-miśraṇāt**

Completion occurs through the mixing of moments.

On the Vaibhāṣika account, completion is reached when a **single contaminated moment** is inserted between two uncontaminated moments:

```text
anāsrava kṣaṇa
    → sāsrava kṣaṇa
        → anāsrava kṣaṇa
```

The Bhāṣya states:

> **evaṃ sāsravasya kṣaṇasyānāsravābhyāṃ miśraṇād vyavakiraṇaṃ niṣpannaṃ bhavati iti vaibhāṣikāḥ**

Thus, according to the Vaibhāṣikas, the mixing is accomplished by mixing one contaminated moment with two uncontaminated moments.

They further interpret the two uncontaminated moments and the inserted contaminated moment by analogy with the Path structure:

> **dvau hi kṣaṇāv ānantaryamārga-sadṛśau tṛtīyo vimuktimārga-sadṛśaḥ**

The first two are like uninterrupted paths; the third is like a path of liberation.

This analogy is striking because it extends the earlier path-connection architecture into meditative construction itself.

## 4. Vasubandhu's Objection

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

The exact single-moment alternation just described is explicitly attributed to the Vaibhāṣikas.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu then rejects, or at minimum sharply restricts, this account:

> **aśakyaṃ tu kṣaṇa-vyavakiraṇam anyatra buddhāt**

Moment-by-moment mixing is impossible except for a Buddha.

This is an important doctrinal caution. The Bhāṣya does not simply endorse the finely atomized procedure it has just reported.

Vasubandhu instead says:

> **ata icchātaḥ pravāha-traya-samāpattito niṣpannaṃ bhavatīti paśyāmaḥ**

Therefore, we understand the mixing to be completed through entering the three streams at will.

His operative model is thus less atomized:

```text
anāsrava stream
    → sāsrava stream
        → anāsrava stream

entered at will
    = completed mixing
```

The doctrinal difference is therefore not whether contaminated and uncontaminated dhyāna are alternated, but **the temporal granularity at which that alternation can actually be controlled**.

## 5. The Other Dhyānas

Once the fourth dhyāna has been mixed, the others can be mixed by its power:

> **evaṃ caturthaṃ dhyānaṃ vyavakīrya tad-balena anyāny api vyavakīryante**

The fourth dhyāna therefore functions as the enabling basis for the same operation elsewhere.

The Bhāṣya also restricts where the practice is first performed: initially in the desire realm among the three continents; later, if one has fallen away, it may be undertaken in the form realm.

## 6. Why Mix the Dhyāna?

Vasubandhu now asks:

> **kimarthaṃ punar dhyānaṃ vyavakīryate**

For what purpose is meditation mixed?

The kārikā answers:

> **upapatti-vihārārthaṃ kleśa-bhīrutayāpi ca**

There are three purposes:

```text
1. upapatti
   rebirth

2. vihāra
   meditative abiding in the present life

3. kleśa-bhīrutā
   fear of afflictions / protection against falling away
```

The Bhāṣya distributes these purposes according to faculty and path-person.

### Sharp-faculty non-returner

The sharp-faculty non-returner cultivates mixed dhyāna:

- for rebirth in the Pure Abodes;
- for pleasant abiding in the present life.

### Mild-faculty non-returner

The mild-faculty non-returner also cultivates it through fear of affliction, in order to distance samādhi associated with savoring and prevent regression.

### Sharp-faculty arhat

The sharp-faculty arhat cultivates it for pleasant abiding in the present life.

### Mild-faculty arhat

The mild-faculty arhat additionally cultivates it from fear of affliction, as protection against falling away.

Thus the identical meditative operation can serve different functions depending on the practitioner's faculty and path-status.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.42 gives one of the clearest examples yet of a deliberately constructed cognitive transition-pattern.

At the reported Vaibhāṣika level:

```text
anāsrava
    → sāsrava
        → anāsrava
```

is progressively compressed from streams to pairs of moments and finally to a three-moment configuration.

The technical structure is therefore:

```text
state A
    → controlled transition
state B
    → controlled transition
state A'
```

where A' is not simply repetition of the first state, but the completion of a deliberately mixed meditative capacity.

But Vasubandhu imposes an epistemic and practical limit on this microscopic model:

```text
Vaibhāṣika:
    exact kṣaṇa-by-kṣaṇa control

Vasubandhu:
    such control belongs only to a Buddha;
    ordinary completion is by mastery of the three streams
```

This is methodologically important. The text distinguishes between:

```text
formal micro-analysis of cognitive succession
    and
practically realizable control of cognitive succession
```

The same meditation is then parameterized by purpose:

```text
mixed dhyāna
    + practitioner type
    + faculty
        → rebirth function
        → present-life abiding
        → protection against regression
```

## New Technical Terms

- **vyavakiraṇa / vyavakīrṇa** — mixing or interspersing contaminated and uncontaminated dhyāna; **status:** previously encountered in VAK 6.37–6.38 but technically explained here; **function:** meditative construction and causal preparation.
- **pravāhayukta** — arranged as or connected in a continuous stream; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit; **function:** specifies the initial temporal form of the alternating meditations.
- **kṣaṇa-vyavakiraṇa** — moment-by-moment mixing; **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical specification here; **function:** Vaibhāṣika microscopic account of completed mixing, explicitly restricted by Vasubandhu.
- **niṣpatti** — completion or accomplished state of the mixing practice; **status:** present in the Kārikā; **function:** distinguishes preparatory alternation from completed mastery.
- **kleśa-bhīrutā** — fear or wariness of affliction; **status:** present in the Kārikā; **function:** motive for protective meditative cultivation, especially in mild-faculty practitioners.
- **dṛṣṭadharma-sukha-vihāra** — pleasant abiding in this present life; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit; **function:** one purpose of mixed dhyāna for both non-returners and arhats.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

The moment-level account must not be presented simply as Vasubandhu's own doctrine.

The crucial marker is:

> **iti vaibhāṣikāḥ**

followed immediately by Vasubandhu's restriction:

> **aśakyaṃ tu kṣaṇa-vyavakiraṇam anyatra buddhāt**

Accordingly:

- exact three-moment mixing = **Vaibhāṣika position**;
- rejection of ordinary kṣaṇa-level mastery = **Vasubandhu**;
- completion through freely entering the three larger streams = **Vasubandhu's preferred account**.

This is a good example of why the Bhāṣya cannot be flattened into a single doctrinal voice.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.42 explains the mixed meditation underlying the Akaniṣṭha trajectory.

The fourth dhyāna is mixed first because it is maximally serviceable. The reported Vaibhāṣika procedure alternates uncontaminated and contaminated fourth dhyāna, progressively compressing the alternation until one contaminated moment stands between two uncontaminated moments.

Vasubandhu explicitly doubts that such exact moment-mixing is possible for anyone except a Buddha. He instead understands completion through voluntary mastery of the three larger streams.

The practice serves three functions:

```text
rebirth
present-life abiding
protection against afflictive regression
```

and those functions vary according to faculty and path-person.

The decisive methodological result is that the Kośa here distinguishes **a formal micro-model of cognitive succession from the level of cognitive control actually realizable by a practitioner**.
