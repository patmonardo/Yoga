# VAK 6.09 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

### Devanāgarī

तत्रावतारोऽशुभया चानापानस्मृतेन च ।
अधिरागवितर्काणां शंकला सर्वरागिणाम् ॥ ६.९ ॥

### IAST

*tatrāvatāro ’śubhayā cānāpānasmṛtena ca |*  
*adhirāgavitarkāṇāṃ śaṃkalā sarvarāgiṇām || 6.9 ||*

## Bhāṣya Context

VAK 6.08 concluded the practical preparation of the practitioner. Vasubandhu now asks:

> For one who has thus become a suitable vessel (*pātrībhūta*), how does entry (*avatāra*) into that cultivation occur?

The answer is through two diagnostic entry-practices:

```text
rāga-dominant practitioner
    → aśubha

vitarka-dominant practitioner
    → ānāpānasmṛti
```

The Bhāṣya therefore shifts from the general conditions of cultivation to differentiated path-technology.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, reporting and organizing received Abhidharma/Yogācāra practice distinctions.  
**Voice confidence:** high.

Several explanatory alternatives concerning why breath-recollection counteracts vitarka are explicitly attributed to different interpreters (*eke*, *apare*).

## Bhāṣya Analysis

### 1. Entry is conditioned by the practitioner's dominant affliction

Vasubandhu glosses *adhirāgavitarkāḥ* as practitioners in whom desire or discursive thought is excessive.

For one whose character is strongly dominated by desire (*rāgacarita*), entry is through **aśubha-bhāvanā**, cultivation of the unattractive.

For one dominated by discursive thought (*vitarkacarita*), entry is through **ānāpānasmṛti**, recollection of inhalation and exhalation.

Thus the Path begins diagnostically rather than uniformly:

```text
same goal
    liberation

different obstruction
    rāga / vitarka

different initial counterforce
    aśubha / ānāpānasmṛti
```

This is an important specification of *pratipakṣa*: a counterforce is effective only insofar as it is adequate to the affliction actually predominating.

### 2. Why breath-recollection opposes vitarka

The Bhāṣya records more than one explanation.

One view says ānāpānasmṛti cuts off vitarka because its object-support is **non-variegated** (*avicitrālambana*). Discursive proliferation feeds on multiplicity; recollection of breathing constrains cognition to a simple recurring process.

Another interpretation contrasts aśubha as outward-facing: like visual knowledge, it attends to an externally presented object. Breath-recollection therefore functions differently, drawing cognition away from proliferating outward representation.

The important doctrinal point is not that Vasubandhu forces one explanation, but that the practice is understood functionally through its capacity to reduce discursive proliferation.

### 3. Four forms of rāga and their distinct counter-objects

Vasubandhu then differentiates rāga into four types:

```text
varṇa-rāga
    attachment to color / complexion

saṃsthāna-rāga
    attachment to bodily form / configuration

sparśa-rāga
    attachment to touch

upacāra-rāga
    attachment to bodily behavior, movement, bearing, or personal presentation
```

Each is assigned a corresponding unattractive object-support.

The details vary according to the aspect of bodily attractiveness being countered: discoloration and decomposition counter color-attraction; dismemberment and dispersal counter attraction to bodily configuration; putrefaction and exposed bone counter tactile attraction; the inert corpse counters attraction to animation, behavior, and bearing.

Thus *aśubha* is not a single undifferentiated visualization. It is a family of counter-representations selected according to the structure of desire.

### 4. Why the skeleton is recommended universally for rāga

The Kārikā concludes:

> **śaṃkalā sarvarāgiṇām** — the skeleton is recommended for all who are affected by desire.

Vasubandhu explains why. In the skeletal representation, the four features upon which the four forms of rāga depend are absent together.

The skeleton is therefore a general counter-object:

```text
color-attraction
form-attraction
touch-attraction
behavior/presentation-attraction
        ↓
skeletal framework
        ↓
common deprivation of their attractive supports
```

The skeleton does not merely negate one particular attraction; it provides a common analytical reduction of the attractive body.

### 5. Aśubha suppresses; it does not yet abandon kleśa

A crucial limitation follows immediately.

Because aśubha operates through **adhimukti-manaskāra**, a constructed or resolutive mode of attention directed to a partial domain, Vasubandhu states that it does **not** accomplish *kleśa-prahāṇa*, abandonment of the affliction.

It accomplishes **viṣkambhaṇa** — suppression, blocking, or temporary inhibition.

```text
aśubha
    counteracts active rāga
    ↓
viṣkambhaṇa
    suppression

not yet:

kleśa-prahāṇa
    definitive abandonment
```

This distinction is essential for Chapter 6. The preparatory counter-practice can make the affliction non-operative without yet producing the supramundane power that uproots it.

This gives a precise boundary between preparatory practice and the Path of Seeing introduced in 6.01.

## New Technical Terms

- **avatāra** — entry into cultivation; the transition from general preparedness to a concrete practice suited to the practitioner's dominant affliction.  
  **status:** newly central in the Mārga sequence.  
  **function:** marks practical entry.

- **rāgacarita** — one whose conduct or character is dominated by desire.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya specification.  
  **function:** diagnostic practitioner-type.

- **vitarkacarita** — one whose conduct or character is dominated by discursive thought.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya specification.  
  **function:** diagnostic practitioner-type.

- **avicitrālambana** — a non-variegated object-support.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary here.  
  **function:** explains why breath-recollection can cut off vitarka.

- **varṇa-rāga** — attachment to color or complexion.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya differentiation.  
  **function:** subtype of rāga.

- **saṃsthāna-rāga** — attachment to bodily configuration or form.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya differentiation.  
  **function:** subtype of rāga.

- **sparśa-rāga** — attachment to touch.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya differentiation.  
  **function:** subtype of rāga.

- **upacāra-rāga** — attachment to bodily behavior, bearing, or presentation.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya differentiation.  
  **function:** subtype of rāga.

- **adhimukti-manaskāra** — resolutive/constructed attention.  
  **status:** important Bhāṣya technical term.  
  **function:** explains the limited, preparatory status of aśubha.

- **viṣkambhaṇa** — suppression, blocking, inhibition.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-only distinction here.  
  **function:** effect of preparatory counter-practice short of definitive abandonment.

## Logical Determination

VAK 6.09 introduces a differentiated relation between affliction and counterforce:

```text
Affliction
    must be diagnosed
        ↓
appropriate Pratipakṣa
    is selected
        ↓
affliction becomes non-operative
        ↓
preparatory stabilization
```

The important distinction is:

```text
suppression
    ≠
abandonment
```

A practice may possess genuine counter-effective power without yet possessing the definitive supramundane *śakti* required for *prahāṇa*.

## Relation to VAK 6.01

The opening verse stated that a mundane path lacks the power to perform the decisive abandonment accomplished by the Path of Seeing.

VAK 6.09 now supplies an empirical example of the distinction:

```text
aśubha
    genuine pratipakṣa
    genuine suppressive efficacy
    but no definitive kleśa-prahāṇa
```

Thus Vasubandhu is already differentiating **degrees and kinds of Path efficacy** rather than opposing total power to total powerlessness.

## Determinate Summary

```text
prepared practitioner
    ↓
diagnostic entry

rāga-dominant
    → aśubha

vitarka-dominant
    → ānāpānasmṛti
```

For rāga, four subtypes are distinguished and matched with corresponding unattractive object-supports. The skeleton serves as the general counter-object for all forms of desire.

Yet aśubha remains preparatory: it suppresses (*viṣkambhaṇa*) the affliction but does not yet accomplish definitive abandonment (*kleśa-prahāṇa*).
