# VAK 6.51 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> śrāmaṇyam amalo mārgaḥ /  
> saṃskṛtāsaṃskṛtaṃ phalam /  
> ekānnavatis tāni muktimārgāḥ saha kṣayaiḥ // VAkK_6.51 //

**Best current translation:**  
The stainless path is śrāmaṇya. Its fruit is both conditioned and unconditioned. These are eighty-nine: the liberation-paths together with the corresponding cessations.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.51.md`](./VAK_6.51.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 369|08–370|02.

The prose first defines `śrāmaṇya`, then distinguishes conditioned and unconditioned fruits, and finally gives the exact enumeration of eighty-nine.

## 1. What Is Śrāmaṇya?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya asks what the four previously stated fruits are fruits of:

> `yāny etāni catvāri phalāny uktāni kasyaitāni phalāni / śrāmaṇyaphalāni /`

They are **fruits of śrāmaṇya**.

It then asks:

> `kim idaṃ śrāmaṇyaṃ nāma /`

and answers:

> `śrāmaṇyam amalo mārgaḥ`

The Bhāṣya glosses this immediately:

> `anāsravo mārgaḥ śrāmaṇyam`

Thus:

```text
śrāmaṇya
    = amala-mārga
    = anāsrava-mārga
    = uncontaminated path
```

This is not merely a social or institutional designation. One is a śramaṇa in the strict sense because the afflictions are pacified through the uncontaminated path.

The Bhāṣya cites a sūtra explaining the designation `śramaṇa` through the pacification of numerous harmful and unwholesome dharmas.

## 2. Why the Ordinary Person Is Not a Śramaṇa in the Ultimate Sense

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The text adds:

> `anatyantaśamanān na pṛthagjanaḥ paramārthaśramaṇaḥ`

An ordinary person is not a **śramaṇa in the ultimate sense** because the pacification is not complete.

This is important. The technical definition is teleological and path-relative:

```text
partial pacification
    ≠
paramārtha-śrāmaṇya

complete uncontaminated path
    →
śrāmaṇya in the strict sense
```

The category is therefore determined by effective liberation from affliction, not merely by external discipline or renunciant status.

## 3. The Fruit Is Both Conditioned and Unconditioned

The kārikā continues:

> `saṃskṛtāsaṃskṛtaṃ phalam`

The Bhāṣya says that the four śrāmaṇya-fruits spoken of in the sūtra comprise both conditioned and unconditioned determinations.

This distinction is then made exact.

### Conditioned fruit

The **liberation-paths** (`vimukti-mārga`) are conditioned fruits.

The text states:

> `vimuktimārgāḥ saṃskṛtāni śrāmaṇyaphalāni`

and gives their causal status:

> `tanniṣyandapuruṣakāraphalatvāt`

They are fruits because they are the **outflow/result** and **personal-effort result** of the preceding paths.

Thus the conditioned fruit is not merely “liberation” abstractly. It is the arisen path-cognition in which the accomplished separation is possessed.

### Unconditioned fruit

The Bhāṣya then states:

> `teṣāṃ kleśānāṃ prahāṇāny asaṃskṛtāni śrāmaṇyaphalāni`

The abandonments of those afflictions are the **unconditioned śrāmaṇya-fruits**.

So:

```text
conditioned fruit
    vimukti-mārga

unconditioned fruit
    kleśa-prahāṇa / kṣaya
```

The two sides of fruit must not be collapsed.

## 4. The Uninterrupted Paths Are Śrāmaṇya Itself

A very important distinction appears in the prose:

> `tatrānantaryamārgāḥ śrāmaṇyaṃ`

The **uninterrupted paths** (`ānantarya-mārga`) are **śrāmaṇya itself**.

By contrast:

> `vimuktimārgāḥ saṃskṛtāni śrāmaṇyaphalāni`

The liberation-paths are the **conditioned fruits of śrāmaṇya**.

Therefore:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    = śrāmaṇya as effective abandoning activity

vimukti-mārga
    = conditioned fruit of that activity

prahāṇa / kṣaya
    = unconditioned fruit
```

This refines the earlier immediate/liberation-path distinction. The uninterrupted path is not itself the fruit in this classification; it is the operative śrāmaṇya whose fruit appears in the liberation-path and corresponding cessation.

## 5. The Exact Enumeration of Eighty-Nine

The kārikā says:

> `ekānnavatis tāni`

The Bhāṣya gives the arithmetic explicitly.

### Abandonment by seeing

For the afflictions abandoned by seeing:

> `darśanaheyaprahāṇāyāṣṭāv ānantaryamārgā aṣṭau vimuktimārgāḥ`

There are:

```text
8 ānantarya-mārgas
8 vimukti-mārgas
```

for abandonment of what is to be abandoned by seeing.

### Abandonment by cultivation

For what is abandoned by cultivation, across the nine grounds:

> `navasu bhūmiṣu pratyekaṃ nava prakārāṇāṃ kleśānāṃ prahāṇāya tāvanta evānantaryamārgā vimuktimārgāś ca`

Each of the nine grounds contains nine grades of affliction:

```text
9 grounds
    ×
9 grades
    =
81
```

Thus there are:

```text
81 ānantarya-mārgas
81 vimukti-mārgas
```

for abandonment by cultivation.

Combining seeing and cultivation:

```text
8 + 81 = 89
```

Hence:

```text
89 liberation-paths
89 corresponding abandonments / cessations
```

These are the `ekānnavati` referred to by the verse.

## 6. Why the Number Eighty-Nine Does Not Mean 178 Fruits

The phrase:

> `muktimārgāḥ saha kṣayaiḥ`

pairs each liberation-path with its corresponding cessation.

The point is not to say that the verse lists 178 independent fruit-types. Rather, the same complete series of eighty-nine abandonments has two fruit-determinations:

```text
for each abandonment:

conditioned side
    vimukti-mārga

unconditioned side
    kṣaya / prahāṇa
```

The enumeration is therefore structurally paired.

## 7. A Precise Path–Fruit Architecture

The Bhāṣya now allows a very exact sequence:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    directly removes the relevant obstruction
    = śrāmaṇya
        ↓
vimukti-mārga
    arises as conditioned possession of freedom
    = conditioned śrāmaṇya-phala
        +
kṣaya / prahāṇa
    unconditioned cessation
    = unconditioned śrāmaṇya-phala
```

This is more precise than saying simply that “the path produces liberation.”

The path has an operative phase, a conditioned fruit, and an unconditioned fruit.

## 8. Relation to VAK 6.50

VAK 6.50 distinguished:

```text
kṣaya
    destruction / exhaustion

kṣayajñāna
    knowledge of exhaustion

anutpādajñāna
    knowledge of non-arising
```

VAK 6.51 now places the underlying path-result relation into a full classification:

```text
operative abandoning path
    ānantarya-mārga

conditioned fruit
    vimukti-mārga

unconditioned fruit
    kṣaya / prahāṇa
```

So the unconditioned cessation is not identical with the cognition that possesses or knows it.

## 9. Restrained Organon Contact

The Bhāṣya gives a particularly clean practical structure:

```text
operation
    ānantarya-mārga
        ↓
result as conditioned actuality
    vimukti-mārga
        +
result as unconditioned freedom-from
    kṣaya
```

The significance is not that conditioned and unconditioned become one ontological kind. Rather, one liberating process has two irreducible determinations of fruit.

The conditioned path makes the unconditioned cessation **attained and operative for the practitioner**, without thereby producing the unconditioned as a conditioned event.

This is an excellent example of the Kośa's Rational–Empirical discrimination: causal process, attained cognition, and unconditioned cessation must each be kept distinct if liberation is to be described without category error.

## New Technical Terms

- **śrāmaṇya** — the uncontaminated path considered as the effective śramaṇa-practice; **status:** newly focal; **function:** defines the operative path whose fruits are classified.
- **paramārthaśramaṇa** — śramaṇa in the ultimate/strict sense; **status:** Bhāṣya clarification; **function:** excludes the ordinary person whose pacification is incomplete.
- **niṣyanda-phala** — outflow/result; **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification here; **function:** explains one causal sense in which the liberation-path is fruit.
- **puruṣakāra-phala** — result of personal effort/activity; **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification here; **function:** explains the liberation-path as produced result of prior path-operation.
- **darśana-heya** — what is to be abandoned by seeing; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** supplies the eightfold seeing-path count.
- **bhāvanā-heya** — what is to be abandoned by cultivation; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** supplies the 9 × 9 cultivation-path count.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the principal explanation.

The sūtra quotation explaining `śramaṇa` is cited as authoritative support for the etymological/doctrinal definition.

The text immediately after VAK 6.51 raises the objection that, if there are so many fruits, the Buddha's canonical enumeration of four fruits would seem to require supplementation. That belongs naturally to VAK 6.52 and is not developed here.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.51 defines `śrāmaṇya` as the uncontaminated path and distinguishes it sharply from its fruits.

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    = śrāmaṇya

vimukti-mārga
    = conditioned śrāmaṇya-fruit

kṣaya / prahāṇa
    = unconditioned śrāmaṇya-fruit
```

The complete enumeration is:

```text
seeing:
    8 liberation-paths

cultivation:
    9 grounds × 9 grades = 81 liberation-paths

8 + 81 = 89
```

and each of those eighty-nine liberation-paths is paired with the corresponding unconditioned abandonment.

Thus liberation is articulated as a precise relation between **operative path**, **conditioned possession**, and **unconditioned cessation**, rather than being reduced to any one of these determinations alone.
