# VAK 6.41 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**na parāvṛtta-janmāryaḥ kāme dhātv-antaropagaḥ |**  
**sa cordhvajaś ca naivākṣa-saṃcāra-parihāṇi-bhāk // VAK 6.41 //**

The verse concerns two Noble practitioners after a decisive change of birth-position: the Noble who has undergone a further birth in the desire realm, and the Noble who has been reborn in a higher realm. Neither undergoes a transfer of faculties, nor does either fall away.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 361|16–362|09, with the kārikā at 362|01.

## 1. The Preceding Qualification: Change of Birth Restricts Later Movement

Immediately before the verse Vasubandhu states:

> **kāmadhātau parāvṛttajanmāntara āryo na dhātvantaraṃ gacchati**

A Noble who has undergone a further birth in the desire realm does not afterwards pass into another realm, because after attainment of the fruit of non-return the practitioner reaches parinirvāṇa in that same life.

By contrast, a Noble reborn in the form realm can in some cases enter the formless attainments, namely the upward-streamer whose highest destination is the summit of existence.

This sets the domain of VAK 6.41: the verse concerns what becomes impossible once the Noble continuum has matured through an intervening rebirth.

## 2. Neither Faculty-Transfer nor Falling Away

Vasubandhu glosses the verse:

> **sa ca kāmadhātau parivṛttajanmā ūrdhvadhātūpapannaścāryo naivendriyāṇi saṃcarati nāpi kathañcit parihīyate**

The Noble who has undergone another birth in the desire realm, and the Noble born in an upper realm:

- does not transfer or transform the faculties (**indriyāṇi saṃcarati**), and
- does not in any way fall away (**parihīyate**).

The important point is negative but highly determinate. A later birth does not merely add chronological distance. It changes the stability conditions of the Noble continuum.

## 3. Why Are These Changes No Longer Admitted?

The Bhāṣya asks explicitly why four possibilities are denied to the Noble after an intervening birth:

- entry from that desire-realm rebirth into the form realm,
- entry into the formless realm,
- transfer of faculties,
- falling away.

The first answer is almost brutally simple:

> **yasmān na santi** — because they do not occur.

But Vasubandhu immediately asks why they do not occur and gives the causal explanation:

> **janmāntaraparivāsenendriyāṇāṃ paripakvataratvād āśrayaviśeṣalābhāc ca**

Because through residence over another birth:

1. the faculties have become more mature (**paripakvataratva**), and
2. a superior basis/support has been obtained (**āśraya-viśeṣa-lābha**).

Thus the prohibition is not arbitrary. The continuum has crossed a maturation threshold.

## 4. Maturation as Irreversibility

This gives VAK 6.41 a distinctive place in the path-person analysis.

Earlier verses classified practitioners by:

```text
faculty
prior abandonment
fruit-status
remaining affliction
rebirth trajectory
```

Here Vasubandhu adds a temporal-causal variable:

```text
intervening Noble rebirth
    ↓
faculty maturation
    +
acquisition of a superior basis
    ↓
no faculty-transfer
no falling away
```

The path-state becomes stabilized by maturation of the continuum itself.

This is stronger than merely saying that the person has the same doctrinal status after rebirth. The range of possible future transitions has narrowed.

## 5. Why Does the Desire-Detached Trainee Not Attain Parinirvāṇa in the Intermediate State?

Vasubandhu then introduces a new question that prepares the subsequent discussion.

Why does a **vītarāga śaikṣa**, a trainee already free from desire, not necessarily attain parinirvāṇa in the intermediate state?

The Vaibhāṣika answer gives several grounds:

- the Path has not yet been fully mastered (**mārgasyājitatvāt**),
- it is not fully present or actualized (**asaṃmukhībhāvataḥ**),
- the latent tendencies are not yet exceedingly weak (**anuśayānāṃ nātimandatvāt**),
- the force of the desire-realm current is difficult to cross (**duḥsamatikrāmatvāt kāmavātoḥ**).

Vasubandhu summarizes the practical reason:

> **bahu hy anena kartavyaṃ bhavati** — much remains for this practitioner to do.

Specifically:

- abandonment of unwholesome and indeterminate afflictions,
- attainment of two or three further śrāmaṇya fruits,
- transcendence of the three realms.

The intermediate-state continuum is not capable of accomplishing all of that work.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.41 adds an important state-stability rule to the path-person system.

```text
Noble path-state
    +
intervening rebirth
        ↓
faculty maturation
        +
superior basis
        ↓
reduced transition-space
        ↓
no faculty-transfer
no falling away
```

This is one of the clearest cases so far in which path-development changes not merely the practitioner's label but the set of transitions that remain possible.

The second half of the Bhāṣya also makes clear that liberation cannot be inferred from one local attainment such as desire-detachment. The remaining work is multi-dimensional:

```text
affliction-abandonment
fruit-acquisition
realm-transcendence
```

and the capacity of the current mode of existence must be adequate to that work.

## New Technical Terms

- **indriya-saṃcāra** — transfer or change of faculty-type.  
  **status:** previously encountered as a background possibility, now explicitly denied after a certain maturation point.  
  **function:** marks a change in practitioner-capacity or faculty classification.

- **parihāṇi / parihīyate** — falling away, regression, loss of attained condition.  
  **status:** previously encountered doctrinally; central here.  
  **function:** names a backward transition now excluded by maturation.

- **paripakvataratva** — greater maturity, more fully ripened condition.  
  **status:** new local causal explanation.  
  **function:** explains why faculty-transfer and regression cease after an intervening Noble rebirth.

- **āśraya-viśeṣa-lābha** — acquisition of a superior basis/support.  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-level explanatory term.  
  **function:** second causal ground for stabilization of the Noble continuum.

- **mārgasyājitatva** — the Path's not yet being mastered/conquered.  
  **status:** new local expression.  
  **function:** explains why a desire-detached trainee still cannot complete liberation in the intermediate state.

- **asaṃmukhībhāva** — non-presence, non-actualization before the practitioner.  
  **status:** new local expression.  
  **function:** marks lack of effective present availability of the Path.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu in the primary explanatory framework.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position explicitly at 362|06–08 for the explanation of why the desire-detached trainee does not attain parinirvāṇa in the intermediate state.

The immediate causal explanation of post-rebirth stability—greater faculty maturity and acquisition of a superior basis—is presented straightforwardly and should not be over-read as a general metaphysical thesis about irreversible spiritual progress in every case.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.41 establishes a maturation threshold in the Noble path-person.

After an intervening Noble rebirth, the practitioner has:

```text
more mature faculties
    +
a superior basis
```

and therefore no longer undergoes:

```text
faculty-transfer
falling away
```

The Bhāṣya then prevents a premature inference from desire-detachment to final liberation. A trainee may still have extensive work remaining: afflictions must be abandoned, further fruits attained, and the three realms transcended. Thus path-status is determined not by a single attainment but by the full causal capacity and remaining work of the continuum.
