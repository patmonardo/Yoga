# VAK 6.21 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**kāmāśrayāṇy agradharmān dvayāśrayān labhate ’ṅganā |**  
**bhūmityāgāt tyajaty āryas tāny anāryas tu mṛtyunā || 6.21 ||**

## Bhāṣya Passage

```text
sarvāṇyapi tvetāni catvāri

kāmāśrayāṇi

trīṇi manuṣyeṣvevotpadyante /
triṣu dvīpeṣu /
utpāditapūrvāṇāṃ tu deveṣu saṃmukhībhāvaḥ /
caturthaṃ deveṣvapi /
trīṇi strīpuruṣā ubhayāśrayāṇi labhante /

agradharmān dvacyāśrayān labhate 'ṅganā /

agradharmāstu stryeva dvacyāśrayān labhate /
puruṣaḥ puruṣāśrayāneva /
strītvasya apratisaṃkhyānirodhalābhāt /

kathaṃ nirvedhabhāgīyānāṃ tyāgaḥ /

bhūmityāgāttyajatyāryastāni

yadbhūmikānyanena pratilabdhāni bhavanti tāṃ bhūmiṃ tyajann āryas tānyapi tyajati nānyathā /
bhūmityāgaḥ punarbhūmisaṃcārāt /

anāryastu mṛtyunā /

pṛthagjanastu nikāyasabhāgatyāgenaiva tyajati saty asati vā bhūmisaṃcāre /
```

## Voice

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika / Abhidharma classification reported and explained by Vasubandhu.

## Translation and Exposition

All four nirvedhabhāgīya wholesome roots have the **desire-domain as their living support** (*kāmāśraya*).

The first three—Heat, Summits, and Acceptance—arise initially only among human beings, specifically on the three inhabited continents. Once they have previously been produced, however, they may become manifest among gods. The fourth, the Highest Mundane Dharmas, may arise among gods as well.

The first three may be obtained on either female or male bodily support. With the Highest Mundane Dharmas, the classification becomes narrower: a woman may obtain them on either support, whereas a man obtains them only on male support. The Bhāṣya explains this by reference to the acquisition of non-analytic cessation with respect to femaleness.

Vasubandhu then asks how the nirvedhabhāgīyas are relinquished.

A Noble practitioner loses them through **abandonment of the meditative level** on which they were obtained. Such abandonment occurs by moving to another level. Apart from abandoning that level, the Noble one does not lose them.

An ordinary person (*pṛthagjana*), by contrast, loses them through death—through relinquishing the present *nikāyasabhāga*, the present life-continuum or class-membership—whether or not there has been movement to another meditative level.

## Analysis

### 1. Āśraya is not Bhūmi

VAK 6.20 assigned the nirvedhabhāgīyas to concentration-levels such as anāgamya, dhyānāntara, and the four dhyānas. VAK 6.21 now says all four nevertheless have **kāma-dhātu embodiment as āśraya**.

Thus two axes must be kept distinct:

```text
āśraya
    living psycho-physical support

bhūmi
    meditative/cognitive level
```

A cognition may therefore be:

```text
supported by desire-domain embodiment
    while
operating on a form-domain concentration level
```

This is a major practical clarification. Path Knowledge is not produced by escaping embodiment. It is generated in and through a determinate embodied continuum while cognition operates at a different level of refinement.

### 2. Genesis and Manifestation are Distinct

The Bhāṣya carefully distinguishes **first production** from later **manifestation**.

For the first three nirvedhabhāgīyas:

```text
initial production
    humans only
    three inhabited continents

later manifestation
    may occur among gods
    if previously produced
```

This means the operative Path-capacity has a history. Once generated, it can later become manifest under conditions in which its original acquisition would not have occurred.

So once again the Kośa distinguishes:

```text
acquisition
    ≠
manifestation
```

The Path has memory in the strong causal sense of a previously established capacity.

### 3. Highest Mundane Dharmas and the Narrowing Threshold

The fourth stage, the Highest Mundane Dharmas, is exceptional. It can arise among gods as well, unlike the first acquisition of the first three.

This fits the structure established in 6.19:

```text
Highest Mundane Dharmas
    immediately draw in the Path of Seeing
```

At this threshold, the decisive factor is no longer simply the human location in which the earlier preparatory roots were first generated. The cultivated capacity has become sufficiently determinate to operate under a wider support-condition.

### 4. Sex-support Classification

The Bhāṣya preserves a technical Abhidharma classification concerning female and male bodily supports.

The first three roots can be acquired on either support by either sex. For the Highest Mundane Dharmas, however:

```text
woman
    may obtain them with either support

man
    obtains them only with male support
```

The explanation invokes **apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha**, non-analytic cessation, with respect to femaleness.

This should be recorded as part of the received Abhidharma physiology without making it carry more philosophical weight than the passage itself gives it.

### 5. Loss Reveals the Difference Between Noble and Ordinary Continuity

The second half is philosophically stronger.

For the Noble practitioner:

```text
nirvedhabhāgīya acquired on bhūmi X
    ↓
remains
    ↓
unless bhūmi X is abandoned
```

For the ordinary person:

```text
nirvedhabhāgīya acquired
    ↓
life-continuum ends
    ↓
root is relinquished
```

This reveals a difference in the **mode of possession** of Path Knowledge.

The ordinary practitioner possesses the preparation contingently within the present life-continuum. The Noble practitioner possesses it in a way stabilized by the transformed Path-status; loss is tied not simply to death but to the abandonment of the specific cognitive ground.

This is another step toward understanding how Truth becomes operative Path Knowledge:

```text
ordinary possession
    dependent on present continuum

Noble possession
    structurally tied to attained Path-level
```

The change is not only what is known. The **conditions under which that knowledge can be lost have changed**.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

The current sequence can now be refined:

```text
Truth heard
    ↓
Truth reflected
    ↓
Truth cultivated
    ↓
Truth structured as a total field
    ↓
Truth strengthened as a wholesome root
    ↓
Truth draws in supramundane penetration
    ↓
Path Knowledge changes its mode of possession
```

VAK 6.21 adds a new criterion for operativity:

> A cognition becomes more fully Path-like not only when it grows stronger, but when its **continuity becomes less dependent on accidental conditions of ordinary existence**.

The question of liberation is therefore also a question of **stability across changing supports**.

## New Technical Terms

- **āśraya** — living or psycho-physical support on which a Path-state depends.
  - **status:** previously encountered, but newly contrasted with *bhūmi* here.
  - **function:** distinguishes bodily support from meditative level.

- **saṃmukhībhāva** — becoming present or manifest.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-level technical usage.
  - **function:** distinguishes later manifestation from initial acquisition.

- **nikāyasabhāga** — membership in a particular life-continuum/class of existence.
  - **status:** previously encountered in Abhidharma ontology.
  - **function:** explains why the ordinary person loses these roots at death.

- **bhūmisaṃcāra** — movement from one meditative level to another.
  - **status:** local technical usage.
  - **function:** explains *bhūmityāga*, abandonment of a level.

- **apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha** — non-analytic cessation.
  - **status:** established Kośa technical term.
  - **function:** invoked to explain the sex-support restriction for the Highest Mundane Dharmas.

## Determinate Summary

```text
Nirvedhabhāgīyas:
    living support — desire-domain embodiment

First three:
    initially arise among humans
    later may manifest among gods

Highest Mundane Dharmas:
    may arise among gods as well

Loss:
    Noble — by abandoning the attained bhūmi
    ordinary person — by death / ending the present life-continuum
```

The decisive philosophical result is that **operative Path Knowledge has a support, a level, a history of acquisition, a mode of manifestation, and a characteristic mode of loss**. The Kośa is therefore describing not propositions in a mind, but the causal architecture by which Truth becomes a stable power in a living continuum.
