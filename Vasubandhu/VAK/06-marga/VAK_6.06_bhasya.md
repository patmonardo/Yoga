# VAK 6.06 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

### Devanāgarī

व्यपकर्षद्वयवतो नासन्तुष्टमहेच्छयोः ।
लब्धे भूयःस्पृहातुष्टिरलब्धेच्छा महेच्छता ॥ ६.६ ॥

### IAST

vyapakarṣa-dvayavato nāsantuṣṭa-mahecchayoḥ |
labdhe bhūyaḥ-spṛhātuṣṭir alabdhēcchā mahecchatā || 6.6 ||

## Bhāṣya Context

VAK 6.05 concluded by distinguishing the three Prajñās genetically:

- śrutamayī — certainty generated from authoritative testimony
- cintāmayī — generated from examination by reasoning
- bhāvanāmayī — generated from samādhi

The Bhāṣya now asks:

> For one thus engaged in cultivation, how does cultivation become accomplished?

The answer is practical rather than merely cognitive: the practitioner must possess **twofold withdrawal**.

## Twofold Withdrawal

**Voice: Vasubandhu / exposition**

The Bhāṣya glosses `vyapakarṣa-dvaya` explicitly:

```text
kāya-vyapakarṣa
    bodily withdrawal

citta-vyapakarṣa
    mental withdrawal
```

One becomes withdrawn through body and mind by distancing oneself from:

```text
saṃsarga
    association / social entanglement

akuśala-vitarka
    unskillful thought
```

Thus cultivation requires both an external and an internal reorganization.

```text
bodily withdrawal
    removes entangling situations

mental withdrawal
    removes the corresponding unskillful thought-activity
```

Neither is presented as sufficient in abstraction from the other.

## Who Can Accomplish This Withdrawal?

The Bhāṣya asks for whom the twofold withdrawal is easy.

The answer:

```text
alpeccha
    one of few desires

saṃtuṣṭa
    one who is content
```

Hence the negative statement in the Kārikā:

```text
not for:
    asaṃtuṣṭa
    the discontented

    maheccha
    one of great desires
```

The Path therefore requires not merely theoretical cognition but a determinate economy of desire.

## The Inherited Definitions

**Voice: Ābhidhārmikas**

The inherited explanation states:

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi
    desire for more of fine robes and similar things
    already obtained

mahecchatā
    desire for such things not yet obtained
```

This corresponds to the Kārikā's concise formulation:

```text
labdhe bhūyaḥ-spṛhā
    craving for more of what has been obtained
        = discontent

alabdhe icchā
    desire for what has not been obtained
        = great desire
```

## Vasubandhu's Objection

**Voice: Vasubandhu / critical objection**

Vasubandhu notices that this distinction is unstable.

If desire for a greater amount is desire precisely for an amount **not yet obtained**, then the first definition collapses into the second.

He therefore asks, in effect:

```text
If the additional quantity is not yet possessed,
what really distinguishes discontent from great desire?
```

This is a characteristic Bhāṣya move: an inherited taxonomic distinction is tested for whether its members are actually differentiated by their own grounds.

## Vasubandhu's Revised Definition

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

He says:

> `evaṃ tu yujyate` — rather, it is appropriate in this way.

He then reformulates the distinction:

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi
    distress concerning what has already been obtained
    because it is inferior or insufficient

mahecchatā
    desire for superior and abundant things
    that have not been obtained
```

More literally:

```text
labdhena apraṇītena aprabhūtena paritāpaḥ
    vexation because what is possessed
    is not excellent and not abundant
        = discontent

alabdha-praṇīta-prabhūta-icchā
    desire for what is unpossessed,
    excellent, and abundant
        = great desire
```

This is a considerably cleaner distinction.

The difference is no longer simply:

```text
obtained
versus
unobtained
```

but:

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi
    negative relation to present possession
    "what I have is not good/enough"

mahecchatā
    positive projection toward superior future possession
    "I want the better/more abundant thing"
```

## Bhāṣya Analysis

The practical sequence from 6.05 to 6.06 is now clearer:

```text
śruta
    ↓
cintā
    ↓
samādhi-generated bhāvanāmayī Prajñā
    ↓
requires viable cultivation
    ↓
requires bodily + mental withdrawal
    ↓
requires few desires + contentment
```

Thus the arising of Path-Prajñā is conditioned by the organization of the practitioner's life.

This does not mean that contentment itself is the Path of Seeing. Rather, it removes conditions incompatible with sustained cultivation.

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes:

```text
conditions favorable to cultivation
    ≠
liberating cognition itself
```

but the former are real conditions for the genesis of the latter.

## Relation to the Śakti Problem

VAK 6.01 attributed a distinctive `śakti` to the supramundane Path. VAK 6.05–6.06 are beginning to show that such capacity has a genesis.

It does not arise in an abstract knower detached from practical conditions.

```text
conduct
    ↓
learning
    ↓
reasoned examination
    ↓
samādhi
    ↓
cultivation

while simultaneously:

few desires
contentment
bodily withdrawal
mental withdrawal
```

The capacity for liberating Seeing is thus prepared through both cognition and practical reconfiguration.

## New Technical Terms

- **vyapakarṣa-dvaya** — twofold withdrawal: bodily and mental
  - **status:** introduced as an explicit Bhāṣya explanation here
  - **function:** condition enabling cultivation

- **saṃsarga** — association, social entanglement
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** external condition from which bodily withdrawal distances the practitioner

- **akuśala-vitarka** — unskillful thought
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** mental activity from which mental withdrawal distances the practitioner

- **alpecchatā** — fewness of desires
  - **status:** central practical Path-term
  - **function:** makes withdrawal practicable; opposed to great desire

- **saṃtuṣṭi** — contentment
  - **status:** central practical Path-term
  - **function:** makes withdrawal practicable; opposed to discontent

- **paritāpa** — distress, vexation
  - **status:** important in Vasubandhu's revised definition
  - **function:** defines discontent as a negative relation toward what is already possessed

## Voice Register

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / exposition** — bodily and mental withdrawal as the condition of cultivation.
- **Voice: Ābhidhārmikas** — inherited definition of discontent and great desire by obtained/unobtained objects.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu / critical objection** — inherited distinction risks collapsing because additional quantity is itself unobtained.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — revised definition using distress over inferior/insufficient possession versus desire for superior/abundant unpossessed goods.

## Logical Determination

The first distinction can be expressed as two opposed orientations of desire:

```text
asaṃtuṣṭi
    present possession
        ↓
    judged deficient
        ↓
    vexation

mahecchatā
    imagined superior possession
        ↓
    projected as desirable
        ↓
    acquisition-drive
```

The opposed Path-conditions are:

```text
saṃtuṣṭi
    present sufficiency

alpecchatā
    contraction of the field of acquisition
```

The practitioner thereby becomes capable of `vyapakarṣa`: withdrawing from the social and mental circuits that continually reproduce acquisition-oriented thought.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.06 does more than recommend ascetic simplicity.

It explains a practical condition for the genesis of cultivation:

```text
cultivation
    requires twofold withdrawal

withdrawal
    requires few desires and contentment

bodily withdrawal
    distances social entanglement

mental withdrawal
    distances unskillful thought
```

Vasubandhu also improves the inherited distinction between discontent and great desire:

```text
discontent
    vexation at the inferiority or insufficiency
    of what one already possesses

great desire
    desire for superior and abundant things
    not yet possessed
```

The result is that Mārga is already being exhibited as a **genetic discipline of knowing whose cognitive transformation depends upon a corresponding transformation of practical life**.
