# VAK 6.28 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**kṣānti-jñānāny ānantarya-mukti-mārgā yathākramam |**  
**adṛṣṭa-dṛṣṭer dṛṅ-mārgas tatra pañcadaśa kṣaṇāḥ || 6.28 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 352|12–353|11.

This passage completes the functional analysis of the sixteen-moment **satyābhisamaya** introduced in VAK 6.25–6.27. Vasubandhu first explains why the sequence must contain both **kṣānti** and **jñāna**, then determines the strict boundary between **darśanamārga** and **bhāvanāmārga**.

## 1. Why Must There Be Both Kṣānti and Jñāna?

Vasubandhu asks explicitly:

> **kasmāt punaḥ kṣāntayo jñānāni cāvaśyaṃ bhavanti** — why must there necessarily be both acceptances and knowledges?

The answer is the first half of the Kārikā:

> **kṣāntijñānāny ānantarya-muktimārgā yathākramam**

The distribution is exact:

```text
kṣāntayaḥ
    ānantaryamārgāḥ

jñānāni
    muktimārgāḥ
```

The acceptances are **uninterrupted paths**; the knowledges are **liberation paths**.

This is not merely a terminological pairing. The two moments perform different functions in one causal operation.

## 2. Kṣānti as Ānantaryamārga

Vasubandhu explains:

> **ānantaryamārgāḥ kṣāntayaḥ kleśaprāptivicchedaṃ praty antarāyitum aśakyatvāt**

The kṣāntis are uninterrupted paths because, with respect to cutting off the acquisition or possession of the affliction, their operation cannot be interrupted.

The operative object is therefore not simply an abstract false proposition. The Path attacks the **kleśaprāpti** — the acquisition/possession by which the affliction belongs to and remains effective in the continuum.

The local structure is:

```text
kleśaprāpti
    possession of the affliction
        ↓

kṣānti / ānantaryamārga
    cuts its continuity
        ↓

kleśaprāptiviccheda
    severance of afflictive acquisition
```

This clarifies the eliminative function already anticipated in the Kārikā pass. **Kṣānti** is not merely assent to a truth; it is a supramundane cognitive moment that operates as the immediate counterforce to the corresponding afflictive possession.

## 3. Jñāna as Muktimārga

The corresponding knowledges are:

> **muktimārgāstu jñānāni** — the knowledges, however, are liberation paths.

Vasubandhu explains this through the arising of **visaṃyogaprāpti**, acquisition of disconnection:

> **kleśaprāptivimuktānāṃ visaṃyogaprāptisahotpādāt**

When the continuum has been freed from the relevant afflictive acquisition, the acquisition of disconnection arises together with the liberation path.

Thus the functional pair becomes more exact:

```text
kṣānti
    severs kleśaprāpti
    ↓

jñāna
    arises with visaṃyogaprāpti
    possession of disconnection
```

The first moment destroys the affliction's hold; the second is the path-moment in which the resulting disconnection is possessed.

## 4. Why Both Are Necessary: Thief and Door

Vasubandhu concludes:

> **ata ubhayair avaśyaṃ bhavitavyam** — therefore both must necessarily occur.

He gives the analogy:

> **dvābhyāṃ cauraniṣkāsana-kapāṭapidhānavat**

```text
ānantaryamārga
    expelling the thief

muktimārga
    shutting the door
```

The analogy is unusually illuminating because the two operations are inseparable but not identical.

```text
1. remove what is present and hostile
2. establish the condition in which its presence has been excluded
```

Accordingly:

```text
kṣānti
    active severance

jñāna
    established release
```

This gives the sixteen-moment sequence the character of repeated determinate operations rather than a series of synonymous cognitions.

## 5. Why Not Let Disconnection Arise with the Next Uninterrupted Path?

Vasubandhu considers a technical alternative. Suppose the **visaṃyogaprāpti** were to arise only together with the second uninterrupted path rather than with the liberation path immediately following the first.

He answers that, in that case, there would be no knowledge concerning that very object in which doubt had already been abandoned:

> **prahīṇavicikitsaṃ jñānaṃ tatraivālambane notpannaṃ syāt**

The pair therefore secures a determinate cognitive completion:

```text
obstruction severed
    ↓
knowledge on that same object-support
    free from the abandoned doubt
```

The path does not merely destroy and move on. It produces an uncontaminated knowledge corresponding to the field just liberated.

## 6. Objection: Are the Defilements Not Said to Be Destroyed by Knowledge?

An objection appeals to doctrinal language according to which nine groups of fetters are said to be destroyed by **jñāna**:

> **nava saṃyojananikāyā jñānavadhyāḥ**

If the actual abandonment occurs through the kṣāntis, does this contradict the treatise?

Vasubandhu answers:

> **na** — no.

The reason is:

> **kṣāntīnāṃ jñānaparivāratvāt** — because the kṣāntis belong to the retinue or surrounding complex of knowledge.

He gives the analogy:

> **rājaparivārakṛtasya rājakṛtavyapadeśavat**

What is done by the king's retinue may conventionally be said to have been done by the king.

So the doctrinal statement “destroyed by knowledge” need not imply that the succeeding knowledge-moment itself performs the severance.

The functional distinction remains:

```text
kṣānti
    performs the immediate cutting

jñāna
    is the governing cognitive complex
    with which that cutting is doctrinally associated
```

This is an important caution against collapsing the two moments merely because a broader canonical expression attributes the entire operation to knowledge.

## 7. Are All Sixteen Moments the Path of Seeing?

Vasubandhu now asks:

> **kiṃ punaḥ sarvāṇi ṣoḍaśacittāni satyadarśanād darśanamārgaḥ** — are all sixteen cognitive moments the Path of Seeing because they see the truths?

His answer is explicit:

> **na** — no.

The defining principle is:

> **adṛṣṭadṛṣṭer dṛṅmārgaḥ** — it is the Path of Seeing because it sees what had not been seen.

And therefore:

> **tatra pañcadaśa kṣaṇāḥ** — within it there are fifteen moments.

Vasubandhu specifies the range:

> **duḥkhadharmajñānakṣāntim ārabhya yāvat mārge 'nvayajñānakṣāntiḥ**

From the first Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance concerning suffering through the Anvaya-Knowledge-Acceptance concerning the Path — these fifteen moments constitute the Path of Seeing.

## 8. The Criterion: Seeing a Truth Not Previously Seen

Why exactly fifteen?

Vasubandhu answers:

> **adṛṣṭasatyadarśanāt** — because they see truths not previously seen.

At the sixteenth moment, however:

> **ṣoḍaśe tu nāsty apūrvaṃ dravyam**

There is no longer a newly encountered truth-object remaining in the relevant sense.

The sixteenth moment therefore belongs to:

> **yathādṛṣṭābhyasanād bhāvanāmārga eva**

**bhāvanāmārga**, the Path of Cultivation, because it is repeated development or practice according to what has already been seen.

The strict transition is thus:

```text
moments 1–15
    adṛṣṭa → dṛṣṭa
    first seeing
    darśanamārga

moment 16
    yathādṛṣṭābhyasana
    cultivation according to what has been seen
    bhāvanāmārga
```

This confirms and sharpens the principle already stated in the preceding discussion:

> **yathādarśanaṃ bhāvanā** — cultivation is according to seeing.

## 9. Objection: Does the Sixteenth Moment Not See Something Previously Unseen?

An objection presses the definition.

The sixteenth moment, **mārgānvayajñāna**, follows the fifteenth moment, **mārgānvayajñānakṣānti**. Does it not therefore see that immediately preceding moment, which had never before been seen?

Vasubandhu answers by distinguishing the **truth** from an individual **moment**:

> **satyaṃ prati cintā na kṣaṇam**

The criterion concerns the truth, not the novelty of every single moment.

He gives the analogy:

> **na hi kṣaṇenādṛṣṭena satyam adṛṣṭaṃ bhavati | yathā naikena luṅgenālūnena kedāram alūnaṃ bhavati**

Just as leaving one small portion uncut does not make the whole field “unharvested,” the novelty of one individual moment does not mean that the relevant truth remains unseen.

The definition of **darśanamārga** is therefore object-structural, not a trivial temporal definition according to which every newly arisen thing would count as unseen.

```text
novel moment
    ≠
unseen truth
```

What matters is whether the Truth itself remains to be disclosed for the first time.

## 10. Why the Sixteenth Is Bhāvanāmārga

Vasubandhu adds several technical grounds for classifying **mārgānvayajñāna** as cultivation rather than seeing. The passage lists its status as a **fruit**, its cultivation of the eight knowledges and sixteen aspects, and further continuity-related characteristics of the prior path.

The Sanskrit here is compressed:

> **phalatvād aṣṭajñānaṣoḍaśākārabhāvanāt pūrvamārgavihāneḥ prabandhikatvāc ca mārgānvayajñānaṃ bhāvanāmārgaḥ**

For the present pass the secure determination is:

```text
mārgānvayajñāna
    is no longer first disclosure
    functions as result
    cultivates the established knowledge/aspect structure
    continues the path on the basis of what has already been seen
```

We should not force every compressed technical term here into a broader philosophical reconstruction before the subsequent verses clarify the cultivation structure.

## 11. A Final Objection: Does Its Preservation of Seeing-Results Make It Seeing?

The sixteenth moment maintains the abandonment of afflictions removable by seeing:

> **darśanaheyakleśaprahāṇasaṃdhāraṇāt**

Could that make it itself part of the Path of Seeing?

Vasubandhu rejects the inference:

> **na | atiprasaṅgāt** — no, because the consequence would extend too far.

The earlier seven knowledge-moments are nevertheless counted within the Path of Seeing because:

> **darśanasyāsamāptatvāt** — the process of seeing has not yet been completed.

Until the whole truth-sequence is complete, those liberation-path knowledges occur **within the interval of an unfinished first seeing**:

> **na hi sarvaṃ satyadarśanaṃ samāptam iti tadantarālatvāt tāny api darśanamārgaḥ**

This is a decisive clarification.

The first seven jñānas are not Path of Seeing because “jñāna” inherently equals seeing. They are Path of Seeing because the total first-seeing operation is still underway.

The sixteenth jñāna differs because the total first-seeing operation is already complete.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.28 exposes the operative grammar of the Path more clearly than any preceding verse:

```text
Truth-object
    ↓

kṣānti
    ānantaryamārga
    cuts kleśaprāpti
    ↓

severance
    ↓

jñāna
    muktimārga
    arises with visaṃyogaprāpti
    ↓

liberated cognition of that same object
    ↓

next determinate Truth-operation
```

The thief-and-door analogy captures the pair:

```text
expel the thief
    ≠
shut the door

but

expelling without securing
    is incomplete
```

So the “suffering-consuming automaton” can now be stated more precisely, provisionally:

```text
input:
    determinate Truth / determinate afflictive possession

operation A:
    kṣānti
    sever the corresponding kleśaprāpti

operation B:
    jñāna
    establish and cognize disconnection

advance:
    proceed to the next determinate object
```

But the mechanism is not endless. It contains its own transition-rule:

```text
while Truth remains adṛṣṭa:
    darśanamārga

when the Truth-field is fully dṛṣṭa:
    darśanamārga terminates
    bhāvanāmārga begins
```

This is genuine method: the process is defined by a condition, performs determinate transformations, and changes its own mode once that condition has been fulfilled.

## New Technical Terms

- **ānantaryamārga** — uninterrupted path.
  - **status:** explicitly defined here as the function of the kṣāntis.
  - **function:** directly severs the possession/acquisition of the corresponding affliction.

- **muktimārga** — liberation path.
  - **status:** explicitly defined here as the function of the jñānas.
  - **function:** arises with acquisition of disconnection after the relevant afflictive possession has been severed.

- **kleśaprāpti** — acquisition/possession of an affliction.
  - **status:** technical Sarvāstivāda causal vocabulary.
  - **function:** the determinate bond severed by the uninterrupted path.

- **kleśaprāptiviccheda** — severance of afflictive acquisition.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory formulation.
  - **function:** immediate work of the kṣānti as uninterrupted path.

- **visaṃyogaprāpti** — acquisition of disconnection.
  - **status:** technical Sarvāstivāda result vocabulary.
  - **function:** arises with the liberation path and marks possession of the accomplished separation.

- **jñānaparivāra** — retinue/surrounding complex of knowledge.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term.
  - **function:** explains why abandonment performed by kṣānti may doctrinally be attributed to knowledge.

- **adṛṣṭasatyadarśana** — seeing a truth not previously seen.
  - **status:** defining expression for darśanamārga.
  - **function:** establishes both the special power and exact limit of the Path of Seeing.

- **yathādṛṣṭābhyasana** — repeated development/practice according to what has been seen.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definition of the transition to cultivation.
  - **function:** distinguishes bhāvanāmārga from first seeing.

- **saṃdhāraṇa** — maintaining, sustaining.
  - **status:** explanatory vocabulary in the final objection.
  - **function:** describes preservation of abandonment already accomplished by the Path of Seeing.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** primarily Vasubandhu presenting and defending the Abhidharma path analysis, with several explicit objections introduced dialectically.

The passage is especially argumentative. We should distinguish the settled functional schema from the objections used to test it:

```text
settled schema:
    kṣānti = ānantaryamārga
    jñāna = muktimārga
    first fifteen moments = darśanamārga
    sixteenth = bhāvanāmārga

objections test:
    timing of visaṃyogaprāpti
    scriptural attribution of destruction to jñāna
    whether the sixteenth sees something novel
    whether sustaining seeing-produced abandonment makes it Seeing
```

The exact ontology of **prāpti** and **visaṃyogaprāpti** is Sarvāstivāda technical machinery and should not yet be identified with an Organon category. Its functional role in this passage is sufficiently clear without doing so.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.28 reveals the internal operating method of the sixteen-moment realization:

- the **kṣāntis** are uninterrupted paths because they directly sever the possession of the relevant afflictions;
- the **jñānas** are liberation paths because acquisition of disconnection arises with them;
- both are necessary, compared to expelling a thief and then shutting the door;
- the Bhāṣya defends this distinction against the objection that canonical language attributes destruction to knowledge;
- the strict Path of Seeing consists of the first fifteen moments, from the first suffering-Dharma-Acceptance through Path-Anvaya-Acceptance;
- the defining criterion is **seeing a truth not previously seen**;
- the sixteenth moment is not first seeing, even though it follows a newly arisen fifteenth moment, because novelty of an individual moment is not the same as an unseen Truth;
- **mārgānvayajñāna**, the sixteenth moment, begins the Path of Cultivation as development according to what has already been seen.

The Path now displays a clear rule-governed transformation:

```text
kṣānti
    sever

jñāna
    secure release

repeat across determinate Truth-fields

until no Truth remains unseen

then:
    Seeing → Cultivation
```

The method should continue to be analyzed before we attempt a higher synthesis.
