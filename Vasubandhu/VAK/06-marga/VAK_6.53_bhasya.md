# VAK 6.53 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> jñānāṣṭakasya lābho 'tha ṣoḍaśākārabhāvanā /  
> laukikāptaṃ tu miśratvānāsravāptiḥ dhṛteḥ phalam // VAkK_6.53 //

**Best current translation:**  
And there is acquisition of the eight knowledges and cultivation of the sixteen aspects. What was attained through a mundane path nevertheless belongs to the fruit because it is combined with the uncontaminated and is maintained by an uncontaminated attainment.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.53.md`](./VAK_6.53.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 370|08–370|23.

The first half completes the five causes announced in VAK 6.52. The second half begins a new objection: if `śrāmaṇya` is strictly the uncontaminated path, how can fruits partly obtained through a mundane path count as `śrāmaṇya-phala`? Vasubandhu answers by distinguishing the historical means by which particular abandonments were first obtained from the integrated attainment under which the fruit is presently possessed.

## 1. The Fourth Criterion: Acquisition of the Eight Knowledges

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya glosses:

> `yugapadaṣṭajñānalābhaścaturvidhānāṃ dharmānvayajñānānām`

At each principal fruit there is simultaneous acquisition of the **eight knowledges**: the four `dharma-jñānas` and the four `anvaya-jñānas`.

```text
four truths
    ×
(dharma-jñāna + anvaya-jñāna)
        ↓
eight knowledges
```

The fruit is therefore not defined merely by having fewer afflictions. It includes possession of an articulated system of noble knowing.

## 2. The Fifth Criterion: Cultivation of the Sixteen Aspects

The Bhāṣya continues:

> `ṣoḍaśākārabhāvanā anityādyākārāṇām`

There is cultivation of the sixteen aspects beginning with impermanence.

These are the four aspects belonging to each of the four truths:

```text
4 truths × 4 aspects = 16 aspects
```

The important point here is structural: the principal fruit includes not only acquisition of knowledges but cultivation of the determinate forms through which the truths are cognized.

## 3. Completion of the Fivefold Fruit-Test

Vasubandhu sums up:

> `imāni hi pañca kāraṇāni phale phale bhavanti`

These five causes are present in each fruit.

The complete set from VAK 6.52–6.53 is:

1. relinquishment of the preceding path-status;
2. acquisition of a previously unattained path;
3. gathering the abandonments under one attainment;
4. simultaneous acquisition of the eight knowledges;
5. cultivation of the sixteen aspects.

Thus the four principal fruits are established at points where the whole path-position is reorganized.

```text
previous path-status relinquished
    ↓
new path acquired
    ↓
abandonments integrated
    ↓
eight knowledges possessed
    ↓
sixteen aspects cultivated
    ↓
principal fruit
```

## 4. Objection: How Can a Mundanely Obtained Result Be Śrāmaṇya-Fruit?

**Voice:** Objector

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `yadyanāsravo mārgaḥ śrāmaṇyaṃ kathaṃ laukikamārgaprāptaṃ phaladvayaṃ śrāmaṇyaphalaṃ yujyate`

If the uncontaminated path is `śrāmaṇya`, how can the two fruits that may partly be obtained through a mundane path — the once-returner and non-returner fruits — properly be called fruits of `śrāmaṇya`?

This objection follows directly from VAK 6.51. If:

```text
śrāmaṇya = anāsrava-mārga
```

then a result originally produced by `laukika-mārga` seems excluded from `śrāmaṇya-phala`.

## 5. Vasubandhu's Answer: The Fruit Is Mixed

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The kārikā answers:

> `laukikāptaṃ tu miśratvāt ... phalam`

The abandonment obtained mundanely can nevertheless belong to the fruit **because the fruit is mixed**.

Vasubandhu explains that the abandonment included in the once-returner or non-returner fruit is not merely the result of the mundane path:

> `na hi tatra laukikamārgaphalameva prahāṇaṃ ...`

Rather:

> `darśanamārgaphalamapi prahāṇaṃ tatra miśrīkriyate`

The abandonment resulting from the **path of seeing** is mixed together with it.

So the fruit contains an integrated history:

```text
earliest noble abandonments
    from darśana-mārga
        +
later abandonments
    possibly from laukika-mārga
        ↓
one fruit-totality
```

The earlier worldly acquisition is not reclassified as having originally been supramundane. It is incorporated into a fruit that necessarily also contains uncontaminated path-results.

## 6. One Attainment Gathers the Whole Fruit

The decisive explanatory sentence is:

> `sarvasya tatphalasaṃgṛhītaikavisaṃyogaprāptilābhāt`

There is acquisition of **one attainment of separation** embracing everything included in that fruit.

This directly continues the `kṣayasaṃkalana` principle of VAK 6.52:

```text
plural abandonments
    with different historical routes
        ↓
one visaṃyoga-prāpti
        ↓
one fruit-position
```

The unity of the fruit is therefore not the claim that every abandonment was produced in the same manner. Its unity lies in the present attainment under which all the relevant abandonments are possessed together.

## 7. Scriptural Definitions Confirm the Integration

Vasubandhu cites sūtra definitions.

The once-returner fruit is described through:

- abandonment of the three fetters;
- attenuation of passion, hatred, and delusion.

The non-returner fruit is described through:

- abandonment of the five lower fetters.

These definitions gather together abandonments accomplished at different stages rather than naming only the immediately preceding path-event.

This supports the cumulative structure of fruit:

```text
fruit
    = integrated totality of relevant abandonments

not

fruit
    = only the most recently produced abandonment
```

## 8. The Uncontaminated Attainment Maintains the Abandonment

The Bhāṣya then states:

> `anāsravayā ca visaṃyogaprāptyā tatprahāṇaṃ sadhāryate`

That abandonment is maintained by an **uncontaminated attainment of separation**.

This clarifies the difficult `anāsravāptiḥ dhṛteḥ` of the kārikā.

The fruit is not merely historically mixed. Its achieved unity is held under an uncontaminated determination:

```text
mundanely obtained abandonment
        +
seeing-path abandonment
        ↓
integrated fruit
        ↓
anāsrava visaṃyoga-prāpti
        ↓
maintenance of the whole abandonment
```

Thus the qualification `śrāmaṇya-phala` is justified by the **present noble attainment that sustains the integrated fruit**, not by pretending that every component abandonment originated through an uncontaminated path.

## 9. A Precise Preservation/Supersession Structure

The doctrinal logic is subtle and should not be flattened.

The mundane path remains mundane:

```text
laukika-mārga
    produced a real abandonment
```

The noble path does not retroactively change that fact.

But once the practitioner reaches the relevant fruit:

```text
mundane abandonment
    is preserved

seeing-path abandonment
    is combined with it

uncontaminated attainment
    gathers and sustains both
```

So the distinction of origin is preserved while the results are integrated under a new governing determination.

This is stronger than saying simply that the mundane attainment “becomes supramundane.” It does not. Rather, **a mundanely produced result becomes a constituent of a fruit whose unity and maintenance are uncontaminated**.

## 10. Relation to VAK 6.51–6.52

VAK 6.51 established:

```text
śrāmaṇya
    = uncontaminated path

śrāmaṇya-phala
    = conditioned liberation-path
      + unconditioned abandonment
```

VAK 6.52 then established that a principal fruit gathers multiple abandonments into one attainment.

VAK 6.53 now shows why those two claims are compatible with mundane preliminary abandonments:

```text
historical production
    may be partly mundane

fruit integration
    is noble and uncontaminated
```

The category of fruit is therefore **genetic and synthetic**: it preserves the route by which each abandonment arose while determining their present unity through the noble path.

## New Technical Terms

- **jñānāṣṭaka** — the eight knowledges; four dharma-jñānas and four anvaya-jñānas.  
  **Status:** previously encountered individually; here gathered as a fruit-condition.  
  **Function:** fourth criterion for establishment of a principal fruit.

- **ṣoḍaśākāra-bhāvanā** — cultivation of the sixteen truth-aspects beginning with impermanence.  
  **Status:** previously encountered in the Path analysis.  
  **Function:** fifth criterion for establishment of a principal fruit.

- **miśratva** — mixed or combined character.  
  **Status:** important Bhāṣya clarification of the kārikā.  
  **Function:** explains how mundanely produced abandonments can enter a śrāmaṇya-fruit.

- **ekavisaṃyoga-prāpti** — one attainment of separation gathering the abandonments included in a fruit.  
  **Status:** developed form of the visaṃyoga-prāpti doctrine from VAK 6.46.  
  **Function:** supplies the unity of the fruit across multiple abandonments.

- **dhṛti / sadhāryate** — maintenance, sustaining.  
  **Status:** kārikā term clarified by the Bhāṣya.  
  **Function:** the uncontaminated attainment sustains the abandonment included in the fruit.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the fourth and fifth fruit-criteria and the integrated character of the fruit.
- **Voice: Objector** — asks how mundanely obtained abandonments can count as fruits of an uncontaminated path.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — resolves the objection through `miśratva`, unified attainment, scriptural fruit-definitions, and maintenance by uncontaminated attainment.

The text does **not** say that a mundane path becomes supramundane. It says that mundanely obtained abandonments can be incorporated into a fruit whose totality includes seeing-path abandonments and is gathered and sustained by an uncontaminated attainment of separation.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.53 first completes the fivefold criterion of the four principal fruits:

```text
new path-position
    +
integrated abandonments
    +
eight knowledges
    +
sixteen cultivated aspects
        ↓
principal fruit
```

It then resolves an apparent contradiction in the definition of `śrāmaṇya-phala`:

```text
some abandonments first obtained mundanely
        +
uncontaminated abandonments from seeing
        ↓
miśra — integrated together
        ↓
one uncontaminated visaṃyoga-prāpti
        ↓
whole abandonment sustained
        ↓
śrāmaṇya-phala
```

The path therefore preserves the real history of its prior attainments while reorganizing them into a new, uncontaminated unity. Fruit is neither a bare last event nor an undifferentiated heap: it is an achieved totality whose components retain their genesis while being possessed together under one noble determination.
