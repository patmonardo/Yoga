# VAK 6.58 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> gotrāc caturṇāṃ pañcānāṃ phalād dhānir na pūrvakāt /  
> śaikṣānāryāś ca ṣaḍgotrāḥ saṃcāro nāsti darśane // VAK_6.58 //

**Best current translation:**  
For four there is regression from an acquired disposition, and for five regression from the fruit, but not from what was originally theirs. Trainees and non-Āryas also possess the six dispositions; there is no transition of faculties during the path of seeing.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.58.md`](./VAK_6.58.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 374|09–377|11.

This passage greatly expands the compressed kārikā. It first specifies what may be lost as the six Arhat dispositions change, then develops a long dispute over whether Arhatship itself can be lost. The Vaibhāṣika accepts regression from Arhatship for the first five Arhat types; the Sautrāntika denies this and relocates the relevant instability to meditative attainments. The passage closes by extending the six dispositions to trainees and ordinary persons and by excluding transition of faculties from the path of seeing.

## 1. Two Different Objects of Regression

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the Abhidharma classification

The prose asks:

> `kaḥ punareṣāṃ kutaḥ parihīyate / phalāt gotrād vā`

From what does each of these persons regress — from the fruit, or from the disposition (*gotra*)?

The kārikā answers:

> `gotrāc caturṇāṃ pañcānāṃ phalād dhāniḥ`

The Bhāṣya distributes the two possibilities precisely.

### Regression from disposition

> `cetanādharmādīnāṃ caturṇāṃ gotrāt parihāṇiḥ`

The four beginning with the *cetanādharman* may regress from an acquired disposition.

The *parihāṇadharman* is excluded because:

> `na hi parihāṇadharmā punaḥ svagotrāt parihīyate`

One cannot fall below the lowest disposition that is already one's own.

Thus:

```text
parihāṇadharman
    no lower gotra from which to regress

cetanādharman
anurakṣaṇādharman
sthitākampya
prativedhanābhavya
    may regress from an acquired higher gotra

akopyadharman
    excluded from regression
```

### Regression from fruit

> `parihāṇadharmādīnāṃ pañcānāṃ phalāt parihāṇiḥ`

According to the Vaibhāṣika scheme, the first five Arhat types may regress from the Arhat fruit. The unshakeable Arhat is excluded by definition.

This is stronger than the first-pass kārikā summary: the Bhāṣya explicitly distinguishes **loss of an acquired disposition** from **loss of the Arhat fruit**.

## 2. `na pūrvakāt`: What Is Original Cannot Be Lost

The next phrase is:

> `na pūrvakāt`

Vasubandhu explains it in two parallel ways.

First, with regard to disposition:

> `yasya yat prathamagotraṃ sa tasmān na parihīyate`

Whatever is a person's first or original disposition, from that disposition the person does not regress.

Why?

> `śaikṣāśaikṣamārgābhyāṃ dṛḍhīkṛtatvāt`

Because it has been made firm by the paths of the trainee and the beyond-training one.

For the trainee specifically:

> `laukikalokottarābhyāṃ dṛḍhīkṛtatvāt`

the original disposition has been made firm through mundane and supramundane paths.

But:

> `yattu paścāt pratilabdham uttāpanayā tasmāt parihīyate`

what is subsequently obtained by the sharpening of the faculties may be lost.

This gives an important distinction:

```text
original gotra
    repeatedly stabilized by Path
    → not lost

later acquired gotra
    obtained through uttāpana
    → may be lost
```

Second, the same rule applies to fruits:

> `yasya ca yat prathamaṃ phalaṃ sa tasmān na parihīyate`

Whatever is a person's first fruit, from that fruit one does not regress.

Thus:

> `ata eva srotaāpattiphalān nāsti parihāṇiḥ`

There is no regression from the fruit of stream-entry.

This is more precise than saying merely that a regressible Arhat “does not become an ordinary person.” The Bhāṣya identifies a structural floor: what has been first established as fruit is not subsequently undone.

## 3. Regression Does Not Erase the Whole Path-History

The passage then works out how many possible outcomes arise for the different dispositions.

For the *parihāṇadharman*, three possibilities are stated:

1. parinirvāṇa while remaining in that state,
2. transition of faculties (*indriyasaṃcāra*),
3. regression to the trainee state.

For the *cetanādharman*, a fourth becomes possible: return to the *parihāṇadharman* disposition.

For the following three types, the number of possibilities increases successively.

The important principle is not the arithmetic by itself, but the layered architecture it expresses:

```text
Path-history
    establishes a lower determinate achievement

uttāpana
    may generate a stronger acquired disposition

regression
    can undo the acquired strengthening
    without abolishing the original floor
```

Thus mobility is real but bounded.

## 4. Why Is There No Regression from the First Achievements?

The Bhāṣya next asks why the primary or first attainments are irreversible.

The first answer concerns the afflictions abandoned through seeing:

> `darśanaheyānām avastukatvāt`

They are called *avastuka* — without a real basis or object in the relevant sense.

Vasubandhu explains that these afflictions operate through superimposition upon selfhood:

> `satkāyadṛṣṭimūlakatvāt`

They are rooted in the view of a real self or personality.

The imagined self, however, does not exist:

> `sa cātmā nāsti`

This does **not** mean that such afflictions literally have no object whatsoever. The Bhāṣya immediately qualifies:

> `nāsadālambanāḥ / satyālambanatvāt / vitathālambanāstu`

They do take real constituents as objects, but they apprehend them falsely. Self-view superimposes upon rūpa and the other aggregates the unreal character of an agent, experiencer, controller, or self.

By contrast, afflictions abandoned through cultivation — attachment, aversion, conceit, ignorance — arise with respect to actually given agreeable or disagreeable features and are therefore called *savastuka* in this technical contrast.

Hence the crucial distinction is:

```text
darśanaheya kleśa
    real aggregates are present
    but selfhood is falsely superimposed
    → vitathālambana

bhāvanāheya kleśa
    attachment / aversion etc.
    can re-arise toward actually given features
    → savastuka in this contrast
```

The Path of Seeing destroys the self-based error in a way that cannot simply be reconstituted through inadvertence.

## 5. Memory-Lapse Can Reawaken Cultivation-Afflictions, Not Self-View

Vasubandhu gives a phenomenological comparison:

> `smṛtisaṃpramoṣāt kleśa utpadyate ... rajjvām iva sarpasaṃjñā`

Through failure of mindfulness an affliction may arise, as the notion of a snake may arise with regard to a rope.

But self-view and related afflictions require a more determinate cognitive construction and therefore cannot simply reappear in the same inattentive manner after seeing has destroyed them.

Hence:

> `nāsti darśanaheyakleśaprahāṇāt parihāṇiḥ`

There is no regression from the abandonment of the afflictions eliminated by seeing.

This supplies the deeper rationale for the irreversibility of the first noble breakthrough.

## 6. Sautrāntika Objection: There Is No Regression from Arhatship Either

**Voice:** Sautrāntika position

The Bhāṣya now introduces a major disagreement:

> `arhatvād api nāsti parihāṇir iti sautrāntikāḥ`

The Sautrāntikas hold that there is **no regression from Arhatship either**.

They support this by both scripture and reasoning:

> `āgamād yuktitaś ca`

This is an important voice shift. The subsequent argument should not be read as simple Vaibhāṣika doctrine.

## 7. Sautrāntika Scriptural Argument

The Sautrāntika cites the principle that what has been abandoned by noble wisdom is truly abandoned.

It also distinguishes the injunction to vigilance addressed to a trainee from statements concerning the Arhat. The Arhat may still be vulnerable to disturbance in present-life meditative ease through gain, honor, and distraction, but this need not entail loss of Arhatship itself.

A decisive scriptural statement is cited concerning *akopyā cetovimukti*:

> from that unshakeable liberation of mind, no regression is declared in any respect.

The Sautrāntika therefore asks what exactly *sāmayikī vimukti* designates.

## 8. Sautrāntika Reinterpretation of `sāmayikī vimukti`

This is one of the most important corrections in the passage.

The Sautrāntika argues that *sāmayikī kāntā cetovimukti* need not mean Arhatship itself.

Rather:

> `maulo hi dhyānasamādhiḥ samaye saṃmukhībhāvāt sāmayikī vimuktir ity ucyate`

A fundamental dhyāna-concentration is called *sāmayikī vimukti* because it is brought into presence when the appropriate occasion or conditions are present.

It is called *kāntā* because it is repeatedly sought for the sake of pleasant dwelling in this life (*dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāra*).

Thus the Sautrāntika interpretation is:

```text
Arhatship itself
    irreversible

special meditative liberation / dhyāna
    condition-dependent
    may fail to be brought into presence
    may be disturbed
```

On this reading, the instability previously attributed to certain Arhats does **not** imply that the liberating destruction of the defilements itself is undone.

## 9. Sautrāntika Reasoning against Regression from Arhatship

The rational argument is very sharp.

If the Arhat has generated the kind of antidote through which the afflictions have become absolutely incapable of future arising, then:

> how could there subsequently be regression?

But if such an antidote has **not** arisen, then:

> how could the person be one whose influxes are destroyed (*kṣīṇāsrava*) and how could that person be an Arhat?

The dilemma is:

```text
complete antidote arisen
    → kleśas rendered incapable of future arising
    → regression incoherent

complete antidote not arisen
    → not genuinely kṣīṇāsrava
    → not genuinely Arhat
```

This is one of the philosophically strongest moments in the passage.

## 10. Vaibhāṣika Position Retained

After reviewing the scriptural material, the Bhāṣya marks the school difference explicitly:

> `arhattvād api tu parihāṇiṃ varṇayanti vaibhāṣikāḥ`

The **Vaibhāṣikas**, however, teach regression even from Arhatship.

Thus the verse's statement:

> `pañcānāṃ phalād dhāniḥ`

belongs to the Vaibhāṣika Abhidharma classification Vasubandhu is expounding, while the Sautrāntika critique contests the interpretation of that regression.

We should therefore preserve the doctrinal tension:

```text
Vaibhāṣika
    first five Arhat types may regress from Arhat fruit

Sautrāntika
    no regression from Arhatship
    instability belongs to meditative attainments
```

The Bhāṣya does not permit us to erase this difference.

## 11. A More Precise Meaning of the Arhat Types

Within the Sautrāntika discussion, Vasubandhu also clarifies distinctions among seemingly similar labels.

An *aparihāṇadharman* has not reached that condition through later sharpening (*anuttāpanāgata*), whereas an *akopyadharman* has attained it through sharpening (*uttāpanāgata*).

Both do not regress from whatever specific attainment they generate.

The *sthitākampya*, however, does not regress from the state in which he presently stands, yet does not necessarily generate another superior quality without effort.

This makes the six-type system much more developmental than a mere personality typology.

## 12. The Six Dispositions Extend Beyond Arhats

The Bhāṣya returns to the kārikā and asks:

> Are only Arhats sixfold in disposition, or others as well?

The answer is:

> `śaikṣānāryāś ca ṣaḍgotrāḥ`

And the prose states:

> `śaikṣapṛthagjanā apy evaṃ ṣaḍgotrāḥ`

Trainees and ordinary persons (*pṛthagjana*) are likewise divided into six dispositions.

Why?

> `tatpūrvakāṇy eva hy arhatāṃ gotrāṇi`

Because the dispositions of the Arhats are precisely continuations of those earlier dispositions.

This is an important genetic statement:

```text
pṛthagjana disposition
    ↓
śaikṣa disposition
    ↓
Arhat disposition
```

The Arhat taxonomy is therefore not invented only at the end of the Path. It is the completed expression of tendencies already operating earlier.

## 13. No Transition During the Path of Seeing

The final statement is:

> `saṃcāro nāsti darśane`

The Bhāṣya glosses:

> `darśanamārgād anyatrendriyasaṃcāro bhavati`

Transition of faculties occurs outside the path of seeing.

Why not during seeing?

> `prayogāsaṃbhavān na darśanamārge`

Because the preparatory effort required for such transformation is impossible within the path of seeing itself.

The Bhāṣya then notes that such transition may occur while one is still an ordinary person or while one is a *śraddhādhimukta*.

So the final structural distinction is:

```text
indriyasaṃcāra
    requires prayoga
    requires cultivation over time

Path of Seeing
    immediate determinate sequence
    no room for the required preparatory practice
    → no transition of faculties
```

This strongly confirms that *saṃcāra* belongs to cultivation rather than to the instantaneous structure of seeing.

## 14. Mārga Determination

VAK 6.58 gives one of the clearest demonstrations so far that the Kośa's Path architecture contains both irreversible and reversible determinations.

```text
irreversible
    first noble fruit
    destruction of darśanaheya error
    original Path-ground

reversible according to Vaibhāṣika
    later-acquired gotra
    Arhat fruit of first five types

reversible according to Sautrāntika
    not Arhatship itself
    but certain meditative attainments / present-life ease
```

Thus “regression” is not one simple event. Its object must always be specified.

This is the principal Bhāṣya correction to the compressed Kārikā formulation.

## 15. Restrained Organon Contact

The most useful conceptual distinction is between **constitutive attainment** and **subsequent strengthening**.

The first noble breakthrough establishes a determination that is not simply erased by later fluctuation. Later cultivation can add stronger dispositions, and these acquired determinations can, within the Vaibhāṣika scheme, be lost.

```text
constitutive determination
    genetically foundational
    stabilized by Path

acquired intensification
    genuinely new
    yet defeasible
```

The Sautrāntika pushes this further by arguing that genuine Arhatship itself must be constitutively irreversible: if the causal basis of the afflictions has truly been destroyed, their return would contradict the meaning of that destruction.

This is a philosophically important dispute, but the Bhāṣya's own school distinctions must remain primary.

## New Technical Terms

- **gotraparihāṇi** — regression from a spiritual disposition  
  **status:** explicitly analyzed here  
  **function:** distinguishes loss of an acquired type from loss of a fruit

- **phalaparihāṇi** — regression from a noble fruit  
  **status:** central controversy in this passage  
  **function:** accepted for the first five Arhat types by Vaibhāṣika; rejected for Arhatship by Sautrāntika

- **prathamagotra** — one's first or original disposition  
  **status:** Bhāṣya technical determination  
  **function:** marks the disposition from which one does not regress

- **dṛḍhīkṛta** — made firm, stabilized  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term  
  **function:** explains the irreversibility of an original disposition through repeated Path-confirmation

- **avastuka** — without a real basis/object in the technical sense used for afflictions abandoned by seeing  
  **status:** major Abhidharma distinction  
  **function:** explains why self-view and related darśanaheya afflictions cannot re-arise after being destroyed

- **vitathālambana** — falsely apprehending its object  
  **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of *avastuka*  
  **function:** prevents the mistaken inference that self-view literally has no object at all

- **savastuka** — having a real basis/object  
  **status:** contrasted here with *avastuka*  
  **function:** characterizes cultivation-afflictions such as attachment and aversion in this argument

- **smṛtisaṃpramoṣa** — lapse or failure of mindfulness  
  **status:** explanatory term  
  **function:** accounts for re-arising of certain cultivation-afflictions

- **dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāra** — pleasant abiding in the present life  
  **status:** important in the Sautrāntika reinterpretation  
  **function:** identifies meditative ease from which an Arhat may be disturbed without losing Arhatship

- **indriyasaṃcāra** — transition/change of faculties  
  **status:** central Path-development term  
  **function:** transformation from one spiritual disposition to another through cultivation

- **prayoga** — preparatory application or effort  
  **status:** recurrent Path term  
  **function:** required for transition of faculties and therefore incompatible with the immediate path of seeing

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika Abhidharma classification** — four may regress from acquired disposition; five may regress from Arhat fruit.
- **Voice: Sautrāntika** — no one regresses from Arhatship itself; *sāmayikī vimukti* is interpreted as a condition-dependent meditative liberation.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu as expositor** — presents both positions and preserves their arguments rather than collapsing them.
- The Bhāṣya source contains several e-text irregularities; the analysis follows the argumentative structure without silently using them as doctrinal evidence.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.58 limits and differentiates the mobility introduced in 6.57. Four Arhat types may lose a subsequently acquired disposition, while the Vaibhāṣika holds that five may also lose the Arhat fruit. Yet neither one's original disposition nor one's first noble fruit is lost; these have been made firm by the Path. Vasubandhu explains this irreversibility through the special character of the afflictions destroyed by seeing, whose self-based superimpositions cannot simply reappear after noble insight.

The Bhāṣya then records a major Sautrāntika objection: Arhatship itself is irreversible, and the instability described by *sāmayikī vimukti* concerns meditative attainments rather than the destruction of the defilements. Finally, the six dispositions are extended genetically through ordinary persons, trainees, and Arhats, while transition of faculties is excluded from the path of seeing because such transition requires preparatory cultivation. The Path is therefore neither absolutely static nor indefinitely reversible: foundational attainments are secured, later intensifications remain developmental, and the schools disagree over how far regression can reach.
