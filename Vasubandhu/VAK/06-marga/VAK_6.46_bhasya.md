# VAK 6.46 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> laukikenāryavairāgye visaṃyogāptayo dvidhā /  
> lokottareṇa cety eke tyakte kleśāsamanvayāt // VAkK_6.46 //

**Best current translation:**  
When an Ārya gains dispassion by a mundane path, the attainments of separation are twofold. Some say that this is also so when dispassion is gained by the supramundane path, on the ground that otherwise, when the relevant attainment is relinquished, there would be renewed possession of the afflictions.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.46.md`](./VAK_6.46.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 366|17–367|10.

The natural argument extends slightly beyond the formal end of VAK 6.46 into the opening resolution that introduces VAK 6.47. This file therefore preserves the full objection-and-counterexample sequence rather than cutting it mechanically at the verse marker.

Source caution: the reference e-text contains minor instability around `tyakte` / `tyavate` and the opening compound of the next kārikā. No silent emendation is assumed here.

## 1. The Firm Case: Mundane Dispassion of an Ārya

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya first explains the opening pāda:

> `laukikena mārgeṇāryavairāgyaṃ gacchato dvividhā visaṃyogaprāptaya utpadyante laukikyo lokottarāśca`

When an Ārya attains dispassion by a **mundane path**, two kinds of attainment of separation arise:

```text
laukikī visaṃyoga-prāpti
    mundane attainment of separation

lokottarā visaṃyoga-prāpti
    supramundane attainment of separation
```

This is the basic doctrine presupposed by the verse.

The same abandonment is therefore supported by two attainments. The point is not that the kleśa is destroyed twice. Rather, the achieved separation is possessed under two distinct path-determinations.

```text
one abandonment
    ↓
separation attained
    ↓
recognized / possessed in two path registers
        mundane
        supramundane
```

That much is presented straightforwardly.

## 2. The Disputed Extension: What if the Dispassion Was Gained Supramundanely?

The kārikā then says:

> `lokottareṇa cety eke`

**Voice:** A reported doctrinal position (`eke`)

Some maintain that the same twofold attainment also arises when the Ārya gains dispassion by the **supramundane path**.

So the disputed thesis is:

```text
if vairāgya is gained by laukika-mārga
    → mundane + supramundane visaṃyoga-prāpti

if vairāgya is gained by lokottara-mārga
    → also mundane + supramundane visaṃyoga-prāpti
```

The Bhāṣya asks why this second extension should be accepted.

## 3. The Argument from Loss of Path-Attainment

**Voice:** Defender of the `eke` position

The argument turns on what happens when a previously acquired supramundane path-attainment is relinquished.

The Bhāṣya says, in effect: suppose an Ārya attained dispassion from the sphere of nothingness (`ākiṃcanyāyatana`) by the noble path and later underwent a change of faculties (`indriyāṇi saṃcarati`) while relying on dhyāna. In that change, the previously acquired path is relinquished and only the corresponding fruit-path is newly obtained.

If no **mundane attainment of separation** had arisen alongside the supramundane one, then relinquishing the former noble-path acquisition would appear to leave the practitioner without possession of the separation from the higher-level kleśas.

The feared consequence is:

```text
supramundane dispassion attained
    ↓
only supramundane separation-attainment exists
    ↓
change of faculties
    ↓
former path-attainment relinquished
    ↓
no remaining attainment of separation
    ↓
apparent renewed possession of the previously abandoned kleśas
```

This is what the kārikā's final phrase is invoking with:

> `tyakte kleśāsamanvayāt`

The force is not that abandonment itself literally becomes undone at every change of faculty. Rather, the defender argues that without a second, mundane attainment of separation there would be no remaining acquisition to secure the practitioner's non-possession of the abandoned afflictions once the supramundane path-attainment is relinquished.

## 4. Vasubandhu's Counterexample

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / counter-position

Vasubandhu rejects the necessity of that inference.

The next line of the source introduces the counterexample with a case concerning one partly liberated from the summit of existence, and the Bhāṣya explains:

> even without a mundane attainment of separation, relinquishing the supramundane attainment through a change of faculties does **not** entail renewed possession of those afflictions.

This is decisive because bhavāgra is precisely the case established in VAK 6.45 where dispassion can be produced **only supramundanely**. There is no corresponding mundane path of dispassion from the summit of existence.

Yet the practitioner does not simply reacquire the abandoned bhavāgra kleśas when the relevant supramundane path-attainment is relinquished in the technical context of faculty transformation.

Thus:

```text
absence of a mundane visaṃyoga-prāpti
    ≠
necessary renewed possession of the kleśa
```

This directly undercuts the argument offered for the `eke` thesis.

## 5. A Second Counterexample: Rebirth Above the First Dhyāna

Vasubandhu strengthens the point with another case.

A pṛthagjana born above the level of the first dhyāna may relinquish the mundane attainment of separation from desire-realm afflictions that had previously been acquired. Even so, this does not mean that the person thereby once again becomes possessed of those desire-realm kleśas.

The example shows that:

```text
loss of a particular visaṃyoga-prāpti
    does not by itself prove
renewed samanvāgama with the abandoned kleśa
```

Accordingly, the proposed argument is **not probative**.

Vasubandhu concludes:

> `ajñāpakam etat`

—this does not establish the point.

That short verdict is important. The Bhāṣya is not merely cataloguing alternatives; it is evaluating whether the reason given actually proves the doctrinal extension.

## 6. What Exactly Has Been Refuted?

The passage should be read carefully.

The firm doctrine is:

```text
Ārya + mundane dispassion
    → two kinds of separation-attainment
```

The disputed doctrine is:

```text
Ārya + supramundane dispassion
    → necessarily the same two kinds
```

The argument offered for the disputed doctrine is:

```text
otherwise relinquishment of the supramundane attainment
    would entail renewed possession of kleśas
```

Vasubandhu's response is:

```text
No.
There are cases where an attainment is relinquished
without renewed kleśa-possession.
Therefore that reason does not prove the thesis.
```

So the Bhāṣya does **not** allow us to turn `eke` into an unqualified statement of Vasubandhu's own position.

## 7. Separation, Attainment, and Non-Possession

This passage refines a distinction that the kārikā seed only partially captured.

Three things must be kept apart:

```text
1. kleśa-prahāṇa
   abandonment of the affliction

2. visaṃyoga-prāpti
   attainment / acquisition of separation

3. kleśa-asamanvāgama
   non-possession of the affliction
```

The doctrinal dispute arises because one side treats continued non-possession as requiring a corresponding surviving attainment of separation. Vasubandhu's counterexamples show that this inference is too simple.

A practitioner's relation to an abandoned kleśa cannot be reconstructed merely by counting which prāptis are presently retained after every technical transformation of the path continuum.

That is a significant refinement of the path mechanics.

## 8. Logical Determination

The passage is a compact exercise in Abhidharma reasoning by consequence and counterexample.

```text
Thesis:
    supramundane vairāgya also yields twofold visaṃyoga-prāpti

Reason:
    otherwise loss of supramundane attainment
    would restore possession of kleśas

Counterexample 1:
    bhavāgra case
    no mundane separation-attainment
    yet no restored kleśa-possession

Counterexample 2:
    rebirth above first dhyāna
    prior separation-attainment relinquished
    yet no restored desire-realm kleśa-possession

Conclusion:
    the stated reason is ajñāpaka
    non-probative
```

This is exactly the kind of Bhāṣya reasoning our second pass is meant to preserve: definition is not enough; the school claim is tested by consequences and defeated by a counterexample.

## 9. Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.46 is not mainly about producing a new path-stage. It analyzes the **mode of possession of accomplished dispassion**.

The Aśaikṣa-path block is therefore already becoming more subtle than a simple terminal-state doctrine.

The question is now:

```text
When an abandonment has been accomplished,
how is that accomplished separation retained,
classified, and related to subsequent transformations of the continuum?
```

The Bhāṣya's answer is deliberately non-naive. One cannot infer renewed bondage simply from the technical relinquishment of one acquisition.

The liberated or progressively liberated continuum has a history whose achieved determinations are not reducible to the presently manifest path-factor alone.

## 10. Organon Contact

A restrained Organon contact is unusually clear here.

The distinction is between:

```text
achieved determination
    and
present acquisition-form of that determination
```

Vasubandhu refuses an atomistic inference of the form:

```text
this prāpti is gone
    therefore
its entire achieved content is undone
```

The path continuum is genetically articulated. A later transformation can relinquish a particular path-acquisition without returning the practitioner to the prior afflictive determination.

That is a genuine mediation structure:

```text
prior path-operation
    ↓
achieved separation
    ↓
later transformation of path-acquisitions
    ↓
achieved separation need not collapse
```

For our Rational Kośa reading, this is valuable evidence that practical liberation is not represented as a heap of detachable states. The continuum preserves determinate results through transformations whose logical conditions must be analyzed separately.

## 11. New Technical Terms

- **visaṃyoga-prāpti** — attainment / acquisition of separation from an affliction.
  - **status:** previously encountered; made technically decisive here.
  - **function:** distinguishes achieved separation as possessed under mundane or supramundane path-determinations.

- **laukikī visaṃyoga-prāpti** — mundane attainment of separation.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya differentiation.
  - **function:** one of the two attainments arising when an Ārya gains dispassion by a mundane path.

- **lokottarā visaṃyoga-prāpti** — supramundane attainment of separation.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya differentiation.
  - **function:** noble-path acquisition of accomplished separation.

- **indriya-saṃcāra** — change / transformation of faculties.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya operative term in this argument.
  - **function:** supplies the technical case in which a previously acquired path is relinquished and tests whether abandonment would thereby be undone.

- **samanvāgama / asamanvāgama** — possession / non-possession.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary; central here.
  - **function:** distinguishes whether the continuum remains connected with an affliction from whether a particular separation-attainment is presently retained.

- **ajñāpaka** — non-indicative; non-probative; failing to establish the claimed conclusion.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative term.
  - **function:** Vasubandhu's verdict on the reason offered for the `eke` position.

## 12. Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu for the undisputed statement that mundane dispassion of an Ārya gives rise to mundane and supramundane separation-attainments.

**Voice:** reported school position (`eke` / `apare`) for the claim that the same must also occur when dispassion is gained supramundanely.

**Voice:** defender of that position for the argument from change of faculties and feared renewed kleśa-possession.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / counter-position for the bhavāgra and rebirth counterexamples and the judgment `ajñāpakam etat`.

The exact school attribution of the reported alternative is not named in the immediate passage and should therefore remain unspecified.

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation of the last pāda was too compressed if read as a direct assertion:

> `tyakte kleśāsamanvayāt`

The Bhāṣya shows that this phrase gives the **reason advanced by the `eke` position**, not an uncontested doctrinal conclusion.

A more accurate philosophical rendering is therefore:

> When an Ārya gains dispassion by a mundane path, the attainments of separation are twofold. Some say the same is true when dispassion is gained supramundanely, arguing that otherwise relinquishment of the relevant attainment would entail renewed possession of the afflictions.

The Bhāṣya then rejects that reason as non-probative.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.46 analyzes how accomplished dispassion is possessed. When an Ārya gains dispassion through a mundane path, two attainments of separation arise: mundane and supramundane. Some extend this rule to supramundane dispassion as well, arguing that without a parallel mundane attainment, relinquishment of the supramundane path-attainment during a change of faculties would cause renewed possession of the abandoned kleśas.

Vasubandhu answers with counterexamples. At bhavāgra there is no mundane path of dispassion, yet relinquishment of the relevant supramundane attainment does not simply restore the abandoned afflictions. Likewise, a pṛthagjana reborn above the first dhyāna may relinquish a prior mundane separation-attainment without thereby reacquiring desire-realm kleśas. The proposed reason is therefore `ajñāpaka`: it does not establish the disputed thesis.

The verse consequently forces a finer distinction among **abandonment**, **attainment of separation**, and **non-possession of affliction**. The practical continuum cannot be understood as though loss of one acquisition automatically erased the liberation already genetically accomplished through the path.
