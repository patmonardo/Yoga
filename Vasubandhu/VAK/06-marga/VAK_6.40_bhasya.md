# VAK 6.40 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**ūrdhvasrotur abhedena sapta sadgatayo matāḥ |**  
**sadasadvṛttyavṛttibhyāṃ gatāpratyāgateś ca tāḥ || 6.40 ||**

The verse reconciles the detailed Abhidharma classification of non-returners with the sūtra teaching of the **seven satpuruṣa-gatis**, the seven courses of the good person.

## 1. The Problem Is a Reconciliation with Sūtra

Vasubandhu opens with a direct question:

```text
kathaṃ tarhi sūtre
sapta satpuruṣagatayo deśitāḥ /

How then are seven courses of the good person
stated in the sūtra?
```

The difficulty arises because VAK 6.37–6.39 has produced a much more elaborate classification of non-returners. The sūtra, however, gives only seven **satpuruṣa-gatis**.

VAK 6.40 therefore does not simply add another taxonomy. It explains how the finer Abhidharma analysis is related to a coarser canonical enumeration.

```text
fine-grained Abhidharma analysis
    many differentiated non-returner types

sūtra enumeration
    seven satpuruṣa-gatis
```

The relation is one of abstraction rather than contradiction.

## 2. The Upward-Streamer Is Counted Without Internal Division

The first half states:

```text
ūrdhvasrotur abhedena
sapta sadgatayo matāḥ
```

Vasubandhu explains:

```text
tasyābhedanirdeśāt
sapta satpuruṣagatayaḥ sūtre 'bhihitāḥ
```

The sūtra teaches seven because the **ūrdhvasrotas**, the upward-streamer, is stated **without subdivision**.

This is important after the preceding verses, which had divided the upward-streamer into several routes such as:

```text
pluta
ardhapluta
sarvacyuta
Akaniṣṭha-bound
bhavāgra-bound
```

For purposes of the sūtra's sevenfold enumeration, these finer differences are collapsed under the single heading:

```text
ūrdhvasrotas
    upward-streamer
```

Thus one and the same path-field admits different resolutions of description:

```text
sūtra level
    coarser enumeration

Abhidharma level
    finer causal differentiation
```

## 3. What Makes These Specifically Satpuruṣa-Gatis?

Vasubandhu then asks why these courses alone receive this designation rather than all the courses of trainees:

```text
kasmāt punaretā eva satpuruṣagatayo
nānyāḥ śaikṣagatayaḥ /
```

His answer gives two criteria.

### A. Wholesome activity, absence of unwholesome activity

```text
satyeva karmāṇi vṛttiḥ kuśale
asaty avṛttir akuśale
```

For those whose courses these are, activity occurs in the wholesome; there is non-activity in the unwholesome.

The compact kārikā expression:

```text
sat-asat-vṛtti-avṛtti
```

is therefore not primarily a metaphysical contrast between being and non-being. It concerns the practitioner’s operative relation to:

```text
kuśala
    wholesome action

akuśala
    unwholesome action
```

The course is called a **satpuruṣa-gati** because the continuum has become directionally fixed toward wholesome operation and away from unwholesome operation.

### B. Having entered these courses, there is no return

Vasubandhu adds:

```text
etāśca gatīr gatānāṃ
na punaḥ pratyāgatir asti
```

Those who have gone into these courses do not return again.

This supplies the second determination:

```text
entry into the course
    → irreversible direction
    → no return
```

The kārikā's **gatāpratyāgata** therefore expresses a directional asymmetry: once the relevant course has actually been entered, it is not traversed backward.

## 4. The Seven Are Defined by Operation and Irreversibility

The two criteria can be set together:

```text
satpuruṣa-gati
    =
wholesome activity
    +
non-activity in the unwholesome
    +
no return after entry
```

This is more than a title of honor. The designation identifies a transformed causal structure of the practitioner.

The continuum is determined both by:

```text
what it now does
    kuśala-vṛtti

what it no longer does
    akuśala-avṛtti

and where it can no longer go
    pratyāgati absent
```

The path-person is therefore characterized simultaneously through positive operation, negative exclusion, and irreversible direction.

## 5. Other Trainees Can Also Be Called Satpuruṣa — But Only Relatively

An objection immediately follows. The sūtras elsewhere define a **satpuruṣa** more broadly, for example as a trainee endowed with right view.

Vasubandhu concedes:

```text
anyeṣām apy asti paryāyikaṃ satpuruṣatvam
```

Others too possess "good-personhood" in a qualified or derivative sense.

He explains this through:

```text
pañcavidhasya pāpasya
atyantam akaraṇa-saṃvara-pratilambha
```

They have acquired restraint involving complete non-performance of the five kinds of evil, and have for the most part abandoned the unwholesome.

Thus there is a broader, contextual use of **satpuruṣa**.

But Vasubandhu concludes:

```text
yeṣāṃ tu niṣparyāyeṇa
teṣām ihādhikāraḥ
```

The present discussion concerns those who are satpuruṣas **without qualification**, in the strict sense.

This produces an important lexical distinction:

```text
paryāyika satpuruṣatva
    qualified / contextual good-personhood

niṣparyāya satpuruṣatva
    strict / unqualified good-personhood
```

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.40 provides a new kind of compression principle.

The preceding verses produced highly differentiated path-persons through variables such as:

```text
karma
kleśa
indriya
meditative configuration
rebirth trajectory
```

Here Vasubandhu shows that a canonical enumeration may intentionally suppress some of those internal differences:

```text
fine causal differentiation
    ↓ abstraction
ūrdhvasrotas treated as one
    ↓
seven satpuruṣa-gatis
```

The seven courses themselves are then identified not merely by destination but by a threefold operative determination:

```text
positive operation:
    wholesome action occurs

negative operation:
    unwholesome action does not occur

irreversibility:
    having gone, there is no return
```

This is another strong instance in which a path-person is defined through rules governing the continuum rather than through an enduring personal essence.

## New Technical Terms

- **satpuruṣa-gati** — a "course of the good person"; here one of seven strict courses marked by wholesome operation and irreversibility.
  - **status** — newly foregrounded in this passage.
  - **function** — canonical classification of advanced trainee trajectories.

- **abheda-nirdeśa** — statement without subdivision.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya explanatory terminology.
  - **function** — reconciles a coarse sūtra enumeration with finer Abhidharma analysis.

- **paryāyika** — qualified, contextual, or derivative usage.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-only distinction in this local argument.
  - **function** — allows a broader sense of *satpuruṣa* without confusing it with the strict sevenfold classification.

- **niṣparyāya** — unqualified, strict, non-derivative usage.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-only distinction here.
  - **function** — marks the exact sense relevant to the present classification.

- **akaraṇa-saṃvara** — restraint consisting in non-performance.
  - **status** — technical ethical expression.
  - **function** — explains the broader, qualified application of *satpuruṣa* to other trainees.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting the Abhidharma reconciliation of the detailed taxonomy with sūtra terminology.

No major explicit school dispute occurs in this unit. The important caution is semantic: the verse's **sat/asat** should be interpreted through the Bhāṣya's explicit **kuśala/akuśala** contrast, not generalized prematurely into an ontological opposition.

Likewise, **sapta satpuruṣa-gatayaḥ** are not seven newly invented subdivisions of the upward-streamer. The seven arise because the sūtra counts the upward-streamer without the finer subdivisions already developed in VAK 6.37–6.39.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.40 explains why the sūtra speaks of seven courses of the good person despite the more elaborate Abhidharma classification of non-returners.

The reconciliation is:

```text
finer Abhidharma taxonomy
    ↓
upward-streamer counted without subdivision
    ↓
seven satpuruṣa-gatis
```

These seven are strict good-person courses because their practitioners are determined by:

```text
activity in the wholesome
non-activity in the unwholesome
and
no return once the course has been entered
```

Other trainees may also be called *satpuruṣa* in a qualified sense because of acquired restraint and extensive abandonment of the unwholesome. The present verse, however, concerns **niṣparyāya satpuruṣatva**—good-personhood in the strict sense.

The verse therefore contributes two methodological principles at once:

```text
1. one causal field may be described
   at different classificatory resolutions

2. a mature path-person is specified
   by operative and irreversible constraints
   on the continuum
```

VAK 6.40 is thus not another arbitrary enumeration but an explicit account of how canonical compression and Abhidharma differentiation relate to one another.
