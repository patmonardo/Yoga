# VAK 6.27 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**iti ṣoḍaśacitto 'yaṃ satyābhisamayas tridhā |**  
**darśanālambakāryākhyaḥ so 'gradharmaikabhūmikaḥ || 6.27 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 351|07–352|12.

VAK 6.27 gathers the sixteen moments enumerated in 6.25–6.26 into one **satyābhisamaya**, then confronts an important doctrinal question: in what sense can realization be one if the truths are seen successively? Vasubandhu's answer is not to suppress the sequence, but to distinguish three senses of **abhisamaya**.

## 1. One Satyābhisamaya, Sixteen Cognitive Moments

Vasubandhu begins:

> **ity anena krameṇāyaṃ satyānām abhisamayaḥ ṣoḍaśacittako bhavati**

By this sequence, the direct realization of the truths consists of sixteen cognitive moments.

The decisive phrase is:

> **anena krameṇa** — by this order / by this sequence.

Thus the sixteen moments are not an accidental scholastic enumeration added after realization. Their ordered succession constitutes the form in which **satyābhisamaya** occurs.

```text
Four Truths
    ↓
for each truth:
    Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance
    Dharma-Knowledge
    Anvaya-Knowledge-Acceptance
    Anvaya-Knowledge
    ↓
16 successive moments
    ↓
one satyābhisamaya
```

The unity of realization therefore contains determinate internal articulation.

## 2. The Objection: Other Schools Teach a Single Realization

Vasubandhu immediately introduces another doctrinal position:

> **ye tarhi nikāyāntarīyāḥ satyānām ekābhisamayaṃ varṇayanti**

What, then, of members of other schools who teach a single realization of the truths?

His initial response is conciliatory:

> **abhiprāya eṣa draṣṭavyaḥ** — their intention should be understood.

He does not simply dismiss the language of one realization. Instead, he asks what valid meaning such language can have.

This is important methodologically. The disagreement can be resolved only by determining **which sense of abhisamaya** is intended.

## 3. Abhisamaya Is Threefold

The Kārikā states:

> **tridhā / darśanālambakāryākhyaḥ**

Vasubandhu explains three distinct but related senses:

```text
1. darśanābhisamaya
   realization as seeing

2. ālambanābhisamaya
   realization through object-support

3. kāryābhisamaya
   realization through accomplished function
```

These are not three independent realizations. They distinguish different determinations of one path-event.

## 4. Darśanābhisamaya — Realization as Seeing

Vasubandhu defines it:

> **darśanābhisamayo 'nāsravayā prajñayā satyānām**

The realization-as-seeing of the truths is by uncontaminated **prajñā**.

This is the strict cognitive sense of abhisamaya:

```text
anāsrava prajñā
    ↓
direct seeing of a truth
    ↓
darśanābhisamaya
```

Here the operative factor is specifically **prajñā**, discriminative knowing/principle, not the entire associated mental complex indiscriminately.

This gives an exact answer to the question, “What sees?”

> The truths are seen by uncontaminated prajñā.

The Path of Seeing is therefore structurally cognitive, but its cognition is not ordinary conceptual cognition: it is **anāsrava-prajñā**.

## 5. Ālambanābhisamaya — Realization Through Object-Support

Vasubandhu continues:

> **ālambanābhisamayas tatsaṃprayuktair vedanādibhir api**

Object-support realization includes also the factors associated with that prajñā, such as feeling and the other concomitants.

The distinction is precise.

Strictly speaking:

```text
prajñā
    sees / discriminates the truth
```

But the associated mental factors:

```text
vedanā and the other saṃprayukta dharmas
    take the same truth as object-support
```

Therefore they participate in **ālambanābhisamaya**, though they are not thereby all called seeing-prajñā.

This prevents the path-event from being reduced to a single isolated cognitive faculty. One cognitive moment is an associated complex:

```text
uncontaminated prajñā
    = seeing-function

associated caittas
    = share the same ālambana

one cognitive event
    = internally articulated complex
```

## 6. Kāryābhisamaya — Realization Through What Is Accomplished

Vasubandhu then extends the field still further:

> **kāryābhisamayo viprayuktair api śīlajātyādibhiḥ**

Functional realization includes even dissociated factors such as discipline and acquisition/status-related factors.

The point is striking. Something need not itself cognitively see the truth, nor even be a mental factor taking the truth as object, in order to belong to **abhisamaya** in the functional sense.

The realization is also identified through the work accomplished in the practitioner's continuum.

Thus the three levels widen progressively:

```text
darśanābhisamaya
    uncontaminated prajñā itself

ālambanābhisamaya
    prajñā + associated mental factors
    sharing the truth as object

kāryābhisamaya
    includes further factors
    transformed through the realization's efficacy
```

This is considerably richer than treating **abhisamaya** as a synonym for a private intuition.

## 7. Seeing One Truth Performs Work Concerning the Others

Vasubandhu gives the crucial example:

> **duḥkhe hi dṛśyamāne tasya trividho 'bhisamayaḥ samudayādīnāṃ kāryābhisamayaḥ**

When suffering is being seen, there is threefold realization with respect to suffering, while with respect to origin and the remaining truths there is **functional realization**.

Why?

> **prahāṇa-sākṣātkaraṇa-bhāvanāt**

because of:

```text
prahāṇa
    abandonment

sākṣātkaraṇa
    realization / making directly present

bhāvanā
    cultivation
```

These correspond to the classical tasks attached to the truths:

```text
duḥkha
    is to be fully known

samudaya
    is to be abandoned

nirodha
    is to be directly realized

mārga
    is to be cultivated
```

The Bhāṣya therefore establishes an extraordinarily important distinction:

> A truth need not yet be **seen in its own darśanābhisamaya moment** for the realization presently occurring to be already accomplishing work relative to it.

This is one legitimate sense in which the truths can be spoken of as involved in one realization.

## 8. Why the Four Truths Cannot Simply Be Collapsed into One Seeing

Vasubandhu now tests stronger versions of the “single realization” thesis.

Suppose one claims that **darśanābhisamaya itself** occurs for all truths at once.

He rejects this:

> **ayuktaṃ brūyād ākārabhedāt**

That would be unreasonable because the **ākāras differ**.

Each truth is cognized under its own determinate aspect. The difference among suffering, origin, cessation, and path is therefore not eliminable at the level of direct seeing.

```text
duḥkha
    ≠ samudaya
    ≠ nirodha
    ≠ mārga

because:
    their cognitive ākāras differ
```

This is a major methodological statement.

Unity of realization cannot be purchased by abolishing the determinations through which truth is known.

## 9. The Anātman Proposal Also Fails

Another possible attempt at unity would be to claim that all four truths are seen simultaneously simply under the aspect of **anātman**.

Vasubandhu replies that then the truths would not be seen specifically as:

```text
duḥkha
samudaya
nirodha
mārga
```

and this would conflict with scripture.

He cites a passage concerning the Noble disciple who attends to suffering as suffering, origin as origin, and so forth, with an uncontaminated mental orientation and discrimination of dharmas.

The point is exact:

```text
a universal characteristic
    cannot replace
truth-specific determination
```

Even a valid characteristic such as non-self cannot dissolve the distinct cognitive work demanded by each truth.

## 10. Could the Scripture Refer Only to the Path of Cultivation?

An objection proposes that the scriptural differentiation might belong only to **bhāvanāmārga**, not to the initial seeing.

Vasubandhu rejects this:

> **na / yathādarśanaṃ bhāvanāt**

No, because cultivation proceeds **according to how there has been seeing**.

This is an exceptionally important genetic relation:

```text
darśana
    establishes the determinate form
        ↓

bhāvanā
    cultivates according to that seeing
```

Cultivation does not later invent distinctions absent from Seeing. It develops, stabilizes, and repeatedly exercises the articulation first disclosed by direct realization.

The method therefore has temporal depth:

```text
Seeing determines
    what Cultivation can cultivate.
```

## 11. A Defensible Meaning of “One Realization”

Vasubandhu does allow several weaker meanings in which one may speak of a single realization.

### One truth gives mastery concerning the others

If one says that through seeing one truth there is acquisition of capacity or mastery regarding the remaining truths, such a formulation need not be faulty.

The text notes that one would then have to examine whether there can be an interruption or emergence from concentration in between.

### One truth is seen while the functions regarding all four are accomplished

A still clearer formulation is:

> while fully knowing suffering, one abandons origin, directly realizes cessation, and cultivates path.

Vasubandhu accepts that this may be called one realization because, as already explained, when one truth is being seen there can be **kāryābhisamaya** concerning the remaining truths.

Thus:

```text
one darśana-object
    does not imply
one isolated effect

seeing one truth
    can perform work
    across the four-truth system
```

This is the strongest textual basis so far for treating the Path of Seeing as an integrated transformation rather than a mere list of cognitions.

## 12. But Darśanābhisamaya Itself Is Sequential

Vasubandhu finally returns to the strict issue:

> **darśanābhisamayaṃ tu prati sūtre satyānāṃ krameṇābhisamaya ukto lakṣyate**

With respect to realization-as-seeing, scripture indicates the truths are realized **in sequence**.

He appeals to multiple sūtras to defend this successive structure.

Another scriptural argument for one realization is also considered and rejected as proving only a necessary abandonment of doubt/confusion once a truth has been penetrated, not simultaneous direct seeing of all four truths.

The result is carefully balanced:

```text
One realization is valid
    at the level of integrated function
    and certain broader senses of abhisamaya.

One simultaneous darśana of all four truths
    is rejected.

Strict seeing:
    sequential.

Functional efficacy:
    systemic.
```

This distinction is the heart of VAK 6.27.

## 13. Same Bhūmi as the Highest Mundane Dharmas

The verse closes:

> **so 'gradharmaikabhūmikaḥ**

Vasubandhu explains:

> **yadbhūmiko 'gradharmas tadbhūmikāny etāni ṣoḍaśa cittāni**

The sixteen moments belong to the same level as the Highest Mundane Dharmas that immediately preceded them.

Earlier the Highest Mundane Dharmas were said to occur on six possible levels; whichever of those levels supports the preparatory culmination also supports the sixteen-moment realization.

Thus:

```text
bhūmi
    remains continuous

quality of cognition
    changes radically

laukika agradharma
    ↓
anāsrava satyābhisamaya
```

The supramundane breakthrough does not require a change of meditative level at the instant of transition. The transformation is in the status and function of cognition.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.27 reveals the method behind the sixteen-moment architecture.

The Path of Seeing is neither:

```text
sixteen disconnected flashes
```

nor:

```text
one undifferentiated intuition
```

It is:

```text
ONE integrated satyābhisamaya
    whose strict seeing unfolds sequentially
    through SIXTEEN determinate moments,
    while each moment belongs to a wider
    network of object-sharing and accomplished function.
```

The threefold structure can be stated as:

```text
DARŚANA
    what cognitively sees?
    → uncontaminated prajñā

ĀLAMBANA
    what participates through the same object?
    → associated mental factors

KĀRYA
    what is transformed/accomplished by the realization?
    → the broader path-complex and its effects
```

This makes the realization operational.

A single moment does not merely register a proposition. It has:

```text
an object
an intrinsic cognitive function
associated factors
systemic consequences
an ordered successor
```

The user's provisional description of this structure as a **“suffering-consuming automaton”** therefore identifies something genuinely present in the text, provided **automaton** is understood structurally rather than mechanically or reductively.

The Bhāṣya presents a rule-governed transformation in which:

```text
Truth is discriminated
    ↓
its opposed affliction is affected
    ↓
its corresponding task is accomplished
    ↓
the continuum is thereby differently determined
    ↓
the next path-moment arises
```

Most importantly, Vasubandhu's formula:

> **yathādarśanaṃ bhāvanāt** — cultivation is according to seeing

shows that the Path of Seeing is generative. It provides the determinate form that the Path of Cultivation will subsequently enact and stabilize.

This is stronger than an inventory of states. It is a method.

## New Technical Terms

- **satyābhisamaya** — direct realization of the truths.
  - **status:** established in the Kārikā, here extensively differentiated by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** names the integrated sixteen-moment realization-process.

- **darśanābhisamaya** — realization as seeing.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical differentiation of abhisamaya.
  - **function:** strict cognitive realization of a truth by uncontaminated prajñā.

- **ālambanābhisamaya** — realization through object-support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical differentiation.
  - **function:** includes associated mental factors insofar as they share the realized truth as object-support.

- **kāryābhisamaya** — functional realization; realization through accomplished work.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical differentiation.
  - **function:** extends realization to factors and effects transformed by the path even when they do not themselves perform the seeing.

- **anāsrava-prajñā** — uncontaminated discriminative knowing/principle.
  - **status:** decisive Bhāṣya determination.
  - **function:** proper agent/factor of darśanābhisamaya.

- **saṃprayukta** — associated/conjoined.
  - **status:** established Abhidharma relation-term.
  - **function:** characterizes mental factors sharing the same cognitive event and object-support.

- **viprayukta** — dissociated/not conjoined as a mental concomitant.
  - **status:** established Abhidharma category.
  - **function:** marks factors that can nevertheless participate in kāryābhisamaya through the effects of realization.

- **ākārabheda** — difference of cognitive aspect/form.
  - **status:** important argumentative term in this passage.
  - **function:** grounds Vasubandhu's rejection of simultaneous undifferentiated seeing of all four truths.

- **yathādarśanaṃ bhāvanā** — cultivation according to seeing.
  - **status:** compact Bhāṣya methodological principle.
  - **function:** makes direct Seeing genetically prior to and determinative of subsequent cultivation.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, explaining the Sarvāstivāda/Vaibhāṣika sixteen-moment sequence while engaging positions attributed to **nikāyāntarīyas**, members of other schools.

Vasubandhu does not reject every possible meaning of **ekābhisamaya**, “one realization.” He accepts formulations grounded in integrated function or acquisition of mastery, while rejecting simultaneous **darśanābhisamaya** of all four truths because their cognitive aspects differ and because scripture presents their seeing sequentially.

The distinction must therefore be preserved:

```text
eka-abhisamaya
    defensible in functional/systemic senses

simultaneous single darśana of all truths
    rejected
```

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.27 explains why the sixteen-moment Path of Seeing is both one realization and an ordered multiplicity.

- The direct realization of the truths consists of sixteen successive cognitive moments.
- Vasubandhu distinguishes **darśana-**, **ālambana-**, and **kāryābhisamaya**.
- **Darśanābhisamaya** belongs specifically to uncontaminated prajñā that sees a truth.
- **Ālambanābhisamaya** includes associated mental factors sharing that truth as object-support.
- **Kāryābhisamaya** includes the broader effects and factors transformed through realization.
- When one truth is directly seen, functions concerning the other truths can already be accomplished; thus the path is systemically integrated.
- Nevertheless, the four truths cannot be collapsed into one simultaneous act of seeing, because their **ākāras differ** and scripture presents their direct realization sequentially.
- Cultivation occurs **according to seeing** (*yathādarśanaṃ bhāvanāt*), making the Path of Seeing generative of the Path of Cultivation.
- The sixteen moments arise on the same meditative level as the immediately preceding Highest Mundane Dharmas.

The method behind the sixteen moments is therefore becoming explicit: **sequential discrimination in seeing, unified object-participation, and systemic accomplishment of path-functions form one integrated transformation-process.**
