# VAK 6.38 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**sa pluto 'rdha-plutaḥ sarva-cyutaś cānyo bhavāgragaḥ |**  
**ārūpyagaś caturdhānya iha nirvāpako 'paraḥ || 6.38 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 359|15–360|12.

## 1. The Akaniṣṭha-Bound Upward-Streamer Is Threefold

VAK 6.37 established the non-returner called **ūrdhvasrotas**, the “upward-streamer,” and distinguished an Akaniṣṭha-bound trajectory associated with mixed meditation. VAK 6.38 now subdivides that trajectory:

```text
sa pluto 'rdhaplutaḥ sarvacyutaś ca
```

Vasubandhu states that the upward-streamer whose final limit is Akaniṣṭha is threefold according to the distinction of **pluta**, **ardhapluta**, and **sarvacyuta**.

These names describe not three different fruits but three routes through the higher rebirth-field.

## 2. Pluta — the Leaper

The **pluta** is described as follows.

He has mixed the meditations in the present life. After falling away from a meditation and taking delight in the first dhyāna without having fallen from that attainment, he is reborn among the Brahmakāyika gods. Through the force of previous cultivation, he again mixes the fourth dhyāna. Falling from that existence, he is then reborn among the Akaniṣṭhas.

Vasubandhu explains the name:

```text
madhyānimajjanāt plutaḥ
```

He is called “leaper” because he does not sink into or pass through the intermediate stations.

The operative pattern is:

```text
lower form-realm rebirth
    ↓
intermediate stations skipped
    ↓
Akaniṣṭha
```

The defining feature is therefore discontinuous ascent across the available stations.

## 3. Ardhapluta — the Half-Leaper

The **ardhapluta** first arises among the Pure Abodes and then reaches Akaniṣṭha while skipping at least one intermediate station.

Thus:

```text
Pure Abodes
    ↓
one or more intermediate stations bypassed
    ↓
Akaniṣṭha
```

Vasubandhu also notes that rebirth as Mahābrahmā does not occur in this sequence, because that position is associated with a locus of false view and with a unique leadership status.

The important distinction from the pluta is that the half-leaper does enter the higher Pure-Abode sequence, but does not traverse every station.

## 4. Sarvacyuta — the One Who Passes Through All Stations

The **sarvacyuta** proceeds through all the relevant intermediate stations and then enters Akaniṣṭha:

```text
sarvāṇi sthānāntarāṇi saṃcarya
akaniṣṭhān praviśati
```

The term should therefore not be understood as a simple “fall from everything.” In this context it marks the upward-streamer who successively passes through the complete sequence of available stations before reaching Akaniṣṭha.

Vasubandhu adds an important restriction: a non-returner does not produce a second birth in the very same sphere of rebirth. The trajectory is one of **viśeṣagāmitva**, movement toward a superior state.

He says that in this way the practitioner’s non-returning becomes complete:

```text
wherever he has been reborn,
he never again returns below that state
or to that same lower field.
```

This gives the upward-streamer a strictly directional structure.

## 5. Another Upward-Streamer Goes to the Summit of Existence

The verse continues:

```text
anyo bhavāgragaḥ
```

Another upward-streamer has the **summit of existence** (*bhavāgra*) as his final limit.

Vasubandhu contrasts this practitioner with the Akaniṣṭha-bound type. The bhavāgra-bound upward-streamer has **unmixed meditation** (*avyavakīrṇa-dhyāna*). Taking delight successively in the intervening attainments, he passes through all the relevant stations, including the formless attainments, reaches the summit of existence, and there attains final nirvāṇa.

The trajectory is:

```text
unmixed dhyāna
    ↓
successive samāpattis
    ↓
form-realm stations
    ↓
formless attainments
    ↓
bhavāgra
    ↓
parinirvāṇa
```

## 6. Śamatha and Vipaśyanā as Trajectory-Conditions

Vasubandhu then provides an especially important causal distinction:

```text
śamathacarito hyeṣaḥ
pūrvakas tu vipaśyanācaritaḥ
```

The bhavāgra-bound upward-streamer is **śamatha-carita**, one whose disposition or practice is characterized by calm-abiding.

The preceding Akaniṣṭha-bound upward-streamer is **vipaśyanā-carita**, one characterized by insight.

This is stronger than a mere taxonomic distinction. It links contemplative disposition to the subsequent trajectory of the non-returner.

```text
vipaśyanā-carita
    → mixed meditation
    → Akaniṣṭha trajectory

śamatha-carita
    → unmixed meditation
    → bhavāgra trajectory
```

The Bhāṣya therefore explains the destination in part through the practitioner’s cultivated mode of meditation.

## 7. The “Final Limit” Does Not Exclude Earlier Liberation

Vasubandhu remarks that an upward-streamer may in principle attain final nirvāṇa at an intermediate point. Akaniṣṭha and bhavāgra are called the respective **parama**, the furthest possible limits, because there is no higher destination beyond them.

The comparison is with the stream-enterer called “seven-at-most”: the designation gives a maximal trajectory, not a necessity that every intermediate stage must actually occur.

Thus:

```text
Akaniṣṭha / bhavāgra
    = maximal possible terminus
not
    = mandatory final location in every case
```

## 8. Five Form-Realm Non-Returners

After the preceding analysis, Vasubandhu states:

```text
ime tāvat pañca rūpopagā anāgāminaḥ
```

“These, then, are the five non-returners who go to the form realm.”

The five broad types are the forms already established in the sequence beginning with VAK 6.37:

```text
antarā-parinirvāyin
upapadya-parinirvāyin
sābhisaṃskāra-parinirvāyin
anabhisaṃskāra-parinirvāyin
ūrdhvasrotas
```

VAK 6.38 has expanded the internal routes of the fifth type rather than replacing that fivefold classification.

## 9. The Formless-Realm Non-Returner

The verse then states:

```text
ārūpyagaś caturdhānyaḥ
```

There is another non-returner who goes to the formless realm. Vasubandhu explains that this occurs when one already detached from form falls away from the present life and is reborn among the formless attainments.

This type is fourfold according to the modes beginning with **upapadya-parinirvāyin**.

The Bhāṣya’s wording indicates the four forms:

```text
upapadya-parinirvāyin
sābhisaṃskāra-parinirvāyin
anabhisaṃskāra-parinirvāyin
ūrdhvasrotas
```

The intermediate-state type does not appear here, fitting the absence of an intermediate-state form-body in the formless trajectory.

Vasubandhu then counts this as a sixth broad type of non-returner.

## 10. One Who Attains Nirvāṇa Here

Finally:

```text
iha nirvāpako 'paraḥ
```

Another attains final nirvāṇa **here**, in this very life.

Vasubandhu identifies this as:

```text
dṛṣṭadharma-parinirvāyin
```

one who attains final nirvāṇa in the presently experienced existence.

This becomes the seventh broad non-returner type in the present classification.

The sequence is therefore no longer only a map of future rebirth. It explicitly includes the possibility that the remaining path is completed before death.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.38 adds a new layer to the path-person analysis. Once desire-realm return has been eliminated, the remaining continuum is classified by **trajectory through higher meditative worlds** and by the contemplative disposition that conditions that trajectory.

```text
anāgāmin
    ↓
remaining contemplative disposition
    ↓
meditative configuration
    ↓
route through higher existence
    ↓
maximal destination
    ↓
parinirvāṇa
```

The most important structural result is the explicit pairing:

```text
vipaśyanā-carita
    → Akaniṣṭha-bound upward stream

śamatha-carita
    → bhavāgra-bound upward stream
```

The non-returner is therefore not simply classified by “where he goes.” His route is tied to the form of cultivation already operative in the continuum.

The three Akaniṣṭha routes further distinguish the completeness of traversal:

```text
pluta
    skips the intermediate sequence

ardhapluta
    traverses part and skips part

sarvacyuta
    traverses the complete sequence
```

The classification is thus a specification of the manner in which an already transformed path-subject completes the remaining trajectory toward final liberation.

## New Technical Terms

- **pluta** — “leaper”; an Akaniṣṭha-bound upward-streamer who bypasses the intermediate stations.  
  **status:** new in this passage.  
  **function:** route-classification within the upward-streamer.

- **ardhapluta** — “half-leaper”; one who enters the Pure-Abode sequence but skips at least one intermediate station before Akaniṣṭha.  
  **status:** new in this passage.  
  **function:** intermediate route-classification.

- **sarvacyuta** — here, the upward-streamer who passes successively through all relevant stations before Akaniṣṭha.  
  **status:** new in this passage.  
  **function:** complete-route classification; the literal compound should not be allowed to obscure the Bhāṣya’s explicit explanation.

- **bhavāgra** — summit of existence, the highest conditioned mode of existence in the formless sphere.  
  **status:** previously encountered.  
  **function:** maximal destination of one upward-streamer trajectory.

- **śamatha-carita** — one characterized by calm-abiding.  
  **status:** rare/new as explicit path-person qualification here.  
  **function:** causal qualification of the bhavāgra-bound upward-streamer.

- **vipaśyanā-carita** — one characterized by insight.  
  **status:** rare/new as explicit path-person qualification here.  
  **function:** causal qualification of the Akaniṣṭha-bound upward-streamer.

- **dṛṣṭadharma-parinirvāyin** — one attaining final nirvāṇa in this very life.  
  **status:** new in this passage.  
  **function:** seventh broad classification of the non-returner.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, presenting the received Abhidharma classification and giving etymological and causal explanations.

The terms **pluta**, **ardhapluta**, and especially **sarvacyuta** should be translated through the Bhāṣya’s explanations rather than by bare etymology alone.

The śamatha/vipaśyanā distinction is explicit in the text and should be preserved, but it should not yet be expanded into a broader Yoga or Organon theory during this pass.

The statement that the formless-realm type is fourfold is grounded in the Bhāṣya’s phrase *upapadyādi-parinirvāyibhedāt*; the specific fourfold expansion follows the immediately preceding fivefold scheme with the intermediate-state type excluded.

## Determinate Summary

```text
The non-returner’s remaining trajectory is further differentiated.

Akaniṣṭha-bound upward-streamer:
    pluta
        skips intermediate stations

    ardhapluta
        skips some stations

    sarvacyuta
        passes through all relevant stations

Another upward-streamer:
    unmixed meditation
    śamatha-carita
    proceeds through successive attainments
    reaches bhavāgra

The Akaniṣṭha-bound type:
    mixed meditation
    vipaśyanā-carita

Another non-returner:
    goes to the formless realm
    fourfold

Another:
    attains parinirvāṇa here in this very life
    dṛṣṭadharma-parinirvāyin
```

VAK 6.38 shows the path-person increasingly as a determinate causal trajectory: contemplative disposition, meditative configuration, route through higher existence, and final liberation are linked rather than merely listed.
