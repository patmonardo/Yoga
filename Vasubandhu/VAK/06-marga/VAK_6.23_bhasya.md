# VAK 6.23 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**mūrdhalābhī na mūlacchit kṣāntilābhy anapāyagaḥ |**  
**śiṣyagotrān nivartya dve buddhaḥ syāt trīṇy apītaraḥ || 6.23 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 348|01–348|19.

The passage develops two themes: (1) the progressively irreversible consequences of Summits and Acceptance, and (2) the remaining possibility of redirecting spiritual lineage (*gotra*) before certain thresholds are crossed.

## 1. Summits Prevent Severance of the Wholesome Roots

Vasubandhu states:

> **parihīṇo 'pi mūrdhalābhī kuśalamūlāni na samucchinatti**

Even if one who has attained the Summits later regresses, that practitioner no longer severs the wholesome roots.

This is stronger than simple persistence of an attainment. The present possession of the Summit may be lost, yet one destructive possibility has been permanently removed from the continuum.

```text
Summits acquired
    ↓
Summits later lost
    ↓
still impossible:
    complete severance of wholesome roots
```

The Path has therefore become operative at the level of future possibility.

Vasubandhu immediately adds a limit: such a practitioner can still perform grave evil acts and can still go to bad destinies. Summits are therefore a real irreversible threshold, but not yet complete security.

## 2. Acceptance Prevents Bad Destinies

The next threshold is sharper:

> **kṣāntilābhy anapāyagaḥ**

One who has attained Acceptance does not subsequently go to an *apāya*, a bad destiny.

Vasubandhu says this remains true **even if Acceptance itself is later lost**:

> **vihīnāyām api kṣāntau na punar apāyāny āti**

The reason is crucial:

> **tadbhūmikakarmakleśadūrīkaraṇāt**

because the karma and afflictions belonging to those levels have been driven far away.

So Acceptance is not merely a cognition held in the present. It has already altered the causal field supporting future becoming.

## 3. Anutpattidharmatā — Acquisition of Non-Arising

Vasubandhu gives a much more detailed account of what happens at Acceptance:

> **kṣāntilābhād eva ... keṣāñcid anutpattidharmatāṃ pratilabhate**

From the attainment of Acceptance itself, the practitioner acquires **anutpattidharmatā**, the condition that certain future states are no longer capable of arising.

The Bhāṣya lists several classes:

- rebirth in bad destinies;
- egg-born and moisture-born modes of birth;
- birth among non-cognitive beings;
- birth in Uttarakuru;
- birth as Mahābrahmā;
- certain sex-supports such as eunuch, paṇḍaka, and dual-sex support;
- an eighth and later rebirth;
- afflictions abandoned by the Path of Seeing.

The exact distribution depends on the grade of Acceptance: weaker Acceptance excludes bad destinies; the strongest grade excludes the remaining listed possibilities.

This is one of the strongest statements yet of Path Knowledge becoming causally operative:

```text
Truth cognition cultivated
    ↓
Acceptance attained
    ↓
specific future states
become incapable of arising
```

The Path is changing not only what is known, but what can still become actual.

## 4. Three Gotras

Vasubandhu then says the nirvedhabhāgīyas are **trigotrāṇi**, belonging to three spiritual lineages according to the distinction among:

- Śrāvaka,
- Pratyekabuddha,
- Buddha/Bodhisattva orientation.

This introduces a new axis into the preparatory stages. The same formal stages can occur within different trajectories of awakening.

## 5. Redirection from the Śrāvaka Lineage

The verse says:

> **śiṣyagotrān nivartya dve buddhaḥ syāt**

After Heat and Summits have arisen in the Śrāvaka lineage, it remains possible to turn away from that lineage and become oriented toward Buddhahood.

But once **kṣānti** has been attained, Vasubandhu says this possibility no longer exists.

The Bhāṣya reports an explanation that this is because bad destinies have been excluded, while Bodhisattvas may enter bad destinies for the sake of benefiting others. Vasubandhu then gives the more decisive reason:

> **tasyaiva tu gotrasyāvivartyatvād asaṃbhavaḥ**

The impossibility lies in the non-reversibility of that very lineage.

So gotra itself acquires an irreversible structure.

## 6. Redirection toward Pratyekabuddhahood

The final phrase:

> **trīṇy apītaraḥ**

means that after all three earlier preparations—Heat, Summits, and Acceptance—have arisen in the Śrāvaka lineage, redirection toward the “other Buddha,” the Pratyekabuddha, remains possible.

But Vasubandhu adds that a Pratyekabuddha lineage itself cannot be redirected.

Thus the Path is progressively narrowing the range of possible trajectories:

```text
early preparations
    multiple lineages still open

higher preparations
    some redirections closed

fixed lineage
    trajectory irreversible
```

## Operative Path Knowledge

VAK 6.23 gives a particularly concrete definition of what it means for Truth to become operative:

```text
Summits:
    one destructive possibility disappears

Acceptance:
    whole classes of rebirth and affliction
    become incapable of arising

Gotra stabilization:
    whole awakening-trajectories
    become irreversible
```

The progression is therefore not merely epistemic intensity. It is **modal contraction**: the Path progressively excludes futures incompatible with the Truth that has become causally effective.

## New Technical Terms

- **mūlaccheda / kuśalamūlasamuccheda** — severance of the wholesome roots.
  - **status:** earlier doctrinal vocabulary, newly crucial here.
  - **function:** marks the destructive possibility excluded by attaining Summits.

- **anapāyaga** — one who no longer goes to bad destinies.
  - **status:** consequence of Acceptance.
  - **function:** marks a stronger irreversible threshold.

- **anutpattidharmatā** — the condition of being incapable of future arising.
  - **status:** major Bhāṣya technical term in this passage.
  - **function:** explains how Acceptance alters the causal possibility-space of the continuum.

- **gotra** — spiritual lineage, disposition, or trajectory.
  - **status:** becomes explicit here as an axis of the nirvedhabhāgīyas.
  - **function:** differentiates Śrāvaka, Pratyekabuddha, and Buddha/Bodhisattva trajectories.

- **avivartyatva** — non-reversibility.
  - **status:** used to explain why a lineage can cease to be redirectable.
  - **function:** gives the structural ground of irreversible path-direction.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, with one reported explanation marked by **kila** concerning Bodhisattvas entering bad destinies for others' benefit.

The Bhāṣya's stronger internal explanation is lineage non-reversibility (*gotrasyāv ivartyatva*), so the altruistic-rebirth explanation should not be treated as the final argument.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.23 deepens the theory of irreversible Path transformation:

- Attainment of Summits permanently prevents severance of wholesome roots, even after regression.
- Attainment of Acceptance permanently prevents bad destinies, even after loss of the present attainment.
- Acceptance generates *anutpattidharmatā*: whole classes of future states become incapable of arising.
- The nirvedhabhāgīyas occur within three spiritual lineages.
- Early stages still permit redirection among some lineages; higher stages progressively close those possibilities.

The operative criterion is now unmistakable:

> **Truth has become Path Knowledge when its cultivation changes what can still happen.**
