# VAK 6.34 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**akṣīṇabhāvanāheyaḥ phalasthaḥ saptakṛtparaḥ |**  
**tricaturvidhamuktas tu dvitrijanmā kulaṃkulaḥ || 6.34 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 355|22–357|20.

VAK 6.33 established the ninefold grading of afflictions and their antidotal qualities. VAK 6.34 now applies that scale to persons already established in the first fruit, **srota-āpatti**. The Bhāṣya first analyzes the **saptakṛtvaḥparama**, “seven-at-most,” and then the **kulaṃkula**, “family-to-family,” stream-enterer.

## 1. The Stream-Enterer with Cultivation-Removable Afflictions Remaining

Vasubandhu states:

> **yasya hi phalasthasyaiko 'pi bhāvanāheyaḥ prakāro 'prahīṇaḥ sa srotaāpannaḥ**

A practitioner established in the fruit for whom even one grade of what is abandonable by cultivation remains unabandoned is a stream-enterer.

The crucial point is that **stream-entry does not imply completion of the Path of Cultivation**.

```text
Path of Seeing completed
    ↓
first fruit attained
    ↓
bhāvanā-heya may still remain
```

Thus the fruit determines a real attainment while leaving a graded residue for cultivation.

## 2. Saptakṛtvaḥparama — Seven at Most

The Bhāṣya explains the expression carefully:

> **saptajanmāni karotīti saptakṛt**

“Seven-times” means one who undergoes seven births.

But **parama** marks the maximum:

> **saptakṛtvaḥ paramaṃ janmā 'syetyarthaḥ**

The meaning is not that exactly seven births must occur, but that seven is the upper limit.

```text
saptakṛtvaḥparama
    ≠ exactly seven required births

saptakṛtvaḥparama
    = seven births at most
```

This is already an important precision in the classification: the title encodes a **bound**, not a fixed itinerary.

## 3. Why “Stream-Enterer”?

Vasubandhu then etymologizes the designation:

> **nirvāṇasroto hi mārgaḥ**

The Path is the **stream toward Nirvāṇa**.

Because the practitioner has entered, reached, or attained that stream:

> **tadasāv āpanna āgataḥ prāpta iti srotaāpannaḥ**

he is called **srota-āpanna**, one who has entered the stream.

The conceptual structure is therefore:

```text
mārga
    stream flowing toward Nirvāṇa

āpanna
    entered / reached / attained

srota-āpanna
    one who has entered that stream
```

The designation is path-functional, not merely biographical.

## 4. Why Not Call the Eighth Person a Stream-Enterer?

Vasubandhu raises a technical difficulty.

If one is a stream-enterer merely through obtaining the first Path moment, then the **aṣṭamaka**, the person in the initial Path-of-Seeing position, would already count as a stream-enterer.

If one is so called merely through obtaining the first fruit, then even someone already detached from the desire realm could be included without qualification.

The Bhāṣya resolves this by taking the designation in relation to the practitioner who has fully reached the first fruit and the corresponding stream of realization.

It invokes:

```text
pratipannaka-phala-mārga-lābha
    attainment of the path toward the fruit

darśana-bhāvanā-mārga-lābha
    attainment of Seeing and Cultivation paths

sakala-sroto-'bhisamaya
    complete realization of the stream
```

The name therefore belongs to a determinate achievement in the path-process, not simply to the first temporal contact with it.

## 5. The Seven-Life Count

The Bhāṣya now examines how the scriptural “seven times” should be understood.

It states that the stream-enterer may reconnect with up to seven rebirth-existences among humans and likewise among gods, with intermediate existences counted separately in the technical analysis.

Vasubandhu reports the Vaibhāṣika explanation that the expression “seven times” is used because of the common sevenfold characterization:

> **saptakasāmānyāt tu saptakṛtvaḥ**

The discussion then tests this against sūtra wording denying an eighth existence for one endowed with right view.

The important methodological point is that Vasubandhu does not merely repeat the inherited number. He asks:

```text
What exactly is counted?
In which destiny?
Does the limit apply collectively or distributively?
What happens to intermediate existence?
```

This is a good example of the Bhāṣya treating scriptural numerical language as a problem requiring causal and classificatory clarification.

## 6. The Limit Is a Maximum, Not a Necessity

The Bhāṣya explicitly allows that another stream-enterer may attain the end of suffering in fewer than seven cycles:

> **anyo 'pi ca syāt saptakṛtvaḥparamo na tu niyata iti nocyate**

The classification therefore expresses a maximum causal range, not destiny in the fatalistic sense.

```text
first fruit attained
    ↓
remaining cultivation-work
    ↓
up to seven births
    ↓
end of suffering
```

The path-status constrains the future without reducing it to one mandatory sequence.

## 7. What Is “Making an End of Suffering”?

Vasubandhu asks:

> **duḥkhasyāntaṃ karotīti ko duḥkhasyāntaḥ**

What is the “end of suffering” that the stream-enterer makes?

He gives two compatible formulations.

First:

```text
that beyond which
no further suffering occurs
```

That is, suffering without a new rebirth-link:

> **apratisaṃdhikaṃ duḥkhaṃ karoti**

Second:

```text
Nirvāṇa itself
    is the end
```

To “make” Nirvāṇa does not mean producing an unconditioned thing. Vasubandhu explains the idiom through removal of the obstruction to its attainment:

> **tatprāptivibandhāpanayanāt**

Thus:

```text
Nirvāṇa
    is not produced

obstruction to its attainment
    is removed

therefore
    the end of suffering is accomplished
```

This is an important causal clarification.

## 8. Kulaṃkula — Family-to-Family

The verse then introduces the more advanced stream-enterer:

> **tricaturvidhamuktas tu dvitrijanmā kulaṃkulaḥ**

Vasubandhu explains that this same stream-enterer is determined in three ways:

```text
1. kleśa-prahāṇa
   affliction-abandonment

2. indriya
   faculty / antidotal uncontaminated faculty

3. janma
   remaining births
```

The practitioner has abandoned **three or four grades** of the cultivation-removable afflictions.

Correspondingly, the antidotal uncontaminated faculties have been acquired, and only **two or three births** remain.

So the classification is multi-dimensional:

```text
amount abandoned
    ↔ antidotal faculty acquired
    ↔ remaining rebirth-range
```

This is more than naming a person. It correlates inner causal transformation with future existential consequence.

## 9. Why Three or Four Grades, Not Five?

Vasubandhu explicitly asks:

> **kasmān na pañcaprakāraprahāṇāt**

Why is the family-to-family practitioner not defined by abandonment of five grades?

His answer is structural:

> **tatprahāṇe ṣaṣṭhasyāvaśyaṃ prahāṇāt**

If the fifth grade is abandoned, the sixth must necessarily be abandoned as well.

Why?

> **na hi tasyaikaḥ prakāraḥ phalaṃ vighnayituṃ samarthaḥ**

That single remaining grade is not powerful enough to obstruct the next fruit.

Therefore the classification boundary is not arbitrary:

```text
3–4 grades abandoned
    → family-to-family

5th abandoned
    → 6th necessarily falls
    → next fruit-threshold is crossed
```

This is a particularly important example of the ancient grading scheme behaving like a **threshold system** rather than a list of labels.

## 10. Two Kinds of Family-to-Family

Vasubandhu distinguishes:

```text
deva-kulaṃkula
    family-to-family among gods

manuṣya-kulaṃkula
    family-to-family among humans
```

The divine type passes through two or three divine families or communities before parinirvāṇa, whether within the same or another divine class.

The human type similarly passes through two or three human families, whether in the same or another inhabited continent, before parinirvāṇa.

The core classification remains the same; the distinction specifies the domain in which the remaining births occur.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.34 shows the consequence of converting the ninefold grading of VAK 6.33 into a path-person model.

```text
first fruit
    does not erase graded residue

remaining bhāvanā-heya
    determines further cultivation

amount already abandoned
    determines practitioner-type

practitioner-type
    correlates with remaining rebirth-range
```

The causal structure is therefore:

```text
Path attainment
    ↓
residual affliction-profile
    ↓
amount of antidotal acquisition
    ↓
remaining existential range
```

The **saptakṛtvaḥparama** and **kulaṃkula** are not two different Paths. They are two determinate states of the same first-fruit continuum under different degrees of cultivation-progress.

The deepest methodological feature is the threshold at the fifth/sixth grades:

```text
classification is not continuous counting alone

some removals trigger
necessary further transition
```

That is exactly why the Bhāṣya has to argue about the boundaries instead of merely listing them.

## New Technical Terms

- **saptakṛtvaḥparama** — one for whom seven births are the maximum.
  - **status:** established path-person designation, extensively clarified in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** classifies a first-fruit practitioner with cultivation-removable afflictions still remaining and a bounded future rebirth-range.

- **srota-āpanna** — stream-enterer.
  - **status:** established Buddhist technical term, etymologically analyzed here.
  - **function:** one who has entered the Path understood as the stream toward Nirvāṇa.

- **aṣṭamaka** — “eighth person,” technical designation for the practitioner at the initial noble path position.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya classificatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** used to test what exactly qualifies one for the name stream-enterer.

- **sakala-sroto-'bhisamaya** — complete realization of the stream.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression.
  - **function:** helps distinguish full stream-entry status from merely beginning the first noble path moment.

- **kulaṃkula** — family-to-family.
  - **status:** established path-person designation introduced by the Kārikā and expanded by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** classifies a stream-enterer who has abandoned three or four cultivation-removable grades and has only two or three births remaining.

- **deva-kulaṃkula** — divine family-to-family practitioner.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya subdivision.
  - **function:** locates the remaining two or three births within divine existence.

- **manuṣya-kulaṃkula** — human family-to-family practitioner.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya subdivision.
  - **function:** locates the remaining two or three births within human existence.

- **tatprāptivibandha** — obstruction to attainment of that [Nirvāṇa].
  - **status:** Bhāṣya causal vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains how one can “make the end” without producing Nirvāṇa: the obstruction to attainment is removed.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** predominantly Vasubandhu explaining the received Sarvāstivāda classification.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position explicitly marked in the explanation of the sevenfold usage.

**Voice:** Kāśyapīya reading is reported during the scriptural dispute over how “seven times among gods and humans” should be parsed.

The numerical rebirth discussion should therefore not be flattened into a single unqualified formula. The stable doctrinal core is the **maximum** of seven and the correlation between abandonment-grade and remaining rebirth-range; details of scriptural counting receive sectarian analysis.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.34 applies the ninefold cultivation scale to the stream-enterer.

- A first-fruit practitioner with cultivation-removable afflictions still remaining is a **saptakṛtvaḥparama**, one whose further rebirths are bounded by seven at most.
- *Srota-āpanna* is explained as one who has entered the Path, the stream toward Nirvāṇa.
- The seven-life formula expresses a maximum rather than a compulsory itinerary, and the Bhāṣya carefully debates how scriptural counting is to be understood.
- “Making the end of suffering” means removing the obstruction to Nirvāṇa, not producing Nirvāṇa itself.
- A stream-enterer who has abandoned three or four cultivation-removable grades is **kulaṃkula**, family-to-family, with two or three births remaining.
- The practitioner is determined simultaneously by affliction-abandonment, antidotal faculty, and remaining birth-range.
- Five grades do not define a separate family-to-family state because abandonment of the fifth necessarily carries the sixth with it; one remaining grade cannot obstruct the next fruit.

The verse therefore turns the ninefold scale into a causal threshold model of the path-person: **residual affliction, acquired antidote, fruit-position, and remaining existence are systematically correlated.**
