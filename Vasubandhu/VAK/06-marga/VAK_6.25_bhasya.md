# VAK 6.25 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**śrutacintāmayaṃ trīṇi karmāṇy ākṣipyate nṛṣu |**  
**laukikebhyo 'gradharmebhyo dharmakṣāntir anāsravā || 6.25 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 349|12–350|11.

The first half of the verse is glossed before Vasubandhu explicitly closes the digression on the **mokṣabhāgīya** and returns to the sequence of **abhisamaya**. That material was already developed at the end of VAK 6.24. The genuinely new movement of VAK 6.25 begins at Pradhan 349|21–350|11 with the transition from the Highest Mundane Dharmas to the first uncontaminated **dharmajñānakṣānti**.

## 1. Final Recapitulation of the Mokṣabhāgīya

The Bhāṣya first completes the determinations compressed into the opening half of the Kārikā.

The liberation-conducive root is:

> **śrutacintāmayaṃ na bhāvanāmayam**

It is formed through hearing and reflection, not through cultivation in the strict technical sense.

All three actions can belong to it:

> **trīṇi karmāṇi**

but Vasubandhu adds:

> **prādhānyena tu manaskarma** — primarily mental action.

Bodily and verbal actions become liberation-conducive insofar as they are taken up by the resolve for liberation. Even a single gift or a single training rule may project such a root when strengthened by aspiration for liberation.

Its initial projection occurs:

> **manuṣyeṣv eva triṣu dvīpeṣu**

among humans on the three inhabited continents.

Vasubandhu then explicitly closes this side discussion:

> **uktaṃ prasaṅgena mokṣabhāgīyam**

and resumes the subject actually underway:

> **abhisamayakramas tu vaktum ārabdhaḥ** — the sequence of direct realization is what had begun to be explained.

Everything through the **agradharmas**, the Highest Mundane Dharmas, has now been stated. What remains is the breakthrough itself.

## 2. Immediately After the Highest Mundane Dharmas

Vasubandhu glosses the second half of the verse:

> **laukikāgradharmānantaram anāsravā dharmajñānakṣāntir utpadyate**

Immediately after the Highest Mundane Dharmas there arises the uncontaminated **Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance**.

The transition is exact:

```text
laukikāgradharmāḥ
    Highest Mundane Dharmas
    final mundane preparation
        ↓ immediately

dharmajñānakṣānti
    Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance
    anāsrava
    first uncontaminated realization
```

No additional preparatory state is inserted between them.

The important determination is therefore not merely succession but a change of order:

```text
laukika
    mundane
        ↓

anāsrava
    uncontaminated
```

The entire preparatory architecture of 6a terminates here in an immediate arising of a qualitatively different path-moment.

## 3. Its Object: Suffering in the Desire Realm

The Bhāṣya immediately asks:

> **kasminn ālambane** — with what object-support?

The answer, supplied by the beginning of VAK 6.26, is:

> **kāmaduḥkhe**

Its object-support is suffering belonging to the desire realm:

> **kāmāvacaraduḥkham asyā ālambanam**

Hence the full technical designation is:

> **duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti**

```text
Truth:
    duḥkha

Domain:
    kāmāvacara

Mode:
    dharmajñānakṣānti

Status:
    anāsrava
```

This matters because the first supramundane moment is not an indeterminate mystical event. It is a cognitively articulated relation to a determinate Truth and a determinate object-domain.

## 4. Why It Is Called Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance

Vasubandhu gives a compressed explanation of the name:

> **anāsravajñānārthaṃ niḥṣyandena viśeṣaṇam**  
> **karmajñānārthaṃ kṣāntiḥ | puṣpaphalavṛkṣavat**

The syntax and exact force of this explanation are unusually compressed. What is clear from the passage is that **kṣānti** is named in relation to the uncontaminated **jñāna** that follows from it, and Vasubandhu compares their relation to that of flower and fruit on a tree.

The safest local determination is therefore:

```text
kṣānti
    preparatory/causally effective moment within the supramundane sequence
        ↓

jñāna
    its succeeding cognitive fruition

analogy:
    flower → fruit
```

This should prevent two opposite mistakes.

First, **kṣānti** is not merely ordinary psychological patience.

Second, it should not yet be collapsed into the succeeding **jñāna**. The Bhāṣya itself distinguishes them while relating them genetically.

The fuller functional distinction will become explicit in VAK 6.28, where the kṣāntis and jñānas are distributed respectively as uninterrupted and liberation paths. For 6.25 we should retain only what the present passage establishes: the first kṣānti is already uncontaminated and stands in a productive relation to the knowledge that follows.

## 5. Niyāmāvakrānti — Entry into the Determinate Noble Order

Vasubandhu gives this first kṣānti another name:

> **saiva ca niyāmāvakrāntir ity ucyate**

It is called **niyāmāvakrānti**.

He explains:

> **samyaktvaniyāmāvakramaṇāt**

because it is an entry into **samyaktva-niyāma**.

The terms are then defined:

> **samyaktvaṃ nirvāṇam uktaṃ sūtre** — in the sūtra, *samyaktva* is said to be Nirvāṇa.

> **tatra niyamo niyāma ekāntībhāvaḥ** — *niyama/niyāma* is definitiveness, an becoming-one-sided or settled determination.

> **tasyābhigamanam avakramaṇam** — *avakramaṇa* is the entering into or reaching of that.

Thus **niyāmāvakrānti** names more than “having a correct opinion.” It marks a decisive Path-entry:

```text
Nirvāṇa
    samyaktva

its definitive determination
    niyāma

entry into that determination
    avakramaṇa

first uncontaminated kṣānti
    niyāmāvakrānti
```

The preparatory practitioner has approached the Noble Path; with this moment the continuum has actually entered its determinate order.

## 6. The Arising of the Noble Person

The consequence is immediate:

> **tasyāṃ cotpannāyām āryapudgala ucyate**

When this kṣānti has arisen, the practitioner is called an **āryapudgala**, a Noble Person.

This gives 6.25 a particularly sharp boundary:

```text
before dharmajñānakṣānti
    pṛthagjana
    ordinary person

with its arising
    āryapudgala
    Noble Person
```

The Noble Person is therefore not defined here merely by accumulated preparation, ethical seriousness, meditative attainment, or even the Highest Mundane Dharmas. The decisive criterion is entry into the uncontaminated realization itself.

This is one of the clearest places in the chapter where a cognitive Path-event and a transformation of practitioner-status coincide.

## 7. What Eliminates Pṛthagjanatva?

The Bhāṣya now enters a technical doctrinal dispute over the precise causal moment by which **pṛthagjanatva**, ordinary-person status, is displaced.

The primary account states:

> **anāgatayā pṛthagjanatvaṃ vyāvartyate**

and adds that this is the sole function admitted for the kṣānti while it is future:

> **etad eva tasyāḥ kāritram anāgatāyām abhyupagamyate nānyat**

with the analogy:

> **pradīpajātivat** — like the arising/birth of a lamp.

This belongs to the Sarvāstivāda temporal analysis of causal efficacy and should not be expanded beyond what the passage itself establishes. The important doctrinal point is that the transition from ordinary to Noble status is given a precise causal location in relation to the first uncontaminated kṣānti.

### First alternative

Vasubandhu reports:

> **laukikāgradharmair ity apare** — others say it is by the Highest Mundane Dharmas.

An objection is apparent: how could dharmas belonging to the ordinary-person level destroy ordinary-person status?

The response is:

> **na taddharmatvāt tadvirodhitvād adoṣaḥ**

There is no fault, because despite belonging to that level they are opposed to it.

The analogy is vivid:

> **śatruskandhāruḍhatadghātanavat**

like one who mounts the enemy's shoulders and then kills him.

The very summit of the mundane order can therefore, on this view, function as the immediate destroyer of that order.

### Second alternative

A further position is reported:

> **ubhayair ity apare** — others say: by both.

The reason given is their resemblance to the pair:

> **ānantarya-vimukti-mārga-sādharmyāt**

that is, because of similarity to the uninterrupted path and liberation path.

This anticipates terminology that will become explicit in VAK 6.28.

So the Bhāṣya preserves three analyses:

```text
1. first uncontaminated kṣānti
    displaces pṛthagjanatva

2. Highest Mundane Dharmas
    displace pṛthagjanatva

3. both together
    are assigned the transition
    by analogy with ānantarya / vimukti path structure
```

The text here reports the alternatives rather than dissolving the disagreement into a single formula.

## 8. The Path Boundary Is Both Cognitive and Ontological-in-Status

VAK 6.25 does more than introduce the first member of a sixteen-moment list.

The Bhāṣya establishes four simultaneous determinations:

```text
1. temporal determination
   immediately after the Highest Mundane Dharmas

2. cognitive determination
   dharmajñānakṣānti

3. purity determination
   anāsrava

4. practitioner determination
   āryapudgala
```

This gives the first Path-of-Seeing moment a remarkable density.

The practitioner does not first become Noble and then later acquire uncontaminated seeing. Nor is uncontaminated seeing merely another property added to an otherwise unchanged continuum.

Within the Bhāṣya's own path analysis, the arising of this realization **is precisely the event by which the practitioner crosses into Noble status**.

That is the central determination of 6.25.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

The preparatory sequence developed through VAK 6.17–6.24 can now be completed:

```text
śruta + cintā
    Truth understood and reflected upon
        ↓

bhāvanā
    Truth cultivated as capacity
        ↓

uṣmagata → mūrdhan → kṣānti → agradharmāḥ
    mundane penetration progressively stabilized
        ↓

laukikāgradharmāḥ
    final mundane moment
        ↓ immediately

duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti
    first uncontaminated Path moment
        ↓

niyāmāvakrānti
    entry into the determinate Noble order
        ↓

āryapudgala
    Noble Person
```

The decisive philosophical point is now textually secure:

> **The Path of Seeing begins as a transformation in the mode of knowing that simultaneously transforms the status of the knower.**

This is stronger than saying that the practitioner has merely accumulated sufficient information about the Four Truths.

The Truth has passed through hearing, reflection, and cultivation; at **dharmajñānakṣānti** it becomes uncontaminated operative realization.

Yet the Bhāṣya also preserves an important mediation between **kṣānti** and **jñāna**. The first uncontaminated moment is already decisive, but it is not yet identical with the knowledge-moment that follows. The flower–fruit analogy indicates a generated sequence rather than a static identity.

That distinction should be carried forward carefully into VAK 6.26–6.28.

## New Technical Terms

- **dharmajñānakṣānti** — Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance; here specifically the first uncontaminated realization concerning suffering in the desire realm.
  - **status:** fuller Bhāṣya form of the Kārikā's compressed **dharmakṣānti**.
  - **function:** first uncontaminated moment of the realization sequence and entry into Noble status.

- **niyāmāvakrānti** — entry into determinate certainty / entry into the fixed Noble order.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya-only explanatory designation in this passage.
  - **function:** names the first kṣānti as the decisive entrance into **samyaktvaniyāma**.

- **samyaktva** — rightness/correctness; identified here by citation of sūtra with Nirvāṇa.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya doctrinal explanation.
  - **function:** gives the goal-content of the **niyāma** entered by the Noble Path.

- **niyāma / niyama** — definitiveness, settled determination; glossed as **ekāntībhāva**.
  - **status:** central path-transition vocabulary.
  - **function:** characterizes the irreversible/determinate order into which the practitioner enters.

- **avakramaṇa** — entering, stepping into, reaching.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanation of the compound **niyāmāvakrānti**.
  - **function:** expresses the transition from preparation to actual Noble Path-entry.

- **āryapudgala** — Noble Person.
  - **status:** established Buddhist classification, made causally precise here.
  - **function:** practitioner-status beginning with the arising of the first uncontaminated kṣānti.

- **pṛthagjanatva** — ordinary-person status.
  - **status:** important contrast-term in the Bhāṣya discussion.
  - **function:** that which is displaced at the threshold of the Noble Path; the precise causal moment is disputed.

- **kāritra** — activity, causal function.
  - **status:** technical Sarvāstivāda explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** used to specify the function attributed to the future kṣānti in excluding ordinary-person status.

- **ekāntībhāva** — definitiveness, becoming settled in one determinate direction.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss on **niyāma**.
  - **function:** explains the determinacy characteristic of Noble Path-entry.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / main exposition for the arising, object, naming, and **niyāmāvakrānti** character of the first uncontaminated kṣānti.

**Voice:** reported alternatives (`apare`) concerning what precisely displaces **pṛthagjanatva**:

1. the first uncontaminated kṣānti;
2. the Highest Mundane Dharmas;
3. both.

The passage is embedded in Sarvāstivāda temporal-causal theory, especially in the claim concerning the function of the future kṣānti. We should preserve that doctrinal machinery without prematurely converting it into a generalized theory of temporal causation.

The flower–fruit explanation of **kṣānti → jñāna** is compressed in the Sanskrit. Its exact philological force should therefore remain provisional until the following verses supply the explicit functional distinction.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.25 is the actual threshold of the Noble Path.

- The first half completes the account of the **mokṣabhāgīya**: hearing-and-reflection-formed, expressible through the three actions but principally mental, and initially projected in human existence.
- Vasubandhu then explicitly returns to the **abhisamayakrama**, the sequence of realization.
- Immediately after the Highest Mundane Dharmas arises the first uncontaminated **dharmajñānakṣānti**.
- Its object is suffering in the desire realm; its full name is **duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti**.
- Kṣānti is genetically related to the succeeding jñāna; Vasubandhu uses a flower–fruit analogy, but does not identify the two.
- The first kṣānti is also called **niyāmāvakrānti**, entry into the determinate Noble order.
- With its arising the practitioner is called an **āryapudgala**.
- The Bhāṣya records competing accounts of whether ordinary-person status is displaced by the first kṣānti, by the Highest Mundane Dharmas, or by both.

Thus the transition at 6.25 is simultaneously:

```text
mundane → uncontaminated
preparation → realization
pṛthagjana → āryapudgala
approach to the Path → entry into the Path
```

The Path of Seeing therefore begins not as the addition of one more doctrine to the practitioner's knowledge, but as the first uncontaminated realization through which the practitioner becomes Noble.
