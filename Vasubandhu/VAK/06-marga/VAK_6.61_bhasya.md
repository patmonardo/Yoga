# VAK 6.61 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dṛṣṭyāptatāyām ekaikaḥ anāsravāḥ nṛṣu vardhanam /  
> aśaikṣo nava niśritya bhūmīḥ śaikṣas tu ṣaṭ yataḥ // VAK_6.61 //

**Best current translation:**  
In the transition to the condition of the *dṛṣṭiprāpta*, there is one [uninterrupted path] and one [liberation path]. These paths are uncontaminated. The strengthening of the faculties occurs among humans. The one beyond training relies on nine grounds, but the trainee on six—because [of the reason stated next].

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.61.md`](./VAK_6.61.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 378|20–379|12, with the explanation continuing directly into VAK 6.62 at 379|13.

The working e-text reads `dṛṣṭacyāptatāyām` at 378|20; the prose immediately glosses the expression with `dṛṣṭiprāptāyāṃ pratividhyamānāyām`, so the doctrinal sense is secure even if the transmitted pāda is textually awkward.

VAK 6.61 continues the discussion of *indriyasaṃcāra*, transition or strengthening of the faculties. It determines four things: the number of paths required for the transition to the *dṛṣṭiprāpta* type, the uncontaminated character of those paths, the human basis of faculty-transition, and the distinct contemplative grounds available to the *śaikṣa* and *aśaikṣa*.

## 1. Transition to the Dṛṣṭiprāpta Requires One Pair of Paths

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The kārikā says:

> `dṛṣṭyāptatāyām ekaikaḥ`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `dṛṣṭiprāptāyāṃ pratividhyamānāyām eka evānantaryamārgo bhavaty eko vimuktimārgaḥ`

When the condition of the *dṛṣṭiprāpta* is being brought about, there is only one uninterrupted path and one liberation path.

This contrasts directly with VAK 6.60. There, the transformation into the *akopya* disposition required nine uninterrupted paths and nine liberation paths because the weak-faculty disposition had been deeply habituated through both trainee and beyond-training paths.

Here the transition is lighter:

```text
transition to dṛṣṭiprāpta
    ↓
1 ānantarya-mārga
    +
1 vimukti-mārga
```

Thus not every change of spiritual disposition has the same depth or resistance.

## 2. The Preparatory Path Is One in Every Case

The Bhāṣya adds:

> `prayogamārgas tu sarvatraika eva`

The preparatory path is one in every case.

The full structure is therefore:

```text
prayoga-mārga
    one

then, for dṛṣṭiprāpta-transition
    1 ānantarya-mārga
    1 vimukti-mārga

for akopya-transition
    9 ānantarya-mārgas
    9 vimukti-mārgas
```

The difference lies not in the number of preparatory paths but in the number of direct removals and liberated acquisitions required by the depth of the disposition being transformed.

## 3. All These Paths Are Uncontaminated

The kārikā gives:

> `anāsravāḥ`

The Bhāṣya states:

> `na hi sāsraveṇa mārgeṇāryāṇām indriyasaṃcāraḥ`

For Āryas there is no transition of the faculties by means of a contaminated path.

This is decisive. The strengthening under discussion is not ordinary psychological cultivation or merely worldly meditative improvement. It is an alteration internal to noble Path-capacity and therefore requires uncontaminated paths.

```text
worldly / sāsrava path
    ≠
faculty-transition of Āryas

anāsrava path
    ↓
indriyasaṃcāra
```

The Path transforms its own operative capacities through Path-events of the same uncontaminated order.

## 4. Faculty-Transition Occurs Only Among Humans

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `kva punar indriyāṇi vardhante`

Where, then, are the faculties strengthened?

The kārikā answers:

> `nṛṣu vardhanam`

and the prose makes this explicit:

> `manuṣyeṣv evendriyasaṃcāro nānyatra`

The transition of the faculties occurs only among humans, not elsewhere.

The reason supplied is:

> `parihāṇyasaṃbhavāt`

because regression is possible there.

This is subtler than the first-pass Kārikā interpretation. The Bhāṣya does **not** here explain the human basis by invoking suffering, urgency, or a general superiority of human existence. It gives the specific technical reason that the relevant mobility of faculties belongs to a field in which regression is possible.

We should therefore keep the claim narrow:

```text
human basis
    ↓
field in which parihāṇi is possible
    ↓
field in which indriyasaṃcāra occurs
```

The stronger existential interpretation from VAK 6.55 should not be imported automatically into this verse.

## 5. Which Grounds Support Faculty-Transition?

The Bhāṣya next asks:

> `kaḥ punaḥ katamāṃ bhūmi niśrityendriyāṇi saṃcarati`

Which person, relying on which ground, changes or strengthens the faculties?

The kārikā answers first for the one beyond training:

> `aśaikṣo nava niśritya bhūmīḥ`

The Bhāṣya enumerates:

> `anāgamyasyānantaraṃ catvāri dhyānāni trīṇi cārupyāṇi`

That is:

```text
1 anāgamya
+ 1 dhyānāntara
+ 4 dhyānas
+ 3 lower ārūpyas
= 9 grounds
```

The aśaikṣa therefore has nine grounds available as bases for this faculty-transition.

## 6. The Śaikṣa Has Only Six Grounds

The kārikā continues:

> `śaikṣas tu ṣaṭ`

The Bhāṣya glosses:

> `ārupyavarjyaḥ`

excluding the formless attainments.

Thus the trainee has:

```text
anāgamya
+ dhyānāntara
+ four dhyānas
= 6 grounds
```

The contrast is exact:

```text
śaikṣa
    6 grounds
    no ārūpya grounds

aśaikṣa
    9 grounds
    includes 3 lower ārūpya grounds
```

## 7. Why Are the Formless Grounds Excluded for the Śaikṣa?

The kārikā ends with:

> `yataḥ`

“because”—deliberately carrying the explanation into the next verse.

The Bhāṣya immediately continues at VAK 6.62:

> `saviśeṣaṃ phalaṃ tyaktvā phalam āpnoti vardhayan`

and explains that one strengthening the faculties gives up a fruit together with its inferior-faculty path and acquires the corresponding fruit/path of a sharp-faculty disposition.

The specific reason for excluding the formless grounds from the trainee is then stated:

> `na cānāgāmiphalam ārupyabhūmisaṃgṛhītam asti`

There is no Anāgāmin fruit included within an formless ground.

So the six-versus-nine distinction is not an arbitrary hierarchy of spiritual status. It follows from the actual ground on which the relevant trainee fruit exists.

```text
faculty-strengthening
    requires replacing
    the fruit + its weak-faculty path
    with
    the corresponding sharp-faculty fruit/path

Anāgāmin fruit
    is not included in an ārūpya ground

therefore
    śaikṣa faculty-transition
    cannot use the ārūpya grounds
```

This is a much more precise causal explanation than merely saying that an Arhat has a “broader contemplative field.”

## 8. Transition Is Determinate Replacement, Not Mere Addition

The continuation into 6.62 supplies an important clarification for 6.61. Faculty-strengthening does not simply add a sharper capacity on top of an unchanged underlying attainment.

Rather:

```text
weak-faculty fruit/path
    relinquished
        ↓
sharp-faculty fruit/path
    acquired
```

This helps explain why specific grounds matter. The transition is indexed to the fruit/path complex actually possessed on that ground.

Thus *indriyasaṃcāra* is structural conversion within Mārga, not an abstract increase in personal ability.

## 9. Relation to VAK 6.57–6.60

The sequence is now quite coherent:

```text
6.57
some gotras original,
some attained through faculty-sharpening

6.58
mobility has limits;
no transition during darśanamārga

6.59
parihāṇi itself has several senses

6.60
akopya-transition requires 9 + 9 paths
because the weaker gotra is deeply habituated

6.61
other faculty-transition may require only 1 + 1;
all such transition is uncontaminated;
it occurs in humans;
and its available grounds differ by Path-status
```

This block is therefore not simply an Arhat taxonomy. It is an analysis of the controlled plasticity of Path-capacity.

## 10. Restrained Organon Contact

The strongest conceptual point is the relation between **depth of determination** and **work required for transformation**.

```text
lightly established determination
    ↓
1 direct removal + 1 liberated acquisition

deeply habituated determination
    ↓
9 direct removals + 9 liberated acquisitions
```

The Path does not treat every difference as equally transformable. The amount of mediation required corresponds to how deeply the prior form has been stabilized.

A second useful distinction is that the operative field of transformation is not abstractly universal. The same noble Path operates through determinate grounds, and some transformations are impossible on grounds where the corresponding fruit does not exist.

But the Bhāṣya's own categories should remain primary: *indriya*, *saṃcāra*, *ānantaryamārga*, *vimuktimārga*, *anāsrava*, *bhūmi*, and the presence or absence of the relevant fruit.

## New Technical Terms

- **indriyasaṃcāra** — transition or change of the spiritual faculties  
  **status:** active technical term in this Bhāṣya sequence  
  **function:** names transformation from one faculty-disposition to another

- **indriyavardhana** — strengthening/increase of the faculties  
  **status:** expressed by `vardhanam` in the kārikā and glossed through *indriyasaṃcāra*  
  **function:** describes the upward transformation of Path-capacity

- **pratividhyamāna** — being brought about / transformed into  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory usage  
  **function:** describes the production of the *dṛṣṭiprāpta* condition

- **ānantaryamārga** — uninterrupted path  
  **status:** recurrent technical Path term  
  **function:** directly removes the obstruction involved in a faculty-transition

- **vimuktimārga** — path of liberation  
  **status:** recurrent technical Path term  
  **function:** immediately follows the uninterrupted path and possesses the new liberated determination

- **prayogamārga** — preparatory path  
  **status:** Bhāṣya clarification here  
  **function:** single preparatory phase preceding faculty-transition in every case

- **anāsrava** — uncontaminated  
  **status:** recurrent; made decisive here  
  **function:** characterizes all paths that effect faculty-transition for Āryas

- **bhūmi** — contemplative ground  
  **status:** recurrent  
  **function:** specifies the meditative basis from which faculty-transition can occur

- **ārupyavarjya** — excluding the formless attainments  
  **status:** Bhāṣya gloss  
  **function:** limits the śaikṣa to six grounds

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout the surviving explanatory unit.

Two cautions matter:

1. The working e-text has a damaged or awkward reading `dṛṣṭacyāptatāyām`; the prose securely identifies the intended reference as transition into the *dṛṣṭiprāpta* condition.
2. The explanation of `yataḥ` crosses the verse boundary into VAK 6.62. It is necessary to use that continuation to understand why the śaikṣa has only six grounds, but the full analysis of VAK 6.62 should remain for the next unit.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.61 specifies the mechanics and field of faculty-transition. Becoming *dṛṣṭiprāpta* requires one uninterrupted path and one liberation path, with a single preparatory path; all such paths are uncontaminated. Faculty-transition occurs only among humans, where regression is possible. An aśaikṣa can effect the transition relying on nine grounds—*anāgamya*, *dhyānāntara*, the four dhyānas, and the three lower formless attainments—whereas a śaikṣa has only six, excluding the formless grounds. The reason, supplied immediately in the continuation, is structural: faculty-strengthening replaces the existing weak-faculty fruit/path with its sharp-faculty counterpart, and the Anāgāmin fruit is not included in a formless ground. The verse therefore presents spiritual development as a determinate transformation of Path-capacity whose means, depth, existential basis, and available contemplative ground are all precisely constrained.
