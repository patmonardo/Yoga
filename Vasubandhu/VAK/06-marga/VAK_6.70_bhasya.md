# VAK 6.70 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

आदिकर्मिकनिर्वेधभागीयेषु प्रभाविताः ।
भावने दर्शने चैव सप्त वर्गा यथाक्रमम् ॥

*ādikarmika-nirvedhabhāgīyeṣu prabhāvitāḥ /
bhāvane darśane caiva sapta vargā yathākramam //*

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 384|15–385|11.

The prose begins with the question:

*kasyām avasthāyāṃ katame te bodhipakṣyāḥ prabhāvyante?*

“In which stage are which of these Bodhi-aligned factors especially developed?”

This establishes that the problem is not exclusive presence but developmental predominance.

## Literal Prose Translation

In the beginner’s stage, the establishments of recollection are developed for the purpose of discerning body and the rest.

At the stage of Heat, because exertion is bound to the attainment of a special distinction, the correct abandonments are developed.

At the Summits, because one has entered wholesome roots from which there is no falling away, the bases of accomplishment are developed.

At Receptivity, because one has attained governing power such that there is no further falling away, the faculties are developed.

At the Highest Mundane Dharmas, because they cannot be overcome by the afflictions — or because they cannot be overcome by mundane dharmas — the powers are developed.

In the Path of Cultivation, because awakening is near, the factors of awakening are developed.

In the Path of Seeing, because going is especially effective there, the factors of the Path are developed, since that Path goes swiftly.

The seven are stated before the eight only in order to preserve numerical sequence.

The Vaibhāṣikas say: the investigation-of-dharmas factor of awakening is both Bodhi and a factor of Bodhi; right view is both the Path and a factor of the Path.

Others explain the sequence of the Bodhi-aligned factors without breaking the order itself. At first the establishments of recollection are bound to the mind in order to restrain cognitions that disperse among many objects and to remove recollective conceptual positings dependent on craving. Through their strength, exertion is increased, and the mind is correctly applied to accomplish the four tasks: hence the correct abandonments. From the purification of samādhi come the bases of accomplishment. On the support of samādhi, confidence and the other faculties become governing powers with respect to supramundane dharmas. Those same factors, once the occurrence of their opposing factors has been conquered, are powers. In the Path of Seeing are the factors of awakening, because dharma-reality is then seen for the first time. The factors of the Path belong to both Paths. Thus scripture says that cultivation comes to completion in the noble eightfold Path, and likewise that the four establishments of recollection through the seven factors of awakening come to completion in cultivation. Therefore the noble eightfold Path should be accepted in both Seeing and Cultivation.

Thus the sequence is established.

## Course of the Argument

The Bhāṣya gives two related explanations of the sevenfold order.

### 1. Stage-by-stage predominance

```text
beginner
    → 4 smṛtyupasthānas

uṣmagata (Heat)
    → 4 samyakprahāṇas

mūrdhan (Summits)
    → 4 ṛddhipādas

kṣānti (Receptivity)
    → 5 indriyas

laukikāgradharma (Highest Mundane Dharmas)
    → 5 balas

bhāvanāmārga
    → 7 bodhyaṅgas

darśanamārga
    → 8 mārgāṅgas
```

Each group is predominant because a new practical capacity has become decisive at that stage.

### 2. A second account of the same order

Another interpretation preserves the sequence but explains it genetically:

```text
smṛtyupasthāna
    restrains scattered cognition
        ↓
samyakprahāṇa
    intensifies exertion for four tasks
        ↓
ṛddhipāda
    purifies samādhi
        ↓
indriya
    gains governing efficacy toward supramundane dharmas
        ↓
bala
    becomes unopposable after conquering its contrary
        ↓
bodhyaṅga
    accompanies the first seeing of dharma-reality
        ↓
mārgāṅga
    operates in both Seeing and Cultivation
```

This second account is not identical to the first in its placement of the last two groups. It therefore should not be silently harmonized with the Kārikā’s primary distribution.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames the question of developmental predominance and gives the primary explanations attached to each stage.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — `dharmapravicayasaṃbodhyaṅgaṃ bodhir bodhyaṅgaṃ ca samyagdṛṣṭir mārgo mārgāṅgaṃ ca`: investigation of dharmas is both Bodhi and a factor of Bodhi; right view is both Path and a factor of Path.

**Voice: uncertain / alternative masters** — `apare punar...`: an alternative account preserves the order but gives a more explicitly genetic explanation and places bodhyaṅgas in Seeing and mārgāṅgas in both Seeing and Cultivation.

## Prabhāvita: Predominance, Not Exclusivity

The governing verb is *prabhāvyante* / *prabhāvitāḥ*: these groups are especially developed or made predominant at determinate stages.

This continues the principle of VAK 6.69:

```text
principal determination
    ≠ solitary determination
```

A stage receives its characteristic form from one bodhipakṣya group even though other wholesome factors remain operative.

The Path is therefore not a sequence in which one set disappears and another replaces it. It is cumulative differentiation through changing predominance.

## Why Each Group Becomes Principal

### Smṛtyupasthāna — beginner

Its task is *kāyādyupalakṣaṇa*: discerning body, feeling, mind, and dharmas.

The alternative explanation makes the function even sharper: recollection restrains cognition dispersed among many objects and checks conceptual positings bound to craving.

### Samyakprahāṇa — Heat

A special attainment has begun, so exertion becomes decisive. The fourfold task of prevention, abandonment, production, and development requires intensified *vīrya*.

### Ṛddhipāda — Summits

The practitioner has entered wholesome roots characterized as non-declining. Samādhi can therefore become the stable basis of accomplishment.

### Indriya — Receptivity

The same factors now acquire *ādhipatya*, governing efficacy. This fits the earlier account of an indriya as a factor capable of exercising dominance.

### Bala — Highest Mundane Dharmas

The faculties become powers insofar as their opposites can no longer overcome them. The transition is therefore not to different substances but to a new degree of efficacy.

### Bodhyaṅga and Mārgāṅga

In the Kārikā’s primary explanation, bodhyaṅgas characterize Cultivation because Bodhi is near; mārgāṅgas characterize Seeing because the Path moves swiftly there.

The alternative account places bodhyaṅgas in Seeing because dharma-reality is first directly seen there, and extends the eightfold Path over both Seeing and Cultivation on scriptural grounds.

The disagreement concerns distribution, not the reality of the developmental order itself.

## New Technical Terms

- **prabhāvita / prabhāvyante** — especially developed, made predominant at a stage
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās
  - **function:** developmental classification

- **ādikarmikāvasthā** — beginner’s stage
  - **status:** Bhāṣya elaboration of the Kārikā
  - **function:** stage specification

- **aparihāṇīya-kuśalamūla** — wholesome root from which there is no falling away
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** explanation of the Summits

- **ādhipatya** — governing efficacy, dominance
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary
  - **function:** explains why the factors are called faculties at Receptivity

- **vipakṣa** — opposing factor, contrary
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** explains the transition from faculty to power

- **dharmatattva** — reality/truth of dharmas
  - **status:** rare
  - **function:** object first seen in the alternative account of the Path of Seeing

- **ānupūrvī** — ordered succession
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only terminology in this unit
  - **function:** explicit statement that the bodhipakṣya groups possess an ordered developmental sequence

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 6.67–6.69 gave the structural relation:

```text
37 functional names
    → 10 operative dharmas
    → organized through principal factors
```

VAK 6.70 adds the missing developmental dimension:

```text
same operative dharmas
    recur in changing configurations
        ↓
one group becomes predominant
    at a determinate stage
        ↓
the Path acquires a new practical capacity
```

Thus the seven groups are neither seven independent substances nor a flat simultaneous checklist.

They constitute an ordered development of one Path.

The strongest evidence is the alternative account, where one achievement explicitly supplies the condition for the next:

```text
recollection stabilizes the field
→ exertion performs the required work
→ samādhi is purified
→ faculties obtain governing efficacy
→ powers become unopposable
→ dharma-reality is directly seen
→ the noble Path is fulfilled in Seeing and Cultivation
```

This is genuine practical mediation, although the Bhāṣya preserves more than one doctrinal allocation of the final groups.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Kārikā’s *yathākramam* should carry real weight: “in corresponding sequence” or “in ordered succession,” not merely “respectively.”

Likewise *prabhāvitāḥ* should not be rendered as if each group existed only at its assigned stage. “Especially developed,” “predominant,” or “brought to prominence” better preserves the Bhāṣya.

A refined translation is therefore:

> **The seven groups are brought to predominance in ordered succession: in the beginner, in the factors conducive to penetration, in Cultivation, and in Seeing.**

The four nirvedhabhāgīya stages supply four internal positions between beginner and the two noble Paths.

## Light Organon Contact

The importance of 6.70 is that the Concept-structure found in 6.69 is now explicitly developmental.

```text
one Path
    does not remain abstractly identical

its determinations
    change their predominance

those changes
    generate new capacities

new capacities
    condition subsequent determinations
```

This is stronger than a taxonomy and stronger than Substance-with-modes. The unity persists by becoming differently organized through its own operative moments.

But the Bhāṣya also imposes a useful restraint: there is more than one legitimate account of how the final groups are distributed. The genetic unity therefore must not be confused with one rigid external stage table.

## Dream of Philosophy — New Development

VAK 6.70 strengthens the newly identified Kośa model of Practical Concept:

> **A practical whole maintains its identity not by remaining unchanged, but by reorganizing which of its already-operative determinations is principal as it advances through stages.**

The new element here is **developmental predominance**. The Concept is not only internally differentiated and genetically connected; its internal hierarchy changes as the Path develops.

This is a precise form of genetic continuity: later stages do not discard the earlier determinations but reorganize and intensify them.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.70 distributes the seven bodhipakṣya groups across determinate stages of the Path according to predominance. Vasubandhu’s primary explanation gives one allocation; an alternative account preserves the sequence while modifying the placement of the bodhyaṅgas and mārgāṅgas. Both accounts agree that the seven groups form an ordered development rather than a static collection. The same operative dharmas persist, but their principality and efficacy change as the Path advances.
