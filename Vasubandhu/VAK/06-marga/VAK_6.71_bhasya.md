# VAK 6.71 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 6.71**

*anāsravāṇi bodhyaṅga-mārgāṅgāni dvidhetare /
sakalāḥ prathame dhyāne anāgamye prīti-varjitāḥ //*

The factors of Bodhi and the factors of the Path are without outflows; the others are twofold. All thirty-seven are present in the first dhyāna; in the preliminary ground they are present without joy.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 385|12–385|23.

The passage begins from a new question:

*kati bodhipakṣā dharmāḥ sāsravā iti katyanāsravā iti* — how many Bodhi-aligned dharmas are with outflows, and how many are without outflows?

It then turns to a second question:

*kasyāṃ bhūmau kati bodhipakṣāḥ* — in which ground are how many Bodhi-aligned factors present?

## Bhāṣya Translation and Segmentation

### 1. Bodhyaṅgas and mārgāṅgas are anāsrava

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

*anāsravāṇi bodhyaṅga-mārgāṅgāni*

The factors of Bodhi and the factors of the Path are without outflows.

*bhāvanā-darśana-mārgayos tad-vyavasthāpanāt* — because they are established with reference to the Path of Cultivation and the Path of Seeing.

Vasubandhu immediately qualifies the terminology:

*laukikā api hi samyagdṛṣṭy-ādayaḥ santi / te tu nāryamārgaśabdaṃ labhante /*

There are indeed mundane right view and the other corresponding factors, but they do not receive the designation “Noble Path.”

Thus the doctrinal point is not that no mundane analogue of right view, right effort, and so forth can occur. It is that the technical names **bodhyaṅga** and **mārgāṅga**, in this classification, are fixed by their location within the Noble Paths of Cultivation and Seeing.

### 2. The other five groups are twofold

*dvidhetare* — the others are of two kinds.

The remaining five groups may occur in both modes:

- with outflows (*sāsrava*)
- without outflows (*anāsrava*)

These are the establishments of recollection, correct abandonments, bases of accomplishment, faculties, and powers.

Thus purity-status is not determined by the functional name alone for these five groups.

### 3. All thirty-seven occur in the first dhyāna

The Bhāṣya asks:

*kasyāṃ bhūmau kati bodhipakṣāḥ* — in which ground are how many Bodhi-aligned factors?

The answer begins:

*sakalāḥ prathame dhyāne*

All are present in the first dhyāna.

Vasubandhu makes the count explicit:

*sarve saptatriṃśat prathame dhyāne* — all thirty-seven are present in the first dhyāna.

The first dhyāna is therefore the meditative ground in which the complete thirty-sevenfold bodhipakṣya configuration is possible.

### 4. The preliminary ground lacks prīti

*anāgamye prīti-varjitāḥ* — in the preliminary ground they are without joy.

Vasubandhu then asks why joy is absent there:

*kasmād anāgamye prītyabhāvaḥ* — why is there no joy in the preliminary ground?

His explanation is:

*sāmantakānāṃ balavāhanīyatvād adharabhūmi-sāśaṅkatvācca /*

Because the preliminary attainments are laborious / require strong effort, and because they remain apprehensive with respect to the lower ground.

The absence of prīti is therefore not an arbitrary exclusion. It follows from the structural character of the preliminary ground itself: it is not yet the settled, fully favorable meditative situation of the first dhyāna.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 6.71 introduces two new axes of determination for the same bodhipakṣya system.

```text
purity-status
    sāsrava / anāsrava

meditative ground
    where the factor can occur
```

These axes must not be collapsed into one another.

The first distinction is terminological and soteriological:

```text
bodhyaṅga + mārgāṅga
    technically established in Noble Cultivation / Seeing
    therefore anāsrava

other five groups
    may function before or within the Noble Path
    therefore sāsrava or anāsrava
```

The second distinction concerns meditative support:

```text
first dhyāna
    all 37 present

anāgamya
    all except prīti
```

So the same Path-system is now being determined according to both **status** and **ground**.

## New Technical Terms

- **sāsrava / anāsrava** — with outflows / without outflows
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** purity-status distinction

- **āryamārgaśabda** — the designation “Noble Path”
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās
  - **function:** terminological restriction; distinguishes mundane analogues from technical Noble Path factors

- **sāmantaka** — preliminary or neighboring meditative attainment
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** grounds the explanation of why prīti is absent in anāgamya

- **adharabhūmi-sāśaṅkatva** — apprehensiveness with respect to the lower ground
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** explanatory reason for the absence of joy

## Doctrinal Determination

The crucial Bhāṣya point is that **functional resemblance is not sufficient for technical identity**.

There can be mundane right view, right effort, and analogous wholesome factors, but they do not thereby become members of the Noble Eightfold Path in the strict sense.

Thus:

```text
same or similar operative quality
    ≠
same soteriological status
```

This prevents the thirty-sevenfold system from being read as a timeless list of psychological properties. The very same functional determination changes doctrinal status according to its location in the Path.

The second half adds that the completeness of the system also depends on its meditative ground. The first dhyāna supports all thirty-seven; the preliminary ground does not support prīti.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

`anāsravāṇi bodhyaṅga-mārgāṅgāni` should not be translated as though mundane right view, right effort, and related qualities simply do not exist. The Bhāṣya explicitly denies that inference. What is exclusively anāsrava is their status as **bodhyaṅga** and **mārgāṅga** in the strict Noble Path classification.

Likewise, `prīti-varjitāḥ` is not merely a numerical subtraction. Vasubandhu supplies a causal explanation tied to the unstable and effortful character of the preliminary ground.

## Logical Determination

The same operative content is now shown to admit further determinations without losing its identity:

```text
operative dharma
    receives a functional role
    receives a soteriological status
    occurs in a determinate ground
```

This is another move away from substance-style reduction. The truth of a Path factor is not exhausted by identifying its underlying dravya. Its complete determination requires **relation, status, and field of actuality**.

The first dhyāna is especially important here because it is the first ground in which the complete thirty-sevenfold configuration can be actualized at once.

## Organon Contact Point

The result of 6.68–6.71 can now be written:

```text
underlying dharma
    → functional determination
    → principal organization
    → developmental predominance
    → purity-status
    → meditative-ground actuality
```

The Kośa is therefore not reducing the Path to a smaller inventory of substances. It is progressively reconstructing the **conditions under which determinate functions become actual as the Path**.

That is a stronger form of the Practical Concept than a static substance/mode schema: the operative identity is preserved, but its truth requires increasingly concrete relational determinations.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.71 distinguishes purity-status from meditative distribution. The bodhyaṅgas and mārgāṅgas are exclusively anāsrava because those technical designations are fixed to the Noble Paths of Cultivation and Seeing, although mundane analogues may exist. The other five bodhipakṣya groups may be either sāsrava or anāsrava. All thirty-seven factors can occur in the first dhyāna, while the preliminary ground lacks prīti because of its effortful and unstable relation to the lower ground.
