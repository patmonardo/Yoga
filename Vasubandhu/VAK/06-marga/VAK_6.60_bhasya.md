# VAK 6.60 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> mriyate na phalabhraṣṭas tad-akāryaṃ karoti na /  
> vimukty-ānantarya-pathā navākopye 'tisevanāt // VAK_6.60 //

**Best current translation:**  
One who has fallen from the fruit does not die [while remaining fallen], nor does he perform an action incompatible with that fruit. In the transition to the unshakeable disposition there are nine liberation paths and nine uninterrupted paths, because [the former disposition] has been deeply habituated.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.60.md`](./VAK_6.60.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 378|05–378|19.

VAK 6.60 continues the Vaibhāṣika account of regression from Arhatship developed in VAK 6.58–6.59. Vasubandhu now asks what happens to an Arhat who has fallen from the fruit and then turns to the technical problem of transforming the faculties from a regressible disposition into the unshakeable one.

The verse therefore contains two tightly related determinations of the completed Path:

```text
regression
    is bounded by necessary recovery

transformation
    requires a determinate sequence of cultivation
```

## 1. Can an Arhat Who Has Fallen from the Fruit Die in That State?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu reporting the Vaibhāṣika framework under discussion

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `atha yo 'rhatphalāt parihīyate kim asau punar jāyate`

If one falls from the fruit of Arhatship, is that person born again?

The answer is categorical:

> `nāsty etat`

No such thing occurs.

The reason is the first pāda:

> `mriyate na phalabhraṣṭaḥ`

The prose explains:

> `naiva hi kaścit phalāt parihīṇaḥ kālaṃ karoti`

No one who has fallen from the fruit dies while remaining in that fallen condition.

Thus the Vaibhāṣika doctrine of regression does **not** entail that an Arhat can lose Arhatship, die in that condition, and thereby resume ordinary saṃsāric rebirth.

```text
fall from Arhat fruit
    ↓
not death
    ↓
recovery must occur
    ↓
only then can the life-course terminate
```

Regression is therefore bounded from within by the prior attainment.

## 2. Scriptural Support: The Lapse Is Brief

Vasubandhu cites a sūtra passage concerning the noble disciple's lapse of mindfulness:

> `... āryaśrāvakasya smṛtisaṃpramoṣā atha ca punaḥ kṣipram evāntaṃ parikṣayaṃ sapadi saṃgacchanti`

The transmitted wording of the opening of this citation is textually difficult in the e-text, so it should not be silently normalized. Its argumentative force, however, is clear: lapses of mindfulness may occur, but the noble disciple rapidly reaches the end again.

The Bhāṣya then adds:

> `anyathā hy anāśvāsikaṃ brahmacaryaṃ syāt`

Otherwise the holy life would provide no assurance.

This is an important doctrinal limit. If loss of Arhatship could remain unresolved until death, the liberating Path would fail to provide the security its attainment is supposed to establish.

```text
possible lapse
    ≠
indefinite loss

possible regression
    ≠
renewed saṃsāric destiny
```

## 3. The Fallen Arhat Does Not Perform What Is Incompatible with the Fruit

The second pāda states:

> `tad-akāryaṃ karoti na`

The prose explains:

> `parihīṇo 'pi saṃs tatphala-viruddhāṃ kriyāṃ na karoti`

Even while fallen, he does not perform an action opposed to that fruit.

This is stronger than the claim that he merely remembers his previous status. The prior attainment continues to constrain what actions are genuinely possible for him.

```text
fruit presently lost
    but
prior transformation remains causally operative
    therefore
fruit-opposed conduct does not occur
```

The Bhāṣya illustrates this with:

> `śūrapraskhalanāpatanavat`

Like the stumbling and falling of a hero.

The image is exact. A hero may stumble and fall, but the fall does not erase the constitution by virtue of which he is a hero. In the same way, regression is a disturbance within an attained structure, not reversion to the pre-Path condition.

## 4. Bounded Regression

VAK 6.58 already stated:

> `na pūrvakāt`

There is no regression from the earlier fruit.

VAK 6.60 now adds two further limits:

```text
phala-parihāṇi
    ↓
no death while fallen
    ↓
no fruit-incompatible action
    ↓
recovery remains structurally required
```

The completed Path therefore has what might be called doctrinal memory: even according to the Vaibhāṣika theory that allows loss of Arhatship, the person is not restored to the condition of an ordinary worldling.

The Bhāṣya does not say that the fruit was never genuinely lost. Rather, it constrains the consequences of that loss.

## 5. Transition of the Faculties

The second half of the verse shifts from regression to transformation.

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `athendriyāṇi saṃcaratāṃ katy ānantarya-vimukti-mārgā bhavanti`

For those whose faculties are undergoing transition, how many uninterrupted paths and liberation paths are there?

The answer is:

> `vimuktyānantaryapathā navākopye`

In the transition to the unshakeable disposition there are nine uninterrupted paths and nine liberation paths.

The prose specifies the case:

> `akopyagotre pratividhyamāne prativedhabhāvanābhavyasya navānantaryamārgā vimuktimārgāś ca bhavanti`

For the *prativedhanābhavya* practitioner who is being transformed into the unshakeable disposition, there are nine uninterrupted paths and nine liberation paths.

Vasubandhu compares this explicitly with the acquisition of Arhatship:

> `yathārhattvaṃ prāpnuvataḥ`

It proceeds as in the case of one attaining Arhatship.

Thus the change of disposition is not an instantaneous relabeling. It reproduces a full graded Path-structure:

```text
9 ānantarya-mārgas
    each directly overcoming a corresponding obstacle

9 vimukti-mārgas
    each establishing the liberation consequent upon that overcoming
```

## 6. Why Are Nine Pairs Required?

The kārikā answers:

> `atisevanāt`

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `tasya mṛdvindriyagotraṃ bhavatīti nālpena vyāvarttayituṃ śakyate`

Because his disposition is one of weak faculties, it cannot be turned away by a small amount [of cultivation].

The reason follows:

> `śaikṣāśaikṣamārgābhyāṃ dṛḍhīkṛtatvāt`

It has been made firm by both the Path of the trainee and the Path beyond training.

This is the exact force of *atisevanā* in context. The weaker-faculty disposition has not merely existed for a long time in a vague sense; it has been repeatedly exercised and strengthened throughout both major phases of noble development.

```text
mṛdvindriya disposition
    ↓
repeatedly exercised during śaikṣa Path
    +
repeatedly exercised during aśaikṣa Path
    ↓
firmly habituated
    ↓
not removable by slight cultivation
    ↓
requires nine uninterrupted + nine liberation paths
```

Thus repeated cultivation cuts both ways. Practice can strengthen the existing configuration, but further practice can also transform it.

## 7. Gotra Is Developmental, Not an Immutable Essence

VAK 6.57 already showed that some are of the unshakeable disposition from the beginning while others arrive there through *uttāpana*, the sharpening of the faculties.

VAK 6.60 now supplies the mechanism of the latter transformation.

The sequence is:

```text
inherited / established disposition
    ↓
long habituation
    ↓
firm faculty-configuration
    ↓
intensive counter-cultivation
    ↓
nine graded transformations
    ↓
akopya-gotra
```

This confirms that *gotra* in this section should not be treated as an unalterable metaphysical essence of a person. It names a determinate capacity-profile that can be stabilized, weakened, or transformed through Path-practice.

## 8. The Path Both Preserves and Transforms

The two halves of VAK 6.60 belong together more closely than they initially appear.

The first shows that a previously generated determination of the Path continues to constrain the practitioner even through regression.

The second shows that a deeply established disposition can nevertheless be transformed through sufficiently strong cultivation.

```text
Path as preservation
    prior attainment constrains later possibility

Path as transformation
    prior disposition is not absolutely fixed
```

The Path therefore possesses both continuity and plasticity.

It neither permits unrestricted collapse nor freezes the practitioner into an immutable spiritual type.

## 9. Translation Consequence

The first-pass translation was substantially correct but *atisevanāt* can now be rendered more determinately.

The Bhāṣya does not merely mean “through repeated cultivation” as a positive description of the new practice. It explains why nine pairs are necessary: the weak-faculty disposition has itself been **deeply habituated and made firm** through both the trainee and beyond-training paths.

A stabilized translation is therefore:

> **One who has fallen from the fruit does not die [while remaining fallen], nor does he perform an action incompatible with that fruit. In the transition to the unshakeable disposition there are nine liberation paths and nine uninterrupted paths, because [the former weak-faculty disposition] has been deeply habituated.**

## 10. Restrained Organon Contact

The strongest conceptual result is the distinction between **loss of an attained determination** and **loss of the structure generated by its attainment**.

The Vaibhāṣika can therefore maintain both:

```text
Arhat fruit can be lost

and

the practitioner does not return to the old field of possibilities
```

The hero's stumble is an unusually good image for this structure. The realized determination may fail at the level of present possession while its mediation remains effective in the constitution of the practitioner.

The second half adds the converse:

```text
what has become firmly constituted
    is difficult to transform
but
    is not absolutely immutable
```

For the later Organon pass, this is potentially useful as a concrete model of generated necessity: mediation produces a structure that persists through disturbance while remaining itself susceptible to higher mediation.

At the Bhāṣya stage, however, the operative categories remain *phala-parihāṇi*, action compatible or incompatible with the fruit, *indriya-saṃcāra*, and the ninefold uninterrupted/liberation sequence.

## New Technical Terms

- **phalabhraṣṭa** — one fallen from a fruit  
  **status:** kārikā term technically elaborated here  
  **function:** names the Arhat considered during temporary regression from the fruit

- **kālaṃ karoti** — dies; reaches the end of the present life  
  **status:** standard Buddhist idiom  
  **function:** marks the limit placed upon regression: the fallen Arhat does not die while remaining fallen

- **smṛtisaṃpramoṣa** — lapse or loss of mindfulness  
  **status:** appears in the cited sūtra  
  **function:** scriptural model for a disturbance that is rapidly overcome

- **anāśvāsika** — providing no assurance or security  
  **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative term  
  **function:** explains why indefinite regression would undermine the meaning of the holy life

- **phalaviruddha-kriyā** — action opposed or incompatible with the fruit  
  **status:** Bhāṣya gloss on *tad-akārya*  
  **function:** marks conduct impossible even for one temporarily fallen from the fruit

- **śūrapraskhalanāpatanavat** — like the stumbling and falling of a hero  
  **status:** Bhāṣya simile  
  **function:** illustrates bounded regression without destruction of the attained constitution

- **indriyasaṃcāra** — transition or transformation of the faculties  
  **status:** technical term governing this and following verses  
  **function:** names movement from one faculty/disposition configuration to another

- **atisevanā** — deep or repeated habituation  
  **status:** kārikā term clarified by the Bhāṣya  
  **function:** explains why the weak-faculty disposition requires a full ninefold sequence to transform

- **mṛdvindriya-gotra** — disposition characterized by weak faculties  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression  
  **function:** identifies the entrenched configuration transformed on the way to unshakeability

- **prativedhanābhavya** — one capable of further penetration  
  **status:** previously encountered among the Arhat types  
  **function:** practitioner specified here as capable of transformation into the unshakeable disposition

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

The first half continues within the Vaibhāṣika theory that an Arhat can regress from the fruit, a position contested by the Sautrāntika argument presented in VAK 6.58–6.59. The present verse should therefore not be universalized as Vasubandhu's unqualified final position on Arhat regression.

What the passage establishes internally to the Vaibhāṣika account is that such regression is strictly bounded: no death while fallen, no action contrary to the fruit, and necessary restoration of the Path's effective determination.

The second half, concerning transformation of faculties by nine uninterrupted and nine liberation paths, belongs to the technical architecture of *indriyasaṃcāra* and is not dependent upon interpreting *gotra* as an immutable essence.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.60 places firm limits on the Vaibhāṣika doctrine of regression from Arhatship. One who has fallen from the fruit does not die while remaining fallen and does not perform an action incompatible with the fruit; Vasubandhu compares the event to a hero stumbling and falling. Regression therefore interrupts present possession without erasing the deeper transformation effected by the Path. The verse then explains the transition to the unshakeable disposition: because the weak-faculty configuration has been deeply habituated and made firm through both the trainee and beyond-training paths, it cannot be transformed by slight cultivation. The practitioner must traverse nine uninterrupted paths and nine liberation paths, as in the graded attainment of Arhatship itself. The completed Path thus exhibits both structural persistence and genuine transformability.
