# VAK 6.30 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**yāvat pañca-prakāra-ghnau dvitīye 'rvāṅ nava-kṣayāt |**  
**kāmād viraktāv ūrdhvaṃ vā tṛtīya-pratipannakau || 6.30 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 353|23–354|08.

VAK 6.30 continues the classification of the two practitioners introduced in VAK 6.29:

- **śraddhānusārin** — Faith-Follower;
- **dharmānusārin** — Dharma-Follower.

The new variable is not faculty but **prior abandonment through the mundane path**. Vasubandhu now sorts the same two Path-of-Seeing practitioners according to how many desire-realm cultivation-abandonable grades had already been eliminated before entry into the Noble Path.

## 1. Up to Five Grades Destroyed — Still Candidates for the First Fruit

The first phrase is:

> **yāvat pañcaprakāraghnau**

Vasubandhu glosses:

> **yadi pūrvaṃ laukikena mārgeṇa kāmāvacarāṇāṃ bhāvanāheyānāṃ yāvat pañca prakārāḥ prahīṇā bhavanti | tathaiva prathamaphalapratipannakāv ucyete**

If, before the Path of Seeing, as many as five grades of the desire-realm defilements abandonable by cultivation have been abandoned by the mundane path, the two practitioners are **still called candidates for the first fruit**.

This is an important precision.

```text
0 grades previously abandoned
    → first-fruit candidate

1–5 grades previously abandoned
    → still first-fruit candidate
```

The first fruit remains:

> **srotaāpattiphala** — stream-entry.

Thus partial mundane abandonment up through the fifth grade does not yet change the Noble fruit-status toward which the Seeing sequence is directed.

## 2. Six, Seven, or Eight Grades Destroyed — Candidates for the Second Fruit

The next phrase is:

> **dvitīye 'rvāṅ navakṣayāt**

Vasubandhu explains:

> **yadi tayos tasmāt pareṇa ṣaṭ saptāṣṭau vā prakārāḥ pūrvaprahīṇā bhavanti | dvau tau dvitīyaphalapratipannakāv ucyete**

If six, seven, or eight grades have previously been abandoned, the two become candidates for the **second fruit**.

Vasubandhu identifies it explicitly:

> **katamac ca dvitīyam | sakṛdāgāmiphalam**

The second fruit is the fruit of the Once-Returner.

The exact range is therefore:

```text
6 grades previously abandoned
7 grades previously abandoned
8 grades previously abandoned
    ↓
second-fruit candidate
    ↓
sakṛdāgāmi
```

The phrase **arvāk navakṣayāt** marks the upper boundary: this classification holds only **short of destruction of the ninth grade**.

## 3. Destruction of the Ninth Grade — Candidates for the Third Fruit

The second half states:

> **kāmād viraktāv ūrdhvaṃ vā tṛtīyapratipannakau**

Vasubandhu glosses:

> **yadi punar navamasyāpi prakārasya prahāṇāt kāmadhātor vītarāgau bhavata ūrdhvaṃ vā yāvad ākiñcanyāyatanāt tau tṛtīyapratipannakāv ucyete**

When the ninth grade has also been abandoned, the practitioner is **vītarāga with respect to the desire realm**—detached from kāmadhātu.

At that point the two practitioners are candidates for the **third fruit**.

Vasubandhu names it:

> **katamac ca tṛtīyam | anāgāmiphalam**

The third fruit is the fruit of the Non-Returner.

So:

```text
9 desire-realm grades abandoned
    ↓
kāmadhātu-vairāgya
    ↓
third-fruit candidate
    ↓
anāgāmi
```

## 4. Detachment May Extend Above the Desire Realm

The phrase:

> **ūrdhvaṃ vā**

extends the same classification upward.

Vasubandhu specifies:

> **yāvad ākiñcanyāyatanāt**

Prior detachment may extend from the desire realm through higher levels as far as the Sphere of Nothingness.

Even then, during the fifteen moments of the Path of Seeing, the practitioner is still classified as a candidate for the **third fruit**, not as an arhat-candidate from the outset.

That restriction will become important in VAK 6.31, where Vasubandhu explains why arhatship cannot be attained immediately through the Path of Seeing.

## 5. The Exact Classification Matrix

VAK 6.29 and 6.30 together now yield a precise table:

```text
prior mundane abandonment
of desire-realm bhāvanā-heya grades

0–5
    → first-fruit candidate
    → srotaāpatti

6–8
    → second-fruit candidate
    → sakṛdāgāmi

9
    → third-fruit candidate
    → anāgāmi

9 + higher-level detachment
    → still third-fruit candidate
    → anāgāmi
```

This is the first fully explicit demonstration that the **same fifteen-moment Path of Seeing** can lead toward different fruits depending on the condition of the continuum that enters it.

## 6. The Path Does Not Reset the Practitioner

The Bhāṣya makes clear that prior mundane cultivation is not erased when the Noble Path begins.

The practitioner enters Seeing with a determinate inherited state:

```text
faculty
    gentle / sharp

prior abandonment
    0–5 / 6–8 / 9+

same Path of Seeing
    fifteen moments
```

The Noble Path therefore does not operate on an abstractly fresh subject.

It takes up a continuum that already contains:

- a determinate faculty-strength;
- a determinate history of mundane abandonment;
- a determinate current relation to desire-realm attachment.

The resulting **pratipannaka-status** is computed from that prior state.

## 7. Path-Process to Path-Person, Continued

VAK 6.28 defined the functional mechanics of the Path:

```text
kṣānti
    ānantaryamārga

jñāna
    vimuktimārga
```

VAK 6.29 introduced the path-person according to faculty:

```text
mṛdu-indriya
    → śraddhānusārin

tīkṣṇa-indriya
    → dharmānusārin
```

VAK 6.30 now adds historical state:

```text
prior abandonment
    → determines fruit-candidacy
```

Thus the classification of the path-person is becoming multidimensional:

```text
PathPerson {
    faculty
    priorAbandonment
    currentPathMoment
    targetFruit
}
```

This is not a digression away from the Path. It is the text's attempt to specify how the universal Path-process becomes instantiated in a concrete continuum.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

The sequence now has two layers.

### Universal operative structure

```text
same four truths
same fifteen Seeing moments
same kṣānti/jñāna machinery
```

### Variable practitioner-state

```text
faculty
    determines follower-type

prior abandonment
    determines target fruit
```

The result is:

```text
one Path
    ×
different prepared continua
    ↓
different Noble trajectories
```

VAK 6.30 therefore shows that direct Seeing is **state-sensitive** without becoming arbitrary.

The path-function is invariant; the result-position depends lawfully on what has already been accomplished.

## New Technical Terms

- **prakāra** — grade, mode, subdivision; here one of the nine grades of cultivation-abandonable defilement.
  - **status:** established Abhidharma classificatory term, becoming operationally important here.
  - **function:** measures the prior abandonment-state of the practitioner.

- **pūrvaprahīṇa** — previously abandoned.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** distinguishes abandonment already achieved by the mundane path from abandonment produced by the current Noble Path.

- **dvitīyaphalapratipannaka** — candidate progressing toward the second fruit.
  - **status:** technical path-person designation.
  - **function:** applies when six through eight desire-realm grades were previously abandoned.

- **sakṛdāgāmiphala** — fruit of Once-Return.
  - **status:** established Noble fruit.
  - **function:** second fruit toward which the 6–8 grade practitioner proceeds.

- **vītarāga** — free from attachment, dispassionate with respect to a domain.
  - **status:** established liberation vocabulary.
  - **function:** here marks complete desire-realm detachment after destruction of all nine grades.

- **tṛtīyaphalapratipannaka** — candidate progressing toward the third fruit.
  - **status:** technical path-person designation.
  - **function:** applies when all nine desire-realm grades have been abandoned, or detachment extends higher.

- **anāgāmiphala** — fruit of Non-Return.
  - **status:** established Noble fruit.
  - **function:** third fruit toward which the fully desire-detached practitioner proceeds.

- **ākiñcanyāyatana** — Sphere of Nothingness.
  - **status:** previously established meditative domain.
  - **function:** upper limit mentioned here for prior mundane detachment compatible with third-fruit candidacy during Seeing.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the standard classification directly.

No competing school position is introduced in this passage.

A textual caution is necessary: the compressed Kārikā can easily be misread as assigning “up to five grades destroyed” to the second fruit. The Bhāṣya makes the ranges explicit and removes the ambiguity:

```text
0–5 → first fruit
6–8 → second fruit
9+ → third fruit
```

The Bhāṣya's explicit prose should govern the interpretation.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.30 makes prior abandonment a precise variable in the classification of the Path-of-Seeing practitioner.

- If zero through five desire-realm cultivation-abandonable grades were previously abandoned, the Faith-Follower or Dharma-Follower remains a candidate for stream-entry.
- If six, seven, or eight grades were previously abandoned, the practitioner is a candidate for Once-Return.
- If the ninth grade has also been abandoned, the practitioner is detached from the desire realm and becomes a candidate for Non-Return.
- Prior detachment may extend still higher, as far as the Sphere of Nothingness, without changing this third-fruit candidacy during the Path of Seeing.

The Path remains one; the concrete path-person is determined by the state of the continuum entering it.
