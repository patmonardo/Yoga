# VAK 6.02 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

satyāny uktāni catvāri duḥkhaṃ samudayas tathā |
nirodho mārga ity eṣāṃ yathābhisamayaṃ kramaḥ || 6.2 ||

### Current Translation

Four truths have been stated: suffering, origin, cessation, and Path. Their sequence is according to the order of direct realization.

See: `VAK_6.02.md`

---

## Bhāṣya Source — Natural Argument

Vasubandhu first recalls that the four truths have already been stated elsewhere in the Kośa: the Path truth among the uncontaminated dharmas, cessation as analytical cessation / disconnection, and suffering and origin in the characterization of the world.

He then asks whether the order in which they are now presented is merely inherited from that prior exposition. The answer is no.

The operative sequence is:

```text
duḥkha
    ↓
samudaya
    ↓
nirodha
    ↓
mārga
```

and its principle is:

```text
yathā-abhisamayam
    according to direct realization
```

The Bhāṣya states:

> yasya hi satyasyābhisamayaḥ pūrvasya pūrvanirdeśaḥ /

The truth whose direct realization occurs first is taught first.

Vasubandhu immediately excludes causal sequence as the governing principle:

> itarathā hi pūrvaṃ hetunirdeśo 'bhaviṣyat paścāt phalanirdeśaḥ /

If causal order were intended, the cause would have been stated first and the result afterward.

This is decisive because in the afflicted pair the causal relation is:

```text
samudaya
    cause
    ↓
duḥkha
    result
```

but the order of realization is the reverse:

```text
duḥkha
    first examined
    ↓
samudaya
    its ground sought
```

The Four Truth sequence is therefore epistemic-practical rather than merely ontological-causal.

---

## Different Principles of Doctrinal Ordering

Vasubandhu next gives an important general hermeneutic principle: teachings are not always ordered by one rule.

Some teachings are arranged according to the order favorable to their **arising** (`utpatty-anukūlā deśanā`), as with the foundations of recollection and the meditations.

Others are arranged according to the order favorable to their **formulation / specification** (`prarūpaṇānukūlā deśanā`), as with the right efforts. Their textual order need not correspond rigidly to the chronological order in which their acts arise.

The Four Truths, however, are arranged according to what is favorable to **direct realization**:

```text
satyānāṃ tv abhisamayānukūlā deśanā
```

Thus the sequence of a doctrine must be read according to the function that the teaching is designed to serve.

---

## Why Direct Realization Has This Order

Vasubandhu asks why the Four Truths are directly realized in precisely this sequence.

His answer begins from the situation of the afflicted being:

```text
that in which one is situated
and by which one is afflicted
and from which one seeks release
```

is first examined as **duḥkha-satya**.

Only then does inquiry proceed:

```text
What is its ground?
    → samudaya-satya

What is its cessation?
    → nirodha-satya

What is the Path to that cessation?
    → mārga-satya
```

Vasubandhu compares this to medicine:

```text
disease
    ↓
its cause
    ↓
its removal
    ↓
medicine
```

The point is not merely pedagogical. The order begins from the **experienced actuality of bondage** and advances through diagnosis to liberation.

---

## Examination and Realization

The Bhāṣya then makes a subtle distinction between two stages:

```text
vyavacāraṇā-avasthā
    stage of examination / discrimination

abhisamaya-avasthā
    stage of direct realization
```

The truths are first investigated in the same sequence in which they are subsequently directly realized.

Vasubandhu explains the continuity through `pūrvavedha`: a prior penetration or preparatory striking-through establishes the direction later followed in direct realization.

He compares this to the running of a horse already oriented toward a previously seen region: once the direction has been established, the later movement follows that orientation.

Thus direct realization is not an arbitrary flash detached from preparatory inquiry. The preceding discrimination prepares its trajectory.

---

## What Is Abhisamaya?

Vasubandhu explicitly asks:

```text
abhisamaya iti ko 'rthaḥ?
```

What does *abhisamaya* mean?

He answers:

```text
abhisaṃbodha
    direct / complete awakening-knowing
```

because the verbal root carries the sense of knowing or awakening.

He then asks why this realization is uncontaminated rather than contaminated.

The answer is that it is:

```text
nirvāṇābhimukhaḥ samyak-bodhaḥ
```

**right knowing oriented toward nirvāṇa**.

And `samyak` means:

```text
tattvena
    according to reality / as it is
```

This gives abhisamaya a remarkably precise determination:

```text
abhisamaya
    = right knowing
    = according to reality
    = oriented toward nirvāṇa
    = therefore anāsrava
```

So direct realization is not defined merely by immediacy. It is defined simultaneously by **truth**, **orientation**, and **liberating efficacy**.

---

## The Ontological Relation of the Four Truths

The Bhāṣya then sharpens the distinction among the truths.

The appropriated aggregates insofar as they are results are **duḥkha-satya**.

The same conditioned complex insofar as it functions as cause is **samudaya-satya**.

Therefore suffering and origin differ:

```text
nāmataḥ
    by designation

not

dravyataḥ
    as distinct substances / real entities
```

Their distinction is grounded in their relation as result and cause.

By contrast, cessation and Path differ **dravyataḥ api** — also in real determination.

Thus:

```text
duḥkha ↔ samudaya
    same conditioned complex
    differently determined as result / cause

nirodha ↔ mārga
    really distinct determinations
```

This is an important control on any overly schematic treatment of the Four Truths as four homogeneous items.

---

## Why “Noble Truths”?

The Bhāṣya next asks why the scriptures call them `ārya-satya`, Noble Truths.

The initial answer is:

```text
āryāṇām etāni satyāni
```

They are the truths of the noble ones.

An objection follows:

```text
kim anyeṣām etāni mṛṣā?
```

Are they therefore false for everyone else?

No. They are truths for everyone because they are not inverted (`aviparīta`). But noble ones see them **as they actually are**, whereas others do not.

Hence they are called Noble Truths because:

```text
ārya
    sees them according to their actual determination

anārya
    sees through inversion
```

The distinction is not that truth changes with the knower. The distinction is whether the knower is adequate to the truth.

This closes the natural argumentative unit and prepares VAK 6.03, which asks how all contaminated formations can count as suffering.

---

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of the Four Truth sequence and its basis in direct realization.

**Voice: Objector** — asks whether the designation “Noble Truths” implies that the truths are false for non-noble beings.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — resolves the objection by distinguishing truth itself from adequate or inverted seeing.

**Voice: uncertain / reported alternative** — the closing remark that others take two truths as truths of noble ones and two as truths both noble in themselves and of noble ones is reported without endorsement before the transition to VAK 6.03.

## New Technical Terms

- **abhisamaya** — direct realization; explicitly glossed as `abhisaṃbodha`, right knowing according to reality and oriented toward nirvāṇa
  - **status:** made technically operative here
  - **function:** definition; determination of the sequence of the Four Truths

- **abhisaṃbodha** — complete/direct awakening-knowing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss
  - **function:** definition of `abhisamaya`

- **abhisamayānukūla** — favorable to / ordered for direct realization
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** principle of doctrinal ordering

- **utpattyanukūla** — favorable to arising
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** distinguishes one possible principle of textual sequence

- **prarūpaṇānukūla** — favorable to formulation / specification
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** distinguishes another possible principle of textual sequence

- **vyavacāraṇā** — examination, analytic discrimination
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only reflective vocabulary
  - **function:** preparatory stage preceding direct realization

- **pūrvavedha** — prior penetration / preparatory orientation
  - **status:** rare technical explanatory term
  - **function:** explains continuity from prior examination to direct realization

- **nirvāṇābhimukha** — oriented toward nirvāṇa
  - **status:** Bhāṣya qualification
  - **function:** explains why abhisamaya is uncontaminated

- **aviparīta / viparīta-darśana** — non-inverted / inverted seeing
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptual field
  - **function:** distinguishes truth itself from inadequate apprehension of truth

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 6.02 introduces a distinction between **causal order** and **path-order**.

Causal order of the afflicted pair:

```text
samudaya
    ↓
duḥkha
```

Path-order of realization:

```text
duḥkha
    ↓
samudaya
```

The path does not begin by reconstructing the universe from first causes. It begins from the afflicted actuality that must be overcome.

This makes the Four Truths fundamentally practical without making them merely subjective:

```text
objective truth
    remains what it is

path-order
    is determined by how truth must be adequately realized
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives us a first strong determination of the *form* of liberating knowledge:

```text
actual affliction
    → ground
    → possible cessation
    → effective mediation
```

This directly continues VAK 6.01. There the supramundane Path was distinguished by a `śakti` unavailable to the mundane path. Here Vasubandhu begins showing how such capacity is generated: the Path is structured by an ordered realization adequate to the real structure of bondage and release.

---

## Translation Consequences

The existing translation of `yathābhisamayaṃ kramaḥ` as:

> “Their sequence is according to the order of direct realization.”

is strongly confirmed.

The Bhāṣya rules out weaker renderings such as merely “in the traditional order” or “in causal sequence.”

For `abhisamaya`, **direct realization** remains the best compact working translation, but the Bhāṣya now requires us to hear within it:

```text
right knowing
according to reality
oriented toward nirvāṇa
```

This semantic content should be retained whenever the term becomes philosophically decisive later in the chapter.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest contact point is not a ready-made fourfold correspondence but the distinction between two kinds of order:

```text
order of being / causation
    ≠
order of liberating cognition
```

The Path begins from the determinate actuality confronting the practitioner, then regresses to its ground, determines its possible negation, and finally determines the mediation capable of effecting that negation.

In practical-reason language:

```text
What is the condition?
What grounds it?
Can it be overcome?
What act / Path is adequate to overcoming it?
```

The Bhāṣya itself warrants this much. Stronger Kantian or Hegelian identification should wait.

---

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.02 is not merely an enumeration of the Four Truths.

It determines the **logic of their order**:

```text
duḥkha
    actuality of bondage

samudaya
    ground of bondage

nirodha
    determinate possibility / actuality of release

mārga
    effective mediation of release
```

The sequence is neither arbitrary nor causal. It follows `abhisamaya`.

And Vasubandhu defines that realization as:

```text
samyak-bodha
    right knowing

tattvena
    according to reality

nirvāṇābhimukha
    oriented toward nirvāṇa
```

This is the first substantial indication in Chapter 6 of how **knowing becomes Path-capacity**.
