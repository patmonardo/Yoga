# VAK 6.11 — Bhāṣya Analysis

## Kārikā

अतिक्रान्तमनस्कारो भ्रूमध्ये चित्तधारणात् ।
अलोभो दशभूः कामदृश्यालम्बा नृजाशुभा ॥ ६.११ ॥

**atikrānta-manaskāro bhrūmadhye citta-dhāraṇāt |**  
**alobho daśabhūḥ kāma-dṛśyālambā nṛjāśubhā || 6.11 ||**

## Bhāṣya Argument

VAK 6.10 distinguished the beginner and the practitioner who has acquired mastery over the aśubha representation. VAK 6.11 completes the sequence with the practitioner called **atikrānta-manaskāra**.

After progressively relinquishing the skeletal representation until only half the skull remains, the practitioner relinquishes even that remainder and holds cognition between the eyebrows:

```text
half-skull
    ↓ relinquished
bhrūmadhye citta-dhāraṇā
    fixation of cognition between the eyebrows
```

Vasubandhu says that such a yogācāra is called **atikrānta-manaskāra with respect to aśubha**. The phrase should therefore not be inflated into a general claim about transcending mind or cognition. It marks mastery over this particular mode of constructed attention.

The Bhāṣya then adds a fourfold analysis concerning whether aśubha is limited by smallness of object-support and whether it is limited by lack of mastery. The point is that **extent of object and degree of mastery are independent determinations**. A practitioner may have a narrow object but mastery over it, or a vast object without full mastery.

Vasubandhu then asks four classificatory questions:

1. What is the intrinsic nature of aśubha?
2. On how many grounds does it occur?
3. What is its object-support?
4. Where does it arise?

The Kārikā answers them in order.

## Intrinsic Nature: alobha

Aśubha is **alobha-svabhāva**—non-greed by intrinsic nature.

This is a decisive doctrinal safeguard. The severe imagery of decomposition and skeletons is not a cultivation of hatred or aversion. Its function is the countering of rāga, and its wholesome root is non-greed.

```text
rāga
    appropriative attraction

pratipakṣa
    aśubha

intrinsic nature
    alobha
```

Thus the counter-practice does not replace attachment with dveṣa. It transforms the appropriative relation itself.

## Ten Grounds

Aśubha is **daśabhūmikā**, present on ten grounds. Vasubandhu counts:

- the desire-domain;
- the four dhyānas;
- their neighboring preparatory states, including the intermediate-dhyāna structure required by the Vaibhāṣika enumeration.

The important point for the present study is not merely the number ten, but that aśubha is a cultivated practice whose valid range is explicitly delimited by meditative level.

## Object-Support

Aśubha takes **visible form belonging to the desire-domain** as its object-support:

```text
kāmāvacara dṛśya
    desire-domain visible form
```

The Bhāṣya specifies that the object is the visible bodily configuration or shape. This confirms that the practice is an **artha-object practice** directed to a constructed bodily appearance rather than to verbal designation.

Because it is an adhimukti-based attention, it can also take temporally displaced objects according to the relevant mode of production; the Bhāṣya notes the relation between the temporal status of the practice and the temporal status of its object.

## Human Production

Aśubha arises **only among humans**:

```text
manuṣyeṣv eva utpadyate
nānyasyāṃ gatau
```

It does not arise in the other destinies, and even among humans Vasubandhu excludes Uttarakuru.

This is another reminder that the Path is not described as a free-floating cognitive technology. Particular practices require a determinate embodied and existential support.

## Contaminated Status

The Bhāṣya adds a classification not explicit in the verse:

```text
adhimukti-manaskāratvāt
    sāsravā
```

Aśubha is **contaminated** because it operates through **adhimukti-manaskāra**, a constructed or resolutive mode of attention.

This is crucial for the progression begun in 6.09. Aśubha is a genuine pratipakṣa and genuinely suppresses rāga, but it is not itself the supramundane Path of Seeing. Its efficacy remains preparatory.

```text
aśubha
    wholesome
    alobha-natured
    effective against rāga
    cultivated with mastery

but

    sāsrava
    constructed attention
    not definitive abandonment
```

Vasubandhu also distinguishes aśubha acquired through detachment from aśubha produced through deliberate practice, depending on whether the practitioner is already habituated to the cultivation.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the received Abhidharma classification, with explanatory refinements.

No major external opponent dominates this unit; the principal work is technical determination.

## New Technical Terms

- **atikrānta-manaskāra** — one whose attention has passed beyond the previously constructed aśubha support; here specifically mastery of the aśubha operation, not transcendence of cognition in general.
  - **status:** introduced in this practice sequence.
  - **function:** practitioner-stage classification.

- **bhrūmadhye citta-dhāraṇā** — fixation or sustaining of cognition between the eyebrows after relinquishing the residual skeletal support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā.
  - **function:** marks completed mastery over the aśubha construction.

- **adhimukti-manaskāra** — resolutive or constructed attention by which the practitioner intentionally apprehends the aśubha representation.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya-only classificatory term in this unit.
  - **function:** explains why aśubha remains sāsrava.

- **alobha-svabhāva** — non-greed as intrinsic nature.
  - **status:** confirms the Kārikā.
  - **function:** prevents confusion of aśubha with aversion.

## Path-of-Knowing Determination

The full 6.09–6.11 sequence can now be stated precisely:

```text
diagnose dominant rāga
    ↓
assign aśubha as pratipakṣa
    ↓
generate skeletal representation
    ↓
expand it
    ↓
contract it
    ↓
progressively relinquish it
    ↓
relinquish even the final residual support
    ↓
mastery over the therapeutic construction
```

The important result is that mastery does not consist in permanently retaining the counter-image. The practitioner becomes capable of **producing, governing, and relinquishing** it.

That gives a refined hierarchy of efficacy:

```text
ordinary afflictive cognition
    ↓
strategic counter-representation
    ↓
viṣkambhaṇa of rāga
    ↓
mastery over the counter-representation
    ↓
[still sāsrava]
    ↓
later supramundane prahāṇa
```

So mastery of a therapeutic representation is not yet liberation. Vasubandhu carefully refuses to confuse **control over cognition** with **definitive abandonment of the kleśas**.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.11 completes the aśubha training sequence and then classifies the practice:

```text
atikrānta-manaskāra
    mastery after relinquishing even the residual skeletal support

aśubha
    intrinsic nature: alobha
    levels: ten
    object-support: desire-domain visible form
    producer: human practitioner
    status: sāsrava because based on adhimukti-manaskāra
```

The decisive practical-psychological result is:

> Aśubha is an intentionally constructed, wholesome counter-practice whose mastery includes the capacity to relinquish the construction itself; precisely because it remains constructed and contaminated, it must not be mistaken for the supramundane power of definitive abandonment.
