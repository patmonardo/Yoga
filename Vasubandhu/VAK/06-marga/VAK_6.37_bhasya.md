# VAK 6.37 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**so 'ntarotpanna-saṃskārāsaṃskāra-parinirvṛtiḥ |**  
**ūrdhvasrotāś ca sa dhyāne vyavakīrṇo 'kaniṣṭhagaḥ || 6.37 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 358|19–359|14.

## 1. From Attainment-Threshold to Completion-Mode

VAK 6.36 completed the desire-realm abandonment sequence:

```text
nine grades destroyed
    → anāgāmin
    → no return to kāmadhātu
```

VAK 6.37 asks a new question. Once non-return has already been attained, **how does the remaining path terminate in parinirvāṇa?**

The organizing variable is no longer primarily the number of desire-realm grades abandoned. It is now the manner in which the non-returner's remaining trajectory unfolds.

The Bhāṣya states:

```text
sa evānāgāmī punaḥ pañcadhā bhavati
```

The same non-returner is further fivefold.

## 2. The Five Principal Non-Returners

The compressed compound of the kārikā unfolds into five path-persons.

### 2.1 Antarāparinirvāyin

```text
antarāparinirvāyī yo 'ntarābhave parinirvāti
```

The **antarāparinirvāyin** reaches parinirvāṇa in the intermediate existence.

Its distinguishing condition is temporal-position within the post-mortem sequence:

```text
death in kāmadhātu
    ↓
antarābhava
    ↓
parinirvāṇa before new rebirth is established
```

The classification therefore concerns **where in the remaining continuity liberation is completed**.

### 2.2 Upapadyaparinirvāyin

```text
upapadyaparinirvāyī ya upapannamātrī na cirāt parinirvāti
```

The **upapadyaparinirvāyin** reaches parinirvāṇa shortly after rebirth.

Vasubandhu attributes this to an already strongly developed path-current:

```text
abhiyukta-vāhi-mārga
    a path whose current is strongly engaged
```

The practitioner does not terminate in the intermediate state, but the remaining interval after rebirth is short.

The Bhāṣya reports a dispute concerning the exact mode of nirvāṇa involved:

- one account associates this with **sopadhiśeṣa-nirvāṇa**;
- **apare** say even **nirupadhiśeṣa**;
- the latter is rejected here because such a person has no mastery over relinquishing lifespan.

**Voice:** main exposition with a reported alternative and rejection.

## 3. With Exertion and Without Exertion

### 3.1 Sābhisaṃskāraparinirvāyin

```text
sābhisaṃskāraparinirvāyī ... apratiprasrabdha-prayogaḥ
```

This non-returner reaches parinirvāṇa **with renewed exertion / constructed effort**.

The important term is **prayoga**. The prior application has not relaxed completely; continued deliberate path-activity remains necessary.

The Bhāṣya characterizes the path as:

```text
abhiyukta-avāhi-mārga
```

That is, the path does not simply carry itself forward without intervention.

### 3.2 Anabhisaṃskāraparinirvāyin

The contrasting type reaches parinirvāṇa **without such renewed exertion**.

The Bhāṣya explicitly rejects one proposed explanation according to which the difference would simply be whether the path takes conditioned or unconditioned objects:

```text
saṃskṛtāsaṃskṛtālambana-mārga-nirvāṇād ity apare /
tat tu na / atiprasaṅgāt /
```

That explanation overgenerates and is therefore rejected.

Instead Vasubandhu grounds the distinction functionally:

```text
anabhisaṃskāra
    completion without renewed effort

sābhisaṃskāra
    completion requiring effort
```

He also notes that the sūtra order places the effortless type first and says this is appropriate because the contrast is precisely:

```text
ayatna-prāpti
    attainment without exertion

vs.

yatna-prāpti
    attainment through exertion
```

This is another important example of the Kośa classifying states by **transition-condition**, not by static label alone.

## 4. Relative Strength of Path and Residual Anuśaya

The Bhāṣya then makes a comparative determination.

For the **upapadyaparinirvāyin**:

```text
vāhitaro 'dhimātrataraś ca mārgaḥ
mṛdutarāś cānuśayāḥ
```

The path is more powerfully carrying and stronger, while the residual anuśayas are weaker.

Thus the time to completion depends jointly upon:

```text
strength of path
    +
weakness of remaining anuśaya
```

This continues the inverse-strength logic introduced in VAK 6.33.

## 5. Ūrdhvasrotas — The Upward-Streamer

The fifth principal type is:

```text
ūrdhvasrotā
```

Vasubandhu glosses:

```text
yasyordhvaṃ gatir na tatraiva parinirvāṇaṃ yatropapannaḥ
```

The upward-streamer does not attain parinirvāṇa in the very place in which rebirth first occurs; the trajectory proceeds upward.

He adds:

```text
sroto gatir ity eko 'rthaḥ
```

Here **srotas** and **gati** are treated as equivalent in force: the stream is the directed course of progression.

This is important structurally. The path-person is classified by a **trajectory through successive loci**, not by a single state.

## 6. Upward-Streamer: Cause and Result

The Bhāṣya states that the upward-streamer is twofold from two perspectives:

```text
ūrdhvasrotā dvividho hetutaḥ phalataś ca
```

### From the cause

```text
vyavakīrṇa-dhyāna
avyavakīrṇa-dhyāna
```

The distinction concerns whether dhyāna has been **intermingled / mixed** (*vyavakīrṇa*) or not.

### From the result

```text
akaniṣṭha-parama
bhavāgra-parama
```

The corresponding maximal destinations are:

```text
Akaniṣṭha
    or
bhavāgra, the summit of existence
```

VAK 6.37 treats the first member explicitly:

```text
yena dhyānaṃ vyavakīrṇaṃ
    → akaniṣṭhān gatvā parinirvāti
```

The practitioner whose dhyāna has been mixed/intermingled proceeds to **Akaniṣṭha** and there reaches parinirvāṇa.

The non-mixed case is continued in VAK 6.38.

## 7. What `vyavakīrṇa-dhyāna` Is Doing Here

At this point the Bhāṣya does not yet give a general philosophical definition of mixed dhyāna. What it establishes locally is functional:

```text
meditative history
    determines
upward rebirth trajectory
    which determines
maximal destination of the non-returner
```

Thus dhyāna is no longer merely a preparatory concentration-level. It has become part of the causal specification of the path-person's post-desire-realm trajectory.

We should therefore avoid reducing **vyavakīrṇa** to an ordinary psychological mixture. In this passage it names a technical meditative condition with determinate cosmological-path consequences.

## 8. Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.25–6.36 progressively determined:

```text
Seeing-process
    ↓
path-person
    ↓
fruit-candidate
    ↓
fruit-established person
    ↓
ninefold abandonment
    ↓
non-returner
```

VAK 6.37 adds a new dimension:

```text
attained fruit
    does not uniquely determine
mode of completion
```

The non-returner is further determined by:

```text
where liberation completes
when it completes
whether renewed exertion is required
strength of residual anuśaya
strength of path-current
meditative history
upward trajectory
final destination
```

The path-person has therefore become a **dynamical classification of a continuum**.

The five principal types can be represented locally as:

```text
anāgāmin
    ├─ antarāparinirvāyin
    ├─ upapadyaparinirvāyin
    ├─ sābhisaṃskāraparinirvāyin
    ├─ anabhisaṃskāraparinirvāyin
    └─ ūrdhvasrotas
```

The fifth then divides according to causal meditative condition and resulting destination.

## New Technical Terms

- **antarāparinirvāyin** — non-returner who reaches parinirvāṇa in the intermediate existence.  
  **Status:** newly foregrounded here.  
  **Function:** path-person classification by temporal locus of final liberation.

- **upapadyaparinirvāyin** — non-returner who reaches parinirvāṇa soon after rebirth.  
  **Status:** newly foregrounded here.  
  **Function:** classification by rapid post-rebirth completion.

- **sābhisaṃskāraparinirvāyin** — one who reaches parinirvāṇa with renewed exertion.  
  **Status:** newly foregrounded here.  
  **Function:** classification by effort-condition of completion.

- **anabhisaṃskāraparinirvāyin** — one who reaches parinirvāṇa without renewed exertion.  
  **Status:** newly foregrounded here.  
  **Function:** classification by effortless completion.

- **ūrdhvasrotas** — upward-streamer; one whose course proceeds to higher rebirth rather than terminating in the first place of rebirth.  
  **Status:** newly foregrounded here.  
  **Function:** classification by trajectory.

- **abhisaṃskāra** — deliberate/constructed exertion in bringing the remaining path to completion.  
  **Status:** technical here.  
  **Function:** discriminates effortful from non-effortful completion.

- **prayoga** — directed application or exertion.  
  **Status:** previously encountered.  
  **Function:** operative condition required in the effortful completion-type.

- **vāhi-mārga** — path as a carrying current.  
  **Status:** locally important expression.  
  **Function:** characterizes how strongly the path carries the continuum toward completion.

- **vyavakīrṇa-dhyāna** — technically intermingled/mixed dhyāna.  
  **Status:** newly important here.  
  **Function:** causal determinant of the Akaniṣṭha trajectory.

- **Akaniṣṭha** — highest Pure Abode destination in this classification.  
  **Status:** previously known cosmological term, newly path-functional here.  
  **Function:** maximal destination of the mixed-dhyāna upward-streamer.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presents the received Abhidharma classification of five non-returners.

**Voice:** `apare` appears twice for alternative doctrinal explanations:

1. regarding the nirvāṇa-type of the upapadyaparinirvāyin;
2. regarding a proposed saṃskṛta/asaṃskṛta-object explanation of the effortful/effortless distinction.

The second alternative is explicitly rejected as overbroad (*atiprasaṅga*).

The exact larger doctrine of **vyavakīrṇa-dhyāna** should not be imported into this verse beyond what the Bhāṣya establishes locally.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.37 moves beyond the attainment of non-return and classifies **how the remaining continuum reaches completion**.

```text
anāgāmin
    can reach parinirvāṇa:

    in the intermediate existence
    shortly after rebirth
    through continued exertion
    without renewed exertion
    or by an upward stream through higher existences
```

The upward-streamer is then determined by:

```text
cause:
    mixed vs non-mixed dhyāna

result:
    Akaniṣṭha vs summit-of-existence trajectory
```

For the case stated in this verse:

```text
vyavakīrṇa-dhyāna
    → upward-stream
    → Akaniṣṭha
    → parinirvāṇa
```

The Kośa's path-person is therefore not merely a name for an attained state. It is a specification of the **remaining causal trajectory of a transformed continuum**.
