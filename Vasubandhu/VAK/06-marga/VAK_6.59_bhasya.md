# VAK 6.59 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> parihāṇis tridhā jñeyā prāptāprāptopabhogataḥ /  
> antyā śāstur akopyasya madhyā cānyasya tu tridhā // VAK_6.59 //

**Best current translation:**  
Regression is to be understood in three ways: through loss of what has been attained, through non-attainment of what remains to be attained, and through failure to make use of what has been attained. Of these, the last applies to the Teacher; the last and the middle to the unshakeable one; for another [Arhat], all three occur.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.59.md`](./VAK_6.59.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 377|16–378|04.

The Bhāṣya continues the dispute opened in VAK 6.58 concerning regression. The crucial move is terminological: *parihāṇi* does not name one homogeneous event. Vasubandhu distinguishes three quite different senses of loss, and this distinction explains how even an unshakeable Arhat—or the Buddha—can be said to undergo a kind of regression without thereby losing liberation.

## 1. The Sūtra Problem

**Voice:** Vasubandhu introducing scriptural evidence and a difficulty

The discussion begins from a sūtra stating that regression can occur from four superior mental pleasant abidings (*ādhicaitasika dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihāra*), whereas no regression is asserted from the unshakeable liberation of mind directly realized by the practitioner.

This raises the question:

> `katham akopyadharmaṇo dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihārebhyaḥ parihāṇiḥ`

How can an *akopyadharman* regress from the pleasant abidings experienced in this very life?

The difficulty arises only if *parihāṇi* is taken univocally. The kārikā answers by distinguishing three senses.

## 2. Three Kinds of Regression

The verse states:

> `parihāṇis tridhā jñeyā prāptāprāptopabhogataḥ`

The Bhāṣya glosses each form precisely.

### 2.1 Prāptaparihāṇi — Loss of an Attained Quality

> `prāptaparihāṇir yadi pratilabdhād guṇāt parihīyate`

There is *prāptaparihāṇi* when one falls away from a quality already obtained.

```text
quality obtained
    ↓
quality actually lost
```

This is the strongest sense of regression.

### 2.2 Aprāptaparihāṇi — Failure to Attain a Further Quality

> `aprāptaparihāṇir yadi prāpyaṃ guṇaṃ na prāpnoti`

There is *aprāptaparihāṇi* when one does not obtain a quality that remains capable of being obtained.

```text
further attainable quality
    ↓
not reached
```

Nothing already possessed need be lost. The regression lies in failure of further acquisition.

### 2.3 Upabhogaparihāṇi — Failure to Bring an Attained Quality into Use

> `upabhogaparihāṇir yadi prāptaguṇaṃ na saṃmukhīkaroti`

There is *upabhogaparihāṇi* when one does not bring an already attained quality into immediate presence or actual exercise.

```text
quality remains attained
    ↓
not made present / not exercised
```

This is not loss of possession but loss of present enjoyment or actualization.

## 3. The Three Forms Are Distributed Differently

The kārikā continues:

> `antyā śāstur akopyasya madhyā cānyasya tu tridhā`

The prose gives the distribution.

### Buddha

> `buddhasyopabhogaparihāṇir eva nānyā`

For the Buddha there is only *upabhogaparihāṇi*.

This cannot mean loss of liberation or failure of final attainment. It can only mean that a quality already possessed is not presently brought into manifestation or enjoyment.

### Akopyadharman

> `akopyadharmaṇaḥ sā cāprāptaparihāṇiś ca`

For the unshakeable Arhat there are two:

- *upabhogaparihāṇi*
- *aprāptaparihāṇi*

The reason for the latter is:

> `pudgalaviśeṣadharmāprāpaṇāt`

because such a person may fail to obtain some special quality belonging to a distinctive type of person.

Thus an unshakeable Arhat can fail to acquire a further special excellence without losing already attained liberation.

### Other Arhats

> `anyasyārhataḥ prāptaparihāṇir apy asti`

For another Arhat, *prāptaparihāṇi* also occurs.

Hence the full distribution is:

```text
Buddha
    upabhoga-parihāṇi

akopyadharman
    aprāpta-parihāṇi
    upabhoga-parihāṇi

other Arhat
    prāpta-parihāṇi
    aprāpta-parihāṇi
    upabhoga-parihāṇi
```

## 4. Regression Is Not One Thing

This distinction is the central doctrinal gain of the verse.

The word *parihāṇi* can indicate:

```text
loss of possession
failure of further acquisition
failure of present actualization
```

These are structurally different.

This prevents the discussion from collapsing into the crude opposition:

```text
regression
vs.
no regression
```

Instead the question becomes:

```text
What is lost?
What remains possessed?
What fails to be newly acquired?
What merely fails to become presently manifest?
```

This is exactly the clarification required by the dispute of VAK 6.58.

## 5. Scriptural Tension and the Unshakeable Arhat

The prose then returns to the apparent conflict with the sūtra. If the sūtra says that no regression occurs from unshakeable liberation, how can *upabhogaparihāṇi* be predicated of the *akopyadharman*?

The answer is that the regression in question need not be regression from uncontaminated liberation itself.

The Bhāṣya states:

> `sarvasyānāsravā vimuktir akopyā`

The uncontaminated liberation of every [Arhat] is unshakeable.

And:

> `akopyadharmavyavasthānaṃ tu yathā tathoktam`

The special classification of the *akopyadharman* is to be understood in the manner already explained.

This formulation is important because it separates two claims:

```text
anāsravā vimukti
    unshakeable as liberation

akopyadharman
    a special pudgala-classification
```

The special designation of one Arhat as *akopyadharman* therefore should not force every use of *akopya* into one identical technical sense.

**Voice:** the final line of this exchange is textually compact and should be treated cautiously; it appears to preserve the standpoint of the no-regression interpretation introduced in the preceding dispute.

## 6. Relation to VAK 6.58

VAK 6.58 asked whether an Arhat can regress from the fruit.

VAK 6.59 now shows why the answer depends upon what exactly *parihāṇi* means.

```text
VAK 6.58
    Is there regression?

VAK 6.59
    Regression of what kind?
```

The conceptual advance is therefore classificatory rather than merely polemical.

Even where schools disagree over *phala-parihāṇi*, they must still distinguish:

- losing an acquired state,
- failing to reach a further state,
- failing to actualize a state already possessed.

## 7. Mārga Determination

For Chapter 6, the verse makes an important point about completion.

Attainment is not adequately described by a simple binary:

```text
attained
not attained
```

The Path produces a more articulated structure:

```text
possession
    what has been acquired

advance
    what further qualities remain attainable

actualization
    what among possessed qualities is presently made manifest
```

A completed practitioner may therefore differ from another not only in what has been attained, but in what can still be attained and in what is presently brought into operation.

This preserves the dynamism of *Mārga* even at its mature end.

## 8. Restrained Organon Contact

The strongest logical clarification is the separation of **having**, **becoming capable of more**, and **making present**.

```text
prāpti
    possession

aprāpti of a further guṇa
    blocked development

upabhoga
    actual exercise / manifestation
```

This distinction prevents a category mistake: failure of manifestation is not identical with loss of possession, and failure of further development is not identical with either.

The Bhāṣya thus replaces an undifferentiated notion of regression with a modal analysis of attainment.

## New Technical Terms

- **prāptaparihāṇi** — regression through loss of a quality already attained  
  **status:** explicitly defined in this Bhāṣya passage  
  **function:** strongest form of regression; actual loss of possession

- **aprāptaparihāṇi** — regression through failure to attain a further attainable quality  
  **status:** explicitly defined here  
  **function:** distinguishes blocked further development from loss of what is already possessed

- **upabhogaparihāṇi** — regression through failure to bring an attained quality into present exercise or enjoyment  
  **status:** explicitly defined here  
  **function:** explains how regression-language can apply even where the attained quality itself is not lost

- **saṃmukhīkaraṇa** — bringing into immediate presence / actual manifestation  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term  
  **function:** defines what fails in *upabhogaparihāṇi*

- **pudgalaviśeṣadharma** — a special quality belonging to a distinctive type of person  
  **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression  
  **function:** explains *aprāptaparihāṇi* in the unshakeable Arhat

- **anāsravā vimukti** — uncontaminated liberation  
  **status:** recurrent technical expression  
  **function:** here asserted to be *akopyā* in itself, requiring distinction from the narrower pudgala-classification *akopyadharman*

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

The threefold definition and its distribution are presented in Vasubandhu's exposition of the received Abhidharma structure. The final lines continue the doctrinal tension already visible in VAK 6.58 between interpretations that allow regression from Arhatship and those that deny it. The passage should therefore not be homogenized into a single uncontested theory of Arhat regression.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.59 distinguishes three meanings of *parihāṇi*: actual loss of an attained quality, failure to attain a further quality, and failure to bring an attained quality into present exercise. The Buddha is subject only to the third; the unshakeable Arhat to the second and third; another Arhat may be subject to all three. The distinction resolves an apparent contradiction in speaking of regression for an *akopyadharman*: failure of actualization or further acquisition need not imply loss of uncontaminated liberation. The verse therefore converts the vague category of regression into a precise analysis of possession, development, and present manifestation.
