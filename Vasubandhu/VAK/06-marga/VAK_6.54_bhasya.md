# VAK 6.54 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> brāhmaṇyaṃ brahmacakraṃ ca tad eva brahmavartanāt /  
> dharmacakraṃ tu dṛṅmārga āśugatvād arādibhiḥ // VAkK_6.54 //

**Best current translation:**  
That same [śrāmaṇya] is also called *brāhmaṇya* and the Brahma-wheel, because it is set in motion by Brahmā. The Dharma-wheel, however, is the path of seeing, because of its swift movement and its similarity to spokes and the other parts of a wheel.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.54.md`](./VAK_6.54.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 370|22–371|24.

This passage closes the discussion of *śrāmaṇya* by extending it through the names *brāhmaṇya*, *brahmacakra*, and *dharmacakra*. The Bhāṣya then records several explanations of what exactly is meant by the Dharma-wheel. These should not be flattened into one undifferentiated doctrine.

## 1. From Śrāmaṇya to Brāhmaṇya

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya first says that the same reality previously called *śrāmaṇya* is also called *brāhmaṇya*:

> `brāhmaṇyaṃ ... tad eva`

and explains:

> `kleśānāṃ vāhanād brāhmaṇyam`

It is called *brāhmaṇya* because it carries away / removes the afflictions.

The point is functional rather than social. The determining mark is not birth or status but the actual removal of the kleśas by the noble path.

```text
śrāmaṇya
    uncontaminated path
        ↓
removal of kleśa
        ↓
brāhmaṇya
```

## 2. Why It Is Called the Brahma-Wheel

The same path is also called *brahmacakra*.

The Bhāṣya explains:

> `anuttarabrahmaṇyayogāt bhagavān brahmā`

The Blessed One is called Brahmā because of connection with unsurpassed *brāhmaṇya*.

A sūtra is cited in which the Blessed One is called Brahmā, peaceful, and cooled. Therefore:

> `tasyedaṃ cakram iti brāhmaṃ tena pravartitatvāt`

This is his wheel, hence the Brahma-wheel, because it was set in motion by him.

Thus the expression emphasizes the Path as publicly inaugurated and transmitted by the Buddha.

```text
unsurpassed brāhmaṇya
    ↓
Buddha called Brahmā
    ↓
Path set in motion by him
    ↓
Brahma-wheel
```

## 3. The First Definition of the Dharma-Wheel: Path of Seeing

The kārikā states:

> `dharmacakraṃ tu dṛṅmārgaḥ`

**Voice:** Vasubandhu introducing a standard explanation

The path of seeing is called the Dharma-wheel because it resembles a wheel through motion:

> `caṅkramaṇāc cakraṃ tatsādharmyād darśanamārgo dharmacakram`

The question then becomes: in what respects is the resemblance present?

## 4. The Dynamic Similarity: Swift Movement and Conquest

The kārikā answers:

> `āśugatvādy arādibhiḥ`

The Bhāṣya expands the dynamic analogy:

> `āśugatvāt tyajanakramaṇāt ajitajayajitādhyavasanād utpatananipattanāc ca`

The path resembles a wheel because of:

- swift movement,
- leaving one place and proceeding to another,
- conquering what has not yet been conquered,
- holding fast what has been conquered,
- rising and descending.

The analogy is therefore not merely visual. It concerns an ordered, rapid, effective movement.

```text
rapid movement
    +
progressive transition
    +
conquest
    +
retention of conquest
    +
rising / descending
        ↓
wheel-like path
```

## 5. Bhadanta Ghoṣaka: The Noble Eightfold Path as Wheel

**Voice:** Bhadanta Ghoṣaka

Vasubandhu records another explanation:

> `āryāṣṭāṅgo mārgaś cakram iti bhadantaghoṣakaḥ`

For Bhadanta Ghoṣaka, the noble eightfold path itself is the wheel because its members correspond to the parts of a wheel.

The assignments are explicit:

### Spokes — *ara*

- right view — `samyagdṛṣṭi`
- right intention — `samyaksaṃkalpa`
- right effort — `samyagvyāyāma`
- right recollection — `samyaksmṛti`

These are compared to spokes.

### Hub — *nābhi*

- right speech — `samyagvāk`
- right action — `samyakkarmānta`
- right livelihood — `samyagājīva`

These are compared to the hub.

### Rim — *nemi*

- right concentration — `samyaksamādhi`

This is compared to the rim.

So the wheel is an articulated whole:

```text
spokes
    view / intention / effort / recollection

hub
    speech / action / livelihood

rim
    concentration
```

The path is not a heap of eight factors. The wheel image expresses their functional unity.

## 6. Why Identify the Dharma-Wheel with the Path of Seeing?

The Bhāṣya asks:

> `kuta etat`

Why identify the Dharma-wheel specifically with the path of seeing?

The answer appeals to the statement made when noble Kauṇḍinya attained that path:

> `pravartitaṃ dharmacakram`

"The Dharma-wheel has been set in motion."

Thus the arising of the path of seeing in the first disciple serves as textual evidence for this identification.

This already introduces an important shift:

```text
Buddha's realization / teaching
    ↓
path of seeing arises in another continuum
    ↓
Dharma-wheel is said to turn
```

## 7. The Three Turnings and Twelve Aspects

The Bhāṣya then asks:

> `kathaṃ tat triparivartaṃ dvādaśākāraṃ ca`

How is the Dharma-wheel threefold in turning and twelvefold in aspect?

For the truth of suffering, three formulations are given:

```text
This is the noble truth of suffering.
It is to be fully known.
It has been fully known.
```

These are the three turnings.

In each turning, four expressions of cognition are said to arise:

- eye — `cakṣus`
- knowledge — `jñāna`
- knowledge/light — `vidyā`
- intelligence — `buddhi`

Thus:

```text
3 turnings × 4 cognitive formulations
    = 12 aspects
```

The same structure applies to each truth.

## 8. Vaibhāṣika Interpretation: Three Turnings as Three Paths

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position

The Vaibhāṣikas interpret the three turnings as showing, respectively:

```text
darśana-mārga
bhāvanā-mārga
aśaikṣa-mārga
```

That is:

- seeing,
- cultivation,
- beyond-training.

This interpretation distributes the three turnings across the whole noble path.

## 9. Vasubandhu's Objection

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu immediately points out the difficulty:

If the three turnings correspond to seeing, cultivation, and beyond-training, then the path of seeing **alone** cannot itself be three-turning and twelve-aspect.

Therefore the identification:

```text
dharmacakra = path of seeing alone
```

cannot straightforwardly explain the traditional formula of the three-turning, twelve-aspect Dharma-wheel.

This is an important critical moment. Vasubandhu does not simply repeat inherited symbolic identifications; he checks whether the explanatory relations actually cohere.

## 10. The Dharma-Exposition Itself as Dharma-Wheel

Vasubandhu therefore proposes that the Dharma exposition itself is more appropriately called the three-turning, twelve-aspect Dharma-wheel:

> `tasmāt sa eva dharmaparyāyo dharmacakraṃ triparivartaṃ dvādaśākāraṃ ca yujyate`

The three turnings arise because each truth is turned three times:

### Truth

```text
duḥkha
samudaya
nirodha
mārga
```

### Task

```text
parijñeyam      to be fully known
praheyam        to be abandoned
sākṣātkartavyam to be directly realized
bhāvayitavyam   to be cultivated
```

### Completion

```text
parijñātam      fully known
prahīṇam        abandoned
sākṣātkṛtam     directly realized
bhāvitam        cultivated
```

Hence:

```text
4 truths × 3 determinations
    = 12 aspects
```

This is a much more systematic interpretation of the traditional formula.

## 11. What Does It Mean to "Turn" the Wheel?

The Bhāṣya then gives a striking definition of *pravartana*:

> `tasya punaḥ pravartanaṃ parasaṃtāne gamanam arthajñāpanāt`

Its turning is its **going into another continuum through making the meaning known**.

This is stronger than the simple image of public preaching.

```text
meaning known
    ↓
Dharma enters another continuum
    ↓
wheel turns
```

The decisive event is successful cognitive transmission.

## 12. Alternative Broad Definition: The Whole Noble Path

The Bhāṣya finally offers a broader possibility:

> `athavā sarva evāryamārgo dharmacakraṃ veneyasaṃtānakramaṇāt`

Or the entire noble path is the Dharma-wheel because it proceeds into the continuum of those to be trained.

Yet it is said to have been "set in motion" specifically when the path of seeing was produced in another continuum:

> `parasaṃtāne darśanamārgotpādanād vartayitum ārabdham ataḥ pravartitam ity ucyate`

Thus the first arising of noble seeing in another person is the inaugural turning of the wheel.

## 13. The Core Structure of the Passage

The Bhāṣya preserves several determinations without reducing them to one:

```text
śrāmaṇya
    uncontaminated path

brāhmaṇya
    same path as remover of affliction

brahmacakra
    same path as set in motion by the Buddha

dharmacakra
    path of seeing
        or
    noble eightfold path as articulated wheel
        or
    three-turn/twelve-aspect Dharma exposition
        or
    whole noble path entering another continuum
```

These are not mere synonyms. Each names a different functional determination of the same liberating order.

## 14. Restrained Organon Contact

This passage gives an unusually clear account of transmission without reducing truth to communication.

The Path is first possessed and enacted; then its meaning is made known; finally the same determination arises in another continuum.

```text
realized path
    ↓
articulated meaning
    ↓
meaning made known
    ↓
path arises in another continuum
```

The "turning" therefore names successful mediation from one rational-practical continuum to another.

This remains firmly within Vasubandhu's own argument. A stronger Organon interpretation should be deferred to the later Concept pass.

## New Technical Terms

- **brāhmaṇya** — the same uncontaminated path considered as carrying away the afflictions  
  **status:** developed here from *śrāmaṇya*  
  **function:** functional renaming of the noble path

- **brahmacakra** — Brahma-wheel; the path considered as set in motion by the Buddha, who is called Brahmā through unsurpassed *brāhmaṇya*  
  **status:** new in this immediate discussion  
  **function:** identifies the path through its authoritative inauguration

- **dharmacakra** — Dharma-wheel  
  **status:** central new term in this passage  
  **function:** variously identifies the path of seeing, the noble eightfold path, the Dharma exposition, or the whole noble path in transmission

- **triparivarta** — threefold turning  
  **status:** developed in the Bhāṣya  
  **function:** threefold determination of each truth: statement, task, completion

- **dvādaśākāra** — twelve-aspect  
  **status:** developed in the Bhāṣya  
  **function:** numerical articulation of the three turnings across four truths / cognitive formulations

- **dharmaparyāya** — Dharma exposition / doctrinal formulation  
  **status:** Bhāṣya term  
  **function:** Vasubandhu's preferred candidate for the three-turning, twelve-aspect Dharma-wheel

- **parasaṃtāna** — another continuum  
  **status:** important here  
  **function:** locus into which the Dharma/path proceeds when the wheel is turned

- **arthajñāpana** — making the meaning known  
  **status:** important Bhāṣya expression  
  **function:** explains how the Dharma-wheel proceeds into another continuum

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

- **Vasubandhu:** explains *brāhmaṇya*, *brahmacakra*, critiques the narrow three-turning interpretation, and offers the Dharma exposition / whole noble path alternatives.
- **Bhadanta Ghoṣaka:** explicitly identified as assigning the eightfold path factors to spokes, hub, and rim.
- **Vaibhāṣikas:** explicitly identified as correlating the three turnings with seeing, cultivation, and beyond-training.
- **Sūtra citation:** the statement that the Dharma-wheel was set in motion when noble Kauṇḍinya attained the path of seeing functions as scriptural support for one definition.

The passage should therefore be read as a small doctrinal debate rather than as a single flat symbolic explanation.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.54 extends the uncontaminated path through three functional names. It is *brāhmaṇya* as the remover of afflictions, *brahmacakra* as the wheel set in motion by the Buddha, and *dharmacakra* insofar as the path or Dharma moves effectively into another continuum.

The Bhāṣya's strongest determination of "turning" is:

```text
meaning made known
    ↓
Dharma enters another continuum
    ↓
path of seeing arises there
    ↓
Dharma-wheel has been set in motion
```

The wheel is therefore not merely an image of motion. It names an articulated liberating order that becomes effective in another practitioner's continuum through the communicated meaning of the truths.
