# VAK 6.39 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**tadviśeṣaḥ punaḥ karmakleśendriyaviśeṣataḥ // VAkK_6.39 //**

Their further differentiation is due to differences in karma, affliction, and faculty.

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 360|13–361|04.

The Bhāṣya first completes the previous classification by explaining how three principal non-returner types are each divided threefold, yielding nine form-realm non-returners. It then gives the causal basis of those distinctions.

## 1. From Three Non-Returners to Nine

Before commenting directly on the verse, Vasubandhu states that three non-returner types are each subdivided threefold:

- the one who attains parinirvāṇa in the intermediate state,
- the one who attains parinirvāṇa after rebirth,
- the upward-streamer.

The first is divided according to rapid, non-rapid, and delayed parinirvāṇa. The second is divided according to parinirvāṇa immediately after arising, with renewed exertion, and without renewed exertion. The upward-streamer is divided as the leaper, half-leaper, and one who passes through all relevant stations.

Thus the form-realm non-returners become nine.

## 2. The Verse Gives the Generating Variables

Vasubandhu then states:

> **teṣāṃ punas trayāṇāṃ navānāṃ cānāgāmināṃ karmakleśendriyaviśeṣād viśeṣaḥ**

The differentiation of those three and of those nine non-returners is due to differences in:

```text
karma
kleśa
indriya
```

The classification is therefore not merely terminological. The different path-persons are generated by determinate differences in causal history, afflictive activity, and faculty-strength.

## 3. The Three Principal Types

For the principal threefold differentiation, Vasubandhu correlates three kinds of karma with three grades of afflictive manifestation and three grades of faculty.

The karmic series is:

```text
abhinirvṛtti-vedanīya karma
upapadya-vedanīya karma
aparaparyāya-vedanīya karma
```

The Bhāṣya then gives the corresponding afflictive activity:

```text
mṛdu kleśa-samudācāra
madhya kleśa-samudācāra
adhimātra kleśa-samudācāra
```

and the corresponding faculties in inverse order:

```text
adhimātra indriya
madhya indriya
mṛdu indriya
```

Thus the relation is:

```text
mild afflictive activity
    ↔ strong faculty

medium afflictive activity
    ↔ medium faculty

strong afflictive activity
    ↔ weak faculty
```

This is a significant structural point. The same non-returner fruit can be internally differentiated because the residual causal continuum is differently configured.

## 4. Karma as Temporal Distribution of Result

The three karmic terms distinguish when accumulated karma is to be experienced.

At this stage the Bhāṣya uses them to explain why the three principal completion-trajectories differ. Their future course is conditioned not only by their present cognitive state but also by the temporal disposition of previously accumulated karma.

The practitioner-type therefore carries a temporal structure:

```text
what karma has been accumulated
    ↓
when that karma bears result
    ↓
which completion-trajectory becomes possible
```

This is one reason the person-classification cannot be reduced to a static psychological description.

## 5. Kleśa and Indriya Covary Inversely

The relation between affliction and faculty is especially clear:

```text
stronger faculty
    → milder kleśa activity

weaker faculty
    → stronger kleśa activity
```

The Bhāṣya does not present this as an abstract metaphysical law. It is the specific differentiating principle among these non-returner types.

Still, within the local system it gives a precise rule:

```text
faculty-strength
    and
residual afflictive manifestation

are inversely ordered
```

This extends the earlier pattern in which stronger path-power confronts subtler remaining obstruction.

## 6. How the Nine Are Further Differentiated

Vasubandhu then explains that each of the nine is again distinguished, as appropriate, by the same causal variables.

For the first two triads, the differentiation is primarily by:

```text
kleśa-viśeṣa
indriya-viśeṣa
```

as in the preceding threefold scheme.

For the final triad, the upward-streamers, the distinction is tied especially to differences in:

```text
aparaparyāya-vedanīya karma
```

that is, karma whose result is experienced in a later subsequent existence.

This makes sense of the route-differences described in VAK 6.38: the leaper, half-leaper, and all-stations type are not arbitrary labels but different trajectories conditioned by different later-result karma.

## 7. Nine Non-Returners

The Bhāṣya concludes:

> **ta ete navaprakārakleśendriyatvān navānāgāmino bhavanti**

Because of the ninefold differentiation of afflictions and faculties, there are nine non-returners.

This conclusion connects VAK 6.39 back to the ninefold grading principle introduced in VAK 6.33. The ninefold form is no longer only a scale of faults and qualities. It now determines distinct completion-trajectories within one noble fruit.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.39 gives the clearest causal schema yet for the path-person classifications:

```text
same noble fruit
    ↓
variation in karma
variation in kleśa
variation in indriya
    ↓
variation in completion-trajectory
    ↓
distinct path-person type
```

The important methodological advance is that the person is not being classified by an essence or substantial identity. The classification records the operative condition of a continuum:

```text
causal history
    +
residual afflictive activity
    +
faculty-strength
    =
determinate future path-course
```

This gives the preceding taxonomy a scientific form: the names are outputs of a small set of differentiating variables.

## New Technical Terms

- **abhinirvṛtti-vedanīya-karman** — karma whose result belongs to the initial formation or coming-to-be relevant to the next stage of existence.
  - **status** — Bhāṣya-specific technical differentiation in this passage.
  - **function** — distinguishes one temporal mode of karmic result among the principal non-returner types.

- **upapadya-vedanīya-karman** — karma to be experienced upon rebirth.
  - **status** — technical karmic classification.
  - **function** — determines a distinct rebirth-conditioned completion trajectory.

- **aparaparyāya-vedanīya-karman** — karma to be experienced in a later subsequent existence.
  - **status** — technical karmic classification.
  - **function** — especially differentiates the upward-streamer trajectories.

- **kleśa-samudācāra** — active manifestation or occurrence of affliction.
  - **status** — important local refinement of the general term *kleśa*.
  - **function** — grades the residual active force of affliction among non-returners.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu presenting the Abhidharma classificatory explanation.

The verse itself gives only the three variables: karma, kleśa, and indriya. The detailed correlations come from the Bhāṣya and should not be projected backward into the bare Kārikā without qualification.

The karmic terminology is highly technical. It is safer in this pass to preserve the Sanskrit compounds and describe their temporal function than to force them into modern categories such as “immediate,” “short-term,” or “long-term” causation.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.39 explains why the many non-returner types exist.

Their distinctions are generated by three variables:

```text
karma
    temporal disposition of accumulated result

kleśa
    degree of residual afflictive manifestation

indriya
    strength of faculty
```

For the principal threefold differentiation:

```text
mild kleśa   ↔ strong faculty
medium kleśa ↔ medium faculty
strong kleśa ↔ weak faculty
```

The ninefold refinement applies the same logic further. The first two triads differ especially through kleśa and faculty; the upward-streamer triad differs especially through later-result karma.

The path-person classifications therefore have an explicit causal basis:

```text
configuration of the continuum
    → determinate path-trajectory
    → determinate practitioner-type
```

VAK 6.39 transforms the preceding taxonomy into a parameterized account of how one and the same noble fruit can unfold through different causal trajectories.
