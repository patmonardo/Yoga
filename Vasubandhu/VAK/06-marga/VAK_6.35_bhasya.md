# VAK 6.35 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**āpañcama-prakāra-ghno dvitīya-pratipannakaḥ |**  
**kṣīṇa-ṣaṣṭha-prakāras tu sakṛdāgāmy asau punaḥ || 6.35 ||**

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 357|20–358|03.

## 1. The Stream-Enterer Can Re-enter Candidate Status

Vasubandhu begins from the practitioner already established in the first fruit:

> **sa eva punaḥ phalasthaḥ** — that very one, again established in the fruit.

The important point is that **phalastha** is not a terminal identity. A practitioner established in one fruit can, through renewed cultivation, become a **pratipannaka** toward a higher fruit.

Thus the sequence developed in VAK 6.32 is now instantiated:

```text
first fruit attained
    ↓
renewed cultivation
    ↓
further abandonment
    ↓
second-fruit candidacy
```

This makes the relation between fruit and path dynamic rather than static.

## 2. One through Five Grades Abandoned: Candidate for the Second Fruit

The first half is glossed:

> **yasya phalasthasyaika-prakāro yāvat pañcamaḥ prahīṇo bhavati asau dvitīya-phala-pratipannako veditavyaḥ**

If, for one established in the fruit, one grade through as many as five grades have been abandoned, that practitioner is to be understood as a candidate for the second fruit.

So the range is exact:

```text
1 grade abandoned
2 grades abandoned
3 grades abandoned
4 grades abandoned
5 grades abandoned
    ↓
second-fruit candidate
```

This is a different situation from VAK 6.30, where prior abandonment before the Path of Seeing determined which fruit the practitioner approached during Seeing. Here the practitioner is already established in stream-entry and now advances sequentially through cultivation.

The same numerical state therefore has a different path-significance depending on **when** it occurs.

## 3. The Sixth Grade Is the Threshold

The second half states:

> **kṣīṇa-ṣaṣṭha-prakāras tu sakṛdāgāmy asau punaḥ**

When the sixth grade has been exhausted, that practitioner is now a **sakṛdāgāmin**, a once-returner.

The Bhāṣya glosses simply:

> **dvitīya-phalaprāpto bhavati** — one has attained the second fruit.

Thus:

```text
1–5 grades abandoned
    → pratipannaka toward second fruit

6th grade abandoned
    → second fruit attained
    → sakṛdāgāmin
```

The sixth grade therefore functions as a genuine threshold. It does not merely add one more quantitative unit; crossing it changes practitioner-status.

## 4. Why “Once-Returner”?

Vasubandhu explains the designation:

> **devān gatvā sakṛn manuṣya-lokāgamanāt sakṛdāgāmī**

The practitioner is called **sakṛdāgāmin** because, after going among the gods, there is one further coming to the human world.

The classification is therefore tied to the remaining structure of rebirth:

```text
second fruit
    ↓
maximum remaining circuit
    ↓
one further return to the human world
```

This does not mean that the practitioner is defined merely by geography or cosmology. The rebirth count is an external expression of a determinate degree of remaining afflictive causality.

## 5. Why Only One More Birth?

The Bhāṣya gives two connected explanations:

> **pareṇa janmābhāvāt** — because there is no further birth beyond that limit.

and:

> **rāga-dveṣa-mohānāṃ ca tanutvād ity ucyate** — it is also said because greed, hatred, and delusion have become attenuated.

Vasubandhu then specifies the reason for that attenuation:

> **mṛdu-prakārāvaśeṣatvāt** — because only the mild grades remain.

This is a very important connection between the ninefold grading and the traditional fruit-name.

The once-returner is not simply “someone scheduled for one more rebirth.” Rather:

```text
six grades destroyed
    ↓
only mild grades remain
    ↓
rāga / dveṣa / moha are attenuated
    ↓
rebirth-generating force is correspondingly reduced
    ↓
one-return status
```

The cosmological classification therefore rests on an internal causal condition.

## 6. The Ninefold Scale Is Now Functionally Active

VAK 6.33 introduced the ninefold grading abstractly. VAK 6.35 shows it operating as a threshold system.

```text
stronger/coarser grades
    abandoned first

progress through grades 1–5
    candidate-status changes
    but fruit does not yet change

6th grade destroyed
    qualitative threshold crossed
    second fruit attained
```

This clarifies why the grading cannot be treated as a mere list of nine equal units.

The sequence is ordered by function:

```text
grade-count
    → degree of residual affliction
    → path-status
    → fruit-status
    → remaining rebirth-range
```

## 7. A Path-Person Is Defined by State and Transition

The same individual continuum can now carry several successive designations:

```text
stream-enterer established in first fruit
    ↓
abandons grades 1–5
    ↓
second-fruit candidate
    ↓
abandons sixth grade
    ↓
once-returner
```

This confirms an important feature of Vasubandhu's path-person classifications: they are **state-sensitive and transition-sensitive**, not fixed personal types.

The designation changes when a determinate causal threshold is crossed.

## Path-Knowledge Determination

The local process can be represented as:

```text
phalastha
    first fruit possessed
        ↓

renewed bhāvanā
    cultivation resumes
        ↓

1–5 grades abandoned
    residual affliction progressively reduced
        ↓

dvitīya-phala-pratipannaka
    second fruit becomes the active target
        ↓

6th grade abandoned
    threshold crossed
        ↓

sakṛdāgāmin
    second fruit possessed
        ↓

only mild grades remain
    rāga / dveṣa / moha attenuated
        ↓

at most one return to the human world
```

This verse therefore links four things that earlier verses had treated separately:

- numerical grade of abandonment;
- current path-position;
- fruit-attainment;
- remaining rebirth-potential.

The scientific structure is becoming explicit:

```text
internal causal state
    determines
path designation
    determines
fruit-status
    correlates with
future-life range
```

## New Technical Terms

- **sakṛdāgāmin** — once-returner.
  - **status:** established noble-fruit designation, explicitly derived here.
  - **function:** names the practitioner who has attained the second fruit after destruction of the sixth cultivation-abandonable grade.

- **dvitīyaphalaprāpta** — one who has attained the second fruit.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression.
  - **function:** makes explicit that destruction of the sixth grade marks fruit-attainment rather than mere candidacy.

- **tanutva** — attenuation, thinning.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** characterizes the reduced force of greed, hatred, and delusion in the once-returner.

- **mṛduprakārāvaśeṣatva** — the condition in which only mild grades remain.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory phrase.
  - **function:** gives the causal basis for the attenuation of the principal afflictions.

## Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Vasubandhu reporting the standard path classification.

The rebirth explanation should not be detached from the afflictive grading. The Bhāṣya itself connects the once-return designation with the attenuation of greed, hatred, and delusion because only the mild grades remain.

The text does not here fully analyze which precise subgrades correspond to the sixth destruction; that ordering is inherited from the ninefold scheme already introduced.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.35 applies the ninefold abandonment scale to sequential progress after stream-entry:

- A practitioner established in the first fruit who abandons one through five grades becomes a candidate for the second fruit.
- Destruction of the sixth grade marks attainment of the second fruit itself.
- The practitioner is then called a **sakṛdāgāmin**, once-returner.
- The name is explained by the limited remaining rebirth-range and by the attenuation of greed, hatred, and delusion.
- That attenuation is itself explained by the fact that only the mild grades of the relevant afflictions remain.

Thus the verse gives a threshold relation:

```text
1–5 grades destroyed
    → progress toward second fruit

6th grade destroyed
    → second fruit attained
    → once-returner
```

The ninefold scale is now functioning as a causal transition system rather than as a merely descriptive taxonomy.
