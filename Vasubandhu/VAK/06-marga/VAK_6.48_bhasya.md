# VAK 6.48 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dhyānāt sāmantakād vāntyo muktimārgas tribhūjaye /  
> nordhvaṃ sāmantakād āryair aṣṭābhiḥ svordhvabhūjayaḥ // VAkK_6.48 //

**Best current translation:**  
In the conquest of three grounds, the final liberation-path may arise from the fundamental dhyāna or from its preliminary concentration. Above that, it does not arise from a preliminary concentration. By the eight noble uncontaminated bases, one conquers one's own and higher grounds.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_6.48.md`](./VAK_6.48.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 367|13–368|07.

This passage continues the distinction opened in VAK 6.47 between the meditative basis from which an uncontaminated path arises and the range of grounds over which that path can produce dispassion.

## 1. The Question: Must Every Liberation-Path Remain in the Sāmantaka?

**Voice:** Objector / questioner

Vasubandhu begins with a precise technical question:

> `atha yaḥ sāmantakaṃ niśrityādharabhūmivairāgyaṃ prāpnoti kimasyānantaryamārgavatsarve vimuktimārgāḥ sāmantakādbhavanti`

If a practitioner, relying on a **sāmantaka** (preliminary / neighboring concentration), attains dispassion from a lower ground, do all of that practitioner's liberation-paths arise from the sāmantaka, just as the uninterrupted paths do?

The answer is:

> `na`

No.

This immediately preserves an important distinction:

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    and
vimukti-mārga

need not always arise from exactly the same meditative basis
```

The path-series is functionally continuous without being mechanically tied to one identical local support at every moment.

## 2. The Final Liberation-Path in the Conquest of Three Grounds

The kārikā states:

> `dhyānāt sāmantakād vāntyo muktimārgas tribhūjaye`

Vasubandhu first defines the field.

There are nine `upapatti-bhūmis`, grounds of rebirth:

```text
1. kāmadhātu
2–5. the four dhyānas
6–9. the four ārūpyas
```

He then explains:

> `tatra yāvat dvitīyadhyānavairāgyaṃ tribhūmijayaḥ`

The **conquest of three grounds** extends through dispassion from the second dhyāna.

That is:

```text
kāmadhātu
    ↓
first dhyāna
    ↓
second dhyāna

= tribhūmi-jaya
```

In this range, the **last liberation-path** may arise either:

```text
from the sāmantaka
or
from the fundamental dhyāna (maula-dhyāna)
```

Vasubandhu says:

> `tasmin paścimo vimuktimārgaḥ sāmantakādbhavati dhyānādvā maulāt`

Thus the first half of the verse is not a general statement that every liberation-path freely alternates between preliminary and fundamental concentration. It concerns specifically the **final liberation-path** in the conquest of these first three grounds.

## 3. Why This Dual Possibility Exists

The Bhāṣya explains the reason through the faculties (*indriya*).

Above the conquest of three grounds:

> `maulādeva na punaḥ sāmantakāt`

—the final liberation-path arises only from the fundamental concentration, not again from the sāmantaka.

Why?

> `upekṣendriyasāmānyāt`

because there is commonality of the equanimity faculty (*upekṣendriya*).

But in the first three dhyāna-level relations, the sāmantaka and the fundamental concentration can differ in faculty:

> `triṣu hi dhyāneṣu sāmantakamaulayor indriyabhedāt`

Because of this difference, some practitioners cannot immediately enter the fundamental dhyāna.

Vasubandhu gives the reason:

> `indriyasaṃcārasya duṣkaratvāt`

because **transition of faculty is difficult**.

Hence:

> `atas tribhūmivairāgye dhyānasāmantakād apy antyo vimuktimārgo bhavati`

In dispassion from these three grounds, the final liberation-path can arise either from the dhyāna or from the sāmantaka.

This is an important practical nuance. The distinction of supports is not arbitrary; it responds to the concrete constitution of the practitioner's faculties.

```text
path-function fixed
    ↓
local meditative support variable
    according to
indriya-capacity
```

## 4. Above the Three-Ground Conquest: Not from the Sāmantaka

The kārikā's next phrase is:

> `nordhvaṃ sāmantakāt`

Vasubandhu glosses:

> `tribhūmijayādūrdhvaṃ maulādeva na punaḥ sāmantakāt`

Above the conquest of the three grounds, the final liberation-path arises from the **fundamental concentration itself**, not from the preliminary concentration.

So the verse establishes a limit to the flexibility just described:

```text
through conquest of three grounds:
    final vimukti-mārga
        = sāmantaka OR maula

above that:
    final vimukti-mārga
        = maula only
```

This sharply corrects any loose reading in which preliminary concentration could function universally as the immediate support of every final liberation-path.

## 5. The Question Left by VAK 6.47

Vasubandhu then explicitly recalls the preceding verse:

> `anāsraveṇa vairāgyam anāgamyena sarvata ityuktam anyaistu noktam / ata ucyate`

It has been stated that, through the uncontaminated path based on **anāgamya**, dispassion can be attained from all grounds. But nothing had yet been said about the other meditative bases.

Therefore the kārikā adds:

> `āryair aṣṭābhiḥ svordhvabhūjayaḥ`

This is the real second doctrine of VAK 6.48.

## 6. The Eight Noble Uncontaminated Bases

Vasubandhu explains the `aṣṭābhiḥ` as eight uncontaminated meditative bases:

> `anāsravair aṣṭābhir dhyānadhyānāntarārūpyaiḥ`

These are:

```text
four dhyānas
    +
one dhyānāntara
    +
three relevant ārūpya bases
    = eight
```

The exact arithmetic follows the Abhidharma distribution of meditative bases capable of supporting the noble uncontaminated path; bhavāgra itself does not function as the required uncontaminated counteractive basis against itself.

The important functional claim is:

> `svasyā ūrdhvāyāśca bhūmer vairāgyaṃ nādharāyā vītarāgatvāt`

By each of these eight, one may attain dispassion from:

```text
its own ground
and
higher grounds
```

but **not lower grounds**.

Why not lower grounds?

> `vītarāgatvāt`

because the practitioner is already dispassionate from them.

Thus the directionality of the noble cultivation path is structurally irreversible:

```text
lower ground already overcome
    ↓
current noble basis
    ↓
own ground or higher ground can be conquered

not:
    return downward to reconquer what is already abandoned
```

## 7. The Eight Bases Do Not Merely Correspond One-to-One with Eight Objects

The phrase `svordhvabhūjayaḥ` is important.

It does not mean:

```text
one basis
    → only its own ground
```

Rather:

```text
one noble uncontaminated basis
    → its own ground
    + potentially higher grounds
```

This continues and generalizes VAK 6.47. A meditative support does not determine the path's range by simple identity.

The relation is asymmetric:

```text
support-ground
    ≤
range of liberating operation
```

The path may reach upward beyond its immediate support, provided the relevant uncontaminated cognition is available.

## 8. Supramundane Paths Operate through the Truths

The Bhāṣya closes the verse with an important determination:

> `tatra lokottarā ānantaryavimuktimārgāḥ satyālambanatvāt satyākārapravṛttā iti siddham`

The supramundane uninterrupted paths and liberation-paths operate in the **modes of the truths** because the truths are their object-support.

This explicitly grounds the liberating efficacy of the path in cognition of the truths:

```text
ārya path
    ↓
object-support = satya
    ↓
operation in satya-ākāras
    ↓
counteractive abandonment / liberation
```

This is decisive for the chapter's science of the Path. The noble path is not simply a stronger meditative state. Its distinctive operation is determined by its **truth-object and truth-form**.

That is why a relatively lower meditative basis can support a path whose counteractive range extends upward beyond that basis.

## 9. Path-Knowledge Determination

VAK 6.48 establishes three distinct variables that must not be collapsed:

```text
1. meditative support
    sāmantaka / maula / one of eight noble bases

2. path-function
    ānantarya-mārga / vimukti-mārga

3. range of conquest
    own ground / higher grounds
```

Their relations are constrained, but they are not identical.

The Bhāṣya therefore yields a much more precise architecture than a simple vertical ladder of meditation:

```text
meditative basis
    ↓ supports
truth-determined noble cognition
    ↓ functions as
uninterrupted or liberation path
    ↓ produces
abandonment / attained separation
    ↓ across
own and higher grounds
```

The meditative basis supplies the operational condition; the **truth-determined noble path** supplies the liberating determination.

## 10. Organon Contact

The restrained Organon contact is especially clear here.

A local basis is not identical with the scope or principle of the operation it supports.

```text
local meditative support
    ≠
logical range of liberating cognition
```

The Bhāṣya itself supplies the reason: supramundane paths operate through the forms of the truths because the truths are their object-support.

Thus the universal reach of the path is not produced merely by climbing to an ever-higher empirical state. The determining form lies in the truth-cognition that operates from the basis.

For the Rational Kośa this is a strong textual anchor for distinguishing:

```text
condition of operation
from
principle of determination
```

without importing a foreign architecture into the primary analysis.

## 11. New Technical Terms

- **tribhūmi-jaya** — conquest of three grounds; here the span through dispassion from the second dhyāna.
  - **status:** clarified by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** delimits the range in which the final liberation-path may arise either from sāmantaka or fundamental dhyāna.

- **maula-dhyāna** — fundamental / principal dhyāna.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of `dhyānāt`.
  - **function:** the fully established concentration contrasted with its preliminary sāmantaka.

- **indriya-saṃcāra** — transition / shift of faculty.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this context.
  - **function:** explains why some practitioners cannot pass immediately from sāmantaka to the fundamental dhyāna.

- **upekṣendriya-sāmānya** — commonality of the equanimity faculty.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory formulation.
  - **function:** explains why above the three-ground range the final liberation-path arises from the fundamental basis rather than the sāmantaka.

- **svordhvabhū-jaya** — conquest of one's own and higher grounds.
  - **status:** kārikā technical compound explicitly unpacked in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** defines the upward range of the eight noble uncontaminated bases.

- **satyālambanatva** — having the truths as object-support.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory determination.
  - **function:** explains why supramundane paths operate in truth-forms.

- **satyākāra-pravṛtti** — operation in the modes / forms of the truths.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory determination.
  - **function:** identifies the cognitive form of supramundane uninterrupted and liberation paths.

## 12. Voice / Doctrinal Caution

**Voice:** Objector / questioner for the opening question about whether all liberation-paths must arise from the sāmantaka.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu for the resolution, the three-ground limitation, the faculty explanation, the eight noble bases, and the truth-object determination of the supramundane paths.

The precise list represented by `aṣṭābhiḥ` belongs to Abhidharma meditative systematics, but the structural point is entirely clear from the Bhāṣya:

```text
noble uncontaminated bases
    conquer
own + higher grounds

never lower grounds already abandoned
```

## Determinate Summary

VAK 6.48 refines the relation between meditative basis and liberating operation. When dispassion through the first three grounds is being completed, the **final liberation-path** may arise either from the preliminary concentration or from the corresponding fundamental dhyāna, because transition of faculties can be difficult. Above this range, however, that final liberation-path arises only from the fundamental concentration.

The verse then generalizes VAK 6.47 beyond anāgamya. Eight noble uncontaminated meditative bases can produce dispassion from their **own and higher grounds**, though not from lower grounds that have already been abandoned. Their liberating range therefore exceeds simple identity with their local meditative support.

Finally, Vasubandhu states why the supramundane path has this distinctive character: its uninterrupted and liberation paths have the **truths as their object-support** and therefore operate in the **forms of the truths**.

The resulting architecture is precise:

```text
meditative basis
    ↓
supports noble cognition
    ↓
truth-object determines path-form
    ↓
ānantarya / vimukti function
    ↓
conquest of own and higher grounds
```

The verse therefore strengthens the distinction already emerging in VAK 6.47: **the support of liberation is not the principle or range of liberation.**
