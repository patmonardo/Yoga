## Natural Argument

### 1. The question: in which vijñāna do death and rebirth occur?

Vasubandhu has already explained how beings arise through dependent co-arising, how they persist, and how death occurs through causes such as exhaustion of lifespan. He now asks a more precise question:

```text
In which vijñāna, while it is occurring,
do death and rebirth take place?
```

The Kārikā answers by first identifying a set of six events that belong exclusively to `manovijñāna`.

### 2. The six events are explicitly unpacked

The Bhāṣya removes the ambiguity of the compact compound:

```text
cheda
    = kuśalamūla-samuccheda
    = cutting off the wholesome roots

saṃdhāna
    = kuśalamūla-pratisaṃdhāna
    = reconnection of the wholesome roots

vairāgya
    = dhātu-bhūmi-vairāgya
    = dispassion from a realm or level

hāni
    = parihāṇi
    = decline / falling away

cyuti
    = death / falling away from the present existence

upapatti
    = rebirth / arising into another existence
```

Vasubandhu then states directly:

```iast
ete ṣaṭ dharmā manovijñāna eveṣyante nānyatra
```

“These six dharmas are admitted only in mental knowledge, nowhere else.”

The phrase `upapatti` also includes the linking (`pratisaṃdhi`) of the intermediate existence previously described.

### 3. Death and arising occur only with upekṣā

The Kārikā then determines the accompanying feeling:

```iast
upekṣāyāṃ cyutodbhavau
```

Vasubandhu glosses:

```text
cyuti = cyuta
upapatti = udbhava
```

Death and arising occur only with `aduḥkhāsukha-vedanā`, neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, because this feeling is not sharp or intense (`apaṭu`).

Pleasant and painful feelings are `paṭu`, acute or forceful. Vasubandhu argues that death and rebirth do not appropriately occur while vijñāna is in such an acute condition.

Thus `upekṣā` here is not merely a broad ethical attitude. It is the neutral feeling-tone appropriate to the transition of death and rebirth.

### 4. Not in concentrated mind and not in absence of mind

Even within `manovijñāna`, Vasubandhu further restricts the condition.

Death and arising do not occur in an `ekāgra` or concentrated mind. A person whose mind is collected in samādhi does not die or take rebirth in that very state.

The Bhāṣya gives three reasons concerning concentrated mind:

```text
visabhāga-bhūmikatva
    → it belongs to a dissimilar / different level

abhisaṃskārikatva
    → it is actively constructed / deliberately produced

anugrāhakatva
    → it is supportive rather than conducive to dissolution
```

Nor do death and rebirth occur when there is no mind (`acitta`). One who is without mind cannot be directly struck down in that very mindless condition. When the bodily support begins to undergo fatal alteration, mind necessarily becomes manifest again, bound to that support, and only afterward does death occur.

Likewise rebirth cannot be mindless: there is no cause for a break in mind at rebirth, and rebirth does not occur without affliction.

### 5. The arhat dies in an indeterminate mind

The Bhāṣya finally specifies the arhat's case:

```iast
nirvāty avyākṛtadvaye
```

The arhat enters final extinction in one of two indeterminate (`avyākṛta`) minds:

```text
airyāpathika
    → mind associated with ordinary bodily deportment

vipāka-citta
    → maturation-result mind
```

If there is an appropriate neutral maturation-result in the desire realm, death may occur in that; otherwise it occurs in an `airyāpathika` mind.

Why indeterminate rather than wholesome or unwholesome? Because an indeterminate mind, being weak (`durbala`), is conducive to the cutting off of the mental series (`citta-cheda-anukūla`).

## Literal Prose Translation

It has been explained how dependent co-arising operates for beings, how they persist, and also how death occurs through such causes as the exhaustion of lifespan. Now this must be stated: while which vijñāna is occurring do death and rebirth take place?

The cutting off of wholesome roots, their reconnection, dispassion from a realm or level, decline, death, and rebirth are accepted only in mental knowledge. These six dharmas are admitted only in manovijñāna and nowhere else. By mentioning rebirth, the linking of the intermediate existence described above is also to be understood.

As regards feeling, death and arising occur with neutral feeling. Death is `cyuti`; rebirth is `udbhava`. Both occur only with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling because that feeling is not acute. The other two feelings are acute, and death or rebirth is not appropriate while vijñāna is in an acute state.

Even within manovijñāna, death and arising do not occur in concentrated mind. There is no death or rebirth for one whose mind is collected in samādhi, because such a state belongs to a different level, is deliberately produced, and is supportive. Nor do they occur in a mindless state. When the bodily support begins to alter fatally, a mind bound to that support must first become manifest and only afterward can death occur. At rebirth, likewise, mindlessness is impossible, because there is no cause for a break in mind and because rebirth does not occur without affliction.

As for the arhat, final extinction occurs in one of two indeterminate minds: an `airyāpathika` mind or a maturation-result mind. The indeterminate mind is suitable for the cutting off of the mental series because of its weakness.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAkK 3.42 is substantially more precise in the Bhāṣya than the compact verse suggests.

The first half is not a generic list of “transition-functions.” It names six specifically defined events:

```text
cutting wholesome roots
reconnecting wholesome roots
dispassion from realm/level
decline
death
rebirth
```

All six occur only in `manovijñāna`.

The second half then narrows the death/rebirth case still further:

```text
locus:
    manovijñāna

feeling:
    aduḥkhāsukha / upekṣā

not:
    acute pleasant or painful feeling
    concentrated mind
    mindless state
```

The Bhāṣya therefore analyzes death and rebirth as highly determinate mental events rather than as merely biological endpoints.

The arhat's death receives a separate determination: final extinction occurs in a weak, indeterminate mind appropriate to the termination of the mental series.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The compact terms should now be expanded in translation rather than left generic:

```text

    → cutting off of wholesome roots

saṃdhāna
    → reconnection of wholesome roots

vairāgya
    → dispassion from a realm or level

hāni
    → decline

    → death / falling away

upapatti
    → rebirth
```

And:

```text

```

should be read technically as:

> Death and rebirth occur while neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling is present.

This completes the Bhāṣya unit for VAkK 3.42.
