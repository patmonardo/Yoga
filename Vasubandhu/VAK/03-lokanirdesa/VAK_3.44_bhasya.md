## Natural Argument

### 1. The sentient world is divided into three groups

Having described the arising, persistence, and passing away of the sentient world (`sattvaloka`), Vasubandhu says that the Blessed One placed beings into three `rāśis`:

```text
samyaktva-niyata-rāśi
    fixed with respect to rightness

mithyātva-niyata-rāśi
    fixed with respect to wrongness

aniyata-rāśi
    not fixed
```

The verse identifies the paradigmatic members of the first two groups.

### 2. What is `samyaktva`?

The Bhāṣya quotes a sūtra definition:

```text
samyaktva
    = complete abandonment of rāga
    = complete abandonment of dveṣa
    = complete abandonment of moha
    = complete abandonment of all kleśas
```

Thus `samyaktva` is not merely correct opinion or moral rectitude. In this passage it is defined by the exhaustion of the afflictions.

### 3. Why are the āryas fixed in samyaktva?

Vasubandhu asks who the `āryas` are and answers:

```text
those in whom the anāsrava-mārga has arisen
```

They are called noble because they have become distant from evil dharmas and have attained the acquisition of final disjunction (`ātyantika-visaṃyoga-prāpti`).

They are `samyaktva-niyata` because they are fixed with respect to the destruction of the afflictions (`kleśakṣaya`).

Thus the force of `niyata` is not simply that they currently possess a wholesome state. Their course is determined toward the exhaustion of affliction.

### 4. Why not everyone who has acquired liberation-conducive roots?

The Bhāṣya introduces an important qualification. Those who have acquired `mokṣabhāgīya` dharmas will eventually attain parinirvāṇa, but Vasubandhu asks why they are not therefore already called fixed in samyaktva.

His answer is that such persons could still become fixed in wrongness. Moreover, their attainment of samyaktva is not temporally fixed in the strict manner of beings such as the `saptakṛtvaḥparama`.

Therefore:

```text
future liberation is certain
    ≠
already fixed in samyaktva
```

The technical classification concerns a stronger determination than eventual liberation alone.

### 5. What is `mithyātva`?

The Bhāṣya defines `mithyātva` here through the three bad destinies:

```text
naraka
preta
tiryañc
```

Those who perform an `ānantarya` action are fixed in wrongness because they are fixed for rebirth in hell.

Thus:

```text
ānantaryakārin
    → niyata for naraka
    → mithyātva-niyata
```

### 6. The third group: `aniyata`

Those who belong to neither fixed group are `aniyata`.

Vasubandhu states why:

```text
pratyayāpekṣam
```

Their becoming one or the other—or neither—depends upon conditions.

The third group is therefore defined precisely by the absence of fixed determination toward either of the two extremes.

### 7. End of `sattvaloka`

The Bhāṣya closes the section explicitly:

```iast
uktaḥ sattvaloko bhājanaloka idānīṃ vaktavyaḥ
```

“The sentient world has been explained; now the container-world is to be explained.”

This gives a decisive structural boundary. VAkK 3.45 begins the next major section, `bhājanaloka`.

## Literal Prose Translation

This sentient world, arising, persisting, and passing away in this manner, was placed by the Blessed One into three groups. There are three groups: the group fixed in rightness, the group fixed in wrongness, and the group not fixed.

Among these, the noble ones are fixed in rightness, while those who perform an immediate-result action are fixed in wrongness.

What is rightness? The sūtra says: “That which consists in the complete abandonment of attachment, the complete abandonment of hatred, the complete abandonment of delusion, the complete abandonment of all afflictions—this is called rightness.”

Who are the noble ones? Those in whom the path without outflows has arisen. They are called noble because they have become distant from evil dharmas, through attaining the acquisition of final disjunction. Since they are fixed with respect to the exhaustion of the afflictions, they are fixed in rightness.

Even those who have acquired dharmas conducive to liberation will necessarily attain parinirvāṇa. Why, then, are they not called fixed in rightness? Because they could also become fixed in wrongness, and they are not temporally fixed with respect to rightness in the way that beings such as those with at most seven returns are.

What is wrongness? Hell beings, pretas, and animals are called wrongness. Those who perform an immediate-result action are fixed in wrongness because they are fixed for hell.

Those other than the fixed groups are established as not fixed, for whether they become participants in either, or in neither, depends upon conditions.

The sentient world has now been explained. The container-world is next to be explained.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — framing and explanatory prose throughout.
- **Voice: Sūtra quotation** — definition of `samyaktva` through complete abandonment of the afflictions.
- No independent objector is required for the rhetorical questions in this unit; they function as Vasubandhu's internal exposition.

## New Technical Terms

- **rāśi** — group / class
  - **status:** Bhāṣya term framing the Kārikā
  - **function:** threefold classification of beings

- **samyaktva-niyata** — fixed in rightness
  - **status:** Kārikā term explained by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** classification of āryas according to fixed orientation toward kleśa-exhaustion

- **mithyātva-niyata** — fixed in wrongness
  - **status:** Kārikā term explained by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** classification of ānantarya-doers through fixed hell rebirth

- **aniyata** — not fixed / indeterminate
  - **status:** Bhāṣya framing term
  - **function:** third group, whose determination depends upon conditions

- **anāsrava-mārga** — path without outflows
  - **status:** previously encountered vocabulary, decisive here
  - **function:** criterion for ārya status

- **ātyantika-visaṃyoga-prāpti** — acquisition of final disjunction
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation here
  - **function:** explains the nobility of the ārya and irreversible separation from evil dharmas

- **mokṣabhāgīya** — conducive to liberation / participating in liberation
  - **status:** technical Bhāṣya term
  - **function:** marks beings certain eventually to attain parinirvāṇa but not yet necessarily `samyaktva-niyata`

- **ānantaryakārin** — performer of an immediate-result action
  - **status:** Kārikā term explained by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** paradigmatic member of the `mithyātva-niyata` group

- **pratyayāpekṣa** — dependent upon conditions
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** explains the `aniyata` group

## Doctrinal Determination

The three `rāśis` are not simply ethical labels. They distinguish beings according to whether their course has become fixed toward one of two outcomes.

```text
samyaktva-niyata
    → ārya
    → anāsrava-mārga arisen
    → fixed toward kleśakṣaya

mithyātva-niyata
    → ānantaryakārin
    → fixed toward naraka

aniyata
    → neither fixed determination
    → outcome depends on pratyayas
```

The distinction between `mokṣabhāgīya` and `samyaktva-niyata` is especially important. Eventual liberation does not by itself establish present fixity in rightness.

The Bhāṣya therefore uses `niyata` in a strong technical sense: a determinate course has already been established.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya supports reading the compact verse as an ordered identification:

```text
samyaktva-niyata
    → āryāḥ

mithyātva-niyata
    → ānantaryakāriṇaḥ
```

Thus a clean translation is:

> The noble ones are fixed in rightness; those who perform immediate-result actions are fixed in wrongness.

The third category, `aniyata`, is supplied by the preceding prose and explained after the verse rather than stated in the Kārikā itself.

This completes the Bhāṣya treatment of `sattvaloka` in this block. VAkK 3.45 begins `bhājanaloka`.
