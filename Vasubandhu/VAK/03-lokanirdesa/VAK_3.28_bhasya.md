## Natural Argument

### 1. `Pratītyasamutpāda` and `pratītyasamutpanna` are relational determinations

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The sūtra distinguishes:

```text
pratītyasamutpāda
pratītyasamutpanna dharmāḥ
```

Vasubandhu asks what the distinction amounts to.

At the level of the śāstra, he first says that both belong to conditioned dharmas. But the **intention of the sūtra** (`sūtrābhiprāya`) is to distinguish them according to causal role:

```text
pratītyasamutpāda
    = the member insofar as it is cause

pratītyasamutpanna
    = the member insofar as it is result
```

The crucial point is that **the same member may stand under both descriptions**, depending on the relation considered.

```text
A relative to B:
    A = cause
    B = result

B relative to C:
    B = cause
    C = result
```

This does not create disorder (`avyavasthā`) because the standpoint of dependence changes (`apekṣā-bheda`).

Vasubandhu's analogies are simple and strong:

```text
cause / result
father / son
```

A man can be a son relative to his father and a father relative to his own child. The predicates are relational, not mutually exclusive substances.

### 2. The Bhāṣya then asks what `avidyā` actually means

**Voice: Vasubandhu / dialectical exposition**

The first proposal is etymologically obvious:

```iast
yā na vidyā
```

> That which is not vidyā.

But this is immediately rejected as too broad. If `avidyā` meant simply “whatever is not vidyā,” then the eye and other non-cognitive dharmas would count as avidyā.

```text
not vidyā
    ≠ sufficient definition of avidyā
```

### 3. Avidyā is also not the mere absence of vidyā

A second proposal is privative:

```text
avidyā = absence of vidyā
```

Vasubandhu rejects this too.

If avidyā were mere absence, it would not be a positive dharma capable of functioning as a condition in dependent co-arising.

This is why the Kārikā concludes:

```iast
vidyāvipakṣo dharmo 'nyaḥ
```

> It is another dharma opposed to vidyā.

### 4. `Vipakṣa` means determinate contrary, not everything other than the term

The examples explain the logical form.

An `amitra`, an enemy/non-friend, is not simply **anything other than a friend** and not merely the **absence of a friend**. It is something standing opposed to friendship.

Likewise `anṛta`, untruth, is not merely absence of truth; it is speech opposed to truth.

And similarly:

```text
adharma
anartha
akārya
```

are taken as determinate counterparts of:

```text
dharma
artha
kārya
```

Thus:

```text
avidyā
    ≠ any non-vidyā dharma
    ≠ mere vidyā-absence
    = vidyā-pratidvandvin dharmāntara
```

Avidyā is a distinct dharma standing in opposition to vidyā.

### 5. Why must avidyā be a positive dharma?

Vasubandhu gives two doctrinal reasons.

First:

```iast
pratyayabhāvenopadeśāt
```

It is taught as having the status of a condition.

That already requires more than sheer absence.

Second, the sūtras call avidyā:

```text
saṃyojana    fetter
bandhana     bondage
anuśaya      latent tendency
ogha         flood
Yoga         yoke/bond
```

A mere absence could not appropriately perform these functions.

Therefore:

```iast
tasmād dharmāntaram evāvidyā
```

> Therefore avidyā is indeed another dharma.

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

The sūtra distinguishes dependent co-arising from the dependently arisen dharmas. Its intention is relational: a member is called dependent co-arising insofar as it functions as a cause from which another member arises, and it is called dependently arisen insofar as it stands as a result. Thus every member can be determined in both ways according to the relation considered, just as the same person can be a father relative to one person and a son relative to another.

What, then, is ignorance? It cannot mean merely “that which is not knowledge,” since the eye and innumerable other dharmas would then be ignorance. Nor can it mean merely the absence of knowledge, since sheer absence cannot function in the manner attributed to ignorance. Ignorance must therefore be understood as another dharma standing in determinate opposition to knowledge.

An enemy is not simply anything that is not a friend, nor the mere absence of friendship; it is something opposed to a friend. Untruth is likewise opposed to truth. In the same manner, ignorance is a dharma opposed to knowledge.

This is further confirmed because ignorance is taught as a condition and is described in the sūtras as fetter, bondage, latent tendency, flood, and yoke. Such determinations cannot belong to mere absence. Avidyā is therefore a distinct operative dharma.

---

## The Crucial Result: Avidyā Is an Operative Opposition to Knowledge

The Kārikā pass correctly saw that avidyā cannot be reduced to blank non-knowing. The Bhāṣya gives the exact argument.

Three possibilities are separated:

```text
1. everything other than vidyā
       too broad

2. mere absence of vidyā
       causally inert

3. dharma opposed to vidyā
       accepted
```

The third is the doctrinal determination.

This matters immediately for the micro-theory of karma developed in 3.26–3.27.

```text
avidyā
    is kleśa
    ↓
conditions saṃskāra / karma
    ↓
produces vastu / result
```

For that role, avidyā must be something capable of entering causal relations. Vasubandhu therefore refuses to make ignorance a simple blank.

---

## A Second Crucial Result: Cause and Result Are Roles Within a Relation

The first half of the verse supplies a different but complementary logical result.

```text
samutpāda
    cause-role

samutpanna
    result-role
```

These are not two fixed populations of dharmas.

The same conditioned member can be:

```text
result of what precedes it
        and
cause of what follows it
```

So:

```text
A → B → C

B as related to A:
    samutpanna / result

B as related to C:
    samutpāda / cause
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly grounds this flexibility in `apekṣā-bheda`, difference of relation or standpoint.

This is another reason the twelvefold series cannot be read as a row of independently fixed substances.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — reconstructs the sūtra's intended cause/result distinction and gives the accepted definition of avidyā as a distinct dharma opposed to vidyā.

**Voice: dialectical proposal / objector** — “avidyā is simply what is not vidyā”; “avidyā is absence of vidyā.” These are raised and rejected.

**Voice: scriptural support** — descriptions of avidyā as `saṃyojana`, `bandhana`, `anuśaya`, `ogha`, and `yoga` support its positive operative status.

## New Technical Terms

- **sūtrābhiprāya** — the intention or purport of the sūtra.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reflective vocabulary.
  - **function:** distinguishes the relational causal intention of the sūtra from a simple śāstric classification.

- **apekṣā-bheda** — difference according to relation, reference, or standpoint.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only logical vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** explains how the same member can be cause in one relation and result in another without disorder.

- **vipakṣa** — opposed factor, contrary.
  - **status:** Kārikā term extensively clarified in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** defines avidyā as determinate opposition to vidyā rather than mere absence.

- **pratidvandvin / pratidvandva-bhūta** — standing as a counterpart or adversarial opposite.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only logical vocabulary.
  - **function:** specifies the kind of opposition involved in `vipakṣa`.

- **dharmāntara** — another/distinct dharma.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** affirms avidyā's positive distinctness from vidyā and from mere privation.

- **pratyaya-bhāva** — status/function as a condition.
  - **status:** previously encountered vocabulary, decisive here.
  - **function:** reason avidyā cannot be a mere absence.

- **saṃyojana** — fetter.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya scriptural vocabulary.
  - **function:** one characterization establishing avidyā as an operative afflictive dharma.

- **bandhana** — bondage/bond.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya scriptural vocabulary.
  - **function:** characterization of avidyā's binding function.

- **ogha** — flood.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya scriptural vocabulary.
  - **function:** characterization of avidyā as an overwhelming afflictive factor.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.28 establishes two principles.

### Cause/result principle

```text
pratītyasamutpāda
    = member considered as hetu

pratītyasamutpanna
    = member considered as phala
```

The distinction is relational (`apekṣā-bheda`).

### Avidyā principle

```text
avidyā
    ≠ non-vidyā in general
    ≠ vidyā-abhāva
    = vidyā-vipakṣa dharmāntara
```

Because avidyā:

```text
functions as pratyaya
is called fetter
is called bondage
is called latent tendency
is called flood
is called yoke
```

it must be an operative afflictive dharma.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Refinement required.**

The first-pass translation “co-arising is the cause; the arisen is the fruit” is acceptable, but the Bhāṣya makes the relation more precise:

> A member is called dependent co-arising insofar as it functions as cause, and dependently arisen insofar as it functions as result.

For the second half, `vipakṣa` should be read strongly as **determinate opponent/contrary**, not merely “different from.”

A Bhāṣya-informed translation is therefore:

> Here a member, insofar as it is cause, is dependent co-arising; insofar as it is result, it is dependently arisen. Avidyā is a distinct dharma standing opposed to vidyā, as an enemy stands opposed to a friend and untruth to truth.

## Logical Determination

Two logical structures emerge.

### 1. Relational predication

```text
one determination
    ↓ relation to antecedent
result

same determination
    ↓ relation to consequent
cause
```

Cause and result are not intrinsic names attached once and for all. They are relational functions.

### 2. Determinate opposition

```text
vidyā
    ↔
avidyā
```

But not as:

```text
something
    ↔
nothing
```

Rather:

```text
positive determination
    ↔
operative contrary determination
```

This is exactly why avidyā can enter the causal grammar described in 3.27.

## Organon Light

The Organon Light sees two unusually valuable structures here, but neither requires identifying Vasubandhu with Hegel.

### A. Cause and result are View-relative functions

The same member can carry different rational roles according to the relation under which it is viewed:

```text
B viewed from A
    = result

B viewed toward C
    = cause
```

This looks like an elementary rule for an Agential View system:

```text
entity / event
    does not exhaust its intelligibility

relation selected
    ↓
functional determination appears
```

The Bhāṣya's own term `apekṣā-bheda` is enough to support the observation. The Organon need not import a foreign category to see that **determination depends upon articulated relation**.

### B. Error is not simply missing information

Avidyā is especially important for a future Science of Learning.

A learner can fail because knowledge is absent. But Vasubandhu's avidyā is stronger:

```text
not:
    Knowledge = 0

but:
    an operative contrary to Knowledge
```

So the practical problem is not always filling an empty slot.

```text
mere absence
    → supply information

operative contrary
    → requires opposition / transformation
```

That is exactly where the later Kośa vocabulary of `pratipakṣa` may become decisive.

The first-pass Organon hypothesis is therefore:

```text
avidyā
    = not merely informational deficiency
    = a positively operative distortion of the knowing-agent's field
```

That formulation remains provisional. But it follows the nose of Vasubandhu's argument rather than imposing a ready-made psychology upon it.
