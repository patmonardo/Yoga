## Natural Argument

### 1. Karma as a grain enclosed in husk (`tuṣitaṇḍula`)

The Bhāṣya first clarifies the compound. `Tuṣī` means something that possesses a husk. In the previous verse kleśa was identified with the husk; now karma is compared with the grain enclosed in that husk.

Thus the analogy must be read together with VAK 3.36:

```text
kleśa
    = husk

karma joined with kleśa
    = grain enclosed in husk
```

The point is not two independent images, “husk” and “grain.” It is the relation between affliction and karma that preserves karma's capacity to produce another birth.

### 2. Karma as plant (`auṣadhi`)

Some plants terminate in the ripening of their fruit. Likewise karmas, having ripened, do not go on producing further results of maturation.

The analogy emphasizes that karmic maturation reaches a terminus:

```text
karma
    → ripening (`vipāka`)
    → result
```

The same karma does not endlessly generate further vipākas once its maturation has occurred.

### 3. Karma as flower (`puṣpa`)

A flower is the proximate cause of the arising of fruit. Likewise karma is to be understood as proximate to the arising of its maturation-result.

The flower analogy therefore differs from the plant analogy:

```text
plant
    → maturation reaches completion

flower
    → immediate causal proximity to fruit
```

### 4. Vastu as prepared food and drink (`siddhānnapāna`)

Prepared food and drink are suited simply for consumption; they are not suited for germination.

So too `vastu` insofar as it is called `vipāka`:

```text
vipāka-vastu
    → result available for experience
    → does not itself generate another vipāka
```

Vasubandhu states this directly:

```iast
nahi punar vipākād vipākāntaraṃ janmāntareṣu pravarhate
```

A further result of maturation does not proceed from a maturation-result across subsequent births.

He then gives the decisive consequence:

```iast
yadi hi pravardheta mokṣo na syāt
```

If maturation-result itself went on producing another maturation-result, liberation would be impossible.

### 5. The four modes of becoming

The series of births produced through dependent co-arising does not pass beyond four modes of becoming:

```text
antarābhava
    intermediate becoming

upapattibhava
    rebirth / arising becoming

pūrvakālabhava
    prior-time / life-course becoming

maraṇabhava
    death becoming
```

Vasubandhu notes that these have already been explained. The phrase `tasmin bhavacatuṣṭaye` therefore locates the kleśa–karma–vastu process within this fourfold articulation of the birth-continuum.

The detailed properties of the four bhavas begin with the next Kārikā.

## Literal Prose Translation

Karma is like a grain enclosed in its husk, and likewise like a plant and like a flower. A thing possessing a husk is called `tuṣī`; affliction has already been said to occupy the place of the husk. Now karma is said to occupy the place of the grain enclosed in the husk.

There are plants whose culmination is the ripening of their fruit. In the same way karmas, once they have ripened, do not again produce further maturation-results. And just as a flower is the proximate cause of the arising of fruit, so karmas are to be understood in relation to the arising of maturation-result.

Vastu is like prepared food and drink. Just as prepared food and drink are suited only for consumption and not for sprouting again, so is vastu called maturation-result. Another maturation-result does not proceed from a maturation-result through subsequent births. If it did, liberation would not be possible.

This birth-continuum, arising in dependence in this way, does not go beyond the fourfold becoming: intermediate becoming, rebirth becoming, prior-time becoming, and death becoming. These have already been explained. [The process occurs] within that fourfold becoming.

## New Technical Terms

- **tuṣī** — possessing a husk
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific grammatical clarification
  - **function:** explains `tuṣitaṇḍula` as grain enclosed in husk

- **phalapāka** — ripening of fruit
  - **status:** local Bhāṣya expression
  - **function:** plant analogy for completion of karmic maturation

- **vipac** / **vipakva** — to ripen / having ripened
  - **status:** previously encountered through `vipāka`
  - **function:** describes maturation of karma

- **abhinirvartayati** — produces / brings forth
  - **status:** recurring technical verb
  - **function:** distinguishes production by karma from non-production by mature result

- **phalotpatti-āsanna-kāraṇa** — cause proximate to the arising of fruit
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanatory phrase
  - **function:** defines the force of the flower analogy

- **paribhoga** — consumption / enjoyment / use
  - **status:** local Bhāṣya usage
  - **function:** explains mature vastu as result available for experience

- **vipāka-ākhya vastu** — vastu called maturation-result
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** identifies the resultant status of vastu

- **janma-saṃtāna** — continuum of births
  - **status:** closely related to previously encountered `skandhasaṃtāna`
  - **function:** names the continuing birth-series articulated by the four bhavas

- **bhava-catuṣṭaya** — fourfold becoming
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly enumerated by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** division of the birth-continuum into four phases

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.37 completes the functional differentiation of `kleśa–karma–vastu` begun in 3.36.

The Bhāṣya gives karma two distinct causal determinations:

```text
karma as plant
    → ripens and terminates in its fruit

karma as flower
    → is proximate cause of the arising of fruit
```

The husked-grain image links karma back to kleśa:

```text
kleśa
    enables karma's reproductive capacity
```

But `vastu` as `vipāka` has a different status:

```text
karma
    → produces vipāka-vastu

vipāka-vastu
    ↛ further vipāka merely as vipāka
```

This distinction is essential to the possibility of liberation. If results automatically generated further results simply by being results, there would be no possible termination of the series.

The fourfold becoming then gives the temporal articulation within which this causal process occurs:

```text
antarābhava
→ upapattibhava
→ pūrvakālabhava
→ maraṇabhava
```

The next verse begins the detailed determination of these four modes.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first compound should not be rendered loosely as “like husk and grain.” The Bhāṣya clearly requires:

```text
tuṣitaṇḍulavat
    = like a grain enclosed in its husk
```

This preserves the connection with the immediately preceding explanation:

```text
kleśa = husk
karma = husked grain
```

Likewise `auṣadhi` and `puṣpa` should be kept as distinct similes because Vasubandhu assigns them different functions:

- plant — maturation culminates in fruit;
- flower — proximate cause of fruit-arising.

Finally, `siddhānnapānavat vastu` should be understood as a result that is available for consumption/experience rather than for renewed germination.

This completes the Bhāṣya explanation of VAkK 3.37. The detailed analysis of `bhavacatuṣṭaya` begins with VAkK 3.38.
