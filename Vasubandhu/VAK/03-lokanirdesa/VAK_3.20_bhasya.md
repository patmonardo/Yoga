# VAK_3.20_bhasya — Abhidharmakośabhāṣya on VAkK 3.20

## Kārikā

```iast
sa pratītyasamutpādo dvādaśāṅgas trikāṇḍakaḥ /
pūrvāparāntayor dve dve madhye ’ṣṭau paripūriṇaḥ // VAkK_3.20 //
```

**Best translation:**

> This dependent co-arising is the twelvefold structure of conditioned becoming, articulated into three sections: two determinations at the prior limit, two at the subsequent limit, and eight in the middle that complete the presently manifest process.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya takes the beginningless `skandhasaṃtāna` established in VAK 3.18–3.19 and now articulates that continuum as twelve-member dependent co-arising across three births.

### 1. Dependent co-arising is first identified with the three-birth aggregate continuum

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The opening sentence is decisive:

```iast
ya eṣa skandhasaṃtāno janmatrayāvastha upadiṣṭaḥ
```

The object under analysis is the `skandhasaṃtāna`, the continuum of aggregates already established against an enduring self. The Bhāṣya now says that this continuum, insofar as it is taught in the condition of three births, is the twelve-member, three-section dependent co-arising.

```text
skandha-saṃtāna
    ↓
three-birth articulation
    ↓
pratītyasamutpāda
```

Thus the twelve members are not introduced here as twelve independent things. They articulate the conditioned continuity already established in the preceding verses.

### 2. The twelve members are enumerated before their temporal distribution

Vasubandhu lists:

```text
1. avidyā
2. saṃskāra
3. vijñāna
4. nāmarūpa
5. ṣaḍāyatana
6. sparśa
7. vedanā
8. tṛṣṇā
9. upādāna
10. bhava
11. jāti
12. jarāmaraṇa
```

The three `kāṇḍa`, or sections, are then explicitly identified with three births:

```text
pūrvānta   = atīta-janman      past birth
madhya      = pratyutpanna      present birth
aparānta    = anāgata-janman    future birth
```

The ordering of the prose is noteworthy: the Bhāṣya names `pūrvānta`, `aparānta`, and `madhya`, then glosses them as past, future, and present births. The middle is therefore not an abstract logical middle imposed from outside; it is explicitly the presently occurring birth within this temporal scheme.

### 3. The verse distributes the twelve members across the three births

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The distribution is:

```text
Past / pūrvānta
    avidyā
    saṃskāra

Present / madhya
    vijñāna
    nāmarūpa
    ṣaḍāyatana
    sparśa
    vedanā
    tṛṣṇā
    upādāna
    bhava

Future / aparānta
    jāti
    jarāmaraṇa
```

The Kārikā's `dve dve ... aṣṭau` therefore receives a direct temporal interpretation in the Bhāṣya.

### 4. `paripūriṇaḥ` limits the scope of the eight middle members

The Bhāṣya then asks an important question:

```iast
kiṃ punaretānyaṣṭāṅgāni sarvasyāṃ jātau bhavanti /
```

> Do these eight members occur in every birth?

The answer is no.

The Kārikā's final word, `paripūriṇaḥ`, supplies the qualification. Vasubandhu explains the intended person as one who reaches all the relevant successive states (`sarvāṇy evāvasthāntarāṇi spṛśati`).

The scheme therefore does **not** describe every possible birth in exactly the same way.

It excludes:

```text
one who dies in the interval
rūpāvacara birth
ārūpyāvacara birth
```

The cited Mahānidāna passage confirms that the complete sequence presupposed here is a desire-realm (`kāmāvacara`) life involving descent of vijñāna into the mother's womb.

This is an important restriction on the Kārikā's apparent universality.

### 5. A second, twofold arrangement reorganizes the same twelve members

The Bhāṣya finally notes another presentation of dependent co-arising:

```text
paurvāntika
    seven members through vedanā

aparāntika
    five members
```

This gives:

```text
Former-end dependent co-arising
    avidyā → vedanā
    past causes together with their present result

Latter-end dependent co-arising
    tṛṣṇā → jarāmaraṇa
    present causes together with their future result
```

The twelvefold series is therefore capable of more than one doctrinal segmentation. The three-birth arrangement and the twofold former/latter arrangement are alternate organizations of the same conditioned continuum.

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

The continuum of aggregates previously described through three births is dependent co-arising itself, articulated into twelve members and three sections. Its twelve members are ignorance, formations, knowledge, name-and-form, the six spheres, contact, feeling, craving, appropriation, becoming, birth, and aging-and-death. Its three sections are the prior, later, and middle limits: past, future, and present births.

Ignorance and formations belong to the prior limit; birth and aging-and-death belong to the later limit; the remaining eight belong to the middle.

These eight, however, are not present in every birth. The intended case is a complete life that reaches all of the relevant successive states. One who dies before completing them is excluded, as are beings born in the form and formless realms. The scriptural model presupposed here is therefore specifically a desire-realm life involving entry into a mother's womb.

Dependent co-arising may also be divided into two: a former-end series of seven members extending through feeling, and a latter-end series of five members. This alternative grouping includes the former and latter limits together with the result and cause that connect them to the adjacent life.

## The Crucial Result: The Twelve Members Are an Articulation of Continuity

The strongest determination in this unit is the identification:

```text
skandha-saṃtāna
    =
pratītyasamutpāda as three-birth articulation
```

This materially sharpens the Kārikā pass.

VAK 3.20 can look, in isolation, like an architectonic classification of twelve causes. The Bhāṣya instead places the twelvefold structure directly within the aggregate continuum established in 3.18–3.19.

```text
no enduring ātman
    ↓
skandha-saṃtāna
    ↓
conditioned continuity through births
    ↓
twelve-member dependent co-arising
```

The twelve members are therefore functional-temporal determinations of conditioned continuity.

The second important result is that the standard three-life scheme is a **complete-case schema**, not an invariant empirical sequence imposed upon every possible rebirth.

```text
three-life schema
    ≠ every birth without qualification

three-life schema
    = complete desire-realm course
      used to display the full twelve-member articulation
```

That restriction is supplied by the Bhāṣya through `paripūriṇaḥ`.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — identifies the previously established aggregate continuum with twelve-member dependent co-arising, enumerates and distributes the members, poses the scope question, and explains the qualification `paripūriṇaḥ`.

**Voice: Scriptural citation** — the Mahānidāna passage concerning vijñāna entering the mother's womb supports the restriction to the complete desire-realm birth model.

No independent opponent is required for this unit; the questions function as Vasubandhu's exegetical interrogation of the Kārikā.

## New Technical Terms

- **janmatrayāvastha** — the condition or articulation of three births.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation in this passage.
  - **function:** identifies the temporal scope within which the aggregate continuum is presented.

- **kāṇḍa** — section, division.
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā through `trikāṇḍaka`, made explicit in the prose.
  - **function:** doctrinal division of the twelve members into past, present, and future births.

- **vyavasthāpana / vyavasthāpyante** — arrangement, systematic placement.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reflective vocabulary.
  - **function:** asks how the twelve members are distributed within the three sections.

- **paripūra / paripūriṇ** — complete; one possessing the complete course.
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly interpreted by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** restricts the eight middle members to a life traversing all relevant successive stages.

- **avasthāntara** — successive or distinct state.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** explains what it means for the life-course to be complete.

- **paurvāntika** — pertaining to the former end; former-end dependent co-arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this unit.
  - **function:** alternate seven-member organization extending through vedanā.

- **aparāntika** — pertaining to the latter end; latter-end dependent co-arising.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this unit.
  - **function:** alternate five-member organization from tṛṣṇā through jarāmaraṇa.

- **saphalahetuka** — taken together with result and cause.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only compound in this unit.
  - **function:** explains why the former/latter twofold grouping reaches across adjacent temporal sections.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.20 is the first formal articulation of the beginningless continuum described in 3.18–3.19.

The Bhāṣya establishes four controls on its interpretation:

```text
1. Subject matter:
   skandha-saṃtāna, not ātman.

2. Temporal form:
   past birth / present birth / future birth.

3. Twelve-member distribution:
   2 / 8 / 2.

4. Scope:
   the complete eight-member middle belongs to a complete kāmāvacara life,
   not indiscriminately to every possible birth.
```

The alternate seven/five division further shows that dependent co-arising is not tied to only one classificatory cut. What remains invariant is the conditioned relation of causes, results, and continuity; the doctrinal segmentation may vary according to the explanatory purpose.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The established translation of the verse remains serviceable, but the phrase:

> “eight in the middle that complete the presently manifest process”

should not be read as saying that every present birth necessarily manifests all eight members.

The Bhāṣya requires the qualification:

> the eight-member middle is the complete present-life articulation for the `paripūra` case, specifically the fully traversed desire-realm life used as the doctrinal model.

No canonical vocabulary change is required at this stage.

## Logical Determination

The Kārikā gave the formal structure:

```text
2 → 8 → 2
```

The Bhāṣya now gives its ground:

```text
aggregate continuum
    ↓
temporal differentiation across three births
    ↓
functional differentiation into twelve members
```

This is therefore not simply a list of causes. It is a **reflection of one conditioned continuum into temporally and functionally distinct determinations**.

The qualification `paripūriṇaḥ` is logically important because it prevents the schema from being mistaken for an exceptionless sequence at the level of every empirical birth.

```text
systematic form
    ↓
complete-case articulation
    ↓
qualified empirical applicability
```

The Bhāṣya thus mediates between the universal doctrinal schema and the diversity of actual modes of birth.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained Organon contact is methodological rather than identificatory.

The Bhāṣya shows how a rational schema must remain answerable to the empirical field it organizes. The twelvefold structure has systematic unity, but Vasubandhu immediately limits its scope when the concrete cases do not instantiate every middle stage.

```text
rational articulation
    ↕
conditioned empirical course
```

That reciprocity is valuable for the Rational–Empirical System of Science: a formal structure is not made true by being imposed uniformly. Its universality must include the determinate conditions under which its full articulation is actually instantiated.

No Hegelian or Yoga correspondence is required to establish this result.
