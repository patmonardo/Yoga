## Natural Argument

VAK 3.24 established the `āvasthika` interpretation of dependent co-arising and the rule of naming each stage by its predominant determination. VAK 3.25 now explains **why the sūtra chooses this particular sentient-being, three-birth presentation**.

### 1. If every limb-stage contains five aggregates, why name only avidyā, saṃskāra, and so on?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The question is explicit:

```iast
yadyaṅgamaṅgaṃ pañca skandhāḥ kiṃ kāraṇam avidyādīn eva dharmān kīrtayati sma /
```

If every member is a five-aggregate stage, why does the teaching name only ignorance, formations, and the other individual dharmas?

The answer is the rule already stated in the Kārikā:

```iast
prādhānyāt tv aṅgakīrtanam
```

> The member is named according to predominance.

Thus:

```text
avidyā-predominant stage
    → called avidyā

saṃskāra-predominant stage
    → called saṃskāra

...

jarāmaraṇa-predominant stage
    → called jarāmaraṇa
```

The doctrinal name is therefore a **predominance-name for a composite stage**.

### 2. Sūtra and Abhidharma are intentionally operating at different levels

Vasubandhu next asks why the sūtra speaks of twelve-member dependent co-arising when Abhidharma treatises can define dependent co-arising much more broadly as:

```iast
sarve saṃskṛtā dharmāḥ
```

> all conditioned dharmas.

The answer is one of the most important methodological distinctions in the passage:

```iast
abhiprāyikaḥ sūtre lākṣaṇiko 'bhidharmaḥ
```

```text
Sūtra
    abhiprāyika
    according to pedagogical/intended purpose

Abhidharma
    lākṣaṇika
    according to characteristic determination
```

The two formulations are therefore not simply competing definitions.

The sūtra selects an intentional presentation suited to a specific cognitive problem. Abhidharma gives the more characteristic or analytically exact determination.

### 3. The sūtra deliberately gives a `sattvākhya` presentation

The Bhāṣya distinguishes further modes:

```text
āvasthika
kṣaṇika
prākarṣika
sāṃbandhika
sattvākhya
asattvākhya
```

The immediate question is:

```iast
kimarthaṃ punaḥ sūtre sattvākhya eva /
```

> Why, then, does the sūtra use specifically the sentient-being presentation?

The answer is the second half of the Kārikā:

```iast
pūrvāparāntamadhyeṣu saṃmohavinivṛttaye
```

> For the removal of confusion concerning the prior limit, later limit, and middle.

This is why the series is also `trikāṇḍa`, three-sectioned.

### 4. The three temporal sections correspond to three forms of self-confusion

Vasubandhu makes the pedagogical target concrete.

#### Prior-limit confusion

```text
Was I in the past?
Was I not?
What was I?
How was I?
```

This is `pūrvānta-saṃmoha`.

#### Later-limit confusion

```text
Will I exist in the future?
What will I be?
How will I be?
```

This is `aparānta-saṃmoha`.

#### Middle confusion

```text
What is this?
What are we now?
What will we become?
```

This is `madhya-saṃmoha`.

The doctrinal point is not to answer these questions by supplying a better theory of an enduring self.

It is to **dissolve the form of the question** by exhibiting conditioned continuity.

```text
self-question
    ↓
analysis into conditioned stages
    ↓
confusion removed
```

### 5. The 2 / 8 / 2 structure is therefore therapeutically organized

The three sections target the three temporal confusions in order:

```text
Prior limit
    avidyā + saṃskāra
    → removes past-oriented confusion

Later limit
    jāti + jarāmaraṇa
    → removes future-oriented confusion

Middle
    vijñāna through bhava
    → removes present-oriented confusion
```

The order in the explanatory prose is striking: past confusion, future confusion, present confusion. The sūtra's threefold arrangement is not only descriptive chronology; it is structured for the removal of determinate errors.

### 6. Right seeing of dependent co-arising replaces speculative self-reconstruction

The cited sūtra states that when dependent co-arising and dependently arisen dharmas are seen:

```iast
yathābhūtaṃ samyak prajñayā
```

> as they really are, by right prajñā,

the monk no longer runs back toward the prior limit asking:

```text
Was I?
What was I?
How was I?
```

The cure for temporal self-speculation is therefore not dogmatic assertion but **right discriminative seeing of conditioned structure**.

A final reported view adds that `tṛṣṇā`, `upādāna`, and `bhava` may likewise be understood as serving the removal of confusion concerning the future end, because they are precisely its causes.

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

Although every member of the stage-based dependent co-arising is a five-aggregate stage, each stage is named after the dharma predominant within it: the stage in which ignorance predominates is called ignorance, the stage in which formations predominate is called formations, and so on through aging-and-death.

The sūtra's twelve-member formulation is intentional and pedagogical, whereas Abhidharma gives a characteristic determination of dependent co-arising. Accordingly, dependent co-arising may be presented in several modes, including stage-based, momentary, extended, relational, in terms of sentient beings, and without such a sentient-being designation.

The sūtra specifically adopts the sentient-being and three-section presentation in order to remove confusion concerning the past, future, and present. Past confusion takes the form of questions such as “Was I in the past? What was I? How was I?” Future confusion asks whether and what one will be. Present confusion asks what this is and what we are.

To turn aside these three forms of confusion, the sūtra teaches dependent co-arising in a sentient-being, three-section form: ignorance and formations for the prior limit; birth and aging-and-death for the later limit; and knowledge through becoming for the middle.

When dependent co-arising and dependently arisen dharmas are seen rightly, as they really are, through prajñā, speculative return to these self-questions ceases.

## The Crucial Result: The Twelvefold Series Is a Pedagogical Anti-Self Architecture

The most important result of this passage is that the familiar twelve-member, three-life presentation is **not the only possible analytic form of dependent co-arising**.

Vasubandhu explicitly distinguishes:

```text
characteristic scope:
    all conditioned dharmas

sūtra presentation:
    twelve-member sentient-being sequence
```

Why the narrower presentation?

Because it is designed to remove a determinate cognitive pathology:

```text
I in the past
I in the future
I in the present
```

The three-life architecture therefore has a practical epistemic function.

```text
three temporal self-confusions
        ↓
three-section dependent co-arising
        ↓
right prajñā
        ↓
saṃmoha-vinivṛtti
```

That places VAK 3.25 squarely within Practical Reason: the analysis is not performed merely to classify the world, but to transform the mode in which the agent understands its own continuity.

## New Technical Terms

- **abhiprāyika** — according to intention, intended pedagogically.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only methodological vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** characterizes the sūtra's selective presentation of dependent co-arising.

- **lākṣaṇika** — according to characteristic determination.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only methodological vocabulary.
  - **function:** characterizes the Abhidharma mode of exact analytic definition.

- **sattvākhya** — designated in terms of a sentient being.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classification here.
  - **function:** identifies the mode adopted by the sūtra for the twelve-member sequence.

- **asattvākhya** — not designated in terms of a sentient being.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classification here.
  - **function:** marks the contrasting non-personal analytic presentation.

- **pūrvānta-saṃmoha** — confusion concerning the prior limit.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** names past-oriented self-speculation.

- **aparānta-saṃmoha** — confusion concerning the later limit.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** names future-oriented self-speculation.

- **madhya-saṃmoha** — confusion concerning the middle/present.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** names confusion concerning present identity and becoming.

- **saṃmoha-vyāvartana / vinivṛtti** — turning away / removal of confusion.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya and Kārikā vocabulary.
  - **function:** states the practical-epistemic purpose of the three-section teaching.

- **yathābhūta** — as it really is / according to actuality.
  - **status:** scriptural vocabulary cited by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** qualifies right seeing of conditioned dharmas.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.25 gives the hermeneutic rule for the twelvefold series.

```text
What the series is:
    āvasthika
    stage-based

How members are named:
    prādhānya
    according to predominance

Why the sūtra uses twelve sentient-being members:
    abhiprāya
    pedagogical intention

What the teaching removes:
    saṃmoha concerning past / future / present

By what:
    right prajñā seeing conditioned dharmas as they are
```

This prevents two errors at once:

```text
Error 1:
    treating the limb-name as an isolated substance

Error 2:
    treating the twelve-member series as the exhaustive definition
    of dependent co-arising in every analytic context
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly blocks both.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The established translation is substantially correct.

The Bhāṣya adds two important qualifications:

1. `āvasthika` refers specifically to five-aggregate stages named by their predominant dharma.
2. `saṃmohavinivṛtti` is not generic clarification; it is the removal of concrete self-confusions concerning past, future, and present existence.

A Bhāṣya-informed philosophical translation could therefore read:

> The intended presentation is stage-based, each member being named after what predominates in its five-aggregate stage; this three-section teaching is given to remove confusion about oneself in the past, future, and present.

## Logical Determination

The logical structure is now clearer than in the Kārikā pass.

The teaching moves from a complex conditioned field to a pedagogically selected representation:

```text
all conditioned dharmas
    ↓
possible analytic modes
    ↓
selected sentient-being model
    ↓
three temporal sections
    ↓
removal of three determinate confusions
```

The sūtra is therefore not less rational because it is selective.

Its selectivity is **teleologically rational**:

```text
error to be removed
    determines
form of teaching
```

This is the strongest logical result of the unit.

## Organon Light

The Organon should notice something very important here without importing any foreign identity into Vasubandhu.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes **two legitimate views of one field**:

```text
lākṣaṇika view
    What is the characteristic structure?

abhiprāyika view
    What representation should be selected
    for this agent and this practical end?
```

That is already an embryonic architecture of **View selection by purpose**.

The same domain can be rendered differently because the rational task differs.

```text
Known field
    all conditioned dharmas

Task
    remove temporal self-confusion

Selected View
    sattvākhya + trikāṇḍa + twelve members
```

This is much richer than merely saying “different descriptions are possible.”

The form of representation is determined by the **error to be overcome**.

```text
Agent has saṃmoha
        ↓
Practical end = saṃmoha-vinivṛtti
        ↓
Reason selects an appropriate View
        ↓
right prajñā of dependent structure
        ↓
misframed self-question loses its ground
```

That is very close to what a future Organon Bhāṣya will need from an Agential View System:

- a field can support multiple valid articulations;
- one articulation may be more exact characteristically;
- another may be selected for a determinate practical-cognitive purpose;
- the View must be judged by whether it rationally accomplishes that purpose.

Most important, Vasubandhu's practical move is **not to answer the bad question**.

He changes the representation under which the question arose.

```text
“Was I?”
    ↓
not: better theory of I
    ↓
conditioned causal articulation
    ↓
the original confusion is dissolved
```

That is an extremely strong first-pass Organon result, but it remains downstream of the Bhāṣya's own explicit distinction between `abhiprāyika` and `lākṣaṇika`.
