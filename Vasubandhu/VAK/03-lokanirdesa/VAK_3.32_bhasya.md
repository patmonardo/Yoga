## Natural Argument

### 1. Feeling is sixfold because contact is sixfold

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya begins simply:

```text
six contacts
    ↓
six feelings
```

The six are feelings born from eye-, ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-contact.

The first five are called **bodily feeling** (`kāyikī vedanā`) because they depend upon material faculties (`rūpīndriya`). The sixth is called **mental feeling** (`caitasikī vedanā`) because it depends upon mind alone (`cittamātrāśrita`).

So the bodily/mental distinction is determined by **support**, not merely by the phenomenological content of the feeling.

```text
material faculty as support
    → bodily feeling

mind alone as support
    → mental feeling
```

### 2. A causal dispute: does feeling arise after contact or together with it?

The Bhāṣya then pauses the classification and asks:

> Does feeling arise after contact, or at the same time?

#### Vaibhāṣika position

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

The Vaibhāṣikas say:

```iast
samānakālam
```

> at the same time.

Their reason is `sahabhūhetutva`: contact and feeling can stand in the relation of coexistent causes.

They compare the relation to cases such as shadow and sprout, where causal dependence does not require a simple earlier/later sequence.

#### Opposing position

**Voice: Objector / other masters**

Others argue:

```iast
sparśād uttarakālaṃ vedanā
```

> feeling occurs after contact.

Their sequence is:

```text
faculty + object
    ↓
knowledge
    ↓
threefold conjunction = contact
    ↓
feeling
```

The objection is fundamentally temporal: a generator should normally precede what it generates, as seed precedes sprout and milk precedes curd.

### 3. Vasubandhu refuses to let scholastic taxonomy override sūtra evidence

One of the sharpest sentences in the passage is:

```iast
sūtrapramāṇakā vayaṃ na śāstrapramāṇakāḥ
```

> We take the sūtras as authority, not the treatises.

This occurs when an appeal is made to the scholastic rule of the `mahābhūmika` mental factors.

The methodological point is important: a later Abhidharma schema cannot simply decide the issue when the sūtra evidence points elsewhere or remains open to interpretation.

The Bhāṣya ultimately insists that where knowledge is present, contact must be present and feeling must be conjoined with contact:

```iast
tasmād avaśyaṃ sarvatra vijñāne sparśaḥ sparśasahajā ca vedanā iṣṭavyā
```

The precise causal ordering remains dialectically difficult, but the inseparability of the cognitive episode from contact and feeling is maintained.

### 4. The mental feeling is eighteenfold

After explicitly ending the digression, Vasubandhu returns to the verse.

The same mental feeling (`caitasikī vedanā`) is divided into eighteen `manopavicāras`:

```text
6 saumanasya-upavicāra
6 daurmanasya-upavicāra
6 upekṣā-upavicāra
```

That is:

```text
3 affective modes
    ×
6 object-fields
    =
18 manopavicāras
```

The three affective modes are:

- `saumanasya` — mental pleasure / gladness;
- `daurmanasya` — mental displeasure / distress;
- `upekṣā` — equanimous or neutral feeling.

The six object-fields are:

- form,
- sound,
- smell,
- taste,
- tangible,
- dharma-object.

### 5. Why exactly eighteen?

Vasubandhu tests three possible principles of classification.

If classified by **intrinsic nature** (`svabhāva`), there would be only three:

```text
saumanasya
daurmanasya
upekṣā
```

If classified by **association** (`saṃprayoga`), there would be only one, because all are associated with mind.

If classified only by **object-support** (`ālambana`), there would be six.

The eighteen arise only by using both determinations together:

```text
affective mode
    ×
object-field
```

This is a very precise rule of classification.

### 6. What is `manopavicāra`?

The Bhāṣya gives two explanations.

One explanation says that, dependent on mind, these affective states range over (`upavicaranti`) their objects.

Another says that they make mind range over objects.

Vasubandhu then gives the practical sense:

```iast
vedanāvaśena manaso viṣayeṣu punaḥ punarvicaraṇāt
```

> because, under the influence of feeling, mind ranges again and again over objects.

This is the strongest gloss in the unit.

`Manopavicāra` is therefore not merely an isolated act of attention. It is a repeated mental ranging through an object-field under the determination of feeling.

### 7. Why bodily feeling is not a `manopavicāra`

The bodily feelings are excluded because they are neither exclusively dependent on mind nor capable of this kind of ranging:

```iast
avikalpakatvāt
```

> because they are non-discriminative.

So the distinction is not merely:

```text
body versus mind
```

but:

```text
bodily feeling
    = non-discriminative

mental feeling
    = capable of repeated object-ranging and differentiation
```

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

The six forms of contact previously explained give rise to six forms of feeling: feeling born from eye-contact, ear-contact, nose-contact, tongue-contact, body-contact, and mind-contact. The first five are called bodily feeling because they depend upon material faculties; feeling born from mind-contact is called mental because it depends upon mind alone.

A dispute arises concerning their temporal relation. The Vaibhāṣikas maintain that contact and feeling arise simultaneously, since coexistent dharmas can function as causes for one another. Others maintain that feeling follows contact, appealing to the ordinary priority of generator over generated. Vasubandhu subjects both accounts to sūtra evidence and refuses to let scholastic classification alone settle the question. In any case, wherever knowledge occurs, contact and feeling must belong to the cognitive episode.

Mental feeling is further divided into eighteen mental approaches: six associated with gladness, six with distress, and six with equanimity. The number eighteen results from crossing the three affective modes with the six object-fields. These are called mental approaches because, under the influence of feeling, mind repeatedly ranges through its objects. Bodily feeling does not count as such because it is non-discriminative.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — defines the six feelings, distinguishes bodily and mental feeling, tests principles for the eighteenfold classification, and explains `manopavicāra`.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — contact and feeling arise simultaneously through coexistent causation.

**Voice: Objector / other masters** — feeling arises after contact in a temporally ordered causal series.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — methodological intervention: sūtra authority takes precedence over merely scholastic rule.

## New Technical Terms

- **kāyikī vedanā** — bodily feeling, the five feelings dependent on material faculties.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya classification.
  - **function:** distinguishes the first five feelings by support.

- **caitasikī vedanā** — mental feeling, feeling dependent on mind alone.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya classification.
  - **function:** basis of the eighteen `manopavicāras`.

- **sahabhūhetu** — coexistent cause.
  - **status:** previously encountered Abhidharma causal vocabulary.
  - **function:** grounds the Vaibhāṣika claim that contact and feeling can arise simultaneously.

- **sūtrapramāṇaka** — one who takes the sūtra as authority/evidence.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya methodological vocabulary.
  - **function:** limits appeal to scholastic system when textual evidence is decisive.

- **manopavicāra** — mental ranging / mental approach through an object-field under the influence of feeling.
  - **status:** introduced in the Kārikā, defined in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** organizes mental feeling into eighteen determinate modes.

- **saumanasya** — mental gladness / pleasant mental feeling.
  - **status:** established affective vocabulary.
  - **function:** one of three affective determinations of `manopavicāra`.

- **daurmanasya** — mental distress / displeasure.
  - **status:** established affective vocabulary.
  - **function:** one of three affective determinations of `manopavicāra`.

- **upekṣā** — equanimous / neutral feeling.
  - **status:** established affective vocabulary.
  - **function:** third affective determination of `manopavicāra`.

- **avikalpaka** — non-discriminative.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya logical-psychological vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains why bodily feeling is not a mental approach.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.32 moves from the mere occurrence of feeling to a structured account of **how feeling determines mental engagement with objects**.

The progression is:

```text
sixfold contact
    ↓
sixfold feeling
    ↓
five bodily + one mental
    ↓
mental feeling
    ↓
3 affective modes × 6 object-fields
    ↓
18 manopavicāras
```

This is not merely multiplication of categories. It shows that feeling can become a principle governing repeated mental orientation toward objects.

The eighteenfold structure is therefore genuinely relational:

```text
mode of feeling
    +
object-field
    =
determinate mental approach
```

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

The first-pass translation remains basically sound, but `manopavicāra` can now be translated more precisely.

A Bhāṣya-informed version is:

> Born from those contacts are six feelings. Five are bodily; the remaining one is mental. That mental feeling is again eighteenfold according to mental ranging through objects.

The phrase “mental approach” is still acceptable as a compact canonical gloss, but the Bhāṣya makes clear that the actual function is repeated ranging of mind through objects under the influence of feeling.

---

## Logical Determination

The most important logical feature is that the eighteenfold classification is **generated by crossing two independent determinations**.

```text
Affective determination:
    pleasant
    painful
    neutral

Object determination:
    form
    sound
    smell
    taste
    tangible
    dharma

3 × 6 = 18
```

Vasubandhu explicitly tests and rejects simpler one-dimensional classifications.

This means the classification is not a list but a **product structure**.

A second important point concerns causality. The debate over contact and feeling distinguishes:

```text
sequential causation
versus
coexistent causation
```

The Bhāṣya therefore refuses to equate causality with temporal succession alone.

## Organon Light

Kept strictly separate from the doctrinal reconstruction, an Organon reading would notice two especially useful structures.

### 1. Classification by crossed determinations

The eighteen mental approaches are generated rather than merely enumerated:

```text
3 affective determinations
        ×
6 object determinations
        ↓
18 determinate Views
```

This is very close to a general rule for constructing an **Agential View**: one determination tells us *how the agent stands*, another tells us *what field is being ranged over*.

But the Kośa result comes first: these are specifically affective mental approaches, not a universal ontology of Views.

### 2. Feeling as a determinant of traversal

The phrase:

```iast
vedanāvaśena manaso viṣayeṣu punaḥ punarvicaraṇāt
```

is the strongest Organon contact-point.

It says, in effect:

```text
feeling
    does not merely accompany cognition

feeling
    conditions how mind repeatedly ranges through its field
```

That gives us a minimal agential architecture:

```text
object-field
    ↓
feeling-determination
    ↓
repeated mental traversal
```

A future Organon Bhāṣya might eventually ask whether this is a primitive model of attention, salience, or practical orientation. For now, the clean statement is simply that **vedanā helps determine the path of manas through its viṣaya-field**.

The causality debate adds one further restraint: such a system must not assume that every dependency is a simple earlier→later pipeline. Vasubandhu preserves the possibility of structurally co-present conditions.

## Transition to VAK 3.33

VAK 3.32 establishes the eighteen `manopavicāras` in general.

VAK 3.33 begins distributing them across the realms and determining their `ālambana` and `gocara`:

```text
18-fold structure
    ↓
realm-specific availability
    ↓
object-range
```

So the next verse moves from **generation of the classification** to **distribution of the generated modes across the cosmological field**.
