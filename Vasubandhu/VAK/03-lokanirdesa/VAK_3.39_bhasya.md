**Working translation:**

Morsel nutriment exists in the desire realm and consists of three spheres. The visible-form sphere is not [nutriment], because it does not benefit its own faculty or the liberated.

## Natural Argument

### 1. The four nutriments are introduced

Vasubandhu begins from the sūtra statement that beings persist through nutriment and asks which nutriments are meant.

He enumerates four:

```text
1. kavaḍīkāra-āhāra   — morsel nutriment
2. sparśa             — contact
3. manaḥsaṃcetanā     — mental volition
4. vijñāna            — knowledge
```

The present verse analyzes only the first.

### 2. Morsel nutriment may be gross or subtle

`Kavaḍīkāra-āhāra` is said to be either:

```text
audārika
    → gross

sūkṣma
    → subtle
```

The Bhāṣya gives examples of subtle nutriment. Intermediate beings are said to subsist on odor (`gandhāhāratva`). Gods and beings of the first cosmic age do not have ordinary excretive residue (`niḥṣyanda`); nutriment penetrates their bodies like oil entering sand. The text also mentions subtle beings such as infants and sweat-born creatures.

The point is that `kavaḍīkāra` is not restricted to coarse chewing of visible food.

### 3. Morsel nutriment occurs only in the desire realm

The Kārikā says:

```iast
kavaḍīkāra āhāraḥ kāme
```

Vasubandhu explains that it does not occur in the form or formless realms because beings born there are already free from desire for this kind of nutriment.

Thus:

```text
kāmadhātu
    → kavaḍīkāra-āhāra present

rūpadhātu / ārūpyadhātu
    → absent
```

### 4. Its nature is three spheres

`Tryāyatanātmaka` is explicitly resolved as the three desire-realm spheres:

```text
gandha-āyatana      — odor sphere
rasa-āyatana        — taste sphere
spraṣṭavya-āyatana  — tangible sphere
```

These are called morsel nutriment because they are taken in by making them into morsels (`kavaḍīkṛtyābhyavaharaṇa`). The Bhāṣya explains the ordinary delimitation by mouthful or nasal intake.

It then qualifies this ordinary description: some things that are not literally swallowed can nevertheless sustain existence and therefore count as subtle nutriment, like bathing and anointing.

### 5. Why visible form is excluded

An objection asks why `rūpāyatana`, the visible-form sphere, is not also nutriment, since visible food too may be taken in morsels.

Vasubandhu answers by giving a functional criterion:

```iast
āhāro hi nāma ya indriyamahābhūtānāmanugrahāya saṃvarttate
```

“Nutriment is that which conduces to the support or benefit of the faculties and great elements.”

Visible form does not, at the time of ingestion, support its own faculty—the eye—or the great elements underlying that faculty. Still less does it support the other faculties, since visible form is not their field.

### 6. Pleasant seeing does not make visible form nutriment

A further qualification follows. Seeing an attractive form may produce pleasure or gladness. Even then, Vasubandhu says, the nutriment is not the visible form itself. Rather, it is the pleasant-feeling-producing `sparśa` that has that form as its object-support.

The proof is furnished by liberated persons—non-returners and arhats. They may see something pleasing, but the sight does not provide the sustaining benefit in question.

Thus visible form is excluded not because it cannot occasion pleasant experience, but because it does not itself satisfy the technical sustaining function of `āhāra`.

## Literal Prose Translation

The Blessed One, having directly known it himself, declared a single principle connected with this matter in the sūtra: “All beings subsist through nutriment.” What, then, are the nutriments? There are four nutriments. The first is morsel nutriment. It is gross and subtle. It is subtle for intermediate beings because they subsist on odor; likewise for gods and beings of the first cosmic age because there is no excretive residue, nutriment penetrating their limbs as oil penetrates sand. Or subtle nutriment belongs to subtle beings such as infants and sweat-born creatures. Contact is the second nutriment, mental volition the third, and knowledge the fourth.

Now, morsel nutriment exists in the desire realm, not in the form and formless realms, because beings are born there having become dispassionate toward it. It consists of three spheres: all the desire-realm spheres of odor, taste, and tangibles are morsel nutriment, because they are ingested after being formed into morsels, as delimited by mouthful or nasal intake. As for shadow, heat, flame, and radiance, how can they be nutriment? This account is said to be stated according to the predominant cases. Whatever is not literally swallowed but nevertheless sustains existence is also subtle nutriment, like bathing and anointing.

Why is the visible-form sphere not nutriment? It too is ingested after being formed into morsels. Because the visible-form sphere does not support its own faculty or the liberated. Nutriment is so called when it conduces to the support of the faculties and the great elements. But visible form, at the time of ingestion, does not support its own faculty or the great elements belonging to it; how much less the others, since it is not their field. Even when a visible form gives rise to pleasure and gladness while being seen, the nutriment is the pleasant-feeling-producing contact having that visible form as object-support, not the visible form itself. This is shown by liberated persons—non-returners and arhats—who may see pleasing nutriment without thereby receiving such support.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose throughout.
- **Voice: Objector** — `kasmānna rūpāyatanam āhāraḥ? tad api kavaḍīkṛtyābhyavahriyate`: why is visible form not nutriment, since it too is ingested as a morsel?
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — replies by defining nutriment through `anugraha`, sustaining benefit.

## New Technical Terms

- **kavaḍīkāra-āhāra** — morsel nutriment
  - **status:** Kārikā term receiving full Bhāṣya explanation
  - **function:** first of the four nutriments

- **audārika** — gross / coarse
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification here
  - **function:** classification of morsel nutriment

- **sūkṣma** — subtle
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification here
  - **function:** classification of morsel nutriment

- **gandhāhāra** — one whose nutriment is odor / odor-nutriment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific here
  - **function:** explains subtle nutriment of intermediate beings

- **niḥṣyanda** — exudation / residual outflow
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific here
  - **function:** explains subtle assimilation among gods and primordial beings

- **abhyavaharaṇa** — ingestion / taking in
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanation of `kavaḍīkāra`
  - **function:** defines ordinary morsel intake

- **anugraha** — support / benefit / sustaining aid
  - **status:** decisive Bhāṣya term in this passage
  - **function:** functional criterion for what counts as nutriment

- **indriya-mahābhūta** — faculties and great elements
  - **status:** previously encountered vocabulary used together here
  - **function:** what nutriment sustains

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a functional, not merely material, definition of nutriment.

```text
āhāra
    = that which supports
      indriyas + mahābhūtas
```

Therefore morsel nutriment is not simply “whatever appears as food.” It is the threefold sensory content that performs a sustaining function:

```text
gandha
rasa
spraṣṭavya
```

Visible form is excluded because:

```text
rūpāyatana
    may be seen
    may occasion pleasure
    may accompany eating

but

rūpāyatana
    does not itself perform the required sustaining function
```

The distinction between visible form and the pleasant `sparśa` occasioned through seeing is especially important. A pleasant effect associated with an item does not by itself establish that the item is technically `āhāra`.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first pāda is now secure:

```text
kavaḍīkāra āhāraḥ kāme tryāyatanātmakaḥ
```

should be understood as:

> Morsel nutriment exists in the desire realm and consists of three spheres—odor, taste, and tangibles.

The difficult final compound is controlled by the Bhāṣya. `svākṣa` refers to its own faculty, the eye; `mukta` refers to liberated persons such as non-returners and arhats; and `anugraha` supplies the criterion of sustaining benefit.

So the second half means substantially:

> The visible-form sphere is not nutriment, because it does not support its own faculty and does not provide the relevant sustaining benefit for the liberated.

The Bhāṣya therefore corrects any reading that treats `rūpāyatana` as nutriment merely because visible food can be swallowed. Technical `āhāra` is determined by sustaining efficacy, not by appearance or ordinary linguistic usage.
