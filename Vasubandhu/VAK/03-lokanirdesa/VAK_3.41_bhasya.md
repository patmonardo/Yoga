### Source correction

The first-pass Kārikā file begins with `nirvṛttiś ca`, but the Bhāṣya source shows that this belongs to the preceding discussion of `antarābhava` and `abhinirvṛtti`. VAkK 3.41 itself begins with `iha puṣṭyartham`.

The source line prints `ākṣpanivṛttyarthaṃ`; the syntax and Bhāṣya explanation require `ākṣepa-nirvṛtti-artham`. This is treated here as an e-text defect rather than silently normalized in the source quotation.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Why exactly four nutriments?

An objection opens the unit:

**Voice: Objector**

If all `sāsrava` dharmas nourish becoming, why does the teaching single out only four nutriments?

Vasubandhu answers that the four are stated because of their **predominance** (`prādhānya`). The claim is not that no other conditioned factor can contribute to continuation, but that these four perform the principal nutritive functions.

### 2. The first pair nourishes already-arisen existence

The first two nutriments are assigned to `āśraya` and `āśrita`.

```text
āśraya
    = sendriya-kāya
    = the body endowed with faculties

āśrita
    = citta-caitta
    = mind and associated mental factors
```

Their corresponding nutriments are:

```text
kavaḍīkāra-āhāra
    → nourishes the bodily support

sparśa
    → nourishes mind and mental factors supported upon it
```

Vasubandhu states their common function explicitly: these two are predominant in nourishing **existence already arisen here** (`ihotpannasya bhavasya poṣaṇa`).

### 3. The second pair produces existence not yet arisen

The remaining two nutriments have a different temporal function.

```text
manaḥsaṃcetanā
    → punarbhavasya ākṣepa
    → projects / throws forward renewed existence

vijñāna
    → from a vijñāna-seed conditioned by karma
    → abhinirvṛtti of the projected existence
```

Thus the second pair is predominant in bringing about **existence not yet arisen** (`anutpannasya bhavasya karaṇa`).

The Kārikā's `yathākramam` is therefore exact:

```text
manaḥsaṃcetanā → ākṣepa
vijñāna        → abhinirvṛtti / nirvṛtti
```

### 4. Nurse and mother analogies

Vasubandhu summarizes the two pairs through another analogy:

```text
first pair
    = like nurses (dhātrī-sthānīya)
    = because they nourish what has already been born

second pair
    = like mothers (mātṛ-sthānīya)
    = because they generate what is to be born
```

This distinction is not ornamental. It separates two different functions of nutriment:

```text
maintenance of present bhava
production of future bhava
```

### 5. Not every morsel is nutriment: the catuṣkoṭi

The Bhāṣya next asks whether every `kavaḍīkāra` is therefore `āhāra` and answers through a four-cornered analysis (`catuṣkoṭika`).

The basic distinction is between:

```text
kavaḍīkāra
    morsel / ingested material

āhāra
    what performs the technical nutritive function
```

The corners include cases where something ingested causes deterioration of the faculties and disruption of the great elements, and cases where ingestion produces increase and growth. The same analytical procedure is to be applied, as appropriate, to `sparśa` and the other nutriments.

The important doctrinal point is that the extension of the ordinary term and the extension of the technical term are not simply identical.

### 6. Harmful ingestion can still count as āhāra

A further difficulty arises. Something consumed may later harm the consumer. Vasubandhu nevertheless says it can still be nutriment because it afforded support **at the moment of taking it** (`āpāte 'nugrahāt`).

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

The Vaibhāṣikas say nutriment performs its nutritive activity at two times:

```text
while being consumed
while being digested
```

Thus later harmful consequences do not automatically erase the earlier nutritive function.

### 7. All four nutriments occur in every destiny and mode of birth

The Bhāṣya asks how many nutriments occur in each `gati` and answers:

```text
sarvāsu sarve
```

All four occur in all destinies.

The same applies to the modes of birth (`yoni`).

An objection is then raised concerning hell beings: how could morsel nutriment exist there? The answer points to the burning iron balls and molten copper forced upon them. A subsequent qualification distinguishes nutriment that produces increase from nutriment satisfying the defining mark in a more destructive context. Vasubandhu also notes the existence of morsel nutriment in isolated hells (`pratyeka-naraka`) in a manner comparable to humans.

## Literal Prose Translation

Why are precisely four nutriments taught? Are not all dharmas with outflows nourishers of the forms of becoming? Although this is so, these four are taught because of their predominance.

Here, two are for the nourishment of the support and what is supported; the other two are, respectively, for the projection and production of another existence. The support is the body endowed with faculties, and morsel nutriment is for its nourishment. Mind and mental factors are what depend upon that support, and contact is for their nourishment. These first two are therefore predominant in nourishing a form of becoming that has already arisen here.

Through mental volition there is the projection of renewed existence. Then, from the seed of knowledge conditioned by karma, there is the emergence of what has been projected. These latter two are therefore predominant in producing a form of becoming that has not yet arisen. For this reason four nutriments are taught. The first two are like nurses, because they nourish what has already been born; the latter two are like mothers, because they generate it.

The Bhāṣya then analyzes whether every morsel is nutriment by means of a four-cornered analysis. An ingested morsel may lead to decline of the faculties and disruption of the great elements, while another may lead to increase of the faculties and growth of the great elements. Corresponding four-cornered analyses are to be made for contact and the remaining nutriments as appropriate.

Even something consumed that subsequently harms the consumer can still be nutriment, because at the moment of intake it afforded support. According to the Vaibhāṣikas, nutriment performs its nutritive function at two times: while it is being consumed and while it is being digested.

How many nutriments occur in each destiny? All four occur in every destiny, and likewise in every mode of birth. In hell, morsel nutriment includes such things as burning iron balls and boiling copper. Further distinctions are then made between nutritive increase and cases that satisfy the defining mark of nutriment despite destructive consequences.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Objector** — asks why only four nutriments are taught if all `sāsrava` dharmas nourish becoming.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — answers through `prādhānya`, assigns the four functions, and develops the `catuṣkoṭi` analysis.
- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — explicitly identified in the thesis that nutriment performs its function both while being consumed and while being digested.

## New Technical Terms

- **prādhānya** — predominance / principal efficacy
  - **status:** previously encountered as a classificatory principle
  - **function:** explains why four nutriments are singled out

- **sendriya-kāya** — body endowed with faculties
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation here
  - **function:** defines `āśraya`

- **āśrita** — that which depends upon a support
  - **status:** Kārikā term clarified by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** identifies mind and associated mental factors as supported

- **poṣaṇa** — nourishment / fostering
  - **status:** recurring Bhāṣya vocabulary
  - **function:** maintenance of already-arisen becoming

- **ākṣepa** — projection / throwing forward
  - **status:** Kārikā term receiving exact functional assignment
  - **function:** function of `manaḥsaṃcetanā` in producing renewed existence

- **karmaparibhāvita** — conditioned / impregnated by karma
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific here
  - **function:** qualifies the `vijñāna-bīja` from which future existence emerges

- **vijñāna-bīja** — seed of knowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific expression here
  - **function:** basis from which projected future existence emerges

- **abhinirvṛtti** — emergence / coming into completion
  - **status:** encountered in the preceding `antarābhava` discussion
  - **function:** production of projected renewed existence

- **dhātrī-sthānīya** — analogous to a nurse
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only analogy here
  - **function:** characterizes the first two nutriments as maintainers

- **mātṛ-sthānīya** — analogous to a mother
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only analogy here
  - **function:** characterizes the latter two nutriments as generators

- **catuṣkoṭika** — four-cornered analysis
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical procedure
  - **function:** distinguishes ordinary ingestion from technical nutriment

- **āpāta** — moment of occurrence / immediate incidence
  - **status:** local Bhāṣya usage
  - **function:** explains why harmful food can initially count as nutriment

- **āhāra-kṛtya** — nutritive function
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation
  - **function:** functional test for nutriment

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives the four nutriments an exact ordered architecture:

```text
PRESENT EXISTENCE

āśraya: sendriya-kāya
    ← nourished by kavaḍīkāra

āśrita: citta-caitta
    ← nourished by sparśa

FUTURE EXISTENCE

manaḥsaṃcetanā
    → ākṣepa of punarbhava

karmaparibhāvita vijñāna-bīja
    → abhinirvṛtti of the projected bhava
```

This explains why four are privileged even though other `sāsrava` dharmas can contribute to the continuation of becoming. These four are the predominant maintainers and generators.

It also corrects the first-pass reading of the final compound. The verse does **not** assign one of the latter nutriments to the cessation of another existence. The Bhāṣya unmistakably interprets the pair as:

```text
ākṣepa
    projection

nirvṛtti / abhinirvṛtti
    production / emergence
```

Thus `nivṛtti` in the earlier first-pass analysis was a mistaken segmentation. The intended term is `nirvṛtti`.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Kārikā should be read:

```iast
iha puṣṭyartham āśrayāśritayor dvayam /
dvayam anyabhavākṣepa-nirvṛtty-arthaṃ yathākramam //
```

A controlled translation is:

> Here, two are for the nourishment of the support and what is supported; the other two are, respectively, for the projection and production of another existence.

The Bhāṣya fixes the correspondence:

```text
kavaḍīkāra → nourishment of āśraya
sparśa      → nourishment of āśrita
saṃcetanā   → projection of another bhava
vijñāna     → production of that projected bhava
```

This completes the functional explanation of the four nutriments in VAkK 3.41.
