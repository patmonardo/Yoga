## Natural Argument

VAK 3.28 established that avidyā is neither mere absence of vidyā nor an indeterminate non-being. VAK 3.29 now rejects a second reduction: avidyā is not simply faulty prajñā.

### 1. The proposed reduction

**Voice: Objector / proposed interpretation**

```iast
atha manyase kuprajñaivāvidyeti
```

> Suppose you think that avidyā is simply faulty prajñā.

This is a natural proposal. If avidyā concerns error or non-knowing, perhaps it is merely a defective mode of discriminative knowing.

### 2. Vasubandhu rejects the identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

```iast
na /
```

> No.

The reason is `darśanāt`, because of the case of views.

```iast
dṛṣṭayo hi kuprajñāḥ
```

> Views are indeed faulty prajñā.

Thus Vasubandhu grants that there really are cases of bad or defective prajñā. Wrong views belong to that class.

### 3. But wrong views are associated with avidyā

The decisive step is:

```iast
tāś cāvidyayā saṃprayuktā uktāḥ
```

> And those views are said to be associated with avidyā.

This establishes a distinction between the two factors.

```text
ku-prajñā / dṛṣṭi
    is associated with
avidyā
```

If the two were identical, this association relation would collapse.

Therefore:

```iast
tasmād avidyā na kuprajñā
```

> Therefore avidyā is not faulty prajñā.

### 4. Avidyā is taught as a defilement of prajñā

Vasubandhu adds a second argument:

```iast
avidyā hi prajñāyā upakleśa iti deśyate
```

> Avidyā is taught as a defilement of prajñā.

Then the reductio:

```iast
yadi saiva prajñā syāt kathaṃ prajñāyā upakleśaḥ syāt
```

> If it were itself prajñā, how could it be a defilement of prajñā?

Again, the relation requires distinction.

```text
prajñā
    can be defiled by
avidyā

therefore
avidyā ≠ prajñā
```

---

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

Suppose one proposes that ignorance is simply faulty prajñā. That cannot be maintained. Wrong views are themselves instances of faulty prajñā, yet they are taught as associated with ignorance. Therefore ignorance cannot be identical with faulty prajñā.

Moreover, ignorance is taught as a defilement of prajñā. If ignorance were itself prajñā, it could not stand to prajñā as a defiling condition. Avidyā must therefore be maintained as a distinct afflictive dharma whose presence can deform or accompany cognitive determinations without being reducible to those determinations.

---

## The Crucial Result: Avidyā Is the Condition of Cognitive Error, Not Simply the Error Itself

The Bhāṣya gives us a precise three-term distinction:

```text
prajñā
    discriminative knowing

ku-prajñā / dṛṣṭi
    defective discriminative knowing

avidyā
    distinct afflictive condition
    associated with and defiling prajñā
```

This is stronger than saying:

```text
avidyā = wrong belief
```

or:

```text
avidyā = bad reasoning
```

Instead:

```text
avidyā
    ↓
conditions / accompanies cognitive distortion
    ↓
ku-prajñā / dṛṣṭi
```

That makes avidyā structurally prior to many specific cognitive errors without making it a mere absence.

## Voices

**Voice: Objector / proposed interpretation** — suggests that avidyā might simply be `ku-prajñā`, faulty prajñā.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — rejects the identification by distinguishing avidyā from wrong views and by invoking the teaching that avidyā defiles prajñā.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **ku-prajñā** — faulty or defective prajñā.
  - **status:** explicit in the Kārikā and Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** rejected candidate definition of avidyā; includes wrong views.

- **dṛṣṭi** — view, especially wrong view in this context.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal term.
  - **function:** example of faulty prajñā that is nevertheless associated with avidyā.

- **saṃprayukta** — associated, conjoined.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya technical relation.
  - **function:** preserves distinction between avidyā and dṛṣṭi while allowing them to occur together.

- **upakleśa** — defilement, contaminating factor.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit in this unit.
  - **function:** defines avidyā's relation to prajñā as contaminating rather than identical.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 3.28–3.29 together eliminate three inadequate definitions:

```text
avidyā ≠ absence of vidyā
avidyā ≠ everything non-vidyā
avidyā ≠ ku-prajñā
```

The positive determination emerging is:

```text
avidyā
    = distinct dharma
    opposed to vidyā
    associated with wrong views
    defiling prajñā
```

This explains how avidyā can function causally at the head of dependent co-arising while specific erroneous views remain distinct mental determinations.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Refinement, not replacement.**

The Kārikā should not be read as saying merely that ignorance is "not bad intelligence." The Bhāṣya's technical distinction is sharper:

> Wrong views are themselves faulty prajñā, yet they are associated with avidyā; avidyā is also taught as a defilement of prajñā. Therefore avidyā cannot be identical with faulty prajñā.

This preserves the relational architecture of the mental factors.

---

## Logical Determination

The argument has the form of a non-identity proof through relation.

```text
A associated with B
    → A and B are not identical in the relevant analysis

A defiles B
    → A and B must be distinguishable as functions
```

Applied here:

```text
dṛṣṭi associated with avidyā
    → dṛṣṭi ≠ avidyā

avidyā defiles prajñā
    → avidyā ≠ prajñā
```

This is systematic determination by functional relation.

A term is not defined in isolation; its identity is constrained by the relations it can enter into.

---

## Organon Light

The restrained Organon result is very strong.

An Organon concerned with learning and correction must distinguish at least two layers of error:

```text
1. erroneous determination
   wrong view / ku-prajñā

2. condition that systematically distorts determination
   avidyā
```

That means a learning system cannot assume every failure is repaired by replacing one false proposition with a true one.

Sometimes:

```text
wrong output
    is downstream of
faulted capacity / condition of judging
```

So there are at least two possible interventions:

```text
correct the judgment

or

transform the condition under which judgments are generated
```

This is precisely where the later Practical Path vocabulary should become important. `Pratipakṣa` need not merely negate a proposition; it may oppose the afflictive condition that keeps producing bad determinations.

That is only an Organon Light at this stage, but the Bhāṣya has earned the distinction.

## Commit Note

VAK_3.29 Bhāṣya analysis.

Established:

- avidyā is not reducible to `ku-prajñā`;
- wrong views (`dṛṣṭi`) are instances of faulty prajñā but are associated with avidyā;
- avidyā is taught as an `upakleśa` of prajñā;

> Error may exist at the level of a particular judgment, but it may also arise from a deeper condition that systematically deforms the capacity for judgment. A complete Science of Learning must distinguish correction of outputs from transformation of the conditions that generate them.
