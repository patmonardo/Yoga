# VAK_3.23_bhasya — Abhidharmakośabhāṣya on VAkK 3.23

## Kārikā

```iast
vittiḥ prāk maithunāt tṛṣṇā bhogamaithunarāgiṇaḥ /
upādānaṃ tu bhogānāṃ prāptaye paridhāvataḥ // VAkK_3.23 //
```

**Best translation:**

> Feeling occupies the stage before sexual coupling. Craving is the state of one attached to enjoyment and coupling. Appropriation is the state of one running about in pursuit of the acquisition of enjoyments.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_3.23.md`](./VAK_3.23.md)

### Source note

The Bhāṣya directly identifies the Kārikā's `vittiḥ` with a `vedanā-avasthā`, a stage of feeling. The supplied source is clear in this unit and no conjectural normalization is required.

---

## Natural Argument

The passage continues directly from the end of VAK 3.22. Once the continuum has acquired the capacity for `pariccheda`—discrimination of the causes of the three feelings—the next stage is called `vittiḥ`, which Vasubandhu explicitly glosses as the stage of `vedanā`.

The movement through 3.23 is therefore not simply:

```text
feeling → craving → appropriation
```

It is a developmental transition from **affective experience**, to **passionate orientation toward objects**, to **active pursuit for their acquisition**.

### 1. `Vittiḥ` is explicitly the vedanā-stage

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The opening condition comes from 3.22:

```iast
paricchedasāmarthye sati
```

> when the capacity for discrimination is present

Then the Kārikā says:

```iast
vittiḥ prāk maithunāt
```

Vasubandhu glosses:

```iast
vedanāvasthā
```

> the stage of feeling

Thus the Bhāṣya settles the first-pass uncertainty around `vittiḥ`: in this context it is the designation of the `vedanā` member of dependent co-arising.

The stage extends until sexual passion becomes actively manifest:

```iast
yāvan maithunarāgo na samudācarati
```

So `vedanā` here is not yet craving. Feeling has become discriminable, but passionate pursuit has not yet arisen.

```text
pariccheda-sāmarthya
    ↓
vedanā / vitti-stage
    ↓
maithuna-rāga becomes active
```

### 2. `Tṛṣṇā` is the stage in which passion becomes actively manifest

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu defines craving through:

```iast
kāmaguṇa-maithuna-rāga-samudācāra-avasthā
```

> the stage in which passion for sensual enjoyments and sexual intercourse becomes actively manifest

This is more precise than treating `tṛṣṇā` as abstract desire.

The key term is `samudācāra`: manifestation, active occurrence, coming into operation.

```text
vedanā
    affective experience

rāga-samudācāra
    passion becomes operative
        ↓
tṛṣṇā
```

Craving is therefore a stage characterized by the **actual activation of passion** toward enjoyment.

### 3. Tṛṣṇā remains distinct from active search

The Bhāṣya sets a clear upper boundary for the craving-stage:

```iast
yāvanna tadviṣayaparyeṣṭimāpadyate
```

> until one enters into search for that object

This is doctrinally important.

Craving is not yet the same as pursuing the object.

```text
passion toward object
    = tṛṣṇā

search for object
    = transition beyond tṛṣṇā
```

The practical distinction is therefore not merely quantitative, as though `upādāna` were simply stronger craving. A new mode of activity appears.

### 4. `Upādāna` is the stage of active acquisition-oriented pursuit

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The prose explanation is vivid:

```iast
yasyāmavasthāyāṃ viṣayaprāptaye paryeṣṭimāpannaḥ sarvato dhāvati
```

> the stage in which, having entered upon a search for obtaining the object, one runs about everywhere

That stage is called `upādāna`.

The structure is:

```text
tṛṣṇā
    object is desired
        ↓
paryeṣṭi
    search begins
        ↓
sarvato dhāvati
    active pursuit in every direction
        ↓
upādāna
```

The decisive practical term is `prāpti`: acquisition, obtaining.

`Upādāna` is thus defined here by a teleological orientation toward acquisition.

---

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

Once the capacity for discrimination has arisen, the stage of feeling continues until sexual passion becomes actively manifest. This is the stage called `vittiḥ`, that is, the stage of `vedanā`.

When passion for sensual enjoyments and sexual intercourse becomes active, the stage is called craving. It continues as craving until the person enters into a search for the desired object.

When, for the sake of obtaining the object, the person enters upon that search and runs about in every direction, this stage is called appropriation.

Thus the sequence marks a transition from affective experience, to active passion, to acquisition-oriented pursuit.

---

## The Crucial Result: Desire Becomes Practical in Stages

VAK 3.23 provides a very clean practical articulation:

```text
vedanā
    affective experience
        ↓

tṛṣṇā
    passion becomes operative
        ↓

upādāna
    active search for acquisition
```

The important point is that these are **functionally distinct stages**.

`Tṛṣṇā` is not yet acquisition.

`Upādāna` begins when desire reorganizes activity around obtaining its object.

This is one of the clearest points so far where dependent co-arising moves from receptive-affective determination into practical action.

## New Technical Terms

- **vedanā-avasthā** — stage of feeling.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit clarification of the Kārikā's `vittiḥ`.
  - **function:** identifies `vittiḥ` with the vedanā member of dependent co-arising.

- **samudācāra** — active manifestation, occurrence, coming into operation.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya process vocabulary.
  - **function:** marks the transition from feeling to craving when passion becomes active.

- **kāmaguṇa** — sensual enjoyment / sensual quality.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit in this unit.
  - **function:** object-domain toward which craving is directed.

- **rāga** — passion, attachment.
  - **status:** previously known doctrinal term, functionally specified here.
  - **function:** operative affective determination defining the tṛṣṇā-stage.

- **paryeṣṭi** — search, seeking, pursuit.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific practical vocabulary.
  - **function:** marks the transition from craving to appropriation.

- **viṣaya-prāpti** — obtaining/acquisition of the object.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya practical vocabulary.
  - **function:** teleological end of the upādāna-stage.

- **sarvato dhāvati** — runs about everywhere.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya descriptive formulation.
  - **function:** makes the practical activation of appropriation concrete.

## Doctrinal Determination

The passage gives the next three stages of the present-life middle:

```text
7. vedanā / vittiḥ
8. tṛṣṇā
9. upādāna
```

Their functional determination is:

```text
vedanā
    feeling is available to discrimination
    but passion has not yet become operative

 tṛṣṇā
    passion for enjoyment becomes actively manifest
    but active search has not yet begun

upādāna
    search and acquisition-oriented pursuit are underway
```

The stages are therefore distinguished by **what the agent can now do and what motivational structure is active**.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Refinement required.**

The Bhāṣya removes the uncertainty around `vittiḥ` by explicitly calling it a `vedanā-avasthā`.

Therefore the preferred translation should use **feeling** rather than the more tentative “affective experience.”

A Bhāṣya-informed translation is:

> Feeling occupies the stage before sexual passion becomes active. Craving is the stage of one in whom passion for sensual enjoyment and sexual intercourse is active. Appropriation is the stage of one who, seeking to obtain enjoyments, runs about in pursuit of them.

The main conceptual correction is that `upādāna` is not simply intensified desire; it is desire entering **search and acquisition-oriented activity**.

---

## Logical Determination

The transition can be expressed as:

```text
being affected
    ↓
passion becomes operative
    ↓
activity becomes organized around acquisition
```

Or more formally:

```text
vedanā
    receptive-affective determination

        ↓ samudācāra

tṛṣṇā
    motivational determination

        ↓ paryeṣṭi

upādāna
    practical-teleological determination
```

The new element at `upādāna` is not simply intensity but **purposeful pursuit**.

## Organon Light

This passage is a particularly strong first-pass contact point for an Organon of agency, provided the Kośa determination remains primary.

Vasubandhu gives us three distinct moments:

```text
1. something is felt
2. the object becomes desired
3. action is reorganized toward acquiring it
```

An Organon reading would notice that the practical agent does not appear all at once.

The agent becomes increasingly determinate through the sequence:

```text
Affective View
    "this affects me"
        ↓
Motivated View
    "this is desirable"
        ↓
Practical View
    "this is to be obtained"
```

The transition from `tṛṣṇā` to `upādāna` is especially important for a future Agential View architecture:

```text
object as desired
    ↓
object as end
    ↓
search-space organized around acquisition
```

That is a recognizable transition from **representation with affective value** to **teleological practical organization**.

But the negative practical character must remain visible. In this context the teleology is generated by craving and appropriation; it belongs to the machinery of saṃsāra, not yet to rational freedom.

That gives the future Organon Bhāṣya a useful distinction:

```text
teleology as such
    ≠ freedom

teleology under tṛṣṇā-upādāna
    = conditioned pursuit
```

A Science of Agency will eventually need to discriminate between:

```text
conditioned teleology
    object acquisition under craving

and

rational teleology
    Knowledge / Freedom as end
```

Vasubandhu is already supplying the anatomy of the first.
