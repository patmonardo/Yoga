## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu now reorganizes the twelve members a second time. The previous verses gave their temporal and developmental articulation; 3.26 groups them by **functional nature**.

### 1. The twelve-member series has three natures

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The opening statement is explicit:

```iast
dvādaśāṅgaḥ pratītyasamutpādas trisvabhāvo veditavyaḥ
```

> The twelve-member dependent co-arising is to be understood as having three natures.

Those three are:

```text
kleśa
karma
vastu
```

This is not another chronological division. It is a functional analysis of what the members are doing within the process.

### 2. Three members are kleśa

The three afflictive members are:

```text
avidyā
tṛṣṇā
upādāna
```

These are `kleśasvabhāva`: their functional nature is afflictive.

```text
avidyā
    prior obscuration

tṛṣṇā
    craving

upādāna
    appropriation
```

The middle section therefore contains not merely result but also the renewed afflictive causes that make future becoming possible.

### 3. Two members are karma

The karmic members are:

```text
saṃskāra
bhava
```

Vasubandhu explicitly calls both `karmasvabhāva`.

This confirms the Bhāṣya determination already reached at 3.24: `bhava` in this twelve-member analysis is not merely static existence. It belongs structurally with `saṃskāra` as karma productive of further existence.

### 4. Seven members are vastu

The seven are:

```text
vijñāna
nāmarūpa
ṣaḍāyatana
sparśa
vedanā
jāti
jarāmaraṇa
```

Vasubandhu calls them `vastusvabhāva` because they serve as the support (`āśraya`) of affliction and karma:

```iast
kleśakarmāśrayatvāt
```

So `vastu` should not be flattened here into “substance.” Its role is structural:

```text
vastu
    = the conditioned basis/support
      upon which kleśa and karma operate
```

This gives the functional cycle:

```text
vastu
    supports kleśa and karma
        ↓
kleśa and karma
    generate further vastu
```

### 5. The same seven are also phala

Vasubandhu immediately adds:

```iast
saptaivāṅgāni phalabhūtāni
```

> Those same seven members are results.

The remaining five — the three kleśas and two karmas — are causes (`hetubhūta`).

Thus:

```text
CAUSE SIDE — 5
    kleśa 3
    karma 2

RESULT SIDE — 7
    vastu 7
```

The remarkable point is that `vastu` and `phala` are two determinations of the same seven members:

```text
as support:
    vastu

as produced:
    phala
```

### 6. Why is the middle analyzed in greater detail?

Vasubandhu asks directly why cause and result are expanded in the middle but compressed at the two temporal limits.

The answer is structural.

In the middle:

```text
result / vastu
    differentiated fivefold

kleśa
    differentiated twofold

karma
    represented by bhava
```

The future result is compressed into only two members:

```text
jāti
jarāmaraṇa
```

And the past cause is compressed into:

```text
avidyā
saṃskāra
```

The differences are not omissions. They are deliberate summary.

### 7. The past and future are reconstructible from the middle

The decisive sentence is:

```iast
madhyenaiva hi pūrvāntāparāntayor api hetuphalavistaraḥ śakyo 'numātum
```

> From the middle itself, the expanded cause-and-result structure of the prior and later limits can also be inferred.

Therefore it is not stated again.

Vasubandhu even gives the practical reason: there is no need to undertake the same effort redundantly.

```text
analyze the middle fully
        ↓
infer the causal structure of the past
        ↓
infer the result structure of the future
```

This makes the middle not merely temporally central but **epistemically privileged**.

## Philosophical Translation of the Bhāṣya

The twelve-member dependent co-arising is to be understood as having three functional natures: affliction, karma, and factual support.

Ignorance, craving, and appropriation are afflictions. Formations and becoming are karma. Knowledge, name-and-form, the six spheres, contact, feeling, birth, and aging-and-death are factual supports, because they serve as the support of affliction and karma.

Those same seven factual-support members are also results, while the remaining five members — the three afflictions and two karmic members — are causes.

Cause and result are displayed in expanded form in the middle section because the resultant factual series and the afflictive causes are differentiated there. At the future limit the result is compressed into birth and aging-and-death, while at the past limit the causes are compressed into ignorance and formations.

This compression does not leave the two limits unexplained. The expanded cause-and-result structure of both the prior and later limits can be inferred from the middle itself; hence it is unnecessary to repeat the same analysis.

## The Crucial Result: One Series, Three Functional Views

The same twelve-member process can now be viewed in three ways:

```text
as afflictive determination:
    kleśa

as productive action:
    karma

as conditioned support/result:
    vastu / phala
```

This is not arbitrary relabeling. The categories express distinct functions within one conditioned process.

Most importantly:

```text
vastu
    is support for kleśa and karma

but also

vastu
    is result of prior kleśa and karma
```

So the result becomes the support upon which new causes operate.

```text
kleśa + karma
    ↓
phala / vastu
    ↓
new kleśa + karma
```

This is the internal reproductive logic of saṃsāra.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — gives the threefold functional classification, identifies the seven `vastu` members with the seven result-members, and explains the compression of the prior and later limits by inference from the middle.

No separate objector appears. The questions are exegetical questions raised within Vasubandhu's exposition.

## New Technical Terms

- **trisvabhāva** — having three functional natures.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit formulation in this unit.
  - **function:** groups the twelve members as kleśa, karma, and vastu.

- **kleśasvabhāva** — having affliction as its functional nature.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** classifies avidyā, tṛṣṇā, and upādāna.

- **karmasvabhāva** — having karma as its functional nature.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** classifies saṃskāra and bhava.

- **vastusvabhāva** — having the nature of conditioned support/basis.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** classifies vijñāna through vedanā together with jāti and jarāmaraṇa.

- **āśraya** — support.
  - **status:** previously encountered, newly decisive here.
  - **function:** explains why the seven members are called `vastu`: they support the operation of kleśa and karma.

- **phalabhūta** — having the nature of result.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** identifies the seven vastu members as the result side of the process.

- **hetubhūta** — having the nature of cause/ground.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **function:** identifies the three kleśa and two karma members as causal.

- **abhisaṃkṣepa** — compression, condensed presentation.
  - **status:** Kārikā term clarified by prose.
  - **function:** explains why past cause and future result are not displayed with the same internal detail as the middle.

- **anumāna / anumātum** — inference / to infer.
  - **status:** explicitly important here.
  - **function:** establishes the epistemic relation by which the two limits are reconstructed from the middle.

## Doctrinal Determination

The twelve-member process now admits two superimposed architectures.

Temporal:

```text
past cause
    2

present middle
    8

future result
    2
```

Functional:

```text
kleśa
    3

karma
    2

vastu / phala
    7
```

The two schemes intersect rather than replace one another.

The present middle contains both:

```text
result inherited from prior causes
        +
new afflictive and karmic causes
```

That is why the middle can reveal both directions of the cycle.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Refinement rather than replacement.**

The Kārikā analysis was substantially correct, but `vastu` should be read more precisely from the Bhāṣya as the **conditioned support/basis of kleśa and karma**, not as “real thing” or substance.

The final pāda should also be understood strongly:

```iast
madhyānumānataḥ
```

means that the expanded causal/result structure of the two limits is **inferable from the middle**, and for that reason is compressed in the verse.

A Bhāṣya-informed translation is therefore:

> Three are afflictions, two are karma, and seven are conditioned supports, which are likewise results. Cause and result are compressed at the two limits because their expanded structure can be inferred from the middle.

## Logical Determination

The logic of 3.26 is recursive rather than merely linear.

```text
CAUSE
kleśa + karma
    ↓

RESULT
vastu / phala
    ↓

SUPPORT
for renewed kleśa + karma
```

A result is not inert. Once produced, it becomes the conditioned basis for a new cycle of causal activity.

Equally important is the epistemic architecture:

```text
manifest middle
    ↓
analysis of cause and result within it
    ↓
inference backward to prior cause
    ↓
inference forward to future result
```

Thus the middle has a dual function:

```text
ontological:
    where inherited result becomes new cause

epistemic:
    where the hidden two limits become inferable
```

## Organon Light

Kept separate from the doctrinal reconstruction, an Organon immediately notices two structures here.

First, the same determinate field is being viewed according to **functional role**:

```text
one conditioned series
    ↓
View as obstruction/motive
    = kleśa

View as productive activity
    = karma

View as conditioned support/result
    = vastu / phala
```

That is close to the emerging idea of an **Agential View**: the object is not changed arbitrarily; its systematic role changes according to the rational question being asked.

Second, Vasubandhu gives a striking rule of rational-empirical reconstruction:

```text
Middle
    = accessible articulated field

Prior limit
    = inferred causal ground

Later limit
    = inferred consequence
```

The Organon Light should resist translating this immediately into a fixed Hegelian category. What matters first is the architecture Vasubandhu himself supplies:

> **A sufficiently articulated present field carries within it the rational grounds for reconstructing both what conditioned it and what it conditions.**

This may eventually be extremely important for a Science of Learning. The Agent does not need direct possession of every temporal state. It can know the unseen limits through a principled analysis of the middle.

That is already more than statistical projection, but we should let the Kośa continue to determine what kind of inference it actually means before formalizing it.
