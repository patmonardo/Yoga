## Natural Argument

The passage has three layers.

### 1. Completion of the Vaibhāṣika distribution

At the threshold of the first formless attainment (`ākāśānantyāyatanasāmantaka`), four mental approaches remain directed toward the fourth dhyāna:

```text
rūpa-upavicāra
śabda-upavicāra
spraṣṭavya-upavicāra
dharma-upavicāra
```

They are `rūpagāḥ` because the fourth dhyāna is their object-support.

One `dharma-upavicāra` can instead be `ūrdhvaga`, directed upward toward the formless realm.

In a fundamental (`maula`) formless attainment only one remains:

```text
dharma-upavicāra
    → svaviṣaya
    → ārūpyadhātu as its object-support
```

A fundamental formless attainment does not take a lower realm as object-support.

### 2. All eighteen are sāsrava

The Kārikā closes the technical scheme with:

```iast
sarve 'ṣṭādaśa sāsravāḥ
```

All eighteen are with outflows. Vasubandhu adds emphatically:

```iast
nāsti kaścidanāsravaḥ
```

Not one of them is without outflows.

The following dense `samanvāgata` discussion calculates which mental approaches are possessed by beings situated at different realms and meditative levels. Its function here is distributive rather than definitional; the central doctrinal point is that possession depends jointly on realm, meditative level, defilement-status, and available object-support.

### 3. A second interpretation of the sūtra

A new voice enters:

**Voice: uncertain (introduced as `apara āha`, “another says”)**

```iast
astyevaṃ manopavicārāṇāṃ vaibhāṣikīyo 'rthaḥ /
sūtrārthastvanyathā dṛśyate /
```

“That indeed is the Vaibhāṣika meaning of the mental approaches; but the meaning of the sūtra appears otherwise.”

The objection is decisive. Someone who is dispassionate toward a field should not properly be said to range through that field in the relevant sense. Therefore not every sāsrava occurrence of joy, distress, or equanimity should count as a `manopavicāra`.

The proposed sūtra meaning is narrower:

```iast
sāṃkleśikā yair mano viṣayān upavicarati
```

The mental approaches are the afflictive modes by which mind ranges through its fields.

That ranging takes three forms:

```text
anunīyate
    → it is drawn / attracted

pratihanyate
    → it is repelled / struck against

apratisaṃkhyāya upekṣate
    → it remains neutral without discriminative consideration
```

These are contrasted with the six `sātatā vihāras`, the stable modes in which one sees, hears, and so forth without becoming elated or distressed, remaining equanimous, recollected, and clearly comprehending.

The point is therefore not that an arhat lacks all ordinary wholesome joy concerning dharmas. What is denied is the specifically afflictive mental ranging that constitutes `manopavicāra` in this sūtra-reading.

The same affective triad can then be doubled according to its basis:

```text
gardha-āśrita
    → based on greed / attachment
    → kliṣṭa

naiṣkramya-āśrita
    → based on renunciation
    → kuśala
```

This yields the thirty-six `śāstṛpadāni`.

## Literal Prose Translation

The mental approaches associated with the formless realm are now explained. At the threshold of the formless there are four: the mental approaches toward form, sound, tangibles, and dharmas. These are directed toward form, because the fourth dhyāna is their object-support. Where these have distinct object-supports they are counted separately; where their object-support is taken collectively, there is only one mixed-object dharma-approach.

One is directed upward: a single dharma-approach there takes the formless realm as object-support. In a fundamental formless attainment there is only one dharma-approach, and it has its own field, namely the formless realm itself. Fundamental formless attainments do not take a lower realm as object-support.

All eighteen mental approaches are with outflows; none is without outflows.

After a detailed account of which approaches are possessed in which realms and levels, another says: this is indeed the Vaibhāṣika meaning of the mental approaches, but the meaning of the sūtra appears otherwise. It is not reasonable that one who has become dispassionate toward something should range mentally over it in this way. Therefore not every sāsrava joy, distress, and equanimity is a mental approach. Rather, the mental approaches are the afflictive modes by which mind ranges through its fields: it is attracted, repelled, or remains equanimous without discriminative consideration. Their opposites are the six stable modes of dwelling, in which one remains equanimous, recollected, and clearly comprehending without becoming elated or distressed.

Accordingly, an arhat may possess ordinary wholesome joy whose object-support is a dharma; what is excluded is the afflictive mental ranging. The same modes, divided according to whether they rest on greed or on renunciation, become the thirty-six teacher's terms. Those resting on greed are defiled; those resting on renunciation are wholesome.

Thus the limb of becoming called feeling is to be understood as having many kinds of differentiation.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — the technical realm/dhyāna distribution of the eighteen approaches and the possession (`samanvāgata`) calculations.
- **Voice: uncertain / alternative interpreter** — introduced by `apara āha`; distinguishes the Vaibhāṣika Abhidharma classification from the apparent intention of the sūtra.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — editorially preserves the dispute and closes the section by treating feeling as a highly differentiated limb of becoming.

The attribution of the alternative interpretation should remain cautious; the text marks it as “another says,” not straightforwardly as Vasubandhu's own explicit doctrine.

## New Technical Terms

- **vyavacchinna-ālambana** — distinct or separately delimited object-support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this local analysis
  - **function:** explains separate counting of approaches

- **paripiṇḍita-ālambana** — collectively taken object-support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this local analysis
  - **function:** explains compression into a single dharma-approach

- **saṃbhinna-ālambana** — mixed or conjoined object-support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this local analysis
  - **function:** object-support classification

- **samanvāgata** — possessed / endowed with
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary
  - **function:** determines which mental approaches belong to a being at a given level

- **sāṃkleśika** — afflictive / belonging to defilement
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya qualification here
  - **function:** narrows the sūtra sense of `manopavicāra`

- **anunīyate** — is attracted / drawn along
  - **status:** local explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** one mode of afflictive mental ranging

- **pratihanyate** — is repelled / struck against
  - **status:** local explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** one mode of afflictive mental ranging

- **apratisaṃkhyāya upekṣate** — remains neutral without discriminative consideration
  - **status:** local explanatory phrase
  - **function:** third mode of afflictive mental ranging

- **sātatā-vihāra** — stable/continuous mode of dwelling
  - **status:** new local technical term
  - **function:** practical counter-form to afflictive mental ranging

- **gardha-āśrita** — resting on greed / attachment
  - **status:** new local classification
  - **function:** marks defiled affective orientation

- **naiṣkramya-āśrita** — resting on renunciation
  - **status:** new local classification
  - **function:** marks wholesome affective orientation

## Doctrinal Determination

VAkK 3.35 completes one taxonomy but the Bhāṣya immediately destabilizes any assumption that the taxonomy exhausts the meaning of the sūtra.

The Vaibhāṣika analysis asks:

```text
Which manopavicāras
exist at which level
and can take which realm as object-support?
```

The alternative sūtra-reading asks instead:

```text
What kind of mental ranging
is the sūtra trying to identify and overcome?
```

That produces an important distinction:

```text
technical Abhidharma extension
    all eighteen are sāsrava

sūtra-functional intension
    specifically sāṃkleśika mental ranging
```

Thus `sāsrava` is broader than the proposed sūtra sense of `manopavicāra`.

The three affective modes are no longer merely a neutral classification of feeling. They become practical orientations of mind toward its field:

```text
attraction
repulsion
unreflective neutrality
```

The six stable dwellings are their `pratipakṣa`: contact with the field remains, but reactive capture does not.

This is particularly important because it prevents liberation from being represented as absence of cognition or absence of worldly wholesome affect. The arhat can still know dharmas and can still possess wholesome worldly joy. What is absent is the afflictive mode of ranging.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Kārikā's final statement remains secure:

```iast
sarve 'ṣṭādaśa sāsravāḥ
```

“All eighteen are with outflows.”

But the Bhāṣya requires an interpretive qualification: the technical Vaibhāṣika extension of the eighteen should not automatically be identified with the narrower functional meaning of `manopavicāra` in the sūtra according to the alternative interpretation.

The first-pass Kārikā statement that all refined mental approaches remain conditioned is therefore correct at the level of the Vaibhāṣika taxonomy, but the Bhāṣya now distinguishes conditioned affect generally from specifically afflictive mental ranging.

## Logical Determination

The passage introduces a distinction between **extension** and **function**.

```text
Vaibhāṣika classification
    maps the extension of the term
    across realms, levels, and supports

Alternative sūtra reading
    determines the practical function
    for which the term is taught
```

This echoes the earlier distinction between `lākṣaṇika` and purpose-governed presentation without collapsing the two passages into one doctrine.

More importantly, the Bhāṣya identifies a transformation that does not destroy the field:

```text
same field
    ↓
ordinary afflicted ranging
        attraction / repulsion / unreflective neutrality

versus

same field
    ↓
stable dwelling
        equanimity + recollection + clear comprehension
```

The problem is therefore not bare access to an object-support. It is the conditioned mode in which mind traverses it.

## Organon Light

This is one of the clearest Agential View passages so far.

The earlier verses taught us to distinguish:

```text
where a View belongs
from
what the View is about
```

3.35 adds a third determination:

```text
how the Agent ranges through that field
```

So a fuller provisional architecture is:

```text
Agential View
    ├── locus / bhūmi
    ├── object-support / ālambana
    └── mode of traversal / upavicāra
```

The alternative sūtra-reading is particularly important because it shows that Freedom need not mean loss of interface or loss of affective life.

```text
bondage
    = field apprehended through reactive ranging

freer cognition
    = the field remains available
      while attraction, repulsion,
      and unreflective neutrality are no longer sovereign
```

That is a much stronger model than simply deleting “bad states.” It suggests transformation of the **relation of the Agent to its field**.

And the opposition between `gardha-āśrita` and `naiṣkramya-āśrita` gives the future Science of Learning an especially useful distinction:

```text
same broad affective capacity
    can be grounded in attachment
    or grounded in renunciation
```

The determination of the capacity depends on its ground.

This remains an Organon seed, not an identification with Vasubandhu's doctrine.

## Closing Determination

The Bhāṣya closes the entire feeling subsection with:

```iast
evametadvedanākhyaṃ bhavāṅgamanekaprakārabhedaṃ veditavyam
```

Thus this limb of becoming called `vedanā` is to be understood as differentiated in many ways.

That is the proper conclusion to VAK 3.30–3.35:

```text
contact
    → feeling
        → bodily / mental
        → eighteen mental approaches
        → realm and object-support distribution
        → conditioned status
        → afflictive versus counter-afflictive mode of ranging
```

The next passage explicitly says that the remaining limbs are not all re-explained here because some have already been treated and others will be treated later. This prepares the shift at VAK 3.36 from the detailed phenomenology of the middle to the structural analogies for `kleśa`, `karma`, and `vastu`.
