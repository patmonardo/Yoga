# VAK_3.34_bhasya — Lokanirdeśa

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAkK 3.34**

```iast
svo 'ṣṭālambanam ārūpyo dvayoḥ dhyānadvaye tu ṣaṭ /
kāmāḥ ṣaṇṇāṃ caturṇāṃ sva ekasyālambanaṃ paraḥ // VAkK_3.34 //
```

**Corrected working translation:**

Its own [form realm] is the object-support of eight; the formless realm is [the object-support] of two. In the latter pair of dhyānas there are only six. The desire realm is the object-support of all six, their own realm of four, and the higher realm of one.

## Natural Segmentation

### 1. The form realm is the “own” object-support of eight

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The pronoun `svaḥ` continues to refer to the form realm for the form-associated mental approaches discussed at the end of 3.33.

Vasubandhu makes the count explicit:

```text
rūpadhātu
    = own field of 8 manopavicāras
```

Why eight rather than twelve? Four approaches connected with odor and taste are excluded:

```text
gandha-upavicāra × 2 affective modes
rasa-upavicāra   × 2 affective modes
    = 4 excluded
```

Thus the first pāda does **not** mean that the formless realm has eight object-supports. The Bhāṣya corrects that possible first-pass parsing.

### 2. The formless realm is object-support for two

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

```text
ārūpyo dvayoḥ
```

means:

```text
ārūpyadhātu
    = ālambana of two dharma-upavicāras
```

In the first two dhyānas the relevant mental approaches include `saumanasya` and `upekṣā`; therefore two dharma-directed mental approaches can take the formless realm as their object-support.

### 3. In the third and fourth dhyānas only six remain

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

```text
dhyānadvaye tu ṣaṭ
```

The Bhāṣya identifies this pair unambiguously as the **third and fourth dhyānas**.

Only the six `upekṣā-upavicāras` exist there:

```text
3rd–4th dhyāna
    = 6 upekṣā-manopavicāras
    = no saumanasya set
    = no daurmanasya set
```

This is a major restriction of the affective mode while retaining the sixfold object-field structure.

### 4. Their lower, own, and higher object-supports

For these six equanimity-based approaches:

```text
kāmadhātu
    = object-support of all 6

rūpadhātu (their own realm)
    = object-support of 4
    odor and taste excluded

ārūpyadhātu (higher realm)
    = object-support of only 1
    the dharma-upavicāra
```

The final three pādas therefore encode a compact lower / own / higher topology of object-support.

## Literal Prose Translation

Their own [realm], namely the form realm, is the object-support of eight mental approaches, four approaches concerning odor and taste being omitted. The formless realm is the object-support of two dharma-approaches.

But in the third and fourth dhyānas there are only six, namely the six equanimity-based mental approaches and no others. For these six, the desire realm is an object-support for all six; their own form realm is an object-support for four, since odor and taste are absent there; and the higher, formless realm is the object-support of only one, the dharma-approach.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 3.34 continues the distribution begun in 3.33, but the Bhāṣya gives us the exact syntax and the exact counts.

```text
First two dhyānas:
    rūpadhātu as own support → 8
    ārūpyadhātu as higher support → 2

Third and fourth dhyānas:
    only 6 upekṣā-upavicāras exist

    lower kāmadhātu support → 6
    own rūpadhātu support   → 4
    higher ārūpyadhātu      → 1
```

The structure is therefore not a single hierarchy of mental states. It is a relation among at least three determinations:

```text
where the mental approach belongs
what affective mode it has
what realm it can take as object-support
```

These must not be collapsed.

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya corrects the first-pass treatment of the opening half.

The earlier provisional reading:

> “The formless one has its own eight object-supports.”

is incorrect.

The syntax is instead:

```text
svo 'ṣṭālambanam
    = its own [rūpadhātu] is object-support of eight

ārūpyo dvayoḥ
    = ārūpyadhātu is object-support of two
```

A better translation is:

> Their own [form realm] is the object-support of eight, and the formless realm of two. In the third and fourth dhyānas there are only six [equanimity approaches]. The desire realm is the object-support of all six, their own realm of four, and the higher realm of one.

This is a genuine Bhāṣya-driven correction to `VAK_3.34.md` and should be carried into the Kārikā cleanup at the end of Lokanirdeśa rather than repaired piecemeal now.

## New Technical Terms

- **adhara / lower** — implicit relational status of kāmadhātu relative to form-realm dhyāna
  - **status:** concept already structurally present, not foregrounded in the Kārikā
  - **function:** relational ordering of object-support fields

- **para / higher** — the higher realm serving as an object-support
  - **status:** present in Kārikā; clarified by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** relational ordering of object-support fields

- **upekṣopavicāra** — mental approach characterized by equanimity
  - **status:** derived from the preceding eighteenfold classification
  - **function:** sole manopavicāra type present in the third and fourth dhyānas

- **gandharasābhāva** — absence of odor and taste
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory condition here
  - **function:** explains the reduction from six possible object classes to four within rūpadhātu

## Doctrinal Determination

This passage finishes the form-associated distribution begun in 3.33.

The decisive point is not merely numerical. The numbers express **constraints on possible intentional range**.

```text
A manopavicāra has:
    a realm of association
    an affective determination
    a possible ālambana-range
```

Higher meditative placement does not imply unrestricted reach. In fact, the higher the relevant dhyāna here, the more restricted the affective repertoire becomes:

```text
first/second dhyāna
    saumanasya + upekṣā available

third/fourth dhyāna
    upekṣā only
```

At the same time, an approach constituted in a higher field may still take a lower realm as object-support.

Thus:

```text
higher mode of constitution
    ≠
exclusion of lower object-domain
```

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a relational matrix rather than a simple ladder:

```text
Agent-position / field of association
        ×
Affective mode
        ×
Object-support field
```

The validity of a particular mental approach depends on the compatibility of all three.

This develops the lesson of 3.33:

```text
location of cognition
    ≠ object of cognition
```

and adds:

```text
affective capacity
    constrains which approaches are available
```

So the Abhidharma map is already a constrained theory of possible cognitive-affective relations.

## Organon Light

Kept separate from the Bhāṣya reconstruction:

The striking point here is **typed accessibility**.

A View is not characterized merely by having an object. It has a determinate standpoint and a determinate mode, and those jointly constrain what can be its object-support.

```text
View
    standpoint / field
    affective mode
    admissible object-support range
```

That is much stronger than saying “a concept has a container.” It suggests an eventual Agential View should encode **conditions of admissible access**.

And there is another useful result:

```text
refinement of the agent-state
    does not simply erase the lower field;
    it changes the mode under which that field can be taken up.
```

The third and fourth dhyānas can still range over kāmadhātu, but only through the six equanimity approaches. The object-field remains available while the **mode of apprehension** has changed.

That is a promising seed for the future Organon Bhāṣya, but no identification with an Organon category is required here.

## Carry-Forward

VAK 3.35 now turns from the form-associated approaches to the **formless-associated** approaches and then states that all eighteen manopavicāras are `sāsrava`. The Bhāṣya also immediately preserves a dissenting interpretation of the sūtra against the Vaibhāṣika enumeration, so 3.35 should be another especially important Bhāṣya unit.
