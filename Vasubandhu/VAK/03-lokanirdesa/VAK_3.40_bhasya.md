**Working translation:**

Contact, volition, and knowledge are nutriments, with outflows, in the three realms. The intermediate existence is mind-made, seeking birth, and called a gandharva.

## Natural Argument

### 1. The remaining three nutriments

After the analysis of morsel nutriment in 3.39, Vasubandhu explains the other three:

```text
sparśa
manaḥsaṃcetanā
vijñāna
```

`Sparśa` is contact born from the conjunction of the triad (`trika-saṃnipāta`). `Cetanā` is identified as mental karma (`manas-karma`). `Vijñāna` is the fourth nutriment.

All three are nutriments only insofar as they are `sāsrava`, and all three occur in each of the three realms.

### 2. Why the anāsrava forms are not nutriment

An objection asks:

```iast
kimarthaṃ nānāsravāṇi /
```

Why are the without-outflow forms not nutriments?

The Vaibhāṣika answer is functional:

```iast
bhavāpoṣaṇārtha āhārārthaḥ
```

The meaning of nutriment is the nourishment or fostering of becoming.

But the anāsrava dharmas arise for the exhaustion of becoming (`bhavakṣaya`), not for its nourishment. Hence they are not nutriments.

Vasubandhu then appeals to the sūtra definition:

```text
The four nutriments are for:
    the persistence of already arisen beings,
    their maintenance,
    and the support of those seeking birth.
```

Anāsrava dharmas do not perform that function, so they are excluded from `āhāra`.

### 3. Who are the `saṃbhavaiṣiṇaḥ`?

The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
bhūta sattva
    = a being already arisen / reborn

saṃbhavaiṣin
    = one seeking coming-to-be
```

The question then becomes: who is this birth-seeking being?

The answer is `antarābhava`.

### 4. Four names of antarābhava

The intermediate existence is referred to by four expressions:

```text
manomaya
saṃbhavaiṣin
gandharva
abhinirvṛtti / nirvṛtti
```

#### `manomaya`

The intermediate existence is called mind-made because it is produced from mind (`manonirjāta`) and exists without taking up an external material basis such as semen and blood.

#### `saṃbhavaiṣin`

It is “seeking birth” because seeking coming-to-be is its characteristic activity (`saṃbhavaiṣaṇaśīlatva`).

#### `gandharva`

It is called `gandharva`; the Bhāṣya derives the designation from `gandharvaṇa`. The etymological force is not further explained in this local passage, so it should not be over-translated.

#### `abhinirvṛtti`

It is called `abhinirvṛtti` because it faces toward rebirth (`upapattyabhimukhatva`). Vasubandhu supports the usage with sūtra passages concerning the production of a bodily existence and the distinction between `abhinirvṛtti-saṃyojana` and `upapatti-saṃyojana`.

### 5. Scriptural confirmation by the fourfold alternative

A sūtra gives a fourfold alternative concerning the abandonment of the fetter of `abhinirvṛtti` and the fetter of `upapatti`.

Vasubandhu assigns the four cases:

```text
1. upward-streaming non-returner dispassionate toward two realms
2. one who attains parinirvāṇa in the intermediate existence
3. arhat
4. all remaining cases after excluding these configurations
```

The immediate function of this discussion is to establish that `abhinirvṛtti` and `upapatti` are distinct enough in scriptural usage to support `abhinirvṛtti` as a designation of the intermediate existence.

The passage then prepares the next question: how the four nutriments relate respectively to already-arisen beings and to those seeking birth.

## Literal Prose Translation

Contact, volition, and knowledge are nutriments, with outflows, in the three realms. Contact is born from the conjunction of the triad. Volition is mental karma, and knowledge also, insofar as they are with outflows, are nutriments and occur in all three realms.

Why are the without-outflow forms not nutriments? Because the meaning of nutriment is the fostering of becoming, whereas those arise for the exhaustion of becoming, according to the Vaibhāṣikas. Moreover, the sūtra says: “These four nutriments are for the persistence and maintenance of beings who have arisen, and for the support of those seeking birth.” Without-outflow dharmas are not of this kind and therefore are not nutriments.

Already-arisen beings are understood to be those who have been reborn. Who, then, are those seeking birth? The intermediate existence is mind-made, a seeker of birth, and a gandharva; it is also called `abhinirvṛtti`. The intermediate existence is called mind-made because it is born from mind and exists without taking up some external basis such as semen and blood. It is called one seeking birth because seeking coming-to-be is its characteristic. It is called gandharva, and it is called abhinirvṛtti because it faces toward rebirth.

This usage is supported by sūtra passages speaking of producing a bodily existence and of the distinction between the fetter of abhinirvṛtti and the fetter of rebirth. The fourfold scriptural alternative is then distributed among an upward-streaming non-returner, one who attains parinirvāṇa in the intermediate existence, an arhat, and the remaining cases.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose and scriptural coordination throughout.
- **Voice: Objector** — `kimarthaṃ nānāsravāṇi?`, asking why anāsrava forms are not nutriment.
- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — the answer that nutriment serves `bhavāpoṣaṇa`, whereas anāsrava dharmas arise for `bhavakṣaya`.

## New Technical Terms

- **bhavāpoṣaṇa** — nourishment / fostering of becoming
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation here
  - **function:** defines the function of nutriment

- **bhavakṣaya** — exhaustion / destruction of becoming
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific contrast here
  - **function:** explains why anāsrava dharmas are not nutriments

- **bhūta sattva** — already-arisen being
  - **status:** sūtra-derived terminology explained in Bhāṣya
  - **function:** distinguishes those already reborn from those seeking rebirth

- **saṃbhavaiṣin** — one seeking coming-to-be / seeking birth
  - **status:** Kārikā term explained by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** designation of antarābhava

- **manonirjāta** — born from mind
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanation
  - **function:** explains `manomaya`

- **saṃbhavaiṣaṇaśīla** — characterized by seeking coming-to-be
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanation
  - **function:** explains `saṃbhavaiṣin`

- **abhinirvṛtti** — production / emergence directed toward rebirth
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-added designation of antarābhava
  - **function:** scriptural synonym/near-synonym for the intermediate existence

- **upapattyabhimukha** — facing toward rebirth
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific explanation
  - **function:** explains `abhinirvṛtti`

- **abhinirvṛtti-saṃyojana** — fetter of intermediate production
  - **status:** scriptural term cited by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** distinguishes intermediate production from rebirth proper

- **upapatti-saṃyojana** — fetter of rebirth
  - **status:** scriptural term cited by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** paired with `abhinirvṛtti-saṃyojana`

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya gives `āhāra` a precise teleological definition within saṃsāra:

```text
āhāra
    → sustains already-arisen beings
    → supports beings seeking rebirth
    → fosters bhava
```

Therefore:

```text
sāsrava sparśa
sāsrava manaḥsaṃcetanā
sāsrava vijñāna
    = āhāra

anāsrava dharmas
    ≠ āhāra
```

because the latter tend toward `bhavakṣaya`, the exhaustion of becoming.

The second half then identifies the `saṃbhavaiṣin` of the sūtra with `antarābhava` and explains its terminology through function and mode of production.

This provides the transition from the doctrine of nutriment to the doctrine of intermediate existence without changing subjects arbitrarily: the sūtra definition of nutriment itself requires an account of the being who is “seeking birth.”

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first half should be translated with `sāsrava` governing the three mental nutriments:

```text
sparśa
manaḥsaṃcetanā
vijñāna
```

They are nutriments only as with-outflow factors operating within the three realms.

The second half should not treat `manomaya`, `saṃbhavaiṣin`, and `gandharva` as three different beings. They are three designations of the same `antarābhava`.

The Bhāṣya also adds a fourth designation, `abhinirvṛtti`, which should be noted in commentary even though it lies just beyond the metrical wording of the Kārikā.
