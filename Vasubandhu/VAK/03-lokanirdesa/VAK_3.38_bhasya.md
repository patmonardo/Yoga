## Natural Argument

### 1. Rebirth-becoming is necessarily defiled

The first claim is categorical:

```iast
upapattibhavaḥ kliṣṭaḥ
```

Vasubandhu glosses this with `ekāntena`: rebirth-becoming is **necessarily** or **without exception** defiled.

The question then becomes: by which affliction?

```iast
sarvakleśaiḥ svabhūmikaiḥ
```

By all afflictions belonging to the same level (`bhūmi`) as that rebirth-becoming.

The Abhidharmikas state that there is no affliction by which rebirth-linking (`pratisaṃdhi-bandha`) cannot occur. The qualification is important: the rebirth-linking is produced by the kleśas themselves, not by independent `paryavasthānas` taken as separate factors.

Although this state is weak (`mandikā`), the particular affliction that appears at that time is one that has been frequently cultivated and is proximate. Vasubandhu explains this through `pūrvāvedha`, the impulse or momentum coming from what preceded.

The same determination applies to the linking into intermediate existence:

```text
antarābhava-pratisaṃdhi
    → necessarily kliṣṭa
```

### 2. The other three bhavas are threefold

The old source uncertainty `tridhānye` is resolved directly by the Bhāṣya:

```iast
tridhā 'nye
```

The other three becomings are each of three kinds:

```text
kuśala
kliṣṭa
avyākṛta
```

The three are the remaining members of the fourfold becoming previously enumerated:

```text
antarābhava
pūrvakālabhava
maraṇabhava
```

The exceptional member is `upapattibhava`, which is necessarily defiled.

### 3. Only three bhavas occur in the formless realm

```iast
traya ārūpye
```

In the formless realm there are only three of the four becomings, because `antarābhava` is absent.

The Bhāṣya gives the reason:

```iast
nahyārūpyadhātuḥ sthānāntaraparicchinnaḥ
```

The formless realm is not delimited as another spatial place whose attainment would require the production of an intermediate existence.

Thus:

```text
ārūpyadhātu
    → upapattibhava
    → pūrvakālabhava
    → maraṇabhava
    → no antarābhava
```

By contrast, because no such restriction is stated for the desire and form realms, all four becomings are admitted there.

### 4. From arising to persistence

Vasubandhu then marks a clear transition in the argument.

Dependent co-arising has explained in detail **how sentient beings arise**:

```iast
ukto yathā sattvānāṃ pratītyasamutpādo vistareṇa
```

He now asks:

```iast
atha kathaṃ sattvānāṃ sthitir bhavati
```

How do sentient beings remain or persist?

The answer is the final pāda:

```iast
āhārasthitikaṃ jagat
```

The world persists through nutriment.

Vasubandhu cites the sūtra statement that the Blessed One, having directly known this one dharma, declared:

```iast
sarvasattvā āhārasthitikāḥ
```

“All sentient beings persist through nutriment.”

This opens the next analysis by asking what those nutriments are. The answer is that there are four, whose detailed treatment belongs to the following verses.

## Literal Prose Translation

Rebirth-becoming is defiled without exception. By which affliction? By all afflictions belonging to its own level. Whatever the level of a rebirth-becoming, it is linked by all the afflictions of that same level. The Abhidharmikas say that there is no affliction by which rebirth-linking cannot occur. This occurs through the afflictions themselves, however, not through independent paryavasthānas. Although that state is weak, whichever affliction has been frequently practiced and is near at hand is the one that then presents itself, through the impulse of what preceded. The linking into intermediate existence is likewise necessarily to be understood as defiled.

The other three becomings are threefold: wholesome, defiled, or indeterminate. In the formless realm there are three becomings, intermediate existence being excluded, because the formless realm is not spatially delimited as another place for whose attainment an intermediate existence would have to arise. Since no such restriction is stated for the desire and form realms, all four becomings are admitted there.

It has now been explained in detail how sentient beings arise through dependent co-arising. How, then, do sentient beings persist? The world persists through nutriment. The Blessed One directly knew and declared the sūtra statement that all sentient beings persist through nutriment. What, then, are the nutriments? There are four nutriments.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory framework and transitions.
- **Voice: Abhidharmikas** — explicitly marked by `ity ābhidharmikāḥ` in the claim that every same-level kleśa can participate in rebirth-linking.
- No explicit objector occurs in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratisaṃdhi-bandha** — binding or establishment of rebirth-linking
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific clarification in this passage
  - **function:** explains the afflictive determination of `upapattibhava`

- **paryavasthāna** — active afflictive outbreak / enveloping affliction
  - **status:** technical Bhāṣya vocabulary
  - **function:** distinguished here from kleśa itself in the explanation of rebirth-linking

- **mandikā** — weak / slight
  - **status:** local Bhāṣya usage
  - **function:** characterizes the rebirth-linking state

- **abhīkṣṇa-carita** — frequently practiced / repeatedly operative
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific expression here
  - **function:** explains which kleśa becomes manifest at rebirth-linking

- **pūrvāvedha** — prior impulse / momentum from what preceded
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this local argument
  - **function:** explains the appearance of a proximate habitual kleśa

- **sthānāntara-paricchinna** — delimited as another spatial place
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific expression
  - **function:** explains why there is no intermediate existence in ārūpyadhātu

- **āhāra-sthitika** — persisting through nutriment
  - **status:** Kārikā and sūtra expression explained here
  - **function:** introduces the principle governing the persistence of sentient beings

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes three distinctions precise.

First, `upapattibhava` differs from the other three becomings ethically:

```text
upapattibhava
    → necessarily kliṣṭa

other three bhavas
    → kuśala | kliṣṭa | avyākṛta
```

Second, the distribution by realm is:

```text
kāmadhātu / rūpadhātu
    → all four bhavas

ārūpyadhātu
    → three bhavas
    → no antarābhava
```

The reason for excluding intermediate existence in the formless realm is not merely numerical or conventional. It follows from the absence of spatial delimitation between places.

Third, the final pāda initiates a new doctrinal question:

```text
pratītyasamutpāda
    → explains arising of sentient beings

āhāra
    → explains their persistence
```

Thus `āhārasthitikaṃ jagat` is not an incidental remark about food. It begins the analysis of the conditions by which already-arisen sentient existence is maintained.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya resolves the corrupted-looking or compact source form `tridhānye` as:

```iast
tridhā 'nye
```

Therefore the second pāda should be read:

> “The other [three becomings] are threefold.”

The threefold division is explicitly:

```text
kuśala
kliṣṭa
avyākṛta
```

Likewise `traya ārūpye` means that three of the four modes of becoming exist in the formless realm, with `antarābhava` excluded.

Finally, `āhārasthitikaṃ jagat` is best understood in the Bhāṣya's own transition:

> “The world persists through nutriment.”

It answers not how beings originate, but how beings already arisen are maintained.
