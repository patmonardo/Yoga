# VAK_7.45 — Moral Status and the Three Vidyās

## Sanskrit

### IAST

```text
avyākṛte śrotracakṣurabhijñe itarāḥ śubhāḥ /
tisro vidyāḥ avidyāyāḥ pūrvāntādau nivarttanāt // VAK_7.45 //
```

## Padaccheda

```text
avyākṛte
śrotra-cakṣur-abhijñe
itarāḥ
śubhāḥ

tisraḥ
vidyāḥ
avidyāyāḥ
pūrva-anta-ādau
nivartanāt
```

| Term | Working sense |
|---|---|
| avyākṛte | morally indeterminate or ethically neutral |
| śrotra-cakṣur-abhijñe | the higher knowledges of hearing and sight |
| itarāḥ | the others |
| śubhāḥ | wholesome |
| tisraḥ vidyāḥ | three sciences or illuminating knowledges |
| avidyāyāḥ nivartanāt | because they turn back ignorance |
| pūrvāntādau | concerning the past limit and the other relevant temporal or liberative limits |

## Literal Translation

The direct knowledges of hearing and sight are morally indeterminate; the others are wholesome. Three are sciences because they turn back ignorance concerning the past limit and the other limits.

## Philosophical Translation

Purified hearing and purified sight are themselves ethically neutral capacities: their expanded range does not yet determine the practical quality of their employment. The remaining direct knowledges are wholesome because their exercise is internally joined to cultivated, beneficial cognition. Among the six, three are properly called **vidyās**, illuminating sciences, because they directly reverse ignorance concerning the temporal structure of existence and the cessation of bondage.

# 1. Extraordinary Cognition Is Not Automatically Good

The verse begins with a severe practical distinction:

```text
śrotra-abhijñā
cakṣur-abhijñā
    =
avyākṛta
```

Purified hearing and purified sight can disclose things ordinarily inaccessible:

```text
remote sound
subtle sound
distant forms
hidden events
death and arising
```

But expanded access does not by itself possess moral worth.

```text
more perception
    ≠
better reason
```

The Kośa refuses the primitive equation:

```text
powerful
    =
holy
```

A faculty can be extraordinary while remaining ethically indeterminate.

# 2. Avyākṛta as Practical Indeterminacy

Here **avyākṛta** should not be read simply as unintelligible or unclassified.

It means that the faculty, considered in itself, does not necessarily produce wholesome or unwholesome karmic determination.

```text
capacity
    gives access

intention and Path-orientation
    determine practical worth
```

A refined perceptual instrument may serve:

```text
compassionate guidance
curiosity
display
domination
surveillance
```

The bare faculty does not settle which of these will occur.

This is exactly the field we called **Impure Practical Reason**: a power operating within Appearance whose ethical determination depends upon its conjunction with Principle.

# 3. The Others Are Wholesome

```text
itarāḥ śubhāḥ
    the others are wholesome
```

The other four are:

```text
ṛddhi
knowledge of another mind
recollection of former lives
knowledge of the exhaustion of the influxes
```

The precise Abhidharma rationale may vary by faculty, but the broad structure is clear: these capacities are classified as wholesome because their developed form belongs to cultivated Path-cognition.

Especially:

```text
other-mind knowledge
    supports appropriate instruction

former-life knowledge
    discloses conditioned continuity

exhaustion-knowledge
    directly certifies liberation
```

Even ṛddhi, although dangerous when abstracted from the Path, is here considered in its cultivated abhijñā form rather than as mere technical power.

# 4. A Critical Science of Powers

This classification is strikingly Kantian.

The Kośa does not ask only:

```text
What can this faculty know?
```

It also asks:

```text
What is its practical status?
Is goodness intrinsic to the faculty?
Does its possession imply liberation?
How must it be governed?
```

That is a **Critique of Practical Cognitive Power**.

```text
theoretical reach
    one determination

ethical quality
    another determination

liberative function
    a third determination
```

These cannot be collapsed.

# 5. The Three Vidyās

The verse then distinguishes three of the abhijñās as **vidyās**:

```text
1. recollection of former lives
2. knowledge of death and reappearance
3. knowledge of the exhaustion of the influxes
```

This is the classic threefold science associated with awakening.

They form a complete movement:

```text
past
    how this conditioned continuity arose

circulation
    how beings pass according to conditions

cessation
    how the principle sustaining bondage ends
```

Or more compactly:

```text
origin
process
termination
```

These are not merely three spectacular abilities. Together they make saṃsāra intelligible as a whole.

# 6. Vidyā Is More Than Abhijñā

All three vidyās are abhijñās, but not every abhijñā is vidyā.

```text
abhijñā
    direct superior cognition

vidyā
    direct cognition
    that reverses a fundamental ignorance
```

This is a decisive distinction.

A power becomes **science** not merely because it reveals a hidden object, but because it removes a determinate form of avidyā.

```text
hidden object made visible
    =
expanded cognition

ignorance structurally reversed
    =
vidyā
```

Thus vidyā is defined negatively and positively:

```text
negative
    ignorance is turned back

positive
    the structure of the Path becomes evident
```

# 7. The Three Ignorances

The compressed phrase:

```text
avidyāyāḥ pūrvāntādau nivartanāt
```

indicates ignorance regarding the great limits or dimensions of conditioned existence.

The three vidyās may be understood as reversing ignorance concerning:

```text
pūrvānta
    the prior limit
    former existence

aparānta or conditioned continuation
    death and renewed arising

liberative termination
    the exhaustion of the influxes
```

A cautious conceptual rendering is:

```text
Where did this stream come from?

How does it continue?

How does bondage cease?
```

The three sciences answer those three questions directly.

# 8. A Complete Science of Saṃsāra

The triad gives a remarkably complete architecture:

```text
Former-life knowledge
    genetic comprehension

Death-and-reappearance knowledge
    systemic or causal comprehension

Exhaustion-knowledge
    liberative comprehension
```

The first tells us that the present is not self-grounded.

The second tells us that becoming is lawful rather than accidental.

The third tells us that the law of bondage is not absolutely necessary.

```text
conditioned origin
    ↓
conditioned circulation
    ↓
possible cessation
```

That is why these three are **vidyā**.

They transform existence from an opaque fate into a scientifically intelligible Path.

# 9. Pure Practical Reason and the Three Vidyās

The first two vidyās remain deeply involved in conditioned Appearance:

```text
former lives
death
rebirth
karma
worlds
```

The third gives the pure practical Principle:

```text
bondage can cease
the influxes are exhausted
freedom is actual
```

Their conjunction is crucial:

```text
Impure Practical Reason
    knows the conditioned history
    and circulation of beings

Pure Practical Reason
    knows the Principle of cessation

Three Vidyās
    comprehend both
    as one Path-structure
```

This is not pure reason fleeing from impurity.

It is pure reason becoming effective through complete knowledge of the conditioned field.

# 10. The Ethical Lesson

The opening contrast and the closing triad belong together.

```text
hearing and sight
    may expand access
    without abolishing ignorance

the three vidyās
    abolish determinate ignorance
```

So the real measure of cognition is not:

```text
How far can it see?
How much data can it acquire?
How hidden are its objects?
```

The measure is:

```text
Which ignorance does it remove?
Which freedom does it make possible?
```

This is an exceptionally modern lesson.

An agent may possess immense sensory and informational capacity while remaining fundamentally ignorant.

```text
surveillance
prediction
memory
pattern detection
    ≠
vidyā
```

Only cognition ordered toward the removal of ignorance and bondage becomes science in the strong sense.

# 11. Organon Interpretation

The verse gives three levels for evaluating any rational capacity:

```text
Capability status
    what can it access?

Ethical status
    wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate?

Scientific status
    which ignorance does it remove?
```

This should become part of the Organon grammar.

A power is not fully characterized by its input-output behavior.

It must also be typed by:

```text
moral valence
relation to error
relation to Principle
relation to liberation
```

Thus:

```text
faculty
    gives capacity

judgment
    gives ethical employment

vidyā
    gives rational illumination
```

# 12. Elements of the Kośa

VAK 7.45 adds two essential determinations to the Doctrine of Elements:

```text
ethical classification of powers

and

epistemic classification
according to the ignorance removed
```

This is the Kośa’s critical precision.

It does not allow:

```text
power
knowledge
science
wisdom
liberation
```

to become vague synonyms.

Each has a determinate place.

# Kārikā-Pass Determination

```text
Purified hearing and sight
are ethically indeterminate:
expanded access is not yet wisdom.

The remaining direct knowledges
are wholesome in their cultivated Path-form.

Three become vidyās:

    knowledge of former existence,
    knowledge of death and reappearance,
    knowledge of the exhaustion of the influxes.

They are sciences because they do not merely reveal hidden objects.

They reverse ignorance concerning
the origin,
circulation,
and cessation
of conditioned existence.
```

This verse gives us an exact maxim for the Organon:

> A cognitive power is measured not by how much hidden material it reveals, but by the ignorance it is capable of overcoming.
