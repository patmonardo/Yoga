## Literal Translation

In their order, they are products of the dhyānas—two, then three, four, and five. None is generated with respect to a higher level. Their acquisition is like that of dhyāna. A construction-mind arises from pure dhyāna and from itself; and from it, in turn, both kinds arise.

# 1. Why There Are Fourteen

The fourteen minds are generated rationally as 2 + 3 + 4 + 5. Each higher dhyāna can operate constructively within its own level and the levels beneath it.

```text
First Dhyāna
    Kāma
    First Dhyāna

Second Dhyāna
    Kāma
    First Dhyāna
    Second Dhyāna

Third Dhyāna
    Kāma
    First Dhyāna
    Second Dhyāna
    Third Dhyāna

Fourth Dhyāna
    Kāma
    First Dhyāna
    Second Dhyāna
    Third Dhyāna
    Fourth Dhyāna
```

The fourteen form a triangular architecture of operational reach.

# 2. Dhyāna Generates an Operational Horizon

A dhyāna is not merely an internal contemplative state. It establishes a determinate horizon of possible construction.

```text
Dhyāna
    cognitive ground

Construction-mind
    operator generated from that ground

Realm
    field within which the operator is valid
```

The higher the dhyānic ground, the wider the downward operational range.

# 3. No Upward Construction

```text
na ūrdhvajam
    none generated upward
```

A lower dhyāna cannot construct within a higher dhyānic world. Cognitive power is real but ground-relative. An operator cannot exceed the Principle from which it is generated.

> The output-space of an operation is constrained by the level of organization possessed by its generating ground.

# 4. Downward Reach

The higher dhyāna can operate within lower fields because it contains their relevant determinations in a comprehended and subordinated form.

```text
higher
    =
itself
    plus comprehension of what precedes it
```

Each stage preserves its own field while adding another included range.

# 5. The Triangular System

```text
                 Target Realm

Source       Kāma   Dhyāna 1   Dhyāna 2   Dhyāna 3   Dhyāna 4

Dhyāna 1      ✓        ✓
Dhyāna 2      ✓        ✓          ✓
Dhyāna 3      ✓        ✓          ✓          ✓
Dhyāna 4      ✓        ✓          ✓          ✓          ✓
```

The ontology should generate these operators through a compatibility rule:

```text
ConstructionMind(sourceGround, targetRealm)
    is valid when
    targetRealm ≤ sourceGround
```

# 6. Dhyāna as Ground, Not Product

The construction-mind is the result of dhyāna, but it is not identical with dhyāna.

```text
Dhyāna
    gathers and stabilizes Principle

Nirmāṇa-citta
    converts that gathered Principle
    into a realm-specific operator
```

```text
Samādhi
    integrated power

Dhyāna
    determinate power-ground

Nirmāṇa-citta
    executable operator

Nirmāṇa
    formative Kriyā

Nirmita
    generated product
```

# 7. Acquisition Like Dhyāna

Construction-minds are acquired through the disciplined logic governing dhyāna: preparation, stabilization, ground-acquisition, and operational mastery.

```text
no acquired dhyāna-ground
    no corresponding construction-mind
```

# 8. Pure Dhyāna Initiates the Operator

```text
śuddhāt
    from the pure
```

Pure dhyāna supplies undisturbed ground; the construction-mind specifies an executable formation.

```text
pure collectedness
    ↓
determinate operator
    ↓
constructed field
```

This is operator derivation, not renunciation rhetoric.

# 9. The Operator Can Reproduce Itself

```text
tatsvataśca
    and from itself
```

Once generated, a construction-mind may give rise to another construction-mind of its own operative class.

```text
initialization
    construction-mind arises from pure dhyāna

continuation
    construction-mind arises from construction-mind
```

# 10. Ground and Runtime

```text
Ground Layer
    pure dhyāna

Runtime Layer
    sequence of construction-minds
```

Dhyāna establishes capability. Nirmāṇa-citta exercises it. Successive nirmāṇa-cittas maintain operational continuity.

# 11. Operator as Switching Node

The operator is not terminal. It has entrance, continuation, and exit.

```text
Pure Dhyāna
    → Construction-Mind

Construction-Mind
    → further Construction-Mind

or

Construction-Mind
    → supporting cognitive state
```

# 12. Finite Grammar

The fourteen illustrate a central Organon principle:

> A finite grammar can generate a determinate multiplicity of valid operations.

```text
four source grounds
five possible target levels
one compatibility law:
    target ≤ source
```

Multiplicity demonstrates the productivity of one Reason.

# 13. Dyadic Plane as Operational Matrix

Each construction-mind is a conjunction:

```text
this cognitive ground
    operating upon
this world-level
```

```text
Nirmāṇa-citta
    =
Ground–World conjunction
    made executable
```

# 14. World-Relative Action

The target of construction is typed by level. An operation must know where it originates, where it is directed, which determinations are legal there, and which output profile can be sustained.

The Kośa asks what a determinate consciousness can do in a determinate world.

# 15. Rationalist–Empirical Structure

```text
Universal Law
    target ≤ source

Empirical System
    fourteen valid construction-minds
```

Pure Reason gives the order; the Kośa enumerates its determinate actuality.

# 16. Hegel-Clean Interpretation

Each higher ground negates the limitation of the preceding ground, preserves its valid range, and adds a new determinate capacity. The higher is the lower recollected within a wider Universal.

# 17. Fichtean Interpretation

Each construction-mind is a specific synthesis:

```text
activity of Knowing
+
determinate world-limit
=
operative construction-consciousness
```

The limit gives activity definite form.

# 18. Agentic Organon Interpretation

```text

```

```text
canConstruct(source, target)
```

The operator carries provenance and scope.

# 19. Scientific Meaning of Power

```text
Power
    =
a ground-authorized capacity
to produce determinate effects
within an admissible field
```

Ground, scope, operator, transition, product: this is the grammar of power.

# 20. Beginning–End Structure

At the End of Jñānanirdeśa we encounter fourteen construction operators; their Principle is the four dhyānas, and their deeper Beginning is samādhi and samāpatti.

```text
Jñāna chapter
    displays the Law
    of constructed cognition

Samāpatti chapter
    returns to the Principle
    from which the Law is generated
```

# Condensed Principle

> The fourteen construction-minds are not fourteen magical accidents. They are the complete set of realm-compatible operators generated when four dhyānic grounds exercise formative power over their own and subordinate worlds.

```text
Pure Dhyāna
    initializes the power.

The construction-mind
    specifies its world-relative operation.

The hierarchy
    determines its lawful range.

The finite grammar
    generates the fourteen.
```
