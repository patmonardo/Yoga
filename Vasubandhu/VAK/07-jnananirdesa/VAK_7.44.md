# VAK_7.44 — Range, Acquisition, and Mindfulness-Structure of the Abhijñās

## Sanskrit

```text
svādhobhūviṣayāḥ labhyā ucitās tu virāgataḥ /
tṛtīyā trīṇy upasthānāni ādyaṃ śrotrarddhir cakṣuṣi // VAK_7.44 //
```

## Padaccheda

```text
sva-adho-bhūmi-viṣayāḥ
labhyāḥ
ucitāḥ
tu
virāgataḥ

tṛtīyā
trīṇi
upasthānāni

ādyam
śrotra
ṛddhiḥ
cakṣuṣi
```

## Literal Translation

Their objects belong to their own ground or to a lower ground. When previously cultivated, they are obtained through detachment. The third involves three applications of mindfulness. The first, hearing, and extraordinary power belong to the application concerning embodied or visible appearance.

## Philosophical Translation

The direct knowledges do not operate without order: each reaches objects on its own contemplative level or below it. When their capacities have already been cultivated, they become available through detachment from the lower ground. Knowledge of another mind functions through three modes of mindful determination, while extraordinary power, purified hearing, and purified sight are grounded in cognition of embodied and sensible appearance.

# 1. Own Ground and Lower Ground

```text
sva-adho-bhūmi-viṣayāḥ
    having as objects
    their own ground
    or a lower ground
```

A cognition generated from a higher contemplative ground may determine its own level and levels beneath it. The reverse does not follow. A more integrated consciousness can reconstruct less integrated states because it contains their determinations within a broader form.

```text
higher cognition
    preserves the lower
    as a comprehended moment
```

# 2. Cognitive Power Has a Determinate Horizon

The abhijñās may look unlimited from ordinary cognition, but the Kośa assigns each a ground, range, object-domain, and lawful direction of operation.

```text
extraordinary
    does not mean
indeterminate
```

Power is intelligible only through its conditions.

# 3. Acquisition Through Detachment

```text
labhyā ucitās tu virāgataḥ
```

When previously cultivated, the powers become available through virāga—detachment from the lower ground.

```text
cultivation
    develops the capacity

detachment
    releases it
```

The power is produced through positive formation and the negation of its limitation.

# 4. Virāga as Productive Release

Virāga is not mere withdrawal. It frees cognition from exclusive confinement to one field.

```text
rāga
    power bound to one field

virāga
    power released from fixation

abhijñā
    released power functioning
    across its lawful range
```

# 5. The Third Abhijñā and Three Applications of Mindfulness

The third abhijñā is knowledge of another mind. Its three applications of mindfulness concern feeling, mind, and dharmas.

```text
How does this mind feel?
What state is the mind in?
Which dharmic determinations constitute that state?
```

This is not crude mind-reading but a threefold practical diagnosis supporting appropriate teaching.

# 6. The First, Hearing, and Sight

Ṛddhi, purified hearing, and purified sight remain related to embodied and sensible appearance: body, movement, sound, and visible form. Their power is extraordinary, but their object-field remains rūpic.

# 7. Pure and Impure Practical Reason

The pure principle supplies the end—liberation. The conditioned field supplies this body, this feeling, this mind, and these dharmas. Mindfulness mediates the two.

```text
same rational activity
    different object-support
    different mode of attention
    different practical function
```

# 8. Elements of the Kośa

The verse determines four elemental relations:

```text
Ground
    own or lower contemplative level

Acquisition
    cultivation plus detachment

Object-form
    body, feeling, mind, dharmas

Practical function
    direct cognition adapted to the object
```

This is Kantian Science in the strong sense: a critical determination of the lawful employment of cognitive powers.

# 9. Organon Interpretation

A rational power must be determined through source-ground, reachable domain, acquisition-history, attention-form, object-type, and ethical purpose.

```text
power
    +
self-knowledge of ground and limit
    =
free rational capacity
```

The Organon Dataset therefore records not only what a capacity does, but from where it arises, what it may reach, how it is released, and through which form it knows.
