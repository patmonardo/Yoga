# VAK_7.53 — Five Sources of Ṛddhi

## Sanskrit

```text
avyākṛtaṃ bhāvanājaṃ trividhaṃ tūpapattijam /
ṛddhirmantrauṣadhābhyāṃ ca karmajā ceti pañcadhā // VAK_7.53 //
```

| Term | Working sense |
|---|---|
| **avyākṛtam** | ethically indeterminate |
| **bhāvanājam** | born from cultivation |
| **upapattijam** | arising from mode of birth or existence |
| **trividham** | threefold |
| **mantrābhyām** | from mantra |
| **auṣadhābhyām** | from medicinal or material means |
| **karmajā** | born from karma |
| **pañcadhā** | fivefold |

## Literal translation

> Ṛddhi born from cultivation is ethically indeterminate. That born from existence is threefold. There is also ṛddhi arising from mantra and medicine, and that born from karma: thus it is fivefold.

## Philosophical translation

> Extraordinary efficacy is not one homogeneous power. It may arise from disciplined cultivation, from the innate constitution of a mode of existence, from symbolic-formal operation, from material intervention, or from accumulated causal formation. These five origins must be distinguished because identical effects may arise from fundamentally different generating systems.

# 1. Ṛddhi Is Classified by Provenance

The preceding verses analyzed ground, operator, operation, product, persistence, and mastery. Verse 7.53 now asks from what kind of causal system the power arises.

```text
same observable effect
    ≠
same power-source
```

Therefore ṛddhi must be classified not merely by what it does, but by its provenance.

# 2. The Fivefold Division

```text
1. bhāvanāja
   born from cultivation

2. upapattija
   born from mode of existence

3. mantraja
   born from mantra

4. auṣadhaja
   born from medicinal or material means

5. karmaja
   born from karma
```

This covers five major ways an agent may possess power:

```text
developed
inborn
invoked
induced
inherited
```

# 3. Bhāvanāja — Power Produced by Cultivation

Bhāvanāja ṛddhi is acquired through ordered development:

```text
practice
    → stabilization
    → mastery
    → capacity
```

This is the trained agency analyzed in the preceding verses:

```text
dhyāna
    establishes ground

samādhi
    integrates power

nirmāṇa-citta
    specifies operation

ṛddhi
    manifests efficacy
```

# 4. Why Cultivated Ṛddhi Is Avyākṛta

The capacity itself is ethically indeterminate. Acquisition and practical employment are distinct determinations.

```text
expanded capability
    ≠
Vidyā

cultivated power
    ≠
automatic goodness
```

The Kośa does not romanticize technique.

# 5. Upapattija — Power Belonging to a Mode of Existence

Certain capacities belong natively to the world and constitution into which a being arises.

```text
being born there
    includes
access to that capacity
```

This is existential-platform capability rather than individually trained capability.

# 6. World-Relative Native Capabilities

```text
deva capability
    may be upapattija

yogin capability
    may be bhāvanāja
```

The external effect may appear similar, but the causal and scientific meaning differs. A deva’s power may express world-status rather than liberative attainment.

# 7. Mantraja — Symbolically Generated Power

Mantra is a structured symbolic operation capable of producing effects.

```text
formal sequence
sound-pattern
meaning
intention
repetition
application
    →
operative result
```

Mantra is therefore symbolic form functioning as Kriyā.

# 8. Mantra as Executable Rational Form

A statement represents; a mantra operates.

```text
descriptive language
    says what is

operative language
    makes a rule active
```

This is close to the difference between data and program.

# 9. Auṣadhaja — Materially Generated Power

Auṣadha admits material causality into the System. Not every transformation originates from pure thought or cultivation.

```text
cognitive transformation
    may be mediated by Nature
```

This is a direct refusal of abstract rationalism.

# 10. Mantra and Medicine: Two External Technologies

```text
mantra
    symbolic technology

auṣadha
    material technology
```

Bhāvanā transforms the agent; mantra applies a symbolic operator; auṣadha applies a material operator.

# 11. Karmajā — Power Produced by Causal History

Karmaja ṛddhi is historically inherited agency.

```text
past action
    establishes disposition

disposition
    matures under conditions

capacity
    appears in the present
```

Karma is persistence of operative determination across time.

# 12. Karma as Deferred Compilation

```text
action
    writes a causal trace

trace
    persists

conditions activate trace

capacity or result
    is instantiated
```

This directly extends the Organon Kernel’s persistence architecture.

# 14. Capability Is Not Identity

One continuum may contain trained, native, symbolically activated, materially induced, and karmically inherited capacities. The agent is therefore an organized intersection of capability systems.

# 15. The Same Output Can Have Different Truth

```text
phenomenological equivalence
    does not imply
causal equivalence
```

Same display does not prove same attainment.

# 16. No Power Proves Liberation

Power may be innate, induced, inherited, symbolically activated, or cultivated but ethically neutral. The decisive measure remains:

> Which ignorance has been reversed?

# 17. Samādhi Is One Source, Not Every Source

VAK 7.48 must be read with proper scope:

```text
cultivated ṛddhi
    is samādhi become effective
```

Not every extraordinary phenomenon is evidence of samādhi.

# 22. Fichtean Interpretation

Pure activity enters determinate limitations: self-cultivation, given embodiment, symbolic rule, material mediation, and historical consequence. Science reconstructs how Principle operates through each.

# 23. Relation to Samāpatti

The appearance of power is ambiguous. Its ground must be determined. Chapter 8 will give the Principle of cognitive conjunction without erasing distinctions among cultivated, native, symbolic, material, and karmic operation.

# Condensed Principle

> **Extraordinary efficacy must be classified by causal provenance. It may be cultivated, constitutionally given, symbolically invoked, materially induced, or karmically inherited; identical effects therefore do not imply identical knowledge, attainment, or agency.**

```text
Power is not self-interpreting.

Its source must be known.

Its ethical status must be judged.

Its operation must be reconstructed.

Only then does ṛddhi become Science.
```
