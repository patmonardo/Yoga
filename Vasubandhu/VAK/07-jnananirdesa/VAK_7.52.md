# VAK_7.52 — Persistence Beyond the Active Operator

## Sanskrit

```text
mṛtasyāpyastyadhiṣṭhānaṃ nāsthirasya apare tu na /
ādāvekamanekena jitāyāṃ tu viparyayāt // VAK_7.52 //
```

## Literal Translation

Sustaining determination can remain even after the constructor’s death, though not in the case of what is unstable. Others deny this. At first, one construction is produced through many construction-minds; once mastery has been attained, the reverse occurs.

## Philosophical Translation

A constructed configuration can acquire sufficient operative stability to outlast the currently active—and even the living—continuum that established it. Such persistence belongs only to a stabilized construction, and the tradition records disagreement over whether it can truly survive the constructor. The developmental law is equally striking: before mastery, many cognitive operations are required to sustain one result; after mastery, one operation can coordinate many results.

## Scientific Analysis

### Adhiṣṭhāna as objective persistence

Adhiṣṭhāna is not merely present attention. It is an established persistence-condition belonging to the constructed configuration. Generation and continued existence need not be the same operation.

```text
generation
    establishes structure

adhiṣṭhāna
    preserves structure

continued existence
    may exceed the active duration
    of the producer
```

### Persistence does not imply jīva

A constructed form may remain, preserve organization, and continue functioning without possessing an independent soul.

```text
persistence ≠ selfhood
duration ≠ independent consciousness
functional continuity ≠ jīva
```

The construction is neither momentary fantasy nor autonomous person, but a conditionally persistent configuration.

### The product can outlast the producer

The verse distinguishes genetic dependence from continued operational dependence. A product may depend on an agent for its arising without depending on that agent’s presently active cognition for every subsequent moment.

### Stability as transferred law

Only the stable construction persists. A weakly integrated construction collapses when active support is removed; a stable one inherits a continuation-rule. Adhiṣṭhāna is therefore stability imparted as law.

### Doctrinal disagreement

`apare tu na` records a real dispute: whether sustaining efficacy is transferred into the object or continuously supplied by the living constructor. This is a systems question about the location of causal persistence.

### Initial operational cost

At first:

```text
many construction-cittas
    → one construction
```

The practitioner must repeatedly construct, reinforce, correct, and stabilize.

### Mastery reverses the ratio

After mastery:

```text
one construction-citta
    → many constructions
```

This is not merely more force but a new operational regime: one mastered universal command coordinates a manifold of singular products.

### Citta as operation-instance

The verse confirms that citta here is not a miniature person. It is a temporally instantiated cognitive operation. Many operation-moments can establish one result; one mastered operation-moment can govern many results.

### Samādhi as compression

Samādhi is cognitive compression without loss of determinate power.

```text
before mastery
    many acts maintain one unity

after mastery
    one collected act contains
    many determinate outputs
```

Ekāgratā is therefore not poverty of content but concentrated command over articulated multiplicity.

A constructed formation can acquire persistence exceeding the active duration of its producer, and mastery reverses the economy of cognition: instead of many operations producing one result, one comprehended operation governs many results.

