# VAK_7.39 — The Knowledge-Composition and Range of the Pratisaṃvids

## Grammatical Structure

The verse continues directly from the fourth discriminating knowledge, **pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid**.

```text
vāk-mārga-ālambanā ca asau
```

- **asau** — that one, namely the fourth pratisaṃvid;
- **vāk-mārga-ālambanā** — having speech and the Path as its object-support.

Then:

```text
nava jñānāni
```

It consists of nine kinds of knowledge.

```text
sarva-bhūḥ
```

It is associated with or produced on the basis of all levels or grounds.

The second half turns to **artha-pratisaṃvid**:

```text
daśa ṣaṭ vā artha-saṃvit
```

The discriminating knowledge of meaning consists of ten knowledges—or six, depending upon how **artha** is understood.

```text
sā sarvatra
```

It too occurs on the basis of all levels.

Finally:

```text
anye tu sāṃvṛtam
```

The other two are conventional knowledge.

## Literal Translation

That fourth knowledge has speech and the Path as its object-support; it consists of nine knowledges and belongs to all grounds. The discriminating knowledge of meaning consists of ten knowledges, or six, and occurs everywhere; the other two are conventional knowledge.

## Philosophical Translation

The mastery of inspired articulation operates upon both speech and the liberating Path. It is therefore not one simple cognition but a coordinated complex of nine forms of knowledge, available across every contemplative level. The mastery of meaning is still more comprehensive: when meaning includes all dharmas it draws upon all ten knowledges, but when meaning is restricted to nirvāṇa it draws upon six. The knowledges of designation and linguistic form, by contrast, remain conventional cognition.

# 1. Pratibhāna Has Two Object-Supports

VAK 7.38 characterized the fourth pratisaṃvid as mastery of coherent and fluent discourse together with mastery of the Path.

VAK 7.39 now makes that structure explicit:

```text
pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid
    has as object-support:

    vāk
        speech

    mārga
        Path
```

Pratibhāna is their unity:

```text
Path comprehended
    ↓
Path articulated
    ↓
Path communicated
```

The object of the fourth pratisaṃvid is simultaneously the form of expression and the liberating content expressed. The Path possesses the power of its own adequate expression.

# 2. Why It Consists of Nine Knowledges

The fourth pratisaṃvid is not a single isolated mental instrument.

```text
pratibhāna
    = organized availability
      of multiple cognitive powers
```

Its unity is functional rather than numerically simple. It gathers the knowledges needed to determine what is being taught, why it is true, how it belongs to the Path, how the audience is situated, and how expression should proceed.

```text
free expression
    is not absence of structure

free expression
    is structure completely mastered
```

The apparent immediacy of pratibhāna is achieved mediation.

# 3. Sarvabhūḥ — Belonging to All Grounds

The verse says that this fourth knowledge is **sarvabhūḥ**.

```text
pratibhāna
    is not imprisoned
    within one contemplative ground
```

Its field is vertically extensive. Teaching requires movement between levels.

```text
a teacher fixed in one standpoint
    can state only that standpoint

a teacher mastering the levels
    can articulate the transition
    from one standpoint to another
```

Pratibhāna is therefore not only verbal flexibility. It is **systematic mobility**.

# 4. Artha-Pratisaṃvid: Ten or Six Knowledges

The alternatives depend upon the scope of **artha**.

## Artha as all dharmas

When **artha** means the complete field of things or dharmas:

```text
artha
    = all knowable determinate content
```

then artha-pratisaṃvid involves all ten kinds of knowledge.

## Artha as nirvāṇa

When **artha** is taken more narrowly as the supreme purpose or final meaning—nirvāṇa—the count is six.

```text
artha as total content
    → ten knowledges

artha as final purpose
    → six knowledges
```

Meaning is both what a determination signifies and what the entire system is for.

# 5. Artha as Meaning and End

The mastery of artha may concern the meaning of every dharma or the ultimate end of the Dharma.

```text
encyclopedic meaning
    all determinate contents

teleological meaning
    their final liberating end
```

The Kośa gathers both the complete inventory and the purpose of that inventory.

# 6. “The Other Two Are Conventional Knowledge”

The remaining two pratisaṃvids are:

```text
dharma-pratisaṃvid
    knowledge of names and designations

nirukti-pratisaṃvid
    knowledge of linguistic expression
```

These are **sāṃvṛta-jñāna**, conventional knowledge. Yet conventionality is not the negation of truth. It is the medium through which truth becomes shareable.

```text
ultimate meaning
    cannot be taught

without
    conventional designation
    and conventional speech
```

# 7. The Four Pratisaṃvids as a System

```text
Dharma-pratisaṃvid
    object: names and designations
    cognitive type: conventional knowledge

Artha-pratisaṃvid
    object: meanings, things, or final purpose
    cognitive type: ten or six knowledges

Nirukti-pratisaṃvid
    object: speech and linguistic form
    cognitive type: conventional knowledge

Pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid
    object: speech and Path
    cognitive type: nine knowledges
    range: all grounds
```

The structure is:

```text
Name
    conventional determination

Meaning
    total or ultimate intelligible content

Speech
    conventional expression

Eloquence
    synthetic mastery of speech and Path
```

# 8. Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes the simple object of a cognition from the knowledge-system required to master that object.

```text
unity of capacity
    ≠
simplicity of internal constitution
```

A mature power may be phenomenologically one while logically containing many moments. The immediacy is achieved mediation.

# 9. Organon Interpretation

The verse supplies an advanced model of philosophical discourse.

```text
Speech
    the medium

Path
    the systematic movement

Meaning
    the complete content or final end

Knowledge-system
    the coordinated powers required
    to unite them
```

Genuine philosophical teaching cannot be reduced to propositions.

```text
proposition
    is linguistic form

philosophical discourse
    is linguistic form
    governed by the Path
    and animated by comprehensive meaning
```

The fourth pratisaṃvid is close to the actuality of the system speaking itself.

```text
the Path is known
the Path determines speech
speech displays the Path
the hearer is led through the Path
```

It is **Reason becoming communicatively active**.

## Kārikā-Pass Determination

```text
Pratibhāna masters both speech and the Path.
Its apparent unity contains nine coordinated knowledges
and operates throughout all contemplative grounds.

Artha-pratisaṃvid masters either
the complete meaning of all dharmas
or their final meaning in nirvāṇa,
and therefore comprises ten or six knowledges.

Name and linguistic expression remain conventional,
but they are the indispensable medium
through which the Path becomes communicable.
```

The sequence VAK 7.37–7.39 is one of the richest presentations in the chapter:

```text
resolve
    determines the object

knowledge
    comprehends it

name
    determines it conceptually

meaning
    supplies its content and end

speech
    gives it expression

pratibhāna
    makes the Path speak freely
```
