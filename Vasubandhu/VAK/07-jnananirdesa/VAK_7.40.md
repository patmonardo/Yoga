# VAK_7.40 — Grounds, Acquisition, and the Six Final-Edge Qualities

## Sanskrit

### IAST

```text
kāmadhyāneṣu dharme vit vāci prathamakāmayoḥ /
vikalābhir na tallābhī ṣaḍ ete prāntakoṭikāḥ // VAK_7.40 //
```

The source text is compressed and the reading `dharme vit` requires caution. The general structure, however, is clear: the verse assigns the discriminating knowledges to specific contemplative grounds, states that they are not acquired in incomplete form, and then gathers six qualities under the designation **prāntakoṭika**.

## Padaccheda


## Preliminary Grammatical Structure

The first half distributes the pratisaṃvids according to their grounds.

A cautious working reconstruction is:

```text
dharma-pratisaṃvid
    desire domain and dhyānas

nirukti / speech-related mastery
    first dhyāna and desire domain
```

The exact allocation of **artha** and **pratibhāna** must be checked carefully against the Bhāṣya, because the kārikā compresses several locative expressions into a very small space.

The second half is more secure:

```text
vikalābhiḥ na tat-lābhī
```

One does not possess them in a deficient or incomplete form.

Then:

```text
ṣaṭ ete prāntakoṭikāḥ
```

These six are “final-edge” or “ultimate-limit” qualities.

The six appear to be:

```text
araṇā
praṇidhi-jñāna
four pratisaṃvids
```

Thus:

```text
1. araṇā
2. praṇidhi-jñāna
3. dharma-pratisaṃvid
4. artha-pratisaṃvid
5. nirukti-pratisaṃvid
6. pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid
```

## Literal Translation

The discriminating knowledge concerning dharma occurs in the desire domain and the dhyānas; that concerning speech occurs in the first dhyāna and the desire domain. One does not possess them in incomplete form. These six are final-edge qualities.

## Philosophical Translation

The discriminating knowledges arise only upon determinate contemplative and linguistic grounds. They are not genuinely possessed as fragmented talents: acquisition means the completed power itself. Together with freedom from conflict and knowledge governed by resolve, the four discriminating knowledges constitute six capacities brought to the furthest limit of cultivation.

# 1. Knowledge Has a Ground

The verse immediately limits any loose interpretation of these capacities.

They do not arise merely because someone is intelligent, learned, or verbally gifted.

```text
knowledge-capacity
    requires
    a determinate ground
```

The relevant grounds include:

```text
desire-domain embodiment
dhyānic cultivation
linguistic availability
Path realization
```

This is important because the preceding verses could sound as though pratibhāna were a free-floating spiritual eloquence.

VAK 7.40 corrects that impression:

```text
free expression
    is conditioned by
    specific contemplative attainments
```

The power is free in operation, but not groundless in genesis.

# 2. Why Speech Is Restricted to Lower Grounds

The speech-related discriminating knowledge is associated with the desire domain and the first dhyāna.

The underlying principle is intelligible:

```text
verbal expression
    requires
    the level at which speech remains operative
```

Higher contemplative levels may provide greater subtlety of cognition, but articulated speech does not function there in the same immediate manner.

Thus the Kośa distinguishes:

```text
the ground from which something is known
```

from:

```text
the ground in which it can be verbally expressed
```

A cognition may have a vertically extensive object-field while its communicative enactment depends upon a lower embodied-linguistic basis.

```text
higher cognition
    ↓
    must descend into
    speech-capable embodiment
    ↓
    to become teaching
```

# 3. Embodiment Is Not an Accident of Teaching

Speech requires:

```text
body
voice
language
audience
temporal sequence
```

These are indispensable to transmission.

```text
Buddha-mind
    does not merely transcend convention

Buddha-mind
    commands convention
```

The highest knowledge must possess the ability to operate through the lower field without being reduced to it.

# 4. “One Does Not Possess Them Incompletely”

The phrase:

```text
vikalābhiḥ na tat-lābhī
```

introduces a strong criterion of possession.

```text
vikala
    deficient
    incomplete
    lacking a component
```

The implication is:

```text
partial resemblance
    ≠
    full acquisition
```

Possession requires completeness according to the nature of the capacity.

# 5. The Difference Between Talent and Siddhi

A natural or cultivated talent may be partial, but a perfected spiritual quality is internally integrated.

```text
talent
    may be fragmentary

guṇa at its final edge
    is systematic
```

The Kośa is describing powers whose internal conditions have been completed.

# 6. The Six Prāntakoṭika Qualities

```text
araṇā
    preventive knowledge of possible conflict

praṇidhi-jñāna
    knowledge governed by resolve

dharma-pratisaṃvid
    mastery of names and doctrinal designations

artha-pratisaṃvid
    mastery of meanings and ends

nirukti-pratisaṃvid
    mastery of linguistic expression

pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid
    mastery of free Path-governed discourse
```

They form a remarkable sequence:

```text
relation to others
    araṇā

determination of an object
    praṇidhi-jñāna

conceptual designation
    dharma-pratisaṃvid

intelligible content
    artha-pratisaṃvid

linguistic embodiment
    nirukti-pratisaṃvid

free communicative actuality
    pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid
```

# 7. Prāntakoṭika — Reaching the Final Edge

```text
prānta
    end
    boundary
    extreme

koṭi
    summit
    limit
    terminal point
```

A **prāntakoṭika** quality is one cultivated to its terminal degree: the capacity has reached the limit intrinsic to its form.

# 8. The Crown Structure

The six qualities can be divided into three dyads.

```text
araṇā ↔ praṇidhi-jñāna
    ethical and intentional cognition

dharma ↔ artha
    designation and meaning

nirukti ↔ pratibhāna
    linguistic form and living teaching
```

The complete structure is:

```text
Conduct
    araṇā

Resolve
    praṇidhi-jñāna

Determination
    dharma

Meaning
    artha

Language
    nirukti

Teaching
    pratibhāna
```

# 9. Path to Buddha-Mind

These qualities are shared with disciples, but they display the architecture required for Buddha-activity:

```text
know beings without provoking affliction
determine any needed object
master the doctrinal vocabulary
comprehend its complete meaning
command linguistic expression
make the Path speak freely
```

Buddha-mind is knowledge capable of becoming universally appropriate activity.

# 10. Organon Interpretation

The six qualities form a completed practical syllogism of knowledge.

```text
Universal
    the Path and its meaning

Particular
    the determinate being,
    situation,
    language,
    and required teaching

Singular
    the actual free expression
    that benefits this being now
```

Araṇā ensures that the singular action does not produce conflict. Praṇidhi establishes the cognitive aim. The four pratisaṃvids carry the content from designation to actual teaching.

```text
knowledge
    determines itself as resolve

resolve
    acquires its content

content
    acquires language

language
    becomes appropriate action
```

# Kārikā-Pass Determination

```text
The six shared excellences are not fragmentary talents.
Each depends upon an appropriate contemplative ground
and is possessed only as a completed power.

Together they reach the final edge of cultivation:

    non-production of conflict,
    determination by resolve,
    mastery of designation,
    mastery of meaning,
    mastery of language,
    and free articulation of the Path.
```
