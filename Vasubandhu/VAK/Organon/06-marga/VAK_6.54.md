# VAK 6.54 — Dharma-Wheel as the Transmission of Path-Meaning

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

ब्राह्मण्यं ब्रह्मचक्रं च तदेव ब्रह्मवर्तनात् ।
धर्मचक्रं तु दृङ्मार्ग आशुगत्वादरादिभिः ॥ ६.५४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

brāhmaṇyaṃ brahmacakraṃ ca tad eva brahmavartanāt |
dharmacakraṃ tu dṛṅmārga āśugatvād arādibhiḥ || 6.54 ||

The Bhāṣya does not reduce **dharmacakra** to one uncontested identification. It records the Path of Seeing, Bhadanta Ghoṣaka's eightfold-Path wheel, the three-turning Dharma exposition, and the whole noble Path entering another continuum. These attributed determinations are kept distinct below.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

brāhmaṇyam | brahma-cakram | ca | tat | eva | brahma-vartanāt |
dharma-cakram | tu | dṛk-mārgaḥ | āśu-gatvāt | ara-ādibhiḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **brāhmaṇyam** | neuter nominative singular abstract noun | brāhmaṇa-efficacy; the uncontaminated Path as remover of affliction |
| **brahma-cakram** | neuter nominative singular compound | Brahma-wheel; the Path as set in motion by the Buddha |
| **tat eva** | neuter pronoun plus emphatic particle | that very same [śrāmaṇya] |
| **brahma-vartanāt** | ablative singular compound | because of being set in motion by Brahmā; Bhāṣya-controlled construe |
| **dharma-cakram** | neuter nominative singular compound | Dharma-wheel |
| **tu** | adversative or specifying particle | but; introduces the more particular wheel determination |
| **dṛk-mārgaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | Path of Seeing, *darśana-mārga* |
| **āśu-gatvāt** | neuter ablative singular abstract compound | because of swift movement |
| **ara-ādibhiḥ** | masculine instrumental plural compound | through resemblance to spokes and the other wheel-parts |

The Bhāṣya derives **brāhmaṇya** from the carrying away of afflictions and calls the Buddha “Brahmā” through his unsurpassed relation to that liberating efficacy. The wheel is his because he sets it in motion.

## 4. Grammar

**tat eva** refers back to the uncontaminated Path defined as **śrāmaṇya** in 6.51. Two further predicates are applied to that same Path: **brāhmaṇyam** and **brahmacakram**. The ablative **brahmavartanāt** gives the reason for the second designation.

In the second half, **dharma-cakram** and **dṛk-mārgaḥ** stand in apposition: “the Dharma-wheel, however, is the Path of Seeing.” The ablative **āśugatvāt** expresses wheel-likeness by reason of rapid motion, while instrumental **arādibhiḥ** indicates comparison through spokes and other constituent parts.

The Bhāṣya subjects this compact identification to analysis. It first unfolds several senses of wheel-like motion, then records alternative explanations, and finally questions whether the Path of Seeing alone can account for the traditional three turnings and twelve aspects.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

That very [śrāmaṇya] is also brāhmaṇya and the Brahma-wheel, because it is set in motion by Brahmā. But the Dharma-wheel is the Path of Seeing, because of its swift movement and through [resemblance to] spokes and the other [parts].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The same uncontaminated Path is called brāhmaṇya insofar as it carries away afflictions, and the Brahma-wheel insofar as the Buddha—called Brahmā through unsurpassed brāhmaṇya—sets it in motion. The Path of Seeing is called the Dharma-wheel because it moves swiftly and effectively like a wheel. Other explanations identify the wheel with the functionally organized noble eightfold Path, with the three-turning and twelve-aspect Dharma exposition, or with the whole noble Path insofar as its meaning enters another practitioner's continuum and produces Seeing there.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The uncontaminated Path is not complete as a merely private possession. It carries affliction away; it is articulated by the awakened teacher; its meaning is made known; and the same noble determination becomes actual within another continuum. This successful passage from realized meaning to another's Seeing is the turning of the Dharma-wheel.

The wheel is therefore neither a static emblem nor a bare repetition of words. It is a differentiated practical whole in motion: truth, task, accomplishment, teaching, reception, and renewed Path-realization belong to one transmissible order.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**brāhmaṇya and brahmacakra**

The Bhāṣya explains the three names functionally:

```text
śrāmaṇya
    uncontaminated Path

brāhmaṇya
    the same Path as carrying away kleśas

brahmacakra
    the same Path as set in motion by the Buddha
```

The Buddha is called **Brahmā** here because of his unsurpassed *brāhmaṇya*, not through identification with a cosmological creator.

**Wheel-like movement**

The Bhāṣya unpacks the Path of Seeing's resemblance to a moving wheel through:

```text
swift motion
leaving one position and proceeding to another
conquering what is unconquered
holding fast what has been conquered
rising and descending
```

The analogy concerns an ordered and effective operation, not visual circularity alone.

**Bhadanta Ghoṣaka's eightfold wheel**

Bhadanta Ghoṣaka identifies the wheel with the noble eightfold Path and assigns its members to wheel-parts:

```text
spokes
    right view
    right intention
    right effort
    right recollection

hub
    right speech
    right action
    right livelihood

rim
    right concentration
```

This is an attributed explanation. Its value lies in functional articulation: the factors operate as one wheel rather than as a heap.

**Three turnings and twelve aspects**

The traditional formula gives each Truth three determinations:

```text
Truth stated
    this is suffering, origin, cessation, Path

task prescribed
    to be fully known, abandoned,
    realized, cultivated

task completed
    fully known, abandoned,
    realized, cultivated
```

Across the four Truths:

```text
four Truths × three determinations
    = twelve aspects
```

The Vaibhāṣikas associate the three turnings with Seeing, cultivation, and beyond-training. Vasubandhu objects that this cannot make the Path of Seeing **alone** three-turning and twelve-aspect. He therefore regards the Dharma exposition itself as the more fitting bearer of that formula.

**Turning as transmission of meaning**

The Bhāṣya's strongest definition is:

```text
arthajñāpana
    making the meaning known
        ↓
gamana in parasaṃtāna
    Dharma goes into another continuum
        ↓
darśana-mārga arises there
        ↓
Dharma-wheel is turned
```

An alternative broad formulation identifies the whole noble Path as the Dharma-wheel insofar as it proceeds through the continuums of those to be trained. Its inaugural turning is marked when the Path of Seeing first arises in another continuum.

## 8. Logical Determination

The passage distinguishes related but non-identical functions:

```text
UncontaminatedPath
    as removes(Affliction)
        → Brāhmaṇya

UncontaminatedPath
    as inauguratedBy(Buddha)
        → BrahmaWheel

NoblePathOrTeaching
    as transmits(Meaning, OtherContinuum)
        → DharmaWheel
```

Successful transmission has a determinate sequence:

```text
RealizedPath(p)
    → articulatedAs(DharmaExposition)
    → meaningMadeKnownTo(OtherContinuum)
    → produces(PathOfSeeing, OtherContinuum)
```

Consequently:

```text
verbal utterance alone
    ≠ complete turning

meaning transmitted
    + noble Seeing actualized in another
    → wheel set in motion
```

The three-turning structure also joins object and practical demand:

```text
Truth
    → determines Task
    → verified in Completion
```

The wheel's unity is therefore dynamic and semantic-practical: differentiated determinations hold together by producing the Path's operation anew.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.54 is a textual anchor for the LogoGenesis of the Path. The Path first exists as realized liberating order, but its “wheel” determination appears when that order is made communicable and becomes effective beyond its initial continuum. Meaning is not a representation inserted between realization and action; it is the mediation through which realization generates Path in another.

For the Organon NLP Pipeline of Practical Meaning, the sequence is exact and unusually powerful:

```text
realized Truth
    → articulated task-structure
    → meaning made known
    → reception in another continuum
    → renewed Seeing and Path-action
```

This is not merely information transfer. The transmitted meaning succeeds when it reorganizes another continuum into the practical form of the Path. Nor should this synthesis erase the Bhāṣya's debate: Path of Seeing, eightfold Path, Dharma exposition, and whole noble Path are distinct proposed determinations of the wheel, not interchangeable labels flattened into one diagram.
