# VAK 6.26 Bhāṣya — Four Moments for Each Truth

## Kārikā Anchor

> kāmaduḥkhe tato 'traiva dharmajñānaṃ tathā punaḥ |
> śeṣe duḥkhe 'nvayakṣāntijñāne satyatraye tathā || 6.26 ||

> Then Dharma-Knowledge arises concerning that very suffering in
> the desire-domain. Acceptance and Knowledge by Anvaya concern
> the remaining suffering. The same pattern applies to the other
> three Truths.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.26.md). The first
Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance was introduced in 6.25; the present
verse unfolds the moments that follow it.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Immediately after Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance concerning suffering
> in the desire-domain, Dharma-Knowledge arises with that same
> suffering as its object. It is called Dharma-Knowledge concerning
> suffering. Uncontaminated status is to be understood throughout
> this account.
>
> Just as the Acceptance and Knowledge of the Dharma type arise
> concerning desire-domain suffering, so concerning the remaining
> suffering there arise Acceptance and Knowledge of the Anvaya
> type. Immediately after Dharma-Knowledge, Anvaya-Knowledge-
> Acceptance takes the suffering of the form and formless domains
> collectively as its object. Then Anvaya-Knowledge arises. The
> first is called Dharma-Knowledge because it first knows the truth
> of the dharma; the higher-domain knowledge is called Anvaya-
> Knowledge because it follows in accordance with that first
> realization.
>
> As these four Acceptances and Knowledges arise concerning the
> Truth of suffering, so they arise concerning the other three
> Truths. Immediately after Anvaya-Knowledge concerning suffering,
> Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance arises concerning origin in the
> desire-domain, followed by Dharma-Knowledge. In immediately
> successive order come the Anvaya pair for remaining origin, the
> Dharma and Anvaya pairs for cessation, and the Dharma and Anvaya
> pairs for the Path. For the desire-domain Path pair, the object
> is specifically the Path counteracting desire-domain suffering.

The long final Sanskrit sentence explicitly supplies the order.
Its conventional translation is condensed here without dropping
any of the four Truths or the domain distinction.

## Movement of the Commentary

The invariant unit is an ordered pair of cognitive functions,
`kṣānti → jñāna`, first with the desire-domain Truth as object and
then with the corresponding form- and formless-domain Truth as a
combined object. For suffering, the Bhāṣya names each of the four
moments. It then extends the same tetrad to origin, cessation, and
Path. The result is four ordered moments for each of four Truths.

`Anvaya` names the following-through from the first realization
to its higher-domain correspondence. The Bhāṣya's explanation is
brief but relational: the Truth is carried through a change of
object-domain. The statement `anāsravādhikāraḥ sarvatra
veditavyaḥ` keeps every moment in the uncontaminated register
opened in 6.25.

The sequence is not a simultaneous total intuition. The Bhāṣya
uses `samanantarotpattikrama`, immediately successive arising, in
its enumeration. The specific path-functions later assigned to
Acceptance and Knowledge are not needed to establish their
distinctness and order here.

## Organon Response — Direct Knowing Has Articulation

The transition to uncontaminated cognition does not erase
determination. It orders each Truth through two domains and two
cognitive functions. The object changes from desire-domain to the
combined higher-domain field; the mode changes from Acceptance to
Knowledge; the sequence retains their relation across all four
Truths.

For our Science of Knowing, this is a demanding model of direct
Knowing: immediacy of Path-entry is compatible with a necessary
articulation of what is known and how it is known. The Bhāṣya
establishes the Abhidharma sequence. Reading it as a route toward
knowledge of the Absolute is the Organon's further thesis.
