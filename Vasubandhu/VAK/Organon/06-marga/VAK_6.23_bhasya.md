# VAK 6.23 Bhāṣya — Irreversible Consequences and Lineage

## Kārikā Anchor

> mūrdhalābhī na mūlacchit kṣāntilābhy anapāyagaḥ |
> śiṣyagotrān nivartya dve buddhaḥ syāt trīṇy apītaraḥ || 6.23 ||

> One who has attained Summit does not sever the wholesome roots;
> one who has attained Acceptance does not go to a painful destiny.
> After the first two roots, one may turn from the disciple lineage
> toward Buddhahood; after the first three, toward the other
> Buddhahood, that of a Pratyekabuddha.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.23.md). The passage develops
6.22's claim that the effects of an attainment can survive its loss.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Even after regressing, one who has attained Summit does not sever
> the wholesome roots. That person may still go to painful destinies
> and commit grave acts. One who has attained Acceptance does not
> again go to a painful destiny, even if Acceptance is later lost,
> because the karma and afflictions connected with those levels have
> been driven far away.
>
> From attaining Acceptance, one gains the non-arising of certain
> possible future states: painful destinies, specified modes of
> birth, births and bodily supports, an eighth or later existence,
> and afflictions abandoned by Seeing. The detailed list is given in
> the Sanskrit above. The distribution is graded: weak Acceptance
> excludes painful destinies; the highest grade excludes the other
> listed possibilities.
>
> The roots conducive to penetration occur in three lineages,
> beginning with the disciple lineage. One may turn from a disciple
> course that has produced Heat and Summit toward Buddhahood. After
> Acceptance has been obtained, this is no longer possible. An
> explanation says this is because painful destinies have been
> excluded, whereas Bodhisattvas may enter them for others' sake.
> The commentary gives the firmer reason that the relevant lineage
> itself has become irreversible.
>
> “The other [Buddha]” is the Pratyekabuddha. Even after three roots
> have arisen in the disciple lineage, redirection toward that course
> remains possible. A Pratyekabuddha lineage, however, cannot itself
> be redirected.

The source's long list of excluded futures is retained in full
above. Its individual technical categories require collation before
a fully secure item-by-item translation can be published.

## Movement of the Commentary

Summit and Acceptance close different possibilities. Summit prevents
complete severance of wholesome roots, even after regression, but
does not yet bar grave acts or painful rebirth. Acceptance prevents
the latter even after the presently held stage has been lost. The
Bhāṣya specifies this through `anutpattidharmatā`, the acquired
non-arising of certain future states. Its scope widens with the
grade of Acceptance.

The second part changes axis from future rebirth to `gotra`, the
course of awakening. The three lineages share the preparatory
architecture without sharing one destination. Redirection from a
disciple course toward full Buddhahood remains possible after Heat
and Summit, but not after Acceptance. The passage reports one
explanation based on the Bodhisattva's possible entry into painful
destinies, then states that the lineage's own irreversibility is
the relevant reason. Redirection toward Pratyekabuddhahood is
allowed after three roots; a Pratyekabuddha lineage itself is not
redirectable.

These claims are about different limits and should not be combined
into one vague notion of irreversible progress. Each attainment
excludes a determinate class of futures while leaving other
possibilities open.

## Organon Response — Path as Determination of Possibility

The passage gives a strong operational meaning to cultivation:
what has been known and practiced changes what can still happen.
Present possession of a root may cease, yet some future courses
remain closed. The Path's actuality is therefore legible in
specified consequences, not merely in the intensity of a present
experience.

For our Science of Knowing, these distinctions discipline the
claim that Knowing transforms the knower. Summit, Acceptance, and
lineage each alter a different horizon of possibility. The Bhāṣya
sets out those specific Abhidharma consequences; relating them to
knowledge of the Absolute is our Organon reconstruction.
