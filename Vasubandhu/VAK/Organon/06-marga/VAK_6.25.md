# VAK 6.25 — From Liberative Orientation to Uncontaminated Acceptance

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

श्रुतचिन्तामयं त्रीणि कर्माण्याक्षिप्यते नृषु ।
लौकिकेभ्योऽग्रधर्मेभ्यो धर्मक्षान्तिरनास्रवा ॥ ६.२५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

śrutacintāmayaṃ trīṇi karmāṇy ākṣipyate nṛṣu |
laukikebhyo ’gradharmebhyo dharmakṣāntir anāsravā || 6.25 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

śruta-cintā-mayam | trīṇi | karmāṇi | ākṣipyate | nṛṣu |
laukikebhyaḥ | agra-dharmebhyaḥ | dharma-kṣāntiḥ | anāsravā

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **śruta-cintā-mayam** | neuter nominative singular compound | formed through hearing and reflection; qualifying the liberation-conducive root inherited from 6.24 |
| **trīṇi karmāṇi** | neuter nominative plural | the three actions: bodily, verbal, and mental |
| **ākṣipyate** | third-person singular present passive from *ā-√kṣip* | is projected, planted, or initiated |
| **nṛṣu** | masculine locative plural | among human beings |
| **laukikebhyaḥ agra-dharmebhyaḥ** | masculine ablative plural | immediately from or after the Highest Mundane Dharmas |
| **dharma-kṣāntiḥ** | feminine nominative singular compound | Dharma-Acceptance; expanded by the Bhāṣya as Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance |
| **anāsravā** | feminine nominative singular | uncontaminated, without outflows; agreeing with *dharmakṣāntiḥ* |

The first half continues the understood **mokṣabhāgīyam** from 6.24. The second half resumes the sequence of direct realization and introduces a new subject, **dharmakṣāntiḥ**.

## 4. Grammar

**śrutacintāmayam** is a predicate of the unexpressed *mokṣabhāgīyam*: “[the liberation-conducive root] is formed through hearing and reflection.” It should not be carried forward as a modifier of the uncontaminated Acceptance in the second line.

**trīṇi karmāṇi** is an elliptical predication: all three kinds of action can constitute or project the liberation-conducive root. The singular passive **ākṣipyate** returns to the singular root as subject: “it is projected among human beings.”

The ablative **laukikebhyo ’gradharmebhyaḥ** establishes immediate succession from the Highest Mundane Dharmas. The Bhāṣya supplies **anantaram**, “immediately after”; no intermediate stage is inserted.

**dharmakṣāntiḥ** is the nominative subject of the implied verb “arises,” and **anāsravā** is its predicate adjective. The syntax marks both succession and a change of status:

```text
from the Highest Mundane Dharmas
    arises
uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

[The liberation-conducive root] is formed through hearing and reflection; it is the three actions and is projected among human beings. From the Highest Mundane Dharmas [arises] uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The wholesome root conducive to liberation is produced through hearing and reflection. It can be projected through bodily, verbal, or mental action—principally through mental action—and first arises among human beings. Immediately after the Highest Mundane Dharmas there arises uncontaminated Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance, the first moment of entry into the determinate Noble order.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Liberative orientation begins within received and reflected Meaning and takes practical form through action governed by aspiration. This orientation is then cultivated through the penetration-conducive stages until mundane cognition reaches its highest possible determination.

Immediately there arises a cognition of another order: uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance. This is not one further degree of the mundane preparation. It is the first actual entry into the Noble Path, a transformation in the mode of knowing that simultaneously transforms the status of the practitioner.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**mokṣabhāgīya recapitulated**

The first half completes the account of the wholesome root conducive to liberation. It is:

- formed through hearing and reflection, not cultivation in the strict technical sense;
- expressible through bodily, verbal, and mental action;
- principally mental action;
- first projected among humans on the three inhabited continents.

The Bhāṣya says bodily and verbal actions become liberation-conducive when embraced by the resolve for liberation. The causal determination lies not in external form alone but in the aspiration governing the act.

**laukikāgradharma**

The Highest Mundane Dharmas are the final mundane preparation. Their causal efficacy draws in the Path, but they remain **laukika**. The ablative construction places Dharma-Acceptance immediately after them and exposes the precise threshold:

```text
laukika → anāsrava
mundane → uncontaminated
preparation → realization
```

**dharmajñānakṣānti**

The kārikā's **dharmakṣānti** is expanded by the Bhāṣya as **dharmajñānakṣānti**, Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance. It is already uncontaminated, but it is not simply identical with the knowledge-moment that follows it.

The Bhāṣya relates kṣānti and jñāna through the analogy of flower and fruit:

```text
kṣānti → jñāna
flower → fruit
```

The exact philological explanation is compressed. The safe determination here is genetic: Acceptance is the causally effective uncontaminated moment related to the succeeding Knowledge, without collapsing the two functions.

**Determinate object-support**

The Bhāṣya immediately asks for the object-support and identifies it, with the opening of 6.26, as suffering belonging to the desire-domain. The fuller designation is **duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti**.

The detailed sequence belongs to 6.26. For the present verse, the essential point is that the first supramundane moment is not indeterminate: it has a specific Truth, domain, mode, and purity-status.

**anāsrava**

**Anāsrava** means uncontaminated or without outflows. This predicate distinguishes the first Dharma-Acceptance categorically from the Highest Mundane Dharmas. The transition is a change of order, not merely increased intensity within the same order.

**niyāmāvakrānti**

The Bhāṣya gives the first Dharma-Acceptance another designation: **niyāmāvakrānti**, entry into determinate certainty or the fixed Noble order. It analyzes the expression through:

```text
samyaktva  — rightness, identified here with Nirvāṇa
niyāma     — settled or one-sided definitiveness
avakramaṇa — entry into that determination
```

This is Path-entry, not merely adoption of a correct opinion.

**āryapudgala and pṛthagjanatva**

With the arising of this Acceptance, the practitioner is called an **āryapudgala**, a Noble Person. Before it, even the possessor of the Highest Mundane Dharmas remains a *pṛthagjana*, an ordinary person.

The Bhāṣya records three analyses of what precisely displaces ordinary-person status:

1. the first uncontaminated Acceptance;
2. the Highest Mundane Dharmas, despite being mundane, because they oppose that status;
3. both, by analogy with the uninterrupted and liberation paths.

These positions should remain visible as reported alternatives. The passage does not erase their disagreement.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse contains both a recapitulation and a threshold:

```text
LiberationConduciveRoot {
    genesis: hearing + reflection
    expression: bodily + verbal + mental action
    primaryMode: mental action
    firstSupport: human existence
}

HighestMundaneDharmas
    → immediately
DharmaKnowledgeAcceptance {
    status: uncontaminated
    pathFunction: entry into Noble order
}
```

The Bhāṣya coordinates four transformations in one arising:

```text
temporal:     immediately after the final mundane moment
cognitive:    Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance
purity:       uncontaminated
practitioner: ordinary person → Noble Person
```

This is a prepared discontinuity in its complete form. The long causal preparation is necessary, but its result is not homogeneous with its final mundane condition.

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, the **Ideal Copula becomes Path-entry**: the cultivated relation between Truth, object, aspiration, and practice no longer merely prepares Meaning; it becomes an uncontaminated operation whose arising determines both what is seen and who the practitioner now is within the Path.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.24 completed the architecture of preparation. VAK 6.25 crosses its boundary. The movement from the Highest Mundane Dharmas to Dharma-Acceptance is immediate, yet it is not a smooth identity. *Laukika* and *anāsrava* name a qualitative difference in Path-status.

This is the first place where the project's intuition that Meaning itself is Path becomes textually concrete. The decisive event is not the addition of a proposition about suffering. A determinate Truth-object is apprehended through an uncontaminated operation, and the arising of that operation is simultaneously entry into the Noble order.

Still, Acceptance must not be collapsed prematurely into Knowledge. The Bhāṣya's flower–fruit analogy preserves a generated distinction between *kṣānti* and the *jñāna* that follows. VAK 6.26 will begin to articulate that sequence, and the present study stops before completing it.

The kārikā states the constitution and human projection of the liberation-conducive root, immediate succession from the Highest Mundane Dharmas, and uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance. Mental primacy, aspiration-governed action, the determinate object, the flower–fruit relation, *niyāmāvakrānti*, Noble status, and the three accounts of displacing ordinary-person status are Bhāṣya determinations. “Ideal Copula becomes Path-entry” is the Organon's reconstruction.
