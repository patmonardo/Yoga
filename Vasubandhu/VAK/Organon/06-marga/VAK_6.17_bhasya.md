# VAK 6.17 Bhāṣya — Heat and the Summits

## Kārikā Anchor

> tata ūṣmagatotpattis tac catuḥsatyagocaram |
> ṣoḍaśākāram ūṣmabhyo mūrdhānas te 'pi tādṛśāḥ || 6.17 ||

> From that arises Heat. It ranges over the Four Truths and has
> sixteen modes. From Heat arise the Summits; they too are of that
> kind.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.17.md). “From that” refers to
the dharma-establishment cultivated with the combined object-field in
6.16.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> From the dharma-establishment of recollection cultivated in this
> way, a wholesome root called Heat gradually arises. It is called
> Heat because it is the preliminary sign of the fire of the Noble
> Path, which burns the fuel of the afflictions. Heat takes the Four
> Truths as its object. It has sixteen modes: suffering is seen as
> impermanent, suffering, empty, and not-self; origin as cause,
> arising, production, and condition; cessation as cessation, peace,
> excellence, and escape; the Path as path, right method, practice,
> and leading out. The particular explanation of these modes will
> come later.
>
> From Heat arise the Summits. They too are like Heat in taking the
> Four Truths as their object and having sixteen modes. They receive
> another name because they are more excellent: they are the summit
> of the unstable wholesome roots. From this point one may either
> fall away or pass beyond.

The Bhāṣya gives the full sixteen-mode list; the kārikā gives only
the number. The translation leaves the precise force of each mode
open for the promised later explanation rather than importing it
prematurely.

## Movement of the Commentary

The genetic source of Heat is explicit: `tasmād
dharmasmṛtyupasthānād evam abhyastāt`, from the dharma-establishment
practiced in this manner. Heat is a `kuśalamūla`, a wholesome root,
and its image is carefully limited. It is the preliminary sign of
the Noble Path's fire, not the fire itself or the completed burning
of affliction.

Its cognitive range is the Four Truths, each seen under four modes.
The previously combined field has become a truth-structured field
with sixteen articulated ways of apprehending it. Vasubandhu says
the particular explanation of the modes will come later; the list
here establishes the architecture without exhausting its analysis.

The Summits arise from Heat. `Tādṛśāḥ` preserves their Four-Truth
object and sixteen-mode structure, while `utkṛṣṭataratvāt` marks
greater development. Their name indicates the summit of unstable
wholesome roots. The closing alternative, falling away or passing
beyond, prevents greater development from being mistaken for
irreversible realization.

## Organon Response — Truth-Structure and Path-Power

The same truth-object and mode-structure can become more effective
without becoming a different doctrine. Heat and Summit thus give us
a precise distinction between knowing *what* the Four Truths are
about and cultivating a cognition capable of advancing through
them. The difference is a developed wholesome root in the
practitioner's continuum.

For our Science of Knowing, this is another constraint on the claim
“I can know the Absolute.” Correctly articulated content alone does
not establish completed Knowing; its power and irreversibility must
also be demonstrated. The source gives the Heat–Summit sequence and
its possible reversal. The Absolute-knowing application remains our
Organon reconstruction.
