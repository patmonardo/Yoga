# VAK 6.40 — Seven Good Courses as Canonical Compression

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

ऊर्ध्वस्रोतुरभेदेन सप्त सद्गतयो मताः ।
सदसद्वृत्त्यवृत्तिभ्यां गताप्रत्यागतेश्च ताः ॥ ६.४० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

ūrdhva-srotur abhedena sapta sad-gatayo matāḥ |
sad-asad-vṛtty-avṛttibhyāṃ gatāpratyāgateś ca tāḥ || 6.40 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

ūrdhva-srotuḥ | abhedena | sapta | sat-gatayaḥ | matāḥ |
sat-asat-vṛtti-avṛttibhyām | gata-apratyāgateḥ | ca | tāḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **ūrdhva-srotuḥ** | masculine genitive singular | of the upward-streamer |
| **abhedena** | masculine instrumental singular | by non-division, through being stated without subdivision |
| **sapta** | numeral | seven |
| **sat-gatayaḥ** | feminine nominative plural compound | good courses; courses of the good person |
| **matāḥ** | feminine nominative plural past participle | are held, accepted, or taught |
| **sat-asat-vṛtti-avṛttibhyām** | instrumental dual compressed compound | because of activity in the wholesome and non-activity in the unwholesome |
| **gata-apratyāgateḥ** | ablatival/genitival compound expression | because, once gone, there is no return |
| **ca** | indeclinable | and |
| **tāḥ** | feminine nominative plural pronoun | those [seven courses] |

The bare compounds in the second line are semantically underdetermined without the Bhāṣya. In particular, **sat/asat** is explained through wholesome and unwholesome activity, not as an abstract opposition of being and non-being.

## 4. Grammar

The genitive **ūrdhvasrotuḥ** depends upon **abhedena**: the upward-streamer is counted without internal division. **sapta sadgatayaḥ** is the subject and **matāḥ** its predicate—seven good-person courses are accepted under that mode of enumeration.

The instrumental dual **vṛtty-avṛttibhyām** gives two grounds: positive activity and non-activity. The preceding **sat-asat** members are construed through the Bhāṣya as wholesome and unwholesome respectively. **gatāpratyāgateḥ ca** adds the third ground: having entered these courses, the practitioner does not return.

Thus the verse contains both an enumeration rule and a criterion of membership.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Because the upward-streamer is not subdivided, seven courses of the good person are accepted. They are such because of activity in the good, non-activity in the non-good, and because, once gone [into them], there is no return.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The sūtra teaches seven strict courses of the good person because it counts the upward-streamer as one type without the finer route-divisions of Abhidharma. These courses are distinguished by wholesome activity, cessation of unwholesome activity, and irreversibility: once entered, they are not traversed backward.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

One Path-field may be expressed at more than one valid resolution. Fine analysis distinguishes the causal routes of the upward stream; canonical teaching gathers them under one functional unity and therefore counts seven courses.

That unity is not merely nominal. A good course is one whose operation has become asymmetrical: wholesome action is active, unwholesome action no longer operates, and achieved direction cannot be reversed. The Path is known through what it does, what it excludes, and where it can no longer return.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**sapta satpuruṣa-gatayaḥ**

The Bhāṣya identifies the **seven good courses** as the sūtra's seven *satpuruṣa-gatis*. The problem is reconciliatory: preceding Abhidharma analysis produced more finely differentiated non-returners, whereas the sūtra names seven.

The difference is classificatory resolution, not contradictory doctrine.

**abheda-nirdeśa**

The sūtra reaches seven because the upward-streamer is stated **without subdivision**:

```text
fine Abhidharma resolution:
    upward-streamer
        → leaper
        → half-leaper
        → complete traverser
        → other destination-differences

sūtra resolution:
    upward-streamer counted as one
        → seven good-person courses
```

Abstraction here preserves the functional unity while suppressing route-specific detail.

**kuśala-vṛtti**

The first strict criterion is activity in the wholesome. The practitioner is defined positively by the operation that now governs the course, not merely by absence of a defect.

**akuśala-avṛtti**

The second criterion is non-activity in the unwholesome. **Avṛtti** is not bare non-being; it is the cessation or exclusion of an operation that could otherwise determine conduct and continuation.

Together:

```text
positive determination:
    wholesome operation occurs

negative determination:
    unwholesome operation does not occur
```

**gata-apratyāgati**

The third criterion is directional irreversibility. Once the practitioner has entered these courses, there is no return. This does not mean immobility; it means that further movement cannot reinstate the lower rule already overcome.

**Strict and qualified satpuruṣa**

The Bhāṣya acknowledges that other trainees may also be called good persons in a qualified or contextual sense because they possess restraint from five forms of wrongdoing and have largely abandoned the unwholesome.

It therefore distinguishes:

```text
paryāyika satpuruṣatva
    qualified or contextual good-personhood

niṣparyāya satpuruṣatva
    strict or unqualified good-personhood
```

The present seven concern the strict sense. This lexical qualification prevents a broader sūtra usage from dissolving the technical boundary of the present classification.

## 8. Logical Determination

The first rule changes classificatory resolution:

```text
FineTaxonomy
    --collapse internal UpwardStreamer subtypes-->
CanonicalSevenfoldTaxonomy
```

This is a controlled many-to-one abstraction:

```text
different routes
    + same decisive Path-function
    → one canonical class
```

The second rule defines strict membership:

```text
StrictGoodPersonCourse =
    WholesomeActivity
    + UnwholesomeNonActivity
    + NoReturnAfterEntry
```

The Path-person is therefore specified by transition constraints:

```text
permitted operation:
    kuśala

excluded operation:
    akuśala

excluded transition:
    return to overcome course
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, the course has become an a priori practical morphology: Indriya permits one order of action, excludes its contrary, and preserves the result as irreversible direction. Its identity lies in this rule-governed operation rather than in a substantial bearer.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.39 generated the fine differences among Path-persons from Karma, Kleśa, and Indriya. VAK 6.40 demonstrates that the same generated field can be validly represented at a coarser level when the suppressed differences do not alter the criterion presently at issue.

This is directly relevant to an NLP Pipeline of Practical Meaning. Meaning-preserving abstraction does not simply discard detail; it identifies which differences remain operative at the selected level. At the sūtra level, the routes of the upward-streamer differ, but their wholesome operation and irreversible direction make them one course-type.

The result also clarifies the “good.” It is not an honorific attached to a person. It is the practical form of a course in which the wholesome can operate, the unwholesome has become inoperative, and regression has ceased to be a possible transition.

The sevenfold count and its compressed criteria belong to the kārikā. The reconciliation with the sūtra, non-division of the upward-streamer, kuśala/akuśala construe, strict/qualified distinction, and restraint explanation belong to the Bhāṣya. The formulations in terms of resolution-preserving abstraction, transition constraints, and practical morphology are Organon synthesis.
