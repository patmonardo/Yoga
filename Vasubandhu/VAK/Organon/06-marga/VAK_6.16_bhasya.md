# VAK 6.16 Bhāṣya — The Combined Field of Seeing

## Kārikā Anchor

> sa dharmasmṛtyupasthāne samastālambane sthitaḥ |
> ānityaduḥkhataḥ śūnyānātmatas tān vipaśyati || 6.16 ||

> Established in the dharma-establishment of recollection with the
> complete field as its object, the practitioner sees those fields as
> impermanent, suffering, empty, and not-self.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.16.md). The fourth
establishment, which 6.15 said can take a mixed object, is here
practiced with all four fields together.

## Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> evaṃ kāyādyālambanāni smṛtyupasthānānyabhyasya
> sa dharmasmṛtyupasthāne samastālambane sthitaḥ /
> ānatyaduḥkhataḥ śūnyānātmatastānvipaśyati // VAkK_6.16 //
> saṃbhinnalambane dharmasmṛtyupasthāne sthitastān kāyādīn sarvānabhisamasya caturbhirākāraiḥ paśyati /
> anityato duḥkhataḥ śūnyato 'nātmataśca /

The unit runs from Pradhan [343|04–05] through [343|09] in the
[running source](../../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt). The next
half-verse, `tat ūṣmagatotpattiḥ`, begins at [343|10] and belongs to
the genesis of Heat in 6.17. Page labels have been removed. The
running witness reads `ānatyaduḥkhataḥ` and `saṃbhinnalambane`;
the official kārikā study prints `ānityaduḥkhataḥ` and the prose
clearly concerns a `saṃbhinna-ālambana`, a combined object-support.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Having thus practiced the establishments of recollection with body
> and the other fields as their objects, the practitioner stands in
> the dharma-establishment whose object-support is the whole and sees
> those fields as impermanent, suffering, empty, and not-self. That
> is, standing in the dharma-establishment with a combined object,
> the practitioner gathers all the fields beginning with body and
> sees them through four modes: as impermanent, as suffering, as
> empty, and as not-self.

The translation of the four modes follows the Bhāṣya's explicit
`anityato duḥkhataḥ śūnyato 'nātmataśca`, which clarifies the
irregular form of the kārikā in this witness.

## Movement of the Commentary

The earlier establishments examine body, feeling, and citta in
distinction, and the fourth can examine dharmas alone or a mixed
field. Here it takes the latter form at its fullest extent. The
pronoun `tān` refers back to the fields beginning with body; their
distinctions remain within the combined object rather than being
cancelled by the word `samasta`.

The Bhāṣya identifies the contemplative act as seeing through four
`ākāras`: impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and not-self. This is
the actual exercise of the twofold examination prescribed in 6.14:
the distinct fields have been identified, and now the practitioner
comprehends them together under common modes. The verse speaks of a
stabilized act of vipaśyanā, not merely the recital of four
predicates.

The next line of the running source gives a further consequence:
Heat arises from this cultivation. Its nature and four-truth object
are developed with 6.17. They are not additional claims within the
6.16 commentary unit.

## Organon Response — Universality Through the Differentiated Whole

This is a strong test for our Science of Knowing. Prajñā does not
leave the four fields behind when it reaches a whole; it sees them
together as an articulated object. The four modes are acts of
discrimination across that object. The universal has force because
the particulars it determines remain present to the seeing.

Our claim to know the Absolute cannot be obtained simply by naming
this total field “Absolute.” The source gives a determinate
contemplative synthesis and four ways of seeing it. How that
culminates in ultimate knowledge remains the Path's further work.
This distinction between the Bhāṣya and the Organon thesis is part
of the study's interpretive control.
