# VAK 6.18 Bhāṣya — Formation, Development, and Acceptance

## Kārikā Anchor

> ubhayākaraṇaṃ dharmeṇānyair api tu vardhanam |
> tebhyaḥ kṣāntir dvidhā tadvat kṣāntyā dharmeṇa vardhanam || 6.18 ||

> Heat and the Summits are initially formed through the
> dharma-establishment; their development can also use the other
> establishments. From them arises Acceptance. Its first two grades
> are formed in the same way; its development is through the
> dharma-establishment.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.18.md). The commentary
distinguishes the first placing of modes on the Truths from later
development of the stages already begun.

The unit runs from Pradhan [344|03] through [344|15] in the

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Both Heat and the Summits are initially formed through the
> dharma-establishment of recollection. What is this initial
> formation? It is the first placing of modes upon the Truths. Their
> subsequent development may also use the other establishments: all
> four can develop them. As the stages develop, previously attained
> grades cease to be present, owing to the lesser regard now given
> them. From these stages, developed successively as weak, medium,
> and strong, Acceptance arises.
>
> It is called Acceptance because the highest grade is accepted
> without decline. Acceptance itself is threefold: weak, medium, and
> strong. Its weak and medium grades are initially formed in the
> same way as the Summits. In development, however, there is this
> difference: every grade of Acceptance is developed through the
> dharma-establishment alone, not through another establishment.

The reading of `pūrvapratilabdhānām asaṃmukhībhāvaḥ` as the
non-presence of previously attained grades follows the negative
prefix and is supported by `avahumānatvāt`, the reduced regard for
those earlier grades. This does not deny that later grades develop;
it distinguishes their emergence from continued manifestation of
the earlier ones.

## Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya defines `ākaraṇa` narrowly: the first placing of the
sixteen modes upon the Four Truths. Both Heat and the Summits are
formed through the dharma-establishment. Once formed, they may be
developed through all four establishments. Initial formation and
subsequent development are therefore different operations with
different ranges.

Development proceeds through weak, medium, and strong grades. The
commentary adds a qualification easy to miss: earlier attained
grades need not remain manifest as a stronger grade is cultivated.
Their diminished standing is given as the reason. Thus the genetic
continuity of the series should not be pictured as simultaneous
presence of every former grade.

Acceptance arises from the strengthened Summits and has its own
three grades. The weak and medium grades are formed in the same
manner as the Summits. Every grade of Acceptance, however, is
developed only through the dharma-establishment. The next verse
specifies the narrowing object of highest Acceptance; this unit
does not yet give that specification.

## Organon Response — Formation Is Not Retention of Every Stage

For our Science of Knowing, the distinction between placing the
modes on the Truths and developing a stage is crucial. A true
structure must first be formed; its cultivation then changes the
practitioner's relation to it. But genetic preservation is not
equivalent to the continued manifest presence of every weaker
attainment. The stronger grade can supersede the earlier mode of
practice while owing its genesis to it.

Acceptance names a new stabilization in this trajectory, with its
development concentrated in the comprehensive dharma-establishment.
The Bhāṣya gives this Path taxonomy. Treating it as a step in our
claim to know the Absolute is Organon reconstruction, whose
adequacy must be tested through the later stages.
