# VAK 6.32 Bhāṣya — Fruit Established, Further Path Undertaken

## Kārikā Anchor

> phale phalaviśiṣṭasya lābho mārgasya nāsty ataḥ |
> nāprayukto viśeṣāya phalasthaḥ pratipannakaḥ || 6.32 ||

> When a fruit is attained, the Path superior to that fruit is
> not thereby attained. Thus one established in a fruit is not
> yet proceeding toward another while not applied to further
> distinction.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.32.md). The question is
why someone who entered Seeing with five grades already abandoned
stands in stream-entry at the sixteenth moment, rather than
immediately being a candidate for once-return.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Why, even if five grades have been abandoned, is one called a
> stream-enterer at the sixteenth cognition rather than one
> proceeding toward the fruit of once-return? Because, when a
> fruit is attained, the Path distinguished beyond that fruit is
> not attained at the same time. Thus a fruit-established person
> who has not applied himself toward further distinction is not
> one proceeding toward another fruit. This is a fixed rule:
> when the fruit is being attained, its superior Path is not
> acquired. Until a person established in a fruit applies himself
> toward further distinction for attainment of another fruit,
> that person is not called a candidate. The same should be
> understood for the other fruits.
>
> But one already detached from the third dhyāna who enters the
> determinate Noble order relying on a lower ground must bring
> the Path superior to that fruit into presence. Otherwise, if
> reborn above that ground, the person would lack possession of
> the pleasure faculty. Thus far is the classification of persons
> entering the Noble order with different degrees of prior
> desire-domain detachment.

The lower-ground case is translated as a local qualification,
without supplying unstated details of its faculty mechanics.
`Niyāmam avakrāmati` is rendered “enters the determinate Noble
order”; its precise scope remains part of the larger Path doctrine.

## Movement of the Commentary

The opening question exposes a possible invalid inference:
five grades were previously abandoned, so perhaps establishment
in stream-entry should immediately make the person a candidate
for once-return. The Bhāṣya rejects this. Attaining a **fruit**
and attaining the **Path superior to that fruit** are distinct
events. It calls this separation a `niyama`, a fixed rule.

Being **phalastha** describes an attained position. Being
**pratipannaka** describes present movement toward a further fruit.
The transition requires `prayoga`, application toward `viśeṣa`,
a further distinction. Previous abandonment and present fruit
make a higher course possible; they do not by themselves make
that course active. The Bhāṣya explicitly extends this rule to
the other fruits.

The third-dhyāna case adds a bounded qualification. A person
already detached from that dhyāna who enters the Noble order
using a lower ground must make the superior Path present,
because otherwise a later birth above that ground would leave
the person without the pleasure faculty. The source gives this
support-and-faculty reason but does not here replace the general
rule with an automatic transition for every fruit-established
person. The unit then closes the classification of those who
entered with prior desire-domain detachment.

## Organon Response — Attainment and New Direction

For our Science of Knowing, the distinction is between a completed
position and an actually undertaken further course. An attained
End can become the basis of a new Beginning, but the new movement
requires a determinate further aim and application. This is our
Organon formulation of the transition; the source states the
fruit/Path rule and its technical qualification.

This precision also limits any easy rhetoric of inevitable
ascent. A person can stand in a real fruit without presently
being a candidate for the next. Knowing what has been attained
and undertaking what remains are different determinations.
