# VAK_6.03

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दुःखास्त्रिदुःखतायोगाद्यथायोगमशेषतः ।
मनापा अमनापाश्च तदन्ये चैव सास्रवाः ॥ ६.३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
duḥkhās tri-duḥkhatā-yogād yathāyogam aśeṣataḥ |
manāpā amanāpāś ca tadanye caiva sāsravāḥ || 6.3 ||
```

VAK 6.03 determines the extension of the first truth named in 6.02. All
contaminated formations—agreeable, disagreeable, and the remainder—belong to
the truth of suffering, without remainder but according to the mode applicable
to each.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
duḥkhāḥ | tri-duḥkhatā-yogāt | yathā-yogam | aśeṣataḥ |
manāpāḥ | amanāpāḥ | ca | tad-anye | ca | eva | sa-āsravāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `duḥkhāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | are suffering; belong to the truth of suffering |
| `tri-duḥkhatā-yogāt` | ablative singular compound | because of connection with the three modes of suffering |
| `yathā-yogam` | adverbial compound | according to applicability in each case |
| `aśeṣataḥ` | indeclinable adverb | without remainder, exhaustively |
| `manāpāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | agreeable or pleasing [formations] |
| `amanāpāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | disagreeable or displeasing [formations] |
| `ca` | connective particle | and |
| `tad-anye` | nominative plural masculine | those other than these |
| `ca eva` | connective plus emphatic particle | and precisely or indeed |
| `sāsravāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | contaminated, accompanied by influx |

The understood substantive is supplied by the Bhāṣya as `saṃskārāḥ`,
formations. `Yathāyogam` distributes the relevant modes without claiming that
every formation bears every special mode in the same respect. `Aśeṣataḥ`
prevents any contaminated formation from falling outside duḥkha-satya.

## 4. Grammar

The principal predication is:

```text
sāsravāḥ [saṃskārāḥ]
    duḥkhāḥ

contaminated formations
    are suffering
```

The ablative gives the ground:

```text
tri-duḥkhatā-yogāt
    because of connection with the three modes of suffering
```

The two adverbs jointly control scope and distribution:

```text
aśeṣataḥ
    all contaminated formations are included

yathāyogam
    each is included according to the mode applicable to it
```

The second half supplies an exhaustive affective division:

```text
manāpāḥ
    agreeable

amanāpāḥ
    disagreeable

tadanye
    those other than these
```

The Bhāṣya initially correlates these with transformation-suffering,
pain-suffering, and formation-suffering. It then adds an essential refinement:
`saṃskāra-duḥkhatā` is the universal depth-condition of contaminated
formations, not only an exclusive property of the neutral remainder.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Agreeable, disagreeable, and other contaminated formations are, without
> remainder and according to applicability, suffering because of their
> connection with the three modes of suffering.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Every contaminated formation belongs to the truth of suffering through its
> connection, as applicable, with the three modes of suffering. What is
> disagreeable bears suffering as pain; what is agreeable bears suffering
> through transformation; and the remaining formations disclose suffering
> through conditioned formation. More deeply, formation-suffering pervades the
> contaminated conditioned field as such. This classification does not deny
> that pleasant feeling is genuinely pleasant by its own characteristic; it
> sees that same pleasure under impermanence, transformation, and its capacity
> to sustain attachment.

The universal reach of formation-suffering and the preservation of pleasure's
own characteristic are explicit determinations of the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The truth of suffering does not falsify the immediate differences among
> pleasure, pain, and neutrality. It comprehends each within the larger
> contaminated relation that determines its practical destiny. Pain is adverse
> in its felt character; pleasure becomes adverse through its instability and
> transformation; even what appears neutral remains structurally dependent.
> Thus reflection preserves the local character of experience while changing
> its systematic determination—and, with that determination, its consequence
> for bondage or release.

The same content can therefore sustain distinct predicates under distinct
relations:

```text
pleasant feeling
    according to its own characteristic
        is pleasant

pleasant feeling
    as impermanent, transformable, contaminated formation
        belongs to duḥkha-satya
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Sāsrava-saṃskāra` — contaminated formation**

The entire field classified as duḥkha in this verse. The qualification
`sāsrava` is indispensable: the Bhāṣya rejects the inference that the Path
must be duḥkha merely because the Path too is conditioned.

**`Tri-duḥkhatā` — the three modes of suffering**

```text
duḥkha-duḥkhatā
    suffering as painful experience

vipariṇāma-duḥkhatā
    suffering through transformation

saṃskāra-duḥkhatā
    suffering through conditioned formation
```

These modes explain how the full contaminated field belongs to duḥkha-satya
without making every experience phenomenally painful.

**`Duḥkha-duḥkhatā` — suffering as suffering**

The intrinsic painfulness of disagreeable feeling and the formations that
produce it. This is the gross mode ordinary cognition most readily detects.

**`Vipariṇāma-duḥkhatā` — suffering through transformation**

The adversity disclosed in agreeable experience because it changes, cannot be
securely maintained, and can become the basis of heedlessness and attachment.
Its application does not require denying the present pleasantness of pleasure.

**`Saṃskāra-duḥkhatā` — suffering through conditioned formation**

The deepest and most comprehensive mode. Although the initial distribution
associates it especially with the neither-agreeable-nor-disagreeable remainder,
the Bhāṣya states that formations generally are duḥkha through this structural
mode. In the present context, its universal target is the contaminated field.

**`Manāpa` and `amanāpa` — agreeable and disagreeable**

These preserve ordinary affective determinations. The verse does not simply
rename the agreeable as disagreeable. It places both within a truth whose range
is governed by contamination and the three duḥkhatās.

**`Yathāyogam` — according to applicability**

The distributive control. It prevents an undifferentiated assertion that every
formation bears all three modes identically.

**`Aśeṣataḥ` — without remainder**

The extensional control. Every contaminated formation is included, even when
its immediate affective character does not announce itself as pain.

**`Pratikūla` — adverse or contrary**

The Bhāṣya's decisive definition: `pratikūlaṃ hi duḥkham`, suffering is what
is adverse. Because the arising of the Path brings about the exhaustion of all
suffering, it is not adverse to noble persons and is not classified under
duḥkha-satya merely because it is conditioned.

**`Svalakṣaṇa` — own characteristic**

The Abhidharmika defense of genuine pleasure rests here. Pleasant feeling is
pleasant by its own characteristic. Noble seeing does not erase this local
determination; it apprehends the same feeling under another relevant aspect.

**`Paryāya` — mode or aspect**

The relation under which the same pleasant feeling is understood as duḥkha:
it is impermanent, difficult to maintain, transformable, and liable to support
attachment.

**`Anitya-ākāra` and `duḥkha-ākāra` — aspects of impermanence and suffering**

The first apprehends arising and passing away. The second apprehends adversity.
Impermanence mediates the disclosure of adversity without converting the
pleasant feeling's own characteristic into intrinsic pain.

**`Vairāgya` — dispassion**

The practical result of seeing contaminated pleasure under duḥkha. This
redetermination is taught because it conduces to liberation rather than
enjoyment and attachment.

## 8. Logical Determination

The exhaustive inclusion rule is:

```text
SasravaFormation(x)
    → ObjectOf(x, DuhkhaSatya)
```

The special distribution is:

```text
Disagreeable(x)
    → Bears(x, DuhkhaDuhkhata)

Agreeable(x)
    → Bears(x, ViparinamaDuhkhata)

NeitherAgreeableNorDisagreeable(x)
    → Bears(x, SamskaraDuhkhata)
```

But the Bhāṣya requires the deeper non-exclusive rule:

```text
SasravaFormation(x)
    → Bears(x, SamskaraDuhkhata)
```

The Path objection blocks an overgeneralization:

```text
Conditioned(x)
    ↛ ObjectOf(x, DuhkhaSatya)

Path(x)
AND BringsAbout(x, ExhaustionOfAllDuhkha)
    → NotAdverseTo(x, Arya)
    → NotObjectOf(x, DuhkhaSatya)
```

The two determinations of pleasure are compatible:

```text
PleasantByOwnCharacteristic(x)
AND Impermanent(x)
AND Transformable(x)
AND SasravaFormation(x)
    → Pleasant(x)
    AND ObjectOf(x, DuhkhaSatya)
```

Their practical consequences differ by apprehended relation:

```text
ApprehendsMerelyAsPleasant(person, x)
AND Relishes(person, x)
    → Supports(x, Attachment)
    → ConducesTo(person, Bondage)

ApprehendsUnderDuhkha(person, x)
    → Produces(person, Dispassion)
    → ConducesTo(person, Liberation)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The threefold map is not an exclusive partition.** Pain and pleasure have
distinctive suffering-modes, while formation-suffering supplies the universal
depth of the contaminated field. The Bhāṣya explicitly prevents us from
restricting `saṃskāra-duḥkhatā` to neutral feeling alone.

**Pleasure is not secretly pain.** Vasubandhu presents and rejects the thesis
that pleasant feeling does not really exist. The Abhidharmika conclusion is
explicit: pleasure exists and is pleasant by `svalakṣaṇa`.

**One content receives two non-contradictory determinations.** Pleasure is
pleasant intrinsically and duḥkha under transformation, impermanence, and
contamination. These predicates answer different relational questions; their
coexistence is not equivocation or contradiction.

**The Path is conditioned but not duḥkha-satya.** Conditionedness alone is not
the sufficient criterion. The Path is directed toward the exhaustion of
suffering and therefore is not adverse to the noble ones. This distinction
keeps the liberating mediation from being swallowed by the field it negates.

**Noble seeing is finer discrimination.** The hair-in-the-eye analogy does not
mean that noble persons merely feel more gross pain. It indicates their exact
apprehension of structural adversity even at the summit of conditioned
existence.

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 6.03 gives a concrete case of the Ideal Copula
as Practical Meaning. This is an Organon reconstruction, not terminology
attributed to Vasubandhu:

```text
immediate determination
    pleasure is genuinely pleasant

reflective relation
    pleasure is impermanent, transformable, and contaminated

systematic determination
    pleasure belongs to duḥkha-satya

practical consequence
    enjoyment can become dispassion
    bondage can give way to liberation
```

Meaning here is not a replacement label inserted between a datum and its
knowledge. It is the determinate relation under which the same content acquires
a different practical consequence. The Path-power opened in 6.01 now becomes
visible in a specific operation: adequate seeing changes what pleasure does
without falsifying what pleasure immediately is.
