# VAK 6.27 Bhāṣya — One Realization, Three Senses

## Kārikā Anchor

> iti ṣoḍaśacitto 'yaṃ satyābhisamayas tridhā |
> darśanālambakāryākhyaḥ so 'gradharmaikabhūmikaḥ || 6.27 ||

> Thus this direct realization of the Truths consists of sixteen
> cognitive moments. It is called threefold—as seeing, as sharing
> an object, and as accomplishing a function—and belongs to the
> same ground as the Highest Mundane Dharma.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.27.md). The sixteen moments
were enumerated in 6.25–6.26. Here the Bhāṣya asks in what sense
their realization is one.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> “Thus this realization of the Truths consists of sixteen cognitive
> moments”: by the sequence just given, realization of the Truths
> has sixteen moments. What, then, of members of other schools who
> describe one realization of the Truths? Their intended meaning
> should be examined. Realization concerning the Truths is spoken
> of without differentiation as threefold: realization as seeing,
> as taking an object, and as accomplishing a function. Realization
> as seeing the Truths is by uncontaminated prajñā. Realization
> through an object belongs also to the factors associated with it,
> such as feeling. Functional realization belongs even to
> dissociated factors, such as discipline and birth or status.
> For, while suffering is being seen, there is threefold realization
> concerning suffering, but functional realization concerning origin
> and the other Truths, through abandoning, directly realizing,
> and cultivating.
>
> If someone were to claim that realization as seeing all the Truths
> is a single realization, that would be untenable because their
> aspects differ. If one instead claimed that all are seen through
> the aspect of not-self, the Truths would no longer be seen
> specifically as suffering and the others; this would conflict
> with scripture. Scripture describes the noble disciple attending
> to suffering as suffering, origin as origin, and so on up to the
> Path as the Path, with discrimination of dharmas associated with
> uncontaminated attention. If one says this belongs only to the
> Path of Cultivation: no, because cultivation proceeds according
> to seeing.
>
> If one means that seeing one Truth yields mastery concerning the
> rest, calling this one realization would not be at fault; whether
> emergence from absorption can intervene would still need to be
> examined. Or if one means that, in fully knowing suffering, one
> abandons origin, directly realizes cessation, and cultivates the
> Path, this too can be called one realization. Seeing one Truth
> can amount to functional realization concerning the others.
> But with respect to realization as seeing, scripture indicates
> realization of the Truths in sequence. Three sūtras with examples
> are adduced. If another sūtra, concerning freedom from doubt
> about suffering and the Buddha, is offered as proof of one
> simultaneous realization, the reply is no: its intention concerns
> the necessary abandonment of doubt's manifestation.
>
> “This sixteen-moment realization belongs to the same ground as
> the Highest Mundane Dharma”: the sixteen cognitions occur on
> whichever ground supports that Highest Mundane Dharma. Earlier
> it was said that these can occur on six grounds.

The translation follows the argumentative sense where the witness
is damaged, especially the phrase rendered “mastery concerning the
rest” and the final sūtra's account of doubt. Those phrases require
collation before a critical translation is claimed. The cited
sūtras are translated as cited and abbreviated here; this study
does not reconstruct their missing full contexts.

## Movement of the Commentary

The opening `anena krameṇa` makes the order from 6.25–6.26
constitutive: one *satyābhisamaya* unfolds in sixteen successive
cognitive moments. The objection from other schools is not simply
dismissed. Vasubandhu first asks what their “one realization” could
mean.

The three senses specify different ranges. **Darśanābhisamaya**
strictly names seeing by uncontaminated prajñā. **Ālambanābhisamaya**
includes associated factors, such as feeling, insofar as they share
the Truth as object. **Kāryābhisamaya** extends to dissociated
factors and to the work that realization effects. When suffering
is seen, the other Truths can already be involved functionally:
origin is abandoned, cessation directly realized, and Path
cultivated. Shared function does not turn those other Truths into
simultaneous objects of strict seeing.

The proposed simultaneous **darśanābhisamaya** fails because the
Truths have different `ākāras`, or cognitive aspects. A single
not-self aspect does not supply the determinate seeing of suffering
as suffering, origin as origin, and so forth. The cited scriptural
formulation preserves those differences. Shifting that formulation
to the Path of Cultivation fails too: `yathādarśanaṃ bhāvanāt`,
cultivation follows the form of seeing.

Two more modest senses of unity remain possible. Seeing one Truth
may confer mastery concerning the others, although the text leaves
open a question about intervening emergence. More securely, the
functions concerning the other Truths can be accomplished while
one is seen. Vasubandhu consequently maintains sequential seeing
alongside integrated efficacy. The final line locates all sixteen
moments on the same meditative ground as the immediately preceding
Highest Mundane Dharma; the change to uncontaminated cognition is
not a required shift of ground.

## Organon Response — Unity Determined Through the Truths

For our Science of Knowing, the contrast between **seeing** and
**accomplished function** is decisive. The unity of the Path is
real without erasing the differences it knows. A truth's own
determination governs how it is seen, while the work of that seeing
has consequences across the whole four-Truth structure. “One” here
is therefore a mediated unity, not an undifferentiated glance.

This is a project reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's assertion that
the Absolute or Ātman has been established. The claim that we can
know the Absolute must earn its form one determinate act of knowing
at a time. In 6.27, the test is whether unity can include an actual
sequence of truth-specific seeing and effective transformation.
