# VAK 6.15 — The Three Senses and Fourfold Order of the Foundations of Mindfulness

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

प्रज्ञा श्रुतादिमयी अन्ये संसर्गालम्बनाः क्रमः ।
यथोत्पत्ति चतुष्कं तु विपर्यासविपक्षतः ॥ ६.१५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

prajñā śrutādimayī anye saṃsargālambanāḥ kramaḥ |
yathotpatti catuṣkaṃ tu viparyāsavipakṣataḥ || 6.15 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

prajñā | śruta-ādi-mayī | anye | saṃsarga-ālambanāḥ | kramaḥ |
yathā-utpatti | catuṣkam | tu | viparyāsa-vipakṣataḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **prajñā** | feminine nominative singular | discriminative understanding; the intrinsic nature of a foundation of mindfulness |
| **śruta-ādi-mayī** | feminine nominative singular compound agreeing with *prajñā* | produced through hearing and the succeeding modes, namely reflection and cultivation |
| **anye** | masculine nominative plural | the other factors, that is, the dharmas associated with Prajñā |
| **saṃsarga-ālambanāḥ** | masculine nominative plural compound | belonging respectively to association and object-support; the other two senses in which a foundation is designated |
| **kramaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | sequence or ordered succession |
| **yathā-utpatti** | indeclinable compound | according to arising; in the order in which the foundations arise |
| **catuṣkam** | neuter nominative singular | a fourfold set: body, feeling, citta, and dharmas |
| **tu** | adversative or contrastive particle | but; introduces the distinct reason for the number four |
| **viparyāsa-vipakṣataḥ** | ablative singular used adverbially | because of being the counterforce to the inversions |

The compact expression **saṃsargālambanāḥ** must be read through the Bhāṣya. It does not identify Prajñā itself as association and object-support. It supplies the remaining two applications of the name *smṛtyupasthāna*: to the factors associated with Prajñā and to the object upon which Prajñā operates.

## 4. Grammar

The opening nominative **prajñā** answers the question concerning the intrinsic nature of the foundations of mindfulness: “[Their intrinsic nature is] Prajñā.” The finite copula is omitted, as is common in kārikā style.

**śrutādimayī** is a feminine nominative singular predicate agreeing with **prajñā**. The element **ādi** expands the compressed expression: Prajñā may be *śrutamayī*, *cintāmayī*, or *bhāvanāmayī*—produced through hearing, reflection, or cultivation.

In **anye saṃsargālambanāḥ**, the understood noun after **anye** is supplied by the Bhāṣya as the other dharmas involved in the practice. The compound **saṃsarga-ālambana** distributes two determinations across two extensions of the designation: the associated factors constitute the foundation by association, and the cognized object constitutes it by object-support.

**kramaḥ yathotpatti** forms an elliptical nominal clause: “the sequence is according to arising.” The verse thus distinguishes the reason for the order of the four foundations from the reason for their number.

**catuṣkaṃ tu viparyāsavipakṣataḥ** is a second nominal clause: “but the fourfold division is because of opposition to the inversions.” The ablative ending in **-taḥ** states the ground of the fourfold determination.

The argument therefore has four grammatical moments:

1. intrinsic nature — **prajñā**;
2. genesis — **śrutādimayī**;
3. extended designation — **saṃsarga** and **ālambana**;
4. systematic articulation — an arising-order and a fourfold counteractive function.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Prajñā, produced through hearing and the succeeding modes, [is the intrinsic foundation of mindfulness]; the others are [foundations] by association and by object-support. Their sequence is according to arising, but the fourfold division is because they oppose the inversions.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The foundation of mindfulness in its intrinsic nature is Prajñā generated through hearing, reflection, or cultivation. The factors conjoined with that Prajñā are called foundations by association, and the object upon which it operates is called a foundation by object-support. Body, feeling, citta, and dharmas are ordered according to the sequence in which their contemplation arises; they are exactly four because each functions as the counterforce to one of the four inversions.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

A foundation of mindfulness is intrinsically the Prajñā that sees its field in accordance with how it is. Yet the practice is not exhausted by naming this cognitive factor in isolation. Its associated dharmas and the object-support upon which it operates also receive the name because they belong to the same operative complex.

The four foundations form a path-order rather than a static classification. Contemplation proceeds through body, feeling, citta, and dharmas according to the order of its arising. Their number is governed by a corrective necessity: body counters the inversion of purity, feeling the inversion of pleasure, citta the inversion of permanence, and dharmas the inversion of self.

Thus mindfulness becomes a foundation through Prajñā's determinate operation upon a field of error. The Path does not merely collect four objects. It organizes seeing so that each entrenched inversion meets its corresponding counterforce.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**prajñā**

Here **Prajñā** is the intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*) of a foundation of mindfulness. The Bhāṣya identifies the contemplative “seeing” (*anupaśyanā*) with Prajñā: it discriminates the object in the mode required by the practice.

This Prajñā is genetically differentiated as:

- **śrutamayī prajñā** — Prajñā produced through hearing or learning;
- **cintāmayī prajñā** — Prajñā produced through reflection;
- **bhāvanāmayī prajñā** — Prajñā produced through cultivation.

The formulation continues the developmental sequence stated in VAK 6.05 rather than introducing three unrelated faculties.

**smṛtyupasthāna**

The Bhāṣya distinguishes three senses of “foundation of mindfulness”:

1. **svabhāva-smṛtyupasthāna** — the foundation in its intrinsic nature: Prajñā itself;
2. **saṃsarga-smṛtyupasthāna** — the foundation by association: the dharmas arising together with Prajñā;
3. **ālambana-smṛtyupasthāna** — the foundation by object-support: that upon which Prajñā operates.

The term therefore names one practice-complex under three relational determinations. It must not be flattened into either a subjective faculty alone or an objective topic alone.

**Why “foundation of mindfulness” if its nature is Prajñā?**

The Vaibhāṣika explanation gives mindfulness a predominant role: Prajñā's operation depends upon the strength of mindfulness, just as split wood is held by a wedge.

Vasubandhu presents another explanation as preferable: mindfulness is established through Prajñā. What has been seen by Prajñā can thereby be retained and articulated. The name marks this establishment of mindfulness through discriminative seeing, not the replacement of Prajñā by a separate substance.

**yathotpatti-krama**

**yathotpatti** means that the four foundations are stated in their order of arising. The Bhāṣya records an explanation by relative coarseness: the coarser field is taken first. It also gives the Vaibhāṣika causal sequence:

- the body is the basis for attachment to sensual pleasure;
- feeling is desired on account of the body;
- citta is undisciplined because of that desire;
- this condition persists because the afflictive dharmas have not been abandoned.

The ordered practice reverses this implicated structure by making each moment an explicit field of contemplation.

**viparyāsa and vipakṣa**

**viparyāsa** is an inversion: a systematic taking of what is otherwise than it is. **vipakṣa** is the opposing or counteractive factor. The fourfold correspondence is:

| Foundation | Inversion opposed |
|---|---|
| contemplation of body | taking the impure to be pure |
| contemplation of feeling | taking suffering to be pleasure |
| contemplation of citta | taking the impermanent to be permanent |
| contemplation of dharmas | taking what is not-self to be self |

The four are therefore functionally determined by the four errors they counter.

**ālambana**

**Ālambana** is the object-support of cognition, not a self-standing “object” detached from the cognitive operation. The first three foundations have their respectively unmixed fields: body, feeling, and citta. The foundation concerned with dharmas may take dharmas alone or a mixed field including body, feeling, citta, and dharmas.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 6.15 determines the foundations of mindfulness through two intersecting structures.

First, one practice is extended through three senses:

```text
Intrinsic nature:  Prajñā
Association:       dharmas conjoined with Prajñā
Object-support:    the field upon which Prajñā operates
```

Second, that practice is differentiated into four counteractive operations:

```text
body     → counters purity-inversion
feeling  → counters pleasure-inversion
citta    → counters permanence-inversion
dharmas  → counters self-inversion
```

The verse thereby prevents two reductions. A foundation is not merely an internal mental event, because it necessarily has associates and an object-support. Nor is it merely an externally given object, because its path-character lies in the Prajñā that transforms the object's mode of apprehension.

The sequence and the number also answer different questions:

```text
Why this order?   According to their arising.
Why exactly four? According to the inversions they oppose.
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis rather than source doctrine, the complete practical determination may be represented as:

```text
genesis      = hearing → reflection → cultivation
operation    = Prajñā
associates   = conjoined dharmas
object-field = ālambana
defect       = viparyāsa
counterforce = the corresponding smṛtyupasthāna
```

Meaning here is systematic because the Path determines a cognition by its genesis, operation, associates, object-field, opposed error, and corrective consequence. None of these moments alone constitutes the whole practice.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.14 established the functional transition from tranquility to the foundations of mindfulness and from those foundations to insight. VAK 6.15 now determines what the foundations are. Their intrinsic nature is not bare recollection but Prajñā; nevertheless, they bear the name of mindfulness because the discriminative operation and the establishment of mindfulness are internally connected.

This is important for the Path as Practical Meaning. The fourfold field is not a catalogue placed beside the practitioner. Body, feeling, citta, and dharmas become path-determinations only within a corrective act of seeing. Conversely, Prajñā is not an indeterminate power that could operate without a field. It is specified by what it takes as support and by the inversion it overcomes.

The source doctrine establishes the three uses of *smṛtyupasthāna*, the arising-order of the four, and their opposition to four inversions. The further formulation—practice as the unity of genesis, cognitive operation, association, object-support, defect, and counterforce—is an Organon reconstruction of that doctrinal structure. It should not be attributed verbatim to Vasubandhu.

The result is a precise sense in which the Path itself bears Meaning: an object becomes practically meaningful through the form of error under which it is grasped and the determinate insight by which that error is undone. Paramārtha is not supplied here as a detached item of Knowledge. It is approached through the ordered transformation of apprehension.
