# VAK 6.50 Bhāṣya — Exhaustion, Non-Arising, and Immovability

## Kārikā Anchor

> yady akopyaḥ kṣayajñānād anutpādamatiḥ / na cet
> kṣayajñānam aśaikṣī vā dṛṣṭiḥ sarvasya sā 'rhataḥ // 6.50 //

> If [the Arhat] is immovable, after knowledge of exhaustion there is
> cognition of non-arising. If not, there is knowledge of exhaustion or
> right view beyond training. That [right view] belongs to every Arhat.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.50.md). After the incidental
comparison of mundane and supramundane paths in 6.48–6.49, Vasubandhu
returns to the sequence opened in 6.45: what cognition arises after the
first knowledge of exhaustion?

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> This, however, must be said: What arises immediately after knowledge
> of exhaustion?
>
> “If [the Arhat] is immovable, after knowledge of exhaustion [there is]
> cognition of non-arising.” If the Arhat is of immovable nature, then
> immediately after knowledge of exhaustion knowledge of non-arising
> arises for that person.
>
> “If not, [there is] knowledge of exhaustion or right view beyond
> training.” If the Arhat is not of immovable nature, then after
> knowledge of exhaustion there arises knowledge of exhaustion itself,
> or right view beyond training. Knowledge of non-arising does not
> arise, because falling away is possible.
>
> Does that [right view] then not arise at all for one of immovable
> nature? “That [right view] belongs to every Arhat.” Even for one of
> immovable nature, after knowledge of non-arising there sometimes
> arises knowledge of non-arising itself, and sometimes right view
> beyond training.

The verse's `anutpādamatiḥ` is explained by the prose as
`anutpādajñāna`, knowledge of non-arising. The restrictive reason,
`parihāṇisaṃbhavāt`, belongs specifically to the non-immovable case:
where falling away remains possible, knowledge of non-arising does not
arise. It does not deny that such a person has become an Arhat.

## Movement of the Commentary

**The resumed question.** The opening `idaṃ tu vaktavyam` marks a return
from the incidental matter closed at 6.49. The question is strictly
successive: after `kṣayajñāna`, knowledge of exhaustion, what arises?

**The immovable case.** For an `akopyadharman` Arhat, one of immovable
nature, knowledge of non-arising arises immediately after knowledge of
exhaustion. The Bhāṣya names both the condition and the exact temporal
relation: `samanantaram`, without an intervening moment.

**The non-immovable case.** If the Arhat is not immovable, another
knowledge of exhaustion or right view beyond training arises instead.
The exclusion of knowledge of non-arising is explicit and tightly
qualified: `parihāṇisaṃbhavāt`, because falling away is possible. The
passage therefore distinguishes completion of training from the special
condition in which falling away is no longer possible.

**The universal correction.** The questioner asks whether right view
beyond training consequently fails to arise for the immovable Arhat.
The verse corrects that inference: it belongs to every Arhat. The
Bhāṣya then specifies that, after knowledge of non-arising, the
immovable Arhat may have another knowledge of non-arising or that same
right view beyond training.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
first knowledge of exhaustion
    |
    +-- Arhat of immovable nature
    |       -> knowledge of non-arising
    |       -> later: knowledge of non-arising
    |                 or right view beyond training
    |
    +-- Arhat not of immovable nature
            -> knowledge of exhaustion
               or right view beyond training
            -> not knowledge of non-arising,
               because falling away is possible
```

The three cognitions are not interchangeable:

```text
kṣayajñāna
    knowledge of exhaustion

anutpādajñāna
    knowledge of non-arising

aśaikṣī samyagdṛṣṭi
    right view beyond training
```

The Bhāṣya's minimal claim is structural. Right view beyond training is
common to every Arhat. Knowledge of non-arising is restricted to the
immovable case, because its contrary condition is the possibility of
falling away.

## Organon Response — Completion and Non-Recurrence

For the Organon, the passage keeps two determinations apart: the
completed exhaustion of an obstruction and its established
non-recurrence. The second is not inferred merely from the first; the
Bhāṣya makes it depend upon the further determination that falling away
is impossible.

```text
exhaustion known
    != non-arising known

immovability
    -> non-arising can be known
```

This is a bounded reconstruction of the distinction made in the
Bhāṣya. It does not treat `akopyadharman` as a psychological feeling of
certainty, or identify the technical possibility of falling away with
an ordinary moral lapse. The conventional sequence remains primary.
