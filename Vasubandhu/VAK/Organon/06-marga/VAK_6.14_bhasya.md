# VAK 6.14 Bhāṣya — From Śamatha to Twofold Examination

## Kārikā Anchor

> niṣpanna-śamathaḥ kuryāt smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanām |
> kāya-vic-citta-dharmāṇāṃ dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇāt || 6.14 ||

> One whose śamatha is accomplished should cultivate the
> establishments of recollection by examining body, feeling, citta,
> and dharmas according to two kinds of characteristic.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.14.md). The two entry-practices
of 6.09–6.13 lead into this new examination.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> The two entrance-gates have been stated. Having gained samādhi
> through them and accomplished śamatha, one should cultivate the
> establishments of recollection in order to bring vipaśyanā to
> completion. How should one do so? By examining body, feeling,
> citta, and dharmas according to two kinds of characteristic.
>
> One examines the body according to its own characteristic and its
> common characteristic; likewise feeling, citta, and dharmas. Their
> own nature is their own characteristic. The common characteristics
> are impermanence of conditioned dharmas, suffering of contaminated
> dharmas, and emptiness and not-self of all dharmas. What is the
> body's own nature? Its constitution by the great elements and
> derivative material form. “Dharmas” here are those other than the
> first three fields. It is said that for a concentrated practitioner
> who sees the body atom by atom and moment by moment, establishment
> of recollection with respect to the body becomes complete.

The translation follows the explicit scopes in the Sanskrit:
`saṃskṛtānām`, `sāsravāṇām`, and `sarvadharmāṇām` do not name one
undifferentiated range. The final `kila` marks the reported completion
criterion.

## Movement of the Commentary

Vasubandhu closes the two entry-practices and places accomplished
śamatha before a new task. Stabilization has made samādhi available;
the prescribed cultivation of the four establishments serves the
completion of vipaśyanā. The optative `kuryāt` gives the practitioner
work to do, so achieved calming is not presented as already completed
insight.

The method is `dvi-lakṣaṇa-parīkṣaṇa`. Each field is examined in its
own nature and through common characteristics. The common terms have
different extensions: impermanence applies to the conditioned,
suffering to the contaminated, and emptiness and not-self to all
dharmas. Body receives a specific own-nature account as great-element
and derivative-material constitution. The fourth field, “dharmas,”
is defined here in relation to the other three fields, not used as an
unrestricted label for everything at once.

The closing report adds a concrete analytical accomplishment for the
body-field: seeing it atomically and moment by moment. It shows how
the twofold examination may be carried out; it does not erase the
other three fields or supply the answer to the next unit's question
about the intrinsic nature of the establishments themselves.

## Organon Response — Stability Becomes Examination

This is a decisive change in the Path's cognitive work. The governed
supports of the earlier practices yield a stable field, and that
field must now be examined. Neither own nature nor common law can be
discarded: the body, feeling, citta, and dharmas require distinct
determination, while their conditioned, contaminated, and universal
aspects have exact ranges.

For our Science of Knowing, 6.14 gives a disciplined form to the
claim that the Absolute can be known: a claim to universality must
answer to the determinate field and to the scope of each predication.
The Bhāṣya itself is specifying cultivation of vipaśyanā; the
Absolute-knowing formulation remains our Organon reconstruction.
