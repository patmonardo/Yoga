# VAK 6.72 — Ground-Conditioned Path Configurations

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

द्वितीयेऽन्यत्र संकल्पाद् द्वयोस्तद्द्वयवर्जिताः ।
ध्यानान्तरे च शीलाङ्गैस्ताभ्यां च त्रिष्वरूपिषु ॥ ६.७२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

dvitīye ’nyatra saṃkalpād dvayos tad-dvaya-varjitāḥ |
dhyānāntare ca śīlāṅgais tābhyāṃ ca triṣv arūpiṣu || 6.72 ||

The distribution begun in 6.71 continues through the higher dhyānas, the intermediate concentration, and the first three formless attainments. The governing question remains: which Bodhi-aligned functions can a given contemplative ground sustain?

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

dvitīye | anyatra | saṃkalpāt | dvayoḥ | tad-dvaya-varjitāḥ |
dhyāna-antare | ca | śīla-aṅgaiḥ | tābhyām | ca | triṣu | arūpiṣu

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **dvitīye** | neuter locative singular | in the second dhyāna |
| **anyatra** | indeclinable | except, apart from |
| **saṃkalpāt** | masculine ablative singular | from right resolve; excluding it |
| **dvayoḥ** | neuter locative dual | in the two: third and fourth dhyānas |
| **tad-dvaya-varjitāḥ** | masculine nominative plural compound | deprived of that pair, prīti and saṃkalpa |
| **dhyāna-antare** | neuter locative singular | in the intermediate concentration |
| **śīla-aṅgaiḥ** | neuter instrumental plural | by the ethical Path-limbs |
| **tābhyām** | instrumental dual | by those two, prīti and saṃkalpa |
| **triṣu arūpiṣu** | neuter locative plural | in the first three formless attainments |

The understood subject throughout is the Bodhi-aligned factors. The following verse treats Bhavāgra separately, so **triṣv arūpiṣu** denotes the three lower formless attainments.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause is elliptical: in the second dhyāna “[all occur] except saṃkalpa.” In **dvayos tad-dvayavarjitāḥ**, the dual locative refers to the third and fourth dhyānas, while “that pair” resolves contextually as joy and resolve.

**Dhyānāntare ca** continues the same predicate: the intermediate concentration is likewise deprived of that pair.

In the final pāda, **varjitāḥ** continues to govern both instrumentals:

```text
śīlāṅgaiḥ
    deprived of the ethical limbs

tābhyām
    and deprived of those two
```

The compact syntax therefore expresses successive subtraction from the full thirty-sevenfold configuration.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

In the second [dhyāna], except for resolve; in the two [higher dhyānas] they are deprived of that pair, and likewise in the intermediate dhyāna. In the three formless [attainments] they are deprived of the ethical limbs and those two.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The second dhyāna contains thirty-six Bodhi-aligned factors, lacking right resolve because vitarka is absent. The third and fourth dhyānas and the intermediate concentration contain thirty-five, lacking both joy and right resolve. The first three formless attainments contain thirty-two, lacking joy and resolve together with right speech, right action, and right livelihood.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The same universal Path does not become actual in an identical configuration within every contemplative field. A ground admits only those functions whose supporting conditions belong to its morphology. As discursive application, joy, and form-mediated ethical operation cease to be available, the Path is correspondingly reconfigured without ceasing to be Mārga.

Higher abstraction is therefore not simple completeness. Each contemplative determination both enables a mode of realization and excludes operations that require another field.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Exact distribution by ground**

| Ground | Count | Factors absent |
|---|---:|---|
| First dhyāna | 37 | none |
| Preliminary concentration, anāgamya | 36 | prīti |
| Second dhyāna | 36 | samyaksaṃkalpa |
| Third dhyāna | 35 | prīti, samyaksaṃkalpa |
| Fourth dhyāna | 35 | prīti, samyaksaṃkalpa |
| Intermediate concentration | 35 | prīti, samyaksaṃkalpa |
| First three formless attainments | 32 | prīti, samyaksaṃkalpa, samyagvāc, samyakkarmānta, samyagājīva |

These counts concern the thirty-seven named functions, not a count of the ten underlying dravyas.

**Second dhyāna: right resolve excluded**

The Bhāṣya states the dependency directly:

```text
absence of vitarka
    → absence of samyaksaṃkalpa
```

Here **saṃkalpa** is treated under its relation to applied thought. When that operation is not supported by the second dhyāna, the corresponding Noble Path-function cannot occur there.

**The pair: prīti and saṃkalpa**

The shorthand **tad-dvaya** gathers:

```text
prīti
    joy or rapture

samyaksaṃkalpa
    right resolve
```

The third and fourth dhyānas lack this pair. The intermediate concentration receives the same count in the immediate classification.

**The three lower formless attainments**

These are:

```text
ākāśānantyāyatana
    sphere of infinite space

vijñānānantyāyatana
    sphere of infinite consciousness

ākiṃcanyāyatana
    sphere of nothingness
```

Bhavāgra, the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception, is reserved for 6.73.

**The ethical limbs**

The three are:

```text
samyagvāc
    right speech

samyakkarmānta
    right action

samyagājīva
    right livelihood
```

The verse directly excludes them from the formless attainments. The standard doctrinal rationale is their dependence upon bodily and verbal form, which the formless contemplative grounds do not support; the immediate Bhāṣya unit gives the distribution without separately arguing that rationale.

**Ground is not merely location**

A **bhūmi** supplies conditions of possible operation:

```text
Ground
    supportsFactor when
    the factor’s required operation is available

Ground
    excludesFactor when
    its supporting operation is absent
```

The factors are not transported unchanged into a neutral container. Their possible actuality is determined by the field.

**Not a simple scale of superiority**

The count declines as the analysis rises through certain contemplative grounds, but this does not establish:

```text
more factors = universally higher attainment
fewer factors = universally deficient practitioner
```

It establishes only that different grounds sustain different Path-configurations. The first dhyāna is maximally inclusive for this thirty-sevenfold system because its morphology supports all the named functions.

## 8. Logical Determination

The general rule is:

```text
FactorRequires(f, condition)
GroundLacks(g, condition)
    → GroundExcludes(g, f)
```

The direct verse and Bhāṣya mappings are:

```text
SecondDhyāna lacks Vitarka
    → excludes RightResolve

ThirdDhyāna, FourthDhyāna, IntermediateDhyāna
    → exclude Joy and RightResolve

LowerFormlessGround
    → excludes Joy, RightResolve,
      RightSpeech, RightAction, RightLivelihood
```

Hence:

```text
UniversalPathSystem
    does not entail
UniformInstantiationAcrossGrounds
```

Rather:

```text
PathConfiguration(g)
    = BodhipakṣyaFunctions compatibleWith g
```

The system remains one through rule-governed variation, not through identical manifestation everywhere.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This is an especially clear confirmation of Dhātu/Bhūmi as the presupposed domain of possible movement. A Path-action is not first complete in abstraction and then placed inside a ground. Its concrete form is generated under the a priori permissions and exclusions of that ground.

```text
ground changes
    → available operations change
    → Path-configuration changes
```

The formless attainment is therefore not merely “higher” than form. Its very abstraction removes the medium in which speech, bodily conduct, and livelihood operate as presently actual Path-limbs. Conversely, the first dhyāna’s completeness is not an abstract supremacy; it is the breadth of operations that its balanced morphology can sustain.

For the Organon reconstruction, each Bhūmi is a domain grammar: it determines which predicates can become practical acts there. The source-level conclusion is more exact and limited—the numerical distribution of Bodhi-aligned functions across contemplative grounds, with the absence of vitarka explicitly explaining the loss of right resolve.
