# VAK 6.31 Bhāṣya — From Fruit-Candidate to Fruit-Established

## Kārikā Anchor

> ṣoḍaśe tu phalasthau tau yatra yaḥ pratipannakaḥ |
> śraddhādhimuktadṛṣṭyāptau mṛdutīkṣṇendriyau tadā || 6.31 ||

> At the sixteenth moment, the two stand in the fruit toward which
> each had been proceeding. Then the gentle- and sharp-faculty
> practitioners are respectively the Faith-Resolved and the one
> Who Has Attained through Seeing.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.31.md). The prior degree
of abandonment established fruit-candidacy in 6.29–6.30; the
sixteenth moment now changes candidacy into attainment.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> “At the sixteenth moment the two stand in the fruit toward which
> each had been proceeding.” When the sixteenth cognition arises,
> they are no longer called Faith-Follower and Dharma-Follower, nor
> are they called persons proceeding toward a fruit. What, then?
> They stand in the fruit. Whichever fruit each had been approaching,
> in that fruit each is then established: stream-entry, once-return,
> or non-return. Arhatship cannot be attained immediately at the
> outset, because the Path of Seeing has not abandoned what is
> removable through cultivation, and because prior detachment from
> the summit of existence is impossible.
>
> “Then the gentle- and sharp-faculty persons are Faith-Resolved
> and one Who Has Attained through Seeing.” At that time, one of
> gentle faculty who formerly was a Faith-Follower is called
> *śraddhādhimukta*; one of sharp faculty who formerly was a
> Dharma-Follower is called *dṛṣṭiprāpta*. This is because faith
> and prajñā respectively predominate, giving prominence to
> resolve and seeing respectively.

The Sanskrit technical names remain alongside the English because
`śraddhādhimukta` can misleadingly suggest complete liberation
when rendered simply “Faith-Liberated.” The witness's malformed
second-half compound is understood with the commentary's explicit
`dṛṣṭiprāpta`, not silently repaired in the Sanskrit quotation.

## Movement of the Commentary

The first fifteen moments are the Path of Seeing, during which the
two are **pratipannaka**, proceeding toward a fruit. At the
sixteenth cognition they become **phalastha**, established in the
fruit already approached. The prior-abandonment condition remains
decisive: the fruit can be stream-entry, once-return, or non-return.
This moment actualizes an already determinate trajectory rather
than assigning a fresh result.

Arhatship is expressly excluded from this initial attainment.
Seeing does not eliminate `bhāvanā-heya` afflictions, and a mundane
path cannot previously have secured detachment from `bhavāgra`,
the summit of existence. The two limits together rule out the
fourth fruit at the first completion of Seeing.

The same practitioner also receives a new name. Gentle faculty
and the earlier Faith-Follower designation yield
**śraddhādhimukta**; sharp faculty and the earlier Dharma-Follower
designation yield **dṛṣṭiprāpta**. The Bhāṣya explains the names
through relative predominance of faith and prajñā, expressed in
resolve and seeing. Faculty persists; the path-position changes.
These are technical classifications of a continuum, not new
substantial persons created at the sixteenth moment.

## Organon Response — The End Becomes a Present Position

For our Science of Knowing, 6.31 places the End of the preceding
course in the present: what was approached during Seeing is now
attained. The fruit's identity still reflects the practitioner's
prior condition. Because further cultivation-removable afflictions
remain, an attained fruit can also become the beginning of another
course.

This Beginning–End formulation is our Organon reconstruction.
Vasubandhu's explicit determination is narrower and exact: at the
sixteenth moment the person stands in the previously approached
fruit and receives a faculty-dependent designation. The passage
does not claim that fruit-establishment is knowledge of the
Absolute.
