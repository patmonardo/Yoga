# VAK 6.42 Bhāṣya — Intermingled Dhyāna and the Scale of Practice

## Kārikā Anchor

> ākīryate caturthaṃ prāk niṣpattiḥ kṣaṇamiśraṇāt |
> upapattivihārārthaṃ kleśabhīrutayāpi ca || 6.42 ||

> The fourth dhyāna is intermingled first; completion comes
> through mixing moments. It is cultivated for rebirth and
> present abiding, and also from fear of affliction.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.42.md). This verse
explains the intermingled dhyāna associated with the
Akaniṣṭha-bound upward-streamer in 6.37–6.38.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Earlier it was said that one with intermingled dhyāna goes
> to Akaniṣṭha. Which dhyāna is intermingled first? The fourth,
> because that concentration is capable of every function and
> is foremost among pleasant courses of practice. An arhat or
> non-returner enters a continuous stream of uncontaminated
> fourth dhyāna, then emerges and enters the same dhyāna in a
> continuous contaminated stream, then the uncontaminated again.
> In the reported preparation, the practitioner shortens this
> sequence to two uncontaminated moments, two contaminated,
> and two uncontaminated.
>
> “Completion through mixing moments”: according to the
> Vaibhāṣikas, completed intermingling occurs when one
> contaminated moment is set between uncontaminated moments.
> They compare moments in that construction with uninterrupted
> and liberation Paths. After the fourth dhyāna has been
> intermingled, the others are intermingled through its power.
> The practice is first done in three continents of the desire-
> domain; later, after falling away, it may be done in the
> form-domain.
>
> But moment-by-moment intermingling is impossible for anyone
> other than a Buddha. Therefore, we understand completion to
> consist in entering the three continuous streams at will.
> Why is dhyāna intermingled? For rebirth, present abiding, and
> fear of affliction. Sharp-faculty non-returners cultivate it
> for rebirth in the Pure Abodes and pleasant abiding in this
> life. Mild-faculty non-returners also cultivate it from
> wariness of affliction, to keep savoring-associated samādhi
> at a distance and prevent falling away. Sharp-faculty arhats
> cultivate it for pleasant abiding; mild-faculty arhats also
> do so from wariness of affliction, to prevent falling away.

The source's phrase about the Path analogy at [362|21–22]
is compressed and textually irregular; the translation does
not assign each of its three moments a more exact function
than the witness securely supports. The distinction between
the Vaibhāṣikas' microscopic completion criterion and
Vasubandhu's stream-level alternative is explicit.

## Movement of the Commentary

**Fourth dhyāna first.** It is presented as maximally serviceable
samādhi. Once intermingled, its power enables the operation in
other dhyānas. The initial sequence alternates continuous
uncontaminated, contaminated, and uncontaminated streams of
the same fourth dhyāna.

**Two accounts of completion.** The reported Vaibhāṣika account
compresses that alternation through two-moment runs to a
single contaminated moment between uncontaminated moments.
`Iti vaibhāṣikāḥ` marks this as their position. Vasubandhu
then says exact moment-level intermingling is possible only
for a Buddha. His preferred account counts the ability to
enter the three streams at will as completion. The difference
concerns what granularity of temporal control the practitioner
can actually exercise; both accounts retain the ordered
alternation of contaminated and uncontaminated dhyāna.

**Purpose varies with person.** The second half lists rebirth,
present pleasant abiding, and wariness of affliction. The
Bhāṣya distributes them across sharp- and mild-faculty
non-returners and arhats. The protective function is local:
it addresses the risk of savoring-associated samādhi and
falling away. The same meditation therefore has different
purposes according to faculty and path-status.

## Organon Response — A Practicable Scale of Knowing

For our Science of Knowing, this passage raises a sharp
methodological question: can the temporal scale at which a
theory describes cognition be mastered in practice? Vasubandhu
accepts the stream-level operation while resisting a claim of
ordinary control over individual moments. That distinction
helps us keep formal analysis answerable to operative capacity.

This is an Organon response, not a claim that Vasubandhu is
describing our nondual Samāpatti. The source concerns a precise
Abhidharma practice, its competing explanatory models, and
its purposes for specific practitioners.
