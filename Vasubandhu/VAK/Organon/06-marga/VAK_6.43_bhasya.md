# VAK 6.43 Bhāṣya — Five Pure Abodes and the Bodily Witness

## Kārikā Anchor

> tatpāñcavidhyāt pañcaiva śuddhāvāsopapattayaḥ |
> nirodhalābhy anāgāmī kāyasākṣī punar mataḥ || 6.43 ||

> Because that cultivation is fivefold, there are exactly five
> Pure-Abode rebirths. A non-returner who has attained cessation
> is further called a bodily witness.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.43.md). The first half
completes 6.42's intermingled-dhyāna account; the second defines
the *kāyasākṣin* and tests what “direct witnessing” can mean
when cognition is absent during cessation.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Why are there exactly five Pure-Abode rebirths? Because the
> previously described intermingled cultivation of fourth dhyāna
> is fivefold: it develops through five degrees from mild to
> stronger. In the first degree, three cognitions are made
> present—uncontaminated, contaminated, uncontaminated. In the
> second there are six; then nine, twelve, and fifteen. The five
> Pure Abodes are their corresponding results. Rebirth there
> occurs through the force of the contaminated component. Others
> instead explain the five by predominance of faculties beginning
> with faith.
>
> “A non-returner who has attained cessation is called a bodily
> witness.” Whoever among non-returners has attained the
> cessation attainment is so named, because a nirvāṇa-like
> dharma is directly realized through the body. How can it be
> bodily realized? An initial answer points to the absence of
> mind and dependence on the bodily support. But it should be
> understood more precisely: after emerging, the practitioner
> acquires a previously unattained bodily tranquility accompanied
> by cognition, and recognizes how peaceful and nirvāṇa-like
> cessation is. In this way its peace is bodily realized. Direct
> realization is making something present through attainment
> and knowledge.
>
> Why is the bodily witness not separately named among the
> eighteen trainees in the sūtra? There is no appropriate
> classificatory cause. Trainees are classified through the
> three uncontaminated trainings and their fruits; the cessation
> attainment is neither specifically a trainee nor a non-trainee
> fruit. Possessing it therefore does not add a basic trainee
> class.
>
> So far the non-returners have been differentiated coarsely.
> On finer division they differ by the thousands: by mild,
> medium, or sharp faculty; by ground; by six lineage types
> beginning with the regression-prone; by sixteen stations; and
> by thirty-six degrees of detachment from a ground. Within a
> station there are six lineages, and within each lineage nine
> persons from fully bound through eight grades detached. This
> gives fifty-four per station, 864 across sixteen stations,
> then 2,592 with three faculty grades. Applying the same count
> across the five broad form-going types gives 12,960
> non-returner variants. A person fully detached from one lower
> dhyāna is counted as fully bound with respect to the next for
> purposes of a uniform reckoning.

The long final calculation is translated by its stated factors
and sum. Its Sanskrit number words and several witness forms
need collation; the study does not treat the arithmetic as a
new basic taxonomy. The five-faculty explanation is explicitly
reported as an `apare` view. The commentary's `evaṃ tu
bhavitavyam` marks its refinement of the first bodily-witness
explanation.

## Movement of the Commentary

**Fivefold cultivation.** The intermingled fourth dhyāna of
6.42 has five developed grades, represented by three, six,
nine, twelve, and fifteen cognitions. These correspond in order
to the five Pure Abodes. The Bhāṣya specifies the **sāsrava**
component as the cause of rebirth, even though the practice also
contains **anāsrava** moments. An alternative faculty-based
explanation is reported, without being merged into this one.

**Bodily witness.** Any non-returner attaining
`nirodhasamāpatti` is called **kāyasākṣin**. Because cognition
is absent in cessation, witnessing cannot be a concealed
perception occurring inside it. The first explanation invokes
bodily support; the subsequent `evaṃ tu bhavitavyam` explains
the designation through the unprecedented bodily peace known
upon emergence. The Bhāṣya's formula brings **prāpti** and
**jñāna** together in direct realization without making them
simultaneous conscious acts during cessation.

**Classificatory scope.** Cessation attainment does not create
a nineteenth basic trainee class. The existing trainee count
rests on the uncontaminated trainings and their fruits. The
bodily witness is a qualification crossing that taxonomy.
Vasubandhu then distinguishes the coarse named types from a
fine combinatorial count. The omitted end of the running unit
explicitly computes 2,592 variants per broad type and 12,960
across five. The named classes are useful resolutions of a much
more differentiated causal field.

## Organon Response — Attainment and Its Knowing

For our Science of Knowing, the bodily witness is a rigorous
case of knowing that is temporally articulated. Cessation is
actually attained without cognition; on emergence, bodily
tranquility is present with cognition, and its peace is
recognized. The knowledge does not manufacture the earlier
attainment. It makes explicit what the continuum has undergone.

That is our philosophical reconstruction of the Bhāṣya's
sequence. The source calls cessation nirvāṇa-like; it does not
identify that attainment with Nirvāṇa, Ātman, or our nondual
Samāpatti. The fivefold rebirth mapping and the fine counts
also remain within its Abhidharma explanatory framework.
