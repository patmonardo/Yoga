# VAK 6.45 Bhāṣya — Exhaustion, Arhatship, and Dispassion

## Kārikā Anchor

> tat-kṣayāptyā kṣaya-jñānam aśaikṣo 'rhann asau tadā |
> lokottareṇa vairāgyaṃ bhavāgrād anyato dvidhā || 6.45 ||

> With attainment of the destruction of that [last grade], knowledge of exhaustion [arises];
> that person is then beyond training, an arhat.
> Dispassion from the summit of existence is gained by the supramundane [Path];
> from elsewhere, in two ways.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.45.md). The first line completes
the transition begun by 6.44's diamond-like uninterrupted Path. The
second specifies which kinds of Path can produce dispassion from each
ground.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Of the ninth grade at the summit of existence, which was said to be
> abandoned by the diamond-like [concentration], the verse says:
> “With attainment of its destruction, knowledge of exhaustion.”
> Knowledge of exhaustion arises together with attainment of the
> destruction of that ninth grade. It is the last liberation Path,
> immediately after diamond-like concentration. It is first called
> knowledge of exhaustion because it arises together with attainment
> of the destruction of all influxes.
>
> “That person is then beyond training, an arhat.” When knowledge of
> exhaustion has arisen, the candidate for arhatship becomes one
> beyond training and an arhat, having attained the fruit of arhatship.
> The person is beyond training because nothing remains to be trained
> for with regard to a further fruit. The person is an arhat because
> the required task has been done and is worthy of veneration even by
> those still attached. Consequently the other seven previously
> mentioned persons are trainees.
>
> Why are they trainees? Because they continually train for the
> destruction of the influxes in the three trainings: higher
> discipline, higher mind, and higher wisdom. These have discipline,
> concentration, and wisdom as their respective natures. Does that
> make an ordinary person a trainee too? No: the ordinary person
> does not know the Truths as they are and can also train contrary to
> them. The repeated wording of the cited sūtra, “trains in the
> training, trains in the training,” marks the person who trains and
> does not train contrary to it. How does a Noble person resting in
> the ordinary state continue to be a trainee? Through intention,
> like a traveler who has stopped, and through continuing possession
> of the three trainings.
>
> Which dharmas are trainee dharmas? The uncontaminated dharmas of
> a trainee. Which are beyond-training dharmas? The uncontaminated
> dharmas of a person beyond training. Why is Nirvāṇa neither?
> It can be connected with both a person beyond training and an
> ordinary person, so it is not a trainee dharma; it can also be
> connected with both a trainee and an ordinary person, so it is
> not a beyond-training dharma.
>
> Thus there are eight Noble persons: four candidates and four
> persons established in fruits. There is the candidate for
> realization of stream-entry and the stream-enterer, and so on
> through the candidate for realization of arhatship and the arhat.
> These are eight by designation, but five as distinct actual
> persons: the first candidate and the four fruit-holders. The
> other three candidates are not distinct from persons established
> in the first three fruits. This is said with reference to
> sequential attainment. Someone already more fully detached from
> sensual desire, however, can be a candidate for once-returner or
> non-returner fruit during the Path of Seeing without previously
> being a stream-enterer or once-returner, respectively.
>
> The Path of Cultivation was said to be twofold, mundane and
> supramundane. By which does this trainee gain dispassion, and from
> what? “Dispassion from the summit of existence by the
> supramundane [Path]”: not by the mundane one. Why? There is no
> higher mundane ground above it; nor can its own ground serve as
> counteragent. Why not? Its affliction lies latent there. An
> object in which an affliction lies latent does not bring about
> abandonment of that affliction; where a counteragent operates,
> that affliction does not lie latent. “From elsewhere, in two
> ways”: from every ground other than the summit of existence,
> dispassion is gained either by a mundane or by a supramundane Path.

The translation follows the argument in the question and answer at
[366|11–14]. The witness reads `svabhūmikasya vā pratipakṣatvāt`
(literally, “or because its own ground is a counteragent”), while
the next question asks why it is **not** a counteragent. This appears
to lack a negation; the continuous Sanskrit retains the reading and
the translation renders the explicit sense of the surrounding
argument. The cited sūtra wording and the “traveler who has stopped”
simile also remain provisional pending source collation.

## Movement of the Commentary

**The final two Path moments.** The diamond-like concentration of
6.44 is the last uninterrupted Path directed against the ninth
`bhavāgra` grade. The ensuing `vimuktimārga` is knowledge of
exhaustion. Its arising is simultaneous with `kṣayaprāpti`, the
attainment of the last grade's destruction and thereby of the
exhaustion of all influxes. This sequence distinguishes the
counteractive work, its attained result, and the cognition arising
with that result.

**Aśaikṣa and the seven trainees.** At that moment the candidate
possesses the arhat fruit and has no further fruit to train toward.
The other seven named Noble persons are trainees in the three higher
trainings. An ordinary practitioner's exercises alone do not confer
the technical status of `śaikṣa`; knowledge of the Truths and a
noncontrary orientation matter. Even outside a manifest Path moment,
the Noble trainee retains that orientation and the attainments of
training. The terms `śaikṣa-dharma` and `aśaikṣa-dharma` classify
their uncontaminated dharmas, while Nirvāṇa is neither kind.

**Eight names, five distinct positions.** Four candidates and four
fruit-holders make eight designations. Under sequential attainment,
the first candidate and four fruit-holders make five distinct
person-positions, because the three later candidates already occupy
earlier fruits. The passage then qualifies this count for persons
who have already weakened or abandoned sensual attachment before
entering the Path of Seeing. The fivefold count is thus tied to the
specified sequential course, not a universal claim that each later
candidacy always presupposes the earlier fruit.

**The upper limit of mundane dispassion.** Ordinary cultivation can
produce dispassion from lower grounds by using a higher mundane
basis. At `bhavāgra` no higher mundane ground exists. Its own ground
cannot function as its final counteragent while its affliction can
remain latent there. The supramundane Path is therefore required
there, whereas dispassion from other grounds can arise by either
kind of Path.

## Organon Response — Attained Destruction and Its Knowledge

For our Science of Knowing, the final result is a determinate
transition: the last obstruction is counteracted, its destruction
is attained, knowledge arises with that attainment, and the
practitioner occupies a new fruit-status. This makes the claim to
*know* the completed result answerable to the Path's articulated
operation, one verse at a time.

This is Organon reconstruction. Vasubandhu's account specifies the
Path sequence, its knowledge and person classifications, and the
limits of mundane counteraction. It does not identify the arhat's
`kṣayajñāna` with our Nondual Absolute Reason or with Ātman. Those
larger determinations require their own argument rather than being
read into this verse.
