# VAK 6.25 Bhāṣya — Entry into the Uncontaminated Path

## Kārikā Anchor

> śrutacintāmayaṃ trīṇi karmāṇy ākṣipyate nṛṣu |
> laukikebhyo 'gradharmebhyo dharmakṣāntir anāsravā || 6.25 ||

> The liberation-conducive root comes from hearing and reflection,
> may involve the three kinds of action, and is projected among
> humans. Immediately after the Highest Mundane Dharmas arises
> uncontaminated Dharma-Acceptance.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.25.md). The first half
finishes the digression of 6.24; the second resumes `abhisamaya`.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> The liberation-conducive root is born of hearing and reflection,
> not cultivation in the strict technical sense. All three kinds
> of action can produce it, though mental action is primary. Bodily
> and verbal action can also become liberation-conducive when taken
> up by the resolve for liberation. A single gift or undertaken
> training rule may project such a root through the force of
> longing for liberation. It is projected among humans on the
> three inhabited continents. The digression on this root now ends;
> the sequence of direct realization, covered so far through the
> Highest Mundane Dharmas, resumes.
>
> Immediately after the Highest Mundane Dharmas, uncontaminated
> Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance arises. What is its object? Suffering
> in the desire domain. Hence it is called Dharma-Knowledge-
> Acceptance with respect to suffering. The explanation of its
> relation to the knowledge that follows is compressed; the
> commentary compares Acceptance and Knowledge to flower and fruit.
>
> This Acceptance is also called entry into the fixed order of
> rightness, `niyāmāvakrānti`. The commentary identifies rightness
> with Nirvāṇa as taught in scripture, explains `niyāma` as
> definitiveness, and `avakramaṇa` as entering it. When this
> Acceptance arises, the practitioner is called a Noble Person.
> On the primary account, future Acceptance displaces ordinary-
> person status, and this is the only function ascribed to it while
> still future. Others assign that displacement to the Highest
> Mundane Dharmas; the commentary answers an objection to that view
> by saying they oppose the ordinary status even while belonging
> to its level, like someone who climbs onto an enemy's shoulders
> to strike. Still others assign the effect to both, by analogy
> with uninterrupted and liberation paths.

The flower–fruit comparison and the future-function argument are
kept at the level established here; the witness does not license
collapsing Acceptance into the succeeding Knowledge moment.

## Movement of the Commentary

The first half completes the earlier inquiry into liberative
orientation. The second half crosses a marked status boundary:
`laukikāgradharma` is the highest mundane preparation, and its
immediate successor is `anāsrava` Acceptance. This moment already
has a determinate object, desire-domain suffering. It is named
`dharmajñānakṣānti` in relation to a knowledge that follows; the
Bhāṣya's image of flower and fruit preserves their sequence.

The passage then identifies this moment as `niyāmāvakrānti` and
the practitioner as an `āryapudgala`. Its discussion of the removal
of `pṛthagjanatva` has distinct voices. The primary account locates
the effect in the first uncontaminated Acceptance. `Apare` marks
two alternatives: the Highest Mundane Dharmas, or both the Highest
Mundane Dharmas and Acceptance. The source offers an answer to an
objection against the first alternative; it does not erase the
reported difference.

## Organon Response — The Threshold Is an Act of Knowing

Here the preparation does not merely become stronger. An
uncontaminated operation arises immediately after the final mundane
moment, and the practitioner's Path-status changes with it. Yet
the operation is highly specific: it concerns suffering in the
desire domain and stands in a generated relation to a following
Knowledge. Its determinate object prevents “breakthrough” from
becoming an empty label.

For our Science of Knowing, this is where the challenge becomes
sharpest so far. We may propose that the Absolute is knowable, but
this text demands an account of the precise transition in knowing,
not only a declaration of its end. The Bhāṣya establishes the
uncontaminated Path-entry and its doctrinal alternatives; the
Absolute-knowing identification remains Organon reconstruction.
