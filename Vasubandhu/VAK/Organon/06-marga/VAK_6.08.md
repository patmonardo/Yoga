# VAK_6.08

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कर्मान्तेन त्रिभिर्वृत्तिस्तृष्णोत्पादविपक्षतः ।
ममाहंकारवस्त्विच्छा तत्कालात्यन्तशान्तये ॥ ६.८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
karmāntena tribhir vṛttis tṛṣṇotpāda-vipakṣataḥ |
mamāhaṃkāra-vastv-icchā tat-kālātyanta-śāntaye || 6.8 ||
```

VAK 6.08 completes the four noble lineages introduced in 6.07. The first three
provide a bounded livelihood; the fourth supplies the work of abandonment and
cultivation. Together they counter four productions of thirst and distinguish
present pacification of desire for objects made “mine” from final pacification
of desire centered on both “mine” and “I.”

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
karmāntena | tribhiḥ | vṛttiḥ | tṛṣṇā-utpāda-vipakṣataḥ |
mama-ahaṃkāra-vastu-icchā | tat-kāla-atyanta-śāntaye
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `karmāntena` | instrumental singular masculine/neuter | by the work; through the fourth lineage as work |
| `tribhiḥ` | instrumental plural | by the three [noble lineages] |
| `vṛttiḥ` | nominative singular feminine | livelihood, maintenance |
| `tṛṣṇā-utpāda-vipakṣataḥ` | ablative in adverbial causal use | because of being counterforces to productions of thirst |
| `mama-ahaṃkāra-vastu-icchā` | compound with nominative singular feminine head | desire concerning objects of mine-making and I-making |
| `tat-kāla-atyanta-śāntaye` | dative singular compound | for pacification at the present time and final pacification |

The opening is compressed and is resolved by the Bhāṣya as a distributive
three-plus-one construction:

```text
tribhiḥ vṛttiḥ
    livelihood by the first three

[caturthena] karmāntaḥ
    work by the fourth
```

The second half likewise receives a distributive explanation: the first three
produce present pacification of desire toward mine-making objects; the fourth
aims at final pacification of both mine-making and I-making desire.

## 4. Grammar

The surface syntax condenses two prescribed dimensions of the renunciant life:

```text
vṛttiḥ
    tribhiḥ [āryavaṃśaiḥ]

livelihood
    by the three [noble lineages]
```

```text
karmāntaḥ
    [caturthena āryavaṃśena]

work
    by the [fourth noble lineage]
```

The Bhāṣya makes this ellipsis explicit by distinguishing `vṛtti` from
`karma`. `Karmānta` should therefore not be joined with `vṛtti` as though the
verse named four equivalent means of subsistence.

The causal expression is:

```text
tṛṣṇotpāda-vipakṣataḥ
    because the four lineages are counterforces
    to four productions of thirst
```

The Sūtra material cited in the prose gives their respective fields: robes,
alms-food, lodging, and existence/non-existence.

The dative compound states purpose:

```text
tat-kāla-śāntaye
    for present pacification

atyanta-śāntaye
    for final pacification
```

The Bhāṣya controls the distribution. The first three presently pacify desire
for objects of mine-making; the fourth finally pacifies desire directed toward
both mine-making and I-making.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Livelihood is by the three, and work by [the fourth], because they are
> counterforces to productions of thirst. [They are prescribed] for the
> present and final pacification of desire concerning objects of mine-making
> and I-making.

The bracketed distribution follows the Bhāṣya's explicit resolution of the
kārikā's ellipsis.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The first three noble lineages provide the practitioner's bounded livelihood;
> the fourth—delight in abandonment and cultivation—provides the work to be
> done. They are prescribed as counterforces to the four productions of thirst
> associated with robes, alms-food, lodging, and existence or non-existence.
> The first three pacify for the present desire toward objects appropriated as
> “mine”; the fourth aims at final pacification of desire centered on both
> “mine” and one's own existence as “I.”

The livelihood/work distinction, the four thirsts, and the allocation of
present and final pacification are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The Path reorganizes life into maintenance and task. Material supports are
> accepted within contentment so that embodied practice can continue; they are
> not permitted to become an autonomous project of acquisition. The life thus
> maintained is subordinated to its proper work, abandonment and cultivation.
> This work reaches beyond the present restraint of possessive desire toward
> the final pacification of the appropriation of one's own continuing existence.

The governing structure is:

```text
bounded maintenance
    sustains the bearer of the Path

liberating work
    determines why that life is sustained
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vṛtti` — livelihood or maintenance**

The bounded mode by which the practitioner's life is sustained through
contentment with robes, alms-food, and lodging. It answers the material
conditions of practice without converting them into independent acquisitive
ends.

**`Karmānta` / `karma` — work to be done**

The fourth noble lineage as the practitioner's proper work: delight in
abandonment and cultivation (`prahāṇa-bhāvanā-rāmatā`). The Bhāṣya explicitly
contrasts this work with livelihood.

**`Prahāṇa-bhāvanā-rāmatā` — delight in abandonment and cultivation**

The fourth `āryavaṃśa`. It has `alobha` as its nature because it turns away
from craving for existence and sensual desire. It does not merely regulate
possessions but directs the maintained life toward liberation.

**`Tṛṣṇotpāda` — production of thirst**

The Bhāṣya cites four productions: thirst concerning robes, food, lodging, and
`bhava/vibhava`, existence or non-existence. Each noble lineage is specified as
a counterforce to a determinate production rather than to craving in an
undifferentiated sense.

**`Vipakṣa` / `pratipakṣa` — counterforce**

The operative relation between each noble lineage and its corresponding
production of thirst. This continues the positive counter-determination
established in 6.07.

**`Mamakāra` — mine-making**

Appropriation of robes, food, lodging, and similar supports as “mine.” The
first three lineages regulate desire in this material field.

**`Mamakāra-vastu` — object of mine-making**

The support appropriated into the possessive relation. The Bhāṣya identifies
the three immediate classes as robes, food, and lodging.

**`Ahaṃkāra` — I-making**

The appropriative determination centered on one's own existence or personal
continuum. It should not be reduced here to a mere linguistic use of the
pronoun “I.”

**`Ahaṃkāra-vastu` — object of I-making**

Identified by the Bhāṣya as `ātmabhāva`, one's own existence or embodied
personal continuum. The fourth lineage addresses craving at this deeper level.

**`Tat-kāla-śānti` — pacification for the present**

The limited effect of the first three noble lineages upon desire for objects
of mine-making. It stabilizes livelihood but does not by itself complete the
destruction of appropriation.

**`Atyanta-śānti` — final pacification**

The aim of the fourth lineage: final pacification of both mine-making and
I-making desire through the work of abandonment and cultivation.

**`Bhava/vibhava-tṛṣṇā` — thirst for existence/non-existence**

The fourth production of thirst in the cited Sūtra enumeration. Its precise
relation to the fourth lineage must preserve both poles rather than translating
the compound as ordinary material desire.

## 8. Logical Determination

The four noble lineages form a three-plus-one structure:

```text
RobeContentment
AND AlmsFoodContentment
AND LodgingContentment
    → Provides(BoundedLivelihood)

DelightInAbandonmentAndCultivation
    → Provides(LiberatingWork)
```

Their counterforce mapping is:

```text
RobeContentment
    counteracts ThirstForRobes

AlmsFoodContentment
    counteracts ThirstForFood

LodgingContentment
    counteracts ThirstForLodging

DelightInAbandonmentAndCultivation
    counteracts ThirstForExistenceOrNonexistence
```

The pacification ranges are asymmetric:

```text
FirstThreeNobleLineages
    presentlyPacify DesireForMineMakingObjects

FourthNobleLineage
    finallyPacifies DesireForMineMakingObjects
    AND finallyPacifies DesireForIMakingObject
```

Maintenance is functionally subordinated to work:

```text
Maintains(person, BoundedLivelihood)
AND Performs(person, LiberatingWork)
    → Orients(person, Liberation)

BoundedLivelihood
    enables LiberatingWork
    but isNotIdenticalTo LiberatingWork
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Renunciation is not inactivity.** The reduction of material demand defines
`vṛtti`, not the whole Path. The fourth lineage assigns a positive `karma`:
abandonment and cultivation.

**Maintenance receives its Meaning from the task.** Robes, food, and lodging
are preserved as necessary supports, but the fourth lineage determines their
telos. Life is maintained for a work rather than maintenance becoming the
final aim.

**The two forms of appropriation must remain distinct.** `Mamakāra` concerns
objects established as mine; `ahaṃkāra` concerns one's own existence as the
object of I-making. Restricting possessions does not by itself eliminate the
deeper appropriation of the personal continuum.

**Present pacification is not final pacification.** The first three lineages
bound material desire here and now. The fourth performs the work oriented
toward final cessation. This prevents practical simplicity from being mistaken
for completed liberation.

**The fourth integrates rather than merely follows the first three.** Its task
determines why the bounded livelihood exists. The structure is not an
accidental list of four parallel habits but a practical whole of support and
purpose.

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 6.08 gives Path Meaning an explicit practical
teleology. This is project synthesis rather than source terminology:

```text
vṛtti
    conditions that keep the Path-bearer viable

karma
    the operation for which viability is maintained

telos
    final pacification of appropriative desire
```

The Ideal Copula here joins means and end by subordinating maintenance to the
work that gives it Meaning. A genuine `saṃyama-bhūmi` cannot be defined merely
as withdrawal or simplicity; it must sustain the positive operations of
`prahāṇa` and `bhāvanā`. The Kośa's own articulation remains exact:
`vṛtti + karma`, present pacification + final pacification.
