# VAK 6.46 Bhāṣya — Attainments of Separation and Their Loss

## Kārikā Anchor

> laukikenāryavairāgye visaṃyogāptayo dvidhā /
> lokottareṇa cety eke tyakte kleśāsamanvayāt // 6.46 //

> When an Ārya attains dispassion by the mundane [Path], the attainments
> of separation are twofold. Some say [they are twofold] also by the
> supramundane [Path], because — when [the attainment] is relinquished —
> [otherwise] possession of the afflictions [would follow].

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.46.md). The last clause states
the reported position's reason, not Vasubandhu's own conclusion; the
Bhāṣya below unpacks it and then rejects it. VAK 6.45 closed with the
upper limit of mundane dispassion at the summit of existence and the
transition `tatra punaḥ`, taken up here.

## Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> tatra punaḥ
> laukikenāryavairāgye visaṃyogāptayo dvidhā /
> laukikena mārgeṇāryavairāgyaṃ gacchato dvividhā visaṃyogaprāptaya
> utpadyante laukikyo lokottarāśca /
> lokottareṇa cetyeke
> lokottareṇāpyevamityapare /
> kiṃ kāraṇam /
> tyavate kleśāsamanvayāt // VAkK_6.46 //
> yadi hyāryamārgeṇa vairāgyaṃ prāpnuvato laukikī visaṃyogaprāpotir
> notpadyate evaṃ sati ya āryamārgeṇākiñcanyāyatanādvītarāgo dhyānaṃ
> niśrityendriyāṇi saṃcarati sakṛtsnapūrvamārgatyāgātkevalaphalamārgalābhāccordhvabhūmikleśavisaṃyogenāsamanvāgataḥ syāt /
> tyakte ca tasmin taiḥ kleśaiḥ samanvāgataḥ syāditi /
> bhavāgrādhavimuktordhvajātavattvasamanvayaḥ /
> asatyāmapi tu tasyāṃ laukikyāṃ visaṃyogaprāptau na taiḥ samanvāgamaḥ syāt /
> tadyathā bhavāgrādardha prakāravimuktasyāsatyāmapi tu laukikyāṃ
> tadvisaṃyogaprāptau tyaktāyāmapi cendriyasaṃcāreṇa lokottarāyāṃ na
> punastaiḥ kleśaiḥ samanvāgamo bhavati /
> yathā ca pṛthagjanasya prathamadhyānabhūmerurdhvaṃ jātasya
> kāmāvacarakleśavisaṃyogaprāptityāgānna punastaiḥ samanvāgamo
> bhavatītyajñāpakametat /

This is the natural unit from Pradhan [366|16] through [367|10] in the
[running source](../../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt). It begins with
`tatra punaḥ`, left over from 6.45's boundary, and ends at Vasubandhu's
verdict `ajñāpakam etat`. The line `bhavāgrādhavimuktordhvajātavattvasamanvayaḥ`
is metrically the opening half-verse of VAK 6.47 — its formal marker
`VAkK_6.47` appears later, after the second half — but it is included
here because it supplies Vasubandhu's own counterexample to the
argument posed within 6.46, and the prose does not permit the two to be
separated. The boundary with 6.47 proper falls after `ajñāpakam etat`,
at the next question, `katapayā punar bhūmyā kuto vairāgyaṃ bhavati`.

The witness shows several irregularities, retained without silent
correction: the kārikā reads `tyavate` where the official kārikā study
reads `tyakte`; the prose has `visaṃyogaprāpotir` for `visaṃyogaprāptir`
and the run-together `cordhvabhūmi-` for `ca ūrdhvabhūmi-`. The
compressed half-verse `bhavāgrādhavimuktordhvajātavattvasamanvayaḥ` is
also unstable: the following prose glosses the example as
`bhavāgrād ardha-prakāra-vimuktasya`, "one liberated from half the
grades of the summit of existence," which does not visibly match the
transmitted compound. This is recorded as an open difficulty, not
resolved here. This source is a working reference text, not a critical
edition; no collation is claimed.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> “When an Ārya attains dispassion by the mundane [Path], the
> attainments of separation are twofold.” For one who attains an
> Ārya's dispassion by a mundane Path, two attainments of separation
> arise: mundane and supramundane.
>
> “Some say [they are twofold] also by the supramundane [Path]” — so
> others likewise say it is so by the supramundane Path. What is the
> reason? “Because, when [the attainment] is relinquished, there would
> [otherwise] be possession of the afflictions.”
>
> Suppose that, for one who attains dispassion by the Noble [Path], no
> mundane attainment of separation arose. This being so, consider one
> who, having become dispassionate from the sphere of nothingness by
> the Noble Path, relies on a dhyāna and undergoes a transformation of
> the faculties. Because the entire prior Path is thereby relinquished
> and only the fruit-Path is newly gained, that person would not
> possess separation from the afflictions of the higher ground. And
> when that [attainment] is relinquished, the person would then be
> possessed by those afflictions.
>
> [Vasubandhu's counterexample, opening VAK 6.47; the compressed
> half-verse here is uncertain and is not translated in isolation —
> see the source note above:] Even in the absence of that mundane
> attainment of separation, there would not be possession by those
> afflictions. For example, for one liberated from half the grades at
> the summit of existence: although the mundane attainment of
> separation from them is absent, and even when the supramundane
> attainment is relinquished through a transformation of the
> faculties, there is no renewed possession by those afflictions. And
> just as, for an ordinary person born above the level of the first
> dhyāna, from relinquishing the attainment of separation from
> desire-realm afflictions, there is no renewed possession of them —
> this [reason offered] is non-probative.

The reported position's reason and Vasubandhu's counterexamples are
kept as distinct voices, following the official kārikā study. The
kārikā's own reason-clause, `tyakte kleśāsamanvayāt`, is elliptical on
its own; its full sense — that loss of the sole supramundane attainment
would otherwise leave the practitioner possessed again by the
afflictions — only becomes clear from the prose that follows it, and
the translation above renders it accordingly rather than leaving it as
an unglossed fragment. The compressed half-verse opening the reply is
left with its difficulty noted above rather than smoothed into a
confident rendering.

## Movement of the Commentary

**The firm mundane rule.** When an Ārya gains dispassion by a mundane
Path, one abandonment is possessed under two Path-registers: a mundane
and a supramundane attainment of separation. Nothing is destroyed
twice; the achieved separation is simply recognized in both.

**The reported extension.** Some — named `eke` in the verse, `apare`
in the prose gloss — hold that the same twofold attainment must also
arise when dispassion is gained by the supramundane Path itself. Their
argument is by consequence: they describe a practitioner dispassionate
from the sphere of nothingness through the Noble Path who later
undergoes a transformation of the faculties while relying on a dhyāna.
Because the whole prior Path-acquisition is relinquished and only the
corresponding fruit-Path is gained, this practitioner would lose the
separation-attainment for the higher afflictions — and so, they argue,
would be possessed by those afflictions once again, unless a second,
mundane attainment had also secured the abandonment independently.

**Vasubandhu's reply.** He does not deny that a technical acquisition
is relinquished during faculty transformation. He denies that its loss
entails renewed possession of the afflictions. The summit-of-existence
case is decisive: dispassion from that ground is established in 6.45
as supramundane only, so no mundane attainment of separation is even
available there — yet relinquishing the supramundane attainment through
faculty transformation still does not restore the abandoned
afflictions. The case of an ordinary person born above the first
dhyāna, who relinquishes a mundane attainment without reacquiring the
desire-realm afflictions, reinforces the same point from the opposite
direction. The verdict, `ajñāpakam etat`, is narrowly logical: the
reason offered does not prove what it was meant to prove.

## Argument Reconstruction

**1. The undisputed case.**

```text
Ārya attains dispassion by mundane Path
    -> mundane attainment of separation
    -> supramundane attainment of separation
```

**2. The reported extension and its reason.**

```text
reported claim (eke / apare):
    supramundane dispassion also requires both attainments

reason offered:
    faculty transformation relinquishes the supramundane attainment
        -> no attainment of separation remains
        -> possession of the afflictions returns
```

**3. Vasubandhu's counterexamples.**

```text
bhavāgra case:
    no mundane attainment ever available
    + supramundane attainment relinquished
    -> no renewed possession

rebirth-above-first-dhyāna case:
    mundane attainment relinquished
    -> no renewed possession
```

**4. Terms that must not be collapsed.**

```text
kleśa-prahāṇa           abandonment of the affliction
visaṃyoga-prāpti        attainment of separation from it
kleśa-samanvāgama /
  asamanvāgama          possession / non-possession of it
```

The reported position treats continued non-possession as requiring a
presently held attainment of separation. Vasubandhu's counterexamples
show this is not necessary: loss of a particular attainment does not
by itself re-establish possession of the affliction it once secured
separation from.

## Organon Response — Accomplished Separation Beyond Its Present Form

For the Organon, the passage distinguishes an accomplished determination
from the acquisition-form presently classifying it. A later
transformation can relinquish a specific technical acquisition without
returning the continuum to the afflictive determination the Path had
already broken.

```text
prior Path-operation
    -> accomplished separation
    -> later change in acquisition-form
    -> no necessary return of the affliction
```

This is a bounded philosophical reading of Vasubandhu's own refusal of
the reported inference. It does not identify a `prāpti` with any modern
category of state or memory, nor claim that Vasubandhu offers a general
theory of continuity here. The conventional result stands on its own:
loss of a particular attainment of separation does not entail renewed
possession of the affliction.

## Review Status

Provisional official Bhāṣya study. The full unit from `tatra punaḥ`
through `ajñāpakam etat`, the reported `eke`/`apare` position and its
reason, both of Vasubandhu's counterexamples, and the boundary with
6.47 were checked against the running source and against the official
kārikā study's terminology. Witness irregularities and the unresolved
`bhavāgrādhavimukta-` compound remain open and are not silently
