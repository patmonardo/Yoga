# VAK 6.46 Bhāṣya — Two Attainments and a Failed Inference

## Kārikā Anchor

> laukikenārya-vairāgye visaṃyogāptayo dvidhā |
> lokottareṇa cety eke tyakte kleśāsamanvayāt || 6.46 ||

> When an Ārya gains dispassion through the mundane Path, attainments
> of separation are twofold. Some say the same is true through the
> supramundane Path: otherwise, relinquishment would bring possession
> of the afflictions.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.46.md). The second claim and
its proposed reason belong to the reported `eke` position. The
Bhāṣya tests that reason rather than simply endorsing it.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Now, when an Ārya gains dispassion through a mundane Path,
> “attainments of separation are twofold”: for one gaining
> dispassion by a mundane Path, two kinds of attainment of
> separation arise, mundane and supramundane.
>
> “And by the supramundane [Path], say some.” Others say it is so
> even by the supramundane Path. Why? “[Otherwise,] when [the
> relevant attainment] is relinquished, there would be possession
> of the afflictions.” Suppose a person gains dispassion from the
> sphere of nothingness by the Noble Path, then changes faculties
> while relying on a dhyāna. If mundane attainment of separation
> did not also arise, that person would relinquish the entire
> former Path and acquire only the fruit-Path, and so would be
> without the attainment of separation from afflictions of the
> higher ground. When that attainment was relinquished, the person
> would again possess those afflictions, according to this argument.
>
> “[As in one] partly liberated from the summit of existence and
> one reborn above [the first dhyāna, there is no renewed]
> possession.” Even without a mundane attainment of separation,
> there would be no possession of those afflictions. For example,
> one partly liberated from the summit of existence has no mundane
> attainment of separation from its afflictions; even when the
> supramundane attainment is relinquished through a change of
> faculties, one does not again possess those afflictions. Likewise,
> an ordinary person born above the level of the first dhyāna does
> not again possess desire-realm afflictions upon relinquishing the
> attainment of separation from them. Thus the proposed reason
> does not establish the claim.

The supplied brackets in the translation mark what the surrounding

is translated only to complete 6.46's counterexample; its full

## Movement of the Commentary

**The stated rule.** For an Ārya who gains dispassion by a mundane
Path, both mundane and supramundane `visaṃyogaprāpti` arise. The
twofoldness concerns attainments of separation, not two distinct
abandonments of the same affliction.

**The reported extension.** `Eke`, further specified as `apare`,
extends that rule to dispassion won by a supramundane Path. The
defender argues that a later change of faculties might relinquish
the prior noble Path acquisition. Without a parallel mundane
attainment of separation, the person would allegedly reacquire
the higher-ground afflictions. This is a consequence alleged by
the defender, not the Bhāṣya's final finding.

**Counterexamples and verdict.** The partially liberated person
at `bhavāgra` cannot have a mundane attainment of separation from
that summit, as 6.45 established. Nevertheless, relinquishment of
the supramundane attainment through faculty change does not restore
the afflictions. An ordinary person reborn above the first dhyāna
likewise does not reacquire desire-realm afflictions upon losing
their prior separation-attainment. Thus `ajñāpakam etat`: the
defender's proposed reason fails to prove the extension. The
Bhāṣya does not thereby demonstrate that every version of the
reported extension is false.

**Three distinct terms.** Abandonment of a `kleśa`, attainment of
separation (`visaṃyogaprāpti`), and possession or non-possession of
that `kleśa` have related but nonidentical roles. The examples
defeat the unrestricted inference from the loss of one attainment
to renewed afflictive possession.

## Organon Response — What the Counterexample Establishes

For our Science of Knowing, this is a useful test of an attempted
necessity. A rule about how a result is acquired cannot be proved
merely by imagining that its loss would undo the result; the
continuum must be checked in cases where an acquisition actually
changes. Here those cases preserve non-possession of the affliction
despite relinquishment of an attainment.

That is our reconstruction of the argument's logical force.
Vasubandhu's narrower conclusion is `ajñāpaka`: the defender's
reason does not establish the proposed twofold acquisition for
supramundane dispassion. The text does not formulate a general
theory of genetically preserved actuality or identify separation
with Nondual Absolute Reason.
