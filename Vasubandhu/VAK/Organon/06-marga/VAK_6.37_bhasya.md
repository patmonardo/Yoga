# VAK 6.37 Bhāṣya — Five Non-Returners and the Upward Stream

## Kārikā Anchor

> so 'ntarotpanna-saṃskārāsaṃskāra-parinirvṛtiḥ |
> ūrdhvasrotāś ca sa dhyāne vyavakīrṇo 'kaniṣṭhagaḥ || 6.37 ||

> That non-returner may reach final nirvāṇa in the intermediate
> existence, after arising, with exertion, without exertion, or
> as an upward-streamer. With intermingled dhyāna, the upward-
> streamer goes to Akaniṣṭha.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.37.md). After 6.36's
threshold of non-return, the question becomes how the remaining
course reaches completion.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> “That one attains final nirvāṇa in the intermediate state,
> after arising, with exertion, or without exertion; and is an
> upward-streamer.” The first name means that final nirvāṇa
> occurs in the interval; the other designations are to be
> construed similarly. This same non-returner is fivefold.
> The intermediate-completer reaches final nirvāṇa in the
> intermediate existence. The one completing after rebirth does
> so soon after arising, because of a strongly engaged carrying
> Path; this is described as nirvāṇa with remainder. Others say
> without remainder, but the reply notes a lack of mastery over
> relinquishing the lifespan.
>
> The exertion-completer has not relaxed application after
> rebirth and reaches final nirvāṇa through effort. The one
> completing without exertion does so without that renewed
> effort. Others explain the pair through Paths with conditioned
> or unconditioned objects; this is rejected because it would
> extend too far. Scripture lists the non-exertion type first,
> appropriately, since the difference concerns attainment
> without effort and with effort. The person completing soon
> after rebirth has a more powerful carrying Path and weaker
> latent tendencies.
>
> An upward-streamer has an upward course and does not attain
> final nirvāṇa in the first place of rebirth. “Stream” and
> “course” have the same sense here. “With intermingled dhyāna,
> that one goes to Akaniṣṭha.” The upward-streamer is twofold
> by cause and by result: by cause, intermingled or unintermingled
> dhyāna; by result, Akaniṣṭha or the summit of existence as
> furthest destination. The one whose dhyāna is intermingled
> reaches Akaniṣṭha and attains final nirvāṇa there.

The translation retains the reported `apare` explanations as
other voices. The compressed terms for Path-current and effort
at [359|07–09] are interpreted only to the extent required by
the explicit contrast between effortful and effortless
completion; their detailed morphology remains for collation.

## Movement of the Commentary

**Five modes of completion.** The same **anāgāmin** may complete
in the intermediate existence, soon after rebirth, with exertion,
without renewed exertion, or after a higher-moving course. These
are five types within the third-fruit position, not five further
fruits. The first two distinguish the temporal locus of final
nirvāṇa; the next two distinguish whether application remains
necessary; the fifth distinguishes the trajectory itself.

**Reported alternatives.** An `apare` view takes the completion
soon after rebirth to involve nirvāṇa without remainder; the
reply objects from lack of control over relinquishing lifespan.
Another `apare` view analyzes exertion and non-exertion by the
conditioned or unconditioned object of the Path. Vasubandhu
rejects this as overextending the distinction. The contrast
is one of effort in attaining completion. The Path-current and
the strength of residual **anuśaya** jointly affect how soon
that completion comes.

**The upward stream.** `Ūrdhvasrotas` names one whose course
continues upward rather than ending in the first higher place
of rebirth. The Bhāṣya explicitly equates `srotas` and `gati`
in this context. It then gives two correlated divisions: the
cause is intermingled or unintermingled dhyāna; the result is
an Akaniṣṭha or summit-of-existence maximum. This verse
explains the intermingled-dhyāna, Akaniṣṭha-bound case. The
technical meaning of `vyavakīrṇa-dhyāna` should be kept at
the local level established here, a causal meditative condition,
without equating it with ordinary distraction.

## Organon Response — One Fruit, Distinct Remaining Courses

For our Science of Knowing, non-return fixes a real direction:
the desire-domain no longer holds the practitioner. Yet the
remaining movement has several forms, determined by Path-power,
residual tendencies, effort, and meditative history. An attained
fruit therefore does not erase the determinate manner of its
future completion.

This is our Organon reconstruction of the classification. The
five types, their source debates, and the Akaniṣṭha relation
belong to Vasubandhu's Abhidharma account. The passage does not
itself identify intermingled dhyāna with our nondual Samāpatti.
