# VAK 6.41 — Maturation of Faculty and the Closure of Regression

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

न परावृत्तजन्मार्यः कामे धात्वन्तरोपगः ।
स चोर्ध्वजश्च नैवाक्षसञ्चारपरिहाणिभाक् ॥ ६.४१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

na parāvṛtta-janmāryaḥ kāme dhātv-antaropagaḥ |
sa cordhva-jaś ca naivākṣa-saṃcāra-parihāṇi-bhāk || 6.41 ||

The Bhāṣya controls the first compound sequence: it concerns a Noble who has undergone a further birth **within the desire realm**, not a Noble returning to desire after entry into another realm.

## 4. Grammar

In the first line, **kāme** locates **parāvṛtta-janmā āryaḥ**: “the Noble who has undergone another birth in the desire realm.” The negated predicate is **dhātvantaropagaḥ**: such a practitioner does not subsequently go to another realm.

In the second line, the correlatives **saḥ ca ... ūrdhvajaḥ ca** join two subjects—the desire-realm Noble after another birth and the Noble born in an upper realm. Both fall under **na eva ... bhāk**. The compound coordinates **akṣasaṃcāra** and **parihāṇi** as excluded possibilities.

The kārikā states the exclusions. Greater faculty-maturity and acquisition of a superior support as their causes belong to the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

A Noble who has undergone another birth in the desire realm does not go to another realm. Both that one and the Noble born above certainly undergo neither transfer of faculties nor falling away.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

After a Noble practitioner has passed through another desire-realm birth, that practitioner does not subsequently enter a different realm, because non-returning and final nirvāṇa are completed within that life. Neither such a Noble nor one reborn in a higher realm changes faculty-type or regresses: another Noble birth has matured the faculties and supplied a superior basis.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The Path does not leave every earlier possibility perpetually open. Its continuation through another birth matures the capacity already formed and gives it a more adequate basis. As this formation ripens, certain changes that were once possible—alteration of faculty and loss of attainment—cease to belong to the practitioner's transition-space.

Irreversibility is therefore not imposed as an external prohibition. It is the positive consequence of maturation: the developed faculty and its superior support no longer admit the former mode of instability.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**parāvṛtta-janman in kāmadhātu**

Immediately before the kārikā, the Bhāṣya states that a Noble who undergoes another birth in the desire realm does not afterward go to a different realm. Upon attaining non-returning, this practitioner reaches final nirvāṇa in that same life.

This restriction does not apply identically to every higher-born Noble. The summit-bound upward-streamer born in the form realm may still enter the formless attainments. The rule is tied to the practitioner's exact birth-position and remaining course.

**ūrdhva-ja**

The **higher-born** Noble has been reborn in an upper realm. Together with the desire-realm Noble after another birth, this practitioner has crossed a maturation threshold that excludes faculty-transfer and falling away.

**indriya-saṃcāra**

**Indriya-saṃcāra** is transfer or transformation of faculty-type. Its exclusion means that the practitioner's operative capacity has become sufficiently mature and stabilized that a further change of this classification no longer occurs.

**parihāṇi**

**Parihāṇi** is falling away or regression from attainment. The emphatic **naiva** excludes it for both practitioner positions under discussion. This is a local rule concerning Nobles after an intervening birth, not an unrestricted claim that falling away is impossible at every stage.

**paripakvataratva**

The first Bhāṣya cause is the greater ripeness or maturity of the faculties produced through residence over another birth:

```text
intervening Noble birth
    → faculties become more mature
    → faculty-transfer and regression cease
```

Time here is not empty duration. It is the causal maturation of an already determined Path-capacity.

**āśraya-viśeṣa-lābha**

The second cause is acquisition of a superior **āśraya**, basis or support. The faculty is stabilized not in abstraction but through a transformed supporting condition:

```text
greater faculty-maturity
    + superior support acquired
    → narrowed transition-space
```

**Why desire-detachment is not enough**

The subsequent Bhāṣya asks why a trainee already free from desire does not necessarily attain final nirvāṇa in the intermediate existence. The Vaibhāṣika answer is that the Path may remain unmastered or not actually present, the latent tendencies may not be sufficiently weak, and the desire-realm current is difficult to cross.

Much work can therefore remain:

```text
unwholesome and indeterminate afflictions abandoned
    + further fruits attained
    + three realms transcended
```

One local attainment does not substitute for the complete capacity required by this compound task.

## 8. Logical Determination

The kārikā excludes transitions from two matured positions:

```text
DesireRealmNobleAfterAnotherBirth
    ↛ entry into another realm
    ↛ faculty-transfer
    ↛ falling away

HigherBornNoble
    ↛ faculty-transfer
    ↛ falling away
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the causal invariant:

```text
InterveningNobleBirth
    → GreaterFacultyMaturity
    + SuperiorBasisAcquired
    → NoFacultyTransfer
    + NoRegression
```

It also blocks an invalid inference:

```text
DesireDetached
    ⇏ PathMastered
    ⇏ PathActuallyPresent
    ⇏ AllRemainingWorkPerformable
    ⇏ ImmediateFinalLiberation
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, maturation converts a previously revisable Path-morphology into an invariant practical rule. The continuum does not need reflective machinery to prohibit regression; its formed capacity has made regression no longer an admissible operation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.40 defined the strict good course through irreversible wholesome operation. VAK 6.41 now gives that irreversibility a causal morphology: faculty becomes mature and receives a superior support. The restriction on possible transitions is the result of positive development, not an arbitrary ban.

This supports the methodological priority of Seeing. A Path begun in direct Noble discrimination is not perfected by replacing Seeing with increasingly heavy representation. Its certainty is carried forward by maturation of the faculty and transformation of its basis. Reflection has a legitimate role in cultivation, but it must serve the actuality first disclosed rather than reconstruct it as a merely conceptual object.

The domain and transition exclusions belong to the kārikā. Their exact scope, maturation causes, qualifications, and the analysis of remaining work belong to the Bhāṣya; the latter explanation is explicitly Vaibhāṣika. The contrast between perceptual disclosure and reflective machinery is Organon synthesis and is not attributed to Vasubandhu's wording.
