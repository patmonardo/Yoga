# VAK_6.10

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आसमुद्रास्थिविस्तारसंक्षेपादादिकर्मिकः ।
पादास्थ्न आ कपालार्धत्यागात् कृतजयः स्मृतः ॥ ६.१० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
āsamudrāsthi-vistāra-saṃkṣepād ādikarmikaḥ |
pādāsthna ā kapālārdha-tyāgāt kṛtajayaḥ smṛtaḥ || 6.10 ||
```

VAK 6.10 describes two stages in the cultivation of the unattractive. The
beginner learns to establish, expand, and contract the skeletal representation.
The practitioner who has gained mastery progressively relinquishes that
representation from the bones of the feet until only half the skull remains.

The transmitted `pādāsthna` is morphologically difficult. Its practical sense
is secured by the Bhāṣya: progressive removal begins with the bones of the feet.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ā-samudra | asthi-vistāra-saṃkṣepāt | ādikarmikaḥ |
pāda-asthna | ā-kapāla-ardha-tyāgāt | kṛta-jayaḥ | smṛtaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ā-samudra` | prepositional compound | as far as the ocean |
| `asthi-vistāra-saṃkṣepāt` | ablative singular compound | through expansion and contraction of the skeletal representation |
| `ādikarmikaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | beginner in the practice |
| `pādāsthna` | transmitted difficult form | beginning from the bones of the feet |
| `ā-kapāla-ardha-tyāgāt` | ablative singular compound | through relinquishing progressively up to half the skull |
| `kṛta-jayaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | one who has achieved mastery |
| `smṛtaḥ` | nominative singular masculine past passive participle of `√smṛ` | is considered or designated |

The paired ablatives specify the operations by which the two practitioner
stages are known. The Bhāṣya uses the fuller expression `kṛtaparijaya` for the
mastered practitioner and introduces a third stage, `atikrāntamanaskāra`, whose
operation is completed in 6.11.

## 4. Grammar

The first half predicates the beginner-stage:

```text
ā samudram
    as far as the ocean

asthi-vistāra-saṃkṣepāt
    through expansion and contraction of the skeleton

ādikarmikaḥ [smṛtaḥ]
    one is considered a beginner
```

The Bhāṣya expands the compressed operation into a sequence. The practitioner
fixes attention on a readily accessible part of the body, removes flesh and
other coverings in imagination, establishes the complete skeleton, extends
that determination through progressively wider surroundings to the ocean, and
then contracts it back to the practitioner's own skeleton.

The second half predicates mastery:

```text
pādāsthna [ārabhya]
    beginning with the bones of the feet

ā kapālārdha-tyāgāt
    through relinquishment up to half the skull

kṛtajayaḥ smṛtaḥ
    one is considered to have achieved mastery
```

The prose makes clear that only half the skull remains after the progressive
subtraction; the counter-object has been brought under controlled removal.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Through expansion and contraction of the skeletal representation as far as
> the ocean, one is [known as] a beginner. Through relinquishing [the
> represented bones] beginning with those of the feet until half the skull
> [remains], one is considered to have achieved mastery.

The supplied references to representation and the final remainder follow the
Bhāṣya's explicit practice-description.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The beginner first fixes attention upon a part of the body, imaginatively
> removes its coverings until the complete skeleton appears, extends that
> skeletal determination through other bodies and the surrounding world as far
> as the ocean, and then contracts the field back to the practitioner's own
> skeleton. Expansion increases determinate imaginative resolution; contraction
> gathers citta. Having mastered these operations, the practitioner progressively
> relinquishes the counter-representation itself, beginning with the bones of
> the feet and continuing until only half the skull remains.

The local starting point, the removal of coverings, the purposes of expansion
and contraction, and the exact procedure of subtraction are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Preparatory Path-capacity is trained as command over a constructed cognitive
> field. The practitioner must be able to generate a determinate representation,
> extend it without dispersion, contract it without losing stability, and then
> relinquish it without returning to the afflictive presentation it opposed.
> Mastery lies not in clinging more intensely to the antidote but in becoming
> free with respect to its production, scope, concentration, and removal.

The complete movement is:

```text
local fixation
    → construction
    → universal extension
    → concentration
    → progressive relinquishment
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ādikarmika` — beginner**

The practitioner at the initial stage of the actual exercise. The beginner
must establish the skeletal support and acquire control over its extension and
contraction before proceeding to its systematic removal.

**`Asthi` — bone or skeletal framework**

The counter-representational material selected in 6.09 for all forms of bodily
desire. Here it is not a static object but a field subjected to controlled
cognitive operations.

**`Asthi-vistāra` — expansion of the skeletal representation**

The practitioner extends the determination from one's own body to another
body, then monastery, park, field, and finally the earth as far as the ocean.
The Bhāṣya assigns this operation the purpose of increasing `adhimokṣa`.

**`Saṃkṣepa` — contraction or gathering**

The return from the ocean-wide skeletal field to the singular representation
of one's own skeleton. Its stated purpose is `citta-saṃkṣepa`, gathering or
contracting the mind.

**`Adhimokṣa` — determinate imaginative resolution**

The capacity strengthened by expansion. It enables one mode of apprehension to
be held across an increasingly broad field. The operation is deliberate
resolution, not a claim that the earth is literally perceived as filled with
skeletons.

**`Citta-saṃkṣepa` — gathering of citta**

The function of contraction. The representation is drawn back from universal
extension into a stable concentrated field.

**`Kṛtajaya` / `kṛtaparijaya` — accomplished mastery**

The kārikā uses `kṛtajaya`; the Bhāṣya gives the fuller stage-name
`kṛtaparijaya`. Mastery is demonstrated by controlled progressive subtraction,
not merely by maintaining the complete skeleton more forcefully.

**`Tyāga` — relinquishment**

Removal of the represented bones in an ordered sequence. Because the
counter-object itself is relinquished, `aśubha` is shown to be instrumental
rather than an ultimate picture to which cognition must remain attached.

**`Kapālārdha` — half the skull**

The minimal remainder at the mastery-stage described in this verse. Its final
removal belongs to the third practitioner-stage completed in 6.11.

**`Atikrāntamanaskāra` — one whose attention has passed beyond**

The third stage named in the Bhāṣya's classification but not yet described by
this kārikā. It is recorded here only to preserve the commentary's staged
context; its defining operation belongs to the next verse.

## 8. Logical Determination

The practice operations are ordered:

```text
FixAttention(person, BodilyPart)
    → RemoveCoveringFeatures(person)
    → Establish(person, CompleteSkeletonRepresentation)

Establish(person, CompleteSkeletonRepresentation)
    → Expand(person, LocalBody, OceanBoundedField)
    → Increase(person, DeterminateResolution)

Expand(person, OceanBoundedField)
    → Contract(person, OwnSkeleton)
    → Gather(person, Citta)
```

The beginner-stage requires bidirectional control of scope:

```text
CanExpand(person, SkeletonRepresentation)
AND CanContract(person, SkeletonRepresentation)
    → Stage(person, BeginnerPracticeEstablished)
```

The mastery-stage requires controlled internal subtraction:

```text
ProgressivelyRelinquishes(
    person,
    SkeletonRepresentation,
    FromFeet,
    UntilHalfSkullRemains
)
    → Stage(person, MasteredPractitioner)
```

The functional dimensions remain distinct:

```text
Expansion
    trains ExtensionOfDeterminateResolution

Contraction
    trains GatheringOfCitta

Relinquishment
    trains FreedomFromTherapeuticSupport
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Aśubha is an operation, not a static image.** The exercise consists in fixing,
constructing, extending, contracting, and subtracting. Reducing it to “visualize
a skeleton” loses the Bhāṣya's exact training sequence.

**Expansion is not cosmological assertion.** Its purpose is
`adhimokṣābhivardhana`, increase of determinate resolution. The earth filled
with skeletons is the governed scope of the counter-representation, not a claim
about the world's literal appearance.

**Contraction is not reversal through failure.** It performs another task:
gathering citta. The practitioner must command both extension and concentration.

**Mastery includes relinquishing the antidote.** The skeleton is a functional
`pratipakṣa`, not a replacement dogma about what the body ultimately is. A
therapeutic representation that cannot be released would remain another
dependency.

**The practice remains preparatory.** This mastery concerns the construction
and governance of an object-support. It does not erase 6.09's distinction
between suppression and definitive abandonment.

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 6.10 exhibits bhāvanāmayī Prajñā as governed
transformation of the cognitive field. This is project synthesis:

```text
construct
    produce the counter-determination

extend
    apply it across the relevant object-field

contract
    gather it into concentrated cognition

relinquish
    remove dependence upon the counter-support itself
```

This reveals an essential requirement of Path Meaning: the operation must
preserve provenance and intended scope, achieve its counteractive result, and
then release its own provisional representation. In terms of 6.04, the
skeletal field is a cultivated configuration within `saṃvṛti`; it must not be
mistaken for the direct disclosure of `paramārtha`. Its truth is practical—its
capacity to suppress desire and train cognition for the subsequent Path.
