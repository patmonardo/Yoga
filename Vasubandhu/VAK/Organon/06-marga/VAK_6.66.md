# VAK 6.66 — Four Courses of Path-Progress

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

ध्यानेषु मार्गः प्रतिपत्सुखा दुःखान्यभूमिषु ।
दन्धाभिज्ञा मृदुमतेः क्षिप्राभिज्ञेतरस्य तु ॥ ६.६६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

dhyāneṣu mārgaḥ pratipat sukhā duḥkhānyabhūmiṣu |
dhandhābhijñā mṛdumateḥ kṣiprābhijñetarasya tu || 6.66 ||

The electronic kārikā witness also transmits **dhanyābhijñā**, but the explanatory prose consistently reads and defines the technical contrast **dhandhābhijñā / kṣiprābhijñā**, slow and quick realization. The controlled reading follows that explicit Bhāṣya determination.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

dhyāneṣu | mārgaḥ | pratipat | sukhā | duḥkhā | anya-bhūmiṣu |
dhandha-abhijñā | mṛdu-mateḥ | kṣipra-abhijñā | itarasya | tu

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **dhyāneṣu** | neuter locative plural | in the four principal dhyānas |
| **mārgaḥ** | masculine nominative singular | the Path under consideration |
| **pratipat** | feminine nominative singular | course or mode of progress conducting to nirvāṇa |
| **sukhā** | feminine nominative singular | easy, structurally well-supported |
| **duḥkhā** | feminine nominative singular | difficult, requiring effort because of disequilibrium |
| **anya-bhūmiṣu** | locative plural compound | in the other contemplative grounds |
| **dhandha-abhijñā** | feminine nominative singular compound | having slow realization |
| **mṛdu-mateḥ** | masculine genitive singular | for one whose intelligence or faculty is mild |
| **kṣipra-abhijñā** | feminine nominative singular compound | having quick realization |
| **itarasya** | masculine genitive singular | for the other, one of sharp faculty |
| **tu** | adversative particle | but, by contrast |

The Bhāṣya replaces the verse’s **mati** language with the technical faculty contrast **mṛdvindriya / tīkṣṇendriya**.

## 4. Grammar

The Path is identified with **pratipad** because it conducts the practitioner to nirvāṇa. Two locative constructions establish the first contrast:

```text
dhyāneṣu
    in the dhyānas
        → sukhā

anya-bhūmiṣu
    in the other grounds
        → duḥkhā
```

The genitives **mṛdumateḥ** and **itarasya** establish the second contrast according to the practitioner: slow realization for the mild-faculty person, quick realization for the other.

The syntax crosses two predications; it does not give a serial sequence:

```text
easy / difficult
        ×
slow / quick realization
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

In the dhyānas the Path is an easy course; in the other grounds it is difficult. For one of mild faculty it has slow realization, but for the other it has quick realization.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The Path is an easy course in the four dhyānas, where its requisite factors are present, calm and insight are balanced, and progress flows without strenuous effort. In the preparatory concentration, intermediate concentration, and immaterial grounds it is difficult because the Path-factors are incomplete and calm or insight is deficient. Independently, realization is slow for one of dull faculty and quick for one of sharp faculty, producing four possible courses of progress.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

The concrete course of Mārga is determined jointly by the field through which it proceeds and the capacity that proceeds through it. The contemplative ground determines resistance: balanced calm and insight permit an easy course, while their disequilibrium makes progress effortful. Faculty determines velocity: mild capacity realizes slowly and sharp capacity quickly.

Ease and speed are therefore not the same determination. An easy field may be traversed slowly, and a difficult field may be penetrated quickly.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Mārga as pratipad**

The Bhāṣya says that Mārga is also called **pratipad** because it conducts one to nirvāṇa:

```text
mārga
    Path as road to the End

pratipad
    that same Path as concrete course of progress
```

This is a change of determination, not the introduction of another Path.

**The four courses**

Vasubandhu enumerates:

```text
1. difficult + slow realization
2. difficult + quick realization
3. easy + slow realization
4. easy + quick realization
```

They result from a two-axis matrix:

| Ground / Faculty | Mild faculty | Sharp faculty |
|---|---|---|
| Four dhyānas | easy and slow | easy and quick |
| Other grounds | difficult and slow | difficult and quick |

**Easy progress in the four dhyānas**

The Bhāṣya gives three determinations:

```text
aṅga-parigraha
    the requisite Path-factors are possessed

śamatha-vipaśyanā-samatā
    calm and insight are in equilibrium

ayatna-vāhitva
    progress flows without strenuous effort
```

Accordingly, **sukhā** does not principally mean pleasant sensation. It means a course adequately supported by its contemplative structure.

**Difficult progress in the other grounds**

The relevant grounds are:

```text
anāgamya
    preliminary concentration

dhyānāntara
    intermediate concentration

ārūpya-bhūmis
    immaterial grounds
```

The Bhāṣya differentiates their deficiencies:

```text
anāgamya and dhyānāntara
    śamatha deficient

ārūpya grounds
    vipaśyanā deficient
```

Because the requisite configuration is incomplete, the course proceeds through effort (**yatnavāhitva**) and is called difficult.

These **ārūpya-bhūmis** are contemplative grounds. They should not be confused with the existential Ārūpyadhātu, although the attainments have a systematic relation to that domain.

**Slow and quick realization**

The second axis is determined by faculty:

```text
mṛdvindriya
    dull or mild faculty
        → dhandhābhijñā

tīkṣṇendriya
    sharp faculty
        → kṣiprābhijñā
```

The Bhāṣya allows the compound **dhandhābhijñā** to be construed either as a course on which realization is slow or as the course belonging to a slow person. Both construals preserve faculty-strength as the operative determination.

**Textual caution**

The kārikā line is transmitted as **dhanyābhijñā** in one electronic form. The Bhāṣya repeatedly gives **dhandhābhijñā**, defines its meaning, and pairs it with **kṣiprābhijñā**. The latter is therefore adopted as the doctrinally controlled text while the variant remains recorded.

**Transition to Bodhipakṣya**

At the close of the passage, the Bhāṣya says:

```text
punar apy eṣa mārgo bodhipakṣyākhyāṃ labhate

Again this same Path receives
the designation bodhipakṣya.
```

Thus the next block does not introduce a different discipline. It articulates this same Mārga according to the dharmas aligned with Bodhi.

## 8. Logical Determination

The governing functions are independent:

```text
EaseOfProgress = function(ContemplativeGround)

SpeedOfRealization = function(FacultyStrength)
```

More exactly:

```text
Balanced(Śamatha, Vipaśyanā)
    + PossessionOfFactors
    → EasyCourse

Deficiency(Śamatha or Vipaśyanā)
    + IncompleteFactors
    → DifficultCourse

MildFaculty
    → SlowRealization

SharpFaculty
    → QuickRealization
```

Consequently:

```text
EasyCourse does not entail QuickRealization
DifficultCourse does not entail SlowRealization
```

This guards against treating Path-development as a single scalar quantity. Ground and faculty are distinct conditions whose conjunction produces the concrete pratipad.

## 9. Interpretive Note

Here the earlier Dhātu–Indriya insight reaches a particularly exact Path-form. **Bhūmi** supplies the determinate field in which movement is possible; **indriya** supplies the operative capacity by which that field is traversed.

```text
bhūmi
    determines the resistance of movement

indriya
    determines the velocity of realization

pratipad
    their concrete conjunction as a course
```

This is not merely an objective field plus an externally added subject. The Path appears concretely only through their relation: the same faculty moves differently under different contemplative conditions, and the same ground is realized at different speeds by different faculties.

The Organon reconstruction may therefore describe pratipad as the determinate actuality of Path between Domain and Faculty. But the source-layer conclusion is narrower: the Bhāṣya explicitly grounds ease in meditative equilibrium and speed in faculty-strength, thereby refusing to infer one from the other.

This verse completes the approach to the Bodhi block. VAK 6.65 stated what Mārga does; 6.66 states how that operation is concretely traversed; 6.67 will name the same Path from the side of its End, as **bodhipakṣya**.
