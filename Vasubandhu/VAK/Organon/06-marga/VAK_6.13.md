# VAK_6.13

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आनापानौ यतः कायः सत्त्वाख्यावनुपात्तकौ ।
नैःष्यन्दिकौ नाधरेण लक्ष्येते मनसा च तौ ॥ ६.१३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ānāpānau yataḥ kāyaḥ sattvākhyāv anupāttakau |
naiḥṣyandikau nādhareṇa lakṣyete manasā ca tau || 6.13 ||
```

VAK 6.13 classifies the breaths that serve as the object-range of the Prajñā
analyzed in 6.12. Inhalation and exhalation belong to the same level as the
body, are counted within the sentient continuum, remain unappropriated, arise
as homogeneous-flow products, and are apprehended by cognition of their own or
a higher level rather than by a lower-level cognition.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ānāpānau | yataḥ | kāyaḥ | sattva-ākhyau | anupāttakau |
naiḥṣyandikau | na | adhareṇa | lakṣyete | manasā | ca | tau
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ānāpānau` | nominative dual masculine compound | inhalation and exhalation |
| `yataḥ` | relative adverb | from whatever level; wherefrom |
| `kāyaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | body |
| `sattva-ākhyau` | nominative dual masculine compound | designated or counted within a sentient being |
| `anupāttakau` | nominative dual masculine | unappropriated |
| `naiḥṣyandikau` | nominative dual masculine | homogeneous-flow products |
| `na` | negative particle | not |
| `adhareṇa` | instrumental singular comparative | by a lower [level cognition] |
| `lakṣyete` | third-person dual present passive of `√lakṣ` | are discerned or apprehended |
| `manasā` | instrumental singular neuter of `manas` | by mind or cognition |
| `ca` | conjunction | and, also |
| `tau` | nominative dual masculine demonstrative | those two |

The dual morphology consistently treats inhalation and exhalation as two
members of one classified process. The Bhāṣya supplies the comparison implicit
in the compressed final clause: cognition of the same or a higher `bhūmi` can
apprehend them; cognition of a lower `bhūmi` cannot.

## 4. Grammar

The opening is a relative determination of level:

```text
yadbhūmikaḥ kāyaḥ
    whatever level the body belongs to

tadbhūmikau ānāpānau
    inhalation and exhalation belong to that same level
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the correlative form and reason: the breaths share the
body's level because they are part of the body (`kāyaikadeśatva`).

Three further dual predicates classify the process:

```text
sattvākhyau
    counted within the sentient continuum

anupāttakau
    unappropriated

naiḥṣyandikau
    homogeneous-flow products
```

The final passive construction is level-sensitive:

```text
tau [ānāpānau]
    those two breaths

na adhareṇa manasā lakṣyete
    are not apprehended by lower-level cognition

[svabhūmi-uparibhūmikena cittena lakṣyete]
    are apprehended by cognition of their own or a higher level
```

The positive comparison in brackets is explicit in the Bhāṣya and should not
be mistaken for wording present in the kārikā.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Inhalation and exhalation belong to whatever level the body [belongs to].
> They are designated within a sentient being, are unappropriated, and are
> homogeneous-flow products. Those two are not apprehended by a lower-level
> mind [but by cognition of their own or a higher level].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Inhalation and exhalation belong to the same `bhūmi` as the body because they
> are bodily parts. They occur only in dependence upon a particular
> configuration of body and citta; they are absent where that configuration is
> absent, including the formless domain, the embryonic `kalala` state,
> non-cognitive attainment, and fourth dhyāna. Although counted within the
> sentient continuum, they are unappropriated because separable from the
> faculties. They are homogeneous-flow products rather than products of bodily
> accumulation or karmic maturation, and cognition of their own or a higher
> level apprehends them, not cognition of a lower level.

The body–citta condition, absence cases, reason for non-appropriation, excluded
causal types, and positive cognition-range are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Breath becomes an adequate object of Prajñā only when its distinct relations
> are discriminated. It belongs to embodied life without being an appropriated
> faculty; it continues causally without being a substance or direct maturation
> product; it depends upon a specific body–citta configuration rather than
> accompanying every possible embodied state; and its accessibility varies
> with cognitive level. The possessive immediacy “my breath” is therefore
> resolved into level, continuum, appropriation, causal, conditional, and
> epistemic determinations.

The verse and Bhāṣya jointly establish:

```text
adequate cognition
    requires
adequately differentiated object-relations
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ānāpāna` — inhalation and exhalation**

The paired wind-process classified here after 6.12 identified it as the object
of breath-recollection. The dual form preserves inhalation and exhalation as
distinguishable phases within one conditioned process.

**`Yadbhūmika kāya, tadbhūmika ānāpāna` — same level as the body**

The Bhāṣya's expanded rule. Breath belongs to the body's `bhūmi` because it is
a bodily part. This is a level-classification, not merely the claim that breath
is spatially located inside a body.

**`Kāyacittaviśeṣasaṃniśrita` — dependent upon a specific body–citta condition**

Breathing occurs only when the requisite bodily openness and cognition of a
breathing-level are present. This prevents the body alone from being treated as
a sufficient cause.

**States without breathing**

The Bhāṣya excludes the formless domain, the `kalala` embryonic state,
non-cognitive attainment, and fourth dhyāna. Breath begins at birth and upon
emergence from fourth dhyāna; it ceases at death and upon entry into fourth
dhyāna.

**`Sattvākhyā` — designated within a sentient continuum**

The breath is counted as belonging to the living being rather than as an
unrelated external wind. This continuum-status does not entail appropriation
as an indriya.

**`Anupāttaka` — unappropriated**

The Bhāṣya explains this by `indriyavinirbhāgitva`, separability from the
faculties. Breath participates in embodied life without sharing the organic
appropriation-status of faculty-related material form.

**`Indriyavinirbhāgitva` — separability from the faculties**

The ground for classifying the breaths as unappropriated. It is a functional
and mereological distinction within the body, not a claim that breathing is
external to life.

**`Naiḥṣyandika` — homogeneous-flow product**

The causal type assigned to breath. A present breath proceeds as a conditioned
continuation of prior homogeneous conditions; it is neither a product of
accumulation (`aupacayika`) nor a maturation-born product (`vipākaja`).

**Why breath is not `aupacayika`**

The Bhāṣya notes that bodily increase does not entail proportionately increased
breathing. Breath therefore does not behave as a simple accumulation-produced
material quantity.

**Why breath is not `vipākaja`**

After breathing has been interrupted it can reconnect. The Bhāṣya treats this
as incompatible with classifying the breaths as maturation-born material form.

**`Nādhareṇa` — not by a lower-level cognition**

The restriction concerns `bhūmi`, not a vague judgment of intellectual
inferiority. Cognition of the same or a higher level apprehends the breaths;
lower-level cognition does not.

**`Lakṣyete` — are discerned or apprehended**

The epistemic relation between the breath-process and level-adequate cognition.
The process does not become real only when attended to, but its determinate
apprehension is constrained by the level of cognition.

## 8. Logical Determination

The breath-process has a multi-axial type:

```text
BreathProcess {
    phases: Inhalation + Exhalation
    level: SameAsBody
    continuumStatus: CountedWithinSentientContinuum
    appropriationStatus: Unappropriated
    causalType: HomogeneousFlowProduct
    sufficientContext: SpecificBodyCittaConfiguration
    cognitiveAccess: SameOrHigherLevelCognition
}
```

Its level relation is:

```text
Level(body, bhumi)
AND PartOf(breath, body)
    → Level(breath, bhumi)
```

Its occurrence requires a conjunction of conditions:

```text
SuitableBodilyOpenness(person)
AND CognitionAtBreathingLevel(person)
    → CanOccur(BreathProcess, person)
```

The excluded states are:

```text
State(x, FormlessDomain)
OR State(x, Kalala)
OR State(x, NonCognitiveAttainment)
OR State(x, FourthDhyana)
    → NotOccurs(BreathProcess, x)
```

The process transitions are:

```text
Birth
OR EmergenceFromFourthDhyana
    → Begins(BreathProcess)

Death
OR EntryIntoFourthDhyana
    → Ceases(BreathProcess)
```

Its continuum and appropriation statuses remain independent:

```text
CountedWithin(BreathProcess, SentientContinuum)
AND SeparableFrom(BreathProcess, Faculties)
    → Unappropriated(BreathProcess)
```

Its epistemic constraint is:

```text
Level(cognition) >= Level(breath)
    → CanApprehend(cognition, breath)

Level(cognition) < Level(breath)
    → CannotApprehend(cognition, breath)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Breath is not universally present wherever there is body.** A specific
body–citta configuration is required. The absence and transition cases make
that dependency explicit.

**Belonging is not appropriation.** `Sattvākhyā` includes breath within the
sentient continuum; `anupāttaka` excludes it from faculty-appropriation. The
two predicates establish a middle determination rather than contradicting one
another.

**Causal flow is not substance.** `Naiḥṣyandika` identifies a conditioned
continuity whose recurrence depends upon prior conditions. The breath-object
of 6.12 is therefore a process, not a self-identical thing passing unchanged
through time.

**Cognitive accessibility is level-sensitive.** `Nādhareṇa` must retain its
Abhidharma precision: a lower `bhūmi` cognition cannot apprehend the relevant
breath, while same- or higher-level cognition can.

**Prajñā and object must be determined together.** VAK 6.12 classified the
cognition; 6.13 classifies the wind-process it knows. Neither a powerful
cognitive label nor an underdetermined object is sufficient for Path-science.

**Organon interpretation.** VAK 6.13 supplies a native schema for the Object of
Practical Meaning. This is project synthesis:

```text
object identity
    BreathProcess

position
    same bhūmi as body

relations
    bodily part
    member of sentient continuum
    separable from faculties

transition
    begins and ceases under specified state changes

operation
    homogeneous conditioned flow

access condition
    cognition of same or higher level
```

Meaning does not arise by replacing breath with a dictionary gloss or by
appropriating it as “mine.” It emerges through the system of determinations
that make this process what it is and specify how Prajñā can apprehend it. The
Path does not arbitrarily create the object; it discriminates the lawful
relations already required for adequate knowing.
