# VAK 6.28 Bhāṣya — Severance, Release, and the Limit of Seeing

## Kārikā Anchor

> kṣāntijñānāny ānantaryamuktimārgā yathākramam |
> adṛṣṭadṛṣṭer dṛṅmārgas tatra pañcadaśa kṣaṇāḥ || 6.28 ||

> Acceptances and Knowledges are, respectively, uninterrupted
> paths and liberation paths. The Path of Seeing sees what has
> not been seen; fifteen moments belong to it.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.28.md). The sixteen-moment
realization of 6.27 remains the frame. This verse distinguishes the
functions of its alternating moments and the exact extent of Seeing.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> Why must there necessarily be Acceptances and Knowledges? Because
> “Acceptances and Knowledges are, respectively, uninterrupted paths
> and liberation paths.” Acceptances are uninterrupted paths: their
> severance of possession of the afflictions cannot be obstructed.
> Knowledges are liberation paths because acquisition of
> disconnection arises together with those freed from afflictive
> possession. Thus both are necessary, like expelling a thief and
> shutting the door. What if acquisition of disconnection arose
> only with the next uninterrupted path? Then knowledge, free from
> the abandoned doubt, would not arise with that same object.
>
> If the Acceptances abandon afflictions, would this contradict the
> treatise's statement that nine groups of fetters are destroyed by
> Knowledge? No. The Acceptances belong to Knowledge's retinue;
> what the king's retinue does may be attributed to the king.
>
> Are all sixteen cognitions the Path of Seeing because they see the
> Truths? No. “The Path of Seeing sees what has not been seen;
> fifteen moments belong to it.” From the first Dharma-Knowledge-
> Acceptance concerning suffering through Anvaya-Knowledge-
> Acceptance concerning the Path, these fifteen moments are the
> Path of Seeing, because they see a Truth not previously seen. In
> the sixteenth there is no new truth-object; since it develops what
> has been seen, it is the Path of Cultivation.
>
> But does the sixteenth not see something previously unseen,
> namely the preceding Path-Anvaya-Acceptance? The criterion
> concerns the Truth, not an individual moment. One moment not
> previously seen does not make the Truth unseen, just as one
> uncut portion does not make a field unharvested. Path-Anvaya-
> Knowledge is cultivation because it is a fruit, cultivates the
> eight Knowledges and sixteen aspects, and has further relations
> to the preceding Path and its continuity. It maintains the
> abandonment of afflictions removable by Seeing. Does that make
> it the Path of Seeing? No; that inference would extend too far.
> Why then do the preceding seven Knowledge-moments count as the
> Path of Seeing? Because Seeing has not yet been completed: they
> occur within the still unfinished direct seeing of all the
> Truths. This explains how the Paths of Seeing and Cultivation
> arise.

The source's compressed sequence at [353|05–07], especially
`pūrvamārgavihāneḥ pravandhikatvāc ca`, needs collation for a
more exact translation. “Further relations to the preceding Path
and its continuity” records its argumentative role without
claiming that its damaged morphology is settled.

## Movement of the Commentary

The question “why both?” receives a functional answer. **Kṣānti**
is the uninterrupted path that severs `kleśaprāpti`, the possession
of an affliction. **Jñāna** is the liberation path arising with
`visaṃyogaprāpti`, acquisition of disconnection. The thief-and-door
image distinguishes removal from secured release. A delayed
acquisition of disconnection would omit knowledge free of doubt
on the very object whose obstruction has been removed.

The apparent contradiction with the treatise's “destroyed by
Knowledge” is resolved by attribution across an associated
operation: the Acceptance is in Knowledge's retinue, as an act of
the king's retinue can be called the king's act. That broader
attribution does not change which moment actually severs.

The second question asks why sixteen moments of realization yield
only **fifteen moments of darśanamārga**. Seeing means first seeing
of a Truth. The range ends with Path-Anvaya-Acceptance, moment
fifteen. Path-Anvaya-Knowledge, moment sixteen, develops the Truth
already disclosed and thus begins **bhāvanāmārga**. The objection
that it sees the preceding moment for the first time misses the
criterion: the Truth as a whole, rather than each newly arising
moment, is what must have remained unseen. The field analogy
clarifies this difference of scale.

The final objection points out that the sixteenth moment preserves
abandonment achieved in Seeing. Preservation alone cannot make a
moment Seeing, or the category would extend too far. The previous
seven Knowledge-moments occur while the total first-seeing
sequence is still incomplete. The last Knowledge-moment occurs
after that sequence has reached its limit.

## Organon Response — A Path With an Internal Limit

For our Science of Knowing, the pair `kṣānti → jñāna` has a precise
course: sever an actual bond, then establish knowledge with the
accomplished disconnection. The distinction between possession of
affliction and acquisition of disconnection is Vasubandhu's
technical account; the broader claim that a course is determined
by its completed End is our Organon reconstruction.

The boundary at moment fifteen is especially instructive. Once
the Truths have been first seen, continued knowing cannot simply
repeat the claim of first disclosure. It takes up the disclosed
field as cultivation. Thus an operation, by succeeding, changes
the kind of operation that can follow it. This is a proposal for
understanding a Science of Knowing, not an assertion that the
Bhāṣya has already demonstrated the Absolute.
