# VAK 6.60 — Structural Memory and the Transformation of Faculty

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

म्रियते न फलभ्रष्टस्तदकार्यं करोति न ।
विमुक्त्यानन्तर्यपथा नवाकोप्येऽतिसेवनात् ॥ ६.६० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

mriyate na phalabhraṣṭas tadakāryaṃ karoti na |
vimuktyānantaryapathā navākopye ’tisevanāt || 6.60 ||

The first half continues the Vaibhāṣika account permitting regression from Arhat-fruit. The second half concerns transformation into the unshakeable disposition. The Bhāṣya clarifies **atisevanā** as the deep habituation of the former weak-faculty disposition, explaining why nine pairs of Paths are required.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

mriyate | na | phala-bhraṣṭaḥ | tat-akāryam | karoti | na |
vimukti-ānantarya-pathāḥ | nava | akopye | ati-sevanāt

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **mriyate** | third-person singular present middle | dies |
| **na** | negative particle | not |
| **phala-bhraṣṭaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | one fallen from the fruit |
| **tat-akāryam** | neuter accusative singular compound | what ought not be done in relation to that fruit; an incompatible action |
| **karoti** | third-person singular present active | performs |
| **vimukti-ānantarya-pathāḥ** | masculine nominative plural coordinative compound | liberation-paths and uninterrupted paths |
| **nava** | numeral | nine [of each, according to the Bhāṣya] |
| **akopye** | neuter locative singular | in the transition to the unshakeable disposition |
| **ati-sevanāt** | neuter ablative singular compound | because of deep or repeated habituation |

The Bhāṣya specifies the transforming practitioner as the *prativedhanābhavya* entering **akopya-gotra**, and the entrenched disposition as **mṛdvindriya-gotra**, a weak-faculty configuration.

## 4. Grammar

The first two clauses share **phala-bhraṣṭaḥ** as subject. The negations frame two prohibited outcomes: the one fallen from the fruit neither **mriyate**, dies while fallen, nor **tadakāryaṃ karoti**, performs an action opposed to that fruit.

In the second half, **nava** distributes over both members of **vimuktyānantaryapathāḥ** under the Bhāṣya's construe: nine liberation-paths and nine uninterrupted paths. Locative **akopye** expresses the target disposition, while ablative **atisevanāt** gives the reason for the full series—the previous weak-faculty disposition has been deeply practiced and firmly established.

The line does not say merely that repetition produces immovability. It explains why entrenched prior formation requires a graded counter-transformation.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

One fallen from the fruit does not die, nor does he perform what is incompatible with it. In [the transition to] the unshakeable [disposition], there are nine liberation-paths and uninterrupted paths because of deep habituation.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Within the Vaibhāṣika account, an Arhat who has fallen from the fruit does not die while remaining fallen and performs no action contrary to that fruit; recovery necessarily intervenes. To transform a *prativedhanābhavya* into the unshakeable disposition requires nine uninterrupted paths and nine corresponding liberation-paths, because the former weak-faculty disposition has been deeply habituated and made firm throughout both training and beyond-training.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Regression interrupts the highest present possession without restoring the old field of possibilities. The prior Path remains operative in the fallen person: death cannot seal the regression, and conduct fundamentally contrary to the fruit cannot arise. The loss is real within its school framework, but it is bounded by a constitution already transformed.

That same persistence explains the labor of further transformation. A faculty-morphology repeatedly enacted throughout the Path has acquired stability; it cannot be displaced by a slight effort. Yet it is not immutable. A complete graded sequence can reorganize it into unshakeability.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Scope of the regression claim**

The first half develops the Vaibhāṣika position described in 6.58. The Sautrāntikas deny that Arhatship itself can be lost. VAK 6.60 should therefore not be reported as an uncontested assertion that every tradition accepts regression from Arhat-fruit.

Internally to the Vaibhāṣika account, the limitations are strict:

```text
fall from Arhat-fruit
    → no death while fallen
    → no fruit-opposed action
    → recovery before the life-course ends
```

**No death while fallen**

The Bhāṣya asks whether one who falls from Arhatship is born again and answers no. A sūtra citation indicates that a noble disciple's lapse of mindfulness is rapidly brought to an end. Otherwise, Vasubandhu argues, the holy life would provide no assurance (**anāśvāsika**).

```text
possible lapse
    ≠ indefinite loss

possible regression
    ≠ renewed saṃsāric destiny
```

The cited wording is textually difficult in the working prose witness, so only its argumentative force is stabilized here.

**No action contrary to the fruit**

The Bhāṣya glosses **tadakāryam** as **tatphala-viruddhā kriyā**, action opposed to the fruit. Even while fallen, the practitioner does not perform it.

Vasubandhu's simile is a hero who stumbles and falls:

```text
fall is actual
    but
the hero's formed constitution is not erased
```

The prior fruit has changed the person's practical possibilities more deeply than its present loss can undo.

**Nine uninterrupted and nine liberation paths**

The transition of faculties reproduces the paired structure familiar from abandonment:

```text
9 ānantarya-mārgas
    each directly counteracts a grade

9 vimukti-mārgas
    each possesses the corresponding achieved transformation
```

The Bhāṣya compares this sequence to attaining Arhatship itself. Becoming unshakeable is not an instantaneous change of label.

**atisevanā**

The reason nine pairs are needed is the former disposition's deep habituation:

```text
mṛdvindriya-gotra
    exercised during Śaikṣa Path
    + exercised during Aśaikṣa Path
    → made firm
    → not transformed by slight cultivation
```

Thus **atisevanā** primarily characterizes the entrenched weak-faculty configuration whose transformation demands intensive counter-cultivation.

**prativedhanābhavya to akopyadharman**

The practitioner is the fifth Arhat-type, capable of penetrating to the unshakeable condition:

```text
prativedhanābhavya
    + nine graded counteractions
    + nine achieved liberations
    → akopya-gotra
```

This confirms that *gotra* is a formed capacity-profile rather than an unalterable personal essence.

## 8. Logical Determination

The bounded character of regression is:

```text
FallsFrom(x, ArhatFruit)
    → not DiesWhileFallen(x)
    → not Performs(x, FruitOpposedAction)
    → mustRecover(x, Fruit)
```

The inference rejected is:

```text
LossOfPresentFruit
    → ReturnToPrePathConstitution
```

The transformation rule is:

```text
DeeplyHabituated(x, WeakFacultyDisposition)
    → ResistantTo(x, SlightCultivation)

NineUninterruptedPaths
    + NineLiberationPaths
    → transforms(WeakFacultyDisposition,
                  UnshakeableDisposition)
```

The same Path-history produces both effects:

```text
past mediation
    → persists as constraint through regression
    → persists as resistance to transformation

higher mediation
    → reorganizes that persistence
```

Path-memory is therefore neither indestructibility of every attained state nor passive residue. It is a formed rule of what can happen next.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.60 makes the earlier claim that *indriya* is a morphology fully concrete. Repeated Path-action forms a stable configuration of possible action. Even when the highest fruit is lost in the Vaibhāṣika scheme, the practitioner cannot act as though the Path had never occurred; and because the existing morphology is deeply established, changing it requires an equally articulated Path-process.

In the Organon reconstruction, this is structural memory. The past is not preserved as a detached representation but as the present constraint and capacity of the continuum. It governs both the boundary of regression and the resistance encountered by further development.

The source-layer distinctions remain primary: the regression claims belong to the Vaibhāṣika framework, while the ninefold sequence concerns technical *indriyasaṃcāra*. The synthesis names their common genetic form without dissolving the doctrinal controversy.
