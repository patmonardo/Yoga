# VAK 6.19 Bhāṣya — The Last Mundane Threshold

## Kārikā Anchor

> kāmāptaduḥkhaviṣayā tv adhimātrā kṣaṇaṃ ca sā |
> tathāgradharmāḥ sarve tu pañcaskandhā vināptibhiḥ || 6.19 ||

> Highest Acceptance has desire-domain suffering as its object and
> lasts one moment. The Highest Mundane Dharmas are likewise. All
> these stages, taken with their associated complex, comprise five
> aggregates, excluding acquisitions.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.19.md). This verse brings the
preparatory cognition to its most concentrated mundane form.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> “Highest” continues to qualify Acceptance. Its object is only
> suffering in the desire domain. The restriction implies that Heat
> and the earlier stages can have objects across the three domains,
> since no such restriction was stated for them. As attention to
> objects of the form and formless domains and to the several Truths
> is progressively reduced, an attention still taking desire-domain
> suffering in two moments belongs to medium Acceptance; when it
> takes that object in one moment, it is highest Acceptance. Highest
> Acceptance is momentary, not an extended series.
>
> The Highest Mundane Dharmas are like highest Acceptance in object
> and duration: they take desire-domain suffering, last one moment,
> and remain mundane. They are called highest because they are
> supreme among mundane dharmas. Without being a homogeneous cause
> of the Path, they draw it in through their own causal efficacy.
>
> Heat and the stages beginning with it are called prajñā-natured
> because they are establishments of recollection in their own
> nature. Taken with their associated complex, however, they
> comprise five aggregates. Acquisitions are excluded: otherwise
> the presence of such acquisitions in an ārya would imply the
> present manifestation of Heat and the other preparatory stages.
>
> The commentary then distinguishes, for the initial formation and
> development of Heat, Summit, Acceptance, and the Highest Mundane
> Dharmas, which establishments are cultivated now and in the future
> and how many modes accompany them. It differentiates cases that
> take three Truths from those that take cessation, and says that the
> final mundane stage has only four modes, in likeness to the Path of
> Seeing. The detailed case allocation remains provisional pending
> collation of this compressed witness.

The final paragraph summarizes rather than pretending to translate
the technical case matrix line by line. The Sanskrit witness above
retains that matrix in full for a later controlled collation.

## Movement of the Commentary

Highest Acceptance differs from its earlier grades by a narrowed
object and a one-moment duration. The Bhāṣya relates this restriction
to the broader range of the earlier stages. The Highest Mundane
Dharmas share the narrow object and momentary character, yet remain
`laukika`. Their special efficacy is causal: they draw in the Path
without serving as its `sabhāgahetu`, homogeneous cause. Thus the
threshold is determined by both connection and difference.

The commentary next distinguishes intrinsic nature from complete
associated constitution. In nature these stages are prajñā; with
their retinue they comprise the five aggregates. `Prāpti` is
excluded to prevent possession of an acquisition from being equated
with present manifestation of the acquired preparatory stage.

The final case matrix is still part of this unit. Its changing
counts of present and future establishments and modes place the
four stages in a precise cultivation taxonomy. The last restriction
to four modes at the Highest Mundane Dharmas anticipates the form of
the Path of Seeing. The witness is too compressed for an unqualified
English reconstruction of every case here; the study preserves the
text and records the secure structural result.

## Organon Response — A Prepared Threshold

The contraction to one object for one moment gives the mundane
preparation an exact limit. Its relation to the Path is neither
identity nor mere adjacency: the Highest Mundane Dharmas exercise
causal efficacy that draws in a cognition of another order. This is
a stronger determination than simply saying that practice becomes
more intense.

For our Science of Knowing, the claim to know the Absolute faces
this threshold as a test. A formed and cultivated truth-field can
prepare the event of Seeing, but the Bhāṣya does not equate that
preparation with the event. The causal analysis is Vasubandhu's;
the Absolute-knowing reading is our Organon question.
