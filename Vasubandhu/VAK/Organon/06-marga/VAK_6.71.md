# VAK 6.71 — Purity and Ground of the Bodhi-Aligned Factors

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

अनास्रवाणि बोध्यङ्गमार्गाङ्गानि द्विधेतरे ।
सकलाः प्रथमे ध्याने अनागम्ये प्रीतिवर्जिताः ॥ ६.७१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

anāsravāṇi bodhyaṅgamārgāṅgāni dvidhetare |
sakalāḥ prathame dhyāne anāgamye prītivarjitāḥ || 6.71 ||

The verse introduces two independent determinations of the same thirty-sevenfold system: purity-status and meditative ground. Neither the underlying dharma nor its functional name alone exhausts the factor’s concrete Path-status.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |

| **anāsravāṇi** | neuter nominative plural | without outflows; uncontaminated |
| **bodhyaṅgāni** | neuter nominative plural | factors of awakening |

The gender shift from neuter group-names to masculine **itare / sakalāḥ / varjitāḥ** reflects the understood plural **bodhipakṣā dharmāḥ**.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda predicates **anāsravāṇi** of the Bodhi- and Path-factors. **Dvidhā itare** is elliptical: “the others [are] twofold,” namely with outflows or without outflows.

The second line contains two locative distributions:

```text
prathame dhyāne
    all are present

anāgamye
    [all are present] except prīti
```

The omitted predicate in the second clause is recovered from **sakalāḥ**. The verse therefore asks first what purity-status the groups bear and then where the full or reduced configuration can occur.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

The factors of awakening and factors of the Path are without outflows; the others are twofold. All are present in the first dhyāna; in the preliminary concentration they are without joy.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The seven factors of awakening and eight factors of the Noble Path are technically uncontaminated because these designations are established within the noble Paths of Cultivation and Seeing, although mundane analogues of such qualities also occur. The other five groups may be either contaminated or uncontaminated. All thirty-seven functions are available in the first dhyāna; the preliminary concentration supports them except for joy, because its effortful and unsettled relation to the lower ground does not sustain prīti.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

An operative quality becomes a Path-factor only under its complete determination. Its intrinsic operation, systematic function, purity-status, and contemplative field must all coincide. A mundane right view may resemble noble right view in content, yet it does not receive the technical name of Noble Path-factor before the uncontaminated Path is actual.

The first dhyāna supplies a field capable of sustaining the complete thirty-sevenfold organization. The preliminary ground remains transitional and effortful; it can support the system only without joy.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Two questions, two axes**

The Bhāṣya asks:

```text
How many Bodhi-aligned dharmas
are sāsrava or anāsrava?

In which bhūmi
are how many factors present?
```

These give independent dimensions:

```text
purity-status
    sāsrava / anāsrava

ground-distribution
    contemplative field of occurrence
```

A ground does not automatically determine the complete doctrinal status of every factor appearing within it.

**Bodhyaṅga and Mārgāṅga are anāsrava**

The technical reason is their establishment with reference to:

```text
bhāvanāmārga
    Path of Cultivation

darśanamārga
    Path of Seeing
```

As strict canonical group-designations, the seven Bodhyaṅgas and eight Mārgāṅgas belong to noble Path-actuality and are therefore without outflows.

**Mundane analogues**

Vasubandhu immediately prevents an overstatement:

```text
mundane right view and analogous qualities
    do exist

but

they do not receive the designation
    āryamārga
```

Accordingly:

```text
functional resemblance
    ≠ strict Noble Path identity
```

The verse’s purity claim concerns the factors under their technical Bodhyaṅga and Mārgāṅga determinations, not the impossibility of wholesome mundane counterparts.

**The other five groups are twofold**

```text
4 smṛtyupasthānas
4 samyakprahāṇas
4 ṛddhipādas
5 indriyas
5 balas
```

These can function:

```text
before noble realization
    → sāsrava

within noble Path-actuality
    → anāsrava
```

The same named practice therefore changes soteriological status according to its Path-location.

**All thirty-seven in the first dhyāna**

The Bhāṣya is explicit:

```text
sarve saptatriṃśat prathame dhyāne

all thirty-seven are present
in the first dhyāna
```

The first dhyāna is thus the first meditative ground supporting the complete named configuration. This accords with 6.66, where the principal dhyānas were easy courses because the relevant factors are possessed and śamatha and vipaśyanā are balanced.

**The preliminary ground lacks prīti**

In **anāgamya**, all occur except joy. The Bhāṣya supplies two connected reasons:

```text
sāmantaka attainments
    proceed laboriously or through strong effort

the preliminary ground
    remains apprehensive with respect to the lower ground
```

Prīti is absent not by arbitrary numerical subtraction but because the preliminary field lacks the settled favorable character capable of sustaining it.

**Anāgamya as contemplative ground**

Here **anāgamya** is the preliminary concentration approaching the first dhyāna. It is not the person called an Anāgāmin, “non-returner.” The shared surface form must not collapse ground and person.

**Purity beyond underlying identity**

The ten-dharma reduction of 6.68 remains true, but is insufficient by itself:

```text
underlying dharma
    what operative capacity is present

functional designation
    what role it performs

purity-status
    whether its operation is contaminated

ground
    where that configuration is actual
```

Full Path-meaning requires all four relations.

## 8. Logical Determination

The purity rules are:

```text
Bodhyanga(x)
    → Anāsrava(x)

NobleMārgāṅga(x)
    → Anāsrava(x)

MemberOfOtherFiveGroups(x)
    → mayBe Sāsrava(x)
    or mayBe Anāsrava(x)
```

The terminology rule is:

```text
MundaneAnalogue(x, NoblePathFactor)
    does not entail
NoblePathFactor(x)
```

The ground rules are:

```text
FirstDhyāna
    supports AllThirtySevenFunctions

Anāgamya
    supports ThirtySixFunctions
    excludes Prīti
```

Thus:

```text
OperativeIdentity
    + FunctionalRole
    + PurityStatus
    + GroundOfOccurrence
    = ConcretePathDetermination
```

None of these axes can be inferred without qualification from another.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The sequence from 6.68 through 6.71 now shows why Meaning cannot be reduced to the inventory of ten dravyas:

```text
underlying capacity
    → systematic function
    → predominance within a practice
    → developmental stage
    → purity-status
    → possible contemplative ground
```

The same capacity can be present mundanely or supramundanely, but its meaning as an **āryamārgāṅga** arises only within the noble Path-relation. This is not a verbal relabeling after the fact; the status of the operation itself has changed.

The first dhyāna likewise functions as a genuine domain of possibility. It is not simply a location containing factors. Its balanced structure permits the complete Bodhi-aligned system to become actual. Anāgamya permits almost the same inventory, but its effortful and unsettled morphology excludes prīti.

In Organon terms, Dhātu/Bhūmi is therefore constitutive rather than external: it determines which practical configuration can exist. That reconstruction remains distinct from the Bhāṣya’s explicit claim, which is the exact distribution of purity-status and factors by ground.
