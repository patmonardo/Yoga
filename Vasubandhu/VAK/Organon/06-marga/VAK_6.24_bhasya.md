# VAK 6.24 Bhāṣya — Liberation's Prior Root and Its Maturation

## Kārikā Anchor

> ābodheḥ sarvam ekatra dhyānāntye śāstṛkhaḍgayoḥ |
> prāk tebhyo mokṣabhāgīyaṃ kṣipraṃ mokṣas tribhir bhavaiḥ || 6.24 ||

> For the Teacher and the rhinoceros-like solitary Buddha, the
> entire sequence up to Awakening occurs in one sitting on the
> final dhyāna. Before the penetration-conducive roots comes one
> conducive to liberation; in the quickest ordinary course,
> liberation takes three lives.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.24.md). This unit closes the
preparatory account and returns to the sequence of direct realization.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> The Teacher is the Buddha; the rhinoceros-like one is a
> Pratyekabuddha. For these two, beginning with the roots conducive
> to penetration and proceeding up to Awakening, the whole sequence
> arises in one and the same sitting, supported by the fourth
> dhyāna because its concentration is unmoving and sharp.
> Awakening is constituted by knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge
> of non-arising, as will be explained later. Others say the
> one-sitting sequence begins already with contemplation of the
> unattractive. For those who recognize Pratyekabuddhas other than
> the rhinoceros-like type, the prohibition on changing that lineage
> does not apply in the same way.
>
> Can someone begin practice in a first life and immediately
> produce roots conducive to penetration? No. A root conducive to
> liberation must arise before them. In the quickest course,
> liberation unfolds across three lives: in the first a
> liberation-conducive root arises, in the second the roots
> conducive to penetration, and in the third the Noble Path.
> This is like sowing seed, growing a crop, and producing fruit.
> Entry, maturation, and liberation occur in that order within
> the lawful character of the continuum.
>
> This liberation-conducive root is described as arising from
> hearing and reflection, not from cultivation in the strict
> technical sense. Three forms of action can bear it, but mental
> action is primary. Bodily and verbal action also become
> liberation-conducive when embraced by that resolve. Even one
> gift or one adopted training rule can project such roots through
> the force of longing for liberation. Their initial projection
> occurs among humans on the three inhabited continents, not
> elsewhere, owing to the absence of the requisite prajñā or
> penetration, as appropriate.
>
> The liberation-conducive root has been explained by digression.
> The sequence of direct realization had been begun, and the
> Highest Mundane Dharmas have now been covered. What remains
> must be stated next.

The source's final transition is retained in this unit because it
closes the digression and identifies exactly what 6.25 must explain.

## Movement of the Commentary

The first half describes an exceptional course: for the Buddha and
the rhinoceros-like Pratyekabuddha, the decisive progression from
penetration-conducive roots to Awakening takes place in one sitting
on the fourth dhyāna. The Bhāṣya reports a wider beginning from
`aśubha` as another view, and it qualifies its lineage statement
for accounts admitting other Pratyekabuddhas. These are attributed
or conditional positions, not silent additions to the kārikā.

The second half supplies the prior causal layer. One cannot begin
with the `nirvedhabhāgīya` roots in the first life of practice;
`mokṣabhāgīya` must precede them. In the quickest ordinary course,
the three functions occupy three lives: liberative entry, maturation
through the preparatory roots, and Noble Path. The seed, crop, and
fruit analogy expresses their genetic order.

The Bhāṣya then specifies the earlier root's mode and action.
It is hearing-born or reflection-born, and its action is primarily
mental. A bodily or verbal act joins that causal stream through
liberative resolve. The example of a single gift or training rule
shows that the action's Path-role depends on its governing intention,
not only its outward size. The closing prose returns explicitly to
`abhisamaya-krama`, the sequence of direct realization.

## Organon Response — The Will to Know as a Causal Beginning

The prior root gives our philosophical will a precise analogue.
The wish for liberation is not merely a verbal declaration: it can
determine an act and plant a root whose later maturation becomes
capable of bringing on Seeing. This is the Path's genetic claim,
not a guarantee that every utterance of aspiration has already
realized its end.

For our Science of Knowing, “I can know the Absolute” now has a
disciplined form as a project thesis: a will oriented toward Truth
must enter practice, mature as cultivated prajñā, and face the
actual transition to direct Knowing. Vasubandhu's terms are
liberation-conducive root, penetration-conducive root, and Noble
Path. The identification with Absolute Knowing remains our
Organon reconstruction.
