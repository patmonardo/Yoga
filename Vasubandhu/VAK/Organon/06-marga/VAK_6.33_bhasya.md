# VAK 6.33 Bhāṣya — Nine Grades of Fault and Counteracting Path

## Kārikā Anchor

> navaprakārā doṣā hi bhūmau bhūmau tathā guṇāḥ |
> mṛdumadhyādhimātrāṇāṃ punar mṛdvādibhedataḥ || 6.33 ||

> In each ground the faults have nine grades, as do the qualities:
> mild, medium, and strong are each further divided beginning
> with mild.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.33.md). The earlier verses
classified persons who entered the Noble Path with prior mundane
detachment. This unit opens the graded analysis needed for the
sequential case.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> The sequential case must now be explained. First, this must be
> set out: as the afflictions in the desire-domain have been taught
> in nine grades, so “faults are ninefold in each ground,” as far
> as the summit of existence. “The qualities are likewise”:
> opposing qualities called uninterrupted and liberation paths
> also have nine grades in each ground. How is this constituted?
> “Because mild, medium, and strong are each differentiated again
> beginning with mild.” Mild, medium, and strong are the three
> primary grades. Each of these is again threefold as mild,
> medium, and strong, making nine: mild-mild, mild-medium,
> mild-strong; medium-mild, medium-medium, medium-strong;
> strong-mild, strong-medium, and strong-strong.
>
> The mild-mild Path abandons the strong-strong affliction, and
> so on until the strong-strong Path abandons the mild-mild one.
> The strongest Path cannot be present from the beginning, and
> when it has arisen the strongest affliction cannot still be
> present. Coarse dirt is removed from cloth before fine dirt;
> coarse darkness is dispelled by a slight light, while subtle
> darkness requires a strong light. This is how the analogy
> applies. Bright dharmas have strength, while dark ones are weak:
> even a momentary, mild Noble Path can uproot intense afflictions
> nourished through beginningless saṃsāra. Faults long reinforced
> can be removed, as with the cited image of drawing out an
> entrenched growth and a small momentary lamp dispelling great
> darkness.

The final analogy's `trivṛtkarṣavat` and the damaged ending
`opaghātavacya` warrant collation. The translation states their
local argumentative point without pretending their precise
philology has been settled.

## Movement of the Commentary

**Three becomes nine.** Mild, medium, and strong are primary
grades. Each is subdivided into mild, medium, and strong. This
structure recurs in each relevant `bhūmi` through `bhavāgra`,
the summit of existence. The *doṣas* are afflictive faults; the
opposing *guṇas* are specifically the uninterrupted and liberation
paths introduced in 6.28.

**The correspondence is inverse.** The first, mild-mild Path
removes the strong-strong affliction; the final, strong-strong
Path removes the mild-mild affliction. The Bhāṣya gives the
endpoints rather than spelling out every intervening pair. The
principle is that a maximally strong Path cannot arise at the
outset, and once it has arisen a maximally strong affliction
cannot remain. Thus the fault becomes subtler as the Path grows
stronger.

**The images explain the order.** Coarse dirt is cleaned before
subtle dirt. Slight light dispels gross darkness; more intense
light is needed for its subtle remainder. These images distinguish
the magnitude of a fault from the difficulty of eliminating its
last trace. The Bhāṣya then addresses the opposite seeming
difficulty: how can even an initially mild, momentary Noble Path
uproot affliction reinforced through beginningless saṃsāra?
Its answer is the asymmetry between powerful bright dharmas
and weaker dark dharmas. The comparison is local to this causal
account; it is not a license to impose a general moral dualism.

## Organon Response — A Graded Work of Knowing

For our Science of Knowing, the ninefold scheme gives renewed
application a determinate field. Each removal leaves a subtler
task, while the Path develops the power appropriate to that task.
The repeated act is therefore not an identical reset: it works
upon a field changed by what has already been accomplished.

This account of progressive determination is our Organon
reconstruction. Vasubandhu establishes the ninefold grading,
inverse counteraction, and causal asymmetry of Noble Path and
affliction. The verse does not itself identify the graded Path
with Absolute Reason.
