# VAK 6.43 — The Bodily Witness and Temporally Articulated Realization

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

तत्पाञ्चविध्यात् पञ्चैव शुद्धावासोपपत्तयः ।
निरोधलाभ्यनागामी कायसाक्षी पुनर्मतः ॥ ६.४३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

tad-pāñcavidhyāt pañcaiva śuddhāvāsopapattayaḥ |
nirodha-lābhy anāgāmī kāya-sākṣī punar mataḥ || 6.43 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

nirodha-lābhī | anāgāmī | kāya-sākṣī | punaḥ | mataḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **tat-pañca-vidhyāt** | neuter ablative singular compound | because of its fivefold differentiation |
| **pañca eva** | numeral plus emphatic particle | exactly five |
| **śuddhāvāsa-upapattayaḥ** | feminine nominative plural compound | rebirths in the Pure Abodes |
| **nirodha-lābhī** | masculine nominative singular compound | one who possesses attainment of cessation |
| **anāgāmī** | masculine nominative singular | non-returner |
| **kāya-sākṣī** | masculine nominative singular compound | bodily witness |
| **punaḥ** | indeclinable | further, in turn |
| **mataḥ** | masculine nominative singular past participle | is regarded or held to be |

The first **tat** refers to the mixed cultivation of the preceding verse. The second line gives a definition by apposition: the cessation-attaining non-returner is called a bodily witness.

## 4. Grammar

The ablative **tat-pāñcavidhyāt** gives the causal ground for **pañca eva śuddhāvāsopapattayaḥ**: exactly five Pure-Abode rebirths correspond to the fivefold differentiation of that cultivation.

In the second line, **nirodhalābhī** qualifies **anāgāmī**. **kāyasākṣī** is the new predicate and **mataḥ** marks the accepted designation. The grammar states possession of cessation and the resulting title; it does not state that cognition occurs during cessation.

The five grades, their moment counts, the causal role of the contaminated component, and the explanation of bodily witnessing belong to the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Because of its fivefold differentiation, there are exactly five rebirths in the Pure Abodes. A non-returner who possesses attainment of cessation is further regarded as a bodily witness.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The five progressively developed grades of mixed cultivation yield, in corresponding order, rebirth in the five Pure Abodes; the contaminated component of each cultivation supplies the rebirth-producing cause. A non-returner who has attained the cessation attainment is called a bodily witness because, upon emerging from noncognitive cessation, the practitioner experiences unprecedented bodily tranquility and recognizes cessation as peaceful and resembling nirvāṇa.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

One cultivated form can contain distinguishable causal functions. Its uncontaminated side belongs to liberation, while its contaminated component projects the corresponding Pure-Abode existence. Their intermingling does not erase their different operations.

Likewise, direct realization need not be one homogeneous conscious instant. Cessation is attained without cognition; its transformation is borne by the bodily continuum; cognition returns upon emergence and recognizes the unprecedented peace. Attainment and knowledge together make cessation directly present.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Fivefold mixed cultivation**

The Bhāṣya grades mixed cultivation in five progressively developed forms. It gives the following cognitive counts:

| Grade | Number of cittas |
|---|---:|
| first | 3 |
| second | 6 |
| third | 9 |
| fourth | 12 |
| fifth | 15 |

Each grade preserves the same contaminated–uncontaminated architecture while extending its cultivated scope. The numbers belong to the reported technical analysis; they should be read with VAK 6.42's caution concerning exact moment-level control.

**Five Pure Abodes**

The five cultivation grades correspond in order to the five Pure-Abode rebirths:

```text
MixedCultivationGrade[n]
    → PureAbodeRebirth[n]
```

The Bhāṣya says specifically that the **sāsrava**, contaminated, component causes rebirth there. The uncontaminated component does not produce rebirth merely because it participates in the same mixed practice.

A reported alternative derives the five Pure Abodes from predominance of the five faculties beginning with faith. The Bhāṣya records but does not develop that view here.

**nirodha-lābhin**

A **nirodhalābhin** possesses attainment of **nirodha-samāpatti**, the attainment of cessation. In the present classification this person is already a non-returner.

**kāya-sākṣin**

Any non-returner who has attained cessation is called a **bodily witness**. The initial explanation says that a nirvāṇa-like dharma is directly realized through the body because cognition is absent and the occurrence depends upon the bodily support.

Vasubandhu then refines this explanation. The witnessing is not a concealed cognitive perception during cessation:

```text
entry into cessation attainment
    → cognition absent
    → cessation supported by bodily continuum
    → emergence
    → unprecedented bodily tranquility with cognition
    → recognition: cessation is peaceful and nirvāṇa-like
    → bodily witnessing established
```

The body bears the attained transformation; cognition recognizes its peace upon emergence.

**sākṣātkriyā**

The Bhāṣya defines direct realization or making-present through two factors:

```text
prāpti
    attainment

jñāna
    knowledge

prāpti + jñāna
    → pratyakṣīkāra / sākṣātkriyā
```

These factors need not occur as one simultaneous conscious event. In this case, attainment belongs to noncognitive cessation and knowledge to the emergence from it.

**Not an additional trainee class**

The bodily witness is not separately counted among the eighteen trainees. Those basic classifications rest upon the three uncontaminated trainings and their fruits, whereas cessation attainment is neither specifically a trainee nor non-trainee fruit. **Kāyasākṣin** is therefore a cross-cutting qualification of a non-returner, not another Noble fruit-position.

**Coarse and fine classification**

Vasubandhu closes by calling the foregoing non-returner distinctions coarse. Fine differentiation yields thousands of variants through faculty grade, ground, lineage-type, station, and degree of detachment. The named classes are stable projections over a much richer causal field, not exhaustive personal essences.

## 8. Logical Determination

The first half specifies an ordered causal mapping:

```text
for n in {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}:
    MixedCultivationGrade[n]
        has ContaminatedComponent[n]
        → PureAbodeRebirth[n]
```

The second half defines a cross-cutting qualification:

```text
NonReturner
    + AttainmentOfCessation
    → BodilyWitness
```

The Bhāṣya gives a temporally distributed realization:

```text
t1: Attainment without cognition
t2: Bodily continuum transformed
t3: Emergence with cognition
t4: Knowledge recognizes attained tranquility

Attainment(t1–t2) + Knowledge(t3–t4)
    → DirectRealization
```

This blocks two invalid inferences:

```text
DirectRealization
    ⇏ cognition must occur throughout the attainment

AdditionalQualification
    ⇏ additional basic fruit-class
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, direct presence can be the unity of a real transformation and its subsequent recognition. Reflection does not manufacture the truth of the attainment; it recognizes a transformation already borne by the continuum.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.43 supplies a rigorous model for keeping Seeing primary without denying knowledge. The bodily witness does not turn cessation into an object while cessation occurs. The event is first actually undergone; only upon emergence does cognition articulate what the transformed body directly attests.

This is more exact than opposing perception and reflection absolutely. The error is not subsequent cognition but assigning it the constitutive work of the attainment. Here the order is irreversible:

```text
attainment transforms
    → bodily peace is given
    → knowledge recognizes
```

The fivefold correspondence and bodily-witness designation belong to the kārikā. The cognitive counts, contaminated rebirth-cause, alternative faculty account, noncognitive cessation, retrospective explanation, definition of direct realization, and classificatory qualification belong to the Bhāṣya. The formulation of reflection as recognition rather than constitution of attained truth is Organon synthesis.
