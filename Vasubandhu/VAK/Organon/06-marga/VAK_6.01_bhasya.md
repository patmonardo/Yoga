# VAK 6.01 Bhāṣya — The Power of Seeing

## Kārikā Anchor

> kleśa-prahāṇam ākhyātaṃ satya-darśana-bhāvanāt |
> dvividho bhāvanā-mārgo darśanākhyas tv anāsravaḥ || 6.01 ||

> The abandonment of afflictions has been explained through seeing the truths
> and through cultivation. The Path of Cultivation is twofold, whereas the Path
> called Seeing is uncontaminated.

The [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.01.md) treats the verse's grammar and
translation. The Bhāṣya begins by recalling the *parijñā* discussion that
closes Chapter 5.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> It has been explained how an abandonment receives the designation
> *parijñā*. The abandonment of afflictions has also been explained as
> occurring through seeing the truths and through cultivation. It has been
> explained in detail that afflictions are to be abandoned either through
> Seeing or through Cultivation. Now it must be stated whether these two
> paths, Seeing and Cultivation, are uncontaminated or contaminated.
> Accordingly it is said: “The Path of Cultivation is twofold, whereas the
> Path called Seeing is uncontaminated.” The Path of Cultivation is of two
> kinds, mundane and supramundane. The Path of Seeing, however, is solely
> supramundane, because it is the counteragent to the three domains and
> because it abandons at once the nine kinds of afflictions to be abandoned
> through Seeing. A mundane path does not possess this power.

## Movement of the Commentary

Vasubandhu opens Mārga from the result just reached in Anuśaya. Chapter 5
established when an abandonment receives the name *parijñā*; its final
discussion concerns the acquisition, relinquishment, and consolidation of
those completed abandonments. The question now turns to the paths capable of
abandoning afflictions. The transition is explicit in `uktaṃ yathā prahāṇaṃ
parijñākhyāṃ labhate` and should not be made to depend on a later Organon
analogy.

The question `kim anāsravau sāsravau` initially places the two paths under
one inquiry. Vasubandhu answers asymmetrically. Cultivation may be mundane
or supramundane; Seeing is solely supramundane. He gives two reasons for the
latter: Seeing counters the afflictive field of all three domains, and it
abandons the nine kinds of Seeing-abandonable affliction at once. The final
sentence states the limit of mundane efficacy: `na hi laukikasya eṣā śaktir
asti`.

## Doctrinal Determination

`Parijñā` here names the designation of a completed abandonment. Vasubandhu
does not use it here as a general synonym for cognition. Its retrospective
placement makes accomplished abandonment the point from which the inquiry
into Mārga begins.

`Bhāvanā-mārga` has two forms, `laukika` and `lokottara`. Consequently,
cultivation by itself does not establish that a path is uncontaminated. The
Path of Seeing is `lokottara` because of its specified counteractive range
and its power of immediate abandonment. The Bhāṣya thus explains the
kārikā's `anāsravaḥ` by what this path can do. `Śakti` names that effective
capacity; the passage does not describe a merely elevated experience.

The nine kinds are invoked as an already intelligible class of afflictions
to be abandoned by Seeing. This short unit does not enumerate them or yet
define the four truths. The next Bhāṣya unit asks what the truths are.

## Organon Response — Path and Result

The completed *parijñās* make the question of Path unavoidable: what power
could bring about an abandonment of this kind? Conversely, the Path is
determined by the scope and manner of its result. This is our reciprocal
reading of the Chapter 5–6 transition; Vasubandhu's explicit claim is the
more exact distinction between the two paths and their powers.

For the developing Yoga–Kośa study, this passage offers a test for what we
call a Path of Discipline: the name of a practice alone does not establish
its result. We have to ask which affliction it counters, over what range,
and with what efficacy. The comparison with Saṃyama belongs to that later
inquiry, not to the translation of 6.01.
