# VAK 6.30 Bhāṣya — Prior Abandonment and Fruit Candidacy

## Kārikā Anchor

> yāvat pañcaprakāraghnau dvitīye 'rvāṅ navakṣayāt |
> kāmād viraktāv ūrdhvaṃ vā tṛtīyapratipannakau || 6.30 ||

> Having destroyed up to five grades, the two [still approach the
> first fruit]; short of destruction of all nine, [they approach]
> the second. Detached from the desire-domain, or even from a
> higher domain, they proceed toward the third.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.30.md). The two persons are
the Faith-Follower and Dharma-Follower of 6.29, both within the
fifteen moments of the Path of Seeing.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> “The two who have destroyed up to five grades”: if they have
> previously abandoned, by a mundane path, as many as five grades
> of the desire-domain afflictions abandonable through cultivation,
> they are still called persons proceeding toward the first fruit.
>
> “Toward the second, short of destruction of all nine”: “toward
> the second” means toward the second fruit. If the two have
> previously abandoned six, seven, or eight grades, they are called
> persons proceeding toward the second fruit. Which is the second?
> The fruit of once-return.
>
> “Detached from the desire-domain, or higher, the two proceed
> toward the third”: if, through abandonment of the ninth grade
> too, they are detached from the desire-domain—or if their prior
> detachment extends further, as far as the Sphere of Nothingness—
> they are called persons proceeding toward the third fruit. Which
> is the third? The fruit of non-return.

The first-fruit range is supplied by the immediately preceding
verse and the Bhāṣya's explicit `tathaiva prathamaphala-`.
The source therefore controls the compressed kārikā's ellipsis:
the “up to five” range belongs to the first fruit, not the second.

## Movement of the Commentary

Vasubandhu varies one condition: how many desire-domain
`bhāvanā-heya` grades had already been abandoned through a mundane
path before Noble Seeing. He makes the outcome numerical and exact:

| Previously abandoned grades | Status during Seeing | Fruit approached |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | first-fruit candidate | stream-entry |
| 6–8 | second-fruit candidate | once-return |
| All 9 | third-fruit candidate | non-return |
| All 9, with detachment extending higher | third-fruit candidate | non-return |

The zero-grade case was established in 6.29. This verse extends
the first range through five. Six through eight fall short of
`navakṣaya`, destruction of all nine, and lead toward once-return.
Removal of the ninth makes the person `kāmadhātor vītarāga`,
detached from the desire-domain, and changes the immediate target
to non-return. Even if prior mundane detachment extends higher,
up to the Sphere of Nothingness, the target within Seeing remains
the third fruit. Why the fourth fruit is unavailable at this point
is explained in the following unit, not by this verse alone.

The count concerns prior **cultivation-removable** afflictions. It
must not be confused with the seeing-removable afflictions addressed
by the current fifteen-moment uncontaminated sequence. The Path of
Seeing is common to these persons, yet their inherited abandonment
states determine the fruit toward which each is proceeding.

## Organon Response — A Common Path With Conditioned Results

For our Science of Knowing, this is an exact example of one Path
operating in differently prepared continua. The operative sequence
does not change; the result-position takes account of what was
already accomplished. This is a project account of the relation
between a universal course and a determinate beginning-state.

The Bhāṣya itself establishes the grade thresholds and fruit
classifications. Calling that mapping a Beginning–End structure is
our Organon reconstruction, not a claim that Vasubandhu here
defines the Absolute or endorses our wider Kosa architecture.
