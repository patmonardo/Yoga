# VAK 6.27 — One Realization as Seeing, Object-Support, and Function

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

इति षोडशचित्तोऽयं सत्याभिसमयस्त्रिधा ।
दर्शनालम्बकार्याख्यः सोऽग्रधर्मैकभूमिकः ॥ ६.२७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

iti ṣoḍaśacitto ’yaṃ satyābhisamayas tridhā |
darśanālambakāryākhyaḥ so ’gradharmaikabhūmikaḥ || 6.27 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

| **agra-dharma-eka-bhūmikaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | belonging to the same meditative ground as the Highest Mundane Dharmas |

The compound **darśanālambakāryākhyaḥ** must be distributed into three technical designations supplied by the Bhāṣya: **darśanābhisamaya**, **ālambanābhisamaya**, and **kāryābhisamaya**.

## 4. Grammar

The opening **iti** gathers the strictly successive cognitions just enumerated. **ayam satyābhisamayaḥ** is the central nominal statement: “this is the direct realization of the Truths.” **ṣoḍaśacittaḥ** predicates internal articulation of that singular realization—it consists of sixteen cognitive moments.

The singular **satyābhisamayaḥ** and **ṣoḍaśacittaḥ** hold unity and multiplicity together grammatically:

```text
one realization
    consisting of
sixteen successive cognitions
```

**tridhā** does not divide the sixteen moments into three temporal groups. It introduces three senses in which realization is named. The coordinate compound **darśana-ālambana-kārya-ākhyaḥ** supplies those names according to seeing, shared object-support, and accomplished work.

The final predicate **agradharmaikabhūmikaḥ** says that the realization has one and the same **bhūmi** as the Highest Mundane Dharmas. The supporting concentration remains continuous across the change from mundane preparation to uncontaminated realization.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

Thus, this direct realization of the Truths consists of sixteen cognitive moments. It is threefold, called [realization as] seeing, object-support, and function. It belongs to the same ground as the Highest Mundane Dharmas.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The sixteen successive uncontaminated cognitions constitute one direct realization of the Four Truths. This realization is spoken of in three senses: as the seeing performed by uncontaminated Prajñā, as the shared object-relation of its associated mental factors, and as the work accomplished throughout the wider path-complex. All sixteen moments arise on the same meditative ground as the immediately preceding Highest Mundane Dharmas.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Direct realization is one movement internally articulated into sixteen moments. Its unity does not abolish the distinct Truths or the successive forms under which they are seen. Rather, each determinate seeing participates in one system of object-relation and accomplished transformation.

The Path-event therefore exceeds a private intuition. Prajñā sees; associated factors participate by taking the same Truth as support; and the realization accomplishes work throughout the practitioner's continuum. One movement is thus determined through its cognitive operation, objective field, and effective End.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**satyābhisamaya**

**Satyābhisamaya** is direct realization of the Truths. The kārikā gathers the sixteen moments of 6.25–6.26 into this singular designation. The Bhāṣya emphasizes **anena krameṇa**, “by this sequence”: their order is constitutive of the realization, not an incidental list imposed afterward.

**ṣoḍaśacitta**

The compound means “consisting of sixteen cognitive moments,” not sixteen enduring minds or agents. Each of the Four Truths is realized through:

```text
Dharma-Knowledge-Acceptance
Dharma-Knowledge
Anvaya-Knowledge-Acceptance
Anvaya-Knowledge
```

The result is one realization with sixteen internally ordered moments.

**darśanābhisamaya**

The Bhāṣya defines realization as seeing through **uncontaminated Prajñā**. Prajñā is the factor that strictly sees and discriminates the Truth:

```text
anāsrava-prajñā
    → sees a determinate Truth
    → darśanābhisamaya
```

This is the narrow cognitive sense of realization. It should not be extended indiscriminately to every factor associated with the moment.

**ālambanābhisamaya**

Realization through object-support includes the mental factors associated with Prajñā, such as feeling. They do not all perform Prajñā's seeing-function, but they share the same Truth as **ālambana**.

Thus one cognitive event is internally complex:

```text
Prajñā                → seeing-function
associated caittas    → shared object-support
whole associated event → ālambanābhisamaya
```

**kāryābhisamaya**

Functional realization includes even dissociated factors, such as discipline and status-related factors, insofar as the Path accomplishes work in relation to them. Something need not itself see the Truth or take it as a mental object to belong to realization in this functional sense.

The classical tasks of the Truths make this efficacy explicit:

```text
duḥkha    → fully known
samudaya  → abandoned
nirodha   → directly realized
mārga     → cultivated
```

When suffering is directly seen, the Bhāṣya says realization is already accomplishing work concerning the remaining Truths through abandonment, direct realization, and cultivation.

**One realization and sequential seeing**

The Bhāṣya considers other schools' teaching of one realization. Vasubandhu does not reject every meaning of unity. One realization is defensible at the level of integrated function or acquired mastery concerning the Truths.

He rejects, however, one simultaneous **darśanābhisamaya** of all four Truths. Their cognitive modes differ (*ākārabheda*), and scripture presents suffering, origin, cessation, and Path as specifically and successively realized.

Nor can the differences be dissolved by saying all four are seen simply as not-self. A universal characteristic cannot replace the Truth-specific determination demanded by each object.

The balanced result is:

```text
strict seeing      → sequential and Truth-specific
object-participation → shared within each cognitive complex
functional efficacy → integrated across the Truth-system
```

**yathādarśanaṃ bhāvanā**

Against the suggestion that Truth-specific differences arise only later in the Path of Cultivation, the Bhāṣya states: cultivation proceeds **according to Seeing**. Seeing supplies the determinate morphology that cultivation subsequently develops and stabilizes.

This is a genetic relation within Mārga:

```text
darśana determines the form
    → bhāvanā enacts and stabilizes that form
```

**agra-dharma-eka-bhūmika**

The sixteen moments occur on whichever concentration-ground supported the preceding Highest Mundane Dharmas. The ground remains the same while the status and efficacy of cognition change from mundane to uncontaminated.

## 8. Logical Determination

The verse first unifies ordered multiplicity:

```text
SixteenSuccessiveMoments
    = one SatyaAbhisamaya
```

It then differentiates that unity through three ranges:

| Sense of realization | Proper range |
|---|---|
| **darśana** | uncontaminated Prajñā that sees |
| **ālambana** | associated factors sharing the Truth-object |
| **kārya** | broader factors and effects transformed by its work |

These form widening circles, not three unrelated realizations:

```text
seeing-operation
    ⊂ object-sharing cognitive complex
        ⊂ functionally transformed Path-complex
```

Unity is therefore functional and systemic, while strict seeing preserves sequence and difference:

```text
one Path-operation
    + four irreducibly distinct Truths
    + sixteen successive cognitions
    + coordinated work across the whole system
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, the Beginning–End principle becomes concrete here:

```text
ālambana — determinate field from which the operation begins
darśana  — Indriyic morphology of uncontaminated seeing
kārya    — End present as the work to be accomplished
```

This is not the order in which the kārikā lists the three terms, nor an equation Vasubandhu states. It is the Organon's reconstruction of their reciprocal unity. The object-field determines what seeing can do; seeing makes the field practically explicit; the required work gives both their Path-meaning.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.26 displayed direct realization as a rigorous succession. VAK 6.27 now prevents two opposed reductions. The Path is not sixteen disconnected flashes, but neither is it one featureless intuition. It is a single functionally integrated realization whose seeing remains sequentially differentiated.

This directly advances the objective genesis of the Path. Every moment possesses a determinate support, an operative cognitive morphology, and an accomplished or incipient function. The End is not added after seeing; the task belonging to each Truth already governs what counts as its adequate realization.

The relation to the earlier Kośa architecture can therefore be stated carefully as project synthesis:

```text
Dhātu   → presupposed domain of movement
Indriya → a priori morphology of possible efficacy
Karma   → objective course of action as proto-Dharma
Mārga   → that structure explicit as Truth-governed liberative operation
```

In Mārga, this root principle becomes conscious in its two sides. The determinate field is the Beginning from which action proceeds; the task to be accomplished is the End governing the course from within. Darśana is their living mediation.

The kārikā states one sixteen-moment realization, its three names, and continuity of meditative ground. The exact scopes of the three realizations, the debate over single realization, the rejection of simultaneous seeing, the four Truth-tasks, and cultivation according to Seeing are Bhāṣya determinations. The Beginning–End and Dhātu–Indriya–Karma–Mārga articulation is explicitly Organon synthesis.
