# VAK 6.23 — Successive Irreversibilities and the Direction of Lineage

## 1. Sanskrit Devanāgarī

मूर्धलाभी न मूलच्छित् क्षान्तिलाभ्यनपायगः ।
शिष्यगोत्रान्निवर्त्य द्वे बुद्धः स्यात् त्रीण्यपीतरः ॥ ६.२३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit IAST

mūrdhalābhī na mūlacchit kṣāntilābhy anapāyagaḥ |
śiṣyagotrān nivartya dve buddhaḥ syāt trīṇy apītaraḥ || 6.23 ||

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

**Padaccheda**

mūrdha-lābhī | na | mūla-cchit | kṣānti-lābhī | anapāya-gaḥ |
śiṣya-gotrāt | nivartya | dve | buddhaḥ | syāt | trīṇi | api | itaraḥ

| Form | Analysis | Contextual force |
|---|---|---|
| **mūrdha-lābhī** | masculine nominative singular possessive formation | one who has attained the Summits |
| **na mūla-cchit** | negative plus masculine nominative agent noun | not one who severs the roots; does not cut off the wholesome roots |
| **kṣānti-lābhī** | masculine nominative singular possessive formation | one who has attained Acceptance |
| **anapāya-gaḥ** | masculine nominative singular compound | one who does not go to a painful destiny |
| **śiṣya-gotrāt** | neuter ablative singular compound | from the lineage of a disciple, the Śrāvaka lineage |
| **nivartya** | absolutive from *ni-√vṛt* | having turned away or redirected oneself |
| **dve** | neuter accusative dual | the first two preparations: Heat and Summits |
| **buddhaḥ syāt** | nominative plus third-person optative of √as | one may become a Buddha |
| **trīṇi api** | neuter accusative plural | even the first three preparations: Heat, Summits, and Acceptance |
| **itaraḥ** | masculine nominative singular | the other [Buddha], interpreted as a Pratyekabuddha |

The transmitted sandhi **śiṣyagotrān nivartya** is resolved as **śiṣyagotrāt nivartya**: “having turned away from the disciple-lineage.” The Bhāṣya determines **buddhaḥ** as complete Buddhahood and **itaraḥ** as Pratyekabuddhahood.

## 4. Grammar

The first pāda contains two parallel nominal statements. **mūrdhalābhī** is the subject of **na mūlacchit**: “one who has attained Summit does not sever the roots.” **kṣāntilābhī** is the subject of **anapāyagaḥ**: “one who has attained Acceptance does not go to a painful destiny.” No future verb is expressed, but the compounds state enduring consequences of attainment.

The second line is highly compressed. **śiṣyagotrāt nivartya** provides the shared condition of redirection from the Śrāvaka lineage. **dve** and **trīṇi** count how many preparatory stages have already arisen. The optative **syāt** expresses the remaining possibility of becoming.

The two constructions are distributed through the Bhāṣya:

```text
after two, one may redirect toward complete Buddhahood;
after even three, the other may redirect toward Pratyekabuddhahood.
```

“Two” means Heat and Summits. “Three” adds Acceptance. The verse therefore states different limits for two possible redirections rather than one undifferentiated freedom of lineage-choice.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

One who has attained the Summits does not sever the roots; one who has attained Acceptance does not go to a painful destiny. Having turned away from the disciple-lineage after two, one may become a Buddha; after even three, the other [Buddha].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

Even if later regressing, one who has attained the Summits will no longer sever the wholesome roots. Even if Acceptance is later lost, one who has attained it will no longer enter a painful destiny. A practitioner whose first two preparations arose within the Śrāvaka lineage may still redirect the course toward complete Buddhahood; after the first three, redirection toward Pratyekabuddhahood remains possible.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

Each higher preparation changes the practitioner's field of possible futures. Summit does not yet prevent grave wrongdoing or painful rebirth, but it permanently excludes the complete severance of wholesome roots. Acceptance goes further: it renders painful destinies incapable of becoming the practitioner's future course.

The same logic enters spiritual lineage. The preparatory forms may initially be shared across different trajectories of awakening, yet their cultivation progressively fixes the direction in which the continuum can develop. Path advancement is therefore a contraction of incompatible possibilities together with a determination of the form of awakening still attainable.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**mūlaccheda**

**Mūlaccheda** is severance of the wholesome roots—non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion. According to the Bhāṣya, even if a Summit-attainer later regresses from that attainment, complete severance of the wholesome roots will no longer occur.

This protection is limited. The practitioner may still perform grave unwholesome actions and may still enter a painful destiny. Summit removes one destructive possibility, not every possibility of decline.

**anapāyaga**

An **apāya** is a painful destiny. One who has attained Acceptance becomes **anapāyaga**, no longer one who goes there. The Bhāṣya says this remains true even if Acceptance is subsequently lost, because the relevant karma and afflictions have been driven far away.

Acceptance thus produces a stronger security than Summit:

```text
Summit     → severance of wholesome roots becomes impossible
Acceptance → rebirth in painful destinies becomes impossible
```

**anutpattidharmatā**

The Bhāṣya explains Acceptance through **anutpattidharmatā**, the condition in which specified future states have become incapable of arising. Its graded account includes painful destinies and, at stronger Acceptance, further modes of birth, support, rebirth, and the afflictions abandoned by the Path of Seeing.

The full list belongs to the Bhāṣya. Its governing point is that cultivated cognition changes causal possibility itself:

```text
Acceptance attained
    → specified causal conditions are displaced
    → corresponding futures can no longer arise
```

**gotra**

**Gotra** is spiritual lineage, disposition, or trajectory. The Bhāṣya states that the factors conducive to penetration occur in three lineages:

- Śrāvaka, the disciple trajectory;
- Pratyekabuddha;
- Buddha or Bodhisattva trajectory.

The common formal preparation does not imply an identical telos. Gotra determines the awakening-course within which the preparation functions.

**Redirection and avivartyatva**

From a Śrāvaka preparation, redirection toward complete Buddhahood remains possible after Heat and Summits but not after Acceptance. The Bhāṣya reports an explanation involving the Bodhisattva's possible entry into painful destinies for others' benefit, then gives the more decisive reason: the relevant lineage has become non-reversible (*avivartya*).

Redirection toward Pratyekabuddhahood remains possible after the first three preparations. The Bhāṣya adds that an established Pratyekabuddha lineage itself is not redirectable.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 6.23 determines advancement by excluded futures:

| Threshold | Possibility excluded |
|---|---|
| attainment of Summit | severance of wholesome roots |
| attainment of Acceptance | entry into painful destinies |
| stabilization of gotra | incompatible awakening-trajectories |

The exclusions survive loss of present attainment:

```text
Summit attained → Summit later lost → no severance of wholesome roots
Acceptance attained → Acceptance later lost → no painful destiny
```

This extends 6.22. Present non-possession does not restore every future that existed before acquisition.

The lineage rules add direction to the same modal structure:

```text
Śrāvaka lineage + first two preparations
    → complete-Buddha redirection still open

Śrāvaka lineage + first three preparations
    → Pratyekabuddha redirection still open

fixed gotra
    → incompatible redirection closed
```

In Organon terms, explicitly as project synthesis, Path Meaning becomes **counterfactual efficacy**: its actuality is displayed in what the continuum can no longer do or become. Meaning is operative when it restructures the range of possible consequences.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 6.22 showed that loss of a presently possessed preparatory dharma need not erase the causal transformation produced by its attainment. VAK 6.23 identifies those residual transformations exactly. Summit and Acceptance establish distinct irreversibilities that survive subsequent loss.

This offers a strong criterion for the difference between Knowledge and Path. A proposition may be held and forgotten without reorganizing the agent's future. Here the cultivation of the Truths becomes Path because it removes causal possibilities: first a radical ethical collapse, then painful rebirth, and finally alternative trajectories as lineage becomes fixed.

The movement is not simple deprivation. Closing incompatible possibilities positively determines a course. Gotra supplies the telos under which the same preparatory architecture acquires a distinct direction toward Śrāvaka awakening, Pratyekabuddhahood, or complete Buddhahood.

The kārikā states the protections following Summit and Acceptance and the two lineage-redirection limits. Their persistence after loss, the limited nature of Summit's protection, *anutpattidharmatā*, the detailed excluded futures, the three lineages, and the preferred explanation by lineage non-reversibility are Bhāṣya determinations. “Counterfactual efficacy” is the Organon's reconstruction.
