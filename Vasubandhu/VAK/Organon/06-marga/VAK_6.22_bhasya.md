# VAK_6.22 Bhāṣya — Regression; New Acquisition; Non-Possession

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ādye dve parihāṇyā ca maules tatraiva satyadṛk |
> apūrvāptir vihīneṣu hānī dve asamanvitiḥ || 6.22 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> The first two [are lost] also through regression. [One whose preparation
> belongs] to a principal [dhyāna] sees the Truths there itself. When [the
> preparations] have been lost, there is a new acquisition. The two losses are
> non-possession.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_6.22.md`](./VAK_6.22.md). Next: 6.23.

**Witness.** Pradhan 347|08–347|24. Voice: Vasubandhu. 6.23 at 348|01.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> An ordinary person can lose the first two — Heat and Summits — by regression as
> well as by death. A Noble one has no such regression from them. Even an ordinary
> person has no regression from Acceptance or the Highest Dharmas.
>
> One who produces these roots on a principal-dhyāna ground necessarily sees the
> Truths in that very birth, from intense impetus. When lost ones are obtained
> again, what is obtained is new — not the very ones formerly left. Compared to
> renewed prātimokṣa restraint: effort again.
>
> What is the nature of the two losses? Both are non-possession. Regression is
> made by a fault; relinquishment need not be — it can come from a difference of
> quality. Even one who has regressed after obtaining Heat is determined toward
> final nirvāṇa, and nearer to seeing the Truths than one with only a liberation-
> conducive root, if no obstacle stands in the way.
