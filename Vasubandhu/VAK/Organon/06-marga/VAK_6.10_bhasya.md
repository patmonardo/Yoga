# VAK_6.10 Bhāṣya — Beginner Expands and Contracts; Mastery Relinquishes

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> āsamudrāsthi-vistāra-saṃkṣepād ādikarmikaḥ |
> pādāsthna ā kapālārdha-tyāgāt kṛtajayaḥ smṛtaḥ || 6.10 ||

Literal (from the kārikā study):

> Through expansion and contraction of the skeletal representation as far as
> the ocean, one is [known as] a beginner. Through relinquishing [the
> represented bones] beginning with those of the feet until half the skull
> [remains], one is considered to have achieved mastery.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_6.10.md`](./VAK_6.10.md). Next: 6.11, attention past the support.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The yogācāra cultivating the unattractive is said to be of three kinds:
> beginner; one who has gained familiarity; one whose attention has gone past.
> Of these: through expansion and contraction of bone as far as the ocean, the
> beginner.
>
> One who wants to cultivate the unattractive first binds citta on a part of his
> own body — the big toe, the forehead, or wherever it settles readily. There,
> purifying the bone by resolute stages, he sees the whole skeleton. In the same
> way he resolves on a second, then monastery, park, field, until the earth as
> far as the ocean is resolved as filled with skeletons — for increase of
> adhimokṣa. Then contracting, he resolves on just one, his own skeleton — for
> gathering citta. After a certain time, they say, the unattractive is established.
> This is the beginner yogācāra.
>
> Through relinquishing from the foot-bones as far as half the skull, one is
> called mastered. For a finer gathering of citta he drops the foot-bones in that
> skeleton and keeps the rest in mind. In that order, until he drops half the
> skull and keeps half in mind: that is gained familiarity.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
three stages named; this verse does the first two
fix → clean to bone → expand to ocean (adhimokṣa) → contract to own (citta-saṃkṣepa)
mastery = subtract the counter-object itself down to half-skull
```

## 5. The Image Is Not the End

Expansion is not a claim that the earth is bone. Contraction is not failure.
Mastery is not holding the skeleton harder. 6.09’s bar still stands: this is
still not kleśa-prahāṇa.

## 6. Two Operations, Two Ends

| operation | stated purpose |
|---|---|
| vistāra to the ocean | increase adhimokṣa |
| saṃkṣepa to one’s own skeleton | gather citta |
| tyāga from feet to half-skull | finer gathering; mastery |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- three yogācāras; verse covers two;
- `kṛtajaya` = `kṛtaparijaya`;
- half-skull remainder is in the prose, not a silent rewrite of `kapālārtha`.
