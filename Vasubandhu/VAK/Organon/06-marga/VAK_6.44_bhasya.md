# VAK 6.44 Bhāṣya — The Final Diamond-Like Path

## Kārikā Anchor

> ābhavāgrāṣṭabhāgakṣid arhattve pratipannakaḥ |
> navamasyāpy ānantaryapathe vajropamaś ca saḥ || 6.44 ||

> From the first higher-ground abandonment through eight grades
> at the summit of existence, one proceeds toward arhatship.
> Even on the uninterrupted Path against the ninth grade, that
> Path is diamond-like.

See the [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.44.md). Its first-half
`pratipannakaḥ` agrees with the Bhāṣya's repeated explanation,
whereas the running kārikā line reads `pratipakṣakaḥ`. This
variant is recorded below rather than silently harmonized.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> “One proceeding toward arhatship up to the destruction of
> eight portions at the summit of existence”: the non-returner
> is under discussion. Beginning with detachment from one grade
> belonging to the first dhyāna and continuing through the
> abandonment of eight grades at the summit of existence,
> that practitioner proceeds toward arhatship. Even on the
> uninterrupted Path directed to abandoning the ninth grade
> at the summit, the person remains a candidate for arhatship.
>
> “That [Path] is diamond-like”: this uninterrupted Path is
> called diamond-like concentration because it can break every
> latent affliction. It does not now break all of them—they
> have already been broken—but is capable of breaking all,
> being the strongest of the uninterrupted Paths.
>
> A fine analysis describes many diamond-like concentrations.
> For the level preparatory to first dhyāna, eight are associated
> with modes of knowledge concerning suffering and origin at
> the summit; eight with Dharma-knowledge modes concerning
> cessation and Path; further sets of four with cessation at
> each ground; and four with Anvaya-knowledge modes concerning
> the Path. Distinguished by knowledge-mode and object-support,
> these total fifty-two. Comparable counts extend through the
> fourth dhyāna. For the three lower formless levels, the counts
> are respectively twenty-eight, twenty-four, and twenty,
> because some knowledge modes and lower-ground cessation
> objects are unavailable there, although lower-ground
> counteractive Paths can still be objects.
>
> For those who allow Path-Anvaya-Knowledge to take the
> counteractive Path of each ground separately as object,
> twenty-eight more are added, yielding eighty for the
> preparatory level and corresponding larger counts through
> the fourth dhyāna. In the lower formless levels the counts
> then become forty, thirty-two, and twenty-four. Further
> lineage and faculty distinctions multiply these forms.

The continuous translation condenses repeated members of the
arithmetic while retaining each group, alternative assumption,
and stated total. The source explicitly reports the fine
enumeration as `varṇayanti`, an account others expound. The
exact scholastic distribution requires independent collation
before it is treated as a critical count.

## Movement of the Commentary

**Candidate through the entire final course.** The non-returner
becomes an **arhattva-pratipannaka** with the first higher-ground
cultivation abandonment. This designation continues through
the eighth *bhavāgra* grade and even during the uninterrupted
Path directed against the ninth. The final counteractive
operation and the possession of the arhat fruit are still
distinct; 6.45 takes up the following liberation Path.

**Diamond-like capacity.** The final `ānantaryamārga` is
`vajropama-samādhi`, the strongest such Path. The Bhāṣya
immediately distinguishes **what it can break** from **what it
actually breaks now**. It is capable of breaking all `anuśayas`,
but prior grades are already gone, leaving the last summit-
of-existence grade as its present target. The diamond image
therefore names counteractive adequacy, not a simultaneous
re-destruction of the entire past.

**Fine variants.** The extended enumeration differentiates
diamond-like Paths by knowledge-mode, object-support, meditative
ground, and then lineage and faculty. It also gives an alternative
count if Path-Anvaya-Knowledge may take each ground's
counteractive Path separately as object. These are variants
of a common final function, not separate final fruits.

## Organon Response — Universal Capacity, Exact Present Work

For our Science of Knowing, the strongest Path has a universal
capacity while performing one exact present task. The already
abandoned grades need not be represented as newly present in
order for the final Path to be adequate to them in power.
Universality is expressed through fully determinate efficacy.

That is our Organon reconstruction. The source establishes
the capacity/actual-target distinction and the fine Abhidharma
variants. It does not itself name this Path Absolute Reason,
and the fruit still awaits the following liberation moment.
