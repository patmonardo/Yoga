# VAK 6.39 Bhāṣya — Karma, Affliction, and Faculty

## Kārikā Anchor

> punas trīṃs trividhān kṛtvā nava rūpopagāḥ smṛtāḥ |
> tadviśeṣaḥ punaḥ karmakleśendriyaviśeṣataḥ || 6.39 ||

> Again, by making three [types] each threefold, nine form-going
> [non-returners] are recognized. Their further distinctions
> arise from differences in karma, affliction, and faculty.

The [official kārikā study](./VAK_6.39.md) prints only the second
half of this verse; the first half is present in the continuous
[running source](../../../Sources/kosabhasya.txt) at [360|13].
This Bhāṣya study records the complete two-half kārikā without
altering the existing kārikā file. The textual discrepancy needs
editorial collation.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

> “Again, by making three [types] each threefold, nine form-going
> [non-returners] are recognized.” Three kinds of non-returner
> are each divided in three. Which three? The one completing in
> the intermediate existence, the one completing after rebirth,
> and the upward-streamer. How are they divided? The first is
> distinguished by quick, less quick, or delayed final nirvāṇa,
> illustrated through three comparisons. The after-rebirth type
> is divided into completion shortly after arising, with
> exertion, and without exertion; all three complete after
> rebirth and are gathered under that designation. The upward-
> streamer is divided by the leaper and the other routes.
> Alternatively, each of the three may be called threefold
> according to quick, less quick, or delayed completion.
>
> “Their differentiation is due to differences in karma,
> affliction, and faculty.” The distinction among both three
> and nine non-returners arises from these differences. The
> principal three are associated respectively with karma to
> be experienced at formation, after rebirth, or in a later
> subsequent existence; with mild, medium, or strong active
> manifestation of affliction; and with strong, medium, or mild
> faculty. The nine are differentiated accordingly as fits each
> case. For the first two triads, affliction and faculty provide
> the further differences; for the last, differences in karma
> to be experienced later do so. Thus, through ninefold
> affliction-and-faculty differences, there are nine non-returners.

The three karmic compounds are kept close to their temporal
function. The compressed Sanskrit does not warrant a fully
specified modern taxonomy of when each karmic result occurs.
The alternative quick/less-quick/delayed division is preserved
as an alternative rather than merged with the named triads.

## Movement of the Commentary

The first half generates **nine form-going non-returners** from
three broader types: intermediate-state completer, post-rebirth
completer, and upward-streamer. Each is divided threefold. The
Bhāṣya gives specific subdivisions and also permits a more
general three-speed reading. These are different ways of
articulating the same broader non-returner classification, not
nine new Noble fruits.

The second half supplies the causal variables: **karma** as
temporally disposed result, **kleśa** as actively manifesting
residue, and **indriya** as faculty strength. For the principal
three, mild, medium, and strong afflictive manifestation are
paired respectively with strong, medium, and mild faculty.
The relation is inverse in this local classification. The
first two triads are further distinguished by kleśa and
indriya, the upward-streamer triad especially by later-result
karma.

The method here is explanatory rather than a bare multiplication
of names. The shared fruit leaves distinct causal conditions
in the continua that hold it. Those differences affect timing
and route of completion. The final sentence ties the ninefold
person-classification back to the graded affliction and faculty
scheme, while keeping its detailed allocation context-specific.

## Organon Response — Determinate Courses Within One Fruit

For our Science of Knowing, 6.39 shows how a single attained
fruit can support several actual trajectories. Karma, residual
affliction, and faculty describe different determinations of
the course: what can ripen, what still arises, and what power
the practitioner has to complete the Path. None alone exhausts
the path-person's situation.

This is an Organon reconstruction of the causal triad. The
Bhāṣya states the three variables and their local correlations.
It does not identify this taxonomy with our Objective and
Subjective Logic or prove a claim about Absolute Reason.
