# VAK_8.39 — Transmission, Realization, and Their Bearers

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सद्धर्मो द्विविधः शास्तुरागमाधिगमात्मकः ।
धातारस्तस्य वक्तारः प्रतिपत्तार एव च ॥ ८.३९ ॥
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sat-dharmaḥ | dvi-vidhaḥ | śāstuḥ | āgama-adhigama-ātmakaḥ |

```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `saddharmaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | the true teaching; true Dharma |
| `dvividhaḥ` | nominative singular masculine adjective | twofold |
| `śāstuḥ` | genitive singular of `śāstṛ` | of the Teacher |
| `āgamādhigamātmakaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound adjective | consisting of transmission and realization |
| `dhātāraḥ` | nominative plural masculine agent noun | bearers, sustainers |
| `tasya` | genitive singular pronoun | of it, of that true teaching |
| `vaktāraḥ` | nominative plural masculine of `vaktṛ` | speakers, expounders |

| `eva` | emphatic particle | precisely; indeed |

Sat + dharma gives saddharma. Āgama + adhigama
and adhigama + ātmaka give the long vowels
in āgamādhigamātmaka. Śāstuḥ before āgamādhigamātmaka
appears as śāstur; the final visargas of
dhātāraḥ and pratipattāraḥ likewise undergo the displayed sandhi.

## 4. Grammar

**One teaching is classified in two forms.**
Saddharmaḥ is the subject; dvividhaḥ and
āgamādhigamātmakaḥ supply its predicates. Śāstuḥ identifies
it as the Teacher's teaching, not an
unqualified definition of every body of doctrine.

**The compound names the two forms.** Āgama
and adhigama are coordinated within the final
ātmaka compound. Here ātmaka expresses constitution or
character; it does not introduce a separate
metaphysical self as a third member.

**The second line identifies the sustaining functions.**
Tasya refers to saddharma. Vaktāraḥ and pratipattāraḥ
are the bearers named by the verse. The
commentary distributes the functions: preservers and speakers
for transmission, practitioners for realization.

**Emphasis does not require disjoint populations.** Eva
emphasizes the named bearers. Nothing here prevents
one person from both expounding and practicing.
The text distinguishes functions without asserting that
their bearers must be different individuals.

**Close construe.** The Teacher's true teaching is
twofold, consisting of transmission and realization. Its
sustainers are the expounders and the practitioners.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The Teacher's true Dharma is twofold, consisting
> of transmission and realization. Its bearers are
> precisely the expounders and the practitioners.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Teacher's true teaching has two forms.
> Transmission consists of Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma;
> realization consists of the factors conducive to awakening.
>
> Transmission is sustained by those who preserve
> and expound it, and realization by those
> who practice. The true teaching is to
> be understood as remaining for as long
> as these bearers remain.

Source: Pradhan 459|07–459|14, checked against the local
kārikā and Bhāṣya witnesses and the running source.
The opening question asks how long this teaching,
in which such varieties of dharmas are discerned,
will remain. The closing prose reports differing
duration accounts, treated separately below.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The continuity of the teaching has an
> articulated and a realized dimension. Each has
> its corresponding sustaining activity: preservation and exposition,
> or practice of the factors conducive to
> awakening. Asking how long the teaching remains
> therefore requires distinguishing what is transmitted from
> what continues to be realized.

This formulation preserves both forms without requiring
their historical durations to be identical. The
commentary expressly reports an account in which
transmission remains longer than realization.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Saddharma`.** The true Dharma or authentic teaching
of the Teacher. Here its twofold constitution
answers a question about its continuing presence.

**`Āgama`.** Transmitted teaching. The commentary specifies
Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma. The term should
not be restricted here to collections bearing
“Āgama” as their title, nor reduced to
an unspecified inherited custom.

**`Adhigama`.** Realization or attainment. The source
specifies it through bodhipakṣya dharmas. Generic comprehension
of propositions or private conviction would be
too loose a rendering of this realized dimension.

**`Bodhipakṣya`.** Belonging to or conducive to awakening.
This identifies the practical content of adhigama
in the commentary. The passage refers to
that established class rather than enumerating its
members again in this unit.

**`Dhātṛ / dhārayitṛ`.** Bearer or sustainer /
one who retains or preserves. The prose
explains the bearing of transmission through retention
and exposition; a bearer is not merely
a passive possessor of a text.

**`Vaktṛ`.** Speaker or expounder. The role concerns
articulating the transmitted teaching. The verse does
not add a new institutional office or lineage
requirement to the term.

**`Pratipattṛ`.** Practitioner, one who undertakes the
path. The commentary assigns this function to
adhigama. Translating simply “believer” would lose the
relation to actual practice and realization.

**`Sthāsyati / avasthāna`.** Will remain / continuance.
These organize the prose's duration question and
its reported answers. They do not by
themselves establish a calendar starting date.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The two forms and their bearers.**

| Form of the true teaching | Content specified in the prose | Sustaining function |
|---|---|---|
| Āgama | Sūtra, Vinaya, Abhidharma | Retaining and expounding |
| Adhigama | Factors conducive to awakening | Practicing |

The content distinction and the bearer distinction
are related but not interchangeable. A practitioner
is the bearer of the realized dimension;
the person is not simply another item added
to the list of awakening factors.

**The persistence criterion.** Yāvad ete sthāsyanti,
“as long as these remain,” refers back
to the identified bearers. The account ties
continuity to sustaining activity. It gives no
warrant for inferring realization solely from the
existence of an archive, or for denying
the transmitted form any status as Dharma
when the realized form no longer persists.

**The reported duration and its qualification.** The
prose first reports that their continuance is
one thousand years: āhuḥ, “they say.” It
then gives the other account, marked by
ity apare: this applies to realization, whereas
transmission lasts for a longer time.

| Report | Stated scope |
|---|---|
| “They say” | A thousand years of continuance, initially stated without the later distinction |
| “Others” | That duration for realization; a longer duration for transmission |

The second account is best read as qualifying
the first, not as a reason to silently
make the initial report exclusively about realization.
No named school or precise longer duration
is supplied in this unit. These are reported
traditional estimates, not dates independently established by
this study or a diagnosis of the present age.

**Twofold does not mean equal-duration.** The commentary's
alternative allows the transmitted form to outlast
the realized form. The research's statement that
the Dharma exists “only where these remain joined”
therefore needs qualification. Both belong to its
full twofold account; each can also be
discussed with its own bearers and duration.

**From attainment to preservation.** The question follows
the detailed exposition of contemplative dharmas. It
asks about the continuance of the teaching
in which such distinctions are discerned. The
transition connects doctrinal articulation and realized practice
without treating them as identical activities.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source voices remain distinct.** The twofold classification
and bearer mapping are presented expositionally. The
duration statements are explicitly reported through āhuḥ
and apare. The official study preserves those
attributions rather than presenting one unqualified prediction
as the conclusion of the kārikā.

**Refinement of the first-pass interpretation.** The research
usefully stresses transmission and realization together, but
its stronger claim of inseparability obscures the
reported unequal durations. Likewise “empty transmission” is
an evaluative research expression, not the source's
name for āgama surviving longer than adhigama.
The official account retains the two forms
and their functional relation without that extra judgment.

**Witness caution.** The running source separates varṣasahasra
mavasthānam where the local excerpt joins varṣasahasramavasthānam.
This spacing does not alter the thousand-year
report. The more substantive editorial issue is
attribution of the qualification beginning adhigamasyaivam,
read with the closing ity apare as above.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the teaching's determinations and
makes them available for study and exposition.
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns effective
practice. The comparison with āgama and adhigama
is useful while preserving the source's specific
contents: Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma on one
side, awakening factors on the other.

For the Organon, preservation of a corpus and
continuance of learned competence can be represented
as related functions. That is a project
application, not a replacement of adhigama by
generic technical proficiency or of the Teacher's
Dharma by any formal knowledge system.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The two forms, their specified contents, corresponding
bearer functions, persistence statement, and differently scoped
duration reports have been checked against the
local witnesses and running source. The research
claim of inseparability is qualified without altering
the research originals.
