# VAK_8.30 Bhāṣya — Equanimity, Aspiration, and the Immeasurables' Object-Field

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> upekṣālobhaḥ ākāraḥ sukhitā duḥkhitā vata |
> modantām iti sattvāś ca kāmasattvās tu gocaraḥ || 8.30 ||

> Equanimity is non-greed. The forms are “happy,” “alas, suffering,” “may they
> rejoice,” and “beings.” Beings of the desire domain are their object-field.

Levels and how far the counter-work goes: 8.31.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.30.md`](./VAK_8.30.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Equanimity has the nature of non-greed. How then does it oppose ill will?
> Ill will is drawn by greed. A nature of both [non-greed and non-hatred] is
> fitting.
>
> Attending “alas, beings are happy” one enters friendliness; “alas, beings
> suffer,” compassion; “may beings rejoice,” sympathetic joy; simply “beings,”
> equanimity — from impartiality.
>
> If they are not thus happy, how is the resolve not inverted? Intention: *may
> they be*. Or the disposition is not inverted, as resolve. Or even if inverted:
> not unwholesome — wholesome roots, and it counters ill will and the rest.
>
> Desire-domain beings are the object. Scriptural “one direction”: the container
> shows the contained.

## 5. Wish ≠ Description

`Sukhitāḥ` is an adjective; `santu` supplies the wish. Impartiality ≠ upekṣendriya.
Immeasurable count of beings ≠ all domains as objects. Direction ≠ empty space as object.

## 6. Four Forms

| | attends |
|---|---|
| maitrī | may they be happy |
| karuṇā | alas, they suffer |
| muditā | may they rejoice |
| upekṣā | beings |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `sattvāś ca` = fourth form;
- `gocara` = kāmāvacara beings;
- combined nature offered after the verse's alobha.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

The House has manners. Vidyā: nature, form, intention, field. Technē of “may they
be happy” is Practical Reason in kāmadhātu — not a report that they already are.
Appearance would call equanimity empty shine. It is *beings*, without grab.
Yama still waits outside the verse. The verse teaches how one stands.
