# VAK_8.03 — Formless Naming, Absence of Form, and Its Renewed Arising

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
विभूतरूपसंज्ञाख्याः सह सामन्तकैस्त्रिभिः ।
नारूप्ये रूपसद्भावः रूपोत्पत्तिस्तु चित्ततः ॥ ८.०३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vibhūta-rūpa-saṃjñā-ākhyāḥ | saha | sāmantakaiḥ | tribhiḥ |
na | ārūpye | rūpa-sadbhāvaḥ | rūpa-utpattiḥ | tu | cittataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `vibhūtarūpasaṃjñākhyāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound adjective | designated as having form-perception passed away |
| `saha` | indeclinable governing instrumental | together with |
| `sāmantakaiḥ` | instrumental plural neuter | neighboring or access attainments |
| `tribhiḥ` | instrumental plural numeral | three |
| `ārūpye` | locative singular | in the formless domain |
| `rūpasadbhāvaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | presence or existence of material form |
| `rūpotpattiḥ` | nominative singular feminine compound | arising of material form |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | however |
| `cittataḥ` | ablatival adverb | from citta, mind |

`Saṃjñā + ākhyāḥ` yields `saṃjñākhyāḥ`;
`na + ārūpye` yields `nārūpye`; `rūpa + utpattiḥ`
yields `rūpotpattiḥ`. The prose glosses `vibhūtā` with
`vigatā`, gone away. The local sense is disappearance,
not manifestation or magnificence.

## 4. Grammar

**The inherited subject.** The plural designation resumes
the four formless attainments. `Saha sāmantakais tribhiḥ`
adds three access attainments. It does not reduce the
principal formless attainments to three.

**The excluded access stage.** The Bhāṣya excludes the
access to infinite space because it takes the fourth
dhyāna as object. Form-perception has therefore not
passed away there. The three included access stages
are those of the subsequent formless attainments.

**Presence and arising.** `Na ... rūpasadbhāvaḥ` denies
present form in the formless domain. `Rūpotpattis tu
cittataḥ` answers a different question: how form arises
again after a being leaves formless existence. The
second clause does not retract the first by positing
hidden form throughout the interval.

**The causal ablative.** `Cittataḥ` supplies the source
of renewed form. The prose qualifies that citta through
`tadvipākahetuparibhāvita`, conditioned by the cause
whose maturation is the relevant form. Bare “mind creates
matter” would omit the qualification decisive to the answer.

**Close construe.** The formless attainments, together
with three access attainments, receive the designation
that form-perception has disappeared. Form is not present
in the formless domain; its renewed arising, however,
is from causally conditioned citta.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> They are designated as having form-perception passed
> away, together with three access attainments. There
> is no presence of form in the formless domain; the
> arising of form, however, is from citta.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The four formless attainments and the access stages
> of the latter three receive the designation that
> perception of form has disappeared. Access to infinite
> space is excluded, since it still takes the fourth
> dhyāna as object.
>
> The formless domain contains no material form, even
> as a subtle remainder. When form arises again after
> departure from that existence, it arises from citta
> conditioned by the karmic cause whose maturation is
> that form, when the relevant causal functioning becomes
> available.

Source: Pradhan 434|10–435|26, local Bhāṣya checked
against the complete running passage. The last expression
renders the compressed `labdhavṛtti` provisionally; it
is not a fully specified account of every condition
of renewed embodiment.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Absence of a constituent does not by itself establish
> impossibility of its later arising. The account denies
> form in the formless domain while locating the conditions
> for renewed form in a causally determined citta. Naming,
> present constitution, and future genesis remain distinct.

The renewed form need not be numerically the same form
that existed before. The passage explains arising after
an interruption; it does not establish identity of a
material object across that interruption.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vibhūtarūpasaṃjña`.** Having perception of form passed
away. The designation has a specific extension among
principal and access attainments; proximity to a formless
attainment alone is insufficient for the name.

**`Sāmantaka`.** Neighboring or access attainment. The
first access stage's fourth-dhyāna object explains its
exclusion. The main infinite-space attainment remains
included; access and principal attainment must not be
confused.

**`Rūpajāti`.** The class of material form. The reply
insists that this entire class is transcended, whereas
the classes of feeling and the other nonmaterial aggregates
are not. This supports the four-aggregate constitution
stated in VAK 8.02.

**`Bhūta / bhautika`.** Material elements and what is
derived from them. The objection proposing bodily and
verbal restraint as residual form is challenged because
derivative materiality cannot stand without its elemental
basis.

**`Tadvipākahetuparibhāvita`.** Conditioned by the cause
whose maturation is that form. This qualifies the mind
from which form later arises. It does not describe
arbitrary creation by a present wish.

**`Labdhavṛtti`.** Having obtained functioning or an
occasion for activity. The precise attachment and causal
nuance of this compressed phrase remain provisional,
while its role in the qualified arising account is clear.

**`Analpakalpocchinna`.** Interrupted for many kalpas.
The question expressly rules out explaining renewed
form by a merely short gap in its present occurrence.
It introduces no modern chronological measurement.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The naming rule.**

| State | Form-perception-passed-away designation |
|---|---|
| Four principal formless attainments | Included |
| Access to infinite space | Excluded: fourth dhyāna remains its object |
| Access to the remaining three formless attainments | Included |

**Objection: perhaps form is merely slight.** The interlocutor
proposes that “formless” could mean very little form.
The reply asks what this alleged form consists of.
If only bodily and verbal restraint is proposed, the
material basis must be accounted for. The analogy to
uncontaminated restraint is answered by its existing
material support, rather than accepted as support-free
materiality.

If a body and material faculties are proposed, the
criterion for “slight” becomes problematic. Small size
would include tiny creatures; transparency or subtlety
would also include intermediate-existence and form-realm
beings. Defining the category by unsurpassed subtlety
would threaten to leave only the highest existence
as genuinely formless. The reply tests the proposed
criterion's scope rather than relying on the name alone.

**Objection: canonical dependence requires form.** The
opponent invokes life with heat, the mutual support
of name and form, name-and-form conditioned by consciousness,
and the denial of consciousness proceeding apart from
the other aggregates. Vasubandhu asks what each statement
quantifies over: which domains, whether all consciousness,
and whether absence of all supports or of any single
aggregate is intended.

Unrestricted readings would wrongly extend life-dependence
to external heat, mental dependence to all external form,
and gross-food requirements to the higher domains.
The reply therefore does not reject the cited statements;
it rejects extending their scope without control.

**Positive scriptural grounds.** The text adduces statements
of escape from form and of beings beyond form-perception.
The proposal that only gross lower form is excluded
is challenged: dhyānas too leave lower grounds behind,
while formless states specifically transcend the form
class. Feeling and the other nonmaterial classes remain.

The aggregate enumerations provide a final comparison:
dhyāna descriptions begin with material form and continue
through consciousness; the formless description begins
with feeling. The omission is used as evidence within
the source's doctrinal argument, not as an independent
physical observation.

**Renewed form after formless existence.**

```text
During formless existence:
    Material form absent
    Citta continues

Renewed form:
    Arises from citta qualified by the relevant maturation cause
    When its causal functioning becomes available
```

The final objection appeals to ordinary experience:
how can mind exist without form when this is not observed
here? The response challenges that universal inference
by comparison with form-realm existence without gross
food, likewise not exemplified in the ordinary domain.
This is an argument about the limits of extrapolation
within the cosmological account, not present-day empirical
verification of disembodied consciousness.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Complete passage control.** The local Bhāṣya excerpt
omits portions of the material-basis and scriptural-scope
debates. The running source supplies those portions,
including the smallness, transparency, heat, external
form, and food examples. The study therefore treats
the excerpt as a selection, not a complete transcription.

**Witness cautions.** The running text contains forms
such as `smāpattāvapi`, `hyaṣmaṇa`, `binā`, `kutsnām`,
and the awkward `kavaḍīkārāntareṇā 'pi vinā`. The
comparative argument about gross food is intelligible
from its context, but the final expression is not a
clean transmitted construction. The `īṣadrūpa` analogy
also has an irregular junction around `dāpiṅgalavat`.
No conjectural restoration is substituted into the witnesses.

**Scope of the absence claim.** The argument concerns
formless-domain constitution and return from formless
existence. It should not become an assertion that the
physical body of a human meditator disappears whenever
that person enters a formless attainment. The birth
and attainment distinction remains essential.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the difference between current
constitution and the conditions of later genesis. Yoga
Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns the operative
causal determination through which a later form can arise.
The source keeps those relations together without retaining
a hidden material constituent in the formless interval.

For Organon, the useful distinction is between preserving
an instantiated form and retaining conditions for a
new formation. The research analogy to regeneration
can be developed from that distinction, but numerical
identity of the earlier and later form is not supplied
by the text. Nor does the passage define Meaning simply
as the ability to regenerate an output.

The source's own explanation is karmic and specifically
qualified: citta conditioned by a maturation cause.
A project model must preserve that attribution instead
of presenting an unrestricted idealism or a software
storage theory as Vasubandhu's doctrine.
