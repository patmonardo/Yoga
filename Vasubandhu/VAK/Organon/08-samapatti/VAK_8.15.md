# VAK_8.15 — Immediate Successors of Wholesome Attainments

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तृतीयाद्यावदूर्ध्वाधोऽनास्रवानन्तरं शुभम् ।
उत्पद्यते तथा शुद्धात् क्लिष्टं चापि स्वभूमिकम् ॥ ८.१५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
tṛtīyāt | yāvat | ūrdhva-adhaḥ | anāsrava-anantaram | śubham |
utpadyate | tathā | śuddhāt | kliṣṭam | ca | api | sva-bhūmikam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `tṛtīyāt` | ablative singular ordinal | the third, marking the limit in the compressed expression |
| `yāvat` | limiting particle | as far as; up to |
| `ūrdhvādhaḥ` | paired adverbial expression | upward and downward |
| `anāsravānantaram` | adverbial compound | immediately after an uncontaminated attainment |
| `śubham` | nominative singular neuter | wholesome [attainment] |
| `utpadyate` | third-person singular present middle of ud-√pad | arises |
| `tathā` | adverb | likewise; in the same manner |
| `śuddhāt` | ablative singular neuter | after a pure attainment |
| `kliṣṭam` | nominative singular neuter | defiled [attainment] |
| `ca api` | additive particles | and also |
| `svabhūmikam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | belonging to the same level |

`Tṛtīyāt + yāvat` yields `tṛtīyād yāvat` in the
continuous text. It should not be segmented into
“third and following.” `Yāvat + ūrdhva-` yields
`yāvad ūrdhva-`; `adhaḥ + anāsrava-` yields
`adho 'nāsrava-`; `ca + api` yields `cāpi`.

## 4. Grammar

**The limit is relative to the starting level.**
The examples show a reach from first dhyāna through
third, and from second through fourth. “As far as
the third” thus counts the starting level as first:
at most two level-steps, with one intervening level
when a level is skipped. It does not allow three
level-steps or an unbounded “third and following.”

**The same level is included.** Although the verse
compresses its spatial language into upward/downward,
the prose explicitly includes same-level pure and
uncontaminated successors.

**Śubha is the grammatical subject.** The wholesome
attainment arises after the uncontaminated predecessor.
The Bhāṣya expressly includes both śuddha and anāsrava
under śubha because both are wholesome.

**Tathā carries forward the wholesome rule.** After
a pure attainment, the previously described wholesome
successions are available under the relevant conditions.
The additional `kliṣṭaṃ cāpi` does not replace that
rule with a solely pure-to-defiled transition.

**Svabhūmika restricts the addition.** The defiled
successor additionally permitted after a pure attainment
belongs to the same level. This qualifier does not
restrict all wholesome successors to that level.

**Close construe.** Immediately after an uncontaminated
attainment, wholesome attainment arises on the same
level or within two levels above or below. Likewise
after a pure attainment; additionally, a defiled
attainment of its own level may arise.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Immediately after an uncontaminated [attainment],
> a wholesome [attainment] arises upward or downward
> as far as the third. Likewise after a pure
> [attainment]; also a defiled [attainment] belonging
> to its own level.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> An uncontaminated attainment may be followed immediately
> by a pure or uncontaminated attainment on the same
> level, or up to two levels higher or lower, wherever
> those modes exist. The limit counts the starting
> level as the first. At the summit of existence,
> only the pure mode is available.
>
> The same general wholesome-successor rule applies
> after a pure attainment, with the additional possibility
> of a defiled attainment on that same level. A
> defiled attainment does not arise immediately after
> an uncontaminated attainment.

Source: Pradhan 443|13–444|08, checked against the local
Bhāṣya study and running source. The knowledge-condition
for entering ārūpya is stated below. These are general
mode-and-level rules; the later analysis further differentiates
pure attainments by subtype.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An attainment is situated not only by its level
> and mode but also by its permitted immediate successors.
> A change of level can preserve wholesomeness; a
> change from pure to defiled can occur on the same
> level. Uncontaminated attainment excludes that immediate
> defiled successor.

“May follow” describes a permitted succession, not
an inevitable event. The prohibition concerns immediate
succession and does not by itself settle every later
state in a person's continuum.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Anantara / samanantara`.** Immediately following.
This concerns the next occurrence, not simply an
attainment reached eventually through intermediate states.
It differs from VAK 8.14's acquisition of possession.

**`Śubha`.** Wholesome, encompassing pure and uncontaminated
attainments in this passage. Translating it as pure
alone would remove half of the stated alternatives.

**`Śuddha`.** Pure but contaminated. Its possible
same-level defiled successor distinguishes it here
from anāsrava. The later subtype analysis prevents
treating every pure attainment as interchangeable.

**`Vyutkrāntaka-samāpatti`.** Attainment involving skipping
levels. The prose invokes these practitioners to
explain the upper bound on a leap. It does not
state that any practitioner can perform every permitted transition.

**`Anvayajñāna / dharmajñāna`.** The established knowledge
categories from Chapter 7. In the uncontaminated predecessor
context, formless attainments are entered after anvayajñāna,
not dharmajñāna; the latter's lower support and object
supply the stated reason.

**`Samāpattidravya`.** A distinct attainment item in
the present enumeration, specified by level and mode.
The counts here are not the eleven distinct dhyāna
factors of VAK 8.09.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The ordered levels used in the count.** The sequence
is the four dhyānas, then infinite space, infinite
knowledge, nothingness, and the summit of existence.
The last has a pure but no uncontaminated attainment.

**The examples determine the range.** The following
counts concern successors of an uncontaminated predecessor.

| Predecessor level | Eligible destination levels | Wholesome successor count |
|---|---|---:|
| First dhyāna | First through third dhyānas | 6 |
| Second dhyāna | First through fourth dhyānas | 8 |
| Third dhyāna | First dhyāna through infinite space | 10 |
| Fourth dhyāna | Second dhyāna through infinite knowledge | 10 |
| Infinite space | Third dhyāna through nothingness | 10 |
| Infinite knowledge | Fourth dhyāna through the summit | 9 |
| Nothingness | Infinite space through the summit | 7 |

The prose explicitly gives six, seven, eight, and
nine, and directs that the other cases be construed
as ten. The middle rows expand that instruction.
Each eligible level contributes pure and uncontaminated
modes, except the summit, which contributes pure alone.
These are general level/mode possibilities, subject
to the knowledge-condition below.

**A boundary is not an exception to the arithmetic.**
First dhyāna has only its own and two higher levels
within the principal-attainment sequence. Nothingness
has two lower levels, its own, and the summit above.
The missing uncontaminated summit explains the odd
totals seven and nine. No uncontaminated summit is
invented to make the pattern uniform.

**Knowledge constrains formless entry.** The prose
specifies entry into ārūpya immediately after anvayajñāna
and excludes dharmajñāna because of its lower support
and object. Thus a permitted level-distance alone
is insufficient to validate every particular transition.
The condition is supplied in the discussion of uncontaminated
predecessors; it is not a denial of every pure-to-pure
formless transition.

**The mode asymmetry.**

| Predecessor | General wholesome successors | Additional defiled successor |
|---|---|---|
| Uncontaminated | Same level and bounded higher/lower range | None immediately |
| Pure | Corresponding wholesome possibilities | Same level only |

The pure row states the general rule. VAK 8.17's
opening clarification that not every pure attainment
produces an uncontaminated successor will further
qualify it by subtype. This study therefore does
not convert the table into a claim about every
individual pure state.

**The present scope is meditative succession.** The
next verse separately treats defiled predecessors and
then distinguishes the death-time case. Those rules
must not be silently imported into the present
same-level restriction or inferred by reversing its arrows.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Corrections to the research first pass.** Its vague
“third and following” obscures the bounded range,
and its shortened pure-successor account can omit
uncontaminated successors. The commentary's examples,
definition of śubha, and `śeṣaṃ yathaivānāsravāt`
control the fuller reading here. Originals remain intact.

**Witness cautions.** The running source prints
`tṛtīvādyāvat` in prose and `kliṭaṃ` in the second
half of the verse. The local kārikā regularizes the
latter with notice. The preceding verse line, explicit
third-level limit, and enumerated examples secure the
range without relying on the irregular prose spelling.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the predecessor, successor,
level-distance, mode, and knowledge-condition. Yoga
Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns the effective
exercise of succession within those conditions. A
list of attainable levels alone does not specify
which passage from one to another is admissible.

For Organon representation, retain the directed relation
and its conditions. Separate the general permission
from an assertion that a particular transition actually
occurred. Include mode availability at the destination
and preserve later subtype refinements. This is a
project reconstruction, not a software architecture
attributed to the source.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The inclusive third-level limit, successor counts,
śubha expansion, summit exception, knowledge-condition,
and additional same-level defiled successor have been
checked against the local research and running source.
