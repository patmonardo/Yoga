# VAK_8.01 Bhāṣya — Dhyāna as Attainment and the Question of Collectedness

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dvidhā dhyānāni catvāri proktās tadupapattayaḥ |
> samāpattiḥ śubhaikāgryaṃ pañcaskandhās tu sānugam || 8.01 ||

> The four dhyānas are twofold; their corresponding births have been explained.
> Attainment is wholesome one-pointedness; with its accompaniment, however, it
> is the five aggregates.

Birth-side already in Lokanirdeśa. This chapter takes the attainment-side.
Differentiation of the four, and ārūpya: 8.02.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_8.01.md`](./VAK_8.01.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Under the heading of knowledge the knowledge-qualities were set out. Now the
> dhyānas are begun, because they are the support of all qualities. In summary
> the dhyānas are twofold: by the distinction of birth-dhyāna and attainment-
> dhyāna. The corresponding births were already stated in the World chapter.
> Attainment-dhyāna is what must be stated.
>
> Without distinction, dhyāna is wholesome one-pointedness of mind — because its
> nature is samādhi. With its retinue it is to be known as five-aggregate in
> nature. What is this one-pointedness? Having one object-support.
>
> Then would the minds themselves, being one-supported, be samādhi, and no
> distinct mental factor? The minds themselves are not samādhi. That dharma by
> which they operate one-pointed — that is samādhi. Presence as a great-ground
> factor would make every mind one-pointed? No: because samādhi can be weak.
> Others: the one-pointed minds themselves are samādhi.
>
> What does dhyāna mean? They contemplate by it — that is, they know — because a
> collected mind knows things as they are. Not every samādhi receives the name:
> only the excellent, as the sun is called light-maker. That excellence is the
> samādhi endowed with factors, because tranquillity and insight proceed yoked.
>
> How is an afflicted state dhyāna? From wrong contemplation. Overextension?
> No: the name is set on a counterpart, like a rotten seed. And the Blessed One
> also spoke of unwholesome dhyānas.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
Ch. 7 qualities → dhyāna as support
four dhyānas × two aspects; births already done
defining nature: kuśala-citta-ekāgratā
with retinue: five aggregates
ekāgratā = one ālambana → is samādhi a separate factor?
two accounts kept apart
name reserved for factor-endowed, yoked śamatha–vipaśyanā
afflicted naming by resemblance
```

## 5. Defining Nature ≠ Retinue

Five aggregates ≠ the five first-dhyāna factors. One object ≠ the full account
of collectedness. Universal occurrence ≠ equal strength. Do not merge `apare`
into the distinct-factor reply. Afflicted naming does not cancel `śubha` in the
definition. `Sarvaguṇāśraya` is why this chapter starts here — not that every
property of everything is born from concentration.

## 6. Two Accounts of Samādhi

| | what collectedness is |
|---|---|
| main reply | a distinct dharma by which minds operate one-pointed |
| `apare` | the one-pointed minds themselves |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `dvidhā` = birth / attainment;
- `samāpattiḥ` = attainment-dhyāna;
- `sānugam` = with retinue, five-aggregate.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Jñāna as Idea needed a ground of operation. That ground is not a third eye.
Vidyā: collected mind *is* the condition of knowing-as-it-is — `dhyāyanti` =
`prajānanti`. Technē: śamatha and vipaśyanā yoked, not trance and not bare
analysis. Appearance would stop at one object. The Concept of one-ālambana stays
inside the Idea of excellent samādhi. Birth-dhyāna remains World; this is Path-
work of collectedness. Do not import Marga Hell yet.
