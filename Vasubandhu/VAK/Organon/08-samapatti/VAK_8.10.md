# VAK_8.10 — Factors Absent from Defiled Dhyānas

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
क्लिष्टेष्वसत्प्रीतिसुखं प्रसादः सम्प्रधीः स्मृतिः ।
उपेक्षास्मृतिशुद्धिश्च केचित् प्रस्रब्ध्युपेक्षणे ॥ ८.१० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kliṣṭeṣv asat prītisukhaṃ prasādaḥ saṃpradhīḥ smṛtiḥ |
upekṣāsmṛtiśuddhiś ca kecit prasrabdhyupekṣaṇe || 8.10 ||
```

The verse follows the local kārikā witness. The running
source's `smṛṭiḥ` differs from its `smṛtiḥ`. The prose
controls the distribution across the four dhyānas and
the interpretation of the compressed `saṃpradhīḥ`.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kliṣṭeṣu | asat | prīti-sukham | prasādaḥ | saṃpradhīḥ | smṛtiḥ |
upekṣā-smṛti-śuddhiḥ | ca | kecit | prasrabdhi-upekṣaṇe
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `kliṣṭeṣu` | locative plural | in the defiled [dhyānas] |
| `asat` | neuter singular of negated sant, participle of √as | absent; not present |
| `prītisukham` | neuter singular collective expression | rapture and pleasure |
| `prasādaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | inward clarity |
| `saṃpradhīḥ` | nominative singular feminine, compressed designation | clear comprehension, resolved by the prose |
| `smṛtiḥ` | nominative singular feminine | mindfulness or recollection |
| `upekṣāsmṛtiśuddhiḥ` | nominative singular feminine compound | purification of equanimity and mindfulness |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `kecit` | nominative plural masculine indefinite pronoun | some [authorities] |
| `prasrabdhyupekṣaṇe` | coordinated dual expression | pliancy and equanimity |

`Kliṣṭeṣu + asat` yields `kliṣṭeṣv asat`;
`śuddhiḥ + ca` yields `śuddhiś ca`; `prasrabdhi +
upekṣaṇe` yields `prasrabdhyupekṣaṇe`. The last member
is resolved as upekṣā by the prose. Its surface form
is retained rather than silently replaced.

## 4. Grammar

**A distributed statement.** `Kliṣṭeṣu` supplies
`dhyāneṣu`. The absences are assigned successively to
the first, second, third, and fourth dhyānas. The prose
repeats `nāsti` for each assignment; the verse's `asat`
introduces this compressed absence-list.

**The first absence is qualified.** The verse's
`prītisukham` receives `vivekajam`, born of separation,
in prose. That qualification determines what is denied.
The passage does not simply say that every pleasant
experience is impossible in a defiled attainment.

**Clear comprehension and mindfulness.** The verse
places `saṃpradhīḥ` before `smṛtiḥ`; the prose gives
`smṛtisaṃprajanyam`. The reversed order supplies a
contextual identification without requiring an invented
independent doctrine for the compressed verse term.

**Purification governs both members.** The fourth
expression concerns the purification of equanimity and
mindfulness. Its denial must retain that qualification.
The second account's denial of equanimity itself is
a separately reported formulation.

**An elliptical report of opinion.** `Kecit` supplies
“some say.” The prose fills out the dual: pliancy is
absent in the first two defiled dhyānas, equanimity in
the third and fourth. These are not two extra items
to append indiscriminately to every preceding list.

**Close construe.** In the defiled dhyānas, respectively,
separation-born rapture and pleasure, inward clarity,
clear comprehension and mindfulness, and purification
of equanimity and mindfulness are absent. Some say
that pliancy and equanimity are absent, respectively
in the first pair and the last pair.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In the defiled [dhyānas], rapture-and-pleasure, clarity,
> clear comprehension and mindfulness, and purification
> of equanimity and mindfulness are absent. Some [say
> that] pliancy and equanimity [are absent].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Some explain the absences in this way. The defiled
> first dhyāna lacks rapture and pleasure born of
> separation, because it is not separated from defilements.
> The second lacks inward clarity, because defilements
> make it turbid. The third lacks mindfulness and clear
> comprehension, because defiled pleasure bewilders it.
> The fourth lacks purification of equanimity and
> mindfulness, because defilements stain it.
>
> Others say that the first and second defiled dhyānas
> lack pliancy, and the third and fourth lack equanimity,
> because these two factors belong to the class common
> to wholesome states.

Source: Pradhan 441|01–441|11, checked against the local
Bhāṣya study and running source. The following sentence
at 441|11–12 introduces the next verse's discussion of
movement and immovability.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An attainment's level does not alone determine the
> quality of its factors. Defilement makes a difference
> within the classification: separation-born joy cannot
> be asserted without separation, nor purified factors
> without their purification. A second account states
> the exclusions through the wholesome classification
> of pliancy and equanimity.

These two ways of explaining absence should remain
attributed. The passage does not adjudicate them here
or authorize a single combined subtraction from all
four factor inventories.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kliṣṭa`.** Defiled or afflicted. This is not simply
a synonym of sāsrava, contaminated. The earlier chapter
classification distinguishes wholesome contaminated
attainment from defiled attainment; that distinction
must survive the translation.

**`Vivekaja prītisukha`.** Rapture and pleasure born of
separation. The first explanation denies this qualified
factor because separation from defilements is lacking.
The qualification is essential to the inference.

**`Adhyātmasaṃprasāda`.** Inward clarity, identified with
confidence in VAK 8.09's stated account. Here defilement's
turbidity explains its absence in the second dhyāna.

**`Smṛtisaṃprajanya`.** Mindfulness and clear comprehension.
These are the third-dhyāna factors under discussion.
Their absence as the specified factors should not be
expanded into an assertion that a defiled mind has
no capacity to retain or discriminate anything.

**`Upekṣāsmṛtipariśuddhi`.** Purification of equanimity
and mindfulness. The prose expands the verse's śuddhi
as pariśuddhi. In the first reported account it is
this purification that the fourth defiled dhyāna lacks.

**`Kuśalamahābhūmika`.** Belonging to the class of mental
factors common to wholesome states. The second account
uses this doctrinal classification to explain why
pliancy and equanimity cannot occur in defiled states.
The pairing by dhyāna follows their relevant factor roles.

## 8. Logical Determination

**First reported account: level, absence, reason.**

| Defiled dhyāna | Absent factor or qualification | Reason in the prose |
|---|---|---|
| First | Separation-born rapture and pleasure | Non-separation from defilements |
| Second | Inward clarity | Turbidity through defilements |
| Third | Mindfulness and clear comprehension | Bewilderment through defiled pleasure |
| Fourth | Purification of equanimity and mindfulness | Staining through defilements |

The third explanation itself refers to defiled pleasure.
Consequently, denying the first's separation-born pleasure
must not become a universal denial of pleasure wherever
there is defilement.

**Second reported account: classification of the factors.**

| Defiled dhyānas | Factor specified as absent | Shared reason |
|---|---|---|
| First and second | Pliancy | It belongs to the wholesome class |
| Third and fourth | Equanimity | It belongs to the wholesome class |

VAK 8.09 identifies the first two dhyānas' sukha with
pliancy in its stated scheme. This makes the present
exclusion intelligible in relation to the earlier
inventory. The table does not imply that pliancy could
be defiled in another level: the stated reason concerns
its wholesome classification, while the distribution
concerns its role in these factor lists.

**Absence of purification and absence of a factor.**
The first account specifies the fourth's missing
purification; the second specifies missing equanimity.
The former statement alone does not settle whether
unpurified equanimity remains. Avoid filling that gap
by inference and then presenting it as an explicit
claim of the Bhāṣya.

**The attribution reaches backward.** After the first
four explanations, `evaṃ tāvat kecid āhuḥ` says that
some speak in this way. `Kecit punaḥ` then introduces
others. Vasubandhu frames and reports the discussion,
but the first list should not be presented as his
unqualified personal verdict against the second.

**No revised universal factor total.** The eighteen
named roles and eleven distinct dharmas of the preceding
verses answer inventory questions. This verse tests
specified factors against defiled attainment. It supplies
neither a new universal total nor a license to subtract
every mentioned absence from each dhyāna alike.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Read the qualification with the noun.** “Born of
separation” and “purification” are operative conditions,
not decorative additions. Retaining them prevents the
study from claiming more than the source denies.
Likewise, wholesome contaminated and defiled attainments
must not collapse into a single category through the
English word “contaminated.”

**Witness cautions.** The running text preserves
`smṛṭiḥ`, `ccaturthe`, and `kuśalalmahābhūmikatvāt`.
The local verse supplies smṛtiḥ; the prose's context
supports “fourth” and the kuśalamahābhūmika classification.
The transmitted `saṃbrhamitatvāt` is understood through
the contextual sense of bewilderment. The official
exposition explains these readings without changing
the research files or running source.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the relation between level,
factor, qualification, and mode of attainment. Yoga
Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns their effective
exercise. Here a level label alone cannot certify
the operation of the qualified factors associated
with that level's wholesome account.

For Organon representation, record both the scope of
an absence and the authority's account. “Purification
absent,” “factor absent,” and “factor not listed” are
different assertions. Preserving their difference allows
the source's causal explanations to remain intelligible.
This is a project reconstruction, not a source claim
about Organon or a new contemplative instruction.
