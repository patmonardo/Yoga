# VAK_3.06 Bhāṣya — Cognition-Stations and Sentient Abodes

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vijñānasthitayaḥ sapta śeṣaṃ tatparibhedavat /
> bhavāgrāsaṃjñisattvāś ca sattvāvāsā nava smṛtāḥ // 3.06 //

> The stations of differentiated cognition are seven; the remainder possesses
> conditions that disrupt it. Together with the peak of existence and the
> non-percipient beings, nine sentient abodes are traditionally recognized.

The Bhāṣya determines `paribheda` as that by which `vijñāna` is disrupted,
not as a neutral subdivision. It also distinguishes two scopes: all seven
cognition-stations are sentient abodes, but the peak of existence and the
non-percipient beings are abodes without being cognition-stations.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

There are seven stations of differentiated cognition.

What here constitutes a station of differentiated cognition? As appropriate
to the case, it is the five aggregates or the four aggregates connected with
that state.

Why is the remainder not a cognition-station? Because “the remainder
possesses conditions that disrupt it.” What is the remainder? The bad
destinies, the fourth dhyāna, and the peak of existence. Conditions that
disrupt differentiated cognition occur in these; for that very reason they
are not cognition-stations.

What is the disruption by which differentiated cognition is broken apart? In
the bad destinies, painful feeling is disruptive because of its injurious
character. In the fourth dhyāna, the non-percipient condition and the
attainment of non-perception are disruptive. At the peak of existence, the
attainment of cessation is disruptive because it cuts the continuity of
consciousness.

Another explanation is given. A cognition-station is said to be a state to
which those here wish to go and from which those already there do not wish to
depart. In the bad destinies neither condition is present. In the fourth
dhyāna, beings have unsettled intentions: ordinary persons wish to enter the
non-percipient state, while noble ones wish to enter the Pure Abodes. At the
peak of existence, differentiated cognition does not operate vigorously.
Consequently these are not cognition-stations.

These, then, are the seven cognition-stations described above. Together with
the peak of existence and the non-percipient beings, nine sentient abodes are
traditionally recognized, for beings dwell in them by their own inclination.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The unit makes three moves:

```text
define what constitutes a cognition-station
    ↓
explain why the remaining states are excluded
    ↓
place the seven inside a broader set of nine sentient abodes
```

The expository voice answers the constitutive question through the associated
aggregates. It then gives disruption as the first exclusion criterion.
`Punar āha`, “another explanation is given,” introduces a second criterion
based on willingness to enter and remain together with vigor of cognitive
operation. The source does not name a school or speaker for this alternative,
so the Study leaves it unattributed.

The final explanation of `sattvāvāsa` is brief and positive: these are modes
in which beings dwell willingly. The contrast with unwilling residence in the
bad destinies belongs to the next verse and is not imported into this unit.

## 5. What Constitutes a Cognition-Station

The question `kātra vijñānasthitiḥ` prevents “station” from being read merely
as a celestial address. The answer is:

```text
form-bearing cognition-station
    five associated aggregates

formless cognition-station
    four associated aggregates
```

The difference follows the presence or absence of the form aggregate. A
`vijñānasthiti` is therefore constituted by the aggregate-complex connected
with the corresponding mode, not by an independent place or substantial
inhabitant.

The term retains a functional force: it is a configuration in which
domain-differentiated cognition can stand or become established. This does not
make every occurrence of cognition a cognition-station. The passage
immediately tests the stability of that establishment through conditions of
disruption.

## 6. Three Modes of Disruption

The excluded remainder comprises three different cases:

| Excluded range | Disruptive condition | Bhāṣya's reason |
|---|---|---|
| bad destinies | painful feeling | its injurious character disrupts cognition |
| fourth dhyāna | non-percipient condition and attainment | perception and cognitive continuity are suspended |
| peak of existence | attainment of cessation | continuity of consciousness is cut |

`Paribheda` is thus not one uniform event. Pain may injure cognition,
non-perception may suspend its establishment, and cessation-attainment may
interrupt its continuity. What unifies these cases is their incompatibility
with the kind of stable establishment designated by `vijñānasthiti`.

The statement about the peak of existence must remain exact. The Bhāṣya does
not say that cognition is simply absent there. It says that cessation-
attainment can cut consciousness-continuity and, in the alternate account,
that cognition operates weakly or without vigor there.

## 7. The Alternative Criterion of Inclination

The second explanation defines a cognition-station conatively:

```text
beings elsewhere wish to enter it
    and
beings within it do not wish to leave it
```

The bad destinies fail both conditions. The fourth dhyāna contains divergent
aims rather than settled residence: ordinary beings seek the non-percipient
state, while noble ones seek the Pure Abodes. At the peak of existence,
cognitive operation lacks vigor.

This criterion should not be reduced to a popularity test. Desire to enter
and unwillingness to depart indicate a mode in which conditioned cognition
can take and retain standing. The account supplements the structural language
of disruption with the orientation of the beings inhabiting the state.

There is nevertheless a change of criterion between the two explanations.
The first asks what damages or interrupts cognition; the second asks whether
the state supports settled cognitive residence. The Bhāṣya records both
without explicitly deriving one from the other.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

`Vijñāna` retains the controlled rendering “differentiated cognition,” while
`sthiti` is standing or establishment rather than the spatial `sthāna` of VAK
3.02. “Cognition-station” preserves the compound without confusing it with a
physical station.

`Tatparibhedavat` means possessing what disrupts differentiated cognition.
The Bhāṣya's question `kaḥ punaḥ paribhedo yena vijñānaṃ paribhidyate` makes
the active force explicit. A rendering such as “the remainder has further
subdivisions” would miss the entire explanation.

`Bhavāgra` is the peak of conditioned existence, not an absolute beyond the
world-system. `Asaṃjñisattva` is rendered “non-percipient being” here because
the local discussion concerns the suspension of `saṃjñā`; it should not be
silently expanded into a universal claim about every sense of consciousness.

`Sattvāvāsa`, sentient abode, is broader than `vijñānasthiti`. The seven
cognition-stations plus the peak of existence and the non-percipient beings
make nine. Dwelling and cognitive establishment are therefore related but
non-identical classifications.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The modest conceptual result is a distinction between residence and the
stable establishment of cognition:

```text
sentient abode
    beings can dwell there

cognition-station
    differentiated cognition can take stable standing there
```

This sharpens the body–perception matrix of VAK 3.05. A configuration of body
and perception is not yet sufficient; the associated aggregate-field must
also support cognition against injury, suspension, or interruption.

For the Organon learning pass, this will matter because mere location of a
Rational Moral Agent does not establish its cognitive world. Its embodiment,
perceptual configuration, continuity, and conditions of stable cognition must
be distinguished. But those larger conclusions need not be developed during
the present translation pass.

The textual achievement is already precise: the Kośa distinguishes where
beings dwell from where differentiated cognition stands. It thereby analyzes
world not only as a distribution of inhabitants but as a field containing
different conditions of cognitive stability.
