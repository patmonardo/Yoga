# VAK_3.42 Bhāṣya — Mental Cognition at Death and Arising

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
chedasaṃdhāna vairāgyahānicyutyupapattayaḥ /
manovijñāna eveṣṭā upekṣāyāṃ cyutodbhavau // 3.42 //
```

> Cutting, reconnection, dispassion, decline, death, and rebirth are accepted
> only in mental differentiated cognition. Death and arising occur with
> equanimity.

The Bhāṣya expands the six compressed events, assigns them exclusively to
mental cognition, includes the linking of intermediate existence under
rebirth, and determines the feeling accompanying death and arising as
neither-painful-nor-pleasant.

```iast
manovijñāna eveṣṭāḥ.

```

The running witness has minor mechanical corruption in compounds and case
endings. The continuous text regularizes these provisionally. The opening
summary of persistence and death, and the exact force of `paṭu` and `apaṭu`,
require comparison with critical witnesses.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

It has been explained how dependent co-arising operates for sentient beings
and how they persist. Death through such causes as the exhaustion of lifespan
has also been explained.

This must now be stated: while which differentiated cognition is occurring do
death and rebirth take place? The answer is that cutting, reconnection,
dispassion, decline, death, and rebirth are accepted only in mental
differentiated cognition.

These are the cutting off of wholesome roots, the reconnection of wholesome
roots, dispassion from a realm or level, decline from an attainment, death,
and rebirth. These six dharmas are accepted only in mental differentiated
cognition and nowhere else.

Because rebirth is mentioned, the linking of intermediate existence described
above must also be understood as included.

As for feeling, death and arising occur with equanimity. `Cyuta` is death and
`udbhava` is rebirth. Both occur with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
because that feeling is non-acute. The other two feelings are acute, and death
and rebirth are not compatible with cognition in an acute affective state.

## 4. Scope and Natural Boundary

This unit asks a narrowly specified question:

```text
while which vijñāna is operative
do death and rebirth occur?
```

The answer first locates a class of six transformations exclusively in
`manovijñāna`, then adds the affective condition for the final two.

The next half-line in the running source—`naikāgrācittayor etau`—is the
opening of VAK 3.43. Although it continues the same death-and-rebirth argument,
it does not belong metrically to VAK 3.42. Accordingly, the restrictions on
concentrated and mindless states, and the arhat's final mind, are reserved for
the next Study.

No objector or separately named school speaks in this compact unit.

## 5. Six Transformations in Mental Cognition

The Bhāṣya resolves the compound into six exact events:

| Kārikā term | Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|
| `cheda` | cutting off the wholesome roots |
| `saṃdhāna` | reconnecting the wholesome roots |
| `vairāgya` | dispassion from a realm or meditative level |
| `hāni` | decline or falling away from an attainment |
| `cyuti` | death, falling from the present existence |
| `upapatti` | rebirth, establishment in another existence |

These are not six consecutive stages through which every continuum must pass.
They are six system-level changes sharing one cognitive locus:

```text
all six
    occur only in manovijñāna
    not in the five sensory cognitions
```

Mental differentiated cognition is therefore not merely a sixth sensory
channel. It is the locus in which ethical roots, attachment to a realm,
attainment, and embodiment can be reconfigured.

## 6. Intermediate Linking Is Included in Rebirth

The term `upapatti` includes:

```text
ordinary rebirth-linking
    and
antarābhava-pratisaṃdhi
    linking into intermediate existence
```

This carry-forward connects VAK 3.42 with the preceding identification of the
birth-seeking intermediate being. The transition into intermediate existence
is already a rebirth-linking event for purposes of the cognitive-locus rule.

The inclusion does not erase the distinction between intermediate emergence
and rebirth proper established in VAK 3.40. One general rule can cover both
events while their phases and fetters remain doctrinally distinct.

## 7. Death and Arising Occur with Neutral Feeling

The affective restriction is:

```text
cyuti + udbhava
    → aduḥkhāsukha-vedanā
      neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
    → upekṣā
```

The reason is compatibility of intensity:

```text
pleasant feeling and painful feeling
    = paṭu
      acute or forceful

neutral feeling
    = apaṭu
      non-acute

death and rebirth
    are not compatible with acute cognition
```

`Upekṣā` here is a technical feeling-tone, not necessarily cultivated moral
equanimity. The claim does not imply that the dying person's surrounding
experience is peaceful or that no painful conditions precede the terminal
mental event.

## 8. Locus and Condition Must Remain Distinct

The unit coordinates two independent determinations:

```text
cognitive locus
    manovijñāna

affective condition for death and arising
    aduḥkhāsukha-vedanā
```

The first applies to all six transformations; the second applies only to
death and arising. Thus it would be incorrect to infer that cutting wholesome
roots, reconnecting them, dispassion, or decline must all occur with neutral
feeling from this verse.

Likewise, the exclusive mental locus does not yet settle whether the mind is
concentrated, absent, wholesome, defiled, or indeterminate. Those further
constraints begin with VAK 3.43. The present Study should not import them
backward merely because the continuing prose discusses them.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

For the Organon, the six-event list identifies `manovijñāna` as the locus of
global reconfiguration rather than local sensory registration:

```text
sensory cognition
    registers a domain-specific presentation

mental differentiated cognition
    can alter ethical capacity
    release a realm or level
    lose an attainment
    terminate or establish embodiment
```

This is a source-grounded distinction. Describing it as “global
reconfiguration” is the Organon's reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's phrase.

The affective restriction adds a state-compatibility rule. A transition is
not specified solely by its operation name; the executing cognitive event
must occupy an admissible affective range. In an Ocular model, event type,
locus, and state condition therefore require separate fields.

The distinction also clarifies the relation between Outflow and Project.
Rebirth is a produced transition, but its decisive establishment occurs in a
mental event rather than in the sensory appearance of a new body. The
projection and production analyzed under nutriment culminate in a
reconfiguration whose locus is `manovijñāna`.

Finally, the verse is restrained. It does not yet describe death as
concentrated, mindless, or located in a bodily center. Those belong to the
next analysis. Preserving this boundary is itself a lesson for the Dataset:
semantic continuity across records does not authorize merging their distinct
claims.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete VAK 3.42 unit at 155.18–156.03: the question
of cognitive locus, the six explicitly expanded transformations, inclusion
of intermediate-existence linking, and the neutral feeling required for
death and arising.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The opening recap,
`kuśalamūlapratisaṃdhāna`, the scope of `dhātubhūmivairāgya`, and the
`paṭu`/`apaṭu` contrast require comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan,
Chinese, and established translations. The Study stops before the first
half-line of VAK 3.43 and does not absorb its concentrated-mind, mindless-state,
or arhat material.
