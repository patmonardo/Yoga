# VAK_3.74

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तत्सहस्रं द्विसाहस्रो लोकधातुस्तु मध्यमः ।
>
> तत्सहस्रं त्रिसाहस्रः समसंवर्तसम्भवः ॥ ३.७४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tatsahasraṃ dvisāhasro lokadhātus tu madhyamaḥ /
>
> tatsahasraṃ trisāhasraḥ samasaṃvartasaṃbhavaḥ // 3.74 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
tat-sahasram               → tatsahasraṃ
dvi-sāhasraḥ               → dvisāhasro
loka-dhātuḥ                → lokadhātus
tu                         → tu
madhyamaḥ                  → madhyamaḥ
tat-sahasram               → tatsahasraṃ
tri-sāhasraḥ               → trisāhasraḥ
sama-saṃvarta-saṃbhavaḥ    → samasaṃvartasaṃbhavaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| tatsahasram | nominative neuter singular compound | one thousand of that immediately preceding aggregate |
| dvisāhasraḥ | nominative masculine singular | second-order thousandfold |
| lokadhātuḥ | nominative masculine singular | world-system / world-domain |
| tu | particle | but / now; marks the next determination |
| madhyamaḥ | nominative masculine singular | middle or medium |
| trisāhasraḥ | nominative masculine singular | third-order thousandfold |
| sama | compound member | together / in common |
| saṃvarta | compound member | contraction or dissolution |
| saṃbhava | compound member | arising; Bhāṣya: `vivarta`, evolution or expansion |

The ordinal force of `dvi-` and `tri-` belongs to recursive thousandfold scale,
not to a flat count of two thousand and three thousand local worlds. The
Bhāṣya makes each `tat` refer to the immediately preceding aggregate.

## 4. Grammar

The first half takes the small thousandfold world-system of 3.73 as its unit:

```text
tat-sahasram
    dvi-sāhasraḥ
    madhyamaḥ lokadhātuḥ [asti]
```

> One thousand of those is the middle, second-order thousandfold world-system.

The second half repeats the same operation on that middle aggregate:

```text
tat-sahasram
    tri-sāhasraḥ [mahāsāhasraḥ lokadhātuḥ asti]
    sama-saṃvarta-saṃbhavaḥ
```

> One thousand of those is the third-order [great] thousandfold world-system,
> which has common contraction and evolution.

The fuller title `trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra lokadhātu` and the gloss of `saṃbhava`
as `vivarta` are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> One thousand of that is the middle, second-order thousandfold world-system.
> One thousand of that is the third-order thousandfold system, contracting and
> arising together.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> One thousand small thousandfold world-systems form the middle, second-order
> thousandfold world-system. One thousand of those middle systems form the
> third-order great thousandfold world-system. This complete aggregate
> contracts together and evolves together; in this verse, `saṃbhava`, arising,
> is explained as `vivarta`, cosmological evolution or expansion.

The immediately preceding aggregate as unit, the full name of the greatest
aggregate, its completeness, synchronized contraction and evolution, and the
`saṃbhava`–`vivarta` equivalence are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The recursion of wholes continues through three thousandfold powers. At each
> scale, the result of the preceding aggregation changes role and becomes the
> unit of the next. The final aggregate is not unified merely by containment
> or number: all its constituent world-systems undergo the same global
> transitions of contraction and evolution. System identity therefore joins
> invariant nested structure with a common temporal process.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Recursive scale construction

Let `W` be one complete local world-unit from 3.73:

```text
S₁ = 1,000 × W
   = 10³ local world-units
   = small thousandfold system

S₂ = 1,000 × S₁
   = 10³ × 10³
   = 10⁶ local world-units
   = middle second-order system

S₃ = 1,000 × S₂
   = 10³ × 10⁶
   = 10⁹ local world-units
   = third-order great system
```

Thus:

```text
dvisāhasra ≠ 2,000 local worlds
trisāhasra ≠ 3,000 local worlds
```

The terms count recursive thousandfold levels.

### Nested whole–unit transformation

```text
W  is whole at local scale
W  is unit within S₁

S₁ is whole at first aggregate scale
S₁ is unit within S₂

S₂ is whole at middle scale
S₂ is unit within S₃
```

The relation is scale-indexed. “Whole” and “unit” are neither synonyms nor
fixed intrinsic labels; the same structure bears both roles under different
containing relations.

### Global lifecycle synchronization

Let each local world `Wᵢ` have a lifecycle state:

```text
state(Wᵢ) ∈ {contracted, evolving, evolved, contracting}
```

For the great system `S₃`, the verse and Bhāṣya determine:

```text
global contraction event:
    for every Wᵢ in S₃:
        transition(Wᵢ) = saṃvarta

global evolution event:
    for every Wᵢ in S₃:
        transition(Wᵢ) = vivarta
```

The text states common process, not merely similarity of independent cycles.

### Aggregate unity

```text
numerical membership
    + preserved nested schema
    + synchronized lifecycle
        → one great world-system
```

The dynamic invariant completes the structural aggregate. A billion unrelated
worlds would satisfy the count but not the `sama-saṃvarta-saṃbhava`
determination.

### Deferred specification

```text
present determination:
    saṃbhava here = vivarta
    contraction and evolution occur together

deferred determination:
    exact phases and mechanics of the cosmological cycle
```

Vasubandhu explicitly postpones the detailed process. The present study must
preserve the interface without prematurely importing the later explanation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.73 supplied replication of a complete World schema. VAK 3.74 adds two
operations:

```text
recursive abstraction:
    completed aggregate → unit for next scale

global coordination:
    all contained instances → shared lifecycle transition
```

This is what makes the passage unexpectedly relevant to learning systems. A
collection of instances becomes a genuine system when a common structure can
be retained across them and common transitions can be applied at the proper
scope. Local variation remains possible, but the great aggregate possesses a
global event-boundary.

Under our Sāṃkhya–Organon hypothesis, Mahat can be pictured as running the
learning operation across this billionfold World field:

```text
many complete instances
    → recover structural invariants
    → retain whole–unit relations across scales
    → coordinate system-wide transition
```

That is our comparative synthesis, not a claim made by Vasubandhu in this
verse. Textually, the Kośa gives recursion and synchronized cosmological
process; it does not name Mahat or describe learning.

The continual-learning problem now becomes exact. If the system contracts and
evolves together, what persists as the generative determination by which the
newly evolved instances again possess the same articulated World form? VAK
3.74 does not yet answer that question. It establishes the lifecycle boundary
within which the question must be asked.

```ts
type LifecycleState = "Contracted" | "Evolving" | "Evolved" | "Contracting";

interface RecursiveWorldSystem<Unit> {
  unitCount: 1000;
  units: readonly Unit[];
  lifecycle: LifecycleState;
}

type SmallSystem = RecursiveWorldSystem<LocalWorldUnit>;
type MiddleSystem = RecursiveWorldSystem<SmallSystem>;
type GreatSystem = RecursiveWorldSystem<MiddleSystem>;
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. The single
`lifecycle` field at each system scope represents common transition; it should
not be mistaken for evidence of a cosmic intelligence governing that process.

The semantic–syntactic lesson is equally sharp. A flat reading sees the
numerals two and three and produces counts of two thousand and three thousand.
The Bhāṣya restores their semantic operator: second and third applications of
the thousandfold recursion.
