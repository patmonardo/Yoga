# VAK_3.21

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> पूर्वक्लेशा दशाविद्या संस्काराः पूर्वकर्मणः ।
>
> संधिस्कन्धास्तु विज्ञानं नामरूपमतः परम् ॥ ३.२१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pūrvakleśā daśāvidyā saṃskārāḥ pūrvakarmaṇaḥ /
>
> saṃdhiskandhās tu vijñānaṃ nāmarūpam ataḥ param // 3.21 //

The first pāda must be segmented with `daśā`, “state,” not `daśa`, “ten”:

```text
pūrva-kleśa-daśā + avidyā
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
pūrva-kleśā daśā        → pūrvakleśā daśā
avidyā                  → avidyā
saṃskārāḥ               → saṃskārāḥ
pūrva-karmaṇaḥ          → pūrvakarmaṇaḥ
saṃdhi-skandhāḥ         → saṃdhiskandhāḥ
tu                      → tu
vijñānam                → vijñānam
nāma-rūpam              → nāmarūpam
ataḥ param              → ataḥ param
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| pūrvakleśā daśā | nominative feminine singular phrase | the afflictive state of the former birth |
| avidyā | nominative feminine singular | ignorance; designation of that entire former afflictive state |
| saṃskārāḥ | nominative masculine plural | formations; designation of the former karmic state |
| pūrvakarmaṇaḥ | genitive neuter singular | of former karma |
| saṃdhiskandhāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | the five aggregates at the junction of rebirth |
| tu | adversative or transitional particle | but; now marks entry into the present-life section |
| vijñānam | nominative neuter singular | differentiated cognition; designation of the junction-aggregates |
| nāmarūpam | nominative neuter singular compound | name-and-form; the aggregate-stage following the junction |
| ataḥ | ablative singular pronoun used adverbially | from this; after this |
| param | nominative neuter singular adjective/adverbial expression | subsequent; beyond; afterward |

The Bhāṣya explicitly states `daśeti varttate`: “state” is carried forward into
the second definition. Thus `saṃskāra` designates the karmic state of the
former birth whose maturation occurs in the present birth.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The verse supplies four stage-definitions:

```text
pūrva-kleśa-daśā
    avidyā [ucyate]

pūrva-karmaṇaḥ [daśā]
    saṃskārāḥ [ucyante]

saṃdhi-skandhāḥ
    vijñānam [ucyante]

ataḥ param
    nāmarūpam [ucyate]
```

> The former afflictive state is called ignorance; the state of former karma
> is called formations. But the aggregates at the junction are called
> differentiated cognition; after this comes name-and-form.

The supplied research draft's “tenfold ignorance” is grammatically and
exegetically untenable. The Bhāṣya glosses `daśā` with `avasthā`, “state,” and
uses the same construction for `saṃskāra`.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The former afflictive state is ignorance; formations are the state of former
> karma. But the junction-aggregates are differentiated cognition; after this
> is name-and-form.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The entire afflictive condition of the prior birth is designated
> **ignorance**, because the other afflictions occur in association with and
> under its influence. The prior karmic condition whose result matures in the
> present birth is designated **formations**. At the moment of rebirth-linking
> in the mother's womb, all five aggregates are designated **differentiated
> cognition**; the aggregate-stage following that junction, until the arising
> of the six spheres, is designated **name-and-form**.

Association and predominance, the five aggregates at rebirth-linking, and the
six-sphere boundary of `nāmarūpa` are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Dependent origination does not consist here of isolated entities passing a
> causal signal along a line. One conditioned continuity differentiates into
> stages, and each concrete stage is named from its predominant determination.
> Prior affliction is gathered under ignorance; effective prior action under
> formations; the whole rebirth-junction under differentiated cognition; and
> the ensuing embodied development under name-and-form.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| daśā | state / condition | crucial reading; not the numeral “ten” |
| kleśāvasthā | afflictive state | Bhāṣya's referent for the ignorance-member |
| avidyā | ignorance | predominant condition naming the former afflictive state |
| sāhacarya | association / accompaniment | other afflictions accompany ignorance |
| samudācāra | active occurrence | other afflictions become active under ignorance's influence |
| karmāvasthā | karmic state | Bhāṣya's referent for the formations-member |
| saṃskāra | formations | former meritorious and other karma whose result matures here |
| vipāka | maturation / result | present maturation of former karma |
| saṃdhi | junction | transition into the present birth |
| pratisaṃdhikṣaṇa | moment of rebirth-linking | womb-entry moment at which five aggregates are present |
| saṃdhiskandha | junction-aggregates | all five aggregates at rebirth-linking |
| vijñāna | differentiated cognition | predominant determination naming the whole junction-stage |
| saṃdhicitta | linking consciousness | Bhāṣya term marking the temporal boundary before `nāmarūpa` |
| nāmarūpa | name-and-form | post-linking aggregate-stage before the six spheres arise |
| avasthā | stage / state | concrete temporal condition designated by a predominant factor |

`Vijñāna` is not translated as `jñāna` or achieved knowing. The Bhāṣya does not
isolate it as a self-standing traveler; it uses the predominant member to name
all five aggregates present at the junction.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Designation by predominance

The Bhāṣya's royal analogy supplies the semantic rule:

```text
“the king comes”
    includes the accompanying retinue

therefore:

the predominant determination
    names the entire concrete state
```

Applied to the first four members:

```text
former afflictive state
    → designated avidyā

former karmic state
    → designated saṃskāra

five aggregates at rebirth-junction
    → designated vijñāna

post-junction developmental aggregate-state
    → designated nāmarūpa
```

This anticipates the explicit stage-based determination of dependent
origination in 3.25.

### From inherited causality to embodiment

```text
avidyā
    former affliction as governing condition

        ↓ enables

saṃskāra
    former karmic formation capable of maturation

        ↓ matures as

vijñāna
    the five-aggregate junction designated by differentiated cognition

        ↓ develops as

nāmarūpa
    the embodied aggregate-stage before full sensory articulation
```

The World System therefore does not begin the present life with an already
finished organism. A causal inheritance acquires a junction, and that
junction unfolds into a differentiated embodied platform.

### What this verse does—and does not—say about transit

The Bhāṣya locates `vijñāna` at `pratisaṃdhi`, the moment of rebirth-linking.
But its subject at that moment is the five-aggregate complex collectively
designated from differentiated cognition. Therefore:

```text
3.21 establishes:
    a junctional function and stage-transition

3.21 does not by itself establish:
    an isolated consciousness-substance ejected from one body
    and inserted into another
```

This local determination neither proves nor disproves separate ascensional
techniques described in Yoga, Pāli materials, or later Buddhist traditions.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.21 begins the promised logical-scientific meditation on dependent
origination. The crucial operation is not upward movement through cosmic
stations but reflective differentiation of the process by which a present
embodiment obtains its footing.

```text
not merely:
    one thing causes another thing

but:
    one conditioned course
        differentiates into stages
        each named by its governing determination
```

This is where the World System becomes more than cosmography. The prior world
does not remain behind as a vanished location. Its afflictive and karmic
determinations are effective in the present as the conditions of a new
junction. The Middle is Dharma functioning as transition: inherited action
reaches maturation, differentiated cognition names the rebirth-link, and
name-and-form begins the construction of an embodied cognition-platform.

For our Darśana/Jñāna investigation, restraint is essential. The verse gives a
rebirth-junction, not a complete theory of Ascension Dhyāna. It tells us how
Vasubandhu analyzes the transition once it is placed inside dependent
origination. Comparative claims about ejecting consciousness, touching the
Deathless, and suprasensuous ascent must remain separate witnesses until the
Kośa supplies the relevant operations.

What the verse does establish is philosophically formidable: `vijñāna` is not
the achieved comprehension of the World System. It is a conditioned footing
within that system. Jñāna must therefore be sought at another logical level:
not merely as a link undergoing rebirth, but as knowing capable of
comprehending the entire linkage.
