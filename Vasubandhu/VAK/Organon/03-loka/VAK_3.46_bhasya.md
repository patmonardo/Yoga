# VAK_3.46 Bhāṣya — Water, Containment, and the Golden Earth

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
apām ekādaśodvedhaṃ sahasrāṇi ca viṃśatiḥ /
aṣṭalakṣaucchrayaṃ paścāc cheṣaṃ bhavati kāñcanam // 3.46 //
```

> The water-maṇḍala has a vertical extent of eleven hundred-thousand plus
> twenty thousand yojanas. Afterwards, eight hundred-thousand yojanas of it
> become gold, leaving a remainder.

The Bhāṣya supplies the formation of the waters, asks why they do not disperse
laterally, preserves two explanatory positions, and resolves the compressed
arithmetic: 800,000 yojanas become golden earth and 320,000 yojanas of water
remain.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Tasyopariṣṭāt:

apām ekādaśodvedhaṃ sahasrāṇi ca viṃśatiḥ.

Maṇḍalam iti vartate. Tasmin vāyumaṇḍale sattvānāṃ karmabhir meghāḥ
saṃbhūyākṣamātrābhir dhārābhir abhivarṣanti. Tad bhavaty apāṃ maṇḍalam.
Tasya yojanānām ekādaśa lakṣāṇy ūrdhvād adho, viṃśatiś ca sahasrāṇi.

Kathaṃ tā āpo na tiryaṅ visravanti? Sattvānāṃ karmādhipatyena. Yathā hi
bhuktapītam annapānaṃ nāpakvaṃ pakvāśayam āpatatīty eke. Kusūlanyāyena
vāyunā saṃdhāryanta iti nikāyāntarīyāḥ.

Tāś ca punar āpaḥ sattvānāṃ karmaprabhāvasaṃbhūtair vāyubhir āvartyamānā
upariṣṭāt kāñcanībhavanti, pakvakṣīraśarībhāvayogena. Tad bhavaty apāṃ
maṇḍalam:

aṣṭalakṣaucchrayaṃ paścāc cheṣaṃ bhavati kāñcanam.

Kiṃ ca śeṣam? Trayo lakṣāḥ, sahasrāṇi ca viṃśatiḥ. Sā kāñcanamayī mahī
bhavaty apām upariṣṭāt. Ukto jalakāñcanamaṇḍalocchrāyaḥ.
```

The running witness contains evident mechanical damage, including
`tat bhavatyaṣāṃ`, `laklṣāḥ`, and a compressed milk-skin comparison. The
continuous text makes provisional spacing and sandhi repairs sufficient for
readability; it is not offered as a critical edition. The exact form of
`pakvakṣīraśarībhāva` especially requires control against other witnesses.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Above it, the water-maṇḍala has a vertical extent of eleven
hundred-thousand plus twenty thousand yojanas. The word “maṇḍala” is carried
forward from the preceding verse.

Upon that wind-maṇḍala, clouds form through the karma of beings and pour down
rain in streams the size of an axle. From this the water-maṇḍala comes to be.
Its vertical extent from top to bottom is eleven hundred-thousand plus twenty
thousand yojanas.

Why do those waters not flow away laterally? Because of the governing
efficacy of the karma of beings. Some say that it is just as eaten food and
drink do not enter the lower digestive tract undigested. Teachers of another
school say that the waters are held together by wind, according to the
analogy of a granary or enclosing storehouse.

Those waters are then turned by winds produced through the power of the karma
of beings. Their upper portion becomes golden, like the formation of a skin
upon cooked milk. Thus the maṇḍala of the waters becomes, in its upper part,
gold to a height of eight hundred-thousand yojanas, with water remaining.

And how much remains? Three hundred-thousand plus twenty thousand yojanas.
The golden earth lies above those remaining waters. The vertical extent of
the water-and-gold maṇḍala has now been stated.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voices

The commentary turns a compressed dimensional statement into a process:

```text
clouds generated through beings' karma
    → axle-sized streams of rain
        → formation of the water-maṇḍala

question of lateral dispersal
    → karmic governance, illustrated by digestion
    → alternative: enclosing wind, illustrated by a granary

karma-generated winds turn the waters
    → upper water becomes golden earth
        → lower water remains
```

Three voices must be distinguished:

- Vasubandhu presents the construction and its coherence question;
- `eke`, “some,” give the digestive analogy under the karmic-governance
  answer;
- the `nikāyāntarīyāḥ`, teachers of another school, explain containment by
  wind through the granary analogy.

The local passage reports the two explanatory forms without an explicit
final adjudication between them.

## 5. Formation and Exact Measure

The water-domain does not simply appear as a layer already in place. Its
formation has a stated sequence:

```text
karma of beings
    → clouds form over the wind-maṇḍala
        → rain falls in immense streams
            → water is gathered as a maṇḍala
```

The Bhāṣya also disambiguates the first half-verse. Its measure is:

```text
11 lakṣas + 20,000 yojanas
    = 1,120,000 yojanas
```

`Ūrdhvād adhaḥ`, from top to bottom, confirms that this is the vertical depth
of the original water-maṇḍala. It is not the lateral measure and not the
combined height of separately added water and gold.

The word `maṇḍala` is supplied from the preceding construction. This is a
small but important grammatical determination: the genitive `apām`, “of the
waters,” qualifies an understood maṇḍala rather than standing as an isolated
mass noun.

## 6. Why the Waters Do Not Disperse

The question `kathaṃ tā āpo na tiryaṅ visravanti?` tests the internal
coherence of the model. A vast water-domain situated above wind appears to
require an explanation for its lateral containment.

The immediate answer is the same causal category used for the formation of
the wind support:

```text
sattvānāṃ karmādhipatya
    → the waters remain in their ordered configuration
```

Some teachers illustrate this through digestion: consumed food and drink do
not simply pass undigested into the lower digestive tract. The comparison
does not identify world-water with bodily fluid. It exhibits an ordered
process in which material does not move merely according to its immediately
apparent direction.

The teachers of another school provide a more explicitly mediating mechanism:

```text
wind encloses or sustains the waters
    as a granary contains what is stored within it
```

`Karmādhipatya` and wind-enclosure should not be silently fused. The first
names the governing causal efficacy; the second identifies a supporting
physical configuration. The Bhāṣya preserves their difference even though
the broader account can relate karmic power and wind.

## 7. Transformation into Golden Earth

The golden ground is generated from the water rather than placed upon it as
an independently introduced substance:

```text
power of beings' karma
    → winds arise
        → winds turn the water-maṇḍala
            → upper water becomes golden
```

The milk comparison indicates a differentiated upper layer: as a skin or
film forms upon cooked milk, the upper waters acquire a new determination.
Because the Sanskrit expression is damaged or compressed, “skin upon cooked
milk” is a provisional rendering of the evident general comparison.

The resulting `kāñcanamayī mahī`, golden earth, is therefore both continuous
with and distinct from the water-domain. It arises by transformation of its
upper portion and then functions as the ground above the water that remains.

This is not elemental creation ex nihilo. The commentary presents an
operation upon an already formed support, mediated once again by winds whose
own arising is attributed to karmic power.

## 8. The Arithmetic of Transformation and Remainder

The Bhāṣya resolves the difficult `śeṣa` construction through explicit
subtraction:

| Determination | Vertical extent |
|---|---:|
| original water-maṇḍala | 1,120,000 yojanas |
| upper portion transformed into gold | 800,000 yojanas |
| water remaining below | 320,000 yojanas |

Thus:

```text
1,120,000
    = 800,000 transformed
    + 320,000 remaining
```

A translation that calls the 800,000-yojana golden portion itself “the
remainder” obscures the prose explanation. The commentary asks `kiṃ ca
śeṣam?`, “And what remains?”, and answers with three lakṣas plus twenty
thousand. The remaining water is 320,000 yojanas deep.

The closing phrase `jalakāñcanamaṇḍalocchrāya` treats the resulting structure
as a water-and-gold maṇḍala. Transformation differentiates the whole without
destroying its measured continuity.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

VAK 3.46 gives the container-world an operational grammar:

```text
formation
    → containment
        → transformation
            → differentiated support
```

The passage is more than a stack of cosmological substances. Each support
has a genesis; the stability of the water becomes an explicit problem; rival
accounts of that stability are preserved; and a new ground is produced by a
measured transformation of the preceding domain.

For the Organon Dataset, at least three explanatory levels must remain
distinct:

```text
governing condition
    karmic efficacy of beings

mediating process
    clouds, rain, and karma-generated winds

resulting configuration
    contained water plus transformed golden earth
```

This is an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's own formal vocabulary.
It avoids reducing `karma` to a substitute name for a physical mechanism,
while also avoiding a world-model in which karmic efficacy eliminates the
need to describe formation, support, and transformation.

The reciprocal structure is now visible but should remain source-bounded:
the karma of sentient beings conditions the common container, and that
container supplies the objective supports of embodied existence. The passage
does not say that an individual projects a private world, nor does it derive
space itself from karma.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete VAK 3.46 unit at 158.04–158.15: formation
and measurement of the water-maṇḍala, the problem of lateral dispersal, the
karmic and Nikāyāntarīya containment explanations, the action of
karma-generated winds, transformation of the upper waters into golden earth,
and the exact remaining water depth.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The corrupt `tat
bhavatyaṣāṃ` readings, the digestive syntax, the granary comparison, and
especially the cooked-milk-skin expression require comparison with critical
Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations. The numerical
relation itself is explicit in the prose and comparatively secure.

The Study stops with the stated height of the combined water-and-gold
maṇḍala. It does not begin VAK 3.47 or its determination of the maṇḍala's
breadth.
