# VAK_3.11

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> व्रीहिसन्तानसाधर्म्यादविच्छिन्नभवोद्भवः ।
>
> प्रतिबिम्बमसिद्धत्वादसाम्याच्चानिदर्शनम् ॥ ३.११ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vrīhisantānasādharmyād avicchinnabhavodbhavaḥ /
>
> pratibimbam asiddhatvād asāmyāc cānidarśanam // 3.11 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vrīhi-santāna-sādharmyāt  → vrīhisantānasādharmyāt
avicchinna-bhava-udbhavaḥ  → avicchinnabhavodbhavaḥ
pratibimbam                → pratibimbam
asiddhatvāt                → asiddhatvāt
asāmyāt + ca               → asāmyāc ca
ca + anidarśanam           → cānidarśanam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| vrīhi | masculine noun in compound | rice; the generative rice-series |
| santāna | masculine noun in compound | continuity; serial continuum |
| sādharmyāt | ablative singular of sādharmya | because of relevant similarity |
| avicchinna | adjective in compound | uninterrupted; not severed |
| bhava | masculine noun in compound | existence; becoming |
| udbhavaḥ | nominative masculine singular | arising; origination |
| pratibimbam | nominative neuter singular | reflected image |
| asiddhatvāt | ablative singular abstract noun | because of being unestablished |
| asāmyāt | ablative singular | because of dissimilarity |
| anidarśanam | nominative neuter singular | not a probative example |

The two ablatives in the second half state independent defects: reflection is
not established in the manner required, and—even if provisionally granted—it
lacks the relevant similarity.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first half gives the positive inference:

```text
vrīhi-santāna-sādharmyāt
    avicchinnaḥ bhavasya udbhavaḥ [asti]
```

> Because of its relevant similarity to the continuity of rice, the arising
> of existence is uninterrupted.

The second half rejects a proposed counterexample:

```text
pratibimbam
    asiddhatvāt
    asāmyāt ca
    anidarśanam [asti]
```

> A reflected image is not a valid example, because it is unestablished and
> dissimilar.

The Bhāṣya supplies the dialectical setting. An opponent proposes reflection
as a case of appearance at another place without continuous succession.
Vasubandhu denies that it proves the required discontinuity.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Because of similarity to the continuity of rice, the arising of existence
> is uninterrupted. A reflected image is not a valid example, because it is
> unestablished and dissimilar.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Dharmas belonging to a continuity appear elsewhere through an uninterrupted
> series, as does the continuity of rice; so too does a sentient continuity.
> Reflection cannot establish rebirth directly across a break: its status as a
> separately arisen dharma is unproved, and its causal and serial structure is
> unlike the transition from death to rebirth.

The application to a sentient continuity and the detailed defects of the
reflection example are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System requires an unsevered generative series. A successor
> existence arises as one member of a rice-series conditions another. A
> reflection cannot model the process, because it is
> neither established as an independently generated successor nor related to
> its source as a member of one serial continuum.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Positive model: generative continuity

```text
rice member₁
    causally conditions
rice member₂
    causally conditions
rice member₃

different members
    + one uninterrupted generative series
```

By relevant similarity:

```text
death-existence
    → intermediate existence
    → rebirth-existence

successive configurations
    + one conditioned santāna
```

The inference concerns the structure of succession, not the biological matter
of rice.

### Proposed countermodel: reflection

```text
original image
    -- apparent production at a distance -->
reflection
```

The opponent offers this as evidence that rebirth could arise directly from
death across a discontinuity. Vasubandhu gives two levels of refusal.

First, `asiddhatva`:

```text
the reflection's status
    as a separately arisen dharmāntara
    is not established
```

Its appearance depends upon a causal complex whose differentiated powers must
be analyzed; it cannot simply be counted as the required instance.

Second, `asāmya`, even if such arising is granted:

```text
reflection
    does not belong to the original's santāna
    coexists with the original
    depends upon original + mirror

rebirth-existence
    succeeds death-existence
    does not coexist with it as its image
    is not produced by death + an analogous second principal support
```

Therefore reflection is `anidarśana`: it cannot prove discontinuous rebirth.

### Scope of the conclusion

The Bhāṣya concludes:

```text
discontinuous arising of rebirth from death
    is not accepted by yukti

therefore
    antarābhava is rationally required
```

This completes the reasoning branch locally. The `āgama` branch announced in
VAK 3.10 remains to follow.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.11 is a compact specification of generative continuity. The World System
does not permit an unexplained jump between disconnected embodiments. It
executes a lawful successor relation among configurations:

```text
discontinuous apparition
    rejected as unproved

santāna
    serial causal mediation
```

This is Transcendental Technē in the project's sense. The subtle transition
process hidden from ordinary Agents is now constrained by a testable formal
requirement: a later station must be generated within a continuity, not merely
displayed as though in a mirror.

The contrast also clarifies the difference between representation and
generation. A reflection can present the look of a source elsewhere while
remaining dependent upon a simultaneous source and another support. A new
embodied cognition-station, by contrast, must be the successor produced by
the operative continuity after the former station has ended.

```text
reflection
    simultaneous presentation through a supporting complex

rebirth
    diachronic production of a successor configuration
```

Rudra's work is therefore not cosmetic image-manipulation. In our project
language, withdrawal and renewed embodiment concern the generative law of the
continuity itself. Yet the Kārikā supplies no free Rudra-operator: its explicit
principles are `santāna`, uninterrupted arising, and the rational rejection of
an inadequate causal model.

The dinner joke now acquires its exact limit. The steak's decomposition shows
that no configuration secures its own persistence; but mere corruption does
not explain the successor-series. VAK 3.11 adds the stronger determination:
transformation must be articulated through lawful continuity among successive
dharmas.
