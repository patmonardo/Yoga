# VAK_3.23

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> वित्तिः प्राक् मैथुनात् तृष्णा भोगमैथुनरागिणः ।
>
> उपादानं तु भोगानां प्राप्तये परिधावतः ॥ ३.२३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vittiḥ prāk maithunāt tṛṣṇā bhogamaithunarāgiṇaḥ /
>
> upādānaṃ tu bhogānāṃ prāptaye paridhāvataḥ // 3.23 //

The Sanskrit witness reads `vittiḥ`. The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies it here
as the `vedanā-avasthā`, the stage of feeling.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vittiḥ                  → vittiḥ
prāk                    → prāk
maithunāt               → maithunāt
tṛṣṇā                   → tṛṣṇā
bhoga-maithuna-rāgiṇaḥ  → bhogamaithunarāgiṇaḥ
upādānam                → upādānam
tu                      → tu
bhogānām                → bhogānām
prāptaye                → prāptaye
paridhāvataḥ            → paridhāvataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| vittiḥ | nominative feminine singular | feeling; affective experience, glossed as `vedanā` by the Bhāṣya |
| prāk | indeclinable governing the ablative | before; until prior to |
| maithunāt | ablative neuter singular | before sexual coupling or the activation of sexual passion |
| tṛṣṇā | nominative feminine singular | thirst; craving |
| bhogamaithunarāgiṇaḥ | genitive masculine singular compound | of one passionately attached to enjoyment and sexual coupling |
| upādānam | nominative neuter singular | appropriation; grasping that has entered acquisition-oriented pursuit |
| tu | transitional/adversative particle | but; now distinguishes appropriation from craving |
| bhogānām | genitive masculine plural | of enjoyments |
| prāptaye | dative feminine singular | for obtaining; for acquisition |
| paridhāvataḥ | genitive masculine singular present participle | of one running about in pursuit |

The genitives in the latter two definitions qualify the person whose concrete
developmental state receives the name `tṛṣṇā` or `upādāna`.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The verse defines three successive stages:

```text
vittiḥ
    prāk maithunāt

tṛṣṇā
    bhoga-maithuna-rāgiṇaḥ

upādānam tu
    bhogānāṃ prāptaye
    paridhāvataḥ
```

> Feeling extends until sexual passion becomes active. Craving is [the state]
> of one passionately attached to enjoyment and sexual coupling. But
> appropriation is [the state] of one running about to obtain enjoyments.

The Kārikā says `prāk maithunāt`, literally “before coupling.” The Bhāṣya
defines the boundary more precisely as the active manifestation
(`samudācāra`) of `maithuna-rāga`, sexual passion.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Feeling is prior to sexual coupling; craving belongs to one passionately
> attached to enjoyment and coupling. Appropriation, however, belongs to one
> running about to obtain enjoyments.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Once affective discrimination has become possible, the developmental stage
> of **feeling** continues until sexual passion becomes active. The stage in
> which passion for sensual enjoyment and sexual coupling is active is called
> **craving**, and it continues until search for the desired object begins.
> When activity becomes organized around searching everywhere to obtain that
> object, the stage is called **appropriation**.

The identification of `vittiḥ` with `vedanā`, the manifestation of passion,
and the boundary marked by active search are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The contacted World acquires practical force in three stages. First the
> object is felt; then it becomes desired; finally it is posited as something
> to be obtained, and activity is reorganized around its acquisition. Feeling
> becomes craving when passion becomes operative. Craving becomes
> appropriation when orientation toward the object becomes purposeful search.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| vitti | feeling | Kārikā form explicitly identified with the `vedanā` stage |
| vedanāvasthā | stage of feeling | affective determination after the capacity for discrimination has arisen |
| paricchedasāmarthya | capacity for discrimination | inherited from 3.22; condition for the feeling-stage |
| maithuna | sexual coupling | developmental threshold named by the Kārikā |
| rāga | passion / attachment | active affective fixation upon enjoyment |
| samudācāra | active manifestation | coming into operation of passion, marking craving |
| kāmaguṇa | sensual enjoyment | field toward which craving is directed |
| tṛṣṇā | craving / thirst | stage of operative passion before acquisition-oriented search begins |
| paryeṣṭi | search / seeking | new practical operation marking the passage to appropriation |
| upādāna | appropriation | acquisition-oriented pursuit, not merely stronger craving |
| viṣaya | object / cognitive field | what becomes desired and then sought for acquisition |
| viṣayaprāpti | acquisition of the object | end organizing appropriative pursuit |
| paridhāvana | running about / pursuit | practical activity directed everywhere toward acquisition |

The Bhāṣya's boundaries are exact:

```text
feeling without operative passion
    = vedanā

operative passion without active search
    = tṛṣṇā

active search directed toward acquisition
    = upādāna
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### From affect to conditioned teleology

```text
sparśa
    actual contact

        ↓ is felt as

vedanā
    affective determination

        ↓ passion becomes operative

tṛṣṇā
    motivational orientation

        ↓ search begins

upādāna
    acquisition-oriented practical activity
```

The transition from `tṛṣṇā` to `upādāna` is qualitative, not merely an
increase in intensity. A new operation appears:

```text
object as desired
    → object as end to be obtained
    → field of activity organized as search
```

### “All object” before and through appropriation

At 3.22, faculty, cognitive field, and differentiated cognition could be
reflected upon as determinate contents—“all object”—without that inventory
itself constituting contact. Their actual conjunction generated `sparśa`.
VAK 3.23 now follows what becomes of the contacted object:

```text
object encountered
    → object felt
    → object desired
    → object pursued
```

No independently finished empirical subject needs to be inserted into the
Kārikā's primary account. The verse analyzes the progressive determination of
the conditioned course through affect, motivation, and pursuit.

### Conditioned purpose is not freedom

`Upādāna` introduces teleological organization: activity has an end,
`viṣayaprāpti`. But having an end does not make the activity rationally free.
Here its end is supplied by craving.

```text
teleological activity
    under tṛṣṇā
    = conditioned acquisition
```

This distinction will matter when the Path determines an end through knowing
rather than through appropriation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.23 shows the World System entering agency. The World is no longer merely
accessible through spheres or actual in contact. It now matters affectively,
motivates orientation, and organizes conduct.

```text
World as contacted
    → World as felt
    → World as wanted
    → World as something to be seized
```

This is a powerful specification of how the World is established in Me. The
object is not simply projected once and left standing. It is continually
differentiated according to the operative relation: first as felt content,
then as desirable content, then as an end governing a practical field. This
Yogācāra assimilation is our philosophical reconstruction; Vasubandhu's
primary doctrine remains the developmental analysis of aggregate-stages.

The sequence also explains why mere contact does not yet make an agent.
Agency becomes increasingly determinate as affect acquires motivational and
practical form. Yet the first practical agent disclosed here is not free. It
runs everywhere because its field has been organized by craving.

That is the beginning of karmic reconstruction. Prior karma produced the
present cognition-platform; the present platform now converts encountered
objects into ends of appropriation. VAK 3.24 can therefore show how the
activity accumulated in this pursuit becomes the `bhava` that projects
another birth.
