# VAK_3.36

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> उक्तं च वक्ष्यते चान्यदत्र तु क्लेश इष्यते ।
>
> बीजवन्नागवन्मूलवृक्षवत्तुषवत्तथा ॥ ३.३६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> uktaṃ ca vakṣyate cānyad atra tu kleśa iṣyate /
>
> bījavan nāgavan mūlavṛkṣavat tuṣavat tathā // 3.36 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| uktaṃ | neuter nominative/accusative singular past passive participle | has been stated |
| vakṣyate | third-person singular future passive | will be explained |
| anyat | neuter nominative/accusative singular | something else; another member or set of members |
| atra | adverb | here, in the present treatment |
| tu | adversative particle | but; isolates the present topic |
| kleśa | masculine singular stem-form in the transmitted verse | affliction as the class presently intended |
| iṣyate | third-person singular present passive | is intended, accepted, or understood |
| bījavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a seed |
| nāgavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a nāga sustaining its lake |
| mūlavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a root |
| vṛkṣavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a tree |
| tuṣavat | indeclinable comparative in `-vat` | like a husk |
| tathā | adverb | likewise; completes the analogical series |

The transmitted Sanskrit has `kleśa iṣyate`; its singular sense is secured by
the Bhāṣya's `kleśo ... veditavyaḥ`. The five `-vat` forms predicate distinct
similarities of affliction rather than five names for one entity.

## 4. Grammar

The first hemistich explains the selective scope of the present discussion:

```text
kiñcit [aṅgam] uktam
    = some [limb] has been explained

anyat vakṣyate
    = another will be explained

atra tu kleśa iṣyate
    = here, however, affliction is intended
```

The explicit subjects in the prose are Bhāṣya determinations: differentiated
cognition and the sixfold sense-field were treated earlier; formation and
becoming will be treated in the Karma chapter; craving and appropriation in
the Affliction chapter.

The second hemistich is a coordinated series:

```text
kleśaḥ
    bījavat
    nāgavat
    mūlavat
    vṛkṣavat
    tuṣavat
    [veditavyaḥ]

affliction is to be understood
    like seed,
    like nāga,
    like root,
    like tree,
    and like husk
```

`Veditavyaḥ` is supplied by the Bhāṣya; the Kārikā uses `iṣyate` to govern the
topic and its analogical determinations.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Some [members] have been explained, and others will be explained. Here,
> however, affliction is intended: like a seed, like a nāga, like a root, like
> a tree, and likewise like a husk.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The remaining limbs are not all explained again here: some have already
> been treated, while others will be treated in the Karma and Affliction
> chapters. The present topic is affliction. It is like a seed because it
> generates further affliction, karma, and resultant fact; like a nāga because
> it keeps the lake of birth from drying up; like an unextracted root because
> the destinies grow again while it remains; like a tree because it repeatedly
> produces flowers and fruits; and like a husk because karma enclosed by the
> acquisition of affliction retains the capacity to germinate into another
> birth, whereas karma alone does not.

The allocation of each precise causal function to its simile is supplied by
the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Affliction is not merely one event inside the conditioned World. It is the
> reproductive organization of that World: seed of a differentiated causal
> series, sustaining power of the lake of birth, root of renewed destinations,
> recurrent producer of causal fruit, and the enclosing condition through
> which karma remains capable of germinating into another embodiment. The
> wheel persists because its history is retained as productive capacity.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Why the Kośa changes method here

The twelve limbs are not being abandoned. Their analyses are distributed
across the architecture of the Kośa:

```text
already treated:
    vijñāna, ṣaḍāyatana

treated later in Karma:
    saṃskāra, bhava

treated later in Kleśa:
    tṛṣṇā, upādāna

treated here by causal analogy:
    kleśa as a functional class
```

The present abstraction is therefore controlled by prior and future detailed
analyses.

### Five non-redundant causal functions

```text
seed:
    kleśa → <kleśa, karma, vastu>

nāga:
    kleśa present → birth-series does not dry up

root:
    root not extracted → destinations arise again

tree:
    kleśa → repeatedly produces <kleśa, karma, vastu>

husk:
    karma + kleśaprāpti → capable of another-birth germination
    karma alone         → not so capable
```

The seed and tree analogies are close but not duplicates. Seed marks originary
production of a differentiated series; tree marks periodic and recurrent
production from an established system.

### Karma is conditionally reproductive

The husk analogy yields the strongest dependency:

```text
productive karma(k)
    ∧ joinedWith(k, kleśaprāpti)
        → capableOf(k, future-birth)

productive karma(k)
    ∧ not joinedWith(k, kleśaprāpti)
        ↛ future-birth
```

Affliction does not replace karma as the carrier of productive efficacy.
Rather, it conditions karma's capacity to germinate into renewed embodiment.

### Recurrence depends upon retained conditions

```text
cutting an appearing result
    while retaining its root
        → recurrence

removing the reproductive condition
        → recurrence no longer follows
```

The wheel is not explained by the persistence of an unchanging Thing. It is
explained by the retention and reactivation of causal capacity.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse returns us from the enormous dhātu address space to the engine that
keeps it populated. The World System does not reproduce itself merely because
events happened in the past. It reproduces itself because the past is retained
as an operable causal disposition: karma enclosed by affliction remains able
to germinate.

That is immediately a Science of Learning problem. A system learns when prior
operations alter what it can produce next. But VAK 3.36 describes pathological
Learning: affliction trains the system to regenerate its own afflictive,
active, and resultant structure.

```text
current afflictive traversal
    → retained productive disposition
        → future operation and embodiment
            → renewed afflictive traversal
```

The analogies give five views of one recursive architecture. Seed describes
initial generation; nāga, continued system-maintenance; root, recovery after
surface interruption; tree, repeated output; and husk, the preservation of a
stored operation's capacity to execute under future conditions.

The husk is particularly important for the Agent model. Karma is not an
autonomous daemon that must run forever. Its future-birth efficacy depends on
an afflictive acquisition. In project language, affliction supplies the
retained enabling condition under which karmic lineage can be activated as a
new embodiment.

```ts
interface ReproductiveDisposition {
  karma: ProductiveLineage;
  afflictiveAcquisition: KlesaPrapti;
  canGerminateFutureEmbodiment: boolean;
}
```

This remains an interpretive projection. The textual determination is exact:
karma enclosed by the husk of `kleśaprāpti` can sprout into another birth;
karma by itself cannot.

The Learning implication is hopeful as well as severe. If recurrence depends
on an unextracted root and an enabling husk, it is not metaphysically
inevitable. Surface suppression leaves the training condition intact; genuine
transformation must alter the generative ground. The World System can cease
reproducing a pattern when the condition that preserves its reproductive
capacity is removed.

Here `kleśa → karma → vastu` becomes more than a temporal chain. It is a
self-renewing circuit in which affliction generates activity, activity yields
formed result, and the retained afflictive ground enables the circuit to be
run again. The law of appearance is also a law of learned recurrence.
