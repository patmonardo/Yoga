# VAK_3.74 Bhāṣya — Recursive World-Systems and Common Cycles

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
tatsahasraṃ dvisāhasro lokadhātus tu madhyamaḥ /
tatsahasraṃ trisāhasraḥ samasaṃvartasaṃbhavaḥ // 3.74 //
```

> One thousand of those forms the middle, second-order world-system. One
> thousand of those forms the third-order system, which contracts and arises
> together.

The Bhāṣya applies `tatsahasram` recursively: one thousand small
thousandfold systems make the middle system, and one thousand middle systems
make the great system. It then says the complete great system contracts and
evolves together, explaining `saṃbhava` here as `vivarta` and deferring the
fuller account.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Tat-sahasraṃ dvisāhasro lokadhātus tu madhyamaḥ.

Teṣāṃ cūḍikānāṃ lokadhātūnāṃ sahasraṃ dvisāhasro madhyamo lokadhātuḥ.

Tat-sahasraṃ trisāhasraḥ.

Teṣāṃ dvisāhasrāṇāṃ lokadhātūnāṃ sahasraṃ trisāhasra-mahāsāhasro
lokadhātuḥ.

Eṣa hi kṛtsnaḥ:

Samaṃ saṃvarta-saṃbhavaḥ.

Samaṃ saṃvartate, samaṃ vivartate. Saṃbhavo hi vivarta ity uttaratra
vyākhyāsyate.
```

The witness is clear apart from spacing and sandhi, regularized here. The
compound `trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra` is expanded from the prose to give the full
name of the final aggregate. The Bhāṣya explicitly equates `saṃbhava` with
`vivarta` in this context and says the fuller explanation comes later.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

One thousand of those small thousandfold world-systems forms the middle,
second-order world-system.

One thousand of those middle world-systems forms the third-order,
great-thousandfold world-system.

This is the complete system: it contracts together and evolves together. For
here `saṃbhava`, “arising,” means `vivarta`, “evolution”; this will be
explained later.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

Vasubandhu explains the two halves in sequence:

1. The small thousandfold aggregate from 3.73 becomes the unit for a second
   thousandfold aggregation, called the middle system.
2. The middle aggregate becomes the unit for a third thousandfold
   aggregation, called the great system.
3. The great system is qualified as complete and as undergoing common
   contraction and evolution.
4. The term `saṃbhava` is glossed as `vivarta`; detailed explanation is
   explicitly postponed.

No opponent or alternative account appears in this short unit. The two
`tatsahasra` phrases are resolved by the prose so that each “thousand of
those” refers to the immediately preceding aggregate.

## 5. The Middle System

The first recursive step is:

```text
1,000 small thousandfold systems
    → 1 middle, second-order system
```

Since each small system comprises one thousand local world-units, the middle
system contains one million local units:

```text
1,000 × 1,000 = 1,000,000
```

`Madhyama`, “middle,” names the second aggregate in this sequence. It does
not mean an average-sized individual world; it identifies the intermediate
scale between the small and great systems.

## 6. The Great System

The second recursive step is:

```text
1,000 middle systems
    → 1 trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra system
```

The resulting count is:

```text
1,000 × 1,000 × 1,000 = 1,000,000,000 local world-units
```

The compound name `trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra lokadhātu` is supplied in the
Bhāṣya's prose. It identifies the third recursive thousandfold scale. The
terms do not mean a flat two thousand or three thousand local worlds; each
scale multiplies the immediately preceding complete system by one thousand.

## 7. Whole and Unit Across Scales

The same aggregate changes its role as the scale rises:

```text
local world-unit
    → member of the small system

small system
    → member of the middle system

middle system
    → member of the great system
```

At each level, a complete whole becomes the unit of the next construction.
The nesting preserves what was defined at the previous scale while assigning
it a new role within a larger totality.

## 8. Common Contraction and Evolution

The great system is `kṛtsna`, complete, and `samaṃ saṃvarta-saṃbhava`.
Vasubandhu unpacks this as:

```text
samaṃ saṃvartate → it contracts together
samaṃ vivartate  → it evolves together
```

`Saṃbhava` in the verse is explained as `vivarta`, not as a separately
specified kind of arising. The claim of `samaṃ` is coordination across the
complete aggregate: contraction and evolution are common processes of the
world-system.

The Bhāṣya explicitly postpones the fuller explanation. This passage
establishes the shared lifecycle but does not yet describe its phases or
mechanics.

## 9. Doctrinal and Organon Study

Across 3.73–3.74 the cosmology has three nested scales:

| Scale | Construction | Local world-units |
|---|---|---:|
| Small | 1,000 local units | 1,000 |
| Middle | 1,000 small systems | 1,000,000 |
| Great | 1,000 middle systems | 1,000,000,000 |

The final system is defined by more than membership and number. The Bhāṣya
adds a common temporal process in which the complete aggregate contracts and
evolves together.

For the Organon, this is a compact model of nested aggregation with a shared
transition across its members. This is a project-level reconstruction, not
Vasubandhu's terminology. The precise cosmological-cycle mechanics remain
deferred, as the Bhāṣya itself indicates.
