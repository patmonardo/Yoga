# VAK_3.62 Bhāṣya — Incremental Time and the Incomplete Moon

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
lavaśo rātryaharvṛddhī dakṣiṇottarage ravau /
svacchāyayā 'rkasāmīpyād vikalendusamīkṣaṇam // 3.62 //
```

> Night and day increase lava by lava as the sun moves southward and
> northward. Because of its shadow when near the sun, the moon is seen as
> incomplete.

The Bhāṣya first supplies the increment and directional rule left open by VAK
3.61. It then turns from temporal measure to lunar appearance and preserves
two explanations: an account attributed to the *Prajñapti* and an alternative
attributed to earlier teachers.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Kiyatyā mātrayā vardhate?

Lavaśo rātryaharvṛddhī.

Lavaṃ lavaṃ rātrir vardhate, divaso vā. Te ca hānivṛddhī yathākramaṃ:

Dakṣiṇottarage ravau.

Jambūdvīpasya dakṣiṇaṃ pārśvaṃ gacchati bhāskare rātrivṛddhiḥ; uttaraṃ
gacchaty aharvṛddhiḥ.

Śuklapakṣasyādau candramaso vikalaṃ maṇḍalaṃ dṛśyate. Kiṃ tatra kāraṇam?

Svacchāyayā 'rkasāmīpyād vikalendusamīkṣaṇam.

Yadā hi saurasya vimānasyāsanne cāndramasaṃ vimānaṃ vahati, tadā kila
sauryo bhāsas tasmin vimāne patanti. Tato 'parapārśve chāyā patantī
vikalaṃ maṇḍalaṃ darśayatīti prājñaptiko nirdeśaḥ.

Vāhayogaḥ sa tādṛśo bhavati yat kadācid vimānasyārdhaṃ dṛśyata iti
pūrvācāryāḥ.
```

The running witness is readable but mechanically joins several compounds and
clauses. The continuous text regularizes spacing, sandhi, and punctuation.
`Sauryo bhāsaḥ` is read as the plural “solar rays,” following the syntax of
`patanti`. `Vāhayoga` is provisionally rendered as the configuration of the
lunar residence's conveyance or motion; the passage itself does not further
analyze the compound.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

By what measure does night or day increase?

The increases of night and day occur lava by lava. Night increases one lava
at a time, or day does. Their decrease and increase occur respectively as the
sun moves southward and northward. When the sun moves toward the southern
side of Jambūdvīpa, night increases; when it moves toward the northern side,
day increases.

At the beginning of the bright fortnight, the moon's disk is seen as
incomplete. What is the cause of this?

The moon is seen as incomplete because of its shadow when it is near the sun.
For when the lunar celestial residence is carried near the solar celestial
residence, solar rays are said to fall upon it. A shadow then falls upon the
opposite side and makes the disk appear incomplete. This is the explanation
given in the *Prajñapti*.

The earlier teachers say that the configuration of its conveyance is such
that, at certain times, only half of the celestial residence is seen.

## 4. Argumentative Movement and Voices

The unit contains two inquiries joined by celestial motion:

1. How much do night and day increase, and which solar direction governs each
   increase?
2. Why is the lunar disk seen as incomplete at the beginning of the bright
   fortnight?

The first receives a direct measurement rule. The second receives two
attributed causal accounts. Their voices must remain separate:

- `prājñaptiko nirdeśaḥ` attributes the ray-and-shadow explanation to the
  *Prajñapti* tradition;
- `pūrvācāryāḥ` attributes the alternative visibility-through-motion account
  to earlier teachers.

The Bhāṣya records both. It does not explicitly conclude this passage by
pronouncing one account victorious. The shared observation—an incomplete
lunar appearance—must therefore be distinguished from each explanation of
that observation.

## 5. The Lava-by-Lava Rule

`Lavaśaḥ` is distributive: the change occurs one lava at a time. The passage
does not provide a conversion from `lava` into a modern unit, so “minute”
would give the translation a precision not established by this unit.

VAK 3.61 gave the inverse relation:

```text
night increases ↔ day decreases
night decreases ↔ day increases
```

VAK 3.62 adds the increment and the directional selector:

```text
sun moving southward → night increases lava by lava
sun moving northward → day increases lava by lava
```

The full paired rule follows only by reading the verses together:

```text
southward course:
    night + one lava
    day   − one lava

northward course:
    night − one lava
    day   + one lava
```

The Bhāṣya expressly states the increasing member in each direction. The
decreasing member is fixed by the inverse relation already established in
3.61.

## 6. Lunar Appearance and the Prajñapti Account

The phenomenon to be explained is carefully located: at the beginning of the
bright fortnight the lunar disk is seen as `vikala`, incomplete or lacking a
part. The text asks for the cause of an appearance, not whether the lunar
residence itself has ceased to exist in its complete form.

The *Prajñapti* account has a relational sequence:

```text
lunar residence approaches solar residence
    → solar rays fall upon the lunar residence
        → shadow falls on the opposite side
            → the lunar disk appears incomplete
```

The prose qualifies the kārikā's `svacchāyayā`. The shadow belongs to the
lunar appearance, but its explanatory conditions include proximity to the
sun and the incidence of solar rays. “Its own shadow” must not be read as a
wholly self-generated condition independent of the sun–moon relation.

## 7. The Earlier Teachers' Alternative

The earlier teachers explain the same visible result through `vāhayoga`: the
configuration of the lunar residence's conveyance or course is such that only
half of it is visible at certain times.

```text
Prajñapti:
    solar proximity + rays + opposite-side shadow
        → incomplete appearance

earlier teachers:
    configuration of conveyance or motion
        → partial visibility
```

These are not two descriptions of different observed moons. They are rival or
alternative accounts of how one admitted appearance arises. The second is
very compressed, and the Bhāṣya does not specify here whether the unseen half
is occluded, differently oriented, or otherwise unavailable to vision.
Translation should preserve that restraint.

## 8. Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The first half advances the temporal process developed across 3.60–3.62:

```text
3.60: regional position → simultaneous local phase
3.61: calendar address  → direction of seasonal change
3.62: solar direction   → member increased by one-lava steps
```

The result distinguishes state, trend, increment, and governing direction.
Knowing that it is night does not determine whether night is lengthening;
knowing that it is lengthening does not yet state the increment. Each
determination contributes different information to one process.

The second half distinguishes observation from causal explanation:

```text
observation:
    the moon is seen as incomplete

explanations:
    Prajñapti model
    earlier-teacher model
```

Agreement about the perceptible determination does not entail agreement about
its production. Conversely, disagreement about causal mechanism does not make
the shared observation disappear. This is a modest but exact doxographical
discipline within the cosmological exposition.

## 9. Organon Study

For the Organon, the passage supplies two complementary forms of analysis.
The day–night portion gives a transformation rule whose magnitude and sign
must remain distinct. The lunar portion gives one appearance with multiple
provenanced explanatory models.

```text
measured process:
    current state + direction + increment → next state

explained appearance:
    observation + context + attributed model
```

A Dataset should not duplicate the lunar observation as though each authority
observed a different phenomenon. Nor should it merge the authorities into one
anonymous account. The stable observation and the attributed explanatory
sequences are different dimensions of analysis.

This is an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's technical vocabulary. It
also sharpens the idea of projection: an appearance can be relationally
conditioned without being arbitrary or unreal, while the causal account of
that appearance remains open to comparison and revision.
