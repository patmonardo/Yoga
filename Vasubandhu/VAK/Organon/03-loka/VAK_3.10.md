# VAK_3.10

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> मृत्यूपपत्तिभवयोरन्तरा भवतीह यः ।
>
> गम्यदेशानुपेतत्वान्नोपपन्नोऽन्तराभवः ॥ ३.१० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> mṛtyupapattibhavayor antarā bhavatīha yaḥ /
>
> gamyadeśānupetatvān nopapanno 'ntarābhavaḥ // 3.10 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
mṛtyu-upapatti-bhavayoḥ → mṛtyupapattibhavayoḥ
antarā                   → antarā
bhavati + iha            → bhavatīha
yaḥ                      → yaḥ
gamya-deśa-anupetatvāt   → gamyadeśānupetatvāt
na + upapannaḥ           → nopapannaḥ
antarā-bhavaḥ            → antarābhavaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| mṛtyu-upapatti-bhavayoḥ | locative dual compound | between death-existence and rebirth-existence |
| antarā | indeclinable | between; in the interval |
| bhavati | third-person singular present of √bhū | exists; comes to be |
| iha | indeclinable | here; in the account under discussion |
| yaḥ | nominative masculine singular relative pronoun | that which |
| gamya | future passive participle of √gam | to be reached |
| deśa | masculine noun | place; locus |
| anupetatvāt | ablative singular abstract noun from an-upeta | because of not having reached |
| upapannaḥ | nominative masculine singular past participle of upa-√pad | arrived into rebirth; reborn |
| antarābhavaḥ | nominative masculine singular | intermediate existence |

The compound `mṛtyu-upapatti-bhavayoḥ` establishes two terminal phases and
locates the referent of `yaḥ` between them. The second half names that referent
by excluding arrival at the destination.

## 4. Grammar

The relative clause and its nominal resolution may be construed as follows:

```text
yaḥ
    mṛtyu-upapatti-bhavayoḥ antarā
    iha bhavati,

saḥ, gamya-deśa-anupetatvāt,
    na upapannaḥ:
    antarābhavaḥ [asti].
```

> That which exists here between death-existence and rebirth-existence—because
> it has not reached the destination—is not yet reborn: it is intermediate
> existence.

The Bhāṣya sharpens a lexical distinction concealed by a loose translation of
both terms as “born”:

```text
jāta
    arisen as an intermediate configuration

upapanna
    gone into and arrived at the ensuing rebirth
```

It derives the latter force from the motion-sense of √pad. Thus existence in
the interval does not already count as arrival in the subsequent birth.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> That which exists here between death-existence and rebirth-existence, because
> it has not reached the destination, is not yet reborn: it is intermediate
> existence.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Intermediate existence is the arisen sentient configuration between death
> and rebirth, directed toward rebirth elsewhere but not yet arrived there. Its
> destination is the locus in which the projected karmic result becomes
> manifest and reaches completion.

“Sentient configuration,” orientation toward another rebirth, and the
definition of the destination through `vipāka` are Bhāṣya determinations. The
Kārikā itself defines the interval by non-arrival.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System does not pass from death to rebirth as an unexplained jump.
> It determines an intermediate phase: a continuity has ceased to occupy its
> former embodiment but has not yet arrived at the locus where its projected
> result will become manifest and complete its realization. Transition is
> therefore directed and mediated through a determinate transition-phase.

## 8. Logical Determination

### The transition has three positions

```text
maraṇabhava
    preceding embodiment reaches death
        ↓
antarābhava
    an intermediate configuration has arisen
    destination has not yet been reached
        ↓
upapattibhava
    the continuity arrives into the subsequent rebirth
```

Hence:

```text
arisen
    does not entail
arrived into rebirth
```

The criterion is operational rather than merely chronological:

```text
not yet at gamya-deśa
    → not yet upapanna
    → antarābhava
```

### Destination is karmically determined manifestation

The Bhāṣya answers “What place is to be reached?” with:

```text
gamya-deśa
    = locus where ākṣipta-vipāka
        becomes manifest
        and reaches completion
```

The destination is therefore not bare geography. It is a position in the
World System defined by the realization of a projected result.

### The continuity claim remains under proof

The Nikāyāntarīya thesis is:

```text
maraṇabhava
    discontinuous production
upapattibhava

no antarābhava required
```

Vasubandhu rejects that thesis and announces two standards:

```text
yukti + āgama
```

VAK 3.10 states the definition and the dispute. The promised proof begins in
VAK 3.11 and must not be treated as already completed here.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.10 gives the World System a transition protocol. Death, transit, and
rebirth are distinguishable system-states, while the destination is specified
by the manifestation and completion of projected `vipāka`. This is
Transcendental Technē as our project characterization, not Vasubandhu's own
name for his doctrine.

The previously hidden subtle process is now partly exposed:

```text
withdrawal from a prior embodied station
    ↓
arising of an intermediate configuration
    ↓
directed transition without arrival
    ↓
manifestation of projected result at its fitting locus
    ↓
new embodied station
```

This is not yet the **Siva Trademark** code for freely taking up and disposing
of bodies. It is the conditioned World-process within which embodiments are
exchanged. The text determines a directed continuity and its successive
configurations.

For the Rational Moral Agent, the decisive distinction is between an
available position and an achieved cognition-station:

```text
sthāna
    a locus that can be occupied

vijñānasthiti
    a lawful station of differentiated cognition
```

A physical refuge can alter the former without securing the latter. Within
the World System, the operative result determines where a configuration can
manifest and complete—not the Agent's declaration that some place is secure.
Rudra's destabilization of a cognition-station remains our speculative
Siva-level reading; the Kārikā's textual machinery is `antarābhava`,
`gamya-deśa`, and projected `vipāka`.

Finally, the passage is methodological as well as cosmological. Vasubandhu
does not answer a rival transition-model by spectacle. The contested process
must submit to both inferential articulation and received testimony. The
World System is thus reconstructed as an accountable Technē whose hidden
operations are to be demonstrated, beginning in the next verse.
