# VAK_3.51

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अर्धार्धहानिरष्टासु समोच्छ्रायघनाश्च ते ।
>
> शीताः सप्तान्तराण्येषामाद्याशीतिसहस्रिका ॥ ३.५१ ॥

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening gives a rule over the eight-member mountain series:

```text
aṣṭāsu [parvatocchrāyeṣu]
    ardha-ardha-hāniḥ [asti]

across the eight [mountain heights]
    there is diminution by successive halves
```

The following compound predicates a dimensional invariant:

```text
te [parvatāḥ]
    sama-ucchrāya-ghanāḥ [santi]

those [mountains]
    have thickness equal to their height
```

The second hemistich identifies the intervals and measures the first:

```text
eṣām [parvatānām]
    sapta antarāṇi
    śītāḥ [ucyante]

ādyā [śītā]
    aśīti-sahasrikā [vaipulyena]
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the understood mountains, the width-axis `vaipulya`, and
the first interval's location between Sumeru and Yugandhara.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Across the eight there is successive diminution by halves, and their
> thickness equals their height. The seven intervals between them are the
> Śītās; the first measures eighty thousand [yojanas].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Beginning from Sumeru's height above the water, each successive surrounding
> mountain is half the height of the preceding one; each mountain's horizontal
> thickness equals its own height. The seven spaces between Sumeru and
> Nimindhara are cool inner seas called Śītās. The first, between Sumeru and
> Yugandhara, is 80,000 yojanas wide, and its water possesses eight beneficial
> sensible qualities.

The starting term of the halving sequence, interpretation of `ghana`, precise
location and width-axis of the first sea, and eight water qualities are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The concentric world is generated by a recursive proportional rule. Each
> outward mountain receives its scale from the immediately preceding member,
> while preserving an internal invariant: its thickness equals its height.
> The resulting series alternates solid boundaries with fluid intervals, and
> those intervals are determined not only quantitatively by width but
> qualitatively by the character of their water. The World System thus combines
> recursion, invariant form, alternation, and sensible quality in one ordered
> construction.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Recursive generation of scale

```text
H₀ = 80,000
Hₙ₊₁ = Hₙ / 2
```

One rule generates the complete ordered series. The measure of each outer
member depends upon its predecessor rather than requiring eight independent
stipulations.

### Invariant within variation

```text
for every mountain M:
    thickness(M) = height(M)
```

Absolute magnitude changes at every step, while the height-to-thickness
relation remains constant. The series is therefore transformation under a
preserved form, not arbitrary shrinkage.

### Alternating topology

```text
mountain₀
    → sea₀
        → mountain₁
            → sea₁
                → ...
                    → mountain₇
```

The seven intervals are positive regions of the system, not empty gaps. Each
is occupied by a Śītā sea and therefore has its own quantitative and sensible
determinations.

### Quantity and quality

```text
first Śītā
    quantitative determination: width = 80,000 yojanas
    qualitative determination: water endowed with eight qualities
```

A complete domain specification requires both measure and mode of possible
experience. Width locates the sea in the spatial system; the water's qualities
determine how embodied beings encounter it.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.51 demonstrates why the Kośa's cosmography rewards abstraction. A long
mountain inventory becomes a compact generative system once its rule is found:

```text
initial value
    + recursive ratio
    + invariant aspect relation
    + alternating interval type
        → complete concentric series
```

This is the difference between storing eight unrelated facts and possessing
the principle of their organization. The rule compresses the Matrix without
discarding its determinate content: every mountain height can be regenerated,
and each thickness follows from the invariant.

The Śītās prevent this from becoming only an abstract geometry. An interval is
not a mere numerical distance between two objects. It is itself a positive
domain with material content and sensible-functional qualities. Thus the world
is constructed through alternating types of region:

```text
boundary-domain ↔ interval-domain
```

For the Organon projection:

```ts
interface RecursiveRingSystem {
  initialHeight: 80_000;
  scaleRatio: 0.5;
  mountainCount: 8;
  intervalCount: 7;
  invariant: "thickness-equals-height";
  intervalDomain: "eight-qualified-cool-sea";
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. Its purpose is to
extract the law by which the cosmological data form a system. The central
abstraction is controlled recursion: difference is produced repeatedly by one
ratio while a structural identity is preserved through every term.
