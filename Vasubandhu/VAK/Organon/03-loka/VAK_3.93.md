# VAK_3.93

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विवर्ततेऽथ संवृत्त आस्ते संवर्तते समम् ।
>
> ते ह्यशीतिर्महाकल्पस्तदसंख्यत्रयोद्भवम् ॥ ३.९३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vivartate 'tha saṃvṛtta āste saṃvartate samam /
>
> te hy aśītir mahākalpas tad asaṃkhyatrayodbhavam // 3.93 //

The first line completes the sentence begun in the Bhāṣya immediately after
3.92: the duration for which the World stands expanded is also the duration
for which it expands, contracts, and stands contracted. `Saṃvṛtta` here means
contracted, not expanded.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
vivartate                → vivartate
atha                     → 'tha (after avagraha)
saṃvṛttaḥ                → saṃvṛtta (before ā-)
āste                     → āste
saṃvartate               → saṃvartate
samam                    → samam
te                       → te
hi                       → hy (before a-)
aśītiḥ                   → aśītir (before m-)
mahā-kalpaḥ              → mahākalpas (before t-)
tat                      → tad (before a-)
asaṃkhya-traya-udbhavam  → asaṃkhyatrayodbhavam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| vivartate | third-person singular middle present | the World expands or forms |
| saṃvṛttaḥ | past passive participle, nominative masculine singular | contracted; having completed contraction |
| āste | third-person singular middle present | remains, stands in the achieved state |
| saṃvartate | third-person singular middle present | the World contracts |
| samam | adverbial neuter singular | equally, for an equal duration |
| te | nominative masculine plural | those four groups of twenty intermediate kalpas |
| aśītiḥ | nominative numeral | eighty |
| mahākalpaḥ | nominative masculine singular | great kalpa; complete four-phase World-cycle |
| tat | pronoun | that mahākalpa as the unit counted in the next compound |
| asaṃkhya | compound member | enormous named number at the sixtieth numerical place |
| traya | compound member | three |
| udbhavam | nominative/accusative neuter singular compound-final | arising or attainment, resolved as buddhahood by the Bhāṣya |

The compound `asaṃkhyatrayodbhavam` does not mean that three logically
uncountable quantities somehow become countable. The Bhāṣya assigns
`asaṃkhya` a determinate place in a conventional large-number sequence.

## 4. Grammar

The first line must be construed with the immediately preceding statement:

```text
[yāvantaṃ kālaṃ vivṛttaḥ tiṣṭhati]
    tāvantam eva kālaṃ
        vivartate
        atha saṃvṛttaḥ āste
        saṃvartate
        samam
```

> For exactly as long as the World stands expanded, it expands, contracts,
> and stands contracted—each for an equal duration.

The surface order is not the chronological order of all four phases. The
completed cycle, established through 3.92 and the Bhāṣya, is:

```text
vivarta
    → vivṛtta-sthiti
        → saṃvarta
            → saṃvṛtta-sthiti
```

The second line defines the comprehensive measure:

```text
te [catvāraḥ viṃśatayaḥ]
    hi aśītiḥ [antar-kalpāḥ]

[te]
    mahā-kalpaḥ [bhavanti]
```

> Those four groups of twenty are eighty intermediate kalpas; they constitute
> one mahākalpa.

The final compound is Bhāṣya-dependent:

```text
tat-[mahākalpa]-asaṃkhya-trayāt
    buddhatvam udbhavati / prāpyate
```

> Buddhahood arises or is attained through three `asaṃkhyeyas` of those
> mahākalpas.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> It expands; then it stands contracted and contracts—for an equal duration.
> Those [four groups of twenty] are eighty, one mahākalpa; from three
> `asaṃkhyeyas` of those arises [buddhahood].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Expansion, standing while expanded, contraction, and standing while
> contracted each last twenty intermediate kalpas. These four equal temporal
> enclosures total eighty intermediate kalpas, which constitute one
> mahākalpa. The mahākalpa is the unit used in the three-`asaṃkhyeya`
> accumulation through which buddhahood is attained. Here `asaṃkhyeya` is an
> enormous determinate number reached at the sixtieth place in a conventional
> numerical series, after which counting recommences.

The complete four-phase construe, the five-aggregate nature of kalpa,
identification of the relevant unit as mahākalpa, and sixty-place meaning of
`asaṃkhyeya` are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The complete World-cycle is a temporal whole composed of four equally
> measured but qualitatively different phases. Its enclosure is not an empty
> cosmic duration standing apart from what happens within it: Vasubandhu says
> that kalpa has the five aggregates as its nature. Even the apparently
> incalculable accumulation required for buddhahood remains logically
> determinate. The Kośa preserves measure at both limits—through the complete
> aggregate-process of a World and through magnitudes exceeding ordinary
> representation.

`Pañcaskandhasvabhāva` is registered positively as the constitution of the
kalpa-process. The point needed here is not a polemic but a precise answer to
`kalpaḥ kiṃsvabhāvaḥ?`—what is a kalpa by nature?

## 8. Logical Determination

### Complete mahākalpa equation

```text
Expansion             = 20 antarakalpas
Expanded standing     = 20 antarakalpas
Contraction           = 20 antarakalpas
Contracted standing   = 20 antarakalpas
------------------------------------------------
1 mahākalpa            = 80 antarakalpas
```

Formally:

```text
Mahakalpa =
    TemporalEnclosure([
      Vivarta(20),
      VivrttaSthiti(20),
      Samvarta(20),
      SamvrttaSthiti(20)
    ])
```

### Process and state alternation

The cycle alternates transition with achieved state:

```text
process: vivarta
    → state: vivṛtta
        → process: saṃvarta
            → state: saṃvṛtta
```

This is more determinate than a binary creation-destruction model. Each
transition has a corresponding standing-state whose duration equals the
transition duration.

### Nature of the temporal enclosure

The Bhāṣya asks and answers:

```text
kalpaḥ kiṃsvabhāvaḥ?
pañcaskandhasvabhāvaḥ.
```

The logical form is constitutive:

```text
Kalpa
    → is not specified merely by an external clock-value
    → has the five-aggregate process as its determinate nature
```

Thus:

```text
temporal enclosure
    ↔ organized aggregate-process it encloses
```

This reciprocal relation must not be flattened into either extreme:

```text
kalpa = empty time-container
```

or:

```text
kalpa = one isolated aggregate-item
```

Its measure is the unity of the complete conditioned process.

### Determinate `asaṃkhyeya`

The objector's inference is:

```text
asaṃkhyeya = absolutely uncountable
    → “three asaṃkhyeyas” is incoherent
```

Vasubandhu rejects the premise. The cited scheme gives:

```text
60 successive numerical places
    → terminal named magnitude: asaṃkhya

reach terminal magnitude
    → restart counting

repeat three times
    → three asaṃkhyeyas
```

Therefore:

```text
enormous beyond ordinary representation
    ≠ logically unavailable to enumeration
```

### Path-scale unit typing

The unit in the threefold accumulation is not an unspecified `kalpa`:

```text
PathAccumulationUnit = Mahakalpa(80 antarakalpas)
```

The verse therefore connects the exact World-cycle measure with the duration
of bodhisattva cultivation without collapsing cosmology into path attainment.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya's `pañcaskandhasvabhāva` gives extraordinary support to the
Enclosure analysis. Temporal enclosure is not a void surrounding an externally
given sequence. Its determinate nature is the articulated aggregate-process
through which the World expands, stands, contracts, and remains contracted.

```text
temporal Enclosure
    → gives the four phases one measured unity

five-aggregate process
    → supplies the concrete determination of that unity

reciprocal relation
    → constitutes the mahākalpa as a real World-process
```

This also sharpens your claim that `vijñānas` are Sattvic temporal enclosures.
`Vijñāna` belongs within the five-aggregate constitution named by the Bhāṣya.
At the cognitive scale it illuminates and differentiates an appearing as one
temporal station; at the cosmic scale the kalpa encloses the vast serial field
of aggregate-events as one World-cycle.

```text
Sattvic micro-enclosure:
    vijñāna holds differentiated appearing as one occurrence

cosmic macro-enclosure:
    mahākalpa holds differentiated aggregate-process as one cycle
```

The correspondence is structural, not numerical. The cognitive enclosure is
not eighty intermediate kalpas long, and Vasubandhu does not designate the
mahākalpa itself as `vijñāna`. Our reconstruction identifies the same
Enclosure-form operating at distinct resolutions.

The `asaṃkhyeya` analysis completes the Kant-power of the passage. Reason does
not surrender determinacy merely because imagination cannot survey the
magnitude. The number exceeds ordinary presentation, yet remains positioned
within a lawful numerical system. Similarly, the World-cycle exceeds ordinary
experience while remaining exactly articulated by temporal form.
