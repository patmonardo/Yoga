# VAK_3.65

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> मेरुमूर्ध्नि त्रयस्त्रिंशाः स चाशीतीसहस्रदिक् ।
>
> विदिक्षु कूटाश्चत्वार उषिता वज्रपाणिभिः ॥ ३.६५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> merumūrdhni trayastriṃśāḥ sa cāśītisahasradik /
>
> vidikṣu kūṭāś catvāra uṣitā vajrapāṇibhiḥ // 3.65 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
meru-mūrdhni               → merumūrdhni
trayastriṃśāḥ              → trayastriṃśāḥ
saḥ                        → sa

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| merumūrdhni | locative singular of compound with `mūrdhan` | on the summit or crown of Meru |
| trayastriṃśāḥ | nominative masculine plural | the Trāyastriṃśas, the Thirty-Three gods |
| saḥ | nominative masculine singular pronoun | it, referring to Meru's summit |
| ca | conjunction | and |
| aśītisahasradik | nominative masculine singular compound adjective | having an extent of eighty thousand toward the directions |
| vidikṣu | locative feminine plural | in the intermediate directions, interpreted as the corners |
| kūṭāḥ | nominative masculine plural | peaks or projecting summits |
| catvāraḥ | nominative masculine plural numeral | four |
| uṣitāḥ | nominative masculine plural past participle of `√vas` | inhabited or dwelt in |
| vajrapāṇibhiḥ | instrumental masculine plural compound | by the Vajrapāṇis, the vajra-handed ones |

`Trāyastriṃśa` and `Vajrapāṇi` function as population names here. The Bhāṣya
specifies the latter as a class of yakṣas rather than merely translating the
compound as an epithet.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening locative establishes the summit population:

```text
meru-mūrdhni
    trayastriṃśāḥ [prativasanti]
```

The pronoun then makes the summit the subject of a dimensional predicate:

```text
saḥ [meru-mūrdhā]
    ca
    aśīti-sahasra-dik [asti]
```

The exact scope of the measure is disputed in the Bhāṣya. The principal
account understands each side as 80,000 yojanas. An attributed alternative
understands each side as 20,000, producing a total circumference of 80,000.

The second line relates corner locations, peaks, and inhabitants:

```text
vidikṣu
    kūṭāḥ catvāraḥ
    vajrapāṇibhiḥ uṣitāḥ [santi]
```

> In the intermediate directions are four peaks inhabited by Vajrapāṇis.

The Bhāṣya identifies the intermediate directions with the four corners of
the summit and gives each projecting peak a measure of 500 yojanas.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> On Meru's summit are the Trāyastriṃśas, and it has an eighty-thousand extent
> toward the directions. In the intermediate directions are four peaks
> inhabited by Vajrapāṇis.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Trāyastriṃśa gods inhabit Meru's summit. On the principal explanation,
> each side of the summit measures 80,000 yojanas, as does the corresponding
> measure below. According to another opinion, each of its four sides measures
> 20,000, making 80,000 around the whole circumference. At the summit's four
> corners rise four peaks, each measuring 500 yojanas, inhabited by yakṣas
> called Vajrapāṇis.

The competing interpretations of the summit measure, the identification of
`vidiś` with corner, the 500-yojana peak measure, and the classification of
the Vajrapāṇis as yakṣas are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The upward articulation of Meru reaches a summit domain occupied by the
> Thirty-Three. This domain is specified through a coordinate structure:
> sides establish directional extent, their intersections establish corners,
> and the corners support four further peaks with determinate inhabitants.
> Yet the transmitted number does not determine its own metric semantics. Two
> models preserve “eighty thousand” while assigning it either to each side or
> to the total perimeter. The World System therefore requires not only
> quantities, but explicit relations stating what each quantity measures.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Coordinate construction

```text
four directional sides
    → four intersections
        → four corners
            → four projecting peaks
                → Vajrapāṇi inhabitants
```

The corners are relationally determined by the sides; they are not additional
directions inserted independently into the structure.

### Competing metric models

Let `s` be side length and `p` the perimeter of the four-sided summit.

```text
principal model:
    s = 80,000 yojanas
    p = 4s = 320,000 yojanas

alternative model:
    s = 20,000 yojanas
    p = 4s = 80,000 yojanas
```

Both accounts retain the number 80,000. They disagree about its predicate:

```text
80,000 measures each side
    versus
80,000 measures the whole perimeter
```

A quantity without a measurement relation is therefore underdetermined.

### Summit and peak scales

```text
summit domain:
    measured by side or perimeter

corner nodes:
    four, derived from the summit boundary

corner peaks:
    one per corner
    500 yojanas each

peak population:
    Vajrapāṇi yakṣas
```

The passage nests a smaller measured habitation structure inside the boundary
of a larger measured habitation domain.

### Source-preserving representation

```text
asserted datum:
    summit + eighty-thousand directional measure

model A:
    measureOf(80,000, each-side)

model B:
    measureOf(80,000, total-perimeter)
```

The two interpretations should be stored with provenance rather than merged
into one falsely definite geometry.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The movement across 3.63–3.65 is systematic:

```text
3.63  levels and dimensions
3.64  populations, class-membership, and distributed settlement
3.65  summit boundary, coordinate frame, and corner populations
```

The summit is not simply “above” the preceding domain. It introduces another
scale of organization. The lower terraces are arranged by ordinal elevation;
the summit is articulated laterally by direction, side, corner, and perimeter.
The World System changes its mode of spatial determination as it reaches a new
region.

The most useful abstraction is metric typing. The number 80,000 cannot be
used correctly until its measured feature is known. Side length and perimeter
are not rival numbers but rival semantic attachments for the same number.
This is exactly the point at which a merely syntactic record fails:

```text
quantity: 80,000
```

is insufficient, whereas:

```text
quantity: 80,000
unit: yojanas
measure-of: each-side | total-perimeter
authority: principal-account | alternative-account
```

preserves the actual dispute.

```ts
type SummitMetric =
  | { authority: "PrincipalAccount"; measureOf: "EachSide"; yojanas: 80000 }
  | { authority: "AlternativeAccount"; measureOf: "TotalPerimeter"; yojanas: 80000 };

interface CornerPeak {
  corner: "NE" | "SE" | "SW" | "NW";
  measureYojanas: 500;
  inhabitants: "VajrapaniYaksas";
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It keeps
the measured entity, measurement relation, and attributed model distinct.

The Vajrapāṇis should likewise remain inhabitants in this study. Their name
and corner placement may invite a protective interpretation, but the Bhāṣya
here states residence and yakṣa classification, not a guardian function.
