# VAK_3.95

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चक्रवर्तिसमुत्पत्तिर्नाधोऽशीतिसहस्रकात् ।
>
> सुवर्णरूप्यताम्रायश्चक्रिणस्तेऽधरक्रमात् ॥ ३.९५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> cakravarti-samutpattir nādho 'śītisahasrakāt /
>
> suvarṇa-rūpya-tāmrāyaś-cakriṇas te 'dhara-kramāt // 3.95 //

The verse fixes both an eligibility threshold and a ranked classification.
The Bhāṣya specifies that cakravartins arise while human lifespan ranges from
immeasurable duration down to eighty thousand years, never below that limit.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| cakravarti-samutpattiḥ | nominative feminine singular compound | arising of a wheel-turning sovereign |
| na adhaḥ | negative plus adverb | not below |
| aśīti-sahasrakāt | ablative singular | below the eighty-thousand-year lifespan threshold |
| suvarṇa | compound member | gold; material of the highest wheel |
| rūpya | compound member | silver; material of the next-highest wheel |
| tāmra | compound member | copper; material of the middling wheel |
| ayaḥ | compound member | iron; material of the lowest wheel |
| cakriṇaḥ | nominative masculine plural | possessors of wheels; wheel-kings |
| te | nominative masculine plural | those four cakravartins |
| adhara-kramāt | ablative used adverbially | in successively lower order |

The long nominal compound enumerates four kinds of `cakrin`; it does not
describe one wheel composed of four materials. The Bhāṣya confirms that each
material marks a distinct sovereign rank.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first half gives the temporal boundary:

```text
cakravarti-samutpattiḥ
    na [bhavati]
    adhaḥ aśīti-sahasrakāt
```

> The arising of a wheel-turning sovereign does not occur below [a human
> lifespan of] eighty thousand [years].

The ablative `aśītisahasrakāt` supplies the standard relative to which `adhaḥ`
marks inferiority. The omitted reference to lifespan is restored explicitly
by the Bhāṣya.

The second half first enumerates and then orders the four kinds:

```text
[suvarṇa-rūpya-tāmra-ayaḥ]-cakriṇaḥ
    te
    adhara-kramāt [vyavasthitāḥ]
```

> They are possessors of gold, silver, copper, and iron wheels, arranged in
> successively lower order.

The Bhāṣya resolves the sequence exactly:

```text
suvarṇa → uttama
rūpya  → upottama
tāmra  → madhyama
ayaḥ   → adhama
```

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> A wheel-turning sovereign does not arise below [the lifespan of] eighty
> thousand. They are wheel-possessors of gold, silver, copper, and iron, in
> descending order.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Wheel-turning sovereigns arise while the human lifespan ranges from
> immeasurable length down to eighty thousand years, but never below that
> threshold. They are fourfold according to whether their sovereign wheel is
> gold, silver, copper, or iron—respectively the highest, next-highest,
> middling, and lowest types.

The upper range, the material condition of extraordinary crop-abundance, and
the four explicit rank-names are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A determinate form of sovereignty can appear only in a World capable of
> sustaining it. The lifespan threshold is therefore not a bare date on a
> cosmic clock: below it, embodied beings and their environment no longer
> constitute a suitable support for the extraordinary abundance associated
> with cakravartin rule. Where that material capacity is present, sovereignty
> itself appears in four ordered grades, each expressed through the wheel by
> which rule proceeds.

The derivational explanation in the Bhāṣya—`rājyaṃ cakreṇa vartayitum`—makes
the wheel operational: these sovereigns are called `cakravartin` because they
characteristically cause rule to proceed by means of it.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Eligibility through World-capacity

```text
human lifespan ≥ 80,000 years
    + World capable of extraordinary crop-abundance
        → cakravartin arising is possible

human lifespan < 80,000 years
    → embodied beings are abhājana for that abundance
    → cakravartin arising is excluded
```

The threshold therefore indexes a conjunction rather than an isolated
quantity:

```text
TemporalEligibility(x)
    ∧ MaterialCapacity(x)
        → Permits(CakravartinArising, x)
```

Neither long lifespan by itself nor abundance abstracted from its embodied
support is presented as the efficient cause of the sovereign. Together they
determine whether the World is adequate to this historical form.

### Wheel as operative classification

```text
gold wheel   → highest sovereignty
silver wheel → next-highest sovereignty
copper wheel → middling sovereignty
iron wheel   → lowest sovereignty
```

Material difference is simultaneously rank-difference. The wheel is not an
incidental badge subsequently attached to an already classified ruler; it is
the instrument through which the type of sovereignty is identified and
operates.

### Buddha and cakravartin thresholds

The sequence 3.94–3.95 differentiates two World-historical operations:

```text
decline below 80,000 down to 100
    → field permitting Buddha-arising and instruction

lifespan at or above 80,000
    → field permitting cakravartin sovereignty
```

These adjoining ranges disclose different forms of World-order. They should
not be collapsed into an identity between Dharma authority and royal power.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.95 advances the method from **temporal enclosure** to the **capacity of
the enclosed World**. A phase is concretely determined by what its embodied
and environmental organization can support.

```text
temporal range
    ↔ lifespan and embodied capacity
        ↔ material abundance
            ↔ possible political form
```

The Bhāṣya's `abhājana` is especially powerful here. It does not say merely
that the sovereign happens not to appear after a certain date. It says that
the declining World has ceased to be an adequate support for the abundance
belonging to that sovereignty. The enclosure and the enclosed reciprocally
determine the historical possibility:

```text
World-condition
    → determines which agential form it can sustain

agential form
    → expresses the operative capacity of that World-condition
```

This remains produced Loka. The cakravartin is not elevated into a
transcendental ground. The methodical gain is that even apparently fabulous
royal imagery yields a strict systems principle: no operation appears without
an environment adequate to its resource requirements, embodied agents, and
mode of coordination.
