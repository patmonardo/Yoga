# VAK_3.102 Bhāṣya — The Sixty-Four Contractions

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
saptāgninā adbhir ekā evaṃ gate 'dbhiḥ saptake punaḥ /
tejasā saptakaḥ paścād vāyusaṃvartanī tataḥ // 3.102 //
```

> Seven [contractions] by fire, one by water; when the sevenfold series by
> water has thus been completed, another sevenfold series by fire, and then
> a contraction by wind.

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed sequence into its full count and closes
the Lokanirdeśa, the third Kośasthāna.

## Continuous Sanskrit

The natural unit runs from the question about the order of contractions
through the explanatory count, its correlation with the Śubhakṛtsna lifespan,
the closing causal stanza, and the chapter colophon. The source's compressed
forms and apparent textual irregularities are retained; punctuation and
spacing are lightly regularized.

Paścād ekā vāyusaṃvartanī bhavati. Kiṃ kāraṇam?

## Continuous Conventional Translation

In what sequence, then, do these contractions occur? First, without
interruption, seven occur by fire. Then one occurs by water, after the seven
fire-contractions. When seven water-contractions have occurred in this way,
another sevenfold series of fire-contractions follows. After that comes one
contraction by wind.

Why? Just as the stability of the beings' modes of existence differs
according to differences in their meditative attainments, so does the
stability of the receptacle-world. Thus there are fifty-six fire-
contractions, seven water-contractions, and one wind-contraction.

In this way the statement in the *Prajñaptibhāṣya* is well explained: “The
lifespan of the Śubhakṛtsna gods is sixty-four kalpas.”

“For those dharmas that arise from causes, the Tathāgata declared their
cause; and their cessation too: thus teaches the Great Ascetic.”

Thus in the *Abhidharmakośabhāṣya*, the section called the Lokanirdeśa, the
third Kośasthāna, is complete.

## Natural Movement

Seven fire-contractions precede each water-contraction. Seven such groups
yield forty-nine fire-contractions and seven water-contractions; these are
followed by seven more fire-contractions and one wind-contraction. The total
is sixty-four: fifty-six by fire, seven by water, and one by wind. The
Bhāṣya connects this count to the sixty-four-kalpa lifespan of the
Śubhakṛtsna gods, then closes with a causal verse and the chapter colophon.

The stability of the receptacle-world is said to vary in correspondence with
the stability of beings' modes of existence, which in turn differs according
to their meditative attainment. This is the Bhāṣya's local explanation for
the differing durations, not an independent Organon reconstruction.

## Textual Note

The witness reads `yathiva`; it may reflect a corruption of `yathaiva`, but
that correction is not silently made. The closing stanza is also retained
close to the witness (`hetum teṣāṃ tathāgato hy avadat`) rather than
normalized to a familiar parallel reading. The stanza is translated
conventionally; its exact textual form remains for review.
