# VAK_3.57

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> इहोत्तरेण कीटाद्रिनवकाद्धिमवांस्ततः ।
>
> पञ्चाशद्विस्तृतायामं सरोऽर्वाग्गन्धमादनात् ॥ ३.५७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> ihottareṇa kīṭādrinavakād dhimavāṃs tataḥ /
>
> pañcāśadvistṛtāyāmaṃ saro 'rvāg gandhamādanāt // 3.57 //

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first line gives a directed traversal:

```text
iha [jambūdvīpe]
    uttareṇa
    kīṭa-adri-navakāt [pareṇa]
    himavān [asti]

here [in Jambūdvīpa],
    northward,
    beyond the group of nine Kīṭa mountains,
    is Himavān
```

The second line places and measures the lake:

```text
tataḥ [himavataḥ pareṇa]
    gandhamādanāt arvāk
    saraḥ [asti]
        pañcāśat-vistṛta-āyāmam
```

The compound means that both breadth and length are fifty yojanas. The Bhāṣya
resolves the nine mountains as three groups of three and names the lake
Anavatapta.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Here, northward beyond the group of nine Kīṭa mountains, is Himavān. Beyond
> it, on this side of Gandhamādana, is a lake fifty [yojanas] in breadth and
> length.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Within Jambūdvīpa, proceeding north across three groups of three black or
> Kīṭa mountains, one reaches Himavān. Beyond Himavān but before Gandhamādana
> lies Lake Anavatapta, fifty yojanas wide and fifty long. Its water possesses
> eight qualities, and four rivers—the Gaṅgā, Sindhu, Śītā, and Vakṣu—flow from
> it. Humans lacking extraordinary power cannot readily reach it. Nearby grows
> the sweet-fruited Jambū tree, from which Jambūdvīpa is named—or, according to
> an alternative explanation, from its fruit.

The three-by-three grouping, name and qualities of the lake, river list,
accessibility condition, Jambū tree, and two naming explanations are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A continent now becomes a traversable regional system. Its places are not
> given as isolated coordinates but as a directed sequence of barriers,
> landmarks, source-waters, and flows. Reachability varies with the powers of
> the traveler, while a single source-locus distributes water into four river
> systems. Finally, the whole continent receives its name from one salient
> internal feature. Spatial order, Agent-capability, network flow, and semantic
> designation are thus integrated within one regional account.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Directed regional traversal

```text
Jambūdvīpa
    → northward
        → three black mountains
        → three further mountains
        → three further mountains
            → Himavān
                → Anavatapta
                    → Gandhamādana beyond
```

The places form an ordered path, not a bag of geographical names. Each
location is specified by a traversal relation to the preceding and following
landmarks.

### Capability-indexed reachability

```text
Agent without ṛddhi
    → Anavatapta difficult to reach

Agent possessing ṛddhi
    → excluded from that stated limitation
```

Accessibility is not an intrinsic yes/no property of the lake alone. It is a
relation between a route, a locus, and an Agent's capabilities.

### Source-node and distributed flows

```text
Anavatapta
    ├── Gaṅgā
    ├── Sindhu
    ├── Śītā
    └── Vakṣu
```

One bounded source-domain generates four outgoing river relations. The lake's
identity includes not only its location and dimensions but its role within a
larger hydrological network.

### Naming the whole from an internal feature

```text
principal explanation:
    Jambū tree → designation “Jambūdvīpa”

alternative explanation:
    Jambū fruit → designation “Jambūdvīpa”
```

The name of the whole is grounded in a salient contained feature. The two
accounts agree on the semantic family while differing over whether the tree or
its fruit is the immediate naming-ground.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.57 shifts from static regional morphology to an operational geography.
A usable world-model must answer more than “what exists?” It must represent:

```text
where a route begins
    → what must be crossed
        → what can be reached
            → by which Agent
                → what flows outward from the reached locus
```

This is an important methodical advance. Reachability is capability-indexed:
the same lake occupies the same world-position, yet its practical accessibility
differs according to whether the traveler possesses `ṛddhi`. The distinction
from 3.53 recurs in mobile form. A locus has capacities, and an Agent has
capabilities; an operation becomes possible where those two specifications
meet.

The naming account adds a semantic operation:

```text
salient internal determination
    → designation of containing whole
```

This is controlled metonymic naming. “Jambūdvīpa” compresses a relation between
the whole region and a characteristic feature inside it. The alternative tree-
versus-fruit account should remain provenance-visible rather than being
flattened into a single unsupported etymology.

For the Organon projection:

```ts
interface OperationalRegion {
  traversalGraph: readonly Landmark[];
  target: "Anavatapta";
  accessRequirement: "riddhi";
  outgoingFlows: readonly ["Ganga", "Sindhu", "Shita", "Vakshu"];
  namingGround: "Jambu-tree" | "Jambu-fruit";
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It extracts the
method by which a named region becomes intelligible as a traversable,
capability-sensitive, flow-generating, and semantically designated system.
