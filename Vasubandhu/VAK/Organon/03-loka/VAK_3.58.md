# VAK_3.58

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अधः सहस्रैर्विंशत्या तन्मात्रोऽवीचिरस्य हि ।
>
> तदूर्ध्वं सप्त नरकाः सर्वेऽष्टौ षोडशोत्सदाः ॥ ३.५८ ॥

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening is reference-relative:

```text
asya [jambūdvīpasya]
    adhaḥ
    viṃśatyā sahasraiḥ [yojanānām]
    avīciḥ [asti]
```

`Tanmātraḥ` then transfers the same magnitude to Avīci's internal dimensions:

```text
avīciḥ
    tat-mātraḥ
    [udvedhena vistāreṇa ca]
```

The second line establishes plurality and attachment:

```text
tat-ūrdhvam
    sapta narakāḥ [santi]

sarve aṣṭau [narakāḥ]
    ṣoḍaśa-utsadāḥ [santi]
```

The Bhāṣya thus resolves both ambiguous pronouns: `asya` refers to Jambūdvīpa,
and `tat` in `tadūrdhvam` refers to Avīci.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Twenty thousand [yojanas] beneath this [Jambūdvīpa] is Avīci, itself of that
> same measure. Above it are seven hells. All eight have sixteen subsidiary
> regions.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Avīci lies 20,000 yojanas beneath Jambūdvīpa and itself measures 20,000
> yojanas in both depth and breadth, placing its lower floor 40,000 yojanas
> below the surface reference. On the principal account, seven further hells
> are arranged successively above it: Pratāpana, Tāpana, Mahāraurava, Raurava,
> Saṃghāta, Kālasūtra, and Saṃjīva. Another account places them at Avīci's
> sides. All eight principal hells have sixteen subsidiary regions.

The Jambūdvīpa referent, two internal dimensions, derived lower-floor depth,
names and principal arrangement of the seven hells, alternative lateral model,
and subsidiary structure are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System acquires a lower topology whose regions are simultaneously
> spatial, experiential, and karmic. Avīci is addressed relative to the
> inhabited surface, bounded by measurable dimensions, and distinguished by an
> experiential invariant: suffering there lacks interval. Neighboring painful
> domains differ in the temporal structure and causal classification of their
> inhabitants' experience. The physical container is therefore already an
> operational result-field for Agents, not an inert geometry awaiting an
> unrelated doctrine of karma.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Reference-relative address and derived boundary

```text
surface of Jambūdvīpa = reference 0
Avīci upper boundary  = −20,000
Avīci depth           =  20,000
Avīci lower floor     = −40,000
```

The lower-floor position is derived rather than separately stipulated. A
location plus an internal extent yields the far boundary of the domain.

### Inventory and topology are separable

```text
shared inventory:
    Avīci + seven named hells

principal topology:
    seven arranged successively above Avīci

alternative topology:
    seven arranged at Avīci's sides
```

Disagreement about relations among regions does not entail disagreement about
which regions exist. The model must therefore separate entity inventory from
topological arrangement and retain provenance for each topology.

### Region classified by experiential continuity

```text
Avīci:
    suffering(t) has no interval

other hells:
    suffering may be interrupted
```

The distinctive predicate is temporal and Agential. Spatial location alone
does not define the domain; the continuous structure of lived suffering does.

### Name tested by causal classification

The alternative explains Avīci as absence of an interval of pleasure. The
reply discriminates:

```text
pleasant feeling as vipāka
    → absent in the other hells also

pleasant feeling as naiḥṣyandika
    → not thereby excluded
```

An etymology is evaluated by whether its proposed characteristic uniquely
classifies the named domain. When it fails, a more exact causal type-distinction
is required.

### Multiplicative subsidiary structure

```text
8 principal hells
    × 16 utsadas each
        = 128 subsidiary-region assignments
```

This expands the lower world through a uniform attachment rule while
preserving the difference between principal and subsidiary domains.

### Explicit bridge toward Karma

The authoritative verse cited by Vasubandhu describes the hells as filled by
cruel karmas:

```text
raudra-karman
    → population / determination of painful result-domains
```

This does not replace the detailed causal analysis reserved for Karma-nirdeśa,
but it shows that Loka's physical topology is already indexed to karmic result.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.58 strongly supports the thought that the Physical World is methodically
Agential. The Kośa asks where the hells are and how large they are, but it does
not stop there. It determines them through the temporal continuity of feeling,
the revival of damaged bodies, modes of karmic result, and the cruel actions
with which these regions are said to be filled.

The resulting schema is:

```text
physical address
    + bounded extent
    + experiential regime
    + karmic result-type
        → operational world-domain
```

This looks forward to Karma-nirdeśa in a precise architectural sense. Loka
specifies the result-environments and the forms of embodied experience
available within them. Karma can then determine the actions, causal pathways,
and maturation rules by which Agents become connected to such environments.
That is an Organon synthesis, but its bridge is textually grounded here by
`vipāka` analysis and the citation of `raudra-karman`.

The naming dispute also models Abhidharma method. A proposed definition is not
accepted because it sounds plausible. It is tested for discriminating power:
if “absence of pleasant maturation” also applies elsewhere, it cannot by
itself uniquely explain this name. Technical causal analysis repairs the
failed classification.

For the Organon projection:

```ts
interface KarmicallyIndexedWorldDomain {
  spatialAddress: number;
  boundedExtent: readonly [number, number];
  experientialContinuity: "interrupted" | "uninterrupted";
  feelingResultType: "vipaka" | "naishyandika";
  karmicPopulationCondition: string;
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. Its purpose is to
show why “Physical World” cannot mean mere inert extension in this chapter.
The container is systematically specified as the field in which embodied
Agents undergo differentiated karmic results.
