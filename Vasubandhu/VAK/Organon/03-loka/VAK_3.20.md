# VAK_3.20

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> स प्रतीत्यसमुत्पादो द्वादशाङ्गस्त्रिकाण्डकः ।
>
> पूर्वापरान्तयोर्द्वे द्वे मध्येऽष्टौ परिपूरिणः ॥ ३.२० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> sa pratītyasamutpādo dvādaśāṅgas trikāṇḍakaḥ /
>
> pūrvāparāntayor dve dve madhye 'ṣṭau paripūriṇaḥ // 3.20 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
saḥ                     → sa
pratītya-samutpādaḥ     → pratītyasamutpādaḥ
dvādaśa-aṅgaḥ           → dvādaśāṅgaḥ
tri-kāṇḍakaḥ            → trikāṇḍakaḥ
pūrva-apara-antayoḥ     → pūrvāparāntayoḥ
dve dve                 → dve dve
madhye                  → madhye
aṣṭau                   → 'ṣṭau
paripūriṇaḥ             → paripūriṇaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| saḥ | nominative masculine singular pronoun | that previously described continuum |
| pratītyasamutpādaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | dependent co-arising; conditioned arising in mutual sequence |
| dvādaśāṅgaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | possessing twelve members |
| trikāṇḍakaḥ | nominative masculine singular adjective | divided into three sections |
| pūrvāparāntayoḥ | locative dual compound | at the prior and subsequent limits |
| dve dve | nominative neuter dual, distributive repetition | two at each of the two limits |
| madhye | locative singular | in the middle section |
| aṣṭau | nominative plural numeral | eight members |
| paripūriṇaḥ | genitive singular of `paripūrin` | of one who completes the relevant life-course |

The Bhāṣya supplies the antecedent of `saḥ`: the aggregate-continuum described
across three births in 3.18–3.19. It also construes `paripūriṇaḥ` as qualifying
the person whose present life traverses all the relevant successive states.

## 4. Grammar

The first half identifies and predicates:

```text
saḥ pratītyasamutpādaḥ
    dvādaśa-aṅgaḥ
    tri-kāṇḍakaḥ [asti]
```

> That dependent co-arising has twelve members and three sections.

The second half distributes the members:

```text
pūrva-anta + apara-anta
    dve dve

madhya
    aṣṭau
    paripūriṇaḥ
```

> At the prior and subsequent limits there are two each; in the middle there
> are eight for one who completes the course.

The Kārikā gives only the numerical architecture. The identification of the
three sections with past, present, and future births—and the restriction of
the complete middle to a fully traversed desire-realm life—comes from the
Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> That dependent co-arising is twelve-membered and three-sectioned: two at
> each of the prior and subsequent limits, and eight in the middle for one who
> completes the course.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The aggregate-continuum previously followed through rebirth is dependent
> co-arising articulated into twelve members across three lives: ignorance and
> formations belong to the past life; birth and aging-and-death to the future
> life; and the other eight to the complete present desire-realm life that
> traverses all the relevant stages.

The enumeration of the members, the three-life interpretation, and the scope
restriction are Bhāṣya determinations rather than additional Kārikā wording.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The beginningless wheel is now made an object of discriminative
> comprehension. One conditioned continuity is reflected into twelve
> functional moments and three temporal sections. The middle is decisive: it
> is where inherited conditions become an embodied course and where that
> course generates the conditions of another birth. Yet this is a
> complete-case model, not a sequence mechanically instantiated by every mode
> of existence.

The twelve members named by the Bhāṣya are:

```text
Prior limit:       avidyā, saṃskāra

Middle:            vijñāna, nāmarūpa, ṣaḍāyatana, sparśa,
                   vedanā, tṛṣṇā, upādāna, bhava

Subsequent limit:  jāti, jarāmaraṇa
```

Here `vijñāna` retains the controlled rendering **differentiated cognition**.

## 8. Logical Determination

### The wheel becomes a knowable structure

VAK 3.19 presented recursive becoming:

```text
conditioned continuity
    → embodiment
    → renewed affliction and action
    → another world
```

VAK 3.20 reflects that movement into a formal structure:

```text
one continuum
    → twelve functions
    → three temporal sections
    → 2 / 8 / 2 distribution
```

This is logical-scientific meditation in the strict project sense. The course
is neither watched as cosmic spectacle nor escaped by ascending to a subtler
station. Its determinate dependencies are held together as a single object of
comprehension.

### The middle is productive mediation

```text
past causes
    avidyā + saṃskāra

        ↓ condition

present embodied course
    vijñāna → nāmarūpa → ṣaḍāyatana → sparśa
    → vedanā → tṛṣṇā → upādāna → bhava

        ↓ projects

future result
    jāti + jarāmaraṇa
```

The middle does not merely occupy the time between two endpoints. It is the
complete mediation in which a received causal inheritance becomes an
embodied cognition-platform and produces a subsequent consequence.

### The schema is qualified by its field of application

The Bhāṣya asks whether all eight middle members occur in every birth and
answers no. The complete sequence excludes one who dies during the interval
and births in the form and formless realms. Its cited scriptural model is a
desire-realm life involving the entry of differentiated cognition into a
mother's womb.

```text
systematic unity
    does not entail
uniform empirical instantiation

complete model
    requires
specified conditions of application
```

The alternate seven/five division confirms that the causal relations remain
stable while their explanatory grouping may change.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This is the turn from **Ascension Dhyāna** to what we are calling
**Post-Ascension Dhyāna**.

Ascension treats the World System vertically:

```text
coarser station
    → subtler station
    → still subtler station
```

VAK 3.20 instead treats it genetically:

```text
What conditions a station?
How does embodied life acquire its footing?
How does the present course reproduce a future world-position?
How can the series cease when its conditions cease?
```

The comparison is project interpretation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It
helps specify the Bodhi-tree standpoint: after the ladder of attainments has
been exhausted, dependent origination becomes the object of a discursive,
logical meditation. `Anuloma` comprehends the conditioned construction of the
World; `pratiloma` will comprehend its determinate withdrawal.

The eightfold middle is therefore philosophically deeper than a third
geographical region. It is the Dharma of transition rendered analytically:
the lawful mediation by which past conditioning becomes present embodiment
and present appropriation becomes future birth.

But the Bhāṣya prevents us from turning that insight into an absolute diagram.
`Paripūriṇaḥ` marks a model with determinate scope. A rational World System
must disclose not only its law but also the conditions under which a complete
empirical course instantiates that law.

The extraordinary achievement here is quiet. Vasubandhu takes the whole
pageant of birth, death, transit, and renewed embodiment and converts it into
a structure that Prajñā can inspect. The spectacle of worlds yields to the
Dharma that generates their sequence.
