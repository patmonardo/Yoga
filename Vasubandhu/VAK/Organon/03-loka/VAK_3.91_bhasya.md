# VAK_3.91 Bhāṣya — The Twenty Intermediate Kalpas

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
antaḥkalpo 'mitād yāvad daśavarṣāyuṣas tataḥ /
utkarṣā apakarṣāś ca kalpā aṣṭādaśāpare // 3.91 //
```

> The first intermediate kalpa runs from immeasurable lifespan down to a
> ten-year lifespan. After that, the next eighteen kalpas have increases and
> decreases.

## Continuous Sanskrit

This unit begins with the Bhāṣya's completion of the first intermediate kalpa
and ends with its statement that all twenty have equal duration. The next
kārikā begins after this passage.

```iast
Vivartamāne loke ekānna viṃśatir antarakalpā aparimitāyuṣāṃ manuṣyāṇām
atikrāmanti. Aparimitāyuṣām eva kramatāṃ yāvad daśavarṣāyuṣo bhavanti.
So 'sau vivṛttānāṃ tiṣṭhatāṃ prathamo 'ntarakalpaḥ.

Utkarṣā apakarṣāś ca kalpā aṣṭādaśāpare // 3.91 //

Tasmād apare 'ṣṭādaśotkarṣā apakarṣāś cāṣṭādaśāntarakalpā bhavanti. Kathaṃ
kṛtvā? Tebhyo hi daśavarṣāyuṣkebhya utkarṣaṃ gacchantaḥ krameṇāśītivarṣa-
sahasrāyuṣo bhavanti. Punaś cāpakarṣaṃ gacchanto daśavarṣāyuṣo bhavanti.
Evaṃ dvitīyo 'ntarakalpaḥ. Evaṃ yāvad aṣṭādaśa.

Utkarṣa ekaḥ.

Ekāntarakalpo viṃśatitama utkarṣa evam apakarṣaḥ. Daśavarṣāyuṣkebhyo yāvad
aśītivarṣāyuṣāṃ manuṣyāṇām iti vartate.

Te 'śītisahasrād yāvad āyuṣaḥ.

Nātaḥ pareṇa vardhante. Yāvān eva cānyeṣām antarakalpānām utkarṣāpakarṣakālas,
tāvān eva prathamasyāpakarṣakālaḥ paścimasya cotkarṣakāla iti samānakālāḥ
sarve bhavanti.
```

The prose is punctuated and spaced for reading. In the final sentence,
`utkarṣāpakarṣakāla` is read as the time occupied by the increase and decrease
of the middle kalpas; the first kalpa's decline and the last kalpa's increase
each occupy that same duration.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

While the world is expanding, nineteen intermediate kalpas pass during which
human beings have immeasurable lifespans. As those lifespans gradually
decrease, they eventually reach ten years. This is the first intermediate
kalpa of the formed world while it stands.

The next eighteen kalpas each have an increase and a decrease. Beginning from
a ten-year lifespan, human lifespans gradually increase until they reach
eighty thousand years; they then decrease again until they reach ten years.
This is the second intermediate kalpa, and the same pattern continues through
eighteen.

There is one further increase: the twentieth intermediate kalpa consists of
an increase, with human lifespans rising from ten years up to eighty thousand.
They do not increase beyond that. The time occupied by the increase and
decrease in the other intermediate kalpas is equal to the time occupied by
the first kalpa's decrease and the final kalpa's increase. Thus all are equal
in duration.

## Natural Movement

The Bhāṣya distinguishes the nineteen intermediate kalpas that pass during
world-formation from the twenty that articulate the formed world's standing
period. Of those twenty, the first is a one-way decline from immeasurable
lifespan to ten years; the next eighteen each rise from ten to eighty thousand
and descend again; the last is a one-way rise from ten to eighty thousand.
The one-way first and last kalpas are not shorter: their durations match the
full increase-and-decrease pattern of each middle kalpa.

The verse names the first decline and the next eighteen paired movements. The
Bhāṣya supplies the final, increase-only kalpa and the equality of duration.
This completes the unit before the next kārikā takes up the standing-world
duration.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: equal temporal spans can have different internal articulations.
That is a comparison prompted by the passage, not a separate technical claim
made by Vasubandhu.
