# VAK_3.68

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विंशत्यन्तरितान्येषां सुभूमीनि चतुर्दिशम् ।
>
> पूर्वोत्तरे पारिजातः सुधर्मा दक्षिणावरे ॥ ३.६८ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> viṃśatyantaritāny eṣāṃ subhūmīni caturdiśam /
>
> pūrvottare pārijātaḥ sudharmā dakṣiṇāvare // 3.68 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
viṃśati-antaritāni         → viṃśatyantaritāny
eṣām                       → eṣāṃ
su-bhūmīni                 → subhūmīni
catur-diśam                → caturdiśam
pūrva-uttare               → pūrvottare
pārijātaḥ                  → pārijātaḥ
sudharmā                   → sudharmā
dakṣiṇa-avare              → dakṣiṇāvare
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| viṃśatyantaritāni | nominative neuter plural compound | separated by twenty |
| eṣām | genitive plural pronoun | of these, referring to the four gardens |
| subhūmīni | nominative neuter plural compound | pleasant or excellent grounds |
| caturdiśam | adverbial directional expression | in the four directions |
| pūrvottare | locative singular compound | in the north-eastern quarter |
| pārijātaḥ | nominative masculine singular | Pārijāta, the named divine tree |
| sudharmā | nominative feminine singular | Sudharmā, the named divine assembly hall |
| dakṣiṇāvare | locative singular compound | in the south-western quarter |

The Bhāṣya explicitly glosses `dakṣiṇāvara` as `dakṣiṇapaścima`, south-west,
and determines `subhūmi` as a deva recreation-place. The name `Sudharmā` is
retained rather than converted into a doctrinal claim by etymology alone.

## 4. Grammar

The first half relates a second set of recreation-grounds to the four gardens
of 3.67:

```text
eṣām [udyānānām]
    catur-diśam
    subhūmīni [catvāri santi]
    viṃśati-antaritāni
```

> In the four directions from these [gardens] are pleasant grounds separated
> by twenty.

The Bhāṣya supplies both the count and unit: four grounds, each located twenty
yojanas from its associated garden.

The second half assigns two named sites to opposite inter-cardinal quarters:

```text
pūrva-uttare [digbhāge]
    pārijātaḥ [asti]

dakṣiṇa-avare [digbhāge]
    sudharmā [asti]
```

> Pārijāta is in the north-east; Sudharmā is in the south-west.

The Bhāṣya identifies Pārijāta as a kovidāra tree and Sudharmā as the divine
assembly hall.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> In the four directions from these [gardens] are pleasant grounds separated
> by twenty [yojanas]. Pārijāta is in the north-east, and Sudharmā in the
> south-west.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Twenty yojanas from each of the four gardens lies a pleasant ground, itself
> a recreation-place of the gods; through mutual rivalry these four grounds
> spread their beauty. Outside the city in the north-east stands Pārijāta, a
> kovidāra tree supporting the Trāyastriṃśas' most intense sensual enjoyment.
> Its root-base measures five yojanas, its height one hundred, and its spread
> of branches and foliage fifty. In full bloom its fragrance travels one
> hundred yojanas downwind and fifty against the wind. In the south-west is
> Sudharmā, the divine assembly hall where the seated gods deliberate what is
> to be done and what is not to be done.

The fourfold count, twenty-yojana spacing, recreational function, rivalry,
Pārijāta's classification and dimensions, fragrance measures, and Sudharmā's
institutional and deliberative functions are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The summit field differentiates two opposed but coordinated functions.
> Pārijāta intensifies conditioned enjoyment and occasions analysis of causal
> transmission; Sudharmā institutes collective deliberation concerning action
> and non-action. The same produced World therefore contains pleasure,
> empirical objection, competing causal models, and practical judgment. Deva
> existence does not eliminate the question of what ought to be done; it gives
> that question an assembly, a location, and a communal procedure.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Extension from gardens to grounds

Let `G₁–G₄` be the four gardens and `B₁–B₄` the four pleasant grounds:

```text
for each n in {1, 2, 3, 4}:
    distance(Gₙ, Bₙ) = 20 yojanas
    function(Bₙ) = divine recreation
```

The verse relates two four-member sets through measured separation. It does
not merely add four more names to an inventory.

### Rivalry as distributed production

```text
B₁ ↔ B₂ ↔ B₃ ↔ B₄
    mutual rivalry
        → increased total splendor
```

On the Bhāṣya account, the beauty of the field is generated relationally:
each ground contributes under comparison with the others.

### Pārijāta measurement schema

```text
root-base extent       = 5 yojanas
height                 = 100 yojanas
branch/foliage spread  = 50 yojanas
fragrance downwind     = 100 yojanas
fragrance against wind = 50 yojanas
```

Structural dimensions and causal reach are separately typed. Tree height is
not the same predicate as fragrance range even where the number is equal.

### Fragrance objection and models

```text
ordinary causal expectation:
    wind carries scent downwind

reported determination:
    scent also occurs against the wind

question:
    how can the reported effect occur under opposing wind?
```

The Bhāṣya preserves several responses:

```text
scope-restriction model:
    “against the wind” does not pass beyond the tree's own extent

continuation model:
    fragrance efficacy
        + resistance by gentle divine wind
        → weaker further fragrance-series
        → earlier cessation

substrate model A:
    series supported by its own material constituents

substrate model B:
    air becomes perfumed
```

The teachers accept both substrate models; the text states no exclusive rule.
Observation, propagation mechanism, substrate, resistance, and authority must
therefore remain separate fields.

### Practical deliberation

```text
devas seated in Sudharmā
    consider possible conduct
        → determine kṛtya
        → determine akṛtya
```

This is a Practical-Reason structure:

```text
possible action X
    → ought X be done?
        → X is to be done | X is not to be done
```

The assembly does not merely register events; it discriminates conduct under
the opposition of the to-be-done and the not-to-be-done.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.68 is where the divine spectacle unexpectedly becomes explicit
methodology. Its two named sites organize two sides of conditioned agency:

```text
Pārijāta
    → enjoyment
    → sensed effect
    → causal objection
    → alternative explanatory models

Sudharmā
    → assembly
    → possible conduct
    → collective deliberation
    → practical determination
```

The fragrance discussion is disciplined Abhidharma. A marvelous report does
not end inquiry; it begins a discrimination of scope, causal efficacy,
continuity, supporting condition, and attenuation. Even where no single model
is imposed, the alternatives are sharply typed.

Sudharmā is still more important for the Organon. The devas inhabit a world of
extraordinary pleasure, yet they must deliberate `kṛtya/akṛtya`. Divine
station does not itself settle what is to be done. In that exact sense, the
normative question is higher than the mere location or enjoyment-status of
the Agent.

This lends real support to our working hypothesis that the Human Dharma may
be the Absolute Dharma. The cautious form of the claim is:

```text
if even devas must deliberate the to-be-done and not-to-be-done,
then ascent in Loka does not supersede the form of Practical Reason.
```

The text does not call this form “Human Dharma” or “Absolute Dharma”; that is
our comparative reconstruction. But it does show that the divine world cannot
replace practical discrimination with bliss. Pārijāta supplies maximal
enjoyment; Sudharmā remains necessary.

```ts
interface PracticalAssembly<Action> {
  deliberate(action: Action): "ToBeDone" | "NotToBeDone";
}

type FragranceModel =
  | { substrate: "OwnMaterialConstituents"; authority: "AcceptedByTeachers" }
  | { substrate: "PerfumedAir"; authority: "AcceptedByTeachers" };
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It keeps
practical determination distinct from causal explanation while recognizing
that the complete World System must support both.
