# VAK_3.43

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> नैकाग्राचित्तयोरेतौ निर्वात्यव्याकृतद्वये ।
>
> क्रमच्युतौ पादनाभिहृदयेषु मनश्च्युतिः ॥ ३.४३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> naikāgrācittayor etau nirvāty avyākṛtadvaye /
>
> kramacyutau pādanābhihṛdayeṣu manaścyutiḥ // 3.43 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
na                          → na
ekāgra-acittayoḥ            → ekāgrācittayoḥ
etau                        → etau
nirvāti                     → nirvāty
avyākṛta-dvaye              → avyākṛtadvaye
krama-cyutau                → kramacyutau
pāda-nābhi-hṛdayeṣu         → pādanābhihṛdayeṣu
manaḥ-cyutiḥ                → manaścyutiḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| na | negative particle | excludes two cognitive conditions |
| ekāgrācittayoḥ | locative dual compound | in concentrated consciousness and in a mindless state |
| etau | nominative masculine dual pronoun | these two, death and arising from 3.42 |
| nirvāti | third-person singular present | becomes extinguished; here the arhat's final extinction |
| avyākṛtadvaye | locative neuter singular compound | in one of two indeterminate minds |
| kramacyutau | locative singular compound | in gradual falling-away / gradual death |
| pāda | first member of locative coordination | feet; cessation locus for one going to a lower destination |
| nābhi | second member | navel; cessation locus for one going to human birth |
| hṛdaya | third member | heart; cessation locus for one going to deva birth and, in the principal account, an arhat |
| manaścyutiḥ | nominative feminine singular compound | cessation or falling-away of mind |

The compound `ekāgrācittayoḥ` is resolved as `ekāgra-acittayoḥ`, not as one
undivided category. `Etau` carries forward death and arising from the preceding
verse.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening is grammatically dependent upon 3.42:

```text
etau [cyutodbhavau]
    na ekāgra-acittayoḥ

these two [death and arising]
    [occur] neither in concentrated consciousness
    nor in a mindless state
```

The next clause has the arhat as its contextually supplied subject:

```text
arhan
    avyākṛta-dvaye
    nirvāti

the arhat
    becomes finally extinguished
    in one of two indeterminate minds
```

The last hemistich states a bodily-locus classification:

```text
krama-cyutau
    pāda-nābhi-hṛdayeṣu
    manaḥ-cyutiḥ

in gradual death,
    cessation of mind occurs
    at the feet, navel, or heart
```

The destination-specific correlation is supplied by the continuing Bhāṣya;
the three loci are alternatives, not successive stations in every death.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> These two [death and arising] occur neither in concentrated consciousness nor
> in a mindless state. The [arhat] becomes extinguished in one of two
> indeterminate minds. In gradual death, cessation of mind occurs at the feet,
> navel, or heart.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Death and rebirth do not occur while consciousness is one-pointedly
> concentrated or while mind is absent. The arhat enters final extinction in
> one of two indeterminate minds: a mind associated with ordinary bodily
> deportment or a maturation-result consciousness. In sudden death, the bodily
> faculty together with mind disappears abruptly. In gradual death, the final
> cessation of differentiated cognition occurs at a bodily locus correlated
> with destination: at the feet for one going to a lower destiny, at the navel
> for one going to human birth, and at the heart for one going to deva birth.
> For an arhat, the principal account also gives the heart; another opinion
> gives the crown of the head.

The arhat as supplied subject, the two indeterminate minds, the distinction
between sudden and gradual death, and the destination–locus mapping are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Embodiment does not terminate through concentration or through the bare
> absence of mind. At the boundary, support-bound consciousness must become
> operative and then cease. For the liberated one, final extinction occurs in
> a weak, indeterminate cognitive register suited to termination. Where death
> is gradual, the bodily faculty withdraws progressively and differentiated
> cognition ceases at a destination-correlated locus. The World System thus
> maps departure as an embodied operation with determinate cognitive, ethical,
> and somatic conditions.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Two excluded states

```text
death / arising
    not in concentrated consciousness
    not in mindlessness
```

Concentrated consciousness is deliberately established, supportive, and
located at a dissimilar level. Mindlessness supplies no operative cognitive
locus. In the latter case:

```text
bodily support begins fatal alteration
    → support-bound consciousness manifests
        → consciousness ceases
```

Thus the return of mind is a necessary intermediate determination before
death, according to this analysis.

### The arhat's final cognitive register

```text
arhat's final extinction
    → airyāpathika-citta
      or vipāka-citta
    → both avyākṛta
```

The Bhāṣya's reason is functional:

```text
weak indeterminate consciousness
    → conducive to termination of the conscious series
```

Wholesome and unwholesome status are not being judged inferior in general;
the claim concerns compatibility with this terminal operation.

### Sudden and gradual death

```text
sudden death:
    bodily faculty + mind disappear abruptly

gradual death:
    bodily faculty withdraws progressively
    → final cognition ceases at a determinate locus
```

The Bhāṣya compares the progressive contraction to water cast upon a heated
stone.

### Alternative loci, not universal stages

The verse's three places must not be construed as:

```text
feet → navel → heart
    for every dying being
```

The Bhāṣya instead construes:

```text
destination class
    → corresponding terminal bodily locus
```

This is a cross-classification of destination and cessation-location. The
arhat case retains an explicitly reported disagreement.

### Vital-point impairment

For ordinary gradual death, the Bhāṣya adds:

```text
excess predominance of water, fire, or wind domain
    → severe impairment of vital points
        → intense pain and loss of function
            → death
```

The vital points are not literally cut like wood. “Cutting” names functional
destruction. Earth is excluded under one medical explanation; an alternative
derives the pattern by analogy with dissolution of the container-world.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.43 continues the Kośa's Practical Theory of Consciousness by mapping
body-disposal as a typed operation. The text does not simply announce that
mind leaves the body. It distinguishes sudden from gradual termination,
specifies the cognitive state compatible with departure, correlates bodily
locus with destination, and preserves dissent where the arhat's locus is
disputed.

The most important interpretive restraint is also the most revealing. Feet,
navel, and heart are not a universal ascent-channel. They are alternative
terminal addresses selected by destination within the Bhāṣya's model. We
should therefore not import a suṣumnā sequence into the primary reading.

Within the Agent projection:

```ts
  cessationLocus: "feet" | "navel" | "heart" | "crown";
  locusAuthority: "principal" | "alternative";
}
```

This remains an Organon reconstruction. It makes explicit that destination
and bodily locus are linked fields, while the crown reading belongs only to an
alternative authority.

The support relation from 3.41–3.42 remains active. As the bodily faculty
contracts, consciousness bound to that support undergoes its final operation.
Body and mental differentiated cognition are not two independent objects
whose later separation is unexplained. Their reciprocal support is precisely
what changes at death.

The arhat's indeterminate final mind gives another important determination.
Final extinction is not described here as the triumph of an acutely heroic
mental state. It occurs through a weak cognitive register compatible with the
cutting of the series. The operation is terminal because it no longer projects
or nourishes another becoming.

For semantic modeling, `marmaccheda` reinforces a rule already established by
the food verses: technical classification follows function, not surface image.
The vital point is called “cut” because it ceases to function, not because it
has been divided like timber. The Kośa continually moves from ordinary words
to operational discrimination.
