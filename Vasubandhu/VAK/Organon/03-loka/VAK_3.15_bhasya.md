# VAK_3.15 Bhāṣya — Inverted Cognition at the Place of Rebirth

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> viparyastamatir yāti gatideśaṃ riraṃsayā /
> gandhasthānābhikāmo 'nya ūrdhvapādas tu nārakaḥ // 3.15 //

> With inverted understanding, the intermediate being goes to the place of
> its destiny through longing. Another is attracted toward a scent-place; but
> the hell-bound intermediate being goes with feet upward.

The Bhāṣya explains how a destination already projected by karma becomes an
object of approach. Its central claim is not that desire freely chooses the
next existence, but that karmically conditioned perception presents the
destination in a form toward which desire can move.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

This intermediate existence arises for the purpose of reaching the place of
its destiny. “With inverted understanding, it goes to the place of its
destiny through longing.” For even when it is very far away, it sees its own
place of rebirth with an eye produced through the power of karma.

There it sees its mother and father in sexual union. If it is male, masculine
desire arises in it for the mother; if it is female, feminine desire arises
for the father. Aversion arises toward the other parent. Thus it is stated in
the *Prajñapti*: “At that time one or the other of two mental states becomes
present in the gandharva, accompanied either by attraction or by aversion.”
Distorted by these two, and wishing to enjoy, it embraces that place and
imagines that condition in itself. Having reached that impure place of the
womb, it becomes delighted and settles there. Its aggregates then become
dense and the aggregates of intermediate existence disappear; in this way it
has been reborn.

If it is male, it develops crouched in the mother's right side, facing her
back; if female, in her left side, facing her abdomen. It may then be asked:
if the fetus is intersex, does it remain in accordance with the desire through
which it embraced the place? There is no sexually indeterminate intermediate
being, the commentary answers, because an intermediate being has its faculties
complete. It therefore enters as female or male and remains in the
corresponding position; afterward, as the fetus develops, it becomes intersex.

The following question is considered. Do the great elements of the semen and
blood themselves, through the force of karma, assume the status of supports
for the faculties? Or do other great elements arise through karma while
depending upon them?

Some say that the same elements do so. The semen and blood, which lack
faculties, cease together with the intermediate existence, and material
possessing faculties appears. This is described as occurring at the same
moment according to the principle governing the cessation of a seed and the
arising of its sprout. Understood in this way, the scriptural statement “one
arisen as a *kalala* from the impurity of mother and father” is well
explained. So too is the statement addressed to the monks that over a long
time they have nourished a form that is a drop of blood.

Others say that entirely different great elements arise, as in the case of a
worm upon a leaf. There is nevertheless no conflict with the sūtra, because
its statement about the *kalala* expresses an association: it arises in
dependence upon the parental impurity. Such is the account of entry into
egg-born and womb-born modes of birth. In the other cases, they say, the
explanation should be adapted as appropriate. The following application is
found there.

“Another is attracted toward a scent-place.” One entering a moisture-born
mode of birth goes to its place of rebirth through attraction, toward an
impure or a pure place as the case may be. One entering a spontaneous mode of
birth does so through attraction to the place itself.

How can there be attraction to a place in the hells? Because cognition is
inverted. The being sees itself afflicted by cold wind and driving rain, and
sees fire blazing in the hells; desiring warmth, it runs there. Or it sees
itself afflicted by hot wind, sunlight, and fire, and sees coolness in the
hells; desiring cold, it runs there.

The former teachers say that it runs while seeing itself in the condition in
which it performed the karma productive of that rebirth, and seeing those
beings as well.

The intermediate existence of a deva goes upward, as though rising from a
seat. Those of humans, animals, and pretas move in a manner corresponding to
humans and the rest. “But the hell-bound intermediate being goes with feet
upward.” This is supported by the verse: “They indeed fall into hell with
feet upward and faces downward—those who revile the seers, the restrained
ascetics.”

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The unit contains two connected investigations:

```text
karmically projected destination
    → destination perceived through a karma-produced eye
    → attraction and aversion configure approach
    → intermediate aggregates disappear at rebirth
    → dispute over the new body's material basis
    → different attractions for other modes of birth
    → inverted cognition makes hell appear as relief
```

The womb-entry account is Vasubandhu's exposition of inherited Abhidharma
doctrine. The *Prajñapti* is cited for the pair of affective states. The
embryological question receives two attributed answers: `eke` holds that the
parental material itself is transformed into faculty-bearing material;
`apare` holds that new great elements arise while supported upon it. The text
does not announce a final adjudication between them. The representation of a
past karmic condition is separately assigned to the former teachers.

## 5. Karmically Conditioned Vision and Womb-Entry

The intermediate being does not first survey several possible lives. Its eye
is produced through karmic power, and what it sees is expressly “its own”
place of rebirth. Attraction and aversion therefore operate within a course
already delimited by the karma that projected the intermediate existence.

The sequence is exact:

```text
own rebirth-place becomes visible
    → parents are perceived
    → desire for one and aversion toward the other arise
    → the being appropriates the perceived situation
    → delight fixes it in the womb-place
    → intermediate aggregates disappear as embodied aggregates become dense
```

`Adhimucyate` is important here. The being takes or resolves the perceived
condition as its own; this is part of the distortion, not a lucid recognition
of embryological reality. The account concerning sex, fetal position, and
intersex development should likewise be retained as the historical doctrinal
model stated by the commentary, not projected into modern embryology.

## 6. The Embryological Dispute

The disagreement concerns material continuity, not whether karma conditions
rebirth:

```text
same-element account
parental semen and blood cease in a non-faculty-bearing state
    → arise as material supporting faculties
    → seed-and-sprout analogy

other-element account
new great elements arise through karma
    → depend upon parental material
    → leaf-and-worm analogy
```

Both sides preserve causal dependence upon the parental contribution. They
disagree over whether the faculty-bearing body is a new phase of those very
elements or consists of other elements arising upon that support. Their rival
readings of scripture turn on the same issue: material identity versus
dependence and association.

## 7. Other Birth-Modes and Hell's Counterfeit Relief

For moisture-birth, scent marks the attractive place. For spontaneous birth,
the place itself is the object of attraction. Hell generates the sharpest
objection because no one would desire torment under an adequate description.
The answer is not that hell is desired as hell. It is presented under inverted
cognition as relief from the being's present distress:

```text
affliction by cold → fire appears as warmth → movement toward hell
affliction by heat → cold appears as relief → movement toward hell
```

Here “counterfeit appearance” is a justified analytic description: the
appearance offers the form of a remedy while conveying the being into the
opposite of relief. Yet the deception is not an independent cosmic agent in
this passage. It is the perceptual efficacy of karma joined to craving and
inverted understanding.

## 8. Bhāṣya Decisions for the Kārikā

The prose fixes the verse's compressed terms:

- `gatideśa` is the being's own already projected place of rebirth.
- `viparyastamati` denotes cognition under which attraction and aversion
  mispresent the transition, most explicitly when hell appears as relief.
- `riraṃsayā` is the longing that carries the being into womb-birth; it does
  not establish an unconditioned choice of destiny.
- `gandhasthānābhikāma` belongs specifically to the account of moisture-birth.
- `ūrdhvapāda` distinguishes the inverted orientation of the hell-bound
  intermediate being and is reinforced by a cited verse.

The kārikā thus coordinates four birth-modes without reducing their proximate
conditions to one uniform image.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage distinguishes determination from manifestation. Karma determines
the destination, but the destination must still appear within a cognition and
be approached through an affective orientation. The outflow into a new
existence is therefore also a projection in the precise sense that a
karmically produced faculty discloses a field already typed as “one's own
place,” while desire supplies its practical pull.

This does not make the Bhāṣya itself a theory of the Oculus, nor does it turn
`viparyaya` into the complete Yoga account of misapprehension. It gives a
narrower and valuable result: causal efficacy includes the configuration of
appearance. The hell example makes that result especially visible because the
destination can operate only by appearing as the remedy for the distress that
drives the being toward it.

The material dispute then prevents “projection” from becoming merely mental.
Arrival requires a determinate material support, and the commentators dispute
the kind of continuity involved. Perceptual, affective, karmic, and material
determinations cooperate, but they should not be collapsed into one process.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit at 126.19–127.25, including the
womb-entry sequence and embryological controversy omitted from the earlier
research pass. The translation is continuous and provisional. The damaged
second scriptural citation, several gendered technical expressions, and the
precise force of the seed–sprout and leaf-worm analogies require comparison
with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations in a
later learning pass.

The present analysis deliberately stops before VAK 3.16. It preserves the two
embryological positions and the former teachers' account as attributed views,
and it does not convert the inherited reproductive model into a modern
biological claim.
