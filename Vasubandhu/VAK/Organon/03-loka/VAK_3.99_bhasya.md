# VAK_3.99 Bhāṣya — Three Calamities and Their Durations

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
kalpasya śastrarogābhyāṃ durbhikṣeṇa ca nirgamaḥ /
divasān sapta māsāṃś ca varṣāṇi ca yathākramam // 3.99 //
```

> The intermediate age ends through weapons, disease, and famine; their
> durations, respectively, are seven days, months, and years.

The Bhāṣya specifies that the durations accumulate: seven days; seven months
and seven days; seven years, seven months, and seven days. Here `kalpa` is
explicitly an intermediate kalpa.

## Continuous Sanskrit

The source splits this verse around its extended exposition: the first line
is cited before the account of the calamities; the second line follows the
question about their duration. The full unit runs from `[187|24]` through
`[188|25]`, stopping before the next verse.

```iast

āha:

Kalpasya śastrarogābhyāṃ durbhikṣeṇa ca nirgamaḥ.

Tribhir antarakalpasya niryāṇaṃ bhavati: śastreṇa, rogeṇa, durbhikṣeṇa ca.
Antarakalpasya niryāṇakāle daśavarṣāyuṣo manuṣyā adharmarāgaraktā bhavanti,
viṣamalobhābhibhūtā mithyādharmaparītāḥ.

Teṣāṃ vyāpāda utkarṣaṃ gato; anyonyaṃ sattvaṃ dṛṣṭvā tīvram āghātacittaṃ
vadhakacittaṃ ca pratyupasthitaṃ bhavati. Tadyathedānīṃ mṛgalubdhakasyāraṇyakaṃ
mṛgaṃ dṛṣṭvā, te yadyad eva gṛhṇanti—kāṣṭhaṃ vā loṣṭaṃ vā—tat teṣāṃ tīkṣṇaṃ
śastraṃ prādurbhavati. Te 'nyonyaṃ sattvaṃ jīvitād vyaparopayanti.

Punaḥ kalpasya niryāṇakāle daśavarṣāyuṣāṃ manuṣyāṇāṃ taireva doṣair amanuṣyā
ītiṃ muñcanti. Teṣām asādhyā vyādhyādayaḥ prādurbhavanti, yato mriyante.

Punar daśavarṣāyuṣāṃ taireva doṣair devā varṣaṃ notsṛjanti, yato durbhikṣaṃ
jāyate: cañcaḥ, śvetāsthi, śalākāvṛttiś ca.

Kathaṃ cañcaḥ? Dvābhyāṃ kāraṇābhyām. Ya idānīṃ samavāyaḥ, sa tadānīṃ cañca

kālaṃ kurvanti; samudreṣu cānāgatajanatānugrahārthaṃ bījāny avasthāpayanti.
Atas tad durbhikṣaṃ cañcam ity ucyate.

Kathaṃ śvetāsthi? Dvābhyāṃ kāraṇābhyām. Teṣāṃ hi śuṣkarukṣakāyāṇāṃ kālaṃ
kurvatām āśv eva asthīni śvetāni bhavanti; bubhukṣāhatāś ca śvetāny asthīni
saṃhṛtya kvāthayitvā pibanti.

Kathaṃ śalākāvṛttiḥ? Dvābhyāṃ kāraṇābhyām. Te hi sattvāḥ śalākoddeśikayā
gṛheṣv āmiṣaṃ saṃvibhajante: “Adya gṛhasvāmī bhokṣyate, śvo gṛhasvāminī,”
ity evamādi; dhānyasthānavivarebhyaś ca śalākayā dhānyaphalāni niṣkṛṣya
bahulodakena kvāthayitvā pibanti.

Evaṃ varṇayanti: “Yenaikāham api prāṇātipātaviratiḥ saṃrakṣitā bhavati,
ekaharītakī vā saṃghāyaikapiṇḍapāto vā satkṛtyānupradatto bhavati, sa teṣu
śastrarogadurbhikṣāntarakalpeṣu notpadyate.”

Atha kiyantaṃ kālam etāni śastrāghātarogadurbhikṣāṇi teṣāṃ sattvānāṃ bhavanti?

Divasān sapta māsāṃś ca varṣāṇi ca yathākramam // 3.99 //

Śastrakaḥ prāṇātipātaḥ sapta divasān bhavati; rogaḥ sapta māsāṃś ca divasāṃś
ca; durbhikṣaṃ saptavarṣāṇi ca māsāṃś ca divasāṃś ceti samuccayārthaś cakāraḥ.

Tadā ca dvayor dvīpayoḥ teṣāṃ pratirūpakāṇi bhavanti. Vyāpāda udrekaprāpto
bhavati, vaivastryadaurbalye jighatsāpipāse ca. Yad uktam, evam anyasyām api
saṃvartanyāṃ veditavyaṃ yathāyogam iti.
```

Punctuation and sandhi are lightly regularized. The source's famine names and
the expression `vaivastryadaurbalye` are obscure; they are retained rather
than given a falsely certain technical interpretation.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

For people whose lifespans are ten years, the intermediate age reaches its
conclusion. How does it end? Through three calamities: weapons, disease, and
famine. At that time people are attached to unrighteous desire, overwhelmed
by extreme greed, and surrounded by false teaching.

Their hostility reaches its height. Seeing one another, an intense impulse
to assault and kill arises. As when a hunter sees a wild deer, whatever they
pick up—a stick or a clod of earth—appears as a sharp weapon in their hands.
They kill one another.

Again, at the age's end, through those same faults, nonhuman beings release a
plague among people with ten-year lifespans. Incurable diseases and the like
arise, and people die from them. Again, through those faults, the gods
withhold rain and famine arises in forms called `cañca`, `śvetāsthi` (“white
bone”), and `śalākāvṛtti`.

Why `cañca`? For two reasons. What is now called a collection is then called
`cañca`; a container is also called `cañca`. People overcome by hunger and
weakness gather and die; they also set seeds aside in the oceans for the
benefit of people yet to come. Hence that famine is called `cañca`.

Why “white bone”? For two reasons. The bodies of those who die become dry
and withered, and their bones quickly turn white. Beaten by hunger, people
collect white bones, boil them, and drink the broth.

Why `śalākāvṛtti`? For two reasons. People distribute food in their homes
using tally-sticks: “Today the master of the house will eat; tomorrow the
mistress,” and so on. They also use a stick to draw grain and its fruit from
the openings of grain stores, boil it with much water, and drink it.

It is said that one who has guarded abstention from taking life even for a
single day, or has respectfully given even one harītakī fruit or a single
bowl of alms-food to the Saṅgha, will not be reborn in those intermediate
ages of weapons, disease, or famine.

How long do these calamities last for those beings? The weapon-calamity—the
killing—lasts seven days. Disease lasts seven months and seven days. Famine
lasts seven years, seven months, and seven days. The Bhāṣya explicitly says
that the repeated “and” is cumulative.

At that time corresponding forms of these calamities occur on the other two
continents. Hostility becomes excessive, together with `vaivastrya`,
weakness, hunger, and thirst. As stated, this is to be understood
appropriately in other contractions as well.

## Natural Movement

The unit connects the ten-year lifespan with the end of an intermediate
kalpa. It describes three alternative calamity forms and their durations,
then adds two qualifications: counterparts arise on the other continents,
and the pattern applies, as appropriate, to other contractions. `Ca` is
cumulative in the duration clause: 7 days; 7 months plus 7 days; 7 years
plus 7 months plus 7 days.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: the passage relates ethical deterioration to distinct
world-crises and gives those crises determinate durations. This is a
comparison prompted by the Bhāṣya, not an additional system asserted by
Vasubandhu.
