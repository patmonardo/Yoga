# VAK_3.32

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तज्जाः षड्वेदनाः पञ्च कायिकी चैतसी परा ।
>
> पुनश्चाष्टादशविधा सा मनोपविचारतः ॥ ३.३२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tajjāḥ ṣaḍvedanāḥ pañca kāyikī caitasī parā /
>
> punaścāṣṭādaśavidhā sā manopavicārataḥ // 3.32 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
tat-jāḥ                    → tajjāḥ
ṣaṭ-vedanāḥ                → ṣaḍvedanāḥ
pañca                      → pañca
kāyikī                     → kāyikī
caitasī                    → caitasī
parā                       → parā
punaḥ ca                   → punaś ca
aṣṭādaśa-vidhā             → aṣṭādaśavidhā
sā                         → sā
manaḥ-upavicārataḥ         → manopavicārataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| tajjāḥ | nominative feminine plural; `tad` + `ja` | born from those, namely the six contacts of 3.30 |
| ṣaḍvedanāḥ | nominative feminine plural | six feelings |
| pañca | nominative feminine plural numeral | five |
| kāyikī | nominative feminine singular, collective predicate | bodily; supported by the five material faculties |
| caitasī | nominative feminine singular | mental; supported by mind alone |
| parā | nominative feminine singular | the other, the remaining one |
| punaḥ | adverb | again; introducing a further classification |
| aṣṭādaśavidhā | nominative feminine singular | having eighteen divisions |
| sā | nominative feminine singular pronoun | that, namely mental feeling |
| manopavicārataḥ | ablative singular used as ground of classification | according to mental ranging; by the determination of the mental traversals |

The sixfold division follows causal origin in contact. The five-and-one division
follows support. The eighteenfold division follows the structure of mental
ranging.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first hemistich states the production and then divides the result:

```text
tajjāḥ ṣaḍ vedanāḥ
    = from those [six contacts], six feelings

pañca kāyikī
    = five [constitute] bodily [feeling]

parā caitasī
    = the other [is] mental
```

`Kāyikī` is singular because it names one class containing the first five
feelings. `Parā` identifies the sixth, mind-contact-born feeling, as the other
class.

The second hemistich subjects that mental feeling to a further division:

```text
sā punaḥ aṣṭādaśavidhā
    manopavicārataḥ

that [mental feeling] is again eighteenfold
    according to mental ranging
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the matrix presupposed by `aṣṭādaśavidhā`: gladness,
distress, and equanimity, each ranging through the six object-fields.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Born from those [contacts] are six feelings. Five are bodily; the other is
> mental. That [mental feeling] is again eighteenfold according to mental
> ranging.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Six feelings arise in relation to the six contacts: feeling born from eye-,
> ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-contact. The first five are called
> **bodily feeling** because they are supported by material faculties; the
> remaining one is called **mental feeling** because it is supported by mind
> alone. Mental feeling is further established as eighteen mental rangings:
> six of gladness, six of distress, and six of equanimity, corresponding in
> each case to the six object-fields of form, sound, smell, taste, tangible,
> and dharma.

The material-faculty and mind-only grounds, as well as the internal `3 × 6`
construction of the eighteen, are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Contact becomes feeling in a sixfold articulation. Five feelings retain the
> bodily support of the material faculties; the sixth has mind alone as its
> support. This mental feeling is not merely an affective mark attached to an
> already completed appearance. As gladness, distress, or equanimity, it
> determines the mind's repeated ranging through each of the six object-fields.
> Feeling thereby becomes a law of traversal within the appearing World.

`Manopavicāra` is not bare occurrence in mind:

```text
vedanā-vaśena
    manasaḥ
        viṣayeṣu
            punaḥ punaḥ vicaraṇam

under the force of feeling,
    mind's repeated ranging
        through the object-fields
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### Three successive grounds of articulation

```text
six contacts
    → six feelings                    [ground: causal relation]

five material-faculty-supported
    → bodily feeling                  [ground: support]

one mind-only-supported
    → mental feeling                  [ground: support]

three mental-feeling modes
    × six object-fields
    → eighteen mental rangings        [ground: mode + object-field]
```

The eighteen are not eighteen new substances. They are the product of two
axes:

| affective mode \ object-field | form | sound | smell | taste | tangible | dharma |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gladness | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| distress | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| equanimity | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Thus:

```text
by intrinsic character alone: 3
by association with mind alone: 1
by object-support alone: 6
by all required grounds together: 3 × 6 = 18
```

### Feeling determines traversal

```text
contact-field
    → felt mode
        → repeated mental ranging through an object-field
```

The arrow here expresses the order of determination, not an uncontested
chronology of separate moments. Feeling is operational: it helps determine
which formed content the mind ranges through again.

### The temporal relation remains disputed

```text
Vaibhāṣika:
    contact ∥ feeling
    relation: coexistent causality

rival account:
    contact → feeling
    relation: successive moments
```

Vasubandhu tests these models against sūtra rather than allowing the treatise
schema to decide by itself. He insists that wherever differentiated cognition
occurs, contact and feeling conjoined with it must be admitted; the surrounding
debate does not reduce without remainder to a simple pipeline.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This is where our word **Learning** becomes exact. Feeling is not a passive
value stored after contact. Under its force, mind repeatedly ranges through
the six object-fields. What has been contacted therefore acquires a route of
return. The World System begins to learn because its own felt determinations
condition how its appearances are traversed again.

In the Agent projection, 3.32 supplies a compact Client SDK:

```ts
type AffectiveMode = "gladness" | "distress" | "equanimity";
type ObjectField =
  | "form"
  | "sound"
  | "smell"
  | "taste"
  | "tangible"
  | "dharma";

interface MentalRanging {
  affectiveMode: AffectiveMode;
  objectField: ObjectField;
}
```

This projection does not translate `manopavicāra` as a modern software policy.
It displays its logical form: affective mode and object-field jointly determine
one of eighteen possible rangings. The DataFrame is generated by crossed
grounds, not by eighteen independently fabricated Things.

The Oculus analogy now becomes stronger. Formed content entered through
contact is not merely displayed and archived. Feeling affects its subsequent
traversal in the Agent. In modern terms we may model this as a routing or
revisitation condition, provided we preserve the textual fact beneath the
model: Vasubandhu says that mind ranges repeatedly through its fields under
the force of feeling.

This is also why bodily feeling is excluded from `manopavicāra`. The Bhāṣya
does not deny bodily feeling; it distinguishes its operation. It is neither
supported by mind alone nor discriminatively ranging. The mental class begins
where feeling can articulate a repeated passage through formed and
name-bearing content.

The causality debate prevents premature engineering. If contact and feeling
may be coexistent factors, the Agent cannot assume that every determination
must be serialized as one completed stage emitting the next. Learning may be
a mutually conditioned station within differentiated cognition, not merely a
queue of externally ordered events.

Finally, Vasubandhu's declaration that sūtra, not treatise, is authoritative
states a discipline for our own construction. The schema is powerful, but it
must remain answerable to the field from which it was learned. The model does
not legislate appearance; it must preserve the relations appearance discloses.
