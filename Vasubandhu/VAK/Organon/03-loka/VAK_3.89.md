# VAK_3.89

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> त्रयो मुहूर्त्ताहोरात्रमासाः द्वादशमासकः ।
>
> संवत्सरः सोनरात्रः कल्पो बहुविधः स्मृतः ॥ ३.८९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> trayo muhūrttāhorātramāsā dvādaśamāsakaḥ /
>
> saṃvatsaraḥ sonarātraḥ kalpo bahuvidhaḥ smṛtaḥ // 3.89 //

The first line is metrically compressed and does not expose its arithmetic
cleanly. The Bhāṣya supplies the distribution: thirty lavas make a muhūrta,
thirty muhūrtas a day-night, and thirty day-nights a month. `Sonarātraḥ` is
likewise controlled by the explanation of six `ūnarātras`, omitted or
deficient nights used in constructing the year.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
trayaḥ / [triṃśat]        → trayo
muhūrta                   → muhūrttā-
ahas-rātra                → -ahorātra-
māsaḥ                     → -māsāḥ / māsā
dvādaśa-māsakaḥ           → dvādaśamāsakaḥ
saṃvatsaraḥ               → saṃvatsaraḥ
sa-ūna-rātraḥ             → sonarātraḥ
kalpaḥ                    → kalpo (before b-)
bahu-vidhaḥ               → bahuvidhaḥ
smṛtaḥ                    → smṛtaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| muhūrta | compound member | time-unit composed of thirty lavas |
| ahorātra | compound member | complete day-night cycle composed of thirty muhūrtas |
| māsa | compound member / nominative singular in resolved construe | month composed of thirty day-nights |
| dvādaśamāsakaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | consisting of twelve months |
| saṃvatsaraḥ | nominative masculine singular | year |
| sonarātraḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | accompanied by calendrical `ūnarātra` adjustment |
| kalpaḥ | nominative masculine singular | cosmic duration classified by World-process |

The Bhāṣya is indispensable here. Surface segmentation alone cannot safely
recover either the repeated factor thirty or the technical force of
`sonarātraḥ`.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening continues the `triṃśadguṇottarāḥ` rule announced in 3.88. The
Bhāṣya distributes three parallel equations:

```text
triṃśat lavāḥ
    muhūrtaḥ [bhavanti]

triṃśat muhūrtāḥ
    ahorātraḥ [bhavanti]

triṃśat ahorātrāḥ
    māsaḥ [bhavanti]
```

> Thirty lavas make a muhūrta; thirty muhūrtas make a day-and-night; thirty
> day-and-nights make a month.

The annual construction is:

```text
dvādaśa-māsakaḥ
    saṃvatsaraḥ
    sa-ūna-rātraḥ [bhavati]
```

> A year consists of twelve months, with deficient-night adjustments.

The final clause opens a classification rather than another simple conversion:

```text
kalpaḥ
    bahu-vidhaḥ
    smṛtaḥ
```

> The kalpa is taught as having several kinds.

The Bhāṣya enumerates four: antarakalpa, saṃvartakalpa, vivartakalpa, and
mahākalpa.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> [Thirty lavas make] a muhūrta, [thirty muhūrtas] a day-and-night, and
> [thirty day-and-nights] a month. A year consists of twelve months with
> deficient-night adjustments. The kalpa is taught as manyfold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Thirty lavas constitute one muhūrta, thirty muhūrtas one complete
> day-and-night, and thirty day-and-nights one month. Twelve months constitute
> a year: four months each of winter, summer, and the rains, with six
> `ūnarātras` omitted during the year as calendrical corrections. Having
> completed the measure of the year, the Kośa distinguishes four kinds of
> kalpa: intermediate, contraction, expansion, and great kalpa.

The repeated factor thirty, variable proportions of day and night, seasonal
division, six annual omissions, and fourfold kalpa classification are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Temporal enclosure reaches the ordinary calendar through repeated
> aggregation, but the calendar cannot remain an untouched arithmetic grid.
> Its ideal monthly series is corrected through periodic omissions so that
> counted time remains aligned with the appearing cycle. At the kalpa level,
> measure changes form again: a vast duration is not understood merely by its
> size, but by the phase of World-transformation it encloses. Time becomes
> calendar through correction and cosmic cycle through process-content.

`Ahorātra` names the complete cycle. The Bhāṣya explicitly permits the night
portion to be longer, shorter, or equal; equality of the two subperiods is not
part of the unit's definition.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Repeated temporal enclosure

```text
30 lavas       = 1 muhūrta
30 muhūrtas    = 1 day-night
30 day-nights  = 1 nominal month
12 months      = 1 nominal year
```

From 3.88:

```text
1 lava = 7,200 moments
```

Therefore:

| Unit | Moments |
|---|---:|
| muhūrta | 216,000 |
| day-night | 6,480,000 |
| nominal month | 194,400,000 |
| twelve nominal months | 2,332,800,000 |

These moment totals are arithmetic consequences of the conversion ladder, not
separately stated figures in the text.

### Variable internal partition

The invariant is the whole day-night:

```text
DaylightDuration + NightDuration = AhoratraDuration
```

But its partition varies:

```text
NightDuration > DaylightDuration
or
NightDuration < DaylightDuration
or
NightDuration = DaylightDuration
```

Thus the enclosure remains stable while the proportions of its enclosed phases
change.

### Calendrical correction

The nominal annual construction is:

```text
12 × 30 day-nights = 360 day-nights
```

The Bhāṣya then specifies:

```text
ūnarātras omitted per year = 6
```

Hence the adjusted calendar count is:

```text
360 − 6 = 354 day-nights
```

The 354 count is a direct arithmetic consequence of the stated annual
omissions. The mnemonic distributes the six corrections across the two halves
of each of the three four-month seasons.

### Change from unit to process-type

The year remains primarily a composed calendar quantity. `Kalpa`, however, is
immediately dispatched into four semantic classes:

```text
KalpaType =
    Intermediate
    | Contraction
    | Expansion
    | GreatCycle
```

The classification question is no longer only:

```text
How many lower units does it contain?
```

It becomes:

```text
Which complete process of World-change does it enclose?
```

The verse declares this shift; subsequent verses define the processes.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.89 shows that the Mind of the Enclosure is not blind aggregation. It can
preserve a whole while adjusting the internal organization needed for that
whole to remain adequate to appearance.

```text
nominal calendar
    → supplies repeatable arithmetic structure

observed periodicity
    → requires correction

ūnarātra rule
    → reconciles the enclosure with its appearing contents
```

This is precisely the difference between the Mind of the Enclosure and a mere
count of the Enclosed. A purely enclosed collection would insist that twelve
months means an untouched 360 day-nights. The organizing calendar knows when a
boundary must be adjusted so that the year continues to enclose the cycle it
is meant to determine.

The day-night supplies a second example. Its identity persists although the
relative lengths of daylight and night vary. The whole is not reducible to a
fixed arrangement of its phases:

```text
stable enclosure
    + variable internal relation
    = one determinate temporal cycle
```

At `kalpa`, enclosure becomes explicitly historical. The kalpa is determined
by the World-process it contains—expansion, contraction, an intermediate
phase, or the comprehensive cycle. This prepares the decisive movement from
measuring Loka to modelling its state transitions.

```ts
interface AdjustedYear {
  nominalMonths: 12;
  nominalDayNights: 360;
  omittedNights: 6;
  adjustedDayNights: 354;
}

type KalpaKind =
  | "Antarakalpa"
  | "Samvartakalpa"
  | "Vivartakalpa"
  | "Mahakalpa";
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It keeps
arithmetic composition, correction policy, and process-classification as
distinct operations.
