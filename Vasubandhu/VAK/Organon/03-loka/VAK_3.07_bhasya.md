# VAK_3.07 Bhāṣya — Unwilling Residence and the Four Supports

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> anicchāvasanān nānye catasraḥ sthitayaḥ punaḥ /
> catvāraḥ sāsravāḥ skandhāḥ svabhūmāv eva kevalam // 3.07 //

> No other states are sentient abodes, because beings reside there
> unwillingly. In another classification there are four cognition-supports:
> the four aggregates with outflows, each exclusively in its own ground.

The first half closes the ninefold classification of sentient abodes by
excluding the bad destinies. The second half introduces a distinct scriptural
classification of four `vijñānasthiti`. Here “support” conveys the Bhāṣya's
functional explanation: these are the four aggregates upon which
differentiated cognition becomes established.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

For in these nine abodes beings dwell by their own wish.

“There are no others because residence is unwilling.” What are the others?
The bad destinies. In them, unwilling beings are made to reside by the
rākṣasas of karma. They do not dwell there by choice. Therefore those states
are not sentient abodes, just as a place of confinement is not an abode.

In one sūtra seven stations of differentiated cognition are taught. In
another, “there are again four supports.” What are the four? A support of
differentiated cognition associated with form, one associated with feeling,
one associated with perception, and one associated with formations.

What is their own-nature? Respectively, they are “the four aggregates with
outflows.” And these are “exclusively in their own ground,” not in another
ground. Why? Because a `sthiti` is an establishment. Under the power of
craving, differentiated cognition does not become established in aggregates
belonging to a dissimilar ground.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The passage contains two compact movements:

```text
nine sentient abodes
    bad destinies excluded because residence is unwilling

seven cognition-stations taught in one context
    four aggregate-supports taught in another context
```

The expository voice makes explicit both objects suppressed by the verse:
“no others” means no bad destinies among the sentient abodes, and the four
`sthiti` are supports of differentiated cognition associated with form,
feeling, perception, and formations.

No opposing school is named. The questions are expository. The image of
`karmarākṣasas` belongs to the Bhāṣya's explanation and should be preserved as
an image rather than transformed into a separately asserted class of demonic
beings.

## 5. Unwilling Occupancy Is Not Abode

The criterion of `sattvāvāsa` is stronger than bare location:

```text
sentient being occupies a state
    does not yet entail
that state is a sentient abode
```

The nine recognized abodes are states in which beings dwell `svecchayā`, by
their own wish. Beings in the bad destinies are `akāmakāḥ`, unwilling. Their
presence is karmically enforced and resembles confinement rather than
dwelling.

This is a classification of the present relation to residence, not a claim
that beings outside the bad destinies freely selected every condition of
their lives. Nor does karmic causation imply that suffering beings presently
desire or morally deserve their pain. The Bhāṣya's exact point is narrower:
forced occupancy does not satisfy the criterion by which the nine states are
called abodes.

## 6. The Rākṣasas of Karma

`Karmarākṣasair āvāsyante` is deliberately forceful: beings are “made to
reside by the rākṣasas of karma.” The phrase gives figurative form to a
determining causal power experienced as coercive.

```text
past karmic determination
    places a being in a bad destiny

present wish
    opposes that residence

phenomenal form of the determining power
    karmic rākṣasa
```

The text does not identify a particular god or demon who administers karma.
It personifies karmic compulsion. This is important for reading Māra and other
figures fairly: a demonic image may express a function within conditioned
existence without requiring an independently sovereign evil principle.

The full rational analysis of karma belongs to Karmanirdeśa. Here the image
does one local job: it distinguishes imposed placement from willing dwelling.

## 7. Four Aggregate-Supports in Their Own Ground

The second classification is not another list of cosmic populations. It names
four supports associated with differentiated cognition:

| Support named in the prose | Aggregate that constitutes it |
|---|---|
| associated with form | form aggregate with outflows |
| associated with feeling | feeling aggregate with outflows |
| associated with perception | perception aggregate with outflows |
| associated with formations | formations aggregate with outflows |

`Sāsrava` restricts these supports to conditioned aggregates with outflows.
The cognition aggregate is not included in this fourfold list; the reason for
that exclusion is raised and argued in VAK 3.08, not in the present unit.

The final restriction is equally important. A support functions only in its
own `bhūmi`, not in a dissimilar ground. The Bhāṣya explains this through the
meaning of `sthiti`: it is establishment. Cognition, governed by craving, is
established upon aggregates belonging to its corresponding ground. The text
therefore gives a ground-indexed relation, not four free-standing substances.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

`Anicchāvasanāt` is causal: no further states count as sentient abodes because
beings reside in the bad destinies unwillingly. “Because residence is
undesirable” would be too weak and would obscure the explicit contrast
between `svecchayā` and `akāmakāḥ`.

`Punaḥ` marks a second scriptural classification. The seven
`vijñānasthiti` of VAK 3.05–3.06 classify modes of sentient existence; these
four classify the aggregate-supports upon which cognition is established.
Their shared name does not make the enumerated members identical.

`Catvāraḥ sāsravāḥ skandhāḥ` refers to form, feeling, perception, and
formations. “Aggregates with outflows” retains the Sanskrit structure;
“contaminated aggregates” is the doctrinal shorthand.

`Svabhūmāv eva kevalam` is emphatically restrictive: each functions only in
its own ground. The omitted final prose in the research witness confirms that
this is a rule of establishment rather than simply a geographical statement.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The first half distinguishes placement from dwelling:

```text
objective placement
    a being is there

relation to placement
    the being dwells willingly or is confined unwillingly
```

This gives Loka a moral history without yet giving the full theory of moral
action. Present experience may confront its determining ground as an alien
power—the “rākṣasas of karma”—because the causal history of the placement is
not transparent within the present wish.

For the Organon learning pass, the image may later be understood as a
counterfeit appearance of absolute external power: karma appears as an alien
demon when the causal continuity generating the present configuration is not
recognized. That is a project hypothesis, not the Bhāṣya's explicit
formulation, and it must not diminish the reality of involuntary suffering.

The second half supplies a restrained architectural result. Differentiated
cognition does not establish itself without relations to form, feeling,
perception, and formations, and these relations are valid only within their
proper ground. This is enough for the translation pass: a cognition-support
is relational, conditioned, and ground-specific.
