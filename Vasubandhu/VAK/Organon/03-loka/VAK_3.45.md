# VAK_3.45

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तत्र भाजनलोकस्य संनिवेशमुशन्त्यधः ।
>
> लक्षषोडशकोद्वेधमसंख्यं वायुमण्डलम् ॥ ३.४५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tatra bhājanalokasya saṃniveśam uśanty adhaḥ /
>
> lakṣaṣoḍaśakodvedham asaṃkhyaṃ vāyumaṇḍalam // 3.45 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
tatra                       → tatra
bhājana-lokasya             → bhājanalokasya
saṃniveśam                  → saṃniveśam
uśanti                      → uśanty
adhaḥ                       → adhaḥ
lakṣa-ṣoḍaśaka-udvedham     → lakṣaṣoḍaśakodvedham
asaṃkhyam                   → asaṃkhyam
vāyu-maṇḍalam               → vāyumaṇḍalam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| tatra | indeclinable | there; in the newly announced account |
| bhājanalokasya | genitive singular compound | of the container-world |
| saṃniveśam | accusative masculine singular | arrangement, ordered disposition, configuration |
| uśanti | third-person plural present | they declare, teach, or maintain |
| adhaḥ | indeclinable | below, beneath |
| lakṣa-ṣoḍaśaka-udvedham | accusative singular compound | having a vertical extent of sixteen hundred-thousands |
| asaṃkhyam | accusative singular | incalculable; the Bhāṣya applies this to breadth |
| vāyumaṇḍalam | accusative neuter singular compound | wind-maṇḍala, wind-disk |

`Lakṣa` is one hundred thousand. Sixteen `lakṣas` therefore gives 1,600,000;
the Bhāṣya supplies `yojana` as the unit.

## 4. Grammar

The principal construction is:

```text
[ācāryāḥ]
    bhājanalokasya saṃniveśam
    uśanti

[the teachers]
    declare the arrangement
    of the container-world
```

The arrangement begins with an appositional object:

```text
adhaḥ
    vāyumaṇḍalam
        lakṣa-ṣoḍaśaka-udvedham
        asaṃkhyam

below,
    a wind-maṇḍala
        sixteen hundred-thousands in vertical extent,
        incalculable [in extent]
```

The agents of `uśanti` are unexpressed. The Bhāṣya's `icchanti`, “they
maintain,” confirms that this is a received cosmological account. The Kārikā
does not itself state the dimension in which the maṇḍala is incalculable; the
Bhāṣya identifies it as breadth.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> There they declare the arrangement of the container-world: below is an
> incalculable wind-maṇḍala, sixteen hundred-thousands in vertical extent.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> They describe the arrangement of the great world-system as follows. Below,
> established in space, a wind-maṇḍala comes into formation through the
> governing efficacy of the karma of all beings. It is 1,600,000 yojanas in
> vertical extent, incalculable in breadth, and extraordinarily firm.

The great world-system, establishment in space, karmic formation, yojana unit,
lateral dimension, and firmness are Bhāṣya determinations rather than wording
contained in the Kārikā.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The Kośa begins the Physical World not with a self-standing lump of matter,
> but with the configuration of a common world-support. The first articulated
> support is an immense wind-domain beneath the world-system. Although shared,
> stable, and objective for its inhabitants, its formation is attributed to
> the collective causal efficacy of embodied Agents. The World System is thus
> neither private appearance nor causally independent scenery: it is a common,
> conditioned field within which sentient trajectories can be embodied.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| bhājanaloka | container-world | shared receptacle or environmental world, distinguished from `sattvaloka` |
| saṃniveśa | arrangement / configuration | ordered disposition of the world-system |
| trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra-lokadhātu | great world-system / great trichiliocosm | scale specified by the Bhāṣya |
| adhaḥ | below | initial spatial determination of the arrangement |
| vāyumaṇḍala | wind-maṇḍala | first support described beneath the container-world |
| udvedha | vertical extent | height or thickness, distinguished from breadth |
| pariṇāha | breadth / lateral extent | dimension to which the Bhāṣya applies `asaṃkhya` |
| asaṃkhya | incalculable | beyond enumeration; here said of lateral extent |
| ākāśa-pratiṣṭha | established in space | Bhāṣya description of the wind-maṇḍala's situation |
| abhinirvṛtta | come into formation | genetic determination supplied by the Bhāṣya |
| karmādhipatya | governing efficacy of karma | causal condition of the common world-support's formation |
| sarvasattva | all sentient beings | bearers of the karma whose efficacy produces the support |

`Maṇḍala` is retained because “disk” alone can falsely suggest only a visible
solid object. Here it denotes a vast, bounded cosmological domain with a
supporting function.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Structural transition

```text
sattvaloka explained
    → bhājanaloka now configured
```

The change of topic is from sentient trajectories to their shared world-field,
not from causality to something outside causality.

### The first configuration

```text
container-world
    → ordered arrangement
        → below: wind-maṇḍala
```

The verse begins from a support relation. Mountains, continents, and visible
habitats have not yet appeared.

### Dimensional discrimination

```text
vertical extent
    = sixteen lakṣas of yojanas

lateral extent
    = incalculable
```

The second equality depends upon the Bhāṣya. It prevents `asaṃkhya` from being
misread as a denial of the expressly numbered vertical measure.

### Genetic ground supplied by the Bhāṣya

```text
karma of all beings
    → governing efficacy
        → formation of the common wind-support
```

This relation does not make the resulting field private or insubstantial. The
Bhāṣya emphasizes its firmness. Conversely, firmness does not make it
uncaused or independent of the sentient causal order.

### Epistemic restraint

The verbs `uśanti` and `icchanti` mark the configuration as what cosmological
teachers declare or maintain. The study should therefore preserve the
received-model status of the account rather than silently convert it into
modern empirical physics.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.45 opens the Physical World as a theory of world-configuration. The
decisive term is not only `bhājanaloka`, but `saṃniveśa`: a world is presented
through an ordered placement of mutually supporting domains. The first member
of that order is invisible habitation-wise yet structurally indispensable—the
wind-maṇḍala below.

The Bhāṣya makes this more philosophically powerful by locating the genesis of
the common support in `sarvasattvānāṃ karmādhipatyena`, the governing efficacy
of the karma of all beings. The Physical World is not simply placed beside the
Human World as a second unrelated thing. The causal histories of beings and
the formation of their common container belong to one World System.

That relation may be modeled without reducing the world to an individual's
projection:

```ts
interface SharedWorldSupport {
  domain: "wind-mandala";
  position: "below";
  verticalExtentYojanas: 1_600_000;
  lateralExtent: "incalculable";
  formationCondition: "collective-karmic-efficacy";
  stability: "extremely-firm";
}
```

This is an Organon explanatory projection, not Vasubandhu's terminology. Its
important constraint is semantic: collective causal formation and common
objective stability must both be retained. Neither a privately imagined world
nor independently self-grounding matter expresses the Bhāṣya's determination.

The remaining physical verses can now articulate the higher supports upon
this first field. We should let that construction proceed in its own sequence
before drawing wider Sāṃkhya, Yoga, or transcendental correspondences.
