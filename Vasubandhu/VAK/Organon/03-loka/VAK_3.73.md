# VAK_3.73

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चतुर्द्वीपकचन्द्रार्कमेरुकामदिवौकसाम् ।
>
> ब्रह्मलोकसहस्रं च साहस्रश्चूडिको मतः ॥ ३.७३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> caturdvīpakacandrārkamerukāmadivaukasām /
>
> brahmalokasahasraṃ ca sāhasraś cūḍiko mataḥ // 3.73 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
catur-dvīpaka-candra-arka-
  meru-kāma-divaukasām     → caturdvīpakacandrārkamerukāmadivaukasām
brahma-loka-sahasram       → brahmalokasahasraṃ
ca                         → ca
sāhasraḥ                   → sāhasraś
cūḍikaḥ                    → cūḍiko
mataḥ                      → mataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| caturdvīpaka | compound member | four-continent world-unit |
| candra | compound member | moon |
| arka | compound member | sun |
| Meru | compound member | Meru, the axial mountain |
| kāmadivaukasām | genitive masculine plural compound | of the celestial inhabitants of the sensual domain |
| brahmalokasahasram | nominative neuter singular compound | a thousand Brahma-worlds |
| ca | conjunction | and |
| sāhasraḥ | nominative masculine singular | thousandfold |
| cūḍikaḥ | nominative masculine singular | small / minor, naming the first thousandfold aggregate |
| mataḥ | nominative masculine singular past participle of `√man` | regarded, held, or defined as |

The first compound is compressed: the understood count `sahasra` ranges over
its coordinated members. The Bhāṣya expands the series and identifies the
precise continents and deva classes included.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The genitive compound supplies the heterogeneous things counted by thousands:

```text
catur-dvīpakānām
candrāṇām
arkāṇām
merūṇām
kāma-divaukasām
    [sahasram]

brahma-lokānām
    sahasram ca
```

The predication then names the resulting aggregate:

```text
[etat]
    sāhasraḥ cūḍikaḥ [lokadhātuḥ]
    mataḥ
```

> This is regarded as the small thousandfold world-system.

The Bhāṣya makes clear that the count applies to each constituent class, not
to one undifferentiated collection of a thousand mixed items.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> A thousand four-continent units, moons, suns, Merus, sensual-domain devas,
> and Brahma-worlds is regarded as the small thousandfold world-system.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> One thousand Jambūdvīpas, one thousand Pūrvavidehas, one thousand
> Aparagodānīyas, and one thousand Uttarakurus; one thousand suns, moons, and
> Sumerus; one thousand complete sets of sensual devas from the
> Cāturmahārājakāyikas through the Paranirmitavaśavartins; and one thousand
> Brahma-worlds together constitute what is called the small thousandfold
> world-system.

The separate thousandfold count for all four continents, the complete limits
of the sensual-deva series, and the definition as a `sāhasra-cūḍika lokadhātu`
are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The fully articulated local cosmos now changes logical role: what was a
> whole becomes one repeatable member of a larger whole. The larger system is
> not produced by stretching one Meru, one sun, or one divine hierarchy to a
> greater size. It is constituted by one thousand coordinated instances of a
> complete heterogeneous schema. Multiplicity therefore preserves structure:
> each repetition carries continents, celestial bodies, axial mountain,
> inhabitants, and higher world together.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Base world-unit schema

Let one local world-unit `W` be represented by the ordered structure:

```text
W = {
    continents: {
        Jambūdvīpa,
        Pūrvavideha,
        Aparagodānīya,
        Uttarakuru
    },
    moon,
    sun,
    Sumeru,
    sensual-deva-series: {
        Cāturmahārājakāyika,
        Trāyastriṃśa,
        Yāma,
        Tuṣita,
        Nirmāṇarati,
        Paranirmitavaśavartin
    },
    Brahma-world
}
```

The Bhāṣya's enumeration determines the membership of the repeatable schema.

### Thousandfold aggregation

```text
small thousandfold world-system
    = {W₁, W₂, ..., W₁₀₀₀}
```

Consequently:

```text
count(Jambūdvīpa)      = 1,000
count(Pūrvavideha)     = 1,000
count(Aparagodānīya)   = 1,000
count(Uttarakuru)      = 1,000
count(sun)             = 1,000
count(moon)            = 1,000
count(Sumeru)          = 1,000
count(kāma-deva series)= 1,000
count(Brahma-world)    = 1,000
```

The repeated count is synchronized across heterogeneous component types.

### Replication versus enlargement

```text
rejected construal:
    enlarge one local world by factor 1,000

Bhāṣya construal:
    instantiate complete local schema 1,000 times
```

The resulting plurality contains many suns and Merus, not one gigantic sun or
Meru.

### Role transformation

```text
at local scale:
    W = complete totality

at thousandfold scale:
    W = member-unit

aggregate:
    Σ(W₁ ... W₁₀₀₀) = new complete totality
```

Whole and unit are relative roles. The internal structure of `W` remains
determinate while its external function changes under aggregation.

### Structural invariance

For every instance `Wᵢ`:

```text
schema(Wᵢ) = schema(W)
```

The definition requires preservation of component-types and their coordinated
membership. Numerical multiplication without structural invariance would not
produce the defined world-system.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.73 is the clearest statement yet that `Loka` means World System rather
than a solitary Earth-like object. The preceding verses painstakingly formed
one domain through geography, embodiment, celestial stations, object-fields,
and faculty-ranges. Now that entire articulated order becomes iterable:

```text
constructed whole
    → typed unit
        → thousand coordinated instances
            → higher-order whole
```

This is a semantic change of role, not an erasure of content. The local world
does not become less complete when it functions as a member of the larger
aggregate. Conversely, the aggregate is not a heap: it is defined through the
repetition of complete structured members.

The human station is therefore not lost when scale becomes cosmic. Every
four-continent unit includes a Jambūdvīpa. Under our working Human-Dharma
hypothesis, that means the site of human practical disclosure recurs within
the schema rather than appearing as a single accidental point in an otherwise
divine expanse. This recurrence is a project inference; the verse itself
enumerates Jambūdvīpas, not an Absolute Human Dharma.

```ts
interface LocalWorldUnit {
  continents: readonly ["Jambudvipa", "Purvavideha", "Aparagodaniya", "Uttarakuru"];
  moon: "Moon";
  sun: "Sun";
  axis: "Sumeru";
  kamaDevaHierarchy: "CompleteSixClassSeries";
  upperWorld: "BrahmaWorld";
}

interface SmallThousandfoldWorldSystem {
  instances: readonly LocalWorldUnit[];
  invariant: "instances.length === 1000";
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It shows
why the unit must be modeled as a typed bundle before multiplication. If the
relations among components are discarded, one can preserve the numeral one
thousand while losing the World System being counted.

The semantic–syntactic method also guards the scope of `sahasra`. Syntactically
it can look like a final numeral attached only to Brahma-world. Semantically,
the Bhāṣya distributes it across every coordinated member of the compressed
compound. The count has broad scope; each component retains its distinct type.
