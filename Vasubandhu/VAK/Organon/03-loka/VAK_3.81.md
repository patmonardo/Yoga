# VAK_3.81

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> आरूप्ये विंशतिः कल्पसहस्राण्यधिकाधिकम् ।
>
> महाकल्पः परीत्ताभात् प्रभृत्यर्धमधस्ततः ॥ ३.८१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> ārūpye viṃśatiḥ kalpasahasrāṇy adhikādhikam /
>
> mahākalpaḥ parīttābhāt prabhṛty ardham adhas tataḥ // 3.81 //

The second line follows the reading confirmed by the Bhāṣya citation and
exposition. It resolves the corrupted compression in the immediate metrical
witness as `prabhṛti ardham adhas tataḥ`: beginning from Parīttābha the unit is
a mahākalpa; below that boundary it is half.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ārūpye                 → ārūpye
viṃśatiḥ               → viṃśatiḥ
kalpa-sahasrāṇi        → kalpasahasrāṇi
adhika-adhikam         → adhikādhikam
mahā-kalpaḥ            → mahākalpaḥ
parītta-ābhāt          → parīttābhāt
prabhṛti               → prabhṛty (before a-)
ardham                 → ardham
adhas                  → adhas
tataḥ                  → tataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| ārūpye | locative neuter singular | in the formless realm |
| viṃśatiḥ | cardinal numeral | twenty; distributive increment in thousands |
| kalpasahasrāṇi | nominative neuter plural compound | thousands of kalpas |
| adhikādhikam | distributive adverbial compound | successively greater; twenty thousand more at each station |
| mahākalpaḥ | nominative masculine singular | a great kalpa; the full unit from Parīttābha upward |
| parīttābhāt | ablative masculine singular | beginning from the Parīttābha class as boundary |
| prabhṛti | indeclinable | beginning with, onward from |
| ardham | nominative/accusative neuter singular | one half, namely half a mahākalpa |
| adhas | adverb | below |
| tataḥ | ablatival adverb | from that boundary |

`adhikādhikam` governs the numerical sequence; `mahākalpaḥ ... ardham`
governs the magnitude of the unit called `kalpa`. These are separate
determinations.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first line states an increasing series within the formless realm:

```text
ārūpye
    [āyuḥ]
    viṃśatiḥ kalpa-sahasrāṇi
    adhika-adhikam [bhavati]
```

> In the formless realm, lifespan is twenty thousand kalpas and successively
> twenty thousand more.

The Bhāṣya distributes this over four stations, yielding 20,000, 40,000,
60,000, and 80,000 kalpas.

The second line answers an implicit unit question—what kind of `kalpa` is
being counted?

```text
parīttābhāt prabhṛti
    [kalpaḥ] mahā-kalpaḥ

tataḥ adhas
    [kalpaḥ mahā-kalpasya] ardham
```

> Beginning with Parīttābha, one kalpa is a mahākalpa; below that, one kalpa
> is half of a mahākalpa.

The words `prabhṛti` and `adhas tataḥ` establish complementary scopes around
one boundary. They do not place Parīttābha simultaneously above and below the
same division.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> In the formless realm, lifespan is twenty thousand kalpas, successively
> increasing by that amount. Beginning with Parīttābha, the kalpa is a
> mahākalpa; below that, it is half.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Lifespans in the four formless stations are respectively 20,000 kalpas in
> the Sphere of Infinite Space, 40,000 in the Sphere of Infinite Cognition,
> 60,000 in the Sphere of Nothingness, and 80,000 at the Summit of Existence.
> In these lifespan calculations, `kalpa` means a full mahākalpa from the
> Parīttābha class upward; below Parīttābha, half a mahākalpa is conventionally
> counted as one kalpa.

The names and exact distribution of the four formless stations, the explicit
question about which kind of kalpa is intended, and the conventional status
of the lower unit are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The highest saṃsāric stations remain quantitatively temporal: their immense
> lifespans form an exact additive series. Yet the number alone is
> insufficient. The World System also determines what counts as one unit at a
> given level. Crossing the Parīttābha boundary changes the magnitude carried
> by the term `kalpa`; the same unit-name therefore receives its determinate
> value from its cosmological scope. Measurement requires quantity and typed
> unit together.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Formless lifespan series

Let `n` be the ordinal of the formless station from 1 through 4:

```text
L(n) = n × 20,000 kalpas
```

| n | Formless station | Lifespan |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Sphere of Infinite Space | 20,000 kalpas |
| 2 | Sphere of Infinite Cognition | 40,000 kalpas |
| 3 | Sphere of Nothingness | 60,000 kalpas |
| 4 | Summit of Existence | 80,000 kalpas |

This is an additive progression:

```text
Lₙ₊₁ = Lₙ + 20,000 kalpas
```

### Unit-resolution function

The numeral cannot be evaluated until the realm supplies the meaning of one
`kalpa`:

```text
KalpaMagnitude(station) =
    FullMahakalpa       if station ≥ Parīttābha
    HalfMahakalpa       if station < Parīttābha
```

Thus:

```text
<quantity: 20,000, unit: FullMahakalpa>
```

is a complete temporal measure, whereas bare `20,000` or even untyped
`20,000 kalpas` is not.

### Lower conventional kalpa

The Bhāṣya demonstrates the convention through Mahābrahmā:

```text
20 antarakalpas: world expands
20 antarakalpas: world remains expanded
20 antarakalpas: world contracts
------------------------------------------------
60 antarakalpas = Mahābrahmā's 1.5 lower kalpas
```

Therefore:

```text
1 lower kalpa = 40 antarakalpas = 0.5 mahākalpa
```

The verb `kalpīkṛtya` is logically important: half a mahākalpa is made the
counting-unit for the lower range. The measure is conventional but exact.

### Two-stage determination

```text
station
    → determines numerical lifespan in kalpas

station relative to Parīttābha
    → determines which kalpa-magnitude applies

resolved quantity + resolved unit
    → determines actual lifespan-measure
```

The verse thereby separates the recurrence governing the number from the
scope rule governing the unit.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.81 exposes a principle already latent in the previous temporal verses:
a measure is not a number with a decorative label. It is a semantic pair whose
quantity and unit must both be determined.

```text
Measure = <Quantity, UnitUnderDomain>
```

This matters because `kalpa` is overloaded across the Parīttābha boundary.
A syntactic pipeline may recognize the same lexical token on both sides and
wrongly infer one invariant duration. The semantic pipeline must resolve the
token through its realm-scope before performing arithmetic. The physical
catalogue has therefore become a practical theory of typed quantities.

The formless sequence makes the same point at the greatest scale. Infinite
space and infinite cognition name the contents or range of their respective
attainments; they do not make the lifespan numerically infinite. Each station
still receives a finite and exact duration. Even Bhavāgra, the Summit of
Existence, is temporally stationed within the World System.

The reciprocal determination is precise:

```text
realm-station
    → selects the valid magnitude of “kalpa”

resolved kalpa-unit
    → makes the station's stated lifespan operationally intelligible
```

Neither term is reducible to the other. Cosmological classification supplies
measurement context; temporal measurement returns a determinate duration for
the classified station.

```ts
type KalpaUnit =
  | { kind: "FullMahakalpa"; lowerBound: "Parittabha" }
  | { kind: "HalfMahakalpa"; upperBoundExclusive: "Parittabha" };

interface LifespanMeasure {
  station: string;
  quantity: number;
  unit: KalpaUnit;
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. Its type
discipline blocks calculations until the cosmological scope has resolved the
otherwise ambiguous unit-name.
