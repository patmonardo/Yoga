# VAK_3.50

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चतूरत्नमयो मेरुर्जलेऽशीतिसहस्रके ।
>
> मग्नाः ऊर्ध्वं जलान्मेरुर्भूयोऽशीतिसहस्रकः ॥ ३.५० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> catūratnamayo merur jale 'śītisahasrake /
>
> magnāḥ ūrdhvaṃ jalān merur bhūyo 'śītisahasrakaḥ // 3.50 //

The plural `magnāḥ`, confirmed by the Bhāṣya, refers to the mountains
collectively. It must not be silently changed to singular `magnaḥ` and made to
refer only to Meru.


| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |

| aśīti-sahasrake | locative singular numerical compound | at a depth of eighty thousand |

| jalāt | ablative neuter singular | from or above the water |

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first predicate qualifies Meru materially:

```text
meruḥ
    catū-ratna-mayaḥ [asti]
```

The next clause has an understood plural subject supplied by the preceding
mountain enumeration:

```text
[aṣṭau mahāparvatāḥ]
    aśīti-sahasrake jale
    magnāḥ [santi]

[the eight great mountains]
    in eighty-thousand [yojanas] of water
    are submerged
```

The closing clause returns explicitly to singular Meru:

```text
meruḥ
    jalāt ūrdhvam
    bhūyaḥ aśīti-sahasrakaḥ

Meru,
    above the water,
    is a further eighty thousand [yojanas]
```

The alternation `magnāḥ ... meruḥ` is structurally decisive: shared submerged
depth is followed by Meru's distinctive emergent height.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Meru is made of four precious substances. [The mountains] are submerged in
> eighty thousand [yojanas] of water; above the water, Meru extends a further
> eighty thousand.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Meru has four sides made respectively of gold, silver, vaidūrya, and crystal.
> The eight great mountains are submerged 80,000 yojanas in the water resting
> upon the golden earth. Meru rises another 80,000 yojanas above the
> water. The radiance of each precious face colors the appearance of the sky in
> its direction; the face toward Jambūdvīpa is traditionally described as
> vaidūrya, through whose luminous coloring the sky here appears blue-green.

The four materials and their ordered distribution, collective scope of the
submerged measure, directional coloration, Jambūdvīpa-facing material, and
role of radiance are Bhāṣya determinations. The last identification is reported
tradition, marked by “they describe.”

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The centered World System now acquires a differentiated power of appearance.
> Its mountains share one submerged depth, while Meru rises uniquely above
> their common support. Meru's four materially distinct faces then condition
> four directional fields of visible color through their radiance. What is
> seen as the color of the sky is therefore explained neither by the sky alone
> nor by an isolated observer: sensible appearance is relationally determined
> by structure, material face, radiance, direction, and location within the
> common world.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Common depth and distinctive elevation

```text
all eight great mountains
    → submerged depth: 80,000 yojanas

Meru specifically
    → height above water: 80,000 yojanas
```

The first measure establishes a shared support relation; the second produces
vertical asymmetry within that common foundation. Meru is central not only by
horizontal position but also by distinctive elevation.

### Fourfold material distribution

```text
four sides of Meru
    ↔ gold, silver, vaidūrya, crystal
```

`Yathāsaṃkhyam` in the Bhāṣya makes this a one-to-one ordered mapping, not an
undifferentiated mixture of four materials.

### Relational genesis of sensible appearance

```text
material constitution of a face
    → radiance and efficacy
        → directional coloring
            → sky appears with that color in the corresponding region
```

The perceived color is an effect of a relation. It cannot be assigned without
qualification either to bare sky or to the precious face considered in
isolation.

### Perspective is located but not merely private

```text
Jambūdvīpa location
    + Meru-facing direction
    + vaidūrya radiance
        → this region's visible sky-color
```

The account is perspective-dependent because direction and location matter.
It is nevertheless a common appearance for inhabitants positioned within the
same world-relation, not a private fabrication by each observer.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.50 yields the first explicit bridge from cosmological configuration to
the determination of appearance. Earlier verses generated and positioned the
world's supports. This Bhāṣya now explains how an oriented material structure
conditions what becomes visible within that world:

```text
structure
    → directional material difference
        → radiance
            → regionally determinate appearance
```

This is a powerful abstraction for our Semantic pipeline. A visible predicate
such as “the sky is blue” is syntactically simple, but its semantic ground is a
relation among a field, an illuminating source, an oriented surface, and the
observer's region. The appearance is real as appearance while its truth
requires reconstruction of the conditions under which it appears.

That is the “Truth behind the Matrix” in a precise sense: not a second picture
hidden behind the first, but the system of determinations through which the
visible picture is produced. The Kośa's cosmology has moved from generating a
common container to explaining a local quality of experience within it.

For the Organon projection:

```ts
interface DirectionalAppearance {
  worldStructure: "Meru";
  orientedFace: "gold" | "silver" | "vaidurya" | "crystal";
  mediation: "radiance";
  observerRegion: string;
  visibleDetermination: string;
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It preserves the
main insight without inflating it into a universal theory: within this account,
sensible color is generated through a stable common relation among material
constitution, radiance, direction, and region.
