# VAK_3.27

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> क्लेशात् क्लेशः क्रिया चैव ततो वस्तु ततः पुनः ।
>
> वस्तु क्लेशाश्च जायन्ते भवाङ्गानामयं नयः ॥ ३.२७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kleśāt kleśaḥ kriyā caiva tato vastu tataḥ punaḥ /
>
> vastukleśāś ca jāyante bhavāṅgānām ayaṃ nayaḥ // 3.27 //

The second line is construed as `tataḥ punaḥ vastu kleśāḥ ca jāyante`:
from the resultant support, further support and afflictions arise again.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kleśāt                 → kleśāt

kriyā                  → kriyā

tataḥ punaḥ            → tataḥ punaḥ
vastu-kleśāḥ           → vastukleśāḥ

jāyante                → jāyante
bhava-aṅgānām          → bhavāṅgānām
ayam                   → ayam
nayaḥ                  → nayaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| kleśāt | ablative masculine singular | from affliction; causal source |
| kleśaḥ | nominative masculine singular | another affliction |
| kriyā | nominative feminine singular | activity; karmic action |
| caiva | particles `ca + eva` | and indeed; both are produced |
| tataḥ | ablative adverb | from that; the Bhāṣya takes the transition through karma |
| vastu | nominative neuter singular | resultant conditioned support |
| tataḥ punaḥ | ablative adverb plus particle | from that again |
| vastukleśāḥ | nominative coordination resolved contextually | further support and afflictions |
| jāyante | third-person plural middle present of √jan | arise; are generated |
| bhavāṅgānām | genitive neuter plural compound | of the members of becoming |
| ayam | nominative masculine singular pronoun | this rule just stated |
| nayaḥ | nominative masculine singular | governing principle; causal rule or method |

The Bhāṣya identifies `kriyā` functionally with karma and expands the compact
verse into five permitted transition-types.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first half states two productions from affliction and the production from
action:

```text
kleśāt
    kleśaḥ [jāyate]
    kriyā ca eva [jāyate]

tataḥ [kriyātaḥ]
    vastu [jāyate]
```

> From affliction arise both affliction and activity; from that activity
> arises the resultant support.

The second half closes the recurrence:

```text
tataḥ punaḥ [vastunaḥ]
    vastu
    kleśāḥ ca
    jāyante
```

> From that support again arise further support and afflictions.

The concluding nominal sentence identifies the rule:

```text
bhava-aṅgānām
    ayam nayaḥ
```

> This is the governing principle of the members of becoming.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> From affliction arise both affliction and activity; from that arises the
> resultant support. From that again arise further support and afflictions.
> This is the governing principle of the members of becoming.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Affliction can generate another affliction or karmic action; karmic action
> generates a resultant conditioned support; that support develops into
> further resultant stages and also conditions renewed affliction. This
> recursive causal grammar governs the twelve members and reconnects their
> apparent beginning and end without requiring any additional member.

The five transition-types, their concrete member-pairs, and the response to
the boundary objection are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The list has become a generative law. Affliction can reproduce its own
> functional kind or pass into projective action; action produces an embodied
> support; the support develops immanently and becomes the condition of
> renewed affliction. The World System therefore regenerates the conditions
> of its own recurrence. Its apparent first and final members are boundaries
> of exposition, not absolute boundaries of the operating law.

The five transitions and their principal examples are:

```text
kleśa → kleśa
    tṛṣṇā → upādāna

kleśa → karma
    avidyā → saṃskāra
    upādāna → bhava

karma → vastu
    saṃskāra → vijñāna
    bhava → jāti

vastu → vastu
    vijñāna → nāmarūpa → ṣaḍāyatana → sparśa → vedanā
    jāti → jarāmaraṇa

vastu → kleśa
    vedanā → tṛṣṇā
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### The boundary objection

Read only as a finite list:

```text
avidyā
    has no cause explicitly enumerated before it

jarāmaraṇa
    has no result explicitly enumerated after it
```

This seems to entail either a first beginning and final end or the need to add
further members. But each added member would demand another ground:

```text
add a thirteenth member
    → it requires a fourteenth
    → indefinite supplementation
```

Vasubandhu rejects supplementation. The answer is not a longer list but a
higher-order rule governing the functional types already present.

### The conditional grammar

In hypothetical form:

```text
If a kleśa is operative,
then either renewed kleśa or karma may arise.

If karma becomes projectively efficacious,
then a corresponding vastu arises.

If a vastu has arisen,
then further vastu-development or renewed kleśa may arise.
```

Formally:

```text
K → K
K → A
A → V
V → V
V → K
```

where:

```text
K = kleśa
A = karmic activity
V = resultant support
```

These rules explain every transition required for recurrence. The listed
endpoints are reconnectible because they belong to functional classes whose
production rules extend beyond the boundaries of the displayed sequence.

### Enumeration becomes principle

```text
3.20–3.24
    concrete twelve-stage data

3.25
    principle of stage-naming and View selection

3.26
    functional classes and inference from the middle

3.27
    generative relations among the classes
```

Vasubandhu has moved from components to classification to executable rule.
The `naya` explains more than the enumeration because it can generate the
relations of the enumeration and reconnect its apparent limits.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This is the clearest statement yet of the Kośa's Technē. The World System is
not merely described; its lawful reproduction is specified:

```text
afflictive orientation
    → projective operation
    → embodied result
    → developed experiential field
    → renewed afflictive orientation
```

The system has “skin in the game” because every arrow is assigned concrete
members. `Avidyā → saṃskāra`, `saṃskāra → vijñāna`, `vedanā → tṛṣṇā`, and
`upādāna → bhava` are not ornamental cosmological claims. Together they form
a compact causal program for life, action, death, and renewed embodiment.

In our Ground–Condition architecture, 3.27 supplies the transition algebra:

```text
hetu
    is typed as kleśa or karma

phala
    arises as vastu

vastu
    becomes āśraya for renewed hetu

naya
    determines the licensed transitions
```

The Truth of the system is therefore not exhausted by one isolated
hypothetical judgment. It is the closed family of conditionals through which
the World reproduces its own grounds.

Within the Yogācāra assimilation, this recursive World is established in Me:
object-field becomes feeling, feeling becomes craving, craving organizes
action, action projects another cognition-platform, and that platform
differentiates another object-field. The recurrence is immanent to the
World's cognitive-practical articulation.

The corresponding Science of Liberation now has an exact problem. It must
not merely reject an undesirable object or ascend away from one station. It
must determine where this transition grammar can be interrupted so that
result no longer becomes the support of the same renewed cause. That problem
belongs to the Path; 3.27 has supplied the machinery the Path must transform.
