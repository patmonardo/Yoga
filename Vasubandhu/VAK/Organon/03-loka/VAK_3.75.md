# VAK_3.75

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> जाम्बूद्वीपाः प्रमाणेन चतुःसार्धत्रिहस्तकाः ।
>
> द्विगुणोत्तरवृद्धया तु पूर्वगोदोत्तराव्हयाः ॥ ३.७५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> jāmbūdvīpāḥ pramāṇena catuḥsārdhatrihastakāḥ /
>
> dviguṇottaravṛddhayā tu pūrvagodottarāvhayāḥ // 3.75 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
jāmbū-dvīpāḥ               → jāmbūdvīpāḥ
pramāṇena                  → pramāṇena
catuḥ-sārdha-tri-hastakāḥ  → catuḥsārdhatrihastakāḥ
dvi-guṇa-uttara-vṛddhyā    → dviguṇottaravṛddhayā
tu                         → tu
pūrva-goda-uttara-āhvayāḥ  → pūrvagodottarāvhayāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| jāmbūdvīpāḥ | nominative masculine plural | humans belonging to Jambūdvīpa |
| pramāṇena | instrumental neuter singular | with respect to measure or magnitude |
| catuḥsārdhatrihastakāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | having the compressed stated measure in hastas |
| dviguṇottaravṛddhyā | instrumental feminine singular compound | by successive twofold increase |
| tu | particle | but / whereas; marks the distinct rule for the remaining populations |
| pūrva | compound member | Pūrvavideha |
| goda | compound member | Godānīya / Aparagodānīya |
| uttara | compound member | Uttarakuru |
| āhvayāḥ | nominative masculine plural | those called or named |

The transmitted numerical compound in the first line is unusually compressed.
The Bhāṣya resolves the principal Jambūdvīpa measure as 3½ hastas and records
an alternative of 4 hastas.

## 4. Grammar

The first line assigns a body-measure to the Jambūdvīpa population:

```text
jāmbūdvīpāḥ [manuṣyāḥ]
    pramāṇena
    [ardha-catur]-hastakāḥ [santi]
```

> The humans of Jambūdvīpa measure three and one-half hastas.

The numerical resolution is Bhāṣya-dependent. The commentary adds:

```text
principal account: 3½ hastas
some teachers:      4 hastas
```

The second line assigns a separate ordered rule to the other three
populations:

```text
pūrva-goda-uttara-āhvayāḥ
    dvi-guṇa-uttara-vṛddhyā [vardhante]
```

> Those called Pūrva, Goda, and Uttara increase successively twofold.

The Bhāṣya fixes the resulting measures as 8, 16, and 32 hastas respectively.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The people of Jambūdvīpa have the stated measure in hastas; those called
> Pūrva, Goda, and Uttara increase successively twofold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Humans of Jambūdvīpa measure 3½ hastas in height according to the principal
> account, though some give 4 hastas. The humans of Pūrvavideha measure 8
> hastas, those of Godānīya 16, and those of Uttarakuru 32, each of these three
> successive populations being twice the measure of the preceding one.

The principal and alternative Jambūdvīpa values, exact measures of the other
three populations, and restricted scope of the doubling rule are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> After constructing a billionfold cosmological aggregate, the analysis
> returns to the body of an inhabitant. Difference of World-container is
> accompanied by difference of embodied magnitude, but the correlation is not
> reduced to one universal formula. Jambūdvīpa has its own disputed parameter;
> the other three populations form a clean doubling series. The World System
> therefore determines embodiment through domain-specific rules that preserve
> both regularity and exception.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Container-to-inhabitant question

The Bhāṣya explicitly frames the inference:

```text
premise:
    world-containers differ in magnitude

question:
    do the beings inhabiting them also differ in magnitude?

answer:
    yes
```

This establishes correlated difference, not numerical identity between the
size of an environment and the body of its inhabitant.

### Population-measure table

| Population | Body height | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| Jambūdvīpa | 3½ hastas | principal account |
| Jambūdvīpa | 4 hastas | attributed alternative |
| Pūrvavideha | 8 hastas | fixed in Bhāṣya |
| Godānīya | 16 hastas | fixed in Bhāṣya |
| Uttarakuru | 32 hastas | fixed in Bhāṣya |

### Restricted doubling recurrence

Let the ordered series be:

```text
P₁ = Pūrvavideha
P₂ = Godānīya
P₃ = Uttarakuru
```

Then:

```text
height(P₁) = 8
height(P₂) = 2 × height(P₁) = 16
height(P₃) = 2 × height(P₂) = 32
```

The rule does not license:

```text
height(Pūrvavideha) = 2 × height(Jambūdvīpa)
```

because twice the principal Jambūdvīpa measure would be 7, not 8. The particle
`tu` and the Bhāṣya distribution mark the other three as the recurrence's
proper scope.

### Contested parameter branch

```text
JambūdvīpaHeight =
    3.5 hastas  [principal]
    OR
    4 hastas    [some teachers]
```

The alternative must be stored with attribution. Averaging the values or
silently selecting the cleaner doubling base would destroy the witness.

### Scale contraction

```text
3.74:
    10⁹ local world-units

3.75:
    one embodied inhabitant measured in hastas
```

The same analysis can change granularity from maximal aggregate to local body
without confusing the predicates belonging to each scale.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.75 gives a methodological answer to the billionfold scale of 3.74:
World System comprehension must preserve the smallest embodied determinations
inside the largest aggregate.

```text
global lifecycle
    contains local worlds
        containing differentiated environments
            conditioning differentiated bodies
```

The body is therefore not an interchangeable token inserted after the World
has been built. Its measurable presentation is indexed to the domain in which
it arises. This extends the class-conditioned initialization seen in 3.70 from
deva birth-form to human bodily magnitude.

The semantic–syntactic discipline prevents a false elegance here. A surface
reading invites one doubling function across all four populations. The Bhāṣya
instead preserves two structures:

```text
Jambūdvīpa:
    disputed local parameter

other three continents:
    exact ordered recurrence
```

This is better World science: a regular law is applied only over the domain
for which the source warrants it.

Under your present architecture, Pāramārthika is enclosed within the complete
Purusha–Prakṛti field rather than positioned as an external fourth world. The
relation can be stated without flattening its two directions:

```text
Purusha–Prakṛti totality
    → contains the locus in which Pāramārthika is disclosed

Pāramārthika determination
    → sublates the merely empirical finality of every measured form
```

Thus enclosure and sublation are not competing spatial claims. They express
different semantic relations. This is our project architecture, not a doctrine
asserted by VAK 3.75.
