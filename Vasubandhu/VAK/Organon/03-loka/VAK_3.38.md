# VAK_3.38

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> उपपत्तिभवः क्लिष्टः सर्वक्लेशैः स्वभूमिकैः ।
>
> त्रिधान्ये त्रय आरूप्ये आहारस्थितिकं जगत् ॥ ३.३८ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> upapattibhavaḥ kliṣṭaḥ sarvakleśaiḥ svabhūmikaiḥ /
>
> tridhā 'nye traya ārūpye āhārasthitikaṃ jagat // 3.38 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
upapatti-bhavaḥ             → upapattibhavaḥ
kliṣṭaḥ                    → kliṣṭaḥ
sarva-kleśaiḥ              → sarvakleśaiḥ
sva-bhūmikaiḥ              → svabhūmikaiḥ
tridhā                     → tridhā
anye                       → 'nye
trayaḥ                     → traya
ārūpye                     → ārūpye
āhāra-sthitikam            → āhārasthitikaṃ
jagat                      → jagat
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| upapattibhavaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | rebirth-becoming; arising in a destination |
| kliṣṭaḥ | nominative masculine singular adjective | defiled; necessarily so according to the Bhāṣya |
| sarvakleśaiḥ | instrumental masculine plural | by all afflictions |
| svabhūmikaiḥ | instrumental masculine plural | belonging to its own level or ground |
| tridhā | indeclinable adverb | in three ways / threefold |
| anye | nominative masculine plural | the other [three modes of becoming] |
| trayaḥ | nominative masculine plural numeral | three |
| ārūpye | locative neuter singular | in the formless realm |
| āhārasthitikam | nominative neuter singular compound adjective | having persistence through nutriment |
| jagat | nominative neuter singular | the world; here the world of sentient beings |

The continuous-looking witness `tridhānye` must be segmented as `tridhā
'nye`. The Bhāṣya makes both terms explicit: the three modes other than
rebirth-becoming can each be wholesome, defiled, or indeterminate.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first hemistich predicates an exceptionless condition of rebirth-becoming:

```text
upapatti-bhavaḥ kliṣṭaḥ
    = rebirth-becoming is defiled

sarva-kleśaiḥ sva-bhūmikaiḥ
    = by all afflictions belonging to its own level
```

The Bhāṣya supplies `ekāntena`, “necessarily” or “without exception.” It also
understands the instrumental phrase as specifying the range of afflictions
capable of establishing rebirth-linking at the corresponding level.

The second hemistich contains three compact predications:

```text
anye [trayo bhavāḥ] tridhā
    = the other [three modes of becoming] are threefold

ārūpye trayaḥ [bhavāḥ]
    = in the formless realm there are three [modes of becoming]

jagat āhāra-sthitikam
    = the World persists through nutriment
```

The threefold ethical classification and the omission of intermediate
becoming in the formless realm are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Rebirth-becoming is defiled by all the afflictions belonging to its own
> level. The others are threefold. In the formless realm there are three. The
> World persists through nutriment.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Rebirth-becoming is necessarily defiled and may be bound by any affliction
> belonging to the level in which that rebirth occurs. The other three modes
> of becoming—intermediate, life-course, and death becoming—may each be
> wholesome, defiled, or indeterminate. In the formless realm only three modes
> occur: rebirth, life-course, and death; intermediate becoming is absent
> because that realm is not spatially delimited as another place requiring an
> intervening transition-body. Having explained how sentient beings arise
> through dependent co-arising, the text now states how they remain: the World
> persists through nutriment.

The necessity of defilement, the three ethical classes, the excluded mode in
the formless realm, and the arising-versus-persistence transition are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Entry into a new embodiment is never a neutral insertion. It is bound by the
> afflictive history proper to the level in which the embodiment arises. Once
> arisen, however, the other phases of existence admit wholesome, defiled, and
> indeterminate operation. Nor does every transition require passage between
> spatially separate worlds: formless becoming has no intermediate mode. The
> World System is therefore governed by two distinct laws—dependent co-arising
> accounts for production, while nutriment accounts for continued station.

The two governing questions are distinct:

```text
kathaṃ sattvānām utpādaḥ?
    How do sentient beings arise?

kathaṃ sattvānāṃ sthitiḥ?
    How do sentient beings remain stationed?
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### The four becomings do not have identical status

```text
upapattibhava
    → necessarily kliṣṭa

antarābhava | pūrvakālabhava | maraṇabhava
    → kuśala | kliṣṭa | avyākṛta
```

Rebirth-becoming is distinguished because it is the binding establishment of
a renewed series. The Bhāṣya extends this necessity to the linking into
intermediate existence.

### Rebirth-linking is historically conditioned

```text
affliction frequently enacted
    + affliction presently proximate
    + momentum from what preceded
        → affliction manifest at rebirth-linking
```

The state at linking may be weak, but its determination is not arbitrary. A
retained history of operation conditions which affliction becomes present.

### Realm topology determines the need for transition

```text
desire realm / form realm:
    spatially differentiated destinations
        → all four modes admitted

formless realm:
    not delimited as another spatial place
        → no intermediate becoming
        → three modes only
```

Intermediate existence is therefore functional, not an obligatory fifth
substance inserted between every death and rebirth.

### Production and maintenance are different relations

```text
pratītyasamutpāda
    → law of arising

āhāra
    → law of persistence
```

Hence:

```text
cause sufficient for arising
    does not by itself explain continued station

continued station
    requires sustaining conditions
```

The final pāda opens the question of those sustaining conditions. The Bhāṣya
announces four nutriments; their detailed determination follows in the next
verses.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This is the proper final structural hinge before the cosmographic expansion.
The World System has now been given both a genesis and a maintenance law:

```text
World comes into operation
    through dependent co-arising

World remains in operation
    through nutriment
```

“Food” is therefore much larger than edible matter. The continuing Bhāṣya
announces four nutriments: material morsel, contact, mental volition, and
differentiated cognition. Material embodiment must be fed, but so must the
affective, purposive, and cognitive organization through which an Agent
remains stationed in a World.

This extends the Yoga Citta Theory into a Practical Theory of maintenance:

```text
embodied support
    + contact support
    + purposive support
    + cognition support
        → continued Agent station
```

The list is a Bhāṣya-forward bridge, not wording contained in the Kārikā's
single compound. Its philosophical importance is already contained in
`āhārasthitika`: persistence is relational and continuously conditioned.

For the Oculus architecture, a generated View does not remain available merely
because it was once produced. It requires continuing supports:

```ts
interface StationMaintenance {
  embodiedNutriment: MaterialSupport;
  contactNutriment: ContactSupport;
  volitionalNutriment: PurposiveSupport;
  cognitionNutriment: DifferentiatedCognitionSupport;
}
```

This is an Organon projection, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It distinguishes
construction from retention. A pipeline that can generate Meaning but cannot
sustain its conscious, purposive, and evidential relations has not yet built a
World System.

The first half of the verse also sharpens Learning. At the weak station of
rebirth-linking, what has been frequently enacted and lies nearest becomes
operative through prior momentum. Saṃskāra-like habituation is not a passive
archive: history becomes selection pressure upon the next station.

Yet the subsequent life-course is not condemned to one ethical value. The
other modes of becoming remain threefold. A defiled entry condition does not
make every later operation necessarily defiled; wholesome transformation
remains structurally available within the embodied course.

Finally, the absence of intermediate becoming in the formless realm confirms
that the World System is not a naive geography. Transition is required where
there is a determinate passage between spatial destinations. Where no such
spatial difference obtains, the intermediate mode has no work to perform.
Vasubandhu's cosmology is already a functional topology.
