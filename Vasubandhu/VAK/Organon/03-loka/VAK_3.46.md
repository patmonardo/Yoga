# VAK_3.46

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अपामेकादशोद्वेधं सहस्राणि च विंशतिः ।
>
> अष्टलक्षौच्छ्रयं पश्चाच्छेषं भवति काञ्चनम् ॥ ३.४६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> apām ekādaśodvedhaṃ sahasrāṇi ca viṃśatiḥ /
>
> aṣṭalakṣaucchrayaṃ paścāc cheṣaṃ bhavati kāñcanam // 3.46 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
apām                         → apām
ekādaśa-udvedham             → ekādaśodvedham
sahasrāṇi                    → sahasrāṇi
ca                           → ca
viṃśatiḥ                     → viṃśatiḥ
aṣṭa-lakṣa-ucchrayam         → aṣṭalakṣaucchrayaṃ
paścāt                       → paścāc
śeṣam                        → śeṣam
bhavati                      → bhavati
kāñcanam                     → kāñcanam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| apām | genitive plural of `ap` | of the waters; of the water-maṇḍala |
| ekādaśa | cardinal number | eleven; the Bhāṣya supplies `lakṣas` |
| udvedham | accusative singular compound member | having a vertical extent |
| sahasrāṇi | neuter plural | thousands |
| viṃśatiḥ | cardinal number | twenty |
| aṣṭa-lakṣa | numerical compound | eight hundred-thousands |
| ucchrayam | accusative singular compound member | having a height or elevation |
| paścāt | indeclinable | afterwards, subsequently |
| śeṣam | neuter singular | what remains, the remainder |
| bhavati | third-person singular present | becomes, comes to be |
| kāñcanam | neuter singular predicate | gold; golden substance |

The compressed number in the first hemistich is resolved by the Bhāṣya as
eleven `lakṣas` plus twenty thousand yojanas: 1,120,000 yojanas.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first clause continues `maṇḍalam` from 3.45, as the Bhāṣya explicitly
states:

```text
apām [maṇḍalam]
    ekādaśa-[lakṣa]-udvedham
    viṃśati-sahasrāṇi ca

the [maṇḍala] of the waters
    [has] eleven lakṣas in vertical extent
    and twenty thousand [yojanas]
```

The second clause is highly compressed:

```text
paścāt
    [aṣṭa-lakṣa-ucchrayaṃ bhāgam]
    kāñcanam bhavati
    [apāṃ śeṣam avaśiṣyate]
```

The Bhāṣya determines the intended arithmetic: an eight-`lakṣa` upper portion
becomes gold, while three `lakṣas` plus twenty thousand yojanas of water remain.
Accordingly, `śeṣam` must be interpreted with the prose explanation; a purely
linear construe could misleadingly call the golden portion itself the
remainder.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The water[-maṇḍala] has a vertical extent of eleven hundred-thousands and
> twenty thousand. Afterwards, to a height of eight hundred-thousands, [it]
> becomes gold, with a remainder.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Above the wind-maṇḍala forms a water-maṇḍala 1,120,000 yojanas deep. Winds
> generated through the karmic efficacy of beings turn its waters, and the
> upper 800,000 yojanas become golden earth, like the skin that forms upon
> cooked milk. A water layer 320,000 yojanas deep remains beneath it.

The location above the wind-maṇḍala, formation by clouds and rain, karmically
generated winds, milk-skin analogy, and exact division between gold and
remaining water are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The common world-field advances from support to formation, containment, and
> transformation. Upon the wind-domain, water gathers into a measured whole.
> Its persistence calls for a retaining condition; its upper portion is then
> transformed by karmically generated winds into a stable golden ground. The
> Physical World is therefore constructed neither by a bare inventory of
> elements nor by simple stacking. Each new domain arises through an operation
> that determines how the preceding field is gathered, retained, and converted
> into a support for what follows.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Formation of the water-domain

```text
karma of beings
    → clouds arise above the wind-maṇḍala
        → immense rain
            → water-maṇḍala
```

The static measure given by the Kārikā therefore rests upon a genetic process
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

### The coherence question

The Bhāṣya does not merely place water above wind. It asks:

```text
given a vast mass of water,
    why does it not flow away laterally?
```

Two explanatory registers follow:

```text
principal account:
    governing efficacy of beings' karma
        → water remains ordered

Nikāyāntarīya account:
    enclosing wind, like a granary
        → water is physically held together
```

A digestive analogy illustrates the first account: ingested food and drink do
not simply pass onward without undergoing the ordered process appropriate to
the organism. The Bhāṣya records the alternative without clearly adjudicating
between the two local explanations.

### Transformation rather than addition

```text
karma-produced winds
    → turning of the water-domain
        → upper water becomes golden
            → golden earth rests above remaining water
```

Gold is not introduced here as an externally added third layer. It is a new
support-determination generated from the upper portion of the water-domain.

### Conservation of measure

```text
1,120,000
    = 800,000 transformed
    + 320,000 remaining
```

The arithmetic gives `śeṣa` an exact structural meaning. Transformation
changes the determination of a portion without erasing the measured whole.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.46 improves the Physical World account in two ways. First, it shows that
`saṃniveśa`, configuration, is genetic: clouds form, rain accumulates, winds
turn the waters, and golden earth results. Second, the Bhāṣya subjects the
received cosmology to a coherence test. A world-model must explain not only
which domains exist, but how a domain retains its organization.

This yields a useful sequence:

```text
formation
    → containment
        → transformation
            → new support
```

The two containment accounts should remain distinct. `Karmādhipatya` names the
governing causal efficacy of the collective sentient field. The Nikāyāntarīya
model names an intermediate physical mechanism—wind holds the water like a
receptacle. They can be related, especially because the transforming winds are
themselves karmically generated, but the Bhāṣya does not simply identify the
two explanations.

For the Organon, the strongest transferable determination is that the common
world has to be both generated and maintained:

```ts

  priorSupport: "wind-mandala";
  formedDomain: "water-mandala";
  retentionAccount: "karmic-governance" | "wind-enclosure";
  transformedPortion: "golden-earth";
  remainingPortion: "water";
}
```

This is an explanatory projection, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It preserves
the passage's causal levels and the reported disagreement. It also carries
forward the result of the Human World analysis without forcing an analogy:
the karma of sentient trajectories does not merely select locations within a
ready-made physical container; it participates in the formation, stability,
and transformation of that shared container.

The passage still does not derive space itself from karma. The wind-domain was
said to be established in space; here the layered container is constructed
within that framework. The status of space should remain an open question.
