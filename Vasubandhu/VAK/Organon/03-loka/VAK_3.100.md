# VAK_3.100

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> संवर्तन्यः पुनस्तिस्रो भवन्त्यग्न्यम्बुवायुभिः ।
>
> ध्यानत्रयं द्वितीयादि शीर्ष तासां यथाक्रमम् ॥ ३.१०० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> saṃvartanyaḥ punas tisro bhavanty agny-ambu-vāyubhiḥ /
>
> dhyānatrayaṃ dvitīyādi śīrṣa tāsāṃ yathākramam // 3.100 //

The Bhāṣya places a sustained argument against eternal atoms between these
two pādas. That excursus belongs to the interpretation of the verse: the
claim that cosmic contraction leaves no subtle part of the receptacle-world
remaining raises the question of how another World can form without an
indestructible material seed.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
saṃvartanyaḥ               → saṃvartanyaḥ
punaḥ                      → punas (before t-)
tisraḥ                     → tisro (before bh-)
bhavanti                   → bhavanty (before vowel)
agni-ambu-vāyubhiḥ         → agnyambuvāyubhiḥ
dhyāna-trayam              → dhyānatrayam
dvitīya-ādi                → dvitīyādi
śīrṣa                      → śīrṣa
tāsām                      → tāsāṃ
yathā-kramam               → yathākramam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| saṃvartanyaḥ | nominative feminine plural | cosmic contractions or destructions |
| punaḥ | indeclinable | moreover; resumes with a larger class after the antarakalpa crises |
| tisraḥ | nominative feminine plural | three |
| bhavanti | third-person plural present | there are, they occur |
| agny-ambu-vāyubhiḥ | instrumental plural compound | by fire, water, and wind |
| dhyānatrayam | nominative neuter singular | a triad of dhyāna levels |
| dvitīyādi | nominative neuter singular compound | beginning with the second |
| śīrṣa | nominative neuter singular | head or upper boundary; level immediately above the destroyed region |
| tāsām | genitive feminine plural | of those three contractions |
| yathākramam | indeclinable | respectively, in the stated order |

`Saṃvartanī` is not simply any destructive event. The Bhāṣya derives it from
the collective contraction of beings associated with a dhyāna level.
`Śīrṣa` does not name the highest level destroyed; it names the level
immediately above the destruction's reach.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first half enumerates three contractions through their instrumental
media:

```text
saṃvartanyaḥ
    tisraḥ bhavanti
    agninā
    ambunā
    vāyunā ca
```

> Moreover, there are three cosmic contractions: by fire, by water, and by
> wind.

The second half distributes three dhyānas as their respective upper
boundaries:

```text
dvitīya-ādi dhyāna-trayam
    tāsām
    yathākramam
    śīrṣa [bhavati]
```

> The triad of dhyānas beginning with the second constitutes their respective
> upper boundaries.

Expanded through the Bhāṣya:

```text
fire-contraction  → second dhyāna is śīrṣa → everything below it burns
water-contraction → third dhyāna is śīrṣa  → everything below it dissolves
wind-contraction  → fourth dhyāna is śīrṣa → everything below it disperses
```

Thus the boundary level itself remains above the affected region.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Moreover, there are three cosmic contractions, through fire, water, and
> wind. The three dhyānas beginning with the second are respectively their
> upper boundaries.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> There are three contractions of the receptacle-world. Fire, generated by
> seven suns, destroys everything below the second dhyāna; torrential water
> destroys everything below the third; and violent wind disperses everything
> below the fourth. The second, third, and fourth dhyānas are therefore the
> respective levels immediately above the reach of these contractions. The
> first three dhyāna-worlds are vulnerable because each contains an internal
> disturbance analogous to its external destructive medium, whereas the
> fourth dhyāna is immovable and lacks such a disturbance.

The seven suns, complete destruction of the receptacle-world, internal
analogues, and fourth-dhyāna immovability are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Cosmic stability is differentiated according to the internal organization
of each dhyāna-domain. A level containing a scorching, liquefying, or moving
disturbance has a corresponding external mode of dissolution. The external
World and the cognitive-embodied station belonging to it exhibit the same
structural vulnerability. Contraction leaves no eternal atomic residue; a
later World arises through renewed causal organization, karmic efficacy, and
formative conditions rather than through a permanently preserved material
core.

The atom discussion is Bhāṣya argument, not metrical wording. Its positive
result is that renewed formation can be causally intelligible without an
eternal material remainder.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Contraction matrix

| Contraction | External operation | Region affected | `śīrṣa` | Internal analogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fire | burning | below second dhyāna | second dhyāna | `vitarka-vicāra` scorch the mind |
| water | saturation and dissolution | below third dhyāna | third dhyāna | `prīti` softens the support |
| wind | dispersal | below fourth dhyāna | fourth dhyāna | breathing is wind |

The correspondence stated by the Bhāṣya is:

```text
InternalDisturbance(domain, type)
    → CorrespondingExternalInstability(domain, type)
```

More exactly:

```text
first dhyāna has FireLike(vitarka-vicāra)
    → first-dhyāna World is vulnerable to fire

second dhyāna has WaterLike(prīti)
    → second-dhyāna World is vulnerable to water

third dhyāna has Wind(āśvāsa-praśvāsa)
    → third-dhyāna World is vulnerable to wind
```

This is analogical causal explanation within the Kośa cosmology. It does not
assert that a meditator's private episode individually causes cosmic
destruction.

### Fourth-dhyāna stability

```text
fourth dhyāna
    → no internal apakṣāla
    → āneñja
    → no corresponding external saṃvartanī
```

Immunity is determined positively by the absence of the relevant internal
destabilizing operation, not merely by spatial height.

### No eternal atomic remainder

The opponent argues:

```text
future gross formation requires material seed
    → eternal atoms must survive contraction
```

Vasubandhu denies the inference:

```text
karma
    + formative winds
    + relevant causal conditions
        → renewed World-formation

therefore:
future formation
    ↛ eternal atomic survival
```

He also rejects an additional whole-substance:

```text
reeds in determinate arrangement
    → designated “mat”

threads in determinate arrangement
    → designated “cloth”

ants in determinate arrangement
    → designated “row”
```

In each case:

```text
organized constituents
    + determinate arrangement
        → whole-designation
        ≠ second substance added to the constituents
```

Because `paramāṇu` is established only relative to sensible
form-determinations, their complete destruction leaves no independently
ascertainable eternal atom behind.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.100 unites three domains we have been tracking: spatial enclosure,
temporal enclosure, and `vijñāna`-station. The dhyāna is not merely an inner
psychological condition, while its corresponding World is not merely an
external container. Each names one ordered domain from its cognitive and
cosmological sides.

```text
internal station
    ↔ characteristic operation
        ↔ external World-domain
            ↔ characteristic instability
```

This is the strongest form yet of the Citta–Caitta copula in the Physical
World material. The internal disturbance determines how the station is
operatively articulated; the external contraction displays that same
articulation at the scale of its World-domain. The two are neither identical
things nor unrelated parallels. They are corresponding determinations within
one produced Loka architecture.

The fourth dhyāna then gives a clean stability principle:

```text
remove the internal destabilizing operation
    → no homologous external failure mode belongs to that domain
```

This does not elevate the fourth dhyāna into the Absolute. It remains a
conditioned domain, but one whose enclosure is `āneñja` relative to these
three contraction-types.

The atom excursus adds an equally important systems principle. Destruction of
an organized World does not require that its future possibility be stored as
a surviving miniature copy. Causal law, karmic continuity, and formative
operations are sufficient for reformation. Organization can recur without a
permanent whole-substance or an eternal particle archive.

This is why the chapter's apparent “Bollywood Universe” repeatedly yields
method. Fire, water, and wind are not merely spectacle: they type the failure
modes of World-enclosures according to the operations internal to their
domains.
