# VAK_3.61 Bhāṣya — Seasonal Reversal of Night and Day

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
prāvṛṇmāse dvitīye 'ntyanavamyāṃ vardhate niśā /
hemantānāṃ caturthe tu hīyate 'har viparyayāt // 3.61 //
```

> In the second month of the rains, on the ninth day of the latter fortnight,
> night increases. In the fourth winter month, on that corresponding date, it
> decreases; day varies inversely.

The Bhāṣya names the two months, resolves `antyanavamī` as the ninth day of
the second fortnight, and makes the inverse relation between night and day
explicit. The measure of their daily change is asked only after this unit and
belongs to VAK 3.62.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Tatra punaḥ:

Prāvṛṇmāse dvitīye 'ntyanavamyāṃ vardhate niśā.

Varṣāṇāṃ dvitīye māse Bhādrapade dvitīyasya pakṣasya navamyāṃ vardhate
rātriḥ.

Hemantānāṃ caturthe tu hīyate.

Saiva hemantānāṃ caturthe māse punar hīyate. Antyanavamyām iti vartate:
Phālgunamāsasya dvitīyapakṣanavamyām.

Ahar viparyayāt.

Yadā rātrir vardhate tadā divaso hīyate. Yadā rātrir hīyate tadā divaso
vardhate.
```

The running witness is substantially clear. The continuous text regularizes
spacing, sandhi, capitalization of month names, and punctuation. The supplied
predicate `vartate` makes explicit that `antyanavamyām` is carried forward
from the first half of the verse; it is already present in the Bhāṣya and is
not an independent calendrical conjecture.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Regarding this matter, moreover:

In the second month of the rainy season, on the ninth day of the latter
fortnight, night increases. That is, night increases in Bhādrapada, the second
month of the rains, on the ninth day of its second fortnight.

In the fourth winter month, however, that same night again decreases. The
phrase “on the ninth day of the latter fortnight” remains in force: this is
the ninth day of the second fortnight of the month Phālguna.

Day varies inversely. When night increases, day decreases; when night
decreases, day increases.

## 4. Commentary Movement and Voice

This is a short explanatory unit with one voice, Vasubandhu's. It performs
three operations:

1. it identifies the second rainy-season month as Bhādrapada;
2. it identifies the fourth winter month as Phālguna and carries the same
   fortnight-and-day specification into the second clause;
3. it unfolds `ahar viparyayāt` into two reciprocal conditionals.

There is no stated opponent, alternative school, or contested explanation.
The Bhāṣya is resolving the compression of the kārikā rather than defending
one position against another.

The relation to 3.60 is immediate. That verse ended by attributing the growth
and decrease of night and day to differences in the sun's course. VAK 3.61
now gives the calendar points governing the opposed phases. It does not yet
state the magnitude of each change.

## 5. Calendrical Resolution

The abbreviated calendar expressions are expanded as follows:

| Kārikā expression | Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|
| second rainy-season month | Bhādrapada |
| latter ninth day | ninth day of the second fortnight |
| fourth winter month | Phālguna |
| implied date in second clause | ninth day of its second fortnight |

`Antya` is therefore relational. It distinguishes the latter of the two
fortnights within the month; it does not identify an otherwise unexplained
“last ninth day.” The complete date has three coordinates:

```text
month + second fortnight + ninth lunar day
```

The verse's second line does not repeat all three coordinates. The Bhāṣya
explicitly carries `antyanavamyām` forward, preventing the fourth winter month
from being treated as an imprecise interval.

## 6. Reciprocal Variation

The Bhāṣya expands the compressed `ahar viparyayāt` symmetrically:

```text
when night increases → day decreases
when night decreases → day increases
```

The verse is not describing two independent quantities that happen to change
near one another. Day and night are complementary durations within one
day–night cycle. An increase in one is reciprocally a decrease in the other.

This yields two distinct determinations that should not be collapsed:

```text
calendar address → which direction of change applies
inverse relation  → how the two durations vary together
```

The first identifies the seasonal phase; the second specifies the coupling of
the variables. VAK 3.62 will add the size of the incremental change and its
relation to the sun's northward or southward motion.

## 7. Translation and Doctrinal Limits

The conventional translation uses “increases” and “decreases,” matching the
present forms `vardhate` and `hīyate`. It is natural to understand the named
dates as points at which the trend is operative or reverses, but the Bhāṣya
does not introduce a separate technical term for a solstice or formulate a
modern astronomical model.

Similarly, “day varies inversely” renders the kārikā's compact expression,
while the prose supplies its exact two-way meaning. `Viparyaya` here is not a
general metaphysical inversion. Its scope is the reciprocal variation of the
durations of day and night.

The calendrical identifications belong to the Bhāṣya, not to the literal words
of the kārikā. The root verse says “second month of the rains” and “fourth of
the winter months”; Bhādrapada and Phālguna are Vasubandhu's explicit
commentarial determinations.

## 8. Logical Determination

The passage specifies a coupled cyclic process:

```text
at the Bhādrapada address:
    night-direction = increase
    day-direction   = decrease

at the Phālguna address:
    night-direction = decrease
    day-direction   = increase
```

The two dates do not create night and day. They index opposed phases in an
already continuing process. What changes is the direction of variation of
each duration.

The Bhāṣya also distinguishes state from trend. “It is night” identifies a
current temporal phase; “night is increasing” states how the duration of that
phase changes across the seasonal cycle. The locale-indexed temporal states
of 3.60 and the calendar-indexed trends of 3.61 are therefore complementary,
not interchangeable.

No numerical conservation equation is stated. A restrained reconstruction
may represent the inverse relation as redistribution within a fixed
day–night whole, but the precise increment is withheld until the next verse.

## 9. Organon Study

For the Organon, this unit shows how a date functions as more than a label. It
is an address into a cyclic process and selects a determinate trend-state:

```text
calendar address
    → phase of cycle
        → paired directions of change
```

The important Feature is not merely `month = Bhādrapada` or `month =
Phālguna`. Its meaning lies in the configured relation among month, fortnight,
lunar day, and the reciprocal day–night process. A Dataset that stores the
date but loses the selected transition has retained the syntax of the calendar
while discarding its determination here.

This is a project-level reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's terminology. The
text itself determines a calendrical rule within produced Loka: two temporal
magnitudes are intelligible through their reciprocal movement in one ordered
cycle.
