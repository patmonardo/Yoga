# VAK_3.40 Bhāṣya — Mental Nutriments and the Birth-Seeking Intermediate Being

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
sparśasaṃcetanāvijñā āhārāḥ sāsravās triṣu /
manomayaḥ saṃbhavaiṣī gandharvaś cāntarābhavaḥ // 3.40 //
```

> Contact, volition, and differentiated cognition are nutriments when with
> outflows, in the three realms. Intermediate existence is mind-made, seeking
> birth, and a gandharva.

The Bhāṣya completes the enumeration of the four nutriments, excludes the
uncontaminated forms through the function of nutriment, and identifies the
sūtra's “one seeking birth” with intermediate existence under several
functional designations.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

The running witness is mechanically damaged in compounds, the explanation of
`abhinirvṛtti`, and the final compressed sentence. The continuous text
regularizes evident forms provisionally. The local derivation of `gandharva`,
the exact wording of both sūtra citations, and the distribution of the
fourfold alternative require comparison with critical witnesses.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Contact, volition, and differentiated cognition are nutriments when they are
with outflows, and they occur in all three realms. Contact is born from the
conjunction of the triad. Volition is mental action. Volition and
differentiated cognition are nutriments only in their contaminated forms.

Why are the uncontaminated forms not nutriments? Because the meaning of
nutriment is the fostering of becoming, while the uncontaminated dharmas arise
for the exhaustion of becoming, according to the Vaibhāṣikas.

Moreover, the sūtra says: “These four nutriments are for the persistence and
maintenance of beings already arisen, and for the support of those seeking
birth.” Uncontaminated dharmas do not function in this way and therefore are
not nutriments.

“Already-arisen beings” are understood as those who have been reborn. Who,
then, are those seeking birth? Intermediate existence is mind-made, seeking
birth, and a gandharva; it is also called emergence. The Blessed One referred
to intermediate existence by these designations.

It is called mind-made because it is born from mind and exists without first
appropriating some external material such as semen and blood. It is called
birth-seeking because seeking coming-to-be is its characteristic. It is called
a gandharva, and it is called emergence because it faces toward rebirth.

This terminology is supported by the sūtra statement that, having produced an
unafflicted bodily existence, one is reborn in an afflicted world. It is also
supported by the scriptural fourfold alternative concerning a person whose
fetter of emergence is abandoned while the fetter of rebirth is not.

The first case is an upward-streaming non-returner who is dispassionate toward
two realms; the second, one who attains complete nirvāṇa in intermediate
existence; the third, arhats; and the fourth comprises the remaining
configurations after these are set aside. The closing sentence appears to
contrast arhats among the already-arisen with thirst-possessing beings who
seek birth, but its exact syntax is textually compressed and remains
provisional.

## 4. Argument and Voices

The unit has two connected arguments:

1. The three non-material nutriments are contact, mental volition, and
   differentiated cognition, but only insofar as they are `sāsrava` and
   operative within the three realms.
2. The sūtra defines nutriment by support of beings already arisen and beings
   seeking birth; the latter expression therefore requires an account of
   intermediate existence.

An objector asks why uncontaminated contact, volition, and cognition are not
nutriments. The Vaibhāṣikas answer through the opposition between fostering
becoming and exhausting becoming. Vasubandhu then adds the sūtra's own
functional definition.

The several names of intermediate existence come from scriptural usage and
explain different relations of one referent. They do not posit several
different transitional beings.

## 5. Three Nutriments in Three Realms

Together with morsel nutriment from VAK 3.39, the class is now complete:

```text
1. kavaḍīkārāhāra
   morsel nutriment

2. sparśāhāra
   contact nutriment

3. manaḥsaṃcetanā
   mental-volition nutriment

4. vijñānāhāra
   differentiated-cognition nutriment
```

The three stated in VAK 3.40 occur in the desire, form, and formless realms.
This differs from morsel nutriment, which is restricted to the desire realm.
The verse therefore coordinates two axes that must remain separate:

```text
kind of nutriment
contamination status
realm of occurrence
```

The Bhāṣya glosses contact by its threefold genesis and volition by its status
as mental action. Their precise pairwise functions in maintaining present and
future existence are developed in the next unit.

## 6. Nutriment Fosters Becoming

The decisive restriction is teleological:

```text
sāsrava factor
    operative toward bhavāpoṣaṇa
    fostering of becoming
        → nutriment

anāsrava factor
    operative toward bhavakṣaya
    exhaustion of becoming
        → not nutriment
```

The category is therefore not fixed by the broad name `sparśa`, `cetanā`, or
`vijñāna` alone. A formally similar factor can have a contrary direction and
thereby fall outside the technical function of nutriment.

The sūtra confirms the functional criterion: nutriment maintains beings who
have arisen and supports those oriented toward a further birth.
Uncontaminated dharmas do neither in the relevant sense because their
operation tends toward the exhaustion of conditioned becoming.

## 7. The Birth-Seeking Intermediate Existence

The transition from nutriment to intermediate existence is logically
generated, not a change of subject:

```text
nutriment supports
    already-arisen beings
    + birth-seeking beings

question
    who is the birth-seeking being?

answer
    antarābhava
```

The designations distinguish different axes:

| Designation | Determination |
|---|---|
| `manomaya` | produced from mind without first appropriating external reproductive matter |
| `saṃbhavaiṣin` | characterized by seeking coming-to-be |
| `gandharva` | scriptural designation; local etymology uncertain |
| `abhinirvṛtti` | emergence facing toward rebirth |

“Mind-made” does not mean imaginary or produced by arbitrary thought. It
marks a mode of production before the material basis of the coming embodiment
has been appropriated.

## 8. Emergence and Rebirth Are Distinct

The scriptural fourfold alternative depends upon distinguishing:

```text
abhinirvṛttisaṃyojana
    fetter of emergence into intermediate existence

upapattisaṃyojana
    fetter of rebirth proper
```

If the two were simply identical, the four cases could not be distributed
among upward-streaming non-returners, those attaining nirvāṇa in the
intermediate state, arhats, and the remaining configurations. The citation
therefore supports `abhinirvṛtti` as a designation of the transitional mode
facing rebirth rather than rebirth itself.

The argument is limited. It establishes a doctrinal and scriptural
distinction between two phases and their fetters; it does not yet provide the
detailed allocation of nutriments to beings already arisen and those seeking
birth. That question opens VAK 3.41.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The four nutriments show that a conditioned world is maintained materially,
relationally, purposively, and cognitively. Yet VAK 3.40 adds the decisive
qualification: an operation counts as alimentary because of the future it
supports.

For the Organon, this gives a direction-sensitive classification:

```text
same broad operation type
    + reinforces continued configuration
        → maintenance input

same broad operation type
    + exhausts that configuration
        → not maintenance input
```

Thus the syntax or data type of an operation does not settle its semantic
function. Contact, intention, and differentiated cognition can perpetuate a
conditioned configuration or participate in its cessation. Provenance must
record direction and effect, not merely operation name.

The intermediate existence also gives a disciplined model of a transition
record. `Manomaya`, `saṃbhavaiṣin`, `gandharva`, and `abhinirvṛtti` are not
arbitrary aliases: each selects production mode, directed activity,
scriptural designation, or terminal orientation. Multiple valid Views over
one entity require an explicit principle of projection.

Finally, the passage presents transition as sustained and purposive without
turning it into an eternal subject. The intermediate mode is generated,
nutritionally supported, and oriented toward another arising. This can inform
the project's theory of an Outflow as a Project—an operation directed toward
a possible configuration—while remaining clearly an Organon reconstruction,
not Vasubandhu's terminology.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit at 153.04–153.20: the three
non-material nutriments, their contaminated status and three-realm extent,
the exclusion of uncontaminated factors, the sūtra's already-arisen and
birth-seeking classes, four designations of intermediate existence, and the
scriptural distinction between emergence and rebirth fetters.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The two sūtra citations,
the etymology of `gandharva`, `abhinirvṛtti`, the allocation of the fourfold
alternative, and especially the compressed final sentence require comparison
with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations. The
Study stops before the question at 153.21 and does not begin VAK 3.41's
allocation of the four nutriments.
