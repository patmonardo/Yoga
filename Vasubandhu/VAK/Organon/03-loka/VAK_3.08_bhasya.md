# VAK_3.08 Bhāṣya — Consciousness, Field, Inclusion, and Birth

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vijñānaṃ na sthitiḥ proktaṃ catuṣkoṭi tu saṃgrahe /
> catasro yonayas tatra sattvānām aṇḍajādayaḥ // 3.08 //

> Consciousness is not itself declared to be one of the four
> supports; when inclusion is examined, however, the relation is
> four-cornered. Within that three-domain world, beings have four modes of
> birth, beginning with egg-birth.

The first half completes the relation between the seven cognition-stations
and the four aggregate-supports. The second introduces a new axis: the manner
in which sentient continuities arise. The Bhāṣya calls `yoni` a `jāti`, a kind
or mode of birth, and groups beings through similarity of generation.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Why is consciousness not itself called a station of consciousness, whereas
the seven stations include all five aggregates? Because a station is designated
apart from what stands upon it. The one that stands is not itself its seat,
just as the king is not himself the royal throne. The Vaibhāṣikas say that
the dharmas which consciousness mounts and by which it travels, like a boat
passenger, are called its stations. Consciousness does not mount itself and
travel; therefore it is not included among the four.

But what, then, of the sūtra statement: “When there is delight and craving for
consciousness as nutriment, consciousness is established and has mounted
there”? And how is it said that “the seven stations of consciousness are
included within the five aggregates”?

In that case, when consciousness occurs with delight in the aggregates taken
together, without distinction, within a birthplace, consciousness too belongs
to a station of consciousness. But when the four are considered individually
as form and the rest, each becomes a condition for the defilement of
consciousness. Therefore, among the four stations alone, “consciousness is
not declared to be a station.”

Moreover, the Blessed One taught the four stations of consciousness under
the aspect of field, while consciousness together with appropriation, taken
as a whole, was taught under the aspect of seed. He did not establish the
seed itself as the field of that seed. The dharmas that coexist with
consciousness are better fitted to function as its field; therefore they
alone are called its stations.

How, then, are the four supports included within the seven cognition-stations,
or the seven within the four? The four are not simply included within the
seven, nor are the seven simply included within the four.

“When inclusion is examined, the relation is four-cornered.” Four cases must
be understood. Something may be included within the seven but not within the
four, and so forth. The first corner is the consciousness occurring within the
seven stations. The second is the aggregates other than consciousness, as
present in the bad destinies, the fourth dhyāna, and the peak of existence.
The third is the four support aggregate types, as applicable, within the
seven stations. The fourth is what remains after these cases are set aside.

The three-domain world described above as differentiated by destinies and the
other distinctions should also be understood as containing “four modes of
birth for beings, beginning with egg-birth.” These are egg-birth, womb-birth,
moisture-birth, and spontaneous birth. `Yoni` means a kind of birth. Beings
[join or mingle] within it, grouped by similarity in their manner of
generation.

Which is egg-birth? Beings born from eggs, such as geese, cranes, cakravāka
birds, peacocks, parrots, mynahs, and others.

Which is womb-birth? Beings born from a womb or enclosing membrane, such as
elephants, horses, cattle, buffalo, donkeys, boars, and others.

Which is moisture-birth? Beings born from the moisture of the earth, such as
worms, insects, moths, mosquitoes, and others.

Which is spontaneous birth? Beings who arise all at once, complete, with
unimpaired faculties and furnished with all major and minor limbs. They are
called spontaneously born because their production is [well accomplished].
Devas, hell-beings, beings in intermediate existence, and others are
examples.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The passage has four successive movements:

```text
Vaibhāṣika rider/support explanation
    challenged by scriptural uses including cognition

seed–field distinction
    explains the restricted fourfold classification

four-cornered inclusion analysis
    relates the seven and four without reducing either

four modes of birth
    reopen the three-domain world under generation
```

The Vaibhāṣikas are explicitly named for the rider-and-support account. An
unnamed questioning voice introduces scriptural counterevidence. The
subsequent answer distinguishes collective classification by birthplace from
the four supports considered individually, then adds the field-and-seed
explanation. The remaining questions are expository.

The commentary therefore does not merely state that cognition is seed and
the other aggregates are field. It reaches that distinction through pressure
upon a simpler account in which the bearer cannot be its own support.

## 5. Why Cognition Is Not Its Own Support

The first explanation distinguishes what stands from that upon which it
stands:

```text
vijñāna
    rider, voyager, or standing member

four coexisting aggregates
    what it mounts
    what carries it
    its supports
```

The king and royal seat analogy makes the asymmetry vivid. Yet the sūtra can
speak of delight and craving in cognition itself and can include all five
aggregates within the seven cognition-stations. The Bhāṣya consequently
distinguishes two scopes:

```text
associated aggregates taken together within a birthplace
    cognition may be included in a cognition-station

four aggregates considered individually as fields of cognition
    cognition is not its own support
```

Thus “cognition is not a support” is not an unrestricted statement that
cognition never belongs to anything called a `vijñānasthiti`. It applies to
the fourfold support-classification.

## 6. Seed and Field

The second explanation assigns different functions:

| Function | Member |
|---|---|
| seed | consciousness together with appropriation |
| field | the four coexisting aggregates |

A seed is not the field for itself. The aggregates coexisting with cognition
are especially suited to function as its field, and so they are called its
supports. The analogy establishes functional non-identity within a conditioned
relation; it does not turn cognition into a permanent inner germ or the other
aggregates into an independently existing container.

`Sopādānaṃ vijñānam` should remain “cognition together with appropriation.”
The passage does not license translating `upādāna` as a substantial owner of
the aggregates. Seed and field are explanatory roles occupied by conditioned
dharmas.

The field account fits the earlier qualification `sāsrava`: these aggregates
can serve as fields for consciousness under appropriation, delight, craving,
and defilement. The Bhāṣya does not derive their with-outflow status from the
seed-and-field analogy alone.

## 7. Four-Cornered Inclusion and Four Birth-Modes

The `catuṣkoṭi` is an exact inclusion analysis, not a metaphysical denial of
four propositions:

| Corner | In the seven | In the four | Bhāṣya case |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| first | yes | no | cognition occurring within the seven cognition-stations |
| second | no | yes | aggregates other than consciousness in bad destinies, fourth dhyāna, and peak of existence |
| third | yes | yes | the four support aggregate types, where present, within the seven |
| fourth | no | no | remaining cases after the preceding three are excluded |

The two classifications overlap but neither simply contains the other. Their
different membership follows from their different questions: the seven
classify modes of existence in which cognition stands; the four classify the
aggregate-fields supporting cognition.

The second corner says `vijñānavarjyāḥ skandhāḥ`, aggregates other than
consciousness. It must not be glossed as four aggregates in every excluded
state: the formless peak of existence lacks the form aggregate. The same
qualification applies to the third corner's four support types wherever a
formless station is under consideration.

The verse then moves from establishment to generation. The four `yoni` are
not four substantial species of self but four similarities in the manner of
birth:

```text
egg-born
womb-born
moisture-born
spontaneously arisen
```

The last mode is defined positively by complete arising at once, with
faculties and bodily members intact. Intermediate-existence beings are
included here, a fact that prepares the coming sustained analysis of their
status and transition.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

`Vijñānaṃ na sthitiḥ proktam` is restricted by context: cognition is not
declared to be one of the four aggregate-supports, although it is included
when all five aggregates constitute one of the seven cognition-stations. The
translation makes this scope explicit.

`Saṃgraha` means inclusion or extensional containment. `Catuṣkoṭi` names the
four combinations generated by membership and non-membership in the seven
and the four. “Fourfold classification” would be too vague.

`Tatra` returns to the already differentiated three-domain world. `Yoni` is
best rendered “mode of birth” rather than simply “womb,” because the Bhāṣya
defines it through `jāti` and applies it to four manners of generation.

`Upapāduka` is “spontaneously arisen”: the being arises complete at once.
This does not mean uncaused. Devas, hell-beings, and intermediate-existence
beings arise through conditioned causes even though their bodily formation is
not gradual in the manner of egg- or womb-birth.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The restrained philosophical result is that cognition cannot be understood
as self-supporting:

```text
cognition as seed
    is established through
coexisting aggregates as field
```

The two terms are inseparable within the process but not interchangeable in
function. This is more precise than saying that consciousness merely
“projects” its own world. The projecting or seed-function requires a field,
while the field is the conditioned complex through which that cognition can
stand and become defiled.

The four-corner analysis adds a methodological lesson for the later Oculus
work. Two valid analytical schemas may cross-classify the same domain without
one being reducible to the other. Their alignment must be computed through
explicit inclusion cases rather than assumed from shared terminology.

The move to birth then adds genesis to standing. Loka is now analyzed through
where beings occur, how cognition is supported there, and how a sentient
continuity arises into its configuration. A later learning pass can examine
the relation of this seed–field account to Projection and Outflow. For now it
is enough to retain the textual distinctions and avoid making `vijñāna` an
absolute projector or `yoni` a substantial vessel of self.
