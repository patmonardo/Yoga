# VAK_3.91

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अन्तःकल्पोऽमिताद्यावद्दशवर्षायुषस्ततः ।
>
> उत्कर्षा अपकर्षाश्च कल्पा अष्टादशापरे ॥ ३.९१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> antaḥkalpo 'mitād yāvad daśavarṣāyuṣas tataḥ /
>
> utkarṣā apakarṣāś ca kalpā aṣṭādaśāpare // 3.91 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| antaḥkalpaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | intermediate kalpa; internal temporal enclosure of the standing World |
| amitāt | ablative neuter singular | from immeasurable lifespan |
| yāvat | indeclinable | until, as far as |
| daśavarṣāyuṣaḥ | genitive singular or compound limit-expression | of beings whose lifespan is ten years |
| tataḥ | indeclinable | thereafter, following the first intermediate kalpa |
| utkarṣāḥ | nominative masculine plural | upward movements or increases of lifespan |

| aṣṭādaśa | cardinal numeral | eighteen |
| apare | nominative masculine plural | the subsequent or remaining ones |

`Utkarṣa` and `apakarṣa` name directed processes, not static high and low
values. The Bhāṣya fixes their ordinary endpoints at ten and eighty thousand
years.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first line defines the first intermediate kalpa of the standing World by
its directed lifespan-change:

```text
amitāt [āyuṣaḥ]
    yāvat daśa-varṣa-āyuṣaḥ [manuṣyāḥ]
    [yaḥ kālaḥ]
    antaḥ-kalpaḥ [saḥ]
```

> The interval from immeasurable lifespan until humans have a ten-year
> lifespan is one intermediate kalpa.

The second line describes the next eighteen:

```text
tataḥ
    apare kalpāḥ aṣṭādaśa
    utkarṣāḥ apakarṣāḥ ca [bhavanti]
```

> Thereafter, the next eighteen kalpas each contain increase and decrease.

The plural coordination is distributive: it does not divide the eighteen into
one group of increases and another of decreases. The Bhāṣya states that each
of the eighteen contains both movements.

The twentieth intermediate kalpa, consisting only of increase, belongs to the
Bhāṣya continuation and is completed textually in 3.92.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> One intermediate kalpa extends from immeasurable lifespan until humans have
> a lifespan of ten years. Thereafter, the next eighteen kalpas contain
> increases and decreases.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The first intermediate kalpa of the standing World is the gradual decline
> of human lifespan from immeasurable duration to ten years. Each of the next
> eighteen intermediate kalpas rises from ten years to the fixed upper limit
> of 80,000 years and then declines again to ten. A twentieth intermediate
> kalpa consists only of the final increase from ten to 80,000 years. Despite
> these different internal trajectories, all twenty intermediate kalpas are
> equal in duration.

The 80,000-year upper bound, the one-way twentieth increase, and equality in
duration of all twenty kalpas are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Temporal enclosure has both magnitude and internal articulation. One equal
> duration can contain a simple decline, another a complete ascent-and-decline,
> and another a simple ascent. Equality of temporal measure therefore does not
> erase difference of temporal form. Human lifespan functions as the
> state-variable through which the World-cycle becomes legible: its direction,
> extrema, and reversals determine which phase the temporal enclosure contains.

| vivṛtta | expanded / formed | condition of the World whose standing period is internally measured |

| amita / aparimita | immeasurable | initial lifespan of the first decline |
| daśavarṣāyuṣ | having a ten-year lifespan | lower bound of the oscillating sequence |
| aśītivarṣasahasrāyuṣ | having an 80,000-year lifespan | upper bound of ordinary increases |

| apara | following / remaining | the eighteen two-directional intermediate kalpas |

The scale is not symmetrical at the immeasurable endpoint. Only the first
decline begins from immeasurable lifespan; ordinary subsequent increases stop
at 80,000 years.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Twentyfold standing-world structure

Let `Tᵢ` be the `i`th intermediate kalpa:

| Position | Internal trajectory | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| `T₁` | immeasurable → 10 | 1 |
| `T₂`–`T₁₉` | 10 → 80,000 → 10 | 18 |
| `T₂₀` | 10 → 80,000 | 1 |

Thus:

```text
1 + 18 + 1 = 20 intermediate kalpas
```

The first line of the verse states `T₁`; the second states `T₂` through `T₁₉`.
The Bhāṣya supplies `T₂₀`, whose kārikā continuation follows in 3.92.

### Directed state transitions

For the middle cycles:

```text
lowerBound = 10 years
upperBound = 80,000 years

utkarṣa:
    lifespan increases monotonically from lowerBound to upperBound

apakarṣa:
    lifespan decreases monotonically from upperBound to lowerBound
```

The lifespan value is not the temporal enclosure itself. It is the state
variable whose ordered change articulates the enclosure's internal process.

### Equal duration, unequal shape

Let `duration(Tᵢ)` denote external temporal magnitude and `shape(Tᵢ)` its
internal transition pattern. The Bhāṣya asserts:

```text
for all i, j in 1..20:
    duration(Tᵢ) = duration(Tⱼ)
```

while:

```text
shape(T₁)  ≠ shape(T₂)
shape(T₂)  ≠ shape(T₂₀)
shape(T₁)  ≠ shape(T₂₀)
```

The one-way first decline and final increase therefore proceed more slowly
than either corresponding half of a middle kalpa. Equal duration is preserved
through different rates of change.

### Temporal enclosure schema

```text
TemporalEnclosure =
    <fixed duration,
     initial state,
     transition path,
     terminal state>
```

Two enclosures may share `fixed duration` while differing in every remaining
field.

### Relation to formation

The Bhāṣya separately notes that nineteen intermediate kalpas pass during
World-formation while human lifespan remains immeasurable. These formation
intervals are not the same series as the twenty intermediate kalpas of the
formed World's standing phase analyzed above.

## 9. Interpretive Note

Your distinction between spatial and temporal enclosure is exactly the
Kant-power required here:

```text
spatial enclosure
    → determines coexistence, placement, and support of World-stations

temporal enclosure
    → determines succession, direction, rate, and transformation of states
```

Neither can replace the other. The same spatially articulated human World can
be temporally enclosed under decline, oscillation, or increase. Conversely,
the lifespan trajectory requires a stationed population whose condition can
change. Spatial and temporal enclosure reciprocally determine the concrete
World-process.

VAK 3.91 then adds a particularly strong result: the Mind of the Temporal
Enclosure preserves equal magnitude through unequal internal articulation.

```text
same temporal capacity
    can home:
        one long decline
        one ascent plus decline
        one long ascent
```

The enclosure is therefore not the sum of a fixed list of enclosed transitions.
It determines the duration within which different transition-shapes can be
organized. This is Kant-clean because temporal form supplies the condition of
succession; it is Kant-power because the Kośa makes that form operational as a
state machine with direction, bounds, rates, and equality constraints.

The lifespan of embodied agents becomes the observable state-variable of the
cosmic process. The temporal enclosure is known through what it makes possible
for those agents, while their ordered transformation displays the internal
articulation of the enclosure.

```ts
type Trajectory =
  | { kind: "Decline"; from: "Immeasurable"; to: 10 }
  | { kind: "Oscillation"; from: 10; peak: 80000; to: 10 }
  | { kind: "Increase"; from: 10; to: 80000 };

interface IntermediateKalpa {
  ordinal: number;
  equalDurationClass: "StandingWorldAntarakalpa";
  trajectory: Trajectory;
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It keeps
external duration distinct from the process-shape enclosed within it.
