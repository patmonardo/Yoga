# VAK_3.84

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> वाहाद्वर्षशतेनैकतिलोद्धारक्षयायुषः ।
>
> अर्बुदाद् विंशतिगुणप्रतिवृद्ध्यायुषः परे ॥ ३.८४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vāhād varṣaśatenaika-tiloddhāra-kṣayāyuṣaḥ /
>
> arbudād viṃśatiguṇa-prativṛddhyāyuṣaḥ pare // 3.84 //

The normalized `arbudāt` follows the Bhāṣya's citation and syntax: after
Arbuda, the later cold-naraka lifespans increase successively twentyfold. The
immediate metrical witness has an unstable form at this point.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

varṣa-śatena                 → varṣaśatena
eka-tila-uddhāra             → ekatiloddhāra

arbudāt                      → arbudād (before v-)

prati-vṛddhi-āyuṣaḥ          → prativṛddhyāyuṣaḥ

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| vāhāt | ablative masculine singular | from a vāha-capacity filled with sesame seeds |
| varṣaśatena | instrumental neuter singular | at an interval of one hundred years |
| eka | compound member | one at each operation |
| tila | compound member | sesame seed; minimal removed item |
| uddhāra | compound member | removal, taking out |
| kṣaya | compound member | exhaustion or depletion of the store |
| āyuṣaḥ | nominative masculine plural | beings possessing the stated or compared lifespans |

| prativṛddhi | compound member | successive increase at each following station |
| pare | nominative masculine plural | the later cold-naraka beings or classes |

The compact first line can sound like an equation between Arbuda lifespan and
the depletion time. The Bhāṣya explicitly blocks that construal by calling the
procedure `upamānamātra`, comparison alone.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first line presents the comparison through a compressed lifespan
compound:

```text
[arbudopapannāḥ]
    vāhāt
    varṣa-śatena
    eka-tila-uddhāra-kṣaya-[upamāna]-āyuṣaḥ
```

> Those born in Arbuda have a lifespan compared with the exhaustion of a vāha
> from which one sesame seed is removed every hundred years.

The Bhāṣya supplies the missing relation: even the complete depletion of that
store would occur sooner than the end of an Arbuda lifespan. Thus “lasting
until exhaustion” is too strong as a literal equality and too weak as a
statement of Arbuda duration.

The second line states an exact recurrence:

```text
arbudāt [ūrdhvam]
    pare
    viṃśati-guṇa-prativṛddhi-āyuṣaḥ [santi]
```

> After Arbuda, the later classes have lifespans successively increased
> twentyfold.

The ablative `arbudāt` establishes Arbuda as the base station; `pare` ranges
over the following cold narakas.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The Arbuda lifespan is indicated through the exhaustion of a vāha by the
> removal of one sesame seed every hundred years; after Arbuda, the later
> lifespans increase successively twentyfold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Suppose a sealed Māgadha vāha of twenty khārīs were completely filled with
> sesame seeds, and one seed were removed only at the end of every hundred
> years. That entire store would be exhausted before the lifespan of a being
> born in Arbuda reached its limit. This comparison indicates the magnitude of
> the Arbuda lifespan without equating the two. Each subsequent cold-naraka
> lifespan is exactly twenty times the preceding one: twenty Arbudas make one
> Nirarbuda, and the same recurrence continues through Mahāpadma.

The twenty-khārī specification, sealed-container description, strict
comparative inequality, and cited examples of the recurrence are Bhāṣya
determinations supplied through a saying attributed to the Buddha.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System distinguishes what can be calculated from what can only be
> indicated. Arbuda's base duration is presented by a finite depletion
> procedure that still fails to reach its endpoint; the comparison therefore
> establishes magnitude without pretending to exhaust it. Once that base is
> given, however, the relation among the later cold narakas is exact. An
> inexactly specified base can support a perfectly determinate recurrence.

exact calendar total while preserving a finite ordered lifespan within Loka.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Finite depletion procedure

Let `S` be the number of sesame seeds contained in the twenty-khārī vāha.
The comparison procedure is:

```text
initial store = S seeds
interval      = 100 years
operation     = remove 1 seed
termination   = store reaches 0 seeds
```

Its depletion duration is:

```text
D = 100S years
```

The Buddha's comparison, as quoted in the Bhāṣya, asserts:

```text
D < Lifespan(Arbuda)
```

It does not assert:

```text
D = Lifespan(Arbuda)
```

Because `S` is finite, the procedure has a determinate endpoint. Its function
is to establish a strict lower comparison for a still greater duration.

### Exact recurrence above the base

Let `C₀ = Arbuda` and let `Cₙ₊₁` be the next cold naraka. Then:

```text
Lifespan(Cₙ₊₁) = 20 × Lifespan(Cₙ)
```

The eight-member series is:

| n | Cold naraka | Lifespan relative to Arbuda |
|---:|---|---:|
| 0 | Arbuda | `A` |
| 1 | Nirarbuda | `20A` |
| 2 | Aṭaṭa | `20²A` |
| 3 | Hahava | `20³A` |
| 4 | Huhuva | `20⁴A` |
| 5 | Utpala | `20⁵A` |
| 6 | Padma | `20⁶A` |
| 7 | Mahāpadma | `20⁷A` |

The names of the full cold-naraka sequence are established earlier in the
Loka analysis; in this unit the Bhāṣya explicitly confirms the first and last
recurrence examples.

### Mixed epistemic specification

The complete result is:

```text
base A:
    known by strict comparison
    A > 100S years

series relation:
    known by exact multiplier
    Cₙ = 20ⁿA
```

Hence every later lifespan inherits a corresponding lower comparison:

```text
Lifespan(Cₙ) > 20ⁿ × 100S years
```

The passage combines an open base magnitude with a closed transformation law.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.84 is a small lesson in scientific honesty. The Kośa does not replace an
unavailable total with false precision. It states exactly what the comparison
can warrant:

```text
illustrative procedure
    → establishes a strict lower comparison
    → does not calculate the target duration

recurrence rule
    → establishes an exact ratio between stations
    → does not require the base to be numerically known
```

The semantic distinction between comparison and equation is decisive. A
syntactic system may see the depletion procedure beside `āyus` and serialize
it as the lifespan's value. The Bhāṣya instead assigns it an epistemic role:
the procedure makes magnitude conceivable while explicitly failing to exhaust
the thing compared.

This produces a sophisticated World-System representation. Not every field
must be a scalar. Arbuda's lifespan can be stored as a constraint, while every
successor can be stored as an exact function of that constrained base:

```text
Arbuda:
    lowerBoundExclusive = sesameDepletionDuration

Nirarbuda:
    exactRelation = 20 × Arbuda
```

The Rational Agent therefore needs provenance not only for quantities and
units, but for the epistemic force of a statement: equality, bound, analogy,
or recurrence.

```ts
type DurationKnowledge =
  | { kind: "ExactValue"; years: bigint }
  | { kind: "StrictLowerComparison"; procedure: "SesameVahaDepletion" }
  | { kind: "ExactRecurrence"; factor: 20; base: "PreviousColdNaraka" };
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It
prevents an illustrative comparison from being silently promoted into an
exact measurement.
