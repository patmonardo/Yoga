# VAK_3.67 Bhāṣya — Vaijayanta and Sudarśana's Gardens

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
sārdhadviśatapārśvo 'tra vaijayantaḥ bahiḥ punaḥ /
tac caitrarathapāruṣyamiśranandanabhūṣitam // 3.67 //
```

> Here Vaijayanta has sides of 250 yojanas. Outside, the city is adorned by
> Caitraratha, Pāruṣya, Miśra, and Nandana.

The Bhāṣya identifies Vaijayanta as Śakra's palace at the center of Sudarśana,
explains its jeweled arrangement and comparative splendor, then turns to four
divine recreation grounds outside the city. The gardens adorn Sudarśana; the
next verse gives additional spatial and functional details.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Sārdhadviśatapārśvo 'tra Vaijayantaḥ.

Śakrasya devānām Indrasya Vaijayanto nāma prāsādo nagarasya madhye,
nānāratnasthānavidhānasaṃpadā sarvānyabhavanaśrīmahimnā hrepaṇo,
dairghyeṇārdhatṛtīye yojanaśate pārśvaṃ pārśvam.

Iyaṃ tāvan nagarasyābhirāmatā.

Bahiḥ punaḥ:

Tac Caitrarathapāruṣyamiśranandanabhūṣitam.

Tasya hi nagarasya bahiś caturṣu pārśveṣu catvāry udyānādīni devānāṃ
krīḍābhūmayaḥ.

Caitraratham udyānaṃ, Pāruṣyakaṃ, Miśrakāvaṇaṃ, Nandanavanaṃ ca. Tais tan
nagaraṃ bahir alaṅkṛtam.
```

The witness has routine word-joining and spelling variation, regularized here
for continuous reading. `Dairghyeṇārdhatṛtīye yojanaśate` is rendered as a
measure of 250 yojanas on each side, in agreement with the kārikā's clear
`sārdhadviśata-pārśva`. The prose phrase describing the palace's comparative
splendor is syntactically compressed; the translation preserves its sense
without supplying a more elaborate construction.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Here Vaijayanta has sides of 250 yojanas.

Vaijayanta is the palace of Śakra, Indra of the gods, in the middle of the
city. Through the excellence of its arrangement of many kinds of jewels, it
puts to shame the splendor and magnificence of all other residences. Its
length is 250 yojanas on each side.

So much for the city's beauty.

Outside, in turn, it is adorned by Caitraratha, Pāruṣya, Miśra, and Nandana.

Outside that city, on its four sides, are four gardens and related places,
the gods' recreation grounds. They are the Caitraratha garden, Pāruṣyaka, the
Miśraka grove, and the Nandana grove. By these, the city is adorned on the
outside.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voices

Vasubandhu moves from the city's center to its exterior:

1. Vaijayanta is identified as Śakra's palace in the middle of Sudarśana.
2. Its jeweled arrangement and comparative splendor complete the description
   of the city's interior beauty.
3. The phrase `bahiḥ punaḥ` turns explicitly to the outside.
4. Four recreation grounds occupy the city's four sides and adorn it
   externally.

No opponent or alternative school is named. The names in the kārikā are
retained as proper names; the Bhāṣya supplies their forms and classifies them
as gardens, grounds, and groves used by the gods for recreation.

## 5. Vaijayanta at the City's Center

The palace has a nested location:

```text
Meru summit
    → Sudarśana at the summit's center
        → Vaijayanta at Sudarśana's center
```

The Bhāṣya identifies Vaijayanta as the palace of Śakra, “Indra of the gods.”
It measures 250 yojanas on each side. Its splendor is attributed to the
excellent arrangement of many kinds of jewels, which surpasses the majesty of
other residences.

The prose therefore attributes magnificence to both material variety and
arrangement. It is not simply an inventory of jewels; their ordered placement
contributes to the palace's distinction.

## 6. Four Recreation Grounds

Outside Sudarśana, on its four sides, are four places for divine recreation:

| Name | Bhāṣya description |
|---|---|
| Caitraratha | garden |
| Pāruṣyaka | named recreation ground |
| Miśraka | grove |
| Nandana | grove |

The general description `udyānādīni` allows a garden and related kinds of
grounds; the names are not forced into one botanical form. Their shared
function is explicit: they are `krīḍābhūmi`, places where the gods play or
recreate.

The prose locates the four grounds around the four sides but does not assign
each name to a particular cardinal direction. That more specific mapping is
not supplied here.

## 7. Adornment and Spatial Relation

The city is adorned externally by the grounds surrounding it:

```text
Vaijayanta → central palace
Sudarśana  → containing city
four grounds → exterior recreation spaces
```

`Bahiḥ` marks the transition from the inner civic center to the outside, and
`bahir alaṅkṛtam` describes the effect of the grounds upon the city as a
whole. The gardens remain distinct places with their own recreation function;
their relation to Sudarśana also makes them its external adornment.

## 8. Doctrinal Determination

The description connects spatial arrangement, political residence, jeweled
construction, and recreation:

```text
city center
    → ruler's palace
        → ordered jewel arrangement

city exterior
    → four recreation grounds
        → adornment of the city
```

The city is characterized through both internal and surrounding relations.
Its beauty is not confined to the palace, and the exterior grounds are not
merely empty scenery: the Bhāṣya identifies them as places of divine activity.

The passage describes a refined domain within the desire realm's cosmography.
Its sensory and political splendor remains part of produced Loka.

## 9. Organon Study

For the Organon, this unit shows how a place receives several simultaneous
determinations without collapsing them:

```text
Vaijayanta: location + ruler + palace function + jewel arrangement
gardens:    exterior position + recreation function + adornment relation
```

One feature belongs to the palace; another describes its arrangement. One
relation places the gardens outside the city; another describes their
contribution to the city's appearance. Retaining these distinctions makes it
possible to describe a whole whose character arises through the ordered
relations of its parts.

This is an Organon reconstruction, not terminology attributed to Vasubandhu.
