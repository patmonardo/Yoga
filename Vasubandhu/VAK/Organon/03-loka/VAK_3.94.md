# VAK_3.94

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> बुद्धत्वमपकर्षे हि शताद्यावत्तदुद्भवः ।
>
> द्वयोः प्रत्येकबुद्धानां खड्गः कल्पशतान्वयः ॥ ३.९४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> buddhatvam apakarṣe hi śatād yāvat tadudbhavaḥ /
>
> dvayoḥ pratyekabuddhānāṃ khaḍgaḥ kalpaśatānvayaḥ // 3.94 //

The Bhāṣya fixes two compressions in the verse: the interval for the arising
of a Buddha extends through the declining phase from below an eighty-thousand-
year human lifespan down to a lifespan of one hundred years; and the
`kalpaśata` associated with the solitary Pratyekabuddha consists specifically
of one hundred **mahākalpas**.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
buddhatvam                → buddhatvam
apakarṣe                  → apakarṣe
hi                        → hi
śatāt                     → śatād (before y-)
yāvat                     → yāvat
tat-udbhavaḥ              → tadudbhavaḥ
dvayoḥ                    → dvayoḥ
pratyeka-buddhānām        → pratyekabuddhānāṃ
khaḍgaḥ                   → khaḍgaḥ
kalpa-śata-anvayaḥ        → kalpaśatānvayaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| buddhatvam | nominative neuter singular | buddhahood; the state whose arising is temporally located |
| apakarṣe | locative masculine singular | during decrease or decline of the human lifespan |
| hi | indeclinable | for, indeed; introduces the determination |
| śatāt | ablative singular of `śata` | from the hundred-year limit; its full directional sense is supplied by the Bhāṣya |
| yāvat | indeclinable limit-correlative | as far as, until; marks the bounded interval |
| tat-udbhavaḥ | genitive tat-compound, nominative masculine singular | the arising of that, namely buddhahood |
| dvayoḥ | locative dual | in both, resolved by the Bhāṣya as increase and decrease |
| pratyeka-buddhānām | genitive plural | of the independently awakened ones |
| khaḍgaḥ | nominative masculine singular | abbreviated designation of the rhinoceros-horn-like solitary type |
| kalpa-śata-anvayaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | preceded or conditioned by a continuity of one hundred kalpas |

The surface `khaḍga`, literally “rhinoceros,” is expanded by the commentary to
`khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpa`, “like a rhinoceros horn.” `Anvaya` retains the force of a
connected preparatory continuity; it does not make the one hundred kalpas a
mere external date.

## 4. Grammar

The first half answers the Bhāṣya's question whether Buddhas arise during
lifespan-increase or lifespan-decrease:

```text
buddhatvam
    apakarṣe hi [bhavati]

śatāt yāvat
    tad-udbhavaḥ [bhavati]
```

> Buddhahood [occurs] during decline; its arising [is possible] throughout
> the interval bounded by the hundred-year lifespan.

The Kārikā states the lower marker elliptically. The Bhāṣya supplies the full
range: after decline has begun below a lifespan of eighty thousand years and
until human lifespan reaches one hundred years. Its prose therefore governs
the readable translation without being inserted into the verse as though
explicitly stated there.

The second half contrasts the temporal scope of Pratyekabuddhas:

```text
pratyekabuddhānām [udbhavaḥ]
    dvayoḥ [utkarṣe apakarṣe ca]

[teṣāṃ]
    khaḍgaḥ
    kalpa-śata-anvayaḥ
```

> Pratyekabuddhas [arise] in both [increase and decline]; the solitary
> rhinoceros-horn-like one has a continuity of one hundred kalpas [behind
> him].

The identification of those kalpas as mahākalpas and of their content as
`bodhisaṃbhāra` are explicit Bhāṣya determinations.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Buddhahood indeed [arises] during decline; its arising extends as far as
> the hundred [year lifespan]. Pratyekabuddhas [arise] in both [phases]; the
> rhinoceros-like solitary one is connected with a hundred kalpas.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A Buddha arises only during the decrease of human lifespan: after the
> lifespan has begun to fall below eighty thousand years and before it falls
> below one hundred. Pratyekabuddhas may arise during either increase or
> decrease. Of their two kinds, the rhinoceros-horn-like solitary type is
> preceded by a preparatory accumulation for awakening extending through one
> hundred mahākalpas.

The precise upper bound, the two Pratyekabuddha types, and the interpretation
of the hundred mahākalpas as preparation for awakening come from the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World-cycle is now determined as a field of practical receptivity.
> Awakened teaching does not appear at an arbitrary temporal station. During
> increase, beings are too difficult to move to urgency concerning suffering;
> below the hundred-year lifespan, the five corruptions become predominant.
> A Buddha can appear and teach within the interval in which suffering has
> become compelling while the capacities required to receive Dharma remain
> operative. Pratyekabuddha awakening has a wider temporal range because it
> does not perform the same world-historical teaching function.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| apakarṣa | lifespan-decrease / decline | phase in which a Buddha may arise |
| utkarṣa | lifespan-increase | Bhāṣya contrast; beings are then difficult to arouse to urgency |
| udbhava | arising | historical appearance of an awakened agent within a World-phase |
| śata | hundred | lower lifespan boundary for Buddha-arising |
| pratyekabuddha | independently awakened one | may arise during both increase and decrease |
| vargacārin | group-going | Bhāṣya's first Pratyekabuddha type |
| khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpa | rhinoceros-horn-like | solitary `ekavihārin` Pratyekabuddha type |
| kalpaśatānvaya | continuity of one hundred kalpas | one hundred mahākalpas of preparatory accumulation |
| bodhisaṃbhāra | equipment / accumulation for awakening | content of the long preparation named by the Bhāṣya |
| duḥsamudveja | difficult to arouse to urgency | excludes Buddha-arising during lifespan-increase |
| kaṣāya | corruption / turbidity | deterioration predominant below the hundred-year lifespan |
| alpotsukatā | little involvement | cultivated disposition explaining limited teaching by Pratyekabuddhas |

The five corruptions named in the Bhāṣya are those of lifespan
(`āyuṣkaṣāya`), the age (`kalpakaṣāya`), afflictions (`kleśakaṣāya`), views
(`dṛṣṭikaṣāya`), and beings (`sattvakaṣāya`). They explain the lower boundary;
they are not words contained in the Kārikā itself.

## 8. Logical Determination

### The bounded field of Buddha-arising

```text
lifespan increase
    → suffering does not readily produce urgency
    → beings are difficult to teach
    → no Buddha-arising

lifespan decline: below 80,000 to 100 years
    → suffering is manifest
    → receptive capacities remain operative
    → Buddha-arising is possible

lifespan below 100 years
    → five corruptions predominate
    → the effective field of instruction has collapsed
    → no Buddha-arising
```

The middle interval is a necessary field-condition, not a sufficient cause of
buddhahood:

```text
ViableWorldPhase(x) → Permits(BuddhaArising, x)

ViableWorldPhase(x) ↛ Produces(Buddhahood, x)
```

Thus 3.94 moves from the quantitative temporal enclosure of 3.93 to the
qualitative determination of what can become historically operative within a
given phase.

### Different awakened functions, different temporal ranges

```text
Samyaksaṃbuddha
    → awakens and establishes effective teaching for others
    → requires a bounded field of receptivity

Pratyekabuddha
    → awakens independently
    → may arise in both increase and decline
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly refuses three defective explanations for the solitary
Pratyekabuddha's limited instruction: incapacity, lack of compassion, and the
absolute unteachability of others. It instead identifies a cultivated
disposition toward little involvement and reluctance to undertake the work of
communicating profound Dharma.

### Preparatory continuity

```text
100 mahākalpas of bodhisaṃbhāra
    → connected preparatory continuity
    → khaḍgaviṣāṇakalpa Pratyekabuddha
```

`Anvaya` makes the temporal measure agential: the enclosure carries a
continuity of cultivation, not merely elapsed cosmic time.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse gives an exact transition from **temporal enclosure** to
**Practical Reason**. The mahākalpa machinery of 3.93 is no longer simply a
measure of cosmic succession. Its phases discriminate which agential and
pedagogical operations can appear.

```text
temporal phase
    → determines the condition of embodied agents
    → determines receptivity to suffering and instruction
    → permits or excludes a world-historical Dharma operation
```

This is why the “Physical World” remains profoundly agential. Loka is not a
neutral stage to which agents are subsequently added. Its temporal state and
the practical powers of its inhabitants are reciprocally determining aspects
of one World-condition.

The earlier Sattvic account of `vijñāna` remains a useful formal comparison:

```text
vijñāna
    → micro-temporal enclosure of differentiated appearing

kalpa-phase
    → macro-temporal enclosure of agential receptivity
```

The comparison concerns enclosing function and scale. The source does not
identify a kalpa-phase with `vijñāna`, nor does it derive Buddhahood from the
phase. Rather, the phase determines when awakened agency can enter appearance
as effective instruction.
