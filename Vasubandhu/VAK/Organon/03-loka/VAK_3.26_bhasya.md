# VAK_3.26 Bhāṣya — Affliction, Karma, Basis, and Inference from the Middle

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kleśās trīṇi dvayaṃ karma sapta vastu phalaṃ tathā /
> phalahetvabhisaṃkṣepo dvayor madhyānumānataḥ // 3.26 //

> Three members are afflictions, two are karma, and seven are bases and
> likewise results. Cause and result are compressed at the two limits because
> they can be inferred from the middle.

The Bhāṣya reclassifies the twelve temporal members by causal function. Five
are causes—three afflictions and two karmic members—while seven are both the
results of prior causes and the bases supporting renewed affliction and karma.

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The e-text's `phābhūtāni` has

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

This twelve-member dependent arising is further to be understood as having
three natures: affliction, karma, and basis.

“Three are afflictions.” The three members whose nature is affliction are
ignorance, craving, and appropriation.

“Two are karma.” The two members whose nature is karma are formations and
becoming.

“Seven are bases.” The seven members whose nature is basis are consciousness,
name-and-form, the six sense spheres, contact, feeling, birth, and aging-and-
death, because they support affliction and karma.

And just as these seven members are bases, “so too they are results.” Those
same seven members have the nature of results. The remaining five have the
nature of causes because their natures are karma and affliction.

Why, then, are result and cause expanded in the middle? Because the basis is
differentiated there fivefold and affliction twofold. In the future time the
result is compressed through a twofold division; in the past time the cause
is compressed because affliction is taught under a single heading.

“Cause and result are compressed at the two limits because they can be
inferred from the middle.” From the middle alone, the expanded cause-and-
result structure of the prior and subsequent limits can also be inferred.
Therefore it is not stated again, so that the same effort need not be
undertaken a second time.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

Vasubandhu speaks throughout, using exegetical questions rather than staging
an independent opponent. The unit performs three operations:

```text
classify the twelve members by functional nature
    → distinguish five causes from seven results
    → explain why the temporal limits are compressed
       and the middle expanded
```

The temporal 2/8/2 arrangement from VAK 3.20 remains intact. The new 3/2/7
arrangement crosses it by asking what causal function each member performs.

## 5. Three Functional Natures

The classification is exact:

```text
affliction — 3
    avidyā
    tṛṣṇā
    upādāna

karma — 2
    saṃskāra
    bhava

basis — 7
    vijñāna
    nāmarūpa
    ṣaḍāyatana
    sparśa
    vedanā
    jāti
    jarāmaraṇa
```

The placement of `bhava` with karma confirms VAK 3.24: becoming here is the
renewal-producing karma accumulated through appropriative activity, not the
future result considered by itself.

`Vastu` should not be rendered “substance.” The Bhāṣya defines its function
through `āśraya`: these seven are the conditioned bases upon which affliction
and karma operate.

## 6. The Same Seven as Basis and Result

The seven `vastu` members receive a double relational determination:

```text
relative to prior affliction and karma
    → result

relative to renewed affliction and karma
    → basis or support
```

Thus result does not remain inert:

```text
prior affliction + karma
    → present result
    → present basis
    → renewed affliction + karma
    → future result
```

The remaining five members are causes because they have affliction or karma
as their functional nature. “Cause,” “result,” and “basis” therefore express
relations within the cycle, not three self-standing kinds of substance.

## 7. Why the Middle Is Expanded

The present middle displays causal structure at higher resolution:

```text
five resultant bases
    vijñāna through vedanā

two afflictions
    tṛṣṇā and upādāna

one karma
    bhava
```

At the future limit, the result is compressed into birth and aging-and-death.
At the past limit, causal affliction is gathered under ignorance alongside
formations as karma. The limits are concise presentations of structures shown
more fully in the middle.

Compression is therefore not loss of causal determination. It presupposes
that the expanded pattern is available elsewhere in the model.

## 8. Inference from the Middle

`Madhyānumāna` makes the present middle epistemically decisive:

```text
analyze the present result
    → infer corresponding prior affliction and karma

analyze present affliction and karma
    → infer corresponding future result
```

The past need not be recovered as personal memory, and the future need not be
perceived as spectacle. Their causal articulation is inferable from the
manifest structure of the present course.

The Bhāṣya also gives an economy principle: once the full pattern has been
shown in the middle, repeating the same expansion at both limits would impose
unnecessary analytic effort.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

VAK 3.26 superimposes two valid configurations upon one continuum:

```text
temporal configuration
    past 2 / present 8 / future 2

functional configuration
    affliction 3 / karma 2 / basis-result 7
```

For the Organon, this is a strong example of multidimensional analysis. A
member's temporal location does not determine its causal role, and its causal
role does not exhaust its relational status. The same seven artifacts can be
typed as results relative to their ground and as supports relative to what
operates upon them next.

This is precisely why an Ocular dataset cannot store one final label as the
thing's essence. It must preserve the relation under which the label is true:

```text
member
    + temporal position
    + causal role
    + support relation
    + analytic resolution
```

The source-grounded practical result is equally important. The hidden limits
become intelligible through the present middle. Rational comprehension need
not invent a permanent traveler or claim visionary access to the past and
future; it reconstructs them from the causal organization presently given.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit at 134.04–134.20. It preserves
the three functional natures, the five-cause/seven-result division, the double
role of the seven as results and supports, the expanded structure of the
middle, and the inference by which the compressed temporal limits are
understood.

The translation remains provisional. The final `punar aparātmakaṃ` phrase,
the exact nuance of `vastu`, and the compressed explanation of the past cause
require comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established
translations during the later learning pass. The Study stops before the
mutual-production formula and does not enter VAK 3.27.
