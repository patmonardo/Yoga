# VAK_3.42

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> छेदसंधानवैराग्यहानिच्युत्युपपत्तयः ।
>
> मनोविज्ञान एवेष्टाः उपेक्षायां च्युतोद्भवौ ॥ ३.४२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> chedasaṃdhāna vairāgyahānicyutyupapattayaḥ /
>
> manovijñāna eveṣṭāḥ upekṣāyāṃ cyutodbhavau // 3.42 //

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| cheda | first member of coordinated nominative list | cutting off; specified as cutting wholesome roots |
| saṃdhāna | second member of list | reconnection; specified as reconnecting wholesome roots |
| vairāgya | third member of list | dispassion from a realm or meditative level |
| hāni | fourth member of list | decline / falling away from an attainment |
| cyuti | fifth member of list | death; falling from the present existence |
| upapatti | sixth member of list | rebirth / arising in another existence |
| manovijñāne | locative singular compound | in mental differentiated cognition alone |
| eva | restrictive particle | only |
| iṣṭāḥ | nominative masculine plural past passive participle | accepted / maintained [to occur] |
| upekṣāyām | locative singular | with equanimity; technically neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling |
| cyutodbhavau | nominative masculine dual | death and arising |

The first line is not a generic sequence of cutting and joining. The Bhāṣya
expands each compact member into a specific doctrinal event.

## 4. Grammar

The first predication assigns six events to one cognitive locus:

```text
cheda-saṃdhāna-vairāgya-hāni-cyuti-upapattayaḥ
    mano-vijñāne eva
    iṣṭāḥ

cutting, reconnection, dispassion, decline, death, and rebirth
    are accepted
    only in mental differentiated cognition
```

The second predication specifies the feeling accompanying two of those events:

```text
cyuti-udbhavau
    upekṣāyām

death and arising
    [occur] with equanimity
```

The Bhāṣya equates `udbhava` here with `upapatti` and determines `upekṣā` as
neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Cutting, reconnection, dispassion, decline, death, and rebirth are accepted
> only in mental differentiated cognition. Death and arising occur with
> equanimity.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The cutting off of wholesome roots, their reconnection, dispassion from a
> realm or level, decline from an attainment, death, and rebirth occur only
> while mental differentiated cognition is operative. Rebirth here includes
> the linking of intermediate existence. Death and arising occur only with
> neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, whose non-acute character is compatible
> with transition. They do not occur while consciousness is concentrated in
> samādhi, nor while mind is absent: when a mindless being's bodily support
> begins its fatal alteration, mind bound to that support must first become
> manifest, and death follows only afterward.

The expansions of the six events, the technical determination of equanimity,
and the exclusions of concentrated and mindless states are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The decisive transformations of an embodied series belong to mental
> differentiated cognition: ethical capacity may be severed or restored, a
> realm may lose its hold, an attainment may decline, and one embodiment may
> be relinquished while another is entered. At death and arising, the intense
> polarities of pleasure and pain subside into neutral feeling. Transition
> requires consciousness to be operative, yet not fixed in concentrated
> station. The World changes embodiments through a determinate mental event,
> not through either maximal cognitive intensity or the mere absence of mind.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Six events, one cognitive locus

```text
cutting wholesome roots
reconnecting wholesome roots
dispassion from realm or level
decline from attainment
death
rebirth
    → occur only in manovijñāna
```

These transformations require the integrative reach of mental differentiated
cognition; they are not assigned to any of the five sensory cognitions.

### Transition requires a non-acute affective station

```text
pleasant feeling     → acute
painful feeling      → acute
neutral feeling      → non-acute

death / rebirth
    → incompatible with acute vijñāna
    → occur with neutral feeling
```

This is a technical compatibility claim, not the ethical claim that all dying
persons possess cultivated equanimity.

### Neither samādhi nor mindlessness is the transition event

```text
concentrated consciousness
    → deliberately established
    → supportive
    → belongs to a different station
    → no death or rebirth in that very state

mindless condition
    → no operative mental locus
    → no death or rebirth in that very state
```

When bodily support begins fatal transformation during mindlessness:

```text
bodily support alters
    → support-bound consciousness manifests
        → death occurs
```

The restoration of consciousness is structurally prior to the death event.

### Embodied transition is support-bound

```text
death-event
    requires
        operative manovijñāna
        bound to the changing bodily support
```

The body and consciousness are neither independent transition-machines nor
one undifferentiated event. Their support relation must become operative at
the boundary.

### The arhat's terminal case

```text
arhat's final extinction
    → indeterminate consciousness
        airyāpathika
        or vipāka-citta
```

The Bhāṣya explains that a weak indeterminate mind is compatible with cutting
the conscious series. This determination belongs to the commentary beyond the
metrical assertion about death and arising generally.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse gives the Client SDK for embodiment transition. The decisive
operation occurs neither in the sensory interfaces nor in an unconscious
shutdown. It occurs in mental differentiated cognition, at a non-acute
affective station, while consciousness is bound to a bodily support undergoing
transition.

```text
current embodiment
    → bodily support begins transformation
        → support-bound manovijñāna becomes operative
            → death transition
                → intermediate linking / renewed arising
```

This strongly confirms the Kośa as a Practical Theory of Consciousness. It
does not merely assert that beings die and are reborn. It discriminates the
cognitive locus, affective condition, concentration-state, and support relation
under which those operations can occur.

The samādhi restriction is philosophically important. Samādhi is not being
devalued; its very stability, deliberate formation, and supportive power make
it unlike the transition station. Within this analysis, one does not die or
take rebirth while consciousness is actually concentrated. If death approaches
during a mindless attainment, consciousness must first return.

This places a precise limit on any simple “Ascension Dhyāna equals death or
rebirth” model. An ascent may transform the Agent's station, but the Kośa
assigns the actual embodiment transition to a different configuration:

```text
manovijñāna
    + neutral feeling
    + non-concentrated operation
    + bodily-support binding
```

The point is textual and can now enter our larger debate without being allowed
to decide every Yoga or Pāli account in advance.

transition, and sensory cognition is not the transition-controller.

The six-event list also reveals the reach of `manovijñāna`. It is the station
where the Agent can lose or restore its ethical roots, withdraw from a realm,
decline from an attainment, and change embodiments. Mental differentiated
cognition is not merely another sensory channel; it governs system-level
reconfiguration.
