# VAK_3.73 Bhāṣya — The Small Thousandfold World-System

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
caturdvīpakacandrārkamerukāmadivaukasām /
brahmalokasahasraṃ ca sāhasraś cūḍiko mataḥ // 3.73 //
```

> A thousand four-continent units, moons, suns, Merus, sensual-domain devas,
> and Brahma-worlds is regarded as the small thousandfold world-system.

Before defining that system, the Bhāṣya records competing estimates for the
extent of the higher residences and dhyāna realms. It then explains the
inventory in the kārikā: each named kind is counted by thousands, including
each of the four continents and a complete series of sensual-domain deva
classes.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Athaiṣāṃ Yāmādivimānānāṃ kiyatpramāṇam?

Caturṇāṃ tāvad yāvat Sumerumūrdhna ity eke. Dviguṇottaram ity apare.

Prathamaṃ tu dhyānaṃ yāvāṃś cāturdvīpakaḥ; dvitīyaṃ yāvān sāhasraś
cūḍiko lokadhātuḥ; tṛtīyaṃ yāvān dvisāhasraḥ; caturthaṃ yāvān
trisāhasra iti eke.

Prathamādīni sāhasrādiparimāṇāni; caturthaṃ tv aparimāṇam ity apare.

Atha ko 'yaṃ sāhasraś cūḍiko lokadhātuḥ, ko dvisāhasras trisāhasro vā?

Caturdvīpakacandrārkamerukāmadivaukasām /
brahmalokasahasraṃ ca sāhasraś cūḍiko mataḥ.

Sahasraṃ Jambūdvīpānāṃ Pūrvavidehānām Aparagodānīyānām Uttarakurūṇām;
sahasraṃ Sūryāṇāṃ Candrāṇāṃ Sumerūṇām; sahasraṃ
Cāturmahārājakāyikānāṃ devānāṃ yāvat Paranirmitavaśavartinām;
sahasraṃ Brahmalokānām. Ayam ucyate sāhasraś cūḍiko lokadhātuḥ.
```

The witness has routine spacing and sandhi irregularities regularized here.
The phrase `caturṇāṃ tāvad yāvat Sumerumūrdhnaḥ` is compressed and its
referent is not explicit in this excerpt; it is translated cautiously below.
The question asks about the middle and great thousandfold systems as well,
but this study stops after the definition of the small system. Their
definitions follow with VAK 3.74.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Now, how large are these residences beginning with those of the Yāmas?

Some say that the four extend as far as the summit of Sumeru. Others say that
they increase by successive doublings.

Some say that the first dhyāna extends as far as one four-continent world;
the second as far as one small thousandfold world-system; the third as far as
one middle, second-order thousandfold system; and the fourth as far as one
great, third-order thousandfold system.

Others say that the first and succeeding dhyānas have measures of the
thousandfold kinds, but the fourth is immeasurable.

Now, what is this small thousandfold world-system? And what are the middle,
second-order and great, third-order systems?

A thousand four-continent units, moons, suns, Merus, and sensual-domain devas,
and a thousand Brahma-worlds, is regarded as the small thousandfold
world-system.

That is: one thousand Jambūdvīpas, one thousand Pūrvavidehas, one thousand
Aparagodānīyas, and one thousand Uttarakurus; one thousand suns, one thousand
moons, and one thousand Sumerus; one thousand sets of devas from the
Cāturmahārājakāyikas through the Paranirmitavaśavartins; and one thousand
Brahma-worlds. This is called the small thousandfold world-system.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voices

The Bhāṣya makes two moves:

1. It notes differing accounts of the extent of higher deva residences and
   dhyāna realms.
2. It defines the `sāhasra-cūḍika lokadhātu` by enumerating what occurs one
   thousand times.

The extent proposals are explicitly attributed: `eke`, “some,” and `apare`,
“others.” The first phrase in that discussion is textually compressed, so
this study does not claim a fully certain reconstruction of its referent.
The definition of the small thousandfold system, by contrast, is directly
enumerated in the prose.

## 5. Competing Measures for Higher Residences

The Bhāṣya preserves alternative spatial schemes:

| Account | Description in the witness |
|---|---|
| First view | The four extend as far as the summit of Sumeru; the referent of “four” is compressed here. |
| Other view | The extent increases by successive doublings. |
| One dhyāna-scale view | First through fourth dhyānas correspond to a four-continent world, small thousandfold, middle thousandfold, and great thousandfold systems. |
| Other dhyāna-scale view | The first and following levels have thousandfold measures, while the fourth is immeasurable. |

These are reported alternatives, not one harmonized calculation. The
Sumeru-based phrase is especially terse; the translation marks its uncertainty
rather than silently supplying a subject.

The verse's explicit definition of a small thousandfold system follows these
estimates and should be kept distinct from them. The alternatives concern the
extent of residences and dhyāna realms; the root verse and its prose
definition specify the membership of the small aggregate.

## 6. The Counted World-Unit

The compound in the kārikā is unpacked by the Bhāṣya into parallel
thousandfold counts:

| Component type | Count |
|---|---:|
| Jambūdvīpas | 1,000 |
| Pūrvavidehas | 1,000 |
| Aparagodānīyas | 1,000 |
| Uttarakurus | 1,000 |
| Suns | 1,000 |
| Moons | 1,000 |
| Sumerus | 1,000 |
| Sets of kāma-devas, from Cāturmahārājika through Paranirmitavaśavartin | 1,000 |
| Brahma-worlds | 1,000 |

The count is parallel across distinct kinds. The Bhāṣya does not describe one
thousand mixed items; it repeats each component-type one thousand times.

## 7. The Deva Series and Brahma-Worlds

`Kāmadivaukasām` is clarified through the endpoints of the deva series: from
the Cāturmahārājikas up to the Paranirmitavaśavartins. Each repeated unit
therefore includes the complete sequence of six sensual-domain deva classes
described earlier.

The prose separately counts one thousand Brahma-worlds. It does not collapse
these into the sensual deva series; both categories occur in the inventory as
distinct components of each repeated cosmological schema.

## 8. Meaning of “Small Thousandfold”

The Bhāṣya identifies `sāhasra-cūḍika lokadhātu` as a world-system in which
the enumerated component-types each occur one thousand times. The name marks
this as the first of the larger thousandfold scales discussed in this
sequence.

The opening estimate of dhyāna extents already uses “small,” “middle,” and
“great” thousandfold systems as measures. This verse defines only the small
one. The recursive construction of the middle and great systems belongs to
the following verse.

## 9. Doctrinal and Organon Study

The local cosmology now serves as a repeated unit within a larger world-system.
The scale change preserves kinds: continents remain distinct from celestial
bodies, axial mountains, sensual deva sets, and Brahma-worlds, although each
is multiplied by the same count.

```text
one local cosmological schema
    → each component-type repeated 1,000 times
        → small thousandfold world-system
```

For the Organon, this is a model of structured aggregation: shared multiplicity
does not erase the internal type distinctions of the repeated unit. This is a
project-level reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's terminology.
