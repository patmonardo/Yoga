# VAK_3.21 Bhāṣya — Four State-Designations of the Continuum

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> pūrvakleśadaśāvidyā saṃskārāḥ pūrvakarmaṇaḥ /
> saṃdhiskandhās tu vijñānaṃ nāmarūmam ataḥ param // 3.21 //

> The former afflictive state is ignorance; formations are the state of
> former karma. The aggregates at the junction are consciousness; after this
> comes name-and-form.

The Bhāṣya begins defining the individual members of dependent arising as
temporally determinate states of the aggregate-continuum. Its first decisive
correction is grammatical: `daśā` means “state,” not the numeral “ten.”

This verse-unit runs from printed 131.19 through 132.01 in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasaya.txt`. The next pāda, `prāk
ṣaḍāyatanotpādāt`, supplies the ending boundary of the name-and-form stage and
belongs to VAK 3.22. It has not been absorbed into the present Study.

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The first pāda has been joined
as `pūrvakleśadaśāvidyā` to display the verse construction, while the prose
establishes its analysis as `pūrvakleśadaśā avidyā`. The e-text's `mātuḥ
kukṣī` has provisionally been read as locative `mātuḥ kukṣau`. The running
source remains unchanged.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What, then, are these members beginning with ignorance?

“The former afflictive state is ignorance.” The state of the afflictions in a
former birth is here called ignorance. This is because ignorance accompanies
the other afflictions and because they become active under its influence. It
is like the accepted implication that, when one says “the king comes,” his
retinue also comes.

“Formations are the state of former karma.” The word “state” continues to be
understood. The state of karma—meritorious and otherwise—in a former birth,
whose maturation occurs here in the present birth, is here called formations.

“The aggregates at the junction are consciousness.” At the moment of
rebirth-linking in the mother's womb, the five aggregates are called
consciousness.

“After this comes name-and-form.”

The present verse announces the post-junction stage but does not yet state its
terminal boundary. That definition continues with the first words of VAK
3.22.

## 4. Movement and Voice of the Commentary

Vasubandhu asks what the twelve members actually designate and answers through
four successive states:

```text
former afflictive state
    → called ignorance

former karmic state whose result matures now
    → called formations

five aggregates at the rebirth-junction
    → called consciousness

stage after the junction
    → called name-and-form
```

No independent opponent appears. The royal-retinue comparison explains how a
predominant member can name a broader associated state.

## 5. `Daśā`: State, Not Ten

The first pāda must not be segmented with `daśa`, “ten.” The commentary
explicitly glosses the phrase through `kleśāvasthā`, “afflictive state,” and
then says `daśeti vartate`: the word “state” continues into the definition of
formations.

The parallel structure is therefore:

```text
pūrvakleśa-daśā
    former afflictive state
    → avidyā

pūrvakarma-daśā
    former karmic state
    → saṃskāra
```

This is a substantive philological control. A numerical reading would destroy
both the prose explanation and the parallel syntax of the two members.

## 6. Designation Through Predominance and Association

Why is the whole former afflictive state called “ignorance”? The Bhāṣya gives
two related reasons: other afflictions accompany ignorance, and they become
active under its influence. The royal analogy supplies the semantic form:

```text
“the king comes”
    includes the accompanying retinue

“ignorance”
    includes the associated afflictive state governed by it
```

The designation does not claim that ignorance is numerically identical with
every affliction. A principal determination names the concrete state in which
it governs and is accompanied by the others.

The same state-based method applies to formations. `Saṃskāra` names the former
karmic condition—meritorious and otherwise—specifically insofar as its result
matures in the present life.

## 7. Consciousness at the Rebirth-Junction

The prose prevents `vijñāna` from being isolated as a substance traveling by
itself. At the `pratisaṃdhikṣaṇa`, all five aggregates are present, and the
whole junction-state is called consciousness:

```text
moment of rebirth-linking in the womb
    → five aggregates
    → designated vijñāna
```

This accords with VAK 3.18: causal continuity does not require an independent
transmigrator. `Vijñāna` supplies the predominant designation of the junction,
not a replacement ātman concealed inside the aggregate-series.

In the conventional translation, `vijñāna` is rendered “consciousness.” In
the controlled Organon vocabulary it may be specified as differentiated
cognition, but it must remain distinct from `jñāna`, achieved knowing.

## 8. Name-and-Form: An Open Boundary

`Nāmarūmam ataḥ param` establishes succession: name-and-form is the state
after the rebirth-junction. The present verse does not say how long that state
lasts. Its terminating condition appears only in VAK 3.22:

```text
rebirth-junction designated consciousness
    → thereafter, name-and-form
    → terminal boundary supplied by the next verse
```

Preserving this open boundary matters. Supplying the six-sphere endpoint here
would be Bhāṣya-informed and correct in the larger argument, but it would
silently import the next kārikā's wording into the present verse.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The twelvefold series is beginning to reveal its semantic operation. It does
not merely list twelve small entities. A temporally extended aggregate-state
is selected and named according to the determination governing its function
within the causal continuum.

For the Organon, this is a restrained example of labeling and typing:

```text
source range
    concrete aggregate-state

governing feature
    affliction / karma / junction-consciousness

assigned type
    ignorance / formations / consciousness
```

The labels are projections only in the disciplined sense that analysis
selects and types a range. They are not arbitrary overlays: the Bhāṣya gives
the causal and associational grounds for each assignment. Nor does a label
erase the other constituents of the state; “consciousness,” for example,
names a junction containing all five aggregates.

This gives an important constraint for an eventual Ocular dataset. A source
artifact may bear a predominant analytic designation without being reducible
to that one feature. Range, governing determination, temporal boundary, and
associated constituents must remain separately recoverable.

## 10. Review Status

This Study covers VAK 3.21 from 131.19 through 132.01. It preserves the
`daśā` correction, the royal-retinue account of designation, the former
karmic state whose result matures in the present, and the five-aggregate
character of consciousness at rebirth-linking.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The locative at 131.27 and
the precise grammatical formation of the first pāda require comparison with
critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations during the
later learning pass. The definition of name-and-form remains deliberately
open because its terminal boundary begins VAK 3.22; that verse was not
started here.
