# VAK_3.70 Bhāṣya — Divine Birth and Initial Form

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
pañcavarṣopamo yāvad daśavarṣopamaḥ śiśuḥ /
saṃbhavaty eṣu saṃpūrṇāḥ savastrāś caiva rūpiṇaḥ // 3.70 //
```

> Among these, a child resembling one of five years up through one resembling
> one of ten years arises. The form-realm devas arise complete and clothed.

The Bhāṣya distributes the five-to-ten-year range across the six kāma-deva
classes, says that these children mature rapidly, and then shifts subjects:
the complete and clothed beings are rūpāvacara devas. It adds that all devas
speak the ārya language.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Kiyatpramāṇo jāyate?

Pañcavarṣopamo yāvad daśavarṣopamaḥ śiśuḥ /
saṃbhavaty eṣu.

Yathāsaṃkhyaṃ ṣaṭsu devanikāyeṣu. Te tu kṣipram evābhivardhante.

Saṃpūrṇāḥ savastrāś caiva rūpiṇaḥ.

Rūpāvacarā devāḥ saṃpūrṇakāyā vastreṇa saṃvītā upapadyante.

Sarve devā āryabhāṣābhāṣiṇaḥ.
```

The opening question continues the prior discussion of divine children. The
witness is clear apart from spacing and sandhi, regularized here. The
commentary explicitly supplies `yathāsaṃkhyam`, “respectively,” and identifies
`rūpiṇaḥ` as rūpāvacara devas.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

How large are they when they are born?

Among these, a child resembling one of five years up through one resembling
one of ten years arises. This occurs respectively in the six deva classes.
They, however, mature very quickly.

The form-realm devas arise complete and clothed: they are born with complete
bodies, enveloped in clothing.

All devas speak the ārya language.

## 4. Commentary Movement and Subject Shift

The commentary answers the question of a divine child's size, clarifies how
the range applies, then turns to another class of beings:

1. The newborn's apparent age ranges from five to ten years.
2. `Yathāsaṃkhyam` distributes those ages in order across the six classes.
3. The children grow rapidly after arising.
4. The second half describes rūpāvacara devas as complete-bodied and clothed
   at rebirth.
5. The commentary adds that all devas speak the ārya language.

The crucial grammatical determination is the subject shift. The first half
concerns children among the six kāmāvacara classes; the second concerns
rūpāvacara devas. The second predicate is not an additional description of
the first population.

## 5. The Sixfold Age Distribution

The Bhāṣya's `yathāsaṃkhyam` yields this ordered correspondence, using the
class sequence established in VAK 3.69:

| Class | Apparent age at arising |
|---|---:|
| Cāturmahārājakāyika | 5 years |
| Trāyastriṃśa | 6 years |
| Yāma | 7 years |
| Tuṣita | 8 years |
| Nirmāṇarati | 9 years |
| Paranirmitavaśavartin | 10 years |

The kārikā states the endpoints; the prose makes the sixfold application
explicit. `Upama`, “resembling,” marks these as comparisons to human ages,
not a claim that divine childhood follows human biological development in
every respect.

## 6. Rapid Maturation

The Bhāṣya adds `kṣipram evābhivardhante`: the children mature very quickly.
This separates two temporal determinations:

```text
state at arising → class-indexed apparent age
subsequent course → rapid maturation
```

The starting appearance and the rate of later development are not the same
feature. Nor does the commentary give a numerical duration for this rapid
growth.

## 7. Rūpāvacara Birth

The phrase `saṃpūrṇāḥ savastrāś caiva rūpiṇaḥ` is explained as referring to
the rūpāvacara devas. They `upapadyante`—arise through rebirth—with complete
bodies and enveloped in clothing.

```text
six kāma-deva classes:
    child-form, graded by class
    rapid maturation

rūpāvacara devas:
    complete body at arising
    clothing present at arising
```

These are distinct descriptions of initial embodiment in different domains.
The Bhāṣya does not treat bodily completeness as release from conditioned
arising; it specifies the form in which these devas arise.

## 8. Language of the Devas

The final sentence states that all devas are speakers of the ārya language.
It is a general linguistic determination added by the Bhāṣya; the verse
itself concerns age, bodily completeness, and clothing.

The statement ranges across the deva classes just discussed, despite their
different modes of birth and habitation. The source gives no further
linguistic description here, so “ārya language” is retained without
identifying it with a specific historical human language.

## 9. Doctrinal and Organon Study

The main doctrinal distinction is between destination-class and initial
embodiment:

```text
class of arising
    → initial bodily presentation
        → later developmental course, where specified
```

For the six kāma-deva classes, class determines the apparent age of the
newborn, followed by rapid growth. For rūpāvacara devas, the commentary
describes a complete body and clothing at arising. The shared ārya-language
statement adds a common feature across those differences.

For the Organon, this is a source-grounded example of why a population label,
initial state, and subsequent process should be represented separately. This
is a project-level reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's technical vocabulary.
