# VAK_3.82

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कामदेवायुषा तुल्या अहोरात्रा यथाक्रमम् ।
>
> संजीवादिषु षट्स्वायुस्तैस्तेषां कामदेववत् ॥ ३.८२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kāmadevāyuṣā tulyā ahorātrā yathākramam /
>
> saṃjīvādiṣu ṣaṭsv āyus tais teṣāṃ kāmadevavat // 3.82 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
kāma-deva-āyuṣā      → kāmadevāyuṣā
tulyāḥ                → tulyā (before a-)
ahas-rātrāḥ           → ahorātrāḥ → ahorātrā (before y-)
yathā-kramam          → yathākramam
saṃjīva-ādiṣu        → saṃjīvādiṣu
ṣaṭsu                 → ṣaṭsv (before ā-)
āyuḥ                  → āyus (before t-)
taiḥ                  → tais (before t-)
teṣām                 → teṣāṃ
kāma-deva-vat         → kāmadevavat
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| kāmadevāyuṣā | instrumental neuter singular compound | with the lifespan of a desire-realm deva class |
| tulyāḥ | nominative masculine plural | equal or corresponding in temporal magnitude |
| ahorātrāḥ | nominative masculine plural | day-and-night units of the hells |
| yathākramam | indeclinable | in the corresponding order, one-to-one |
| saṃjīvādiṣu | locative masculine plural | in Saṃjīva and those following it |
| ṣaṭsu | locative masculine plural | in the six hot hells under discussion |
| āyuḥ | nominative neuter singular | lifespan |
| taiḥ | instrumental masculine plural | by those day-and-night units |
| teṣām | genitive masculine plural | of the beings in those six hells |
| kāmadevavat | adverbial `-vat` formation | like the desire-realm devas with respect to numerical year-count |

The two apparent comparisons have different functions. `tulya` equates a
deva lifespan with a hell day-night; `-vat` transfers the corresponding deva
lifespan's numerical year-count into the hell calendar.

## 4. Grammar

The first construction establishes six ordered unit conversions:

```text
saṃjīva-ādiṣu ṣaṭsu
    ahorātrāḥ
    kāma-deva-āyuṣā tulyāḥ
    yathā-kramam
```

> In the six hells beginning with Saṃjīva, the day-and-nights correspond in
> order to the lifespans of the desire-realm devas.

The instrumental singular `kāmadevāyuṣā` is distributive under
`yathākramam`: each hell day-night corresponds to the lifespan of its matched
deva class, not to one undifferentiated aggregate of all deva lifespans.

The second construction establishes the total lifespan within each resulting
hell calendar:

```text
teṣām āyuḥ
    taiḥ [ahorātraiḥ]
    kāma-deva-vat [bhavati]
```

> Reckoned by those day-and-nights, their lifespan is like that of the
> corresponding desire-realm devas.

The Bhāṣya restricts the likeness to a precise feature: the number of local
years. It does not identify the actual duration of a deva lifespan with the
total duration of the corresponding hell lifespan.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> In the six hells beginning with Saṃjīva, the day-and-nights are respectively
> equal to the lifespans of the desire-realm devas; reckoned by those units,
> the lifespans of their inhabitants are like those of the desire-realm devas.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> For each of the first six hot hells, the entire lifespan of the corresponding
> desire-realm deva class constitutes one hell day-and-night. Using that
> enlarged day-night to construct the local calendar, the hell lifespan has
> the same numerical number of years as the matched deva lifespan: 500 years
> in Saṃjīva, 1,000 in Kālasūtra, 2,000 in Saṃghāta, 4,000 in Raurava, 8,000 in
> Mahāraurava, and 16,000 in Tāpana.

The names and ordering of the six hells, the exact pairings, and the
restriction of `kāmadevavat` to numerical year-count are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The temporal system of one destiny becomes the unit-constructor of another.
> A complete divine lifespan is compressed into a single hell day-and-night;
> that newly constituted unit is then expanded into a calendar whose lifespan
> repeats the divine numerical sequence. The World System preserves an ordered
> correspondence while transforming the magnitude carried by every count.
> Temporal equality is therefore typed and relational, never the assertion of
> one homogeneous cosmic clock.

with its own temporal units.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Ordered cross-realm mapping

Let the two sequences be aligned by ordinal position:

| n | Source deva class | Target hell |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Cāturmahārājakāyika | Saṃjīva |
| 2 | Trāyastriṃśa | Kālasūtra |
| 3 | Yāma | Saṃghāta |
| 4 | Tuṣita | Raurava |
| 5 | Nirmāṇarati | Mahāraurava |
| 6 | Paranirmitavaśavartin | Tāpana |

`yathākramam` licenses:

```text
pair(n) = <KāmaDevaClass(n), HotHell(n)>
```

It does not permit rearranging the pairings by numerical resemblance or name.

### Two-stage transformation

For each matched pair, let `Dₙ` be the complete deva lifespan and `Nₙ` its
numerical magnitude in divine years.

Stage one constructs the hell day-night:

```text
1 HellDayNight(n) = Dₙ
```

Stage two constructs the hell lifespan:

```text
HellLifespan(n) = Nₙ hell-years
```

where one hell-year retains the established calendar form:

```text
1 hell-year = 12 months × 30 HellDayNight(n)
```

Thus the source lifespan contributes twice but in different semantic roles:

```text
its full duration → target day-night magnitude
its numeric count → target lifespan-year count
```

### Resulting year-count series

| Hell | One hell day-night equals | Hell lifespan |
|---|---|---:|
| Saṃjīva | Cāturmahārājakāyika lifespan | 500 hell-years |
| Kālasūtra | Trāyastriṃśa lifespan | 1,000 hell-years |
| Saṃghāta | Yāma lifespan | 2,000 hell-years |
| Raurava | Tuṣita lifespan | 4,000 hell-years |
| Mahāraurava | Nirmāṇarati lifespan | 8,000 hell-years |
| Tāpana | Paranirmitavaśavartin lifespan | 16,000 hell-years |

The year-count doubles across the series, but the total duration grows more
rapidly because the day-night serving as its unit also grows with the matched
deva lifespan.

### Typed equality

The relation asserted by `tulya` is:

```text
duration(DevaLifespan(n))
    = duration(HellDayNight(n))
```

It is not:

```text
DevaLifespan(n) = HellLifespan(n)
```

The distinction between equal duration and identical temporal role is
essential to the verse's logic.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.82 reveals that Loka is not simply a hierarchy of isolated clocks. Its
temporal regimes can be composed: the output duration of one world-domain
becomes the primitive unit of another.

```text
deva lifespan
    → recast as one hell day-night
        → composed into hell months and years
            → multiplied by the inherited numerical year-count
                → hell lifespan
```

This is the temporal analogue of an interface transformation. The numeric
sequence `500, 1000, 2000, ...` survives the crossing, but its unit changes so
radically that preserving the number alone would conceal the determination.
Syntax recognizes the repeated figures; semantics tracks what each figure is
counting.

The copular equality is likewise exact without being an identity. One deva
lifespan and one hell day-night possess equal duration, but they occupy
different roles inside different calendars. Their relation determines a
conversion while preserving realm, unit-kind, and use. This gives the Kośa a
sophisticated cross-domain account of duration:

```text
same magnitude
    + different temporal role
    + ordered realm correspondence
    = valid conversion
```

```ts
interface RealmDuration {
  realm: string;
  unitKind: "DayNight" | "Year" | "Lifespan";
  magnitude: number;
}

interface CrossRealmClockConstruction {
  source: RealmDuration & { unitKind: "Lifespan" };
  target: RealmDuration & { unitKind: "DayNight" };
  preservesDuration: true;
  preservesTemporalRole: false;
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It prevents
the equality of magnitudes from collapsing into identity of realm-time or
calendar function.
