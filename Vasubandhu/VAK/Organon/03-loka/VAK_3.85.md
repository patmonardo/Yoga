# VAK_3.85

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कुरुबाह्योऽन्तरामृत्युः परमाण्वक्षरक्षणाः ।
>
> रूपनामाध्वपर्यन्ताः परमाणुरणुस्तथा ॥ ३.८५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kurubāhyo 'ntarāmṛtyuḥ paramāṇv-akṣara-kṣaṇāḥ /
>
> rūpa-nāmādhva-paryantāḥ paramāṇur aṇus tathā // 3.85 //

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |

| akṣaram | nominative neuter singular, represented in the plural coordination | letter or syllabic unit; limit of a name-expression |

The coordinated compounds encode a respective mapping rather than three
undifferentiated minima: `paramāṇu → rūpa`, `akṣara → nāma`, and `kṣaṇa →
adhvan`.

## 4. Grammar

The opening statement qualifies the lifespan tables just completed:

```text
kuru-bāhyaḥ
    antarā-mṛtyuḥ [asti]
```

> Outside Uttarakuru, death before completion of the lifespan can occur.

The Bhāṣya supplies the modality. Uttarakuru beings necessarily complete their
fixed lifespan. Elsewhere completion is not universally necessary; this does
not mean that every being outside Uttarakuru dies prematurely.

The middle construction is a three-place respective predication:

```text
paramāṇu-akṣara-kṣaṇāḥ
    rūpa-nāma-adhva-paryantāḥ [yathākramam]
```

> The ultimate atom, the letter, and the moment are respectively the limits
> of form, name, and time.

The unexpressed `yathākramam` is supplied by the ordered parallelism and
confirmed by the Bhāṣya's separate definitions.

The closing words begin a new ascending scale:

```text
paramāṇuḥ, aṇuḥ, tathā [rajor...]
```

> The ultimate atom, the aṇu, and likewise [the subsequent measures].

This is a forward hinge, not a complete equation between `paramāṇu` and
`aṇu`. Their numerical relation is supplied in 3.86.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Outside Uttarakuru there is death before completion of the lifespan. The
> ultimate atom, the letter, and the moment are respectively the limits of
> form, name, and time. The ultimate atom, the aṇu, and likewise [form an
> ascending series].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Beings in Uttarakuru necessarily live out their fixed lifespan; outside it,
> death before the allotted term is possible, though special classes are
> exempt. At the opposite end of measurement, the ultimate atom is the limit
> reached by diminishing form, the letter or syllabic unit is the limit of a
> name-expression, and the moment is the limit of time. A moment is
> operationally determined as the minimal duration in which, given complete
> conditions, a dharma obtains its occurrence, or in which a moving dharma
> traverses the interval from one ultimate atom to the next. The final words
> begin the ascending material scale continued in 3.86.

The universal Uttarakuru guarantee, special exemptions elsewhere, operational
definitions of a moment, and attribution of sixty-five moments per finger-snap
to the Abhidharmikas are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A stated lifespan and an actually completed life are different temporal
> determinations. The World System may allocate a duration while still
> permitting an interrupting event; only under specified conditions does the
> allocation become guaranteed completion. The analysis then turns from the
> greatest durations to the smallest operational units. Form terminates in an
> atomic limit, naming in an articulable limit, and temporal course in the
> minimal occurrence or traversal of a conditioned process.

| rūpa | form | measurable domain whose diminution terminates in the ultimate atom |

Here `nāma` is narrowly demonstrated through a linguistic example. This unit
does not by itself establish that every non-form determination is reducible to
an `akṣara`.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Allocated lifespan versus completed lifespan

Let `A(x)` be the lifespan allocated to a being and `C(x)` the duration it
actually completes:

```text
ordinary possibility:
    C(x) ≤ A(x)

premature death:
    C(x) < A(x)

guaranteed completion:
    C(x) = A(x)
```

The domain rules are:

```text
x in Uttarakuru
    → necessarily C(x) = A(x)

x outside Uttarakuru
    → possibly C(x) < A(x)
```

The second implication is permissive, not universal. The Bhāṣya lists special
classes outside Uttarakuru for whom premature death does not occur. The exact
transmitted list is textually dense, but its logical function is clear:

```text
outside Uttarakuru
    + exempt class
    → guaranteed completion
```

### Respective minima

The verse defines a typed map:

| Measured domain | Limiting unit | Operation reaching the limit |
|---|---|---|
| form | ultimate atom | continued diminution |
| name-expression | akṣara | minimal articulation |
| time | moment | minimal conditioned occurrence or traversal |

These units are minima within different domains, not mutually convertible
objects.

### Operational definitions of a moment

The Bhāṣya gives two criteria:

```text
Occurrence criterion:
    complete conditions
        → dharma obtains its own occurrence
        → elapsed duration = 1 moment

Traversal criterion:
    moving dharma traverses
        one ultimate-atom interval
        → elapsed duration = 1 moment
```

The first is event-based; the second couples minimal time with minimal spatial
distance through motion.

The Abhidharmika comparison adds:

```text
1 finger-snap by a strong person = 65 moments
```

This count is explicitly attributed to the Abhidharmikas. It should not be
presented as though separately asserted in the kārikā.

### Scale hinge

```text
3.78–3.84:
    macro-temporal measures of lives and realms

3.85a:
    possible interruption of those measures

3.85b:
    minima required to ground measurement

3.85d → 3.86:
    construction upward from the material minimum
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.85 gives the measurement system a genuine event semantics. A lifespan
table states an available or fixed term; death is an event that may terminate
the process before that term. The two must not be stored as one fact:

```text
lifespan specification
    → defines expected or available duration

death event
    → determines actual termination

realm and class constraints
    → determine whether early termination is permitted
```

This is directly preparatory for an agential account of karma without
prematurely importing that later chapter. An embodied agent has a temporal
allocation, an actual execution duration, and event constraints that may or
may not permit interruption.

The second half supplies the complementary analytic requirement. A practical
measurement system needs terminal granularity. Its three minima should not be
flattened merely because the syntax coordinates them:

```text
paramāṇu
    → material granularity

akṣara
    → articulatory granularity

kṣaṇa
    → event granularity
```

The definition of `kṣaṇa` is especially important. Time is made operational
through what a conditioned dharma does: it becomes occurrent or traverses a
minimal interval. The moment is not introduced here as an independent
container into which the event is dropped. Event and duration reciprocally
determine one another: the minimal process specifies the moment, and the
moment specifies the temporal resolution at which that process is registered.

```ts
type CompletionPolicy =
  | { mode: "Guaranteed" }
  | { mode: "Interruptible"; exemptions: string[] };

interface LifespanExecution {
  allocatedDuration: bigint;
  completedDuration: bigint;
  policy: CompletionPolicy;
}

type AnalyticMinimum =
  | { domain: "Form"; unit: "Paramanu" }
  | { domain: "NameExpression"; unit: "Aksara" }
  | { domain: "TemporalCourse"; unit: "Ksana" };
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It keeps
modal lifespan policy distinct from actual termination and preserves the type
of each analytic minimum.
