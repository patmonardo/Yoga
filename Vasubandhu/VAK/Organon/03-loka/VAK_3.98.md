# VAK_3.98

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> आलस्यात्संनिधिं कृत्वा साग्रहैः क्षेत्रपो भृतः ।
>
> ततः कर्मपथाधिक्यादपहरासे दशायुषः ॥ ३.९८ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> ālasyāt saṃnidhiṃ kṛtvā sāgrahaiḥ kṣetrapo bhṛtaḥ /
>
> tataḥ karmapathādhikyād apahrāse daśāyuṣaḥ // 3.98 //

The Kārikā compresses the origin of storage, property, theft, political
authority, and lifespan decline into one sequence. The Bhāṣya is required to
recover its intermediate links and to determine `sāgrahaiḥ` as possessive
appropriation rather than a generic act of grasping.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ālasyāt                     → ālasyāt
saṃnidhim                   → saṃnidhiṃ (before k-)
kṛtvā                       → kṛtvā
sa-āgrahaiḥ                 → sāgrahaiḥ
kṣetra-paḥ                  → kṣetrapo (before bh-)
bhṛtaḥ                      → bhṛtaḥ
tataḥ                       → tataḥ
karma-patha-ādhikyāt        → karmapathādhikyād (before vowel)
apahrāse                    → apahrāse
daśa-āyuṣaḥ                → daśāyuṣaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| ālasyāt | ablative neuter singular | because of laziness; initiating condition of storage |
| saṃnidhim | accusative masculine singular | a store, stockpile, or reserved accumulation |
| kṛtvā | absolutive of √kṛ | having made |
| sāgrahaiḥ | instrumental plural | with possessive appropriations or claims |
| kṣetrapaḥ | nominative masculine singular | protector or guardian of the fields |
| bhṛtaḥ | past passive participle, nominative masculine singular | maintained, hired, or materially supported |
| tataḥ | adverb | thereafter, from that sequence |
| karmapathādhikyāt | ablative neuter singular compound | because of the increase or predominance of courses of action |
| apahrāse | locative masculine singular | during decrease or decline |
| daśāyuṣaḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | persons whose lifespan is ten years |

The Bhāṣya specifies that the relevant `karmapathas` are the worsening courses
of conduct exemplified by theft, armed punishment, and false speech. The bare
compound should therefore not be translated as though ethically neutral
activity in general shortened lifespan.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first line condenses two stages linked by the absolutive:

```text
ālasyāt
    saṃnidhiṃ kṛtvā

sa-āgrahaiḥ
    kṣetra-paḥ bhṛtaḥ
```

> Because of laziness, having made a store, [beings developed] possessive
> claims; a protector of fields was maintained.

`Sāgrahaiḥ` is syntactically compressed. The Bhāṣya unfolds it as:

```text
storage
    → mamakāra, making something “mine”
        → division of fields
            → āgraha over one's portion
                → taking another's portion
```

The instrumental plural refers to the field-owning community whose
appropriative relations occasion the appointment of the protector. `Bhṛtaḥ`
is concrete: they support him with a sixth share.

The second line gives the ensuing decline:

```text
tataḥ
    karma-patha-ādhikyāt
    apahrāse [sati]
    daśa-āyuṣaḥ [manuṣyāḥ bhavanti]
```

> Thereafter, because the unwholesome courses of action increase, during the
> decline there come to be humans whose lifespan is ten years.

The lifespan subject and final verb are supplied by the Bhāṣya. `Daśāyuṣaḥ`
designates the humans produced at the endpoint; it is not simply an abstract
measure called “ten-year lifespan.”

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Because of laziness, having made a store, [beings entered] possessive
> appropriation, and a protector of fields was maintained. Thereafter, from
> the increase of [unwholesome] courses of action, humans in the decline come
> to have a lifespan of ten years.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A lazy being began storing naturally arising rice, and others imitated him.
> Storage produced the making of things as “mine”; fields were divided,
> possessive claims were established, and theft began. To restrain theft, the
> community selected a distinguished person to guard the fields and supported
> him with one sixth of the produce. From this office arose the designations
> `kṣatriya` and Mahāsammata King and the succession of royal authority.
> Greed, failed distribution, theft, punishment, and false speech then
> multiplied the unwholesome courses of action, until human lifespan declined
> to ten years.

The imitation sequence, `mamakāra`, sixth-share support, royal designations,
and named forms of ethical decline are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The differentiated social World arises from transformations in resource
> practice. Deferred consumption creates storage; storage creates enduring
> possession; possession divides a common field into mine and another's;
> exclusion creates theft; and theft generates the collective requirement for
> an agent authorized to restrain it. Political authority is therefore neither
> primordial nor externally inserted. It is a derivative control-function
> maintained by the community inside a World already reorganized by
> appropriation. Because the ruler remains an agent within that same field,
> authority can itself be captured by greed and amplify the disorder it was
> instituted to regulate.

| rājaparaṃparā | succession of kings | institutional continuation of royal authority |

The derivations of `kṣatriya` and Mahāsammata are part of Vasubandhu's local
genealogy. They should not be substituted for the Kārikā's narrower
`kṣetrapa` in the close translation.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Genesis of property

```text
immediate taking according to need
    → laziness
        → storage
            → imitation of storage
                → mamakāra
                    → division of fields
                        → exclusive claim
                            → theft
```

Storage is not yet theft. It changes the temporal form of resource relation:

```text
consume-now
    → resource relation ends with present need

store-for-later
    → resource relation persists through time
        → persistent relation becomes possessive identity: “mine”
```

The property relation is thus a temporal enclosure laid over a portion of the
resource field.

### Genesis of public authority

```text
private possessive claims
    → boundary violation and theft
        → shared need for restraint
            → collective selection of field-protector
                → payment of one-sixth share
                    → kṣatriya / Mahāsammata King
                        → royal succession
```

Formally:

```text
CommunityAuthorizes(agent, ProtectFields)
    ∧ CommunitySupports(agent, OneSixthShare)
        → PublicOffice(agent, FieldProtector)
```

The protector is not simply the strongest private claimant. His office is
generated by collective agreement and sustained through a publicly assigned
share.

### Regulatory feedback and institutional failure

```text
authority restrains theft
    → intended regulatory operation

greed enters authority
    → distribution fails
        → theft proliferates
            → armed punishment
                → false speech
                    → unwholesome karmapathas increase
                        → lifespan decreases to ten years
```

The regulator belongs to the regulated system and can reproduce its defect.
This makes political order a feedback structure rather than a final escape
from the genealogy.

### Two root conditions

The Bhāṣya closes by naming:

```text
rasarāga
    → root of gross nourishment and embodied decline

ālasya
    → root of storage and social-political decline

together
    → mūlayoni of the entire anarthaugha
```

These are roots inside the conditioned sequence, not an ultimate genetic
Principle beyond produced Loka.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.98 extends the Agent–World feedback of 3.97 into institutional form.
The critical transformation is not merely “people become selfish.” A new data
structure of the Human World appears:

```text
resource
    + persistent temporal reservation
    + agent-indexed claim
    + spatial boundary
        → property
```

Once this structure exists, theft becomes logically possible. Before
agent-indexed boundaries, one may consume excessively, but one cannot yet
take **another's** field. The transgression and the institution regulating it
are both generated by the same prior differentiation.

This also clarifies the practical character of Loka. The World-system now
contains not only spatial and temporal enclosures but normative ones:

```text
field boundary
    → determines mine / another's

public office
    → determines permitted / prohibited intervention

karmapatha
    → determines the practical consequence of repeated conduct
```

The Mahāsammata construction is therefore a Semantic Copula between community
and ruler. The community determines the agent as public protector through
selection and support; the protector reciprocally determines the multitude as
a politically ordered field. Neither term has that political meaning in
isolation.

Yet the copula remains fragile. When greed captures the distributive function,
the institution ceases to constrain the original defect and becomes one of
its transmission channels. That is the methodical bridge toward
Karmanirdeśa: a World persists and declines through repeated agential
operations, not through inert scenery.
