# VAK_3.39

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कवडीकार आहारः कामे त्र्यायतनात्मकः ।
>
> न रूपायतनं तेन स्वाक्षमुक्ताननुग्रहात् ॥ ३.३९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kavaḍīkāra āhāraḥ kāme tryāyatanātmakaḥ /
>
> na rūpāyatanaṃ tena svākṣamuktānanugrahāt // 3.39 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kavaḍīkāraḥ                 → kavaḍīkāra
āhāraḥ                     → āhāraḥ
kāme                       → kāme
tri-āyatana-ātmakaḥ        → tryāyatanātmakaḥ
na                         → na
rūpa-āyatanam              → rūpāyatanam
tena                       → tena
sva-akṣa-mukta-an-anugrahāt → svākṣamuktānanugrahāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| kavaḍīkāra | nominative masculine singular in compound relation | morsel-making; material nutriment taken in gross or subtle form |
| āhāraḥ | nominative masculine singular | nutriment; that which sustains |
| kāme | locative singular | in the desire realm |
| tryāyatanātmakaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound adjective | constituted by three spheres |
| na | negative particle | excludes visible form from nutriment |
| rūpāyatanam | nominative neuter singular | the visible-form sphere |
| tena | instrumental singular pronoun used consequentially | therefore / for that reason |
| svākṣa | compound member; `sva` + `akṣa` | its own faculty, namely the eye |
| mukta | compound member | the liberated; specified as non-returners and arhats in the Bhāṣya |
| ananugrahāt | ablative singular of negative abstract | because of not supporting or benefiting |

The three spheres are not named in the Kārikā. The Bhāṣya specifies them as
odor, taste, and tangible. It also controls the dense final compound through
the functional criterion of `anugraha`, sustaining support.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first hemistich defines the realm and constitution of morsel nutriment:

```text
kavaḍīkāraḥ āhāraḥ
    kāme
    tri-āyatana-ātmakaḥ

morsel nutriment
    [exists] in the desire realm
    and is constituted by three spheres
```

The second hemistich excludes a plausible fourth sphere:

```text
rūpa-āyatanam [āhāraḥ] na
    = the visible-form sphere is not nutriment

tena sva-akṣa-mukta-an-anugrahāt
    = because it does not provide the relevant support
      to its own faculty or to the liberated
```

The Bhāṣya explains the causal phrase by first defining nutriment as what
supports the faculties and their great elements, and then testing visible form
against that definition.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Morsel nutriment in the desire realm is constituted by three spheres. The
> visible-form sphere is not [nutriment], because it does not support its own
> faculty or the liberated.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Morsel nutriment occurs only in the desire realm and is constituted by the
> three desire-realm spheres of odor, taste, and tangible. It may be gross or
> subtle, and sustaining influences not literally swallowed may be included
> under its subtle form. Visible form itself is not nutriment, because
> nutriment must support the faculties and their material elements, whereas
> visible form does not support even its own faculty, the eye, in the act of
> ingestion. When seeing attractive food occasions pleasure, the nourishing
> factor is the pleasant-feeling-producing contact that takes the form as its
> object-support, not the visible form itself. Liberated persons confirm the
> distinction: they may see pleasing food without receiving that sustaining
> benefit from its appearance.

The identification of the three spheres, the gross/subtle distinction, the
functional definition of nutriment, and the contact analysis are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Nutriment is determined by sustaining efficacy, not by appearance or by the
> ordinary name “food.” Odor, taste, and tangible enter the material economy
> of desire-realm embodiment; visible form does not. Even when an appearance
> delights, the operative support lies in the contact through which it is felt.
> The World persists not by consuming images of things, but through determinate
> relations that actually maintain its embodied faculties and their ground.

```text
āhāra
    = yad indriya-mahābhūtānām anugrahāya saṃvartate

    = what conduces to sustaining the faculties and great elements
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### Realm restriction

```text
kāmadhātu
    → morsel nutriment present

rūpadhātu | ārūpyadhātu
    → morsel nutriment absent
```

The Bhāṣya grounds the exclusion in dispassion toward this kind of nutriment
among beings born in the two higher realms.

### Constitutive threefold classification

```text
kavaḍīkārāhāra
    = gandha-āyatana
      + rasa-āyatana
      + spraṣṭavya-āyatana
```

The ordinary description by mouthful or nasal intake states the predominant
case, not an exceptionless physical mechanism. Whatever materially sustains
without literal swallowing may count as subtle nutriment, as with bathing or
anointing.

### Appearance is not sustaining function

```text
visible(x)
    ∧ ordinarilyCalledFood(x)
        does not entail
nutriment(x)
```

The sufficient test is operational:

```text
supports(x, faculties + great elements)
    → eligibleFor(x, nutriment)
```

Visible form fails this test. Its relation to the eye is that of object-field,
not material sustenance.

### Object-support and nourishing operation must not be collapsed

```text
visible form
    → object-support of pleasant contact

pleasant contact
    → nourishing operation

therefore:
    object-support of nourishment
        ≠ nourishing factor itself
```

This preserves the causal roles of `rūpa`, `sparśa`, and `vedanā` rather than
crediting the displayed appearance with the work performed by contact.

### The liberated function as a discrimination test

```text
pleasing visible form present
    + liberated viewer
        → no relevant sustaining benefit

therefore:
    pleasing appearance alone is insufficient for nutriment
```

The argument distinguishes aesthetic presentation from conditioned affective
support.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse is an exceptionally clean example of Dharma discrimination. An
ordinary Thing called “food” is decomposed according to the different work
performed by its dharmas:

```text
visible form
    → appears to the eye

odor, taste, tangible
    → constitute material nutriment

pleasant contact
    → may provide affective nourishment
```

The Kośa refuses object-level shorthand. “I ate the beautiful steak” is
ordinary language; Abhidharma asks which determinations actually sustained the
faculties, which served as object-support, and which generated pleasant
feeling. The apparent unity of the meal is resolved into functional domains.

This gives our Semantic-oriented NLP pipeline a precise rule:

```text
surface noun
    ≠ semantic operation
```

A token labeled `food` does not settle the semantic analysis. Oculus must
discriminate the roles carried within the event:

```ts
interface NutrimentEvent {
  displayedForm: ObjectSupport;
  materialSupports: Array<Odor | Taste | Tangible>;
  contact?: PleasantFeelingProducingContact;
  sustainingEffect: FacultyElementSupport;
}
```

This is an Organon projection, not the wording of Vasubandhu. It shows why the
Kośa extends Yoga Citta Theory into Practical Reason: it distinguishes what
appears, what is contacted, what is felt, and what actually maintains the
embodied Agent.

The verse also sharpens our account of the Semantic Copula. The statement
“this is food” becomes true in the technical sense only through the sustaining
relation:

```text
x is nutriment
    because
x actually supports the embodied system
```

The copula is not classification by resemblance. It is functional
determination.

Finally, subtle nutriment prevents us from reducing material support to crude
ingestion. Bathing, anointing, odor-support, and pervasive assimilation can
maintain embodiment without the stereotyped act of chewing. The definition
follows the sustaining operation wherever it occurs.
