# VAK_3.15

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विपर्यस्तमतिर्याति गतिदेशं रिरंसया ।
>
> गन्धस्थानाभिकामोऽन्यः ऊर्ध्वपादस्तु नारकः ॥ ३.१५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> viparyastamatir yāti gatideśaṃ riraṃsayā /
>
> gandhasthānābhikāmo 'nyaḥ ūrdhvapādas tu nārakaḥ // 3.15 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
viparyasta-matiḥ + yāti       → viparyastamatir yāti
gati-deśam                    → gatideśam
riraṃsayā                     → riraṃsayā
gandha-sthāna-abhikāmaḥ       → gandhasthānābhikāmaḥ
anyaḥ                         → anyaḥ
ūrdhva-pādaḥ + tu             → ūrdhvapādas tu
nārakaḥ                       → nārakaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| viparyasta-matiḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | having inverted or distorted understanding |
| yāti | third-person singular present of √yā | goes; proceeds |
| gati-deśam | accusative masculine singular compound | place belonging to the projected destiny |
| riraṃsayā | instrumental feminine singular | through desire to enjoy; through longing |
| gandha-sthāna | compound member | place characterized or indicated by scent |
| abhikāmaḥ | nominative masculine singular adjective | strongly desiring; attracted toward |
| anyaḥ | nominative masculine singular | another [intermediate being or birth-case] |
| ūrdhva-pādaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | with feet upward; inverted in orientation |
| tu | contrastive particle | but; however |
| nārakaḥ | nominative masculine singular | hell-bound intermediate being |

The subject of `yāti` is the intermediate being carried forward from VAK
3.14. `Riraṃsayā` expresses the affective means of approach, while
`gatideśam` supplies its accusative destination.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first clause describes approach to womb-birth according to the Bhāṣya:

```text
[antarābhavaḥ]
    viparyasta-matiḥ
    riraṃsayā
    gati-deśaṃ yāti
```

> With inverted understanding, the intermediate being goes to the place of
> its destiny through longing.

The second clause introduces another mode of approach:

```text
anyaḥ [antarābhavaḥ]
    gandha-sthāna-abhikāmaḥ [yāti]
```

> Another, attracted toward a scent-place, [goes there].

The final clause specifies the hell-bound orientation:

```text
nārakaḥ tu
    ūrdhva-pādaḥ [yāti]
```

> But the hell-bound intermediate being goes with feet upward.

The Kārikā compresses multiple birth-modes. The Bhāṣya assigns longing under
distortion to womb-entry, scent-attraction to moisture-birth, place-attraction
to spontaneous birth, and distinctive orientation to different destinies.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> With inverted understanding, it goes to the place of its destiny through
> longing. Another is attracted toward a scent-place; but the hell-bound
> intermediate being goes with feet upward.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The intermediate being approaches its karmically projected rebirth-place
> through a distorted presentation and the longing corresponding to its mode
> of birth. A moisture-born transition is attracted toward a pure or impure
> scent-place; a spontaneously arisen transition is attracted toward its
> place. A hell-bound transition moves inverted, with feet upward.

The distribution across womb, moisture, and spontaneous birth and the pure or
impure character of scent are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A destination can be objectively fixed while appearing subjectively under a
> different aspect. Karma projects the place of rebirth and conditions its
> perception; inverted understanding presents that place as desirable, and
> longing carries the intermediate continuity toward the result already in
> force. Error does not interrupt the World System: it becomes one of the
> modes through which the projected course is completed.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Destination and presentation are distinct determinations

```text
prior karma
    → projects gati
    → produces capacity to see upapatti-deśa

viparyasta-mati
    → presents the destination under an attractive aspect

riraṃsā / abhilāṣa
    → supplies proximate attraction

yāti
    → realizes movement toward the projected place
```

Therefore:

```text
destination-determination
    ≠ conscious recognition of the destination as it is

desire
    mediates the projected course
    rather than selecting an open destination
```

### Hell is sought under the appearance of relief

The Bhāṣya answers the objection “How could anyone desire a place in hell?”
with two symmetrical cases:

```text
present torment by cold
    → hell-fire appears as warmth
    → movement toward apparent relief

present torment by heat
    → hell-cold appears as cooling
    → movement toward apparent relief
```

The destination is not desired under the determination “hell.” It is
approached through an inverted appearance fitted to the operative attraction.

### Birth-mode determines approach-mode

```text
womb-birth
    inverted presentation + longing

moisture-birth
    attraction toward a scent-place

spontaneous birth
    attraction toward a place

destiny-class
    determines bodily orientation in transit
```

The Bhāṣya says the deva intermediate being moves upward; human, animal, and
preta transitions move correspondingly to their destinations; the hell-bound
case is specifically feet-upward.

### Older-teacher account

The `pūrvācāryāḥ` add:

```text
condition in which the productive karma was performed
    → re-presented appearance of oneself and others
    → movement toward the corresponding rebirth
```

This is an attributed alternative and must not be silently promoted to
Vasubandhu's unqualified conclusion.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.15 reveals the World System as an appearance-driven transition engine.
The projected result does not need to appear truthfully in order to operate.
It needs an appearance capable of mobilizing the appropriate attraction.

```text
Karma fixes the course.
Viparyaya makes the course appear otherwise.
Desire carries the continuity into the fixed result.
```

This is Transcendental Technē as project architecture. The machinery joins
objective determination, perceptual presentation, affective attraction, and
embodied movement without collapsing them into one event.

The result is more unsettling than a simple punishment story. The Agent runs
toward relief, but the sought relief is the appearance-form under which its
destiny becomes approachable. In Tāmasic World, misrecognition can be the
interface through which a lawful result installs itself.

For our NLP Pipeline of Meaning:

```text
projected destination
    → generates a presentation
    → presentation is evaluated under current distress
    → attraction becomes action
    → action completes transition
```

The representation is false as disclosure of the destination yet effective
as transition-control. `Jagan mithyā` now receives an exact local contact:
appearance is neither ultimate truth nor causal nothing. It can govern the
Agent's passage through the World System.

Rudra's higher comprehension does not belong to the primary doctrine here.
Within the Kārikā, the painful wonder is sufficient: destiny is fixed,
perception is inverted, desire is sincere, and the Agent moves.
