# VAK_3.63 Bhāṣya — The Four Terraces of Sumeru

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
pariṣaṇḍāś catasro 'sya daśasāhasrikāntarāḥ /
ṣoḍaśāṣṭau sahasrāṇi catvāri dve ca nirgatāḥ // 3.63 //
```

> Sumeru has four terraces at intervals of ten thousand yojanas. They project
> outward respectively sixteen thousand, eight thousand, four thousand, and
> two thousand yojanas.

The Bhāṣya reaches this geometry through a question about the inhabitants of
the sun, moon, and stellar residences. It distinguishes devas who dwell in
celestial residences from those who dwell upon the ground, then determines
the number, elevation, and outward extent of Sumeru's terraces. The named
inhabitants of the four terraces belong to VAK 3.64.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Athaitāni sūryādivimānāni katame sattvā adhyāvasanti?

Devāś Cāturmahārājakāyikāḥ.

Kim etāny eva teṣāṃ sthānāni?

Vimānavāsinām etāni. Bhūminivāsināṃ punaḥ Sumerupariṣaṇḍādīni.

Kati cāsya pariṣaṇḍāḥ? Kiyatyo vā?

Pariṣaṇḍāś catasro 'sya daśasāhasrikāntarāḥ.

Daśayojanasahasrāṇy udgamyaikā; evaṃ yāvac caturthī. Tābhiḥ Sumeror
ardham ākṣiptam. Tāś ca tato yathāsaṃkhyam:

Ṣoḍaśāṣṭau sahasrāṇi catvāri dve ca nirgatāḥ.

Prathamā pariṣaṇḍā ṣoḍaśa sahasrāṇi yojanānāṃ Sumeror nirgatā; dvitīyā
'ṣṭau, tṛtīyā catvāri, caturthī dve.
```

The running witness is clear apart from mechanical word joining and
punctuation. The continuous text regularizes these features and capitalizes
proper names. `Kiyatyaḥ` is understood contextually as asking the terraces'
extent or dimensions; the following answer differentiates their vertical
interval from their outward projection.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What beings inhabit these celestial residences beginning with those of the
sun?

They are devas belonging to the host of the Four Great Kings.

Are these their only dwelling places?

They are the dwelling places of those who reside in celestial residences.
For those who dwell upon the ground there are, among other places, the
terraces of Sumeru.

How many terraces does Sumeru have, and what are their dimensions?

It has four terraces at intervals of ten thousand yojanas. One terrace is
reached after ascending ten thousand yojanas, and so on as far as the fourth.
Through them half of Sumeru is occupied. From Sumeru they project outward,
respectively, sixteen thousand, eight thousand, four thousand, and two
thousand yojanas.

The first terrace projects sixteen thousand yojanas from Sumeru, the second
eight thousand, the third four thousand, and the fourth two thousand.

## 4. Natural Movement and Voice

The passage has one explanatory voice, Vasubandhu's, and moves through two
connected problems:

1. Who inhabits the celestial residences just discussed, and are those the
   only dwellings of that deva class?
2. What spatial structure provides dwelling places for its ground-dwelling
   members?

The answer first differentiates modes of habitation:

```text
Cāturmahārājakāyika devas
    ├── celestial-residence dwellers → sun, moon, and stellar residences
    └── ground dwellers              → Sumeru terraces and other places
```

The terrace geometry is therefore not an isolated interruption in the
cosmography. It establishes the ground-level domain whose inhabitants will be
distributed in the following verse.

No opponent or alternative school is named in this unit. The questions unpack
the spatial consequences of the preceding celestial account.

## 5. Vertical Order

`Daśasāhasrikāntarāḥ` is resolved through repeated ascent. The first terrace
is reached after rising 10,000 yojanas, and the same interval continues to the
fourth:

| Terrace | Elevation by successive ascent |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 10,000 yojanas |
| 2 | 20,000 yojanas |
| 3 | 30,000 yojanas |
| 4 | 40,000 yojanas |

The Bhāṣya then states that these terraces occupy half of Sumeru. This depends
upon the previously established measure of the mountain above the water:
40,000 yojanas reaches the midpoint of its 80,000-yojana emerged height.

The four terraces are thus levels within the lower half of the emerged
mountain. They are not four equal divisions of Sumeru's complete height, nor
does the ten-thousand measure describe their horizontal extension.

## 6. Outward Projection

The horizontal sequence follows a different rule:

| Terrace | Projection from Sumeru |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 16,000 yojanas |
| 2 | 8,000 yojanas |
| 3 | 4,000 yojanas |
| 4 | 2,000 yojanas |

`Yathāsaṃkhyam` binds the terraces and measures in their stated order. The
prose then repeats the correspondence individually, removing any ambiguity in
the compressed numerical series.

The projections halve successively:

```text
16,000 → 8,000 → 4,000 → 2,000
```

This halving is an evident relation within the transmitted numbers. The text
does not separately state it as an abstract law, but its ordered assignment
makes the pattern exact.

## 7. Two Spatial Axes

The identity of a terrace requires at least two independent coordinates:

```text
vertical address:
    equal 10,000-yojana steps upward

horizontal capacity:
    successively halved outward projections
```

Higher terraces are not simply “larger” or “smaller” without qualification.
Elevation increases while projection decreases. The same ordinal succession
governs both axes, but it does so through different quantitative relations.

This distinction also clarifies `nirgatāḥ`: the terraces extend or project
outward from Sumeru. Their figures are not additional vertical heights. A
single undifferentiated `size` field would lose the geometry established by
the commentary.

## 8. Doctrinal Determination

The opening classification prevents a premature identification of a deva
class with one kind of dwelling. The Cāturmahārājakāyikas include both
`vimānavāsin`, inhabitants of celestial residences, and `bhūminivāsin`,
ground-dwellers. Class membership and location are related but not identical
determinations.

```text
same deva class
    → more than one habitation mode
        → differentiated locations within one world-system
```

The Bhāṣya then constructs the ground domain before assigning named groups to
its levels. Spatial order and population order are consecutive layers of the
exposition. VAK 3.63 gives the positions and measures; VAK 3.64 will specify
the inhabitants.

The terraces remain determinations of produced Loka. Their ascending order is
not by itself a scale of liberation or spiritual realization.

## 9. Organon Study

For the Organon, this passage is useful because it refuses to collapse bearer,
class, habitation mode, and spatial coordinate:

```text
deva class
    ≠ one unique residence

terrace identity
    = ordinal level + elevation + outward projection
```

The geometry supplies a domain into which inhabitants can subsequently be
bound. Yet the domain is not produced merely by listing those inhabitants;
its vertical and horizontal relations are independently determined first.

In Dataset terms, `elevation` and `projection` are distinct Features governed
by distinct transformations. This is a project-level reconstruction, not
Vasubandhu's vocabulary. Its value is exact: it preserves the difference
between increasing height and decreasing breadth rather than reducing both to
an ambiguous notion of rank.
