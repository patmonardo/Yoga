# VAK_3.13 Bhāṣya — Projected Form and the Four Phases of Existence

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ekākṣepād asāv aiṣyatpūrvakālabhavākṛtiḥ /
> sa punar maraṇāt pūrva upapattikṣaṇāt paraḥ // 3.13 //

> Through one and the same projection, this intermediate existence has the
> form of the coming prior-time existence. That prior-time existence, in turn,
> is after the moment of rebirth and before death.

The pronoun changes referent across the verse. `Asau` denotes intermediate
existence; `saḥ` resumes `pūrvakālabhava`, the embodied life-phase after the
rebirth-junction and before death. The Bhāṣya identifies the “one projection”
as the same karma projecting both the destiny and the intermediate form that
leads to it.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What form does intermediate existence assume for one who is going toward a
particular destiny?

“Through one and the same projection, this intermediate existence has the
form of the coming prior-time existence.” The very karma by which a destiny
is projected also projects intermediate existence for attaining that destiny.
Therefore, whatever destiny one is going toward, intermediate existence has
the form of the embodied life-phase that will arise in that destiny.

An objection follows. If intermediate beings belonging to the five destinies
can arise in one womb, such as that of a dog, would an intermediate being
destined for hell not burn the womb?

Even during embodied life, hell-beings wandering through the auxiliary hells
are not always aflame; how much less must intermediate beings always be so.
But suppose the intermediate being is aflame. Because its sentient
configuration is transparent and subtle, just as it cannot ordinarily be
seen, neither can it be touched. The objection therefore does not arise.
Moreover, intermediate beings do not adhere physically to one another in the
womb, and karmic obstruction prevents burning.

As for size, an intermediate being is like a child of five or six years, but
it possesses all six faculties. The Bodhisattva's intermediate form, however,
is like that of a fully grown young man and possesses the major and minor
marks. Accordingly, when the Bodhisattva in intermediate existence entered
his mother's womb, hundreds of millions of four-continent world-systems were
illuminated.

But the Bodhisattva's mother saw in a dream a young white elephant entering
her womb. That was only a sign, because the Bodhisattva had long been removed
from animal birth—just as King Kṛkī saw ten symbolic dreams. The transmitted
verse then lists those dreams.

An intermediate being does not enter by breaking through the womb; it enters
through the mother's birth passage. For this reason, among twins the one born
later is called the elder and the one born first the younger: the later-born
twin entered first and lies farther within.

How should a commentary on a Dharma text be understood when it poetically
says that the sage, assuming the form of a splendid white elephant with six
tusks and four feet, entered his mother's womb as though entering a hermitage?
It need not be interpreted literally. It is neither sūtra, Vinaya, nor
Abhidharma; it is poetry, and poets composing verse sometimes superimpose
certain features. Or it may be interpreted as describing the appearance seen
by his mother in her dream, and the poet composed the verse accordingly.

An intermediate being destined for the form-domain appears at full size and
clothed because modesty predominates there. The Bodhisattva likewise appears
clothed, and the nun Śuklā is also given as a special case connected with the
power of aspiration; the exact transmitted sentence concerning her remains
uncertain. Other intermediate beings are naked because shamelessness
predominates in the desire-domain.

What, then, is this prior-time existence? “That, in turn, is after the moment
of rebirth and before death.” Existence in general means the five appropriated
aggregates, and it is divided fourfold. Intermediate existence is as already
explained. Rebirth-existence is the junction-moment within the destinies. All
existence after that, apart from the death-moment, is prior-time existence.
The final moment is death-existence; after it, intermediate existence arises
when rebirth is to occur among form-possessing beings.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The unit has two principal determinations with an extended middle inquiry:

```text
one karmic projection
    coordinates future destiny and intermediate form

questions about the intermediate body's physical appearance
    fire, contact, size, marks, womb-entry, dream-symbol, clothing

fourfold bhava
    places transition within a complete temporal cycle
```

The main expository voice gives the projection principle. An unnamed objector
raises the burning-womb problem and the white-elephant dream. The reply
distinguishes doctrinal description from crude physical inference and from
poetic imagery. The final question returns to formal definition.

The verse attributed to a commentary on a Dharma text is explicitly ranked
below sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma as poetry. This is a source-critical
judgment within the Bhāṣya itself, not a modern dismissal added by the Study.

## 5. One Projection Configures Transition and Result

The governing relation is explicit:

```text
one karma
    ├── projects the future destiny
    └── projects the intermediate existence for reaching it

therefore
    intermediate form corresponds to coming embodied form
```

The transition is neither neutral nor a self selecting among destinations.
It is already shaped by the result toward which the causal series is directed.
Yet correspondence of form does not erase the distinction between phases:
the intermediate being has not arrived in the rebirth whose form it
anticipates.

`Ekākṣepa` consequently means more than simultaneous occurrence. The same
projective karmic determination governs destination and the form of the
passage toward it. The mechanics of karma will be analyzed in Karmanirdeśa;
Lokanirdeśa here establishes the function that such karma must perform.

## 6. Intermediate Embodiment and Symbolic Appearance

The burning-womb objection literalizes the doctrine as ordinary gross bodies
occupying and affecting one another. The response assigns the intermediate
configuration different conditions of visibility, tangibility, contact, and
karmic obstruction. Whether this subtle-body physics can be defended is a
later question; the Bhāṣya's local point is that destiny-corresponding form
does not entail every gross property of the completed rebirth-body.

The Bodhisattva's elephant appearance is then sharply controlled:

```text
intermediate form
    human and fully marked according to coming existence

white elephant seen in dream
    sign or symbolic appearance

poetic verse
    may superimpose the dream image
    does not override higher textual genres
```

This is a valuable instance of refusing counterfeit literalism. The image is
not rejected as meaningless; it is retained as a sign while its apparent
zoological claim is denied. Appearance, signification, and bodily
configuration remain distinct.

The account of clothing likewise makes intermediate appearance
domain-conditioned. Full size and clothing in the form-domain are connected
with modesty; ordinary desire-domain intermediate beings are described as
naked. These are Bhāṣya determinations, not words of the Kārikā.

## 7. Four Temporal Functions of Existence

The Bhāṣya defines `bhava` generally as the five appropriated aggregates and
divides it according to temporal function:

| Phase | Determination |
|---|---|
| `upapattibhava` | junction-moment constituting arrival in a destiny |
| `pūrvakālabhava` | all subsequent embodied existence before the death-moment |
| `maraṇabhava` | final moment of that embodied existence |
| `antarābhava` | transition after death toward another form-bearing rebirth |

The sequence is cyclical but not substantival:

```text
rebirth-junction
    → embodied life-phase
        → death-moment
            → intermediate phase
                → rebirth-junction
```

What occupies these functions is not an enduring self but the conditioned
series of appropriated aggregates. The distinction is temporal and causal,
not a division into four separate entities.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

`Ekākṣepāt` is “through one and the same projection.” The Bhāṣya supplies the
agentive condition: the same karma projects both destiny and intermediate
existence.

`Aiṣyatpūrvakālabhavākṛti` is the form of the prior-time existence that is
going to arise. It does not say that intermediate existence is already the
future embodied life.

In the second half, `saḥ` refers to `pūrvakālabhava`, not to intermediate
existence. Hence its bounds are after the rebirth-moment and before death.
Reading the line as another definition of intermediate existence would invert
both grammar and Bhāṣya.

The prose's discussion of the intermediate body must remain commentary-level:
its size, six faculties, transparency, clothing, and manner of womb-entry are
not lexically present in the verse. The dream elephant is explicitly treated
as a sign and poetic representation rather than the literal intermediate
form.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

VAK 3.13 determines a transition whose form is governed by its coming result:

```text
projective cause
    configures passage
    in correspondence with destination
```

This comes close to the Organon's theory of Outflow as Projection, but the
text enforces a crucial restraint. Projection is not arbitrary labeling by a
free observer; it is the causal efficacy of karma coordinating successive
configurations within one sentient continuity.

The dream discussion adds an equally important distinction for an Oculus
theory of appearances:

```text
literal configuration
    what form the intermediate being has

symbolic appearance
    what the mother sees in a dream

poetic superimposition
    how a later verse narrates the sign
```

A single event can therefore receive multiple representational forms without
those forms having equal ontological or textual status. The Bhāṣya itself
performs this discrimination; the Organon need not impose it from outside.

For the present translation pass, the secure gain is limited and strong: one
karmic projection coordinates transition and destination, and the four
existence-phases are functions of an aggregate-continuity. A deeper account of
embodiment and symbolic appearance belongs to the later learning pass.
