# VAK_3.102

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> सप्ताग्निना अद्भिरेका एवं गतेऽद्भिः सप्तके पुनः ।
>
> तेजसा सप्तकः पश्चाद्वायुसंवर्तनी ततः ॥ ३.१०२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> saptāgninā adbhir ekā evaṃ gate 'dbhiḥ saptake punaḥ /
>
> tejasā saptakaḥ paścād vāyusaṃvartanī tataḥ // 3.102 //

The Kārikā gives a recurrence rule rather than merely listing three kinds of
destruction. The Bhāṣya resolves its compressed count: seven fire-contractions
precede each of seven water-contractions; seven further fire-contractions are
then followed by one wind-contraction, producing sixty-four events in all.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sapta                       → sapta
agninā                      → agninā
adbhiḥ                      → adbhir (before vowel)
ekā                         → ekā

saptake                     → saptake
punaḥ                       → punaḥ
tejasā                      → tejasā
saptakaḥ                    → saptakaḥ
paścāt                      → paścād (before v-)

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| sapta agninā | numeral with instrumental masculine singular | seven contractions by fire |
| adbhiḥ ekā | instrumental feminine plural with nominative feminine singular | one contraction by water |
| evaṃ gate | locative absolute | when the sequence has proceeded in this manner |
| adbhiḥ saptake | locative neuter singular phrase | when the sevenfold water series has been completed |
| punaḥ | indeclinable | again; adds a final fire series |
| tejasā saptakaḥ | instrumental neuter singular with nominative masculine singular | a sevenfold series by fire |
| paścāt | indeclinable | afterward |
| vāyusaṃvartanī | nominative feminine singular compound | cosmic contraction by wind |
| tataḥ | indeclinable | then, following that sequence |

`Saptāgninā` is metrically compressed, but the Bhāṣya explicitly expands it
as seven `tejaḥsaṃvartanīs`. The arithmetic is therefore commentary-resolved,
not fully transparent from the isolated verse.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening gives the recurrent unit:

```text
sapta [saṃvartanyaḥ]
    agninā

[tataḥ]
    adbhiḥ ekā [saṃvartanī]
```

> Seven [contractions occur] by fire; then one by water.

The locative absolute marks completion of the repeated series:

```text
evaṃ gate
    adbhiḥ saptake
```

> When it has proceeded thus to a sevenfold [series] by water ...

The closing adds the final pair:

```text
punaḥ
    tejasā saptakaḥ

paścāt tataḥ
    vāyu-saṃvartanī
```

> again there is a sevenfold series by fire; after that, a wind-contraction.

The Bhāṣya confirms that each of the seven water-contractions follows seven
fire-contractions. It then adds seven more fire-contractions and one wind-
contraction.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Seven [contractions occur] by fire, [then] one by water. When it has
> proceeded thus to a sevenfold [series] by water, there is again a sevenfold
> [series] by fire; after that comes a wind-contraction.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Seven successive fire-contractions are followed by one water-contraction.
> This sequence repeats until seven water-contractions have occurred, yielding
> forty-nine contractions by fire and seven by water. Seven more fire-
> contractions then occur, followed by one contraction by wind. The complete
> cycle therefore comprises fifty-six fire-contractions, seven water-
> contractions, and one wind-contraction: sixty-four in all.

The totals, their correspondence with the lifespan of the Śubhakṛtsna gods,
and the closing causal stanza are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> World-dissolution is neither a unique apocalypse nor an unstructured return
> to chaos. It is a counted recurrence whose differences of duration follow
> differences in the stability of beings' embodied stations, themselves
> conditioned by differences of meditative attainment. The receptacle-world
> and the continuities it supports therefore share one differentiated causal
> order. At the greatest cosmological scale, Loka remains what arises from
> conditions and ceases when those conditions cease.

The final causal stanza is a cited formula after the Kārikā, not part of VAK
3.102 itself. It nevertheless determines how Vasubandhu closes the chapter.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Exact recurrence

```text
(7 fire + 1 water) × 7
    = 49 fire + 7 water

+ 7 fire
+ 1 wind

= 56 fire + 7 water + 1 wind
= 64 contractions
```

The cycle is a nested counter, not a loose alternation:

```text
for each of 7 water-contractions:
    perform 7 fire-contractions
    perform 1 water-contraction

perform 7 fire-contractions
perform 1 wind-contraction
```

### Beings and receptacle-worlds

The Bhāṣya states the correspondence:

```text
differentiated samāpatti
    → differentiated stability of embodied beings
        ↔ differentiated stability of receptacle-worlds
            → differentiated destruction intervals
```

The relation is not that a private meditative act manufactures a cosmos. A
shared causal differentiation is expressed on both the side of beings and
the side of the World supporting them.

### Causal closure

```text
dharmas arise from causes
    → causes can be stated

conditions cease
    → conditioned formation ceases
```

The final formula gathers the chapter's enormous spatial and temporal
architecture back under dependent arising. Counted recurrence does not
require an eternal wheel-substance behind the sequence.

## 9. Interpretive Note

Lokanirdeśa ends by converting the most extravagant cosmological image into
an exact logical form:

```text
World
    = differentiated field of beings and receptacles
    + spatially typed stations
    + temporally typed continuities
    + practically generated transformations
    + lawful arising and cessation
```

The sixty-fourfold destruction-series is therefore not important because its
scale is spectacular. It demonstrates that even dissolution has measure,
order, and a causal basis. Fire, water, and wind are the cosmological syntax;
the semantic determination is the lawful transformation of a conditioned
World-field.

This ending also completes the chapter's Agent–World copula. Differences in
`samāpatti` determine differences in the stability of embodied continuity;
those differences reciprocally appear as differences in the stability of
the receptacle-world. Neither side is reducible to the other, and neither
subsists independently:

```text
station of beings
    ↔ stability of World-support
        → one conditioned Loka
```

The closing `ye dharmā hetuprabhavā` stanza supplies the final guardrail. The
Kośa does not end Loka with an eternal cosmic ground. It ends with causes and
cessation. The Whole is not a thing behind the cycle; it is the intelligible
order of the conditioned relations through which a World arises, persists,
contracts, and can arise again.
