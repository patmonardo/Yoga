# VAK_3.04

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> नरकादिस्वनामोक्ता गतयः पञ्च तेषु ताः ।
>
> अक्लिष्टाव्याकृता एव सत्त्वाख्या नान्तराभवः ॥ ३.४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> narakādisvanāmoktā gatayaḥ pañca teṣu tāḥ /
>
> akliṣṭāvyākṛtā eva sattvākhyā nāntarābhavaḥ // 3.4 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| narakādi-svanāmoktāḥ | nominative feminine plural compound | called by their own names beginning with naraka |
| gatayaḥ | nominative feminine plural of `gati` | destinies, courses of rebirth |
| pañca | numeral | five |
| teṣu | locative plural pronoun | among those, referring to the three domains |
| tāḥ | nominative feminine plural pronoun | those five destinies |
| akliṣṭa-avyākṛtāḥ | nominative feminine plural compound | unafflicted and indeterminate |
| eva | restrictive particle | only, precisely |
| sattva-ākhyāḥ | nominative feminine plural compound | designated as beings |
| na | negation | not |
| antarā-bhavaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | intermediate existence / intermediate becoming |

`Narakādi-svanāmoktāḥ` refers back to names already introduced rather than
deriving a new list. The verbal force of `gati`, from √`gam`, remains audible:
a destiny is a course or going, not merely a static place.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first half places and identifies the five destinies:

```text
teṣu
    gatayaḥ pañca
    naraka-ādi-sva-nāma-uktāḥ
```

“Among those” means within the three domains just determined. The Bhāṣya
enumerates the five as hell-being, animal, preta, deva, and human destinies.

The second half restricts their status and excludes an apparent sixth member:

```text
tāḥ gatayaḥ
    akliṣṭa-avyākṛtāḥ eva
    sattva-ākhyāḥ

antarā-bhavaḥ
    na [gatiḥ]
```

The Bhāṣya sharpens `akliṣṭāvyākṛta` to `anivṛtāvyākṛta`, “non-obscured
indeterminate.” `Na antarābhavaḥ` excludes intermediate existence from the
five destinies; it does not deny intermediate existence.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Among those [domains] are the five destinies, called by their own names
> beginning with naraka. They alone are unafflicted-indeterminate and
> designated as beings; intermediate existence is not [a destiny].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Within the three domains are five destinies—hell, animal, preta, deva, and
> human. They are resultant, non-obscured indeterminate configurations
> designated as beings. Intermediate existence is not itself one of these
> destinies.

The explicit five-member enumeration, “resultant,” and “non-obscured” are
Bhāṣya determinations. “Resultant configuration” anticipates the cited account
in which karmically matured aggregates receive the designation of a being; it
does not translate a single word of the Kārikā.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System contains five determinate courses of sentient placement.
> These destinies are not themselves moral acts but resultant configurations
> upon which the designation “being” is conferred. The transition between
> destinies is real within the system, yet it is not itself a destination.

This distinguishes the moral cause of placement, the resulting world-position,
the transition between positions, and the designation applied to the result.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| dhātu | domain | world-field broader than any destiny occurring within it |
| gati | destiny / course | resultant course of sentient placement within a domain |
| akliṣṭa | unafflicted | the destiny is not itself an affliction, even when its karmic cause was afflicted |
| avyākṛta | indeterminate | not itself ethically productive as wholesome or unwholesome |
| anivṛtāvyākṛta | non-obscured indeterminate | Bhāṣya precision for the status of a destiny |
| sattva-ākhya | designated as a being | conventional designation applied to a resultant aggregate-configuration |
| karmabhava | karmic becoming | causal activity producing a destiny; introduced through the Bhāṣya's sūtra citation |
| antarābhava | intermediate existence / intermediate becoming | transition between established destinies; a `bhava` but not a `gati` |
| vipāka | maturation-result | resultant dharmas upon which a destiny-designation is imposed |
| saṃkhyāṃ gacchati | receives a designation | Bhāṣya phrase explaining how “hell-being” is predicated |

The distribution stated by the Bhāṣya is not symmetrical. Kāmadhātu contains
four full destinies and part of the deva destiny; rūpadhātu and ārūpyadhātu
contain only portions of the deva destiny. The three domains are therefore
broader than the five destinies and do not simply map one-to-one onto them.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 3.04 differentiates five logical types:

```text
Field
    dhātu

Result-position
    gati

Generating cause
    karmabhava

Transition between result-positions
    antarābhava

Designation of a resultant aggregate-complex
    sattva
```

Their relations must not be collapsed:

```text
dhātu > gati

karmabhava
    generates
        gati
    but is not itself
        gati

antarābhava
    conducts transition toward a gati
    but is not itself a gati

vipāka-dharmas
    receive the designation sattva
```

The Bhāṣya's Śāriputra citation is decisive: once the five aggregates ripen as
the relevant result, the designation “hell-being” applies, but no hell-being
is found apart from those dharmas. The citation establishes the conditions
under which the designation applies.

The ethical classification also becomes exact:

```text
moral or afflicted act
    cause of placement

destiny
    matured result-position
    unafflicted and indeterminate in itself
```

Thus suffering within a destiny does not make the destiny itself an afflicted
mental factor or a fresh unwholesome action.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The World System now acquires transit logic. A sentient continuity is not only
placed in a domain and destiny; it can pass between resultant configurations.
VAK 3.04 does not yet explain the mechanics of taking up or relinquishing a
body. It makes that later inquiry possible by separating destination from
transition and both from their karmic cause.

```text
settled embodiment and world-position
    gati

transition between embodied positions
    antarābhava

causal determination of the next position
    karmabhava
```

This gives a disciplined form to the project's live hypothesis:

```text
Rational Moral Agent
    acts within the World System
    generates determinate moral consequences
    undergoes transitions between embodied configurations

Subtle Transition Process
    mediates relinquishment and acquisition of embodiment
    is ordinarily hidden from the empirical agent
```

These capitalized terms are Organon constructions, not claims made by VAK
3.04. At this point the text has earned only the more modest structure:
`karmabhava`, `antarābhava`, `gati`, and the designation of a resultant being
are distinct. The subsequent verses must show whether and how intermediate
existence carries the continuity that takes up a new body.

The Śāriputra citation determines “being” as a designation upon the functioning
resultant complex. For the Rational Moral Agent, the relevant question is how
that role functions within morally generated placement and transition.

The large transition is therefore:

```text
World System as static map
    ↓
World System as moral-transitional process

placement
    requires a cause

change of placement
    requires a transition

designation of the resultant configuration
    requires a determinate resultant configuration
```

This is the internal bridge from Loka toward Karma. The World System is not
only Cosmic Frame; it is an ordered economy of morally generated placements
and transitions.
