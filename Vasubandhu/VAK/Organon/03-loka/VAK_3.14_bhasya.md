# VAK_3.14 Bhāṣya — The Operating Conditions of Intermediate Existence

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sajātiśuddhadivyākṣidṛśyaḥ karmarddhivegavān /
> sakalākṣo 'pratighavān anivartyaḥ sa gandhabhuk // 3.14 //

> The intermediate being is visible to those of its own class and to a
> purified divine eye. It possesses the speed of karmic power, all five
> faculties, and freedom from material obstruction; it cannot turn away from
> its projected destiny, and it feeds upon scent.

The Bhāṣya treats these not as decorative marvels but as constraints upon a
transition already configured by karma. It then asks how long the intermediate
phase lasts and records several incompatible answers.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

This intermediate existence is “visible to those of its own class and to a
purified divine eye.” It is seen only by intermediate beings of the same kind.
Those whose divine eye is extremely purified and produced through
superknowledge also see it. It is not seen by the ordinary eye acquired at
birth because of the transparency and subtlety belonging to its mode of
arising.

An intermediate deva sees all intermediate beings. Others say that human,
preta, animal, and hell intermediate beings see the classes successively
below them.

It “possesses the speed of karmic power.” Extraordinary power is movement
through space; extraordinary power produced by karma is karmic power; and the
speed of that power is its rapidity. Possessing it, the intermediate being
cannot be obstructed even by Buddhas, because its operative karma is stronger
with respect to this result.

It is “complete in its faculties”: it possesses the complete five faculties.

It is “unobstructed.” Obstruction is impact; one subject to it is obstructed,
while this being is unobstructed. It cannot be stopped even by diamond and
similar substances. Thus it is reported that, when a mass of heated iron was
split open, a worm was found living inside it.

With respect to the destiny in which it is going to arise, it is in every way
“unable to turn back.” A human intermediate being never disappears and
becomes a deva intermediate being or some other kind. In whatever destiny it
has arisen oriented toward, it must be reborn in that very destiny and not
another.

Does a desire-domain intermediate being consume morsel-food? Yes, but not
gross food. What, then? “It feeds upon scent.” For this reason it is called a
gandharva. Verbal roots have many meanings, and the shortened form is explained
by analogy with similar words. One of little status consumes foul scent; one
of great status consumes fragrant scent.

How long does it remain? The venerable teachers say there is no fixed rule:
it remains until it obtains the complete conditions for rebirth. Its lifespan
is not projected separately, because it belongs to the same class-continuity
as the coming rebirth. Otherwise, exhaustion of an independent lifespan would
entail another death-existence.

An objection follows. If a mass of flesh as large as Mount Sumeru were
present, it would become filled with worms during the rainy season. Were the
intermediate beings destined to become those worms waiting all that time? And
from where did they arrive at that moment?

This account is not found in sūtra or treatise. A better explanation is that
there is no end to short-lived creatures avid for scent and taste. Smelling
the flesh and becoming avid for its scent and taste, they die, activate karma
leading to worm-existence, and through that craving are reborn among worms.

Alternatively, karmas leading to that result become capable of producing
their maturation only at a time when the relevant conditions are abundant,
not at other times. Thus karma leading to birth as a wheel-turning monarch
produces such monarchs only when the human lifespan is eighty thousand years
or more, not in another period. Hence the Blessed One said that the karmic
maturation of beings is inconceivable.

The venerable Vasumitra says that intermediate existence lasts seven days. If
the complete conditions are not obtained within that period, the being dies
and arises again in the intermediate state. Others say it lasts seven weeks.
The Vaibhāṣikas say it lasts only a short time: because it seeks a place of
arising, it goes quickly and forms the junction whenever the appropriate
conditions have assembled.

If its place and class are fixed, it must be born in that place and class; at
the proper time the karmas themselves bring about the conjunction of
conditions. Others say that if these are not fixed, it is born elsewhere in a
similar class.

For example, cattle mate predominantly in the hot season, dogs in autumn,
bears in winter, and horses in their season, whereas wild cattle, jackals,
donkeys, and hyenas have different or unrestricted times. On this alternate
account, one destined for cattle may arise among wild cattle, one destined for
dogs among jackals, one destined for horses among donkeys, and one destined
for bears among hyenas when the original conditions are unavailable.

But this must be answered: an intermediate existence has one determinate
class-character and cannot arise in another class-character, because it is
projected by one karma.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The commentary first explains each predicate of the verse, then opens a
separate controversy over duration:

```text
visibility
karmic speed
complete faculties
non-obstruction
destination-fixity
scent as nutriment
    ↓
how long does the transition last?
    ↓
no fixed duration / seven days / seven weeks / a short time
    ↓
how do delayed or seasonal rebirth-conditions arise?
    ↓
can a similar species substitute for the projected one?
```

The visibility hierarchy is explicitly attributed to “others.” The no-fixed-
rule answer is attributed generically to venerable teachers; seven days to
Vasumitra; seven weeks to others; and a short time to the Vaibhāṣikas. The
seasonal substitution theory is again introduced as another view and receives
a final objection from the principle of one karmic projection.

The Bhāṣya therefore records disagreement rather than delivering one
unqualified duration doctrine.

## 5. Visibility, Faculties, and Material Passage

Visibility is relational rather than absolute:

```text
observer is an intermediate being of the same class
    or
observer possesses a highly purified divine eye
        → intermediate form can be seen
```

The ordinary eye acquired at birth does not suffice. This restriction should
not be converted into proof from present non-observation; it belongs to the
system's explanation of why an intermediate body is normally unseen.

`Sakalākṣa` is glossed as complete possession of the five faculties. The
intermediate phase is therefore a functioning sentient configuration, not a
mere causal point. `Apratigha` adds that ordinary material resistance does not
block it. The reported worm in heated iron is offered as supporting rhetoric,
not as the definition of non-obstruction.

## 6. Karmic Speed and Irreversible Destination

`Karmarddhivega` combines propulsion and cause:

```text
karma-produced extraordinary movement
    → rapid transit through space
```

The assertion that even a Buddha cannot block it is local. It says that, once
this karmic result is operative, an external intervention does not cancel its
movement. It does not establish a general metaphysical superiority of karma
over Buddhahood.

`Anivartya` is more important than mere speed. It means that the intermediate
being cannot exchange its projected destiny:

```text
human-directed intermediate existence
    cannot become
deva-directed intermediate existence
```

Transit is therefore constrained by the single projection established in VAK
3.13. The intermediate being moves powerfully but does not choose among open
destinations.

## 7. Nutriment and the Disputed Duration

For a desire-domain intermediate being, scent supplies the subtle form of
morsel-food. The Bhāṣya connects `gandhabhuk`, scent-eater, with the name
`gandharva` and distinguishes foul from fragrant scent according to status.
This is its technical nutriment account, not an exhaustive history of the
word `gandharva`.

Four duration positions are visible:

| Position | Duration |
|---|---|
| unnamed venerable teachers | until rebirth-conditions are obtained; no fixed rule |
| Vasumitra | seven days, followed by renewed intermediate arising if necessary |
| other teachers | seven weeks |
| Vaibhāṣikas | only a short time |

The no-fixed-rule position grounds duration in the absence of a separately
projected lifespan. The Vaibhāṣika position instead emphasizes the
intermediate being's active search for rebirth and the rapid formation of the
junction once conditions are present.

The worm and seasonal examples ask how rebirth-conditions can appear suddenly
or only at fitting times. The replies appeal either to newly dying beings
whose craving activates appropriate karma or to dormant karmas becoming
effective when their conditions are abundant. Neither account requires a
fixed stock of intermediate beings waiting indefinitely.

The final dispute over similar species is unresolved in form but receives a
strong objection: one intermediate class-character cannot turn into another,
because one karma projected the series. This restates destination-fixity at
the level of species membership.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

The compound `sajātiśuddhadivyākṣidṛśya` is distributive: visible to its own
class and to a purified divine eye. `Sajāti` does not qualify the divine eye.

`Karmarddhivegavān` is “possessing the speed of karmic power,” not merely
“swift because of karma.” The prose defines `ṛddhi` here as movement through
space and derives its force from operative karma.

`Sakalākṣa` means complete in the five sense-faculties. `Apratighavān` means
not subject to ordinary material obstruction. These do not imply gross
embodiment of the sort denied in VAK 3.13.

`Anivartya` is fixed to the projected destiny, not simply unable to reverse
physical direction. `Gandhabhuk` is scent-eating and applies to the subtle
nutriment of a desire-domain intermediate being.

The lengthy duration debate is Bhāṣya-only. Since it preserves several views,
no single number should be silently inserted into the Kārikā translation.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The verse specifies a constrained transition rather than a free wandering
spirit:

```text
limited visibility
complete faculties
karmically powered movement
ordinary matter non-obstruction
fixed destination
subtle nutriment
condition-bound duration
```

For the Organon, this is a useful model of an Outflow once projection has
become operative. The projected series has access conditions, propulsion,
support, and termination conditions. Yet it has no freedom to redefine its
type or destination during execution.

The duration dispute adds an important restraint to any proposed Oculus
schema. The source does not give one settled timeout. It gives rival models:
condition-bound persistence, periodic renewal, a seven-week maximum, and
rapid junction. A faithful dataset would preserve these as attributed
alternatives rather than collapse them into one property value.

That is enough for this translation pass. The later learning pass can ask how
visibility relative to a faculty, karmic propulsion, and type-invariance map
onto the Organon's theory of appearances. Here the textual result is already
precise: intermediate transition is powerful, subtle, and constrained by the
same projection that determines its completion.
