# VAK_3.02

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> ऊर्ध्वं सप्तदशस्थानो रूपधातुः पृथक् पृथक् ।
>
> ध्यानं त्रिभूमिकं तत्र चतुर्थं त्वष्टभूमिकम् ॥ ३.२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> ūrdhvaṃ saptadaśasthāno rūpadhātuḥ pṛthak pṛthak /
>
> dhyānaṃ tribhūmikaṃ tatra caturthaṃ tvaṣṭabhūmikam // 3.2 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
ūrdhvam                → ūrdhvam
saptadaśa-sthānaḥ      → saptadaśa-sthānaḥ
rūpa-dhātuḥ            → rūpa-dhātuḥ
pṛthak pṛthak          → pṛthak pṛthak
dhyānam                → dhyānam
tri-bhūmikam           → tri-bhūmikam
tatra                  → tatra
caturtham              → caturtham
tu aṣṭa-bhūmikam       → tv aṣṭa-bhūmikam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| ūrdhvam | adverbial accusative / indeclinable use of `ūrdhva` | above, upward from the desire-domain |
| saptadaśa-sthānaḥ | nominative masculine singular possessive compound | having seventeen stations |
| rūpa-dhātuḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | form-domain |
| pṛthak pṛthak | repeated indeclinable | separately, each distinctly |
| dhyānam | nominative neuter singular | a dhyāna; meditative absorption-order |
| tri-bhūmikam | nominative neuter singular adjective | having three levels or grounds |
| tatra | locative adverb | there, within the form-domain |
| caturtham | nominative neuter singular | the fourth dhyāna |
| tu | contrastive particle | but, whereas |
| aṣṭa-bhūmikam | nominative neuter singular adjective | having eight levels or grounds |

The transmitted `tvaṣṭabhūmikam` resolves as `tu + aṣṭa-bhūmikam`. The
Bhāṣya glosses distributive `pṛthak pṛthak` with `pratyekam`, “each
separately.”

## 4. Grammar

The first clause locates and counts the next domain:

```text
ūrdhvam [etasmāt kāmadhātoḥ]
    saptadaśa-sthānaḥ
    rūpa-dhātuḥ
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the point of comparison: “above this desire-domain.”
`Saptadaśa-sthānaḥ` predicates possession of seventeen stations of
`rūpadhātuḥ`.

The second clause distributes those stations through four dhyānas:

```text
tatra
    pṛthak pṛthak
    [prathama-dvitīya-tṛtīyaṃ] dhyānam tri-bhūmikam
    caturtham tu aṣṭa-bhūmikam
```

The first three dhyānas each have three levels; the contrastive `tu` marks the
fourth as eight-levelled:

| Dhyāna | Levels in the seventeen-station reckoning |
|---|---:|
| first | 3 |
| second | 3 |
| third | 3 |
| fourth | 8 |
| **Total** | **17** |

The Bhāṣya records a Kāśmīra alternative of sixteen. On that account,
Mahābrahmā has a more elevated `sthāna` among the Brahmapurohitas but not a
distinct `bhūmi`. The passage reports this alternative without explicitly
adjudicating it.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Above, the form-domain has seventeen stations. There, each [of the first
> three] dhyānas has three levels, but the fourth has eight levels.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Above the desire-domain lies the form-domain, comprising seventeen stations.
> Its first three dhyānas each comprise three levels, while the fourth
> comprises eight. The Kāśmīras instead count sixteen, treating Mahābrahmā as
> an elevated station but not as a distinct ground.

“Above the desire-domain,” “the first three,” and the sixteenfold alternative
are Bhāṣya determinations. The Kārikā itself states “above,” seventeen
stations, the distributive three-level rule, and the eight-level exception.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System rises above the desire-domain into the form-domain, yet
> remains internally ordered World. Here dhyāna is not merely an inward state:
> it articulates a cosmic hierarchy of grounds and stations. The first three
> dhyāna-orders each have three levels, and the fourth has eight, yielding the
> seventeen stations of refined form.

This is the Organon rendering of the verse's structure. “World System” and
“cosmic hierarchy” do not replace the close translations `rūpadhātu`,
`dhyāna`, `bhūmi`, or `sthāna`.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| ūrdhvam | above | ordered vertical relation to kāmadhātu; not liberation or transcendence beyond World |
| rūpadhātu | form-domain | the domain of refined form above kāmadhātu and organized through dhyāna |
| sthāna | station | determinate position within a domain; not automatically a distinct structural ground |
| dhyāna | dhyāna | meditative absorption functioning here as a principle of world-articulation |
| bhūmi | level / ground | a distinct structural level within a dhyāna-order |
| pṛthak pṛthak | each separately | distributive rule applied to the first three dhyānas |
| pratyekam | each individually | Bhāṣya clarification of `pṛthak pṛthak` |
| utkṛṣṭatara | more elevated | Bhāṣya description of Mahābrahmā's exceptional station |
| bhūmyantara | distinct ground | Bhāṣya criterion denied of Mahābrahmā by the Kāśmīras |

The seventeen-station scheme is:

```text
First dhyāna
    Brahmakāyika
    Brahmapurohita
    Mahābrahmā

Second dhyāna
    Parīttābha
    Apramāṇābha
    Ābhāsvara

Third dhyāna
    Parīttaśubha
    Apramāṇaśubha
    Śubhakṛtsna

Fourth dhyāna
    Anabhraka
    Puṇyaprasava
    Bṛhatphala
    Abṛha
    Atapa
    Sudṛśa
    Sudarśana
    Akaniṣṭha
```

The names are Bhāṣya enumeration, not wording present in the Kārikā.

## 8. Logical Determination

VAK 3.01 determined the first World System through sentient orders, locations,
Cosmic Frame, and limits. VAK 3.02 adds vertical and intensional articulation:

```text
World System
    kāmadhātu
        differentiated principally by destiny and placement
    rūpadhātu
        differentiated by dhyāna
            bhūmi
                sthāna
                    inhabitant
```

The relation `ūrdhvam` extends the World System; it does not leave it. Higher
world therefore remains world:

```text
vertical refinement
    ≠ release from the World System

dhyāna-ground
    ≠ pāramārthika principle
```

The Kāśmīra alternative then forces a crucial type distinction:

```text
greater rank of sthāna
    does not entail
distinctness of bhūmi
```

Cardinality depends upon the criterion of structural individuation. On the
seventeenfold account Mahābrahmā is counted as a distinct station; on the
Kāśmīra sixteenfold account his superior position does not produce a new
ground. A formal World System must therefore preserve both position and the
ground on which position depends rather than treating every named rank as a
new ontological level.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse makes the scale of Loka-nirdeśa unmistakable. We are not receiving
a parade of gods detached from philosophical necessity. We are receiving a
World System whose modes of consciousness, grounds, stations, inhabitants,
and Cosmic Frame are internally correlated.

The first great result is that dhyāna belongs to the architecture of Cosmos.
Within the Kośa's presentation, a meditative distinction can articulate a
world-domain. “Inner state” and “cosmic station” therefore cannot simply be
presupposed as two unrelated orders.

The second result is negative but decisive: ascent within the World System is
not transcendence of the World System. `Ūrdhvam` carries us from desire to
refined form, but every dhyāna-level remains a `bhūmi`, every station remains a
`sthāna`, and rūpadhātu remains a `dhātu` of conditioned existence.

This gives the Organon project room to think big while remaining textually
clean:

```text
Tāmasic World System
    kāmadhātu
    rūpadhātu
    ārūpyadhātu
    every internal ascent and refinement

Pāramārthika
    does not occupy the highest station inside that system
    comprehends the system as a whole
    sublates its claim to independent ultimacy
    preserves its determinate articulation within a higher truth
```

Here **Tāmasic World System** and **Pāramārthika** are explicit project-level
determinations, not terms or conclusions of VAK 3.02. Sublation does not mean
that the articulated world is erased. It means that the complete cosmic order
is enclosed and comprehended: retained as determinate appearance, overcome in
its claim to independent ultimacy.

The scale relation is therefore not:

```text
kāmadhātu → rūpadhātu → ārūpyadhātu → pāramārthika
```

as though the Pure Principles were one more cosmic address. It is:

```text
Pāramārthika / Pure Principles
    comprehends and sublates
        the entire Tāmasic World System
            kāmadhātu
            rūpadhātu
            ārūpyadhātu
```

That is the large philosophical promise of Loka-nirdeśa: it may supply the
complete Cosmic Frame and articulated World System which the pāramārthika does
not merely abandon, but comprehends.
