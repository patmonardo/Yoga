# VAK_3.09 Bhāṣya — Birth-Modes Across the Destinies

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> caturdhā naratiryañco nārakā upapādukāḥ /
> antarābhavadevāś ca pretā api jarāyujāḥ // 3.09 //

> Humans and animals occur through all four modes of birth. Hell-beings,
> intermediate-existence beings, and devas arise spontaneously. Pretas are
> also womb-born.

The Bhāṣya interprets `api`, “also,” to mean that pretas may be spontaneously
arisen as well as womb-born. The verse cross-classifies destiny and mode of
birth; it does not assign every destiny one exclusive biological type.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The question is raised: in which destiny are how many modes of birth found?

“Humans and animals are fourfold.” Humans occur in four ways. Egg-born humans
include the elders Śaila and Upaśaila, who emerged from Krauñcī, the thirty-two
sons of Mṛgāramātṛ, and the five hundred sons of the king of Pañcāla.
Womb-born humans are those commonly observed now. Moisture-born humans include
Māndhātṛ, Cāru, Upacāru, Kapotamālinī, Āmrapālī, and others. The spontaneously
arisen humans are those at the beginning of a cosmic age.

Animals likewise occur in four ways. Three modes are directly observed; the
spontaneously arisen include nāgas, suparṇins, and the like.

“Hell-beings are spontaneously arisen; intermediate-existence beings and
devas likewise.” All hell-beings, intermediate-existence beings, and devas
are exclusively spontaneously arisen.

“Pretas are also womb-born.” From the word “also,” it follows that they may
also be spontaneously arisen. A female preta reported to the venerable
Mahāmaudgalyāyana: “I give birth to five sons at night and five others by day.
Having given birth, I devour them, yet even so I find no satisfaction.”

Which mode of birth is best of all? Spontaneous birth. Why, then, does the
Bodhisattva in his final existence adopt womb-birth, although he has attained
mastery over his mode of birth? Because he sees a great purpose in acting this
way: through kinship the great Śākya lineage may be brought into the Dharma;
others may be drawn to esteem and respect him as one descended from a lineage
of universal monarchs; and those to be trained may be encouraged by seeing
that even one who has become human can attain such accomplishment.

Otherwise, with his family and lineage unknown, people might imagine, “Who is
this magical person?” or suppose that he were a deva or a piśāca. Other
sectarians might slander him by saying: “After hundreds of cosmic ages, such
a magician has appeared in the world and is devouring the world through
magic.”

Others say that he adopts womb-birth so that bodily relics may remain. Through
confidence arising in relation to them, humans and other beings have by the
thousands reached heaven and liberation. For spontaneously arisen beings lack
an external seed, and their bodies do not remain after death; they disappear
like the flame of an oil lamp at the end of night.

For those who accept the Blessed One's sustaining extraordinary power,
however, this explanation is not adequate.

One question gives rise to another. Although the corpse of a spontaneously
arisen being is not perceived, why does a sūtra say that a spontaneously
arisen suparṇin carries off a spontaneously arisen nāga for food? It does not
say that the suparṇin eats the corpse, only that it carries the nāga away for
food, not knowing what will occur; hence there is no fault. Or it eats the nāga
while it is still alive. Once the nāga has died, its body is no longer
available for the suparṇin to be satisfied by eating it.

Which mode of birth is the most numerous? Spontaneous birth alone. It includes
two destinies in their entirety, portions of three others, and all beings in
intermediate existence.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The commentary moves beyond a simple distribution table:

```text
map birth-modes across destinies
    ↓
ask which birth-mode is best
    ↓
explain the Bodhisattva's deliberate womb-birth
    ↓
report and criticize an alternative relic explanation
    ↓
resolve a scriptural difficulty about spontaneously arisen bodies
    ↓
identify the most numerous birth-mode
```

The expository voice gives the distribution and main Bodhisattva explanation.
The preta's verse and the suparṇin passage are scriptural testimony. `Apare tv
āhuḥ`, “others say,” explicitly introduces the relic explanation, after which
the text records a criticism from those who accept the Buddha's sustaining
extraordinary power.

The numerous proper names used as examples are inherited testimony within the
Bhāṣya. The present Study translates their classificatory role; it does not
attempt historical verification of each narrative.

## 5. Destiny and Birth-Mode as Independent Axes

The distribution is many-to-many:

| Class | Egg | Womb | Moisture | Spontaneous |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| human | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| animal | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| hell-being | no | no | no | yes |
| intermediate-existence being | no | no | no | yes |
| deva | no | no | no | yes |
| preta | not stated | yes | not stated | yes |

`Gati` determines a resultant course or destiny. `Yoni` determines the manner
of generation within such a course. Their intersection prevents either axis
from being mistaken for a complete account of the being.

The evidence also appears in different registers. Three animal birth-modes
are said to be directly observed, while spontaneous animal birth is supported
through examples such as nāgas and suparṇins. The Bhāṣya does not claim that
all members of its classification are established through ordinary
observation.

## 6. Why the Bodhisattva Chooses Womb-Birth

The passage explicitly ranks spontaneous birth as best, then shows that an
abstract ranking does not decide every concrete case. The final-existence
Bodhisattva possesses `upapattivaśitva`, mastery over the manner of birth, yet
chooses womb-birth because it serves a `mahān artha`, a great purpose:

```text
kinship
    brings the Śākya lineage into Dharma

known royal lineage
    elicits esteem and respect

human birth
    encourages those to be trained
    by showing human attainment to be possible
```

The choice also prevents misrecognition. A being who appeared without known
family or lineage might be dismissed as a magical apparition, deva, or
piśāca. Womb-birth makes the manifestation intelligible within a human social
and historical field.

This is not a rejection of spontaneous birth's superiority within its own
criterion. It distinguishes general ranking from fitness for a determinate
purpose. Mastery is expressed through selecting the birth-mode appropriate to
the beings to be trained.

## 7. Relics and the Bodies of Spontaneously Arisen Beings

The alternative explanation says that womb-birth permits bodily relics to
remain and thereby become occasions for confidence, heaven, and liberation.
It supports this by claiming that spontaneously arisen beings lack an external
seed and leave no corpse; their bodies disappear at death like an oil-lamp
flame.

The Bhāṣya does not leave this explanation unqualified. If the Buddha has the
extraordinary sustaining power attributed to him, absence of a naturally
remaining corpse need not prevent him from leaving relics. The relic account
therefore cannot be the necessary explanation of his womb-birth for those who
accept that power.

The suparṇin objection clarifies the narrower claim about spontaneously arisen
bodies. The bird may carry off or eat the living nāga; the cited statement
does not prove that a spontaneously arisen corpse persists after death. The
discussion concerns bodily remainder, not whether spontaneously arisen beings
possess functioning bodies while alive.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

`Caturdhā` distributes all four birth-modes to humans and animals separately.
It does not mean that humans and animals together form four types.

`Upapādukāḥ` applies to hell-beings, intermediate-existence beings, and devas.
The prose makes the restriction exclusive for all members of those classes.

`Pretā api jarāyujāḥ` means that pretas are also womb-born. The commentary
draws a second conclusion from `api`: they may also be spontaneously arisen.
The translation should therefore avoid implying that womb-birth is their
exclusive mode.

`Sarvasādhvī` and `sarvabahvī` ask different questions. Spontaneous birth is
both the best mode and the most numerous, but its superiority and numerical
extent require separate arguments. The Bodhisattva discussion concerns the
first; the closing count establishes the second.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The modest philosophical result is the difference between abstract
superiority and purposive fitness:

```text
spontaneous birth
    best birth-mode in general

womb-birth of the Bodhisattva
    selected for a determinate pedagogical purpose
```

For the later Organon learning pass, this supplies a concrete form of
manifestation governed by intelligibility. The most immediate or powerful
appearance is not necessarily the one through which truth can best become
known among embodied agents. Lineage, trust, shared human possibility, and
the capacities of those to be trained belong to the efficacy of the
appearance.

That conclusion should remain close to the text. VAK 3.09 does not posit an
unchanging operator who chooses and discards bodies. It speaks of the
final-existence Bodhisattva's mastery over birth and purposeful adoption of a
human womb-birth. The larger Yoga or Organon account of embodiment must be
developed later and separately.

The passage nevertheless prepares the next inquiry. Because every
intermediate-existence being is spontaneously arisen, the question of what
intermediate existence is and how it reaches its destination now becomes
unavoidable.
