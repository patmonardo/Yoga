# VAK_3.97

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> देशस्थोत्तप्तपूर्णत्वैर्लक्षणातिशयो मुनेः ।
>
> प्रागासन् रूपिवत् सत्त्वा रसरागात्ततः शनैः ॥ ३.९७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> deśasthottaptapūrṇatvair lakṣaṇātiśayo muneḥ /
>
> prāg āsan rūpivat sattvā rasarāgāt tataḥ śanaiḥ // 3.97 //

The first half completes the Buddha–cakravartin comparison. The second begins
a new genetic exposition of the Human World. The Bhāṣya fixes `rūpivat` as
“like beings of the rūpa realm” and unfolds the otherwise elliptical
`rasarāgāt tataḥ śanaiḥ` into a long sequence of gradual transformation.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
deśa-stha-uttapta-pūrṇatvaiḥ  → deśasthottaptapūrṇatvair (before l-)
lakṣaṇa-atiśayaḥ              → lakṣaṇātiśayo (before m-)
muneḥ                          → muneḥ
prāk                           → prāg (before vowel)
āsan                           → āsan
rūpi-vat                       → rūpivat
sattvāḥ                        → sattvā (before r-)
rasa-rāgāt                     → rasarāgāt
tataḥ                          → tataḥ
śanaiḥ                         → śanaiḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| deśastha | compound member | situated in the proper place |
| uttapta | compound member | intensely or vividly manifest, not merely clear |
| pūrṇatva | abstract neuter noun in instrumental plural compound | completeness or full development |
| lakṣaṇātiśayaḥ | nominative masculine singular | superiority of bodily marks |
| muneḥ | genitive masculine singular | of the Muni, here the Buddha |
| prāk | temporal adverb | formerly, at the beginning of the kalpa |
| āsan | third-person plural imperfect of √as | they were |
| rūpivat | comparative formation in `-vat` | like rūpāvacara beings, by Bhāṣya determination |
| sattvāḥ | nominative masculine plural | the first-kalpa beings |
| rasarāgāt | ablative masculine singular compound | because of attachment to savor |
| tataḥ | adverb | from that, thereafter |
| śanaiḥ | adverb | gradually, through ordered stages |

The instrumental abstracts `deśasthatva`, `uttaptatva`, and `pūrṇatva` state
the respects in which the marks are superior. `Rasarāgāt` is causal: the
genealogy turns not on the mere presence of savor but on attachment to it.

## 4. Grammar

The opening clause compares the thirty-two marks possessed by Buddhas and
cakravartins:

```text
muneḥ
    lakṣaṇa-atiśayaḥ [bhavati]

deśastha-tva-
uttapta-tva-
pūrṇa-tvaiḥ
```

> The Muni's marks are superior by reason of their proper placement, vivid
> manifestation, and completeness.

The Bhāṣya uses comparatives—`deśasthatarāṇi`, `uttaptatarāṇi`, and
`saṃpūrṇatarāṇi`. Thus the point is not that cakravartins lack the marks, but
that the Buddha's corresponding marks are more perfectly determined.

The second clause establishes an initial state and the cause of its gradual
transformation:

```text
prāk
    sattvāḥ
    rūpivat āsan

rasa-rāgāt
    tataḥ
    śanaiḥ [parihīyante / pariṇamante]
```

> Formerly beings were like rūpa-realm beings; thereafter, because of
> attachment to savor, [they changed or declined] gradually.

The final process-verb is elliptical in the Kārikā. The Bhāṣya supplies its
content stage by stage; the study translation therefore makes “declined”
explicit while preserving its commentary-dependent status.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The Muni's marks are superior through proper placement, vividness, and
> completeness. Formerly beings were like rūpa-realm beings; thereafter,
> because of attachment to savor, [they changed] gradually.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Buddha's marks surpass the corresponding marks of a cakravartin by
> being more exactly situated, more vividly manifest, and more completely
> developed. At the beginning of the kalpa, human beings resembled beings of
> the rūpa realm: they were mind-made, self-luminous, moved through the sky,
> fed on joy, and lived for a long time. When attachment to the newly appeared
> earth-savor arose, they gradually entered gross nourishment, heavier
> embodiment, environmental differentiation, sexual differentiation, and the
> social conditions of the present Human World.

The detailed qualities of first-kalpa beings and every stage after
`rasarāga` are supplied by the Bhāṣya and its cited sūtra, not enumerated in
the verse.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The Human World is not introduced as a finished natural arrangement. It has
> a genealogy. Initially subtle, luminous, joy-fed agents encounter a new
> sensible affordance. Savor becomes attachment; attachment becomes repeated
> appropriation; appropriation alters nourishment; nourishment alters the
> body; and altered embodiment reorganizes the appearing environment and the
> relations among agents. The World differentiates through the consequences
> of its agents' own operations.

`Rasa` alone is not the sufficient cause. The Bhāṣya first describes a
greed-prone being who smells, tastes, and consumes it; others then reproduce
the act. The operative determination is the copula of available object and
appropriative attachment.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Genetic sequence of the Human World

```text
rūpāvacara-like first-kalpa beings
    → mind-made, self-luminous, joy-fed embodiment

appearance of bhūmirasa
    → smell and taste
        → rasarāga
            → consumption and imitation
                → beginning of morsel-food

repeated gross nourishment
    → bodily hardness and heaviness
        → loss of self-luminosity
            → darkness becomes operative
                → sun and moon become manifest

attachment to successive food-forms
    → earth-savor disappears
        → earth-film appears and disappears
            → forest-creeper appears and disappears
                → naturally arising rice becomes nourishment

increasingly gross food
    → excretory channels
        + sexual organs
        + differentiated bodily configuration
            → reciprocal bodily perception
                → ayoniśomanaskāra
                    → kāmarāga
                        → misconduct
```

The logical form is not a collection of simultaneous properties. It is an
ordered dependency chain in which each achieved state supplies the conditions
for the next transition.

### Appropriation as feedback

```text
ObjectAvailable(rasa)
    ∧ Attachment(agent, rasa)
    ∧ RepeatedConsumption(agent, rasa)
        → BodyTransformation(agent)
        → EnvironmentTransformation(world)
        → NewObjectField(agent, world)
```

The agent acts upon the available World, but the transformed World returns as
a newly determined field of appearance and action. This reciprocal sequence
is the genetic engine of the passage.

### Marks as degrees of determination

```text
Buddha and cakravartin
    → both possess thirty-two marks

Buddha's marks
    → more exact placement
    + greater manifested intensity
    + fuller completion
        → lakṣaṇātiśaya
```

Difference here is intensive and structural, not the simple presence versus
absence of the shared predicate.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse turns from the classification of extraordinary agents to the
genesis of ordinary embodied agency. Its method is extraordinarily modern:
the body, environment, sensory field, affect, and social relation arise as a
coupled process.

```text
Agent appropriates World
    → appropriation modifies Agent
        → modified Agent encounters a modified World
            → new possibilities of appropriation arise
```

This is Semantic Copulation in a precise systems sense. `Rasa` is not merely
an inert object and `rāga` is not merely an interior state. Their operative
relation generates a new mode of nourishment, a new body, and eventually a
new shared World. Neither side remains what it was before entering the
relation.

The loss of self-luminosity is especially striking for the Enclosure theory.
Once agents no longer supply their own light, darkness becomes a meaningful
World-condition and sun and moon become necessary external regulators of
appearance. A change in embodiment therefore reorganizes the cosmic field as
experienced:

```text
self-luminous embodiment
    → no external luminary required for ordinary appearing

loss of luminosity
    → darkness
        → sun-and-moon regulated appearing
```

The Bhāṣya presents this as conditioned cosmological genealogy. The Organon
abstraction is that an enclosure is transformed when the capacities of its
agents change: what counts as environment, resource, light, difference, and
relation changes with them.
