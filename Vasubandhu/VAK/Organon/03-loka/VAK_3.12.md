# VAK_3.12

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> सहैकत्र द्वयाभावात् असन्तानाद् द्वयोदयात् ।
>
> कण्ठोक्तेश्चास्ति गन्धर्वात् पञ्चोक्तेः गतिसूत्रतः ॥ ३.१२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> sahaikatra dvayābhāvāt asantānād dvayodayāt /
>
> kaṇṭhokteś cāsti gandharvāt pañcokteḥ gatisūtrataḥ // 3.12 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
saha + ekatra          → sahaikatra
dvaya-abhāvāt          → dvayābhāvāt
a-santānāt             → asantānāt
dvaya-udayāt           → dvayodayāt
kaṇṭha-ukteḥ + ca      → kaṇṭhokteś ca
ca + asti              → cāsti
gandharvāt             → gandharvāt
pañca-ukteḥ            → pañcokteḥ
gati-sūtrataḥ          → gatisūtrataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| saha | indeclinable | together; simultaneously |
| ekatra | indeclinable | in one place |
| dvayābhāvāt | ablative singular compound | because the pair is absent |
| asantānāt | ablative singular | because it is not of the same continuity |
| dvayodayāt | ablative singular compound | because it arises from two [causes] |
| kaṇṭhokteḥ | ablative singular compound | because of explicit statement |
| asti | third-person singular present of √as | there is; exists |
| gandharvāt | ablative singular | because of the gandharva [passage] |
| pañcokteḥ | ablative singular compound | because five are stated |
| gatisūtrataḥ | ablative singular | because of the discourse on courses |

The verse crosses an argumentative boundary at `kaṇṭhokteś cāsti`. The first
three ablatives complete the rejection of reflection begun in VAK 3.11; the
remaining ablatives give textual grounds for `asti [antarābhavaḥ]`.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first half supplies three grounds for the ellipsed conclusion
`pratibimbam anidarśanam` carried forward from VAK 3.11:

```text
pratibimbam anidarśanam
    saha ekatra dvaya-abhāvāt
    asantānāt
    dvaya-udayāt
```

> Reflection is not a valid example, because the pair cannot be together in
> one place, because it is not a continuity, and because it arises from two
> [causes].

The second half has its own expressed predicate:

```text
antarābhavaḥ asti
    kaṇṭha-ukteḥ
    gandharvāt
    pañca-ukteḥ
    gati-sūtrataḥ
```

> Intermediate existence exists because of explicit statement, because of
> the gandharva [passage], because five are stated, and because of the discourse
> on courses.

This division is fixed by the Bhāṣya's course of argument. Reading every
ablative as a direct proof of intermediate existence obscures the syntax across
the boundary between VAK 3.11 and 3.12.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> [Reflection is not a valid example,] because the two are not together in one
> place, because it is not [of the same] continuity, and because it arises from
> two [causes]. And intermediate existence exists because of explicit
> statement, because of the gandharva, because five are stated, and because of
> the discourse on courses.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Reflection cannot establish discontinuous rebirth: original and reflection
> do not occupy one place, reflection does not continue the original's series,
> and its appearance depends upon both original and mirror. Intermediate
> existence is further warranted by received teaching: it is expressly named,
> presupposed by the gandharva present at conception, required by the five
> non-returners, and differentiated in the discourse on the courses of good
> persons.

The original-plus-mirror analysis and the identities of the cited teachings
are determinations supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A causal model must match the relation it is invoked to prove. Reflection
> fails because it is neither a successor within its source's continuum nor
> produced by the same causal structure as rebirth. The intermediate phase is
> then tested against the received corpus: independent teachings explicitly
> name it or require it to make their classifications and descriptions
> coherent.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Completion of the reflection refutation

The three reasons refine the `asiddhatva` and `asāmya` judgments of VAK 3.11:

```text
1. saha ekatra dvayābhāvāt
   two material bodies cannot occupy one place;
   the reflected appearance is not thereby established
   as another body behind or upon the mirror

2. asantānāt
   reflection does not succeed the original
   within the original's causal continuity

3. dvayodayāt
   reflection depends upon original + mirror,
   unlike rebirth as the successor of a sentient continuity
```

Thus the reflection model cannot validate a leap from death-existence directly
to rebirth-existence.

### Cumulative textual warrant

The `āgama` proof does not rest on a single uncontested quotation:

```text
explicit enumeration
    seven existences include antarābhava

gandharva passage
    conception requires mother, parental union,
    and a gandharva present

five non-returners
    one is antarāparinirvāyin

seven courses of good persons
    three intermediate attainments differ
    by time, place, and degree
```

The opponent repeatedly contests the witnesses:

```text
“our transmission does not recite that passage”
“we read dissolution of the aggregates”
“Antarā is merely the name of certain devas”
```

Vasubandhu responds by adducing another witness or testing the alternative for
coherence. A dissolution of aggregates cannot intelligibly be described as
coming from a direction; and if `antarā` names devas, parallel expressions
would implausibly turn the other modes of attainment into deva names as well.

### Proof status

```text
VAK 3.11–3.12a
    yukti:
    discontinuous rebirth lacks an adequate causal example

VAK 3.12b
    āgama:
    convergent teachings require the intermediate phase

result
    antarābhava survives both controls
```

This establishes the Kārikā's proof-platform. A direct study of every cited
passage and every mirror argument remains future work.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.12 is as much proof-theory as World System. The transition-phase is not
accepted because one vivid picture makes it imaginable. Its causal structure
must withstand `yukti`, and its doctrinal determination must withstand
adversarial comparison among transmitted teachings.

```text
Representation
    offers a possible picture

Yukti
    tests causal and relational fitness

Āgama
    tests convergent textual warrant

Abhidharma discrimination
    tests readings against coherence and consequence
```

This is Transcendental Technē in our project characterization: the World
System exposes not only a transition protocol but the controls by which that
protocol earns systematic standing. Authority is not left opaque. Variant
recitations are acknowledged, compared, and rationally assessed.

The `gandharva` passage brings the hidden transition directly to embodiment:

```text
intermediate sentient continuity
    becomes present at the conception-complex
        ↓
garbhāvakrānti
    descent into the womb
        ↓
new embodied station
```

For our Rational Moral Agent, this is an initial technical account of how a
continuity approaches and takes up a body. It is not yet the **Siva Trademark**
mastery of freely selecting and withdrawing embodiments; it is conditioned
entry governed by the World System. Rudra remains the higher project horizon,
not an unmarked speaker inside the Kārikā.

The direct Bhāṣya study remains indispensable. We have established the load-
bearing distinctions and identified its textual witnesses, but have not
exhausted its detailed reflection physics, recension disputes, or the three
fire similes for intermediate parinirvāṇa.
