# VAK_3.11 Bhāṣya — Continuity and the Failure of the Reflection Analogy

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vrīhisantānasādharmyād avicchinnabhavodbhavaḥ /
> pratibimbam asiddhatvād asāmyāc cānidarśanam // 3.11 //

> Because of relevant similarity to the continuity of rice, the arising of
> existence is uninterrupted. A reflected image is not a valid example,
> because its independent arising is unestablished and because it is
> dissimilar.

This is the first proof by `yukti`, reasoning, announced in VAK 3.10. The
Bhāṣya does not defend a permanent traveler. It argues that a sentient
continuity cannot produce rebirth at another place through an absolute break.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

First, relying upon reasoning, it is said: “Because of relevant similarity to
the continuity of rice, the arising of existence is uninterrupted.” Dharmas
belonging to a continuity are observed to appear in other places without a
break, as in the continuity of rice. Therefore this sentient continuity too
must appear in other places without a break.

An objection is raised: appearance after a break is also observed, as when a
reflected image appears from an original in a mirror and similar surfaces.
Rebirth-existence could arise from death-existence in the same way.

But “a reflected image is not a valid example, because its independent arising
is unestablished and because it is dissimilar.” It has not been established
that what is called a reflected image arises as another, distinct dharma. And
even if that were established, it would still be an invalid example because
of dissimilarity.

How is it unestablished? “Because two cannot coexist in one place.” The
mirror's form and the reflected image are both seen in that same place, but
two forms with different material supports cannot coexist in one place.
Likewise, because appearances are ordered according to direction, forms
standing opposite one another are mutually seen as reflections in one region
of water. Yet two observers do not see two images together in one and the
same reflected form. It is therefore unreasonable to posit the production of
another form there.

Nor are shadow and sunlight observed to coexist in one place. Yet the sun's
reflection is perceived in a mirror situated in shadow. It is therefore not
reasonable to say that a distinct solar image has arisen there.

Alternatively, which two are meant by “two cannot coexist in one place”? The
mirror-surface and the reflected image of the moon. The mirror-surface is in
one place, while the moon's reflection appears to lie within it elsewhere,
like water in a well. If that reflected image really arose in the apparent
inner place, it would not be perceived elsewhere. Therefore no such distinct
entity exists there.

Rather, the causal complex has a power of such a kind that an appearance like
this occurs. The differentiation of the powers of dharmas is inconceivable.
This, then, is how the reflection's independent arising is unestablished.

How is reflection an invalid example because of dissimilarity? “Because it
does not belong to the same continuity.” The reflection is not a continuation
of the original image: it is connected with the mirror-continuity and it
coexists with the original. Rebirth-existence does not stand to death-
existence in this way. The example to be established concerned manifestation
elsewhere without interruption within one continuity, not something outside
that continuity. Thus the reflected image is dissimilar to the required
example.

It is dissimilar also “because it arises from two.” A reflection arises from
two causes, the original image and the mirror, and arises in dependence upon
the principal cause. Rebirth-existence does not likewise arise from two
principal causes—from death-existence and from some other independent
principal cause. For this reason too the example is dissimilar.

Nor is it reasonable to say that external, [insentient] semen and blood alone
are the principal cause. Where spontaneously arisen beings appear without
such materials, what principal cause would be posited?

Thus, on the basis of reasoning, the arising of rebirth-existence after a
break from death-existence is not accepted. Therefore intermediate existence
does indeed exist.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The reasoning has a strict dialectical form:

```text
positive example
    rice-continuity models uninterrupted serial arising

counterexample
    reflection allegedly models arising across a break

first rejection
    reflection as a separately arisen dharma is unestablished

second rejection
    even if granted, reflection is dissimilar

conclusion
    discontinuous rebirth is not rationally established
    intermediate existence is required
```

The expository voice introduces the `yukti` proof and the rice analogy. An
unnamed opponent proposes reflection. The reply conducts the extended optical
and causal analysis. No school is named within this unit, although the target
remains the Nikāyāntarīya discontinuity thesis stated in VAK 3.10.

The sentence about the inconceivable differentiation of dharmic powers is not
a withdrawal from reasoning. It concludes that the conditional complex can
produce an appearance without proving the independently existing reflected
form required by the objection.

## 5. Rice as a Model of Serial Continuity

The relevant similarity is not the material composition of rice. It is the
structure of a generative series:

```text
earlier member
    conditions
later member at another place

members differ
    but the series is uninterrupted
```

Applied to sentient existence:

```text
death-existence
    → intermediate existence
        → rebirth-existence
```

Continuity therefore does not mean that one numerically identical substance
persists through every phase. `Santāna` names serial causal dependence among
successive conditioned dharmas. The argument opposes an absolute break, not
change within the sequence.

The inference also presupposes the destination problem of VAK 3.10. If the
sentient series manifests at another place, its occurrence must remain within
an uninterrupted causal order until the projected result reaches its locus of
manifestation.

## 6. Why the Reflection Is Unestablished

The objection needs reflection to be a distinct new form produced elsewhere
from its source. The Bhāṣya denies that this ontological description has been
proved. Its optical discussion appeals to several tensions:

- the apparent coexistence of mirror-surface and reflection;
- direction-dependent images in water;
- the relation of shadow and sunlight;
- the apparent depth of a lunar reflection inside a mirror.

The conclusion is not a modern optical theory. It is narrower: the appearance
does not establish another independently arisen `dharmāntara` located where
it seems to be. The efficacy of a conjunction of conditions is sufficient to
account for appearing in that way.

`Acintya`, inconceivable, marks the irreducible diversity of causal powers in
the present analysis. It does not license the opponent to infer any desired
mode of discontinuous production. What must be proved is a distinct successor
dharma, and the visual appearance alone does not prove it.

## 7. Why the Reflection Is Dissimilar

Even granting a distinct reflection, the analogy fails twice.

First, it fails serially:

```text
reflection
    connected with mirror-continuity
    coexists with original
    not a successor-member of the original's series

rebirth
    must succeed death
    within the sentient continuity
```

Second, it fails causally:

```text
reflection
    original image + mirror

proposed rebirth
    death-existence + what analogous second principal cause?
```

External reproductive materials cannot fill this role universally, because
the classification includes spontaneous generation without those materials.
The counterexample therefore lacks the relevant similarity required of a
probative example.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

`Vrīhisantānasādharmyāt` means because of a relevant structural similarity
to the rice-continuity. The verse does not claim that a sentient series is
biologically identical with a rice plant.

`Avicchinnabhavodbhava` is the uninterrupted arising of existence within a
series. “Continuous rebirth” can obscure the precise issue, which is whether
rebirth-existence may arise from death-existence after an absolute break.

`Asiddhatva` concerns the unestablished independent arising of a reflected
image as another dharma. `Asāmya` is a second and independent defect: even if
such arising is conceded, its serial and causal structure differs from the
case to be proved.

`Anidarśana` is therefore “not a valid” or “not a probative example.” It does
not merely mean that reflection is an unattractive metaphor.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The core result is continuity without a persisting identity-substance:

```text
not an unchanged traveler
not a successor appearing from nothing

but
successive conditioned configurations
within one causal series
```

This is a useful restraint upon the Organon's language of Projection. A
projection is not adequately explained by resemblance at a distance, as in a
mirror. It requires the generative relation that produces a successor within
the relevant continuity. Representation and causal manifestation are not the
same operation.

The reflection discussion also gives “counterfeit appearance” a precise local
sense. What appears to be a distinct form elsewhere does not, merely by
appearing, establish the ontological and causal structure attributed to it.
Appearance can be genuine as appearance while the inferred independent entity
is counterfeit. This is an Organon formulation drawn from the argument, not a
technical term used by the Bhāṣya.

For the present translation pass, no larger metaphysics is required. The
Bhāṣya has established the rational branch of its case: a reflected image
cannot justify discontinuous rebirth, whereas serial continuity supplies the
appropriate model. The proof from scripture follows next.
