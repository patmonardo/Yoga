# VAK_3.56

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> देहा विदेहाः कुरवः कौरवाश्चामरावराः ।
>
> अष्टौ तदन्तरद्वीपा गाठा उत्तरमन्त्रिणः ॥ ३.५६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> dehā videhāḥ kuravaḥ kauravāś cāmarāvarāḥ /
>
> aṣṭau tadantaradvīpā gāṭhā uttaramantriṇaḥ // 3.56 //

The Bhāṣya confirms the segmentation into eight names: Deha, Videha, Kuru,
Kaurava, Cāmara, Avara, Gāṭha, and Uttaramantriṇa. The local witness spellings
are retained pending comparison with a critical edition.

## 4. Grammar

The eight names stand in apposition to the class term:

```text
dehāḥ videhāḥ
kuravaḥ kauravāḥ
cāmarāḥ avarāḥ
gāṭhāḥ uttaramantriṇaḥ
    = aṣṭau tad-antara-dvīpāḥ
```

The compressed surface order separates `gāṭhāḥ uttaramantriṇaḥ` from the
earlier names so that `aṣṭau tadantaradvīpāḥ` can state their count and class.
The Bhāṣya reorganizes the list by parent-continent:

```text
Deha + Videha               → attendants of Pūrvavideha
Kuru + Kaurava              → attendants of Uttarakuru
Gāṭha + Uttaramantriṇa      → attendants of Aparagodānīya
Cāmara + Avara              → attendants of Jambūdvīpa
```

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Deha, Videha, Kuru, Kaurava, Cāmara, Avara, Gāṭha, and Uttaramantriṇa are
> their eight intermediate islands.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Each of the four great continents has two attendant islands: Deha and Videha
> belong to Pūrvavideha; Kuru and Kaurava to Uttarakuru; Gāṭha and
> Uttaramantriṇa to Aparagodānīya; and Cāmara and Avara to Jambūdvīpa. On the
> principal account, all eight are inhabited by humans. Another tradition says
> that one is inhabited by rākṣasas.

The exact pairings, attendant relation, habitation, and alternative rākṣasa
report are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The fourfold regional system expands without losing its organizing frame.
> Each principal continent generates a local pair of attendant regions, so a
> uniform branching rule produces eight further inhabited domains. These
> subordinate regions are neither anonymous gaps nor additional peers of the
> primary four: their identity includes a determinate relation to a parent
> region. The World System increases its particularity through structured
> association rather than through an ungoverned accumulation of places.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Uniform branching rule

```text
4 principal continents
    × 2 attendant islands per continent
        = 8 intermediate islands
```

The cardinality follows from a repeated association rule. The list can be
regenerated from the parent-pair matrix rather than stored as eight unrelated
regions.

### Relational identity

```text
attendant island identity
    = proper name
    + parent-continent relation
    + position within the continent system
```

`Parivāra` adds semantic structure that the bare name-list does not display.
To know which island a name denotes includes knowing the principal regional
system to which it belongs.

### Hierarchical expansion without type confusion

```text
continent system
    ├── principal continent
    │     ├── attendant island
    │     └── attendant island
    └── repeated four times
```

Both levels are `dvīpa` regions, but their roles differ. Type membership does
not erase hierarchy: principal and attendant remain distinct relational
positions inside the same larger class.

### Default rule and attributed exception

```text
principal account:
    all eight → human-inhabited

alternative account:
    one unspecified island → rākṣasa-inhabited
```

The exception must retain its provenance and limited scope. It neither changes
the main account silently nor licenses assigning rākṣasas to a named island
that the source leaves unspecified.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.56 offers a method of controlled system expansion:

```text
stable parent schema
    + uniform branching cardinality
        + explicit parent–child associations
            → increased regional particularity
```

The new islands do not require a second independent cosmological plan. They
extend the four-continent matrix by a constant branching factor of two. Yet
they are not mere duplicates, because every child retains its own name and
inhabitation while acquiring a relation to a specific parent.

This is valuable for the Semantic pipeline. A flat syntactic list contains
eight labels; the Bhāṣya recovers the relational DataFrame that makes the list
intelligible:

```ts
interface AttendantRegion {
  name: string;
  parentContinent: "east" | "north" | "west" | "south";
  role: "antaradvipa";
  inhabitants: "human" | "rakshasa-reported-alternative";
  authority: "principal" | "others-say";
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It captures the
difference between parsing the names and understanding their system. Semantic
discrimination requires association, role, scope, and provenance—not labels
alone.

The reported exception supplies a second methodological gain. A disciplined
knowledge system can preserve a default account and a dissenting variant
without forcing an artificial reconciliation. The alternative remains useful
because its authority and unresolved target are represented explicitly.
