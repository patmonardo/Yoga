# VAK_3.78 Bhāṣya — Human Lifespans Across the Continents

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
sahasram āyuḥ kuruṣu dvayor ardhārdhavarjitam /
ihāniyatam ante tu daśābdā ādito 'mitam // 3.78 //
```

> Lifespan among the Kurus is one thousand years; in the other two continents
> it is reduced successively by half. Here it is unfixed: ten years at the end,
> but immeasurable at the beginning.

The verse's opening is read as `sahasram āyuḥ`, following the Bhāṣya's
segmentation.

## Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Evaṃ pramāṇabhinnānāṃ kim āyuṣo 'py asti bhedaḥ?

Asti, ity āha:

Sahasram āyuḥ kuruṣu.

Varṣāṇām.

Dvayor ardhārdhavarjitam.

Dvayor dvīpayor ardhārdhaṃ varjayitvā pañca varṣaśatāni Godānīyānām;

ardhatṛtīye varṣaśate Pūrvavidehānām.

Iha aniyatam.

Jambūdvīpe nāsty āyuṣo niyamaḥ; kadācit bhūyo bhavati, kadācid alpīyaḥ.

Ante tu daśābdāḥ.

Abdaḥ saṃvatsaraḥ. Ante hīyamānaṃ daśa varṣāṇy āyur bhavati.

Ādito 'mitam.

Āditaḥ prāthamakalpikānāṃ manuṣyāṇām aparimāṇam āyur bhavati.

Sahasrādisaṃkhyayā parimātuṃ na śakyate.
```

Spacing and sandhi have been regularized. `Ardhatṛtīye varṣaśate` is
translated as 250 years, in line with the preceding halving and the Bhāṣya's
construction of the continental sequence.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Just as the measures differ, do lifespans also differ? They do, the Bhāṣya
says.

Among the Kurus, lifespan is one thousand years. In the other two continents
it is reduced successively by half: five hundred years for the Godānīyas and
two hundred and fifty for the Pūrvavidehas.

Here, in Jambūdvīpa, there is no fixed lifespan; sometimes it is greater,
sometimes smaller. At the end, as it declines, lifespan becomes ten years—a
year being a full year. At the beginning, however, the first humans of the
kalpa have an immeasurable lifespan. It cannot be measured by numerical
reckoning beginning with a thousand.

## Natural Movement

Following the previous discussion of bodily measures, the Bhāṣya asks whether
lifespans also differ. It first assigns a fixed descending series to three
continents: 1,000 years among the Kurus, 500 among the Godānīyas, and 250
among the Pūrvavidehas. It then distinguishes Jambūdvīpa, where lifespan has
no fixed value and changes over the kalpa. The ten-year terminal lifespan
and immeasurable lifespan of the first humans mark the two ends of that
temporal change.

## Fixed and Changing Lifespans

| Human domain | Lifespan | Determination |
|---|---:|---|
| Uttarakuru (“the Kurus”) | 1,000 years | Fixed |
| Godānīya | 500 years | First halving |
| Pūrvavideha | 250 years | Second halving |
| Jambūdvīpa | Unfixed | Varies over the kalpa |
| Jambūdvīpa, terminal decline | 10 years | Explicit minimum at the end |
| First humans of the kalpa | Immeasurable | Not numerically measurable by counts beginning with thousands |

The prose's `kadācit bhūyo bhavati kadācid alpīyaḥ` says that lifespan is
sometimes greater and sometimes less. The two endpoints are kalpic conditions,
not a claim that one person's lifespan moves between them during an individual
life.

## Doctrinal Note

Three continental populations receive fixed lifespans related by successive
halving. Jambūdvīpa instead has an unfixed lifespan across the kalpa's
temporal course. The Bhāṣya explains `amita` not simply as “very long” but as
`aparimāṇa`: beyond numerical measurement, even by a reckoning that begins
with thousands.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: the text distinguishes a fixed temporal parameter from one
that varies with the world's phase. This is a project-level observation, not
Vasubandhu's technical terminology.
