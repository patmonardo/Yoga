# VAK_3.66

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> मध्ये सार्धद्विसाहस्रपार्श्वमध्यर्धयोजनम् ।
>
> पुरं सुदर्शनं नाम हैमं चित्रतलं मृदु ॥ ३.६६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> madhye sārdhadvisāhasrapārśvam adhyardhayojanam /
>
> puraṃ sudarśanaṃ nāma haimaṃ citratalaṃ mṛdu // 3.66 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
madhye                     → madhye
sārdha-dvi-sāhasra-pārśvam → sārdhadvisāhasrapārśvam
adhyardha-yojanam          → adhyardhayojanam
puram                      → puraṃ
sudarśanam                 → sudarśanaṃ
nāma                       → nāma
haimam                     → haimaṃ
citra-talam                → citratalaṃ
mṛdu                       → mṛdu
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| madhye | locative neuter singular | in the center, namely of Meru's summit |
| sārdhadvisāhasrapārśvam | nominative neuter singular compound | having each side measure two thousand five hundred |
| adhyardhayojanam | nominative neuter singular compound | having a height of one and one-half yojanas |
| puram | nominative neuter singular | city |
| sudarśanam | nominative neuter singular | Sudarśana, literally beautiful to behold |
| nāma | indeclinable | named, called |
| haimam | nominative neuter singular adjective | golden / made of gold |
| citratalam | nominative neuter singular compound | having a variegated ground or surface |
| mṛdu | nominative neuter singular adjective | soft |

The numerical compound `sārdhadvisāhasra` is resolved by the Bhāṣya as 2,500,
not merely as an unspecified “two thousand with a half.” The second measure is
explicitly assigned to height in the commentary.

## 4. Grammar

The locative places the city at the center of the summit established in 3.65:

```text
[sumerutalasya] madhye
    puram [asti]
```

The remaining nominative neuter forms qualify that city:

```text
puram
    sudarśanam nāma
    sārdha-dvi-sāhasra-pārśvam
    adhyardha-yojanam
    haimam
    citra-talam
    mṛdu
```

> In the center is a city named Sudarśana, 2,500 yojanas on each side and 1½
> yojanas high, golden, variegated in surface, and soft.

The verse predicates `haima`, `citratala`, and `mṛdu` of the city in compressed
form. The Bhāṣya distributes these determinations more precisely: its enclosing
wall is golden; its ground is variegated with 101 substances; and that ground
is soft to the touch.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> In the center is the city named Sudarśana, having sides of 2,500 yojanas and
> a height of 1½ yojanas; it is golden, variegated in surface, and soft.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> At the center of Meru's summit stands the city Sudarśana, Śakra's capital.
> Each side measures 2,500 yojanas and its height is 1½ yojanas. Its enclosing
> wall is golden, and its ground is variegated with 101 substances. The ground
> is soft to the touch like cotton fluff: it sinks when a foot is placed upon
> it and rises again when the foot is lifted.

The assignment of the measures to side and height, golden wall, 101 ground
constituents, cotton-fluff comparison, action-responsive surface, and
identification as Śakra's capital are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The directional summit-domain of 3.65 now receives a determinate center.
> Sudarśana is specified simultaneously as geometrical enclosure, named city,
> materially differentiated surface, sensuous environment, and political
> capital. Its softness is not merely an isolated quality: the Bhāṣya states a
> rule relating bodily movement to environmental response. Thus produced Loka
> becomes a practical habitation-field in which an embodied action and the
> state of its support are reciprocally coordinated.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Progressive specification of the city

```text
summit domain
    → center
        → named city
            → dimensions
            → enclosure material
            → ground composition
            → tactile disposition
            → political function
```

Each predicate answers a different question about one civic domain. Location,
measure, material, sensation, and function remain semantically distinct.

### Dimensional schema

```text
each side = 2,500 yojanas
height    = 1.5 yojanas
```

The Bhāṣya removes the ambiguity of the Kārikā's compressed compounds by
typing one value as horizontal side-length and the other as vertical height.

### Material articulation

```text
city boundary:
    golden wall

city ground:
    variegated by 101 constituents
    soft like cotton fluff
```

The enclosing boundary and supporting surface are determined independently.
Calling the city simply “golden” would lose this Bhāṣya-level distinction.

### Action–response law

Let `G` be the local state of the ground under an inhabitant's foot:

```text
foot placed:
    G → depressed

foot lifted:
    G → elevated/restored
```

The ground's disposition is manifested under an operation. Its practical
meaning is expressed by a conditional transition, not exhausted by the static
predicate “soft.”

### Centrality and political function

```text
spatial center of summit
    + capital of Śakra
        → centrality across two relation-types
```

The spatial predicate `located-at-center` and the political predicate
`capital-of` converge on Sudarśana without becoming identical.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.66 gives us a compact model of progressive concretion:

```text
coordinate field
    → selected center
        → constructed civic domain
            → embodied interaction
```

This is the most useful abstraction behind the ornamental description. A
world-domain is not adequately specified merely by assigning it coordinates.
It becomes habitable through boundaries, differentiated materials, perceptible
qualities, and lawful responses to the actions of its inhabitants.

The Bhāṣya's treatment of softness is especially important. `Mṛdu` begins as
a quality-word, but the commentary expands it into operational semantics:

```text
if foot is placed, then surface sinks
if foot is lifted, then surface rises
```

The quality is known through the transformations it supports. Physical-world
description thereby becomes Agential without ceasing to be physical: the
Agent does not invent the ground, but its operation discloses and activates a
determinate disposition of the ground.

```ts
interface SudarsanaGround {
  constituentCount: 101;
  tactileQuality: "SoftLikeCottonFluff";
  transition(action: "FootPlaced" | "FootLifted"): "Depressed" | "Elevated";
}

interface SudarsanaCity {
  location: "CenterOfMeruSummit";
  sideLengthYojanas: 2500;
  heightYojanas: 1.5;
  wallMaterial: "Gold";
  politicalFunction: "CapitalOfSakra";
  ground: SudarsanaGround;
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It
preserves the difference between an attributed static quality and the
Bhāṣya's explicit action-dependent behavior.

The name Sudarśana and the variegated ground also establish a visible and
tactile refinement proper to this deva habitation. That refinement remains a
determination within produced Loka; the verse does not convert aesthetic
perfection into liberation.
