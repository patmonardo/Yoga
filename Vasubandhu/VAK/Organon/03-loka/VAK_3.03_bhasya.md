# VAK_3.03 Bhāṣya — The Formless Domain Has No Station

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ārūpyadhātur asthānaḥ upapattyā caturvidhaḥ /
> nikāyaṃ jīvitaṃ cātra niśritā cittasantatiḥ // 3.03 //

> The formless-domain has no spatial station, yet it is fourfold according to
> rebirth. In this domain, the continuity of consciousness is said to depend
> upon the common class-character and the life-faculty.

“Spatial” and the expanded terms “common class-character” and “life-faculty”
come from the Bhāṣya. “Is said to” is also necessary: the commentary first
gives the Ābhidhārmika interpretation of the verse and then subjects its
support-model to sustained criticism.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The formless-domain has no station. For formless dharmas have no spatial
station. It is the rule that past and future dharmas, avijñapti, and formless
dharmas are not spatially situated.

Nevertheless, it is fourfold according to rebirth. The formless-domain is
fourfold through differentiation of rebirth: namely, the sphere of the
infinitude of space, the sphere of the infinitude of differentiated cognition,
the sphere of nothingness, and the sphere of neither perception nor
non-perception. But these are not distinguished as higher and lower by spatial
location. In whatever place those who have attained the corresponding
attainment die, in that very place they are reborn. And when they later die
from that existence, their intermediate existence arises in that same place.

Just as the continuity of consciousness among beings possessing form proceeds
in dependence upon form, upon what does it proceed among beings in the
formless-domain?

“Here the continuity of consciousness depends upon class and life.” The
Ābhidhārmikas explain this as dependence upon the common class-character and
the life-faculty.

If so, why does the continuity of consciousness among beings possessing form
not proceed in dependence upon those same two? Because they are weak. What
makes them strong in the formless-domain? Their arising from a distinctive
attainment. For that attainment has removed the representation of form. If
they are strong for precisely that reason, however, the continuity should
proceed by that strength itself; what need is there of a further support?

Another difficulty must also be stated. Just as the common class-character and
the life-faculty among beings possessing form proceed in dependence upon form,
upon what do they proceed among formless beings? The same two support one
another. Then why do those same two not support one another among beings
possessing form? Because they are weak there. What, then, makes them strong in
the formless-domain? Their arising from a distinctive attainment. But this
applies equally to the continuity of consciousness, or to consciousness and
the mental factors themselves.

The Sautrāntikas therefore say that there is no such mutual support for the
continuity of consciousness among formless beings. Rather, when the projecting
cause of a continuity of consciousness has not become free from craving for
form, that continuity arises together with form and proceeds in dependence
upon form. But when its cause has become free from craving for form, it
proceeds without requiring form, because its cause has turned away from form.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The passage moves through two related problems:

```text
no spatial station
    yet fourfold differentiation by rebirth

no material support
    yet continuity of consciousness
```

The opening expository voice explains `asthāna`, enumerates the four rebirths,
and frames the question of continuity. The Ābhidhārmikas are explicitly named
as interpreting `nikāya` and `jīvita` as `nikāyasabhāga` and `jīvitendriya`.
A questioning voice then tests this interpretation through repeated symmetry
arguments. The final rejection and causal alternative are explicitly
attributed to the Sautrāntikas.

The individual objections need not all be assigned personally to Vasubandhu.
The text marks the two school positions, but it does not separately identify
the speaker of every intervening question and reply.

## 5. Non-stationed Yet Fourfold by Rebirth

`Asthāna` is not merely a statement that the formless-domain is subtle or
distant. The prose gives it a precise scope: formless dharmas are
`adeśastha`, not situated in a spatial place. It places them in this respect
with past dharmas, future dharmas, and avijñapti.

Fourfoldness nevertheless remains. Its differentiating principle is not
`deśa`, place, but `upapattibheda`, difference of rebirth:

| Formless rebirth | Controlled rendering |
|---|---|
| `ākāśānantyāyatana` | sphere of the infinitude of space |
| `vijñānānantyāyatana` | sphere of the infinitude of differentiated cognition |
| `ākiñcanyāyatana` | sphere of nothingness |
| `naivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñāyatana` | sphere of neither perception nor non-perception |

The Bhāṣya expressly denies that their higher and lower order is produced by
spatial location. Its remarks about death, rebirth, and intermediate existence
do not covertly restore four formless places. They explain why a being's
formless rebirth and subsequent intermediate existence arise in the place
where the relevant transition occurs, although the formless dharmas
themselves are not spatially stationed there.

## 6. The Ābhidhārmika Support-Model

Removal of material station produces a further question. In form-bearing
beings, the continuity of consciousness proceeds in dependence upon form. If
there is no form in ārūpya, what bears the corresponding continuity?

The Ābhidhārmika answer expands the Kārikā's compressed terms:

```text
nikāya  → nikāyasabhāga   common class-character
jīvita  → jīvitendriya    life-faculty

cittasantati
    depends upon both
```

Neither term should be converted into an enduring self or an immaterial
substance. They are conditioned dharmas proposed as supports within this
doctrinal account. The reply that they are strong because they arise from a
distinctive attainment adds a genetic condition: the relevant attainment has
cleared away the representation of form.

That reply does not settle the matter. If the attainment can confer sufficient
strength, the critic asks why that strength cannot belong directly to the
continuity whose occurrence is being explained. The proposed supports begin
to look explanatorily redundant.

## 7. From Mutual Support to a Genetic Account

The second objection presses more deeply. In embodied existence,
`nikāyasabhāga` and `jīvitendriya` themselves depend upon form. Asked what
supports them without form, the Ābhidhārmika reply makes the two support one
another. This produces a symmetry problem:

```text
if mutual support suffices in the formless-domain,
why does it not suffice among beings possessing form?
```

The renewed appeal to their strength from a distinctive attainment does not
break the symmetry, because that explanation can equally be applied to the
continuity of consciousness—or to consciousness and its mental factors.

The Sautrāntika conclusion replaces the reciprocal support-pair with a causal
account:

```text
projecting cause not free from craving for form
    → continuity arises together with form
    → continuity depends upon form

projecting cause free from craving for form
    → continuity arises without form
    → no material support is required
```

`Ākṣepahetu`, the projecting or impelling cause, does not here mean a free
subject projecting an appearance. It is the prior causal determinant of the
continuum. Whether that cause remains oriented toward form explains whether
the resulting continuity arises with form or without it.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya determines `asthāna` as absence of spatial station. It does not
deny order, causality, rebirth, or continuity. Hence “without station” must not
be paraphrased as unconditioned, omnipresent, or absolute.

`Upapattyā caturvidhaḥ` is resolved as fourfold through differences of
rebirth. The four attainments supply the names of those rebirths, while the
prose blocks their interpretation as four vertically arranged places.

The second half of the verse receives an explicitly Ābhidhārmika gloss:
`nikāya` is `nikāyasabhāga`, and `jīvita` is `jīvitendriya`. Because the
commentary subsequently rejects the proposed mutual-support explanation, the
translation should not present this gloss as the uncontested final doctrine.
“Is said to depend” preserves both the verse's assertion and its dialectical
status in the Bhāṣya.

Finally, the Sautrāntika argument does not deny conditioned continuity. It
denies that continuity without form requires the stated pair of mutually
sustaining supports. Its alternative ground is the causal orientation of the
series that gives rise to the continuity.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The opening sequence of Lokanirdeśa has now successively loosened the equation
of world with place:

```text
VAK 3.01 — desire-domain
    sentient orders, locations, and supporting receptacle-world

VAK 3.02 — form-domain
    stations articulated through dhyāna-levels

VAK 3.03 — formless-domain
    spatial station negated
    differentiation retained through rebirth
    continuity retained through causal genesis
```

The strong result is that `loka` survives the negation of location. A world in
the Kośa sense is not fundamentally a physical container. The formless-domain
remains within conditioned existence because it still has rebirth,
consciousness-continuity, and a cause determining the mode of its arising.

The Sautrāntika move is especially important for the Organon reconstruction.
It shifts the question from an inventory of hidden supports to the genesis of
the continuum: not “what immaterial thing holds this series up?” but “what
causal orientation projects or impels this series with or without form?” This
is a textual use of `ākṣepa`, not yet the Organon's more general theory of
Projection or Outflow.

Accordingly, ārūpya cannot simply be identified with the Absolute. It is a
maximal abstraction from material station internal to the World System, not
an escape from conditioned genesis. That boundary is an Organon consequence
drawn from the text's retained `upapatti`, `cittasantati`, and `hetu`; it is
not wording placed in Vasubandhu's mouth.
