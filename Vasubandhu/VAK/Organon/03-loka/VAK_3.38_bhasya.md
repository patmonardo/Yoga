# VAK_3.38 Bhāṣya — Rebirth-Linking and the Persistence of the World

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
upapattibhavaḥ kliṣṭaḥ sarvakleśaiḥ svabhūmikaiḥ /
tridhā 'nye traya ārūpye āhārasthitikaṃ jagat // 3.38 //
```

> Rebirth-becoming is defiled by all afflictions belonging to its own level.
> The other three are threefold. In the formless realm there are three. The
> world persists through nutriment.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes rebirth-becoming from the other three modes,
explains why intermediate becoming is absent from the formless realm, and
then changes the governing question from the arising of sentient beings to
their continued persistence.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

The running witness has multiple mechanical corruptions in compounds and
case endings, especially at 151.21–24, and a damaged verb in the sūtra
introduction. The continuous text regularizes these provisionally. The exact
relation between `kleśa` and `paryavasthāna` and the wording of the cited
sūtra require critical comparison.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Rebirth-becoming is defiled—necessarily so. By which affliction? By all the
afflictions belonging to its own level. Whatever level the rebirth-becoming
belongs to, it is bound by all the afflictions of that same level. The
Abhidharmikas say that there is no affliction by which rebirth-linking cannot
be effected. It is effected by the afflictions themselves, however, not by
independent active outbreaks taken as autonomous factors.

Although the state at that moment is weak, whichever affliction has been
frequently enacted in a given case and is near at hand is the very affliction
that then presents itself, through the impulse of what preceded. Linking into
intermediate becoming must likewise be understood as necessarily defiled.

The other three modes of becoming are threefold. The three beginning with
intermediate becoming may be wholesome, defiled, or indeterminate.

Which of these modes belongs to which realm? In the formless realm there are
three, intermediate becoming being excluded. The formless realm is not
delimited as another spatial place for whose attainment an intermediate
becoming would have to arise. Since the desire and form realms are not
similarly excluded, it is thereby allowed that all four modes exist in them.

It has now been explained in detail how sentient beings arise through
dependent co-arising. How, then, do sentient beings persist? The answer is:
the world persists through nutriment. The Blessed One, having directly known
and fully understood this single dharma, declared the sūtra statement, “All
sentient beings persist through nutriment.” What, then, are the nutriments?
There are four nutriments.

## 4. Argument and Voices

The unit makes four moves:

1. Rebirth-becoming is necessarily defiled by an affliction belonging to its
   own level.
2. The Abhidharmikas hold that every same-level affliction can bind
   rebirth-linking; habitual and proximate affliction becomes manifest through
   prior momentum.
3. The other modes admit three ethical statuses, while the formless realm
   lacks intermediate becoming because it is not another spatially delimited
   place.
4. The exposition changes from the production of sentient beings through
   dependent co-arising to their maintenance through nutriment.

The universal rebirth-linking claim is explicitly attributed to the
Abhidharmikas. No objector speaks in this unit. The four nutriments are only
announced at the endpoint; their determination belongs to the next Study.

## 5. Rebirth-Being Is Necessarily Defiled

The ethical asymmetry among the four becomings is:

```text
upapattibhava
    necessarily kliṣṭa

antarābhava
pūrvakālabhava
maraṇabhava
    each may be kuśala, kliṣṭa, or avyākṛta
```

Rebirth-becoming is the establishment or binding of a renewed series and is
therefore never ethically neutral in the present account. The same necessity
applies to the linking into intermediate becoming.

`Svabhūmika` restricts the afflictive condition by level. The relevant
affliction belongs to the same ground as the rebirth-being it binds. “All
afflictions” therefore does not erase the architecture of levels or claim
that an affliction from any arbitrary ground directly binds every rebirth.

## 6. Habit, Proximity, and Prior Momentum

The state at rebirth-linking is described as weak, yet its particular
afflictive determination is not random:

```text
frequent prior enactment
    + present proximity
    + pūrvāvedha
      impulse from what preceded
        → the affliction that presents itself at linking
```

This passage distinguishes the affliction as a causal disposition from an
independent active outbreak. The transmitted wording is difficult, but the
Bhāṣya clearly refuses to treat rebirth-linking as an unconditioned selection
among possible afflictions.

The present state may be slight while still carrying the directional force
of a repeatedly enacted history. Manifest intensity and conditioning efficacy
are therefore different axes.

## 7. Why the Formless Realm Has Only Three Becomings

The realm distribution is:

```text
desire realm
    all four becomings

form realm
    all four becomings

formless realm
    rebirth + life-course + death
    no intermediate becoming
```

The reason is functional rather than merely numerical. Intermediate becoming
serves passage toward another spatially delimited destination. The formless
realm is not delimited as another place in that sense, so no intermediate
body or mode is produced for reaching it.

This keeps topology and ontology distinct. The omission of intermediate
becoming does not mean that the remaining three are compressed into a single
event; it means that one transitional function has no work to perform under
formless conditions.

## 8. From Arising to Persistence

The final pāda establishes a new governing distinction:

```text
pratītyasamutpāda
    explains how sentient beings arise

āhāra
    explains how arisen sentient beings persist
```

Production and maintenance are not the same relation. Conditions sufficient
to account for the arising of a process do not thereby explain its continued
station.

`Jagat` here is the sentient world addressed by the cited statement that all
sentient beings persist through nutriment. “Nutriment” cannot yet be reduced
to edible matter: the prose announces four kinds whose identities and
functions remain to be explained in the following unit.

The natural boundary therefore includes the question and numerical answer—
there are four nutriments—but not their subsequent analysis.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

This Study gives the World System two irreducible laws:

```text
genesis
    dependent co-arising

maintenance
    nutriment
```

For the Organon, the distinction prevents the architecture from confusing
construction with persistence. A Study may be generated once, but its
continued intelligibility depends on maintained links to Source, vocabulary,
argument, revision state, and readers capable of taking it up. Generation
does not automatically preserve a meaningful artifact.

The account of rebirth-linking also sharpens the project's theory of Learning.
A weak present event can be selected by a strong history of repeated
operation. In modern terms, prior traversal creates momentum that biases what
becomes active at the next station. This is an Organon analogy; the source
speaks more exactly of frequent enactment, proximity, and `pūrvāvedha`.

Crucially, the other phases remain ethically threefold. A defiled linking
condition does not entail that every later operation in the life-course is
defiled. The architecture preserves the possibility of wholesome action
within a series whose entry was afflictively conditioned.

Finally, the formless exception demonstrates functional modeling. A stage is
not retained merely because a generic lifecycle template contains it. Where
there is no spatial passage for intermediate becoming to perform, the stage
is absent. An Ocular workflow should likewise justify each transition by its
function rather than instantiate empty stages for formal symmetry.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit at 151.19–152.10: necessarily
defiled rebirth-being, same-level afflictions, habitual momentum at linking,
the threefold status of the other becomings, the absence of intermediate
becoming from the formless realm, and the transition from arising to
persistence through nutriment.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The corrupted wording of
the rebirth-linking claim, the distinction between `kleśa` and
`paryavasthāna`, `mandikā`, `pūrvāvedha`, the spatial argument for the
formless realm, and the sūtra citation require comparison with critical
Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established translations. The Study ends with
the announcement of four nutriments and does not begin their analysis in VAK
3.39.
