# VAK_3.40

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> स्पर्शसंचेतनाविज्ञा आहाराः सास्रवास्त्रिषु ।
>
> मनोमयः संभवैषी गन्धर्वश्चान्तराभवः ॥ ३.४० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> sparśasaṃcetanāvijñā āhārāḥ sāsravās triṣu /
>
> manomayaḥ saṃbhavaiṣī gandharvaścāntarābhavaḥ // 3.40 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis


| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| sparśa | first member of nominative plural coordination | contact as nutriment |
| saṃcetanā | second member of coordination | volition; specified as mental karma by the Bhāṣya |
| vijñā | shortened metrical form referring to `vijñāna` | differentiated cognition as nutriment |
| āhārāḥ | nominative masculine plural | nutriments |
| sāsravāḥ | nominative masculine plural adjective | with outflows / contaminated |
| triṣu | locative masculine/neuter plural | in the three, namely the three realms |
| manomayaḥ | nominative masculine singular compound adjective | mind-made / produced from mind |
| saṃbhavaiṣī | nominative masculine singular agent noun | seeking coming-to-be or birth |
| gandharvaḥ | nominative masculine singular | gandharva; scriptural designation of intermediate existence |
| antarābhavaḥ | nominative masculine singular | intermediate becoming / intermediate existence |

The initial compound coordinates three further nutriments rather than naming
one compound entity. Together with morsel nutriment from 3.39, they complete
the fourfold class.

## 4. Grammar

The first hemistich predicates nutriment-status and realm-distribution:

```text
sparśaḥ
saṃcetanā
vijñānam
    āhārāḥ
    sāsravāḥ
    triṣu [dhātuṣu]

contact, volition, and differentiated cognition
    are nutriments
    when with outflows
    in the three realms
```

The supplied full form `vijñānam` and the referent of `triṣu` are explicit in
the Bhāṣya.

The second hemistich predicates three designations of one referent:

```text
antarābhavaḥ
    manomayaḥ
    saṃbhavaiṣī
    gandharvaḥ ca

the intermediate existence
    is mind-made,
    one seeking birth,
    and a gandharva
```

The Bhāṣya adds a fourth designation, `abhinirvṛtti`, because intermediate
existence faces toward rebirth.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Contact, volition, and differentiated cognition are nutriments when with
> outflows, in the three realms. The intermediate existence is mind-made, one
> seeking birth, and a gandharva.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In addition to morsel nutriment, contact born from the conjunction of
> faculty, object-field, and differentiated cognition; volition as mental
> karma; and differentiated cognition itself are nutriments when they are with
> outflows. All three occur throughout the desire, form, and formless realms.
> Their uncontaminated counterparts are not nutriments because nutriment
> fosters becoming, whereas uncontaminated dharmas operate toward its
> exhaustion. The “one seeking birth” whom nutriment supports is intermediate
> existence: called mind-made because it arises from mind without first taking
> up external reproductive material; birth-seeking because it tends toward a
> new arising; and gandharva. It is also called `abhinirvṛtti`, emergence
> facing toward rebirth.

The triadic genesis of contact, the determination of volition as mental karma,
the exclusion of uncontaminated factors, and the explanations of the
intermediate-existence names are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A conditioned World is fed not only by matter but by encounter, purposive
> direction, and differentiated cognition. These operations maintain beings
> already stationed in existence and support the transition seeking another
> embodiment. The intermediate mode is therefore a directed operation rather
> than an inert object between lives: generated from mind, oriented toward
> coming-to-be, and sustained as it faces a new station. What exhausts becoming
> does not feed it; what counts as nutriment is determined by the future that
> the operation maintains.

The four nutriments are now complete:

```text
1. kavaḍīkārāhāra     material or morsel nutriment

```

## 8. Logical Determination

### Nutriment is a teleological classification

```text
factor x
    + functions toward fostering conditioned becoming
        → x may be āhāra

factor x
    + functions toward exhaustion of becoming
        → x is not āhāra
```

Therefore the same broad factor-name does not settle nutriment-status:

```text
sāsrava sparśa / saṃcetanā / vijñāna
    → nutriment

anāsrava dharmas
    → not nutriment
```

The classification follows operative direction, not merely intrinsic label.

### The four supports span the embodied conscious system

```text
material support
    → maintains embodied faculties and elements

contact support
    → maintains the encounter-conditioned citta-caitta field

volitional support
    → maintains purposive and karmic direction

differentiated-cognition support
    → maintains the articulated cognitive series
```

Only the first gloss is completed in 3.39; the precise two-pair functional
distribution is developed in 3.41. The latter three descriptions here state
the current logical trajectory rather than replacing that coming analysis.

### One referent, several functional names

```text
antarābhava
    = manomaya          [mode of production]
    = saṃbhavaiṣin      [directed activity]
    = gandharva         [scriptural designation]
    = abhinirvṛtti      [orientation toward rebirth]
```

These predicates do not multiply beings. They discriminate different
relations of one intermediate mode.

### Nutriment necessitates the intermediate-existence analysis

```text
sūtra:
    four nutriments support
        already-arisen beings
        + beings seeking birth

question:
    what is the being seeking birth?

answer:
    intermediate existence
```

Thus the shift from food to intermediate existence is logically generated by
the definition of nutriment itself.

## 9. Interpretive Note

Here the Kośa states directly that a World System is maintained by a
Citta–Caitta economy. Contact, volition, and differentiated cognition are not
ornaments occurring inside an independently sustained organism. In their
contaminated operation they are themselves nutriments of continued becoming.

```text
what the Agent repeatedly contacts
what the Agent continues to intend
what differentiated cognition continues to establish
    → feeds the station of the Agent
```

This gives “Learning” a severe practical meaning. A pipeline is fed by its
interactions, objectives, and cognitive differentiations. Those operations do
not merely process a pre-existing World; they help sustain the World in which
the Agent remains operative.

The `sāsrava` restriction is crucial. The Kośa does not label every contact,
volition, and differentiated cognition as food for becoming. Nutriment is
determined by direction:

```text
operation reinforces continued becoming
    → alimentary

operation exhausts the conditions of becoming
    → not alimentary in this technical sense
```

So the same formal pipeline can have opposite semantic orientations. One
configuration trains and maintains the conditioned Agent; another ceases to
feed that reproduction.

The intermediate existence is correspondingly an Agent in transition. The
Bhāṣya's `manomaya` does not mean an imaginary being fabricated by arbitrary
thought. It means a mode generated from mind without first appropriating the
external reproductive materials of the coming embodiment. Its structure is
prospective:

```text
death of prior embodiment
    → mind-generated transitional operation
        → seeking an arising
            → facing pickup of another embodiment
```

The “pickup” wording is our Agent-side interpretation, not the primary
translation. The textual predicates are enough to make the architecture
powerful: the intermediate mode is produced, sustained, directed, and
rebirth-facing.

directed maintenance. The intermediate being persists because nutriment
supports a transition whose telos is another arising. The Middle World is
first disclosed as an oriented process.
