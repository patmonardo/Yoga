# VAK_3.16 Bhāṣya — Four Cognition-Profiles of Womb-Descent

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> saṃprajānan viśaty ekas tiṣṭhaty apy aparo 'paraḥ /
> niṣkrāmaty api sarvāṇi mūḍho 'nyo nityam aṇḍajaḥ // 3.16 //

> One enters with clear awareness; another also remains with clear awareness;
> a further one also emerges with clear awareness. Another is deluded through
> all three; one who will be egg-born is always deluded.

The Bhāṣya restricts the preceding account of inverted cognition. Womb-descent
does not invariably occur in delusion: its three phases—entry, residence, and
emergence—permit four progressively differentiated cognition-profiles.

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The e-text's `nityāha` has been

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Concerning the statement “with inverted understanding, it goes”: must every
intermediate being necessarily enter its mother's womb in that way? No, he
says. What then? Four descents into the womb are taught in the sūtra. What are
the four?

“One enters with clear awareness.” He remains and emerges without clear
awareness.

“Another also remains.” The expression “with clear awareness” continues to
govern the sentence; and from the word “also,” it follows that he also enters
with clear awareness.

“A further one also emerges.” He enters with clear awareness and also remains
with clear awareness.

“Another is deluded through all three.” A certain being performs all three
without clear awareness: without clear awareness he enters, remains, and also
emerges. These four descents into the womb are stated in reverse order so as
to conform to the construction of the verse.

“One who will be egg-born is always deluded.” An egg-born being always
performs all three in a deluded state. How does one born from an egg enter a
womb? One who is going to be born from an egg is also called egg-born. Or
designations are made according to a future state as well. Thus in a sūtra
one “constructs what is conditioned,” and in ordinary speech one “cooks rice”
or “grinds meal,” although the finished thing is still being produced. Hence
there is no fault.

How, then, does one enter the mother's womb without clear awareness and
continue so until emerging? And how does one do so with clear awareness?

For a being of little status, while it enters the mother's womb two inversions
arise—recognition and determination: “The wind is blowing; the sky is raining;
it is cold and overcast; there is the uproar of a great crowd. Ah! I am
entering dense grass or a dense forest, a grass hut or a leaf hut; I am
creeping beneath the root of a tree or the foot of a wall.” While remaining,
it thinks, “I remain among these”; while emerging, “I am going out from these.”

For a being of great status the thought is: “I am entering a park or garden;
I am ascending a palace, pavilion, or couch.” In the same way it thinks, “I
remain there” and “I emerge from there.” In this manner one enters without
clear awareness and continues so until emerging.

But one who is clearly aware knows correctly: “I enter my mother's womb; I
remain precisely here; from precisely here I emerge.” Inverted recognition
and inverted determination do not arise in that being.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The opening objection carries VAK 3.15 directly into this unit: does its
`viparyastamati` govern every womb-entry? Vasubandhu answers no and appeals to
a sūtra teaching four `garbhāvakrāntis`. He then performs three exegetical
operations:

```text
unpacks the cumulative force of api
    → explains why the four appear in reverse order
    → defends the prospective designation “egg-born”
    → specifies delusion and clear awareness experientially
```

The final description of clear awareness is supplied by the commentary, not
stated in full by the kārikā. The examples of parks, huts, palaces, and
forests describe the content of inverted experience; they are not additional
destinations in the cosmological classification.

## 5. Four Cumulative Cognition-Profiles

The particles and carried syntax yield four profiles rather than four
isolated moments:

```text
Profile 1: clear at entry; unclear during residence and emergence
Profile 2: clear at entry and residence; unclear at emergence
Profile 3: clear at entry, residence, and emergence
Profile 4: unclear at entry, residence, and emergence
```

The commentary says that these are expressed in reverse order
(`pratiloma`) for conformity with verse-construction. The prose's doctrinal
enumeration therefore cannot be inferred simply from the surface order of the
pādas.

The fourth profile is not a fifth beside “egg-born.” Rather, an egg-born
being invariably belongs to the fully deluded profile. The expression applies
prospectively to a being that will emerge from an egg.

## 6. Prospective Designation

The egg objection is answered linguistically. A designation can name a
process by the product or state toward which it is tending:

```text
rice is being cooked     → “one cooks rice”
meal is being ground     → “one grinds meal”
being will emerge from egg → “egg-born” during womb-descent
```

This is `bhāvinyā saṃjñayā`, designation according to a future condition. It
does not posit a separate biological mechanism and does not erase the
distinction between present process and future result. It explains how the
name ranges temporally across them.

## 7. Inverted Recognition and Determination

Delusion is positively structured as the operation of two inverted factors:

```text
viparīta-saṃjñā
    the process appears under the recognition of another environment

viparīta-adhimokṣa
    that presented environment is taken or determined as the actual situation
```

The content differs with the being's status. A being of little status
experiences threatening weather, noise, thickets, huts, or makeshift shelter;
a being of great status experiences parks, gardens, palaces, pavilions, or a
couch. The pleasantness of the projected scene does not make the cognition
less inverted. In both cases the womb-process is labeled, presented, and
taken as another environment.

The inversion persists phase by phase: the being thinks that it enters,
inhabits, and leaves the projected environment. Delusion is thus not a blank
absence of cognition but an organized substitution for the process actually
underway.

## 8. Clear Awareness Positively and Negatively Defined

The running source gives both sides of the definition. Positively, the being
knows the three phases as they are:

```text
“I enter my mother's womb.”
“I remain precisely here.”
“From precisely here I emerge.”
```

Negatively, neither inverted recognition nor inverted determination operates.
The positive statement matters: `saṃprajānan` is not mere immunity from a
false picture but correct situational awareness of entry, residence, and
emergence. Yet the Bhāṣya does not identify it here with liberating `prajñā`
or claim that such awareness by itself ends rebirth.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

VAK 3.15 showed the destination appearing as an attractive object. VAK 3.16
now differentiates appearance from awareness more exactly. The same embodied
transition can be operationally successful under either correct recognition
or a counterfeit experiential scene. Causal completion therefore does not
guarantee cognitive truth.

For the Organon, this yields a restrained account of projection primitives.
`Saṃjñā` configures what kind of situation is recognized; `adhimokṣa`
stabilizes the taking of that configuration as actual. Together they make an
experienced world sufficiently coherent for entry, residence, and emergence,
even while misclassifying the process itself.

The clear case supplies the counter-configuration: the stages remain the
same, but their labels and situational determination agree with the process.
This is not yet an Oculus implementation or a general theory of all natural
language. It is a precise source-grounded instance in which process,
presentation, determination, and awareness can be recorded as distinct axes.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit at 127.26–128.25. In particular,
it restores the positive definition of clear awareness omitted from the first
Bhāṣya pass and retains the cumulative syntax, reverse ordering, prospective
naming argument, and differentiated imagery of low- and high-status beings.

The translation remains provisional. The corrupt prose at 128.02, the reading
of `kuṇḍayamūla`, and the exact examples supporting prospective designation
require comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established
translations during the later learning pass. The Study stops before
`atra punar apadiśyate` and does not enter VAK 3.17.
