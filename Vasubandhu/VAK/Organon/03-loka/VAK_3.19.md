# VAK_3.19

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> यथाक्षेपं क्रमाद्वृद्धः सन्तानः क्लेशकर्मभिः ।
>
> परलोकं पुनर्याति इत्यनादिभवचक्रकम् ॥ ३.१९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> yathākṣepaṃ kramād vṛddhaḥ santānaḥ kleśakarmabhiḥ /
>
> paralokaṃ punar yāti ity anādibhavacakrakam // 3.19 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
yathā-ākṣepam           → yathākṣepam
kramāt + vṛddhaḥ        → kramād vṛddhaḥ
santānaḥ                → santānaḥ
kleśa-karmabhiḥ         → kleśakarmabhiḥ
para-lokam              → paralokam
punaḥ + yāti            → punar yāti
iti + anādi             → ity anādi
anādi-bhava-cakrakam    → anādibhavacakrakam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| yathākṣepam | adverbial compound | according to the operative projection |
| kramāt | ablative singular used adverbially | in sequence; gradually; by ordered stages |
| vṛddhaḥ | nominative masculine singular past participle | grown; developed |
| santānaḥ | nominative masculine singular | continuum; serial continuity |
| kleśakarmabhiḥ | instrumental plural compound | by afflictions and karmic actions |
| paralokam | accusative masculine singular compound | another world; a subsequent world-position |
| punaḥ | indeclinable | again |
| yāti | third-person singular present of √yā | goes; proceeds |
| iti | quotative/conclusive particle | thus; marks the completed determination |
| anādi | compound member | without a first beginning |
| bhavacakrakam | nominative neuter singular | wheel of becoming |

The subject is `santānaḥ`. It is modified by `vṛddhaḥ`, while `kramāt`,
`yathākṣepam`, and `kleśakarmabhiḥ` determine the order, measure, and formative
conditions of its development.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first construction states the development of the continuum:

```text
santānaḥ
    yathākṣepam
    kramāt
    kleśa-karmabhiḥ
    vṛddhaḥ [bhavati]
```

> The continuum develops in sequence, according to its projection, through
> afflictions and karmic actions.

The second states recurrence:

```text
[saḥ santānaḥ]
    paralokaṃ
    punaḥ yāti
```

> It goes again to another world.

The concluding `iti` gathers the entire mechanism under one name:

```text
iti
    anādi-bhava-cakrakam
```

> Thus is the beginningless wheel of becoming.

`Anādi` denies a first temporal member; it does not mean that the wheel cannot
end. The Bhāṣya explicitly argues for its termination through exhaustion of
its causes.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The continuum, developed in sequence according to its projection through
> afflictions and karmic actions, goes again to another world. Thus is the
> beginningless wheel of becoming.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The projected continuum develops through ordered embryonic and embodied
> stages. As its faculties mature, afflictions become active again and further
> karma accumulates; after death it passes, as before, through intermediate
> continuity to another world. Birth produces conditions for renewed
> affliction and action, and these produce birth again: this is the
> beginningless—but causally terminable—wheel of becoming.

Embryonic development, faculty-maturation, renewed causal accumulation, and
termination through cause-exhaustion are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System reproduces itself recursively. A projected continuity
> develops into embodiment; mature embodiment becomes a field for renewed
> affliction and action; these conditions project another embodiment. No first
> cycle can be admitted without abandoning causal determination, yet no cycle
> is necessary once its causes are exhausted. Beginninglessness belongs to the
> regress of conditioned arising; liberation belongs to the exhaustibility of
> its grounds.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Ordered embodied development

The Bhāṣya answers `kena krameṇa`—“by what sequence?”—with five embryonic
stages:

```text
kalala
    → arbuda
    → peśī
    → ghana
    → praśākhā
    → differentiated bodily features and faculties
```

Karmically matured bodily conditions then orient the fetus toward birth. The
graphic account of gestation, fetal death, delivery, care, feeding, and growth
places the previously subtle transition inside material development.

### The wheel regenerates its own conditions

The decisive recursion is:

```text
previous kleśa + karma
    → ākṣepa
    → intermediate existence
    → birth
    → ordered growth
    → maturation of faculties
    → renewed kleśa
    → accumulation of new karma
    → death
    → intermediate existence, as before
    → another world
```

An earlier action does not propel the series forever by itself. Each matured
life becomes the field in which the conditions of another life are produced.

### Why no first beginning?

```text
Assume a first birth.

Then:
    no prior kleśa-karma causes exist for it.

Therefore:
    first birth would be causeless.

But:
    arising exhibits determinate causal capacities
    and restrictions of place and time.

Therefore:
    no causeless first birth is admitted.
```

Hence the wheel is `anādi`.

### Why can it end?

```text
birth depends upon causes

causes exhausted
    → no further birth

as:
seed exhausted
    → no sprout
```

Beginning and end are therefore asymmetrical:

```text
first beginning
    would require an uncaused arising

cessation
    requires exhaustion of conditioned causes
```

The strongest result is:

```text
saṃsāra is beginningless
    but not necessary
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.19 closes the first great World-System movement. The spectacular Middle
World of karmic flight, distorted appearance, clarified womb-entry, and subtle
transition now descends into embryology, growth, mature faculties, renewed
affliction, action, death, and another passage through the Middle.

```text
hidden transition
    becomes body

body
    becomes an operative cognition-platform

mature platform
    regenerates affliction and action

those conditions
    project another hidden transition
```

This is Transcendental Technē as project architecture: a complete recursive
specification of conditioned re-worlding. Yet the very completeness of the
mechanism exposes its limit. Nothing in the wheel possesses necessity in
itself; every phase depends upon conditions and vanishes into its successor.

Here Hegel enters more powerfully than `skandhamātra`. The series cannot be
promoted into a stable replacement for what 3.18 rejected:

```text
each skandha-determination
    arises by conditions
    persists as transition
    vanishes into the next determination

santāna
    is the comprehension of ordered vanishing
    not an independently standing permanence
```

The NonBeing of Abhidharma is disclosed from within its own success. Its
determinations are exact, causally effective, and immediately passing away.
Persistence falls into Dharma—the law of transformation through which each
moment is already surrendering itself.

```text
Being of the conditioned determination
    = its passage into NonBeing

continuity
    = this vanishing comprehended as ordered Becoming
```

That does not refute the Kośa. It comprehends why its World System cannot be
ultimate. `Jagan mithyā`: the wheel is effective as appearance, terrible in
its consequences, and incapable of grounding itself as the One True Thing.

The possibility of `hetu-kṣaya` is therefore philosophically decisive. The
World System admits that its own operation can cease. Its beginninglessness
does not confer eternity; its causal structure contains the opening through
which the Path terminates recurrence.

```text
causal recursion
    explains the persistence of World

exhaustion of causes
    reveals the non-necessity of World

comprehension of that non-necessity
    opens beyond the World System
```

The Kārikā itself names only the beginningless wheel. The argument for its
possible end and the embryological recursion are Bhāṣya determinations; the
Hegelian NonBeing and Vedāntic sublation are our declared philosophical
reconstruction.
