# VAK_3.31 Bhāṣya — The Six Contacts Cross-Classified

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
vidyāvidyetarasparśāḥ amalakliṣṭaśeṣitāḥ /
vyāpādānunayasparśau sukhavedyādayas trayaḥ // 3.31 //
```

> Contacts associated with knowledge, ignorance, and the other are,
> respectively, uncontaminated, defiled, and remaining. There are also the
> contacts of aversion and attraction, and the three beginning with contact
> bearing pleasant feeling.

The Bhāṣya reclassifies the same six contacts under several principles. It
does not add another temporal sequence of contacts. The unit ends by totaling
the classifications and explicitly closing the exposition of contact.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast

vidyāvidyetarasparśāḥ.

vidyāsaṃsparśo 'vidyāsaṃsparśaḥ, tābhyāṃ cānyo
naivavidyānāvidyāsaṃsparśa iti. ete punar yathākramaṃ veditavyāḥ:

amalakliṣṭaśeṣitāḥ.

anāsravaḥ sparśo vidyāsaṃsparśaḥ; kliṣṭo 'vidyāsaṃsparśo

ubhābhyām asaṃprayogāt. kaḥ punaḥ śeṣaḥ? kuśalasāsravo
'nivṛtāvyākṛtaś ca.

punar avidyāsaṃsparśasyābhīkṣṇasamudācāriṇa ekadeśasya grahaṇād dvau

sukhavedanādihitatvāt. athavā vedyate tad vedayituṃ vā śakyam iti
vedanīyam. kiṃ tat? veditam. sukhaṃ vedanīyam asminn iti
sukhavedanīyaḥ sparśo yatra sukhaṃ veditam asti. evaṃ

```

The running witness reads `vidyā 'vidyābhyāṃ saṃprayuktatvāt` at
144.12–13. The continuous text provisionally joins this as
`vidyāvidyābhyāṃ`, a paired reference to association with knowledge and
ignorance respectively, rather than assigning the clause only to ignorance.
The e-text's `soparśau` at 144.14–15 is repaired to `sparśau`. These are
working readings, not claims to a critical text. The running source remains
unchanged.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Again, those same six contacts become three: contact associated with
knowledge, contact associated with ignorance, and another distinct from those
two—contact associated with neither knowledge nor ignorance. These are to be
understood respectively as uncontaminated, defiled, and remaining.

Uncontaminated contact is contact associated with knowledge; defiled contact
is contact associated with ignorance, because they are associated with
knowledge and ignorance respectively. The remainder is contact associated
with neither knowledge nor ignorance, because it is conjoined with neither.
What constitutes the remainder? Wholesome but contaminated contact and
unobstructed-indeterminate contact.

Again, by taking a frequently manifesting portion of contact associated with
ignorance, there are two contacts: contact of aversion and contact of
attraction, because of association with aversion and attraction.

Again, gathering all contacts comprehensively, there are three beginning with
contact bearing pleasant feeling: contact bearing pleasant feeling, contact
bearing painful feeling, and contact bearing neither-painful-nor-pleasant
feeling. They are so called because they are conducive to pleasant feeling
and the others. Alternatively, something is `vedanīya` because it is
experienced or can be made to be experienced. What is that? An experience.
Contact is pleasant-feeling-bearing where pleasant experience is present in
it. The painful and neither-painful-nor-pleasant cases are to be construed in
the same way.

Thus there are these sixteen contacts. Contact has been explained.

## 4. Movement of the Classification

The prose makes four moves:

1. The same six contacts are divided by association with `vidyā`, `avidyā`,
   or neither.
2. These correspond respectively to uncontaminated, defiled, and residual
   contact; the residual field is then specified positively.
3. A frequently manifesting portion of ignorance-contact is divided by
   association with aversion and attraction.
4. All contacts are gathered by their relation to pleasant, painful, or
   neutral feeling, after which the analysis of contact is closed.

No independent objector speaks in this unit. The alternative derivation of
`vedanīya`, introduced by `athavā`, is supplied within the exposition and
should not be inflated into a school controversy unsupported by the passage.

## 5. Knowledge, Ignorance, and the Remainder

The first division correlates two vocabularies:

```text
vidyā-contact
    = anāsrava
      uncontaminated

avidyā-contact
    = kliṣṭa
      defiled

neither-contact
    = śeṣa
      remainder
```

The Bhāṣya makes `itara`, “the other,” exact by expanding it as
`naiva-vidyā-nāvidyā-saṃsparśa`. Absence of association with knowledge does
not by itself establish association with ignorance. The third field contains:

```text
kuśala-sāsrava
    wholesome but contaminated

anivṛta-avyākṛta
    unobstructed-indeterminate
```

The remainder is therefore determinate, not a miscellaneous residue. It
prevents the classificatory opposition from becoming a false exhaustive
binary.

## 6. Aversion and Attraction Within Ignorance-Contact

The second classification does not divide every contact directly. It selects
an `ekadeśa`, a portion, of ignorance-contact characterized as frequently
manifesting and distinguishes:

```text
vyāpāda-saṃsparśa
    contact associated with aversion or hostility

anunaya-saṃsparśa
    contact associated with attraction or following-after
```

Their ground is `saṃprayoga`, association with aversion or attraction. These
are afflictive orientations within the ignorance-conditioned field, not yet
the later threefold classification by felt pleasure, pain, and neutrality.
Attraction must therefore not be equated without qualification with pleasant
feeling, nor aversion with painful feeling.

## 7. The Three Feeling-Bearing Contacts

The expression `sarvasaṃgraheṇa` marks a comprehensive regrouping:

```text
sukhavedanīya
    pleasant-feeling-bearing contact

duḥkhavedanīya
    painful-feeling-bearing contact

aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya
    neither-painful-nor-pleasant-feeling-bearing contact
```

The first derivation relates contact to the benefit or production of the
corresponding feeling. The alternative emphasizes what is experienced or can
be made the object of experience. In either case, `vedanīya` indicates a
relation between contact and feeling; it does not simply identify contact
with feeling.

This prepares the next dependent-arising member without erasing the boundary:

```text
sparśa
    bears or conduces to a feeling determination

vedanā
    is the feeling whose distinct exposition follows
```

## 8. Why the Total Is Sixteen

The closing total is obtained from the classified types stated across VAK
3.30–3.31:

```text
6  contacts by faculty and cognition
3  knowledge / ignorance / neither contacts
2  aversion / attraction contacts
3  pleasant / painful / neutral-feeling-bearing contacts
2  resistant / designation contacts
--
16 classified contacts
```

This is a total of classificatory kinds, not sixteen mutually exclusive
events. The same contact can fall under more than one classification because
the grounds differ. For example, support, cognitive-afflictive association,
motivational association, and feeling relation are distinct axes.

The closure `uktaḥ sparśaḥ` is decisive for the natural boundary. The next
sentence in the running source—“feeling must be explained”—belongs to VAK
3.32 and is not included here.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The unit offers a disciplined model of multidimensional analysis:

```text
ContactEvent {
    cognitive_association: vidyā | avidyā | neither
    contamination_status: anāsrava | kliṣṭa | remainder
    afflictive_orientation: vyāpāda | anunaya | not_selected_here
    feeling_relation: sukha | duḥkha | neither
}
```

This schema is an Organon reconstruction, not the Bhāṣya's literal format.
Its warrant lies in the text's repeated regrouping of the same six contacts
by explicitly different grounds. A new determination need not mean a new
substance or event; it may disclose another valid relation of the same event.

For Ocular analysis, this is more exact than uncontrolled multilabeling. Each
dimension requires a stated principle: association, contamination, selected
afflictive orientation, or relation to feeling. The “neither” class is equally
important. A typed Dataset must be able to represent a determinate remainder
instead of coercing every item into the most dramatic opposition available.

The passage also guards the transition from contact to feeling. Contact can
already be classified by the feeling it bears without becoming identical to
feeling. A relation to a downstream determination is not the downstream
determination itself.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete natural unit at 144.07–144.22: the threefold
division by knowledge, ignorance, and neither; the positive contents of the
remainder; the selected pair of aversion and attraction; the comprehensive
threefold feeling classification; and the total of sixteen contacts.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The damaged association
clause at 144.12–13, the scope of `abhīkṣṇasamudācārin`, the force of
`sukhavedanādihitatvāt`, and the alternative derivation of `vedanīya` require
comparison with critical Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and established
translations. The Study stops at `uktaḥ sparśaḥ` and does not begin the
exposition of feeling belonging to VAK 3.32.
