# VAK_3.87

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चतुर्विंशतिरङ्गुल्यो हस्तो हस्तचतुष्टयम् ।
>
> धनुः पञ्चशतान्येषां क्रोशोऽरण्यं च तन्मतम् ॥ ३.८७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> caturviṃśatir aṅgulyo hasto hastacatuṣṭayam /
>
> dhanuḥ pañcaśatāny eṣāṃ krośo 'raṇyaṃ ca tan matam // 3.87 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| caturviṃśatiḥ | cardinal numeral | twenty-four |
| aṅgulyaḥ | nominative feminine plural | aṅgulis or finger-breadths |
| hastaḥ | nominative masculine singular | hasta, cubit, or forearm measure |
| hastacatuṣṭayam | nominative neuter singular compound | a group of four hastas |
| dhanuḥ | nominative neuter singular | bow-length or span-measure |
| pañcaśatāni | nominative neuter plural | five hundreds, five hundred |
| eṣām | genitive plural | of these, referring to dhanu measures |

| araṇyam | nominative neuter singular | wilderness-region |
| tat | nominative neuter singular | that, the region at approximately that measured distance |
| matam | past passive participle, nominative neuter singular | regarded or conventionally determined |

The final words change category. `Araṇya` is not another member of the
arithmetic unit-series; the Bhāṣya makes it a spatial designation applied to a
region at about a krośa from a village.

## 4. Grammar

The first three predications are conversion equations:

```text
caturviṃśatiḥ aṅgulyaḥ
    hastaḥ [bhavanti]
```

> Twenty-four aṅgulis make one hasta.

```text
hasta-catuṣṭayam
    dhanuḥ [bhavati]
```

> Four hastas make one dhanu.

```text
eṣāṃ [dhanuṣām] pañca-śatāni
    krośaḥ [bhavanti]
```

> Five hundred of these dhanu measures make one krośa.

The closing construction is not an equation of units:

```text
krośa-mātraṃ [grāmāt]
    araṇyaṃ
    tat matam
```

> A region at approximately a krośa from a village is regarded as wilderness.

The deictic `tat` points to a region determined through the established
distance. `Matam` marks conventional recognition rather than another act of
numerical composition.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Twenty-four aṅgulis are one hasta; four hastas are one dhanu. Five hundred
> of these are one krośa, and a region at that distance is regarded as
> wilderness.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Twenty-four finger-breadths constitute one hasta; four hastas constitute one
> dhanu or measured span; and five hundred dhanu measures constitute one
> krośa. A region beginning at approximately one krośa from a village is
> conventionally regarded as wilderness.

The clarification of `dhanu` as a span or extent and the village-relative
definition of `araṇya` are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Recursive material enclosure reaches the human body and then expands into
> traversable World-space. Finger-breadths are enclosed as a cubit, cubits as
> a bow-length, and bow-lengths as a krośa. Once this quantitative scale is
> established, it supports a new kind of determination: a measured distance
> from habitation becomes the conventional distinction between village-world
> and wilderness. Quantity here becomes the ground of a qualitative spatial
> boundary.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| hasta | hasta / cubit | 24 aṅgulis |
| catuṣṭaya | group of four | aggregation factor from hasta to dhanu |
| dhanuḥ | dhanu / bow-length | 4 hastas; clarified as span or extent |
| vyāsa | span / extent | Bhāṣya clarification of the dhanu measure |
| krośa | krośa | 500 dhanu measures |
| krośamātra | approximately one krośa | threshold-distance used in territorial designation |

| mata | regarded / conventionally established | marks accepted worldly classification |

The controlled term `araṇya` therefore carries a relation:

```text
region + measured distance from village → wilderness
```

It is not simply a synonym for `krośa`.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Variable-ratio enclosure chain

Unlike 3.86, this verse does not use one invariant aggregation factor:

```text
24 aṅgulis = 1 hasta
4 hastas    = 1 dhanu
500 dhanus  = 1 krośa
```

The general form remains:

```text
Enclose(multiplierₙ × lowerUnitₙ) = one higherUnitₙ₊₁
```

but `multiplierₙ` is selected by the conversion edge rather than inherited
globally.

### Continuity with the atomic scale

The Bhāṣya to 3.86 supplies:

```text
3 finger-joints = 1 aṅguli
```

Therefore:

| Unit | Finger-joints | Ultimate atoms |
|---|---:|---:|
| aṅguli | 3 | 5,931,980,229 |
| hasta | 72 | 142,367,525,496 |
| dhanu | 288 | 569,470,101,984 |
| krośa | 144,000 | 284,735,050,992,000 |

The ultimate-atom totals are arithmetic consequences of the eleven sevenfold
transitions established in 3.86, followed by the conversion rules here. They
are not figures separately asserted in the source.

### Operator change at `araṇya`

The first three steps instantiate quantitative composition:

```text
Compose(count, unit) → larger unit
```

The final step instantiates relational classification:

```text
distance(region, village) ≈ 1 krośa
    → classify(region, Wilderness)
```

It would therefore be a category error to append:

```text
1 krośa = 1 araṇya
```

`Krośa` supplies a quantity; `araṇya` names the spatial status conventionally
assigned through that quantity and a reference point.

### Resolution path

```text
atomic constituent
    → bodily measure
        → constructed linear measure
            → traversable distance
                → territorial classification
```

The path remains continuous even though its final predicate changes type.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.87 deepens the Mind-of-the-Enclosure account. In 3.86 the enclosure rule
was invariant: seven became one at every resolution. Here the same logical
form persists while the cardinality changes by edge:

```text
24 → 1
4  → 1
500 → 1
```

The Mind of the Enclosure is therefore not the number seven. It is the higher
operation that knows which plurality, under which rule, is to count as one at
the next resolution. The multiplier is data belonging to the transition;
enclosure is the semantic operation.

The final clause then shows measure passing into World. A krośa does not by
itself contain “wilderness” as one more measurable component. Rather, the
established measure allows a region to be judged relative to habitation:

```text
village
    → supplies reference point

krośa
    → supplies quantitative threshold

conventional judgment
    → determines the region as araṇya
```

This is a true Semantic Copula. “That is wilderness” does not merely attach a
word to an independently completed object. Village, distance, region, and
accepted classification reciprocally determine the worldly meaning of
`araṇya`.

```ts
type LinearConversion = {
  lowerUnit: "Anguli" | "Hasta" | "Dhanu";
  multiplier: 24 | 4 | 500;
  higherUnit: "Hasta" | "Dhanu" | "Krosa";
};

  reference: "Village";
  approximateDistance: { magnitude: 1; unit: "Krosa" };
  designation: "Aranya";
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It keeps
unit conversion distinct from qualitative spatial designation.
