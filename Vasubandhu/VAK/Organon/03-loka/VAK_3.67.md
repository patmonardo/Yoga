# VAK_3.67

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> सार्धद्विशतपार्श्वोऽत्र वैजयन्तः बहिः पुनः ।
>
> तच्चैत्ररथपारुष्यामिश्रनन्दनभूषितम् ॥ ३.६७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> sārdhadviśatapārśvo 'tra vaijayantaḥ bahiḥ punaḥ /
>
> tac caitrarathapāruṣyamiśranandanabhūṣitam // 3.67 //

The names are retained rather than replaced by speculative etymological
translations. The Bhāṣya expands the middle names as `Pāruṣyaka` and
`Miśraka` and specifies the types and uses of all four locations.

## 4. Grammar

The first half makes Vaijayanta the subject and gives it a location and
measure:

```text
atra [sudarśananagare]
    vaijayantaḥ
    sārdha-dvi-śata-pārśvaḥ [asti]
```

> Here [in Sudarśana] is Vaijayanta, measuring 250 yojanas on each side.

The Bhāṣya supplies the exact relation: Vaijayanta is Śakra's palace, located
in the middle of the city.

The second half turns from the interior center to the exterior:

```text
bahiḥ punaḥ
    tat [puram]
    Caitraratha-Pāruṣya-Miśra-Nandana-bhūṣitam [asti]
```

> Outside, in turn, that [city] is adorned by Caitraratha, Pāruṣya, Miśra, and
> Nandana.

The Bhāṣya distributes the four recreation-grounds around the city's four
sides rather than treating the names as an unordered decoration.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Here is Vaijayanta, having sides of 250 yojanas. Outside, in turn, that
> [city] is adorned by Caitraratha, Pāruṣya, Miśra, and Nandana.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> At the center of Sudarśana stands Vaijayanta, the palace of Śakra, Indra of
> the gods. Each of its sides measures 250 yojanas. Through the excellent
> arrangement of many kinds of jewels, its magnificence surpasses all other
> divine residences. Outside the city, on its four sides, lie four recreation-
> grounds of the gods: the Caitraratha garden, Pāruṣyaka, the Miśraka grove,
> and the Nandana grove. These adorn Sudarśana externally.

The identification of Vaijayanta as Śakra's central palace, its comparative
magnificence, the jewel arrangement, the one-per-side distribution, and the
classification of the exterior places as divine recreation-grounds are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Sudarśana's center is further centered by the residence of its ruler, while
> its exterior is differentiated into four regions of divine recreation. One
> spatial structure thus receives distinct institutional functions: central
> palace and peripheral pleasure-grounds. Adornment is not merely an added
> visible predicate; it is produced by an ordered arrangement of materials
> and by surrounding places in which characteristic activities occur. The
> divine city becomes concrete as a field of position, office, construction,
> and use.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Nested center relation

```text
Meru summit
    contains Sudarśana at its center

Sudarśana
    contains Vaijayanta at its center
```

Centrality is relative to a containing domain. Vaijayanta is not the center of
Meru without qualification; it is the center of the city that is itself
centrally located on the summit.

### Functional zoning

```text
interior center:
    palace of Śakra
    → residence and political function

four exterior sides:
    four named grounds
    → recreation function
```

Location and use jointly determine the zones. `Inside/outside` supplies the
spatial distinction; `palace/recreation-ground` supplies the institutional
distinction.

### Ordered exterior distribution

Let the four sides of Sudarśana be `S₁–S₄` and the four grounds be `G₁–G₄`:

```text
count(S) = 4
count(G) = 4

each exterior side supports one recreation-ground:
    S₁ ↦ G₁
    S₂ ↦ G₂
    S₃ ↦ G₃
    S₄ ↦ G₄
```

The Bhāṣya establishes the cardinal distribution but does not yet assign the
individual garden names to particular directions. That additional directional
mapping must not be invented here.

### Arrangement and emergent magnificence

```text
many jewel types
    + excellent spatial arrangement
        → surpassing palace magnificence
```

The description attributes magnificence not to an inventory alone, but to the
organized disposition of heterogeneous constituents.

### Adornment as relational effect

```text
four exterior grounds
    → adorn Sudarśana externally
```

`Adorned` is a relational predicate. The gardens retain their own locations
and uses while contributing to a determination of the city as a whole.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.67 advances from responsive habitation to functional organization:

```text
3.66  city + constructed environment + action-responsive ground
3.67  ruler's palace + exterior recreation zones
```

The methodical abstraction is semantic zoning. A spatial region becomes a
palace or a recreation-ground through more than position. Its occupant,
institutional role, characteristic activity, and relation to surrounding
regions must all be determined.

This also sharpens the hierarchy of relative centers:

```text
world-system
    → Meru
        → summit
            → Sudarśana
                → Vaijayanta
```

Each level can serve as a center relative to one domain and as a contained
member relative to another. A flat list of locations would lose this nested
scope.

```ts
interface FunctionalZone {
  position: "Center" | "ExteriorSide";
  structure: "Palace" | "Garden" | "Grove";
  function: "RoyalResidence" | "DivineRecreation";
  containingDomain: "Sudarsana";
}

interface VaijayantaPalace extends FunctionalZone {
  position: "Center";
  structure: "Palace";
  function: "RoyalResidence";
  occupant: "Sakra";
  sideLengthYojanas: 250;
}
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. It
preserves the difference between geometry and the institutional semantics
attached to geometry.

The verse belongs to the refined pleasure-world of the Trāyastriṃśas, but its
logical value is broader than the spectacle. It shows how a World System
becomes usable: not every place performs the same operation, and the
differentiation of activity is encoded into the organization of space.
