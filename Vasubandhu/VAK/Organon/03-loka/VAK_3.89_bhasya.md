# VAK_3.89 Bhāṣya — The Year and the Kinds of Kalpa

## Kārikā Anchor

```iast
trayo muhūrttāhorātramāsā dvādaśamāsakaḥ /
saṃvatsaraḥ sonarātraḥ kalpo bahuvidhaḥ smṛtaḥ // 3.89 //
```

> Thirty lavas make a muhūrta, thirty muhūrtas a day-and-night, and thirty
> day-and-nights a month. A year consists of twelve months with deficient-night
> adjustments; the kalpa is remembered as having several kinds.

The verse is metrically compressed. The Bhāṣya supplies the repeated factor
of thirty and explains the annual `ūnarātra` adjustments.

## Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
Trayo muhūrttāhorātramāsāḥ.

Triṃśal lavā muhūrtaḥ; triṃśan muhūrtā ahorātraḥ.

Kadācit tu rātrir adhikā bhavati, kadācid ūnā, kadācit samā.

Triṃśad ahorātrā māsaḥ.

Dvādaśamāsakaḥ.

Saṃvatsaraḥ sonarātraḥ.

Catvāro māsā Hemantānāṃ, catvāro Grīṣmāṇāṃ, catvāro Varṣāṇām; ity ete
dvādaśa māsāḥ saṃvatsaraḥ sārdham ūnarātraiḥ.

Saṃvatsareṇa hi ṣaḍ ūnarātrā nipātyante. Kathaṃ kṛtvā?

“Hemantagrīṣmavarṣāṇām adhyardhe māsi nirgate /
śeṣe 'rdhamāse vidvadbhir ūnarātro nipātyate.”

Uktā varṣapramāṇam.

Kalpasyedānīṃ vaktavyam.

Kalpo bahuvidhaḥ smṛtaḥ // 3.89 //

Antarakalpaḥ, saṃvartakalpaḥ, vivartakalpaḥ, mahākalpaś ceti.
```

Spacing and sandhi are regularized. The mnemonic verse on the placement of
the `ūnarātra` is translated cautiously: its wording is compressed, while
the prose explicitly states that six such nights are omitted in a year.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Thirty lavas make one muhūrta; thirty muhūrtas make one day-and-night. The
night is sometimes longer, sometimes shorter, and sometimes equal [to the
day]. Thirty day-and-nights make one month.

A year consists of twelve months: four in winter, four in summer, and four in
the rains. The year includes deficient-night adjustments; six `ūnarātras`
are omitted in the course of a year. A cited verse gives their placement in
the three seasons, though its exact calendrical wording is compressed.
Roughly, it says that after a month and a half of winter, summer, or the rains
has passed, the learned omit one deficient night in the remaining half-month;
the prose totals six such adjustments in the year.

The measure of the year has now been stated. The kalpa is remembered as
having several kinds: intermediate, contraction, evolution, and great kalpa.

## Natural Movement

The Bhāṣya first completes ordinary time-measure from lava through muhūrta,
day-and-night, month, and year. It then qualifies the calendar: night does
not have one fixed length relative to day, and the twelve-month year includes
six omitted or deficient nights. Having stated the year-measure, Vasubandhu
opens the next scale by listing four kinds of kalpa; their definitions
belong to the following discussion.

## Calendar and Classification

| Measure | Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|
| 30 lavas | 1 muhūrta |
| 30 muhūrtas | 1 ahorātra |
| Night relative to day | Sometimes longer, shorter, or equal |
| 30 ahorātras | 1 month |
| 12 months | 1 year: four months each of winter, summer, and rains |
| Annual correction | Six `ūnarātras` are omitted |

The Bhāṣya then names four kalpa-types without yet explaining them:

- `antarakalpa` — intermediate kalpa
- `saṃvartakalpa` — contraction kalpa
- `vivartakalpa` — evolution kalpa
- `mahākalpa` — great kalpa

## Doctrinal Note

The ordinary calendar is an ordered measure but not a perfectly uniform
division of day from night: the full `ahorātra` remains the unit while its
internal proportions vary. The annual `ūnarātra` rule is part of the stated
calendar construction. `Kalpa`, in turn, is not treated as one undifferentiated
duration; the Bhāṣya announces a fourfold classification whose members are
to be distinguished in the next passage.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact: the text distinguishes the complete temporal unit from
variation in its internal partition, then classifies larger cycles by kind.
This is a project-level observation, not Vasubandhu's technical terminology.
