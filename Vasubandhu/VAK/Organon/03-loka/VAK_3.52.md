# VAK_3.52

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> आभ्यन्तरः समुद्रोऽसौ त्रिगुणः स तु पार्श्वतः ।
>
> अर्धार्धेनापराः शीताः शेषं बाह्यो महोदधेः ॥ ३.५२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> ābhyantaraḥ samudro 'sau triguṇaḥ sa tu pārśvataḥ /
>
> ardhārdhenāparāḥ śītāḥ śeṣaṃ bāhyo mahodadheḥ // 3.52 //

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ābhyantaraḥ                 → ābhyantaraḥ
samudraḥ                    → samudro
asau                        → 'sau
tri-guṇaḥ                   → triguṇaḥ
saḥ                         → sa
tu                          → tu
pārśvataḥ                   → pārśvataḥ
ardha-ardhena               → ardhārdhena
aparāḥ                      → aparāḥ
śītāḥ                       → śītāḥ
śeṣam                       → śeṣam
bāhyaḥ                      → bāhyo
mahā-udadheḥ                → mahodadheḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| ābhyantaraḥ | nominative masculine singular | inner, internal |
| samudraḥ | nominative masculine singular | sea, ocean |
| asau | nominative masculine singular pronoun | that one, the first Śītā |
| triguṇaḥ | nominative masculine singular | threefold |
| pārśvataḥ | indeclinable | along the side or shore |
| ardhārdhena | instrumental singular compound | by successive halves |
| aparāḥ śītāḥ | nominative feminine plural | the other cool inner seas |
| śeṣam | neuter singular | what remains, residual interval |
| bāhyaḥ | nominative masculine singular | outer |
| mahodadheḥ | genitive singular compound | of the great ocean |

The final expression is elliptical. The Bhāṣya resolves `śeṣam` as the interval
between Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa and identifies it as the outer great ocean.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first Śītā is reclassified:

```text
asau [ādyā śītā]
    ābhyantaraḥ samudraḥ [asti]

that [first Śītā]
    is the inner ocean
```

Its second dimension is then stated:

```text
saḥ tu
    pārśvataḥ
    triguṇaḥ [bhavati]

but it,
    along the side,
    is threefold
```

The remaining Śītās inherit a recursive rule:

```text
aparāḥ śītāḥ
    ardha-ardhena [bhavanti]
```

The closing syntax is supplied by the commentary:

```text
[nimindhara-cakravāḍayor antaram]
    śeṣam [asti]
    [tat] bāhyaḥ mahodadhiḥ [asti]
```

Thus the remainder is not “of” an independently mentioned ocean in a merely
possessive sense; the residual interval itself constitutes the outer ocean.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> That is the inner ocean; along its side it is threefold. The other Śītās
> [diminish] by successive halves. The remainder is the outer great ocean.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The first Śītā, between Sumeru and Yugandhara, is the inner ocean. Its radial
> width is 80,000 yojanas, while its measure along the Yugandhara shore is three
> times that, 240,000. Each of the six remaining Śītā seas has half the width
> of the preceding one. Their great longitudinal extension is left
> unenumerated. The residual interval between Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa is the
> outer great ocean, filled with saline water; within it lie the four
> continents.

The radial and shore-wise axes, numerical shore measure, unenumerated length,
precise boundaries of the remainder, saline character, and placement of the
continents are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The recursive inner order closes by producing an exterior domain. Within the
> mountain system, each sea receives its width through a proportional law, yet
> the same sea admits another measure along its shore. Beyond that articulated
> sequence, the residual interval is not discarded as geometrical waste: it is
> positively typed as the saline outer ocean and becomes the field of
> habitation. The World System advances by converting a remainder of one
> construction into the ground of the next.

## 8. Logical Determination

### One domain, multiple axes

```text
first inner ocean
    radial width:       80,000
    shore-wise measure: 240,000
```

The threefold relation does not contradict the 80,000 measure because the two
numbers answer different spatial questions. Correct predication requires an
explicit measurement axis.

### Transfer of the recursive rule

```text
W₀ = 80,000
Wₙ₊₁ = Wₙ / 2
```

The same halving operation that ordered mountain heights now orders sea
widths. One law coordinates two different entity types without making
mountains and seas identical.

### Enumerated and unenumerated dimensions

```text
width
    → recursively enumerated

longitudinal extension
    → explicitly not enumerated
```

Systematicity does not require pretending that every magnitude has been
calculated. The model marks both what it determines and where enumeration
stops.

### Remainder becomes domain

```text
bounded inner construction completed
    → residual Nimindhara–Cakravāḍa interval
        → outer great ocean
            → field containing four continents
```

`Śeṣa` is operational: what remains after one ordered series is completed
becomes the positive spatial ground for a new order.

### Qualitative boundary

```text
inner seas
    → cool, sweet, eight-qualified water

outer ocean
    → saline water
```

The inner/outer distinction is therefore simultaneously topological,
quantitative, and qualitative.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.52 closes the foundation-and-ring sequence with a powerful rule of
system development:

```text
remainder of completed subsystem
    → typed domain
        → ground of succeeding subsystem
```

The outer ocean is not an unarticulated “everything else.” Its bounding terms
are precise—Nimindhara and Cakravāḍa—its material quality differs from the
inner seas, and it has a new function: it receives the inhabited continents.
The remainder is therefore determined by the structure it follows and by the
structure it enables.

This also refines the dimension-indexed semantics found in 3.47. A single sea
has width, shore-wise extension, and an unenumerated longitudinal reach. There
is no context-free answer to “how large is it?” The semantic predicate must
carry its axis and measurement operation.

For the Organon projection:

```ts
interface TypedRemainderDomain {
  boundedBy: readonly ["Nimindhara", "Cakravada"];
  inheritedRole: "remainder-of-inner-ring-system";
  domainType: "outer-saline-ocean";
  succeedingRole: "field-for-four-continents";
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It captures the
important transition: a remainder is intelligible only relative to the
operation that leaves it, but once positively determined it can become the
support for a new phase of world-construction.

The next verse can therefore begin the continents without beginning from
nothing. Habitation is placed inside a field already generated, bounded, and
qualitatively specified by the preceding cosmological operations.
