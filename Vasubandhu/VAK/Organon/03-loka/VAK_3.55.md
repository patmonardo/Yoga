# VAK_3.55

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> गोदानीयः सहस्राणि सप्त सार्धानि मण्डलः ।
>
> सार्धे द्वे मध्यमस्याष्टौ चतुरस्रः कुरुः समः ॥ ३.५५ ॥

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first continent receives total measure and shape:

```text
godānīyaḥ
    sākalyena sapta-sārdha-sahasrāṇi [asti]
    maṇḍalaḥ [asti]
```

The next phrase supplies its middle span:

```text
[godānīyasya]
    madhyamasya [pramāṇam]
    sārdhe dve [sahasre]
```

The subject then changes to Kuru:

```text
kuruḥ
    sākalyena aṣṭau [sahasrāṇi]
    catur-asraḥ
    samaḥ [sarveṣu pārśveṣu]
```

The Bhāṣya resolves both compressed measures: Godānīya totals 7,500 yojanas
with a 2,500-yojana middle span; Kuru totals 8,000, distributed equally across
four sides of 2,000 each.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Godānīya is seven and one-half thousand [yojanas] and circular; its middle
> [measure] is two and one-half [thousand]. Kuru is eight [thousand], square,
> and equal.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> West of Sumeru, Aparagodānīya is circular like the full moon. Its total
> encircling measure is 7,500 yojanas and its middle span is 2,500. North of
> Sumeru, Uttarakuru is square, with a total boundary of 8,000 yojanas and four
> exactly equal sides of 2,000 each. In every continent, the faces of its human
> inhabitants have the same form as the continent itself.

The western and northern locations, full-moon comparison, interpretation of
the total and middle measures, equality of all four Kuru sides, and formal
correspondence between continent and human face are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The four-continent system closes through shape-specific rules of measure. A
> circular region relates its encircling total to a middle span; a square
> region distributes its total equally across four sides. The Bhāṣya then
> extends regional morphology into embodiment: inhabitants bear faces
> corresponding to the form of their continent. The World System is thus not
> represented as an indifferent container beside its Agents, but as a field in
> which environmental and embodied forms exhibit systematic correspondence.

The metric relations are:

```text
Godānīya:
    total encircling measure = 7,500
    middle span             = 2,500
    total                   = 3 × middle span

Kuru:
    total boundary measure  = 8,000
    side                    = 2,000
    total                   = 4 × side
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### Shape selects the measurement operator

```text
circular region
    → encircling total / middle span

square region
    → total boundary / equal side
```

The same word “measure” cannot be interpreted identically without attending
to morphology. Shape determines which local dimension reconstructs the total.

### Whole and generating measure

```text
Godānīya:
    middle span × 3 → whole circumference

Kuru:
    side length × 4 → whole perimeter
```

The whole is not an additional unrelated datum. Given the shape-rule, it is
generated from a characteristic local measure; conversely, the local measure
can be recovered from the stated total.

### Closure of fourfold differentiation

```text
south  → Jambūdvīpa   → cart form
east   → Prāgvideha   → half-moon form
west   → Godānīya     → full-moon circle
north  → Kuru         → equal square
```

Directional identity, metric structure, and morphology jointly specify each
regional type.

### Domain–embodiment correspondence

The Bhāṣya states:

```text
shape of a continent
    ↔ shape of its inhabitants' faces
```

This establishes formal correspondence. It does not explicitly state a
one-way causal mechanism by which geography produces facial form; that
stronger claim must remain interpretive rather than textual.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.55 completes the continent matrix and adds a methodical principle about
measurement: the semantic meaning of a number depends upon the form whose
dimension it measures. For a circle, the relevant pair is circumference and
middle span; for a square, perimeter and side. Raw values become intelligible
only through a shape-specific operator.

This produces a reusable schema:

```text
entity type
    → selects dimensional relations
        → makes local and total measures mutually derivable
```

The final Bhāṣya sentence is even more striking. Regional form and embodied
human form correspond. Textually, we should call this a morphological
correspondence; Vasubandhu does not supply a separate causal derivation here.
For the Organon, however, it offers a disciplined model of Domain–Agent
congruence: the form in which an Agent appears is not unrelated to the
determinate Domain it inhabits.

```ts
interface RegionalEmbodimentSchema {
  direction: "south" | "east" | "west" | "north";
  regionShape: "cart" | "half-moon" | "circle" | "square";
  characteristicMeasure: number;
  humanFaceShape: "corresponds-to-region";
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It preserves two
levels that must not be conflated:

```text
textual determination:
    continent form corresponds to facial form

Organon hypothesis:
    a Domain may constrain the form in which its Agents appear
```

The hypothesis is warranted as a direction for later synthesis, but the
strong causal verb “constrains” exceeds what this local Bhāṣya explicitly
states. Keeping that boundary visible is itself part of the methodical lesson.
