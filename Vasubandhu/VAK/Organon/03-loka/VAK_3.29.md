# VAK_3.29

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> संयोजनादिवचनात् कुप्रज्ञा चेन्न दर्शनात् ।
>
> दृष्टेस्तत्संप्रयुक्तत्वात् प्रज्ञोपक्लेशदेशनात् ॥ ३.२९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> saṃyojanādivacanāt kuprajñā cen na darśanāt /
>
> dṛṣṭes tatsaṃprayuktatvāt prajñopakleśadeśanāt // 3.29 //

The first pāda closes the positive argument of 3.28 and then introduces a new
proposal:

```text
saṃyojana-ādi-vacanāt
[avidyā dharmāntaram]

ku-prajñā cet?
na
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
saṃyojana-ādi-vacanāt    → saṃyojanādivacanāt
ku-prajñā                → kuprajñā
cet                      → cet
na                       → na
darśanāt                 → darśanāt
dṛṣṭeḥ                   → dṛṣṭes
tat-saṃprayuktatvāt      → tatsaṃprayuktatvāt
prajñā-upakleśa-deśanāt  → prajñopakleśadeśanāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| saṃyojanādivacanāt | ablative singular compound | because ignorance is called a fetter and by related functional names |
| kuprajñā | nominative feminine singular compound | faulty or defective discriminative comprehension |
| cet | conditional particle | if; suppose one identifies ignorance this way |
| na | negative response | no; rejects the proposed identity |
| darśanāt | ablative neuter singular | because of the case of views; wrong views are faulty prajñā |
| dṛṣṭeḥ | genitive feminine singular | of view / wrong view |
| tatsaṃprayuktatvāt | ablative singular abstract compound | because of its being associated with that, namely avidyā |
| prajñopakleśadeśanāt | ablative singular compound | because ignorance is taught as a defilement of prajñā |

`Darśanāt` is elliptical. The Bhāṣya supplies `dṛṣṭayo hi kuprajñāḥ`: wrong
views are instances of faulty prajñā.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening ablative completes the proof that ignorance is an operative
factor:

```text
saṃyojana-ādi-vacanāt
    [avidyā dharmāntaram]
```

> Because it is spoken of as a fetter and so forth, ignorance is a distinct
> operative factor.

The proposed reduction and rejection are:

```text
avidyā
    ku-prajñā cet?

na
    darśanāt
```

> If ignorance is faulty prajñā? No, because of the case of views.

Two ablatives give the reasons:

```text
dṛṣṭeḥ
    avidyā-saṃprayuktatvāt

avidyāyāḥ
    prajñā-upakleśatvena deśanāt
```

> Because views are associated with ignorance, and because ignorance is
> taught as a defilement of prajñā.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> [Ignorance is a distinct operative factor] because it is spoken of as a
> fetter and so forth. If it is faulty prajñā? No, because of views: because
> views are associated with it, and because it is taught as a defilement of
> prajñā.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The scriptural descriptions of ignorance as fetter, bondage, latent
> tendency, flood, and yoke confirm that it is an operative factor rather than
> a mere absence. Nor can ignorance be reduced to faulty discriminative
> comprehension. Wrong views are themselves instances of faulty prajñā, yet
> they are said to be associated with ignorance; moreover, ignorance is
> taught as that which defiles prajñā. These relations require ignorance,
> faulty view, and discriminative comprehension to remain functionally
> distinct.

The explicit identification of wrong views with `ku-prajñā` and the two
non-identity arguments are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Ignorance is not identical with a particular erroneous determination. A
> wrong view is already defective discrimination, while ignorance is the
> associated condition that deforms discriminative comprehension and enables
> such determinations to arise. Error therefore has both a determinate output
> and an operative condition of production. Correcting the former does not by
> itself guarantee transformation of the latter.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| saṃyojana | fetter | functional scriptural designation showing causal-operational status |
| ku | bad / defective | prefix qualifying the operation of prajñā |
| kuprajñā | faulty discriminative comprehension | class to which wrong views belong; rejected identity of avidyā |
| prajñā | discriminative comprehension | cognitive operation that can be defiled by avidyā |
| darśana | seeing / view | used elliptically for the doctrinal case of wrong views |
| dṛṣṭi | view / wrong view | faulty prajñā associated with avidyā |
| saṃprayukta | associated / conjoined | technical relation requiring distinguishable factors |
| upakleśa | defilement / contaminating factor | role of avidyā relative to prajñā |
| deśanā | teaching / exposition | doctrinal statement used as evidence for the distinction |
| avidyā | ignorance | distinct afflictive condition associated with view and defiling prajñā |

VAK 3.28–3.29 jointly exclude:

```text
avidyā = everything other than vidyā
avidyā = absence of vidyā
avidyā = faulty prajñā
avidyā = wrong view
```

The positive working determination is:

```text
avidyā
    = operative afflictive dharma
    = contrary to vidyā
    = associated with wrong view
    = defiling prajñā
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### Non-identity through association

The first proof has the form:

```text
dṛṣṭi is ku-prajñā

dṛṣṭi is associated with avidyā

therefore:
avidyā is not identical with ku-prajñā
in the relevant functional analysis
```

If the factors were identical, the association relation would collapse into
self-association and fail to express the doctrinal distinction being taught.

### Non-identity through defilement

The second proof is even more direct:

```text
avidyā defiles prajñā

what defiles and what is defiled
must be functionally distinguishable

therefore:
avidyā ≠ prajñā
```

The Bhāṣya formulates the reductio: if ignorance were itself prajñā, how could
it be a defilement of prajñā?

### Two levels of error

```text
level 1:
    dṛṣṭi / ku-prajñā
    a determinate erroneous view

level 2:
    avidyā
    the operative condition associated with and deforming discrimination
```

This distinction extends the causal grammar of 3.27:

```text
avidyā
    conditions distorted cognitive determination
    and karmic formation

distorted determinations
    reproduce the practical field
    in which avidyā remains operative
```

The law of appearance can therefore be faulted at the generative level, not
only at the level of one finished appearance.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse is crucial for understanding why `Loka` cannot mean merely this
Earth or an inventory of Things. Vasubandhu is analyzing the conditions under
which anything becomes determinately available within a World.

```text
World
    ≠ another Thing behind appearing things

World
    = lawful articulation through which
      determinations arise, relate, affect, motivate, and reproduce
```

VAK 3.29 locates ignorance inside that law of appearance. Avidyā is not simply
one false object blocking an otherwise unobstructed view. Nor is it identical
with the wrong view that results. It is the associated defiling condition
under which discriminative comprehension becomes faulty.

```text
avidyā
    → prajñā becomes defiled
    → dṛṣṭi arises as faulty determination
    → the World is practically organized through that determination
```

This is Hegel-clean because the distinction remains determinate. We do not
place a mysterious World behind appearances, and we do not flatten appearance
into isolated Things. We reconstruct the relations through which a Thing
comes to count as cause, result, desired object, practical end, and support
for renewed determination.

Within our Yogācāra assimilation, the World System is established in Me as
this law of appearance. The work of Jñāna-mārga cannot stop with correcting a
single false proposition. It must transform the condition that repeatedly
generates distorted Views and the practical World organized through them.

The Kośa's primary claim is more exact and limited: wrong views are faulty
prajñā associated with avidyā, while avidyā is a distinct factor taught as a
defilement of prajñā. That compact distinction is the basic Kārikā result.
