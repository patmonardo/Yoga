# VAK_3.05

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> नानात्वकायसंज्ञाश्च नानाकायैकसंज्ञिनः ।
>
> विपर्ययाच्चैककायसंज्ञाश्चारूपिणस्त्रयः ॥ ३.५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nānātvakāyasaṃjñāś ca nānākāyaikasaṃjñinaḥ /
>
> viparyayāc caikakāyasaṃjñāś cārūpiṇas trayaḥ // 3.5 //

### Textual note

The working e-text transmits the final sequence as `cārūpiṇasrayaḥ`. The
reading `ca ārūpiṇaḥ trayaḥ`, “and the three formless [classes],” is adopted
because the Bhāṣya explicitly completes the classification with three
formless stations and VAK 3.06 names the resulting seven. This is a marked
source decision, not a silent normalization.

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
nānātva-kāya-saṃjñāḥ + ca → nānātvakāyasaṃjñāś ca
nānā-kāya-eka-saṃjñinaḥ   → nānākāyaikasaṃjñinaḥ
viparyayāt + ca            → viparyayāc ca
eka-kāya-saṃjñāḥ           → ekakāyasaṃjñāḥ
ca + ārūpiṇaḥ              → cārūpiṇaḥ
trayaḥ                     → trayaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| nānātva-kāya-saṃjñāḥ | nominative plural compressed expression | those having diversity of body and perception |
| nānā-kāya-eka-saṃjñinaḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | those having diverse bodies and one perception |
| viparyayāt | ablative singular of `viparyaya` | by reversal of the preceding combinations |
| eka-kāya-saṃjñāḥ | nominative plural compressed expression | those having one body with the corresponding diverse and single perceptions |
| ārūpiṇaḥ | nominative masculine plural | the formless ones |
| trayaḥ | nominative masculine plural numeral | three |

The compressed verse does not restate both one-body combinations in full.
The Bhāṣya supplies them: one body with diverse perceptions and one body with
one perception.

## 4. Grammar

The verse constructs four embodied classes through two binary features:

```text
kāya
    nānā — diverse
    eka  — one

saṃjñā
    nānā — diverse
    eka  — one
```

The overt first two combinations are:

```text
nānā-kāya + nānā-saṃjñā
nānā-kāya + eka-saṃjñā
```

`Viparyayāt`, “by reversal,” directs recovery of the two one-body cases:

```text
eka-kāya + nānā-saṃjñā
eka-kāya + eka-saṃjñā
```

The three formless classes are then added:

| Body configuration | Perception configuration | Class number |
|---|---|---:|
| diverse | diverse | 1 |
| diverse | one | 2 |
| one | diverse | 3 |
| one | one | 4 |
| formless | first included mode | 5 |
| formless | second included mode | 6 |
| formless | third included mode | 7 |

VAK 3.05 constructs the seven-member list. The name `vijñānasthiti`, station
or establishment of differentiated cognition, occurs in VAK 3.06 and the
Bhāṣya's continuous exposition, not in the wording of this verse.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Those with diverse bodies and diverse perceptions; those with diverse
> bodies and one perception; by reversal, those with one body and [diverse or
> one] perception; and the three formless [classes].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Four embodied configurations are distinguished: diverse bodies with diverse
> perceptions, diverse bodies with one perception, one body with diverse
> perceptions, and one body with one perception. Together with three formless
> classes, these form the seven stations of differentiated cognition named in
> the following verse.

The matrix completion and identification with seven `vijñānasthiti` are
Bhāṣya-informed. They clarify the elliptical Kārikā rather than adding a new
primary claim to it.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The World System is further articulated through configurations of
> embodiment and perception. Bodily unity does not guarantee perceptual unity,
> nor bodily diversity perceptual diversity. Their independent variation
> generates four embodied modes, to which three formless modes are added as
> distinct establishments of differentiated cognition.

The verse thus moves beyond the destination occupied by a being to the formal
configuration through which a world is embodied and recognized.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Sanskrit | Controlled rendering | Determination in this unit |
|---|---|---|
| kāya | body / embodiment-configuration | bodily complex classified by color, sex-characteristics, and configuration in the Bhāṣya |
| saṃjñā | perception / recognition | recognitional determination through which pleasure, pain, neutrality, danger, or safety is apprehended |
| nānā / nānātva | diverse / diversity | differentiated feature-value of either body or perception |
| eka / ekatva | one / unity | common or non-different feature-value of either body or perception |
| viparyaya | reversal | instruction to invert the preceding feature combinations; no developed doctrine of epistemic error is asserted here |
| ārūpin | formless one | a class belonging to the formless-domain |
| vijñānasthiti | station of differentiated cognition | Bhāṣya and VAK 3.06 name for the completed sevenfold classification |
| abhinirvṛtta | newly arisen / produced | Bhāṣya term distinguishing newly arisen from long-established members of a class |
| saṃvartanīkuśala | knowledgeable about contraction | Bhāṣya term explaining one perceptual response to a cosmic event |
| vivartanīkuśala | knowledgeable about expansion | corresponding knowledge of the cosmic cycle |

The four embodied classes are exemplified in the Bhāṣya as follows:

| Configuration | Exemplary beings | Bhāṣya criterion |
|---|---|---|
| diverse body, diverse perception | humans and certain devas | bodily differences and perceptions of pleasure, pain, or neutrality |
| diverse body, one perception | newly arisen Brahmakāyikas | diverse bodies with a common perception |
| one body, diverse perception | Ābhāsvaras | common bodily form but fear or non-fear before the cosmic fire |
| one body, one perception | Śubhakṛtsnas | common bodily characteristics and common perception of pleasure |

These examples belong to the commentary. They are not names present in VAK
3.05 itself.

## 8. Logical Determination

The World System now has a layered address:

```text
dhātu
    domain

gati
    resultant destiny within a domain

kāya-saṃjñā configuration
    embodiment and perceptual determination within a destiny

vijñānasthiti
    station in which differentiated cognition is established
```

The verse performs a genuine feature construction:

```text
EmbodiedConfiguration
    = BodyValue × PerceptionValue

BodyValue
    = {Diverse, One}

PerceptionValue
    = {Diverse, One}

cardinality
    = 2 × 2
    = 4
```

Three formless modes extend the classification to seven, but they do not fit
the embodied 2×2 matrix as though they merely had a null body-value. Their
formlessness was already determined in VAK 3.03 through the negation of
station and a different principle of differentiation.

The Ābhāsvara example establishes an important non-entailment:

```text
same body-configuration
    does not entail
same perceptual determination
```

Those newly arisen apprehend the cosmic fire through fear; those acquainted
with contraction and expansion recognize the same event without fear. The
event is common, but the recognitional relation differs.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.05 supplies an interface schema for the World System. A sentient
continuity is not adequately determined merely by saying where it has arrived.
Its embodiment and mode of recognition must also be specified.

```text
AgentAppearance {
    domain,
    destiny,
    bodyConfiguration,
    perceptionConfiguration,
    cognitionStation
}
```

This is an Organon reconstruction, not an Abhidharma data structure. Yet it is
earned by the Kārikā's matrix: `kāya` and `saṃjñā` vary independently, and
their lawful combinations determine distinguishable world-modes.

The hidden embodiment process remains a live hypothesis:

```text
Subtle Transition Process
    relinquishes one embodied configuration
    mediates antarābhava
    installs another body-perception interface

Empirical Agent
    ordinarily encounters the installed configuration
    without cognizing the transition machinery
```

VAK 3.05 does not reveal that machinery. It tells us what must be configured
on the embodied side: body and perception. The preceding verse distinguished
transition from destination; this verse begins specifying the interface that
a destination exhibits.

This is where the playful **Siva Trademark** can remain philosophically alive
without becoming textual attribution. If “picking up and disposing of bodies”
names a divine-level operation, the Kośa has not given us that code here. It
has given a disciplined schema of the configurations upon which such an
operation would have to act. Any relation to Siva or to Sankara's own teaching
must be developed from their sources in a separate comparative layer.

The strongest immediate result is neither mythological nor reductively
physical:

```text
World appearance
    is embodied
    but is not determined by body alone

Perception
    is configured with embodiment
    but is not mechanically identical to bodily sameness

Cognition-station
    is the determinate relation of a sentient continuity
    to a mode of World
```
