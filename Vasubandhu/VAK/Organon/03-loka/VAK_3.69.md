# VAK_3.69

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तत ऊर्ध्वं विमानेषु देवाः कामभुजस्तु षट् ।
>
> द्वन्द्वालिङ्गनपाण्याप्तिहसितेक्षितमैथुनाः ॥ ३.६९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tata ūrdhvaṃ vimāneṣu devāḥ kāmabhujas tu ṣaṭ /
>
> dvandvāliṅganapāṇyāptihasitekṣitamaithunāḥ // 3.69 //

The local Kārikā witness has apparent orthographic noise in the second line,
including `vasita`. The Bhāṣya's distributive explanation confirms `hasita`,
“smile” or “laughter,” adopted here.

```text

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| tataḥ | ablatival adverb | from there, namely above the Trāyastriṃśa level |
| ūrdhvam | adverbial accusative | upward / above |
| vimāneṣu | locative neuter plural | in celestial residences or aerial mansions |
| devāḥ | nominative masculine plural | devas |
| kāmabhujaḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | enjoyers of sensual pleasures |
| tu | adversative or restrictive particle | but; restricts enjoyment of kāma to six classes |
| ṣaṭ | numeral | six |

| āliṅgana | compound member | embrace |

| hasita | compound member | smile or laughter |
| īkṣita | compound member | look or glance |
| maithunāḥ | nominative masculine plural | having sexual union through the enumerated modes |

The compound lists five modes for six classes. The Bhāṣya resolves the apparent
count mismatch by assigning the first mode, bodily pairing, jointly to the
first two classes.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first half contains two related determinations with different scopes:

```text
tataḥ ūrdhvam
    devāḥ vimāneṣu [pratiṣṭhitāḥ]

kāma-bhujaḥ tu
    ṣaṭ [deva-nikāyāḥ]
```

> Above that, devas are established in celestial residences; but the classes
> that enjoy sensual pleasures are six.

The Bhāṣya explains the first statement broadly: above the Thirty-Three are
the Yāmas, Tuṣitas, Nirmāṇaratis, Paranirmitavaśavartins, and the sixteen
previously stated higher stations beginning with the Brahmakāyikas. The
restrictive `tu` then returns to the six kāmāvacara deva classes alone.

The second line is distributive rather than a single undifferentiated
predicate:

```text
six kāma-deva classes
    [respectively] have maithuna through
    pairing, embrace, hand-contact, smile, and glance
```

The first mode applies to two classes; the remaining four modes apply to one
class each.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Above that, devas dwell in celestial residences; but the enjoyers of sensual
> pleasures are six. Their sexual unions occur through bodily pairing,
> embrace, hand-contact, smile, and glance.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Above the Trāyastriṃśas, devas dwell in celestial residences. Only six deva
> classes, however—from the Cāturmahārājakāyikas through the
> Paranirmitavaśavartins—enjoy the pleasures of kāma. The earth-connected
> Cāturmahārājakāyikas and Trāyastriṃśas unite bodily as humans do, although
> without emission of semen; their sexual heat ceases through the release of
> wind. For the Yāmas an embrace suffices, for the Tuṣitas contact of the hand,
> for the Nirmāṇaratis a smile, and for the Paranirmitavaśavartins a glance.
> As the objects become more intense, passion too becomes more intense.

The enumeration of the higher deva stations, restriction to six kāma classes,
class-by-class distribution, physiological qualifications, and relation of
object-intensity to passion-intensity are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Elevation differentiates the medium of enjoyment without necessarily
> weakening desire. Across the six kāma-deva classes, satisfaction requires
> progressively less gross bodily contact: intercourse gives way to embrace,
> hand-contact, smile, and glance. Yet the Bhāṣya simultaneously states that
> more intense objects correspond to more intense passion. Spatial height,
> physical mediation, object-power, and affective intensity therefore vary on
> distinct axes. A subtler interface with an object is not thereby freedom
> from the determining force of that object.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Six-class distributive mapping

| Ordinal | Deva class | Habitation relation | Mode sufficient for sexual satisfaction |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cāturmahārājakāyika | earth-connected | bodily intercourse |
| 2 | Trāyastriṃśa | earth-connected | bodily intercourse |
| 3 | Yāma | celestial residence | embrace |
| 4 | Tuṣita | celestial residence | hand-contact |
| 5 | Nirmāṇarati | celestial residence | smile |
| 6 | Paranirmitavaśavartin | celestial residence | glance |

The syntax supplies five mode-terms; the Bhāṣya supplies the non-bijective
mapping:

```text
mode 1 → classes 1 and 2
mode 2 → class 3
mode 3 → class 4
mode 4 → class 5
mode 5 → class 6
```

### Scope discrimination

```text
devas in celestial residences above Trāyastriṃśa:
    four higher kāma classes
    + sixteen previously stated higher stations

devas who enjoy sensual pleasures:
    six kāmāvacara classes only
```

The location-predicate `dwells-in-vimāna` and the domain-predicate
`enjoys-kāma` have different extensions. The restrictive `tu` prevents their
collapse.

### Independent gradients

Let `C` be gross bodily contact, `O` object-intensity, and `R` passion-intensity
as the series ascends:

```text
C decreases:
    intercourse > embrace > hand-contact > smile > glance

O increases:
    lower objects < higher objects

R increases with O:
    O↑ → R↑
```

The passage therefore does not license:

```text
less bodily contact → less passion
```

Instead, reduced physical mediation can coexist with intensified object and
passion.

### Satisfaction threshold

For each class `D`, let `M(D)` be the minimal described mode whose occurrence
terminates sexual heat:

```text
M(Yāma)                       = embrace
M(Tuṣita)                     = hand-contact
M(Nirmāṇarati)                = smile
M(Paranirmitavaśavartin)      = glance
```

The sequence classifies differences in the operative threshold of
satisfaction, not degrees of moral or liberative attainment.

### Birth and relational assignment

The Bhāṣya further states:

```text
divine child manifests in lap of god or goddess
    → child is reckoned son or daughter of that pair
```

Parentage is assigned through the manifestation-event and its relational
location. This is a commentary determination ancillary to the verse's main
classification.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.69 begins the aerial ascent by refusing a single-dimensional conception
of “higher.” Several sequences are coordinated, but they do not all move in
the same evaluative direction:

```text
spatial elevation                 ↑
gross bodily mediation            ↓
intensity of desirable objects     ↑
intensity of passion               ↑
```

This is precisely where semantic discrimination must govern syntactic
enumeration. The visible list can be paraphrased as a smooth refinement from
intercourse to glance. Semantically, however, the Bhāṣya denies that this is a
purification from desire: the object becomes more powerful and passion more
intense even as the required physical operation becomes slighter.

Thus deva ascent is not merely “refined kāma” if refinement is heard as moral
improvement. It is better described as increased efficacy of the
object-relation:

```text
less gross mediation
    + stronger object-intensity
        → satisfaction through a slighter sign
        → no release from rāga
```

This follows directly from 3.68. Sudharmā showed that devas still require
practical deliberation; 3.69 shows why. Height and extraordinary capacity do
not make desire self-legislating or transform enjoyment into Dharma.

```ts
interface KamaDevaMode {
  devaClass: string;
  spatialLevel: number;
  satisfactionMode: "Intercourse" | "Embrace" | "HandContact" | "Smile" | "Glance";
  bodilyMediation: number;
  objectIntensity: number;
  passionIntensity: number;
}

const doctrinalConstraint =
  (lower: KamaDevaMode, higher: KamaDevaMode) =>
    higher.bodilyMediation < lower.bodilyMediation &&
    higher.objectIntensity > lower.objectIntensity &&
    higher.passionIntensity > lower.passionIntensity;
```

This is explanatory project notation, not Vasubandhu's vocabulary. Its purpose
is to block the false inference that all predicates attached to vertical
ascent form one monotonic scale.

The verse therefore strengthens the working distinction between World rank
and Dharma determination. A being may occupy a higher world, respond through
a subtler interface, and nevertheless be more intensely bound through its
objects.
