# VAK_3.60

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अर्धेन मेरोश्चन्द्रार्कौ पञ्चाशत्सैकयोजनौ ।
>
> अर्धरात्रोऽस्तंगमनं मध्याह्न उदयः सकृत् ॥ ३.६० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> ardhena meroś candrārkau pañcāśat-saika-yojanau /
>
> ardharātro 'staṃgamanaṃ madhyāhna udayaḥ sakṛt // 3.60 //

The Kārikā boundary is confirmed by both local primary Sanskrit and the
Bhāṣya. The primary e-text prints `madhyānha` and `sakṛta`; the grammatically
expected forms `madhyāhna` and `sakṛt`, also used by the Bhāṣya witness, are
adopted here with the e-text forms recorded as witness irregularities.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first statement gives level and ordered measures:

```text
candra-arkau
    meroḥ ardhena [ucchrāyeṇa]
    [carataḥ]
    yathākramam
        pañcāśat-yojanaḥ [candraḥ]
        saika-pañcāśat-yojanaḥ [arkaḥ]
```

The Bhāṣya interprets the level as even with Yugandhara's summit, forty
thousand yojanas above the water.

The second line coordinates four predicates under `sakṛt`:

```text
ardharātraḥ
astaṃgamanam
madhyāhnaḥ
udayaḥ
    sakṛt [bhavanti]
```

The Kārikā does not name the four local subjects. The Bhāṣya distributes the
states respectively across Uttarakuru, Pūrvavideha, Jambūdvīpa, and Godānīya.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> At half the height of Meru move the moon and sun, measuring fifty and
> fifty-one yojanas [respectively]. Midnight, sunset, midday, and sunrise occur
> simultaneously.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The moon and sun move at the level of Yugandhara's summit, halfway up Meru.
> The lunar disk measures fifty yojanas and the solar disk fifty-one. At one
> moment it is midnight in Uttarakuru, sunset in Pūrvavideha, midday in
> Jambūdvīpa, and sunrise in Godānīya; the assignment rotates cyclically with
> the celestial course. Beneath the solar vehicle is a fiery crystalline disk
> that heats and illuminates, while beneath the lunar vehicle is a watery disk
> that cools and illuminates. Their beneficial and harmful effects upon beings
> and organic life occur within the karmically conditioned common world.

The Yugandhara level, ordered disk sizes, continent-state mapping, cyclic
application, material disks, functions, and karmic qualification are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A single ordered celestial process yields four different but simultaneous
> local times. Midnight, sunset, midday, and sunrise are not independent
> absolute events; each is the phase under which one world-process appears at
> a determinate regional position. The unity of the process does not erase
> local difference, and local difference does not fragment the common world.
> Bhājanaloka has therefore become a synchronized multi-perspectival field in
> which Agents share one process while inhabiting distinct phase-relations to
> it.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Ordered distribution inside a dual expression

```text
[moon, sun]
    ↔ [50, 51] yojanas
```

The dual numerical compound does not assign fifty-one to both bodies. The
Bhāṣya's `yathākramam` establishes ordered correspondence.

### One process, four simultaneous local states

```text
global celestial phase at t
    + regional position
        → local temporal determination
```

At the stated moment:

| Region | Local state |
|---|---|
| Uttarakuru | midnight |
| Pūrvavideha | sunset |
| Jambūdvīpa | midday |
| Godānīya | sunrise |

The mapping is cyclic rather than permanent. As the common process advances,
each region successively occupies the other phase-relations.

### Relational opposition

```text
midnight ↔ midday
sunset   ↔ sunrise
```

These oppositions belong to one synchronized configuration. Each local state
is determined through its relation to the others and to the common celestial
course.

### Function is location-indexed

The Bhāṣya rejects the suggestion that one sun performs the same function at
the same time in all four continents:

```text
same celestial body
    ≠ same local function everywhere simultaneously
```

Identity of source does not imply identity of output when receiver-position
differs.

### Environmental functions remain karmically indexed

```text
solar/lunar material process
    → heating, cooling, illumination
        → effects on beings, bodies, fruits, flowers, crops, medicines
            within the efficacy of beings' karma
```

The physical process has stable functions, yet their concrete beneficial or
harmful effects belong to the karmically constituted Agent–environment field.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 3.60 supplies a rigorous method for reconciling global unity with local
plurality:

```text
one shared process
    + indexed regional position
        → multiple simultaneous local states
```

This is more exact than saying merely that different observers have different
perspectives. The Bhāṣya gives an explicit state vector across four regions at
one time. Each entry is locally true, all entries are globally compatible, and
the mapping changes according to one cyclic process.

For our Semantic pipeline, a temporal predicate must therefore be indexed:

```ts
interface LocalWorldTime {
  globalProcess: "solar-course";
  instant: string;
  region: "Uttarakuru" | "Purvavideha" | "Jambudvipa" | "Godaniya";
  localPhase: "midnight" | "sunset" | "midday" | "sunrise";
}
```

This is explanatory notation, not Vasubandhu's terminology. It demonstrates
why “it is midday” is incomplete as a semantic claim unless the regional frame
is supplied. The syntax may be monadic, but the truth-condition is relational.

The Agential character of the Physical World continues. Celestial functions
are specified by the differentiated effects they have upon embodied beings
and living processes. Loka provides the synchronized environment and its local
outputs; Karma remains implicated in how those outputs become beneficial or
harmful results for Agents.
