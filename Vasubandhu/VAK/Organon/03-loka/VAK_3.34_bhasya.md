# VAK_3.34 Bhāṣya — Lower, Own, and Higher Object-Support

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
svo 'ṣṭālambanam ārūpyo dvayor dhyānadvaye tu ṣaṭ /
kāmāḥ ṣaṇṇāṃ caturṇāṃ sva ekasyālambanaṃ paraḥ // 3.34 //
```

> Their own realm is the object-support of eight, and the formless realm of
> two. But in the latter pair of concentrations there are six. The desire
> realm is the object-support of all six, their own realm of four, and the
> higher realm of one.

The Bhāṣya completes the distribution of mental rangings connected with the
form realm. The opening continues the first and second concentrations from
VAK 3.33; the remainder treats the third and fourth concentrations.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
gandharasopavicārāṃś caturo hitvā.

ārūpyo dvayoḥ.

dhyānadvaye tu ṣaṭ.

tṛtīyacaturthayor dhyānayoḥ ṣaḍ upekṣopavicārā eva santi, nānye. teṣāṃ
punar ālambanam:

kāmāḥ ṣaṇṇām.

kāmadhātuḥ ṣaṇṇām apy ālambanam.

caturṇāṃ svaḥ.

rūpadhātuś caturṇāṃ, tatra gandharasābhāvāt.

ārūpyadhātur ekasyaiva dharmopavicārasyālambanam. uktā

```

The running witness duplicates `sa eva rūpadhātus teṣāṃ svaḥ` at 148.13 and
has minor mechanical irregularities in compounds and sandhi. The continuous
text removes the duplicate and regularizes those forms provisionally without
claiming to establish a critical edition.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Their own realm is the object-support of eight. That very form realm is
“their own” realm. It is the object-support of eight mental rangings, the four
rangings over odor and taste being omitted.

The formless realm is the object-support of two—the two dharma-rangings.

But in the other pair of concentrations there are six. In the third and
fourth concentrations only the six equanimity-rangings exist, and no others.
Their object-supports are as follows.

The desire realm is the object-support of all six. Their own form realm is
the object-support of four because odor and taste are absent there. The
higher, formless realm is the object-support of only one, the dharma-ranging.

The mental rangings connected with the form realm have now been stated.

## 4. Argument and Natural Boundary

This unit contains no extended objection. It completes one table in two
steps:

1. For the twelve rangings of the first and second concentrations established
   in VAK 3.33, it specifies eight with the form realm and two with the
   formless realm as object-support.
2. For the third and fourth concentrations, it restricts the available
   affective class to six equanimity-rangings and distributes their lower,
   own, and higher object-supports as six, four, and one.

The closure `uktā rūpapratisaṃyuktāḥ`—“the form-realm-connected rangings have
been stated”—marks the natural endpoint. The next unit turns to rangings
connected with the formless realm and belongs to VAK 3.35.

## 5. First and Second Concentrations

VAK 3.33 established twelve rangings in the first two concentrations:

```text
6 gladness-rangings
+ 6 equanimity-rangings
= 12
```

VAK 3.34 specifies their upward and own-realm reach:

```text
own form realm
    4 available object-fields
    × 2 affective modes
    = 8 rangings

higher formless realm
    1 available dharma-field
    × 2 affective modes
    = 2 rangings
```

The four exclusions from the own realm are odor and taste under each of the
two affective modes. The two rangings toward the formless realm are the
gladness-dharma and equanimity-dharma rangings.

## 6. Third and Fourth Concentrations

The latter pair contains only:

```text
6 upekṣopavicāras
    equanimity-rangings across six object-fields
```

Neither the six distress-rangings nor the six gladness-rangings occur there.
This is a restriction of affective mode, not yet a restriction to one
object-field. The six equanimity-rangings retain differentiated object-range
according to the realm serving as object-support.

Thus higher meditative placement does not simply subtract objects. It first
changes which affective mode can operate across them.

## 7. The Six-Four-One Distribution

For the six equanimity-rangings of the third and fourth concentrations:

| Object-support | Count | Available fields |
|---|---:|---|
| lower desire realm | 6 | form, sound, odor, taste, tangible, dharma |
| own form realm | 4 | form, sound, tangible, dharma |
| higher formless realm | 1 | dharma only |

The counts are not arbitrary permissions. They follow from the constitution
of each realm:

```text
desire realm
    all six fields present → 6

form realm
    odor and taste absent → 4

formless realm
    sensory fields absent → 1
```

`Sva` and `para` are relational terms centered on the form-realm station.
The desire realm is lower, the form realm is own, and the formless realm is
higher. None of these object-relations changes the form-realm membership of
the rangings themselves.

## 8. Three Axes of Admissibility

An admissible ranging is determined by at least three coordinates:

```text
realm of belonging
    × affective mode
    × object-support field
```

The unit shows why no coordinate can replace the others:

- form-realm membership does not restrict the object to the form realm;
- third- or fourth-concentration station removes gladness and distress but
  leaves six equanimity-directed fields available toward the desire realm;
- the target realm restricts which of those fields it can actually supply.

Consequently, ascent in concentration is not equivalent to the disappearance
of the lower realm. The lower field remains cognitively available, but it is
taken up under a transformed affective mode.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

For the Organon, the passage offers a constrained access relation:

```text
Access {
    agentStation
    affectiveMode
    objectRealm
    objectField
}
```

This is a project reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's data model. Its source
warrant is the Bhāṣya's refusal to infer object-range from station alone. A
form-realm operation can take the desire realm as object-support while
remaining form-realm-connected.

The shift from the first two to the latter two concentrations is also a model
of transformation without content-erasure. The lower Dataset remains
available; what changes is the mode of access. Gladness and distress no
longer organize the ranging, while equanimity does. A new agent-state can
therefore reconfigure how an existing domain is traversed without deleting or
relocating that domain.

At the same time, access is objectively typed. The target realm cannot supply
an object-field absent from its constitution. In Ocular terms, the validity
of a query depends jointly upon agent-state and target schema. Cross-domain
analysis is possible, but it does not license fabrication of unavailable
dimensions.

This continues the previous Study's distinction among continuum,
realm-membership, eliciting condition, and object-support. Here the special
advance is the explicit lower–own–higher topology and its numerical
constraints.

## 10. Review Status

This Study restores the complete compact unit at 148.12–148.24. It preserves
the continuation from VAK 3.33, the eight and two object-ranges of the first
two concentrations, the restriction of the latter two concentrations to six
equanimity-rangings, and their six-four-one lower, own, and higher
distribution.

The Sanskrit and translation remain provisional. The duplicated source line,
the compact syntax of `svo 'ṣṭālambanam`, the precise extension of
`dhyānadvaya`, and the relation between the verse's counts and the inherited
eighteenfold matrix should be checked against critical Sanskrit, Tibetan,
Chinese, and established translations. The Study stops at the explicit
closure of the form-realm-connected rangings and does not begin VAK 3.35.
