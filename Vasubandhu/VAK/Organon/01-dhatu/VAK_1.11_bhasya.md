# VAK_1.11 Bhāṣya — Avijñapti as Ethically Determined Material Continuity

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // 1.11 //

> The wholesome or unwholesome continuity that belongs even to one whose
> mind is distracted or absent, depending upon the great elements, is called
> avijñapti.

The Bhāṣya explains distraction as having a different mind, identifies the
mindless attainments, and expands the scope of “even.” It then distinguishes
this continuity from acquisition, explains the name avijñapti, and gives a
summary definition through material nature, ethical quality, and origin.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> avijñaptir idānīṃ vaktavyā / seyam ucyate / vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo
> 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ / mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate /
> vikṣiptacittakasyeti tadanyacittasyāpi / acittakasyāpīty
> asaṃjñinirodhasamāpattisamāpannasyāpi / apiśabdenāvikṣiptasacittasyāpīti
> vijñāyate / yo 'nubandha iti yaḥ pravāhaḥ / śubhāśubha iti kuśalākuśalaḥ /
> kuśalākuśale prāptivāho 'py astīdṛśa iti tadviśeṣaṇārtham ucyate
> mahābhūtāny upādāyeti / hetvartha upādāyārtha iti vaibhāṣikāḥ /
> jananādihetubhāvāt /
>
> sa hy avijñaptir iti hiśabdas tannāmakaraṇavijñāpanārthaḥ /
> rūpakriyāsvabhāvāpi satī vijñaptivat paraṃ na vijñāpayatīty avijñaptiḥ /
> ucyata iti ācāryavacanaṃ darśayati / samāsatas tu
> vijñaptisamādhisaṃbhūtaṃ kuśalākuśalaṃ rūpam avijñaptiḥ /

The natural unit begins with the announcement of avijñapti at printed
location 007.29 and ends with the summary at 008.09–10. The preceding
closure concerning the five sensory objects belongs to 1.10. The following
question, asking which great elements have been mentioned, opens 1.12.

Word division and punctuation have been supplied for reading. The running
source's `anubaṇdhaḥ` and separated `anu bandha` are normalized to
`anubandhaḥ` and `anubandha`; a broken page marker before `ucyata iti` is
removed. The research kārikā's Devanāgarī first line reads
`vikṣiptacittakasyāpi`, while its IAST and the running verse read
`vikṣiptācittakasyāpi`. The latter is followed here: the Bhāṣya separately
explains both distracted and mindless cases. The original witnesses are
preserved, and this is a normalized reading text rather than a critical
edition.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Avijñapti must now be explained. It is stated as follows: the wholesome or
> unwholesome continuity that belongs even to one whose mind is distracted
> or absent, depending upon the great elements, is called avijñapti.
>
> “Of one whose mind is distracted” means also of one who has a different
> mind. “Even of one without mind” means also of one who has entered the
> attainment of non-perception or the attainment of cessation. By the word
> “even,” one who is undistracted and has mind is also understood. “The
> continuity” means the stream. “Wholesome or unwholesome” means kuśala or
> akuśala.
>
> A similar stream of acquisition also exists with regard to the wholesome
> and unwholesome. To distinguish this from that, “depending upon the great
> elements” is stated. The Vaibhāṣikas say that “depending upon” has a causal
> meaning, because the elements function as causes beginning with production.
>
> In “that indeed is avijñapti,” the word “indeed” serves to indicate the
> assignment of that name. Although it has the nature of material form and
> action, it does not make something known to another as manifestation does;
> hence it is avijñapti. “Is called” indicates the teacher's statement. In
> summary, avijñapti is wholesome or unwholesome material form arising from
> manifestation or samādhi.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The definition completes the three-part enumeration of material form begun
in 1.09: five faculties, five sensory objects, and avijñapti. The prose
unpacks the verse in order and then adds a compact summary:

```text
cases in which continuity persists
    → different mind, mindless attainment, and undistracted mind
continuity and ethical quality
    → stream; kuśala or akuśala
distinguishing qualification
    → dependence on great elements, separating it from acquisition
explanation of the designation
    → material form and action without disclosure to another
summary
    → ethically determined rūpa arising from manifestation or samādhi
```

No distinct objector speaks within this unit. The comparison with acquisition
is an explanatory test of the definition. The causal gloss of `upādāya` is
expressly attributed to the Vaibhāṣikas; `ucyate` is explained as reporting
a teacher's statement. That teacher is not named more precisely here.

## 5. A Stream across Different Mental Conditions

**The scope of “even.”** The kārikā's compressed
`vikṣiptācittakasyāpi` is unpacked through two separate expressions:
`vikṣiptacittakasya` and `acittakasya`. The first is glossed by
`tadanyacittasya`, one who has a mind other than that. “Distracted” therefore
has the local sense of a different mental occurrence, rather than requiring
agitation. The originating mental orientation supplies the intelligible
contrast, but the brief gloss does not narrate a particular originating act.

The second expression is explained through the attainments of non-perception
and cessation. The prose thus specifies technical cases of absence of mind.
It does not equate every ordinary lapse of attention with a mindless
attainment.

`Api` includes the undistracted person who has mind as well. These are cases
in which avijñapti can continue, not an assertion that every person in each
mental condition necessarily possesses every kind of avijñapti.

**Continuity and ethical determination.** `Yo ... sa` correlates the stream
specified by the verse with the designation avijñapti. The singular
`anubandhaḥ`, qualified by `śubhāśubhaḥ`, is glossed as `pravāha`, a stream.
This supports translating “continuity” without making it a static deposit.
The passage does not yet describe the full mechanism of the stream's
successive production or the conditions that terminate it.

The gloss `kuśalākuśalaḥ` gives the technical ethical determination of
`śubhāśubhaḥ`. The definition includes wholesome and unwholesome material
continuities. It supplies no third, ethically indeterminate category here.

The central temporal claim is precise: continuation of this stream does not
require uninterrupted continuation of the same occurrent mind. Its ethical
determination and its present mental accompaniment must therefore be
analyzed separately.

## 6. Why Dependence on the Great Elements Is Necessary

Ethically determined continuity alone would leave the definition too broad.
The Bhāṣya names a similar `prāptivāha`, stream of acquisition, with regard
to wholesome and unwholesome states. The locative `kuśalākuśale` introduces
that field of comparison; `tadviśeṣaṇārtham` states the purpose of the
additional qualification: to distinguish the present stream from it.

```text
continuity associated with wholesome or unwholesome determination
    → also applicable to acquisition

continuity dependent upon great elements
    → the distinguishing material qualification supplied here
```

The accusative `mahābhūtāni` is governed by the absolutive `upādāya`,
“taking as basis” or “depending upon.” The commentary then marks a specific
interpretation: the Vaibhāṣikas understand that dependence causally. The
reason is `jananādihetubhāvāt`, because of causal functioning beginning
with production. The `ādi` indicates further functions without enumerating
them in this passage.

The summary's explicit `rūpam` confirms the material classification. The
comparison does not license treating acquisition and avijñapti as two names
for the same continuity. Nor does the elemental clause explain every causal
detail of avijñapti here; the great elements themselves become the next
subject of analysis.

## 7. Non-Disclosure, Doctrinal Designation, and Origin

**What is not disclosed.** The concessive `api satī` in
`rūpakriyāsvabhāvāpi satī` means “although having the nature of material
form and action.” It is followed by the contrast with `vijñapti`:
`paraṃ na vijñāpayati`, it does not make something known to another.

`Param` identifies the other person to whom disclosure would occur. The
content made known is not separately expressed in this sentence; “something”
in the translation preserves that ellipsis. Action or intention can clarify
the contrast in explanatory discussion, but neither is inserted as an
explicit Sanskrit object.

The non-disclosure is consequently a particular relation. The passage does
not define avijñapti as unknowable in every respect, nor does it identify
non-disclosure with the absence of cognition in its bearer. Indeed, the
previous explanation of `api` has included the undistracted conscious case.

**The assigned name.** The Bhāṣya explains `hi` through the communication
of the act of naming: the verse indicates why this stream bears the name
avijñapti. The passive `ucyate`, “is called,” points to a teacher's
statement. These remarks register a technical doctrinal designation rather
than treating the term as self-explanatory. The local wording does not
identify the teacher or settle the author's broader relation to that
teacher's position.

**Manifestation and samādhi.** The final compound
`vijñaptisamādhisaṃbhūtam` qualifies `rūpam`, together with
`kuśalākuśalam`. Its paired sources are manifestation and samādhi. The
summary thereby adds origin to the verse's characterization by continuity,
ethical quality, and elemental dependence.

| Determination | Bhāṣya expression |
|---|---|
| continuity | `pravāha` |
| ethical quality | `kuśala` or `akuśala` |
| material dependence | `mahābhūtāny upādāya` |
| lack of outward disclosure | `paraṃ na vijñāpayati` |
| origins named in the summary | `vijñapti` and `samādhi` |
| explicit category | `rūpa` |

The paired origins and paired ethical qualities are not an unrestricted
cross-classification. The sentence does not say that each origin produces
both ethical qualities in every combination. Their detailed distribution
must await further textual determination.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. The distracted and mindless cases are both required. The Bhāṣya supports
   the IAST and running-source reading where the research Devanāgarī differs.
2. “Distracted” means having a different mind in the local gloss; the
   mindless cases are specified through two attainments.
3. “Even” also includes an undistracted person with mind, so avijñapti is
   not restricted to mental absence or diversion.
4. `Anubandha` is a stream. “Trace” alone would leave its continuity unclear.
5. Dependence on great elements distinguishes it from a similar stream of
   acquisition. The causal explanation is explicitly Vaibhāṣika.
6. Non-disclosure concerns making something known to another, while the
   stream retains the nature of material form and action.
7. Origin from manifestation or samādhi belongs to the Bhāṣya's summary,
   rather than to the wording of the kārikā itself.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage separates three relations that can easily be merged: the mental
state presently occurring, the continuation of an ethical determination,
and its disclosure to another person. A continuing determination can remain
while the mind changes and without manifesting that determination outwardly.
The account gives this continuity a material status through dependence on
the great elements.

For the Organon reconstruction, this is a precise expansion of the field
under study. A determination's continuation, its present mental
accompaniment, and its communicability are distinct questions. The comparison
with acquisition adds a fourth: two continuities can resemble one another
in ethical and temporal respects while differing in their constitutive
basis.

Our inquiry into learning can use these distinctions to ask how a developed
determination persists beyond the act in which it was formed, and how that
persistence differs from a present act of knowing or an outward expression.
This is an analogous question for the Organon study. The Bhāṣya's avijñapti
is specifically ethically determined rūpa; the passage does not identify
it with learned understanding, prajñā, or memory.

The inclusion of samādhi as an origin is especially significant for the
relation between cultivation and its continuing determination. Here the
text supplies that origin without explaining its complete operation. The
next inquiry into the elements develops the material basis of the account.
