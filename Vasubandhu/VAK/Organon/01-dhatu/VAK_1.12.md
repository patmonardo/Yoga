# VAK_1.12

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> भूतानि पृथिवीधातुरप्तेजोवायुधातवः ।
>
> धृत्यादिकर्मसंसिद्धाः खरस्नेहोष्णतेरणाः ॥ १.१२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> bhūtāni pṛthivīdhātur aptejovāyudhātavaḥ /
>
> dhṛtyādikarmasaṃsiddhāḥ kharasnehoṣṇateraṇāḥ // 1.12 //

## 3. Padaccheda

```text
pṛthivīdhātur           → pṛthivī-dhātuḥ
aptejovāyudhātavaḥ      → ap-tejo-vāyu-dhātavaḥ
dhṛtyādikarmasaṃsiddhāḥ → dhṛti-ādi-karma-saṃsiddhāḥ
kharasnehoṣṇateraṇāḥ    → khara-sneha-uṣṇatā-īraṇāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| bhūtāni | nominative neuter plural | the elements; the great material bases |
| pṛthivī-dhātuḥ | nominative masculine singular | earth-domain |
| ap-tejo-vāyu-dhātavaḥ | nominative masculine plural | the water-, fire-, and wind-domains |
| dhṛti | feminine noun in compound | support, sustaining |
| ādi | first member of a compound | beginning with; indicates a series supplied by the Bhāṣya |
| karma | neuter noun in compound | function, operation |
| saṃsiddhāḥ | nominative masculine plural, past passive participle | established, realized, proved |
| khara | adjectival substantive | hardness, resistance |
| sneha | masculine noun | cohesion, adhesiveness, moisture |
| uṣṇatā | feminine abstract noun | heat |
| īraṇā | feminine action noun | impulsion, setting in motion |

The Bhāṣya expands the compressed functional series in `dhṛtyādi`:

```text
dhṛti      = support
saṃgraha   = cohesion or gathering
pakti      = maturation or ripening
vyūhana    = expansion, growth, and spreading
```

It then treats `khara`, `sneha`, `uṣṇatā`, and `īraṇā` as the corresponding
intrinsic characteristics rather than as a repetition of those functions.

## 4. Grammar

The first half identifies the members of the class `bhūtāni`:

```text
bhūtāni
    = pṛthivī-dhātuḥ
    + ap-dhātuḥ
    + tejo-dhātuḥ
    + vāyu-dhātuḥ
```

In `ap-tejo-vāyu-dhātavaḥ`, the final member `dhātu` distributes across
water, fire, and wind. Together with the separately stated
`pṛthivī-dhātuḥ`, the expression names four domains.

`Dhṛti-ādi-karma-saṃsiddhāḥ` is a plural predicate agreeing with the four
domains. The compound says that they are established in functions beginning
with support; the Bhāṣya supplies all four functions and distributes them in
the order of the domains.

The final series supplies a second, parallel distribution:

| Domain | Function (`karma`) | Intrinsic characteristic (`svabhāva`) |
|---|---|---|
| earth | `dhṛti` — support | `khara` — hardness |
| water | `saṃgraha` — cohesion | `sneha` — cohesiveness |
| fire | `pakti` — maturation | `uṣṇatā` — heat |
| wind | `vyūhana` — expansion | `īraṇā` — impulsion |

This grammatical parallel must not collapse function into characteristic.
The prose explicitly asks both “in what function are they established?” and
“what is their intrinsic nature?”

The full construe is:

```text
[bhūtāni]
[pṛthivī-dhātuḥ ap-tejo-vāyu-dhātavaḥ];
[dhṛti-ādi-karma-saṃsiddhāḥ],
[khara-sneha-uṣṇatā-īraṇāḥ].
```

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The elements are the earth-domain and the water-, fire-, and wind-domains. They are established in functions beginning with support; their characteristics are hardness, cohesion, heat, and impulsion.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The four great elements are the earth-, water-, fire-, and wind-domains. Respectively, their functions are support, cohesion, maturation, and expansion, while their intrinsic characteristics are hardness, cohesiveness, heat, and impulsion.

The second translation makes explicit the Bhāṣya's distinction between
`karma` and `svabhāva`. “Expansion” renders `vyūhana`, which the prose
explains through growth and spreading; mere “motion” is too narrow.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Material manifestation is grounded in four irreducible domains. Earth sustains, water gathers, fire matures, and wind expands. Each domain is known both through what it does within a material complex and through its own characteristic determination.

Organon rendering:

> The elements are not first presented as outward masses or inert stuffs. A mahābhūta is a sustaining domain: it holds its own characteristic and supports derivative material form. Materiality becomes intelligible through the coordinated determinations of support, cohesion, maturation, and expansion.

The Bhāṣya explains `dhātu` here through `dhāraṇa`, sustaining. It explains
“great” through the elements' support of all other material form and through
their gross prominence. Ordinary masses called earth, water, fire, and wind
are derivative formations in which the corresponding elemental domain is
especially concentrated.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya makes VAK 1.12 answer a question generated by VAK 1.11:

```text
avijñapti
    depends upon the great elements
        ↓
which great elements?
        ↓
earth + water + fire + wind domains
```

The definition of an elemental domain has two axes:

```text
ElementalDomain(d)
    → HasFunction(d, k)
    ∧ HasIntrinsicCharacteristic(d, s)
    ∧ Sustains(d, s)
    ∧ Supports(d, DerivativeMaterialForm)
```

Their ordered distribution is:

```text
earth  : support    ↔ hardness
water  : cohesion   ↔ cohesiveness
fire   : maturation ↔ heat
wind   : expansion  ↔ impulsion
```

The relation between domain and ordinary elemental mass is not identity:

```text
ElementalDomain(x)
    ≠ OrdinaryVisibleMass(x)

OrdinaryElementalMass(m)
    → DerivativeFormation(m)
    ∧ PredominanceOf(m, ElementalDomain)
```

Wind makes the process-character especially clear:

```text
material stream at location A
    → impulsion
    → successive production at location B
```

The lamp-flame example therefore analyzes movement as ordered continuation,
not as the transport of one permanently identical substance.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 1.12 is not an independent excursion into physical cosmology. Its first
question is inherited directly from the preceding verse: if avijñapti is
form dependent upon the great elements, what are those elements? The answer
places even non-disclosive form within an articulated elemental ground.

This establishes a decisive order inside the rūpa analysis:

```text
sensory faculties and sensory domains
    + avijñapti
        ↓ depend upon
four elemental domains
        ↓ support
derivative material manifestation
```

For the comparative Organon, this is the point at which the Sāṃkhya
distinction may be introduced cautiously. A `tanmātra` is not a tiny sensed
object but the supersensible principle of a determinate sensory quality; a
`mahābhūta` is that determination in manifest elemental organization. The
Kośa does not state this Sāṃkhya derivation, so it must not enter the primary
translation. It supplies a controlled comparison for understanding why the
sensory domain is conceptually prior to the gross element.

Avijñapti occupies the hinge. It belongs to rūpa and depends on the elements,
yet it is not outwardly disclosed as a sensory object. It therefore prevents
the passage from sensory determination to elemental manifestation from
being mistaken for a descent into merely external things.

“First manifest world” may become an Organon determination of this threshold,
but “heaven” would presently say more than the Kārikā or Bhāṣya establishes.
The warranted result at VAK 1.12 is exact: the inwardly articulated rūpa-field
has an elemental ground, and that ground consists of sustaining functional
domains rather than four self-subsistent external substances.
