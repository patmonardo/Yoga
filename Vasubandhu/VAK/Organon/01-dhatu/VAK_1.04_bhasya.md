# VAK_1.04 Bhāṣya — The Root Division and the Lodging of Outflows

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sāsravānāsravā dharmāḥ saṃskṛtā mārgavarjitāḥ /
>
> sāsravā āsravās teṣu yasmāt samanuśerate // 1.04 //

> Dharmas are with outflows and without outflows. Conditioned dharmas,
> excluding the Truth of the Path, are with outflows, because the outflows
> settle and continue within them.

The Bhāṣya identifies this as the summary designation of all dharmas. It also
specifies `mārga` as the Truth of the Path and distinguishes the object of an
outflow from the field in which an outflow can settle.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> katame punas te dharmā yeṣāṃ pravicayārtham abhidharmopadeśa ity āha /
> sāsravā anāsravā dharmāḥ / eṣa sarvadharmāṇāṃ samāsanirdeśaḥ / tatra
> katame sāsravā dharmā ity āha / saṃskṛtā mārgavarjitāḥ sāsravāḥ /
> mārgasatyaṃ varjayitvā 'nye saṃskṛtā dharmāḥ sāsravāḥ / kiṃ kāraṇam /
> āsravās teṣu yasmāt samanuśerate / kāmaṃ nirodhamārgasatyālambanā api
> āsravā upajāyante na tv anuśerate tatreti na tayoḥ sāsravatvaprasaṅgaḥ /
> yathā 'tra nānuśerate tat paścād anuśayanirdeśa eva jñāpayiṣyāmaḥ /
> uktāḥ sāsravāḥ /

This is the natural unit from the question carried forward by 1.03 through
the completion of the account of dharmas with outflows. Word division and
obvious sandhi have been supplied for reading. The running witness has
`samāsanirddeśa`, `na tinuśerate`, and `sāsravatvaprasañgaḥ`; the readable
text above provisionally normalizes these forms without claiming a critical
edition.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Which, then, are those dharmas for whose discrimination Abhidharma
> instruction is given? He says: dharmas are with outflows and without
> outflows. This is the summary designation of all dharmas.
>
> Among them, which dharmas are with outflows? He says: conditioned dharmas,
> excluding the Path, are with outflows. That is, conditioned dharmas other
> than the Truth of the Path are with outflows. Why? Because the outflows
> settle and continue within them.
>
> Granted, outflows also arise with the Truth of Cessation and the Truth of
> the Path as their objects. But they do not settle there; consequently, it
> does not follow that those two are with outflows. How the outflows do not
> settle in them will be explained later, precisely in the Exposition of the
> Anuśayas. The dharmas with outflows have now been stated.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The question generated by 1.03 now receives its first comprehensive answer:

```text
Which dharmas are to be discriminated?
    → all dharmas: with outflows or without outflows
    → which are with outflows?
        → conditioned dharmas excluding the Truth of the Path
    → why?
        → outflows settle and continue within them
    → objection: outflows also take cessation and Path as objects
    → reply: taking as object is not settling within
    → full proof deferred to the Anuśayanirdeśa
```

The two active topics are the comprehensive `sāsrava / anāsrava` division
and the distinction between object-relation and afflictive lodging. The first
organizes the entire field; the second supplies the criterion governing the
first side of that division.

## 5. The Comprehensive Division and the Path Exception

The Bhāṣya calls the opening division `sarvadharmāṇāṃ samāsanirdeśa`, the
summary designation of all dharmas:

```text
all dharmas
    → sāsrava, with outflows
    → anāsrava, without outflows
```

Within the conditioned field, the Truth of the Path is the decisive
exception:

```text
sāsrava
    = conditioned dharmas excluding the Truth of the Path

therefore

conditioned
    does not entail
with outflows
```

Conditioning and outflow-status are distinct determinations. The Path arises
through conditions while remaining without outflows. The Bhāṣya does not yet
enumerate all dharmas without outflows; that becomes the work of 1.05.

## 6. Object and Lodging

The anticipated objection observes that outflows can arise while taking the
Truth of Cessation or the Truth of the Path as their object. If object-relation
were sufficient, both truths would have to be classified as with outflows.
The reply blocks that inference:

```text
an outflow takes x as object
    does not entail
an outflow settles and continues within x
```

`Samanuśerate` supplies the operative criterion of `sāsrava`: outflows can
settle or remain latent within those dharmas. Cessation and Path may be
objects of an afflicted occurrence without becoming fields that lodge and
sustain the affliction.

The passage defers the full explanation to the Anuśayanirdeśa. The present
study should retain the distinction without importing Chapter 5's entire
theory into this initial division.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

The commentary controls the final reading in four ways:

1. The opening is an exhaustive summary division of all dharmas, rather than
   a local classification of some dharmas.
2. `Mārga` is specified as `mārgasatya`, the Truth of the Path.
3. `Samanuśerate` is better rendered “settle and continue within” than by a
   merely external association.
4. Being taken as an outflow's object must remain distinct from being the
   field in which an outflow settles.

The anchor translation includes the specification of the Path and the
stronger rendering of `samanuśerate`. The objection, its reply, and the
cross-reference to Chapter 5 remain Bhāṣya determinations.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

The first doctrinal division is governed by the practical problem established
in 1.03. Dharmas are initially distinguished according to whether they can
sustain the outflows whose pacification requires discrimination. The division
is therefore simultaneously comprehensive and directed toward liberation.

For the Organon, `samāsanirdeśa` provides a first root articulation:

```text
sarvadharmāḥ
    → sāsrava | anāsrava
    → further determinations within the divided whole
```

The scientific question for later divisions is whether they can be understood
as further determinations of this already articulated field. The Path
exception also prevents a crude opposition between everything conditioned
and liberation: transformative practice occurs within conditioned existence
without lodging the outflows it counteracts.

This supports the Yoga Vidyā–Technē project at a precise point. Vidyā must
distinguish object, field, and outflow-status; Technē must determine whether
a conditioned operation sustains affliction or counteracts it. The genetic
reading of later Kośa divisions is our Organon hypothesis. Vasubandhu's stated
claims here are the comprehensive division, the Path exception, and the
difference between object and lodging.

## 9. Review Status

Provisional fourth example of the final Bhāṣya-pass method. The first three
sections provide the textual foundation without internal editorial
commentary. The later study is concentrated on the comprehensive division
and the distinction between object and lodging. The natural boundary and
principal determinations have been checked against the local research witness
and running Sanskrit source; the full theory of non-settling remains
explicitly deferred to the Anuśayanirdeśa.
