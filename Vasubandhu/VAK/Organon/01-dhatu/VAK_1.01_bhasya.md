# VAK_1.01 Bhāṣya — Liberation through Instruction According to Reality

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> yaḥ sarvathāsarvahatāndhakāraḥ saṃsārapaṅkājjagadujjahāra /
>
> tasmai namaskṛtya yathārthaśāstre śāstraṃ pravakṣyāmy abhidharmakośam // 1.01 //

> Having bowed to him whose darkness has been destroyed in every way and
> concerning everything, who raised the world from the mire of saṃsāra—to
> that teacher according to reality—I shall proclaim the treatise, the
> *Abhidharmakośa*.

The Bhāṣya determines `yathārthaśāstre` as a dative singular in apposition
to `tasmai`: homage is offered to the Buddha as `yathārthaśāstṛ`, the
teacher according to reality.

This presents the natural Bhāṣya unit from the intention to compose the
treatise through its identification as the *Abhidharmakośa*. Word division
and obvious sandhi have been supplied for reading. The running witness has
minor irregular forms, including `pratyaikabuddha`,
`bhūtārthadarśanapratibandhādhakāram`, and `bhavanntasau`; no substantive
decision rests on silently treating those forms as authoritative readings.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Desiring to compose the treatise, and wishing to make known the greatness
> of his own Teacher, Vasubandhu begins the homage expressed by the words “he
> who.” He prefaces that homage with a declaration of the Teacher’s qualities.
>
> He speaks with reference to the Blessed Buddha. “One whose darkness has
> been destroyed” may mean one in whom darkness has been destroyed, or one by
> whom darkness has been destroyed. He is “one whose darkness has been
> destroyed in every way and concerning everything” because darkness has
> been destroyed in him in every manner and with respect to every object of
> knowledge. For ignorance is darkness: it obstructs the seeing of things as
> they really are. In the Blessed Buddha that ignorance has been destroyed
> absolutely through the attainment of its counteragent, since it has acquired
> the character of never arising again, in any way, with regard to anything
> knowable. Therefore he is one whose darkness has been destroyed in every
> way and concerning everything.
>
> Pratyekabuddhas and śrāvakas, it is true, have darkness destroyed
> throughout, because afflicted delusion has been completely removed. But
> their darkness has not been destroyed in every way. They still have
> non-afflicted ignorance concerning the distinctive qualities of Buddhas,
> things belonging to extremely distant places and times, and objects in
> their limitless divisions.
>
> Having thus praised the Blessed One for the accomplishment of the practice
> directed toward his own benefit, Vasubandhu next praises that same Blessed
> One for the accomplishment of the practice directed toward the benefit of
> others, saying, “who raised the world from the mire of saṃsāra.” Saṃsāra is
> like mire because it is a place in which the world becomes stuck and because
> it is difficult to cross. Moved by compassion for the world submerged there
> and without protection, the Blessed One raised it up according to the
> capacities of beings by extending to them the hands of the teaching of the
> True Dharma.
>
> “Having bowed to him” therefore means having bowed one’s head to the one
> endowed with these accomplishments of practice for his own benefit and for
> the benefit of others. He teaches what is real without inversion, and is
> therefore the teacher according to reality. This expression reveals the
> means by which he accomplishes the benefit of others. It was by teaching
> things as they really are that he became the Teacher and raised the world
> from the mire of saṃsāra, not through the power of granting miraculous
> favors.
>
> What will Vasubandhu do after bowing to him? He says, “I shall proclaim the
> treatise.” It is called a `śāstra` because it instructs disciples. Which
> treatise? He says: the *Abhidharmakośa*.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya converts the opening homage into a reasoned account of knowledge,
authority, and instruction:

```text
intention to compose a śāstra
    → declaration of the Teacher's qualities
    → destruction of ignorance
    → accomplishment of the Teacher's own benefit
    → compassion for beings caught in saṃsāra
    → accomplishment of the benefit of others
    → truthful instruction as the means of liberation
    → proclamation of the Abhidharmakośa as śāstra
```

The two active topics are the completeness of the Buddha's knowing and the
means by which that knowing benefits others. The first grounds the Teacher's
authority; the second determines truthful instruction as the exercise of
that authority. No distinct opponent or school position is marked here.

## 5. Complete Destruction of Ignorance

The Bhāṣya defines darkness functionally:

```text
ignorance
    = obstruction of seeing things as they really are
```

Its destruction is then explained genetically rather than asserted as a
bare absence:

```text
attainment of the counteragent
    → absolute destruction of ignorance
    → incapacity of that ignorance to arise again
```

The distinction between the Buddha and other liberated knowers depends on
two different coordinates:

| Knower | Afflicted ignorance | Non-afflicted ignorance |
|---|---:|---:|
| Buddha | eliminated | eliminated |
| pratyekabuddha or śrāvaka | eliminated | may remain |

The two universals carry different determinations:

```text
sarvathā
    → in every manner

sarva / sarvatra jñeye
    → concerning everything knowable
```

Pratyekabuddhas and śrāvakas have completely abandoned afflicted delusion,
but non-afflicted ignorance may remain concerning Buddha-qualities, remote
places and times, and limitless divisions of objects. Liberation from
afflicted ignorance therefore does not by itself entail the Buddha's
unrestricted range of knowledge. The contrast between `sarvatra` and
`sarvathā` is the conceptual center of this first topic.

## 6. Liberation through Instruction

The Bhāṣya reads the verse's two descriptions as two accomplishments:

```text
destruction of darkness
    → accomplishment directed toward the Buddha's own benefit

raising the world from saṃsāra
    → accomplishment directed toward the benefit of others
```

The first supplies the non-inverted knowing exercised in the second. The
second is neither an external gift of liberation nor an exercise of power
upon passive beings. The means is specified as instruction:

```text
benefit of others
    = raising beings from saṃsāra

means of that benefit
    = teaching things as they really are

rejected means
    = granting liberation through miraculous power or favors
```

The mire image is similarly controlled: saṃsāra is where beings become stuck
and what is difficult to cross. Compassion moves the Buddha to extend the
hand of True-Dharma teaching `yathābhavyam`, according to the capacities of
those taught. The hearer's capacity belongs to the operation of instruction.

The relation between `śāstṛ` and `śāstra` closes the unit. The Buddha is the
Teacher because he teaches reality without inversion; the treatise is a
`śāstra` because it instructs disciples. The *Abhidharmakośa* is thus
announced within the same movement by which knowing becomes communicable and
practically effective.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

The commentary settles the main grammatical ambiguity in the earlier
translation:

```text
tasmai yathārthaśāstre
    → to him, the teacher according to reality
```

It also gives `ujjahāra` a determinate mediation that the verse alone does
not express:

```text
the Buddha raised the world
    → through instruction according to reality
    → not through the bestowal of miraculous liberation
```

The anchor translation incorporates the grammatical decision. The means by
which the Buddha raises beings remains a Bhāṣya determination and is not
inserted into the words of the Kārikā. Likewise, the distinctions between
afflicted and non-afflicted ignorance and between the two accomplishments
remain attributed to the commentary.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage gives knowledge an intrinsically communicable and practical form:

```text
seeing reality without obstruction
    → teaching reality without inversion
    → enabling liberation through understanding and practice
```

For our Science of Consciousness, this is an initial Vidyā–Technē relation.
Yoga Vidyā requires determinate, non-inverted knowing. Yoga Technē concerns
the communicable practice through which another consciousness develops in
accordance with that knowing. Their unity is mediated by instruction adapted
to the learner's capacity. This is our reconstruction; the Bhāṣya's own terms
are perfected knowledge, compassion, instruction according to reality, and
the capacities of those taught.

sections retain the textual foundation without internal editorial commentary.
The later study is concentrated on its two active topics: complete destruction
of ignorance and liberation through instruction. The natural unit and its
principal determinations have been checked against the local research witness
