# VAK_1.05 Bhāṣya — The Anāsrava Field and Space as Non-Obstruction

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> anāsravā mārgasatyaṃ trividhaṃ cāpy asaṃskṛtam /
>
> ākāśaṃ dvau nirodhau ca tatrākāśam anāvṛtiḥ // 1.05 //

> The anāsrava dharmas are the Truth of the Path and also the threefold
> unconditioned: space and the two cessations. Among these, space is
> non-obstruction.

The Bhāṣya supplies the names of the two cessations and explains why the Path
and the three unconditioned dharmas are all anāsrava. It then defines space
through the unobstructed movement of material form.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> anāsravāḥ katame / anāsravā mārgasatyaṃ trividhaṃ cāpy asaṃskṛtam /
> katamat trividham / ākāśaṃ dvau nirodhau ca / katamau dvau /
> pratisaṃkhyānirodho 'pratisaṃkhyānirodhaś ca / ity etad ākāśādi
> trividham asaṃskṛtaṃ mārgasatyaṃ cānāsravā dharmāḥ / kiṃ kāraṇam /
> na hi teṣv āsravā anuśerata iti / yad etat trividham asaṃskṛtam
> uddiṣṭam / tatrākāśam anāvṛtiḥ / anāvaraṇasvabhāvam ākāśaṃ yatra
> rūpasya gatiḥ /

This is the natural unit from the question concerning the anāsrava dharmas
through the definition of space. Word division and obvious sandhi have been
supplied for reading. The running witness has `anasravāḥ`, `katamattri
vidham`, and other joined forms; the readable text above provisionally
normalizes them without claiming a critical edition.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Which dharmas are without outflows? The Truth of the Path is without
> outflows, as is the threefold unconditioned. What is the threefold
> unconditioned? Space and the two cessations. Which are the two? Cessation
> through discrimination and cessation not through discrimination.
>
> Thus the threefold unconditioned beginning with space, together with the
> Truth of the Path, constitutes the dharmas without outflows. Why? Because
> the outflows do not settle within them.
>
> Concerning the threefold unconditioned that has been enumerated: among
> these, space is non-obstruction. Space has non-obstruction as its intrinsic
> nature; it is that in which material form can move.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya completes the division begun in 1.04 and then passes from
enumeration to the first definition of an unconditioned dharma:

```text
Which dharmas are anāsrava?
    → the Truth of the Path
    → the threefold unconditioned
        → space
        → cessation through discrimination
        → cessation not through discrimination
    → why are all four anāsrava?
        → outflows do not settle within them
    → what is space?
        → non-obstruction in which material form can move
```

The two active topics are the composition of the anāsrava field and the exact
definition of space. The two cessations are named here but their determinate
analysis belongs to 1.06.

## 5. Two Axes within the Anāsrava Field

The Path and the three unconditioned dharmas share outflow-status without
sharing conditioned status:

| Dharma class | Conditioned? | With outflows? |
|---|---:|---:|
| ordinary conditioned dharmas | yes | yes |
| Truth of the Path | yes | no |
| space and the two cessations | no | no |

Thus `anāsrava` is not another name for `asaṃskṛta`:

```text
anāsrava dharmas
    = conditioned Path
    + three unconditioned dharmas
```

The criterion established in 1.04 remains decisive: outflows do not settle
in any of these four. Classification therefore requires at least two distinct
questions—whether a dharma is produced through conditions and whether it can
lodge the outflows.

## 6. Space as Non-Obstruction

The transition from `uddiṣṭam`, what has been enumerated, to the definition
of space begins the detailed determination of the three unconditioned
dharmas. The definition is precise:

```text
ākāśa
    = anāvṛti
    = anāvaraṇa-svabhāva
    = that in which rūpa can move
```

Non-obstruction is the defining nature; the movement of material form gives
its concrete indication. The passage does not define space as a material
container, an infinite substance, absolute openness, or pure receptivity.
Those broader meanings should not be introduced into the conventional
translation.

The two cessations are only named at this stage. “Through discrimination” and
“not through discrimination” distinguish them provisionally; their precise
grounds must be taken from the following Bhāṣya rather than inferred here.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

The commentary controls the final reading in four ways:

1. The supplied subject is “the anāsrava dharmas,” rather than an abstract
   entity called “the anāsrava.”
2. The Path is conditioned and anāsrava, while the other three members are
   unconditioned and anāsrava.
3. The Bhāṣya supplies the names `pratisaṃkhyānirodha` and
   `apratisaṃkhyānirodha`, but their definitions belong to 1.06.
4. Space is exactly non-obstruction in which material form can move; a wider
   metaphysical account is not part of this unit.

The anchor translation states the complete inventory but does not insert the
Bhāṣya's explanation of material movement into the Kārikā itself.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

The chief systematic result is multi-axis determination. A dharma is not
comprehended by assigning it one predicate. Conditioned status and
outflow-status intersect, and space introduces a further relation of
obstruction or non-obstruction.

```text
dharma determination
    → conditioned or unconditioned
    → with outflows or without outflows
    → obstructive or non-obstructive where relevant
```

For the Yoga Vidyā–Technē project, this shows why a Science of Consciousness
cannot reduce its objects to a single hierarchy. Vidyā articulates distinct
coordinates without conflating them; Technē determines the practical role of
a conditioned operation such as the Path, which occurs within conditions but
does not sustain the outflows.

Space contributes a more restrained lesson. Its meaning is fixed here by a
specific relation to material movement. The Organon may later test whether
non-obstruction has a broader systematic role, but it cannot take that wider
meaning as already stated by Vasubandhu.

## 9. Review Status

Provisional fifth example of the final Bhāṣya-pass method. The first three
sections provide the textual foundation without internal editorial
commentary. The later study is concentrated on the two-axis constitution of
the anāsrava field and the bounded definition of space. The natural boundary
and principal determinations have been checked against the local research
witness and running Sanskrit source; the definitions of the two cessations
remain for 1.06.
