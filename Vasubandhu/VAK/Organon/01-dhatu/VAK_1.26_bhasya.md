# VAK_1.26 Bhāṣya — The Measure of a Dharma-Collection

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> śāstrapramāṇa ity eke skandhādīnāṃ kathaikaśaḥ /
>
> caritapratipakṣas tu dharmaskandho 'nuvarṇitaḥ // 1.26 //

> Some say a Dharma-collection has the measure of the treatise; an exposition
> of the aggregates and other topics, each separately, is another account.
> A Dharma-collection, however, is described as a counteragent to a disposition.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes textual extent, individual doctrinal exposition,
and a teaching's counteracting relation to a disposition. The last account
explains the stated eighty thousand collections through the corresponding
variety of beings' dispositions.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> kiṃ punar dharmaskandhasya pramāṇam / śāstrapramāṇa ity eke /
> eke tāvad āhur dharmaskandhasaṃjñakasyaivābhidharmaśāstrasyāsya
> pramāṇam iti / tac ca ṣaṭsahasrāṇi / apare punar āhuḥ /
> skandhādīnāṃ kathaikaśaḥ /
> skandhāyatanadhātupratītyasamutpādasatyāhāradhyānāpramāṇārūpya-
> vimokṣābhibhvāyatanakṛtsnāyatanabodhipākṣikābhijñāpratisaṃvit-
> praṇidhijñānāraṇādīnāṃ kathā pratyekaṃ dharmaskandha iti /
>
> caritapratipakṣas tu dharmaskandho 'nuvarṇitaḥ / evaṃ tu varṇayanty
> aśītiś caritasahasrāṇi sattvānām / rāgadveṣamohamānādicaritabhedena /
> teṣāṃ pratipakṣeṇa bhagavatā 'śītir dharmaskandhasahasrāṇy uktāni /

The unit begins with the measure question at printed location 017.10 and
ends with the disposition/counteragent explanation at 017.20. The following
`yathaitāni ... tathā 'nye 'pi` construction connects this example to the
general rule of 1.27; its opening is reserved with that next unit so the
comparison remains together.

Word division and punctuation are editorial. Hyphens at the ends of two
lines mark the continuation of one long Sanskrit compound, not divisions
into new sentences. The running `opramaṇam`, `skandhādīnaṃ`,
`prātyekam`, and `caritasahasraṇi` are normalized to `pramāṇam`,
`skandhādīnāṃ`, `pratyekam`, and `caritasahasrāṇi`.

The research kārikā reads `śāstrapramāṇā ity eke`, with a long final
vowel, whereas the running verse and research Bhāṣya have
`śāstrapramāṇa ity eke`. The latter is retained in the anchor and reading
text. It permits the sandhi of singular `śāstrapramāṇaḥ` before `iti`,
consistent with the question about one dharmaskandha. The variant is
recorded without changing either research witness.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> What is the measure of a Dharma-collection? Some say that it has the
> measure of the treatise. Certain teachers say its measure is that of the
> Abhidharma treatise named Dharmaskandha; that is six thousand [textual
> units]. Others say: an exposition of the aggregates and the other topics,
> each separately. An exposition of each of the aggregates, āyatanas,
> dhātus, dependent arising, truths, nutriments, dhyānas, immeasurables,
> formless attainments, liberations, bases of mastery, bases of totality,
> factors conducive to awakening, higher knowledges, discriminations,
> knowledge through resolve, freedom from conflict, and the rest is a
> Dharma-collection.
>
> A Dharma-collection, however, is described as a counteragent to a
> disposition. They explain it in this way: beings have eighty thousand
> dispositions, differentiated through attachment, hatred, delusion, pride,
> and other dispositions. As counteragents to these, the Blessed One taught
> eighty thousand Dharma-collections.

## 4. Movement and Reported Positions

The question asks what makes a dharmaskandha a measured unit. The passage
answers through three accounts:

| Account | Basis of the unit | Attribution |
|---|---|---|
| textual extent | the extent of the named Dharmaskandha treatise | some teachers |
| doctrinal exposition | each treatment of a specified topic | others |
| counteracting function | a teaching corresponding to a disposition | “they explain it in this way” |

`Ity eke` and `apare punar āhuḥ` explicitly distinguish the first two
positions. `Tu` introduces the third contrastively, and `evaṃ ...
varṇayanti` reports its explanation. The third supplies the reason for
the eighty-thousand count; the unit contains no explicit refutation of
the first two or identification of the schools holding them.

## 5. Extent of a Treatise and Extent of a Topic

`Pramāṇa` here means measure or extent. The question is not about a means
of valid knowledge. The first account names a particular Abhidharma work,
`dharmaskandhasaṃjñaka`, called Dharmaskandha. The same word thus occurs
as a title used to measure a teaching-collection.

`Ṣaṭsahasrāṇi` gives six thousand without an expressed unit noun. “Textual
units” is bracketed in the translation; the witness does not license silently
specifying six thousand modern pages, words, or metrical verses. The
account's quantitative character is clear, while its precise unit remains
unstated here.

The second account changes the criterion to `kathā`, exposition, qualified
by `ekaśaḥ` in the verse and `pratyekam` in the prose: each separately.
The long list illustrates doctrinal subjects. It does not state that each
individual aggregate, each nutriment, or each factor of awakening must
constitute a separate collection of fixed length. The securely stated
principle is a separate exposition of a topic.

The list's `praṇidhijñāna` is rendered as one technical expression,
knowledge through resolve. The research report separates “aspirations”
and “knowledges”; the continuous compound more naturally supports the
combined term here. `Araṇa` follows as freedom from conflict. These brief
renderings identify the listed subjects without importing their later
full definitions into this unit.

## 6. A Counteragent Corresponding to a Disposition

`Caritapratipakṣa` defines the teaching through the disposition it counters.
`Carita` is illustrated by attachment, hatred, delusion, pride, and the
rest. These specify modes of disposition or conduct; the sentence does
not identify them with fixed personal essences.

The instrumental `rāgadveṣamohamānādicaritabhedena` explains the
differentiation of the dispositions. `Teṣāṃ pratipakṣeṇa` then relates
the teachings to those dispositions as counteragents. The matching
formulations of eighty thousand link the diversity addressed with the
diversity of instruction.

This is an explanatory correspondence, not a supplied enumeration of all
eighty thousand cases. The passage states the count and its rationale but
does not provide an algorithm deriving the number or a complete pairing
of named teachings and dispositions. “Counteragent” retains the active
opposition expressed by `pratipakṣa`; it is more specific than merely
being information about a disposition.

## 7. What the Three Measures Distinguish

The accounts answer the measure question under different respects:

```text
how much text
    → extent of a named treatise
what is expounded
    → a doctrinal subject
what the teaching counters
    → a disposition
```

A thematic treatment and a counteracting teaching may overlap in an actual
exposition. The Bhāṣya does not reduce that possible relation to a single
criterion here. It preserves the distinct proposals and elaborates the
third through the traditional count.

This inquiry also differs from 1.25. There the issue was whether Buddha-word
is speech or name and how it is included in the aggregate scheme. Here the
issue is what makes one teaching-collection a unit. Textual measure,
subject matter, and practical function do not themselves decide the earlier
speech/name question.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. The first measure refers to the named Dharmaskandha treatise, whose
   extent is stated as six thousand with the unit left implicit.
2. The prose supplies “others” for the second account: each doctrinal
   exposition is taken as a Dharma-collection.
3. The topic list retains `praṇidhijñāna` as knowledge through resolve.
4. The third account defines the unit by its counteracting relation to a
   disposition and explains the eighty-thousand correspondence.
5. The accounts remain attributed; the passage does not explicitly reject
   the first two.
6. The variant at `śāstrapramāṇa` is recorded, and the comparison leading
   into 1.27 is kept with that next study.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage brings the individuation of instruction into view. A unit of
teaching can be measured by its extent, by the subject it articulates, or
by the work it performs in relation to a disposition. These grounds need
not be confused merely because each can identify a body of teaching.

For the Organon reconstruction, the third account provides the most direct
connection to our question about learning. Instruction is determined through
the condition it addresses, not only through the amount or subject of its
text. Understanding what a teaching is for requires identifying the
particular disposition against which it operates.

The text supplies that directed relation without explaining every step by
which instruction becomes effective understanding or prajñā. The further
inquiry is how a treatment of a subject becomes a counteragent for a learner.
Its answer must connect doctrinal content with the transformation intended,
while preserving the distinct measures the Bhāṣya has reported.
