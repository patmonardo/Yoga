# VAK_1.34 Bhāṣya — Having an Object and Being Appropriated as a Living Support

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sapta sālambanāś cittadhātavo 'rdhaṃ ca dharmataḥ /
>
> navānupāttās te cāṣṭau śabdaś cānye nava dvidhā // 1.34 //

> The seven citta-domains have objects, and a half of the dharma-domain.
> Nine are unappropriated: those eight and sound. The other nine are
> twofold.

The Bhāṣya identifies the “half” as the mental-factor portion of the
dharma-domain. In the second classification it explicitly counts the
whole dharma-domain among the nine unappropriated domains. The compact
resumption “those eight” must be read with that change of scope.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

How many have an object, and how many do not have an object?

The seven citta-domains have objects. The visual, auditory, olfactory,
gustatory, bodily, and mental cognition-domains, together with the
manas-domain, are these seven citta-domains. They have objects because
they apprehend an object-field.

And a half of the dharma-domain: the portion whose nature is that of
mental factors has an object. It is therefore established that the
remaining ten material domains and the unassociated portion of the
dharma-domain do not have objects.

How many are appropriated, and how many unappropriated? Nine are
unappropriated. Which nine? The seven described as having objects,
together with a half of the eighth [are recalled]. Those eight, and
sound: these are the nine unappropriated domains—the seven
citta-domains, the dharma-domain, and the sound-domain.

The other nine are twofold: appropriated and unappropriated. Of these,
the eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body faculties are appropriated when
present; when past or future, they are unappropriated.

The visible-form, smell, taste, and tangible domains are appropriated
when present and inseparable from a faculty. Other instances are
unappropriated: for example, [those] in hair, body-hair, nails, and teeth
apart from their roots; in feces, urine, saliva, mucus, blood, and the
like; and in earth, water, and so forth.

What does “appropriated” mean? That which citta and mental factors have
taken up as a supporting basis, because they reciprocally follow one
another in benefit and injury. It is what is called “animate” in
ordinary usage.

The bracketed phrase in the resumption marks the compressed reference
to the previous count. The explicit enumeration that follows supplies
the full dharma-domain; the translation does not invent a second
half-domain to make the arithmetic work.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

Two questions organize the unit. The first classifies domains by having
an object. The second classifies them by appropriation as a living
support. Each receives an enumeration, and the latter also receives a
temporal distinction, material examples, and a definition.

There is no marked rival-school voice or sustained objection. “What
does appropriated mean?” asks for the force of the term already used.
The final appeal to what “in the world” is called *sacetana* cites
ordinary usage; it is not introduced as a scriptural quotation.

The sequence matters. Citta and mental factors are first classified as
object-taking and then as unappropriated. In the definition they take
up a material basis. The two predicates therefore cannot be treated as
synonyms for participation in cognition.

## 5. Having an Object Is an Active Cognitive Relation

*Viṣayagrahaṇāt*, “because of apprehending an object-field,” supplies
the reason the seven are *sālambana*. They comprise the six
cognition-domains and manas. “Having an object” describes their
apprehensive relation; it does not mean being physically supported by
an external thing.

The distinction also runs in the other direction. A visible form may
be an object of visual cognition while being *anālambana*: it does
not itself apprehend an object. Consequently *anālambana* should not
be expanded into “unavailable to cognition” or “outside experience.”

For the dharma-domain, *yac caitasikasvabhāvam* identifies the relevant
portion as mental factors. The rest is described as unassociated.
*Ardham*, “half,” marks this division into portions; the prose supplies
no numerical equality between their members.

The unassociated portion must not be restricted without warrant to
unassociated formations alone. In the established inventory the
dharma-domain also includes avijñapti and the unconditioned. The
present division turns on mental association and object-taking, not
solely on the subdivisions of the formations aggregate.

## 6. Why the Second Count Includes the Whole Dharma-Domain

The first enumeration reaches seven domains plus a portion of an eighth.
The second states nine whole domains: seven citta-domains, the
dharma-domain, and sound. This explicit list governs *te cāṣṭau*.

| Domain or portion | Has an object? | Appropriation status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seven citta-domains | Yes | Unappropriated |
| Mental-factor portion of dharma-domain | Yes | Unappropriated |
| Unassociated portion of dharma-domain | No | Unappropriated |
| Sound-domain | No | Unappropriated |
| Five faculty-domains | No | Twofold according to time |
| Visible form, smell, taste, tangible | No | Twofold according to time and faculty-inseparability |

This makes the two classifications reviewable without combining their
counts. The nine twofold domains are five faculties plus four material
object-domains. Sound is expressly excluded from that group, even
though it may be produced by a living body. The passage states its
classification without separately arguing the cause of that exclusion.

“Unappropriated” here does not mean disconnected from a living being,
unconscious, or ethically neutral. Citta and mental factors themselves
are included among the unappropriated domains. The predicate concerns
the relation defined at the end of the unit.

## 7. Present Faculties, Material Inseparability, and Reciprocal Effects

For the five faculties, the prose says present instances are
appropriated and past or future instances unappropriated. It does not
add a condition that a faculty must be currently producing a sensory
cognition. The research phrase “presently functioning” can obscure
this difference between being a present faculty and exercising it in
an occurrent act.

For the four material object-domains, two conditions are conjoined:
*pratyutpanna*, present, and *indriyāvinirbhāgin*, inseparable from a
faculty. Present external matter is therefore not automatically
appropriated. Nor does merely being located within the body suffice,
as the listed excretions and bodily materials show.

The examples are locative: the relevant visible form, smell, taste,
and tangible occur “in” the listed things. *Mūlavarjeṣu* excludes roots
from the hair, body-hair, nail, and tooth examples. The prose then lists
blood among the unappropriated examples without giving a further
qualification here. That feature of the transmitted list is retained;
it should not be silently rewritten to fit a modern account of tissue
or circulation.

The definition uses *adhiṣṭhānabhāvena*, “as a supporting basis,” and
*upagṛhīta*, “taken up.” *Anyonyānuvidhāna* gives reciprocity: benefit
and injury on one side are correspondingly followed on the other.
This is more specific than ownership, spatial inclusion, or merely
being a cause of a mental event. An external event can affect cognition
without thereby becoming appropriated bodily matter.

The final *sacetana* is rendered “animate” to connect the definition
with ordinary recognition of a living support. “Ensouled” would import
an unnecessary substantial soul into the English. The remark also
does not reverse the earlier result by making material domains
object-taking cognitions.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- The seven are six cognition-domains and manas; they have objects
  because they apprehend an object-field.
- The object-taking “half” of the dharma-domain consists of mental
  factors, without a claim of numerical equality between halves.
- The appropriation count includes the whole dharma-domain, together
  with the seven citta-domains and sound.
- The other nine are five faculties and four material object-domains.
- Present status governs appropriation of faculties; present status
  and faculty-inseparability jointly govern the four object-domains.
- Appropriation means being taken up as a supporting basis with
  reciprocal responsiveness to benefit and injury.

The original research kārikā and commentary are preserved. These
clarifications belong to the present edition.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage separates cognition's relation to an object from its
relation to an embodied support. The object is apprehended; the
support is taken up in a relation of reciprocal benefit and injury.
Neither relation can stand in for the other.

For the inquiry into how prajñā manifests and how learning occurs,
this supplies a further distinction. An associated factor has an
object; the bodily basis can sustain cognition without itself taking
an object. A change in understanding and a change in its embodied
conditions may be related while requiring different descriptions.
The paragraph specifies those relations without yet explaining the
acquisition of understanding.

The definition of appropriation also gives content to embodiment
beyond physical enclosure. Reciprocal responsiveness matters. An
Organon reconstruction can investigate how bodily conditions support
attention, examination, and retention, but those applications remain
further work rather than claims contained in this enumeration.

No allocation of these categories to saṃvṛti and paramārtha is stated
here. Their contribution to that project-level inquiry is the precision
of the relations: being apprehended, apprehending, and supporting
apprehension are distinguishable determinations.
