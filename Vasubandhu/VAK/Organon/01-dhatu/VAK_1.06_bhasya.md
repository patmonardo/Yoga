# VAK_1.06 Bhāṣya — Disjunction and the Obstruction of Arising

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> pratisaṃkhyānirodho yo visaṃyogaḥ pṛthak pṛthak /
>
> utpādātyantavighno 'nyo nirodho 'pratisaṃkhyayā // 1.06 //

> Cessation through discriminative consideration is disjunction, separately
> in each case. The other cessation, not through discriminative consideration,
> is the absolute obstruction of arising.

The Bhāṣya identifies the first cessation as disjunction from sāsrava dharmas
and defines `pratisaṃkhyā` as a particular prajñā directed to the Noble
Truths. It then explains the second cessation through an irreversible
deficiency of conditions and distributes both cessations across four classes
of dharmas.

This is the natural unit from the definition of cessation through
discriminative consideration through the fourfold classification and the
conclusion of the three unconditioned dharmas. Word division, punctuation,

text above follows the provisional normalization of the local Bhāṣya study.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The disjunction from dharmas with outflows is cessation through
> discriminative consideration. Discriminative consideration is the
> consideration of the Noble Truths beginning with suffering; it is a
> particular kind of wisdom. The cessation attained through it is cessation
> through discriminative consideration. This expression results from the
> omission of the middle member, as with “ox-cart.”
>
> Is there, then, just one cessation through discriminative consideration for
> all dharmas with outflows? No, he says. What then? Separately in each case.
> For there are as many real disjunctions as there are real conjunctions.
> Otherwise, realization of the cessation of the defilements abandoned
> through seeing suffering would entail realization of the cessation of all
> defilements. If that were so, cultivation of the remaining counteragents
> would be pointless.
>
> What, then, is the meaning of the statement, “Cessation is without a
> homogeneous cause”? It means that cessation has no homogeneous cause and
> is the homogeneous cause of nothing. It does not mean that cessation has no
> homogeneous counterpart. Cessation through discriminative consideration
> has been explained.
>
> The cessation other than disjunction, which is an absolute obstruction to
> the arising of future dharmas, is cessation not through discriminative
> consideration. It is not attained through discriminative consideration.
> How, then, is it attained? Through a deficiency of conditions.
>
> For example, when the eye and mind are occupied with one visible form,
> other visible forms, sounds, odors, tastes, and tangible objects pass by.
> The five groups of consciousness that would have taken them as objects
> cannot afterward arise, for they cannot then take a past object as their
> object. Therefore cessation not through discriminative consideration is
> obtained for them through a deficiency of conditions.
>
> Here there is also a fourfold classification. There are dharmas for which
> cessation through discriminative consideration alone is obtained: past,
> present, and future dharmas with outflows that are capable of arising.
> There are dharmas for which cessation not through discriminative
> consideration alone is obtained: future conditioned dharmas without
> outflows that are incapable of arising. There are dharmas for which both
> are obtained: dharmas with outflows that are incapable of arising. There
> are dharmas for which neither is obtained: past, present, and future
> dharmas without outflows that are capable of arising. The threefold
> unconditioned has now been explained.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya defines each cessation by its ground and then tests their ranges:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    → disjunction from sāsrava dharmas
    → attained through a special prajñā concerning the Noble Truths
    → distinct for each determinate conjunction
    → required in this plurality by the successive cultivation of the Path

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    → absolute obstruction of a future arising
    → obtained through deficiency of conditions
    → illustrated by sensory occasions that have irretrievably passed

both definitions
    → tested through a fourfold classification
```

Two topics govern the unit: the distinct disjunctions produced through
discriminative consideration and the non-arising established when the
necessary conditions are no longer available.

## 5. Disjunction through Discriminative Consideration

The Bhāṣya prevents `pratisaṃkhyā` from becoming an indefinite power of
analysis. It is a `prajñāviśeṣa`, a particular wisdom whose field is the Noble
Truths beginning with suffering. The compound names the cessation attained
through that wisdom; it does not identify cessation with the cognitive act
itself.

`Pṛthak pṛthak` then determines the structure of release. Disjunction is not
one undifferentiated event shared by every sāsrava dharma. As many determinate
conjunctions as there are, so many determinate disjunctions there are. The
Bhāṣya confirms this through the Path: if realizing the cessation of one
class of defilements realized every cessation at once, cultivation of the
remaining counteragents would have no work to do.

The explanation of `asabhāga` protects this plurality. Cessation is “without
a homogeneous cause” because it neither has nor supplies a `sabhāgahetu`;
the statement does not deny distinct corresponding cessations. Its
unconditioned and causally non-generative status is therefore compatible
with the plurality required by the articulated work of release.

## 6. Non-Arising through Deficiency of Conditions

The other cessation concerns a future dharma whose arising has become
absolutely impossible. Its ground is not liberating discernment but
`pratyayavaikalya`, deficiency of conditions. The sensory example makes the
point concrete: while attention is occupied with one form, other possible
objects pass; the particular sensory cognitions that depended upon those
occasions can no longer arise.

The fourfold classification shows that outflow-status and capacity for
arising remain independent determinations:

| Dharma class | Through discrimination | Through deficient conditions |
|---|---:|---:|
| sāsrava and capable of arising | yes | no |
| anāsrava, conditioned, and incapable of arising | no | yes |
| sāsrava and incapable of arising | yes | yes |
| anāsrava and capable of arising | no | no |

Thus the same dharma may require disjunction because it is sāsrava and also
be incapable of arising because its conditions are absent. Conversely, an
anāsrava dharma requires no liberating disjunction even when its arising is
impossible. “Cessation” alone does not state which relation has been
established.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

The commentary fixes four points for the final Kārikā translation:

1. `Pratisaṃkhyā` is discriminative consideration as a particular prajñā
   directed to the Noble Truths, rather than discrimination in a generic
   sense.
2. `Visaṃyoga` is disjunction specifically from sāsrava dharmas.
3. `Pṛthak pṛthak` means distinct disjunctions corresponding to distinct
   conjunctions; “separately in each case” preserves this force.
4. `Utpādātyantavighna` is the absolute obstruction of a future arising,
   established through deficiency of conditions.

The concise anchor retains “cessation through discriminative consideration”
for `pratisaṃkhyānirodha`. The longer Bhāṣya is needed to state the precise
kind of consideration and the practical reason for the plurality of
disjunctions.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This passage requires every cessation to be known through its ground. A bare
result such as “does not arise” is insufficient: it may express disjunction
from bondage through Noble-Truth wisdom, or the impossibility of an event
whose conditions have irreversibly failed. The distinction coordinates
object-range, cognitive operation, causal condition, and practical status
without reducing one to another.

For the Yoga Vidyā–Technē project, this suggests a disciplined science of
cessation. Vidyā must determine what has ceased, in relation to what, and on
what ground. Technē must distinguish a cultivated operation that realizes
release from the causal management or recognition of non-occurrence. This is
an Organon reconstruction from the Bhāṣya's distinctions; it is not a claim
that Vasubandhu presents the later project in these terms.
