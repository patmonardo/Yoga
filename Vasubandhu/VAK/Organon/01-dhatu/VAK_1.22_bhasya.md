# VAK_1.22 Bhāṣya — The Unconditioned and the Order of the Aggregates

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> skandheṣv asaṃskṛtaṃ noktam arthāyogāt kramaḥ punaḥ /
>
> yathaudārikasaṃkleśabhājanādyarthadhātutaḥ // 1.22 //

> The unconditioned is not stated among the aggregates because the meaning
> does not apply. Their order, again, follows grossness, affliction, the
> analogy beginning with a vessel, and the existential domains.

The Bhāṣya explains both the scope of the aggregate classification and
its internal order. It concludes by connecting those ordering accounts
with the separate status of vedanā and saṃjñā discussed in 1.21.

The unit begins with the opening verse clause at printed location 014.24
and ends with the recap at 015.20–21. The next inquiry concerns the order
of the six faculties within the āyatana and dhātu arrangements and belongs
to 1.23.

The running witness is reproduced with editorial word division and repairs
to `skandheṣva saṃskṛtam`, `antarṇetum`, `teśu`, `arupiṇām`,
`cau dārikatarā`, `cittaṃ aca`, `bhajanabhojana`, `karttṛ bhoktṛ`,
`kṣetrabija`, and `pṛthaka skandhīkṛte`. The quotation mark before
`apy asaṃskṛtam` is normalized as an avagraha.

Two substantive issues remain uncorrected. The closing recap has
`bhājanavyañjanabhūte`, “vessel and seasoning,” although the preceding
fivefold analogy assigns vedanā and saṃjñā the roles of food and seasoning.
Both the running text and the research report preserve that discrepancy.
Also, 1.21 promised a demonstration in three ways, while this passage
presents four ordering accounts. The edition records the transmitted
sequence without silently harmonizing either point.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The unconditioned is not stated among the aggregates because the meaning
> does not apply. If it were stated among them, it could not be included
> within these existing aggregates, because the meaning does not apply:
> it is neither rūpa nor any of the others through vijñāna. Nor can it be
> stated as a sixth aggregate. Why? Because the meaning does not apply.
> It has been said that skandha means collection. The unconditioned is not
> divided by distinctions such as past and the rest, as rūpa and the other
> aggregates are, so that all of it could be gathered together into one and
> designated an aggregate of the unconditioned.
>
> The expression “aggregates of appropriation” makes known the basis of
> affliction; “aggregates” makes known the basis of affliction and
> purification. The unconditioned is neither in this manner, so it is not
> established among them because the meaning does not apply. Others say:
> just as the cessation of a pot is not a pot, the cessation of aggregates
> cannot be an aggregate. For them the same consequence follows concerning
> dhātus and āyatanas too. Another aspect of the aggregates has been explained.
>
> Their order, again, follows grossness, affliction, the analogy beginning
> with a vessel, and the existential domains. Rūpa is grossest because
> of resistance. Among the non-material aggregates, vedanā is gross in
> its manifestation: people say, “There is feeling in my hand,” or “There
> is feeling in my foot.” Saṃjñā is grosser than the two remaining
> aggregates, and saṃskāras are grosser than vijñāna. Thus what is grosser
> is stated first.
>
> Alternatively, in beginningless saṃsāra women and men delight in one
> another's forms because of greed for the savor of feeling. That greed
> arises from distortion of saṃjñā; that distortion arises through the
> afflictions, and citta is afflicted by them. Thus the order also follows
> affliction.
>
> Or it follows the analogy beginning with a vessel: the aggregates beginning
> with rūpa serve as vessel, food, seasoning, maker, and eater. Or it follows
> the existential domains. The desire-domain is distinguished by material
> form belonging to sensual pleasures; the dhyānas by vedanā; the first
> three formless attainments by saṃjñā; and the summit of existence by
> formations alone. These are the stations of vijñāna, and vijñāna is
> established in them. Thus the aggregate sequence displays field and seed.
>
> For these reasons there are five aggregates, neither fewer nor more.
> For these reasons of order, too, vedanā and saṃjñā are made into separate
> aggregates apart from the formations: they are grosser, are causes in
> the sequence of affliction, serve as vessel and seasoning, and distinguish
> two domain groupings.

The last paragraph retains “vessel” as transmitted. The earlier analogy's
“food” would fit vedanā's assigned position, but is not silently substituted.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The first question concerns inclusion: why neither an existing aggregate
nor a sixth aggregate accommodates the unconditioned. The second concerns
order: why the five are arranged as rūpa, vedanā, saṃjñā, saṃskāra,
and vijñāna.

The pot-cessation analogy is explicitly attributed to others and met with
an objection. The four ordering accounts are introduced through the
expository alternatives. No school is expressly named in this unit.
The closing recap returns to the question of 1.21 without repeating all
its causal discussion.

## 5. Why the Meaning Does Not Apply

`Arthāyogāt` resolves here as `artha-ayogāt`, because of non-applicability
of the meaning. Its negative force is decisive. The prose applies it first
to the existing five aggregates: the unconditioned has none of their
specified natures. It then applies it to a proposed sixth aggregate through
the definition of skandha as collection.

The unconditioned does not admit the past and other divisions in the manner
of rūpa and the remaining aggregates. The argument is not merely that the
unconditioned has no plurality of any kind: three unconditioned dharmas
have already been enumerated. It concerns the particular gathering by
which an aggregate was defined in 1.20.

The further explanation concerns the purposes of the names. Aggregates of
appropriation specify the basis of affliction; aggregates more broadly
specify the basis of affliction and purification. The unconditioned does
not function as such a basis in the stated manner. This does not remove
nirvāṇa from the goal of liberation; the sentence concerns what these
aggregate terms designate.

The pot analogy is challenged because the same reasoning would affect
āyatana and dhātu inclusion, where the unconditioned has already been
placed. The reply exposes the analogy's failure to distinguish the ranges
of the classifications. The positive account rests on their meanings and
uses, rather than on an unrestricted equation of the unconditioned with
cessation of every named category.

## 6. Grossness and the Explanation through Affliction

The grossness account places the more manifest first. Material resistance
introduces rūpa; the bodily localization of feeling illustrates vedanā's
prominence among the non-material aggregates. `Dvābhyām ... audārikatarā`
means saṃjñā is grosser than the two remaining aggregates, saṃskāra and
vijñāna. The next comparative places formations before vijñāna.

The opening generalization about rūpa's resistance is retained in this
ordering account. It does not erase the earlier analysis of non-resistant
form or the special status of avijñapti. The paragraph gives a rationale
of presentation, not a fresh exhaustive definition of every material factor.

The affliction account requires care about direction. Attraction to form
is explained through greed for feeling's savor; that greed through distorted
saṃjñā; and that distortion through afflictions. The exposition follows
the aggregate order while tracing grounds of the attraction. It does not
say that form first produces feeling, which then produces distortion and
then produces afflictions as a simple forward temporal chain.

Citta is finally described as afflicted. The passage thus connects the
objects of attraction, the savoring, the distorted grasping, the afflictions,
and afflicted citta without making every arrow of explanation run in the
same causal direction as the order of the list.

## 7. The Meal Analogy and Field–Seed Relation

The third account assigns five roles:

| Aggregate | Role in the analogy |
|---|---|
| rūpa | vessel |
| vedanā | food |
| saṃjñā | seasoning |
| saṃskāra | maker |
| vijñāna | eater |

These are analogical roles within a coordinated activity. “Maker” can be
understood as the preparer in this context; the prose does not add a
separate entity beyond the aggregates to perform the eating. The closing
recap's conflicting vessel/food reading remains a textual issue.

The fourth account invokes `dhātutaḥ`, according to existential domains
or levels. It concerns the desire-domain, dhyānas, three formless
attainments, and the summit of existence. It is not an enumeration of the
eighteen cognition-domains.

`Prabhāvita` is rendered “distinguished by.” The sequence associates these
levels with material sensual qualities, vedanā, saṃjñā, and formations,
respectively. “Formations alone” at the summit is retained within this
account of predominance; it does not authorize deleting vijñāna, which
the next sentence explicitly places within its stations.

The field–seed conclusion relates the preceding aggregate grounds to
vijñāna established in them. `Tāsu ... pratiṣṭhitam` places vijñāna in
the stations. The direction of that relation must remain visible. The
local `vijñānasthiti` statement should not be mechanically equated with
every other numbered classification of stations found elsewhere in the
Kośa.

## 8. Number, Separation, and the Promised Demonstration

The commentary uses the preceding reasons to explain five aggregates,
neither fewer nor more, and the separate treatment of vedanā and saṃjñā.
The recap invokes their grossness, their causal roles in affliction, the
meal analogy, and the two domain groupings they distinguish.

This gives four explicit respects in the present passage. The research
report rightly enumerates four here, but the previous prose's `tredhā`,
in three ways, remains in the running text. A grouping of the accounts
might explain that difference, but no such grouping is stated locally.
The edition leaves it as an unresolved expository count rather than
altering the earlier promise or suppressing an account.

## 9. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. `Arthāyogāt` means the category's meaning is inapplicable; it does not
   mean positive fittingness.
2. The unconditioned belongs neither within an existing aggregate nor as
   a sixth collection in the specified sense.
3. The alternative pot analogy is challenged by the āyatana/dhātu case.
4. Four ordering accounts are supplied, with the earlier threefold promise
   retained as a discrepancy for review.
5. The explanation through affliction must preserve its causal directions.
6. `Bhājanādi` refers to a full fivefold analogy; the recap's vessel/food
   conflict remains explicit.
7. `Dhātutaḥ` concerns existential levels, concluding with vijñāna as seed
   established in the preceding fields.

## 10. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage tests systematic meaning in two ways: whether a category's
meaning applies, and whether the order of its members is intelligible.
Excluding the unconditioned from aggregates does not exclude it from every
classification. The reason is specific to the aggregate's definition and
purpose.

For the Organon reconstruction, the ordering accounts show how one sequence
can receive several grounds without making those grounds interchangeable.
Manifestness, the explanation of affliction, an analogy of coordinated
activity, and existential predominance articulate different relations.
Preserving the causal direction within each is part of understanding the
sequence itself.

The field–seed account bears directly on our domain inquiry. Vijñāna is
stated as established in differentiated stations; those stations provide
a concrete relation through which its occurrence is considered. That supports
investigating how knowing manifests in an articulated field without
identifying the field and knowing as the same aggregate.

For learning, the passage supplies grounds through which an ordered
exposition can become intelligible. It does not yet explain the full passage
from following those grounds to attained prajñā. Our Organon question
remains connected to the specific distinctions and explanatory relations
that such understanding must preserve.
