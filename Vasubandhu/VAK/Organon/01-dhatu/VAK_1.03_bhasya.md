# VAK_1.03 Bhāṣya — Why Abhidharma Instruction Is Necessary

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dharmāṇāṃ pravicayam antareṇa nāsti kleśānāṃ yata upaśāntaye 'bhyupāyaḥ /
>
> kleśaiś ca bhramati bhavārṇave 'tra lokas taddhetor ata uditaḥ kilaiṣa śāstraḥ // 1.03 //

> Since there is no means for pacifying the afflictions without discrimination
> of dharmas, and since the world wanders here in the ocean of becoming
> through those afflictions, for that reason this treatise is said to have
> been taught.

The Bhāṣya makes the necessity claim more concrete: the disciple cannot
discriminate dharmas without Abhidharma instruction. It then attributes the
account of the Buddha's dispersed teachings and their systematic collection
to the Vaibhāṣikas.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> kimarthaṃ punar abhidharmopadeśaḥ kena cāyaṃ prathamata upadiṣṭo yata
> ācāryo 'bhidharmakośaṃ vaktum ādriyata iti / āha / yato na vinā
> dharmapravicayenāsti kleśopaśamābhyupāyaḥ / kleśāś ca lokaṃ
> bhramayanti saṃsāramahārṇave 'smin / atas tad-dhetos tasya
> dharmapravicayasyārthe śāstraṃ kila buddhenābhidharma uktaḥ / na hi
> vinābhidharmopadeśena śiṣyaḥ śakto dharmān pravicetum iti / sa tu
> prakīrṇa ukto bhagavatā bhadantakātyāyanīputraprabhṛtibhiḥ piṇḍīkṛtya
> sthāpito bhadantadharmatrātodānavargīyakaraṇavad ity āhur vaibhāṣikāḥ /
> katame punas te dharmā yeṣāṃ pravicayārtham abhidharmopadeśa ity āha /

This is the natural Bhāṣya unit from the questions about the purpose and first
teacher of Abhidharma through the question that introduces VAK 1.04. Word
division and obvious sandhi have been supplied for reading. The running
witness has irregular forms including `yaṭa`, `saṃsāramahārṇeve 'sminan`,
`śāstrā`, and the compressed sequence around `sthāpito bhadanta`; the
readable text above records provisional normalization rather than a critical
edition.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> But for what purpose is Abhidharma taught? By whom was it first taught, such
> that the master undertakes to expound the *Abhidharmakośa*? He answers:
> without discrimination of dharmas there is no means for pacifying the
> afflictions, and the afflictions cause the world to wander in this great
> ocean of saṃsāra. Therefore, for that reason, Abhidharma is said to have
> been taught as a treatise by the Buddha for the sake of this discrimination
> of dharmas. For without instruction in Abhidharma, a disciple is not capable
> of discriminating the dharmas.
>
> The Vaibhāṣikas say that it was taught in dispersed form by the Blessed One
> and was then collected and established by the venerable Kātyāyanīputra and
> others, just as the venerable Dharmatrāta compiled the *Udānavarga*.
>
> But which are those dharmas for whose discrimination Abhidharma instruction
> is given? He states them next.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The Bhāṣya turns the definition of Abhidharma in 1.02 into a justification of
its instruction:

```text
Why teach Abhidharma, and who first taught it?
    → afflictions cause wandering in saṃsāra
    → their pacification requires discrimination of dharmas
    → the disciple requires instruction to acquire that capacity
    → Abhidharma is taught for the sake of discrimination
    → Vaibhāṣika account of dispersed teaching and systematic collection
    → which dharmas are to be discriminated?
```

The unit proceeds through questions, a necessity argument, causal diagnosis,
pedagogical explanation, attributed historical account, and transition into
the first classification of dharmas.

The two active topics are the necessity of `dharmapravicaya` and the
instructional formation of the disciple's capacity. The attributed account
of compilation belongs to the second topic because it concerns how dispersed
teaching becomes transmissible as systematic Abhidharma.

## 5. Why Dharma-Discrimination Is Necessary

The Bhāṣya states a strict practical dependence:

```text
without dharmapravicaya
    → no means for kleśopaśama

kleśa
    → causes the world to wander in saṃsāra

therefore

dharmapravicaya
    → is required as the operation answering that cause
```

The claim is stronger than saying that analysis is helpful. `Antareṇa nāsti`
excludes an alternative means within the scope of the assertion. The Bhāṣya
does not say that enumeration by itself pacifies affliction; it names
`dharmapravicaya`, discriminative examination of dharmas, as indispensable.

The causal order also matters. Beings do not wander because a treatise is
absent as a physical object. They wander through kleśas. The need for the
treatise is mediated by the need to develop the discrimination through which
those kleśas may be pacified.

## 6. Instruction and Systematic Transmission

The prose supplies the pedagogical middle term compressed out of the verse:

```text
Abhidharma instruction
    → enables the disciple to discriminate dharmas
    → supplies the indispensable operation for pacifying kleśa
```

This completes the account begun in the first two verses:

```text
1.01  the Buddha benefits others through instruction according to reality
1.02  śāstra is equipment for attaining stainless prajñā
1.03  instruction produces the disciple's capacity for dharmapravicaya
```

The text is neither the accomplished result nor an inert container of
information. Its stated function is to mediate the formation of a capacity in
the disciple. The final explanation then turns from the necessity of
instruction to its received historical form. `Ity āhur vaibhāṣikāḥ`
explicitly marks the following as the Vaibhāṣika account:

```text
the Blessed One
    → taught Abhidharma in dispersed form

Kātyāyanīputra and others
    → collected and established it

Dharmatrāta's formation of the Udānavarga
    → analogy for that compilation
```

This distinguishes the authority of the teaching from the historical form
of its systematic arrangement. The Bhāṣya does not present the received
Abhidharma corpus as a single discourse delivered by the Buddha in its later
organization. Nor does Vasubandhu endorse the compilation account without
qualification; he reports what the Vaibhāṣikas say.

The voice distinction is indispensable: without it, a school attribution
would become an unqualified assertion by Vasubandhu. The closing question
then performs the systematic transition into 1.04:

```text
1.01  Who is the Teacher, and how does he liberate?
1.02  What is Abhidharma?
1.03  Why is its instruction necessary?
1.04  Which dharmas are to be discriminated?
```

The practical end generates the need for discrimination, and that need
generates the demand for a complete initial division of the field. The
question introducing 1.04 is part of 1.03's movement even though its answer
belongs to the next kārikā.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

The commentary requires four controls on the final Kārikā translation and
study:

1. `Nāsti ... abhyupāyaḥ` must retain the force “there is no means,” rather
   than being weakened to usefulness or preference.
2. The instrumental `kleśaiḥ` expresses the causal efficacy of the
   afflictions in producing wandering.
3. `Kila` should remain audible as “is said” or “according to the received
   account,” especially in light of the following Vaibhāṣika attribution.
4. `Bhavārṇava`, the ocean of becoming, should remain the Kārikā wording;
   `saṃsāramahārṇava` is its Bhāṣya expansion.

The disciple's dependence upon Abhidharma instruction and the historical
collection account are Bhāṣya determinations. They should inform the study
without being inserted into a close translation of the verse.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

The first three Bhāṣyas now form a single pedagogical argument. Liberating
knowledge must be communicated according to reality; the śāstra serves as
equipment for attaining stainless discrimination; and its instruction forms
the disciple's own power to discriminate the dharmas whose confusion sustains
affliction.

This is a strong basis for the Yoga Vidyā–Technē reconstruction:

```text
Yoga Vidyā
    → articulates the dharmas and their determinate relations

Yoga Technē
    → forms the disciple's capacity to discriminate them

their practical criterion
    → whether that discrimination answers the kleśas
       that sustain wandering
```

The teaching may first occur in dispersed form and later be gathered and
established as a śāstra. For our project, systematic organization is thus an
act of Technē because it makes a field available for disciplined
discrimination.

This is why a Science of Consciousness requires an Organon rather than a
collection of isolated insights. That conclusion is our reconstruction;
Vasubandhu's explicit claims remain the necessity of `dharmapravicaya`, the
disciple's need for instruction, and the Vaibhāṣika account of compilation.
