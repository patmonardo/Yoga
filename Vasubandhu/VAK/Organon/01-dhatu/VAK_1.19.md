# VAK_1.19

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> जातिगोचरविज्ञानसामान्यादेकधातुता ।
>
> द्वित्वेऽपि चक्षुरादीनां शोभार्थं तु द्वयोद्भवः ॥ १.१९ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> jātigocaravijñānasāmānyād ekadhātutā /
>
> dvitve 'pi cakṣurādīnāṃ śobhārthaṃ tu dvayodbhavaḥ // 1.19 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

```text
jātigocaravijñānasāmānyāt → jāti-gocara-vijñāna-sāmānyāt
ekadhātutā                 → eka-dhātutā
dvitve 'pi                 → dvitve api
cakṣurādīnām               → cakṣus-ādīnām
śobhārtham                 → śobhā-artham
dvayodbhavaḥ               → dvaya-udbhavaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| jāti | feminine noun in compound | intrinsic faculty-kind or class |
| gocara | masculine noun in compound | object-range, field within which a faculty operates |
| vijñāna | neuter noun in compound | corresponding type of object-specific cognition |
| sāmānyāt | ablative neuter singular | because of commonality or shared determination |
| eka-dhātutā | nominative feminine singular abstract noun | status of constituting one domain |
| dvitve | locative neuter singular | despite or in the condition of duality |
| api | concessive particle | even though |
| cakṣus-ādīnām | genitive plural | of the eye and the analogous paired faculties |
| śobhā-artham | accusative neuter singular used adverbially | for the sake of beauty or symmetry |
| tu | contrastive particle | however; changes from classification to bodily arrangement |
| dvaya-udbhavaḥ | nominative masculine singular | arising or occurrence as a pair |

The Bhāṣya restricts `cakṣus-ādīni` here to the paired eye, ear, and nose
faculties. Tongue and body do not generate the proposed increase from
eighteen to twenty-one domains.

## 4. Grammar

The first line gives an ablative cause:

```text
jāti-gocara-vijñāna-sāmānyāt
    because of commonality of
        kind,
        object-range,
        and corresponding cognition

eka-dhātutā
    there is one-domain status
```

`Dvitve api` is concessive: the bodily faculty occurs in two sites, yet this
numerical duality does not overturn domain-unity.

The Bhāṣya distributes the three commonalities for the eye:

```text
two eyes
    share eye-nature                 → jāti-sāmānya
    have visible form as object      → gocara-sāmānya
    support one type of eye-cognition → vijñāna-sāmānya
```

The same construction is to be applied analogically to the two ears and two
nostril sites.

`Tu` marks a change of question in the second line. The first line explains
why two bodily sites count as one domain. The second explains why one domain
has two bodily sites:

```text
classificatory unity
    → kind + range + cognition

paired embodiment
    → beauty or symmetry of the support
```

The aesthetic explanation is not a fourth criterion of domain-unity.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Because of commonality of kind, object-range, and cognition, the eye and the others have one-domain status even though dual. Their arising as a pair, however, is for the sake of beauty.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The two eyes constitute one eye-domain because they share one faculty-kind, take the same class of visible objects, and support the same type of visual cognition. The same reasoning applies to the paired ears and nostril sites. Their bodily occurrence in pairs serves the symmetry of the supporting organism rather than multiplying the domains.

The second translation distinguishes anatomical instances from the classes
recognized by the dhātu system. The faculties remain numerically paired and
fully real as bodily supports.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Domain-unity is not numerical singleness. Multiple bodily sites form one domain when they share an intrinsic kind, an object-range, and a corresponding mode of cognition. The domain is therefore a unity of lawful determination rather than a count of visible organs.

Organon rendering:

> The method distinguishes empirical multiplicity from conceptual unity. Two supports are not arbitrarily declared one; they are one domain because the same kind relates them to the same object-field and the same cognitive form. Unity is demonstrated through an articulated relation.

VAK 1.18 established classification through own-nature and difference from
other-nature. VAK 1.19 now shows how that rule operates when one nature has
more than one bodily instance.

## 8. Logical Determination

The objector proposes an anatomical counting rule:

```text
18 domains
+ second eye
+ second ear
+ second nostril site
= 21 domains
```

Vasubandhu rejects the inference:

```text
NumericallyDistinct(x, y)
    ↛ DomainDistinct(x, y)
```

One-domain status requires three commonalities:

```text
SameDomain(x, y)
    ↔ SameFacultyKind(x, y)
    ∧ SameObjectRange(x, y)
    ∧ SupportsSameCognitionType(x, y)
```

For the eyes:

```text
EyeNature(leftEye) = EyeNature(rightEye)
ObjectRange(leftEye) = VisibleForm = ObjectRange(rightEye)
CognitionType(leftEye) = VisualCognition = CognitionType(rightEye)
    → OneEyeDomain
```

The explanatory levels remain distinct:

```text
Why one domain?
    → shared kind, range, and cognition

Why two bodily sites?
    → symmetry of the support
```

The method therefore counts domains intensionally and relationally rather
than by gross anatomical enumeration.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 1.19 is the first worked test of the classification method. The system
encounters an apparent counterexample: if classification follows what a
dharma is, should two eyes not be two eye-domains? The answer refines
`svabhāva`. Own-nature is not numerical individuality. It is the determinate
kind together with its object-range and cognition.

This is an important reason Dhātu is conceptual rather than tamasically
external. An outward count sees two organs and demands two domains. Dharma
analysis comprehends the two as moments of one visual architecture:

```text
shared faculty-kind
    ↕
shared visible domain
    ↕
shared visual cognition-type
```

The cognitive criterion is decisive but not solitary. Vasubandhu gives three
irreducible grounds—kind, object-range, and cognition. We should therefore
not rewrite the verse as though vijñānaskandha alone produces the unity. The
domain is the structured correlation of all three.

This gives the Organon method a second rule:

```text
do not multiply domains merely because their empirical supports are many;
determine whether kind, range, and cognition are genuinely different
```

The remark about beauty belongs to the embodiment of the faculty, not to the
logic of classification. It may be historically curious, but it should not
obscure the powerful methodological distinction between numerical
multiplicity and domain difference.
