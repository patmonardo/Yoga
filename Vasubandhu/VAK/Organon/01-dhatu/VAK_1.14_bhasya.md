# VAK_1.14 Bhāṣya — Ten Material Bases and Domains, Feeling, and Mark-Grasping

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> indriyārthās ta eveṣṭā daśāyatanadhātavaḥ /
>
> vedanānubhavaḥ saṃjñā nimittodgrahaṇātmikā // 1.14 //

> Those very faculties and objects are accepted as ten āyatanas and ten
> dhātus. Vedanā is felt experience; saṃjñā has the nature of grasping a mark.

The Bhāṣya places the five faculties and five sensory objects already
analyzed within the āyatana and dhātu arrangements. It then defines the
aggregates of vedanā and saṃjñā, distinguishing their operations and their
sixfold divisions.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> daśāyatanadhātavaḥ / āyatanavyavasthāyāṃ daśāyatanāni /
> cakṣurāyatanaṃ rūpāyatanaṃ yāvat kāyāyatanaṃ spraṣṭavyāyatanam iti /
> dhātuvyavasthāyāṃ ta eva daśa dhātavaś cakṣurdhātū rūpadhātur yāvat
> kāyadhātuḥ spraṣṭavyadhātur iti / ukto rūpaskandhas tasya
> cāyatanavyavasthānam / vedanādayo vaktavyāḥ / tatra vedanānubhavaḥ /
> trividho 'nubhavo vedanāskandhaḥ / sukho duḥkho 'duḥkhāsukhaś ca /
> sa punar bhidyamānaḥ ṣaḍ vedanākāyāḥ / cakṣuḥsaṃsparśajā vedanā
> yāvan manaḥsaṃsparśajā vedanā iti /
>
> saṃjñā nimittodgrahaṇātmikā / yāvan
> nīlapītadīrghahrasvastripuruṣamitrāmitrasukhaduḥkhādinimittodgrahaṇam
> asau saṃjñāskandhaḥ / sa punar bhidyamānaḥ ṣaṭsaṃjñākāyā vedanāvat /

The unit runs from the return to the constituents of rūpa at printed
location 010.07 through the sixfold saṃjñā statement at 010.18. The
following `caturbhyo 'nye tu saṃskāraskandhaḥ` begins 1.15. The local
research Bhāṣya report includes that next verse's opening and its commentary;
here it is reserved for the next numbered study so that the sequence does
not duplicate or anticipate its analysis.

The running text has several evident defects. Its `indriyārthasta`,
`aśāyatanadhātavaḥ`, `cakṣurchātū`, `vaktavyaḥ`, and
`yāvamvanaḥsaṃsparśajā` are read as `indriyārthās ta`,
`daśāyatanadhātavaḥ`, `cakṣurdhātū`, `vaktavyāḥ`, and
`yāvan manaḥsaṃsparśajā`. The damaged third feeling term,
`, duḥkhāsukhaś ca`, is read as `'duḥkhāsukhaś ca`, following the
research report and the explicit threefold division. Word division and
punctuation are editorial. `Cakṣurdhātū` before `rūpadhātuḥ` preserves
the sandhi of the masculine singular `cakṣurdhātuḥ`.

In the mark list, both local texts print `mitramitra`; the reading
`mitrāmitra`, friend and enemy, is supplied provisionally and recorded as
a repair rather than silently treated as an intact witness reading.
The introductory `yāvan` is retained and rendered “as far as” below; its
compressed connection with the following enumeration remains provisional.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Those very faculties and objects that have been stated to have the nature
> of the rūpa aggregate are accepted as ten āyatanas and ten dhātus. In the
> arrangement by āyatanas, they are ten āyatanas: the eye-base and
> visible-form base, through the body-base and tangible base. In the
> arrangement by dhātus, those same factors are ten dhātus: the eye-domain
> and visible-form domain, through the body-domain and tangible domain.
>
> The rūpa aggregate and its arrangement as āyatanas have been explained.
> Vedanā and the rest must be explained. Here vedanā is experience. The
> vedanā aggregate is threefold experience: pleasant, painful, and neither
> painful nor pleasant. Further divided, it comprises six groups of vedanā:
> vedanā born from eye-contact through vedanā born from mind-contact.
>
> Saṃjñā has the nature of grasping a mark. As far as the grasping of marks
> such as blue, yellow, long, short, woman, man, friend, enemy, pleasure,
> pain, and the rest extends, that is the saṃjñā aggregate. Further divided,
> it comprises six groups of saṃjñā, as with vedanā.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

The verse bridges two tasks: arranging already analyzed material factors
within two classifications, and beginning the definitions of further
aggregates.

```text
five faculties and five sensory objects
    → ten entries in the āyatana arrangement
    → the same ten entries in the dhātu arrangement

vedanā aggregate
    → experience: pleasant, painful, neither painful nor pleasant
    → six groups according to contact

saṃjñā aggregate
    → grasping a mark
    → six groups as with vedanā
```

The prose is explanatory. No distinct objector, scriptural quotation, or
school attribution appears within this bounded unit. The volition and
scriptural discussion in the research report belongs to the commentary on
the opening of 1.15, not to these sentences.

## 5. The Same Ten Factors under Two Arrangements

The relative-correlative `ya eva ... ta eva` preserves identity of the
factors across the classificatory change. `Indriyārthāḥ` joins the
faculties and their sensory objects; `daśāyatanadhātavaḥ` is explicitly
unpacked in the prose as ten āyatanas and ten dhātus.

The repeated locative `vyavasthāyām` specifies the arrangement under which
the factors are being counted. The list proceeds in faculty/object pairs:

| Pair | Āyatana arrangement | Dhātu arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| eye and visible form | eye-base and visible-form base | eye-domain and visible-form domain |
| ear and sound | ear-base and sound-base | ear-domain and sound-domain |
| nose and odor | nose-base and odor-base | nose-domain and odor-domain |
| tongue and taste | tongue-base and taste-base | tongue-domain and taste-domain |
| body and tangible | body-base and tangible base | body-domain and tangible domain |

The prose gives the endpoints with `yāvat`, “through”; the intervening
pairs are expanded here from the fivefold enumeration already established
in 1.09. Each faculty and each object supplies an entry. The claim is not
that each faculty/object pair becomes one āyatana or one dhātu.

The ten are constituents of the rūpa aggregate, not its entire extension.
Avijñapti was its eleventh constituent in 1.09 and remains outside this
particular tenfold enumeration. The passage therefore does not equate
`rūpaskandha` without qualification with ten material factors.

Identity of the factors also does not make the classifications synonymous.
The present unit establishes correspondence and counts; it does not yet
give the complete derivations of āyatana and dhātu or enumerate their
remaining members. “Base” and “domain” in the translation keep the two
series distinguishable without supplying those later explanations.

## 6. Vedanā: Threefold Experience and Sixfold Contact

`Vedanānubhavaḥ` resolves as `vedanā anubhavaḥ`: vedanā is experience or
undergoing. The prose immediately determines the scope of that word by
pleasant, painful, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant experience. “Felt
experience” in the anchor anticipates this clarification without making
vedanā the entirety of cognition or experience in every sense.

The next division has a different basis. `Sa punar bhidyamānaḥ` introduces
further differentiation, and `ṣaḍ vedanākāyāḥ` names six groups. The
examples are formed with `saṃsparśaja`, born from contact, beginning with
eye-contact and ending with mind-contact.

The two determinations answer different questions:

| Respect | Local division |
|---|---|
| how experience is felt | pleasant, painful, neither painful nor pleasant |
| the contact from which it arises | eye through mind, six groups |

The verse and prose do not enumerate eighteen unrestricted combinations.
Their statement of two divisions should not be expanded into a claim that
every feeling quality occurs through every contact without qualification.
The local expression also makes origin through contact explicit, rather
than merely assigning a label from an associated faculty.

## 7. Saṃjñā: Grasping a Mark

`Nimittodgrahaṇātmikā` qualifies the feminine `saṃjñā`: having the nature
of grasping a mark. The compound joins `nimitta`, mark, with `udgrahaṇa`,
taking up or grasping. The prose identifies this operation with the
saṃjñā aggregate and illustrates it through several kinds of marks.

Blue and yellow return to color; long and short return to configuration.
Woman and man, friend and enemy, and pleasure and pain extend the examples
beyond an exclusively visual definition. The list is open, as indicated by
`ādi`, “and the rest.” The repaired friend/enemy pair remains a textual
qualification of the reading presented here.

Pleasure and pain occur in the account of both vedanā and saṃjñā, but
with different grammatical and explanatory roles. In vedanā they specify
the quality of experience. Here they occur within a list of marks being
grasped. The felt quality and its apprehension as a distinguishing mark
must therefore remain separate operations in the analysis.

`Vedanāvat`, “as with vedanā,” supplies the model for the sixfold division
of saṃjñā. It transfers the pattern of six groups, not the preceding
threefold classification of feeling quality. The sentence does not require
the two aggregates to have the same function simply because their contact
classification corresponds.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

1. “Those very” refers to the five faculties and five sensory objects
   already included within rūpa; it does not include avijñapti in this ten.
2. The ten factors furnish ten entries in each arrangement. Correspondence
   of constituents preserves the distinction between āyatana and dhātu.
3. `Anubhava` is locally determined through pleasant, painful, and
   neither-painful-nor-pleasant experience.
4. The sixfold division of vedanā concerns birth from contact, eye through
   mind; it is a different classificatory respect from feeling quality.
5. Saṃjñā is the operation of grasping marks, illustrated through visible,
   personal, relational, and experiential examples.
6. Its sixfold classification follows vedanā, while its operation remains
   distinct from felt experience.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The first movement gives a precise case of the same determinations entering
different systematic arrangements. The content is preserved, but its
classified position is articulated anew. This supports the Organon inquiry
into systematic meaning: identity across arrangements must be established
through the specific factors and relations, not presumed from shared words.

The second movement distinguishes undergoing an experience from taking up
a mark. Pleasure can be felt and pleasure can be grasped as a mark; those
statements concern different operations. Their distinction is useful for
our inquiry into learning, because becoming able to discriminate what is
experienced requires an account of more than the experience's occurrence.

This is an Organon question prompted by the definitions, not a completed
theory of learning stated here. The passage does not identify mark-grasping
with developed prajñā or present vedanā followed by saṃjñā as a temporal
sequence of cognitive stages. Its sequence is expository; the different
operations and corresponding sixfold groupings are the determinations it
actually supplies.

The domain inquiry consequently gains two kinds of precision: the same
material factors retain their identity across classifications, while
mental operations remain distinguishable even when their examples or
contact groupings correspond. These are complementary requirements for
understanding how a field becomes systematically articulated.
