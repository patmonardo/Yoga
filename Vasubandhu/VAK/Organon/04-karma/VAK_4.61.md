# VAK_4.61

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
धर्मक्षान्तिषु वैराग्ये चानन्तर्यपथाष्टके ।
या चेतना द्वादशधा कर्म कृष्णक्षयाय तत् ॥ ४.६१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
dharmakṣāntiṣu vairāgye cānantaryapathāṣṭake /
yā cetanā dvādaśadhā karma kṛṣṇakṣayāya tat // 4.61 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
dharma-kṣāntiṣu vairāgye ca ānantarya-patha-aṣṭake /
yā cetanā dvādaśadhā karma kṛṣṇa-kṣayāya tat //
```

The Bhāṣya identifies the four patiences as `dharmajñānakṣānti` on the Path of
Seeing and the eight paths as uninterrupted paths of desire-domain detachment.

|---|---|---|

| `vairāgye` | locative neuter singular | in detachment; here detachment from the desire domain |

**`dharmajñānakṣānti`**

The four dharma-knowledge patiences are Path-of-Seeing moments corresponding
to the four truths. Their associated volitions participate in abandoning
black karma.

**`ānantaryamārga`**

An uninterrupted path is the active abandoning phase immediately connected
with the liberation-path that follows. Here the first eight moments of
desire-domain detachment are counted.

**`kṣaya / prahāṇa`**

The kārikā says exhaustion; the Bhāṣya glosses the function as abandonment.
The target is a determinate karmic class, not an entity annihilated without
analysis.

## 4. Grammar

**The twelvefold volition**

```text
dharma-kṣāntiṣu
kāma-vairāgye ca
ānantarya-patha-aṣṭake
yā cetanā
sā dvādaśadhā [bhavati]
```

> The volition present in the dharma-knowledge patiences and in the eight
> uninterrupted paths of desire-domain detachment is twelvefold.

The locatives state the path-moments in which the volition occurs. The number
is the sum of four and eight.

**Its karmic function**

```text
tat [cetanā-rūpam]
karma
kṛṣṇa-kṣayāya [bhavati]
```

> That karma is for the exhaustion of black karma.

The dative expresses the target and direction of the path-volition's activity.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The volition in the dharma-patiences and in the eight uninterrupted paths of
> detachment is twelvefold; that karma is for the exhaustion of black karma.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Four volitions occur in the dharma-knowledge patiences of the Path of Seeing,
> and eight occur in the first eight uninterrupted paths of desire-domain
> detachment. These twelve volitions are karma without influxes whose specific
> function is the abandonment of black karma. Not every karma without influxes
> exhausts every ordinary karmic class indiscriminately; the Path's negative
> efficacy is differentiated by stage and target.

The exact identities of the four and eight, the restriction to desire-domain
detachment, and the gloss of exhaustion as abandonment are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Liberating activity is not an empty negation of karma in general. Each
> path-volition is a positive determination whose function is the removal of a
> corresponding practical determination. Four seeing-path operations and eight
> uninterrupted operations of desire-domain detachment together constitute a
> twelvefold activity directed specifically against black karma. Negation is
> therefore rational because it is internally differentiated by what negates,
> what is negated, and the stage at which the relation becomes actual.

The Path operates upon the karmic circuit through determinate volition.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The twelvefold derivation**

```text
Path of Seeing
    4 dharma-knowledge patiences

desire-domain detachment
    first 8 uninterrupted paths

total
    12 cetanās
```

**`cetanā` as karma**

The verse explicitly identifies the relevant karma as volition:

```text
cetanā
    occurring in a determinate Path moment
    → anāsrava karma
    → abandonment-function
```

This does not say that every form of karma is reducible without remainder to
one undifferentiated volition. It identifies the karmic factor operative here.

**Why only the first eight uninterrupted paths**

The ninth uninterrupted path of desire-domain detachment is assigned a
different target in VAK 4.62. The arithmetic is therefore doctrinal, not merely
convenient.

**The initial question rejected**

The Bhāṣya asks:

```text
does every anāsrava karma
exhaust all three productive karmic classes?
```

and answers no. Exhaustive activity has a differentiated extension.

**Black karma as target**

The twelve volitions are classified by function rather than by a future
pleasant or painful maturation:

```text
ordinary karma
    named through the vipāka it generates

path karma
    named through the karma it abandons
```

## 8. Logical Determination

**Typed negative efficacy**

```text
PATH_STAGE(P)
    → VOLITION(C)
    → TARGET_OF_ABANDONMENT(C, K)
```

For this verse:

```text
4 SEEING_PATH_VOLITIONS
    + 8 DESIRE_DETACHMENT_VOLITIONS
    → ABANDON(BLACK_KARMA)
```

**No indiscriminate universal rule**

```text
ANASRAVA(K)
    does not entail
EXHAUSTS(K, every productive karma)
```

The specific path-location determines the target.

**Positive operation of negation**

```text
absence of reproductive kleśa-ground
    → does not yield inactivity

instead
    → determinate cetanā
    → active abandonment of black karma
```

**Ground and counter-ground**

```text
black karma
    grounded in afflictive practical production

anāsrava path-volition
    counter-ground whose actuality removes that production
```

The counter-ground is specified by its target-relation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**`Tadabhāva` requires a method of removal**

If kleśa is the Ground of crooked, corrupting, or staining activity, its
absence cannot be understood merely as verbally prefixing `a-` to the result.
VAK 4.61 gives the practical mediation:

```text
path-stage
    → uncontaminated volition
    → abandonment-operation
    → non-being of the targeted karmic determination
```

The absence is achieved and structured.

**Practical Dharma theory becomes surgical**

The Path does not vaguely “purify karma.” It supplies different operations for
different targets. VAK 4.61 isolates the twelve operations directed against
black karma; VAK 4.62 continues the distribution.

**Cetanā is now explicitly operational**

Earlier we asked whether `cetanā` is the Concept dividing into manifest and
nonmanifest karma. This verse does not settle that whole architectural
question, but it confirms that volition can itself be the path-karma performing
karmic exhaustion.

**Rational and Practical Karma meet in the Path**

The target must first be discriminated correctly; then a determinate practical
operation removes it. Knowing the Ground and transforming the Ground become
two moments of one Path.

This is the Organon contact. The initial rejection, arithmetic, Path locations,
and abandonment target belong to the Bhāṣya; the counter-ground formulation is
project synthesis.
