# VAK 4.35 Bhāṣya — The Bases and Times of Three Restraints

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
sarvobhayebhyaḥ kāmāpto vartamānebhya āpyate /
maulebhyaḥ sarvakālebhyo dhyānānāsravasaṃvarau // 4.35 //
```

> Desire-realm restraint is acquired from present bases across all
> [three stages] and both [relevant divisions]. Meditation-born and
> uncontaminated restraints are acquired from root karmic courses
> across all times.

Spacing and punctuation are editorial. The running source's
`skandha dhātvāyatanāti` and `sāmantakapṛṣṭhemyaḥ` are retained
as uncertain forms. The latter is contextually parallel to
`sāmantakapṛṣṭhebhyaḥ` in the first corner, but no silent repair
is made. The Sanskrit calls the three restraints
`prātimokṣa`, `dhyāna`, and `anāsrava`; the verse calls the first
`kāmāpta`, belonging to the desire realm.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

There are these three restraints: prātimokṣa, meditation-born, and
uncontaminated. If one of them is acquired from a given basis, are
the other two acquired from it as well? No. What, then? “The
desire-realm restraint is acquired from present bases across all
and both.” The desire-realm restraint, that is, prātimokṣa, is
acquired only from presently existing aggregates, sense bases,
and elements. “All” means the principal act, its preparation,
and its aftermath. “Both” means bases designated sentient and
non-sentient, and faults naturally blameworthy and blameworthy by
prohibition. It is acquired from present aggregates, sense bases,
and elements only, because it operates with living beings as its
support; past and future ones are not counted as living beings in
that operative sense.

“The meditation-born and uncontaminated restraints are acquired
from root karmic courses across all times.” These two restraints
are acquired only from the principal karmic courses, not their
preparation or aftermath, still less from merely prohibited
faults. They are acquired from aggregates, sense bases, and
elements belonging to all times, including past and future.

For this reason a four-cornered analysis is made. There are
aggregates, elements, and sense bases from which prātimokṣa
restraint alone is acquired, not the two higher restraints; and
so on. The first corner comprises present bases connected with
preparatory or subsequent stages and with faults blameworthy by
prohibition. The second comprises past and future bases connected
with principal karmic courses. The third comprises present bases
connected with principal karmic courses. The fourth comprises
past and future bases connected with preparatory or subsequent
stages [the final Sanskrit form is uncertain].

One should not infer that principal karmic courses are actually
occurring at the very moment restraint is acquired. The proper
expression is “from present supports.” Only future actions can
be restrained; past and present actions cannot literally be
restrained.

## 4. Scope and the Four Corners

`Sarva` covers three stages: `maula` (the root act), `prayoga`
(preparation), and `pṛṣṭha` (aftermath). `Ubhaya` covers two
different pairs: sentient and non-sentient bases; naturally
blameworthy and prohibition-based faults. Thus prātimokṣa
restraint has a broad *kind* of coverage but a present basis.
The two higher restraints have narrower coverage of karmic
stages, yet include bases of past, present, and future times.

| Bases and stages | Prātimokṣa | Dhyāna and anāsrava |
|---|---|---|
| Present; preparation, aftermath, or prohibited fault | Yes | No |
| Past or future; root karmic course | No | Yes |
| Present; root karmic course | Yes | Yes |
| Past or future; preparation or aftermath | No | No |

This is the `catuṣkoṭika` actually supplied by the Bhāṣya. It
keeps the three restraints from being treated as interchangeable
merely because all are called `saṃvara`.

## 5. Present Support, Future Restraint

The final correction separates **acquisition from a basis** and
**restraint of an action**. Present aggregates, sense bases, and
elements can ground acquisition of prātimokṣa; this does not mean
an already occurring karmic course is being prevented at that
instant. The action to be restrained lies ahead. The text's
formula `vartamānādhiṣṭhānebhyaḥ` therefore controls the earlier
shorthand `vartamānebhyaḥ`.

This temporal distinction is especially important because the
Bhāṣya also permits the two higher restraints to be acquired
with respect to bases of all three times. Their *range of bases*
must not be confused with a claim that a completed past deed can
be undone or that an occurring present deed can be prevented
after it is underway.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our Practical World System, 4.35 supplies a precise way to
describe a vow's domain: what its present support is, which
classes of action it covers, and when its protective operation
can take effect. Our wheel—adhyāsa ceases and the Idea of the
True and Good emerges—also needs this temporal discipline. A
new determination of action arises now, while its restraint of
future action remains to be enacted. This is our systematic use
of the Kośa's distinctions, not an attribution of the wheel to
Vasubandhu.
