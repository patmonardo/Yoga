# VAK_4.47

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सुखवेद्यं शुभं ध्यानादातृतीयादतः परम् ।
अदुःखासुखवेद्यं तु दुःखवेद्यमिहाशुभम् ॥ ४.४७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
sukhavedyaṃ śubhaṃ dhyānād ā tṛtīyād ataḥ param /
aduḥkhāsukhavedyaṃ tu duḥkhavedyam ihāśubham // 4.47 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
sukha-vedyam śubham dhyānāt ā tṛtīyāt ataḥ param /
aduḥkha-asukha-vedyam tu duḥkha-vedyam iha aśubham //
```

The Bhāṣya makes explicit that the field of pleasant maturation begins in the
desire domain and extends through the third dhyāna.


| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `sukhavedyam` | nominative neuter singular compound | to be experienced as pleasure |
| `śubham` | nominative neuter singular | wholesome karma |
| `dhyānāt` | ablative neuter singular | from meditation; beginning with the meditative range |
| `ā` | preposition with ablative | as far as; through |
| `tṛtīyāt` | ablative neuter singular | through the third [dhyāna] |
| `ataḥ param` | ablative phrase and adjective | beyond this; above the third dhyāna |
| `aduḥkhāsukhavedyam` | nominative neuter singular compound | to be experienced as neither pain nor pleasure |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but; whereas |
| `duḥkhavedyam` | nominative neuter singular compound | to be experienced as pain |
| `iha` | adverb | here; here in the desire domain |
| `aśubham` | nominative neuter singular | unwholesome karma |

**`vedya`**

The gerundive marks what is to be experienced when karma matures. It does not
describe the feeling necessarily accompanying the action at its production.

**`iha`**

The Bhāṣya explains “here” as the desire domain. Unwholesome karma occurs only
there and is classified as painfully experienced.

**`sasaṃbhāra`**

This Bhāṣya term means “together with its associated assemblage.” Feeling
characterizes the maturation, but feeling alone is not its entire fruit.

## 4. Grammar

**Pleasantly experienced karma**

```text
śubham [karma]
[kāmadhātau]
dhyānāt ā tṛtīyāt
sukha-vedyam [bhavati]
```

> Wholesome karma in the desire domain and through the third dhyāna is to be
> experienced as pleasure.

The kārikā compresses the lower limit. The Bhāṣya states that the relevant
`bhūmi` includes the desire domain together with the first three dhyānas.

**Neutral-feeling maturation**

```text
ataḥ param
[śubham karma]
aduḥkha-asukha-vedyam tu [bhavati]
```

> Beyond the third dhyāna, however, wholesome karma is to be experienced as
> neither pain nor pleasure.

`Śubham` continues into this clause. The contrast follows the affective
constitution of the levels beyond the third dhyāna.

**Painfully experienced karma**

```text
iha
aśubham [karma]
duḥkha-vedyam [bhavati]
```

> Here in the desire domain, unwholesome karma is to be experienced as pain.

`Iha` restricts the occurrence of unwholesome karma to the desire domain.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Wholesome karma from the desire domain through the third dhyāna is to be
> experienced as pleasure. Beyond this it is to be experienced as neither
> pain nor pleasure; here, unwholesome karma is to be experienced as pain.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Wholesome karma belonging to the desire domain and the first three dhyānas
> matures with pleasant feeling. Above the third dhyāna, wholesome karma
> matures with feeling that is neither painful nor pleasant. Here in the
> desire domain, unwholesome karma matures with painful feeling. These names
> characterize the maturation by its feeling, although feeling is not the
> whole fruit: its associated complex matures with it.

The inclusion of the desire domain and the qualification concerning the whole
associated result-complex are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Karma matures not only in a determinate domain and level but through a
> determinate mode of experience. Wholesome karma through the third dhyāna has
> pleasure as the characteristic feeling of its result; higher wholesome karma
> has neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling; desire-domain unwholesome karma has
> pain. Yet the experienced feeling is only the dominant determination by
> which the concrete maturation-complex is named, not the totality of that
> complex.

The verse therefore connects karmic production to experience without reducing
the fruit of karma to an isolated sensation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The three `vedanīya` classes**

```text
sukhavedanīya
    karma whose maturation is characterized by pleasant feeling

duḥkhavedanīya
    karma whose maturation is characterized by painful feeling

aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya
    karma whose maturation is characterized by neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
```

**Distribution by level**

```text
desire domain through third dhyāna
    wholesome karma → pleasant maturation

above third dhyāna
    wholesome karma → neither-painful-nor-pleasant maturation

desire domain
    unwholesome karma → painful maturation
```

**Feeling at production and feeling at maturation**

```text
feeling accompanying the action
    ≠ necessarily
feeling characterizing its maturation
```

`Vedanīya` classifies the latter relation.

**Feeling and associated assemblage**

The Bhāṣya rejects:

```text
vedanā alone = the whole fruit
```

and affirms:

```text
fruit
    = characteristic feeling
    + associated maturation-complex
```

The precise members of that complex are not enumerated in this verse.

## 8. Logical Determination

**From level to experiential form**

VAK 4.46 established a relation between production-level and maturation-level.
VAK 4.47 adds:

```text
maturation-level
    → available feeling-form
```

Together:

```text
karma-quality
    + production field
    + maturation-level
    → characteristic feeling of result
```

**The Indriya application**

```text
karma
    determines a maturation-complex

vedanā-indriya
    supplies its characteristic experienced mode
```

The feeling-faculty does not replace the karmic result. It is the moment
through which that result is experientially determined.

**Determination of the whole by a characteristic moment**

```text
EXPERIENTIAL_CHARACTER(result) = feeling class

but

RESULT ≠ feeling alone
```

The name selects a decisive aspect without dissolving the concrete whole into
that aspect.

**No simple ascent of pleasure**

```text
higher than third dhyāna
    does not mean more pleasure

instead

pleasure/pain opposition
    → neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
```

The hierarchy changes the form of feeling rather than increasing one member
of the lower opposition indefinitely.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Dhātu, bhūmi, and Indriya now operate together**

```text
Dhātu
    field in which karma is produced and matures

Bhūmi
    level determining the available affective constitution

Indriya
    faculty through which the matured result is experienced

Karma
    law connecting production, maturation, and experience
```

This is precisely an application of the earlier theoretical architecture.
None of the moments can substitute for the others.

**The result is a concrete complex**

The `sasaṃbhāra` qualification is the decisive safeguard. A classification by
feeling can tempt us to treat pleasure or pain as the entire karmic result.
Vasubandhu instead preserves the concrete complex while identifying its
experiential character.

**Neutral feeling is positive determination**

Neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling is not the absence of experience. It is a
determinate feeling-mode appropriate to the higher levels. The middle must not
be read as a blank between two positive extremes.

This is the Organon contact. The distribution, restriction of `iha`, and
`sasaṃbhāra` qualification belong to the Bhāṣya.
