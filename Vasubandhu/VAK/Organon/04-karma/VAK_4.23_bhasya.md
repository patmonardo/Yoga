# VAK 4.23 Bhāṣya — Possession of Manifest Action

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
vijñaptyā tu punaḥ sarve kurvanto madhyayānvitāḥ /
atītayā kṣaṇād ūrdhvam atyāgān nāsty ajātayā // 4.23 //
```

> In turn, all who perform a manifest action are endowed with it as
> present while performing it. After the first moment, they are also
> endowed with it as past, provided it has not been abandoned. No one
> is endowed with a future manifest action.

The running unit begins at [210|20] and ends at [211|01] in
`Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The preceding sentence closes
the avijñapti discussion; the first half of 4.24 starts at [211|02].

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
vijñaptyā tu punaḥ sarve kurvanto madhyayānvitāḥ.

sarve saṃvarāsaṃvaramadhyasthā yāvadijñaptiṃ kurvanti tāvat tayā
vartamānayā samanvāgatāḥ.

atītayā kṣaṇād ūrdhvam ātyāgāt.

prathamāt kṣaṇād ūrdhvam ātyāgād atītayā vijñaptyā samanvāgato bhavati.

nāsty ajātayā.

anāgatayā tu vijñaptyā na kaścit samanvāgataḥ.
```

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The source has
`yāvadijñaptiṃ` at [210|21], retained here rather than silently
changed to the expected `yāvad vijñaptiṃ`. It also reads `ātyāgāt`
and `ātyāgād` in the two following lines; the separate Kārikā
witness prints `atyāgān` by sandhi before `nāsti`. These spellings
are recorded without manufacturing a substantive disagreement.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Now, in turn, all who are acting are endowed with manifest action in
the middle temporal mode.” All three classes—those established in
restraint, those established in non-restraint, and those in neither—are
endowed with a manifest action as present for as long as they perform
it.

“After the first moment, with past [manifest action], provided it has
not been abandoned.” After the first moment, one is also endowed with
past manifest action under the condition of non-abandonment.

“There is no [possession] with the not-yet-arisen.” No one is endowed
with a future manifest action.

## 4. Actual Production and Present Possession

The contrastive `tu punaḥ` marks the change from avijñapti to
`vijñapti`, manifest bodily or verbal action. `Sarve` is expressly
glossed as **all three** practical standings. Standing in restraint,
non-restraint, or neither does not alter the immediate temporal rule:
while producing a vijñapti, its agent possesses it as present.

`Madhyayā`, “in the middle mode,” means **present**, the temporal
middle between past and future. The Bhāṣya gives its extent precisely:
`yāvat ... kurvanti tāvat`, as long as they perform the manifest
action. This describes possession of the action as present; it does
not decide its moral quality merely from the bearer's standing.

## 5. Past and Future Are Asymmetric

After the first moment, the agent may also possess the manifest action
as **past**, subject to the inherited non-abandonment condition. The
last line excludes possession of **future** vijñapti altogether:
`anāgatayā tu vijñaptyā na kaścit samanvāgataḥ`. This is a rule about
being endowed with a not-yet-arisen manifestation, not a denial that
a person can intend or plan a future deed.

The resulting pattern is:

| Temporal mode | Possession of vijñapti here |
|---|---|
| Present | While the action is actually performed |
| Past | After its first moment, while not abandoned |
| Future | No one is endowed with it |

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For the Organon, the passage checks any slide from a **Plan** to an
**accomplished act**. An intention to speak or act can be real as an
intention, but the future manifest action is not already possessed as
`vijñapti`. A vow's standing determination, a current performance,
and a projected performance therefore need separate places in our
practical account. This is the Organon's use of the Kośa distinction,
not a translation of the Kārikā into our architecture.
