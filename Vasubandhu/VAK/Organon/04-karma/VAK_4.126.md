# VAK_4.126

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
योगप्रवर्तितं कर्म ससमुत्थापकं त्रिधा ।
लिपिमुद्रे सगणनं काव्यं संख्या यथाक्रमम् ॥ ४.१२६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
yogapravartitaṃ karma sasamutthāpakaṃ tridhā |
lipimudre sagaṇanaṃ kāvyaṃ saṃkhyā yathākramam || 4.126 ||
```

The Bhāṣya glosses `yogapravartita` as `upāyaviśeṣapravartita`, set in
motion by a specific method or means. The ordered grouping is bodily, verbal,
and mental activity respectively.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
yoga-pravartitam | karma | sa-samutthāpakam | tridhā |
lipi-mudre | sa-gaṇanam | kāvyam | saṃkhyā | yathā-kramam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `yogapravartitam` | nominative singular neuter compound | set in motion through a specific applied method |
| `karma` | nominative singular neuter | activity |
| `sasamutthāpakam` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective | together with that which gives rise to it |
| `tridhā` | indeclinable | threefold |
| `lipi-mudre` | nominative dual neuter | writing and sealing |
| `sagaṇanam` | coordinated neuter singular form | together with calculation |
| `kāvyam` | nominative singular neuter | poetry |
| `saṃkhyā` | nominative singular feminine | mental reckoning or collecting |
| `yathākramam` | indeclinable | respectively, in the stated order |

**Bhāṣya-resolved distribution**

```text
kāyakarma  → lipi, mudrā
vākkarma   → gaṇanā, kāvya
manaskarma → saṃkhyā
```

The operative channel, not the subject matter, governs the classification.

## 4. Grammar

**Threefold activity with its initiating factor**

```text
yogapravartitaṃ karma
    sasamutthāpakam
    tridhā [bhavati]
```

> Activity set in motion by a specific method, considered together with what
> gives rise to it, is threefold.

`Sa-` includes the initiating factor in the analysis of the activity.
`Tridhā` is explained by the Bhāṣya as body, speech, and mind.

**Respectively ordered examples**

```text
lipi-mudre       → kāyakarma
gaṇanā-kāvyam    → vākkarma
saṃkhyā          → manaskarma
```

`Yathākramam` fixes this pairing and removes the first-pass uncertainty.
`Saṃkhyā` is further defined as `manasā saṃkalanaṃ dharmāṇām`, mental
collecting or reckoning of dharmas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Activity set in motion through applied method, together with what gives
> rise to it, is threefold: writing and sealing, calculation and poetry, and
> reckoning, respectively.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Familiar learned activities are activities set in motion through a specific
> method and considered together with their initiating factors. They remain
> classifiable through the three ordinary channels of karma: writing and
> sealing are bodily activity; calculation and poetry are verbal activity;
> and `saṃkhyā` is mental activity—the mind's collecting or reckoning of
> dharmas. The first four activities are events of the nature of the five
> aggregates rather than free-standing abstract symbolic objects.

The gloss of `yoga`, the exact ordered distribution, the definition of
`saṃkhyā`, and the five-aggregate determination are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A learned method becomes actual only through an operative channel. Writing,
> sealing, calculation, poetry, and mental reckoning are not a separate realm
> of disembodied forms: a specific means organizes an initiating condition,
> and the resulting activity occurs through body, speech, or mind. Even the
> internal collection of determinations is genuine activity. Technique is
> therefore neither the method alone nor the observable output alone, but the
> method's concrete activation within an aggregate-based process.

The restrained architecture is:

```text
specific method
    → initiating condition
    → bodily, verbal, or mental channel
    → determinate learned activity
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Yoga` — applied method in this passage**

The Bhāṣya fixes the contextual meaning through `upāyaviśeṣa`: a specific
means, method, or technique. The word should not here be translated as
contemplative union or made to carry the whole doctrine of Yoga.

**`Pravartita` — set in motion**

Marks the passage from an available method or capacity to its actual
operation.

**`Samutthāpaka` — initiating factor**

That which gives rise to or sets the learned activity in motion. The activity
is analyzed together with this causal factor without identifying initiator
and execution.

**`Lipi` — writing**

A methodically produced visible inscription, classified as bodily karma.

**`Mudrā` — sealing or stamping**

Production of a mark through bodily application of a fixed instrument, also
classified as bodily karma.

**`Gaṇanā` — calculation**

Calculation as an expressed operation, classified here as verbal karma. This
must be distinguished from purely mental reckoning.

**`Kāvya` — poetry**

Methodically formed verbal composition, classified as verbal karma.

**`Saṃkhyā` — mental reckoning**

Defined as the mental collecting or putting together of dharmas. It is mental
karma, not merely the resulting number or an inert enumeration.

**`Pañcaskandhasvabhāva` — of the nature of the five aggregates**

The Bhāṣya's determination of writing, sealing, calculation, and poetry. They
are aggregate-complex events rather than independently subsisting symbolic
entities.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
M = specific applied method
I = initiating factor
C = operative channel
A = determinate activity
```

Then:

```text
Activates(M, I)
    + OperatesThrough(I, C)
    → ActualizedAs(A)
```

The channel classification is exact:

```text
Writing ∨ Sealing       → BodilyKarma
Calculation ∨ Poetry    → VerbalKarma
MentalReckoning         → MentalKarma
```

And:

```text
ConcernsNumbers(x)
    does not determine
KarmicChannel(x)
```

because expressed calculation and internal reckoning concern related
material while belonging to different operative channels.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.126 shifts from the teleological classification of wholesome activity
to the `svabhāva` of five worldly learned practices. Its immediate purpose is
taxonomic and modest: a technical art does not escape the threefold karma
system merely because it is learned, symbolic, or rule-governed.

Nevertheless, the passage gives a precise contact point with Yoga Technē:

```text
upāyaviśeṣa      specific method
    → samutthāpaka   initiating determination
    → pravartita     running operation
    → kāya/vāk/manas concrete execution channel
```

This is the passage from Principle to operation within a determinate Bhūmi.
The method does not float above appearances; it becomes actual as embodied
inscription, verbal calculation or composition, or mental organization of
dharmas. Conversely, the visible mark alone does not disclose the complete
activity without its initiating method and determination.

`Manasā saṃkalanaṃ dharmāṇām` is especially important for the Organon: the
internal systematic collection of determinations already counts as karma.
Schema construction is an activity, not a passive representation waiting for
“real” action to begin.

This comparative application remains deliberately light. The source passage
classifies five learned worldly activities by their bodily, verbal, and
mental channels; it does not itself present a general theory of programming
or higher Art.
