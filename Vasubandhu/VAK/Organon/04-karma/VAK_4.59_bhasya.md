# VAK 4.59 Bhāṣya — Crookedness, Fault, Stain, and Four Karmas

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
vaṅkadoṣakaṣāyoktiḥ śāṭhyadveṣajarāgaje /
kṛṣṇaśuklādibhedena punaḥ karma caturvidham // 4.59 //
```

> “Crooked,” “faulty,” and “stained” are said of actions born,
> respectively, of deceit, hatred, and attachment. Karma is also
> fourfold by the distinction beginning with black and white.

The second half announces a sūtra classification whose terms and
allocation are supplied across 4.59–4.60.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Again, a sūtra speaks of three crooked actions: crookedness of body,
speech, and mind; three faults: faults of body, speech, and mind; and
three stains: stains of body, speech, and mind. These are to be
understood respectively: “Crooked,” “faulty,” and “stained” are said
of actions born of deceit, hatred, and attachment. Bodily action
born of deceit is called bodily crookedness because it follows
deviousness; the same applies to crooked speech and mind. Bodily
action born of hatred is called a bodily fault because it follows
the corruption of mind; the same applies to faults of speech and
mind. Bodily action born of attachment is called a bodily stain
because it follows coloring by attachment; the same applies to
stains of speech and mind.

“Karma is also fourfold by the distinction beginning with black and
white.” The cited sūtra states: there is black karma with black
maturation; white karma with white maturation; black-and-white karma
with black-and-white maturation; and karma neither black nor white,
without maturation, which leads to the exhaustion of karma. The
Bhāṣya now turns to the allocation of these classes.

## 4. Two Sūtra Classifications

The first classification crosses **three sources** of action with
**three channels**:

| Source | Bodily, verbal, and mental actions are called |
|---|---|
| Deceit (`śāṭhya`) | Crooked (`vaṅka`) |
| Hatred (`dveṣa`) | Faulty (`doṣa`) |
| Attachment (`rāga`) | Stained (`kaṣāya`) |

The bodily example is explained in each case, and the same relation
is extended to speech and mind. The second classification is a new
fourfold scheme of karmic quality and maturation. Its fourth member
also has a function: it tends toward the exhaustion of karma. Its
precise domain and the first three allocations are argued in 4.60;
they are not yet established by the present verse alone.

## 5. Organon Contact Point

The first scheme distinguishes an action's channel from its
afflictive source. The second distinguishes an action's quality,
its maturation, and its capacity to end karmic production. These
are useful separate fields in our practical classification, while
the source's particular ethical judgments remain Abhidharma claims.
