# VAK 4.122 Bhāṣya — Abstention and Purified Discipline

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
dauḥśīlyam aśubhaṃ rūpaṃ śīlaṃ tadviratir dvidhā /
pratikṣiptāc ca buddhena viśuddhaṃ tu caturguṇam // 4.122 //
```

> Misconduct is unwholesome form. Discipline is abstention from it
> in two modes, and also abstention from what the Buddha has
> prohibited. Purified discipline has four qualities.

The Bhāṣya immediately quotes 4.123a to enumerate those four
qualities before turning to cultivation.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The giving-based basis of merit has been discussed; now comes
discipline. Misconduct is unwholesome material form. Discipline is
abstention from it, occurring as both manifest indication
(`vijñapti`) and nonmanifest indication (`avijñapti`). The same
twofold abstention applies to conduct that is not misconduct by
its nature but has been prohibited by the Buddha, such as eating at
an improper time. For one who has undertaken the training, breaching
that rule becomes misconduct.

Purified discipline has four features: it is undamaged by misconduct;
undamaged by causes such as greed and other afflictions; based on the
opponent to misconduct, here mindfulness establishments; and directed
toward peace or nirvāṇa rather than a superior rebirth. Other teachers
offer a fivefold account distinguishing root and adjacent courses,
thoughts, mindfulness, and dedication to nirvāṇa. Still others
describe four kinds of discipline: held from fear, held from hope
of worldly benefit, aligned with awakening factors for liberation,
and undefiled discipline.

## 4. Twofold Abstention and Fourfold Purity

The initial `dvidhā` is the **vijñapti–avijñapti** distinction,
not the two sources of rules. The latter are misconduct by nature and
actions prohibited in a training one has undertaken. Thus one can
ask separately what is abstained from and how the abstention exists.

The four qualities of purity are also distinct from the alternative
four **types** of discipline at the end. The first classifies the
conditions of purified discipline; the final list distinguishes
different reasons or levels at which discipline is maintained.

## 5. Organon Contact Point — Project Reconstruction

For our study of action, this passage matters because discipline can
have both an observable performance and a continuing nonmanifest
aspect. The source treats them as forms of abstention; any mapping
to persistent agent policy is an Organon reconstruction, not an
equation stated by Vasubandhu.
