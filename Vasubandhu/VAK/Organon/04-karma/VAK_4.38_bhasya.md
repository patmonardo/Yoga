# VAK 4.38 Bhāṣya — How Prātimokṣa Restraint Is Lost

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
prātimokṣadamas tyāgaḥ śikṣānikṣepaṇāc cyuteḥ /
ubhayavyañjanotpatter mūlacchedān niśātyayāt // 4.38 //
```

> Prātimokṣa discipline is lost through laying down the training,
> death, the arising of both sexual characteristics, and severance
> of the wholesome roots; the passing of the night also ends the
> one-day observance.

The running unit begins with the transition to abandonment at
[222|18] and ends with the explanation of the five grounds at
[222|26–27] in `Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The
alternative theories introduced at [223|01] belong to 4.39.


Word spacing, punctuation, and the verse-end label are editorial.
The source has `prātimokṣadamatyāgaḥ`; the Kārikā witness separates
`prātimokṣadamas tyāgaḥ`. `Dama` is immediately glossed as
`saṃvara`, so this is the same restraint under discussion.
The list of five in the verse is distributed by the prose: four
general causes, plus one specific to `upavāsa`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Abandonment must now be explained. First: “Prātimokṣa discipline
is lost through laying down the training, death, the arising of
both sexual characteristics, severance of the roots, and the
passing of the night.” It is called `dama` because by it the
faculties are restrained; `saṃvara` is intended.

Prātimokṣa restraint is lost through four causes, leaving aside
the one-day observance: repudiating the training rules, with an
intention to do so, before a person able to understand; departure
from one's present class of life at death; the simultaneous
manifestation of both sexual characteristics; and complete
severance of the wholesome roots. The upavāsa restraint is lost
through these four and also when the night ends. Taken together,
these make five causes of abandonment.

Why do these events cause abandonment? Because, respectively,
there arises a manifestation contrary to the original undertaking;
the living support is relinquished; the support is disrupted;
the causal basis is cut off; or the observance was projected to
last only until that time.

## 4. Five Events, Five Grounds

The passage moves from the **events** of loss to the reasons they
terminate possession of the restraint.

| Event in the Bhāṣya | Ground given for loss |
|---|---|
| Deliberate repudiation before an understanding person | A manifestation contrary to the undertaking |
| Death | Loss of the present support (`āśraya`) |
| Simultaneous appearance of both sexual characteristics | Disruption of the qualifying support |
| Severance of wholesome roots | Cutting off the causal basis (`nidāna`) |
| End of the night, for upavāsa | Expiry of the period for which it was projected |

Laying down the training is an enacted, intelligible repudiation;
the source does not equate a passing wish or a particular breach
with this event. Death and the change in sexual characteristics
are treated under the inherited disciplinary account of what
qualifies as the restraint's bodily support. The latter is a
claim within that account, not a general description of persons
with diverse sex characteristics. Severance of wholesome roots
concerns the restraint's causal ground. Completion of the
upavāsa period is different again: its limited term has simply
run its course.

`Niśātyaya` does not apply to every prātimokṣa restraint. The
Bhāṣya explicitly says four causes operate apart from upavāsa,
and the end of the night adds a fifth only for that temporary
observance. It is therefore misleading to read the Kārikā's
compressed list as five uniform loss conditions for all forms.

## 5. Organon Contact Point

After 4.37's account of acquisition, this verse asks what
maintains or terminates a standing practical form. For our vow,
the useful distinction is among **renouncing an undertaking**,
**losing its support**, **cutting off its sustaining cause**, and
**reaching an intended endpoint**. These are different operations;
one failure in practice does not by itself identify which has
occurred. Our formulation of the Self, adhyāsa, and the Idea of
the True and Good remains an Organon reconstruction, while the
Kośa here analyzes prātimokṣa and upavāsa restraint.
