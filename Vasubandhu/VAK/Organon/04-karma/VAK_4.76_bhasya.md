# VAK 4.76 Bhāṣya — Division, Injury, and Afflicted Discourse

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
paiśunyaṃ kliṣṭacittasya vacanaṃ parabhedane /
pāruṣyamapriyaṃ sarvaṃ kliṣṭaṃ bhinna pralāpitā // 4.76 //
```

> Divisive speech is the utterance of one whose citta is afflicted,
> directed toward dividing others. Harsh speech is disagreeable speech.
> All afflicted speech is disordered discourse.

The verse completes the definitions of the verbal courses. This unit gives
the broad definition of the fourth course. The alternative residual account
begins as the first half of VAK 4.77 and belongs to the next study.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Divisive speech is the utterance of one whose citta is afflicted,
directed toward dividing others.” Speech arising from an afflicted citta
and directed toward dividing others and producing estrangement is divisive
speech. The conditions “one understanding the meaning” and “without
mistake” continue to apply.

“Harsh speech is disagreeable speech.” The expressions “utterance of one
whose citta is afflicted,” “one understanding the meaning,” and “without
mistake” also continue here. Afflicted speech that is disagreeable to
others, whose meaning is understood, and which is addressed to the very
person intended is harsh speech.

“All afflicted speech is disordered discourse.” The governing term
“statement” continues. Every afflicted statement is `saṃbhinnapralāpa`.
This condition is `saṃbhinnapralāpitā`, and through conjunction with it
someone becomes one who engages in disordered discourse.

## 4. Three Determinations of Verbal Action

```text
DIVISIVE SPEECH
    afflicted, understood, correctly targeted speech
    directed toward division and estrangement

HARSH SPEECH
    afflicted, understood, correctly targeted
    disagreeable or painful address

DISORDERED SPEECH — BROAD ACCOUNT
    every afflicted statement
```

Divisive and harsh speech inherit `arthābhijña` and `abhrānti`. The
utterance must become intelligible for the recipient and reach the person
or relation actually intended.

Harsh speech is more determinate than whatever a hearer happens to dislike.
Its disagreeable character belongs to an afflicted semantic act directed
successfully toward the intended recipient.

The final definition is taxonomically broad. False, divisive, and harsh
speech can all also fall under `saṃbhinnapralāpa` insofar as each is
afflicted. The alternative exclusive remainder begins only in VAK 4.77.

## 5. Organon Contact Point

The verbal course is constituted through:

```text
<Root,
 Speaker Determination,
 Statement,
 Recipient,
 Target Relation,
 Intended Effect,
 Uptake>
```

Content alone cannot classify speech. An accurate statement can be directed
toward division; an unwelcome statement need not be harsh speech; and a
meaningful statement can remain afflicted in its governing root.

The broad account introduces overlapping classification:

```text
AfflictedSpeech
    contains FalseSpeech
    contains DivisiveSpeech
    contains HarshSpeech
    and extends beyond them
```

Before executing a classification, the agent must know whether the governing
account allows overlap or requires exclusive remainder classes. This is the
Organon use of the Bhāṣya's broad definition.
