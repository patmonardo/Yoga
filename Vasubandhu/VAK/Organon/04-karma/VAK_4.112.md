# VAK_4.112

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तिष्यस्तोत्रेण वीर्यस्य धीसमाध्योरनन्तरम् ।
पुण्यं क्रियाथ तद्वस्तु त्रयं कर्मपथा यथा ॥ ४.११२ ॥
```

**Source-boundary caution**

The repository kārikā witness joins both half-verses as VAK 4.112. The
electronic Bhāṣya source, however, places the Tiṣya, vigor, concentration, and
wisdom material before the explicit verse marker and marks only the second
half as 4.112. The official study preserves the repository sequence while
registering this segmentation problem rather than silently normalizing it.

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

tiṣyastotreṇa vīryasya dhīsamādhyor anantaram /

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
tiṣya-stotreṇa | vīryasya | dhī-samādhyoḥ | anantaram |
puṇyam | kriyā | atha | tad-vastu | trayam | karma-pathāḥ | yathā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `tiṣya-stotreṇa` | instrumental neuter singular | through the praise of the Buddha Tiṣya |
| `vīryasya` | genitive neuter singular | of the perfection of vigor |
| `dhī-samādhyoḥ` | genitive feminine/masculine dual | of wisdom and concentration |
| `anantaram` | adverb | without interval; resolved as immediately before awakening |
| `puṇyam` | nominative neuter singular | merit, wholesome value or causal efficacy |
| `kriyā` | nominative feminine singular | activity, practical performance |
| `atha` | transitional particle | now, then; opens the merit analysis |
| `tad-vastu` | nominative neuter singular | its basis or determinate practical field |
| `trayam` | nominative neuter singular collective | the triad of giving, discipline, and cultivation |
| `karma-pathāḥ` | nominative masculine plural | paths of karma |
| `yathā` | comparative adverb | just as, analogously to |

**Completion of the perfections**

```text
tiṣya-stotreṇa
    vīryasya [pūraṇam]

bodheḥ pūrvasamanantaram
    vajropama-samādhau
    dhyāna-prajñayoḥ [pūraṇam]
```

The second construction is supplied by the Bhāṣya. `Anantaram` does not mean
that concentration and wisdom were perfected immediately after the Tiṣya
episode.

**Functional analysis of the triad**

```text
tat trayam
    puṇyam api
    kriyā api
    vastu api
    yathāyogam
```

The Bhāṣya's `yathāyogam`, according to the appropriate case, prevents a
simple identity reading of the compound.

## 4. Grammar

**Completion of vigor**

```text
tiṣya-stotreṇa
    vīryasya [paripūraṇam abhūt]
```

> Through the praise of Tiṣya, vigor was perfected.

The instrumental names the act through which completion occurred; the
genitive identifies the perfection completed. The narrative details belong
to the Bhāṣya.

**Completion immediately before awakening**

```text
dhī-samādhyoḥ
    [bodheḥ pūrvam] anantaram
    [paripūraṇam abhūt]
```

> Wisdom and concentration were perfected immediately before awakening.

The genitive dual binds the two perfections together. The commentary locates
their completion in diamond-like concentration at the immediate threshold of
awakening.

**Transition to the three merit-activity bases**

```text
atha
    tat trayam
    puṇyam
    kriyā
    vastu [ca]
    yathāyogam [bhavati]
```

> Now that triad is, according to the case, merit, activity, and basis.

The three predicates do not map one-to-one onto giving, discipline, and
cultivation. A factor can bear one, two, or all three designations according
to its function in a concrete structure.

**Analogy with paths of karma**

```text
karma-pathāḥ yathā
```

> Just as with the paths of karma.

The Bhāṣya explains that some factors are karma and path, while others are
paths through which karma operates. The compound preserves differentiated
relations rather than asserting simple identity of all members.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Through the praise of Tiṣya [came the completion] of vigor; [immediately
> before awakening came the completion] of wisdom and concentration. Now the
> triad is, according to the case, merit, activity, and basis, just as with the
> paths of karma.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Bodhisattva completed vigor by standing on one foot and praising the
> Buddha Tiṣya continuously for seven days with a single verse; this intensity
> is said also to have shortened the career by nine kalpas. Concentration and
> wisdom were not completed immediately afterward, but in diamond-like
> concentration immediately before awakening, when each reached the far shore
> of its own excellence. The analysis then turns to the three bases of
> meritorious activity—giving, discipline, and cultivation. Within each
> concrete structure, factors function as merit, activity, basis, or more than
> one of these, just as the relations gathered under “paths of karma” vary
> according to the case.

The Tiṣya narrative, nine-kalpa contraction, temporal correction, diamond-like
concentration, definition of perfection, scriptural triad, functional role
distribution, and strict volition-centered alternative are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The six perfections are completed as forms of practically unsurpassable
> activity: vigor as uninterrupted self-sustaining effort, concentration as
> collected unity, and wisdom as discrimination perfected at awakening's
> threshold. The subsequent merit analysis refuses to turn these activities
> into stored moral substances. Merit, activity, and basis are relational
> functions within a concrete deed; one factor may bear several functions,
> while wholesome volition supplies the strict practical center.

The rational structure is:

```text
wholesome volition
    → practical determining activity

giving / discipline / cultivation
    → determinate bases of actualization

co-arising factors and manifested deeds
    → receive merit, activity, and basis predicates according to function
```

Meaning belongs to position in the whole, not to a permanently fixed
one-word identity.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Tiṣya-stotra` — praise of the Buddha Tiṣya**

The Bhāṣya describes the Bodhisattva standing on one foot for seven days and
praising Tiṣya, who was absorbed in a fire-element attainment in a jewel cave,
with one continuous verse. The episode is the completion-test of vigor.

**`Vīrya-pāramitā` — perfection of vigor**

Unbroken energetic constancy directed toward a supreme Buddha-field. Its
completion is not mere physical exertion but invariance of wholesome effort
through duration.

**`Vajropama-samādhi` — diamond-like concentration**

The immediate pre-awakening attainment in which concentration and wisdom are
perfected. It is not the state immediately following the Tiṣya praise.

**`Dhī` / `prajñā` — discriminative wisdom**

The Bhāṣya glosses the kārikā's `dhī` through the perfection of `prajñā`.
Wisdom reaches the far shore of its own excellence together with concentration
at the terminal threshold.

**`Samādhi` / `dhyāna` — concentration**

Collected stability of the knowing field. The prose discusses its perfection
as `dhyāna-pāramitā` within diamond-like concentration.

**`Pāramitā` — gone to the far shore of its excellence**

The Bhāṣya explains the name through `svasyāḥ saṃpadaḥ pāragamana`: each
capacity has reached the terminal completeness proper to its own excellence.

**`Puṇyakriyāvastu` — basis of meritorious activity**

A functional compound whose members cannot be translated as three fixed
things. The sūtra gives three structures: giving-made, discipline-made, and
cultivation-made.

**`Dānamaya` — made of giving**

In the giving structure, bodily and verbal karma may bear all three
designations. The initiating volition is merit and activity; co-arising
factors can be merit alone.

**`Śīlamaya` — made of discipline**

In the discipline structure, bodily and verbal karma itself can be merit,
activity, and basis according to the Bhāṣya's analysis.

**`Bhāvanāmaya` — made of cultivation**

The cultivation structure is exemplified by loving-kindness. Loving-kindness
is merit and basis; its associated volition is merit and activity; accompanying
factors receive their predicates according to function.

**`Kuśalacetanā paramārthena puṇyakriyā`**

An alternative strict analysis: in the ultimate doctrinal sense, wholesome
volition is meritorious activity, while giving, discipline, and cultivation
are the bases through which it operates. This is a Bhāṣya-level position, not
the literal wording of the verse.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
P = merit predicate
K = activity predicate
V = basis predicate
x = a factor within a concrete meritorious structure
```

Then the Bhāṣya permits:

```text
P(x)
K(x)
V(x)
P(x) ∧ K(x)
P(x) ∧ V(x)
P(x) ∧ K(x) ∧ V(x)
```

The valid assignment depends on:

```text
RoleOf(x, ConcreteMeritStructure)
```

not on:

```text
FixedDictionaryIdentity(x)
```

**Strict volition-centered analysis**

```text
WholesomeVolition(x)
    → MeritoriousActivityInStrictSense(x)

GivingOrDisciplineOrCultivation(y)
    → BasisFor(x, y)
```

**Three practical structures**

```text
GivingMadeStructure
DisciplineMadeStructure
CultivationMadeStructure
```

Each distributes the predicates differently among volition, bodily/verbal
manifestation, primary field, and accompanying dharmas.

**Analogy with karmapatha**

```text
Some(x): Karma(x) ∧ Path(x)
Some(y): PathFor(Karma, y)

therefore
Compound(Karmapatha)
    preserves variable functional relation
```

The same rule governs `puṇyakriyāvastu`.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.111 established practical perfection through extensive universality and
intensive invariance. VAK 4.112 completes the sixfold agent: outward giving,
disciplined non-retaliation, patient endurance, self-sustaining vigor,
collected concentration, and discriminative wisdom.

The temporal correction is philosophically significant. Vigor reaches
completion in the Tiṣya episode, but concentration and wisdom reach completion
only immediately before awakening. The six perfections form one career, yet
their completions occupy different Bhūmis and cannot be collapsed into one
simultaneous event.

The second half then gives perhaps the clearest local statement of the
project's semantic method. `Puṇya`, `kriyā`, and `vastu` do not name isolated
objects with permanent one-to-one meanings. They are determinations a factor
receives through its role in a concrete practical whole:

```text
factor
    → position in meritorious structure
    → functional relation
    → valid predicate
```

This is a direct grammar for the five-layered Object. Volition, manifest
action, basis, accompanying factors, and causal value can belong to one event
without becoming one undifferentiated item. Jñāna traverses the layers and
assigns each predicate at the level where it is valid.

The alternative statement `kuśalacetanā paramārthena puṇyakriyā` is especially
important for Rational Yoga. Wholesome volition is Practical Reason in its
determining activity; giving, discipline, and cultivation are not inert
containers but the Bhūmis in which that activity becomes actual. This is the
same architecture already seen throughout the Karma chapter:

```text
cetanā as practical Principle
    → determinate basis or Bhūmi
    → bodily, verbal, and mental actualization
    → puṇya as wholesome causal efficacy
```

Samyama as Schema Inference is the comprehension and production of precisely
this organized whole. It does not ask for a dictionary equation such as
“merit equals X”; it determines how the same factor functions as merit,
activity, basis, accompaniment, or manifestation within a specific course.
