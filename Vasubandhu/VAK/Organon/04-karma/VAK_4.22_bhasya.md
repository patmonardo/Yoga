# VAK 4.22 Bhāṣya — Contrary Action Within an Established Standing

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

Word division and punctuation are editorial. The introductory question
reads `vijñaptyā` in the running witness, while the Kārikā, its
explanation, and the final sentence expressly concern `avijñapti`.
The question's reading is preserved above; its discrepancy is not
silently repaired. `Prasāda` is rendered “confidence” here, retaining
its force of clarity and trust rather than treating it as mere assent.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Can someone established in non-restraint ever be endowed with a
wholesome manifestation, or someone established in restraint with an
unwholesome one? If so, when and for how long? He says: “One
established in non-restraint is endowed with wholesome avijñapti, and
one established in restraint with unwholesome avijñapti, for as long
as the force of confidence or affliction continues.”

Through a force of confidence, wholesome avijñapti arises in someone
established in non-restraint while that person performs acts such as
praise or reverence. Through a force of affliction, unwholesome
avijñapti arises in someone established in restraint while that person
performs acts such as killing, binding, or striking. For as long as
those two motivating forces continue, the corresponding two
avijñaptis continue too.

In the first moment, the person is endowed only with present
avijñapti; in later moments, also with past avijñapti. The discussion
of avijñapti is concluded.

## 4. Standing and Contrary Episode

The answer crosses the two classifications without equating them. The
person's established standing (`asaṃvarastha` or `saṃvare sthita`) does
not predetermine the ethical quality of every occasional avijñapti:

| Established standing | Force and example | Contrary avijñapti |
|---|---|---|
| Non-restraint | `prasādavega`; praise or reverence | Wholesome |
| Restraint | `kleśavega`; killing, binding, or striking | Unwholesome |

The Bhāṣya does not say that one wholesome act automatically ends
non-restraint, or that one unwholesome act automatically ends restraint.
Nor does it make either standing morally irrelevant. It identifies a
second, local determination that can arise within a bearer of either
standing. Whether an established standing is abandoned is a further
question, not settled by this sentence alone.

## 5. Force and Duration

`Yāvat ... tāvat` connects the duration of the two avijñaptis with
the continuation of the two forces that generated them. The point is
temporal and causal: the contrary avijñapti does not simply last for
as long as the person's standing status lasts. `Prasāda` and `kleśa`
are not interchangeable sources; they account for wholesome and
unwholesome production respectively.

The last temporal sentence follows the pattern of the preceding
verses. The first moment carries present avijñapti alone; subsequent
moments also carry past avijñapti. The text then expressly closes the
`avijñapti` topic, setting up the new treatment of manifest action in
4.23.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our vow work, 4.22 is a useful adversarial test. A standing
commitment and a present deed must each be determined; neither is a
shortcut to the other. A Pure Reason vow cannot certify every act
merely because its bearer claims the vow. Conversely, a local failure
must be read with its generating conditions and its actual duration,
not as a complete description of the bearer. This is an Organon
application of the Kośa's two-axis account, not Vasubandhu's own
statement of our vow.
