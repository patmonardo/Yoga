# VAK_4.14

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अष्टधा प्रातिमोक्षाख्यो द्रव्यतस्तु चतुर्विधः ।
>
> लिङ्गतो नामसंचारात् पृथक् ते चाविरोधिनः ॥ ४.१४ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> aṣṭadhā prātimokṣākhyo dravyatas tu caturvidhaḥ /
>
> liṅgato nāmasaṃcārāt pṛthak te cāvirodhinaḥ // 4.14 //

VAK 4.13 introduced `prātimokṣa` as one of three kinds of restraint.

VAK 4.14 analyzes its internal differentiation.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
eight disciplinary names
    from
four distinct restraint-types
```

It then asks whether successively acquired lay, novice, and monastic restraints
are merely larger quantities of one restraint. They are not. They arise
separately, yet remain mutually compatible.

**Eight by disciplinary name**

The Bhāṣya enumerates:

```text
1. bhikṣu restraint
2. bhikṣuṇī restraint
3. śikṣamāṇā restraint
4. śrāmaṇera restraint
5. śrāmaṇerī restraint
6. upāsaka restraint
7. upāsikā restraint
8. upavāsa restraint
```

**Four by distinct type**

The four real restraint-types are:

```text
1. bhikṣu restraint
2. śrāmaṇera restraint
3. upāsaka restraint
4. upavāsa restraint
```

The corresponding female disciplinary designations do not add new restraint-
natures to this fourfold classification.

**`Dravya` caution**

`Dravyataḥ` asks how many genuinely distinct restraint-determinations are
recognized within this Abhidharma classification.

It should not be made to carry an imported Aristotelian or modern material
notion of “substance.” The controlled rendering is “as distinct real type.”

**`Liṅga` determined by the Bhāṣya**

The prose glosses `liṅga` with `vyañjana`: the bodily sex-characteristic by
which female and male are distinguished in this disciplinary system.

This is narrower than the full lexical range of `liṅga`, which can otherwise
mean mark, sign, or characteristic.

**Witness caution**

The prose alternates `śramaṇera` and `śrāmaṇera`. The source readings remain
untouched.

The compact second line contains two arguments. The Bhāṣya is required to avoid
attaching `pṛthak ... avirodhinaḥ` to the wrong plurality.

## 4. Grammar

The first line contrasts two standards of enumeration:

```text
prātimokṣa-ākhyaḥ [saṃvaraḥ]
    aṣṭadhā [nāmataḥ]

the restraint called prātimokṣa
    is eightfold by name
```

```text
dravyataḥ tu
    caturvidhaḥ

but as distinct real type
    it is fourfold
```

`Nāmataḥ`, “by name,” is supplied explicitly by the Bhāṣya.

The first half of the second line gives the proof:

```text
liṅgataḥ
    nāma-saṃcārāt

because disciplinary designation transfers
    according to a change of sex-characteristic
```

The prose explains the inference:

```text
change of sex-characteristic
    → change of disciplinary designation

but does not cause
    → abandonment of the former restraint
    → acquisition of a newly produced restraint
```

Therefore the difference of the paired male and female designations is nominal
rather than a new difference of restraint-nature.

The final phrase begins a second determination:

```text
te [uttarottara-saṃvarāḥ]
    pṛthak
    avirodhinaḥ ca

those [successively acquired restraints]
    arise separately
    and are mutually compatible
```

`Te` refers to lay, novice, and monastic restraints acquired in succession—not
to the male and female names just discussed.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The restraint called `prātimokṣa` is eightfold, but as distinct real type it
> is fourfold. Because designation transfers according to the sex-
> characteristic, [the corresponding names do not establish new restraint-
> types]. Those [successively acquired restraints] arise separately and are
> mutually compatible.

The two bracketed referential clarifications are fixed by the Bhāṣya.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> `Prātimokṣa` restraint has eight disciplinary designations but only four
> distinct restraint-types. A change of bodily sex-characteristic transfers
> the corresponding disciplinary designation without causing abandonment of
> the former restraint or acquisition of a newly produced one; the paired male
> and female names therefore do not establish different restraint-natures.
> Lay, novice, and monastic restraints acquired successively, however, arise as
> separate complete determinations because their causal bases differ. They are
> nevertheless compatible and can coexist.

The change-of-characteristic proof, the separate arising of successive
restraints, their `nidāna` difference, and their coexistence are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Discipline must be differentiated according to the kind of difference
> involved. A change of designation need not alter the practical determination
> itself. Conversely, adding further training rules need not be a merely
> quantitative enlargement of one restraint: a wider discipline can arise as
> a new complete determination because it withdraws from a wider field of
> causal occasions for misconduct. Real difference, however, does not imply
> opposition; distinct restraints may remain jointly operative.

The verse therefore separates name, real determination, causal basis, and
compatibility.


## 8. Logical Determination

**Nominal and real classification**

```text
eight names
    → institutional and sex-characteristic differentiation

four real types
    → specifically fixed restraint-determinations
```

The change-of-designation proof is:

```text
if the sex-characteristic changes,
    the disciplinary name changes

but
    no former restraint is abandoned
    no new restraint is acquired

therefore
    name-change alone does not entail restraint-type change
```

**Qualitative distinction versus quantitative increase**

The next question concerns a practitioner who successively undertakes:

```text
lay restraint
    → novice restraint
        → monastic restraint
```

Are these like fifteen and twenty coins—different only in quantity?

The Bhāṣya answers no:

```text
each restraint
    → arises separately
    → has its own characteristic
    → remains unmixed with the others
```

The ground of difference is `nidāna-viśeṣa`:

```text
more training rules undertaken
    → withdrawal from more occasions of heedlessness
    → withdrawal from more causal bases of killing and other misconduct
    → entry into more causal bases of abstention

therefore
    → a distinct restraint arises
```

This is qualitative expansion of the practical field, not simple arithmetic
addition.

**Difference without opposition**

The restraints are distinct but compatible:

```text
higher restraint acquired
    does not entail
abandonment of the earlier restraint
```

The Bhāṣya gives the practical reason. If monastic restraint automatically
cancelled lay restraint, abandoning monastic restraint would leave the person
without even lay status. That consequence is rejected.

Thus:

```text
real difference
    and
simultaneous coexistence

are compatible
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Discipline as differentiated practical fields**

VAK 4.14 does not merely list institutional statuses.

It asks what counts as a real difference in Discipline.

Three tests emerge:

```text
Does only the name change?

Does the causal field of possible misconduct change?

Can the resulting determinations coexist?
```

The first test blocks over-differentiation. The second blocks reduction of
higher discipline to a numerical pile of additional rules. The third blocks
the assumption that difference must mean opposition.

**Contact with Yoga discipline**

The Yoga comparison remains strongest at the level of restraint from causal
occasions of misconduct.

The Bhāṣya does not treat a fuller discipline as merely “more prohibitions.”
Undertaking more rules reorganizes the field:

```text
fewer occasions of heedlessness
fewer causal openings for misconduct
more conditions supporting abstention
```

That resembles the function of yama as a systematic governance of practical
possibility rather than a checklist of disconnected commands.

The present verse still does not supply Yoga's positive `niyama` structure.
Nor should the institutional prātimokṣa classes be translated as Yoga limbs.

**Aspect discrimination**

In Organon Light, the verse distinguishes three Aspects of difference:

```text
Nominal Aspect
    → designation changes while restraint remains

Causal Aspect
    → nidāna-field changes and a distinct restraint arises

Compatibility Aspect
    → distinct restraints remain jointly operative
```

This is precisely the restraint needed in classification: neither multiplying
dharmas from every name nor collapsing genuinely different practical grounds
into one undifferentiated discipline.
