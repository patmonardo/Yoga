# VAK_4.84

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आरूप्यासंज्ञिसत्त्वेषु लाभतः सप्त शेषिते ।
संमुखीभावतश्चापि हित्वा सनरकान् कुरून् ॥ ४.८४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ārūpyāsaṃjñi-sattveṣu lābhataḥ sapta śeṣite /
saṃmukhībhāvataś cāpi hitvā sa-narakān kurūn // 4.84 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ārūpya | asaṃjñi-sattveṣu | lābhataḥ | sapta | śeṣite |
saṃmukhībhāvataḥ | ca | api | hitvā | sa-narakān | kurūn
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ārūpye` | locative neuter singular, metrically `ārūpya` in compound coordination | in the formless domain |
| `asaṃjñi-sattveṣu` | locative masculine plural | among non-percipient beings |
| `lābhataḥ` | ablative used adverbially | by acquisition or continuum-endowment |
| `sapta` | nominative plural numeral | the seven wholesome bodily and verbal courses |
| `śeṣite` | locative singular from `śeṣita` | in what remains; in the remaining domains or destinies |
| `saṃmukhībhāvataḥ` | ablative used adverbially | by becoming presently manifest |
| `ca api` | coordinating and additive particles | also; in addition to acquisition |
| `hitvā` | absolutive of √`hā` | having excluded; excepting |
| `sa-narakān` | accusative masculine plural | those together with hell beings; hell beings included in the exclusion |
| `kurūn` | accusative masculine plural | the Kurus, here the inhabitants of Uttarakuru |

**The seven courses**

The numeral carries forward the wholesome counterparts of the three bodily
and four verbal unwholesome courses:

```text
bodily     abstention from killing, taking what is not given,
           and sexual misconduct

verbal     abstention from false, divisive, harsh,
           and disordered or frivolous afflicted speech
```

The verse therefore concerns the distribution of wholesome bodily and verbal
conduct. The three wholesome mental courses discussed in VAK 4.83 are not
included in this numeral.

## 4. Grammar

**Acquisition without manifestation**

```text
ārūpye asaṃjñi-sattveṣu
    sapta
    lābhataḥ [vidyante]
```

> In the formless domain and among non-percipient beings, the seven exist by
> acquisition.

The locatives delimit two cases in which the seven belong to a continuum but
do not become presently manifest. The ellipsed verb is supplied from the
ongoing distribution inquiry.

**The residual field**

```text
śeṣite [dhātau gatau vā]
    sapta
    saṃmukhībhāvataḥ ca api [vidyante]
```

> In the remaining domain or destiny, the seven also exist by present
> manifestation.

`Śeṣite` is not a name for one further place. It is a residual-scope operator:
after the two acquisition-only cases have been removed, it ranges over the
remaining domains and destinies.

**The exception within the residual field**

```text
hitvā
    sa-narakān
    kurūn
```

> Excepting hell beings and the Kurus.

The absolutive restricts present manifestation, not acquisition as such. The
Bhāṣya identifies the Kurus as the inhabitants of Uttarakuru.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In the formless domain and among non-percipient beings, the seven occur by
> acquisition. In what remains they occur also by present manifestation,
> excepting hell beings and the Kurus.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The seven wholesome bodily and verbal courses belong only by endowment in
> two cases where they cannot be enacted in present bodily or verbal conduct:
> noble beings born in the formless domain remain endowed with past and future
> non-outflow restraint, while non-percipient beings are endowed through
> concentration-born restraint. In the remaining domains and destinies these
> seven can also become presently manifest, with hell beings and the people of
> Uttarakuru excepted. Their manifestation must still be classified by its
> relation to formal restraint: among animals and pretas it is independent of
> formal restraint, in the form domain it is included within restraint, and
> elsewhere either mode is possible.

The identifications of the seven, the qualified formless case, the two kinds
of restraint, and the final threefold distribution are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A wholesome course is not exhausted by a presently observable deed. It may
> belong operatively to a continuum even where the bodily or verbal conditions
> for its current enactment are absent. Conversely, present wholesome
> abstention need not derive from a formally acquired restraint. Domain and
> destiny determine the available mode of the course: endowment without
> manifestation, manifestation within restraint, manifestation outside
> restraint, or both. Ethical determination therefore requires the relation
> among course-form, continuum, mode of presence, and domain—not inference
> from an isolated external non-event.

The source distinction may be represented as:

```text
wholesome course-form
    → continuum-endowment
    → possibility of present manifestation
    → relation to formal restraint
    → domain-and-destiny distribution
```

This relational display is an Organon reconstruction of the Bhāṣya's
distribution; it is not terminology attributed to Vasubandhu.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kuśala karmapatha` — wholesome course of action**

Here the seven bodily and verbal courses are abstentions opposed to the seven
unwholesome bodily and verbal courses. The term specifies ethically determined
conduct, not mere physical inactivity.

**`Lābha` — acquisition or endowment**

A continuum-level possession relation. A course can belong to a continuum by
acquisition although it is not presently manifest. `Lābha` must therefore not
be translated as the occurrence of a current act.

**`Saṃmukhībhāva` — present manifestation**

The event-level actualization of a course in present conduct. Its addition by
`ca api` marks a mode distinct from, though compatible with, acquisition.

**`Ārūpya-dhātu` — formless domain**

The Bhāṣya's claim is qualified: noble beings born there remain endowed with
non-outflow restraint generated in dependence on appropriate levels. It does
not assert indiscriminately that every being in the formless domain possesses
the seven.

**`Anāsrava-saṃvara` — non-outflow restraint**

Restraint belonging to the noble path. The Bhāṣya explains that a noble being
in the formless domain may remain endowed with past restraint that was
generated and ceased, and with future restraint supported by five levels.
This temporal endowment accounts for the seven there without present bodily
or verbal manifestation.

**`Asaṃjñi-sattva` — non-percipient being**

A being in the non-percipient attainment of rebirth, not a general expression
for unconscious matter. The seven belong here through concentration-born
restraint rather than current bodily or verbal enactment.

**`Dhyāna-saṃvara` — concentration-born restraint**

Restraint arising with meditative concentration. It supplies the endowment of
the seven among non-percipient beings.

**`Saṃvara-saṃgṛhīta` — included within restraint**

Wholesome conduct formally comprised by restraint. The Bhāṣya assigns this
mode exclusively to the seven as they occur in the form domain.

**`Saṃvara-nirmukta` — independent of formal restraint**

Wholesome conduct not included in an acquired restraint. The seven occur only
in this mode among animals and pretas. Thus wholesome abstention and formal
restraint are not coextensive.

**`Śeṣita` — the remaining field**

A relational remainder after specified cases have been excluded. Its meaning
depends on the preceding partition of domains and destinies rather than on an
independent lexical referent.

**`Naraka` and `Uttarakuru` — exceptions to manifestation**

Hell beings and the inhabitants of Uttarakuru are excluded here from present
manifestation of these seven wholesome courses. The exclusion does not license
the claim that mere absence of a corresponding violation is itself a presently
manifest wholesome course.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Four questions must remain distinct**

```text
1. Which course-type is under discussion?
   the seven wholesome bodily and verbal courses

2. Does the course belong to the continuum?
   acquisition / endowment

3. Is the course presently operative?
   present manifestation

4. Is its operation included in formal restraint?
   restraint-included / restraint-independent
```

Collapsing any two of these produces a false distribution.

**Distribution table**

| Location or class | Endowment | Present manifestation | Relation to restraint |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| qualified noble beings in the formless domain | yes | no | non-outflow restraint |
| non-percipient beings | yes | no | concentration-born restraint |
| hell beings | not denied by this line | no | not classified here |
| inhabitants of Uttarakuru | not denied by this line | no | not classified here |
| animals and pretas where manifested | yes | yes | restraint-independent |
| form domain where manifested | yes | yes | restraint-included |
| other remaining cases | yes | yes | either mode is possible |

The cautious wording in the exception rows matters: `hitvā` restricts the
claim of present manifestation. It does not by itself settle every possible
endowment relation.

**Non-equivalences**

```text
endowed                         ≠ presently manifest
wholesome abstention            ≠ formal restraint in every case
absence of present violation    ≠ present wholesome course
domain membership               ≠ one uniform mode of ethical presence
```

**Modal partition**

```text
seven wholesome bodily-verbal courses
├── acquisition only
│   ├── formless noble continuum: non-outflow restraint
│   └── non-percipient continuum: concentration-born restraint
└── acquisition plus possible manifestation
    ├── hell and Uttarakuru: manifestation excluded
    ├── animals and pretas: outside formal restraint
    ├── form domain: within formal restraint
    └── other remaining cases: either relation to restraint
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.84 prevents a behaviorist reduction of wholesome karma. The verse first
separates belonging from occurring: a restraint can remain an operative
determination of a continuum even when the organs and situation required for
bodily or verbal enactment are unavailable. The Bhāṣya then prevents the
opposite reduction, according to which every wholesome manifestation would
have to be the exercise of a formal vow or restraint. Animals and pretas can
manifest the seven independently of such restraint, while the form domain
admits them only as restraint-included.

The resulting system has four logical levels: the universal specification of
the seven, their acquisition by a continuum, their possible actualization,
and the normative registration of that actualization within or outside formal
restraint. “Wholesome” names the ethical determination of the course;
`saṃvara` names one structured mode in which that determination can belong and
operate. Their distinction should be developed, not erased.

For the Organon, this supports a restrained analogy with capacity and
actualization. `Lābha` is not a hidden substance or stored object, but a
relational determination of a continuum; `saṃmukhībhāva` is its presently
operative occurrence. This is a project-level logical articulation and must
not be back-projected into the Sanskrit as Vasubandhu's explicit vocabulary.
