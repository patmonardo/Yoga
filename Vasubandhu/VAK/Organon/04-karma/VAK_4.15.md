# VAK_4.15

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> पञ्चाष्टदशसर्वेभ्यो वर्ज्येभ्यो विरतिग्रहात् ।
>
> उपासकोपवासस्थश्रमणोद्देशभिक्षुता ॥ ४.१५ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pañcāṣṭadaśasarvebhyo varjyebhyo viratigrahāt /
>
> upāsakopavāsasthaśramaṇoddeśabhikṣutā // 4.15 //

VAK 4.14 stated that `prātimokṣa` restraint is eightfold by disciplinary name
but fourfold as distinct restraint-type.

VAK 4.15 identifies those four types through the scope of abstention
undertaken:

```text
five
eight
ten
all
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the contents of each field and makes `virati-samādāna`—the
undertaking of abstention—the operative criterion.

**The numerical compound**

```text
pañca + aṣṭa + daśa + sarvebhyaḥ
```

Each number is construed with `varjyebhyaḥ`:

```text
from five things to be avoided
from eight things to be avoided
from ten things to be avoided
from all things to be avoided
```

**`Virati-graha` and `virati-samādāna`**

The kārikā says `virati-graha`, taking up abstention.

The Bhāṣya repeatedly explains it with:

```text
virati-samādāna
    undertaking or acceptance of abstention
```

The restraint is therefore not inferred from accidental non-performance.

**Witness caution**

The prose witness reads `pañccabhyo`, an evident e-text irregularity for the
fivefold construction. It remains preserved in the research witness.

The status terms are retained rather than replaced by approximate modern
institutional equivalents.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The governing ablative is causal:

```text
virati-grahāt
    through undertaking abstention
```

The field is expressed by the ablative plurals:

```text
pañca / aṣṭa / daśa / sarvebhyaḥ
    varjyebhyaḥ

from five / eight / ten / all
    prohibited fields
```

The second line gives the corresponding status in the same order. The Bhāṣya
explicitly instructs that the enumeration is respective: `yathāsaṃkhyam`.

```text
fivefold undertaking
    → upāsaka restraint

eightfold undertaking
    → upavāsa observance

tenfold undertaking
    → śramaṇoddeśa / novice restraint

complete undertaking
    → bhikṣu restraint
```

The final member is expanded by the Bhāṣya:

```text
sarvebhyaḥ varjanīyebhyaḥ
    kāya-vāk-karmabhyaḥ

from all prohibited
    bodily and verbal actions
```

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Through undertaking abstention from five, eight, ten, or all prohibited
> actions, there is respectively the state of an `upāsaka`, one established in
> the `upavāsa`, a novice, or a `bhikṣu`.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> One becomes established in lay restraint by undertaking abstention from five
> prohibited fields; in the `upavāsa` observance by undertaking eight; in
> novice restraint by undertaking ten; and in `bhikṣu` restraint by
> undertaking abstention from the complete field of prohibited bodily and
> verbal actions. These are the four distinct forms of `prātimokṣa` restraint.

The respective correspondence, the enumerated contents, and the specification
of complete restraint as covering all prohibited bodily and verbal karma are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Disciplinary status is constituted by an undertaken relation to a specified
> field of possible action. The field may contain five, eight, ten, or the
> complete body of prohibitions. Mere omission does not establish restraint;
> the practitioner actively takes up abstention. As the field is expanded and
> internally rearticulated, a distinct practical determination arises.

The status-name expresses the restraint. It does not replace the undertaken
practical relation that constitutes it.

**The enumerated fields**

The five lay restraints are:

```text
1. abstention from killing
2. abstention from taking what is not given
3. abstention from sexual misconduct
4. abstention from false speech
5. abstention from intoxicating drink
```

The eightfold `upavāsa` observance replaces sexual misconduct with complete
celibacy and adds:

```text
adornment and entertainment, counted together
high or luxurious beds
untimely eating
```

The novice ten separate adornment from entertainment and add abstention from
accepting gold and silver.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Undertaking, not mere omission**

```text
person x does not presently perform prohibited action y

does not entail

x possesses prātimokṣa restraint concerning y
```

The sufficient structure requires:

```text
specified prohibited field
    + undertaken abstention over that field
        → corresponding restraint-status
```

**The four scopes**

| Scope | Restraint-status | Structural determination |
|---|---|---|
| five | `upāsaka` | basic lay field |
| eight | `upavāsastha` | expanded temporary observance |
| ten | novice | expanded and more finely articulated field |
| all | `bhikṣu` | complete bodily/verbal disciplinary extension |

**Not merely a numerical ladder**

The progression is internally heterogeneous:

```text
5 → 8
    expansion of the prohibited field

8 → 10
    further prohibition
    + separate counting of material previously grouped together

10 → all
    transition from finite enumeration
    to complete disciplinary scope
```

Therefore number records the articulation of discipline, but number alone does
not explain the distinct restraint-type.

**Relation to `avirodhinaḥ`**

VAK 4.14 established that successively acquired restraints are distinct yet
compatible.

`Avirodhinaḥ` does not mean that a later vow performs an operation of removing
logical contradictions from an earlier vow.

It means:

```text
lay restraint
novice restraint
monastic restraint
    → may coexist

acquiring the higher
    → does not cancel the earlier
```

At the Organon level, this compatibility permits the vows to form a coherent
practical system. That is the result of `avirodha`, not its literal grammatical
operation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Discipline as an undertaken field**

The verse gives Discipline a precise structure:

```text
possible bodily and verbal actions
    → prohibited field selected or completed
        → abstention undertaken
            → restraint-status constituted
```

The person is not defined merely by a social label. Nor is restraint reduced to
an inner intention without objective scope.

Discipline joins:

```text
subjective undertaking
    with
objective field of possible action
```

**Yama contact and difference**

The fivefold lay restraint has a strong structural relation to Yoga's yamas:

```text
non-killing
non-stealing
sexual discipline
truthful speech
```

But the lists are not identical. The Buddhist fifth is abstention from
intoxicants, whereas Yoga's classical fifth yama is non-grasping.

The eightfold and tenfold systems also add renunciatory disciplines concerning
food, adornment, entertainment, beds, and wealth. These should not be forced
into the Yoga `niyamas`, whose organization and positive-observance character
are different.

The safe comparison is:

```text
both systems
    → constitute disciplined practical fields

but
    → enumerate and ground those fields differently
```

**Compatible differentiation**

VAK 4.14–4.15 together show that fuller discipline is neither simple
replacement nor mere accumulation.

```text
new restraint
    → distinct because its causal and prohibited field differs

earlier restraint
    → remains operative because the determinations are compatible
```

In Organon Light, the higher discipline integrates rather than contradicts the
earlier determination. But the textual claim remains the coexistence of
distinct restraints.
