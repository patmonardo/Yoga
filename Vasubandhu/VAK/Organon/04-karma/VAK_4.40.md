# VAK_4.40

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
भूमिसंचारहानिभ्यां ध्यानाप्तं त्यज्यते शुभम् ।
तथारूप्याप्तमार्यं तु फलाप्त्युत्पत्तिहानिभिः ॥ ४.४० ॥
```

The kārikā witness transmits `फलाप्त्युत्पत्तिहानिभिः`, suggesting
`utpatti`. The Bhāṣya anchor and its prose explanation instead establish
`phalāptyuttaptihānibhiḥ`, with `uttapti` explained as strengthening of the
faculties (`indriyottāpana`). The witness is preserved above; the
Bhāṣya-supported reading governs the translation.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
bhūmi-saṃcāra-hānibhyām
dhyāna-āptam
tyajyate
śubham

tathā
ārūpya-āptam

āryam
tu

phala-āpti-uttapti-hānibhiḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `bhūmisaṃcārahānibhyām` | ablative dual compound | through transition of ground and through falling away |
| `dhyānāptam` | nominative neuter singular compound | acquired through dhyāna |
| `tyajyate` | third-person singular present passive from `√tyaj` | is relinquished or lost |
| `śubham` | nominative neuter singular | wholesome determination |
| `tathā` | indeclinable | likewise; in the same manner |
| `ārūpyāptam` | nominative neuter singular compound | acquired through a formless attainment |
| `āryam` | nominative neuter singular | noble; uncontaminated Path-dharma |
| `phalāpti` | compound member | attainment of a fruit |
| `uttapti` | compound member | strengthening or sharpening |
| `hāni` | compound member | falling away, regression, loss |

**`bhūmisaṃcāra`**

`Bhūmi` is a determinate ground or level. `Saṃcāra` is transition from one
ground to another, specified by the Bhāṣya as including rebirth upward into
another level.

The former dharma is relinquished because the continuum no longer occupies
the ground from which that dharma was acquired.

**`hāni` and `parihāṇi`**

The Bhāṣya glosses loss as falling away from an attainment. Unlike transition
to another ground, this is regression rather than supersession.

**`dhyānāpta śubha`**

The expression includes all wholesome dharmas acquired through dhyāna, not
only restraint considered in isolation. The discussion is prompted by the
question of dhyāna and uncontaminated restraints but answers through their
larger classes of wholesome dharmas.

**`ārūpyāpta`**

Wholesome dharmas belonging to the formless attainments follow the same rule
as those belonging to the Form Realm: transition of ground or falling away.

**`uttapti`**

The Bhāṣya’s prose is decisive:

```text
indriya-uttāpana
    strengthening, sharpening, or intensifying the faculties
```

Through this development, a Path belonging to weaker faculties is
relinquished.

## 4. Grammar

**Dhyāna-acquired wholesome dharmas**

```text
dhyāna-āptam śubham
bhūmi-saṃcāra-hānibhyām
tyajyate
```

> Wholesome determination acquired through dhyāna is relinquished through
> transition of ground or falling away.

The ablative dual gives exactly two causes. `Dhyānāptam` and `śubham` agree as
neuter singular expressions naming the class collectively.

**Formless-acquired wholesome dharmas**

```text
tathā ārūpya-āptam [śubham tyajyate]
```

> Wholesome determination acquired through the formless attainments is
> relinquished in the same manner.

`Tathā` carries forward the two preceding causes and the verbal construction.

**Noble wholesome dharmas**

```text
āryam tu [śubham tyajyate]
phala-āpti-uttapti-hānibhiḥ
```

> Noble wholesome determination, however, is relinquished through attainment
> of a fruit, strengthening [of the faculties], or falling away.

The instrumental plural gives three causes. `Tu` marks the difference between
the lifecycle of noble Path-dharmas and ground-bound meditative wholesome
dharmas.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Wholesome determination acquired through dhyāna is relinquished through
> transition of ground or falling away; that acquired through the formless
> attainments is relinquished likewise. Noble determination, however, is
> relinquished through attainment of a fruit, strengthening [of the
> faculties], or falling away.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Wholesome dharmas acquired through dhyāna are lost when the practitioner
> passes to another ground or falls away from the attainment; wholesome
> dharmas belonging to the formless attainments are lost in the same two ways.
> Uncontaminated noble dharmas have three modes of relinquishment: attainment
> of a fruit relinquishes the preceding Path, strengthening the faculties
> relinquishes the Path associated with weaker faculties, and regression
> relinquishes a higher Path or, where possible, a fruit or an attainment
> distinguished by that fruit.

The specific dharmas relinquished under each noble cause are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A determination may cease because its sustaining ground has been left, its
> attainment has collapsed, its function has successfully produced its fruit,
> or its subjective capacity has been strengthened beyond the form appropriate
> to weaker faculties. Relinquishment therefore names both regression and
> development. In the developmental cases the earlier Path is not simply
> erased: it ceases as the presently possessed operative form because its work
> is fulfilled or a stronger form now determines the continuum.

This distinction must not be used to redescribe every loss as progress.
`Parihāṇi` remains genuine falling away.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Ground-dependent wholesome dharmas**

```text
Form-Realm wholesome dharmas
    depend on a dhyāna ground

formless wholesome dharmas
    depend on an ārūpya ground
```

Their possession changes when the continuum moves between grounds or loses
the relevant attainment.

**Transition through rebirth**

The Bhāṣya identifies rebirth upward as one form of `bhūmisaṃcāra`. The
transition is not necessarily a present-session meditative event; it may be a
change in the realm or level of rebirth supporting the wholesome dharmas.

Some dharmas also cease through loss of membership in the relevant class of
beings (`nikāyasabhāgatva`).

**Fruit attainment**

```text
preceding Path
    performs its function
    → fruit attained
    → preceding Path relinquished
```

This is cessation by fulfillment. The result does not refute the Path; it is
the Path’s accomplished end.

**Strengthening the faculties**

```text
Path of weak faculties
    + faculties strengthened
    → stronger-faculty determination
    → weaker Path relinquished
```

The Bhāṣya speaks of `mṛdvindriyamārga`, the Path belonging to weak faculties.
Its relinquishment is a qualitative upgrade, not regression.

**Falling away from the noble state**

Through `parihāṇi`, a higher Path may be relinquished. The Bhāṣya also names a
fruit or an attainment distinguished by fruit where such regression is
doctrinally possible.

```text
uttara mārga
phala
phalaviśiṣṭa
    → possible objects of loss through regression
```

**Four logical modes under `tyāga`**

```text
ground-transition
    former ground left

falling-away
    attainment deteriorates

fruit-attainment
    Path-function fulfilled

faculty-strengthening
    former capacity-form superseded
```

The shared predicate “relinquished” does not make their internal logic
identical.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Lifecycle matrix**

| Dharma class | Cause | Logical form |
|---|---|---|
| Dhyāna-acquired wholesome | ground-transition | change of supporting level |
| Dhyāna-acquired wholesome | falling-away | regression from attainment |
| Formless-acquired wholesome | same two causes | change or regression |
| Noble wholesome | fruit-attainment | fulfillment |
| Noble wholesome | faculty-strengthening | qualitative supersession |
| Noble wholesome | falling-away | regression |

**Destructive loss and determinate supersession**

```text
parihāṇi
    prior determination lost by regression

phalaprāpti
    prior Path relinquished through fulfilled function

indriyottāpana
    weaker Path relinquished through stronger capacity
```

The result “no longer possessed in the former mode” is common; the process
producing it differs.

**Ground indexed determination**

```text
dharma D belongs to ground G

if G is left or lost
    D is relinquished
```

The dharma is not restraint in abstraction but a wholesome determination
acquired through this ground.

**Path indexed determination**

```text
Path P leads to fruit F

when F is attained
    P ceases as preceding operative Path
```

The Path’s temporal limit is internal to its function.

**Textual correction controls conceptual result**

Reading `utpatti`, “arising,” would leave the middle cause vague. Reading
`uttapti` through `indriyottāpana` gives a precise transition from
weaker-faculty to stronger-faculty Path. The philosophical structure depends
on the philological correction.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A form may cease because it succeeds**

VAK 4.40 makes `tyāga` richer than simple destruction:

```text
Path arises
    → performs its function
    → fruit is attained
    → prior Path is relinquished
```

The disappearance of the means at the attained end is not failure. The means
persists in its truth as accomplished result, although it is no longer the
operative stage.

**Qualitative strengthening**

Faculty-strengthening supplies a second developmental negation:

```text
weaker capacity-form
    → transformation of faculties
    → stronger capacity-form
```

The former Path is not externally expelled. The continuum has become
inadequately described by its earlier weak-faculty determination.

**Regression remains real**

The same verse refuses a romantic dialectic in which every negation is
progress. `Hāni` names actual falling away:

```text
higher Path or attainment possessed
    → supporting capacity fails
    → possession lost
```

Developmental supersession and regression must remain separately typed.

**The ground gives the mode of cessation**

Prātimokṣa, meditative, and noble restraints end differently because their
grounds differ:

```text
undertaken disciplinary status
    → institutional, bodily, causal, and temporal losses

meditative ground
    → transition or falling away

noble Path-stage
    → fruit, faculty-strengthening, or falling away
```

This is not an external taxonomy. The cause of cessation expresses the kind
of determination that was acquired.

This is the Organon contact. The two meditative causes, three noble causes,
and the `uttapti` correction belong explicitly to the Bhāṣya.
