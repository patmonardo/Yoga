# VAK_4.116

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
वर्णादिसम्पदा वस्तु सुरूपत्वं यशस्वि वा ।
प्रियता सुकुमारर्तुसुखस्पर्शाङ्गता ततः ॥ ४.११६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
varṇādisampadā vastu surūpatvaṃ yaśasvi vā |
priyatā sukumārartusukhasparśāṅgatā tataḥ || 4.116 ||
```

The transmitted electronic reading `yaśasvi vā` is retained in the witness.
The Bhāṣya construes the intended result as `yaśasvī bhavati`, “one becomes
renowned.”

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
varṇa-ādi-sampadā | vastu |
su-rūpatvam | yaśasvi | vā | priyatā |
su-kumāra-aṅgatā | ṛtu-sukha-sparśa-aṅgatā | tataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `varṇa-ādi-sampadā` | instrumental singular feminine compound | through excellence of color and the other sensible qualities |
| `vastu` | nominative singular neuter | the gift-object |
| `surūpatvam` | nominative singular neuter abstract noun | beauty of form |
| `yaśasvi` | transmitted form; Bhāṣya construes `yaśasvī` | renowned, possessing fame |
| `priyatā` | nominative singular feminine abstract noun | belovedness, agreeableness |
| `sukumārāṅgatā` | nominative singular feminine compound | possession of delicate limbs |
| `ṛtusukhasparśāṅgatā` | nominative singular feminine compound | possession of limbs pleasant to touch according to the season |
| `tataḥ` | ablatival adverb | from that, consequently |

**Bhāṣya specification of `varṇādi`**

```text
varṇa   color or appearance
gandha  smell or fragrance
rasa    taste
sparśa  touch
```

**Ordered correspondence**

```text
excellent color → beauty
excellent smell → renown
excellent taste → belovedness
excellent touch → delicate limbs
                + seasonally pleasant bodily touch
```

The four sensible qualities and their ordered pairing with the fruits are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 4. Grammar

**Continuation of the qualification from VAK 4.114**

```text
vastu
    varṇa-ādi-sampadā
    viśiṣṭam [bhavati]
```

> The gift-object is distinguished through excellence of color and the other
> sensible qualities.

The understood `viśiṣṭam` is explicitly carried forward by the Bhāṣya. The
instrumental `sampadā` states that through which the gift-object is excellent.

**Transition from qualified object to maturation**

```text
tataḥ
    surūpatvam
    yaśasvitā
    priyatā
    sukumārāṅgatā
    ṛtusukhasparśāṅgatā [bhavati]
```

> From that arise beauty, renown, belovedness, delicate limbs, and limbs
> pleasant to touch according to the seasons.

The Bhāṣya's `yathākramam`, “in order,” governs the correspondence. The final
member is not a fifth sensible cause: excellent touch has two related bodily
fruits.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The gift-object is distinguished through excellence of color and the other
> sensible qualities. From that come beauty, renown, belovedness, delicate
> limbs, and limbs whose touch is pleasant according to the seasons.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A gift-object is distinguished when it is excellent in even one of color,
> smell, taste, or touch. Their maturation follows in order: giving something
> excellent in color yields beauty; excellence of fragrance yields renown;
> excellence of taste yields being beloved; and excellence of touch yields
> both delicate limbs and a bodily touch pleasant under the conditions of the
> different seasons.

The fourfold analysis, ordered correspondences, and the two fruits of touch
are stated by the Bhāṣya rather than independently enumerated by the Kārikā.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Intention becomes effective for another only through a determinately formed
> gift. The sensible quality of this objective middle therefore belongs to the
> specification of the act itself. In maturation, karma preserves neither the
> bare identity nor merely the quantity of the thing transferred, but the form
> of benefit made available through it: excellence of appearance, diffusion,
> agreeableness, and tactile fitness return as correspondingly articulated
> modes of embodiment and social presence.

The rational movement is:

```text
wholesome intention
    → sensibly qualified gift-object
    → determinate benefit for the recipient
    → formally corresponding maturation
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vastu-viśeṣa` — distinction of the gift-object**

The second of the three excellence-determinants announced in VAK 4.114. The
object is not an inert carrier of intention; its actual fitness to benefit the
recipient helps determine the giving-event.

**`Varṇādisampad` — excellence of color and the other qualities**

The Bhāṣya expands `ādi` as smell, taste, and touch. Excellence in even one of
these makes the object distinguished in the relevant respect.

**`Surūpatva` — beauty of form**

The maturation corresponding to giving an object excellent in color or
appearance.

**`Yaśasvitā` — renown**

The maturation corresponding to fragrant gifts. The commentary explains the
analogy: reputation spreads in the directions as fragrance does.

**`Priyatā` — belovedness or agreeableness**

The maturation corresponding to excellence of taste. As good flavor is
agreeable, the giver matures as agreeable or beloved.

**`Sukumārāṅgatā` — possession of delicate limbs**

The first of two bodily maturations corresponding to excellent touch.

**`Ṛtusukhasparśāṅgatā` — seasonally pleasant bodily touch**

Possession of limbs whose tactile condition is pleasant according to seasonal
circumstances. The Bhāṣya compares this with the bodily quality attributed to
the jewel-woman (`strīratna`).

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
O = gift-object
Q1 = excellence of color
Q2 = excellence of smell
Q3 = excellence of taste
Q4 = excellence of touch
F1 ... F5 = specifically qualified fruits
```

Then:

```text
Possesses(O, any-of {Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4})
    → DistinguishedGiftObject(O)
```

And, under the Bhāṣya's explicit ordered rule:

```text
Q1 → F1 = BeautyOfForm
Q2 → F2 = Renown
Q3 → F3 = Belovedness
Q4 → F4 = DelicateLimbs
Q4 → F5 = SeasonallyPleasantTouch
```

This is a four-to-five mapping because touch has two effects. It must not be
flattened into either five independent causes or one undifferentiated reward.

**Analogical preservation of form**

```text
fragrance diffuses through space
    → reputation diffuses socially

pleasant taste is agreeable
    → the person becomes agreeable
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.115 determined the subjective side of excellent giving: qualities of
the giver become modes of action. VAK 4.116 now determines its objective
middle. A good will that selects an unsuitable or defective object has not yet
made its purpose adequately actual for the recipient.

The transition is therefore:

```text
formation of giver
    → formation of deed
    → formation of gift-object
    → recipient's determinate sensible benefit
```

This gives the Kriyā schema objective content. The relevant appearance is not
a random datum but the formed appearance of the gift within the practical
Bhūmi. Samyama on giving must therefore discriminate the object's actual
qualities and their fitness for the recipient; the principle becomes Jñāna
only through this articulated field of application.

The Organon claim is comparative and reconstructive. The source's narrower
doctrinal point is the Bhāṣya's ordered karmic correspondence between sensible
qualities bestowed and qualities later matured.
