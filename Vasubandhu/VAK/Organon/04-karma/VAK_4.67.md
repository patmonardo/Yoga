# VAK_4.67

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अशुभाः षडविज्ञप्तिर्द्विधैकस्तेऽपि कुर्वतः ।
द्विविधाः सप्त कुशलाः अविज्ञप्तिः समाधिजाः ॥ ४.६७ ॥
```


| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `aśubhāḥ` | nominative masculine plural | unwholesome courses |
| `ṣaṭ` | numeral | six |
| `avijñaptiḥ` | nominative feminine singular, predicative and collective | non-manifest karmic determination |
| `dvidhā` | indeclinable | in two modes |
| `ekaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | one course |
| `te` | nominative masculine plural pronoun | those six |
| `api` | particle | also; in turn |
| `kurvataḥ` | genitive singular present participle of `√kṛ` | for one personally doing them |
| `dvividhāḥ` | nominative masculine plural | of two kinds; twofold |
| `sapta` | numeral | seven |
| `kuśalāḥ` | nominative masculine plural | wholesome courses |
| `samādhi-jāḥ` | nominative masculine plural compound | born from concentration |

**The classified field**

The numerals concern the seven bodily and verbal courses on each ethical
side:

```text
three bodily courses
    +
four verbal courses
    =
seven rūpin karmapathas
```

The three mental courses do not enter this classification by bodily or verbal
`vijñapti` and `avijñapti`.

**The six and the one**

The Bhāṣya identifies the six as killing, taking what is not given, false
speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, and disordered speech. Sexual misconduct
is the one (`ekaḥ`) distinguished from them.

## 4. Grammar

**The unwholesome distribution**

```text
aśubhāḥ ṣaṭ
    avijñaptiḥ [bhavanti]

ekaḥ
    dvidhā [bhavati]
```

> Six unwholesome courses are non-manifest; one is twofold.

The feminine singular `avijñaptiḥ` names the mode predicated collectively of
the six masculine courses. `Dvidhā` contrasts the two modes `vijñapti` and
`avijñapti`.

**Personal performance**

```text
te api
    kurvataḥ
    [dvidhā bhavanti]
```

> Those too are twofold for one who performs them personally.

The omitted predicate is recovered from `dvidhā`. The genitive participle
specifies the case of the personal performer, in contrast to one who causes
another to carry out the act.

**The wholesome distribution**

```text
sapta kuśalāḥ
    dvividhāḥ [bhavanti]

samādhijāḥ
    avijñaptiḥ [bhavanti]
```

> The seven wholesome courses are twofold; those born from concentration are
> non-manifest.

The Bhāṣya restricts the first statement to undertaken discipline and the
second to restraint born from meditative concentration.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Six unwholesome courses are non-manifest; one is twofold. Those too are
> twofold for one who performs them. The seven wholesome courses are twofold;
> those born from concentration are non-manifest.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Of the seven unwholesome bodily and verbal courses, six can reach principal
> completion for an instigator as non-manifest karmic determination, since
> another may execute the visible act. Sexual misconduct, whose completion is
> necessarily personal, is both manifest action and non-manifest
> determination. When one personally performs the other six, they too can be
> twofold, although delayed completion may leave only non-manifest
> determination at the principal moment. The seven wholesome courses grounded
> in undertaken discipline possess both modes; restraint born from meditative
> concentration is non-manifest alone because it depends upon mind alone.

The identities of the six, the delegated-action rationale, the temporal
qualification, and the contrast between undertaken and concentration-born
restraint are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A karmic course is not exhausted by the action that becomes outwardly
> visible. Its agent of execution, bearer of responsibility, and moment of
> completion need not coincide. The course may therefore attain effective
> ethical determination where the responsible continuum has no principal
> manifestation of its own. Conversely, wholesome restraint can be generated
> through an explicit undertaking or arise from concentrated consciousness;
> its mode of existence follows its mode of generation.

This is still a classification of karmic modes, not a claim that inward
intention by itself is `avijñapti`.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vijñapti` — manifest action**

The bodily or verbal action insofar as it makes the practical determination
outwardly evident. Here the decisive expression is the Bhāṣya's
`maula-vijñapti`, the manifest component belonging to the principal completed
course.

**`Avijñapti` — non-manifest karmic determination**

The karmically effective form that is not itself an outwardly manifest action.
It must not be reduced to an unexpressed thought: in this passage it accounts
for delegated completion, completion after the visible act has ceased, and
restraint generated by concentration.

**The six delegable courses**

```text
prāṇātipāta          killing
adattādāna           taking what is not given
mṛṣāvāda             false speech
paiśunya             divisive speech
pāruṣya              harsh speech
saṃbhinnapralāpa     disordered speech
```

According to the Bhāṣya, an instigator may have another person complete any of
these. The instigator then lacks a principal manifest act of the relevant kind
but acquires the non-manifest determination.

**`Kāmamithyācāra` — sexual misconduct**

The exceptional seventh course. The Bhāṣya says its defining completion must
be personal; hence it necessarily contains both manifestation and
non-manifest determination.

**`Niṣṭhāpana` — bringing to completion**

Bhāṣya vocabulary for fulfillment of the conditions that constitute the
principal karmic course. It distinguishes completion from preparation and
from mere visible movement.

**`Samādānaśīla` — undertaken discipline**

Restraint assumed through an explicit undertaking. Its dependence on
`vijñapti` explains why its seven wholesome courses possess both manifest and
non-manifest modes.

**`Samādhija` — born from concentration**

The Bhāṣya includes restraint associated with dhyāna and uncontaminated
restraint. Because this discipline depends upon mind alone
(`cittamātrādhīna`), it is `avijñapti` alone.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Content and mode**

```text
ethical content
    wholesome / unwholesome course

mode of karmic existence
    vijñapti / avijñapti
```

These are independent dimensions of classification. Identifying which course
occurred does not yet determine the mode in which it became karmically
effective.

**Three terms that must not be collapsed**

```text
visible executor
karmically responsible continuum
moment of principal completion
```

Delegation separates executor from responsible continuum:

```text
instigator commands
    → another performs the manifest act
    → course completes for the instigator as avijñapti
```

Delayed effect separates visible action from completion:

```text
manifest blow now
    → death later
    → principal completion after that manifestation has ceased
```

The killing example and its timing are Bhāṣya determinations. The following
verse will articulate preparation, principal completion, and aftermath.

**Generation determines modality**

```text
explicit undertaking
    → vijñapti + avijñapti

concentration dependent on mind alone
    → avijñapti
```

The contrast is genetic. The common wholesome content does not erase the
difference between the ways restraint is acquired.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Moral efficacy exceeds appearance**

VAK 4.67 makes visible behavior insufficient as the complete measure of
karmic determination. This does not make appearance irrelevant: `vijñapti`
remains a real mode of bodily and verbal karma. It means that the systematic
relation among agency, execution, completion, and retained discipline cannot
be reconstructed from visible motion alone.

**Objectivity without a detached object**

The karmic course becomes objectively effective through a relational
structure:

```text
volitional direction
    → execution by oneself or another
    → fulfillment of the course
    → manifest and/or non-manifest determination
```

`Avijñapti` names a determination within this structure. It is neither a
second hidden deed nor a free-floating substance added behind the deed.

**Practical form and its genesis**

Undertaken restraint and concentration-born restraint can determine the same
seven abstentions while differing formally in their genesis. Meaning therefore
lies in the relation:

```text
source of restraint
    → mode of acquisition
    → mode of karmic existence
```

This is the Organon contact. The sixfold list, the exception, the delegated
and delayed-completion analyses, and the two kinds of restraint belong to the
Bhāṣya; the formulation in terms of relational objectivity and Practical Form
is the project synthesis.
