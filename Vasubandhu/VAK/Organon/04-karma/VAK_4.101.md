# VAK_4.101

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
चक्रभेदः स च मतो जम्बूद्वीपे नवादिभिः ।
कर्मभेदस्त्रिषु द्वीपेष्वष्टभिरधिकैश्च सः ॥ ४.१०१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

**Two divisions**

```text
saḥ [pūrvoktaḥ saṃghabhedaḥ]
    cakrabhedaḥ mataḥ
    jambūdvīpe
    navādibhiḥ

saḥ karmabhedaḥ
    triṣu dvīpeṣu
    aṣṭabhiḥ adhikaiḥ ca
```

The first `saḥ` links `cakrabheda` to the schism defined in VAK 4.98–4.100.
The final `saḥ` resumes `karmabheda` as a distinct type.

## 4. Grammar

**Identification of the preceding schism**

```text
saḥ ca
    cakra-bhedaḥ
    mataḥ [asti]
```

> That [schism] is regarded as division of the wheel.

The demonstrative gathers the preceding analysis—false speech, assent to
another teacher and path, and communal non-concord—under the more exact name
`cakrabheda`.

**Locus and number for division of the wheel**

```text
[cakrabhedaḥ]
    jambūdvīpe
    nava-ādibhiḥ [bhavati]
```

> Division of the wheel occurs in Jambūdvīpa through nine or more.

The locative supplies the field; the instrumental supplies the participant
structure. `Nava-ādi` establishes nine as a lower bound, not a fixed total.

**Division of communal action**

```text
saḥ karma-bhedaḥ
    triṣu dvīpeṣu
    aṣṭabhiḥ adhikaiḥ ca [bhavati]
```

> Division of communal action occurs in the three continents through eight
> or more.

The second locative has wider extension. The Bhāṣya restricts it to the three
continents in which the Teaching exists and defines the act as two factions
within one ecclesiastical boundary performing communal karma separately.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> That [schism] is regarded as division of the wheel; [it occurs] in
> Jambūdvīpa through nine or more. Division of communal action [occurs] in the
> three continents through eight or more.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The schism described in the preceding verses is division of the
> Dharma-wheel because it obstructs the functioning of the Path. It occurs
> only in Jambūdvīpa and requires at least nine monks: four constituting each
> of two opposing Saṅghas and a ninth acting as divider. A distinct division
> of communal action occurs when two factions within one ecclesiastical
> boundary perform formal Saṅgha acts separately. It can occur in the three
> human continents where the Teaching exists and requires at least eight
> monks, four on each side.

The obstruction of Path-functioning, the two numerical derivations, the
single-boundary condition, and the distinction between the two divisions are
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A spiritual community can be divided at two different logical levels. Its
> governing unity is ruptured when rival recognition of teacher and Path
> obstructs the Path from functioning as one transmitted order. Its operative
> unity is ruptured when groups remaining within one common institutional
> field perform communal action separately. Division of Principle and
> division of execution are therefore distinct determinations with different
> conditions and extensions.

The rational distinction is:

```text
governing unity
    = one Teacher + one Path + one functioning Dharma-wheel

operative unity
    = one boundary + one coordinated communal action

negation of governing unity
    → cakrabheda

negation of operative unity
    → karmabheda
```

The verse does not collapse every disagreement or procedural fracture into
the exceptional schism that constitutes immediate-result karma.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Cakrabheda` — division of the Dharma-wheel**

The special Saṅgha schism analyzed in VAK 4.98–4.100. Its defining functional
content is not a loose wheel metaphor but the Bhāṣya's
`mārgapravṛtti-viṣṭhāpana`: obstruction or displacement of the functioning
of the Path.

**`Dharmacakra` — Dharma-wheel**

The Buddha's Teaching as a functioning order of Teacher, Path,
transmission, and practice. It is “divided” when rival normative recognition
prevents that order from functioning as one.

**`Mārgapravṛtti` — functioning of the Path**

The active course or operation of the Path within the Saṅgha. This expression
makes explicit why the Path is the higher criterion of the karmic analysis:
the gravest division is defined by obstruction of Path-operation.

**`Navādibhiḥ` — by nine or more**

The minimum structure of `cakrabheda`: four monks constitute one Saṅgha,
four constitute the opposing Saṅgha, and the ninth is the divider. There is
no fixed upper limit.

**`Karmabheda` — division of communal action**

A procedural division in which factions sharing one ecclesiastical boundary
perform a formal Saṅgha act separately. It does not necessarily replace the
common Teacher or Path and is not identical with `cakrabheda`.

**`Ekasīmā` — one ecclesiastical boundary**

The common institutional field within which separate performance of
communal karma counts as `karmabheda`. Shared boundary and divided operation
coexist.

**`Aṣṭabhiḥ adhikaiś ca` — by eight or more**

Two groups of four are sufficient for separate Saṅgha acts. No additional
divider is required because the procedural separation consists immediately
in the opposed actions of the two constituted groups.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
N = normative unity of Teacher and Path
P = functioning of the Path as one order
O = operative unity of communal action
C = cakrabheda
K = karmabheda
```

Then:

```text
Negates(C, N)
Obstructs(C, P)

Negates(K, O)
```

But:

```text
K ≠ C
Negates(K, O)
    does not entail
Negates(K, N)
```

**Relational derivation of the numerical minima**

```text
Cakrabheda:
    Saṅgha(A) + Saṅgha(B) + Divider(D)
    minimum = 4 + 4 + 1 = 9

Karmabheda:
    Saṅgha(A) + Saṅgha(B)
    minimum = 4 + 4 = 8
```

The numbers express the structure required for each relation; they are not
external quantities attached after the type of division is already complete.

**Difference of extension**

```text
OccursIn(C, Jambudvipa)
OccursIn(K, ThreeTeachingContinents)
```

The narrower extension of `cakrabheda` belongs to the exceptional conditions
of rupture in the Dharma-wheel; procedural division has a wider field.

## 9. Interpretive Note

This verse gives direct textual confirmation of the insight that the Path is
the higher truth of karmic courses of action. The preceding verses began with
the divider's false speech as karma, followed its recognitive mediation into
collective non-concord, and traced its maturation. VAK 4.101 now names the
truth of that whole course: it is exceptionally grave because it obstructs
the functioning of the Path.

The sequence is therefore:

```text
false verbal Kriyā
    → assent and rival normative organization
    → division of the spiritual Object
    → obstruction of mārgapravṛtti
```

The Path is “higher” here without being externally appended to Karma. It is
the criterion that reveals what the karmic course actually accomplishes in
the complete system. The karmic act reaches its full determination through
its consequence for the possibility of rational and liberating practice.

The distinction from `karmabheda` is equally important for Rational Yoga.
A Bhūmi can fail at the level of Principle or at the level of coordinated
application. In the first case, rival Teacher and Path divide the governing
meaning of the field. In the second, the common framework may remain while
its agents no longer execute one communal operation. Samyama as Practical
Reason must diagnose which unity has been negated before it can determine the
appropriate course of reintegration.

This is a genuine two-level logic of the spiritual Object:

```text
Principle
    → governs what the community recognizes as Teacher and Path

Operation
    → realizes that unity in coordinated communal karma
```

Their distinction prevents both errors: treating every procedural conflict
as destruction of the Path, and treating a rupture of governing truth as a
mere administrative disagreement.
