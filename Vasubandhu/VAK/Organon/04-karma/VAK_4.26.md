# VAK_4.26

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ध्यानजो ध्यानभूम्यैव लभ्यतेऽनास्रवस्तया ।
आर्यया प्रातिमोक्षाख्यः परविज्ञापनादिभिः ॥ ४.२६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
dhyānajo dhyānabhūmyaiva labhyate 'nāsravas tayā /
āryayā prātimokṣākhyaḥ paravijñāpanādibhiḥ // 4.26 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
dhyāna-jaḥ dhyāna-bhūmyā eva labhyate /
anāsravaḥ tayā āryayā /
prātimokṣa-ākhyaḥ para-vijñāpana-ādibhiḥ //
```

The verb `labhyate`, “is acquired,” governs all three restraints. The particle
`ādi`, “and the others,” is doctrinally important: the ordinary interpersonal
procedure is not the only recognized acquisition of prātimokṣa restraint.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dhyāna-jaḥ
dhyāna-bhūmyā
eva
labhyate

anāsravaḥ
tayā
āryayā

prātimokṣa-ākhyaḥ
para-vijñāpana-ādibhiḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `dhyānajaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | meditation-born [restraint] |
| `dhyānabhūmyā` | instrumental feminine singular | through a dhyāna ground or level |
| `eva` | restrictive particle | only; precisely |
| `labhyate` | third-person singular present passive of `labh` | is obtained; is acquired |
| `anāsravaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | uncontaminated [restraint] |
| `tayā` | instrumental feminine singular pronoun | through that same [ground] |
| `āryayā` | instrumental feminine singular | through it as noble |
| `prātimokṣākhyaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | the restraint called prātimokṣa |
| `paravijñāpanādibhiḥ` | instrumental plural compound | through notification by another and the other recognized means |

### `dhyānaja`

```text
dhyāna + ja
    born from dhyāna
```

The Bhāṣya specifies that this restraint is acquired together with a
dhyāna-level consciousness because the two are co-arising.

### `dhyānabhūmi`

`Bhūmi` is a ground or level. Here it includes both:

```text
maulīya
    belonging to the fundamental dhyāna

sāmantakīya
    neighboring or preparatory
```

For meditation-born restraint, the relevant consciousness is contaminated
(`sāsrava`) yet belongs to the dhyāna level.

### `ārya`

The same meditative ground, when uncontaminated and noble, is the acquisition
ground for uncontaminated restraint. `Ārya` therefore qualifies the ground,
not merely the social status of a bearer.

### `paravijñāpana`

```text
para
    another person

vijñāpana
    notification
    formal communication
    making known
```

The ordinary procedure involves formal notification between persons or by the
Saṃgha. It is an efficacious disciplinary act, not mere information transfer.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

### Meditation-born restraint

```text
dhyāna-jaḥ [saṃvaraḥ]
dhyāna-bhūmyā eva
labhyate
```

> Meditation-born restraint is acquired only through a dhyāna ground.

The Bhāṣya sharpens “through”: when dhyāna-level consciousness is acquired,
the restraint is acquired simultaneously because it is `sahabhū`, co-arising.

### Uncontaminated restraint

```text
anāsravaḥ [saṃvaraḥ]
tayā [dhyāna-bhūmyā]
āryayā
[labhyate]
```

> Uncontaminated restraint is acquired through that same ground when noble.

The prior noun `dhyānabhūmi` and verb `labhyate` are carried forward
elliptically.

### Prātimokṣa restraint

```text
prātimokṣa-ākhyaḥ [saṃvaraḥ]
para-vijñāpana-ādibhiḥ
[labhyate]
```

> The restraint called prātimokṣa is acquired through notification by another
> and the other recognized means.

The instrumental plural prevents reduction to a single universal ceremony.

### Close construe

> Meditation-born restraint is acquired only through a dhyāna ground;
> uncontaminated restraint [is acquired] through that same [ground] when noble;
> the restraint called prātimokṣa [is acquired] through notification by
> another and the other [recognized procedures].

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic translation

> Meditation-born restraint is acquired only through a dhyāna ground;
> uncontaminated restraint is acquired through that same ground in its noble
> form; the restraint called prātimokṣa is acquired through notification by
> another and related means.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Meditation-born restraint is acquired together with contaminated
> dhyāna-level consciousness—whether fundamental or preparatory—because the
> two co-arise. Uncontaminated restraint is acquired when that meditative
> ground is itself acquired as noble and uncontaminated. Prātimokṣa restraint
> is ordinarily acquired through formal notification by another person or the
> Saṃgha, while the verse’s “and the others” includes additional recognized
> Vinaya modes that do not all depend upon another person’s manifestation.

The types of meditative consciousness, co-arising relation, Saṃgha/individual
distinction, and procedural plurality are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Each restraint becomes actual through a ground adequate to its nature.
> Meditation restraint co-arises with attained meditative consciousness;
> uncontaminated restraint co-arises with that ground transformed into noble
> Path-attainment; prātimokṣa restraint arises through a valid practical
> procedure, ordinarily interpersonal and declarative but not reducible to one
> outward speech-event. The unity of restraint therefore contains distinct
> modes of genesis: state-acquisition, Path-acquisition, and institutionally
> valid undertaking.

“Modes of genesis” and “institutionally valid” are interpretive expressions.
The source specifies the acquisition grounds and recognized procedures.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

### `sahabhū`

`Sahabhū`, co-arising, explains the acquisition of meditation-born restraint:

```text
acquisition of dhyāna-level consciousness
    ↔ simultaneous acquisition of dhyāna restraint
```

The relation is co-presence and causal coordination, not identity of
consciousness and restraint.

### Contaminated and uncontaminated grounds

```text
sāsrava dhyāna ground
    → meditation-born restraint

anāsrava ārya dhyāna ground
    → uncontaminated restraint
```

The same general domain of meditative ground is differently determined by its
contamination-status and relation to the noble Path.

### Six uncontaminated dhyāna grounds

The Bhāṣya notes that six dhyāna grounds can be uncontaminated: the four
dhyānas, the `anāgamya`, and the intermediate attainment. Their detailed
treatment is explicitly postponed and need not be imported into this verse.

### Acquisition from Saṃgha or individual

The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
from the Saṃgha
    restraints of monk, nun, and probationer

from an individual
    the other prātimokṣa restraints
```

This is a Vinaya determination of valid acquisition, not merely a sociological
description.

### Ten modes of ordination

The Vinayavibhāṣikas recognize ten modes of `upasaṃpad`. The Bhāṣya’s long
enumeration includes self-arising acquisition for Buddhas and solitary
Buddhas, entry into certainty, the “come, monk” formula, acceptance of a
teacher, satisfaction through answers, acceptance of the weighty rules,
ordination by messenger, procedures involving a Vinaya-holder and a reduced
quorum in frontier regions, the full quorum in central regions, and a
threefold-refuge procedure.

The list explains `ādi`; its purpose here is not to reconstruct every Vinaya
case but to establish procedural plurality.

### Not universally `vijñaptyadhīna`

Vasubandhu concludes:

```text
prātimokṣa restraint
    is not necessarily dependent
    upon another person’s vijñapti
```

Ordinary declaration remains important, but the validity of the restraint is
not universally reducible to that one manifest event.

## 8. Logical Determination

### One genus, three adequate grounds

```text
saṃvara
    ├── dhyānaja
    │     acquired with dhyāna-level consciousness
    ├── anāsrava
    │     acquired with noble uncontaminated ground
    └── prātimokṣa
          acquired through valid disciplinary procedure
```

Shared classification as restraint does not entail identical genesis.

### State and restraint co-arise

```text
meditative consciousness acquired
    → meditation restraint acquired simultaneously

noble uncontaminated ground acquired
    → uncontaminated restraint acquired simultaneously
```

The state does not first exist as a complete acquisition and then manufacture
restraint in an externally subsequent moment.

### Procedure is causally efficacious

```text
valid notification or recognized alternative procedure
    → acquisition of prātimokṣa restraint
```

Convention here is practical form with causal consequences. It neither merely
describes an already acquired vow nor creates it through arbitrary words.

### The `ādi` prevents a false universal

```text
ordinary prātimokṣa acquisition
    often depends on interpersonal manifestation

all prātimokṣa acquisition
    does not universally depend on interpersonal manifestation
```

The exceptional cases belong to the definition of the acquisition field.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### From possession to genesis

VAK 4.19–4.25 asked which determinations are possessed, in what temporal mode,
and whether vijñapti and avijñapti can separate. VAK 4.26 changes the question:

```text
not merely:
    who possesses restraint?

but:
    how does restraint first become possessed?
```

The result is a practical science of genesis. A durable determination cannot
be explained only by pointing to its later persistence; its adequate ground of
acquisition must be specified.

### Does cetanā divide into vijñapti and avijñapti?

The proposal remains powerful but requires qualification. VAK 4.25 showed
that weak cetanā may produce vijñapti without avijñapti, so cetanā certainly
mediates the division. VAK 4.26 now shows that acquisition is not exhausted by
one abstract volitional mechanism:

```text
cetanā
    determines and directs action

but restraint is acquired through
    a meditative consciousness-ground,
    a noble Path-ground,
    or a valid undertaking procedure
```

The provisional architecture should therefore remain layered:

```text
Karma
    encompassing Concept of practical determination

cetanā
    determining and genetic mediation

vijñapti / avijñapti
    differentiated modes of bodily-verbal karma

specific acquisition-ground
    condition adequate to the kind of restraint established
```

This preserves the user’s insight without prematurely forcing every
acquisition into a single derivation.

### The vow is more than a prohibition

The contrast with conventional yama commentary is now especially clear. This
sequence does not merely enumerate prohibited acts or praise ethical ideals.
It asks:

```text
what determination is acquired?
through what ground?
at what moment?
with what temporal range?
under what conditions does it persist or cease?
```

That is why this section exceeds a simple yama–niyama comparison. It supplies
the missing ontology of discipline: how a rule becomes an actual form of the
continuum.

### Institution as practical causality

Prātimokṣa acquisition is philosophically striking because formal procedure
can institute real karmic form. A declaration does not merely report an inward
intention; when validly situated, it changes what the practitioner possesses.

Yet the `ādi` prevents us from identifying institution with one outward
formula. The essence lies in valid acquisition, whose recognized modes can
include exceptional cases without ordinary interpersonal notification.

At the Organon level:

```text
manifest declaration
    can mediate persistent form

persistent form
    is not reducible to the sound of declaration

valid practical institution
    is the Copula joining procedure and acquired status
```

This is an Organon reconstruction. Vasubandhu explicitly teaches the three
acquisition grounds, co-arising with meditative consciousness, and the Vinaya
plurality included by `ādi`.
