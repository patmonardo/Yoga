# VAK_4.54

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तीव्रक्लेशप्रसादेन सातत्येन च यत्कृतम् ।
गुणक्षेत्रे च नियतं तत्पित्रोर्घातकं च यत् ॥ ४.५४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
tīvrakleśaprasādena sātatyena ca yat kṛtam /
guṇakṣetre ca niyataṃ tat pitror ghātakaṃ ca yat // 4.54 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
tīvra-kleśa-prasādena sātatyena ca yat kṛtam /
guṇa-kṣetre ca niyatam tat pitroḥ ghātakam ca yat //
```

The opening compound compresses two alternatives: intense affliction and
intense confidence.

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `tīvra` | compound qualifier | intense; powerful |
| `kleśa` | compound member | affliction |
| `prasādena` | instrumental masculine singular | through confidence, clarity, or serene faith |
| `sātatyena` | instrumental neuter singular | through continuity or sustained repetition |
| `yat kṛtam` | relative pronoun with past participle | whatever karma is performed |
| `guṇakṣetre` | locative neuter singular compound | in a field of qualities |
| `niyatam` | nominative neuter singular | determinate; necessarily maturing |

**`tīvrakleśaprasāda`**

The Bhāṣya expands the compact expression:

```text
tīvreṇa kleśena
    through intense affliction

tīvreṇa prasādena
    through intense confidence
```

These generate opposite ethical directions but share exceptional causal
intensity.

**`prasāda`**

`Prasāda` combines clarity, serene confidence, trust, and devotional
receptivity. “Confidence” is the readable translation here; it should not be
reduced to assent to a proposition.

**`guṇakṣetra`**

A “field of qualities” is an exceptionally qualified recipient-field. The
Bhāṣya specifies the Three Jewels and distinguished persons who have attained
a particular fruit or meditative attainment.

## 4. Grammar

**Intensity and continuity**

```text
yat [karma]
tīvreṇa kleśena vā
tīvreṇa prasādena vā
sātatyena ca
kṛtam
tat niyatam
```

> Karma performed through intense affliction, intense confidence, or sustained
> continuity is determinate.

The prose makes the alternatives explicit. The instrumental phrases state
distinct causal grounds.

**Field of qualities**

```text
yat [karma]
guṇa-kṣetre [kṛtam]
tat ca niyatam
```

> Karma performed in a field of qualities is also determinate.

The understood participle is carried forward. The Bhāṣya treats the field as
an independently sufficient ground.

**Killing either parent**

```text
yat [karma]
pitroḥ ghātakam
tat ca niyatam
```

> Karma that kills either parent is also determinate.

The dual `pitroḥ` is unpacked by the Bhāṣya as mother and father. Killing either
is sufficient; both need not be killed.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Karma performed through intense affliction or intense confidence, through
> sustained continuity, or in a field of qualities is determinate; so too is
> karma that kills either mother or father.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Karma becomes necessarily maturing when it is produced through exceptionally
> intense affliction, exceptionally intense confidence, or sustained repetition.
> Wholesome or unwholesome karma directed toward a field of qualities—the Three
> Jewels or a person distinguished by spiritual fruit or meditative
> attainment—can become determinate even without such intensity or continuity.
> Killing either one's mother or one's father is likewise determinate karma.

The expansion of the opening compound, definition and independent force of
`guṇakṣetra`, and exact scope of `pitroḥ` are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Necessity of karmic fruition is generated by the strength of the concrete
> causal configuration. Determinacy may arise intensively through the force of
> motivation, extensively through repetition across time, relationally through
> an exceptional recipient-field, or through the gravity of a foundational
> human relation. Necessity is therefore not attached externally to an act
> after its performance; it is produced through the manner in which the act is
> grounded and realized.

The external description of an action alone does not disclose its full karmic
force.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Intense affliction**

```text
intense kleśa
    → exceptional unwholesome force
    → determinate karma
```

The relevant affliction may include powerful greed, hatred, or delusion. The
verse concerns intensity of the afflictive ground, not a new species of karma.

**Intense confidence**

```text
intense prasāda
    → exceptional wholesome force
    → determinate karma
```

Intensity functions in both ethical directions without making those directions
equivalent.

**Continuity**

```text
repeated or sustained performance
    → reinforcement of causal disposition
    → determinate karma
```

Fixity can arise through persistence even when no single occurrence has
extreme intensity.

**Field of qualities**

The Bhāṣya defines:

```text
Three Jewels
    Buddha
    Dharma
    Saṅgha

or a distinguished person
    possessing a particular fruit
    or a particular samāpatti
```

In this field, wholesome or unwholesome karma can become determinate without
intense motivation and without continuity.

**Killing a parent**

```text
killing mother
    or
killing father
    → determinate karma
```

The verse does not state that every action involving one's parents is
determinate; it specifies killing.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Alternative sufficient grounds**

```text
INTENSE_AFFLICTION(K)  → DETERMINATE(K)
INTENSE_CONFIDENCE(K)  → DETERMINATE(K)
CONTINUOUS_ACTION(K)   → DETERMINATE(K)
QUALIFIED_FIELD(K)     → DETERMINATE(K)
KILLS_PARENT(K)        → DETERMINATE(K)
```

These are not presented as jointly necessary conditions.

**Dimensions of causal intensification**

| Dimension | Ground of determinacy |
|---|---|
| Agent-side | intensity of affliction or confidence |
| Activity-side | sustained continuity |
| Field-side | exceptional recipient-field |
| Relation-side | killing a parent |

**Genesis of modal necessity**

VAK 4.50 stated the modal difference:

```text
determinate / indeterminate karma
```

VAK 4.54 now gives genetic conditions:

```text
causal configuration acquires exceptional force
    → maturation becomes necessary
```

**Kleśa and karma remain distinct**

```text
kleśa
    motivational affliction

karma
    causal determination generated through action

intense kleśa
    can strengthen karma into determinate fruition
```

The affliction conditions the karma; it is not identical with the karma.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Intense Kriyā/Karma theory**

The verse shows why the visible deed cannot be the complete unit of analysis:

```text
kriyā
    what is performed

karma
    causal determination weighted by
    motive, repetition, field, and relation
```

Externally similar acts can therefore acquire radically different modal force.

**Repetition transforms possibility into trajectory**

Continuity is especially important for discipline. Repeated performance does
not merely add identical acts numerically; it reinforces the continuum from
which further action arises. A defeasible tendency can become a determinate
trajectory.

**The field is causally active within the relation**

`Guṇakṣetra` prevents karma from being modeled solely from the agent's inward
state. The recipient's qualities enter the causal determination of the act.
The field is not an inert object upon which an already completed karma lands.

**Positive and negative intensification**

Intense affliction and intense confidence demonstrate the same formal power of
intensification while producing opposed karmic directions. The logical form
must not erase the ethical difference.

This is the Organon contact. The five grounds, concrete definition of the
field, and sufficiency of that field belong to the Bhāṣya.
