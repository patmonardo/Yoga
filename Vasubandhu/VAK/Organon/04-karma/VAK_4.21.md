# VAK_4.21

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
समाहितार्यमार्गस्थौ तौ युक्तौ वर्तमानया ।
मध्यस्थस्यास्ति चेदादौ मध्यया ऊर्ध्वं द्विकालया ॥ ४.२१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
samāhītāryamārgasthau tau yuktau vartamānayā /
madhyasthasyāsti ced ādau madhyayā ūrdhvaṃ dvikālayā // 4.21 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
samāhita-ārya-mārga-sthau tau yuktau vartamānayā /
madhyasthasya asti cet ādau madhyayā ūrdhvam dvi-kālayā //
```

The transmitted `samāhītāryamārgasthau` is compressed and textually awkward.
The Bhāṣya resolves the intended pair as the practitioner entered into
concentration and the practitioner entered into the noble Path.

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |

| `samāhita` | prior-participial/adjectival member of a compressed dual construction | concentrated; entered into concentration |
| `āryamārgasthau` | nominative masculine dual construction as resolved by the prose | the concentrated practitioner and one established in the noble Path |

| `yuktau` | nominative masculine dual | joined with; endowed with |
| `vartamānayā` | instrumental feminine singular | with present [avijñapti] |
| `madhyasthasya` | genitive masculine singular | of one standing in the middle |

| `cet` | conditional particle | if |
| `ādau` | locative singular used adverbially | at the beginning; in the first moment |
| `madhyayā` | instrumental feminine singular | with the middle [temporal mode], the present |
| `ūrdhvam` | adverb | afterward; subsequently |
| `dvikālayā` | instrumental feminine singular compound | with [avijñapti in] two times |

**`samāhita`**

`Samāhita` means collected, concentrated, or actually entered into meditative
concentration. The Bhāṣya contrasts this with `vyutthita`, one who has emerged
from that state.

**`āryamārgastha`**

One “standing in the noble Path” is presently entered into the Path-state.
The corresponding present avijñapti is uncontaminated restraint.

**`madhyastha`**

The Bhāṣya defines the term exactly:

```text
neither established in restraint
nor established in non-restraint
```

“Middle” here names a third classificatory position. It does not by itself
mean moderation, synthesis, neutrality of every action, or spiritual
attainment.

**`madhyā`**

The present is called the middle temporal mode because it stands between past
and future:

```text
past — present — future
       middle
```

**`dvikāla`**

The two times are past and present. Future possession is not predicated of the
occasional avijñapti belonging to the middle person here.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

**The dual subject**

```text
samāhita [and] āryamārgastha
tau yuktau vartamānayā [avijñaptyā]
```

`Tau` and `yuktau` are dual. The Bhāṣya distributes the predication in order:

```text
one entered into concentration
    → present meditation-born avijñapti

one entered into the noble Path
    → present uncontaminated avijñapti
```

**Actual entry, not mere qualification**

The prose adds `na tu vyutthitau`, “but not when emerged.” Present possession
requires actual occurrence of the corresponding consciousness. This completes
the meaning of `cittānuvartin` from VAK 4.17.

**The conditional middle case**

```text
madhyasthasya asti cet
```

> if one in the middle position possesses [avijñapti]

The conditional is doctrinally important. A person established in neither
system does not necessarily possess avijñapti at all.

**First and later moments**

```text
ādau madhyayā
    at first, with the present mode

ūrdhvaṃ dvikālayā
    afterward, with the two temporal modes
```

The Bhāṣya identifies the latter as past and present. `Atyāga`,
non-abandonment, continues to govern this possession.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The concentrated practitioner and the one established in the noble Path are
> joined with present avijñapti. If one standing in the middle possesses
> avijñapti, at first it is possessed as present, and afterward in two times.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The practitioner actually entered into concentration and the practitioner
> actually entered into the noble Path are respectively endowed with present
> meditation-born and uncontaminated avijñapti; they are not so endowed as
> present when they have emerged from those states. If a person established in
> neither restraint nor non-restraint possesses an occasional avijñapti, it is
> possessed only as present in its first moment and as both past and present
> thereafter, so long as it has not been abandoned.

The identifications of the two restraints, the emerged-state qualification,
the definition of `madhyastha`, and the continuing non-abandonment condition
are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Mind-following restraint exists presently only with its corresponding
> consciousness: meditation restraint while concentration is occurring, and
> uncontaminated restraint while the noble Path is occurring. A person
> established in neither restraint nor non-restraint has no necessary
> avijñapti-system, yet a particular deed may produce an occasional
> non-manifest determination. In its first moment that determination is
> present; afterward it is possessed as both present and past.

This distinguishes state-dependent restraint from occasional karmic
determination without converting the `madhyastha` into a metaphysical Middle.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`vyutthita`**

`Vyutthita` means emerged from or no longer entered into a meditative or Path
state. A person may retain the status of a meditator or noble person while not
presently possessing the corresponding mind-following avijñapti.

**Present mind-following restraint**

```text
corresponding consciousness present
    → corresponding restraint present

corresponding consciousness absent
    → corresponding restraint not present
```

This is narrower than saying the continuum has lost every temporal relation to
that restraint. VAK 4.20 already distinguished possession in past and future
modes.

**The middle practical position**

The threefold field from VAK 4.13 is now temporally completed:

```text
restraint
non-restraint
neither restraint nor non-restraint
```

The third position lacks an established restraint-system or non-restraint-
system. It is nevertheless open to determinate karmic production.

**Occasional avijñapti**

The Bhāṣya mentions avijñapti included under misconduct, components of
discipline, and related cases. Such avijñapti is episodic rather than the
continuous mark of an established practical status.

**`dauḥśīlyaśīlāṅgādi-saṃgṛhīta`**

This Bhāṣya expression gathers occasional avijñapti connected with bad
conduct, components of discipline, and similar cases. It shows that the
middle person’s action can receive either practical coloration without making
the person established in the corresponding comprehensive system.

**Non-abandonment**

The inherited `atyāga` condition remains operative. Once an occasional
avijñapti has arisen, its past-and-present possession continues only so long as
it has not been abandoned.

## 8. Logical Determination

**State-dependent present determination**

```text
meditative consciousness
    ↔ present meditation restraint

noble-Path consciousness
    ↔ present uncontaminated restraint
```

The double arrow marks co-presence in the present analysis, not identity of
consciousness and restraint.

**System and episode**

```text
established restraint or non-restraint
    → continuing practical system

madhyastha
    → no necessary avijñapti-system
    → possible occasional avijñapti
```

One action-trace does not automatically constitute the bearer as established
in a comprehensive vow or counter-vow.

**Temporal development of the episode**

```text
first moment
    occasional avijñapti as present

subsequent moments
    occasional avijñapti as present and past
```

Even an episodic determination acquires temporal depth once produced.

**The opposition is not exhaustive**

```text
not established in restraint
    does not imply
established in non-restraint

not established in non-restraint
    does not imply
established in restraint
```

The `madhyastha` blocks conversion of contrary practical systems into a simple
exhaustive binary.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A vow is a system, not an isolated good deed**

VAK 4.21 helps distinguish a vow from an occasional action. A middle person
may perform a deed connected with discipline and thereby acquire an
avijñapti, yet this does not make the person established in restraint. The act
has karmic determination, but it has not necessarily instituted a
comprehensive practical system.

This is important for our coming discussion of vows:

```text
isolated disciplined act
    → determinate karmic episode

undertaken vow
    → instituted rule governing a continuum
```

The difference is not that one is real and the other unreal. It concerns the
scope and organization of the practical determination.

**The third position is not the speculative Middle**

The word `madhyastha` is tempting because the preceding studies have developed
the productive Middle of the Ideal Copula. Here the Sanskrit licenses a much
narrower determination: a person who belongs to neither established practical
class.

The philosophical value of the category is nevertheless considerable. It
shows that the opposition of restraint and non-restraint does not exhaust the
field. But the third position is initially indeterminate with respect to those
systems; it is not their accomplished reconciliation.

```text
logical Middle
    grounds and differentiates opposed determinations

madhyastha here
    lacks establishment in either opposed system
```

Keeping those levels separate prevents a useful analogy from falsifying the
text.

**Practice beyond formal vows**

This verse also explains why practical knowledge cannot be reduced to the
formal taxonomy of vows. A person outside both established systems can still
produce disciplined or undisciplined determinations. Practice is therefore
larger than institutional status, even though an instituted vow possesses a
more comprehensive continuity.

This connects indirectly with the user’s yama/niyama observation. The present
Karma sequence analyzes restraint with exceptional precision: acquisition,
temporal persistence, abandonment, state-dependence, and episodic action. It
does not organize self-study or dedication to Īśvara as parallel niyama
categories. Those inward disciplines would have to be reconstructed from
other domains of study, contemplation, and Path rather than extracted from
this restraint taxonomy.

**Two ways determination enters actuality**

At the Organon level, VAK 4.21 distinguishes:

```text
determination through an actual state
    the corresponding consciousness and restraint co-occur

determination through a contingent deed
    an occasional avijñapti is produced and gains temporal depth
```

The first is structurally state-dependent; the second is episodically
generated. Both are real forms of practical determination, but neither should
be made the universal model of the other.

This is an Organon reconstruction. Vasubandhu’s explicit claims concern
present possession during concentration or Path, its absence when emerged,
and the conditional temporal possession of a middle person’s avijñapti.
