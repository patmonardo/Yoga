# VAK_4.25

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
विज्ञप्त्यैवान्वितः कुर्वन् मध्यस्थो मृदुचेतनः ।
त्यक्तानुत्पन्नविज्ञप्तिरविज्ञप्त्यार्यपुद्गलः ॥ ४.२५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
vijñaptyaivānvitaḥ kurvan madhyastho mṛducetanaḥ /
tyaktānutpannavijñaptir avijñapty āryapudgalaḥ // 4.25 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
vijñaptyā eva anvitaḥ kurvan madhyasthaḥ mṛdu-cetanaḥ /
tyakta-anutpanna-vijñaptiḥ avijñaptyā ārya-pudgalaḥ //
```

The verse gives the two crossed cases announced by the Bhāṣya’s
`catuṣkoṭika`: vijñapti without avijñapti, then avijñapti without vijñapti.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vijñaptyā
eva
anvitaḥ
kurvan
madhyasthaḥ
mṛdu-cetanaḥ

tyakta-anutpanna-vijñaptiḥ
avijñaptyā
ārya-pudgalaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `vijñaptyā` | instrumental feminine singular | with manifestation; with manifest action |
| `eva` | restrictive particle | only; precisely |
| `anvitaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | endowed with; possessed of |
| `kurvan` | nominative masculine singular present participle | acting; while performing |
| `madhyasthaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | one established in neither restraint nor non-restraint |
| `mṛducetanaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | one whose intention is weak |
| `tyakta` | compound member, past passive participle | abandoned; relinquished |
| `anutpanna` | compound member, past passive participle with privative prefix | not arisen; not yet produced |
| `vijñaptiḥ` | compound-final feminine noun | whose vijñapti is abandoned or unarisen |
| `avijñaptyā` | instrumental feminine singular | with non-manifest action |
| `āryapudgalaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | noble person |

**`mṛducetanā`**

```text
mṛdu
    weak
    mild
    lacking force

cetanā
    volition
    action-directing determination
```

The weakness belongs to the volitional determination. The act can become
manifest without acquiring sufficient force to establish avijñapti.

**Restrictive `eva`**

`Eva` is essential:

```text
vijñaptyā eva
    with vijñapti only
    not with avijñapti
```

The verse is not merely emphasizing manifestation; it excludes the other mode
of possession in this case.

**`tyaktānutpannavijñapti`**

The compound joins two alternatives:

```text
tyakta-vijñapti
    vijñapti has been abandoned

anutpanna-vijñapti
    vijñapti has not yet arisen
```

The Bhāṣya locates the latter case after transition into another birth.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

**First crossed case**

```text
madhyasthaḥ mṛdu-cetanaḥ
kurvan
vijñaptyā eva anvitaḥ
```

> A weakly intending middle-position person, while acting, is endowed with
> vijñapti alone.

The Bhāṣya supplies the excluded predicate: not endowed with avijñapti. The
action may be wholesome or unwholesome.

**Second crossed case**

```text
tyakta-anutpanna-vijñaptiḥ
ārya-pudgalaḥ
avijñaptyā [eva anvitaḥ]
```

> A noble person whose vijñapti has been abandoned or has not arisen is
> endowed with avijñapti [alone].

The possession-predicate is carried forward elliptically. The Bhāṣya makes the
exclusion explicit: avijñapti but not vijñapti.

**Scope of the noble-person alternatives**

The Bhāṣya specifies:

```text
after transition into another birth
    the relevant vijñapti has not yet arisen

or

the relevant vijñapti
    has subsequently been abandoned
```

In either circumstance the noble person remains endowed with avijñapti.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> A person established in neither restraint nor non-restraint, acting with
> weak intention, is endowed only with manifestation. A noble person whose
> manifestation has been abandoned or has not arisen is endowed with
> non-manifest action.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A middle-position person performing wholesome or unwholesome action with
> weak intention possesses the manifest action alone, without avijñapti. A
> noble person whose corresponding manifestation has either not yet arisen
> after transition into another birth or has been abandoned possesses
> avijñapti alone, without vijñapti.

The ethical range of the first action, the new-birth setting, and both explicit
exclusions are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Manifestation and non-manifest karmic determination can be separated in
> either direction. Weak volition may produce an actual bodily or verbal
> manifestation without instituting a continuing avijñapti. Conversely, the
> noble Path’s non-manifest restraint can remain possessed when no
> corresponding manifestation is presently possessed—whether because it has
> not yet arisen in the new birth or because it has been relinquished.

The verse establishes distinct possession-relations. It does not reduce
avijñapti to volition or claim that all strong volition necessarily produces
the same kind of avijñapti.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**The middle-position agent**

The `madhyastha` possesses neither the comprehensive restraint system nor the
comprehensive non-restraint system. A particular act can therefore remain an
episode rather than establishing either standing form.

**Weak intention and manifest action**

```text
weak cetanā
    → action is bodily or verbally manifest
    → vijñapti is possessed
    → avijñapti need not arise
```

The sensible occurrence of action does not by itself prove the acquisition of
a persistent practical determination.

**Indeterminate action and the exception**

The Bhāṣya says the absence of avijñapti is still more evident for
indeterminate action, while preserving an exception expressed through the
compressed term `aupadika-puṇyakriyā-vastu-karmapatha`. Because the exact scope
of this technical expression is not developed in the present unit, the
exception is retained rather than expanded into the primary translation.

**Noble avijñapti without vijñapti**

The noble person’s avijñapti is the uncontaminated restraint associated with
the Path. Its possession is not conditional upon a simultaneous bodily or
verbal expression.

**`janmāntaraparivṛtti`**

```text
janmāntara
    another birth

parivṛtti
    transition; turning into
```

The continuum can enter another birth already possessing noble avijñapti,
before a new corresponding vijñapti has arisen there.

**Closure of the possession analysis**

After these cases, Vasubandhu declares the arrangement of possession of
vijñapti and avijñapti for restraint bearers, non-restraint bearers, and the
middle position complete. The next topic is how the restraints are acquired.

## 8. Logical Determination

**First independence relation**

```text
vijñapti
    does not entail
avijñapti
```

The weakly intending middle-position person is the witness.

**Reverse independence relation**

```text
avijñapti
    does not entail
presently possessed vijñapti
```

The noble person with abandoned or unarisen vijñapti is the witness.

**The catuṣkoṭika frame**

The four formal possibilities are:

```text
1. vijñapti without avijñapti
2. avijñapti without vijñapti
3. both vijñapti and avijñapti
4. neither vijñapti nor avijñapti
```

VAK 4.25 states the first two crossed cases because the preceding analysis has
already supplied the wider possession arrangement. This formal completion is
a reconstruction of the announced four-cornered question, not four clauses
contained in the kārikā.

**Cetanā mediates without replacing the dyad**

```text
cetanā
    determines the force and direction of action

vijñapti
    determines actual manifestation

avijñapti
    determines continuing non-manifest possession
```

Weak cetanā explains one separation-case. It does not make vijñapti and
avijñapti mere names for volitional strength.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Reciprocation is stronger than inseparability**

This verse gives the dialectical Wheel an exact test. If vijñapti and
avijñapti were merely two inseparable properties of every bodily and verbal
act, neither crossed case could occur. Yet the Kośa explicitly establishes:

```text
vijñapti without avijñapti
avijñapti without vijñapti
```

Their reciprocity is therefore semantic and functional, not the requirement
that both be instantiated at every moment. Each term can be understood through
the other while remaining capable of independent possession.

**The Wheel permits passage in both directions**

```text
vijñapti → avijñapti
    when manifest action institutes persistent determination

vijñapti ↛ avijñapti
    when weak intention leaves the act at manifestation

avijñapti → vijñapti
    when persistent determination becomes expressed again

avijñapti without current vijñapti
    when the determination persists without expression
```

The Wheel is not a machine forcing every transition. It is the semantic plane
on which the possible relations between the two Aspects are articulated.

**Cetanā is the determining mediation**

Karma first divided into volition and action produced from volition. The
present verse returns that original determination to the vijñapti-avijñapti
dyad. The strength of cetanā helps determine whether a manifest episode
institutes a continuing practical form.

```text
Karma as Concept
    → cetanā, determining activity
    → vijñapti, manifest actuality
    → avijñapti, persistent efficacy
```

But the sequence must remain reciprocal. Persistent form can return into new
manifestation, and current manifestation can either generate or fail to
generate persistent form. Karma is the Concept holding these differentiated
relations together.

**A practical act is not exhausted by appearance**

The weak-intention case proves that appearance alone does not establish
persistence. The noble-person case proves that absence of present appearance
does not establish absence of practical form. Together they defeat both
reductions:

```text
what appears is not necessarily all that persists

what persists is not necessarily presently apparent
```

This is the positive value of the dyadic analysis. It does not install a
transcendent hidden object behind appearance; it distinguishes the conditions
of manifest occurrence from those of continuing karmic attribution.

This is an Organon reconstruction. Vasubandhu explicitly establishes the two
crossed possession-cases and closes the local arrangement of vijñapti and
avijñapti.
