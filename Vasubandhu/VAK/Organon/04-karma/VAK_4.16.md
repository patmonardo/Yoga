# VAK_4.16

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> शीलं सुचरितं कर्म संवरश्चोच्यते पुनः ।
>
> आद्ये विज्ञप्त्यविज्ञप्ती प्रातिमोक्षक्रियापथः ॥ ४.१६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> śīlaṃ sucaritaṃ karma saṃvaraś cocyate punaḥ /
>
> ādye vijñaptyavijñaptī prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ // 4.16 //

VAK 4.13–4.15 classified `prātimokṣa` restraint by kind, designation, and
scope.

VAK 4.16 now gives four valid names for the restraint and distinguishes its
first acquisition-moment from its subsequent continuation.

```text
one restraint-complex
    → śīla
    → sucarita
    → karma
    → saṃvara

first moment
    → vijñapti + avijñapti
    → strictly prātimokṣa

later moments
    → continuing prātimokṣasaṃvara
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
śīlam                    → śīlam
sucaritam                → sucaritam
karma                    → karma
saṃvaraḥ                 → saṃvaraś before c-
ca                       → ca
ucyate                   → ucyate
punaḥ                    → punaḥ
ādye                     → ādye
vijñapti-avijñaptī       → vijñaptyavijñaptī
prātimokṣa-kriyāpathaḥ   → prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| śīlam | nominative neuter singular | discipline; trained ethical conduct |
| sucaritam | nominative neuter singular | good conduct; conduct praised as well performed |
| karma | nominative neuter singular | action; causally produced practical activity |
| saṃvaraḥ | nominative masculine singular | restraint of body and speech |
| ucyate | third-person singular present passive of `√vac` | is called |
| punaḥ | adverb | again; furthermore |
| ādye | locative singular neuter | in the first; Bhāṣya: first moment of undertaking restraint |
| vijñapti | first member of dual coordination | manifest action |
| avijñaptī | dual ending governing the coordinated pair | manifest and non-manifest action, two |
| prātimokṣa | technical term | strict first-moment release or casting off of evil |
| kriyāpatha | course of activity | transmitted kārikā expression; prose explains `karmapatha` |

### Correction of `ādye`

The initial seed construed `ādye` as “the first two” names, `śīla` and
`sucarita`.

The Bhāṣya explicitly rules that out:

```text
saṃvara-samādānasya prathame
    in the first [moment] of undertaking restraint
```

Thus `ādye` is locative singular, not nominative dual.

### Textual caution in the final expression

The kārikā witness gives `prātimokṣakriyāpathaḥ`; the prose e-text contains
`prātimokṣakiyāpathaḥ` at the verse citation but immediately explains the term
as `karmapatha`.

The official study preserves this difference:

```text
kārikā witness
    → kriyāpatha

Bhāṣya doctrinal gloss
    → karmapatha
```

No silent replacement is made.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first line gives four coordinated predicates of the understood
`prātimokṣasaṃvara`:

```text
[prātimokṣasaṃvaraḥ]
    śīlam ucyate
    sucaritam [ucyate]
    karma [ucyate]
    saṃvaraḥ ca ucyate

[prātimokṣa restraint]
    is called discipline,
    good conduct,
    action,
    and restraint
```

The Bhāṣya gives a different ground for each predicate:

```text
śīla
    → undertaking abstention from improper actions
    → explanatory association with coolness

sucarita
    → praised by the learned

karma
    → possesses the nature of causally produced activity

saṃvara
    → restrains body and speech
```

The second line distinguishes the acquisition-moment:

```text
ādye [kṣaṇe]
    vijñapti-avijñaptī
    prātimokṣa [ity ucyete]

in the first moment,
    manifestation and non-manifest action
    are called prātimokṣa
```

The final predicate is Bhāṣya-resolved:

```text
prātimokṣasaṃvaraḥ
    karmapathaḥ [ucyate]
    maula-saṃgṛhītatvāt

prātimokṣa restraint
    is called an action-course
    because it is included in the fundamental phase
```

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> It is further called discipline, good conduct, action, and restraint. In the
> first [moment], manifest and non-manifest action are `prātimokṣa`; [it is] a
> course of activity.

The final clause remains close to the transmitted `kriyāpatha` wording.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> `Prātimokṣa` restraint is called discipline, good conduct, action, and
> restraint. In the first moment of undertaking restraint, both its manifest
> and non-manifest determinations are strictly called `prātimokṣa`, because
> through them evil is released or cast off. From the second moment onward, the
> continuing determination is called `prātimokṣasaṃvara`, but no longer
> `prātimokṣa` in that strict acquisition sense. It is also called an
> action-course insofar as it belongs to the fundamental phase of action.

The grounds of the four names, the strict first-moment usage, and the
fundamental/subsequent distinction are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Restraint is one practical determination viewed through four functions: as
> trained conduct, as ethically praiseworthy conduct, as an activity that is
> positively produced, and as inhibition of bodily and verbal misconduct. Its
> acquisition and persistence are not identical. In the first moment, manifest
> undertaking and non-manifest efficacy jointly accomplish release from evil;
> afterward, the restraint continues as an established practical
> determination.

The paradox is exact:

```text
restraint prevents an evil action
    and is therefore called non-activity in that respect

yet restraint is itself produced and causally efficacious
    and is therefore karma in that respect
```


Vasubandhu's preceding explanation.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Four predicates, four grounds

| Predicate | Ground of predication |
|---|---|
| `śīla` | undertaken abstention; cooling rather than burning |
| `sucarita` | praise by the learned |
| `karma` | positive causal production |
| `saṃvara` | restraint of body and speech |

The terms converge upon one restraint-complex without becoming synonyms in
every context.

### How `avijñapti` can be `akriyā` and `karma`

The objection is:

```text
avijñapti has been called akriyā
    → non-activity

how can it now be called karma or kriyā?
```

The answer distinguishes relational Aspects:

```text
Preventive Aspect
    one who possesses the restraint
    does not perform evil through it
        → akriyā

Productive Aspect
    avijñapti is produced
    by vijñapti and citta
        → kriyā / karma
```

There is no contradiction because the predicates answer different questions.

```text
What deed does restraint prevent?
    → evil activity does not occur

How does restraint itself become actual?
    → it is positively produced
```

### Acquisition and continuation

```text
first moment of restraint-taking
    vijñapti + avijñapti
        → prātimokṣa strictly

second and later moments
    continuing avijñapti restraint
        → prātimokṣasaṃvara
        → not prātimokṣa in the strict first-moment sense
```

The chapter therefore repeats at the level of Discipline the distinction it
previously made within action:

```text
initiation
    ≠ continuation
```

### Fundamental and subsequent phases

The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
maula
    → fundamental action-course

pṛṣṭha
    → what follows afterward
```

`Prātimokṣasaṃvara` is called a `karmapatha` insofar as it is included in the
fundamental phase. The subsequent phase is not itself that fundamental
action-course.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### Discipline is positive practical activity

VAK 4.16 guards against treating restraint as an empty absence.

```text
the prohibited deed does not occur

but the restraint does occur
```

The non-occurrence of evil is achieved through a positively constituted
discipline. `Avijñapti` is called non-activity relative to the deed prevented,
not because restraint lacks causal production or practical efficacy.

This gives a promising—but still incomplete—basis for the positive,
Kant-clean and Yoga-clean interpretation of `avijñapti`:

```text
not a hidden transcendent object

but a positively established practical determination
whose function is the non-performance of evil
```

The precise ontological reconstruction remains open. The textual distinction
is already secure.

### Acquisition is not persistence

The strict meaning of `prātimokṣa` belongs to the first moment because release
from evil is then actively accomplished through manifest and non-manifest
determination together.

The subsequent restraint persists, but its function is now continuation of
what was acquired.

```text
acquisition
    → establishes the practical determination

continuation
    → preserves it through later moments
```

This is another example of Dharma arranging itself through functional Aspects.
The same discipline has an initiating station and a continuing station without
requiring those stations to be identical.

### Contact with yama

The Yoga contact remains clear but controlled.

`Śīla` and `saṃvara` describe a discipline that is positively undertaken and
that restrains bodily and verbal misconduct. This resembles yama as an
established governance of practical possibility, not mere accidental omission.

But VAK 4.16 is especially concerned with the Buddhist technical relation
among `vijñapti`, `avijñapti`, `prātimokṣa`, and `karmapatha`. Those terms should
not be replaced by Yoga vocabulary.
