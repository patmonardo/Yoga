# VAK_4.19

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
प्रातिमोक्षस्थितो नित्यमत्यागाद्वर्तमानया ।
अविज्ञप्त्यान्वितः पूर्वात् क्षणादूर्ध्वमतीतया ॥ ४.१९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
prātimokṣasthito nityam atyāgād vartamānayā /
avijñaptyānvitaḥ pūrvāt kṣaṇād ūrdhvam atītayā // 4.19 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
prātimokṣa-sthitaḥ nityam atyāgāt vartamānayā /
avijñaptyā anvitaḥ pūrvāt kṣaṇāt ūrdhvam atītayā //
```

The Bhāṣya’s prose citation contains two evident electronic irregularities in
forms of `prātimokṣa`. They do not create a substantive alternative to the
kārikā reading printed above.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis


| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `prātimokṣasthitaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | one established in prātimokṣa restraint |
| `nityam` | adverbial accusative | continually; throughout the relevant duration |
| `atyāgāt` | ablative singular of `atyāga` | because of non-abandonment; so long as it is not relinquished |
| `vartamānayā` | instrumental feminine singular | with the present [avijñapti] |
| `avijñaptyā` | instrumental feminine singular | with avijñapti |
| `anvitaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | endowed with; connected with |
| `pūrvāt` | ablative singular | from the prior or first [moment] |
| `kṣaṇāt` | ablative singular | from the moment |
| `ūrdhvam` | adverb | afterward; subsequent to |
| `atītayā` | instrumental feminine singular | with the past [avijñapti] |

### `nityam`

`Nityam` means continually while the stated condition holds. It does not
ascribe metaphysical eternity to avijñapti. The delimiting condition is
`atyāga`: continued non-relinquishment.

### `atyāga`

```text
a-tyāga
    non-abandonment
    non-relinquishment
```

The Bhāṣya expressly says that `atyāgāt` must be understood as governing
throughout. Both present and past possession remain conditional upon the
restraint’s not having been abandoned.

### `vartamāna` and `atīta`

These are Sarvāstivāda temporal determinations of the avijñapti possessed by
the bearer:

```text
vartamāna
    presently occurring

atīta
    belonging to an earlier moment
    possessed as past
```

The verse does not predicate future avijñapti of the prātimokṣa bearer here.

### `anvita` and `samanvāgata`

The kārikā’s `anvita` is explained by `samanvāgata` in the Bhāṣya. “Endowed
with” names a technical possession-relation between the person’s continuum
and avijñapti in a specified temporal mode.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The governing subject is `prātimokṣasthitaḥ`, “one established in
prātimokṣa.” Its predicate is `anvitaḥ`, “endowed.” Two instrumental phrases
specify what the person possesses.

### Present possession

```text
vartamānayā avijñaptyā anvitaḥ
```

> endowed with present avijñapti

`Nityam` gives the continuity of this possession, while `atyāgāt` gives its
condition.

### Past possession

```text
pūrvāt kṣaṇāt ūrdhvam atītayā [avijñaptyā] [anvitaḥ]
```

The noun `avijñapti` and predicate `anvitaḥ` are carried forward elliptically:

> after the first moment, [he is also endowed] with past [avijñapti].

The Bhāṣya resolves `pūrvāt kṣaṇāt` as `prathamāt kṣaṇāt`, “from the first
moment.” The past mode becomes available only after an earlier moment of the
restraint has occurred.

### Scope of `atyāgāt`

The syntax might initially suggest that `atyāgāt` qualifies only present
possession. Vasubandhu explicitly prevents this restriction:

```text
atyāgāt
    governs present possession
    and past possession
    throughout the analysis
```

### Close construe

> One established in prātimokṣa is continually endowed, because of
> non-abandonment, with present avijñapti; after the first moment, [he is]
> also [endowed] with past [avijñapti].

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic translation

> One established in prātimokṣa restraint is continually endowed with present
> avijñapti because it has not been abandoned; after the first moment, he is
> also endowed with past avijñapti.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> One established in prātimokṣa restraint is continually endowed with present
> avijñapti for as long as that restraint has not been relinquished. From the
> moment following the first, he is also endowed with past avijñapti, likewise
> so long as the restraint has not been relinquished.

The repetition of the non-abandonment condition in English makes explicit the
scope stated by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The bearer of prātimokṣa restraint continuously possesses avijñapti in its
> present mode while the restraint remains un-abandoned. At the first moment,
> only present avijñapti is possessed. Thereafter each current occurrence is
> possessed as present, while the avijñapti of preceding moments is also
> possessed under the determination of pastness. The restraint’s continuity
> is therefore temporally articulated and governed throughout by
> non-relinquishment.

This states the Sarvāstivāda temporal account. It does not yet substitute the
Organon’s account of Persistence for the source doctrine.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

### `prātimokṣastha`

The compound designates a person established in prātimokṣa restraint. The
verse is not defining how the restraint is first acquired; VAK 4.16 already
distinguished acquisition from continuation. It now asks how long the bearer
is technically endowed with its avijñapti.

### `samanvāgama`

The Bhāṣya introduces the general inquiry:

```text
which person
    is endowed with which vijñapti or avijñapti
    and for how long?
```

`Samanvāgama` is not simple physical containment. It is the Abhidharma
possession relation through which a dharma belongs to a continuum in a
specified temporal status.

### Present avijñapti

The presently arising avijñapti is possessed at every moment during which the
restraint continues. The verse therefore supplies a positive present term; it
does not define persistence only through memory of an inaugural vow.

### Past avijñapti

After the first moment, the prior avijñapti is possessed as past. “Past” does
not mean merely that a present representation remembers something no longer
doctrinally relevant. Pastness is itself a formal temporal determination in
the Sarvāstivāda analysis of possession.

### Non-abandonment

`Atyāga` is the condition maintaining the possession relation. It is not a
second act that must recreate the original undertaking at every instant. It
marks that the acquired restraint has not been relinquished by any condition
that terminates it.

## 8. Logical Determination

### The first moment

```text
moment 1
    present avijñapti
    no past avijñapti of this restraint yet
```

This is the temporal side of the acquisition-moment distinguished in VAK
4.16.

### Every subsequent moment

```text
moment 2
    present avijñapti at moment 2
    moment-1 avijñapti possessed as past

moment 3
    present avijñapti at moment 3
    preceding avijñapti possessed as past
```

The temporal determinations accumulate without making the current moment
numerically identical with the first.

### The governing condition

```text
acquisition
    establishes prātimokṣa restraint

non-abandonment
    maintains the possession relation

abandonment
    terminates the relevant possession
```

Continuity is therefore conditional but not intermittent.

### Persistence and temporal identity

The explicit source analysis distinguishes:

```text
continuity of restraint
    from
numerical identity of one present moment across time
```

The bearer has a presently determined avijñapti and, after the first moment,
the earlier determination as past. The Kośa thus constructs persistence
through ordered temporal predication.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### The vow is a temporal rule

VAK 4.17 established that prātimokṣa restraint does not merely follow the
currently occurring consciousness. VAK 4.19 now gives that independence a
temporal articulation. Once acquired, the restraint is continually present so
long as it is not abandoned, while its preceding determinations remain
possessed as past.

The vow is therefore neither an evanescent mental intention nor a timeless
entity. It is a rule instantiated through time:

```text
undertaking
    → first present determination

non-abandonment
    → continuing present determination
    + possession of prior determination as past
```

This is the practical force of `atyāga`. The rule remains valid without
requiring the practitioner to repeat its original manifest expression in
every moment.

### Appearance, Dharma, and temporal modality

At the Organon level, we must not infer from the vanishing of an appearance
that the Dharma itself has been annihilated. The Kośa speaks here through the
Sarvāstivāda grammar of present and past dharmas. Our reconstruction can read
these as temporal modalities through which one practical determination is
articulated:

```text
present
    the determination in current appearance

past
    the determination retained under pastness

Persistence
    the Dharma governing both temporal modes
```

This does not silently rewrite the primary translation. It identifies the
philosophical problem that the source classification is attempting to solve:
how a determination remains efficacious when its first appearance has
vanished.

The answer cannot be a transcendent stockpile of invisible things. Nor can it
be the claim that nothing persists because each appearance passes away. The
positive transcendental question concerns the law by which present and past
belong to one intelligible practical continuity.

### Avijñapti as instituted validity

VAK 4.19 gives avijñapti a precise positive sense. It is not simply what fails
to appear. It is the acquired determination that remains attributable to the
continuum under the condition of non-abandonment. Its non-manifest character
allows the vow to govern conduct beyond the moment of explicit declaration.

In the language of the Ideal Copula:

```text
the bearer
    is endowed with
the restraint

not as static identity,
but through a temporally governed possession relation
```

The Copula here contains its condition: “is endowed” means “continues to bear
as long as not abandoned.” The predicate includes a rule of persistence.

This is an Organon development. Vasubandhu’s explicit determination remains:
continuous possession of present avijñapti, and possession of past avijñapti
after the first moment, all governed by `atyāga`.
