# VAK 4.84 Bhāṣya — The Seven Wholesome Material Courses

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
ārūpyāsaṃjñisattveṣu lābhataḥ sapta śeṣite /
saṃmukhībhāvataś cāpi hitvā sanarakān kurūn // 4.84 //
```

> In the formless domain and among non-percipient beings, the seven occur by
> acquisition. In the remainder they occur also through manifestation,
> excluding the hells together with the Kurus.

The three wholesome mental courses were distributed in 4.83. The “seven” are
the wholesome bodily and verbal courses: the seven abstentions corresponding
to the three bodily and four verbal unwholesome courses.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast

ārūpyopapannānām āryāṇām atītānāgatānāsravasaṃvarasamanvāgamāt,
asaṃjñisattvānāṃ ca dhyānasaṃvarasamanvāgamāt.

yadbhūmyāśrayeṇāryeṇānāsravaṃ śīlam utpāditaṃ nirodhitaṃ bhavati,
tenārūpyeṣv atītena samanvāgato bhavati. pañcabhūmyāśrayeṇa tv anāgatena.

śeṣite /

saṃmukhībhāvataś cāpi hitvā sanarakān kurūn // 4.84 //

śeṣaḥ kṛtaḥ śeṣitaḥ. yo 'nyo dhātuḥ śeṣito gatir vā, tatrāite sapta
kuśalāḥ karmapathāḥ saṃmukhībhāvato 'pi saṃvidyante 'nyatra
narakottarakurubhyaḥ.

te punas tiryakpreteṣu saṃvaranirmuktāḥ, rūpadhātau tu
saṃvarasaṃgṛhītāḥ, anyatrobhayathā.

```

The final phrase begins the transition to 4.85 and grammatically gathers the
ten unwholesome and ten wholesome courses for the coming analysis of their
results. It is included because it closes the distribution just completed;
the new predicate concerning results begins with the next kārikā.

The running witness has `ārūpyoopapannānām` at `[253|14–16]`, an evident
vowel duplication normalized above as `ārūpyopapannānām`. It also prints
`śesaḥ` at `[253|21]`; the expected form is `śeṣaḥ`.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

In the formless domain and among non-percipient beings, the seven wholesome
bodily and verbal courses exist only through endowment.

For noble persons reborn in the formless domain, this is because they are
endowed with past and future uncontaminated restraint. Non-percipient beings
are endowed with dhyāna restraint.

When a noble person has produced and subsequently ceased uncontaminated
conduct while supported by a particular ground, that person, while in the
formless domain, is endowed with that conduct as past conduct associated with
that ground. As future conduct, the person is endowed with it in dependence
upon five grounds.

“In the remainder” means that a remainder has been made: whichever other
domain or destiny remains. There the seven wholesome courses also exist by
present manifestation, except in the hells and Uttarakuru.

Among animals and hungry ghosts these seven are independent of formal
restraint; in the form realm they are included within restraint; elsewhere
they occur in both ways. These, then, are the ten unwholesome and ten
wholesome courses of action whose results will next be explained.

## 4. What the Seven Are

The seven are the bodily and verbal abstentions:

1. abstention from killing,
2. abstention from taking what is not given,
3. abstention from sexual misconduct,
4. abstention from false speech,
5. abstention from divisive speech,
6. abstention from harsh speech,
7. abstention from frivolous or disconnected speech.

Unlike the three wholesome mental courses, these seven have a material
dimension. Their present manifestation ordinarily requires bodily or verbal
support. This makes their distribution narrower than the universal
distribution stated at the end of 4.83.

## 5. Endowment Without Present Manifestation

The formless domain supplies no body or speech through which the seven could
presently operate. Non-percipient beings lack the presently active cognition
required for intentional abstention. Nevertheless, the Bhāṣya says the seven
can belong to both kinds of continuum through `samanvāgama`.

```text
continuum is endowed with restraint
    while
conditions for its present exercise are absent
```

For formless noble beings, the endowment is specifically an endowment with
uncontaminated restraint across temporal modes. For non-percipient beings,
it derives from dhyāna restraint. Thus “not now manifest” does not mean that
the restraint has no standing in the continuum.

## 6. Past and Future Uncontaminated Restraint

The temporal qualification is exact. Past endowment corresponds to the
ground upon which the noble person actually produced and ceased the
uncontaminated conduct. Future endowment extends across five supporting
grounds.

```text
past restraint
    indexed to the ground of actual prior production

future restraint
    available in relation to five supporting grounds
```

The passage therefore combines three coordinates:

```text
course × temporal mode × supporting ground
```

The present formless existence does not erase the restraint's past and future
relations merely because it prevents current bodily and verbal manifestation.
This is a technical Sarvāstivāda account of temporal endowment; it should not
be silently replaced by a modern memory or disposition theory.

## 7. Manifestation in the Remaining Fields

`Śeṣita` is explained directly: the text has made a remainder by removing the
cases already treated. In every remaining suitable domain or destiny, the
seven can also become presently manifest, with two exceptions:

```text
hells       — excluded
Uttarakuru  — excluded
```

The mode of their manifestation then differs:

| Field | Status of the seven when manifest |
|---|---|
| animals and hungry ghosts | independent of formal restraint |
| form realm | included within restraint |
| other remaining cases | either within restraint or independent of it |

Abstaining from an act and possessing a formally constituted restraint are
therefore not identical. A wholesome course can occur without the continuing
juridical structure of `saṃvara`; in other environments its manifestation is
precisely an expression of that structure.

## 8. The Completed Distribution

VAK 4.82–4.84 now yield a layered classification:

```text
10 unwholesome courses
    classified by destiny,
    endowment,
    manifestation,
    and non-restraint

10 wholesome courses
    3 mental courses distributed universally
    7 bodily/verbal courses distributed by support and restraint
```

The classification is not a mere list of moral predicates. It determines
where each course can belong, where it can appear, and under what practical
status its appearance occurs.

## 9. Organon Contact Point

For the Organon, the strongest determination is the separation of standing
power from current execution:

```text
<Endowment : Manifestation>
    =
<operative standing : present execution>
```

A Root Agent may retain an acquired practical constraint while operating in
a domain where its executable channel is absent. The constraint remains part
of the agent's rational state and may become executable again when the
required support returns.

The Sarvāstivāda temporal claim goes further: standing is indexed not only to
the agent but also to time and ground. An adequate schema would therefore
require at least:

```text
RestraintStanding {
    course
    continuum
    temporalMode
    supportingGround
    formalStatus
    manifestableHere
}
```

This is the Organon reconstruction. The ontological status of past and future
dharmas, the five grounds, and the particular realm classifications remain
source doctrine.

## 10. Review Status

The complete 4.84 unit `[253|13]–[253|24]` is included. It covers the seven
courses as endowments in formless and non-percipient existence, the temporal
and ground-indexed account of uncontaminated restraint, manifestation in the
remaining fields, and the three restraint statuses. It stops before the 4.85
predicate assigning three kinds of result. Two witness irregularities are
recorded explicitly.
