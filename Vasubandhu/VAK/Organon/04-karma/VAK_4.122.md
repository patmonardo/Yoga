# VAK_4.122

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दौःशील्यमशुभं रूपं शीलं तद्विरतिर्द्विधा ।
प्रतिक्षिप्ताच्च बुद्धेन विशुद्धं तु चतुर्गुणम् ॥ ४.१२२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

**Bhāṣya resolution of `dvidhā`**

```text
vijñapti
    manifest bodily or verbal activity by which one refrains

avijñapti
    the continuing unmanifest restraint constituted by that abstention
```

These two modes apply both to intrinsic misconduct and, for one who has
undertaken the training, to conduct prohibited by the Buddha.

## 4. Grammar

**Definition of misconduct and discipline**

```text
dauḥśīlyam
    aśubhaṃ rūpam [asti]

śīlam
    tasya viratiḥ [asti]
```

> Misconduct is unwholesome bodily-verbal form; discipline is abstention from
> it.

The genitival relation within `tadviratiḥ` gives the object from which one
refrains. The Bhāṣya treats `virati` as positively constituted restraint, not
mere accidental non-occurrence.

**Two modes and extended domain**

```text
sā viratiḥ dvidhā [bhavati]
    vijñaptiḥ avijñaptiḥ ca

buddhena pratikṣiptāt api [viratiḥ śīlam]
```

The first statement divides restraint by mode. The second extends the domain
of restraint to training prohibitions. They are distinct classifications and
must not be collapsed.

**Purified discipline**

```text
viśuddhaṃ [śīlam]
    caturguṇam [bhavati]
```

The contrastive `tu` marks purified discipline as more than basic abstention.
The four qualities are supplied by the ensuing Bhāṣya explanation.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Misconduct is unwholesome bodily-verbal form; discipline is abstention from
> it, and that abstention is twofold. It is also abstention from what has been
> prohibited by the Buddha. Purified discipline, however, possesses four
> qualities.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Misconduct is unwholesome bodily or verbal form, and discipline is a
> determinate abstention from it. Restraint has two modes: the manifest action
> by which one refrains and the continuing unmanifest restraint established
> through that abstention. Both also apply to conduct that is not intrinsically
> misconduct but has been prohibited by the Buddha for one who has undertaken
> the training. Purified discipline is undamaged by misconduct and by its
> afflictive causes, rests upon the antidote through the establishments of
> mindfulness, and is directed toward nirvāṇa rather than a superior rebirth.

The manifest/unmanifest division, the adopted-training qualification, the
example of improper-time eating, and the four qualities of purification are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Discipline is not the empty absence of harmful output but a practical form
> that actively prevents such output from becoming actual. It exists both in
> visible restraint and as a continuing determination of the agent. Purified
> discipline goes further: it secures the deed-level boundary, transforms the
> afflictive generator, grounds itself in a counter-principle of mindful
> discernment, and directs the entire system toward liberation rather than a
> conditioned reward.

The fourfold purification moves as follows:

```text
freedom from misconduct
    → freedom from its afflictive causes
    → grounding in its antidote
    → transformation toward nirvāṇa
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dauḥśīlya` — misconduct**

Defined here as unwholesome bodily-verbal form. The definition concerns
actual karmic expression, not merely an inward moral defect.

**`Śīla` — discipline**

Determinate abstention from misconduct and, within an adopted training, from
what the Buddha has prohibited.

**`Virati` — abstention or restraint**

A positively constituted prevention of transgression. Failure to act through
accident or lack of opportunity is not thereby discipline.

**`Vijñapti` — manifest restraint**

The bodily or verbal activity by which abstention is enacted and made
communicatively manifest.

**`Avijñapti` — unmanifest continuing restraint**

The persisting karmic determination that is the abstention. The Bhāṣya uses
the Chapter 4 distinction to explain the twofold mode of discipline.

**`Prakṛtyā dauḥśīlya` — misconduct by nature**

Conduct intrinsically unwholesome. This domain is distinguished from rules
whose binding force arises within an adopted discipline.

**`Buddhena pratikṣipta` — prohibited by the Buddha**

Training conduct such as eating at an improper time, which is not misconduct
by nature but becomes a transgression for one who has undertaken that
training.

**`Samāttaśikṣa` — one who has undertaken the training**

The agent-relative qualification that explains why violation of a training
rule becomes misconduct without converting the rule into intrinsic evil.

**`Smṛtyupasthāna` — establishment of mindfulness**

The counter-principle supporting purified discipline. Restraint is grounded
in systematic mindful discernment rather than sheer external inhibition.

**`Nirvāṇapariṇāmita` — transformed or directed toward nirvāṇa**

The teleological quality of purified discipline. Its end is liberation, not
merely acquisition of a superior rebirth.

## 8. Logical Determination

The basic definitions are:

```text
UnwholesomeBodilyVerbalForm(x) → Misconduct(x)
DeterminateAbstention(a, x)     → Discipline(a)
```

Restraint has two modes:

```text
Discipline(a)
    → ManifestRestraint(a)
    + ContinuingUnmanifestRestraint(a)
```

Its domain has two grounds:

```text
IntrinsicMisconduct(x)
    → proper object of restraint generally

ProhibitedByBuddha(x)
    + UndertookTraining(a)
    → proper object of restraint for a
```

Purified discipline requires four determinations:

```text
NotDamagedBy(Misconduct)
NotDamagedBy(CausesOfMisconduct)
GroundedIn(AntidoteThroughMindfulness)
DirectedToward(Nirvana)
```

The same visible restraint may therefore have different logical status
according to its cause, supporting principle, and end.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The Bhāṣya explicitly closes the giving-based merit discussion and begins the
merit-basis consisting in discipline. The movement is from positive transfer
to determinate restraint, but restraint is itself a karmic action-form:

```text
possible misconduct
    → recognition and prohibition
    → manifest refusal
    → continuing practical restraint
```

The four qualities of purified discipline align closely with the project's
Kriyā Yoga architecture:

```text
output      → no misconduct
generator   → no kleśa-based corruption
discipline  → smṛtyupasthāna as counter-principle
telos       → nirvāṇa rather than conditioned becoming
```

This is stronger than inhibition. Purified `śīla` is an action-generating
instrument whose production, causal basis, corrective discipline, and end
have been transformed. In the language of the conditioned dialectic, basic
restraint negates a prohibited course, while mindfulness and nirvāṇa give
that negation a positive principle and liberative direction.

The distinction between intrinsic misconduct and adopted training is also
essential for Practical Reason. A universal ethical prohibition and a rule of
a freely undertaken discipline are both binding, but their grounds differ.
Schema Inference must retain that difference when applying the rule within a
specific Bhūmi.

This Organon interpretation is reconstructive. The source doctrine defines
misconduct, the two modes and two domains of restraint, and the four qualities
of purified discipline.
