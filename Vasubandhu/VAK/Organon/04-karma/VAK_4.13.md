# VAK_4.13

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अविज्ञप्तिस्त्रिधा ज्ञेया संवरासंवरेतरा ।
>
> संवरः प्रातिमोक्षाख्यो ध्यानजोऽनास्रवस्तथा ॥ ४.१३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> avijñaptis tridhā jñeyā saṃvarāsaṃvaretarā /
>
> saṃvaraḥ prātimokṣākhyo dhyānajo 'nāsravas tathā // 4.13 //

VAK 4.01–4.12 analyzed the constitution, support, ethical classification, and
causal functioning of bodily and verbal karma.

VAK 4.13 turns toward stable practical discipline.

It first divides `avijñapti` into three classes and then divides restraint into
three modes.

```text
avijñapti
    → restraint
    → non-restraint
    → neither

restraint
    → prātimokṣa
    → meditation-born
    → uncontaminated
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
avijñaptiḥ                → avijñaptis before tr-
tridhā                    → tridhā
jñeyā                     → jñeyā
saṃvara                   → saṃvara
asaṃvara                  → asaṃvara
itarā                     → itarā
saṃvaraḥ                  → saṃvaraḥ
prātimokṣa-ākhyaḥ         → prātimokṣākhyaḥ
dhyāna-jaḥ                → dhyānajaḥ
anāsravaḥ                 → 'nāsravaḥ after -o
tathā                     → tathā
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| avijñaptiḥ | nominative feminine singular | non-manifest action / non-disclosive form |
| tridhā | indeclinable adverb | in three ways |
| jñeyā | feminine nominative singular gerundive of `√jñā` | to be known or understood |
| saṃvara | compound/list member | restraint; active containment |
| asaṃvara | compound/list member | non-restraint; contrary classified orientation |
| itarā | feminine nominative singular | the other; Bhāṣya: neither restraint nor non-restraint |
| prātimokṣākhya | nominative masculine singular compound adjective | called `prātimokṣa` |
| dhyānaja | nominative masculine singular compound adjective | born from meditation |
| anāsrava | nominative masculine singular adjective | without outflows; uncontaminated |
| tathā | adverb | likewise; also |

The Bhāṣya resolves the compact `saṃvarāsaṃvaretarā` explicitly:

```text
saṃvaraḥ
asaṃvaraḥ
tābhyāṃ itaraḥ
    na eva saṃvaraḥ
    na asaṃvaraḥ
```

The third class is neither of the two poles. It should not be translated merely
as a vague “other.”

`Prātimokṣa` is retained as a technical term. Its internal divisions begin in
the following verse.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first predication is:

```text
avijñaptiḥ
    tridhā
    jñeyā

non-manifest action
    is to be understood
    as threefold
```

The following compressed list supplies the three members:

```text
saṃvara
asaṃvara
itarā
```

The second line carries an understood threefold predication:

```text
saṃvaraḥ [trividhaḥ]
    prātimokṣa-ākhyaḥ
    dhyāna-jaḥ
    anāsravaḥ tathā

restraint [is threefold]:
    called prātimokṣa,
    born from meditation,
    and likewise uncontaminated
```

The Bhāṣya then maps these three forms onto `śīla`:

```text
prātimokṣa-saṃvara
    → kāmāvacara śīla

dhyāna-saṃvara
    → rūpāvacara śīla

anāsrava-saṃvara
    → anāsrava śīla
```

These domain identifications belong to the commentary, not to the explicit
words of the kārikā.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Non-manifest action is to be understood as threefold: restraint,
> non-restraint, and the other. Restraint is that called `prātimokṣa`, that born
> from meditation, and likewise the uncontaminated.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Non-manifest action has three practical forms: restraint, non-restraint, and
> that which is neither restraint nor non-restraint. Restraint actively checks
> the spread of misconduct. It is threefold: `prātimokṣa` restraint, identified
> with desire-domain discipline; meditation-born restraint, identified with
> form-domain discipline; and uncontaminated restraint, identified with
> uncontaminated discipline.

The definition through blocking misconduct and the three `śīla` identifications
are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Practical determination is not exhausted by presently visible action.
> Restraint can remain operative as a continuing inhibition against misconduct;
> non-restraint forms its contrary class, while a third field belongs to
> neither. Restraint itself may be constituted through formal discipline,
> generated through meditative attainment, or realized as an uncontaminated
> Path-determination.

The verse thus moves from the causal architecture of action into the forms by
which conduct is stably governed.

| anāsrava | without outflows / uncontaminated | status of the third restraint |

Three distinctions must remain clear:

```text
saṃvara
    ≠ accidental non-performance

asaṃvara
    ≠ merely not possessing restraint

neither
    ≠ a weakened mixture of the two poles
```

## 8. Logical Determination

### The first triad

```text
Avijñapti =
    Saṃvara
    or Asaṃvara
    or Neither
```

The third member proves that `asaṃvara` cannot be treated as the simple logical
complement of `saṃvara`:

```text
not saṃvara
    includes
        asaṃvara
        and neither
```

The exact positive constitution of `asaṃvara` remains for the subsequent
verses.

### The functional definition of restraint

The Bhāṣya gives:

```text
dauḥśīlya-prasara
    → possible extension of misconduct

saṃvaraṇa / saṃrodha
    → checking that extension

therefore
saṃvara
    → continuing inhibitory determination
```

Restraint is not identical with one presently manifest abstention. It governs
whether misconduct is allowed to extend into action.

### The second triad

| Restraint | Mode of constitution | Discipline/domain |
|---|---|---|
| `prātimokṣa` | formal disciplinary restraint | desire-domain `śīla` |
| meditation-born | generated through dhyāna | form-domain `śīla` |
| uncontaminated | generated through the uncontaminated Path | uncontaminated `śīla` |

The classification is therefore not based only on prohibited content. It also
asks how the restraint is constituted and within what field it functions.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### The transition into Discipline

VAK 4.13 confirms the structural transition anticipated after 4.12.

The opening twelve verses established how an action is generated, expressed,
continued, materially supported, ethically classified, and distributed across
cognitive functions.

The present verse asks how practical determination becomes restraint.

```text
action-process
    → stable ethical orientation
        → discipline
```

This is why `avijñapti` remains central. A discipline cannot exist only during
the instant in which a visible abstention occurs. It must continue to govern
conduct when no corresponding act is presently manifest.

### Contact with yama and niyama

The strongest Yoga comparison here is with `yama`.

The Bhāṣya defines `saṃvara` negatively but actively:

```text
misconduct tends to spread
    → restraint checks its extension
```

This resembles the prohibitive and containing force of the yamas: non-killing,
truthfulness, non-stealing, sexual discipline, and non-grasping organize what
must not be allowed to extend into action.

VAK 4.13 does not yet present a corresponding list of positive observances
comparable to Yoga's `niyamas`. Therefore:

```text
saṃvara block
    → strong yama contact

saṃvara block
    ≠ automatically the complete yama-niyama system
```

The comparison remains Organon/Yoga interpretation. Vasubandhu's terms here
are `saṃvara` and `śīla`.

### Three grounds of discipline

The three restraints also prevent discipline from being reduced to a rule
externally imposed upon action.

```text
prātimokṣa
    → discipline established formally

dhyāna
    → discipline generated by transformation of the contemplative field

anāsrava Path
    → discipline belonging to uncontaminated realization
```

The same practical content may therefore possess different causal and
contemplative grounds.

This is enough Organon Light for the present verse. The following units can
develop the precise forms of restraint without repeating the deep metaphysical
architecture already established.
