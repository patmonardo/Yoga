# VAK_4.74

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अगम्यगमनं काममिथ्याचारश्चतुर्विधः ।
अन्यथासंज्ञिनो वाक्यमर्थाभिज्ञे मृषावचः ॥ ४.७४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
agamyagamanaṃ kāmamithyācāraś caturvidhaḥ /
anyathāsaṃjñino vākyam arthābhijñe mṛṣāvacaḥ // 4.74 //
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
a-gamya-gamanam | kāma-mithyā-ācāraḥ | catur-vidhaḥ |
anyathā-saṃjñinaḥ | vākyam | artha-abhijñe | mṛṣā-vacaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `a-gamya-gamanam` | nominative neuter singular compound | going where or to whom one should not sexually approach |
| `kāma-mithyā-ācāraḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | wrong conduct in the sexual domain |
| `catur-vidhaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | fourfold |
| `anyathā-saṃjñinaḥ` | genitive masculine singular | of one whose recognition is otherwise |
| `vākyam` | nominative neuter singular | statement; meaning-bearing utterance |
| `artha-abhijñe` | locative masculine singular | in relation to one who understands the meaning |
| `mṛṣā-vacaḥ` | nominative neuter singular compound | false speech |

**`Agamya` is relational**

The privative compound does not describe intrinsic impurity in a person. It
marks an approach rendered impermissible by the relation, bodily mode, place,
or time specified in the Bhāṣya.

**Recognition, statement, and understanding**

The false-speech definition coordinates three terms:

```text
speaker's saṃjñā
speaker's vākya
hearer's arthābhijñā
```

Sound production alone does not satisfy this syntax of completion.

## 4. Grammar

**Sexual misconduct**

```text
agamya-gamanam
    kāma-mithyā-ācāraḥ [bhavati]

saḥ
    catur-vidhaḥ [bhavati]
```

> Going where one should not approach is sexual misconduct; it is fourfold.

The neuter definition is predicated of the masculine course-name. The Bhāṣya
expands `caturvidhaḥ` into four dimensions of impermissibility.

**False speech**

```text
anyathā-saṃjñinaḥ
    vākyam
    artha-abhijñe
        mṛṣā-vacaḥ [bhavati]
```

> The statement of one whose recognition is otherwise, when addressed to one
> who understands its meaning, is false speech.

The genitive qualifies the speaker through the divergence between inward
recognition and statement. The locative gives the recipient-side condition:
the hearer understands the meaning asserted.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Going where one should not approach is sexual misconduct, and it is
> fourfold. The statement of one whose recognition is otherwise, when the
> hearer understands its meaning, is false speech.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Sexual misconduct is sexual activity violating one of four governing
> conditions recognized by the commentary: the person or relation, bodily
> locus, place, or time. The inherited non-mistake condition also applies,
> although the Bhāṣya reports disagreement about cases in which intended and
> actual partners do not coincide. False speech is completed when a speaker
> knowingly states a meaning contrary to their own recognition and the hearer
> understands the asserted meaning. If comprehension does not occur, the
> speech is classified instead as disordered speech.

The four dimensions, their historically specific cases, the disputed
object-recognition analysis, and the treatment of failed comprehension belong
to the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Bodily and verbal acts receive their practical determination only within the
> relations they realize. Sexual activity is classified through the governing
> interpersonal, bodily, spatial, and temporal conditions of its performance.
> False speech is a semantic act in which the speaker deliberately produces
> for another a meaning divergent from the speaker's own recognition. Its
> completion therefore lies not in sound alone but in successfully constituted
> understanding.

The two definitions converge:

```text
bodily action
    becomes determinate within a governed relational field

verbal action
    becomes determinate within a completed semantic relation
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Agamya` — impermissible to approach**

Relationally or circumstantially prohibited within the Bhāṣya's received
disciplinary classification. It should not be translated as though the person
were intrinsically impure or ontologically forbidden.

**`Kāma-mithyācāra` — sexual misconduct**

Wrongly conducted sexual activity. The defect lies in violation of a
governing condition, not in sexuality as such.

**The Bhāṣya's fourfold classification**

```text
agamya
    impermissible person or relation

anaṅga
    impermissible bodily locus

adeśa
    impermissible place

akāla
    impermissible time
```

The prose illustrates these with kinship, protection and partnership norms;
bodily modes; public and sacred places; pregnancy, nursing, and observance.
These examples are a historical doctrinal witness and remain visibly
attributed to the Bhāṣya.

**`Abhrānti` — non-mistaken object relation**

Carried forward from VAK 4.73. If an actual prohibited partner is mistakenly
taken to be one's permitted partner, the Bhāṣya denies the completed course.
For the converse—approaching one person while taking them to be a prohibited
partner—it reports two views: one finds completion through intention plus use
of the basis; another finds preparation only, by analogy with killing.

**`Saṃjñā` — recognition**

The speaker's apprehension of the matter. `Anyathāsaṃjñin` recognizes the
matter otherwise than the statement represents it.

**`Vākya` — statement**

A structured, meaning-bearing utterance. It may consist of many phonetic units
and is not reducible to a single sound considered in isolation.

**`Arthābhijña` — understanding the meaning**

The recipient who grasps what the statement asserts. Hearing sounds without
understanding their meaning does not complete false speech under this rule.

**`Mṛṣāvacas` / `mṛṣāvāda` — false speech**

Speech whose asserted meaning diverges from the speaker's own recognition and
is successfully understood by the recipient.

**`Varṇa` — phonetic unit**

The Bhāṣya asks which unit of a multi-unit statement bears the principal
course. Earlier units are preparation; the final unit at which the hearer
understands, together with `avijñapti`, constitutes completion.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Four-dimensional sexual classification**

```text
sexual activity
    × person or governing relation
    × bodily locus
    × place
    × time
    × object-recognition
        → determinate practical status
```

The same generic bodily activity is not self-classifying. Its karmic identity
depends upon the complete relational context specified by the received
discipline.

**The object-recognition dispute remains open**

```text
actual prohibited basis
    + recognized as permitted
        → no completed course

actual different basis
    + recognized as prohibited
        → view one: completed course
        → view two: preparation only
```

The Bhāṣya reports rather than resolves the second disagreement; the official
study therefore preserves both positions.

**Constitutive conditions of false speech**

```text
speaker recognizes P
    + speaker asserts Q
    + P and Q diverge
    + hearer understands Q
        = completed mṛṣāvāda
```

This distinguishes three cases:

```text
speaker mistakenly believes Q and states Q
    = error, not false speech by this definition

speaker recognizes P and states contrary Q
but hearer does not understand Q
    = not completed false speech;
      classified as disordered speech by the Bhāṣya

speaker recognizes P and states contrary Q
and hearer understands Q
    = completed false speech
```

**The temporal structure of a statement**

```text
earlier phonetic units
    = prayoga

final meaning-completing unit
    + avijñapti
    = maula karmapatha
```

Semantic production and semantic completion are distinct moments.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Verbal karma is semantic action**

False speech cannot be reconstructed from acoustic output alone. Its complete
form relates:

```text
inward recognition
    → deliberately divergent statement
    → recipient comprehension
```

The falsehood lies in what the speaker makes another understand in relation to
what the speaker recognizes. The hearer is therefore not an incidental
external observer but a constitutive pole of the completed speech-act.

**Meaning emerges through the statement**

A multi-unit statement becomes practically effective only when its parts form
an intelligible whole for the recipient. The Bhāṣya's distinction between
preparatory `varṇa` and the completion-bearing final unit prevents the
name-complex of VAK 4.71 from collapsing into isolated words or sounds.

**Historical norms and systematic form**

The specific sexual prohibitions belong to the Bhāṣya's received social and
disciplinary world. Their systematic contribution is the four-dimensional
determination of conduct through relation, embodiment, place, and time. The
study records the witness accurately without silently converting every
historical example into a contemporary norm.

This is the Organon contact. The fourfold classification, examples,
object-recognition dispute, comprehension rule, and phonetic completion point
belong to the Bhāṣya; the formulation as relational and semantic Practical
Form is the project synthesis.
