# VAK_4.11

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> प्रवर्तकं दृष्टिहेयं विज्ञानमुभयं पुनः ।
>
> मानसं भावनाहेयं पञ्चकं त्वनुवर्तकम् ॥ ४.११ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pravartakaṃ dṛṣṭiheyaṃ vijñānam ubhayaṃ punaḥ /
>
> mānasaṃ bhāvanāheyaṃ pañcakaṃ tv anuvartakam // 4.11 //

VAK 4.10 distinguished two functional Aspects of action-origination:

```text
pravartaka
    → initiator

anuvartaka
    → continuator during performance
```

VAK 4.11 distributes kinds of cognition across those functions.

The Bhāṣya completes the distribution as an explicit fourfold alternative:

```text
initiator only
continuator only
both
neither
```

The verse therefore crosses three classifications:

```text
kind of cognition
    ×
Path-mode of abandonment
    ×
function within action
```


| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| pravartakam | nominative neuter singular | initiating; setting action into operation |
| dṛṣṭi-heya | nominative neuter singular compound adjective | to be abandoned by seeing; Bhāṣya: `darśana-prahātavya` |
| vijñānam | nominative neuter singular | knowledge / differentiated cognition |
| ubhayam | nominative neuter singular | both functions |
| punaḥ | connective adverb | in turn; again |
| mānasam | nominative neuter singular adjective used substantivally | mental; Bhāṣya: mental knowledge, `manovijñāna` |
| bhāvanā-heya | nominative neuter singular compound adjective | to be abandoned by cultivation |
| pañcakam | nominative neuter singular collective | the fivefold group of sensory cognitions |
| tu | contrastive particle | but; whereas |
| anuvartakam | nominative neuter singular | continuing; accompanying action during execution |

### Controlled translation of `vijñāna`

`Vijñāna` is not translated here with the project's controlled term
“consciousness,” which is reserved for `citta` when that distinction matters.

The present passage differentiates:

```text
citta
    → the mental occurrence considered as a functioning state

vijñāna
    → knowledge or differentiated cognition classified by faculty
```

Accordingly, the readable translation uses “cognition,” while the vocabulary
table preserves “knowledge / differentiated cognition.”

### The scope of `pañcakam`

The Bhāṣya resolves the collective form:

```text
pañca vijñāna-kāyāḥ
    the five sensory cognition-bodies
```

These are the visual, auditory, olfactory, gustatory, and bodily sensory
cognitions.

### Kārikā compression and Bhāṣya completion

The verse explicitly states three members:

```text
seeing-abandoned cognition
cultivation-abandoned mental cognition
five sensory cognition-bodies
```

The fourth member—uncontaminated cognition—is supplied by the Bhāṣya when it
formulates the complete `catuṣkoṭika`.

It therefore belongs in the Bhāṣya-informed translation and logical
determination, not as an unmarked word of the kārikā.

### Witness caution

The prose witness alternates `pravartaka` and `pravarttaka`. The kārikā spelling
`pravartaka` is used analytically without altering the research witness.

The difficult objection concerning the Path-classification of produced form is
preserved as an argument. It should not be simplified into a claim that every
predicate of a cause transfers to its effect.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first pāda is a direct predication:

```text
dṛṣṭi-heyaṃ vijñānam
    pravartakam

cognition to be abandoned by seeing
    is initiating
```

The Bhāṣya restates it with controlled prose terminology:

```text
darśana-prahātavyaṃ cittam
    vijñapteḥ pravartakam

a mind to be abandoned by seeing
    initiates manifestation
```

It initiates indirectly by functioning as the ground of the `vitarka` and
`vicāra` that immediately generate manifestation.

The middle construction assigns both predicates:

```text
bhāvanā-heyaṃ mānasaṃ [vijñānam]
    ubhayam

mental cognition to be abandoned by cultivation
    is both
```

“Both” means:

```text
pravartakam ca
anuvartakam ca

initiating
and continuing
```

The final construction is contrastive:

```text
pañcakam tu
    anuvartakam

the fivefold [sensory cognition group], however,
    is continuing
```

The Bhāṣya adds the fourth logical position:

```text
anāsravam [vijñānam]
    na ubhayam

uncontaminated cognition
    is neither
```

Here `na ubhayam` means neither initiator nor continuator within this
classification of contaminated bodily and verbal action.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Cognition to be abandoned by seeing is initiating. Mental cognition to be
> abandoned by cultivation is, in turn, both; the fivefold [sensory cognition
> group], however, is continuing.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A cognition abandoned through seeing functions only as an initiator of
> manifest action: it grounds the initial and sustained thought that project
> the deed, but it is no longer present when the outward performance occurs.
> Mental cognition abandoned through cultivation can both initiate an action
> and accompany its execution. The five sensory cognition-bodies can accompany
> an ongoing performance but cannot initiate it. Uncontaminated cognition
> occupies neither causal role in this classification.

The exclusive “only” assignments, the causal mediation through `vitarka` and
`vicāra`, the outward-facing mind during performance, and uncontaminated
cognition as neither are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> One action-process need not express one numerically continuous cognition.
> A cognition may establish the causal orientation from which the action
> begins and then cease before the outward deed is performed. Another mental
> or sensory cognition can accompany the execution. Practical unity therefore
> belongs to the organized action, while initiation and continuation are
> distributed across different cognitive Aspects.

The causal pipeline is unified without being homogeneous.

```text
one practical determination
    → may be projected by one cognitive function
    → maintained by another
    → materially expressed without inheriting every predicate of its cause
```

This is a stronger account of persistence than the assumption that the same
inner state must remain numerically present throughout the deed.

## 8. Logical Determination

### The explicit `catuṣkoṭika`

The Bhāṣya gives a complete fourfold matrix:

| Cognitive determination | Initiator | Continuator |
|---|---:|---:|
| cognition abandoned by seeing | yes | no |
| five sensory cognition-bodies | no | yes |
| cultivation-abandoned mental cognition | yes | yes |
| uncontaminated cognition | no | no |

In formal notation:

```text
P(x) = x functions as pravartaka
A(x) = x functions as anuvartaka

P ∧ ¬A
    → darśana-prahātavya citta

¬P ∧ A
    → five sensory vijñāna-kāyas

P ∧ A
    → bhāvanā-heya manovijñāna

¬P ∧ ¬A
    → anāsrava vijñāna
```

The four alternatives are exhaustive relative to these two functional
predicates.

They are not four metaphysical worlds.

### Why seeing-abandoned cognition initiates

The Bhāṣya gives a mediated causal structure:

```text
darśana-prahātavya citta
    → functions as nidāna
        → for vitarka and vicāra
            → which originate vijñapti
```

Thus the initial cognition need not be the immediate producer present during
the action.

It is `hetu-samutthāna`: the causal ground that projects the sequence.

### Why it does not continue

At the time of bodily or verbal performance:

```text
the initiating inward cognition
    → is absent

an outward-facing citta
    → is present
    → accompanies the performed action
```

Therefore:

```text
genetic belonging to the action
    ≠ contemporaneous presence during execution
```

The action retains causal unity even though the initiating cognition has
vanished.

### Why sensory cognition cannot initiate

The five sensory cognition-bodies participate in the outward field of
performance.

They can accompany an action already under way:

```text
visual cognition
auditory cognition
olfactory cognition
gustatory cognition
bodily cognition
    → possible anuvartaka functions
```

But they do not supply the mental projection that sets the deed into operation.

Their role is operative rather than projective.

### Why mental cognition abandoned by cultivation is both

Mental cognition has the functional range to:

```text
form and project the practical determination
    → pravartaka

remain present in outward execution
    → anuvartaka
```

The same type of cognition can therefore occupy both Aspects, though the
functions remain logically distinguishable.

### Why causal classification does not transfer to form

The objector presses a difficult inference:

```text
a form is generated by cognition abandoned by seeing

therefore
    the produced form should itself be abandoned by seeing
```

The Bhāṣya blocks this result.

Form is not classified as abandoned by seeing. As an undefiled determination,
it is not opposed to knowledge or ignorance in the manner required for that
Path-classification.

The key logical correction is:

```text
cause possesses predicate Q
    and produces effect y

does not entail

effect y possesses predicate Q
```

The relation of production must determine which predicates transmit.

`Hetu-samutthāna` transmits projective causal orientation. It does not transmit
every ontological or Path-classification of the initiating cognition to the
material product.

### Scriptural and Abhidharma levels

The scriptural sequence from wrong view to wrong action speaks at the level of
causal genesis:

```text
wrong view
    → wrong conceptual determination
        → wrong speech
        → wrong bodily action
```

The Abhidharma classification speaks at the level of immediate functional role:

```text
what initiates
what accompanies
what is abandoned by seeing
what is abandoned by cultivation
```

Once `hetu-samutthāna` is distinguished from `tat-kṣaṇa-samutthāna`, the two
accounts no longer conflict.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### One deed, several cognitive Aspects

VAK 4.11 decisively rejects the picture of action as the outward expression of
one simple inner state that remains numerically identical from intention to
completion.

The Bhāṣya instead gives:

```text
initiating cognition
    → grounds vitarka and vicāra

vitarka and vicāra
    → project manifest action

outward-facing mental or sensory cognition
    → accompanies execution

material form
    → realizes bodily or verbal performance
```

The practical determination is one.

The cognitive stations through which it becomes actual are many.

That is the exact sense in which Dharma arranges itself as functional pipelines.

### Persistence after the initiating cognition vanishes

This verse returns us to the central problem of persistence in a kṣaṇa-dominated
field.

The cognition abandoned by seeing initiates the action but is absent during
the outward performance.

If persistence required numerical endurance of the initiating appearance, the
action would lose its unity as soon as that cognition vanished.

But the Bhāṣya does not infer such a loss.

It distinguishes causal function:

```text
the initiating cognition vanishes

its projective determination remains effective
    through vitarka-vicāra mediation
    and through a later operative cognition
```

What persists is not one appearance.

What persists is the practical determination as it reorganizes itself across
functional Aspects.

This is an especially clear case of Essence beneath vanishing Appearance.

### Aspect is not predicate contagion

Functional unity does not mean that every determination flows indiscriminately
through the pipeline.

The Path-status of the initiating cognition does not become the Path-status of
the produced form.

```text
projective determination
    → transmits through the causal relation

classification as abandoned by seeing
    → does not transmit to material form
```

An Aspect identifies which function is active and therefore which
determination is validly carried forward.

This is why Aspect analysis is stricter than saying that everything is
interconnected.

It asks:

```text
Which relation is operative?

Which determination does that relation transmit?

Which determination remains local to its bearer?
```

### The sensory field continues but does not project

The five sensory cognitions are `anuvartaka` only.

This gives the differentiated perceptual field a precise station in action.

Sensory cognition participates in the performance already under way. It
registers and accompanies the outward field through which the deed is
executed.

But the senses do not by themselves project the practical unity of the deed.

```text
mental cognition
    → can determine the deed as a whole

sensory cognition
    → participates in its differentiated execution
```

This closely approaches our distinction between the Principle that organizes
a field and the opened eye that receives its differentiated appearances.

The comparison is Organon-level. The textual claim is the narrower functional
distribution of `manovijñāna` and the five `vijñāna-kāyas`.

### The fourfold alternative as pipeline completeness

The `catuṣkoṭika` is not merely a scholastic display.

It tests every possible relation between two functions:

```text
initiation without continuation
continuation without initiation
both functions
neither function
```

This demonstrates that the two Aspects are independent enough to be
distinguished but coordinated enough to define one complete functional field.

In Organon terms:

```text
the Concept divides its causal functions

the fourfold matrix exhausts their possible distributions

the action-process unifies the distributed functions
```

We need not turn this into a rigid Hegelian identity. The Bhāṣya already
performs the required logical work.

### The limits of the present block

The user's proposed chapter map remains plausible:

```text
4.01–4.12
    → difficult architecture of karma and its causal-functional modes

4.13 onward
    → anticipated transition toward restraints
       comparable at the Organon level with yama and niyama
```

VAK 4.11 strongly belongs to the first block. It refines the machinery of
origination rather than yet giving the discipline of restraint itself.

The transition should be confirmed from 4.12 and 4.13 rather than asserted in
advance as kārikā terminology.
