# VAK_4.88

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अनास्रवं पुनः शेषं त्रिभिरव्याकृतं च यत् ।
चत्वारि द्वे तथा त्रीणि कुशलस्य शुभादयः ॥ ४.८८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
anāsravaṃ punaḥ śeṣaṃ tribhir avyākṛtaṃ ca yat /
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
an-āsravam | punaḥ | śeṣam | tribhiḥ | avyākṛtam | ca | yat |
catvāri | dve | tathā | trīṇi | kuśalasya | śubha-ādayaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `anāsravam` | nominative neuter singular | uncontaminated karma |
| `punaḥ` | indeclinable | in turn; continuing the classification |
| `śeṣam` | nominative neuter singular | the remainder; uncontaminated karma outside the immediate path of abandoning |
| `tribhiḥ` | instrumental plural numeral, with `phalaiḥ` understood | with three results |
| `avyākṛtam` | nominative neuter singular | indeterminate karma, neither wholesome nor unwholesome |
| `ca` | coordinating particle | and, also |
| `yat` | relative nominative neuter singular | whatever karma is indeterminate |
| `catvāri` | nominative neuter plural | four results or fruit-relations |
| `dve` | nominative neuter dual | two results or fruit-relations |
| `tathā` | indeclinable | likewise; then in the ordered series |
| `trīṇi` | nominative neuter plural | three results or fruit-relations |
| `kuśalasya` | genitive singular | of wholesome karma |
| `śubhādayaḥ` | nominative plural compound | those beginning with wholesome: wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dharmas |

**Two classifications meet in one verse**

The first half completes the result-profiles begun in VAK 4.87:

```text
remaining uncontaminated karma  → three results
indeterminate karma             → three results
```

The second half begins a new matrix:

```text
wholesome karma as cause
    → wholesome resulting dharmas:     four relations
    → unwholesome resulting dharmas:   two relations
    → indeterminate resulting dharmas: three relations
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the exact memberships and confirms that the numbers are
to be read respectively.

## 4. Grammar

**Residual uncontaminated and indeterminate karma**

```text
śeṣam anāsravaṃ [karma]
    tribhiḥ [phalaiḥ sa-phalam]

yat ca avyākṛtaṃ [karma]
    [tat api tribhiḥ phalaiḥ sa-phalam]
```

> Remaining uncontaminated karma has three results, and whatever karma is
> indeterminate likewise has three.

`Śeṣam` is determined retrospectively: it excludes the uncontaminated
immediate path of abandoning already classified in 4.87. In both residual
classes the Bhāṣya excludes maturation and disconnection.

**The ordered quality-series**

```text
kuśalasya [karmaṇaḥ]
    śubhādayaḥ [dharmāḥ phalāni bhavanti]
    catvāri
    dve
    tathā trīṇi
    [anukramam]
```

> For wholesome karma, dharmas beginning with the wholesome are results in
> four, two, and three ways respectively.

`Śubhādayaḥ` initiates the conventional ordered series wholesome,
unwholesome, and indeterminate. The Bhāṣya makes the distributive force of the
three numerals explicit through `anukramam`, “respectively.”

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Remaining uncontaminated karma, and whatever karma is indeterminate, have
> three results. For wholesome karma, those beginning with the wholesome are
> four, two, and three respectively.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Uncontaminated karma other than the immediate path of abandoning, and
> indeterminate karma, each stand in three fruit-relations: correspondence,
> activity-produced result, and dominant result; maturation and disconnection
> are absent. Taking wholesome karma as the causal source, wholesome dharmas
> can be its results in four ways—correspondence, disconnection,
> activity-production, and dominant conditioning. Unwholesome dharmas can be
> related to it in two ways—activity-production and dominant conditioning.
> Indeterminate dharmas can be its results in three ways—maturation,
> activity-production, and dominant conditioning.

The exact memberships, the ordered reading of the counts, and the exclusions
are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A cause does not transmit its ethical quality unchanged into every result.
> Wholesome karma relates differently to wholesome, unwholesome, and
> indeterminate dharmas because causal efficacy is mediated by distinct
> result-forms. Maturation yields an indeterminate resultant dharma;
> disconnection belongs only within the wholesome liberative field; active
> production and dominant conditioning can cross ethical qualities. The truth
> of the cause therefore lies not in an abstract label repeated in its effects,
> but in the articulated system of relations through which it becomes actual.

The new form is:

```text
quality of causal karma
    × quality of resulting dharma
    × mode of causal relation
    = determinate fruit-structure
```

Hegelian-transcendental comprehension shows why this matrix is necessary.
Cause and effect are not fixed things connected by an external arrow; each is
determined through the specific mediation that joins it to the other. The
five fruits name those mediations.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Śeṣam anāsravam` — remaining uncontaminated karma**

Uncontaminated karma other than that of the immediate path of abandoning.
Because it does not generate karmic maturation and does not itself perform the
abandoning operation, it lacks both `vipāka` and `visaṃyoga` results.

**`Avyākṛta` — ethically indeterminate**

Neither wholesome nor unwholesome. This does not mean unintelligible,
unclassified, or causally inert. Indeterminate karma retains correspondence,
activity-produced, and dominant result-relations.

**`Śubhādayaḥ` — the series beginning with wholesome**

A compact ordered reference to wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate
resulting dharmas. It marks the result-quality axis rather than three further
classes of causal karma.

**`Anukramam` — respectively**

The Bhāṣya's explicit distributive instruction. It binds four to wholesome
effects, two to unwholesome effects, and three to indeterminate effects.

**`Vipāka-phala` — maturation result**

In the wholesome-cause row, maturation belongs among indeterminate resulting
dharmas, not wholesome ones. The ethical quality of causal karma and the
quality of its maturation-result must therefore remain distinct.

**`Niṣyanda-phala` — corresponding result**

Belongs here only where wholesome karma is related to wholesome resulting
dharmas. Its similarity-structure does not cross into the unwholesome or
indeterminate result columns.

**`Visaṃyoga-phala` — disconnection-result**

Appears among wholesome resulting dharmas. Disconnection is a positive
liberative determination, not an ethically neutral absence and not a generic
effect of every wholesome action.

**`Puruṣakāra-phala` — activity-produced result**

The broad causal relation through which an operation actively brings forth
dharmas. In this row it can connect wholesome karma with wholesome,
unwholesome, or indeterminate resulting dharmas.

**`Adhipati-phala` — dominant result**

The equally broad relation of governing or conditioning efficacy. Like
`puruṣakāra`, it crosses all three result-quality columns for wholesome karma.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Completion of the general profiles**

Let:

```text
V = vipāka
N = niṣyanda
D = visaṃyoga
P = puruṣakāra
A = adhipati
```

Then:

```text
RemainingUncontaminatedKarma = {N, P, A}
IndeterminateKarma           = {N, P, A}
```

The two profiles have both equal cardinality and equal membership, but remain
different causal classes because contamination-status and ethical quality are
independent determinations.

**Wholesome-cause matrix**

| Resulting dharma-quality | `vipāka` | `niṣyanda` | `visaṃyoga` | `puruṣakāra` | `adhipati` | Count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| wholesome | no | yes | yes | yes | yes | 4 |
| unwholesome | no | no | no | yes | yes | 2 |
| indeterminate | yes | no | no | yes | yes | 3 |

Formally:

```text
FruitRelations(WholesomeCause, WholesomeEffect)
    = {N, D, P, A}

FruitRelations(WholesomeCause, UnwholesomeEffect)
    = {P, A}

FruitRelations(WholesomeCause, IndeterminateEffect)
    = {V, P, A}
```

**Three independent coordinates**

```text
cause-quality
    wholesome / unwholesome / indeterminate

effect-quality
    wholesome / unwholesome / indeterminate

fruit-relation
    maturation / correspondence / disconnection
    / activity-production / dominant conditioning
```

A scalar expression such as `FruitCount(WholesomeKarma) = 4` destroys the
meaning because it suppresses both effect-quality and relation-type.

**Non-equivalences**

```text
ethical quality of cause       ≠ ethical quality of every result
same result-count              ≠ same causal class
effect following a cause       ≠ maturation-result in every case
causal relation                ≠ external link between fixed terms
```

**Breadth of result-relations**

```text
Puruṣakāra and Adhipati
    cross all three result qualities

Niṣyanda
    preserves qualitative correspondence here

Visaṃyoga
    belongs to wholesome liberative determination

Vipāka
    yields indeterminate resultant dharmas
```

The taxonomy differentiates modes of dependence rather than merely kinds of
later events.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.88 marks a major advance in the causal logic of the Karma chapter. Up
to this point, the question could still appear to be a property of one causal
class: how many results does this karma possess? The second half makes clear
that this scalar model is insufficient. A result is always a dharma of some
ethical quality standing to a causal karma through a determinate relation.
The adequate form is a matrix.

This explains how wholesome karma can have unwholesome dharmas among its
results without “turning into” unwholesomeness. They are not its maturation or
corresponding continuation. They stand to it through the broader relations of
active production or dominant conditioning. The fruit-relation mediates the
passage from cause-quality to result-quality; it cannot be omitted without
producing a moralized caricature of causality.

The maturation column is equally instructive. Wholesome karma matures as an
indeterminate resultant dharma. The value of the cause determines the
direction of fruition, but the resultant state is not itself a fresh morally
responsible act. Cause-quality is preserved through transformation rather than
copied as an identical predicate.

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy reveals the five fruits as forms of causal
mediation. The terms joined by a relation do not possess their complete
meaning prior to the relation: wholesome cause, indeterminate maturation,
liberative disconnection, and dominant field-conditioning acquire their
determinate stations within the whole matrix. This is why dictionary entries
cannot disclose the architecture. The Concept is the organized relation of
the determinations.

The transition also deepens the conclusion of Objective Logic. Karma no
longer appears as a linear sequence from act to consequence. It is the
structured network through which qualities, operations, and fields determine
one another. The agent that emerges from this network will enter Anuśaya as
the inwardly conditioned result of these objective mediations.
