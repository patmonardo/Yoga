# VAK_4.86

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
लोभजं कायवाक्कर्म मिथ्याजीवः पृथक्कृतः ।
दुःशोधत्वात् परिष्कारलोभोत्थं चेन्न सूत्रतः ॥ ४.८६ ॥
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis


| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `lobha-jam` | neuter nominative singular compound agreeing with `karma` | born from greed |
| `kāya-vāk-karma` | neuter nominative singular collective compound | bodily or verbal action |
| `mithyā-ājīvaḥ` | masculine nominative singular | wrong livelihood; wrong practical sustaining of life |
| `pṛthak` | indeclinable | separately, distinctly |
| `kṛtaḥ` | masculine nominative singular past passive participle of √`kṛ` | made, treated, classified |
| `duḥ-śodhatvāt` | ablative singular abstract compound | because of being difficult to purify |
| `pariṣkāra` | compound member | requisite, equipment, material support of life |
| `lobha-uttham` | neuter nominative singular compound | arisen from greed |
| `cet` | conditional particle | if it is argued |
| `na` | negative particle | no; not so |
| `sūtrataḥ` | ablative used adverbially | according to the sūtra; on scriptural grounds |

**The understood contrast**

The Bhāṣya begins from the three path expressions:

```text
wrong speech
wrong bodily action
wrong livelihood
```

It immediately denies that the third introduces another action-medium.
Wrong livelihood remains bodily or verbal karma, but is differently
determined by its causal root and practical field.

## 4. Grammar

**Definition and separate treatment**

```text
lobhajaṃ kāya-vāk-karma
    mithyājīvaḥ [asti]

[saḥ]
    pṛthak kṛtaḥ
    duḥśodhatvāt
```

> Bodily or verbal action born from greed is wrong livelihood. It is treated
> separately because it is difficult to purify.

`Mithyājīvaḥ` predicates a practical classification of `karma`; the shift in
grammatical gender follows the masculine predicate noun. `Pṛthak kṛtaḥ` does
not assert a separate substance or expressive faculty.

**Objection and rejection**

```text
[mithyājīvaḥ]
    pariṣkāra-lobhottham [eva]
    cet

na
    sūtrataḥ
```

> If it is claimed that wrong livelihood is only what arises from greed for
> requisites—no, not according to the sūtra.

The restrictive `eva` is supplied by the Bhāṣya's formulation of the
objection. `Na sūtrataḥ` rejects the proposed equivalence because the
scriptural extension includes cases beyond acquisitive pursuit of material
necessities.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Bodily or verbal action born from greed is wrong livelihood. It is treated
> separately because it is difficult to purify. If it is said to be that which
> arises from greed for requisites—no, not according to the sūtra.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Wrong livelihood is not a third kind of expressive action beside wrong
> speech and wrong bodily conduct. It is bodily or verbal action generated by
> greed and is named separately because greed has a dragging power: the citta
> is especially difficult to guard and livelihood especially difficult to
> purify when action proceeds from it. Nor may wrong livelihood be restricted
> to greed-driven acquisition of food, clothing, shelter, medicine, and other
> requisites. The sūtra includes a wider economy of improper engagement and
> enjoyment, exemplified by going to spectacles such as elephant fights.

The dragging character of greed, the pedagogical purpose of separate naming,
the vulnerability created by dependence on support, and the scriptural
example are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Wrong livelihood is not recognizable from the visible shape of an act
> alone. Body and speech become moments of wrong livelihood when greed governs
> their participation in the practical economy through which life sustains
> and enjoys itself. Its separate name marks no new faculty; it makes explicit
> a contradiction within ordinary self-maintenance. The activity by which the
> agent preserves life can simultaneously place that life under an appetitive
> power that is exceptionally difficult to govern.

The determination is therefore relational:

```text
body or speech
    + greed as governing root
    + practical economy of support and enjoyment
    + difficulty of self-correction
    = wrong livelihood
```

In Hegelian-transcendental terms, livelihood is a necessary mediation between
the individual and its social-objective conditions. Greed converts this
mediation into a relation in which the means of sustaining the subject
dominate the subject. Separate ethical classification makes that inversion
thinkable and therefore practically corrigible.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Mithyājīva` — wrong livelihood**

Greed-born bodily or verbal karma within the practical economy of sustaining
and enjoying life. It is wider than fraudulent occupation and narrower than
every instance of greed: medium, root, and practical field jointly determine
the classification.

**`Ājīva` — sustaining life**

The means and activity through which life is maintained. The Bhāṣya widens
the field beyond occupational acquisition to include the use and enjoyment of
objects within an economy of support.

**`Lobhaja` — born from greed**

The causal-root determination. Hatred-born and delusion-born bodily or verbal
actions remain wrong bodily or verbal conduct, while greed-born action in the
relevant practical field receives the additional classification of wrong
livelihood.

**`Hārī` — carrying, dragging, captivating**

The Bhāṣya's characterization of greed. Greed carries the citta along, making
action that arises from it difficult to guard. This supplies the causal reason
for `duḥśodhatva`.

**`Duḥśodhatva` — difficulty of purification**

The reason for separate treatment. The category is pedagogically prominent
because its operation is persistent, difficult to detect, and difficult to
correct—not because it possesses a separate action-substrate.

**`Ādarotpādana` — generating careful attention**

The Bhāṣya's stated purpose for naming wrong livelihood separately. A
classification can be doctrinally necessary because it directs practice
toward a structurally vulnerable field.

**`Pariṣkāra` — requisite or support**

Food, clothing, dwelling, medicine, and other equipment of life. Greed for
such supports is an important instance of wrong livelihood, especially for a
renunciant dependent on others, but it does not exhaust the category.

**`Mithyā-viṣaya-paribhoga` — improper enjoyment or use of an object-field**

The Bhāṣya's reason for including activities such as attendance at elephant
fights. Wrong livelihood concerns not only how supports are acquired but how
the agent practically enters, uses, and enjoys an object-field.

**`Sūtrataḥ` — according to the sūtra**

A constraint on conceptual narrowing. A plausible definition must be revised
when it fails to include cases explicitly assigned to the category in the
scriptural teaching.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Cross-classification**

Wrong livelihood cuts across the bodily/verbal division:

```text
Action A
├── expressive medium
│   ├── bodily
│   └── verbal
├── causal root
│   └── greed
├── practical field
│   └── support, maintenance, use, or enjoyment
└── correction profile
    └── difficult to purify
```

Thus:

```text
separate doctrinal treatment    ≠ separate expressive medium
separate practical category     ≠ separate ontological substrate
surface form of action          ≠ complete ethical determination
wrong livelihood                ≠ merely a socially disapproved job
```

**Root distinction**

```text
body/speech arising from greed
    → wrong livelihood in the relevant practical economy

body/speech arising from hatred or delusion
    → wrong bodily or verbal conduct under those roots
```

The Bhāṣya's contrast identifies the differentiating ground, but it does not
make every greed-born movement automatically wrong livelihood without regard
to the practical field established by the passage.

**Rejected restrictive definition**

```text
proposed:
WrongLivelihood
    ↔ greed-driven acquisition of life-requisites

scriptural counter-instance:
improper engagement with or enjoyment of an object-field
    can also instantiate WrongLivelihood

therefore:
greed for requisites
    ⊂ wider extension of wrong livelihood
```

**Why separate naming is rational**

```text
greed has dragging efficacy
    → citta becomes difficult to guard
    → livelihood becomes difficult to purify
    → explicit classification generates vigilance
    → disciplined correction becomes possible
```

The classification is consequently teleological: its conceptual difference
is justified by the practical work it enables.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.86 demonstrates that ethical meaning cannot be read directly from an
isolated bodily movement or utterance. The same expressive medium receives
different determination according to its root and its station within the
agent's practical relations. Wrong livelihood is therefore a systematic
meaning: body or speech, greed, dependence on support, enjoyment of objects,
difficulty of purification, and the pedagogical need for vigilance determine
one another within the category.

The renunciant case exposes the contradiction sharply. Dependence on others
for support is not itself a fault; it is an objective condition of that form
of life. Yet precisely because bodily survival is mediated by the recognition
and generosity of others, speech and bodily presentation can become
instruments of greed. The necessary social mediation of livelihood can be
inverted into domination by the supports it was meant to mediate.

This is why “wrong livelihood” cannot be reduced to earning money through a
forbidden occupation. Such a gloss isolates a modern institutional form and
misses the Concept. The Bhāṣya ranges across acquisition, dependence,
presentation, use, and enjoyment. Livelihood names the complete practical
relation through which life reproduces itself in an objective and social
field.

Hegelian Transcendental Philosophy reveals the force of the separate naming.
An abstract taxonomy would object that wrong livelihood adds no new action
medium. Practical Reason answers that a difference can be essential because
it expresses an inversion of mediation and demands a distinct discipline of
correction. The category is not ontologically primitive; it is practically
necessary. Its truth lies in the self-transformation it makes possible.
