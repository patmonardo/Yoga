# VAK_4.97

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
त्रिषु द्वीपेष्वानन्तर्यं षण्ढादीनां तु नेष्यते ।
अल्पोपकारालज्जित्वाच्छेषे गतिषु पञ्चसु ॥ ४.९७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
triṣu dvīpeṣv ānantaryaṃ śaṇḍhādīnāṃ tu neṣyate /
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
triṣu | dvīpeṣu | ānantaryam | śaṇḍha-ādīnām | tu | na | iṣyate |
alpa-upakārāt | alajjitvāt | śeṣe | gatiṣu | pañcasu
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `triṣu` | locative masculine plural | in three |
| `dvīpeṣu` | locative masculine plural | continents |
| `ānantaryam` | nominative neuter singular | immediate-result karma as a technical class |
| `śaṇḍhādīnām` | genitive plural | for/of `śaṇḍha` and related embodied cases |
| `tu` | adversative particle | but |
| `na iṣyate` | third-person singular passive of `√iṣ`, with negation | is not accepted or maintained |
| `alpa-upakārāt` | ablative masculine singular | because of comparatively little parental benefit |
| `alajjitvāt` | ablative neuter singular abstract | because of absence or weakness of the relevant moral inhibition |

| `gatiṣu` | locative feminine plural | in the destinies |
| `pañcasu` | locative feminine plural | five |

**Bhāṣya-resolved distribution**

```text
ānantaryaṃ
    → karmāvaraṇa
    → triṣu dvīpeṣu

śeṣe [āvaraṇe]
    → kleśāvaraṇa + vipākāvaraṇa
    → gatiṣu pañcasu
```

The dual `śeṣe` is decisive. It refers back to the other two members of the
three obstructions established in VAK 4.96; it does not introduce a class of
“remaining acts.”

## 4. Grammar

**Locus of immediate-result karma**

```text
triṣu dvīpeṣu
    ānantaryam [iṣyate]
```

> Immediate-result karma is accepted in the three continents.

The affirmative verb is supplied from the following negative construction.
The Bhāṣya specifies Jambudvīpa, Pūrvavideha, and Aparagodānīya, excluding
Uttarakuru, other destinies, and other realms. Within those three continents,
the commentary further restricts the technical category to women and men.

**Qualified exclusion**

```text
śaṇḍhādīnām tu
    ānantaryaṃ na iṣyate
```

> But it is not accepted for `śaṇḍha` and related cases.

The genitive marks the persons for whom this complete karmic classification
is denied. It does not deny that harmful action occurs; it denies the
specific technical constitution of an `ānantarya` karma.

**Two ablatives of reason**

```text
alpa-upakārāt
alajjitvāt ca
```

> Because of comparatively little benefit and because of lack of the relevant
> moral inhibition.

The Bhāṣya distributes these reasons respectively: reduced beneficence is
predicated of the parents, while reduced `apatrāpya`, inhibition before the
parents, is predicated of the agent. This traditional explanation is
Bhāṣya-specific and must not be expanded beyond its stated classification.

**The remaining obstructions**

```text
śeṣe [āvaraṇe]
    gatiṣu pañcasu [iṣyete]
```

> The remaining two obstructions are accepted in the five destinies.

Both the omitted noun and the dual verb are recovered from the preceding
classification and the dual adjective `śeṣe`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Immediate-result karma [is accepted] in the three continents, but is not
> accepted for `śaṇḍha` and related cases, because of comparatively little
> benefit and lack of moral inhibition. The remaining two [obstructions occur]
> in the five destinies.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Karmic obstruction in the form of an immediate-result karma is technically
> possible among women and men in the three human continents, but not in
> Uttarakuru, the other destinies, or other realms. The received analysis does
> not constitute it for `śaṇḍha` and related cases, on the stated grounds of
> diminished parental beneficence and diminished inhibition before the
> parents. The other two obstructions—afflictive and resultant—extend across
> the five destinies according to their respective forms.

The restriction to women and men, the detailed exclusions, the distribution
of the two reasons, and the identification of “the remaining two” are
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An externally described deed does not by itself constitute an
> immediate-result karma. The complete determination requires an agent, a
> karmically qualified field, a determinate relation of received beneficence
> and inhibition, and a world-locus capable of sustaining that relation. The
> same visible event therefore does not bear the same technical predicate in
> every field. Afflictive and resultant obstruction have a wider distribution
> because their constitutive conditions differ from those of karmic
> obstruction.

The rational form is:

```text
external event-description
    + qualified agent
    + qualified field
    + determinate relation
    + appropriate world-locus
    → complete karmic determination
```

This is not a retreat from objectivity into a private intention. The
relations constitute what the karma objectively is within the Kośa's system.
Its meaning arises through the whole in which the act operates.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Tri-dvīpa` — the three human continents**

Jambudvīpa, Pūrvavideha, and Aparagodānīya. They form the stated locus for
`ānantarya` karma. Uttarakuru is excluded even though it is a human
continent.

**`Ānantarya` — immediate-result karmic determination**

The class of five deeds introduced under karmic obstruction in VAK 4.96.
VAK 4.97 specifies that the predicate requires more than an externally
similar physical event: its locus, agent, field, and relation must be
qualified.

**`Śaṇḍha-ādi` — `śaṇḍha` and related cases**

A received embodied classification excluded here from the technical
constitution of `ānantarya`. The Bhāṣya connects this with its earlier account
of the absence of non-restraint and adds the two reasons stated in the verse.

**`Upakāra` — benefit or beneficence**

Here, the constitutive benefit rendered by parents. The exceptional gravity
of parental killing is mediated through this prior relation; it is not
exhausted by the physical description of killing.

**`Lajjā` / `apatrāpya` — moral inhibition before another**

The verse uses the abstract `alajjitva`; the Bhāṣya explains the relevant
factor as powerful `apatrāpya` in relation to one's parents. The received
argument denies that this factor is sufficiently present in the excluded
cases.

**The Bhadanta qualification**

A reported venerable teacher allows that `ānantarya` might occur among an
animal of exceptionally sharp intelligence, illustrated by a well-bred
horse. The report tests whether the determining condition is merely species
or the cognitive-relational capacity required by the karmic field; it is not
presented as the unqualified main rule.

**`Śeṣe āvaraṇe` — the remaining two obstructions**

Afflictive obstruction and resultant obstruction. Their occurrence in all
five destinies completes the distribution begun with karmic obstruction.
Resultant obstruction is instantiated specifically by Uttarakuru existence
among humans and non-percipient existence among gods, as the Bhāṣya notes.

**Bhāṣya transition to intrinsic nature**

After explaining the verse, the prose classifies the five `ānantarya` karmas
as four bodily and one verbal: three completed killings, one false speech,
and one undertaking toward killing. This prepares the analysis of Saṅgha
schism in VAK 4.98 and should not be read back into the kārikā's wording.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
E = an externally described harmful event
A = a qualified agent
F = a qualified karmic field
R = the relevant beneficence-and-inhibition relation
L = an appropriate human world-locus
I = an ānantarya karma
```

Then:

```text
E alone
    does not entail I

E + A + F + R + L
    → eligible for complete determination as I
```

The verse therefore blocks the inference:

```text
ExternallySimilar(E₁, E₂)
    → KarmicallyIdentical(E₁, E₂)
```

The correct rule is relational:

```text
KarmicIdentity(E)
    = determination of E within Agent–Field–Relation–Locus
```

**Distribution of the three obstructions**

```text
KarmaAvarana(x)
    → restricted to the qualified three-continent human locus

KlesaAvarana(x)
    → possible within the five destinies

VipakaAvarana(x)
    → possible within the five destinies
```

The different extensions follow from different constitutive mechanisms. One
common function—obstruction of the Path—does not erase the specific
conditions under which each obstruction exists.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.96 determined obstruction through its relation to Path-capacity. VAK
4.97 now shows that karmic obstruction itself is not an isolated predicate
attached to a bare occurrence. The act becomes the karma it is only within a
complete field of agent, recipient, prior relation, intention, and world.

The parental case makes this mediation especially clear. The karmic gravity
is reflexive: the agent turns against the beneficent relation through which
its own embodied existence was produced and sustained. The visible act is
indispensable, but its full practical meaning comes from negating that
determinate ground. This is why a merely physical taxonomy cannot reproduce
the Kośa's karmic classification.

For Rational Yoga, this supplies another condition of Schema Inference. A
Principle is not applied to random appearances but to the determinate
appearances of a Bhūmi. Likewise, the karmic predicate is valid only when the
event is construed through the relations that constitute its field. The
complete inference moves from the apparent deed through its structured Bhūmi
to its karmic meaning.

The difficult traditional treatment of `śaṇḍha` and related cases belongs to
the received doctrinal explanation. Its systematic value here lies in the
logical claim it makes explicit: externally similar events can differ in
complete karmic determination because the constitutive agent–field relation
differs. This Organon extraction neither adds the historical anthropology to
the universal logical form nor suppresses what the Bhāṣya actually says.
