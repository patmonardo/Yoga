# VAK_4.45

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
क्षेमाक्षेमेतरत्कर्म कुशलाकुशलेतरत् ।
पुण्यापुण्यमनिञ्जं च सुखवेद्यादि च त्रयम् ॥ ४.४५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
kṣemākṣemetarat karma kuśalākuśaletarat /
puṇyāpuṇyam aniñjaṃ ca sukhavedyādi ca trayam // 4.45 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
kṣema-akṣema-itarat karma kuśala-akuśala-itarat /
puṇya-apuṇyam aniñjam ca sukha-vedya-ādi ca trayam //
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly marks a new major section: Vasubandhu now begins the
exposition of the karmas enumerated in the sūtras.

| `trayam` | neuter nominative/accusative singular | a triad; threefold |

**`kṣema`**

`Kṣema` carries the sense of security, safety, well-being, and freedom from
danger. The Bhāṣya defines its force here through desirable maturation and
conduciveness to nirvāṇa because it protects from suffering.

**`itarat`**

The first `itarat` means neither secure nor insecure. The second means neither
wholesome nor unwholesome, which the prose identifies as `avyākṛta`, ethically
indeterminate.

**`aniñja`**

`Aniñja` means unmoving or immovable. The term is only enumerated here; VAK
4.46 explains its karmic and cosmological determination.

**`vedya`**

The gerundive `vedya` means “to be experienced.” It classifies karma by the
feeling-character of its maturation, not necessarily by the feeling present
during the originating action.

## 4. Grammar

**The first definition**

```text
kṣema-akṣema-itarat karma
kuśala-akuśala-itarat [bhavati]
```

> Karma that is secure, insecure, or other is respectively wholesome,
> unwholesome, or other.

The paired compounds are distributive and correlated in order:

```text
kṣema      → kuśala
akṣema     → akuśala
itarat     → itarat, namely avyākṛta
```

The Bhāṣya confirms that the first triad gives the `lakṣaṇa`, the defining
characteristic, of the second. They are not two independent classifications.

**Meritorious, demeritorious, and immovable**

```text
[karma]
puṇyam apuṇyam aniñjam ca [trayam]
```

> Karma is also threefold as meritorious, demeritorious, and immovable.

The repeated `karma` and `trayam` are understood across the compressed
enumeration.

**Three forms beginning with pleasantly experienced**

```text
[karma]
sukha-vedya-ādi ca trayam
```

> Karma is also a triad beginning with that which is to be experienced as
> pleasure.

The Bhāṣya supplies the remaining members: painfully experienced and
experienced as neither pain nor pleasure.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Karma that is secure, insecure, or other is wholesome, unwholesome, or
> other, respectively. It is also meritorious, demeritorious, or immovable,
> and threefold beginning with that which is to be experienced as pleasure.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Wholesome karma is secure: it brings desirable maturation and conduces to
> nirvāṇa by protecting from suffering. Unwholesome karma is insecure, its
> contrary, and brings undesirable maturation; indeterminate karma is neither.
> Karma is further classified as meritorious, demeritorious, or immovable, and
> according to whether its maturation is experienced as pleasure, pain, or
> neither pleasure nor pain.

The desirable and undesirable maturations, nirvāṇa-direction, protection from
suffering, identification of the residual class as `avyākṛta`, and expansion
of the final triad are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Karma receives its ethical determination through the actuality it produces
> and the direction in which it moves the continuum. Wholesome action is
> secure because its maturation is desirable and its ultimate tendency is
> release from suffering; unwholesome action is its practical contrary;
> indeterminate action falls under neither determination. The same karma-field
> can then be divided on other grounds—merit and cosmological stability, or
> the pleasure, pain, and neutrality through which maturation is experienced.

The verse therefore does not attach one abstract moral predicate to an
isolated act. It begins a multidimensional determination of action by quality,
causal direction, domain, and experienced result.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`lakṣaṇa`**

The Bhāṣya says:

```text
idaṃ kuśalādīnāṃ lakṣaṇam
    this is the defining characteristic of the wholesome and the others
```

Accordingly, `kṣema / akṣema / itarat` grounds the determination of `kuśala /
akuśala / avyākṛta` in this passage.

**Wholesome as secure**

```text
kuśala karma
    → desirable maturation
    → conducive to nirvāṇa
    → protection from suffering
    → kṣema
```

`Kuśala` is not reduced to social approval or a merely formal label. Its
character is disclosed by what it generates and where it leads.

**Unwholesome as the practical contrary**

```text
akuśala karma
    → undesirable maturation
    → contrary of kṣema
    → akṣema
```

The opposition is determined relationally: insecurity is intelligible as the
contrary of the security produced by wholesome karma.

**`avyākṛta`**

```text
neither kṣema nor akṣema
    → neither kuśala nor akuśala
    → avyākṛta
```

“Indeterminate” does not mean nonexistent or causally inert. It means not
determined under this wholesome/unwholesome opposition.

**`puṇya / apuṇya / aniñja`**

This second triad concerns karmic production across cosmological domains. It
must not simply be treated as a repetition of wholesome, unwholesome, and
indeterminate. Its exact range follows in VAK 4.46.

**The three `vedanīya` karmas**

```text
sukha-vedanīya
    maturation to be experienced as pleasure

duḥkha-vedanīya
    maturation to be experienced as pain

aduḥkhāsukha-vedanīya
    maturation to be experienced as neither pain nor pleasure
```

The classification concerns the feeling that receives the matured result.

## 8. Logical Determination

**One definition, not two parallel triads**

```text
KṢEMA(K)       ↔ KUŚALA(K)
AKṢEMA(K)      ↔ AKUŚALA(K)
NEITHER(K)     ↔ AVYĀKṚTA(K)
```

Within this local definition, the first series gives the character of the
second. This correction from the Bhāṣya is necessary for the syntax to become
doctrinally determinate.

**Wholesomeness through consequence and direction**

```text
KUŚALA(K)
    → DESIRABLE_MATURATION(K)
    → PROTECTS_FROM_SUFFERING(K)
    → CONDUCES_TO_NIRVĀṆA(K)
```

The arrows distinguish related determinations; they should not be read as a
claim that every wholesome act immediately produces nirvāṇa.

**Multiple grounds of division**

| Ground | Threefold determination |
|---|---|
| Ethical-soteriological character | secure / insecure / neither = wholesome / unwholesome / indeterminate |
| Karmic-cosmological mode | meritorious / demeritorious / immovable |
| Felt maturation | pleasure / pain / neither |

The same karma may receive determinations along each axis. The verse does not
enumerate nine unrelated substances.

**Dhātu in application**

Dhātu now operates as a differentiated field in which karmic causes and
results have determinate ranges:

```text
karma
    → arises within a domain
    → has a domain-conditioned efficacy
    → matures within a determinate field
```

VAK 4.46 will make this explicit for the desire, form, and formless domains.

**Indriya in application**

The final triad connects karmic maturation with the faculties of feeling:

```text
karma matures
    → result is received as pleasure, pain, or neither
```

The karmic classification and the feeling-faculty are not identical. One
determines the causal result; the other is the mode in which that result is
experienced.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The beginning of “Karma in the Sūtras”**

Vasubandhu explicitly announces the transition:

```text
Abhidharma analysis
    form, acquisition, persistence, and bearer of karma

Sūtra classifications
    quality, domain, maturation, time, and experienced result
```

The second movement does not abandon the first. It applies the established
apparatus to action as classified in the sūtras.

**Dhātu and Indriya must work together without collapsing**

The new section tests whether the earlier architecture is complete:

```text
Dhātu
    differentiates the fields and ranges of karmic operation

Indriya
    differentiates the capacities and modes through which maturation is lived

Karma
    connects action, field, maturation, and experience
```

Dhātu does not itself feel the result, and Indriya does not itself supply the
whole causal classification. Their determinations meet in the application.

**Security is practical, not merely subjective**

`Kṣema` does not name a feeling of reassurance. It names the objective
direction of karma toward desirable maturation and protection from suffering.
The Bhāṣya therefore gives a genuinely practical criterion for `kuśala`.

**Meaning and Idea remain contextual translations of `artha`**

This verse does not contain `artha`, but its method confirms why no single
English equivalent can be imposed mechanically. Sanskrit terms receive their
determinate Meaning from their function within a relation. Where `artha`
names intelligible content, “Meaning” may be right; where it names a purposive
determination governing realization, “Idea” may be right; and ordinary
referent, purpose, or benefit remain possible in other constructions.

This is the Organon contact. The definitions, consequences, and section
transition belong to the Bhāṣya; the Dhātu–Indriya application and the note on
`artha` are project interpretations.
