# VAK 4.54 Bhāṣya — Conditions That Make Karma Determinate

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
tīvrakleśaprasādena sātatyena ca yat kṛtam /
guṇakṣetre ca niyataṃ tat pitror ghātakaṃ ca yat // 4.54 //
```

> Karma done through intense affliction or intense confidence,
> through sustained repetition, or in a field of qualities is
> determinate; so is karma that kills either parent.

The unit begins with the question at [231|22] and ends at [232|04].
The next question, concerning present-life-result karma, begins at

running e-text has `gunakṣetre` and a compressed construction at
`tīvrakleśaprasādānāṃ sātatyaṃ ca`. The latter's exact syntax is
uncertain, though the distinction it makes between a qualified
field and the other conditions is clear from context.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What kind of karma should be understood as determinate? “Whatever
is done through intense affliction or confidence, through
continuity, or in a field of qualities is determinate; so too what
kills either parent.” Karma done through intense affliction, karma
done through intense confidence, and karma done continuously or in
a field of qualities should be understood as determinate.

Here the field of qualities comprises the Three Jewels, or a
distinguished person who has attained a particular fruit or
meditative attainment. In such a field, wholesome or unwholesome
karma can become determinate even without intense affliction or
confidence and without sustained repetition. “The two parents”
means mother and father. An act that kills either one becomes
determinate however it is performed; other acts concerning them are
not thereby included.

## 4. Distinct Grounds of Determinacy

| Ground | What the Bhāṣya specifies |
|---|---|
| `tīvra kleśa` | Intense affliction in the doing of karma |
| `tīvra prasāda` | Intense confidence or serene trust |
| `sātatya` | Continuity of performance |
| `guṇakṣetra` | The Three Jewels or a person distinguished by fruit or attainment |
| `pitror ghāta` | Killing mother or father |

The compound `tīvrakleśaprasādena` is unpacked by Vasubandhu into
two alternatives. They can issue in opposite ethical qualities,
while intensity is common to both. The field is a distinct ground:
the Bhāṣya expressly allows both wholesome and unwholesome karma
there to become determinate without the intensity or continuity
just named. The conditions should therefore not be read as a list
that must all be satisfied together.

The final `nānyat` limits the parent clause. The claim is about
killing either parent, not about every action involving one's
parents. The passage specifies the *certainty of maturation* under
these conditions; it does not here assign the life in which the
result will occur.

## 5. Practical Determination

After classifying karma by the possibility and time of maturation,
the Kośa asks what features of an actual performance make its
fruition fixed. This is an exercise in case judgment: intensity of
motive, persistence, recipient-field, and the particular act each
matter. The verse gives conditions for `niyata` karma, rather than
an instruction to generate those conditions.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our study of Practical Reason, 4.54 makes the relation between
an act and its field part of its causal description. An external
action label alone leaves out motive, repetition, and the recipient.
That is an Organon methodological comparison; the specific
classification of the Three Jewels and parental killing belongs to
the Kośa's Buddhist karmic account.
