# VAK_4.118

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
मातृपितृग्लानधार्मकथिकेभ्योऽन्त्यजन्मने ।
बोधिसत्त्वाय चामेया अनार्येभ्योऽपि दक्षिणा ॥ ४.११८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
mātṛpitṛglānadhārmakathikebhyo 'ntyajanmane |
bodhisattvāya cāmeyā anāryebhyo 'pi dakṣiṇā || 4.118 ||
```

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
mātṛ-pitṛ-glāna-dhārma-kathikebhyaḥ | antya-janmane |
bodhisattvāya | ca | ameyā | anāryebhyaḥ | api | dakṣiṇā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `mātṛ-pitṛ-glāna-dhārma-kathikebhyaḥ` | dative plural compound | to mother, father, a sick person, and a Dharma speaker |
| `antya-janmane` | dative singular masculine compound | to one in the final birth |
| `bodhisattvāya` | dative singular masculine | to the Bodhisattva |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `ameyā` | nominative singular feminine adjective | immeasurable |
| `anāryebhyaḥ` | dative plural masculine | to non-noble persons |
| `api` | concessive particle | even |
| `dakṣiṇā` | nominative singular feminine | offering, religious gift |

**Five recipient-fields**

```text
mother
father
sick person
Dharma speaker
final-birth Bodhisattva
```

`Antyajanmane bodhisattvāya` designates one recipient, the Bodhisattva in his
final existence, not two independent recipients. The Bhāṣya confirms both the
fivefold count and this construal.

## 4. Grammar

**Dative recipients and the predication**

```text
[mātre ... antyajanmane bodhisattvāya ca]
    dakṣiṇā
    ameyā [bhavati]
```

> An offering to mother and the other stated recipients is immeasurable.

The datives state the fields toward which the offering is directed. Feminine
`ameyā` agrees with `dakṣiṇā`; the immeasurability belongs to the offering's
fruit, as the Bhāṣya explicitly supplies, not to its physical size.

**Concessive determination**

```text
anāryebhyaḥ api
    even when [the five] are non-noble persons
```

The commentary glosses these recipients as `pṛthagjanabhūta`, being ordinary
persons. Thus lack of noble Path-status does not cancel the field's
immeasurable karmic significance.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> An offering to mother, father, a sick person, a Dharma speaker, or the
> Bodhisattva in his final birth is immeasurable, even when [these recipients]
> are non-noble persons.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> An offering made to any of these five fields—one's mother, one's father, a
> sick person, a Dharma speaker, or the Bodhisattva in the final
> existence—has immeasurable fruit, even if the recipient is still an ordinary
> person rather than a noble one. The Dharma speaker belongs to the benefactor
> field: as a great spiritual friend, the speaker gives beings blinded by
> ignorance the eye of prajñā, reveals the passable and impassable, turns them
> toward the uncontaminated Dharma-body, and thereby performs Buddha-work.

The identification of the five as ordinary persons, the restriction of
immeasurability to fruit, and the functional account of the Dharma speaker
are determinations of the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The measure of giving is not fixed by the object's quantity or by a single
> hierarchy of spiritual rank. A finite gift enters an immeasurable practical
> relation when it answers constitutive past benefit, urgent present
> suffering, liberating instruction, or assured future Buddha-activity. The
> field is therefore temporal and functional: what the recipient has done,
> presently undergoes, gives to others, or is determinately becoming enters
> the Concept of the deed.

The temporal articulation is:

```text
parents                 → past constitutive benefit
sick person             → present suffering requiring relief
Dharma speaker          → present teaching with distributed future effects
final-birth Bodhisattva → future Buddhahood determining the present field
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dakṣiṇā` — offering**

A gift made within a recognized ethical or religious relation. Here it is not
a neutral transfer but a practical response to a distinguished field.

**`Ameyā` or `aprameyā` — immeasurable**

The Kārikā's `ameyā` is explained by the Bhāṣya as immeasurable with respect
to fruit. A finite object can have incalculable karmic significance because
of the relation into which it is given.

**`Anārya` and `pṛthagjana` — non-noble and ordinary person**

The verse and commentary deny that immeasurable field-value requires noble
Path attainment. Noble status is one possible determination of a field, not
the exhaustive measure of every field.

**`Glāna` — sick or afflicted person**

The concrete `duḥkha-kṣetra`. Its significance arises through present need
and the recipient's urgent capacity to be benefited.

**`Dhārmakathika` — Dharma speaker**

Assigned explicitly by the Bhāṣya to the benefactor class (`upakāripakṣa`).
The benefit consists in making discriminative insight and Path-direction
available to others.

**`Antyajanman` or `caramabhavika` — one in the final existence**

The Bhāṣya's gloss for the Bodhisattva whose present birth culminates in
Buddhahood. Its future function is already a determination of the present
recipient-field.

**`Mahākalyāṇamitra` — great spiritual friend**

The Dharma speaker as an exceptionally powerful benefactor for beings blinded
by ignorance.

**`Prajñācakṣus` — eye of prajñā**

The discriminative capacity given through Dharma instruction. The speaker
does not merely transmit propositions but enables beings to discern Path from
non-Path.

**`Buddhakṛtya` — Buddha-work**

The Bhāṣya's culminating characterization of Dharma exposition: the speaker
performs, in summary, the practical work of a Buddha.

## 8. Logical Determination

The central exclusion is:

```text
ImmeasurableFieldValue(R)
    does not imply
NobleStatus(R)
```

The five cases instantiate heterogeneous grounds:

```text
Mother or Father
    → PriorBeneficence

SickPerson
    → PresentSuffering

DharmaSpeaker
    → LiberatingBeneficence

FinalBirthBodhisattva
    → AssuredFutureBuddhaFunction
```

The Dharma speaker also displays mediated causal reach:

```text
Donor supports DharmaSpeaker
    → DharmaSpeaker gives PrajñāEye
    → beings discriminate Path and non-Path
    → benefit propagates beyond the original dyad
```

Thus recipient-field determination includes intrinsic condition, historical
relation, actual function, and prospective causal reach.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.117 established four grounds of field-distinction; VAK 4.118 tests that
framework against five concrete recipients. The result blocks a flat merit
hierarchy. The parents and Dharma speaker are benefactor-fields for different
but structurally related reasons; the sick person is a field through
suffering; the final-birth Bodhisattva is determined through a future
universal function already fixed in the present continuum.

The Dharma speaker is especially important for the Rational Yoga project:

```text
ignorance-blindness
    → Dharma exposition
    → eye of prajñā
    → discrimination of even and uneven
    → orientation toward the uncontaminated Path
```

Here instruction supplies neither an isolated definition nor a merely verbal
representation. It gives the recipient a principle of seeing and the
discriminative structure required to apply it across the practical field.
This is close to the project's account of a Samyama Bhūmi yielding Jñāna: a
principle becomes a systematic capacity to distinguish Path from non-Path
within determinate appearances.

That Organon comparison is reconstructive. The source itself establishes the
Dharma speaker as an immeasurable benefactor because the speaker gives
prajñā-vision and performs Buddha-work.
