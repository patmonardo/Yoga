# VAK 4.52 Bhāṣya — Detachment and the Karma One Can Still Produce

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
yadviraktaḥ sthiro bālas tatra notpadyavedyakṛt /
nānyavedyakṛd apy āryaḥ kāme 'gre vāsthiro 'pi na // 4.52 //
```

> A stable ordinary person detached from a level does not make
> karma to be experienced there at the next birth. A stable noble
> person does not make later-life karma for it either; at the desire
> domain or the summit, even one liable to fall back does not.

The unit begins at [230|26] and ends with a forward reference at
[231|07–08]. The question about karma in the intermediate existence
at [231|09] begins the next unit.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
yadviraktaḥ sthiro bālas tatra notpadyavedyakṛt /

yato bhūmeḥ vītarāgapṛthagjano bhavaty asau ca sthiro bhavaty
aparihāṇadharmā sa tatropapadyavedanīyaṃ karma na karoti /
kim anyat karoti /

nānyavedyakṛd apy āryaḥ

thara iti vartate /
āryapudgalas tu yato vītarāgo na ca parihāṇadharmā sa
tatropapadyadanīyaṃ cāparaparyāyavedanīyaṃ ca karma na karoti /
na hy asau bhavyaḥ punar ādhastanīṃ bhūmim āyātum aniyataṃ
kuryād dṛṣṭadharmavedanīyaṃ cātropapannaḥ /

kāme 'gre vā 'sthiro 'pi na // 4.52 //

parihāṇadharmāpi tv āryapudgalaḥ kāmadhātor bhavāgrād
vītarāgaḥ tayor upapadyāparaparyāyavedanīyaṃ karmābhavyaḥ
kartum /
kiṃ kāraṇam /
phalād dhi sa parihīṇo bhavati /
na cāsti laparihīṇasya kālakriyeti paścāt pravedayiṣyāmaḥ /
```

Word division, punctuation, and verse number are editorial. The
running e-text has `thara iti vartate` where context strongly points
to `sthira iti vartate` (“stable continues”), and has
`tatropapadyadanīyaṃ` and `laparihīṇasya`, both suspect. These
forms are preserved above; the readable translation indicates where
it relies on the local context rather than a secure reading.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“A stable ordinary person detached from a given level does not make
karma to be experienced there at the next birth.” When an ordinary
person has become free of desire for a level and is stable, that is,
not liable to fall back, that person does not produce karma to be
experienced there at the next birth. Does the person produce other
karma?

“Nor does a noble person make karma to be experienced later.” The
word “stable” continues to apply. A noble person who is free of
desire for a level and is not liable to fall back makes neither
karma to be experienced there at the next birth nor karma to be
experienced there in a later life. Such a person cannot return to a
lower level. They might make indeterminate karma, and present-life
karma if born there.

“Even one liable to fall back does not, with respect to the desire
domain or the summit.” A noble person who is liable to fall back,
yet has become free of desire for the desire domain or for the
summit of existence, cannot make karma for either of those levels
to be experienced at the next birth or in a later life. Why? Such
a person falls back from the fruit. As will be explained later, one
who has fallen from the fruit does not die in that fallen condition.
The final statement is contextually clear but rests on a damaged
word in this e-text.

## 4. Three Cases, Three Scopes

| Agent and attainment | Future karma excluded for the detached level |
|---|---|
| Stable ordinary person | Next-birth-result karma |
| Stable noble person | Next-birth and later-life-result karma |
| Noble person liable to fall back, detached from desire domain or summit | Those two future-result classes at these boundary levels |

`Sthira` is glossed by `aparihāṇadharmā`, not liable to fall back.
`Asthira` in the final line is explained by `parihāṇadharmā`, liable
to fall back. The research Kārikā witness separates the final
phrase as `vā sthiro`, but the running source and explanation support
`vā 'sthiro`: “or even unstable.”

The prohibition concerns karma newly made under the stated
conditions. It does not by itself say that every earlier karma for
that level has ceased. Nor does the permission for `aniyata`
karma imply assured fruition; 4.50 defined that class by the
absence of necessity.

## 5. Practical Determination

Here the classification of 4.50–4.51 is applied to the capacity of
a particular agent. The question is no longer only “what type of
karma is this?” but “can someone at this attained condition still
make karma requiring that future birth?” The stronger restriction
for the stable noble person rests on the impossibility of returning
to the lower level. The final exception relies on a rule about
falling from a fruit that Vasubandhu promises to explain later;
this unit does not establish that rule independently.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For our account of Practical Reason, this is case judgment in the
precise sense we discussed: the possible action depends on the
agent's attained condition, the level from which they are detached,
and whether their detachment can be lost. Yoga Vidyā's concern with
Self-Knowledge may inform our interpretation of such a change in
capacity, but the Bhāṣya itself speaks in terms of `bhūmi`, noble
status, stability, and karmic fruition.
