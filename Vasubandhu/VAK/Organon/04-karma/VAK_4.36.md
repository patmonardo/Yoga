# VAK_4.36

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
संवरः सर्वसत्त्वेभ्यो विभाषा त्वङ्गकारणैः ।
असंवरस्तु सर्वेभ्यः सर्वाङ्गेभ्यो न कारणैः ॥ ४.३६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
saṃvaraḥ sarvasattvebhyo vibhāṣā tv aṅgakāraṇaiḥ /
asaṃvaras tu sarvebhyaḥ sarvāṅgebhyo na kāraṇaiḥ // 4.36 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
saṃvaraḥ sarva-sattvebhyaḥ vibhāṣā tu aṅga-kāraṇaiḥ /
asaṃvaraḥ tu sarvebhyaḥ sarva-aṅgebhyaḥ na kāraṇaiḥ //
```

The verse analyzes restraint and non-restraint along three axes: beings,
karmic limbs, and causes. The Bhāṣya gives two possible meanings of `kāraṇa`,
then uses degrees of mental strength in its detailed classification.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
saṃvaraḥ
sarva-sattvebhyaḥ

vibhāṣā
tu
aṅga-kāraṇaiḥ

asaṃvaraḥ
tu
sarvebhyaḥ

sarva-aṅgebhyaḥ

na
kāraṇaiḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `saṃvaraḥ` | nominative masculine singular | restraint |
| `sarvasattvebhyaḥ` | ablative plural compound | with respect to all beings |
| `vibhāṣā` | nominative feminine singular | differentiated alternatives; variation |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but; whereas |
| `aṅgakāraṇaiḥ` | instrumental plural compound | with respect to limbs and causes |
| `asaṃvaraḥ` | nominative masculine singular | non-restraint |
| `sarvebhyaḥ` | ablative plural | with respect to all [beings] |
| `sarvāṅgebhyaḥ` | ablative plural compound | with respect to all karmic limbs |
| `na kāraṇaiḥ` | instrumental plural under negation | not through all causes |

**`sarvasattvebhyaḥ`**

The ablative marks the field with respect to which restraint is acquired. A
prātimokṣa restraint must extend to all beings, not merely to beings currently
known, reachable, favored, or feared by its bearer.

**`vibhāṣā`**

`Vibhāṣā` announces differentiated alternatives rather than uncertainty. The
being-scope is fixed as universal, while the limb-scope and causal scope admit
different valid combinations.

**`aṅga`**

Here the limbs are karmapatha-components of restraint. The Bhāṣya contrasts:

```text
bhikṣu restraint
    all relevant limbs

other restraints
    four limbs or another authorized range
```

Variation by limb therefore concerns the determinate type of restraint
undertaken.

**Two analyses of `kāraṇa`**

The Bhāṣya first allows:

```text
alobha
adveṣa
amoha
    the three wholesome roots
```

Under that analysis, restraint may be said to arise through all three.

It then gives the analysis governing the detailed alternatives:

```text
mṛdu citta
    weak mind

madhya citta
    medium mind

adhimātra citta
    strong mind
```

One undertaking occurs at one strength, although the same person may
undertake different restraints at different strengths.

**`asaṃvara`**

Non-restraint is not a single harmful occurrence. It is the standing form of
a harmful practical life. The Vaibhāṣika position assigns it universal
being-scope and all karmapatha-limbs, but only one causal strength at a time.

## 4. Grammar

**Fixed and variable dimensions of restraint**

```text
saṃvaraḥ sarva-sattvebhyaḥ [labhyate]
aṅgaiḥ kāraṇaiḥ tu vibhāṣā
```

> Restraint is acquired with respect to all beings, while there are
> differentiated alternatives with respect to limbs and causes.

The verb `labhyate`, “is acquired,” is supplied by the Bhāṣya and the ongoing
discussion. `Vibhāṣā` governs both members of the compound
`aṅgakāraṇaiḥ`.

**Non-restraint**

```text
asaṃvaraḥ tu
sarvebhyaḥ [sattvebhyaḥ]
sarva-aṅgebhyaḥ [labhyate]
na [sarva-]kāraṇaiḥ
```

> Non-restraint, however, is acquired with respect to all beings and all
> limbs, though not through all causes.

`Sarvebhyaḥ` carries forward `sattvebhyaḥ`. In the final expression, the
Bhāṣya understands the negated universal: not all three degrees of mental
strength simultaneously.

**Universality within a limb**

Variation in the number of authorized limbs does not qualify the universal
`sarvasattvebhyaḥ`. A four-limbed restraint can still extend to all beings in
each limb it contains.

```text
fewer predicates undertaken
    does not entail
fewer beings within those predicates
```

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Restraint is acquired with respect to all beings, but there are
> differentiated alternatives according to its limbs and causes.
> Non-restraint, however, is acquired with respect to all beings and all
> limbs, though not through all causes.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Prātimokṣa restraint necessarily extends to all beings, although its
> karmapatha-limbs vary with the kind of restraint undertaken and its causal
> strength may be weak, medium, or strong. According to the Vaibhāṣikas,
> non-restraint likewise extends to all beings and includes all the relevant
> karmapathas, but it is acquired through only one degree of mind at a time,
> not through weak, medium, and strong minds simultaneously.

The identification of causes with degrees of mind and the attribution of the
complete non-restraint account to the Vaibhāṣikas are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A restraint can be universal in extension without containing every possible
> practical predicate. Its non-killing limb, for example, excludes every
> being from the class of permitted victims, while the particular discipline
> may contain four limbs rather than the complete monastic range. The
> universality belongs to the intention’s unrestricted relation to beings;
> content and intensity remain separately determined. The Vaibhāṣika account
> gives non-restraint the same universal extension but treats it as an
> integrated complex of all harmful limbs arising at a determinate strength.

The Bhāṣya preserves a Sautrāntika objection to the final account: outside
the fixed eightfold restraint systems, restraint and non-restraint may be
partial according to what is actually undertaken.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Three axes of practical determination**

```text
sattva
    toward which beings?

aṅga
    which karmic limbs?

kāraṇa
    through what causal root or strength?
```

The axes are related but irreducible. Universal being-scope does not entail
complete limb-scope or simultaneous possession of every causal strength.

**Four restraint combinations**

Using mental strength as the operative meaning of `kāraṇa`, the Bhāṣya gives:

```text
1. all beings
   not all limbs
   not all strengths

2. all beings
   all limbs
   not all strengths

3. all beings
   all limbs
   all strengths across three undertakings

4. all beings
   all strengths across three undertakings
   not all limbs
```

The third and fourth cases require three restraints undertaken with weak,
medium, and strong minds respectively. No single undertaking simultaneously
has all three strengths.

**The impossible fifth case**

There is no prātimokṣa restraint that fails to extend to all beings. The
Bhāṣya grounds acquisition in a wholesome intention (`kalyāṇāśaya`) directed
toward all beings.

**Five limiting determinations**

The Bhāṣya lists possible restrictions:

```text
sattva-niyama
    “I abstain regarding this being”

aṅga-niyama
    restriction by karmic limb

deśa-niyama
    “in this place”

kāla-niyama
    “for this month”

samaya-niyama
    “except in war”
```

Arbitrary restriction of the universal practical relation yields good
conduct (`sucarita`) rather than full prātimokṣa restraint. This must be
distinguished from undertaking a recognized restraint-type whose authorized
content contains fewer limbs. In the latter case, every included limb still
extends to all beings without private exceptions.

**Possible and impossible objects**

How can restraint concern beings whom the practitioner could never harm? The
answer lies in the undertaking:

```text
intention not to injure the life of any being
    → restraint with respect to all beings
```

If its scope followed only current opportunity, restraint would increase and
decrease whenever a being moved into or out of practical reach.

**Strength of non-restraint**

A person may acquire non-restraint with a weak mind and later perform a strong
manifest killing. The standing non-restraint remains weak; the current
killing-manifestation may be strong.

```text
strength of acquired non-restraint
    ≠ strength of every later manifestation
```

**School dispute over partial forms**

```text
Vaibhāṣika
    no person bears a mutilated or partial non-restraint
    non-restraint includes all beings and all karmapathas

Sautrāntika
    outside the eight fixed restraint systems,
    restraint and non-restraint may be partial
    according to the undertaking
```

The verse states the Vaibhāṣika structure; the Bhāṣya records the
Sautrāntika qualification.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Scope matrix**

| Form | Being-scope | Limb-scope | Causal strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prātimokṣa restraint | necessarily all beings | variable by recognized type | weak, medium, or strong |
| Non-restraint, Vaibhāṣika | all beings | all karmapathas | one strength at acquisition |
| Partial forms, Sautrāntika | according to undertaking | according to undertaking | determinate strength |

**Universal extension and variable content**

```text
for every being x
    the undertaken non-killing restraint applies to x

but
    non-killing does not thereby become
    every other restraint-limb
```

Universality over objects and completeness over predicates are different
logical relations.

**The universal is not an enumeration**

```text
universal restraint
    does not arise by adding individual beings
    one after another

universal intention
    institutes the relation to all beings
```

Empirical reachability tests possible action; it does not determine the
extension of the vow.

**Authorized differentiation and arbitrary exception**

```text
recognized four-limbed restraint
    determinate practical form

“I will not kill except in this place or circumstance”
    private restriction of the universal relation
```

The former may be genuine restraint. The latter is merely good conduct on the
Vaibhāṣika analysis given here.

**Standing form and occurrent strength**

The non-restraint example preserves two temporal levels:

```text
strength at acquisition
    determines standing non-restraint

strength of later mind
    determines current manifestation
```

Later intensity does not retrospectively rewrite the acquired form.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A universal practical relation**

VAK 4.36 gives a precise account of what “all beings” means in a vow. It is
not a completed inventory and does not depend on the agent’s physical power:

```text
I undertake not to injure living beings
    → no being is admitted as an exception
```

The universal lies in the form of the intention. Unknown, distant, extinct,
or presently unreachable beings need not be separately represented as
individual objects for the undertaking to exclude exceptions by kind.

**Extension, content, and intensity**

The practical determination has at least three Aspects:

```text
extension
    all beings

content
    the limbs actually belonging to this restraint

intensity
    weak, medium, or strong undertaking-mind
```

A judgment about one Aspect cannot be transferred automatically to the
others. This is the same discipline of non-contagious predication already
encountered in the analysis of vijñapti and avijñapti.

**The good intention is not sentimental universality**

`Kalyāṇāśaya` is operative because it removes private exceptions from the
field of abstention. It does not posit a feeling of equal emotional warmth
toward every being. The vow universally determines what the agent will not
make of another being.

**The school dispute locates the frontier**

The Vaibhāṣika system gives restraint and non-restraint strong standardized
forms. The Sautrāntika answer returns determination to the actual undertaking:

```text
institutionally fixed form
    versus
content actually undertaken
```

Neither side treats a persistent practical form as an isolated occurrence.
They dispute how fully its scope must be standardized before it counts as
restraint or non-restraint.

This is the Organon contact. The three axes, four combinations, five possible
restrictions, reachability argument, and school dispute belong to the Bhāṣya.
