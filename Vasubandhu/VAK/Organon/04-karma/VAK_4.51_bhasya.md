# VAK 4.51 Bhāṣya — Four Corners and the Projection of a Rebirth Series

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
catuṣkoṭikam ity anye nikāyākṣepaṇaṃ tribhiḥ /
sarvatra catur ākṣepaḥ śubhasya narake tridhā // 4.51 //
```

> Others set out four alternatives. Three kinds project a rebirth
> series. In general four kinds can be undertaken everywhere; for

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Others set out four alternatives.” The Dārṣṭāntikas make a
four-cornered analysis. There is karma determinate as to its stage
but not determinate as to maturation: karma assigned to this life
and the other specified lives whose maturation is not assured.
There is karma determinate as to maturation but not as to its stage:
indeterminate-time karma whose maturation is assured. There is
karma determinate in both respects: karma assigned to this life or
another specified life whose maturation is assured. There is karma
determinate in neither respect: indeterminate-time karma whose
maturation is not assured.

For them, karma is consequently eightfold: present-life-result
karma may be fixed or unfixed as to maturation, and so on through
indeterminate-time karma. The other account describes the three
classes beginning with present-life-result karma as determinate
and makes the indeterminate class fourth. Four kinds of karma could
be initiated in a single moment: one might induce another person in
three actions while engaging in sexual misconduct oneself, if all
four actions are completed simultaneously. The source does not name
those three actions here.

“Three kinds project a rebirth series.” Of these four kinds of karma,
present-life-result karma does not project a `nikāyasabhāga`, a
continuum of the same class. In which domain, and in which destiny,
can how many kinds of karma be initiated? “Everywhere, four kinds
can be initiated.” In all three domains and all destinies, the four
kinds can be initiated as wholesome or unwholesome, as applicable.
There is an exception to this general rule: “For wholesome karma in
hell, there are three.” In hell, three kinds of wholesome karma may
be initiated, but not present-life-result wholesome karma, because
there is no desirable maturation there.

## 4. Two Independent Fixities

The Dārṣṭāntika `catuṣkoṭika` crosses **stage** (`avasthā`) with
**maturation** (`vipāka`):

| | Maturation assured | Maturation unassured |
|---|---|---|
| Stage fixed | Both determinate | Stage alone determinate |
| Stage unfixed | Maturation alone determinate | Neither determinate |

This yields eight kinds when each of the four timing classes is
further divided by whether maturation is assured. The contrasting
fourfold account keeps the three specified-life classes fixed and
the fourth indeterminate. These are reported positions, not one
undifferentiated Kośa assertion.

## 5. Initiation and Projection

The Bhāṣya uses `ākṣepa` in nearby but distinct contexts. Four
kinds of karma may be *initiated*, even at one simultaneous
completion. Yet only three can *project* a `nikāyasabhāga`:
karma whose fruit belongs to the present life does not establish
the new rebirth series. The example about four simultaneous acts
establishes a possibility of initiation; it does not say that a
single act projects four rebirths.

The final `sarvatra` is a general rule (`utsarga`) qualified by
`yathāsaṃbhavam`, “as applicable.” The exception (`apavāda`) is
specific: in hell, wholesome karma with desirable fruition **in
that same life** is excluded. The passage does not deny every
possibility of wholesome action in hell.

## 6. Organon Contact Point

For the Practical Reason of our agent, this verse is a worked
example of case classification. It keeps assurance of a result,
temporal placement, initiation of action, and projection of a
future series as separate questions. The hell case then tests a
general rule against the conditions of a particular domain. This
is our methodological use of the passage; the source's claims
remain about karma, domains, and destinies.
