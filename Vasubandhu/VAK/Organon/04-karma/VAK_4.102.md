# VAK_4.102

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आदावन्तेऽर्बुदात्पूर्वं युगाच्चोपरते मुनौ ।
सीमायां चाप्यबद्धायां चक्रभेदो न जायते ॥ ४.१०२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)


## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ādau | ante | arbudāt | pūrvam | yugāt | ca | uparate | munau |
sīmāyām | ca | api | abaddhāyām | cakra-bhedaḥ | na | jāyate
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ādau` | locative masculine singular | at the beginning of the dispensation |
| `ante` | locative masculine singular | at its end |
| `arbudāt` | ablative neuter singular | before the internal corruptions have arisen |
| `pūrvam` | adverb governing the ablative | before; also understood with `yugāt` |
| `yugāt` | ablative neuter singular | before the foremost pair of disciples has appeared |
| `ca` | coordinating particle | and |
| `uparate` | locative masculine singular past passive participle | when ceased; when passed away |
| `munau` | locative masculine singular | when the Sage has passed away |
| `sīmāyām` | locative feminine singular | with respect to the monastic boundary |
| `api` | emphatic particle | also, even |
| `abaddhāyām` | locative feminine singular past passive participle | when not formally bound or established |
| `cakra-bhedaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | division of the Dharma-wheel |
| `na jāyate` | third-person singular present middle/passive with negation | does not arise |

**Six exclusions resolved by the Bhāṣya**

```text
1. ādau
2. ante
3. arbudāt pūrvam
4. yugāt [pūrvam]
5. uparate munau
6. sīmāyām abaddhāyām

in each case:
    cakrabhedaḥ na jāyate
```

The Bhāṣya is indispensable for identifying `arbuda` as corruptions of
discipline and view and `yuga` as the foremost pair of disciples.

## 4. Grammar

**Beginning and end**

```text
ādau [cakrabhedaḥ na jāyate]
ante [cakrabhedaḥ na jāyate]
```

> It does not arise at the beginning or at the end.

The locatives state phases of the dispensation. The Bhāṣya understands the
beginning as the time when the Dharma-wheel has only recently begun to turn
and the end as the time of the Blessed One's parinirvāṇa. In both conditions,
the Saṅgha is `ekarasa`, “of one flavor.”

**Before corruption and before the pair**

```text
arbudāt pūrvam
yugāt [pūrvam] ca
```

> Before the corruptions, and before the pair.

The adverb `pūrvam` governs the ablative `arbudāt` and is carried forward to
`yugāt`. The commentary supplies the technical referents rather than leaving
the words at their ordinary senses of “swelling” and “pair.”

**After the Sage's passing**

```text
uparate munau
    [cakrabhedaḥ na jāyate]
```

> When the Sage has passed away, it does not arise.

This locative absolute supplies a temporal condition. The special opposition
between the living Buddha and a rival claimant can no longer be constituted.

**Before a common boundary is established**

```text
sīmāyām abaddhāyām
    [cakrabhedaḥ na jāyate]
```

> When the boundary is unbound, it does not arise.

The second locative absolute supplies an institutional condition. Until one
common `sīmā` is formally established, there cannot be two factions of that
one bounded Saṅgha.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Division of the wheel does not arise at the beginning or at the end, before
> the `arbuda`, before the pair, when the Sage has passed away, or when the
> boundary has not been established.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Division of the Dharma-wheel cannot arise when the dispensation has only
> just begun or at its end; before corruptions of discipline and doctrinal
> view have developed; before the foremost pair of disciples capable of
> reuniting the Saṅgha has appeared; after the Buddha has entered
> parinirvāṇa; or before one common monastic boundary has been formally
> established. Even where these necessary conditions are present, schism does
> not follow inevitably, because its actuality depends upon karma.

The meanings of `arbuda` and `yuga`, the reasons for each exclusion, and the
final distinction between necessary conditions and karmic actualization are
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A determinate division presupposes a determinate unity. The Dharma-wheel
> must already function as an established order; contradiction must have
> developed immanently within its discipline and view; opposed factions must
> occupy one bounded institutional field; and the historical agents of both
> rivalry and reconciliation must be present. Structure defines the field in
> which schism is possible, but karma determines whether that possibility
> becomes actual.

The rational movement is:

```text
established unity
    → internal corruption and differentiation
    → possible organized opposition
    → karmically actualized division
    → possible reconciliation
```

Mere plurality is not division. Division is the negation of a prior common
whole within the domain that continues to relate the opposed sides.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Cakrabheda` — division of the Dharma-wheel**

The exceptional rupture defined in VAK 4.98–4.101 through rival Teacher and
Path and obstruction of Path-functioning. VAK 4.102 states six conditions in
which this exact structure cannot arise.

**`Ekarasa` — of one flavor**

The Bhāṣya's description of the Saṅgha at the beginning and end of the
dispensation. The determinate internal opposition required for schism is not
available in these phases.

**`Arbuda` — internal corruption or excrescence**

The prose resolves the singular through two forms:

```text
śīlārbuda
    corruption of discipline

dṛṣṭyarbuda
    corruption of doctrinal view
```

Schism becomes possible only after contradiction has appeared within the
dispensation's own practical and doctrinal determinations.

**`Śrāvakāgrayuga` — the foremost pair of disciples**

The technical referent of `yuga`. The pair is required because the divided
Saṅgha is to be reunited through them according to the established procedure
of reconciliation. The possibility-condition of division thus includes an
agent of restoration.

**`Uparate munau` — after the Sage has passed away**

After the Buddha's parinirvāṇa there is no `pratidvandvin`, no rival who can
oppose himself to the living Teacher in the precise form required for
`cakrabheda`.

**`Sīmā-bandha` — formal binding of a monastic boundary**

The establishment of one common institutional domain. Two factions count as
a divided Saṅgha only when they take their opposed stands within this one
boundary.

**`Karmādhīna` — dependent upon karma**

The Bhāṣya's final qualification. A mature dispensation, internal corruption,
the foremost pair, a living Buddha, and an established boundary make schism
possible; they do not cause it by themselves.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
U = established unity of the Dharma-wheel
C = developed internal contradiction
Y = presence of the foremost disciple-pair
T = living Buddha as Teacher
B = one formally bounded Saṅgha
K = appropriate schism-producing karma
D = actual division of the wheel
```

Necessary background conditions can be expressed as:

```text
D → U
D → C
D → Y
D → T
D → B
D → K
```

But the converse does not follow merely from structure:

```text
U + C + Y + T + B
    does not entail D

U + C + Y + T + B + K
    → D may become actual
```

**Division presupposes unity**

```text
TwoGroups(x, y)
    does not entail
DivisionOfOneWhole(x, y)

CommonBoundary(x, y)
    + PriorCommunalUnity(x, y)
    + OrganizedOpposition(x, y)
    → eligible for FormalDivision(x, y)
```

**Internal contradiction**

```text
NoDisciplinaryOrDoctrinalCorruption
    → no developed ground of opposition
    → no cakrabheda

DevelopedCorruption
    → possibility of opposition
    ≠ inevitability of schism
```

**Division and reconciliation**

```text
PossibleCakrabheda(D)
    requires
PossibleInstitutionalReconciliation(D)
```

This requirement belongs to the received definition through the presence of
the foremost disciple-pair.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.101 distinguished rupture of the Dharma-wheel from division of
communal operation. VAK 4.102 now determines the complete field in which the
first kind of rupture can exist. The field must possess historical maturity,
internal differentiation, a common formal boundary, a living normative
center, and an available course of reintegration.

This yields an exact conditioned dialectic. At the beginning the unity is
immediate and has not yet developed the opposition required for division. In
the middle, corruptions of discipline and view can arise within the unity,
making organized opposition possible. At the end, the exact opposition to
the living Teacher is no longer available. `Cakrabheda` therefore belongs to
a determinate middle, not to an abstract timeless possibility.

The role of the foremost pair is especially important for the Path. The
divided Dharma-wheel is defined not only by obstruction of Path-functioning
but within a system that contains a valid procedure and agents for restoring
that functioning. The conditioned negative does not abolish the Path's
positive capacity for return. Division and reconciliation are opposed
courses within one institutional Bhūmi.

For Samyama as Practical Reason, the lesson is that a Principle can be
applied only within a sufficiently constituted field. A Bhūmi must have
boundaries, developed determinations, real opposition, and operative agents.
Schema Inference cannot infer division from mere difference, or actuality
from structural possibility:

```text
constituted Bhūmi
    → defines possible courses

karma
    → actualizes one course

Path
    → supplies the principle and procedure of reintegration
```

This is why the final Bhāṣya qualification matters so much. Structure is not
destiny. The necessary conditions establish the space of practical reason;
karma determines the conditioned actuality arising within it.
