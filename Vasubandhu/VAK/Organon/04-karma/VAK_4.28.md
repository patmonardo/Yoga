# VAK_4.28

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
काल्यं ग्राह्योऽन्यतो नीचैः स्थितेनोक्तानुवादिता ।
उपवासः समग्राङ्गो निर्भूषेणानिशाक्षयात् ॥ ४.२८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
kālyaṃ grāhyo 'nyato nīcaiḥ sthitenoktānuvāditā /
upavāsaḥ samagrāṅgo nirbhūṣeṇāniśākṣayāt // 4.28 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
kālyam grāhyaḥ anyataḥ nīcaiḥ sthitena ukta-anuvāditā /
upavāsaḥ samagra-aṅgaḥ nirbhūṣeṇa ā-niśā-kṣayāt //
```

The Bhāṣya resolves `kālyam` as sunrise and `āniśākṣayāt` as continuing until
the following sunrise. It also restricts `nirbhūṣeṇa`: exceptional adornment
is excluded, but habitual adornment need not be removed.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kālyam
grāhyaḥ
anyataḥ
nīcaiḥ
sthitena
ukta-anuvāditā

upavāsaḥ
samagra-aṅgaḥ
nirbhūṣeṇa
ā-niśā-kṣayāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `kālyam` | adverbial form | at the proper time; at sunrise |
| `grāhyaḥ` | nominative masculine singular gerundive | is to be received or undertaken |
| `anyataḥ` | ablative/adverbial form | from another person |
| `nīcaiḥ` | adverb | lower down; in a deferential position |
| `sthitena` | instrumental masculine singular participle | by one positioned or standing |
| `uktānuvāditā` | compound expression from `ukta` and `anu-vad` | repetition after the words have been spoken |
| `upavāsaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | upavāsa restraint or observance |
| `samagrāṅgaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | complete in all its limbs |
| `nirbhūṣeṇa` | instrumental masculine singular | by one without exceptional adornment |
| `āniśākṣayāt` | temporal compound with ablative terminus | until the exhaustion of the night; until next sunrise |

**`kālyam`**

The proper time is the initial sunrise of the day-and-night observance. The
vow is normally instituted as the temporal period begins.

**`grāhya`**

The gerundive means “to be received” as well as “to be undertaken.” The
Bhāṣya’s giver–receiver analysis makes reception the more precise value in the
ordinary procedure.

**`uktānuvāditā`**

```text
ukta
    spoken by the giver

anu-vad
    speak after
    repeat responsively
```

The sequence is constitutive: the recipient neither anticipates the formula
nor speaks simultaneously with the giver.

**`samagrāṅga`**

The observance must contain all eight limbs. `Samagra` means complete or
integral; `aṅga` means constituent limb.

**`nirbhūṣa`**

The restriction concerns adornment productive of vanity or intoxication.
Habitual adornment is expressly excepted by the Bhāṣya because it does not
strongly generate `mada`.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

**Main obligation**

```text
upavāsaḥ grāhyaḥ
```

> The upavāsa restraint is to be received.

The remaining expressions specify how, from whom, in what condition, and for
what duration.

**Temporal and interpersonal conditions**

```text
kālyam
    at sunrise

anyataḥ
    from another
```

The Bhāṣya adds an exception to the morning rule: one who has previously
formed the standing undertaking, “I shall always observe the eight,” may
receive it even after eating.

**Recipient’s embodied condition**

```text
nīcaiḥ sthitena
    by one positioned lower

nirbhūṣeṇa
    by one without exceptional adornment
```

Both instrumentals qualify the recipient. Bodily posture and presentation are
parts of the practical act of reception.

**Speech sequence**

```text
ukta-anuvāditā
    the giver speaks
    then the recipient repeats
```

The Bhāṣya denies both prior and simultaneous recitation. Otherwise the
relation of giving and receiving would not be established.

**Completeness and terminus**

```text
samagra-aṅgaḥ
    complete in all eight limbs

ā-niśā-kṣayāt
    through the end of the night
    until the following sunrise
```

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The upavāsa restraint is to be received at sunrise from another, by one
> positioned deferentially and repeating the words after they are spoken. It
> is to be complete in all its limbs, undertaken without adornment, and
> maintained until the end of the night.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Ordinarily, the upavāsa restraint is received at sunrise from another
> person. The recipient assumes a deferential posture and repeats the formula
> only after the giver has spoken it. The observance must be undertaken with
> all eight limbs, without exceptional adornment that fosters vanity, and
> maintained until the following sunrise. Habitual adornment is not excluded,
> and one with a prior standing commitment to observe the eight may receive
> the restraint even after eating.

The exact sunrise boundaries, the prior-undertaking exception, sequential
speech rule, eightfold completeness, and qualification concerning habitual
adornment are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A finite vow becomes actual through a complete practical mediation. Its
> temporal beginning and end are fixed; another person gives its articulated
> form; the recipient bodily expresses respect, receives the words in their
> proper sequence, and appropriates them through responsive repetition; the
> content is accepted as an integral eight-limbed whole and embodied without
> exceptional self-display. Mere conformity to the relevant conduct may be
> good, but it is not thereby the technically acquired upavāsa restraint.

“Appropriation” and “practical mediation” articulate the procedure at the
Organon level; they are not translations of single Sanskrit words.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Sunrise-to-sunrise form**

```text
initial sunrise
    → acquisition
    → day
    → night
    → following sunrise
    → completion
```

The temporal determination discussed abstractly in VAK 4.27 is now embodied
as a concrete procedure.

**Reception from another**

The Bhāṣya says the observance is not ordinarily to be assumed privately in
disregard of another when the proper conditions are available. Receiving it
from another helps guard against transgression.

**`agaurava`**

```text
gaurava
    respect; gravity

a-gaurava
    absence of respect
```

Vasubandhu states that restraint does not arise in one lacking respect. The
lower posture is therefore the embodied expression of a required practical
disposition, not decorative etiquette.

**`dānagrahaṇa`**

Giving and receiving require ordered difference:

```text
giver articulates
    → recipient hears
    → recipient repeats
```

If both speak simultaneously, or the recipient speaks first, the specific
relation “received from another” is not constituted.

**`vikalāṅga`**

`Vikalāṅga` means deficient in limbs. A selectively truncated observance may
contain good practices, but it is not the complete eight-limbed upavāsa.

**`ājasrika-alaṅkāra`**

Habitual adornment is permitted because it does not strongly produce vanity.
This prevents the terse `nirbhūṣeṇa` from becoming an absolute ban unsupported
by the commentary.

**`sucaritamātra`**

The Bhāṣya draws the decisive boundary:

```text
conduct otherwise conforming to goodness
    → mere good conduct

conduct generated through the complete acquisition procedure
    → upavāsa restraint
```

The distinction is between ethical quality and technically instituted
practical status.

**`upavāsa` and `poṣadha`**

The Bhāṣya offers complementary explanations:

```text
upavāsa
    dwelling near the Arhats through imitation of their discipline

or

    dwelling near lifelong restraint through temporary observance

poṣadha
    nourishment of wholesome roots
```

The etymologies express the observance’s exemplary and cultivational functions.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Necessary procedural structure**

```text
proper time
+ giver–receiver relation
+ respectful disposition
+ embodied deference
+ sequential verbal repetition
+ all eight limbs
+ regulated adornment
+ complete duration
    → formally acquired upavāsa restraint
```

The elements are coordinated conditions of one acquisition, not independent
ritual decorations.

**Good conduct does not entail vow-status**

```text
sucarita
    does not by itself entail
upavāsa-saṃvara
```

The same outward acts can have different practical status according to their
genesis and formal determination.

**Repetition is not duplication**

```text
the giver’s statement
    is addressed to the recipient

the recipient’s repetition
    makes the stated norm a first-person undertaking
```

Identity of words is compatible with difference of practical function.

**Completeness is internal articulation**

```text
whole observance
    differentiates into eight limbs

eight limbs
    jointly constitute the whole observance
```

Removing a limb changes the acquired form rather than merely reducing its
quantity.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The vow is generated, not merely intended**

VAK 4.28 provides a detailed answer to a question often left vague in ethical
commentary: how does an intention become an instituted discipline? Private
resolve alone is not the normal complete acquisition of this upavāsa. The vow
is generated through a coordinated unity of consciousness, body, speech,
another person, articulated content, and time.

```text
intention to undertake
    → entry into procedure
    → responsive declaration
    → complete embodied observance
    → acquired practical form
```

This does not make the procedure magical. It makes explicit the mediations by
which a practical determination gains objectivity and persistence.

**The Copula is the giver–receiver relation**

The spoken formula travels through an ordered relation:

```text
spoken by another
    → heard as an offered determination
    → repeated by the recipient
    → possessed as the recipient’s vow
```

The words remain recognizably the same while their practical subject changes.
Before repetition, the norm is offered to me; in repetition, it is posited by
me; afterward, it determines me. This is a concrete practical Copula.

The difference between giver and receiver must be preserved for mediation to
occur. Simultaneous speech would flatten the relation into undifferentiated
sound and fail to constitute reception from another.

**Form and conduct**

The distinction between `sucaritamātra` and upavāsa restraint is perhaps the
most important teaching in the passage. An observer may accidentally or
privately conform to every visible prohibition yet lack the formally acquired
vow. Conversely, the acquired vow organizes each compliant act as a moment of
one articulated practical whole.

```text
good acts collected externally
    ≠
acts inhering in one instituted discipline
```

This is why the section is more than yama commentary. It does not merely tell
the practitioner what not to do; it analyzes the genesis through which a
universal norm becomes the practitioner’s continuing form.

**Respect is productive**

The bodily lowering is not submission to arbitrary personality. It marks the
recipient’s willingness to receive a form not invented from momentary private
preference. The Bhāṣya’s claim is strong: without respect, restraint does not
arise.

At the Organon level, respect opens the subject to determination by a valid
universal. Posture, speech, and intention therefore belong to one act of
acquisition. None should be reduced to a merely external symbol of an already
complete inward event.

This is an Organon reconstruction. Vasubandhu explicitly teaches the complete
procedure, its exceptions, and the distinction between mere good conduct and
upavāsa restraint.
