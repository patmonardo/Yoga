# VAK_4.35

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सर्वोभयेभ्यः कामाप्तो वर्तमानेभ्य आप्यते ।
मौलेभ्यः सर्वकालेभ्यो ध्यानानास्रवसंवरौ ॥ ४.३५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
sarvobhayebhyaḥ kāmāpto vartamānebhya āpyate /
maulebhyaḥ sarvakālebhyo dhyānānāsravasaṃvarau // 4.35 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
sarva-ubhayebhyaḥ kāma-āptaḥ vartamānebhyaḥ āpyate /
maulebhyaḥ sarva-kālebhyaḥ dhyāna-anāsrava-saṃvarau //
```

The compact `sarva-ubhaya` receives two separate decompositions in the
Bhāṣya. Neither “all” nor “both” may be left as a vague universal.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sarva-ubhayebhyaḥ
kāma-āptaḥ
vartamānebhyaḥ
āpyate

maulebhyaḥ
sarva-kālebhyaḥ
dhyāna-anāsrava-saṃvarau
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `sarvobhayebhyaḥ` | ablative plural compound | with respect to all three stages and both relevant divisions |
| `kāmāptaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | belonging to the desire realm; prātimokṣa restraint |
| `vartamānebhyaḥ` | ablative plural | from or with respect to present bases |
| `āpyate` | third-person singular present passive from `√āp` | is acquired |
| `maulebhyaḥ` | ablative plural from `maula` | from or with respect to root karmapathas |
| `sarvakālebhyaḥ` | ablative plural compound | from bases belonging to all times |
| `dhyānānāsravasaṃvarau` | nominative masculine dual compound | meditation-born and uncontaminated restraints |

**`kāmāpta`**

The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies the desire-realm restraint here with
prātimokṣa restraint:

```text
kāma-āpta saṃvara
    = prātimokṣa-saṃvara
```

This restraint is acquired through an undertaking in the desire-realm field
of embodied conduct.

**The range of `sarva`**

`Sarva`, “all,” covers three stages of a karmic course:

```text
maula
    root or principal karmic act

prayoga
    preparation leading to the root act

pṛṣṭha
    aftermath, continuation, or subsequent stage
```

Prātimokṣa restraint extends across all three.

**The two ranges of `ubhaya`**

The Bhāṣya gives two binary divisions:

```text
sattva-ākhya
    designated as sentient

asattva-ākhya
    designated as non-sentient
```

and:

```text
prakṛti-sāvadya
    naturally blameworthy

pratikṣepaṇa-sāvadya
    blameworthy because prohibited
```

Prātimokṣa restraint is acquired with respect to both sides of both
divisions.

**`vartamāna`**

The Bhāṣya specifies present `skandha`, `āyatana`, and `dhātu` bases. Its
reason is `sattvādhiṣṭhānapravṛttatva`: prātimokṣa operates with a living
being as its support or objective basis. Past and future beings are not
presently counted as living beings in that operative sense.

**`maula`**

For the two higher restraints, `maula` means the root karmapathas only. Their
acquisition does not extend to preparation, aftermath, or merely
prohibition-based faults.

**`sarvakāla`**

All times means:

```text
past
present
future
```

Meditation-born and uncontaminated restraints are acquired from bases
belonging to all three times.

## 4. Grammar

**Desire-realm restraint**

```text
kāma-āptaḥ [saṃvaraḥ]
sarva-ubhayebhyaḥ
vartamānebhyaḥ
āpyate
```

> Desire-realm restraint is acquired, from present bases, with respect to all
> [three stages] and both [classes].

The singular passive `āpyate` agrees with the understood singular `saṃvaraḥ`.
The two ablative expressions distinguish the range included and the temporal
status of its acquisition-bases.

**The two higher restraints**

```text
dhyāna-anāsrava-saṃvarau
maulebhyaḥ
sarva-kālebhyaḥ
[āpyete]
```

> Meditation-born and uncontaminated restraint are acquired with respect to
> root karmapathas, from bases belonging to all times.

The subject is dual. A corresponding dual verb is understood from the first
half.

**Acquisition-basis is not the action restrained**

The Bhāṣya corrects a possible misunderstanding. “From present [bases]” does
not mean that presently occurring karmapathas are prevented at the exact
moment of acquisition. It means:

```text
vartamāna-adhiṣṭhāna
    a presently existing support or objective basis
```

Strictly, only future action can be restrained. A past act is complete, and a
present act is already occurring.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Desire-realm restraint is acquired, from present bases, with respect to all
> and both. Meditation-born and uncontaminated restraint are acquired with
> respect to root [karmapathas], from bases belonging to all times.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Prātimokṣa restraint, which belongs to the desire realm, is acquired from
> present objective bases with respect to all three stages of misconduct—the
> root act, its preparation, and its aftermath—and with respect to both
> sentient and non-sentient bases and both naturally blameworthy and
> prohibition-based faults. Meditation-born and uncontaminated restraints are
> acquired only with respect to root karmapathas, but from bases belonging to
> the past, present, and future.

The three stages, the two binary divisions, and the `skandha`–`āyatana`–
`dhātu` account of temporal bases are determinations supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The three restraints do not form a scalar hierarchy in which each higher
> form simply contains everything below it. Prātimokṣa is temporally grounded
> in present embodied relations yet ranges broadly across preparation, root
> action, aftermath, and both natural and prescribed faults. Meditation-born
> and uncontaminated restraint penetrate the root karmic course across all
> three times, but do not possess the same breadth across stages and
> prohibition-types. Each restraint has a universality determined by its own
> mode of acquisition.

The temporal reach of the higher restraints must not be confused with the
possibility of preventing completed action. Their acquisition concerns bases
in all three times; restraint as prohibition bears upon future action.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Three restraints, different fields**

```text
prātimokṣa-saṃvara
    desire-realm undertaking

dhyāna-saṃvara
    born from meditative attainment

anāsrava-saṃvara
    born from the uncontaminated noble Path
```

The opening question asks whether acquiring one restraint from a given basis
entails acquiring the other two from that same basis. The answer is no.

**`maula`, `prayoga`, and `pṛṣṭha`**

These are phases, not three independent substances:

```text
preparation
    → root karmic act
    → aftermath or continuation
```

Prātimokṣa covers the articulated practical course. The higher restraints in
this analysis are acquired from the root karmapatha alone.

**Sentient and non-sentient bases**

Prātimokṣa may concern a living being directly or a non-sentient object
involved in a prescribed discipline. The Bhāṣya’s `ubhaya` prevents its field
from being reduced to direct injury of sentient beings alone.

**Natural and prescribed fault**

The distinction from VAK 4.34 becomes a scope-marker here:

```text
prātimokṣa
    natural faults
    + prohibition-based faults

dhyāna and uncontaminated restraint
    root karmapathas
    not merely prescribed faults
```

**`sattvādhiṣṭhānapravṛtta`**

Prātimokṣa operates on the basis of living beings. This explains its
restriction to present objective bases. The point is not that its ethical
consequences last for only one moment, but that its relation is instituted
within a presently existing field of living supports.

**`vartamānādhiṣṭhāna`**

This term resolves the final objection:

```text
present basis of acquisition
    ≠ presently occurring prohibited action
```

The vow arises with respect to present supports while determining future
non-performance.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Scope matrix**

| Restraint | Temporal bases | Act stages | Fault classes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prātimokṣa | present | root, preparation, aftermath | natural and prohibition-based |
| Meditation-born | past, present, future | root karmapatha | root fault, not merely prescribed fault |
| Uncontaminated | past, present, future | root karmapatha | root fault, not merely prescribed fault |

This table shows why “higher” cannot mean “strict superset.”

**The four-cornered analysis**

The Bhāṣya makes the crossing scopes exact:

```text
1. prātimokṣa only
    present preparation or aftermath
    and prohibition-based faults

2. higher restraints only
    past or future root karmapathas

3. both
    present root karmapathas

4. neither
    past or future preparation and aftermath
```

The fourth corner concerns the acquisition relation under analysis. It does
not claim that past and future preparatory acts are unreal or doctrinally
meaningless.

**Crossing universals**

```text
prātimokṣa
    narrower in temporal basis
    broader in stage and fault-type

higher restraints
    broader in temporal basis
    narrower in stage and fault-type
```

There is no single quantity called “scope” along which the restraints can be
ranked without remainder.

**Basis and prohibitive direction**

```text
acquired from present supports
    does not mean
past or present action is prevented

restraint strictly governs
    future non-performance
```

The distinction prevents acquisition, objective basis, and temporal efficacy
from being collapsed.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Universality is multidimensional**

VAK 4.35 is a strong correction to a habitual picture of spiritual ascent:

```text
ordinary
    < meditative
    < uncontaminated
```

That ordering may hold with respect to origin or liberating status, but it
does not determine every feature. Prātimokṣa includes preparatory,
fundamental, and subsequent phases that the two higher restraints do not
include in this acquisition analysis.

The appropriate structure is a matrix, not a ladder.

**The catuṣkoṭika as exact discrimination**

The four corners are not skeptical alternatives. Together they exhaust the
crossing relation between temporal basis and act-stage:

```text
ordinary only
higher only
both
neither
```

The method prevents one genuine overlap—present root karmapathas—from being
mistaken for complete identity between the systems.

**The present institutes a future determination**

Prātimokṣa arises through a present relation to living beings, yet its
prohibitive force is prospective:

```text
present practical field
    → acquisition of restraint
    → future action held under determination
```

This is not retroactive control over vanished action. Nor is it a mysterious
substance projected into the future. The present undertaking institutes a
standing practical relation whose content concerns what the bearer will not
do.

**Restraint as a typed determination**

A restraint must therefore be understood through several coordinated fields:

```text
origin
realm
objective basis
temporal basis
act-stage
fault-class
direction of prohibition
```

Calling all three simply “restraint” states their genus but not their complete
determination. The differences are functional Aspects of distinct modes of
acquisition.

This is the Organon contact. The full decompositions, temporal doctrine, and
four corners remain Bhāṣya determinations rather than additions to the verse.
