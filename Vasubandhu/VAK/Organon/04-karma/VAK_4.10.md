# VAK_4.10

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> समुत्थानं द्विधा हेतुतत्क्षणोत्थानसंज्ञितम् ।
>
> प्रवर्तकं तयोः आद्यं द्वितीयमनुवर्तकम् ॥ ४.१० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> samutthānaṃ dvidhā hetu-tat-kṣaṇotthāna-saṃjñitam /
>
> pravartakaṃ tayor ādyaṃ dvitīyam anuvartakam // 4.10 //

VAK 4.09 showed how ethical determination passes from an associated cognitive
complex into action and other products through `samutthāna`.

VAK 4.10 now differentiates `samutthāna` itself.

```text
hetu-samutthāna
    → causal origination
    → projects the action
    → pravartaka

tat-kṣaṇa-samutthāna
    → origination present in the moment of performance
    → accompanies the execution
    → anuvartaka
```

The distinction is temporal, causal, and functional.

What initiates an action is not conceptually identical with what keeps pace
with that action while it is being performed.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

dvidhā                     → dvidhā

saṃjñitam                  → saṃjñitam
pravartakam                → pravartakam
tayoḥ                      → tayoḥ
ādyam                      → ādyam
dvitīyam                   → dvitīyam
anuvartakam                → anuvartakam
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| samutthānam | nominative neuter singular action noun | originating condition; activation from which action proceeds |
| dvidhā | indeclinable adverb | in two ways; twofold |
| hetu | compound member | cause; prior causal ground |
| tat | demonstrative compound member | that; the action under discussion |
| kṣaṇa | compound member | moment; here the moment in which the action occurs |
| utthāna | action noun from `ud-√sthā` | arising; activation |
| saṃjñitam | nominative neuter singular past passive participle | designated; named |
| pravartakam | nominative neuter singular agent/function noun | initiating; setting into operation |
| tayoḥ | genitive dual pronoun | of those two |
| ādyam | nominative neuter singular ordinal adjective | the first |
| dvitīyam | nominative neuter singular ordinal adjective | the second |
| anuvartakam | nominative neuter singular agent/function noun | continuing with; accompanying and sustaining |

### The compound naming the two forms

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed compound explicitly:

```text
hetu-samutthānam
tat-kṣaṇa-samutthānam ca
```

The first is the causal source that impels or projects an action.

The second exists in the very moment in which the action is being performed:

```text
tatra eva kṣaṇe tad-bhāvāt
    because it exists in that very moment
```

### `Pravartaka` and `anuvartaka`

Both terms derive from `√vṛt`, but their prefixes specify different functions:

```text
pra-√vṛt
    → set forth
    → initiate
    → bring into operation

anu-√vṛt
    → follow along
    → continue with
    → accompany in execution
```

`Anuvartaka` should not be reduced to an abstract substance that survives
through time. It names a contemporaneous causal function.

### `Tat-kṣaṇa` and momentary presentation

The expression does not by itself posit a separately existing temporal atom.

Its secure grammatical force is relational:

```text
the condition is present
    in the same moment as the performed action
```

Any stronger theory about the ultimate ontology of moments belongs to a wider
doctrinal argument, not to the morphology of this compound alone.

### Witness caution

The compound boundaries are lightly restored for analysis. The research
witness remains unchanged.

The rapid dialogue following the verse makes the argumentative roles clear,
but not every brief objection and response carries an independently marked
speaker. The uncertainty concerns voice assignment, not the twofold definition
of `samutthāna`.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first sentence is a nominal predication:

```text
samutthānam
    dvidhā [bhavati]

the originating condition
    is twofold
```

The compound supplies the two designations:

```text
[samutthānam]
    hetu-samutthāna-[iti]
    tat-kṣaṇa-samutthāna-[iti] ca
    saṃjñitam

[the originating condition]
    is designated
    causal origination
    and origination in that very moment
```

The second line distributes the functions:

```text
tayoḥ
    ādyam
        pravartakam

    dvitīyam
        anuvartakam

of those two,
    the first
        is the initiator

    the second
        is the continuator
```

The Bhāṣya explains the predicates with causal nouns:

```text
hetu-samutthānam
    pravartakam
    ākṣepakatvāt

causal origination
    is initiating
    because it possesses projecting or impelling capacity
```

```text
tat-kṣaṇa-samutthānam
    anuvartakam
    kriyā-kāla-anuvartanāt

contemporaneous origination
    is continuing
    because it follows along during the time of performance
```

The relation can therefore be construed without treating the two conditions as
two independent actions:

```text
one performed action
    has an initiating causal Aspect
    and a contemporaneous operative Aspect
```

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The originating condition is twofold, designated causal origination and
> origination in that very moment. Of these two, the first is the initiator;
> the second is the continuator.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The mental origination of bodily or verbal action has two distinct causal
> functions. The first is causal origination: it initiates the action because
> it projects or impels the action into operation. The second is
> contemporaneous origination: it is present in the moment of performance and
> continues along with the action throughout its execution. Without this
> contemporaneous accompaniment, even an action already projected would lack
> the full operative character of the activity of a living agent.

Projection through `ākṣepakatva`, continuation throughout `kriyākāla`, and the
contrast with a corpse are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Action is not explained by one isolated intention followed by a mechanically
> unfolding bodily event. Its genesis contains two functional moments. A prior
> determination makes the transition into action possible and projects the
> deed. A contemporaneous determination inhabits the performance, maintaining
> its character as the deed is executed. Initiation and execution belong to
> one practical process, but they are not the same causal relation.

This gives the functional pipeline an internal temporal articulation:

```text
projection
    → transition into action

operative accompaniment
    → continuation throughout performance
```

Persistence here is already being analyzed without the image of an unchanged
entity travelling through successive moments.

The determination persists because distinct Aspects of the process project and
continue it.

| Organon term | Function here |
|---|---|
| Projective Aspect | causal function that determines transition into action |
| Operative Aspect | contemporaneous function that maintains action during execution |
| Functional pipeline | ordered unity of initiation, performance, continuation, and result |

These terms expose the logical work of the passage. They do not replace
`hetu-samutthāna`, `tat-kṣaṇa-samutthāna`, `pravartaka`, or `anuvartaka` in the
textual translation.

## 8. Logical Determination

### The question inherited from 4.09

VAK 4.09 stated that bodily and verbal karma receive ethical determination
through their originating condition.

An objector asks:

```text
if bodily and verbal karma are wholesome or unwholesome
    through samutthāna,

why are the great elements supporting that karma
    not equally wholesome or unwholesome?
```

The first answer distinguishes purposive direction:

```text
the performer's intention
    is directed toward the karma

the performer's intention
    is not directed toward the supporting mahābhūtas
```

Therefore causal or material dependence alone does not transmit every
predicate indiscriminately to every supporting condition.

This is another Aspect distinction:

```text
material support
    ≠ intentional object
    ≠ ethically originated action
```

### The unresolved samādhi-born `avijñapti` problem

The answer immediately encounters a harder case.

```text
samādhi-born avijñapti
    → has no distinct intention directed toward the avijñapti itself

non-concentrated citta
    → cannot originate it
    → because it belongs to a different contemplative level
```

If intention toward the produced action explains ethical transmission, how is
the goodness of samādhi-born `avijñapti` established?

The Bhāṣya adds a pressure test:

```text
if arising from samādhi were sufficient by itself,
then the divine eye and divine ear
might also have to be called wholesome
```

The text responds:

```text
kartavyo 'tra yatnaḥ
    effort or careful work is required here
```

This is not a completed solution.

The official study therefore preserves the question:

```text
What exact relation transmits ethical determination
to samādhi-born avijñapti
without transmitting it indiscriminately
to every samādhi-produced capacity or support?
```

That question is central to the future positive Kant-clean and Yoga-clean
account of `avijñapti`.

### Scriptural sequence and Abhidharma analysis

Another apparent contradiction concerns wrong view.

The preceding analysis stated that a cognition abandoned by seeing does not
immediately originate outward manifestation because it proceeds inwardly.

But scripture gives a sequence:

```text
wrong view
    → wrong conceptual determination
        → wrong speech
            → wrong bodily action
```

The distinction between the two `samutthānas` removes the contradiction.

```text
wrong view or wrong conceptual determination
    may function as hetu-samutthāna
    → causal initiator of the sequence

another citta present during performance
    functions as tat-kṣaṇa-samutthāna
    → contemporaneous continuator
```

A cognition can therefore belong to the causal genesis of an action without
being the cognition present at the moment of bodily or verbal manifestation.

### Projective and operative Aspects

The causal structure is:

```text
Projective Aspect
    hetu-samutthāna
    pravartaka
    ākṣepakatva

        ↓ transition into performance

Operative Aspect
    tat-kṣaṇa-samutthāna
    anuvartaka
    kriyākālānuvartana
```

The first answers:

```text
Why did this action begin?
```

The second answers:

```text
What makes the projected action operative
throughout its actual performance?
```

### The corpse argument

The objector asks what efficacy the contemporaneous condition adds.

If the prior condition has already projected the action, why is another mental
condition needed during performance?

The Bhāṣya answers:

```text
without contemporaneous accompaniment,
the projected action would not be fully operative;
it would be like the activity of a corpse
```

The comparison is functional.

An externally describable bodily movement is not yet equivalent to a living,
presently enacted performance.

The contemporaneous mind gives the action its explicit operative actuality.

### The unconscious-state objection

The objector then invokes restraint arising in an `acittaka` condition.

If restraint can arise without active mind, how can contemporaneous mental
accompaniment be necessary?

The response is carefully limited:

```text
when mind is present,
the action or restraint is more explicit,
more distinct,
or more fully forceful
```

Thus the argument need not establish that no practical determination can occur
without active consciousness.

It establishes that conscious contemporaneous accompaniment makes the
performance `sphuṭatara`.

The difference is one of operative articulation, not simple existence versus
nonexistence.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### The pipeline becomes internally temporal

VAK 4.09 showed Dharma arranging itself through intrinsic, associative,
originative, ultimate, and maturation Aspects.

VAK 4.10 enters the originative link and discovers that it is itself internally
differentiated.

```text
samutthāna
    is not one undivided push

it contains
    projection into action
    and continuation in performance
```

The functional pipeline therefore has temporal depth:

```text
ethical determination
    → projective initiation
        → contemporaneous execution
            → completed action
                → continuing efficacy
```

An action is not simply present or absent. It becomes actual through a
structured passage.

### `Kṣaṇa` as an operative relation

The expression `tat-kṣaṇa-samutthāna` is especially important after our
discussion of momentariness.

The Bhāṣya uses `kṣaṇa` to locate a relation of contemporaneity:

```text
the mental continuator exists
in the moment of the action
```

Nothing in this function requires us to imagine the moment as a transcendent
temporal atom.

The moment is the station at which the action and its operative mental
condition are jointly actual.

```text
kṣaṇa
    → modal station of performance

not necessarily
    → self-subsisting temporal particle
```

The appearance of the action vanishes. Yet the action is intelligible because
its projective ground, operative accompaniment, and later efficacy form one
differentiated process.

### Persistence through differentiated functions

This verse gives a positive model of persistence.

The action does not persist because one identical sensible presentation
remains unchanged.

Nor must one posit a hidden substance that travels underneath its appearances.

It persists because its determination is handed through distinct functional
Aspects:

```text
the initiator projects

the continuator accompanies

the performance realizes

the karmic structure retains efficacy
```

The unity belongs to the practical determination.

The difference belongs to the functions through which that determination
becomes actual.

### A living action is more than bodily displacement

The corpse analogy makes an existential distinction.

The same external configuration of bodily movement does not by itself exhaust
the meaning of action.

```text
mere material displacement
    → externally describable occurrence

living action
    → projected determination
    → contemporaneous conscious participation
    → practical performance
```

This does not make matter unreal.

It shows that the semantic unity of a deed cannot be derived from its material
support alone.

The performer's intention is directed toward the action, not toward the great
elements as such. The material, intentional, ethical, and operative Aspects
must therefore be discriminated.

### The positive `avijñapti` problem becomes exact

The Bhāṣya's refusal to give an easy answer is valuable.

Samādhi-born `avijñapti` cannot be explained simply by saying:

```text
the agent explicitly intended the avijñapti
```

Nor can it be explained by saying:

```text
everything generated through samādhi is wholesome
```

The divine eye and divine ear counterexample blocks that overgeneralization.

We therefore need a more exact positive account:

```text
samādhi
    → establishes a disciplined field

within that field
    → restraint functions as a continuing practical determination

its ethical validity
    → belongs through its role in that disciplined pipeline

not merely through
    explicit intention toward a hidden object
    or causal proximity to samādhi
```

That is only the emerging Organon hypothesis. The Bhāṣya leaves the technical
problem open, and so do we.

But the required form is becoming visible: a positive `avijñapti` must be an
Aspect of disciplined practical persistence, not a transcendent carrier.

### Abhidharma as discrimination of causal functions

The strength of the passage is that it does not solve a contradiction by
discarding either scripture or analysis.

It discriminates causal function:

```text
scripture expresses the genetic sequence

Abhidharma distinguishes
    causal initiation
    from immediate execution
```

Wrong view can initiate a process whose manifest action is accompanied by a
different contemporaneous cognition.

The apparent conflict disappears once the functional Aspects are separated.

This is Abhidharma at its best: not atomization, but exact discrimination of
the roles through which one action becomes a complete practical process.
