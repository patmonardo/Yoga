# VAK_4.77

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)


**Two movements in one verse**

The first half gives an attributed residual definition of
`saṃbhinnapralāpa`. The second begins the exact definition of the three mental
unwholesome courses with `abhidhyā`.

## 4. Grammar

**Residual afflicted speech**

```text
ataḥ anyat kliṣṭam [vacanam]
    [saṃbhinnapralāpaḥ]
    iti anye [āhuḥ]

lapanā-gīta-nāṭya-vat
kuśāstra-vat ca
```

> Others say that afflicted speech other than these is disordered or
> frivolous speech, as with flattery, song, drama, and corrupt treatises.

The understood comparison is with false, divisive, and harsh speech. `Anye`
explicitly marks this residual taxonomy as an attributed position.

**Definition of covetousness**

```text
para-sve
    viṣamā spṛhā
        abhidhyā [bhavati]
```

> Covetousness, however, is wrongful longing for another's possession.

The compound is relational: `para-sva` supplies the possession belonging to
another; `viṣamā` qualifies the longing as improper or unjust rather than
desire as such.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Others say that afflicted speech other than these is disordered or
> frivolous speech, as in flattery, song, drama, and corrupt treatises.
> Covetousness, however, is wrongful longing for another's possession.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> According to one transmitted view, `saṃbhinnapralāpa` is the residual class
> of afflicted verbal action outside false, divisive, and harsh speech; its
> examples include ingratiating talk, passion-oriented song, entertainment,
> and corrupt teaching. These expressive forms are not intrinsically
> unwholesome: their classification depends upon their afflictive orientation,
> and some afflicted talk may still fail to reach the decisive status of a
> karmapatha. Covetousness as a mental karmapatha is the wrongful wish to make
> another's possession one's own, whether by force or by some device. It is
> not desire in general and is not yet the bodily act of theft.

The qualifications concerning song, renunciation, non-karmapatha chatter,
appropriative means, and alternative extensions of `abhidhyā` are supplied by
the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Formal coherence does not guarantee wholesome discourse. Speech may possess
> grammar, rhythm, dramatic organization, or systematic argument while its
> practical orientation remains afflicted. Likewise, desire becomes the
> mental course of covetousness only when it projects a wrongful
> transformation of an existing relation: what belongs to another is
> practically posited as what should become mine. The course is already
> relationally determinate before bodily appropriation occurs.

The verse distinguishes two kinds of form:

```text
organized verbal form
    may lack wholesome practical orientation

mental practical form
    may project an objective transformation
    before bodily execution
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Saṃbhinnapralāpa` — disordered or frivolous afflicted speech**

Under the position marked by `anye`, afflicted verbal action remaining after
false, divisive, and harsh speech have been excluded. VAK 4.76 preserved the
broader competing view under which all afflicted speech bears this name.

**`Lapanā` — flattering or ingratiating talk**

The Bhāṣya associates it particularly with wrong livelihood: speech shaped to
gain advantage through pleasing another.

**`Gīta` and `nāṭya` — song and dramatic expression**

Surface forms whose ethical determination varies by orientation. The Bhāṣya
contrasts sensual or vulgar taste (`grāmyarasa`) with connection to
renunciation (`naiṣkramya`).

**`Kuśāstra` — corrupt treatise**

A defective or ethically corrupt systematic discourse. Its inclusion shows
that `saṃbhinnapralāpa` can possess internal organization and intelligible
argument rather than being mere nonsense.

**`Abhidhyā` — covetousness**

The first of the three mental unwholesome karmapathas. It is a determinate
appropriative project directed toward another's possession.

**`Viṣamā spṛhā` — wrongful longing**

The Bhāṣya glosses `viṣamā` through `anyāya`, injustice or improper order. The
longing becomes karmically decisive because it negates the rightful possessive
relation.

**`Svīkaraṇecchā` — wish to make one's own**

The Bhāṣya's exact practical gloss of covetousness:

```text
aho vata yat pareṣāṃ tan mama syāt
    Ah, may what belongs to others become mine.
```

The contemplated appropriation may be by force (`balāt`) or by a device or
stratagem (`upāyāt`).

**`Kāmāvacarī tṛṣṇā` — desire-realm craving**

One reported view uses `abhidhyā` broadly for all desire-realm craving.
Another allows the broad usage but restricts `abhidhyā` as a karmapatha to the
gross, ethically decisive form selected among bad conduct.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Three levels in the verbal field**

The Bhāṣya's cakravartin discussion distinguishes:

```text
verbal activity
    ≠ necessarily afflicted verbal activity

afflicted verbal activity
    ≠ necessarily a completed karmapatha
```

For example:

```text
song + sensual-afflictive orientation
    → afflicted speech

song + renunciation orientation
    → not afflicted in that way

ordinary afflicted chatter
    → may exist without reaching karmapatha status
      according to one transmitted view
```

**Residual taxonomic rule**

```text
AfflictedSpeech(x)
and not FalseSpeech(x)
and not DivisiveSpeech(x)
and not HarshSpeech(x)
    → Saṃbhinnapralāpa(x)
```

This belongs to the position explicitly introduced as that of “others.”

**Constitutive form of covetousness**

```text
actual relation
    belongsTo(object, other)

projected wrongful relation
    belongsTo(object, self)

appropriative wish
    projects the second by negating the first
        = abhidhyā-karmapatha
```

**Root, mental course, bodily course**

```text
lobha
    general unwholesome root
        ↓
abhidhyā
    determinate projected appropriation
        ↓
adattādāna
    completed bodily appropriation
```

The sequence is genetic, not an identity. Covetousness need not culminate in
theft, and theft remains a distinct bodily karmapatha.

**Broad term and selected course**

```text
abhidhyā in broad scriptural usage
    may include desire-realm craving

abhidhyā as karmapatha
    selected gross and decisive wrongful appropriation-project
```

This applies the `audārika-saṃgraha` principle of VAK 4.66: karmapathas select
principal forms from a wider conduct-field.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A mental course is already practical**

`Abhidhyā` is not an inert private item hidden inside a subject. It has an
objective relational content:

```text
another's possession
    → projected as mine
    → without rightful transfer
```

The body has not yet executed the appropriation, but practical determination
has already posited the relation otherwise.

**Coherence and governing end**

The `kuśāstra` example prevents formal organization from serving as a proxy
for truth or ethical validity. A discourse can be fluent, systematic, and
inferentially elaborate while being generated by an afflicted orientation.
The relevant question is how its form, ground, purpose, and consequence belong
together.

**Do not identify analogy with source category**

Modern phenomena such as coherent fabrication or false explanatory closure
may illuminate the structural problem, but they are not simply identical with
the ancient classification. The Bhāṣya's direct determination concerns
afflicted verbal activity and its disputed relation to karmapatha status.

This is the Organon contact. The residual view, examples, song qualification,
three extensions of `abhidhyā`, and selection principle belong to the Bhāṣya;
the projected-relational-transition analysis is the project synthesis.
