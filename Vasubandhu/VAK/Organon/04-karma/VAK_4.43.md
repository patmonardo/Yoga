# VAK_4.43

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नॄणामसंवरो हित्वा षण्ढपण्डद्विधाकृतीन् ।
कुरूंश्च संवरोऽप्येवं देवानां च नॄणां त्रयः ॥ ४.४३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
nṛṇām asaṃvaro hitvā śaṇḍhapaṇḍadvidhākṛtīn /
kurūṃś ca saṃvaro 'py evaṃ devānāṃ ca nṛṇāṃ trayaḥ // 4.43 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
nṛṇām asaṃvaraḥ hitvā śaṇḍha-paṇḍa-dvidhākṛtīn /
kurūn ca saṃvaraḥ api evam devānām ca nṛṇām trayaḥ //
```

The verse uses inherited Abhidharma and Vinaya classifications of bodily
supports. They are retained as textual categories and are not silently mapped
onto modern identities.

**`asaṃvara`**

Non-restraint is not mere absence of restraint. It is an acquired standing
unwholesome form. The question is therefore not which beings can perform an
unwholesome act, but which supports can acquire this persistent determination.

**`saṃvara`**

The three forms already established are:

```text
prātimokṣa-saṃvara
dhyānaja-saṃvara
anāsrava-saṃvara
```

The final `trayaḥ` refers to these three.

**`dvidhākṛti`**

```text
dvidhā
    twofold

kṛti
    formation or constitution
```

The Bhāṣya uses `ubhayavyañjana`, possessing both sexual characteristics, as
the corresponding technical category.

**`Kuru`**

The Kurus are the inhabitants of Uttarakuru. Their exclusion is based on the
Kośa’s account of that world: the required undertaking, meditative discipline,
and harmful practical intention are absent there.

## 4. Grammar

**Distribution of non-restraint**

```text
nṛṇām asaṃvaraḥ [asti]
śaṇḍha-paṇḍa-dvidhākṛtīn kurūn ca hitvā
```

> Non-restraint occurs among humans, excluding the listed bodily categories
> and the inhabitants of Uttarakuru.

The existential verb is understood. `Hitvā` governs both the compound
enumeration and `kurūn`.

**Distribution of restraint**

```text
saṃvaraḥ api evam [nṛṇām asti]
devānām ca [asti]
```

> Restraint occurs likewise among eligible humans and also among gods.

`Evam` carries the human restriction from the preceding clause. The precise
forms available among gods are stated in VAK 4.44.

**The three among humans**

```text
nṛṇām trayaḥ [saṃvarāḥ santi]
```

> Among humans are the three restraints.

The Bhāṣya makes this distribution explicit. The phrase should not be joined
with `devānām` so as to attribute all three indiscriminately to both domains.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Non-restraint occurs among humans, excluding the śaṇḍhas, paṇḍakas, persons
> of twofold constitution, and the Kurus. Restraint occurs likewise and also
> among gods. Among humans are the three [restraints].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Acquired non-restraint occurs only in the human mode of existence, excluding
> the bodily categories listed by the text and the inhabitants of Uttarakuru.
> Acquired restraint occurs among eligible humans under the corresponding
> exclusions and also among gods. Eligible humans can possess all three forms:
> prātimokṣa restraint, meditation-born restraint, and uncontaminated
> restraint; the forms available among gods are specified in the following
> verse.

The phrase “only in the human mode,” the identification of the three, and the
enjambment into VAK 4.44 are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A wholesome or harmful act may occur without producing the technical
> standing form called restraint or non-restraint. Acquisition requires a
> support held by this doctrine to possess the stability, discrimination, and
> affective conditions necessary for the determination to take root. The
> verse therefore distributes not moral worth but eligibility for specific
> persistent karmic forms across modes of embodied existence.

The inherited eligibility claims belong to Vasubandhu’s doctrinal framework;
their support-logic can be analyzed without generalizing those classifications
beyond the text.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Act and standing form**

```text
wholesome action
    ≠ necessarily acquired saṃvara

unwholesome action
    ≠ necessarily acquired asaṃvara
```

Both standing forms require a suitable support and sufficiently stable
conditions of acquisition.

**Bhāṣya account of excluded supports**

For the inherited human categories, the text invokes strong affliction,
incapacity for the required discriminative reflection, and absence of intense
`hrī` and `vyapatrāpya`. It then explains absence of non-restraint through
instability of harmful intention.

These are reported doctrinal claims, not neutral modern descriptions of the
persons to whom the categories might be applied.

**`pratisaṃkhyānākṣamatva`**

This means incapacity for the discriminative reflection the system regards as
necessary for undertaking restraint. It concerns the conditions of technical
acquisition, not the mere ability to perform an outwardly wholesome action.

**`hrī` and `vyapatrāpya`**

```text
hrī
    moral shame or inward ethical concern

vyapatrāpya
    moral apprehension regarding blame or consequence
```

The Bhāṣya treats their intensity as part of the causal field in which
restraint can become stable.

**`asthirāśayatva`**

Instability of practical intention is invoked to explain why non-restraint
also fails to take root. A harmful act-token is insufficient without the
stable practical orientation required for the standing form.

**Uttarakuru**

```text
no relevant samādāna
no relevant samādhi
no intention toward harmful activity
    → neither saṃvara nor asaṃvara
```

The reason differs from that assigned to the bodily categories.

**Barren-field analogy**

The Bhāṣya compares the excluded supports to a barren or saline field
(`ūṣarakṣetra`) in which neither crop grows properly:

```text
causal seed
    + unsuitable field
    → no standing determination
```

The analogy concerns capacity to sustain technical restraint or non-restraint.

**Nāga observing upavāsa**

An objection cites a nāga who observes the eight-limbed upavāsa. Vasubandhu
classifies this as `sucaritamātra`, good conduct only, rather than technical
restraint.

```text
outward conduct matches the observance
    but
technical saṃvara is not acquired
```

## 8. Logical Determination

**Eligibility structure**

```text
act-token
    requires occasion and intention

standing karmic form
    requires eligible support
    + stable causal disposition
    + appropriate acquisition condition
```

The second cannot be inferred merely from the first.

**Contrary standing forms share a field**

The Bhāṣya reasons that restraint and non-restraint are direct contraries
(`pratidvandva`) and arise in the same general field of eligible supports.

```text
support capable of stable practical determination
    → may bear restraint or non-restraint

support judged incapable of such stability
    → bears neither technical form
```

**Three restraints among humans**

```text
eligible human support
    → possible prātimokṣa restraint
    → possible dhyāna-born restraint
    → possible uncontaminated restraint
```

This is a possibility-distribution, not the claim that every eligible human
possesses all three.

**Gods require the next verse**

```text
restraint occurs among gods
    but
which restraint occurs where
    → VAK 4.44
```

The syntactic compression must not anticipate the missing distribution.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The bearer is a condition of actuality**

The verse moves from the internal structure of karmic forms to their concrete
bearers:

```text
definition of restraint
    insufficient by itself

conditions under which it can become actual
    require determinate support
```

A universal form is not therefore a free-floating abstraction. It becomes
actual only through a support capable of acquiring and sustaining it.

**Doctrinal support-theory and inherited exclusion**

The barren-field analogy gives a real causal model, but it is applied through
inherited anthropological classifications that must remain visible as such.
We should neither erase what Vasubandhu says nor convert it directly into a
timeless judgment about modern persons.

The reusable determination is narrower:

```text
persistent practical form
    requires more than matching behavior
```

**Good conduct without technical restraint**

The nāga example prevents technical ontology from swallowing ethical
appearance:

```text
good conduct is genuinely good conduct

yet

it need not instantiate the specific acquired form
called prātimokṣa-saṃvara
```

Different predicates can apply without contamination or cancellation.

**From genesis to distribution**

The chapter has now moved through:

```text
what restraint is
how it is acquired
how it persists
how it is lost
which supports can bear it
```

VAK 4.44 completes the realm-distribution of the three restraints.

This is the Organon contact. The inherited categories, causal explanations,
barren-field analogy, and nāga case belong to the Bhāṣya.
