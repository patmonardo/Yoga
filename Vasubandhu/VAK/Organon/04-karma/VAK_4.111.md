# VAK_4.111

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सर्वत्र सर्वं ददतः कारुण्याद्दानपूरणम् ।
अङ्गच्छेदेऽप्यकोपात्तु रागिणः क्षान्तिशीलयोः ॥ ४.१११ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sarvatra sarvaṃ dadataḥ kāruṇyād dānapūraṇam /
aṅgacchede 'py akopāt tu rāgiṇaḥ kṣāntiśīlayoḥ // 4.111 //
```

The verse begins the account of when the six perfections become complete.
VAK 4.111 determines giving, patience, and discipline; the remaining three
are reserved for the following kārikā.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sarvatra | sarvam | dadataḥ | kāruṇyāt | dāna-pūraṇam |
aṅga-cchede | api | akopāt | tu | rāgiṇaḥ | kṣānti-śīlayoḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `sarvatra` | indeclinable adverb | everywhere, in every relevant field |
| `sarvam` | accusative neuter singular | everything that can be given |
| `dadataḥ` | genitive masculine singular present participle of `√dā` | of one giving |
| `kāruṇyāt` | ablative neuter singular | from compassion, because compassion governs the gift |
| `dāna-pūraṇam` | nominative neuter singular compound | completion of giving |
| `aṅga-cchede` | locative masculine singular compound | while the limbs are being severed |
| `api` | concessive particle | even, although |
| `akopāt` | ablative masculine singular | because of non-arising of anger |
| `tu` | connective or contrastive particle | and, but now |
| `rāgiṇaḥ` | genitive masculine singular | of one still possessing passion |
| `kṣānti-śīlayoḥ` | genitive feminine dual | of patience and moral discipline |

**Two forms of completion**

```text
kāruṇyāt
    sarvatra sarvaṃ dadataḥ
    → dāna-pūraṇam

rāgiṇaḥ
    aṅgacchede api akopāt
    → kṣānti-pūraṇam
    + śīla-pūraṇam
```

The dual `kṣāntiśīlayoḥ` is decisive: one practical response completes two
perfections under different determinations.

## 4. Grammar

**Universal and unreserved giving**

```text
sarvatra
    sarvaṃ
    dadataḥ
    kāruṇyāt
    dāna-pūraṇam [bhavati]
```

> For one giving everything everywhere from compassion, giving is completed.

The genitive participle depends on the completion noun. `Sarvatra` gives the
extension of the field, `sarvam` the completeness of the gift, and the
ablative `kāruṇyāt` the determining ground of the activity.

**Concession under maximal injury**

```text
aṅga-cchede api
```

> Even while the limbs are being severed.

The locative identifies the extreme situation in which the practical
determination is tested; `api` marks the failure of that opposition to reverse
the course.

**Perfection before eradication of passion**

```text
rāgiṇaḥ
    akopāt
    kṣānti-śīlayoḥ [pūraṇam bhavati]
```

> For one still possessing passion, through absence of anger, patience and
> discipline are completed.

`Rāgiṇaḥ` and `kṣāntiśīlayoḥ` are genitives under the understood completion
construction. The Bhāṣya strengthens `akopa`: not even the slightest anger
arises.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> For one giving everything everywhere from compassion, giving is completed.
> And for one still possessing passion, through absence of anger even while
> the limbs are being severed, patience and discipline are completed.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Bodhisattva completes the perfection of giving when everything is given
> to everyone—even bodily eye and marrow—from compassion alone, without
> seeking superior worldly prosperity or distinction in return. Although not
> yet completely free from passion, the Bodhisattva completes patience and
> moral discipline when not even the slightest anger arises while the limbs
> are being cut apart: the injury is endured without hostility, and no
> retaliatory transgression disrupts right conduct.

The extension to everyone, bodily examples, exclusion of worldly reward,
gloss of `rāgin` as not yet free from passion, absence of even minimal anger,
and distinct patience/discipline functions are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Practical perfection has two complementary forms. Giving becomes
> extensively universal when no recipient and no gift is excluded and
> compassion alone supplies the end. Patience and discipline become
> intensively invariant when no degree of external opposition can make anger
> or retaliation govern the deed. Contrary afflictive capacity may remain,
> while the Principle of action is already complete in actuality.

The rational opposition is:

```text
extensive universality
    = every recipient + every gift + one compassionate ground

intensive invariance
    = maximal injury + no anger + no retaliatory transgression
```

Thus:

```text
remaining possibility of passion
    ≠ practical incompletion

perfection of the course
    = invariance of its governing Principle in actuality
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Pāramitā-paripūraṇa` — completion of a perfection**

The stage at which a Bodhisattva activity has become unsurpassable under its
decisive practical test. The criterion is the actual course under a complete
field, not mere repetition of ordinary instances.

**`Sarvatra sarvam` — everywhere, everything**

The two dimensions of giving's extension: no relevant recipient is excluded,
and nothing that can be given is reserved. The Bhāṣya restates this as
`sarvasmai sarvam`, everything to everyone.

**`Kāruṇya` — compassion**

The sole determining ground of perfected giving. The gift is not used as a
means to superior worldly prosperity, status, or distinction for the giver.

**`Abhyudaya-viśeṣa` — superior worldly prosperity or distinction**

The excluded return sought through ordinary meritorious giving. Its absence
separates universal compassionate Kriyā from an exchange aimed at the agent's
own conditioned elevation.

**`Aṅgaccheda` — severing of limbs**

The extreme bodily opposition under which patience and discipline are tested.
The test concerns actual non-anger and non-retaliation, not an abstract claim
about what the agent might endure.

**`Akopa` — non-arising of anger**

The common practical ground completing two perfections. As patience it is
non-hostile endurance; as discipline it prevents retaliatory misconduct.

**`Rāgin` / `avītarāga` — still possessing passion**

The important qualification that total eradication of passion has not yet
occurred. The pāramitās can nevertheless be complete as governing courses of
conduct.

**`Kṣānti` — patience**

Invariance of non-hostility under injury. It determines how opposition is
received without allowing anger to become the Principle of response.

**`Śīla` — moral discipline**

Preservation of non-transgressive activity under the same pressure. The
absence of retaliatory action gives the external practical determination of
the internal non-anger.

## 8. Logical Determination

Let:

```text
A = Bodhisattva agent
R = relevant recipient field
G = everything available to give
C = compassion
I = maximal bodily injury
P = remaining passion-capacity
H = anger
T = retaliatory transgression
```

**Giving**

```text
ForAll(R): Gives(A, G, R)
GroundedIn(Giving(A), C)
NotAimedAt(Giving(A), WorldlyReward)
    → Completes(A, GivingPerfection)
```

**Patience and discipline**

```text
Possesses(A, P)
Suffers(A, I)
NotArises(H, A)
NotPerforms(A, T)
    → Completes(A, PatiencePerfection)
    → Completes(A, DisciplinePerfection)
```

**Capacity and actuality**

```text
PossibleAfflictiveCapacity(A)
    does not entail
AfflictionGovernsActualCourse(A)
```

**One event, two determinations**

```text
NonAngryNonRetaliation(A, I)
    → InternalDetermination(A, Patience)
    → ExternalCourseDetermination(A, Discipline)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.110 showed quantitative karmic multiplicity integrating into Buddha-form.
VAK 4.111 now shows how particular pāramitās reach qualitative completion.
Their perfection is demonstrated when the governing Principle extends across
the complete relevant field or remains invariant under the strongest possible
opposition.

This gives us an exact practical definition of universality. Generosity is
not universal because it entertains the thought “all beings.” It becomes
universal because every relevant recipient and every giveable object is
actually subsumed under compassion, with no self-directed worldly return
governing the exchange.

Patience and discipline give the negative side of the same structure. The
body can be literally divided without dividing the ethical unity of the
course. This sharply reverses the Saṅgha-schism sequence:

```text
false speech divides normative recognition
    → spiritual Object loses unity

bodily division fails to produce anger or retaliation
    → practical subject preserves unity
```

For Samyama as Practical Reason, these are two complete Schema tests:

```text
Principle applied to the whole recipient-object Bhūmi
    → extensive completion of dāna

Principle maintained through maximal contradictory appearance
    → intensive completion of kṣānti and śīla
```

The word `rāgiṇaḥ` is crucial for the Path. Practical perfection can precede
complete ontological purification of the continuum. The contrary capacity
has not vanished, but it no longer determines the relevant Kriyā. This is why
the Path can be understood through perfected courses of action rather than
only through a static inventory of eliminated afflictions.

VAK 4.111 therefore confirms that the Path is the truth of Karma in a positive
form: a karmic course becomes pāramitā when its Principle attains universality
and invariance in practice.
