# VAK_4.60

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अशुभं रूपकामाप्तं शुभं चैव यथाक्रमम् ।
कृष्णशुक्लोभयं कर्म तत्क्षयाय निरास्रवम् ॥ ४.६० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
aśubhaṃ rūpakāmāptaṃ śubhaṃ caiva yathākramam /
kṛṣṇaśuklobhayaṃ karma tatkṣayāya nirāsravam // 4.60 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
aśubham rūpa-āptam kāma-āptam śubham ca eva yathā-kramam /
kṛṣṇa-śukla-ubhayam karma tat-kṣayāya nir-āsravam //
```

The Bhāṣya distributes three karmic fields across black, white, and both, then
identifies karma without influxes as directed toward their exhaustion.

**Three corresponding classes**

```text
aśubha karma
    → kṛṣṇa

rūpāpta śubha karma
    → śukla

kāmāpta śubha karma
    → kṛṣṇa-śukla
```

**`nirāsrava / anāsrava`**

The kārikā uses `nirāsrava`; the Bhāṣya uses the standard `anāsrava`. Both mean
karma without influxes in this passage.

**`tatkṣaya`**

`Tat` refers to the preceding three productive karmic classes. The fourth
karma is oriented toward their exhaustion.

## 4. Grammar

**Respectively black, white, and both**

```text
aśubham [karma]
rūpa-āptam śubham [karma]
kāma-āptam śubham [karma]

yathā-kramam
kṛṣṇam śuklam ubhayam [bhavati]
```

> Unwholesome karma, wholesome form-domain karma, and wholesome desire-domain
> karma are respectively black, white, and both.

`Yathākramam` controls the ordered distribution. “Both” is clarified by the
Bhāṣya at the level of the continuum, not as one karma with two intrinsic
natures.

**Karma without influxes**

```text
nir-āsravam karma
tat-kṣayāya [bhavati]
```

> Karma without influxes is for the exhaustion of those.

The dative expresses purpose or direction. The fourth karma acts upon the
preceding productive karmic system.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Unwholesome karma, wholesome karma belonging to the form domain, and
> wholesome karma belonging to the desire domain are respectively black,
> white, and both. Karma without influxes is for the exhaustion of those.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Unwholesome karma is black and has undesirable black maturation. Wholesome
> form-domain karma is white and has desirable white maturation. Wholesome
> desire-domain karma is called black-and-white with respect to the continuum,
> where wholesome and unwholesome karmas and their results are interspersed;
> no single karma is intrinsically both. Karma without influxes is neither
> black nor white in this classification, produces no ordinary maturation, and
> conduces to the exhaustion of the preceding three karmic classes.

The continuum-relative account of mixture, qualification of “not white,” and
the absence of ordinary maturation are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Productive karma differentiates into dark, bright, and mixed continuums of
> conditioned maturation. The mixture belongs to the articulated whole, not
> to the intrinsic nature of one member: opposed determinations can coexist in
> a continuum without becoming identical in a single act. Karma without
> influxes then changes the function of activity itself. Rather than producing
> another conditioned result, it operates toward the exhaustion of the karmic
> circuit that generates such results.

Negation here is practical and determinate: neither black nor white means an
activity no longer functioning within the reproductive opposition.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Black karma**

```text
unwholesome karma
    → defiled in its causal character
    → black

undesirable maturation
    → black maturation
```

The karma and its maturation receive corresponding but separately grounded
predicates.

**White karma**

```text
form-domain wholesome karma
    → not interspersed with unwholesome karma there
    → white

desirable maturation
    → white maturation
```

**Why formless-domain karma is omitted**

The omission follows the scope of the particular sūtra formulation, which
presupposes intermediate and rebirth existence together with bodily, verbal,
and mental karma. Vasubandhu notes that wholesome formless-domain karma is
included in another sūtra. Its omission here is not an ontological denial.

**Black-and-white karma**

The decisive statement is:

```text
saṃtānataḥ
    with respect to the continuum

na svabhāvataḥ
    not with respect to intrinsic nature
```

Black and white are mutually opposed. One karma cannot intrinsically possess
both characters, but one desire-domain continuum can contain interspersed
wholesome and unwholesome karmas.

**Asymmetry of mixture**

The Bhāṣya does not call unwholesome karma mixed merely because wholesome
karma can occur in the same continuum. Unwholesome karma is strong in the
desire domain, whereas weaker wholesome karma is characteristically
interspersed with it.

**Neither black nor white**

```text
anāsrava karma
    not black because not defiled
    not white because it lacks white karmic maturation
    without ordinary vipāka
    opposed to continued pravṛtti
    conducive to karmakṣaya
```

The Bhāṣya says `aśukla` is context-governed here. Elsewhere wholesome dharmas
may be called white; here “not white” denies membership in the white-vipāka
class.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Member-property and continuum-property**

```text
SVABHAVA LEVEL
    BLACK(K) xor WHITE(K)

SAMTANA LEVEL
    contains black karmas
    and contains white karmas
    → MIXED(continuum)
```

Therefore:

```text
MIXED(continuum)
    does not entail
MIXED(each member intrinsically)
```

**Productive and exhaustive activity**

```text
black / white / mixed karma
    → generates conditioned vipāka

anāsrava karma
    → no ordinary vipāka
    → opposes continued karmic production
    → conduces to exhaustion of karma
```

**Negative determination has positive function**

```text
not black
not white
    does not mean
indeterminate inactivity

it means
determinate activity whose function is karmic exhaustion
```

**System-level self-operation**

```text
karmic activity
    can reproduce the karmic circuit

karmic activity without influxes
    can terminate that circuit
```

The category “karma” therefore contains both production and exhaustion.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Kleśa as Ground and its absence**

The first three classes remain within action grounded by conditioned
affliction and its maturation. The fourth is determined through absence of the
influx-ground:

```text
kleśa-ground present
    → reproductive karma

kleśa-ground absent
    → anāsrava activity
    → exhaustion rather than further production
```

The absence is not an empty nothing. It changes the function of activity.

**`Tadabhāva` as practical transformation**

In the Yoga–Kośa synthesis, the non-being of the deforming Ground explains why
the same general category of action no longer produces the former circuit.
The negative determination is expressed by its positive practical consequence:
the exhaustion of karma.

**The wheel operates upon itself**

Practical Dharma theory reaches reflexivity here:

```text
karma produces world and experience

anāsrava karma acts upon karma
    → karmakṣaya
```

Liberation is not represented as mere inactivity but as an operation that
terminates reproductive causality.

**Contradiction belongs at the correct level**

Black and white cannot be intrinsic predicates of one karma, but they can be
determinations within one continuum. The Bhāṣya's explicit level distinction
is exactly what prevents false contradiction.

This is the Organon contact. The four allocations, continuum distinction,
ārūpya scope, asymmetry, and semantic qualification of `aśukla` belong to the
Bhāṣya; the `tadabhāva` formulation is project synthesis.
