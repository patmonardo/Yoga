# VAK 4.89 Bhāṣya — Completion of the Action-quality Matrix

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
aśubhasya śubhādyā dve trīṇi catvāry anukramam /
avyākṛtasya dve trīṇi trīṇi caite śubhādayaḥ // 4.89 //
```

> For unwholesome action, results beginning with the wholesome are
> respectively two, three, and four. For indeterminate action, these results
> beginning with the wholesome are two, three, and three.

Together with 4.88, this verse completes a three-by-three matrix relating the
ethical quality of action to the ethical quality of dharmas functioning as
its results under the five technical result relations.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

For unwholesome action, results beginning with the wholesome are two, three,
and four respectively.

Wholesome dharmas stand as two results of unwholesome action: the
activity-produced and dominant results. Unwholesome dharmas stand as three
results, with maturation and disconnection excluded. Indeterminate dharmas
stand as four results, with disconnection excluded. For the indeterminate
views of a real personality and of grasping an extreme are corresponding
results of the pervasive unwholesome dharmas and of those abandoned by seeing
suffering.

For indeterminate action, these same classes beginning with wholesome results
are two, three, and three.

Wholesome dharmas stand as two results of indeterminate action: the
activity-produced and dominant results. Unwholesome dharmas stand as three
results, with maturation and disconnection excluded. For the unwholesome
dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering and the other truths are corresponding
results of the two indeterminate views. Indeterminate dharmas stand under
these same three result relations.

The table makes two global constraints visible:

- only indeterminate dharmas function as maturation results;
- only wholesome dharmas function as disconnection results.

Activity-produced and dominant relations cross every quality boundary. The
corresponding relation is more selective but can still cross between
unwholesome and indeterminate dharmas.

## 6. Why Quality Is Not Causal Isolation

A simple moral pipeline would say:

```text
wholesome action   → wholesome result
unwholesome action → unwholesome result
indeterminate      → indeterminate
```

The Bhāṣya rejects that architecture. The ethical quality of the source and
the ethical quality of a result are separate coordinates. What connects them
is a typed causal relation.

Thus an unwholesome action may condition a wholesome dharma as an
activity-produced or dominant result without ripening into wholesomeness. A
wholesome action may similarly occasion an unwholesome dharma without that
being its maturation, corresponding result, or release.

The relation type prevents a cross-quality causal connection from being
misread as moral transformation.

## 7. Reciprocal Correspondence of Views and Afflictions

The Bhāṣya explains the cross-quality `niṣyanda` relation through two
indeterminate views:

1. personality view (`satkāyadṛṣṭi`),
2. extreme view (`antagrāhadṛṣṭi`).

These views are ethically indeterminate in the Sarvāstivāda classification,
yet they stand as corresponding results of pervasive unwholesome dharmas and
unwholesome dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering. Conversely, unwholesome
dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering and the remaining truths can stand as
corresponding results of the two views.

```text
unwholesome pervasive affliction
    ↔ corresponding continuity ↔
indeterminate personality and extreme views
```

The reciprocity does not make their ethical qualities identical. It says
that similar causal continuation can cross the boundary because the views
and afflictions mutually reinforce one another within the continuum.

## 8. Indeterminate Is Determinate in Every Other Respect

`Avyākṛta` means that a dharma is not declared wholesome or unwholesome. It
does not mean causally inert, structurally vague, or outside the practical
system.

The indeterminate row demonstrates this strongly. Indeterminate action can:

- produce or dominate wholesome dharmas;
- correspond to, produce, or dominate unwholesome dharmas;
- correspond to, produce, or dominate indeterminate dharmas.

Ethical nonclassification therefore coexists with exact causal typing.

## 9. Organon Contact Point

This is a genuine agent architecture table. The correct primitive is a typed
edge:

```text
Action
    -[ResultType]->
Dharma
```

Both nodes carry quality, while the edge carries the causal determination:

```text
ActionResultEdge {
    sourceAction
    sourceQuality
    resultDharma
    resultQuality
    resultType
}
```

No inference may be made from node quality alone. In particular:

```text
sourceQuality ≠ resultQuality
crossQualityEdge ≠ maturation
sameQualityEdge ≠ correspondence automatically
```

The five-class result table classifies action contexts; this action matrix
classifies the typed edges available between differently qualified dharmas.
Together they form a compact causal schema for the Rational Moral Agent.

This schema formulation is the Organon reconstruction. The exact matrix,
views, abandonment classes, and result memberships belong to the Bhāṣya.
