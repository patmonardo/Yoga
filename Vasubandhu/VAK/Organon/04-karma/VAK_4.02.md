# VAK_4.02

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> ते तु विज्ञप्त्यविज्ञप्ती कायविज्ञप्तिरिष्यते ।
>
> संस्थानं न गतिर्यस्मात् संस्कृतं क्षणिकं व्ययात् ॥ ४.२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> te tu vijñaptyavijñaptī kāyavijñaptir iṣyate /
>
> saṃsthānaṃ na gatir yasmāt saṃskṛtaṃ kṣaṇikaṃ vyayāt // 4.2 //

VAK 4.01 distinguished volition from the bodily and verbal actions generated
by it. `Te` now resumes those latter two actions. The Bhāṣya makes the opening
compression explicit: bodily and verbal karma are **each** to be understood as
having manifest and non-manifest modes.

| kṣaṇikam | nominative neuter singular adjective | momentary; immediately perishing after arising |

`Vijñapti` belongs to the semantic field of causing something to be distinctly
known or manifest. It must remain distinct from `vijñāna`:

```text
vijñāna
    differentiated cognition

vijñapti
    manifestation / making-known through action
```

`Avijñapti` is not simple nonexistence or failure to act. The verse names it as
a mode of bodily and verbal karma, but its positive constitution must be
established by the following units.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening resumes 4.01:

```text
te tu
    [kāya-vāk-karmaṇī]

as for those two
    [bodily and verbal actions]
```

The isolated syntax is compressed because `te` resumes a neuter dual whereas
`vijñaptyavijñaptī` names a feminine dual pair. The Bhāṣya supplies the exact
distribution:

```text
te tu kāyavākkarmaṇī
    pratyekaṃ
    vijñapti-avijñapti-svabhāve

those bodily and verbal actions
    each
    have the natures of manifestation and non-manifestation
```

Thus the verse does not coordinate the terms one-to-one:

```text
not:
    bodily action = vijñapti
    verbal action = avijñapti

but:
    bodily action = vijñapti and avijñapti
    verbal action = vijñapti and avijñapti
```

The next predication states the maintained Abhidharma position:

```text
kāya-vijñaptiḥ
    saṃsthānam iṣyate
    na gatiḥ

bodily manifestation
    is held to be configuration,
    not motion
```

The final relative construction gives its reason:

```text
yasmāt
    saṃskṛtaṃ kṣaṇikaṃ
    vyayāt

because
    the conditioned is momentary
    owing to its perishing
```

The Bhāṣya defines the momentary as what acquires its own existence and
perishes immediately thereafter. Its extended proof of momentariness begins
here but continues into VAK 4.03.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Those two, however, are manifestation and non-manifestation. Bodily
> manifestation is held to be configuration, not motion, because the
> conditioned is momentary owing to its perishing.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Bodily and verbal action each possess a manifest and a non-manifest mode.
> Bodily manifestation is held to be the body's determinate configuration
> under the direction of consciousness, not the motion of one enduring thing
> from place to place; for whatever is conditioned is momentary and perishes
> where it arises.

The distributive **each**, the body's being configured `cittavaśena`, “under
the direction of consciousness,” and the denial of passage by one identical
thing into another place are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Volition becomes objectively manifest not by sending one enduring bodily
> entity through a continuous path, but by determining the body's
> configuration anew within a succession of conditioned moments. The visible
> act is therefore real as expression without being a self-identical motion
> that persists across places. Yet bodily and verbal karma also possess a
> non-manifest mode. The Abhidharma inquiry must consequently explain how
> action remains existentially effective when its manifest configuration has
> already perished.

This does not deny experienced movement. It denies that movement is one
conditioned entity which remains numerically identical while migrating from
place to place.

The working distinction is:

```text
saṃsthāna
    the presently determinate articulation of the body

gati
    the alleged transit of one identical conditioned entity
    across successive places
```

The first is maintained here; the second is rejected.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The two axes of karma**

VAK 4.01 classified action by domain and genetic relation:

```text
mental action
    = cetanā

bodily and verbal action
    = generated from cetanā
```

VAK 4.02 crosses that division with mode of manifestation:

```text
bodily action
    ├── vijñapti
    └── avijñapti

verbal action
    ├── vijñapti
    └── avijñapti
```

This is the first technical matrix of the Abhidharma Karma section.

**From apparent movement to momentary configuration**

```text
rival premise:
    bodily karma occurs in a moving body
    → bodily karma is motion

Abhidharma reply:
    genuine transit would require
        the same conditioned entity at place A
        and then at place B

    but every conditioned entity
        perishes where it arises

    therefore
        no numerically identical conditioned entity
        transfers from A to B

    bodily vijñapti
        = determinate configuration
        ≠ enduring motion
```

Phenomenal movement is reconstructed as conditioned succession:

```text
configuration A₁ ceases
    → configuration A₂ is produced
        → configuration A₃ is produced
```

The arrows express causal succession, not the migration of one persistent
substrate.

**The proof shifts the causal burden**

The Bhāṣya begins a deeper argument:

```text
destruction is absence
    → absence is not a positive effect to be produced
    → destruction does not require a separate destroying cause
```

The familiar example of wood and fire does not settle the issue by perception
alone. Subsequent non-perception is compatible with two accounts:

```text
fire destroyed an enduring wood-thing

or

the prior wood-moment ceased
and fire prevented a similar successor from arising
```

The Bhāṣya therefore declares the matter inferential. VAK 4.03 must continue
the proof; VAK 4.02 establishes only its opening movement.

**The existential problem generated by the verse**

```text
manifest action
    is momentary
    and perishes

karma
    nevertheless produces differentiated existence

therefore the section must distinguish
    visible occurrence
    from karmic efficacy
```

`Avijñapti` is introduced at exactly this pressure point, but it should not yet
be treated as a fully explained persistence mechanism.

## 9. Interpretive Note

The first forty-four verses form the major Abhidharma Karma section. VAK 4.01
gave its existential problem:

```text
How does volitional activity generate differentiated existence?
```

VAK 4.02 gives the first specifically Abhidharma answer by asking what sort of
existence an action itself possesses. It refuses to begin from the ordinary
picture of an enduring body carrying an action through time and space. The
visible bodily act is a momentary expressive configuration arising under the
determination of consciousness.

This makes `vijñapti` an event of objective expression:

```text
cetanā
    inward determining activity

cittavaśena saṃsthāna
    body configured under that determination

kāya-vijñapti
    activity made manifest
```

The reciprocal difficulty is now unavoidable. If manifestation perishes in
its moment, the chapter cannot locate the whole reality of karma in what is
presently visible. `Avijñapti` names a non-manifest mode of bodily and verbal
karma, but this verse announces rather than completes its theory.

The existential determination is therefore precise:

```text
action exists as event,
not as an enduring moving thing;

yet its efficacy cannot be reduced
to the event's present visibility.
```

The Bhāṣya's theory of momentariness intensifies rather than dissolves the
problem of responsibility. It shifts the question from ownership by a
persistent substrate to causal production within a continuum:

```text
not:
    what identical thing carries the action forward?

but:
    what determination makes the next occurrence possible,
    and what prevents or redirects its production?
```

That question will govern the technical analysis of `avijñapti`, restraint,
and karmic continuity through the remainder of this opening section.
