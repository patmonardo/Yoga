# VAK_4.20

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
तथैवासंवरस्थोऽपि ध्यानसंवरवान् सदा ।
अतीताजातया आर्यस्तु प्रथमे नाभ्यतीतया ॥ ४.२० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
tathaivāsaṃvarastho ’pi dhyānasaṃvaravān sadā /
atītājātayā āryas tu prathame nābhyatītayā // 4.20 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
tathā eva asaṃvara-sthaḥ api dhyāna-saṃvara-vān sadā /
atīta-ajātayā āryaḥ tu prathame na abhi-atītayā //
```

The compound `atītājātayā` is compressed. The Bhāṣya resolves it as possession
of past and future avijñapti. That prose explanation controls the study
translation.

### `asaṃvara`

`Asaṃvara`, non-restraint, is not the mere absence of a vow. It is the
acquired contrary practical determination discussed in VAK 4.13. Its temporal
possession can therefore be analyzed in parallel with prātimokṣa restraint.

### `atītājāta`

```text
atīta
    past

ajāta
    unborn
    not yet arisen
    future
```

The Bhāṣya paraphrases the expression with the dual
`atītānāgatābhyām avijñaptibhyām`, “with past and future avijñaptis.” The
translation therefore makes both temporal modes explicit.

### `abhyatīta`

In context `abhyatītayā` is understood as past avijñapti. The negation applies
only to the noble person’s first Path moment, not to all subsequent noble
moments.

### `sadā` and `atyāga`

VAK 4.19’s condition `atyāgāt`, “because of non-abandonment,” remains in force
throughout. `Sadā` therefore means continual possession while the restraint
has not been abandoned, not unconditional eternity.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

### Non-restraint by anaphora

```text
tathā eva asaṃvara-sthaḥ api
```

The predicate is supplied from VAK 4.19:

> One established in non-restraint is also precisely so.

The Bhāṣya spells out the anaphora. While non-restraint is not abandoned, its
bearer possesses present avijñapti; after the first moment, the bearer also
possesses past avijñapti.

### The bearer of meditation restraint

```text
dhyāna-saṃvara-vān sadā atīta-ajātayā [avijñaptyā anvitaḥ]
```

The instrumental object and possession-predicate are completed from the
preceding discussion:

> One possessing meditation restraint is continually endowed with past and
> future avijñapti.

Present possession is supplied by its previously established status as
`cittānuvartin`: the present restraint accompanies the corresponding present
consciousness. The present verse adds the past and future modes.

### The noble exception

```text
āryaḥ tu [evam]
prathame [kṣaṇe]
na atītayā [avijñaptyā samanvāgataḥ]
```

> The noble person is likewise; however, in the first moment he is not endowed
> with past avijñapti.

The Bhāṣya supplies “likewise” for uncontaminated restraint, then states the
exception. At the first noble Path moment, present and future uncontaminated
restraint are possessed, but no past instance exists.

### Close construe

> One established in non-restraint is also just so. One possessing meditation
> restraint is continually [endowed] with past and future [avijñapti]. The
> noble person is likewise; but in the first [Path moment] he is not [endowed]
> with past [avijñapti].

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic translation

> One established in non-restraint is likewise. One possessing meditation
> restraint is continually endowed with past and future avijñapti. The noble
> person is likewise, but in the first moment is not endowed with past
> avijñapti.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> One established in non-restraint follows the same pattern: while it has not
> been abandoned, he possesses present avijñapti and, after the first moment,
> past avijñapti as well. One possessing meditation-born restraint continually
> possesses past and future avijñapti, together with the present restraint
> accompanying the corresponding consciousness. A noble person similarly
> possesses uncontaminated restraint, except that at the first noble Path
> moment no past uncontaminated avijñapti is yet possessed.

The expanded first clause, the explicit present mode, and the identification
of uncontaminated restraint are determinations supplied by the Bhāṣya and the
immediately preceding verses.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The temporal range of a practical determination depends upon its history in
> the continuum. Non-restraint continues like prātimokṣa restraint through
> present possession and, after its first moment, possession as past.
> Meditation-born restraint can already possess a past at its first current
> attainment because restraint abandoned in another birth is recovered; it
> also possesses a future range. Uncontaminated restraint has no past at the
> first noble Path moment because that Path has never before arisen in the
> continuum.

This is a Bhāṣya-informed statement of the source doctrine. “Temporal range”
is interpretive language for the explicitly distinguished modes of
possession.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

### The temporal pattern of non-restraint

```text
first moment
    present non-restraint avijñapti

later moments
    present non-restraint avijñapti
    + past non-restraint avijñapti

condition throughout
    non-abandonment
```

This parallel confirms that avijñapti can sustain either disciplined or
undisciplined practical determination. Persistence alone does not decide
ethical value.

### `dhyānasaṃvaralābhin`

The Bhāṣya calls the bearer one who has obtained meditation restraint. Such a
bearer possesses the restraint as past and future, and possesses its present
mode when the corresponding meditative consciousness occurs.

### `janmāntaratyakta`

```text
janma-antara
    another birth

tyakta
    abandoned
```

At the first current moment of meditation restraint, the practitioner obtains
past restraint abandoned in another birth. The present acquisition is thus not
the absolute beginning of this class of determination in the continuum.

### `pūrvam anutpādita`

The first noble Path moment differs because the Path was “not previously
produced.” No earlier uncontaminated restraint can therefore be possessed as
past at that moment.

### `ārya`

Here `ārya` is not merely an honorific. It identifies the person in whom the
uncontaminated Path has arisen. The temporal exception applies to the first
moment of that Path.

### Three-times possession

For meditation restraint, and for uncontaminated restraint apart from its
first-moment exception, the analysis involves:

```text
past
present
future
```

The verse states past and future explicitly; present possession follows from
the mind-following relation established in VAK 4.17.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Similar present status, different past

```text
first present moment of recovered meditation restraint
    → past restraint possessed

first present moment of the noble Path
    → no past uncontaminated restraint possessed
```

The difference cannot be inferred from “first present moment” alone.

### Prior history is the discriminator

```text
if determination D previously arose in continuum C
    D may be possessed as past

if determination D never previously arose in continuum C
    D cannot be possessed as past at its first occurrence
```

Temporal possession therefore requires both a present classification and the
history of the continuum.

### Ethical polarity does not alter the basic temporal form

```text
prātimokṣa restraint
    present, then present + past

non-restraint
    present, then present + past
```

The same formal pattern can carry opposed practical contents. The form of
continuity and the value of what continues are distinct determinations.

### The governing non-abandonment condition

```text
possession through the temporal modes
    remains valid while
the determination has not been abandoned
```

This inherited condition applies to non-restraint, meditation restraint, and
uncontaminated restraint throughout the Bhāṣya’s discussion.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### The present does not contain its whole truth immediately

VAK 4.20 demonstrates that present actuality cannot by itself disclose the
complete determination of a continuum. Two practitioners may each be in a
first present moment, yet one possesses a past and the other does not. The
meaning of “first” differs according to what has already occurred.

The first current attainment of meditation restraint is a recovery. The
restraint has a past because this type of determination belonged to the
continuum in another birth. The first noble Path moment is a genuine first:
the Path had never arisen before. Its lack of past is therefore a positive
historical determination, not an empty omission.

```text
current appearance
    + history of the continuum
    → complete temporal determination
```

### Persistence is content-neutral

The parallel between restraint and non-restraint is philosophically severe.
Avijñapti does not guarantee goodness. It is the mode by which a practical
determination continues beyond immediate manifestation; the determination
can be disciplined or undisciplined.

This prevents a romantic account of hidden continuity. A persistent habit of
misconduct and a persistent vow share a formal capacity to govern later
action. Their ethical opposition belongs to their content and causal
orientation, not merely to the fact that they persist.

At the level of yama, the implication is exact: discipline must not only posit
a good rule but displace the contrary instituted continuity. Non-restraint is
already organized practical form, not blank material awaiting instruction.

### History as a determination of the Copula

In the Organon reconstruction, the Ideal Copula must carry more than present
predication:

```text
the practitioner is endowed with restraint
```

The “is endowed” includes temporal depth. What can be predicated now depends
upon whether the determination has arisen before, whether it was abandoned,
and whether it is recoverable. The Copula relates the current bearer to the
determination’s complete history in the continuum.

The distinction is especially valuable for the Dharma/appearance problem.
The current appearance is only one temporal presentation. The Dharma is the
intelligible determination through which past, present, and future modes form
one ordered field. But that Organon thesis must remain distinguished from the
Sarvāstivāda claim that avijñapti is possessed in three temporal modes.

### A genuine beginning

The first noble Path moment reveals what a genuine beginning means. It is not
merely the latest appearance in a repeating series. Because the Path was never
previously produced, there is no past instance to possess. Nevertheless a
future range is already attributable to the new determination.

Thus beginning is not temporal emptiness. It is a positive transition in which
a determination without a prior instance becomes actual and opens a new
future. This is an Organon development of the contrast Vasubandhu explicitly
draws between recovered meditation restraint and the unprecedented noble Path.
