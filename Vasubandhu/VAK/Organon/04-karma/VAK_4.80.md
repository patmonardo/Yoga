# VAK_4.80

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
छिनत्ति स्त्री पुमान् दृष्टिचरितः सोऽसमन्वयः ।
संधिः काङ्क्षास्तिदृष्टिभ्यां नेहानन्तर्यकारिणः ॥ ४.८० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `chinatti` | third-person singular present of `√chid` | severs; cuts off |
| `strī` | nominative feminine singular | a woman |
| `pumān` | nominative masculine singular | a man |
| `dṛṣṭi-caritaḥ` | nominative masculine singular compound | one whose disposition is governed by view |
| `saḥ` | nominative masculine singular pronoun | that severance; the state just described |
| `a-samanvayaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | non-endowment; non-possession |
| `saṃdhiḥ` | nominative masculine singular | reconnection; restoration of continuity |
| `kāṅkṣā` | compound member | doubt |
| `asti-dṛṣṭi` | compound member | the view that ground and result exist |
| `-bhyām` | instrumental dual ending | through either of the two |
| `na iha` | negation plus locative adverb | not here; not in this present life |
| `ānantarya-kāriṇaḥ` | genitive masculine singular compound | for one who has committed an immediate-retribution action |

**The referent of `saḥ`**

The Bhāṣya asks for the intrinsic character of root-severance and answers
`so 'samanvayaḥ`: that severance is non-endowment. The clause defines the
state of severance, rather than merely describing the person as absent from
the roots.

## 4. Grammar

**Who severs the roots**

```text
strī [vā]
pumān [vā]
    dṛṣṭi-caritaḥ
    [kuśala-mūlāni] chinatti
```

> A woman or a man whose disposition is governed by view severs the wholesome
> roots.

The object `kuśala-mūlāni` carries forward from VAK 4.79. The Bhāṣya records
and rejects a transmitted restriction denying this capacity to women.

**Definition of severance**

```text
saḥ [mūla-cchedaḥ]
    a-samanvayaḥ [bhavati]
```

> That severance is non-endowment.

The technical prose expands this through cessation of renewed `prāpti` and
arising of `aprāpti`.

**Conditions of reconnection**

```text
kāṅkṣayā
asti-dṛṣṭyā vā
    saṃdhiḥ [bhavati]
```

> Reconnection occurs through doubt or through the view that they exist.

The instrumental dual coordinates two alternative conditions. The Bhāṣya
glosses `asti-dṛṣṭi` as `samyagdṛṣṭi`, right view concerning karmic ground and
result.

**The present-life exception**

```text
ānantarya-kāriṇaḥ
    iha [saṃdhiḥ]
    na [bhavati]
```

> For one who has committed an immediate-retribution action, reconnection does
> not occur here in the present life.

`Na iha` restricts time; it does not deny every future reconnection.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> A woman or a man whose disposition is governed by view severs them; that
> severance is non-endowment. Reconnection occurs through doubt or through the
> view that they exist, but not here for one who has committed an
> immediate-retribution action.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A woman or a man whose disposition has become firmly governed by false view
> can sever the wholesome roots; a person governed merely by unstable craving
> does not complete this severance. Root-severance is the state of
> non-endowment in which acquisition of the wholesome roots no longer arises
> and non-acquisition arises. The roots reconnect when doubt about karmic
> ground and result breaks the certainty of denial, or when right view that
> they exist arises. All nine grades reconnect together at the level of
> endowment, although their actual manifestation returns successively. For one
> who has committed an immediate-retribution action, this reconnection cannot
> occur in the present life, though it is not absolutely excluded thereafter.

The craving/view contrast, technical acquisition account, simultaneous
reconnection, gradual manifestation, and temporal scope of the exception are
supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Radical ethical collapse is a loss of operative endowment, not the
> annihilation of a permanent entity. Recovery begins when the governing
> certainty of denial is destabilized: doubt can reopen the practical field
> before full right view has been established. Once the relation of endowment
> is restored, capacity and manifestation remain distinct—the whole capacity
> reconnects together, while its effective exercise returns progressively.
> Restoration of principle also does not erase consequences already fixed by
> completed action.

The local cycle is:

```text
fixed false view
    → severance
    → non-endowment
    → doubt or right view
    → restored endowment
    → gradual manifestation
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dṛṣṭi-carita` — governed by view**

A disposition whose practical course is principially organized by view. The
Bhāṣya attributes its capacity for severance to a firm and deeply established
unwholesome orientation (`dṛḍha-gūḍha-pāpāśaya`).

**`Tṛṣṇā-carita` — governed by craving**

The contrasted type. Craving may be intense but its orientation is unstable
(`calāśaya`), so it does not complete the severance described here.

**The gender dispute**

The Kārikā explicitly states `strī pumān`, woman and man. The Bhāṣya records
an alternative denying severance to women on an alleged weakness of desire,
energy, and discriminative capacity, then answers that this would contradict
an established doctrinal statement concerning the female faculty. The
official study preserves the verse's inclusive statement and attributes the
rejected restriction historically.

**`Asamanvaya` / `asamanvāgama` — non-endowment**

The intrinsic state of root-severance. The continuum no longer stands in the
operative possession-relation to the wholesome roots.

**`Prāpti` — acquisition or endowment relation**

In the Vaibhāṣika account, wholesome-root `prāpti` no longer arises when the
roots are severed. Its renewed arising defines reconnection.

**`Aprāpti` — non-acquisition relation**

Arises when the wholesome roots have been severed. Together with the failure
of `prāpti` to recur, it constitutes non-endowment.

**`Saṃdhi` / `pratisaṃdhi` — reconnection**

Restoration of the endowment relation through renewed `prāpti`. It is not
creation of new eternal entities.

**`Kāṅkṣā` / `vicikitsā` — doubt**

Doubt concerning karmic ground and result. Although doubt is not right
knowledge, here it has a positive transitional role relative to fixed
nihilistic certainty.

**`Asti-dṛṣṭi` — affirmative or right view**

The view that karmic ground and result exist. The Bhāṣya expressly gives
`samyagdṛṣṭi`, right view, as its meaning.

**`Saṃmukhībhāva` — manifestation**

Actual coming into presence or operation. All grades reconnect together, but
their manifestation occurs successively, like the gradual return of strength
after illness.

**`Ānantarya-kārin` — immediate-retribution actor**

One who has completed an action whose result follows without an intervening
birth. Present-life reconnection is blocked; the Bhāṣya places restoration at
death or arising into the next existence according to the relevant causal
pattern.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Who can sever**

```text
human continuum
    + fixed view-governed disposition
    + fully intensified false view
        → capable of root-severance

craving-governed unstable disposition
        → does not complete root-severance
```

The decisive variable is governing practical disposition, not sex.

**Severance as change of relation**

```text
prāpti of wholesome roots ceases to recur
    + aprāpti arises
        → asamanvaya
        → roots called severed
```

Thus:

```text
root-severance
    ≠ substance annihilation

root-severance
    = transition from endowment to non-endowment
```

**The first mediation of recovery**

Let `P` be “karmic ground and result exist.”

```text
fixed false view
    settles not-P

doubt
    unsettles not-P
    and reopens P as possible

right view
    affirms P
```

Doubt is not thereby wholesome wisdom in general; its local genetic function
is to break the closure of completed denial.

**Reconnection and manifestation are asymmetric**

```text
SEVERANCE
    nine grades removed successively

RECONNECTION
    all nine grades reconnect simultaneously

MANIFESTATION
    wholesome activity returns successively
```

Possession of capacity and active exercise of capacity must remain distinct.

**Present recovery and fixed consequence**

```text
ordinary root-severer
    may reconnect in the present life

ānantarya-kārin
    cannot reconnect in the present life
    but reconnects at death or subsequent arising
```

Corrected orientation does not automatically reverse all consequences already
determined by completed action.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Principle is more stable than appetite**

The Bhāṣya's contrast is not praise of view as such. It identifies the greater
structural stability of a principially fixed orientation. Fluctuating craving
can repeatedly produce harm; fixed false view can reorganize the continuum so
deeply that wholesome endowment is severed.

**Recovery begins before full knowledge**

The practical importance of doubt is relative and transitional:

```text
“There is no ground or result.”
    → “Perhaps there is.”
    → “There is.”
```

The first opening is not yet accomplished wisdom, but it destroys the
dogmatic closure that prevented reconnection.

**Capacity and manifestation**

The illness analogy gives a precise recovery model. The relevant endowment can
be restored as a whole before its strength becomes manifest across actual
conduct. A restored ground is real even while its developed consequences
remain incomplete.

This is the Organon contact. The agent-type dispute, `prāpti`/`aprāpti`
account, conditions of reconnection, nine-grade asymmetry, and `ānantarya`
exception belong to the Bhāṣya; the distinction between restored capacity and
progressive exercise is the project synthesis.
