# VAK_4.06

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> नैःष्यन्दिकी च सत्त्वाख्या निष्यन्दोपात्तभूतजा ।
>
> समाधिजौपचयिकानुपात्ताभिन्नभूतजा ॥ ४.६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> naiḥṣyandikī ca sattvākhyā niṣyandopātta-bhūtajā /
>
> samādhijā aupacayikānupāttābhinna-bhūtajā // 4.6 //

VAK 4.05 determined the level-relative material support of bodily and verbal
karma. VAK 4.06 now differentiates `avijñapti` according to its genesis and
according to the organization of its material basis. The Bhāṣya divides the
verse between ordinary, non-concentrated `avijñapti` and the `avijñapti` born
from samādhi.

```text
non-concentrated level
    → homogeneous-outflow continuation
    → related to a sentient continuum

samādhi-born restraint
    → produced through cultivation
    → non-appropriated
    → seven restraints share one undivided material basis
```

This is not merely a list of properties. It contrasts two modes of production
and, through the Bhāṣya, two ways in which the plurality of restraints is
materially articulated.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
naiḥṣyandikī               → naiḥṣyandikī
ca                         → ca
sattva-ākhyā               → sattvākhyā
niṣyanda-upātta-bhūta-jā   → niṣyandopāttabhūtajā
samādhi-jā                 → samādhijā
aupacayikā                 → aupacayikā
an-upāttā                  → anupāttā
abhinna-bhūta-jā           → abhinnabhūtajā
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| naiḥṣyandikī | nominative feminine singular adjective from `niṣyanda` | homogeneous-outflow-derived; continuing as a result of a homogeneous cause |
| ca | conjunction | and; joins the predicates of ordinary `avijñapti` |
| sattvākhyā | nominative feminine singular compound | designated with reference to a sentient being or sentient continuum |
| niṣyanda | prior member of compound | homogeneous causal outflow or continuation |
| upātta | past participle of `upa-ā-√dā` | appropriated into the living bodily support |
| bhūta | compound member, neuter | great material determination / great element |
| jā | feminine compound-final from `√jan` | born from; arisen in dependence upon |
| samādhijā | nominative feminine singular compound | born from concentration |
| aupacayikā | nominative feminine singular adjective from `upacaya` | produced by development, cultivation, or increase |
| anupāttā | nominative feminine singular adjective | non-appropriated |
| abhinna | compound member | undivided, not differentiated; Bhāṣya: the same supporting elements for all seven restraints |
| abhinnabhūtajā | nominative feminine singular compound | born from an undivided or common great-element basis |

The subject `avijñaptiḥ` is carried forward from VAK 4.05 and is understood
throughout both lines. All the principal predicates are feminine singular in
agreement with it.

`Sattvākhyā` must not be inflated into a substantial self. It marks the
conventional designation of ordinary `avijñapti` in relation to a sentient
causal continuum. Likewise, `abhinna` does not name an indivisible metaphysical
stuff. The commentary construes it relationally: the seven samādhi-born
restraints arise dependent upon the same set of great elements.

### Witness caution

The kārikā witness gives `anupāttā`, “non-appropriated.” In the available
commentarial e-text, the corresponding phrase appears as `anupātā` and the
prose as `anupātāni`. The doctrinal explanation and VAK 4.05 both support the
normalized reading `anupāttā` / `anupāttāni`. The textual variation is retained
here as a witness caution rather than silently made philosophically decisive.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first hemistich supplies four coordinated determinations of ordinary
`avijñapti`:

```text
[avijñaptiḥ]
    naiḥṣyandikī ca
    sattvākhyā [ca]
    niṣyanda-upātta-bhūta-jā [ca]

[non-manifest action]
    is homogeneous-outflow-derived,
    is designated in relation to a sentient being,
    and is born from homogeneous-outflow-derived,
        appropriated great elements
```

The compound `niṣyandopāttabhūtajā` is read as:

```text
niṣyanda + upātta + bhūta + jā

born from great elements that are
    homogeneous-outflow-derived
    and appropriated
```

The Bhāṣya adds a second condition not stated independently in the compound:

```text
ordinary avijñapti
    arises depending upon naiḥṣyandika mahābhūtas
    and depending upon citta-caittas

this is the mode belonging to a non-concentrated level
    (asamāhita-bhūmikā)
```

The second hemistich changes the subject specification from ordinary
`avijñapti` to that born from samādhi:

```text
samādhi-jā [avijñaptiḥ]
    aupacayikā
    anupāttā
    abhinna-bhūta-jā

samādhi-born non-manifest action
    is cultivation-produced,
    is non-appropriated,
    and is born from an undivided great-element basis
```

The Bhāṣya identifies `samādhijā avijñapti` as the non-manifest restraint of
dhyāna and of the uncontaminated path. It explains `abhinnabhūtajā` by taking
the seven abstentions—from killing through incoherent or frivolous speech—as
dependent upon the very same great elements:

```text
one samādhi-produced material basis
    → abstention from killing
    → abstention from taking what is not given
    → abstention from sexual misconduct
    → abstention from false speech
    → abstention from divisive speech
    → abstention from harsh speech
    → abstention from incoherent or frivolous speech
```

This shared basis is contrasted with `prātimokṣa` restraint, for which the
seven `avijñaptis` depend upon distinct sets of great elements.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> [Non-manifest action] is homogeneous-outflow-derived and designated in
> relation to a sentient being, born from homogeneous-outflow-derived,
> appropriated great elements. That born from samādhi is
> cultivation-produced, non-appropriated, and born from an undivided
> great-element basis.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> At a non-concentrated level, `avijñapti` is a homogeneous causal
> continuation designated in relation to a sentient continuum. It arises in
> dependence upon homogeneous-outflow-derived, appropriated great elements
> and upon mind and mental factors. The `avijñapti` born from samādhi—the
> restraints of dhyāna and of the uncontaminated path—is produced through
> cultivation and is non-appropriated. Its seven abstentions arise from one
> common, undivided material basis rather than from seven distinct bases.

Dependence upon `citta-caitta`, the non-concentrated level, the identification
of the two samādhi restraints, the enumeration of seven abstentions, and the
contrast with `prātimokṣa` are determinations supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An enduring practical determination is articulated according to the manner
> of its production. In the ordinary field, it continues homogeneously within
> a sentient psycho-physical series and is supported by appropriated matter.
> When born from samādhi, however, restraint is the product of cultivation:
> its seven normative differences are generated through one concentrated,
> non-appropriated material basis. Unity here does not erase difference. One
> cultivated ground is determinately expressed as seven abstentions.

This does not turn `avijñapti` into a transcendent bearer standing behind
action. The text gives a conditioned causal account: ordinary `avijñapti`
depends upon material elements and `citta-caittas`; samādhi-born `avijñapti`
depends upon elements generated through samādhi. What cannot be directly
disclosed is nevertheless specified through its genesis, support, and causal
function.

The philosophical interest therefore lies less in an occult entity than in a
problem of practical continuity: how a determination of action persists and
how a cultivated unity differentiates itself into several definite restraints.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Two axes of classification

The verse crosses two logically distinct questions:

```text
Axis 1 — How is the avijñapti generated?

    ordinary field
        → niṣyanda: homogeneous causal continuation

    concentrated field
        → upacaya: cultivation or development

Axis 2 — How is its material support organized?

    ordinary living continuum
        → appropriated material basis

    samādhi-born sevenfold restraint
        → non-appropriated, common material basis
```

The second axis is sharpened by the Bhāṣya's comparison:

```text
samādhi restraint:
    seven avijñaptis
    ← one and the same set of mahābhūtas

prātimokṣa restraint:
    seven avijñaptis
    ← seven differentiated material bases
```

Thus numerical plurality at the level of normative content does not determine
numerical plurality at the level of material support.

### Causal determination without a hidden subject

The ordinary sequence can be stated as:

```text
citta-caitta determination
    + homogeneous-outflow-derived appropriated matter
        → ordinary avijñapti in a sentient continuum
```

The samādhi sequence is different:

```text
samādhi
    → cultivation-produced mahābhūtas
        → one common support
            → seven determinate abstentions
```

Nothing in this inference requires an imperceptible agent outside the series.
`Avijñapti` names the conditioned practical determination whose persistence is
being analyzed; its explanatory conditions remain within the psycho-physical
and contemplative series.

### Unity and differentiation

The Bhāṣya explains the common basis by analogy with mind:

```text
cittavat bhūtābhedāt
    “as with mind, because the material elements are not divided”
```

The force of the analogy is structural:

```text
one concentrated causal ground
    does not produce an indeterminate restraint

it produces
    seven distinguishable practical negations
    without seven separately generated supports
```

The unity belongs to genesis and support; the difference belongs to the
determinate objects of abstention.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### Samādhi as productive practical unity

VAK 4.06 gives unusually concrete support to the top-level architecture now
guiding our reading of the Kośa. Samādhi is not presented only as a terminal
state contemplated after the theory of karma is complete. It has productive
force within karma: samādhi generates a mode of restraint whose several
determinations share one basis.

At the textual level, the claim is exact and limited. The `avijñapti` of
dhyāna-restraint and uncontaminated restraint is `samādhijā` and
`aupacayikā`. Because the same samādhi-generated great elements support all
seven abstentions, it is `abhinnabhūtajā`. The Bhāṣya contrasts this with the
seven separately based `avijñaptis` of `prātimokṣa` restraint. This is the
Abhidharma determination and must stand on its own terms.

At the Organon level, the result is richer. The plurality of Right Action is
not merely collected from outside. A developed concentration becomes an
inwardly unified ground capable of differentiating itself into the seven
bodily and verbal restraints. The whole is present in each determination not
as an eighth item, but as their common genesis. This is a precise way to read
the movement proposed in our four-volume schema:

```text
Prajñā + Action
    → Path
        → Samādhi

Samādhi, once achieved,
    → returns as the unified productive ground of action
```

The return must be marked as our systematic synthesis, not attributed to the
kārikā as Hegelian terminology. Yet VAK 4.06 supplies its real textual
occasion. The culmination does not merely leave the preceding moments behind;
it gathers them and makes their practical articulation internally one.

### Transcendental determination, not a transcendent object

This verse also disciplines the treatment of `avijñapti`. If it is imagined as
an invisible thing residing behind appearances, it becomes transcendent in the
bad sense: an asserted object exempted from possible determination. But the
Kośa does not leave it in that semantic fog. It classifies `avijñapti` by:

```text
its causal mode
its material support
its relation to appropriation
its contemplative level
its differentiated normative function
```

The object may be non-disclosive, but its concept is not empty. Kantian
discipline therefore does not simply “kill” the doctrine; it first asks whether
the claimed determination has conditions of intelligibility. Here the
Abhidharma answers with a highly articulated causal grammar. The remaining
question is whether that grammar requires `avijñapti` as a distinct material
dharma or whether practical continuity can be reconstructed through the
organization of the causal series itself.

The Hegelian pressure is then immanent: does the posited item explain the unity
of the process, or is it the process—samādhi differentiating itself as seven
restraints—that supplies the truth which the item only represents? VAK 4.06
does not settle that question, but it gives us the exact terrain on which to
pose it without caricaturing the Vaibhāṣika position.

### The first appearance of the larger result

The “Absolute Idea” should therefore not be imported here as a finished slogan.
What appears is one of its required shapes: a unity that is productive,
internally differentiated, and returned into practice. The seven restraints
are neither an aggregate without unity nor a unity without articulation.
Samādhi is their cultivated common ground.

That is why 4.06 matters beyond the local taxonomy. The Karma chapter is
beginning to show how Path-determination can become objective practical form.
The sought unity of knowledge, action, Path, and samādhi is not yet proven, but
the machinery required for such a synthesis is visibly entering the text.
