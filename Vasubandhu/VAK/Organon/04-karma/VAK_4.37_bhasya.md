# VAK 4.37 Bhāṣya — How Standing Karmic Forms Are Acquired

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
asaṃvarasya kriyayā lābho 'bhyupagamena vā /
śeṣāvijñaptilābhas tu kṣetrādānādarehanāt // 4.37 //
```

> Non-restraint is acquired through action or through adopting it.
> The remaining avijñapti arises through a qualified field,
> undertaking, or earnest pursuit of an action.

next topic, abandonment (`tyāga`), at [222|18] opens 4.38.

Word division, punctuation, and verse numbering are editorial.
The compressed `kṣetrādānādarehanāt` is interpreted below through
the prose's three alternatives. `Śeṣāvijñapti` is the remaining
unmanifest karmic form, distinct from the standing forms of
restraint and non-restraint just discussed.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

It has been explained **with respect to what** non-restraint is
acquired; **how** it is acquired has not yet been stated. So this
is now said: “Non-restraint is acquired through action or through
adoption.” It is acquired through two causes. For those born into
the relevant occupational lineage, it arises through performing
the activity involved in killing. For people from another lineage,
it arises through adopting that activity: “We too shall live by
this livelihood.”

“The remaining avijñapti is acquired through a field, an
undertaking, or earnest action.” A field can be such that merely
giving a monastery or similar support there generates avijñapti,
as with bases of meritorious action involving material goods. Or
one undertakes a practice: “I will not eat without first paying
homage to the Buddha,” or “I will regularly provide meals on
specified days, monthly or fortnightly,” and so on. Or one pursues
a wholesome or unwholesome action with such earnestness that
avijñapti arises. Thus the acquisition of restraint, non-restraint,
and what is neither has been explained.

## 4. Action, Adoption, and the Remaining Avijñapti

The first half answers a gap left by 4.36. That verse established
the **scope** of non-restraint; this one establishes its **mode of
acquisition**. `Kriyā` means actual performance in the occupational
case. Birth into a lineage is not itself described as enough.
`Abhyupagama` is a deliberate adoption of the livelihood by
someone outside it. These are alternatives, not two necessary
stages of every acquisition.

The second half treats avijñapti other than complete `saṃvara` or
`asaṃvara`. The prose distinguishes three occasions:

| Occasion | Bhāṣya example | What gives the act continuing force |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified `kṣetra` | Giving a monastery or material support | The field in which the gift is made |
| `samādāna` | A standing commitment to homage or regular meals | An expressly undertaken practice |
| `ādara` with `īhā` | Earnest pursuit of a wholesome or unwholesome act | The manner and intensity of performance |

The compound in the Kārikā does not require four independent
causes. The Bhāṣya groups earnestness and pursuit in its third
example: `ādareṇa ... kriyām īhate`. Nor does it imply that only
wholesome acts create the remaining avijñapti; the last case
explicitly includes `akuśala` action.

## 5. Organon Contact Point

For our study of vows, 4.37 separates a declaration, a standing
undertaking, an act in a consequential field, and earnest
performance. These are distinct ways in which action gains a
continuing determination. Our wheel of adhyāsa ceasing and the
Idea of the True and Good emerging must be tested in those
practical terms: what was undertaken, where it acts, and whether
the subsequent action sustains it. This is an Organon use of the
Kośa's acquisition analysis; the passage itself classifies
`asaṃvara` and the remaining `avijñapti`.
