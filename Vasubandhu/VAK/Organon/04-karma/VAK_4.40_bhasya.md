# VAK 4.40 Bhāṣya — Relinquishment by Regression and by Advance

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
bhūmisaṃcārahānibhyāṃ dhyānāptaṃ tyajyate śubham /
tathārūpyāptam āryaṃ tu phalāptyuttaptihānibhiḥ // 4.40 //
```

> Wholesome dharmas acquired through dhyāna are relinquished
> through a change of ground or falling away; likewise those of
> the formless levels. Noble uncontaminated dharmas are relinquished
> through attaining a fruit, strengthening the faculties, or
> falling away.

The Kārikā witness reads `phalāptyutpattihānibhiḥ`, but the
running Bhāṣya verse reads `phalāptyuttaptihānibhiḥ` and the
prose explains this as `indriyottāpana`, strengthening of the
faculties. The unit runs from [224|19] through the closing line
at [225|02] in `Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt`. The next
verse turns to the loss of non-restraint at [225|03].

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

```iast
atha dhyānānāsravasaṃvarayoḥ kathaṃ tyāgaḥ /

bhūmisaṃcārahānibhyāṃ dhyānāptaṃ tyajyate śubham /

sarvam eva dhyānāptaṃ kuśalaṃ dvābhyāṃ kāraṇābhyāṃ parityajyate /
upapattito vā bhūmisaṃcārād ūrdhvaṃ cāvaśyaṃ parihāṇito vā
samāpatter nikāyasabhāgatvāc ca kiñcit /
yathā ca rūpāptaṃ kuśalaṃ bhūmisaṃcārahānibhyāṃ tyajyate

tathārūpyāptam āryaṃ tu phalāptyuttaptihānibhiḥ // 4.40 //

anāsravaṃ tu kuśalaṃ tribhiḥ kāraṇaiḥ parityakṣyate /
phalaprāptitaḥ pūrvako mārgaḥ parityakṣyate /
indriyottāpanena mṛddhindriyamārgaḥ /
parihāṇita uttaro mārgaḥ /
phalaṃ phalaviśiṣṭo vā /
evaṃ tāvat saṃvarās tyajyante /
```

Word division, punctuation, and verse numbering are editorial.
The running source has the difficult sequence `ūrdhvaṃ cāvaśyaṃ`
and the likely irregular `mṛddhindriyamārgaḥ`; the translation
keeps their contextual sense provisional. The Kārikā witness's
`utpatti` is recorded above rather than silently replaced.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

How are meditation-born and uncontaminated restraints relinquished?
“Wholesome dharmas acquired through dhyāna are relinquished
through a change of ground or falling away.” All wholesome
dharmas acquired through dhyāna are relinquished through these
two causes: through a change of ground by rebirth, including an
upward transition [the wording is compressed], or through
falling away from the attainment. Some are also relinquished
through a change in membership in the relevant class of beings.
Just as wholesome dharmas belonging to the Form Realm are lost
through change of ground or falling away, so are those acquired
in the formless levels.

“Noble [uncontaminated dharmas], however, are relinquished through
fruit-attainment, strengthening, or falling away.” Uncontaminated
wholesome dharmas are relinquished in three ways. When a fruit is
attained, the preceding path is relinquished. When the faculties
are strengthened, the path belonging to weaker faculties is
relinquished. Through falling away, a higher path is lost, or a
fruit or something distinguished by a fruit [the final phrase is
compressed]. Thus the modes in which restraints are relinquished
have been explained.

## 4. The Different Senses of Loss

The question names `dhyānasaṃvara` and `anāsravasaṃvara`, while
the answer speaks of the wider classes of wholesome dharmas to
which they belong. Dhyāna-acquired and formless wholesome
determinations depend on a ground and on attainment. A change
of ground or falling away from attainment can end their
possession in the relevant mode.

The uncontaminated case has a different sequence:

| Cause | What is relinquished | Character of change |
|---|---|---|
| Attainment of a fruit | The preceding path | Its function has been fulfilled |
| Strengthening of faculties | A weaker-faculty path | The operative capacity has advanced |
| Falling away | A higher path, fruit, or fruit-distinguished state | Regression where possible |

The word `tyāga` therefore does not by itself mean a moral or
meditative failure. Two of the three noble-path cases are forms
of progress. `Uttapti` is crucial here: the Bhāṣya's
`indriyottāpana` makes strengthening of faculties explicit,
whereas the separate Kārikā witness's `utpatti` would suggest
mere arising and obscure the particular path being superseded.

## 5. Organon Contact Point

In our wheel, what ceases and what emerges must be identified
at the right level. The Kośa here shows a practical analogue:
a prior determination may cease because its work has borne a
fruit or because a stronger capacity now carries that work.
Such supersession differs from falling away. The Organon can
use this distinction when describing adhyāsa's cessation and
the emergence of the Idea of the True and Good, without
claiming that Vasubandhu uses those terms in this passage.
