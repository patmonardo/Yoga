# VAK 4.83 Bhāṣya — The Courses in Uttarakuru and the Desire Realm

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
saptamaḥ svayam apy atra kāme 'nyatra daśāśubhāḥ /
śubhās trayas tu sarvatra saṃmukhībhāvalābhataḥ // 4.83 //
```

> Here the seventh also occurs through manifestation itself. Elsewhere in the
> desire realm, all ten unwholesome courses occur. But the three wholesome
> mental courses occur everywhere through manifestation and acquisition.

The verse completes the regional distribution begun in 4.82. `Atra` carries
forward Uttarakuru; `anyatra` then excludes both Uttarakuru and the hells
from its general statement about the rest of the desire realm.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

niyatāyuṣkatvād dravyastrīparigrahābhāvāt prayojanābhāvāc ca.

At `[252|26]` the running witness reads `asaṃbhinnapralāpaḥ`, although the
seventh course and the context require `saṃbhinnapralāpaḥ`. The normalized
reading is printed above and the witness form is preserved here. The witness
also has `bhriyanta` at `[253|08–09]`; `mriyante`, “they die,” is the secure
contextual reading.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Here in Uttarakuru, the seventh course—frivolous or disconnected speech—also
exists through present manifestation. For its inhabitants sometimes sing
with afflicted minds. The courses beginning with killing do not occur because
their dispositions are not unwholesome, because their lifespans are fixed,
because possessions and possession of women are absent, and because there is
no purpose for the relevant acts.

How, then, is there non-celibacy among them? When a man wishes to enjoy
himself with a woman, he takes her by the arm and approaches the foot of a
tree. If she is an eligible partner, the tree covers them, and they have
intercourse. If she is ineligible, the tree does not cover them, and they do
not have intercourse.

Elsewhere in the desire realm, all ten unwholesome courses occur through
present manifestation as well. “Through manifestation itself” continues to
govern. Outside hell and Uttarakuru, all ten are found in the desire realm by
present manifestation. Among animals, hungry ghosts, and gods they are
independent of restraint and non-restraint. Among humans they may also be
included within non-restraint.

Although one god does not kill another god, a god can kill a being situated
in another destiny. Others maintain that gods themselves die when their heads
are cut through the middle.

The unwholesome courses have now been stated. The three wholesome mental
courses occur everywhere through present manifestation and endowment. In all
five destinies throughout the three realms, non-covetousness, non-ill-will,
and right view are found both as presently manifest and as belonging to the
continuum.

## 4. Uttarakuru: The Seventh Course

VAK 4.82 said that the three mental unwholesome courses are only endowments
in Uttarakuru. VAK 4.83 adds one actively manifested course: the seventh,
`saṃbhinnapralāpa`.

```text
occasional afflicted singing
    → frivolous or disconnected speech manifests
```

The example is exacting. Pleasant or musical expression is not condemned as
such. The Bhāṣya specifies singing performed with an afflicted mind. The
determining factor is the afflicted practical character of the occurrence.

The other unwholesome courses lack their required supports. Fixed lifespan
blocks killing; the property regime blocks theft; the sexual convention
blocks misconduct; lack of malicious purpose blocks the remaining verbal
courses. A favorable world does not abolish action. It configures which
courses can reach completion.

## 5. The Tree as a Public Eligibility Test

The tree episode answers a narrow doctrinal objection. If sexual misconduct
is absent in Uttarakuru, how can sexual activity occur? The tree makes the
eligibility relation manifest:

```text
eligible relation   → tree provides cover → intercourse occurs
ineligible relation → no cover            → intercourse does not occur
```

The story belongs to the source's cosmography. Its logical function is clear:
sexual activity and sexual misconduct are not coextensive. Misconduct
requires violation of a determinate relational boundary; the Uttarakuru
environment externalizes that boundary and prevents its transgression.

## 6. Elsewhere in the Desire Realm

`Anyatra` means outside the two exceptional environments already analyzed:

```text
desire realm − {hell, Uttarakuru}
```

Across that remainder, every unwholesome course can become manifest. Yet its
relation to restraint differs by destiny:

| Destiny | Relation to restraint classification |
|---|---|
| animals, hungry ghosts, gods | outside both restraint and non-restraint |
| humans | may be included within non-restraint |

The distinction matters because occurrence of an unwholesome act is broader
than possession of the technical Abhidharma state called non-restraint.
Nonhuman beings can manifest the course without possessing that juridical or
continuing practical status.

The dispute about gods protects the universality of the killing course in the
remaining desire realm. One account denies intra-divine killing but permits
a god to kill a being in another destiny. The alternative allows divine death
through bisection of the head. The Bhāṣya records both rather than resolving
the disagreement here.

## 7. The Three Wholesome Mental Courses

The closing half-verse changes from unwholesome to wholesome courses:

1. non-covetousness (`anabhidhyā`),
2. non-ill-will (`avyāpāda`),
3. right view (`samyagdṛṣṭi`).

Unlike bodily and verbal courses, these three occur universally across all
five destinies and all three realms. Both modes are affirmed:

```text
present manifestation + endowment
```

This universality is carefully limited to the three mental courses. VAK 4.84
will classify the seven wholesome bodily and verbal courses, whose dependence
upon body, speech, and restraint gives them a different distribution.

## 8. Organon Contact Point

The verse refines the Loka–Karma relation. A World System supplies more than
a location for an already complete action. It can expose, suppress, or make
decidable the relational conditions through which a course becomes complete.

```text

    f(agent-state,

      available-object,

```

The tree is a vivid model of an externally legible constraint: the world
itself returns whether the proposed relation is eligible. In an Organon
Program Feature, the corresponding determination would have to be represented
as an explicit rule and an auditable decision, rather than as unexplained
environmental magic.

This is the Organon reconstruction. Uttarakuru cosmography, the restraint
classifications, the divine-killing dispute, and the five-destiny claim are
