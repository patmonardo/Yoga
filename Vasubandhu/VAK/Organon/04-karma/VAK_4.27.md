# VAK_4.27

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
यावज्जीवं समादानमहोरात्रं च संवृतेः ।
नासंवरोऽस्त्यहोरात्रं न किलैवं प्रगृह्यते ॥ ४.२७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
yāvajjīvaṃ samādānam ahorātraṃ ca saṃvṛteḥ /
nāsaṃvaro 'sty ahorātraṃ na kilaivaṃ pragṛhyate // 4.27 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
yāvat-jīvam samādānam aho-rātram ca saṃvṛteḥ /
na asaṃvaraḥ asti aho-rātram na kila evam pragṛhyate //
```

The witness reads `saṃvṛteḥ`. The Bhāṣya makes clear that the subject is the
undertaking of prātimokṣa restraint; the form must not be confused with
`saṃvṛti` in the philosophical sense of convention.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `yāvajjīvam` | indeclinable adverbial compound | for as long as life lasts |
| `samādānam` | nominative/accusative neuter singular | undertaking; intentional assumption |
| `ahorātram` | accusative neuter singular used adverbially | for one day and night |
| `saṃvṛteḥ` | genitive singular transmitted form | of restraint, as resolved by the Bhāṣya |
| `asaṃvaraḥ` | nominative masculine singular | established non-restraint |
| `asti` | third-person singular present of `as` | exists; is recognized |
| `kila` | reportive particle | according to the received account; it is said |
| `evam` | adverb | in this manner |
| `pragṛhyate` | third-person singular present passive of `pra-grah` | is undertaken; is formally assumed |

**`samādāna`**

`Samādāna` is intentional taking-up. The Bhāṣya later speaks of
`samādānabalādhāna`, the imparting of force through undertaking. The vow is not
merely described; the undertaking contributes to its acquisition.

**`yāvajjīva`**

```text
yāvat jīvam
    as long as life
    for the duration of this life
```

The seven lifelong prātimokṣa classes are governed by this limit.

**`ahorātra`**

```text
ahas
    day

rātra
    night
```

Together they form one complete day-and-night observance, the recognized
duration of upavāsa restraint.

**`pragṛhyate`**

The verb signifies deliberate undertaking. The verse’s denial concerns the
recognized mode in which non-restraint is normally acquired, not the mere
possibility of harmful action during a day.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

**Two durations of restraint**

```text
saṃvṛteḥ samādānam
    yāvat-jīvam
    aho-rātram ca
```

> The undertaking of restraint is for life and [in another case] for one day
> and night.

The Bhāṣya distributes the durations:

```text
seven prātimokṣa classes
    → lifelong

upavāsa restraint
    → one day and night
```

**The received denial**

```text
na asaṃvaraḥ asti aho-rātram
```

> There is no [recognized] non-restraint for one day and night.

The bare kārikā is categorical. The Bhāṣya qualifies its force by examining
how non-restraint is actually acquired.

**Reason given by the verse**

```text
na kila evam pragṛhyate
```

> For, it is said, it is not undertaken in this manner.

`Kila` marks the received or reported character of the explanation. The
Bhāṣya does not treat this first answer as the end of inquiry.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Restraint is undertaken for life or for one day and night. Non-restraint is
> not recognized for merely one day and night, since it is not ordinarily
> undertaken in that manner.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The seven lifelong classes of prātimokṣa restraint are undertaken for the
> duration of life, while upavāsa restraint is undertaken for one complete day
> and night. Non-restraint is not ordinarily classified as a one-day-and-night
> determination because no one is observed deliberately undertaking it in the
> manner of upavāsa—although Vasubandhu concedes that, if someone genuinely
> sought and undertook such non-restraint for a limited period, it would be
> acquired.

The distribution of the restraint classes, the empirical qualification, and
the concession concerning possible limited non-restraint are Bhāṣya
determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A practical form includes its temporal limit within the manner of its
> acquisition. Lifelong restraint and day-and-night upavāsa are complete but
> differently bounded vows. Standing non-restraint ordinarily arises not from
> a ritual declaration of duration but from an utterly corrupted practical
> intention; temporary corruption produces harmful action without necessarily
> instituting that standing form. Yet the asymmetry is genetic and customary,
> not an absolute logical impossibility: a genuinely undertaken finite
> non-restraint would also be acquired.

This incorporates Vasubandhu’s interrogation of the received rule while
preserving the verse’s stated classification.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Two `kālaparyanta`**

The Bhāṣya identifies two temporal termini:

```text
ahorātra-paryanta
    limit of one day and night

jīvita-paryanta
    limit of a lifetime
```

Longer conventional intervals repeat day-and-night units; they do not add a
third fundamental terminus to the received classification.

**Time as indication of conditioned processes**

Vasubandhu asks what dharma “time” is and answers:

```text
kāla
    saṃskāra-paridīpana-adhivacana
    an expression indicating conditioned processes
```

Day names the illuminated state of the inhabited world; night names its dark
state. Time is not introduced here as a separately existing substance.

**Why lifelong restraint ends at death**

The restraint does not arise beyond that life because:

```text
the support has become dissimilar
there is no corresponding practical application
there is no recollection of the undertaking
```

The duration is therefore tied to the continuum in which the vow was acquired.

**`yuktyavirodha`**

Vasubandhu observes that restraint arising beyond one day and night contains
no contradiction with reasoning. This opens a genuine question about the
scriptural one-day upavāsa rule.

**Pedagogical prescription**

Two readings are distinguished:

```text
one-day upavāsa was taught
    because longer restraint cannot arise

or

one-day upavāsa was taught
    so persons of weak faculties could enter restraint
    through a manageable undertaking
```

The Vaibhāṣikas do not accept extending the prescribed upavāsa beyond one day
and night, but Vasubandhu has exposed that doctrinal prescription and intrinsic
possibility are not self-evidently identical.

**`āśaya`**

`Āśaya` is the underlying practical intention or disposition. The Bhāṣya
distinguishes:

```text
atyanta-vipanna āśaya
    utterly corrupted intention
    → standing non-restraint

kālāntara-vipanna āśaya
    intention corrupted only temporarily
    → harmful action
    → not necessarily standing non-restraint
```

**`samādānabalādhāna`**

Formal undertaking imparts force. Upavāsa can therefore be acquired even when
the practitioner’s underlying disposition is not already absolute or lifelong,
because the practitioner seeks restraint.

**Sautrāntika reconstruction**

The Sautrāntikas deny that non-restraint exists as a separate substance. They
identify it with:

```text
pāpakriyābhisaṃdhi
    intention toward evil activity

sānubandha
    together with its continuing connection
```

So long as this intention has not been repudiated, the person may be called a
non-restraint bearer even while a wholesome consciousness presently occurs.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Duration belongs to the undertaking**

```text
lifelong undertaking
    → lifelong restraint

day-and-night undertaking
    → day-and-night restraint
```

The temporal boundary is an internal determination of the vow acquired, not a
quantity added afterward to an otherwise complete form.

**Prescription does not prove impossibility**

```text
scripture prescribes one-day upavāsa
    does not by itself entail
longer finite restraint is intrinsically impossible
```

Vasubandhu’s `yuktyavirodha` keeps the logical possibility open even while the
Vaibhāṣika rule remains the received classification.

**Occurrence and standing form**

```text
temporary harmful act
    ≠ standing non-restraint

utterly corrupted practical intention
    → standing non-restraint
```

Duration of misconduct alone does not determine whether `asaṃvara` has been
acquired; the depth and persistence of intention matter.

**The asymmetry is defeasible**

```text
ordinary observation:
    no formally undertaken one-day non-restraint

conditional concession:
    genuine limited undertaking of non-restraint
    → non-restraint would be acquired
```

Restraint and non-restraint are genetically asymmetric without being related
by a feature-for-feature metaphysical prohibition.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The vow produces its own time**

VAK 4.27 does not treat a vow as a timeless content later placed inside an
external temporal container. The undertaking determines the temporal form of
what is acquired:

```text
samādāna
    → practical form
    → its valid temporal limit
```

A day-and-night vow is not an incomplete lifelong vow. Its finitude belongs to
its Concept. It is complete precisely as a determination whose validity ends
at the prescribed terminus.

The Bhāṣya’s account of time reinforces this. `Kāla` indicates differentiated
states of conditioned processes; it is not a second substance in which a
ready-made vow is deposited.

**Vasubandhu interrogates the failed symmetry**

The received system initially appears rigid:

```text
restraint
    lifelong or one day

non-restraint
    lifelong, never one day
```

Vasubandhu asks whether the asymmetry expresses intrinsic structure or merely
recognized practice. His concession is decisive: if someone genuinely desired
and undertook limited non-restraint, it would arise. The classification rests
partly upon how agents actually institute these forms.

This is exactly why the Kośa surpasses a merely artificial list of ethical
rules. It tests the conditions under which the rule becomes a real practical
determination.

**Cetanā returns as the candidate ground**

The Sautrāntika intervention gives strong support to the user’s hypothesis:

```text
asaṃvara
    is not a separate avijñapti substance

asaṃvara
    is evil practical intention
    with continuing connection
```

Here persistence is carried by the intentional continuum itself. This makes
`cetanā` or intentional determination a powerful candidate for the immediate
ground differentiating manifestation and non-manifest continuity.

But the distinction of levels remains necessary:

```text
Karma
    encompassing Concept

cetanā / āśaya / abhisaṃdhi
    determining practical interior

vijñapti
    manifestation of action

avijñapti or anubandha
    persistence beyond manifestation
```

The question is whether avijñapti must be hypostatized as a separate entity or
whether intentional determination, together with its connection, already
accounts for the required persistence. The Bhāṣya records this as a dispute
between Vaibhāṣika and Sautrāntika; our final Organon decision should await the
full argument.

**More than yama–niyama commentary**

This passage supplies what a merely enumerative commentary on discipline
usually lacks:

```text
the force of undertaking
the depth of underlying intention
the difference between occurrence and standing form
the internal temporal limit of a vow
the difference between prescription and rational possibility
```

It is a practical logic of vow-formation. The ethical content matters, but so
does the form by which that content becomes effective in a continuum.

This is an Organon development. Vasubandhu explicitly raises the rational
question about longer upavāsa, qualifies the one-day non-restraint rule, and
records the Sautrāntika intentional reconstruction.
