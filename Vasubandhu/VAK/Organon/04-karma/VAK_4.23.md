# VAK_4.23

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
विज्ञप्त्या तु पुनः सर्वे कुर्वन्तो मध्ययान्विताः ।
अतीतया क्षणादूर्ध्वमात्यागान्नास्त्यजातया ॥ ४.२३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
vijñaptyā tu punaḥ sarve kurvanto madhyayānvitāḥ /
atītayā kṣaṇād ūrdhvam atyāgān nāsty ajātayā // 4.23 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
vijñaptyā tu punaḥ sarve kurvantaḥ madhyayā anvitāḥ /
atītayā kṣaṇāt ūrdhvam atyāgāt na asti ajātayā //
```

The preceding Bhāṣya explicitly closes the avijñapti topic. `Tu punaḥ`, “but
now, in turn,” therefore marks a real transition back to the temporal
possession of `vijñapti`.

### `tu punaḥ`

The two particles jointly carry the transition:

```text
tu
    contrast with the preceding avijñapti analysis

punaḥ
    return to vijñapti in its turn
```

The phrase is modest grammatically but structurally decisive.

### `sarve`

The Bhāṣya identifies “all” as the three practical positions already
distinguished:

```text
one established in restraint
one established in non-restraint
one established in neither
```

The temporal rule for manifestation applies irrespective of standing ethical
status.

### `madhyā`

As in VAK 4.21, the present is the middle of past and future. Thus
`madhyayā anvitaḥ` means endowed with vijñapti in its present temporal mode.

### `ajāta`

“Not yet arisen” functions as the future temporal determination. The Bhāṣya
restates it with `anāgata`, future, and categorically denies possession of
future vijñapti.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

### Present production and possession

```text
sarve kurvantaḥ
vijñaptyā madhyayā anvitāḥ
```

> All, while producing [manifest action], are endowed with present vijñapti.

The Bhāṣya supplies an exact coextension:

```text
yāvat vijñaptiṃ kurvanti tāvat
    for as long as they produce the vijñapti,
    for that long they possess it as present
```

### Possession after the first moment

```text
atītayā [vijñaptyā anvitāḥ]
kṣaṇāt ūrdhvam
atyāgāt
```

> After the first moment, provided it has not been abandoned, they are also
> endowed with past vijñapti.

The Bhāṣya resolves the implicit moment as `prathamāt kṣaṇāt`, the first
moment.

### Categorical denial of future possession

```text
na asti ajātayā [vijñaptyā samanvāgataḥ]
```

> There is no one endowed with future vijñapti.

This is a rule about `samanvāgama`, technical possession. It does not deny
that future actions may later be performed; it denies that their not-yet-
arisen manifestation is presently possessed as vijñapti.

### Close construe

> But now, in turn, all who are acting are endowed with present vijñapti. After
> the first moment, because it has not been abandoned, [they are also endowed]
> with past [vijñapti]. There is no [possession] of future [vijñapti].

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic translation

> But now, with vijñapti in turn: all who are acting are endowed with it as
> present. After the first moment, provided it has not been abandoned, they are
> also endowed with it as past. No one is endowed with future vijñapti.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> But now, turning again to manifest action: everyone—whether established in
> restraint, non-restraint, or neither—possesses present vijñapti for exactly
> as long as he is producing it. After its first moment, and so long as it has
> not been abandoned, he also possesses that vijñapti as past. No one possesses
> a future, not-yet-arisen vijñapti.

The identification of “all,” the exact duration of present possession, and the
universal force of the final denial are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Manifest action belongs to the present only in its actual production. Once a
> moment of that production has passed, the action is also possessed under the
> determination of pastness, provided it has not been abandoned. But
> manifestation cannot be possessed in advance as future manifestation: what
> has not yet arisen may be intended or otherwise conditioned, but it is not
> yet `vijñapti` as an actually expressed bodily or verbal act.

This states the temporal asymmetry of vijñapti without importing the Organon
Wheel into the source translation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

### `vijñapti`

`Vijñapti` is bodily or verbal action insofar as it manifests, indicates, or
makes the act known. The present verse treats not its ethical content but the
temporal modes in which the agent is technically endowed with it.

### Actual production

Present possession is coextensive with doing:

```text
action is being manifestly produced
    → present vijñapti is possessed

production has ceased
    → that occurrence is no longer present vijñapti
```

### Past vijñapti

After the first moment, the prior manifest occurrence is possessed as past.
This supplies the return from current expression into the temporal history of
the agent’s action.

### No future possession

No agent possesses a future vijñapti because manifestation requires actual
arising. This distinguishes a presently operative disposition or projected
possibility from an action that has become manifest.

### `atyāga`

Non-abandonment governs possession of past vijñapti. The verse does not claim
that every performed manifestation remains indefinitely attributable under
all conditions.

### Transition marker

The sequence is explicit:

```text
avijñaptyadhikāraḥ samāptaḥ
    the avijñapti topic is complete

vijñaptyā tu punaḥ
    but now, vijñapti again in its turn
```

This is the textual basis for recognizing the return, though the Wheel and its
reciprocation belong to the Organon interpretation.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Temporal grammar of manifestation

```text
actual production
    → present vijñapti

completed preceding moment
    → past vijñapti

not-yet-arisen action
    → no future vijñapti possession
```

Manifestation is therefore indexed to actual expression.

### Universal across practical status

```text
restraint bearer
non-restraint bearer
middle-position person
    ↓
same temporal rule while producing vijñapti
```

Standing ethical classification affects the quality and wider karmic context
of action, but not this elementary temporal form of manifestation.

### Reciprocation is not temporal symmetry

The return from avijñapti to vijñapti does not make their temporal ranges
identical:

```text
some avijñapti
    can be possessed across past, present, and future

vijñapti
    present during production
    past after production
    never possessed as future
```

Semantic reciprocity preserves rather than erases this difference.

### Manifestation requires actuality

```text
future possibility of action
    ≠
future vijñapti presently possessed
```

The predicate `vijñapti` becomes valid only with the act’s manifest arising.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### The return to vijñapti

The textual movement now exhibits the Wheel’s dyadic plane with unusual
clarity:

```text
vijñapti
    manifest act
        ↓
avijñapti
    non-manifest persistence and efficacy
        ↓
vijñapti again
    manifestation newly determined through the contrast
```

The final term is not a return to the untouched starting point. After the
analysis of avijñapti, vijñapti can no longer be mistaken for the whole act.
It is now known as the manifest Aspect of a practical determination whose
effects, possession, and continuity can exceed immediate expression.

Likewise, avijñapti cannot be understood without returning to vijñapti. Its
non-manifest determination is produced through action, attributed to an agent,
and made intelligible through the manifest pole it negates. Each term sends
semantic determination back into the other.

### The dyadic plane of the Wheel

The reciprocation can be stated exactly:

```text
vijñapti → avijñapti
    manifestation posits a determination
    that persists beyond manifestation

avijñapti → vijñapti
    persistence returns to manifestation
    as its ground, expression, and possible renewal
```

This is not alternation between two independent objects. The Wheel is the
relation that lets either pole be semantically traversed into the other. Each
is an Aspect because each contains the reference to its contrary within its
own determination.

The privative `a-` is therefore productive. It negates manifestness while
preserving action-determination. The return removes the temptation to make
avijñapti a transcendent hidden thing: its truth must be expressible back in
the field of vijñapti.

### Reciprocation preserves asymmetry

A dialectical return is not a flattening symmetry. VAK 4.23 draws a strict
temporal boundary around manifestation. Future vijñapti cannot be possessed;
manifestation must actually arise. Some forms of avijñapti, however, have a
future temporal range.

The terms reciprocate semantically precisely because they contribute
different determinations:

```text
vijñapti contributes
    actuality of expression

avijñapti contributes
    continuity beyond expression

their reciprocal unity contributes
    determinate action through time
```

Without vijñapti, the analysis loses actual expression. Without avijñapti, it
loses instituted persistence. Without their return into one another, it loses
the practical act as a complete movement.

### Paramārthika dyads

At the Organon level, this provides a model for arranging the Paramārthika
Principles as dyadic oppositions. A Principle differentiates itself into
opposed Aspects; semantic movement can pass from either Aspect to the other;
and the Wheel holds their opposition as one reciprocating plane.

```text
Principle
    → positive Aspect
    → privative or counter-Aspect
    → return through reciprocal determination
```

The pair is not exhaustive because it names two objects. It is complete when
the movement between the terms has been constructed. This is the Ideal Copula
as Wheel: not static identity, but reversible determination preserving the
difference of its poles.

This is our Organon synthesis. Vasubandhu explicitly supplies the structural
return to vijñapti and its temporal possession-rule; he does not formulate the
Paramārthika Wheel in these terms.

two Aspects reversible without making them identical.
