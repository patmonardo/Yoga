# VAK 4.80 Bhāṣya — Reconnection of the Wholesome Roots

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
chinatti strī pumān dṛṣṭicaritaḥ so 'samanvayaḥ /
saṃdhiḥ kāṅkṣāstidṛṣṭibhyāṃ nehānantaryakāriṇaḥ // 4.80 //
```

> A woman or a man governed by views severs them; that is non-endowment.
> Reconnection occurs through doubt or the view that they exist, but not here
> for one who has committed an immediate-retribution action.

The kārikā completes the account begun at 4.79. It identifies who can sever
the wholesome roots, defines the severance as non-endowment, and specifies
the cognition through which their continuity is restored.

compounds and sandhi divisions have likewise been opened for readability;
the doctrine has not been rewritten.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Both a woman and a man sever the roots. Others say that a woman does not,
because her resolve, vigor, and Prajñā are weak. But the following canonical
statement would contradict them: “One endowed with the female faculty is
necessarily endowed with eight faculties.”

Even among those persons, one governed by craving does not sever the roots,
because the underlying disposition is unstable. Who, then, does so? One
**governed by views**, because such a person's unwholesome disposition is
firm and concealed. For the same reason, eunuchs and the like do not sever
them: they belong to the class governed by craving. The same applies to
beings in the painful destinies.

What is the intrinsic character of severance of the wholesome roots? It is
**non-endowment**. When acquisition of the wholesome roots no longer arises
and non-acquisition arises, then, upon the arising of this non-endowment, the
wholesome roots are said to have been severed.

How are those severed roots subsequently reconnected? Reconnection occurs
through doubt or through the view that there is. When doubt concerning cause
and result arises in that person, or the view that they exist—which means
right view—then the wholesome roots are said to be reconnected because their
acquisition arises again. All nine grades are reconnected at once, although
their manifestation occurs successively, as when health is recovered first
and strength afterward.

This reconnection can occur here in the present life for another person, but
not for one who has performed an immediate-retribution action. With that
person in view, the text says: “This person is incapable of reconnecting the
wholesome roots in this very life. Necessarily, either while falling from the
hells or while being reborn, this person will reconnect the wholesome roots.”

“While being reborn” means while existing in the intermediate state;
“while falling” means when approaching death. One who severed the roots by
the force of an internal cause reconnects them while dying; one who severed
them through the force of conditions reconnects them while being reborn.
The same distinction applies respectively to one's own force and another's
force.

It is also said that one corrupted only in disposition, and thereby severing
the roots, reconnects them in the present life; one corrupted both in
disposition and undertaking reconnects them after the breakup of the body.
The parallel distinction is between one corrupted in view and one corrupted
in both view and conduct.

Can someone have severed the wholesome roots without being fixed in
wrongness? There is a fourfold analysis. Pūraṇa and the others exemplify the
first alternative; Ajātaśatru the second; Devadatta the third; and the fourth
comprises those apart from these cases. The false view that severs the
wholesome roots ripens in Avīci. An immediate-retribution agent may ripen
either there or in another hell.

## 4. Who Can Sever the Roots

The first dispute concerns sexual faculty. Vasubandhu's initial formulation
admits both woman and man. The alternative excludes women by alleging weak
resolve, vigor, and Prajñā. The Bhāṣya answers from an authoritative faculty
classification: possession of the female faculty entails possession of the
requisite eight faculties. The alternative restriction therefore conflicts
with its own canonical framework.

The operative distinction is subsequently displaced from sex to the
character of conduct:

```text
tṛṣṇācarita — governed by craving — unstable disposition
dṛṣṭicarita — governed by view    — firm, concealed disposition
```

Only the second supplies the fixity required for complete root-severance.
This does not make craving harmless. It says that craving, precisely through
its variability, does not establish the fully settled denial analyzed here.

## 5. Severance as Non-endowment

The question `kiṃsvabhāvaḥ` asks for the determinate character of severance.
The answer is not annihilation of a substance. It is `asamanvaya`, the
continuum's non-endowment with the wholesome roots:

```text
acquisition of wholesome roots no longer arises
    + non-acquisition arises
        ↓
non-endowment is established
        ↓
the roots are designated “severed”
```

This formulation belongs to the Sarvāstivāda analysis of acquisition and
non-acquisition. The Organon need not silently identify those dharmas with a
modern notion of stored capacity. The decisive source claim is relational:
the continuum ceases to be endowed with the roots as presently operative
bases of wholesome activity.

## 6. Reconnection: Doubt and Right View

Reconnection begins in either of two cognitive events:

1. **Doubt concerning cause and result.** The certainty of denial is broken.
2. **The view that cause and result exist.** The Bhāṣya glosses this as right
   view.

Doubt is therefore efficacious without yet being knowledge. Relative to
settled false view, it reopens the relation that denial had closed. Right
view determines that relation positively.

The nine grades are acquired together, but do not all become manifest at
once. Vasubandhu's medical analogy keeps possession distinct from exercise:

```text
health recovered at once
    ≠
strength displayed completely at once
```

Likewise, reconnection restores the graded root-structure together, while
its actual manifestation unfolds in sequence.

## 7. Present Life and the Immediate Action

The compound `nehānantaryakāriṇaḥ` restricts the time of restoration. A
person who has only severed the wholesome roots may reconnect them in the
present life. For one who has also completed an immediate-retribution act,
that present-life restoration is excluded.

The Bhāṣya then distinguishes two moments associated with the next birth:

| Basis of the severance | Moment of reconnection |
|---|---|
| force of an internal cause; one's own force | while falling, approaching death |
| force of external conditions; another's force | while being reborn, in the intermediate state |

The next distinction tracks the depth to which false view has entered
action. Corruption of disposition alone permits present-life restoration.
Corruption of both disposition and undertaking postpones it until the body
breaks up. The corresponding vocabulary is corruption in view alone versus
corruption in both view and conduct.

## 8. The Fourfold Matrix

The closing question separates two predicates:

```text
A = wholesome roots have been severed
B = the person is fixed in wrongness
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly applies the four alternatives:

| Alternative | A | B | Example |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| first | yes | no | Pūraṇa and others |
| second | no | yes | Ajātaśatru |
| third | yes | yes | Devadatta |
| fourth | no | no | all cases outside the preceding three |

The named examples establish logical independence. Root-severance and
fixation in wrongness overlap but are not synonyms.

The resulting maturation is also discriminated. The particular false view
that severs wholesome roots matures in Avīci. Immediate-retribution action
has a broader hell destination: Avīci or another hell. This difference helps
explain why the predicates cannot be collapsed.

## 9. Organon Contact Point

At the project level, this is a compact theory of recoverability. A rational
moral agent becomes practically closed when denial of ground and result is
installed as its governing view. Recovery can begin before the correct
principle is fully known:

```text
settled denial
    → doubt about the denial
    → right recognition of ground and result
    → restored wholesome endowment
    → successive practical manifestation
```

The decisive movement is not the importation of an isolated wholesome
output. The governing relation becomes questionable and then determinately
true again. This gives doubt a precise transitional power: it negates the
closure of false certainty.

The distinction between simultaneous reconnection and successive
manifestation is equally useful for an agent architecture. Restoration of a
principle may restore a complete capability structure while competent use of
its grades still requires an ordered return through practice.

These formulations are an Organon reconstruction. The Sarvāstivāda dharmas
of acquisition and non-acquisition, the intermediate-state chronology, the
named persons, and the maturation claims remain the doctrine of the source.
