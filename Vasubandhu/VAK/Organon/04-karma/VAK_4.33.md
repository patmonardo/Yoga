# VAK_4.33

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
मिथ्याचारातिगर्ह्यत्वात्सौकर्यादक्रियाप्तितः ।
यथाभ्युपगमं लाभः संवरस्य न संतते ॥ ४.३३ ॥
```

The kārikā witness transmits `न संतते` without a final visarga. The Bhāṣya
anchor transmits `na saṃtateḥ` and explains the expression through the
practitioner’s continuum (`saṃtāna`). The difference is retained rather than
silently normalized.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
mithyā-ācāra-ati-garhya-tvāt
saukaryāt
akriyā-āptitaḥ

yathā-abhyupagamam
lābhaḥ
saṃvarasya

saṃtate / saṃtateḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `mithyācārātigarhyatvāt` | ablative singular abstract compound | because sexual misconduct is especially blameworthy |
| `saukaryāt` | ablative singular from `saukarya` | because of practicability; because it is comparatively easy |
| `akriyāptitaḥ` | ablative expression from `akriyā` and `āpti` | because restraint is acquired as fixed non-performance |
| `yathābhyupagamam` | adverbial compound | according to the undertaking; in conformity with what is accepted |
| `lābhaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | acquisition |
| `saṃvarasya` | genitive masculine singular | of restraint |
| `na` | negative particle | not |
| `saṃtate` | transmitted form in the kārikā witness | does not extend; contextually connected with the continuum |
| `saṃtateḥ` | reading in the Bhāṣya anchor | from or with respect to the continuum |

**`mithyācāra`**

The Bhāṣya supplies the concrete subject as `kāmamithyācāra`, sexual
misconduct. The question is not why lay followers avoid wrong conduct in
general. It is why their sexual training rule prohibits misconduct rather
than requiring the complete celibacy (`abrahmacarya-virati`) imposed by
stricter restraints.

**`atigarhyatva`**

Sexual misconduct is especially condemned in ordinary life because it injures
the marital relations of others and leads toward unfortunate rebirth. The
Bhāṣya does not say that complete celibacy is blameworthy; it says that sexual
misconduct supplies the especially blameworthy target appropriate to the lay
rule.

**`saukarya`**

`Saukarya` is ease, feasibility, or practicability. Abstention from sexual
misconduct is practicable for householders; complete celibacy is difficult
for those who continue to inhabit household life. A rule whose undertaking
householders could not sustain would obstruct entry into lay discipline.

**`akriyāpti`**

The Bhāṣya explains this through:

```text
akriyā-niyama
    fixed non-performance

akaraṇa-saṃvara
    restraint of non-doing
```

Noble persons acquire a fixed non-performance with respect to sexual
misconduct, including across other births. The same is not asserted of
complete celibacy.

**`abhyupagama`**

`Abhyupagama` is deliberate acceptance or undertaking. The operative formula
is:

```text
kāmamithyācārād viramāmi
    I abstain from sexual misconduct
```

It is not:

```text
mayā brahmacaryaṃ kartavyam
    complete celibacy is to be practiced by me
```

## 4. Grammar

**Three ablative grounds**

The first half gives three reasons for establishing the lay rule as
abstention from sexual misconduct:

```text
mithyācāra-atigarhyatvāt
    because misconduct is especially blameworthy

saukaryāt
    because this abstention is practicable

akriyā-āptitaḥ
    because fixed non-performance is acquired in this form
```

The finite predication is supplied by the Bhāṣya’s question: why has this
training rule been established (`śikṣāpadaṃ vyavasthāpitam`) for the upāsaka?

**Measure of acquisition**

```text
yathā abhyupagamam
saṃvarasya lābhaḥ
```

> Restraint is acquired according to the undertaking.

`Yathā` establishes conformity between the content undertaken and the
content acquired. The phrase answers the marriage objection: the lay follower
undertook abstention from misconduct, not abstention from every sexual
relation.

**The final expression**

The transmitted `na saṃtate` is grammatically compressed or uncertain. The
Bhāṣya anchor reads `na saṃtateḥ`, and the prose explanation says:

```text
na tu atra saṃtāne
mayā brahmacaryaṃ kartavyam
```

> But [the person does] not [undertake] with respect to this continuum: “I
> must practice complete celibacy.”

Accordingly, the denial blocks derivation of a continuum-wide celibacy vow
from the narrower undertaking. It should not be generalized without
qualification into a doctrine about every possible implication of every vow.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Because sexual misconduct is especially blameworthy, because [abstention
> from it] is practicable, and because fixed non-performance is acquired,
> restraint is acquired according to the undertaking, not [as an unrestricted
> celibate determination] of the continuum.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Abstention from sexual misconduct—rather than complete celibacy—is
> established as the lay follower’s training rule because sexual misconduct
> is especially blameworthy, because householders can practicably abstain from
> it, and because noble persons acquire a fixed restraint of non-performance
> with respect to it. The restraint acquired corresponds to the actual
> undertaking: “I abstain from sexual misconduct.” It does not thereby become
> an undertaking of complete celibacy extending through the person’s
> continuum.

The contrast with complete celibacy, the householder, the noble persons, and
the two undertaking formulas are supplied by the Bhāṣya. The translation of
the final clause follows its explanation while preserving the textual
uncertainty.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A practical determination acquires its universality through the stability
> of its determinate content, not through unlimited enlargement of its
> predicate. The lay follower renounces sexual misconduct universally as
> misconduct; the undertaking does not silently transform household life into
> monastic celibacy. Accordingly, lawful marriage does not disturb the vow,
> because it does not contradict what the vow actually determines.

This is a contextual determination of the lay sexual precept. It should not
be detached from that context and inflated into a complete theory of the
semantic scope of every restraint.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`kāmamithyācāra`**

Sexual misconduct is the prohibited field of the lay precept. The Bhāṣya
mentions injury to another’s spouse and unfortunate rebirth as reasons for
its exceptional blameworthiness.

**`abrahmacarya`**

In this contrast, abstention from `abrahmacarya` means complete sexual
abstinence or celibacy. It is required by other, stricter restraints but is
not the content of the ordinary upāsaka undertaking.

**Householder practicability**

```text
householder life
    + abstention from sexual misconduct
    → practicable lay discipline

householder life
    + compulsory complete celibacy
    → undertaking many would not sustain
```

Practicability does not make the rule arbitrary. It helps determine a form of
discipline adequate to the life in which it is undertaken.

**`akaraṇasaṃvara`**

This is restraint consisting in fixed non-performance. It is glossed as
`akriyāniyama`, a determination not to perform the prohibited action. It is
not merely the factual absence of an action on one occasion.

**`prādeśikasaṃvara`**

When the marriage objection arises, the rejected answer would say that the
upāsaka never acquired restraint regarding the future spouse. That would make
the restraint regionally or personally partial.

Vasubandhu’s answer is different: the restraint is not partial with respect
to sexual misconduct. Rather, lawful marital relations fall outside the
prohibited determination actually undertaken.

**`saṃvarakṣobha`**

`Saṃvarakṣobha` is disturbance of restraint. Marriage does not disturb this
restraint because marriage as such does not contradict abstention from sexual
misconduct.

**Scope of `yathābhyupagamam`**

The principle is exact but local:

```text
undertaken content
    abstention from sexual misconduct

acquired restraint
    sexual-misconduct restraint

excluded expansion
    complete celibacy
```

It does not support the broad claim that restraint can contain no implicit or
institutionally determined content beyond the words a recipient happens to
recite.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The concrete question**

```text
monastic and temporary restraints
    prohibit sexual activity as such

ordinary lay restraint
    prohibits sexual misconduct

question
    why is the lay determination narrower?
```

The three answers are ethical gravity, practicability, and the form in which
fixed non-performance is acquired.

**Determinate undertaking**

```text
I abstain from X
    entails restraint concerning X

it does not automatically entail
    I abstain from every broader class containing X
```

Thus:

```text
abstention from sexual misconduct
    ≠ complete celibacy
```

**Universality is not unrestricted extension**

The vow applies universally to every act falling under sexual misconduct. Its
universality does not erase the distinction between misconduct and lawful
sexual relation.

```text
universal within a determinate predicate
    not
indeterminate expansion beyond that predicate
```

**Marriage objection resolved**

```text
upāsaka marries
    + lawful relation does not instantiate sexual misconduct
    → no disturbance of the restraint
```

This does not require positing a personal gap in the restraint concerning the
future spouse. The restraint remains complete relative to its own content.

**Limit of the conclusion**

VAK 4.31 disputed whether different practitioner classes concerned
preservation, knowledge of the measure, or possession of restraint. VAK 4.33
does not resolve that entire dispute by announcing a universal partial-vow
theory. It determines the scope of this particular lay precept.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A practical universal must be determinate**

The philosophical force of the verse lies in refusing a false measure of
ethical rigor. A rule is not made more universal merely by making its
predicate less determinate:

```text
strict determination
    every instance of sexual misconduct is renounced

empty enlargement
    every sexual relation is treated as though it were misconduct
```

The first is a genuine universal of lay practice. The second substitutes a
different discipline without acknowledging the change.

**The form reorganizes household life**

Lay restraint does not leave household existence ethically untouched, but it
also does not abolish it. It determines household sexuality through the
opposition between lawful relation and misconduct.

```text
householder
    does not become a monastic subject

household relation
    becomes subject to determinate restraint
```

This is why `saukarya` should not be translated as mere convenience. The rule
is a realizable discipline adequate to its bearer’s form of life.

**Positive non-doing**

`Akaraṇasaṃvara` shows that non-performance can be positively determined:

```text
the action happens not to occur
    mere absence

the action is held under a standing prohibition
    restraint of non-doing
```

The second has practical continuity because it is grounded in an undertaken
determination. This is the positive, disciplined sense in which restraint
persists without requiring a manifest action at every moment.

**Semantic restraint in interpretation**

The first seed generalized this verse too quickly. The Bhāṣya teaches the
same discipline to the interpreter that the verse gives to the practitioner:
do not extend a determination beyond the content actually established.

The broader principle is legitimate only when retained with its concrete
ground:

```text
content of undertaking
    determines acquired practical form

but

the full content of an undertaking
    may require institutional and doctrinal explication
```

This remains an Organon contact point. The sexual-precept contrast, the three
reasons, the marriage objection, and its answer belong to the Bhāṣya.
