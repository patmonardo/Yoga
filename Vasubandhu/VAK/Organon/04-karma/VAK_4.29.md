# VAK_4.29

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
शीलाङ्गान्यप्रमादाङ्गं व्रताङ्गानि यथाक्रमम् ।
चत्वार्येकं तथा त्रीणि स्मृतिनाशो मदश्च तैः ॥ ४.२९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
śīlāṅgāny apramādāṅgaṃ vratāṅgāni yathākramam /
catvāry ekaṃ tathā trīṇi smṛtināśo madaś ca taiḥ // 4.29 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
śīla-aṅgāni apramāda-aṅgam vrata-aṅgāni yathā-kramam /
catvāri ekam tathā trīṇi smṛti-nāśaḥ madaḥ ca taiḥ //
```

The final instrumental `taiḥ`, “through those,” is compressed. The Bhāṣya
distributes the causal functions: intoxicants destroy recollection, indulgent
luxury produces `mada`, and abstention from untimely eating positively sustains
recollection of the observance and `saṃvega`.

| `śīlāṅgāni` | nominative neuter plural compound | limbs of discipline |
| `apramādāṅgam` | nominative neuter singular compound | limb of non-negligence or heedfulness |
| `vratāṅgāni` | nominative neuter plural compound | limbs of the observance |
| `yathākramam` | indeclinable compound | respectively; in the stated order |
| `catvāri` | nominative neuter plural numeral | four |
| `ekam` | nominative neuter singular numeral | one |
| `trīṇi` | nominative neuter plural numeral | three |

| `madaḥ` | nominative masculine singular | intoxication, self-inflation, indulgent exhilaration |
| `taiḥ` | instrumental masculine/neuter plural pronoun | through those; by those factors |

### `śīlāṅga`

```text
śīla
    discipline
    ethical form

aṅga
    limb
    functional constituent
```

The first four limbs are direct abstentions from naturally blameworthy acts.

### `apramāda`

`Apramāda` is non-negligence, heedfulness, or vigilant care. It is not another
primary prohibition of the same kind as the first four; it preserves their
operative presence.

### `vrata`

`Vrata` is the special observance or vowed mode of life. Its three limbs
reconfigure eating, entertainment/adornment, and sleeping arrangements so the
discipline can remain effective.

### `smṛtināśa`

The Bhāṣya specifies the destroyed recollection as memory of what ought and
ought not to be done (`kāryākāryasmṛti`). The term is practical recollection,
not bare storage of information.

### `mada`

Here `mada` is intoxicated self-inflation or indulgent exhilaration. It is
generated by luxury and entertainment and brings breach of discipline near.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

### Threefold distribution

`Yathākramam` directs the numerals to the three preceding compounds in order:

```text
śīla-aṅgāni
    catvāri

apramāda-aṅgam
    ekam

vrata-aṅgāni
    trīṇi
```

> Four are limbs of discipline, one a limb of heedfulness, and three limbs of
> observance, respectively.

### First four limbs

The Bhāṣya identifies them as abstention from:

1. killing;
2. taking what is not given;
3. sexual activity;
4. false speech.

They are grouped because their objects are naturally blameworthy
(`prakṛtisāvadya`).

### Fifth limb

Abstention from intoxicants is the one `apramādāṅga`. Even a person who has
undertaken discipline can become negligent through drinking.

### Final three limbs

The observance-limbs are abstention from:

1. eating at an improper time;
2. dancing, singing, music, exceptional adornment, and related indulgence;
3. high or luxurious beds and seats.

They accord with `saṃvega`, the sober urgency appropriate to the observance.

### Force of the final pāda

The kārikā’s compression is expanded by the Bhāṣya:

```text
intoxicants
    → destruction of recollection

luxury and entertainment
    → mada
    → breach of discipline becomes near

departure from ordinary meal-time
    → recollection of upavāsa
    + saṃvega
```

### Close construe

> Respectively, four are limbs of discipline, one is a limb of heedfulness,
> and three are limbs of observance; through [failure regarding] those there is
> destruction of recollection and intoxicated self-indulgence.

The bracketed clarification reflects the Bhāṣya’s causal explanation rather
than an additional word in the kārikā.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic translation

> Respectively, four are limbs of discipline, one is a limb of heedfulness,
> and three are limbs of observance. Through those [contrary factors] there is
> destruction of recollection and intoxicated self-indulgence.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Of the eight upavāsa limbs, the first four are limbs of discipline, the fifth
> is the limb safeguarding heedfulness, and the final three are limbs shaping
> the special observance. Intoxicants destroy recollection of what should and
> should not be done; luxury and entertainment produce intoxicated
> self-indulgence, bringing breach of discipline near; and abstention from
> untimely eating sustains recollection of the upavāsa and sober urgency.

The individual assignments and positive function of meal-time restriction are
Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The eightfold vow is an articulated practical whole. Four limbs directly
> constitute ethical discipline; one preserves the vigilance without which
> that discipline would be forgotten; three organize the surrounding mode of
> life so indulgence does not dissolve the vow’s active force. The whole is
> therefore neither a ninth entity beyond the limbs nor a mere list: it exists
> as the differentiated functional unity of constitutive, preservative, and
> supporting determinations.

The final whole–part formulation develops the Bhāṣya’s `samudāya / avayava`
analysis at the Organon level.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

### Four `śīlāṅga`

```text
non-killing
non-stealing
sexual abstinence
truthful speech
```

Their shared function is direct abstention from naturally blameworthy conduct.

### One `apramādāṅga`

```text
abstention from intoxicants
    → preserves recollection
    → prevents negligence
    → protects already undertaken śīla
```

The guarding condition is internal to the effective discipline even though it
is not one of the first four primary abstentions.

### Three `vratāṅga`

```text
no eating at an improper time
no indulgent entertainment or exceptional adornment
no high or luxurious beds and seats
```

These produce the temporary environment and embodied style appropriate to the
observance.

### `saṃvega`

`Saṃvega` is sober urgency, religious activation, or the felt seriousness that
turns the practitioner away from ordinary indulgence. The observance-limbs are
`saṃvegānuguṇa`, conducive to this orientation.

### `upavāsasmṛti`

Departing from one’s customary meal-time repeatedly brings the fact of the
observance to mind. The material alteration of routine supports practical
recollection.

### `samudāya` and `avayava`

The Bhāṣya answers the question “what is the upavāsa whose limbs these are?”
with:

```text
samudāya
    articulated aggregate or whole

avayava / aṅga
    constituent part or limb
```

The upavāsa is the eightfold aggregate. There is no ninth upavāsa-entity
standing behind its limbs.

### Whole and limb in different relations

The same determination-type can be named as whole and as limb in distinct
relations. The Bhāṣya compares:

```text
right view
    Path
    and Path-limb

samādhi
    dhyāna
    and dhyāna-limb

abstention from untimely eating
    upavāsa
    and upavāsa-limb
```

This is functional-temporal articulation, not crude self-membership.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Three functions within one form

```text
constitutive function
    four śīla-limbs
    directly restrain blameworthy action

preservative function
    one apramāda-limb
    prevents loss of practical recollection

supporting function
    three vrata-limbs
    organize a conducive form of life
```

The distinct functions are irreducible but jointly necessary for the complete
observance specified by VAK 4.28.

### Whole is not an additional object

```text
upavāsa
    = articulated unity of eight limbs

not:
    eight limbs + a ninth hidden whole
```

The whole is real as organization, not as an extra member added to the list.

### Aggregate is not mere collection

```text
eight unrelated prohibitions
    ≠ upavāsa

eight functionally ordered limbs
    → upavāsa as practical whole
```

Functional relations distinguish an articulated system from extensional
aggregation.

### A term can change logical role

```text
determination D as complete form in relation R1
    may function as limb in relation R2
```

Identity of the determination-type does not force identity of logical role.

## 9. Interpretive Essay

### The logic of aṣṭāṅga

VAK 4.29 gives a precise answer to what an eight-limbed discipline is. The
eight are not simply neighboring rules and the whole is not an invisible ninth
thing. The observance exists as their ordered functional unity.

This gives us a powerful criterion for every `aṣṭāṅga` claim:

```text
What function does each limb perform?
How do the limbs preserve one another?
What whole exists through their articulation?
```

Without these relations, “eight limbs” is merely enumeration. With them, the
system becomes a practical organism.

### Yama requires preserving and supporting conditions

The first four resemble direct yama: they prohibit naturally blameworthy
conduct. But the Kośa immediately shows why direct prohibition is insufficient.
A vow can be undertaken and then rendered practically absent through
intoxication, forgetfulness, vanity, luxury, and habitual environmental cues.

```text
direct restraint
    requires
preservation of recollection
    and
organization of a supporting life-context
```

This is why the section exceeds yama in the narrow Yoga-commentarial sense. It
does not treat discipline as a list of moral contents. It specifies the
functional system required for those contents to remain operative.

### Smṛti is practical persistence

The role of `smṛti` is exact. It keeps before the practitioner what has been
undertaken and what ought or ought not to be done. Here recollection is the
cognitive persistence of the vow within present conduct.

The fifth limb protects this persistence negatively by excluding intoxication.
The meal-time rule supports it positively by making the observance repeatedly
present through alteration of ordinary routine. Thus external practice and
internal recollection reciprocally determine one another.

### Whole, limb, and the Wheel

The Bhāṣya’s right-view example reconnects this local vow analysis with the
larger Path architecture. Right view can name the Path in one relation and
function as a Path-limb in another. A prior occurrence can contribute to a
later articulated whole without being a thing that literally contains itself.

At the Organon level:

```text
whole
    differentiates into limbs

limbs
    reciprocally sustain the whole

the Wheel
    is their organized return into one functioning form
```

This is not merely a metaphor imported into the text. It develops the
Bhāṣya’s explicit account of aggregate, constituent, function, and temporal
relation.

This remains an Organon synthesis. Vasubandhu explicitly supplies the
threefold functional classification and the whole–part analysis of the
eightfold upavāsa.
