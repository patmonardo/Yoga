# VAK 4.81 Bhāṣya — Intention and Simultaneous Courses of Action

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

```iast
yugapad yāvad aṣṭābhir aśubhaiḥ saha vartate /
cetanā daśabhir yāvac chubhair naikāṣṭapañcabhiḥ // 4.81 //
```

> Intention occurs simultaneously with as many as eight unwholesome courses;
> with as many as ten wholesome courses, but not with one, eight, or five.

The verse asks how many courses of action can occur together with one
intention. The Bhāṣya constructs the permitted numbers from concrete
configurations rather than treating the ten courses as freely combinable
items.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

The present discussion remains concerned with the courses of action. It must
now be stated with how many courses an intention arising at one time can
coexist.

Intention occurs simultaneously with as many as eight unwholesome courses.
It occurs with one when covetousness or another mental course becomes
manifest without any other course; or when, through the preparatory action
of a person whose mind is not presently afflicted, one of the material
courses reaches completion.

It occurs with two when someone with a hostile mind kills a living being;
or when someone possessed by covetousness commits taking what is not given,
sexual misconduct, or frivolous speech.

It occurs with three when someone with a hostile mind simultaneously kills
another person's living being and carries it away. Would taking what is not
given, then, no longer necessarily reach completion through greed? That rule
must be understood when the agent has no different state of mind at the
completion. Three also occur when someone possessed by covetousness or
another mental course causes two material courses to reach completion
through the corresponding preparation.

It occurs with four when a person intending division utters false speech or
harsh speech. There is then one mental course and three verbal courses. Four
also occur when someone under covetousness or another mental course causes
three other material courses to reach completion through the corresponding
preparation. Combinations with five, six, and seven are to be constructed in
the same manner.

Intention occurs with eight when someone has prepared six courses and,
personally committing sexual misconduct, causes all of them to reach
completion together. So much for the unwholesome courses.

With wholesome courses intention occurs with as many as ten. Having stated
the general rule that intention can coexist with up to ten wholesome courses,
the verse supplies an exception: not with one, eight, or five.

It occurs with two in the five wholesome sense consciousnesses, and in the
formless attainments with the knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising. It
occurs with three in a mental consciousness conjoined with right view. It
occurs with four when a person whose present mind is unwholesome or
indeterminate undertakes the restraint of a lay follower or novice. It
occurs with six when that restraint is undertaken while one of the five
sense consciousnesses is wholesome.

It occurs with seven when that restraint is undertaken in a wholesome mental
consciousness, and also when a person with an unwholesome or indeterminate
mind undertakes monastic restraint. It occurs with nine when the restraint
is undertaken with one of the five wholesome sense consciousnesses; in a
mental consciousness conjoined with the knowledges of exhaustion or
non-arising; and in that same mental consciousness when it belongs to
dhyāna. In other wholesome mental consciousness, intention occurs with ten
when monastic restraint is undertaken.

Every intention accompanying dhyāna restraint or uncontaminated restraint
also belongs here, apart from the knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising.
Outside a complete restraint, however, intention can occur even with one
course when a person in another state of mind undertakes abstention from a
single limb. It can also occur with five or eight in wholesome mental
consciousness when two-limbed or five-limbed abstention is undertaken at
once.

## 4. The Question Is Event-level Conjunction

The question is not how many courses a person possesses across a lifetime.
It asks how many occur `yugapat`, simultaneously, with one presently arisen
intention. Three layers must therefore be kept apart:

```text
endowment across the continuum
present manifestation of a course
simultaneous conjunction within one intention-event
```

The Bhāṣya counts courses at the moment of completion. Preparation may have
been distributed across time or delegated to others; several prepared
courses can nevertheless complete together under the relevant intention.

## 5. Unwholesome Conjunctions: One Through Eight

The unwholesome series is constructive. Vasubandhu supplies configurations
for one, two, three, four, and eight, then directs the reader to compose five,
six, and seven analogously.

| Number | Illustrative structure |
|---:|---|
| 1 | one mental course alone, or one material course completing from prior preparation |
| 2 | ill will plus killing; or covetousness plus one specified bodily or verbal course |
| 3 | ill will plus killing and taking; or one mental course plus two material completions |
| 4 | one mental and three verbal courses, or one mental plus three material completions |
| 5–7 | constructed by extending the same completion logic |
| 8 | six prepared courses plus two courses involved in personally performed sexual misconduct |

The objection about taking what is not given is significant. A course
normally said to complete from greed can complete while hostility is the
agent's present governing mind. Vasubandhu limits the greed rule to a case in
which the mind has not changed by the time of completion. The root associated
with a normal completion and the actual cognition at a complex simultaneous
completion must not be mechanically identified.

No configuration of nine or ten unwholesome courses is admitted here. The
different bodily, verbal, and mental requirements prevent total simultaneous
composition.

## 6. Wholesome Conjunctions: The Regular Series

The wholesome series is organized by cognition and restraint. Its regular
numbers are:

```text
2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10
```

The changing total records the combination of present wholesome cognition
with abstentions acquired through a particular restraint undertaking. A
wholesome mental consciousness with right view supplies three mental courses.
Lay or novice restraint adds four abstentions; monastic restraint supplies
seven. The five sense consciousnesses and the special knowledges alter which
mental courses can be present, producing the stated totals.

Thus the number is not a degree on a single moral scale. It is the cardinality
of a configuration generated by:

```text
kind of consciousness
    + courses present in that cognition
    + restraint being undertaken or already accompanying it
```

## 7. Why One, Five, and Eight Reappear

The kārikā excludes wholesome totals of one, five, and eight under the
general, fully classified restraint pattern. The end of the Bhāṣya then
supplies bounded exceptions:

- **one** occurs outside a complete restraint when a single abstention is
  undertaken while the agent's concurrent mind has another character;
- **five** can occur when two abstention limbs are undertaken together in a
  wholesome mental consciousness;
- **eight** can occur when five abstention limbs are undertaken together in
  a wholesome mental consciousness.

This is not a cancellation of the verse. The Bhāṣya marks a change in scope
with `saṃvaranirmuktena tu`, “but apart from restraint.” The verse states the
regular combinations within the established restraint classes; partial
undertakings permit the otherwise excluded totals.

## 8. Intention as Practical Unity

`Cetanā` is counted alongside the courses with which it occurs, yet it is not
merely another item in their heap. One intention coordinates a structured
event in which several determinations can complete simultaneously.

```text
one intention
    → preparation of several possible courses
    → bodily, verbal, and mental determinations
    → one completion-event with a constrained cardinality
```

This makes the passage practically agential. The unity lies in volitional
organization; the plurality lies in the courses that attain their determinate
completion through it.

## 9. Organon Contact Point

For the Organon, 4.81 supplies a combinatorial grammar of action. A Program
Feature should not reduce one intention to one output. It must admit one
governing practical determination whose preparations produce several
coordinated completions:

```text
PracticalEvent =
    <Intention,
     Preparations,
     MaterialCourses,
     MentalCourses,
     CompletionRelation,
     RestraintContext>
```

The permitted cardinality is derived from the relations among these fields.
It is not an arbitrary validation constant. The Bhāṣya demonstrates the
method: construct each allowed total from cognition, restraint, preparation,
and completion; then state the exceptional scope explicitly.

This is a project reconstruction. Vasubandhu's own concern is the Abhidharma
enumeration of jointly occurring courses, the relevant consciousnesses, and
the restraint classes.
