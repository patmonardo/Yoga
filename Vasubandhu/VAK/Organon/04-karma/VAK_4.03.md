# VAK_4.03

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> न कस्यचिदहेतोः स्याद्धेतुः स्याच्च विनाशकः ।
>
> द्विग्राह्यं स्यान्न चाणौ तद्वाग्विज्ञप्तिस्तु वाग्ध्वनिः ॥ ४.३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> na kasyacid ahetoḥ syād hetuḥ syāc ca vināśakaḥ /
>
> dvigrāhyaṃ syān na cāṇau tad vāgvijñaptis tu vāgdhvaniḥ // 4.3 //

This unusually compressed verse completes the argument against a separate
cause of destruction, turns the resulting momentariness against enduring
motion, and then records the Sautrāntika objections to configuration as an
independently real material entity. Its final pāda determines verbal
manifestation as vocal sound.

The argumentative subjects and school positions are not recoverable securely
from the isolated Kārikā alone. They are resolved by the continuous Bhāṣya
discussion extending from VAK 4.02 into this unit.

| na | negative particle | not |
| kasyacit | genitive neuter singular indefinite pronoun | of anything / for anything |
| ahetoḥ | ablative singular compound | without a cause; because no cause is required |

| tat | nominative neuter singular pronoun | that, namely configuration |

| tu | contrastive particle | but / however |
| vāgdhvaniḥ | nominative masculine singular compound | vocal or speech-sound |

The Bhāṣya secures two otherwise uncertain referents:

```text
dvigrāhyam
    = configuration would be apprehensible by both eye and bodily touch

tat in na cāṇau tat
    = configuration is not present in a single atom
```

`Vināśaka` must be distinguished from `vināśa`:

```text
vināśa
    destruction / cessation

vināśaka
    something functioning as a destroyer
```

The verse contests the need for the second in order to explain the first.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The opening is elliptical because `vināśa`, destruction, is carried from the
Bhāṣya argument under VAK 4.02:

```text
[vināśaḥ]
    na kasyacit
    ahetoḥ syāt

[destruction]
    would, for nothing,
    be without a cause
```

The surface clause states a consequence of supposing that destruction requires
a cause: no destruction would then be causeless. The Bhāṣya counters that
momentary cognitions, sounds, and flames are observed to cease without a
separate destroyer. The next clause states another unwanted consequence of
requiring such a cause:

```text
hetuḥ eva
    vināśakaḥ syāt

the productive cause itself
    would become a destroyer
```

The second pāda condenses two Sautrāntika objections to `saṃsthāna` as a
distinct `dravya`:

```text
[saṃsthānam]
    dvigrāhyaṃ syāt

[configuration]
    would be apprehensible by two [faculties]

tat
    na ca aṇau [vidyate]

and that
    does not exist in a single atom
```

The final nominal predication is comparatively direct:

```text
vāk-vijñaptiḥ tu
    vāk-dhvaniḥ

verbal manifestation, however,
    [is] vocal sound
```

The particle `tu` marks the transition from the disputed status of bodily
manifestation to the identification of manifest verbal action with sound.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> For nothing would [destruction] be causeless, and the cause would become a
> destroyer. [Configuration] would be apprehensible by two [faculties], and it
> does not exist in a single atom. Verbal manifestation, however, is vocal
> sound.

The bracketed subjects and the conditional force of the first sentence are
supplied from the continuous Bhāṣya argument.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> If destruction required a cause as arising does, no destruction would be
> causeless; yet momentary cognitions, sounds, and flames cease without a
> separate destroyer, and a productive cause would otherwise have to function
> as a destroyer. Moreover, if bodily configuration were an independently real
> material determination, the same form would be directly apprehensible by
> both sight and touch, although no such configuration exists within a single
> atom. Verbal manifestation, by contrast, is speech-sound.

The interpretation of `dvigrāhya` as apprehension by eye and bodily faculty,
the inference from atomic absence, and the formulation in terms of independent
material reality are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A conditioned occurrence does not first endure and then receive destruction
> from a second cause. It is intrinsically perishable: causes account for the
> arising of its successor, not for manufacturing its absence. What appears as
> bodily motion is consequently a serial production of momentary material
> arrangements. Yet configuration cannot simply be installed as another
> self-subsisting material thing, for it is intelligible only through an
> arranged plurality and is not contained in any isolated atom. Bodily action
> therefore divides into determining volition and its designated manifest
> form, whereas manifest verbal action is the produced sound of speech.

This rendering follows the Sautrāntika reconstruction developed in the
Bhāṣya. The Vaibhāṣikas retain configuration as a real material determination;
the Kārikā records the controversy rather than erasing it.

The two schools preserve different meanings of `saṃsthāna`:

```text
Vaibhāṣika:
    saṃsthāna is a real material determination

Sautrāntika:
    saṃsthāna is a grounded designation
    upon material determinations arranged in a certain manner
```

`Prajñapti` does not here mean arbitrary fiction. The configuration is grounded
in a real arrangement; what is denied is an additional independent entity over
and above the arranged plurality.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Production and destruction are asymmetric

```text
arising of a conditioned occurrence
    requires productive causes

its destruction
    is not a second positive occurrence
    and therefore is not produced by a destroying cause

what requires renewed causation
    is the production of a successor
```

The fire-and-wood example is reconstructed accordingly:

```text
not necessarily:
    fire positively produces the destruction of enduring wood

but:
    the prior wood-moment perishes
    fire alters the causal complex
    a similar wood-successor is no longer produced
```

This establishes `kṣaṇabhaṅga` without treating non-being as a manufactured
effect.

### Motion becomes serial appearance

```text
moment A₁ arises and perishes at place A
moment A₂ arises and perishes at adjacent place B
moment A₃ arises and perishes at adjacent place C

uninterrupted serial arising
    → gatyabhimāna
    → appearance of continuous motion
```

No numerically identical mover traverses A, B, and C.

### Configuration undergoes immanent criticism

The rejection of motion initially appeared to secure configuration as bodily
manifestation. The Sautrāntika now tests that proposed entity:

```text
if configuration were one independently real rūpa:
    sight would apprehend it
    bodily touch would apprehend it
    → one rūpa would cross two sensory domains

if configuration were independently real resistant rūpa:
    it would occur in a single atom

but:
    longness, shortness, roundness, and the rest
    do not occur in an isolated atom

therefore:
    configuration is not a separate dravya
```

The positive reconstruction is relational:

```text
many material determinations
    + determinate saṃniveśa
        → grounded prajñapti of configuration
```

### The location of bodily karma shifts

```text
saṃkalpa-cetanā
    “I shall do this”
        ↓
kriyā-cetanā
    volition actively directing the body
        ↓
arranged momentary bodily succession
        ↓
saṃsthāna designated as bodily manifestation
```

Thus the Sautrāntika distinguishes:

```text
karmically determining activity
    = cetanā

manifest bodily form
    = designated configuration
```

The Vaibhāṣika refuses the reduction of configuration to designation and
retains `saṃsthāna` itself as a real material determination. This disagreement
must remain visible because it governs the impending dispute over
`avijñapti`.

### Verbal manifestation

```text
operative volition
    → production of speech-sound
        → vāk-vijñapti
```

The verse identifies the manifest verbal act with vocal sound; it does not yet
determine the status of verbal `avijñapti`.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.03 enacts the immediate discipline of Transcendental Philosophy. It
does not permit an explanatory demand to generate a new entity merely because
the entity would make the explanation easy.

First, destruction cannot be hypostatized as a positive product with its own
producer. The question must instead be returned to the conditions under which
a successor arises or fails to arise:

```text
not:
    what thing produces non-being?

but:
    what conditions the next determination?
```

Second, configuration cannot be installed behind the arranged plurality as an
extra material object. The Sautrāntika criticism preserves its semantic work
while refusing its substantialization:

```text
configuration is objectively grounded
    in determinate arrangement

configuration is not therefore
    an additional self-subsisting thing
```

This is already the form of the impending `avijñapti` trial. A function may be
necessary without licensing a transcendent bearer of that function. The text
has not yet reached its verdict on non-manifest action, but it has established
the discipline under which the claim must be judged.

The Hegelian contact is strongest at the level of method: a proposed
determination is not refuted from outside but made to disclose the relations it
presupposes. `Saṃsthāna` cannot maintain itself as an isolated entity; its
truth passes into the determinate arrangement of a plurality and the activity
through which that arrangement manifests volition.

The Sāṃkhya–Yoga comparison should remain open at this stage. The passage has
already distinguished determining activity, material plurality, configuration,
manifestation, and causal succession—the exact kinds of difference that the
project's Sāṃkhya–Yoga resources can articulate. But no one-to-one mapping has
yet been established by the Sanskrit or Bhāṣya.

The achieved sequence is:

```text
cetanā
    determining activity

saṃniveśa
    material articulation

saṃsthāna
    manifest relational form

vijñapti
    action made known

causal succession
    efficacy beyond any isolated visible moment
```

The semantic task is not to erase any member of this sequence. It is to deny
that each required distinction must become a separately subsisting entity.
