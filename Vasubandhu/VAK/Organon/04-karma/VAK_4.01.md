# VAK_4.01

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कर्मजं लोकवैचित्र्यं चेतना तत्कृतं च तत् ।
>
> चेतना मानसं कर्म तज्जं वाक्कायकर्मणी ॥ ४.१ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> karmajaṃ lokavaicitryaṃ cetanā tatkṛtaṃ ca tat /
>
> cetanā mānasaṃ karma tajjaṃ vākkāyakarmaṇī // 4.1 //

The opening statement joins Karmanirdeśa directly to the differentiated World
described in Lokanirdeśa. The Kārikā first names that World as karma-born and
then analyzes karma into volition and the activity produced from volition.
The Bhāṣya specifies that `loka` here includes both the world of beings and the
receptacle-world, and that the relevant karma is the karma of beings.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
karma-jam                  → karmajaṃ
loka-vaicitryam            → lokavaicitryaṃ
cetanā                     → cetanā
tat-kṛtam                  → tatkṛtaṃ
ca                         → ca
tat                        → tat
cetanā                     → cetanā
mānasam                    → mānasaṃ
karma                      → karma
tat-jam                    → tajjaṃ
vāk-kāya-karmaṇī           → vākkāyakarmaṇī
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| karmajam | nominative neuter singular compound adjective | born from or generated by karma |
| lokavaicitryam | nominative neuter singular compound | differentiated manifoldness of the world |
| cetanā | nominative feminine singular | volition; action-directing mental determination |
| tatkṛtam | nominative neuter singular compound | what is produced or done by that volition |
| ca | coordinating particle | and |
| tat | nominative neuter singular pronoun | that, namely the karma just defined |
| mānasam | nominative neuter singular adjective | mental; belonging to mind |
| karma | nominative neuter singular | action / karma |
| tajjam | nominative neuter singular compound | born from that, namely from volition |
| vākkāyakarmaṇī | nominative neuter dual compound | verbal action and bodily action |

The final pāda is compressed. `Tajjaṃ` is neuter singular whereas
`vākkāyakarmaṇī` is neuter dual. The Bhāṣya resolves the intended relation by
identifying the action generated by volition (`cetanājanita`, `cetayitvā
karma`) with bodily and verbal action. A close construe therefore supplies
`karma` with `tajjaṃ` rather than silently regularizing the witness.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

The first pāda is a nominal predication:

```text
loka-vaicitryam
    karma-jam

the differentiation of the world
    is born from karma
```

The second pāda defines the referent of `tat`, karma:

```text
tat [karma]
    cetanā
    ca tat-kṛtam

that [karma is]
    volition
    and what is produced by it
```

The second hemistich correlates this causal division with the three domains of
action:

```text
cetanā
    mānasaṃ karma

volition
    [is] mental action

tat-jaṃ [karma]
    vāk-kāya-karmaṇī

action born from that
    [is] verbal and bodily action
```

The Bhāṣya cites the corresponding sūtra division:

```text
cetanā karma
    volition as karma

cetayitvā karma
    karma after having willed
```

It then asks whether bodily, verbal, and mental action are classified by
support (`āśraya`), intrinsic nature (`svabhāva`), or origination
(`samutthāna`). The Vaibhāṣika answer distributes the three grounds among the
three actions respectively. This classificatory argument belongs to the
commentary, not to the explicit syntax of the verse.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> The differentiation of the world is born from karma. That [karma] is
> volition and what is produced by it. Volition is mental action; action born
> from it is verbal and bodily action.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The manifold differentiation of the world of beings and their
> receptacle-world is generated by the karmas of beings. Karma comprises
> volition itself and action produced after volition: volition is mental
> action, while the bodily and verbal actions it generates are action after
> having willed.

The specification of the two worlds, the attribution to beings' karmas, and
the equivalence of `tatkṛta` with `cetayitvā karma` are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The differentiated actuality in which beings live is not first posited as
> a finished World to which action is subsequently added. It is karma-born:
> the accumulated result of determining activity within conditioned
> existence. This activity begins as volition, becomes actual through bodily
> and verbal expression, and thereby participates in producing the different
> embodiments, enjoyments, capacities, and environments encountered as
> World. Yet karma is not reducible to outward behavior, for its primary
> determination is volition itself.

The Bhāṣya adds an essential limit to this reading. The differentiated World
is not deliberately constructed by an external intelligent maker. Its ground
is immanent karmic activity; this does not make any single private volition
the designer of the whole receptacle-world.

`Cetanā` is more than a wish and need not be an explicit deliberative choice.
Here it is the action-directing mental determination that is itself karma.

`Tatkṛta` establishes genetic dependence:

```text
cetanā
    → bodily and verbal action
```

It does not yet explain how a vanished action remains efficacious. That
problem opens with `vijñapti` and `avijñapti` in the following unit.

## 8. Logical Determination

### Explanatory and genetic orders

The exposition begins from actuality and asks for its ground:

```text
loka-vaicitrya
    ← karma
        ← cetanā
```

The process itself runs forward:

```text
cetanā
    → verbal and bodily action
        → karmic efficacy
            → differentiated World
```

The middle relation—how momentary activity acquires or retains the efficacy
to produce later results—is not yet supplied by VAK 4.01. It is the problem
bequeathed to the chapter.

### Two intersecting divisions

```text
causal division:
    karma = cetanā + cetanā-produced action

domain division:
    karma = mental + verbal + bodily action

intersection:
    mental action = cetanā
    verbal and bodily action = cetanā-produced action
```

The members of the threefold division are therefore not coordinate in every
respect. Mental action is the determining activity; bodily and verbal actions
are its generated expressions.

### Bhāṣya test of classification

No single undifferentiated criterion preserves the threefold distinction:

```text
support alone
    → all three depend upon body
    → everything would be bodily action

intrinsic nature alone
    → speech itself has karmic nature
    → everything would collapse into verbal action

origination alone
    → all three arise from mind
    → everything would be mental action
```

The attributed Vaibhāṣika solution uses distinct classificatory grounds in
sequence. The Bhāṣya thereby turns a received list into a question about what
makes each distinction valid.

### Immanent rather than external production

```text
not:
    external designing intelligence
        → deliberately fabricated World

but:
    karmic activity of conditioned beings
        → differentiated conditioned World
```

The second formulation is textual doctrine at the Bhāṣya level. Its further
description as immanent existential production is the Organon interpretation.

## 9. Interpretive Note

Karmanirdeśa begins as the existential return into the ground of Loka.
Lokanirdeśa displayed a World already differentiated into beings,
receptacles, destinies, embodiments, durations, enjoyments, and dissolutions.
The opening question of Chapter 4 is not simply, “Which deeds are good or
bad?” It is:

```text
How does this differentiated existence come to be?
```

The first answer is `karmaja`: it is born from activity. The second answer
locates the nucleus of that activity in `cetanā`. Thus the chapter moves from
the accomplished fact of existence to the activity by which existence is
determined.

This does not identify `cetanā` with an independent metaphysical subject.
The Kārikā says positively that volition **is mental action**. Nor does it say
that mind alone immediately creates the World. It articulates a mediated
sequence in which inward determination generates bodily and verbal action,
and karmic efficacy generates differentiated result.

The Bhāṣya's mixed-karma objection prevents a crude correspondence theory.
Attractive enjoyments can coexist with disagreeable embodiment because a
continuum's karmic determination can be mixed. Karma is therefore not a
single deed mirrored by a single visibly matching result; it is a structured
causal complex whose differentiated results can appear on different sides of
existence.

The controlled Organon formulation is:

```text
Karma
    = existentially effective determination

not because every action creates being from nothing,
but because volitional activity enters the causal production
of the mode of conditioned existence that can be lived and undergone.
```

This is the chapter's first determination, not yet its completed theory. The
remaining inquiry must establish expression, persistence, ethical quality,
appropriation, maturation, and release.
