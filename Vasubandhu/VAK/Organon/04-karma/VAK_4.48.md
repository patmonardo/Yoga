# VAK_4.48

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अधोऽपि मध्यमस्त्येके ध्यानान्तरविपाकतः ।
अपूर्वाचरमः पाकस्त्रयाणां चेष्यते यतः ॥ ४.४८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
adho 'pi madhyam asty eke dhyānāntaravipākataḥ /
apūrvācaramaḥ pākas trayāṇāṃ ceṣyate yataḥ // 4.48 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
adhaḥ api madhyamaḥ asti eke dhyāna-antara-vipākataḥ /
apūrva-acaramaḥ pākaḥ trayāṇām ca iṣyate yataḥ //
```

`Madhyamaḥ` denotes the middle of the three preceding result-classes: karma
to be experienced as neither pain nor pleasure.

**`madhyama`**

The middle class is `aduḥkhāsukhavedanīya`: karma whose maturation is
experienced as neither painful nor pleasant. The verse extends its range; it
does not introduce a fourth result-class.

**`dhyānāntara`**

The intermediate dhyāna lacks both pleasant and painful feeling. Some
authorities therefore argue that its karmic maturation requires the neutral
feeling-class.

**`apūrvācarama`**

The compound means “neither prior nor posterior.” The Bhāṣya's cited sūtra
fixes its force: the three maturations occur together, so none is temporally
first or last.

## 4. Grammar

**The position of some authorities**

```text
eke [āhuḥ]
adhaḥ api
madhyamaḥ asti
```

> Some authorities maintain that the middle class exists even below.

The verb of saying is understood with `eke`. “Below” means below the fourth
dhyāna, where VAK 4.47 placed the principal range of neutral maturation.

**The intermediate-dhyāna argument**

```text
dhyāna-antara-vipākataḥ
```

> Because it is the maturation of the intermediate dhyāna.

The ablative in `-taḥ` supplies the causal ground for extending the middle
class downward.

**The simultaneous-maturation argument**

```text
yataḥ
trayāṇām pākaḥ
apūrva-acaramaḥ
ca iṣyate
```

> And because a maturation of the three is accepted in which none is prior or
> posterior.

The Bhāṣya explains this as simultaneous maturation, not as an ordering of the
three within the meditative hierarchy.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Some maintain that the middle class exists even below, because it is the
> maturation of the intermediate dhyāna, and because a maturation of the three
> is accepted in which none is prior or posterior.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Some authorities maintain that karma whose maturation is experienced as
> neither pain nor pleasure also occurs below the fourth dhyāna. The
> intermediate dhyāna, which has neither pleasant nor painful feeling, requires
> such a result; scripture also recognizes the simultaneous maturation of
> pleasant-, painful-, and neutral-result karmas, something possible only in
> the desire domain. The distribution stated in VAK 4.47 is therefore the
> predominant rule rather than an exceptionless one.

The simultaneous force of `apūrvācarama`, the desire-domain restriction, and
the qualification of the preceding rule as predominant are supplied by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A general classification must be revised when the concrete field presents a
> boundary case. The intermediate dhyāna lacks the feelings assigned by the
> neighboring categories, while the desire domain permits all three karmic
> result-types to mature together. The original law is not discarded; its
> quantificational force is corrected from exceptionless universality to
> predominance, and the extension of the neutral class becomes more adequate
> to the field.

Distinct causal determinations need not produce their effects successively:
their maturations can remain distinct while becoming actual simultaneously.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**First ground: affective structure of the intermediate dhyāna**

```text
intermediate dhyāna
    lacks pleasant feeling
    lacks painful feeling
    → neutral-feeling maturation required
```

The Bhāṣya records alternatives. One view assigns `sukhendriya` as the
maturation; another says its maturation is not feeling at all. The latter is
rejected as contrary to the treatise because the śāstra allows mental feeling
as the maturation of wholesome karma without `vitarka`.

**Second ground: the cited sūtra**

```text
pleasant-result karma
    → rūpa

painful-result karma
    → citta-caitta dharmas

neutral-result karma
    → citta-viprayukta dharmas
```

These three results can mature simultaneously. The Bhāṣya says this conjunction
of all three maturations occurs only in the desire domain.

**`yugapat`**

`Yugapat`, “simultaneously,” is the Bhāṣya's decisive temporal explanation of
the kārikā's `apūrvācarama`.

**`bāhulika`**

The preceding rule is `bāhulika`: a description of what occurs predominantly
or in the general run of cases.

```text
predominant rule
    ≠ exceptionless universal
```

**Textual uncertainty**

After asking whether the exceptional karma is wholesome or unwholesome, the
available Sanskrit witness reads only `durbalaṃ tu tat`, “but it is weak.” The
passage is too compressed to reconstruct the missing doctrinal determination
securely; the uncertainty remains open.

## 8. Logical Determination

**General rule and boundary case**

```text
GENERAL RULE
    wholesome karma through third dhyāna
        → pleasant maturation

BOUNDARY CASE
    intermediate dhyāna has neither pleasure nor pain
        → neutral maturation
```

The boundary case does not destroy the classification. It corrects the range
assigned to one member.

**Scriptural counterexample**

```text
desire domain
    → simultaneous maturation of all three result-classes
    → neutral-result karma exists below the fourth dhyāna
```

This directly prevents the earlier distribution from being read as universal.

**Revised quantification**

```text
initial appearance
    ALL lower wholesome karma → pleasant result

Bhāṣya determination
    PREDOMINANTLY lower wholesome karma → pleasant result
    SOME lower karma → neutral result
```

**Simultaneity without identity**

```text
three karmic causes
    → three differently constituted maturations
    → one temporal conjunction
```

Simultaneous actuality does not collapse the differences among the results.

**Dhātu as concrete range**

The desire domain is not defined here by one exclusive feeling-result. Its
concrete structure can support the conjunction of all three:

```text
one dhātu
    → multiple karmic determinations
    → simultaneous differentiated maturation
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The field corrects the abstract rule**

VAK 4.47 gave a clean distribution by level. VAK 4.48 tests it against the
concrete content of intermediate dhyāna and against the sūtra's counterexample.
The result is not skepticism about classification but a more exact law.

```text
classification
    → application
    → counterexample
    → qualified classification
```

**The middle appears below**

Neutral maturation is not restricted to a highest terminal position. As the
middle determination, it can appear within the lower field when the concrete
conditions exclude both pleasure and pain.

**Dhātu permits differentiated coexistence**

The simultaneous desire-domain case strengthens the field interpretation of
Dhātu. A field is not a homogeneous quality shared by everything within it.
It is the systematic range in which distinct determinations can arise, relate,
and become actual together.

**Doctrinal restraint**

The kārikā attributes the initial intermediate-dhyāna position to “some.” The
Bhāṣya then supplies arguments and a scriptural case. The textual uncertainty
after `durbalaṃ tu tat` prevents us from forcing a complete sectarian
resolution beyond what the witness supports.

This is the Organon contact. The alternative views, śāstra rejection, sūtra
distribution, desire-domain restriction, and `bāhulika` qualification belong
to the Bhāṣya.
