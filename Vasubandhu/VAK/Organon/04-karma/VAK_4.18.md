# VAK_4.18

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अनागम्ये प्रहाणाख्यौ तावानन्तर्यमार्गजौ ।
सम्प्रज्ञानस्मृती द्वे तु मनइन्द्रियसंवरौ ॥ ४.१८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```iast
anāgamye prahāṇākhyau tāv ānantaryamārgajau /
saṃprajñānasmṛtī dve tu manaindriyasaṃvarau // 4.18 //
```

Padapāṭha-style separation:

```iast
anāgamye prahāṇa-ākhyau tau ānantarya-mārga-jau /
saṃprajñāna-smṛtī dve tu manaḥ-indriya-saṃvarau //
```

The transmitted `tāv ānantarya-` displays regular sandhi from `tau` before a
vowel. The final half is syntactically compressed and must be read with the
Bhāṣya: both restraints have both `smṛti` and `saṃprajñāna` as their nature.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
anāgamye
prahāṇa-ākhyau
tau
ānantarya-mārga-jau

saṃprajñāna-smṛtī
dve
tu
manaḥ-indriya-saṃvarau
```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical value here |
|---|---|---|
| `anāgamye` | locative singular neuter of `anāgamya` | in the preliminary or threshold concentration |
| `prahāṇākhyau` | nominative masculine dual, `prahāṇa-ākhya` | the two called “abandonment restraint” |
| `tau` | nominative masculine dual pronoun | those two restraints |
| `ānantaryamārgajau` | nominative masculine dual, `ānantarya-mārga-ja` | born in the uninterrupted paths |
| `saṃprajñāna-smṛtī` | coordinated dual expression | clear comprehension and recollection |
| `dve` | nominative dual | two; twofold in nature |
| `tu` | adversative particle | but; however |
| `manaindriyasaṃvarau` | nominative masculine dual compound | mind-restraint and faculty-restraint |

**`anāgamya`**

`Anāgamya` names the preliminary or threshold concentration. The Bhāṣya
restricts the rule to its nine uninterrupted paths; it does not apply the name
`prahāṇasaṃvara` to every occurrence of meditation-born or uncontaminated
restraint.

**`prahāṇākhya`**

```text
prahāṇa
    abandonment
    relinquishment

ākhya
    named
    designated
```

The compound states a designation acquired under definite conditions. It does
not posit a fourth substance beside the previously distinguished restraints.

**`ānantaryamārga`**

```text
ānantarya
    without an interval
    immediate
    uninterrupted

mārga
    path
```

“Uninterrupted path” is retained as the technical rendering. Here it refers to
the nine path-moments specified by the Bhāṣya.

**`saṃprajñāna` and `smṛti`**

`Saṃprajñāna` is situated discriminative awareness: the operation through
which what is being done and its relevant conditions are clearly comprehended.
It must not be flattened into `prajñā` itself. In the Organon vocabulary,
`prajñā` is the Discriminator-Principle that grounds this operation; clear
comprehension is the resulting lucidity. `Smṛti` is recollection or
mindfulness: the non-loss of the practical or contemplative orientation.

They operate jointly in each of the two cognitive restraints.

## 4. Grammar and Construe

**First predication**

```text
anāgamye prahāṇa-ākhyau
```

The locative `anāgamye` supplies the domain in which the two restraints receive
the predicate `prahāṇākhyau`:

> In the preliminary concentration, the two are called abandonment
> restraints.

The identity of “the two” is carried forward from VAK 4.17: meditation-born
restraint and uncontaminated restraint.

**Qualification of the two**

```text
tau ānantarya-mārga-jau
```

The pronoun and compound are nominative dual:

> those two being born in the uninterrupted paths.

The Bhāṣya specifies the nine uninterrupted paths of the `anāgamya`. The
participial force may be conveyed more naturally in English by “when born in.”

**The apparent distributive reading**

The surface sequence may tempt the reader to distribute the terms in order:

```text
saṃprajñāna → manaḥsaṃvara
smṛti       → indriyasaṃvara
```

The Bhāṣya expressly blocks this `yathāsaṃkhya` construction. The repeated
indication of duality is meant to show that each restraint is twofold in
nature.

**Bhāṣya-governed construction**

```text
manaḥsaṃvara
    = smṛti + saṃprajñāna

indriyasaṃvara
    = smṛti + saṃprajñāna
```

Thus `saṃprajñānasmṛtī dve` predicates a twofold constitution of both members
of `manaindriyasaṃvarau`.

**Force of `tu`**

`Tu`, “however,” marks a categorical turn. Mind-restraint and faculty-restraint
are called restraints, but the Bhāṣya says that they are not by nature
avijñapti-discipline. Their nature is cognitive: recollection together with
clear comprehension.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> In the preliminary concentration the two are called abandonment
> restraints—those two born in the uninterrupted paths. Mind-restraint and
> faculty-restraint, however, are [each] the two: clear comprehension and
> recollection.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the preliminary concentration, meditation-born and uncontaminated
> restraint, when born in the nine uninterrupted paths, are called
> abandonment restraints. Mind-restraint and faculty-restraint, however, each
> have a twofold nature: recollection and clear comprehension.

“Meditation-born and uncontaminated,” “nine,” and “each have a twofold nature”
are Bhāṣya-supplied resolutions of the kārikā’s compressed references.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Meditation-born and uncontaminated restraint acquire the further name
> “abandonment restraint” only when, in the preliminary concentration, they
> function within the nine uninterrupted paths to abandon misconduct and the
> defilements that generate it. Mind-restraint and restraint of the faculties
> belong to a different order: neither is avijñapti-discipline; each consists
> in the joint operation of mindfulness and clear comprehension.

This rendering incorporates the Bhāṣya’s account of the abandoning function
and its explicit categorical distinction. It does not insert those
determinations into the bare words of the kārikā.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`prahāṇasaṃvara`**

Abandonment restraint names meditation-born and uncontaminated restraint only
under a specific functional determination:

```text
within the nine uninterrupted paths of the anāgamya
    restraint abandons bad conduct
    and abandons the defilements producing bad conduct
```

The name therefore expresses what the restraint accomplishes in that Path
relation.

**`dauḥśīlya`**

`Dauḥśīlya` is bad conduct or breach of discipline. The Bhāṣya does not stop at
the inhibition of its outward expression. Abandonment restraint reaches both:

```text
the misconduct
the kleśas that generate it
```

This is the passage’s decisive deepening of discipline.

**`tatsamutthāpaka kleśa`**

The compound expression means the defilements that give rise to that bad
conduct. `Samutthāpaka` identifies a productive condition, not merely a state
that happens to accompany the deed.

**`catuṣkoṭika`**

The Bhāṣya applies a four-cornered analysis to prevent the identity of
meditation restraint and abandonment restraint:

1. meditation restraint but not abandonment restraint;
2. abandonment restraint but not contaminated meditation restraint;
3. both;
4. neither.

An analogous analysis applies when uncontaminated restraint is taken as the
starting predicate. The exact membership varies with contamination-status,
path, and moment.

**`manaḥsaṃvara`**

Mind-restraint regulates the mental field. Its nature is not avijñapti but the
joint presence of mindfulness and clear comprehension.

**`indriyasaṃvara`**

Faculty-restraint governs engagement through the sensory faculties. It too is
constituted by both mindfulness and clear comprehension; it is not assigned to
mindfulness alone.

**`avijñaptiśīla`**

The Bhāṣya’s denial is exact:

```text
mind-restraint and faculty-restraint
    are not by nature avijñapti-discipline
```

The shared word `saṃvara` therefore crosses a categorical boundary. It does
not establish one identical ontological nature in everything called
“restraint.”

## 8. Logical Determination

**A functional predicate is not a new substance**

```text
meditation-born restraint or uncontaminated restraint
    +
occurrence in the relevant uninterrupted path
    +
function of abandoning misconduct and its generating defilements
    =
valid designation as prahāṇasaṃvara
```

The restraint receives a new predicate because of its function in a
determinate relation. The new predicate neither erases its prior determination
nor produces a numerically separate restraint.

**The classes cross-cut**

```text
dhyānasaṃvara ≠ prahāṇasaṃvara
anāsravasaṃvara ≠ prahāṇasaṃvara
```

But instances can overlap. The `catuṣkoṭika` represents the extension of the
predicates rather than reducing one definition to the other.

**One term, different natures**

```text
saṃvara as avijñapti restraint
    prātimokṣa restraint
    meditation-born restraint
    uncontaminated restraint

saṃvara as cognitive restraint
    mind-restraint
    faculty-restraint
    = smṛti + saṃprajñāna
```

Identity of designation does not entail identity of dharma-nature. Conversely,
one restraint can acquire multiple valid designations under different
relations.

**Both factors belong to both restraints**

```text
not:
    clear comprehension → mind-restraint only
    mindfulness         → faculty-restraint only

but:
    mind-restraint       → mindfulness + clear comprehension
    faculty-restraint    → mindfulness + clear comprehension
```

Mindfulness preserves the governing determination; clear comprehension
discriminates the present activity and situation. Either without the other
would leave the restraint incomplete.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Yama reaches the producer of misconduct**

The profound advance in VAK 4.18 is that restraint is no longer considered
only as the inhibition of an outward extreme. Abandonment restraint removes
both `dauḥśīlya` and the kleśas that produce it. Discipline becomes genuinely
genetic: it addresses the principle of the deed’s arising.

At the controlled point of contact with Yoga, this is the deeper truth of
`yama`. A prohibition that merely suppresses an already completed impulse is
external regulation. Discipline becomes inwardly effective when it transforms
the generative pipeline through which the impulse becomes action.

```text
kleśa
    → produces misconduct

abandonment restraint
    → abandons misconduct
    → abandons its productive kleśa
```

This comparison does not identify Yoga’s categories with the nine
uninterrupted paths. It identifies the shared practical demand that restraint
reach the source of action rather than merely police its result.

**The Middle discriminates its own functions**

The previous verses showed the Middle projecting manifest and non-manifest
Aspects and sustaining their practical unity. Here that Middle performs a
further discrimination. The same restraint can be determined by what it is,
where it occurs, and what it does. None of these predicates may simply replace
the others.

The `catuṣkoṭika` is therefore not scholastic excess. It protects the movement
of the Concept from a premature identity:

```text
same instance
    may bear intersecting predicates

same predicate
    may apply across distinct natures
```

This is how the Ideal Copula remains productive rather than becoming a static
equation. It joins a determination to its valid predicate through the relation
that licenses the predication. “This restraint is abandonment restraint” means
that it functions as abandonment here—not that the two expressions are
universally interchangeable.

**Mindfulness and clear comprehension**

The second half makes the practical Middle explicitly cognitive. Mindfulness
retains the rule; clear comprehension discriminates the present field. Their
unity makes restraint operative:

```text
smṛti
    persistence of the practical determination

saṃprajñāna
    situated discriminative operation
    grounded in prajñā

clear comprehension
    resulting lucidity concerning the present situation

their unity
    regulated mind and regulated faculty-engagement
```

This is not the avijñapti mode of discipline. The Bhāṣya explicitly says so.
It is the lucid, presently operative discipline by which the field is
continually known and governed. Thus the wheel turns through both instituted
continuity and conscious discrimination, without collapsing either into the
other.

This is an Organon reconstruction of the relations. Vasubandhu’s explicit
teaching is the conditional designation of abandonment restraint, the
cross-cutting classifications, and the dual cognitive nature of both
mind-restraint and faculty-restraint.
