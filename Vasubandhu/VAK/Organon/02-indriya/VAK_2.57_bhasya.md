# VAK_2.57 Bhāṣya — Maturation, Homogeneous Outflow, and Disconnection

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vipāko 'vyākṛto dharmaḥ sattvākhyo vyākṛtodbhavaḥ /
> niḥṣyando hetusadṛśo visaṃyogaḥ kṣayo dhiyā // 2.57 //

> Maturation is an indeterminate dharma associated with a sentient
> being, arising from what is ethically determinate. Homogeneous
> outflow resembles its cause. Disconnection is cessation through wisdom.

The unit at 95.09–96.02 defines three results. The definitions of
efficacy and predominance continue in VAK 2.58.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

What is this maturation-result, and what are the others through
predominant result? “Maturation is an indeterminate dharma.” A
maturation is an unobscured-indeterminate dharma. But this could
also include something not associated with a sentient being;
therefore it says, “associated with a sentient being.” This could
also include something produced through increase or a homogeneous
outflow; therefore it says, “arising from what is determinate.”
Wholesome and unwholesome dharmas are determinate because they
are specified with respect to maturation. What arises from them
at a later time, neither simultaneously nor immediately afterward,
is maturation. This is the definition of maturation.

Why is something produced by karma but not associated with a
sentient being not maturation? Because it is shared: someone else
can also make use of it in the same way. Maturation, however, is
unshared; one person does not experience the maturation of karma
performed by another. Why, then, does one experience a predominant
result? Because it arises from common karma.

“Homogeneous outflow resembles its cause.” A dharma resembling its
cause is a homogeneous-outflow result, as with the homogeneous and
pervasive causes. If the pervasive cause also has a similar result,
why is it not accepted simply as a homogeneous cause? Because its
similarity is in level and afflicted status, not in specific type.
Where there is also similarity in specific type, it is indeed
accepted as a homogeneous cause.

For this reason a fourfold analysis is made: if something is a
homogeneous cause of something else, is it also its pervasive cause?
The first case is a homogeneous cause that is not pervasive. The
second is a pervasive cause belonging to another class. The third
is a pervasive cause belonging to the same class. The fourth is
what remains when these cases are excluded.

“Disconnection is cessation through wisdom.” *Kṣaya* means cessation;
*dhī* means wisdom. Thus cessation through discrimination is said
to be the result of disconnection.

## 4. The Maturation Definition Is Cumulative

Each qualification narrows the preceding description. Ethical
indeterminacy alone would include non-sentient things. Association
with a sentient being would still include products of increase
and homogeneous outflows. The ethically determinate source and
temporal qualification complete the present definition.

The cause is wholesome or unwholesome; the maturation-result is
unobscured-indeterminate. Their ethical predicates differ. The
result's indeterminacy does not erase the ethical determination
of its cause, and being indeterminate does not make every such
dharma a maturation-result. *Aupacayika*, produced through increase,
can include growth through nourishment; the source uses the more
general category here.

## 5. Shared Results and Individual Maturation

The distinction concerns a particular causal attribution. Something
can be karmically produced yet fall under predominant result rather
than maturation in the strict sense. The account explains shared
availability through common karma, while reserving maturation for
the relevant individual's karmic result.

This does not say that another person's actions cannot affect one's
experience. It distinguishes experiencing the maturation of another's
karma from participating in a shared result. Nor does the short reply
explain every mechanism or boundary of common karma; those details
should not be supplied by inference here.

## 6. Similarity and the Four Cases

The pervasive cause can resemble its result in level and afflicted
status while crossing the classes distinguished by abandonment.
Similarity in the relevant class permits it also to be a homogeneous
cause. The four cases classify a cause in relation to a particular
result, not as a thing with one immutable label independent of its
causal relation.

| Case | Homogeneous cause | Pervasive cause |
|---|---|---|
| Non-pervasive homogeneous cause | Yes | No |
| Pervasive cause of another class | No | Yes |
| Pervasive cause of the same class | Yes | Yes |
| Remaining relations | No | No |

“Another class” and “the same class” compare cause with result.
They do not change the same-level restriction of pervasive causality.
*Prakāra*, specific type, is locally clarified by *nikāya*, class;
it need not mean exact identity of every feature or the very same
named affliction.

## 7. Disconnection and Wisdom

The final explanation is short: *kṣaya* is cessation, *dhī* is
*prajñā*, and the resulting definition is cessation through
discrimination. “Cessation” is preferable here to an unqualified
“destruction,” which could suggest that a newly produced event
of destruction exhausts the meaning.

VAK 2.55 has already distinguished producing an acquisition from
attaining disconnection and recorded competing accounts of the
unconditioned. This brief gloss does not settle that dispute or
replace it with a single account of destroying a causal capacity.
The efficacy of wisdom remains explicit without assimilating this
result to ordinary maturation.

## 8. Textual and Translation Decisions

The running Sanskrit is preserved apart from source labels and
surrounding spacing. It includes *utarakālaṃ*, *tṛtīyaikanaikāyikaḥ*,
*caturthyatānākārān*, and *tenā*. The translation follows their
contextually clear temporal, enumerative, and explanatory senses;
no corrected Sanskrit edition is asserted.

*Sattvākhya* is rendered “associated with a sentient being,” retaining
the distinction from *asattvākhya*. It is not translated as an
assertion of a permanent bearer. *Vyākṛta* is ethically determinate
here, specifically wholesome or unwholesome, while *avyākṛta* in
the verse is narrowed to unobscured-indeterminate by the prose.

The research file preserves this unit's main translation reasonably
fully. Its subsequent explanation that wisdom destroys the causal
capacity of bondage goes beyond the local gloss and draws on one
side of the earlier cessation debate. That account is not imported
into the continuous translation here. The question about all results
opens this unit, but the remaining definitions belong to VAK 2.58.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, the commentary teaches how a definition
becomes adequate through counterexamples. A neutral result, a result
within a living continuum, and a karmically produced result are each
insufficient descriptions until their relations are distinguished.
The similarity argument performs the same work: resemblance must
be specified by the respect in which it holds.

This gives a concrete discipline to our inquiry into learning.
Knowing the result-name is a beginning; understanding requires
being able to distinguish its application from neighboring cases.
Disconnection then requires a further distinction, since knowing
through wisdom is related to a result whose account cannot simply
be borrowed from produced maturation.
