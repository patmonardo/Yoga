# VAK_2.68

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> पञ्चभ्योऽनिवृतं तस्मात्सप्त चित्तान्यनन्तरम् ।
> रूपे दशैकं च शुभान्नवभ्यस्तदनन्तरम् ॥ २.६८ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> pañcabhyo 'nivṛtaṃ tasmāt sapta cittāny anantaram /
> rūpe daśaikaṃ ca śubhān navabhyas tad anantaram // 2.68 //

The display applies external sandhi in *śubhān navabhyaḥ* and separates
*tad anantaram* according to its syntax. The commentary at 104.09–21
completes desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind and specifies
form-realm wholesome mind. The local *anivṛta* spelling is retained.

The final *tat* is the subject that arises from nine. It is not the
first member of a compound meaning “immediately after that.” The
Bhāṣya expressly makes form-realm wholesome mind the resulting mind.

## 4. Grammar

The opening inherits *kāme*, “in the desire realm,” from the previous
verse. *Anivṛtam* is expanded by the commentary to unobscured-indeterminate
mind. The first two clauses alternate direction:

```text
five kinds → desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind
that mind → seven kinds
```

*Rūpe* then changes the domain of the focal mind. The remaining clauses
likewise distinguish successor from predecessor counts:

```text
form-realm wholesome mind → eleven kinds
nine kinds → that form-realm wholesome mind
```

Arising is understood throughout. *Anantaram* explicitly states immediate
succession. The realm locatives identify the focal minds; they do not
restrict every kind in the corresponding lists to that realm.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The unobscured [mind arises] from five; immediately after it,
> seven minds. In the form realm, ten and one [arise] from wholesome
> [mind]; that [wholesome mind arises] immediately after nine.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind can arise immediately
> after five kinds of mind and can be followed immediately by seven.
> Form-realm wholesome mind can be followed immediately by eleven
> kinds and can arise immediately after nine.

The counts refer to the twelve kinds of 2.66, with specific transition
contexts given by the commentary. They are not unrestricted choices
available after every individual occurrence of a kind.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A mind's classification does not by itself specify its succession.
> Transformation-mind explains one connection between neutral desire-realm
> mind and wholesome form-realm mind; rebirth explains different
> cross-realm connections. The incoming and outgoing lists must each
> be established, rather than inferred as converses of one another.

This interpretation preserves the distinction between the kinds counted
and the functions permitting particular transitions.

| pratisaṃdhikāla | time of rebirth-linking | context of the higher-realm afflicted successors |

“Transformation-mind” is a specific technical function within the neutral
kind; it does not license every ordinary constructive thought to make
the same cross-realm transition. Higher-realm “afflicted” retains its
obscured-indeterminate status, since unwholesome mind is absent there.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind.**

| Other kind or group | Can precede it | Can follow it |
|---|---:|---:|
| Four desire-realm kinds | 4 | 4 |
| Form-realm wholesome | 1 | 1 |
| Form-realm obscured-indeterminate | 0 | 1 |
| Formless obscured-indeterminate | 0 | 1 |
| Total | 5 | 7 |

The form-wholesome predecessor leads to transformation-mind. In the
reverse direction, form-wholesome mind follows transformation-mind.
The form and formless afflicted successors are specified at rebirth-linking;
the formless case is expressly restricted by *eva*, “only,” to that context.
The two additional outgoing kinds do not become incoming kinds merely
because they share an affliction label.

**Form-realm wholesome mind.**

| Other kind or group | Can precede it | Can follow it |
|---|---:|---:|
| Desire-realm wholesome | 1 | 1 |
| Desire-realm unwholesome | 0 | 1 |
| Desire-realm obscured-indeterminate | 0 | 1 |
| Desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate | 1 | 1 |
| Three form-realm kinds | 3 | 3 |
| Formless wholesome | 1 | 1 |
| Formless obscured-indeterminate | 1 | 1 |
| Formless unobscured-indeterminate | 0 | 0 |
| Trainee and beyond-training | 2 | 2 |
| Total | 9 | 11 |

The source gives these lists by exclusions: eleven means all twelve
except formless unobscured-indeterminate; nine additionally excludes
the two afflicted desire-realm kinds. The local passage does not give
a separate explanation for every included connection. Reasons should
not be invented merely to complete the table.

The form-wholesome/desire-neutral pair agrees with both directions
stated in the opening half. The form-wholesome/desire-wholesome pair
also agrees with the preceding study. Such agreement checks direction;
it does not make all remaining transitions reciprocal.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya control.** The first-pass translation's final “immediately
after that, from nine” leaves the subject unclear. Separating *tat*
as the wholesome mind restores the incoming relation. The Bhāṣya also
specifies the exact five, seven, eleven, and nine members, which the
first-pass kārikā analysis left as counts alone.

The running transcription has *avyākṛṭāt*, *yatkukśalam*, and
*ārupyāvacaram* among its irregular forms. The recurrent classifications
make their contextual meanings clear; no corrected manuscript text is
asserted. *Nirmāṇacitta* is nominative in the first explanation: it
identifies the resulting neutral mind, not an extra intervening mind
inserted between the two immediately successive occurrences.

**Organon interpretation.** This passage distinguishes knowing a type
from knowing its permitted relations. The broad neutral kind contains
a functionally specific transformation case. A model that records only
realm and ethical classification would lose what explains the selected
cross-realm link.

For our study, the counts therefore remain answers to relational
questions: which can precede, which can follow, and in what context?
Their articulation advances the inquiry into mediation without reducing
the source to an unqualified transition diagram or identifying the
classification with a modern computational theory.

**Review status.** Provisional polished study checked against the research
kārikā, research Bhāṣya, and running Sanskrit at 104.09–21. The enumerated
members reproduce all four counts and agree with the relevant relations
already studied. Original witnesses remain unchanged. VAK 2.69 continues
with form-realm obscured and unobscured mind and the formless-realm account.
