# VAK_2.71 Bhāṣya — Beginning the Refinement into Twenty

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tasmāc catvāri cittāni dvādaśaitāni viṃśatiḥ /
> prāyogikopapattyāptaṃ śubhaṃ bhittvā triṣu dvidhā // 2.71 //

> From that [beyond-training mind arise] four minds. These twelve
> are made twenty, having divided wholesome mind in the three realms
> into two: application-produced and obtained through birth …

The opening clause and its commentary were translated in 2.70_bhasya,
through the explicit closure of the twelve-kind succession account
at 105.26. This study begins at 105.27 and translates the first step
of the new classification through 106.04. The division continues into
2.72; the present short paragraph does not by itself complete the
twentyfold count.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> punaḥ kriyante
> dvādaśaitāni viṃśatiḥ /
> kathaṃ kṛtvā /
> prāyogikopapattyāptaṃ śubhaṃ bhittvā triṣu dvidhā // 2.71 //
> triṣu dhātuṣu kuśalaṃ cittaṃ dvidhā bhidyate /
> prāyogikaṃ copapattilābhikaṃ ca

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

These twelve are made twenty in turn. How? “By dividing wholesome
mind in the three [realms] into two: application-produced and obtained
through birth.” Wholesome mind in the three realms is divided into
two: application-produced and birth-acquired.

The division continues in the next passage. This continuation notice
is editorial; it makes the incomplete scope of the current explanation
explicit without adding further subdivisions to its translation.

## 4. The First Subdivision

| Coarser kind | Refined kinds |
|---|---|
| Desire-realm wholesome | Application-produced; birth-acquired |
| Form-realm wholesome | Application-produced; birth-acquired |
| Formless-realm wholesome | Application-produced; birth-acquired |

The Bhāṣya's *prāyogikaṃ ca upapattilābhikaṃ ca* makes the two
alternatives explicit. The compact compound in the kārikā does not
describe a single kind jointly produced through practice and birth.
The split applies to the wholesome kind in each realm. It does not
create two trainee kinds or two beyond-training kinds for each realm.

Birth-acquired wholesome mind remains wholesome. It must not be
identified with maturation-born mind, which belongs to the
unobscured-indeterminate category. The two designations answer
different questions and occupy different places in the refined
classification.

## 5. Why This Step Gives Fifteen, Not Twenty

Replacing three wholesome kinds with six adds three to the original
twelve: **12 − 3 + 6 = 15**. The research Bhāṣya's claim that this
single subdivision yields twenty is therefore incomplete. The next
verse supplies the further divisions needed to reach the announced
total.

| Operation | Increase | Running total |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original classification | — | 12 |
| Three wholesome kinds become six: 2.71 | 3 | 15 |
| Desire unobscured-indeterminate becomes four: 2.72 | 3 | 18 |
| Form unobscured-indeterminate becomes three: 2.72 | 2 | 20 |

The last two rows are a forward reference to 106.05–12, not additional
content of the paragraph translated here. The next commentary expressly
carries forward *bhittvā*, “having divided,” and confirms twenty only
after those further divisions. Formless unobscured-indeterminate and
the two uncontaminated kinds remain undivided in this scheme.

## 6. Translation and Source Decisions

The opening prose *punaḥ kriyante* joins the verse's *dvādaśaitāni
viṃśatiḥ*: “these twelve are made twenty in turn.” The English keeps
the announced total and then the question explaining its construction.
It does not silently replace twenty with fifteen in the source claim;
fifteen is the intermediate count established in the analysis.

*Prāyogika* is rendered “application-produced,” referring to application
or practice. *Upapattyāpta* in the verse and *upapattilābhika* in
the prose are rendered “obtained through birth” and “birth-acquired.”
The short local explanation states the distinction without specifying
all its later practical or transition consequences.

The Sanskrit retains the witness's wording and its lack of terminal
punctuation after *ca*. The full verse is displayed in the anchor,
while the continuous source begins only with the new refinement
passage. The opening beyond-training successor clause is neither
omitted from the edition nor translated twice as though it introduced
this subdivision.

## 7. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, refinement must account for the actual
distinctions it introduces. Naming a new feature does not establish
the size or content of the resulting classification. Here the mode
of obtaining wholesome mind explains three additional kinds; the
remaining increase requires another division.

This also limits what can be transferred from the twelve-kind account.
A coarser kind's permitted successors do not automatically become
permitted successors of every refined member. The extended commentary
following 2.72 will specify the finer relations. The present paragraph
establishes the distinction that makes that further inquiry possible.
