# VAK_2.66 Bhāṣya — Twelve Kinds of Mind

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kuśalākuśalaṃ kāme nivṛtānivṛtaṃ manaḥ /
> rūpārūpyeṣv akuśalād anyatrānāsravaṃ dvidhā // 2.66 //

> In the desire realm, mind is wholesome, unwholesome, obscured,
> and unobscured. In the form and formless realms, the same applies
> except for the unwholesome. Uncontaminated mind is twofold.

The natural unit at 103.09–18 introduces the classification needed
for the ensuing succession account. The verse's obscured and unobscured
members are specified as indeterminate in the commentary. The actual
successor counts begin at 103.19 with 2.67. The final connective
*tatra*, “among these,” is retained below as the transition.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Mind and mental factors have been stated generally to be the immediately
antecedent condition, but the particular rule has not been stated:
which mind arises immediately after which? That must now be explained.
First, in summary, there are twelve kinds of mind. Why is this said?
“In the desire realm, mind is wholesome, unwholesome, obscured, and
unobscured.” In the desire realm there are four kinds of mind:
wholesome, unwholesome, obscured-indeterminate, and unobscured-indeterminate.
“In the form and formless realms, except for the unwholesome.” In
the form realm there is no unwholesome mind; there are three kinds.
The same applies in the formless realm. These constitute ten contaminated
kinds of mind. “Uncontaminated mind is twofold”: trainee and beyond-training.
Thus there are twelve kinds of mind. Among these—

## 4. The Classification and Its Purpose

| Group | Kinds distinguished | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Desire realm | Wholesome, unwholesome, obscured-indeterminate, unobscured-indeterminate | 4 |
| Form realm | Wholesome, obscured-indeterminate, unobscured-indeterminate | 3 |
| Formless realm | Wholesome, obscured-indeterminate, unobscured-indeterminate | 3 |
| Uncontaminated | Trainee, beyond-training | 2 |

The total is **4 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 12**. The Bhāṣya groups the first
ten as contaminated and adds the two uncontaminated kinds. The latter
do not constitute a fourth realm.

The opening question fixes the purpose of the enumeration. The account
already identifies mind and mental factors as immediately antecedent
condition; it now needs to determine the particular successions among
kinds of mind. This paragraph prepares that determination. It does
not yet license every transition between the twelve kinds, or state
which particular transitions are excluded.

## 5. Distinctions That Must Remain Visible

Wholesome mind occurs within the ten contaminated kinds. Wholesomeness
and freedom from contamination therefore cannot be treated as the
same determination. Likewise, absence of unwholesome mind in the
higher realms does not mean absence of affliction: obscured-indeterminate
mind remains in each.

The two indeterminate kinds are both ethically indeterminate in this
classification; their difference concerns obscuration. They should not
be translated as another wholesome/unwholesome pair. The Bhāṣya's
explicit *avyākṛta* supplies what the verse abbreviates.

Trainee and beyond-training here distinguish uncontaminated kinds of
mind. They do not imply that every mind occurring in a trainee or
arhat is therefore the corresponding uncontaminated mind. The class
of a cognitive occurrence and the status of its bearer remain distinct.
The preceding exclusion of the arhat's final mental complex from
immediately antecedent conditioning also remains in force; the opening
recapitulation does not revoke it.

## 6. Textual and Translation Decisions

The continuous Sanskrit retains *utpaktir*, the anomalous internal
page label having been removed along with the other source labels,
and *sānusravāṇi*. The last is translated “contaminated,” following
its contrast with *anāsrava*. It is not presented as a newly established
technical category distinct from the usual *sāsrava*.

The local forms *nivṛta* and *anivṛta* are retained in the Sanskrit
and understood contextually as obscured and unobscured. The study
does not silently substitute another spelling or construe them as
“ceased” and “unceasing.” The anchor applies external sandhi in
*anyatrānāsravam*; the running Sanskrit retains the separate quoted
clauses of the commentary.

*Kimartham ity āha* is rendered “Why is this said?” It introduces
the explanation of the stated twelvefold count. “Why twelve?” in
the research translation gives its contextual force but is a freer
rendering. *Tatra* at the end introduces the next account, and is
translated as an unfinished connective rather than supplied with
succession details from 2.67.

The research Bhāṣya correctly identifies trainee and beyond-training
as the two uncontaminated kinds. These are explicit in the Sanskrit,
not conjectural identifications. No rival position or independent
objector is introduced in this short expository unit.

## 7. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, the passage makes classification answerable
to the question it prepares us to ask. Distinguishing the kinds is
a beginning of the succession analysis; membership alone does not
explain how one kind can follow another. The text itself marks this
difference between the general condition and the particular rule
still to be stated.

This is a precise place to retain our distinction between beginning
mediation with a genus and exhausting mediation in genus membership.
The twelve kinds make the terms available for inquiry; the following
commentary must establish their relations. Treating the enumeration
as an already complete account of a cognitive continuum would omit
that further work.
