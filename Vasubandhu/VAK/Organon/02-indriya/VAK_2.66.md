# VAK_2.66

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> कुशलाकुशलं कामे निवृतानिवृतं मनः ।
> रूपारूप्येष्वकुशलादन्यत्रानास्रवं द्विधा ॥ २.६६ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> kuśalākuśalaṃ kāme nivṛtānivṛtaṃ manaḥ /
> rūpārūpyeṣv akuśalād anyatrānāsravaṃ dvidhā // 2.66 //

The transmitted *nivṛta/anivṛta* forms are retained; here they denote
obscured and unobscured, conventionally *nivṛta/ anivṛta-avyākṛta*
in the local transcription. Their contextual sense must not be inferred
as “ceased” and “unceasing.” External sandhi joins *anyatra anāsravam*.

The commentary at 103.09–18 introduces twelve kinds of mind to answer
which mind can arise immediately after which. The actual succession
counts begin with the next verse.


| Form | Morphology | Local sense |
|---|---|---|
| kuśalākuśalam | nominative neuter singular collective compound | wholesome and unwholesome [mind] |
| kāme | locative singular | in the desire realm |
| nivṛtānivṛtam | nominative neuter singular collective compound | obscured and unobscured [indeterminate mind] |
| manaḥ | nominative neuter singular | mind |
| rūpārūpyeṣu | locative plural compound | in the form and formless domains |
| akuśalāt | ablative neuter singular | apart from the unwholesome |

| anāsravam | nominative neuter singular adjective | uncontaminated [mind] |
| dvidhā | numerical adverb | in two ways / twofold |

The Bhāṣya supplies *avyākṛta*, indeterminate, for both obscured and
unobscured members. It also supplies *śaikṣa* and *aśaikṣa* as the two
uncontaminated divisions. Neither expansion is explicit in the verse.

## 4. Grammar

*Manaḥ* is the singular collective subject of the first classification.
The paired expressions yield four alternatives, not a mind simultaneously
wholesome, unwholesome, obscured, and unobscured.

The second clause carries forward that classification under a restriction:
in the form and formless realms, *akuśalāt anyatra*, “except the
unwholesome.” The Bhāṣya explicitly gives three minds in each realm.
It does not say that the higher realms lack all affliction.

The final clause supplies a new subject, *anāsravaṃ [manaḥ]*, with
*dvidhā* as predicate. The two uncontaminated kinds are additional to
the ten contaminated kinds. They are not a fourth realm alongside
kāma, rūpa, and ārūpya.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In the desire realm, mind is wholesome and unwholesome, obscured
> and unobscured. In the form and formless realms, [the same applies]
> except for the unwholesome. Uncontaminated [mind] is twofold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Desire-realm mind has four kinds: wholesome, unwholesome,
> obscured-indeterminate, and unobscured-indeterminate. Form-realm
> and formless-realm mind each have three, excluding the unwholesome.
> These ten are contaminated. Uncontaminated mind has two kinds:
> trainee and beyond-training. Together these constitute twelve
> kinds of mind.

The commentary expressly introduces this enumeration to prepare the
rules of immediate succession. The twelve are kinds, not twelve
simultaneously occurring minds.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> To determine which cognition can follow which, the account first
> distinguishes their kinds. Realm affiliation, ethical determination,
> obscuration, and training status contribute different distinctions.
> Higher-realm affiliation does not itself establish freedom from
> affliction; uncontaminated mind is separately distinguished by
> its relation to training.

This study formulation interprets the classification's stated purpose.
It does not yet infer any particular allowed or forbidden transition.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Term | Controlled rendering | Determination |
|---|---|---|
| manas / citta | mind | *manas* in the verse; *citta* in the explanatory enumeration |
| kuśala | wholesome | occurs among the contaminated realm-bound kinds as well |
| akuśala | unwholesome | present in the desire-realm list, absent from the higher two |
| nivṛtāvyākṛta | obscured-indeterminate | afflicted yet ethically indeterminate |
| anivṛtāvyākṛta | unobscured-indeterminate | distinct from both wholesome and unwholesome |
| anāsrava | uncontaminated | divided here into trainee and beyond-training |
| śaikṣa | trainee / belonging to training | first uncontaminated division |
| aśaikṣa | beyond-training | second uncontaminated division |
| dvādaśa cittāni | twelve kinds of mind | commentary's total for the succession inquiry |

The running source calls the first ten *sānusravāṇi*, an irregular
form interpreted contextually as “with influxes,” or contaminated.
Wholesomeness and uncontaminated status must remain distinct: the
realm-bound wholesome kinds are included among those ten.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Domain | Wholesome | Unwholesome | Obscured-indeterminate | Unobscured-indeterminate | Count |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Desire realm | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | 4 |
| Form realm | Yes | No | Yes | Yes | 3 |
| Formless realm | Yes | No | Yes | Yes | 3 |

| Uncontaminated division | Count |
|---|---:|
| Trainee | 1 |
| Beyond-training | 1 |

The total is 4 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 12. “No unwholesome mind” in the higher
realms does not imply “no obscured mind.” Likewise, “unobscured” does
not make an indeterminate mind a wholesome mind.

The trainee and beyond-training labels classify the uncontaminated
minds under discussion. They should not be read as saying that every
mind occurring in a trainee or an arhat is necessarily uncontaminated.
The person's path status and the present mind's classification are
not interchangeable.

The transition inquiry requires two stages:

```text
Identify the twelve kinds.
Then determine which kinds can immediately follow which.
```

Membership in the list alone does not license a transition. The verse
supplies the terms of the later relation, not its complete rules.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya control.** The opening states that mind and mental factors
have already been described generally as immediately antecedent
condition, but the particular succession rule remains to be explained.
The present twelvefold enumeration serves that question. It is not
an unrelated taxonomy appended to the material analysis.

The first-pass study left the twofold uncontaminated division as
“likely” trainee/beyond-training. The Bhāṣya explicitly states
*śaikṣam aśaikṣaṃ ca*, removing that uncertainty. It also supplies
indeterminacy for both obscured and unobscured kinds, preventing a
false opposition between four independent ethical qualities.

The obscuration terms retain the local witness's spelling. Their
intended sense is established by the fourfold prose enumeration.
The running source's page label 101.13
within the page-103 passage is anomalous and does not change the
order. Independent collation would be needed to finalize orthography.

**Organon interpretation.** This is a concrete instance of classification
preparing mediation: the kinds are distinguished so their succession
can be investigated. Their names do not themselves explain the passage
from one to another. Learning the system requires holding the domains
and statuses apart while discovering the permitted relations among them.

A model of cognitive succession should therefore preserve these twelve
kinds without treating the count as a completed theory of cognition.
The transition rules and their grounds are the next task.
