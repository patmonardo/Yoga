# VAK_2.69 Bhāṣya — A Repeated Count with Different Members

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> aṣṭābhyo nivṛtaṃ tasmāt ṣaṭ tribhyo 'nivṛtaṃ punaḥ /
> tasmāt ṣaḍ evam ārūpye tasya nītiḥ śubhāt punaḥ // 2.69 //

> The obscured [mind arises] from eight; from it, six. The unobscured,
> in turn, arises from three; from it, six. Its rule is likewise
> in the formless realm. From wholesome mind, however …

The unit at 104.22–105.07 completes form-realm obscured and unobscured
mind and gives the corresponding formless unobscured profile. The
last phrase starts a clause completed by “nine minds” in 2.70.
It is retained as an unfinished transition, not treated as part of
the preceding three/six rule.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“The obscured [mind arises] from eight.” Form-realm obscured-indeterminate
mind arises from eight kinds, setting aside the two afflicted
desire-realm kinds and trainee and beyond-training mind. “From it,
six.” Immediately after form-realm obscured-indeterminate mind arise
six kinds: the three of its own level and the desire-realm kinds
apart from unobscured-indeterminate mind.

“The unobscured, in turn, [arises] from three.” Form-realm
unobscured-indeterminate mind arises only from the three of its own
level. “From it, six”: the three of its own level, the two afflicted
desire-realm kinds, and formless-realm [afflicted mind]. As stated
for unobscured-indeterminate mind in the form realm, “its rule is
likewise in the formless realm.” There too, unobscured-indeterminate
mind arises only from the three kinds of its own level. From it
likewise arise six: the three of its own level and the afflicted
kinds belonging to the lower realms. “From wholesome mind, however …”

## 4. The Enumerated Sets

| Focal kind | Predecessors | Successors |
|---|---|---|
| Form obscured-indeterminate | Desire wholesome and unobscured; all three form; all three formless: **8** | All three form; desire wholesome, unwholesome, and obscured: **6** |
| Form unobscured-indeterminate | All three form: **3** | All three form; desire unwholesome and obscured; formless obscured: **6** |
| Formless unobscured-indeterminate | All three formless: **3** | All three formless; desire unwholesome and obscured; form obscured: **6** |

The first eight exclude four members of the twelve-kind classification:
the two desire afflicted kinds and the two uncontaminated kinds.
Its six successors exclude desire unobscured rather than desire
wholesome. The research translation's bracketed reference to merely
“appropriate” desire kinds leaves an explicit list needlessly vague.

For form unobscured, the Sanskrit *kāmāvacare ca kliṣṭe* is dual:
two afflicted desire-realm kinds, not one generic afflicted mind.
The following *ārūpyāvacaraṃ ca* carries forward “afflicted,” giving
the remaining sixth member. In the formless unobscured list, the
lower-realm afflicted kinds likewise comprise two desire kinds and
one form kind. These expansions follow the twelvefold inventory;
they introduce no additional mind-types.

## 5. What Carries Over

The commentary expressly limits “its rule is likewise” to
unobscured-indeterminate mind. The shared pattern is three predecessors
from its own realm and six successors. The form-obscured eight/six
profile does not carry over with it.

Even within the repeated three/six pattern, the six members differ.
Form unobscured can be followed by formless afflicted mind; formless
unobscured can be followed by form afflicted mind. Each also has
its own three realm-specific kinds and the two desire afflicted
kinds as successors. Repetition of the numerical pattern does not
make the two sets identical.

The unit enumerates those sets without separately explaining the
occasion of every transition. Earlier accounts supply contexts such
as rebirth, but a continuous translation should not insert an
unstated contextual clause into each sentence here.

## 6. Translation and Source Decisions

The form-realm setting continues from 2.68. *Nivṛta* and *anivṛta*
are rendered with the commentary's full specifications, obscured-
and unobscured-indeterminate. *Svabhūmika* is “of its own level” in
the translation and identifies the respective realm's three kinds
in this enumeration.

The anchor displays sandhi in *ṣaḍ evam*. The word is *evam*, “thus,”
not *eva*, “only.” *Tasya*, “its,” refers to unobscured-indeterminate
mind, as the prose makes explicit. *Nīti* is rendered “rule”; here
its content is the particular parallel just stated. It need not
be expanded into a general doctrine of rule inheritance.

The continuous Sanskrit retains *aṣṭabhya* and the compressed
*ārūpyāvacaraṃ ca*. The bracketed “afflicted mind” in the English
makes the latter's inherited qualification visible. No independent
critical edition is claimed.

*Śubhāt punaḥ* remains incomplete at this verse boundary. Its
formless wholesome subject and the next verse's “nine minds” belong
to the following profile. That continuation is identified in the
anchor and here, while its full translation and analysis remain
for 2.70_bhasya.

## 7. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, the passage distinguishes sameness of
pattern from sameness of members. A count can recur across realms
while the actual relations change. Understanding “likewise” therefore
requires identifying what is preserved and what must be specified
afresh.

This continues the work beyond genus membership. The common
unobscured-indeterminate classification does not alone yield its
successions; the commentary supplies the realm-specific sets. A
formal representation could preserve that explicit correspondence,
but should not infer unrestricted transfer of every relation between
classes that share a feature.

The immediate learning task is concrete: distinguish the obscured
profile from the unobscured one, then retain the different members
within the repeated three/six account. The source provides these
determinations without requiring the larger computational claims
of the first-pass interpretation.
