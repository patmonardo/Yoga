# VAK_2.62

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चित्तचैत्ता अचरमा उत्पन्नाः समनन्तरः ।
> आलम्बनं सर्वधर्माः कारणाख्योऽधिपः स्मृतः ॥ २.६२ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> cittacaittā acaramā utpannāḥ samanantaraḥ /
> ālambanaṃ sarvadharmāḥ kāraṇākhyo 'dhipaḥ smṛtaḥ // 2.62 //

The verse defines the remaining three conditions after the cause-condition
of 2.61. Its commentary extends from 98.09 through 100.18 in the running
source. The next question concerns the stage at which conditions exercise
activity and belongs to 2.63.

The Bhāṣya explicitly parses *samanantara* through *sama* and *anantara*.
Its explanation concerns ordered mental succession; “equal” here must
not imply identical ethical status in successive minds.

## 4. Grammar

The three nominal constructions have different subjects and scopes.

**Immediately antecedent condition.** *Cittacaittāḥ* is qualified by
both *acaramāḥ* and *utpannāḥ*. The singular predicate
*samanantaraḥ [pratyayaḥ]* names the condition constituted by these
plural factors. The Bhāṣya identifies the excluded final factors as
those of an arhat; that identification is commentary, not an explicit
noun in the verse.

**Object-condition.** *Sarvadharmāḥ* supplies the scope; *ālambanam*
names the object relation under discussion. The commentary's *yathāyogam*,
“as appropriate,” distributes objects according to the relevant
consciousness. The clause is not a universal assertion that each
consciousness apprehends every dharma.

**Predominant condition.** *Kāraṇākhyaḥ [hetuḥ]* is what is traditionally
recognized as *adhipaḥ [pratyayaḥ]*. The Bhāṣya states the correspondence
explicitly: what is conditioning cause is predominant condition.
The singular *smṛtaḥ* agrees with this clause, not with *sarvadharmāḥ*.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Arisen mind and mental factors, except the final ones, constitute
> the immediately antecedent condition. All dharmas are object-condition.
> What is named the conditioning [cause] is held to be the predominant
> [condition].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Arisen mind and mental factors, excluding an arhat's final mind
> and factors, serve as the immediately antecedent condition. All
> dharmas can serve as object-condition according to the appropriate
> consciousness. The conditioning cause (*kāraṇa-hetu*) is the
> predominant condition (*adhipati-pratyaya*).

“Can serve” distinguishes object-character from being presently
apprehended, as the Bhāṣya's fuel analogy requires. It does not replace
the doctrinal scope “all dharmas” with a merely speculative possibility.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Cognitive succession, the object of cognition, and the wider
> non-obstructing relation are distinct determinations of conditioning.
> An earlier mental complex conditions a successor through its
> efficacy; an object retains its object-character even when not
> presently apprehended; predominance extends beyond direct agency.

This study interpretation draws on the commentary. It does not make
all conditioned events mental events or attribute an object-condition
to every material process.

“Object-condition” is preferred to “object-support” here so that
*ālambana* remains distinct from the explicitly discussed *āśraya*.
The table includes commentary vocabulary; these terms are not all
literal words of the verse.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Succession concerns the complex.** The Bhāṣya rejects the restriction
that mind conditions only mind, feeling only feeling, and so forth
under immediate antecedence. The whole complex is the condition of
the succeeding whole complex. Its objection invokes the first
uncontaminated mind: requiring a previously arisen member of the very
same kind would prevent that beginning.

Material dharmas and dissociated formations are excluded from serving
as this condition because their occurrence does not follow the relevant
unmixed pattern. This does not make them causeless. The seed-and-sprout
example expressly illustrates dependence without this mental condition.

**Immediacy is not simply elapsed time.** The emergence-mind after an
attainment without mind is immediately successive to the preceding mind
because no other mind intervenes. The commentary also distinguishes
technical succession from temporal contiguity through fourfold examples.
The definition must not be reduced to “the event one clock-moment earlier.”

**Final mind and support.** An arhat's final mind is excluded because
no further mind connects with it. The answer to the ensuing objection
distinguishes its character as mental support from functioning as an
immediately antecedent condition. Being classified as support is not
identical with actually giving rise to a successor under this condition.

**Objects are distributed.**

| Consciousness and associated factors | Appropriate object |
|---|---|
| Eye-consciousness | Visible form |
| Ear-consciousness | Sound |
| Nose-consciousness | Smell |
| Tongue-consciousness | Taste |
| Body-consciousness | Tangibles |
| Mental consciousness | All dharmas |

The fuel analogy separates object-character from present apprehension.
The account does not claim that a single cognition apprehends all objects
at once, or that all dharmas are presently cognized.

**Predominance is a relation.** All dharmas other than the conditioned
target itself fall within its broad predominant-conditioning account.
The target is conditioned; the conditioning domain is not restricted
to conditioned dharmas. The relation is irreflexive in this account.
The final question gives cases lacking any of the four conditions:
a dharma in relation to itself, and conditioned or unconditioned dharmas
in relation to an unconditioned target. It does not ask whether a single
pair must exemplify all four conditions.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya decisions that govern the reading.** The discussion of future
order records several positions. Explanations of the Buddha's future
knowledge through inference or a special sign are challenged, while
the Sautrāntika account appeals to the Buddha's inconceivable cognitive
scope. These proposals do not belong in the literal translation.
The separate answer concerning ordered path-moments appeals to dependent
arising: what has its arising bound to a given condition arises after it.
That answer does not establish a universal immediately-antecedent mental
condition for every causal sequence.

The research Bhāṣya makes Vasumitra's statement sound like a defense of
material form as immediately antecedent condition. In context, the
overlapping occurrence of material streams supplies a reason for its
exclusion. The exact punctuation is compressed, but its argumentative
setting should not be reversed.

The support passage at 100.09–10 is damaged: *utpannās tv āśrayasahitā
anutpannā hy atītā āśrayaviśliṣṭāḥ* does not securely support the research
translation's smooth division of future and past cases. The initial
answer affirms support-determination; “others” explicitly retain a
support relation for past events. A detailed reconstruction of the
intervening wording remains open pending collation.

The predominance discussion compares its range with object-conditioning:
co-arisen dharmas can condition one another predominantly without being
one another's objects in the relevant relation. It is not a prohibition
on their becoming objects of a later mental cognition.

**Organon interpretation.** The verse supplies distinct questions about
one cognitive occurrence: its mental antecedent, what it cognizes, and
its wider conditioning relations. Understanding one does not answer
the others. In our terms, learning must determine the relation, not
merely attach the general word “condition.”

The first-pass contrast between objective grounds and subjective
conditions is too strong as a textual claim. The cause-condition already
retains five causes, and predominant conditioning also ranges beyond
cognition. A comparison with Yoga or a proposed synthesis of ground
and condition requires its own argument; it should preserve these
specific distinctions rather than replace them.
