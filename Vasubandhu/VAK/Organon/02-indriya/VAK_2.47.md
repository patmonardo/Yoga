# VAK_2.47

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> नामकायादयः संज्ञावाक्याक्षरसमुक्तयः ।
>
> कामरूपाप्तसत्त्वाख्या निःष्यन्दाव्याकृतास्तथा ॥ २.४७ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nāmakāyādayaḥ saṃjñāvākyākṣarasamuktayaḥ /
>
> kāmarūpāptasattvākhyā niḥṣyandāvyākṛtās tathā // 2.47 //

## 3. Padaccheda

```text
nāmakāyādayaḥ                 → nāma-kāya-ādayaḥ
saṃjñāvākyākṣarasamuktayaḥ    → saṃjñā-vākya-akṣara-samuktayaḥ

```

| Form | Morphology | Lexical force here |
|---|---|---|
| nāma-kāya-ādayaḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | name-groups and the remaining linguistic groups |
| saṃjñā-vākya-akṣara-samuktayaḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | collections of designations, sentences, and phonetic elements |
| kāma-rūpa-āpta | nominative masculine plural compound | belonging to the desire and form realms |
| sattva-ākhyāḥ | nominative masculine plural compound | classified as associated with sentient beings |
| niḥṣyanda | nominative masculine plural in compound | homogeneous outflows |
| avyākṛtāḥ | nominative masculine plural adjective | indeterminate |
| tathā | indeclinable | likewise / also |

`Kāya` means a group or collection here, not an animate body. `Akṣara` is
determined by the Bhāṣya as a phonetic element such as `a` or `ā`; “letter” is
therefore misleading if it suggests that writing is primary.

## 4. Grammar

The first half defines the members denoted by `nāmakāyādayaḥ`:

```text
nāma-kāya-ādayaḥ
    = saṃjñā-vākya-akṣara-samuktayaḥ

name-groups and the rest
    = collections of designation, sentence, and phonetic element
```

The Bhāṣya unfolds the elliptical `ādayaḥ` as three parallel groups:

```text
nāma-kāya       name-group
vākya/pada-kāya sentence-group
akṣara-kāya     phonetic-element group
```

`Samukti` is explained from √`uc` in the sense of coming together and is
treated as equivalent to collection or aggregation. The second half predicates
four classifications of all three groups:

```text
kāma-rūpa-āpta     → realm classification
sattva-ākhyā       → association with a sentient continuum
niḥṣyanda          → result classification
avyākṛta           → ethical classification
```

The surface sandhi `avyākṛtās tathā` represents `avyākṛtāḥ tathā`.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> Name-groups and the rest are collections of designations, sentences, and phonetic elements. They belong to the desire and form realms, are classified as associated with sentient beings, are homogeneous outflows, and are likewise indeterminate.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The linguistic groups comprise collected designations, meaning-completing sentences, and phonetic elements. The Vaibhāṣikas classify them as formations belonging to the desire and form realms, possessed by the sentient being who signifies through them, produced as homogeneous outflows, and ethically indeterminate. Vasubandhu nevertheless argues that their semantic work is already performed by sound whose application has been conventionally fixed and cognitively synthesized; a second linguistic substance is unnecessary.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Language is a temporally ordered practice of knowing. Audible elements occur successively; convention determines their semantic range; cognition retains and synthesizes the sequence; and a sentence completes an articulated meaning. This organization is real as a function and form of intelligibility without becoming an occult entity inserted between sound and meaning.

The middle position is exact:

```text
not undifferentiated noise
not a hypostatized Name-substance
but meaningful sound organized by convention and cognitive synthesis
```

single undifferentiated English rendering across both discussions.

## 8. Logical Determination

The three linguistic functions are:

```text
Designation(sound, convention)
    → fixes a semantic application

Sentence(sequence)
    → articulates relations of action, quality, time, and the like
    → reaches completion of meaning

PhoneticElement(event)
    → supplies a minimal audible articulation
```

Their empirical genesis is successive:

```text
sound₁@t₁ → sound₂@t₂ → ... → soundₙ@tₙ

For i ≠ j:
    soundᵢ and soundⱼ need not coexist
```

Therefore a hearer must retain and synthesize the sequence:

```text
LinguisticUnderstanding =
    Retention(successiveSoundEvents)
    + ConventionalDetermination
    + OrderedSynthesis
    + CompletionOfMeaning
```

The Bhāṣya's production/manifestation dilemma is:

```text
If every sound produces or manifests NameEntity:
    mere noise should do so.

If only conventionally specific sound does so:
    that specific meaningful sound already performs the semantic work.

Therefore:
    no additional NameEntity is explanatorily required.
```

The final-sound hypothesis also fails:

```text
If finalSound alone produces the completed name:
    hearing finalSound alone should yield the meaning.

It does not.
```

And co-arising cannot ground reference:

```text
CoArises(Name, Referent)
    cannot explain past referents
    cannot explain future referents
    cannot explain freely assigned names
    cannot explain names of unconditioned dharmas
```

The general rule is:

```text
stable semantic function
    ⇏ separately existing semantic substance
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 2.47 is the first explicit linguistic machine in this sequence. Its three
levels are not interchangeable. A designation fixes an application; a
sentence completes a meaning through relations; a phonetic element is an
event within the audible succession. Their grouping is intelligible, but the
group does not require an entity numerically additional to its members and
their order—just as a row is not a further substance beside the arranged
items.

The temporal argument is decisive for the Organon. A spoken sequence is never
given as a simultaneously completed acoustic object. Earlier elements have
ceased when the final element occurs. Linguistic understanding therefore
requires a continuum capable of retaining difference and synthesizing its
`krama` into one completed determination:

```text
phonetic event
    → retained sequence
    → conventionally determined articulation
    → completed sentence-meaning
```

This does not turn `viṣaya` into an external object or reduce meaning to a
token. The sounds are registered events; the intelligible result depends upon
their ordered synthesis. A corpus architecture should consequently model at
least four distinct layers:

```text
PhoneticEvent
DesignationRule
OrderedExpression
CompletedMeaning
```

The Kośa catalogue calls the three groups formations dissociated from mind.
That label belongs to the reported Vaibhāṣika classification. Vasubandhu's
strongest argument instead makes linguistic form immanent in conventionally
fixed meaningful sound and the cognitive synthesis of its temporal order.
The Organon should preserve the operations without reifying the catalogue's
separate substance.

This is already a functional constraint on the later Technē Agent:

```text
tokenization alone ≠ knowing

Knowing(expression) requires:
    provenance of registered events
    + ordered retention
    + controlled semantic convention
    + relational completion
```

The kārikā itself states the linguistic groups and their classifications. The
anti-reification argument belongs to Vasubandhu's Bhāṣya; the temporal
synthesis and Agent architecture are the present Organon reconstruction.
