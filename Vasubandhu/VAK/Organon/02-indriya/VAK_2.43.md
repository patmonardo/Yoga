# VAK_2.43

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> निरोधाख्या तथैवेयं विहारार्थं भवाग्रजा ।
>
> शुभा द्विवेद्यानियता चार्यस्याप्या प्रयोगतः ॥ २.४३ ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nirodhākhyā tathaiveyaṃ vihārārthaṃ bhavāgrajā /
>
> śubhā dvivedyāniyatā cāryasyāpyā prayogataḥ // 2.43 //

| tathā eva | adverbial phrase | likewise; it too is cessation of citta and caittas |

`Dvivedyā` and `aniyatā` give alternative karmic-result profiles. The
Bhāṣya explains the two times as the immediately following life or a later
life; maturation may also remain unfixed because final nirvāṇa can occur in
the present life.

## 4. Grammar

The feminine subject is the second of the two attainments introduced in VAK
2.35, `nirodha-samāpatti`, supplied by context:

```text
nirodha-ākhyā iyam
    → this [attainment] is called cessation

tathā eva
    → like the preceding attainment,
      it is cessation of citta and caittas

vihāra-artham
    → it is entered for the sake of abiding

bhava-agra-jā
    → it belongs to the summit of existence

śubhā
    → it is wholesome

dvi-vedyā aniyatā ca
    → its maturation may occur at two times
      or remain unfixed

āryasya āpyā prayogataḥ
    → it is acquired by a noble person through practice
```

The Bhāṣya specifies `bhavāgra` as the sphere of neither-reflection-nor-
non-reflection. This is a doctrinal location, not merely a metaphor for
height.

## 5. Translation

### Close syntactic construe

> This attainment, called cessation, is likewise [a cessation of consciousness and mental factors]. It is for the sake of abiding, belongs to the summit of existence, is wholesome, has maturation experience at either of two times or unfixed, and is acquired by a noble person through deliberate practice.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The attainment of cessation, like the non-reflective attainment, suspends consciousness and associated mental factors; unlike it, this attainment is entered for peaceful abiding rather than from the mistaken conception that suppression itself is release. It belongs exclusively to the summit of existence, is wholesome, and can mature in the next life, in a later life, or not at all if final nirvāṇa is attained in the present life. It belongs to noble practitioners and is normally acquired through deliberate preparation grounded in the noble Path.

The Bhāṣya treats the Buddha as an exception to ordinary acquisition by
practice: he acquires the attainment together with awakening and knowledge of
exhaustion.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Two attainments may share the abstract description “absence of active consciousness and mental factors” while possessing opposite rational determinations. The non-reflective attainment is generated by imagined escape and leads necessarily to conditioned rebirth. The attainment of cessation is generated through the noble Path, entered for peaceful abiding, and need not generate another existence. Its meaning lies in its genesis, not in the abstract absence of mental operation.

The governing distinction is:

```text
same extensional state-description
    ≠
same intensional and genetic determination
```

continues the project distinction around `saṃjñā`. The conventional
translation “neither-perception-nor-non-perception” may still be useful as a
cross-reference but is not allowed to collapse `saṃjñā` into consciousness.

## 8. Logical Determination

The two cessation profiles share one operation-state but differ across every
genetic index that matters:

```text
Shared:
    ActiveCittaCaitta = false

AsamjniSamapatti {
    motive: ImaginedRelease,
    ground: FourthDhyana,
    practitioner: Ordinary,
    pathBasis: NotNoblePath,
    result: NecessaryNextRebirth
}

NirodhaSamapatti {
    motive: PeacefulAbiding,
    ground: Bhavagra,
    practitioner: Noble,
    pathBasis: NoblePath,
    result: NextLife | LaterLife | Unfixed
}
```

Therefore:

```text
NOT ActiveCittaCaitta(State)
    ⇏ Liberative(State)

LiberativeDetermination(State)
    requires Genesis(State)
       + PathBasis(State)
       + Motive(State)
       + Ground(State)
       + ResultProfile(State)
```

The ordinary acquisition rule is:

```text
NoblePractitioner(S)
∧ DeliberatePractice(S, NirodhaSamapatti)
    → Prapti(S, NirodhaSamapatti)
```

The Buddha exception is:

```text
Awakening(Buddha)
∧ Arises(KsayaJnana)
    → Prapti(Buddha, NirodhaSamapatti)
```

The disagreement over prior bodhisattva attainment is Bhāṣya-level:

```text

    PriorPraptiAsTrainee = permitted

KashmirVaibhasikas:
    PriorPraptiAsTrainee = denied
    because uninterrupted thirty-four-moment awakening leaves no interval
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 2.43 supplies the positive counterpart to VAK 2.42. Bare cessation is
underdetermined. What makes a cessation what it is must be reconstructed from
its Path, motive, ground, mode of acquisition, and result.

This is precisely the conceptual structure discovered in `prāpti`:

```text
possession of a state
    is insufficient

possession of its Dharma and its Path
    determines what the state actually means
```

The attainment of cessation belongs to a noble person because it is produced
through the noble Path. The Path is not an external itinerary leading to an
otherwise identical state; it is the state's internal genesis. Consequently:

```text
Path-difference
    → Dharma-difference
even where the terminal event-description appears identical
```

This is the strongest Kośa support yet for the methodological claim that
Knowing contains the recoverable genesis of its Concept. The Bhāṣya does not
formulate that claim in the language of Absolute Reason, but it insists on
the decisive premise: cessation acquires its soteriological determination
from the Knowledge and Path that produced it.

`Bhavāgra` now occupies the exact summit position in the textual profile. In
the Kośa it remains the highest conditioned level. In the Brahma-Yoga
reconstruction, it can mark the pinnacle of the spiritual Object only if the
difference between conditioned cosmological level and reconstructed
transcendental function remains explicit.

The two forms of `nirodha` can therefore be stated without ambiguity:

```text
abstract nirodha:
    operation has ceased

Path-determined nirodha:
    cessation is generated and mastered through noble Knowledge
```

At the project level, the second approaches fulfilled non-reflective Knowing:

```text
Mārga-prāpti
    → Dharma-prāpti
    → Jñāna
    → reflection no longer required for immediate availability
```

This final sequence is an Organon reconstruction. It must not replace the
Kośa's narrower claim that `nirodha-samāpatti` is cessation of citta and
caittas at the summit of existence.
