# VAK_2.09 Bhāṣya — Path-Designations and Outflow Classification

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> dṛgbhāvanāśaikṣapathe nava trīṇi amalaṃ trayam /
> rūpīṇi jīvitaṃ duḥkhe sāsravāṇi dvidhā nava // 2.09 //

> On the paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training, the nine
> are the three faculties; the triad is stainless. The material
> faculties, life, and the two painful faculties are with outflows;
> nine are twofold.

The Bhāṣya identifies the nine, distributes their three designations
across the paths, and identifies the dual *duḥkhe* as pain and distress.
The separated *trīṇi amalam* follows the research kārikā. The initial
compound includes *aśaikṣa*, no-more-training, as the prose confirms.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“On the paths of seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training, the nine
are the three.” The nine are mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling,
and the five beginning with faith. These nine faculties are called
three faculties on the three paths: on the path of seeing, the faculty
“I shall know what has not been known”; on the path of cultivation,
the knowledge-faculty; and on the path of no-more-training, the faculty
of one who has known. Why? On the path of seeing, one is engaged in
knowing what has not been known. On the path of cultivation, there
is nothing unprecedented to be known; rather, one knows that same
thing for the purpose of abandoning the remaining latent afflictions.
On the path of no-more-training, the ascertainment “it has been known”
is called *ājñātāva*. One who possesses this is *ājñātāvin*. Or the
name means one whose disposition is to preserve what has been known,
because of attaining the knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising.
The faculty of one in such a condition—“Suffering has been fully
known by me; it is not to be fully known again”—is the faculty of
one who has known.

Having explained their own nature, their distinctions of kind must
be stated: how many faculties are with outflows, how many without
outflows, and so forth. First, the group just mentioned, beginning
with the faculty “I shall know,” is “the stainless triad.” This means
without outflows, since stains is another designation for outflows.
“The material faculties, life, and the two painful faculties are
with outflows.” The seven material faculties, the life-faculty,
and the faculties of pain and distress are exclusively with outflows.
The seven material faculties are eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, female,
and male, because they are included in the aggregate of form.
“Nine are twofold”: mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, and
the five beginning with faith are both with outflows and without
outflows.

Some say that those beginning with faith are exclusively without
outflows. For the Blessed One said: “One in whom these five faculties
are altogether absent I call an outsider, standing among ordinary
persons.” This does not establish the claim, because the statement
was made with reference to the faculties without outflows. For he
first set out the classification of noble persons and then said
“one in whom these…” Or ordinary persons are of two kinds: an insider
whose wholesome roots have not been severed, and an outsider whose
wholesome roots have been severed. With reference to the latter he
says: “I call an outsider, standing among ordinary persons.”

It is also said in a sūtra, before the Wheel of Dharma had been
set in motion: “There are beings born in the world and grown up
in the world with sharp faculties, middling faculties, and weak
faculties.” Therefore those beginning with faith certainly do exist
with outflows. Again it is said: “So long as I had not known as they
really are the arising, disappearance, gratification, danger, and
escape concerning these five faculties, so long I … from this world
with its gods”—and so forth. But this is not the manner of examination
of dharmas without outflows.

The final quotation is abbreviated in the Sanskrit itself. Its
unfinished clause is preserved rather than completed from an
unprovided version of the sūtra. The argument depends on the five
stated aspects of examination, not on an editorial completion.

## 4. Movement and Voices of the Commentary

The first movement completes the definition of the three realization
faculties. A new classificatory phase is then expressly announced:
from explanation of own nature to distinctions of kind. The first
axis is outflow-status.

The initial classification is challenged by “some,” who restrict
faith and the following four to the without-outflow class. The reply
first offers two contextual readings of their citation and then
adds two scriptural grounds for admitting with-outflow instances.
The *vā*, “or,” introducing the insider/outsider distinction marks
an alternative reply, not a second step that must be combined with
the first. No school is explicitly named in this unit.

## 5. Nine Faculty-Types and Three Path-Designations

The nine comprise mind; three feeling-faculties, pleasure, gladness,
and neutral feeling; and faith, vigor, recollection, concentration,
and prajñā. The three realization names classify these in relation
to seeing, cultivation, and no-more-training.

The text does not posit three unrelated additional constituents.
Nor does listing nine establish that all nine must occur simultaneously
in every path-event. In particular, the list includes distinct
feeling-faculties whose distributions have just been examined.
“Constituted from the nine faculty-types according to path” is safer
than treating nine as a fixed count of co-present factors in every
instance.

The names are explained by their tasks. Seeing concerns what was
not known; cultivation continues knowing the same for abandonment
of remaining *anuśayas*; no-more-training carries the ascertainment
that the task has been accomplished. “Nothing unprecedented to be
known” concerns this path-task, not omniscience about every possible
object. The statement about suffering likewise concerns completed
full knowledge, not loss of the ability to cognize it again.

## 6. The Threefold Outflow Classification

| Status | Faculties | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Exclusively without outflows (*anāsrava*) | Three realization faculties | 3 |
| Exclusively with outflows (*sāsrava*) | Seven material faculties, life, pain, distress | 10 |
| Either status (*dvidhā*) | Mind, pleasure, gladness, neutral feeling, five beginning with faith | 9 |

The two occurrences of “nine” refer to the same list under different
questions: its role in the path-designations and its possible
outflow-status. Their being twofold as faculty-types does not make
the three path-designations partly with outflows. The triad itself
is expressly without outflows.

“Twofold” means that instances may have either status, not that
one instance simultaneously bears both. “With outflows” also should
not be substituted by “unwholesome” or “personally afflicted.” The
classification includes material faculties and life, and the preceding
unit explicitly allowed bodily feeling in an arhat. Outflow-status,
ethical quality, and the attainment of the bearer remain distinct
questions. This passage does not classify conditioned versus
unconditioned dharmas.

## 7. Why Faith and the Others Are Not Exclusively Anāsrava

The opposing citation links absence of the five faculties to the
status of an outsider. The first reply restricts its reference:
in a discussion distinguishing noble persons, “these” can designate
the without-outflow faculties specifically. Their absence need not
exclude with-outflow faith and the others.

The alternative reply distinguishes ordinary persons with intact
wholesome roots from those whose roots are severed. On that reading,
the outsider statement concerns the latter. Neither reply denies
the quotation; each tests what its wording establishes in context.

The subsequent citation describes differing faculties before the
Wheel of Dharma was set in motion. The commentary takes this as
evidence for with-outflow faculties beginning with faith. Finally,
it invokes examination of their arising, disappearance, gratification,
danger, and escape, asserting that without-outflow dharmas are not
examined in this manner. These are the text's arguments for its
classification; the quoted sūtras are not independently identified
or collated in this study.

## 8. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for Translation

*Amala*, stainless, is explicitly glossed as *anāsrava*. The working
translations remain “with outflows” and “without outflows,” avoiding
an inference that the latter are without causal efficacy. *Duḥkhe*
is a dual naming pain and distress, not a locative meaning “in pain.”

The derivation of *ājñātāvin* has two stated forms: possession of
the ascertainment “it has been known,” and a disposition to preserve
what has been known. Both are retained as the commentary's explanations,
without presenting the unusual *ājñātāva* as an independently verified
historical derivation.

The last scriptural extract ends mid-clause with *iti vistaraḥ*.
The research translation supplies an awakening claim beyond the
quoted Sanskrit. This continuous translation instead marks the
ellipsis and preserves exactly the argumentative content available
in the local witness.

## 9. Philosophical and Organon Study

The passage brings two relations into focus: a faculty-type can
receive a path-designation through the task in which it operates,
and that type can have different outflow-status across instances.
Neither its bare name nor its membership in the nine settles every
question about its occurrence.

For Organon reconstruction, the important distinction is between
constituent type, path-function, and qualification. The three
realization faculties express a unity determined through the path;
that unity preserves the differences among their constituent types.
It should not be represented as a literal nine-part assembly that
must be present in every moment.

The scriptural debate adds a methodological point: a statement's
scope must be established before using it to classify all instances
of a term. The commentary tests contextual reference and alternative
construals before concluding that faith and the others can occur
with outflows. This is a precise contribution to systematic study,
without requiring an additional Organon doctrine to enter the translation.
