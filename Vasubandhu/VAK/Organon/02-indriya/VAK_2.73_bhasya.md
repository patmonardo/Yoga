# VAK_2.73 Bhāṣya — Acquisition and the Close of Indriyanirdeśa

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> kliṣṭe traidhātuke lābhaḥ ṣaṇṇāṃ ṣaṇṇāṃ dvayoḥ śubhe /
> trayāṇāṃ rūpaje śaikṣe caturṇāṃ tasya śeṣite // 2.73 //

> In afflicted mind of the three realms, acquisition is of six,
> six, and two kinds respectively; in wholesome form-realm mind,
> of three; in trainee mind, of four; in the remaining cases,
> of that mind itself.

The unit at 109.04–110.08 returns explicitly to the twelve-kind
classification and asks about acquisition rather than immediate
succession. It concludes with the summary verse, closure of the
conditions discussion, and chapter colophon. The Sanskrit retains
the running witness's irregularities; the damaged possession phrase
is marked in the translation and discussed below.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

Of the twelve kinds of mind stated, in which mind is there acquisition
of how many kinds? “In afflicted mind of the three realms, acquisition
is of six, six, and two.” When desire-realm afflicted mind is manifest,
there is acquisition of six kinds of mind. [The transmitted possession
phrase here is problematic.] Desire-realm wholesome mind is acquired
through restoration of wholesome roots by doubt, and through return
to the realm. Desire-realm unwholesome and obscured-indeterminate mind,
and form-realm afflicted mind, are acquired through return to the
realm and through decline. Formless afflicted mind and trainee mind
are acquired through decline.

In form-realm afflicted mind too there is acquisition of six: the
three form-realm kinds and desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind,
through return to the realm; and formless afflicted mind and trainee
mind, through decline. In formless afflicted mind, however, there is
acquisition of two: that afflicted mind itself and trainee mind,
through decline.

“In wholesome form-realm mind, of three.” In form-realm wholesome
mind there is acquisition of three kinds: that wholesome mind itself,
and desire- and form-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind. “In trainee
mind, of four”: that trainee mind itself, desire- and form-realm
unobscured-indeterminate mind, and formless wholesome mind, upon
dispassion from the desire and form realms through the noble path.

“In the remaining cases, of that mind itself.” What has been left
over is called the remainder. Wherever acquisition has not been
explained for a mind, acquisition should be understood as of that
mind itself, not another.

Others, however, state the account without these distinctions:

> In afflicted mind there is acquisition of nine, say the wise;
> in wholesome mind, of six; in indeterminate mind, of that alone.

Here one should say, “In wholesome mind, of seven.” Desire-realm
wholesome mind is acquired through restoration of wholesome roots
by right view; desire- and form-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind
through dispassion; form- and formless-realm wholesome mind through
acquisition of the respective concentrations; and trainee and
beyond-training mind upon entry into the fixed course and attainment
of arhatship, respectively. The remainder should be determined from
the explanation already given.

A summary verse:

> At rebirth, meditative attainment, dispassion, decline,
> and restoration of wholesome roots,
> mind is acquired by one who does not possess it.

The discussion occasioned by conditions is complete.

In the Abhidharmakośabhāṣya, the second Kośa chapter, called the
Exposition of the Faculties, is complete.

## 4. Acquisition Is a Different Relation

The opening question names twelve kinds, not the twenty just discussed.
The commentary now asks what is obtained when a given mind is manifest.
A kind newly acquired need not itself be the manifest mind or its
immediate successor. Nor does every occurrence of a mind constitute
new acquisition of that kind.

The final *atadvataḥ*, “for one not possessing that,” is decisive.
Acquisition concerns a change in possession and can include reacquisition
after loss. The word does not require first-ever acquisition in a
person's history. The remainder rule restricts which kind is acquired
where no cross-acquisition is specified; it does not say that every
manifestation renews acquisition automatically.

| Manifest kind | Kinds acquired across the specified occasions | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Desire afflicted | Desire wholesome, unwholesome, obscured; form afflicted; formless afflicted; trainee | 6 |
| Form afflicted | Three form kinds; desire unobscured; formless afflicted; trainee | 6 |
| Formless afflicted | Formless afflicted; trainee | 2 |
| Form wholesome | Form wholesome; desire and form unobscured | 3 |
| Trainee | Trainee; desire and form unobscured; formless wholesome | 4 |
| Remaining cases | The manifest kind itself | 1 |

“Obscured” and “unobscured” here abbreviate their respective indeterminate
kinds. These lists collect possibilities across the occasions named
in the prose. They do not require all listed kinds to be acquired
simultaneously whenever the focal mind appears.

## 5. The Occasions and the Alternative Summary

Restoration of wholesome roots appears twice, with different occasions:
doubt in the desire-afflicted account, and right view in the wholesome
aggregate account. The translation preserves both. It does not change
the first into wholesome right view or describe doubt itself as wholesome.

Return to a realm and decline likewise have distinct explanatory
roles. In the form-afflicted account, return is attached to the three
form kinds and desire unobscured mind; decline to formless afflicted
and trainee mind. The research translation's “return to that realm”
after desire unobscured could misleadingly imply return to the desire
realm. Realm affiliation of an acquired mind must remain distinct
from the realm of the person's rebirth.

The acquisition of trainee mind through decline also shows why the
immediate-successor restrictions of 2.70 cannot alone prove a general
absence of regression. Acquisition and manifestation answer different
questions. This paragraph registers the acquisition occasion without
supplying an unexpressed intermediate sequence of minds.

The other teachers' “nine with afflicted mind” is an aggregate count.
The union of the three afflicted profiles contains nine kinds:
three desire kinds excluding unobscured, all three form kinds,
formless afflicted, trainee, and desire unobscured. Those are nine
distinct members, not nine simultaneous acquisitions in each afflicted
occurrence.

The correction from six to seven in the wholesome aggregate comprises
three realm-wholesome kinds, two unobscured kinds belonging to desire
and form, and the two uncontaminated kinds. This does not correct
the verse's three for form wholesome or four for trainee; those are
particular profiles. The rejected six belongs to the alternative
undifferentiated summary.

## 6. Textual, Voice, and Translation Decisions

At 109.06–07 the witness reads *tairsamanvāgatasya*, which appears
to say “for one possessing those,” although the passage concerns
acquisition and its closing summary says “for one not possessing
that.” The continuous translation marks this problem where it occurs
instead of silently supplying a negation or omitting the phrase
without notice. The surrounding acquisition list is clear; resolving
the exact possession wording requires another witness.

Other retained transcription irregularities include *klilṣṭe*,
*śekṣasya*, *lābhho*, *tasyaivā vyākṛṭe*, and *tatastyasamādhilābhataḥ*.
The English construes the last through the respective concentrations
already named. The other teachers' neutral clause is read as referring
to indeterminate mind, consistent with the research witness and the
threefold summary. No corrected critical Sanskrit is asserted.

The kārikā syntax crosses its line break: *śubhe ... rūpaje* means
“in wholesome form-realm mind” and takes “of three.” “Of two” belongs
to formless afflicted mind. *Tasya śeṣite* receives an explicit prose
explanation and is not left as an unresolved final count.

The phrase *anye punar abhedenāhuḥ* marks the other teachers' summary.
The following “one should say seven” is the commentary's correction,
not part of their quoted position. The summary verse then gathers
the acquisition occasions, followed by two explicit closures. The
colophon is included through 110.08; the following isolated attribution
at 110.09 lies outside this commentary unit.

## 7. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, the chapter's final distinction is between
what occurs and what one comes to possess. Classification identified
the kinds; succession specified their directed relations; acquisition
now requires an account of the person's prior possession and the
occasion of change. None of these questions can simply replace another.

This is particularly relevant to learning. An acquired capacity need
not be presently exercised, and its present exercise need not be its
first acquisition. The Kośa's particular acquisition cases give this
distinction a determinate setting. A broader account of learning can
work from it while retaining the source's path, realm, and karma
contexts rather than treating them as interchangeable metaphors.

The chapter closes by naming the discussion of conditions it has
completed. Its movement from faculties through causal and conditional
relations provides material for our inquiry into synthesis; the
colophon itself does not announce an Organon system or a modern
formal theory. That further reconstruction remains our responsibility.
