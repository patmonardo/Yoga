# VAK_2.61

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> चित्तचैत्तास्तथान्येऽपि संप्रयुक्तकवर्जिताः ।
> चत्वारः प्रत्यया उक्ता हेत्वाख्यः पञ्च हेतवः ॥ २.६१ ॥

The displayed reading regularizes the opening to *cittacaittās tathā*,
following the running Bhāṣya's *cittacaittāḥ*. The standalone kārikā
transcription and research file instead have *cittacaitāḥ tathā*.
The Devanāgarī here is an editorial rendering of the stated IAST reading,
not an independently collated witness.

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> cittacaittās tathānye 'pi saṃprayuktakavarjitāḥ /
> catvāraḥ pratyayā uktā hetvākhyaḥ pañca hetavaḥ // 2.61 //

The full verse includes both the closing qualification of the cause
counts and the opening of the conditions. The research Bhāṣya's claim
that only the second half is the actual verse is not supported by the
standalone kārikā witness. A commentary-unit boundary is not a verse
boundary. The running commentary treats the first half at 97.26–98.02
and the second at 98.03–08.

The singular *hetvākhyaḥ* refers to an understood *pratyayaḥ*.
It does not mean that the five causes are plural “cause-named conditions.”
The spelling and number in the research grammar table require correction.

## 4. Grammar

The verse has three constructions, not one sentence identifying mental
factors as four conditions.

**Retrospective subject.** *Cittacaittāḥ* answers the commentary's
question about the four profiles in 2.60: they concern mind and mental
factors. The predicate “arise from the remaining causes” is inherited
from that verse.

**Extension with an exclusion.** *Tathā anye api* extends the four
profiles to other dharmas. *Saṃprayuktakavarjitāḥ* qualifies their causal
account: associated cause is additionally removed. The Bhāṣya identifies
these others as material dharmas and formations dissociated from mind.
It does not exclude the associated cause from the six-cause system itself.

**New topic.** *Catvāraḥ pratyayā uktāḥ* independently announces four
conditions. *Hetvākhyaḥ [pratyayaḥ] pañca hetavaḥ* is a nominal definition:
the condition called “cause” consists of five causes. Its singular subject
and plural predicate describe a grouping, not five additional conditions.
The Bhāṣya identifies the omitted sixth cause as *kāraṇahetu*.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> [The foregoing concern] mind and mental factors. The others also
> [arise] likewise, excluding associated cause. Four conditions are
> taught. The [condition] named “cause” is the five causes.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The preceding four causal profiles apply to mind and mental factors.
> Material dharmas and formations dissociated from mind follow the same
> pattern, with associated cause additionally excluded. Four kinds of
> condition are taught. Of these, the cause-condition comprises the five
> causes other than the conditioning cause (*kāraṇahetu*).

The names of the four conditions are supplied by the Bhāṣya's scriptural
citation: cause-condition, immediately antecedent condition, object-condition,
and predominant condition. The verse itself gives their number and
specifies only the cause-condition. The other definitions follow in 2.62.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The causal account must be adjusted to the kind of dharma being
> explained: the association proper to mind and its factors cannot
> simply be attributed to material and dissociated dharmas. The inquiry
> then distinguishes four kinds of condition, gathering five already
> explained causes under one of them.

This is a study interpretation of the transition. It does not identify
causes with an exclusively objective domain or conditions with an
exclusively subjective domain. The grouping explicitly links the two
classifications.

“Conditioning cause” remains the working English for *kāraṇahetu*;
its wording must not obscure its distinction from *hetupratyaya*.
Associated cause is retained within cause-condition despite being
excluded from the production profiles of material and dissociated dharmas.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Two exclusions with different scopes.**

| Operation | Excluded cause | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Extend 2.60's profiles beyond mind and factors | Associated cause | Causal profiles of material and dissociated dharmas |
| Define cause-condition | Conditioning cause | Grouping of five causes within the four-condition classification |

For the four classes, the counts become:

| Class | Mind and mental factors | Material and dissociated dharmas |
|---|---:|---:|
| Afflicted | 5 | 4 |
| Maturation-born | 5 | 4 |
| Remaining | 4 | 3 |
| First uncontaminated | 3 | 2 |

The shared conditioning and co-arisen causes remain even in the last
non-associated case. The commentary closes this account by stating
that no dharma arises from only one cause.

The new classification is:

```text
CauseCondition comprises:
    CoArisenCause
    AssociatedCause
    HomogeneousCause
    PervasiveCause
    MaturationCause

ConditioningCause is excluded from this grouping.
```

This is a classification of causal relations, not a claim that all
five must operate in every occurrence. The preceding profiles already
show why such a reading would fail. The four conditions are not four
dharma classes corresponding one-to-one to the four profiles.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Organon interpretation.** The transition makes the relation between
grounds and conditions a concrete question of classification and scope.
The five causes are retained under one condition; the causal analysis
is carried forward into a differently organized account. This supplies
a basis for investigating synthesis without assuming that the earlier
account was merely quantitative or that the new one is purely subjective.

For learning, the decisive correction is being able to say what each
exclusion concerns. Knowing the numbers “six,” “five,” and “four” does
not yet establish the relations. The first half restricts a causal
profile by the kind of dharma; the second groups causal types under
a kind of condition. Confusing these operations reverses the doctrine.

**Textual review.** This polished study corrects the first-pass translation
that made mind, mental factors, and other dharmas the four conditions,
and its mistaken exclusion of associated cause from cause-condition.
It also preserves the complete kārikā despite the commentary's topic
boundary within it. The following study of 2.62 must determine the
remaining conditions from their own verse and running commentary;
the extended research 2.61 Bhāṣya cannot substitute for that boundary check.

The reading is provisional. Research witnesses remain intact; no
independent manuscript collation has been performed.
