# VAK_2.72 Bhāṣya — Twenty Kinds, Their Successions, and Attention

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> vipākajairyāpathikaśailpasthānikanairmitam /
> caturdhāvyākṛtaṃ kāme rūpe śilpavivarjitam // 2.72 //

> [Having divided] indeterminate mind in the desire realm fourfold
> into maturation-born, deportment-related, craft-related, and
> creation-related; in the form realm, excluding craft.

The commentary at 106.05–109.03 completes the refinement begun in
2.71, enumerates succession among twenty kinds, explains selected
asymmetries, and discusses attention in relation to the noble path.
The next question, at 109.04, returns to twelve kinds to examine
acquisition and belongs to 2.73. The continuous Sanskrit preserves
the running transcription; the anchor follows the standalone verse
as explained in the polished kārikā study.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

“Maturation-born, deportment-related, craft-related, and creation-related:
indeterminate mind in the desire realm [is divided] fourfold.” “Having
divided” continues to apply. Desire-realm unobscured-indeterminate mind
is divided fourfold: maturation-born, deportment-related, craft-related,
and the mind of supernormal creation. “In the form realm, excluding
craft.” In the form realm it is divided threefold, excluding the
craft-related kind, because there is no craft there. In this way
these twelve minds become twenty. Wholesome mind is divided sixfold,
and unobscured-indeterminate mind sevenfold. Deportment-related and
the other such minds do not occur in the formless realm, because
deportment and the corresponding activities are absent there.

Visible form, smell, taste, and tangibles are their objects; sound
also is an object of craft-related mind. These are mental consciousnesses
only. The five groups of sensory consciousness, however, prepare
deportment-related and craft-related activity. Others say that there
is mental consciousness brought forth by deportment which takes the
twelve sense-fields as its objects.

Now, among these twenty minds, which is immediately successive to
which? First, among the eight desire-realm kinds, ten minds arise
after application-produced mind: seven of its own level, excluding
the result of supernormal knowledge; form-realm application-produced
mind; and trainee and beyond-training mind. That application-produced
mind itself arises after eight: its own wholesome and afflicted kinds,
the form-realm application-produced and afflicted kinds, and trainee
and beyond-training mind.

After birth-acquired wholesome mind arise nine: seven of its own
level, excluding the result of supernormal knowledge, and the afflicted
minds of the form and formless realms. It itself arises after eleven:
the seven of its own realm as before, the form-realm application-produced
and afflicted kinds, and trainee and beyond-training mind.

After unwholesome or obscured-indeterminate mind arise seven, those
of its own realm as before. Each itself arises after fourteen: seven
of its own realm, four form-realm kinds excluding application-produced
mind and the result of supernormal knowledge, and three formless
kinds excluding application-produced mind.

After deportment-related or maturation-born mind arise eight: six
of its own level, excluding application-produced mind and the result
of supernormal knowledge, and the afflicted minds of the form and
formless realms. Each itself arises after seven minds of its own
realm, as before. After craft-related mind arise six, those of its
own realm excluding application-produced mind and the result of
supernormal knowledge. It itself arises after seven of its own realm,
excluding the result of supernormal knowledge.

After the result of supernormal knowledge arise two: its own kind
of result of supernormal knowledge, and form-realm application-produced
mind. It too arises only from these two.

We shall now speak of the six form-realm kinds. After application-produced
mind arise twelve: the two desire-realm wholesome kinds and the result
of supernormal knowledge; its own six; formless application-produced
mind; and trainee and beyond-training mind. It itself arises after
ten: desire-realm application-produced mind and the result of supernormal
knowledge; four of its own realm, excluding deportment-related and
maturation-born mind; formless application-produced and afflicted mind;
and trainee and beyond-training mind.

After birth-acquired wholesome mind arise eight: the two afflicted
desire-realm kinds, five of its own realm excluding the result of
supernormal knowledge, and formless afflicted mind. It itself arises
only from those five of its own realm. After afflicted mind arise
nine: the four desire-realm wholesome and afflicted kinds, and five
of its own realm excluding the result of supernormal knowledge. It
itself arises after eleven: desire-realm birth-acquired wholesome,
deportment-related, and maturation-born mind; five of its own realm,
excluding the result of supernormal knowledge; and three formless
kinds, excluding application-produced mind.

After deportment-related mind arise seven: the two afflicted desire-realm
kinds, four of its own realm excluding application-produced mind and
the result of supernormal knowledge, and formless afflicted mind.
It itself arises after five of its own realm, excluding the result
of supernormal knowledge. The same should be said of maturation-born
mind. After the result of supernormal knowledge arise two, its own
realm's application-produced mind and result of supernormal knowledge.
It too arises only from these two.

We shall now speak of the four formless kinds. After application-produced
mind arise seven: form-realm application-produced mind, its own four,
and trainee and beyond-training mind. It itself arises after six:
form-realm application-produced mind, three of its own realm excluding
maturation-born mind, and trainee and beyond-training mind.

After birth-acquired wholesome mind arise seven: its own four and
the afflicted kinds of the lower levels. It itself arises only from
the four of its own realm. After afflicted mind arise eight: its own
four, form-realm application-produced and afflicted mind, and the two
afflicted desire-realm kinds. It itself arises after ten: its own
four and the birth-acquired wholesome, deportment-related, and
maturation-born minds of the desire and form realms. After maturation-born
mind arise six: three of its own realm excluding application-produced
mind, and the three afflicted kinds of the lower realms. It itself
arises only from the four of its own realm.

After trainee mind arise six: the application-produced wholesome kinds
of the three realms, desire-realm birth-acquired wholesome mind,
trainee mind, and beyond-training mind. It itself arises after four:
the three application-produced kinds and trainee mind. After
beyond-training mind arise five, as after trainee mind but omitting
trainee mind itself. It itself arises after five: the three
application-produced kinds, trainee mind, and beyond-training mind.

Why do maturation-born, deportment-related, and craft-related minds
arise after application-produced mind, whereas application-produced
mind does not arise after them? Because they operate in the preparation
of deportment and craft, or proceed weakly without deliberate preparation,
they are not conducive to application-produced mind. An emergence-mind,
however, proceeds without deliberate preparation; its arising after
application-produced mind is therefore appropriate. “Then application-produced
mind should not arise after afflicted minds either, because they
are contrary in quality.” Even so, its manifestation is appropriate
for someone wearied by the occurrence of affliction, for the sake
of fully understanding that affliction.

Desire-realm birth-acquired wholesome mind arises immediately after
trainee and beyond-training mind and form-realm application-produced
mind because it is keen. Those minds do not arise from it, because
it proceeds without deliberate preparation. Desire-realm birth-acquired
wholesome mind arises after form-realm afflicted mind because it is
keen. But form-realm birth-acquired wholesome mind does not arise
after formless afflicted mind, because it lacks that keenness.

There are three kinds of attention: attention to the specific
characteristic—for example, “material form has being affected as
its characteristic,” and so forth; attention to the general
characteristic, associated with the sixteen aspects; and attention
based on resolve, in contemplation of the unattractive, the immeasurables,
formless liberations, spheres of mastery, spheres of totality, and
so forth. One makes the noble path manifest immediately after any
of the three kinds of attention, and after it any of the three
kinds of attention. On this account the statement “one develops
the awakening factor of mindfulness accompanied by contemplation
of the unattractive” is appropriate.

Others say that one makes the noble path manifest only after attention
to the general characteristic, but after the path all three kinds
of attention occur. Having subdued the mind through contemplation
of the unattractive, one makes the path manifest after attention
to the general characteristic. Thus the statement “one develops
the awakening factor of mindfulness accompanied by contemplation
of the unattractive” refers to an indirect succession.

Others say that even immediately after the noble path there is only
attention to the general characteristic. If entry into the fixed
course rests on one of the three levels beginning with the preparatory
attainment, it might be possible to make desire-realm attention to
the general characteristic manifest after that path. But what if
entry into the fixed course rests on the second or higher absorptions?
Desire-realm attention is impossible because it is too remote. Nor
has attention of that level been acquired apart from what is conducive
to penetration; and a noble person does not again make what is
conducive to penetration manifest. For someone who has obtained
the fruit, manifesting its preparatory application is inappropriate.

[The proposed reply is:] Another attention to the general characteristic
of that kind is also cultivated for that person—for example, “all
formations are impermanent; all dharmas are not-self; nirvāṇa is
peaceful”—and that is what the person will make manifest. They do
not approve this [explanation].

For someone attaining arhatship supported by the preparatory attainment,
the emergence-mind belongs either to that level or to the desire
realm. When supported by the sphere of nothingness, it belongs either
to that level or to the summit of existence. In the remaining cases
it belongs only to the same level.

In the desire realm there are three kinds of attention: learning-born,
reflection-born, and birth-acquired; cultivation-born attention is
absent. In the form realm there are three: learning-born, cultivation-born,
and birth-acquired; reflection-born is absent, because when they begin
to reflect, concentration itself becomes present. In the formless
realm there are cultivation-born and birth-acquired attention. Among
these, the noble path becomes manifest after five kinds of attention,
excluding the birth-acquired kinds, because it depends on application.
After the path, however, desire-realm birth-acquired attention too
can become manifest, because of its keenness.

## 4. The Completed Classification and Its Counts

The wholesome split of 2.71 adds three kinds to twelve. The desire
neutral split adds three more, and the form neutral split adds two:
**12 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 20**. The final groups are eight desire, six
form, four formless, and two uncontaminated kinds.

The prose's “sevenfold” neutral division counts the four desire and
three form kinds being subdivided. The unchanged formless maturation-born
kind makes eight unobscured-indeterminate kinds in the complete inventory.
Birth-acquired wholesome and maturation-born neutral remain distinct.

The creation kind is designated *nirmāṇacitta* in the subdivision and
*abhijñāphala*, result of supernormal knowledge, in the succession
account. The latter does not name all supernormal knowledge indiscriminately.
Its two-neighbor profile must not be extended to every neutral mind.

## 5. Why Refinement Changes the Account

The coarse desire wholesome class had a direct connection to trainee
and beyond-training cognition. Here only its application-produced
member precedes those path minds, while either desire wholesome
member can follow them. The prose explains the birth-acquired member's
outgoing restriction through its lack of deliberate preparation,
even though its keenness permits the incoming connection.

This is more precise than copying every class-level transition to
every subtype. The two creation minds provide another check: each
has only two predecessors and successors, despite belonging to a
broader neutral class with more connections.

The objection concerning afflicted mind is preserved as an objection.
The reply does not say affliction is itself a wholesome preparation;
it describes someone wearied by its occurrence turning toward its
full understanding. That circumstance explains how application-produced
mind can follow a contrary kind.

## 6. Two Classifications of Attention

The first threefold classification concerns specific characteristic,
general characteristic, and resolve. The commentary then presents
three positions concerning the noble path:

| Position | Before the path | After the path |
|---|---|---|
| Initial account | Any of the three | Any of the three |
| First alternative | General-characteristic attention only | Any of the three |
| Further alternative | General-characteristic attention only in the preceding discussion | General-characteristic attention only |

The second account explains the cited connection with contemplation
of the unattractive through an intervening general-characteristic
attention. The further restriction on emergence is challenged through
entry into the path at higher meditative levels. The proposed reply
about cultivating another general-characteristic attention is followed
by *tad etan na varṇayanti*, “they do not approve this.” It is not
presented as the commentator's settled solution. The approving or
rejecting group is not named locally.

The later classification concerns origin instead:

| Realm | Learning-born | Reflection-born | Cultivation-born | Birth-acquired |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desire | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Form | Yes | No | Yes | Yes |
| Formless | No | No | Yes | Yes |

The five non-birth-acquired combinations are desire learning and
reflection, form learning and cultivation, and formless cultivation.
They account for the five preceding the path in the final paragraph.
The exception on emergence is desire birth-acquired attention. These
five combinations are not five extra members of the twenty-mind list,
and the two classifications of attention should not be conflated.

The technical absence of cultivation-born attention in the desire
realm does not mean that a person born there cannot meditate. The
realm qualification concerns the attention classified. Similarly,
the form-realm statement about reflection is explained by the immediate
presence of concentration; it is not a claim that beings there have
no understanding.

## 7. Textual, Voice, and Translation Decisions

The opening desire-realm subdivision, absent from the research continuous
translation, is restored from 106.05–08. The Sanskrit is retained
through 109.03 without silently correcting transcription problems.
Examples include *vipākajaiyapithika*, *śelpasthānika*,
*viśartirbharvanti*, *viśatekścittānāṃ*, *śaikṣalmaśaikṣaṃ*,
*kliṣṭānantaramṣṭau*, *māskāram*, and *upapattipratilabhbhikebhyaḥ*.
The explicit enumerations and repeated terminology support the English
construals. No independently collated critical edition is claimed.

At 106.13, “their objects” is compressed. In context it describes the
activity-related mental consciousnesses, with sound added for craft;
it should not be extended indiscriminately to every maturation-born
or creation mind in every realm. The following sentence explicitly
identifies sensory consciousnesses as preparatory for deportment and
craft, while another teachers' account permits deportment-produced
mental consciousness to range over all twelve sense-fields. The
translation retains the pronoun rather than supplying an exhaustive
object theory for all four neutral kinds.

The source's duals matter. *Kāmāvacare kuśale* at 107.03–05 means
both desire wholesome kinds, and *kāmāvacare kliṣṭe* means both desire
afflicted kinds. Their explicit translation repairs the research
English's singular formulations and makes the totals recoverable.

At 108.02–03 the explanation distributes over deportment, craft, and
weak unprepared occurrence; it need not make every named mind both
craft-producing and weak. *Anabhisaṃskāravāhin* is rendered “proceeding
without deliberate preparation,” including the emergence-mind. The
source does not explicitly call the emergence-mind “weakly constructed,”
as the first pass did.

The specific-characteristic example *rūpaṇālakṣaṇaṃ rūpam* is rendered
“material form has being affected as its characteristic.” Reducing
it to “form has the characteristic of being form” loses the explanatory
force of *rūpaṇa*. *Ārūpyavimokṣa* is construed as formless liberations
in the compact practice list. *Anāgamya* is rendered “preparatory
attainment”; the three levels beginning with it are retained as the
source states them without inserting an unmarked enumeration.

The final origin classification renders *śrutamaya* as “learning-born,”
*cintāmaya* as “reflection-born,” and *bhāvanāmaya* as “cultivation-born.”
These preserve the source's distinct means of development. The word
“attention” throughout translates *manaskāra*, not an undifferentiated
modern faculty of concentration.

The competing accounts are introduced by *apare*, “others.” No school
names are supplied. Brackets identify the proposed reply at 108.23–26;
its rejection remains explicit. The emergence-level rules that follow
are translated separately, rather than made proof that the rejected
reply has been accepted.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

As Organon interpretation, refinement does more than multiply labels.
It tests the relations previously stated at a broader level. Application
and birth acquisition matter because they distinguish how a wholesome
mind can enter and leave particular sequences. The commentary then
asks why those directional differences hold.

The attention discussion adds another demand: distinguish an immediate
relation from one mediated by another act of attention. A cited
connection between contemplation and the path can be construed directly
or through an intervening determination, and the text preserves that
dispute. The general term “attention” cannot decide it in advance.

These are useful materials for our inquiry into learning and mediation.
Their value depends on retaining the source's distinctions and contested
explanations. Neither the transition table nor a formal representation
of it replaces the arguments about preparation, emergence, and the
status of the practitioner.
