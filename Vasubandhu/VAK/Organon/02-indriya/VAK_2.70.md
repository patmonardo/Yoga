# VAK_2.70

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> नव चित्तानि तत्षण्णां निवृतात्सप्त तत्तथा ।
> चतुर्भ्यः शैक्षमस्मात्तु पञ्चाशैक्षं तु पञ्चकात् ॥ २.७० ॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nava cittāni tat ṣaṇṇāṃ nivṛtāt sapta tat tathā /
> caturbhyaḥ śaikṣam asmāt tu pañcāśaikṣaṃ tu pañcakāt // 2.70 //

The opening completes *śubhāt punaḥ*, “from wholesome [formless mind],
however,” in 2.69. Sandhi joins *pañca aśaikṣam* in the display; the
words remain separate syntactically. The running commentary at 105.08–23
explains this verse. Beyond-training successors are stated in the first
clause of 2.71, immediately afterward.

*Ṣaṇṇām* is morphologically genitive, not an ablative. The Bhāṣya's
explicit *svebhyas tribhyaḥ ...* resolves the compressed relation as
arising from six. Translating “from six” follows that explanation
without changing the grammatical identification of the transmitted word.

## 4. Grammar

The inherited source of *nava cittāni* is wholesome formless mind.
The first *tat* then reverses the direction: that mind arises from six.
The next source is obscured-indeterminate formless mind, from which
seven arise; *tat tathā* says that it too arises from seven. The two
seven-member sets need not be identical.

*Caturbhyaḥ śaikṣam* introduces the trainee kind as result, while
*asmāt tu pañca* makes it the source of five. *Aśaikṣaṃ tu pañcakāt*
identifies beyond-training mind as arising from that immediately
preceding group of five. Its outgoing count is not in this verse.

Arising in immediate succession remains the understood predicate.
The passage alternates sources and targets rather than giving one
undirected list of related minds.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> [From wholesome formless mind arise] nine minds; that [arises]
> from six. From the obscured [mind], seven; that [arises] likewise.
> Trainee [mind arises] from four; from this, five. Beyond-training
> [mind arises] from the group of five.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Wholesome formless mind has nine successor kinds and six predecessor
> kinds. Obscured-indeterminate formless mind has seven successor
> kinds and seven predecessor kinds. Trainee uncontaminated mind
> arises after the wholesome minds of the three realms or trainee
> mind, and can be followed by those four or beyond-training mind.
> Beyond-training mind arises after these same five kinds.

These are relations among kinds in the twelvefold account, not claims
that every occurrence admits all listed alternatives irrespective of
faculties, path stage, or transition context.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Equal numbers of antecedents and successors do not imply reciprocal
> membership. The path kinds also require distinct incoming and
> outgoing relations. Their causal place becomes intelligible through
> these directed connections, not through the labels “trainee” and
> “beyond-training” alone.

This study interpretation does not equate a mind's immediate successor
with a permanent change in the person's entire path status.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

| Term | Controlled rendering | Determination |
|---|---|---|
| ārūpyāvacara | belonging to the formless realm | inherited setting of the first two profiles |
| nivṛta | obscured-indeterminate | formless afflicted kind, not unwholesome |
| śaikṣa | trainee | uncontaminated mind belonging to training |
| aśaikṣa | beyond-training | uncontaminated mind belonging to completed training |
| traidhātuka kuśala | wholesome in the three realms | three distinct realm-bound wholesome kinds |
| pañcaka | group of five | three realm-wholesome kinds plus the two uncontaminated kinds |
| tat tathā | that likewise | equality of count, not necessarily identity of members |

Realm-bound wholesome mind remains contaminated in this classification;
its presence among the predecessors of uncontaminated mind does not
make these classifications synonymous.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Wholesome formless mind.**

```text
Successors (9):
    2 desire-realm afflicted kinds
    form-realm wholesome and obscured-indeterminate
    3 formless-realm kinds
    trainee and beyond-training

Predecessors (6):
    3 formless-realm kinds
    form-realm wholesome
    trainee and beyond-training
```

The nine exclude desire-realm wholesome and the desire- and form-realm
unobscured-indeterminate kinds. The six are specified directly by the
commentary. There is no direct wholesome desire-to-formless or reverse
connection in these lists.

**Obscured-indeterminate formless mind.**

```text
Successors (7):
    3 formless-realm kinds
    form-realm wholesome and obscured-indeterminate
    2 desire-realm afflicted kinds

Predecessors (7):
    3 formless-realm kinds
    form-realm wholesome and unobscured-indeterminate
    desire-realm wholesome and unobscured-indeterminate
```

The source specifies the incoming seven by excluding the three afflicted
kinds in the lower realms and the two uncontaminated kinds. Thus the
seven/seven count is not a symmetric edge-list. Several predecessor
connections were explained as rebirth-linking in the preceding studies;
the present prose does not restate all contextual qualifications.

**Uncontaminated kinds.**

| Focal kind | Predecessors | Successors stated in this verse |
|---|---|---|
| Trainee | Three realm-wholesome kinds and trainee: 4 | The same four plus beyond-training: 5 |
| Beyond-training | Three realm-wholesome kinds, trainee, and beyond-training: 5 | Deferred to 2.71 |

The following clause of 2.71 gives four successors for beyond-training:
the three realm-wholesome kinds and beyond-training itself. That forward
reference clarifies the immediate asymmetry with trainee mind, but is
not silently included as literal content of 2.70.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya control.** The first-pass study's title and analysis emphasized
path mind while leaving the formless profiles largely unexpanded. This
study restores both profiles and their exact members. In particular,
*tathā* repeats the seven count, not its member set.

The research Bhāṣya incorporates the first clause of 2.71 and calls
the transition to beyond-training “irreversible.” The immediate list
only excludes a direct beyond-training-to-trainee successor. It does
not, by itself, establish a general doctrine that every later regression
is impossible; earlier local discussions explicitly considered decline
from arhatship. Mind-kind succession and a person's enduring path
status must therefore remain distinct.

The running transcription includes *ārūpyāvacarātṛ kukśalāt* and repeated
*kukśala*. The surrounding enumeration makes “wholesome formless mind”
clear. These irregularities do not warrant inventing an additional kind.
The reading of *ṣaṇṇām* remains grammatically marked as genitive, with
its intended relation clarified by prose rather than silently emended.

**Organon interpretation.** The passage sharpens what it means to know
a relation rather than a count. Seven predecessors and seven successors
can differ in content. Likewise, a transition between two path-mind
kinds is a local relation whose wider implications require further
argument. Learning the system includes learning those limits.

**Review status.** Provisional polished study checked against the research
kārikā, research Bhāṣya, and running Sanskrit at 105.07–26, with the
2.71 continuation explicitly distinguished. Original witnesses remain
unchanged. Next is VAK 2.71: the final outgoing count and the expansion
