# VAK_7.28 — The Buddha's Eighteen Distinctive Qualities

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अष्टादशावेणिकास्तु बुद्धधर्मा बलादयः ।
स्थानास्थाने दश ज्ञानानि अष्टौ कर्मफले नव ॥ ७.२८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
aṣṭādaśāveṇikās tu buddhadharmā balādayaḥ |
sthānāsthāne daśa jñānāni aṣṭau karmaphale nava || 7.28 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness, with a word
space before `tu`. The final `nava`, “nine,” belongs to
the construction continued in VAK 7.29. It does not revise
the eightfold count immediately before it.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis


`sthāna + asthāna` yields `sthānāsthāna`.
The commentary specifies `karmaphala` here through
`karmavipāka`, karmic maturation.

## 4. Grammar

**The eighteen and their scope.** `Āveṇikāḥ` and `balādayaḥ`
qualify `buddhadharmāḥ`. The Bhāṣya supplies the decisive
restriction: these enter cultivation at the Blessed Buddha's
exhaustion-knowledge alone, not another practitioner's.
`Balādayaḥ` introduces an enumeration, not an indefinite
“powers and anything else.”

**Locative field and nominal constitution.** The verse's
`sthānāsthāne` and `karmaphale` identify the fields of the
powers under discussion. The prose expands the first
clause as `sthānāsthānajñānabalaṃ daśa jñānāni`: the
knowledge-power concerning possibility and impossibility
is the ten knowledges. The singular power and plural
knowledge categories belong to different levels of the
classification; they do not require ten present events.

**The explicit subtraction.** In the second clause supply
`jñānāni` with `aṣṭau`. The prose's `nirodhamārgajñāne
hitvā` uses an accusative dual with the absolutive “excluding”:
excluding cessation-knowledge and Path-knowledge.

**The open ending.** `Nava` carries forward into the next
verse's fields. It is neither an alternative karmic count
nor a third fully specified power within this verse alone.

**Close construe.** The unshared Buddha-qualities, beginning
with the powers, are eighteen. Concerning possibility and
impossibility, [the knowledge-power is] ten knowledges;
concerning karmic result, eight; nine [in the following fields].

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The distinctive qualities of a Buddha are eighteen,
> beginning with the powers. Concerning possibility and
> impossibility there are ten knowledges; concerning the
> result of action, eight. Nine [in the fields continued next].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Eighteen qualities enter cultivation at the Blessed
> Buddha's exhaustion-knowledge alone, not at another
> practitioner's: ten powers, four fearlessnesses, three
> establishments of mindfulness, and great compassion.
> “Distinctive” means unshared.
>
> Among the powers, the power of knowledge concerning
> possibility and impossibility comprises all ten knowledge
> categories. The power of knowledge concerning karmic
> maturation comprises eight, excluding cessation-knowledge
> and Path-knowledge. The count of nine begins the next
> group, whose fields are stated in the following verse.

Source: Pradhan 411|07–411|17. The bounded continuation
at 411|18–20 confirms the attachment of `nava`; its full
power-distribution belongs to VAK 7.29.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The general cultivation account now receives a Buddha-
> specific determination. Eighteen qualities are unshared
> in the stated sense, yet the first powers are analyzed
> through the knowledge categories already established.
> Their fields differ, and the admissible knowledge range
> differs accordingly: ten for possibility and impossibility,
> eight for karmic maturation.

The distinctive powers are not established merely by
possessing the same numbered knowledge categories. The
passage classifies Buddha-powers through those categories;
it does not give a converse rule making every possessor
of ten knowledges a Buddha. The account of what makes
these knowledges powers requires the ensuing discussion.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Āveṇika` — distinctive / unshared.** Explicitly glossed
as `asādhāraṇa`. The qualification is not that no other
practitioner has any knowledge, mindfulness, or compassion;
it concerns these specifically enumerated Buddha-qualities.

**`Buddhadharma` — quality of a Buddha.** Here a member of
the eighteenfold inventory. The compound should not be
rendered as the Buddha's teaching in this context.

**`Bala` — power.** The first group comprises ten powers.
The present prose gives the first two powers' knowledge
constitution, without yet fully explaining their distinctive
strength or unrestricted efficacy.

**`Sthānāsthānajñānabala` — power of knowledge of possibility
and impossibility.** “Possible and impossible” is the
working rendering of the field. The passage gives its
tenfold classification; it does not here enumerate particular
possible and impossible cases or supply a modal calculus.

**`Karmavipākajñānabala` — power of knowledge of karmic
maturation.** The prose's more specific designation of
the verse's `karmaphala` power. It supplies eight categories
and the two exclusions explicitly.

**`Vaiśāradya` — fearlessness / assurance.** Four are named
in the inventory. Their individual determinations belong
to the later exposition rather than to this introductory list.

**`Trīṇi smṛtyupasthānāni` — three establishments of mindfulness.**
The Buddha-specific triad. It must not be silently replaced
by the four body/feeling/mind/dharma applications used in
the earlier knowledge classification.

**`Mahākaruṇā` — great compassion.** The single final member
completing eighteen. Its relation to compassion more generally
is reserved for its own later analysis.

## 8. Logical Determination

The introductory inventory is exact:

| Group | Number |
|---|---:|
| Powers | 10 |
| Fearlessnesses | 4 |
| Establishments of mindfulness | 3 |
| Great compassion | 1 |
| Total | 18 |

The two completed power classifications are:

| Power-field | Knowledge-category count | Exclusions |
|---|---:|---|
| Possibility and impossibility | 10 | None |
| Karmic maturation | 8 | Cessation-knowledge and Path-knowledge |

The eight are therefore conventional, dharma, anvaya,
suffering, origination, other-mind, exhaustion, and
non-arising knowledge. This list is obtained by the
source's explicit subtraction from the established ten.
It counts overlapping categories, not mutually exclusive
individual cognitive events.

The exclusions are stated without an accompanying reason
in this short passage. An object-field explanation may
be developed from the preceding chapter analysis, but
must be marked as inference rather than quoted as a
Bhāṣya explanation given here.

Three assertions must remain distinct:

```text
Quality is one of the eighteen unshared Buddha-qualities.
Quality enters cultivation at the Buddha's exhaustion-knowledge.
Knowledge-power is classified through a specified knowledge-category range.
```

None alone licenses the inference that the eighteen are
eighteen new knowledge categories or that the Buddha
actualizes all their constituents simultaneously.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The transition preserves the cultivation question.** The
opening prose recalls the general account of qualities
at exhaustion-knowledge. Its “for all persons” is governed
by that occasion; it is not a claim that every ordinary
person has exhaustion-knowledge. The Buddha-specific
restriction now identifies what is unshared within that
discussion.

**Use this passage's eighteenfold inventory.** The source
itself supplies ten plus four plus three plus one. The
phrase “eighteen distinctive qualities” must be interpreted
through this enumeration, without substituting another
inventory on the strength of the shared title alone.

**Textual caution.** At 411|16 the running source prints
`kamavipākajñānabalamaṣṭī`. The verse's `karmaphale` and
`aṣṭau`, together with the explicit exclusion of two from
ten, secure karmic maturation and the count eight. The
study uses those supported readings without altering
the source witness.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
We now reach the Buddha's qualities through the cultivated
knowledge system already developed. Yoga Vidyā articulates
the field and internal constitution of knowing; Yoga
Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, articulates its relation
to the cultivation through which the quality is established.

The first two powers offer a concrete distinction for
our reconstruction: knowing possibility and impossibility,
and knowing karmic maturation. These fields must remain
distinct even where they employ overlapping knowledge
categories. Their relation is richer than a count of
available items, and cannot be replaced by a generic
notion of superior intelligence.

The source also keeps the Buddha's unshared determination
explicit. Our Yoga-system interpretation can investigate
the relation of knowledge, cultivated quality, and power
without claiming that any arrangement of ten ordinary
competencies reproduces Buddhahood. The coming verses
must determine the powers more fully before that relation
is formalized beyond the present classification.
