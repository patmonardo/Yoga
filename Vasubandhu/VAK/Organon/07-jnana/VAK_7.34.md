# VAK_7.34 — Buddha-Equality through Cause, Fruit, and Beneficence

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
संभारधर्मकायाभ्यां जगतश्चार्थचर्यया ।
समता सर्वबुद्धानां नायुर्जातिप्रमाणतः ॥ ७.३४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
saṃbhāradharmakāyābhyāṃ jagataś cārthacaryayā |
samatā sarvabuddhānāṃ nāyurjātipramāṇataḥ || 7.34 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness, with its
editorial compound-hyphens removed. The opening question
asks whether all Buddhas are alike in every respect.
The answer is no. The verse specifies the grounds of
equality and difference; the following prose develops
the conclusion of the eighteen distinctive qualities.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
saṃbhāra-dharmakāyābhyām | jagataḥ | ca | artha-caryayā |
samatā | sarva-buddhānām | na | āyus-jāti-pramāṇataḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `saṃbhāradharmakāyābhyām` | instrumental dual coordinated compound | through accumulation and Dharma-body |
| `jagataḥ` | genitive singular neuter | of / for the world |
| `arthacaryayā` | instrumental singular feminine | through activity for welfare |
| `samatā` | nominative singular feminine | equality |
| `sarvabuddhānām` | genitive plural masculine | of all Buddhas |
| `na` | negation | not |
| `āyurjātipramāṇataḥ` | compound with adverbial `-tas` | with respect to lifespan, birth, and measure |

`Jagataḥ + ca` appears as `jagataś ca`; `ca + arthacaryayā`
yields `cārthacaryayā`. `Na + āyus` yields `nāyus-`, with
the following compound form `āyurjāti-`.

## 4. Grammar

**Two plus one grounds.** The instrumental dual coordinates
`saṃbhāra` and `dharmakāya`; the separate instrumental
`arthacaryayā` adds the third ground. The Bhāṣya confirms
`tribhiḥ kāraṇaiḥ`, for three reasons. It is not a single
“accumulation-Dharma-body” entity.

**Welfare of the world.** `Jagataḥ` belongs with `arthacaryā`.
The prose restates this as `arthacaryayā ... lokasya`.
`Artha` here means welfare or benefit, not a lexical
meaning abstracted from its context.

**The negative clause.** Supply `samatā` after `na`.
Equality is denied with respect to the listed conditions,
not denied absolutely. The commentary expands birth into
social birth and clan, adding `gotra` in its explanation.

**Close construe.** There is equality of all Buddhas through
accumulation, Dharma-body, and activity for the world's
welfare; not with respect to lifespan, birth, and bodily
measure.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> All Buddhas are equal through accumulation and Dharma-body,
> and through activity for the welfare of the world; not
> with respect to lifespan, birth, or bodily measure.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> All Buddhas are equal in the complete accumulation of
> merit and knowledge, the perfection of the Dharma-body,
> and activity for the world's welfare. They differ in
> lifespan, social birth, clan, and bodily size according
> to the time: longer or shorter lives, kṣatriya or brāhmaṇa
> birth, clans such as Kāśyapa or Gautama, and smaller or
> larger bodily measure.
>
> Reflection on their excellence of cause, fruit, and
> beneficence can produce intense love and reverence toward
> the Buddhas. The commentary unfolds these excellences
> into further groups, then praises the Buddhas as an
> unsurpassed field of merit and closes the account of
> their eighteen distinctive qualities.

Source: Pradhan 415|11–416|17. The full closing passage,
including its devotional argument, belongs to this study.
Clan is a Bhāṣya expansion, not an inserted kārikā word.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Equality is specified through completed preparation,
> perfected constitution, and beneficent activity. Difference
> in lifespan, birth, clan, or bodily measure does not
> contradict that equality, because the predicates concern
> different respects. The shared perfection is expressed
> through cause, fruit, and benefit to others, while the
> differences remain explicitly acknowledged.

Equality of completed accumulation does not by itself
assert an identical sequence of individual biographical
events. Likewise, equality in welfare-activity does not
erase different circumstances of teaching and embodiment.
The source states the grounds of equality; it does not
collapse all Buddhas into one numerically identical person.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Saṃbhāra`.** Accumulation, here the complete accumulation
of merit and knowledge. Its development as cause-excellence
includes completeness, long duration, continuity, and
respectful application.

**`Dharmakāyapariniṣpatti`.** Perfection of the Dharma-body.
“Perfected Dharma-constitution” is a contextual study gloss.
The passage should not be made to teach a later three-body
system merely through the occurrence of `dharmakāya`.

**`Arthacaryā`.** Activity for welfare. Its beneficiary
is the world; the expanded account calls this beneficence,
`upakāra`.

**`Hetusaṃpad`, `phalasaṃpad`, `upakārasaṃpad`.** Excellence
of cause, fruit, and beneficence. These organize the
extended reflection, not three additional members of
the eighteen-quality inventory.

**`Jāti` and `gotra`.** Social birth and clan in this local
explanation. The examples fix their scope; `gotra` should
not be replaced here by the technical disposition-sense
found in other path discussions.

**`Anupadiṣṭajñāna`, `sarvatrajñāna`, `sarvathājñāna`,
`ayatnajñāna`.** Knowledge not instructed by another,
knowledge everywhere, knowledge in every respect, and
effortless knowledge. These four specify knowledge-excellence;
effortlessness at this stage does not negate the long
and continuous preparation named under cause.

**`Puṇyakṣetra`.** Field of merit. The closing praise
connects the Buddhas' qualities with the fruitfulness
of confidence and acts directed toward them.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The verse's comparison.**

| Equality | Difference |
|---|---|
| Complete merit-and-knowledge accumulation | Lifespan |
| Perfected Dharma-body | Social birth and clan |
| Activity for the world's welfare | Bodily measure according to the time |

These are distinct sets of respects. The table does not
claim a one-to-one causal correspondence between its rows.

**The expanded excellences.**

| Group | Fourfold articulation |
|---|---|
| Cause | Practice of all merit-and-knowledge accumulations; long practice; uninterrupted practice; respectful practice |
| Fruit | Knowledge; abandonment; effective power; material body |
| Knowledge | Uninstructed; everywhere; in every respect; effortless |
| Abandonment | All afflictions; definitive abandonment; together with residual traces; all obstructions to concentrations and attainments |
| Material body | Major marks; subsidiary marks; strength; adamantine firmness of bones |

Effective power also has four heads. The prose describes
mastery of creation, transformation, and determination
of external objects; mastery concerning relinquishing
and sustaining life; a compressed group concerning movement
through obstruction or space, distant and swift movement,
and entry involving small and large; and varied distinctive
marvelous qualities. Several compounds are damaged. These
are a cautious account of the named heads, not a reconstructed
technical procedure.

Beneficence is fourfold, expressed in two ways. The
compressed first formulation is naturally read as complete
release from the three bad destinies and from saṃsāra's
suffering; the alternative is establishment in the three
vehicles and in a good destiny. The three-plus-one structure
explains the count in each formulation. The individual
destinies and vehicles are not enumerated in this sentence.

**The devotional conclusion.** The Bhāṣya calls this only
a summary: detailed differentiation is limitless, and
only Buddhas could know and express the whole, with life
sustained for innumerable eons. It depicts Tathāgatas as
great treasuries of extraordinary qualities, knowledge,
power, and beneficence.

It contrasts those who disregard the Buddha and Dharma
despite hearing these qualities with the wise person's
wholehearted commitment. It then attributes to resolute
confidence the overcoming of accumulated evil whose
maturation is not fixed, surpassing divine and human
prosperity, and orientation toward nirvāṇa. The restriction
to unfixed maturation is explicit; it must not be dropped
to produce an unrestricted claim that confidence cancels
all karmic results.

The concluding scriptural quotation associates acts of
honor toward the Victor with heavenly fruit and the
deathless goal. Its opening is damaged, so a full restored
verse is not printed here. The source then explicitly
closes the eighteen distinctive Buddha-dharmas.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The closing praise is part of the argument.** The text
moves from analysis of excellences to the love and reverence
that reflection on them can produce, and then to their
significance as a field of merit. Removing this passage
would omit the source's stated practical purpose. These
are attributed Buddhist doctrinal claims, not independently
verified predictions about any present reader's karmic future.

**Equality does not require identical bodily dimensions.**
The fruit catalogue includes excellence of the material
body, while the verse permits variation in bodily measure.
The source therefore distinguishes bodily excellence from
uniform size. The distinction follows its actual predicates,
rather than requiring a split between a wholly immaterial
equality and an irrelevant body.

**Textual limits.** The running source has malformed
compounds in the power catalogue, the field-of-merit
explanation, and the final quotation. The research excerpt
uses an ellipsis over much of the devotional conclusion.
This study includes its readable argument while leaving
damaged details unexpanded. The three principal excellences
and the clear fourfold lists do not depend on repairing
those passages.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Our Yoga system can articulate this equality through
formation, attained constitution, and activity for others.
Yoga Vidyā determines the knowledge and completed Dharma-
constitution; Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion,
articulates continuous preparation and beneficent undertaking.
Neither side alone exhausts the threefold account.

The respect-relative comparison is equally important.
Shared completion does not erase differences of time,
embodiment, or biography. Our reconstruction must specify
what is equal and what differs, instead of letting a
general word such as “same” suppress those determinations.
Here meaning is carried by the relations among cause,
fruit, and welfare-activity, with the concrete differences
preserved.

The devotional conclusion also belongs to the project
reading: understanding the qualities can change one's
orientation toward their realization and toward teaching.
That interpretive contact should retain the source's
language of reverence and merit, without recasting its
entire purpose as an abstract technical architecture.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The equality/difference account and full natural conclusion
have been read through the explicit closure at 416|17.
The compressed beneficence and effective-power catalogues,
and damaged closing quotation, retain stated limits.
