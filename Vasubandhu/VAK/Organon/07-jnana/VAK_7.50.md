# VAK_7.50 — Construction-Minds: Dhyāna Results and Immediate Succession

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
यथाक्रमं ध्यानफलं द्वे यावत् पञ्च नोर्ध्वजम् ।
तल्लाभो ध्यानवत् शुद्धात्तत्स्वतश्च ततो ऽपि ते ॥ ७.५० ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
yathākramaṃ dhyānaphalaṃ dve yāvat pañca nordhvajam |
tallābho dhyānavat śuddhāttatsvataśca tato 'pi te || 7.50 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The
Devanāgarī renders the same reading. The prose expands
the compressed pronouns and specifies both directions
of immediate succession.

`svataḥ + ca` yields `svataś ca`; `tataḥ + api` yields
`tato 'pi`. The final dual is resolved by the prose's
explicit pair, not by the fourteenfold count.

## 4. Grammar

**Two through five in order.** `Yathākramam` correlates
the four dhyānas with successive counts: two, three,
four, and five. The singular `dhyānaphalam` expresses
the result relation distributively; the numbers count
the construction-minds resulting from each dhyāna.

**A relational prohibition.** `Na ūrdhvajam` does not
mean that no construction-mind can ever belong to a
higher ground. It means that a construction-mind of a
higher ground is not the result of a lower dhyāna. The
source dhyāna and resulting mind must both be specified.

**Acquisition by comparison.** `Tallābhaḥ` resumes the
construction-minds collectively. `Dhyānavat` says that
their acquisition is like that of dhyāna. The local prose
repeats this comparison without enumerating a fresh set
of acquisition procedures.

**Predecessor relation.** `Śuddhāt tat svataś ca` supplies
an understood verb of arising: the construction-mind
arises from pure dhyāna or from its own kind. The prose
uses `anantaram`, “immediately after,” and excludes
other predecessors. “From itself” must not be read as
a single event causing its own prior existence.

**Successor relation.** `Tato 'pi te` reverses the direction:
from the construction-mind those two may arise. The prose
again names pure dhyāna and construction-mind and excludes
anything else. This states allowed categories, not the
simultaneous arising of both successors.

**Close construe.** In order, two up to five [construction-
minds] are dhyāna results; no higher-ground one [is the
result of a lower dhyāna]. Their acquisition is as with
dhyāna. From pure dhyāna and from its own kind that
construction-mind arises; from it, in turn, those two arise.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In order, two up to five are results of dhyāna; none
> belongs to a higher ground [than its source dhyāna].
> Their acquisition is like that of dhyāna. That mind
> arises from pure dhyāna and from its own kind; from
> it, in turn, those two arise.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The first, second, third, and fourth dhyānas have
> respectively two, three, four, and five construction-
> minds as their results. These belong to the source
> dhyāna's own ground or lower grounds. A lower dhyāna
> has no higher-ground construction-mind as its result.
>
> Their acquisition is like the acquisition of dhyāna.
> A construction-mind arises immediately after pure dhyāna
> or another construction-mind, and not after anything
> else. Immediately after it, likewise, pure dhyāna or
> another construction-mind arises, and nothing else.
> One established in a result of samādhi must re-enter
> samādhi before emerging from it.

Source: Pradhan 426|11–427|05, local Bhāṣya and running
source. The final sentence follows the clear argumentative
context of a mechanically damaged transmitted expression.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A construction-mind is determined both by the dhyāna
> of which it is a result and by the ground to which
> it belongs. Its occurrence also requires a specified
> immediate cognitive context. The result of concentration
> remains connected to concentration through the conditions
> of entry and return.

The count, acquisition comparison, and succession rules
answer different questions. Being included in the fourteenfold
inventory is not by itself a statement that a particular
construction-mind can arise after any arbitrary cognition.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dhyānaphala`.** Result of dhyāna. This is a relation
between a source attainment and a resulting construction-
mind, not an assertion that the two belong to the same
ground in every case.

**`Svabhūmika / adharabhūmika`.** Belonging to the same
or a lower ground, relative to the source dhyāna. These
prose expressions determine the allowed result range.

**`Ūrdhvaja`.** Higher-ground in the prohibited source–
result relation. The prose expands the abbreviated verse
with `ūrdhvabhūmikaṃ nirmāṇacittam` and `adharadhyānaphalam`.
It does not concern upward physical movement.

**`Lābha`.** Acquisition. Here the source directs the
reader to the dhyāna acquisition model. It does not
identify acquisition with each subsequent occurrence
of a construction-mind.

**`Śuddhaka-dhyāna`.** Pure dhyāna, the prose expansion
of `śuddhāt`. “Pure” is retained as the technical qualifier;
it should not be silently replaced with “final liberation”
or equated with every conceivable meditative state.

**`Anantaram`.** Immediately after. This determines
the succession relation. It is more specific than a
general claim that concentration at some earlier time
causally contributed to the capacity.

**`Vyutthāna`.** Emergence from the meditative condition.
The question is whether emergence may occur directly
from the construction-mind; the answer requires returning
to samādhi before emergence.

**`Samādhiphalastha`.** Established in a result of samādhi.
This description connects the transition restriction
with the preceding result analysis. It does not identify
the construction-mind with the source samādhi itself.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The fourteenfold distribution.**

| Source dhyāna | Grounds of its resulting construction-minds | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| First | Desire domain, first dhyāna | 2 |
| Second | Desire domain, first and second dhyānas | 3 |
| Third | Desire domain, first through third dhyānas | 4 |
| Fourth | Desire domain, first through fourth dhyānas | 5 |
| Total | Source–result combinations | 14 |

The fourteen are distinguished through source and result
ground together. Counting only the possible result grounds
would give five and lose the difference between, for
example, a desire-domain result of the first dhyāna
and one of the fourth.

The prohibition is directional: a lower source does
not yield a higher-ground result. This is not yet the
product-level matching rule of VAK 7.51. Here the relation
is source dhyāna to construction-mind; the next verse
will relate a construction-mind to its manifestation.

**A distinction within one product domain.** The prose
adds that a desire-domain construction resulting from
the second and subsequent dhyānas differs from the one
associated with the first. The phrase containing `gatitaḥ`
is compressed. The source makes a distinction of efficacy
or manner, but its exact dimension remains provisional;
a precise speed hierarchy is not established here.

**Immediate predecessor and successor classes.**

| Relation to a construction-mind | Classes admitted here |
|---|---|
| Immediate predecessor | Pure dhyāna or construction-mind |
| Immediate successor | Pure dhyāna or construction-mind |

The repeated exclusions in the prose make these necessary
class restrictions. They do not establish that every
one of the fourteen minds may immediately follow every
other one without further ground or sequence constraints.
A category-level table is not a complete transition matrix
for all individual states.

```text
Pure dhyāna → Construction-mind
Construction-mind → Construction-mind
Construction-mind → Pure dhyāna

Before emergence:
    Construction-mind → return to samādhi → emergence
```

The same-category arrow means another occurrence of a
construction-mind. It does not mean persistence of one
unchanging cognition or production of itself without
a preceding event.

**The emergence question.** The prose asks whether one
could emerge directly from construction-mind. The reply
denies this and gives the predecessor and successor rules.
Its reason is that one abiding in a result of samādhi
must enter samādhi again before emerging. This makes
the return part of the account, rather than leaving
the result as an independently detachable state.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Control of formal reconstruction.** The local Bhāṣya
analysis expresses transition eligibility with “if and
only if.” That is too strong if applied to arbitrary
individual states: the text excludes other categories,
but does not list every permitted pair of grounds or
every additional condition. The official seed therefore
records admitted classes and exclusions without claiming
a complete sufficient-condition model.

Likewise, `svataḥ` is resolved through the prose as
succession from a construction-mind. “Self-reproduction”
would obscure the temporal relation and risk making one
cognition its own cause.

**Witness cautions.** The running source and local extract
retain `śuddhakāddhacyānād`, with a corrupt intrusion
around `dhyānād`, and `nirmāṇacittadvā`. The closing
`samādhiphalasthitasvāpraviśya` also contains an irregular
junction. The surrounding sentences secure pure dhyāna,
construction-mind succession, and re-entry before emergence;
the damaged strings are not presented as clean Sanskrit.
The nuance of `gatitaḥ` in the comparative sentence
remains separately marked. Originals are unchanged.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the source–result distinctions
and the ground structure of the fourteenfold count.
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns acquisition,
exercise, and the conditions of entering and leaving
the relevant cognitive operation.

For Organon, an available capacity is not adequately
represented by a bare possession flag. Its provenance
and immediate operating context may matter. This passage
supplies a source-controlled example: the same result
ground appears under different source dhyānas, while
the immediate succession of construction-minds remains
restricted to specified cognitive classes.

A software transition model can use this structural
analogy, provided it distinguishes source-derived necessary
constraints from implementation choices that complete
the model. The Bhāṣya supplies neither a software runtime
nor a full pairwise transition table.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The fourteenfold distribution, prohibition on higher-ground
results of lower dhyānas, acquisition comparison, two-sided
immediate succession restrictions, and return-before-
emergence rule have been checked against both local
witnesses. Damaged wording and the comparative `gatitaḥ`
remain for textual review.
