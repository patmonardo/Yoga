# VAK_7.19 — Progressive Possession of Knowledge

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
एकज्ञानान्वितो रागी प्रथमेऽनास्रवक्षणे ।
द्वितीये त्रिभिरूर्ध्वस्तु चतुर्ष्वेकैकवृद्धिमान् ॥ ७.१९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ekajñānānvito rāgī prathame 'nāsravakṣaṇe |
dvitīye tribhir ūrdhvas tu caturṣv ekaikavṛddhimān || 7.19 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The Bhāṣya now
asks who possesses how many knowledges. This changes the inquiry
from the classification of possible knowledge to the possession
of knowledge by a practitioner at a particular Path-moment.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
eka-jñāna-anvitaḥ | rāgī | prathame | anāsrava-kṣaṇe |
dvitīye | tribhiḥ | ūrdhvaḥ | tu | caturṣu | eka-eka-vṛddhimān
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ekajñānānvitaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | endowed with one knowledge |
| `rāgī` | nominative singular masculine of `rāgin` | one with attachment; here not detached from the desire domain |
| `prathame` | locative singular masculine adjective | in the first |
| `anāsravakṣaṇe` | locative singular masculine compound | uncontaminated moment |
| `dvitīye` | locative singular masculine adjective | in the second [moment] |
| `tribhiḥ` | instrumental plural numeral | with three [knowledges] |
| `ūrdhvaḥ` | nominative singular masculine adjective, compressed contextual usage | further advanced, subsequently |
| `tu` | connective particle | then, however |
| `caturṣu` | locative plural numeral | at four [further moments] |
| `ekaikavṛddhimān` | nominative singular masculine possessive adjective | having an increase of one at each |

`Jñāna + anvita` gives `jñānānvita`; this is possession-language,
not the technical name `anvayajñāna`. `Eka + eka` gives the
distributive `ekaika`. The prose resolves `ūrdhvaḥ` through
`ataḥ param`, after this, without requiring its surface form
to be silently replaced by an adverb.

## 4. Grammar

**The qualified practitioner.** `Rāgī` is the subject of
`ekajñānānvitaḥ`. The Bhāṣya specifies `kāmāvītarāga`, one
not detached from the desire domain. The count therefore is
not asserted indiscriminately of every entrant into Seeing.

**First and second moments.** `Prathame anāsravakṣaṇe`
specifies the first occurrence; `dvitīye` supplies the next
with `kṣaṇe` understood. For `tribhiḥ`, supply `jñānaiḥ`
and the possession predicate. The commentary uses
`samanvāgataḥ`, possessed of, throughout.

**Four increases.** `Caturṣu` qualifies understood `kṣaṇeṣu`.
`Ekaikavṛddhimān` predicates one addition at each of four
specified later moments, not an addition at every succeeding
moment. The prose names suffering-anvaya-knowledge, followed
by origination-, cessation-, and Path-dharma-knowledge.

**Close construe.** The practitioner still attached [is]
endowed with one knowledge in the first uncontaminated moment;
in the second, with three. Further advanced, at four [moments]
he has an increase of one at each.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The practitioner still attached possesses one knowledge in
> the first uncontaminated moment; in the second, three.
> Subsequently, at four moments, he gains one more at each.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A practitioner not previously detached from the desire domain
> possesses conventional knowledge alone at the first
> uncontaminated moment, the acceptance of dharma-knowledge
> concerning suffering. At suffering-dharma-knowledge, the
> second moment, three knowledge-categories are possessed:
> conventional, dharma, and suffering knowledge. Thereafter
> anvaya-knowledge is added at suffering-anvaya-knowledge;
> origination-, cessation-, and Path-knowledge are added at
> their respective dharma-knowledge moments. At Path-dharma-
> knowledge the practitioner possesses seven. The previously
> detached practitioner is described as possessing other-mind
> knowledge in addition throughout this sequence.

This follows Pradhan 405|07–405|17. The prose begins with the
ordinary practitioner's conventional knowledge and the detached
case's additional other-mind knowledge, then specifies the noble
sequence. The question of which knowledges are cultivated is
introduced only afterward, for VAK 7.20.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The knowledge-system becomes possessed through a determinate
> sequence. Entry into uncontaminated Seeing does not itself
> add a knowledge-category, because its first act is acceptance.
> The next act introduces two classifications of one knowledge;
> later moments add further categories until seven are possessed.
> Prior detachment changes the baseline through the additional
> possession of other-mind knowledge.

The count concerns the practitioner's possession, not the number
of simultaneous cognitions. A single present cognition can
instantiate both dharma-knowledge and suffering-knowledge, while
conventional knowledge remains possessed without being the
present Path-cognition. Possession must also remain distinct
from cultivation and unrestricted immediate availability.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Anvita` / `samanvāgata` — endowed with / possessed of.** The
kārikā and prose expressions for the governing relation. The
verse counts knowledge-categories belonging to the continuum
at a specified stage, not isolated mental events alone.

**`Rāgī` / `kāmāvītarāga` — attached / not detached from the
desire domain.** The literal marker and its contextual expansion.
It does not say that the uncontaminated acceptance itself is
an afflicted cognition associated with passion.

**`Duḥkhadharmajñānakṣānti` — acceptance of dharma-knowledge
concerning suffering.** The first uncontaminated moment. VAK
7.01 excluded uncontaminated acceptances from `jñāna`; that
classification now explains why the present acceptance does
not increase the count of possessed knowledge-categories.

**`Duḥkhadharmajñāna` — suffering-dharma-knowledge.** The second
moment. Its two classifications add dharma-knowledge and
suffering-knowledge without requiring two simultaneous acts.

**`Ekaikajñānavṛddhi` — one-knowledge increase at each specified
moment.** The prose expansion of `ekaikavṛddhimān`. Later
acceptances and knowledge-moments that introduce no new
category do not automatically add to this count.

**`Vītarāga` — detached practitioner.** The contrasting case
in the local account has other-mind knowledge in addition.
That possession does not mean other-mind knowledge occurs
within Seeing: VAK 7.06 explicitly excluded such occurrence.
The statement is retained as the source's possession account,
not generalized into a claim that detachment makes every
exercise of other-mind knowledge automatic.

## 8. Logical Determination

The prose names the decisive moments. Their numbers follow
the established sixteen-moment direct-realization sequence:

| Moment | Present Path-cognition | New knowledge-categories | Total for the non-detached case |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Suffering-dharma acceptance | None; conventional knowledge is already possessed | 1 |
| 2 | Suffering-dharma knowledge | Dharma and suffering | 3 |
| 4 | Suffering-anvaya knowledge | Anvaya | 4 |
| 6 | Origination-dharma knowledge | Origination | 5 |
| 10 | Cessation-dharma knowledge | Cessation | 6 |
| 14 | Path-dharma knowledge | Path | 7 |

The possessed set by moment fourteen is:

```text
Conventional, Dharma, Anvaya,
Suffering, Origination, Cessation, Path
```

The contrasting detached case adds other-mind knowledge at
each stage, yielding counts 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8 at these
same checkpoints. This is the Bhāṣya's additional-possession
rule applied to the table, not an additional series quoted
verbatim from it.

```text
PossessedKnowledgeCategories(practitioner, moment)
    differs from
PresentCognition(practitioner, moment)
```

At moment two, one event adds two category memberships.
At moment one, an uncontaminated event adds none. These
results depend on the systematic distinctions already
established; they cannot be recovered by counting events
or by treating uncontaminated status as sufficient for
knowledge-status.

The later additions are cumulative in the described sequence.
They do not constitute a universal rule that knowledge-
possession can never be relinquished under other conditions.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The earlier taxonomy becomes a possession account.** The
first eighteen studies established what the knowledges are
and their relations. This verse asks which of them belong
to a practitioner at each specified stage. The shift introduces
the temporal sequence without replacing the classificatory
grounds.

**Purity and knowledge remain distinct.** The first moment
already belongs to the uncontaminated Path, but its acceptance
is not counted as knowledge. This applies the chapter's
opening distinction rather than indicating a failed or
merely conventional entry into Seeing.

**Possession is not present performance.** The detached
practitioner's additional other-mind knowledge is possessed
even though other-mind knowledge does not occur in Seeing.
Keeping these relations separate makes the commentary
consistent with its earlier actualization restrictions.

**Textual caution.** The raw e-text joins `rāgīprathame` and
has `catuṣrvekaika-` in the verse quotation. The local study
provides the readable separation and `caturṣv ekaika-`.
The prose explicitly names the four additions, so their
identification does not depend on emending that damaged
string without contextual control.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our
Yoga system now receives a concrete account of the practitioner's
development through acquired determinations. Yoga Vidyā
articulates the knowledge-categories and the distinct grounds
by which one act can have more than one meaning. Yoga Technē,
its kriyā–karma companion, develops the ordered undertaking
through which these determinations become possessed.

The difference between entering the Path, possessing knowledge,
and exercising a particular cognition becomes practically
decisive. An actual transition can occur before a new knowledge-
category is gained; another single act can bring two categories
into possession. Our reconstruction must therefore describe
the transformation of the practitioner through more than a
count of experiences or statements learned.

Vidyā and Technē meet in the relation between determinate
acts and the capacity acquired through them. The source
provides a precise sequence and a prior-detachment qualification.
Its further account of cultivation will add another relation
rather than merely repeat this possession table.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The
named additions and prior-detachment contrast are explicit.
Moment numbers are supplied from the established Path sequence;
the cultivation question remains for VAK 7.20.
