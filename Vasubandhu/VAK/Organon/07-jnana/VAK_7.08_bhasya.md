# VAK_7.08 Bhāṣya — Seven Grounds for the Ten Knowledges

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> svabhāvapratipakṣābhyām ākārākāragocarāt |
> prayogakṛtakṛtyatvahetūpacayato daśa || 7.8 ||

> By intrinsic nature and counteraction, by aspect and aspect together with
> object-range, by preparation, the state of having accomplished what had
> to be done, and accumulation of causes, [the knowledges are] ten.

The Bhāṣya asks why, given the forms already shown, exactly ten are
established, then distributes seven grounds across the ten names. `Kila`
marks the seven as reported. 7.09 will qualify the counteraction map.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.08.md`](./VAK_7.08.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> [The knowledges] are established as ten. Through seven grounds, it is said.
>
> By intrinsic nature: conventional knowledge, because it is not knowledge
> of ultimate meaning.
>
> By counteraction: dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge, because they are
> counteragents with respect to the desire domain and the higher domains.
>
> By aspect: suffering-knowledge and origination-knowledge, because the
> object-support is not different.
>
> By aspect and object-support: cessation-knowledge and Path-knowledge.
>
> By preparatory application: knowledge of another mind. [It] also knows
> mental factors; [it is so named] because, having made a preparation for
> the sake of knowing another mind, it is obtained from that.
>
> By having accomplished what had to be done: exhaustion-knowledge, [arising]
> in a continuum whose task has been accomplished.
>
> By accumulation of causes: non-arising-knowledge, because it has all
> uncontaminated [dharmas] as causes.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
why ten, given the forms already shown?

    → 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 designations
```

The arithmetic counts names under grounds. It does not count ten disjoint
events. Earlier overlaps (7.01–7.05) stay intact.

## 5. Seven Grounds, Ten Names

| Ground | Designations | Stated reason |
|---|---|---|
| `svabhāva` | conventional | not ultimate-meaning knowledge |
| `pratipakṣa` | dharma, anvaya | desire-domain / higher-domain counteraction |
| `ākāra` | suffering, origination | object-support not different |
| `ākāra` + `ālambana` | cessation, Path | both differ |
| `prayoga` | other-mind | aim of the preparation; caittas also known |
| `kṛtakṛtyatva` | exhaustion | arises in a finished-task continuum |
| `hetūpacaya` | non-arising | all uncontaminated dharmas as causes |

Repeated `ākāra` in the verse is two grounds, not a dittography. Same
object-support with different aspect yields two truth-names. Different
object-support *and* aspect yields the other two.

`Aparamārthajñānatva` does not cancel 7.03: conventional knowledge can take
unconditioned dharmas as objects. Nature ≠ range.

`Pratipakṣa` here is a summary map. 7.09 will qualify it. Do not freeze
“dharma-knowledge counters only the desire domain.”

Paracitta: name from preparatory *aim*, not exclusive object. Classificatory
inclusion under four (7.05) and naming from prayoga are different relations.

Terminal pair keeps two grounds. Do not merge kṣaya and anutpāda into one
“completion.” Causal statement stays as transmitted: all uncontaminated
as causes — not a global causal engine.

## 6. What a Ground Is Allowed to Do

A ground explains why a *name* is established. It does not impose the same
seven slots on every cognition. Preparation does not have to precede every
knowledge in the printed order. `Kila` keeps the scheme reported, not as an
independent invention of this sentence.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- understood predicate: `vyavasthāpyante`;
- seven, not five or six, because `ākāra` splits;
- `gocara` in the verse = `ālambana` in the prose for the fourth ground;
- `prayoga` names by aim;
- `hetūpacaya` specified as `sarvānāsravahetukatva`.

`Prathamataḥ` remains a punctuation issue. Counteraction scope waits for 7.09.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Jñāna does not flow as artha flows. Artha is what is held — object, aim,
meaning. Jñāna is the holding that moves *around* it: nature, counteraction,
aspect, aspect-with-support, preparation, accomplished continuum, causal
basis. Same `ālambana`, two `ākāra` — two knowledges. Meaning is not yet
knowledge; knowledge is not a second stream of meaning. Modern philosophy
usually either collapses them (knowledge as justified meaning) or splits
them so hard that knowing never touches what is meant. The Kośa does neither.
It asks *which relation* makes this holding a distinct knowledge.

`Aparamārthajñāna` is the verse's own Meaning/Knowledge cut: conventional
knowing is knowledge and is not ultimate-artha knowledge. That is why pots
and unconditioned dharmas can both sit in its range (7.02–7.03) without
making it paramārtha.

This is the Grand Synthesis as method, not as a triad: seven questions, ten
names, one field of holding-to-be-true. Hegel does not give these grounds.
Appearance would print ten and stop. The Idea asks why the ten are ten.

Yoga Vidyā asks the ground. Yoga Technē works the counteraction, the prayoga,
the finished task, the causes. They use the whole table. Neither owns
conventional knowledge or the terminal pair alone.
