## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dvi-tri-sāhasraka-asaṃkhya-dṛśaḥ | arhat-khaḍga-daiśikāḥ |
anyat | api | upapattyā | āptam | tad-dṛśyaḥ | na | antarī-bhavaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dvitrisāhasrakāsaṃkhyadṛśaḥ` | nominative plural compound adjective | seeing the two-thousandfold, three-thousandfold, and innumerable world-systems, respectively |
| `arhatkhaḍgadaiśikāḥ` | nominative plural masculine coordinated compound | arhats, rhinoceros-like solitary Buddhas, and the Teacher |
| `anyat` | nominative singular neuter | another [capacity]; others collectively in prose |
| `api` | additive particle | also |
| `upapattyā` | instrumental singular feminine | through birth or mode of existence |
| `āptam` | nominative singular neuter participle | acquired |
| `taddṛśyaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound adjective | visible to that [birth-acquired eye] |
| `antarībhavaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | intermediate being or existence, expanded as `antarābhava` |

`Upapattyā + āptam` yields `upapattyāptam`;
`na + antarībhavaḥ` yields `nāntarībhavaḥ`.
The compressed range compound shares `sāhasraka` across
the first two numerical members. The prose controls
their respective assignment.

**Quoted-verse vocabulary.** `Dūrastham`, `āvṛtam`, and
`sūkṣmam` are accusative singular neuter qualifications
of `rūpam`: distant, covered, and subtle visible form.
`Sarvataḥ` is adverbial, “from every direction.”
`Māṃsacakṣuḥ` is the fleshly eye, the subject of
`na paśyati`. `Yataḥ ... ataḥ` connects the ordinary
limitation to the admission of divine sight. The printed
`divyaṃ dṛgiṣyate` has an irregular agreement or junction;
the broad contrast is clear, but its exact restoration
is not supplied here.

## 4. Grammar

**Respectively, with exertion.** The verse's first compound
pairs three bearers with three ranges. The prose immediately
introduces it with `abhisaṃskāreṇa tu`, “with deliberate
exertion, however.” That condition must enter the study
translation even though the verse abbreviates it.

**Arhat in this contrast.** The prose identifies the
first bearer as a śrāvaka and the second as
`khaṅgaviṣāṇakalpa`. Thus the first term is not a category
that absorbs the Buddha and makes the threefold mapping
redundant. `Daiśika`, instructor or Teacher, is resolved
as the Buddha by the following explanation.

**More than ṛddhi is birth-acquired.** `Anyad api`
answers whether ṛddhi alone is acquired through birth.
The prose supplies four further capacities beginning
with divine hearing. It does not introduce only one
additional kind of vision.

**The antecedent of that.** `Tad-` in `taddṛśyaḥ` refers
to the birth-acquired divine eye explicitly named in
prose. The negation denies visibility to that eye, not
the existence of the intermediate being and not its
visibility to every kind of sight.

**Close construe.** With deliberate exertion, the śrāvaka
arhat, solitary Buddha, and Teacher see the two-thousandfold,
three-thousandfold, and innumerable world-systems respectively.
Other capacities too are acquired through birth. The
intermediate being is not visible to that birth-acquired eye.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Arhats, solitary Buddhas, and the Teacher see respectively
> the two-thousandfold, three-thousandfold, and innumerable
> world-systems. Other capacities too are acquired through
> birth; the intermediate being is not visible to that eye.

**Preceding quotation, close sense.** Because the fleshly
eye does not see distant, covered, and subtle form or
see in every direction, divine sight is admitted. This
renders the contrast without pretending to repair the
quoted verse's final wording.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Without deliberate exertion, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas,
> and Buddhas see respectively the thousandfold,
> two-thousandfold, and three-thousandfold world-systems.
> With deliberate exertion, the corresponding ranges
> are two-thousandfold, three-thousandfold, and innumerable
> world-systems; the Buddha sees as far as he wishes.
>
> Divine hearing and the other members of the fourfold
> group following ṛddhi may also be acquired through
> birth. None of these merely birth-acquired capacities
> receives the designation abhijñā. The intermediate being
> is visible to the abhijñā-eye, not to the birth-acquired
> divine eye.

Source: Pradhan 429|16–430|10, local Bhāṣya checked
against the continuous running source. The world-system
terms are retained as technical scales, not rendered
as literal distances or simple counts of two thousand
and three thousand individual worlds.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A faculty's name does not settle its reach or its
> classificatory status. Range depends on bearer and
> exertion; acquisition through birth differs from the
> cultivated abhijñā designation. An object may fall
> outside one kind of divine vision while remaining
> accessible to another.

The intermediate being is the passage's explicit
object-range distinction. It is not merely a farther
object requiring a larger radius of otherwise identical
perception.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Abhisaṃskāra / anabhisaṃskāra`.** Deliberate exertion
or intensification, and its absence. The contrast modifies
exercise of the faculty. “Without exertion” does not
mean that its original acquisition required no cultivation.

**`Sāhasra / dvisāhasra / trisāhasra`.** The thousandfold,
two-thousandfold, and three-thousandfold world-system
scales. The terminology belongs to the nested cosmological
classification. The present study does not convert these
into modern spatial units.

**`Asaṃkhya / asaṃkhyeya`.** Innumerable. The verse and
prose describe the Buddha's extended range without
providing a finite numeral here. This should not become
a claim of simultaneous perception of every possible object.

**`Khaḍga`.** Abbreviated designation for the rhinoceros-
like solitary Buddha, resolved through the prose's
`khaṅgaviṣāṇakalpa`. “Sword” would miss the referent in
this compound despite other lexical uses of the word.

**`Upapattyāpta`.** Acquired through birth. This causal
provenance is not sufficient for the technical name
abhijñā in the present discussion.

**`Abhijñākhyā`.** Designation as abhijñā. The prose
withholds this designation from the birth-acquired
capacities while admitting their occurrence. Denial of
the name is not denial of the capacity.

**`Antarībhava / antarābhava`.** Intermediate being or
existence between death and rebirth. The verse's compressed
form and the prose's usual form refer to the same local
object of the visibility restriction.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Range and exertion.**

| Bearer | Without deliberate exertion | With deliberate exertion |
|---|---|---|
| Śrāvaka, arhat in the verse's comparison | Thousandfold system | Two-thousandfold system |
| Rhinoceros-like pratyekabuddha in the specified comparison | Two-thousandfold system | Three-thousandfold system |
| Buddha | Three-thousandfold system | Innumerable systems, as desired |

The prose first answers the question of range by saying
that it depends on the kind of eye possessed. The table
records the stated comparison; it is not a warrant to
attribute the faculty to every member of each bearer
class regardless of acquisition.

**The four further birth-acquired capacities.** In the
sixfold order established earlier, the group beginning
with divine hearing comprises hearing, other-mind knowledge,
former-abode recollection, and death-and-rebirth vision.
Together with ṛddhi, these are the five non-exhaustion
capacities. The passage does not add birth-acquired
influx-exhaustion knowledge as a sixth.

**Designation and object constraint.**

| Eye under consideration | Abhijñā designation here | Intermediate being visible |
|---|---|---|
| Birth-acquired divine eye | No | No |
| Divine eye as abhijñā | Yes | Yes |

The second row describes the source's eligible faculty-
type, not an assertion that every intermediate being
is actually seen by every possessor in every situation.
Earlier ground and range restrictions remain relevant.

```text
Admitted birth-acquired capacity
    does not entail
Designation as abhijna

Divine-eye name
    does not alone determine
Visibility of the intermediate being
```

The restriction is local to birth-acquired vision. It
must not be broadened into the claim that no being in
intermediate existence can ever perceive another; the
verse is specifying a faculty's eligibility, not giving
a complete theory of perception in intermediate existence.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Numbered verse versus preceding quotation.** The local
research file groups the fleshly-eye quotation with 7.55.
The running source distinguishes that quotation from
the numbered kārikā. Both are preserved in the Sanskrit
stages, with their status explicit. The quotation explains
a contrast of vision; it does not by itself constitute
an empirical proof of the extraordinary faculty.

**Witness cautions.** The local kārikā prints
`arhatkhaḍgadaiśikāḥ`, while the running source has
`arhatkhaṅgavaiśikāḥ`. The prose's śrāvaka, solitary
Buddha, and Buddha references secure the three referents;
the variation is not silently suppressed. The local
Bhāṣya extract abbreviates part of the range passage
with an ellipsis, so the complete running source controls
that part of the study.

The running text also has `anabhhisaṃskāreṇa`,
`sarvābhhisaṃskāreṇa`, and `lokahātūn`; the extract
regularizes some of these. The quoted `divyaṃ dṛgiṣyate`
remains a separate textual issue. Original files are
unchanged.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the relation between faculty,
object eligibility, and warranted designation. Yoga
Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns acquisition
and the exertion through which a capacity is exercised.
The present verse shows why these must remain related:
a faculty name alone cannot settle either range or status.

For Organon, provenance can affect the admissible object-
field, while exertion can affect range within an acquired
capacity. Those are different relations. A model that
stores only “divine vision available” would lose both
the exertion table and the intermediate-being exclusion.

These are structural project uses of the text, not
claims that its cosmological ranges have been physically
measured or that software provenance reproduces Buddhist
attainment. The study preserves what the source classifies
and what its specific restrictions exclude.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
