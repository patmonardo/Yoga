# VAK_7.36 — Araṇā: Preventing Afflictive Conflict in Others

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
संवृतिज्ञानमरणा ध्यानेऽन्त्येऽकोप्यधर्मणः ।
नृजा अनुत्पन्नकामाप्तसवस्तुक्लेशगोचराः ॥ ७.३६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
saṃvṛtijñānam araṇā dhyāne 'ntye 'kopyadharmaṇaḥ |
nṛjā anutpannakāmāptasavastukleśagocarāḥ || 7.36 ||
```

The Sanskrit preserves the local kārikā witness, including
its final plural-looking `gocarāḥ`. The prose consistently
has singular araṇā as its subject, including `asyā ālambanam`.
The expected agreeing feminine singular would be `gocarā`;
the discrepancy is recorded rather than silently corrected.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
saṃvṛti-jñānam | araṇā | dhyāne | antye | akopya-dharmaṇaḥ |
nṛ-jā | anutpanna-kāma-āpta-savastuka-kleśa-gocarāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `saṃvṛtijñānam` | nominative singular neuter | conventional knowledge |
| `araṇā` | nominative singular feminine | freedom from conflict |
| `dhyāne 'ntye` | locative singular noun and adjective | in the final dhyāna |
| `akopyadharmaṇaḥ` | genitive singular of `akopyadharman` | of the immovable practitioner |
| `nṛjā` | nominative singular feminine adjective | arising among humans |
| `anutpanna` | compound member | not arisen |
| `kāmāpta` | compound adjective | belonging to the desire domain |
| `savastuka` | compound member | connected with a determinate object |
| `kleśa` | compound member | affliction |
| `gocarāḥ` | transmitted ending; singular agreement problematic | having these as its field |

The split restores lexical `savastuka`; the transmitted
compound joins it to `kleśa` as `savastukleśa`. The
commentary explicitly supplies plural `savastukāḥ kleśāḥ`.

## 4. Grammar

**Constitution.** The feminine subject `araṇā` is identified
with the neuter noun `saṃvṛtijñānam`. The nouns need not
share gender for this nominal equation. The prose calls
this its `svabhāva`, constitution or nature.

**Support and bearer.** The two locatives specify the
last dhyāna, explicitly the fourth. The genitive specifies
whose attainment it is. The commentary supplies arhat
and expressly excludes other arhats from this particular
attainment.

**Human arising.** `Nṛjā` agrees with araṇā. The prose
limits arising to humans in three continents. This is
a personal-support condition, distinct from the fourth
dhyāna as meditative support.

**The restricted field.** The long final compound is
expanded as future, desire-domain, determinate-object
afflictions. These are conjoint restrictions, not three
alternative kinds of object. The final agreement defect
does not obscure that explicit prose expansion.

**Close construe.** Araṇā is conventional knowledge, in
the final dhyāna, belonging to an immovable [arhat],
arising among humans, with unarisen desire-domain afflictions
connected with determinate objects as its field.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Freedom from conflict is conventional knowledge, supported
> in the final dhyāna and belonging to an immovable practitioner.
> It arises among humans and has unarisen, desire-domain,
> determinate-object afflictions as its field.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A certain arhat, knowing that beings suffer through
> afflictions and recognizing himself as an especially
> worthy recipient of offerings, seeks to avoid afflictions
> arising in others with himself as their object. He
> produces the knowledge through which he does not occasion
> such conflict: attachment, hatred, or pride toward him.
> This course is therefore called araṇā, freedom from conflict.
>
> It is conventional knowledge, supported in the fourth
> dhyāna, and belongs to an immovable arhat. It arises
> among humans in three continents. Its objects are future
> desire-domain afflictions connected with determinate
> objects. The contrasting avastuka afflictions cannot
> be avoided through this undertaking, because universally
> operative afflictions take the whole of their own level
> as their object.

Source: Pradhan 417|02–417|18. The introduction overlaps
the bounded preview in VAK 7.35; the present study gives
the full definition and restrictions.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Araṇā concerns the practitioner's place in another's
> afflictive response. Its knowing is directed toward
> a future affliction that could take him as its object,
> and its undertaking avoids occasioning that response.
> Its efficacy is expressly limited by the kind, domain,
> and object-relation of the affliction concerned.

The source describes a specific attainment. It does
not assign the practitioner responsibility for controlling
every state in another person's mind, nor does it reduce
araṇā to his own absence of hostility.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Raṇa` / `araṇā`.** Conflict or afflictive disturbance /
freedom from that conflict. The prose explains the name
through a course that does not occasion conflict in
another, not through etymology alone.

**`Dakṣiṇīyaviśeṣa`.** A distinguished recipient of offerings.
This is the arhat's recognition of his own position in
the encounter. The source connects that recognition to
the wish to avoid becoming an afflictive object for others.

**`Tadālambana`.** Having that person as object. The arhat
is the object of the potential affliction; the potential
affliction is the object of araṇā's knowledge. These two
object-relations must remain distinct.

**`Sukhapratipadāmagratva`.** Being foremost among courses
of ease. The stated reason for the fourth-dhyāna support
does not amount to a practical instruction that the
attainment is easy for an untrained person.

**`Akopyadharman`.** An immovable arhat in this passage.
The Bhāṣya explains that another arhat may sometimes
be unable to avoid afflictive disturbance even in his
own continuum. The argument restricts the bearer; it
does not establish that every immovable arhat automatically
exercises araṇā at every moment.

**`Savastuka` / `avastuka`.** Connected with a determinate
object / the contrasting class. “Objectless” is misleading
if taken literally: the source itself explains the latter
through an object-range encompassing an entire level.

**`Sarvatraga`.** Universally operative or pervasive. Its
scope here is `sakala-svabhūmi`, the entirety of its own
level, not an unrestricted claim about every level or
every dharma in existence.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Coordinate | Source determination |
|---|---|
| Nature | Conventional knowledge |
| Meditative support | Fourth dhyāna |
| Bearer | Immovable arhat |
| Personal arising | Humans in three continents |
| Temporal object-status | Future / unarisen |
| Object-domain | Desire domain |
| Object-kind | Savastuka afflictions |
| Intended relation | Avoid others' afflictions taking the arhat as their object |

The three continents are not individually named in this
passage. Its support statement must not be replaced by
the Buddha-specific Jambūdvīpa restriction from VAK 7.30.

The two object-relations are:

```text
AranaKnowledge → hasObject → PotentialAfflictionInAnother
PotentialAfflictionInAnother → wouldHaveObject → ThisArhat
```

The restriction can be recorded as:

```text
EligibleObject(k) requires:
    Future(k)
    DesireDomain(k)
    Savastuka(k)
```

These predicates specify the knowledge's field; they
do not independently guarantee success by any practitioner.
The bearer and support conditions remain necessary parts
of the described attainment.

The exclusion of avastuka afflictions supplies an express
limit. The commentary grounds it in the all-pervasive
afflictions' whole-level object-range. Interpreting this
as a contrast between a modifiable encounter and a broader
afflictive orientation is useful reconstruction, but the
prose does not prescribe a detailed method of changing
speech, appearance, or movement in each encounter.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A necessary restriction is not a sufficient rule.**
The research study describes the immovable-arhat argument
as a sufficiency condition. The actual prose says araṇā
belongs to that bearer and not to other arhats, explaining
the exclusion. It does not prove that immovability alone
produces this particular knowledge without its cultivation.

**Future does not mean guaranteed later occurrence.** The
affliction is precisely what the undertaking seeks not
to occasion. Calling its object future cannot be turned
into a prediction that the affliction must later arise.
The relation between knowing its possibility and avoiding
its arising is central to the account.

**Textual caution.** The final `gocarāḥ` conflicts with
singular araṇā, and the purpose-formulation at 417|16–17
has compressed `nāpareṣāṃ kleśa udapādi`. The surrounding
wish to avoid arising and the explicit future-object
statement control the study rendering. The initial prose
also prints `bhena` in a damaged relative construction.
These defects are retained as witness issues, rather
than described as an entirely problem-free source.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Araṇā gives our Yoga system a concrete relation between
knowing and undertaking. Yoga Vidyā articulates the possible
affliction, its object, and its conditions. Yoga Technē,
its kriyā–karma companion, concerns how the practitioner
undertakes not to become its occasion.

The practitioner is included within the relation he knows.
His recognition of another's possible response bears
on his own conduct. This is a precise project interpretation
of practical reciprocity, while the source's restricted
object-range prevents it from becoming a theory of total
control over others.

Our reconstruction should therefore preserve both the
positive other-directed concern and its boundary. The
knowledge of an encounter, the conduct informed by it,
and the non-arising sought in another are related but
distinct determinations. The source does not reduce them
to a generic instruction to avoid all disagreement.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The definition, support, bearer, human arising, restricted
object-field, and explicit avastuka exclusion are checked.
The verse's final agreement and damaged prose expressions
remain marked for textual review.
