# VAK_7.13 — Sixteen Aspects and the Manner of Knowing

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
द्रव्यतः षोडशाकाराः प्रज्ञाकारस्तया सह ।
आकारयन्ति सालम्बाः सर्वमाकार्यते तु सत् ॥ ७.१३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dravyataḥ ṣoḍaśākārāḥ prajñākāras tayā saha |
ākārayanti sālambāḥ sarvam ākāryate tu sat || 7.13 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness, with connected sandhi
`prajñākāras tayā`. The Bhāṣya spans Pradhan 399|21–402|05. It first
discusses the substantial count and explains the sixteen aspects;
it then questions what an aspect is. The verse's identification with
prajñā and the prose's proposed reformulation remain distinct stages
of that argument.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dravyataḥ | ṣoḍaśa | ākārāḥ | prajñā | ākāraḥ |
tayā | saha | ākārayanti | sa-ālambāḥ |
sarvam | ākāryate | tu | sat
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dravyataḥ` | adverbial formation in `-tas` | in substantial distinction, not merely by name |
| `ṣoḍaśa` | numeral, nominative plural here | sixteen |
| `ākārāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | aspects |
| `prajñā` | nominative singular feminine | discriminative knowing |
| `ākāraḥ` | nominative singular masculine | aspect |
| `tayā` | instrumental singular feminine pronoun | with that prajñā |
| `saha` | postposition governing instrumental | together with |
| `ākārayanti` | third-person plural active verbal form in `-ayati` | apprehend under an aspect |
| `sālambāḥ` | nominative plural masculine adjective, substantivally used | those possessing object-supports |
| `sarvam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | all, everything |
| `ākāryate` | third-person singular passive corresponding to `ākārayati` | is apprehended under an aspect |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | whereas, however |
| `sat` | nominative singular neuter participle of `√as`, substantivally used | what exists |

`Ṣoḍaśa + ākārāḥ` gives `ṣoḍaśākārāḥ`; it means “sixteen aspects,”
not a predicate assigning sixteen aspects to each aspect. The verbal
forms are interpreted through the prose's object-taking explanation,
without imposing an image-production theory on their morphology.

## 4. Grammar

**Substantial count.** Supply the copula: `ākārāḥ dravyataḥ ṣoḍaśa`,
the aspects are sixteen in substantial distinction. The preceding
question opposes `nāmataḥ`, by name, and `dravyataḥ`, substantially.

**The initial identification.** `Prajñā ākāraḥ` is a nominal sentence:
“prajñā is the aspect.” The nouns retain their different genders.
This is the kārikā's stated identification, subsequently challenged
in the prose.

**The accompaniment phrase belongs forward.** `Tayā saha` refers
to prajñā and modifies the next clause:

```text
[anye] sālambāḥ [dharmāḥ]
    tayā saha ākārayanti
```

Other object-bearing dharmas apprehend together with it. The phrase
must not be stranded in the translation as “the aspect is prajñā,
together with it.” The prose explicitly includes prajñā and all
other object-bearing dharmas in the active side.

**The passive object-side statement.** `Sarvaṃ sat` is the subject
of `ākāryate`. The general statement concerns what can stand on
the apprehended side; it does not assign every existent to every
individual act of knowing.

**Close construe.** In substantial distinction, the aspects [are]
sixteen. Prajñā [is] the aspect. Together with it, those possessing
object-supports apprehend under an aspect. Whatever exists, however,
is apprehended under an aspect.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The aspects are sixteen in substantial distinction. Prajñā is the
> aspect. Together with it, the object-bearing [dharmas] apprehend
> under an aspect; whatever exists, however, is apprehended under
> an aspect.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The account presented here distinguishes sixteen aspects substantially,
> rather than merely sixteen names. It initially identifies aspect
> with discriminative knowing. The prose objects that prajñā would
> then lack an aspect, since it is not associated with another prajñā,
> and proposes instead: aspect is the manner in which mind and mental
> factors take their object-support. Prajñā and the other object-
> bearing dharmas apprehend under an aspect; whatever exists can
> itself become an object of such apprehension.

The numerical discussion and aspect explanations occupy
399|21–401|17; the definitional argument and active/passive
distinction occupy 401|18–402|05. The reformulation is attributed
to the prose rather than inserted into the literal kārikā as though
the verse already stated it.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The sixteen aspects receive distinct determinations through their
> grounds and functions. The question then turns from their number
> to their nature: an aspect must account for how cognition takes
> its object, including how prajñā itself is aspect-bearing. Knowing
> and being known are different roles, and cognition can occupy
> either without those roles becoming identical.

The proposed object-taking definition makes the manner of apprehension
explicit. It does not require a second prajñā to accompany the first
as an additional internal knower. Nor does the possibility of knowing
prajñā establish that a cognition necessarily apprehends itself in
the same moment.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dravyataḥ` and `nāmataḥ` — substantially and nominally.** The
preliminary alternative counts seven substantially and sixteen by
name: four for suffering, and one each for origination, cessation,
and Path. The kārikā presents sixteen substantially. This is a
doctrinal distinction among aspects, not the assertion of sixteen
permanent substances or sixteen simultaneous mental factors.

**The sixteen aspects.** The Bhāṣya provides several explanatory
series. The following table preserves the aspect-names and gives
selected grounds from those series, rather than presenting one
unmarked synthesis as a single Sanskrit passage.

| Truth | Aspect | Working sense | Selected Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suffering | `anitya` | impermanent | Dependence on conditions; arising and passing away |
| Suffering | `duḥkha` | suffering | Oppressive or adverse character |
| Suffering | `śūnya` | empty | Counteracts the view of what belongs to a self |
| Suffering | `anātman` | non-self | Counteracts self-view |
| Origination | `hetu` | cause | Seed-like causal role |
| Origination | `samudaya` | origination | Emergence or coming forth |
| Origination | `prabhava` | production | Continuation or production of the arising series |
| Origination | `pratyaya` | condition | Bringing the result to accomplishment |
| Cessation | `nirodha` | cessation | Stopping of the aggregates or their continued occurrence |
| Cessation | `śānta` | peaceful | Quenching; freedom from suffering |
| Cessation | `praṇīta` | excellent | Freedom from disturbance; unsurpassed character |
| Cessation | `niḥsaraṇa` | escape | Definitive release without return |
| Path | `mārga` | path | Being the way or course |
| Path | `nyāya` | right method / correctness | Appropriate operation in accordance with actuality |
| Path | `pratipad` | course of practice | Right, determinate proceeding |
| Path | `nairyāṇika` | leading out | Definitive passage beyond |

These are contextual renderings. The distinctions are established
through their relations and explanatory grounds, not by forcing
sixteen English words to be independently self-explanatory.

**Multiple causal explanations.** The initial series explains causal
production through seed-like function, emergence, continuity, and
accomplishment, including a pot-making example. A later account
relates origination to four forms of `chanda`: desire for present
self-existence, renewed existence, a specifically determined renewed
existence, and the linking of rebirth or karmic formation. Seed,
sprout, field conditions, and the culmination in flowering illustrate
their differentiated roles. These accounts should not be collapsed
into one universal lexical equation for each causal term.

**Counteractive explanation.** Another series differentiates aspects
through the views they oppose. The suffering quartet counters
permanence, pleasure, ownership, and self. The origination quartet
addresses causelessness, a single cause, transformation, and deliberate
intelligent production. The cessation quartet counters denial of
liberation, its characterization as suffering, the superiority of
meditative pleasure, and the claim that liberation is impermanent
through repeated decline. The Path quartet counters denial of a
Path, its being a wrong Path, an alternative Path, and a returning
Path. These are the local corrective functions stated in the prose.

**`Ālambanagrahaṇaprakāra` — manner of taking an object-support.**
The proposed definition after the objection to identifying aspect
simply with prajñā. `Prakāra` specifies manner; the definition applies
to citta and caittas, not only to one discriminative factor.

**`Sālamba` / `sālambana` — possessing an object-support.** The
kārikā and prose forms. They mark the active cognitive side of the
relation. The text does not grant apprehending activity to every
existent merely because it can be an object.

**`Ākārayati` / `ākāryate` — apprehends / is apprehended under an
aspect.** The active/passive distinction gives the final clause its
structure. Prajñā can apprehend and be apprehended; an object-support
considered simply in its object-role is apprehended.

## 8. Logical Determination

The preliminary numerical alternatives are:

```text
Seven-substantial account:
    4 suffering aspects + 1 origination + 1 cessation + 1 Path
    = 7 substantially, 16 nominally

Presented sixteen-substantial account:
    distinct determinations across all sixteen aspects
```

The definitional discussion has a different logical task:

```text
Initial identification: Aspect = Prajna

Objection:
    Prajna is not associated with another Prajna
    → the identification cannot explain Prajna's own aspect-bearing status

Proposed reformulation:
    Aspect = manner of object-taking of citta and caittas
```

The ensuing distinction of roles is:

```text
ObjectBearingDharma → apprehends under an aspect
ExistentDharma → can be apprehended under an aspect
```

The second rule does not imply the first. An existent need not be
a cognizing dharma in order to become an object. Conversely, a
cognizing dharma can itself stand as object of another cognition.

```text
Prajna:
    aspect in the stated identification
    active in apprehension
    available as object

ObjectSupport qua object:
    apprehended
```

The latter qualification matters: the same dharma may be cognitive
in one relation and an object in another. Neither universal
objectifiability nor this role distinction implies simultaneous
omniscience or same-moment self-apprehension.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Enumeration receives its grounds.** The postponed sixteen-aspect
teaching now appears. The prose offers multiple explanatory routes,
then develops a preferred explanation and another counteractive
series. The study preserves this plurality rather than reducing
the passage to a bare four-by-four vocabulary list.

**The objection remains visible.** The prose says that the manner-
of-object-taking formulation would be appropriate. Its subsequent
summary still uses the language of prajñā as aspect, apprehending,
and apprehended. The passage should therefore be represented as a
doctrinal presentation with a critical reformulation, rather than
rewritten as though every sentence belonged to one uncontested
theory. The local text does not explicitly name a school for every
step of this exchange.

**Source cautions.** The e-text contains numerous corrupt forms,
including `ṣīḍātmakatvāt`, `akndhoparamatvāt`, `ākāraynti`, and the
grammatically irregular concluding `ākāryante`. The study follows
the contextual exposition without silently printing restored prose
as a critical text. In particular, dependence on `pratyaya` means
dependence on conditions here; the research summary's “representations/
conditions” should not introduce a representational theory into that
ground of impermanence.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our Yoga
system now receives both an articulated field of truth-aspects and
an inquiry into what their operation means. Yoga Vidyā determines
the grounds through which an object is known as impermanent, causal,
peaceful, or leading out. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion,
develops the cognitive and practical undertaking in which those
determinations perform their work.

The counteractive series makes the companionship concrete: an aspect
has a particular corrective function because it addresses a
particular error. The manner-of-object-taking formulation then
asks how that determination belongs to the whole cognitive
occurrence, including citta and caittas. Discriminative knowing
operates with associated factors rather than requiring a second
internal discriminative knower to explain its own aspect.

For our reconstruction, systematic meaning therefore includes
the aspect, its ground, its corrective function, and its mode of
operation with the other cognitive factors. These relations support
a Yoga discipline in which knowing and cultivation become mutually
determinate. The source's competing formulations remain preserved
as the material through which that reconstruction must be argued.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The sixteen
names and selected explanatory series are now included. The numerical
alternative, critical reformulation, and residual textual difficulties
remain explicit rather than being harmonized away.
