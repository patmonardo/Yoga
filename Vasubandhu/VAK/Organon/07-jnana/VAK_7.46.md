# VAK_7.46 — Vidyā: Intrinsic Status and Designation through the Continuum

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अशैक्ष्यन्त्या तदाख्ये द्वे तत्संतानमुद्भवात् ।
इष्टे शैक्षस्य नोक्ते तु विद्ये साविद्यसंततेः ॥ ७.४६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
aśaikṣyantyā tadākhye dve tatsaṃtānamudbhavāt |
iṣṭe śaikṣasya nokte tu vidye sāvidyasaṃtateḥ || 7.46 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. Its
`tatsaṃtānamudbhavāt` is grammatically problematic; the
prose securely supplies the intended continuum-based
reason. The Devanāgarī reproduces the local reading,
not a conjectural restoration.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
aśaikṣī | antyā | tad-ākhye | dve |
tat-saṃtāna-m-udbhavāt [problematic transmitted junction] |
iṣṭe | śaikṣasya | na | ukte | tu | vidye |
sa-avidya-saṃtateḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `aśaikṣī` | nominative singular feminine adjective | beyond training, qualifying vidyā |
| `antyā` | nominative singular feminine adjective | the last of the three vidyās |
| `tadākhye` | nominative dual feminine compound adjective | bearing that designation |
| `dve` | nominative dual feminine numeral here | the two [other vidyās] |
| `tatsaṃtānamudbhavāt` | problematic compound/junction with ablative `udbhavāt` | because of arising in that continuum, as clarified in prose |
| `iṣṭe` | nominative dual feminine participle | admitted or accepted |
| `śaikṣasya` | genitive singular masculine | of or for the learner |
| `ukte` | nominative dual feminine participle | said, designated, or called |
| `tu` | adversative particle | however |
| `vidye` | nominative dual feminine | two vidyās |
| `sāvidyasaṃtateḥ` | causal ablative singular on the contextual construal | because of a continuum accompanied by ignorance |

`Aśaikṣī + antyā` yields `aśaikṣyantyā`; `na + ukte`
yields `nokte`. `Sa + avidya` yields `sāvidya`, “with
ignorance.” `Saṃtateḥ` is formally genitive or ablative
singular of `saṃtati`; the explanatory force and prose
support the causal ablative here.

The printed `-m-` in `tatsaṃtānamudbhavāt` is not silently
licensed as regular compound sandhi. The analysis identifies
the intended members and records the unresolved junction.

## 4. Grammar

**The last of three.** `Antyā` refers to influx-exhaustion
vidyā, the third of the triad named in VAK 7.45. It does
not refer to a fourth, newly introduced knowledge. The
prose's introductory `āsāṃ paramārthena`, “of these, in
the strict sense,” determines the force of `aśaikṣī`.

**Two bear the same name.** `Dve` and `tadākhye` are
feminine dual, referring to former-abode recollection
and death-and-rebirth knowledge. `Tad-` refers to the
beyond-training designation. The following reason refers
to their arising in the continuum of one beyond training.

The prose makes the distinction explicit: they are called
beyond training because they arise in such a continuum,
but in themselves they are neither learner nor beyond-
training cognitions. Designation through the bearer does
not replace intrinsic classification.

**Admitted, but not so named.** `Iṣṭe` supplies the two
higher knowledges as its subject; `śaikṣasya` specifies
the learner who may possess them. `Na ukte tu vidye`
withholds the twofold vidyā designation in this discussion.
It does not deny the occurrence of the two abhijñās.

**Reason through the continuum.** `Sāvidyasaṃtateḥ`
explains the restriction. The prose expands it into a
locative statement: establishment as vidyā is inappropriate
in a continuum accompanied by ignorance, because ignorance
can again overpower. The explanation concerns the condition
of the continuum, not a claim that every cognition in it
is false or currently conjoined with ignorance.

**Close construe.** The last [vidyā] is beyond training
in the strict sense. The other two bear that designation
because they arise in that continuum. They are admitted
for the learner, but are not called vidyās, because the
learner's continuum is accompanied by ignorance.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The last is beyond training. The two bear that designation
> because they arise in that continuum. They are admitted
> for the learner, but are not called vidyās, because the
> continuum is accompanied by ignorance.

The first causal phrase is rendered through its unambiguous
prose expansion; its local verse reading remains uncertain.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Of the three vidyās, only direct cognitive realization
> of the exhaustion of the influxes is beyond training
> in the strict sense. Former-abode recollection and
> death-and-rebirth knowledge are called beyond training
> because they arise in the continuum of one beyond
> training. In themselves, however, those two are neither
> learner nor beyond-training cognitions.
>
> These two higher knowledges are also admitted for a
> learner. They are not designated learner vidyās here:
> establishing them as vidyā is inappropriate in a continuum
> still accompanied by ignorance, since ignorance can
> again overpower.

Source: the local Bhāṣya and running source, Pradhan
423|20–424|07, including the introductory `paramārthena`.
The next discussion, beginning at 424|08 and quoting
VAK 7.47 at 424|09, concerns the three prātihāryas.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A cognition's own classification and the designation
> it receives through its bearer are distinct. The first
> two capacities can occur in a learner, yet their occurrence
> does not establish the same vidyā designation that they
> receive in one beyond training. What remains possible
> in the continuum matters to that designation.

The relevant remaining possibility is renewed overpowering
by ignorance. This does not make the learner's admitted
higher knowledge unreal, nor does it turn every cognition
of an accomplished practitioner into an intrinsically
beyond-training cognition.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Aśaikṣī`.** Beyond training, feminine in agreement
with `vidyā`. The prose distinguishes its strict application
to influx-exhaustion realization from its derivative
application to the other two.

**`Tadākhyā`.** Bearing that name or designation. This
marks a classificatory relation; it is not a statement
that the first two have changed their intrinsic nature.

**`Saṃtāna / saṃtati`.** Continuum. Both expressions
occur in the unit and identify the continuing bearer-
context of a cognition. “System” is an Organon interpretation
of the relations within that continuum, not a replacement
for the source vocabulary in translation.

**`Śaikṣa`.** A learner or one still in training on the
noble path. The term is not a generic designation for
any novice learning a skill. Admitting these capacities
for a learner does not assert their possession by every
learner.

**`Sāvidya`.** Accompanied by ignorance. Here it qualifies
the continuum. It must not be transferred automatically
to the constitution of each particular cognitive event.

**`Vidyāvyavasthāna`.** Establishment or determination
as vidyā. The immediate issue is warranted classification.
“Stable establishment” may help explain the reference to
renewed ignorance, but the noun does not independently
define a measurable duration of cognitive stability.

**`Punar apy avidyābhibhava`.** Renewed overpowering by
ignorance. This is the Bhāṣya's reason for withholding
the designation in the learner's continuum. It does not
state that the particular memory or perception must become
false afterward.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Capacity and bearer-context | Intrinsic status stated here | Designation in the present triad |
|---|---|---|
| Influx-exhaustion realization | Beyond training | Beyond-training vidyā in the strict sense |
| Former-abode recollection in one beyond training | Neither learner nor beyond training | Beyond-training vidyā through the continuum |
| Death-and-rebirth knowledge in one beyond training | Neither learner nor beyond training | Beyond-training vidyā through the continuum |
| Either of the first two in a learner | Same intrinsic classification of those two | Admitted as abhijñā; not designated learner vidyā here |

The table distinguishes classification of the cognition
from the path-status of its bearer. The two intrinsically
neither-learner-nor-beyond-training cognitions do not become
intrinsically learner cognitions when a learner possesses
them, or intrinsically beyond-training cognitions when
an aśaikṣa possesses them.

**The objection.** If the two are not called learner
vidyās, are their corresponding higher knowledges not
admitted for the learner? This tests whether refusal
of the name implies refusal of the capacity.

**The reply.** The capacities are admitted. The name
is withheld because the continuum still permits renewed
overpowering by ignorance. Thus the argument separates
occurrence, intrinsic category, bearer-condition, and
contextual designation.

```text
For former-abode recollection and death-and-rebirth knowledge:

Intrinsic category:
    neither learner nor beyond training

Arising in a beyond-training continuum:
    receives the beyond-training vidyā designation

Admitted in a learner's continuum:
    does not receive the learner-vidyā designation here
    reason: ignorance can again overpower
```

This carries forward VAK 7.45's distinctions rather than
replacing them. Death-and-rebirth knowledge remains ethically
indeterminate; former-abode recollection remains wholesome.
Neither ethical class is equivalent to intrinsic learner
or beyond-training status.

The source admits the same kinds of capacity in the two
bearer-contexts. It does not require identical object-range,
identical content, or equal proficiency in particular
occurrences. No universal inference from a successful
cognition to completed liberation follows.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Scope of the vidyā restriction.** The discussion concerns
the three vidyās just enumerated and the question of naming
the first two learner vidyās. It should not become a
universal claim that the learner possesses no knowledge,
no insight, or nothing elsewhere called vidyā. Its positive
point is that the two abhijñās are admitted while the
specific designation is withheld.

**Witness cautions.** The kārikā file prints
`tadākhye dve tatsaṃtānamudbhavāt`; the running source
and Bhāṣya extract instead print the damaged
`tadāsye dve tatsaṃtānasudbhavāt`. Their prose explicitly
states arising in an aśaikṣa continuum, securing the
meaning without securing an exact restored pāda.
The running verse also has `śaikṣsya`, where the local
kārikā has `śaikṣasya`. These differences remain visible;
the underlying files are untouched.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
The source gives our project a precise relation between
a cognition and its continuing conditions. Yoga Vidyā
articulates what the cognition knows and what designation
its ignorance-removing function warrants. Yoga Technē,
its kriyā–karma companion, concerns the path-transformation
of the continuum in which such knowing operates.

For Organon, a correct occurrence need not establish that
the corresponding source of error has been overcome in
the system that produces it. This is an analogy to the
Bhāṣya's distinction between an admitted capacity and
renewed overpowering by ignorance. It does not identify
Abhidharma ignorance with a software defect or make an
isolated computational test a criterion of liberation.

The research kārikā develops the stronger image that
truth becomes the governing principle of the whole
continuum, with Hegelian, Fichtean, and Śrī Vidyā contacts.
Those remain project interpretations. The immediate source
determination is narrower and exact: intrinsic status,
continuum-based designation, admitted learner capacities,
and the possibility of renewed ignorance.
