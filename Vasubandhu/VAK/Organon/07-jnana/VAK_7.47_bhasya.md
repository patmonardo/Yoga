# VAK_7.47 Bhāṣya — The Three Prātihāryas and the Primacy of Instruction

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ādyā tṛtīyā ṣaṣṭhī ca prātihāryāṇi śāsanam |
> agryam avyabhicāritvāddhiteṣṭaphalayojanāt || 7.47 ||

> The first, third, and sixth are the prātihāryas. Instruction is foremost
> because of non-deviation and because it connects with beneficial and desired
> fruit.

Ordinals from the six abhijñās. Next: ṛiddhi as samādhi and modes of going.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> The higher knowledges of extraordinary efficacy, another mind, and influx-
> exhaustion are, respectively, the wonders of extraordinary efficacy, indication,
> and instruction. They are so called from powerfully drawing the minds of those
> to be trained at the outset; or: they turn the minds of the resistant and the
> indifferent.
>
> Of these, the wonder of instruction is foremost. The first two can be produced
> by [other] vidyās: Gāndhārī, by which one goes through the sky; Īkṣaṇikā, by
> which one knows another's mind. Instruction according to things as they are
> cannot be done otherwise.
>
> Those two principally attract. Through the wonder of instruction there is
> conjunction with a fruit that is beneficial and desired — from teaching the
> means.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

## 5. Spectacle Does Not Certify

Gāndhārī / Īkṣaṇikā are arts — not the three vidyās of 7.45. Imitation ≠ fraud
by default; other means exist. Non-deviation ≠ every sermon succeeds. No rank
of indication over ṛiddhi. Fruit is one, beneficial *and* desired — not two
fruits. Teaching means ≠ guaranteeing every listener's attainment.

## 6. The Three

| abhijñā | prātihārya |
|---|---|
| 1 ṛiddhi | ṛiddhi |
| 3 other-mind | ādeśanā |
| 6 exhaustion | anuśāsana |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- ordinals = those three of six;
- `śāsanam` = anuśāsana;
- two ablatives = two reasons.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Practical Reason: Vidyā states the condition and the fruit; Technē is the means
taught. Instruction is the relation that hands Technē to another. Attraction
is not that relation. Appearance would crown the flying body. Sevā to the
Philosopher's God is instruction according to things as they are — not a third
miracle stacked on the first two.
