# VAK_7.31 Bhāṣya — Bodily Strength: Measure, Support, and Material Constitution

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nārāyaṇabalaṃ kāye saṃdhiṣv anye daśādhikam |
> hastyādisaptakabalaṃ spraṣṭavyāyatanaṃ ca tat || 7.31 ||

> Nārāyaṇa-strength is in the body; others [place it] in the joints. The
> strength of the sevenfold series beginning with the elephant increases
> tenfold. That [strength] belongs to the tangible Sphere.

Same word `bala`. Not an eleventh knowledge-power. Ayatana here = Sphere
(capacity/field), not “sense-base.”

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.31.md`](./VAK_7.31.md).

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit

> kāye punar buddhasya nārāyaṇaṃ balaṃ varṇayati /
> sandhau sandhau nārāyaṇabalam ity apare /
> mānasavat kāyikam apy asyānantaṃ balam iti bhadantaḥ /
> anyathā hy anantajñānabalasahiṣṇu na syāt /
> kiṃ punar nārāyaṇasya balasya pramāṇam /
> yad daśānāṃ prākṛtahastināṃ balaṃ tad ekasya gandhahastinaḥ /
> … daśottaravṛddrhir vaktavyā /
> … yathā tu bahutaraṃ tathā yujyate /
> tac ca tat kāyikaṃ balaṃ sarvasyaiva spraṣṭavyāyatanasvabhāvaṃ mahābhūtaviśeṣa eva /
> upādāyarūpaṃ saptabhyo 'rthāntaram ity apare /

**Witness.** Pradhan 413|04–19. Voices: stated account; apare (joints;
alternative series; derived form); Bhadanta (boundless body). `Uktāni balāni`
closes the powers before 7.32.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> In the body, the Buddha's strength is described as Nārāyaṇa-strength.
> Others: Nārāyaṇa-strength in each joint.
>
> Bhadanta: his bodily strength too is boundless, like the mental. Otherwise
> it would not be able to bear boundless knowledge-power.
>
> What is the measure of Nārāyaṇa-strength? What is the strength of ten
> ordinary elephants is that of one gandhahastin. Likewise mahānagna,
> praskandin, varāṅga, cānūra, Nārāyaṇa — tenfold increase is to be stated.
> [Another series.] As it is greater, so it is fitting.
>
> That bodily strength, of everyone, has the nature of the tangible Sphere —
> a special determination of the great elements. Others: derived form, a
> distinct item from the seven.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
where: body / each joint

```

## 5. Keep the Questions Apart

Nārāyaṇa is a grade-name, not a theological identification. Series is
traditional comparison, not newtons. Six tenfold steps → a derived million;
the prose does not print that figure as a measurement.

Bhadanta's adequacy-of-support is recorded, not converted into a law that
knowledge-range equals mechanical force. Preference for the larger reckoning
≠ endorsement of infinity.

`Sarvasyaiva`: tangible classification is general. Magnitude claims above it
are Buddha-specific. Seven derived tangibles ≠ the whole tangible inventory
(great elements remain).

Joint-images (nāgagranthi / chain / peg for Buddha, pratyekabuddha, cakravartin)
are images, not anatomy.

## 6. Two Balas

Cognitive power: jñāna, unobstructed. Bodily power: rūpa, tangible. Same
lexeme, different domain.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- first claim = named bodily strength;
- `anye` = per-joint;
- `daśādhikam` = tenfold steps of the seven;
- `tat` = bodily strength as spraṣṭavya-Sphere.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

A quality of the body is still a quality. Qualitative Concept of strength
stays inside the Idea; it does not become a second mental power. Vidyā knows;
Technē needs an adequate support. Bhadanta states that relation. Do not
engineer infinity out of it. Appearance would equate the two balas because
the word matches.
