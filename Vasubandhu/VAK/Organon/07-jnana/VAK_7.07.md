# VAK_7.07 — Accomplishment and Knowledge of Non-Repetition

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
क्षयज्ञानं हि सत्येषु परिज्ञातादिनिश्चयः ।
न परिज्ञेयमित्यादिरनुत्पादमतिर्मता ॥ ७.७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kṣayajñānaṃ hi satyeṣu parijñātādiniścayaḥ |
na parijñeyam ity ādir anutpādamatir matā || 7.7 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. Having located the two
terminal knowledges within the knowledge classification, the Bhāṣya now
asks what distinguishes them. The answer concerns the four truth-tasks:
their accomplishment and their not needing to be performed again.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kṣaya-jñānam | hi | satyeṣu | parijñāta-ādi-niścayaḥ |
na | parijñeyam | iti | ādiḥ | anutpāda-matiḥ | matā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `kṣaya-jñānam` | nominative singular neuter compound | knowledge of exhaustion |
| `hi` | explanatory/emphatic particle | for, indeed |
| `satyeṣu` | locative plural neuter | with respect to the truths |
| `parijñāta-ādi-niścayaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | ascertainment beginning with “fully known” |
| `na` | negative particle | not |
| `parijñeyam` | nominative singular neuter gerundive of `pari-√jñā` | to be fully known |
| `iti` | quotation marker | marks the preceding formulation |
| `ādiḥ` | nominative singular masculine in the abbreviated expression | beginning with [that formulation], and so forth |
| `anutpāda-matiḥ` | nominative singular feminine compound | cognition of non-arising |
| `matā` | nominative singular feminine past passive participle of `√man` | held, regarded, designated |

`Parijñāta` is a past passive participle: “fully known.” The contrast with
the gerundive `parijñeya`, “to be fully known,” is the grammatical hinge.
The Bhāṣya supplies `punaḥ`, “again,” in unfolding the second formulation.

## 4. Grammar

**Knowledge of exhaustion.** `Kṣayajñānam` receives the predicate
`parijñātādiniścayaḥ`. The different grammatical genders belong to the
two nouns, knowledge and ascertainment; they do not require adjectival
agreement. `Satyeṣu` states the field with respect to which the
ascertainment is made.

The initial participle stands for a series completed by `ādi`:

```text
duḥkhaṃ — parijñātam
samudayaḥ — prahīṇaḥ
nirodhaḥ — sākṣātkṛtaḥ
mārgaḥ — bhāvitaḥ
```

The Bhāṣya supplies these four completed predicates. Its first clause
includes `me`: “suffering has been fully known by me,” naturally rendered
“I have fully known suffering.”

**Knowledge of non-arising.** `Na parijñeyam iti` quotes the first
non-repetition formulation. `Ādiḥ` abbreviates the corresponding series;
the masculine form can be construed with the preceding `niścayaḥ`
understood. `Anutpādamatiḥ matā` gives its designation, with `matā`
agreeing with the feminine `matiḥ`.

The Bhāṣya combines accomplished and prospective predicates:

```text
duḥkhaṃ me parijñātam
    na punaḥ parijñeyam

mārgo bhāvitaḥ
    na punar bhāvayitavyaḥ
```

It uses `yāvat`, “up to,” to abbreviate the intervening truth-tasks.
The study's full fourfold expansion of non-repetition makes that
abbreviation explicit rather than claiming four separately quoted clauses.

**Close construe.** Exhaustion-knowledge, indeed, [is] ascertainment with
respect to the truths beginning with “[it is] fully known.” [Ascertainment]
beginning with “[it is] not to be fully known” is held to be cognition of
non-arising.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Knowledge of exhaustion is indeed ascertainment concerning the truths,
> beginning with “fully known.” That beginning with “not to be fully
> known” is held to be cognition of non-arising.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Exhaustion-knowledge is characterized through the ascertainment:
> “I have fully known suffering; its origination has been abandoned;
> cessation has been realized; the Path has been cultivated.”
>
> Non-arising-knowledge is characterized through the further ascertainment:
> “I have fully known suffering, and it is not again to be fully known;
> origination has been abandoned, and is not again to be abandoned;
> cessation has been realized, and is not again to be realized;
> the Path has been cultivated, and is not again to be cultivated.”

These expansions follow Pradhan 394|08–394|12. The Bhāṣya then questions
how uncontaminated knowledge knows in this form. In the Kashmiri account,
the explicit knowing occurs through subsequently acquired cognition upon
emergence; the difference between those subsequent cognitions indicates
the difference between the two knowledges. Other teachers say that one
knows in this way through uncontaminated knowledge itself as well
(394|12–394|15). The first-person formulations therefore have differently
explained cognitive status within the source discussion.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Exhaustion-knowledge determines the truth-tasks as accomplished.
> Non-arising-knowledge determines their accomplishment together with
> their not requiring renewed performance. The same field of work receives
> two distinct determinations: what has been accomplished, and what is
> secured against becoming a task again.

The second determination is not simply another name for the first. Nor
does the verse establish that every occurrence of exhaustion-knowledge
automatically entails possession of non-arising-knowledge. It defines
their difference; the conditions governing their possession remain
distinct questions.

The accompanying debate concerns how this accomplishment becomes
explicitly knowable. The Kashmiri account distinguishes uncontaminated
knowledge from its subsequent articulation; the other account allows
this knowing in uncontaminated cognition too. Both accounts must remain
visible before drawing a project-level conclusion about reflection.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Niścaya` — ascertainment / certainty.** The predicate defining
exhaustion-knowledge. Here certainty has a specified content and task-field;
it is not merely a feeling of assurance.

**`Parijñāna` — full knowing.** The task assigned to suffering. Its
completion is expressed by `parijñātam`; the prospective task by
`parijñeyam`. The other truths receive their own differentiated operations.

**`Prahāṇa`, `sākṣātkaraṇa`, `bhāvanā` — abandonment, realization,
cultivation.** The respective tasks of origination, cessation, and Path.
The Bhāṣya's participles present their accomplished forms. They should
not be flattened into four identical instances of “understanding.”

**`Kṣayajñāna` — exhaustion-knowledge.** Knowledge characterized through
the completed truth-tasks. The supplied definition gives the term a
determinate practical content beyond the isolated gloss “exhaustion.”

**`Anutpādamati` / `anutpādajñāna` — cognition / knowledge of non-arising.**
The verse's designation and the prose's equivalent technical name.
Its definition adds that the completed tasks are not again to be performed.
The claim is scoped to this liberative work; it does not prohibit every
subsequent wholesome activity or all further exercise of knowledge.

**`Tatpṛṣṭhalabdha` — acquired subsequently to that.** The instrumental
`tatpṛṣṭhalabdhena` supplies the cognition through which, according to
the Kashmiri explanation, one knows in the explicit stated form.
The uncontaminated knowledge and the subsequent cognition should not
be merged into a single undifferentiated event in reporting this account.

**`Vyutthita` — emerged.** The subject's condition in the Kashmiri
explanation: having emerged, one knows in this way through the subsequently
acquired cognition. The word marks the transition relevant to the
explanation, without itself supplying a complete theory of reflection.

**`Jñāna`, `darśana`, `vidyā`, `buddhi`, `bodhi`, `prajñā`, `āloka`,
`abhisamaya`.** The cited śāstra formula gathers these designations around
the knowing in question. Their co-occurrence does not erase all technical
distinctions among them. In particular, the appearance of `darśana`
requires attention alongside the exclusion from technical view-status
established in VAK 7.01.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Truth | Task | Accomplishment formulation | Non-repetition formulation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suffering | Fully know | Fully known | Not again to be fully known |
| Origination | Abandon | Abandoned | Not again to be abandoned |
| Cessation | Realize | Realized | Not again to be realized |
| Path | Cultivate | Cultivated | Not again to be cultivated |

The table unfolds the Bhāṣya's abbreviated series. It specifies the
content used to distinguish the knowledges, not the assertion that every
single knowledge-event apprehends all four truths simultaneously.

```text
ExhaustionKnowledge:
    ascertainment of accomplished truth-tasks

NonArisingKnowledge:
    ascertainment of accomplishment
    with no renewed requirement to perform those tasks
```

The two explanations of explicit formulation are separately attributed:

```text
KashmiriAccount:
    UncontaminatedKnowledge
        → SubsequentlyAcquiredCognitionUponEmergence
        → ExplicitFormulationOfAccomplishment

OtherTeachersAccount:
    SuchKnowingIsPossibleThroughUncontaminatedKnowledgeAlso
```

The arrows in the first account represent dependence and subsequent
articulation. They do not turn exhaustion-knowledge itself into a merely
retrospective opinion.

The local source subsequently recapitulates the ten names at
394|16–394|18: dharma, anvaya, conventional, suffering, origination,
cessation, Path, other-mind, exhaustion, and non-arising knowledge.
Their relations remain overlapping classifications, as the previous
studies have shown.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The source extends beyond the local extract.** The research Bhāṣya
file stops its quotation at the Kashmiri attribution. The underlying
source continues with `anāsraveṇāpy evaṃ jānātīty apare`: others say
that one knows thus through uncontaminated knowledge also. This official
study includes that qualification rather than treating the Kashmiri
explanation as an unopposed conclusion.

**The word `darśana`.** The following prose discusses its occurrence
in the cited formula. The e-text includes the difficult
`bhāṣyākṣepāt` and then `jpratyakṣavṛttitvādvā`; the latter appears
to offer direct operation as an alternative ground. Exact restoration
and translation of the former clause remain open. The official study
therefore records the issue without constructing a settled explanation
from damaged wording or reversing VAK 7.01's explicit classification.

**Other textual details.** The raw source has `na pounaḥ` and
`jñāpiota`, normalized in the local research quotation as `na punaḥ`
and `jñāpita`. The translation follows the readable contextual sense;
the underlying witnesses remain intact.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** This verse
gives our reconstruction a direct articulation of their companionship.
Yoga Technē's kriyā–karma field comprises differentiated work: fully
knowing, abandoning, realizing, and cultivating. Yoga Vidyā articulates
what each task requires and what counts as its accomplished result.
The knowledge of accomplishment belongs to their reciprocal development.

The distinction between exhaustion and non-arising then asks more than
whether an undertaking has reached completion. It asks whether that
completion is secured against the work becoming necessary again. Our
system must preserve these distinct determinations rather than using
“accomplished” for both without further analysis.

The Bhāṣya debate adds a second problem: how accomplishment becomes
explicitly articulated as “I have accomplished this.” The Kashmiri
account supplies a model of subsequent reflective articulation; the
other teachers allow that knowing within uncontaminated cognition too.
These provide distinct resources for our reconstruction. Choosing
between them requires an argument beyond merely repeating either view.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The truth-task
distinction and both accounts of explicit knowing are retained. The
damaged explanation of `darśana` remains a localized textual question.
