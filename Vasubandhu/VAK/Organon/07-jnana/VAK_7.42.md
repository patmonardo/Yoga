# VAK_7.42 — Six Higher Knowledges as Direct Cognitive Realizations

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
ऋद्धिश्रोत्रमनःपूर्वजन्मच्युत्युदयक्षये ।
ज्ञातसाक्षीक्रियाभिज्ञाः षड्विधा मुक्तिमार्गधीः ॥ ७.४२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ṛddhiśrotramanaḥpūrvajanmacyutyudayakṣaye |
jñātasākṣīkriyābhijñāḥ ṣaḍvidhā muktimārgadhīḥ || 7.42 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The running
Bhāṣya prints `ṛddhhi-` and `cyutyutpapāda-`; these are
electronic transcription defects. Its prose clarifies the
compressed `jñātasākṣīkriyā` through repeated expressions
of the form `jñānasākṣātkriyā`.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ṛddhi-śrotra-manaḥ-pūrva-janma-cyuti-udaya-kṣaye |
jñāta-sākṣī-kriyā-abhijñāḥ | ṣaṭ-vidhāḥ | mukti-mārga-dhīḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ṛddhi` | compound member | extraordinary efficacy or accomplishment |
| `śrotra` | compound member | hearing, expanded as divine hearing |
| `manaḥ` | compound member | mind, expanded as another's mental course |
| `pūrvajanma` | compound member | former birth, expanded as former abodes |
| `cyutyudaya` | compound member | death and arising / reappearance |
| `kṣaye` | locative singular masculine | concerning exhaustion of the influxes |
| `jñātasākṣīkriyā` | compressed compound member | direct cognitive realization |
| `abhijñāḥ` | nominative plural feminine | higher knowledges |
| `ṣaḍvidhāḥ` | nominative plural feminine adjective | sixfold |
| `muktimārgadhīḥ` | nominative plural feminine compound | cognitions of the liberation path |

`Cyuti + udaya` yields `cyutyudaya`; `kriyā + abhijñāḥ`
yields `kriyābhijñāḥ`. The string of fields in the first
half is distributive: it names six objects for six higher
knowledges, not one compound object possessed by each.

## 4. Grammar

**The six fields.** The prose expands the abbreviated
members as extraordinary efficacy, divine hearing,
another mind, recollection of former abodes, death and
rebirth, and exhaustion of the influxes. `Kṣaye` is
controlled by `āsravakṣaya` in that expansion.

**Definition through direct realization.** The Bhāṣya first
defines the extraordinary-efficacy abhijñā as direct
cognitive realization in that field, then applies the
same compound to the remaining five. `Sākṣātkriyā` marks
making directly present or realizing; it should not be
reduced to knowing a proposition merely by report.

**Two plural predicates.** `Ṣaḍvidhāḥ` and `muktimārgadhīḥ`
agree with `abhijñāḥ`. The second identifies their cognitive
nature as liberation-path prajñā in the prose. It does
not state that “liberation path” is a seventh abhijñā.

**Close construe.** The higher knowledges are sixfold:
direct cognitive realizations concerning extraordinary
efficacy, hearing, another mind, former birth, death and
arising, and exhaustion. They are cognitions belonging
to liberation paths.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The higher knowledges are sixfold direct cognitive
> realizations concerning extraordinary efficacy, hearing,
> mind, former birth, death and arising, and exhaustion.
> They are cognitions of the liberation path.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The six higher knowledges are direct cognitive realizations
> concerning extraordinary efficacy, divine hearing,
> another's mental course, recollection of former abodes,
> the death and reappearance of beings, and exhaustion
> of the influxes. Five of these are shared with ordinary
> persons. All six have liberation-path discriminative
> knowing as their nature, like the fruits of the ascetic path.

Source: Pradhan 421|03–421|12. The category and level
distributions begin immediately afterward in VAK 7.43;
they should control further claims about the difference
between the first five and the sixth.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Higher knowledge makes a determinate field directly
> cognitively present. Yet expansion of access and liberation
> remain distinct determinations: five such capacities
> can occur in ordinary persons. The path-function of
> the cognition does not by itself establish the attained
> status of every bearer who possesses one of its forms.

The sixth has exhaustion of the influxes as its field.
That gives it a liberative object distinct from the
conditioned fields of the first five, but this verse
does not yet present the six as a graded sequence or
claim that the sixth governs every exercise of the others.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Abhijñā`.** Higher knowledge or superknowledge. The
Bhāṣya defines it through `jñānasākṣātkriyā`, direct
cognitive realization. “Supernormal power” captures
some members poorly if used as the definition of the whole.

**`Ṛddhiviṣaya`.** The field of extraordinary efficacy.
The present passage names the field and direct realization;
the detailed kinds and manifestations of ṛddhi belong
to the later analysis.

**`Divyaśrotra`.** Divine hearing. The adjective belongs
to the prose expansion. It specifies a developed auditory
capacity without changing hearing into a non-cognitive force.

**`Cetaḥparyāya`.** Another's mental course or states of
mind. This expands the verse's `manaḥ`. Its exact limits
remain controlled by the earlier paracittajñāna analysis
and the forthcoming abhijñā classification.

**`Pūrvanivāsānusmṛti`.** Recollection of former abodes
or past modes of existence. It is more precise here than
the verse's compressed “former birth”; the source does
not limit it to one immediately preceding life.

**`Cyutyutpāda`.** Death and subsequent arising. The prose
uses `utpāda` where the verse has `udaya`; the contextual
determination is the death and reappearance of beings.

**`Āsravakṣaya`.** Exhaustion of the influxes. This is
the field of the sixth direct realization, not an assertion
that every kind of hidden-state knowledge exhausts bondage.

**`Vimuktimārgaprajñā`.** Discriminative knowing belonging
to a liberation path. `Prajñā` should be translated as
cognition or discriminative knowing here, rather than
the abstract “principle” used in the research file.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Number | Higher knowledge | Directly realized field | Shared with ordinary persons here |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ṛddhi | Extraordinary efficacy | Yes |
| 2 | Divine hearing | Sounds beyond ordinary auditory range | Yes |
| 3 | Another-mind knowledge | Another mental course | Yes |
| 4 | Former-abode recollection | Past modes of existence | Yes |
| 5 | Death-and-rebirth knowledge | Death and subsequent arising | Yes |
| 6 | Influx-exhaustion knowledge | Exhaustion of the influxes | No |

The first five are the five referred to by the prose's
`āsāṃ pañca pṛthagjanaiḥ sādhāraṇāḥ`. The source does
not state that every ordinary person possesses them;
it states that these kinds can be shared with that
practitioner class.

The shared cognitive characterization is:

```text
For each a in SixAbhijnas:
    a is DirectCognitiveRealization
    a hasNature LiberationPathPrajna
```

This does not yield:

```text
PossessesAnyOfFirstFive(p) → p is Liberated
```

Worldly paths also have liberation-path functions relative
to the attainments they accomplish. “Liberation path”
therefore requires its technical path-function and context;
it must not be flattened into final liberation from all influxes.

The Bhāṣya's analogy `śrāmaṇyaphalavat`, “like the fruits
of the ascetic path,” supports the liberation-path nature.
It does not make the six abhijñās identical with the
four fruits or add those fruits to the sixfold inventory.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Correcting the first-pass progression.** The research
kārikā organizes the six as increasingly comprehensive
and says the first five expand reach while the sixth
governs them through liberation. That is an intelligible
Organon construction, but the present source unit does
not state either a progressive ordering or a governing
relation. Its explicit determinations are enumeration,
direct realization, sharing of five, and liberation-path nature.

**Direct does not mean unconditioned.** Each abhijñā is
a cultivated cognition with its own support and acquisition
conditions. `Sākṣātkriyā` distinguishes direct realization
from mere report or inference; it does not mean causeless,
simultaneous, or universally infallible cognition in every bearer.

**Textual caution.** The verse's `jñātasākṣīkriyā` is
highly compressed. The repeated prose form
`jñānasākṣātkriyā` controls its semantic determination.
The running source's `śrādaka`, `ṛddhhi`, and
`cyutyutpapāda` are evident transcription problems and
are left untouched in the witness.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
This verse presents knowledge as cultivated direct access
to determinate fields. Yoga Vidyā articulates what is
known and the epistemic character of direct realization.
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, articulates the
liberation-path operation and cultivated conditions through
which that access becomes possible.

For our Yoga system, the fivefold ordinary-person sharing
is crucial. Greater access does not establish wisdom,
ethical orientation, or freedom. A technical capacity
must be related to the condition of its bearer and to
the end served by its exercise. That is a systematic
determination, not a warning added from outside the source:
the Bhāṣya itself places ordinary-person sharing beside
liberation-path cognitive nature.

The sixth supplies the knowledge-field of influx-exhaustion,
but we should let the next verses determine its constitution
and levels before building a stronger hierarchy. Vidyā
and Technē meet here through direct realization and path-
function while remaining distinct from spectacle or
mere augmentation of reach.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The six fields, direct-realization definition, fivefold
sharing, and liberation-path cognition are checked against
