# VAK_7.40 — Linguistic Levels, Complete Acquisition, and Six Final-Limit Qualities

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कामध्यानेषु धर्मे वित् वाचि प्रथमकामयोः ।
विकलाभिर्न तल्लाभी षडेते प्रान्तकोटिकाः ॥ ७.४० ॥
```

The local research kārikā supplies only IAST. This
Devanāgarī is an editorial transcription of that reading,
not an independently supplied witness. The awkward
`dharme vit` is preserved rather than silently emended.

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kāmadhyāneṣu dharme vit vāci prathamakāmayoḥ |
vikalābhir na tallābhī ṣaḍ ete prāntakoṭikāḥ || 7.40 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The Bhāṣya
resolves the first pāda as a statement about dharma-
pratisaṃvid on five levels, despite the compressed
`dharme vit`. Its prose then supplies the technical
content of the remaining clauses.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kāma-dhyāneṣu | dharme | vit | vāci | prathama-kāmayoḥ |
vikalābhiḥ | na | tat-lābhī | ṣaṭ | ete | prāntakoṭikāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `kāmadhyāneṣu` | locative coordinate compound | in the desire domain and the dhyānas |
| `dharme` | locative singular masculine/neuter | concerning dharma; in the dharma-pratisaṃvid clause |
| `vit` | nominative singular agent noun | knower; syntactically compressed with `dharme` |
| `vāci` | locative singular feminine | concerning speech |
| `prathamakāmayoḥ` | locative dual compound | in the first dhyāna and desire domain |
| `vikalābhiḥ` | instrumental plural feminine | with incomplete [pratisaṃvids] |
| `na` | negation | not |
| `tallābhī` | nominative singular masculine agent noun | one who has acquired that [fourfold set] |
| `ṣaṭ ete` | numeral and pronoun | these six |
| `prāntakoṭikāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | connected with cultivation to the final limit |

`Tat + lābhī` yields `tallābhī`; `vikalābhiḥ + na`
appears as `vikalābhir na`. The masculine plural ending
of `prāntakoṭikāḥ` agrees with `guṇāḥ`, qualities, supplied
by the prose.

## 4. Grammar

**The difficult opening.** Taken alone, `dharme vit` permits
more than one surface construe. The commentary explicitly
states `dharmapratisaṃvit pañcabhūmikā`: dharma-pratisaṃvid
belongs to five levels. This controlled semantic expansion
governs the translation without rewriting the transmitted
words.

**Two level ranges.** `Kāmadhyāneṣu` means the desire domain
and the four dhyānas in the first statement. In the second,
`prathamakāmayoḥ` is a dual naming the first dhyāna and
the desire domain. These are ranges of five and two,
not five and two new kinds of discriminating knowledge.

**Incomplete possession.** The instrumental `vikalābhiḥ`
describes possession with members missing. The negation
and `tallābhī` deny that such a person counts as one who
has acquired the pratisaṃvids. The prose supplies the
positive implication: whoever has one necessarily has four.

**The six.** `Ete` refers to the qualities beginning with
araṇā already taught: araṇā, resolve-knowledge, and the
four discriminating knowledges. `Prāntakoṭikāḥ` states
their dependence on final-limit cultivation; it does
not make all six fourth-dhyāna states in their own level.

**Close construe.** The dharma-knower [has its range]
in the desire domain and the dhyānas; [the knowledge]
concerning speech is in the first dhyāna and desire
domain. One with an incomplete set has not acquired
that [pratisaṃvid competence]. These six are final-limit
qualities.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The discriminating knowledge concerning dharma is in
> the desire domain and the dhyānas; that concerning
> speech is in the first dhyāna and desire domain.
> One does not acquire that [fourfold competence] with
> incomplete members. These six are final-limit qualities.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Dharma-pratisaṃvid occurs on five levels: the desire
> domain and the four dhyānas. It does not occur higher,
> because bodies of names are absent there. Nirukti-
> pratisaṃvid occurs only in the desire domain and first
> dhyāna, because reflective application, `vitarka`, is
> absent above the first dhyāna.
>
> Whoever has acquired one pratisaṃvid necessarily has
> all four; an incomplete subset does not constitute
> acquisition of the pratisaṃvids. The six qualities—
> araṇā, resolve-knowledge, and all four pratisaṃvids—are
> acquired through the power of final-limit dhyāna.
> Nirukti-pratisaṃvid is acquired through that power
> although it is not itself supported on the fourth dhyāna.

Source: Pradhan 419|12–420|11. The definition of final-limit
dhyāna begins immediately after this verse and is completed
under VAK 7.41.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Linguistic knowledge is conditioned by the levels on
> which its linguistic objects and operations remain
> available. Yet the four discriminating knowledges form
> one acquired competence: their functions remain distinct,
> while genuine acquisition is not fragmentary. The six
> exceptional qualities share a cultivated basis without
> sharing one identical supporting level.

Systematic unity here means necessary co-acquisition of
differentiated capacities. It does not mean identity of
their objects, knowledge-natures, or levels, all of which
the preceding verses carefully distinguished.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Pañcabhūmika`.** Belonging to five levels. For dharma-
pratisaṃvid these are the desire domain and four dhyānas.
The count follows the presence of the name-complexes
that constitute its object-field.

**`Nāmakāyābhāva`.** Absence of bodies or collections of
names. This is the explicit reason dharma-pratisaṃvid
does not occur above the fourth dhyāna. It is an object-
availability restriction, not a claim that higher levels
are devoid of every kind of knowledge.

**`Vāṅnirukti`.** Speech and linguistic explanation,
treated as having one meaning here. This local equation
does not abolish the distinction between speech and
nirukti-pratisaṃvid as object and knowledge.

**`Vitarka`.** Reflective application or initial application
of thought. Its absence above the first dhyāna supplies
the upper boundary for nirukti-pratisaṃvid in this account.

**`Pratisaṃvillābhin`.** One who has acquired the four
discriminating knowledges. The source reserves this status
for complete fourfold possession.

**`Prāntakoṭika`.** Developed to a terminal or final limit.
The present verse states the six qualities' relation
to that attainment; VAK 7.41 defines the cultivated
fourth dhyāna bearing the designation.

**`Pūrvaprayoga`.** Prior preparation. The prose records
specific disciplines proposed as preparations for the
four knowledges, then another account making the Buddha's
word the preparation for all. These are alternative
preparatory analyses, not parts of the definition of
each knowledge's nature.

## 8. Logical Determination

**Completed level matrix.**

| Pratisaṃvid | Level-range | Stated upper-limit reason |
|---|---|---|
| Dharma | Desire domain plus four dhyānas | No name-complexes above |
| Artha | All levels | Established in VAK 7.39 |
| Nirukti | Desire domain plus first dhyāna | No `vitarka` above first dhyāna |
| Pratibhāna | All levels | Established in VAK 7.39 |

The source also gives conventional or nominal explanations
of the four in sequence: unfailing knowledge concerning
word and syllabic expression; its meaning; singular,
dual, plural, feminine, masculine, and related designations;
and freedom from impediment in that field. The running
quotation is compressed and corrupt around the compound
names. It confirms an ordered designation/meaning/language/
facility analysis without overriding the direct definitions
of VAK 7.38–7.40.

The prose separately defines `nirukti` as derivational
explanation, illustrated by “it is `rūpa` because it is
affected/formable,” and records another view defining
`pratibhāna` through successively arising inspiration.
These are attributed explanatory formulations; the latter
does not replace the verse's speech-and-Path object analysis.

**Complete co-acquisition.**

```text
HasAnyPratisamvid(p)
    → HasDharma(p)
      AND HasArtha(p)
      AND HasNirukti(p)
      AND HasPratibhana(p)
```

This implication concerns acquisition of the technical
pratisaṃvids. A person displaying one ordinary linguistic
skill is not thereby proved to possess all four attainments.
Nor does complete possession make their different functions
interchangeable.

**Preparatory accounts.** One reported distribution assigns
calculation, the Buddha's word, linguistic science, medicine,
and reasoning science as prior preparations, in an order
whose exact attachment is difficult because five disciplines
are juxtaposed with four pratisaṃvids. The prose concludes
that someone without cultivated skill in these cannot
produce the knowledges. Another account describes the
Buddha's word alone as the preparation for all four.
The study preserves the disagreement and does not fabricate
a clean one-to-one table from the awkward list.

**The six-quality dependency.**

```text
SixQualities = {Arana, ResolveKnowledge,
    DharmaPratisamvid, ArthaPratisamvid,
    NiruktiPratisamvid, PratibhanaPratisamvid}

Acquisition(SixQualities)
    dependsOn PowerOf(FinalLimitDhyana)
```

The continuation makes a crucial qualification. Araṇā,
resolve-knowledge, and three pratisaṃvids are enumerated
as forms of the final-limit fourth dhyāna. Nirukti is
the sixth dependent quality: it is acquired through
that dhyāna's power while itself remaining confined to
the desire domain and first dhyāna. Shared acquisition-
ground does not imply identical intrinsic support.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The syntax is controlled without being erased.** The
research file rightly warns about `dharme vit`, but the
Bhāṣya does more than make the “general structure” clear:
it explicitly supplies dharma-pratisaṃvid and its five
levels. The official study translates that determination
while preserving the awkward transmitted form for review.

**Object-availability generates level restriction.** The
two restricted capacities do not receive arbitrary level
assignments. Name-complexes and reflective linguistic
application cease above their respective limits. This
does not make artha and pratibhāna “nonlinguistic” in
every respect; it says their broader level-ranges follow
different object and knowledge determinations.

**Six qualities, five fourth-dhyāna forms.** The research
kārikā study risks calling all six final-edge qualities
one sixfold fourth-dhyāna attainment. The continuation's
own qualification prevents that flattening. Nirukti belongs
to the sixfold dependency-set but is not fourth-dhyāna-
grounded. The distinction between acquisition through
a power and intrinsic level must remain explicit.

**Textual caution.** Besides `dharme vit`, the nominal
definition quotation contains damaged compounds, and
the prior-preparation list has an uncertain distribution.
The source also prints `prāṇtakoṭikāḥ` and `tatṣaḍivadhaṃ`
near the transition. The supported `prāntakoṭika` and
`ṣaḍvidha` readings are used in the study while the
electronic witness remains untouched.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
This verse realizes a central form of our Yoga system:
differentiated knowledge functioning as an acquired whole.
Yoga Vidyā articulates designation, meaning, language,
and fitting Path-oriented expression. Yoga Technē, its
kriyā–karma companion, articulates the preparation and
cultivated power through which those capacities become
available.

Their unity is systematic rather than homogeneous. Each
capacity has its own object and level, yet technical
acquisition of one entails the complete fourfold competence.
The six qualities extend this structure: one final-limit
cultivation grounds several powers without making their
individual operations or supports identical.

This supplies a precise Yoga Vidyā–Technē relation. The
knowledge-system determines what practical cultivation
must make possible; the cultivated attainment reciprocally
makes the differentiated knowledge effective. That is
project reconstruction, while the Sanskrit and Bhāṣya
remain the controlling source layers.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The five- and two-level ranges, their reasons, complete
co-acquisition, preparatory disagreement, and six-quality
dependency are source-checked. The compressed opening
and preparation-list attachment remain flagged.
