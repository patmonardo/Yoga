# VAK_7.32 — Knowledge-Grounded Fearlessness and the Mindfulness Triad

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
वैशारद्यं चतुर्धा तु यथाद्यदशमे बले ।
द्वितीयसप्तमे चैव स्मृतिप्रज्ञात्मकं त्रयम् ॥ ७.३२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
vaiśāradyaṃ caturdhā tu yathādyadaśame bale |
dvitīyasaptame caiva smṛtiprajñātmakaṃ trayam || 7.32 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The verse
turns from the powers to the next seven of the eighteen
distinctive qualities: four fearlessnesses and three
establishments of mindfulness. The Bhāṣya distinguishes
the relations through which each group is explained.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
vaiśāradyam | caturdhā | tu | yathā | ādya-daśame | bale |
dvitīya-saptame | ca | eva | smṛti-prajñā-ātmakam | trayam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `vaiśāradyam` | nominative singular neuter | fearlessness |
| `caturdhā` | adverb | in four ways, fourfold |
| `tu` | particle | however, now |
| `yathā` | comparative adverb | as, corresponding to |
| `ādyadaśame` | nominative dual neuter coordinated adjective | the first and tenth |
| `bale` | nominative dual neuter | the two powers |
| `dvitīyasaptame` | nominative dual neuter coordinated adjective | the second and seventh [powers] |
| `ca eva` | particles | and likewise |
| `smṛtiprajñātmakam` | nominative singular neuter adjective | having mindfulness and discriminative knowing as its nature |
| `trayam` | nominative singular neuter collective | the triad |

The dual forms enumerate pairs of powers. Their contextual
construe is nominative under the comparison, not an occasion
located “in the first and tenth.” `Yathā + ādya` yields
`yathādya`; `prajñā + ātmaka` yields `prajñātmaka`.

## 4. Grammar

**Four correspondences in order.** `Vaiśāradyaṃ caturdhā`
states the fourfold division. The two dual expressions
then provide first/tenth and second/seventh as the sequence
of corresponding powers. The Bhāṣya expands each comparison
with `yathā ... evam`, as that power, so this fearlessness.

**Correspondence is not identity.** The commentary explicitly
examines whether fearlessness is knowledge itself. Its
answer distinguishes `jñānakṛtam`, effected by knowledge,
from `jñānam eva`, knowledge itself. The comparative verse
must be read with that qualification.

**A new subject in the final pāda.** `Trayam` names the
three establishments of mindfulness, supplied by the prose.
It does not reduce the four fearlessnesses to three.
`Smṛtiprajñātmakam` agrees with the collective singular;
the commentary expands it in the plural as the three
having mindfulness and clear comprehension as their nature.

**Close construe.** Fearlessness is fourfold, corresponding
to the first and tenth powers and likewise to the second
and seventh. The triad [of mindfulness establishments]
has mindfulness and discriminative knowing as its nature.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Fearlessness is fourfold, corresponding to the first
> and tenth powers, and likewise to the second and seventh.
> The triad has mindfulness and discriminative knowing
> as its nature.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The Buddha's four fearlessnesses concern complete awakening,
> exhaustion of the influxes, the obstructive factors he
> declares to disciples, and the path he teaches for leading
> out. Their knowledge-grounds correspond respectively to
> the first, tenth, second, and seventh powers. Fearlessness
> is absence of fear effected by these knowledges; it is
> not simply knowledge itself.
>
> The three establishments of mindfulness are distinguished
> by the assembly's response: listening, not listening,
> or a mixture of both. They have mindfulness and clear
> comprehension as their nature. Their Buddha-specificity
> is explained by abandonment together with residual traces,
> or alternatively by the extraordinary non-arising of
> reactive pleasure and related responses toward his own
> disciples, where such reactions would have particular occasion.

Source: Pradhan 413|20–414|16. The passage refers to the
sūtras but quotes only abbreviated formulations. This
study does not reconstruct complete sūtra declarations
from those fragments.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Knowledge grounds fearlessness without becoming identical
> with it. The mindfulness triad receives a different
> explanation: its constitution is mindfulness and clear
> comprehension, while its threefold differentiation comes
> from the assembly. Its distinctiveness is then defended
> against the observation that disciples too may remain
> composed. Constitution, occasion, and distinctive attainment
> are separate determinations.

The source therefore does more than attach seven new
names to the powers. It explains one group through what
produces it and another through its constituents and
the situations in which it is established.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vaiśāradya` / `nirbhayatā`.** Fearlessness / absence of
fear. The latter is the explicit gloss. Confidence can
serve as an explanatory word, but should not erase the
source's defining reference to fearlessness.

**`Jñānakṛta`.** Effected or produced by knowledge. This
is the stated relation between the relevant knowledges
and fearlessness. It does not specify a separate detailed
temporal mechanism or make fearlessness an eleventh knowledge.

**`Antarāyika dharma`.** An obstructive factor. The third
fearlessness concerns the factors declared obstructive
to disciples; its corresponding power is the second,
concerning action and maturation.

**`Niryāṇa`.** Leading out, escape. The fourth declaration
concerns the path taught for this purpose. It should not
be narrowed to ordination merely because disciples are
mentioned in the same sentence.

**`Smṛti` and `saṃprajñāna`.** Mindfulness or recollection,
and clear comprehension. The prose uses `saṃprajñāna`
to explain the verse's `prajñā` in the triad's constitution.

**`Parṣadbheda`.** Difference in the assembly. This supplies
the triad's situational differentiation, not the fourfold
body/feeling/mind/dharma object scheme encountered earlier.

**`Savāsanaprahāṇa`.** Abandonment together with residual
traces. The concise source phrase gives one answer to
the challenge about Buddha-specificity. “Residual traces”
should not be silently equated with a modern psychological
diagnosis or with every use of `anuśaya` elsewhere.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Fearlessness concerning | Corresponding power | Number |
|---|---|---:|
| Complete awakening | Possibility and impossibility | 1 |
| Exhausted influxes | Influx-exhaustion | 10 |
| Declared obstructive factors | Karmic maturation | 2 |
| The taught path leading out | Courses leading everywhere | 7 |

The prior knowledge-category analyses of these powers
provide the cognitive correspondences. The present prose
then gives the qualification:

```text
RelevantKnowledge → EffectsFearlessness
Fearlessness ≠ KnowledgeItself
```

For the triad:

| Assembly situation | Relevant composure |
|---|---|
| Disciples listen | No reactive delight |
| Disciples do not listen | No resentment |
| Some listen and others do not | Neither reaction toward the divided assembly |

This is the local response-context, not a general assertion
that the Buddha has no wholesome joy or concern for others.
The question explicitly acknowledges comparable composure
in a disciple, then asks why these are unshared Buddha-qualities.

The two answers are retained separately:

```text
Answer 1: Abandonment together with residual traces.

Answer 2: In relation to one's own disciples, the occasion
    for pleasure and related reactions is especially strong;
    their non-arising there establishes the extraordinary quality.
```

`Athavā` introduces the second explanation as an alternative.
It should not be reduced to a repetition of the first or
turned into a claim that disciples can never show composure.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Do not collapse ground and result.** The statement
`jñānakṛtaṃ vaiśāradyaṃ yujyate / na jñānam eva` directly
controls how the opening correspondences are understood.
The fearlessnesses can be explained through the powers'
knowledges without being ontologically identified with them.

**The relational situation matters.** The alternative
explanation of the triad concerns the teacher's own disciples.
An observer's indifference to an unrelated assembly is
not automatically the same determination. The source
locates the extraordinary quality where the occasion
for personal reaction would otherwise be especially strong.

**Textual caution.** The source prints `karmasvakajñānavala`
in the third comparison. The verse's explicit “second”
and the prior power-list secure its correspondence with
karmic maturation; this malformed expression is not made
a new power-name. The assembly passage also contains
damaged forms of “listening” and “not listening.” Its
three cases and the contrast of delight and resentment
remain readable, but the abbreviated wording is not
silently presented as a critical reconstruction.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā articulates the knowledge grounding a declaration.
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, concerns the concrete
exercise of teaching and its relation to those addressed.
The knowledge-ground and the resulting fearlessness must
remain related without being collapsed into one category.

The mindfulness triad adds the response of the assembly
to that relation. Listening, refusal to listen, and mixed
reception do not call for reactive delight or resentment;
mindfulness and clear comprehension remain the stated
constitution. For our Yoga system, this is a precise
way to investigate teaching as an undertaking whose
determination includes both knowing and conduct toward
the recipients of instruction.

The project interpretation should preserve the source's
two explanations of Buddha-specificity and its acknowledgment
of disciple composure. It should not replace the passage
with a generic rule about emotional suppression or personal
confidence. The distinctions of knowledge-ground, practical
fearlessness, and mindful relation to an assembly do the
philosophical work.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The four correspondences, explicit nonidentity claim,
triad's constitution, and alternative explanations of
distinctiveness have been checked against the running source.
