# VAK_7.08 — Seven Grounds for the Ten Knowledges

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
स्वभावप्रतिपक्षाभ्यामाकाराकारगोचरात् ।
प्रयोगकृतकृत्यत्वहेतूपचयतो दश ॥ ७.८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
svabhāvapratipakṣābhyām ākārākāragocarāt |
prayogakṛtakṛtyatvahetūpacayato daśa || 7.8 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā study. The Bhāṣya now distributes
seven grounds across the ten knowledge-designations explicitly; the
allocation need not await later verses. Its raw verse quotation has
`pratipakṣānyām` and `hetūpacayoto`, whereas the study reading above
uses `pratipakṣābhyām` and `hetūpacayato`. These working normalizations
are recorded rather than treated as a diplomatic transcription.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
svabhāva-pratipakṣābhyām | ākāra-ākāra-gocarāt |
prayoga-kṛta-kṛtyatva-hetu-upacayataḥ | daśa
```

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed series into seven grounds:

```text
svabhāva | pratipakṣa | ākāra | ākāra-ālambana |
prayoga | kṛtakṛtyatva | hetūpacaya
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `svabhāva-pratipakṣābhyām` | instrumental dual compound | by intrinsic nature and counteraction |
| `ākāra-ākāra-gocarāt` | ablative singular compressed compound | from aspect, and from aspect together with object-range |
| `prayoga` | member of final compound series | preparatory application |
| `kṛta-kṛtyatva` | abstract noun in `-tva`, compound member | the state of having done what had to be done |
| `hetu-upacaya` | compound member | accumulation of causes |
| `-taḥ` | adverbial suffix expressing a ground | on account of, from the standpoint of |
| `daśa` | numeral, nominative plural here | ten [knowledges] |

`Kṛta` means done; `kṛtya` means to be done. Their relation gives the
accomplished-task sense before the abstract suffix `-tva` is added.
`Hetu + upacaya` yields `hetūpacaya`.

## 4. Grammar

The understood subject is `jñānāni`, knowledges, and the governing
predicate is supplied by the Bhāṣya as `vyavasthāpyante`, “are
established.” The numeral gives the result; the preceding expressions
give its grounds.

```text
[jñānāni] — daśa [vyavasthāpyante]
    by / on account of the stated grounds
```

The instrumental dual gathers the first two grounds. The next compound
must be unfolded as aspect alone and aspect with object-range. The
Bhāṣya distinguishes `ākārataḥ` from `ākārālambanataḥ`; the repeated
`ākāra` therefore carries a real distinction and is not redundant text.

The final compound shares its adverbial force across preparation,
accomplished-task status, and causal accumulation. Its three grounds are
not three stages that every knowledge must traverse in the order printed.

The prose introduces the explanation with `saptabhiḥ kila kāraṇaiḥ`,
“through seven grounds, it is said.” `Kila` marks the explanation as
reported; it does not by itself establish a developed objection or
identify a particular school.

**Close construe.** By intrinsic nature and counteraction; from aspect
and aspect-with-object-range; from preparation, having accomplished
what had to be done, and accumulation of causes—[the knowledges are
established as] ten.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> By intrinsic nature and counteraction, by aspect and aspect together
> with object-range, by preparation, the state of having accomplished
> what had to be done, and accumulation of causes, [the knowledges are] ten.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The ten knowledges are established, it is said, through seven grounds.
> Conventional knowledge is distinguished by its nature, as not being
> knowledge of ultimate reality. Dharma- and anvaya-knowledge are
> distinguished by counteraction with respect to the desire and higher
> domains. Suffering- and origination-knowledge differ by aspect while
> their object-support is undifferentiated. Cessation- and Path-knowledge
> differ by both aspect and object-support. Other-mind knowledge is named
> through its preparation, undertaken to know another mind, although it
> also knows mental factors. Exhaustion-knowledge is distinguished through
> accomplished-task status, arising in a continuum whose work has been
> done. Non-arising-knowledge is distinguished through accumulation of
> causes, having all uncontaminated [dharmas] as causes.

This distribution follows Pradhan 395|03–395|09. The source's
`prathamataḥ` stands immediately between the exhaustion and causal-
accumulation explanations. It may complete the former as “arising first
in a continuum whose task is accomplished”; its exact punctuation and
attachment remain marked for textual review rather than silently omitted
from the source record.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The tenfold classification is justified through different grounds of
> distinction. A knowledge can receive its designation through what it
> is, what it counteracts, its mode of apprehension, its mode together
> with its object, the aim of its preparation, the accomplished condition
> of its continuum, or its causal basis. The relevant ground must be
> specified for each designation.

These are seven explanatory grounds supporting ten names, not seven
successive divisions whose Cartesian product generates ten exclusive
species. The chapter's earlier overlaps remain intact. In particular,
identity of object-support does not entail identity of knowledge, and a
name derived from preparation need not enumerate every possible object.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Svabhāva` — intrinsic nature.** The ground assigned to conventional
knowledge. The prose explains it with `aparamārthajñānatvāt`, because
it is not ultimate knowledge. This must be read alongside VAK 7.03:
conventional knowledge can have unconditioned dharmas as objects. The
present nature-predicate does not impose a new exclusion on object-range.

**`Pratipakṣa` — counteraction / counteragent.** The ground distinguishing
dharma- and anvaya-knowledge in relation to the desire and higher domains.
This is a functional relation, not merely a restatement of a spatial
location. Its exact scope receives further qualification in VAK 7.09;
no universal “dharma-knowledge counters only the desire domain” rule is
established here.

**`Ākāra` — aspect.** Suffering- and origination-knowledge are
distinguished by aspect because their object-support is not different:
`ālambanābhedāt`. The same field is known under different truth-aspects.
The four aspects of each are not enumerated in this verse.

**`Ākāra-gocara` / `ākāra-ālambana` — aspect together with object-range /
object-support.** The kārikā expression and the Bhāṣya expansion. For
cessation- and Path-knowledge, both aspect and object-support differ.
This paired ground must remain distinct from aspect alone.

**`Prayoga` — preparatory application.** Other-mind knowledge is so named
because the undertaking aims at knowing another's mind and the cognition
is accomplished from that preparation. The Bhāṣya explicitly notes that
mental factors are also known. Here the preparation's aim explains the
designation; the name is not an exclusive object-definition.

**`Kṛtakṛtyatva` — having accomplished what had to be done.** The ground
assigned to exhaustion-knowledge. The prose refers to its arising in a
`kṛtakṛtyasaṃtāna`, a continuum whose task has been accomplished. The
criterion is the continuum's accomplished status, not a bare verbal
assertion of success.

**`Hetūpacaya` — accumulation of causes.** The ground assigned to
non-arising-knowledge. The precise local statement is
`sarvānāsravahetukatvāt`, having all uncontaminated [dharmas] as causes.
This is more specific than a general claim that its causes have become
stronger. The passage does not here enumerate the causal relations or
authorize a global all-to-all causal model across practitioners.

## 8. Logical Determination

| Ground | Knowledge-designations | Bhāṣya reason |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Conventional | Not ultimate knowledge |
| Counteraction | Dharma, anvaya | Desire-domain / higher-domain counteraction |
| Aspect | Suffering, origination | Object-support is not different |
| Aspect and object-support | Cessation, Path | Both differ |
| Preparation | Other-mind | The undertaking aims at knowing another mind |
| Accomplished-task status | Exhaustion | Arises in a continuum whose task is done |
| Causal accumulation | Non-arising | Has all uncontaminated dharmas as causes |

The distribution accounts for the ten names:

```text
1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 10
```

This arithmetic counts designations under their stated explanatory
grounds. It does not count ten disjoint sets of knowledge-events.

```text
SameObjectSupport(SufferingKnowledge, OriginationKnowledge)
AND DifferentAspects(SufferingKnowledge, OriginationKnowledge)
    → DistinctTruthKnowledgeDesignations

DifferentObjectSupports(CessationKnowledge, PathKnowledge)
AND DifferentAspects(CessationKnowledge, PathKnowledge)
    → DistinctTruthKnowledgeDesignations
```

Naming through preparation has another logical form:

```text
PreparationAimsAt(KnowingAnotherMind)
AND KnowledgeAccomplishedFromThatPreparation
    → Designation: OtherMindKnowledge

This designation does not exclude mental factors from its object-range.
```

The grounds for the terminal names differ. Accomplished-task status is
assigned to exhaustion; causal accumulation to non-arising. They should
not be merged into one undifferentiated “completion” criterion.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The allocation is already explicit.** The exploratory kārikā study
deferred the exact mapping. The immediate Bhāṣya supplies all seven
allocations, so the official study presents them here. No extrapolation
from the names is needed.

**The force of a preparatory aim.** VAK 7.05 classified other-mind
knowledge within four knowledge-categories without asserting that those
categories causally produce it. The present passage separately explains
its accomplishment from an undertaking aimed at another mind. These
are different relations: classificatory inclusion and preparation.

**Textual caution.** The raw introductory question contains `vīṇi` and
the prose includes `kāmathāturdhvadhātu`. Their exact restoration is not
required to recover the explicit sevenfold allocation. The placement of
`prathamataḥ` is retained as a local issue. The reading of causal
accumulation follows the transmitted `sarvānāsravahetukatvāt` without
inventing a fuller causal explanation.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** This verse
gives our Yoga system a concrete method for articulating its knowledge.
Yoga Vidyā determines the grounds through which a cognition receives
its meaning. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, develops the
counteraction, preparation, accomplished work, and causal conditions
through which that knowing becomes an operative capacity.

The distinction between them remains reciprocal. Preparation already
has a determinate cognitive aim; knowledge is itself distinguished
through practical relations. Vidyā and Technē therefore work within
one articulated system rather than occupying separate lists of purely
theoretical and purely practical terms.

For our reconstruction, the seven grounds supply differentiated questions
to ask of a capacity. We must preserve which ground explains which
designation, and avoid imposing identical entries under every heading.
Meaning becomes determinate through the relevant relations: nature,
counteraction, aspect, object-support, undertaking, accomplishment,
and causal basis.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The seven-to-ten
allocation is explicit. The precise attachment of `prathamataḥ` and the
detailed scope of the causal statement remain open to review; the
counteraction qualification proceeds in the next kārikā.
