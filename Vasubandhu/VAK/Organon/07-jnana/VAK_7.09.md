# VAK_7.09 — Object-Range and Counteractive Efficacy

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
धर्मज्ञानं निरोधे यन्मार्गे वा भावनापथे ।
त्रिधातुप्रतिपक्षस्तत् कामधातोस्तु नान्वयम् ॥ ७.९ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
dharmajñānaṃ nirodhe yan mārge vā bhāvanāpathe |
tridhātupratipakṣas tat kāmadhātos tu nānvayam || 7.9 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā study. The raw Bhāṣya quotation
joins the opening as `dharmajñānanirodhe`; the study reading separates
`dharmajñānaṃ nirodhe`, supported by the following prose explanation.
The verse qualifies the counteraction distinction stated in VAK 7.08.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
dharma-jñānam | nirodhe | yat | mārge | vā | bhāvanā-pathe |
tri-dhātu-pratipakṣaḥ | tat | kāma-dhātoḥ | tu | na | anvayam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `dharmajñānam` | nominative singular neuter compound | dharma-knowledge |
| `nirodhe` | locative singular masculine | concerning cessation |
| `yat` | nominative singular neuter relative pronoun | which; agrees with dharma-knowledge |
| `mārge` | locative singular masculine | concerning the Path |
| `vā` | alternative particle | or |
| `bhāvanāpathe` | locative singular masculine compound | in the Path of Cultivation |
| `tridhātupratipakṣaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound noun | counteragent to the three domains |
| `tat` | nominative singular neuter correlative pronoun | that [knowledge] |
| `kāmadhātoḥ` | genitive singular masculine here | of / with respect to the desire domain |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but, whereas |
| `na` | negative particle | not |
| `anvayam` | nominative singular neuter elliptical designation | anvaya-[knowledge] |

`Yat + mārge` appears as `yan mārge`; `na + anvayam` yields `nānvayam`.
The genitive `kāmadhātoḥ` specifies the domain to which the omitted
predicate “counteragent” would relate.

## 4. Grammar

**Relative and correlative.** `Yat … tat` joins the qualified knowledge
to its predicate:

```text
yat dharmajñānam
    nirodhe vā mārge
    bhāvanāpathe
tat — tridhātupratipakṣaḥ
```

The masculine predicate noun `pratipakṣaḥ` does not require the neuter
subject `tat` to change gender. The statement identifies that knowledge
as a counteragent.

**Two kinds of locative relation.** `Nirodhe` and `mārge` specify the
truth concerned; `bhāvanāpathe` specifies Path-membership. The occurrence
of Path as truth-object and Cultivation as the Path in which the
knowledge is included must remain distinct.

The Bhāṣya makes the latter restriction explicit with
`bhāvanāmārgasaṃgṛhīte`, “included in the Path of Cultivation.” Its
dual `nirodhamārgajñāne` identifies the cessation- and Path-knowledge
forms of the dharma-knowledge already under discussion. It does not
remove that governing qualification.

**The negative clause.** Supply `pratipakṣaḥ` and the full designation
`anvayajñānam`:

```text
anvaya[jñānaṃ] tu
    kāmadhātoḥ na [pratipakṣaḥ]
```

The prose adds `sarvathā`, “in every way / under any circumstances,”
making the exclusion categorical. It is not merely a lack of evidence
for desire-domain counteraction.

**Close construe.** Which dharma-knowledge concerns cessation or Path
and belongs to Cultivation—that is a counteragent to the three domains.
Anvaya-[knowledge], however, is not a counteragent to the desire domain.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> That dharma-knowledge which concerns cessation or the Path in the
> Path of Cultivation is a counteragent to the three domains; anvaya-
> knowledge, however, is not a counteragent to the desire domain.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Dharma-knowledge was called the counteragent to the desire domain
> with regard to its complete counteraction of that domain. There is,
> however, a further qualification: its cessation- and Path-knowledge
> forms, when included in the Path of Cultivation, are counteragents
> to all three domains. Anvaya-knowledge is in no way a counteragent
> to the desire domain.

The explanation follows Pradhan 395|10–395|16. The opening
`sakalasya sakalapratipakṣatvāt` is compressed: the local Bhāṣya study
construes it through complete dharma-knowledge and complete desire-domain
counteraction. That retrospective summary does not say that each isolated
knowledge-event abandons every desire-domain affliction at once.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A knowledge's truth-object and its counteractive efficacy require
> separate determination. Dharma-knowledge retains its established
> identity, while its cessation- and Path-forms in Cultivation have
> counteractive reach across the three domains. The corresponding
> extension does not run in reverse: anvaya-knowledge never counters
> the desire domain.

The wider range belongs to a specified conjunction of knowledge-kind,
truth-object, and Path-membership. It is not an unrestricted predicate
of every dharma-knowledge. Nor does efficacy across three domains mean
that its object-range has simply been replaced by anvaya-knowledge's
object-range.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Dharmajñāna` — dharma-knowledge.** The knowledge-kind previously
specified through the desire-domain truth-field. The present statement
selects its cessation- and Path-forms within Cultivation. The selection
is essential to the three-domain efficacy claim.

**`Nirodha` and `mārga` — cessation and Path.** The two truth-objects
named in the condition. Suffering- and origination-knowledge are not
included in this particular extension merely because they also belong
to the dharma-knowledge field.

**`Bhāvanāpatha` / `bhāvanāmārga` — Path of Cultivation.** The kārikā
and prose expressions for the relevant Path-membership. The chapter's
earlier Seeing discussion does not supply an interchangeable condition.
As established in VAK 6.28, strict Seeing has fifteen moments; the
sixteenth begins Cultivation.

**`Saṃgṛhīta` — included / comprised.** The commentary's participle
specifies inclusion within Cultivation. This is more exact than treating
`bhāvanā` here as any repeated practice whatsoever.

**`Pratipakṣa` — counteragent.** The functional predicate. In the
present discussion it concerns opposition to the afflictive field of
the domains, not destruction of domains as locations or mere possession
of information about them.

**`Tridhātu` — the three domains.** Desire, form, and formlessness.
These are the existential domains operative in the Path's counteraction,
not Chapter 1's eighteen cognition-domains.

**`Anvayajñāna` — anvaya-knowledge.** Its higher-domain truth-range
does not establish desire-domain counteraction. The Bhāṣya explicitly
excludes the latter in every case; it does not state a reciprocal
exception corresponding to the dharma-knowledge extension.

**`Sarvathā` — in every way / under any circumstances.** The prose
strengthening of the negative clause. Its force must remain attached
to anvaya-knowledge's exclusion from desire-domain counteraction.

## 8. Logical Determination

The positive rule requires all stated conditions:

```text
DharmaKnowledge(x)
AND [CessationKnowledge(x) OR PathKnowledge(x)]
AND IncludedInCultivationPath(x)
    → CounteragentAcrossThreeDomains(x)
```

The negative rule is categorical:

```text
AnvayaKnowledge(x)
    → NOT CounteragentToDesireDomain(x)
```

| Knowledge | Truth qualification | Path qualification | Determination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dharma-knowledge | Cessation or Path | Included in Cultivation | Counteragent across the three domains |
| Anvaya-knowledge | No exception supplied | No exception supplied | Never counteragent to the desire domain |

These statements must not be converted into the broader rule:

```text
EveryDharmaKnowledge → CounteragentAcrossThreeDomains
```

The source gives a qualified extension, not that universal assertion.
Likewise, counteractive range does not entail a single simultaneous
operation against every affliction in all three domains.

VAK 7.03 and VAK 7.09 therefore contribute different relations:

```text
hasObjectRange
hasCounteractiveRange
```

The second cannot be mechanically inferred from the first. Their
interaction must preserve truth-kind and Path-membership. The summary
counteraction allocation of VAK 7.08 remains intelligible through this
more precise asymmetrical qualification.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A qualification of the preceding ground.** The Bhāṣya's `api tu`,
“however,” introduces the exact extension needed after assigning
dharma-knowledge to desire-domain counteraction. The classification
is refined through its conditions rather than replaced with a new
unqualified division.

**No change of object follows automatically.** The source does not
say that dharma-knowledge becomes anvaya-knowledge by acquiring wider
counteractive reach. Object and efficacy are distinguishable relations
of the same knowledge. The truth-object qualification concerns cessation
or Path relative to the established field; it does not classify those
objects themselves as contaminated desire-domain dharmas.

**Textual caution.** The raw witness contains `pratīpakṣo`, the joined
`dharmajñānanirodhe`, and `kāmadhātuporatipakṣaḥ`. The study follows
the local witnesses' readable technical forms while preserving these
source irregularities in the record. The doctrinal conjunction is
explicit in the explanatory prose; the compressed opening use of
`sakala` remains suitable for further philological review.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** This verse
gives our Yoga system a precise relation between intelligibility and
effective practice. Yoga Vidyā articulates what the knowledge knows;
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, determines the counteraction
in which that knowing can function. Their reciprocal determination
requires the conditions joining object, truth-kind, and Path-stage.

The distinction becomes especially productive here because practical
reach can exceed what a simple reading of the initial domain-label
would suggest. That reach is established through definite conditions:
cessation or Path, dharma-knowledge, and inclusion in Cultivation.
Our reconstruction should preserve those conditions whenever it speaks
of extending a capacity's application.

Thus knowing a field and acting effectively upon a field belong
together without becoming the same relation. The asymmetry with
anvaya-knowledge further requires that a successful extension in one
direction be demonstrated independently in the other. The source's
specific counteraction law supplies a disciplined model for this
Vidyā–Technē articulation.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The qualified
three-domain reach and categorical anvaya exclusion are explicit.
The compressed retrospective sentence and minor textual forms remain
marked for review.
