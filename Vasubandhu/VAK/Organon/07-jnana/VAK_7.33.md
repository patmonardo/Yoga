# VAK_7.33 — Great Compassion: Constitution, Greatness, and Eight Distinctions

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
महाकृपा संवृतिधीः संभाराकारगोचरैः ।
समत्वादाधिमात्र्याच्च नानाकरणमष्टधा ॥ ७.३३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
mahākṛpā saṃvṛtidhīḥ saṃbhārākāragocaraiḥ |
samatvād ādhimātryāc ca nānākaraṇam aṣṭadhā || 7.33 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The Bhāṣya
uses `mahākaruṇā` for the verse's `mahākṛpā`. It first
determines its nature, then explains five grounds of
greatness, then compares compassion and great compassion
in eight respects. These are three related questions,
not one undifferentiated enumeration.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
mahā-kṛpā | saṃvṛti-dhīḥ | saṃbhāra-ākāra-gocaraiḥ |
samatvāt | ādhimātryāt | ca | nānā-karaṇam | aṣṭadhā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `mahākṛpā` | nominative singular feminine | great compassion |
| `saṃvṛtidhīḥ` | nominative singular feminine compound | conventional knowing |
| `saṃbhārākāragocaraiḥ` | instrumental plural coordinated compound | through accumulation, aspect, and object-field |
| `samatvāt` | ablative singular neuter | because of equality |
| `ādhimātryāt` | ablative singular neuter | because of surpassing intensity |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `nānākaraṇam` | nominative singular neuter | differentiation, distinction |
| `aṣṭadhā` | adverb | in eight ways |

`Saṃbhāra + ākāra` yields `saṃbhārākāra`; `samatvāt +
ādhimātryāt` yields `samatvād ādhimātryāt`. Before `ca`,
the final dental of the latter appears as `c`.

## 4. Grammar

**The initial equation.** `Mahākṛpā` and `saṃvṛtidhīḥ`
are nominative singular feminine. The prose makes the
constitution explicit: `saṃvṛtijñānātmikā mahākaruṇā`,
great compassion has conventional knowledge as its nature.

**Five grounds of greatness.** Supply “is called great”
with the instrumental triad and two causal ablatives.
The prose asks why it is called great compassion and
answers under these five headings. Its `ālambana` expands
the verse's `gocara` as object-field.

**A new comparison.** Supply the two compared terms with
`nānākaraṇam`: the prose asks the difference between
`karuṇā` and `mahākaruṇā`. The eightfold differentiation
is not a count of the preceding five grounds.

**Paired values in the commentary.** Expressions such as
`adveṣāmohasvabhāvatvāt` distribute their two terms in
the order compassion / great compassion. Likewise,
`caturdhyānacaturthadhyāna` means four dhyānas / fourth
dhyāna. The second member is a definite support restriction,
not “especially” the fourth while leaving other levels open.

**Close construe.** Great compassion is conventional knowing.
It is great through accumulation, aspect, and object-field,
because of equality and surpassing intensity. Its distinction
from compassion is eightfold.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Great compassion is conventional knowing. It is great
> through accumulation, aspect, and object-field, through
> equality and surpassing intensity. The differentiation
> is eightfold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Great compassion has conventional knowledge as its nature;
> otherwise it could not take all beings as its object
> and the three forms of suffering as its aspects.
>
> It is called great because it arises from a great
> accumulation of merit and knowledge, takes threefold
> suffering as its aspect, ranges over the three domains,
> operates equally toward all beings, and has no greater
> compassion beyond it.
>
> Compassion and great compassion differ in nature, aspect,
> object, supporting level, continuum, acquisition, protection,
> and a final compassion-comparison whose compressed wording
> requires textual review.

Source: Pradhan 414|17–415|10. The first six paired
distinctions are explicit. The last two are retained
with the limits of the local wording rather than supplied
from an unverified reconstruction.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Great compassion is determined through a form of knowing
> capable of addressing beings and their suffering. Its
> greatness is articulated through preparation, aspect,
> reach, equality, and unsurpassed degree. The same name
> “compassion” does not settle its nature or scope: the
> comparison locates each form within a definite system
> of supports, attainments, and functions.

“Conventional” is the knowledge classification given here,
not a judgment that the Buddha's great compassion is
inferior or mistaken. Conversely, the source does not
make every conventional cognition great compassion merely
because that category can encompass the required field.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Saṃvṛtijñānātmikā`.** Having conventional knowledge as
its nature. This constitution differs from VAK 7.32's
knowledge-produced fearlessness: the relation here is
explicitly constitutive.

**`Mahāpuṇyajñānasaṃbhāra`.** Great accumulation of merit
and knowledge. This is the stated preparation from which
great compassion arises, not a prescribed numerical total.

**`Triduḥkhatā`.** Threefold suffering. The local prose
gives the count; the established doctrinal expansion is
the suffering of pain, of change, and of conditioned
formations. That expansion is a study gloss, not a list
spelled out in this sentence.

**`Tridhātuka`.** Of the three existential domains: desire,
form, and formlessness. Here these specify the object-range;
they are not the supporting levels of great compassion.

**`Samavṛtti`.** Equal operation toward all beings. This
explains equality. Equal concern does not by itself specify
identical actions toward every being in every circumstance.

**`Adhimātratva`.** Surpassing intensity or degree. The
prose explains it through the absence of anything greater,
not merely through comparison with a weak instance.

**`Adveṣa` / `amoha`.** Non-hatred / non-delusion. The
paired nature distinction is stronger than saying ordinary
compassion is merely associated with non-hatred and great
compassion merely includes non-delusion. These are the
respective constitutive determinations in the comparison.

**`Paritrāṇa`.** Protection or rescue. The source contrasts
non-protection with protection. It does not here explain
the mechanism or assert that every being is immediately
and unconditionally liberated.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The five grounds of greatness.**

| Ground | Bhāṣya determination |
|---|---|
| Accumulation | Arising from great merit-and-knowledge accumulation |
| Aspect | Threefold suffering |
| Object-field | The three existential domains |
| Equality | Equal operation toward all beings |
| Surpassing degree | Nothing greater beyond it |

**The eightfold comparison.**

| Coordinate | Compassion | Great compassion |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Non-hatred | Non-delusion |
| Aspect | One form of suffering | Three forms of suffering |
| Object-field | One domain | Three domains |
| Supporting level | Four dhyānas | Fourth dhyāna |
| Continuum | Disciples and others | Buddha |
| Acquisition | Through desire-domain detachment | Through Bhavāgra detachment |
| Protection | Non-protection | Protection |
| Final comparison | Paired values unresolved in local wording | `atulyakaruṇāyanācca` requires review |

The one-domain member is understood as the desire domain
in this established contrast; the local compound itself
compresses it to “one / three domains.” Similarly, the
one-form suffering member is conventionally expanded as
suffering of pain; the verse's local prose gives only
the one/three contrast.

The eighth expression should not be replaced automatically
by the fifth greatness-ground, unsurpassed intensity.
That would make a doubtful comparison look settled merely
because a related determination occurs earlier.

Object-range and support-range differ sharply: great
compassion concerns beings across all three domains while
its support is the fourth dhyāna. Its acquisition through
Bhavāgra detachment is a third relation, not another
name for either object or support.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Constitution and scope explain one another.** The source
states that without conventional knowledge the universal
being-object and threefold suffering-aspect would not
be established. That is a specific argument about great
compassion's classification. It is not a general reduction
of concern for others to detached factual description.

**Two defects require restraint.** The greatness explanation
contains a duplicated equality sentence under a malformed
intensity heading. The verse and the clear “nothing greater”
clause secure five grounds; the duplicate is not counted
as a sixth. The final `atulyakaruṇāyanācca` is also too
compressed to fix its paired values confidently. The
study preserves the eightfold count while leaving that
last comparison open.

**Technical compassion is not every ordinary act of kindness.**
The four-dhyāna support and desire-detachment acquisition
locate the compassion being compared within a cultivated
technical account. “Ordinary compassion” can be a convenient
contrast-label, but should not make the non-protection
predicate a dismissal of every helpful action by a non-Buddha.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Great compassion makes the relation of knowing and practical
concern explicit in our Yoga system. Yoga Vidyā determines
beings under the aspects of suffering; Yoga Technē, its
kriyā–karma companion, concerns the undertaking of protection
and assistance. The source supplies their contact through
constitution, field, preparation, and efficacy, while our
project terms remain interpretive.

The five grounds keep “great” from becoming an empty
commendation. The eightfold comparison then asks a different
question: what specifically distinguishes the two forms?
Our reconstruction should retain both structures. It must
also preserve the distinction between equal concern and
the determinate means of helping, which this passage does
not itself enumerate.

Meaning here emerges through the connected determinations:
what compassion is, what it knows, where it is supported,
how it is acquired, and what function is attributed to it.
An unresolved final expression should remain visible within
that system rather than being completed by a convenient
but unverified gloss.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The constitution, five greatness-grounds, and first six
paired differences are explicit. Protection is retained
at the source's level of compression; the eighth comparison
remains open for textual review.
