# VAK_7.56 Bhāṣya — Other-Mind Knowledge, Provenance, and Chapter Closure

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> cetojñānaṃ tu tattredhā tarkavidyākṛtaṃ ca yat |
> jānate nārakā ādau nṛṇāṃ notpattilabhikam || 7.56 ||

> That [birth-acquired] knowledge of another mind is threefold, as is what is
> produced through inference or vidyā. Hell-beings know at the beginning. For
> humans, it is not acquired through birth.

`Tat` resumes 7.55's birth-acquired line. Colophon: seventh kośasthāna.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.56.md`](./VAK_7.56.md).

**Witness.** Pradhan 430|11–431|03; colophon 431|04. Voices: Vasubandhu;
counterexample of natural former-life memory.

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> “Birth-acquired” continues. Birth-acquired other-mind knowledge is threefold:
> wholesome, unwholesome, indeterminate. Inferential other-mind knowledge of
> sign-interpreters, and that produced by a vidyā, are also threefold — not like
> the cultivation-result, which is wholesome only.
>
> By birth-acquired other-mind knowledge and former-abode recollection, hell-
> beings know at first, until struck by painful feeling. Those in other destinies
> know continually.
>
> For humans the previously named capacities beginning with ṛiddhi are not
> birth-acquired. What of those who naturally remember former births? That is
> produced by a special karma. Former-abode recollection is threefold:
> cultivation-result, birth-acquired, and karma-produced.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
birth-acquired paracitta: three ethical cells
inference and vidyā-technique: same three
bhāvanā-phala paracitta: kuśala only
hell: two capacities until pain
humans: not by birth; apparent memory = special karma
recollection inventory (provenance, not ethics)
colophon
```

## 5. Two Threefolds

Verse `tredhā` = ethical. Closing `trividhā` = three provenances of recollection.
This vidyā ≠ the three of 7.45. Cultivation-result here is other-mind knowledge,
not the construction-mind of 7.53. Natural occurrence ≠ birth-acquisition.
No guaranteed reactivation after pain.

## 6. Other-Mind Ethics

| route | ethical |
|---|---|
| birth-acquired | three |
| inference (naimittika) | three |
| vidyā-produced | three |
| cultivation-result | wholesome only |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- `tat` = birth-acquired other-mind knowledge;
- `tarka` and `vidyā` are two routes;
- `nṛṇām` withholds birth-acquisition of the ṛiddhi-group.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

The Idea of knowing another mind is not one Concept. Content does not name the
operation. Appearance would call every accurate guess abhijñā. Chapter 7 ends
in discrimination, not spectacle — which is why instruction (7.47) still ranks
first. Samāpatti next, on a further signal.
