# VAK_7.53 Bhāṣya — Construction-Mind Ethics and Five Sources of Ṛiddhi

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> avyākṛtaṃ bhāvanājaṃ trividhaṃ tūpapattijam |
> ṛiddhirmantrauṣadhābhyāṃ ca karmajā ceti pañcadhā || 7.53 ||

> The cultivation-produced construction-mind is ethically indeterminate; the
> birth-produced one, however, is threefold. Ṛiddhi also arises through mantra
> and medicinal means, and from karma: thus it is fivefold.

Divine ear and eye: 7.54.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.53.md`](./VAK_7.53.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Is every construction-mind indeterminate? What is a result of cultivation is
> necessarily indeterminate. A birth-acquired construction-mind is wholesome,
> unwholesome, or indeterminate — of devas, nāgas, piśācas, and the rest. A
> faculty itself is not constructed.
>
> Is ṛiddhi only these two — cultivation-made and birth-acquired? They describe
> fivefold ṛiddhi in summary: result of cultivation, acquired through birth,
> mantra-produced, medicine-produced, and karma-produced — as of Māndhātṛ and
> beings in intermediate existence.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
not every nirmāṇa-citta is avyākṛta
bhāvanā-phala: necessarily so
birth-acquired: three ethical cells
faculty not constructed
ṛiddhi not only those two → five sources
```

## 5. Capacity ≠ Result-Mind

7.45 called ṛiddhi-abhijñā wholesome. This verse calls the *cultivated
construction-mind* indeterminate. Different cognitions (7.49). Three ethical
classes ≠ three of the five sources. Mantra and medicine counted separately —
no procedure given. No ethical table for mantra / medicine / karma here.
Birth-list and karma-list are not a denial that birth has karmic conditions.

## 6. Two Answers

| nirmāṇa-citta | ethical |
|---|---|
| cultivation-result | necessarily avyākṛta |
| birth-acquired | kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta |

| ṛiddhi source |
|---|
| cultivation |
| birth |
| mantra |
| medicinal means |
| karma |

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- first half = construction-mind;
- `trividham` = three ethical possibilities;
- second half = five sources of ṛiddhi.
