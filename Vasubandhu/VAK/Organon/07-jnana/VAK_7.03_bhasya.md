# VAK_7.03 Bhāṣya — The Object-Ranges of the Three Knowledges

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sāṃvṛtaṃ sarvaviṣayaṃ kāmaduḥkhādigocaram |
> dharmākhyaṃ anvayajñānaṃ tūrdhvaduḥkhādigocaram || 7.3 ||

> Conventional knowledge has all objects as its range. That called
> dharma-knowledge has desire-domain suffering and so forth as its range.
> Anvaya-knowledge, however, has higher-domain suffering and so forth as
> its range.

`Kāmaduḥkhādigocaram` construes with `dharmākhyam`, not with `sāṃvṛtam`.
The Bhāṣya expands `sarva` through conditioned and unconditioned dharmas,
qualifies that range by `saṃbhavataḥ`, unfolds `ādi` as origination,
cessation, and counteragent, and reads `ūrdhva` as form and formless
domains.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.03.md`](./VAK_7.03.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> Conventional [knowledge] has all objects.
>
> For conventional knowledge, all dharmas — conditioned and unconditioned —
> are object-supports, according to possibility.
>
> Having desire-domain suffering and so forth as its range, it is called
> dharma-[knowledge].
>
> For dharma-knowledge, suffering belonging to the desire domain, and its
> origination, cessation, and counteragent, are the object-supports.
>
> Anvaya-knowledge, however, has higher-domain suffering and so forth as
> its range.
>
> For anvaya-knowledge, suffering belonging to the form and formless
> domains, and its origination, cessation, and counteragent, are the
> object-supports.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

7.02 named the nested three. 7.03 assigns ranges:

```text
conventional knowledge
    → all dharmas, conditioned and unconditioned, where possible
dharma-knowledge
    → desire-domain suffering + its origination, cessation, counteragent
anvaya-knowledge
    → form- and formless-domain suffering + the same three relatives
```

`Kāma` here is the existential desire-realm, not one of Chapter 1's eighteen
cognition-Domains. `Pratipakṣa` is the Path as counteragent — the fourth
truth under a practical name.

## 5. Conventional Range and Possibility

`Sarvaviṣayam` is a range-claim, not an event-claim. `Saṃbhavataḥ` blocks
reading it as one ordinary cognition knowing every dharma at once, and
blocks reading it as Buddha-omniscience smuggled into the definition of
worldly knowledge.

This is why 7.02's `prāyeṇa` (pots, cloth, persons) was not a restriction of
objects. Those were typical holdings. Even unconditioned dharmas can be
object-supports of conventional knowledge. Shared objects do not cancel the
7.02 contamination-cut: an unconditioned object does not make the knowing
uncontaminated.

`Viṣaya` / `gocara` / `ālambana` do the same local job — what the knowledge
takes up. They are not three ontological storeys in this unit.

## 6. One Truth-Structure, Two Domain-Sides

The Bhāṣya keeps `tat-` on origination, cessation, and counteragent. Domain
qualifies *suffering*; the other three are relatives of that suffering. So:
cessation known under dharma-knowledge is not thereby a desire-domain dharma.

```text
TruthField(domain) =
    suffering belonging to that domain
    + its origination
    + its cessation
    + its counteragent
```

Form and formless are gathered as `ūrdhva`. Three realms, two knowledge-sides.
The truths are not multiplied. The holding is specified by which suffering
it takes, and therefore which origination, cessation, and Path it takes with it.

That is the first earned sense of `anvaya`: the same order, carried to the
higher side. “Homologous” is now a fair explanatory word. It is not yet a
final translation, and it is not an inference-rule from desire-domain truths
to higher-domain truths. The verse assigns ranges; it does not teach a method
of transfer.

Knowing the counteragent is not yet exercising it. Object-range ≠ Path-work.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

Already in the Organon kārikā translation:

- `kāmaduḥkhādi` belongs to dharma-knowledge;
- `tu` contrasts the two uncontaminated ranges;
- `ūrdhva` is the higher side.

Attributed to the commentary:

- conditioned / unconditioned expansion of `sarva`;
- `saṃbhavataḥ`;
- the four-member unfolding of `ādi` with `pratipakṣa`;
- explicit `kāmāvacara` / `rūpārūpyāvacara`.

No change to the literal kārikā line is required.

## 8. Philosophical and Organon Study

This section is reconstruction.

Practical Reason now has *where* the holding takes its object. Conventional
knowledge: maximum possible extension of holding-to-be-true. Uncontaminated
knowledge: the same Four-Truth form of holding, cut by realm-side. That is
not Hegel. The Idea does not generate kāma / rūpa / ārūpya. The Kośa Path
does.

`Dharmajñāna` as knowledge of holding determined in the desire-realm remains
a thesis, not a gloss of `dharma` in the verse. What the verse actually does
is bind that name to a truth-field. `Anvaya` as the holding continued in the
same order fits the assigned range; 7.04+ must still show how the remaining
knowledges grow from this cut.

View/Belief is not re-opened here. These are ranges of `jñāna` already sorted
in 7.01–7.02. Breadth of object is not Belief; Path-range is not Opinion.

Yoga Vidyā needs the domain-specified truth-field; Yoga Technē needs the
counteragent as object before it is work. Neither equation (Vidyā =
uncontaminated, Technē = conventional) survives 7.03: conventional knowledge
can take unconditioned objects, and uncontaminated knowledge includes the
Path as something known.

No Concept-level closure. Reciprocity of form and realm-side is Essence
work — reflection determining the Particular of knowing. The Singular
(Buddha Mind as unrestricted actual range) is exactly what `saṃbhavataḥ`
refuses to attribute to conventional knowledge.
