# VAK_7.14 — Ethical Character and Levels of Knowledge

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
त्रिधाद्यं कुशलान्यन्यानि आद्यं सर्वासु भूमिषु ।
धर्माख्यं षट्सु नवसु त्वन्वयाख्यं तथैव षट् ॥ ७.१४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
tridhādyaṃ kuśalāny anyāni ādyaṃ sarvāsu bhūmiṣu |
dharmākhyaṃ ṣaṭsu navasu tv anvayākhyaṃ tathaiva ṣaṭ || 7.14 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. After the aspect
discussion, the Bhāṣya explicitly turns to wholesome and related
distinctions. The verse also distributes knowledge across levels;
its last six names inherit their level-ranges through dharma- or
anvaya-classification.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
tridhā | ādyam | kuśalāni | anyāni | ādyam | sarvāsu | bhūmiṣu |
dharma-ākhyam | ṣaṭsu | navasu | tu | anvaya-ākhyam |
tathā | eva | ṣaṭ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `tridhā` | indeclinable adverb | in three ways |
| `ādyam` | nominative singular neuter adjective, substantivally used | the first [knowledge], conventional knowledge |
| `kuśalāni` | nominative plural neuter adjective | wholesome |
| `anyāni` | nominative plural neuter pronoun | the others, the remaining nine knowledge-designations |
| `sarvāsu` | locative plural feminine adjective | in all |
| `bhūmiṣu` | locative plural feminine noun | levels |
| `dharmākhyam` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective | that called dharma-[knowledge] |
| `ṣaṭsu` | locative plural numeral | in six [levels] |
| `navasu` | locative plural numeral | in nine [levels] |

| `anvayākhyam` | nominative singular neuter compound adjective | that called anvaya-[knowledge] |

`Tridhā + ādyam` yields `tridhādyam`; `dharma + ākhyam` yields
`dharmākhyam`; `tathā + eva` yields `tathaiva`. The first `ṣaṭsu`
counts levels, whereas final `ṣaṭ` counts knowledge-designations.

## 4. Grammar

**The first knowledge.** The prose identifies `ādyam` as conventional
knowledge through its earlier position in the verse exposition:
`saṃvṛtijñānaṃ ślokādau … ādyam`. This reference should not be
converted into a claim that conventional knowledge stands first in
every prose enumeration: the recapitulation following VAK 7.07
began with dharma-knowledge.

`Ādyam tridhā` is a nominal classification, expanded as wholesome,
unwholesome, and indeterminate. `Anyāni kuśalāni` gives the other
nine designations their ethical predicate; the Bhāṣya strengthens
it with `eva`, exclusively wholesome.

**Distribution over levels.** Supply knowledge and occurrence in
the locative clauses:

```text
ādyam [jñānam] — sarvāsu bhūmiṣu
dharmākhyam [jñānam] — ṣaṭsu [bhūmiṣu]
anvayākhyam [jñānam] tu — navasu [bhūmiṣu]
```

The verse states the levels to which these knowledges belong. It
does not specify a distinct event of acquisition every time the
locative occurs. Level-membership is also different from the
embodied support discussed in VAK 7.15.

**“Likewise the six.”** The six are suffering-, origination-,
cessation-, Path-, exhaustion-, and non-arising-knowledge. The
Bhāṣya distinguishes two scopes:

```text
abhedena — without differentiating dharma/anvaya inclusion:
    the same nine levels

bhedena — with that differentiation:
    dharmajñānasaṃgṛhītāni — six levels
    anvayajñānasaṃgṛhītāni — nine levels
```

The difference is controlled by classification, not by competing
counts of the same fully specified case.

**Close construe.** The first [is] threefold; the others [are]
wholesome. The first [is] in all levels. That called dharma [is]
in six; that called anvaya, however, in nine; likewise the six.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The first is threefold; the others are wholesome. The first is
> in all levels. That called dharma-knowledge is in six, whereas
> that called anvaya-knowledge is in nine; likewise the six.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Conventional knowledge is threefold: wholesome, unwholesome,
> or indeterminate. The other nine knowledges are wholesome only.
> Conventional knowledge occurs throughout the levels, from the
> desire domain to the summit of existence. Dharma-knowledge belongs
> to six levels: the four dhyānas, preliminary concentration, and
> intermediate dhyāna. Anvaya-knowledge belongs to those six and
> the first three formless levels. The four truth-knowledges and
> exhaustion- and non-arising-knowledge range across those nine
> without further differentiation; when distinguished by inclusion,
> their dharma-knowledge forms belong to six and their anvaya-
> knowledge forms to nine.

This follows Pradhan 402|06–402|18. The level of other-mind knowledge,
the remaining item in that distribution, is stated in VAK 7.15.
Its wholesome status is already included among the nine here.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The classification of knowing must preserve both ethical character
> and the level at which it operates. Conventional knowledge admits
> all three ethical characters and the full range of levels. The
> other knowledge-designations are wholesome, while their level-
> ranges require further specification. For the truth and terminal
> knowledges, inclusion under dharma or anvaya determines whether
> the relevant range is six or nine.

Wholesomeness and uncontaminated status are different determinations.
The nine include other-mind knowledge, whose contaminated form was
explicitly discussed in VAK 7.11. Thus “the nine are wholesome”
must not become “the nine are all uncontaminated.” The wholesome
portion of conventional knowledge can overlap another wholesome
knowledge-category without making the whole conventional category
exclusively wholesome.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kuśala`, `akuśala`, `avyākṛta` — wholesome, unwholesome,
indeterminate.** The threefold ethical classification of conventional
knowledge. `Avyākṛta` is the technical third category, not merely
an unresolved question about which ethical value applies.

**`Bhūmi` — level.** Here the level of the knowledge's occurrence
or affiliation. It must remain distinct from its object-domain,
counteractive range, and the practitioner's embodied support.

**`Anāgamya` — preliminary concentration.** One of the six bases
listed alongside the four dhyānas and intermediate dhyāna. The
local prose lists it without developing its full definition.

**`Dhyānāntara` — intermediate dhyāna.** The sixth item when the
four dhyānas and preliminary concentration are counted. It is
not a fifth principal dhyāna.

**`Ārūpyatraya` — the first three formless levels.** The additions
that extend the six-level range to nine for anvaya-knowledge.
The highest formless level is not included in this addition.

**`Bhavāgra` — summit of existence.** The upper endpoint explicitly
included in conventional knowledge's level-range. It is not an
additional tenth level for anvaya-knowledge. This also differs
from VAK 7.05, where Bhavāgra aggregates were specified as objects
of the terminal knowledges' first arising.

**`Saṃgṛhīta` — included / subsumed.** The relation controlling the
six knowledges' differentiated ranges. A suffering-knowledge
instance, for example, may be classified under dharma-knowledge
or anvaya-knowledge; that qualification matters to its level-range.

**`Abhedena` / `bhedena` — without / with differentiation.** These
adverbs specify how the six are being considered. The undifferentiated
nine-level range does not license a dharma-subsumed instance in
one of the additional formless levels.

## 8. Logical Determination

The two primary dimensions are separate:

| Knowledge category | Ethical character | Level-range in this verse |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional | Wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate | All, through Bhavāgra |
| Dharma | Wholesome | Six |
| Anvaya | Wholesome | Nine |
| Four truth and two terminal knowledges | Wholesome | Nine collectively; six when dharma-subsumed, nine when anvaya-subsumed |
| Other-mind | Wholesome | Deferred to VAK 7.15 |

```text
SixLevels =
    FourDhyanas + Anagamya + Dhyanantara

NineLevels =
    SixLevels + FirstThreeFormlessLevels
```

For each of the six truth/terminal knowledge-designations:

```text
IncludedInDharmaKnowledge(x)
    → Level(x) belongs to SixLevels

IncludedInAnvayaKnowledge(x)
    → Level(x) belongs to NineLevels
```

The class-level distribution does not imply that one act occurs
in every listed level, or that every ethical type of conventional
knowledge is available at every level. The verse's two ranges
must not be multiplied into an unrestricted combination table.

```text
PossibleEthicalCharacters(ConventionalKnowledge)
    = {Wholesome, Unwholesome, Indeterminate}

PossibleLevels(ConventionalKnowledge)
    = all levels in the stated range

These statements alone do not license every quality-level pair.
```

The whole taxonomy also remains overlapping. The ethical assertion
about another knowledge-category does not require it to be disjoint
from the wholesome portion of conventional knowledge.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**A new axis with earlier distinctions intact.** The discussion of
aspects has been completed sufficiently to turn to ethical character
and levels. The source does not replace aspect, object, or counteraction
with these new predicates. Each answers a different question about
the cognition being classified.

**The six/nine distinction is qualified.** The Bhāṣya explicitly
distinguishes an aggregate statement from the more determinate
dharma/anvaya inclusion. The official study therefore retains both
`abhedena` and `bhedena`, rather than reporting two apparently
incompatible ranges for an undifferentiated name.

**Precision concerning “first” and “pure.”** The local research
discussion sometimes describes conventional knowledge as first in
the tenfold enumeration and the other nine as pure. The source
controls these expressions more narrowly: “first” refers to its
earlier verse placement; the nine are explicitly wholesome.
Neither phrasing should override the chapter's actual list order
or its contaminated other-mind knowledge.

**Textual caution.** The raw prose includes `bhavatvāt`,
`kāmadhātī`, `ārupyatraye`, and `punr`. These are not silently
printed as a critically restored Sanskrit passage. The named
levels and the differentiated inclusion are nevertheless explicit
in the surrounding text. The study's use of occurrence or
level-membership avoids imposing an acquisition-only meaning
on every locative.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our Yoga
system must articulate not only what a capacity knows or counters,
but its ethical character and the conditions of its operation.
Yoga Vidyā supplies these distinctions in their systematic relations;
Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma companion, develops an undertaking
whose appropriate level and wholesome character must themselves
be determined.

The source's overlap is especially useful here. Conventional
knowledge belongs within the field of practice and can be wholesome;
its broad classification alone does not settle the character of
a particular act. Conversely, a wholesome capacity's general name
does not specify every level at which it can operate.

For our reconstruction, the distinction between an aggregate range
and a qualified occurrence prevents practical overextension. Vidyā
articulates the range; Technē must meet the conditions belonging
to the particular operation. Their companionship therefore requires
ethical character, level, and classificatory inclusion to remain
connected without being collapsed into one predicate.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. The ethical
division and six/nine allocation are explicit. Minor source forms
remain recorded; the remaining other-mind level and support
distinctions proceed in the next verse.
