# VAK_7.17 — The Directed Field of Knowledge-Objects

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
नव मार्गान्वयधियोः दुःखहेतुधियोर्द्वयम् ।
चतुर्णां दश नैकस्य योज्या धर्माः पुनर्दश ॥ ७.१७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
nava mārgānvayadhiyoḥ duḥkhahetudhiyor dvayam |
caturṇāṃ daśa naikasya yojyā dharmāḥ punar daśa || 7.17 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The governing question
continues from VAK 7.16: how many knowledge-categories serve as
objects of each knowledge? The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies all
the numerical groups. The final clause opens a different tenfold
classification, developed in VAK 7.18.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
nava | mārga-anvaya-dhiyoḥ | duḥkha-hetu-dhiyoḥ | dvayam |
caturṇām | daśa | na | ekasya | yojyāḥ | dharmāḥ | punaḥ | daśa
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `nava` | nominative plural numeral here | nine [knowledges as objects] |
| `mārgānvayadhiyoḥ` | genitive dual compound ending in feminine `dhī` | of Path-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge |
| `duḥkhahetudhiyoḥ` | genitive dual compound | of suffering- and origination-knowledge |
| `dvayam` | nominative singular neuter collective numeral | a pair [of knowledge-objects] |
| `caturṇām` | genitive plural numeral | of four [knowing categories] |
| `daśa` | nominative plural numeral | ten [knowledge-objects] |
| `na` | negation | none, with the omitted object statement |
| `ekasya` | genitive singular numeral | of one [knowledge] |
| `yojyāḥ` | nominative plural masculine gerundive of `√yuj` | to be assigned or brought into relation |
| `dharmāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | dharmas, here grouped into classes |
| `punaḥ` | connective adverb | again, in turn |
| `daśa` | nominative plural numeral | ten [dharma classes] |

`Hetu` is resolved by the Bhāṣya as `samudaya`, origination.
`Na + ekasya` yields `naikasya`; `yojyāḥ + dharmāḥ` appears
as `yojyā dharmāḥ`. The two occurrences of ten have different
referents: knowledge-categories and the forthcoming dharma classes.

## 4. Grammar

**Nine for each of two.** The genitive dual `mārgānvayadhiyoḥ`
specifies the knowing standpoints. The numeral is distributive:
each has nine knowledge-categories as possible objects. Their
excluded categories differ, as the prose explains.

**Two for each of two.** `Duḥkhahetudhiyoḥ dvayam` similarly assigns
the same pair of object-categories to suffering- and origination-
knowledge. It does not count the knowing pair itself as the objects.

**Four knowing categories and ten objects.** `Caturṇām` refers to
conventional, other-mind, exhaustion, and non-arising knowledge.
The Bhāṣya identifies them explicitly. `Daśa` gives each category's
possible knowledge-object range, with `jñānāni ālambanam` supplied.

**One knowing category and no knowledge-object.** `Na ekasya`
means that no knowledge serves as object for the single category
cessation-knowledge. It does not say that cessation-knowledge has
no object whatsoever.

**A new assignment.** `Yojyāḥ` agrees with `dharmāḥ`, and the
final `daśa` counts ten classes to be related to the knowledges.
The next prose asks `katame daśa`, “which ten?” and proceeds
into VAK 7.18. The present closing clause introduces that task
without itself enumerating the classes.

**Close construe.** For Path- and anvaya-knowledge, nine [knowledges
are objects]; for suffering- and origination-knowledge, a pair;
for four, ten; for one, none. In turn, ten [classes of] dharmas
are to be assigned.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Nine are objects of Path- and anvaya-knowledge; two of suffering-
> and origination-knowledge; ten of four; none of one. In turn,
> ten classes of dharmas are to be assigned.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Path-knowledge has nine knowledge-categories as objects, excluding
> conventional knowledge. Anvaya-knowledge also has nine, excluding
> dharma-knowledge. Suffering- and origination-knowledge each have
> two: conventional and other-mind knowledge. Conventional,
> other-mind, exhaustion, and non-arising knowledge each have all
> ten knowledge-categories in their possible object-range.
> Cessation-knowledge has none of the ten as its object. The
> inquiry next assigns ten classes of dharmas to the knowledges.

This follows Pradhan 404|01–404|10. Together with the dharma-
knowledge entry at 403|17–403|18, it completes the knowledge-object
count. The ten dharma classes belong to the next verse's exposition.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Knowing can itself become an object, but the relation is
> differentiated. Some knowledge-kinds admit nine categories,
> some two, some all ten, and cessation-knowledge admits none
> of these cognitive objects. The distinctions concern what
> can be known from a specified knowing standpoint; they do
> not measure a knowledge's liberating value by the size of
> its object-field.

Cessation-knowledge's zero is specific to knowledge-objects. It
knows cessation. Conversely, ten means coverage across the named
knowledge-categories, not unrestricted access to every individual
cognition or simultaneous apprehension of the whole field.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Knowledge as `ālambana`.** The commentary continues to use
object-support for the relation. Calling it a reflective relation
in our study does not make it an automatic act of self-reflection:
one cognition can have another cognition as object.

**`Mārga-dhī` / `anvaya-dhī` — Path- / anvaya-knowledge.** Both
have nine knowledge-object categories, but equal counts do not
mean equal ranges. Path-knowledge excludes conventional knowledge;
anvaya-knowledge excludes dharma-knowledge.

**`Dvaya` — the pair.** Conventional and other-mind knowledge,
the two object-categories of suffering- and origination-knowledge.
The latter category includes contaminated and uncontaminated forms;
the count does not erase the particular object's status. The
contaminated portion is relevant to this suffering/origination
object-field, rather than every instance indiscriminately.

**`Caturṇām` — of the four.** Conventional, other-mind, exhaustion,
and non-arising knowledge. These four share the ten-category
count, but their detailed conditions of knowing remain different.
In particular, other-mind knowledge retains its present-object,
level, faculty, practitioner, and single-entity restrictions.

**`Ekasya` — of the one.** Cessation-knowledge. Its object is
cessation rather than one of the ten cognitions. The exclusion
does not make it objectless or less determinate.

**`Yojya` — to be assigned / brought into relation.** The closing
instruction requires the next tenfold dharma classification to
be related to the knowledges. It does not add ten new knowledge-
designations to the existing ten.

## 8. Logical Determination

The complete category-level table, including VAK 7.16's entry, is:

| Knowing category | Knowledge-objects | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Dharma | All categories except anvaya | 9 |
| Anvaya | All categories except dharma | 9 |
| Path | All categories except conventional | 9 |
| Suffering | Conventional and other-mind | 2 |
| Origination | Conventional and other-mind | 2 |
| Conventional | All ten categories | 10 |
| Other-mind | All ten categories | 10 |
| Exhaustion | All ten categories | 10 |
| Non-arising | All ten categories | 10 |
| Cessation | No knowledge-category | 0 |

The relation is directed. For example, conventional knowledge
can take cessation-knowledge as object, whereas cessation-
knowledge does not take conventional knowledge as object.

```text
CanHaveKnowledgeCategoryAsObject(A, B)
    does not entail
CanHaveKnowledgeCategoryAsObject(B, A)
```

The taxonomy remains overlapping. A positive category entry
means that the category is represented within the permitted
object-range, subject to the more specific distinctions; it
does not admit every instance bearing that name.

```text
ObjectCategoryCount = 10
    does not imply
EveryInstanceAccessible
    or
AllTenSimultaneouslyApprehended
```

Thus other-mind knowledge can have all ten as object-categories
without violating VAK 7.06's separation of dharma-side and
anvaya-side acts. The broad category includes appropriately
different acts and preparations. It does not make a single
dharma-side act know an anvaya-side target.

Self-inclusion at the category level likewise does not establish
same-moment self-cognition. The table classifies possible
relations among knowledges, not an unrestricted recursion rule.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The Bhāṣya resolves the numerical ellipses.** The research
kārikā file left the four and the one open. The immediate prose
names them, identifies the two-object pair, and distinguishes
the exclusions for the two nine-object entries. The official
study presents the complete mapping here.

**Range is not rank.** Ten, nine, two, and zero count members
of this particular object-category field. They do not rank
knowledge by ethical character, Path-completion, or liberating
efficacy. Those require the distinct determinations established
earlier in the chapter.

**The closing change of unit.** “Ten dharmas” introduces a
tenfold classification of the wider object-field, rather than
repeating the ten cognitions under a new label. Keeping this
transition clear is necessary for the next verse's distribution.

**Textual caution.** The raw prose has `paracitajñāne` in the
two-object clause; the surrounding use of `paracitta` and the
local Bhāṣya analysis identify other-mind knowledge securely.
The study retains that readable designation while leaving the
source files untouched.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** Our
Yoga system can now specify reflective examination as a
determinate practice. Yoga Vidyā articulates which knowing
standpoint can take which cognition as object. Yoga Technē,
its kriyā–karma companion, develops the undertaking through
which an appropriate instance of that examination is actualized.

The distinction between category-range and actual access matters
practically. Possessing a broad kind of knowledge does not
guarantee every examination; the particular operation must
retain its preparation, support, object, and temporal conditions.
Likewise, a restricted reflective range does not negate the
specific work that a knowledge accomplishes.

Our reconstruction therefore preserves the direction of the
relation and its conditions. Vidyā supplies the intelligible
map; Technē realizes a specified operation within it. The
Kośa's table provides the concrete source distinctions, while
their development into this Yoga-system account remains our
Organon interpretation.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study. All
knowledge-object counts are now specified. Their category-level
scope and earlier restrictions remain explicit; the ten dharma
classes are reserved for VAK 7.18.
