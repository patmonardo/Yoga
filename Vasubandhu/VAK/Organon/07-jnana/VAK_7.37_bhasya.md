# VAK_7.37 Bhāṣya — Resolve-Knowledge and the Four Discriminating Knowledges

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tathaiva praṇidhijñānaṃ sarvālambaṃ tu tat tathā |
> dharmārthayor niruktau ca pratibhāne ca saṃvidaḥ || 7.37 ||

> Resolve-knowledge is likewise, but it has all as its object-range. Likewise
> are the knowledges concerning dharma and meaning, linguistic expression,
> and ready articulation.

Two “likewise” — different inheritances. Definitions of the four: 7.38.

Kārikā study: [`VAK_7.37.md`](./VAK_7.37.md).

## 3. Continuous Conventional Translation

> This too is conventional knowledge, in the final dhyāna, of an immovable
> one, with human support. The difference: resolve-knowledge has all dharmas
> as object-range. Formless attainments, however, are not known directly by
> it — rather through a distinction of accomplished conduct. And there is the
> farmer illustration. So the Vaibhāṣikas.
>
> Knowledge preceded by resolve is resolve-knowledge. Having resolved, one
> enters the fourth dhyāna developed to its culminating limit: “may I know
> this” — and knows that as it is.
>
> The four discriminating knowledges likewise as araṇā — in what? Immovable
> nature and human support. Difference of object, level, and nature is stated
> separately.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary


## 5. Range Is Not Simultaneous Omniscience

“All” = eligible field. “This” = directed exercise. Not every object present
at once. Not uniform access. Wish alone ≠ the attainment. Farmer story not
invented here.

Do not copy fourth-dhyāna or conventional-knowledge onto all four pratisaṃvids
yet.

## 7. The Bhāṣya's Decisions for the Kārikā

- first `tathaiva` = the four inherited conditions;
- `tu tat` = object-range difference;
- second `tathā` = only bearer and human support;
- four names introduced, not defined.
