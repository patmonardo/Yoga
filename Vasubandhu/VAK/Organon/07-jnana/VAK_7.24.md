# VAK_7.24 — Faculty-Strengthening, Path Function, and Alternative Counts

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
शैक्षोत्तापनमुक्तौ वा षट् सप्तज्ञानभावना ।
आनन्तर्यपथे षण्णां भवाग्रविजये तथा ॥ ७.२४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
śaikṣottāpanamuktau vā ṣaṭ saptajñānabhāvanā |
ānantaryapathe ṣaṇṇāṃ bhavāgravijaye tathā || 7.24 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The running
Bhāṣya joins `ṣaṭsaptajñānabhāvanā` and prints the interrupted
lemma `ānantaryapatha ṣaṇṇāṃ`; the full verse preserves the
locative `ānantaryapathe`. Its counts require the commentary's
distinctions of detachment, path function, and stated opinion.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
śaikṣa-uttāpana-muktau | vā | ṣaṭ | sapta-jñāna-bhāvanā |
ānantarya-pathe | ṣaṇṇām | bhavāgra-vijaye | tathā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `śaikṣa` | compound member | trainee, one still under training |
| `uttāpana` | action noun in compound | strengthening / sharpening [of faculties] |
| `muktau` | locative singular feminine | in the liberation [path] |
| `vā` | alternative particle | or; marking an alternative opinion here |
| `ṣaṭ` | numeral | six |
| `sapta` | numeral within the cultivation expression | seven |
| `jñānabhāvanā` | nominative singular feminine compound | cultivation of knowledges |
| `ānantaryapathe` | locative singular masculine | in the uninterrupted path |
| `ṣaṇṇām` | genitive plural numeral | of six [knowledges] |
| `bhavāgravijaye` | locative singular masculine compound | in conquest of the summit of existence |
| `tathā` | adverb | likewise |

`Śaikṣa + uttāpana` yields `śaikṣottāpana`. The Bhāṣya
supplies the faculties explicitly through `indriyottāpanā`.
The compound does not name a literal heating procedure.

## 4. Grammar

**The compound's scope.** The prose expands
`śaikṣottāpanamuktau` as `śaikṣasya indriyottāpanāyāṃ
vimuktimārge`: in the liberation path within the strengthening
of a trainee's faculties. Faculty-strengthening is the
undertaking; liberation is a path function within it.

**Six and seven are conditional.** The commentary supplies
`sarāgasya` and `vītarāgasya`: six for the trainee not detached
from desire, seven for the detached trainee. It specifies
`paracittajñānaṃ prakṣipya`, adding knowledge of another mind,
for the latter count.

**The force of `vā`.** The prose explicitly says that this
particle makes a difference of opinion known. Some teachers
also include conventional knowledge for both practitioners
in the liberation path. Translating “six or seven” alone
does not convey this additional doctrinal alternative.

**The second half.** Supply `jñānānāṃ bhāvanā` with `ṣaṇṇām`.
The first locative concerns faculty-strengthening's uninterrupted
path. With `bhavāgravijaye tathā`, retain the uninterrupted-path
restriction: the prose says `bhavāgravairāgye 'py ānantaryamārgeṣu`.
The sixfold rule does not cover every phase of Bhavāgra detachment.

**Close construe.** In the liberation path of strengthening a
trainee's faculties, there is cultivation of six or seven
knowledges [with an alternative opinion indicated by `vā`].
In the uninterrupted path, cultivation is of six; likewise
in the uninterrupted paths of conquest of Bhavāgra.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Or, in the liberation path of strengthening a trainee's
> faculties, there is cultivation of six or seven knowledges.
> In the uninterrupted path, cultivation is of six; likewise
> in the conquest of the summit of existence.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the liberation path of strengthening a trainee's faculties,
> six knowledge categories are cultivated if the trainee is
> not detached from desire: dharma, anvaya, suffering,
> origination, cessation, and Path. For the detached trainee,
> knowledge of another mind is added, making seven. Some
> teachers also include conventional knowledge for both;
> the verse's “or” indicates this difference of opinion.
>
> In the preparatory path, conventional knowledge is also
> cultivated for both. In faculty-strengthening's uninterrupted
> path, however, the same six are cultivated whether the
> trainee is detached or undetached. Conventional knowledge
> is excluded because of resemblance to the Path of Seeing.
> Knowledge of another mind is excluded from all uninterrupted
> paths because it is not a counteragent. Likewise, six are
> cultivated in the uninterrupted paths of detachment from
> Bhavāgra.

Source: Pradhan 408|20–409|07. The sentence excluding
conventional knowledge at 409|03 is present in the running
source but absent from the research Bhāṣya excerpt. It is
restored to the study's argument here.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Strengthening the faculties has an internally differentiated
> course. Preparation, uninterrupted work, and liberation
> do not cultivate an identical field. Prior detachment
> enlarges the liberation-path range in the principal
> account; it does not enlarge the sixfold range of the
> uninterrupted path. The grounds for exclusion also differ,
> and the recorded alternative must remain attributed.

The count is the result of several determinations, not a
free-standing measure of spiritual strength. The prose
distinguishes two reasons for the uninterrupted exclusions;
neither should be replaced by a generic demand that all
cultivated knowledge perform one identical function.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Śaikṣa` — trainee.** The practitioner under training whose
faculties are being strengthened. This study does not extend
the trainee's counts to the corresponding undertakings of
one beyond training.

**`Indriyottāpanā` — strengthening / sharpening the faculties.**
The explicit expansion of the verse's compressed `uttāpana`.
The present passage determines its cultivation ranges by
path function; it does not provide a sequence of exercises
or enumerate which faculties are changed in each exercise.

**`Sarāga` / `vītarāga` — undetached / detached from desire.**
The status distinction carried forward from VAK 7.22. It
concerns attained detachment, not the occurrence of an
afflicted desire during the noble path-moment.

**`Matavikalpa` — difference of opinion.** The prose's explicit
explanation of `vā`. The alternative is attributed to `kecit`,
“some”; no school identity is supplied here.

**`Prakṣipya` — adding / including.** An absolutive specifying
the addition of knowledge of another mind to the six-category
set for the detached trainee's liberation path.

**`Darśanamārgasādṛśya` — resemblance to the Path of Seeing.**
The stated reason for excluding conventional knowledge from
the faculty-strengthening uninterrupted path. Resemblance
does not identify this path as a second Path of Seeing or
cancel VAK 7.20–7.21's special conventional cultivation.

**`Apratipakṣatva` — not being a counteragent.** The reason
given when the prose asks why knowledge of another mind
is excluded from all uninterrupted paths. This negative
predicate must be kept distinct from the resemblance
argument governing conventional knowledge here.

**`Bhavāgravijaya` — conquest of Bhavāgra.** Expanded as
detachment from the summit of existence. The repeated
sixfold count is restricted to its uninterrupted paths.

## 8. Logical Determination

Use the established six-category set:

```text
B = {DharmaKnowledge, AnvayaKnowledge, SufferingKnowledge,
     OriginationKnowledge, CessationKnowledge, PathKnowledge}
P = OtherMindKnowledge
C = ConventionalKnowledge
```

| Faculty-strengthening phase | Undetached trainee | Detached trainee | Attribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liberation | B: six | B + P: seven | Principal account |
| Liberation | B + C: seven | B + P + C: eight | Some teachers |
| Preparation | Conventional knowledge also included | Conventional knowledge also included | Unqualified prose statement |
| Uninterrupted | B: six | B: six | Explicit rule for both |

The seven/eight totals for the alternative are arithmetic
consequences of its explicit addition, not separate numerals
quoted from the prose. For preparation, the table preserves
the explicit inclusion statement without converting it into
an independently enumerated full set.

The uninterrupted exclusions require separate grounds:

| Excluded knowledge | Stated ground | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional | Resemblance to Seeing | Faculty-strengthening uninterrupted path |
| Knowledge of another mind | Not being a counteragent | All uninterrupted paths in the stated rule |

For Bhavāgra detachment:

```text
PathFunction = Uninterrupted
    → cultivatedSet = B
```

VAK 7.23's eight liberation paths instead cultivate `B + P`.
The two rules are consistent because their path functions
differ. The remaining liberation-path case belongs to the
continuation rather than to an expansion of this sixfold rule.

The exclusions are not a sufficient-condition theorem.
“Not counteractive, therefore excluded” does not entail
“anything counteractive is cultivated here,” nor does
possession alone establish cultivation at a given moment.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The omitted sentence changes the explanation.** The research
excerpt moves directly from the sixfold uninterrupted count
to the exclusion of knowledge of another mind. The running
source first states `na saṃvṛtijñānasya / darśanamārga
sādṛśyāt /`. Restoring this sentence prevents an account
in which a single counteraction criterion is made to explain
both exclusions without textual support.

**The alternative belongs in the translation.** The six/seven
distinction already follows from detachment. The prose assigns
`vā` the further task of registering disagreement about
conventional knowledge. Its alternative seven/eight counts
must remain distinct from the principal six/seven account.

**Source-form caution.** The running transcription prints
`pratipiddhatvāt` and `pratipidhyate` in the exclusion exchange.
The preceding `na paracittajñānasya`, the reference to all
uninterrupted paths, and the answer `apratipakṣatvāt` secure
the exclusion and its reason. The study translates that
argument without silently repairing the witness.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.** This
verse lets our Yoga system distinguish developing a faculty
from merely increasing the number of cultivated categories.
The strengthening undertaking has preparation, uninterrupted
work, and liberation as internal functions. Its narrower
uninterrupted range is not thereby a failure of development.

Yoga Vidyā articulates the relevant knowledge categories and
the reasons governing their inclusion. Yoga Technē, its
kriyā–karma companion, articulates the undertaking through
its function and the practitioner's attained condition.
Their relation explains why the detached trainee can cultivate
knowledge of another mind in one phase while it remains
excluded in another.

The reconstruction must also retain the source of each rule.
A count derived under the principal account and a count
derived under the opinion of “some” are different attributed
determinations. Preserving that distinction makes the model
reviewable and prevents doctrinal alternatives from becoming
an unexplained contradiction in a single cultivation table.

This is project reconstruction. The source supplies the
conditions, counts, exclusions, and disagreement; it does
not name Yoga Vidyā or Yoga Technē as its own categories.

**Review status.** Provisional, Bhāṣya-controlled study.
The complete source span, including the omitted conventional-
