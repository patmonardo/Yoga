# VAK_7.52 — Disputed Persistence after Death and Mastery of Construction

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
मृतस्याप्यस्त्यधिष्ठानं नास्थिरस्य अपरे तु न ।
आदावेकमनेकेन जितायां तु विपर्ययात् ॥ ७.५२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
mṛtasyāpyastyadhiṣṭhānaṃ nāsthirasya apare tu na |
ādāvekamanekena jitāyāṃ tu viparyayāt || 7.52 ||
```

The Sanskrit follows the local kārikā witness. The
Devanāgarī renders that reading. The first half records
a position, its restriction, and an explicit dissent;
the second half changes to the number of constructions
produced through construction-minds.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
mṛtasya | api | asti | adhiṣṭhānam | na | asthirasya |
apare | tu | na | ādau | ekam | anekena |
jitāyām | tu | viparyayāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `mṛtasya` | genitive singular masculine participle | of one who has died |
| `api` | particle | even; also |
| `asti` | third-person singular present of √as | exists or remains |
| `adhiṣṭhānam` | nominative singular neuter | sustaining determination |
| `asthirasya` | genitive singular adjective, neuter in the prose expansion | of or in respect of an unstable thing |
| `apare` | nominative plural masculine | others [say] |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | however |
| `na` | negation | not; the second occurrence denies the post-mortem claim |
| `ādau` | locative singular of `ādi` | at the beginning |
| `ekam` | accusative singular neuter here | one [constructed manifestation] |
| `anekena` | instrumental singular neuter adjective | by many [construction-minds, collectively expressed] |
| `jitāyām` | locative singular feminine participle of √ji | when mastered, qualifying the understood higher knowledge |
| `viparyayāt` | ablative singular masculine | by reversal; with the relation reversed |

`Mṛtasya + api + asti + adhiṣṭhānam` yields
`mṛtasyāpyastyadhiṣṭhānam`; `na + asthirasya` yields
`nāsthirasya`. `Ādau + ekam` yields `ādāv ekam`.
The prose repeats the singular instrumental construction
`anekena nirmāṇacittena` while giving the sense of
multiple construction-minds.

## 4. Grammar

**Whose sustaining determination?** `Mṛtasya` identifies
the person whose determination is at issue. The question
is whether the effect continues after death, not whether
a dead person is now performing a new cognitive act.

**What is unstable?** The prose supplies `asthirasya
bhāvasya`, “of an unstable thing.” The restriction concerns
the sustained object, not an unstable practitioner's
temperament or an insufficiently steady concentration.

**The dissent has wider scope.** `Apare tu na` supplies
an understood verb of saying. The prose expressly expands
it as denial of the dead person's adhiṣṭhāna. The dissenters
do not merely repeat the restriction on unstable objects;
they deny the post-mortem explanation itself.

**One produced through many.** `Ādau ekam anekena`
supplies a verb of constructing. The prose identifies
one constructed manifestation and many construction-minds.
The topic is production, not how many minds must continually
sustain one already produced object.

**When the capacity is mastered.** `Jitāyām` supplies
`abhijñāyām`, forming the locative condition “when the
higher knowledge has been mastered.” `Viparyayāt` reverses
the numerical relation: one mind produces many manifestations.
It does not reverse the causal direction from product
to producer.

**Close construe.** Sustaining determination remains even
of one who has died, but not with respect to an unstable
thing; others, however, deny this. At first one manifestation
is constructed through many minds; when the higher knowledge
is mastered, the relation is reversed.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Sustaining determination remains even after death,
> but not for an unstable thing; others, however, deny
> this. At first, one is constructed through many; when
> mastery is attained, the relation is reversed.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> According to the first account, a person's sustaining
> determination may continue after death, as illustrated
> by the continued skeletal framework of Ārya Mahākāśyapa.
> This does not extend to unstable things. Other teachers
> deny post-mortem sustaining determination and attribute
> the skeleton's persistence to the efficacy of deities.
>
> At the beginning, many construction-minds produce one
> manifestation. When the higher knowledge has been
> mastered, one mind produces many manifestations—as
> many as the practitioner wishes to construct.

Source: Pradhan 428|01–428|12, local Bhāṣya checked
against the running source. The flesh example in the
first account contains a damaged expression and is
examined below rather than silently restored in translation.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Continued presence and its causal explanation are
> distinct questions. The exposition records agreement
> on the example under discussion but disagreement about
> what accounts for its persistence. Mastery introduces
> another distinction: the relation between the number
> of cognitive acts and the number of products changes.

The first question concerns sustaining something after
its producer has died. The second concerns producing
manifestations with an acquired capacity. The numerical
reversal is not presented as proof that post-mortem
sustaining determination is possible.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Adhiṣṭhāna`.** Sustaining determination, continuing
the function identified in VAK 7.51. The present extension
beyond death is disputed. It should not be narrated as
an uncontested consequence of the preceding verse.

**`Sthira / asthira`.** Stable and unstable, relative
to the sustained thing in this discussion. The contrast
is not between metaphysically eternal and impermanent
entities. It supplies no universal test of material
durability or duration.

**`Asthisaṃkalā / asthiśaṅkalā`.** The transmitted forms
for the skeletal framework or connected bones. The source
cites Mahākāśyapa's remains; it does not identify those
remains as a constructed apparition. The example broadens
the discussion of sustaining determination beyond the
immediately preceding construction example.

**`Devatānubhāva`.** Efficacy or power of deities. This
is the alternative causal explanation offered by the
teachers designated `apare`. It is a source-reported
claim, not an independently verified explanation here.

**`Jitā abhijñā`.** A mastered higher knowledge. This
is the prose expansion of the verse's feminine locative.
The passage does not identify this mastery by itself
with exhaustion of the influxes or final liberation.

**`Viparyaya`.** Reversal. Here the many-minds/one-product
relation becomes one-mind/many-products. Neither sustaining
conditions nor the ground rules established earlier are
thereby reversed or removed.

## 8. Logical Determination

**The two explanations must remain distinct.**

| Account | Post-mortem sustaining determination | Explanation of the skeletal remains |
|---|---|---|
| First exposition | Admitted, with the stable-object restriction | Mahākāśyapa's sustaining determination |
| Other teachers | Denied | Efficacy of deities |

The local analysis calls the first account Vaibhāṣika,
consistent with the framing of the preceding ṛddhi
exposition. This particular source unit does not repeat
that school name. It explicitly labels the dissenters
only as “others”; their school is not supplied here.

The case is an example cited within the text, not a
present-day observation verified by this study. Even
within the argument, continued remains do not uniquely
establish the first explanation, since the alternative
accepts the persistence while assigning another cause.

**The stable-object restriction.** The first exposition
excludes unstable things and introduces flesh and similar
material in its illustration. This restricts what its
claim allows; it is not sufficient evidence for a rule
that every stable thing will persist after death if
someone wishes it to remain.

**Production before and after mastery.**

| Condition | Construction-minds | Constructed manifestations |
|---|---|---|
| At the beginning | Many | One |
| Higher knowledge mastered | One | Many, as desired |

The text's verbs are forms of `nirmimīte / nirmiṇoti`
in the transmitted construction discussion: producing
the manifestation is the issue. The research kārikā's
phrase “many operations are required to sustain one
result” changes that issue and should not govern the
study translation.

```text
Persistence question:
    Does the earlier sustaining determination continue after death?
    Two competing explanations are recorded.

Production question:
    How many construction-minds produce how many manifestations?
    Initial: many → one
    Mastered: one → many
```

“As many as desired” describes the mastered production
in this passage. It does not explicitly remove the
previously established product constitution and cognitive
ground conditions or assert infinitely many simultaneous
products. Nor does a count of cognitive acts alone
provide a physical energy or elapsed-time measurement.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Avoid harmonizing the dispute.** The two explanations
cannot be merged into a single doctrine that deities
necessarily implement the deceased practitioner's sustaining
will. That relation is not stated. The first account
attributes persistence to the practitioner's determination;
the second denies that post-mortem cause and substitutes
divine efficacy.

**Witness caution in the flesh example.** The source
prints `āryakāśyapena māṃsādīnāmadhhiṣṭhānāt`. Its
last expression is defective and, on the surface, does
not clearly express the negative force expected after
the exclusion of unstable things. The surrounding sentence
secures that exclusion, but the exact construal of the
flesh example remains for collation. It is not enough
to remove the doubled `h` and declare the passage repaired.

The running question also has `nirmita` where the intended
constructed object is clear from the following answer.
Variant skeletal-framework spellings remain in the witness.
The official Sanskrit verse is unaffected by these prose
issues; the underlying files are unchanged.

**Organon reconstruction — Yoga Vidyā and Yoga Technē.**
Yoga Vidyā distinguishes a continuing result from the
account offered of its cause. Yoga Technē, its kriyā–karma
companion, concerns the production and sustaining operations
and the transformation of their exercise through mastery.

For Organon, two useful relations emerge. A persistent
result does not by itself identify the mechanism maintaining
it. A developed capacity can also change the relation
between operations and products. These are distinct
questions of causal attribution and operational development.

The software analogy of a persistent artifact or a
single operation producing several outputs is a project
application. It does not verify the Mahākāśyapa narrative,
settle its recorded disagreement, or convert a cognitive
count into a general efficiency metric. The source's
positive determination is the explicit numerical reversal,
not an unrestricted claim of reduced cost in every sense.
