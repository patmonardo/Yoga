# VAK 5.36 Bhāṣya — One Existence Āsrava, Separate Ignorance

## Kārikā Anchor

> avyākṛtāntarmukhā hi te samāhitabhūmikāḥ |
> ata ekīkṛtā mūlam avidyety āsravaḥ pṛthak || 5.36 ||

> The higher-domain anuśayas are ethically indeterminate,
> inward directed, and situated on concentrated levels;
> therefore they are grouped together. Ignorance is a
> separate āsrava because it is the root.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Why are the anuśayas belonging to the form and formless
domains gathered together as one existence āsrava?
“Because they are ethically indeterminate, inward directed,
and located on concentrated levels; therefore they are made
one.” Both groups share those three features. For the same
reason that craving for existence was named as a single
category, the existence āsrava is also named as one.

Ignorance in the three domains is thereby established as
the ignorance āsrava, totaling fifteen factors. Why is it
set apart? “Because ignorance is the root of all of these,
the āsrava is separate.”

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The grouping of form and formless afflictions is grounded
in a **threefold shared character**: their ethical status
is indeterminate; their activity is inward directed; and
they belong to concentrated levels. The prose invokes
the earlier unification of `bhavarāga` as a parallel.
This supplies the reason that 5.35 announced but did not
yet explain.

Ignorance is classified by a different criterion. Five
ignorance types in each of three domains yield fifteen
factors. They form one separate āsrava because ignorance
is the root of the other afflictions. The difference of
criterion matters: similarity of mode groups the higher
domains, while causal priority distinguishes ignorance
within the same afflictive field. These are source-level
claims of the present classification, not a claim that
ignorance is outside conditioned existence.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Avyākṛta` retains the ethical
sense established in 5.19; it does not mean unafflicted.
`Antarmukha` is explained as inward-directed operation
(`antarmukhapravṛtta`), while `samāhitabhūmika` locates
the anuśayas on concentrated levels. The malformed
`sādhamyerṇa` is provisionally understood as threefold
similarity. `Mūlam avidyā` makes ignorance's causal rank
the stated reason for a separate category.

**Organon reconstruction.** A classification can unify
factors across domains through a shared mode of operation,
then distinguish a factor present within those domains
because it grounds the others. For the Science of Citta,
this is a reminder that membership and explanatory priority
are different relations. This is a project-level use of
the Kośa, separate from any implemented formal system.
