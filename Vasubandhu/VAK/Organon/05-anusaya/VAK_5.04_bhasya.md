# VAK 5.04 Bhāṣya — Thirty-Two Abandoned Through Seeing

## Kārikā Anchor

> daśaite saptasaptāṣṭau tridvidṛṣṭivivarjitāḥ |
> yathākramaṃ prahīyante kāme duḥkhādidarśanaiḥ || 5.04 ||

> In the desire-domain, these ten, then seven, seven, and eight—
> respectively excluding three and two views—are abandoned through
> seeing suffering and the remaining truths in order.

The question `katame ta ity āha` at the end of 5.03 introduces this
verse. Its own commentary ends after the explanation of thirty-two
items abandoned through seeing.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

“In the desire-domain, these ten, then seven, seven, and eight,
respectively excluding three and two views, are abandoned through
seeing suffering and the remaining truths in order.”

All ten anuśayas already named are to be abandoned in the
desire-domain through seeing suffering. From these same ten, seven
are to be abandoned through seeing origination. Seven are to be
abandoned through seeing cessation, excluding satkāya-view,
extreme-grasping view, and adherence to discipline and observance.
Eight are to be abandoned through seeing the path, excluding
satkāya-view and extreme-grasping view.

Thus these thirty-two anuśayas belonging to the desire-domain are
to be abandoned through seeing, because they are abandoned through
the mere seeing of the truths.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The commentary supplies the explicit membership behind the four
counts. The tenfold base is present in full for suffering. For
origination the source gives seven without naming the omitted three
in this sentence; its `ebhya eva` keeps the same tenfold base in view.
For cessation it explicitly omits satkāya-view, extreme-grasping
view, and adherence to discipline and observance. For the path it
omits the first two views. The four counts total thirty-two.

The source's decisive explanation is `satyānāṃ darśanamātreṇa
prahāṇāt`: the relevant anuśayas are abandoned by the seeing of
the truths itself. This is a technical mode of removal, not simply
the adoption of a better verbal opinion. At this point the
commentary has established the desire-domain classes abandoned
through seeing; it has not yet given the cultivation-abandoned four,
which open 5.05.

`Dṛṣṭi` in the exclusions names afflicted views. `Darśana` in the
instrumental `duḥkhādidarśanaiḥ` names seeing the truths. The shared
verbal root does not erase their opposed roles in this passage.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Nirodha` is rendered “cessation” in the
continuous translation, while the technical Sanskrit term remains
visible here. `Darśanamātra` is rendered “mere seeing” to preserve
the Bhāṣya's explicit account of the removal mode. The exact three
excluded for origination are not silently supplied from a later
summary; this local prose gives the count and leaves that list
implicit.

**Organon reconstruction.** The chapter now relates each afflicted
determination to a particular truth-disclosure. This is a stricter
structure than a generic true/false flag: a view has a domain and a
specific condition under which it is abandoned. That structural
lesson is our use of the Kośa, not an implementation claim or an
additional doctrine attributed to Vasubandhu.
