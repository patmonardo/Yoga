# VAK_5.56

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
दौर्मनस्येन काङ्क्षा अन्ये सौमनस्येन कामजाः ।
सर्वेऽप्युपेक्षया स्वैः स्वैर्यथाभूम्यूर्ध्वभूमिकाः ॥ ५.५६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
daurmanasyena kāṅkṣā anye saumanasyena kāmajāḥ |
sarve 'py upekṣayā svaiḥ svair yathā-bhūmy ūrdhva-bhūmikāḥ || 5.56 ||
```

VAK 5.56 completes the anuśaya–feeling analysis begun in 5.55. It first
specifies further desire-domain pairings, then states a general equanimity
rule, and finally constrains higher-level conjunctions by the faculties
available in each level.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
daurmanasyena | kāṅkṣā | anye | saumanasyena | kāma-jāḥ |
sarve | api | upekṣayā | svaiḥ | svaiḥ | yathā-bhūmi | ūrdhva-bhūmikāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `daurmanasyena` | instrumental singular neuter | with mental distress or dejection |
| `kāṅkṣā` | nominative singular feminine | afflictive doubt; the Bhāṣya treats it as unresolved seeking for certainty |
| `anye` | nominative plural masculine | the others: specifically the other four views and conceit |
| `saumanasyena` | instrumental singular neuter | with mental gladness |
| `kāma-jāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | arising in the desire-domain |
| `sarve api` | nominative plural plus particle | all also; all the anuśayas under discussion |
| `upekṣayā` | instrumental singular feminine | with the faculty of neutral or equanimous feeling |
| `svaiḥ svaiḥ` | repeated instrumental plural masculine/neuter | with their own respective faculties |
| `yathā-bhūmi` | adverbial compound | according to the level or domain |
| `ūrdhva-bhūmikāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | afflictions belonging to higher levels |

`Kāmajāḥ` limits the determinate pairings in the first half to the desire-
domain. `Yathābhūmi` makes the final rule domain-relative rather than a simple
extension of that matrix upward.

## 4. Grammar

The first clause is elliptical for the conjunction predicate carried forward
from VAK 5.55:

```text
kāṅkṣā
    daurmanasyena [saṃprayuktā]
```

The next clause shares the same omitted predicate:

```text
anye kāmajāḥ
    saumanasyena [saṃprayuktāḥ]
```

Although `anye` is lexically “others,” the Bhāṣya explicitly answers its own
question `ke punar anye?`: the other four views and conceit. It must not be
expanded to every remaining affliction.

The third clause shifts from specifically fixed pairings to a general rule:

```text
sarve api
    upekṣayā [saṃprayuktāḥ]
```

The final clause is distributive:

```text
ūrdhva-bhūmikāḥ
    svaiḥ svaiḥ [indriyaiḥ saṃprayuktāḥ]
    yathā-bhūmi
```

The repeated `svaiḥ svaiḥ` assigns to each higher-level affliction the
faculties proper to its own level. The Bhāṣya further preserves the distinction
between factors belonging to consciousness-bodies and those restricted to the
mental plane.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Doubt is [conjoined] with mental distress; the other desire-born
> [afflictions] with mental gladness. All are also [conjoined] with
> equanimity. Those belonging to higher levels [are conjoined] with their own
> respective [faculties], according to the level.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Doubt is associated with mental distress. The other four views and conceit,
> when arising in the desire-domain, are associated with mental gladness. All
> these anuśayas can also be associated with the faculty of equanimity.
> Afflictions belonging to higher levels are associated with the feeling-
> faculties proper to their respective levels, according to which faculties
> are available there.

The identification of `anye`, the scope of `kāmajāḥ`, and the reference of
`svaiḥ svaiḥ` to the available faculties are supplied explicitly by the
Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Afflictive doubt seeks determination yet remains unable to attain it, and
> therefore occurs with mental distress. The remaining four views and conceit
> can affirm themselves with gladness in the desire-domain. Yet characteristic
> affect is not exclusive affect: every anuśaya can also persist with neutral
> feeling. In higher levels, possible conjunction is governed by the affective
> and cognitive constitution of each field. Calmness, subtlety, and elevation
> accordingly alter the mode in which affliction appears without, by
> themselves, abolishing affliction.

The verse replaces any one-to-one equation between affliction and feeling
with a domain-indexed structure of possible conjunctions.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kāṅkṣā` — afflictive doubt**

The Bhāṣya explains doubt through the doubter's desire for certainty
(`niścayena arthin`) and consequent mental distress. Doubt is the unresolved
cognitive-afflictive orientation; distress is its conjoined painful mental
tone.

**`Anye` — the specified others**

Not an unrestricted remainder. The Bhāṣya identifies:

```text
four other views
    +
conceit
```

These are associated with gladness because they operate in a mode of
exhilaration or elation (`harṣākāravartitva`).

**`Kāmaja` — arising in the desire-domain**

A domain predicate delimiting the specific conjunctions established across
VAK 5.55–5.56. It does not mean merely sensual in an ordinary psychological
sense.

**`Pratiniyata-saṃprayoga` — specifically fixed conjunction**

The Bhāṣya's description of the characteristic pairings stated before the
general equanimity rule. A characteristic pairing remains compatible with an
additional neutral pairing.

**`Upekṣā` — neutral or equanimous feeling-faculty**

Here one of the five feeling-faculties, not perfected liberative equanimity.
Its affective neutrality does not entail the absence of an afflicted
orientation.

**`Pravāha-ccheda-kāla` — time of cutting the stream**

The reported (`kila`) doctrinal explanation for why equanimity remains when
the stream of kleśas is being cut off. The general compatibility rule is
secure; its prose rationale should retain its reported status.

**`Yathābhūmi` — according to the level**

The set of possible feeling-conjunctions is constrained by the faculties
constituting each domain or level.

**`Ūrdhvabhūmika` — belonging to a higher level**

Higher in domain does not mean unafflicted. It indicates a subtler field with
its own available faculties and cognitive supports.

## 8. Logical Determination

The specific desire-domain rules are:

```text
PermitsConjunction(Kāṅkṣā, Daurmanasya, DesireDomain)

For x in {FourOtherViews, Māna}:
    PermitsConjunction(x, Saumanasya, DesireDomain)
```

The general rule adds:

```text
For every anuśaya x under discussion:
    PermitsConjunction(x, Upekṣā, AppropriateDomain)
```

Consequently:

```text
CharacteristicFeeling(x, f)
    ⇏ ExclusiveFeeling(x, f)

EquanimousFeeling(event)
    ⇏ Unafflicted(event)
```

The higher-level rule is a constraint on possibility:

```text
PermitsConjunction(x, f, d)
    → AfflictionAvailableIn(x, d)
      AND FeelingFacultyAvailableIn(f, d)
      AND CognitiveSupportCompatible(x, f, d)
```

At least four coordinates must therefore remain distinct:

```text
AfflictionType
FeelingFaculty
DomainOrLevel
CognitivePlane
```

This also maintains the difference between relations already established in
Chapter 5:

```text
niḥṣyanda    genetic derivation
saṃprayoga   synchronic conjunction
bhūmi        field of possible occurrence
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Bhāṣya-controlled scope.** The prose corrects the tempting but overbroad
reading of `anye`: only the four views other than false view and conceit are
meant. It also makes the first-half classification specifically a desire-
domain matrix.

**Doubt is not inquiry.** The verse concerns afflictive suspension that seeks
certainty but cannot settle itself, not disciplined investigation whose
openness is ordered toward knowledge.

**Neutrality is not liberation.** `Sarve 'py upekṣayā` blocks inference from
low affective intensity to purity. Attachment, view, ignorance, doubt, or
conceit can remain operative without vivid pleasure or distress.

**Domain is constitutive.** `Yathābhūmi` does not add an external location to
an otherwise complete state. Each level determines which feeling-faculties
and cognitive supports are available, thereby delimiting possible
conjunctions.

**Closure and transition.** The Bhāṣya concludes that the conjunction of the
kleśas with the faculties has now been explained. VAK 5.57 therefore begins
the corresponding upakleśa analysis rather than adding another anuśaya rule.

**Organon interpretation.** The source structure can later be represented by
a qualified compatibility assertion:

```text
PermittedConjunction
    hasAfflictionType
    hasFeelingFaculty
    occursInDomain
    hasCognitivePlane
    hasScope {Characteristic, General}
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This modeling proposal preserves the source distinctions; it is not projected
back into the Abhidharma exposition as source doctrine.
