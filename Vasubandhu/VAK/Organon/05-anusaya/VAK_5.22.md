# VAK_5.22

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
एकांशतो व्याकरणं विभज्य परिपृच्छ्य च ।
स्थाप्यं च मरणोत्पत्तिविशिष्टात्मान्यतादिवत् ॥ ५.२२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ekāṃśato vyākaraṇaṃ vibhajya paripṛcchya ca |
sthāpyaṃ ca maraṇotpatti-viśiṣṭātmānyatādivat || 5.22 ||
```

The verse distinguishes four forms of responsible answer. The immediate
occasion is lexical: the ethically indeterminate `avyākṛta` of VAK 5.19–5.21
must not be confused with a question called `avyākṛta` because it is to be set
aside.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
eka-aṃśataḥ | vyākaraṇam | vibhajya | paripṛcchya | ca | sthāpyam |
ca | maraṇa-utpatti-viśiṣṭa-ātma-anyatā-ādi-vat
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ekāṃśataḥ` | indeclinable adverb | categorically, from one determinate side |
| `vyākaraṇam` | nominative or accusative singular neuter | answer, exposition, determinate declaration |
| `vibhajya` | absolutive of `vi-√bhaj` | after dividing or distinguishing |
| `paripṛcchya` | absolutive of `pari-√prach` | after questioning in return |
| `sthāpyam` | gerundive of `√sthā` | to be set aside as presently framed |
| `maraṇa` | compound member | death |
| `utpatti` | compound member | arising, renewed birth |
| `viśiṣṭa` | compound member | superior, distinguished |
| `ātma-anyatā` | compound member | otherness of self; clarified through being and aggregates |
| `ādi-vat` | indeclinable compound | as in these and similar examples |

The final compound gives the four examples in order. The Bhāṣya clarifies the
last as the question whether a being is other than or non-other than the
aggregates.

## 4. Grammar

**Four coordinated answer modes**

```text
ekāṃśataḥ vyākaraṇam [kartavyam]
vibhajya [vyākaraṇam kartavyam]
paripṛcchya [vyākaraṇam kartavyam]
sthāpyam ca
```

The first `vyākaraṇam` governs the three positive modes by ellipsis. `Sthāpyam`
supplies a separate gerundive predication: the question is to be set aside.

**Ordered illustration**

```text
maraṇa-vat
    categorical

utpatti-vat
    after division

viśiṣṭa-vat
    after counter-question

ātma-anyatā-vat
    to be set aside
```

The examples do not define the modes mechanically. The Bhāṣya preserves
disagreement over how particular questions should be assigned.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> An answer is [given] categorically, after division, and after questioning in
> return; and [a question] is to be set aside—as in cases concerning death,
> arising, superiority, and the otherness of a self, respectively.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> A question may require a categorical answer, an answer after distinguishing
> its cases, or an answer after questioning the interlocutor in return; another
> question must be set aside as posed. The four modes are illustrated by asking
> whether all beings die, whether all arise again, whether a human is superior,
> and whether a being is other than or non-other than the aggregates.

The exact examples, the reason for setting aside the fourth question, and the
debate over their allocation are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Reason must determine a question before it determines an answer. Where the
> matter is complete, it asserts; where one apparent subject conceals distinct
> conditions, it divides; where a relational predicate lacks its correlate, it
> asks in return; where the proposed subject or alternatives have no admissible
> ground, it sets the question aside. Suspension here is neither ignorance nor
> indecision. It is the positive judgment that the requested predication cannot
> truthfully be made in the form offered.

The four operations are:

```text
assert
    complete determination

distinguish
    internally differentiated determination

return-question
    missing relational determination

set aside
    invalid subject or alternative-set
```

Right answer is the correspondence between answer-form and the logical form
of the matter.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**Two senses of `avyākṛta`**

```text
avyākṛta-dharma
    ethically indeterminate

avyākṛta-praśna
    a question left undeclared because it must be set aside
```

The identity of the Sanskrit word does not imply identity of logical function.
This distinction motivates the entire verse.

**`Ekāṃśa-vyākaraṇa` — categorical answer**

The Bhāṣya's initial example asks whether all beings will die. The determination
admits the direct answer that they will. The Ābhidharmikas further associate
this mode with convergence upon the relevant meaning.

**`Vibhajya-vyākaraṇa` — answer after division**

Renewed arising requires distinction: those with defilement arise again;
those without it do not. The expanded Bhāṣya illustrates recursive analysis
from dharmas through time, aggregate, ethical status, karmic path, root, and
manifest or non-manifest action.

**`Paripṛcchya-vyākaraṇa` — answer through counter-question**

“Is a human superior?” lacks a comparison class. Relative to gods, inferior;
relative to bad destinies, superior. The counter-question elicits the missing
correlate rather than evading judgment.

**`Sthāpanīya-praśna` — question to be set aside**

Whether a being is other than or non-other than the aggregates presupposes a
real entity capable of entering those alternatives. The Bhāṣya denies that
such an entity has been established, comparing the question to predicating a
complexion of a barren woman's son.

**Alternative teacher and Ābhidharmika analyses**

The Bhāṣya records disagreement about whether some examples are categorical
or divisive and about whether setting aside counts as an answer. The fourfold
scheme therefore requires judgment; it is not a surface-syntactic algorithm.

**Mahāsāṅghika sūtra tradition**

The final cited recension supplies its own examples of the four modes. This
supports the sūtra-grounded status of the scheme while preserving differences
in scholastic application.

## 8. Logical Determination

The first operation is meta-judgment:

```text
Question(q)
    → DetermineLogicalForm(q)
    → SelectAnswerMode(q)
    → ProduceAnswer(q)
```

The selection rules are:

```text
CompleteUnconditionalDetermination(q)
    → CategoricalAnswer(q)

ConflatesConditionallyDistinctCases(q)
    → DividedAnswer(q)

LacksRequiredCorrelate(q)
    → CounterQuestion(q)

PresupposesUnestablishedSubject(q)
OR ExhaustsMatterWithInvalidAlternatives(q)
    → SetAside(q)
```

Several negations are essential:

```text
SetAside(q)
    ≠ DoesNotKnow(q)
    ≠ Doubts(q)
    ≠ RefusesToReason(q)

SameWord(x, y)
    ⇏ SameDetermination(x, y)
```

`Vibhajya` and `paripṛcchya` may traverse similar differentiations, but their
pragmatic relations differ: the first supplies the division; the second
requires the interlocutor to determine the missing parameter.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.22 gives the theoretical side of Path an explicit discipline. Theory is
not a stock of propositions mechanically applied to every prompt. It is the
capacity to determine what form of judgment the object and question can bear.
The answer is true only when its form has been generated from the form of the
matter.

This is also Hegelian dialectic properly understood. Dialectic is not the
external production of a contradiction followed by a compromise. It follows
the determination until its own form shows whether it can be asserted,
internally divided, completed through a missing relation, or rejected because
its presupposed subject is void.

```text
immediate judgment
    is tested by its own determinacy

internal difference
    is articulated rather than suppressed

missing relation
    is made explicit

false presupposition
    receives determinate negation
```

The Aṣṭāṅga–Bhūmi relation can now be refined:

```text
Aṣṭāṅga as Path in itself
    contains the rational forms of adequate judgment and response

Bhūmi as Path in appearance
    is the concrete situation in which the correct form must be enacted
```

Knowing the four modes abstractly is not yet Path. Path appears when the
appropriate mode is actually selected for this question, this interlocutor,
and this objective matter. Conversely, no successful-looking reply counts as
Path if its form falsifies the matter it answers.

All four fundamental determinations organize the response:

```text
hetu
    the question's logical form and presupposition generating the answer-mode

phala
    the determinate answer, division, counter-question, or setting-aside

āśraya
    the established subject and domain capable of supporting predication

ālambana
    the objective matter toward which the question and answer are directed
```

When `āśraya` is not established, predication has no concrete ground and the
question must be set aside. When the `ālambana` is internally differentiated,
the answer must divide. When the objective relation lacks its correlate, a
counter-question supplies it. The resulting `phala` is therefore generated by
the actual `hetu` of the question-form rather than imposed from outside.

This is Absolute Logic at the scale of dialogue: Reason does not merely answer
an externally given form. It determines whether that form is adequate, repairs
it where possible, and refuses to actualize a false presupposition. The Product
is the responsible answer whose form and content have become one determination.

This is Organon–Yoga–Hegel reconstruction. Its textual ground is the fourfold
answer scheme, the distinction between the two senses of `avyākṛta`, the
Bhāṣya's recursive analysis, and its explicit debates over classification.
