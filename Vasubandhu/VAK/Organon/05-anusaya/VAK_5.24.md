# VAK_5.24

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सर्वत्रानागतैरेभिर्मानसैः स्वाध्विके परैः ।
अजैः सर्वत्र शेषैस्तु सर्वैः सर्वत्र संयुतः ॥ ५.२४ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sarvatrānāgatairebhir mānasaiḥ svādhvike paraiḥ |
ajaiḥ sarvatra śeṣais tu sarvaiḥ sarvatra saṃyutaḥ || 5.24 ||
```

The verse completes the contrast between particular-character and general-
character afflictions by determining their binding range across cognitive
locus, temporal object, and future mode of actualization.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sarvatra | anāgataiḥ | ebhiḥ | mānasaiḥ | sva-ādhvike | paraiḥ |
ajaiḥ | sarvatra | śeṣaiḥ | tu | sarvaiḥ | sarvatra | saṃyutaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `sarvatra` | indeclinable adverb | everywhere; with respect to every object |
| `anāgataiḥ` | instrumental plural | by future afflictions |
| `ebhiḥ` | instrumental plural pronoun | by these [attachment, aversion, and conceit] |
| `mānasaiḥ` | instrumental plural adjective | mental; belonging to mind-consciousness |
| `sva-ādhvike` | locative singular adjective used substantivally | in what belongs to their own temporal period |
| `paraiḥ` | instrumental plural | by the others |
| `ajaiḥ` | instrumental plural | by the non-arising ones; those not destined to arise |
| `śeṣaiḥ` | instrumental plural | by the remaining afflictions |
| `tu` | adversative particle | but |
| `sarvaiḥ` | instrumental plural | by all, in every temporal mode |
| `saṃyutaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | connected, bound |

The Bhāṣya resolves the compact expressions: `paraiḥ` denotes future sensory
attachment and aversion; `ajaiḥ` is glossed as `anutpattidharma`, having the
nature not to arise; `śeṣaiḥ` denotes view, doubt, and ignorance.

## 4. Grammar

**Future mental particular afflictions**

```text
anāgataiḥ ebhiḥ mānasaiḥ
sarvatra [vastuni] saṃyutaḥ

by these future afflictions when mental,
one is connected with every object
```

`Ebhiḥ` carries forward attachment, aversion, and conceit from VAK 5.23. The
Bhāṣya specifies that their mental form can take objects in all three times.

**Future sensory afflictions destined to arise**

```text
paraiḥ [anāgataiḥ]
sva-ādhvike [vastuni saṃyutaḥ]

by the other future [afflictions],
one is connected with objects of their own time
```

The “others” are attachment and aversion belonging to the five sensory
consciousness-groups. Conceit is absent because it belongs to the mental plane.

**Non-arising and remaining cases**

```text
ajaiḥ sarvatra [saṃyutaḥ]
śeṣaiḥ tu sarvaiḥ sarvatra saṃyutaḥ

by those not destined to arise, one is bound everywhere;
but by all the remaining afflictions, one is bound everywhere
```

The standing qualification from VAK 5.23—so long as the afflictions remain
unabandoned—continues throughout, according to the Bhāṣya.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> By these future afflictions, when mental, one is bound everywhere; by the
> others, to what belongs to their own temporal period. By those not destined
> to arise, [one is bound] everywhere; but by all the remaining afflictions,
> one is bound everywhere.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Future attachment, aversion, and conceit belonging to mind-consciousness bind
> with respect to objects of all three times. Future attachment and aversion
> belonging to the five sensory consciousnesses, when destined to arise, bind
> only to future objects. Yet those same sensory afflictions, when not destined
> to arise, bind with respect to every temporal object. All past, present, and
> future views, doubts, and ignorances bind universally because they are
> general-character afflictions, so long as they remain unabandoned.

The cognitive loci, the destined/non-destined distinction, the identity of the
remaining afflictions, and the continued abandonment condition are supplied by
the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The scope of bondage is not intrinsic to an affliction's name. It is
> determined through the cognitive system that carries it, the temporal field
> that system can apprehend, whether the future determination is destined to
> occur, and whether it has been abandoned. Mind extends across the three
> times, so its future afflictions inherit a three-time range; sensory cognition
> destined to arise remains tied to its contemporaneous future object. Even a
> future affliction that will not manifest can remain structurally included in
> bondage. Appearance and efficacy are therefore non-identical.

The scope propagates relationally:

```text
cognitive carrier
    → temporal object-range
        → binding range
```

But modal non-actualization does not by itself equal abandonment.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Mānasa / manobhūmika` — belonging to the mental plane**

The Bhāṣya applies this to future attachment, aversion, and conceit associated
with mind-consciousness. Because mental cognition can take past, present, and
future objects, these afflictions bind across all three times.

**`Tryadhva-viṣayatva` — having the three times as object-range**

The explicit Bhāṣya rationale for the universal temporal scope of future
mental afflictions. The carrier's cognitive range determines the affliction's
binding range.

**`Pañca-vijñāna-kāyika` — belonging to the five consciousness-groups**

This identifies `paraiḥ` as sensory attachment and aversion. When destined to
arise, they bind only to future objects because sensory cognition takes an
object contemporaneous with its own occurrence.

**`Utpattidharma` — having the nature of arising**

A future determination destined to become occurrent. This modal property is
distinct from merely being future.

**`Anutpattidharma` — having the nature not to arise**

The Bhāṣya's gloss of `ajaiḥ`. Such a future sensory affliction will not become
an actual occurrence, yet it can still count as binding with respect to every
object.

**`Sāmānya-kleśa` — general-character affliction**

View, doubt, and ignorance across the three times. Their general character is
the reason the Bhāṣya gives for their universal binding range.

**The continued `aprahīṇa` condition**

Every rule remains limited to unabandoned afflictions. Neither wide cognitive
scope nor future ontological status overrides the Path's decisive operation of
abandonment.

## 8. Logical Determination

The mental-carrier rule is:

```text
Future(x)
AND ParticularAffliction(x)
AND CarriedBy(x, MindConsciousness)
AND NotAbandoned(x)
    → BindingRange(x, PastPresentFutureObjects)
```

The sensory rule for determinations destined to arise is:

```text
Future(x)
AND SensoryAttachmentOrAversion(x)
AND DestinedToArise(x)
AND NotAbandoned(x)
    → BindingRange(x, FutureObjects)
```

The non-arising case blocks a simple actuality inference:

```text
Future(x)
AND SensoryAttachmentOrAversion(x)
AND NotDestinedToArise(x)
AND NotAbandoned(x)
    → BindingRange(x, AllThreeTimes)
```

The general-affliction rule is:

```text
GeneralCharacterAffliction(x)
AND TemporalStatus(x) ∈ {Past, Present, Future}
AND NotAbandoned(x)
    → UniversalBindingRange(x)
```

Consequently, these predicates remain distinct:

```text
ExistsAsFutureDetermination
DestinedToArise
ActuallyArises
Binds
IsAbandoned
```

No one may be substituted for another.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.24 forces Path to operate more deeply than the management of appearances.
If a determination may fail ever to manifest and still belong to the structure
of bondage, then freedom cannot be inferred merely from the absence of an
observable affliction. Path must transform the modal field that makes the
affliction structurally available.

This clarifies the Aṣṭāṅga–Bhūmi relation:

```text
Aṣṭāṅga as Path in itself
    reorganizes the generative and modal structure of possible action

Bhūmi as Path in appearance
    displays that transformation as a changed field of actual and possible
    relations
```

The bhūmi is therefore more than a visible achievement. It is the appearance
of a new law of possibility. If the old determination remains available as an
unabandoned bond, the absence of its present manifestation does not yet prove
the corresponding Path-level.

All four functions are again necessary:

```text
hetu
    the carrier and modal conditions determining relational scope

phala
    the binding range generated across temporal objects

āśraya
    mind- or sense-consciousness as the operative cognitive locus

ālambana
    past, present, or future objects within the carrier's range
```

Here `āśraya` is especially concrete. The same broad affliction receives a
different range according to whether mind-consciousness or sensory
consciousness carries it. The `ālambana` is correspondingly tri-temporal or
own-period. From their relation the binding `phala` follows.

The general-character afflictions reveal a further danger. A view, doubt, or
ignorance can configure the entire temporal field even without attaching to
one present object. Path in itself must therefore correct the general rule of
disclosure; Path in appearance must exhibit that correction as the inability
of the old rule to bind across the field.

This is Organon–Yoga–Hegel reconstruction. Its textual ground is the Bhāṣya's
scope propagation from cognitive locus, its distinction between future
determinations destined and not destined to arise, its general-affliction
rule, and its continued `aprahīṇa` condition.
