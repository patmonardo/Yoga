# VAK_5.20

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
कामेऽकुशलमूलानि रागप्रतिघमूढयः ।
त्रीण्यव्याकृतमूलानि तृष्णाविद्या मतिश्च सा ॥ ५.२० ॥
```

The local kārikā-only witness repeats `अकुशलमूलानि` in the third pāda. The
Bhāṣya explicitly asks how many roots of the indeterminate there are, answers
“three roots of the indeterminate,” and names them. The official study
therefore adopts the contextually required reading `अव्याकृतमूलानि` while
recording rather than concealing the received variant.

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kāme 'kuśala-mūlāni rāga-pratigha-mūḍhayaḥ |
trīṇy avyākṛta-mūlāni tṛṣṇāvidyā matiś ca sā || 5.20 ||
```

The verse moves from the ethical classification of VAK 5.19 to generative
ground: which determinations function as roots of the unwholesome and of the
ethically indeterminate.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kāme | akuśala-mūlāni | rāga-pratigha-mūḍhayaḥ |
trīṇi | avyākṛta-mūlāni | tṛṣṇā | avidyā | matiḥ | ca | sā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `kāme` | locative singular | in the desire-domain |
| `akuśala-mūlāni` | nominative plural neuter | roots of the unwholesome |
| `rāga-pratigha-mūḍhayaḥ` | nominative plural expression | attachment, hostility, and deluded ignorance |
| `trīṇi` | nominative plural neuter numeral | three |
| `avyākṛta-mūlāni` | nominative plural neuter | roots of the ethically indeterminate |
| `tṛṣṇā` | nominative singular feminine | craving, thirst |
| `avidyā` | nominative singular feminine | ignorance |
| `matiḥ` | nominative singular feminine | cognition, discriminative understanding |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `sā` | nominative singular feminine pronoun | that [indeterminate one]; marking the indeterminate mode |

The Bhāṣya restates `rāga`, `pratigha`, and delusion as `lobha`, `dveṣa`, and
`moha`, and glosses `mati` as `prajñā`.

## 4. Grammar

**Roots of the unwholesome**

```text
kāme
rāga-pratigha-mūḍhayaḥ
akuśala-mūlāni [trīṇi]

in the desire-domain,
attachment, hostility, and deluded ignorance
are [three] roots of the unwholesome
```

The semantic subjects are expressed through a compound enumeration while the
neuter plural predicate agrees with the understood collective members as
`mūlāni`.

**Roots of the indeterminate**

```text
trīṇi avyākṛta-mūlāni:
tṛṣṇā, avidyā, matiḥ ca sā

the roots of the indeterminate are three:
craving, ignorance, and that cognition
```

According to the Bhāṣya, `sā` shows that the named factors are meant in their
indeterminate form. It is not a fourth member of the triad.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> In the desire-domain, attachment, hostility, and deluded ignorance are roots
> of the unwholesome. The roots of the indeterminate are three: craving,
> ignorance, and that cognition.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> In the desire-domain, attachment, hostility, and unwholesome delusion are the
> three roots of unwholesome dharmas. The roots of ethically indeterminate
> dharmas are likewise three: indeterminate craving, ignorance, and
> discriminative cognition. According to the Kāśmīra position, this includes
> every indeterminate instance of these, even cognition born of maturation.

The qualification of delusion, the force of `sā`, the gloss `mati = prajñā`,
and the broad final classification are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> To possess an ethical quality is not yet to be a root of that quality. A root
> must both have the determination and generate further dharmas of the same
> order. Attachment, hostility, and delusion reproduce the unwholesome field;
> craving, ignorance, and discriminative cognition can reproduce an
> indeterminate field. Cognition is therefore a formal power of determination,
> not an intrinsically liberating principle. Its value depends upon the whole
> system in which it functions.

The movement is from predicate to ground:

```text
ethical status
    what the determination is

root-function
    what field the determination generates and sustains
```

Classification and production must be represented separately.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Mūla` — root or generative ground**

Not merely an early member of a sequence. A `mūla` grounds and reproduces a
field of determinations. The Bhāṣya requires root-status in addition to the
ethical status possessed by the factor itself.

**`Akuśalamūla` — root of the unwholesome**

The Bhāṣya defines it conjunctively:

```text
it is itself unwholesome
and
it is a root of unwholesome dharmas
```

The three are greed or attachment, hatred or hostility, and unwholesome
delusion.

**The restriction on `moha`**

Ignorance conjoined with body-view or extreme-view was classified as
indeterminate in VAK 5.19. It therefore cannot be an unwholesome root. The
third root is the remaining unwholesome delusion.

**`Avyākṛtamūla` — root of the ethically indeterminate**

The corrected reading is guaranteed by the Bhāṣya's explicit question,
answer, and enumeration. The category names a positive generative structure,
not an absence of causal determination.

**`Tṛṣṇā`, `avidyā`, and `mati / prajñā`**

In their indeterminate modes, craving, ignorance, and discriminative cognition
are the three roots. The Kāśmīra classification extends even to resultant
`prajñā`.

**`Vipākaja` — born of maturation**

A determination arising as a result of previous karma. Its inclusion shows
the breadth of the Kāśmīra theory of indeterminate roots.

**Kāśmīra attribution**

The claim that every indeterminate instance of craving, ignorance, and
`prajñā`, including resultant cognition, is an indeterminate root is explicitly
attributed to the Kāśmīras. It should not be presented as an unattributed
consensus.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya supplies a strict conjunction:

```text
UnwholesomeRoot(x)
    ↔ Unwholesome(x)
      AND FunctionsAsRootOf(x, UnwholesomeDharma)
```

Hence:

```text
Unwholesome(x)
    ⇏ UnwholesomeRoot(x)

Indeterminate(x)
    ⇏ IndeterminateRoot(x)
```

Root-function is an additional relation, not a consequence of class
membership alone.

The two triads are:

```text
UnwholesomeRootSet
    = {Attachment, Hostility, UnwholesomeDelusion}

IndeterminateRootSet
    = {IndeterminateCraving,
       IndeterminateIgnorance,
       IndeterminateDiscriminativeCognition}
```

The important formal result is:

```text
Discriminates(x)
    ⇏ Liberates(x)
```

The role of discriminative cognition must be determined through its status,
object, support, and productive function.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.20 gives the fourfold architecture an explicit center: `mūla` is
generative ground. Ethical classification from VAK 5.19 becomes a system only
when we know which determinations reproduce the corresponding field.

```text
hetu
    mūla as the generative reason or root

phala
    the unwholesome or indeterminate field reproduced from that root

āśraya
    the domain and concrete complex in which the root operates

ālambana
    the objective determination through which cognition and affliction proceed
```

The appearance of `mati`, explicitly glossed as `prajñā`, among indeterminate
roots is crucial for the Yoga–Organon architecture. It proves that
discriminative cognition considered in abstraction is not yet Path. A formal
capacity to distinguish can organize bondage, ethical neutrality, or
liberation according to the system in which it is active.

This lets us state the Aṣṭāṅga–Bhūmi distinction exactly:

```text
Aṣṭāṅga
    Path in itself
    the internally complete articulation in which each aṅga
    receives its liberative function from the whole

Bhūmis
    Path in appearance
    the achieved levels through which that complete articulation
    becomes concrete and objectively manifest
```

An `aṅga` abstracted from this whole is merely a formal capacity. `Prajñā`, for
example, does not liberate because its name is exalted; it liberates when its
object, support, causal function, and result are determined within the Path in
itself. The bhūmis then display that inner articulation as achieved existence.

Thus Path Objectivity has both sides:

```text
inner systematic form
    determines what each function means

appearing Path-levels
    actualize and exhibit that meaning
```

Neither side is complete alone. Without Aṣṭāṅga, the bhūmis would be an
external succession of stages. Without the bhūmis, Aṣṭāṅga would remain an
unmanifest schema. Their unity is the Concrete Path.

This is Organon–Yoga reconstruction. The textual ground in VAK 5.20 is the
distinction between ethical status and generative root-function, together with
the Bhāṣya's refusal to treat `prajñā` as intrinsically liberating.
