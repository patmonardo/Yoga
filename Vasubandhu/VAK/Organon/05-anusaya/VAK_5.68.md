# VAK_5.68

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अनास्रववियोगाप्तेर्भवाग्रविकलीकृतेः ।
हेतुद्वयसमुद्घातात् परिज्ञा धात्वतिक्रमात् ॥ ५.६८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
anāsrava-viyogāpter bhavāgra-vikalīkṛteḥ |
hetu-dvaya-samudghātāt parijñā dhātv-atikramāt || 5.68 ||
```

VAK 5.68 asks why every individual abandonment is not independently called a
`parijñā`. The designation marks an abandonment that has reached the required
configuration of completion, not the mere cessation of one afflictive factor.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
anāsrava-viyoga-āpteḥ | bhava-agra-vikalīkṛteḥ |
hetu-dvaya-samudghātāt | parijñā | dhātu-atikramāt
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `anāsrava-viyogāpteḥ` | ablative singular feminine compound | because of acquisition of uncontaminated disconnection |
| `bhavāgra-vikalīkṛteḥ` | ablative singular feminine compound | because the summit of existence has been rendered ineffective |
| `hetu-dvaya-samudghātāt` | ablative singular masculine compound | because of eradication of the two causes |
| `parijñā` | nominative singular feminine | completed abandonment bearing the technical designation parijñā |
| `dhātv-atikramāt` | ablative singular masculine compound | because of complete transcendence of a domain |

The four ablatives supply grounds for the designation. The Bhāṣya distributes
them by type and stage rather than requiring every individual parijñā to
satisfy one undifferentiated four-condition formula.

## 4. Grammar

The nominative `parijñā` is explained through ablative grounds:

```text
parijñā [is so designated]
    anāsrava-viyogāpteḥ
    bhavāgra-vikalīkṛteḥ
    hetu-dvaya-samudghātāt
    dhātv-atikramāt
```

The Bhāṣya first groups the opening three:

```text
yatra etāni trīṇi kāraṇāni bhavanti
    tat prahāṇaṃ parijñā ucyate
```

Where these three causes are present, the abandonment is called parijñā. It
then states that a knowledge-fruit abandonment receives the name from the same
three and from a fourth—domain-transcendence—when one passes completely beyond
a domain.

Thus the syntax presents four grounds compactly, while the prose fixes their
scope and cumulative application across Path-stages.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> [An abandonment is called] a parijñā because of acquisition of
> uncontaminated disconnection, because the summit of existence is rendered
> ineffective, because of eradication of the two causes, and because of
> transcendence of a domain.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> An abandonment that is a fruit of patience receives the designation
> `parijñā` when three conditions coincide: uncontaminated disconnection has
> been acquired, the summit of existence has been rendered ineffective, and
> the relevant twofold causal basis has been eradicated. An abandonment that
> is a fruit of knowledge receives the designation through these three
> conditions and, where applicable, through complete transcendence of a
> domain as a fourth.

The passage does not independently name both members of the “two causes.” It
explicitly identifies the still-unabandoned origin-class universal cause as
the reason the threshold has not yet been reached at the suffering stage.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Local removal does not yet constitute completed discrimination. The Path
> must acquire an uncontaminated disconnection, extend its efficacy through
> the summit of conditioned existence, and eradicate the causal structure
> capable of sustaining recurrence. In knowledge-fruit completion, passage
> beyond an entire domain supplies a further determination. `Parijñā` names
> abandonment only when its relational consequences form the requisite whole;
> it is a threshold-status, not a synonym for every act of removal.

This completion is tested against actual Path-stages rather than inferred from
the abstract meaning of the word.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti` — uncontaminated acquisition of disconnection**

The first condition. It distinguishes Path-grounded acquisition of separation
from mere worldly absence or suppression of an affliction.

**`Bhavāgra-vikalīkaraṇa` — rendering the summit of existence ineffective**

The second condition. `Bhavāgra` is the highest conditioned level; rendering
it ineffective marks counteractive reach to that summit, not simple ascent to
or residence in it.

**`Hetu-dvaya-samudghāta` — eradication of the two causes**

The third condition. The prose makes clear that the universal cause belonging
to the origin-seeing-abandonable class is one still-operative causal dimension
after seeing suffering alone. It does not locally enumerate both causes by
separate names, so their complete lexical identity remains open.

**`Sarvatraga-hetu` — universal cause**

The unremoved origin-class universal cause explains why suffering-
`anvayajñāna` has not yet reached the third condition. Its later abandonment
explains the grouping of suffering and origin in the first parijñā.

**`Dhātv-atikrama` / `dhātu-samatikrama` — transcendence of a domain**

The fourth condition for the relevant knowledge-fruit completion: completely
passing beyond a domain, not spatially relocating within it.

**`Pṛthagjana` — ordinary person**

An ordinary person's worldly abandonment lacks uncontaminated acquisition of
disconnection and the rendering-ineffective of bhavāgra; it therefore does
not receive the parijñā designation.

**`Ubhaya-saṃyoga-viyoga` — disconnection from both conjunctions**

A fifth cause proposed by other teachers (`apare`): through complete
dispassion toward an entire domain, one is also disconnected from another
affliction taking the abandoned type as object.

Vasubandhu accepts no independent fifth member because this feature is already
included under eradication of the two causes and transcendence of the domain.

## 8. Logical Determination

The basic distinction is:

```text
Prahāṇa(x)
    ⇏ Parijñā(x)
```

For patience-fruit abandonments:

```text
Acquired(UncontaminatedDisconnection, x)
AND RenderedIneffective(Bhavāgra)
AND Eradicated(RelevantTwofoldCausalBasis)
    → BearsDesignation(x, Parijñā)
```

For relevant knowledge-fruit abandonments:

```text
FirstThreeConditions(x)
AND CompletelyTranscends(x, Domain)
    → BearsDesignation(x, Parijñā)
```

The Path-stage test is cumulative:

```text
OrdinaryPersonWorldlyAbandonment:
    lacks conditions 1 and 2

Through SufferingAnvayajñānaKṣānti:
    condition 1 present; condition 2 absent

At SufferingAnvayajñāna:
    conditions 1 and 2 present; condition 3 absent

Subsequent Dharma- and Anvayajñānas:
    all first three present
```

The alternative count is reduced without denying its phenomenon:

```text
UbhayaSaṃyogaViyoga
    entailedBy
    {HetuDvayaSamudghāta, DhātvAtikrama}
    → NotIndependentFifthCondition
```

Hence:

```text
RealFeature(x)
    ⇏ IndependentClassificatoryMember(x)
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The verse answers a counting problem.** If parijñā is abandonment, why are
there nine rather than one for every abandoned factor? The answer is a
completion threshold: designation attaches only where the requisite
conditions coincide.

**The first parijñā's grouping is now explained.** VAK 5.64 stated that
suffering- and origin-seeing-abandonments form one parijñā but did not give
the reason. VAK 5.68 shows that after seeing suffering the origin-class
universal cause remains; only after its abandonment is the causal threshold
complete.

**The two causes remain lexically open.** The local prose establishes the
twofold structure and explicitly discusses one remaining universal cause, but
does not independently list two names. The edition preserves this evidential
limit.

**Distributed conditions, not four universal synonyms.** Patience-fruit
abandonments require the first three; knowledge-fruit completions also receive
the domain-transcendence ground where relevant. The four clauses answer
different aspects of completion.

**Attributed fifth condition.** Other teachers distinguish disconnection from
both conjunctions. Vasubandhu treats it as already covered, demonstrating
that acknowledging a phenomenon does not require adding a new taxonomic unit.

**Organon interpretation.** A later Dataset should distinguish abandonment
events from designation-licensing conditions:

```text

    concernsAbandonment
    satisfiesCompletionCondition
    evaluatedAt PathStage
    hasDesignationStatus
    assertedBy SourceVoice
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This is a source-preserving validation model, not terminology attributed to
the kārikā or Bhāṣya.
