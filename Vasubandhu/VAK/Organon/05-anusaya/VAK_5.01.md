# VAK_5.01

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
मूलं भवस्यानुशयाः षड् रागः प्रतिघस्तथा ।
मानोऽविद्या च दृष्टिश्च विचिकित्सा च ते पुनः ॥ ५.१ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
mūlaṃ bhavasyānuśayāḥ ṣaḍ rāgaḥ pratighas tathā |
māno 'vidyā ca dṛṣṭiś ca vicikitsā ca te punaḥ || 5.1 ||
```

The suspended `te punaḥ`, “these, again,” carries the classification into VAK
5.02. The present verse first names six anuśayas; it does not yet complete
their further division.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
mūlam | bhavasya | anuśayāḥ | ṣaṭ | rāgaḥ | pratighaḥ | tathā |
mānaḥ | avidyā | ca | dṛṣṭiḥ | ca | vicikitsā | ca | te | punaḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `mūlam` | nominative singular neuter | root, originating and sustaining basis |
| `bhavasya` | genitive singular masculine | of existence, of conditioned becoming |
| `anuśayāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | anuśayas; underlying afflictive determinations |
| `ṣaṭ` | numeral | six |
| `rāgaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | attachment, passion |
| `pratighaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | resistance, aversion, hostility |
| `tathā` | adverb | likewise, also |
| `mānaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | conceit, comparative self-estimation |
| `avidyā` | nominative singular feminine | non-knowing, ignorance |
| `dṛṣṭiḥ` | nominative singular feminine | view |
| `vicikitsā` | nominative singular feminine | doubt, irresolution |
| `te` | nominative plural masculine pronoun | those, they |
| `punaḥ` | adverb | again, in turn, further |

The technical term `anuśaya` remains visible in the translation. “Latent
tendency” would decide too early how anuśaya is related to an occurrent
`kleśa`, precisely the problem that the chapter must investigate.

## 4. Grammar

**Ground judgment**

```text
anuśayāḥ [santi] bhavasya mūlam

the anuśayas [are] the root of existence
```

The plural subject `anuśayāḥ` is predicated of the singular neuter `mūlam`.
The many anuśayas thus have one collective function: they constitute the root
of `bhava`.

**Enumeration**

```text
[anuśayāḥ] ṣaṭ [santi]:
    rāgaḥ, pratighaḥ, mānaḥ, avidyā, dṛṣṭiḥ, vicikitsā ca
```

Six singular class-names are coordinated under the supplied plural subject.
`Tathā` continues the list after `pratighaḥ`; repeated `ca` coordinates its
last members.

**Forward connective**

```text
te punaḥ ...
    those, in turn ...
```

The sentence is deliberately incomplete. Its predicate is supplied by VAK
5.02, so an official translation should retain the open transition rather
than importing the next verse into this one.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The anuśayas are the root of existence. They are six: attachment,
> resistance, conceit, non-knowing, view, and doubt. These, in turn—

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The anuśayas are the root through whose governing power karma accumulates
> and becomes capable of producing conditioned existence. Six are initially
> distinguished: attachment, aversion, conceit, non-knowing, view, and doubt.
> These, in turn, are further differentiated—

The causal mediation through karma, the sense of accumulation, and the
productive force of `mūla` are supplied by the Bhāṣya. They are not all
explicitly stated in the kārikā.

The Bhāṣya opens by recalling VAK 4.01:

```text
karmajaṃ lokavaicitryam
    the differentiation of the world is born from karma
```

It then states that karmas accumulate under the power of the anuśayas and,
without them, cannot bring `bhava` into manifestation. This establishes the
transition from the accomplished analysis of karma to the inquiry into its
afflictive ground.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Conditioned existence is not reproduced by action considered in isolation.
> Karma acquires accumulation, direction, and world-producing efficacy under
> the governance of anuśaya. The chapter therefore moves behind manifest
> action to the afflictive determination through which action becomes capable
> of sustaining a continuum. This ground is first articulated in six forms:
> attachment, resistant aversion, conceit, non-knowing, view, and doubt; their
> internal differentiation remains to follow.

The Bhāṣya describes an active `kleśa` as performing ten functions: it
strengthens the root, establishes the continuum, prepares the field, produces
an outflow, brings forth karmic existence, takes up its supporting
accumulation, deludes regarding the cognitive support, bends the stream of
`vijñāna`, turns away from the wholesome side, and spreads for the purpose of
binding. This is commentary determination, not additional wording hidden in
the verse.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Anuśaya` — technical term retained**

The kārikā calls the anuśayas the root of `bhava`; the Bhāṣya says that karma
accumulates under their power. A provisional semantic range is underlying
afflictive disposition, persistent adherence, or afflictive tendency. None is
yet adopted as an exhaustive translation.

**`Kleśa pravartamāna` — active affliction**

The Bhāṣya changes vocabulary when it introduces the ten functions: “an
active kleśa” performs them. This closely relates kleśa and anuśaya but does
not, in this passage alone, establish either their simple identity or a final
latent-versus-manifest ontology.

**`Bhava` — conditioned existence or becoming**

Here `bhava` is the conditioned existence that karma brings into
manifestation, not abstract Being.

**`Mūla` — root**

The commentary determines the metaphor functionally: without anuśaya, karma
cannot accumulate into the capacity to generate further existence.

**The six initial forms**

```text
rāga        attachment or passion
pratigha    aversion, resistance, hostility
māna        conceit or comparative self-estimation
avidyā      non-knowing or ignorance
dṛṣṭi       binding view
vicikitsā   doubt or irresolution
```

The list is initial rather than final. `Te punaḥ` expressly announces a
further articulation.

**Bhāṣya terms governing this unit**

| Term | Commentary determination |
|---|---|
| `anuśayavaśa` | governance or power of the anuśayas |
| `upacaya` | accumulation through which karma becomes productive |
| `bhavābhinirvartana` | bringing conditioned existence into manifestation |
| `saṃtati` | the continuum established by active affliction |
| `karmabhava` | karmic existence brought forth by active affliction |
| `vijñānasrotas` | stream of differentiated cognition bent by affliction |
| `kuśalapakṣa` | wholesome side from which affliction causes departure |
| `bandhana` | binding, the culminative function of active affliction |

The transmitted Bhāṣya reading around `svasaṃbhāraṃ arigṛhṇāti` is textually
uncertain in the local witness. Its general placement among the ten functions
is retained, but no silent emendation is made here.

## 8. Logical Determination

The explanatory movement is retrospective:

```text
differentiated world
    ← karma
        ← accumulation under anuśaya
```

The reproductive process runs forward:

```text
anuśaya governs karma
    → karma accumulates
        → karma becomes capable of producing bhava
            → conditioned existence continues
```

The kārikā itself has three logical moments:

```text
1. functional unity
    anuśayāḥ = bhavasya mūlam

2. initial plurality
    ṣaṭ

3. open differentiation
    te punaḥ ...
```

The Bhāṣya mediates the first judgment rather than leaving it as a metaphor:

```text
AnusayaGovernance(x)
    → KarmicAccumulation(x)
    → CapacityToProduceBhava(x)
```

It also gives a functional account of affliction extending from the
strengthening of the root to the binding of the continuum. Chapter 5 thus
begins as an inquiry into a reproductive relation, not as a detached list of
psychological items.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 4.127 ended by placing conditioned wholesome cultivation under liberation
as its unsurpassed end. VAK 5.01 now returns to the opposite structure: why
does conditioned action repeatedly acquire the power to produce another
existence at all?

The Bhāṣya supplies the answer in a strict transition:

```text
Karma
    determining activity and its accumulated efficacy

Anuśaya
    the afflictive ground under whose governance
    karma becomes reproductively effective

Mārga
    the still-future systematic abandonment
    of this organization of bondage
```

For the Organon, anuśaya may provisionally be approached as determination
persisting beneath and through overt activity. But the textual relation must
govern the reconstruction: the chapter itself has not yet licensed a fixed
substance called a “latent tendency,” nor has it reduced anuśaya without
remainder to active kleśa.

The most important positive result is therefore relational:

```text
anuśaya determines the efficacy of karma
karma realizes the reproductive force of anuśaya
their mediated operation sustains bhava
```

This is an Organon formulation of the Bhāṣya's causal account, not terminology
attributed directly to Vasubandhu.
