# VAK_5.53

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
माया शाठ्यं च कामाद्यध्यानयोर्ब्रह्मवञ्चनात् ।
स्त्यानौद्धत्यमदा धातुत्रयेऽन्ये कामधातुजाः ॥ ५.५३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
māyā śāṭhyaṃ ca kāmādya-dhyānayor brahma-vañcanāt |
styānauddhatya-madā dhātu-traye 'nye kāma-dhātujāḥ || 5.53 ||
```

VAK 5.53 completes the upakleśa analysis by distributing the sixteen factors
across domains. The Bhāṣya gives three ranges and then explicitly closes the
discussion of kleśas and upakleśas.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
māyā | śāṭhyam | ca | kāma-ādya-dhyānayoḥ | brahma-vañcanāt |
styāna-auddhatya-madāḥ | dhātu-traye | anye | kāma-dhātu-jāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `māyā` | nominative singular feminine | deceit |
| `śāṭhyam` | nominative singular neuter | crookedness or duplicity |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `kāma-ādya-dhyānayoḥ` | locative dual compound | in the desire-domain and first dhyāna |
| `brahma-vañcanāt` | ablative singular neuter compound | because of the deception of Brahmā |
| `styāna-auddhatya-madāḥ` | nominative plural masculine coordinative compound | torpor, excitation, and intoxication |
| `dhātu-traye` | locative singular neuter | in the three domains |
| `anye` | nominative plural masculine | the others |
| `kāma-dhātu-jāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | arising or occurring in the desire-domain |

The locative dual does not mean the desire-domain and all meditative states. It
specifically joins `kāma` with `ādya-dhyāna`, the first dhyāna.

## 4. Grammar

**Two-domain range**

```text
māyā śāṭhyam ca
    kāma-ādya-dhyānayoḥ [bhavataḥ]
        brahma-vañcanāt
```

The two differently gendered subjects share an elided dual verb. The ablative
states the evidential reason for admitting deceit in the first-dhyāna Brahmā
world: Mahābrahmā attempted to deceive Aśvajit by falsely presenting himself.

**Three-domain range**

```text
styāna-auddhatya-madāḥ
    dhātu-traye [bhavanti]
```

The plural compound takes a plural elided copula. The Bhāṣya restates the three
as `traidhātuka`, belonging to all three domains.

**Desire-domain complement**

```text
anye
    kāma-dhātu-jāḥ
```

`Anye` denotes the complement of the five wider-ranging factors within the
sixteen-member upakleśa field. The Bhāṣya calculates the result as eleven.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Deceit and crookedness occur in the desire-domain and the first dhyāna,
> because of the deception of Brahmā. Torpor, excitation, and intoxication
> occur in the three domains; the others arise in the desire-domain.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Deceit and crookedness occur in both the desire-domain and the first dhyāna.
> Deceit is admitted in the Brahmā world because Mahābrahmā attempted to deceive
> the venerable Aśvajit by falsely presenting himself. Torpor, excitation, and
> intoxication belong to all three domains. Of the sixteen upakleśas, removing
> these five wider-ranging factors leaves eleven that occur only in the
> desire-domain. With this distribution the analysis of kleśas and upakleśas
> is complete.

The narrative evidence, threefold range, sixteen-minus-five arithmetic, and
section closure are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Class-membership does not determine range of occurrence. Two upakleśas cross
> from the desire-domain into the first dhyāna; three range through the entire
> three-domain field; eleven remain restricted to the desire-domain. These
> ranges are additional determinations alongside origin, operation, ethical
> value, and mode of abandonment. The Brahmā case also grounds possibility by
> an attested doctrinal example: higher cosmological position does not by itself
> negate deceit. A transition of domain removes only those factors whose
> asserted range excludes the destination.

The Bhāṣya states the distribution but does not here supply a general causal
theory explaining why each of the three universal factors can occur throughout
all domains.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kutastya` — belonging where or to which domain**

The Bhāṣya's question that opens the range analysis: how many of these factors
are to be understood as belonging to which locations?

**`Māyā` — deceit**

Occurs in the desire-domain and first dhyāna. Its first-dhyāna occurrence is
supported by Mahābrahmā's attempted deception of Aśvajit.

**`Śāṭhya` — crookedness or duplicity**

Shares the same two-domain range. The Bhāṣya notes that it is stated again
because it has become relevant to the present topic (`prasaṅgāgata`).

**`Brahma-vañcana` — deception of Brahmā**

The kārikā's compressed reference to the Brahmā-world example. The Bhāṣya's
subject is Mahābrahmā as the one undertaking deception, not Brahmā as its
victim.

**`Vitathātma-saṃdarśanatā` — false self-presentation**

Presenting oneself as other than one actually is. This characterizes
Mahābrahmā's attempted deception in the cited narrative.

**`Styāna` — torpor**

One of three factors explicitly declared `traidhātuka`, capable of occurrence
in all three domains.

**`Auddhatya` — excitation or restlessness**

The second factor with three-domain range. The Bhāṣya asserts the range here
without a further local explanation of its compatibility with higher
meditative states.

**`Mada` — intoxication**

The third factor occurring across all three domains.

**`Traidhātuka` — belonging to all three domains**

Range over `kāmadhātu`, `rūpadhātu`, and `ārūpyadhātu`. It does not mean that
the factor has the same ethical value in each domain; VAK 5.52 determines that
axis separately.

**`Kāmāvacara` / `kāmadhātuja` — desire-domain occurrence**

The restricted range of the remaining eleven upakleśas.

**Exact range arithmetic**

```text
16 total upakleśas
− 5 with range beyond desire-domain
= 11 desire-domain-only upakleśas
```

The five removed are deceit, crookedness, torpor, excitation, and intoxication.

## 8. Logical Determination

The three domain-range assertions are:

```text
Range(Māyā)    = {DesireDomain, FirstDhyāna}
Range(Śāṭhya)  = {DesireDomain, FirstDhyāna}

For x in {Styāna, Auddhatya, Mada}:
    Range(x) = {DesireDomain, FormDomain, FormlessDomain}

For x in RemainingElevenUpakleśas:
    Range(x) = {DesireDomain}
```

The complement is calculated rather than left vague:

```text
Universe = SixteenUpakleśas
WiderRangeSet = {Māyā, Śāṭhya, Styāna, Auddhatya, Mada}
DesireOnlySet = Universe − WiderRangeSet
Cardinality(DesireOnlySet) = 11
```

Range is independent of the previously determined axes:

```text
SameClass(x, y)
    ⇏ SameDomainRange(x, y)

OccursInHigherDomain(x)
    ⇏ Wholesome(x)

DomainTransition(d1, d2)
    ⇏ EliminationOfEveryUpakleśa
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya explicitly asks for the location of the
upakleśas, assigns three ranges, performs the arithmetic, and then marks the
analysis of kleśas and upakleśas as complete.

**The Brahmā example.** `Brahma-vañcanāt` is potentially misleading if rendered
only as “because Brahmā is deceived.” The prose says Mahābrahmā attempted to
deceive Aśvajit through false self-presentation. The episode demonstrates the
possibility of deceit in the first-dhyāna Brahmā world.

**No inferred universal mechanism.** The source states that torpor, excitation,
and intoxication occur in all three domains but does not here explain each
range through “reflexivity,” “appropriation of attainment,” or another general
theory. Such hypotheses must not displace the stated classification.

**The completed multidimensional field.** VAK 5.46–5.53 has determined the
upakleśas by several independent relations:

```text
class
origin
operation
mode of abandonment
ethical value
domain-range
```

**Organon interpretation.** Domain-range belongs in its own provenance-bearing
assertion:

```text
RangeAssertion
    hasFactor
    includesDomain
    hasScopeQualification
    supportedBy TextualSource
```

A system should evaluate range before carrying or removing a determination
across a domain transition. This is an architectural reconstruction of the
Bhāṣya's matrix, not a new cosmological doctrine.
