# VAK_5.43

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
पञ्चधावरभागीयं द्वाभ्यां कामानतिक्रमः ।
त्रिभिस्तु पुनरावृत्तिर्मुखमूलग्रहात्त्रयम् ॥ ५.४३ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
pañcadhāvarabhāgīyaṃ dvābhyāṃ kāmānatikramaḥ |
tribhis tu punarāvṛttir mukha-mūla-grahāt trayam || 5.43 ||
```

VAK 5.43 presents the five lower-part fetters, divides them functionally into
two and three, and explains why scripture names a triad although six
afflictions are abandoned at stream-entry.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
pañcadhā | avara-bhāgīyam | dvābhyām | kāma-anatikramaḥ |
tribhiḥ | tu | punaḥ-āvṛttiḥ | mukha-mūla-grahāt | trayam
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `pañcadhā` | indeclinable adverb | fivefold, in five ways |
| `avara-bhāgīyam` | nominative singular neuter collective adjective | belonging or conducive to the lower part |
| `dvābhyām` | instrumental dual | through the two fetters |
| `kāma-anatikramaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | failure to pass beyond the desire-domain |
| `tribhiḥ tu` | instrumental plural with adversative particle | through the three, however |
| `punaḥ-āvṛttiḥ` | nominative singular feminine compound | return again |
| `mukha-mūla-grahāt` | ablative singular compound | because the entrances and roots are included by naming them |
| `trayam` | nominative singular neuter collective | the triad, the group of three |

The Bhāṣya fixes `avara-bhāga` primarily as the desire-domain. Its alternative
explanation also distinguishes a lower condition of being—ordinary-person
status—from the lower cosmological domain.

## 4. Grammar

**Five lower-part fetters**

```text
avara-bhāgīyam [saṃyojanam]
    pañcadhā [uktam]
```

The neuter singular is collective. The Bhāṣya supplies the five members:
personality-view, grasping at discipline and observance, doubt, desire for
sense-objects, and hostility.

**Functional division into two and three**

```text
dvābhyām
    kāma-anatikramaḥ

tribhiḥ tu
    punaḥ-āvṛttiḥ
```

The instrumentals state the causal means. Desire for sense-objects and
hostility prevent passage beyond the desire-domain. Through the three
beginning with personality-view, one returns even after having passed beyond.

**Reason for naming the triad**

```text
mukha-mūla-grahāt
    trayam [uktam]
```

The ablative states why only three of the six afflictions abandoned at
stream-entry are explicitly taught. `Graha` is here the scripture's inclusion
or selection of representatives and roots. It does not mean that the three
fetters themselves grasp an entrance and root as their objects.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The lower-part [fetter-group] is fivefold. Through two there is failure to
> pass beyond the desire-domain; through three, however, there is return again.
> The triad [is taught] because the entrances and roots are taken [by naming
> it].

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five lower-part fetters are personality-view, grasping at discipline and
> observance, doubt, desire for sense-objects, and hostility. Desire and
> hostility prevent transcendence of the desire-domain; the other three cause
> return even after it has been passed beyond. Although six afflictions are
> abandoned when the stream-enterer abandons these three fetters, scripture
> names only the triad because its members represent entrances to the broader
> afflictive groupings and are roots of the omitted views: personality-view
> sets extreme view in motion, discipline-and-observance grasping sets
> view-grasping in motion, and doubt sets false view in motion.

The five members, gatekeeper simile, alternative interpretation, sixfold
abandonment, and three root-derivative pairs are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Lower bondage has more than one operation. Desire and hostility retain the
> continuum within a domain; the cognitive triad can restore the lower
> condition even after a passage beyond it. Scripture's abbreviated list is
> also not merely incomplete enumeration. It selects determinations that stand
> at the entrances to wider afflictive groupings and that generate omitted
> derivatives. By taking the operative roots, the teaching includes what
> proceeds from them. Completeness can therefore be genetic: an exposition may
> name fewer items while preserving the relations through which the entire
> relevant field is produced and abandoned.

This rendering develops the Bhāṣya's explicit root-relations. The compressed
account of the numerical “entrances” remains provisional and is not expanded
beyond the source's claim.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Avarabhāgīya` — belonging or conducive to the lower part**

The Bhāṣya's primary explanation identifies the lower part with the desire-
domain and calls the five fetters conformable (`anuguṇa`) to it.

**The five lower-part fetters**

```text
satkāya-dṛṣṭi             personality-view
śīlavrata-parāmarśa       grasping at discipline and observance
vicikitsā                  doubt
kāma-cchanda               desire for sense-objects
vyāpāda                    hostility
```

The first three form the named triad; the last two directly prevent passage
beyond the desire-domain.

**`Kāmānatikrama` — failure to transcend the desire-domain**

The retentive function of desire for sense-objects and hostility. The Bhāṣya
states explicitly: through these two one does not pass beyond `kāmadhātu`.

**`Punarāvṛtti` — return again**

The function of the triad under the primary explanation. Even after passage
beyond the desire-domain, these fetters bring about return.

**`Dauvārika-anucara-sādharmya` — likeness to a gatekeeper and attendants**

The Bhāṣya's simile for the differentiated functions of the lower fetters. It
figures the architecture of blocked passage and return without making the
image itself an additional doctrinal entity.

**`Sattvāvaratā` — lower condition of being**

Identified by the alternative account with ordinary-person status
(`pṛthagjanatva`). On this reading, the three prevent transcendence of this
status.

**`Dhātvavaratā` — lower status of the domain**

Identified with the desire-domain. On the alternative account, the two prevent
transcendence of this cosmological level.

**`Mukha-graha` — inclusion of the entrance or leading representative**

The three selected fetters stand at the entrances to wider numerical
groupings of affliction. The Bhāṣya's enumerative background is compressed;
the secure determination is representative selection, not “entrance into the
Path.”

**`Mūla-graha` — inclusion through the root**

Naming the generating affliction includes its derivative in the rationale of
abandonment. The Bhāṣya gives three explicit pairs.

**`Pravartita` — set in motion**

The relation of each omitted view to its named root. This is a genetic
dependency, not mere membership in the same list.

## 8. Logical Determination

The primary fivefold division yields two operations:

```text
HasFetter(x, Kāmacchanda)
OR HasFetter(x, Vyāpāda)
    → DoesNotTranscend(x, DesireDomain)

HasAnyFetter(x, {Satkāyadṛṣṭi,
                 Śīlavrataparāmarśa,
                 Vicikitsā})
    → MayReturnTo(x, LowerCondition)
```

The alternative account indexes “lower” along two axes:

```text
Triad
    → PreventsTranscendenceOf(OrdinaryPersonStatus)

Dyad
    → PreventsTranscendenceOf(DesireDomain)
```

The three root-relations are exact:

```text
Satkāyadṛṣṭi
    → SetsInMotion(Antagrāhadṛṣṭi)

Śīlavrataparāmarśa
    → SetsInMotion(Dṛṣṭiparāmarśa)

Vicikitsā
    → SetsInMotion(Mithyādṛṣṭi)
```

Thus the abbreviated enumeration follows a genetic rule:

```text
Names(Root)
AND Records(DerivativeOf, Root)
    → IncludesByGenesis(Derivative)
```

This permits a distinction between two kinds of completeness:

```text
CompleteByExtension
    = every member named

CompleteByGenesis
    = sufficient roots and dependencies named
```

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Blessed One is reported elsewhere to have taught
five lower-part fetters. The Bhāṣya identifies the lower part as the desire-
domain, explains the dyad and triad, records another interpretation, and then
answers why only three of six abandoned afflictions are named.

**Retention and return.** The dyad and triad do not represent merely weaker
and stronger versions of one bondage:

```text
desire and hostility
    → retain within the desire-domain

personality-view, discipline-and-observance grasping, and doubt
    → produce return after passage beyond
```

The alternative account recasts the difference as domain-status versus path-
status. Both readings are retained because the Bhāṣya reports rather than
eliminates the alternative.

**Correction of `mukha-mūla-graha`.** The selected three do not “grasp the
entrance and root” as afflictive objects. The compound explains scriptural
selection. By naming the leading representatives and generative roots, the
teaching includes wider groups and their derivative views.

**Organon interpretation.** The passage requires distinct typed relations:

```text
bindsToDomain
preventsStatusTranscendence
causesReturn
representsGroupEntrance
setsDerivativeInMotion
isAbandonedWithRoot
```

A Dataset that retains only list-membership loses the reason why three names
can account for six abandonments. The root graph supplies a genetic mode of
completeness, while provenance preserves which relations are explicit in the
Bhāṣya and which formal structures belong to the Organon reconstruction.
