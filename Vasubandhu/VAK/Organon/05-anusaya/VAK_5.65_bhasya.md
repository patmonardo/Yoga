# VAK 5.65 Bhāṣya — Nine Parijñās Completed

## Kārikā Anchor

> anyā avarabhāgīyarūpasarvāsravakṣayāḥ |
> tisraḥ parijñāḥ ṣaṭ kṣāntiphalaṃ jñānasya śeṣitāḥ || 5.65 ||

> The other three *parijñās* concern abandonment of the
> lower-part bonds, of the form influx, and of all influxes.
> Six are fruits of *kṣānti*; the remaining three belong
> to *jñāna*.

The compact first line is interpreted through the Bhāṣya's
three explicit glosses. `Kṣānti` and `jñāna` are retained
as distinct technical terms; their relation is explained
after the verse rather than flattened into a general
contrast between patience and knowledge.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

The other three *parijñās* are the exhaustion of the
lower-part bonds, the form influx, and all influxes. The
abandonment of the lower-part bonds is one *parijñā*.
Abandonment of the form influx is another, called the
*parijñā* of the exhaustion of attachment to form. The
abandonment of all influxes belonging to the formless range
is one more, called the *parijñā* of the complete exhaustion
of all fetters.

Why are the abandonments of cultivation-abandonable factors
in the form and formless domains counted separately, while
the seeing-abandonable factors are not separated in that
way? Because the cultivation-abandonable factors have
different counteragents. These, then, are the nine
*parijñās*.

The earlier six, whose nature is the abandonment of what is
abandoned through seeing, are fruits of *kṣānti*. The
remaining ones, beginning with abandonment of the
lower-part bonds, belong to *jñāna* because they are fruits
of the path of cultivation. How can *parijñās* be fruits of
the *kṣāntis*? Because the *kṣāntis* belong to the retinue
of the knowledges, as a designation concerning a king can
be extended to his retinue, and because they have one fruit
together with knowledge.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The first six *parijñās* in 5.64 are completed
**seeing-abandonments**. This unit adds three completed
**cultivation-abandonments**:

| Number | Abandonment named in the prose | Resulting designation |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | `avarabhāgīyaprahāṇa` | lower-part abandonment |
| 8 | `rūpāsravaprahāṇa` | `rūparāgakṣayaparijñā` |
| 9 | `ārūpyāptānāṃ sarvāsravaprahāṇa` | `sarvasaṃyojanaparyādāna parijñā` |

The eighth is specifically abandonment of the **form
influx** in the prose, not a generic disappearance of form.
The ninth is named as the exhaustion of **all fetters**;
its condensed wording must be read with the prior
completions rather than as a claim that only formless
factors ever constituted the entire field of bondage.

The text itself asks why form and formless cultivation
abandonments count separately while their seeing-side
abandonments were counted together. Its answer is
`atulya-pratipakṣatvāt`: their counteragents are not the
same. The **mode and counteragent of abandonment** thus
affect the count. The passage does not derive a universal
arithmetic rule for every possible *parijñā* from this
single reason.

The second half reclassifies the nine by their path basis.
Six have the nature of seeing-abandonment and are called
`kṣāntiphala`; three are fruits of the cultivation path and
belong to `jñāna`. The prose immediately tests the first
designation. It does not say *kṣānti* and *jñāna* are
identical: *kṣānti* belongs to the retinue of *jñāna* and
shares a fruit with it. The royal analogy explains a
transfer of designation within a structured relation, not
a collapse of the two technical terms.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Avarabhāgīya` is kept as
“lower-part” because the verse alone does not enumerate the
bonds. `Rūpa` is expanded through `rūpāsrava` and
`rūparāga` in the Bhāṣya. `Kṣānti` is left untranslated in
the anchor to protect its precise path function; “patience”
alone would be too broad here. `Jñānasya śeṣitāḥ` gives the
remaining three to knowledge, with the prose grounding
them in `bhāvanāmārga`.

**Organon reconstruction.** The ninefold count joins
achieved abandonment to its counteragent and path function.
In our Anuśaya:Mārga reading, a completion is determined by
what bond is cut and by the operative path relation that
cuts it. This is a project-level articulation of the
Bhāṣya's stated distinctions; the source's kṣānti–jñāna
relation remains more precise than an abstract sequence of
“reception, then knowledge.”
