# VAK 5.19 Bhāṣya — Affliction and Ethical Classification

## Kārikā Anchor

> ūrdhvam avyākṛtāḥ sarve kāme satkāyadarśanam |
> antagrāhaḥ sahābhyāṃ ca mohaḥ śeṣās tv ihāśubhāḥ || 5.19 ||

> Above, all are ethically indeterminate. In the desire domain,
> self-view, extreme-view, and the delusion associated with those two
> are indeterminate; the remaining anuśayas here are unwholesome.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Of the ninety-eight anuśayas, how many are unwholesome and how
many ethically indeterminate? “Above, all are indeterminate.”
All those belonging to the form and formless domains are
indeterminate. Why? If afflicted dharmas there were unwholesome,
they would have painful maturation. But that is absent in those
two domains because the cause consisting in harm to others is
absent.

“In the desire domain, self-view, extreme-view, and the delusion
associated with those two” are indeterminate. In the desire
domain, self-view and extreme-view, together with the ignorance
associated with them, are indeterminate. Why? They are not
incompatible with giving and similar acts. Thinking “After death
I shall be happy,” someone gives and keeps moral discipline.
Even annihilationist view may be favorable to liberation. Hence
the Blessed One said: “The foremost of outsiders' views is this:
May I not be, may there be nothing of mine; I shall not be, and
there will be nothing of mine.” Moreover, these two views involve
confusion about their own basis and do not tend toward harming
others [reading the compound in context].

But then the same consequence would follow for craving for heaven
and for the conceit “I am.” Innate self-view, which occurs even
in deer and birds, is indeterminate. Conceptually constructed
self-view, however, is unwholesome, according to the earlier
teachers. “The remaining anuśayas here are unwholesome”: the
remaining desire-domain anuśayas are unwholesome.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The verse classifies **ethical status**, not the presence or
absence of affliction. An anuśaya can be `kliṣṭa` and yet
`avyākṛta`. For the higher domains the Bhāṣya argues through
painful karmic maturation and the absence of a cause involving
harm to others. Within the desire domain it selects two views
and precisely the ignorance conjoined with them.

The argument from giving and discipline establishes compatibility,
not the truth of self-view or extreme-view. The quotation about
nonbeing belongs to the Bhāṣya's limited defense of the latter's
possible relation to release; it does not turn that view into
the Kośa's account of liberation.

The sentence about heavenly craving and `asmimāna` presses an
objection against the proposed criterion: they too might qualify
if compatibility or lack of injury were sufficient. The reply
distinguishes innate from constructed self-view and explicitly
attributes the latter's unwholesome status to the earlier teachers.
The passage does not spell out a full independent answer for both
cases raised in the objection, so that argumentative gap remains
visible.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Avyākṛta` names an ethical
classification; it does not mean unafflicted. `Sahābhyāṃ` and
the prose `tatsaṃprayuktā` restrict the relevant ignorance to
what is associated with the two views. `Aparapīḍāpravṛttatva`
is provisionally translated from context as lack of a tendency
to injure others. Its compound structure is not silently
rewritten as `aparapīḍā-apravṛtti`. `Sahajā` and `vikalpitā`
mark innate and conceptually constructed forms of self-view.

**Organon reconstruction.** The distinction between affliction
and ethical status matters for the Science of Citta: a cognitive
distortion, its associated factors, and the practical action it
supports must be determined separately. This is a project-level
use of the Kośa's distinctions, not a claim that Vasubandhu
formulated the Organon's architecture.
