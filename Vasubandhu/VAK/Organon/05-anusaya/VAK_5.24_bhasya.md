# VAK 5.24 Bhāṣya — Future Afflictions and the Range of Bondage

## Kārikā Anchor

> sarvatrānāgatairebhir mānasaiḥ svādhvike paraiḥ |
> ajaiḥ sarvatra śeṣais tu sarvaiḥ sarvatra saṃyutaḥ || 5.24 ||

> By these future afflictions when mental, one is bound with
> respect to objects in all three times; by the other future
> afflictions, to objects in their own time. By those that will
> not arise, one is bound everywhere; by all the remaining
> afflictions, everywhere.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

“By these future mental afflictions, everywhere.” The earlier
condition “where they have not been abandoned” continues.
Through future attachment, aversion, and conceit belonging
to the mental level, one is connected with every object in
the three times, because mental afflictions can take objects
from all three times.

“By the others, to what belongs to their own time.” Through
other future attachment and aversion, one is connected only
with future objects. Which others? Attachment and aversion
belonging to the five groups of sense consciousness, in the
case of those destined to arise.

“But by those that will not arise, everywhere.” Even through
those same sensory afflictions whose nature is not to arise,
one is connected with objects everywhere, including the
three times.

“But by all the remaining afflictions, everywhere.” The
remaining ones are view, doubt, and ignorance in their
past, present, and future forms. Through all of these one
is connected with every object, because they are
general-character afflictions. The governing condition
“so long as they have not been abandoned” still applies.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

This verse completes the object-range account begun in 5.23.
Its distinctions depend on **temporal position**, **cognitive
locus**, and **whether a future affliction will arise**. Future
attachment, aversion, and conceit in the mental domain can
range over past, present, and future objects. The Bhāṣya gives
the reason explicitly: mental afflictions have objects in
all three times (`tryadhvaviṣayatva`).

The “others” (`paraiḥ`) are future attachment and aversion
associated with the five sense consciousnesses. For those
that will arise (`utpattidharma`), bondage is limited to a
future object, their own temporal field. The compact `ajaiḥ`
is then explained as `anutpattidharma`: future sensory
afflictions that will not arise. The Bhāṣya assigns these a
three-time object range. Thus non-arising does not by itself
imply absence of a bond.

The final `śeṣāḥ` are view, doubt, and ignorance across the
three times. Their `sāmānyakleśa` character grounds the
general range. Crucially, the prose repeats that all of
these rules remain under the condition `yāvad aprahīṇāḥ`.
The table of possible bonds must not be read as a claim
that abandoned afflictions continue to bind.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Mānasaiḥ` is restricted by the
prose to the three afflictions inherited from 5.23;
`paraiḥ` identifies the sensory attachment and aversion,
not a new unnamed set. `Svādhvike` means an object belonging
to the same time as those future sensory afflictions, hence
a future object here. `Ajaiḥ` is glossed by the Bhāṣya as
factors whose nature is not to arise; it does not simply
mean factors that have not arisen yet. `Sarvatra` concerns
the range of relevant `vastu`, qualified throughout by
non-abandonment.

**Organon reconstruction.** The passage requires a model to
keep possible range, actual arising, and effective bondage
distinct. An affliction's failure to arise does not by
itself decide whether it binds; the text's decisive practical
condition is abandonment. This is a conceptual use of the
Kośa for the Science of Citta, separate from any implemented
architecture.
