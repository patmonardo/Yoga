# VAK_5.48

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
क्रोधम्रक्षौ च रागोत्था आह्रीक्यौद्धत्यमत्सराः ।
म्रक्षे विवादः अविद्यातः स्त्यानमिद्धानपत्रपाः ॥ ५.४८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
krodha-mrakṣau ca rāgotthā āhrīkya-auddhatya-matsarāḥ |
mrakṣe vivādaḥ avidyātaḥ styāna-middha-anapatrapāḥ || 5.48 ||
```

VAK 5.48 continues the tenfold Vaibhāṣika `paryavasthāna` field of VAK 5.47
and begins assigning particular upakleśas to their afflictive sources. The
Bhāṣya names the relation `niḥṣyanda`, derivative or outflow, and preserves a
three-way dispute concerning concealment.

**Textual caution.** The working Bhāṣya e-text contains forms such as
`āhlīkya`, `bhrakṣa`, and `niḥṣyanada` for expected `āhrīkya`, `mrakṣa`, and
`niḥṣyanda`. They remain witness-level variants and are not silently inserted
into the kārikā.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
krodha-mrakṣau | ca | rāga-utthāḥ | āhrīkya-auddhatya-matsarāḥ |
mrakṣe | vivādaḥ | avidyātaḥ | styāna-middha-anapatrapāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `krodha-mrakṣau` | nominative dual masculine | anger and concealment |
| `ca` | conjunction | and, adding them to the prior eight |
| `rāga-utthāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | arisen from attachment |
| `āhrīkya-auddhatya-matsarāḥ` | nominative plural masculine coordinative compound | shamelessness, excitation, and avarice |
| `mrakṣe` | locative singular masculine | concerning concealment |
| `vivādaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | disagreement or dispute |
| `avidyātaḥ` | ablative singular feminine | from ignorance |
| `styāna-middha-anapatrapāḥ` | nominative plural masculine coordinative compound | torpor, sleepiness, and lack of moral concern |

The ablatives in `rāgotthāḥ` and `avidyātaḥ` mark source-relations. The
Bhāṣya determines these more precisely as `rāga-niḥṣyanda` and
`avidyā-niḥṣyanda`.

## 4. Grammar

**Completion of the tenfold field**

```text
krodha-mrakṣau ca
    [paryavasthāne]
```

The dual adds anger and concealment to the eight of VAK 5.47. It does not yet
state that both arise from the same source.

**Attachment-derived triad**

```text
āhrīkya-auddhatya-matsarāḥ
    rāga-utthāḥ
```

The plural predicate agrees with the three-member compound. The Bhāṣya glosses
the relation: `ete traya upakleśā rāga-niḥṣyandāḥ`, these three upakleśas are
derivatives of attachment.

**Disputed and ignorance-derived cases**

```text
mrakṣe
    vivādaḥ

styāna-middha-anapatrapāḥ
    avidyātaḥ
```

The locative defines the topic of disagreement. The final ablative governs the
three nouns that precede it syntactically in the reconstructed order. The
Bhāṣya again supplies `niḥṣyanda` as the exact relation.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Anger and concealment are also [included]. Shamelessness, excitation, and
> avarice arise from attachment. Concerning concealment there is disagreement.
> Torpor, sleepiness, and lack of moral concern arise from ignorance.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Anger and concealment complete the Vaibhāṣika list of ten envelopments.
> Shamelessness, excitation, and avarice are derivatives or outflows of
> attachment. Concealment of a fault has a disputed source: some call it a
> derivative of craving, others a derivative of ignorance, and still others a
> derivative of both, with the compressed explanation referring respectively
> to known and unknown faults. Torpor, sleepiness, and lack of moral concern
> are derivatives of ignorance.

The technical relation `niḥṣyanda`, three reported positions, and known/unknown
qualification are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Classification of a manifest affliction does not yet complete its
> explanation. Six envelopments receive determinate genetic affiliations:
> attachment flows out into shamelessness, excitation, and avarice; ignorance
> flows out into torpor, sleepiness, and lack of moral concern. Concealment,
> however, is phenomenally defined while its ground remains contested. Its
> occurrence is one determination; whether it issues from craving, ignorance,
> or both is another. Analysis therefore preserves settled derivation and
> disputed derivation as different epistemic states rather than completing the
> taxonomy by artificial certainty.

The particular source-relations are warranted here; they must not be expanded
into a universal claim that every upakleśa is by definition a derivative of
exactly one kleśa.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Krodha` — anger**

Defined in the adjacent Bhāṣya prose as hostile striking or resentment toward
sentient or non-sentient objects, excluding the more specific factors
`vyāpāda` and `vihiṃsā`. Its own source-affiliation is treated in VAK 5.49.

**`Mrakṣa` — concealment**

Defined as concealment of a fault (`avadya-pracchādana`). The factor is
identified securely even though its genetic affiliation remains disputed.

**`Niḥṣyanda` — outflow, derivative, or consequence**

The Bhāṣya's controlling term for the relation between a source-affliction and
the specified upakleśa. This explicit relation licenses genetic language here
that VAK 5.46 did not establish as the general definition of `upakleśa`.

**`Rāga-niḥṣyanda` — derivative of attachment**

The shared affiliation of shamelessness, excitation, and avarice.

**`Avidyā-niḥṣyanda` — derivative of ignorance**

The shared affiliation of torpor, sleepiness, and lack of moral concern.

**`Āhrīkya` — shamelessness**

Here classified as an outflow of attachment. The verse does not itself supply
a further psychological mechanism linking the two.

**`Auddhatya` — excitation or restlessness**

An attachment-derived upakleśa, previously defined as non-pacification of
mind.

**`Mātsarya` — avarice**

An attachment-derived gripping of mind opposed to giving Dharma, goods, or
skill.

**`Styāna` — torpor**

One of the three upakleśas explicitly established as an outflow of ignorance.

**`Middha` — sleepiness**

Likewise an ignorance-derived factor; only its afflicted instance enters the
paryavasthāna classification.

**`Anapatrāpya` — lack of moral concern**

The third ignorance-derived member of the triad.

**`Vivāda` — disagreement**

An explicit marker that the source-affiliation of concealment is not settled
by the tradition reported here.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The verse works within the tenfold Vaibhāṣika field.
It assigns six factors to two sources and marks concealment as disputed. The
Bhāṣya expressly calls the two triads `niḥṣyandas`, turning general
classification into a stated genetic relation.

**What VAK 5.46 did not say.** VAK 5.46 used a separative ablative and defined
the broader upakleśa field through mind-defilement. VAK 5.48 now introduces
source-derivation for specified members:

```text
broad functional membership
    ≠
particular genetic affiliation
```

The latter must be asserted factor by factor where textual evidence supplies
it.

**Disagreement without indeterminacy of the phenomenon.** All reported parties
can recognize concealment as covering a fault while differing over its source.
The disagreement therefore concerns explanatory ground, not whether the
afflictive factor exists or how it is locally defined.

**Path contact.** The passage warrants tracing a named affliction to its source,
but it does not yet prescribe a complete intervention theory. A disciplined
study distinguishes recognition, affiliation, disputed explanation, and later
abandonment rather than claiming that identifying one parent mechanically
solves the affliction.

**Organon interpretation.** Genetic assertions require provenance and status:

```text
GroundAssertion
    hasDerivative
    hasProposedGround
    hasRelationType Niḥṣyanda
    hasStatus {Established, ReportedAlternative, Disputed}
    hasSource
```

This permits convergent or competing grounds without converting a living
doctrinal dispute into a forced tree. It is an Organon representation of the
Bhāṣya's explicit voice structure.
