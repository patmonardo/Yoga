# VAK 5.08 Bhāṣya — Causal Fixation and Its Limit

## Kārikā Anchor

> kāraṇābhiniveśo 'to duḥkhadṛggheya eva saḥ || 5.08 ||

> Fixation upon a cause such as Īśvara proceeds from inversion
> concerning permanence and self. Therefore it is to be abandoned
> precisely through seeing suffering.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

A view of a cause in connection with an actual causal matter can be
adherence to discipline and observance. Why is it not abandoned
through seeing origination? Whoever sees Īśvara, Prajāpati, or
another as a cause has first fixed upon it as permanent, one, a
self, and an agent. Hence the verse says that causal fixation
concerning Īśvara and similar cases proceeds from inversion about
permanence and self, and is therefore abandoned through seeing
suffering. Seeing suffering abandons those two graspings; the
causal fixation produced by them is abandoned there too.

What, then, of someone who regards entering water or fire as a
cause of heavenly rebirth, or discipline and observances as a
means of purification? This too is abandoned through seeing
suffering. The cited scriptural formula describes someone who
undertakes bovine, deer, or canine observances and believes that
thereby they are purified, freed, and pass beyond pleasure and
pain. Such a person apprehends a non-cause as a cause; the text
calls this adherence to discipline and observance to be abandoned
through seeing suffering.

Why is it so abandoned? Because the person is mistaken about
suffering; those whose cognitive supports are with outflows are
mistaken about suffering.

But what, then, is the other kind of adherence to discipline and
observance, abandoned through seeing the path? One answer says:
that which has as support something abandoned through seeing the
path. Yet that person too is mistaken about suffering. And if
someone has a false view or doubt concerning the path, how could
they expect purification through that very denial or doubt? If
they instead cling to another path to liberation while denying
this one, they expect purification through the other path, not
through the false view. The proposed path-related support thus
fails to establish the classification. Likewise, if someone
expects purification through a false view whose support belongs
to what is abandoned through seeing origination or cessation,
why is that view not abandoned through seeing the respective
truth? **Therefore this matter must be examined.**

## Argument and Voices

The initial genetic explanation is clear within its scope:
fixation on a creator-cause follows grasping that supposed cause
as permanent self and agent. Since seeing suffering removes the
underlying permanence and self graspings, the derivative fixation
is assigned to the same seeing. The ritual examples extend the
problem to false means of rebirth or purification.

The source then presses the inherited classification. If any
outflow-bearing support involves a mistake about suffering, what
distinguishes the path-abandoned subclass? If a false view denies
the path, it is not the false view through which its holder expects
purification. A proposed relation to another path, or to the
truths of origination and cessation, reopens the assignment
problem. These are internal dialectical questions; no named school
speaker is attached to them here.

`Parīkṣya eṣo 'rthaḥ` must retain its force. The commentary gives
a reason for the creator-cause case but does not claim to have
settled every case of `śīlavrataparāmarśa` by one effortless rule.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Kāraṇābhiniveśa` is “causal fixation,”
not neutral inquiry into causes. `Viparyāsa` is inversion, and
`vipratipanna` marks being mistaken with respect to a truth;
the two are not flattened into one English “error.” The
scriptural formula is reported as a quotation within Vasubandhu's
argument. His examples concerning Īśvara and particular practices
remain source claims rather than conclusions of our project.

**Organon reconstruction.** A surface judgment about a cause can
depend on a prior determination of permanent self and agency.
That is a useful genetic diagnostic principle. Yet the explicit
reservation prevents a neat universal mapping from each error to
exactly one corrective truth. Any formalization of the path
matrix must preserve that unresolved source pressure.
