# VAK_5.06

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
भवाग्रजाः क्षान्तिवध्या दृग्घेया एव शेषजाः ।
दृग्भावनाभ्यामक्षान्तिवध्या भावनयैव तु ॥ ५.६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
bhavāgrajāḥ kṣāntivadhyā dṛggheyā eva śeṣajāḥ |
dṛgbhāvanābhyām akṣāntivadhyā bhāvanayaiva tu || 5.6 ||
```

The compressed verse qualifies the seeing/cultivation division developed in
VAK 5.04–5.05. The Bhāṣya denies that every anuśaya classified as
`darśanaprahātavya` must, in every case, be abandoned only through seeing.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
bhava-agra-jāḥ | kṣānti-vadhyāḥ | dṛk-heyāḥ | eva | śeṣa-jāḥ |
dṛk-bhāvanābhyām | a-kṣānti-vadhyāḥ | bhāvanayā | eva | tu
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `bhava-agra-jāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | born on the summit-level of existence |
| `kṣānti-vadhyāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | counteracted or destroyable by kṣānti |
| `dṛk-heyāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | to be abandoned through seeing |
| `eva` | restrictive particle | only, precisely |
| `śeṣa-jāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | born on the remaining levels |
| `dṛk-bhāvanābhyām` | instrumental dual | through seeing and cultivation |
| `a-kṣānti-vadhyāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | not counteracted by kṣānti |
| `bhāvanayā` | instrumental singular | through cultivation |
| `tu` | contrastive particle | but, however |

The Bhāṣya interprets `akṣāntivadhya` positively as `jñānavadhya`, counteracted
by knowledge. `Kṣānti` is therefore retained as a technical Path term and not
translated as ordinary patience.

## 4. Grammar

**Summit-level restriction**

```text
bhavāgra-jāḥ kṣānti-vadhyāḥ
    dṛk-heyāḥ eva

the kṣānti-counteracted anuśayas born at bhavāgra
    are abandoned only through seeing
```

The Bhāṣya specifies the counteragent here as `anvayajñānakṣānti`.

**Continuation of the governing predicate**

```text
śeṣa-jāḥ [kṣānti-vadhyāḥ]
    dṛk-bhāvanābhyām [heyāḥ]

those born on the remaining levels and counteracted by kṣānti
    are abandoned through seeing and cultivation
```

The commentary explicitly carries `kṣāntivadhya` forward. The instrumental
dual does not mean that two operations must jointly remove every such
anuśaya. Its distribution depends upon the practitioner: seeing for an
`ārya`, cultivation for a `pṛthagjana`.

**Contrasting final class**

```text
akṣānti-vadhyāḥ tu
    bhāvanayā eva [heyāḥ]

but those not counteracted by kṣānti
    are abandoned only through cultivation
```

The Bhāṣya applies this rule on every level and to both kinds of practitioner.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Those born at the summit of existence and counteracted by kṣānti are to be
> abandoned only through seeing. Those born on the remaining levels [and
> counteracted by kṣānti] are abandoned through seeing and cultivation; but
> those not counteracted by kṣānti are abandoned only through cultivation.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The kṣānti-counteracted anuśayas born at the summit of existence are
> abandoned only through seeing. On the remaining levels, anuśayas
> counteracted as appropriate by dharma-knowledge-kṣānti or
> anvaya-knowledge-kṣānti are abandoned through seeing by noble practitioners,
> but through cultivation by ordinary persons. Anuśayas counteracted by
> knowledge rather than kṣānti are always abandoned through cultivation for
> both.

The named forms of kṣānti, the practitioner-dependent distribution of
`dṛgbhāvanābhyām`, and the equivalence of the final class with
`jñānavadhya` are Bhāṣya determinations.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An affliction does not carry its method of removal as an isolated intrinsic
> label. The valid counteraction depends upon where it operates, what kind of
> Path-capacity opposes it, and the attained condition of the practitioner.
> At the summit of conditioned existence, the relevant kṣānti-counteracted
> anuśayas yield only to seeing. On lower levels, the same counteragent-class
> is seeing-abandoned for one who has entered noble seeing but
> cultivation-abandoned for an ordinary practitioner. Knowledge-counteracted
> anuśayas require cultivation for both. The Path is thus adequate negation:
> its operation must be indexed to the determination and to the agent capable
> of realizing its removal.

The verse changes a static classification into a contextual rule:

```text
same broad affliction-class
    + different level or practitioner-state
    → different valid mode of abandonment
```

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Bhavāgra` — summit of existence**

The highest level of conditioned existence. Its refinement does not place it
beyond `bhava`; it remains a level at which anuśayas require determinate
counteraction.

**`Bhūmi` — level or ground**

The Bhāṣya expands `bhavāgraja` as born on the `bhavāgra-bhūmi` and contrasts
it with the remaining levels. Here level is an operative coordinate in the
abandonment rule.

**`Kṣānti` — technical Path receptivity**

It occurs here within `dharmajñānakṣānti` and `anvayajñānakṣānti`. Ordinary
“patience” is inadequate. The term names a determinate Path-capacity related
to truth-seeing; its full technical structure belongs to the later Path
analysis.

**`Kṣāntivadhya` and `jñānavadhya`**

`Vadhya` classifies an anuśaya by the counteragent capable of striking it
down. The Bhāṣya contrasts what is counteracted by kṣānti with what is
counteracted by knowledge.

**`Heya` — to be abandoned**

Where `vadhya` indexes the counteragent, `heya` indexes the corresponding mode
of removal: through seeing or cultivation. Their distinction prevents the
counteragent and the achieved abandonment from being collapsed.

**`Ārya` — noble practitioner**

For an ārya, the relevant kṣānti-counteracted anuśayas of the remaining levels
are abandoned through seeing.

**`Pṛthagjana` — ordinary person**

For a pṛthagjana, corresponding lower-level anuśayas may be abandoned through
cultivation rather than noble seeing.

**`Bāhyaka` — outsider**

The appended controversy concerns whether non-Buddhist practitioners can
abandon what is formally called seeing-abandonable. This is a polemical
classification, distinct from the neutral sense of `pṛthagjana`.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya resolves the verse as a conditional matrix:

| Level | Counteragent class | Practitioner | Abandonment mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| summit of existence | kṣānti | relevant practitioner | seeing only |
| remaining levels | kṣānti | ārya | seeing |
| remaining levels | kṣānti | pṛthagjana | cultivation |
| every level | knowledge, not kṣānti | both | cultivation only |

Formally:

```text
AbandonmentMode
    = f(
        Anusaya,
        Level,
        CounteragentClass,
        PractitionerStatus
      )
```

This refines the `ālambana` rule of VAK 5.05. The complete determination now
requires both sides:

```text
objective determination:
    cognitive support and level of the affliction

subjective determination:
    achieved status and counteractive capacity of the practitioner

middle:
    the valid operation of abandonment
```

The same named affliction need not receive the same removal-mode when the
knowing and acting capacity of the practitioner differs.

**Appended doctrinal dispute**

Other teachers argue that outsiders do not abandon what is properly
seeing-abandonable. They cite the later occurrence of desire-domain-supported
speculative views among persons free from sensual attachment. The Vaibhāṣikas
reply that earlier afflictions had fallen away at the very moment those later
views arose. The local passage does not fully explain the Devadatta comparison,
so no stronger reconstruction is imposed.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.06 brings the Subject into the Path matrix without turning it into an
abstract subject-pole. Practitioner-status is not an external metadata field
attached to an otherwise finished correction rule. It determines which
counteraction can actually be realized as abandonment.

```text
affliction
    does not by itself determine its correction

affliction-in-a-level
    related to a counteragent
    for a determinately formed practitioner
    → valid abandonment operation
```

This is an important manifestation of the Middle we have begun to identify.
The objective display includes the level and cognitive support of the
anuśaya; the Subject inheres as a specifically developed capacity to see or
cultivate. The Path-operation is the unity in which those determinations meet
and the affliction can actually be overcome.

The Absolute Concept must not be inserted as an unexplained additional term
inside Vasubandhu's classification. Its Organon light appears in the form of
the result: the correction is adequate only when the whole relation—level,
support, counteragent, and practitioner—determines itself as an executable
transformation.

Thus VAK 5.06 adds a decisive implementation principle:

```text
a correction is not valid merely because
it matches an abstract error-type

it must also be realizable
from the achieved state of the agent
```

This is Organon reconstruction. The textual basis is the Bhāṣya's explicit
distribution of abandonment by level, counteragent, and practitioner-status.
