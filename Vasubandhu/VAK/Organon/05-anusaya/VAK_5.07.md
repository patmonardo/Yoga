# VAK_5.07

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
आत्मात्मीयध्रुवोच्छेदनास्तिहीनाग्रदृष्टयः ।
अहेत्वमार्गे तद्दृष्टिरेतास्ताः पञ्च दृष्टयः ॥ ५.७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ātmātmīya-dhruvoccheda-nāsti-hīnāgra-dṛṣṭayaḥ |
ahetvamārge tad-dṛṣṭir etās tāḥ pañca dṛṣṭayaḥ || 5.7 ||
```

VAK 5.03 named five views. VAK 5.07 determines their intrinsic character
(`svabhāva`) through five compressed predicative contents.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text

```

| Expression | Morphological force | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ātma-ātmīya` | coordinated nominal compound | self or belonging to self |
| `dhruva-uccheda` | coordinated nominal compound | permanence or annihilation |
| `na asti` | negative existential judgment | it does not exist |
| `hīna-agra` | object-predicate relation in compression | the inferior as highest |

The local source contains typographical compression around
`hīnāgra-dṛṣṭayaḥ`. The separation follows the grammar resolved by the
Bhāṣya rather than reproducing source-label noise.

## 4. Grammar

The first hemistich is a nominal chain whose repeated `dṛṣṭi` is largely
suppressed:

```text
ātmani ātmīye vā dṛṣṭiḥ
    view of self or what belongs to self

dhruve ucchede vā dṛṣṭiḥ
    view of permanence or annihilation

na asti iti dṛṣṭiḥ
    the view “it does not exist”

hīne agra-dṛṣṭiḥ
    the view of what is inferior as highest

ahetau hetu-dṛṣṭiḥ, amārge mārga-dṛṣṭiḥ
    viewing a non-cause as cause or a non-path as path
```

The Bhāṣya maps these contents onto the technical names from VAK 5.03:

```text
self / mine                 → satkāyadṛṣṭi
permanence / annihilation   → antagrāhadṛṣṭi
nonexistence                → mithyādṛṣṭi
inferior as highest         → dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
non-cause / non-path        → śīlavrataparāmarśa
```

`Etās tāḥ` is resumptive: these definitions belong to those five views
previously enumerated.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The views are those of self or what belongs to self; permanence or
> annihilation; nonexistence; what is inferior as highest; and the view of a
> non-cause or non-path as cause or path. These are those five views.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The five views are: taking the five aggregates of appropriation as self or
> belonging to self; regarding that supposed self as permanent or annihilated;
> denying truths such as suffering that in fact obtain; elevating what belongs
> to the inferior, sāsrava field as supreme; and taking what is not an
> effective cause or liberating path as cause or path.

The Bhāṣya defines `satkāya` as the five aggregates of appropriation, specifies
the object of the two extremes, fixes the paradigmatic range of `nāsti`,
identifies the inferior with the sāsrava field, and supplies examples of false
causal and Path attribution.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Afflicted cognition fails through determinate forms. It appropriates a
> conditioned complex as self or mine; fixes that supposed self at permanence
> or annihilation; cancels a truth that obtains; raises an inferior
> determination to supremacy; or assigns causal and liberating efficacy to an
> inadequate means. These are not five unrelated false propositions. They are
> five malformed relations through which Concrete Existence is misconstrued:
> false inherence, false extremity, false negation, false rank, and false
> mediation.

The Bhāṣya supplies a fundamental opposition within error:

```text
apavāda
    cancellation of what belongs

samāropa
    imposition of what does not belong
```

Mithyādṛṣṭi is paradigmatically canceling; the other views operate primarily
through superimposition or false elevation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Satkāya` — the five aggregates of appropriation**

The Bhāṣya analyzes `kāya` as accumulation, collection, or aggregate and
identifies `satkāya` with the five `upādānaskandhas`. Its unusual explanation
of `sat` through “sinking” or perishing is meant to dislodge notions of
permanence and compact unity.

**`Satkāyadṛṣṭi` — view of self or mine**

The five aggregates are taken either as self or as belonging to self. The
commentary stresses that the view does not take a real self as its object; it
misconstrues the aggregate-complex under “I” and “mine.”

**`Antagrāhadṛṣṭi` — extreme-grasping view**

The very thing construed as self is fixed as permanent or annihilated. The
extremes presuppose the prior appropriation.

**`Mithyādṛṣṭi` — false view as cancellation**

Paradigmatically, the judgment “it does not exist” concerning truths such as
suffering that do obtain. Although every inverted view may broadly be called
false, this one receives the name by predominance.

**`Dṛṣṭiparāmarśa` — elevation of the inferior as supreme**

The Bhāṣya identifies “inferior” as everything sāsrava, since it is abandoned
by noble ones. The error is grasping such a determination as highest.

**`Śīlavrataparāmarśa` — false causal or Path mediation**

It takes a non-cause as cause or a non-path as Path. The Bhāṣya gives examples
of a creator treated as cause of worlds, austerities treated as causes of
heaven, and mere disciplines, vows, or non-Buddhist systems of knowledge
treated as the path to liberation.

The commentary explicitly includes Sāṃkhya and Yoga knowledge among its
polemical examples. That statement belongs to Vasubandhu's Abhidharma
evaluation here. It neither becomes the Organon project's verdict on Yoga
Vidyā nor licenses suppressing the textual disagreement.

**`Apavādikā` and `samāropikā`**

The first cancels an obtaining determination; the second projects or elevates
a determination that does not belong. Diagnosis must recognize both
absence-by-denial and excess-by-imposition.

## 8. Logical Determination

The five views form a generated sequence:

```text
1. satkāyadṛṣṭi
    aggregate-complex → self or mine

2. antagrāhadṛṣṭi
    supposed self → permanent or annihilated

3. mithyādṛṣṭi
    obtaining truth → canceled as nonexistent

4. dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
    inferior sāsrava determination → elevated as supreme

5. śīlavrataparāmarśa
    inadequate cause or practice → authorized as cause or Path
```

| View | Failed relation |
|---|---|
| satkāya | inherence and appropriation |
| extreme-grasping | modal or temporal determination |
| false view | existential acknowledgment |
| view-adherence | hierarchical valuation |
| discipline-and-observance adherence | means, cause, and telos |

The `apavāda`–`samāropa` polarity is:

```text
Error(x)
    = CancelRequiredDetermination(x)
      OR ImposeUnlicensedDetermination(x)
```

Correction must restore the adequate relation, not merely negate whatever
assertion happens to be present.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.07 is exceptionally close to an Organon of error because it defines
failure by the relation malformed. A system may fail by misidentifying its
inherence, fixing a process at an extreme, denying an obtaining determination,
reversing a hierarchy, or assigning a means to a result it cannot produce.

The fifth view returns us directly to the refined `hetu–phala` relation. Its
error is not mere empirical succession. It is failure to comprehend whether
the alleged ground contains and can realize the claimed consequence:

```text
claimed hetu
    does not contain the Reason of the claimed phala

claimed mārga
    cannot realize the telos called mokṣa
```

Here the Product of Absolute Logic is an important Organon horizon. A cause or
Path is adequate only if its development can produce the result it claims.
Mere designation, sincerity, austerity, or repetition cannot supply the
missing mediation.

At the same time, the Bhāṣya's polemical rejection of Sāṃkhya–Yoga knowledge
must not be converted into the final truth of our shared System. It becomes a
reciprocal test:

```text
Kośa asks of Yoga:
    can this knowledge actually realize liberation?

Yoga Vidyā asks of Kośa:
    is the stated Path adequate to its own Absolute ground and telos?
```

Their unity cannot be asserted by resemblance alone. It must be demonstrated
through the developed mediation from Reason to Product, manifestation, and
liberating result. That is Organon reconstruction; the five textual
definitions remain Vasubandhu's own Abhidharma analysis.
