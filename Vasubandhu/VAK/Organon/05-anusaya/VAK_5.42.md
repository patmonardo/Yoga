# VAK_5.42

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
एकान्ताकुशलं यस्मात् स्वतन्त्रं चोभयं यतः ।
ईर्ष्यामात्सर्यमेषूक्तं पृथक् संयोजनद्वयम् ॥ ५.४२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ekāntākuśalaṃ yasmāt svatantraṃ cobhayaṃ yataḥ |
īrṣyā-mātsaryam eṣūktaṃ pṛthak saṃyojana-dvayam || 5.42 ||
```

VAK 5.42 asks why envy and avarice, unlike the other paryavasthānas, are each
counted independently among the nine fetters introduced in VAK 5.41. It offers
two differentiating marks, which the Bhāṣya immediately tests against competing
enumerations.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

The working Bhāṣya e-text transmits `īṣryā` where standard Sanskrit has
`īrṣyā`. The anomalous prose spelling remains a witness-level issue and is not
silently substituted into the kārikā.

## 4. Grammar

**Two proposed grounds**

```text
yasmāt ubhayam ekānta-akuśalam
yataḥ ca [ubhayam] sva-tantram
```

The two causal relatives coordinate two predicates of the collective
`ubhayam`. The distributive force of the second predicate is clarified by the
conclusion: envy and avarice are counted separately, so each has independent
status.

**Classificatory conclusion**

```text
īrṣyā-mātsaryam
    eṣu
    pṛthak
    saṃyojana-dvayam uktam
```

The singular compound names the pair collectively, while `pṛthak` and
`saṃyojana-dvayam` require two separately counted fetters. `Eṣu` refers to the
fetter classification of VAK 5.41.

**Scope established by the Bhāṣya**

The question compares envy and avarice specifically with the other
`paryavasthānas`. The proposed reason is not left unqualified: the closing
`apare`, “others,” reports a criticism of its sufficiency under a ten-member
enumeration.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Because both are exclusively unwholesome and because each is independent,
> envy and avarice are taught separately among these as two fetters.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Envy and avarice are proposed as two separately counted fetters because both
> are invariably unwholesome and independently established. This answer
> uniquely distinguishes them if the comparison class contains eight
> paryavasthānas. If ten are accepted, however, anger and concealment possess
> both marks as well; others therefore object that the proposed reason does not
> adequately explain why envy and avarice alone receive separate fetter-status.

The restriction to paryavasthānas, the rival eightfold and tenfold counts, and
the objection are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> A class distinction is not justified merely because its stated predicates
> are true of the selected members. The predicates must distinguish those
> members within the complete relevant field. Exclusive unwholesomeness and
> independence isolate envy and avarice under an eight-member field; when the
> field expands to ten, the same conjunction also includes anger and
> concealment. The proposed ground then loses the power to generate the
> intended boundary. The reality of the four afflictions is not disputed; what
> fails is the sufficiency of a reason for granting two of them exclusive
> classificatory privilege.

The Bhāṣya thus preserves the kārikā's proposed rationale while also preserving
the counterexample that limits it.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ekāntākuśala` — exclusively unwholesome**

Unwholesome without qualification in the relevant classification. It is the
first proposed mark distinguishing envy and avarice from other
paryavasthānas.

**`Svatantra` — independent**

Independent for purposes of counting and classification. The Bhāṣya does not
here expand this into metaphysical self-subsistence, a detailed account of
intentional direction, or a theory of autonomous causal operation.

**`Īrṣyā` — envy**

One of the paryavasthānas and one of the nine separately named fetters. Its
precise psychological definition is not supplied in this Bhāṣya passage; the
present question concerns why it receives independent fetter-status.

**`Mātsarya` — avarice or stinginess**

The second paryavasthāna separately counted as a fetter. As with envy, the
local argument concerns classification rather than a complete phenomenology.

**`Paryavasthāna` — enveloping or dominating manifestation**

The comparison domain within which the proposed marks are tested. Competing
Abhidharma enumerations recognize either eight or ten such factors.

**`Krodha` — anger**

One of the two additional factors in the tenfold enumeration. Because it too
is exclusively unwholesome and independent, it functions as a counterexample
to the proposed exclusive distinction.

**`Mrakṣa` — concealment**

Covering or concealing one's fault. It supplies the second counterexample
under the tenfold enumeration.

**`Parihāra` — reply or resolution**

A proposed answer to an objection. The critics' statement `na bhavaty ayaṃ
parihāraḥ` judges that the two stated marks do not constitute an adequate
resolution.

**`Apare` — others**

The attribution of the closing criticism. The objection should not be
silently presented either as the kārikā itself or as an unqualified statement
of Vasubandhu's final position.

## 8. Logical Determination

The proposed classificatory rule is:

```text
For x in Paryavasthāna:

ExclusivelyUnwholesome(x)
AND Independent(x)
    → QualifiesForSeparateFetterStatus(x)
```

Its discriminatory success depends upon the comparison domain:

```text
Domain = EightParyavasthānas
Extension(Criteria)
    = {Īrṣyā, Mātsarya}

Domain = TenParyavasthānas
Extension(Criteria)
    = {Īrṣyā, Mātsarya, Krodha, Mrakṣa}
```

Therefore:

```text
TrueOf(IntendedMembers, Criteria)
    ⇏ SufficientToDistinguish(IntendedMembers, Domain)
```

The counterexample does not negate the predicates:

```text
FailureOfDiscriminatorySufficiency(Criteria)
    ≠ FalsityOfCriteriaFor(Īrṣyā, Mātsarya)
```

It shows instead that adequacy is relative to a stated universe of comparison.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** The Bhāṣya frames VAK 5.42 as an answer to a precise
question: why are two paryavasthānas, envy and avarice, counted separately as
fetters while the others are not? The answer proposes exclusive
unwholesomeness and independence as jointly distinguishing marks.

**The counterexample.** The proposed marks succeed only for a school or
enumeration recognizing eight paryavasthānas. For one recognizing ten, anger
and concealment share both marks. The reported critics therefore conclude:

```text
the predicates remain true
    but
the predicates no longer uniquely select the intended pair
```

This is not a decorative scholastic reservation. It tests the explanatory
reason against a differently constituted field and exposes its failure of
sufficiency.

**Correction of the first-pass tendency.** The local source does not ground
the separation in a developed opposition between envy's attitude toward
another's good and avarice's withholding of one's own good. Such an analysis
may be pursued elsewhere if textually supported, but it cannot replace the two
marks actually stated here or the Bhāṣya's immediate objection.

**Organon interpretation.** Every classification rationale must retain its
evaluation domain:

```text
ClassificationRationale
    hasCriteria
    hasComparisonDomain
    hasIntendedExtension
    hasCounterexampleStatus
```

The rule is not validated merely by fitting its positive examples. It must be
tested against every relevant member of the declared domain. This is an
Organon formalization of the Bhāṣya's actual dialectical test, not a doctrine
back-projected into the kārikā.
