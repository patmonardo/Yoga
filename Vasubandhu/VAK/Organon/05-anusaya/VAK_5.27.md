# VAK_5.27

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
किं विघ्नं तत्कथं नान्यदध्वयोगस्तथा सतः ।
अजातनष्टता केन गम्भीरा खलु धर्मता ॥ ५.२७ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
kiṃ vighnaṃ tat kathaṃ nānyad adhva-yogas tathā sataḥ |
ajāta-naṣṭatā kena gambhīrā khalu dharmatā || 5.27 ||
```

The verse concentrates the objections to the preferred Sarvāstivāda theory of
VAK 5.26. The Bhāṣya then develops a positive alternative based on genuine
arising, cessation, and transformation of the continuum.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
kim | vighnam | tat | katham | na | anyat | adhva-yogaḥ | tathā |
sataḥ | ajāta-naṣṭatā | kena | gambhīrā | khalu | dharmatā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `kim` | interrogative pronoun | what? |
| `vighnam` | nominative or accusative singular neuter | obstacle, impediment |
| `tat` | nominative or accusative singular neuter | that [function or dharma] |
| `katham` | interrogative adverb | how? |
| `na anyat` | negative plus nominative singular neuter | not another; not distinct from the dharma |
| `adhva-yogaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | assignment or connection to a temporal course |
| `tathā sataḥ` | genitive singular construction | of that which exists in that manner |
| `ajāta-naṣṭatā` | nominative singular feminine compound | the condition of being unborn and destroyed |
| `kena` | instrumental singular interrogative | by what? through what ground? |
| `gambhīrā` | nominative singular feminine | profound, difficult to penetrate |
| `khalu` | emphatic particle | indeed |
| `dharmatā` | nominative singular feminine | nature or law of dharmas |

The terse interrogatives summarize a longer exchange: what restricts function,
whether function differs from the dharma, how temporal assignment is grounded,
and how an equally existent dharma can be unborn or destroyed.

## 4. Grammar

**Questions concerning `kāritra`**

```text
kiṃ vighnam [asti]?
tat katham na anyat [dharmāt]?

what is the obstacle [to its constant operation]?
how is that [function] not other [than the dharma]?
```

The Bhāṣya supplies the omitted argumentative terms. The first question asks
why a dharma existing in all times does not always function. The second tests
the reply that `kāritra` is not distinct from the dharma.

**Temporal and modal questions**

```text
tathā sataḥ adhva-yogaḥ [katham]?
ajāta-naṣṭatā kena?

how is what exists thus assigned to a temporal course?
by what is it unborn or destroyed?
```

The genitive `tathā sataḥ` governs the two predicative problems. If the dharma
exists substantially like the present in all three times, both temporal
assignment and genuine arising or destruction require explanation.

**Closing declaration**

```text
gambhīrā khalu dharmatā

the nature of dharmas is indeed profound
```

The Bhāṣya places this after an extended dispute. It should not be treated as
Vasubandhu's simple surrender of the problem to mystery.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> What obstructs [its operation]? How is that not another [than the dharma]?
> How is what exists in that manner assigned to a temporal course? By what is
> it unborn and destroyed? The nature of dharmas is indeed profound.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> If a dharma exists substantially in all three times, what prevents it from
> functioning continuously? If operative activity differs from the dharma, its
> own temporality must be explained; if it does not differ, how can it determine
> the dharma as past, present, or future? And if future and past dharmas exist
> just as the present does, by what are they genuinely unborn or destroyed?
> Vasubandhu answers constructively: the past is what formerly occurred, the
> future is what will occur when its cause is present, and delayed fruit arises
> through a special transformation of the continuum rather than from a
> substantially persisting past cause.

The critique of substantial three-time existence, the contextual account of
past and future, and `saṃtāna-viśeṣa` are developed in the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Temporal efficacy does not require the numerically identical past event to
> remain present under another label. The event genuinely arises and ceases,
> but in occurring it transforms the continuum; that achieved transformation
> becomes the basis from which a later result can arise. Memory presents what
> was, anticipation presents what may become when its cause is present, and
> neither presentation requires its object to subsist like a present thing.
> What persists is genetic determination, not an eternal inventory of events.

The positive replacement is:

```text
substantially persistent past cause
    rejected

prior event
    → determinate transformation of continuum
        → later result
```

Identity is preserved as generated continuity, not as an unchanged substrate
carrying external temporal labels.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Vighna` — obstacle to operation**

If the dharma and all its conditions exist eternally, incompleteness of
conditions cannot explain why it functions only sometimes. The question tests
whether the state theory has a genuine principle of restricted actuality.

**`Kāritra`: different or non-different from dharma**

If distinct, function requires a further account of its own function and
temporality. If non-distinct, it cannot explain why the same dharma is sometimes
future, present, or past. This is the central failure of the proposed mediator.

**`Adhva-yoga` — temporal assignment**

Connection with one of the three temporal courses. Vasubandhu demands that the
assignment be generated, not merely attached to a dharma already held to exist
in the same way throughout time.

**`Ajāta` and `naṣṭa` — unborn and destroyed**

These predicates require genuine arising and cessation. Vasubandhu argues that
one must admit, in the appropriate sense, that what was not becomes and what
has become again ceases to be.

**`Dravyataḥ` — substantially**

What Vasubandhu denies of past and future. He does not deny their causal,
intentional, or linguistic determination; he denies that they exist as present-
like substances in their own right.

**`Bhūtapūrva` — what formerly was**

The constructive definition of the past. Its reality is the truth that it
occurred and transformed the continuum, not its continued substantial presence.

**`Sati hetau bhaviṣyati` — it will occur when its cause is present**

The constructive definition of the future. Futurity is a causally determined
mode, not an already subsisting future entity.

**Being and non-being as `ālambana`**

The Bhāṣya concludes that cognition can take both `bhāva` and `abhāva` as
object-support. Recollection apprehends “it was”; anticipation apprehends “it
will be.” Intentional presentation does not entail present substantial
existence.

**`Saṃtāna-viśeṣa` — special determination of the continuum**

The Sautrāntika alternative adopted in the argument. Prior karma produces a
specific transformation of the continuum, and the later fruit arises from
that transformed continuity rather than directly from a still-existing past
event.

**`Gambhīrā khalu dharmatā` — the dharma-nature is profound**

The Vaibhāṣika invokes profundity at the end of the dispute. Vasubandhu's reply
refuses the inference that profundity makes rational establishment unnecessary.

## 8. Logical Determination

The proposed state mediator fails under both alternatives:

```text
Kāritra ≠ Dharma
    → KāritraRequiresTemporalMediator
    → explanatory regress

Kāritra = Dharma
    → no difference capable of generating temporal state
```

Therefore:

```text
MediatorNamesDifference(x)
    ⇏ MediatorGeneratesDifference(x)
```

Vasubandhu's genetic account is:

```text
Occurs(PriorEvent, t1)
AND Transforms(PriorEvent, Continuum, Determination)
AND ConditionsPresentAt(t2)
    → Arises(LaterResult, t2)
```

without:

```text
SubstantiallyExistsAt(PriorEvent, t2)
```

The temporal object rule is correspondingly modal:

```text
Remembers(Cognition, x)
    → PresentsAs(x, FormerlyWas)

Anticipates(Cognition, x)
    → PresentsAs(x, WillBeGivenCause)

PresentsAsObject(Cognition, x)
    ⇏ PresentlyExistsSubstantially(x)
```

Past efficacy is thus distinguished from persistent past entity.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.27 gives the fourfold architecture its decisive genetic form:

```text
hetu
    the prior event that actually occurred and transformed the continuum

phala
    the later result arising when the relevant conditions obtain

āśraya
    saṃtāna-viśeṣa, the continuum as determinately transformed

ālambana
    what cognition presents under the modes “was,” “is,” or “will be”
```

This is stronger than the schema in VAK 5.25. There, the four relations forced
the problem of temporal being. Here Vasubandhu shows how they can remain real
without a substantially persistent past or future entity. `Hetu` does not
survive as the old event; it survives in the produced determination of the
`āśraya`. From that transformed support the `phala` becomes possible. Meanwhile
the `ālambana` is presented according to its proper temporal mode rather than
counterfeited as presently existent.

This is the Product that Absolute Logic required us not to skip. More exactly,
the transition alternates between the inwardly generative pair and the
objectively presented pair:

```text
(hetu–āśraya)ₙ
    → (phala–ālambana)ₙ
        → (hetu–āśraya)ₙ₊₁
```

The prior `hetu` operates from its concrete `āśraya` and produces a result. As
achieved and presented, that result is `phala–ālambana`: the Product stands
forth objectively for cognition. As taken up into the continuum, the same
achieved determination becomes `hetu–āśraya` for the next operation. Thus the
movement is not the transfer of a static thing between boxes. It is the change
of logical office performed by one determinate Product.

```text
outward determination
    result becomes objectively explicit

inward return
    objective result becomes transformed ground

renewed outward determination
    transformed ground produces the next result
```

This is the rigorous content of the apparent “ping-pong.” The movement passes
between manifestation and ground, but each return preserves and transforms
what the preceding operation produced.

The Yoga presentation model is also clarified. Buddhi or citta can present:

```text
memory
    “it was”

present cognition
    “it is occurring”

anticipation
    “it will be if its cause obtains”
```

Puruṣa does not vote these into existence. Its standpoint permits the
presentation to be seen without appropriating the presented mode as the Seer.
Discriminative cognition gives the practical “thumbs up or down” by preserving
the temporal difference—or exposing a presentation that falsely treats memory
or anticipation as present substance.

The Aṣṭāṅga Path in itself supplies this genetic discrimination; the bhūmis are
its appearance as an actually transformed continuum. A true Path-bhūmi is
therefore neither a retained image of an earlier state nor a projected future
identity. It is the present `saṃtāna-viśeṣa` generated by prior Path-operation
and capable of producing a further liberative result.

In the philosophical combat ring, Vasubandhu wins this round by the very
standard Hegel would demand: the mediator must generate the difference. Hegel
then helps us recognize the positive result—the cause is preserved not as a
dead entity but in the transformed actuality it has produced.

This is Organon–Yoga–Hegel reconstruction. Its textual ground is Vasubandhu's
critique of `kāritra`, his modal account of past and future objects, and the
Sautrāntika `saṃtāna-viśeṣa` explanation of delayed fruition.
