# VAK_5.46

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
येऽप्यन्ये चैतसाः क्लिष्टाः संस्कारस्कन्धसंज्ञिताः ।
क्लेशेभ्यस्तेऽप्युपक्लेशास्ते तु न क्लेशसंज्ञिताः ॥ ५.४६ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
ye 'py anye caitasāḥ kliṣṭāḥ saṃskāraskandha-saṃjñitāḥ |
kleśebhyas te 'py upakleśās te tu na kleśa-saṃjñitāḥ || 5.46 ||
```

VAK 5.46 begins the `upakleśa` discussion after VAK 5.45 completes the
fetter and bond classifications. The Bhāṣya establishes `upakleśa` as a broad
mind-defiling designation that includes every kleśa as well as additional
afflicted mental factors.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
ye | api | anye | caitasāḥ | kliṣṭāḥ | saṃskāra-skandha-saṃjñitāḥ |
kleśebhyaḥ | te | api | upakleśāḥ | te | tu | na | kleśa-saṃjñitāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `ye ... te` | nominative plural masculine relative-correlative pair | those which ... they |
| `api` | additive particle | also |
| `anye` | nominative plural masculine | other, distinct |
| `caitasāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | mental factors associated with citta |
| `kliṣṭāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | afflicted or defiled |
| `saṃskāra-skandha-saṃjñitāḥ` | nominative plural masculine compound | designated as included in the formations aggregate |
| `kleśebhyaḥ` | ablative plural masculine | apart from or other than the kleśas |
| `upakleśāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | mind-defilements or secondary afflictions |
| `tu` | adversative particle | but, however |
| `na kleśa-saṃjñitāḥ` | negation with nominative plural compound | not designated as kleśas |

The governing construction is `anye kleśebhyaḥ`, “other than the kleśas.”
The ablative is separative, not causal; the verse does not say that these
factors arise from kleśas.

## 4. Grammar

**Relative specification**

```text
ye api
    anye kleśebhyaḥ
    caitasāḥ
    kliṣṭāḥ
    saṃskāra-skandha-saṃjñitāḥ
```

The relative `ye` carries four coordinated qualifications. These factors are
distinct from the stricter kleśa class, are mental, are afflicted, and are
included in the formations aggregate.

**Correlative classification and restriction**

```text
te api
    upakleśāḥ

te tu
    na kleśa-saṃjñitāḥ
```

The first `te` completes the relative-correlative construction and adds these
factors to the `upakleśa` class. The second introduces the asymmetry: although
they qualify as upakleśas, they are not called kleśas.

**Bhāṣya's prior universal statement**

```text
ye yāvat kleśāḥ
    upakleśāḥ api te
        cittopakleśanāt
```

Whatever factors are kleśas are also upakleśas because they defile citta. This
statement controls the class relation and prevents treating the two terms as
disjoint.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Those other afflicted mental factors, designated as belonging to the
> formations aggregate and distinct from the kleśas, are also upakleśas; but
> they are not designated as kleśas.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Every kleśa is also an upakleśa insofar as it defiles citta. In addition,
> other afflicted mental factors included in the formations aggregate and
> distinct from the kleśas are upakleśas, although they do not receive the
> stricter designation “kleśa.” The broader inventory is taught in the
> Kṣudravastuka; here Vasubandhu will discuss only those included among the
> paryavasthānas and the afflictive stains.

The inclusion of every kleśa, the reference to the Kṣudravastuka, and the
local scope restriction are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> The afflicted field does not divide into two mutually exclusive classes of
> roots and derivatives. `Upakleśa` first names the broader function of
> defiling citta: every kleśa therefore satisfies it. Yet the same functional
> extension also contains afflicted mental factors that lack the stricter
> doctrinal status named by `kleśa`. Inclusion under a broad operative
> predicate does not erase difference under a narrower classifier. Nor does
> the chapter claim to exhaust the broader field; it selects two subgroups for
> the analysis that follows.

This reconstruction preserves the source's overlapping extensions and does
not import genetic relations that are established only for particular factors
in later verses.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Kleśa` — affliction**

The stricter doctrinal class established earlier in the chapter. The Bhāṣya
states that every kleśa also counts as an upakleśa under the mind-defiling
function.

**`Upakleśa` — mind-defilement or secondary affliction**

A broader functional designation grounded in `cittopakleśana`, defiling citta.
“Secondary affliction” remains a useful conventional rendering only if it does
not imply that all upakleśas are non-kleśas or that the term itself encodes
causal derivation.

**`Cittopakleśana` — defiling or disturbing citta**

The Bhāṣya's explicit reason why kleśas themselves fall within the broader
upakleśa class.

**`Caitasa` / `caitasika` — mental factor**

A dharma associated with citta in a cognitive event. The other upakleśas named
by the verse are afflicted caitasikas rather than members of the stricter
kleśa class.

**`Saṃskāra-skandha-saṃgṛhīta` — included in the formations aggregate**

The aggregate location of these additional afflicted mental factors. This is
a classificatory inclusion, not by itself an account of causal origin.

**`Kliṣṭa` — afflicted or defiled**

The shared ethical-afflictive status of the additional caitasikas. Being
afflicted is necessary to the present class but does not entail the stricter
designation `kleśa`.

**`Kṣudravastuka` — Minor Topics**

The textual or doctrinal source to which the Bhāṣya assigns the fuller
inventory of these additional upakleśas. Chapter 5 deliberately treats only a
selected portion.

**`Paryavasthāna` — enveloping manifestation**

One of the two locally selected subgroups to be explained next, beginning in
VAK 5.47.

**`Kleśa-mala` — afflictive stain**

The second subgroup selected for the coming local analysis. It is introduced
as a scope category, not yet fully enumerated in this verse.

## 8. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya establishes an inclusion without equivalence:

```text
Kleśa(x)
    → DefilesCitta(x)
    → Upakleśa(x)

Upakleśa(x)
    ⇏ Kleśa(x)
```

The verse identifies the additional extension:

```text
MentalFactor(x)
AND Afflicted(x)
AND IncludedIn(x, SaṃskāraSkandha)
AND DistinctFromKleśaClass(x)
    → Upakleśa(x)
    AND NOT Kleśa(x)
```

The grammar blocks the former causal inference:

```text
Anya(x, KleśaClass)
    ≠ ArisesFrom(x, Kleśa)
```

The present chapter then projects a scoped view:

```text
AllUpakleśas
    ⊃ LocallyDiscussedUpakleśas

LocallyDiscussedUpakleśas
    = Paryavasthānas ∪ KleśaMalas
```

The last equality states the Bhāṣya's declared discussion scope, not that these
two categories exhaust every upakleśa in all sources.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Source determination.** VAK 5.45 ends by saying that anuśayas have already
been treated and that upakleśas must now be explained. Before enumerating them,
the Bhāṣya defines the breadth of the term and the narrower scope of the coming
discussion.

**Overlap, not disjointness.** The central relation is:

```text
all kleśas are upakleśas
    because all defile citta

some upakleśas are not kleśas
    because additional afflicted caitasikas share that function
```

Thus `upakleśa` is broader in extension under its stated functional criterion.
Calling it “secondary” must not conceal that every primary kleśa also belongs
to this broader designation.

**Correction of causal overreading.** `Kleśebhyaḥ` is governed by `anye` and
marks separation. VAK 5.46 does not define upakleśas as effects generated from
root afflictions. Later verses may connect particular factors to specific
afflictive origins, but those relations must be recorded where the source
actually states them.

**Organon interpretation.** The passage requires overlapping, rationale-bearing
Views rather than a rigid inheritance tree:

```text
KleśaView
    classifier: stricter doctrinal status

UpakleśaView
    classifier: mind-defiling function

ChapterFiveScope
    projection: paryavasthāna and kleśa-mala subsets
```

The Dataset must preserve both membership and the reason for membership. This
is an Organon architectural consequence of the Bhāṣya's classifications, not
an attempt to replace Abhidharma terminology with software ontology.
