# VAK_5.65

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
अन्या अवरभागीयरूपसर्वास्रवक्षयाः ।
तिस्रः परिज्ञाः षट् क्षान्तिफलं ज्ञानस्य शेषिताः ॥ ५.६५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
anyā avarabhāgīya-rūpa-sarvāsrava-kṣayāḥ |
tisraḥ parijñāḥ ṣaṭ kṣānti-phalaṃ jñānasya śeṣitāḥ || 5.65 ||
```

VAK 5.64 enumerated six parijñās whose substance is abandonment of seeing-
abandonable afflictions. VAK 5.65 supplies the remaining three cultivation-
Path completions and overlays the nine with a patience/knowledge result
classification.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
anyāḥ | avara-bhāgīya-kṣayaḥ | rūpa-kṣayaḥ | sarva-āsrava-kṣayaḥ |
tisraḥ | parijñāḥ | ṣaṭ | kṣānti-phalam | jñānasya | śeṣitāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `anyāḥ` | nominative plural feminine | the other, remaining parijñās |
| `avarabhāgīya-kṣayaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | destruction or abandonment of the lower-part fetters |
| `rūpa-kṣayaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | destruction of the form-influx, specified as destruction of attachment to form |
| `sarvāsrava-kṣayaḥ` | nominative singular masculine compound | destruction of all influxes |
| `tisraḥ parijñāḥ` | nominative plural feminine | three parijñās |
| `ṣaṭ` | numeral | six |
| `kṣānti-phalam` | nominative singular neuter used collectively | fruit of patience |
| `jñānasya` | genitive singular neuter | of knowledge |
| `śeṣitāḥ` | nominative plural feminine | the remaining ones |

The first pāda compactly coordinates three `kṣaya` compounds with the three
remaining parijñās. Their precise technical expansions are fixed by the
Bhāṣya.

## 4. Grammar

The opening predicate is elliptical:

```text
anyāḥ tisraḥ parijñāḥ
    [are]
    avarabhāgīya-kṣayaḥ
    rūpa-kṣayaḥ
    sarvāsrava-kṣayaḥ
```

The masculine singular names of the three abandonment-completions stand in
apposition to the feminine plural class `parijñāḥ` by sense rather than simple
surface agreement.

The second half partitions the full set of nine:

```text
ṣaṭ [parijñāḥ]
    kṣānti-phalam

śeṣitāḥ [tisraḥ]
    jñānasya [phalam]
```

The Bhāṣya explains that the six are the seeing-abandonment parijñās and the
remaining three are fruits of the cultivation path. It then qualifies the
`kṣānti-phala` designation through the patiences' relation to knowledge.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The other three parijñās are the destructions of the lower-part [fetters],
> of form, and of all influxes. Six are the fruit of patience; the remaining
> ones are of knowledge.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The remaining three parijñās are abandonment of the lower-part fetters;
> abandonment of the form-influx, called the parijñā of destroying attachment
> to form; and abandonment of all influxes, called the parijñā of completely
> exhausting all fetters. The six seeing-abandonment parijñās are designated
> fruits of patience, while these remaining three, as fruits of the path of
> cultivation, are fruits of knowledge.

The Bhāṣya explains the first designation by saying that the patiences belong
to the retinue of the knowledges and share one fruit with them.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Completed abandonment advances through three remaining ranges: release from
> the fetters binding one to the lower part, release from the form-influx and
> attachment to form, and complete exhaustion of every influx and fetter. The
> counting follows not domain alone but the operative structure of removal:
> distinct counteragents yield distinct cultivation-completions. Patience and
> knowledge name related contributors to the result without becoming
> identical; the result-language follows their ordered association and shared
> fruit.

The nine parijñās are thus completed disconnections classified through
abandonment-mode, range, counteragent, and result-designation.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa` — abandonment of the lower-part fetters**

The seventh parijñā. `Avarabhāgīya` refers to the technically established
fetters binding the practitioner to the lower range, not vaguely to “lower
tendencies.”

**`Rūpāsrava-prahāṇa` — abandonment of the form-influx**

The Bhāṣya's exact expansion of the compressed `rūpa-kṣaya`. This is the
eighth parijñā and is named `rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā`, the completed abandonment
of attachment to form.

**`Sarvāsrava-prahāṇa` — abandonment of all influxes**

The ninth and terminal parijñā. In connection with the remaining formless-
range influxes, the prose names it `sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā`, the
complete exhaustion of all fetters.

**`Atulya-pratipakṣatva` — having non-identical counteragents**

The Bhāṣya's reason that cultivation-abandonments of the form and formless
ranges count as separate parijñās even though the corresponding higher-domain
seeing-abandonments were grouped. The unit of completion follows the structure
of counteraction, not domain-count alone.

**`Kṣānti-phala` — fruit of patience**

Designation of the six parijñās whose nature is abandonment of seeing-
abandonable afflictions. It should not be construed as though patience acted
in isolation from knowledge.

**`Jñāna-parivāra` — retinue of knowledge**

The patiences stand in the entourage of the knowledges. The Bhāṣya compares
the transferred usage to applying the king's designation to the royal
retinue (`rājopacāra`).

**`Jñānaika-phalatva` — sharing one fruit with knowledge**

The second stated reason for calling the six fruits of patience. Patience and
knowledge converge upon one result while remaining distinct Path-factors.

**`Jñāna-phala` — fruit of knowledge**

The remaining three parijñās, because they are fruits of the path of
cultivation (`bhāvanā-mārga-phala`).

## 8. Logical Determination

The final three parijñās are:

```text
Parijñā_7
    = CompleteAbandonment(LowerPartFetters)

Parijñā_8
    = Abandonment(FormInflux)
    = Destruction(AttachmentToForm)

Parijñā_9
    = Abandonment(AllInfluxes)
    = CompleteExhaustion(AllFetters)
```

Together with VAK 5.64:

```text
Count(SeeingAbandonmentParijñā)     = 6
Count(CultivationPathParijñā)       = 3
Count(AllPrahāṇaParijñās)           = 9
```

The counting rule is counteragent-sensitive:

```text
NonIdenticalCounteragent(classA, classB)
    → DistinctParijñāCompletion(classA, classB)
```

Therefore:

```text
SameBroadAbandonmentMode(x, y)
    ⇏ SameParijñā(x, y)
```

The result-designations are relational:

```text
SeeingAbandonmentParijñā(x)
AND RetinueOf(Kṣānti, Jñāna)
AND SharesFruitWith(Kṣānti, Jñāna)
    → BearsResultDesignation(x, KṣāntiPhala)

CultivationPathParijñā(x)
    → BearsResultDesignation(x, JñānaPhala)
```

The royal-retinue analogy explains transferred designation, not numerical or
functional identity between patience and knowledge.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**The final triad is technically precise.** The Bhāṣya replaces the loose
“form-related destruction” with abandonment of the form-influx and destruction
of attachment to form. The final member is strengthened through its formal
designation as exhaustion of all fetters.

**Why cultivation divisions differ.** Form and formless seeing-abandonments
were collectively counted in VAK 5.64. Their cultivation-abandonments separate
because their counteragents are not alike. Relation to the effective remedy,
not parallel wording or domain alone, governs the parijñā unit.

**Patience is not isolated from knowledge.** The Bhāṣya answers an anticipated
problem with two relations: retinue and shared fruit. Calling the six
`kṣānti-phala` therefore depends on structured association rather than an
unqualified causal slogan.

**Completed ninefold system.** The nine coordinate object-range, truth-class,
mode of abandonment, counteragent, disconnection, and result-designation.
They are neither nine propositions nor nine repeated destructions.

**Organon interpretation.** A later Dataset should make the completion rule
and naming grounds explicit:

```text
ParijñāCompletion
    exhausts AfflictiveRange
    hasAbandonmentMode
    requiresCounteragent
    hasResultDesignation
    designationGroundedBy RelationSet
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This formalization preserves the Bhāṣya's actual criteria and is not
attributed to Vasubandhu as his own technical notation.
