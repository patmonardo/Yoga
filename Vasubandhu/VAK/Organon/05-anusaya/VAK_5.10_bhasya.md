# VAK 5.10 Bhāṣya — Seven Conceits and Their Manifestation

## Kārikā Anchor

> sapta mānā navavidhās tribhyo dṛgbhāvanākṣayāḥ |
> vadhādiparyavasthānaṃ heyaṃ bhāvanayā tathā || 5.10 ||

> Conceits are seven; nine modes arise from three. They are
> exhausted through seeing and cultivation. Afflictive occupation
> beginning with killing is abandoned through cultivation; likewise—

The final `tathā` carries the comparison into 5.11. The present
unit stops before that verse's `vibhavecchā`.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

Is view the only anuśaya that admits an internal division? Conceit
does too. It has seven forms: māna, atimāna, mānātimāna,
asmimāna, abhimāna, ūnamāna, and mithyāmāna. In general,
conceit is an elevation of citta; its operations differentiate
it sevenfold.

Māna is the elevation in thinking oneself superior to an inferior
or equal to an equal. Taking oneself as superior to an equal is
the next form, apparently atimāna, although this line of the
e-text calls it `abhimāna`. Mānātimāna is taking oneself as
superior to a superior. Asmimāna takes the five appropriated
aggregates as self or mine. Abhimāna claims a special attainment
not attained. Ūnamāna takes oneself as only slightly inferior to
one greatly superior. Mithyāmāna claims qualities one lacks.

But a treatise speaks of nine modes of conceit: “I am superior,”
“I am equal,” “I am inferior”; “I have a superior,” “I have an
equal,” “I have an inferior”; and “I have no superior,” “I have
no equal,” “I have no inferior.” They arise from three of the
conceits already named: māna, atimāna, and ūnamāna. The prose
assigns each of the three triplets to these comparative modes.
It then asks how “I have no inferior” could elevate citta.
The answer appeals to one's favored class of beings: one may
still esteem oneself highly there despite relative inferiority.
It also notes a rule in the Jñānaprasthāna and says that, in a
contextual description, even “I am superior” can express
different conceits depending on whether the comparison is with
an inferior, an equal, or a superior.

How are the seven conceits abandoned? Through seeing and
cultivation. But must everything still classified as
cultivation-abandonable actually manifest in noble practitioners?
Not necessarily. For example, the afflictive occupation by which
one deliberately kills a living being, up to speaking falsely,
belongs to what is abandoned through cultivation because its
cognitive support belongs to that class. The verse closes with
“likewise,” carrying this point forward.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The generic definition `cittasya unnati` does not turn citta into
a psychological container. It identifies conceit's operation as
an elevation in the citta configuration. The seven forms specify
different distortions of comparison, appropriation, attainment,
and claimed quality.

The nine modes are not nine new substances or a second independent
list. Their three source conceits are **māna, atimāna, and
ūnamāna**. The research summary incorrectly gives mānātimāna
among those three. The running source's compressed
`mānātimānonamānebhyaḥ` must be segmented as
`māna-atimāna-ūnamānebhyaḥ`, as its ensuing triplets confirm.

The close of the unit draws a second distinction: a factor can
remain classed as to be abandoned through cultivation without
necessarily manifesting now in an ārya. `Aprahīṇa` and
`samudācarati` therefore cannot be equated. The killing example
is about a deliberate afflictive occupation (`saṃcintya`), not
about the bare occurrence of bodily movement.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** `Māna` stays visible because
“conceit” only approximates its comparative operation.
`Asmimāna` is not assimilated to the view `satkāyadṛṣṭi` merely
because both concern the aggregates and self-relation. The
irregular `abhimāna` label in the second definition is explicitly
marked. `Citta` is retained in the generic definition.

**Organon reconstruction.** The text distinguishes classification,
continued susceptibility, and present manifestation. A science
of citta must preserve those relations before drawing conclusions
from a reported or observed episode. This is a conceptual use of
the Kośa, not a claim about executable Rust or TypeScript types.
