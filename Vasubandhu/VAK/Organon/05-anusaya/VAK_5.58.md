# VAK_5.58

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
माया शाठ्यमथो म्रक्षो मिद्धं चोभयथा मदः ।
सुखाभ्यां सर्वगोपेक्षा चत्वार्यन्यानि पञ्चभिः ॥ ५.५८ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
māyā śāṭhyam atho mrakṣo middhaṃ cobhayathā madaḥ |
sukhābhyāṃ sarvagopekṣā catvāry anyāni pañcabhiḥ || 5.58 ||
```

VAK 5.58 completes the feeling-association analysis. It distinguishes
upakleśas compatible with both mental polarities, the level-sensitive range
of intoxication, the universal compatibility of neutral feeling, and four
pervasive factors compatible with all five feeling-faculties.

**Textual caution.** The working Bhāṣya e-text transmits `athī bhrakṣo` and
`āhlīkya`; the doctrinally intelligible readings correspond to kārikā
`atho mrakṣo` and to `āhrīkya`. The corrupted prose forms remain untouched in
the research witness and are not silently substituted into the verse.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
māyā | śāṭhyam | atho | mrakṣaḥ | middham | ca | ubhayathā |
madaḥ | sukha-ābhyām | sarva-gā | upekṣā | catvāri | anyāni | pañcabhiḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `māyā` | nominative singular feminine | deception; falsification of outward presentation |
| `śāṭhyam` | nominative singular neuter | crookedness; falsification or concealment of intention |
| `atho` | connective particle | and further, then also |
| `mrakṣaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | concealment of one's fault |
| `middham` | nominative singular neuter | sleep as a derivative afflictive factor |
| `ca` | conjunction | and |
| `ubhayathā` | indeclinable adverb | in both ways: with mental gladness or distress |
| `madaḥ` | nominative singular masculine | intoxication or inflated self-satisfaction |
| `sukhābhyām` | instrumental dual neuter | with the two pleasant feelings |
| `sarvagā` | nominative singular feminine | going with all; universally compatible |
| `upekṣā` | nominative singular feminine | neutral or equanimous feeling-faculty |
| `catvāri anyāni` | nominative plural neuter | the other four specified paryavasthānas |
| `pañcabhiḥ` | instrumental plural | with all five feeling-faculties |

`Sarvagā` agrees with `upekṣā`: equanimity “goes with all.” It does not turn
equanimity into an affliction or assert that every equanimous event is
afflicted.

## 4. Grammar

The first group shares an omitted conjunction predicate:

```text
{māyā, śāṭhyam, mrakṣaḥ, middham}
    ubhayathā [saṃprayuktāni]
```

The Bhāṣya resolves `ubhayathā` as the instrumental dual
`saumanasya-daurmanasyābhyām`: with mental gladness and mental distress.

The next clause is:

```text
madaḥ
    sukhābhyām [saṃprayuktaḥ]
```

The kārikā states the pleasant dual compactly. The prose distributes mada by
level and adds equanimity above the third dhyāna; this qualification must be
kept visible rather than flattened into a timeless two-member set.

The universal construction is:

```text
upekṣā
    sarvagā
```

Finally:

```text
catvāri anyāni
    pañcabhiḥ [indriyaiḥ saṃprayuktāni]
```

The Bhāṣya identifies the four as `āhrīkya`, `anapatrāpya`, `styāna`, and
`auddhatya`, and supplies “feeling-faculties” as the ellipsis after `pañcabhiḥ`.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Deception, crookedness, concealment, and sleep are [associated] in both
> ways. Intoxication is [associated] with the two pleasant [feelings].
> Equanimity goes with all. The other four are [associated] with all five.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Deception, crookedness, concealment of faults, and sleep can be associated
> with either mental gladness or mental distress. Intoxication is associated
> with the feeling appropriate to its level: with mental gladness below the
> third dhyāna, bodily pleasure in the third dhyāna, and equanimity above it.
> Equanimity is compatible with all the anuśayas. The other four—shamelessness,
> lack of moral concern, torpor, and excitation—are associated with all five
> feeling-faculties.

The level-sensitive account of mada and the identities and doctrinal grounds
of the final four are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Deception, crooked intention, concealment, and sleep retain their afflicted
> function under either gladness or distress; opposite appearances do not
> alter what the factor does. Intoxication likewise has no context-free
> feeling: its affective manifestation changes with the meditative level.
> Neutral feeling excludes no anuśaya merely by being neutral, while four
> pervasive derivative factors can inhabit the whole affective range. Feeling
> is therefore neither the identity nor the ethical verdict of an affliction,
> but one domain-conditioned determination of its occurrence.

This closes the feeling matrix as a relational constraint system rather than
a collection of isolated glosses.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Ubhayathā` — in both ways**

The Bhāṣya specifies mental gladness and mental distress. One may deceive,
conceal, or sleep while glad or while distressed; the affective polarity
varies while the upakleśa's function remains identifiable.

**`Māyā` — deception**

The construction of a false outward presentation. Its diagnostic identity is
its falsifying operation, not whether the deceiver presently feels pleased or
distressed.

**`Śāṭhya` — crookedness**

Distortion or concealment of inward intention. It must remain distinct from
māyā even though both share the same twofold feeling range.

**`Mrakṣa` — concealment**

Concealment of one's own fault, especially against disclosure or correction.

**`Middha` — sleep**

Sleep classified here as a derivative afflictive factor. The verse concerns
its possible feeling-conjunctions, not sleep without qualification in every
context.

**`Mada` — intoxication**

Inflated satisfaction based on one's qualities, vitality, status,
possessions, or attainments. The Bhāṣya makes its feeling-conjunction depend
on meditative level:

```text
below third dhyāna   → saumanasya
third dhyāna         → sukha
above third dhyāna   → upekṣā
```

**`Sarvagā upekṣā` — equanimity compatible with all**

A universal compatibility rule for the anuśayas in this analysis. It asserts
the absence of a prohibition, not that every occurrence of neutral feeling
contains affliction.

**The four others**

```text
āhrīkya       shamelessness
anapatrāpya   lack of moral concern
styāna        torpor
auddhatya     excitation or restlessness
```

The first pair are great unwholesome factors (`akuśala-mahābhūmika`); the
second pair are great afflictive factors (`kleśa-mahābhūmika`). The Bhāṣya
uses those statuses to ground their compatibility with all five feelings.

## 8. Logical Determination

The bivalent mental range is:

```text
For x in {Māyā, Śāṭhya, Mrakṣa, Middha}:
    CompatibleFeeling(x) includes {Saumanasya, Daurmanasya}
```

Mada requires a domain-indexed relation:

```text
CompatibleFeeling(Mada, BelowThirdDhyāna) = Saumanasya
CompatibleFeeling(Mada, ThirdDhyāna)      = Sukha
CompatibleFeeling(Mada, AboveThirdDhyāna) = Upekṣā
```

The general neutral rule is modal, not existentially universal:

```text
For every anuśaya x:
    PermitsConjunction(x, Upekṣā)

PermitsConjunction(x, Upekṣā)
    ⇏ EveryUpekṣāEventHas(x)
```

The four pervasive derivatives have complete affective range:

```text
For x in {Āhrīkya, Anapatrāpya, Styāna, Auddhatya}:
    CompatibleFeeling(x)
        = {Sukha, Duḥkha, Saumanasya, Daurmanasya, Upekṣā}
```

Their range is grounded through classification:

```text
MahābhūmikaStatus(x)
    grounds
BroadFeelingCompatibility(x)
```

The completed analysis therefore requires:

```text
CompatibleFeeling(Factor, Feeling, Bhūmi, CognitiveSupport)
```

rather than a unary feeling label attached to a factor.

## 9. Interpretive Note

**Opposite feelings, invariant function.** The first four cases demonstrate
that how a state feels does not by itself disclose what relation it performs.
Deception can be glad or distressed while remaining deception.

**Mada is the decisive domain test.** The kārikā's compact `sukhābhyām` is
unfolded by the prose through the meditative hierarchy, including equanimity
above the third dhyāna. A flat lookup table would lose this Bhāṣya-controlled
determination.

**Neutrality supplies no automatic verdict.** The prose explains universal
compatibility negatively: equanimity is nowhere prohibited as certain other
conjunctions are. This does not convert ordinary neutral feeling into either
affliction or liberation.

**Classification grounds relational range.** The final four do not receive
fivefold range from a modern psychological conjecture. Their great
unwholesome or great afflictive status is the explicit doctrinal ground.

**Transition.** With VAK 5.58 the kleśa/upakleśa association with feeling-
faculties is complete. VAK 5.59 begins the distinct topic of the five
hindrances and the rationale for counting two pairs as single hindrances.

**Organon interpretation.** The minimal project-level representation is a
qualified relation:

```text

    concernsDharma
    permitsFeeling
    atBhūmi
    underCognitiveSupport
    groundedBy Classification
    supportedBy SourceSpan
```

This representation preserves the source's mediations and is not attributed
to the Abhidharma as its own formal vocabulary.
