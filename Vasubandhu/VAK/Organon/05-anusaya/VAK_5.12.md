# VAK_5.12

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सर्वत्रगा दुःखहेतुदृग्घेया दृष्टयस्तथा ।
विमतिः सह ताभिश्च याऽविद्याऽवैणिकी च या ॥ ५.१२ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sarvatragā duḥkha-hetu-dṛg-gheyā dṛṣṭayas tathā |
vimatiḥ saha tābhiś ca yāvidyā 'veṇikī ca yā || 5.12 ||
```

The verse begins a new classification of the ninety-eight anuśayas: those
whose range is universal within their own domain and those whose range is not.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sarvatragāḥ | duḥkha-hetu-dṛk-gheyāḥ | dṛṣṭayaḥ | tathā |
vimatiḥ | saha | tābhiḥ | ca | yā | avidyā | āveṇikī | ca | yā
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `sarvatragāḥ` | nominative plural feminine | universally ranging; extending throughout the appropriate domain |
| `duḥkha-hetu-dṛk-gheyāḥ` | nominative plural feminine compound | to be abandoned by seeing suffering and its cause/origination |
| `dṛṣṭayaḥ` | nominative plural feminine | views |
| `tathā` | indeclinable | likewise; in the same classification |
| `vimatiḥ` | nominative singular feminine | doubt, uncertainty |
| `saha tābhiḥ` | indeclinable plus instrumental plural feminine | together or associated with those [views] |
| `avidyā` | nominative singular feminine | ignorance |
| `āveṇikī` | nominative singular feminine adjective | independent, unassociated |

Here `hetu` does not name cause in an unrestricted sense. In the paired
expression `duḥkha-hetu`, the Bhāṣya resolves it as `duḥkha-samudaya`: the
truth of suffering and the truth of its origination.

## 4. Grammar

**Primary predication**

```text
duḥkha-hetu-dṛk-gheyāḥ dṛṣṭayaḥ sarvatragāḥ

the views abandoned by seeing suffering and origination
are universally ranging
```

`Dṛk` is the compact compound form of `dṛś`, “seeing,” and `gheya` carries the
sense “to be removed or abandoned.” The feminine plurals agree with
`dṛṣṭayaḥ`.

**Extension through `tathā` and `ca`**

```text
tathā vimatiḥ
ca yā avidyā tābhiḥ saha
ca yā avidyā āveṇikī

likewise doubt,
the ignorance associated with those [views],
and the ignorance that is independent
```

The two relative expressions marked by `yā` distinguish associated ignorance
from independent ignorance. The Bhāṣya supplies the understood restriction:
each is among the classes abandoned by seeing suffering and origination.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> The views to be abandoned through seeing suffering and its cause are
> universally ranging; likewise doubt, the ignorance that is associated with
> those [views], and the ignorance that is independent.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> Eleven anuśayas range universally within their own corresponding domain:
> the seven views and two doubts abandoned through seeing suffering and
> origination, together with the two forms of ignorance there—ignorance
> associated with those views and independent ignorance. They are universal
> because their object-range can extend across the whole of their own domain.

The enumeration, the same-domain restriction, and the explanation by
object-range are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> An anuśaya is universal not because it is active at every place and moment,
> but because its way of determining an object is general enough to range
> throughout its proper domain. Views, doubt, and their forms of ignorance can
> misdetermine an entire field under a common character. Attachment and
> conceit may repeatedly follow such views, yet repetition over particulars
> does not itself constitute a general determination of the field.

The Bhāṣya therefore separates three notions:

```text
occurring together with a universal view
    ≠ ranging successively over many particulars
        ≠ determining a domain under a general character
```

Only the third supplies the decisive sense of `sarvatraga` in the Vaibhāṣika
resolution.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Sarvatraga` — universally ranging**

A classification by object-range. The term does not mean omnipresent,
continually manifest, maximally intense, or unrestricted across every domain.

**`Sabhāgadhātu-sarvatraga` — universal within a corresponding domain**

The Bhāṣya's restriction prevents “universal” from becoming absolutely
unbounded. The affliction ranges throughout its own homogeneous domain.

**`Sakala-svadhātu-ālambana` — having the whole of its own domain as object-support**

This is the Bhāṣya's defining rationale. `Ālambana` here names the objective
range upon which cognition and affliction operate. “Whole” does not require a
single simultaneous apprehension of every item.

**The eleven universal anuśayas**

```text
seven views
+ two doubts
+ two ignorances
= eleven universal anuśayas
```

All belong to the classes abandoned through seeing suffering and origination.

**`Vimati / vicikitsā` — doubt**

The kārikā uses `vimati`; the Bhāṣya explains it as `vicikitsā`. Two instances
are counted, corresponding to suffering and origination.

**Associated and independent ignorance**

Associated ignorance occurs in conjunction with the relevant views.
`Āveṇikī avidyā` is ignorance operating independently rather than merely as
their associate. One of each class is counted under suffering and under
origination.

**`Svalakṣaṇa-kleśa` and `sāmānya-kleśa`**

The final distinction is explicitly the Vaibhāṣika answer. Attachment and
conceit concern particular characteristics; the universal afflictions operate
through general characteristics. Broad recurrence does not erase this
difference in logical form.

## 8. Logical Determination

The primary classification can be represented as:

```text
AbandonedBySeeing(x, SufferingOrOrigination)
AND GeneralDomainRange(x)
    → UniversalAnusaya(x)
```

The Bhāṣya sharpens `GeneralDomainRange`:

```text
HasObjectRange(x, WholeOfOwnDomain)
AND RestrictedTo(x, CorrespondingDomain)
    → GeneralDomainRange(x)
```

But the following inferences are invalid:

```text
UniversalAnusaya(x)
    ⇏ SimultaneouslyTakesEveryItem(x)

RepeatedlyRangesOverParticulars(x)
    ⇏ UniversalAnusaya(x)

AssociatedWith(x, UniversalView)
    ⇏ UniversalAnusaya(x)
```

The objection concerning attachment and conceit makes the last two exclusions
necessary. They can accompany or be strengthened by universal views while
remaining particular-characteristic afflictions.

The technical structure is therefore two-dimensional:

```text
abandonment axis
    seeing suffering / origination

range axis
    general domain-character / particular characteristic
```

Neither axis can be derived mechanically from the other.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.12 gives `ālambana` a decisive systematic role. It is not merely the
external item called an “object.” It is the determinate objective field over
which an afflictive form has jurisdiction. `Sarvatraga` describes the scope of
that relation, while `sabhāgadhātu` supplies its boundary.

This lets the Product be represented at two different logical resolutions:

```text
general-character determination
    configures how an entire domain can appear

particular-character relation
    attaches to or ranks a determinate content within that display
```

A view may corrupt the rule under which the objective display is intelligible;
attachment and conceit may then appropriate particular contents disclosed
within it. The latter may propagate widely without thereby becoming the
general rule of disclosure. Thus frequency, extension, and logical scope must
remain distinct.

In Organon terms, this is the difference between a schema-level determination
and any number of instance-level transactions. The general determination is
manifest only through particulars, but it is not reducible to their aggregate.
Conversely, many particular transactions do not by accumulation produce a
universal determination.

This advances the Subject–Product–Object architecture without importing it
into the source doctrine. The Subject is not placed opposite a ready-made
object. The Product first articulates an objective domain under a general
determination; particular relations then occur within that articulated field.
Truth-seeing can therefore transform the principle governing the display, not
merely cancel isolated contents one by one.

This is Organon reconstruction. Its textual ground is the Bhāṣya's account of
whole-domain `ālambana` and the explicitly attributed Vaibhāṣika distinction
between general-character and particular-character affliction.
