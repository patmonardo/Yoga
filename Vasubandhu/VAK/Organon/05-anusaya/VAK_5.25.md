# VAK_5.25

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

```sanskrit
सर्वकालास्तिता उक्तत्वाद् द्वयात् सद्विषयात् फलात् ।
तदस्तिवादात् सर्वास्तिवादा इष्टाश्चतुर्विधाः ॥ ५.२५ ॥
```

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

```text
sarvakālāstitā uktatvād dvayāt sad-viṣayāt phalāt |
tad-asti-vādāt sarvāstivādā iṣṭāś caturvidhāḥ || 5.25 ||
```

The verse makes explicit the temporal ontology presupposed by VAK 5.23–5.24:
if past and future dharmas can be objects, bind, and bear causal relations,
they cannot be reduced to sheer nonbeing.

## 3. Padaccheda and Lexical Analysis

```text
sarva-kāla-astitā | ukta-tvāt | dvayāt | sat-viṣayāt | phalāt |
tat-asti-vādāt | sarva-asti-vādāḥ | iṣṭāḥ | catur-vidhāḥ
```

| Form | Morphology | Contextual sense |
|---|---|---|
| `sarva-kāla-astitā` | nominative singular feminine compound | existence in all times |
| `ukta-tvāt` | ablative singular abstract noun | because it has been stated |
| `dvayāt` | ablative singular numeral | because of the pair [faculty and object] |
| `sat-viṣayāt` | ablative singular compound | because cognition has an existent object |
| `phalāt` | ablative singular | because of fruit or result |
| `tat-asti-vādāt` | ablative singular compound | because of asserting “that exists” or “all exists” |
| `sarva-asti-vādāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | proponents of the doctrine that all exists |
| `iṣṭāḥ` | nominative plural masculine past participle | accepted, admitted, recognized |
| `catur-vidhāḥ` | nominative plural masculine | fourfold, of four kinds |

The Bhāṣya resolves the compressed `dvaya` as the perceptual-cognitive pair:
eye and forms through mind and dharmas. Cognition arises dependent upon these
two sides.

## 4. Grammar

**Four causal ablatives**

```text
sarva-kāla-astitā [iṣṭā]
    uktatvāt
    dvayāt
    sat-viṣayāt
    phalāt

existence in all times is maintained
    because it has been stated,
    because of the pair,
    because cognition has an existent object,
    and because of fruit
```

The four ablatives give grounds for the thesis. The Bhāṣya groups the first two
under scriptural authority (`āgama`) and the latter two under reasoning
(`yukti`).

**Doctrinal naming and division**

```text
tat-asti-vādāt
sarvāstivādāḥ [iti] iṣṭāḥ
[te] catur-vidhāḥ

because they assert that all exists,
they are recognized as Sarvāstivādins;
they are fourfold
```

The verse announces but does not enumerate the four positions. Their distinct
principles of temporal differentiation follow in VAK 5.26.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

> Existence in all times [is maintained] because it has been stated, because
> of the pair, because cognition has an existent object, and because of fruit.
> From the assertion that all exists, Sarvāstivādins are recognized; they are
> accepted as fourfold.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

> The existence of dharmas in all three times is maintained on four grounds:
> scripture states the existence of past and future dharmas; cognition arises
> dependent upon the pair of faculty and object; cognition requires an existent
> object; and past karma must remain capable of producing future maturation.
> Those who affirm the existence of past, present, and future are called
> Sarvāstivādins, of whom four interpretations are admitted.

The division into scriptural and rational grounds, the identity of `dvaya`,
and the karmic argument are supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## 6. Philosophical Translation

> Past and future cannot be sheer nonbeing if they can enter real relations of
> knowledge, bondage, and fruition. Yet their existence cannot be identical
> with unchanging persistence, or conditioned dharmas would become eternal.
> Temporal being must therefore be sufficient for objective reference and
> causal efficacy while remaining internally differentiated as past, present,
> and future. The common thesis establishes the problem; four doctrines will
> attempt to determine the difference of its temporal modes.

The dilemma is exact:

```text
past and future are nothing
    → cognition, binding, and fruition lose their relata

past, present, and future exist identically
    → conditioned formations appear permanent

required middle
    → sufficient being with real temporal differentiation
```

The word `asti` does not solve the contradiction; it makes determination of
temporal difference necessary.

## 7. Technical Vocabulary

**`Sarvakālāstitā` — existence in all times**

The Vaibhāṣika claim that past, present, and future dharmas possess sufficient
existence for the relations attributed to them. It is explicitly distinguished
from the permanence of conditioned formations.

**`Saṃskṛta-lakṣaṇa` — marks of the conditioned**

The Bhāṣya invokes these marks to deny that three-time existence entails
eternity. The precise account of how temporal difference is constituted remains
the problem for the four positions.

**`Uktatvāt` — because it has been stated**

The first scriptural ground. The cited teaching connects the noble disciple's
non-attachment toward past form and non-delight in future form with the
existence of those temporal objects.

**`Dvaya` — the pair of faculty and object**

The second scriptural ground. Cognition arises dependent upon eye and form,
and so on through mind and dharmas. Mental cognition of past and future would
lose the object-side of this pair if those dharmas were absolutely nonexistent.

**`Sadviṣaya` — having an existent object**

The first rational ground. The Bhāṣya states that cognition operates when its
object exists, not when there is no object. `Ālambana` must have sufficient
being to enter the cognitive relation.

**`Phala` — fruit or result**

The second rational ground. When future karmic maturation arises, its past
`vipākahetu` is no longer present. If the past were sheer nonbeing, the causal
continuity from wholesome or unwholesome action to fruit would become
unintelligible.

**`Sarvāstivādin`**

Defined locally as one who asserts the existence of past, present, and future.
The name should not automatically import every doctrine historically associated
with the school.

**`Vibhajyavādin`**

The Bhāṣya contrasts a position affirming the present and past karma whose
fruit has not yet occurred, while denying exhausted past karma and the future.
This discriminating temporal thesis is not one of the four Sarvāstivāda
positions announced for the next verse.

## 8. Logical Determination

The preceding operation generates an ontological requirement:

```text
RealRelation(r, x, y)
    → SufficientBeingForRelation(x)
      AND SufficientBeingForRelation(y)
```

Applied to cognition:

```text
ArisesDependentOn(Cognition, Faculty, Object)
AND Cognizes(Cognition, PastOrFutureDharma)
    → SufficientlyExistsAsObject(PastOrFutureDharma)
```

Applied to karma:

```text
PastKarma(k)
AND Produces(k, FutureMaturation(f))
    → SufficientlyExistsForCausalEfficacy(k)
```

But:

```text
ExistsAcrossThreeTimes(x)
    ⇏ UnchangingPermanentSubstance(x)
```

The common thesis therefore leaves an unsolved determination:

```text
SameDharmaAcrossTimes(x)
AND ConditionedAndImpermanent(x)
    → RequiresPrincipleOfTemporalDifference(x)
```

VAK 5.26 supplies four candidate principles.

## 9. Interpretive Note

VAK 5.25 is a remarkable confirmation of the fourfold architecture. The
Bhāṣya does not present ontology as a detached theory of what “exists.” It is
forced by two concrete systems of relation:

```text
faculty + object
    → cognition

past karmic cause + future fruit
    → temporal efficacy
```

Functionally, these are the two pairs we have been tracking:

```text
āśraya
    the faculty or mind-side supporting the cognitive event

ālambana
    the existent objective support toward which cognition operates

hetu
    past karma remaining efficacious as maturation-cause

phala
    the future result in which that efficacy becomes actual
```

The term `āśraya` is not enumerated in this kārikā; its Organon function is
identified in the faculty-side of the Bhāṣya's `dvaya`. The other three are
textually explicit or immediately operative. Their unity generates the demand
for temporal being:

```text
āśraya–ālambana
    require sufficient being for objective cognition

hetu–phala
    require sufficient being for causal continuity

their unity
    requires a temporal ontology neither nihilistic nor eternalist
```

This realizes the earlier distinction between paramārthika and vyāvahārika
without separating two worlds. `Hetu–phala` expresses Reason as the inner
necessity by which a determination produces its result. `Āśraya–ālambana`
expresses that same Reason in transactional manifestation as supported
cognition of an objective field. Temporal existence is the Product required
for both relations to be real.

Hegel's contribution, properly understood, is the refusal to choose between
empty disappearance and immutable persistence. The past is no longer present,
yet it is preserved in its determinate efficacy; the future is not presently
actual, yet it can already be objectively and causally determined. This is not
enough to identify the Kośa with Hegel's doctrine of time. It is enough to
recognize the shared logical demand: identity must contain temporal difference
rather than abolish it.

The Path in itself must transform this whole fourfold structure. Aṣṭāṅga
determines the right organization of support, object, cause, and result. The
bhūmis show that organization in appearance as progressively achieved temporal
existence. Liberation is therefore not deletion of the past or fantasy about
the future; it is the actual reorganization of what remains cognitively,
causally, and normatively efficacious.

This is Organon–Yoga–Hegel reconstruction. Its textual ground is the Bhāṣya's
faculty-object `dvaya`, existent-object argument, past-cause/future-fruit
argument, and explicit rejection of permanence as the meaning of three-time
existence.
