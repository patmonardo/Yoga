# VAK 5.48 Bhāṣya — The Ten Envelopments and Their Sources

## Kārikā Anchor

> krodha-mrakṣau ca rāgotthā āhrīkya-auddhatya-matsarāḥ |
> mrakṣe vivādaḥ avidyātaḥ styāna-middha-anapatrapāḥ || 5.48 ||

> Anger and concealment are added. Shamelessness, excitation,
> and avarice arise from attachment; concealment is disputed.
> Torpor, sleepiness, and lack of moral concern arise from
> ignorance.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

According to the Vaibhāṣika method, there are ten
enveloping afflictions: the preceding eight, plus anger and
concealment. Shamelessness and lack of moral concern have
already been explained. Envy is irritation of citta at
another's success. Avarice is a grip of citta that resists
giving Dharma, material goods, or skill. Excitation is the
non-pacification of citta. Remorse and torpor have already
been explained. Sleepiness is a contraction of citta that
leaves the body unable to sustain itself; only afflicted
sleepiness, and likewise afflicted remorse, counts here as
an envelopment. Anger is hostility toward beings or
non-beings apart from ill will and harmfulness. Concealment
is hiding a fault.

Of these ten, shamelessness, excitation, and avarice arise
from attachment; the commentary calls these three
*upakleśas* derivatives (*niḥṣyandas*) of attachment.
Concealment is disputed. Some call it a derivative of
craving, others of ignorance, and others of both. The
compressed qualification mentions known and unknown
faults, without clearly distributing those cases among all
three positions. Torpor, sleepiness, and lack of moral
concern arise from ignorance; these three are called
derivatives of ignorance.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The opening `krodhabhrakṣau ca` completes the Vaibhāṣika
tenfold enumeration announced immediately after 5.47.
It also clarifies why 5.42's proposed criteria failed under
a ten-member comparison class: anger and concealment had
to be considered alongside envy and avarice. The definitions
here are Bhāṣya prose, not words supplied by the Kārikā.

`Niḥṣyanda` supplies the explicit relation of derivation
for two groups of three:

| Stated source | Derivatives |
|---|---|
| Attachment (*rāga*) | Shamelessness, excitation, avarice |
| Ignorance (*avidyā*) | Torpor, sleepiness, lack of moral concern |

This is a particular genetic analysis, beyond the broad
functional inclusion under *upakleśa* established in 5.46.
It does not make “derived from exactly one kleśa” the
definition of every upakleśa. Concealment is identified by
its act of hiding a fault, yet its source remains contested:
craving, ignorance, or both are reported. The source's
`yathā kramaṃ jñātājñātānām` relates the disagreement to
known and unknown cases, but the available wording is too
compressed to assign every case securely.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The manuscript-like e-text's
`bhrakṣa` is retained in continuous Sanskrit while
“concealment” follows the local definition
`avadyapracchādana`. The phrase defining `krodha` is terse;
“hostility” renders `āghāta` without adding an unstated
psychological mechanism. `Niḥṣyanda` is translated as
“derivative,” with “outflow” kept as a possible literal
gloss. The three views of concealment's source remain
separate reported positions.

**Organon reconstruction.** The identity and local
definition of an affliction can be recorded even when its
ground is disputed. A genetic claim must carry its source,
relation (`niḥṣyanda`), and attribution. This is a
project-level way to preserve the Bhāṣya's explicit
disagreement, separate from a completed theory of those
afflictions or any implemented graph.
