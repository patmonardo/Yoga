# VAK 5.11 Bhāṣya — View-Nourished Afflictions

## Kārikā Anchor

> vibhavecchā na cāryasya saṃbhavanti vidhādayaḥ |
> nāsmitā dṛṣṭipuṣṭatvāt kaukṛtyaṃ nāpi cāśubham || 5.11 ||

> The wish for nonexistence, the conceit-modes, and I-am conceit do
> not arise in a noble practitioner, because they are nourished by
> views; nor does unwholesome remorse arise.

The verse continues the `tathā` at the end of 5.10. Its problem is
why something classed as cultivation-abandonable need not manifest
in a noble practitioner.

## Continuous Conventional Translation

“The wish for nonexistence.” Thirst for nonexistence too is to be
abandoned through cultivation. What is the dharma here called
`vibhava`? It is impermanence in the three domains. Longing for
that is thirst for nonexistence. The earlier word “likewise” also
includes a particular form of thirst for existence, such as the
wish, “If only I might become Airāvaṇa, king of elephants.” The
modes of conceit and I-am conceit were likewise said to be
abandoned through cultivation.

Yet “the modes and those beginning with them do not arise in a
noble practitioner; nor does I-am conceit.” The word “beginning”
extends the series as far as thirst for nonexistence. Why do
these not manifest even though they have not yet been abandoned?
“Because they are nourished by views.” The conceit-modes and
I-am conceit are nourished by satkāya-view. With that support
broken, they lack the strength to arise again. The afflictive
occupation beginning with killing is nourished by false view;
thirst for nonexistence by annihilationist view; and this
particular thirst for existence by eternalist view.

“Nor does unwholesome remorse arise.” Unwholesome remorse is
also classed as to be abandoned through cultivation, yet it does
not occur in the noble practitioner because it arises from doubt.

## Argument and Doctrinal Determination

The decisive relation is `dṛṣṭipuṣṭatva`, being nourished by a
view. The commentary specifies four dependencies rather than
one vague influence:

| Nourishing view | Dependent formation |
|---|---|
| Satkāya-view | Conceit-modes and I-am conceit |
| False view | Afflictive occupation beginning with killing |
| Annihilationist view | Thirst for nonexistence |
| Eternalist view | A particular thirst for existence |

Unwholesome remorse is separately said to arise from doubt. The
image `bhagnapṛṣṭhatva`, having its back broken, names the loss of
support once the nourishing view has been removed.

The technical distinction is between **classification for
abandonment** and **present capacity to manifest**. These
formations can remain `bhāvanāprahātavya` while no longer arising
in an ārya because the view on which their manifestation depends
has been abandoned through seeing. The text does not equate an
unabandoned class with a currently operative event.

## Translation and Organon Study

**Translation decisions.** The final `na ... aśubham` negates the
occurrence of **unwholesome remorse**; it does not deny that
remorse can be unwholesome. `Vibhava` is given a local definition
as three-domain impermanence; it should not simply be translated
as liberation. `Bhavatṛṣṇāyāḥ pradeśaḥ` means a particular
portion or instance of thirst for existence, not every form of
desiring existence. The Airāvaṇa example fixes that range.

**Organon reconstruction.** The passage separates a formation's
classificatory status, the view that sustains it, and its present
manifestation. This is a stronger relation than a single
present/absent flag. It can inform our Science of Citta while the
Kośa's exact dependency claims remain distinct from any later
formal or executable representation.
