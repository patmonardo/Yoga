# VAK_1.07 Bhāṣya

## Conditioned Dharmas and Their Synonyms

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.07.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya refines or corrects the first-pass translation, the decision is recorded here for a later Kārikā revision pass.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> te punaḥ saṃskṛtā dharmā rūpādiskandhapañcakam /
>
> sa evādhvā kathāvastu saniḥsārāḥ savastukāḥ // VAkK_1.07 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Those conditioned dharmas are the five aggregates beginning with material form. Those very same conditioned dharmas are the temporal courses, the basis of discourse, possessed of an exit, and possessed of a cause.

The Bhāṣya treats the second half as a series of alternative designations for the same conditioned dharmas:

```text
saṃskṛta-dharmas
    = the five aggregates
    = temporal courses
    = basis of discourse
    = possessed of an exit
    = possessed of a cause
```

The final two expressions require special care. `saniḥsāra` does not mean merely that conditioned things occur within a general “release-context.” It means that nirvāṇa is their exit. `savastuka`, according to the explicitly marked Vaibhāṣika explanation, means that they possess causes, since `vastu` is being used in the sense of `hetu`.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question raised from VAK 1.04:

> *yattūktaṃ “saṃskṛtā mārgavarjitāḥ sāsravā” iti katame te saṃskṛtāḥ?*

> As for the statement, “the conditioned dharmas, excluding the Path, are with outflows”: which are those conditioned dharmas?

It continues through the explanation of the five aggregates, the definition of `saṃskṛta`, and the four alternative names given in the second half of the Kārikā.

The argument proceeds as follows:

```text
Which dharmas are conditioned?
    ↓
The five aggregates beginning with material form
    ↓
Why are they called conditioned?
    ↓
They are made through the convergence of conditions
    ↓
Why can future dharmas also be called conditioned?
    ↓
Because they belong to the same kind
    ↓
What further names apply to this same field?
    ↓
temporal courses
basis of discourse
possessed of an exit
possessed of a cause
```

This is one continuous definitional unit. The synonyms do not introduce additional classes of dharmas; they disclose different relations belonging to the same conditioned field.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. The five aggregates are the conditioned dharmas

> *te punaḥ saṃskṛtā dharmā rūpādiskandhapañcakam.*

The Bhāṣya expands:

> *rūpaskandho vedanāskandhaḥ saṃskāraskandho vijñānaskandhaścetyete saṃskṛtā dharmāḥ.*

### Translation

> The aggregate of material form, the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of formations, and the aggregate of knowledge—these are the conditioned dharmas.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The transmitted electronic text appears to omit `saṃjñāskandha` from this prose enumeration, even though the Kārikā explicitly says “the five aggregates” and the standard fivefold list necessarily includes recognition (`saṃjñā`). We should not silently repair the source text, but the doctrinal structure is unambiguous:

```text
rūpa
vedanā
saṃjñā
saṃskāra
vijñāna
```

The main determination is that the five aggregates exhaust the conditioned field under aggregate-description. This is broader than a merely psychological account. Material form, feeling, recognition, formations, and knowledge together articulate conditioned existence.

---

### B. Conditioned means made through converging conditions

> *sametya saṃbhūya pratyayaiḥ kṛtā iti saṃskṛtāḥ.*

### Translation

> They are called conditioned because they are made by conditions coming together and arising together.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya explains `saṃskṛta` through a genuinely relational genesis:

```text
conditions converge
    ↓
conditions function together
    ↓
a conditioned dharma is produced
```

The two verbal forms reinforce one another:

- **sametya** — having come together;
- **saṃbhūya** — having arisen or become together.

A conditioned dharma is therefore not simply something that happens to possess conditions. Its existence is constituted through the cooperative convergence of conditions.

---

### C. Nothing is produced by a single condition

> *na hy ekapratyayajanitaṃ kiñcid asti.*

### Translation

> For there is nothing produced by a single condition.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This short sentence gives the strongest causal principle in the unit:

```text
no conditioned dharma
    is generated by
one isolated condition
```

The Bhāṣya does not define conditioned existence through a simple one-to-one causal relation. Production is necessarily plural and conjunctive.

This should restrain any later translation that makes `saṃskṛta` sound merely “manufactured” or externally assembled. The primary point is conditioned co-production:

```text
plural conditions
    → coordinated production
    → conditioned dharma
```

The determination is important for the later causal analysis of the Kośa. Even before the detailed classifications of grounds and conditions, Vasubandhu establishes that conditioned production is structurally multiple.

---

### D. Why future dharmas are also conditioned

> *tajjātīyatvād anāgateṣv avirodho dugdhendhanavat.*

### Translation

> There is no contradiction in applying the term to future dharmas, because they belong to the same kind, as with milk and fuel.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

An obvious difficulty arises. If a conditioned dharma is something made by converging conditions, how can a future dharma—which has not yet been produced—already be called conditioned?

The Bhāṣya answers through **belonging to the same kind**:

```text
presently produced conditioned dharma
    and
future conditionable dharma
        ↓
belong to the same causal kind
```

The examples of milk and fuel indicate ordinary prospective naming. Milk can be spoken of in relation to the curd it will become, and fuel in relation to the fire it will sustain or produce, because each bears a determinate capacity and belongs to the relevant causal kind.

The precise force of the examples is compressed, so the details should remain provisional. The clear doctrinal point is that the designation `saṃskṛta` includes not only what is presently produced but also future dharmas whose mode of arising belongs to conditioned production.

## 4. The Temporal Courses

> *ta eva saṃskṛtā gatagacchadgamiṣyadbhāvād adhvānaḥ, adyante 'nitayatyeti vā.*

### Translation

> Those very conditioned dharmas are called the temporal courses because they have the states of having gone, going, and being about to go; or because impermanence consumes them.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya offers two explanations of `adhvan`.

### First explanation: the three temporal states

```text
gata
    gone — past

gacchat
    going — present

gamiṣyat
    going to go — future
```

Conditioned dharmas are the temporal courses because they are distributed across past, present, and future modes.

The word does not refer to an independent container called time. The temporal courses are the conditioned dharmas themselves under temporal determination.

### Second explanation: what impermanence consumes

The transmitted text is somewhat unstable here, but the intended explanation is that the conditioned dharmas are called temporal courses because impermanence travels through them or consumes them.

The governing point remains:

```text
time is not added externally to conditioned dharmas

conditioned dharmas themselves
    are temporally articulated
    and subject to impermanence
```

This is more exact than translating `adhvā` simply as “temporal course.” The Bhāṣya identifies the courses with the past, present, and future conditioned dharmas themselves.

## 5. The Basis of Discourse

> *kathā vākyam, tasyā vastu nāma.*

### Translation

> Discourse is speech; that which is its basis is called the basis of discourse.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya then restricts the expression:

> *sārthakavastugrahaṇāt tu saṃskṛtaṃ kathāvastūcyate.*

### Translation

> Because meaningful discourse takes conditioned things as its basis, the conditioned is called the basis of discourse.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The explanation is not that all language refers only to conditioned things in every possible sense. The expression concerns meaningful, doctrinally valid discourse:

```text
meaningful speech
    requires a basis
        ↓
conditioned dharmas provide that basis
```

The Bhāṣya cites an Abhidharma statement:

> *kathāvastūny aṣṭādaśabhir dhātubhiḥ saṃgṛhītāni.*

> The bases of discourse are included within the eighteen domains.

The citation prevents an overly narrow reading. The basis of discourse is not merely the five aggregates considered verbally. It is the analyzable conditioned field as subsequently articulated through the eighteen domains.

The Bhāṣya’s wording also suggests that `kathāvastu` should not be translated as “topic of conversation” in a casual sense. It means the real doctrinal basis upon which meaningful predication and analysis proceed.

## 6. Possessed of an Exit

> *niḥsaraṇaṃ niḥsāraḥ sarvasya saṃskṛtasya nirvāṇam.*

### Translation

> The exit—the going out—from all conditioned existence is nirvāṇa.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> *tad eṣām astīti saniḥsārāḥ.*

### Translation

> Because they possess that exit, they are called “possessed of an exit.”

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is an important correction to the first-pass rendering. `saniḥsāra` does not mean merely “having a release-context” or “including a process of going out.” It states a determinate relation:

```text
conditioned dharmas
    have nirvāṇa
        as their exit
```

The expression does not make nirvāṇa one more conditioned property contained inside them. Rather, conditioned existence is that from which an exit is possible and necessary, and nirvāṇa is that exit.

The relation is asymmetric:

```text
conditioned field
    → has an exit

nirvāṇa
    → is the exit from the conditioned field
```

The Bhāṣya therefore builds liberation into the description of conditioned existence itself. The conditioned is not only produced and temporal; it is also that from which there is release.

## 7. Possessed of a Cause

> *sahetukatvāt savastukāḥ.*

### Translation

> They are called “possessed of a basis” because they possess causes.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu reporting a doctrinal explanation

The Bhāṣya immediately specifies:

> *hetuvacanaḥ kila vastuśabda iti vaibhāṣikāḥ.*

### Translation

> The Vaibhāṣikas say that the word `vastu` here expresses “cause.”

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position

This requires a substantial correction to the first-pass interpretation. The first-pass file rendered `savastuka` as “with real basis” and treated it as a claim that conditioned dharmas are analyzably real. The Bhāṣya does not directly make that claim here.

The explicit derivation is:

```text
savastuka
    = possessing vastu

vastu, in this passage
    = cause

therefore

savastuka
    = possessing causes
```

The particle `kila` and the closing attribution `iti vaibhāṣikāḥ` mark this as a Vaibhāṣika explanation. We should therefore preserve the voice distinction:

- the Kārikā calls conditioned dharmas `savastuka`;
- the Vaibhāṣikas explain `vastu` here as a word for cause;
- the Bhāṣya records that explanation without yet requiring us to make it the project’s universal translation of `vastu`.

The local translation should accordingly be “possessed of a cause” or “having causes.” The broader canonical translation `vastu = real thing` remains relevant elsewhere but should not override this passage-specific derivation.

## 8. Synonyms of Conditioned Dharmas

> *ity ete saṃskṛtadharmaparyāyāḥ.*

### Translation

> These are alternative designations of conditioned dharmas.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya closes the unit by making the status of the terms explicit. They are not separate substances or subdivisions:

```text
five aggregates
adhvan
kathāvastu
saniḥsāra
savastuka
```

are different descriptions of the same conditioned field.

Each description answers a different question:

| Designation | What it discloses |
|---|---|
| five aggregates | the structural composition of conditioned dharmas |
| temporal courses | their past, present, and future articulation |
| basis of discourse | their role as the basis of meaningful doctrinal speech |
| possessed of an exit | their relation to nirvāṇa |
| possessed of a cause | their causal dependence |

The Bhāṣya therefore moves from simple enumeration to a compact reflective vocabulary of conditioned existence.

## 9. Argument Summary

```text
Conditioned dharmas are the five aggregates.
        ↓
They are conditioned because plural conditions converge in their production.
        ↓
Nothing conditioned is produced by one isolated condition.
        ↓
Future dharmas also receive the designation
because they belong to the same causal kind.
        ↓
The same conditioned dharmas are the temporal courses
because they occur as past, present, and future
and are governed by impermanence.
        ↓
They are the basis of discourse
because meaningful doctrinal speech is grounded in them.
        ↓
They possess an exit
because nirvāṇa is the exit from all conditioned existence.
        ↓
They possess causes;
the Vaibhāṣikas explain vastu here as a word for cause.
```

The unity of the passage is:

```text
conditioned existence
    = aggregate structure
    = plural causal production
    = temporal articulation
    = basis of meaningful discourse
    = field from which nirvāṇa is the exit
```

## 10. New Technical Terms

### **sametya**

- **Provisional meaning:** having come together; through convergence
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains the cooperative causal structure of conditioned production

### **saṃbhūya**

- **Provisional meaning:** having arisen or become together
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** reinforces that conditions jointly produce the conditioned dharma

### **ekapratyayajanita**

- **Provisional meaning:** generated by one condition
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** rejected causal model; nothing conditioned is produced by one isolated condition

### **tajjātīyatva**

- **Provisional meaning:** belonging to that same kind
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains why future dharmas may already be designated conditioned

### **gatagacchadgamiṣyadbhāva**

- **Provisional meaning:** the states of having gone, going, and being about to go
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains `adhvan` through past, present, and future

### **sārthaka**

- **Provisional meaning:** meaningful; possessing meaning
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** restricts `kathā` to meaningful discourse grounded in conditioned dharmas

### **niḥsaraṇa**

- **Provisional meaning:** exit, departure, going out
- **Status:** related to `niḥsāra` in the Kārikā but introduced explicitly in the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** defines nirvāṇa as the exit from all conditioned existence

### **sahetuka**

- **Provisional meaning:** possessing a cause or causes
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains the Vaibhāṣika reading of `savastuka`

### **paryāya**

- **Provisional meaning:** alternative designation, synonym, doctrinal equivalent
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** identifies the several terms as descriptions of the same conditioned dharmas

## 12. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass translation is broadly correct in identifying the five aggregates and the synonym structure, but three revisions should be recorded.

### A. `adhvā`

Current:

```text
temporal course
```

Bhāṣya-informed refinement:

```text
the temporal courses
    = conditioned dharmas as past, present, and future
```

The translation “temporal course” may remain, but the note should make clear that the temporal courses are not an external time-container.

### B. `saniḥsārāḥ`

Current:

```text
with a process of going-out / release-context
```

Bhāṣya-supported:

```text
possessed of an exit
    because nirvāṇa is the exit from all conditioned existence
```

### C. `savastukāḥ`

Current:

```text
with real basis
```

Bhāṣya-supported local translation:

```text
possessed of a cause / having causes
```

This is explicitly the Vaibhāṣika explanation:

```text
vastu here expresses hetu
```

The Kārikā file should remain unchanged until the later revision pass, but this correction should be treated as strong.

## 13. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya presents conditioned existence as a reciprocally articulated field rather than a collection of isolated items:

```text
plural conditions
    produce
aggregate existence

aggregate existence
    appears as
past, present, and future

that temporal field
    becomes
basis of meaningful discourse

and the whole conditioned field
    stands in relation to
nirvāṇa as its exit
```

The important result is not merely that conditioned dharmas have several labels. Each label reflects the same field through a different determination:

```text
causal
structural
temporal
linguistic
soteriological
```

This is a genuine development in the Bhāṣya study: the prose is beginning to show how the compact Kārikā categories unfold into a vocabulary of reflection. The same conditioned dharmas are successively determined through their genesis, temporal life, intelligibility, and release.

This remains a provisional Organon contact point. The primary textual conclusion is simpler and firmer:

```text
Conditioned dharmas are jointly produced,
temporally articulated,
meaningfully discussable,
causally grounded,
and possessed of nirvāṇa as their exit.
```
