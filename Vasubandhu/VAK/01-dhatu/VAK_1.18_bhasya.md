# VAK_1.18 — Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sarvasaṃgraha ekena skandhenāyatanena ca /
> dhātunā ca svabhāvena parabhāvaviyogataḥ // VAkK_1.18 //

The Kārikā states that all dharmas are included by one aggregate, one sphere, and one domain, and that such inclusion is determined by intrinsic nature rather than by the nature of something else.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the summary of the three classification systems and extends through the distinction between strict inclusion and conventional grouping:

> tatra skandhaiḥ sarvasaṃskṛtasaṃgrahaḥ /
> upādānaskandhaiḥ sarvasāsravāṇām āyatanadhātubhiḥ sarvadharmāṇām /
> samāsatastu jñātavyaḥ —
>
> sarvasaṃgraha ekena skandhenāyatanena ca /
> dhātunā ca
>
> rūpaskandhena mana āyatanena dharmadhātunā ca sarvadharmāṇāṃ saṃgraho boddhavyaḥ /
> sa khalveṣa saṃgraho yatra kvaciducyamāno veditavyaḥ /
>
> svabhāvena
>
> na parabhāvena / kiṃ kāraṇam /
>
> parabhāvaviyogataḥ // VAkK_1.18 //
>
> viyukto hi parabhāvena dharmaḥ /
> tasmānna yena viyuktastenaiva saṃgṛhīto yujyate /
> tadyathā cakṣurindriyaṃ rūpaskandhena cakṣurāyatanadhātubhyāṃ ca duḥkhasamudayasatyābhyāṃ ca saṃgṛhītam /
> tatsvabhāvatvāt /
> nānyaiḥ skandhādibhistadbhāvaviyuktatvāt /
> yastvanyenānyasya saṃgraha ucyate yathā saṃgrahavastubhiḥ parṣadāṃ sa hi kādācitkatvāt sāṃketiko veditavyaḥ /

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

By the aggregates, all conditioned dharmas are included. By the aggregates of appropriation, all dharmas with outflows are included. By the spheres and domains, all dharmas are included.

In summary, all dharmas are to be understood as included by one aggregate, one sphere, and one domain: by the rūpaskandha, the mana-āyatana, and the dharmadhātu.

Wherever this inclusion is spoken of, it is to be understood as inclusion by intrinsic nature, not by the nature of something else. Why? Because a dharma is separated from another's nature. Therefore it is not reasonable for something to be included by that very nature from which it is distinct.

For example, the eye faculty is included in the rūpaskandha, the eye-sphere, the eye-domain, and the truths of suffering and origin, because those are its own determinations. It is not included in the other aggregates, spheres, or domains, because it is distinct from their nature.

When one thing is said to include another in a different sense—for example, when an assembly is gathered by the means of attraction—this is occasional and conventional inclusion.

### 3.1 Three scopes of inclusion

The Bhāṣya first distinguishes the range of the three major classifications:

```text
skandhas
    include all conditioned dharmas

upādānaskandhas
    include all sāsrava dharmas

āyatanas and dhātus
    include all dharmas
```

The scope widens step by step. The five skandhas do not include the unconditioned, while the twelve āyatanas and eighteen dhātus do.

### 3.2 Why one member from each system can summarize all dharmas

Vasubandhu identifies one especially comprehensive member within each classification:

```text
rūpaskandha
mana-āyatana
dharmadhātu
```

This does not mean that each of these alone is numerically identical with every dharma. It means that, in the compressed cross-mapping of the systems, these three are the relevant comprehensive loci:

- the rūpaskandha gathers the material side;
- the mana-āyatana gathers the vijñānaskandha;
- the dharmadhātu gathers the remaining mental factors, avijñapti, and the unconditioned.

Together they exhaust the entire dharma-field.

### 3.3 Strict inclusion is inclusion by svabhāva

The central rule is:

```text
saṃgraha
    = inclusion by svabhāva
    ≠ inclusion by parabhāva
```

A dharma belongs to a classification because that classification expresses what it is, not because it is externally associated with something else.

The eye faculty belongs to:

```text
rūpaskandha
cakṣurāyatana
cakṣurdhātu
duḥkhasatya
samudayasatya
```

because these classifications state determinations intrinsic to that dharma.

### 3.4 Conventional inclusion is secondary

The Bhāṣya contrasts strict Abhidharma inclusion with a looser conventional use of `saṃgraha`.

An assembly may be "included" or gathered by generosity, pleasant speech, beneficial action, and impartiality—the four means of attraction. But this gathering is:

```text
occasional
relational
conventional
```

It does not express intrinsic nature.

This distinction prevents classificatory ambiguity. Abhidharma taxonomy is not based on accidental association, practical grouping, or rhetorical convenience.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
all conditioned dharmas
    → included by skandhas

all sāsrava dharmas
    → included by upādānaskandhas

all dharmas
    → included by āyatanas and dhātus
```

In compressed form:

```text
rūpaskandha
+ mana-āyatana
+ dharmadhātu
    = exhaustive inclusion of all dharmas
```

The governing rule is:

```text
strict inclusion
    = inclusion by intrinsic nature

not:
    external relation
    temporary association
    conventional gathering
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### saṃgraha

- **Provisional meaning:** inclusion, containment within a classification
- **Status:** central technical term
- **Function:** states how a dharma belongs within the skandha, āyatana, dhātu, or truth systems

### svabhāva

- **Provisional meaning:** intrinsic nature; the determination by which a dharma is what it is
- **Status:** technical doctrinal term
- **Function:** supplies the strict basis of classificatory inclusion

### parabhāva

- **Provisional meaning:** another's nature; a nature belonging to something else
- **Status:** contrast term
- **Function:** marks what cannot ground strict inclusion

### parabhāvaviyoga

- **Provisional meaning:** separation from another's nature
- **Status:** argumentative principle
- **Function:** explains why a dharma cannot be classified through a nature alien to it

### sāṃketika

- **Provisional meaning:** conventional, based on designation or practical usage
- **Status:** secondary mode of inclusion
- **Function:** distinguishes ordinary or occasional grouping from strict Abhidharma classification

### kādācitka

- **Provisional meaning:** occasional, contingent
- **Status:** qualifier of conventional inclusion
- **Function:** shows that external gathering does not express permanent classificatory identity

## 6. Voice Register

- **Vasubandhu:** establishes the scope of skandha, upādānaskandha, āyatana, and dhātu inclusion.
- **Vasubandhu:** defines strict inclusion through `svabhāva` and excludes `parabhāva`.
- **Illustrative conventional usage:** the gathering of an assembly through the means of attraction.
- No explicit objector or separately marked Vaibhāṣika position appears in this unit.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

**Decision: refine.**

The earlier translation should make `saṃgraha` explicitly classificatory and `svabhāvena` explicitly intrinsic:

> All dharmas are included by one aggregate, one sphere, and one domain—by intrinsic nature, because they are distinct from the nature of what is other.

The Bhāṣya also shows that `parabhāvaviyogataḥ` is not a general metaphysical claim of absolute isolation. It has a precise classificatory function: a dharma cannot be strictly included under a nature that is not its own.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya confirms that the Kośa classification is not merely an inventory. It is a system of intrinsic domain-membership.

```text
strict domain-membership
    = grounded in the determination of the dharma itself
```

This supports an Organon Light reading of dhātus as rigorously determined domains. However, the present passage concerns **classification by svabhāva**, not yet a claim that every dhātu is reducible to Vijñānaskandha or to a supersensible unity of knowledge. That stronger interpretation remains a later comparative construction.
