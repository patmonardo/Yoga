# VAK_1.26 Bhāṣya

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> śāstrapramāṇa ity eke skandhādīnāṃ kathaikaśaḥ /
>
> caritapratipakṣas tu dharmaskandho 'nuvarṇitaḥ // VAkK_1.26 //

The completed Kārikā analysis correctly identified three possible ways of measuring a `dharmaskandha`: by textual extent, by doctrinal topic, or by its practical function as an antidote to a disposition. The Bhāṣya supplies the exact competing positions and makes the third interpretation more precise.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question left by VAK 1.25:

> kiṃ punar dharmaskandhasya pramāṇam /

### Translation

> But what is the measure of a Dharma-collection?

It then presents three explanations:

1. the measure of a particular Abhidharma treatise;
2. one doctrinal exposition for each major topic;
3. one antidotal teaching for each type of disposition.

The unit ends by explaining the eighty thousand Dharma-collections through the eighty thousand dispositions of beings and the corresponding antidotes taught by the Bhagavat.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 First view: measurement by treatise

> śāstrapramāṇa ity eke /
>
> eke tāvad āhur dharmaskandhasaṃjñakasyaivābhidharmaśāstrasyāsya pramāṇam iti /
>
> tac ca ṣaṭsahasrāṇi /

### Translation

> Some say that its measure is the measure of a treatise. Certain teachers say that the measure is precisely that of this Abhidharma treatise called the *Dharmaskandha*; and that consists of six thousand [verses or textual units].

**Voice:** Reported opinion of some teachers.

This interpretation treats `dharmaskandha` quantitatively. A single Dharma-collection is measured by the textual extent of the Abhidharma work bearing that title.

The source text transmitted here contains a likely omission or corruption in `opramaṇam`; the syntax and context require `pramāṇam`.

### 3.2 Second view: one collection for each doctrinal exposition

> apare punar āhuḥ /
>
> skandhādīnāṃ kathaikaśaḥ /

### Translation

> Others, however, say: one exposition for each of the aggregates and the other topics.

The Bhāṣya then lists examples:

> skandhāyatana-dhātu-pratītyasamutpāda-satya-āhāra-dhyāna-apramāṇa-ārūpya-vimokṣa-abhibhvāyatana-kṛtsnāyatana-bodhipākṣika-abhijñā-pratisaṃvit-praṇidhi-jñāna-araṇādināṃ kathā pratyekaṃ dharmaskandha iti /

### Translation

> An exposition of each topic—the aggregates, spheres, domains, dependent origination, truths, nutriments, meditations, immeasurables, formless attainments, liberations, masteries, totalities, factors conducive to awakening, superknowledges, discriminations, aspirations, knowledges, freedom from conflict, and so forth—is a Dharma-collection.

**Voice:** A second reported opinion.

Here `dharmaskandha` is neither a fixed number of syllables nor merely a named book. It is a coherent doctrinal treatment of one subject.

This is a thematic measure:

```text
distinct doctrinal topic
    → distinct exposition
    → one dharmaskandha
```

### 3.3 Third view: an antidote matched to a disposition

> caritapratipakṣas tu dharmaskandho 'nuvarṇitaḥ /

### Translation

> But a Dharma-collection is also described as the antidote corresponding to a disposition.

The particle `tu` marks this explanation against the preceding measurements. The Bhāṣya continues:

> evaṃ tu varṇayanty aśītiś caritasahasrāṇi sattvānām /
>
> rāgadveṣamohamānādicaritabhedena /

### Translation

> Those who explain it in this way say that beings possess eighty thousand dispositions, differentiated as dispositions dominated by attachment, hatred, delusion, pride, and so forth.

> teṣāṃ pratipakṣeṇa bhagavatā 'śītirdharmaskandhasahasrāṇy uktāni /

### Translation

> As antidotes to these, the Bhagavat taught eighty thousand Dharma-collections.

**Voice:** Reported doctrinal explanation, presented as the interpretation matching the traditional count.

This account explains the number through a correspondence:

```text
eighty thousand dispositions of beings
    ↔
eighty thousand appropriate antidotes
    ↔
eighty thousand Dharma-collections
```

The Dharma-collection is therefore individuated by therapeutic function. Its unity is determined by the defilement-pattern it counters.

### 3.4 Relation back to the five aggregates

> yathaitāny aśītirdharmaskandhasahasrāṇy eṣv eva pañcaskandheṣu pratipāditāni /

### Translation

> Just as these eighty thousand Dharma-collections have been assigned within these very five aggregates—

This sentence leads directly into VAK 1.27. The point is that the large traditional inventory does not escape the already established five-aggregate classification. Once the intrinsic characteristic of each item is examined, it can be placed within the basic schemes.

The transmitted source again shows an initial stray `o` in `opratipāditāni`; this is best treated as a textual defect, not as a doctrinal feature.

## 4. Argument Summary

The Bhāṣya gives three rival measures of a `dharmaskandha`:

```text
1. textual measure
   one Dharmaskandha treatise = six thousand units

2. thematic measure
   one coherent exposition of one doctrinal topic
       = one dharmaskandha

3. therapeutic measure
   one antidote corresponding to one disposition
       = one dharmaskandha
```

The third view explains the traditional number eighty thousand:

```text
many modes of attachment, hatred, delusion, pride, etc.
    → many differentiated dispositions
    → corresponding differentiated antidotes
    → eighty thousand Dharma-collections
```

The passage does not require us to choose sides beyond reconstructing the positions. Vasubandhu reports the available explanations and prepares the next verse's general rule of classification.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### `pramāṇa`

- **Provisional meaning:** measure, extent, criterion of counting
- **Status:** General technical term used here in a classificatory question
- **Function:** Asks what makes one `dharmaskandha` one unit

### `śāstrapramāṇa`

- **Provisional meaning:** measure supplied by the extent of a treatise
- **Status:** First reported interpretation
- **Function:** Defines the unit quantitatively by a textual work

### `kathā`

- **Provisional meaning:** doctrinal exposition or treatment
- **Status:** Second reported interpretation
- **Function:** Makes each coherent topic-exposition one Dharma-collection

### `carita`

- **Provisional meaning:** disposition, habitual character-pattern, dominant mode of conduct
- **Status:** Central term in the third interpretation
- **Function:** Identifies the particular afflictive orientation for which a teaching is prescribed

### `pratipakṣa`

- **Provisional meaning:** antidote, counteragent, opposing remedy
- **Status:** Practical-path term
- **Function:** Defines a teaching through its capacity to counter a disposition

### `caritapratipakṣa`

- **Provisional meaning:** antidote corresponding to a disposition
- **Status:** Defining expression for the third account of `dharmaskandha`
- **Function:** Grounds doctrinal differentiation in therapeutic correspondence

## 6. Voice Register

| Passage | Voice |
|---|---|
| `śāstrapramāṇa ity eke` | First reported group of teachers |
| `apare punar āhuḥ` | Second reported group of teachers |
| thematic list of doctrinal expositions | Elaboration of the second view |
| `caritapratipakṣas tu...` | Third traditional explanation, reported by Vasubandhu |
| eighty thousand dispositions and antidotes | Elaboration of the third view |
| transition to inclusion in the five aggregates | Vasubandhu's classificatory transition to VAK 1.27 |

No polemical victory needs to be assigned here. The Bhāṣya's immediate work is to preserve and distinguish the three explanations.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

**Decision:** Refine.

The existing translation captures the therapeutic meaning but makes the first half too compressed and slightly obscures the second reported view.

A more productive translation is:

> Some say that a Dharma-collection is measured by the extent of the treatise; others take each exposition of the aggregates and the other topics as one. But it is also described as an antidote corresponding to a disposition.

This preserves:

- the first reported view;
- the implied `apare` governing `skandhādīnāṃ kathaikaśaḥ`;
- the contrastive `tu`;
- the precise relation expressed by `caritapratipakṣa`.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

No substantial Organon construction is needed here.

At most, the Bhāṣya distinguishes three principles by which a body of knowledge may be individuated:

```text
textual extent
systematic subject matter
practical counter-function
```

That may later be useful when organizing an independent Organon Light study, but the present passage is best allowed to remain a Buddhist classificatory and therapeutic discussion.
