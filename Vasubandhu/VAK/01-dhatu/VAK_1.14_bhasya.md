# VAK_1.14 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> indriyārthās ta eveṣṭā daśāyatanadhātavaḥ /
>
> vedanānubhavaḥ saṃjñā nimittodgrahaṇātmikā // VAkK_1.14 //

### Working Translation

> Those very faculties and objects are accepted as the ten āyatanas and ten dhātus. Vedanā is experience; saṃjñā has the nature of grasping a mark.

The verse performs two operations at once. First, it redescribes the five faculties and five material objects as ten āyatanas and ten dhātus. Second, it begins the definition of the remaining aggregates by defining vedanā and saṃjñā.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

> ya eva rūpaskandhasvabhāvā uktāḥ
>
> indriyārthās ta eveṣṭā daśāyatanadhātavaḥ /
>
> āyatanavyavasthāyāṃ daśāyatanāni / cakṣurāyatanaṃ rūpāyatanaṃ yāvat kāyāyatanaṃ spraṣṭavyāyatanam iti /
>
> dhātuvyavasthāyāṃ ta eva daśa dhātavaś cakṣurdhātū rūpadhātur yāvat kāyadhātuḥ spraṣṭavyadhātur iti /
>
> ukto rūpaskandhas tasya cāyatanavyavasthānam / vedanādayo vaktavyāḥ /
>
> tatra
>
> vedanānubhavaḥ
>
> trividho 'nubhavo vedanāskandhaḥ / sukho duḥkho 'duḥkhāsukhaś ca /
>
> sa punar bhidyamānaḥ ṣaḍ vedanākāyāḥ cakṣuḥsaṃsparśajā vedanā yāvan manaḥsaṃsparśajā vedanā iti /
>
> saṃjñā nimittodgrahaṇātmikā // VAkK_1.14 //
>
> yāvan nīlapītadīrghahrasvastripuruṣamitramitrasukhaduḥkhādinimittodgrahaṇam asau saṃjñāskandhaḥ /
>
> sa punar bhidyamānaḥ ṣaṭsaṃjñākāyā vedanāvat //
>
> caturbhyo 'nye tu saṃskāraskandhaḥ
>
> rūpavedanāsaṃjñāvijñānebhyaś caturbhyo 'nye tu saṃskārāḥ saṃskāraskandhaḥ /
>
> bhagavatā tu sūtre ṣaṭ cetanākāyā ity uktaṃ prādhānyāt /
>
> sā hi karmasvarūpatvād abhisaṃskaraṇe pradhānā /
>
> ata evoktaṃ bhagavatā: “saṃskṛtam abhisaṃskaroti / tasmāt saṃskārā upādānaskandha ity ucyate” iti /
>
> anyathā hi śeṣāṇāṃ caitasikānāṃ viprayuktānāṃ ca saṃskārāṇāṃ skandhāsaṃgrahād duḥkhasamudayasatyatvaṃ na syād iti parijñāparihāṇe api na syātām /
>
> uktaṃ ca bhagavatā: “nāham ekadharmam apy anabhijñāyāparijñāya duḥkhasyāntakriyāṃ vadāmi” iti / evam aprahāyety uktam /
>
> tasmād avaśyam eṣāṃ saṃskāraskandhasaṃgraho 'bhyupagantavyaḥ /

### Translation

> The very things already stated to possess the nature of the rūpaskandha—namely, the faculties and their objects—are accepted as the ten āyatanas and the ten dhātus.
>
> In the arrangement by āyatanas, they are the ten āyatanas: the eye-āyatana and form-āyatana, through the body-āyatana and tangible-āyatana.
>
> In the arrangement by dhātus, those same things are the ten dhātus: the eye-dhātu and form-dhātu, through the body-dhātu and tangible-dhātu.
>
> The rūpaskandha and its arrangement as āyatanas have now been explained. The aggregates beginning with vedanā must next be stated.
>
> Here, vedanā is experience.
>
> The vedanāskandha is threefold experience: pleasant, painful, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant. When further divided, it is the six classes of feeling: feeling born from eye-contact through feeling born from mental contact.
>
> Saṃjñā has the nature of grasping a mark.
>
> The grasping of marks such as blue, yellow, long, short, woman, man, friend, enemy, pleasant, painful, and so forth is the saṃjñāskandha. When further divided, it too consists of six classes, just like vedanā.
>
> The saṃskāraskandha consists of the other saṃskāras apart from the four aggregates of rūpa, vedanā, saṃjñā, and vijñāna.
>
> In the sūtra, however, the Blessed One spoke of the six classes of volition because volition is foremost. Since volition has the nature of karma, it is principal in the formation of conditioned existence.
>
> For this very reason the Blessed One said: “It forms the conditioned; therefore it is called the aggregate of formations subject to appropriation.”
>
> Otherwise, the remaining associated mental factors and the formations dissociated from mind would not be included in any aggregate. They would consequently not count as the truths of suffering and origin, and neither full comprehension nor abandonment would apply to them.
>
> But the Blessed One also said: “I do not declare an end of suffering without directly knowing and fully comprehending even a single dharma,” and likewise without abandoning it.
>
> Therefore these remaining factors must necessarily be accepted as included within the saṃskāraskandha.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 The same material factors under three arrangements

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The opening sentence is exact:

```text
rūpaskandha factors
    = five faculties + five objects

those same factors
    = ten āyatanas
    = ten dhātus
```

The Bhāṣya does not introduce new substances when it moves from skandha to āyatana to dhātu. It reorganizes the same factors under different classificatory schemes.

This is stronger and more precise than saying that one reality is merely viewed from several loose “perspectives.” Each arrangement has its own systematic purpose and count.

### 3.2 Āyatana and dhātu are not interchangeable words

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The text names each series separately:

```text
āyatana arrangement:
    eye-āyatana / form-āyatana
    ...
    body-āyatana / tangible-āyatana

dhātu arrangement:
    eye-dhātu / form-dhātu
    ...
    body-dhātu / tangible-dhātu
```

The same material factors occupy positions in both systems, but the systems are not therefore identical. The present passage establishes correspondence, not synonymy.

This is important for the project’s Knowledge Domain hypothesis. The verse permits us to say that the same factors are articulated as dhātus, but it does not yet say that dhātu simply means a mode of Vijñānaskandha. That stronger thesis remains to be tested against VAK 1.15–1.17.

### 3.3 Vedanā is anubhava

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> vedanānubhavaḥ

Vedanā is defined as **anubhava**, experience or undergoing. The Bhāṣya immediately divides this into three:

```text
pleasant
painful
neither-painful-nor-pleasant
```

Thus vedanā is not generic awareness and not modern “emotion.” It is the hedonic undergoing of contact.

The sixfold division shows that feeling is indexed to the six contacts:

```text
eye-contact-born feeling
...
mind-contact-born feeling
```

Vedanā is therefore both threefold by tone and sixfold by sensory-mental basis.

### 3.4 Saṃjñā is mark-grasping

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> saṃjñā nimittodgrahaṇātmikā

Saṃjñā consists in taking up a **nimitta**, a determinate mark. The examples show the range of this function:

```text
sensory marks:
    blue, yellow

spatial marks:
    long, short

social and personal marks:
    woman, man, friend, enemy

felt or evaluative marks:
    pleasant, painful
```

Saṃjñā is therefore broader than simple visual recognition. It stabilizes an encountered field under a distinguishing mark.

Like vedanā, it has six classes according to the six sensory-mental bases.

### 3.5 The saṃskāraskandha is defined residually but not arbitrarily

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> caturbhyo 'nye tu saṃskāraskandhaḥ

The saṃskāraskandha contains the conditioned factors other than:

```text
rūpa
vedanā
saṃjñā
vijñāna
```

This is a residual definition, but the Bhāṣya does not leave it as a miscellaneous remainder. It explains why the sūtras often speak specifically of the six classes of **cetanā**: volition is principal because it is karma by nature and because it performs **abhisaṃskaraṇa**, the active formation of the conditioned.

The relation is:

```text
cetanā
    = principal saṃskāra
    = karma in its primary mental form
    = chief factor in active formation
```

### 3.6 Why the remaining formations must be included

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The argument is systematic:

```text
If other mental factors and mind-dissociated formations
were excluded from saṃskāraskandha,

then they would belong to no aggregate.

If they belonged to no aggregate,
then they would not be included in suffering and origin.

If they were not included in suffering and origin,
then full comprehension and abandonment would not apply to them.
```

That result would contradict the Buddha’s statement that suffering is not ended without directly knowing, fully comprehending, and abandoning every relevant dharma.

This is not merely a classificatory convenience. Inclusion in an aggregate determines inclusion within the work of the Path.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
The rūpaskandha has already been defined.

Its five faculties and five objects
    = ten āyatanas
    = ten dhātus

The remaining aggregates are then defined:

vedanā
    = threefold hedonic experience
    = sixfold by contact-basis

saṃjñā
    = grasping a determinate mark
    = sixfold by contact-basis

saṃskāraskandha
    = all remaining formations apart from
      rūpa, vedanā, saṃjñā, and vijñāna

cetanā
    = chief among formations
    because it is karma and actively forms the conditioned

all remaining formations must be included
    so that they fall within suffering, origin,
    full comprehension, and abandonment
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### anubhava

- **Provisional meaning:** experience; undergoing
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition of vedanā
- **Function:** identifies feeling as the hedonic undergoing of contact

### nimittodgrahaṇa

- **Provisional meaning:** grasping or taking up a determinate mark
- **Status:** Kārikā and Bhāṣya definition of saṃjñā
- **Function:** explains recognition through the stabilization of a distinguishing feature

### vedanākāya

- **Provisional meaning:** class or group of feeling
- **Status:** sixfold analytical subdivision
- **Function:** correlates feeling with the six kinds of contact

### saṃjñākāya

- **Provisional meaning:** class or group of saṃjñā
- **Status:** sixfold analytical subdivision
- **Function:** correlates mark-grasping with the six sensory-mental bases

### cetanā

- **Provisional meaning:** volition
- **Status:** principal member of the saṃskāraskandha
- **Function:** karma in its primary mental form; chief factor in actively forming the conditioned

### abhisaṃskaraṇa

- **Provisional meaning:** active formation; formative construction
- **Status:** explanatory term in the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** names the operation for which cetanā is principal

### viprayukta-saṃskāra

- **Provisional meaning:** formation dissociated from mind
- **Status:** technical Abhidharma class
- **Function:** shows that saṃskāraskandha extends beyond ordinary associated mental factors

### parijñā

- **Provisional meaning:** full comprehension
- **Status:** Path function
- **Function:** complete knowing of the truth of suffering

### prahāṇa

- **Provisional meaning:** abandonment
- **Status:** Path function
- **Function:** removal of factors belonging to origin and bondage

## 6. Voice Register

### Vasubandhu’s explanatory voice

The classification of the ten material factors, the definitions of vedanā and saṃjñā, and the residual definition of saṃskāraskandha are presented directly.

### Scriptural citation

The six classes of volition and the statements about formation, full comprehension, and abandonment are attributed to the Buddha.

### Doctrinal inference

The necessity of including all remaining mental and mind-dissociated formations in the saṃskāraskandha is Vasubandhu’s systematic argument from the requirements of the truths and the Path.

There is no explicit school-label or objector in this unit.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The current Kārikā translation is broadly sound, but two refinements should be recorded for the later revision pass.

First:

```text
indriyārthās ta eva
```

should make clear that these are **the very same faculties and objects already included in the rūpaskandha**.

Second, `anubhava` should not be rendered so broadly that vedanā becomes all experience. The Bhāṣya defines it through pleasant, painful, and neutral undergoing.

### Recommended later translation

> The very same faculties and objects are accepted as the ten āyatanas and ten dhātus. Vedanā is felt undergoing; saṃjñā has the nature of grasping a determinate mark.

No change is made to the Kārikā file during this pass.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The strongest provisional contact is the distinction between **one content-field** and **several lawful organizations**:

```text
same material factors
    → skandha organization
    → āyatana organization
    → dhātu organization
```

This supports the project’s view that dhātus are domains rather than crude substances. But the Bhāṣya here still treats faculties and objects as dhātus in their own right; it does not yet subordinate all dhātus to Vijñānaskandha.

A second contact appears in the sequence:

```text
vedanā
    = undergoing

saṃjñā
    = determination through a mark

cetanā / saṃskāra
    = active formation
```

This suggests a progressive articulation from being affected, through recognition, to formative activity. That sequence is useful for the Organon, but it should remain subordinate to the textual structure until the vijñānaskandha and eighteen-dhātu analysis are complete.
