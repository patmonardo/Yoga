# VAK_1.23 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> prāk pañca vārttamānārthyāt bhautikārthyāc catuṣṭayam /
>
> dūrāśutaravṛtty anyat yathāsthānaṃ kramo 'thavā // VAkK_1.23 //

The established Kārikā analysis correctly recognized that the verse explains the order of the six faculties, their objects, and their corresponding vijñānas through several distinct criteria. The Bhāṣya now specifies those criteria precisely and shows how each applies.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question:

> āyatanadhātūnāṃ ṣaṇṇāṃ cakṣurādīnāmanukramo vaktavyaḥ /

### Translation

> The order of the six spheres and domains beginning with the eye must now be explained.

It ends after the alternative explanation based upon bodily location:

> manaḥ punas tāny eva niśritam adeśasthaṃ ceti yathāsthānam eṣāṃ kramaḥ syāt /

### Translation

> Mind, however, depends upon those very faculties and is not situated in a determinate place. Thus their order may be understood according to their respective locations.

The next question—why only one of the ten material spheres is called the rūpāyatana—opens the Bhāṣya unit for VAK 1.24.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 The order of objects and vijñānas follows the order of the faculties

> tadvaśenaiva hi tadviṣayavijñānānāṃ kramaḥ /

### Translation

> The order of their objects and corresponding vijñānas follows precisely from the order of those faculties.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu.

The Bhāṣya first establishes that the list is organized from the faculty side. Once the faculties are ordered, their respective object-domains and knowledge-events follow in the same sequence.

```text
faculty order
    → object-domain order
    → corresponding vijñāna order
```

This is a classificatory rule, not a claim that the faculties temporally produce all their objects.

### 3.2 The first five precede mind because they take present objects

> prāk pañca vārttamānārthyāt
>
> cakṣurādīni pañca varttamānaviṣayatvāt pūrvam uktāni /
> manas tv aniyataviṣayam kiñcid varttamānaviṣayaṃ kiñcid vyadhvānadhraviṣayam /

### Translation

> The first five come first because their objects are present.
>
> The five beginning with the eye are stated first because they have present objects. Mind, however, has no fixed temporal object: some of its objects are present, while others belong to different times or are non-temporal.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu.

The Kārikā translation should be sharpened here. `vārttamānārthyāt` does not merely mean “because of present objecthood” in an abstract sense. The point is that the five sensory faculties are restricted to presently occurring objects.

Manas differs because its range is temporally unrestricted:

```text
five sensory faculties
    = present objects only

manas
    = present objects
    + past objects
    + future objects
    + non-temporal objects
```

The text form `vyadhvānadhraviṣayam` is compressed or slightly corrupt in the local source, but the intended contrast is clear: mind can take objects belonging to other temporal courses and objects not confined to temporal succession.

### 3.3 Four faculties precede body because their objects are derivative matter

> bhautikārthyāc catuṣṭayam /
>
> pañcānāṃ punaś catvāri pūrvam uktāni /
> bhautikaviṣayatvāt /
> kāyasya tv aniyato viṣayaḥ kadācid bhūtāni kadācid bhautikaṃ kadācid ubhayam /

### Translation

> Four come first because their objects are derivative matter.
>
> Of the five, four are again stated before the body faculty because their objects are derivative material forms. The object of the body faculty is not fixed: sometimes it is the great elements, sometimes derivative matter, and sometimes both.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu.

The four are eye, ear, nose, and tongue. Their corresponding objects—visible form, sound, smell, and taste—are treated as `bhautika`, matter derived from the four great elements.

Touch is different because the tactile field includes:

```text
great elements themselves
+ derivative matter
+ combinations of both
```

Thus the body faculty is placed after the first four because its object-domain is materially more heterogeneous.

### 3.4 Eye and ear precede nose and tongue because they operate at a distance

> dūrāśutaravṛttyā 'nyat
>
> cakṣuḥśrotraṃ hi dūraviṣayam /

### Translation

> The remainder is ordered according to operation at a greater distance or with greater speed.
>
> Eye and ear have objects at a distance.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu.

Eye and ear are placed before nose and tongue because they do not require direct physical contact with their objects.

The Bhāṣya then distinguishes eye from ear:

> tayor api cakṣuṣo dūratare vṛttiḥ /
> paśyato 'pi dūrān nadīṃ tacchabdāśravaṇāt atas tat pūrvam uktam /

### Translation

> Of these two, the eye operates at a still greater distance. A person may see a river from afar without hearing its sound; therefore the eye is stated first.

The criterion is operational reach, not metaphysical superiority.

```text
eye
    = farther range

ear
    = distant range, but less far
```

### 3.5 Nose precedes tongue because it apprehends more quickly

> ghrāṇasya tu nāsti dūre vṛttiḥ /
> jihvāyāś ca /
> tayor āśutaravṛttitvāt ghrāṇaṃ pūrvam uktam aprāptasyaiva jihvābhojyasya gandhagrahaṇāt /

### Translation

> Nose does not operate at a distance, nor does tongue. Of these two, nose is stated first because it functions more quickly: it apprehends the smell of food before that food has reached the tongue.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu.

Here `āśutara-vṛtti` means quicker functional access. Smell reaches cognition before taste because the food need not yet contact the tongue.

```text
nose
    = prior apprehension without oral contact

tongue
    = apprehension only upon contact
```

### 3.6 An alternative ordering follows bodily location

> yathāsthānaṃ kramo 'thavā
>
> athavā asmin śarīre cakṣuṣo 'dhiṣṭhānam upāriṣṭān niviṣṭam /
> tasmād adhaḥ śrotrasya /
> tasmād adho ghrāṇasya /
> tasmāj jihvāyāḥ /
> tataḥ kāyasya bahulyena /
> manaḥ punas tāny eva niśritam adeśasthaṃ ca /

### Translation

> Or the order is according to location.
>
> Alternatively, in this body the support of the eye is situated uppermost. Below it is the support of the ear; below that, the nose; below that, the tongue; and after that the body faculty, which extends throughout most of the body. Mind depends upon those very faculties and has no determinate spatial location.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu.

The local source has a minor damaged reading around `tataḥ kāyasya bahulyena`, but the sense is sufficiently clear: the body faculty is diffusely distributed rather than localized like eye, ear, nose, and tongue.

The final contrast is important:

```text
five bodily faculties
    = spatially located supports

manas
    = dependent upon them
    = not assigned one bodily place
```

The Bhāṣya reports this as an alternative explanation of order, not as a separate theory of mind-substance.

## 4. Argument Summary

VAK 1.23 explains why the six faculties are listed in their established sequence. The Bhāṣya provides four nested criteria:

```text
1. temporal range
   five sensory faculties precede manas
   because they take present objects only

2. material character of the object
   eye, ear, nose, and tongue precede body
   because their objects are derivative matter

3. operational reach and speed
   eye precedes ear by greater distance
   nose precedes tongue by quicker apprehension

4. bodily topology
   eye → ear → nose → tongue → body → non-localized manas
```

The ordering is therefore neither arbitrary nor governed by one single principle. Vasubandhu supplies several valid explanatory perspectives, each tracking a different relational feature of the cognitive system.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### vārttamānaviṣayatva

- **Provisional meaning:** having a presently existing object
- **Status:** Bhāṣya clarification
- **Function:** distinguishes the five sensory faculties from manas

### aniyataviṣaya

- **Provisional meaning:** having no fixed or restricted object-range
- **Status:** Bhāṣya term
- **Function:** characterizes mind as capable of taking objects beyond the present

### bhautikaviṣayatva

- **Provisional meaning:** having derivative material form as object
- **Status:** technical material classification
- **Function:** groups eye, ear, nose, and tongue before body

### dūraviṣaya

- **Provisional meaning:** having an object accessible at a distance
- **Status:** functional description
- **Function:** distinguishes eye and ear from nose and tongue

### dūrataravṛtti

- **Provisional meaning:** operation at a greater distance
- **Status:** comparative functional term
- **Function:** places eye before ear

### āśutaravṛtti

- **Provisional meaning:** quicker operation or prior access to the object
- **Status:** comparative functional term
- **Function:** places nose before tongue

### adhiṣṭhāna

- **Provisional meaning:** bodily support or locus of a faculty
- **Status:** established technical term used topologically
- **Function:** explains the alternative order according to bodily placement

### adeśastha

- **Provisional meaning:** not situated in a determinate place
- **Status:** Bhāṣya characterization of manas
- **Function:** distinguishes mind from spatially localized bodily faculties

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

**Decision: refined.**

The earlier translation was broadly correct, but the Bhāṣya supports a more productive and exact rendering:

> The first five come first because their objects are present; of these, four come first because their objects are derivative matter. The rest are ordered by greater distance or quicker operation—or, alternatively, according to their bodily location.

Important refinements:

```text
vārttamānārthyāt
    = because they take present objects

bhautika-arthyāt
    = because they take derivative material objects

dūrāśutaravṛtti
    = greater distance or quicker operation

yathāsthānam
    = according to bodily location
```

The earlier phrase “mode of object-relation” was useful but too general. The Bhāṣya gives exact temporal, material, operational, and spatial criteria.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The speculative Projection/Factory account is not needed to explain this passage. The Bhāṣya remains productively intelligible as a relational ordering of faculties, object-domains, and vijñānas.

A restrained project contact point is:

```text
a displayed field may be ordered by
    temporal accessibility
    material constitution
    operational reach
    processing priority
    spatial support
```

This may later be useful for modeling a property graph of cognition. But the Bhāṣya itself does not identify Vijñānaskandha as an idealist projector of the field. That penny remains in the monkey cup until the text earns it back.
