# VAK_1.12 Bhāṣya

## The Four Great Elements as Functional Material Domains

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.12.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya sharpens or corrects the first-pass interpretation, the decision is recorded here for later revision.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> bhūtāni pṛthivīdhātur aptejovāyudhātavaḥ /
>
> dhṛtyādikarmasaṃsiddhāḥ kharasnehoṣṇateraṇāḥ // VAkK_1.12 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The great elements are the earth-domain, the water-domain, the fire-domain, and the wind-domain. They are established respectively in the functions of support, cohesion, maturation, and expansion; their intrinsic characteristics are hardness, cohesion, heat, and impulsion.

The Bhāṣya requires a clear distinction between:

```text
karma
    function or operation

svabhāva
    intrinsic characteristic
```

The first-pass translation tends to combine these too quickly. The prose treats them as two related but distinct determinations.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question raised by VAK 1.11:

> *mahābhūtāny upādāyety uktāni katamāni bhūtāni?*

> “It was said that avijñapti depends upon the great elements. Which elements are these?”

It continues through:

1. identification of the four great elements;
2. explanation of why they are called `dhātu`;
3. explanation of why they are called `mahābhūta`;
4. distinction between their functions and intrinsic characteristics;
5. clarification of wind as impulsion;
6. the transition to the difference between ordinary earth, water, fire, and wind and the corresponding elemental domains.

The argument is:

```text
avijñapti depends upon the great elements
    ↓
which great elements?
    ↓
four elemental domains
    ↓
why “domains”?
    ↓
they sustain their own characteristics and derivative form
    ↓
why “great”?
    ↓
they support all other material form
    ↓
how are they known?
    ↓
through distinct functions and intrinsic characteristics
```

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. Which are the great elements?

> *mahābhūtāny upādāyety uktāni katamāni bhūtāni?*

> *bhūtāni pṛthivīdhātur aptejovāyudhātavaḥ.*

### Translation

> It was said that avijñapti depends upon the great elements. Which elements are these? They are the earth-domain, the water-domain, the fire-domain, and the wind-domain.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The verse answers a question generated directly by the preceding definition of avijñapti. The great elements are introduced because subtle material form requires a material basis.

The four are not first presented as visible masses:

```text
earth
water
fire
wind
```

but as:

```text
pṛthivī-dhātu
ap-dhātu
tejo-dhātu
vāyu-dhātu
```

That is, each is already treated as a determinate material domain.

---

### B. Why are they called dhātus?

> *ity ete catvāraḥ svalakṣaṇopādāyarūpadhāraṇād dhātavaś catvāri mahābhūtāny ucyante.*

### Translation

> These four are called domains because they sustain their own particular characteristics and derivative material form; they are called the four great elements.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The term `dhātu` is explained through `dhāraṇa`, sustaining or holding.

They sustain:

```text
1. their own particular characteristics
2. derivative material form
```

Thus a dhātu is not merely a named substance. It is a sustaining basis with a determinate characteristic and a field of dependent material effects.

This supports the project rendering **domain**, provided that “domain” retains this active sense:

```text
dhātu
    = a sustaining field of characteristic and derivative manifestation
```

---

### C. Why are they called great?

> *mahattvam eṣāṃ sarvānyarūpāśrayatvenaudārikatvāt.*

### Translation

> Their greatness consists in their being the support of all other material form and in their grossness.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The great elements are “great” for two reasons:

```text
sarvānyarūpāśrayatva
    being the support of all other material form

audārikatva
    grossness or material prominence
```

Their priority is therefore functional and structural. Derivative materiality depends upon them.

The text does not say that they are metaphysical substances standing beneath every appearance. It says that all other material form depends upon them as its support.

---

### D. An alternative explanation of greatness

> *atha vā tadudbhūtavṛttiṣu pṛthivyaptejovāyuskandheṣv eṣāṃ mahāsaṃniveśatvāt.*

### Translation

> Or they are called great because they occur in great concentration within the masses of earth, water, fire, and wind that arise from them.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The prose offers a second explanation.

The elemental domains are not identical with the ordinary masses conventionally called earth, water, fire, and wind. Rather, those masses arise from them and contain them in large concentration.

```text
elemental domain
    ≠ ordinary visible mass

ordinary mass
    = derivative formation
      in which an elemental domain is especially prominent
```

This distinction becomes explicit immediately after the verse and is important for avoiding a crude elemental physics.

---

### E. Their functions

> *te punar ete dhātavaḥ kasmin karmaṇi saṃsiddhāḥ kiṃsvabhāvāś cety āha—dhṛtyādikarmasaṃsiddhāḥ.*

### Translation

> In what functions are these domains established, and what are their intrinsic characteristics? He says: “They are established in functions beginning with support.”

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya explicitly asks two questions:

```text
What do they do?
What are they?
```

These must not be collapsed.

Their functions are given in order:

> *dhṛtisaṃgrahapaktivyūhanakarmasv ete yathākramaṃ saṃsiddhāḥ pṛthivyaptejovāyudhātavaḥ.*

### Translation

> The earth-, water-, fire-, and wind-domains are respectively established in the functions of support, cohesion, maturation, and expansion.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

| Domain | Function |
|---|---|
| earth | `dhṛti` — support, sustaining |
| water | `saṃgraha` — cohesion, gathering |
| fire | `pakti` — maturation, ripening |
| wind | `vyūhana` — expansion and distribution |

The sequence is not simply:

```text
hardness
wetness
heat
motion
```

Those belong to the next question concerning intrinsic characteristic.

---

### F. The function of wind

> *vyūhanaṃ punar vṛddhiḥ prasarpaṇaṃ ca veditavyam.*

### Translation

> Expansion should be understood as growth and spreading outward.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The wind-domain does not merely cause local movement. Its function includes:

```text
vṛddhi
    increase or growth

prasarpaṇa
    spreading or extension
```

This makes `vyūhana` broader than the first-pass rendering “motion.” Wind is the function through which a material stream expands, extends, and develops.

---

### G. Their intrinsic characteristics

> *idam eṣāṃ karma. svabhāvas tu yathākramaṃ kharasnehoṣṇateraṇāḥ.*

### Translation

> These are their functions. Their intrinsic characteristics, respectively, are hardness, cohesion, heat, and impulsion.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The distinction is now complete:

| Domain | Function | Intrinsic characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| earth | support | hardness |
| water | cohesion/gathering | cohesiveness or moisture |
| fire | maturation | heat |
| wind | expansion | impulsion |

The functions express what the domain accomplishes within a material complex. The intrinsic characteristic identifies its own determinate material nature.

---

### H. Earth, water, and fire

> *kharaḥ pṛthivīdhātuḥ. sneho 'bdhātuḥ. uṣṇatā tejodhātuḥ.*

### Translation

> Hardness is the earth-domain. Cohesiveness is the water-domain. Heat is the fire-domain.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

These are direct identifications:

```text
pṛthivī-dhātu = hardness
ap-dhātu       = cohesion
tejo-dhātu     = heat
```

The ordinary names “earth,” “water,” and “fire” can therefore mislead when they suggest visible substances. At the level of Abhidharma analysis, each refers to a characteristic material determination.

---

### I. Wind as impulsion

> *īraṇā vāyudhātuḥ.*

### Translation

> Impulsion is the wind-domain.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya then explains:

> *īryate 'nayā bhūtasroto deśāntarotpādanāt pradīperaṇavad itīraṇā.*

### Translation

> It is called impulsion because through it a stream of material elements is produced in another place, as when the flame of a lamp is impelled.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is a particularly important definition.

Wind is not described as one enduring thing traveling from one location to another. Rather:

```text
material stream at one location
    ↓
wind-domain as impulsion
    ↓
production of the material stream at another location
```

The lamp-flame analogy indicates continuity through successive production rather than the transportation of one self-identical substance.

This is a material process understood as ordered generation.

---

### J. The apparent scriptural definition of wind

> *“vāyudhātuḥ katamo laghusamudīraṇatvam” iti prakaraṇeṣu nirdiṣṭaṃ sūtre ca.*

### Translation

> In the treatises and in scripture it is stated: “What is the wind-domain? Lightness and movement.”

**Voice:** Objector or textual challenge

The apparent difficulty is that other authoritative texts define wind through `laghutva`, lightness, and movement.

The Bhāṣya replies:

> *tat tu laghutvam upādāyarūpam apy uktaṃ prakaraṇeṣu.*

### Translation

> But in the treatises that lightness is also said to be derivative material form.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Lightness is therefore not the intrinsic characteristic of wind itself. It belongs to derivative materiality.

The resolution is:

> *ato ya īraṇāsvabhāvo dharmaḥ sa vāyur iti karmaṇā 'sya svabhāvo 'bhivyaktaḥ.*

### Translation

> Therefore the dharma whose intrinsic characteristic is impulsion is wind; its intrinsic characteristic is expressed through its function.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Wind is a special case because its function makes its nature evident.

```text
function
    expansion, movement, spreading

reveals

intrinsic characteristic
    impulsion
```

The two are distinct, but closely connected.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Avijñapti depends upon great elements.
        ↓
The great elements are four elemental domains.
        ↓
They are dhātus because they sustain
both their own characteristics and derivative form.
        ↓
They are great because all other material form depends upon them.
        ↓
Each has a distinct function:
support, cohesion, maturation, expansion.
        ↓
Each has a distinct intrinsic characteristic:
hardness, cohesiveness, heat, impulsion.
        ↓
Ordinary earth, water, fire, and wind are derivative masses
in which the corresponding domain is especially prominent.
        ↓
Materiality is therefore analyzed through functional,
characteristic, and dependent relations—not through inert substances.
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### **svalakṣaṇa**

- **Provisional meaning:** own or particular characteristic
- **Status:** newly explicit in this Bhāṣya unit
- **Function:** identifies the intrinsic determination sustained by each elemental domain

### **upādāyarūpa**

- **Provisional meaning:** derivative material form dependent upon the great elements
- **Status:** previously anticipated through `mahābhūtāny upādāya`; now explicitly named
- **Function:** distinguishes elemental materiality from material forms dependent upon it

### **dhāraṇa**

- **Provisional meaning:** sustaining, holding, supporting
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā as an explicit derivation
- **Function:** explains why the great elements are called `dhātu`

### **sarvānyarūpāśrayatva**

- **Provisional meaning:** being the support of all other material form
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains the greatness of the great elements

### **audārikatva**

- **Provisional meaning:** grossness or material prominence
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** second explanation of their designation as great

### **dhṛti**

- **Provisional meaning:** support or sustaining function
- **Status:** present implicitly in the Kārikā through `dhṛtyādi`
- **Function:** function of the earth-domain

### **saṃgraha**

- **Provisional meaning:** cohesion, gathering together
- **Status:** supplied by the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** function of the water-domain

### **pakti**

- **Provisional meaning:** maturation, ripening, transformation through heat
- **Status:** supplied by the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** function of the fire-domain

### **vyūhana**

- **Provisional meaning:** expansion, distribution, growth, and spreading
- **Status:** supplied by the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** function of the wind-domain

### **īraṇā**

- **Provisional meaning:** impulsion; production of a material stream in another place
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā, explained here
- **Function:** intrinsic characteristic of the wind-domain

### **bhūtasrotas**

- **Provisional meaning:** stream or continuum of material elements
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains movement as successive material production

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass translation is broadly correct but should be revised in three ways.

### 1. Preserve the function/characteristic distinction

Current compressed reading:

```text
earth  = hardness / support
water  = cohesion
fire   = heat / maturation
wind   = motion / impulsion
```

Bhāṣya-supported reading:

```text
earth
    function: support
    characteristic: hardness

water
    function: cohesion
    characteristic: cohesiveness

fire
    function: maturation
    characteristic: heat

wind
    function: expansion
    characteristic: impulsion
```

### 2. Render `vyūhana` more fully

“Motion” is too narrow. The Bhāṣya explains it as:

```text
growth
spreading
expansion
```

### 3. Avoid treating the four elements as ordinary substances

The elemental domains must be distinguished from ordinary visible earth, water, fire, and wind. The latter are derivative masses in which the corresponding elemental characteristic is prominent.

These corrections should be applied only during the later Kārikā revision pass.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The text strongly supports a functional-domain interpretation:

```text
dhātu
    = sustaining domain
      of characteristic and derivative manifestation
```

This is compatible with the emerging project thesis that dhātus are Knowledge Domains, but the present verse concerns the object-side of material determination rather than Vijñānaskandha directly.

A cautious formulation is:

```text
The four great elements are not four independent substances.
They are four irreducible determinations through which
material manifestation becomes intelligible:

support
cohesion
maturation
expansion
```

The wind analysis is particularly significant. Motion is understood through the ordered production of a continuum at successive locations. Material process is therefore already intelligible as lawful succession, not as a hidden substance moving unchanged beneath appearances.

That makes the mahābhūtas suitable contents of a knowledge-domain architecture, while the stronger thesis concerning their systematic belonging to Vijñānaskandha remains to be established by the later dhātu analysis.
