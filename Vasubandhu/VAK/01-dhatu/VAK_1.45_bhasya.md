# VAK_1.45_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> tadvikāravikāritvād āśrayāś cakṣurādayaḥ /
>
> ato 'sādhāraṇatvād dhi vijñānaṃ tair nirucyate // VAkK_1.45 //

### Established Kārikā Translation

> The eye and the other faculties are supports because knowledge is altered when they are altered. Therefore, because they are non-common, knowledge is designated by them.

## Bhāṣya Source

### Why the Faculties Are Called Supports

> kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇam ubhayādhīnāyāṃ vijñānotpattau cakṣurādaya eva āśrayā ucyante na rūpādayaḥ /
>
> tadvikāravikāritvād āśrayāś cakṣurādayaḥ /
>
> cakṣurādīnāṃ hi vikāreṇa tadvijñānānāṃ vikāro bhavaty anugrahopaghātapaṭumandatānuvidhānāt na tu rūpādīnāṃ vikāreṇa tadvikāraḥ /
>
> tasmāt sādhīyas tadadhīnatvāt ta evāśrayā na rūpādayaḥ /

Pradhan reference: `[034|17–034|22]`

### Why Knowledge Is Named from the Faculty

> kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇaṃ rūpādayaś ca tair vijñāyante cakṣurvijñānaṃ cocyate yāvan manovijñānam /
>
> na punā rūpavijñānaṃ yāvad dharmavijñānam iti /
>
> ya ete cakṣurādaya āśrayā eṣām /
>
> ato 'sādhāraṇatvād dhi vijñānaṃ tair nirucyate // VAkK_1.45 //
>
> katham asādhāraṇatvam /
>
> na hi cakṣur anyasya vijñānasyāśrayībhavitum utsahate /
>
> rūpaṃ tu manovijñānasyālambanībhavaty anyacakṣurvijñānasyāpi /
>
> evaṃ yāvat kāyo veditavyaḥ /
>
> tasmād āśrayabhāvād asādhāraṇatvāc ca vijñānaṃ tenaiva nirdiśyate na rūpādibhiḥ /
>
> yathā bherīśabdo yavāṅkura iti /

Pradhan reference: `[034|23–035|01]`

The source is unusually clear here. It explicitly contrasts `āśraya` with `ālambana`, and therefore supplies a firm terminological basis for the project.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. Knowledge depends upon both faculty and object

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Since the arising of knowledge depends upon both, why are only the eye and the other faculties called supports, and not visible form and the other objects?

The Bhāṣya does not deny the dependence of knowledge upon the object. The question concerns why one dependent condition receives the technical name `āśraya` while the other does not.

### 2. The faculty is the support because its alteration alters knowledge

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The eye and the other faculties are supports because their alteration alters the corresponding knowledges.

The Bhāṣya specifies the relevant alterations:

```text
anugraha
    benefit or improvement

upaghāta
    injury or impairment

paṭutā
    acuity or sharpness

mandatā
    dullness or weakness
```

Knowledge follows these modifications of the faculty. Improved visual capacity yields sharper visual knowledge; injured or dull capacity yields impaired or indistinct visual knowledge.

### 3. Object-alteration does not determine knowledge in the same way

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The corresponding knowledge does not undergo alteration merely through alteration of visible form and the other objects in this same determining manner.

This does not mean that the object contributes nothing. It means that the object does not determine the **capacity and quality of the knowing operation** in the manner characteristic of the faculty.

Hence:

```text
faculty
    determining support of the knowledge-event

object
    object-support of the knowledge-event
```

### 4. Stronger dependence identifies the support

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Therefore, because knowledge is more strongly dependent upon them, the faculties alone are called supports, not visible form and the other objects.

The expression `sādhīyas tadadhīnatva` indicates a stronger or more decisive dependence. The Bhāṣya is not dividing causal conditions into dependent and independent. It is distinguishing their functions within one event of knowledge.

### 5. Why do we say “eye-knowledge” rather than “form-knowledge”?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Why, although visible form and the other objects are known by these knowledges, do we say “eye-knowledge” through “mental knowledge,” rather than “form-knowledge” through “dharma-knowledge”?

The problem is one of principled designation. Knowledge depends on a support and takes an object. Why is its name derived from the first relation?

### 6. The support is non-common

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Because of their non-common character, knowledge is designated by the eye and the other faculties.

The eye cannot serve as the support of auditory, olfactory, gustatory, bodily, or mental knowledge. Its support-function is specific:

```text
cakṣus
    support only of cakṣurvijñāna

śrotra
    support only of śrotravijñāna

and so forth
```

### 7. The object is common to more than one knowledge

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Visible form, however, can become an object-support for mental knowledge and also for the eye-knowledge of another person.

This sentence is decisive for terminology and representation.

Visible form is not restricted to one knowledge-series:

```text
rūpa
    object-support of present eye-knowledge
    object-support of another person's eye-knowledge
    object-support of subsequent mental knowledge
```

Therefore the object is `sādhāraṇa`, common or shareable, whereas the faculty-support is `asādhāraṇa`, specific to its knowledge-series.

### 8. Naming follows specific support

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Therefore knowledge is designated by its support, because of support-status and non-commonness, and not by visible form and the other objects.

The examples “drum-sound” and “barley-sprout” illustrate naming by the distinctive generating or supporting condition rather than by every condition involved.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Premise:
Knowledge arises in dependence upon both faculty and object.

Question 1:
Why is the faculty called āśraya rather than the object?

Criterion:
Alteration of the faculty alters the corresponding knowledge.

    improvement → improved knowledge
    injury → impaired knowledge
    acuity → acute knowledge
    dullness → dull knowledge

Conclusion 1:
The faculty is the determining support.

Question 2:
Why is knowledge named from the faculty rather than from the object?

Criterion:
The faculty is non-common and specific to one knowledge-domain.

    one faculty → one corresponding kind of sensory knowledge

By contrast:
The object is common to multiple possible knowledges.

    one visible form
        → this eye-knowledge
        → another person's eye-knowledge
        → subsequent mental knowledge

Conclusion 2:
Knowledge is named from its specific support, not from its shareable object-support.
```

## Terminology Determination

This passage gives the strongest textual basis so far for keeping three terms distinct:

```text
āśraya
    determining support
    the condition whose alteration modifies the knowledge-event

ālambana
    object-support
    that upon which knowledge is directed and which may be common to several knowledges

viṣaya
    determinate object-field
    the kind or range of object appropriate to a faculty or knowledge
```

These terms must not be translated interchangeably.

The contrast is not:

```text
subject
    versus
object
```

It is more precise:

```text
support-relation
    determines the capacity and mode of knowledge

object-support relation
    gives knowledge that toward which it is directed

object-field
    determines what kind of content can be apprehended
```

## Translation Consequences

### 1. `Āśraya`

Preferred translation:

```text
āśraya
    = support
    = determining support when contrast is needed
```

“Basis” may be used in explanatory prose, but “support” should remain the stable lexical rendering.

### 2. `Ālambana`

Preferred translation:

```text
ālambana
    = object-support
```

This preserves both its object-directed and supporting functions without collapsing it into `viṣaya`.

### 3. `Asādhāraṇa`

Preferred translation:

```text
asādhāraṇa
    = non-common
    = specific, when explanatory English is required
```

The literal “non-common” matters because it contrasts directly with the shareability of the object.

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> The eye and the other faculties are the determining supports of knowledge because the corresponding knowledge changes with their improvement, injury, acuity, or dullness. Knowledge is named from these supports because each faculty is non-common and specific to its own knowledge-domain, whereas visible form and the other objects may serve as object-supports for multiple knowledges.

## Doctrinal Determination

This verse prevents two reductions.

First:

```text
knowledge
    is not independent of the faculty
```

The quality and capacity of the knowledge-event vary with the faculty.

Second:

```text
knowledge
    is not named from its represented object alone
```

The same object can be apprehended by several subjects and later represented by mental knowledge. Object-content is shareable; the faculty-support is specific.

This gives a carefully bounded representational structure:

```text
specific faculty-support
    conditions a particular knowledge-event

shareable object-support
    becomes represented in that knowledge-event

mental knowledge
    may subsequently take the same represented object as object-support
```

The Bhāṣya itself supplies this structure without requiring Sāṃkhya metaphysics.

---

## Logical Determination

VAK 1.45 distinguishes **formal determination** from **objective direction**.

```text
āśraya
    determines how knowledge occurs

ālambana
    determines what knowledge is directed toward
```

The knowledge-event unites both relations:

```text
faculty-specific determination
    +
object-directed content
    =
particular knowledge-event
```

This also clarifies the quantitative hypothesis raised in the preceding units. Acuity and dullness are explicitly modifications of faculty-capacity. The Bhāṣya does not yet formulate numerical thresholds, but it does affirm degrees of operative precision:

```text
paṭutā
    greater acuity

mandatā
    lesser acuity
```

Thus capacity is not merely present or absent. It admits qualitative—and potentially measurable—variation.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The most important result is the separation of two dimensions of objectivity:

```text
objective content
    shareable across several knowledges

formal capacity
    specific to the support through which the content appears
```

This makes representation neither a copy sealed inside the faculty nor a naked external object entering knowledge unchanged.

Rather:

```text
object
    is common and representable

faculty
    is specific and form-determining

knowledge
    is the event in which specific capacity and shareable object-support are united
```

A limited Sāṃkhya comparison may later help articulate internal instrumentality, but this structure is already established by the Bhāṣya itself and should be allowed to govern the interpretation.
