# VAK_1.27 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tathānye 'pi yathāyogaṃ skandhāyatanadhātavaḥ /
> pratipādyā yathokteṣu saṃpradhārya svalakṣaṇam // VAkK_1.27 //

### Translation

> Likewise, the other aggregates, spheres, and domains taught elsewhere are to be assigned, as appropriate, among those already stated, after carefully determining the intrinsic characteristic of each.

The verse states a general rule for reconciling expanded scriptural classifications with the basic five-skandha, twelve-āyatana, and eighteen-dhātu schemes.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

> ye 'pyanye skandhāyatanadhātavaḥ sūtrāntareṣūktāste 'pyeṣveva yathokteṣu skandhādiṣu pratipādyāḥ svaṃ svaṃ lakṣaṇameṣāṃ yathāvihitamasmiñ śāstre saṃpradhārya /
>
> tatra tāvat pañcānāṃ śīlasamādhiprajñāvimuktijñānadarśanaskandhānāṃ śīlaskandho rūpaskandhena saṃgṛhītaḥ /
>
> śeṣāḥ saṃskāraskandhena /
>
> daśānāṃ kṛtsnāyatanānāmaṣṭāvalobhasvabhāvatvāddharmāyatanena /
>
> saparivārāṇi tu pañcaskandhasvabhāvatvānmanodharmāyatanābhyām /
>
> tathā 'bhibhvāyatanāni /
>
> ākāśavijñānānantyāyatanakṛtsne catvāri cākāśānantyāyatanādīni catuḥskandhasvabhāvatvāt manodharmāyatanābhyām /
>
> pañca vimuktyāyatanāni prajñāsvabhāvatvāddharmāyatanena /
>
> saparivārāṇi tu śabdo manodharmāyatanaiḥ /
>
> dvayorāyatanayorasaṃjñisattvā daśabhirāyatanairgandharasāyatanābhāvāt /
>
> naivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñāyatanopagā manodharmāyatanābhyām /
>
> evaṃ bahudhātuke 'pi dvāṣaṣṭirdhātavo deśitāḥ /
>
> teṣāṃ yathāyogaṃ saṃgraho veditavyaḥ //

### Translation

> Whatever other aggregates, spheres, and domains are taught in the sūtras must likewise be assigned, as appropriate, among the aggregates and other categories already stated, after carefully considering the particular characteristic of each as it has been established in this treatise.
>
> Thus, among the five aggregates of discipline, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and knowledge-and-vision of liberation, the aggregate of discipline is included within the form aggregate; the remaining four are included within the formations aggregate.
>
> Of the ten totality-spheres, eight, because they have non-greed as their intrinsic nature, are included within the dharma-sphere. When taken together with their associated factors, however, they have the nature of all five aggregates and are included within the mind-sphere and dharma-sphere. The same applies to the spheres of mastery.
>
> The totalities of infinite space and infinite consciousness, together with the four spheres beginning with the sphere of infinite space, have the nature of four aggregates and are therefore included within the mind-sphere and dharma-sphere.
>
> The five spheres of liberation, because they have wisdom as their intrinsic nature, are included within the dharma-sphere. When taken together with their associated factors, they are included within sound, mind, and dharma as spheres.
>
> Of the two further spheres, non-conceptual beings are included within ten spheres, because the spheres of smell and taste are absent there; those who have attained the sphere of neither-conceptualization-nor-non-conceptualization are included within the mind-sphere and dharma-sphere.
>
> In the same way, the discourse on the many domains teaches sixty-two domains. Their inclusion is to be understood as appropriate in each case.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 The verse gives a method of reduction without erasure

The expanded classifications found throughout the sūtras do not stand outside the basic Abhidharma framework. They are assigned back into the established five aggregates, twelve spheres, and eighteen domains.

But this assignment is not performed by name or superficial resemblance. Vasubandhu requires prior examination of each item's `svalakṣaṇa`.

The method is:

```text
identify the dharma's intrinsic characteristic
    ↓
determine whether the term names a principal factor
or a principal factor together with its associates
    ↓
assign it to the appropriate skandha, āyatana, and dhātu
```

### 3.2 Principal factor and associated complex must be distinguished

Several classifications change depending on whether a term designates:

1. the principal dharma alone, or
2. that dharma together with its associated factors (`saparivāra`).

For example, eight totality-spheres are intrinsically non-greed. Considered in that narrow sense, they belong to dharmāyatana. Considered together with their full associated complex, they involve all five aggregates and therefore extend across manas and dharma as spheres.

Thus one scriptural expression can receive more than one valid classification because the intended scope differs.

### 3.3 `Svalakṣaṇa` governs inclusion

The Bhāṣya gives concrete examples:

- discipline is classified under rūpaskandha;
- concentration, wisdom, liberation, and knowledge-and-vision are classified under saṃskāraskandha;
- non-greed and wisdom are classified under dharmāyatana;
- immaterial attainments are classified through the four non-material aggregates;
- realms lacking smell and taste are classified through the ten remaining spheres.

The criterion is not the ordinary meaning of the label. It is the actual dharma or complex of dharmas denoted by that label.

### 3.4 The canonical systems are exhaustive frameworks

The verse and commentary assume that expanded teachings can be mapped into the established frameworks without creating additional fundamental categories.

This does not mean that every named teaching is identical. It means that the five-skandha, twelve-āyatana, and eighteen-dhātu analyses are extensionally sufficient to include them once their intrinsic characteristics are known.

### 3.5 The classification is context-sensitive but not arbitrary

`Yathāyogam`, “as appropriate,” does not authorize loose analogy. The appropriate inclusion is constrained by:

- intrinsic nature;
- associated factors;
- presence or absence of particular faculties and objects;
- the doctrinal scope intended by the scriptural expression.

The resulting classification is therefore contextual, but still rule-governed.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Problem:
The sūtras teach many additional aggregates, spheres, and domains.
How do these relate to the basic Abhidharma lists?

Rule:
Determine the svalakṣaṇa of each item.

Then distinguish:
principal dharma alone
from
principal dharma with its associated complex.

Result:
Assign each item, as appropriate,
within the five skandhas,
twelve āyatanas,
and eighteen dhātus already established.
```

The basic systems are not merely short lists beside longer ones. They function as comprehensive analytic frameworks into which the more specialized teachings can be resolved.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### `svalakṣaṇa`

- **Provisional meaning:** intrinsic characteristic; the specific nature by which a dharma is what it is
- **Status:** Vasubandhu's governing criterion for classification
- **Function:** determines the proper inclusion of a named teaching within skandha, āyatana, and dhātu

### `yathāyogam`

- **Provisional meaning:** as appropriate; according to the applicable relation
- **Status:** methodological qualifier
- **Function:** permits different classifications where the denoted scope differs, while requiring doctrinally grounded assignment

### `saparivāra`

- **Provisional meaning:** together with its associates or accompanying complex
- **Status:** technical scope distinction
- **Function:** distinguishes a principal dharma considered alone from the full complex in which it occurs

### `saṃgraha`

- **Provisional meaning:** inclusion or subsumption
- **Status:** technical classificatory relation
- **Function:** places specialized scriptural categories within the established Abhidharma frameworks

### `kṛtsnāyatana`

- **Provisional meaning:** totality-sphere
- **Status:** meditative classification cited from the sūtras
- **Function:** example showing that classification differs depending on whether its principal nature or associated complex is intended

### `abhibhvāyatana`

- **Provisional meaning:** sphere of mastery
- **Status:** meditative classification
- **Function:** another example of inclusion through the same method

## 6. Voice Register

- **Kārikā voice:** states the universal method for assigning additional scriptural classifications.
- **Vasubandhu's expository voice:** explains the rule and applies it through examples.
- **Inherited Abhidharma classification:** the placement of discipline under rūpa, the remaining liberation aggregates under saṃskāra, and meditative factors under dharmāyatana.
- **No active objector:** this unit is systematic exposition rather than controversy.
- **Project interpretation:** any claim that this amounts to a pure a priori property graph or a Projection/Factory architecture remains external to the Bhāṣya.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The existing translation should be refined in two respects.

First, `anye ... skandhāyatanadhātavaḥ` refers specifically to the additional aggregates, spheres, and domains taught elsewhere in scripture, not to “other dharmas” in general.

Second, `pratipādyāḥ` is best rendered here as “to be assigned” or “to be placed,” because the Bhāṣya immediately performs classifications by inclusion.

Recommended translation:

> Likewise, the other aggregates, spheres, and domains taught elsewhere are to be assigned, as appropriate, among those already stated, after carefully determining the intrinsic characteristic of each.

No edit is made to the Kārikā file during this Bhāṣya pass.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

This passage offers a disciplined classificatory pattern:

```text
intrinsic determination
    precedes
systemic placement
```

A named structure is not classified by its label but by what it actually instantiates. Moreover, the same term may denote either a principal factor or a full associated complex.

That can later support a property-graph interpretation in which node type and graph membership follow determinate content rather than naming convention. But this remains a later idealist reconstruction. Vasubandhu's immediate project is the exhaustive inclusion of specialized Buddhist teachings within the established Abhidharma analyses.
