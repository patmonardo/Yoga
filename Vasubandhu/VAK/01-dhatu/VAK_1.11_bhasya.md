# VAK_1.11 Bhāṣya

## Avijñapti as Non-Disclosive Ethical Continuity

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.11.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya sharpens or corrects the first-pass interpretation, that decision is recorded here for a later Kārikā revision pass.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> vikṣiptācittakasyāpi yo 'nubandhaḥ śubhāśubhaḥ /
>
> mahābhūtāny upādāya sa hy avijñaptir ucyate // VAkK_1.11 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The wholesome or unwholesome continuity that persists even for one whose mind is distracted or absent—while depending upon the great elements—is what is called avijñapti.

The Bhāṣya immediately expands the scope of the verse. Avijñapti is present not only when the mind is distracted or absent. The word `api` also allows its continuation when the person is conscious and undistracted.

Accordingly, the defining feature is not simply persistence outside conscious attention. It is a wholesome or unwholesome stream of form that does not disclose itself to another in the way bodily or verbal manifestation does.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with:

> *avijñaptir idānīṃ vaktavyā*

and ends with the concise summary:

> *samāsatas tu vijñaptisamādhisaṃbhūtaṃ kuśalākuśalaṃ rūpam avijñaptiḥ.*

It forms one continuous definition:

```text
states in which avijñapti continues
    ↓
meaning of anubandha as a stream
    ↓
ethical determination as wholesome or unwholesome
    ↓
dependence upon the great elements
    ↓
contrast with ordinary disclosure
    ↓
concise definition by origin and function
```

The following discussion of the four great elements belongs naturally with VAK 1.12 and is therefore not absorbed into this unit.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. Avijñapti must now be explained

> *uktāḥ pañcendriyārthāḥ yathā ca teṣāṃ grahaṇam. avijñaptir idānīṃ vaktavyā.*

### Translation

> The five sense-objects and the manner in which they are apprehended have been explained. Avijñapti must now be discussed.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The sequence is deliberate. VAK 1.09 introduced three divisions of rūpa:

```text
five faculties
five object-domains
avijñapti
```

VAK 1.10 completed the analysis of the object-domains. VAK 1.11 now turns to the remaining member, avijñapti.

---

### B. Distracted mind means a different mind

> *vikṣiptacittakasyeti tadanyacittasyāpi.*

### Translation

> “Of one whose mind is distracted” means: also of one whose mind is otherwise directed.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

`vikṣipta` does not need to mean psychological agitation in a narrow sense. Here it means that the presently occurring mind is different from the mind associated with the action or discipline from which the avijñapti arose.

Thus avijñapti can continue while attention has moved elsewhere.

```text
originating mental state
    ceases

another mental state
    arises

avijñapti stream
    continues
```

---

### C. Mindless states

> *acittakasyāpīty asaṃjñinirodhasamāpattisamāpannasyāpi.*

### Translation

> “Even of one without mind” means: also of one who has entered the attainment of non-perception or the attainment of cessation.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya identifies two technical states:

```text
asaṃjñi-samāpatti
    attainment of non-perception

nirodha-samāpatti
    attainment of cessation
```

During these attainments ordinary mental activity is absent, yet avijñapti can remain in continuity.

This is stronger than saying merely that an ethical trace survives distraction. The stream is not dependent upon the uninterrupted presence of an occurrent mental act.

---

### D. The word “even” expands the scope further

> *apiśabdenāvikṣiptasacittasyāpīti vijñāyate.*

### Translation

> By the word “even,” it is understood that avijñapti also belongs to one who is conscious and not distracted.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This prevents a mistaken restriction.

Avijñapti is not defined as something that exists only when consciousness is absent or diverted. It also continues during ordinary collected consciousness.

```text
undistracted and conscious
 distracted or otherwise minded
 without active mind

all may possess avijñapti
```

The verse names the more striking cases, but the Bhāṣya states the universal range.

---

### E. Anubandha means a stream

> *yo 'nubandha iti yaḥ pravāhaḥ.*

### Translation

> “That which is a continuity” means: that which is a stream.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The key explanatory term is `pravāha`.

```text
anubandha
    = pravāha
    = continuing stream
```

Avijñapti is therefore not best understood as one static residue deposited by a past action. It is a temporal continuity.

The Bhāṣya does not yet explain the later mechanics of how that stream is renewed moment by moment. But it clearly defines avijñapti as an ongoing series rather than a motionless ethical object.

---

### F. Wholesome and unwholesome

> *śubhāśubha iti kuśalākuśalaḥ.*

### Translation

> “Wholesome or unwholesome” means: skillful or unskillful.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya normalizes the verse's `śubha/aśubha` into the standard Abhidharma pair:

```text
kuśala
    wholesome / skillful

akuśala
    unwholesome / unskillful
```

Avijñapti is therefore ethically determined. This passage does not include a neutral or indeterminate avijñapti in the concise definition.

The ethical quality belongs to the continuing form-stream itself, not merely to a remembered judgment about the originating act.

---

### G. Why dependence upon the great elements must be stated

> *kuśalākuśale prāptivāho 'py astīdṛśa iti tadviśeṣaṇārtham ucyate mahābhūtāny upādāyeti.*

### Translation

> There is also a similar stream of acquisition with respect to wholesome and unwholesome states. Therefore, to distinguish avijñapti from that, the verse says: “depending upon the great elements.”

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

A wholesome or unwholesome continuity is not by itself sufficient to define avijñapti.

There is also:

```text
prāpti-vāha
    stream of acquisition
```

which may likewise preserve a wholesome or unwholesome determination.

The differentiating mark is:

```text
mahābhūtāny upādāya
    depending upon the great elements
```

Because avijñapti depends upon the great elements, it belongs to rūpa. The stream of acquisition does not have that same material classification.

This is one of the most important explanatory moves in the unit:

```text
ethical continuity alone
    does not define avijñapti

ethical continuity
    + dependence on great elements
    = avijñapti as rūpa
```

---

### H. The Vaibhāṣika interpretation of upādāya

> *hetvartha upādāyārtha iti vaibhāṣikāḥ. jananādihetubhāvāt.*

### Translation

> The Vaibhāṣikas say that the meaning of “depending upon” is causal, because the great elements function as causes beginning with production.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position

The phrase `mahābhūtāny upādāya` is interpreted causally:

```text
upādāya
    = because of / on the basis of
    = causal dependence
```

The great elements are causes of avijñapti beginning with its production and continuing through other causal functions.

The Bhāṣya marks this specifically as the Vaibhāṣika account. It should not be presented as a neutral lexical gloss without attribution.

---

### I. Why it is called avijñapti

> *sa hy avijñaptir iti hiśabdas tannāmakaraṇavijñāpanārthaḥ.*

### Translation

> In the expression “that indeed is avijñapti,” the word `hi` serves to indicate that this is the designation being assigned to it.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The verse is not merely using an already transparent common term. It announces a technical name.

```text
this form-stream
    is designated
    avijñapti
```

The Bhāṣya then explains the reason for that designation.

---

### J. Form and action that do not disclose

> *rūpakriyāsvabhāvāpi satī vijñaptivat paraṃ na vijñāpayatīty avijñaptiḥ.*

### Translation

> Although it has the nature of form and action, it does not make something known to another as vijñapti does; therefore it is called avijñapti.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is the central definition by contrast.

```text
vijñapti
    makes an action known to another

avijñapti
    does not make an action known to another
```

Avijñapti is not called “non-disclosive” because it is unreal, inactive, or without form. The Bhāṣya explicitly says that it possesses:

```text
rūpa-svabhāva
    the nature of form

kriyā-svabhāva
    the nature of action
```

Yet it lacks the interpersonal function of manifestation.

This corrects any reading that identifies avijñapti simply with an unconscious mental trace. Its primary contrast is with bodily or verbal disclosure to another person.

---

### K. “Is called” indicates the teacher's formulation

> *ucyata ity ācāryavacanaṃ darśayati.*

### Translation

> The phrase “is called” indicates a statement of the Teacher.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The wording signals that the verse is transmitting an established doctrinal designation.

**Voice of the designation:** Teacher or authoritative doctrinal tradition

The Bhāṣya does not identify the teacher more narrowly in this immediate sentence. It is therefore better not to force a more precise attribution here.

---

### L. Concise definition

> *samāsatas tu vijñaptisamādhisaṃbhūtaṃ kuśalākuśalaṃ rūpam avijñaptiḥ.*

### Translation

> In summary, avijñapti is wholesome or unwholesome form produced from disclosure or from meditative concentration.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This compact sentence adds a feature not explicit in the Kārikā itself: the two principal sources of avijñapti.

```text
vijñapti-saṃbhūta
    produced from bodily or verbal disclosure

samādhi-saṃbhūta
    produced from meditative concentration
```

Thus avijñapti is not only the continuing material result of outward ethical action. It may also arise from samādhi.

The concise definition can be stated as:

```text
avijñapti
    = wholesome or unwholesome rūpa
    = produced from vijñapti or samādhi
    = causally dependent upon the great elements
    = continuing without disclosing the action to another
```

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Rūpa includes avijñapti.
        ↓
Avijñapti continues while the mind is otherwise directed,
while mind is absent,
and also while mind is present and undistracted.
        ↓
Its anubandha is a continuing stream.
        ↓
The stream is wholesome or unwholesome.
        ↓
Ethical continuity alone does not distinguish it from prāpti.
        ↓
Its dependence upon the great elements marks it as rūpa.
        ↓
Although it has the nature of form and action,
it does not disclose the action to another as vijñapti does.
        ↓
It may arise from outward disclosure or from samādhi.
```

The unity of the passage is therefore:

```text
ethical causation
    becomes a continuing material determination
without remaining an outward manifestation
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### **pravāha**

- **Provisional meaning:** stream or temporal continuity
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā; introduced to explain `anubandha`
- **Function:** establishes avijñapti as an ongoing series rather than a static residue

### **asaṃjñi-samāpatti**

- **Provisional meaning:** attainment of non-perception
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā; previously relevant to mindless states
- **Function:** example of a state in which avijñapti continues without active mind

### **nirodha-samāpatti**

- **Provisional meaning:** attainment of cessation
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā; later treated in detail
- **Function:** second example of avijñapti continuing during absence of ordinary mental activity

### **prāpti-vāha**

- **Provisional meaning:** stream of acquisition
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** rival kind of wholesome or unwholesome continuity from which avijñapti must be distinguished

### **hetvartha**

- **Provisional meaning:** causal meaning
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** Vaibhāṣika explanation of `upādāya`

### **rūpakriyāsvabhāva**

- **Provisional meaning:** possessing the nature of form and action
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** prevents avijñapti from being interpreted as mere absence or purely mental residue

### **vijñapti-saṃbhūta**

- **Provisional meaning:** produced from manifest bodily or verbal disclosure
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** identifies one origin of avijñapti

### **samādhi-saṃbhūta**

- **Provisional meaning:** produced from meditative concentration
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** identifies the second origin of avijñapti

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass translation is broadly correct but should be revised in four respects.

### A. `vikṣipta`

It should not be restricted to a psychologically agitated mind.

```text
vikṣipta-citta
    = a mind directed to something else
```

### B. Scope of `api`

The verse's wording does not confine avijñapti to distraction and mindlessness. The Bhāṣya explicitly includes the undistracted conscious state.

### C. `anubandha`

“Continuing trace” is useful, but `pravāha` shows that **continuing stream** is more exact.

### D. Nature of avijñapti

The first-pass phrase “non-manifest trace of action” risks making avijñapti sound merely residual. The Bhāṣya instead defines it as form and action that do not disclose something to another.

A later revised translation should therefore remain close to:

> The wholesome or unwholesome continuity that persists even for one whose mind is otherwise directed or absent, while depending upon the great elements, is called avijñapti.

The explanatory note should add that it also continues during undistracted consciousness and may arise from vijñapti or samādhi.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya supports a restrained distinction:

```text
vijñapti
    = action in the mode of outward disclosure

avijñapti
    = action preserved as non-disclosive material continuity
```

This gives avijñapti an unusual position. It is not the knowing of an action and not merely a remembered consequence. It is the persistence of ethical determination in the rūpa-domain.

Within the developing Knowledge Domain interpretation, this matters because the field correlated with vijñāna contains more than what is presently disclosed to cognition. Rūpa includes determinations that are causally and ethically real while remaining non-manifest to another knower.

That project interpretation should remain provisional. The Bhāṣya itself is making a more specific Abhidharma claim:

```text
not everything classified as rūpa
is an immediately perceptible object of disclosure
```

This prepares the transition to the great elements, whose causal function grounds both perceptible and non-disclosive form.
