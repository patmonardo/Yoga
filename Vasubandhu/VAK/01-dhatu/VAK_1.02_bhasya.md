# VAK_1.02 Bhāṣya

## Abhidharma in the Ultimate and Conventional Senses

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.02.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya sharpens or corrects the first-pass analysis, the result is recorded here as a translation decision for later review.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> prajñāmalā sānucarābhidharmaḥ tatprāptaye yāpi ca yacca śāstram /
>
> tasyārthato 'smin samanupraveśāt sa cāśrayo 'syety abhidharmakośam // VAkK_1.02 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Stainless prajñā together with its attendants is Abhidharma. So too are the defiled prajñā directed toward its attainment and the treatise composed for that attainment. Because that Abhidharma enters into this treatise through its meaning, or because that Abhidharma is the support from which this treatise has been drawn out, this is called the *Abhidharmakośa*.

The Bhāṣya does not present a merely twofold opposition between pure knowing and a book. It distinguishes:

```text
pāramārthika Abhidharma
    stainless prajñā with its attendants

sāṃketika Abhidharma
    defiled prajñā cultivated for its attainment
    together with its attendants
    and the treatise serving as its equipment
```

The term `kośa` is then explained in two different ways:

```text
Abhidharma is contained here by meaning
    → this treatise is like its treasury

this treatise has been drawn out from Abhidharma
    → Abhidharma is the treasury or source of this treatise
```

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question:

> *ko 'yam abhidharmaḥ nāma*
>
> What is this that is called Abhidharma?

and extends through the two explanations of the title:

> *ataḥ sa evāsyābhidharmaḥ kośa ityetac chāstram abhidharmakośam.*

It forms one continuous argument:

```text
question concerning the meaning of Abhidharma
    ↓
definition of ultimate Abhidharma
    ↓
analysis of prajñā, stainlessness, and attendants
    ↓
identification as an anāsrava five-aggregate complex
    ↓
definition of conventional Abhidharma
    ↓
three modes of cultivated prajñā and acquired prajñā
    ↓
the treatise as equipment for attaining stainless prajñā
    ↓
etymological interpretation of dharma and abhidharma
    ↓
first derivation of kośa: containment by meaning
    ↓
second derivation of kośa: source from which the treatise is extracted
```

The passage should therefore be read as a single definition of Abhidharma and of the title *Abhidharmakośa*.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. The question: What is Abhidharma?

> *ko 'yam abhidharmaḥ nāma?*

### Translation

> What is this that is called Abhidharma?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya begins by refusing to leave the title as an inherited proper name. The term must be defined before the work can proceed.

```text
Abhidharmakośa
    cannot be understood
        until
Abhidharma
    has been determined
```

The verse therefore begins with the nature of Abhidharma itself rather than with the nature of the book.

---

### B. Stainless prajñā with attendants

> *prajñā 'malā sānucarā 'bhidharmaḥ.*

### Translation

> Stainless prajñā together with its attendants is Abhidharma.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The definition contains three determinations:

```text
prajñā
    discriminative knowing / principle

amalā
    stainless, without outflows

sānucarā
    accompanied by its attendants
```

Abhidharma is not identified with prajñā in abstraction. It is prajñā in its stainless condition and in the full complex that accompanies its occurrence.

---

### C. Prajñā as discrimination of dharmas

> *tatra prajñā dharmapravicayaḥ.*

### Translation

> Here prajñā is the discrimination of dharmas.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya gives an operational definition:

```text
prajñā
    = dharma-pravicaya
    = discrimination of dharmas
```

This is more determinate than treating prajñā simply as wisdom. Prajñā performs a discriminative function upon the field of dharmas.

The definition also anticipates VAK 1.03, where `dharmapravicaya` will be presented as indispensable for calming the kleśas.

---

### D. Stainless means anāsrava

> *amaleti anāsravā.*

### Translation

> “Stainless” means without outflows.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The adjective `amalā` is not left at the level of a general image of purity. It is doctrinally specified as:

```text
amala
    = anāsrava
```

Thus the primary Abhidharma is not merely excellent or morally pure knowing. It belongs to the anāsrava field.

---

### E. “With attendants” means with its retinue

> *sānucareti saparivārā.*

### Translation

> “With attendants” means together with its retinue.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya glosses `anucara` with `parivāra`:

```text
sānucara
    = saparivāra
    = together with the accompanying complex
```

Prajñā does not occur as an isolated substance. The definition includes the dharmas accompanying it.

The Bhāṣya does not enumerate those attendants here. Their exact constitution should therefore remain provisional at this point.

---

### F. Ultimate Abhidharma as an anāsrava five-aggregate complex

> *evam anāsravaḥ pañcaskandhako 'bhidharma ity uktaṃ bhavati.*

### Translation

> In this way, Abhidharma is said to be an anāsrava complex consisting of the five aggregates.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is a major specification of the terse Kārikā definition.

```text
stainless prajñā with attendants
    =
anāsrava pañcaskandhaka
    an outflow-free five-aggregate complex
```

The ultimate Abhidharma is therefore not prajñā abstracted from its concrete occurrence. It is the complete five-aggregate complex insofar as stainless prajñā is present with its retinue.

This result requires caution. The Bhāṣya does not say that every anāsrava five-aggregate complex is simply interchangeable with Abhidharma in every context. It explains what is included when stainless prajñā occurs together with its attendants.

---

### G. The ultimate sense

> *eṣa tāvat pāramārthiko 'bhidharmaḥ.*

### Translation

> This, to begin with, is Abhidharma in the ultimate sense.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya explicitly names the first definition:

```text
pāramārthika Abhidharma
    = stainless prajñā with attendants
```

The contrast with the next definition is therefore not simply primary versus secondary in an informal sense. It is a distinction between:

```text
pāramārthika
    ultimate

sāṃketika
    conventional / designated
```

This distinction should be retained explicitly in later revision of the Kārikā analysis.

---

## 4. Conventional Abhidharma

> *sāṃketikas tu—tatprāptaye yāpi ca yacca śāstram.*

### Translation

> Abhidharma in the conventional sense, however, is the prajñā directed toward attaining that, and the treatise composed for that attainment.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The conventional sense is related to the ultimate sense through attainment:

```text
pāramārthika Abhidharma
    the stainless result

sāṃketika Abhidharma
    the cultivated knowing and textual equipment
    ordered toward attaining that result
```

The conventional Abhidharma is not unrelated discourse that happens to bear the same name. It receives the name because of its functional relation to the attainment of ultimate Abhidharma.

---

### A. The three cultivated forms of prajñā

> *yāpi ca śrutacintābhāvanāmayī sāsravā prajñā upapattipratilambhikā ca sānucarā.*

### Translation

> This includes the sāsrava prajñā constituted by hearing, reflection, and cultivation, as well as the prajñā acquired by birth, together with their attendants.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya identifies four relevant forms of prajñā:

```text
śrutamayī prajñā
    prajñā constituted through hearing or learning

cintāmayī prajñā
    prajñā constituted through reflection

bhāvanāmayī prajñā
    prajñā constituted through cultivation

upapattipratilambhikā prajñā
    prajñā acquired through birth
```

These are described here as `sāsrava`. They belong to the conditioned path toward stainless prajñā rather than already being the ultimate Abhidharma.

The passage establishes an important asymmetry:

```text
ultimate Abhidharma
    anāsrava

prajñā cultivated for its attainment
    sāsrava
```

The defiled or outflow-associated status of the means does not prevent it from serving as the path toward the outflow-free result.

---

### B. Their attendants

The phrase `sānucarā` continues to govern these forms of prajñā.

```text
hearing-based prajñā
reflection-based prajñā
cultivation-based prajñā
birth-acquired prajñā
    are each considered
        with their accompanying complexes
```

The Bhāṣya thus preserves the concrete occurrence of knowing in both the ultimate and conventional definitions.

---

## 5. The Treatise as Abhidharma

> *yacca śāstram asyāḥ prāptyartham anāsravāyāḥ prajñāyāḥ tad api tatsaṃbhārabhāvād abhidharma ity ucyate.*

### Translation

> And the treatise composed for the attainment of that anāsrava prajñā is also called Abhidharma because it has the character of equipment for it.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The treatise is called Abhidharma by virtue of `saṃbhāra`:

```text
śāstra
    serves as saṃbhāra
    equipment / provision / requisite accumulation
        for
anāsrava prajñā
```

This is more precise than saying merely that the treatise “supports” pure prajñā. The text belongs to Abhidharma conventionally because it functions as equipment for attaining the ultimate Abhidharma.

The relation is teleological:

```text
śāstra
    is ordered toward
attainment of anāsrava prajñā
```

The book is not itself ultimate Abhidharma, but it is not external to the path of its production.

---

## 6. The Derivation of Dharma

> *nirvacanaṃ tu svalakṣaṇadhāraṇād dharmaḥ.*

### Translation

> As for the derivation: a dharma is so called because it bears its own determination.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya derives `dharma` from the root `dhṛ`, to hold or bear:

```text
dharma
    bears
svalakṣaṇa
    its own determination
```

The standard rendering “own characteristic” remains possible, but the project rendering “self-determination” preserves the logical force more clearly.

This sentence does not yet provide a full ontology of dharmas. It gives the local etymological basis needed for the term `abhidharma`.

---

## 7. The Derivation of Abhidharma

> *tad ayaṃ paramārthadharmaṃ vā nirvāṇaṃ dharmalakṣaṇaṃ vā pratyabhimukho dharma ity abhidharmaḥ.*

### Translation

> Accordingly, this dharma is directed toward the ultimate dharma, nirvāṇa, or toward the determination of dharmas; therefore it is called Abhidharma.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya offers two objects toward which Abhidharma is directed:

```text
paramārtha-dharma
    the ultimate dharma
    identified here as nirvāṇa

or

dharma-lakṣaṇa
    the determination of dharmas
```

The prefix `abhi-` is interpreted through directedness:

```text
pratyabhimukha
    facing toward
    directed toward
```

Thus:

```text
Abhidharma
    = a dharma directed toward nirvāṇa
      or toward the determinations of dharmas
```

This definition holds together the practical and discriminative orientations of Abhidharma:

```text
orientation toward nirvāṇa
    and
orientation toward determining dharmas
```

The passage does not force a choice between them. Both are supplied as valid explanations.

---

## 8. Transition from Abhidharma to the Kośa

> *ukto 'bhidharmaḥ. idaṃ tu śāstraṃ katham abhidharmakośam?*

### Translation

> Abhidharma has now been explained. But how is this treatise an *Abhidharmakośa*?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The question changes level:

```text
first question
    What is Abhidharma?

second question
    Why is this particular treatise called its kośa?
```

The answer will depend on the relation between Abhidharma and this work.

---

## 9. First Meaning of Kośa: Abhidharma Contained Here

> *tasyārthato 'smin samanupraveśāt.*

### Translation

> Because that Abhidharma enters fully into this treatise through its meaning.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The relation is not one of literal physical containment. Abhidharma is present:

```text
arthataḥ
    through meaning
    in respect of meaning
```

and it `samanupraviśati`:

```text
enters comprehensively
    into this treatise
```

The Bhāṣya explains:

> *sa hi śāstrasaṃjñako 'bhidharma etasminn arthato yathāpradhānam antarbhūta ity etac chāstraṃ tasya kośasthānīyaṃ bhavati.*

### Translation

> For the Abhidharma designated as treatise is included here through its meaning, according to what is principal; therefore this treatise occupies the place of a treasury for it.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The qualification `yathāpradhānam` is important:

```text
not every possible Abhidharma statement
    is reproduced without remainder

rather

the principal meaning of the śāstric Abhidharma
    is included here
```

Under this derivation:

```text
Abhidharma
    is contained by meaning
        in
this treatise

therefore

this treatise
    is kośasthānīya
    treasury-like / occupying the place of a treasury
```

Here the Kośa is the container.

---

## 10. Second Meaning of Kośa: Abhidharma as the Source

> *athavā so 'bhidharma etasyāśrayabhūtaḥ śāstrasya.*

### Translation

> Or else that Abhidharma is the supporting basis of this treatise.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The direction of dependence is now reversed:

```text
first derivation
    the treatise contains Abhidharma

second derivation
    Abhidharma supports the treatise
```

The Bhāṣya continues:

> *tato hy etan nirākṛṣṭam.*

### Translation

> For this treatise has been drawn out from it.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The treatise is extracted from the prior Abhidharma tradition or corpus:

```text
Abhidharma
    source / supporting treasury
        ↓
this treatise
    drawn out from it
```

The conclusion follows:

> *ataḥ sa evāsyābhidharmaḥ kośa ityetac chāstram abhidharmakośam.*

### Translation

> Therefore that Abhidharma itself is the treasury belonging to this treatise, and this treatise is accordingly called the *Abhidharmakośa*.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Under this explanation, `kośa` no longer means only that this work stores Abhidharma. It means that Abhidharma is the treasury from which this work has been extracted.

The title is therefore reciprocal:

```text
this treatise is a treasury of Abhidharma
    because it contains its principal meaning

Abhidharma is the treasury of this treatise
    because the treatise is drawn out from it
```

## 11. Argument Summary

```text
Abhidharma must first be defined.
        ↓
In the ultimate sense it is stainless prajñā
with its attendants.
        ↓
The Bhāṣya specifies this as an anāsrava
five-aggregate complex.
        ↓
In the conventional sense Abhidharma includes
sāsrava prajñā ordered toward its attainment.
        ↓
This includes prajñā born from hearing,
reflection, cultivation, and birth-acquisition,
with their attendants.
        ↓
The treatise is also conventionally Abhidharma
because it serves as equipment for attaining
anāsrava prajñā.
        ↓
A dharma bears its own determination.
        ↓
Abhidharma is directed toward nirvāṇa
or toward the determinations of dharmas.
        ↓
The work is called Kośa because the principal
meaning of Abhidharma is contained within it.
        ↓
It is also called Kośa because it has been
drawn out from Abhidharma as its supporting treasury.
```

The unity of the passage is:

```text
stainless discrimination of dharmas
    ← attained through cultivated prajñā and śāstra
    → directed toward nirvāṇa and dharma-determination
```

## 14. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass file correctly recognized a distinction between pure prajñā and the supporting śāstra, but the Bhāṣya requires several refinements.

### Decision 1: State the ultimate/conventional distinction explicitly

```text
pāramārthika Abhidharma
    stainless prajñā with attendants

sāṃketika Abhidharma
    sāsrava path-prajñā with attendants
    and the treatise serving as equipment
```

The first-pass language of “primary” and “secondary” is directionally correct but less exact than Vasubandhu’s own distinction.

### Decision 2: Do not identify the conventional sense only with the treatise

The Kārikā phrase `tatprāptaye yāpi` refers to prajñā directed toward the attainment of stainless prajñā. The Bhāṣya identifies:

```text
śrutamayī
cintāmayī
bhāvanāmayī
upapattipratilambhikā
```

all with their attendants.

The conventional Abhidharma therefore includes both path-prajñā and śāstra.

### Decision 3: Record the five-aggregate specification

```text
stainless prajñā with attendants
    = anāsrava pañcaskandhaka
```

This should be added to the later Kārikā revision as a Bhāṣya-derived doctrinal specification, not silently inserted into the literal translation.

### Decision 4: Refine the role of the treatise

The treatise is not merely a general support. It is called Abhidharma because it is:

```text
tatsaṃbhāra
    equipment or provision for attaining anāsrava prajñā
```

### Decision 5: Preserve both derivations of kośa

The first-pass file treats the Kośa mainly as a repository or support. The Bhāṣya gives two distinct relations:

```text
Abhidharma enters this treatise by meaning
    → the treatise is a treasury of Abhidharma

this treatise is drawn out from Abhidharma
    → Abhidharma is the treasury/source of the treatise
```

Both should be preserved in the later revision.

### Decision 6: Preserve the etymological orientation of Abhidharma

The later Kārikā note should include:

```text
dharma
    bears its own determination

Abhidharma
    is directed toward nirvāṇa
    or toward the determinations of dharmas
```

The existing Kārikā file remains unchanged during this Bhāṣya commit.

## 15. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya supplies a more rigorous relation than the simple formula “principle, attainment, support.”

```text
pāramārthika Abhidharma
    the attained actuality of stainless discrimination

sāṃketika Abhidharma
    the conditioned formation of prajñā and textual equipment
    through which that actuality is attained
```

The relation is not static identity:

```text
conditioned prajñā and śāstra
    are not yet
stainless prajñā

but

they are named from their directedness
    toward its attainment
```

The title *Abhidharmakośa* then expresses reciprocal mediation:

```text
Abhidharma enters the Kośa through meaning
    while

the Kośa is drawn out from Abhidharma as its source
```

This is a useful structural result, but it remains a local interpretation of the passage rather than a completed global ontology of the Bhāṣya.
