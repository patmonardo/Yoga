# VAK_1.30 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> tridhānye kāmadhātvāptāḥ sarve rūpe caturdaśa /
>
> vinā gandharasaghrāṇajihvāvijñānadhātubhiḥ // VAkK_1.30 //

### Translation

> The remaining [dhātus] are threefold [with respect to realm]. All eighteen belong to the desire realm. In the form realm there are fourteen, excluding smell, taste, and the olfactory- and gustatory-consciousness domains.

The Bhāṣya first explains the eighteen dhātus by realm and then enters a substantial dispute concerning the status of smell, taste, the nose and tongue faculties, and tangible objects in the form realm.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

### Translation

> Of these eighteen domains, how many belong to the desire realm and how many to the form realm?
>
> All belong to the desire realm.
>
> “Belong” means that they are not separated from it and are connected with the desire realm.
>
> In the form realm there are fourteen domains, excluding smell, taste, and the olfactory- and gustatory-consciousness domains.
>
> Smell and taste do not exist there because both belong to morsel-made food, whereas beings born there are detached from such food. Consequently, olfactory and gustatory consciousness do not exist there either, since their objects are absent.
>
> Objection: Then the tangible-object domain should also be absent there, since it too can belong to morsel-made food.
>
> Reply: That part of the tangible which is not itself food exists there. Smell and taste, however, cannot be enjoyed apart from ingestion, whereas tangible objects serve as the support of the faculties, as bodily support, and as covering. Therefore smell and taste are purposeless for those detached from eating, but tangible objects are not.
>
> Others say that, through dependence upon meditative absorption, forms are seen here, sounds are heard, and the body is benefited by a special tangible accompanied by pliancy. Therefore these three alone—form, sound, and tangible—can occur in birth through meditation, but smell and taste cannot.
>
> Objection: Then the nose and tongue faculties should also be absent, since they would have no purpose.
>
> Reply: They do have a purpose: without them the bodily support would lack beauty, and ordinary designation would be impossible.
>
> Objection: If that is their purpose, let there be merely the bodily sites for beauty and speech; they need not be faculties.
>
> Reply: A bodily site without a faculty is impossible, just as with the site of the sexual faculty.
>
> Counter-objection: The absence of that faculty is intelligible because it has no purpose. But the bodily sites of nose and tongue do have a purpose, so they could exist without the faculties.
>
> Reply: Even a purposeless faculty may arise, as in embryos certain to die.
>
> Objection: It may be purposeless, but it is not without a cause. What is the cause of the arising of a faculty?
>
> Reply: A particular karma produced by thirst for that faculty. One who is detached from the object is surely detached from the faculty as well. Thus the nose and tongue faculties should not occur among those detached from their objects.
>
> Objection: Why then does the sexual faculty not also remain?
>
> Reply: Because it would mar beauty.
>
> Counter-objection: Why would concealed sexual organs mar the beauty of a body hidden within its covering?
>
> Moreover, arising does not occur according to usefulness but according to causes. Therefore even something that mars beauty would arise when its cause is present.
>
> Objection: Then the sūtra saying that beings there are “complete and without deficient faculties” would be contradicted.
>
> Reply: They are complete with respect to whatever faculties exist there. Where is the contradiction? Otherwise the same consequence would apply to the sexual faculty.
>
> The accepted explanation is therefore this: the nose and tongue faculties do exist there, but smell and taste do not. Craving may operate toward the six sense spheres through one’s own embodied existence without operating through their external objects. With the sexual faculty, however, craving operates through sexual contact. It is therefore established that fourteen domains belong to the form realm.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. Realm-membership means connection, not mere spatial location

The gloss on `āpta` is precise:

> āptā aviyuktāḥ kāmadhātupratisaṃyuktā ityarthaḥ

A dhātu “belongs to” a realm when it is not separated from that realm and is connected with it. The classification is therefore relational and functional. It does not merely list what objects happen to occupy a place.

### B. Why smell and taste are excluded

The first reason is dietary:

> tayoḥ kavaḍīkārāhāratvāt

Smell and taste are tied to `kavaḍīkārāhāra`, morsel-made or gross ingested food. Beings born in the form realm are detached from that mode of nourishment. Smell and taste therefore lack a function there.

Their corresponding consciousnesses are absent for a more immediate reason:

> ālambanābhāvāt

Without their respective objects, olfactory and gustatory consciousness cannot arise.

This preserves the regular faculty–object–consciousness structure. The consciousness-domains disappear because their object-domains are unavailable.

### C. Why tangible objects remain

The objector notes that tangible objects may also be connected with gross food. The reply distinguishes the edible tangible from tangible form as such.

Tangible form remains because it serves several non-dietary functions:

- support of the faculties (`indriyāśraya`),
- bodily foundation (`ādhāra`),
- covering or clothing (`prāvaraṇa`).

The Bhāṣya therefore does not classify a domain by one incidental use. It determines whether the domain has functions that remain necessary in the higher realm.

### D. Alternative meditative explanation

A cited alternative argues from meditative experience:

- forms are seen,
- sounds are heard,
- the body is benefited by a special tangible associated with pliancy (`prasrabdhi`).

These three domains therefore have a basis in meditative birth. Smell and taste do not.

This is explicitly introduced as another view:

> anye punar āhuḥ

It should not be silently attributed to Vasubandhu as his own unqualified position.

### E. Do nose and tongue faculties remain?

The debate then becomes more difficult. If smell and taste are absent, why retain their faculties?

Several arguments are tested:

1. **Beauty and conventional designation** — the nose and tongue preserve the completeness and appearance of the body.
2. **A bodily site requires a faculty** — rejected through counterexample and further analysis.
3. **Purposeless faculties can arise** — granted, but only where a causal basis exists.
4. **Karma generated by thirst for a faculty** — challenged because detachment from the object should entail detachment from the faculty.
5. **Scriptural completeness of faculties** — interpreted relative to the faculties that exist in that realm.

The final accepted account is:

> sta eva tatra ghrāṇajihvendriye na tu gandharasau

The nose and tongue faculties exist in the form realm, but smell and taste do not.

The reason is that craving may operate toward the six internal spheres through attachment to embodied existence itself, even when it does not operate toward the external smell and taste objects. The sexual faculty differs because craving for it operates through sexual contact, which is absent in the form realm.

### F. Fourteen dhātus in the form realm

The excluded four are:

- gandhadhātu — smell-domain,
- rasadhātu — taste-domain,
- ghrāṇavijñānadhātu — olfactory-consciousness domain,
- jihvāvijñānadhātu — gustatory-consciousness domain.

The nose and tongue faculties themselves remain. Hence the count is fourteen rather than twelve.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
kāmadhātu
    = all eighteen dhātus

rūpadhātu
    = fourteen dhātus
    = eighteen minus:
        smell
        taste
        olfactory consciousness
        gustatory consciousness
```

The governing argument is:

```text
absence of gross ingestion
    → absence of smell and taste as useful object-domains

absence of those objects
    → absence of their corresponding consciousness-domains

continued embodied form
    → continued need for tangible support

attachment to embodied existence
    → nose and tongue faculties may remain
```

The passage also establishes an important methodological point:

```text
realm-membership
    ≠ mere location

realm-membership
    = causal, functional, and object-relational compatibility
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### āpta

- **Provisional meaning:** belonging to; connected with a realm
- **Status:** Bhāṣya gloss
- **Function:** Indicates non-separation and connection with a realm, not merely spatial presence.

### kavaḍīkārāhāra

- **Provisional meaning:** morsel-made food; gross ingested nourishment
- **Status:** Technical Abhidharma term
- **Function:** Explains why smell and taste are absent in the form realm.

### ālambanābhāva

- **Provisional meaning:** absence of an object-support
- **Status:** Causal explanation
- **Function:** Grounds the absence of olfactory and gustatory consciousness when smell and taste are absent.

### indriyāśraya

- **Provisional meaning:** support of a faculty
- **Status:** Functional description
- **Function:** Explains why tangible form remains in the form realm.

### prasrabdhi

- **Provisional meaning:** pliancy; bodily and mental ease associated with meditation
- **Status:** Technical meditative term
- **Function:** In the alternative account, explains the special tangible that benefits the body in meditative attainment.

### ātmabhāvamukha

- **Provisional meaning:** through the aspect of one’s own embodied existence
- **Status:** Explanatory expression
- **Function:** Explains how craving for internal sense faculties can remain without craving for their external objects.

## 6. Voice Register

- **Vasubandhu / primary exposition:** All eighteen dhātus belong to the desire realm; fourteen belong to the form realm; smell, taste, and their two consciousnesses are absent.
- **Objector:** Tangible objects should also be absent; the nose and tongue faculties should be absent; bodily sites might remain without faculties; causal and scriptural objections.
- **Primary reply:** Tangible objects have non-food functions; object-absence removes the corresponding consciousnesses; faculty completeness is realm-relative.
- **Cited alternative (`anye punar āhuḥ`):** Form, sound, and tangible remain because they are encountered or function in meditative absorption.
- **Accepted concluding explanation (`evaṃ tu varṇayanti`):** Nose and tongue faculties remain, while smell and taste do not, because craving may attach to the internal sixfold embodied basis independently of the external objects.
- **Doctrinal affiliation:** The passage transmits a scholastic debate; not every intermediate reply should be treated as Vasubandhu’s final personal commitment.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The existing Kārikā translation is substantially correct. Two refinements are required:

1. `āpta` should be understood as **belonging to / connected with a realm**, not merely “located in.”
2. The excluded `ghrāṇajihvāvijñānadhātus` are specifically the two consciousness-domains; the nose and tongue faculty-domains remain.

Preferred translation:

> The remaining [dhātus] are threefold [with respect to realm]. All eighteen belong to the desire realm. Fourteen belong to the form realm, excluding smell, taste, and the olfactory- and gustatory-consciousness domains.

No change is made to the Kārikā file during this Bhāṣya pass.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya here supports a restrained structural observation:

```text
realm
    = a causally and functionally coherent configuration of domains
```

A domain is not retained simply because its anatomical site can be imagined. Its inclusion depends upon object availability, causal production, functional necessity, karmic conditions, and the mode of craving proper to that realm.

This may later contribute to an Organon account of a field as a lawful configuration of capacities, objects, and acts. But the present passage is a Sarvāstivāda analysis of realm-membership, not an explicit theory of Buddha Mind or idealist projection.
