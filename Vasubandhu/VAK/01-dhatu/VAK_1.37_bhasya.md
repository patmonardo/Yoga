> The five internal domains are born of maturation and accumulation. Sound is not born of maturation. The eight non-resistant domains are born of continuity and maturation.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. The classificatory question

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> How many domains are born of maturation? How many are produced by accumulation? How many arise through continuity? How many possess a real substance? How many are momentary?

VAK 1.37 answers the first three questions. The remaining two lead into VAK 1.38.

### 2. The five internal faculties have two modes of genesis

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> To begin with, the five internal domains—the eye and the others—are both born of maturation and produced by accumulation.

These are the five faculty-domains:

```text
cakṣurdhātu
śrotradhātu
ghrāṇadhātu
jihvādhātu
kāyadhātu
```

They are not classified here as separate *naiḥṣyandika* products, because no continuity-product distinct from their maturation and accumulated continuities is admitted.

### 3. *Vipākaja*: born from the maturation-ground

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Those born from a maturation-ground are called *vipākaja*.

The Bhāṣya then explains the compound. The middle member is omitted, as in compounds such as *goratha*:

```text
vipāka-hetu-ja
    born from a maturation-ground

→ vipāka-ja
```

A second explanation treats karma that has reached the time of its result as *vipāka*, because it is undergoing maturation. What arises from it is therefore *vipākaja*.

The result itself may also be called *vipāka*, since it is the completed maturation. Vasubandhu permits this reciprocal transfer of language between ground and result:

```text
ground called by the name of its result
result called by the name of its ground
```

The cited sūtra—“These six contact-spheres should be understood as old karma”—illustrates designation of the result by the name of its karmic ground.

### 4. *Aupacayika*: organically built up

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Those built up through nourishment, conditioning, sleep, and distinctions of concentration are *aupacayika*.

Some add celibate discipline (*brahmacarya*). Vasubandhu qualifies this addition:

> Through celibate discipline there may be merely non-injury, not positive increase.

The distinction is exact:

```text
anupaghāta
    preservation from injury

upacaya
    positive organic increase or reinforcement
```

The accumulated continuity protects the maturation-continuity like a surrounding wall:

```text
vipāka-santāna
    karmically matured organic continuity

upacaya-santāna
    nutritionally and conditionally reinforced continuity

relation
    protection, like an enclosing wall
```

### 5. Sound may be accumulated and continuity-born, but not maturation-born

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Sound may be produced by accumulation and may arise through continuity. But sound is not born of maturation.

Why?

> Because it proceeds from effort.

The decisive expression is `īhātaḥ pravṛtteḥ`: sound occurs through an exertion or initiating activity. It is therefore not the passive maturation-result that the five organic faculties are.

### 6. Objection: Brahmā’s voice is said to result from karma

**Voice: Objector**

The *Prajñaptiśāstra* says that through the well-cultivated abstention from harsh speech, the great-person mark of a Brahmā-like voice is produced. Does this not make sound a maturation-result?

### 7. Proposed mediated answers

**Voice: uncertain doctrinal respondents**

One answer calls the voice a third-order result:

```text
karma
    → great elements
        → sound
```

Another calls it a fifth-order result:

```text
karma
    → maturation-born great elements
        → accumulated great elements
            → continuity-born great elements
                → sound
```

These explanations preserve a karmic ground for the bodily conditions of voice while denying that the presently produced sound is itself directly *vipākaja*.

### 8. Vasubandhu tests the distinction by bodily feeling

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> In that case, bodily feeling too, since it is produced from great elements born of karma, would fail to be a maturation-result.

The reply is:

> That would follow only if, as with sound, it contradicted reasoning.

Thus mere remoteness in a causal series does not by itself exclude maturation. Sound is excluded because its immediate production through effort conflicts with treating it as karmic maturation.

### 9. The eight non-resistant domains

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The eight are:

```text
seven citta-domains
+
dharma-domain
```

They are *naiḥṣyandika* when generated by homogeneous and universally operative grounds:

```text
sabhāgahetu
sarvatragahetu
```

They are *vipākaja* when generated by a maturation-ground:

```text
vipākahetu
```

They are not *aupacayika*, because non-resistant domains are not accumulated material masses.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Question:
How are the domains differentiated by mode of genesis?

Determination 1:
The five internal faculty-domains are maturation-born.

Ground:
They arise from karma at the time of matured result.

Determination 2:
The same five are also organically accumulated.

Ground:
Nourishment, conditioning, sleep, and concentration reinforce their continuity.

Important distinction:
Preservation from injury is not identical with positive increase.

Determination 3:
Sound can be accumulated and continuity-born, but it is not maturation-born.

Ground:
Its occurrence proceeds from effort.

Objection:
A Brahmā-like voice is taught as the result of abstention from harsh speech.

Mediated answer:
Karma produces the organic material conditions from which sound subsequently proceeds.

Test case:
Bodily feeling may be causally remote from karma and nevertheless be maturation-born.

Resolution:
Causal remoteness is not decisive. The operative criterion is whether treating the event as maturation contradicts its immediate mode of production.

Determination 4:
The eight non-resistant domains may be continuity-born or maturation-born, but not accumulated.

Ground:
They have causal continuity without atomic or organic mass-accumulation.
```

## Translation Consequences

### 1. *Vipākaja*

“Born of maturation” remains correct, but the Bhāṣya supplies its complete causal form:

```text
vipākaja
    = vipāka-hetu-ja
    = born from a maturation-ground
```

It should not be reduced to the loose English “resultant.” The term expresses a specific genetic relation between karma arriving at fruition and the dharma generated from it.

### 2. *Aupacayika*

“Produced by accumulation” needs organic force here. The Bhāṣya identifies nourishment, conditioning, sleep, and concentration as modes of reinforcement.

Preferred working rendering:

```text
aupacayika
    = organically accumulated
    = produced by organic reinforcement
```

### 3. *Naiḥṣyandika*

The Bhāṣya specifies this through generation by *sabhāgahetu* and *sarvatragahetu*. “Continuity-born” remains preferable to a vague “natural outflow,” because the term marks causal reproduction within a homogeneous series.

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> The five internal faculty-domains arise both from karmic maturation and through organic reinforcement. Sound, although it may arise through material accumulation and homogeneous continuity, is not itself a maturation-result, because its immediate production proceeds from effort. The seven knowing-domains together with the dharma-domain arise either through causal continuity or karmic maturation, but not through material accumulation.

## Doctrinal Determination

The five faculties are not merely pieces of gross matter. They have a twofold organic genesis:

```text
karma
    → matured faculty-continuity

nourishment, sleep, concentration, conditioning
    → reinforced faculty-continuity
```

This deepens the result of VAK 1.36. The visible bodily member is not simply identical with the faculty, and the faculty itself is not literally the Seer. Rather, it is an internally generated and maintained capacity whose primary function is for apprehension.

The present Bhāṣya unit does not itself introduce a metaphysical Seer. Its textual contribution is more disciplined:

```text
indriya
    is an internal organic capacity
    generated by maturation
    reinforced through living conditions
    and ordered toward cognitive operation
```

Thus the faculty can be understood as operating **for the sake of seeing or apprehending**, without being identified either with the gross seen organ or with the Seer.

Sound provides the contrasting case:

```text
faculty
    matured and organically maintained capacity

sound
    event produced through present effort
```

The distinction is therefore not simply internal versus external. It is a distinction in genetic and functional mode.

---

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya introduces a layered account of organic instrumentality:

```text
maturation
    gives the faculty its karmically generated continuity

accumulation
    maintains and strengthens that continuity

operation
    actualizes the capacity in apprehension
```

The faculty is therefore neither an inert object nor an independent subject:

```text
not merely seen anatomical matter
not the Seer itself

but

a mediated capacity operating for apprehension
```

This is a more exact determination than identifying the indriya with either pole.

The treatment of sound adds a second distinction:

```text
remote karmic conditioning
    ≠
direct maturation-result
```

A present event may depend upon karmically generated conditions without itself being *vipākaja*. The criterion is the event’s immediate genetic form.

## Organon Contact Point

The principal Organon result is the distinction among:

```text
subject of apprehension
instrument of apprehension
object or event apprehended
```

The indriya belongs primarily to the middle term:

```text
Seer
    ↓ operates through
indriya
    ↓ engages
object-domain
```

This is not yet asserted by Vasubandhu as a Sāṃkhya metaphysics. It is our disciplined contact point with the Bhāṣya’s refusal to collapse faculty into gross anatomy and with its genetic account of the faculty as an internal living capacity.

The new development for the larger project is therefore:

> The indriya should not be identified with the Seer, but understood as the internally generated and sustained instrument that operates primarily for the sake of the Seer’s apprehension.

This preserves mediation. The faculty is neither merely Seen nor itself the Seer; its truth lies in its operative relation between the two.
