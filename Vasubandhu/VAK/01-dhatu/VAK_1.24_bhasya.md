# VAK_1.24 Bhāṣya

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> viśeṣaṇārthaṃ prādhānyād bahudharmāgrasaṃgrahāt /
>
> ekam āyatanaṃ rūpam ekaṃ dharmākhyam ucyate // VAkK_1.24 //

The verse asks why, although ten āyatanas belong to rūpaskandha, only one is specifically called **rūpāyatana**, and why, although all entities are dharmas, only one āyatana is specifically called **dharmāyatana**.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

> kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇaṃ daśasvāyataneṣu rūpaskandhasaṃgṛhīteṣvekaṃ rūpāyatanamucyate /
>
> sarveṣu ca dharmasvabhaveṣvekaṃ dharmāyatanamityāha /
>
> viśeṣaṇārthaṃ prādhānyādbahudharmāgrasaṃgrahāt /
>
> ekamāyatanaṃ rūpamekaṃ dharmākhyamucyate // VAkK_1.24 //
>
> kathaṃ viśeṣaṇārtham /
>
> yathā gamyeta pratyekameṣāṃ daśānāmāyatanatvaṃ viṣayaviṣayitvena vyavasthānaṃ na samastānāmiti /
>
> cakṣurādibhiśca viśeṣitairyanna cakṣurādisaṃjñakaṃ rūpaṃ ca tadrūpakayātanaṃ jñāsyata ityasya punarnāmāntaraṃ nocyate /
>
> athavā rūpāyatanasya prādhānyāt /
>
> taddhi sapratighatvācca pāṇyādisaṃsparśaiḥ spṛṣṭaṃ rūpyate /
>
> sanidarśanatvācca idamihāmutreti nirūpaṇāt /
>
> loke 'pi ca tadrūpamiti pratītaṃ nānyāni /
>
> viśeṣaṇārthameva caikaṃ dharmāyatanamuktaṃ na sarvāṇi /
>
> api cātra bahūnāṃ dharmāṇāṃ saṃgraho vedanādīnām /
>
> ataḥ sāmānyenābhidhānaṃ kriyate dharmaśabdena /
>
> agrasya ca nirvāṇadharmasyātra saṃgraho nānyeṣviti /
>
> viṃśatiprakāratvenaudārikatvānmāṃsadivyāryaprajñācakṣustrayagocaratvāccaikaṃ rūpāyatanamityapare //

### Translation

Why, when ten āyatanas are included in the rūpaskandha, is only one called the rūpa-sphere? And why, when all entities have the nature of dharmas, is only one called the dharma-sphere?

The verse answers: for the sake of specification; because of predominance; and because it gathers many dharmas and the foremost dharma, one sphere is called rūpa and one is called dharma.

How is this “for the sake of specification”? It allows one to understand that each of these ten is individually an āyatana, established through the relation of object and object-possessor, rather than that all ten collectively constitute one āyatana.

Further, once the other material spheres have been specified by names such as eye and so forth, the remaining rūpa that is not called eye and so forth is understood as the rūpāyatana. No additional name is therefore given to it.

Alternatively, it is called rūpāyatana because of its predominance. It is subject to resistance, since it is affected when touched by the hand and so forth; and it is visible, since it can be indicated as “this, here, or there.” In ordinary usage too, this alone is widely recognized as rūpa, not the other material factors.

Likewise, only one sphere is called dharmāyatana for the sake of specification, not all the spheres. Moreover, many dharmas—feeling and the rest—are gathered within it. Hence it is designated generally by the word dharma. And the foremost dharma, nirvāṇa, is included here and not in the other spheres.

Others say that the rūpāyatana is single because it has twenty varieties, because it is gross, and because it is the range of three kinds of eye: the fleshly eye, the divine eye, and the noble eye of wisdom.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 The opening problem is terminological, not ontological

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The issue is not why only one material reality exists, nor why only one dharma exists. The issue is why one member of a larger class receives the unqualified class-name.

```text
all ten material āyatanas
    belong to rūpaskandha

but only one
    is named rūpāyatana
```

Likewise:

```text
all entities are dharmas

but only one sphere
    is named dharmāyatana
```

The Bhāṣya therefore explains a rule of technical naming.

### 3.2 Viśeṣaṇa distinguishes each sphere as a separate relational field

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The first explanation is `viśeṣaṇārtham`: the special name prevents us from mistaking the ten material āyatanas for one collective sphere.

Each āyatana is separately established through a polarity:

```text
viṣaya
    object

viṣayin
    that which has or apprehends the object
```

Thus each faculty and each object-field has its own āyatana-status. The system is distributive, not merely collective.

### 3.3 Rūpāyatana is the residual material object-field after the faculties are named

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body have already received their own specific names. The remaining material factor that is not called by one of those faculty-names is called simply `rūpa`.

This is not a residual category in the weak sense. It is the specifically visible object-field that remains once the bodily supports have been distinguished.

### 3.4 Prādhānya explains the ordinary prominence of visible form

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The second explanation is predominance.

Rūpāyatana is prominent because it is:

```text
sapratigha
    resistant / obstructive

sanidarśana
    visible and indicable
```

It can be located as “this,” “here,” or “there,” and ordinary speech especially applies the word `rūpa` to visible form.

The passage therefore distinguishes technical inclusion from ordinary lexical prominence:

```text
technical Abhidharma
    many material factors are rūpa

ordinary usage
    visible form is especially called rūpa
```

### 3.5 Dharmāyatana receives the generic name because it gathers heterogeneous dharmas

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Dharmāyatana includes many kinds of dharmas, beginning with vedanā and the other non-sensory factors. Since no narrower single name adequately covers this heterogeneous collection, it receives the general name `dharma`.

This is a controlled generality, not a vague remainder.

### 3.6 Nirvāṇa is the agra-dharma included only here

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The compound `bahudharmāgrasaṃgrahāt` is clarified by the prose as two linked reasons:

```text
bahūnāṃ dharmāṇāṃ saṃgrahaḥ
    inclusion of many dharmas

agrasya nirvāṇadharmasya saṃgrahaḥ
    inclusion of the foremost dharma, nirvāṇa
```

Therefore the earlier Kārikā rendering “because it gathers many foremost dharmas” should be refined. The point is not that all the many included dharmas are foremost. Rather:

```text
many dharmas
+ the foremost dharma
    are gathered in dharmāyatana
```

### 3.7 An alternative explanation of rūpāyatana is reported

**Voice:** Other teachers (`apare`)

The final sentence gives a separate account:

- rūpa has twenty varieties;
- it is gross;
- it is the object-range of three eyes: fleshly, divine, and noble wisdom.

This explanation is reported, not explicitly adopted as Vasubandhu’s own decisive account.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Question:
Why is only one material sphere called rūpa,
and only one sphere called dharma?

Answer for rūpāyatana:
1. specification of the ten separate āyatanas
2. visible form remains after the faculties are specially named
3. visible form is predominant in resistance, visibility, and ordinary usage

Answer for dharmāyatana:
1. specification prevents all spheres from being called dharmāyatana
2. it gathers many heterogeneous dharmas
3. it uniquely includes the foremost dharma, nirvāṇa
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### viśeṣaṇa

- **Provisional meaning:** specification, differentiation
- **Status:** Bhāṣya technical explanation
- **Function:** explains why one member of a larger class receives a special unqualified name

### viṣayaviṣayitva

- **Provisional meaning:** object and object-possessor relation
- **Status:** Bhāṣya relational vocabulary
- **Function:** establishes each āyatana individually rather than collectively

### prādhānya

- **Provisional meaning:** predominance, prominence
- **Status:** explanatory principle
- **Function:** explains why visible form especially receives the name rūpa

### sapratigha

- **Provisional meaning:** resistant, obstructive, subject to impact
- **Status:** material characteristic
- **Function:** marks visible form as physically affectable

### sanidarśana

- **Provisional meaning:** visible, demonstrable, spatially indicable
- **Status:** material characteristic
- **Function:** allows rūpa to be pointed out as this, here, or there

### agra-dharma

- **Provisional meaning:** foremost dharma
- **Status:** Bhāṣya designation of nirvāṇa
- **Function:** explains the special scope and dignity of dharmāyatana

### māṃsacakṣus / divyacakṣus / āryaprajñācakṣus

- **Provisional meaning:** fleshly eye, divine eye, noble wisdom-eye
- **Status:** vocabulary from an alternative explanation
- **Function:** identifies three modes for which rūpa is an object-range

## 6. Voice Register

| Passage | Voice |
|---|---|
| Opening question concerning the names rūpāyatana and dharmāyatana | Objector or pedagogical question |
| Explanation through specification, predominance, ordinary usage, and nirvāṇa | Vasubandhu |
| Explanation through twenty varieties, grossness, and three eyes | Other teachers (`apare`) |

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

**Decision:** Refine.

The existing translation should be sharpened in two places.

First, `viśeṣaṇārtham` concerns technical specification: it distinguishes the individual āyatanas and explains why one sphere receives the unqualified name rūpa or dharma.

Second, `bahudharmāgrasaṃgrahāt` should not be rendered as though the sphere gathers “many foremost dharmas.” The Bhāṣya explicitly parses the reason as:

```text
many dharmas
+ the foremost dharma, nirvāṇa
```

A productive translation is:

> For the sake of specification, because of predominance, and because it gathers many dharmas together with the foremost dharma, one sphere is called rūpa and one is called dharma.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya shows two different principles of naming:

```text
rūpāyatana
    receives its name through phenomenal prominence

dharmāyatana
    receives its name through inclusive scope
```

This may later support an Organon distinction between a field named by dominant appearance and a field named by its power of principial inclusion. But the text itself is explaining Abhidharma terminology, not an idealist Projection/Factory ontology.
