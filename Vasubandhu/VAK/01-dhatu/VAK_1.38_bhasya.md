## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> tridhānye dravyavān ekaḥ kṣaṇikāḥ paścimās trayaḥ /
>
> cakṣurvijñānadhātvoḥ syāt pṛthag lābhaḥ sahāpi ca // VAkK_1.38 //

### Revised Kārikā Translation

> The other four are threefold. One domain possesses real substance. The final three may be momentary. The eye-domain and eye-knowledge-domain may be acquired separately and also together.

The corresponding Kārikā analysis is preserved in `VAK_1.38.md`. The Bhāṣya corrects and sharpens several points that had remained provisional there.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. The “other four” are explicitly identified

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The remaining four—the visible-form, taste, smell, and tangible domains—are of all three kinds: maturation-born, accumulation-produced, and continuity-born.

This resolves the ambiguity in the Kārikā analysis. `anye` does not refer vaguely to all remaining domains. It refers specifically to four material object-domains:

```text
rūpadhātu
gandhadhātu
rasadhātu
spraṣṭavyadhātu
```

Sound remains exceptional because it is not maturation-born.

### 2. The dharma-domain alone “possesses substance”

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The unconditioned is substance because of its essential solidity or core-status. Since the unconditioned is present in the dharma-domain, the dharma-domain alone is joined with substance.

The Bhāṣya’s reasoning is:

```text
asaṃskṛta
    is dravya because it has sāra

the asaṃskṛta is included in dharmadhātu

therefore

dharmadhātu alone is dravyayukta
```

`dravyavān` here does not mean that every member of the dharma-domain is an independently substantial entity. It means that this domain uniquely contains the unconditioned, which has the status of `dravya`.

### 3. Why the last three are called momentary

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The final three in the order of enumeration are:

```text
manodhātu
dharmadhātu
manovijñānadhātu
```

They are called `kṣaṇika` because, in the first uncontaminated moment—the complex of receptivity to dharma-knowledge concerning suffering—they exist for one moment without being continuity-born.

The Bhāṣya does **not** say that these three domains are always momentary while the others are not. Rather, it isolates a unique moment in which these three arise without a preceding homogeneous causal continuation.

### 4. The exceptional first uncontaminated moment

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Apart from dharmas arisen in that way, there is no conditioned dharma that is not continuity-born.

The point is genetic. Ordinarily, conditioned dharmas arise from a preceding homogeneous continuity. But the first uncontaminated moment cannot be generated by an earlier uncontaminated homogeneous cause, because none has yet occurred.

Thus:

```text
first anāsrava moment
    conditioned
    but not naiḥṣyandika
```

This is why `kṣaṇika` has a special technical sense in this passage.

### 5. Distribution of the three domains in that moment

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The citta associated with `duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti` counts as both:

```text
manodhātu
manovijñānadhātu
```

The remaining associated and co-arisen dharmas count as:

```text
dharmadhātu
```

The “last three” therefore name the complete cognitive event of the first uncontaminated receptivity:

```text
mind as support/domain
mental knowledge as knowing event
associated dharmas as dharma-domain
```

### 6. The acquisition question

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya asks whether one who newly acquires the eye-domain necessarily acquires eye-knowledge, and conversely whether one acquiring eye-knowledge necessarily acquires the eye-domain.

The answer is no. Their acquisition may occur separately or jointly.

### 7. Eye without eye-knowledge

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

One may acquire the eye-domain without simultaneously acquiring eye-knowledge:

- through gradual acquisition of the eye-faculty in the desire realm;
- when descending from the formless realm and being born in the second or higher dhyānas.

The faculty may therefore be present as an acquired capacity without a simultaneous acquisition of its corresponding knowledge-domain.

### 8. Eye-knowledge without eye

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

One may acquire eye-knowledge without newly acquiring the eye-domain:

- when one already born in the second or higher dhyānas brings eye-knowledge into manifestation;
- when falling from such a state and being born in a lower realm.

This sharpens the distinction between:

```text
possession of faculty
manifestation or acquisition of its cognitive operation
```

### 9. Joint acquisition

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Both may be acquired together when one descends from the formless realm and is born either in the desire realm or in the Brahmā world.

The Bhāṣya also leaves a fourth possibility: neither is acquired, apart from the stated cases.

### 10. Possession is analyzed by a catuṣkoṭi

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The relation between possession of eye and possession of eye-knowledge is fourfold:

```text
1. eye without presently manifest eye-knowledge
2. eye-knowledge relation without a functioning acquired eye in the relevant case
3. both
4. neither
```

The examples are conditioned by realm, meditative level, loss, acquisition, and present manifestation. The important structural result is that possession and manifestation do not collapse into one relation.

### 11. The method generalizes

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The same reasoning should be applied, as appropriate, to:

```text
eye-domain and visible-form domain
eye-knowledge domain and visible-form domain
```

The particle `ca` gathers these further cases into the scope of the analysis.

Answer:
Four external material object-domains:
    visible form

Ontological question:

Answer:
The dharma-domain,

In the first uncontaminated receptivity to dharma-knowledge,

Relational question:
Must acquisition of eye and eye-knowledge coincide?

    eye alone

## Translation Consequences

### 1. `tridhānye`

The prior Kārikā analysis left `anye` too broad. The Bhāṣya fixes it precisely:

```text
anye
    = the four remaining material object-domains
```

### 2. `dravyavān ekaḥ`

The earlier conjecture that the dharma-domain was intended is confirmed, but the reason must be stated exactly:

```text
dharmadhātu is dravyavān
not because every dharma is a substance
but because the unconditioned is contained within it
```

Preferred rendering:

> One domain possesses substance, namely the dharma-domain insofar as it contains the unconditioned.

### 3. `kṣaṇikāḥ paścimās trayaḥ`

The previous translation risked reading the final three as generically or universally momentary. The Bhāṣya gives a specialized meaning:

> The final three can occur in the unique first uncontaminated moment without being continuity-born.

This is not a general contrast between momentary mental dharmas and non-momentary material dharmas.

### 4. `lābha`

The Bhāṣya distinguishes acquisition from possession and manifestation:

```text
lābha / pratilambha
    acquisition

samanvāgama
    possession or endowment

saṃmukhīkaraṇa
    present manifestation
```

These terms should not be translated interchangeably.

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> The remaining four material object-domains may arise through maturation, organic accumulation, or homogeneous continuity. The dharma-domain alone possesses substantial content, because it includes the unconditioned. The final three domains have a uniquely momentary occurrence in the first uncontaminated receptivity, where they arise without prior homogeneous continuity. Eye-faculty and eye-knowledge may be acquired separately or together.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya now decisively distinguishes four layers:

```text
faculty
    cakṣurdhātu

possession of faculty
    samanvāgama

acquisition of faculty
    pratilambha / lābha

manifest cognitive operation
    cakṣurvijñāna-saṃmukhīkaraṇa
```

This strengthens the interpretation developed in the preceding units.

The indriya is not the Seer. Nor is it simply the visible anatomical member. It is an acquired and possessed capacity whose operation may or may not be presently manifest as knowledge.

More precisely:

```text
indriya
    exists for cognitive operation

but

possession of indriya
    ≠ present cognition
```

This is exactly why the eye and eye-knowledge can be acquired separately.

The faculty is therefore teleologically ordered toward apprehension without being identical with either:

```text
the subject for whose sake apprehension occurs
or
the act of apprehension itself
```

### The first uncontaminated moment

The account of `kṣaṇika` introduces a major genetic result. The Path begins with a conditioned event that does not arise from an earlier homogeneous Path-event:

```text
conditioned
but not continuity-born from an earlier anāsrava
```

The first uncontaminated cognition is thus a genuine beginning within the conditioned series. It is not unconditioned, yet it cannot be explained merely as reproduction of what preceded it.

## Logical Determination

### Faculty and operation

```text
faculty acquired
    does not entail
knowledge presently acquired or manifest

knowledge acquired or manifest
    does not entail
new acquisition of faculty
```

The relation is reciprocal but non-identical.

This gives a clearer mediated architecture:

```text
subject of apprehension
    ↓ employs
faculty-capacity
    ↓ under suitable conditions
cognitive manifestation
    ↓ directed toward
object-domain
```

The faculty occupies the middle without becoming either endpoint.

### The genetic beginning of the Path

The first uncontaminated moment establishes a second structure:

```text
contaminated continuity
    cannot homogeneously generate
an earlier uncontaminated continuity

therefore the first anāsrava moment
    is conditioned
    yet uniquely non-naiḥṣyandika
```

This is a strictly genetic determination, not a static classification.

## Organon Contact Point

The principal Organon advance is the distinction between **capacity**, **possession**, **acquisition**, and **actualization**.

```text
Capacity
    indriya

Possession
    samanvāgama

Acquisition
    lābha / pratilambha

Actualization
    saṃmukhīkaraṇa of vijñāna
```

This is directly useful for an agent architecture. A system may possess a capacity without executing it; it may newly acquire an operation without newly acquiring its underlying instrument; and capacity plus operation may arise together under other conditions.

The philosophical development to preserve is:

> The indriya operates for the sake of apprehension, but its possession is not identical with the occurrence of apprehension. It is the mediating capacity between the subject for whom cognition occurs and the knowledge-event through which the object is disclosed.

A second development also deserves later incorporation into the project’s governing statement:

> The first uncontaminated moment is a conditioned but non-reproductive beginning: a genuinely new Path-determination that cannot be explained as homogeneous continuation of an earlier Path-state.
