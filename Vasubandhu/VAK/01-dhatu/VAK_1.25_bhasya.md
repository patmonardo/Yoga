# VAK_1.25 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

> dharmaskandhasahasrāṇi yānyaśītiṃ jagau muniḥ /
>
> tāni vāṅnāma vetyeṣāṃ rūpasaṃskārasaṃgrahaḥ // VAkK_1.25 //

The completed Kārikā analysis established that the Buddha's eighty thousand Dharma-collections must be included within the already established aggregate-system. The Bhāṣya now explains exactly how that inclusion works.

## Natural Bhāṣya Unit

> anyāny api skandhāyatanadhātusaṃśabditāny upalabhyante sūtreṣu teṣāṃ kim ebhir eva saṃgraho veditavya āhosvid vyatirekaḥ /
>
> ebhir eva saṃgraho na vyatirekaḥ /
>
> tatra tāvat
>
> dharmaskandhasahasrāṇi yānyaśītiṃ jagau muniḥ /
>
> tāni vāṅnāma vetyeṣāṃ rūpasaṃskārasaṃgrahaḥ // VAkK_1.25 //
>
> yeṣāṃ vāksvabhāvaṃ buddhavacanaṃ teṣāṃ tāni rūpaskandhasaṃgṛhītāni /
>
> yeṣāṃ nāmasvabhāvaṃ teṣāṃ saṃskāraskandhena //
>
> kiṃ punar dharmaskandhasya pramāṇam /

### Translation

Other aggregates, spheres, and domains bearing those designations are also found in the sūtras. Should they be understood as included within these very categories, or as distinct from them?

They are included within these very categories; they are not distinct.

First of all:

> The eighty thousand Dharma-collections taught by the Sage—whether these are speech or name, they are included respectively within rūpa and saṃskāra.

For those who hold that the Buddha's word has speech as its intrinsic nature, those Dharma-collections are included within the aggregate of form. For those who hold that it has name as its intrinsic nature, they are included within the aggregate of formations.

But what is the measure of a Dharma-collection?

## Bhāṣya Analysis

### 1. The problem is exhaustiveness of classification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya opens with a general question. Sūtras sometimes speak of additional skandhas, āyatanas, or dhātus using names not found in the basic five, twelve, and eighteenfold schemes.

The issue is whether these additional designations introduce new ontological categories:

```text
additional scriptural designation
    → new category?

or

additional scriptural designation
    → included within the established categories?
```

Vasubandhu answers decisively:

```text
na vyatirekaḥ
    = there is no category outside the established schemes
```

The five aggregates, twelve spheres, and eighteen domains are therefore exhaustive at the level of strict Abhidharma classification.

### 2. The eighty thousand Dharma-collections provide the first test case

The eighty thousand `dharmaskandhas` taught by the Buddha appear at first to be additional “aggregates of Dharma.” The Bhāṣya refuses that inference.

Here `dharmaskandha` is not automatically identical with one of the five ontological aggregates. It can mean a collection, division, or body of teaching.

The classification must therefore proceed by asking what sort of existent the Buddha's teaching is.

### 3. First position: Buddha-word is speech

**Voice:** Reported doctrinal position

> yeṣāṃ vāksvabhāvaṃ buddhavacanam

For those who maintain that the Buddha's word has speech as its intrinsic nature, each Dharma-collection consists in articulated sound.

Speech is material sound and therefore belongs to rūpaskandha:

```text
Buddha-word as speech
    → audible sound
    → rūpaskandha
```

This is not a claim that the meaning of the teaching is material. It is a classification of the spoken teaching according to the intrinsic nature of its audible occurrence.

### 4. Second position: Buddha-word is name

**Voice:** Reported doctrinal position

> yeṣāṃ nāmasvabhāvam

For those who maintain that the Buddha's word has name as its intrinsic nature, the Dharma-collections belong to saṃskāraskandha.

`Nāman` here is not merely a physical sound. It is the linguistic designation or name-form through which meaning is articulated.

Thus:

```text
Buddha-word as name
    → linguistic determination
    → saṃskāraskandha
```

The Bhāṣya preserves both analyses without yet deciding between them in this unit. Its immediate purpose is classificatory: either account can be mapped into the five aggregates.

### 5. One teaching, two possible bases of inclusion

The same Dharma-collection may be classified differently depending on what is being treated as its intrinsic nature:

```text
spoken occurrence
    = rūpa

name-structure
    = saṃskāra
```

This is not arbitrary duplication. It distinguishes the material vehicle of teaching from its linguistic formation.

The verse therefore demonstrates a general Abhidharma method:

```text
identify the designated item
    ↓
determine its svabhāva
    ↓
include it in the established category
```

### 6. Transition to the next problem

The closing question asks:

> kiṃ punar dharmaskandhasya pramāṇam

“What, then, is the measure of one Dharma-collection?”

This question initiates the discussion developed through VAK 1.26. It is preserved here as the natural transition but not answered in advance.

## Argument Summary

```text
Scripture mentions additional skandhas, āyatanas, and dhātus.
    ↓
Do these stand outside the established systems?
    ↓
No. The established systems are exhaustive.
    ↓
Example: eighty thousand Dharma-collections.
    ↓
If Buddha-word is speech:
    dharmaskandhas belong to rūpaskandha.
    ↓
If Buddha-word is name:
    dharmaskandhas belong to saṃskāraskandha.
    ↓
Classification follows the svabhāva attributed to the teaching.
```

## New Technical Terms

### `dharmaskandha`

- **Provisional meaning:** Dharma-collection; division or body of teaching
- **Status:** Scriptural designation requiring Abhidharma inclusion
- **Function:** Names units within the Buddha's teaching without adding a sixth ontological aggregate

### `buddhavacana`

- **Provisional meaning:** Buddha-word; the teaching spoken by the Buddha
- **Status:** Object of competing analyses
- **Function:** Classified either as speech-sound or as name

### `vāksvabhāva`

- **Provisional meaning:** Having speech as intrinsic nature
- **Status:** Reported doctrinal interpretation
- **Function:** Grounds inclusion of the Dharma-collections in rūpaskandha

### `nāmasvabhāva`

- **Provisional meaning:** Having name as intrinsic nature
- **Status:** Reported doctrinal interpretation
- **Function:** Grounds inclusion of the Dharma-collections in saṃskāraskandha

### `vyatireka`

- **Provisional meaning:** Distinct existence outside the established categories
- **Status:** Denied
- **Function:** Marks the rejected possibility that scriptural designations introduce additional ontological classes

### `pramāṇa`

- **Provisional meaning:** Measure or extent
- **Status:** Transition term opening the next discussion
- **Function:** Asks what constitutes one complete Dharma-collection

## Voice Register

- **Vasubandhu:** Frames the problem of whether additional scriptural categories fall within or outside the established schemes.
- **Vasubandhu:** Answers that they are included and are not distinct.
- **Reported position 1:** Buddha-word is intrinsically speech and therefore material form.
- **Reported position 2:** Buddha-word is intrinsically name and therefore formation.
- **Unresolved in this unit:** Which analysis of Buddha-word Vasubandhu finally prefers.
- **Transition question:** What determines the measure of one Dharma-collection?

## Kārikā Translation Decision

**Decision:** Refined.

A productive translation is:

> The eighty thousand Dharma-collections taught by the Sage—whether they are speech or name—are included, respectively, within form and formations.

The earlier rendering should avoid suggesting that every Dharma-collection is simultaneously both speech and name. The Bhāṣya presents two analyses:

```text
if speech → rūpaskandha
if name   → saṃskāraskandha
```

`Saṃgraha` should be understood here as strict classificatory inclusion based on the attributed intrinsic nature of Buddha-word.

## Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya offers a useful but limited distinction between:

```text
material display of teaching
    = speech-sound

structured linguistic determination
    = name
```

This may later be useful for distinguishing a displayed field from its symbolic or graph-like articulation. But the present text is not proposing an idealist Projection/Factory theory. Its argument is narrower: the same teaching can be analyzed according to its material vehicle or its linguistic formation, and in either case it remains within the exhaustive five-skandha system.

Monkey-cup valuation: no additional deposit required.
