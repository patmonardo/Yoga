### Established Kārikā Translation

> A portion of the eye-domain and a portion of the dharma-domain are seeing, eightfold. The intelligence co-born with the five sensory knowledges is not seeing, because it does not ascertain.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. Which domains count as seeing?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Of the eighteen domains, how many are seeing and how many are not seeing?

The answer has two components:

```text
one portion of the eye-domain
+
one portion of the dharma-domain
```

The eye is not wholly identified with actual seeing, and the dharma-domain is not wholly identified with view. In each case only a determinate portion bears the relevant status.

### 2. The dharma-domain portion is eightfold

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The eight forms of view are:

```text
five afflicted views beginning with satkāyadṛṣṭi
+
worldly right view
+
trainee’s view
+
adept’s view
```

The five afflicted views are left for detailed treatment in the chapter on latent tendencies. The present unit only establishes their membership in the eightfold structure.

The remainder of the dharma-domain is not view.

### 3. Worldly right view

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Worldly right view is wholesome, contaminated prajñā associated with mental knowledge.

This definition is exact:

```text
laukikī samyagdṛṣṭi
    wholesome
    still contaminated
    prajñā
    associated with manovijñāna
```

Thus right view is not yet necessarily uncontaminated. A cognition can be correctly determining while still belonging to the worldly conditioned field.

### 4. Trainee and adept views

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The uncontaminated view belonging to a trainee is the trainee’s view; that belonging to an adept is the adept’s view.

The distinction is not in the bare content “truth,” but in the path-status of the knower and the corresponding mode of uncontaminated prajñā.

### 5. Views disclose dharmas differently

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu compares these modes of viewing dharmas to seeing visible form under different conditions:

```text
clouded or cloudless
night or day
```

Afflicted view, undefiled worldly view, trainee’s view, and adept’s view do not disclose the dharma-field with equal clarity.

This analogy introduces a graded structure of manifestation without reducing the object to the condition of its appearance.

### 6. Why worldly right view is associated only with mental knowledge

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The intelligence co-born with the five sensory knowledges is not view because it lacks *tīraṇa*—decisive ascertainment.

> View is ascertainative because it proceeds through attentive examination. The prajñā co-born with the five sensory knowledges is not like this. Therefore it is not view.

The Bhāṣya sharpens the Kārikā’s distinction:

```text
sensory prajñā
    accompanies sensory knowledge
    discriminates within presentation
    but does not attentively ascertain

view
    examines
    determines
    settles
```

### 7. Not every mental prajñā is view

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> For the same reason, other prajñā—whether afflicted or unafflicted—is not necessarily view.

The criterion is functional, not merely substantial or taxonomic. Prajñā becomes dṛṣṭi only when it performs the work of ascertainment.

### 8. Why call the eye “seeing” if it does not ascertain?

**Voice: Objector**

> If the eye does not ascertain, how can it be called seeing?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> In the sense that it examines or illuminates visible forms; for the eye sees visible forms.

This introduces a decisive equivocation that must remain controlled:

```text
cakṣus as dṛṣṭi
    visual disclosure of form

dharmadhātu-prajñā as dṛṣṭi
    ascertainment of dharmas
```

The same word *dṛṣṭi* covers two related but non-identical operations.

The controversy over whether the eye or eye-knowledge literally sees begins here but belongs structurally to VAK 1.42.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Question:
Which of the eighteen domains count as seeing?

Determination 1:
A portion of the eye-domain counts as seeing.

Function:
It discloses visible form.

Determination 2:
An eightfold portion of the dharma-domain counts as view.

Members:
    five afflicted views
    worldly right view
    trainee’s view
    adept’s view

Determination 3:
Worldly right view is wholesome but contaminated prajñā associated with mental knowledge.

Determination 4:
Trainee and adept views are uncontaminated and differentiated by path-status.

Determination 5:
Sensory co-born prajñā is not view.

Ground:
It lacks decisive ascertainment and attentive examination.

General rule:
Prajñā is not dṛṣṭi merely because it is prajñā.
It is dṛṣṭi only where ascertainative function is present.

Problem carried forward:
If the eye lacks ascertainment, in what sense does the eye see?

Provisional answer:
It visually discloses form.

Next dispute:
Does the eye see, or does the knowledge dependent upon the eye see?
```

## Translation Consequences

### 1. *Dṛṣṭi*

No single English word covers both uses cleanly.

```text
when predicated of cakṣus:
    seeing / visual disclosure

when predicated of dharmadhātu-prajñā:
    view / ascertainative seeing
```

The translation should preserve the unity of the Sanskrit term while explaining the functional difference.

### 2. *Dhī* and *prajñā*

The Kārikā’s *dhī* is explicitly glossed through *prajñā*. Yet the passage proves that prajñā is not automatically view.

```text
prajñā
    discriminative cognitive factor

dṛṣṭi
    prajñā functioning as decisive ascertainment
```

### 3. *Tīraṇa*

The strongest working rendering is:

```text
tīraṇa
    = ascertainment
    = decisive determination after examination
```

It should not be weakened to mere noticing or discrimination.

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> A functioning portion of the eye-domain is seeing insofar as it visually discloses forms. An eightfold portion of the dharma-domain is view: the five afflicted views, worldly right view, the trainee’s view, and the adept’s view. The prajñā accompanying sensory knowledge is not view, because it does not attentively ascertain and settle its object.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya refuses three collapses:

```text
sensation
    ≠ view

prajñā in general
    ≠ view

eye-faculty
    ≠ ascertainative judgment
```

Yet it also refuses to strip the eye of all seeing-function. The eye belongs to seeing because it is the capacity through which visible form is disclosed.

This supports the instrumental account developed in the preceding units:

```text
indriya
    not the Seer
    not merely gross seen anatomy
    but a determinate capacity for disclosure
```

The dharma-domain supplies the ascertainative side:

```text
visual faculty
    opens a field of sensible appearance

mental prajñā functioning as view
    determines within the field of dharmas
```

The two are related as disclosure and ascertainment, not as two versions of the same undifferentiated cognition.

## Logical Determination

VAK 1.41 distinguishes three levels:

```text
1. sensory presentation
2. discriminative accompaniment
3. ascertainative view
```

The five sensory knowledges reach the first level and possess a co-born discriminative factor. They do not thereby reach the third.

```text
presentation
    gives the sensible content

prajñā
    discriminates within cognition

dṛṣṭi
    determines through attentive ascertainment
```

This makes *capacity* more precise. A faculty is not an unlimited power of cognition. It has a proper function and a proper field:

```text
cakṣus
    capacity for visible disclosure
    not capacity for unrestricted ascertainment
```

Your quantitative hypothesis becomes especially relevant here. The text itself speaks functionally rather than mathematically, but its architecture permits the following Organon formulation:

```text
faculty-capacity
    is bounded by a determinate object-field
    and by a determinate mode of operation
```

The eye does not apprehend every possible determination. It operates only within the range proper to visible form. Objectivity therefore appears not as an indeterminate “outside,” but as a delimited field proportionate to a specific capacity.

This remains an interpretive development, not a direct claim that Vasubandhu has supplied psychophysical threshold theory.

## Organon Contact Point

This unit strongly supports the distinction between qualitative function and quantitative range.

The textual determination is qualitative:

```text
cakṣus
    discloses visible form

sensory prajñā
    discriminates without ascertainment

dṛṣṭi
    decisively determines
```

The Organon hypothesis adds:

```text
any determinate faculty-capacity
    may operate only across a bounded range
    of intensity, magnitude, distance, contrast, or temporal duration
```

That would make objectivity quantitative not because the object is merely number, but because an object becomes sensible only under measures proportionate to the faculty.

The stronger logical statement is:

> A faculty does not merely face an object; it defines the determinate conditions under which something can appear as its object.

VAK 1.41 gives the qualitative basis for that thought by sharply separating visual disclosure from sensory discrimination and ascertainative view.
