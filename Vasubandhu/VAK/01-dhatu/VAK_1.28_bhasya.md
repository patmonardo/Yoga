# VAK_1.28 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> chidram ākāśadhātvākhyam ālokatamasī kila /
>
> vijñānadhātur vijñānaṃ sāsravaṃ janmaniśrayāḥ // VAkK_1.28 //

### Translation

> An aperture is called the space-element—namely light and darkness, according to the received account. The consciousness-element is outflow-bound consciousness, since these six elements are supports of birth.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins after the discussion of the sixty-two elements taught in the *Bahudhātuka-sūtra*. Vasubandhu isolates the six elements:

> pṛthivīdhātuḥ, abdhātuḥ, tejodhātuḥ, vāyudhātuḥ, ākāśadhātuḥ, vijñānadhātuḥ

The characteristics of the last two have not yet been stated. The Bhāṣya therefore asks whether the ākāśadhātu is simply unconditioned space and whether vijñānadhātu means every consciousness. The reply is negative in both cases.

The unit ends by assigning the six elements back into the eighteen-dhātu system:

- the first four enter the tangible-element,
- the fifth enters the visible-form element,
- the sixth enters the seven consciousness-elements.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 The problem: two undefined members of the six-element teaching

The Bhāṣya notes that earth, water, fire, and wind have already been characterized, but ākāśadhātu and vijñānadhātu remain ambiguous.

It formulates two possible identifications:

1. Is ākāśadhātu identical with the unconditioned ākāśa defined earlier as non-obstruction?
2. Is vijñānadhātu identical with consciousness without qualification?

Vasubandhu rejects both unrestricted identifications.

### 3.2 Ākāśadhātu is an aperture, not unconditioned space

The text says:

> dvāravātāyanamukhanāsikādiṣu chidram ākāśadhātvākhyam

The opening found in such places as a door, window, mouth, or nostril is called the space-element.

This `chidra` is then explained through what is perceptually apprehended there:

> ālokatamasī kila

The aperture is, according to the received Vaibhāṣika account, light and darkness. The Bhāṣya argues that no aperture distinct from light and darkness is apprehended. Thus ākāśadhātu is treated as having light-and-darkness as its nature, or as having day-and-night as its nature.

This is therefore conditioned rūpa, not the unconditioned ākāśa of VAK 1.05.

### 3.3 `Aghasāmantaka-rūpa`

The same ākāśadhātu is called:

> aghasāmantakaṃ rūpam

The term receives more than one explanation.

One account takes `agha` as rūpa situated in the mind and derives the designation through extreme striking or obstruction. Another account explains the aperture as both:

- `agha`, because another rūpa is not obstructed there,
- `sāmantaka`, because it borders other rūpa.

The Bhāṣya does not fully adjudicate between these etymological explanations. Their common function is to retain ākāśadhātu within conditioned material form.

### 3.4 Vijñānadhātu is restricted to sāsrava vijñāna

The verse states:

> vijñānadhātur vijñānaṃ sāsravam

Here vijñānadhātu is not every possible consciousness. It is specifically consciousness accompanied by outflows.

The Bhāṣya immediately asks why anāsrava consciousness is excluded. The answer is functional:

> janmaniśrayāḥ

The six elements are accepted as supports of birth.

They are common to the continuum extending from the consciousness of rebirth-linking:

> pratisandhicitta

through to the consciousness of death:

> cyuticitta

Unconditioned or outflow-free dharmas do not function in that way. Therefore anāsrava consciousness is excluded from the six-element teaching under discussion.

### 3.5 Reintegration into the eighteen dhātus

The Bhāṣya closes the unit by showing that the six-element list does not stand outside the eighteen dhātus:

- earth, water, fire, and wind are included in `spraṣṭavyadhātu`, the tangible-element;
- ākāśadhātu, as light-and-darkness rūpa, is included in `rūpadhātu`, the visible-form element;
- vijñānadhātu is included in the seven consciousness-elements.

This confirms the method stated in VAK 1.27: specialized scriptural lists are assigned into the established system after their intrinsic characteristics are determined.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Question:
Are ākāśadhātu and vijñānadhātu unrestricted?

Answer:
No.

ākāśadhātu
    = conditioned aperture
    = light and darkness
    = rūpadhātu

vijñānadhātu
    = sāsrava consciousness
    = support of birth
    = seven vijñānadhātus
```

The decisive criterion is the function of the six elements as `janmaniśrayāḥ`, supports of embodied rebirth from `pratisandhicitta` to `cyuticitta`.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### `chidra`

- **Provisional meaning:** aperture, opening, cavity
- **Status:** conditioned material determination
- **Function:** defines ākāśadhātu in the six-element teaching

### `āloka-tamas`

- **Provisional meaning:** light and darkness
- **Status:** Vaibhāṣika explanation of the nature of the aperture
- **Function:** prevents identification of ākāśadhātu with unconditioned space

### `aghasāmantaka-rūpa`

- **Provisional meaning:** bordering or aperture-associated form
- **Status:** technical and etymologically disputed designation
- **Function:** locates ākāśadhātu within rūpa

### `sāsrava-vijñāna`

- **Provisional meaning:** consciousness accompanied by outflows
- **Status:** conditioned consciousness
- **Function:** defines vijñānadhātu in this six-element context

### `janmaniśraya`

- **Provisional meaning:** support or basis of birth
- **Status:** functional criterion
- **Function:** explains the exclusion of anāsrava consciousness

### `pratisandhicitta`

- **Provisional meaning:** rebirth-linking consciousness
- **Status:** first consciousness of a new life-continuum
- **Function:** marks the beginning of the range supported by the six elements

### `cyuticitta`

- **Provisional meaning:** death-consciousness
- **Status:** final consciousness of a life-continuum
- **Function:** marks the end of the range supported by the six elements

## 6. Voice Register

- **Expository Vasubandhu:** introduces the unresolved characteristics of ākāśadhātu and vijñānadhātu and assigns the six elements into the eighteen.
- **Questioning voice / objector:** asks whether ākāśadhātu is unconditioned space and whether vijñānadhātu is all consciousness.
- **Received Vaibhāṣika position:** marked by `kila`; identifies the aperture with light and darkness and calls it `aghasāmantaka-rūpa`.
- **Alternative interpreters:** introduced by `apare`; provide another explanation of `aghasāmantaka`.
- **Doctrinal reply:** restricts vijñānadhātu to sāsrava consciousness because the six elements are supports of birth.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The earlier translation should be refined in two ways.

First, `janmaniśrayāḥ` is plural and predicates a shared function of all six elements, not only of vijñānadhātu.

Second, `ālokatamasī kila` should preserve the reportive force of `kila`, because the Bhāṣya marks this as a received explanation.

Preferred translation:

> An aperture is called the space-element—namely light and darkness, according to the received account. The consciousness-element is outflow-bound consciousness, since these six elements are supports of birth.

No edit is made to the Kārikā file during this Bhāṣya pass.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya sharply restricts the present `vijñānadhātu` to the conditioned continuum of birth. This matters for the project hypothesis that Vijñānaskandha functions as Projection or Factory: VAK 1.28 concerns the outflow-bound projection sustaining a life-continuum, not pure knowing as such.

A provisional distinction is therefore:

```text
conditioned projection
    = sāsrava vijñāna
    = supports birth and embodied transaction

pure or liberating knowing
    ≠ the vijñānadhātu defined in this verse
```

This contact point remains an Organon reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's explicit metaphysical claim.
