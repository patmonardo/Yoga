### Established Kārikā Translation

> The tangible is twofold. The remaining nine form-bearing domains are derivative-material. And one part of the dharma-domain is form-bearing. The ten form-bearing domains are accumulated.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. Which domains are great elements and which are derivative-material?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> How many domains have the nature of the great elements, and how many are derivative-material?

The question introduces the distinction governing the first three quarters of the Kārikā.

### 2. The tangible domain is twofold

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The tangible is twofold: the great elements and the derivative-material. Among these, the great elements are four, while the derivative-material is sevenfold, beginning with smoothness.

The seven derivative tangibles are the tactile qualities previously enumerated apart from the four great elements.

### 3. Meaning of *bhautika*

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> It is called derivative-material because it exists in dependence upon the great elements.

The phrase `bhūteṣu bhavatvāt` supplies an etymological and ontological explanation. *Bhautika* does not mean merely “physical.” It denotes material determination whose occurrence depends upon the great elements.

### 4. The remaining nine material domains

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The five faculty-domains and the four object-domains are the nine remaining form-bearing domains. These nine are derivative-material alone.

The five faculties are:

```text
cakṣus
śrotra
ghrāṇa
jihvā
kāya
```

The four object-domains are:

```text
rūpa
śabda
gandha
rasa
```

Touch is excluded from this group because the tangible domain includes both the four great elements and the seven derivative tangible qualities.

### 5. The material part of the dharma-domain

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The part of the dharma-domain designated avijñapti is derivative-material.

This confirms the Kārikā analysis: the material part of the dharma-domain is **avijñapti**.

### 6. What is neither great-element nor derivative-material?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The remaining knowledge-domains, and the dharma-domain apart from avijñapti, are neither of the two kinds.

The division into *bhūta* and *bhautika* applies only within the material field. Mental domains and the non-material portion of the dharma-domain are neither great elements nor derivative-material.

### 7. Buddhadeva’s reduction

**Voice: Buddhadeva’s position**

> “The ten spheres are nothing but the great elements.”

This position collapses the distinction between great elements and derivative materiality.

### 8. Vasubandhu’s rejection

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> This is not so, because the sūtra fixes the great elements as four and determines them by hardness and the other characteristics. Moreover, those characteristics belong to the tangible domain. Hardness and the other elemental characteristics are not apprehended by the eye and the other faculties, nor are color and the other object-forms apprehended by the body-faculty.

The refutation turns on differentiated modes of apprehension:

```text
great-element characteristics
    apprehended through touch

color and the other object-domains
    apprehended through their proper faculties
```

Therefore the visible and other material domains cannot simply be identified with the great elements.

### 9. Scriptural confirmation

**Voice: Vasubandhu citing sūtra**

The cited formula says that the faculties and external material objects arise **in dependence upon** the four great elements. By contrast, the tangible sphere explicitly includes both the four great elements themselves and what is derived from them.

The Bhāṣya draws the conclusion:

> Because the great elements are included only through one part of the tangible sphere, it is clearly shown that the remaining material domains are not themselves great elements.

### 10. The ten accumulated domains

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Which domains are accumulated, and which are not accumulated? The five faculty-domains and the five object-domains are accumulated. Since they are aggregates of atoms, it follows that the remaining domains are not accumulated.

This gives a strict Bhāṣya definition of *saṃcita*:

```text
saṃcita
    = paramāṇu-saṃghāta
    = an aggregate of atoms
```

The ten accumulated domains are therefore:

```text
five faculty-domains
+
five object-domains
```

The material part of the dharma-domain, avijñapti, is material but not accumulated in this atomic sense.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Question:
Which domains are elemental, derivative-material, or neither?

Determination 1:
The tangible domain is twofold.

    four great elements
    + seven derivative tangible qualities

Determination 2:
The remaining nine material domains are derivative-material.

    five faculties
    + four non-tangible objects

Determination 3:
Avijñapti, as part of the dharma-domain, is derivative-material.

Determination 4:
The mental domains and the remainder of the dharma-domain are neither elemental nor derivative-material.

Objection / rival thesis:
Buddhadeva reduces all ten material spheres to the great elements.

Resolution:
The sūtra fixes the great elements as four and locates their specific characteristics within tangible apprehension. The other material domains arise in dependence upon the great elements but are not identical with them.

Final determination:
The ten material faculty-and-object domains are accumulated because they are atomic aggregates.
```

## Translation Consequences

The Bhāṣya confirms most of the existing Kārikā translation but sharpens two points.

### 1. *Bhautika*

The strongest working translation remains:

```text
bhautika
    = derivative-material
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly grounds this in `bhūteṣu bhavatvāt`: such materiality exists in dependence upon the great elements.

### 2. *Saṃcita*

The Kārikā analysis rendered *saṃcita* as “accumulated” or “aggregated.” The Bhāṣya now determines the exact sense:

```text
saṃcita
    = atomically aggregated
    = constituted as a paramāṇu-saṃghāta
```

The philosophical translation should therefore avoid treating *saṃcita* as a merely general notion of formation. It has a precise Abhidharma material meaning here.

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> The tangible domain is twofold, comprising the four great elements and seven derivative tangible qualities. The remaining nine material domains, together with avijñapti as the material part of the dharma-domain, are derivative-material. The five material faculties and five material objects are accumulated, that is, constituted as atomic aggregates.

---

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya establishes three non-equivalent levels:

```text
great elements
    elemental tangible determinations

bhautika-rūpa
    materiality dependent upon the great elements

saṃcita-rūpa
    derivative materiality present as atomic aggregation
```

These must not be collapsed.

Most importantly:

```text
all accumulated domains are material
but not all material determinations are accumulated
```

Avijñapti is the decisive case. It is derivative-material, yet it is not one of the ten atomic aggregates.

---

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya transforms the compressed classification into a mediated structure:

```text
Elemental determination
    ↓ dependence
Derivative materiality
    ↓ aggregation
Accumulated material domain
```

This is not a simple three-member list. Each later determination presupposes the earlier one while adding a distinct condition:

```text
bhautika
    presupposes the great elements

saṃcita
    presupposes materiality
    and adds atomic aggregation
```

The living middle is **dependence upon the great elements**. It prevents identity between elemental basis and derivative form while also preventing their separation.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The most important result for the Organon is a disciplined distinction between dependence and identity:

```text
derived from
    ≠ identical with
```

Buddhadeva’s position fails because it turns the material basis into the whole material field. Vasubandhu preserves the mediation:

```text
great elements
    are the basis of derivative materiality

but derivative materiality
    has its own determinate modes of apprehension and constitution
```

This is a genuine reflective relation. The basis is present in the derivative domain through dependence, not through static identity.
