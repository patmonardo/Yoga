# VAK_1.22 — Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> skandheṣv asaṃskṛtaṃ noktam arthāyogāt kramaḥ punaḥ /
>
> yathaudārikasaṃkleśabhājanādyarthadhātutaḥ // VAkK_1.22 //

The established Kārikā analysis correctly identifies two questions:

1. Why is the unconditioned not included among the aggregates?
2. Why are the five aggregates presented in this particular order?

The Bhāṣya confirms the first answer and gives four distinct explanations of the second.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 Why the unconditioned is not an aggregate

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The unconditioned cannot be inserted into any of the five aggregates:

> nahi tad rūpaṃ nāpi yāvad vijñānam

### Translation

> It is neither form nor, proceeding through the series, knowledge.

Nor may one add a sixth aggregate called the “aggregate of the unconditioned.” The reason is semantic and structural:

> rāśyartho hi skandhārthaḥ

### Translation

> The meaning of *skandha* is collection or heap.

A collection is formed by gathering together items differentiated as past, future, present, internal, external, gross, subtle, inferior, superior, distant, or near. Conditioned form and the other aggregates admit this differentiation. The unconditioned does not.

Therefore:

```text
skandha
    = a collectable multiplicity
      articulated by temporal and other differences

asaṃskṛta
    = not divided in that manner
    = therefore not gatherable as a skandha
```

The text also supplies a soteriological criterion. The expression “aggregates of appropriation” teaches the basis of defilement. The broader word “aggregates” teaches the bases of both defilement and purification. The unconditioned does not fit either role in the same way.

### 3.2 A rejected alternative argument

**Voice:** cited alternative position, followed by Vasubandhu’s criticism

> yathā ghaṭoparamo na ghaṭa evaṃ skandhoparamo na skandho bhavitum arhati

### Translation

> Just as the cessation of a pot is not itself a pot, so the cessation of the aggregates should not itself be an aggregate.

This analogy treats the unconditioned as the cessation of aggregate-processes and concludes that it cannot be one of those aggregates.

Vasubandhu rejects this as insufficient:

> teṣāṃ dhātvāyataneṣv apy eṣa prasaṅgaḥ

### Translation

> For them, the same consequence would also follow concerning the domains and spheres.

The problem is that the unconditioned is nevertheless included in dharmadhātu and dharmāyatana. A simple “cessation is not what ceases” argument would wrongly exclude it there as well. Vasubandhu therefore prefers the more exact criterion of whether the category’s meaning applies.

### 3.3 First explanation of aggregate-order: grossness

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The aggregates are ordered from the gross toward the subtle.

> rūpaṃ hi sapratighatvāt sarvaudārikam

### Translation

> Form is altogether gross because it possesses resistance.

Among non-material factors, feeling is the grossest in manifestation. People localize it bodily:

> haste me vedanā pāde me vedanā

### Translation

> “I have a feeling in my hand”; “I have a feeling in my foot.”

Recognition is subtler than form and feeling, formations are subtler than recognition, and knowledge is subtler than formations.

The sequence is therefore:

```text
rūpa
    → vedanā
    → saṃjñā
    → saṃskāra
    → vijñāna

most gross
    → most subtle
```

The earlier Kārikā translation should thus treat `yathā-audārika` as a real ordering principle, not merely “according to accessibility.”

### 3.4 Second explanation: the sequence of defilement

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The order can also be read genetically as a sequence of saṃkleśa.

In beginningless saṃsāra, women and men delight in one another’s forms. That attraction is driven by attachment to the savor of feeling:

```text
rūpa-abhilāṣa
    depends on
vedanā-āsvāda-gardha
```

That craving depends upon distortion in recognition:

> tadgarddhaḥ saṃjñāviparyāsāt

### Translation

> That greed arises from a distortion of recognition.

The distortion is then supported by kleśas, and citta is thereby defiled. The order of the aggregates consequently reconstructs the production of bondage:

```text
form as attractive appearance
    → savor of feeling
    → distorted recognition
    → afflicted formations
    → defiled knowledge/citta
```

This confirms and deepens VAK 1.21. Vedanā and saṃjñā are separate aggregates because they are not minor mental accompaniments; they occupy distinct causal places in the production of saṃsāra.

### 3.5 Third explanation: vessel, food, seasoning, cook, and eater

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The phrase `bhājanādi` introduces an analogy:

> bhajanabhojanavyañjanakartṛbhoktṛbhūtā hi rūpādayaḥ skandhāḥ

### Translation

> The aggregates beginning with form function respectively as vessel, food, seasoning, maker, and enjoyer.

The mapping is:

```text
rūpa       = vessel
vedanā     = food
saṃjñā     = seasoning or distinguishing flavor
saṃskāra   = maker or cook
vijñāna    = enjoyer
```

This is not an ontological claim about an enduring subject. It is a functional analogy showing how the five aggregates cooperate in one experiential complex.

The Kārikā’s `bhājanādi-artha` should therefore not be reduced to “material support.” It refers to this ordered fivefold analogy.

### 3.6 Fourth explanation: correspondence with existential domains

**Voice:** Vasubandhu reporting the systematic Abhidharma mapping

The aggregates also correspond to levels of existence:

```text
kāmadhātu
    is dominated by desirable forms

four dhyānas
    are dominated by feeling

first three ārūpyas
    are dominated by recognition

bhavāgra
    is dominated by formations alone
```

The Sanskrit says:

> kāmaguṇarūpaprabhāvito hi kāmadhātuḥ /
> vedanāprabhāvitāni dhyānāni /
> saṃjñāprabhāvitās traya ārūpyāḥ /
> saṃskāramātraprabhāvitaṃ bhavāgram /

The text then identifies these as stations of vijñāna:

> etā eva vijñānasthitayas tāsu ca pratiṣṭhitaṃ vijñānam

### Translation

> These are precisely the stations of knowledge, and knowledge is established in them.

Vasubandhu explains the sequence through the image of field and seed:

> kṣetrabījasaṃdarśanārthaḥ skandhānukramaḥ

### Translation

> The sequence of the aggregates is intended to display the relation of field and seed.

This is the strongest passage so far for the user’s speculation that the other domains “live in” Vijñānaskandha. But the text must be stated carefully:

```text
four aggregate-configurations
    = fields or stations

vijñāna
    = established within those stations
    = seed in relation to field
```

The Bhāṣya does not say here that Vijñānaskandha metaphysically produces all dhātus. It does, however, depict vijñāna as established in and correlated with the structured fields constituted by the preceding aggregates.

### 3.7 Why there are exactly five aggregates

The multiple orderings explain both the number and the separation:

> ata eva pañcaskandhā nālpīyāṃso na bhūyāṃsaḥ

### Translation

> For this very reason there are five aggregates, neither fewer nor more.

Feeling and recognition are separated from the formations because they are:

- grosser than the remaining formations,
- distinct causes within the sequence of defilement,
- the food and seasoning in the functional analogy,
- dominant principles of two distinct sets of existential domains.

Thus the fivefold scheme is not arbitrary enumeration. Its members are selected and ordered because they perform irreducible explanatory functions.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Question 1:
Why is asaṃskṛta not a skandha?

Answer:
Because skandha means a collectable multiplicity differentiated
by temporal and other divisions, and this meaning does not apply
to the unconditioned.
```

```text
Question 2:
Why this order of the five aggregates?

Answer A — grossness:
rūpa → vedanā → saṃjñā → saṃskāra → vijñāna

Answer B — defilement:
attractive form → savor of feeling → distorted recognition
→ afflicted formations → defiled knowledge

Answer C — functional analogy:
vessel → food → seasoning → maker → enjoyer

Answer D — existential domains:
kāmadhātu → dhyānas → first three ārūpyas → bhavāgra
with vijñāna established in these as seed in fields
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### arthāyoga

- **Term:** `arthāyoga`
- **Provisional meaning:** applicability or fittingness of a category’s meaning
- **Status:** central methodological principle
- **Function:** determines whether a dharma may be placed under a skandha, āyatana, or dhātu

### audārika

- **Term:** `audārika`
- **Provisional meaning:** gross, manifest, comparatively accessible
- **Status:** explicit ordering criterion
- **Function:** orders the five aggregates from resistant matter to subtle knowledge

### vedanāsvādagardha

- **Term:** `vedanā-āsvāda-gardha`
- **Provisional meaning:** greed for or attachment to the savor of feeling
- **Status:** causal term in the saṃkleśa sequence
- **Function:** explains why beings become attached to attractive forms

### saṃjñāviparyāsa

- **Term:** `saṃjñā-viparyāsa`
- **Provisional meaning:** distortion or inversion in recognition
- **Status:** causal term in the saṃkleśa sequence
- **Function:** generates attachment by misapprehending marks

### vijñānasthiti

- **Term:** `vijñāna-sthiti`
- **Provisional meaning:** station or support-field in which vijñāna is established
- **Status:** important doctrinal term
- **Function:** relates aggregate-configurations to the establishment of knowledge across existential domains

### kṣetra-bīja

- **Term:** `kṣetra-bīja`
- **Provisional meaning:** field and seed
- **Status:** explanatory analogy
- **Function:** shows the relation between the first four aggregate-configurations and vijñāna

### bhavāgra

- **Term:** `bhavāgra`
- **Provisional meaning:** summit of existence, the highest formless attainment
- **Status:** cosmological term
- **Function:** identified here as dominated by formations alone

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

### Confirmed

The earlier decision that the unconditioned is excluded because aggregate-meaning does not fit is confirmed.

### Refined

`artha-ayogāt` should be rendered more precisely as:

> because the defining meaning of *skandha* is inapplicable

rather than merely “because the meaning does not fit.”

`bhājanādi` should be revised from a generic “vessel-like basis” to the explicit fivefold analogy:

```text
vessel, food, seasoning, maker, enjoyer
```

`dhātutaḥ` refers not simply to abstract domain-status but to the correspondence between aggregate dominance and the domains or levels of existence.

### Recommended translation

> The unconditioned is not stated among the aggregates because the defining meaning does not apply. Their order, again, is according to grossness, the sequence of defilement, the analogy beginning with vessel, and the existential domains.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya’s own result should remain primary:

```text
five aggregates
    = five irreducible explanatory functions
    = ordered through several intersecting principles
```

The project hypothesis may now be stated more carefully:

```text
paramārthika or sattvic standpoint
    may articulate
transactional forms a priori
without those forms ceasing to be conditioned
```

The `vijñānasthiti` and `kṣetra-bīja` passage is particularly important. It does not establish that all dhātus reduce to Vijñānaskandha. It presents the preceding aggregate-configurations as stations or fields in which vijñāna is established.

A cautious Organon formulation is therefore:

```text
principial field-structure
    → provides the station of factual cognition

vijñāna
    → arises and persists only through such articulated fields
```

This is close to the proposed account of a Dharma: a principial determination constructs or sustains a field in which a corresponding fact can occur. But the identification of this entire Volume with Sattva and the paramārthika remains a project-level reconstruction to be tested against the continuing Bhāṣya.
