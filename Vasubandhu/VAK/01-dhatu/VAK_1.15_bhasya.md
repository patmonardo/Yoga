# VAK_1.15 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> caturbhyo 'nye tu saṃskāraskandhaḥ ete punas trayaḥ /
>
> dharmāyatanadhātvākhyāḥ sahāvijñaptyasaṃskṛtaiḥ // VAkK_1.15 //

### Working translation

> The formations other than the four are the formations aggregate. These three, together with avijñapti and the unconditioned dharmas, are called the dharma-sphere and the dharma-domain.

The first half completes the definition of the five aggregates. The second reorganizes three aggregates, avijñapti, and the unconditioned under the āyatana and dhātu systems.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

> caturbhyo 'nye tu saṃskāraskandhaḥ
>
> rūpavedanāsaṃjñāvijñānebhyaś caturbhyo 'nye tu saṃskārāḥ saṃskāraskandhaḥ /
>
> bhagavatā tu sūtre ṣaṭ cetanākāyā ity uktaṃ prādhānyāt /
>
> sā hi karmasvarūpatvād abhisaṃskaraṇe pradhānā /
>
> ata evoktaṃ bhagavatā: “saṃskṛtam abhisaṃskaroti; tasmāt saṃskārā upādānaskandha ity ucyate” iti /
>
> anyathā hi śeṣāṇāṃ caitasikānāṃ viprayuktānāṃ ca saṃskārāṇāṃ skandhāsaṃgrahād duḥkhasamudayasatyatvaṃ na syād iti parijñāparihāṇe api na syātām /
>
> uktaṃ ca bhagavatā: “nāham ekadharmam apy anabhijñāyāparijñāya duḥkhasyāntakriyāṃ vadāmi” iti / evam “aprahāya” ity uktam /
>
> tasmād avaśyam eṣāṃ saṃskāraskandhasaṃgraho 'bhyupagantavyaḥ /
>
> ete punas trayaḥ /
>
> vedanāsaṃjñāsaṃskāraskandhā āyatanadhātuvyavasthāyāṃ
>
> dharmāyatanadhātvākhyāḥ sahāvijñaptyasaṃskṛtaiḥ // VAkK_1.15 //
>
> ity etāni sapta dravyāṇi dharmāyatanaṃ dharmadhātuś cety ākhyāyante /

### Translation

> “The formations other than the four constitute the formations aggregate.”
>
> The formations other than the four aggregates—form, feeling, recognition, and knowledge—constitute the formations aggregate.
>
> In a sūtra, however, the Blessed One spoke of six classes of volition because of volition’s predominance. Since volition has the nature of karma, it is principal in actively forming the conditioned.
>
> For this very reason the Blessed One said: “It actively forms the conditioned; therefore it is called the formations aggregate of appropriation.”
>
> Otherwise, because the remaining mental factors and the formations dissociated from mind would not be included in any aggregate, they would not belong to the truths of suffering and origin. Consequently, there would be neither full comprehension nor abandonment with respect to them.
>
> The Blessed One has also said: “I do not declare the ending of suffering without directly knowing and fully comprehending even a single dharma.” Likewise, he has said, “without abandoning it.”
>
> Therefore these factors must necessarily be accepted as included in the formations aggregate.
>
> “These three again”—the aggregates of feeling, recognition, and formations—within the arrangements of spheres and domains, “are called the dharma-sphere and the dharma-domain, together with avijñapti and the unconditioned dharmas.”
>
> These seven substances are called the dharma-sphere and the dharma-domain.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 Saṃskāraskandha is defined by exhaustive inclusion

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The formations aggregate is not defined as one narrow psychological faculty. It gathers every conditioned factor not already included under:

```text
rūpa
vedanā
saṃjñā
vijñāna
```

Thus:

```text
all conditioned dharmas
    minus the other four aggregates
        = saṃskāraskandha
```

This is an exhaustive classificatory rule. The aggregate cannot be reduced to volition alone.

### 3.2 Why the sūtra speaks only of six classes of volition

**Voice: Vasubandhu explaining scriptural usage**

The sūtra formula names six classes of `cetanā`, corresponding to the six sensory and mental contacts. The Bhāṣya explains this as a statement made `prādhānyāt`, because volition is predominant.

Its predominance has two closely connected grounds:

```text
cetanā
    = karma in its essential form
    = principal factor in abhisaṃskaraṇa
```

`Abhisaṃskaraṇa` is the active forming or constructing of conditioned existence. Volition is therefore the leading member of the formations aggregate, but it is not coextensive with the aggregate.

### 3.3 Saṃskāra as active formation

The quoted sūtra explains the name through activity:

> saṃskṛtam abhisaṃskaroti

The formation actively forms the conditioned. This is not merely a passive item within an inventory. The term points to productive or formative operation.

The doctrinal sequence is:

```text
cetanā
    → karma
    → active formation of conditioned existence
```

This explains why volition can stand for the aggregate in an abbreviated teaching.

### 3.4 Why all remaining factors must be included

The Bhāṣya gives a path-based argument rather than merely a taxonomic one.

If the remaining mental factors and mind-dissociated formations were excluded from the aggregates, then they would not fall under:

```text
duḥkha-satya
samudaya-satya
```

If they did not belong to suffering and origin, they could not properly become objects of:

```text
parijñā
    full comprehension

prahāṇa
    abandonment
```

The structure of the path therefore requires their inclusion.

This is a strong methodological principle:

```text
whatever participates in bondage
    must be classifiable within the truths
    and therefore within the aggregates
```

### 3.5 The scriptural proof

The Bhāṣya cites the Blessed One to establish that liberation leaves no dharma outside knowledge and treatment:

```text
no ending of suffering
    without direct knowledge and full comprehension

no ending of suffering
    without abandonment where abandonment is required
```

The classificatory system is therefore not merely descriptive. It is organized by the practical requirements of liberation.

### 3.6 The seven dravyas of dharmāyatana and dharmadhātu

The three aggregates are:

```text
vedanāskandha
saṃjñāskandha
saṃskāraskandha
```

To these are added:

```text
avijñapti
ākāśa
pratisaṃkhyānirodha
apratisaṃkhyānirodha
```

Hence:

```text
3 aggregates
+ 1 avijñapti
+ 3 unconditioned dharmas
= 7 dravyas
```

These same seven are called both:

```text
dharmāyatana
    the dharma-sphere

dharmadhātu
    the dharma-domain
```

The same contents are viewed under two classificatory arrangements.

### 3.7 Dharmadhātu is not a mere leftover bin

The first-pass Kārikā file correctly recognized that this is not an arbitrary residual category. The Bhāṣya makes the reason more exact.

Dharmadhātu gathers factors that can serve as the object-domain of mental knowledge but that are not exhausted by the ten material faculties and objects. It includes:

```text
felt experience
mark-recognition
conditioned formative factors
non-disclosive form
unconditioned dharmas
```

It therefore spans conditioned mental factors, subtle material form, and the unconditioned.

This diversity is deliberate. The unity is not material homogeneity but their common placement within the dharma-object domain.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
1. Four aggregates are already independently defined.

2. Every other conditioned formation must enter saṃskāraskandha.

3. Cetanā is named separately in sūtra because it is predominant:
       cetanā = karma
       cetanā leads active formation.

4. Excluding the remaining factors would remove them from suffering and origin.

5. What falls outside suffering and origin could not be fully comprehended or abandoned.

6. Therefore all remaining mental and mind-dissociated formations belong to saṃskāraskandha.

7. Vedanā, saṃjñā, and saṃskāra, together with avijñapti and the three unconditioned dharmas, form seven dravyas.

8. These seven are dharmāyatana and dharmadhātu.
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### `abhisaṃskaraṇa`

- **Provisional meaning:** active forming; constructing the conditioned
- **Status:** Bhāṣya explanation of the predominance of volition
- **Function:** explains why `cetanā` is principal within the formations aggregate

### `prādhānya`

- **Provisional meaning:** predominance; principal status
- **Status:** hermeneutical explanation of abbreviated sūtra usage
- **Function:** explains why six classes of volition can stand for the larger formations aggregate

### `cetanākāya`

- **Provisional meaning:** class or body of volition
- **Status:** scriptural classification
- **Function:** divides volition according to the six contact-domains

### `viprayukta-saṃskāra`

- **Provisional meaning:** formation dissociated from mind
- **Status:** technical Abhidharma category
- **Function:** identifies conditioned factors that are neither material nor ordinary associated mental factors

### `parijñā`

- **Provisional meaning:** full comprehension
- **Status:** path function
- **Function:** the required complete knowing of suffering

### `prahāṇa`

- **Provisional meaning:** abandonment
- **Status:** path function
- **Function:** removal of factors belonging to the origin of suffering

### `dravya`

- **Provisional meaning:** distinct real item or substance in the classificatory count
- **Status:** Vaibhāṣika-Abhidharma technical language
- **Function:** counts the seven distinct contents of dharmāyatana and dharmadhātu

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The existing translation is broadly sound, but the phrase `caturbhyo 'nye` should remain visibly connected to conditioned formations rather than sounding like an undefined collection of “others.”

### Preferred translation

> The formations other than the four constitute the formations aggregate. These three, together with avijñapti and the unconditioned dharmas, are called the dharma-sphere and the dharma-domain.

### Later revision note

The Kārikā file should eventually make the numerical result explicit in its commentary:

```text
vedanā + saṃjñā + saṃskāra
+ avijñapti
+ three asaṃskṛtas
= seven dravyas
```

The present Bhāṣya pass does not modify the Kārikā file.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The strongest project contact is not simply that dharmadhātu is a “domain of determinations.” The Bhāṣya shows that its unity is established through its role in the architecture of knowability and liberation.

```text
dharmadhātu
    gathers what can become a dharma-object of manas
    across mental, subtle-material, and unconditioned status
```

This supports the provisional reading of dhātus as Knowledge Domains, but with an important qualification:

```text
the domain is not generated by vijñāna

rather:
    it is the articulated field
    relative to which a determinate vijñāna can arise
```

The passage also gives a powerful path criterion for system construction:

```text
no relevant determination may remain unclassified,
because whatever remains unclassified
cannot be fully known, comprehended, or abandoned.
```

This contact point should remain provisional until the definitions of Vijñānaskandha, manas, and the eighteen dhātus are fully analyzed in the following verses.
