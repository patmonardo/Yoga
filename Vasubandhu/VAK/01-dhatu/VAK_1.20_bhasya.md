# VAK_1.20 — Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> rāśyāyadvāragotrārthāḥ skandhāyatanadhātavaḥ /
> mohendriyarucitraidhāt tisraḥ skandhādideśanāḥ // VAkK_1.20 //

The completed Kārikā analysis established the preliminary correspondences:

```text
skandha  = rāśi, aggregate or collection
āyatana  = āya-dvāra, entrance or access-way
dhātu    = gotra, source-kind or lineage
```

The Bhāṣya now tests these definitions, especially `rāśi` and `gotra`, and explains why the Buddha teaches the same field through three distinct classificatory schemes.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the definitions of `āyatana` and `dhātu`, continues through a debate concerning whether skandhas and āyatanas are merely conceptual collections, and concludes with the pedagogical explanation of the three teachings. ## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 Āyatana as the expansion-gateway of mind and mental factors

**Voice:** Vasubandhu's explanatory voice.

> cittacaittāyadvārārtha āyatanārthaḥ

### Translation

> The meaning of `āyatana` is a gateway for the arrival or extension of mind and mental factors.

The etymological explanation follows:

> cittacaittānām āyaṃ tanvanti

### Translation

> They extend or spread the arrival of mind and mental factors.

`Āyatana` is therefore not merely a static location. It is a functional access-condition through which mental events extend into their appropriate field.

### 3.2 Dhātu as gotra and ākara

**Voice:** Vasubandhu reporting the standard explanation.

> gotrārtho dhātvarthaḥ

### Translation

> The meaning of `dhātu` is `gotra`.

The Bhāṣya explains this by analogy with a mountain containing many mineral deposits:

```text
one mountain
    contains iron, copper, silver, gold, and other mines

one support or continuum
    contains eighteen gotras
    = eighteen dhātus
```

The decisive gloss is:

> ākarās tatra gotrāṇy ucyante

### Translation

> In that example, the sources or mines are called `gotras`.

Thus `gotra` here is not merely genealogical lineage. It means a source, matrix, or productive deposit from which a determinate kind can arise.

### 3.3 What do the eye and the other dhātus produce?

**Voice:** Question and answer within Vasubandhu's exposition.

> ta ime cakṣurādayaḥ kasyākarāḥ /
> svasyā jāteḥ /
> sabhāgahetutvāt /

### Translation

> Of what are the eye and the other dhātus sources? Of their own kind, because they function as homogeneous causes.

This is the strongest generative statement in the unit. A dhātu is a source of subsequent members of its own class through `sabhāgahetu`, the homogeneous cause.

The structure is:

```text
a dhātu of kind X
    functions as homogeneous cause
    produces another occurrence of kind X
```

This does not make the dhātu an eternal universal. It identifies a lawful continuity of kind across conditioned occurrences.

### 3.4 The problem of the unconditioned

**Voice:** Objector.

> asaṃskṛtaṃ tarhi na dhātuḥ syāt

### Translation

> Then the unconditioned would not be a dhātu.

If a dhātu is defined only as a causal source of its own later kind, unconditioned dharmas cannot qualify because they do not produce homogeneous effects.

**Voice:** Alternative teachers, explicitly marked by `ity apare`.

> cittacittānāṃ tarhi jātivācako 'yaṃ dhātuśabdaḥ

### Translation

> Others therefore say that the word `dhātu` designates the kinds of mind and mental factors.

The following sentence generalizes the definition:

> aṣṭādaśadharmāṇāṃ jātayaḥ svabhāvā aṣṭādaśa dhātavaḥ

### Translation

> The intrinsic kinds—the intrinsic natures—of the eighteen dharmas are the eighteen dhātus.

Here `jāti` is glossed through `svabhāva`. This preserves the unconditioned within the dhātu system by shifting emphasis from causal productivity alone to intrinsic type-determination.

### 3.5 Is a skandha merely conceptual?

**Voice:** Objector.

If `skandha` means heap or collection, then skandhas appear to be merely conceptually existent, like the person construed from a collection of many substances.

**Voice:** Initial reply.

> ekasyāpi dravyaparamāṇoḥ skandhatvāt

### Translation

> Even a single substantial atom has skandha-status.

The objector presses the problem: a single item cannot literally be a heap. Alternative etymologies—burden-bearing or sectional division—are reported and rejected as contrary to scripture (`utsūtra`).

### 3.6 Vasubandhu returns to the scriptural meaning of rāśi

**Voice:** Vasubandhu's critical conclusion.

The sūtra explicitly gathers all past, future, and present form together. Therefore the collective meaning cannot be discarded.

> tasmād rāśivad eva skandhāḥ prajñaptisantaḥ

### Translation

> Therefore the skandhas exist conceptually, precisely as collections do.

This is an important qualification of the earlier Kārikā analysis. `Skandha` does not name one additional substance over and above its constituents. It is the conceptual gathering of the relevant dharmas under one aggregate-heading.

### 3.7 Are the material āyatanas also merely conceptual?

**Voice:** Objector.

If a faculty is composed of many atoms that jointly form the gateway of cognition, the āyatana might also appear merely conceptual.

**Voice:** Reply.

Each constituent can function causally when assembled, or cooperate with the object. The Bhāṣā passage preserves two permissible Abhidharma formulations:

```text
with attention to the conceptual skandha:
    one atom is a portion of one dhātu, āyatana, and skandha

without that attention:
    one atom is itself one dhātu, one āyatana, and one skandha
```

The Bhāṣya explains this as figurative attribution of the whole to a part, as when one says “the cloth is burned” although only one portion is burned.

### 3.8 Why three teachings?

**Voice:** Question followed by Vasubandhu's pedagogical explanation.

The three systems correspond to three differences among those to be trained:

```text
moha      delusion
indriya   faculty or capacity
ruci      inclination or preference
```

The Bhāṣya then subdivides each:

```text
three delusions:
    confusion concerning mental factors,
        through grasping them as a unitary self
    confusion concerning form
    confusion concerning both form and mind

three faculties:
    sharp
    middling
    weak

three inclinations:
    preference for brief exposition
    preference for medium exposition
    preference for extensive exposition
```

These correspond respectively to the teachings of skandhas, āyatanas, and dhātus.

The three schemes are therefore pedagogically differentiated presentations of the same total field, not rival ontologies.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
āyatana
    = functional access-way for mind and mental factors

dhātu
    = gotra
    = ākara, source or mine
    = source of its own kind through homogeneous causation

problem:
    the unconditioned is not causally productive

alternative/general definition:
    dhātu = intrinsic kind or svabhāva of each of eighteen dharmas

skandha
    retains the scriptural meaning of collection
    and is therefore conceptually existent as a gathering

three classifications
    are taught according to differences in delusion,
    cognitive capacity, and textual preference
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### `āya`

- **Provisional meaning:** arrival, acquisition, or extension of mental activity
- **Status:** explanatory etymological term
- **Function:** explains `āyatana` as that which extends the occurrence of mind and mental factors

### `gotra`

- **Provisional meaning:** source-kind, productive lineage, or domain-family
- **Status:** principal gloss of `dhātu`
- **Function:** identifies each dhātu as a determinate source or matrix

### `ākara`

- **Provisional meaning:** mine, deposit, source, generative matrix
- **Status:** explanatory gloss of `gotra`
- **Function:** clarifies the productive sense of dhātu through the mineral-mine analogy

### `sabhāgahetu`

- **Provisional meaning:** homogeneous cause; a cause producing a subsequent factor of the same kind
- **Status:** technical causal category
- **Function:** explains how a dhātu acts as the source of its own `jāti`

### `prajñaptisat`

- **Provisional meaning:** conceptually or nominally existent
- **Status:** ontological classification
- **Function:** characterizes the skandha as a collection rather than an additional substance

### `utsūtra`

- **Provisional meaning:** contrary to scripture or outside the sūtra
- **Status:** critical designation
- **Function:** rejects alternative derivations of `skandha` that abandon the scriptural meaning of collection

### `vineya`

- **Provisional meaning:** one who is to be trained or guided
- **Status:** pedagogical term
- **Function:** identifies the audience whose differences explain the three teaching schemes

## 6. Voice Register

- **Vasubandhu:** explains āyatana as an access-way for mind and mental factors.
- **Standard Abhidharma explanation:** dhātu means gotra, illustrated by mineral sources within a mountain.
- **Questioner:** asks what kind the eye and other dhātus generate.
- **Reply:** they generate their own kind through homogeneous causation.
- **Objector:** argues that this excludes the unconditioned from dhātu-status.
- **Alternative teachers (`apare`):** define dhātu as the intrinsic kinds or natures of the eighteen dharmas.
- **Objector:** argues that the collective meaning makes skandhas merely conceptual.
- **Alternative teachers:** propose burden-bearing or sectional meanings for skandha.
- **Vasubandhu:** rejects those derivations as unscriptural and retains the collective meaning.
- **Vibhāṣā citation:** presents two ways of speaking about the relation of an atom to skandha, āyatana, and dhātu.
- **Vasubandhu:** explains the three teachings through differences among those to be trained.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

**Status:** Refined.

The earlier translations remain broadly correct, but the Bhāṣya requires sharper renderings:

```text
āyatana
    not merely “sphere”
    but functional access-way through which mind and mental factors extend

dhātu
    not merely “domain” or static class
    but gotra/ākara: source-kind or generative matrix

skandha
    genuinely means collection
    and therefore has conceptual, not additional substantial, existence
```

Recommended translation:

> The aggregates, access-spheres, and domains mean respectively collection, gateways of mental extension, and source-kinds. Because those to be trained differ threefold in delusion, faculty, and inclination, there are the three teachings beginning with the aggregates.

The current Kārikā file's “lineage/source-kind” should be retained but supplemented by the Bhāṣya's more concrete `ākara`, “source” or “mine.”

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya supplies a disciplined basis for the project speculation that a Dharma operates as a principle that becomes factual through determinate production:

```text
jāti / svabhāva
    = invariant kind-determination

dhātu as gotra / ākara
    = source capable of reproducing that kind

sabhāgahetu
    = causal realization of the kind

particular dharma-event
    = factual occurrence of that kind
```

This contact point is stronger than a mere analogy because the text explicitly says that the eye and other dhātus are sources of their own kind. Nevertheless, the Bhāṣya remains within a conditioned causal theory. It does not itself identify `jāti`, `gotra`, or `svabhāva` with a paramārthika principle existing apart from the causal continuum.

The safe project formulation is therefore:

```text
Bhāṣya-supported:
    dhātu expresses intrinsic kind and lawful reproduction of kind

Organon speculation:
    the principial Dharma constructs itself within a field
    and appears there as a fact
```

The speculation is now textually motivated, but it should remain distinct from Vasubandhu's own stated doctrine.
