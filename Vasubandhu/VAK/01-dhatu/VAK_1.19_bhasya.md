# VAK_1.19 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> jātigocaravijñānasāmānyād ekadhātutā /
>
> dvitve 'pi cakṣurādīnāṃ śobhārthaṃ tu dvayodbhavaḥ // VAkK_1.19 //

Established Kārikā result:

```text
paired sense faculties
    retain one-domain status
because they share
    kind
    object-range
    corresponding vijñāna
```

The Bhāṣya explains why numerical bodily duality does not require a multiplication of dhātus.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

> nanu caikaviṃśatyā dhātubhir bhavitavyam /
>
> cakṣuṣo dvitvāt śrotraghrāṇayoś ca /
>
> na bhavitavyam /
>
> yasmāt /
>
> jātigocaravijñānasāmānyād ekadhātutā /
>
> dvitve 'pi cakṣurādīnāṃ
>
> tatra jātisāmānyam ubhayoś cakṣuḥsvabhāvatvāt /
>
> gocarasāmānyam ubhayo rūpaviṣayatvāt /
>
> vijñānasāmānyam ubhayor ekacakṣurvijñānāśrayatvāt /
>
> tasmād eka eva cakṣurdhātuḥ /
>
> evaṃ śrotraghrāṇayor api yojyam /
>
> śobhārthaṃ tu dvayodbhavaḥ // VAkK_1.19 //
>
> ekadhātutve 'pi tu cakṣurādīnāṃ dvayoḥ saṃbhava āśrayasya śobhārtham /
>
> anyathā hy ekacakṣuḥśrotrādhiṣṭhānaikanāsikāvilasaṃbhavān mahad vairūpyaṃ syād iti /

### Translation

**Voice: Objector**

> But should there not be twenty-one dhātus, because the eye is twofold, and likewise the ear and nose?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> There should not. Why?

> Because of commonality of kind, object-range, and vijñāna, there is one-domain status even though the eye and the others are dual.

> Here there is commonality of kind because both possess the nature of eye. There is commonality of object-range because both have visible form as their object. There is commonality of vijñāna because both serve as the support of one eye-vijñāna. Therefore there is only one eye-dhātu. The same reasoning should be applied to the ear and nose.

> Their arising as a pair, however, is for beauty.

> Even though the eye and the others have one-domain status, their occurrence as two serves the beauty of the support. Otherwise, because there would be only one eye-site, one ear-site, and one nostril-opening, there would be great deformity.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 The objection concerns the counting rule

The objector does not deny the eighteen-dhātu system outright. The question is whether paired faculties should be counted separately:

```text
two eyes
+ two ears
+ two nostrils
```

If anatomical number controlled classification, three additional dhātus would be required:

```text
18 + 3 = 21 dhātus
```

The Bhāṣya rejects this counting rule.

### 3.2 Dhātu-unity is established through three commonalities

Vasubandhu gives three reasons why the two eyes form one dhātu.

#### Common kind — jāti-sāmānya

Both possess eye-nature:

> ubhayoś cakṣuḥsvabhāvatvāt

The two bodily instances do not differ in their classificatory nature.

#### Common object-range — gocara-sāmānya

Both have visible form as their object:

> ubhayo rūpaviṣayatvāt

The two eyes do not open onto two different classes of object. They share one visual field in the classificatory sense.

#### Common vijñāna — vijñāna-sāmānya

Both are supports of one eye-vijñāna:

> ubhayor ekacakṣurvijñānāśrayatvāt

This is the decisive cognitive criterion. There are two bodily supports, but not two species of eye-vijñāna.

Thus:

```text
bodily multiplicity
    does not entail
domain multiplicity
```

### 3.3 The reasoning applies analogically to ear and nose

Vasubandhu does not repeat the full analysis. He instructs that the same reasoning be applied:

> evaṃ śrotraghrāṇayor api yojyam

The ear pair shares auditory kind, sound as object-range, and ear-vijñāna. The nostril pair shares olfactory kind, smell as object-range, and nose-vijñāna.

### 3.4 Why are the faculties paired at all?

After establishing one-domain status, the Bhāṣya answers a different question: why does the bodily support arise in pairs?

The answer is:

> āśrayasya śobhārtham

For the beauty or symmetry of the bodily support.

This is not the principle that establishes domain-unity. It explains bodily arrangement after domain-unity has already been established.

The argument therefore has two levels:

```text
Abhidharma classification
    determined by kind, range, and vijñāna

bodily configuration
    explained by symmetry or beauty
```

### 3.5 What the passage does not say

The passage does not say that the paired organs are numerically one. Nor does it say that they are unreal. It says only that numerical plurality at the level of bodily support does not generate distinct dhātu classes.

The dhātu count is classificatory and relational, not anatomical.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Objection:
paired eye, ear, and nose should produce 21 dhātus.

Reply:
each pair shares
    one kind
    one object-range
    one corresponding vijñāna-type.

Therefore:
each pair has one-domain status.

Additional question:
why do the faculties arise in pairs?

Answer:
for the beauty and symmetry of the bodily support.
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### jāti-sāmānya

- **Provisional meaning:** commonality of kind or class
- **Status:** Vasubandhu's explanatory term
- **Function:** Establishes that paired faculties share one intrinsic classificatory nature.

### gocara-sāmānya

- **Provisional meaning:** commonality of object-range
- **Status:** Vasubandhu's explanatory term
- **Function:** Establishes that both members of a faculty pair address the same object-domain.

### vijñāna-sāmānya

- **Provisional meaning:** commonality of corresponding vijñāna
- **Status:** Vasubandhu's explanatory term
- **Function:** Establishes that both members support one type of sensory knowledge.

### ekadhātutā

- **Provisional meaning:** one-domain status
- **Status:** Kārikā and Bhāṣya term
- **Function:** Names classificatory unity despite bodily plurality.

### āśrayasya śobhā

- **Provisional meaning:** beauty or symmetry of the bodily support
- **Status:** Vasubandhu's explanation of paired embodiment
- **Function:** Explains why one faculty-domain may possess two bodily sites.

### vairūpya

- **Provisional meaning:** deformity, disfigurement, asymmetry
- **Status:** Vasubandhu's illustrative term
- **Function:** Describes the result imagined if paired faculties had only one bodily site.

No distinct Vaibhāṣika attribution occurs in this natural unit.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

**Decision: confirmed and refined.**

The established rendering is substantially correct. The Bhāṣya sharpens three points:

1. `jāti` means shared faculty-nature, not merely a loose class resemblance.
2. `gocara` is the common object-range of both bodily supports.
3. `vijñāna-sāmānya` means that both supports condition one species of corresponding vijñāna.

A refined translation is:

> Because they share the same kind, object-range, and corresponding vijñāna, the eye and the others have one-domain status even though each is dual. Their arising as a pair, however, serves bodily symmetry.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya confirms a modest structural principle:

```text
domain identity
    is not fixed by numerical embodiment
but by
    intrinsic kind
    object-range
    corresponding mode of knowledge
```

This supports reading the dhātus as coordinated domains within a cognitive architecture.

The stronger project thesis remains speculative:

```text
Vijñānaskandha produces or houses
    the articulated dhātu system
```

VAK 1.19 does not establish that thesis. It does show that vijñāna is one of the three criteria by which the unity of a sensory dhātu is determined. The other two criteria—kind and object-range—remain irreducible elements of Vasubandhu's explicit argument.
