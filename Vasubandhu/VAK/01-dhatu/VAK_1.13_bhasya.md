# VAK_1.13 Bhāṣya

## Worldly Element-Names and the Technical Rūpa Domain

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.13.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Corrections and refinements required by the Bhāṣya are recorded here for a later Kārikā revision pass.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> pṛthivī varṇasaṃsthānam ucyate lokasaṃjñayā /
>
> āpas tejaś ca vāyus tu dhātur eva tathāpi ca // VAkK_1.13 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> By worldly designation, color and configuration are called “earth.” The same is true of water and fire. Wind, however, is the wind-element itself—although it too can be designated through visible appearance.

The first-pass translation correctly recognized the contrast between worldly designation and technical analysis, but the Bhāṣya makes the syntax more exact. The phrase `tathāpi ca` does not simply say that all four are “likewise only domains.” It adds a qualification to wind:

```text
technically
    worldly wind = vāyudhātu itself

but conventionally
    wind may also be named through visible color and configuration
```

The later Kārikā revision should therefore preserve the special status of wind rather than flattening all four cases into one formula.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question:

> *kaḥ punaḥ pṛthivyādināṃ pṛthivīdhātvādīnāṃ ca viśeṣaḥ?*

and continues through the discussion of why the entire series ending in avijñapti is called the rūpaskandha.

It forms one continuous movement:

```text
ordinary earth, water, fire, and wind
    ↓
distinction from the four technical dhātus
    ↓
visible color and configuration as worldly designation
    ↓
special case of wind
    ↓
question: why are all these called rūpa?
    ↓
rūpa as susceptibility to alteration or affliction
    ↓
objections concerning atoms, past and future form, and avijñapti
    ↓
debate over the dependence of avijñapti upon the great elements
```

This is therefore the closing analysis of the rūpaskandha, not merely a lexical note about the common names of the elements.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. The question of ordinary elements and elemental domains

> *kaḥ punaḥ pṛthivyādināṃ pṛthivīdhātvādīnāṃ ca viśeṣaḥ?*

### Translation

> What, then, is the difference between earth and the other ordinary elements, on the one hand, and the earth-element and the other elemental domains, on the other?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya explicitly distinguishes two levels:

```text
pṛthivī, āpas, tejas, vāyu
    ordinary element-names

pṛthivīdhātu, abdhātu, tejodhātu, vāyudhātu
    technical Abhidharma determinations
```

The common word and the dhātu are therefore not automatically coextensive.

---

### B. What the world calls earth

> *pṛthivī varṇasaṃsthānam ucyate lokasaṃjñayā.*

> *tathā hi pṛthivīṃ darśayanto varṇaṃ saṃsthānaṃ ca darśayanti.*

### Translation

> By worldly designation, color and configuration are called “earth.” For when people point out earth, they point out a color and a configuration.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The ordinary object called earth is what is visually presented:

```text
worldly earth
    = visible color
    + visible configuration
```

This is not yet the earth-dhātu defined in VAK 1.12 by support-function and hardness.

The Bhāṣya’s criterion is practical and linguistic. When someone shows another person “earth,” what is shown is a visible field. The technical earth-domain itself is not directly indicated by that ordinary act of pointing.

---

### C. Water and fire follow the same pattern

> *yathā pṛthivī evaṃ āpas tejaś ca.*

> *varṇasaṃsthānam evocyate lokasaṃjñayā.*

### Translation

> As with earth, so also with water and fire: by worldly designation, color and configuration alone are called by those names.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Thus ordinary water and fire are also visible complexes:

```text
worldly water
    = color and configuration associated with water

worldly fire
    = color and configuration associated with fire
```

The water-dhātu as cohesion and the fire-dhātu as heat or maturation are technical determinations beneath the common designation.

---

### D. The special case of wind

> *vāyus tu dhātur eva.*

> *ya eva tu vāyudhātuḥ sa eva loke vāyur ity ucyate.*

### Translation

> Wind, however, is the elemental domain itself. The very wind-element is what is called “wind” in the world.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Wind initially differs from the first three:

```text
earth, water, fire
    ordinary name primarily indicates visible appearance

wind
    ordinary name may indicate vāyudhātu itself
```

Wind can be encountered through impulsion or movement without first being identified through a stable visible configuration.

---

### E. Yet wind can also receive a worldly visible designation

> *tathāpi ca.*

> *yathā pṛthivī varṇasaṃsthānam ucyate lokasaṃjñayā tathā vāyur api.*

> *nīlikā vātyā maṇḍalikā vātyeti.*

### Translation

> Nevertheless, just as color and configuration are called “earth” by worldly designation, so also wind may be designated in that way—as when people speak of a blue windstorm or a circular windstorm.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The qualification prevents an absolute opposition.

Wind itself may be known through its dhātu-function, but worldly language can also name visible effects or formations as wind:

```text
blue windstorm
circular windstorm
```

These expressions refer to visible color and configuration associated with the wind-event.

The relation is therefore:

```text
technical determination
    vāyudhātu = impulsion / movement

worldly presentation
    visible configuration produced or carried by wind
```

## 4. Why the Entire Group Is Called Rūpa

### A. The question

> *kasmāt punar ayam avijñaptiparyanto rūpaskandha ity ucyate?*

### Translation

> Why, then, is this entire series, extending as far as avijñapti, called the aggregate of form?

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The question gathers everything analyzed since VAK 1.09:

```text
five faculties
five object-domains
avijñapti
    ↓
rūpaskandha
```

The Bhāṣya now seeks the common determination that unifies them.

---

### B. Rūpa is so called because it is affected or altered

> *raupaṇāt.*

> *uktaṃ bhagavatā: “rūpyate rūpyata iti bhikṣavas tasmād rūpopādānaskandha ity ucyate.”*

### Translation

> Because it is affected or altered. The Blessed One said: “It is affected, it is affected, monks; therefore it is called the aggregate of appropriated form.”

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, citing the Buddha

The Bhāṣya derives `rūpa` through `rūpyate`:

```text
rūpa
    = that which is affected
    = that which is altered, harmed, or subjected to impact
```

This is more precise than defining rūpa simply as visible or material appearance.

---

### C. What does the affecting?

> *kena rūpyate?*

> *pāṇisparśenāpi spṛṣṭo rūpyata iti vistaraḥ.*

> *rūpyate bādhyata ity arthaḥ.*

### Translation

> By what is it affected? The discourse continues: “Even when touched by the hand, it is affected.” Here `rūpyate` means “it is afflicted, disturbed, or harmed.”

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, citing and explaining scripture

The core sense is not merely geometric change. It is susceptibility to being acted upon:

```text
contact
    ↓
disturbance or alteration
    ↓
rūpa-status
```

This establishes rūpa as the domain of impact and transformation.

---

### D. Scriptural illustration through frustrated desire

The Bhāṣya cites a verse:

> *tasya cet kāmayānasya cchandajātasya dehinaḥ /*
>
> *te kāmā na samṛdhyanti śalyaviddha iva rūpyate //*

### Translation

> When the desires of an embodied person in whom longing has arisen are not fulfilled, he is afflicted as though pierced by an arrow.

**Voice:** Scriptural citation introduced by Vasubandhu

The citation confirms that `rūpyate` can mean being afflicted or altered, not merely possessing visible form.

---

### E. The alteration proper to rūpa

> *rūpasya punaḥ kā bādhanā?*

> *vipariṇāmotpādanā.*

### Translation

> What, then, is the affliction of material form? It is the production of alteration.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The characteristic vulnerability of rūpa is transformation:

```text
rūpa
    is capable of being made otherwise
    through contact and conditions
```

This fits the earlier account of the great elements as functional factors in material change.

---

### F. An alternative definition: obstruction by material form

> *pratighāto rūpeṇety apare.*

### Translation

> Others say: rūpa is so called because there is obstruction through material form.

**Voice:** uncertain — alternative doctrinal position

The alternative takes physical resistance or mutual exclusion as the common mark:

```text
rūpa
    = that which obstructs or is obstructed
```

Vasubandhu records the account but does not simply identify it as his own final definition.

---

## 5. Objections to the Definition of Rūpa

### A. The atomic objection

> *paramāṇurūpaṃ tarhi rūpaṃ na prāpnoty arūpaṇāt.*

### Translation

> In that case, an individual atom would not count as rūpa, because it is not separately affected.

**Voice:** Objector

If susceptibility to alteration is the criterion, an isolated atom appears problematic.

---

### B. Reply: no isolated atom exists as a separate material entity

> *na vai paramāṇurūpam ekaṃ pṛthag bhūtam asti.*

> *saṃghātasthaṃ tu tad rūpyata eva.*

### Translation

> But a single atomic form does not exist separately and independently. Existing within an aggregate, it is indeed affected.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position, presented by Vasubandhu

The reply denies the concrete existence of an isolated atom:

```text
atom apart from aggregation
    not an actual material occurrence

atom within material aggregation
    participates in alteration
```

Rūpa belongs to the functioning aggregate, not to an imagined atom abstracted from every complex.

---

### C. Past and future rūpa

> *atītānāgataṃ tarhi rūpaṃ na prāpnoti.*

### Translation

> Then past and future material form would not count as rūpa.

**Voice:** Objector

Past form is no longer being affected; future form is not yet being affected.

---

### D. Reply through temporal belonging to the same kind

> *tad api rūpitaṃ rūpayiṣyamāṇaṃ tajjātīyaṃ ceti rūpam indhanavat.*

### Translation

> Past form has been affected, future form will be affected, and both belong to that same kind; therefore they are rūpa, just as something is called fuel by belonging to the kind that burns.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika position, presented by Vasubandhu

The category extends through temporal modalization:

```text
past rūpa
    that which has undergone alteration

present rūpa
    that which undergoes alteration

future rūpa
    that which will undergo alteration
```

This resembles the earlier treatment of future conditioned dharmas: membership in the kind is sufficient even when the defining operation is not presently manifest.

---

### E. The avijñapti objection

> *avijñaptis tarhi na prāpnoti.*

### Translation

> Then avijñapti would not count as rūpa.

**Voice:** Objector

Avijñapti is not visibly struck, displaced, or altered in the ordinary way.

This objection is especially important because VAK 1.11 included avijñapti within rūpa while defining it through non-disclosure.

---

## 6. Why Avijñapti Counts as Rūpa

### A. First proposal: it is affected through the alteration of vijñapti

> *sāpi vijñaptirūpaṇād rūpitā bhavati.*

> *vṛkṣapracalane cchāyāpracalanavat.*

### Translation

> It too becomes affected through the affecting of the manifest action, just as a shadow moves when a tree moves.

**Voice:** uncertain — proposed defense of the Vaibhāṣika classification

The analogy is:

```text
vijñapti : avijñapti
    =
tree : shadow
```

Avijñapti would be materially classified because it follows the alteration of the manifest bodily or verbal action from which it arises.

---

### B. Objection: avijñapti does not change in the same manner

> *nāvikārāt.*

### Translation

> No, because avijñapti does not undergo such modification.

**Voice:** Objector

The shadow analogy is too strong. Avijñapti does not continuously mirror every alteration of vijñapti.

---

### C. Further objection: the analogy would require simultaneous cessation

> *vijñaptinivṛttau cāvijñaptinivṛttiḥ syād vṛkṣābhāve cchāyābhāvavat.*

### Translation

> Moreover, when vijñapti ceased, avijñapti would also have to cease, just as a shadow disappears when the tree is absent.

**Voice:** Objector

But avijñapti is defined precisely as a continuing stream that may remain after the initiating manifest action has ceased. The tree-and-shadow model therefore fails.

---

### D. Second proposal: avijñapti is rūpa because its elemental support is affected

> *āśrayabhūtarūpaṇād ity apare.*

### Translation

> Others say that it is rūpa because the great elements serving as its support are affected.

**Voice:** uncertain — alternative doctrinal position

This shifts the argument from production by vijñapti to dependence on the mahābhūtas:

```text
avijñapti
    depends upon material elemental support

that support is susceptible to alteration

therefore avijñapti belongs to rūpa
```

---

### E. Objection from the sensory vijñānas

> *evaṃ tarhi cakṣurvijñānādīnām apy āśrayarūpaṇād rūpatvaprasaṅgaḥ.*

### Translation

> In that case, eye-vijñāna and the other sensory knowledges would also have to be rūpa, because their supports are affected material forms.

**Voice:** Objector

This objection is highly relevant to the project’s Knowledge Domain interpretation.

The mere fact that a vijñāna depends on a material faculty does not make that vijñāna material. Therefore simple dependence upon a material support cannot by itself establish rūpa-status.

---

### F. Reply: avijñapti depends continuously, while vijñāna merely has a condition of arising

> *viṣamo 'yam upanyāsaḥ.*

> *avijñaptir hi cchāyeva vṛkṣaṃ prabheva maṇiṃ bhūtāny āśritya vartate.*

> *na tv evaṃ cakṣurādīny āśritya vartante cakṣurvijñānādīni; kevalaṃ tūtpattinimittamātraṃ tāni teṣāṃ bhavanti.*

### Translation

> The comparison is unequal. Avijñapti exists in dependence upon the great elements as a shadow depends upon a tree or radiance upon a jewel. Eye-vijñāna and the other sensory knowledges do not exist in that same way upon the eye and the other faculties; those faculties are merely conditions for their arising.

**Voice:** Vaibhāṣika defense, presented by Vasubandhu

A crucial distinction is introduced:

```text
continuous supporting dependence
    avijñapti upon mahābhūtas

versus

causal condition for arising
    vijñāna upon indriya
```

This distinction prevents the materialization of vijñāna.

For the project hypothesis, it is equally important that Vijñānaskandha is not reduced to its material supports. The faculties condition the arising of knowledge, but knowledge is not a material property of those faculties.

---

### G. Vasubandhu’s criticism of the shadow analogy

> *idaṃ tāvad avaibhāṣikīyaṃ vṛkṣam āśritya cchāyā vartate maṇiṃ cāśritya prabheti.*

### Translation

> To begin with, the claim that a shadow exists dependent upon a tree and radiance dependent upon a jewel is not Vaibhāṣika doctrine.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu now directly marks the proposed analogy as inconsistent with Vaibhāṣika atomism.

According to that framework, the atoms of shadow and radiance each depend upon their own set of four great elements. They do not literally inhere in the tree or jewel as the analogy suggests.

This is a valuable example of the Bhāṣya refusing to let a convenient illustration substitute for a precise doctrinal account.

---

### H. Even if support were granted, avijñapti would not depend in exactly the same way

> *saty api ca tadāśritatve cchāyāprabhayor nāvijñaptis tathaivāśritā.*

> *niruddheṣv apy avijñaptyāśrayeṣu mahābhūteṣu tasyā anirodho 'bhyupagamyate.*

### Translation

> Even if the dependence of shadow and radiance were granted, avijñapti would not depend in precisely that way. It is admitted that avijñapti does not cease even when the great elements that served as its original support have ceased.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is the decisive pressure point:

```text
originating mahābhūtas cease
    but
avijñapti-stream may continue
```

Therefore the dependence relation cannot be a simple simultaneous inherence.

The ontology of avijñapti remains doctrinally difficult. The Bhāṣya records the Vaibhāṣika classification while exposing the weakness of several standard defenses.

---

### I. A further reply based on differentiated supports

> *anye punar atra parihāram āhuḥ...*

### Translation

> Others offer another reply here: among the supports of the sensory vijñānas, some—such as the eye—are affected material forms, while another—the mind—is not. This is not the case with avijñapti. Therefore the consequence is not parallel, and it is reasonable to call avijñapti rūpa because its support is material.

**Voice:** uncertain — alternative doctrinal defense

This response tries to preserve the distinction by noting that sensory and mental knowledge do not share one uniformly material type of support.

The Bhāṣya records the reply without finally resolving every difficulty.

## 7. Argument Summary

```text
ordinary language names visible complexes
    “earth,” “water,” and “fire”
        ↓
Abhidharma distinguishes those appearances
from hardness, cohesion, and heat as dhātus
        ↓
wind is a special case:
worldly wind may directly name vāyudhātu,
though visible wind-formations also receive the name
        ↓
the text then asks what unifies the entire rūpaskandha
        ↓
rūpa is defined as susceptibility to alteration or affliction
        ↓
objections test this definition against
atoms, past and future form, and avijñapti
        ↓
avijñapti creates the hardest case
because it continues without ordinary disclosure or impact
        ↓
several dependence theories are proposed and criticized
        ↓
the classification of avijñapti as rūpa is retained,
but its exact ontological grounding remains contested
```

The unit therefore moves from a simple distinction between common and technical naming into a serious examination of the unity of the material aggregate.

## 8. New Technical Terms

### **lokasaṃjñā**

- **Provisional meaning:** worldly or conventional designation
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā analyses before this passage
- **Function:** distinguishes ordinary names based on visible presentation from technical Abhidharma determinations

### **raupaṇa**

- **Provisional meaning:** being affected, altered, disturbed, or afflicted
- **Status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya as the derivational basis of `rūpa`
- **Function:** supplies the common characteristic unifying the rūpaskandha

### **bādhanā**

- **Provisional meaning:** affliction, disturbance, or being acted upon
- **Status:** new in this passage
- **Function:** explains the operative sense of `rūpyate`

### **vipariṇāmotpādana**

- **Provisional meaning:** production of alteration
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** defines the specific form of affliction proper to rūpa

### **pratighāta**

- **Provisional meaning:** obstruction, resistance, or mutual impact
- **Status:** introduced as an alternative definition of rūpa
- **Function:** alternative doctrinal account of materiality

### **tajjātīya**

- **Provisional meaning:** belonging to the same kind
- **Status:** new in the local Bhāṣya
- **Function:** explains why past and future rūpa retain rūpa-status

### **utpattinimittamātra**

- **Provisional meaning:** merely a condition for arising
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** distinguishes the relation of vijñāna to its faculty from the proposed dependence of avijñapti upon the great elements

### **āśritatva**

- **Provisional meaning:** state of dependence upon a support
- **Status:** previously encountered in simpler form through `āśraya`; technically developed here
- **Function:** central term in the debate over why avijñapti is classified as rūpa

## 10. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass file should later be revised in two respects.

### A. Preserve the special syntax of wind

Current first-pass rendering:

```text
water, fire, and wind likewise are only domains
```

Bhāṣya-supported structure:

```text
earth, water, fire
    worldly names primarily designate visible color and shape

wind
    may directly designate vāyudhātu itself
    although visible wind-formations also receive the worldly name
```

### B. Avoid treating `dhātu` as merely a hidden substance behind appearance

The contrast is not simply:

```text
visible appearance
    versus
invisible underlying stuff
```

It is:

```text
worldly designation based on what is pointed out
    versus
technical determination by characteristic and function
```

The later Kārikā revision should keep this epistemic and analytic distinction explicit.

## 11. Provisional Organon Contact Point

This passage strongly supports the project distinction between **appearance-name** and **domain-determination**:

```text
loka-saṃjñā
    organizes what appears for ordinary cognition

dhātu-analysis
    determines the functional and characteristic structure
    through which the appearance is intelligible
```

It also places an important limit upon the claim that the dhātus simply “belong” to Vijñānaskandha.

The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
material faculty
    condition for the arising of vijñāna

vijñāna
    knowledge-event not reducible to its material support
```

Thus the Knowledge Domain interpretation remains promising, but the relation must be stated carefully:

```text
indriya-dhātu and artha-dhātu
    articulate the conditions and domain of a determinate vijñāna

vijñāna-dhātu
    is not materially contained in those supports
```

The triad is relational and cognitive without collapsing every dhātu into Vijñānaskandha as a material possession.

The avijñapti debate also introduces a deeper issue: a determination may belong to rūpa because of its lawful dependence upon material conditions even when it is neither visibly disclosed nor synchronically altered like gross form. That question should remain open until the Karma chapter supplies the fuller account.
