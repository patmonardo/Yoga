# VAK_1.09 Bhāṣya

## Rūpa as Faculty, Object-Domain, and Support of Vijñāna

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.09.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya sharpens or corrects the first-pass translation, that decision is recorded here for later review.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> rūpaṃ pañcendriyāṇy arthāḥ pañcāvijñaptir eva ca /
>
> tadvijñānāśrayā rūpaprasādāś cakṣurādayaḥ // VAkK_1.09 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Rūpa consists of the five faculties, the five objects, and avijñapti. The eye and the other faculties are clear material forms that serve as supports for their corresponding vijñānas.

The verse begins the internal analysis of the rūpa-skandha. It does not define rūpa merely as visible shape. It identifies the whole aggregate through three divisions:

```text
rūpa-skandha
    = five faculties
    + five objects
    + avijñapti
```

The Bhāṣya then begins with the five faculties and defines them relationally, through the vijñānas that depend upon them.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit extends from:

> *pañca rūpādayaḥ skandhā ity uktam...*

through:

> *nirddiṣṭāni pañcendriyāṇi. arthāḥ pañca nirdeśyāḥ.*

It forms a compact movement:

```text
the five aggregates have been stated
    ↓
the rūpa-skandha is now analyzed
    ↓
five faculties are enumerated
    ↓
five corresponding object-domains are named
    ↓
avijñapti completes the rūpa-skandha
    ↓
the five faculties are defined as material supports of vijñāna
    ↓
the discussion turns next to the five objects
```

This is a transitional verse. It begins the detailed constitution of rūpa while simultaneously establishing that the sense faculties are intelligible only within relations of knowing.

---

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. Transition from the five aggregates to the rūpa-skandha

> *pañca rūpādayaḥ skandhā ity uktam. tatra—rūpaṃ pañcendriyāṇy arthāḥ pañcāvijñaptir eva ca.*

### Translation

> It has been said that there are five aggregates beginning with rūpa. Among them, rūpa consists of the five faculties, the five objects, and avijñapti.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya now moves from the general aggregate structure to the internal articulation of its first member.

```text
five aggregates
    ↓
rūpa-skandha
    ↓
faculties + objects + avijñapti
```

The rūpa-skandha is therefore not a single homogeneous category. It includes:

1. the material capacities by which sensory vijñāna arises;
2. the corresponding sensory object-domains;
3. avijñapti, a form not disclosed in the ordinary sensory manner.

The third member is only named here. Its full determination is deferred.

---

### B. The five faculties

> *pañcendriyāṇi cakṣuḥśrotraghrāṇajihvākāyendriyāṇi.*

### Translation

> The five faculties are the faculties of eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The list is conventional, but the Bhāṣya does not identify the faculties with the gross anatomical organs. Their actual definition follows through `rūpaprasāda` and `vijñānāśraya`.

The five are:

```text
cakṣur-indriya
śrotra-indriya
ghrāṇa-indriya
jihvā-indriya
kāya-indriya
```

These names indicate powers or faculties rather than merely visible body parts.

---

### C. The five objects are the corresponding domains of the faculties

> *pañcārthās teṣām eva cakṣurādīnām indriyāṇāṃ yathāsvaṃ ye pañca viṣayā rūpaśabdagandharasaspraṣṭavyākhyāḥ.*

### Translation

> The five objects are the five respective domains of those very faculties beginning with the eye, namely visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the tangible.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya glosses `arthāḥ` through `viṣayāḥ`:

```text
artha
    = the corresponding viṣaya
    = the domain in relation to which a faculty and its vijñāna operate
```

The five are paired as follows:

| Faculty | Object-domain |
|---|---|
| eye | visible form |
| ear | sound |
| nose | odor |
| tongue | taste |
| body | tangible |

This supports retaining **object-domain** as the immediate doctrinal rendering here. The project rendering **Meaning** remains philosophically important, but in this passage `artha` has a clearly relational and sensory function: it is what belongs to a faculty as its proper field.

The relation is not simply:

```text
subject
    faces
external object
```

It is a structured triad:

```text
faculty
    ↔ object-domain
    ↔ corresponding vijñāna
```

The Bhāṣya will make the vijñāna-side explicit in its definition of the faculties.

---

### D. Avijñapti completes the rūpa-skandha

> *avijñaptiś ceti. etāvān rūpaskandhaḥ.*

### Translation

> And avijñapti. This is the full extent of the rūpa-skandha.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This brief statement is structurally important:

```text
five faculties
    + five objects
    + avijñapti
    = the complete rūpa-skandha
```

Avijñapti is not treated as an accidental supplement. Without it, the account of rūpa would be incomplete.

At this stage, however, the Bhāṣya does not yet define it. The proper methodological decision is to register its inclusion and defer interpretation until the text supplies its characteristics.

---

### E. The faculties are supports of their corresponding vijñānas

> *tadvijñānāśrayā rūpaprasādāś cakṣurādayaḥ.*

### Translation

> The eye and the other faculties are clear material forms that serve as supports for their corresponding vijñānas.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The compound `tad-vijñāna-āśrayāḥ` is decisive. Each faculty is defined through the vijñāna belonging to its domain:

```text
cakṣus
    = support of visual vijñāna

śrotra
    = support of auditory vijñāna

ghrāṇa
    = support of olfactory vijñāna

jihvā
    = support of gustatory vijñāna

kāya
    = support of bodily vijñāna
```

The faculty is therefore not first identified as a gross organ and then assigned a cognitive use. Its very doctrinal definition is:

```text
rūpaprasāda
    functioning as
vijñānāśraya
```

The faculty belongs to rūpa, but it is defined by the knowledge-event it supports.

This gives the verse a more exact structure than a simple inventory of material entities:

```text
rūpa as faculty
    = refined material support
        of a determinate vijñāna
```

---

### F. The five clear material forms

> *rūpaśabdagandharasaspraṣṭavyavijñānānām āśrayabhūtā ye pañca rūpātmakāḥ prasādās te yathākramaṃ cakṣuḥśrotraghrāṇajihvākāyā veditavyāḥ.*

### Translation

> The five clear forms whose nature is material and which function as supports for the vijñānas of visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the tangible are to be understood, respectively, as eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This prose reverses the order of explanation. Instead of beginning with familiar bodily names, it begins with five refined material supports and tells us how they are to be identified:

```text
support of visible-form vijñāna
    → eye

support of sound vijñāna
    → ear

support of odor vijñāna
    → nose

support of taste vijñāna
    → tongue

support of tangible vijñāna
    → body
```

The sensory faculty is thus determined by function within a knowledge-domain.

The term `prasāda` should not be weakened into mere physical transparency. It indicates a clear, refined, or sensitive material form capable of serving as the specific support of a vijñāna.

---

### G. Scriptural support for the faculty as derived material form

> *yathoktaṃ bhagavatā “cakṣur bhikṣo ādhyātmikam āyatanaṃ catvāri mahābhūtāny upādāya rūpaprasāda” iti vistaraḥ.*

### Translation

> As the Blessed One has said: “The eye, monk, is the internal āyatana, a clear material form derived from the four great elements,” and so forth.

**Voice:** Scriptural citation introduced by Vasubandhu

The passage adds two determinations:

```text
cakṣus
    = internal āyatana
    = rūpaprasāda derived from the four great elements
```

The faculty is material, but not identical with one of the four great elements. It is a derivative or appropriated material form based upon them.

This prevents two reductions:

```text
faculty ≠ gross anatomical organ alone
faculty ≠ immaterial knowing power detached from rūpa
```

Instead:

```text
faculty
    = refined derivative materiality
    = internal domain
    = support of a determinate vijñāna
```

---

### H. The concise definition is restated

> *yāny etāni cakṣurādīny uktāni tadvijñānāśrayā rūpaprasādāś cakṣurādayaḥ. cakṣurvijñānādyāśrayā ity arthaḥ.*

### Translation

> Those faculties beginning with the eye that have been mentioned are clear material forms serving as supports for their corresponding vijñānas. That is, they are supports of visual vijñāna and the rest.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya explicitly glosses the compound:

```text
tad-vijñāna-āśraya
    = cakṣur-vijñāna-ādi-āśraya
    = support of visual vijñāna and the others
```

No independent definition of the faculty is supplied apart from this support relation.

This is the textual basis for reading the sensory architecture as a system of knowledge-domains rather than as a merely physiological classification.

Still, the Bhāṣya itself states the more limited claim:

```text
each faculty is the material support
of its corresponding sensory vijñāna
```

The stronger project claim—that the dhātus as such belong to Vijñānaskandha as supersensible Vidyā—should remain provisional until the later dhātu analysis explicitly determines the relation among skandha, āyatana, dhātu, and vijñāna.

---

### I. Agreement with the Prakaraṇa definition

> *evaṃ kṛtvā prakaraṇagrantho 'py anuvṛtto bhavati: “cakṣuḥ katamat? cakṣurvijñānāśrayo rūpaprasāda” iti vistaraḥ.*

### Translation

> Understood in this way, the definition in the *Prakaraṇa* is also followed: “What is the eye? A clear material form that is the support of visual vijñāna,” and so forth.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu citing an Abhidharma definition

The concise scholastic definition is:

```text
cakṣus
    = cakṣurvijñānāśraya
    + rūpaprasāda
```

It includes both genus and function:

```text
what it is
    rūpaprasāda

what it does
    supports visual vijñāna
```

The faculty is not vijñāna, but neither is it intelligible apart from vijñāna.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This marks the natural boundary of the present unit. The next movement turns from the support-side of sensory knowing to its object-side.

```text

        ↓
it consists of faculties, objects, and avijñapti
        ↓

        ↓
the five objects are their corresponding sensory domains
        ↓
avijñapti completes the rūpa-skandha
        ↓
each faculty is a rūpaprasāda
        ↓
each rūpaprasāda is defined as support of a determinate vijñāna
        ↓

```

The central determination is:

```text
indriya
    = refined material support
        of corresponding vijñāna
```

Rūpa here is not opposed to knowing as dead external matter. One portion of rūpa is precisely the material condition through which determinate vijñāna becomes possible.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### **rūpaskandha**

- **Provisional meaning:** the aggregate of material form, comprising five faculties, five sensory objects, and avijñapti
- **Status:** previously encountered as one of the five aggregates; now internally analyzed
- **Function:** establishes the complete extension of rūpa in this passage

### **pañcendriya**

- **Provisional meaning:** the five sensory faculties—eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body
- **Status:** introduced as the first division within the rūpa-skandha
- **Function:** names the material support-side of the five sensory vijñānas

### **pañcārtha**

- **Provisional meaning:** the five corresponding sensory object-domains
- **Status:** introduced in the Kārikā and glossed in the Bhāṣya by `viṣaya`
- **Function:** names the object-side of the five sensory relations

### **viṣaya**

- **Provisional meaning:** proper domain or field of a faculty and its vijñāna
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā phrase but supplied by the Bhāṣya to explain `artha`
- **Function:** clarifies that each sensory object is a determinate domain of operation

### **tadvijñānāśraya**

- **Provisional meaning:** support of the corresponding vijñāna
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā and explicitly unpacked by the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** gives the defining relation of each sensory faculty

### **rūpaprasāda**

- **Provisional meaning:** clear or refined material form capable of supporting a specific sensory vijñāna
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā; technically explained in the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** distinguishes the faculty from the gross organ while retaining its material nature

### **rūpātmaka**

- **Provisional meaning:** having material form as its nature
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** emphasizes that the refined faculty remains a rūpa-dharma

### **upādāya-rūpa**

- **Provisional meaning:** derived or appropriated material form based upon the four great elements
- **Status:** introduced through scriptural citation
- **Function:** explains the material constitution of the sensory faculty

### **ādhyātmika āyatana**

- **Provisional meaning:** internal domain or internal sense-base
- **Status:** introduced through scriptural citation
- **Function:** locates the faculty on the internal side of the āyatana system

### **Prakaraṇa definition**

- **Provisional meaning:** scholastic definition of the eye as a clear material support of visual vijñāna
- **Status:** cited as a previously authoritative formulation
- **Function:** confirms the Bhāṣya’s grammatical and doctrinal interpretation

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass translation is broadly sound, but three points should be sharpened during the later revision pass.

### A. `arthāḥ` should be rendered contextually as “objects” or “object-domains”

The Bhāṣya explicitly explains:

```text
pañcārthāḥ
    = pañca viṣayāḥ
```

Therefore the primary translation in this verse should not simply read “five meanings.” A suitable rendering is:

> the five objects

or more philosophically:

> the five object-domains.

The broader project rendering of `artha` as **Meaning** can remain as a deeper determination, but the Bhāṣya’s immediate lexical control must be preserved.

### B. `rūpaprasāda` is not merely “clear material form” in a visual sense

It means refined or sensitive derivative materiality capable of serving as a sensory support.

A useful note for revision is:

```text
rūpaprasāda
    = refined sensory materiality
    = support of a determinate vijñāna
```

### C. `vijñāna` should remain knowledge-bearing, but its technical function must stay precise

The project rendering **knowledge** is defensible here because the faculties are supports of determinate knowing. Yet the translation should not silently convert each sensory vijñāna into supersensible Vidyā. In this unit, the text speaks specifically of visual, auditory, olfactory, gustatory, and tactile vijñāna.

A Bhāṣya-informed revision would therefore read:

> Rūpa consists of the five faculties, the five objects, and avijñapti. The eye and the other faculties are refined material forms that support their corresponding sensory vijñānas.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The strongest text-controlled contact point is:

```text
faculty
    = material support of knowing
```

This means the rūpa-skandha already contains a relation to vijñāna within its own definition. Materiality is not merely placed outside knowledge as an alien object. It includes the refined support through which a determinate field of knowing can arise.

A provisional project model is therefore:

```text
indriya
    = support-pole of a knowledge-domain

artha / viṣaya
    = object-pole of that knowledge-domain

vijñāna
    = determinate knowing correlated with both
```

This gives a triadic architecture:

```text
āśraya
    faculty-support

viṣaya
    object-domain

vijñāna
    knowledge-event
```

Your stronger proposal may be stated provisionally as:

```text
dhātus
    = Knowledge Domains

Vijñānaskandha
    = the systematic unity in which these domains are known
```

The present passage supports the first movement toward this reading because faculties are defined through vijñāna and objects through their status as determinate domains. It does **not yet** explicitly state that all dhātus belong to Vijñānaskandha or that Vijñānaskandha is itself paramārthika supersensible Vidyā. Those claims should be tested against the later definitions of the eighteen dhātus and the relation between dhātu, āyatana, and skandha.

The right immediate conclusion is therefore:

```text
rūpa is already internally ordered toward knowledge
```

rather than:

```text
rūpa is a merely external material substrate
```

This is a substantial advance, but the full Knowledge Domain thesis remains open for textual development.
