>
> na dṛṣṭiheyam akliṣṭaṃ na rūpaṃ nāpy aṣaṣṭhajam // VAkK_1.40 //

### Established Kārikā Translation

> Ten are to be abandoned by cultivation, and five also. The last three are threefold. The undefiled is not to be abandoned by seeing, nor form, nor what is not born from the sixth.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. Three modes of path-status

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> How many domains are to be abandoned by seeing? How many by cultivation? How many are not to be abandoned?

The question orders the eighteen domains by their relation to the Path.

### 2. The ten material domains and five sensory knowledge-domains

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The ten form-bearing domains, and the five knowledge-domains, are to be abandoned by cultivation.

The ten are the five material faculties and five material objects. The additional five are the five sensory knowledge-domains.

They are not removed by the first direct seeing of truth. Their defiled modes are relinquished through the continued development of the Path.

### 3. The final three are threefold

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Mind-domain, dharma-domain, and mental-knowledge-domain—the final three in the order of recitation—are threefold.

Their three modes are:

```text
darśanaheya
    to be abandoned by seeing

bhāvanāheya
    to be abandoned by cultivation

aheya
    not to be abandoned
```

The Bhāṣya specifies:

```text
88 anuśayas
+ their co-arising dharmas
+ their acquisitions
+ their attendants
    = darśanaheya

remaining contaminated dharmas
    = bhāvanāheya

uncontaminated dharmas
    = aheya
```

### 4. Objection: surely other things oppose the noble path

**Voice: Objector**

> Is there not something else to be abandoned by seeing—ordinary-person status and bodily and verbal karma leading to a bad destination?

The objection assumes:

```text
whatever opposes the noble path
    must be abandoned by seeing
```

### 5. Vasubandhu rejects that inference

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Although these oppose the noble path, they are not therefore to be abandoned by seeing.

Mere opposition to the noble path is insufficient to determine the mode of abandonment.

### 6. The summary rule

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Nothing undefiled is to be abandoned by seeing; nor is material form; nor what does not arise from the sixth.

The sixth is the mental sphere, `mana-āyatana`.

Thus only defiled dharmas arising on the mental side can be directly abandoned by seeing.

### 7. Ordinary-person status is undefiled-neutral

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Ordinary-person status is undefiled and indeterminate.

Therefore it is not directly cut off by seeing in the strict Abhidharma sense, even though the arising of the noble path excludes it.

The Bhāṣya adds a consequence: if ordinary-person status were directly abandoned by seeing, then at the first moment of receptivity to the knowledge of suffering one would already cease to be an ordinary person in a way that disrupts the precise sequence of the Path.

### 8. Material karma is not abandoned by seeing

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Bodily and verbal karma are material, and materiality is not to be abandoned by seeing.

Even those whose wholesome roots are cut off or who are free from desire may still possess bodily and verbal material karma. Therefore materiality as such cannot be the direct object of seeing-abandonment.

### 9. What is not born from the sixth

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The sixth is the mental sphere. Whatever is born elsewhere, and whatever is born from the five faculties, is not to be abandoned by seeing.

This excludes sensory-born processes from direct abandonment by seeing.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Question:
How are the eighteen domains divided by mode of abandonment?

Determination 1:
Ten material domains and five sensory knowledge-domains
    → abandoned by cultivation

Determination 2:
Mind-domain, dharma-domain, and mental-knowledge-domain
    → threefold
        seeing-abandonable
        cultivation-abandonable
        not abandonable

Exact seeing-abandonable field:
88 anuśayas

+ acquisitions
+ attendants

Remaining contaminated mental dharmas:
    cultivation-abandonable

Uncontaminated dharmas:
    not abandonable

Objection:
Anything opposing the noble path should be seeing-abandonable.

Resolution:
Opposition alone does not determine the mode of abandonment.

Negative rule:
Nothing undefiled,
nothing material,
and nothing not born from the sixth
is abandoned by seeing.
```

## Translation Consequences

### 1. *Bhāvanayā heya*

The phrase should not be translated as though the domain itself were annihilated.

Preferred sense:

```text
to be abandoned through cultivation
    = its contaminated mode is relinquished
      through repeated path-development
```

### 2. *Dṛṣṭiheya*

“Abandoned by seeing” is correct, but the Bhāṣya makes it exact:

```text
direct seeing targets
    defiled mental dharmas
    rooted in the sixth sphere
```

### 3. *Aṣaṣṭhaja*

The Kārikā translation is confirmed:

```text
aṣaṣṭhaja
    = not born from the sixth
    = not generated from the mental sphere
```

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> The ten material domains and five sensory knowledge-domains are relinquished through cultivation. The final three domains—mind, dharma, and mental knowledge—may be abandoned by seeing, abandoned by cultivation, or not abandoned at all. Nothing undefiled, nothing material, and nothing not arising from the mental sphere is directly abandoned by seeing.

---

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya prevents a destructive reading of the Path.

```text
seeing
    does not abolish embodiment
    does not abolish sensory capacity
    does not abolish undefiled dharmas

seeing
    directly removes defiled mental determinations
```

The ten material domains and five sensory knowledge-domains are not rejected as such. Their contaminated operation is transformed through cultivation.

This also sharpens the earlier account of indriya as capacity. A faculty may be limited, conditioned, and path-transformable without being an error directly destroyed by insight.

The user’s quantitative hypothesis can therefore be recorded cautiously:

```text
indriya as capacity
    may possess a determinate operating range
    of magnitude, intensity, or kind

but VAK 1.40 itself
    does not yet quantify that range
```

What the present text supports is the stronger structural point:

```text
capacity is conditioned and cultivable
not simply false and eliminable
```

---

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes two forms of negation:

```text
direct negation by seeing
    removes false mental determination

mediated negation by cultivation
    transforms conditioned operation over time
```

And it preserves a third status:

```text
aheya
    not to be negated
    because it belongs to uncontaminated truth
```

Thus the Path is not one undifferentiated power of cancellation. It discriminates its object and mode.

```text
Seeing
    is intensive and selective

Cultivation
    is extensive and developmental
```

The distinction also protects the faculty as a middle term:

```text
Seer
    ↓ operates through
conditioned faculty
    ↓ transformed by cultivation
object-domain
```

The faculty is not directly annulled by seeing, because it is not itself the false determination that seeing removes.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The most important result is a distinction between truth-correction and capacity-development:

```text
truth-correction
    seeing removes false mental determination

capacity-development
    cultivation reshapes the conditioned range and operation
    of faculty and sensory knowledge
```

This gives a precise opening for the quantitative thought:

```text
Objectivity
    may appear quantitatively
    as the determinate range within which a faculty can function

Cultivation
    may alter the effective operation of that capacity

Seeing
    determines whether the mental form governing that operation is true or false
```

This is an Organon hypothesis, not yet a claim made explicitly by Vasubandhu. Its textual basis is the Bhāṣya’s strict separation of material and sensory capacities from the defiled mental dharmas directly abandoned by seeing.
