# VAK 1.48 — Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā Anchor

**Identifier:** VAK 1.48  
**Kārikā file:** [VAK_1.48.md](./VAK_1.48.md)

> pañca bāhyā dvijñeyāḥ nityā dharmā asaṃskṛtāḥ /
> dharmārdham indriyaṃ ye ca dvādaśādhyātmikāḥ smṛtāḥ // VAkK_1.48 //

> The five external domains are knowable by two. Permanent dharmas are the unconditioned. A portion of the dharma-domain has faculty-status, together with those twelve remembered as internal.

This final verse gathers four residual classifications and closes the first Kośasthāna:

1. which domains are knowable by one or two knowledge-domains;
2. which dharmas are permanent;
3. which domains or portions of domains possess faculty-status;
4. how the twenty-two faculties are distributed within the eighteen-domain system.

---

## Bhāṣya Source

### Knowability of the eighteen domains

> aṣṭādaśānāṃ dhātūnāṃ ṣaṇṇāṃ ca vijñānānāṃ kaḥ kena vijñeyaḥ /  
> āha /  
> pañca bāhyā dvivijñeyāḥ //  
> rūpaśabdagandharasaspraṣṭavyadhātavo yathāsaṃkhyaṃ cakṣuḥśrotraghrāṇajihvākāyavijñānair anubhūtā manovijñānena vijñāyante /  
> evam ete pratyekaṃ dvābhyāṃ vijñānābhyāṃ vijñeyā bhavanti /  
> śeṣās trayodaśa dhātavaḥ pañcānāṃ vijñānakāyānām aviṣayatvād ekena manovijñānena vijñeyā ity ākhyātaṃ bhavati /

— Pradhan 36.21–36.27

### Permanence

> eṣām aṣṭādaśānāṃ dhātūnāṃ madhye kati nityāḥ katy anityāḥ /  
> na kaścit sakalo 'sti nityo dhātur api tu  
> nityā dharmā asaṃskṛtāḥ /  
> tena dharmadhātvekadeśo nityaḥ śeṣā anityāḥ /

— Pradhan 37.1–37.4

### Faculty-status

> katīndriyaṃ kati nendriyam /  
> dharmārdham indriyaṃ ye ca dvādaśādhyātmikāḥ smṛtāḥ // VAkK_1.48 //

> ābhidhārmikās tu ṣaḍāyatanavyavasthānam ādṛtya jīvitendriyānantaraṃ manaindriyaṃ paṭhanti /  
> sālambanatvāt /  
> tatra dharmārdhaṃ jīvitendriyādīny ekādaśendriyāṇi trayāṇāṃ ca bhāgo dharmadhātupradeśatvāt /  
> dvādaśānām ādhyātmikānāṃ cakṣurādayaḥ pañca svanāmoktāḥ /  
> sapta cittadhātavo manaindriyaṃ strīpuruṣendriye kāyadhātupradeśa iti paścād vakṣyati /

— Pradhan 37.5–37.16

The e-text contains minor orthographic corruption in the list of faculties; the doctrinal sequence is nevertheless clear.

## Natural Segmentation of the Argument

### 1. Five external object-domains are known twice

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The five external object-domains are:

```text
rūpa
śabda
gandha
rasa
spraṣṭavya
```

Each is first experienced by its corresponding sensory knowledge:

```text
rūpa          by cakṣurvijñāna
śabda         by śrotravijñāna
gandha        by ghrāṇavijñāna
rasa          by jihvāvijñāna
spraṣṭavya    by kāyavijñāna
```

The same object-domain is then known by mental knowledge, `manovijñāna`.

Thus each of the five external object-domains is `dvi-vijñeya`: knowable by two knowledge-domains.

The remaining thirteen domains are not objects of the five sensory knowledge-groups. They are known only by mental knowledge.

The distinction is therefore:

```text
five external object-domains
    sensory knowledge
    + mental knowledge

remaining thirteen domains
    mental knowledge only
```

The Bhāṣya uses two verbs deliberately:

```text
anubhūta
    experienced by sensory knowledge

vijñāyate
    known by mental knowledge
```

This does not yet establish a complete theory of representation, but it clearly differentiates immediate sensory experience from subsequent or higher mental knowing.

### 2. No complete domain is permanent

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The question asks which of the eighteen domains are permanent and which impermanent.

The answer is exact:

> No whole domain is permanent.

Only the unconditioned dharmas are permanent. Since the unconditioned dharmas form merely one portion of the dharma-domain, only a portion of `dharmadhātu` is permanent.

```text
dharmadhātu
    conditioned portion      impermanent
    unconditioned portion    permanent
```

All remaining domains are impermanent.

This corrects any loose statement that “the dharma-domain is permanent.” The Bhāṣya permits only:

> A portion of the dharma-domain—the unconditioned dharmas—is permanent.

### 3. The twenty-two faculties are mapped into the eighteen domains

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya lists the twenty-two faculties taught in the sūtra:

```text
five sensory faculties
mind faculty
female faculty
male faculty
life faculty
five feeling faculties
five spiritual faculties
three faculties of higher knowledge
```

More explicitly:

```text
1  cakṣurindriya
2  śrotrendriya
3  ghrāṇendriya
4  jihvendriya
5  kāyendriya
6  manaindriya
7  strīndriya
8  puruṣendriya
9  jīvitendriya
10 sukhendriya
11 duḥkhendriya
12 saumanasyendriya
13 daurmanasyendriya
14 upekṣendriya
15 śraddhendriya
16 vīryendriya
17 smṛtīndriya
18 samādhīndriya
19 prajñendriya
20 anājñātamājñāsyāmīndriya
21 ājñendriya
22 ājñātāvīndriya
```

The Abhidharmikas place `manaindriya` immediately after `jīvitendriya`, following the organization of the six spheres.

The reason supplied is:

> `sālambanatvāt` — because it possesses an object-support.

This is especially important for our terminology. Here the text itself explicitly uses `sālambana` of mind-faculty. Mind belongs with the object-directed faculties because it has an `ālambana`.

### 4. What “half of the dharma-domain” means

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

`Dharmārdham` does not mean a mathematically exact half. It means a portion of the dharma-domain.

That portion includes:

```text
the eleven faculties beginning with jīvitendriya
plus portions of three further faculties
```

The reason is that these are portions of `dharmadhātu`.

The twelve internal domains contribute the remaining faculty-elements:

```text
five sensory faculty-domains
seven citta-domains as manaindriya
```

The seven citta-domains are:

```text
five sensory knowledge-domains
manodhātu
manovijñānadhātu
```

Together they constitute `manaindriya` under this classification.

The female and male faculties are portions of the body-domain; Vasubandhu postpones their detailed treatment.

The remaining five domains and the remaining portion of the dharma-domain are not faculties.

## Translation

### Close Translation

> The five external domains are knowable by two. The permanent dharmas are the unconditioned. A portion of the dharma-domain has faculty-status, together with those twelve remembered as internal.

### Bhāṣya-Informed Translation

> Each of the five external object-domains is known first by its corresponding sensory knowledge and again by mental knowledge. Of the eighteen domains, no complete domain is permanent; only the unconditioned dharmas, which form a portion of the dharma-domain, are permanent. Faculty-status belongs to a portion of the dharma-domain and to elements contained within the twelve internal domains.

---

## Argument Reconstruction

The closing argument can be reconstructed as four determinations.

### A. Object-domain and doubled knowability

```text
external object-domain
    ↓ sensory experience
corresponding sensory knowledge
    ↓ mental appropriation or determination
mental knowledge
```

The same object-domain can therefore enter two distinct knowledge-relations.

This strongly supports a distinction between:

```text
object as sensory viṣaya
object as mental ālambana
```

But the Bhāṣya does not say that mental knowledge merely copies sensory knowledge. It says the object-domain, after being experienced by sensory knowledge, is known by mental knowledge.

### B. Domain and permanence

```text
whole domain
    never permanent

unconditioned dharmas
    permanent

unconditioned dharmas
    portion of dharmadhātu
```

Permanence belongs to specific dharmas, not indiscriminately to the whole domain containing them.

### C. Faculty and object-support

The placement of `manaindriya` is justified by `sālambanatva`, possession of an object-support.

```text
manaindriya
    has ālambana
    therefore belongs among object-directed faculties
```

This is direct textual support for treating mind as representational or object-directed, provided “representational” is not imported as a complete foreign theory.

### D. Faculty-status cuts across domains

Faculty-status does not coincide one-to-one with domain-status.

```text
some whole domains are faculties
some portions of domains are faculties
some portions are not faculties
```

`Indriya` is therefore a functional classification imposed across the dhātu architecture, not simply another name for a complete domain.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks and answers which domains are known by which knowledge-domains.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — states that no whole domain is permanent; only unconditioned dharmas are permanent.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — lists the twenty-two faculties and maps them onto the eighteen domains.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — represented by the Abhidharmika ordering of `manaindriya` after `jīvitendriya`, justified by `sālambanatva`.

No explicit objector appears in this closing passage.

## New Technical Terms

- **dvi-vijñeya** — knowable by two knowledge-domains: the relevant sensory knowledge and mental knowledge.  
  **Status:** absent from the Kārikās before this closing classification.  
  **Function:** classification of object-domains by modes of knowability.

- **anubhūta** — experienced or undergone by sensory knowledge.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific verbal clarification.  
  **Function:** distinguishes sensory experience from mental knowing.

- **sakalo dhātuḥ** — a complete or entire domain.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya clarification.  
  **Function:** prevents attributing permanence to the whole dharma-domain.

- **dharmadhātvekadeśa** — one portion of the dharma-domain.  
  **Status:** previously encountered in substance, here made decisive.  
  **Function:** locates permanence and faculty-status in only part of `dharmadhātu`.

- **sālambanatva** — possession of an object-support.  
  **Status:** technically important Bhāṣya term; here explicitly applied to `manaindriya`.  
  **Function:** explains the Abhidharmika ordering of mind among object-directed faculties.

- **ṣaḍāyatanavyavasthāna** — arrangement according to the six spheres.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only organizational expression.  
  **Function:** explains the ordering of the faculties.

- **svanāmokta** — stated by its own name.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only.  
  **Function:** identifies the five sensory faculties directly with their corresponding internal domains.

## Translation Consequences

### 1. `Dvijñeya` should be translated “knowable by two,” not “twofold known”

The Bhāṣya identifies the two knowledge-domains precisely:

```text
corresponding sensory knowledge
mental knowledge
```

### 2. `Nityā dharmā asaṃskṛtāḥ` does not make the whole dharma-domain permanent

The safe translation must preserve the part-whole restriction:

> The unconditioned dharmas are permanent, and they constitute only a portion of the dharma-domain.

### 3. `Dharmārdham` means “a portion of the dharma-domain”

“Half” is too arithmetical unless heavily qualified. The Bhāṣya itself explains the expression through a distribution of faculty-elements across portions of domains.

### 4. `Sālambana` confirms object-directedness

We should consistently translate:

```text
ālambana
    object-support

sālambana
    possessing an object-support
```

This is firmer than speaking vaguely of “having an object.” The emphasis is on the support-relation by which mind or knowledge is directed toward what it knows.

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

Chapter 1 closes by crossing four architectures:

```text
dhātu
    domain classification

vijñāna
    modes of knowing

nitya / anitya
    permanence classification

indriya
    governing functional capacity
```

These architectures overlap but do not coincide.

The final pattern is:

```text
object-domain
    may support more than one knowledge-relation

domain
    may contain both conditioned and unconditioned dharmas

faculty-status
    may belong to a whole domain or only a portion

mind
    is explicitly sālambana
```

This is a disciplined basis for later speaking of the indriyas and mind as representational capacities. The Bhāṣya itself gives us object-directedness, differentiated supports, and doubled knowability. It does not yet give us a complete Sāṃkhya account of a sāttvic internal instrument.

---

## Light Organon Contact

The most important closing distinction is not internal versus external by itself, but the articulation of one object through more than one mode of knowing.

```text
sensory knowledge
    experiences the external object-domain

mental knowledge
    knows that same domain
```

This suggests a transition from presentation to determination without requiring us yet to identify the exact structure of representation.

A second important result is that functional classification cuts across ontological classification:

```text
domain
    says where a dharma belongs

faculty
    says how a dharma governs or exercises capacity
```

And the permanent is not a superior plane laid over the whole system. It is a restricted unconditioned portion within the dharma-domain.

## Closing Determination

The Dhātunirdeśa does not end with a static inventory. It ends with a system of crossings:

```text
external object
    sensory and mental knowability

dharmadhātu
    conditioned and unconditioned portions

internal domains
    faculty and knowledge structures

indriya
    functional governance across domains
```

The final verse therefore closes Chapter 1 by showing that the eighteen domains are not merely eighteen things. They are an articulated field in which object-support, support, knowledge, permanence, and faculty-status intersect without collapsing into one another.
