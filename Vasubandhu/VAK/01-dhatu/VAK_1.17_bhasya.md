# VAK_1.17 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> ṣaṇṇām anantarātītaṃ vijñānaṃ yad dhi tan manaḥ /
>
> ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhyarthaṃ dhātavo 'ṣṭādaśa smṛtāḥ // VAkK_1.17 //

### Working Translation

> Whatever cognition among the six has immediately passed, that is manas. The eighteen domains are taught in order to establish the sixth support.

The verse answers a problem created by VAK 1.16. The six vijñānas already constitute the vijñānaskandha. If manodhātu is then added as a seventh cognitive domain, is it a different substance? Vasubandhu answers no. Manodhātu is one of those same six vijñānas considered in a different temporal and functional role.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

> nanu ca ṣaḍ vijñānakāyā vijñānaskandha ity uktam / atha ko 'yaṃ punas tebhyo 'nyo manodhātuḥ / na khalu kaścid anyaḥ / kiṃ tarhi / teṣām eva ṣaṇṇām anantarātītaṃ vijñānaṃ yad dhi tan manaḥ /
>
> yad yat samanantaraniruddhaṃ vijñānaṃ tan manodhātur ity ucyate / tad yathā sa eva putro 'nyasya pitā bhavati tad eva phalam anyasya bījam iti /
>
> evaṃ tarhi dravyataḥ saptadaśa dhātavo bhavanti dvādaśa vā ṣaḍvijñānadhātumanodhātūnām itaretarāntarbhāvād iti kasmād aṣṭādaśa vyavasthāpyante / yady apy evaṃ tathāpi ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhyarthaṃ dhātavo 'ṣṭādaśa smṛtāḥ /
>
> pañcānāṃ vijñānadhātūnāṃ cakṣurdhātvāyatanādayaḥ pañcāśrayāḥ / ṣaṣṭhasya manovijñānadhātor āśrayo 'nyo nāsti / atas tadāśrayaprasiddhyarthaṃ manodhātur upadiṣṭaḥ /
>
> evam āśrayāśritālambanaṣaṭkavyavasthānād aṣṭādaśa dhātavo bhavantīti /
>
> arhatastarhi caramaṃ cittaṃ na mano bhaviṣyati / na hi tad asti yasya tat samanantarātītaṃ syād iti / na / tasyāpi manobhāvenāvasthitatvāt / anyakāraṇavaikalyāt tu nottaravijñānasaṃbhūtiḥ //

### Translation

**Objector:** But it was said that the six bodies of cognition are the aggregate of cognition. What, then, is this manodhātu that is supposedly different from them?

**Vasubandhu:** It is not in fact anything different.

**Objector:** What, then, is it?

**Vasubandhu:** Whatever cognition among those same six has just ceased immediately before is called manodhātu. Just as the same person is a son in relation to one person and a father in relation to another, and the same thing is a fruit in relation to one cause and a seed in relation to another.

**Objector:** In that case, substantially there would be only seventeen domains—or perhaps twelve—because the six vijñānadhātus and manodhātu are mutually included in one another. Why, then, are eighteen established?

**Vasubandhu:** Even so, the eighteen domains are taught in order to establish the sixth support. The five vijñānadhātus have the five supports beginning with the eye-domain. The sixth, manovijñānadhātu, has no other support. Therefore manodhātu is taught in order to establish its support.

Thus the eighteen domains are established as six supports, six dependents, and six objects.

**Objector:** Then the final citta of an arhat would not be manas, because there is no later cognition for which it could be immediately past.

**Vasubandhu:** No. It remains established in the nature of manas. A later cognition does not arise because another required cause is absent.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 Manodhātu is not a seventh cognitive substance

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The first point is explicitly anti-substantialist:

```text
manodhātu
    ≠ a cognition additional to the six vijñānas
```

It is one of the same six cognitions under a different relational determination:

```text
present cognition
    = vijñānadhātu

that same cognition immediately after ceasing
    = manodhātu
```

The distinction is therefore functional and temporal, not a multiplication of dravyas.

### 3.2 The son/father and fruit/seed analogies

The Bhāṣya gives two analogies:

```text
same person
    son relative to one
    father relative to another

same event
    fruit relative to a prior cause
    seed relative to a later effect
```

These analogies show how one and the same dharma can receive different names according to its relation.

Manas is therefore not a hidden inner organ added behind cognition. It is cognition itself when functioning as the immediately preceding support of another cognition.

### 3.3 Why retain eighteen dhātus?

**Voice:** Objector and Vasubandhu

The objector notices that if manodhātu and the six vijñānadhātus are not substantially different, the eighteenfold count appears inflated.

The answer is architectural rather than substantial:

```text
six supports
six supported cognitions
six object-domains
    = eighteen dhātus
```

The first five cognitions have visible sensory supports:

```text
cakṣurvijñāna → cakṣurdhātu
śrotravijñāna → śrotradhātu
...
```

Manovijñāna has no separate material support. Its support is the immediately past cognition, designated manodhātu.

The eighteenfold system is retained to make the sixth cognitive relation formally explicit.

### 3.4 Manas as temporal support

The crucial determination is:

```text
manas
    = samanantaraniruddha vijñāna
    = cognition ceased immediately before
```

This is stronger than saying merely that “mind is past cognition.” It is the immediately preceding cognition insofar as it has become support for the next cognitive event.

The knowledge-domain architecture is therefore serial:

```text
object-domain
    → present vijñāna
    → immediately past vijñāna as manas
    → subsequent vijñāna
```

### 3.5 The arhat's final cognition

**Voice:** Objector and Vasubandhu

The objection tests whether manas is defined only by the actual arising of a successor. If so, the final cognition of an arhat could not be manas, because no later cognition arises.

Vasubandhu rejects this consequence. The final cognition still has the nature of manas. The next cognition fails to arise because another causal requirement is missing, not because the final cognition lacks the capacity or status of mental support.

This distinguishes:

```text
being a support-condition
    ≠ guaranteeing the production of a successor
```

A causal role can remain properly determined even when the complete causal complex is absent.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
The six vijñānas constitute vijñānaskandha.

Manodhātu is not a seventh substance.

One of those same six vijñānas,
when it has just ceased,
is called manodhātu.

It is called manodhātu because it serves
as the sixth support of manovijñāna.

Therefore the eighteen domains are organized as:
    six supports
    six supported cognitions
    six object-domains.

The final cognition of an arhat still has the nature of manas,
even though no later cognition arises,
because another necessary cause is absent.
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### `anantarātīta`

- **Provisional meaning:** immediately past
- **Status:** temporal determination
- **Function:** identifies the cognition that has just ceased and now functions as manas

### `samanantaraniruddha`

- **Provisional meaning:** ceased immediately before, without an intervening cognition
- **Status:** causal-temporal technical term
- **Function:** specifies the exact status of vijñāna when designated manodhātu

### `manodhātu`

- **Provisional meaning:** mental domain; immediately past cognition functioning as support
- **Status:** one of the eighteen dhātus
- **Function:** provides the sixth support for manovijñāna

### `āśraya`

- **Provisional meaning:** support
- **Status:** relational-causal category
- **Function:** the basis upon which a corresponding cognition arises

### `āśrita`

- **Provisional meaning:** that which depends upon a support
- **Status:** relational category
- **Function:** the six vijñānas as supported cognitive events

### `ālambana`

- **Provisional meaning:** object-support; cognitive object-domain
- **Status:** relational category
- **Function:** that toward which the corresponding vijñāna is directed

### `anyakāraṇavaikalya`

- **Provisional meaning:** deficiency of another required cause
- **Status:** causal explanation
- **Function:** explains why the arhat's final cognition has no successor despite retaining the nature of manas

No position in this unit is explicitly marked as uniquely Vaibhāṣika. The eighteenfold architecture is presented as the operative Abhidharma account, while the dialogue exposes its relational rather than substantially additive character.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The current Kārikā translation is broadly correct, but the following formulation is more precise:

> Whatever cognition among the six has just ceased immediately before, that is manas. The eighteen domains are taught in order to establish the sixth support.

Recommended refinements for the later Kārikā revision pass:

1. Translate `anantarātīta` with “has just ceased immediately before,” because the Bhāṣya glosses it by `samanantaraniruddha`.
2. Keep `manas` rather than simply “mind” where the technical support-function is central.
3. Explain that the eighteenfold count is relational, not a claim to eighteen wholly distinct substances.
4. Preserve `āśraya` as “support,” since this is the decisive term in the argument.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The passage strongly supports a relational Knowledge Domain reading:

```text
support-domain
    + object-domain
    + knowledge-domain
    = one cognitive triad
```

The eighteen dhātus are six such triads.

The most important conceptual result is that manas is not a separate faculty-substance behind cognition. It is knowledge becoming the temporal support of knowledge:

```text
vijñāna
    as present apprehension

vijñāna
    as immediately past support
    = manas
```

This is a genuine architecture of cognitive succession. Yet the Bhāṣya remains paramārthically cautious: the same dravya receives different determinations according to relation. We should therefore avoid turning “Knowledge Domain” into an independent supersensible substance. The domain is the lawful relational position in which a dharma functions.
