# VAK_1.21 Bhāṣya Study

## Kārikā Anchor

> vivādamūlasaṃsārahetutvāt kramakāraṇāt /
> caittebhyo vedanāsaṃjñe pṛthak skandhau niveśitau // VAkK_1.21 //

The completed Kārikā analysis established the basic claim: feeling and recognition are separated from the remaining mental factors because they are principal causes of dispute and saṃsāra, and because this separation serves the ordered sequence of the aggregates.

The Bhāṣya now explains exactly how these causal roles operate.

## Natural Bhāṣya Unit

> kim punaḥ kāraṇaṃ caitasikā ekatra saṃskāraskandhanikṣiptā vedanāsaṃjñe tu pṛthak skandhīkṛte ityāha /
>
> vivādamūlasaṃsārahetutvāt kramakāraṇāt /
> caittebhyo vedanāsaṃjñe pṛthak skandhau niveśitau //
>
> dve vivādamūle / kāmādhyavasānaṃ dṛṣṭyadhyavasānaṃ ca /
>
> tayor vedanāsaṃjñe yathākramaṃ pradhānahetū /
>
> vedanāsvādavaśād dhi kāmān abhiṣvajante viparītasaṃjñāvaśāc ca dṛṣṭīr iti /
>
> saṃsārasyāpi te pradhānahetū /
>
> vedanāsvādagṛddho hi viparyastasaṃjñaḥ saṃsarati /
>
> yac ca skandhakrame kāraṇam upadekṣyamāṇaṃ tato 'pi kramakāraṇād anayor pṛthakskandhīkaraṇaṃ veditavyam /
>
> etac ca tredhopapādayiṣyāmaḥ /

### Translation

Why, then, are the mental factors placed together in the aggregate of formations, while feeling and recognition are made into separate aggregates? He states:

> Because they are roots of dispute and causes of saṃsāra, and because of the reason for the sequence, feeling and recognition are established as separate aggregates from the other mental factors.

There are two roots of dispute: fixation upon sensual desire and fixation upon views. Feeling and recognition are, respectively, their principal causes. For through delight in feeling beings cling to sensual desires, and through distorted recognition they cling to views.

They are likewise principal causes of saṃsāra. One who is greedy for the savor of feeling and whose recognition is inverted continues to wander in saṃsāra.

Moreover, because of the reason for the sequence of the aggregates that will be explained later, their establishment as separate aggregates should also be understood as resting upon the reason of sequence. This will be demonstrated in three ways.

## Bhāṣya Analysis

### 1. The question concerns unequal treatment among mental factors

**Voice:** Objector or pedagogical questioner

> kim punaḥ kāraṇaṃ caitasikā ekatra saṃskāraskandhanikṣiptā vedanāsaṃjñe tu pṛthak skandhīkṛte

The issue is not whether vedanā and saṃjñā are mental factors. They are. The question is why most mental factors are grouped together under saṃskāraskandha, while these two receive independent aggregate status.

The Bhāṣya therefore treats the five-skandha scheme as selective and purposive rather than as a flat inventory.

### 2. There are two roots of dispute

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> dve vivādamūle / kāmādhyavasānaṃ dṛṣṭyadhyavasānaṃ ca /

The two are:

```text
kāmādhyavasāna
    fixation upon sensual desire

dṛṣṭyadhyavasāna
    fixation upon views
```

The important term is **adhyavasāna**. It is stronger than simple interest or cognition. It indicates settling upon, fastening onto, or becoming decisively committed to something.

Thus dispute does not arise merely because pleasure and views occur. It arises because the mind becomes fixed upon them.

### 3. Vedanā and saṃjñā are principal causes in a correlated pair

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> tayor vedanāsaṃjñe yathākramaṃ pradhānahetū

The correlation is ordered:

```text
vedanā
    → kāmādhyavasāna

saṃjñā
    → dṛṣṭyadhyavasāna
```

Vedanā is the principal cause of sensual fixation because delight in feeling produces attachment to desirable objects.

Saṃjñā is the principal cause of fixation upon views because distorted recognition supplies the marks and determinations under which false views become compelling.

The Bhāṣya is not claiming that feeling alone produces desire or that recognition alone produces doctrine. It is identifying their principal causal role within larger conditioned complexes.

### 4. The mechanism of sensual fixation

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> vedanāsvādavaśād dhi kāmān abhiṣvajante

The term **vedanāsvāda** means the savor, relish, or enjoyed taste of feeling.

The sequence is:

```text
feeling
    → savoring of feeling
    → clinging to sensual objects
```

The decisive movement is not bare feeling but enjoyment of its savor. Feeling becomes a basis of bondage when it is relished and appropriated.

### 5. The mechanism of fixation upon views

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> viparītasaṃjñāvaśāc ca dṛṣṭīḥ

Here saṃjñā is qualified as **viparīta**, inverted or distorted.

The sequence is:

```text
distorted recognition
    → wrongly grasped marks
    → attachment to views
```

Because saṃjñā grasps marks, distortion at this level shapes how an object, self, or doctrine is identified. Fixation upon views therefore depends upon a prior misrecognition of what things are.

This gives saṃjñā a deeper role than simple naming. It stabilizes the determinate appearance upon which views are built.

### 6. The same two factors are principal causes of saṃsāra

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> saṃsārasyāpi te pradhānahetū /
> vedanāsvādagṛddho hi viparyastasaṃjñaḥ saṃsarati /

The person who wanders in saṃsāra is described through two characteristics:

```text
vedanāsvādagṛddha
    greedy for the savor of feeling

viparyastasaṃjña
    possessed of inverted recognition
```

The first sustains craving. The second sustains error. Together they reproduce practical attachment and cognitive distortion.

The Bhāṣya therefore links the five-skandha analysis directly to the causal architecture of bondage.

### 7. The third reason is structural sequence

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> yac ca skandhakrame kāraṇam upadekṣyamāṇaṃ ... kramakāraṇāt

The text postpones the full explanation. The separation of vedanā and saṃjñā is also necessary because the order of the aggregates has its own rationale.

VAK 1.22 will explain this ordering through several principles. Thus VAK 1.21 is both a causal analysis and a transition to the doctrine of sequence.

## Argument Summary

```text
Question:
Why are feeling and recognition separate aggregates,
while other mental factors are grouped as formations?

Answer:
1. They are the two principal roots of dispute.
2. They are the two principal causes of saṃsāra.
3. Their separation is required by the ordered sequence of the aggregates.
```

The first two reasons are correlated:

```text
vedanā
    → savor of feeling
    → fixation upon sensual desire
    → continued saṃsāra

saṃjñā
    → distorted recognition
    → fixation upon views
    → continued saṃsāra
```

## New Technical Terms

### adhyavasāna

- **Provisional meaning:** fixation, decisive settling upon, committed attachment
- **Status:** Bhāṣya technical term
- **Function:** Explains how desire and views become stabilized roots of dispute.

### kāmādhyavasāna

- **Provisional meaning:** fixation upon sensual desire
- **Status:** Bhāṣya causal category
- **Function:** One of the two roots of dispute; principally caused by vedanā.

### dṛṣṭyadhyavasāna

- **Provisional meaning:** fixation upon views
- **Status:** Bhāṣya causal category
- **Function:** One of the two roots of dispute; principally caused by saṃjñā.

### vedanāsvāda

- **Provisional meaning:** savor or relish of feeling
- **Status:** Bhāṣya causal term
- **Function:** Mediates the movement from feeling to sensual attachment.

### abhiṣvaj

- **Provisional meaning:** to cling to, embrace, attach oneself to
- **Status:** Verbal description of appropriation
- **Function:** Describes the attachment produced through delight in feeling.

### viparītasaṃjñā / viparyastasaṃjñā

- **Provisional meaning:** inverted or distorted recognition
- **Status:** Bhāṣya epistemic-causal term
- **Function:** Explains how the grasping of marks becomes the basis of attachment to false views.

### pradhānahetu

- **Provisional meaning:** principal cause
- **Status:** Causal qualification
- **Function:** Marks vedanā and saṃjñā as dominant causes without making them exclusive causes.

## Kārikā Translation Decision

**Decision:** Refined.

The earlier translation is substantially confirmed, but several terms require sharper rendering:

```text
vivādamūla
    = root of dispute

saṃsārahetu
    = principal causal basis of continued wandering

kramakāraṇa
    = reason grounded in the ordered sequence of the aggregates
```

Recommended translation:

> Because they are roots of dispute and principal causes of saṃsāra, and because of the reason for the sequence, feeling and recognition are established as aggregates separate from the other mental factors.

The word **principal** is supplied from the Bhāṣya's repeated `pradhānahetu` and should be understood as explanatory rather than as a literal addition to every compound in the verse.

## Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya shows how a dharma functions as more than a static item in a list. Vedanā and saṃjñā are identified by their characteristic activity and by the factual series they help generate:

```text
vedanā
    → savoring
    → attachment
    → renewed saṃsāra

saṃjñā
    → mark-grasping
    → distortion
    → fixation upon views
    → renewed saṃsāra
```

This is compatible with the project speculation that a Dharma is a principial determination that constructs or reproduces itself within a factual field. Here, however, the Bhāṣya speaks in conditioned causal terms. It does not identify vedanā or saṃjñā as paramārthika principles independent of their occurrences.

The disciplined conclusion is therefore:

```text
Bhāṣya result:
    dharmas are functionally and causally generative within experience

Organon speculation:
    their stable causal form may be interpreted as principial determination
```

The second claim remains our reconstruction, not Vasubandhu's explicit voice.
