# VAK_1.32 Bhāṣya

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> savitarkavicārā hi pañca vijñānadhātavaḥ /
>
> antyās trayas triprakārāḥ śeṣā ubhayavarjitāḥ // VAkK_1.32 //

### Translation

> The five sensory cognition-domains are invariably accompanied by vitarka and vicāra. The final three occur in three modes; the remaining ten are always without either.

The particle **hi** is restrictive or emphatic here: the five sensory cognition-domains are **always** conjoined with vitarka and vicāra.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question:

> kati savitarkāḥ savicārāḥ katy avitarkā vicāramātrāḥ katy avitarkā avicārāḥ /

> How many are accompanied by vitarka and vicāra? How many are without vitarka but accompanied by vicāra alone? How many are without both vitarka and vicāra?

It continues through the explanation of the five sensory cognition-domains, the threefold status of the final three domains, the special cases of vitarka and vicāra themselves, and the conclusion that the ten material domains are always without both.

### Sanskrit Bhāṣya

> savitarkavicārā hi pañca vijñānadhātavaḥ /
>
> nityam ete vitarkavicārābhyāṃ saṃprayuktāḥ / avadhāraṇārtho hiśabdaḥ /
>
> antyās trayas triprakārāḥ /
>
> manodhātur dharmadhātur manovijñānadhātuś cāntyāḥ / ete trayas triprakārāḥ /
>
> tatra manodhātur manovijñānadhātuḥ saṃprayuktaś ca dharmadhātur anyatra vitarkavicārābhyāṃ kāmadhātau prathame ca dhyāne savitarkāḥ savicārāḥ /
>
> dhyānāntare 'vitarkā vicāramātrāḥ /
>
> dvitīyād dhyānāt prabhṛty ābhavāgrād avitarkā avicārāḥ /
>
> sarvaś cāsaṃprayukto dharmadhātur dhyānāntare ca vicāraḥ /
>
> vitarkas tu nityam avitarko vicāramātro dvitīyavitarkābhāvād vicārasaṃprayogāc ca /
>
> kāmadhātau prathame dhyāne vicāra eṣu triprakāreṣu nāntarbhavati /
>
> sa kathaṃ vaktavyaḥ /
>
> avicāro vitarkamātraḥ / dvitīyavicārābhāvād vitarkasaṃprayogāc ca /
>
> ata evocyate: syuḥ savitarkasavicārāyāṃ bhūmau dharmāś catuḥprakārāḥ /
>
> savitarkāḥ savicārā vicāravitarkavarjyāḥ saṃprayuktāḥ /
>
> avitarko vicāramātro vitarkaḥ /
>
> avitarkā avicārā asaṃprayuktāḥ /
>
> avicāro vitarkamātro vicāra iti /
>
> śeṣā ubhayavarjitāḥ /
>
> daśa rūpiṇo dhātavaḥ śeṣā nityam avitarkā avicārā asaṃprayogitvāt //

### Translation

> The five sensory cognition-domains are accompanied by vitarka and vicāra. They are always conjoined with vitarka and vicāra; the word *hi* has the force of restriction.
>
> The last three are threefold. The mind-domain, the dharma-domain, and the mental-cognition-domain are the final three, and these three occur in three modes.
>
> In the desire realm and in the first dhyāna, the mind-domain, the mental-cognition-domain, and the associated part of the dharma-domain—apart from vitarka and vicāra themselves—are accompanied by both vitarka and vicāra.
>
> In the intermediate dhyāna they are without vitarka and accompanied by vicāra alone.
>
> From the second dhyāna up through the summit of existence, they are without both vitarka and vicāra.
>
> Every unassociated member of the dharma-domain is also without both. Vicāra in the intermediate dhyāna is likewise without both.
>
> Vitarka itself, however, is always without vitarka and accompanied by vicāra alone, because there is no second vitarka and because it is conjoined with vicāra.
>
> Vicāra in the desire realm and first dhyāna does not fall within those three modes. How should it be described? It is without vicāra and accompanied by vitarka alone, because there is no second vicāra and because it is conjoined with vitarka.
>
> For this very reason it is said that, on a level accompanied by vitarka and vicāra, dharmas may occur in four modes: associated dharmas other than vitarka and vicāra are accompanied by both; vitarka is without vitarka and accompanied by vicāra alone; unassociated dharmas are without both; and vicāra is without vicāra and accompanied by vitarka alone.
>
> The remaining ten material domains are always without both vitarka and vicāra because they are incapable of association.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 The five sensory cognition-domains

The five are:

- eye-cognition-domain
- ear-cognition-domain
- nose-cognition-domain
- tongue-cognition-domain
- body-cognition-domain

The Bhāṣya states that they are **always** associated with both vitarka and vicāra. This is not presented as an occasional property.

The important term is:

> **saṃprayukta** — associated, conjoined

Vitarka and vicāra are mental factors associated with these sensory cognitions. The passage does not identify sensory cognition with vitarka or vicāra.

### 3.2 The final three domains

The final three are:

1. **manodhātu** — mind-domain
2. **dharmadhātu** — dharma-domain
3. **manovijñānadhātu** — mental-cognition-domain

They are threefold according to level:

| Level | Relation to vitarka and vicāra |
|---|---|
| Desire realm and first dhyāna | with both |
| Intermediate dhyāna | without vitarka, with vicāra only |
| Second dhyāna through the summit of existence | without both |

This threefold statement applies most directly to mind, mental cognition, and the **associated** portion of dharmadhātu.

### 3.3 The unassociated dharma-domain

The Bhāṣya separately states:

> sarvaś cāsaṃprayukto dharmadhātuḥ ... avitarko avicāraḥ

Every unassociated member of dharmadhātu is without both vitarka and vicāra.

This prevents a loose reading in which the entire dharmadhātu automatically takes on the mode of the mental level. Only associated dharmas can be conjoined with these mental factors.

### 3.4 Why vitarka cannot possess a second vitarka

Vitarka itself is classified as:

> **avitarka-vicāramātra** — without vitarka, accompanied by vicāra alone

The reason is structural:

- there is no second vitarka associated with the first;
- vitarka is associated with vicāra.

Thus a mental factor does not count itself as being associated with itself.

### 3.5 Why vicāra requires a fourth mode

Vicāra in the desire realm and first dhyāna is:

> **avicāra-vitarkamātra** — without vicāra, accompanied by vitarka alone

This does not fit the three modes listed in the Kārikā. Therefore the Bhāṣya introduces a fuller fourfold classification for dharmas on a level where both vitarka and vicāra operate:

1. with vitarka and vicāra;
2. without vitarka, with vicāra alone;
3. without both;
4. without vicāra, with vitarka alone.

The Kārikā's threefold classification remains correct for the final three domains considered generally, but the internal factors vitarka and vicāra themselves require this finer analysis.

### 3.6 The remaining ten

The remaining ten are the five material faculties and five material objects.

They are always without vitarka and vicāra because:

> **asaṃprayogitvāt** — because they are incapable of mental association

This is stronger than saying they merely happen not to possess these factors. Their category excludes such association.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Five sensory cognition-domains
    → always associated with vitarka and vicāra

Final three domains
    → threefold according to meditative level:
        both
        vicāra only
        neither

Unassociated dharmadhātu
    → neither

Vitarka itself
    → vicāra only

Vicāra itself, where both function
    → vitarka only

Remaining ten material domains
    → neither, because association is impossible
```

The argument is therefore not merely about meditative stages. It is also about the formal conditions of **association** and about why a factor cannot be associated with another instance of itself.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### saṃprayukta

- **Provisional meaning:** associated; conjoined with cognition and other mental factors
- **Status:** explicit Bhāṣya terminology
- **Function:** distinguishes mental factors capable of occurring together from material and unassociated dharmas

### asaṃprayukta

- **Provisional meaning:** unassociated; not conjoined as a mental factor
- **Status:** explicit Bhāṣya terminology
- **Function:** marks the part of dharmadhātu that cannot bear vitarka-vicāra association

### savitarka-savicāra

- **Provisional meaning:** accompanied by both vitarka and vicāra
- **Status:** doctrinal classification
- **Function:** characterizes sensory cognition and the lower-level associated mental triad

### avitarka-vicāramātra

- **Provisional meaning:** without vitarka, accompanied by vicāra alone
- **Status:** doctrinal classification
- **Function:** characterizes the intermediate dhyāna and vitarka itself

### avitarka-avicāra

- **Provisional meaning:** without both vitarka and vicāra
- **Status:** doctrinal classification
- **Function:** characterizes higher levels, unassociated dharmas, and material domains

### avicāra-vitarkamātra

- **Provisional meaning:** without vicāra, accompanied by vitarka alone
- **Status:** Bhāṣya refinement beyond the Kārikā's threefold formula
- **Function:** characterizes vicāra itself in levels where it is associated with vitarka

### dhyānāntara

- **Provisional meaning:** intermediate dhyāna between the first and second dhyānas
- **Status:** established meditative technical term
- **Function:** level where vitarka has ceased but vicāra remains

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The existing Kārikā translation correctly identifies the three main modes, but it should be refined in two respects.

First, **hi** is restrictive:

> The five sensory cognition-domains are **invariably** accompanied by vitarka and vicāra.

Second, `triprakārāḥ` should not conceal the special treatment of vitarka and vicāra themselves. The root verse gives the general threefold organization; the Bhāṣya supplements it with a fourfold analysis on levels where both factors operate.

Recommended translation:

> The five sensory cognition-domains are invariably accompanied by vitarka and vicāra. The final three occur in three modes; the remaining ten are always without either.

No change is made to the Kārikā file during this Bhāṣya pass.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

This passage should first be read as a precise Abhidharma analysis of mental association and meditative levels.

Only provisionally, it supports a distinction between:

```text
field or support
    ≠
operative cognitive articulation
```

The ten material domains are not themselves loci of vitarka and vicāra. The five sensory cognitions and the final mental triad are. This suggests that the field may be given materially while its articulation occurs through associated cognitive factors.

That is compatible with the project's Projection/Factory hypothesis, but the Bhāṣya itself does not formulate an idealist theory of projection here. It classifies which dharmas can be associated with which mental operations and at which meditative levels.
