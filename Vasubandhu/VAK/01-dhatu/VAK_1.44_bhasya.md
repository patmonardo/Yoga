# VAK_1.44_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> tribhir ghrāṇādibhis tulyaviṣayagrahaṇaṃ matam /
>
> caramasyāśrayo 'tītaḥ pañcānāṃ sahajaś ca taiḥ // VAkK_1.44 //

### Established Kārikā Translation

> By the three beginning with the nose, apprehension of an equal object is accepted. The support of the last is past; for the five, the support is co-born with them.

The corresponding Kārikā analysis is preserved in `VAK_1.44.md`.

>
> manovijñānadhātoḥ samanantaraniruddhaṃ mana āśrayaḥ /

### Why the Faculty Is Called the Support

> kiṃ punaḥ kāraṇam ubhayādhīnāyāṃ vijñānotpattau cakṣurādaya eva āśrayā ucyante na rūpādayaḥ /
>
> tadvikāravikāritvād āśrayāś cakṣurādayaḥ /
>
> cakṣurādīnāṃ hi vikāreṇa tadvijñānānāṃ vikāro bhavaty anugrahopaghātapaṭumandatānuvidhānāt na tu rūpādīnāṃ vikāreṇa tadvikāraḥ /
>
> tasmāt sādhīyas tadadhīnatvāt ta evāśrayā na rūpādayaḥ /

Pradhan reference: `[034|17–034|22]`

The final passage naturally prepares VAK 1.45. It is included because it gives the Bhāṣya’s explicit criterion for `āśraya`, without which the distinction from `ālambana` remains incomplete.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. Does a faculty apprehend an object equal to its own extent?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Do the eye and the other faculties apprehend an object equal in extent to themselves, with mountains and similar things being grasped only through rapid succession as in the appearance of a firebrand-circle? Or do they apprehend objects both equal and unequal in extent?

The question makes magnitude explicit. It asks whether object-apprehension is constrained by a one-to-one equality between the extent of a faculty and the extent of its object.

### 2. The three contact faculties apprehend an equal object

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position reported by Vasubandhu**

> For those faculties already said to have contact-objects—the nose, tongue, and body—apprehension of an equal object is accepted.

The Bhāṣya explains:

> As many faculty-atoms as there are, so many object-atoms come together and generate knowledge.

The equality is therefore not an abstract resemblance. It is a quantitative conjunction:

```text
number of faculty-atoms
    =
number of object-atoms participating in contact
```

This is the clearest textual support so far for treating the determinate capacity of an indriya quantitatively.

### 3. Eye and ear have no fixed equality rule

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> With eye and ear, however, there is no fixed rule.

The eye may apprehend:

```text
an object smaller than the faculty-field
    a hair-tip

an object approximately equal
    a grape

an object much larger
    a great mountain
```

The ear likewise apprehends sounds of very different magnitudes, from a mosquito to thunderclouds.

Thus non-contact faculties are representationally bounded, but their object-field is not measured through equal atomic contact.

### 4. Mind is not measurable in this way

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Since mind is non-material, no delimitation of its magnitude is considered.

This does not make manas unlimited in every respect. It only excludes material spatial magnitude as the relevant measure of its object-relation.

### 5. The support of mental knowledge is past

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The support of mental knowledge is the mind that has ceased immediately before it.

Here `āśraya` is explicit. The immediately preceding cognition, once ceased, becomes `manodhātu` and serves as the support for the succeeding `manovijñāna`.

```text
immediately preceding cognition
    ceases
    ↓
becomes manodhātu
    ↓
serves as āśraya
    ↓
next manovijñāna arises
```

Mental knowledge is therefore temporally mediated by the immediately past mental event.

### 6. The five sensory knowledges have co-born faculty-supports

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> For eye-knowledge, the eye is a co-born support; and so on through body-knowledge, whose co-born support is the body-faculty.

The particle `ca` also adds a past support. Each of the five sensory knowledge-bodies therefore has two supports:

```text
present, co-born support
    its own sensory faculty

past support
    immediately preceding manas
```

The sensory knowledge-event is thus not generated by the faculty alone. It stands at the intersection of a present faculty-support and a past mental support.

### 7. Why is the faculty called `āśraya`, but not the object?

**Voice: Objector**

> Since the arising of knowledge depends upon both faculty and object, why are the eye and the other faculties called supports, but visible form and the other objects are not?

### 8. Transformation of the support transforms the knowledge

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The eye and the other faculties are supports because their knowledge is transformed when they are transformed.

The Bhāṣya gives the criterion:

```text
change in faculty
    → corresponding change in knowledge

improvement or injury
sharpness or dullness
    → knowledge follows that condition
```

By contrast, alteration in the object does not transform the cognitive event in this same support-dependent manner.

Therefore:

> Because knowledge depends more decisively upon the faculty, the faculty is called its support, not the object.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Question 1:
Is object-apprehension quantitatively equal to the faculty?

Contact faculties:
Nose, tongue, and body apprehend an equal contact-object.

Meaning of equality:
Equal numbers of faculty-atoms and object-atoms conjoin to generate knowledge.

Non-contact faculties:
Eye and ear apprehend smaller, equal, or larger objects.
Their representational field is not governed by atomic equality.

Mind:
No spatial magnitude is assigned because it is non-material.

Question 2:
What supports knowledge?

Mental knowledge:
The immediately past manodhātu is its support.

Five sensory knowledges:
Each has a co-born sensory faculty as support,
and immediately past manas as an additional support.

Question 3:
Why is faculty called support rather than object?

Criterion:
Transformation of the faculty transforms the knowledge.

Conclusion:
āśraya is the determining basis whose condition is followed by the cognitive event.
The object is indispensable, but it is not support in this technical sense.
```

## Translation Consequences

### `āśraya`

The term is unquestionably used in the Kośa and Bhāṣya. In this passage it has a precise technical meaning:

```text
āśraya
    = support
    = determining basis whose condition governs the knowledge-event
```

It should not be translated as `object-support`. That rendering belongs to `ālambana`.

### `ālambana`

```text
ālambana
    = object-support
    = that upon which cognition takes its object-orientation
```

### `viṣaya`

```text
viṣaya
    = determinate object-field
    = the specific kind and range of object apprehended
```

### `āśraya` versus `ālambana`

```text
āśraya
    answers: on what determining basis does this knowledge arise?

ālambana
    answers: upon what object-support is this knowledge directed?
```

The same knowledge-event can therefore require both without the terms being interchangeable.

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> Nose, tongue, and body apprehend contact-objects quantitatively equal to their operative faculty-atoms. Eye and ear have no fixed equality of magnitude, while mind is not spatially delimited. Mental knowledge rests upon immediately past mind; each sensory knowledge rests upon its co-born faculty and upon immediately past mind. A faculty is called the support because alteration in the faculty is followed by alteration in its knowledge.

## Doctrinal Determination

This unit gives the strongest Bhāṣya-based account yet of faculty-capacity.

For contact faculties:

```text
capacity is quantitatively coordinated
with an equal contacted object-field
```

For eye and ear:

```text
capacity remains determinate
but not through equality of spatial magnitude
```

The examples of hair-tip, grape, mountain, mosquito, and thunder establish variable representational scale.

The Bhāṣya also grounds graded capacity directly:

```text
faculty strengthened or injured
faculty sharp or dull
    ↓
knowledge correspondingly sharp or dull
```

This supports a disciplined account of the indriya as a bounded and variable capacity without importing a Sāṃkhya ontology.

---

## Logical Determination

The cognitive event now has three irreducible relations:

```text
āśraya
    determining support

ālambana
    object-support

viṣaya
    determinate object-field
```

For sensory knowledge:

```text
past manas
    temporal support

present indriya
    co-born determining support

object-domain
    object-support and determinate viṣaya

vijñāna
    resulting knowledge-event
```

This prevents four collapses:

```text
support ≠ object
faculty ≠ knowledge
object-support ≠ object-field
representational capacity ≠ unrestricted power
```

The indriya is representational because its condition determines how the object-field can appear in knowledge. Yet the Bhāṣya explains this through dependence and transformation, not through a doctrine of an internal image-copy.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The central result is a differentiated architecture of representation:

```text
past mental continuity
    ↓
present faculty-capacity
    ↓
object-field
    ↓
knowledge-event
```

Objectivity is quantitative in at least two Bhāṣya-supported senses:

1. Contact apprehension may require numerical equality between faculty-atoms and object-atoms.
2. Faculty sharpness and dullness grade the resulting knowledge.

For eye and ear, however, objective magnitude is not simply copied by equal magnitude in the faculty. Representation preserves determinate object-relation while freeing it from one-to-one spatial equality.

This gives us a careful formulation:

> The faculty does not contain the object as a material duplicate. It contains the formal capacity for a determinate `viṣaya`, and changes in that capacity transform the resulting knowledge.

Sāṃkhya may later help compare this with the sāttvic character of the internal instruments, but the Bhāṣya itself now supplies the governing distinctions.
