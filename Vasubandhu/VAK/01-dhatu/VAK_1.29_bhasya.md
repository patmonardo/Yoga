# VAK_1.29 Bhāṣya

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sanidarśana eko 'tra rūpaṃ sapratighā daśa /
>
> rūpiṇo 'vyākṛtā aṣṭau ta evārūpaśabdakāḥ // VAkK_1.29 //

### Translation

> Among these, one is visible: the form-domain. Ten are resistant, namely the material domains. Eight are ethically indeterminate: those same ten, excluding form and sound.

The Bhāṣya resolves the compact final compound `arūpaśabdakāḥ` as:

```text
those ten material domains
    minus rūpa
    minus śabda
    = eight indeterminate domains
```

It does **not** mean “non-material domains.”

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question of visibility among the eighteen dhātus and continues through resistance and ethical status:

> ye punar ime 'ṣṭādaśa dhātava uktās teṣāṃ kati sanidarśanāḥ katy anidarśanāḥ /
>
> sanidarśana eko 'tra rūpam /
>
> sa hi śakyate nidarśayitum idam ihāmutreti /
>
> uktaṃ bhavati anidarśanāḥ śeṣā iti /

It then asks which are resistant:

> kati sapratighāḥ katy apratighāḥ /
>
> sapratighā daśa /
>
> rūpiṇaḥ /

The Bhāṣya identifies three forms of resistance:

> pratigho nāma pratighātaḥ sa ca trividhaḥ /
>
> āvaraṇaviṣayālambanapratighātaḥ /

Finally it turns to ethical determination:

> eṣām aṣṭādaśadhātūnāṃ kati kuśalāḥ katy akuśalāḥ katy avyākṛtāḥ /
>
> avyākṛtā aṣṭau /
>
> katame aṣṭau /
>
> ya ete sapratighā daśoktāḥ /
>
> ta evārūpaśabdakāḥ /
>
> pañcendriyāṇi gandharasaspraṣṭavyadhātavaś ca /

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 One dhātu is visible

Only the form-domain (`rūpadhātu`) is `sanidarśana`.

The Bhāṣya explains:

> sa hi śakyate nidarśayitum idam ihāmutreti

### Translation

> For it can be pointed out: “this is here” or “that is there.”

Thus `nidarśana` concerns demonstrability through visible location. The remaining seventeen dhātus are `anidarśana`.

This does not mean that only one dhātu can be known. It means that only visible form can be directly indicated through spatial display.

### 3.2 Ten dhātus are resistant

The ten material dhātus are:

```text
five sense faculties
+ five sensory object-domains
```

These are `rūpiṇaḥ`, material, and `sapratighāḥ`, resistant.

The Bhāṣya defines `pratigha` as `pratighāta`, resistance or impediment, and distinguishes three types.

### 3.3 Āvaraṇapratighāta — resistance by obstruction

> svadeśe parasyotpattipratibandhaḥ

### Translation

> Obstructive resistance is the prevention of another thing's arising in one's own place.

The examples are bodily and material:

```text
hand against hand
hand against stone
stone against hand or stone
```

This is the sense relevant when the Kārikā says that the ten material dhātus are resistant. They mutually exclude one another from occupying the same place.

### 3.4 Viṣayapratighāta — limitation by object-range

The faculties are also said to meet resistance relative to their appropriate object-fields.

The Bhāṣya gives examples of faculties whose operation is restricted by environment, time, or species:

```text
vision effective in water but not on land
vision effective on land but not in water
vision effective at night but not by day
vision effective by day but not at night
```

The point is not merely physical collision. A faculty's activity is bounded by the field within which it can function.

The Bhāṣya defines:

> yasmin yasya kāritraṃ sa tasya viṣayaḥ

### Translation

> That within which something has its activity is its object-field.

This gives `viṣaya` a precise functional meaning: the domain in which a faculty exercises its causal activity.

### 3.5 Ālambanapratighāta — restriction by cognitive object

The third form concerns mind and mental factors:

> yaccittacaittair gṛhyate tad ālambanam

### Translation

> That which is apprehended by mind and mental factors is their cognitive object.

The distinction is therefore:

```text
viṣaya
    = field in which a faculty performs its activity

ālambana
    = object apprehended by mind and mental factors
```

The two overlap but are not identical.

### 3.6 Why the ten are called resistant

Although the Bhāṣya develops all three senses of resistance, it states that the ten material dhātus are called `sapratigha` here specifically because of `āvaraṇapratighāta`:

> tad ihāvaraṇapratighātena daśānāṃ sapratighatvaṃ veditavyam anyonyāvaraṇāt

### Translation

> Here the resistance of the ten should be understood as resistance by obstruction, because they obstruct one another.

This prevents a category mistake. The Kārikā's ten resistant dhātus are not defined through cognitive limitation but through material exclusion.

### 3.7 The four-cornered analysis

The Bhāṣya then tests relations among the different senses of resistance through a `catuṣkoṭika`.

Relative to object-field resistance and obstructive resistance:

1. Object-field resistance but not obstructive resistance:
   - the seven mind or consciousness dhātus
   - the associated portion of dharmadhātu
2. Obstructive resistance but not object-field resistance:
   - the five sensory object-domains
3. Both:
   - the five sense faculties
4. Neither:
   - the unassociated remainder of dharmadhātu

This demonstrates that `pratigha` is not univocal. Its extension changes according to the type of limitation under discussion.

### 3.8 Kumāralāta's alternative formulation

The Bhāṣya cites Bhadanta Kumāralāta:

> yatrotpitsor manasaḥ pratighātaḥ śakyate paraiḥ kartum /
>
> tat sapratighaṃ jñeyaṃ viparyayād apratigham iṣṭam iti

### Translation

> That in relation to which others can impede a mind intending to arise should be known as resistant; the opposite is accepted as non-resistant.

This is a cited alternative formulation, not automatically Vasubandhu's own final definition.

### 3.9 Eight are ethically indeterminate

The Bhāṣya then asks which dhātus are wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate.

Eight are `avyākṛta`:

```text
five sense faculties
smell
taste
tangible object
```

These are the ten material resistant domains with visible form and sound removed.

The reason given is:

> ete 'ṣṭau kuśalākuśalabhāvenāvyākaraṇād avyākṛtāḥ

### Translation

> These eight are indeterminate because they are not determined as wholesome or unwholesome.

An alternative says they are indeterminate because they are not specified with regard to karmic result. Vasubandhu immediately notes that this alternative would wrongly extend the category to the outflow-free as well. The alternative is therefore presented critically.

### 3.10 Why form and sound are excluded

Visible form and sound can be produced by wholesome or unwholesome mind through bodily and verbal action. They therefore do not belong among the eight invariably indeterminate material domains.

The eight remaining material dhātus have no intrinsic wholesome or unwholesome status.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
eighteen dhātus
    ↓ classified by visibility
one visible:
    rūpa

    ↓ classified by material resistance
ten resistant:
    five faculties
    five sensory objects

    ↓ resistance disambiguated
obstruction
object-field limitation
cognitive-object limitation

    ↓ classified ethically
eight indeterminate:
    five faculties
    smell, taste, tangible

rūpa and sound excluded
because they can arise from wholesome or unwholesome mind
```

The Bhāṣya is constructing a multidimensional classification. Visibility, materiality, resistance, object-range, cognitive apprehension, and ethical status are independent axes and must not be conflated.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### sanidarśana

- **Provisional meaning:** visible; spatially demonstrable
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition
- **Function:** marks the one dhātu that can be directly pointed out as here or there

### anidarśana

- **Provisional meaning:** non-visible; not spatially demonstrable
- **Status:** implied complement
- **Function:** applies to the remaining seventeen dhātus

### pratigha / pratighāta

- **Provisional meaning:** resistance, impediment, limitation
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition
- **Function:** general heading divided into three distinct forms

### āvaraṇapratighāta

- **Provisional meaning:** obstructive resistance
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition
- **Function:** prevention of another material thing's arising in the same place

### viṣayapratighāta

- **Provisional meaning:** limitation by object-field
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition
- **Function:** bounded operation of a faculty within its appropriate range

### ālambanapratighāta

- **Provisional meaning:** limitation relative to a cognitive object
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition
- **Function:** determination of mind and mental factors by what they apprehend

### viṣaya

- **Provisional meaning:** functional object-field
- **Status:** explicitly defined
- **Function:** that within which a faculty exercises its activity

### ālambana

- **Provisional meaning:** apprehended cognitive object
- **Status:** explicitly defined
- **Function:** that which mind and mental factors grasp

### avyākṛta

- **Provisional meaning:** ethically indeterminate
- **Status:** standard Abhidharma category
- **Function:** neither intrinsically wholesome nor unwholesome

### arūpaśabdaka

- **Provisional meaning:** excluding form and sound
- **Status:** compact Kārikā expression resolved by the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** selects eight from the ten material resistant dhātus

## 6. Voice Register

### Vasubandhu / expository voice

- only rūpa is visible
- ten material dhātus are resistant
- threefold analysis of resistance
- definitions of `viṣaya` and `ālambana`
- the ten are resistant here through mutual material obstruction
- eight are ethically indeterminate

### Scriptural or scholastic examples

- examples of sensory operation in water, on land, by day, and by night
- four-cornered analysis of resistance

### Bhadanta Kumāralāta

- alternative formulation of resistance in relation to the possible obstruction of arising mind

### Alternative interpreters

- `avyākṛta` explained as indeterminate with regard to karmic result
- criticized because the same reasoning would also include the outflow-free

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The earlier Kārikā file left `arūpaśabdakāḥ` unresolved and risked reading the eight as “non-material.” The Bhāṣya decisively corrects this.

The preferred translation is:

> Among these, one is visible: the form-domain. Ten are resistant, namely the material domains. Eight are ethically indeterminate: those same ten, excluding form and sound.

No change is made to the Kārikā file during this Bhāṣya pass, but the correction should be carried into any later revision.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The passage is a model of scientific discrimination. One field is analyzed through independent predicates:

```text
visibility
materiality
spatial obstruction
functional range
cognitive objecthood
ethical determination
```

A dhātu does not receive one total description all at once. It is positioned differently under different principles of classification.

This supports the project thesis that the Kośa is Dharma functioning as science: its method is to prevent the collapse of distinct predicates into a single vague conception.

The movement toward what the project calls **Buddha Mind** is therefore not yet the assertion that ordinary `vijñāna` is Buddha Mind. Rather, it is the scientific purification of the field in which the distinction between outflow-bound cognition and liberated knowing can eventually be made exactly.
