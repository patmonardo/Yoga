# VAK_1.46_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> na kāyasyādharaṃ cakṣuḥ ūrdhvaṃ rūpaṃ na cakṣuṣaḥ /
>
> vijñānaṃ cāsya rūpaṃ tu kāyasyobhe ca sarvataḥ // VAkK_1.46 //

### Established Kārikā Translation

> The eye is not lower than the body; visible form is not higher than the eye. Its knowledge is likewise not higher than the eye. But, relative to eye-knowledge, visible form may be from any level; and, relative to the body, both visible form and eye-knowledge may be from any level.

The corresponding Kārikā analysis is preserved in `VAK_1.46.md`. The Bhāṣya substantially clarifies its compressed syntax.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. The planes under discussion

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Body, eye, and visible form occur on five planes: the desire realm and the four levels of meditative absorption. Eye-knowledge occurs on two planes: the desire realm and the first absorption.

The question is not yet one of geometric position. `Bhūmi` here means a plane or level of existence and meditative attainment.

```text
body, eye, visible form
    five possible planes

visual knowledge
    two possible planes
```

### 2. The eye is never lower than its body

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Relative to a body of a given plane, the eye may belong to that same plane or to a higher plane, but never to a lower plane.

Thus:

```text
body-plane ≤ eye-plane
```

A being embodied on a lower plane may employ an eye belonging to a higher meditative plane. But a higher body is not supported by a lower eye.

### 3. Visible form is never higher than the eye

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Relative to an eye of a given plane, visible form may belong to that same plane or to a lower plane. It is never higher than the eye.

The Bhāṣya states the reason directly:

> A visible form belonging to a higher plane can never be seen by an eye belonging to a lower plane.

Thus:

```text
visible-form plane ≤ eye-plane
```

This is a rule of cognitive reach across existential planes. A lower faculty cannot disclose an object belonging to a higher plane.

### 4. Eye-knowledge is likewise never higher than the eye

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Eye-knowledge, like visible form, is not higher than the eye.

Thus:

```text
eye-knowledge plane ≤ eye-plane
```

The support places an upper bound upon the level of the knowledge-event arising through it.

### 5. Visible form may stand at any level relative to eye-knowledge

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The pronoun `asya` refers to the immediately preceding eye-knowledge:

> Relative to this eye-knowledge, visible form may be higher, lower, or on the same plane.

Thus:

```text
relative to eye-knowledge:
    visible form may be higher
    visible form may be lower
    visible form may be on the same plane
```

This corrects the ambiguity of the isolated Kārikā. The unrestricted relation does not hold between visible form and eye, but between visible form and eye-knowledge.

### 6. Relative to the body, both visible form and eye-knowledge may stand at any level

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Relative to the body, both visible form and eye-knowledge may be higher, lower, or on the same plane.

Thus:

```text
relative to body:
    visible form = higher / lower / same
    eye-knowledge = higher / lower / same
```

The body therefore does not impose the same direct upper-bound relation that the faculty imposes.

### 7. The same structure applies to hearing

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The complete pattern is transferred from eye and visible form to ear and sound:

```text
body-plane ≤ ear-plane
sound-plane ≤ ear-plane
hearing-knowledge plane ≤ ear-plane
```

while sound relative to hearing-knowledge, and both sound and hearing-knowledge relative to body, may stand at any of the three levels: higher, lower, or same.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Question:
How do body, faculty, object, and knowledge relate across planes?

Given:
Body, eye, and visible form occur on five planes.
Eye-knowledge occurs only in the desire realm and first absorption.

Rule 1:
The eye may be on the body's plane or above it,
never below it.

Rule 2:
Visible form may be on the eye's plane or below it,
never above it.

Rule 3:
Eye-knowledge may be on the eye's plane or below it,
never above it.

Rule 4:
Relative to eye-knowledge, visible form may be
higher, lower, or the same.

Rule 5:
Relative to the body, both visible form and eye-knowledge may be
higher, lower, or the same.

Extension:
The same formal distribution applies to ear, sound,
and auditory knowledge.
```

The eye stands at the governing middle of the visual relation:

```text
body
    does not fully determine object or knowledge level

eye
    sets the upper limit for both object and knowledge

visible form and knowledge
    remain variably related to each other
```

## Translation Consequences

The earlier Kārikā analysis correctly recognized a vertical plane-relation but left `sarvataḥ` too vague. The Bhāṣya now fixes every relation.

### Revised Translation

> The eye is never lower than the body. Visible form is never higher than the eye, nor is the eye-knowledge dependent upon it. But relative to that eye-knowledge, visible form may belong to a higher, lower, or equal plane; and relative to the body, both visible form and eye-knowledge may stand in any of these three relations.

### Terminological caution

`Bhūmi` should be translated here as **plane** or **level**, not spatial location. The hierarchy concerns realms and meditative levels:

```text
desire realm
first absorption
second absorption
third absorption
fourth absorption
```

It therefore does not directly answer whether the subtle faculty possesses spatial extension.

---

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya assigns the eye a decisive ordering role:

```text
eye as āśraya
    governs the maximum plane of its object
    and the maximum plane of its knowledge
```

This develops VAK 1.45. The faculty is not only a determining support through qualitative modification; it also bounds the vertical range within which object and knowledge can be coordinated.

However, the present unit does **not** establish:

```text
indriya is spatially extended
```

or:

```text
indriya is wholly non-spatial
```

The classification by `bhūmi` is ontological and meditative, not geometric. Earlier passages classify the five faculties among material domains, but this verse itself concerns their level of operation rather than their spatial constitution.

This is precisely where the Bhāṣya must lead. A Sāṃkhya expectation that a sāttvic faculty should be non-spatial may be a useful comparative question, but it cannot determine the Kośa's account in advance.

---

## Logical Determination

The faculty is the regulating middle between embodiment, object, and knowledge:

```text
body
    permits same-plane or higher faculty

faculty
    permits same-plane or lower object
    permits same-plane or lower knowledge

object and knowledge
    may differ freely in level relative to one another
```

The logical structure is asymmetric:

```text
body → faculty
    lower bound on faculty

faculty → object and knowledge
    upper bound on both

object ↔ knowledge
    no fixed vertical identity
```

This means the support is not merely one condition among others. It sets the admissible range of disclosure.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The most useful contact point is not spatiality but **range-determination**:

```text
faculty-capacity
    = a determinate power of disclosure
    whose support-level bounds what can appear through it
```

This gives a stronger form to the earlier quantitative hypothesis. Capacity is not unlimited. Yet the limitation here is qualitative and hierarchical before it is numerical:

```text
not every faculty
    can disclose every plane of object

not every support
    can generate every plane of knowledge
```

The eye is therefore representational in a disciplined sense: it opens a determinate object-field while imposing conditions upon what can enter that field.

We should not yet infer spatial extension or non-extension from this. The Bhāṣya's own distinctions remain:

```text
bhūmi
    level of being and meditative attainment

āśraya
    determining support

viṣaya
    determinate object-field

vijñāna
    supported knowledge-event
```
