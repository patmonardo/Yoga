# VAK_1.34 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> sapta sālambanāś cittadhātavo 'rdhaṃ ca dharmataḥ /
>
> navānupāttās te cāṣṭau śabdaś cānye nava dvidhā // VAkK_1.34 //

### Translation

> The seven mind-domains have objects, as does the associated portion of the dharma-domain. Nine are unappropriated: those eight together with sound. The other nine are twofold.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins by asking which of the eighteen dhātus possess an object (*sālambana*) and which do not. It then asks which are appropriated (*upātta*) and which are unappropriated (*anupātta*). The unit closes with Vasubandhu’s definition of appropriation and examples distinguishing organic bodily matter from material that is not taken up into the living continuum.

> kati sālambanāḥ katyanālambanāḥ /
>
> sapta sālambanāś cittadhātavaḥ
>
> cakṣuḥśrotraghrāṇajihvākāyamanovijñānadhātavo manodhātuś ca ete sapta cittadhātavaḥ sālambanā viṣayagrahaṇāt /
>
> ardhaṃ ca dharmataḥ /
>
> sālambanaṃ yaccaitasikasvabhāvam /
>
> śeṣā daśa rūpiṇo dhātavo dharmadhātupradeśaś cāsaṃprayuktako 'nālambanā iti siddham /
>
> katyupāttāḥ katyanupāttāḥ /
>
> navānupāttāḥ /
>
> katame nava / ye sapta sālambanā uktā aṣṭamasya ardhena sārdham /
>
> te cāṣṭau śabdaś ca
>
> ime te navānupāttāḥ / sapta cittadhātavo dharmadhātuḥ śabdadhātuś ca /
>
> anye nava dvidhā // VAkK_1.34 //
>
> upāttā anupāttāś ca /
>
> tatra cakṣuḥśrotraghrāṇajihvākāyāḥ pratyutpannā upāttāḥ / atītānāgatā anupāttāḥ /
>
> rūpagandharasaspraṣṭavyadhātavaḥ pratyutpannā indriyāvinirbhāgiṇa upāttāḥ /
>
> anye 'nupāttās tadyathā mūlavarjeṣu keśaromanakhadanteṣu viṇmūtrakheṭasiṃghāṇakaśoṇitādiṣu bhūmyudakādiṣu ca /
>
> upāttam iti ko 'rthaḥ /
>
> yac cittacaittair adhiṣṭhānabhāvenopagṛhītam anugrahopaghātābhyām anyonyānuvidhānāt /
>
> yal loke sacetanam ity ucyate /

### Translation

> How many possess an object, and how many do not possess an object?
>
> The seven mind-domains possess an object.
>
> The eye-, ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mental-consciousness domains, together with the mind-domain, are the seven mind-domains. They possess objects because they apprehend their respective object-ranges.
>
> And half of the dharma-domain.
>
> That part whose intrinsic nature consists in associated mental factors possesses an object.
>
> It is therefore established that the remaining ten material domains and the unassociated portion of the dharma-domain do not possess objects.
>
> How many are appropriated, and how many are unappropriated?
>
> Nine are unappropriated.
>
> Which nine? The seven just described as possessing objects, together with the eighth formed by adding the relevant half [of the dharma-domain].
>
> Those eight, together with sound.
>
> These are the nine unappropriated domains: the seven mind-domains, the dharma-domain, and the sound-domain.
>
> The other nine are twofold.
>
> They may be appropriated or unappropriated.
>
> Among them, the eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body faculties are appropriated when present; when past or future they are unappropriated.
>
> Visible form, smell, taste, and tangible matter are appropriated when presently inseparable from a faculty. Other instances are unappropriated—for example, hair, body-hair, nails, and teeth apart from their roots; feces, urine, saliva, mucus, blood, and the like; and earth, water, and so forth outside the organism.
>
> What does “appropriated” mean?
>
> It is that which mind and mental factors have taken up as their supporting basis, because benefit and injury occurring to either side are followed correspondingly by the other.
>
> It is what ordinary usage calls “sentient” or “ensouled.”

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 The seven *cittadhātus* are object-taking

The seven are:

1. the five sensory consciousness-domains,
2. the mental-consciousness domain,
3. the mind-domain.

They are called *sālambana* because they apprehend an object-range (*viṣayagrahaṇa*). The Bhāṣya thus defines their object-relatedness through cognitive operation, not merely through spatial proximity to an external thing.

### 3.2 Only the associated portion of *dharmadhātu* is object-taking

The Kārikā’s compact phrase *ardhaṃ ca dharmataḥ* is explained as the portion of the dharma-domain whose nature consists in associated mental factors (*caitasikasvabhāva*). Mental factors take objects together with mind.

The unassociated portion of the dharma-domain does not take objects. This includes determinations that are not cognitive events or associated mental factors.

The Bhāṣya therefore divides the dharma-domain internally:

```text
dharmadhātu
    ├── associated mental factors → sālambana
    └── unassociated dharmas      → anālambana
```

The word “half” is classificatory rather than a claim of equal numerical division.

### 3.3 The nine unappropriated domains

The nine are:

```text
seven cittadhātus
+ dharmadhātu
+ śabdadhātu
```

The counting differs slightly from the immediately preceding object-taking division. There, only the associated portion of the dharma-domain was object-taking. Here the **whole dharma-domain** is unappropriated.

This is because object-taking and appropriation are different classificatory axes. A dharma may take an object without being bodily matter appropriated as an organic support.

### 3.4 The remaining nine are twofold

The remaining nine are:

```text
five sense-faculty domains
+ visible form
+ smell
+ taste
+ tangible matter
```

Each may be appropriated or unappropriated depending on its temporal and organic relation to a living continuum.

The five faculties are appropriated only when presently functioning as organic faculties. Their past and future instances are unappropriated.

The four material object-domains are appropriated when presently inseparable from a faculty-bearing organism. The same kinds of matter are unappropriated when external to that organic unity or separated from it.

### 3.5 *Upātta* is a functional-organic relation

The Bhāṣya does not define appropriation as mere ownership or physical enclosure. Matter is appropriated when mind and mental factors have taken it up as their support (*adhiṣṭhānabhāvena upagṛhīta*).

The diagnostic sign is reciprocal responsiveness:

```text
benefit or injury to the bodily basis
    → corresponding effect upon mind and mental factors

benefit or injury to mind and mental factors
    → corresponding effect upon the bodily basis
```

Thus *upātta* marks matter incorporated into a living psycho-physical continuum. Vasubandhu connects this technical definition to ordinary language: it is what people call *sacetana*, “sentient,” “animated,” or “endowed with awareness.”

## 4. Argument Summary

Vasubandhu applies two independent distinctions to the eighteen dhātus.

First, object-taking:

```text
sālambana
    = seven cittadhātus
    + associated mental-factor portion of dharmadhātu

anālambana
    = ten material dhātus
    + unassociated portion of dharmadhātu
```

Second, appropriation:

```text
always anupātta
    = seven cittadhātus
    + whole dharmadhātu
    + sound

upātta or anupātta
    = five faculties
    + visible form, smell, taste, tangible matter
```

The central doctrinal point is that having an object and being incorporated into a living body are not the same relation.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### *sālambana*

- **Provisional meaning:** possessing an object; object-taking
- **Status:** established Abhidharma classification
- **Function:** marks a cognitive dharma that apprehends an object-range

### *anālambana*

- **Provisional meaning:** without an object; non-object-taking
- **Status:** established Abhidharma classification
- **Function:** marks material dharmas and non-associated dharmas that do not cognize

### *upātta*

- **Provisional meaning:** appropriated; organically taken up
- **Status:** technical Abhidharma term defined by Vasubandhu
- **Function:** marks matter incorporated as the living support of mind and mental factors

### *anupātta*

- **Provisional meaning:** unappropriated; not organically taken up
- **Status:** technical Abhidharma classification
- **Function:** marks cognitive dharmas, sound, and matter not incorporated into a present living continuum

### *adhiṣṭhānabhāva*

- **Provisional meaning:** the condition of serving as a supporting basis
- **Status:** explanatory vocabulary in Vasubandhu’s definition of *upātta*
- **Function:** specifies the bodily support-relation through which matter is appropriated by mind and mental factors

### *sacetana*

- **Provisional meaning:** sentient; animated; associated with awareness
- **Status:** ordinary-world designation cited by Vasubandhu
- **Function:** connects the technical category of appropriated matter with ordinary recognition of a living body

## 6. Voice Register

- **Vasubandhu:** asks the classificatory questions; identifies the seven object-taking mind-domains; divides the dharma-domain; enumerates the nine unappropriated domains; defines *upātta*.
- **Abhidharma doctrinal register:** the categories *sālambana/anālambana* and *upātta/anupātta*.
- **Ordinary usage cited by Vasubandhu:** *yal loke sacetanam ity ucyate*—“what in the world is called sentient.”
- **No substantial opponent voice:** the passage is explanatory and classificatory rather than dialectical.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The existing Kārikā translation requires two clarifications.

First, *ardhaṃ ca dharmataḥ* should not be rendered as though exactly half of all dharmas were counted. The Bhāṣya identifies the intended portion as the associated mental factors within the dharma-domain.

Second, “those eight” in the appropriation count must not obscure that the full dharma-domain is ultimately counted among the nine unappropriated domains.

Preferred productive translation:

> The seven mind-domains possess objects, as does the associated portion of the dharma-domain. Nine are unappropriated: those seven, the dharma-domain, and sound. The remaining nine may be either appropriated or unappropriated.

This departs slightly from the Kārikā’s surface compression in order to express the Bhāṣya’s explicit enumeration.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The useful contact point is modest but precise:

```text
object-relation
    ≠ organic incorporation
```

The Kośa distinguishes a knowing’s relation to its object from a body’s appropriation into a living continuum. In project language, this may later help distinguish the intentional structure of a cognitive domain from the embodiment or hosting conditions under which that cognition occurs.

This is a provisional application. Vasubandhu’s immediate concern is the Abhidharma classification of dhātus, not a general theory of an Agent or cognitive architecture.
