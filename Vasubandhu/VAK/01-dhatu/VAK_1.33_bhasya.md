# VAK_1.33 Bhāṣya Study

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> nirūpaṇānusmaraṇavikalpenāvikalpakāḥ /
>
> tau prajñā mānasī vyagrā smṛtiḥ sarvaiva mānasī // VAkK_1.33 //

### Translation

> The five are called non-discriminative because they lack the discriminations of examination and recollection. Of those two, examination is distracted mental discernment, while recollection is memory, all of which is mental.

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the question arising from VAK 1.32:

> yadi pañca vijñānakāyāḥ savitarkāḥ savicārāḥ katham avikalpakā ity ucyante /

> If the five groups of consciousness are accompanied by vitarka and vicāra, why are they called non-discriminative?

The reply introduces three kinds of `vikalpa`:

> trividhaḥ kila vikalpaḥ /
> svabhāvābhinirūpaṇānusmaraṇavikalpaḥ /

> Discrimination is said to be threefold: intrinsic discrimination, examining discrimination, and recollective discrimination.

The five sensory consciousnesses possess only the first:

> tadeṣāṃ svabhāvavikalpo 'sti /
> netarau /
> tasmād avikalpakā ity ucyante /

> They possess intrinsic discrimination, but not the other two. Therefore they are called non-discriminative.

Vasubandhu illustrates the usage:

> yathā ekapādako 'śvo 'pādaka iti /

> Just as a horse with only one foot may be called footless.

He then identifies intrinsic discrimination:

> tatra svabhāvavikalpo vitarkaḥ /

> Among these, intrinsic discrimination is vitarka.

VAK 1.33 identifies the other two:

> manovijñānasaṃprayuktā prajñā mānasīty ucyate /
> asamāhitā vyagrety ucyate /
> sā hy abhinirūpaṇāvikalpaḥ /

> Discernment associated with mental consciousness is called mental; when it is not concentrated, it is called distracted. This is examining discrimination.

And:

> mānasyeva sarvā smṛtiḥ samāhitā cāsamāhitā cānusmaraṇavikalpaḥ /

> All memory is exclusively mental, whether concentrated or unconcentrated; this is recollective discrimination.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### 3.1 Why sensory consciousness can be called `avikalpaka`

The apparent contradiction is only verbal.

VAK 1.32 says that the five sensory consciousnesses are accompanied by `vitarka` and `vicāra`. Since the Bhāṣya now identifies `vitarka` as `svabhāvavikalpa`, sensory consciousness is not utterly devoid of discrimination.

It is called `avikalpaka` because it lacks the two fuller forms:

```text
abhinirūpaṇavikalpa
    = examining discrimination

anusmaraṇavikalpa
    = recollective discrimination
```

Thus `avikalpaka` is a relative designation, not an absolute denial of all differentiation.

### 3.2 The threefold structure of `vikalpa`

The Bhāṣya gives:

```text
1. svabhāvavikalpa
   intrinsic discrimination
   = vitarka

2. abhinirūpaṇavikalpa
   examining discrimination
   = unconcentrated mental prajñā

3. anusmaraṇavikalpa
   recollective discrimination
   = all mental smṛti
```

The first belongs even to the five sensory consciousnesses. The latter two require mental consciousness.

### 3.3 `abhinirūpaṇa` is not all prajñā

The Bhāṣya narrows the definition carefully.

It is:

```text
prajñā
+ associated with manovijñāna
+ asamāhita, unconcentrated
```

The adjective `vyagra` is glossed through `asamāhita`: dispersed, distracted, or not collected in concentration.

Therefore concentrated discernment is not being classified here as examining discrimination. The term designates discursive mental examination operating outside concentration.

### 3.4 All memory is mental

The definition of recollective discrimination is broader with respect to concentration:

```text
smṛti
+ exclusively mental
+ concentrated or unconcentrated
```

The contrast is exact:

```text
abhinirūpaṇa
    only unconcentrated mental prajñā

anusmaraṇa
    all mental smṛti,
    concentrated and unconcentrated
```

Sensory consciousness may occur in dependence upon a present object, but recollection as such belongs only to the mental sphere.

### 3.5 The one-footed horse analogy

The analogy shows how classificatory language works by predominance or completeness.

A horse with one foot can be called “footless” because it lacks the normal complement of feet. Likewise, sensory consciousness can be called `avikalpaka` because it lacks the two developed forms of discrimination, even though intrinsic discrimination remains.

The point is linguistic and technical, not metaphysical.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Objection:
The five sensory consciousnesses have vitarka and vicāra.
Why call them non-discriminative?

Reply:
Vikalpa is threefold.

Sensory consciousness possesses:
    svabhāvavikalpa = vitarka

It lacks:
    abhinirūpaṇavikalpa
    anusmaraṇavikalpa

Therefore it is conventionally called avikalpaka.

The latter two are:
    unconcentrated mental prajñā
    all mental smṛti
```

The Bhāṣya therefore establishes a graded account of discrimination rather than a simple opposition between discrimination and its absence.

## 5. New Technical Terms

### `svabhāvavikalpa`

- **Provisional meaning:** intrinsic discrimination
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition
- **Function:** the minimal discrimination present in sensory consciousness; identified with `vitarka`

### `abhinirūpaṇavikalpa`

- **Provisional meaning:** examining or determining discrimination
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition
- **Function:** unconcentrated discernment associated with mental consciousness

### `anusmaraṇavikalpa`

- **Provisional meaning:** recollective discrimination
- **Status:** Bhāṣya definition
- **Function:** memory operating in the mental sphere, whether concentrated or unconcentrated

### `vyagra`

- **Provisional meaning:** distracted, dispersed, unconcentrated
- **Status:** explicitly glossed by `asamāhita`
- **Function:** restricts the prajñā that counts as examining discrimination

### `mānasī prajñā`

- **Provisional meaning:** mental discernment
- **Status:** Bhāṣya technical usage
- **Function:** prajñā associated with `manovijñāna`

## 6. Voice Register

- **Objector:** asks how sensory consciousness can be non-discriminative if it is accompanied by vitarka and vicāra.
- **Received doctrinal classification:** `trividhaḥ kila vikalpaḥ` — “discrimination is said to be threefold.” The particle `kila` marks reported doctrine.
- **Vasubandhu:** explains why sensory consciousness is called `avikalpaka`, supplies the one-footed horse analogy, identifies `svabhāvavikalpa` with `vitarka`, and glosses the two remaining forms.
- **No independent external school attribution** is made in this unit.

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The earlier Kārikā translation should be refined in three ways.

First, `avikalpakāḥ` is relative:

```text
not devoid of every vikalpa
but devoid of abhinirūpaṇa and anusmaraṇa vikalpa
```

Second, `vyagrā` is explicitly glossed as `asamāhitā`; “unconcentrated” is therefore more precise than merely “engaged.”

Third, `prajñā` here is discriminative discernment associated with mental consciousness, not prajñā in every possible sense.

Preferred productive translation:

> The five are called non-discriminative because they lack examining and recollective discrimination. These two are, respectively, unconcentrated mental discernment and memory, all of which is mental.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya itself gives a graded architecture:

```text
minimal intrinsic differentiation
    → mental examination
    → recollective discrimination
```

For the Organon project, this may later support a distinction between immediate sensory determination and mentally mediated determination. But the present textual result is narrower: sensory consciousness is not blank; it has `svabhāvavikalpa`, while developed examination and recollection belong to mind.
