## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> dvādaśādhyātmikāḥ hitvā rūpādīn dharmasaṃjñakaḥ /
>
> sabhāgaḥ tatsabhāgāś ca śeṣāḥ yo na svakarmakṛt // VAkK_1.39 //

### Established Kārikā Translation

> Twelve are internal, excluding the domains beginning with form and the one called dharma. The remaining domains are functioning and corresponding non-functioning members; that which does not perform its own function is a corresponding non-functioning member.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. Twelve internal and six external domains

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> The six knowledge-domains and their six supports are the twelve internal domains. The six object-domains beginning with form are external.

The Bhāṣya therefore defines internality relationally:

```text
six supports
+
six knowledge-domains
=
twelve internal domains
```

The six external domains are the object-side:

```text
rūpa
śabda
gandha
rasa
spraṣṭavya
dharma
```

### 2. Internal and external do not require a metaphysical self

**Voice: Objector**

> If there is no self, how can anything be called internal or external?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Mind is figuratively called “self” because ego-making rests upon it. Scriptural uses of “self-restraint” are therefore explained through discipline of mind. The faculties are internal because they stand nearest to mind as its supports; objects are external because they stand as objects.

This is not a reintroduction of an ātman. It is a controlled conventional designation:

```text
citta
    conventionally called ātman
    because ahaṅkāra rests upon it

faculties
    internal through proximity as supports

objects
    external through object-function
```

### 3. Why the six knowledge-domains are also internal

**Voice: Objector**

> Before the six knowledge-domains become mind-domain, they are not supports of mind. Why then are they internal?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Their defining mark does not vary across past, present, and future. Otherwise mind-domain would exist only in the past. Since all eighteen domains are admitted across the three times, present and future knowledge must already possess the mark by which it can be determined as mind-domain.

The argument is:

```text
future/present knowledge
    has the same defining mark

when past
    it functions as mind-domain

therefore
    internality belongs by enduring determination,
    not only by present exercise
```

### 4. The dharma-domain is always functioning

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

An object is *sabhāga* when the knowledge fixed upon it has arisen or is capable of arising. No dharma exists that cannot become object-support for an immeasurable mental knowledge.

The decisive example is the cognition:

> “All dharmas are without self.”

For that cognition, all dharmas apart from its own intrinsic nature and co-arisen factors are object-support. That cognition in turn becomes the object-support of another moment of mind. Across the two moments, all dharmas become object-support. Therefore:

```text
dharmadhātu
    is always sabhāga
```

### 5. *Sabhāga* means performance of proper function

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> What performs its own function is *sabhāga*.

Thus an eye by which forms were seen, are seen, or will be seen is a functioning eye. The same determination applies through mind according to each faculty’s proper object-operation.

This corrects a merely presentist reading:

```text
sabhāga
    does not mean only functioning now

it includes
    past function
    present function
    future function
```

### 6. *Tat-sabhāga* is non-operation within the same kind

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

A faculty that does not perform its proper function is *tat-sabhāga*. The term does not mean that it lacks the nature of that faculty. It remains of the same kind as functioning members but does not exercise their proper operation.

The Kāśmīra and western enumerations differ over how many non-functioning eye-types should be counted, especially over whether a never-arising eye should be divided according to whether it is or is not associated with knowledge.

### 7. Common objects and non-common faculties

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

A single visible object may be seen by many streams, as with the moon, a dancer, or a wrestling spectacle. But two streams cannot see through one person’s eye.

Therefore:

```text
faculty
    non-common
    fixed relative to one continuum

object
    common
    available to many continua
```

The same principle extends to sound, smell, taste, and tangible objects, though the discussion notes the special complications of contact-based apprehension.

### 8. The deeper etymology of *sabhāga*

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

*Bhāga* may mean the mutual participation of faculty, object, and knowledge, or participation in operation. One may also explain the term through their sharing the same result, contact.

Thus *sabhāga* is not mere taxonomic membership. It is functional participation in a coordinated event:

```text
faculty
object
knowledge
    ↓
mutual participation
    ↓
contact
```

Those that do not participate functionally are called *tat-sabhāga* because they share the same class-nature as the functioning members.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Question 1:
Which domains are internal and which external?

Answer:
Six supports and six knowledge-domains are internal.
Six object-domains are external.

Objection:
Without a self, “internal” and “external” seem impossible.

Resolution:
Mind is conventionally designated as self because ego-making rests upon it.
Faculties are internal through proximity as supports of mind.
Objects are external through object-function.

Question 2:
Why are the six knowledge-domains internal before becoming mind-domain?

Resolution:
Their defining mark does not vary across the three times.
Present and future knowledge already possess the determination by which past knowledge functions as mind-domain.

Question 3:
What is sabhāga?

Answer:
A domain is sabhāga when it performs, has performed, or will perform its proper function.

Special case:
Dharmadhātu is always sabhāga because every dharma can become object-support for mental knowledge.

Question 4:
What is tat-sabhāga?

Answer:
A member of the same kind that does not perform its proper function.

Deeper determination:
Sabhāga is mutual functional participation among faculty, object, and knowledge, culminating in contact.
```

## Translation Consequences

### 1. *Ādhyātmika*

The established translation “internal” remains correct, but the Bhāṣya prevents a subjectivist or substantial-self reading.

```text
internal
    = proximate to mind as support
    or belonging to the knowledge-side

not
    = contained inside an ātman
```

### 2. *Sabhāga*

The earlier rendering “functioning member” is confirmed but should be strengthened:

```text
sabhāga
    = functionally participating member
```

It includes past, present, or future proper operation, not only current activity.

### 3. *Tat-sabhāga*

“Corresponding non-functioning member” remains strong. The Bhāṣya clarifies that it shares the class-nature of functioning members but lacks their proper functional participation.

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> Twelve domains are internal: the six supports and six knowledge-domains. The six object-domains beginning with form are external. The dharma-domain is always functionally participating. The remaining domains may either participate in their proper operation or remain corresponding members of the same kind without performing it.

## Doctrinal Determination

The most important doctrinal result is that internality is not grounded in an enduring self.

```text
ātman
    not admitted as substance

citta
    conventionally called “self”
    because ego-making rests upon it

internality
    determined through support-relation and proximity

externality
    determined through object-relation
```

The passage therefore gives a relational, not substantial, account of interiority.

The second major result is the distinction between capacity and functional participation:

```text
faculty-nature
    may remain

proper function
    may or may not be exercised
```

This directly supports the earlier determination of indriya:

```text
indriya
    is not the Seer
    and not merely the gross seen organ

it is a non-common capacity
    belonging to one continuum
    ordered toward proper apprehensive operation
```

### New Development for the Dream of Philosophy

The present unit advances the project’s account of mediation:

> The living middle is not exhausted by the possession of a capacity. It becomes actual through reciprocal functional participation among faculty, object, and knowledge.

This preserves the Copula as operative mediation rather than static identity. Faculty, object, and knowledge do not collapse into one another; each becomes determinate through participation in the common work culminating in contact.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya moves from structural membership to functional actuality:

```text
class-nature
    ↓
capacity for proper function
    ↓
participation in operation
    ↓
contact as common result
```

The decisive distinction is:

```text
being of a kind
    ≠
performing the function of that kind
```

But the relation is not external:

```text
tat-sabhāga
    remains determined by sabhāga
    through common class-nature
```

Thus non-function is itself intelligible only through relation to function.

A second logical distinction appears:

```text
faculty
    non-common to one continuum

object
    common across many continua
```

This preserves the singularity of the instrument while allowing universality of the object-field.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The deepest Organon contact point is the structure:

```text
faculty
    instrument-capacity

object
    common field

knowledge
    actual apprehensive event

contact
    common result of their reciprocal participation
```

This is stronger than a simple subject-object diagram. The Bhāṣya explicitly defines *sabhāga* through mutual participation and common operation.

The indriya therefore operates for the sake of apprehension, but its truth is not isolated instrumentality. Its actuality lies in the living relation:

```text
indriya
    ↔ object
    ↔ knowledge
    → contact
```

The middle is reciprocal. No member alone constitutes the cognitive event.
