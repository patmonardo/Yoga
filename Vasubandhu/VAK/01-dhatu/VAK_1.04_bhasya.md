# VAK_1.04 Bhāṣya

## The First Comprehensive Division of Dharmas

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.04.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya sharpens or corrects the first-pass translation, that decision is recorded here for later review.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> sāsravānāsravā dharmāḥ saṃskṛtā mārgavarjitāḥ /
>
> sāsravā āsravās teṣu yasmāt samanuśerate // VAkK_1.04 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Dharmas are with outflows and without outflows. Conditioned dharmas, excluding the Path, are with outflows, because the outflows settle and continue within them.

The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies the opening hemistich as:

> *eṣa sarvadharmāṇāṃ samāsanirdeśaḥ*

That is:

> This is the summary designation of all dharmas.

The verse therefore begins the comprehensive doctrinal classification promised by VAK 1.03.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit extends from:

> *katame punas te dharmā yeṣāṃ pravicayārtham abhidharmopadeśa ity āha...*

through:

> *uktāḥ sāsravāḥ.*

It forms one continuous movement:

```text
Which dharmas are to be discriminated?
    ↓
all dharmas summarized as sāsrava / anāsrava
    ↓
which among them are sāsrava?
    ↓
conditioned dharmas excluding the Path
    ↓
why are they sāsrava?
    ↓
because āsravas settle within them
    ↓
objection from āsravas arising with nirvāṇa or Path as object
    ↓
distinction between arising with an object and settling in that object
```

The unit is not merely classificatory. It immediately introduces a distinction between the object toward which an outflow arises and the field in which that outflow can remain latent.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. Which dharmas are the object of discrimination?

> *katame punas te dharmā yeṣāṃ pravicayārtham abhidharmopadeśa ity āha—sāsravā anāsravā dharmāḥ.*

### Translation

> Which, then, are those dharmas for whose discrimination the instruction in Abhidharma is given? He says: dharmas are with outflows and without outflows.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

VAK 1.03 established `dharmapravicaya` as necessary for pacifying the kleśas. VAK 1.04 now gives the first determination of the field to be discriminated:

```text
all dharmas
    ↓
sāsrava / anāsrava
```

The first division is therefore practical and soteriological before it is ontological. Dharmas are first distinguished according to their relation to the outflows.

---

### B. A summary designation of all dharmas

> *eṣa sarvadharmāṇāṃ samāsanirdeśaḥ.*

### Translation

> This is the summary designation of all dharmas.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The expression `samāsanirdeśa` indicates a compressed total classification. Before individual dharmas are enumerated, the entire domain is divided under two opposed predicates.

```text
sarvadharma
    one comprehensive field

samāsanirdeśa
    its first compressed articulation
```

This gives VAK 1.04 a structurally privileged position. It is not one classification among others; it is the first universal partition of the field.

---

### C. Which dharmas are sāsrava?

> *tatra katame sāsravā dharmā ity āha—saṃskṛtā mārgavarjitāḥ sāsravāḥ.*

### Translation

> Among these, which dharmas are with outflows? He says: conditioned dharmas, excluding the Path, are with outflows.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya restates the Kārikā without adding a new school qualification:

```text
saṃskṛta dharma
    conditioned dharma

minus

mārgasatya
    the Truth of the Path

=

sāsrava dharma
```

The Path is therefore exceptional within the conditioned field.

It is not unconditioned, but neither does it belong to the outflow-bearing domain.

---

### D. The explicit Path exception

> *mārgasatyaṃ varjayitvā 'nye saṃskṛtā dharmāḥ sāsravāḥ.*

### Translation

> Excluding the Truth of the Path, the other conditioned dharmas are with outflows.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya makes explicit that `mārga` here means `mārgasatya`, the Truth of the Path.

The distinction is therefore:

```text
conditioned dharmas generally
    sāsrava

conditioned Path-truth
    anāsrava
```

This prevents a simple identification:

```text
conditioned = contaminated
```

Conditioning is the mode of arising. Outflow-status is a different determination.

The Path belongs to conditioned production while functioning against the structure of bondage.

---

### E. Why they are called sāsrava

> *kiṃ kāraṇam—āsravās teṣu yasmāt samanuśerate.*

### Translation

> Why? Because the outflows settle and continue within them.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The decisive verb is `samanuśerate`.

It is stronger than a merely external relation such as “occur in connection with.” It indicates that the outflows lie down, adhere, or remain latent within these dharmas.

```text
sāsrava dharma
    not merely a dharma accompanied by an occurrent kleśa

sāsrava dharma
    a dharma capable of serving as a field in which āsravas settle
```

The expression anticipates the later theory of `anuśaya`. The outflow-bearing status of dharmas is defined through their suitability as a lodging or basis of continued afflictive adherence.

---

### F. The apparent problem

> *kāmaṃ nirodhamārgasatyālambanā api āsravā upajāyante...*

### Translation

> Granted, outflows also arise having the Truth of Cessation and the Truth of the Path as their object.

**Voice:** Objector or anticipated objection

The Bhāṣya introduces a potential contradiction.

If an outflow can arise with cessation or the Path as its object, why are cessation and Path not themselves called sāsrava?

The objection presupposes:

```text
if an āsrava arises in relation to X
    then X should be sāsrava
```

But this inference is rejected.

---

### G. Object and lodging are not the same relation

> *na tu anuśerate tatra iti na tayoḥ sāsravatvaprasaṅgaḥ.*

### Translation

> But they do not settle there; therefore it does not follow that those two are with outflows.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is the central distinction of the unit:

```text
ālambana-relation
    an outflow arises taking something as object

anuśayana-relation
    an outflow settles and continues within something
```

An outflow may take the Path or cessation as its object without those realities becoming its lodging.

The classification `sāsrava` therefore depends not on being thinkable or objectifiable by an afflicted mind, but on being structurally capable of sustaining the latency of the outflows.

This is a major refinement of the first-pass rendering “structurally available to the outflows.” The Bhāṣya supports that direction, but gives the exact distinction:

```text
object of an outflow
    ≠
field in which an outflow settles
```

---

### H. Deferred proof

> *yathā 'tra nānuśerate tat paścād anuśayanirdeśa eva jñāpayiṣyāmaḥ.*

### Translation

> How they do not settle there will be explained later, precisely in the Exposition of the Anuśayas.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya marks the present account as deliberately incomplete.

The full explanation belongs to Chapter 5, the `Anuśayanirdeśa`.

This creates an explicit internal cross-reference:

```text
VAK 1.04
    states the classification

Anuśayanirdeśa
    supplies the full theory of non-settling in Path and cessation
```

The local study should therefore preserve the distinction without attempting to import the entire later theory prematurely.

---

### I. Closure of the sāsrava definition

> *uktāḥ sāsravāḥ.*

### Translation

> The dharmas with outflows have now been stated.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This closes only the first side of the universal division. VAK 1.05 will begin the account of the anāsrava dharmas.

## 4. Argument Summary

```text
Abhidharma discriminates all dharmas.
        ↓
All dharmas are first summarized as sāsrava or anāsrava.
        ↓
Conditioned dharmas excluding the Path are sāsrava.
        ↓
They are sāsrava because outflows settle and remain latent within them.
        ↓
An outflow may nevertheless arise with cessation or the Path as object.
        ↓
Being an object of an outflow does not make something its lodging.
        ↓
Cessation and Path are therefore not sāsrava.
        ↓
The full account of this non-settling is deferred to the Anuśayanirdeśa.
```

The decisive relation is:

```text
sāsravatva
    depends on anuśayana
    not merely on ālambana
```

## 5. New Technical Terms

### **samāsanirdeśa**

- **Provisional meaning:** compressed or summary designation
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** identifies the sāsrava/anāsrava division as the first comprehensive classification of all dharmas

### **mārgasatya**

- **Provisional meaning:** the Truth of the Path
- **Status:** the Kārikā says `mārga`; the Bhāṣya makes `mārgasatya` explicit
- **Function:** identifies the conditioned exception to the sāsrava field

### **samanuśayana**

- **Provisional meaning:** settling, adhering, or remaining latent within
- **Status:** verbal form present in the Kārikā; its technical force is clarified in the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** supplies the defining relation of sāsravatva

### **ālambana**

- **Provisional meaning:** object-support; that which a cognition or outflow takes as its object
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā; previously established project term
- **Function:** distinguishes object-relation from the relation of latent adherence

### **sāsravatvaprasaṅga**

- **Provisional meaning:** the unwanted consequence that something would be with outflows
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** formulates the objection concerning cessation and the Path as objects of outflows

### **anuśayanirdeśa**

- **Provisional meaning:** Exposition of the Anuśayas
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** internal cross-reference to the later chapter where the unresolved mechanism is explained

## 7. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass translation is substantially sound, but two points should be sharpened during the later revision pass.

### A. `mārga`

The Bhāṣya explicitly glosses:

```text
mārga
    = mārgasatya
    = the Truth of the Path
```

The revised Kārikā translation should therefore preferably say:

> Conditioned dharmas, excluding the Truth of the Path, are with outflows.

### B. `samanuśerate`

“Lie latent” is acceptable, but the Bhāṣya's objection shows that the decisive contrast is between:

```text
arising with something as object
    and
settling within something as a latent basis
```

A stronger translation is therefore:

> because the outflows settle and continue within them.

The current first-pass interpretation that sāsrava dharmas are “structurally available to the outflows” is supported, but should be grounded explicitly in this distinction.

`VAK_1.04.md` remains unchanged until the dedicated Kārikā revision pass.

## 8. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya gives a precise relation between a determination and an afflictive operation.

A determination does not become contaminated merely because an afflicted cognition can take it as object.

```text
objectification by affliction
    does not constitute contamination
```

Contamination requires a deeper relation:

```text
the determination must be capable of sustaining
and reproducing the latent afflictive structure
```

The Path is therefore conditioned but does not reproduce the bondage that conditioning otherwise carries.

This yields a provisional structural distinction:

```text
sāsrava field
    conditioned determination as lodging of recurrent affliction

anāsrava Path
    conditioned determination whose activity counters recurrence
```

### Organon Method Note: Root Division and Genetic Division

The Bhāṣya itself makes a stronger methodological statement than a merely local classification:

> *eṣa sarvadharmāṇāṃ samāsanirdeśaḥ*
>
> This is the summary designation of all dharmas.

Textually, this establishes the `sāsrava / anāsrava` division as a comprehensive first articulation of the entire field of dharmas.

The following is a **provisional Organon interpretation**, not a claim that Vasubandhu explicitly formulates a Kantian, Fichtean, or Hegelian method.

If this division is taken seriously as the root division of `sarvadharma`, then subsequent scientific divisions should not be treated as externally juxtaposed taxonomies. They should be sought as further determinations of a field already articulated by the prior division.

```text
sarvadharmāḥ
    ↓
sāsrava | anāsrava
    ↓
further determinations within the divided whole
    ↓
further division from prior division
```

This gives a possible Abhidharma analogue to the methodological demand of First Philosophy:

```text
no imported term
no merely external juxtaposition
no later division without a prior determining ground

prior determination
    → internal differentiation
    → further determination
```

The philosophical force is that a science cannot simply place independently given predicates side by side if it is operating at the level of principle. External juxtaposition presupposes that the terms have already been supplied by some field of presentation. In a genetic science, difference must instead arise through the determination of what has already been established.

On this reading, `samāsanirdeśa` is therefore methodologically important: it identifies a first comprehensive articulation from which later divisions can be tested for systematic dependence.

This also changes how the Kośa's many later matrices should be approached. Their scientific value would lie not merely in exhaustive sorting, but in whether they can be reconstructed as subordinate determinations of previously established divisions.

A provisional comparative formula is:

```text
Fichtean First Philosophy
    division must be generated from prior determination

Hegelian Logic
    later determination must exhibit its genesis from the earlier

Kośa, read through the Organon
    later dharma-division should be tested as further articulation
    of an already principially divided whole
```

The comparison is intentionally methodological rather than historical. The text warrants the claim that Vasubandhu presents `sāsrava / anāsrava` as a summary division of all dharmas; the stronger thesis of **genetic division from prior division** is the Organon hypothesis to be tested against the subsequent architecture of the Kośa.

This is only a local contact point. The complete logic of `anuśaya` belongs to the later chapter explicitly cited by Vasubandhu.
