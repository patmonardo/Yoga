# VAK_1.47_bhasya

## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> tathā śrotraṃ trayāṇāṃ tu sarvam eva svabhūmikam /
>
> kāyavijñānam adharasvabhūmi aniyataṃ manaḥ // VAkK_1.47 //

### Established Kārikā Translation

> Likewise the ear. But for the three, everything belongs to its own plane. Body-knowledge is of a lower or its own plane. Mind is indeterminate.

The corresponding Kārikā analysis is preserved in `VAK_1.47.md`.

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. The ear is governed by the same relational structure as the eye

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya says that the complete analysis already given for eye, visible form, and eye-knowledge should be applied likewise to:

```text
śrotra
    ear-domain

śabda
    sound-domain

śrotravijñāna
    auditory knowledge-domain
```

Thus:

> The ear is not of a plane lower than the body; sound is not of a plane higher than the ear; auditory knowledge is likewise not higher than the ear. But sound may stand higher, lower, or on the same plane relative to auditory knowledge, and both sound and auditory knowledge may stand in all three relations relative to the body.

This is not a claim about geometric height. It concerns relative `bhūmi`, the level or plane of existence and meditative attainment.

### 2. The three contact faculties normally remain within their own plane

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> For the three—nose, tongue, and body—everything belongs to its own plane.

The Bhāṣya expands “everything” as:

```text
faculty
object
knowledge
```

For smell, taste, and touch, the ordinary rule is therefore:

```text
ghrāṇa + gandha + ghrāṇavijñāna
    = same plane

jihvā + rasa + jihvāvijñāna
    = same plane

kāya + spraṣṭavya + kāyavijñāna
    = same plane
```

This follows their contact-character. Unlike eye and ear, these faculties do not ordinarily operate across distinct planes.

### 3. The text marks a general rule, then introduces an exception

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya explicitly calls the preceding statement an `utsarga`, a general rule. It then introduces an `apavāda`, an exception:

> Body-knowledge may belong to a lower plane or to its own plane.

This distinction is methodological. The Kośa does not treat its classifications as flat absolutes; it states a general law and then identifies the case that does not fit it without qualification.

### 4. Body and tangible object remain own-plane, but body-knowledge can differ

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The body-faculty and tangible object are always own-plane:

```text
kāyadhātu
spraṣṭavya
    = svabhūmika
```

But body-knowledge varies according to the being in whom it arises:

```text
beings born in kāmadhātu or first dhyāna
    → body-knowledge of their own plane

beings born in second and higher dhyānas
    → body-knowledge of a lower plane
```

The reason is that the higher dhyānas lack an autonomous body-knowledge proper to their own plane. Bodily knowledge therefore operates through the lower level available for tactile cognition.

### 5. Mind is `aniyata`, not fixed

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

> Mind is indeterminate.

The Bhāṣya explains this relationally:

```text
sometimes manas is on the same plane
    as body, mental knowledge, and dharmas

sometimes manas is on a higher plane

sometimes manas is on a lower plane
```

`Aniyata` therefore does not mean chaotic or without law. It means that mind has no single invariant plane-relation comparable to the stricter sensory rules.

### 6. Mind can operate across planes in absorption and rebirth

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Even when the body belongs to one of five planes, mind and its associated factors can belong to every plane as appropriate during:

```text
samāpatti
    meditative attainment

upapatti
    rebirth or mode of arising
```

Vasubandhu defers the full treatment to the chapter on meditative attainments. He explicitly closes this digression because the doctrinal utility here is limited and a full explanation would impose too great a textual burden.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Question:
How do the remaining faculties, objects, and knowledges relate by plane?

Step 1:
Apply the eye-model to the ear.

Result:
Ear constrains sound and auditory knowledge as eye constrains visible form and eye-knowledge.

Step 2:
State the general rule for smell, taste, and touch.

Result:
Faculty, object, and knowledge are normally own-plane.

Step 3:
Introduce an exception.

Result:
Body and tangible object remain own-plane,
but body-knowledge may be own-plane or lower-plane.

Step 4:
Explain the exception through higher meditative rebirth.

Result:
A being born above the first dhyāna uses lower-plane body-knowledge.

Step 5:
Classify mind.

Result:
Mind is not fixed to one plane-relation.
It may be equal, higher, or lower relative to body, mental knowledge, and dharmas.

Step 6:
Defer the complete account.

Result:
Cross-plane mental operation belongs properly to the later analysis of samāpatti and rebirth.
```

## Translation Consequences

### 1. `Bhūmi`

The present unit confirms that `bhūmi` should not be rendered simply as spatial “level.” It denotes a structured existential and meditative plane:

```text
bhūmi
    = plane of existence
    = meditative level
    = causal-operational range associated with that level
```

Preferred translation in this sequence:

> plane

with explicit clarification that this is not geometric altitude.

### 2. `Aniyata`

“Indeterminate” may misleadingly suggest absence of determination. Here it means “not fixed” with respect to one plane-relation.

Preferred working rendering:

> not fixed by plane

or, where compactness is required:

> plane-indeterminate

### 3. `Manas`

The text does not identify manas with a transcendent subject. It identifies a functional domain whose relation to planes varies according to meditative attainment and rebirth.

### Revised Philosophical Translation

> The auditory domain follows the same plane-structure as the visual domain. For smell, taste, and touch, faculty, object, and knowledge normally belong to the same plane. Body-knowledge, however, may belong either to its own plane or to a lower one. Mind is not fixed to a single plane-relation.

## Doctrinal Determination

This verse does not derive planes from space and time. Nor does it identify planes with space and time. What the Bhāṣya directly gives is a system of lawful relations among:

```text
birth-plane
meditative plane
faculty
object
knowledge
```

The relation is not purely spatial because a being born in a higher dhyāna may possess a body on that plane while tactile knowledge functions through a lower plane. Nor is it purely temporal, although rebirth and attainment introduce temporal transitions.

A cautious formulation is therefore:

> A `bhūmi` is an ordered field of possible embodiment, objecthood, knowledge, and meditative operation.

Space and time may be conditions through which such relations are manifested, but the present Bhāṣya passage does not reduce `bhūmi` to either one or explicitly derive it from their reciprocity.

The most significant asymmetry is:

```text
body and tangible object
    remain own-plane

body-knowledge
    may operate from a lower plane
```

Thus knowledge need not share the birth-plane of the embodied being. Plane-relation belongs to the causal and functional architecture of cognition, not merely to location.

---

## Logical Determination

The sensory system now divides into three relational patterns:

```text
eye and ear
    cross-plane object possibilities
    under faculty constraints

smell, taste, touch
    normally own-plane contact relations

mind
    no fixed plane-relation
```

But body-knowledge reveals that even the contact system is not absolutely closed:

```text
body-faculty
    own-plane

tangible object
    own-plane

body-knowledge
    own-plane or lower-plane
```

This introduces a crucial separation among:

```text
plane of embodiment
plane of object
plane of knowing
```

They are coordinated, but they are not simply identical.

---

## Organon Contact Point

A useful but provisional Organon hypothesis is:

```text
space
    orders coexistence and external relation

time
    orders succession and transition

bhūmi
    orders the admissible reciprocity of embodiment,
    object-field, and knowledge-operation
```

On this reading, space and time are not themselves planes. Their reciprocity may contribute to the manifestation of a plane, but the Kośa’s `bhūmi` is more determinate: it is a lawful domain within which specific faculties, objects, and knowledges can or cannot operate.

This remains an Organon reconstruction rather than a claim made explicitly by Vasubandhu.

The Bhāṣya’s own result is firmer:

> A plane is known through the admissible relations it permits among body, faculty, object, knowledge, meditative attainment, and rebirth.
