# VAK_1.06 Bhāṣya

## The Two Cessations

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.06.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya sharpens or corrects the first-pass interpretation, that decision is recorded here for later Kārikā revision.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> pratisaṃkhyānirodho yo visaṃyogaḥ pṛthak pṛthak /
>
> utpādātyantavighno 'nyo nirodho 'pratisaṃkhyayā // VAkK_1.06 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Cessation through discriminative consideration is the disjunction from the sāsrava dharmas, separately in each case. The other cessation, not attained through discriminative consideration, is the absolute obstruction of arising.

The first half defines a liberation obtained through a particular form of prajñā. The second defines a different kind of non-arising produced not by insight, but by a deficiency of conditions.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the definition:

> *yaḥ sāsravair dharmair visaṃyogaḥ sa pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ*

and continues through the fourfold classification of which dharmas obtain one cessation, the other, both, or neither.

Its movement is:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha defined as disjunction
    ↓
pratisaṃkhyā defined as a special prajñā
    ↓
disjunctions distinguished one by one
    ↓
objection concerning “without a homogeneous cause”
    ↓
apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha defined
    ↓
condition-deficiency explained by an example
    ↓
fourfold classification of the two cessations
```

The Bhāṣya therefore treats the verse not as two short dictionary definitions, but as a complete contrast between two grounds of non-arising.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. Pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is disjunction

> *yaḥ sāsravair dharmair visaṃyogaḥ sa pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ.*

### Translation

> The disjunction from sāsrava dharmas is cessation through discriminative consideration.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The object of disjunction is not dharma in general. It is specifically the field of sāsrava dharmas established in VAK 1.04.

```text
sāsrava dharmas
    objects of bondage
        ↓
visaṃyoga
    disjunction from them
        ↓
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
```

This makes the first cessation directly continuous with the earlier division of the Dharma-field.

---

### B. What pratisaṃkhyā means

> *duḥkhādīnām āryasatyānāṃ pratisaṃkhyānaṃ pratisaṃkhyā prajñāviśeṣas tena prāpyo nirodhaḥ.*

### Translation

> Pratisaṃkhyā is the discriminative consideration of the Noble Truths beginning with suffering; it is a specific form of prajñā. The cessation attained through it is pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya adds three decisive determinations:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā
    = consideration of the Four Noble Truths
    = a specific prajñā
    = the means through which cessation is attained
```

This is more precise than simply calling it “analytic insight.” It is discriminative knowing ordered to the Noble Truths and effective as a path-practice.

The relation is instrumental:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā
    special prajñā
        ↓
prāpya nirodha
    cessation attained by it
```

---

### C. The compound formation

> *pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ madhyapadalopāt gorathavat.*

### Translation

> It is called “pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha” through omission of the middle member, as in the expression “ox-cart.”

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The full relation is understood as:

```text
pratisaṃkhyayā prāpyo nirodhaḥ
    cessation attained through discriminative consideration
```

The compressed compound omits the middle relation, just as a compound such as “ox-cart” leaves its exact relation to be supplied.

This grammatical note matters because the cessation is not itself identical with the act of discrimination. It is the result attained through that act.

---

### D. Is there one cessation for all sāsrava dharmas?

> *kiṃ punar eka eva sarveṣāṃ sāsravāṇāṃ dharmāṇāṃ pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ? nety āha. kiṃ tarhi? pṛthak pṛthak.*

### Translation

> Is there then a single pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha for all sāsrava dharmas? No. What then? Separately, one by one.

**Voice:** Objector, then Vasubandhu

The phrase `pṛthak pṛthak` rejects one undifferentiated cessation common to all bondage.

```text
not:
    one universal disjunction from all sāsrava dharmas

but:
    distinct disjunction corresponding to each conjunction
```

---

### E. As many disjunctions as conjunctions

> *yāvanti hi saṃyogadravyāṇi tāvanti visaṃyogadravyāṇi.*

### Translation

> For as many real conjunctions as there are, so many real disjunctions there are.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya presents the relation with exact numerical reciprocity:

```text
saṃyoga-dravya
    a determinate conjunction

visaṃyoga-dravya
    its determinate disjunction
```

Disjunction is not a vague state called “release.” It is articulated according to the actual plurality of bonds from which release occurs.

---

### F. Why distinct cessations are necessary

> *anyathā hi duḥkhadarśanaheyakleśanirodhasākṣātkaraṇāt sarvakleśanirodhasākṣātkriyā prasajyeta.*

> *sati caivaṃ śeṣapratipakṣabhāvanāvaiyarthyaṃ syāt.*

### Translation

> Otherwise, from realizing the cessation of the defilements abandoned through seeing suffering, the realization of the cessation of all defilements would follow. If that were so, cultivating the remaining counteragents would be pointless.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is the practical proof for plurality.

If cessation were one indivisible reality, then the first realization of any part of cessation would amount to the realization of all cessation. The remaining path would have no work left to perform.

```text
one indivisible cessation
    ↓
cessation of one class realized
    ↓
all cessation automatically realized
    ↓
remaining counteragent-cultivation becomes useless
```

Because the path does require further cultivation, the disjunctions must be distinct.

---

### G. “Without a homogeneous cause”

> *yat tarhy uktam “asabhāgo nirodha” ity asya ko 'rthaḥ?*

> *nāsya kaścit sabhāgahetur asti nāsau kasyacid ity ayam asya vākyasyārtho na tu nāsya kaścit sabhāgo 'stīti.*

### Translation

> What then is the meaning of the statement, “cessation is without a homogeneous cause”? It means that cessation has no homogeneous cause and is itself the homogeneous cause of nothing. It does not mean that cessation has no homogeneous counterpart.

**Voice:** Objector, then Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya blocks a possible misunderstanding of `asabhāga`.

It does **not** deny plurality or correspondence among cessations. It denies causal participation in the category of homogeneous cause.

```text
asabhāga
    does not mean:
        having no similar counterpart

asabhāga
    means:
        having no sabhāga-hetu
        and serving as sabhāga-hetu for nothing
```

The distinction protects both claims:

```text
cessations are many and determinate
    while
cessation is unconditioned and non-generative
```

---

### H. The other cessation

> *anāgatānāṃ dharmāṇām utpādasyātyantaṃ vighnabhūto visaṃyogād yo 'nyo nirodhaḥ so 'pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ.*

### Translation

> The cessation other than disjunction, which constitutes an absolute obstruction to the arising of future dharmas, is apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The contrast is explicit:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    visaṃyoga
    disjunction from sāsrava dharmas

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    utpādātyanta-vighna
    absolute obstruction of the arising of future dharmas
```

The second cessation is not another kind of liberating disjunction. It concerns dharmas whose arising becomes impossible.

---

### I. It is not attained through prajñā

> *na hy asau pratisaṃkhyayā labhyate. kiṃ tarhi? pratyayavaikalyāt.*

### Translation

> It is not attained through discriminative consideration. How then is it attained? Through deficiency of conditions.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This sentence gives the governing distinction between the two cessations:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    attained through a special prajñā

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    obtained through deficiency of conditions
```

`pratyaya-vaikalya` means that the complete set of required conditions is no longer available. Because the causal opportunity is irrecoverably incomplete, the dharma cannot arise.

---

### J. The example of missed sensory objects

> *yathaikarūpavyāsaktacakṣurmanaso yāni rūpāṇi śabdagandharasaspraṣṭavyāni cātyayante tadālambanaiḥ pañcabhir vijñānakāyair na śakyaṃ punar utpattum.*

> *na hi te saty atītaṃ viṣayam ālambayitum.*

### Translation

> For example, when the eye and mind are fixed upon one visible form, the other visible forms, sounds, odors, tastes, and tangibles that pass by cannot afterward produce the five classes of sensory knowledge that would have taken them as their object-support. For those cognitions cannot arise later taking a past object as their object.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The example is concrete.

A moment of sensory availability passes while attention is occupied elsewhere. Once the object has become past, the sensory cognition that required it as a present object can no longer arise.

```text
present sensory object
    + appropriate faculty and attention
        → possible sensory knowledge

object passes without cognition arising
    ↓
required condition is permanently lost
    ↓
that particular sensory knowledge cannot arise
```

This is not liberation through insight. It is non-arising because the relevant causal occasion has expired.

---

### K. Apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha through condition-deficiency

> *ataḥ sa teṣām apratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ pratyayavaikalyāt prāpyate.*

### Translation

> Therefore their apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is obtained through deficiency of conditions.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The absolute character of the obstruction does not mean that a new force actively destroys the dharma. It means that its required conditions can never again be assembled for that particular arising.

```text
not active destruction
    but
irreversible incompleteness of conditions
```

---

## 4. The Fourfold Classification

The Bhāṣya closes by asking how the two cessations are distributed.

### A. Pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha only

> *santi te dharmā yeṣāṃ pratisaṃkhyānirodha eva labhyate.*

> *tadyathātītapratyutpannotpattidharmāṇāṃ sāsravāṇām.*

### Translation

> There are dharmas for which only pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is obtained: for example, past, present, and future sāsrava dharmas whose arising is still possible.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

These require liberating disjunction. Their cessation is not simply secured by a missing causal opportunity.

---

### B. Apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha only

> *santi yeṣām apratisaṃkhyānirodha eva.*

> *tadyathānutpattidharmāṇām anāsravasaṃskṛtānām.*

### Translation

> There are dharmas for which only apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is obtained: for example, future anāsrava conditioned dharmas that are incapable of arising.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Because they are anāsrava, no liberating disjunction from them is needed. Yet because they will never arise, they possess cessation through condition-deficiency.

---

### C. Both cessations

> *santi te yeṣām ubhayam.*

> *tadyathā sāsravāṇām anutpattidharmāṇām.*

### Translation

> There are dharmas for which both are obtained: for example, sāsrava dharmas incapable of arising.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

They are objects of disjunction because they are sāsrava, and they also never arise because their conditions are deficient.

---

### D. Neither cessation

> *santi yeṣāṃ nobhayam.*

> *tadyathātītapratyutpannotpattidharmāṇām anāsravāṇām.*

### Translation

> There are dharmas for which neither is obtained: for example, past, present, and future anāsrava dharmas whose arising is possible.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

They require no disjunction because they are anāsrava, and their arising is not absolutely obstructed.

---

## 5. Argument Summary

```text
The sāsrava field contains determinate bonds.
        ↓
Pratisaṃkhyā is a special prajñā directed to the Noble Truths.
        ↓
Through it, distinct disjunctions are attained.
        ↓
Each bond has its corresponding release.
        ↓
This plurality preserves the necessity of the successive path.

A second cessation has another ground.
        ↓
A future dharma loses indispensable conditions.
        ↓
Its particular arising becomes permanently impossible.
        ↓
This is apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha.
```

The central contrast is:

```text
liberating non-arising through prajñā
    ≠
causal non-arising through condition-deficiency
```

## 6. New Technical Terms

### **āryasatya-pratisaṃkhyāna**

- **Provisional meaning:** discriminative consideration of the Noble Truths
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** specifies the content and operation of pratisaṃkhyā

### **prajñāviśeṣa**

- **Provisional meaning:** a specific or distinct form of prajñā
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** identifies pratisaṃkhyā as a determinate cognitive power rather than generic analysis

### **madhyapadalopa**

- **Provisional meaning:** omission of the middle member of a compound
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** grammatical explanation of `pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha`

### **saṃyogadravya**

- **Provisional meaning:** a real determinate conjunction or bond
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** establishes the plurality corresponding to distinct disjunctions

### **visaṃyogadravya**

- **Provisional meaning:** a real determinate disjunction
- **Status:** the Kārikā has `visaṃyoga`, but not this expanded form
- **Function:** clarifies that cessation is articulated bond by bond

### **nirodhasākṣātkriyā**

- **Provisional meaning:** realization or direct actualization of cessation
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains why a single undifferentiated cessation would collapse the successive path

### **pratipakṣabhāvanā**

- **Provisional meaning:** cultivation of counteragents
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** practical evidence for the plurality of cessations

### **sabhāgahetu**

- **Provisional meaning:** homogeneous cause
- **Status:** absent from this Kārikā; developed later
- **Function:** distinguishes causal non-participation from lack of similar instances

### **pratyayavaikalya**

- **Provisional meaning:** deficiency or incompleteness of conditions
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** ground of apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha

### **anutpattidharma**

- **Provisional meaning:** a dharma having the status of non-arising or incapable of arising
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** classifies dharmas that obtain apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha

### **catuṣkoṭika**

- **Provisional meaning:** fourfold classification
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** distributes the two cessations across four possible cases

## 8. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass file is broadly correct, but several phrases should be sharpened during the later revision.

### A. `pratisaṃkhyā`

The phrase should not be left as generic “discrimination” alone.

```text
pratisaṃkhyā
    = a specific prajñā
    = discriminative consideration of the Noble Truths
```

### B. `pṛthak pṛthak`

This means more than gradual removal “one determination at a time.” It states an ontological plurality:

```text
as many conjunctions
    as many disjunctions
```

### C. `apratisaṃkhyayā`

The second cessation is not produced by an unspecified “blockage.” Its precise ground is:

```text
pratyayavaikalya
    irreversible deficiency of conditions
```

### D. `utpādātyantavighna`

The obstruction concerns a particular future arising. It should not be rendered as though a general force suppresses manifestation.

The Bhāṣya-informed translation should therefore emphasize:

> Cessation through discriminative consideration is the distinct disjunction from each sāsrava bond. The other cessation, not attained through such consideration, is the absolute obstruction of a future arising through deficiency of its conditions.

## 9. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya establishes two logically different forms of non-arising:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    mediated by knowing
    generated through the Path
    distinct release from determinate bondage

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    mediated by causal incompleteness
    not generated through insight
    impossibility of a determinate occurrence
```

The strongest contact point is not simply “release versus absence.” It is the distinction between:

```text
non-arising grounded in rational discrimination
    and
non-arising grounded in the failed completeness of conditions
```

This remains provisional. The present task is to preserve Vasubandhu’s own distinction before translating it into the broader Organon system.
