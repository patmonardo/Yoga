# VAK_1.06 Bhāṣya

## The Two Cessations

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.06.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya sharpens or corrects the first-pass interpretation, that decision is recorded here for later Kārikā revision.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> pratisaṃkhyānirodho yo visaṃyogaḥ pṛthak pṛthak /
>
> utpādātyantavighno 'nyo nirodho 'pratisaṃkhyayā // VAkK_1.06 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Cessation through discriminative consideration is the disjunction from sāsrava dharmas, separately in each case. The other cessation, not attained through discriminative consideration, is the absolute obstruction of arising.

The first half defines a liberation attained through a specific form of prajñā. The second defines a different kind of non-arising produced not through insight, but through deficiency of conditions.

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with:

> *yaḥ sāsravair dharmair visaṃyogaḥ sa pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ*

and extends through the fourfold classification of dharmas for which one cessation, the other, both, or neither is obtained.

Its movement is:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha defined as disjunction
    ↓
pratisaṃkhyā defined as a special prajñā
    ↓
disjunctions distinguished one by one
    ↓
objection concerning “without a homogeneous cause”
    ↓
apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha defined
    ↓
condition-deficiency explained through sensory cognition
    ↓
fourfold classification of the two cessations
```

The Bhāṣya therefore treats the verse not as two short dictionary definitions, but as a complete contrast between two grounds of cessation.

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. Pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is disjunction

> *yaḥ sāsravair dharmair visaṃyogaḥ sa pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ.*

### Translation

> The disjunction from sāsrava dharmas is cessation through discriminative consideration.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The object of disjunction is not dharma in general. It is specifically the field of sāsrava dharmas established in VAK 1.04.

```text
sāsrava dharmas
    field of conjunction and bondage
        ↓
visaṃyoga
    disjunction from them
        ↓
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
```

The first cessation is therefore directly continuous with the earlier practical division of the Dharma-field.

---

### B. What pratisaṃkhyā means

> *duḥkhādīnām āryasatyānāṃ pratisaṃkhyānaṃ pratisaṃkhyā prajñāviśeṣas tena prāpyo nirodhaḥ.*

### Translation

> Pratisaṃkhyā is the discriminative consideration of the Noble Truths beginning with suffering; it is a specific form of prajñā. The cessation attained through it is pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya adds three decisive determinations:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā
    = discriminative consideration of the Noble Truths
    = a specific prajñā
    = the means through which cessation is attained
```

It is therefore more exact than the general expression “analytic insight.” It is a discriminative cognition ordered to the Four Noble Truths and effective as Path-practice.

The relation is instrumental:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā
    special prajñā
        ↓
prāpya nirodha
    cessation attained through it
```

---

### C. The compound formation

> *pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ madhyapadalopāt gorathavat.*

### Translation

> It is called “pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha” through omission of the middle member, as in the expression “ox-cart.”

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The complete relation is:

```text
pratisaṃkhyayā prāpyo nirodhaḥ
    cessation attained through discriminative consideration
```

The grammatical note prevents identification of cessation with the act of discrimination itself. The prajñā is the means; cessation is what is attained through it.

---

### D. Is there one cessation for all sāsrava dharmas?

> *kiṃ punar eka eva sarveṣāṃ sāsravāṇāṃ dharmāṇāṃ pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ?*

> *nety āha. kiṃ tarhi? pṛthak pṛthak.*

### Translation

> Is there then a single pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha for all sāsrava dharmas? No. What then? Separately, one by one.

**Voice:** Objector, then Vasubandhu

The phrase `pṛthak pṛthak` rejects one undifferentiated cessation common to every bond.

```text
not:
    one universal disjunction from all sāsrava dharmas

but:
    distinct disjunction corresponding to each conjunction
```

This is a major practical determination. Liberation is not presented as one abstract leap outside the conditioned field. It is articulated according to the actual plurality of conjunctions that constitute bondage.

---

### E. As many disjunctions as conjunctions

> *yāvanti hi saṃyogadravyāṇi tāvanti visaṃyogadravyāṇi.*

### Translation

> For as many real conjunctions as there are, so many real disjunctions there are.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya states an exact reciprocity:

```text
saṃyoga-dravya
    a determinate conjunction

visaṃyoga-dravya
    its determinate disjunction
```

Disjunction is not merely a general condition called “release.” Each bond has its corresponding release.

---

### F. Why distinct cessations are necessary

> *anyathā hi duḥkhadarśanaheyakleśanirodhasākṣātkaraṇāt sarvakleśanirodhasākṣātkriyā prasajyeta.*

> *sati caivaṃ śeṣapratipakṣabhāvanāvaiyarthyaṃ syāt.*

### Translation

> Otherwise, from realizing the cessation of the defilements abandoned through seeing suffering, realization of the cessation of all defilements would follow. If that were so, cultivation of the remaining counteragents would be pointless.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This is the practical proof for plurality.

```text
one indivisible cessation
    ↓
cessation of one class realized
    ↓
all cessation automatically realized
    ↓
remaining cultivation becomes useless
```

Because the Path requires further cultivation after the first realization, the disjunctions must be distinct.

The Bhāṣya thereby links ontology to practice: the plurality of cessation-dharmas is established through the articulated work of the Path.

---

### G. “Cessation is without a homogeneous cause”

> *yat tarhy uktam “asabhāgo nirodha” ity asya ko 'rthaḥ?*

> *nāsya kaścit sabhāgahetur asti nāsau kasyacid ity ayam asya vākyasyārtho na tu nāsya kaścit sabhāgo 'stīti.*

### Translation

> What then is the meaning of the statement, “cessation is without a homogeneous cause”? It means that cessation has no homogeneous cause and is itself the homogeneous cause of nothing. It does not mean that cessation has no homogeneous counterpart.

**Voice:** Objector, then Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya blocks a possible misunderstanding of `asabhāga`.

It does not deny multiplicity or correspondence among cessations. It denies participation in the causal relation called homogeneous cause.

```text
asabhāga does not mean:
    having no similar counterpart

asabhāga means:
    having no sabhāga-hetu
    and serving as sabhāga-hetu for nothing
```

This protects both claims:

```text
cessations are many and determinate
    while
cessation is unconditioned and non-generative
```

---

### H. The other cessation

> *anāgatānāṃ dharmāṇām utpādasyātyantaṃ vighnabhūto visaṃyogād yo 'nyo nirodhaḥ so 'pratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ.*

### Translation

> The cessation other than disjunction, which constitutes an absolute obstruction to the arising of future dharmas, is apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The contrast is exact:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    visaṃyoga
    disjunction from sāsrava dharmas

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    utpādātyanta-vighna
    absolute obstruction of the arising of future dharmas
```

The second cessation is not another kind of liberating disjunction. It concerns a particular arising that has become impossible.

---

### I. It is not attained through prajñā

> *na hy asau pratisaṃkhyayā labhyate. kiṃ tarhi? pratyayavaikalyāt.*

### Translation

> It is not attained through discriminative consideration. How then is it attained? Through deficiency of conditions.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

This gives the governing difference:

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    attained through a special prajñā

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    obtained through deficiency of conditions
```

`pratyaya-vaikalya` means that the required set of conditions is incomplete. Because the causal opportunity is irrecoverably deficient, that dharma cannot arise.

---

### J. The example of missed sensory objects

> *yathaikarūpavyāsaktacakṣurmanaso yāni rūpāṇi śabdagandharasaspraṣṭavyāni cātyayante tadālambanaiḥ pañcabhir vijñānakāyair na śakyaṃ punar utpattum.*

> *na hi te saty atītaṃ viṣayam ālambayitum.*

### Translation

> For example, when the eye and mind are fixed upon one visible form, the other visible forms, sounds, odors, tastes, and tangibles that pass by cannot afterward produce the five classes of sensory consciousness that would have taken them as their object. For those cognitions cannot later arise taking a past object as their object.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The example is concrete and practical.

```text
present sensory object
    + appropriate faculty and attention
        → possible sensory consciousness

object passes without that consciousness arising
    ↓
required condition is permanently lost
    ↓
that particular consciousness cannot arise
```

This is not liberation through knowledge. It is non-arising because the relevant causal occasion has expired.

---

### K. Apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha through condition-deficiency

> *ataḥ sa teṣām apratisaṃkhyānirodhaḥ pratyayavaikalyāt prāpyate.*

### Translation

> Therefore their apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is obtained through deficiency of conditions.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The absolute obstruction does not mean that a new force actively destroys the dharma. It means that the complete conditions for that particular arising can never again be assembled.

```text
not active annihilation
    but
irreversible deficiency of conditions
```

---

## 4. The Fourfold Classification

The Bhāṣya closes by distributing the two cessations across four cases.

### A. Pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha only

> *santi te dharmā yeṣāṃ pratisaṃkhyānirodha eva labhyate.*

> *tadyathātītapratyutpannotpattidharmāṇāṃ sāsravāṇām.*

### Translation

> There are dharmas for which only pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is obtained: past, present, and future sāsrava dharmas whose arising is possible.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

These require disjunction because they are sāsrava. They do not obtain apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha merely through condition-deficiency.

---

### B. Apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha only

> *santi yeṣām apratisaṃkhyānirodha eva.*

> *tadyathānutpattidharmāṇām anāsravasaṃskṛtānām.*

### Translation

> There are dharmas for which only apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is obtained: future anāsrava conditioned dharmas incapable of arising.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Because they are anāsrava, no liberating disjunction from them is required. Because they cannot arise, cessation through condition-deficiency is obtained.

---

### C. Both cessations

> *santi te yeṣām ubhayam.*

> *tadyathā sāsravāṇām anutpattidharmāṇām.*

### Translation

> There are dharmas for which both are obtained: sāsrava dharmas incapable of arising.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

They are objects of disjunction because they are sāsrava, and their arising is also absolutely obstructed.

---

### D. Neither cessation

> *santi yeṣāṃ nobhayam.*

> *tadyathātītapratyutpannotpattidharmāṇām anāsravāṇām.*

### Translation

> There are dharmas for which neither is obtained: past, present, and future anāsrava dharmas whose arising is possible.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

They require no disjunction because they are anāsrava, and their arising is not absolutely obstructed.

---

## 5. Practical Structure of the Two Cessations

The two cessations must not be collapsed into a general notion of absence.

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    concerns bondage
    is attained through prajñā
    is disjunction
    is articulated one bond at a time

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    concerns possible arising
    is obtained through condition-deficiency
    is absolute non-arising
    does not require liberating insight
```

The first is a Path-result. The second is a causal impossibility.

This distinction is especially important for learning `sarvadharma`. The same surface description—“this does not arise”—can have completely different grounds and therefore a completely different doctrinal status.

---

## 6. Argument Summary

```text
Sāsrava dharmas constitute determinate conjunctions.
        ↓
Pratisaṃkhyā is a specific prajñā directed to the Noble Truths.
        ↓
Through it, distinct disjunctions are attained.
        ↓
Each conjunction has a corresponding disjunction.
        ↓
This plurality is required by the progressive cultivation of the Path.
        ↓
Pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is therefore liberating cessation through knowledge.
        ↓
Apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is different.
        ↓
It is the absolute obstruction of a future arising.
        ↓
Its ground is deficiency of conditions, not discriminative prajñā.
        ↓
The two cessations can apply separately, jointly, or not at all,
depending on whether a dharma is sāsrava and whether it can arise.
```

The unity of the passage is:

```text
cessation must be known through its ground
```

## 7. New Technical Terms

### **pratisaṃkhyā**

- **Provisional meaning:** discriminative consideration of the Noble Truths; a specific prajñā
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā, explicitly defined only in the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** means through which liberating cessation is attained

### **prajñā-viśeṣa**

- **Provisional meaning:** a specific or distinctive form of prajñā
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** classifies pratisaṃkhyā as a precise cognition rather than generic intelligence

### **visaṃyoga**

- **Provisional meaning:** disjunction from sāsrava dharmas
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā and precisely delimited by the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** defining character of pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha

### **saṃyoga-dravya**

- **Provisional meaning:** a determinate conjunction or bond
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** establishes the plurality of corresponding disjunctions

### **visaṃyoga-dravya**

- **Provisional meaning:** a determinate disjunction
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** identifies each liberation as corresponding to a particular bond

### **duḥkha-darśana-heya**

- **Provisional meaning:** abandoned through seeing the truth of suffering
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā; anticipates later Path classifications
- **Function:** proves that cessation is multiple through the progressive stages of abandonment

### **pratipakṣa-bhāvanā**

- **Provisional meaning:** cultivation of a counteragent
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā; `pratipakṣa` encountered in VAK 1.01 Bhāṣya
- **Function:** practical evidence that distinct bonds require distinct disjunctions

### **sabhāga-hetu**

- **Provisional meaning:** homogeneous cause
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** clarifies the technical meaning of the claim that cessation is `asabhāga`

### **utpādātyanta-vighna**

- **Provisional meaning:** absolute obstruction of arising
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā and expanded by the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** defining character of apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha

### **pratyaya-vaikalya**

- **Provisional meaning:** deficiency or incompleteness of conditions
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** ground through which apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha is obtained

### **anutpatti-dharma**

- **Provisional meaning:** having the determination of not arising
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā; related to `punar-anutpatti-dharmatva` in VAK 1.01 Bhāṣya
- **Function:** distinguishes dharmas subject to apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha

### **catuṣkoṭika**

- **Provisional meaning:** fourfold classification
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** distributes the two cessations into only, only, both, and neither

## 9. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass translation captures the broad distinction, but two refinements should be recorded.

### A. `pratisaṃkhyā` should not be rendered merely as generic discrimination

The Bhāṣya defines it as:

> a specific prajñā consisting in discriminative consideration of the Noble Truths beginning with suffering.

A concise Kārikā translation may still use “discriminative consideration,” but the technical note should preserve its Path-specific meaning.

### B. `pṛthak pṛthak` applies to determinate disjunctions

It does not merely mean that one performs analysis step by step. It means:

```text
as many determinate conjunctions,
so many determinate disjunctions
```

### Recommended later revision

> Cessation through discriminative consideration is the disjunction from sāsrava dharmas, separately in each case. The other cessation, not through discriminative consideration, is the absolute obstruction of arising.

The original Kārikā file should remain unchanged until the deliberate revision pass.

## 10. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The text itself gives a practical determination more exact than a general logical contrast between mediation and immediacy.

```text
pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    negation through knowledge of the bond

apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha
    non-arising through absence of conditions
```

These are not two descriptions of the same negation.

The first is a genetic result of the Path:

```text
conjunction
    → discriminative prajñā
    → counteragent-cultivation
    → determinate disjunction
```

The second is a determination of the causal field:

```text
required conditions fail
    → the relevant arising becomes impossible
```

For the present Bhāṣya pass, the most important conclusion is therefore textual and practical:

```text
Do not ask only whether a dharma ceases.
Ask through what ground its cessation is established.
```

That question is a genuine instrument for comprehending `sarvadharma`.
