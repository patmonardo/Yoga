# VAK_1.08 Bhāṣya

## The Aggregates of Appropriation and the Names of the Sāsrava Field

This file records the Bhāṣya-pass study for `VAK_1.08.md`.

The Kārikā file remains unchanged in this commit. Where the Bhāṣya requires a correction or greater precision, that result is recorded here for a later Kārikā revision pass.

---

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

> ye sāsravā upādānaskandhās te saraṇā api /
>
> duḥkhaṃ samudayo loko dṛṣṭisthānaṃ bhavaś ca te // VAkK_1.08 //

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> Those aggregates that are with outflows are the aggregates of appropriation; they are also accompanied by conflict. They are suffering, origin, world, the station of views, and becoming.

The phrase `ye sāsravāḥ` qualifies the aggregates introduced in VAK 1.07. The verse therefore does not identify every aggregate without qualification as an aggregate of appropriation. It identifies the **sāsrava aggregates** as such.

The term `saraṇa` also requires correction. It is explained through `raṇa`, conflict or injury, and through the presence of the afflictions within the sāsrava field. It should not remain an unresolved expression such as “vulnerable course.”

---

## 2. Natural Bhāṣya Unit

The natural unit begins with the return to the conditioned dharmas:

> *ta eva punaḥ saṃskṛtā dharmāḥ...*

and concludes with:

> *ity ete sāsravāṇāṃ dharmāṇām anvarthaparyāyāḥ.*

The movement is:

```text
conditioned dharmas already identified as aggregates
    ↓
sāsrava aggregates identified as upādānaskandhas
    ↓
three derivations of “aggregate of appropriation”
    ↓
saraṇa explained through the conflictive afflictions
    ↓
five additional names of the same sāsrava field
    ↓
each name derived from its doctrinal function
```

The Bhāṣya is therefore not simply attaching a list of labels to the aggregates. It shows why each designation is appropriate.

---

## 3. Bhāṣya Analysis

### A. The same conditioned dharmas, now under the sāsrava determination

> *ta eva punaḥ saṃskṛtā dharmāḥ.*

### Translation

> Those very same conditioned dharmas are again under consideration.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

VAK 1.07 identified conditioned dharmas with the five aggregates. VAK 1.08 does not introduce a second aggregate system. It takes the same field and asks which aggregates belong to the contaminated domain.

```text
five aggregates
    ↓
when sāsrava
    ↓
aggregates of appropriation
```

The qualification matters because the Path includes conditioned dharmas that are anāsrava. Conditioned existence and appropriation are therefore not simply identical.

---

### B. Sāsrava aggregates are aggregates of appropriation

> *ye sāsravā upādānaskandhās te.*

### Translation

> Those that are with outflows are the aggregates of appropriation.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya immediately draws a logical consequence.

> *ataḥ kiṃ siddham? ya upādānaskandhāḥ skandhā api te syuḥ; evaṃ nopādānaskandhāḥ.*

### Translation

> What follows from this? Whatever are aggregates of appropriation are also aggregates; but not every aggregate is therefore an aggregate of appropriation.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The relation is one-directional:

```text
upādānaskandha
    ⊂
skandha
```

Every aggregate of appropriation is an aggregate, but the anāsrava conditioned aggregates are not aggregates of appropriation.

The first-pass formula:

```text
aggregate + outflow + appropriation
    = world of bondage
```

is useful as a summary, but the Bhāṣya is more exact. It does not first posit neutral aggregates and then add a separate event called appropriation. It explains several structural relations by which the sāsrava aggregates and appropriation belong together.

---

## 4. The Three Derivations of Upādānaskandha

### A. Produced from appropriation

> *tatra upādānāni kleśāḥ. tatsaṃbhūtatvād upādānaskandhāḥ, tṛṇatuṣāgnivat.*

### Translation

> Here, the appropriations are the afflictions. Because the aggregates arise from those appropriations, they are called aggregates of appropriation, like a fire produced from grass or chaff.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya first identifies:

```text
upādāna
    = kleśa
```

The aggregates may be called aggregates of appropriation because they are generated from afflictive appropriation.

The example is causal:

```text
grass or chaff
    → fire produced from it

appropriation / affliction
    → sāsrava aggregates produced from it
```

Here the genitive relation means **origin from**.

---

### B. Governed by appropriation

> *tadvidheyatvād vā rājapuruṣavat.*

### Translation

> Or they are so called because they are subject to appropriation, like the servants of a king.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The second derivation is not production but control or dependence.

```text
king
    → governs his servants

appropriation
    → governs the aggregates
```

The aggregates are `tadvidheya`: placed under the direction, power, or determination of appropriation.

This is an important refinement. Appropriation is not merely an attitude externally directed toward an already complete aggregate-complex. Within the sāsrava field, appropriation also governs the course and functioning of that aggregate-complex.

---

### C. Appropriation arises from the aggregates

> *upādānāni vā tebhyaḥ saṃbhavantīty upādānaskandhāḥ puṣpaphalavṛkṣavat.*

### Translation

> Or they are aggregates of appropriation because the appropriations arise from them, as flowers and fruits arise from a tree.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The third derivation reverses the causal direction:

```text
aggregates
    → appropriation arises from them
```

The example is:

```text
tree
    → flowers and fruit

sāsrava aggregates
    → further appropriations
```

The three derivations together form a reciprocal structure:

```text
appropriation produces the aggregates
appropriation governs the aggregates
appropriation arises from the aggregates
```

This explains how the sāsrava aggregate-field sustains itself. Affliction is both condition and product of the appropriated aggregate-process.

The Bhāṣya does not reduce `upādānaskandha` to the psychological act “I regard this as mine.” That act belongs to the field, but the term names a broader causal and governing relation between affliction and the aggregates.

---

### D. These are the sāsrava dharmas

> *ta eva sāsravā dharmā ucyante.*

### Translation

> These very dharmas are called sāsrava.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The three derivations do not define a new class apart from the sāsrava dharmas. They explain why the contaminated aggregates bear the additional name `upādānaskandha`.

```text
sāsrava aggregates
    = upādānaskandhas
```

The two names emphasize different sides:

| Name | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| `sāsrava` | the afflictions can settle and remain within them |
| `upādānaskandha` | their reciprocal relation to appropriation |

---

## 5. Saraṇa: Accompanied by Conflict

> *saraṇā api.*

### Translation

> They are also accompanied by conflict.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

> *raṇā hi kleśāḥ, ātmaparavyābādhanāt.*

### Translation

> For the afflictions are conflicts, because they injure oneself and others.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya derives `saraṇa` from `raṇa`.

```text
raṇa
    = conflict, injury, afflictive struggle

kleśa
    = raṇa
    because it harms oneself and others
```

The term therefore has an ethical and experiential force. The afflictions are not only errors in classification. They produce injury within the agent and in relation to others.

> *tadanuśayitatvāt saraṇāḥ, sāsravavat.*

### Translation

> Because those conflicts lie latent within them, the aggregates are called accompanied by conflict, just as they are called sāsrava.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The derivation parallels VAK 1.04:

```text
āsravas lie latent in the dharmas
    → sāsrava

raṇas, the afflictions, lie latent in them
    → saraṇa
```

The best provisional rendering is therefore:

> **saraṇa** — accompanied by conflict; containing the latent basis of afflictive injury.

“Conflict-laden” is also possible, but “accompanied by conflict” keeps the formal parallel with `sāsrava` visible.

## 6. The Five Further Names

> *punaḥ duḥkhaṃ samudayo loko dṛṣṭisthānaṃ bhavaś ca te.*

### Translation

> Again, they are suffering, origin, world, the station of views, and becoming.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

Each expression names the same sāsrava dharmas according to a distinct relation.

---

### A. Duḥkha: adverse to the noble ones

> *āryāṇāṃ pratikūlatvād duḥkham.*

### Translation

> They are suffering because they are contrary to the noble ones.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya does not explain `duḥkha` here simply through instability or painful feeling. It gives a path-relative determination:

```text
sāsrava dharmas
    are contrary to the āryas
        ↓
therefore they are duḥkha
```

`Pratikūla` means opposed, adverse, or running against. The field of appropriation is contrary to noble realization and to the direction of the Path.

The first-pass translation should therefore avoid presenting the definition here as merely:

> suffering because conditioned things are unstable.

That may be doctrinally relevant elsewhere, but it is not the explanation supplied in this passage.

---

### B. Samudaya: that from which suffering arises

> *samudety asmād duḥkham iti samudayaḥ.*

### Translation

> It is origin because suffering arises from it.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The relation is productive:

```text
sāsrava aggregate-field
    → suffering arises
```

The same field is both suffering and its origin under different aspects:

```text
as adverse experienced existence
    = duḥkha

as productive ground of further suffering
    = samudaya
```

This is not a contradiction. One and the same contaminated process can be an effect of prior causes and a cause of subsequent suffering.

---

### C. Loka: that which is destroyed or dissolves

> *lupyata iti lokaḥ.*

### Translation

> It is called world because it is destroyed or dissolves.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The Bhāṣya derives `loka` from the verbal root `lup`, to break, destroy, disappear, or dissolve.

The meaning here is not simply “the field of appearance.” It is the conditioned field characterized by dissolution.

```text
loka
    = that which is continually broken down
```

The world is named through perishability rather than through spatial totality.

---

### D. Dṛṣṭisthāna: where views stand through lying latent

> *dṛṣṭir asmiṃs tiṣṭhaty anuśayanād iti dṛṣṭisthānam.*

### Translation

> It is the station of views because a view stands within it by lying latent there.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The term is precise:

```text
dṛṣṭi
    view

sthāna
    station, standing-place, support

anuśayana
    latent persistence within the field
```

The sāsrava aggregates are not merely objects about which false views happen to be asserted. They are the field in which views establish and maintain their latent presence.

This continues the same structural language used for `sāsrava` and `saraṇa`:

```text
outflows lie latent there
conflicts lie latent there
views lie latent there
```

The contaminated aggregate-field is thus a support of persistence for the afflictive structures.

---

### E. Bhava: that which comes to be

> *bhavatīti bhavaḥ.*

### Translation

> It is becoming because it comes to be.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The derivation is deliberately simple:

```text
bhavati
    it becomes, comes into existence
        ↓
bhava
    becoming / existence
```

The name emphasizes the productive continuation of conditioned existence. The aggregates are not a static substance called “existence”; they are the recurrent coming-to-be of the sāsrava process.

---

## 7. Names Appropriate to Their Meanings

> *ity ete sāsravāṇāṃ dharmāṇām anvarthaparyāyāḥ.*

### Translation

> These are synonyms of the sāsrava dharmas whose names correspond to their meanings.

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

`Anvarthaparyāya` means that each synonym is not arbitrary. Each name follows from a real doctrinal function.

```text
upādānaskandha
    reciprocal production and governance by appropriation

saraṇa
    latent conflict and injury

duḥkha
    adverse to the noble ones

samudaya
    source from which suffering arises

loka
    that which dissolves

dṛṣṭisthāna
    station in which views persist latently

bhava
    that which repeatedly comes to be
```

The verse therefore gives a many-sided determination of one field. The terms are coextensive in this passage, but they are not semantically interchangeable without remainder.

## 8. Argument Summary

```text
conditioned dharmas
    = five aggregates
        ↓
those aggregates that are sāsrava
    = aggregates of appropriation
        ↓
appropriation produces them
appropriation governs them
appropriation arises from them
        ↓
the afflictions remain latent within them
        ↓
they are accompanied by conflict
        ↓
the same field is named according to its functions:
    suffering
    origin
    dissolving world
    station of views
    becoming
```

The central structure is reciprocal:

```text
kleśa / upādāna
    → produces and governs the aggregates

sāsrava aggregates
    → produce further kleśa / upādāna
```

This reciprocity explains the continuity of saṃsāra without requiring a permanent bearer behind the aggregate-process.

## 9. New Technical Terms

### **upādāna**

- **Provisional meaning:** appropriation; here explicitly identified with the afflictions
- **Status:** present in the Kārikā but substantially expanded in the Bhāṣya
- **Function:** causal source, governing power, and product of the sāsrava aggregates

### **tatsaṃbhūtatva**

- **Provisional meaning:** being produced from that
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** first derivation of `upādānaskandha`

### **tadvidheyatva**

- **Provisional meaning:** being governed by or subject to that
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** second derivation of `upādānaskandha`

### **tebhyaḥ saṃbhava**

- **Provisional meaning:** arising from those aggregates
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** third derivation of `upādānaskandha`; reverses the causal direction

### **raṇa**

- **Provisional meaning:** conflict, injury, afflictive struggle
- **Status:** introduced to explain `saraṇa`
- **Function:** identifies the kleśas by their injury to self and others

### **ātmaparavyābādhana**

- **Provisional meaning:** injury or affliction of oneself and others
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains why the kleśas are called conflicts

### **pratikūlatva**

- **Provisional meaning:** adversity, opposition, being contrary to
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** explains why sāsrava dharmas are called suffering in relation to the noble ones

### **anvarthaparyāya**

- **Provisional meaning:** a synonym whose expression follows its meaning
- **Status:** absent from the Kārikā
- **Function:** characterizes the doctrinal names as motivated rather than arbitrary

## 11. Kārikā Translation Decision

The first-pass file should later be revised in four places.

### A. Upādānaskandha

The rendering “aggregates of appropriation” is retained, but the note should make clear that appropriation is not merely an added subjective act. The Bhāṣya gives three relations:

```text
produced from appropriation
governed by appropriation
productive of appropriation
```

### B. Saraṇa

Current provisional wording such as “with conflict / vulnerable course” should be replaced by:

> **accompanied by conflict**

with the explanation that the kleśas injure self and others and lie latent in the aggregates.

### C. Loka

The first-pass philosophical rendering “field of appearance” is not the Bhāṣya’s derivation. Here `loka` is:

> that which dissolves or is destroyed.

### D. Duḥkha

The immediate explanation is not instability alone. The Bhāṣya states:

> They are suffering because they are contrary to the noble ones.

The Kārikā file should preserve this path-relative explanation during revision.

## 12. Provisional Organon Contact Point

The philosophically important result is not merely:

```text
aggregate + appropriation = bondage
```

The Bhāṣya presents a self-reproducing relation:

```text
appropriation
    → aggregate-process
    → further appropriation
```

The sāsrava field is therefore not an inert object subsequently misread by a subject. Its conditioned structure has become the bearer and producer of the afflictive relation itself.

This contact point should remain provisional. The textual result is secure: the Bhāṣya explains `upādānaskandha` through reciprocal causal and governing relations. A fuller comparison with the Organon should wait until the later account of appropriation, latent tendencies, and dependent arising has been studied.
