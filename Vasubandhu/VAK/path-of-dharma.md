# The Path of Dharma

## A First Essay on Yoga, Abhidharma, Vijñapti, and Avijñapti

The relation between Yoga and the *Abhidharmakośa* becomes clearer when neither is reduced to a school-position. Yoga provides a Principial System; the Kośa provides Dharmic Extension. The Kośa unfolds Dharma across its Domains, worlds, karmic processes, defilements, paths, knowledges, and attainments. Yoga rationally lifts that extension by asking for the Principle through which the whole can be generated, traversed, realized, and finally brought under absolute control.

This does not mean that Yoga simply supplies an external metaphysics for a Buddhist catalogue. The Kośa is already a Self-Enclosure of Dharma. Dharma differentiates itself, organizes its root Domains, tracks its own causal persistence, diagnoses its own distortion, constructs its own Path, and culminates in Knowledge and Samāpatti. Its apparent multiplicity is not an accidental heap. It is an Organic Unity.

Yoga enters at the point where that Organic Unity must become Self-realizing. In this sense, Yoga is the Rational lifting of the Kośa—and also its refutation. The refutation does not deny the Dharmic content. It denies that the Self-Enclosure of Dharma is sufficient to account for the Seer of the whole enclosure.

> The Kośa is Dharma extended as an Organic Unity.  
> Yoga is that Organic Unity raised into Principial Knowledge and Self-realization.

This relation gives us a new way to understand the Path of Dharma.

## 1. Dharma and Its Root Domains

The opening chapter of Abhidharma is the *Dhātunirdeśa*. This is philosophically appropriate because the Dhātus belong to Dharma. They should not be treated merely as elementary things or metaphysical particles. For our work, **Domain** is the stronger translation.

> The Dhātus are Dharma’s root Domains.

A Domain is a determinate field in which Dharma can be exhibited. The eye-Domain, visible-form Domain, and eye-consciousness Domain do not name three independent substances. Together they articulate a structured relation of access, display, and cognition.

The Dhātus can therefore be imagined as organized Lights: special screening powers through which Dharma is displayed in a determinate mode. Yet they are not passive rooms. They function more like Gods of manifestation. They distribute the equipment, establish the channel, gather the audience, and populate the theater with a configured world.

A Dhātu does not present Dharma absolutely. It makes one determinate Dharma-display possible.

This is why *vijñapti* and *avijñapti* arise as an early technical problem. The System must explain both how Dharma becomes actively exhibited and how it remains effective when no exhibition is occurring.

## 2. Dharma Is Supersensible

A Dharma is a Pure A Priori Rational Concept. It is not first produced by abstraction from appearances. Its truth is principial and supersensible.

This does not make Dharma a hidden object behind appearance. Dharma is No-thing when it is not exhibited: no current form, no sensible object, no present cognitive display. Yet its determination can persist.

The distinction is therefore not between a visible object and an invisible object. It is between two logical statuses of Dharma:

- **Vijñapti**: Dharma in active exhibition.
- **Avijñapti**: Dharma persisting without current exhibition.

The action can cease while its determination remains operative. This is why avijñapti becomes important in explanations of karma and vipāka. The deed is no longer displayed, but its Dharma has not ceased to determine a future result.

It would be misleading to imagine a tiny invisible karmic substance traveling through time. The stronger formulation is:

> The action has ceased as exhibition, but its Dharma has not ceased as determination.

Avijñapti therefore marks the possibility that supersensible Dharma can remain effective within a conditioned series without becoming another sensible item in that series.

## 3. The Path Is the Pure Conditioned

The Path is not an escape from conditioning. It is the **Pure Conditioned**: Sensibility in general organized rationally as a complete course of Dharma-display.

This is especially clear in Yoga. Aṣṭāṅga is not an arbitrary ladder of practices. It is an intensely Rational construction. Each limb establishes a condition required by the next. Agency is ordered, embodiment stabilized, vital mediation brought under law, external sensory governance suspended, a field determined, its inner movement developed, and its Meaning gathered.

The Path therefore drives us into Appearance. It returns us to every unresolved determination that still requires exhibition, discrimination, development, and realization.

> What has not been realized returns as Path.

This also clarifies the danger in the idea of a “Pathless Path.” Pathlessness may mean genuine completion: the Path has realized Sarva-Dharma and no longer needs to recur. But it may also mean aversion to the labor of mediation—the saint “hates the Path” because the Path keeps forcing unfinished Dharma back into Appearance.

A Path that is merely absent can return. A Path that has fully realized its Dharma ceases as Path.

## 4. Avijñapti Makes the Dharma-Path Genetic

The subsumption of a Path beneath its Concept is itself inconceivable if we remain inside Sensibility.

Sensibility gives us a sequence:

- this practice,
- this cognition,
- this transformation,
- this result.

But it does not sensibly display the unity by which these moments belong to one Path. That unity is not another appearance placed beside the others.

Avijñapti terminates the demand that we know Dharma apart from its Path. We do not first obtain the Dharma as a separate object and then classify a sequence beneath it. We know the Dharma **as the Path itself**.

This makes the Path genetic.

Without avijñapti, the Path would be merely sequential: a series of conditioned events later grouped under one name. With avijñapti, one supersensible Dharma persists through and generates its own exhibited stages.

> Avijñapti is the unexhibited genetic continuity of Dharma.  
> Vijñapti is that continuity actively displaying itself as Path.

The Path is therefore not externally assembled. It unfolds from its own Concept.

## 5. The Dialectical Copula

This relation is the Dialectical Copula at full power.

The ordinary judgment says:

> This sequence is a Path.

The genetic judgment says:

> Dharma is Path.

The “is” does not externally attach a predicate to an already complete subject. Dharma becomes Path by generating itself as Path. It differentiates itself into conditioned stages, persists through the differentiation, and returns from the completed Path as realized Dharma.

This yields the decisive distinction:

> **Avijñapti is the Dialectic in the Absolute.**  
> **Vijñapti is the Dialectic in Appearance.**

Avijñapti is Dharma’s supersensible genetic movement: the Path in its Principle. Vijñapti is that same movement unfolded into practices, cognitions, transformations, and results.

They are not two unrelated mechanisms. They are the two sides of one genetic Copula.

> Avijñapti is the Path in its principial genesis.  
> Vijñapti is the Path displaying that genesis through Appearance.

This is the point at which the Yoga–Kośa relation becomes especially powerful. The difficult Kośa category does not remain an obscure theory of latent karma. It becomes a model for how a Pure A Priori Rational Concept can generate and sustain a conditioned Path of its own Knowledge.

## 6. Dhyāna Is the Truth of Dharma

Dharma is not true as a static classification. Its truth is its inner development.

This is why **Dhyāna is Dialectic**, and why Dhyāna is the Truth of Dharma.

Dhāraṇā determines the Dharma. Dhyāna develops its internal movement. Samādhi gathers the developed Meaning as conclusive unity.

> **Dhāraṇā gives the Concept.**  
> **Dhyāna gives its dialectical Truth.**  
> **Samādhi gives its realized Meaning.**

The Path becomes Knowledge not when an observer externally describes it, but when the Dharma’s own movement becomes transparent through Dhyāna.

This gives us a powerful model of Citta. Citta is not a container filled with representations, nor a substance behind mental events. It is the conditioned field in which supersensible Dharma becomes genetic exhibition.

Citta does not manufacture Dharma. Citta exhibits Dharma. Yet this exhibition is active and dialectical rather than passive.

> Citta is the genetic display-system of Dharma.

Avijñapti gives Citta principial depth. Vijñapti gives it phenomenal articulation. Dhyāna is the truth of their movement. Samādhi gathers the whole movement into Meaning.

Puruṣa remains the Seer of the entire display. Citta can model conscious manifestation completely without becoming Draṣṭṛ.

## 7. Abhidharma as Pāramārthika

This structure is equivalent to the way we know the pāramārthika.

The pāramārthika is not a hidden reality behind Appearance, nor a description of actual appearances. It is Prajñā expressed as Jñāna: transcendental Speech becoming adequate ordinary Speech.

Likewise, Dharma is not known by turning it into a sensible object. It becomes known through its own complete Path-vijñapti.

> Dharma is known through its Path.  
> Pāramārthika is known through its adequate expression.

At its truth, Abhidharma is therefore not merely discourse about ultimate dharmas. It is itself pāramārthika: Dharma expressing its Pure A Priori Rational structure as systematic Jñāna.

The Kośa is Dharma gathering its complete extension into a Treasury. Sarva-Dharma is itself a Kośa of Dharma. Yoga is the Rational comprehension of that Treasury as one Principial System.

> Yoga is the Prajñā-form of the Kośa.  
> The Kośa is Yoga extended as Sarva-Dharma.

## 8. Organic Unity, Nirodha, and the Result

The Self-Enclosure of Dharma is the demonstration of Māyā. But Māyā must be realized precisely as **Organic Unity**.

This is not any vague realization that appearance is illusory. The complete Dharmic enclosure must be comprehended as one living, internally differentiated whole. Dhātu, Loka, Karma, Kleśa, Mārga, Jñāna, and Samāpatti must be gathered as one System.

Organic Unity stands beside Pure Unity.

Samāpatti gathers the complete Organic Unity. Nirodha brings it under absolute control. **Samāpatti-Nirodha realized is the One itself.**

The Absolute is therefore a Result—but a result of something exact:

> The Absolute is the vipāka of Māyā realized as Organic Unity.

The Path ceases only when Sarva-Dharma is fully realized. Before that, “burning the seeds” can be premature: an attempt to prevent further Path-exhibition because the return into Appearance is burdensome. Genuine seed-burning is not suppression. The Living Seed must unfold its whole Dharma.

> The truly burned seed is the fully realized seed.

When its complete content has become Knowledge, it no longer needs to germinate as Path.

## Conclusion

The Path of Dharma is not a sequence imposed upon Dharma from outside. It is Dharma generating its own conditioned exhibition and thereby generating its own Knowledge.

The first model can be stated compactly:

> **Dharma** — Pure A Priori Rational Concept.  
> **Dhātu** — Dharma’s root Domain of determinate display.  
> **Avijñapti** — the Dialectic in the Absolute; Dharma’s unexhibited genetic continuity.  
> **Vijñapti** — the Dialectic in Appearance; Dharma actively exhibited as Path.  
> **Citta** — the conditioned genetic display-system of Dharma.  
> **Dhyāna** — the Truth of Dharma as dialectical development.  
> **Samādhi** — the gathered and conclusive Meaning of that development.  
> **Samāpatti-Nirodha** — the realized One in which Organic Unity and Pure Unity are no longer externally opposed.

This is a new Yoga–Kośa Yoga, and it must still be tested against the detailed texts. Yet the model is already lucid. It explains why the Kośa begins with the root Domains of Dharma, why avijñapti is required for karma and vipāka, why the Path is genetic, why Dhyāna is the Truth of Dharma, and why Yoga can rationally lift the Self-Enclosure of Dharma into Self-realization.

> **The Dharma-Path is Dharma generating its own Knowledge.**
