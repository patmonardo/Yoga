# VAK_7.17 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> nava mārgānvayadhiyoḥ duḥkhahetudhiyor dvayam /
> caturṇāṃ daśa naikasya yojyā dharmāḥ punar daśa // VAK_7.17 //

**Best translation:**  
Nine knowledges are objects of path-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge; two are objects of knowledge of suffering and origin; all ten are objects of four knowledges; none is an object of one. Again, the dharmas to be systematically assigned are ten.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.17.md`](./VAK_7.17.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Primary source: `kosabhasaya.txt`, Pradhan [403|16–404|22].

The natural argument begins with the question introduced under VAK 7.16:

> katamasya jñānasya kati jñānāny ālambanam /

“How many knowledges are the object-support of each knowledge?”

Vasubandhu then distributes the ten knowledges as possible objects of one another and closes by opening a second object-classification, the ten dharmas developed under VAK 7.18.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

For dharma-knowledge, nine knowledges are object-supports, except anvaya-knowledge.

For anvaya-knowledge also, nine knowledges are object-supports, except dharma-knowledge.

For path-knowledge also, nine knowledges are object-supports, except conventional knowledge.

For knowledge of suffering and knowledge of origin, two are object-supports: conventional knowledge and knowledge of another mind.

For conventional knowledge, knowledge of another mind, knowledge of exhaustion, and knowledge of non-arising, all ten knowledges are object-supports.

For the single knowledge of cessation, no knowledge at all is an object-support.

The dharmas to be systematically assigned are again ten.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya eliminates the ambiguity left by the compressed numerical Kārikā and gives a complete directed matrix.

| Knowing standpoint | Knowledge-objects |
|---|---|
| dharmajñāna | 9; excludes anvayajñāna |
| anvayajñāna | 9; excludes dharmajñāna |
| mārgajñāna | 9; excludes saṃvṛtijñāna |
| duḥkhajñāna | 2; saṃvṛtijñāna and paracittajñāna |
| samudayajñāna | 2; saṃvṛtijñāna and paracittajñāna |
| saṃvṛtijñāna | all 10 |
| paracittajñāna | all 10 |
| kṣayajñāna | all 10 |
| anutpādajñāna | all 10 |
| nirodhajñāna | none of the 10 |

The relation is therefore not symmetrical. A knowledge may take another knowledge as object without the reverse relation following.

The final clause does not yet finish the discussion. It changes the unit of classification from “which knowledges can know which knowledges?” to “which classes of dharmas can each knowledge take as object?” That second matrix begins immediately in VAK 7.18.

## New Technical Terms

- **jñānālambana** — a knowledge functioning as the object-support of another knowledge
  - **status:** introduced as technically operative in the Bhāṣya discussion
  - **function:** object-range / relational classification

- **yojya** — to be systematically connected, distributed, or assigned
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā, made explicit by the prose transition
  - **function:** classificatory instruction

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

VAK 7.17 shows that the ten knowledges form not merely a list but a **directed relational field**.

```text
knowledge A
    may take
knowledge B
    as object

without
knowledge B
    necessarily taking
knowledge A
    as object
```

The strongest contrast is between:

```text
saṃvṛti / paracitta / kṣaya / anutpāda
    → all ten knowledges available as objects

nirodhajñāna
    → no member of the ten-knowledge field as object
```

Thus reflexive reach is itself a determinate property of a knowledge.

The verse also makes a methodological transition. First the system classifies knowledges by their relations to other knowledges; then it reclassifies the entire object-field into ten kinds of dharmas. VAK 7.18 must therefore be read as the continuation of this same argument rather than as an unrelated new list.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass caution can now be removed. The four knowledges taking all ten as objects are explicitly:

```text
saṃvṛtijñāna
paracittajñāna
kṣayajñāna
anutpādajñāna
```

The one taking none is explicitly:

```text
nirodhajñāna
```

The two objects of suffering- and origin-knowledge are explicitly:

```text
saṃvṛtijñāna
paracittajñāna
```

## Light Organon Contact Point

This is a useful example of disciplined relational classification. The identity of a cognition is not exhausted by its intrinsic definition; its **possible object-relations** also belong to its specification.

For later Organon reconstruction, that suggests a matrix rather than a flat taxonomy:

```text
Knowledge-Type
    → can-take-as-object
    → cannot-take-as-object
```

No deeper genetic derivation is imposed here.
