# VAK_7.53_bhasya — Five Causal Sources of Ṛddhi

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 7.53**

```text
avyākṛtaṃ bhāvanājaṃ trividhaṃ tūpapattijam /
ṛddhir mantrauṣadhābhyāṃ ca karmajā ceti pañcadhā // VAK_7.53 //
```

Working translation:

> A construction-mind born from cultivation is ethically indeterminate, while one acquired through birth is threefold. Supernormal power also arises from mantra and medicine, and from karma: thus it is fivefold.

See: [VAK_7.53.md](./VAK_7.53.md)

## Bhāṣya Source Passage

Pradhan 428|12–429|03:

```text
atha kiṃ sarvanirmāṇacittamavyākṛtaṃ bhavati /

     avyākṛtaṃ bhāvanājaṃ

yadbhāvanāphalaṃ tadavaśyamavyākṛtaṃ bhavati /

     trividhaṃ tūpapatījam /

upapattipratilambhikaṃ tu nirmāṇacittaṃ kuśalākuśalamavyākṛtaṃ bhavati devanāgapiśācādīnām /

tatkṛtaṃ ca svaparaśarīranirmāṇaṃ navāyatanikaṃ bhavatyaśabdarupyāyatanatvādindriyāvinirbhūtatvāt /

kimeṣaiva dvividharddhibhāvanāmayī copapattilābhikā ca /
eṣā ca dvividhā

     ṛddhirmantrauṣadhābhyāṃ ca karmajā ceti pañcadhā // VAkK_7.53 //

samāstaḥ pañcavidhāmṛddhhiṃ varṇayanti /
bhāvanāphalamupapattilābhikaṃ mantrajāmauṣadhajāṃ karmajāṃ ca /
yathā māndhāturantarābhavikānāṃ ca /
```

## Source Cautions

- The e-text reads `upapatījam`; the Kārikā source has `upapattijam`.
- The compound around `aśabdarupyāyatanatvādindriyāvinirbhūtatvāt` is compressed and should not be over-normalized without consulting editions/commentaries.
- The text states `na tvindriyaṃ nirmīyate` explicitly; any interpretation of the preceding `navāyatanika` statement must preserve that restriction.
- `ṛddhhiṃ` in the e-text contains an evident orthographic duplication; source wording is retained here as transmitted.

## Natural Argument

### Voice: Vasubandhu

The discussion begins with a question generated by the preceding analysis of construction-minds:

> Is every construction-mind ethically indeterminate?

The answer is no. The classification depends on causal provenance.

### Voice: Vasubandhu

A construction-mind that is the result of cultivation is necessarily ethically indeterminate:

```text
yad bhāvanā-phalaṃ
    tad avaśyam avyākṛtam
```

This is a strict rule for the cultivated construction-mind under discussion.

### Voice: Vasubandhu

A construction-mind acquired through birth is different. It may be:

```text
kuśala
akuśala
avyākṛta
```

Vasubandhu gives devas, nāgas, piśācas, and similar beings as examples of beings for whom such birth-acquired construction-minds occur.

He then adds a technical qualification concerning constructions of one's own or another body. The transmitted text describes such a construction as `navāyatanika`, while explicitly denying that an indriya itself is constructed. Because the compound explaining this is compressed, the safest conclusion is that the Bhāṣya is carefully restricting the sensory constitution of the constructed body rather than granting the production of genuine sense faculties.

### Voice: Vasubandhu

A further question follows:

> Are cultivation-produced and birth-acquired powers the only two forms of ṛddhi?

No. The text expands the causal taxonomy to five kinds.

## Fivefold Ṛddhi

```text
1. bhāvanā-phala
   result of cultivation

2. upapatti-lābhika
   acquired through mode of birth/existence

3. mantraja
   born from mantra

4. auṣadhaja
   born from medicine

5. karmaja
   born from karma
```

For karmically produced ṛddhi, Vasubandhu gives the examples of **Māndhātṛ** and **beings in the intermediate state (`antarābhavika`)**.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text

```

The verse therefore prevents a phenomenological shortcut:

```text

    ≠

```

The immediate construction-mind distinction is especially exact:

```text
bhāvanāja nirmāṇa-citta
    necessarily avyākṛta

upapattija nirmāṇa-citta
    kuśala | akuśala | avyākṛta
```

So even before the fivefold ṛddhi taxonomy is completed, causal origin has already altered ethical classification.

## New Technical Terms

- **bhāvanā-phala** — a result produced by cultivation.
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptually; explicit here as causal provenance.
  - **function:** distinguishes cultivated construction-power from other sources.

- **upapatti-pratilambhika / upapatti-lābhika** — acquired by virtue of birth or mode of existence.
  - **status:** previously encountered in the Kośa; newly decisive for ṛddhi classification here.
  - **function:** marks a native or birth-acquired capacity rather than one produced by deliberate cultivation.

- **mantraja** — produced from mantra.
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikā analysis.
  - **function:** identifies a distinct causal source of extraordinary efficacy.

- **auṣadhaja** — produced from medicine.
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās.
  - **function:** identifies material/medicinal causation as a distinct source of ṛddhi.

- **karmaja** — produced from karma.
  - **status:** karma as causal source is pervasive; this application to ṛddhi is locally specific.
  - **function:** distinguishes extraordinary efficacy matured from prior action from cultivated or externally induced forms.

- **navāyatanika** — consisting in or associated with nine spheres in the present construction context.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific technical specification here.
  - **function:** restricts the sensory constitution of constructed own/other bodies; exact internal accounting should remain provisional because of the compressed transmitted explanation.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.53 is not merely a list of exotic causes. Its doctrinal work is to block the inference:

```text
extraordinary effect
    → spiritual cultivation
```

That inference is invalid because ṛddhi may arise from at least five causally distinct sources.

A deva's birth-acquired capacity, a mantra-produced effect, a medicinal effect, a karmically matured capacity, and a cultivation-produced power may appear similar while differing fundamentally in origin and ethical status.

This is continuous with VAK 7.47: spectacle does not certify liberative truth.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā-pass translation should be tightened in one respect. The first pāda refers most immediately to **construction-mind** rather than to ṛddhi in general:

> A cultivation-produced construction-mind is ethically indeterminate; a birth-produced one is threefold. Supernormal power also arises from mantra and medicine, and from karma; thus [ṛddhi] is fivefold.

This preserves the Bhāṣya's movement from the local question about `nirmāṇa-citta` to the broader fivefold classification of ṛddhi.
