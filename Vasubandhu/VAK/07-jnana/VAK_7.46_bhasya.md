# VAK_7.46_bhasya — Vidyā and the Continuum Still Accompanied by Ignorance

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 7.46**

```text
aśaikṣyantyā tadākhye dve tatsaṃtānamudbhavāt /
iṣṭe śaikṣasya nokte tu vidye sāvidyasaṃtateḥ // VAK_7.46 //
```

**Current translation:**

> The last is intrinsically beyond training; two are so designated because they arise in that continuum. The corresponding knowledges are admitted for a trainee, but they are not called vidyās because that continuum is still accompanied by ignorance.

See: [VAK_7.46.md](./VAK_7.46.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 424|01–424|08:

```text
āsāṃ paramārthena

aśaikṣyantyā

āsravakṣayajñānasākṣātkriyaivāśaikṣī vidyā /

tadāsye dve tatsaṃtānasudbhavāt /

anye dve aśaikṣyasaṃtānasaṃbhūtatvādaśaikṣyāvucyete /
naiva tu te śaikṣyo nāśaikṣyau /

kiṃ punarete abhijñe śaikṣyasya nocyete /
yataḥ śaikṣyau vidye nocyete /

iṣṭe śaikṣsya nokte tu vidye sāvidyasaṃtateḥ // VAkK_7.46 //

na hi sāvidyasaṃtāne vidyāvyavasthānaṃ yujyate /
punarapyavidyābhibhavāt /
```

## Source Cautions

- The e-text reads `śaikṣsya` in the kārikā line; this is retained as a source artifact rather than silently corrected.
- The Bhāṣya distinguishes intrinsic status from designation by continuum. This distinction should not be flattened into a simple threefold classification of the vidyās.
- The prose immediately following 7.46 opens the three `prātihāryas`; that belongs to VAK 7.47 and is not included in this unit.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Only the last vidyā is intrinsically aśaikṣī

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu first tightens the classification made in 7.45.

```text
āsravakṣayajñānasākṣātkriyā
    direct realization of the exhaustion of the influxes
        ↓
intrinsically aśaikṣī vidyā
```

This is the only one of the three vidyās that is, in the strict sense, intrinsically beyond training.

### 2. The first two are called aśaikṣya because of the continuum in which they arise

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Former-life recollection and knowledge of death and reappearance are called `aśaikṣya` when they arise in an aśaikṣa continuum.

```text
cognition-type
    +
continuum-status
        ↓
classification
```

The designation therefore depends partly on the bearer-system, not solely on the immediate cognitive content.

Vasubandhu then adds a further precision:

```text
naiva tu te śaikṣyo nāśaikṣyau
```

Taken strictly in themselves, those two are neither intrinsically śaikṣa nor intrinsically aśaikṣa. Their designation is relational to the continuum in which they arise.

### 3. Objection: why are the corresponding abhijñās not called trainee vidyās?

**Voice: Objector**

If a trainee can possess the first two abhijñās, why should they not be called `śaikṣya-vidyā`?

The objection is important because it isolates the criterion for the title `vidyā`.

### 4. Resolution: the trainee's continuum is still accompanied by ignorance

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu grants the underlying cognition:

```text
iṣṭe śaikṣasya
    the cognitions are admitted for the trainee
```

But he refuses the title `vidyā`:

```text
nokte tu vidye
    they are not called vidyās
```

Why?

```text
sāvidyasaṃtateḥ
    because the continuum is still accompanied by ignorance
```

He then makes the reason explicit:

```text
na hi sāvidyasaṃtāne vidyāvyavasthānaṃ yujyate /
punarapyavidyābhibhavāt /
```

A stable establishment of the designation `vidyā` is not appropriate in a continuum still accompanied by ignorance, because ignorance can again overpower it.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
1. A trainee may possess the first two extraordinary cognitions.

2. Therefore possession of the cognition-type alone
   does not establish vidyā-status.

3. Vidyā-status requires attention to the continuum
   in which the cognition occurs.

4. In the trainee continuum,
   avidyā remains operative.

5. Because avidyā can again overpower,
   vidyā is not yet stably established there.

Therefore:

true or extraordinary cognition
    ≠
completed vidyā-status
```

The decisive predicate is not merely:

```text
Does this cognition occur?
```

but:

```text
What remains operative in the continuum that bears it?
```

## New Technical Terms

- **sāvidya-saṃtati** — a continuum still accompanied by ignorance.
  - **status:** new explicit Bhāṣya formulation in this block.
  - **function:** explains why corresponding trainee cognitions are not established as vidyā.

- **vidyā-vyavasthāna** — establishment or settled classification as vidyā.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation here.
  - **function:** marks the difference between occurrence of a cognition and stable scientific/soteriological classification.

- **avidyābhibhava** — overpowering or predominance of ignorance.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here.
  - **function:** gives the causal reason why vidyā is not yet stably established in the trainee continuum.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.46 distinguishes three different dimensions that must not be collapsed:

```text
cognitive content
    what is known

cognition-type
    which abhijñā occurs

continuum-status
    whether avidyā remains operative
```

The first two vidyās can have the same immediate cognitive content in trainee and aśaikṣa continua, but they do not receive the same settled doctrinal designation.

Thus:

```text
same cognition-type
    + different continuum-status
        ↓
different classificatory status
```

This is a strong Abhidharma example of classification by systemic locus.

## Translation Consequences

The phrase `sāvidyasaṃtateḥ` should be rendered so that `saṃtati` remains visible. It is not merely:

> because the trainee still has ignorance

but more precisely:

> because the trainee's continuum is still accompanied by ignorance.

Likewise, `vidyāvyavasthāna` is stronger than simple naming. It suggests stable doctrinal establishment or settled classification as vidyā.

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a precise event/system distinction:

```text
Event-level truth
    a cognition may correctly disclose its object

System-level completion
    the continuum may still permit avidyā to reassert dominance
```

Therefore:

```text
truth of an event
    does not entail
completion of the system
```

More formally:

```text
Cognition C has property P
    does not entail
Continuum S is governed by P
```

Vidyā in the strict classificatory sense requires not only a true cognition but a continuum in which the contrary principle is no longer capable of renewed domination.

## Rational Kośa Determination

This is one of the clearest verses in the chapter for distinguishing **local correctness** from **systemic rational completion**.

```text
śaikṣa
    can instantiate true cognition
    while residual avidyā remains operative

aśaikṣa
    cognition occurs in a continuum
    no longer governed by that residual ignorance
```

The Bhāṣya does not say that every cognition in the aśaikṣa is intrinsically of a different species. Instead, it shows that the same cognition-type can take on a different systemic status because of the transformed continuum in which it occurs.

## Organon Contact Point

This gives us a disciplined agent-modeling principle:

```text
successful output
    ≠
error-class eliminated from the agent
```

An agent may produce a correct answer while the generative system remains capable of reproducing the same class of error later.

So capability assessment should distinguish:

```text
event correctness
residual error disposition
system-state
stability of correction
```

The closest Organon formulation is:

> **A rational capacity is not complete merely when the correct state can occur; completion requires that the relevant contrary failure-mode no longer remain structurally dominant in the system.**

This is a restrained but important development of the Rational Kośa model of Knowledge as a property of an organized continuum rather than of isolated cognitive events.

## Transition to VAK 7.47

The Bhāṣya now turns immediately from the three vidyās to the three `prātihāryas`:

```text
ṛddhi
cetaḥparyāya
āsravakṣaya
```

These will be classified respectively as miracles of supernatural efficacy, declaration, and instruction. The next verse therefore shifts from the **status of knowledge in the continuum** to the **manifest communicative efficacy of perfected knowledge**.
