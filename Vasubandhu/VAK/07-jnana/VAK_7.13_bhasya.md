# VAK_7.13 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dravyataḥ ṣoḍaśākārāḥ prajñākāraḥ tayā saha /
> ākārayanti sālambāḥ sarvam ākāryate tu sat // VAK_7.13 //

**Best translation:**  
The sixteen aspects are substantially distinct. The aspect is said to be discriminative knowing; yet together with it, all object-bearing dharmas apprehend under an aspect, and whatever exists may be made an object of such apprehension.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.13.md`](./VAK_7.13.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Primary reference text: Pradhan-based GRETIL e-text, pp. 399–402.

The natural argument begins before the explicit definition of `ākāra`. Vasubandhu first records a dispute over whether the sixteen truth-aspects are sixteen only by name or sixteen in substantial distinction. One view says seven substantially, sixteen nominally; the preferred account states:

> dravyataḥ ṣoḍaśākārāḥ

The prose then supplies several explanatory series for the sixteen aspects and finally turns to the decisive question:

> ākāro nāma ka eṣa dharmaḥ /

“What dharma is this that is called an aspect?”

The source e-text is a reference edition rather than a critical edition; evident orthographic irregularities are therefore preserved in cited source wording rather than silently normalized.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Sixteen in substance

**Voice: uncertain / doctrinal presentation**

An alternative account holds that the sixteen aspects are only seven substantial entities: the four aspects of suffering are four, while the four aspects of origin, cessation, and path each reduce to one substantial entity.

The preferred account states instead that the sixteen are substantially distinct.

Vasubandhu then explains each aspect through a specific determining relation. For example:

- `anitya` because conditioned dharmas depend on representations/conditions;
- `duḥkha` because they are oppressive or liable to injury;
- `śūnya` as counteragent to the view of possession;
- `anātman` as counteragent to self-view;
- the four origin-aspects through different determinations of causal production;
- the four cessation-aspects through stopping, peace, excellence, and escape;
- the four path-aspects through path, correctness, practice, and definitive leading-out.

A second explanatory series is also supplied, followed by a third explicitly framed in terms of counteraction to different erroneous views.

Thus `dravyataḥ` does not merely assert sixteen names. The prose attempts to assign a distinct rational function to each aspect.

### 2. What is an aspect?

**Voice: Vasubandhu / dialectical clarification**

The Bhāṣya asks:

> ākāro nāma ka eṣa dharmaḥ /

The initial answer is:

> prajñākāraḥ

“Discriminative knowing is the aspect.”

An objection immediately follows: if so, discriminative knowing itself would not be aspect-bearing, because it is not associated with another discriminative knowing.

This produces the correction:

> sarveṣāṃ cittacaittānām ālambanagrahaṇaprakāra ākāra iti /

An aspect is the **mode of taking an object-support common to all citta and caittas**.

This is the most important Bhāṣya determination for the verse. `Ākāra` is not simply another cognitive entity appended to cognition. It is the determinate manner in which object-bearing cognition takes its object.

### 3. Does only prajñā form the aspect?

The Bhāṣya then asks whether only discriminative knowing performs this aspectual apprehension.

The answer is no.

> tayā saha /
> ākārayanti sālambāḥ /

Together with prajñā, **all object-bearing dharmas** apprehend under an aspect.

The prose makes this explicit:

> prajñā cānye ca sarve sālambanā dharmā ākārayanti /

Prajñā and all the other dharmas possessing an object-support perform aspectual apprehension.

### 4. What can be aspectualized?

The final clause is universal on the object side:

> sarvam ākāryate tu sat /

Whatever exists can be made an object of such apprehension.

Vasubandhu summarizes the relation:

- prajñā is aspectual;
- prajñā apprehends under an aspect;
- prajñā itself can be apprehended;
- object-supports are only apprehended, not themselves apprehending.

The verse therefore ends with a carefully differentiated relation among cognition, aspect, and object-support rather than with a simple subject-object pair.

## Literal Prose Translation

“What dharma is this that is called an aspect?”

“The aspect is discriminative knowing.”

“If that were so, discriminative knowing itself would not possess an aspect, since it is not associated with another discriminative knowing. Rather, it would be proper to say: the aspect is the mode in which all citta and caittas take their object-support.”

“Does discriminative knowing alone apprehend under an aspect?”

“No. Together with it, all object-bearing dharmas apprehend under an aspect. Whatever exists can be made an object of apprehension.”

Thus discriminative knowing is both aspectual and apprehending; it can itself also be apprehended. Object-supports, however, are on the apprehended side.

## Argument Reconstruction

The argument has a compact dialectical form:

```text
initial thesis
    ākāra = prajñā

objection
    then prajñā itself cannot be sākāra
    because no second prajñā accompanies it

correction
    ākāra = mode of taking the object-support
    common to all object-bearing citta/caittas

consequence
    prajñā is not uniquely aspect-forming
    all sālambana dharmas apprehend aspectually

object-side universal
    whatever exists can become object
```

The Bhāṣya therefore shifts from a substantial identification of aspect with one cognitive factor to a **relational-functional definition**.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: doctrinal presentation / uncertain** — the preliminary disagreement over seven versus sixteen substantial aspects.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — the question “what dharma is aspect?” and the dialectical correction of the simplistic `prajñā = ākāra` identity.
- **Voice: Objector** — the consequence that prajñā would then fail to be aspect-bearing.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — the broader definition through `ālambanagrahaṇaprakāra` and the extension to all `sālambana` dharmas.

## New Technical Terms

- **ālambanagrahaṇaprakāra** — mode or manner of taking an object-support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only compound in this local discussion
  - **function:** corrected definition of `ākāra`

- **sālambana** — possessing an object-support
  - **status:** previously encountered notion, newly made structurally operative here
  - **function:** determines the class of dharmas that apprehend under an aspect

- **ākārya / ākāryate** — that which is made the object of aspectual apprehension / is apprehended under an aspect
  - **status:** rare verbal elaboration of `ākāra`
  - **function:** marks the object-side of the cognition relation

- **dravyataḥ** — substantially, in real doctrinal distinction
  - **status:** present in the Kārikā, expanded decisively by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** distinguishes substantial plurality from merely nominal plurality

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The major doctrinal result is not simply “there are sixteen aspects.” It is the clarification of what an aspect is.

```text
ākāra
    ≠ a second cognitive substance

ākāra
    = determinate mode of object-taking
```

This prevents an infinite duplication of prajñā and makes aspectuality a structural feature of all object-bearing cognition.

The final architecture is:

```text
sālambana dharma
    takes
ālambana
    through
ākāra
```

with an important asymmetry:

```text
cognition
    apprehends and can be apprehended

object-support qua object
    is apprehended
```

The Bhāṣya has therefore moved from enumeration to relation.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass gloss “the aspect is prajñā” is too static if left unqualified. The Bhāṣya itself immediately rejects that as a sufficient definition.

For project purposes, `ākāra` should remain:

> **aspect / determinate mode of apprehension**

and the verse should be read as preserving the traditional identification with prajñā only as the starting point of a clarification whose mature formulation is `ālambanagrahaṇaprakāra`.

Likewise, `ākārayanti` should not be reduced to “produce an image.” It means that object-bearing cognitions take their object in a determinate mode.

## Organon Light

This passage deserves only a restrained contact point, but it is important:

```text
Knowledge is not bare possession of an object.
Determinate knowing includes the mode under which the object is taken.
```

The Bhāṣya also refuses to reify that mode into a second internal knower. `Ākāra` is relational and functional.

That is enough for the present pass. No stronger Kantian or Hegelian reconstruction is imposed here.
