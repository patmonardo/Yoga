# VAK_7.25 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> navānāṃ tu kṣayajñāne akopyasya daśa bhāvanā /
> tatsaṃcare 'ntyamuktau ca proktaśeṣe 'ṣṭabhāvanā // VAK_7.25 //

**Best current translation:**  
At knowledge of destruction, nine knowledges are cultivated; for the immovable one, ten. Ten are also cultivated at the final liberation path in the transition to immovability. In the remaining stated cases, eight are cultivated.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.25.md`](./VAK_7.25.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 409|08–410|01.

```text
navānāṃ tu kṣayajñāne
bhavāgravairāgye navamo vimuktimārgaḥ kṣayajñānam /
tatra navānāṃ jñānānāṃ bhāvanā anyatrānutpādajñānāt /

akopyasya daśa bhāvanā /
yastvakopyadharmā bhavati tasya daśānāṃ jñānānāṃ bhāvanā /
anutpādajñānalābhāt /

tatsaṃcare 'ntyamuktau ca
yo 'pyakopyatāṃ saṃcarati tasyāpyantye vimuktimārge daśānāṃ bhāvanā /

proktaśeṣe 'ṣṭabhāvanā // VAkK_7.25 //
kiṃ punaḥ śeṣam /
kāmavairāgye navamo vimuktimārgaḥ saptabhūmivairāgyābhijñāvyavakīrṇabhāviteṣu vimuktimārgaḥ akopyaprativedho 'ṣṭau vimuktimārgāḥ sarve ca vītarāgasya prayogaviśeṣamārgāḥ /
teṣu sarveṣvaṣṭau jñānāni bhāvyante /
anāgatabhāvanayā kṣayānutpādajñāne hitvā /
śaikṣasyaivam /
```

The Bhāṣya then continues by distinguishing the corresponding counts for the `aśaikṣa` and the ordinary person.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Why nine at knowledge of destruction?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

At detachment from the peak of existence, the ninth liberation path is `kṣayajñāna`, knowledge of destruction.

At that moment nine knowledges are cultivated:

```text
all ten knowledges
minus
anutpādajñāna
```

The exclusion is explicit:

```text
anyatra anutpādajñānāt
    except for knowledge of non-arising
```

Thus the count is not an abstract terminal number. It is generated by one specific missing determination.

### 2. Why ten for the immovable practitioner?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

For one whose liberation is immovable (`akopyadharma`), all ten knowledges are cultivated.

The reason is exact:

```text
anutpādajñāna-lābha
    acquisition of knowledge of non-arising
```

So the transition from nine to ten is caused by acquisition of the one previously absent knowledge.

### 3. Transition into immovability

For one passing into (`saṃcarati`) the immovable state, the final liberation path also cultivates all ten.

The terminal liberation moment is therefore counted according to the state it actualizes:

```text
transition to immovability
    ↓
final liberation path
    ↓
acquisition of anutpādajñāna
    ↓
tenfold cultivation
```

### 4. What are “the remaining stated cases”?

**Voice: question-form**

```text
kiṃ punaḥ śeṣam /
    What, then, is the remainder?
```

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya enumerates the remainder rather than leaving it indeterminate:

```text
1. the ninth liberation path in detachment from desire
2. liberation paths in detachment from the seven levels
3. liberation paths connected with the superknowledges
4. liberation paths in mixed cultivation
5. realization of immovability
6. the eight liberation paths associated with that process
7. all preparatory paths of the already detached practitioner
8. all special paths of the already detached practitioner
```

In all these cases eight knowledges are cultivated.

The two excluded from future cultivation are:

```text
kṣayajñāna
anutpādajñāna
```

Hence:

```text
ten total
minus two terminal knowledges
    = eight
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly limits this formulation here to the trainee (`śaikṣa`).

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

At detachment from the peak of existence, the ninth liberation path is knowledge of destruction. There, nine knowledges are cultivated, except knowledge of non-arising.

For one who is of immovable nature, all ten knowledges are cultivated, because knowledge of non-arising has been acquired.

Likewise, for one who passes into immovability, all ten are cultivated in the final liberation path.

What, then, is the remainder? The ninth liberation path in detachment from desire; the liberation paths in detachment from the seven levels, in the superknowledges, and in mixed cultivation; realization of immovability; the eight liberation paths; and all preparatory and special paths of one already detached. In all of these, eight knowledges are cultivated, by future cultivation excluding knowledge of destruction and knowledge of non-arising. This is so for the trainee.

### Philosophical Translation

The cultivation counts are generated by exact path-status and acquisition conditions. At the first terminal knowledge of liberation, the practitioner cultivates the whole field except knowledge of non-arising. Once immovability is attained, that final knowledge is acquired and the cultivation field becomes complete. In other advanced but still trainee-level contexts, both terminal knowledges remain excluded from future cultivation, producing an eightfold field.

## New Technical Terms

- **akopyadharma** — one whose liberation is of an immovable nature
  - **status:** previously encountered in path analysis
  - **function:** marks the state in which `anutpādajñāna` is acquired and the tenfold cultivation field becomes complete

- **saṃcara** — passage or transition into another attained state
  - **status:** locally important in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** specifies movement from a lower liberation-status into immovability

- **proktaśeṣa** — the remainder among the previously stated cases
  - **status:** Kārikā expression expanded by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** gathers a precisely enumerable residual class of path-events

- **anāgatabhāvanā** — future cultivation
  - **status:** central technical vocabulary in this block
  - **function:** distinguishes the future cultivated field from presently arising cognition

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.25 clarifies the terminal structure of the cultivation matrix:

| Path condition | Cultivated knowledges | Generating exclusion/addition |
|---|---:|---|
| `kṣayajñāna` at detachment from `bhavāgra` | 9 | excludes `anutpādajñāna` |
| immovable practitioner | 10 | `anutpādajñāna` acquired |
| final liberation path in transition to immovability | 10 | transition actualizes immovability |
| stated remainder for trainee | 8 | excludes `kṣayajñāna` and `anutpādajñāna` from future cultivation |

The numerical sequence is therefore not:

```text
8 → 9 → 10
```

as a merely quantitative scale.

It is generated by the logical status of two terminal knowledges:

```text
kṣayajñāna
    knowledge that destruction has been accomplished

anutpādajñāna
    knowledge that what has been destroyed will not arise again
```

The difference between nine and ten is exactly the difference between completed destruction and irreversible non-arising.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing translation is sound, but the Bhāṣya requires two refinements:

1. the nine at `kṣayajñāna` are explicitly all knowledges except `anutpādajñāna`;
2. `proktaśeṣa` is not a vague remainder but an explicitly listed set of advanced trainee path-events.

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya give an excellent example of a generated numerical matrix.

The count is derivative from structural features:

```text
PathStatus
PractitionerStatus
TerminalKnowledgeAcquired
FutureCultivationEligibility
```

Then:

```text
if anutpādajñāna not acquired
    and kṣayajñāna presently terminal
        → 9

if anutpādajñāna acquired
        → 10

if both terminal knowledges excluded
from future cultivation
        → 8
```

The arithmetic is therefore an output of doctrinal predicates, not the primary content.
