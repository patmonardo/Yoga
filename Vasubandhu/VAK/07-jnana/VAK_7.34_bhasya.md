# VAK_7.34 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> saṃbhāradharmakāyābhyāṃ jagataścārthacaryayā /
> samatā sarvabuddhānāṃ nāyurjātipramāṇataḥ // VAK_7.34 //

**Best current translation:**

All Buddhas are equal through their accumulation, Dharma-body, and activity for the welfare of the world; they are not equal with respect to lifespan, birth, or bodily measure.

### Source cautions

- The e-text has several compressed or visibly imperfect compounds in the long catalogue (`rūpakayasaṃpacva`, `āyurutptargā...`, etc.). They are preserved rather than silently normalized.
- The Kārikā says `jāti`; the Bhāṣya expands the difference into both `jāti` and `gotra`, illustrated by kṣatriya/brāhmaṇa birth and Kāśyapa/Gautama clan differences.
- `dharmakāya` is read in this local Abhidharma context as the perfected Dharma-constitution of a Buddha. No later three-body system is imported.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Objector / questioner**

Are all Buddhas alike in every possible respect?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

No. Their equality is determinate rather than indiscriminate.

All Buddhas are equal for three reasons:

1. they have brought the complete accumulation of merit and knowledge to fulfillment;
2. they have perfected the Dharma-body;
3. they carry out activity for the welfare of the world.

Yet Buddhas differ with respect to conditioned manifestation: some live longer or shorter lives; they may be born as kṣatriyas or brāhmaṇas; their clan designations differ, such as Kāśyapa and Gautama; and bodily measure varies according to the age.

Vasubandhu then reorganizes the same threefold equality as three excellences (`saṃpad`):

- excellence of cause (`hetusaṃpad`),
- excellence of fruit (`phalasaṃpad`),
- excellence of beneficence (`upakārasaṃpad`).

Contemplating these three, the learned person can generate intense love and reverence toward the Buddhas.

Cause-excellence is fourfold: cultivation of the complete accumulations of merit and knowledge, cultivation over a long period, uninterrupted cultivation, and respectful/earnest cultivation.

Fruit-excellence is fourfold: excellence of knowledge, abandonment, power/effective capability, and material body.

The Bhāṣya then analyzes these fruit-excellences further. Knowledge-excellence is fourfold: knowledge not taught by another, knowledge extending everywhere, knowledge in every mode, and effortless knowledge. Abandonment-excellence is fourfold: abandonment of all defilements, complete abandonment, abandonment together with residual traces, and abandonment of all obstructions to samādhi and attainment. Power-excellence and bodily excellence are likewise articulated into fourfold sets.

The point is not merely to multiply lists. The Bhāṣya is showing that the equality named in the verse is a **structured perfection**: same completed causal history, same perfected result, same beneficent orientation.

The excursus ends by saying that this is only a summary of the greatness of the Buddhas. Its distinctions become limitless when fully unfolded, and only Buddhas could know and express them completely. Vasubandhu then closes the section: these are the eighteen distinctive (`āveṇika`) Buddha-dharmas.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
question:
    are all Buddhas identical in every respect?

answer:
    no

rational equality:
    complete merit-and-knowledge accumulation
    + perfected Dharma-body
    + welfare-activity toward the world

conditioned difference:
    lifespan
    + birth
    + clan
    + bodily measure

same three equalities redescribed as:
    cause-excellence
    + fruit-excellence
    + beneficence-excellence

therefore:
    equality of Buddhahood
    does not entail
    empirical uniformity of Buddhas
```

This is a precise respect-relative judgment. The Bhāṣya neither reduces all Buddhas to numerical sameness nor allows empirical difference to compromise the perfection proper to Buddhahood.

## New Technical Terms

- **sarvaprakārasāmānya** — sameness in every respect
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this passage
  - **function:** frames the opening objection that the Kārikā denies.

- **sarvapuṇyajñānasaṃbhārasamudāgama** — complete coming-to-fulfillment of the entire accumulation of merit and knowledge
  - **status:** expanded Bhāṣya determination of `saṃbhāra`
  - **function:** first ground of Buddha-equality.

- **dharmakāyapariniṣpatti** — perfection/completion of the Dharma-body
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `dharmakāya`
  - **function:** second ground of Buddha-equality.

- **hetusaṃpad** — excellence/completion of cause
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** redescribes the completed accumulation genetically.

- **phalasaṃpad** — excellence/completion of fruit
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** organizes the realized Buddha-result.

- **upakārasaṃpad** — excellence/completion of beneficence
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this unit
  - **function:** organizes Buddha-activity for beings.

- **jñānasaṃpad** — excellence of knowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only local analytic term
  - **function:** one of four fruit-excellences.

- **prahāṇasaṃpad** — excellence of abandonment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only local analytic term
  - **function:** one of four fruit-excellences.

- **prabhāvasaṃpad** — excellence of effective power
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only local analytic term
  - **function:** one of four fruit-excellences.

- **rūpakāyasaṃpad** — excellence of the material body
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only local analytic term
  - **function:** one of four fruit-excellences.

- **anupadiṣṭajñāna** — knowledge not instructed by another
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local catalogue
  - **function:** first mode of Buddha knowledge-excellence.

- **sarvatrajñāna** — knowledge extending everywhere
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local catalogue
  - **function:** breadth of knowledge-excellence.

- **sarvathājñāna** — knowledge in every mode/respect
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local catalogue
  - **function:** modal completeness of knowledge.

- **ayatnajñāna** — effortless knowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local catalogue

## Doctrinal Determination

### Equality is respect-relative

The Bhāṣya states equality only under three determinations:

```text
cause:
    complete puṇya + jñāna accumulation

result:
    perfected Dharma-body

operation:
    welfare-activity for the world
```

The same Buddhas may differ in conditioned embodiment without any inequality in Buddhahood itself.

### `saṃbhāra` is genetic, not merely possessive

The expansion into `hetusaṃpad` makes an important point. The equality of accumulation is not merely that every Buddha possesses the same completed stock. It is a common **history of cultivation** characterized by completeness, long duration, continuity, and respectful application.

### The Buddha-result is internally articulated

`phalasaṃpad` is not one undifferentiated state. It is analyzed into:

```text
knowledge
abandonment
power/effective capability
material embodiment
```

This recapitulates much of VAK 7.28–7.33: knowledge-powers, absence of obstruction and residual traces, bodily power, fearlessness, recollective composure, and great compassion now appear as determinations of one perfected result.

### Beneficence is constitutive, not incidental

The third equality is not private realization but `arthacaryā` for the world. Buddha-equality therefore includes the outward liberative function already expressed by great compassion in VAK 7.33.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation remains basically correct, but the Bhāṣya requires two refinements.

First, `saṃbhāra` should be heard as the **complete accumulation of merit and knowledge**, not an unspecified spiritual accumulation.

Second, the concluding `jāti` in the Kārikā is expanded in prose to include both social birth and clan (`gotra`). A fuller interpretive translation may therefore read:

> All Buddhas are equal through the completed accumulation of merit and knowledge, the perfected Dharma-body, and their activity for the welfare of the world; they differ, however, in conditioned manifestation—lifespan, birth and clan, and bodily measure.

The additional `gotra` is explanatory prose, not silently inserted into the Sanskrit Kārikā.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.34 gives a particularly clean form of **determinate equality**:

```text
same under U
    does not imply
same under every P
```

More strongly, the Bhāṣya distinguishes three genetic moments of the equality:

```text
hetu
    completed formation

phala
    perfected constitution

upakāra
    effective operation for others
```

This is not a static property-list. It is a path from formation, through realization, into function.

The empirical differences—lifespan, birth/clan, bodily measure—belong to conditioned manifestation. They do not alter the universal determination of Buddhahood because the criterion of equality has already been specified.

## Organon Contact Point

The safest Organon contact is the distinction between **rational equality and empirical non-identity**.

```text
same perfected principle
    may appear
    through different conditioned determinations
```

The Bhāṣya itself gives the genetic structure that licenses this contact:

```text
formation / cause
    → realization / fruit
    → beneficent operation
```

So the universal is not abstracted away from appearance. It is demonstrated through a shared genesis and function, while empirical particulars remain genuinely different.

This is close to the problem we have been encountering in the Organon Dataset architecture: a common rational determination need not force identical concrete appearances. But the Kośa gives its own exact case first, and that case should remain primary.

## VAK_7b Closure

VAK 7.19–7.27 analyzed the cultivation and possession of knowledges through increasingly precise path-conditions. VAK 7.28–7.34 then showed the completed Buddha-side of that cognitive system:

```text
cultivation
    → perfected knowledges
    → powers
    → fearlessness
    → recollective composure
    → great compassion
    → equality of perfected Buddhahood
```

VAK 7.34 therefore closes the block by showing that Buddha-perfection is simultaneously:

- genetically completed (`saṃbhāra` / `hetusaṃpad`),
- structurally perfected (`dharmakāya` / `phalasaṃpad`),
- universally operative (`arthacaryā` / `upakārasaṃpad`).

The Bhāṣya itself marks the close immediately afterward: `ime tāvadaṣṭādaśa buddhānām āveṇikā dharmā ucyante` — these, then, are called the eighteen distinctive qualities of the Buddhas.
