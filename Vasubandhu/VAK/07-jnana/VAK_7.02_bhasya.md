# VAK 7.02 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> sāsravānāsravaṃ jñānam ādyaṃ saṃvṛtijñānakam /  
> anāsravaṃ dvidhā dharmajñānam anvayameva ca // VAK_7.02 //

**Best current translation:**  
Knowledge is contaminated and uncontaminated. The first is conventional knowledge. Uncontaminated knowledge is twofold: dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.02.md`](./VAK_7.02.md)

### Source caution

The Kārikā e-text and Bhāṣya quotation transmit `saṃvṛtijñāpakam`, while Vasubandhu's prose immediately says:

> **yatsāsravaṃ tatsaṃvṛtijñānam**

The first-pass Kārikā file therefore normalized the expression to `saṃvṛtijñānakam`. The Bhāṣya strongly confirms that the intended technical category is **saṃvṛtijñāna**, but the transmitted quotation should remain visible rather than being silently repaired.

## Literal Prose Translation

The five views and worldly right view [make six]. This sixfold worldly discriminative knowing is view; the other [worldly discriminative knowing] is not view. But both this and the other are knowledge.

How much is required to include all knowledge? By ten knowledges.

In summary, however, knowledge is contaminated and uncontaminated.

Of these two, the first is conventional knowledge.

Whatever knowledge is contaminated is conventional knowledge, because for the most part it apprehends conventions such as pot, cloth, woman, man, and so forth.

Others say: because it is covered by ignorance.

Uncontaminated knowledge is twofold: dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge.

Uncontaminated knowledge is divided in two: dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge.

## Natural Argument

VAK 7.01 distinguished `jñāna` from `dṛṣṭi`. Vasubandhu now asks for the complete inclusion of knowledge:

```text
kiyatā sarvajñānasaṃgrahaḥ?
By how many [forms] is all knowledge included?
```

The eventual answer is ten knowledges, but the Bhāṣya first gives their most general division:

```text
jñāna
    ├── sāsrava
    └── anāsrava
```

The contaminated member is identified with `saṃvṛtijñāna`. The uncontaminated member then divides:

```text
anāsrava-jñāna
    ├── dharmajñāna
    └── anvayajñāna
```

The argument is therefore genetic and nested rather than a flat enumeration of three coordinate knowledges.

## Saṃvṛtijñāna

The Bhāṣya adds an explanation not explicit in the Kārikā:

> **yatsāsravaṃ tatsaṃvṛtijñānam**  
> Whatever knowledge is contaminated is conventional knowledge.

Vasubandhu then records two explanations of the name.

### 1. Knowledge taking conventions as its objects

> **prāyeṇa ghaṭapaṭastrīpuruṣādisaṃvṛtigrahāt**

It is called conventional knowledge because, for the most part, it apprehends conventional formations such as:

```text
ghaṭa     pot
paṭa      cloth
strī      woman
puruṣa    man
```

The important qualification is **prāyeṇa**, “for the most part.” The Bhāṣya does not define conventional knowledge by saying that it can know only conventional objects. Its full object-range is specified in VAK 7.03.

### 2. Knowledge covered by ignorance

An alternative explanation is introduced explicitly:

> **ajñānasaṃvṛtatvāt ityapare**  
> Others [say]: because it is covered by ignorance.

Here `saṃvṛti` is explained through covering or concealment rather than primarily through conventional designation.

The Bhāṣya does not resolve this as a developed dispute in the present unit. It records the alternative and moves on.

## Anāsrava Knowledge

The second principal member is not left undifferentiated:

> **anāsravaṃ jñānaṃ dvidhā bhidyate**  
> Uncontaminated knowledge is divided in two.

Its forms are:

```text
dharmajñāna
anvayajñāna
```

The Bhāṣya does not yet define their respective object-ranges. That belongs immediately to VAK 7.03. Accordingly, no stronger translation of `anvaya` should be forced here.

## Argument Reconstruction

The movement is:

```text
all knowledge
    ↓
contaminated / uncontaminated
    ↓
contaminated = conventional knowledge
    ↓
uncontaminated
    → dharmajñāna / anvayajñāna
```

The classification therefore proceeds through successive grounds of difference:

1. whether contaminants remain operative;
2. within uncontaminated knowing, which path-form the cognition has.

The ten knowledges announced by the Bhāṣya are generated from further differentiations developed in the following verses.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — principal classification and explanation of conventional knowledge through ordinary conventional objects.

**Voice: uncertain / alternative teachers** — `ajñānasaṃvṛtatvāt ityapare`, the explanation that conventional knowledge is so called because it is covered by ignorance.

No reason is given here to identify the alternative specifically as Vaibhāṣika.

## New Technical Terms

- **sarvajñānasaṃgraha** — inclusion or comprehensive classification of all knowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** states the classificatory problem that motivates the ten knowledges

- **saṃvṛtigraha** — apprehension of convention / conventional formations
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** first explanation of why contaminated knowledge is called `saṃvṛtijñāna`

- **ajñānasaṃvṛtatva** — being covered by ignorance
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only alternative explanation in this passage
  - **function:** rival etymological/doctrinal explanation of `saṃvṛti`

- **prāyeṇa** — for the most part
  - **status:** ordinary adverb with technical importance here
  - **function:** prevents the examples pot, cloth, woman, and man from becoming an exhaustive object-definition of conventional knowledge

## Doctrinal Determination

The central distinction is not simply:

```text
false knowledge / true knowledge
```

Vasubandhu says:

```text
sāsrava-jñāna
    = knowledge
    = saṃvṛtijñāna
```

Thus contaminated or conventional cognition remains genuinely classed as **knowledge**. Its contaminated status does not erase its cognitive status.

Conversely:

```text
anāsrava-jñāna
    ≠ undifferentiated transcendence
```

It immediately possesses an internal articulation into dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge.

The chapter therefore begins from a graded and functionally differentiated field of knowing rather than a simple opposition between error and absolute truth.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The Bhāṣya confirms the first-pass structural translation:

> Knowledge is contaminated and uncontaminated. The first is conventional knowledge. Uncontaminated knowledge is twofold: dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge.

Two cautions should be retained.

First, `saṃvṛtijñāna` should remain **conventional knowledge** for now, but the Bhāṣya gives both conventional-apprehension and ignorance-covering explanations of the name.

Second, `anvayajñāna` should remain provisional. The present passage names it but does not yet establish the semantic force needed for a final English rendering.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The useful structural point is the form of the division:

```text
Knowledge
    ↓
first difference: contaminated / uncontaminated
    ↓
internal difference of the uncontaminated:
    dharma / anvaya
```

This is not dictionary classification by externally collected labels. A general cognitive field is progressively determined by differences that matter to its path-function.

The stronger comparison with Pure Reason or the reciprocity of Intuition and Concept belongs to the later Organon pass. VAK 7.02 itself establishes only the nested architecture of the field of knowledge.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.02 begins the construction of the ten knowledges by first dividing all knowledge into contaminated and uncontaminated forms. Contaminated knowledge is called conventional knowledge; the Bhāṣya explains the name primarily through its frequent apprehension of conventional formations such as pots, cloth, women, and men, while recording an alternative explanation that it is covered by ignorance. Uncontaminated knowledge then divides into dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge. The classification is therefore hierarchical and genetic rather than a flat list, while conventional cognition remains fully within the category of `jñāna`.
