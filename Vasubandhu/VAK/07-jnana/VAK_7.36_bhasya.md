## Kārikā

> nṛjā anutpanna-kāmāpta-savastuka-kleśa-gocarāḥ // VAK_7.36 //

**Best current translation:**

Freedom from conflict is conventional knowledge. It belongs to one of immovable disposition, is based on the final dhyāna, is produced among humans, and has as its object-field unarisen, object-based afflictions belonging to the desire domain.

### Source cautions

- The supplied e-text contains no major difficulty in this unit, though sandhi and compounds remain compressed.
- `raṇa` carries the sense of conflict/contention or afflictive disturbance. The Bhāṣya defines `araṇā` functionally rather than by etymology alone.
- The Kārikā's compact `akopyadharmaṇaḥ` is sharpened in prose: this belongs specifically to an immovable arhat and not to other arhats.
- The final limitation to `savastuka` kleśas is essential. Vasubandhu explicitly excludes `avastuka` kleśas because universally operative afflictions take the whole level as their object and cannot be avoided by modifying one determinate encounter.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

What is called `araṇā`?

A certain arhat first knows that the suffering of beings arises from afflictions (`kleśaprabhavaṃ ... duḥkham`). He also knows himself to be an especially worthy recipient of offerings (`dakṣiṇīyaviśeṣa`). Precisely because others may take such a person as an object and thereby generate an affliction, he wishes to prevent the arising of kleśa in others with himself as its object.

He therefore generates a specific knowledge by which he does not, in any way, produce `raṇa` in others.

The Bhāṣya makes this concrete:

```text
with him as object,
no one's rāga arises,
no one's dveṣa arises,
no one's māna arises.
```

This mode of practice does not cause anyone to enter afflictive conflict; therefore it is called `araṇā`.

Vasubandhu then determines the capacity analytically.

### Nature

`Araṇā` is `saṃvṛtijñāna`, conventional knowledge. This is its `svabhāva`.

### Meditative basis

It belongs to the final, fourth dhyāna because that level is foremost in ease of practice (`sukhapratipadāmagratvāt`).

### Bearer

It belongs to an `akopyadharman`, an immovable arhat, and not to another arhat. Vasubandhu's reason is striking: another arhat may sometimes be unable to avoid an afflictive disturbance even within his own continuum. Such a practitioner therefore cannot possess the stronger capacity to ensure that his relation to others does not occasion affliction in them.

### Place of production

It arises only among human beings, in the three continents.

### Object-range

Its objects are future, unarisen (`anāgata`) desire-domain (`kāmāvacara`) afflictions that possess a determinate object (`savastuka`). It operates under the determination:

```text
"may an affliction not arise in another because of me as its object"
```

Objectless (`avastuka`) afflictions cannot be prevented in this fashion. Universally operative afflictions (`sarvatraga`) take the whole corresponding level as their object, so no local modification of conduct can remove their object-condition.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
fact about beings:
    suffering arises from kleśa

self-knowledge of the arhat:
    I am a distinctive dakṣiṇīya
    and may become an object for others

practical problem:
    another may generate
        rāga
        dveṣa
        māna
    with me as object

resolve:
    prevent my presence / conduct
    from becoming the occasion
    of another's affliction

means:
    generate a determinate saṃvṛtijñāna
    grounded in fourth dhyāna

result:
    no determinate kleśa arises in another
    with this arhat as its object

limit:
    only savastuka kleśas are preventable this way
    avastuka / sarvatraga kleśas are not
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives `araṇā` a fully practical structure. It is knowledge whose object is a **possible future affliction in another**, and whose function is to alter one's own comportment so that the relevant causal occasion never becomes actual.

## New Technical Terms

- **kleśaprabhava-duḥkha** — suffering arising from affliction
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation in this unit
  - **function:** practical ground for the entire cultivation of `araṇā`.

- **dakṣiṇīyaviśeṣa** — one especially worthy of offerings / a distinguished worthy recipient
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only determination here
  - **function:** explains why the arhat himself may become a powerful object for the reactions of others.

- **tadālambana-kleśotpāda** — arising of affliction having that person as object-support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the object-structure implicit in the Kārikā
  - **function:** identifies exactly what `araṇā` seeks to prevent.

- **raṇa** — conflict, contention, afflictive disturbance
  - **status:** made technically operative by the prose definition of `araṇā`
  - **function:** names the event whose non-production defines the excellence.

- **sukhapratipadāmagratva** — supremacy with respect to ease of practice
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanation in this unit
  - **function:** grounds the fourth dhyāna as the meditative basis of `araṇā`.

- **sarvatraga** — universally operative / all-pervasive affliction
  - **status:** previously established technical term
  - **function:** explains why `avastuka` afflictions cannot be removed by the local preventive operation of `araṇā`.

## Doctrinal Determination

### `araṇā` is other-directed knowledge

The first-pass file correctly saw that `araṇā` is preventive rather than merely corrective. The Bhāṣya now establishes its other-directedness with much greater precision.

The object is not primarily one's own possible affliction. It is:

```text
the possible future affliction of another
    insofar as
I might become its object-condition
```

This is why `araṇā` cannot be reduced to personal serenity, harmlessness, or absence of hostility.

### The practitioner includes himself inside the causal field he knows

The arhat does not stand outside the situation as a spectator. He knows:

```text
beings suffer through kleśa
+
I am a socially and religiously potent object
+
my manner of appearing or acting may occasion kleśa
```

He therefore modifies his own conduct from within the very relational field his knowledge discloses.

This is a stronger determination than simple anticipation:

```text
knowledge of another
    → determination of oneself
    → changed relation
    → non-arising in the other
```

### Why only an immovable arhat?

The Bhāṣya supplies an explicit sufficiency condition. One who cannot invariably prevent afflictive disturbance within his own continuum cannot possess this perfected control over the conditions under which another's affliction might arise.

Thus the hierarchy is genetic:

```text
stability of one's own continuum
    → capacity to govern relational conditions
    → preventive non-production of kleśa in another
```

### Why only `savastuka` kleśas?

This restriction prevents an exaggerated reading of `araṇā` as universal control over another person's mental life.

It can prevent an affliction only where the relevant affliction depends upon a determinate object-condition that can be removed or transformed. It cannot prevent universally operative afflictions whose range is the entire level.

So `araṇā` is powerful precisely because it is **causally discriminating**, not magical.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation remains sound, but the Bhāṣya allows a considerably richer philosophical translation:

> Freedom from conflict is conventional knowledge, grounded in the fourth dhyāna and belonging to an immovable arhat. Arising only in a human continuum, it takes as its field future desire-domain afflictions having determinate objects, so that the practitioner may prevent himself from becoming the occasion for their arising in another.

This final clause is supplied by the Bhāṣya and is the decisive practical meaning of the compressed second pāda.

The gloss also sharpens `savastuka`: here it is not merely an abstract statement that the kleśa has an object, but the condition that makes preventive action possible. A determinate object-relation can be altered; an all-pervasive object-range cannot.

## Logical Determination

The structure is a practical conditional centered on a not-yet-actual determination:

```text
If
    I become object O
    under conditions C
then
    another may produce kleśa K

Araṇā knows:
    O + C → possible K

and practically determines:
    transform O/C

therefore:
    K does not arise
```

This is not knowledge of an already existing fact. It is knowledge of a **grounded possibility** whose ground includes the knower himself.

More precisely:

```text
other's possible determination
        ↓
known by the arhat
        ↓
self-determination of the arhat's conduct
        ↓
changed reciprocal situation
        ↓
non-arising of the other's kleśa
```

The knowledge is therefore practical without ceasing to be cognitive. Its truth is manifested in the transformation of the relation it knows.

## Organon Contact Point

This is one of the cleanest examples so far of knowledge passing immediately into Practical Reason.

The agent does not merely classify the state of another rational subject. Nor does it impose a rule externally. It represents the relational conditions under which its **own action or appearance** would become a ground for an undesirable determination in another, and changes itself accordingly.

For an Organon Rational Moral Agent, the form is unusually concrete:

```text
Model another's possible response
    not as an isolated prediction

but as a function including
    the agent's own proposed act

then determine the act
    so the harmful transition
    is not actualized
```

That is much richer than ordinary predictive safety. The agent itself occurs as a variable inside the causal model it reasons over.

The crucial limitation supplied by `savastuka` should also be retained computationally: a rational agent can be responsible for transforming **determinate conditions it actually supplies**; it cannot rationally claim universal control over every disposition or background condition in another agent.

### Project development

VAK 7.36 gives a useful new determination for the Rational Kośa: **practical reciprocity is self-determination mediated by knowledge of the other's possible determination**.

This should not be substituted for the deeper reciprocal mediation of `asi` in *The Dream of Philosophy*. But it supplies a precise species of that general demand within Practical Reason: neither pole is treated statically. The other's possible response determines the agent's self-determination, and that self-determination changes the field in which the other would respond.
