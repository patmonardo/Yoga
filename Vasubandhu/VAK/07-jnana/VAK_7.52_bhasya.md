# VAK_7.52 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> mṛtasyāpyastyadhiṣṭhānaṃ nāsthirasya apare tu na /
> ādāvekamanekena jitāyāṃ tu viparyayāt // VAK_7.52 //

**Best translation:**  
Sustaining determination can remain even after the constructor’s death, though not in the case of what is unstable; others deny this. At first one construction is produced by many construction-minds, but once mastery has been attained the relation is reversed.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.52.md`](./VAK_7.52.md)

## Source Cautions

- The source reads `adhhiṣṭhānāt` at 428|06; this is preserved as an evident e-text defect rather than silently normalized.
- The Bhāṣya reports a doctrinal disagreement over post-mortem `adhiṣṭhāna`; the file should not harmonize the two positions.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Can adhiṣṭhāna continue after death?

**Voice: Vasubandhu introducing the question**

The preceding verse established that a construction may be sustained while another mind operates. Vasubandhu now asks whether that sustaining determination continues only while the constructor is alive or whether it can remain after death.

### 2. First position: yes, if the supported thing is stable

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

The answer given first is yes. The continued existence of the skeletal framework of Ārya Mahākāśyapa is cited as evidence of his sustaining determination.

But this persistence is restricted:

```text
sthira
    stable configuration
        → adhiṣṭhāna may persist

asthira
    unstable configuration
        → no adhiṣṭhāna
```

The text contrasts the bones with flesh and similar unstable material.

### 3. Alternative position: no post-mortem adhiṣṭhāna

**Voice: Other teachers / `apare`**

Others deny that the sustaining determination of a dead person remains at all. They explain the continued skeletal structure instead by `devatānubhāva`, the efficacy or power of deities.

The dispute is therefore genuinely causal:

```text
same observed persistence

Position A:
    residual adhiṣṭhāna of Mahākāśyapa

Position B:
    power of deities
```

### 4. How many minds are required for how many constructions?

**Voice: Vasubandhu introducing the question**

The next question asks whether one construction-mind produces only one constructed object.

The answer is developmental.

At the beginning:

```text
many nirmāṇa-cittas
    → one nirmita
```

Once the superknowledge has been mastered:

```text
one nirmāṇa-citta
    → many nirmita forms
```

The upper limit is simply however many the adept wishes to construct.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
construction generated
    ↓
adhiṣṭhāna establishes persistence
    ↓
question: must the sustaining agent remain alive?
    ↓
Position A: not necessarily,
            but only a stable support persists

Position B: yes;
            apparent post-mortem persistence
            has another cause
```

The second half introduces a separate developmental law:

```text
initial acquisition
    many acts → one result

mastered abhijñā
    one act → many results
```

## New Technical Terms

- **sthira / asthira** — stable / unstable with respect to continued sustaining determination.
  - **status:** locally explicit in the Bhāṣya explanation.
  - **function:** determines which constructed or sustained configurations can persist under `adhiṣṭhāna`.

- **devatānubhāva** — efficacy or power of deities.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local argument.
  - **function:** alternative causal explanation for Mahākāśyapa's surviving skeletal framework.

- **jitā abhijñā** — a superknowledge that has been mastered or conquered.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `jitāyām`.
  - **function:** marks the transition from costly acquisition to efficient one-to-many operation.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse distinguishes at least three causal layers that should not be collapsed:

```text
production
    nirmāṇa-citta generates the construction

persistence
    adhiṣṭhāna sustains what has been produced

continued observation after death
    disputed causal explanation
```

The Bhāṣya therefore does not present persistence after death as uncontested doctrine.

The text also makes mastery measurable through a reversal in operational economy:

```text
before mastery
    many cognitive acts required for one result

after mastery
    one cognitive act capable of many results
```

## Translation Consequences

`adhiṣṭhāna` should continue to be rendered as **sustaining determination / establishment**, not merely as presence, attention, or supernatural blessing.

`jitāyām` is best understood here as **when the abhijñā has been mastered**, because the Bhāṣya explicitly supplies `jitāyāṃ tv abhijñāyām`.

The phrase `nāsthirasya` must be kept causally narrow: it states that unstable things do not persist under `adhiṣṭhāna`; it does not by itself establish a general metaphysics of durable versus impermanent entities.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.51 distinguished:

```text
cause of production
    ≠
condition of persistence
    ≠
current operator
```

VAK 7.52 now adds:

```text
persistence condition
    may outlast
    the active producer
```

but immediately subjects this to two constraints:

```text
stability constraint
causal-attribution dispute
```

The mastery relation is equally exact:

```text
learning regime
    many operations / one product

mastery regime
    one operation / many products
```

This is not simply “more power.” It is a change in the relation between operation-count and output-count.

A product may continue after the generating operation has ended, while the reason for that persistence remains independently testable.
