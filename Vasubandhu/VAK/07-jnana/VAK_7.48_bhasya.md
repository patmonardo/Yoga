# VAK 7.48 Bhāṣya — Ṛddhi as Samādhi, Movement, and Construction

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 7.48**

`ṛddhiḥ samādhir gamanaṃ nirmāṇaṃ ca gatistridhā /`
`śāstur manojavā anyeṣāṃ vāhiny apy ādhimokṣikī //`

**Working translation:**

Supernormal efficacy is concentration; through it there are movement and construction. Movement is threefold. For the Teacher there is mind-swift movement; for others there are also body-conveying and resolve-based movement.

See: `VAK_7.48.md`.

## Source Caution

The passage is textually straightforward. The phrase `manasa ivāsyā rava iti` is etymological explanation of `manojavā`; the operative doctrinal point is the comparison of the speed of movement to mind and the explicit restriction of this mode to a Buddha.

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

**Voice: Vasubandhu reporting the Vaibhāṣika position**

What is this supernormal efficacy? According to the Vaibhāṣika method:

> Supernormal efficacy is concentration.

It should be construed from the idea, “by this one succeeds/accomplishes.” What is accomplished through it?

> Movement and construction.

Movement is threefold: body-conveying movement, resolve-based movement, and mind-swift movement.

> The Teacher has mind-swift movement.

It is called mind-swift because its speed is like that of mind. This mode of movement belongs only to a Buddha, not to others, because even to an extremely distant place he goes within the time of a single arising of mind. Hence the Blessed One said that the domain of Buddhas is inconceivable.

The other two modes are also established for a Buddha without needing explicit statement.

> Others have body-conveying and resolve-based movement as well.

Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas possess body-conveying movement, carrying the body gradually as a bird does. They also possess resolve-based movement: by resolving that what is distant is near, they travel quickly.

Construction is twofold: desire-domain and form-domain.

The detailed analysis of construction continues with VAK 7.49.

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya proceeds in a strict hierarchy:

```text
ṛddhi
    ↓
samādhi
    ↓
what samādhi accomplishes
    ├── gamana
    │     ├── śarīravāhinī
    │     ├── ādhimokṣikī
    │     └── manojavā
    └── nirmāṇa
          ├── kāmāvacara
          └── rūpāvacara
```

The verse therefore does not define `ṛddhi` as one isolated miraculous act. It defines a **grounding capacity** and then differentiates the families of operation produced through that capacity.

## Voice Structure

- **Voice: Vasubandhu reporting Vaibhāṣika position** — `ṛddhiḥ samādhiḥ` and the formal classification.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanatory prose concerning the three modes of movement and their bearers.
- **Scriptural citation** — the Buddha-domain is called inconceivable.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Ṛddhi is not first defined by spectacle

The Bhāṣya begins not with flying, multiplication, or transformation, but with:

```text
ṛddhi = samādhi
```

The extraordinary acts are effects or functions of a cultivated concentration.

Thus:

```text
manifest extraordinary act
    presupposes
cultivated cognitive ground
```

### 2. One ground, multiple operational families

From the same general grounding power arise two broad classes:

```text
gamana
    change of spatial relation of the practitioner

nirmāṇa
    production of a constructed manifestation
```

This is an important separation of **locomotion** from **construction**.

### 3. Movement has three distinct mechanisms

#### Śarīravāhinī

The body itself is conveyed through space gradually, compared to a bird.

#### Ādhimokṣikī

A distant location is determined as near through `adhimokṣa`; rapid travel follows this resolve.

#### Manojavā

Movement occurs with the speed of mind. Vasubandhu restricts this to a Buddha: an extremely distant place can be reached within the time of one mental arising.

The three should therefore not be collapsed into one vague category of “teleportation.” They differ by operative mode.

## New Technical Terms

- **śarīravāhinī gati** — body-conveying movement; gradual conveyance of the body through space.  
  **status:** introduced here in this detailed form.  
  **function:** classification of supernormal locomotion.

- **ādhimokṣikī gati** — resolve-based movement; rapid travel produced by determining the distant as near.  
  **status:** new technical specialization of `adhimokṣa`.  
  **function:** classification of supernormal locomotion.

- **manojavā gati** — mind-swift movement; movement comparable in speed to mind.  
  **status:** new.  
  **function:** Buddha-specific mode of locomotion.

- **pakṣivat** — like a bird.  
  **status:** local simile.  
  **function:** clarifies the gradual character of body-conveying movement.

- **buddhaviṣaya** — the domain/range of a Buddha.  
  **status:** previously encountered doctrinal idea.  
  **function:** marks the specifically inconceivable range of Buddha-capacity.

## Doctrinal Determination

The hierarchy of bearers is explicit:

```text
Buddha
    śarīravāhinī
    ādhimokṣikī
    manojavā

Śrāvaka / Pratyekabuddha
    śarīravāhinī
    ādhimokṣikī
```

The difference is therefore not that Buddhas alone possess `ṛddhi`. The difference lies in the **mode and degree of operational immediacy**.

This continues the chapter's repeated distinction:

```text
shared capacity-kind
    ≠
identical perfection of capacity
```

## Translation Consequences

`ṛddhi` should not be reduced to “magic” or “miracle.” In this passage its technical architecture is:

```text
ṛddhi
    cultivated efficacy grounded in samādhi
```

Likewise, `nirmāṇa` should remain **construction / constructed manifestation**, not be blurred into generic “illusion.” The following verses will determine its precise mechanics.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.48 gives a strong capability architecture:

```text
Ground
    samādhi

Capability family
    gamana | nirmāṇa

Operational subtype
    śarīravāhinī | ādhimokṣikī | manojavā

Bearer constraint
    Buddha | Śrāvaka | Pratyekabuddha
```

So:

> A power is not adequately defined by its visible outcome; it must be specified by its ground, operation-family, mechanism, and bearer constraints.

The same apparent result—rapid change of location—can arise through different operative structures.
