# VAK_7.19 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ekajñānānvito rāgī prathame 'nāsravakṣaṇe /
> dvitīye tribhir ūrdhvas tu caturṣv ekaikavṛddhimān // VAK_7.19 //

**Best current translation:**  
The one still possessing attachment is endowed with one knowledge in the first uncontaminated moment; in the second, with three. Subsequently, in four moments, he possesses an increase of one at each.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.19.md`](./VAK_7.19.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 405|07–405|17.

This passage opens the second block of Jñānanirdeśa by asking not what the ten knowledges are, but **who possesses how many of them at successive stages**. The natural argument closes immediately before the new question at 405|18 concerning which knowledges are cultivated.

```text
idaṃ tu vaktavyam /
kaḥ katibhirjñānaiḥ samanvāgata iti /
pṛthagjanastāvadekena samanvāgataḥ /
saṃvṛtijñānena /
vītarāgastu paracittajñānenāpi /
āryaḥ punaḥ /

ekajñānānvito rāgī prathame 'nāsravakṣaṇe /

kāmāvītarāgo duḥkhadharmajñānakṣāntavekenaiva saṃvṛtijñānena samanvāgato bhavati /

dvitīye tribhiḥ

duḥkhadharmajñāne tribhiḥ saṃvṛtijñānadharmajñānaduḥkhajñānaiḥ /

ūrdhvastu caturṣvekaikavṛddhimān // VAkK_7.19 //

ataḥ paraṃ caturṣu kṣaṇeṣu ekaikajñānavṛddhirasya jñātavyā /
duḥkhe 'nvayajñāne 'nvayajñānaṃ vardhate /
samudayanirodhamārgadharmajñāneṣu samudayanirodhamārgajñānāni vardhanta iti mārgadharmajñāne saptabhirjñānaiḥ samanvāgato bhavati /
vītarāgastu sarvatrādhikena paracittajñānena samanvāgato veditavyaḥ /
```

## Natural Argument

### 1. The Bhāṣya changes the governing question

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The previous section classified the ten knowledges according to nature, aspect, level, support, and object-range. VAK 7.19 asks a new question:

```text
kaḥ katibhir jñānaiḥ samanvāgataḥ?
    Who is possessed of how many knowledges?
```

The key category is therefore no longer merely the occurrence of a cognition, but **possession** (`samanvāgama`) of classes of knowledge by a continuum.

### 2. The preliminary distinction: ordinary person and detached ordinary person

Before entering the noble path, Vasubandhu establishes the baseline.

A `pṛthagjana` possesses:

```text
1. saṃvṛtijñāna
    conventional knowledge
```

But one who is already detached (`vītarāga`) also possesses:

```text
2. paracittajñāna
    knowledge of another mind
```

This distinction controls the interpretation of `rāgī` in the verse.

### 3. `rāgī` is explicitly restricted to the practitioner not detached from the desire domain

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The prose glosses the verse's `rāgī` as:

```text
kāmāvītarāga
    one not detached from the desire domain
```

Thus the verse is not making a general psychological remark about passion. It is specifying the doctrinal status of the practitioner at entry into the Path of Seeing.

At the first uncontaminated moment:

```text
duḥkhadharmajñānakṣānti
    receptivity toward dharma-knowledge concerning suffering
```

this practitioner still possesses only:

```text
saṃvṛtijñāna
    conventional knowledge
```

The present `kṣānti` is not itself counted as knowledge. This applies the distinction established at the opening of Chapter 7 between the pure receptivities and knowledge proper.

### 4. The second moment produces a double increase in classification

At the second moment:

```text
duḥkhadharmajñāna
    dharma-knowledge concerning suffering
```

the practitioner possesses three knowledges:

```text
1. saṃvṛtijñāna
2. dharmajñāna
3. duḥkhajñāna
```

The Bhāṣya therefore confirms that the numerical count concerns **classes of knowledge possessed**, not a count of simultaneously occurring separate cognitions.

One present cognition falls under two newly actualized determinations:

```text
dharmajñāna
    according to its path/domain classification

duḥkhajñāna
    according to its truth-object and aspect
```

### 5. Four subsequent moments add one knowledge each

The Bhāṣya explains `caturṣv ekaikavṛddhimān` by naming the four additions.

```text
duḥkhe 'nvayajñāne
    anvayajñāna increases

samudayadharmajñāne
    samudayajñāna increases

nirodhadharmajñāne
    nirodhajñāna increases

mārgadharmajñāne
    mārgajñāna increases
```

Thus, by `mārgadharmajñāna`, the practitioner possesses seven knowledges:

```text
1. saṃvṛtijñāna
2. dharmajñāna
3. duḥkhajñāna
4. anvayajñāna
5. samudayajñāna
6. nirodhajñāna
7. mārgajñāna
```

Within the standard sixteen-moment sequence, these additions correspond to the fourth, sixth, tenth, and fourteenth moments. That numerical identification follows from the already established Path-of-Seeing sequence; the Bhāṣya itself names the four cognitive moments rather than numbering them here.

### 6. The previously detached practitioner always possesses one more

Vasubandhu closes the unit by returning to the preliminary distinction:

```text
vītarāgas tu sarvatra adhikena paracittajñānena samanvāgataḥ
```

The detached practitioner is to be understood at every stage as possessing one additional knowledge:

```text
paracittajñāna
    knowledge of another mind
```

Hence the verse's numerical series is specifically the series for the practitioner who enters the noble path without prior desire-domain detachment.

## Argument Reconstruction

The movement is exact:

```text
formal taxonomy of ten knowledges
    ↓
question of possession by a continuum
    ↓
baseline ordinary possession
    ↓
qualification by prior detachment
    ↓
first uncontaminated moment
    one possessed knowledge
    ↓
second moment
    three possessed knowledges
    ↓
four determinate additions
    ↓
seven possessed knowledges by mārgadharmajñāna
```

The decisive distinction is:

```text
occurring cognition
    ≠
complete set of knowledge-classes possessed
```

The Bhāṣya treats the path as a cumulative acquisition structure in which a present cognition may introduce a new classification while previously acquired knowledges remain possessed.

## New Technical Terms

- **samanvāgata / samanvāgama** — possessed of, endowed with; possession of a dharma by a continuum
  - **status:** technically operative in the Bhāṣya and only compressed as `anvita` in the Kārikā
  - **function:** establishes the governing relation counted in the verse

- **kāmāvītarāga** — not detached from the desire domain
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā's `rāgī`
  - **function:** restricts the subject of the numerical sequence

- **vītarāga** — detached / dispassionate practitioner
  - **status:** previously encountered, but newly decisive here as a contrast class
  - **function:** explains why some practitioners possess one additional knowledge

- **ekaikajñānavṛddhi** — increase by one knowledge at each specified stage
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `ekaikavṛddhimān`
  - **function:** explains the cumulative structure of possession

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes three doctrinal points especially clear.

First, `rāgī` must not be read loosely. Its exact force here is:

```text
not previously detached from kāmadhātu
```

Second, the first uncontaminated moment does **not** yet add a knowledge. The practitioner has entered the noble path through `duḥkhadharmajñānakṣānti`, but that receptivity remains distinct from knowledge proper.

Third, possession is cumulative and classificatory. At the second moment the same present cognition brings both `dharmajñāna` and `duḥkhajñāna` into possession because it is determinable under both divisions.

The verse therefore presupposes the whole classificatory architecture of VAK 7.1–7.18 and now places that architecture into the temporal-genetic order of the path.

## Translation Consequences

The existing literal translation is sound.

The Bhāṣya strongly confirms that `rāgī` should be understood contextually as:

> one not previously detached from the desire domain.

For philosophical exposition, however, it is useful to preserve the literal marker **still possessing attachment** while immediately supplying this doctrinal restriction.

`jñānānvita` is best understood through the Bhāṣya's repeated `samanvāgata`: **endowed with / possessing knowledge**, not merely accompanied by a cognition occurring in that instant.

The Kārikā file's distinction between possession and cultivation is also fully confirmed. The Bhāṣya closes VAK 7.19 and only then asks:

```text
atha kasyām avasthāyāṃ kati jñānāni bhāvyante?
    At what stage are how many knowledges cultivated?
```

That question opens VAK 7.20.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.19 introduces **genetic accumulation** into the previously established logical taxonomy.

The important structure is not merely:

```text
one
three
four
five
six
seven
```

but:

```text
new path-position
    ↓
new determinate cognition
    ↓
new knowledge-class becomes possessed
    ↓
prior determinations remain available
```

This yields a system whose content grows without discarding its earlier determinations.

The first two moments are particularly revealing:

```text
first uncontaminated moment
    true path has begun
    but no new knowledge-class is yet possessed

second moment
    the negative work of receptivity has completed its transition
    and positive determinate knowledge is now possessed
```

So the text distinguishes very sharply between:

```text
entry into an uncontaminated relation to truth
    and

completed possession of a determinate knowledge
```

The second moment also demonstrates that one actual cognition can instantiate more than one systematic determination without becoming several numerically distinct cognitions.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

VAK 7.19 gives a disciplined model for the growth of a rational system through **acquired determinations**.

The significant point is not an analogy to a knowledge graph but the genetic relation itself:

```text
a determinate act occurs
    ↓
a new systematic determination becomes possessed
    ↓
the earlier determinations are preserved
    ↓
the rational capacity of the continuum is enlarged
```

This is stronger than merely saying that the practitioner "learns more." The knowing subject is progressively re-determined by what has become genuinely possessed.

The Bhāṣya also prevents a premature identification of purity with completed cognition:

```text
anāsrava
    does not automatically mean
jñāna
```

The first pure moment is `kṣānti`; only the subsequent determination is knowledge. That distinction should remain important when we later compare the Path of Seeing with rational transition structures in the Organon.

## Transition to VAK 7.20

The end of the passage deliberately changes the question:

```text
VAK 7.19
    how many knowledges are possessed?

VAK 7.20
    which knowledges are cultivated, and in what temporal mode?
```

Thus 7.19 begins the new block by establishing the **state of acquired knowledge**, while 7.20 moves into **bhāvanā**, the active cultivation and future actualizability of knowledge.
