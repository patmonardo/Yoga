## Translation of the Bhāṣya

Those same two—dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge—again become four knowledges through division according to the truths: knowledge of suffering, knowledge of origination, knowledge of cessation, and knowledge of the Path, because they take those truths as their objects.

These same dharma-knowledges and subsequent knowledges, fourfold [according to the truths], when their nature is not view, are called knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge of non-arising.

The two, however, when first arisen ...

[Continuing with VAK 7.05:] When knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge of non-arising first arise, they are subsequent knowledge of suffering and subsequent knowledge of origination, possessing the aspects of suffering and origination and taking the aggregates belonging to the summit of existence (`bhavāgra`) as their object.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu performs two successive operations.

First, the two uncontaminated knowledges already established—`dharmajñāna` and `anvayajñāna`—are cross-determined by the Four Truths:

```text
dharmajñāna / anvayajñāna
        ×
duḥkha / samudaya / nirodha / mārga
```

This yields the four truth-knowledges:

```text
duḥkhajñāna
samudayajñāna
nirodhajñāna
mārgajñāna
```

The point is not that the earlier dharma/anvaya distinction disappears. Rather, these knowledges are generated from the same two uncontaminated forms according to their truth-object.

Second, the Bhāṣya locates `kṣayajñāna` and `anutpādajñāna` within this already articulated field. They are not two unrelated additions to the taxonomy. They are those fourfold dharma/anvaya knowledges under the further determination:

```text
adṛṣṭisvabhāva
    = having a nature that is no longer view
```

This directly recalls VAK 7.01, where exhaustion-knowledge and non-arising-knowledge were said to be knowledge but not view.

## The Important Correction: `prathamodite`

The earlier Kārikā-pass file treated `prathamodite` as though it meant “in the two first stated,” referring backward to dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge.

The Bhāṣya does not support that reading.

It explicitly glosses:

> **prathamotpanne tu kṣayajñānānutpādajñāne**

“when the knowledge of exhaustion and the knowledge of non-arising first arise.”

Thus:

```text
prathama-udite
    = first arisen / at their first arising
    ≠ first stated
```

Moreover, the syntax of VAK 7.04 is not self-contained. The final words carry directly into VAK 7.05:

```text
te punaḥ prathamodite
    duḥkhahetvanvayajñāne ...
```

At their first arising, the two terminal knowledges are:

```text
duḥkha-anvayajñāna
samudaya-anvayajñāna
```

with the Bhāṣya explaining that they take the Bhavāgra aggregates as object under the aspects of suffering and origination.

This is a substantive correction supplied by the Bhāṣya pass.

## New Technical Terms

- **satyabheda** — division according to the Truths
  - **status:** explicit in the Kārikā, clarified by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** generates suffering-, origination-, cessation-, and Path-knowledge from dharma/anvaya knowledge

- **adṛṣṭisvabhāva** — having a nature that is not view
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory determination here
  - **function:** explains how the same articulated pure cognition can receive the terminal names `kṣayajñāna` and `anutpādajñāna`

- **prathamotpanna** — first arisen
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of Kārikā `prathamodita`
  - **function:** fixes the temporal/path sense of the closing phrase and forces the construction to continue into VAK 7.05

- **bhavāgrika-skandha** — aggregates belonging to the summit of existence
  - **status:** introduced in the continuation governing VAK 7.05
  - **function:** object of the first arising of exhaustion- and non-arising-knowledge

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.04 shows that the taxonomy of knowledge is cumulative rather than flat.

```text
dharma / anvaya
    ↓ divided by truth-object
four truth-knowledges
    ↓ when no longer functioning as view
kṣayajñāna / anutpādajñāna
```

The terminal knowledges therefore do not abandon the prior cognitive structure. They are determinate transformations of it.

This also strengthens the result of VAK 7.01:

```text
jñāna
    can persist
while
dṛṣṭi-function
    has ceased
```

The same discriminative field reaches a completed mode in which further inspection is no longer its defining function.

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

The existing Kārikā file requires correction at its closing phrase.

Old working interpretation:

```text
prathamodite
    = first stated / previously announced
```

Bhāṣya-controlled interpretation:

```text
prathamodite
    = when first arisen
```

The sentence must also be allowed to run into VAK 7.05 rather than being forced into a self-contained 7.04 proposition.

A better transition translation is:

> Those same two become four knowledges through division according to the truths. Knowledge of non-arising and knowledge of exhaustion are those fourfold [dharma/anvaya knowledges] when their nature is no longer view. The two, however, **when first arisen** ...

with VAK 7.05 completing the construction.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The important logical feature is **determination by successive grounds**, not enumeration:

```text
pure cognition
    → domain determination
    → truth determination
    → completed/non-view determination
```

A later determination does not erase the earlier ones. It preserves them while changing the cognitive function.

This is enough for the Bhāṣya pass. The stronger comparison with Kantian rules of complete division belongs to later Organon synthesis.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.04 divides dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge according to the Four Truths, yielding suffering-, origination-, cessation-, and Path-knowledge. The Bhāṣya then identifies knowledge of exhaustion and knowledge of non-arising as these same fourfold uncontaminated knowledges when their nature is no longer `dṛṣṭi`. Most importantly, it corrects the earlier reading of `prathamodite`: the phrase means **“when first arisen”**, and its syntax continues into VAK 7.05, where the initial arising of the two terminal knowledges is specified as subsequent knowledge of suffering and origination directed toward the Bhavāgra aggregates.
