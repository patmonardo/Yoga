# VAK_7.33 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> mahākṛpā saṃvṛtidhīḥ saṃbhārākāragocaraiḥ /
> samatvād ādhimātryāc ca nānākaraṇam aṣṭadhā // VAK_7.33 //

**Best current translation:**

Great compassion is conventional cognition. It is called great because of its accumulation, cognitive mode, object-field, equality, and unsurpassed intensity; ordinary compassion and great compassion differ in eight respects.

### Source cautions

The reference e-text is not a critical edition. The prose around `samatva` and `adhimātratva` contains a visibly awkward or duplicated sequence (`abhimātratvena ... samavṛttitvāt` followed by `adhimātratvena ...`), so it is preserved here rather than silently normalized. Likewise `aṣṭdhā` is an e-text form for the expected `aṣṭadhā`. The final pair `aparitrāṇaparitrāṇataḥ atulyakaruṇāyanācca` is compressed; its functional sense is clear enough to mark a contrast in efficacy and unsurpassed compassion, but the exact lexical repair should remain provisional.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The discussion now turns to `mahākaruṇā`, great compassion, the eighteenth distinctive Buddha-quality.

The first determination is analytical rather than devotional: great compassion has `saṃvṛtijñāna`, conventional knowledge, as its nature. Vasubandhu immediately gives the reason. If it were not conventional knowledge, it could not have **all sentient beings** as its object and could not assume the modes of the **three kinds of suffering**.

The Bhāṣya then asks why this compassion is called *great*. The answer is not a single intensifier but a structured set of determinations:

- by **accumulation** (`saṃbhāra`), because it arises from the great accumulation of merit and knowledge;
- by **mode** (`ākāra`), because it takes the threefold suffering as its mode;
- by **object-support** (`ālambana` / kārikā `gocara`), because its range is the three domains;
- by **equality** (`samatva`), because it operates equally toward all beings;
- by **superior intensity** (`adhimātratva`), because there is no compassion greater than it.

The Bhāṣya then changes the question. It no longer asks why compassion is called *great*, but how ordinary `karuṇā` and `mahākaruṇā` differ. The answer is an **eightfold differentiation**.

## Argument Reconstruction

The passage has two nested analyses that should not be collapsed.

### 1. Why is great compassion “great”?

```text
mahākaruṇā
    ↓
conventional knowledge
    ↓
can range over all beings
and take threefold suffering as its mode

its greatness is determined by:
    accumulation
    mode
    object-field
    equality
    unsurpassed intensity
```

### 2. How does ordinary compassion differ from great compassion?

The Bhāṣya then compares them under eight coordinates:

```text
svabhāva      nature
ākāra         cognitive mode
ālambana      object-support
bhūmi         level
saṃtāna       continuum/person in whom it occurs
lābha         mode/condition of attainment
paritrāṇa     efficacy in rescue/protection
final superiority / unmatched compassion
```

The terse comparison implies paired values for ordinary compassion and great compassion. The text explicitly gives several of them:

- **nature:** ordinary compassion is associated with non-hatred; great compassion includes non-delusion/discriminative knowing;
- **mode:** one form of suffering versus all three forms;
- **object:** a restricted domain versus all three domains;
- **level:** the four dhyānas versus especially the fourth dhyāna as the Buddha-specific ground;
- **continuum:** disciples and others versus a Buddha-continuum;
- **attainment:** ordinary forms are obtained through detachment from desire-domain conditions, whereas great compassion belongs to the completion associated with detachment through Bhavāgra;
- **efficacy:** ordinary compassion does not have the same rescuing power; great compassion is effective in liberative assistance;
- **superiority:** great compassion is unsurpassed.

Because the final two compressed Sanskrit expressions are textually difficult in this e-text, the last two determinations should remain provisional rather than over-normalized.

## New Technical Terms

- **mahākaruṇā / mahākṛpā** — great compassion, the Buddha’s universally ranged compassion toward suffering beings  
  **status:** introduced here as a distinctive Buddha-quality  
  **function:** eighteenth `āveṇika-buddhadharma`; conventional cognitive function directed toward beings

- **mahāpuṇyajñānasaṃbhāra** — great accumulation of merit and knowledge  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-specific expansion in this passage  
  **function:** causal preparation explaining the greatness of great compassion

- **triduḥkhatā** — the threefold condition/forms of suffering  
  **status:** doctrinally familiar, newly functional here  
  **function:** `ākāra`, the mode under which great compassion apprehends suffering

- **samavṛtti** — equal operation toward all beings  
  **status:** local Bhāṣya determination  
  **function:** explains `samatva`, the impartiality of great compassion

- **adhimātratva** — unsurpassed or maximal intensity  
  **status:** local technical determination  
  **function:** explains why no stronger compassion exists beyond `mahākaruṇā`

- **paritrāṇa** — rescue, protection, effective deliverance  
  **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this local comparison  
  **function:** one of the coordinates distinguishing ordinary compassion from great compassion

## Doctrinal Determination

### Great compassion is conventional knowledge

The most important doctrinal point is the opening identification:

```text
mahākaruṇā
    = saṃvṛtijñānātmikā
```

This does not diminish compassion. It determines its object-field. Great compassion is directed toward beings as they appear within the conditioned world. It must therefore operate at the conventional level if it is to range over all beings and all three forms of suffering.

The passage consequently prevents a false opposition:

```text
ultimate liberating knowledge
    ≠
withdrawal from conventional cognition
```

The Buddha’s perfection includes mastery of the conventional field precisely because beings requiring liberation appear there.

### Five determinations of greatness

The Bhāṣya’s first analysis gives five grounds:

```text
saṃbhāra     vast causal preparation
ākāra        threefold suffering as mode
gocara       all three domains as field
samatva      equal activity toward all beings
adhimātratva unsurpassed intensity
```

These jointly explain the prefix `mahā-`.

### Eightfold difference

The eightfold comparison is not another list of Buddha-qualities. It is a contrastive feature matrix between ordinary compassion and Buddha-great-compassion.

This distinction is crucial for later reconstruction: the same generic term `karuṇā` receives different determinations according to nature, mode, object, level, bearer, acquisition-history, efficacy, and degree.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass rendering “great compassion is conventional cognition” is confirmed strongly by the Bhāṣya. The Bhāṣya explicitly says `saṃvṛtijñānātmikā mahākaruṇā`.

The second half should not be translated as though the kārikā itself simply enumerated eight independent headings. The verse first compresses the grounds of **greatness**, then states that the difference between ordinary and great compassion is eightfold; the full eightfold list belongs to the prose commentary.

Accordingly, the best working translation is:

> Great compassion has conventional knowledge as its nature. It is great through its accumulation, its cognitive mode, its object-field, its equality, and its unsurpassed intensity; ordinary compassion and great compassion differ in eight respects.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.33 is a strong example of **intensional determination before extensional enumeration**.

```text
mahākaruṇā
    ↓ defined by nature
saṃvṛtijñāna
    ↓ qualified by
saṃbhāra + ākāra + gocara + samatva + adhimātratva
    ↓ contrasted with ordinary compassion across
8 coordinates
```

The important point is that “great” is not an unanalyzed value judgment. It is generated by determinate relations.

The Bhāṣya therefore produces something very close to a structured feature comparison:

```text
Compassion
    nature
    mode
    object-field
    level
    bearer/continuum
    acquisition condition
    efficacy
    degree
```

The classification becomes intelligible because each named form is located through these determinations.

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained Organon contact is methodological.

Great compassion is a good example of a rational determination that becomes concrete only through a structured field of features. The noun alone does little analytical work. Its meaning emerges from its relations:

```text
source
mode
object
range
bearer
attainment
function
degree
```

This is precisely why the Kośa repeatedly rewards an NLP-first reconstruction: the philosophical content lies in the structured determinations connecting terms, not merely in extracting labels or counts.

No stronger identification with an Organon category is required here.

## Transition to VAK 7.34

The comparison of ordinary compassion and Buddha-great-compassion raises a broader question immediately taken up next: if Buddhas possess these distinctive perfections, **are all Buddhas identical in every respect?** VAK 7.34 answers by distinguishing the respects in which all Buddhas are equal from those in which they differ.
