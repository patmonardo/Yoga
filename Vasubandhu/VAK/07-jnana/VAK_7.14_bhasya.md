# VAK_7.14 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> tridhādyaṃ kuśalāny anyāni ādyaṃ sarvāsu bhūmiṣu /
> dharmākhyaṃ ṣaṭsu navasu tv anvayākhyaṃ tathaiva ṣaṭ // VAK_7.14 //

**Best translation:**  
The first is threefold; the others are wholesome. The first occurs in all spheres. That called dharma-knowledge occurs in six, while that called anvaya-knowledge occurs in nine; likewise the six.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.14.md`](./VAK_7.14.md)

## Translation

Conventional knowledge is called "the first" because it occurs first in the verse-list. It is threefold: wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate. The other nine knowledges are wholesome only.

"The first in all spheres": conventional knowledge occurs from the desire domain up through the summit of existence.

"That called dharma in six": dharma-knowledge occurs in the four dhyānas, the preliminary concentration (*anāgamya*), and the intermediate dhyāna.

"But that called anvaya in nine": anvaya-knowledge occurs in those same six spheres and also in the first three formless attainments.

"Likewise the six": the knowledges of suffering, origin, cessation, path, exhaustion, and non-arising occur, without distinction, in those same nine spheres. When distinguished according to subsumption, however, insofar as they are included in dharma-knowledge they occur in six; insofar as they are included in anvaya-knowledge they occur in nine.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Why `ādyam` means conventional knowledge

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu does not leave the pronoun implicit. `ādyam`, "the first," means `saṃvṛtijñāna` because conventional knowledge stands first in the tenfold enumeration.

The Bhāṣya then immediately specifies the threefold division:

```text
saṃvṛtijñāna
    kuśala
    akuśala
    avyākṛta
```

This is the only one of the ten knowledges whose ethical quality is threefold.

### 2. The remaining nine are wholesome only

The other nine knowledges are `kuśala` without qualification.

This is an important restriction. Their place in the tenfold system is not morally neutral merely because they are types of cognition. Their occurrence belongs intrinsically to the wholesome architecture of the Path.

### 3. Conventional knowledge has maximal vertical distribution

Vasubandhu glosses "in all spheres" with:

```text
kāmadhātī yāvad bhavāgre
    from the desire domain up to the summit of existence
```

Thus conventional knowledge has the broadest bhūmi-distribution of the ten.

Its universality of occurrence should not be confused with superiority of cognition. It is precisely conventional cognition that can occur throughout the entire conditioned hierarchy.

### 4. Dharma-knowledge is restricted to six spheres

The six are:

```text
anāgamya
    preliminary concentration

dhyānāntara
    intermediate dhyāna

four dhyānas
```

Thus:

```text
4 dhyānas
+ anāgamya
+ dhyānāntara
= 6
```

### 5. Anvaya-knowledge extends through nine spheres

Anvaya-knowledge occurs in those same six plus the first three formless attainments:

```text
6
+ ārūpyatraya
= 9
```

The summit of existence is therefore not added as a tenth sphere for anvaya-knowledge.

### 6. The six other knowledges inherit this structure

The six are:

```text
duḥkhajñāna
samudayajñāna
nirodhajñāna
mārgajñāna
kṣayajñāna
anutpādajñāna
```

Vasubandhu gives two ways of speaking about them.

**Without differentiation (`abhedena`)**:

```text
all six
    occur across the same nine spheres
```

**With differentiation (`bhedena`)**:

```text
when subsumed under dharmajñāna
    → six spheres

when subsumed under anvayajñāna
    → nine spheres
```

This is the decisive structural point of the Bhāṣya. A single named knowledge can receive a different bhūmi-distribution according to the broader knowledge under which it is classified.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.14 changes the axis of Chapter 7's analysis.

The preceding verses determined knowledge through:

```text
aspect
object-support
counteractive function
purity
```

This verse now adds:

```text
ethical quality
bhūmi of occurrence/acquisition
structural subsumption
```

The resulting matrix is:

| Knowledge | Ethical range | Bhūmi range |
|---|---|---|
| saṃvṛtijñāna | wholesome / unwholesome / indeterminate | all, desire domain through bhavāgra |
| dharmajñāna | wholesome | six |
| anvayajñāna | wholesome | nine |
| four truth knowledges + kṣaya + anutpāda | wholesome | six when dharma-subsumed; nine when anvaya-subsumed |

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes **identity of named cognition** from **the larger cognitive structure under which it is instantiated**.

## New Technical Terms

- **bhavāgra** — "summit of existence," the highest conditioned sphere; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** upper boundary of conventional knowledge's bhūmi-range.
- **anāgamya** — preliminary concentration immediately accessible before the first dhyāna; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** one of the six supports of dharma-knowledge.
- **dhyānāntara** — intermediate dhyāna/concentration; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** one of the six supports of dharma-knowledge.
- **ārūpyatraya** — the first three formless attainments; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** the three additional spheres that extend anvaya-knowledge from six to nine.
- **abheda / bheda** — without distinction / with distinction; **status:** common vocabulary, newly important here; **function:** controls whether the six knowledges are counted simply across nine spheres or differentiated according to their subsumption under dharma- or anvaya-knowledge.
- **saṃgṛhīta** — included, subsumed, comprised within; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** expresses the classificatory relation by which a truth-knowledge inherits the bhūmi-range of dharmajñāna or anvayajñāna.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā-pass reading is confirmed, with one important sharpening:

`tathaiva ṣaṭ` should not be understood merely as saying that the six knowledges independently possess two unrelated distributions. The Bhāṣya explicitly says:

```text
abhedena
    nine spheres

bhedena
    six when dharmajñāna-saṃgṛhīta
    nine when anvayajñāna-saṃgṛhīta
```

So the distribution is governed by **subsumption**.

This makes `saṃgṛhīta` a key logical relation for the chapter.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.14 gives a clean example of layered classification.

```text
named knowledge
    ≠ sufficient complete specification
```

A knowledge must additionally be determined by:

```text
ethical quality
bhūmi
higher classificatory inclusion
```

The six truth/terminal knowledges show this especially clearly:

```text
one knowledge-name
    ↓
classified under dharmajñāna or anvayajñāna
    ↓
different legitimate sphere-range
```

This is not contradiction. The differing predicates belong to different determinate relations.

The Bhāṣya therefore continues the Chapter 7 program of replacing flat enumeration with relationally grounded classification.

## Light Organon Contact

The useful point here is modest but strong:

> A determination can inherit properties from a higher classificatory structure without losing its own identity.

The Kośa is already working with something like typed subsumption:

```text
truth-knowledge
    is subsumed under
        dharmajñāna
        or
        anvayajñāna

and thereby inherits
    a determinate bhūmi-range
```

That is structurally richer than simple fact collection. But the present artifact leaves the larger Organon interpretation for the later Comment pass.
