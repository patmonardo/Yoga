# VAK_7.49_bhasya — Realm-Specific Construction and Its Cognitive Products

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 7.49**

`kāmāptaṃ nirmitaṃ bāhyaṃ caturāyatanaṃ dvidhā /`
`rūpāptaṃ dve tu nirmāṇacittaistāni caturdaśa //`

**Current best translation:**

> A desire-realm construction is external and consists of four spheres; it is twofold. A form-realm construction consists of two. These constructions are produced by fourteen construction-minds.

## Source Cautions

The line `dvacyāyātanaṃ nirmiṇotītyapare` at 426|07–08 is textually compressed and likely corrupt in transmission. It is retained without silent repair. The doctrinal point is clear enough: an alternative view is introduced concerning the number of spheres constructed.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The construction discussed under `ṛddhi` is first divided by realm: desire-realm and form-realm.

A desire-realm construction is external (`bāhya`) and has four objective spheres as its nature:

- visible form (`rūpa`)
- smell (`gandha`)
- taste (`rasa`)
- tangible (`spraṣṭavya`)

It is twofold according to whether the construction is connected with one's own body or another body (`sva-para-śarīra-saṃbaddha`).

A form-realm construction has only two spheres:

- visible form
- tangible

because smell and taste are absent there. It too is twofold in the same own-body / other-body sense.

Thus the two realms together yield eight broad modes of construction.

Vasubandhu then raises a technical problem: if one born in the form realm produces a desire-realm construction containing smell and taste, does the constructor thereby become endowed with smell and taste? No. The relation is compared with clothes and ornaments: something may be associated with what is produced without the producer thereby acquiring its properties.

An alternative school view is briefly recorded concerning a more restricted construction of spheres.

Vasubandhu then asks whether the construction itself is produced directly by the superknowledge (`abhijñā`). He explicitly answers no. It is produced by the **results of the abhijñā** (`abhijñā-phala`), namely the fourteen `nirmāṇa-cittas`.

The fourteen are distributed as products of the four dhyānas:

- first dhyāna: two construction-minds
- second dhyāna: three
- third dhyāna: four
- fourth dhyāna: five

Each dhyāna can produce construction-minds belonging to its own ground and lower grounds.

## Translation

> Construction is of the desire realm and of the form realm. First, a desire-realm construction is external and has the four spheres of visible form, taste, smell, and tangibility as its nature. It is twofold, being connected either with one's own body or with another body. A form-realm construction has two spheres, visible form and tangibility, because smell and taste are absent there; it too is twofold in the same way. Thus there are four modes of construction in the desire realm and likewise four in the form realm, eight in summary. If one born in the form realm constructs something belonging to the desire realm, why does one not thereby become endowed with smell and taste? As with clothes and ornaments, there is no such acquisition. Others hold a different view concerning the number of spheres constructed. Is construction produced directly by the superknowledge? No. By what, then? By its results: these are the fourteen construction-minds. Of these fourteen, respectively two through five are products of the dhyānas: the first dhyāna has two construction-minds, belonging to the desire realm and the first dhyāna; the second has three, belonging to the desire realm, first dhyāna, and second dhyāna; and similarly the third and fourth have four and five.

## Voice Attribution

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — principal exposition and questions.
- **Voice: Other school position** — `apare`, introducing an alternative account of the constructed spheres.
- **Voice: uncertain** — the transmitted wording of that alternative view is corrupt enough that its exact formulation should not be forced.

## New Technical Terms

- **sva-para-śarīra-saṃbaddha** — connected with one's own body or another body.
  - **status:** new in this immediate construction discussion.
  - **function:** defines the explicit twofold division (`dvidhā`) of construction.

- **abhijñā-phala** — result or product of a superknowledge.
  - **status:** new technical relation in this block.
  - **function:** distinguishes the superknowledge itself from the cognitive state that directly performs construction.

- **nirmāṇa-citta** — construction-mind; a cognition whose operative function is the production of a constructed form.
  - **status:** introduced by the Kārikā, technically specified by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** immediate cognitive cause of construction.

- **samanvāgama** — possession/acquisition or being endowed with a dharma.
  - **status:** previously encountered Abhidharma vocabulary.
  - **function:** used to deny that producing an object with smell/taste entails acquiring smell/taste oneself.

- **dhyāna-phala** — result of a dhyāna.
  - **status:** previously encountered relation, newly applied to construction-minds.
  - **function:** specifies the ground-dependent generation of the fourteen construction-minds.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya requires several corrections to an overly speculative first-pass reading.

First, `dvidhā` is not an abstract distinction of construction-mode. It is explicitly:

```text
construction connected with one's own body
construction connected with another body
```

Second, the omission of sound is not here explained through a state/event ontology. The text simply defines the constructed object by the listed external spheres. In the form realm smell and taste are absent; hence only visible form and tangibility remain.

Third, and most importantly:

```text
abhijñā
    ≠ direct constructor

abhijñā-phala
    → nirmāṇa-citta
    → nirmāṇa
```

The superknowledge supplies the causal power whose result is a specialized construction-mind. The specialized cognitive result performs the construction.

## The Fourteen Construction-Minds

The count is systematic:

```text
first dhyāna     2
second dhyāna    3
third dhyāna     4
fourth dhyāna    5
                  —
                 14
```

The rule is cumulative downward reach:

```text
a dhyāna produces construction-minds
of its own ground and lower grounds
```

Thus the first dhyāna reaches the desire realm and itself; the second reaches desire + first + second; and so on.

This anticipates VAK 7.50, which will explicitly state the prohibition on an upper-ground construction-mind being the result of a lower dhyāna.

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya distinguish four levels that should not be collapsed:

```text
source capacity
    abhijñā

resulting operator
    abhijñā-phala / nirmāṇa-citta

operation
    nirmāṇa

product
    nirmita
```

This yields the general structure:

```text
capacity
    → specialized resultant operator
    → operation
    → typed product
```

A second logical principle concerns property transmission:

```text
producer creates object with property P
    does not entail
producer acquires property P
```

Vasubandhu's clothes-and-ornaments analogy makes this explicit.

## Translation Consequences

- `bāhya` should be retained as **external**, not weakened to merely "appearing outwardly."
- `caturāyatana` means four external **spheres**, not four vague sensory features.
- `dvidhā` must be translated through the Bhāṣya as connection with one's own body or another body.
- `nirmāṇacitta` should be rendered **construction-mind** or **construction cognition**, preserving its status as a determinate cognitive operator.
- `abhijñā-phala` must remain visible in interpretation because it blocks identification of the abhijñā itself with the immediate act of construction.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage is unusually useful for a capability architecture, but only if its distinctions are preserved rather than generalized away.

A capacity should not be modeled simply as:

```text
canConstruct = true
```

The Kośa gives a layered structure:

```text
Capability
    abhijñā

Derived operator
    nirmāṇa-citta

Allowed target ground
    own or lower

Construction relation
    own-body | other-body

Product profile
    realm-specific āyatana structure
```

The most important software-style lesson is therefore not "objects are feature bundles" in the abstract. It is more exact:

> **A high-level capability may generate a specialized operator, and the operator—not the capability label itself—performs the concrete operation under ground and product constraints.**

That is a disciplined way to make a Feature / FeatStruct architecture flexible without making causal roles ambiguous.

## Bridge to VAK 7.50

VAK 7.49 establishes the fourteen construction-minds and their cumulative relation to the four dhyānas. VAK 7.50 immediately continues by stating the directional constraint:

```text
no construction-mind of a higher ground
is the result of a lower dhyāna
```

and then explains how construction-minds are acquired and what cognitive states may immediately precede or follow them.
