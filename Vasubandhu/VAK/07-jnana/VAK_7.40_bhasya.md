# VAK_7.40 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> kāmadhyāneṣu dharme vit vāci prathamakāmayoḥ /
> vikalābhir na tallābhī ṣaḍ ete prāntakoṭikāḥ // VAK_7.40 //

**Best current translation:**

The discriminating knowledge concerning dharma occurs in the desire domain and the dhyānas; that concerning speech occurs in the first dhyāna and the desire domain. One does not possess the discriminating knowledges incompletely. These six qualities are connected with the final-limit cultivation.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.40.md`](./VAK_7.40.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 419|12–420|13, with the definition of `prāntakoṭika` continuing into VAK 7.41.

```text
kāmadhyāneṣu dharme vit

dharmapratisaṃvit pañcabhūmikā kāmadhātucaturthadhyānasaṃgṛhītā ūrdhvaṃ nāmakāyābhāvāt /

vāci prathamakāmayoḥ /

vāṅniruktir ity eko 'rthaḥ /
niruktipratisaṃvitkāmadhātuprathamadhyānabhūmikā ūrdhvaṃ vitarkābhāvāt /

prajñaptau tu pratisaṃvidāmeva nirdeśaḥ /
...

yasya caikā tasyāvaśyaṃ catasraḥ pratisaṃvidī bhavanti /

vikalābhirna tallābhī

nahi vikalābhistābhiḥ pratisaṃvillābhī bhavati /

ye caita upadiṣṭā araṇādayo guṇāḥ /

ṣaḍete prāntakoṭikāḥ // VAkK_7.40 //

prāntakoṭikadhyānavalenaiṣāṃ lābhaḥ /
```

### Source caution

The transmitted Kārikā reading `dharme vit` is compressed and awkward. The Bhāṣya removes the substantive ambiguity by explicitly interpreting the pāda as `dharmapratisaṃvit pañcabhūmikā`.

The prose immediately following the verse begins the technical analysis of `prāntakoṭika`; that analysis continues into VAK 7.41 and should not be forced entirely into the present unit.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The `dharma-pratisaṃvid` occurs on five levels:

```text
kāmadhātu
+ first dhyāna
+ second dhyāna
+ third dhyāna
+ fourth dhyāna
```

The stated reason is decisive: above the fourth dhyāna there are no `nāmakāya` complexes. Since this discriminating knowledge has names and verbal designations as its field, its possible level is constrained by the existence of its object.

The `nirukti-pratisaṃvid` has an even narrower range:

```text
kāmadhātu
+ first dhyāna
```

Vasubandhu glosses `vāk` and `nirukti` as equivalent here and explains the upper limit by the absence of `vitarka` beyond the first dhyāna.

The Bhāṣya then gives an important rule of acquisition:

> Whoever has one of the pratisaṃvids necessarily has all four.

Therefore one is not called a possessor of the pratisaṃvids through an incomplete subset.

Finally Vasubandhu gathers the qualities beginning with `araṇā` as six `prāntakoṭika` qualities and says that their acquisition depends upon the power of `prāntakoṭika-dhyāna`. The full definition of that dhyāna follows under VAK 7.41.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
dharma-pratisaṃvid

```

## New Technical Terms

- **pañcabhūmika** — belonging to five levels
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explicit determination here
  - **function:** states the level-range of `dharma-pratisaṃvid`.

- **nāmakāyābhāva** — absence of name-complexes
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory condition
  - **function:** grounds the upper limit of `dharma-pratisaṃvid`.

- **vāṅ-nirukti** — speech and linguistic explanation treated as one object-field
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss
  - **function:** explains the Kārikā's `vāci`.

- **akṛtakauśala** — lacking cultivated skill
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in the preceding preparatory discussion
  - **function:** explains why the pratisaṃvids require prior mastery rather than arising accidentally.

- **pratisaṃvillābhin** — one who has acquired the pratisaṃvids
  - **status:** Bhāṣya operative expression
  - **function:** distinguishes genuine acquisition from fragmentary resemblance.

- **prāntakoṭika-dhyāna** — final-limit dhyāna
  - **status:** becomes technically operative here; defined in the next unit
  - **function:** causal basis for acquisition of the six exceptional qualities.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Object and level are internally related

The Bhāṣya does not assign levels arbitrarily. The level-range follows from what the capacity knows and from what cognitive-linguistic functions remain available there.

```text
object-condition
    determines
admissible level
```

For `dharma-pratisaṃvid`, no `nāmakāya` means no corresponding discriminating mastery. For `nirukti-pratisaṃvid`, the absence of `vitarka` marks the upper boundary of operative verbal analysis.

### The four pratisaṃvids are acquired as a system

The strongest proposition in this verse is:

```text
yasya ekā
    tasya avaśyaṃ catasraḥ
```

If one is genuinely present, all four necessarily are.

This does not mean that their objects, levels, or cognitive constitutions are identical. VAK 7.38–7.40 has just differentiated them carefully. Rather, differentiated capacities constitute one integrated competence.

Thus:

```text
difference of function
    +
necessary co-acquisition
        =
systematic unity
```

### Six qualities depend on a common perfected basis

The verse closes by gathering the six qualities beginning with `araṇā` under `prāntakoṭika`. The Bhāṣya immediately explains their acquisition through the power of `prāntakoṭika-dhyāna`.

The detailed meaning of that term is deferred to 7.41, where its sixfold cultivation, conformity to all levels, and reaching the maximum of increase are explained.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā-pass translation needs clarification in two places.

First, `kāmadhyāneṣu dharme` means specifically that `dharma-pratisaṃvid` occurs on five levels: desire realm plus the four dhyānas.

Second, `vikalābhir na tallābhī` should not be generalized merely as “a perfected quality cannot be fragmentary.” Its exact local claim is stronger and narrower:

> One who has acquired the pratisaṃvids does not possess them incompletely; possession of one entails possession of all four.

This should govern future translations.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.40 gives a clean distinction between **functional differentiation** and **systemic acquisition**.

```text
dharma
artha
nirukti
pratibhāna
```

are not interchangeable functions.

Yet:

```text
true acquisition of any one
    implies
acquisition of the fourfold system
```

This is stronger than a list of correlated skills. It is a whole whose members remain specifically differentiated.

The rule therefore is:

```text
systematic unity
    ≠ undifferentiated identity

systematic unity
    = differentiated moments
      whose genuine possession is reciprocal
```

## Organon Contact Point

This is an unusually clean Rational Kośa model of an integrated rational capacity.

A language-capable rational agent cannot count as possessing the complete discriminating competence merely because one isolated sub-function appears locally successful. Name, meaning, linguistic analysis, and free Path-governed articulation are distinguishable, yet at the level of genuine `pratisaṃvid` they belong together.

That gives a useful discipline for deliberate flexibility in the Organon Feature system:

```text
flexible function
    does not mean
independent fragment

feature identity
    may be locally differentiated
    while its full rational competence
    is systemically constrained
```

This is a local structural contact only; the Kośa's specific doctrine remains primary.
