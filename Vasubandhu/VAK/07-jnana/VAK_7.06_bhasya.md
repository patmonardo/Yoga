# VAK_7.06 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ta dharmānvayadhīpakṣyam anyo 'nyaṃ darśanakṣaṇau /
> śrāvako vetti khaṅgas trīn sarvān buddho 'prayogataḥ // VAK_7.06 //

**Best current translation:**  
Knowledge of another mind belonging to the dharma-knowledge side does not know cognition belonging to the anvaya-knowledge side, and vice versa. A disciple knows two moments of the Path of Seeing; a solitary Buddha knows three; a Buddha knows them all without preparatory application.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.06.md`](./VAK_7.06.md)

### Source caution

The e-text transmits `ta dharmānvayadhīpakṣyam`, matching the Kārikā source's difficult opening. The Bhāṣya itself makes the intended construction clear through the paired prose expressions `dharmajñānapakṣyaṃ` and `anvayajñānapakṣyaṃ`. No silent emendation is required for the Bhāṣya analysis.

The e-text also contains an evident corruption at `darjñanamārga` for `darśanamārga`; this is left untouched in the quoted source and normalized only in translation.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu continues the restriction of knowledge of another mind established in VAK 7.05.

First, `paracittajñāna` remains divided according to the two uncontaminated cognitive orders:

```text
dharmajñāna-side paracittajñāna
    does not know
anvayajñāna-side citta

anvayajñāna-side paracittajñāna
    does not know
dharmajñāna-side citta
```

The reason is object-domain: the two sides take respectively the desire domain and the higher domains as their range.

Second, Vasubandhu makes an important distinction concerning the Path of Seeing:

```text
paracittajñāna
    is not itself present in darśanamārga

but

darśanamārga
    can be an object of paracittajñāna
```

Thus the verse is not describing mutual cognition between moments internal to the Path of Seeing. It describes how an external knowledge of another mind can apprehend moments belonging to that path.

Third, the range differs by practitioner.

### Śrāvaka

A disciple knows two moments:

```text
duḥkhe dharmajñānakṣānti
    acceptance of dharma-knowledge concerning suffering

duḥkhe dharmajñāna
    dharma-knowledge concerning suffering
```

To know the later anvaya-side moments requires another preparatory application. By the time that application is completed, the observed practitioner has already reached the sixteenth moment; therefore the intervening moments are not known.

### Pratyekabuddha

A pratyekabuddha knows three moments. Vasubandhu's main explanation gives:

```text
first moment
second moment
eighth moment = samudayānvayajñāna
```

because the preparatory application is gentler or quicker (`mṛduprayogatva`).

He also records another opinion:

```text
first
second
fifteenth
```

The Bhāṣya does not settle this alternative here beyond reporting it.

### Buddha

A Buddha knows all moments of the Path of Seeing:

```text
sarvān eva darśanamārgakṣaṇān
```

and does so:

```text
aprayogeṇa
    without preparatory application
```

The Buddha's superiority therefore concerns both extension and mode of access.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
1. Knowledge of another mind is already limited by domain.

2. Dharma-side and anvaya-side cognition therefore do not mutually penetrate.

3. Knowledge of another mind is absent from the Path of Seeing itself.

4. Nevertheless the Path of Seeing can become its object.

5. Knowing a sequence of moments requires preparatory application for lesser practitioners.

6. Because the path advances while that preparation occurs, a śrāvaka catches only two moments.

7. A pratyekabuddha, through a lighter preparatory process, catches three.

8. A Buddha requires no such preparation and knows the entire sequence.
```

The decisive principle is therefore not merely spiritual rank but the relation between:

```text
speed of cognitive preparation
    /
speed of the object-sequence
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — explanation of the dharma/anvaya restriction, absence of `paracittajñāna` within the Path of Seeing, and the ranges of śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, and Buddha.

**Voice: other teachers / alternative tradition** — `prathamadvitīyapañcadaśānityapare`, the alternative identification of the pratyekabuddha's three known moments.

## New Technical Terms

- **darśanamārga-kṣaṇa** — a determinate moment of the Path of Seeing. **Status:** previously encountered. **Function:** object of knowledge of another mind in this passage.
- **prayoga** — preparatory application or cognitive preparation. **Status:** previously encountered, newly decisive here. **Function:** explains why lesser knowers cannot follow the rapidly advancing Path-of-Seeing sequence continuously.
- **mṛduprayogatva** — lightness/gentleness of preparatory application. **Status:** new in this local Bhāṣya context. **Function:** explains the pratyekabuddha's greater range relative to a śrāvaka.
- **dharmajñānapakṣya / anvayajñānapakṣya** — belonging to the dharma-knowledge side / subsequent-knowledge side. **Status:** new explicit Bhāṣya formulation. **Function:** fixes the mutual object-domain restriction of `paracittajñāna`.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.06 adds two major constraints to the doctrine of knowledge of another mind.

### 1. Cognitive domain remains operative

Exceptional cognition does not erase the distinction established in VAK 7.02–7.03:

```text
kāma-domain
    ↔ dharmajñāna-side

higher domains
    ↔ anvayajñāna-side
```

The very power that knows another cognition remains indexed to these object-ranges.

### 2. The Path of Seeing is knowable but does not itself contain this knowledge

This is the most important correction to the first-pass Kārikā file:

```text
darśanamārge paracittajñānaṃ nāsti
    knowledge of another mind does not occur in the Path of Seeing

tadālambanaṃ tv asti
    but that Path may be its object
```

The distinction is structurally exact:

```text
location of a cognition
    ≠
object of that cognition
```

### 3. Cognitive range depends on preparation

The hierarchy is:

```text
śrāvaka
    two moments
    preparatory delay

pratyekabuddha
    three moments
    lighter preparation

Buddha
    all moments
    no preparation
```

The Buddha is universal here not because temporal articulation disappears, but because no preparatory gap interrupts cognition of the articulated sequence.

## Translation Consequence for Kārikā

The existing Kārikā analysis should be corrected in one important respect.

It currently interprets `darśanakṣaṇau` as though the two moments of seeing themselves "do not know one another." The Bhāṣya does not support that reading.

The prose instead establishes:

```text
paracittajñāna is absent within darśanamārga
but can take darśanamārga as object
```

and then explains `darśanakṣaṇau` as the **two Path-of-Seeing moments known by a śrāvaka**.

A better philosophical translation is therefore:

> Knowledge of another mind belonging to the dharma-knowledge side does not know cognition belonging to the anvaya-knowledge side, and vice versa. Of the moments of the Path of Seeing, a disciple knows two, a solitary Buddha three, and a Buddha all without preparatory application.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest structural point is simple and should not be overextended:

```text
cognitive power
    ≠ unrestricted cognition
```

Knowledge is indexed by:

```text
object-domain
location in the path
preparatory operation
speed of access
rank of knower
```

The distinction:

```text
being in a cognitive process
    /
knowing that process as object
```

is especially valuable. The Bhāṣya treats these as categorically different relations.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.06 further restricts `paracittajñāna`. Dharma-side knowledge of another mind does not know anvaya-side cognition and vice versa because the two operate over different domains. Knowledge of another mind is not itself present in the Path of Seeing, although that path can be known by it. A śrāvaka knows two Path-of-Seeing moments, a pratyekabuddha three, and a Buddha all sixteen without preparatory application. The Bhāṣya thereby explains cognitive hierarchy through exact differences of range and preparation rather than through an undifferentiated notion of clairvoyance.
