# VAK_7.09 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dharmajñānaṃ nirodhe yan mārge vā bhāvanāpathe /
> tridhātupratipakṣas tat kāmadhātos tu nānvayam // VAK_7.09 //

**Best translation:**  
That dharma-knowledge which, in the Path of Cultivation, concerns cessation or the path is a counteragent to the three realms; but anvaya-knowledge is not a counteragent to the desire realm.

### Source caution

The e-text transmits `pratīpakṣa` / `poratipakṣa` in places. These are treated as evident transmission or input errors for `pratipakṣa`; the quoted source is not silently normalized.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu resumes the criterion of **pratipakṣa**, counteractive function, introduced as one of the seven grounds by which the ten knowledges are distinguished in VAK 7.08.

The starting point is the established statement that dharma-knowledge is the counteragent to the desire domain. The Bhāṣya then adds an important restriction and extension: not every form of dharma-knowledge is universally counteractive. Rather, in the **Path of Cultivation**, the dharma-knowledges whose objects are cessation and path function as counteragents with respect to all three domains.

The contrast is categorical: anvaya-knowledge is **never** a counteragent to the desire domain.

Thus the distinction between dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge is not exhausted by their object-ranges. It is also determined by what each cognition can effectively oppose in the path.

## Literal Prose Translation

Because the complete [dharma-knowledge] is the counteragent to the complete [desire domain], dharma-knowledge was said to be the counteragent to the desire domain.

But more specifically:

> That dharma-knowledge which, with respect to cessation or path, belongs to the Path of Cultivation is a counteragent to the three domains.

The knowledges of cessation and path included in the Path of Cultivation are counteragents to the three domains.

> But anvaya-knowledge is not [a counteragent] to the desire domain.

Anvaya-knowledge is in no way a counteragent to the desire domain.

## Argument Reconstruction

1. VAK 7.08 distinguished dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge through their counteractive function.
2. Dharma-knowledge is ordinarily associated with the desire-domain truth-field.
3. In cultivation, however, dharma-knowledge of **cessation** and **path** acquires a counteractive reach extending across all three domains.
4. Therefore the realm in which a knowledge was first specified as an object-field does not by itself determine the full extent of its practical counteractive efficacy.
5. Anvaya-knowledge remains asymmetric with respect to the desire domain: it is never its counteragent.

The Bhāṣya therefore establishes a strict functional asymmetry:

```text
dharmajñāna
    nirodha / mārga
    in bhāvanāmārga
        → counteragent to all three domains

anvayajñāna
        → never counteragent to kāmadhātu
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The passage is straightforward exposition of the classificatory rule. No independent objector or school controversy appears in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **bhāvanāmārgasaṃgṛhīta** — included within / comprised by the Path of Cultivation
  - **status:** previously encountered components; newly operative as a compound here
  - **function:** path-stage restriction determining the scope of counteractive efficacy

- **tridhātupratipakṣa** — counteragent to the three domains
  - **status:** rare in this exact compound
  - **function:** functional classification of knowledge by the extent of what it counteracts

- **sarvathā na ... pratipakṣa** — in no way a counteragent
  - **status:** prose strengthening of the Kārikā
  - **function:** categorical exclusion defining the limit of anvaya-knowledge

## Doctrinal Determination

The central distinction is between **object-range** and **counteractive range**.

Earlier verses established:

```text
dharmajñāna
    → Four Truths in the desire domain

anvayajñāna
    → corresponding truths in the higher domains
```

VAK 7.09 shows that this object-based division does not mechanically fix practical efficacy.

Within the Path of Cultivation:

```text
dharmajñāna of cessation/path
    → counteracts throughout kāma, rūpa, ārūpya
```

while:

```text
anvayajñāna
    → does not counteract kāmadhātu
```

The system therefore distinguishes at least two independent determinations of a knowledge:

```text
what it knows
    /
what it can counteract
```

That is precisely why `pratipakṣa` was required as an independent ground of the tenfold division in VAK 7.08.

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

The existing Kārikā translation is substantially confirmed.

The Bhāṣya makes two points explicit:

1. `bhāvanāpathe` restricts the universal counteractive reach to the **Path of Cultivation**.
2. `kāmadhātos tu nānvayam` is categorical: anvaya-knowledge is **in no way** (`sarvathā`) a counteragent to the desire domain.

The working translation should therefore preserve the functional term **counteragent** for `pratipakṣa`; a weaker rendering such as “related to” or “concerned with” would lose the doctrinal force.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The verse gives a useful scientific rule:

> A cognition is not completely specified by its object. Its effective range must also be determined.

Thus:

```text
knowledge
    object-domain
    + counteractive function
    + path-stage
```

This is enough for the present Bhāṣya pass. No stronger Kant–Hegel mapping is imposed here.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.09 executes the `pratipakṣa` criterion announced in VAK 7.08. Dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge differ not only in the domains of the truths they know but in the domains against which they can function as counteragents. In the Path of Cultivation, dharma-knowledge of cessation and path is a counteragent to all three domains. Anvaya-knowledge, by contrast, is never a counteragent to the desire domain. The verse therefore establishes a precise distinction between cognitive object-range and practical counteractive efficacy.
