# VAK_7.21 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ato 'bhisamayāntyākhyaṃ tad anutpattidharmakam /
> svādhobhūmi nirodhe 'ntyaṃ svasatyākāraṃ yatnikam // VAK_7.21 //

**Best current translation:**  
Therefore it is called [conventional knowledge] at the end of direct comprehension. It has non-arising as its character. It belongs to its own or a lower level; at cessation it is the last [foundation of recollection]. It has the aspects of its own truth and is effort-produced.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.21.md`](./VAK_7.21.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 406|07–407|17.

The natural argument begins directly from VAK 7.20's statement that conventional knowledge is cultivated at three inferential-knowledge moments. Vasubandhu explains why it is called `abhisamayāntika`, why there is no fourth instance at path-inferential knowledge, and then presses the difficult question of how a cognition that never arises can nevertheless be said to be cultivated. The passage ends by determining its level, foundation of recollection, truth-aspect, and effort-dependent acquisition.


### Source caution

The working e-text explicitly warns that errors remain. In particular, `gautrikaṃ` at 407|01 is suspicious and is preserved here exactly as supplied rather than silently normalized; context requires something derived from `gotra`.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Why `abhisamayāntika`?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The name follows directly from the rule established in VAK 7.20:

```text
ekaika-satya-abhisamaya-ante
    at the end of the direct comprehension
    of each truth
```

conventional knowledge is cultivated.

Thus `abhisamayāntika` is not a vague honorific for knowledge subsequent to realization. It marks an exact genetic position:

```text
dharmajñāna
    direct comprehension in the desire domain

anvayajñāna
    corresponding comprehension in the higher domains

completion of that anvayajñāna
    ↓
end of the direct comprehension of that truth
    ↓
abhisamayāntika saṃvṛtijñāna
```

This applies to suffering, origin, and cessation.

### 2. Why is there no `abhisamayāntika` at path-inferential knowledge?

**Voice: Question / objection**

If conventional knowledge is cultivated at the completion of a truth's direct comprehension, why not also at `mārgānvayajñāna`?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Because there is no comparable completion of the Path-truth.

The Bhāṣya gives two initial reasons:

```text
mārgasatyasya pūrva laukikena mārgeṇānabhisamitatvāt
    the Path-truth has not previously been directly comprehended
    by a worldly path

akṛtsnābhisamayāt
    its direct comprehension is not complete in the same sense
```

Vasubandhu then formulates the decisive asymmetry through the four truth-functions:

```text
duḥkha
    can be completely known
    kṛtsnaṃ parijñātum

samudaya
    can be abandoned
    prahātum

nirodha
    can be directly realized
    sākṣātkartum

mārga
    cannot be cultivated in its entirety
    na kṛtsno bhāvayitum
```

Therefore the first three admit an `abhisamaya-anta`, an end-point of truth-comprehension, while the Path does not admit such a terminal completion at this moment.

### 3. Objection from origin: is origin really completely abandoned?

**Voice: Objector**

At this point not all origin has been abandoned. Would this not also prevent conventional knowledge concerning origin from being `abhisamayāntika`?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

No. All of origin that is **abandonable by Seeing** with respect to that truth has been abandoned:

```text
tat-satya-darśana-heyaḥ sarvaḥ prahīṇo bhavati
```

The Path is importantly different. The Path functioning as antidote to what is abandoned by Seeing cannot itself be cultivated in its entirety, because it is of many kinds (`bahugotratva`).

This is the `mahān viśeṣaḥ` — the great difference.

The distinction is therefore not simply:

```text
complete object
vs incomplete object
```

but:

```text
truth-function whose Seeing-task reaches completion
vs
Path whose cultivation remains internally manifold and unfinished
```

### 4. An alternative explanation rejected as non-probative

**Voice: Others (`apare`)**

An alternative explanation says that the special conventional cognition is cultivated because it belongs to the entourage or retinue of the Path of Seeing (`darśanamārga-parivāratva`).

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

He rejects this as `sādhyatvād ajñāpakam`: it does not establish the point because what is appealed to is itself what would need to be proved.

This is a useful example of Vasubandhu refusing an explanatory circle rather than merely recording doctrine.

## The Central Controversy: Cultivation Without Arising

### 5. Does this conventional knowledge ever become present?

**Voice: Question**

Is this `abhisamayāntika saṃvṛtijñāna` ever made manifest (`saṃmukhīkriyate`)?

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position as presented**

No, never:

```text
tad anutpattidharmakam
    it is of a nature not to arise
```

The cognition is cultivated and acquired as a future dharma, but it is never actually produced as present cognition.

### 6. Vasubandhu presses the problem

**Voice: Vasubandhu / critical question**

How, then, can it be cultivated?

The proposed answer is:

```text
alabdhalābhāt
    through acquisition of what had not been acquired
```

But Vasubandhu immediately asks how it can be said to have been acquired if it can never be made present.

The reply `prāptitaḥ` — "because of acquisition" — does not satisfy him.

He comments sharply:

```text
yasmāllabdhaṃ tasmāllabdham
    because it is acquired, it is acquired

ityapūrvaiṣā nirdeśajātiḥ
    this is a novel kind of explanation
```

and concludes:

```text
tasmānnaivaṃ bhāvanā sidhyati
    therefore cultivation is not established in this way
```

This is a genuine argumentative intervention. The Bhāṣya does not merely reproduce a Vaibhāṣika taxonomy here; it tests whether the proposed ground actually explains the claimed cultivation.

### 7. The Former Masters' genetic account

**Voice: Former Masters (`pūrvācāryāḥ`), endorsed here as an explanation that succeeds**

Vasubandhu says:

```text
evaṃ tu sidhyati yad āhuḥ pūrvācāryāḥ
    it is established, however, in the way stated by the Former Masters
```

Their account is:

```text
lokottara-mārga-sāmarthya
    power of the supramundane Path

        ↓

transformation of the support
    so that after emerging from the noble state

        ↓

satyālambanaṃ viśiṣṭataraṃ laukikaṃ jñānam
    a superior worldly/conventional knowledge
    having the truth as object

        ↓

can be made present
```

The decisive definition of acquisition is:

```text
tat-saṃmukhībhāva-samartha-āśraya-lābhaḥ
    acquisition of a support capable
    of bringing that cognition into manifestation
```

Here `lābha` no longer means possession of a future cognitive entity that remains eternally unarisen. It means acquiring a transformed support with a new productive capacity.

The following sentence uses `gotra`:

```text
gotre hi labdhe labdhaṃ gautrikaṃ bhavati
```

The exact e-text form `gautrikaṃ` is uncertain, but the argumentative function is clear: acquisition of the basis/type licenses attribution of what belongs to that basis/type.

The Bhāṣya then notes explicitly:

```text
evaṃ tu necchanti vaibhāṣikāḥ
    the Vaibhāṣikas do not accept it in this way
```

We should therefore preserve the positions exactly:

```text
Vaibhāṣika
    acquired future conventional cognition
    intrinsically non-arising

Former Masters
    acquisition of a support newly capable
    of later producing superior conventional cognition
```

The source here says `pūrvācāryāḥ`; it does **not** identify them as Sautrāntikas. We should not add that school label.

## Further Determinations of the Conventional Knowledge

### 8. `svādhobhūmi` — own or lower level

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya asks on how many levels this conventional knowledge is cultivated.

The rule is:

```text
yadbhūmiko darśanamārgaḥ
    whatever level the Path of Seeing occupies

that level
    +
all lower applicable levels
```

For example:

```text
Path of Seeing on anāgamya
    → conventional knowledge on two levels:
       anāgamya + desire domain

Path of Seeing on fourth dhyāna
    → conventional knowledge on seven levels
```

Thus the cultivated conventional articulation cannot exceed the level supporting the noble realization, but it may extend downward through subordinate levels.

### 9. `nirodhe 'ntyam` — at cessation, only the last foundation of recollection

The Bhāṣya asks which `smṛtyupasthāna` forms this conventional cognition takes.

For cessation:

```text
dharmasmṛtyupasthāna
    recollection established on dharmas
```

alone applies. This is the `antya`, the last of the four foundations.

From the explicit enumeration of this one case, the Bhāṣya infers that in the remaining truth-cases the conventional knowledge may belong to all four foundations of recollection.

### 10. `svasatyākāra` — the aspects of its own truth

The conventional cognition acquired from a given truth-comprehension retains the formal determination of that truth:

```text
yat satyābhisamayāl labhyate
    whichever truth-comprehension it is acquired from

 tat satyākāram eva
    it has precisely the aspects of that truth
```

The Bhāṣya adds that specifying its aspects also fixes its object-support:

```text
tadākāravacanād ālambanasya tadeva satyam
    from the statement of its aspects
    it follows that that very truth is its object
```

So the conventional cognition is not merely a generic after-effect of realization. Its object and aspects are genetically inherited from the specific truth realized.

### 11. `yatnika` — effort-produced

Because this cognition is acquired through the Path of Seeing, it is `yatnika`.

The Bhāṣya glosses:

```text
prāyogika
    produced through preparatory effort / application
```

Its acquisition therefore belongs to deliberate path-development, not merely to spontaneous birth, ordinary acquisition, or passive detachment.

The surrounding complex may include four aggregates in the desire domain and five in the form domain when its entourage is counted.

## New Technical Terms

- **abhisamayāntika** — belonging to the end/completion of direct comprehension
  - **status:** central technical designation explained in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** marks the conventional cognition acquired when a truth's Seeing-task reaches completion

- **akṛtsnābhisamaya** — incomplete direct comprehension
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** helps explain why Path-truth lacks the same terminal conventional cognition

- **bahugotratva** — being of many types / lineages
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only ground in this argument
  - **function:** explains why the Path cannot be cultivated exhaustively at one terminal Seeing-moment

- **saṃmukhīkaraṇa / saṃmukhībhāva** — making present; manifestation in actual cognition
  - **status:** crucial Bhāṣya vocabulary
  - **function:** distinguishes acquisition/capacity from actual occurrence

- **saṃmukhībhāva-samartha-āśraya** — a support capable of making the cognition present
  - **status:** decisive Former Masters formula
  - **function:** provides a genetic account of cultivation as transformation of cognitive capacity

- **lokottaramārga-sāmarthya** — power/capacity of the supramundane Path
  - **status:** Bhāṣya genetic vocabulary
  - **function:** ground by which the support becomes capable of later superior conventional cognition

- **yatnika / prāyogika** — effort-produced / application-produced
  - **status:** Kārikā term glossed by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** identifies the mode of acquisition of this conventional cognition

## Argument Reconstruction

The natural movement of VAK 7.21 is:

```text
three truth-completions in VAK 7.20
    ↓
name: abhisamayāntika
    ↓
why no fourth at Path-truth?
    ↓
Path has no comparable terminal completion
    ↓
can this conventional cognition ever arise?
    ↓
Vaibhāṣika: no; it is intrinsically non-arising
    ↓
how then is cultivation established?
    ↓
"because it is acquired" rejected as circular
    ↓
Former Masters:
Path-power transforms the support
    ↓
new capacity for superior truth-directed
conventional cognition
    ↓
level, recollection-foundation,
truth-aspect, object, effort
```

This is substantially more than a classificatory gloss. The Bhāṣya turns the verse into a dispute over what **cultivation itself means**.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.21 forces a distinction among three notions that could otherwise be collapsed:

```text
prāpti / lābha
    acquisition

bhāvanā
    cultivation

saṃmukhībhāva
    actual manifestation
```

The Vaibhāṣika position permits:

```text
acquisition + cultivation
without
actual manifestation
```

by positing an acquired future cognition whose nature is never to arise.

The Former Masters instead explain cultivation through a changed `āśraya`:

```text
noble realization
    changes the support

changed support
    possesses a new capacity

new capacity
    can later generate
    a superior conventional cognition
```

This is an important doctrinal fork and should remain explicit rather than being harmonized away.

## Translation Consequences

The existing Kārikā translation remains mostly sound, but three corrections/controls arise from the Bhāṣya.

First, `abhisamayāntyākhya` should be understood strictly as:

> called "[conventional knowledge] at the end of direct comprehension," because it is cultivated at the end of the direct comprehension of each of the first three truths.

Second, `antyam` does not mean simply "last in time." It refers specifically to the **last of the four foundations of recollection**, `dharmasmṛtyupasthāna`, in the cessation case.

Third, `yatnika` is directly glossed as `prāyogika`; **effort-produced** or **application-produced** is therefore preferable to a vague "requiring effort."

The Kārikā-pass attribution of the Former Masters' explanation to "Sautrāntika" should be removed unless an external source is later brought in to establish that identification. The Bhāṣya source itself says only `pūrvācāryāḥ`.

## Logical Determination

The deepest local distinction is no longer merely:

```text
present cognition
vs
future cognition
```

but:

```text
actual act
    saṃmukhībhāva

possession
    lābha / prāpti

productive capacity
    samartha-āśraya
```

The Former Masters' position gives a particularly clear genetic relation:

```text
actual noble realization
    ↓ determines
support
    ↓ gains
new generative capacity
    ↓ makes possible
superior conventional cognition
```

This means that the effect of knowledge is not exhausted in either a stored representation or an additional item in an inventory of dharmas. A realized determination can alter the **power of the continuum to produce cognition**.

The Path-truth argument adds a second important logical result. The first three truths have a determinate task that reaches a local completion:

```text
suffering → completely known
origin → Seeing-abandonables completely abandoned
cessation → directly realized
```

But Path is itself cultivation:

```text
mārga
    is not merely a completed object of knowledge
    but an internally manifold activity
    whose cultivation continues
```

Thus there is no symmetrical fourth `abhisamayānta`.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage deserves a somewhat stronger Organon contact than the preceding verse because Vasubandhu himself has generated the relevant genetic alternative.

The key structure is:

```text
knowing a truth
    does not merely add content

it can alter the support
    such that a new species of knowing
    becomes possible
```

That is much closer to a **genesis of capacity** than to a static theory of stored cognition.

Still, we should not collapse this into our own transcendental architecture. The Bhāṣya's actual claim remains local and technical: the supramundane Path can make the continuum capable of a superior worldly cognition directed to the truth after emergence.

The strongest transferable principle is therefore:

> A completed determination of knowing may survive not as a second simultaneous representation, but as a newly generated power of the knowing continuum.

This also illuminates why the Path cannot be treated as just another object alongside suffering, origin, and cessation. The Path is precisely the **ongoing generation and exercise of such powers**.
