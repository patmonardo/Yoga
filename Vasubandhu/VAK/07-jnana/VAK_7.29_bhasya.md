# VAK_7.29 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dhyānādyakṣādhimokṣeṣu dhātau ca pratipatsu tu /
> daśa vā saṃvṛtijñānaṃ dvayoḥ ṣaṭ daśa vā kṣaye // VAK_7.29 //

**Best current translation:**

Nine knowledges [operate] in the powers concerning the dhyānas and related attainments, the faculties, resolutions, and domains; but in the paths, [there are] ten or [nine]. In the two [powers] there is conventional knowledge; in [knowledge of] destruction, six or ten.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.29.md`](./VAK_7.29.md)

### Source caution

The e-text has minor typographical irregularities (`dhhimokṣeṣu`, `saḍ`, `jñānāniṃ`). They are preserved above rather than silently normalized.

At `saṃvṛtijñānaṃ dvayoḥ`, the preserved prose explicitly names only `pūrvanivāsānusmṛtijñānabala`; the dual in the verse requires two powers, and the immediately following discussion in VAK 7.30 explicitly groups `pūrvanivāsa` with `cyutyupapatti`. Thus the second conventional power is recoverable from the local context, but the omission in this line of the e-text should be noted.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The powers concerning meditative attainments, relative strength of faculties, diverse resolutions, and diverse domains are each constituted by nine knowledges. In each case cessation-knowledge is excluded.

The power concerning paths leading everywhere has either ten or nine knowledges. The particle `vā` marks an alternative analysis. If the path is grasped together with its fruit, the power includes all ten knowledges; if the path is taken without its fruit, it has nine, excluding cessation-knowledge.

Two powers are constituted by conventional knowledge: recollection of former abodes and, as the local continuation makes clear, knowledge of death and rebirth.

The power concerning destruction of the influxes admits two analyses. If `āsravakṣayajñāna` means cessation-knowledge itself, it consists of six knowledges: dharma-, anvaya-, cessation-, destruction-, non-arising-, and conventional knowledge. If instead it means whatever knowledge occurs in the continuum of one whose influxes are destroyed, all ten knowledges belong to it.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 7.29 completes the internal analysis of the Buddha's ten powers by asking, for each power, which members of the tenfold knowledge-system can constitute it.

The governing rule is not numerical but intensional:

```text
power-domain
    ↓
what must be known in that domain
    ↓
which knowledge-types can take that domain
    ↓
knowledge-range of the power
```

For the first four powers named here, cessation does not belong to the relevant object-field, so `nirodhajñāna` is excluded and the count is nine.

The path-power is more subtle because its scope may be drawn in two ways:

```text
path without fruit
    → cessation excluded
    → 9

path together with fruit
    → cessation included
    → 10
```

The destruction-power is still more explicitly scope-sensitive:

```text
āsravakṣayajñāna = cessation-knowledge itself
    → 6

āsravakṣayajñāna = knowledge in the continuum of one whose influxes are destroyed
    → 10
```

Thus identical terminology can yield different numerical extensions because the defining intension has changed.

## New Technical Terms

- **sarvatragāminī pratipad** — a path/course leading everywhere or to every destination relevant to liberation.
  - **status:** new in this local Buddha-power analysis.
  - **function:** names the object-domain of the path-knowledge power.

- **saphalā pratipad** — a path taken together with its fruit.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification of the Kārikā's compressed alternative.
  - **function:** determines why cessation-knowledge is included and the count becomes ten.

- **matavikalpa** — alternative of doctrinal opinion / analytical position.
  - **status:** previously encountered in this chapter.
  - **function:** explains one force of `vā`; Vasubandhu also allows the difference to be understood through analytical scope.

- **kṣīṇāsrava-saṃtāna** — the continuum of one whose influxes have been destroyed.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** grounds the broad ten-knowledge interpretation of `āsravakṣayajñānabala`.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Four powers with nine knowledges

The following each have nine knowledges, excluding `nirodhajñāna`:

1. `dhyāna-vimokṣa-samādhi-samāpatti-jñānabala` — power of knowing dhyānas, liberations, concentrations, and attainments.
2. `indriya-parāpara-jñānabala` — power of knowing higher and lower faculties.
3. `nānādhimukti-jñānabala` — power of knowing diverse resolutions/inclinations.
4. `nānādhātu-jñānabala` — power of knowing diverse domains/dispositions.

Here `dhātu` should remain **domain**, not “element.”

### Power of the paths: nine or ten

The Bhāṣya makes the alternative exact:

```text
if the path is taken with its fruit
    10 knowledges

if the path is taken without its fruit
    9 knowledges
    excluding cessation-knowledge
```

This is not an arbitrary disagreement in arithmetic. It depends on what is included in the object `pratipad`.

### The two conventional powers

`saṃvṛtijñānaṃ dvayoḥ` assigns conventional knowledge to the two powers concerned with extended histories of beings:

- recollection of former abodes;
- death and rebirth.

The local e-text prose explicitly names the first here; the second is supplied by the verse's dual and confirmed immediately in the VAK 7.30 continuation.

### Destruction of the influxes: six or ten

On the narrow definition, `āsravakṣayajñāna` is cessation-knowledge itself. The six are:

- `dharmajñāna`
- `anvayajñāna`
- `nirodhajñāna`
- `kṣayajñāna`
- `anutpādajñāna`
- `saṃvṛtijñāna`

On the broad definition, the phrase means knowledge occurring in the continuum of one whose influxes have been destroyed; then all ten knowledges may constitute the power.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass rendering “nine or ten depending on whether the result is included with the path” is confirmed by the Bhāṣya and can now be stated without hesitation.

The destruction-power should not be paraphrased merely as a narrow versus comprehensive enumeration. The Bhāṣya gives two distinct definitions of `āsravakṣayajñāna`:

1. cessation-knowledge itself;
2. knowledge belonging to the continuum of one whose influxes are destroyed.

The numerical difference follows from this semantic difference.

Likewise, `nānādhātu` should be rendered in terms of diverse **domains/dispositions**, not physical elements.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.29 is a striking exercise in **extension determined by intension**.

The same nominal power changes its extension according to how its object is determined:

```text
pratipad
    path alone
        → 9

pratipad + phala
    path-with-fruit
        → 10
```

and:

```text
āsravakṣayajñāna
    cessation-knowledge
        → 6

āsravakṣayajñāna
    any knowledge in a liberated continuum
        → 10
```

The Bhāṣya therefore demonstrates that counting follows definition. A numerical matrix is only the extensional surface of a prior semantic articulation.

## Organon Contact Point

This is unusually close to the NLP-first concern that has emerged in the Organon work, but the contact should remain subordinate to the text.

The Kośa's count cannot be recovered adequately from the numeral alone. One must first resolve the semantic frame:

```text
term
    → intended scope
    → admissible determinations
    → extension
```

In Organon language, the numerical feature is generated by a richer FeatureStructure. Here Vasubandhu himself supplies the decisive feature: whether `pratipad` includes its fruit, and whether `āsravakṣayajñāna` denotes a specific knowledge or knowledge in a liberated continuum.

## Transition to VAK 7.30

The ten powers have now been classified by their knowledge-content. VAK 7.30 changes the coordinate: it asks about their **level (`bhūmi`)**, and then asks why these knowledges are called a Buddha's **powers** at all.
