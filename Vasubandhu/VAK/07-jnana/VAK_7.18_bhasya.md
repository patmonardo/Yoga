# VAK_7.18 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> traidhātukāmalā dharmā akṛtāś ca dvidhā dvidhā /
> sāṃvṛtaṃ svakalāpānyad ekaṃ vidyād anātmataḥ // VAK_7.18 //

**Best current translation:**  
Dharmas belonging to the three domains, pure dharmas, and the unconditioned are divided twofold, respectively. A single conventional knowledge can know what is other than its own complex as non-self.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.18.md`](./VAK_7.18.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 404|12–405|06.

The Bhāṣya first expands the tenfold dharma-field announced at the end of VAK 7.17, then explains the final pāda of VAK 7.18 and closes the first classificatory block.

```text
saṃkṛtā dharmā aṣṭadhā kriyante /
kāmarūpārupyāvacarānāsravāṇāṃ saṃprayuktaviprayuktabhedāt /
asaṃskṛtā dvidhā kriyante /
kuśalāvyākṛtabhedāt /
...
syādekena jñānena sarvadharmān jānīyāt /
na syāt /
api tu
sāṃvṛtaṃ svakalāpānyadekaṃ vidyādanātmataḥ // VAkK_7.18 //
saṃvṛtijñānaṃ svasmātkalāpādanyān sarvadharmānanātvato jānīyāt sarvadharmā anātmāna iti /
svabhāvastatsahabhuvaśca dharmāstasya svakalāpaḥ /
teṣāmagrahaṇaṃ viṣayaviṣayibhedādekālambanatvāditi /
saṃnikṛṣṭatvācca /
```

## Natural Argument

### 1. The ten classes of dharmas

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The ten object-classes are generated by exact division.

Conditioned dharmas are first distributed across four fields:

```text
kāmāvacara
rūpāvacara
ārūpyāvacara
anāsrava
```

Each is then divided into:

```text
saṃprayukta
    associated with mind

viprayukta
    dissociated from mind
```

This yields eight conditioned classes.

The unconditioned is divided into:

```text
kuśala
avyākṛta
```

which yields two further classes.

Thus:

```text
8 conditioned
+ 2 unconditioned
= 10 dharma-classes
```

The Bhāṣya then distributes these ten classes as objects of the ten knowledges:

| Knowledge | Dharma-classes available as objects |
|---|---:|
| saṃvṛtijñāna | 10 |
| dharmajñāna | 5 |
| anvayajñāna | 7 |
| duḥkhajñāna | 6 |
| samudayajñāna | 6 |
| nirodhajñāna | 1 |
| mārgajñāna | 2 |
| paracittajñāna | 3 |
| kṣayajñāna | 9 |
| anutpādajñāna | 9 |

The classification is therefore not merely a list of objects. It is a directed relation between determinate cognitive species and a fully articulated dharma-field.

### 2. Can one knowledge know all dharmas?

**Voice: Objector / question-form**

Can all dharmas be known by a single knowledge?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Not absolutely.

A single conventional knowledge can know all dharmas other than its own complex under the aspect of non-self:

```text
sarvadharmā anātmānaḥ
    all dharmas are non-self
```

The qualification `svakalāpād anyat` is decisive.

### 3. What is the cognition's own complex?

The Bhāṣya defines `svakalāpa` as:

```text
svabhāva
    the cognition itself

+ tatsahabhuvaḥ dharmāḥ
    dharmas co-arising with it
```

So the cognition cannot, in that same act, include:

```text
itself
its co-arising associated factors
its co-arising companion dharmas
```

as objects.

### 4. Why can it not know its own present complex?

Vasubandhu gives three reasons:

```text
viṣaya-viṣayi-bheda
    object and object-knower must be distinct

ekālambanatva
    one cognitive act has one object-support structure

saṃnikṛṣṭatva
    the co-arising complex is too immediate / proximate
```

The exclusion is therefore structural rather than accidental.

### 5. Level and mode of the universal conventional cognition

The cognition capable of the universal non-self determination is further specified:

```text
in kāmadhātu
    śrutamaya
    cintāmaya

in rūpadhātu
    śrutamaya
    bhāvanāmaya
```

Its object is restricted by level (`vyavacchinna-bhūmi`). Vasubandhu adds that otherwise simultaneous dispassion with respect to all levels would follow.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya closes VAK 7a through a precise progression:

```text
complete dharma-field
    ↓
ten determinate object-classes
    ↓
knowledge/object matrix
    ↓
question of universal cognition
    ↓
universal non-self cognition
    ↓
structural exclusion of its own co-arising complex
```

The result is carefully bounded universality.

A conventional cognition may have an extraordinarily wide object-field, but it is not absolutely self-inclusive in the same cognitive moment.

## New Technical Terms

- **svakalāpa** — one's own immediately co-arising complex
  - **status:** introduced as technically operative in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** defines the limit of a cognition's simultaneous object-range

- **viṣaya-viṣayi-bheda** — distinction between object and that which takes the object
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reasoning vocabulary
  - **function:** logical ground for exclusion of self-objectification in the same act

- **saṃnikṛṣṭatva** — excessive proximity / immediate closeness
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage
  - **function:** additional ground for exclusion of the co-arising complex

- **vyavacchinna-bhūmi** — delimited level or sphere
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** restricts the range of the universal conventional cognition by meditative/domain level

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.18 completes the first major task of Jñānanirdeśa: knowledge is now specified not merely by name or object, but through an articulated network of:

```text
nature
counteraction
aspect
object-support
preparation
accomplished task
causal development
ethical quality
level
support
smṛtyupasthāna
object-range
knowledge-as-object relation
dharma-class as object
```

The final universal cognition does not cancel those conditions. It confirms them by exhibiting its own limit.

## Translation Consequences

The existing Kārikā translation should retain **own complex** for `svakalāpa`, but the Bhāṣya makes the phrase more precise:

> its own present cognition together with the dharmas co-arising with it.

The old formulation "co-arising fact-structure" is useful philosophically but should not replace this stricter textual sense.

`anātmataḥ` is correctly rendered **as non-self**. The Bhāṣya explicitly paraphrases the determination as:

```text
sarvadharmā anātmānaḥ
    all dharmas are non-self
```

## Logical Determination

The closing result is not a doctrine of complete reflexive transparency.

Rather:

```text
very wide cognition
    can determine the field universally

but

its own present act and co-arising complex
    remain outside that same act's object-position
```

So the Kośa finishes the formal field of knowledge with an **immanent limit of simultaneous reflection**.

This is particularly valuable because the limit is not asserted abstractly; Vasubandhu grounds it through the structure of object and object-knower, object-support, and co-arising proximity.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

VAK 7.18 gives an excellent boundary condition for later reflection theory:

```text
conditioned knowing
    can classify an immense field
    can even apply a universal determination

but

cannot make its complete present living complex
an object of the very same act
```

The Kośa does not proceed from this to Absolute Reflection. It gives us the disciplined facticity that any stronger theory would have to explain rather than erase.

## Block Note

This closes **VAK 7a — VAK 7.01–7.18**.

The block has established the formal field of the ten knowledges and the grounds by which they differ, operate, and enter one another's object-ranges.
