# VAK_7.45_bhasya — Ethical Status and the Three Vidyās

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 7.45**

```text
avyākṛte śrotracakṣurabhijñe itarāḥ śubhāḥ /
tisro vidyāḥ avidyāyāḥ pūrvāntādau nivarttanāt // VAK_7.45 //
```

**Current translation**

The direct knowledges of divine hearing and divine sight are indeterminate; the others are wholesome. Three are sciences because they turn back ignorance concerning the prior limit and the other temporal dimensions.

See: [VAK_7.45.md](./VAK_7.45.md)

## Source Cautions

The e-text is a reference transcription, not a critical edition. The argument is nevertheless clear. The phrase `pūrvāntādau` in the verse is unpacked by the Bhāṣya as `pūrvānta`, `aparānta`, and `madhya` ignorance. The final classification of the three vidyās as `aśaikṣya` is nuanced and should not be flattened: only the exhaustion-knowledge is intrinsically aśaikṣī in the strict sense; the other two receive that designation because they arise in an aśaikṣa continuum.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu.**

The two perceptual abhijñās—divine sight and divine hearing—are ethically indeterminate (`avyākṛta`). Vasubandhu explains this not by their objects but by their cognitive constitution: their prajñā is conjoined respectively with visual and auditory vijñāna.

An apparent difficulty follows. Earlier these powers were assigned to the four dhyānas. How can sensory cognitions be four-dhyāna-grounded? Vasubandhu answers that the assignment is made according to their **basis (`āśraya`)**: the special eye and ear faculties that serve as their bases belong to the four-dhyāna field. Alternatively, the designation may be made according to the immediately preceding path-state (`ānantaryamārga`).

The other four abhijñās are wholesome (`kuśala`). Vasubandhu then handles a textual objection: another Abhidharma formulation defines abhijñā generally as wholesome prajñā. He replies that such a definition states the principal or predominant case (`pradhānika` / `vāhulika`) rather than excluding the two indeterminate perceptual powers.

The discussion then turns from ethical classification to `vidyā`. Three abhijñās are called sciences:

1. recollection of former lives,
2. knowledge of death and reappearance,
3. direct realization of the exhaustion of the influxes.

Why only these three? Because they remove three determinate forms of ignorance. Vasubandhu explicitly maps them, in order, to delusion concerning:

```text
pūrvānta
    the prior limit

aparānta
    the future limit

madhya
    the middle / present continuity
```

Thus `vidyā` is not merely a more impressive abhijñā. It is a cognition whose function is the reversal of a specific structural ignorance.

Finally Vasubandhu qualifies the common description of these three as `aśaikṣya-vidyā`. Strictly speaking, only the direct realization of the exhaustion of the influxes is intrinsically aśaikṣī. The other two are called aśaikṣya because they arise in the continuum of an aśaikṣa. In themselves they are neither śaikṣa nor aśaikṣa.

The Bhāṣya therefore keeps apart three classifications:

```text

```

These are related but not identical.

## New Technical Terms

- **pradhānika** — principal or primary; **status:** new in this Bhāṣya unit; **function:** explains why another text may define abhijñā by the predominant wholesome cases without denying exceptions.
- **vāhulika** — stated according to the majority or prevailing cases; **status:** new; **function:** alternative explanation of the same textual generalization.
- **pūrvānta** — prior limit; **status:** present in Kārikā in compressed form; **function:** object of ignorance reversed by former-life knowledge.
- **aparānta** — future limit; **status:** supplied explicitly by Bhāṣya; **function:** object of ignorance reversed by death-and-reappearance knowledge.
- **madhya** — middle / present continuity; **status:** supplied explicitly by Bhāṣya; **function:** object of ignorance reversed by exhaustion-knowledge.
- **aśaikṣya-vidyā** — science associated with the one beyond training; **status:** new local specification; **function:** distinguishes intrinsic aśaikṣa status from merely arising in an aśaikṣa continuum.
- **saṃtāna** — continuum; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** bearer-context explaining derivative classification.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse establishes a strict separation between extraordinary perception and ethical value.

```text
divine hearing
    extraordinary access
    but avyākṛta

divine sight
    extraordinary access
    but avyākṛta
```

The capacity to perceive what others cannot perceive does not by itself supply wholesome determination.

The three vidyās introduce a second and stronger criterion:

```text
abhijñā
    directly knows a hidden field

vidyā
    directly removes a determinate avidyā
```

This distinction is the doctrinal center of the verse.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation “three are sciences because they turn back ignorance concerning the past limit and the other limits” should now be sharpened by the Bhāṣya:

> Three are sciences because, respectively, they turn back delusion concerning the prior limit, the future limit, and the middle.

The term `vidyā` is best retained as **science** in this project because its defining function here is not mere illumination but systematic removal of a specific ignorance.

`Avyākṛta` should remain **indeterminate** rather than “neutral” when precision matters: the point is karmic/ethical non-determination, not absence of function.

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a particularly clean example of orthogonal feature classification.

```text
same capacity
    can be classified by:

cognitive mechanism
ethical valence
object domain
ignorance-removing function
path-status
```

No single axis subsumes the others.

The distinction between the eye/ear abhijñās and their four-dhyāna basis is equally important:

```text
operation-type
    sensory vijñāna

basis
    dhyānic eye/ear faculty
```

Therefore:

```text
level of basis
    ≠
level/type of operative cognition
```

## Organon Contact

This verse strongly supports the emerging Feature-system architecture.

A capability should not receive one global label such as “advanced,” “good,” or “rational.” It should be represented through independent but related determinations:

```text
Capability
    access-range
    cognitive operator
    basis
    ethical status
    error-removal function
    training-status
```

The Bhāṣya itself performs exactly this kind of multi-axis typing.

The three vidyās also suggest a stronger criterion for scientific cognition than information gain:

> **A science is characterized by the determinate ignorance it overcomes, not merely by the hidden information it reveals.**

This formulation is directly grounded in Vasubandhu's explanation of `vidyā` here and should remain available for the Rational Kośa synthesis.
