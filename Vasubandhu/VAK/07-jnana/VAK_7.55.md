## Literal Translation

The fleshly eye does not see what is distant, covered, subtle, or in every direction; therefore divine vision is admitted for form. Arhats, solitary buddhas, and the Teacher see respectively a two-thousandfold world, a three-thousandfold world, and innumerable worlds. Other vision may also be obtained by birth, but the intermediate being is not visible to that vision.

## Philosophical Translation

Divine vision is defined not by vague omniscience but by determinate superiority over the ordinary eye. It overcomes distance, obstruction, subtlety, and restricted orientation, yet its range remains graded according to the cognitive stature of the seer. Different beings possess different perceptual horizons, and even extraordinary vision may fail before a domain whose mode of existence is incompatible with its faculty.

# 1. Divine Sight Is Defined by the Limits It Overcomes

The ordinary eye fails under four principal conditions:

```text
distance
obstruction
subtlety
restricted direction
```

Divine vision is therefore defined relationally. It exceeds the ordinary gocara and is measured through determinate dimensions of competence.

# 2. Distance, Obstruction, and Resolution

Distance is a relation between object-location and faculty-range. Obstruction is a blockage in the present mode of access, not the absence of the object. Subtlety is a resolution problem: a refined faculty discriminates what the coarse faculty cannot.

```text
range expansion      distance
penetration          obstruction
resolution expansion subtlety
```

# 3. Directional Completeness

The fleshly eye is perspectivally oriented. Divine vision loosens this directional restriction without becoming a featureless view from nowhere. The limitation of orientation is overcome while the visual determination remains.

# 4. This Is Still Rūpa-Vision

The object remains rūpa, the faculty is refined rūpaprasāda, and the ground is rūpa-dhyāna. This is not arūpya cognition. Arūpya is not a more powerful seeing of form but the cessation of form as the defining cognitive support.

# 5. Perceptual Range Is Hierarchically Graded

```text
Arhat             two-thousandfold world-system
Pratyekabuddha    three-thousandfold world-system
Buddha            innumerable world-systems
```

The same faculty-type can have different scope. Capability-type and capability-magnitude must therefore be distinguished.

# 6. Liberation and Empirical Range Are Distinct

The arhat is liberated but does not possess the Buddha's empirical reach. Thus:

```text
liberation                one axis
scope of empirical cognition another axis
```

Aśaikṣa does not mean maximal knowledge of every empirical field. Vidyā is completion in liberative Principle; Buddha-range is maximal systematic reach across the empirical whole.

# 7. Birth-Derived Vision

Some extraordinary vision is upapattija, obtained by mode of birth. This repeats the causal distinction of VAK 7.53: the same apparent power can have different provenance and therefore different scientific meaning.

# 8. The Intermediate Being Is Not Seen

The intermediate being is not visible to the birth-derived faculty. This introduces ontological compatibility as a separate condition of perception. An object may be invisible not because it is distant, obstructed, subtle, or directionally excluded, but because faculty and object belong to incompatible modes of existence.

```text
CanPerceive(faculty, object)
    requires
    modality compatibility
    range compatibility
    resolution compatibility
    realm compatibility
    existence-mode compatibility
```

# 9. The Invisible Is Not Necessarily Arūpya

Invisibility does not imply bodilessness. A subtle form may be invisible because the wrong faculty is operating. Arūpya is a principled absence of rūpa-support; invisibility is a failure of access.

# 10. A Complete Theory of Perceptual Failure

The verse distinguishes at least five causes of non-perception:

```text
1. too distant
2. obstructed
3. too subtle
4. outside directional range
5. ontologically incompatible
```

This is a scientific decomposition of perceptual limitation.

# 11. Citta–Faculty–Object System

A visual cognition requires lawful alignment among:

```text
citta          visual operation
rūpaprasāda    receptive faculty
rūpa           visible object
gocara         admitted range
existence-mode compatibility condition
```

Knowledge is a conditioned relation among typed elements, not a solitary mind grasping anything whatsoever.

# 12. Organon Kernel Model

```text
PerceptualFaculty {
    modality
    substrate
    ground
    provenance
    maxDistance
    obstructionPolicy
    resolutionThreshold
    directionalScope
    worldScale

```

The hierarchy becomes scientific only when its differences are represented as explicit capability dimensions.

# 13. Hegel-Clean Interpretation

Refined vision negates distance, barrier, fineness, and perspective while preserving the determination of seeing. The result is not bad infinity—merely farther and farther—but a comprehended system of perceptual conditions.

# 14. Fichtean Interpretation

Many limits belong to the form of appearance rather than to the object absolutely. The intermediate being marks a deeper boundary: not limitation within one field, but mismatch between fields of appearance.

# 15. Relation to Vidyā

The divine eye can survey immense world-systems without thereby becoming Vidyā. Empirical scope and reversal of avidyā are distinct. The Buddha possesses both, but their meanings must not be collapsed.

# Condensed Principle

> Divine vision is not unrestricted seeing. It is a rūpa-dhyāna faculty whose range is defined across distance, obstruction, subtlety, direction, world-scale, and ontological compatibility. Its power is graded, typed, and limited according to the organization of the knowing continuum.

```text
Rūpa remains the object.
Dhyāna refines the faculty.
Attainment determines the horizon.
Ontology determines compatibility.
Vidyā remains the ruler of the whole.
```
