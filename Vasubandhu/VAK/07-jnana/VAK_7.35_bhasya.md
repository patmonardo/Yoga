# VAK_7.35 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> śiṣya-sādhāraṇā anye dharmāḥ kecit pṛthagjanaiḥ /
> araṇā-praṇidhi-jñāna-pratisaṃvid-guṇādayaḥ // VAK_7.35 //

**Best current translation:**

Other qualities are shared with disciples, and some with ordinary persons—qualities beginning with freedom from conflict, knowledge arising from resolve, and the discriminating knowledges.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.35.md`](./VAK_7.35.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 416|17–417|02.

The natural argument begins with Vasubandhu's closure of the eighteen distinctive Buddha-dharmas and opens the next class of excellent qualities by distinguishing those shared with disciples and, in some cases, ordinary persons. The detailed explanation of `araṇā` begins immediately afterward and is treated principally under VAK 7.36.

```text
ime tāvadaṣṭādaśa buddhānāmāveṇikā dharmā ucyante //

śiṣyasādhāraṇā anye dharmāḥ

śrāvakasādhāraṇāstvanye guṇā buddhānām /

kecit pṛthagjanaiḥ /

ke punasta iti yathāyogam

araṇāpraṇidhijñānapratisaṃvidguṇādayaḥ // VAkK_7.35 //

araṇapraṇidhijñānapratisaṃvidabhijñādhyānārupyāṃpramāṇavimokṣābhibhvāyatanakṛtsnāyatanādayaḥ /
```

### Source cautions

- The e-text compresses the long final compound without word division; it is retained as supplied rather than silently normalized.
- `śiṣya` in the Kārikā is glossed explicitly by the Bhāṣya as `śrāvaka`.
- The Kārikā names only the first members of the series and closes with `guṇādayaḥ`; the Bhāṣya makes the scope of that `ādi` explicit by adding superknowledges, meditations, formless attainments, immeasurables, liberations, spheres of mastery, and totality-spheres.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The preceding section has now completed the eighteen `āveṇika` dharmas—qualities distinctive to Buddhas.

But not every excellence possessed by a Buddha is exclusive to Buddhahood. There are other Buddha-qualities that are also shared with disciples (`śrāvaka`), and some are shared even with ordinary persons (`pṛthagjana`).

The Kārikā gives representative examples:

```text
araṇā
praṇidhijñāna
pratisaṃvid
```

The Bhāṣya then expands the implied `ādi`:

```text
araṇā
praṇidhijñāna
pratisaṃvid
abhijñā
dhyāna
ārūpya
apramāṇa
vimokṣa
abhibhvāyatana
kṛtsnāyatana
...
```

The point of the verse is therefore classificatory but important. The end of the `āveṇika` section does not exhaust Buddha-excellence. It exhausts only the qualities whose possession is distinctive to a Buddha. A second field must now be opened for high capacities that may occur across several classes of practitioner.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
preceding result:
    eighteen Buddha-dharmas
    are āveṇika
    = distinctive to Buddhas

new question:
    are all remaining Buddha-qualities likewise exclusive?

answer:
    no

other Buddha-qualities:
    some are shared with śrāvakas
    some are shared even with pṛthagjanas

examples:
    araṇā
    praṇidhijñāna
    pratisaṃvid
    abhijñā
    dhyāna
    ārūpya
    apramāṇa
    vimokṣa
    abhibhvāyatana
    kṛtsnāyatana

therefore:
    possession of an excellent dharma
    must be distinguished from
    exclusive possession of that dharma
```

This is the exact transition from the Buddha-exclusive catalogue into the shared field that governs the remainder of Chapter 7.

## New Technical Terms

- **śrāvaka-sādhāraṇa** — shared with disciples / hearer-disciples
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss on Kārikā `śiṣya-sādhāraṇa`
  - **function:** specifies the class with whom these Buddha-qualities may be shared.

- **āveṇika** — distinctive, uncommon, exclusive
  - **status:** established in the immediately preceding Bhāṣya section and repeated here at the transition
  - **function:** provides the contrast-class against which the shared qualities are introduced.

- **abhijñā** — superknowledge
  - **status:** named explicitly in the Bhāṣya expansion of `guṇādayaḥ`; developed later in this block
  - **function:** one class of exceptional capacity shared beyond Buddhas.

- **dhyāna** — meditation / meditative absorption
  - **status:** established earlier in the Kośa; newly included here in the expanded catalogue of shared excellences
  - **function:** meditative basis and excellence not exclusive to Buddhahood.

- **ārūpya** — formless attainment / formless level
  - **status:** established earlier; newly included in this catalogue
  - **function:** one class of refined attainment accessible beyond Buddhas.

- **apramāṇa** — immeasurable
  - **status:** established technical class; introduced here into the shared-quality catalogue
  - **function:** one of the cultivated excellences that crosses practitioner classes.

- **vimokṣa** — liberation / liberative attainment
  - **status:** established technical class
  - **function:** included among the non-exclusive excellent qualities.

- **abhibhvāyatana** — sphere of mastery
  - **status:** established Abhidharma technical class
  - **function:** included among shared meditative capacities.

- **kṛtsnāyatana** — totality-sphere
  - **status:** established Abhidharma technical class
  - **function:** included among shared meditative capacities.

## Doctrinal Determination

### `āveṇika` and `sādhāraṇa` are different classificatory relations

VAK 7.28–7.34 culminated in qualities that characterize the distinctive perfection of Buddhahood. VAK 7.35 now changes the classificatory relation.

The question is no longer simply:

```text
what excellence does a Buddha possess?
```

but:

```text
is this excellence exclusive to a Buddha,
or can it occur in another practitioner?
```

Thus the same bearer—Buddha—may possess both:

```text
āveṇika dharmas
    distinctive Buddha qualities

sādhāraṇa dharmas
    qualities shared with others
```

### `śiṣya` is concretely `śrāvaka`

The Bhāṣya removes possible vagueness from the Kārikā's `śiṣya` by glossing the shared qualities as `śrāvaka-sādhāraṇa`. The immediate contrast is therefore among:

```text
Buddha
śrāvaka
pṛthagjana
```

The verse is not yet giving one exhaustive possession-table for all three classes. It establishes the principle of graded commonality and then opens the qualities that will be analyzed individually.

### `guṇādayaḥ` carries substantial doctrinal weight

The Bhāṣya's expansion shows that `ādi` is not ornamental. It gathers a broad range of capacities already known from meditative and path analysis:

```text
knowledge capacities
    araṇā
    praṇidhijñāna
    pratisaṃvid
    abhijñā

meditative attainments
    dhyāna
    ārūpya
    apramāṇa
    vimokṣa
    abhibhvāyatana
    kṛtsnāyatana
```

Chapter 7 is therefore beginning to recollect earlier determinations of the Kośa under the question of **who can actualize them and in what form**.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation is basically correct, but the Bhāṣya sharpens two points.

First, `śiṣya` should be understood concretely here as a `śrāvaka` disciple.

Second, `guṇādayaḥ` should be heard strongly. The verse does not name a closed triad. `araṇā`, `praṇidhijñāna`, and `pratisaṃvid` begin a much larger class of excellent capacities.

A slightly fuller philosophical translation is therefore:

> Other excellent qualities of a Buddha are shared with disciples, and some even with ordinary persons—beginning with freedom from conflict, knowledge arising from resolve, and the discriminating knowledges, together with further superknowledges and meditative excellences.

The final phrase is explanatory and derives from the Bhāṣya's expansion of `ādi`; it is not a literal replacement for the Kārikā.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.35 introduces a distinction between **intension and distribution of possession**.

```text
quality Q is predicated of Buddha
```

does not imply:

```text
Q belongs exclusively to Buddha
```

The stronger determination is:

```text
same quality-kind
    may be instantiated across
        Buddha
        śrāvaka
        pṛthagjana

while
    range
    purity
    mastery
    mode of acquisition
    and completeness
may differ
```

The Bhāṣya therefore opens a field of continuity without erasing hierarchy. Commonality is real, but commonality is not equivalence of attainment.

## Organon Contact Point

This verse is especially important for the Rational Kośa because it prevents a crude partition of rational capacities by bearer-class.

The determinations are not distributed as three sealed ontologies:

```text
ordinary person qualities
versus disciple qualities
versus Buddha qualities
```

Rather, one field of capacities is differentially actualized, while certain determinations remain genuinely `āveṇika`.

That is a better model of **Absolute Knowledge in species** than a static ladder of unrelated cognitive kinds. A finite practitioner can instantiate genuine forms of knowledge and meditative capacity without therefore possessing the complete perfection of Buddhahood. The universal capacity is present determinately, not merely analogically; its completion, range, and mode of possession remain to be specified.

This preserves the governing genetic discipline of the project: continuity must be demonstrated through the same determinations occurring in different modes, not asserted by superficial analogy.

## VAK_7c Opening

VAK 7.35 opens the final movement of Jñānanirdeśa:

```text
VAK 7a
    knowledge as classified determination

VAK 7b
    knowledge as cultivated and perfected power

VAK 7c
    knowledge and attainment as distributed,
    communicable, and manifest efficacy
```

The immediate next task is `araṇā`. VAK 7.36 will determine its `svabhāva`, meditative level, bearer, place of arising, and object-range.
