# VAK_7.12 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> śeṣe caturdaśākāre śūnyānātmavivarjite /  
> nāmalaḥ ṣoḍaśabhyo 'nya ākāraḥ anye 'sti śāstrataḥ // VAK_7.12 //

**Best translation:**  
The remaining two knowledges have fourteen aspects, emptiness and non-self being excluded. According to the principal Kashmiri position, no pure aspect exists beyond the sixteen; others hold that another does exist, on the authority of the śāstra.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.12.md`](./VAK_7.12.md)

## Prose Segmentation and Translation

### 1. The “remaining” two are exhaustion- and non-arising-knowledge

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

`śeṣe` is not an indefinite remainder. It refers specifically to **kṣayajñāna** and **anutpādajñāna**. These two have fourteen aspects because the aspects of emptiness (`śūnya`) and non-self (`anātman`) are excluded.

The Bhāṣya therefore resolves the cautious wording of the Kārikā pass:

```text
śeṣe
    = kṣayajñāna
      + anutpādajñāna
```

Their fourteenfold range is not an independent scheme. It is generated by subtraction from the sixteen truth-aspects.

### 2. Why can ultimate knowledges participate in conventional expression?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu adds an important qualification. Even these two knowledges, although `pāramārthika`, participate in conventional designation (`saṃvṛtibhajana`). On their strength there can subsequently be expressions such as:

> “My birth is exhausted; I know that there is no further existence from this.”

The important distinction is between the knowledge itself and the subsequent conventional articulation made possible by it.

This continues the distinction already encountered in 7.07:

```text
immediate terminal knowledge
    ≠
subsequent articulated judgment about that knowledge
```

The Bhāṣya thereby avoids treating first-person verbal formulation as the intrinsic form of uncontaminated realization.

### 3. Is there a pure aspect beyond the sixteen?

**Voice: Vasubandhu introducing a doctrinal question**

The question is posed explicitly:

```text
kim anāsravaḥ svalakṣaṇākāro 'sti atha na?
```

Is there an uncontaminated aspect having a self-characteristic as its aspect, or is there not?

This is not simply a numerical question. It asks whether uncontaminated cognition can operate under a mode not contained in the sixteen truth-aspects.

### 4. Kashmiri Vaibhāṣika position

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position — Kashmiri**

For the Kashmiris:

```text
no uncontaminated aspect
exists outside the sixteen aspects
```

`nāstyanāsravākāraḥ ṣoḍaśākāranirmuktaḥ`.

Thus pure knowledge has a closed aspect-system. Whatever is genuinely uncontaminated must be intelligible within the sixteenfold articulation of the Four Truths.

### 5. The non-Kashmiri position

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika / non-Kashmiri position**

The `bahirdeśakāḥ`, the teachers from outside Kashmir, maintain that another uncontaminated aspect does exist.

Their warrant is explicitly textual:

```text
kathaṃ gamyate?
    how is this known?

śāstrataḥ
    from the śāstra
```

The cited śāstra passage describes an unconnected (`apratisaṃyukta`) mind knowing desire-realm dharmas not only under the familiar truth-aspects—impermanent, suffering, empty, non-self, cause, arising, production, condition—but also through formulas such as:

```text
asti etat sthānam
asti etad vastu
```

roughly, “there is such a possibility/ground” and “there is such a thing.”

The non-Kashmiri argument is therefore that the authoritative analytical literature itself appears to recognize an uncontaminated cognitive mode beyond the canonical sixteen.

### 6. Vasubandhu tests the proposed reading

**Voice: Objector / alternative interpreter**

An alternative attempts to read the śāstra less strongly: perhaps it does not mean that “there is such a possibility / thing” is itself an additional aspect. Perhaps it only means that there is a possible case in which something is known as impermanent and so forth.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu rejects this reading with `na / anyatrāvacanāt` — no, because the text does not speak that way elsewhere.

He then compares another śāstra passage concerning a mind to be abandoned by the Path of Seeing. That passage gives a long series of distorted aspects—self, mine, annihilation, eternity, causelessness, non-action, superiority, purity, liberation, doubt, and so forth—without inserting the supposedly merely existential formula. If the weaker reading were correct, the same wording should appear there as well.

The argument is philological and systematic at once: parallel passages constrain the interpretation of the disputed formula.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Ten knowledges have now been differentiated by aspect.
    ↓
kṣayajñāna and anutpādajñāna remain.
    ↓
Their range = sixteen minus śūnya and anātman.
    ↓
Question:
Can uncontaminated cognition have an aspect beyond the sixteen?
    ↓
Kashmiris:
No. The sixteen exhaust the pure aspect-system.
    ↓
Non-Kashmiris:
Yes. A śāstra passage implies another pure aspect.
    ↓
Attempted restrictive reinterpretation of that passage.
    ↓
Vasubandhu tests it against parallel śāstra usage and rejects it.
```

The Bhāṣya is therefore doing more than recording a school disagreement. It tests a doctrinal boundary by comparative textual reasoning.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā pass can now be made more determinate in two places.

First:

```text
śeṣe
    = specifically kṣayajñāna and anutpādajñāna
```

Second, `anye` should not remain vague. The Bhāṣya identifies them as the **bahirdeśakāḥ**, teachers outside Kashmir, opposed here to the Kashmiri position.

The phrase `śāstrataḥ` is also stronger than a generic “according to scripture.” It means that the alternative position is argued **from a specific Abhidharma śāstra passage**.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.12 exposes a real systematic question:

```text
Is the sixteenfold truth-aspect system
    complete for all uncontaminated cognition?
```

The Kashmiri answer is yes.

The non-Kashmiri answer is no: an uncontaminated cognition may have a further self-characteristic aspect warranted by śāstra.

The dispute therefore concerns the **closure of the formal system of pure cognition**.

At the same time, Vasubandhu preserves a distinction between terminal knowledge and its conventional reflexive articulation. Even the highest path-knowledge can support statements expressed in ordinary convention without thereby becoming identical with those statements.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃvṛtibhajana** — participation in or use of conventional designation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific formulation in this local passage
  - **function:** explains how ultimate terminal knowledges support conventional first-person articulation

- **tadvalānuvyavahāra** — subsequent conventional usage on the strength of that knowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only local expression
  - **function:** distinguishes realization from later verbal/reflexive formulation

- **anāsrava-svalakṣaṇākāra** — an uncontaminated cognition having a self-characteristic as its aspect
  - **status:** new explicit Bhāṣya problem
  - **function:** defines the disputed possible seventeenth pure aspect

- **bahirdeśaka** — teacher or doctrinal group from outside Kashmir
  - **status:** school designation introduced in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** identifies the proponents of an additional uncontaminated aspect

- **anyatrāvacana** — absence of the same wording elsewhere / non-statement in a parallel context
  - **status:** local reasoning expression
  - **function:** textual argument against a proposed reinterpretation

## Logical Determination

The passage distinguishes two kinds of systematic warrant:

```text
formal closure
    Kashmiris:
    pure cognition is exhausted by sixteen aspects

textual extension
    bahirdeśakāḥ:
    śāstra warrants an additional aspect
```

Vasubandhu then introduces a third level: **comparative interpretation of the source itself**. Textual authority is not accepted mechanically; the proposed interpretation must remain coherent across parallel passages.

That is a genuinely disciplined scholastic procedure:

```text
systematic thesis
    ↕
textual evidence
    ↕
comparative interpretation
```

## Organon Contact Point

The useful contact is modest but important. The question is one of **system closure**:

```text
Does the established formal manifold
exhaust the possible forms of pure cognition?
```

The Kashmiri answer treats the sixteen as a closed system. The external teachers appeal to evidence requiring extension. Vasubandhu's method is not to settle the issue by assertion but to test whether the proposed extension survives disciplined interpretation of the textual system.

No stronger Organon synthesis is imposed here.
