# VAK_7.05 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

### IAST

> duḥkhahetvanvayajñāne caturbhyaḥ paracittavit /
> bhūmyakṣapudgalotkrāntaṃ naṣṭājātaṃ na vetti tat // VAK_7.05 //

### Best Translation

Knowledge of another's mind is to be understood from four knowledges: the dharma- and subsequent knowledges of suffering and origin. It does not know a mind surpassing it in level, faculty, or person, nor one that is past or future.

### Source Caution

The e-text contains evident orthographic corruption in the practitioner-rank sentence (`anāgāmyahañcchāvaka ... mārgaṇām`, `...vimuktārga`). The doctrinal sequence is nevertheless clear enough to establish the intended hierarchy. No silent normalization is imposed here.

The opening lines continue the natural argument from VAK 7.04 and explain the phrase `prathamodite` before the prose turns to `paracittajñāna` proper.

## Natural Argument

### 1. Completion of the VAK 7.04 sentence

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The two terminal knowledges, when first arisen, are suffering- and origination-subsequent-knowledges because they take the aggregates of Bhavāgra as object under the aspects of suffering and origination.

Vasubandhu briefly asks whether the vajropama path has the same object as those two. His answer is conditional: if the vajropama path takes suffering or origination as object, the object is the same; if it takes cessation or path as object, it is not.

This completes the correction already required by VAK 7.04: `prathamodite` means "when first arisen," not "first stated."

### 2. What does `paracittavit` mean?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

`vit` is glossed through `vetti`: "it knows," hence `vit` here means a knowledge. The phrase therefore denotes `paracittajñāna`, knowledge of another mind.

The prose then says:

```text
paracittajñānaṃ caturbhyo jñānebhyo draṣṭavyam
```

Knowledge of another mind is "to be seen" or classified from four knowledges.

The accompanying prose identifies the relevant field through dharma-knowledge, subsequent knowledge, path-related knowledge, and conventional knowledges. The terse wording of the e-text is compressed, but the Kārikā controls the fourfold determination as the suffering/origination forms of dharma- and subsequent knowledge.

### 3. The rule restricting its range

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu introduces the second half with:

```text
tasya punar ayaṃ niyamaḥ
```

"For it, however, this is the restriction/rule."

Knowledge of another mind does not know what surpasses it in level, faculty, or person, nor what is past or future.

### 4. Level-superiority

A practitioner established in a lower meditative level does not know a mind belonging to a higher meditative level.

Thus `bhūmyutkrānta` is not an abstract metaphysical superiority. It is concretely a difference of meditative level.

### 5. Faculty-superiority

The prose next states that what exceeds the knower in faculty (`indriya`) is not known.

The e-text attaches this to a sequence involving `śraddhādhimukta`, `samayamukta`, `dṛṣṭiprāpta`, and `asamayavimukta`, showing that faculty-grade and practitioner-type are operative constraints rather than merely differences in sensory capacity.

### 6. Person-superiority

The final hierarchy is explicit in principle: a lower practitioner cannot know the mind/path of a higher one. The passage moves through advanced practitioner-types and culminates in Pratyekabuddha and Buddha.

The exact corrupted wording should not be over-normalized, but the logical rule is secure:

```text
lower rank
    cannot cognize
higher rank
```

### 7. Temporal restriction

Past and future minds are not known because knowledge of another mind has presently occurring mind and mental factors as its object:

```text
vartamāna-paracitta-caitta-viṣayatvāt
```

Its direct object-field is therefore present citta together with associated caittas.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
paracittajñāna
    is a determinate knowledge
        ↓
classified through four prior knowledges
        ↓
subject to a niyama, a strict range-rule
        ↓
not upward across bhūmi
not upward across faculty
not upward across practitioner-rank
not backward into past mind
not forward into future mind
        ↓
object = presently occurring other-mind and associated mental factors
```

The Bhāṣya therefore refuses any reading of `paracittajñāna` as unrestricted clairvoyance. Its existence is admitted only together with exact conditions of intelligibility and exact limits of range.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — the complete passage is straightforward exposition and restriction.
- No explicit objector or school attribution occurs in the VAK 7.05 portion.
- The short vajropama question belongs to the continuation of VAK 7.04 rather than to an independent controversy about `paracittajñāna`.

## New Technical Terms

- **niyama** — restriction; determinate rule governing the range of a knowledge
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** logical/doctrinal restriction

- **vartamāna-paracitta-caitta-viṣayatva** — having presently occurring other-mind and associated mental factors as object
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** explanation of why past and future minds are excluded

- **adhara / uttara** — lower / higher within an ordered meditative or practitioner hierarchy
  - **status:** previously encountered vocabulary, newly operative here
  - **function:** explicit rule of epistemic asymmetry

- **vajropama** — vajra-like path, invoked in the continuation of the VAK 7.04 discussion
  - **status:** previously encountered in Chapter 6
  - **function:** comparison of object-supports at the transition to terminal knowledge

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive Bhāṣya contribution is not simply a list of five limitations. It gives a general doctrine of **graded cognitive accessibility**.

Knowledge of another mind is relational, but the relation is asymmetric:

```text
equal or lower
    potentially knowable

higher
    not knowable by the lower
```

This asymmetry applies through several independent dimensions:

```text
meditative level
faculty-grade
practitioner-rank
```

The temporal restriction is different in kind. Past and future are excluded because the object of this knowledge is presently occurring citta and caittas.

Hence the complete range-rule is:

```text
present
×
not-superior-to-knower
```

The passage thereby defines extraordinary cognition by both positive object-condition and negative boundary-condition.

## Translation Consequence for Kārikā

**Clarification and minor revision recommended.**

The existing Kārikā file says that knowledge of another's mind "arises from four knowledges." The Bhāṣya more exactly says:

```text
caturbhyo jñānebhyo draṣṭavyam
```

Therefore the safer rendering is:

> Knowledge of another's mind is to be understood/classified from four knowledges.

This avoids imposing a causal genesis where Vasubandhu is presently giving a classificatory relation.

The second half is confirmed, but `akṣa` should be interpreted through the Bhāṣya's `indriya` as faculty-grade, not merely as a generic cognitive faculty.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.05 introduces a particularly exact form of finite knowledge:

```text
knowledge
    determined by prior species
    and
    bounded by a rule of admissible objects
```

The rule is not merely empirical. It is structural:

```text
if object-rank > knower-rank
    cognition fails

if object-time ≠ present
    cognition fails
```

The important point is that increased cognitive power does not abolish finitude. It acquires a more exactly articulated finite range.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The most useful contact point is the idea of a cognition whose **valid object-domain is specified by rule**.

```text
capacity
    ≠ unrestricted access

capacity
    = determinate operation
      under explicit admissibility constraints
```

This is especially important after the chapter's earlier divisions. The Kośa does not infer universality from exceptional access. It gives `paracittajñāna` a precise domain and then immediately marks the boundaries beyond which its claim is invalid.

That methodological restraint is more important here than any comparison with Yoga siddhi.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.05 establishes knowledge of another mind as a strictly bounded cognition. The Bhāṣya glosses `paracittavit` as `paracittajñāna`, classifies it through four prior knowledges, and then imposes a `niyama`: it cannot know a mind superior in meditative level, faculty, or practitioner-rank, and it cannot know past or future mind because its object is presently occurring citta and associated mental factors.

The principal translation refinement is to replace the stronger first-pass phrase "arises from four knowledges" with "is to be understood/classified from four knowledges."
