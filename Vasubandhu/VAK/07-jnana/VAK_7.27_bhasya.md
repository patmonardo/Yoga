# VAK_7.27 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> pratilambhaniṣevākhye śubhasaṃskṛtabhāvane /
> pratipakṣavinirdhāvabhāvane sāsravasya tu // VAK_7.27 //

**Best current translation:**  
With respect to wholesome conditioned dharmas, cultivation is called acquisition and repeated practice; but with respect to contaminated dharmas, there are cultivation by counteraction and cultivation by expulsion.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.27.md`](./VAK_7.27.md)

## Natural Argument

### 1. Is cultivation merely acquisition?

**Voice: Objector / question-form**

```text
kiṃ khalu pratilambha eva bhāvanā
```

Is `bhāvanā` nothing more than acquisition?

This question is forced by VAK 7.19–7.26, where cultivation has repeatedly been counted through what is newly acquired for the future.

### 2. No: cultivation is fourfold

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

```text
caturvidhā hi bhāvanā
```

Cultivation is fourfold:

```text
1. pratilambha-bhāvanā
   cultivation as acquisition

2. niṣevaṇa-bhāvanā
   cultivation as repeated practice / inhabitation

3. pratipakṣa-bhāvanā
   cultivation as counteraction

4. vinirdhāvana-bhāvanā
   cultivation as expulsion
```

The verse then distributes these according to the kind and temporal status of the dharma involved.

### 3. Wholesome conditioned dharmas

For `kuśala-saṃskṛta` dharmas:

```text
future dharmas
    → pratilambha-bhāvanā only

present dharmas
    → pratilambha-bhāvanā
       + niṣevaṇa-bhāvanā
```

Thus acquisition and repeated use are not equivalent. A future wholesome determination can be acquired as capacity without being presently exercised; a present wholesome determination is both possessed and actively cultivated through occurrence.

### 4. Contaminated dharmas

For `sāsrava` dharmas:

```text
pratipakṣa-bhāvanā
    cultivation through counteraction

vinirdhāvana-bhāvanā
    cultivation through expulsion
```

The term `bhāvanā` therefore extends beyond positive production. It also names the ordered removal of what must cease.

### 5. Distribution across dharma classes

The Bhāṣya gives the resulting matrix explicitly:

```text
wholesome + contaminated
    → all four modes

uncontaminated
    → two modes
       acquisition + repeated practice

defiled and neutral
    → two modes
       counteraction + expulsion
```

This is the doctrinal structure actually stated in the passage.

### 6. A sixfold alternative and the Kāśmīra reduction

**Voice: bāhyābhidharmikāḥ**

Outside Abhidharmikas count six kinds of cultivation. They add:

```text
saṃvara-bhāvanā
    cultivation as restraint

vibhāvana
    cultivation as removal / dissociation
```

The former is associated with the faculties, the latter with the body.

**Voice: Kāśmīras**

The Kāśmīra position subsumes these two under the existing four:

```text
saṃvara-bhāvanā
    within pratipakṣa-bhāvanā

vibhāvana
    within vinirdhāvana-bhāvanā
```

So the fourfold scheme is defended as sufficient by subsumption rather than by denying the phenomena named by the alternative vocabulary.

## Segmentation and Grammar

### `pratilambha eva bhāvanā`

The particle `eva` sharpens the question:

```text
Is acquisition alone cultivation?
```

Vasubandhu's answer, `netyucyate`, rejects that restriction.

### `pratilambhaniṣevaṇabhāvane ... anāgatānām ekā pratyutpannānām ubhe`

The Bhāṣya supplies the temporal distinction omitted by the verse:

```text
anāgata
    future
    → one mode

pratyutpanna
    present
    → both positive modes
```

### `antarbhūte`

The Kāśmīra response is classificatory:

```text
included within
subsumed under
```

It does not merely reject the sixfold vocabulary; it reduces it to the fourfold system.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

Is cultivation, then, acquisition alone? No, it is said. For cultivation is fourfold: cultivation as acquisition, cultivation as repeated practice, cultivation as counteraction, and cultivation as expulsion.

Of wholesome conditioned dharmas, future dharmas have one of the acquisition-and-practice cultivations, while present dharmas have both. Counteraction-cultivation and expulsion-cultivation concern contaminated dharmas. Thus wholesome contaminated dharmas have four cultivations; uncontaminated dharmas have two, and defiled and neutral dharmas likewise have two.

Outside Abhidharmikas teach six cultivations, adding cultivation as restraint and dissociation. The Kāśmīras hold that these two are included respectively within counteraction-cultivation and expulsion-cultivation.

### Philosophical Translation

Cultivation is not reducible to acquiring a capacity. A wholesome determination may be newly acquired, and when presently actual it is also strengthened through exercise. Contaminated determinations are cultivated negatively: through the counterforces that oppose them and through the process that drives them out. The concept `bhāvanā` therefore names the full transformation of the continuum: acquisition, actual practice, counteraction, and elimination.

## New Technical Terms

- **pratilambha-bhāvanā** — cultivation as acquisition
  - **status:** newly defined as a formal species of cultivation
  - **function:** accounts for the acquisition of future wholesome conditioned dharmas

- **niṣevaṇa-bhāvanā** — cultivation as repeated practice or inhabitation
  - **status:** newly defined in this passage
  - **function:** accounts for the strengthening of wholesome dharmas in present exercise

- **pratipakṣa-bhāvanā** — cultivation through counteraction
  - **status:** newly formalized here
  - **function:** accounts for the opposing relation directed against contaminated dharmas

- **vinirdhāvana-bhāvanā** — cultivation through expulsion
  - **status:** newly formalized here
  - **function:** accounts for removal of contaminated dharmas

- **saṃvara-bhāvanā** — cultivation as restraint
  - **status:** introduced as an alternative external-Abhidharma category
  - **function:** additional mode counted by `bāhyābhidharmikāḥ`, subsumed by the Kāśmīras under counteraction

- **vibhāvana** — dissociation / removal
  - **status:** introduced as an alternative sixth category
  - **function:** subsumed by the Kāśmīras under expulsion

- **bāhyābhidharmika** — outside / non-Kāśmīra Abhidharmika position
  - **status:** doctrinal voice label
  - **function:** preserves an alternative sixfold taxonomy of cultivation

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.27 converts the preceding numerical calculus into a definition of the operation being counted.

The essential distinction is not simply positive versus negative. It is generated by two coordinates:

```text
status of the dharma
    wholesome / contaminated

temporal-functional relation
    future acquisition
    present exercise
    opposed by antidote
    expelled from the continuum
```

The resulting structure is:

| Dharma status | Temporal / functional status | Mode of bhāvanā |
|---|---|---|
| wholesome conditioned | future | acquisition |
| wholesome conditioned | present | acquisition + repeated practice |
| contaminated | opposed | counteraction |
| contaminated | expelled | expulsion |

The Bhāṣya then generalizes:

```text
kuśala-sāsrava
    four modes

anāsrava
    acquisition + repeated practice

kliṣṭa / avyākṛta
    counteraction + expulsion
```

This is a more exact doctrinal result than the first-pass analogy with the four right efforts. The present Bhāṣya passage itself does not state that correspondence, so it should not be treated as source-derived here.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā translation is sound, but `niṣevā` should be kept stronger than generic “practice.” In this passage it is specifically the repeated exercise of a wholesome conditioned dharma already presently occurring.

The first-pass note connecting the four modes directly to the four right efforts should be marked as an external doctrinal comparison rather than as something explicitly stated in this Bhāṣya passage.

## Logical Determination

The sequence VAK 7.19–7.26 could suggest that cultivation is primarily a register of acquisition. VAK 7.27 blocks that reduction.

```text
bhāvanā
    ≠ acquisition alone
```

Instead:

```text
acquire what should become available
exercise what is presently wholesome
oppose what should not arise or persist
expel what remains contaminated
```

Thus cultivation is a complete regulation-system for the continuum.

The most important logical point is that a single superordinate operation, `bhāvanā`, is instantiated by distinct functions according to the status of its object. The four species are not four unrelated practices; they are determinations of one transformation-process.

The Kāśmīra handling of the sixfold alternative is also instructive:

```text
apparent additional species
    ↓
shown to be contained within
existing functional determinations
```

The dispute is therefore partly about the granularity of classification.
