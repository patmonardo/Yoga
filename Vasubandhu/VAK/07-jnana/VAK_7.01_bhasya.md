### Source Caution

The reference e-text contains several compressed or imperfect forms, including `agrahīṇatvāt`, `santiraṇātmakattvāt`, and the Kārikā reading `dak` in one source witness. The interpretation below follows the transmitted argument without silently emending the source. The intended doctrinal contrast is nevertheless clear from the surrounding syntax and the established Path terminology.

## Bhāṣya Translation

Acceptances are spoken of, and knowledges are spoken of; likewise right view and right knowledge.

Why, then, are the acceptances not knowledge, and why is right knowledge not necessarily view?

**“The uncontaminated acceptances are not knowledge.”**

Because the underlying tendency to doubt that is to be abandoned by them has not yet been abandoned.

Nevertheless, they are views, because their nature is examination or discernment. Just as the acceptances are view but not knowledge, so conversely:

**“The cognition of exhaustion and non-arising is not view.”**

The knowledge of exhaustion and the knowledge of non-arising are not view, because their disposition is no longer one of examination and further searching.

**“The noble cognition other than those is both.”**

Uncontaminated prajñā other than the acceptances, the knowledge of exhaustion, and the knowledge of non-arising is both view and knowledge.

**“The other is knowledge.”**

All worldly prajñā is knowledge.

**“And six are views.”**

The five views and worldly right view are the six.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu opens Jñānanirdeśa by refusing to identify three terms that might initially appear interchangeable:

```text
kṣānti
jñāna
dṛṣṭi
```

The distinction is functional and genetic.

The uncontaminated acceptances belong to direct noble cognition, but they do not yet count as `jñāna` because the doubt opposed by that moment has not yet been fully abandoned. Their work is still occurring.

At the opposite end, `kṣayajñāna` and `anutpādajñāna` are fully accomplished knowledges, but they no longer count as `dṛṣṭi`, because there is no remaining examining or searching function.

Between these poles, the remaining noble prajñā is both knowledge and view.

The chapter therefore begins not from a dictionary definition of knowledge, but from the **different functions of discriminative cognition within the Path**.

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. The initial problem

The tradition speaks of both:

```text
kṣānti / jñāna
samyagdṛṣṭi / samyagjñāna
```

The question is whether these expressions denote the same cognitive function.

Vasubandhu answers no.

### 2. Acceptance is view but not yet knowledge

The Bhāṣya grounds this distinction in unfinished practical work:

```text
vicikitsā-anuśaya
    still to be abandoned
        ↓
kṣānti
    active discernment
        ↓
not yet completed jñāna
```

The decisive point is temporal-functional rather than merely lexical. `Kṣānti` already penetrates and examines the truth, and therefore qualifies as `dṛṣṭi`; but the corresponding obstacle has not yet been fully removed, so it is not yet classified as accomplished `jñāna`.

### 3. Final knowledge is knowledge but no longer view

The reverse case is supplied by the two final knowledges:

```text
kṣayajñāna
anutpādajñāna
    ↓
completed ascertainment
    ↓
no further examination or search
```

Their exclusion from `dṛṣṭi` is therefore not a defect. It marks completion.

### 4. The middle field is both

Other uncontaminated prajñā both knows and examines. This gives the intersection:

```text
jñāna ∩ dṛṣṭi
```

The categories are thus overlapping but non-identical.

### 5. Worldly prajñā

All worldly prajñā is broadly classified as knowledge. Six worldly forms also satisfy the determination of view: the five views and worldly right view.

## New Technical Terms

- **santīraṇa** — examination, discernment, investigative determination
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification in this passage
  - **function:** explains why kṣānti counts as `dṛṣṭi`

- **parimārgaṇa** — searching, further investigation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification in this passage
  - **function:** explains negatively why the final two knowledges are not `dṛṣṭi`

- **vicikitsā-anuśaya** — underlying tendency to doubt
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary, newly decisive here
  - **function:** supplies the ground for excluding uncontaminated kṣānti from completed `jñāna`

- **āśaya** — disposition, orientation, operative tendency
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** characterizes the absence of further inquiry in exhaustion- and non-arising-knowledge

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes the Kārikā substantially sharper.

`Jñāna` is not merely any occurrence of discriminative cognition. In this opening distinction, it is cognition considered as **accomplished ascertainment**.

`Dṛṣṭi` is cognition considered under its **examining or discerning function**.

Thus:

```text
kṣānti
    discernment still doing its work
    = dṛṣṭi
    ≠ jñāna

ordinary noble prajñā
    discernment + ascertainment
    = dṛṣṭi + jñāna

kṣayajñāna / anutpādajñāna
    completed ascertainment
    = jñāna
    ≠ dṛṣṭi
```

The categories therefore express moments in a cognitive process rather than merely classes arranged side by side.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing Kārikā translation is sound, but the Bhāṣya gives a more precise reason for the exclusions:

- kṣānti is not knowledge because the doubt it counters is not yet fully abandoned;
- kṣaya- and anutpāda-jñāna are not view because examination and further searching have ceased.

The phrase “accomplished knowledge” is therefore justified as an interpretive rendering of the functional contrast, though it should remain an explanatory gloss rather than a replacement for `jñāna` itself.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.01 establishes a genuine crossed determination rather than a hierarchy of synonyms.

```text
                dṛṣṭi
                 yes        no

jñāna   no      kṣānti      —

        yes     other       kṣaya-jñāna
                noble       anutpāda-jñāna
                prajñā
```

The significant feature is that the two predicates are generated from different functions:

```text
dṛṣṭi
    = cognition as examination

jñāna
    = cognition as accomplished ascertainment
```

The Bhāṣya therefore turns the verse into a miniature genetic analysis of knowing:

```text
examination still active
    ↓
examination + ascertainment
    ↓
ascertainment with no further search
```

## Organon Contact Point

This opening is especially important for later Organon work because the Kośa does not define cognition through static marks alone. It determines cognition through **the reciprocal relation of cognitive form and practical completion**.

Theoretical discrimination and Path-efficacy are already inseparable here, but they are not collapsed. The cognitive act is classified according to both what it does as knowing and where it stands in the actual removal of error.

A restrained contact point is therefore:

```text
Intuition / encounter with truth
    ↕
Conceptual-discriminative determination
    ↕
accomplished knowledge
```

The stronger claim that the Principle of Cognitive Science requires the reciprocity of Intuition and Concept belongs to the Organon proper. VAK 7.01 supplies an important empirical-rational specimen: knowledge is differentiated internally by the transition from active discernment to completed ascertainment.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.01 opens Jñānanirdeśa by distinguishing `kṣānti`, `jñāna`, and `dṛṣṭi` according to their cognitive and Path-functions. Uncontaminated kṣānti is already view because it examines and discerns, but it is not yet knowledge because the doubt it must overcome has not yet been fully abandoned. The knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising are completed knowledge but no longer view because there is no further examination or search. Other noble prajñā is both. The chapter therefore begins with a genetic determination of knowledge rather than a merely lexical definition.
