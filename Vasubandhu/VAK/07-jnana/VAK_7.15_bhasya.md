# VAK_7.15 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dhyāneṣv anyamanojñānaṃ kāmarūpāśrayaṃ ca tat /
> kāmāśrayaṃ tu dharmākhyam anyat traidhātukāśrayam // VAK_7.15 //

**Best translation:**  
Knowledge of another mind occurs only in the meditative absorptions, and it has the desire and form domains as its support. Dharma-knowledge has the desire domain as its support; the remaining knowledges have support in the three domains.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.15.md`](./VAK_7.15.md)

## Bhāṣya Passage

> paracittajñānaṃ caturṣv eva dhyāneṣu nānyatra /
>
> kāmarūpadhātvoś ca tat paracittajñānaṃ saṃmukhīkriyate /
>
> dharmajñānaṃ tu kāmadhātvāśrayam eva / na rūpārūpyadhātvoḥ saṃmukhīkriyate /
>
> kiṃ punar anyat / paracittajñānaṃ dharmajñānanirmuktam /

## Bhāṣya Translation

Knowledge of another mind occurs only in the four dhyānas and nowhere else.

And that knowledge of another mind is made present in the desire and form domains.

Dharma-knowledge, however, has only the desire domain as its support. It is not made present in the form or formless domains.

What, then, is meant by “the remaining”? Everything apart from knowledge of another mind and dharma-knowledge.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu.

The passage is straightforward exposition. No explicit objection or competing school position is introduced.

## Argument

The Bhāṣya specifies the support-distribution of the knowledges by distinguishing three relations that the compressed verse can easily blur.

First, knowledge of another mind has a restricted meditative basis:

```text
paracittajñāna
    → four dhyānas only
```

Second, Vasubandhu states separately in which domains this cognition may be made present:

```text
paracittajñāna
    → kāmadhātu
    → rūpadhātu
```

This is important because the Kārikā's `kāmarūpāśrayam` should not be silently converted into an object-range statement. The Bhāṣya gloss is explicitly about `saṃmukhīkriyate`: where the cognition itself is brought into manifestation.

Third, dharma-knowledge is even more sharply localized:

```text
dharmajñāna
    → kāmadhātu only
    → not rūpadhātu
    → not ārūpyadhātu
```

Finally, Vasubandhu resolves the elliptical `anyat`:

```text
“the remaining”
    = all knowledges except
        paracittajñāna
        dharmajñāna
```

These remaining knowledges may therefore be supported throughout the three domains.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.15 continues the formal specification begun in 7.14, but now the governing question is not merely in which `bhūmi` a knowledge is acquired. It is the `āśraya` of the cognition and the domain in which it can be made present.

The Bhāṣya therefore yields the following exact matrix:

| Knowledge | Support / manifestation range |
|---|---|
| paracittajñāna | four dhyānas; made present in desire and form domains |
| dharmajñāna | desire domain only |
| remaining knowledges | all three domains |

The knowledge of another mind remains doubly constrained. It is neither a free-standing faculty nor a cognition available in every meditative level. Its occurrence depends upon the four dhyānas, and its manifestation is confined to the desire and form domains.

## Translation Consequence

The first-pass Kārikā file treated `kāmarūpāśrayaṃ` partly as though it might describe the minds apprehended by knowledge of another mind. The Bhāṣya requires a correction.

```text
kāmarūpāśrayaṃ
    here = domain-support / domain of manifestation
    not directly = object-domain
```

This is confirmed by Vasubandhu's gloss:

```text
kāmarūpadhātvoś ca tat paracittajñānaṃ saṃmukhīkriyate
```

The distinction between `āśraya` and `ālambana` must therefore be preserved strictly.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃmukhīkriyate** — is made present, brought into manifestation, actualized in occurrence.  
  **Status:** important Bhāṣya clarification.  
  **Function:** specifies where a knowledge can actually be brought into presence rather than what it takes as object.

- **nirmukta** — excluded from, apart from.  
  **Status:** ordinary term used technically in the classification.  
  **Function:** resolves the elliptical `anyat` as the knowledges excluding paracittajñāna and dharmajñāna.

## Logical Determination

The important distinction is now:

```text
ālambana
    what cognition takes as object

āśraya
    the support/domain in which cognition occurs

saṃmukhīkaraṇa
    cognition's actual manifestation there
```

These relations cannot be collapsed.

Vasubandhu is progressively making a complete specification of knowledge by orthogonal determinations:

```text
aspect
object
counteractive function
meditative level
support
manifestation-domain
```

This is why the ten knowledges are systematic rather than merely enumerative.

## Organon Light

The immediate lesson is structural rather than speculative:

> A capacity is not completely determined by what it can know; one must also state the conditions and domain under which that capacity can become actual.

For knowledge of another mind, Chapter 7 now distinguishes at least:

```text
what it can know
how it knows
what exceeds its range
which dhyānas support it
where it can become manifest
```

That is enough for the present Bhāṣya pass. No further Yoga or Organon identification is imposed here.
