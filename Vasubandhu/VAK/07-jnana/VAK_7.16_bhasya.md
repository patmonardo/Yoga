# VAK_7.16 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> smṛtyupasthānam ekaṃ dhīr nirodhe paracittadhīḥ /
> trīṇi catvāri śeṣāṇi dharmadhīgocaro nava // VAK_7.16 //

**Best translation:**  
Knowledge of cessation is one application of mindfulness. Knowledge of another mind is three. The remaining knowledges are four. Nine knowledges are the object-range of dharma-knowledge.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.16.md`](./VAK_7.16.md)

## Segmentation by Natural Argument

### 1. Transition from levels/supports to applications of mindfulness

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The preceding classification by `bhūmi` and `āśraya` is declared complete. Vasubandhu now turns to `smṛtyupasthānasaṃgraha`, the classification of the knowledges according to the applications of mindfulness.

### 2. Cessation-knowledge is one application

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu first clarifies terminology:

```text
dhīḥ
prajñā
jñāna
```

are synonyms in this passage.

He then states that `nirodhajñāna` belongs to one application of mindfulness only:

```text
dharmasmṛtyupasthāna
```

Cessation is therefore apprehended only under the application of mindfulness to dharmas.

### 3. Knowledge of another mind is threefold

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

`paracittajñāna` falls under three applications:

```text
vedanā-smṛtyupasthāna
citta-smṛtyupasthāna
dharma-smṛtyupasthāna
```

It is not classified under mindfulness of body.

### 4. The remaining knowledges are fourfold

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

All knowledges other than `nirodhajñāna` and `paracittajñāna` can belong to all four applications of mindfulness.

### 5. Transition to knowledges as objects of knowledges

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu then changes the classificatory axis again:

```text
katamasya jñānasya kati jñānāny ālambanam
```

How many knowledges are the object-support of each knowledge?

The first answer is:

```text
dharmajñāna
    → nine knowledges as objects
    → excludes anvayajñāna
```

The complete matrix continues in VAK 7.17.

## Literal Prose Translation

The determination of levels and supports has been completed. The inclusion under the applications of mindfulness must now be stated; it is as follows.

“Knowledge concerning cessation is one application of mindfulness.” `Dhī`, `prajñā`, and `jñāna` are synonyms. Knowledge of cessation is one application of mindfulness, namely the application of mindfulness to dharmas.

“Knowledge of another mind — three.” Knowledge of another mind is the three applications of mindfulness to feeling, mind, and dharmas.

“The remaining ones — four.” The knowledges other than knowledge of cessation and knowledge of another mind are the four applications of mindfulness.

How many knowledges are objects of each knowledge?

“Nine are the object-range of dharma-knowledge.” Nine knowledges are the object-support of dharma-knowledge, except for anvaya-knowledge.

## Argument Reconstruction

The verse performs two successive classifications.

First:

```text
knowledge
    classified by
application of mindfulness
```

This yields:

```text
nirodhajñāna
    → 1

paracittajñāna
    → 3

remaining knowledges
    → 4
```

Second:

```text
knowledge
    classified by
which other knowledges can become its objects
```

The first entry is:

```text
dharmajñāna
    → 9 knowledge-objects
    → anvayajñāna excluded
```

The chapter is therefore no longer merely listing kinds of knowledge. It is constructing a relational matrix among them.

## New Technical Terms

- **smṛtyupasthānasaṃgraha** — classification or inclusion according to the applications of mindfulness
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation around a Kārikā term
  - **function:** classificatory transition

- **paryāya** — synonym, equivalent expression
  - **status:** previously encountered broadly
  - **function:** terminological clarification

- **ālambana** — object-support, here specifically another knowledge functioning as the object of a knowledge
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** object-relation in the inter-knowledge matrix

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya makes explicit that the same cognition can be classified along multiple independent axes:

```text
level
support
application of mindfulness
object-range
```

The applications of mindfulness do not create new knowledges. They specify the standpoint under which an already distinguished knowledge is operative.

The second half begins a higher-order classification:

```text
knowledge
    can itself become
object of knowledge
```

This is a decisive structural move. The chapter now treats the ten knowledges not merely as isolated species but as members of a determinate network of possible cognitive relations.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The existing translation is confirmed with two clarifications.

1. `dhī` should not be over-differentiated from `prajñā` and `jñāna` here; Vasubandhu explicitly calls them synonyms.
2. `dharmadhīgocaro nava` means that nine of the ten knowledges can function as objects of `dharmajñāna`, with `anvayajñāna` excluded.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The important formal lesson is relational:

```text
knowledge
    is not fully determined by
its own intrinsic type

knowledge is also determined by
    the standpoint under which it operates
    and
    what other knowledges it can take as object
```

VAK 7.16 therefore begins to turn the tenfold taxonomy into a matrix of reflective relations. No stronger Organon synthesis is imposed here.
