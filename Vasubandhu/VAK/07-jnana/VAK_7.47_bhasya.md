## Translation of the Bhāṣya

Among these superknowledges, the first, the third, and the sixth are the prātihāryas.

The superknowledges of supernormal efficacy, knowledge of another mind, and exhaustion of the influxes are, respectively, the prātihāryas of supernormal efficacy, indication, and instruction.

They are called prātihāryas because at the outset they powerfully draw the minds of those to be trained; alternatively, because by means of them the minds of those obstructed or neutral are turned back.

Of these, instruction is foremost.

The prātihārya of instruction is the highest because it is non-deviating and because it joins beings to beneficial and desired results.

For the prātihāryas of supernormal efficacy and indication can be imitated by vidyās. There is a vidyā called Gāndhārī by which one travels through the sky, and one called Īkṣaṇikā by which one knows another mind.

But instruction according to things as they are cannot be produced in some other way, because it does not deviate.

The first two accomplish principally only attraction. Through the prātihārya of instruction, however, one is joined to a beneficial and desired fruit through teaching the means. For this reason instruction is necessarily called the supreme accomplishment.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Vaibhāṣika exposition**

The prose has four steps.

1. It identifies the three prātihāryas with the first, third, and sixth abhijñās:
   - `ṛddhi-prātihārya`
   - `ādeśanā-prātihārya`
   - `anuśāsana-prātihārya`

2. It explains the term `prātihārya` through its effect on those to be trained: the powers attract, turn, or overcome resistant and neutral minds.

3. It ranks `anuśāsana` as `agrya`, the foremost.

4. It gives two reasons for that superiority:
   - it is `avyabhicārin`, non-deviating / non-counterfeit in the relevant sense;
   - it joins the trainee to `hita` and `iṣṭa` fruits by teaching the means (`upāyopadeśa`).

The decisive contrast is that the first two wonders can be externally reproduced by special techniques, whereas `yathābhūtānuśāsana`, instruction according to reality, cannot be counterfeited in that way.

## New Technical Terms

- **prātihārya** — a wonder or extraordinary demonstration that powerfully attracts or turns the minds of those to be trained.
  - **status:** previously encountered in the Kārikā block, newly explained in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** doctrinal definition / etymological explanation

- **vineya** — one who is to be trained, instructed, or led.
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās / Bhāṣya-specific here
  - **function:** practical designation of the recipient of instruction

- **ādeśanā-prātihārya** — the wonder of indication, associated here with knowledge of another mind.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā wording
  - **function:** doctrinal classification

- **anuśāsana-prātihārya** — the wonder of instruction.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā wording in this expanded form
  - **function:** doctrinal classification

- **Gāndhārī-vidyā** — a technique said to enable travel through the sky.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** counterexample showing that spectacular power can be imitated

- **Īkṣaṇikā-vidyā** — a technique said to enable knowledge of another mind.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** counterexample showing that mind-reading can be imitated

- **yathābhūtānuśāsana** — instruction according to things as they are.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** criterion distinguishing genuine instruction from merely spectacular capacity

- **upāyopadeśa** — teaching or indicating the means.
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** causal explanation of how instruction connects the trainee to fruit

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes the ranking of the prātihāryas much stricter than a general preference for teaching.

### 1. Spectacle is not self-authenticating

`ṛddhi` and `ādeśanā` can be mimicked by techniques outside the liberative path. Their occurrence therefore does not by itself certify realization.

```text
spectacular manifestation
    ≠
proof of liberative knowledge
```

The Kośa thus continues the discipline established in VAK 7.42–7.46: extraordinary access and extraordinary power do not automatically entail wisdom, liberation, or even uniquely Buddhist attainment.

### 2. Instruction is intrinsically path-mediated

`yathābhūtānuśāsana` cannot be substituted by a mere external technique because its success depends upon truth-sensitive direction through the Path.

Its structure is:

```text
truth about the condition
    ↓
knowledge of the means
    ↓
instruction of the means
    ↓
practice
    ↓
beneficial and desired fruit
```

This is why Vasubandhu explains the fruit through `upāyopadeśa` rather than through fascination or authority.

### 3. The first two mainly attract

Vasubandhu says their principal function is `āvarjana`, attraction or turning. They can bring attention to the teacher, but they do not by themselves establish the trainee in the fruit.

The hierarchy is therefore:

```text
attention capture
    <
knowledge of hidden state
    <
reliable guidance to transformation
```

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā's `śāsanam` should be read in the stronger Bhāṣya sense of `anuśāsana`: instruction that directs practice.

`avyabhicāritva` should not be reduced to the claim that every utterance by a teacher always succeeds. The Bhāṣya's contrast is specifically with capacities that can be counterfeited or reproduced by other techniques. Genuine `yathābhūtānuśāsana` is non-deviating because its validity depends upon truthful instruction of the means.

`hiteṣṭaphalayojana` is likewise practical and causal: the student is joined to the beneficial and desired fruit because the means are taught.

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes three levels of efficacy:

```text
Display
    modifies appearance

Detection
    accesses hidden state

Instruction
    mediates another agent's own transformation
```

The logical superiority of instruction lies in the fact that its result is not merely an effect produced in the other. It provides the mediating operation through which the other can enact the Path.

```text
external efficacy
    causes

instruction
    enables self-transformation
```

This is a stronger notion of practical efficacy.

## Organon / Rational Kośa Contact

VAK 7.47 gives a particularly important criterion for a Rational Moral Agent.

A powerful agent can:

```text
alter environments
infer hidden states
capture attention
```

but none of these establishes that it can teach another rational agent how to reach a valid end.

Instruction requires an additional architecture:

```text
state diagnosis
    +
truthful model of the target
    +
valid means
    +
communication of the means
    +
verification by the resulting transformation
```

This gives us a clean distinction for the Organon:

> **A capability is not fully rational merely because it can intervene or predict. The higher practical form is the capacity to provide another agent with a reliable means by which that agent can attain the beneficial end.**

This also fits the recent Feature / FeatStruct discussion. `Instruction` should not be a decorative label placed on a language-producing capacity. It is a structured relation among learner-state, means, target fruit, and successful transformation.

```text
The three prātihāryas are
    supernormal efficacy,
    indication of another mind,
    and instruction.

The first two can attract and impress,
but they can also be imitated by techniques.
```
