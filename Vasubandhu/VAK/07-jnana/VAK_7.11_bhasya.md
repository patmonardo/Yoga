# VAK_7.11 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> tathā paramanojñānaṃ nirmalaṃ samalaṃ punaḥ /  
> jñeyasvalakṣaṇākāram ekaikadravyagocaram // VAK_7.11 //

**Best translation:**  
Pure knowledge of another mind is likewise [four-aspected]. The contaminated form, however, takes the self-characteristic of the knowable as its aspect and ranges over one individual entity at a time.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.11.md`](./VAK_7.11.md)

## Natural Argument

The Bhāṣya develops VAK 7.11 in four stages.

### 1. Pure and contaminated knowledge of another mind

Pure (`anāsrava`) knowledge of another mind is four-aspected because it belongs to path-knowledge (`mārgajñāna`).

Contaminated (`sāsrava`) knowledge of another mind is different. It apprehends the **self-characteristic (`svalakṣaṇa`)** of the presently knowable citta or caitta.

The distinction is therefore not merely moral purity-status. It changes the aspect under which the cognition operates.

### 2. One entity at a time

Vasubandhu explains `ekaikadravyagocara` literally and operationally:

```text
when it apprehends citta,
    it does not at that same time apprehend the associated mental factors;

when it apprehends feeling,
    it does not at that same time apprehend recognition;

and so forth.
```

Knowledge of another mind is therefore not a simultaneous total scan of a mental continuum. Its object is singularly delimited.

### 3. How then can scripture say “he knows a lustful mind as lustful”?

An objection arises from sūtra language such as:

```text
sarāgaṃ cittaṃ sarāgaṃ cittam iti yathābhūtaṃ prajānāti
```

If knowledge grasps only one entity at a time, how can it know both a mind and its associated passion?

Vasubandhu's answer is that the two are **not apprehended simultaneously** (`na tayor yugapadgrahaṇam`). He compares this to apprehending cloth and its stain: their relation may be known without one cognition literally grasping two substances at once.

This launches an extended analysis of the standard pairs used to classify another's mind:

```text
sarāga / vigatarāga
sadveṣa / vigatadveṣa
samoha / vigatamoha
saṃkṣipta / vikṣipta
līna / pragṛhīta
parītta / mahadgata
uddhata / anuddhata
asamāhita / samāhita
abhāvita / bhāvita
avimukta / vimukta
```

The prose tests competing interpretations of these pairs against both sūtra and śāstra.

### 4. Sūtra versus śāstra and final restriction

The passage becomes openly dialectical. A proposed Vaibhāṣika interpretation is challenged because it does not fit the wording of the sūtra. The striking methodological statement is:

```text
varaṃ śāstravirodho na sūtravirodhaḥ
```

Better contradiction with the śāstra than contradiction with the sūtra.

Vasubandhu then returns to the central problem: does knowledge of another mind grasp the other mind's **aspect** or its **object-support**?

His answer is no. It does not reproduce the other cognition's own object or aspect. It knows, for example, “this mind is lustful,” but not “this mind is lustful toward that particular form.” Otherwise it would become cognition of that external form as well and would collapse the distinction between knowing another mind and directly taking that mind's object.

The Bhāṣya closes with a compact specification of the field of `paracittajñāna`:

- it takes presently occurring citta and caittas of another continuum as its object;
- it concerns determinate entities and self-characteristics;
- it ranges over desire- and form-domain mental events and also uncontaminated cognition where allowed;
- it is absent from the Path of Seeing;
- it is obtained in the Path of Cultivation;
- it is disconnected from the emptiness and signless samādhis;
- it is not included in knowledge of exhaustion or non-arising;
- it is absent from the uninterrupted path.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
paracittajñāna
    divides as pure / contaminated

pure
    → four-aspected because it is path-knowledge

contaminated
    → apprehends svalakṣaṇa of citta/caitta
    → one individual entity at a time

objection
    scripture speaks of “lustful mind,” etc.

resolution
    mind and associated passion are not simultaneously grasped
    relation can still be known

further dispute
    how should the scriptural mind-pairs be interpreted?

methodological rule
    sūtra has priority over an incompatible śāstra-reading

final restriction
    knowledge of another mind knows the condition of another cognition
    but not that cognition's own object or aspect as such
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — exposition of pure and contaminated `paracittajñāna`, singular object-range, and the final restriction concerning aspect and object-support.

**Voice: Objector** — challenges based on sūtra statements about knowing a lustful mind, and objections concerning contracted/dispersed mind and related pairs.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — one interpretation of the paired mind-states, especially the attempt to classify wholesome/unwholesome states under the standard sūtra vocabulary.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — rejects an interpretation when it conflicts with sūtra, explicitly preferring sūtra over śāstra.

## New Technical Terms

- **yugapadgrahaṇa** — simultaneous apprehension
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-operative
  - **function:** marks what `paracittajñāna` does not do when related mental factors are distinguished.

- **saṃsṛṣṭasarāgatā / saṃyogasarāgatā** — two senses of being “with passion,” through conjunction/association
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only elaboration here
  - **function:** distinguishes different ways in which a mind may be called lustful.

- **sūtravirodha / śāstravirodha** — contradiction with sūtra / contradiction with treatise
  - **status:** previously encountered argumentative vocabulary
  - **function:** hierarchy of textual authority in doctrinal adjudication.

- **prāpti** — acquisition/possession
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** tested as one possible basis for calling a mind lustful, then rejected as the direct criterion relevant to `paracittajñāna`.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.11 sharply limits what “knowing another mind” means.

It is **not**:

```text
unrestricted access to another continuum
simultaneous apprehension of all mental factors
reproduction of the other cognition's object
reproduction of the other cognition's own aspect
```

It is instead:

```text
presently indexed
singular in object
svalakṣaṇa-directed
classificatory with respect to determinate mental conditions
```

The Bhāṣya therefore makes `paracittajñāna` a highly constrained diagnostic cognition rather than a totalizing mental transparency.

## Translation Consequence for Kārikā

The existing translation of `ekaikadravyagocara` as “ranging over one individual entity at a time” is strongly confirmed.

The Bhāṣya also confirms that `jñeyasvalakṣaṇākāra` should be read concretely: contaminated knowledge of another mind takes the self-characteristic of the citta or caitta being known as its operative aspect.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The most useful structural point is that **classification of another cognition is not identity with that cognition**.

A cognition may determine:

```text
this mind is lustful
this mind is concentrated
this mind is liberated
```

without inheriting the object's own object-support or internal aspect.

That is a valuable constraint for any future model of diagnostic cognition: second-order knowledge need not duplicate the first-order representation it classifies.

No fuller Yoga or Guru-model comparison is imposed here.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.11 turns knowledge of another mind into a precisely bounded science of another cognitive state. Pure and contaminated forms differ in aspect-structure; contaminated cognition grasps one determinate mental entity at a time; scriptural classifications do not imply simultaneous apprehension of mind and all associated factors; and second-order cognition does not thereby take over the first-order cognition's own object or aspect.

The passage is also methodologically important because Vasubandhu explicitly allows a śāstra-reading to be rejected when it conflicts with sūtra.
