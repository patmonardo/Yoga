# VAK_7.08_bhasya — Why the Knowledges Are Ten

## Kārikā

```text
svabhāvapratipakṣābhyām ākārākāragocarāt /
prayogakṛtakṛtyatvahetūpacayato daśa // VAK_7.08 //
```

## Bhāṣya Source

Vasubandhu first asks why, if the knowledges under discussion are present in these forms, exactly ten are established. He answers that ten knowledges are established through seven determining grounds.

The Bhāṣya then distributes those grounds explicitly:

```text
svabhāvataḥ
    saṃvṛtijñāna

pratipakṣataḥ
    dharmajñāna / anvayajñāna

ākārataḥ
    duḥkhajñāna / samudayajñāna

ākāra-ālambanataḥ
    nirodhajñāna / mārgajñāna

prayogataḥ
    paracittajñāna

kṛtakṛtyataḥ
    kṣayajñāna

hetūpacayataḥ
    anutpādajñāna
```

## Natural Argument

The ten knowledges are not retained merely because the tradition lists ten names. Their number is justified by distinct grounds of determination.

Vasubandhu proceeds member by member.

### 1. Conventional knowledge — distinguished by intrinsic nature

`Saṃvṛtijñāna` is established by **svabhāva**, intrinsic nature, because it is not knowledge of ultimate meaning (`aparamārthajñānatvāt`).

The distinction here concerns what sort of cognition it is.

### 2. Dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge — distinguished by counteraction

`Dharmajñāna` and `anvayajñāna` are distinguished by **pratipakṣa**, the domain they counteract.

```text
dharmajñāna
    → counteracts the desire domain

anvayajñāna
    → counteracts the higher domains
```

The distinction is functional rather than merely nominal.

### 3. Knowledge of suffering and origin — distinguished by aspect

`Duḥkhajñāna` and `samudayajñāna` are distinguished by **ākāra**, because their object-support is not different (`ālambanābhedāt`).

Thus the same object-field can yield distinct knowledges when it is apprehended under different determinate aspects.

### 4. Knowledge of cessation and path — distinguished by aspect and object-support

`Nirodhajñāna` and `mārgajñāna` are distinguished through both **ākāra** and **ālambana**.

Here both the mode of apprehension and the object-support differ.

This clarifies the compressed kārikā expression `ākārākāragocarāt`: the verse moves from distinction by aspect alone to distinction by aspect together with object-domain.

### 5. Knowledge of another mind — distinguished by preparation

`Paracittajñāna` is distinguished by **prayoga**, preparatory application.

Vasubandhu immediately explains why. Such knowledge can also know associated mental factors (`caitta`), so its name cannot be justified simply by saying that its object is only another's citta.

Rather, it is called knowledge of another mind because the practitioner undertakes the preparation specifically for the purpose of knowing another's mind, and the cognition is accomplished from that preparation.

The name therefore follows the **aim of the preparatory practice**, not an exclusive object restriction.

### 6. Knowledge of exhaustion — distinguished by accomplished task

`Kṣayajñāna` is distinguished by **kṛtakṛtyatva**, the state of having done what had to be done.

Vasubandhu explains this through the arising of such knowledge in a continuum whose task has been completed (`kṛtakṛtyasaṃtāna`).

Its determination therefore depends on the accomplished status of the practitioner-continuum.

### 7. Knowledge of non-arising — distinguished by accumulation of causes

`Anutpādajñāna` is distinguished by **hetūpacaya**, accumulation or completion of causes.

The Bhāṣya states that this knowledge has all uncontaminated factors as causes (`sarvānāsravahetukatvāt`).

Thus its distinctness is grounded in the fully developed causal basis from which it arises.

## Argument Reconstruction

Vasubandhu's reasoning can be stated compactly:

```text
Question:
Why establish exactly ten knowledges?

Answer:
Because the ten are generated as distinct scientific determinations
through seven non-identical grounds.
```

The mapping is:

| Ground | Knowledge(s) distinguished |
|---|---|
| intrinsic nature | conventional knowledge |
| counteraction | dharma / subsequent knowledge |
| aspect | suffering / origin knowledge |
| aspect + object-support | cessation / path knowledge |
| preparation | knowledge of another mind |
| accomplished task | knowledge of exhaustion |
| causal accumulation | knowledge of non-arising |

The number ten is therefore not a flat enumeration. It is the result of applying determinate principles of differentiation.

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu — asks why the knowledges are established as ten and gives the sevenfold explanatory scheme.

**Voice:** inherited Abhidharma analysis — the technical tenfold classification and its causal/path distinctions are being expounded rather than disputed in this passage.

No significant objector voice appears in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **svabhāva** — intrinsic nature; status: previously encountered; function: ground distinguishing conventional knowledge.
- **pratipakṣa** — counteragent / that which is opposed; status: previously encountered; function: distinguishes dharma-knowledge from subsequent knowledge by their respective domains of counteraction.
- **ākāra** — aspect or determinate mode of apprehension; status: previously encountered; function: distinguishes suffering-knowledge from origin-knowledge.
- **ālambana** — object-support; status: previously encountered; function: combines with aspect to distinguish cessation-knowledge and path-knowledge.
- **prayoga** — preparatory application; status: previously encountered; function: grounds the naming and distinction of knowledge of another mind.
- **kṛtakṛtyatva** — state of having done what had to be done; status: rare in the Kārikās; function: distinguishes knowledge of exhaustion by completed path-task.
- **hetūpacaya** — accumulation / completion of causes; status: rare in the Kārikās; function: distinguishes knowledge of non-arising by its fully developed uncontaminated causal basis.
- **aparamārthajñāna** — knowledge that is not knowledge of ultimate meaning; status: Bhāṣya clarification; function: explains why conventional knowledge is distinguished by its intrinsic nature.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.08 is methodological. The chapter is not content with saying that ten knowledges exist. It gives the **grounds according to which the tenfold system is scientifically warranted**.

The crucial distinction is that different knowledges can differ for different reasons:

```text
what the cognition is
what it counteracts
how it apprehends
what it takes as object-support
what preparation produces it
whether the path-task is completed
what causal basis has been accumulated
```

One universal classificatory criterion is therefore not imposed on all ten members.

This is a highly articulated science of cognition: the species are fixed by the relevant ground of difference in each case.

## Translation Consequence for Kārikā

The Bhāṣya confirms that the repeated `ākāra` in the compressed compound should not be flattened.

The intended progression is:

```text
ākārataḥ
    by aspect

ākāra-ālambanataḥ
    by aspect together with object-support
```

So the Kārikā's `ākārākāragocarāt` represents two distinct classificatory grounds.

The Bhāṣya also sharpens `prayoga`: knowledge of another mind is not so named because it knows only citta. It can also know caittas. Its distinct name derives from the practitioner's preparatory aim toward knowing another mind.

Finally, `hetūpacaya` is not merely generic “causal increase.” For `anutpādajñāna`, Vasubandhu specifies the relevant point: all uncontaminated factors function as its causes.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This verse gives a clean principle of scientific classification:

```text
species are justified by grounds of difference
not by enumeration alone
```

That is enough for the present pass.

It is especially useful that Vasubandhu does not force one criterion across the whole system. Different species of knowledge become intelligible through different determining relations.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.08 answers why the system recognizes ten knowledges.

They are ten because seven grounds generate the required distinctions:

```text
svabhāva
pratipakṣa
ākāra
ākāra + ālambana
prayoga
kṛtakṛtyatva
hetūpacaya
```

The Bhāṣya's most important clarifications are:

1. conventional knowledge is distinguished by its non-ultimate intrinsic nature;
2. dharma- and subsequent knowledge differ by the domains they counteract;
3. suffering and origin knowledge differ by aspect despite sharing an object-support;
4. cessation and path knowledge differ by both aspect and object-support;
5. knowledge of another mind is named from its preparatory aim, since it can also know associated mental factors;
6. exhaustion knowledge belongs to a continuum whose task has been completed;
7. non-arising knowledge rests on the accumulation of the complete uncontaminated causal basis.

The tenfold classification is therefore grounded rather than merely inherited.
