# VAK_7.54 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

### IAST

> divyaśrotrākṣiṇī rūpaprasādau dhyānabhūmikau /
> sabhāgāvikale nityaṃ dūrasūkṣmādigocare // VAK_7.54 //

### Best Translation

The divine ear and eye are two refined material faculties grounded in dhyāna. They are invariably functionally active and unimpaired, and their range includes distant, subtle, and related objects.

## Source Cautions

The working e-text is not a critical edition. It contains evident orthographic defects in this passage, including `tayiorviṣyaḥ`, `anabhhisaṃskāreṇa`, and `lokahātūn`. These are preserved here rather than silently repaired.

## Course of the Argument

### 1. How the divine faculties are produced

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

When one is entered into dhyāna, deliberate application to sound and light (`śabdāloka-ābhoga-prayoga`) causes two refined material faculties (`rūpaprasāda`) to arise from material factors belonging to the dhyāna level. They arise adjacent to the ordinary ear and eye and serve as the causes of hearing sound and seeing visible form.

The Bhāṣya therefore explains `dhyānabhūmikau` genetically:

```text
dhyāna-samāpatti
    + deliberate application to sound/light
    ↓
dhyāna-level material factors
    ↓
refined eye/ear rūpaprasāda
    ↓
extended seeing/hearing
```

Their being called "divine" is tied to this dhyāna-grounded material constitution.

### 2. `sabhāga` means functionally active, not merely corresponding

The Bhāṣya says there is no divine eye or ear that is merely `tatsabhāga`: these faculties are always accompanied by the corresponding vijñāna.

```text
divine eye
    + visual vijñāna

divine ear
    + auditory vijñāna
```

Thus `sabhāga` here should not be weakened into mere structural similarity. The faculties are functionally operative.

### 3. `avikala` means free from ordinary sensory defect

They are not impaired: there is no blindness or sensory distortion of the relevant kind. Vasubandhu compares them to the faculties of beings of the form realm.

The Bhāṣya therefore determines a difference between:

```text
ordinary faculty
    may exist with defect

divine faculty
    exists as an unimpaired operative faculty
```

### 4. Their object-range includes distant and subtle objects

Distant and subtle visible forms and sounds can be their objects. The cited verse contrasts ordinary fleshly sight with divine sight: ordinary sight does not see what is distant, obstructed, or subtle in every direction; divine sight is posited because its range exceeds those ordinary limits.

The important point is extension of lawful range, not abandonment of modality:

```text
divine eye → visible form

divine ear → sound
```

The modality remains typed while the range expands.

### 5. Range varies by bearer and by deliberate exertion

Vasubandhu refuses a single universal radius. The answer to "how far?" is: according to the kind of divine eye one possesses.

Without deliberate intensification (`anabhisaṃskāra`), the ranges are given proportionally for śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, and Buddha. With deliberate intensification (`abhisaṃskāra`), the ranges expand further.

The hierarchy culminates in the Buddha, who sees innumerable world-systems as far as he wishes.

```text
same faculty-type
    ≠ same range

range depends on
    bearer-type
    + exertion/intensification
```

## New Technical Terms

- **śabdāloka-ābhoga-prayoga** — deliberate application toward sound and light in dhyāna
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** genetic explanation of the production of divine hearing and sight

- **cakṣuḥśrotra-sāmantaka** — located adjacent to the ordinary eye and ear
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** material-localization clarification

- **tatsabhāga** — corresponding but not actually functioning as the present operative faculty
  - **status:** previously encountered technical Abhidharma distinction
  - **function:** rejected contrast used to clarify `sabhāga`

- **abhisaṃskāra / anabhisaṃskāra** — deliberate intensification / absence of deliberate intensification
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās
  - **function:** explains variation in perceptual range

## Doctrinal Determination

The divine eye and ear are not disembodied acts of an abstract mind. They are refined material faculties produced on a dhyāna ground and function through corresponding visual and auditory vijñāna.

Their distinctiveness lies in:

```text
ground
    dhyāna

substrate
    refined rūpaprasāda

modality
    sight / hearing

integrity
    unimpaired

range
    distant / subtle / extended

measure
    variable by bearer and exertion
```

This preserves the Abhidharma distinction between faculty, vijñāna, object, ground, and range.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation is broadly correct, but `sabhāga` should be understood here as indicating fully operative correspondence rather than merely structural similarity, because the Bhāṣya explicitly says the divine faculties are always accompanied by their corresponding vijñāna.

## Logical Determination

The passage gives a complete perceptual-capability structure:

```text
cultivated ground
    ↓
material faculty generated
    ↓
modality-specific vijñāna
    ↓
object-domain preserved
    ↓
range expanded
```

And it adds a second rule:

```text
capability-type
    does not fix
capability-range
```

The same kind of faculty may have different reach according to bearer and exertion.

## Organon Contact Point

A perceptual capability should therefore be modeled by at least:

```text
Capability {
    modality
    substrate
    generatingGround
    activeCognitiveOperator
    integrity
    objectType
    range
    bearerConstraint
    intensificationState
}
```

The important systems lesson is that **enhanced access is not an untyped global superpower**. It is a modality-specific faculty whose substrate, operator, range, and scaling law must all be represented separately.
