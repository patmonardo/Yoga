# VAK_7.51 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> svabhūmikena nirmāṇaṃ bhāṣaṇaṃ tvadhareṇa ca /
> nirmātraiva sahāśāstuḥ adhiṣṭhāyānyavarttanāt // VAK_7.51 //

**Best current translation:**  
Construction is produced by a construction-mind of its own level; speech, however, may also be produced by a lower-level mind. For one other than the Teacher, the construction speaks together with the constructor; the Teacher's constructions may speak independently, because after sustaining the construction another mind can function.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.51.md`](./VAK_7.51.md)

### Source cautions

- The e-text reads `vahūnāṃ` / `vahavo`; these are evidently forms for `bahūnāṃ` / `bahavo`, but the source is preserved rather than silently normalized.
- The quoted gāthā at 427|16–17 is transmitted in compressed form and appears to contain minor e-text corruption around the closing quotation mark.
- The Kārikā reading `nirmātraiva sahāśāstuḥ` is syntactically compressed. The Bhāṣya is decisive for interpretation: the simultaneous dependence applies to **one other than the Buddha**, while the Buddha's constructions may speak before or after as desired.

## Natural Argument

The passage develops four linked determinations.

### 1. Construction itself requires same-level causation

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository voice**

The construction of a given level is not produced by a construction-mind belonging to another level:

```text
construction at level L
    requires
construction-mind at level L
```

This is stricter than the preceding rule governing which construction-minds a dhyāna can generate. A higher dhyāna can generate lower-level construction-minds, but once a determinate construction is actually produced, the immediate construction-mind must correspond to the level of the product.

### 2. Speech follows a different rule

The `ca` in `adhareṇa ca` is explicitly explained: speech may be produced either by a mind of the same level or, in higher cases, by a lower-level mind.

For desire-realm and first-dhyāna constructions, speech is produced by a mind of their own level. For constructions belonging to higher dhyānas, speech is produced by a first-dhyāna-grounded mind because above the first dhyāna there is no producer of manifest verbal action (`vijñapti-samutthāpaka`).

Thus:

```text
construction rule
    product level = construction-mind level

speech rule
    same-level where speech is available
    otherwise first-dhyāna-level operation
```

Construction and speech therefore have different causal grammars.

### 3. Non-Buddha constructions are synchronized with the constructor

The Bhāṣya corrects the first-pass interpretation decisively.

For one other than a Buddha:

```text
constructor speaks
    → constructed forms speak

constructor is silent
    → constructed forms are silent
```

If there are many constructed forms, they speak simultaneously. Their verbal operation remains synchronized with the constructor.

The cited verse summarizes this dependence: when one speaks, the constructed forms speak with him; when one is silent, all are silent.

### 4. Buddha constructions can speak independently

For a Buddha, the constructed forms may speak **before or after, as desired** (`pūrvaṃ paścād vā yatheccham`).

This raises an objection.

**Voice: Objector**

If the Buddha is now in a speech-mind, there is no construction-mind. If there is no construction-mind, should not the construction itself cease? How, then, can the construction continue to speak?

**Voice: Vasubandhu / resolution**

The construction is first sustained by `adhiṣṭhāna` with the intention that it remain. Then, with another mind, speech is set in motion:

```text
construct
    ↓
adhiṣṭhāna: sustain the construction
    ↓
another mind becomes current
    ↓
speech operates
```

The crucial point is persistence across a change in current cognitive operation.

## Working Translation of the Bhāṣya

Every constructed form is constructed by a construction-mind belonging to its own level; a construction of one level is not produced by a construction-mind of another level.

Speech, however, may also be produced by a lower-level mind. The word `ca` means that it may be by the same level as well. A construction belonging to the desire realm or first dhyāna speaks through a mind of its own level; one belonging to a higher level speaks through a first-dhyāna-grounded mind, because above that there is no producer of manifest verbal action.

As for the speech of many constructed forms: in one other than a Buddha, the construction speaks together with the constructor. When there are many constructed forms, they speak simultaneously. When the constructor speaks, they speak with him; when he is silent, they are all silent.

But a Buddha's constructed forms may speak before or after, according to his wish.

Objection: when a speech-mind is occurring, there is no construction-mind; without a construction-mind, should there not be no constructed form? How then do they make it speak?

Because, after sustaining the construction with the intention that it remain, they set speech in motion with another mind.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
A. Product-generation constraint
    construction at L
        requires construction-mind at L

B. Speech-generation constraint
    speech-capable construction
        may use same-level mind

    higher construction
        uses first-dhyāna speech-producing mind

C. Non-Buddha temporal relation
    construction speech is synchronized
    with constructor speech

D. Buddha temporal relation
    construction can persist independently
    across a change of active mind

E. Persistence mechanism
    adhiṣṭhāna
        preserves constructed form
        while another mind operates
```

The verse therefore distinguishes three things that the first pass partly collapsed:

```text
production
persistence
operation
```

A thing can be produced by one cognitive operation, sustained through `adhiṣṭhāna`, and then used while another cognitive operation becomes current.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **vijñapti-samutthāpaka** — producer or initiator of manifest bodily/verbal expression.  
  **Status:** previously encountered in the karma material; newly decisive in this local construction context.  
  **Function:** explains why higher-dhyāna constructions require a first-dhyāna-level mind for speech.

- **adhiṣṭhāna** — sustaining determination / establishment by which an already produced construction remains while another mind becomes current.  
  **Status:** present in the Kārikā but substantially clarified by the Bhāṣya.  
  **Function:** persistence mechanism; resolves the objection concerning the absence of construction-mind during speech.

- **avasthānakāmatā** — intention or wish that something remain in place / persist.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only in this passage.  
  **Function:** specifies the intentional condition under which `adhiṣṭhāna` sustains the construction.

- **yugapat** — simultaneously.  
  **Status:** common term, newly significant in the Bhāṣya's description of multiple constructions.  
  **Function:** characterizes synchronized speech of multiple non-Buddha constructions.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.51 distinguishes **level-compatibility**, **functional compatibility**, and **temporal persistence**.

The same-level rule applies to construction itself. Speech is governed by a different constraint because manifest verbal action has its own level conditions. Finally, `adhiṣṭhāna` allows a constructed product to persist after the cognitive operation that produced it is no longer current.

The Buddha/non-Buddha difference is not that only the Buddha can coordinate construction and speech simultaneously. The Bhāṣya says the opposite: non-Buddha constructions are synchronized with their constructor. The Buddha's distinctive mastery is that constructions can speak **independently in temporal order**, before or after the Buddha's own speech, according to intention.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass philosophical translation must be corrected.

It currently says:

> Only in the case of the Teacher does the constructed form speak simultaneously with the constructor.

The Bhāṣya establishes the reverse. A safer translation of the third pāda is:

> For one other than the Teacher, it speaks only together with the constructor.

and the fourth:

> [For the Teacher it can operate otherwise,] because after sustaining it, another [mind] functions.

The compressed Sanskrit allows ambiguity in isolation; the Bhāṣya removes it.

---

## Logical Determination

The deepest logical distinction is:

```text
cause of production
    ≠
condition of persistence
    ≠
current operator
```

The constructed form need not disappear merely because the construction-mind ceases to be current, provided a sustaining condition has been established.

Thus:

```text
Operation A
    produces state S

Adhiṣṭhāna
    stabilizes S

Operation B
    becomes current

S remains available
    while B operates
```

This is much stronger than a simple claim about “concurrency.” It is a theory of **state persistence across operator substitution**.

The Buddha's excellence is correspondingly not raw parallelism but greater control over the temporal independence of generated states.
