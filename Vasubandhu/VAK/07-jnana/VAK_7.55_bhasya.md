# VAK_7.55 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dūrasthamāvṛtaṃ sūkṣmaṃ sarvataśca na paśyati /
> māṃsacakṣuryato rūpamato divyaṃ dṛgiṣyate //
>
> dvitrisāhasrakāsaṃkhyadṛśo 'rhatkhaḍgadaiśikāḥ /
> anyadapyupapattyāptaṃ taddṛśyo nāntarībhavaḥ // VAK_7.55 //

**Best translation:**  
Because the fleshly eye does not see form that is distant, covered, subtle, or in every direction, divine sight is admitted. Arhats, solitary Buddhas, and the Teacher see respectively two-thousandfold, three-thousandfold, and innumerable world-systems. Other extraordinary faculties may also be acquired by birth, but the intermediate being is not visible to such birth-acquired sight.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.55.md`](./VAK_7.55.md)

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The ordinary fleshly eye fails to see form when the object is distant, concealed, subtle, or outside its ordinary directional field. This supplies the contrast by which divine sight is understood.

Vasubandhu then asks how far the divine eye reaches. The answer is not one fixed distance: its range depends upon the kind of divine eye possessed. Without deliberate intensification, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and Buddhas possess progressively wider ranges. With `abhisaṃskāra`, a śrāvaka can see a two-thousandfold world-system, a pratyekabuddha a three-thousandfold world-system, and a Buddha innumerable world-systems as far as he wishes.

The discussion then shifts from quantitative range to acquisition-mode. The question is whether only ṛddhi can be acquired by birth. Vasubandhu answers no: divine hearing and the other members of the corresponding group can also be birth-acquired.

But he immediately imposes a terminological and doctrinal restriction: nothing acquired merely by birth receives the designation `abhijñā`.

This distinction governs the final claim. A birth-acquired divine eye cannot see the intermediate being. `Antarābhava` is visible only to the divine eye that is an actual superknowledge.

## New Technical Terms

- **abhisaṃskāra** — deliberate intensification or directed exertion that extends the range of an already acquired faculty
  - **status:** previously encountered in broader Abhidharma usage; technically operative here
  - **function:** range-modifying condition

- **upapattipratilambhika** — acquired through birth or mode of existence
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** provenance classification

- **abhijñākhyā** — designation as superknowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** terminological and doctrinal restriction

- **antarābhava** — intermediate existence between death and rebirth
  - **status:** previously encountered prominently in Chapter 3
  - **function:** diagnostic object distinguishing birth-acquired vision from abhijñā-vision

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya separates at least four axes that the Kārikā can otherwise tempt us to collapse:

```text
faculty-type
    divine eye

provenance
    cultivation / birth

quantitative range
    bearer-specific and effort-sensitive

epistemic status
    abhijñā or not
```

The decisive point is that a birth-acquired extraordinary faculty is real and may resemble the cultivated faculty phenomenally, yet it is not therefore a superknowledge.

The intermediate being functions as a discriminating case. The birth-acquired divine eye lacks that object-range; the abhijñā-eye possesses it.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass wording "other vision may also be obtained by birth" should be retained only with the qualification supplied by the Bhāṣya:

> Other extraordinary faculties may also be acquired by birth, but no merely birth-acquired faculty is designated an `abhijñā`.

The last pāda therefore means specifically:

> The intermediate being is not visible to the birth-acquired divine eye; it is visible only to the divine eye functioning as superknowledge.

## Logical Determination

```text
same modality
    ≠ same provenance

same provenance
    ≠ same range

same outward capacity
    ≠ same epistemic status
```

More strongly:

```text
CapabilityType
    does not determine
CapabilityStatus
```

A faculty must be classified through both what it can do and the mode by which it was acquired.

The verse also gives a clean distinction between qualitative and quantitative determination:

```text
qualitative type
    divine vision

quantitative scope
    how much it can see

status constraint
    whether it is abhijñā

object exception
    whether antarābhava is admissible
```
