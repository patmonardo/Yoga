# VAK_7.26 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> yadvairāgyāya yallābhas tatra cādhaś ca bhāvyate /
> sāsravāś ca kṣayajñāne labdhapūrvaṃ na bhāvyate // VAK_7.26 //

**Best current translation:**  
Whatever attainment is made for the sake of detachment, cultivation occurs at that level and below. At knowledge of exhaustion, contaminated qualities are also cultivated; what was previously acquired is not cultivated anew.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.26.md`](./VAK_7.26.md)

## Natural Argument

### 1. Which levels are cultivated?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya first separates conventional from uncontaminated knowledge.

For conventional knowledge, future cultivation is tied to:

```text
the level of the path presently practiced
and
the level first attained
```

For uncontaminated knowledge, the rule is wider. It is not restricted merely to the level on which the present path is occurring.

The Kārikā therefore gives the governing rule:

```text
for whichever level detachment is being achieved
and whichever level is attained through that detachment,
uncontaminated knowledges of that level
and of lower levels
are cultivated.
```

### 2. Why does cultivation extend downward?

The newly acquired higher position does not erase lower uncontaminated capacities.

The Bhāṣya therefore makes cultivation range-sensitive:

```text
level being transcended
    ↓
level attained through detachment
    ↓
that level + lower levels
    become cultivation-range
```

The rule concerns the level-distribution of future uncontaminated knowledge, not merely a numerical total.

### 3. Special expansion at knowledge of exhaustion

At `kṣayajñāna`, the range expands again.

All-level contaminated wholesome qualities associated with the acquisition of knowledge of exhaustion are cultivated. Vasubandhu lists examples including:

```text
ānāpānasmṛti
    recollection of breathing

smṛtyupasthāna
    foundations of recollection

apramāṇa
    immeasurables

vimokṣa
    liberations
```

The prose presents these qualities as becoming available when sovereignty over one's own mind is attained.

The important point is that `sāsrava` here does not mean defiled. These are wholesome but contaminated qualities belonging to conditioned practice.

### 4. Acquisition and cultivation are not identical

The Bhāṣya then generalizes:

```text
yat kiñcillabhyate tatsarvaṃ bhāvyate
    whatever is acquired is cultivated

yadapūrvaṃ labhyate tad bhāvyate
    what is acquired for the first time is cultivated
```

But the final pāda introduces a qualification:

```text
labdhapūrvaṃ na bhāvyate
    what had been acquired before is not cultivated
```

Vasubandhu immediately explains what this means:

```text
yadvihīnaṃ punarlabhyate
    what was lost and is then acquired again

na tad bhāvyate
    is not cultivated

bhāvitotsṛṣṭatvāt
    because it had already been cultivated and then relinquished
```

So the issue is not simple present possession versus non-possession. The distinction is between **first acquisition** and **reacquisition of a formerly cultivated capacity**.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

Now, on which path is knowledge of how many levels cultivated? First, as for conventional knowledge: future knowledge is cultivated of the level on which the path occurs and of the level first attained. But uncontaminated knowledge is not limited merely to the level of the path. Rather: whatever attainment occurs for detachment, there and below cultivation takes place. For whichever level detachment is being cultivated, and whichever level is gained through that detachment, uncontaminated knowledges belonging to that level and to lower levels enter cultivation.

And at knowledge of exhaustion, contaminated qualities of all levels associated with the acquisition of knowledge of exhaustion also enter cultivation—such as recollection of breathing, foundations of recollection, immeasurables, liberations, and so forth. With sovereignty over one's own mind attained, all wholesome dharmas come forward through their acquisitions.

Whatever is acquired is cultivated; what is acquired for the first time is cultivated. What was previously acquired is not cultivated. That which had been abandoned and is acquired again is not cultivated, because it had already been cultivated and then relinquished.

### Philosophical Translation

Cultivation is not merely a count of cognitive species; it is distributed across levels and governed by the history of acquisition. Uncontaminated knowledge is cultivated at the level newly secured through detachment and throughout the lower relevant levels. At knowledge of exhaustion, mastery broadens further so that wholesome contaminated capacities across the levels become available. Yet cultivation marks genuine first acquisition: a capacity once cultivated, later lost, and subsequently regained is not counted as newly cultivated.

## New Technical Terms

- **sarvabhūmika** — belonging to all levels
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification
  - **function:** defines the unusually broad range of qualities cultivated at knowledge of exhaustion

- **kṣayajñānalābhika** — associated with / acquired together with knowledge of exhaustion
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only compound in this passage
  - **function:** identifies the contaminated wholesome qualities entering cultivation at the terminal acquisition

- **svacittādhirājya** — sovereignty over one's own mind
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary
  - **function:** explains why the full field of wholesome capacities can come forward at knowledge of exhaustion

- **apūrvalābha** — first acquisition, acquisition not possessed before
  - **status:** made technically operative in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** criterion for cultivation in the strict acquisition sense

- **bhāvitotsṛṣṭa** — previously cultivated and later relinquished
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term
  - **function:** explains why reacquisition is not counted as fresh cultivation

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.26 changes the shape of the cultivation calculus in two ways.

First, it adds a **vertical coordinate**:

```text
which knowledge?
    is no longer sufficient

we must also ask
    at which level?
```

Second, it adds a **historical coordinate**:

```text
first acquisition
    counts as cultivation

reacquisition after loss
    does not count as fresh cultivation
```

The resulting structure is therefore not simply:

```text
path-event → number of knowledges
```

but:

```text
path-event
+ level attained
+ lower included levels
+ contaminated/uncontaminated status
+ acquisition history
    → cultivation determination
```

At `kṣayajñāna`, the system also shows that liberation does not abolish wholesome conventional capacities. Rather, sovereignty of mind allows them to become available in a newly comprehensive way.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The existing translation of the final pāda is too broad if read as:

> anything previously acquired is not cultivated.

The Bhāṣya specifically explains it as:

> what had been acquired before, later lost, and then reacquired is not counted as newly cultivated, because it had already been cultivated and subsequently relinquished.

A stricter philosophical rendering is therefore:

> Whatever attainment is made for detachment, cultivation occurs at that level and below. At knowledge of exhaustion, contaminated wholesome qualities are also cultivated; what is merely reacquired after having been cultivated before is not cultivated anew.

## Logical Determination

The verse supplies a three-dimensional cultivation logic:

```text
TYPE
    conventional / uncontaminated

LEVEL
    present path-level
    newly attained level
    lower included levels

HISTORY
    first acquisition
    prior cultivation
    loss
    reacquisition
```

This is why `bhāvanā` cannot be equated with simple possession.

A dharma may be:

```text
possessed now
previously possessed
lost
reacquired
first acquired
```

and those statuses make a difference to whether the present event counts as cultivation.

The Bhāṣya therefore pushes the calculus from a static matrix toward a state-transition system.

## Organon Contact Point

A useful light contact is the distinction between **state** and **state-history**.

Two practitioners can instantiate the same presently available capacity while differing in whether that capacity is:

```text
newly generated
or
restored after prior possession
```

The present Feature-value alone therefore does not exhaust the determination. Its genesis matters.

This is precisely the kind of case where a future formal representation would require both:

```text
current state
and
provenance / transition history
```

But no external architecture is imposed on the text here; the point arises directly from Vasubandhu's distinction between first acquisition and reacquisition.
