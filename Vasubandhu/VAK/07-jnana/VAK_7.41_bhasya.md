# VAK 7.41 — Bhāṣya Analysis

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 7.41**

```text
tat ṣaḍvidhaṃ sarvabhūmyanulomitam /
vṛddhikāṣṭhāgataṃ tac ca buddhānyasya prayogajāḥ // VAK_7.41 //
```

Working translation:

> That final-edge attainment is sixfold, made conformable to all grounds, and brought to the summit of increase. For one other than a Buddha, these qualities arise through deliberate application.

See: [VAK_7.41.md](./VAK_7.41.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

**Pradhan 420|07–421|04**

```text
prāntakoṭikadhyānavalenaiṣāṃ lābhaḥ /

tatṣaḍivadhaṃ

tadapi prāntakoṭikaṃ caturtha dhyānaṃ ṣaḍātmakam /
araṇāpraṇidhijñānaṃ tisraḥ pratisaṃvidaḥ /
tadeva prantakoṭikam /
niruktipratisaṃvidastadvalena lābho na tu sā caturthadhyānabhūmikā /

kiṃ punaridaṃ prāntakoṭikaṃ nāma /
dhyānamantyaṃ caturthaṃ dhyānam /

sarvabhūmyanulomitam /

vṛddhikāṣṭāgataṃ tacca

kathaṃ sarvabhūmyanulomitam /
kāmāvacarāccittātprathamaṃ dhyānaṃ samāpadyate /
tato dvitīyamevaṃ krameṇa yāvannaivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñāyatanam /
pratilomaṃ punaryāvatkāmāvacaraṃ cittaṃ tataḥ punaranulomaṃ yāvaccaturthadhyānamevaṃ sarvabhūmyanulomitam /

katham vṛddhikāṣṭāgatam /

vṛddhiprakarṣo hi vṛddhikāṣṭā /
idamīdṛśaṃ prāntakoṭikaṃ pragatā 'nta koṭirasyeti kṛtvā /
koṭiḥ punaratra vṛddhiḥ prakāro vā /
catuṣkoṭikavat /

ete punaḥ buddhaguṇāḥ

buddhādanyasya prāyogikā na vairāgyalābhikāḥ /
buddhasya nāsti kiñcit prāyogikam /
tasya sarvadharmeśvaratvādicchāmātrapratibaddhaḥ sarvaguṇasaṃpatsaṃmukhībhāvaḥ /
```

## Source Cautions

The e-text reads `tatṣaḍivadhaṃ`, evidently corresponding to the Kārikā's `tat ṣaḍvidham`; the source is preserved rather than silently normalized.

There is also an apparent compression in the Bhāṣya's enumeration. It calls the final-edge fourth dhyāna `ṣaḍātmakam`, yet explicitly names araṇā, praṇidhijñāna, and three pratisaṃvids before separately explaining that nirukti-pratisaṃvid is obtained through the power of that dhyāna although nirukti itself is not fourth-dhyāna-grounded. The doctrinal point is clear even if the transmitted wording is compact: six qualities depend upon the final-edge cultivation, but nirukti-pratisaṃvid is not itself located on the fourth-dhyāna ground.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The six qualities discussed in 7.36–7.40 are obtained through the power of `prāntakoṭika-dhyāna`.

The final-edge attainment is identified with the fourth dhyāna, cultivated in a special manner. Vasubandhu then defines the two features named by the Kārikā:

1. `sarvabhūmyanulomita` — made conformable to all grounds;
2. `vṛddhikāṣṭhāgata` — brought to the summit of increase.

To become conformable to all grounds, the practitioner enters successively from a desire-domain mind into the first dhyāna, then the second, and so on up through the sphere of neither-perception-nor-nonperception. The sequence is then traversed in reverse back to the desire-domain mind, and again forward as far as the fourth dhyāna. This completed ordered traversal is what makes the fourth dhyāna `sarvabhūmyanulomita`.

To bring it to the summit of increase, cultivation passes from weak to medium and from medium to strong. `Vṛddhikāṣṭhā` is the extremity or highest point of this increase.

It is therefore called `prāntakoṭika`: its terminal limit has been reached. Vasubandhu adds that `koṭi` can mean increase or mode/type, comparing the expression with `catuṣkoṭika`.

The final pāda then distinguishes Buddha from non-Buddha possession. In one other than a Buddha these qualities are `prāyogika`: they arise through deliberate application and are not simply obtained by the acquisition of dispassion. In a Buddha, by contrast, nothing is `prāyogika`. Because of mastery over all dharmas, the manifestation of the complete collection of qualities depends only upon mere intention.


## New Technical Terms

- **prāntakoṭika-dhyāna** — the fourth dhyāna cultivated to the terminal limit required for the six excellences.
  - **status:** introduced as such in this block.
  - **function:** common contemplative basis of the six qualities.

- **sarvabhūmyanulomita** — made conformable to all grounds by ordered traversal through the contemplative levels.
  - **status:** explicit definition supplied by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** describes vertical adaptability acquired through systematic traversal.

- **vṛddhikāṣṭhāgata** — having reached the highest point of increase.
  - **status:** explicit definition supplied by the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** marks maximal intensification of cultivation.

- **mṛdu / madhya / adhimātra** — weak / medium / strong degrees of development.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only specification in this passage.
  - **function:** developmental scale used to define `vṛddhikāṣṭhā`.

- **prāyogika** — produced or activated through deliberate application.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification of `prayogaja`.
  - **function:** characterizes non-Buddha acquisition/operation of these qualities.

- **vairāgyalābhika** — acquired simply through the attainment of dispassion.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only contrast term here.
  - **function:** explicitly denied as the mode of acquisition for these qualities in non-Buddhas.

- **sarvadharmeśvaratva** — mastery or sovereignty over all dharmas.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanation of Buddha-mode here.
  - **function:** grounds the Buddha's non-prāyogika availability of qualities.

- **icchāmātrapratibaddha** — dependent only upon mere intention.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only characterization.
  - **function:** defines the modal immediacy of Buddha activity.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya does not define final-edge perfection as mere static intensity. It has two dimensions:

```text
range-integration
    sarvabhūmyanulomita

intensity-perfection
    vṛddhikāṣṭhāgata
```

The first is achieved by ordered passage through the complete hierarchy of contemplative grounds. The second is achieved by intensifying cultivation through graded development.

Thus:

```text
perfection
    = comprehensive adaptability
      + maximal development
```

The nirukti qualification is important. The six excellences form one dependency-set around final-edge cultivation, but not every member is located on the same contemplative ground. `Nirukti-pratisaṃvid` is obtained through the power of the final-edge fourth dhyāna while itself remaining grounded only where speech is possible.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā-pass phrase “made conformable to all grounds” should not be treated as a loose claim of adaptability. The Bhāṣya gives it a technical procedural meaning: a deliberate ordered traversal of the contemplative hierarchy.

Likewise, “summit of increase” is not simply “fully perfected.” It is defined through the graded ascent:

```text
mṛdu → madhya → adhimātra
```

Finally, the Buddha/non-Buddha distinction should be stated more strongly than “spontaneous availability versus preparation.” Vasubandhu's contrast is:

```text
non-Buddha
    prāyogika
    not vairāgyalābhika

Buddha
    nothing prāyogika
    all excellence becomes present
    dependent only on mere intention
```

## Logical Determination

The verse supplies two independent axes of perfected capacity:

```text
extension across levels
    sarvabhūmyanulomita

intension of development
    vṛddhikāṣṭhāgata
```

A capacity is perfected only when it has both:

```text
systematic range
    +
maximal degree
```

This is stronger than a simple notion of “having a power.” The capacity is determined by its lawful relation to a hierarchy of grounds and by the completed degree of its development.

The Buddha contrast then introduces a modal difference:

```text
mediated activation
    prayoga

immediate availability
    icchāmātra
```

The latter is not groundlessness. It presupposes `sarvadharmeśvaratva`: complete mastery of the field.

## Organon Contact

The most useful Organon contact is not “effort disappears into spontaneity” in the abstract. The Bhāṣya gives a more disciplined architecture:

```text
trained system
    traverses the whole state-space

trained function
    is strengthened through graded development

mastery
    makes the appropriate capability available
    without renewed procedural construction
```

This is close to the distinction between an explicitly invoked procedure and a capability whose mediations have become structurally available to the agent. But the Kośa formulation remains stronger and more specific: Buddha-mode depends upon mastery over all dharmas and is activated by mere intention.
