# VAK 7.03 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> sāṃvṛtaṃ sarvaviṣayaṃ kāmaduḥkhādigocaram /  
> dharmākhyam anvayajñānaṃ tūrdhvaduḥkhādigocaram // VAK_7.03 //

**Best translation:**  
Conventional knowledge has every object. Knowledge whose range is suffering and the other truths in the desire domain is called dharma-knowledge. Subsequent knowledge, however, has suffering and the other truths in the higher domains as its range.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

“Conventional [knowledge] has every object.”

For conventional knowledge, all dharmas, conditioned and unconditioned, can serve as object-supports, according to possibility.

“Having suffering and so forth in the desire domain as its range, it is called dharma-[knowledge].”

For dharma-knowledge, suffering belonging to the desire domain, together with its origination, cessation, and counteragent, are the object-supports.

“Subsequent knowledge, however, has suffering and so forth in the higher domains as its range.”

For subsequent knowledge, suffering belonging to the form and formless domains, together with its origination, cessation, and counteragent, are the object-supports.

### Philosophical Translation

Conventional knowledge is unrestricted with respect to object-kind: conditioned and unconditioned dharmas may all become its object-supports when the relevant conditions permit. Dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge are narrower and path-determinate. Dharma-knowledge takes the Four-Truth structure as it applies to the desire domain; subsequent knowledge takes that same structure as it applies to the form and formless domains.

## Natural Argument

VAK 7.02 established three principal knowledges:

```text
saṃvṛtijñāna
dharmajñāna
anvayajñāna
```

VAK 7.03 now distinguishes them by **object-range**.

The Bhāṣya makes three points.

### 1. Conventional knowledge is all-object

Vasubandhu expands `sarvaviṣayam` by saying:

> **sarvadharmāḥ saṃskṛtāsaṃskṛtā ālambanaṃ saṃbhavataḥ**

All conditioned and unconditioned dharmas can, when possible, become the object-support of conventional knowledge.

Thus `saṃvṛtijñāna` is not defined through a narrow empirical object class. Its range is extensionally broad.

### 2. Dharma-knowledge is determined by the desire domain

The Bhāṣya specifies the abbreviated `duḥkhādi`:

```text
duḥkha
samudaya
nirodha
pratipakṣa
```

Here `pratipakṣa` corresponds to the Path as the counteragent to suffering and its origination.

Dharma-knowledge therefore takes as object-support the Four-Truth structure insofar as it belongs to the desire domain.

### 3. Subsequent knowledge extends the same truth-structure upward

For `anvayajñāna`, the same structure is applied to:

```text
rūpāvacara
ārūpyāvacara
```

The difference between dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge is therefore not a different truth-content, but a different **domain-range**.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃbhavataḥ** — according to possibility / where possible
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification in this unit
  - **function:** prevents `sarvaviṣaya` from being read as simultaneous or indiscriminate cognition of literally every dharma

- **ālambana** — object-support
  - **status:** established project vocabulary
  - **function:** specifies the relation between a knowledge and what it cognitively takes up

- **pratipakṣa** — counteragent
  - **status:** established Path terminology
  - **function:** here supplies the fourth member corresponding to `mārga` within the Four-Truth structure

- **kāmāvacara / rūpāvacara / ārūpyāvacara** — belonging respectively to the desire, form, and formless domains
  - **status:** established realm vocabulary
  - **function:** fixes the object-domain difference between dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya confirms the nested structure already established in VAK 7.02:

```text
jñāna
    ├── saṃvṛtijñāna
    │       → all conditioned and unconditioned dharmas, where possible
    │
    └── anāsrava-jñāna
            ├── dharmajñāna
            │       → Four Truths in the desire domain
            │
            └── anvayajñāna
                    → Four Truths in the form and formless domains
```

The key distinction is therefore:

```text
same truth-structure
    +
different domain
        ↓
different uncontaminated knowledge
```

The higher domains do not generate a different set of truths. They generate the corresponding higher-domain application of the same truth-order.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing Kārikā translation is structurally correct.

The Bhāṣya adds two useful precisions:

1. `sarvaviṣaya` includes both conditioned and unconditioned dharmas, **according to possibility**;
2. `duḥkhādi` is explicitly unfolded as suffering, origination, cessation, and the counteragent/Path.

The working rendering of `anvayajñāna` should remain provisional. This verse confirms its homologous higher-domain function but does not yet settle the best English equivalent.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.03 distinguishes **extension** from **determinate path-range**.

```text
saṃvṛtijñāna
    maximum object-extension

anāsrava-jñāna
    exact division of one truth-structure by domain
```

The important relation is not:

```text
many empirical objects
    → many kinds of knowledge
```

but rather:

```text
one truth-form
    ×
complete domain opposition
        ↓
two path-knowledges
```

The form and formless domains are gathered together as the `ūrdhva`, the higher side, opposite the desire domain. Hence the primary division remains dyadic despite the empirical existence of three realms.

## Organon Contact Point

The passage supplies a restrained example of knowledge being individuated not merely by content but by the **relation of a common form to a determinate domain**.

```text
common form
    Four Truths

relative domains
    kāma / ūrdhva

result
    dharmajñāna / anvayajñāna
```

This is useful for later Organon work because the universal form remains invariant while its application to opposed domains generates distinct determinate knowledges.

No stronger Kantian or Hegelian identification is required here.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.03 assigns object-ranges to the three knowledges introduced in 7.02. Conventional knowledge can take any conditioned or unconditioned dharma as object-support where possible. Dharma-knowledge takes suffering, origination, cessation, and their counteragent as they belong to the desire domain. Subsequent knowledge takes the corresponding Four-Truth structure in the form and formless domains. The distinction between the two uncontaminated knowledges is therefore a distinction of domain within one invariant truth-structure.
