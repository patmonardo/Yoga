# VAK 7.43 Bhāṣya — Cognitive Composition and Dhyānic Ground of the Abhijñās

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 7.43**

```text
catasraḥ saṃvṛtijñānaṃ cetasi jñānapañcakam /
kṣayābhijñā balaṃ yadvat pañca dhyānacatuṣṭaye // VAK_7.43 //
```

**Working translation:** Four are conventional knowledge. In the case of mind there is a set of five knowledges. The direct knowledge of exhaustion is as with the corresponding Buddha-power. The remaining five belong to the four dhyānas.

The section after the verse explains why the first five abhijñās are restricted to the four dhyānas and belongs naturally with VAK 7.43. ## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu.**

The verse first classifies the six abhijñās by cognitive constitution.

Four are `saṃvṛtijñāna`: all except the knowledge of another mind and the knowledge of exhaustion. The other-mind abhijñā is constituted by five knowledges: `dharmajñāna`, `anvayajñāna`, `mārgajñāna`, `saṃvṛtijñāna`, and `paracittajñāna`.

The exhaustion abhijñā is to be understood exactly as the corresponding Buddha-power concerning exhaustion was previously explained: according to the relevant person it consists of either six or ten knowledges, and its range across grounds is likewise understood by reference to that earlier account.

The remaining five abhijñās belong to the four dhyānas. Vasubandhu then asks why they do not occur on the formless levels.

Three are excluded immediately because their object-support is form. Other-mind knowledge is also excluded from the formless levels in its genesis because it is produced by proceeding through a form-based sign. One who wishes to know another mind first observes the relation between one's own bodily condition and one's own mind, then applies that relation to others. Once the abhijñā has been produced, however, the cognition can function without depending on form in the same way.

Former-life recollection is likewise generated through a sequential reconstruction. One begins from the immediately ceased mental knowledge, takes up a sign, and proceeds backward through successive states as far as the linking mind and the intermediate existence. Once the abhijñā has been produced, the practitioner can recollect by skipping intermediate stages. The recollection is nevertheless of what has in some sense been previously experienced; the Pure Abodes can be recalled through prior experience by hearing.

Vasubandhu adds a technical qualification for one reborn here after falling from a formless existence: the production of the recollection may depend on another continuum, while in other cases it depends on one's own continuum.

Finally, the preparatory applications for the first powers are named schematically: attention to lightness, sound, and light for `ṛddhi` and the related abhijñās.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
six abhijñās
    ↓
classified by cognitive constitution

four
    → saṃvṛtijñāna

cetaḥparyāya
    → five knowledges

āsravakṣaya
    → six or ten knowledges
      as in the corresponding Buddha-power

remaining five
    → grounded in the four dhyānas
        ↓
    why not formless levels?
        ↓
    their genesis depends upon form,
    form-signs, or form-mediated sequences
```

The crucial distinction is between **genesis** and **completed operation**:

```text
form-dependent production
    ≠
permanent dependence on form
```

Other-mind knowledge is generated through a body–mind sign relation, but once established it can know without renewed dependence on that sign. Former-life recollection is generated through ordered backward reconstruction, but once established it can leap across intermediate stages.

## New Technical Terms

- **rūpatīrtha** — provisional meaning: a form-based route, ford, or point of access through which a cognition is initially produced. **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific technical expression in this passage. **Function:** explains why other-mind knowledge is not generated on formless levels.
- **nimitta-udgrahaṇa** — taking up a sign. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary. **Function:** identifies the mediating sign used in generating other-mind knowledge and former-life recollection.
- **anupūrvāvasthāntara** — successive intermediate states in sequence. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary. **Function:** explains the initial backward reconstruction used in former-life recollection.
- **vilaṅghya-smaraṇa** — recollection by skipping over intermediate stages. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary. **Function:** distinguishes the completed power from its sequential mode of acquisition.
- **parasaṃtatyadhiṣṭhāna** — dependence upon another continuum. **Status:** specialized Bhāṣya qualification. **Function:** accounts for recollection when one's own preceding continuum does not supply the required form-based sequence.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 7.43 does not merely classify the abhijñās by object. It distinguishes three layers:

```text
object-field
cognitive constitution
generative basis
```

This matters because an extraordinary capacity may have a simple name while being internally composed of several knowledges and requiring a specific developmental route.

The five dhyāna-grounded abhijñās are therefore not free-floating supernatural faculties. Their acquisition is conditioned by determinate contemplative and cognitive mediations.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation remains broadly sound, with two refinements.

First, `catasraḥ saṃvṛtijñānam` refers to four of the six after excluding `cetaḥparyāya` and `āsravakṣaya`; it should not be read as identifying the first four merely by sequence.

Second, `pañca dhyānacatuṣṭaye` is best understood as the five remaining non-exhaustion abhijñās being grounded in the four dhyānas. The Bhāṣya makes the reason explicit through their modes of production.

## Logical Determination

The most useful logical distinction is:

```text
capacity as achieved function
    ≠
procedure by which capacity is generated
```

A cognition can require a mediating sign in its genesis and later operate without that sign.

```text
mediated acquisition
    → integrated competence
    → less mediated execution
```

This is not disappearance of mediation but its internalization.

## Organon Contact

VAK 7.43 provides a strong model for disciplined feature acquisition.

A mature capability should not be characterized only by its output. We must distinguish:

```text
feature/capability
    object domain
    internal operators
    acquisition procedure
    runtime dependencies
```

The Bhāṣya's distinction between form-dependent **training** and less form-dependent **execution** is especially useful. A capability may be learned through explicit observable correlations and later become available as an integrated operation without replaying the full acquisition path.

This is a restrained Organon contact rather than an identification: Vasubandhu is describing cultivated cognitive powers, but the structural distinction between genesis and achieved operation is exact and philosophically important.
