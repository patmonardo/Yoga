# VAK_7.50 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> yathākramaṃ dhyānaphalaṃ dve yāvat pañca nordhvajam /
> tallābho dhyānavat śuddhāttatsvataśca tato 'pi te // VAK_7.50 //

**Best current translation:**  
In sequence, the construction-minds are products of the dhyānas—two, then three, four, and five; none is generated with respect to a higher level. Their acquisition is like that of dhyāna. A construction-mind arises from pure dhyāna and from itself; and from it, in turn, pure dhyāna and another construction-mind arise.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.50.md`](./VAK_7.50.md)

## Source Cautions

The e-text reads `śuddhakāddhacyānād` at 427|02, evidently containing a source-level corruption or typographical intrusion around `dhyānād`. It is preserved here rather than silently normalized.

The phrase `dvitīyādidhyānaphalaṃ kāmāvacaraṃ nirmāṇaṃ prathamadhyānabhūmikādgatito viśiṣyate` is compressed. Its clear doctrinal point is that desire-realm constructions produced from progressively higher dhyānas differ in excellence; the exact nuance of `gatitaḥ` should remain provisional.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The fourteen construction-minds are first generated as a determinate series from the four dhyānas:

```text
first dhyāna   → 2
second dhyāna  → 3
third dhyāna   → 4
fourth dhyāna  → 5
                  —
                  14
```

The first dhyāna produces construction-minds belonging to the desire domain and first dhyāna. The second adds a second-dhyāna construction-mind; the third and fourth extend the same pattern.

Vasubandhu states the general rule explicitly:

```text
svabhūmika
    own-level

adharabhūmika
    lower-level
```

A dhyāna produces construction-minds of its own level or a lower one.

It does not produce a higher-level construction-mind:

```text
na ūrdhvajam
```

He then turns from the production-range of the operators to their acquisition and transition rules. Their acquisition is like acquisition of dhyāna itself.

An objection or question is introduced:

**Voice: Objector**

Can one emerge directly from a construction-mind?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Not in an unrestricted way. A construction-mind arises immediately after either:

```text
pure dhyāna
or
another construction-mind
```

and after a construction-mind there may arise only:

```text
pure dhyāna
or
another construction-mind
```

No arbitrary state is admitted in either direction.

The final sentence gives the reason: one who is standing in a result of samādhi does not emerge from it without first entering samādhi again.

## Working Translation of the Prose

Those fourteen are the construction-minds. In sequence, the products of the dhyānas are two, up to five. The first dhyāna has as its results two construction-minds, belonging to the desire domain and the first-dhyāna level. The second dhyāna has three as its results, belonging to the desire domain and to the first and second dhyānas. In the same manner four and five are to be assigned to the third and fourth dhyānas. A construction-mind belonging to one's own level or to a lower level is to be understood as a product of dhyāna.

There is none born upward: a lower dhyāna has no higher-level construction-mind as its result. A desire-domain construction that is the result of the second and following dhyānas is distinguished in excellence from one belonging to the first-dhyāna level.

Their acquisition is like that of dhyāna.

Could there then be emergence directly from a construction-mind? This is not so. A construction-mind arises immediately after pure dhyāna or after a construction-mind, and not after anything else. From a construction-mind likewise either pure dhyāna or another construction-mind arises, and nothing else. For one who is established in a result of samādhi cannot emerge from it without first entering samādhi again.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya separates three questions that should not be collapsed.

### 1. Which construction-minds can a dhyāna generate?

```text
source ground
    → own level
    → lower levels
    × higher level
```

This yields the fourteenfold triangular distribution.

### 2. How are construction-minds acquired?

```text
acquisition of nirmāṇa-citta
    follows the rule of dhyāna acquisition
```

The verse does not treat them as spontaneous free-standing powers.

### 3. Which states may immediately precede or follow a construction-mind?

```text
śuddhaka-dhyāna
      ↘
       nirmāṇa-citta
      ↗        ↓
śuddhaka-dhyāna  nirmāṇa-citta
```

More exactly:

```text
Allowed predecessors of nirmāṇa-citta:
    śuddhaka-dhyāna
    nirmāṇa-citta

Allowed successors of nirmāṇa-citta:
    śuddhaka-dhyāna
    nirmāṇa-citta
```

No other immediate transition is admitted here.

## New Technical Terms

- **ūrdhvaja** — generated with respect to a higher level
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās
  - **function:** marks a prohibited direction of generation

- **adharabhūmika** — belonging to a lower level
  - **status:** previously encountered as a structural distinction
  - **function:** defines the admissible downward range of a dhyāna-product

- **śuddhaka-dhyāna** — pure dhyāna
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** immediate predecessor and possible successor of construction-mind

- **vyutthāna** — emergence from or exit out of a meditative/result state
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** transition-state question

- **samādhiphalastha** — one established in a result of samādhi
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** supplies the causal rationale for the transition restriction

## Doctrinal Determination

The fourteen construction-minds are not merely an enumeration of powers. They form a level-indexed result-system of the four dhyānas.

```text
higher source dhyāna
    permits a wider downward result-range

lower source dhyāna
    cannot generate upward
```

The hierarchy therefore constrains both generation and operation.

The Bhāṣya also makes clear that the construction-mind is a specialized result-state embedded in the meditative system. It is neither identical with dhyāna nor detachable from it.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass rendering of `na ūrdhvajam` as “none is generated upward” is basically correct, but the Bhāṣya gives it a precise relational meaning:

> No lower dhyāna has a higher-level construction-mind as its result.

The final pāda should likewise be read as a transition rule, not merely as a statement that a construction-mind “can reproduce itself.” What is established is a constrained immediate-successor relation:

```text
nirmāṇa-citta
    → śuddhaka-dhyāna
    → or nirmāṇa-citta
```

and no other immediate successor.

## Logical Determination

The strongest logical result is the distinction among:

```text
capability ground
result/operator
admissible target-level
state-transition relation
```

The system is therefore not adequately modeled by a single capability predicate.

A more exact structure is:

```text
Generate(sourceGround, operatorLevel)
    permitted iff operatorLevel ≤ sourceGround

Transition(previousState, nirmāṇa-citta)
    permitted iff previousState ∈ {śuddhaka-dhyāna, nirmāṇa-citta}

Transition(nirmāṇa-citta, nextState)
    permitted iff nextState ∈ {śuddhaka-dhyāna, nirmāṇa-citta}
```

This is a finite transition grammar rather than an unstructured store of powers.

## Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya offers a particularly clean model for typed capability execution.

A capability should record at least:

```text
source ground
operator type
admissible target range
acquisition condition
allowed predecessor states
allowed successor states
```

This gives us a stronger notion than “feature present.” A Feature may be available only within a lawful transition graph.

For current FeatStruct work, the useful principle is:

> A capacity is not only a property of an agent; it is a state-indexed operation whose legality depends upon provenance, level, and transition context.

That contact is close to the text because Vasubandhu himself explicitly distinguishes ground, result, level, acquisition, and state transition.

## Bridge to VAK 7.51

VAK 7.50 determines how the construction-minds are generated and sequenced. VAK 7.51 now turns to the construction itself:

```text
svabhūmikena nirmāṇaṃ
    construction by a construction-mind of the same level

bhāṣaṇaṃ tv adharena ca
    speech may also occur through a lower-level one
```

The next verse therefore moves from the transition grammar of the operator to the level-matching rules governing its product and speech.
