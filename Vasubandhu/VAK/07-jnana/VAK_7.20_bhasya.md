# VAK_7.20 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> yathotpannāni bhāvyante kṣāntijñānāni darśane /
> anāgatāni tatraiva sāṃvṛtaṃ cānvayatraye // VAK_7.20 //

**Best current translation:**  
In the Path of Seeing, future receptivities and knowledges are cultivated according as they arise; and precisely there, at the three inferential knowledges, conventional knowledge is also cultivated.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.20.md`](./VAK_7.20.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 405|18–406|06, with the following discussion continuing directly into VAK 7.21.

```text
atha kasyāmavasthāyāṃ kati jñānāni bhavyante /

yathotpannāni bhāvyante kṣāntijñānāni darśane /
anāgatāni

darśanamārge yadyadevotpadyate kṣāntirjñānaṃ vā tajjātīyamanāgataṃ bhāvanāṃ gacchati tadākārā eva catvāraḥ /
kasmāddarśanamārge sabhāgajñānākārabhāvanaiva gotrāṇāmaporatilabdhatvāt /

tatraiva sāṃvṛtaṃ cānvayatraye // VAkK_7.20 //

tatraiva darśanamārge saṃvṛtijñānaṃ cāpi bhāvyate triṣu duḥkhasamudayanirodhānvayajñāneṣu /
na dharmajñāneṣvakṛtsnasatyābhisamayāt /
```

### Source caution

The e-text reads:

```text
gotrāṇāmaporatilabdhatvāt
```

The form appears textually uncertain or corrupt. The argument is nevertheless clear from the surrounding sentence: in the Path of Seeing cultivation is restricted to cognition and aspects of the same kind. No silent emendation is made here.

## Natural Argument

### 1. A new question: cultivation rather than possession

**Voice: Vasubandhu / question-form**

VAK 7.19 determined how many knowledges are **possessed** (`samanvāgata`) by the practitioner at successive moments.

Vasubandhu now changes the question:

```text
atha kasyām avasthāyāṃ kati jñānāni bhavyante
    At what stage are how many knowledges cultivated?
```

This is not a restatement of possession.

The distinction is now:

```text
samanvāgama
    possession in the continuum

bhāvanā
    cultivation / development
```

### 2. Cultivation follows the cognition actually arisen

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

In the Path of Seeing, whatever receptivity (`kṣānti`) or knowledge (`jñāna`) arises, a future cognition **of that same kind** (`tajjātīya`) enters cultivation.

```text
present arising
    kṣānti or jñāna

        ↓ bhāvanā

future cognition
    of the same kind
```

The Bhāṣya is more precise than a loose reading of `yathotpannāni` would suggest. It does not merely say that cultivation follows the temporal sequence. It says that cultivation is **species-matched** to what has actually arisen.

### 3. The aspects are likewise restricted

The restriction extends not only to the kind of cognition but also to its aspects:

```text
tadākārā eva catvāraḥ
    precisely its four aspects
```

Thus cultivation in the Path of Seeing does not yet indiscriminately develop every possible knowledge and every possible aspect.

For each truth, the relevant four aspects belonging to that truth are cultivated with the corresponding cognition.

The architecture is therefore:

```text
cognitive kind
    determinate

aspect-set
    determinate

future cultivation
    homologous to both
```

### 4. Why only same-kind cultivation?

**Voice: question-form**

Why, in the Path of Seeing, is there cultivation only of knowledge and aspects of the same kind?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The source gives the reason through the not-yet-complete acquisition of the relevant `gotra` classes. Because the e-text is uncertain at precisely this phrase, the safest determination is structural rather than philologically overconfident:

> the Path of Seeing is still genetically acquiring the distinct cognitive classes; therefore cultivation remains bound to the class and aspects presently actualized.

What is not yet warranted is a translation that treats `bhāvanā` here as unrestricted exercise of the entire ten-knowledge system.

### 5. A special addition: conventional knowledge

The second half gives an exception/addition.

Within that same Path of Seeing, conventional knowledge (`saṃvṛtijñāna`) is also cultivated at three `anvayajñāna` moments:

```text
duḥkhānvayajñāna
    inferential / corresponding knowledge of suffering

samudayānvayajñāna
    inferential / corresponding knowledge of origin

nirodhānvayajñāna
    inferential / corresponding knowledge of extinction
```

The fourth `anvayajñāna`, concerning the Path, is excluded here and explained in VAK 7.21.

### 6. Why not at the dharma-knowledge moments?

Vasubandhu explicitly answers this part already in the present passage:

```text
na dharmajñāneṣv akṛtsnasatyābhisamayāt
```

Conventional knowledge is **not** cultivated at the corresponding `dharmajñāna` moments because the truth has not yet been comprehended completely.

The structure of the Path of Seeing explains this.

At a `dharmajñāna` moment the truth has been realized with respect to the desire domain. The corresponding `anvayajñāna` extends realization to the higher domains. Only then is the comprehension of that truth complete across the three domains.

Thus:

```text
dharmajñāna
    partial domain-completion of the truth

anvayajñāna
    completion across the higher domains

        ↓

kṛtsna-satya-abhisamaya
    complete comprehension of that truth

        ↓

cultivation of the corresponding saṃvṛtijñāna
```

This makes the threefold conventional cultivation much less mysterious. It occurs at a **completion-point** in the realization of each of the first three truths.

## Argument Reconstruction

The movement from VAK 7.19 to VAK 7.20 is exact:

```text
VAK 7.19
What knowledge-classes are possessed?
    ↓

VAK 7.20
What future cognitive determinations are cultivated?
    ↓

same-kind cultivation
    cognition ↔ future cognition of same species
    aspect ↔ same four aspects
    ↓

special conventional cultivation
    at completion of suffering
    at completion of origin
    at completion of extinction
```

The Path of Seeing therefore does two things simultaneously:

```text
actualizes a new pure cognition
and
establishes homologous future cognitive capacity
```

At certain completion points it additionally establishes a conventional cognition corresponding to what has now been completely comprehended.

## New Technical Terms

- **tajjātīya** — of that same kind / belonging to the same cognitive species
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification not explicit in the Kārikā
  - **function:** restricts future cultivation to the kind of receptivity or knowledge presently arisen

- **sabhāgajñānākārabhāvanā** — cultivation of knowledge and aspects homogeneous with the present cognition
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reasoning vocabulary
  - **function:** states the governing restriction on cultivation in the Path of Seeing

- **gotra** — class, lineage, cognitive species/type in the present classificatory context
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term; exact local syntax affected by an uncertain e-text reading
  - **function:** grounds why cultivation remains type-restricted during Seeing

- **kṛtsna-satya-abhisamaya** — complete comprehension/direct realization of a truth
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression in this passage
  - **function:** explains why conventional knowledge is cultivated at `anvayajñāna`, not at the preceding `dharmajñāna`

- **akṛtsna-satya-abhisamaya** — incomplete comprehension of a truth
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression
  - **function:** excludes conventional cultivation at the dharma-knowledge moments

## Doctrinal Determination

### Bhāvanā is not simple repetition

The Bhāṣya forces a stricter account of `bhāvanā` than “meditative repetition.”

At these sixteen rapid moments of Seeing, cultivation means that the presently actualized pure cognition establishes a future cognition of the same determinate kind.

```text
utpāda
    actual arising

bhāvanā
    acquisition/development of homologous future capacity
```

The cultivation relation is therefore genetic.

### Cultivation is initially constrained

The Path of Seeing is not yet the free cultivation of every knowledge through every aspect.

It is constrained by what has actually been generated:

```text
what has arisen
    governs
what can now be cultivated
```

This is an important control on the first-pass language of “future availability.” The availability is not generic; it is **typed and aspect-determined**.

### Conventional knowledge follows completion

The special `saṃvṛtijñāna` is not presented as the source of pure realization.

Rather:

```text
pure realization of a truth
    reaches completion

        ↓

corresponding conventional knowledge
    is cultivated
```

This preserves the priority of direct noble realization while explaining how a conventional cognitive determination can be generated within its wake.

## Translation Consequences

The existing Kārikā translation is basically sound, but the Bhāṣya permits a more exact philosophical expansion:

> In the Path of Seeing, as each receptivity or knowledge arises, future cognition of that same kind and with those same aspects is cultivated; and there, at the three inferential knowledges completing suffering, origin, and extinction, conventional knowledge is also cultivated.

Two cautions should be retained:

1. `anvayajñāna` remains a difficult canonical rendering; “inferential knowledge” is usable provisionally but should not obscure its function as the cognition extending the truth-determination to the higher domains.
2. `bhāvanā` here should not be flattened into generic meditation or repetition.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.20 gives a highly determinate account of **genetic conservation**.

The new cognition does not merely occur and vanish.

Its actuality determines a corresponding future possibility:

```text
actual A
    ↓ cultivation
future A-capacity
```

But the relation is not abstract reproduction.

The future is constrained by the exact determination of the present:

```text
same cognitive species
same aspect-structure
```

This yields a stronger principle than the first-pass idea of simple accumulation:

> the Path reproduces its determinations according to their own form.

The second movement is equally important. Conventional cognition is generated only after a truth has reached a determinate completion in direct realization.

```text
partial realization
    no corresponding conventional cultivation

complete realization
    corresponding conventional cultivation
```

So the conventional determination is not externally attached to pure knowing. It is genetically conditioned by the completion of pure knowing.

## Organon Contact Point

Light contact only:

VAK 7.20 offers a useful model for a science in which an achieved determination becomes a **power of further determination without losing its form**.

```text
actual determination
    ↓
reproduction of the same determination as capacity
```

This is closer to a genuine genetic theory of learning than a model in which information is merely stored after an event.

The striking second movement is:

```text
direct determination of truth
    ↓ completion
conventional determination of that truth becomes available
```

For the Organon, this suggests a disciplined distinction between:

```text
truth-generating act
and
subsequent scientific availability of the truth
```

The Bhāṣya itself does not identify this with Absolute Knowledge, and we should not force that conclusion. Its contribution here is more precise: **the form actually achieved governs the form that can be cultivated from it.**
