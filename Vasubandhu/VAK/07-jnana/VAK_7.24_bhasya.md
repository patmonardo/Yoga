# VAK_7.24 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> śaikṣottāpanamuktau vā ṣaṭ saptajñānabhāvanā /
> ānantaryapathe ṣaṇṇāṃ bhavāgravijaye tathā // VAK_7.24 //

**Best current translation:**  
In the liberation path belonging to the sharpening of a trainee's faculties, six or seven knowledges are cultivated. In the uninterrupted path, six are cultivated; likewise in the conquest of the peak of existence.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.24.md`](./VAK_7.24.md)

## Natural Argument

### 1. Liberation path of sharpening the trainee's faculties

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

When a `śaikṣa`, a trainee, intensifies or sharpens the faculties (`indriyottāpanā`), the liberation path cultivates different numbers according to prior detachment.

For one still possessing desire:

```text
6 knowledges
    dharmajñāna
    anvayajñāna
    duḥkhajñāna
    samudayajñāna
    nirodhajñāna
    mārgajñāna
```

For one already detached from desire:

```text
the same 6
    +
paracittajñāna
        knowledge of another's mind

= 7
```

Thus the standard six/seven distinction depends upon the practitioner's attained state.

### 2. A doctrinal alternative signaled by `vā`

**Voice: Alternative teachers / uncertain school (`kecit`)**

Some hold that conventional knowledge (`saṃvṛtijñāna`) is also cultivated for both practitioners.

The Bhāṣya explicitly explains:

```text
matavikalpajñāpanārthaḥ vāśabdaḥ
    the word vā serves to indicate a difference of opinion
```

So `vā` is not merely loose metrical coordination. It marks a doctrinal alternative concerning the cultivation count.

This is an important Bhāṣya correction to a purely mechanical reading of the verse.

### 3. Preparatory path

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

In the preparatory path (`prayogamārga`), conventional knowledge is cultivated for both the attached and detached trainee.

Thus the exclusion or inclusion of `saṃvṛtijñāna` is path-phase dependent.

### 4. Uninterrupted path of sharpening the faculties

Whether the trainee is detached or not detached, six knowledges are cultivated in the uninterrupted path (`ānantaryamārga`), as previously.

Knowledge of another's mind is excluded.

Vasubandhu immediately asks why.

**Voice: Question / Objector**

Why is `paracittajñāna` prohibited in the uninterrupted path?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Because it is not a counteragent (`apratipakṣatvāt`).

The rule is generalized:

```text
paracittajñāna
    is excluded from all uninterrupted paths
```

because it does not directly oppose the obstruction being abandoned.

### 5. Conquest of the peak of existence

The same sixfold cultivation applies in the uninterrupted paths belonging to detachment from `bhavāgra`, the peak of existence.

The governing rule remains counteractive adequacy rather than mere possession of a knowledge.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 7.24 refines the cultivation matrix by introducing three variables:

```text
practitioner status
    attached / detached

path phase
    preparatory / uninterrupted / liberation

functional eligibility
    counteragent / non-counteragent
```

The count follows from their intersection.

| Setting | Standard cultivation |
|---|---|
| liberation path, attached trainee | six |
| liberation path, detached trainee | seven, adding `paracittajñāna` |
| liberation path, alternative view | `saṃvṛtijñāna` also cultivated for both |
| preparatory path | `saṃvṛtijñāna` cultivated for both |
| uninterrupted path, either trainee | six |
| uninterrupted paths conquering `bhavāgra` | six |

The verse is therefore not merely a numerical list. It encodes a rule-governed relation among path-function, prior attainment, and cognitive eligibility.

## Segmentation and Grammar

### śaikṣottāpana

```text
śaikṣa
    trainee

uttāpana
    intensification / sharpening

śaikṣottāpana
    sharpening of the trainee's faculties
```

The Bhāṣya expands this explicitly as `śaikṣasyendriyottāpanāyām`.

### vā

Here `vā` has doctrinal force.

The Bhāṣya states that it indicates `matavikalpa`, a difference or alternative of opinion.

### prakṣipya

```text
paracittajñānaṃ prakṣipya
    adding / including knowledge of another's mind
```

This produces the seventh knowledge for the detached trainee in the standard account.

### pratipakṣa

```text
pratipakṣa
    counteragent
    direct oppositional factor
```

This becomes the decisive functional criterion for inclusion in uninterrupted-path cultivation.

## Bhāṣya Translation

### Literal Translation

In the liberation path belonging to the sharpening of the faculties of a trainee, six knowledges are cultivated for one with desire: knowledge of dharmas, inferential knowledge, and the knowledges of suffering, origin, extinction, and the Path. For one detached from desire, seven are cultivated, knowledge of another's mind being added.

Some say that conventional knowledge also is cultivated for both. Here the word `vā` serves to indicate the difference of opinion.

In the preparatory path, however, conventional knowledge also is cultivated for both of them.

In the uninterrupted path belonging to the sharpening of the faculties of a trainee, whether detached or not detached, six are cultivated as before. Knowledge of another's mind is not cultivated, because it is prohibited in all uninterrupted paths. Why is it prohibited? Because it is not a counteragent.

Likewise, in the uninterrupted paths belonging to detachment from the peak of existence, six knowledges are cultivated in exactly the same way.

### Philosophical Translation

Sharpening the faculties does not produce one invariant field of cultivation. What can be cultivated depends upon the practitioner's prior attainment and upon the function of the path-phase itself. The liberation path may widen the cultivated field, while the uninterrupted path is restricted to knowledges capable of directly counteracting the obstruction being removed. The Bhāṣya even records an alternative opinion concerning conventional knowledge, and explains the verse's `vā` as marking that disagreement.

## New Technical Terms

- **indriyottāpanā** — sharpening or intensification of the faculties
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the Kārikā's compressed `śaikṣottāpana`
  - **function:** names the process whose preparatory, uninterrupted, and liberation paths are being distinguished

- **matavikalpa** — difference or alternative of doctrinal opinion
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this passage
  - **function:** explains the force of `vā` and records disagreement over cultivation of conventional knowledge

- **prakṣipya** — adding, including
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory verb
  - **function:** specifies how `paracittajñāna` produces the seventh knowledge for the detached trainee

- **pratipakṣatva / apratipakṣatva** — being / not being a counteragent
  - **status:** recurring functional vocabulary, decisive here
  - **function:** determines eligibility for cultivation in the uninterrupted path

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya makes clear that cultivation cannot be inferred merely from possession.

A practitioner may possess a knowledge which is nevertheless excluded from a particular path-phase because that knowledge lacks the required counteractive function.

Thus:

```text
possession
    does not entail
cultivation in every path-phase
```

`paracittajñāna` is the decisive example. It may belong to the detached practitioner, yet it is excluded from every uninterrupted path because it does not oppose the obstruction being abandoned.

The treatment of conventional knowledge adds another distinction. Its eligibility varies according to path-phase and, according to `kecit`, even according to doctrinal interpretation.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only, with an important commentary note.**

The existing translation is serviceable, but the Bhāṣya requires the reader to understand that:

1. the standard six/seven distinction concerns attached versus detached trainees in the liberation path;
2. `vā` also signals a doctrinal alternative in which conventional knowledge is cultivated for both;
3. conventional knowledge is explicitly cultivated for both in the preparatory path;
4. uninterrupted-path cultivation is controlled by counteractive function.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.24 exposes a functional rather than merely taxonomic logic of knowledge.

```text
knowledge species
    + practitioner status
    + path phase
    + counteractive function
        ↓
    cultivation eligibility
```

The decisive relation is not simply:

```text
practitioner possesses X
therefore X is cultivated
```

but:

```text
X is available
and
X performs the required function here
therefore X is cultivated in this path-phase
```

This is exactly why `paracittajñāna` can be present in the practitioner's repertoire yet excluded from the uninterrupted path.

The numerical matrix is therefore generated by predicates, not the reverse. The number six or seven is an output of the underlying relational structure.
