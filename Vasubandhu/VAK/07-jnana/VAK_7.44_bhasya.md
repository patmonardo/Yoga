# VAK 7.44 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> svādhobhūviṣayāḥ labhyā ucitās tu virāgataḥ /
> tṛtīyā trīṇy upasthānāni ādyaṃ śrotrarddhir cakṣuṣi // VAK_7.44 //

**Best translation:**  
The five direct knowledges take as their objects their own ground or a lower ground. When already cultivated, they are obtained through detachment; all require application for their production. The third is constituted by three applications of mindfulness, while extraordinary efficacy, divine hearing, and divine sight belong to the first application of mindfulness.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.44.md`](./VAK_7.44.md)

## Source Cautions

The e-text has several minor orthographic irregularities, including forms around `ṛddhyabhijñā`, `ārūpyabhūmika`, and `cittacaittālambana`. The argument is nevertheless clear enough that no conjectural emendation is needed for this pass.

The phrase `ucitās tu virāgataḥ` must not be generalized to mean that the five abhijñās arise without practice. Vasubandhu immediately restricts it to powers already cultivated in previous lives and explicitly states that the production of all five requires `prayoga`.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu.**

The verse first limits the range of the five worldly abhijñās. A power produced on a given contemplative ground reaches that ground and lower grounds, but not a higher one. Vasubandhu illustrates the rule separately:

```text
ṛddhi
    one may go to or construct
    one's own ground or a lower one
    not a higher one

divyaśrotra
    hears sounds of its own or lower ground
    not a higher one

cetaḥparyāya
    does not know a higher-ground mind

pūrvanivāsānusmṛti
    does not recollect a higher-ground state

cyutopapāda
    does not see a higher-ground state
```

This is why other-mind knowledge and former-life recollection do not apprehend a mind belonging to the formless levels when those levels stand above the ground of the abhijñā.

Vasubandhu then asks how these five are acquired. If an abhijñā has already been repeatedly cultivated in previous lives, it can be obtained through detachment (`vairāgya`). But its special degree or excellence (`vaiśeṣikya`) comes through deliberate practice, and in every case actual production requires `prayoga`.

The verse next classifies the abhijñās through the applications of mindfulness (`smṛtyupasthāna`). Other-mind knowledge comprises three: feeling, mind, and dharmas, because its objects are mind and associated mental factors. Extraordinary efficacy, divine hearing, and divine sight belong to the first mindfulness-application because their objects are forms. Ṛddhi takes four external spheres other than sound; divine hearing takes sound; divine sight takes visible form.

An apparent difficulty then arises. Scriptural descriptions of divine sight can include judgments such as beings being endowed with bodily misconduct. Vasubandhu answers that such ethical or karmic determination is not known by the divine-eye abhijñā itself. A distinct accompanying knowledge (`abhijñāparivārajñāna`) arises in noble persons and supplies that determination.

The remaining cases, where no narrower specification is imposed, can be constituted by all four applications of mindfulness.

## Voice Map

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — classification of object-range, acquisition, and mindfulness structure.
- **Voice: Objector / scriptural difficulty** — if divine sight is form-only, how can passages say that it knows beings as endowed with bodily misconduct?
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — the ethical determination is supplied by a distinct accompanying knowledge, not by the divine-eye abhijñā itself.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
1. Every worldly abhijñā arises from a determinate ground.
2. Its cognitive reach is limited to that ground and lower grounds.
3. Therefore extraordinary reach is still ordered and non-symmetric.

4. Prior cultivation can make an abhijñā obtainable through detachment.
5. Yet actual production still requires deliberate application.
6. Special excellence is produced through practice.

7. The mindfulness-application is determined by object-type.
8. Mind and mental factors require feeling/mind/dharma mindfulness.
9. Form-object abhijñās belong to body mindfulness.
10. Judgments not contained in the abhijñā's own object-domain require an accompanying cognition.
```

## New Technical Terms

- **vaiśeṣikya** — a special or superior degree of the capacity. **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific refinement in this passage. **Function:** distinguishes mere acquisition from developed excellence; produced through `prayoga`.
- **abhijñāparivārajñāna** — knowledge accompanying an abhijñā. **Status:** introduced here in the Bhāṣya. **Function:** explains how determinations not directly contained in the abhijñā's own object-field can nevertheless accompany its use.
- **janmāntarābhyasta** — repeatedly cultivated in another birth / previous lives. **Status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification here. **Function:** condition under which an abhijñā may be obtained through detachment.
- **svādhobhūmi** — one's own ground and a lower ground. **Status:** kārikā term expanded by the Bhāṣya. **Function:** defines the asymmetric vertical range of the five worldly abhijñās.

## Doctrinal Determination

The five worldly abhijñās are extraordinary but strictly bounded capacities.

```text
source-ground
    determines
reachable vertical range

own ground
    reachable

lower ground
    reachable

higher ground
    not reachable
```

This prevents `abhijñā` from becoming an indeterminate notion of unlimited perception.

Acquisition is also two-stage:

```text
prior cultivation
    + detachment
        → recovery / acquisition

renewed prayoga
        → actual production
        → special excellence
```

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes **stored cultivation-history**, **conditions of reacquisition**, and **present activation**.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation “when previously cultivated, they become available through detachment” is basically correct but requires the explicit qualification:

> Previously cultivated abhijñās may be obtained through detachment, but all five require deliberate application for their actual production, and their special excellence is practice-produced.

The final pāda should also be read more narrowly than “embodied appearance” in general. Vasubandhu specifically says that ṛddhi, divine hearing, and divine sight are instances of the **first smṛtyupasthāna because their object-support is rūpa**.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.44 gives a compact theory of constrained competence:

```text
capacity
    ≠ unrestricted power

capacity
    =
    source-ground
    + admissible range
    + acquisition-history
    + activation condition
    + object-type
    + accompanying cognition
```

Two distinctions are especially important.

First:

```text
greater reach
    does not erase
order of levels
```

Second:

```text
what a capacity directly knows
    ≠
what can be known in conjunction with it
```

The notion of `abhijñāparivārajñāna` is therefore philosophically important: a developed cognitive act can operate together with companion cognitions without collapsing their functions into one undifferentiated faculty.

## Organon Contact

This verse is unusually close to a clean capability model for the Organon.

A rational feature or capability should carry at least:

```text
origin / ground
reachable domains
training history
activation requirement
direct object-type
companion operators
```

The most useful textual lesson is not that “higher cognition contains the lower,” but the stricter claim actually made by Vasubandhu:

> a capacity generated on one level has a lawful, asymmetric domain of application, and additional determinations may require separate accompanying knowledge.

That is a better model for a constrained `FeatStruct` than treating a capability name as if it already encoded every operation performed around it.
