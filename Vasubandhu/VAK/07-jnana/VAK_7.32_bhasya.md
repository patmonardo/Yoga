# VAK_7.32 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> vaiśāradyaṃ caturdhā tu yathādyadaśame bale /
> dvitīyasaptame caiva smṛtiprajñātmakaṃ trayam // VAK_7.32 //

**Best current translation:**

Fearlessness is fourfold, corresponding to the first and tenth powers and likewise to the second and seventh. The triad has recollection and discriminative knowing as its nature.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.32.md`](./VAK_7.32.md)

### Source cautions

The e-text has several minor orthographic irregularities (`vaiśāradhya`, `vaiśārdyam`, `vala`, etc.). They are preserved above rather than silently normalized. The doctrinal syntax is nevertheless clear.

The third fearlessness passage reads `karmasvakajñānavala` in this witness; the intended correspondence is with the second power concerning action and its maturation.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Buddha has four `vaiśāradyas`, as taught in the sūtras. They correspond respectively to four of the ten powers already analyzed:

1. the first power, knowledge of what is possible and impossible;
2. the tenth power, knowledge of the destruction of the influxes;
3. the second power, knowledge concerning action and its result;
4. the seventh power, knowledge of paths leading everywhere.

These correlations explain the cognitive grounds upon which the Buddha can make four public declarations without fear: that he is fully awakened, that his influxes are exhausted, that the factors he declares obstructive really are obstructive, and that the path he teaches for disciples' going-forth really leads out.

**Voice: Objector**

Why not simply identify fearlessness with knowledge itself?

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Because `vaiśāradya` means `nirbhayatā`, fearlessness. The Buddha becomes fearless through these knowledges. Therefore it is proper to say that fearlessness is **produced by knowledge** (`jñānakṛta`), not that fearlessness simply is knowledge.

The Bhāṣya then turns to the three establishments of recollection (`smṛtyupasthāna`). They arise according to differences within the assembly of disciples and have recollection together with clear discriminative knowing (`smṛti-saṃprajñāna`) as their nature.

Even a disciple may sometimes remain free from delight or resentment when others listen, do not listen, or are divided between the two. Why, then, are the three establishments called distinctive Buddha-dharmas?

Because in the Buddha even the latent traces (`vāsanā`) have been abandoned. Alternatively, the extraordinary feature is that for one who has disciples there would ordinarily remain an opening for pleasure, displeasure, and related reactions toward their listening or failure to listen; in the Buddha these reactions do not arise at all. Their complete non-arising is therefore established as a marvel distinctive to him.

## Argument Reconstruction

The passage performs two distinct reductions.

First:

```text
Buddha-power
    gives the cognitive ground
        ↓
fearlessness
    is the affective/practical consequence
```

The first-pass tendency to equate the fearlessnesses directly with the powers is therefore too strong. Their relation is genetic, not identity:

```text
jñāna
    → produces nirbhayatā
```

Second:

```text
three establishments of recollection
    → constituted by
        smṛti
        + samprajñāna/prajñā
```

Their distinctiveness does not lie in a completely unique material factor; similar composure can occur in disciples. What is Buddha-specific is the perfection of the condition: the disappearance even of residual dispositions capable of producing delight or aversion toward the assembly.

## New Technical Terms

- **vaiśāradya** — fearlessness, confident unhesitating assurance.
  - **status:** previously introduced in the Kārikā block.
  - **function:** one of the eighteen distinctive Buddha-dharmas; here explicitly distinguished from the knowledge that produces it.

- **nirbhayatā** — absence of fear; fearlessness.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** direct gloss defining `vaiśāradya`.

- **jñānakṛta** — produced or effected by knowledge.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification.
  - **function:** establishes the genetic relation between the powers and fearlessnesses while blocking identity.

- **smṛtyupasthāna** — establishment of recollection.
  - **status:** previously encountered doctrinal term; here Buddha-specific triad.
  - **function:** three modes of equanimous recollective presence toward different responses of disciples.

- **samprajñāna** — clear comprehension, discriminative knowing.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya wording corresponding to the Kārikā's `prajñā`.
  - **function:** constitutive cognitive factor paired with `smṛti` in the three establishments.

- **parṣadbheda** — differentiation/division of the assembly.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only in this passage.
  - **function:** supplies the situational basis for the three establishments.

- **savāsanaprahāṇa** — abandonment together with latent traces/dispositions.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation here.
  - **function:** explains why the Buddha's three establishments are genuinely unshared even though disciples may display similar composure.

## Doctrinal Determination

### The four fearlessnesses are not four more knowledges

The four correspond to the following powers:

| Fearlessness | Cognitive ground |
|---|---|
| fearless declaration of complete awakening | possible/impossible power |
| fearless declaration of exhausted influxes | destruction-of-influxes power |
| fearless declaration of obstructive factors | karmic-action/result power |
| fearless declaration of the path leading out | all-leading-paths power |

But the Bhāṣya immediately prevents an ontological collapse:

```text
knowledge ≠ fearlessness

knowledge
    is ground/cause

fearlessness
    is the unobstructed practical assurance produced by it
```

This distinction is doctrinally important because the Buddha's eighteen distinctive qualities are not merely alternative names for one identical set of jñānas.

### The three establishments of recollection

The three arise relative to an assembly in which disciples:

```text
listen

do not listen

are divided between listening and not listening
```

The Buddha remains free from delight and aversion in all three situations.

Their constitution is:

```text
smṛti
    recollection / non-forgetfulness

+

samprajñāna / prajñā
    clear discriminative comprehension
```

The Buddha-specific excellence lies not merely in temporary composure but in the eradication of residual tendencies that could support affective reaction.

## Translation Consequences

The Kārikā's `vaiśāradya` should continue to be translated as **fearlessness** rather than simply "confidence" or "assurance," because the Bhāṣya explicitly glosses it as `nirbhayatā`.

The statement that the four fearlessnesses "correspond" to four powers should not be strengthened into identity. Better:

> The four fearlessnesses are grounded respectively in the first, tenth, second, and seventh powers.

For `smṛtiprajñātmakaṃ trayam`, the Bhāṣya paraphrase `smṛtisaṃprajñānasvabhāva` confirms a functional translation such as:

> the triad has recollection and clear discriminative knowing as its nature.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.32 gives a particularly clean causal distinction:

```text
cognitive determination
    jñāna / bala
        ↓
produced practical modality
    nirbhayatā / vaiśāradya
```

The same conceptual content can therefore appear under different categorical determinations without being identical across them.

The second half gives another pattern:

```text
named practical excellence
    smṛtyupasthāna
        ↓ constituent analysis
    smṛti + samprajñāna
        ↓ Buddha-specific perfection
    savāsanaprahāṇa
```

The Buddha's distinctive quality is thus generated not by adding an exotic new mental factor but by the perfection and complete purification of factors otherwise available within the path.

## Organon Contact Point

The strongest Organon contact is the refusal to collapse **ground into manifestation**.

```text
knowledge-power
    does not equal
fearlessness

rather:
knowledge-power
    grounds / produces
fearless public determination
```

That is more useful than the first-pass idea that a power simply "appears as" a fearlessness. The Bhāṣya gives an explicit genetic predicate: `jñānakṛta`.

Likewise, the three establishments show how a concrete excellence can be analyzed into ordinary constituent determinations while remaining distinctive because those determinations have reached a qualitatively complete state — here, abandonment together with residual traces.

This is exactly the kind of relation that a later FeatureStructure extraction should preserve:
