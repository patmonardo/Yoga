# VAK_7.07 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> kṣayajñānaṃ hi satyeṣu parijñātādiniścayaḥ /
> na parijñeyam ity ādir anutpādamatir matā // VAK_7.07 //

**Best translation:**  
Knowledge of exhaustion is the certainty, with respect to the truths, beginning with “it has been fully known.” Knowledge of non-arising is held to be the cognition beginning with “it is not further to be fully known.”

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.07.md`](./VAK_7.07.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

**Reference:** VAkK_7.7; Pradhan [394|05–394|14]

```text
atha kṣayajñānānutpādajñānayoḥ ko viśeṣaḥ /

kṣayajñānaṃ hi satyeṣu parijñātādiniścayaḥ /
na parijñeyamityādiranutpādamatirmatā // VAkK_7.7 //

kṣayajñānaṃ katamat /
duḥkhaṃ me parijñātamiti jānāti /
samudayaḥ prahīṇo nirodhaḥ sākṣātakṛto mārgo bhāvita iti jānāti /
tadupādāya yat jñānaṃ darśanaṃ vidyā buddhirbodhiḥ prajñā āloko 'bhisamayamidamucyate kṣayajñānam /
anutpādajñānaṃ katamat /
duḥkhaṃ me parijñātaṃ na punaḥ parijñeyamiti jānāti yāvat mārgo bhāvito na punarbhāvayitavya iti /
tadupādāyeti vistareṇoktaṃ śāstre /
kathamanāsraveṇa jñānenaivaṃ jānāti /
tatpṛṣṭhalabdhena vyutthita evaṃ jānāti /
atastadviśeṣeṇa tayorviśeṣaḥ /
śāstre jñāpita iti kāśmīrāḥ /
```

## Natural Argument

The passage begins with a direct problem:

```text
What is the difference
between kṣayajñāna and anutpādajñāna?
```

Vasubandhu first defines each through the four tasks of the truths.

### Knowledge of exhaustion

```text
duḥkha
    “has been fully known”

samudaya
    “has been abandoned”

nirodha
    “has been realized”

mārga
    “has been cultivated”
```

The passage then expands this cognition through a series of equivalent or associated designations:

```text
jñāna
darśana
vidyā
buddhi
bodhi
prajñā
āloka
abhisamaya
```

The point is not merely that a task is complete, but that its completion is determinately known.

### Knowledge of non-arising

The same four tasks are now qualified by non-repetition:

```text
duḥkha
    has been fully known
    and is not again to be fully known

...

mārga
    has been cultivated
    and is not again to be cultivated
```

Thus the difference is not between two different truth-fields. It is between:

```text
completion
    /
completion known as non-recurrent
```

## The Bhāṣya's Additional Problem

Vasubandhu then raises a difficulty not explicit in the Kārikā:

> How does uncontaminated knowledge know in this propositional form?

The reply is:

```text
tat-pṛṣṭha-labdhena
    by the cognition subsequently acquired from it

vyutthitaḥ
    when emerged from the immediate uncontaminated realization
```

Thus the explicit reflective judgment:

```text
“I have fully known suffering”
“I need not fully know it again”
```

is not simply identified with the immediate uncontaminated cognition itself. The articulated self-ascription belongs to a subsequent cognition grounded upon it.

This is the decisive Bhāṣya refinement.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
Four Truths
    ↓
four required tasks
    ↓
completion of those tasks
    ↓
kṣayajñāna
    certainty that the tasks are complete
    ↓
non-repetition of those tasks
    ↓
anutpādajñāna
    certainty that they are not to be performed again
```

But the Bhāṣya inserts a reflective distinction:

```text
immediate uncontaminated realization
    ↓
subsequently acquired cognition
    ↓
explicit judgment concerning what has been completed
```

The final distinction between exhaustion-knowledge and non-arising-knowledge is therefore exhibited through the difference in what this subsequent cognition determines.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — asks the difference between the two knowledges, gives their definitions, and raises the problem of how uncontaminated knowledge can know in the stated propositional form.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — the closing attribution `iti kāśmīrāḥ` assigns the appeal to the śāstra to the Kashmiri Vaibhāṣikas.

## New Technical Terms

- **tatpṛṣṭhalabdha** — cognition obtained subsequently from or following the uncontaminated realization
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** explains how explicit reflective judgment is related to immediate uncontaminated knowledge

- **vyutthita** — emerged from the immediate meditative/path cognition
  - **status:** absent from the Kārikā
  - **function:** marks the cognitive condition in which the explicit judgment occurs

- **abhisamaya** — direct realization / penetration
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** one of the designations associated here with exhaustion-knowledge

- **āloka** — illumination
  - **status:** rare in the Kārikās
  - **function:** one of the associated designations for the realized cognition

## Doctrinal Determination

The two terminal knowledges are distinguished by the modal status of completed path-work:

```text
kṣayajñāna
    the required work has been completed

anutpādajñāna
    the completed work will not become required again
```

The Bhāṣya then prevents a simple identification of immediate uncontaminated realization with reflective propositional self-knowledge. The explicit statement of completion is mediated by a subsequently acquired cognition.

This preserves a distinction between:

```text
direct realization
    /
reflective determination of the realized state
```

## Translation Consequence for the Kārikā

**Clarification only.**

The existing translation correctly distinguishes completed certainty from certainty of non-repetition. The Bhāṣya adds that the explicit first-person formulations should not be taken as a literal transcript of the immediate uncontaminated cognition itself.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The passage supplies a precise distinction between achievement and reflexive knowledge of achievement:

```text
realization
    ≠
judgment that realization has occurred
```

The second is grounded in the first but is not simply identical with it.

This is enough for the present pass. No larger synthesis is required here.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.07 defines knowledge of exhaustion as certainty that the four tasks of the truths have been completed, and knowledge of non-arising as certainty that those completed tasks need never be performed again. The Bhāṣya's distinctive contribution is the reflective distinction between immediate uncontaminated realization and the subsequently acquired cognition that explicitly knows “I have completed this” or “this need not be done again.”
