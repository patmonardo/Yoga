# VAK 7.42 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ṛddhiśrotramanaḥpūrvajanmacyutyudayakṣaye /
> jñātasākṣīkriyā 'bhijñā ṣaḍvidhā muktimārgadhīḥ // VAK_7.42 //

**Best translation:**  
The superknowledges are sixfold: direct cognitive realization concerning supernormal power, divine hearing, another mind, former lives, death and reappearance, and exhaustion. Their cognitive nature is the principle of the path of liberation.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.42.md`](./VAK_7.42.md)

## Source Caution

The e-text is a reference text principally based on Pradhan with corrections from Shastri, not a critical edition. The reading `jñātasākṣīkriyā` in the kārikā is highly compressed; the prose clarifies it through repeated compounds of the form `jñānasākṣātkriyā`.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The discussion begins by moving from the six final-edge qualities to another set of excellences, the `abhijñās`. Vasubandhu explicitly situates them as qualities shared with disciples and, in five cases, even with ordinary persons.

He then asks what an `abhijñā` is and answers by enumerating six fields:

```text
ṛddhi
    supernormal efficacy

divyaśrotra
    divine hearing

cetaḥparyāya
    knowledge of another mind

pūrvanivāsānusmṛti
    recollection of former lives

cyutyutpāda
    death and reappearance

āsravakṣaya
    exhaustion of the influxes
```

The prose repeatedly characterizes an abhijñā as a `jñānasākṣātkriyā`: an act of direct cognitive realization concerning the relevant field.

The decisive doctrinal qualification follows immediately:

```text
five abhijñās
    are shared with pṛthagjanas

all six abhijñās
    are of the nature of
    vimuktimārga-prajñā
```

Thus the set belongs structurally to the cognition of the liberation path, but extraordinary cognitive reach by itself does not establish liberation.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
question
    what is abhijñā?

answer
    direct cognitive realization
    in one of six determinate fields
        ↓
set of six abhijñās
        ↓
qualification
    five are available even to ordinary persons
        ↓
further determination
    all six have the cognitive nature
    of liberation-path principle
```

The important distinction is therefore:

```text
extraordinary capacity
    ≠
liberation
```

while also:

```text
abhijñā as cultivated path-capacity
    is intelligible through
    liberation-path cognition
```

## New Technical Terms

- **jñānasākṣātkriyā** — direct cognitive realization of a determinate field
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the compressed kārikā expression
  - **function:** definition of `abhijñā`

- **cetaḥparyāya** — the cognitive field of another mind or mental continuum
  - **status:** absent from the kārikā wording, which uses `manaḥ`
  - **function:** doctrinal specification of the third abhijñā

- **pūrvanivāsānusmṛti** — recollection of former modes of residence/existence
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `pūrvajanma`
  - **function:** doctrinal specification of the fourth abhijñā

- **cyutyutpāda** — death and subsequent arising
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `cyutyudaya`
  - **function:** doctrinal specification of the fifth abhijñā

- **vimuktimārga-prajñā** — principle/discriminative knowing belonging to the path of liberation
  - **status:** technically operative in the prose
  - **function:** statement of the cognitive nature of the six abhijñās

## Doctrinal Determination

The six abhijñās are not six equal species of liberation. Five may occur in ordinary persons. The Bhāṣya therefore blocks any identification of supernormal ability with spiritual completion.

```text
ordinary person
    may possess abhijñā 1–5

liberated path
    requires more than possession
    of extraordinary cognitive capacities
```

The sixth, `āsravakṣaya-jñānasākṣātkriyā`, is structurally different because its field is the exhaustion of the very basis of bondage.

The present verse does not yet fully classify that asymmetry; later verses will specify the cognitive composition, levels, and the three `vidyās`.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass rendering "direct superior knowledge" remains usable for `abhijñā`, but the Bhāṣya strongly favors emphasizing **direct cognitive realization** rather than generic superiority or spectacle.

The first-pass claim that the six are simply "liberative cognitions" should be weakened. A better determination is:

> The six are direct cognitive realizations whose cognitive nature is articulated through the liberation path, although five may also be possessed by ordinary persons.

This preserves Vasubandhu's explicit distinction.

## Logical Determination

The verse introduces a new relation between knowledge and capacity:

```text
knowledge
    is not merely representation

knowledge
    becomes direct realization
    of a normally unavailable field
```

But the Bhāṣya adds an equally important limit:

```text
extension of cognitive access
    does not entail
transformation of the subject's liberative status
```

Therefore:

```text
range of cognition
    and
status of the knower
```

must be kept logically distinct.

## Organon Contact

This is an important guardrail for the Rational Kośa and for any agent architecture modeled on cultivated capacities.

An agent may acquire:

```text
wider perception
predictive access
memory access
cross-agent state access
operative power
```

without thereby acquiring freedom, wisdom, or a right practical end.

The Kośa thus gives a very clean principle:

> Increased access to hidden state is an epistemic power, not yet a moral or liberative determination.

The later superiority of instruction over spectacle will depend on precisely this distinction.
