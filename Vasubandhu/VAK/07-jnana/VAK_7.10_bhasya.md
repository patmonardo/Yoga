# VAK_7.10 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dharmajñānānvayajñānaṃ ṣoḍaśākāram anyathā /
> tathā ca sāṃvṛtaṃ svaiḥ svaiḥ satyākāraiś catuṣṭayam // VAK_7.10 //

**Best translation:**  
Dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge have sixteen aspects. Conventional knowledge may likewise have those aspects and may also operate otherwise. The four truth-knowledges each proceed through the aspects proper to their own truth.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.10.md`](./VAK_7.10.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Primary source: *Abhidharmakośabhāṣya*, Jñānanirdeśa, Pradhan pagination around [395|17–396|02].

Relevant source sequence:

```text
eṣāṃ daśānāṃ jñānānāṃ

dharmajñānānvayajñānaṃ ṣoḍḍaśākāram

tān purastādupadekṣyāmaḥ /

anyathā /

tathā ca sāṃvṛtaṃ

saṃvṛtijñānaṃ ṣoḍaśākāramanyathākāraṃ ca
sarvadharmāṇāṃ svasāmānyalakṣaṇādigrahaṇāt /

svaiḥ svaiḥ satyākāraiścatuṣṭayam // VAkK_7.10 //

duḥkhasamudayanirodhamārgajñānāni svaiḥ svaiḥ
satyākāraiḥ pravartanta ityekaikaṃ caturākāraṃ bhavanti /
```

### Source Caution

The e-text reads `ṣoḍḍaśākāram` in the Bhāṣya quotation, while the normalized Kārikā file has `ṣoḍaśākāram`. No silent emendation is imposed here.

## Natural Argument

Vasubandhu is now applying the ground of `ākāra` announced in VAK 7.08.

The argument proceeds in three steps.

### 1. Dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge are sixteen-aspected

```text
dharmajñāna
    → sixteen aspects

anvayajñāna
    → sixteen aspects
```

Vasubandhu does not enumerate the sixteen here. He says explicitly:

```text
tān purastād upadekṣyāmaḥ
    we shall teach them later
```

So the present passage establishes the range without yet supplying the detailed list.

### 2. Conventional knowledge has an open aspect-range

Vasubandhu glosses the compressed `anyathā tathā ca sāṃvṛtam` by saying:

```text
saṃvṛtijñānam
    ṣoḍaśākāram
    anyathākāraṃ ca
```

Conventional knowledge can therefore proceed through the sixteen truth-aspects and also through other aspects.

He grounds this breadth in its capacity to grasp:

```text
sarvadharmāṇām
    sva-lakṣaṇa
    sāmānya-lakṣaṇa
    and so forth
```

Thus its broader aspect-range is not arbitrary; it follows from the broader manner in which conventional cognition apprehends dharmas.

### 3. The four truth-knowledges are each four-aspected

The four are:

```text
duḥkhajñāna
samudayajñāna
nirodhajñāna
mārgajñāna
```

Each proceeds only through the aspects proper to its own truth:

```text
svaiḥ svaiḥ satyākāraiḥ pravartante
```

Hence:

```text
each truth-knowledge
    = four aspects
```

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
The ten knowledges differ partly by aspect-range.

Dharma-knowledge and anvaya-knowledge
    traverse the complete sixteenfold truth-aspect field.

Conventional knowledge
    can traverse that field
    but is not restricted to it,
    because it also apprehends self-characteristics,
    common characteristics,
    and related determinations of dharmas.

The four truth-knowledges
    are narrower:
    each proceeds only through the four aspects
    proper to its own truth.
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives three distinct modes of aspect-range:

```text
complete truth-range
    dharmajñāna / anvayajñāna

open range
    saṃvṛtijñāna

truth-specific restricted range
    the four truth-knowledges
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu throughout.

No explicit objector or rival school position is introduced in this unit.

## New Technical Terms

- **svalakṣaṇa** — self-characteristic; the determination distinctive of a dharma in the present classificatory context  
  **status:** previously encountered  
  **function:** explains why conventional knowledge can operate under aspects beyond the sixteen truth-aspects

- **sāmānyalakṣaṇa** — common characteristic; a general determination under which multiple dharmas may be apprehended  
  **status:** previously encountered  
  **function:** together with `svalakṣaṇa`, grounds the broader aspect-range of conventional knowledge

- **satyākāra** — truth-aspect; a determinate mode under which one of the Noble Truths is apprehended  
  **status:** developing technical term in Chapter 7  
  **function:** specifies the aspect-structure of the four truth-knowledges

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive doctrinal point is that **identity of object-field does not entail identity of cognition**.

Several knowledges can operate with truth-aspects, yet they differ in the permitted range of those aspects.

```text
dharmajñāna / anvayajñāna
    complete sixteenfold truth-aspect range

saṃvṛtijñāna
    sixteenfold range + other aspects

four truth-knowledges
    four proper aspects each
```

This confirms that the Kośa classifies cognition not only by what is known, but by **how the object can be determined**.

Conventional knowledge is especially important here. Its conventional status does not make it cognitively narrow. On the contrary, it is broader in aspect-range because it can apprehend dharmas through self-characteristics, common characteristics, and other determinations.

## Translation Consequence for Kārikā

The existing Kārikā analysis correctly recognized the basic distribution, but the Bhāṣya allows the relation of `anyathā` and `tathā ca sāṃvṛtam` to be stated more firmly:

```text
saṃvṛtijñāna
    may have the sixteen aspects
    and may also have other aspects
```

Thus `anyathā` belongs centrally to the breadth of conventional knowledge rather than marking an unspecified alternative concerning the other knowledges.

The Bhāṣya also warns against prematurely enumerating the sixteen aspects in this verse itself. Vasubandhu says that their detailed teaching comes later.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The useful structural lesson is modest but exact:

```text
knowledge
    ≠ object alone

knowledge
    = object
      + permitted mode of determination
      + range of aspects
```

This is already a principled constraint on any later Organon representation of cognition. The same object may support different knowledges because the form and range of determination differ.

No larger Kantian or Hegelian synthesis is required here.

## Determinate Summary

VAK 7.10 begins the explicit analysis of the `ākāra` criterion introduced in 7.08.

Vasubandhu determines:

```text
dharmajñāna
    sixteen-aspected

anvayajñāna
    sixteen-aspected

saṃvṛtijñāna
    sixteen-aspected and otherwise-aspected
    because it apprehends self- and common characteristics of dharmas

four truth-knowledges
    each four-aspected
    according to its own truth
```

The passage therefore distinguishes complete, open, and truth-specific ranges of cognitive aspect without yet supplying the later detailed enumeration of the sixteen aspects.
