# VAK_7.38 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> tisro nāmārthavāgjñānam avivartyaṃ yathākramam /
> caturthī yuktamuktābhilāpamārgavaśitvayoḥ // VAK_7.38 //

**Best current translation:**

The first three are, respectively, unfailing knowledge of name, meaning, and speech. The fourth is mastery concerning appropriate and fluent expression and concerning the Path.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.38.md`](./VAK_7.38.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 418|13–418|18.

The natural argument takes up the four `pratisaṃvids` introduced at VAK 7.37 and begins by determining the cognitive nature of each. Their exact object-supports and levels are continued under VAK 7.39–7.40.

```text
tisro nāmārthavāgjñānamavivartya yathākramam /

caturthī yuktomuktābhilāpamārgavaśitvayoḥ // VAkK_7.38 //

avivartyaṃ jñānamiti vartate /
yuktamutābhilāpitāyāṃ samādhivaśisaṃprakyāne cāvivartyaṃ jñānaṃ pratibhānasaṃvit /
```

### Source cautions

- The e-text is visibly compressed and probably imperfect in the prose gloss, especially `yuktomuktābhilāpa...` and `samādhivaśisaṃprakyāne`. These readings are preserved as transmitted rather than silently repaired.
- The Kārikā itself clearly distinguishes the first three by `nāma / artha / vāk` and assigns the fourth to `yukta-mukta-abhilāpa` and `mārga-vaśitva`. The Bhāṣya confirms that `avivartyaṃ jñānam` governs the fourth as well.
- Because VAK 7.39 immediately specifies the fourth's object-support as `vāk` and `mārga`, this file does not force a speculative emendation of the difficult prose phrase at 418|17–18.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The four discriminating knowledges are not four undifferentiated forms of eloquence. They have distinct cognitive determinations.

The first three are respectively unfailing (`avivartya`) knowledges concerning:

```text
dharma-pratisaṃvid
    name-complexes: nāma, pada, vyañjana

artha-pratisaṃvid
    meaning

nirukti-pratisaṃvid
    speech / verbal expression
```

The fourth, `pratibhāna-pratisaṃvid`, is also an unfailing knowledge. It concerns appropriate and free expression and mastery associated with the path. The exact object-structure is deferred to the next verse, where Vasubandhu states explicitly that speech and path are its object-supports.

## Argument Reconstruction

```text
four pratisaṃvids

```

## New Technical Terms

- **nāmakāya / padakāya / vyañjanakāya** — collections or bodies of names, words, and syllabic/verbal markers
  - **status:** prose expansion of the Kārikā's `nāma`
  - **function:** specifies the field of `dharma-pratisaṃvid` more precisely than the verse alone.

- **avivartya-jñāna** — unfailing / non-reversing knowledge
  - **status:** Kārikā term made explicitly governing in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** common cognitive quality of all four discriminating knowledges.

- **yukta-abhilāpa** — appropriate, connected, fitting expression
  - **status:** compressed Kārikā compound
  - **function:** one aspect of the fourth discriminating knowledge.

- **mukta-abhilāpa** — free, unobstructed expression
  - **status:** compressed Kārikā compound
  - **function:** second expressive aspect of the fourth discriminating knowledge.

- **mārga-vaśitva** — mastery concerning the path
  - **status:** Kārikā term
  - **function:** distinguishes `pratibhāna` from mere verbal fluency.

## Doctrinal Determination

### The first three are not a simple linguistic sequence

The Bhāṣya clarifies that `dharma-pratisaṃvid` is not knowledge of `dharma` in the broad sense of all realities. Here its immediate field is the system of doctrinal/verbal designation:

```text
nāma
pada
vyañjana
```

This is an important correction to an overly abstract reading of the first-pass file.

`artha-pratisaṃvid` then concerns the meaning articulated through those designations, while `nirukti-pratisaṃvid` concerns verbal expression itself.

Thus:

```text
designation-system
    ≠ meaning
    ≠ verbal deployment
```

The fourfold structure depends on maintaining these distinctions.

### `avivartya` is the common form

The phrase `avivartyaṃ jñānam iti vartate` explicitly carries the same determination into the fourth member. So the unity of the four is not merely that they are all forms of knowledge; they are all forms of knowledge that do not turn back, fail, or become obstructed in their respective domains.

### The fourth is practical-communicative mastery

The fourth does not simply add another object beside name, meaning, and speech. It concerns the successful deployment of knowledge in discourse:

```text
appropriate expression
    +
free expression
    +
mastery of the path
```

The Bhāṣya's prose is textually rough here, so the strongest doctrinal determination should await VAK 7.39. What is already secure is that `pratibhāna` is not mere spontaneity of speech: its fluency is path-governed.

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation is broadly sound but benefits from one refinement.

`dharma-pratisaṃvid` should not yet be translated here as direct knowledge of determinations in general. The Bhāṣya ties it specifically to `nāma-pada-vyañjana-kāya`, the structured body of names, words, and verbal markers.

A more precise philosophical translation is therefore:

> The first three discriminating knowledges are, respectively, unfailing knowledge of systems of designation, of meaning, and of speech. The fourth is unfailing mastery in fitting and free expression together with mastery concerning the Path.

This remains slightly interpretive at the fourth member because the source prose is compressed; VAK 7.39 supplies the clearer object-analysis.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.38 gives a clean differentiation of communicable knowledge:

```text
name-system
    what determination is linguistically fixed as

meaning
    what that determination means

speech
    its actual verbal expression

pratibhāna
    mastery of their free and appropriate deployment
    under the governance of the path
```

The first-pass tendency to treat the fourth simply as a synthesis of the previous three should therefore be qualified. It is synthetic in function, but Vasubandhu still treats it as a determinate knowledge with its own object-support and nature, which VAK 7.39 will specify.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage is especially strong for the distinction between **knowledge and exposition**.

A rational system requires more than possession of meaning:

```text
determinate nomenclature
    → determinate meaning
    → determinate expression
    → free path-governed articulation
```

The fourth moment is therefore not rhetoric added externally to science. It is the capacity by which determinate knowledge becomes communicable without losing its relation to the Path.

This supports the Chapter 7c working thesis that knowledge is becoming actual as communicable efficacy, but the Kośa's own fourfold analysis must remain primary.
