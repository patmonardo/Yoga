# VAK_7.30 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> prāṅnivāsa-cyutotpāda-balaṃ dhyāneṣu śeṣitam /
> sarvabhūmiṣu kenāsya balam avyāhataṃ yataḥ // VAK_7.30 //

**Best current translation:**

The powers concerning former abodes and death-and-rebirth are based in the dhyānas; the remaining powers extend through all levels. Why is this called a power in his case? Because it is unobstructed.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_7.30.md`](./VAK_7.30.md)

### Source cautions

The e-text has several obvious transmission/OCR irregularities in this passage, including `prāṅinavis...`, `duddhānutpādāt`, and spacing faults. These are not silently normalized in the quoted source. The sense is recoverable from the surrounding prose and the established technical vocabulary.

The source's `sarvabhūmi` is not vague. Vasubandhu immediately enumerates eleven levels: desire domain, `anāgamya`, `dhyānāntara`, the four dhyānas, and the four formless attainments.

## Natural Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The previous verses established the intrinsic composition (`svabhāva`) of the ten Buddha-powers by identifying which knowledges constitute each. The question now changes from **what each power is made of** to **the level (`bhūmi`) in which it operates**.

The powers of knowledge of former abodes and of death-and-rebirth are based in the four dhyānas. Their operation therefore requires a determinate meditative support.

The remaining powers are gathered under all levels. Vasubandhu does not leave this as an indefinite universal: he specifies eleven levels in total:

```text
kāmadhātu
anāgamya
dhyānāntara
four dhyānas
four ārūpyas
```

These Buddha-powers have a human support in Jambūdvīpa, because Buddhas do not arise elsewhere.

A further question then becomes necessary. The same ten kinds of knowledge can occur in others. Why, then, are they called **powers** only in a Buddha?

The answer is functional rather than merely honorific: in a Buddha, knowledge proceeds unobstructed with respect to every knowable. In others it can be obstructed; even when they wish to know something, cognition may fail to arise. Such knowledge therefore does not merit the designation `bala`.

Vasubandhu gives Śāriputra as the counterexample: even this foremost disciple could fail to know a relevant fact about a candidate for ordination or the remote rebirth-history of a bird attacked by a hawk. The point is not to diminish Śāriputra, but to mark the exact difference between extraordinary disciple-knowledge and Buddha-power.

Thus `bala` means **knowledge whose operation is unrestricted by obstruction within its knowable field**.

## New Technical Terms

- **caturdhyānabhūmika** — based in the four dhyānas
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā's `dhyāneṣu`
  - **function:** fixes the meditative ground of the former-abodes and death/rebirth powers

- **anāgamya** — the preliminary concentration preceding the first dhyāna
  - **status:** previously encountered in path analysis
  - **function:** one of the eleven levels supporting the remaining powers

- **dhyānāntara** — intermediate dhyāna
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** one of the eleven levels counted under `sarvabhūmi`

- **avyāhata-jñāna** — unobstructed knowledge
  - **status:** decisive Bhāṣya formulation here
  - **function:** criterion distinguishing Buddha-power from the same knowledge-types in others

- **vyāhata** — obstructed, impeded
  - **status:** contrast term introduced in the explanation
  - **function:** marks the limitation of non-Buddha cognition

- **balākhyā** — the designation/name 'power'
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory usage
  - **function:** shows that `bala` is a status earned by a mode of operation, not a separate substance

## Doctrinal Determination

### 1. Two powers have a restricted meditative ground

```text
pūrvanivāsajñānabala
cyutyupapattijñānabala
    → caturdhyānabhūmika
```

They are not available from every level indiscriminately.

### 2. The remaining powers span eleven levels

The Bhāṣya enumerates:

```text
1  kāmadhātu
2  anāgamya
3  dhyānāntara
4–7  four dhyānas
8–11 four ārūpyas
```

So `sarvabhūmi` here is a precise technical range.

### 3. The support is human and Jambūdvīpa-based

The powers depend on a human support in Jambūdvīpa because Buddhas arise there. This belongs to the cosmological conditions of Buddhahood, not to the definition of power itself.

### 4. `bala` is defined by unobstructed operation

The same knowledge-types do not automatically count as powers in non-Buddhas.

```text
knowledge present
    ≠ automatically bala

knowledge operating without obstruction
throughout its knowable field
    → bala
```

This is the doctrinal center of the verse.

### 5. Śāriputra establishes the contrast

The Bhāṣya uses Śāriputra to show that even an exceptionally developed disciple can have finite cognitive reach. The distinction is therefore not between knowledge and ignorance, but between:

```text
extraordinary but obstructible knowledge
and
unobstructed Buddha-knowledge
```

## Translation Consequences

The first-pass translation of `avyāhata` as **unobstructed** is strongly confirmed.

The Bhāṣya also shows that `bala` should not be weakened to generic 'faculty' or 'ability'. It is better retained as **power**, provided the technical sense is explained:

> power = knowledge whose exercise is unobstructed with respect to its proper knowable field.

`dhyāneṣu` should be understood specifically as the four dhyānas for the two superknowledge powers.

## Logical Determination

VAK 7.30 completes a three-coordinate determination of Buddha-power:

```text
content
    which jñānas constitute it

scope
    which bhūmis support it

operational status
    whether cognition is obstructible or unobstructed
```

Thus a power is not identified by content alone.

Two agents may possess cognition of the same formal type while differing decisively in **modal availability**:

```text
Agent A:
    can know X under some conditions
    but cognition may fail to arise

Buddha:
    where X falls within the relevant knowable field,
    cognition is not blocked
```

The distinction is therefore between **possession of a cognitive determination** and **unobstructed command of that determination**.

## Organon Contact Point

The restrained Organon contact is unusually clear here.

A Feature or cognitive determination is not fully characterized merely by its type. Its concrete rational status also depends on:

```text
support
scope
availability
obstruction conditions
```

The Bhāṣya therefore anticipates the distinction between a determination merely represented in a system and one that is **operationally available without failure under its defining conditions**.

This remains a structural contact only. `bala` should not be assimilated to a modern computational property.

## Transition

VAK 7.30 has now completed the basic analysis of the ten powers by:

```text
constitution
→ operational ground
→ unobstructedness
```

The Bhāṣya then moves immediately to another question of Buddha-power: bodily strength, beginning with `nārāyaṇabalaṃ kāye`, which belongs to VAK 7.31.

## Commit Note

Bhāṣya pass for VAK 7.30. Clarifies the eleven-level range of the powers and, most importantly, defines Buddha-power by `avyāhata-jñāna`, unobstructed knowledge, rather than by a unique knowledge-substance.
