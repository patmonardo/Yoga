# VAK 5.61 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.61**

`prahāṇādhārabhūtatva-dūṣaṇākhyaś caturvidhaḥ /`
`pratipakṣaḥ prahātavyaḥ kleśa ālambanān mataḥ //`

> The counteragent is fourfold: abandonment, support, distancing, and discrediting. An affliction is held to be abandoned with respect to its object-support.

See: [VAK_5.61.md](./VAK_5.61.md)

## Source Passage

Pradhan 320|07–321|03.

The transmitted source prints the compressed and apparently corrupt form:

`prahāṇādhārabhūtatvadūṣaṇāruyaścaturvidhaḥ`

The Bhāṣya prose immediately resolves the intended four members and therefore controls the interpretation of the Kārikā.

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

A brief alternative position appears concerning whether the liberation path itself may also count as a distancing counteragent:

**Voice:** Other teachers (`apare`)

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

Vasubandhu asks:

> How many kinds of counteragent are there?

He answers that the counteragent is fourfold.

1. **The counteragent of abandonment (`prahāṇa-pratipakṣa`) is the uninterrupted path (`ānantarya-mārga`).**

2. **The counteragent of support (`ādhāra-pratipakṣa`) is the noble path immediately following it, by which the abandonment that has been obtained is maintained.**

3. **The counteragent of distancing (`dūrībhāva-pratipakṣa`) is the path subsequent to the path of liberation, because it distances the already severed acquisition (`prāpti`).**

   Others say that the liberation path itself also belongs here, because it too distances that acquisition.

4. **The counteragent of discrediting (`vidūṣaṇā-pratipakṣa`) is the path by which one discredits a given domain by seeing it as defective.**

Vasubandhu then remarks that a better order of these counteragents would be:

- `vidūṣaṇā-pratipakṣa` — the preparatory path (`prayoga-mārga`) taking suffering and origin as object;
- `prahāṇa-pratipakṣa` — every uninterrupted path (`ānantarya-mārga`);
- `ādhāra-pratipakṣa` — the path of liberation (`vimukti-mārga`);
- `dūrībhāva-pratipakṣa` — the special path (`viśeṣa-mārga`).

Vasubandhu then asks:

> From what is an affliction, while being abandoned, to be abandoned?

The Kārikā answers:

> An affliction is held to be abandoned with respect to its object-support.

The Bhāṣya explains that an affliction cannot be separated from its associated factors (`saṃprayoga`), but it can be separated from its object-support (`ālambana`), because it no longer arises taking that object as support.

A difficulty is then raised: this explanation may work for a future affliction, but how can a past affliction be separated from an object-support?

One might answer that the meaning is: it is abandoned through complete knowledge of the object-support (`ālambana-parijñāna`). But Vasubandhu immediately rejects this as universally valid:

`eṣo 'pi naikāntaḥ`

> This too is not universally decisive.

He therefore reframes the issue: what exactly licenses us to say that an affliction has been abandoned?

For an affliction belonging to one’s own continuum, abandonment is established by severance of its acquisition (`prāpti-cheda`). For an affliction belonging to another continuum, and for material or undefiled dharmas, the relevant relation is abandonment of the afflictions in one’s own continuum that take them as object.

This prepares the doctrine of **distance (`dūrībhāva`)** in VAK 5.62.

## Four Counteragents

The Bhāṣya resolves the Kārikā’s obscure compound into four precise technical relations:

```text
1. prahāṇa-pratipakṣa
   counteragent of abandonment

2. ādhāra-pratipakṣa
   counteragent of support / maintenance

3. dūrībhāva-pratipakṣa
   counteragent of distancing

4. vidūṣaṇā-pratipakṣa
   counteragent of discrediting / seeing as defective
```

These are not synonyms for one generic opposition.

### 1. Prahāṇa-pratipakṣa

```text
ānantarya-mārga
    ↓
actual severance
```

This is the counteragent insofar as it performs abandonment itself.

### 2. Ādhāra-pratipakṣa

```text
vimukti-mārga
    ↓
maintains the abandonment obtained
```

The term `ādhāra` therefore does not mean causal support for the affliction. It means the path-function that sustains the abandonment already achieved.

### 3. Dūrībhāva-pratipakṣa

```text
viśeṣa-mārga
    ↓
progressive distancing from severed acquisition
```

The Bhāṣya records an alternative opinion that the `vimukti-mārga` itself can also be counted here, because it already distances the acquisition.

### 4. Vidūṣaṇā-pratipakṣa

```text
seeing a domain as defective
    ↓
loss of attraction / discrediting
```

Vasubandhu identifies this operationally with the preparatory path when suffering and origin are its objects.

## Taxonomic Order versus Path Order

A major point of the Bhāṣya is that the received enumeration and the practical order need not be identical.

Received list:

```text
prahāṇa
ādhāra
dūrībhāva
vidūṣaṇa
```

Vasubandhu's preferred practical sequence:

```text
vidūṣaṇa
    ↓
prahāṇa
    ↓
ādhāra
    ↓
dūrībhāva
```

or, in path terminology:

```text
prayoga-mārga
    ↓
ānantarya-mārga
    ↓
vimukti-mārga
    ↓
viśeṣa-mārga
```

This is one of the clearest cases in Chapter 5 where **classification order and genetic-operational order must be distinguished**.

## The Object-Support Problem

The second half of the verse turns from the counteragent to the question:

```text
From what is a kleśa abandoned?
```

The initial answer is:

```text
ālambanāt
    with respect to / from its object-support
```

The Bhāṣya explains:

```text
not from saṃprayoga
    because an affliction cannot simply be detached
    from its associated factors

but from ālambana
    because it no longer arises taking that object-support
```

Yet this answer immediately encounters a temporal problem:

```text
future kleśa
    can intelligibly be prevented from arising toward an object

past kleśa
    has already occurred
```

Hence Vasubandhu refuses to make `ālambana-parijñāna` a universal explanation.

The criterion shifts from the object alone to **prāpti**, acquisition:

```text
own-continuum kleśa
    abandoned when its acquisition is severed
```

This is doctrinally more exact than saying merely:

```text
one knows the object
therefore the affliction disappears
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The Bhāṣya securely resolves the first pāda. Preferred translation:

> **The counteragent is fourfold: abandonment, support, distancing, and discrediting. An affliction is held to be abandoned with respect to its object-support.**

However, the second sentence must remain qualified by the Bhāṣya, because Vasubandhu immediately demonstrates that `ālambana` alone does not furnish the final universal criterion of abandonment.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.61 distinguishes at least three questions that would otherwise be conflated:

```text
What weakens attraction to the afflicted field?
    → vidūṣaṇa

What directly cuts the affliction?
    → prahāṇa

What maintains the cut?
    → ādhāra

What progressively removes the severed relation farther away?
    → dūrībhāva
```

So `pratipakṣa` is not one homogeneous negative force. It is differentiated according to **path-function**.

The second half then adds another distinction:

```text
object-relation
    ≠
association relation
    ≠
acquisition relation
```

An affliction may be related to an object, conjoined with other mental factors, and possessed through `prāpti`; abandonment is not reducible to any one of those relations in every case.

## Logical Determination

The strongest logical form here is:

```text
one opposition
    differentiated by function
```

The same general notion `pratipakṣa` becomes four determinate operations:

```text
discredit
cut
maintain
remove farther
```

And the order of exposition is itself relativized:

```text
classification order
    ≠
operational order
```

This is exactly the kind of distinction the later clarifying pass must preserve.

The verse also blocks a simple object-cancellation model:

```text
knowledge(object)
    ≠ automatically
abandonment(kleśa)
```

For the own continuum, the more decisive criterion becomes:

```text
prāpti-cheda
    severance of acquisition
```

## New Technical Terms

- **prahāṇa-pratipakṣa** — counteragent functioning as actual abandonment.  
  **Status:** newly explicit in this form.  
  **Function:** identified with `ānantarya-mārga`.

- **ādhāra-pratipakṣa** — counteragent that supports or maintains achieved abandonment.  
  **Status:** newly explicit.  
  **Function:** identified with the path of liberation.

- **dūrībhāva-pratipakṣa** — counteragent of distancing.  
  **Status:** newly explicit; leads into VAK 5.62.  
  **Function:** progressively distances the severed acquisition.

- **vidūṣaṇā-pratipakṣa** — counteragent of discrediting.  
  **Status:** newly explicit.  
  **Function:** sees a domain as defective and thereby undermines attachment to it.

- **prayoga-mārga** — preparatory path.  
  **Status:** path terminology.  
  **Function:** here identified with the discrediting counteragent when suffering and origin are its objects.

- **ānantarya-mārga** — uninterrupted path.  
  **Status:** previously encountered path term.  
  **Function:** actual abandonment.

- **vimukti-mārga** — path of liberation.  
  **Status:** previously encountered path term.  
  **Function:** maintains the achieved abandonment; according to some, also already distances acquisition.

- **viśeṣa-mārga** — special or further path.  
  **Status:** newly foregrounded here.  
  **Function:** distancing counteragent.

- **prāpti-cheda** — severance of acquisition.  
  **Status:** important Bhāṣya clarification.  
  **Function:** criterion for saying that an affliction in one's own continuum has been abandoned.

## Organon Contact Point

Keep this deliberately restrained for now.

The useful result is not yet a speculative identification but a structural distinction:

```text
Opposition
    can be typed by its role in a transformation.
```

The Kośa distinguishes:

```text
preparation
execution
stabilization
further separation
```

without treating these as four different ultimate goals.

That is enough for the present Bhāṣya pass. A later clarifying pass should first reconstruct the complete path-relation matrix before an Organon comment is attempted.
