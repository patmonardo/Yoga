# VAK_5.15 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> svabhūmyuparamo mārgaḥ ṣaḍbhūminavabhūmikaḥ |
> tadgocarāṇāṃ viṣayo mārgo hyanyo 'nyahetukaḥ || 5.15 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Cessation is the cessation of one's own level. For those whose cognitive support is the path, the path belonging to six levels or nine levels is their object, because one path can be the cause of another.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_5.15.md`](./VAK_5.15.md)

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

For false view and the other anuśayas whose cognitive support is cessation, the cessation of their own level alone is their support: for those belonging to the desire domain, only the cessation of the desire domain; and so on up to those belonging to the summit of existence, for which only the cessation of the summit of existence is the support.

For those belonging to the desire domain whose cognitive support is the path, the entire path on the side of dharmajñāna, belonging to six levels, is their support. The path that is the counteragent to the form and formless domains is also included there.

For those belonging to the form and formless domains, even with respect to levels not directly seen, the path on the side of anvayajñāna, belonging to nine levels, is their support, because one path can be the cause of another.

Although dharmajñāna and anvayajñāna are also mutually causal, anvayajñāna is not the counteragent to the desire domain. Therefore desire-domain anuśayas whose support is the path do not take the anvayajñāna side as their support.

## Course of the Argument

VAK 5.14 established that six anuśayas take uncontaminated supports: cessation and path. VAK 5.15 now determines the exact range of those supports.

The first distinction is strict locality for cessation:

```text
nirodha-ālambana anuśaya
    → takes only cessation of its own bhūmi
```

Thus:

```text
kāmadhātu affliction
    → kāmadhātu-nirodha

...

bhavāgra affliction
    → bhavāgra-nirodha
```

Cessation is therefore not an undifferentiated object. It is indexed to the precise level whose afflictive structure has ceased.

The path is different. Its admissible object-range is broader because path moments stand in causal relations to other path moments.

For desire-domain path-object anuśayas:

```text
dharmajñāna-pakṣa mārga
    = six-bhūmi path
    = object-support
```

For form- and formless-domain path-object anuśayas:

```text
anvayajñāna-pakṣa mārga
    = nine-bhūmi path
    = object-support
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly grounds this in:

```text
mārgasya anyo 'nyahetukatvāt
    because one path is the cause of another
```

This makes the path a causally articulated series rather than a single undifferentiated object.

---

## Why Six and Nine?

The Kārikā pass correctly preserved these numbers as unresolved. The Bhāṣya fixes their referents.

```text
ṣaḍbhūmika
    dharmajñāna side of the path
    object of desire-domain path-oriented anuśayas

navabhūmika
    anvayajñāna side of the path
    object of form/formless path-oriented anuśayas
```

The decisive restriction is also explicit:

```text
anvayajñāna
    is not the pratipakṣa of kāmadhātu
```

Therefore:

```text
kāmāvacara mārgālambana anuśayas
    do not take
anvayajñāna-pakṣa
    as object-support
```

This is a real Bhāṣya correction to the first-pass ambiguity about the two numerical classifications.

## New Technical Terms

- **nirodhālambana** — having cessation as cognitive support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya relational formulation
  - **function:** fixes the object-range of cessation-oriented anuśayas

- **mārgālambana** — having the path as cognitive support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya relational formulation
  - **function:** fixes the object-range of path-oriented anuśayas

- **dharmajñānapakṣa** — the dharmajñāna side/class of the path
  - **status:** technically decisive here; fully developed later in the Path/Jñāna chapters
  - **function:** six-level path object for desire-domain afflictions

- **anvayajñānapakṣa** — the anvayajñāna side/class of the path
  - **status:** technically decisive here
  - **function:** nine-level path object for form/formless afflictions

- **pratipakṣa** — counteragent / opposing factor
  - **status:** previously encountered technical category
  - **function:** explains why a path belongs to the object-range relevant to a domain

- **anyo 'nyahetuka** — one being cause of another / mutually causal in sequence
  - **status:** Kārikā term expanded by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** grounds the wider object-range of mārga

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.15 sharply distinguishes the ontological and practical structure of `nirodha` from `mārga`.

```text
nirodha
    own-level
    local
    determinate termination

mārga
    multi-level
    causally articulated
    capable of broader object-range
```

The distinction is not accidental. A cessation belongs to the exact level whose affliction has ceased. A path moment, however, can condition another path moment and therefore belongs to a wider internal system of liberative causation.

This gives a much more precise sense to the uncontaminated objects introduced in 5.14.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass translation left `ṣaḍbhūmi` and `navabhūmika` intentionally unresolved. The Bhāṣya identifies them precisely.

A better canonical translation is:

> Cessation is the cessation of the affliction's own level. For desire-domain anuśayas taking the path as object, the six-level path on the side of dharmajñāna is their support; for form- and formless-domain anuśayas, the nine-level path on the side of anvayajñāna is their support, because one path can stand as cause for another.

---

## Logical Determination

The verse presents two different forms of relation to an uncontaminated object.

```text
cessation relation
    indexical identity with the level ceased

path relation
    causal mediation across path moments
```

Thus:

```text
uparama
    is determined through what terminates

mārga
    is determined through causal succession and counteraction
```

The path is not merely the negation of affliction. It has an internal causal architecture.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The cleanest contact point is the distinction between a terminal state and a generative process:

```text
Cessation
    target-specific termination

Path
    causally linked sequence of transformations
```

For later Rational Kośa modeling, `mārga` should therefore not be represented as a single scalar state. Its moments have domain, level, counteragent-function, and causal relations to other path moments.

This remains subordinate to the textual result.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.15 converts the bare six-/nine-level architecture into an exact object-range doctrine:

> **Cessation-oriented anuśayas take only the cessation of their own level as support. Path-oriented anuśayas have a broader range: desire-domain afflictions take the six-level dharmajñāna side of the path, while form/formless afflictions take the nine-level anvayajñāna side. This broader range is possible because path moments are causally articulated with one another.**
