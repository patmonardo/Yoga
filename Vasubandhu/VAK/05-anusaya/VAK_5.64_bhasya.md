## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The passage is direct exposition. It defines the technical use of *parijñā* and then enumerates the first six of nine *prahāṇa-parijñās*.

---

## Translation and Argument

The transition from VAK 5.63 is important. There Vasubandhu stated that the **same visaṃyoga** receives the designation *parijñā* at different path-stages. He now explains that *parijñā* itself is used in two senses:

1. **jñāna-parijñā** — complete discrimination as knowledge;
2. **prahāṇa-parijñā** — complete discrimination as abandonment.

The text states:

> `jñānaparijñā sāsravaṃ jñānam`

and

> `prahāṇaparijñā tu prahāṇameva`

The second is literally the abandonment itself. Vasubandhu explains the terminology by:

> `phale hetūpacārāt`

The designation belonging to the cause is applied to its result. Thus the abandonment is called *parijñā* because it is the result of the complete discriminative knowing that produces it.

The question then arises: if abandonment is called *parijñā*, is all abandonment simply one parijñā? Vasubandhu answers no:

> `parijñā nava`

There are nine.

VAK 5.64 gives the first six, all belonging to **darśana-prahātavya** afflictions.

### Desire-domain

The afflictions abandoned through seeing **duḥkha** and **samudaya** together constitute the first parijñā:

```text
kāmadhātu
  duḥkha-darśana-heya
  + samudaya-darśana-heya
      → 1 parijñā
```

The afflictions abandoned through seeing **nirodha** constitute the second:

```text
kāmadhātu
  nirodha-darśana-heya
      → 1 parijñā
```

The afflictions abandoned through seeing **mārga** constitute the third:

```text
kāmadhātu
  mārga-darśana-heya
      → 1 parijñā
```

Hence three desire-domain parijñās.

### Higher domains

The same threefold division applies collectively to the form and formless domains:

```text
rūpa + ārūpya
  duḥkha + samudaya darśana-heya
      → 1 parijñā

  nirodha darśana-heya
      → 1 parijñā

  mārga darśana-heya
      → 1 parijñā
```

Hence another three.

The Bhāṣya concludes explicitly:

```text
all three-domain darśana-heya abandonments
    → 6 parijñās
```

VAK 5.65 supplies the final three, belonging to the cultivation side.

---

## New Technical Terms

### jñāna-parijñā

- **provisional meaning:** complete discrimination as knowledge
- **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the term *parijñā*
- **function:** distinguishes the cognitive cause from the abandonment-result

The source defines it as:

> `sāsravaṃ jñānam`

This wording should be retained as transmitted; its exact doctrinal implications can be revisited in the clarification pass.

### prahāṇa-parijñā

- **provisional meaning:** complete discrimination as accomplished abandonment
- **status:** central technical use in VAK 5.64-5.65
- **function:** names completed configurations of abandonment

The Bhāṣya is explicit:

> `prahāṇameva`

It is the abandonment itself.

### hetūpacāra

- **provisional meaning:** figurative transfer of the cause-name to the result
- **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory terminology in this passage
- **function:** explains why an abandonment can itself be designated *parijñā*

### prakāra-dvaya

- **provisional meaning:** the two seeing-abandonable classes indexed to duḥkha and samudaya
- **status:** Kārikā term fixed by Bhāṣya
- **function:** identifies the paired class whose complete abandonment counts as one parijñā

## Doctrinal Determination

The main correction to the first-pass interpretation is terminological and structural.

### 1. The nine are not nine cognitions

The present enumeration concerns **prahāṇa-parijñā**, not nine acts of knowing.

```text
jñāna-parijñā
    cognitive cause

prahāṇa-parijñā
    abandonment-result
```

The same word is used across the causal relation by *hetūpacāra*.

### 2. Not every individual abandonment is a separate parijñā

The designation is attached to a completed doctrinal grouping.

For seeing-abandonments the grouping is:

```text
{duḥkha, samudaya}
{nirodha}
{mārga}
```

within the relevant domain-range.

### 3. The first six are exactly the darśana-heya completions

```text
kāmadhātu        → 3
rūpa + ārūpya    → 3
--------------------
total             6
```

The verse does not yet explain why duḥkha and samudaya are grouped together while nirodha and mārga are separate. The Bhāṣya here states the classification but does not provide an explicit rationale for that asymmetry. That question should remain open for the later clarifying pass rather than being supplied speculatively.

---

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā translation should make clear that the first six *parijñās* are completed abandonments, not merely acts of knowing.

Preferred translation:

> **There are nine parijñās. In the desire-domain, the complete abandonment of the first two classes [those abandoned through seeing suffering and origin] is one; with the abandonment of the other two [cessation and Path], there are two more. Likewise above, there are precisely three.**

---

## Logical Determination

The relation discovered in 5.63 becomes more precise here:

```text
visaṃyoga
    may be one and the same

but

its completed acquisition under a doctrinally delimited abandonment
    receives a parijñā designation
```

The decisive distinction is therefore:

```text
individual kleśa-destruction
    ≠
completed abandonment-class
    ≠
parijñā-designation
```

The classification is indexed by at least:

```text
truth-class
    ×
domain-range
    ×
mode of abandonment
```

For 5.64 the mode is uniformly **darśana-prahāṇa**.

A further relation is now explicit:

```text
cause-name
    → transferred to result
```

through `hetūpacāra`.

This is a semantic relation, not a claim that knowledge and abandonment are identical.

---

## Organon Contact Point

Minimal for the first pass:

```text
Cognitive cause
    ≠
accomplished result
```

Yet the result can inherit the cause's designation when the causal relation is doctrinally fixed.

Likewise, a completed practical determination is not counted at the level of every atomic event. It is counted when a defined class of removals has reached completion.

No further Organon synthesis is required here before the chapter-level clarification pass.

## Bhāṣya Questions Carried Forward

1. Why exactly are duḥkha- and samudaya-darśana-heya abandonments grouped as one parijñā?
2. Why are nirodha and mārga each counted separately?
3. Why are rūpadhātu and ārūpyadhātu combined for these three higher parijñās?
4. How does the same `visaṃyoga` of 5.63 relate precisely to the distinct parijñā designations?
5. How should `jñānaparijñā sāsravaṃ jñānam` be coordinated with later discussions of knowledge in Chapter 7?

These should remain open until the clarification pass unless later Bhāṣya passages answer them directly.
