## Literal Prose Translation

The dharmas of the three domains abandoned by seeing suffering, seeing origination, and cultivation have been stated.

As for those abandoned by seeing cessation and seeing the path: all have as their object-range their own [citta] in addition. For the dharmas abandoned by seeing cessation, their own citta is precisely the citta abandoned by seeing cessation; those dharmas are to be known as objects of that additional citta.

How so? Desire-domain dharmas abandoned by seeing cessation are objects of six vijñānas: the five stated previously and, as the additional one, the citta itself abandoned by seeing cessation. The same applies to those abandoned by seeing the path: they are objects of the previously stated five and, additionally, the citta abandoned by seeing the path itself.

The same applies to the form and formless domains. Thus dharmas abandoned by seeing cessation or the path are respectively objects of nine and eleven cittas there.

The five classes of dharmas in the three domains have now been stated.

Undefiled dharmas are objects of ten cittas: the final three types in each of the three domains—those abandoned by seeing cessation, seeing the path, and cultivation—and of undefiled citta.

Having known in this way the object-ordering of sixteen dharma-classes with sixteen citta-classes, how is the matter of anuśaya to be applied? We shall show merely an indication of the method.

## Philosophical Translation

The `piṇḍavibhāṣā` now completes its object-matrix. The cessation- and path-abandonable classes do not require a new principle; they are generated by extending the previously established object-range with one additional reflexive relation: each class is also an object of the citta belonging to its own abandonment-type.

The resulting counts are therefore systematic rather than arbitrary:

```text
desire-domain
    previous range 5
    + own corresponding citta
    = 6

form-domain
    previous range 8
    + own corresponding citta
    = 9

formless-domain
    previous range 10
    + own corresponding citta
    = 11
```

Undefiled dharmas form a distinct object-class. They are accessible to the last three citta-types of each domain—cessation-seeing, path-seeing, and cultivation—and to undefiled citta itself, giving ten admissible cognition-types.

The point of the entire construction is methodological. Once the sixteen-by-sixteen object-relation matrix is known, anuśaya questions can be solved by derivation rather than by memorizing isolated cases.

## Course of Argument

1. **Completion of the prior matrix** — 5.29-5.30 established the object-ranges for dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering, seeing origination, and cultivation.
2. **Rule of addition** — `svādhika` means: retain the prior range and add the citta belonging to the same cessation- or path-abandonment class.
3. **Desire-domain result** — each cessation/path class is object of six cittas.
4. **Form-domain result** — each is object of nine cittas.
5. **Formless-domain result** — each is object of eleven cittas.
6. **Undefiled object-class** — undefiled dharmas are objects of ten cittas: three final classes in each domain plus undefiled citta.
7. **Systematic closure** — the Bhāṣya declares the sixteen citta / sixteen dharma object-ordering complete.
8. **Transition to application** — Vasubandhu will now show how this matrix is used to answer anuśaya questions.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya converts the anuśaya problem into a finite relational system:

```text
16 citta-classes
    ×
16 dharma-classes
    →
ordered ālambana relations
```

The crucial rule in 5.31 is not merely a count but a derivation:

```text
cessation/path class
    = prior admissible object-range
    + corresponding own citta
```

Thus the doctrinal system is compressed by reusable operations. The counts 6, 9, and 11 are consequences of a rule rather than independent facts.

Undefiled dharmas also receive a typed object-range. Purity does not imply indiscriminate accessibility:

```text
anāsrava object
    → nirodha-darśana citta
    → mārga-darśana citta
    → bhāvanā-heya citta
across all three domains
    + anāsrava citta
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass rendering of `svādhika` as "one's own plus the further admissible category" was directionally correct, but the Bhāṣya now makes the operation exact:

> `svādhika` = the previously established range **plus the citta of the same cessation- or path-abandonment class**.

The second half is also sharpened: `antyatraya` means specifically the three final citta-types in each domain—cessation-seeing, path-seeing, and cultivation.

## Logical Determination

The logical advance is from classification to executable derivation.

```text
BaseRange(ObjectClass)
    + OwnCorrespondingCitta
    → ExtendedRange(ObjectClass)
```

The system therefore contains at least three levels:

```text
1. type the dharma
2. derive its admissible citta-range
3. use that range to determine possible anuśaya relations
```

This is stronger than a taxonomy. It is a rule-governed calculus over typed relations.

The Bhāṣya itself marks the transition explicitly:

```text
ālambana-vyavasthā known
    → anuśaya-kārya can be applied
```

The relation between knowledge and application is therefore internal to the exposition.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.31 completes the `piṇḍavibhāṣā` object-matrix. `svādhika` is not a vague enlargement but an exact additive rule. Cessation- and path-abandonable dharmas have object-ranges of 6, 9, and 11 cittas in the desire, form, and formless domains respectively. Undefiled dharmas are objects of 10 cittas. The Bhāṣya then declares the sixteen-citta / sixteen-dharma `ālambana-vyavasthā` complete and turns toward examples showing how this matrix generates answers about anuśaya.
