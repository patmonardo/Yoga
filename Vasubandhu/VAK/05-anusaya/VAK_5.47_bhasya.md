## Source Caution

The working e-text explicitly warns that it is not a critical edition. In this passage it transmits forms such as `āhlīkya` and `bhrakṣa`, while the Kārikā tradition and standard terminology point to `āhrīkya` and `mrakṣa`. These are preserved as transmitted in the source excerpt and not silently normalized.

The Kārikā itself has `uddhavaḥ`; the prose explanation gives the expected technical form `auddhatya`.

## Translation

Here, however, we shall explain only those [upakleśas] included among the envelopments and the stains of affliction.

What, then, are the envelopments?

The afflictions themselves can also be called envelopments here, as is shown by the sūtra statement concerning suffering conditioned by the envelopment of desire.

But in the *Prakaraṇaśāstra* [the list is]: shamelessness, lack of moral concern, envy, avarice, excitation, remorse, torpor, and sleepiness. Thus envelopment is eightfold.

According to the Vaibhāṣika method, however, there are ten envelopments: these eight together with anger and concealment.

Shamelessness and lack of moral concern have already been explained. Envy is irritation of mind at another's excellence or success. Avarice is a gripping of mind that opposes giving Dharma, material goods, or skill. Excitation is the non-pacification of mind. Remorse and torpor have already been explained. Sleepiness is a contraction of mind that renders the body incapable of sustaining itself; only its afflicted form is an envelopment.

Anger is hostility toward beings or non-beings, excluding the more specific forms of ill will and harmfulness. Concealment is the covering up of a fault.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu reporting scriptural and Abhidharma classifications; Vaibhāṣika attribution explicit for the tenfold list.**

VAK 5.47 does not simply establish a single universally accepted list of eight. The Bhāṣya distinguishes three levels.

### 1. `paryavasthāna` can be broader than the eightfold list

The opening remark says that even the kleśas themselves may be called `paryavasthāna`, supported by a sūtra expression concerning the envelopment of desire.

Thus:

```text
paryavasthāna
    is not intrinsically restricted
    to eight derivative factors
```

This continues the classificatory lesson of VAK 5.46: one dharma may fall under several designations according to the operative criterion.

### 2. The Prakaraṇaśāstra gives eight

The eight are:

```text
āhrīkya
anapatrāpya
īrṣyā
mātsarya
auddhatya
kaukṛtya
styāna
middha
```

The Kārikā is therefore reproducing a specific Abhidharma enumeration.

### 3. The Vaibhāṣika system gives ten

The Bhāṣya then states explicitly:

```text
vaibhāṣika-nyāyena
    according to the Vaibhāṣika method
```

there are ten, adding:

```text
krodha
mrakṣa
```

This is important because VAK 5.42 had already appealed to the same tenfold alternative when testing the proposed grounds for separately counting envy and avarice as fetters.

The eightfold and tenfold lists are therefore rival or differently scoped enumerations within the transmitted Abhidharma field; they must not be flattened into one list.

## Definitions Supplied by the Bhāṣya

### Īrṣyā

```text
parasaṃpattau cetaso vyāroṣa
```

Irritation or vexation of mind at another's excellence/prosperity.

This confirms the relational direction already suggested in the Kārikā pass, but now textually:

```text
another's success
    → mental vexation
```

### Mātsarya

```text
dharmāmiṣakauśalapradānavirodhī cittāgraha
```

A gripping or holding of mind opposed to giving Dharma, material goods, or skill.

This is considerably more determinate than generic “possessive withholding.” The Bhāṣya identifies three things whose giving is obstructed:

```text
Dharma
material goods
skill / expertise
```

### Auddhatya

```text
cetaso 'vyupaśama
```

Non-pacification of mind.

This is better than treating it primarily as “elevation.” Its defining mark here is lack of settled pacification.

### Middha

```text
kāyasaṃdhāraṇāsamarthaḥ cittābhisaṃkṣepaḥ
```

A contraction of mind that makes the body unable to sustain itself.

The Bhāṣya then imposes an important restriction:

```text
kliṣṭam eva
```

Only afflicted sleepiness counts as `paryavasthāna`.

Thus:

```text
middha
    ≠ always paryavasthāna

kliṣṭa-middha
    = paryavasthāna
```

### Krodha and Mrakṣa

Although formally added in the opening of the next Kārikā segment, they belong naturally to the present enumeration question because they are the two items that make the Vaibhāṣika count ten.

`krodha` is a general striking/hostile reaction toward beings or non-beings, explicitly distinguished from the more determinate `vyāpāda` and `vihiṃsā`.

`mrakṣa` is concealment of one's fault:

```text
avadya-pracchādana
```

## New Technical Terms

- **vaibhāṣika-nyāya** — “the Vaibhāṣika method/system.” **Status:** explicit school attribution. **Function:** marks the tenfold enumeration as specifically Vaibhāṣika.
- **parasaṃpatti** — another's excellence, success, or prosperity. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary. **Function:** object-condition of envy.
- **cittavyāroṣa** — vexation/irritation of mind. **Status:** Bhāṣya definition. **Function:** defines envy.
- **dharmāmiṣakauśalapradāna** — giving of Dharma, material goods, and skill. **Status:** Bhāṣya-specific enumeration. **Function:** specifies what avarice obstructs.
- **cittāgraha** — gripping/holding of mind. **Status:** Bhāṣya definition. **Function:** defines avarice.
- **cetaso 'vyupaśama** — non-pacification of mind. **Status:** Bhāṣya definition. **Function:** defines auddhatya.
- **cittābhisaṃkṣepa** — contraction of mind. **Status:** Bhāṣya definition. **Function:** defines middha.
- **avadya-pracchādana** — concealment of a fault. **Status:** Bhāṣya definition. **Function:** defines mrakṣa.

## Doctrinal Determination

The main doctrinal result is classificatory restraint.

```text
paryavasthāna
    broader functional designation

Prakaraṇaśāstra enumeration
    eight

Vaibhāṣika enumeration
    ten
```

Therefore the verse's eightfold list is not the definition of the full extension of the word `paryavasthāna` in every textual context.

This also corrects the first-pass claim that VAK 5.47 straightforwardly represents a movement:

```text
kleśa → upakleśa → manifest occupation
```

The Bhāṣya does not define `paryavasthāna` here through a distinct ontological stage of manifestation. Indeed it explicitly allows kleśas themselves to be called paryavasthānas. The safe determination is functional/classificatory rather than genetic.

## Effect on Kārikā Interpretation

Three corrections are required.

1. **Do not preserve the four-pair architecture as if supplied by the text.** The Bhāṣya enumerates and defines the factors but does not arrange them as four doctrinal pairs.
2. **Normalize `uddhava` only cautiously in interpretation.** The prose clearly defines `auddhatya`; the transmitted Kārikā reading should still be preserved as source data.
3. **Do not define paryavasthāna simply as the manifest stage of an upakleśa.** The term has a broader extension in the Bhāṣya.

Preferred translation remains:

> Shamelessness, lack of moral concern, envy, avarice, excitation, remorse, torpor, and sleepiness: the envelopments are eightfold.

But the commentary must immediately add that this is the *Prakaraṇaśāstra* enumeration, while the Vaibhāṣikas count ten.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.47 reinforces a central lesson of this block:

```text
term
    ≠ fixed extension independent of context
```

Instead:

```text
scriptural usage
    may include kleśas themselves

Prakaraṇa classification
    selects eight

Vaibhāṣika classification
    selects ten
```

So the correct logical form is contextual classification:

```text
classifiedAs(x, Paryavasthana, textualSystem)
```

not a single context-free equivalence:

```text
Paryavasthana ≡ exactlyEightFactors
```

This is not relativism. Each enumeration has an explicit textual or doctrinal ground.

The definitions also show how classification becomes intensional. Each item is distinguished by a specific operation:

```text
envy
    vexation at another's excellence

avarice
    resistance to giving

excitation
    non-pacification

sleepiness
    afflicted contraction producing bodily incapacity
```

Thus the list gains intelligibility from operative marks rather than from mere names.

## Organon Contact Point

For the Platform, VAK 5.47 strongly favors separating:

```text
Concept
ClassificationScheme
MembershipAssertion
DefiningOperation
```

The same concept can have different extensions under different authoritative schemes without contradiction when the scheme is made explicit.

Example:

```text
PrakaranaScheme:
    Paryavasthana = 8-member enumeration

VaibhasikaScheme:
    Paryavasthana = 10-member enumeration
```

The important thing is not to resolve the difference prematurely but to represent the mediation that produces each count.

This is consonant with the project's larger principle: identity is not fixed first and relation added afterward. Here the determinate extension of `paryavasthāna` becomes explicit only through its relation to a textual/doctrinal system.
