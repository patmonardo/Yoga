# VAK 5.70 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

### IAST

tāsāṃ saṃkalanaṃ dhātu-vairāgya-phala-lābhataḥ |
ekāṃ dve pañca ṣaṭ kaścij jahāty āpnoti pañca na || 5.70 ||

### Translation

Their consolidation occurs through dispassion toward a domain and through attainment of a fruit. A certain practitioner relinquishes one, two, five, or six [parijñās]; one acquires one, two, or six, but not five.

## Translation

Why, then, is only one parijñā established for the non-returner and for the arhat, rather than several?

Because:

> **Their consolidation occurs through dispassion toward a domain and through attainment of a fruit.**

The consolidation of the parijñās — their establishment as one — occurs for two reasons: because of dispassion toward a domain and because of attainment of a fruit. At those two stages both of these occur. Therefore, having gathered together all the abandonments, one parijñā is designated.

Now, who relinquishes or acquires how many parijñās?

> **A certain practitioner relinquishes one, two, five, or six; one acquires [one, two, or six], but not five.**

One parijñā is relinquished by one who falls away from arhatship or from dispassion toward the desire-domain.

Two parijñās are relinquished by a non-returner who is free from passion for form when that practitioner falls away from dispassion toward the desire-domain.

Five are relinquished at path-anvayajñāna by one who had previously become free from passion. For on acquiring the parijñā of abandonment of the lower-part fetters, that practitioner relinquishes the previous five parijñās.

Six parijñās are relinquished by one proceeding in regular sequence when dispassion toward the desire-domain is attained.

The same pattern applies to acquisition. Some acquire one parijñā — whoever acquires one not previously possessed. Some acquire two — one who falls away solely from dispassion toward the formless domain. Some acquire six — one who falls away from the fruit of non-returning. But no one acquires five.

Thus the discussion of parijñā is concluded.

And with this, the fifth Kośa section, called the **Exposition of the Latent Afflictions (Anuśayanirdeśa)**, is completed.

## Argument

The Bhāṣya begins from the apparent puzzle left by VAK 5.69:

```text
Why does the non-returner possess only one parijñā?
Why does the arhat possess only one parijñā?
Why are the preceding completions no longer counted separately?
```

Vasubandhu's answer is the technical notion **saṃkalana**.

```text
saṃkalana
    = ekatvena vyavasthāpana
    = establishment as one
```

Two conditions cause this consolidation:

```text
dhātu-vairāgya
    dispassion toward an entire domain

phala-prāpti / phala-lābha
    attainment of a Path-fruit
```

At the relevant stages, these two conditions coincide:

```text
tayoścāvasthayor etad ubhayaṃ bhavati
```

Therefore:

```text
sarvaṃ prahāṇaṃ saṃkalayya
    all the abandonments are gathered together

→ ekā parijñā ucyate
    one parijñā is designated
```

This does **not** mean that the earlier abandonments are undone. It means that the technical designation of currently possessed parijñās is reorganized at a new stage of completion.

## Exact Loss Cases

The Bhāṣya gives the four possible relinquishment counts.

### One relinquished

```text
arhatship → fall
or
kāmavairāgya → fall

=> relinquishes 1 parijñā
```

The text states:

```text
ekāṃ tyajati arhattvāt kāmavairāgyād vā parihīyamāṇaḥ
```

### Two relinquished

A non-returner who is already free from passion for form, but falls away from desire-domain dispassion, relinquishes two parijñās.

```text
anāgāmin
+ rūpavītarāga
+ fall from kāmavairāgya

=> relinquishes 2
```

### Five relinquished

A practitioner already free from passion before entering the noble sequence relinquishes five at **mārga-anvayajñāna**.

The reason is explicit:

```text
avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa-parijñā is acquired
    ↓
previous five parijñās are relinquished
```

This is the clearest instance of consolidation in the verse.

### Six relinquished

One who proceeds in regular sequence (`ānupūrvika`) relinquishes six upon attainment of dispassion toward the desire-domain.

```text
6 separately counted seeing-parijñās
    ↓
kāmavairāgya
    ↓
consolidation under a new completion
```

## Exact Gain Cases

Vasubandhu then says:

```text
lābhe 'py evam eva
```

The same general kind of accounting applies to acquisition.

### One acquired

Anyone who acquires a parijñā not previously possessed may acquire one.

The source is deliberately general:

```text
yaḥ kaścid apūrvā labhate
```

### Two acquired

One who falls away **only** from dispassion toward the formless domain acquires two.

This is a technical change in current possession-state, not a fresh destruction of previously destroyed afflictions.

### Six acquired

One who falls away from the **fruit of non-returning** acquires six.

### Five never acquired

The text is categorical:

```text
pañca tu naiva kaścid labhate
```

No one acquires five.

Thus five can occur as a set that is relinquished, but not as a newly acquired possession-set.

This asymmetry is part of the exact path-state accounting.

## Doctrinal Determination

The closing verse confirms three different things that must not be collapsed:

```text
prahāṇa
    abandonment itself

parijñā
    technical designation of a completed abandonment

parijñā-samanvāgama
    present possession of one or more such designations
```

And from VAK 5.63:

```text
kṣaya
    destruction occurs once

visaṃyoga-lābha
    acquisition of disconnection may recur
```

VAK 5.70 now adds:

```text
parijñā-count
    can be reorganized by saṃkalana
```

So the same liberative history has several distinguishable relations:

```text
affliction destroyed
    ≠
disconnection acquired
    ≠
completed abandonment designated
    ≠
current parijñā-set possessed
```

This is the mature form of the multi-axial logic that has emerged throughout Chapter 5.

## Saṃkalana

The safest translation is **consolidation** or **collection into one**.

The Bhāṣya itself defines it:

```text
parijñānāṃ saṃkalanaṃ
    = ekatvena vyavasthāpanam
```

Therefore we should not over-interpret `saṃkalana` as ontological fusion of dharmas. The source establishes a **technical reclassification of completed abandonments as one parijñā** when the proper path conditions are reached.

This distinction should be retained for the second clarification pass:

```text
saṃkalana
    = designation/accounting relation
not necessarily
    = destruction of prior prāptis
```

The present passage does not itself settle the deeper ontology of what happens to every earlier `prāpti`.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃkalana** — consolidation; collection of prior abandonments into a single parijñā-designation.
  - **status:** technically decisive in this closing verse.
  - **function:** explains why several earlier parijñās are no longer separately counted at domain-dispassion and fruit-attainment.

- **ekatvena vyavasthāpana** — establishment as one.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss of `saṃkalana`.
  - **function:** gives the exact sense of consolidation.

- **dhātu-vairāgya** — dispassion toward a domain.
  - **status:** previously encountered conceptually; here used as a formal trigger of parijñā-consolidation.
  - **function:** one of the two causes of `saṃkalana`.

- **phala-prāpti / phala-lābha** — attainment of a fruit.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** second cause of `saṃkalana`.

- **parihīyamāṇa** — falling away, declining from an attainment.
  - **status:** important here for loss/gain accounting.
  - **function:** marks transitions in current possession without implying fresh destruction of the same kleśa.

- **ānupūrvika** — one proceeding in regular sequence.
  - **status:** path-person classification.
  - **function:** identifies the practitioner who relinquishes six upon desire-domain dispassion.

- **vītarāgapūrvī** — one previously free from passion.
  - **status:** path-person classification.
  - **function:** identifies the practitioner who relinquishes five at `mārga-anvayajñāna`.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā translation should preserve:

```text
saṃkalana
    = consolidation
```

and should not imply that earlier liberative effects are reversed when parijñās are relinquished.

The Bhāṣya also fixes the elliptical second half precisely:

```text
relinquishment counts = 1, 2, 5, 6
acquisition counts    = 1, 2, 6
acquisition of 5      = impossible
```

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.70 closes the chapter with a **state-transition rule over attained classifications**.

```text
Path-state A
    possesses parijñā-set P

transition:
    dhātu-vairāgya
    or phala-lābha

saṃkalana
    re-establishes the relevant abandonments
    under a new current parijñā-count
```

The invariant is not the count.

The invariant is the accomplished abandonment.

```text
stable:
    prahāṇa-effect

variable:
    parijñā designation
    current possession-count
```

This is why:

```text
5 may be relinquished
but never newly acquired
```

The path-state graph is asymmetric because the permitted transitions are structured by the order of realization, not by arbitrary numerical movement.

## Minimal Organon Contact Point

One strong but restrained lesson is enough:

```text
historical achievement
    ≠
current canonical state-description
```

A later state may **reclassify** several earlier completed results under one more comprehensive designation without invalidating the earlier results.

For any later formal model, at least these must remain distinct:

```text
AbandonmentEvent
DisconnectionAcquisition
CompletionDesignation
CurrentPossession
PathTransition
```

No stronger Organon synthesis is required at this stage.

## Chapter 5 Closure

The Bhāṣya closes explicitly:

```text
samāptaḥ parijñāprasaṅgaḥ
```

“The discussion of parijñā is concluded.”

Then:

```text
abhidharmakośabhāṣye 'nuśayanirddeśo nāma
pañcamaṃ kośasthānaṃ samāptam
```

“The fifth Kośa section in the Abhidharmakośabhāṣya, called the Exposition of the Latent Afflictions, is completed.”

The chapter ends with a striking progression:

```text
anuśaya
    latent afflictive disposition

kleśa / upakleśa
    manifest and derivative affliction

prahāṇa
    abandonment

pratipakṣa
    counteragent

visaṃyoga
    disconnection

parijñā
    completed abandonment under technical designation

saṃkalana
    consolidation of completed abandonments
    at domain-dispassion and fruit-attainment
```

Thus the Anuśayanirdeśa ends not by adding another affliction, but by giving the formal rules under which liberation from affliction is **acquired, completed, counted, lost as a designation, and consolidated**.

That is the proper closing determination of Chapter 5.
