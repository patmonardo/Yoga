# VAK 5.57 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.57**

> daurmanasyena kaukṛtyam īrṣyā krodho vihiṃsanam /
> upanāhaḥ pradāśaś ca mātsaryaṃ tu viparyayāt // 5.57 //

**Best translation**

Regret, envy, anger, harmfulness, rancor, and obstinate indignation are associated with mental distress; avarice, however, with the contrary, namely mental gladness.

See: [VAK_5.57.md](./VAK_5.57.md)

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan-based e-text, pp. 317.09–317.14:

> upakleśānāṃ punaḥ
>
> daurmanasyena kaukṛtyamīrṣyā krodho vihiṃsanam /

>

The transmitted prose has **upanāhaṃ** where the Kārikā has nominative **upanāhaḥ**; this is preserved rather than silently normalized.

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

As for the secondary afflictions:

Regret, envy, anger, harmfulness, rancor, and obstinate indignation are associated with mental distress.

The expression “are associated” is to be supplied. This is because these operate in the mode of dejection (*dainyākāra*) and because they belong to the mental plane (*manobhūmika*).

Avarice, however, is the reverse. That is to say, it is associated with mental gladness. Because it belongs to the lineage of greed (*lobhānvaya*), it operates in a mode of exhilaration (*harṣākāra*).

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 5.55–5.56 completed the association of the principal kleśas with the feeling faculties. VAK 5.57 now begins the corresponding determination for the upakleśas.

The Bhāṣya divides the factors by the affective mode in which they operate:

```text
dainyākāra
    mode of dejection
        ↓
kaukṛtya
īrṣyā
krodha
vihiṃsā
upanāha
pradāśa
        ↓
saṃprayoga with daurmanasya
```

By contrast:

```text
mātsarya
    ↓
lobhānvaya
    lineage of greed
    ↓
harṣākāra
    mode of exhilaration
    ↓
saṃprayoga with saumanasya
```

The contrast is therefore not inferred merely from the modern psychological meanings of the terms. Vasubandhu explicitly gives two mediating determinations:

```text
dainyākāra → daurmanasya
harṣākāra → saumanasya
```

and, for mātsarya, explicitly connects the latter to its **lobha-lineage**.

## Doctrinal Determination

### The six associated with daurmanasya

The six are:

1. **kaukṛtya** — regret/remorse
2. **īrṣyā** — envy
3. **krodha** — anger
4. **vihiṃsā** — harmfulness
5. **upanāha** — rancor / retained hostility
6. **pradāśa** — obstinate indignation / refusal of correction

The Bhāṣya does not derive their feeling-association separately from each individual definition. It gives a common reason:

> **dainyākāravartitvāt** — because they operate in a mode of dejection.

It adds:

> **manobhūmikatvāc ca** — and because they belong to the mental plane.

Thus **daurmanasya** here is specifically mental painful feeling, and these factors are compatible with it because their operation is mental and dejected in form.

### Mātsarya as the contrary case

The Kārikā says only **viparyayāt**, “by the contrary.” The Bhāṣya resolves the ellipsis explicitly:

> **saumanasyenety arthaḥ** — the meaning is: with mental gladness.

It then supplies the genetic/classificatory reason:

> **lobhānvayatvena harṣākāravartitvāt** — because, as belonging to the lineage of greed, it operates in a mode of exhilaration.

This relation should be preserved carefully:

```text
lobhānvaya
    explains
harṣākāra
    explains
saumanasya-saṃprayoga
```

The verse therefore combines two different relations without confusing them:

```text
anvaya
    afflictive lineage

saṃprayoga
    simultaneous association with feeling
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing literal structure is sound, but **viparyayāt** should be made explicit in translation as “with the contrary, namely mental gladness,” because the Bhāṣya states this directly.

Preferred translation:

> Regret, envy, anger, harmfulness, rancor, and obstinate indignation are associated with mental distress; avarice, however, with the contrary, namely mental gladness.

## New Technical Terms

- **dainyākāra** — “mode/form of dejection, wretchedness.”
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory terminology in this passage.
  - **function:** explains why the six listed upakleśas are associated with daurmanasya.

- **harṣākāra** — “mode/form of exhilaration.”
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory terminology.
  - **function:** explains why mātsarya is associated with saumanasya.

- **lobhānvaya** — “belonging to the lineage of greed.”
  - **status:** relation already structurally familiar from earlier anuśaya/upakleśa analyses, here applied explicitly to mātsarya.
  - **function:** provides the afflictive-lineage ground for mātsarya's exhilarated mode.

- **manobhūmika** — “belonging to the mental plane.”
  - **status:** previously encountered in the consciousness-support discussion.
  - **function:** supports the restriction of these factors to mental feeling rather than bodily pleasure/pain.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.57 provides a compact sequence of mediated determination:

```text
afflictive lineage or operative form
    ↓
affective mode
    ↓
compatible feeling
```

For the six:

```text
dainyākāra
    ↓
daurmanasya-saṃprayoga
```

For mātsarya:

```text
lobhānvaya
    ↓
harṣākāra
    ↓
saumanasya-saṃprayoga
```

The important distinction is:

```text
niḥṣyanda / anvaya
    ≠
saṃprayoga
```

The former concerns derivation or lineage; the latter concerns conjunction in a cognitive event.

This verse also demonstrates why feeling cannot serve as a direct ethical criterion. **Saumanasya**, a pleasant mental feeling, can accompany **mātsarya**, an afflictive withholding grounded in greed.

## Organon Contact Point

Minimal only, pending the planned clarification pass:

```text
lineage ≠ operative form ≠ conjunction
```

A determinate relation can mediate another relation without being identical with it. Here greed-lineage mediates an exhilarated mode, which in turn explains compatibility with mental gladness.

No larger Organon synthesis should be imposed at this stage.
