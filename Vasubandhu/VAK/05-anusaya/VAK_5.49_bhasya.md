### Source caution

The working e-text reads `kodherṣye` at [313|06-313|07], while the Kārikā file has `krodherṣye`. The Bhāṣya sense is unambiguous because the prose immediately explains the pair as `pratighasamutthe`. The source reading is therefore preserved rather than silently normalized.

The clause defining `śāṭhya` is textually compact and possibly imperfect in this e-text. Its secure core is `cittakauṭilyaṃ śāṭhyam` and the statement that, through it, one does not disclose matters as they really are. No stronger reconstruction is imposed here.

## Translation

Remorse is from doubt; anger and envy belong to hostility. That is, anger and envy **arise from hostility**. These ten are secondary afflictions that are outflows of the afflictions.

There are also six other **stains of affliction**. They are: deceit, crookedness, intoxication, obstinate resistance to correction, resentment, and harmfulness.

Among these, **deceit** is deceiving another.

**Crookedness** is crookedness of mind, by which one does not reveal things as they actually are; the remainder of the transmitted clause is textually difficult, but its governing sense is evasive or non-straightforward disclosure.

**Intoxication** has already been explained earlier.

**Pradāśa** is firm adherence to a blameworthy matter, by which one does not accept proper admonition or reasoned instruction.

**Upanāha** is the repeated intensification of a basis of resentment or injury.

**Vihiṃsā** is harming: through it one injures others by striking, harshness, and the like.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / reported Abhidharma classification**

### 1. The genealogy of the ten paryavasthānas is completed

VAK 5.48 assigned three factors to attachment and three to ignorance. VAK 5.49 supplies the remaining determinations:

```text
vicikitsā
    ↓
kaukṛtya

pratigha
    ↓
krodha
īrṣyā
```

The prose makes the second relation stronger than the Kārikā's compact `pratighānvaye` might initially suggest:

```text
pratighasamutthe
    arisen from hostility
```

Then Vasubandhu gathers the entire preceding analysis under one statement:

```text
ete ca daśa
kleśaniḥṣyandā upakleśāḥ
```

That is:

```text
the ten paryavasthānas
    are
upakleśas that are niḥṣyandas of kleśas
```

This is the clean closure of the local genetic analysis begun in VAK 5.48.

### 2. A new subclass of upakleśa is introduced: kleśa-mala

The text then changes classification:

```text
anye ca ṣaṭ kleśamalāḥ
    there are also six other stains of affliction
```

The six are:

```text
māyā       deceit
śāṭhya     crookedness / duplicity
mada       intoxication
pradāśa    obstinate resistance to correction
upanāha    resentment / sustained grudge
vihiṃsā    harmfulness
```

The Kārikā names only the first three in 5.49, but the Bhāṣya immediately gives the full six and defines them before VAK 5.50 begins their root-affiliation. Preserving that natural argument avoids an artificial verse-boundary cut.

### 3. The stains are defined by concrete operations

The definitions are strikingly practical.

```text
māyā
    deceives another

śāṭhya
    bends or conceals truthful disclosure

pradāśa
    refuses proper correction while firmly holding a blameworthy matter

upanāha
    repeatedly enlarges a basis of resentment

vihiṃsā
    injures another through striking, harshness, and similar conduct
```

This is not yet the genetic derivation of the six—that belongs principally to VAK 5.50. Here the text establishes **what each stain does**.

## New Technical Terms

- **kleśa-niḥṣyanda** — an outflow or derivative of an affliction.
  - **status:** `niḥṣyanda` becomes explicitly operative across VAK 5.48-5.49.
  - **function:** genetic relation connecting a root kleśa to a derivative upakleśa.

- **pratigha-samuttha** — arisen from hostility.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss of the Kārikā's `pratighānvaya`.
  - **function:** strengthens a lineage-expression into an origination relation.

- **kleśa-mala** — stain of affliction.
  - **status:** new classificatory heading.
  - **function:** names a sixfold group of upakleśas treated after the paryavasthānas.

- **paravañcanā** — deceiving another.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only definition of `māyā` here.
  - **function:** definition.

- **citta-kauṭilya** — crookedness of mind.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only definition of `śāṭhya` here.
  - **function:** definition.

- **sāvadyavastu-dṛḍhagrāhitā** — firm grasping of a blameworthy matter.
  - **status:** new explanatory compound.
  - **function:** definition of `pradāśa`.

- **nyāya-saṃjñapti** — proper / reasoned admonition or representation.
  - **status:** new in this local discussion.
  - **function:** names what the person governed by `pradāśa` refuses to accept.

- **āghātavastu-vahulīkāra** — repeated enlargement or intensification of a basis of resentment/injury.
  - **status:** new explanatory compound.
  - **function:** definition of `upanāha`.

- **viheṭhana** — harming, injuring, harassing.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definition of `vihiṃsā`.
  - **function:** definition / practical manifestation.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.49 closes one classificatory operation and opens another.

First:

```text
10 paryavasthānas
    ↓
all are kleśa-niḥṣyanda upakleśas
```

This confirms that the genetic language introduced locally in 5.48 applies to the whole tenfold Vaibhāṣika paryavasthāna set.

Second:

```text
upakleśa field
    ↓
another six
    ↓
kleśa-malas
```

The text does not yet explain *why* the six deserve the special name `mala`; it defines them and then, in VAK 5.50, traces their roots. We should therefore resist prematurely converting `mala` into a fully theorized level such as "externalized stain" or "behavioral residue." Those may later prove useful, but the present passage secures only the classification and definitions.

The six definitions nevertheless show an important practical tendency: several concern how affliction deforms truthful relation, correction, resentment, and treatment of others.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and revision required.**

The first-pass rendering of `pratighānvaye` as merely "belonging to the lineage of hostility" is too weak in light of the Bhāṣya's explicit:

```text
pratighasamutthe
```

Preferred rendering:

> Remorse arises from doubt; anger and envy arise from hostility. There are also six other stains of affliction: deceit, crookedness, intoxication, and likewise [three further stains].

The first-pass explanation of `kleśa-mala` as already denoting a stain "left in presentation and action" should remain provisional. The Bhāṣya here supplies concrete definitions but does not yet give that general theory.

## Logical Determination

The verse is a hinge between **genetic closure** and **new specification**.

```text
Root kleśas
    ↓ niḥṣyanda
Ten paryavasthāna-upakleśas
    ↓ closure

then

Upakleśa field
    ↓ new classificatory determination
Six kleśa-malas
```

This is not one undifferentiated causal chain. Two logical operations occur:

```text
derivation
    What root does this factor flow from?

classification
    Under what special heading is this factor now treated?
```

The Bhāṣya therefore keeps distinct:

```text
genetic relation
    niḥṣyanda / samuttha

class relation
    paryavasthāna / kleśa-mala
```

A dharma can be determined genetically and taxonomically without those relations being identical.

The definitions of the six stains then add a third relation:

```text
functional manifestation
    what the determination actually does
```

Thus VAK 5.49 yields a particularly useful three-relation schema:

```text
origin
class
operation
```

## Organon Contact Point

The strong Organon lesson is methodological rather than ontological:

```text
Do not collapse
    origin
    classification
    function
into one predicate.
```

For example:

```text
Krodha
    origin: pratigha
    class: upakleśa / paryavasthāna
    function: anger
```

and:

```text
Māyā
    class: kleśa-mala
    function: deceiving another
    root: to be determined in VAK 5.50
```

That separation is exactly what a rational diagnostic system needs. Knowing **what something is called**, **where it comes from**, and **what it does** are three different judgments.

The Bhāṣya is now beginning to supply all three explicitly.
