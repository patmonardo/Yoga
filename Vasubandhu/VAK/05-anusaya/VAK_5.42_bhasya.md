# VAK 5.42 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor


### Source caution

The working e-text prints `īṣryā` in several places for standard `īrṣyā`. The source reading is preserved above rather than silently normalized.

## Translation

Why, then, are envy and avarice stated separately as two fetters, while no other enveloping manifestation is so stated?

Because both are exclusively unwholesome and because each is independent, envy and avarice are stated separately among these as two fetters.

For there is no other enveloping manifestation of this same kind in which both of these features would be present: exclusive unwholesomeness and independence.

For one who accepts eight enveloping manifestations, this explanation would work.

But for one who accepts ten, anger and concealment also possess both characteristics.

Therefore, say others, this explanation does not constitute an adequate answer.

## Course of Argument

**Voice:** Vasubandhu introducing the question and reporting the standard explanation.

**Voice:** `apare` — others / critics of that explanation.

VAK 5.42 continues the problem of classification opened by 5.41, but the direction is reversed. VAK 5.41 asked why several views are grouped into two fetter-classes. Here the question is why two particular `paryavasthānas` — envy and avarice — are each promoted to independent `saṃyojana` status.

The Bhāṣya states the question explicitly:

```text
Why īrṣyā and mātsarya
    as two separate saṃyojanas,
while no other paryavasthāna is treated this way?
```

The proposed answer gives two jointly necessary marks:

```text
ekāntākuśalatva
    exclusive unwholesomeness

svatantratva
    independence
```

Thus the initial classificatory rule is:

```text
paryavasthāna
    + exclusively unwholesome
    + independent
        ↓
separately countable as saṃyojana
```

This confirms one important part of the Kārikā pass: `svatantra` is indeed a criterion of independent status in the taxonomy. But the Bhāṣya does **not** elaborate it into the stronger claims made in the first-pass file about distinct intentional direction, independent arising, or autonomous causal operation. Those may be useful later hypotheses, but they are not supplied here by Vasubandhu and should not be presented as the Bhāṣya's explanation.

### The decisive qualification: the explanation depends on the count of paryavasthānas

The commentary immediately tests the proposed distinction against rival enumerations.

If one accepts:

```text
8 paryavasthānas
```

then envy and avarice alone among them satisfy both criteria, and the explanation succeeds.

But if one accepts:

```text
10 paryavasthānas
```

then:

```text
krodha     anger
mrakṣa     concealment
```

also possess both characteristics:

```text
ekāntākuśala
svatantra
```

At that point the supposedly distinguishing marks cease to distinguish.

The Bhāṣya therefore records the objection:

```text
tasmān na bhavaty ayaṃ parihāraḥ
    therefore this is not an adequate answer
```

This is the crucial result of the verse. The Kārikā offers a classificatory reason; the Bhāṣya immediately subjects that reason to a counterexample generated from another accepted enumeration.

## New Technical Terms

- **ekāntākuśalatva** — the condition of being exclusively unwholesome.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya nominalization of the Kārikā predicate.
  - **function:** proposed criterion for separate fetter-status.

- **svatantratva** — independence.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya nominalization of the Kārikā predicate.
  - **function:** second proposed criterion for separate fetter-status.

- **evañjātīyaka** — of this same kind or character.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** states the claimed uniqueness of envy and avarice within the eightfold list.

- **parihāra** — reply, resolution, answer to an objection.
  - **status:** recurrent dialectical vocabulary.
  - **function:** evaluates whether the proposed explanation actually resolves the classificatory problem.

- **krodha** — anger.
  - **status:** already known among minor afflictions; newly relevant here as a counterexample.
  - **function:** defeats the proposed uniqueness claim under the tenfold enumeration.

- **mrakṣa** — concealment, covering-over of one's fault.
  - **status:** already known among minor afflictions; newly relevant here as a counterexample.
  - **function:** second counterexample under the tenfold enumeration.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse cannot be read simply as establishing that envy and avarice are intrinsically unique because they are exclusively unwholesome and independent.

The Bhāṣya shows instead:

```text
under an eightfold paryavasthāna-list
    the proposed marks uniquely pick out īrṣyā and mātsarya

under a tenfold paryavasthāna-list
    the same marks also pick out krodha and mrakṣa
```

Therefore the justification is **taxonomy-relative**.

This is important because it prevents us from converting a local Abhidharma classification into an absolute ontological distinction. Whether the two marks are sufficient depends upon the prior constitution of the comparison class.

The exact doctrinal issue is not whether envy and avarice are real afflictions. Their status as afflictions is not challenged. What is challenged is the adequacy of the reason given for counting them, and only them, separately among the fetters.

Thus:

```text
reality of the dharma
    ≠
adequacy of the classificatory ground
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation and First-Pass Interpretation

**The basic translation can stand**, but its interpretation must be qualified.

Preferred translation:

> Because both are exclusively unwholesome and independent, envy and avarice are stated separately among these as two fetters.

However, the first-pass philosophical translation went too far when it said that each possesses its own autonomous operation and then developed a detailed opposition between another's good and one's own withholding. None of that is stated in the Bhāṣya passage attached to 5.42.

The Bhāṣya warrants only:

```text
īrṣyā and mātsarya
    are proposed as separately counted fetters
because both are
    exclusively unwholesome
    and independent
```

followed immediately by:

```text
this justification works only on the eightfold count;
on the tenfold count it fails to discriminate uniquely.
```

The first-pass relational interpretation may remain as a later Organon hypothesis if independently supported, but it should not be presented as Vasubandhu's explanation here.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.42 is an unusually clean example of **classification by necessary-and-sufficient marks being defeated by a counterexample**.

Initial proposal:

```text
For x in Paryavasthāna:

if
    exclusivelyUnwholesome(x)
    and
    independent(x)
then
    x qualifies for separate Saṃyojana status
```

Under the eight-member domain:

```text
extension(criteria)
    = {īrṣyā, mātsarya}
```

The rule appears extensionally adequate.

Under the ten-member domain:

```text
extension(criteria)
    = {īrṣyā, mātsarya, krodha, mrakṣa}
```

The same predicate conjunction no longer yields the intended class.

Hence:

```text
criterion adequacy
    depends on
comparison domain
```

This is stronger than a mere disagreement about lists. It shows that a definition can appear satisfactory only because the domain against which it has been tested is incomplete or differently delimited.

The Bhāṣya is therefore doing genuine reflective work:

```text
proposed distinction
    ↓
comparison against the field
    ↓
counterexample
    ↓
failure of sufficiency
```

This is precisely why the Bhāṣya cannot be treated as decorative commentary on the Kārikā. It tests whether the stated determination actually holds under competing doctrinal articulations of the field.

## Organon Contact Point

The Organon lesson should be kept close to the text.

A classification rule must carry its **domain of evaluation** with it.

```text
classificationRule
    hasCriteria
    hasComparisonDomain
    hasIntendedExtension
```

A rule that selects exactly two entities in one dataset may cease to be discriminating when the dataset is enlarged.

Thus the system should distinguish:

```text
predicate truth
from
predicate discriminatory power
```

`ekāntākuśala` and `svatantra` may remain true of envy and avarice. The objection does not deny that. It shows that those truths are insufficient to explain their **exclusive** classificatory privilege once anger and concealment are admitted into the same comparison field.

This gives a very clean Rational-Kośa principle:

```text
A determination is not justified merely because its predicates are true.
Its ground must also generate the intended distinction within the complete relevant field.
```

That is a genuine development of the reflective method, while remaining internal to Vasubandhu's dialectic rather than importing an external architecture.
