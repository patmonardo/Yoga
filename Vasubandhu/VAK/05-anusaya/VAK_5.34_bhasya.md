## Literal Prose Translation

This is the order of arising of these ten kleśas. But when a kleśa arises, it arises from three causes: from an unabandoned anuśaya, from an object that has become present, and from unwise attention.

For example, when the anuśaya of desire is unabandoned and not fully known, when dharmas conducive to the manifestation of sensual desire have appeared, and when unwise attention occurs with respect to them, sensual desire arises.

These three are, respectively, the powers of cause, object, and application. Another kleśa is likewise to be understood as arising when its causal conditions are complete.

Sometimes, however, they are said to arise by the power of the object alone and not by the power of the cause—for example in an arhat whose nature is liable to fall away.

## Philosophical Translation

The ordered genesis of the ten kleśas described in 5.32–5.33 does not imply that a manifest kleśa springs from one isolated inner cause. Manifestation ordinarily requires a complete field of conditions: an unabandoned afflictive disposition, a presently available object capable of occasioning it, and an active mode of unwise attention that takes up that object defectively.

Thus latent disposition, object-presence, and cognitive application are distinct powers. Their conjunction ordinarily produces the manifest kleśa. But Vasubandhu immediately qualifies the rule: in exceptional cases an object itself may suffice to occasion a kleśa even where the ordinary causal power is absent.

## Course of Argument

1. **Transition from genesis to manifestation.**
   - 5.32–5.33 gave the `pravṛttikrama`, the ordered genesis of the ten kleśas.
   - 5.34 asks a different question: under what conditions does a kleśa actually arise?

2. **Three causal factors.**
   - `aprahīṇa-anuśaya` — an unabandoned latent affliction.
   - `pratyupasthita-viṣaya` — an object that has become present.
   - `ayoniśo manaskāra` — unwise attention directed toward that object.

3. **Concrete example: kāmarāga.**
   - the desire-anuśaya remains unabandoned and `aparijñāta`, not fully comprehended;
   - dharmas conducive to sensual desire become manifest;
   - unwise attention operates with respect to them;
   - sensual desire arises.

4. **The three powers.**
   - `hetu-bala` — power of the latent cause.
   - `viṣaya-bala` — power of the object.
   - `prayoga-bala` — power of application or cognitive engagement.

5. **Qualification of the general rule.**
   - A kleśa ordinarily arises when the causal complex is complete.
   - Yet the Bhāṣya immediately notes an exceptional case in which object-power alone can occasion arising.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — frames the general causal analysis and gives the desire example.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃpūrṇa-kāraṇa** — complete causal complex.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-level causal formulation clarified here.
  - **function:** states the normal conjunction of conditions for manifestation.

- **hetu-bala** — power of the cause.
  - **status:** new explicit causal vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** names the causal force of the unabandoned anuśaya.

- **viṣaya-bala** — power of the object.
  - **status:** new explicit causal vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** names the occasioning force of the presented object.

- **prayoga-bala** — power of application.
  - **status:** new explicit causal vocabulary in this unit.
  - **function:** names the operative force of cognitive engagement, here `ayoniśo manaskāra`.

- **aparijñāta** — not fully known or comprehended.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya qualification of the unabandoned anuśaya.
  - **function:** marks incomplete comprehension as part of the condition for manifestation.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya establishes a strict distinction between latent affliction and manifest affliction:

```text
anuśaya
    ≠
kleśa in manifestation
```

The transition normally requires three powers:

```text
hetu-bala
    unabandoned latent disposition

viṣaya-bala
    presently available object

prayoga-bala
    unwise cognitive engagement

→ manifest kleśa
```

This prevents a reduction of defilement either to an inner substance or to an external stimulus.

The object alone is not normally sufficient; nor is the latent anuśaya alone. The cognitive mode by which the object is taken up is itself causally efficacious.

At the same time, the post-verse qualification prevents us from reading the triad as an exceptionless metaphysical law. The Bhāṣya explicitly permits cases where `viṣaya-bala` can operate without the ordinary `hetu-bala`.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation correctly identifies the three causal conditions and the notion of a complete causal complex. The Bhāṣya adds that they correspond respectively to `hetu-bala`, `viṣaya-bala`, and `prayoga-bala`, and that the rule admits exceptional cases.

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a conjunctive causal schema:

```text
latent cause
    +
relevant object
    +
defective application
    →
manifest event
```

But the Bhāṣya immediately prevents a simplistic biconditional reading:

```text
complete causal complex
    normally sufficient

not:
    universally necessary in every case
```

So the logic is better represented as a default generative rule with explicitly acknowledged exceptions.

The especially important distinction is between **availability** and **activation**:

```text
anuśaya exists as an operative possibility
    ≠
kleśa is presently manifest
```

Manifestation is generated when the relevant relations become actual.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This unit gives a useful model of event-generation by a relational field rather than by a single stored state.

```text
Disposition
    × Object
    × ModeOfApplication
    → Event
```

`prayoga-bala` is especially significant: cognition is not merely passive reception of an object. The way the object is taken up contributes causally to what manifests.

For later assimilation, however, the Bhāṣya's own qualification should be preserved. The system is not a rigid three-input mechanism; object-power can sometimes override the ordinary causal pattern.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.34 shifts from the **genetic order of kleśas** to the **conditions of their actual manifestation**. Vasubandhu distinguishes three powers—cause, object, and application—and uses sensual desire as the paradigm. The result is a relational causal model in which latent disposition is activated only under determinate conditions. Yet the immediate exception concerning the fallible arhat shows that this is a normal causal schema rather than an exceptionless law.

No Dream update required at this stage; the passage is important for later assimilation of Vasubandhu's causal model, but it does not yet force a revision of the governing project principle.
