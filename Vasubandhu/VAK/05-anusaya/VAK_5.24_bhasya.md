## Literal Prose Translation

“By these future mental [afflictions], everywhere.” The qualification “wherever they have not been abandoned” continues to apply.

By these very future attachment, aversion, and conceit when they belong to the mental plane, one is connected with every object, including objects of all three times, because mental [afflictions] can take the three times as their objects.

“By the others, with what belongs to their own temporal period.” By the other future attachments and aversions, one is connected only with future objects. What are these others? Attachment and aversion belonging to the five consciousness-groups—specifically those whose nature is to arise.

“But by those not arisen, everywhere.” By those very five-consciousness-group afflictions whose nature is not to arise, one is connected with every object, even with objects of the three times.

“But by all the remaining [afflictions], everywhere.” What are the remaining ones? View, doubt, and ignorance belonging to the three times. By all of these one is connected with every object, because they are general afflictions.

The standing qualification “so long as they have not been abandoned” continues throughout.

---

## Philosophical Translation

The scope of bondage depends not merely on the name of the affliction but on its cognitive locus, temporal mode, and modal status.

Future attachment, aversion, and conceit that belong to mind-consciousness bind with respect to objects in all three times, because mind can range over past, present, and future. Future attachment and aversion associated with the five sensory consciousnesses, when they are destined actually to arise, bind only to future objects, their own temporal field. Yet if those same sensory afflictions are future but not destined to arise, their binding range is universal.

The remaining afflictions—view, doubt, and ignorance—bind universally in all three times because they are `sāmānya-kleśas`, general afflictions. In every case the rule remains restricted to afflictions not yet abandoned.

---

## Course of Argument

### 1. The rule from 5.23 continues

The Bhāṣya first carries forward the condition:

```text
yatrāprahīṇāste
```

The affliction binds only so long as it remains unabandoned.

Thus the temporal and modal expansions of 5.24 do not cancel the practical condition of abandonment.

### 2. Future mental rāga, pratigha, and māna bind universally

The first case concerns the three `svalakṣaṇa-kleśas` from 5.23 when they are:

```text
anāgata
future

manobhūmika
belonging to the mental plane
```

The Bhāṣya gives the reason explicitly:

```text
mānasānāṃ tryadhvaviṣayatvāt
```

because mental afflictions can have objects belonging to all three times.

Hence:

```text
future mental affliction
    + three-time object-range
    → universal binding-range
```

### 3. The “others” are five-consciousness-body rāga and pratigha

The Bhāṣya resolves `paraiḥ` explicitly:

```text
pañca vijñānakāyikā rāgāśca pratighāśca
```

These are attachment and aversion belonging to the five sensory consciousness-groups.

Conceit is not included here because `māna` belongs to the mental plane.

When these sensory afflictions are future **and destined to arise** (`utpattidharma`), they bind only to future objects:

```text
anāgata affliction
    → anāgata object
```

Their object-range is limited by the temporal form of the sensory consciousness through which they arise.

### 4. `ajaiḥ` means afflictions whose nature is not to arise

The Bhāṣya glosses the compact kārikā expression:

```text
ajaiḥ
    → anutpattidharmabhiḥ
```

These are future sensory attachment and aversion that will not actually arise.

Nevertheless:

```text
anutpattidharma
    ≠
non-binding
```

They bind with respect to every object, including all three temporal fields.

This is one of the strongest doctrinal results of the verse. Actual manifestation is not the criterion of bondage.

### 5. The remaining anuśayas are the general afflictions

The Bhāṣya asks directly:

```text
ke punaḥ śeṣāḥ ?
```

and answers:

```text
dṛṣṭi
vicikitsā
avidyā
```

in their past, present, and future forms.

These bind with respect to every object because:

```text
sāmānyakleśatvāt
```

because they are general afflictions.

This completes the distinction opened before 5.23:

```text
svalakṣaṇa-kleśa
    rāga
    pratigha
    māna
    object-indexed and scope-sensitive

sāmānya-kleśa
    dṛṣṭi
    vicikitsā
    avidyā
    universal binding-range
```

## New Technical Terms

- **manobhūmika** — belonging to the mental plane; here, attachment, aversion, and conceit associated with mind-consciousness.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-explicit refinement.
  - **Function:** explains the three-time object-range of future mental afflictions.

- **tryadhvaviṣayatva** — having the three times as object-range.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-explicit.
  - **Function:** causal explanation of universal binding by mental afflictions.

- **pañca-vijñāna-kāyika** — belonging to the five consciousness-groups.
  - **Status:** previously encountered doctrinal vocabulary; newly decisive here.
  - **Function:** identifies `paraiḥ` as sensory attachment and aversion.

- **utpattidharma** — having the nature of arising; destined to arise.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification in this passage.
  - **Function:** distinguishes one class of future sensory afflictions.

- **anutpattidharma** — having the nature of non-arising; not destined to arise.
  - **Status:** Bhāṣya-only clarification.
  - **Function:** glosses `ajaiḥ` and establishes that non-manifestation does not cancel bondage.

- **sāmānya-kleśa** — general affliction.
  - **Status:** introduced immediately before 5.23 and explicitly applied here.
  - **Function:** explains why view, doubt, and ignorance bind universally.

## Doctrinal Determination

### Binding scope is inherited from cognitive scope

The Bhāṣya states the principle directly for mental afflictions:

```text
mind has three-time object-range
    →
mental affliction has three-time binding-range
```

The affliction therefore cannot be analyzed independently of the cognitive domain in which it occurs.

### Future existence and future manifestation are distinct

The verse distinguishes:

```text
anāgata + utpattidharma
future and destined to arise

anāgata + anutpattidharma
future and not destined to arise
```

The second class can still bind.

Therefore:

```text
existence as future determination
    ≠
future manifestation
```

and likewise:

```text
binding efficacy
    ≠
actual occurrence
```

### General affliction is universal in object-range

The contrast with 5.23 is now complete.

The `svalakṣaṇa-kleśas` must be tracked according to particular object, consciousness-system, time, and modal destiny. The `sāmānya-kleśas`, by contrast, bind with respect to every object so long as they remain unabandoned.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation is structurally sound, but the Bhāṣya fixes three expressions more precisely:

```text
paraiḥ
    = the other rāga and pratigha,
      namely those belonging to the five sensory consciousness-groups

ajaiḥ
    = anutpattidharma,
      future afflictions not destined to arise

śeṣaiḥ
    = dṛṣṭi, vicikitsā, avidyā
      across all three times
```

The prose also confirms that `yāvad aprahīṇāḥ` from the preceding rule remains operative throughout.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.24 extends the indexed relation of 5.23 into a logic of **scope propagation**.

```text
carrier scope
    → object scope
        → binding scope
```

For mental afflictions:

```text
manas
    takes past/present/future

therefore

future mental kleśa
    can bind past/present/future objects
```

For sensory afflictions destined to arise:

```text
future sensory cognition
    takes future contemporaneous object

therefore

future sensory kleśa
    binds future object
```

But the `anutpattidharma` case blocks any simple identification of binding with occurrence:

```text
will not manifest
    yet
remains structurally binding
```

The final contrast is one of logical range:

```text
svalakṣaṇa-kleśa
    restricted / indexed relation

sāmānya-kleśa
    universal relation
```

This is not merely quantitative. It expresses two different forms of afflictive determination.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest architectural lesson is that the scope of a determination is not intrinsic to a label alone. It can be inherited from the system that carries it.

```text
state
    carriedBy module
    inherits module scope
    determines relation scope
```

The second lesson is modal:

```text
not destined to become manifest
    does not imply
structurally irrelevant
```

A latent rule, disposition, unresolved constraint, or inherited determination may remain effective even if it never becomes an occurrent event.

The Kośa therefore forces a clean separation among:

```text
exists as determination
is destined to arise
actually arises
binds
is abandoned
```

These predicates must not be collapsed.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.24 completes the contrast begun in 5.23.

The particular afflictions—attachment, aversion, and conceit—have binding ranges conditioned by object, cognitive locus, time, and future actualization. The general afflictions—view, doubt, and ignorance—bind universally across objects and times so long as they remain unabandoned.

Most importantly, the Bhāṣya explicitly distinguishes `utpattidharma` from `anutpattidharma`: an affliction may never arise as an occurrent event and nevertheless remain part of the structure of bondage.
