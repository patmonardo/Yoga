# VAK 5.63 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**IAST**

> sakṛt kṣayo visaṃyoga-lābhas teṣāṃ punaḥ punaḥ /
> pratipakṣodaya-phala-prāptīndriya-vivṛddhiṣu // 5.63 //

**Translation**

> Their destruction occurs once; but acquisition of disconnection from them occurs repeatedly—upon the arising of the counteragent, attainment of a fruit, and development of the faculties.

See: [VAK 5.63](./VAK_5.63.md)

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu

The passage begins with an explicit question and categorical rejection, then supplies the doctrinal distinction and its path conditions.

## Translation and Argument

Vasubandhu asks whether, as one proceeds through further special stages of the Path, there occurs a renewed or further abandonment of already abandoned afflictions.

His answer is unequivocal:

> **No.**

For every kleśa there is only one destruction:

```text
sakṛt kṣayaḥ
    destruction once
```

The reason is precise:

```text
for each kleśa
    its own prahāṇa-mārga
        ↓
    destroys that kleśa
```

Hence:

```text
same kleśa
    ≠ repeatedly destroyed
```

What may occur repeatedly is something different:

```text
visaṃyoga-lābha
    acquisition of disconnection
```

The Bhāṣya then asks how often such acquisition may occur and answers:

```text
ṣaṭsu kāleṣu
    on six occasions
```

The Kārikā gathers those occasions under three headings:

```text
pratipakṣodaya
phala-prāpti
indriya-vivṛddhi
```

The prose fixes their meanings.

### 1. Pratipakṣodaya

Here **pratipakṣa** specifically means:

```text
vimukti-mārga
    path of liberation
```

This is important after VAK 5.61, where pratipakṣa was fourfold. In this verse the word does **not** mean the entire fourfold counteragent taxonomy; Vasubandhu explicitly narrows its referent to the liberation path.

### 2. Phala-prāpti

The fruits are:

```text
catvāri śrāmaṇyaphalāni
    the four fruits of śramaṇa-hood
```

Thus attainment of a new śrāmaṇya fruit can occasion a renewed acquisition of disconnection.

### 3. Indriya-vivṛddhi

Vasubandhu glosses this as:

```text
indriya-saṃcāra
```

that is, transition or development of the spiritual faculties.

The key statement is:

> `eteṣu kāleṣu kleśasya visaṃyogalābhaḥ`

At these occasions there is acquisition of disconnection from the kleśa.

The qualification `yathāyogam` prevents mechanical universalization. Some disconnections are acquired on all six occasions, while others may be acquired on as few as two:

```text
keṣāñcit ṣaṭsu kāleṣu
keṣāñcid yāvad dvayoḥ
```

Thus the repeated acquisition is itself path-relative.

Finally, Vasubandhu states that the **same disconnection** (`sa eva visaṃyogaḥ`) receives, in its various path-stages, the designation **parijñā**. This prepares the next verse and the ninefold parijñā classification.

## Main Determination

The decisive distinction is:

```text
kṣaya
    destruction of the kleśa
    once

visaṃyoga-lābha
    acquisition of disconnection
    repeatedly
```

The Bhāṣya therefore rejects a model in which spiritual progress means repeatedly destroying the same defect at successively higher levels.

Instead:

```text
one destruction
    +
multiple later acquisitions of the relation of disconnection
```

The ontological event and the path-attainment event must remain distinct.

## Relation to VAK 5.61

VAK 5.61 distinguished four counteragents and their operational order. VAK 5.63 now narrows the relevant sense of `pratipakṣa`:

```text
5.61
pratipakṣa
    fourfold taxonomy

5.63
pratipakṣa
    specifically vimukti-mārga
```

This is another example of relation-indexed terminology. The same technical word receives a narrower extension according to the argument currently being made.

## New Technical Terms

- **sakṛt kṣaya** — destruction occurring once.
  - **status:** explicit doctrinal determination in this passage.
  - **function:** blocks repeated-destruction interpretations of path progress.

- **visaṃyoga-lābha** — acquisition of disconnection.
  - **status:** builds on the earlier doctrine of `visaṃyoga`.
  - **function:** denotes the repeatable attainment relation distinguished from destruction itself.

- **vimukti-mārga** — path of liberation.
  - **status:** previously encountered path vocabulary.
  - **function:** the specific referent of `pratipakṣa` in this verse.

- **śrāmaṇya-phala** — fruit of śramaṇa-hood.
  - **status:** established path vocabulary.
  - **function:** one occasion for renewed acquisition of disconnection; four such fruits are intended.

- **indriya-saṃcāra** — transition/development of the faculties.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of `indriya-vivṛddhi`.
  - **function:** another occasion for renewed acquisition of disconnection.

- **parijñā-saṃjñā** — designation as `parijñā`.
  - **status:** transition term into the next doctrinal block.
  - **function:** the same visaṃyoga receives this name under particular path conditions.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Kārikā translation is basically sound, but three points should be made explicit in commentary:

1. `pratipakṣa` here means **vimukti-mārga** specifically.
2. `phala` means the **four śrāmaṇya fruits**.
3. `indriya-vivṛddhi` is glossed as **indriya-saṃcāra**.

The phrase `punaḥ punaḥ` applies to **visaṃyoga-lābha**, not to renewed destruction of the kleśa.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya distinguishes an irreversible event from repeatable qualification:

```text
Destroy(K)
    occurs once

AcquireDisconnection(S, K, stage)
    may recur across path stages
```

Therefore:

```text
identity of the destroyed kleśa
    does not imply
identity of every later attainment-relation to its destruction
```

But Vasubandhu also says:

```text
sa eva visaṃyogaḥ
    it is that very same disconnection
```

So the repeated element is not repeated production of multiple destructions. Rather, one and the same disconnection is newly acquired or receives a new path-status under different conditions.

This distinction will be central in the later clarifying pass:

```text
object-level destruction
    ≠
attainment-level acquisition
    ≠
path-stage designation
```

## Minimal Organon Contact Point

A completed negation and the subject's successive determinate possession of that negation are different relations.

```text
negation once
    ≠
qualification once
```

The Bhāṣya is therefore careful not to confuse what has ceased with the changing path-status of the one who has become disconnected from it.
