# VAK_5.16 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> na rāgas tasya varjyatvān na dveṣo 'napakārataḥ |
> na māno na parāmarśau śānta-śuddhy-agrabhāvataḥ || 5.16 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Attachment does not take the uncontaminated as its support because attachment is itself to be relinquished; resistance does not because cessation and path do no injury; conceit and the two parāmarśas do not because cessation and path are genuinely peaceful, pure, and supreme.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Why are attachment, resistance, conceit, dṛṣṭiparāmarśa, and śīlavrataparāmarśa not admitted as having an uncontaminated cognitive support?

> Attachment does not, because it is to be relinquished.

For attachment is something to be relinquished. If it had the uncontaminated as its cognitive support, it would not be something to be relinquished, just like aspiration toward a wholesome dharma.

> Resistance does not, because there is no injury.

For resistance arises toward something that causes injury. Cessation and path are not like that.

> Nor conceit, nor the two parāmarśas, because [cessation and path] are peaceful, pure, and supreme.

Because cessation and path are peaceful, an elevation of oneself cannot properly occur with respect to them. Because they are genuinely pure in meaning, grasping them as pure cannot constitute śīlavrataparāmarśa. And they are themselves supreme; dṛṣṭiparāmarśa consists in grasping what is inferior as supreme.

Therefore it is inappropriate to regard these afflictions as having an uncontaminated cognitive support.

## Course of the Argument

VAK 5.14 established that six anuśayas can take uncontaminated objects: false view, doubt, and ignorance directed toward cessation and path. VAK 5.16 now explains why the remaining candidate afflictions cannot do so.

The Bhāṣya does not merely list exclusions. It supplies a distinct compatibility rule for each affliction.

```text
rāga
    requires an object toward which attachment is itself relinquishable

pratigha
    requires an object represented as injurious

māna
    requires a basis for elevation / comparative self-estimation

śīlavrataparāmarśa
    requires false attribution of purity or liberative efficacy

dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
    requires grasping an inferior determination as supreme
```

Cessation and path fail to provide the required conditions.

### Attachment

The Bhāṣya's argument is especially precise:

```text
rāga is varjanīya
    something to be relinquished

if rāga genuinely had an anāsrava object
    it would resemble kuśaladharmacchanda
    aspiration toward a wholesome dharma

therefore
    such a relation would no longer have the character of rāga
```

The point is not that every positive orientation toward path or cessation is prohibited. On the contrary, the comparison with `kuśaladharmacchanda` distinguishes wholesome aspiration from afflicted attachment.

This materially improves the first-pass interpretation. The issue is not simply that liberation cannot be "possessed." The Bhāṣya draws a technical distinction between:

```text
rāga
    afflicted attachment

kuśaladharmacchanda
    wholesome aspiration toward a good determination
```

### Resistance

The criterion for `pratigha` is object-character:

```text
apakāravastu
    an injurious / harmful object
        ↓
pratigha can arise
```

But cessation and path are not injurious objects. Thus the required ground for resistance is absent.

### Conceit

The prose explains `māna` through `unnati`, elevation:

```text
nirodha and mārga are śānta
    peaceful

therefore
    they do not provide a basis
    for afflicted self-elevation
```

This continues the determination of `māna` from VAK 5.10 as a distorted self-measurement rather than a free-floating emotion.

### Śīlavrataparāmarśa

The reason here is particularly subtle:

```text
nirodha and mārga
    are bhūtārtha-śuddha
    genuinely / actually pure
```

`Śīlavrataparāmarśa` is not simply the cognition "this is pure." Its error lies in falsely assigning purity or liberative efficacy where it does not belong.

Thus true recognition of the purity of cessation or path cannot itself instantiate this afflicted overvaluation.

### Dṛṣṭiparāmarśa

Finally:

```text
nirodha and mārga
    are agra
    genuinely supreme

but dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
    = hīne agragrāha
      grasping the inferior as supreme
```

Again the Bhāṣya defines the affliction relationally. The same predicate `agra` does not constitute error independently of its object. Error occurs when supremacy is imposed upon what is actually inferior.

## New Technical Terms

- **varjanīya** — something to be relinquished or avoided
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation made decisive here
  - **function:** explains why rāga cannot have the uncontaminated as its support

- **kuśaladharmacchanda** — aspiration toward a wholesome dharma
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only comparison in this unit
  - **function:** distinguishes wholesome aspiration from afflicted attachment

- **apakāravastu** — an injurious or harmful object
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** identifies the required object-condition for pratigha

- **unnati** — elevation, raising oneself up
  - **status:** previously implicated in the definition of māna; explicit explanatory function here
  - **function:** explains why peaceful cessation and path do not support conceit

- **bhūtārthaśuddhi** — genuine or actual purity of the determination
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** distinguishes true purity from the false purity-grasp of śīlavrataparāmarśa

- **agragrāha** — grasping as supreme
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** defines dṛṣṭiparāmarśa when directed toward something inferior

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya establishes a compatibility principle between affliction-type and cognitive support.

```text
An anuśaya cannot be specified
merely by naming an object.

Its characteristic operation
must be possible with respect to that object.
```

Thus anāsrava dharmas can support:

```text
mithyādṛṣṭi
vicikitsā
avidyā
```

because they can be denied, doubted, or not understood.

But they cannot support:

```text
rāga
pratigha
māna
dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
śīlavrataparāmarśa
```

because the defining relation of each would conflict with the actual character of cessation and path.

This is stronger than simply saying that uncontaminated objects are "good." The exclusion is generated from the internal definition of each anuśaya.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised slightly.**

The first-pass phrase "attachment does not take it as object, because it is to be abandoned" leaves the pronoun `tasya` and the logic of `varjyatva` somewhat obscure.

The Bhāṣya shows that what is relinquishable is `rāga` itself. If attachment toward an uncontaminated dharma had the same status as wholesome aspiration toward that dharma, it would no longer be the afflicted attachment that must be abandoned.

A better philosophical translation is:

> Attachment cannot have the uncontaminated as its support, since attachment as such is relinquishable, unlike wholesome aspiration toward a wholesome dharma. Resistance cannot, because cessation and path do no injury. Conceit and the two overvaluations cannot, because cessation and path are genuinely peaceful, pure, and supreme.

## Logical Determination

This verse provides a clear relational logic of affliction.

```text
Affliction A
    requires object-condition C

Object O
    lacks C

therefore
    A cannot take O
    in its characteristic afflicted mode
```

The most philosophically significant consequence is that predicates cannot be classified independently of their relation to their object.

```text
"pure"
    applied truly to path
        ≠ śīlavrataparāmarśa

"supreme"
    applied truly to path
        ≠ dṛṣṭiparāmarśa

positive desire for path
    as kuśaladharmacchanda
        ≠ rāga
```

Affliction is therefore not defined by vocabulary or surface phenomenology but by a malformed relation.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

This gives a useful constraint pattern for the Rational Kośa:

```text
RelationType
    has preconditions

ObjectType
    supplies or fails those preconditions

therefore
    some relations are structurally inadmissible
```

More importantly, the same apparent predicate may be valid or afflicted depending upon whether it corresponds to the object:

```text
inferior → supreme
    afflicted overvaluation

genuinely supreme → supreme
    adequate determination
```

The textual lesson is therefore prior to any software analogy: **error is a defect of determinate relation, not merely the presence of a particular predicate.**

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.16 completes the explanation begun in 5.14:

> **Uncontaminated cessation and path can be denied, doubted, or misunderstood, but their actual character prevents several other anuśayas from taking them as cognitive supports. Attachment, resistance, conceit, and the two parāmarśas each require an object-relation incompatible with what cessation and path genuinely are.**

This gives Chapter 5 an increasingly precise relational taxonomy: anuśayas are distinguished not only by intrinsic name or realm, but by the kinds of cognitive support with which their characteristic operation is possible.
