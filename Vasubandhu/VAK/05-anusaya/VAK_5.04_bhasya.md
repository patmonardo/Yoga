# VAK_5.04 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> daśaite sapta saptāṣṭau tri-dvi-dṛṣṭi-vivarjitāḥ |

**Best current translation:**  
Within the desire domain, the ten anuśayas are distributed according to the four truths by which they are abandoned: ten through seeing suffering, seven through seeing origination, seven through seeing nirodha, and eight through seeing the path.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

In summary, these anuśayas of the three domains are of two kinds: those to be abandoned by seeing and those to be abandoned by cultivation. In the desire domain, first of all, there are thirty-two to be abandoned by seeing. Which are they? Thus he says:

> These ten, seven, seven, and eight, deprived of three and two views respectively, are abandoned in the desire domain through seeing suffering and the others in sequence.

The ten anuśayas previously stated are all to be abandoned in the desire domain through seeing suffering. From these same ten, seven are to be abandoned through seeing origination. Seven are to be abandoned through seeing nirodha, excluding satkāyadṛṣṭi, antagrāhadṛṣṭi, and śīlavrataparāmarśa. Eight are to be abandoned through seeing the path, excluding satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi.

Thus there are thirty-two anuśayas in the desire domain to be abandoned by seeing, because they are abandoned through the mere seeing of the truths.

Four are to be abandoned by cultivation: attachment, resistance, conceit, and non-knowing. They are abandoned after the truths have been seen, through cultivation of the path.

Accordingly, satkāyadṛṣṭi has one mode: it is to be abandoned through seeing suffering. Antagrāhadṛṣṭi and mithyādṛṣṭi have four modes, being abandoned through seeing suffering, origination, nirodha, and path. The same is true of dṛṣṭiparāmarśa and vicikitsā.

## Course of the Argument

The ten basic anuśayas have now been established. The next question is no longer simply how many there are, but how they are distributed across the Path.

Vasubandhu begins with a fundamental distinction:

```text
darśana-prahātavya
    to be abandoned by seeing

bhāvanā-prahātavya
    to be abandoned by cultivation
```

This converts the taxonomy of anuśaya into a path-structure.

The desire-domain counts are:

```text
duḥkha-darśana       10
samudaya-darśana      7
nirodha-darśana       7
mārga-darśana         8
                     --
                     32
```

For seeing nirodha, the Bhāṣya explicitly excludes:

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
antagrāhadṛṣṭi
śīlavrataparāmarśa
```

For seeing the path, it excludes:

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
antagrāhadṛṣṭi
```

The counts are therefore generated by the doctrinal scope of the views, not by arbitrary arithmetic.

The crucial explanation is:

```text
satyānāṃ darśanamātreṇa prahāṇāt
```

because these are abandoned through the mere seeing of the truths.

`Darśana` is therefore functioning as an operative Path category: a truth becomes directly seen, and a corresponding afflicted determination can no longer remain established in the same way.

The prose then contrasts four anuśayas that require cultivation after the truths have been seen:

```text
rāga
pratigha
māna
avidyā
```

They are abandoned through `mārgābhyāsa`, cultivation or repeated practice of the path.

So the text distinguishes two mechanisms:

```text
Darśana
    decisive truth-disclosure

Bhāvanā
    sustained cultivation after disclosure
```

## Dṛṣṭi as a Key Abhidharma Term

VAK 5.04 materially deepens `dṛṣṭi`.

In VAK 5.03, `dṛṣṭi` was differentiated into five afflicted forms. Here those forms are distributed according to the truth whose seeing abandons them.

A `dṛṣṭi` is therefore not merely an opinion. It is a determinate cognitive stance whose structure can be specified by:

```text
1. its field
2. its mode of determination
3. the truth that corrects it
4. the Path mode by which it is abandoned
```

This makes `dṛṣṭi` a bridge-term between cognition and path-transformation.

The passage also shows that `darśana` and `dṛṣṭi`, despite their common root √dṛś, must be distinguished:

```text
dṛṣṭi
    determinate view / cognitive stance

darśana
    truth-seeing capable of abandoning a view
```

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.04 is the first explicit Path matrix of Chapter 5.

Anuśayas are now classified not only by intrinsic type but by the mode of cognition required for their abandonment:

```text
Anuśaya
    × Domain
    × Truth
    × Removal Mode
```

The Path is therefore built into the classification of the affliction itself.

The Bhāṣya also distinguishes a decisive truth-disclosure from the later cultivation needed for other anuśayas. This distinction is structural, not merely chronological.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The Bhāṣya makes the exclusions explicit and confirms that the four counts are truth-specific abandonment classes in the desire domain.

A tightened philosophical translation is:

> In the desire domain, all ten anuśayas are abandoned through seeing suffering; seven through seeing origination; seven through seeing nirodha, excluding satkāyadṛṣṭi, antagrāhadṛṣṭi, and śīlavrataparāmarśa; and eight through seeing the path, excluding satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi. These thirty-two constitute the anuśayas abandoned by seeing.

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya transform the anuśaya list into a relational matrix.

```text
afflicted determination
    is understood together with
    the truth-disclosure that removes its standing
```

For `dṛṣṭi`, this yields a particularly important relation:

```text
afflicted dṛṣṭi
    ↔
corresponding satya-darśana
```

The false or afflicted view is not merely suppressed externally. Its cognitive basis is corrected through the relevant truth-seeing.

The sequence is therefore:

```text
dṛṣṭi
    determinate afflicted seeing

satya-darśana
    determinate truth-seeing

prahāṇa
    abandonment of the former
```

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.04 establishes the first explicit Path matrix of the Anuśayanirdeśa:

> **The anuśayas are classified not only by what they are, but by which truth must be seen for them to be abandoned. Thirty-two desire-domain anuśayas are abandoned by seeing; four further anuśayas persist beyond initial truth-disclosure and require cultivation of the Path.**

This makes `dṛṣṭi` a central Abhidharma term: afflicted views are determinate cognitive structures, while `darśana` is the truth-seeing through which those structures are corrected and abandoned.
