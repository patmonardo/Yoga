## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

It was said: “The diversity of the world is born from karma.” Those karmas undergo accumulation under the power of the anuśayas, and without the anuśayas they are incapable of bringing existence into manifestation. Therefore it should be understood:

> **The anuśayas are the root of existence.**

For a kleśa, when active, performs ten functions: it strengthens the root; establishes the continuum; prepares the field; produces an outflow; brings forth karmic existence; takes up its own supporting accumulation; deludes with respect to the cognitive support; bends the stream of vijñāna; causes departure from the wholesome side; and spreads for the purpose of binding, through the mode by which one does not pass beyond the dhātu.

How many are these anuśayas? In summary, six. Which six?

> Attachment, resistance, conceit, non-knowing, view, and doubt.

The expression `atha` is used in order to indicate, through the case of attachment, the anuśaya-character of the others with respect to their cognitive supports. This will be explained later.

> These, again—

The final phrase carries the argument directly into VAK_5.02.

## Argument Reconstruction

The Bhāṣya opens Chapter 5 by explicitly returning to the opening thesis of Chapter 4:

```text
karmajaṃ lokavaicitryam
    diversity of world is generated by karma
```

It now moves behind karma to its condition of efficacy:

```text
anuśaya
    ↓ power / governance
karma accumulates (upacaya)
    ↓
karma becomes capable of producing bhava
```

The decisive statement is stronger than the Kārikā alone makes explicit:

```text
without anuśaya
    karma is not capable
    of bringing bhava into manifestation
```

Thus `mūla` is not merely a metaphor for an early cause. The anuśayas are the operative root through which karma becomes accumulated and productive of renewed existence.

The prose then shifts from `anuśaya` to `kleśa` and gives the active kleśa ten functions. This is our first important warning against simply identifying the two terms without analysis. The passage relates them intimately, but it also changes vocabulary precisely when describing active operation:

```text
anuśaya
    root-condition of karmic efficacy

kleśa pravartamāna
    affliction in active operation
    performing ten functions
```

The ten functions describe an entire reproductive mechanism of conditioned existence:

```text
1. mūlaṃ dṛḍhīkaroti
   strengthens the root

2. saṃtatim avasthāpayati
   establishes the continuum

3. kṣetram āpādayati
   prepares / produces the field

4. niḥṣyandaṃ nirvartayati
   produces an outflow / derivative result

5. karmabhavam abhinirharati
   brings forth karmic existence

6. svasaṃbhāram ... gṛhṇāti
   takes up its own supporting accumulation

7. ālambane saṃmohayati
   produces delusion regarding the cognitive support

8. vijñānasroto namayati
   bends / inclines the stream of vijñāna

9. kuśalapakṣād vyutkrāmati
   causes departure from the wholesome side

10. bandhanārthaṃ spharati
    spreads for the purpose of binding
```

This makes the opening of Anuśayanirdeśa genetic. Chapter 5 does not merely provide a list of mental defects. It asks why karma has the power to reproduce conditioned existence and answers by exposing the afflictive mechanism that accumulates, directs, and binds the continuum.

## Voice Identification

The entire local unit is best read as:

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

No explicit opponent appears. The opening quotation from Chapter 4 is Vasubandhu's own backward reference establishing the genetic transition from karma to anuśaya.

The ten-function account is presented directly in the explanatory voice; this passage alone does not require a separate Vaibhāṣika voice label.

## New Technical Terms

- **anuśayavaśa** — being under the power or governance of the anuśayas
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the Kārikā's `anuśaya`
  - **function:** explains why karma accumulates and becomes productive

- **upacaya** — accumulation, increase, becoming built up
  - **status:** previously encountered in the Kārikās, technically operative here in the prose
  - **function:** consequence of anuśaya-governed karma

- **bhavābhinirvartana** — bringing existence into manifestation / production
  - **status:** Bhāṣya prose formulation
  - **function:** states the productive capacity karma lacks without anuśaya

- **pravartamāna kleśa** — kleśa in active operation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this unit
  - **function:** introduces the ten active functions of affliction

- **saṃtati** — continuum
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** that which active kleśa establishes and sustains

- **kṣetra** — field
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** produced/prepared condition for further fruition

- **niḥṣyanda** — outflow, derivative result
  - **status:** previously encountered
  - **function:** produced consequence of active kleśa

- **karmabhava** — karmic existence / existence constituted through karma
  - **status:** technically operative compound in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** product brought forth by active kleśa

- **vijñānasrotas** — stream of vijñāna
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** cognitive continuum bent or inclined by kleśa

- **kuśalapakṣa** — wholesome side / wholesome orientation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** normative side from which kleśa causes departure

- **bandhana** — binding
  - **status:** previously encountered concept, explicit function here
  - **function:** culminative function of kleśa within conditioned existence

## Doctrinal and Logical Determination

The Kārikā's judgment:

```text
anuśayāḥ = bhavasya mūlam
```

is now mediated by a causal-functional argument:

```text
anuśaya
    ↓ governs
karma
    ↓ accumulates
productive karmic capacity
    ↓
bhava
```

The Bhāṣya therefore materially strengthens the first-pass interpretation of `mūla` as a sustaining and generative ground.

More importantly, it introduces a distinction requiring continued scrutiny:

```text
anuśaya
    underlying root / governing condition

kleśa in activity
    explicit functional reproduction of bondage
```

We should not yet convert this into a final ontological distinction, because later Chapter 5 passages explicitly debate what `anuśaya` means and how it relates to kleśa. But VAK_5.01 already makes it impossible to treat the chapter as a mere taxonomy of psychological states.

The ten functions form a process extending from root-strengthening through cognitive orientation to binding. This is exactly the kind of Path / non-Path discrimination the chapter must eventually make explicit: the kleśa does not merely occur in consciousness; it organizes a continuum away from the wholesome side and toward renewed bhava.

---

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass translation remains serviceable:

> The anuśayas are the root of existence.

But the Bhāṣya now fixes three important interpretive consequences:

1. **`mūla` is genuinely generative and sustaining.** It is explained through the incapacity of karma to produce bhava without anuśaya.
2. **`bhava` is produced conditioned existence**, not abstract Being.
3. **`anuśaya` should remain untranslated for now.** The Bhāṣya immediately opens a technical problem concerning its relation to active `kleśa`; translating it prematurely as merely “latent tendency” would prejudge that problem.

The philosophical translation should therefore be tightened to:

> The anuśayas are the root through which karma becomes accumulated and capable of generating conditioned existence. Six are initially distinguished: attachment, resistance, conceit, non-knowing, view, and doubt; their further differentiation follows.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The chapter transition is structurally important:

```text
Chapter 4
    karma as determining activity

Chapter 5
    the ground that gives conditioned activity
    its reproductive direction and efficacy
```

The Bhāṣya itself supplies this transition; it is not imposed from outside. The strongest Organon contact is therefore the movement from manifested determining activity to its underlying reflective ground.

The Path implication should remain subordinate but visible:

```text
kleśa
    bends vijñāna
    departs from kuśalapakṣa
    binds the continuum

Path
    must discriminate and undo
    precisely this determinative organization
```

This is especially important for the Rational Kośa reading: liberation requires not merely stopping isolated mental events, but understanding and removing the determinations by which a continuum is repeatedly organized into bondage.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK_5.01 now has a substantially stronger determination than the Kārikā pass alone could provide:

> **Anuśaya is called the root of bhava because karma acquires accumulation and world-producing efficacy only under its power. Active kleśa then performs the functions by which the continuum, its cognitive orientation, karmic production, and bondage are reproduced.**

This result should govern the reading of the opening block VAK_5.01–5.18, while remaining open to correction when the chapter begins its explicit debate over the nature of anuśaya.
