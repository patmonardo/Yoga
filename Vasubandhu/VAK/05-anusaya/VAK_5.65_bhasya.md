## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — enumeration of the final three parijñās, explanation of why the cultivation-abandonments of the form and formless domains are separately counted, and explanation of the kṣānti/jñāna attribution.

**Voice: Objector** — the question asking why the cultivation-abandonments of the form and formless domains are separate parijñās while the corresponding seeing-abandonments are not separately counted in the same way.

---

## Translation and Argument

VAK 5.64 supplied six parijñās whose substance is the abandonment of afflictions to be abandoned by seeing. VAK 5.65 supplies the remaining three.

### 1. Destruction of the lower-part fetters

The Bhāṣya states simply:

> **avarabhāgīyaprahāṇam ekā parijñā**

> The abandonment of the lower-part [fetters] is one parijñā.

This is the seventh parijñā.

The term must be read against the previously established **avarabhāgīya-saṃyojana** classification. It is not merely a vague destruction of “lower tendencies.” It is a technically delimited completion of abandonment.

### 2. Destruction of the form-influx

The Bhāṣya makes the compressed Kārikā expression `rūpa-kṣaya` precise:

> **rūpāsrava-prahāṇam ekā rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā**

> The abandonment of the form-influx is one parijñā, the parijñā of the destruction of attachment to form.

Thus the first-pass phrase “form-related residual bond” was too loose. The text identifies the relevant abandonment specifically as **rūpāsrava-prahāṇa**, and names the resulting completion **rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā**.

This is the eighth parijñā.

### 3. Destruction of all influxes

The final parijñā is:

> **ārūpyāptānāṃ sarvāsrava-prahāṇam ekā sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā**

The transmitted wording is compact. It connects the final abandonment with the remaining influxes associated with the formless range and designates the result:

> **sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā**

> the parijñā of the complete exhaustion of all fetters.

This is the ninth and terminal parijñā.

The sequence is therefore:

```text
7. avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa
   abandonment of the lower-part fetters

8. rūpāsrava-prahāṇa
   = rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā

9. sarvāsrava-prahāṇa
   = sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā
```

The Bhāṣya then explicitly closes the enumeration:

> **ity etā nava parijñāḥ**

> Thus these are the nine parijñās.

## Why Form and Formless Cultivation-Abandonments Are Separate

Vasubandhu raises an important classificatory question:

> Why are the abandonments of the cultivation-abandonable afflictions belonging to the form and formless domains counted as separate parijñās, while the seeing-abandonments were grouped differently?

The answer is terse and decisive:

> **bhāvanāheyānām atulya-pratipakṣatvāt**

> Because the cultivation-abandonable [afflictions] have unequal / non-identical counteragents.

This is crucial for the architecture of the parijñā system.

The numerical grouping is not controlled merely by:

```text
domain
or
number of afflictions
```

It is controlled by the **structure of abandonment**, including the counteragent required.

Thus:

```text
same broad class of bhāvanā-heya
    + different pratipakṣa
    → distinct parijñā-completions
```

This directly continues the doctrine of the fourfold `pratipakṣa` in VAK 5.61.

---

## Six Fruits of Kṣānti, Three Fruits of Jñāna

The Kārikā then gives a second classification of the nine:

```text
six
    → kṣānti-phala

three
    → jñāna-phala
```

The six are exactly those:

> **darśanaheya-prahāṇa-svabhāva**

whose nature consists in the abandonment of what is to be abandoned by seeing.

The remaining three — beginning with the abandonment of the lower-part fetters — are fruits of knowledge because:

> **bhāvanāmārga-phalatvāt**

> they are fruits of the path of cultivation.

This corrects any simple equation:

```text
six = seeing
three = cultivation
```

with a more exact account:

```text
six parijñās
    have darśana-heya-prahāṇa as their nature
    and are designated kṣānti-phala

three parijñās
    are bhāvanā-mārga-phala
    and are designated jñāna-phala
```

### Why call the first six fruits of kṣānti?

Vasubandhu anticipates the problem:

> **kathaṃ kṣāntiphalaṃ parijñā bhavanti**

> How can the parijñās be fruits of the kṣāntis?

He gives two reasons.

First:

> **kṣāntīnāṃ jñānaparivāratvāt**

> because the kṣāntis form the retinue of the knowledges.

He illustrates this with royal usage:

> **rājaparivāre rājopacāravat**

> just as the designation of the king may be extended to the king's retinue.

Second:

> **jñānaikaphalatvācca**

> and because they have one fruit together with knowledge.

So the expression **kṣānti-phala** is not grounded in an isolated causal sequence in which patience alone independently produces the parijñā. The Bhāṣya explains the designation through the intimate relation of the kṣāntis to the knowledges and their shared result.

This is another instance in which doctrinal naming follows a relation rather than a simple one-to-one identity.

## Full Ninefold Enumeration

Combining VAK 5.64 and 5.65:

```text
DARŚANA-HEYA COMPLETIONS

Kāmadhātu
1. duḥkha + samudaya seeing-abandonments
2. nirodha seeing-abandonments
3. mārga seeing-abandonments

Higher domains
4. duḥkha + samudaya seeing-abandonments
5. nirodha seeing-abandonments
6. mārga seeing-abandonments

BHĀVANĀ-MĀRGA COMPLETIONS

7. avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa
8. rūpāsrava-prahāṇa / rūparāga-kṣaya
9. sarvāsrava-prahāṇa / sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna
```

This is the complete ninefold parijñā system as presently established by the Bhāṣya.

## New Technical Terms

- **rūpāsrava-prahāṇa** — abandonment of the form-influx.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the compressed Kārikā `rūpa-kṣaya`.
  - **function:** identifies the precise substance of the eighth parijñā.

- **rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā** — parijñā of the destruction of attachment to form.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific naming in this passage.
  - **function:** technical designation for the eighth parijñā.

- **sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā** — parijñā of complete exhaustion of all fetters.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific naming in this passage.
  - **function:** technical designation for the ninth and terminal parijñā.

- **atulya-pratipakṣatva** — having unequal / non-identical counteragents.
  - **status:** explicit Bhāṣya rationale.
  - **function:** explains why the cultivation-abandonments of the form and formless ranges yield distinct parijñās.

- **jñāna-parivāra** — retinue or entourage of knowledge.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory expression.
  - **function:** explains how kṣānti can receive a result-designation connected with knowledge.

- **rājopacāra** — figurative or transferred designation by comparison with royal usage.
  - **status:** explanatory analogy.
  - **function:** illustrates transferred naming from knowledge to the kṣāntis associated with it.

- **jñānaika-phalatva** — having one/shared fruit with knowledge.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya rationale.
  - **function:** second reason why the first six parijñās are called kṣānti-phala.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass rendering of `rūpa-kṣaya` as a generic “form-related destruction” is too loose. The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies it as:

```text
rūpāsrava-prahāṇa
    abandonment of the form-influx
```

and names it:

```text
rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā
```

The final member should likewise preserve the stronger Bhāṣya designation of **complete exhaustion of all fetters**.

Preferred translation:

> The other three parijñās are the abandonment of the lower-part fetters, the destruction of the form-influx, and the destruction of all influxes. Six are fruits of patience; the remaining three are fruits of knowledge.

---

## Logical Determination

The important result of 5.65 is that the **unit of completion is determined by the structure of counteraction**.

```text
same broad abandonment class
    ≠
same parijñā
```

when:

```text
pratipakṣa differs
```

Thus the parijñā system coordinates at least:

```text
object-range
mode of abandonment
counteragent
completed disconnection
path-result designation
```

The verse also gives another example of relation-indexed naming:

```text
kṣānti
    is called productive of the parijñā
because it belongs to the retinue of jñāna
and shares one fruit with it
```

The name follows a structured relation, not simple identity.

---

## Organon Contact Point

Restrained contact point only:

```text
Classification follows the operative relation that completes a determination.
```

In this passage the decisive relation is **pratipakṣa**. Two abandonments that might look parallel by domain are counted separately when their counteragents are not the same.

This should be retained for the later clarifying pass without yet building a larger speculative architecture.
