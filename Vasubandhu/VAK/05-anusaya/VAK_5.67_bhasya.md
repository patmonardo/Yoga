## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu, reporting the received Abhidharma classification.

No explicit opponent is introduced within this verse's immediate explanation.

## Translation

All nine parijñās are fruits of the noble path. Two are fruits of the mundane path, and two are fruits of anvayajñāna. Three are fruits of dharmajñāna. Six are fruits of the dharmajñāna-side, and five are fruits of the anvayajñāna-side.

## Argument

VAK 5.66 classified the nine parijñās according to meditative basis. VAK 5.67 now classifies the same completed abandonments according to the path or knowledge-operation from which they arise.

### 1. Noble path

The Bhāṣya is unambiguous:

`nava parijñāḥ phalam`

All nine parijñās are fruits of the **āryamārga**.

This is the comprehensive classification.

### 2. Mundane path

The mundane path yields exactly two:

- **avarabhāgīya-kṣaya-parijñā** — destruction of the lower-part fetters;
- **rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā** — destruction of attachment to form.

Thus the mundane path has genuine abandonment-power, but not the full ninefold range of the noble path.

### 3. Anvayajñāna

The Bhāṣya says that **anvayajñāna also has two parijñās as fruit**, namely the last two (`paścime`). In context these are:

- rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā;
- sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā.

The first of the three cultivation-stage parijñās, the lower-fetter destruction, is therefore not an anvayajñāna fruit.

### 4. Dharmajñāna

Dharmajñāna has the **last three** parijñās as fruit.

The Bhāṣya gives the reason:

`tridhātukabhāvanāheya pratipakṣatvāt`

because dharmajñāna functions as counteragent to the cultivation-abandonable afflictions of all three domains.

Thus the three are:

- avarabhāgīya-kṣaya-parijñā;
- rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā;
- sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā.

This is an important distinction between **object-range** and **counteractive range**. Dharmajñāna is named through the desire-domain truth-complex, yet its cultivation-path counteractive efficacy extends through all three domains.

### 5. Dharmajñāna-pakṣa and anvayajñāna-pakṣa

The Bhāṣya explicitly explains the word **pakṣa**:

`pakṣagrahaṇena hi kṣāntijñānāni gṛhyante`

By taking the "side," both the corresponding kṣāntis and jñānas are included.

Therefore:

- **dharmajñāna-pakṣa** → six parijñās;
- **anvayajñāna-pakṣa** → five parijñās.

The increase in count does not come from assigning more fruits to the single knowledge-event. It comes from enlarging the unit of analysis from a knowledge to its complete operative family of **kṣānti + jñāna**.

## Structural Determination

The same parijñā may simultaneously be classified as:

- fruit of the noble path;
- fruit of a determinate knowledge;
- fruit of a larger knowledge-side.

These are not competing causal claims. They are classifications at different levels.

```text
āryamārga
    comprehensive path-class

laukikamārga
    restricted path-class

dharmajñāna / anvayajñāna
    determinate knowledge-operations

jñāna-pakṣa
    kṣānti + jñāna operative family
```

So once again:

```text
same result
    ≠
one exclusive classificatory relation
```

## New Technical Terms

- **āryamārga** — noble path; comprehensive path-class whose fruits include all nine parijñās.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** universal path-level classification.

- **laukikamārga** — mundane path.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** produces two specified cultivation-stage parijñās.

- **dharmajñāna** — dharma-knowledge.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** counteragent to cultivation-abandonable afflictions throughout all three domains; yields the final three parijñās.

- **anvayajñāna** — subsequent/inferential knowledge concerning the higher-domain truths.
  - **status:** previously encountered.
  - **function:** yields the last two parijñās.

- **pakṣa** — side, operative family.
  - **status:** technically important in this passage.
  - **function:** includes both the corresponding kṣānti and jñāna moments.

- **tridhātuka-bhāvanāheya-pratipakṣa** — counteragent to cultivation-abandonable afflictions of all three domains.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** explains why dharmajñāna yields three parijñās.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass numerical distribution is confirmed. The Bhāṣya fixes the contents and grounds of the counts:

```text
āryamārga        → 9
laukikamārga     → 2
anvayajñāna      → 2
dharmajñāna      → 3
dharmajñāna-pakṣa → 6
anvayajñāna-pakṣa → 5
```

The two mundane-path fruits and the exact meaning of `pakṣa` are now secure.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.67 distinguishes the **level of the classificatory subject**.

```text
PathClass
    ≠
KnowledgeOperation
    ≠
KnowledgeFamily
```

A single completed abandonment may belong under all three without contradiction.

The most important doctrinal asymmetry is:

```text
dharmajñāna
    → 3

anvayajñāna
    → 2
```

because dharmajñāna has the wider cultivation-path counteractive range.

And:

```text
dharmajñāna
    → 3

dharmajñāna-pakṣa
    → 6
```

because `pakṣa` includes the corresponding kṣānti-moments in addition to the jñāna-moments.

## Minimal Organon Contact Point

The verse reinforces the chapter's recurring principle that classification is relation-indexed.

A result may be determined relative to:

- its comprehensive path-class;
- its specific knowledge-operation;
- the larger operative family to which that knowledge belongs.

No stronger Organon synthesis is needed in this first Bhāṣya pass.
