# VAK 5.35 — Bhāṣya Analysis

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.35**

*kāme saparyavasthānāḥ kleśāḥ kāmāsravo vinā |*
*mohenānuśayā eva rūpārūpye bhavāsravaḥ || 5.35 ||*

**Current translation:** In the desire-domain, the afflictions together with the paryavasthānas, excluding delusion, constitute the desire-influx. In the form and formless domains, the anuśayas alone, again excluding delusion, constitute the existence-influx.

## Translation of the Bhāṣya

The Blessed One taught these same anuśayas in the sūtra as three influxes: the influx of desire, the influx of existence, and the influx of ignorance. He also taught four floods—desire, existence, views, and ignorance; the same four as yokes; and four appropriations: appropriation of desire, of views, of discipline and observance, and of the doctrine of self.

First, in the desire-domain, the afflictions together with the paryavasthānas, except ignorance, are to be understood as the desire-influx. There are forty-one real items: thirty-one anuśayas after excluding the five modes of ignorance, together with ten paryavasthānas.

In the form and formless domains, the anuśayas alone constitute the existence-influx; “apart from delusion” continues to govern the statement. Thus the anuśayas of the form and formless domains, excluding ignorance, constitute the existence-influx: fifty-two real items, twenty-six in the form-domain and twenty-six in the formless-domain.

**Objector:** But are there not also two paryavasthānas there, torpor and restlessness? And does not the Prakaraṇa say that the existence-influx includes the bindings, bonds, anuśayas, secondary afflictions, and paryavasthānas associated with the form and formless domains?

**Kāśmīra Vaibhāṣika reply:** They are not counted here because they lack independence.

Why then are the anuśayas of the form and formless domains combined into the single category “existence-influx”? Because both groups are ethically indeterminate, inward-directed, and belong to concentrated levels. Owing to this threefold similarity they are unified.

By the same reason that “craving for existence” was established as one category, “existence-influx” is likewise established as one. Consequently, ignorance belonging to the three domains remains to be established separately as the ignorance-influx.

## Course of Argument

### 1. One afflictive field, several classificatory systems

The Bhāṣya begins by saying that **the same anuśayas** are redescribed under several canonical headings:

```text
anuśaya-field
    → 3 āsravas
    → 4 oghas
    → 4 yogas
    → 4 upādānas
```

This is important. Vasubandhu is not introducing four new ontologies of affliction. He is changing the **functional classification** imposed on the already analyzed field.

### 2. Kāmāsrava is quantitatively exact

The desire-influx contains:

```text
31 desire-domain anuśayas
    after excluding fivefold avidyā
+
10 paryavasthānas
=
41 dravyas
```

So `saparyavasthānāḥ` is not rhetorical. The manifest secondary afflictive layer is formally included in the desire-influx.

### 3. Bhavāsrava is deliberately narrower

For the form and formless domains:

```text
26 form-domain anuśayas
+
26 formless-domain anuśayas
=
52 dravyas
```

Again, ignorance is removed because it will constitute its own influx.

The phrase `anuśayā eva` therefore means that, in this enumeration, only the anuśayas are counted.

### 4. Objection: higher domains also have paryavasthānas

The objector notices a potential inconsistency. Torpor (`styāna`) and restlessness (`auddhatya`) occur in higher domains, and another canonical source includes paryavasthānas in its definition of `bhavāsrava`.

The Kāśmīra reply is:

```text
asvātantryāt
because they lack independence
```

Thus the omission is not a denial of their occurrence. It is a decision about **classificatory independence**.

### 5. Why form and formless are one influx

The Bhāṣya gives three grounds for combining the higher domains:

```text
avyākṛta
    ethically indeterminate

antarmukha-pravṛtta
    inward-directed in operation

samāhita-bhūmika
    belonging to concentrated levels
```

The unity of `bhavāsrava` is therefore not merely spatial or cosmological. It is a threefold **sādharmya**, similarity of functional structure.

## Voices

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the canonical classifications, gives the numerical analyses, and frames the problems.

**Voice: Objector** — asks why higher-domain `styāna` and `auddhatya` are omitted despite canonical support for including paryavasthānas.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position (Kāśmīra)** — answers `asvātantryāt`: they are not separately counted because they lack independence.

## New Technical Terms

- **āsrava** — influx or contaminating current; **status:** major canonical category newly foregrounded here; **function:** reorganizes the anuśaya-field by sustained mode of contamination.
- **kāmāsrava** — desire-influx; **status:** new category in this section; **function:** collects desire-domain afflictions and paryavasthānas apart from ignorance.
- **bhavāsrava** — existence-influx; **status:** new category; **function:** collects the non-ignorance anuśayas of the two higher domains.
- **avidyāsrava** — ignorance-influx; **status:** announced but analyzed next; **function:** isolates ignorance as a separate cross-domain current.
- **paryavasthāna** — afflictive envelopment/manifest domination; **status:** previously encountered category, now given classificatory weight; **function:** counted in kāmāsrava but not independently in bhavāsrava here.
- **asvātantrya** — lack of independence; **status:** Bhāṣya-level justificatory term; **function:** explains why higher-domain torpor/restlessness are not independently enumerated.
- **antarmukha-pravṛtta** — inward-directed in operation; **status:** Bhāṣya-only characterization here; **function:** one of three grounds unifying form and formless afflictions.
- **sādharmya** — similarity/common character; **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative term; **function:** grounds the unification of two domains under one influx.

## Reflective / Doctrinal Determination

The strongest result is that these categories are **functional redescriptions of one determinate field**.

```text
same anuśaya
    may be classified
    according to a different practical relation
```

This guards against treating `āsrava`, `ogha`, `yoga`, and `upādāna` as four unrelated inventories.

The Bhāṣya also introduces a useful distinction between:

```text
occurs in a domain
and
is independently counted in a classification
```

Torpor and restlessness may occur in the higher domains, yet fail to receive independent membership in the present `bhavāsrava` enumeration because of `asvātantrya`.

Finally, the unity of the higher domains is explicitly **relational and structural**, based on three common determinations rather than on numerical identity.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing translation is broadly sound. The Bhāṣya adds:

- exact counts: `kāmāsrava = 41`, `bhavāsrava = 52`;
- `anuśayā eva` does not mean that higher-domain paryavasthānas simply do not exist;
- their omission is justified by `asvātantrya`;
- form and formless are unified because of threefold functional similarity.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.35 performs **reclassification by a new predicate**.

Earlier the anuśayas were determined by:

```text
domain
mode of abandonment
object-range
conjunction
latent relation
```

Now the same elements are projected into a new classificatory space:

```text
Membership(x, Kāmāsrava)
Membership(x, Bhavāsrava)
```

Membership is constrained by multiple conditions, not by a single intrinsic mark.

For example:

```text
x belongs to Bhavāsrava
if
    x belongs to form or formless domain
    x is an anuśaya
    x is not avidyā
```

But `styāna` and `auddhatya` demonstrate that mere domain-location is insufficient. The classification also asks whether the factor has enough **independent functional standing** to count separately.

## Organon Contact Point

This verse gives a useful model of **multiple valid taxonomies over one underlying dataset**.

```text
UnderlyingAfflictiveField
    → classifiedBy domain
    → classifiedBy abandonment
    → classifiedBy object-range
    → classifiedBy āsrava-function
```

The crucial constraint is that a new taxonomy must not create new entities merely because it introduces new names.

```text
new classification
    ≠
new ontology
```

It also suggests a rule for aggregation:

```text
aggregate by shared functional determinations
not merely by location or naming convention
```

That is precisely what happens when form and formless anuśayas are combined into `bhavāsrava` by their threefold similarity.
