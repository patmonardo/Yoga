# VAK 5.60 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.60**

> ālambana-parijñānāt tad-ālambana-saṃkṣayāt /
> ālambana-prahāṇāc ca pratipakṣodayāt kṣayaḥ // 5.60 //

**Translation**

> Exhaustion occurs from complete knowledge of the object-support, from the exhaustion of that which has it as object-support, from abandonment of the object-support, and from the arising of the counteragent.

Full Kārikā analysis: [VAK_5.60.md](./VAK_5.60.md)

---

## Bhāṣya Source

Pradhan 319|19–320|06.

The Bhāṣya opens with a problem rather than an immediate gloss:

> *idaṃ vicāryate* — “This is to be examined.”

It identifies cases in which knowing an affliction's object and abandoning the affliction do not coincide:

> *visabhāgadhātusarvatragāṇāṃ nirodhamārgadarśanaprahātavyānāṃ ca sāsravālambanānāṃ yadālambanaṃ parijñāyate tadā na prahīyante / yadā prahīyante tadālambanaṃ na parijñāyate iti kathameṣāṃ prahāṇam /*

> “For the universal afflictions belonging to a different domain, and for those to be abandoned by seeing cessation and the path that take contaminated objects: when their object is completely known, they are not then abandoned; and when they are abandoned, their object is not then being completely known. How, then, are these abandoned?”

Vasubandhu replies:

> *nāvaśyam ālambana-parijñānāt kleśānāṃ kṣayo bhavati /*

> “The exhaustion of afflictions does not necessarily occur through complete knowledge of their object.”

He then states that exhaustion occurs in four ways.

**Textual note:** the source e-text at Pradhan 319|25 prints *ālambanagrahāṇāc ca*, while the Kārikā file and the prose explanation immediately afterward require **ālambana-prahāṇāt**. The transmitted e-text form is preserved here rather than silently normalized.

## Translation and Argument

### 1. Exhaustion through complete knowledge of the object

> *tatrālambanaparijñānād duḥkhasamudayadarśanaheyānāṃ svabhūmyālambanānāmanāsravālambanānāṃ ca /*

For afflictions abandoned by seeing suffering and origin, insofar as they take objects belonging to their own level, exhaustion occurs through complete knowledge of the object. The passage also includes afflictions whose objects are uncontaminated.

The governing relation is direct:

```text
object-support
    ↓ completely known
corresponding seeing-abandonable affliction
    ↓
exhausted
```

Here the cognition which fully determines the object also removes the affliction whose error is sustained by that object-relation.

### 2. Exhaustion through exhaustion of what has that object

> *tadālambanasaṃkṣayād visabhāgadhātusarvatragāṇām /*

The universal afflictions belonging to a different domain are exhausted through the exhaustion of the factors which take them as object-support.

The Bhāṣya explains:

> *tadālambanā hi sabhāgadhātusarvatragāḥ / teṣu prahīṇeṣu te 'pi prahīṇā bhavanti /*

> “For the universal afflictions belonging to the same domain have those as their object; when the former are abandoned, the latter too become abandoned.”

The point is relational rather than directly epistemic. The target affliction need not be removed because its own object is presently known. It can lose its standing when the afflictive complex that takes it as object is exhausted.

```text
A is object-support for B
B is exhausted
    ↓
A is thereby exhausted
```

The exact direction of the antecedents should remain tied to Vasubandhu's stated same-domain / different-domain universal relation rather than generalized beyond this case.

### 3. Exhaustion through abandonment of the object-support

> *ālambanaprahāṇān nirodhamārgaheyānāṃ sāsravālambanānām /*

For contaminated-object afflictions abandoned by seeing cessation and the path, exhaustion occurs through abandonment of their object-support.

The Bhāṣya continues:

> *te hy anāsravālambanās teṣām ālambanam / atas teṣu prahīṇeṣu te 'pi prahīṇā bhavanti /*

> “For those whose object is uncontaminated are the object-support of these; therefore, when those are abandoned, these too become abandoned.”

Again the removal is mediated through an object-relation. The affliction is not eliminated by directly cognizing its own contaminated object at the moment of abandonment. Its object-support is itself removed from the operative afflictive structure.

```text
affliction K
    has object-support O
O is abandoned
    ↓
K is exhausted
```

This sharply distinguishes:

```text
knowing an object
from
abandoning an object-support
```

### 4. Exhaustion through arising of the counteragent

The first three modes concern afflictions abandoned by seeing. Vasubandhu then changes class:

> *bhāvanāheyānāṃ punaḥ pratipakṣodayāt kṣayaḥ /*

> “But for those to be abandoned by cultivation, exhaustion occurs through the arising of the counteragent.”

He explains:

> *yasya hi kleśaprakārasya pratipakṣo mārga utpadyate sa prahīyate /*

> “Whatever type of affliction has the path that is its counteragent arise—that [affliction] is abandoned.”

Thus:

```text
bhāvanā-heya kleśa-type
    ↓
its specific counteragent-path arises
    ↓
that kleśa-type is abandoned
```

Vasubandhu postpones the detailed matching of stronger and weaker affliction-grades with stronger and weaker counteragents:

> *adhimātrādhimātrasya mṛdumṛdur iti vistāreṇa paścāt pravedayiṣyāmaḥ /*

That graded analysis will be explained later.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya decisively rejects a simple rule:

```text
an affliction is abandoned
iff
its own object is completely known
```

Instead:

```text
kleśa-kṣaya
    may occur through

1. ālambana-parijñāna
2. tad-ālambana-saṃkṣaya
3. ālambana-prahāṇa
4. pratipakṣodaya
```

But these four are **not four chronological stages of one process**.

They are assigned to different structural cases:

```text
darśana-heya
    → first three modes

bhāvanā-heya
    → pratipakṣodaya
```

This is the main correction to any first-pass attempt to read the verse as a universal four-stage sequence.

### Knowing and abandoning are non-identical relations

The opening problem is especially important:

```text
when O is known
    K may not yet be abandoned

when K is abandoned
    O may not then be the object being known
```

Therefore:

```text
knowledge relation
    ≠
abandonment relation
```

The system must distinguish the object of cognition from the relational conditions under which an affliction persists.

### Abandonment can be relationally mediated

The second and third cases establish that a kleśa can disappear because another relation in its support-structure has been eliminated.

Thus an affliction is not treated as an isolated atom. It belongs to a network of:

```text
object-support
object-taking afflictions
same-domain universal afflictions
different-domain universal afflictions
contaminated / uncontaminated objects
```

Abandonment can therefore be indirect without being accidental.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Status:** Clarification only.

The existing translation correctly preserves four ablative grounds of *kṣaya*. The Bhāṣya establishes that they should not be read as four successive stages.

A slightly clarified translation is:

> **Exhaustion occurs through complete knowledge of the object-support, through exhaustion of what has that as its object-support, through abandonment of the object-support, or through the arising of the counteragent.**

The disjunctive “or” is interpretive but accurately reflects the Bhāṣya's allocation of distinct modes to distinct cases.

---

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.60 distinguishes at least four relations:

```text
Cognition(K, O)
    K completely knows O

TakesAsObject(A, B)
    A takes B as object-support

Abandons(O)
    an object-support is abandoned

Counteracts(P, K)
    path P functions as counteragent to K
```

No one of these can be substituted for the others.

The deepest logical result is:

```text
removal of a determination
need not be caused by
present direct cognition of that determination's object
```

Instead, removal may follow from alteration of the relational structure in which the determination is sustained.

That makes 5.60 a particularly clear example of **relation-indexed abandonment**.

## New Technical Terms

- **ālambana-parijñāna** — complete knowledge of the object-support.
  - **status:** previously encountered components; new as a precise abandonment-relation.
  - **function:** direct ground of exhaustion for specified seeing-abandonable afflictions.

- **tad-ālambana-saṃkṣaya** — exhaustion of that which has the relevant factor as its object-support.
  - **status:** new compound relation in this passage.
  - **function:** mediated abandonment through exhaustion of an object-taking afflictive relation.

- **ālambana-prahāṇa** — abandonment of the object-support.
  - **status:** new technical combination.
  - **function:** mediated ground of exhaustion for specified cessation/path seeing-abandonables.

- **pratipakṣodaya** — arising of the counteragent.
  - **status:** previously encountered *pratipakṣa*; newly decisive as the mode of exhaustion for cultivation-abandonables.
  - **function:** path-operation causing abandonment of the corresponding kleśa-type.

- **visabhāgadhātu-sarvatraga** — universal affliction belonging to a different domain.
  - **status:** specialized compound.
  - **function:** identifies the principal case motivating the problem of indirect abandonment.

- **sabhāgadhātu-sarvatraga** — universal affliction belonging to the same domain.
  - **status:** specialized compound.
  - **function:** supplies the relational mediator in the second mode of exhaustion.

## Closing Determination

VAK 5.60 replaces a naive epistemic model of abandonment with a relational one.

```text
Not every kleśa disappears
because its own object is directly known.
```

For seeing-abandonable afflictions, exhaustion may follow from direct knowledge of the object, from exhaustion of what takes the relevant factor as object, or from abandonment of the object-support itself. For cultivation-abandonable afflictions, exhaustion occurs when the corresponding counteragent-path arises.

The key distinction is therefore:

```text
object known
    ≠
affliction exhausted
```

Abandonment is determined by the precise relation through which the affliction is sustained and opposed.
