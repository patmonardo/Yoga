# VAK 5.69 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.69**

> naikayā pañcabhiryāvad darśanasthaḥ samanvitaḥ /
> bhāvanāsthaḥ punaḥ ṣaḍbhir ekayā vā dvayena vā // 5.69 //

**Translation:** One who stands in the path of seeing is endowed with none, then with as many as five. One who stands in the path of cultivation, however, is endowed with six, or with one, or with two.

## Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Who is endowed with how many parijñās?

A worldling is not endowed with even one.

Even a noble person standing in the Path of Seeing is not endowed with any parijñā as far as the patience of dharma-knowledge concerning Origin.

At the dharma-knowledge concerning Origin, one is endowed with one parijñā.

At the subsequent knowledge concerning Origin, with two.

At the dharma-knowledge concerning Cessation, with three; at the subsequent knowledge concerning Cessation, with four; at the dharma-knowledge concerning the Path, with five.

A noble person standing in the Path of Cultivation, at the subsequent knowledge concerning the Path, is endowed with six parijñās, so long as desire-domain dispassion has not been attained.

One who has attained desire-domain dispassion, whether previously or subsequently, or who has fallen from it and then attained it, is endowed with one parijñā: the parijñā of abandonment of the lower-part fetters.

One who has attained arhatship is likewise endowed with only one: the parijñā of complete exhaustion of all fetters.

One who has fallen through a form-domain paryavasthāna is again endowed with one: the parijñā of abandonment of the lower-part fetters.

One who has attained dispassion from form is endowed with two: the parijñā of abandonment of the lower-part fetters and the parijñā of destruction of attachment to form.

Even one who has fallen through a formless-domain paryavasthāna remains endowed with those same two.

Why, then, in the cases of the non-returner and the arhat, is only one parijñā established rather than several? This question leads directly into VAK 5.70.

---

## Argument

The question is not how many parijñās have ever arisen historically, but:

```text
kaḥ katibhiḥ parijñābhiḥ samanvāgataḥ
Who is presently endowed with how many parijñās?
```

The governing relation is therefore **samanvāgama**, present possession or endowment.

### 1. Before the first completed parijñā

```text
pṛthagjana
    → 0

darśanamārga through samudaya-dharmajñāna-kṣānti
    → 0
```

This explicitly confirms the completion-rule of VAK 5.68. The earlier destructions do not yet satisfy the conditions required for the technical designation `parijñā`.

### 2. Exact count during the Path of Seeing

The Bhāṣya gives a precise sequence:

| Path moment | Parijñās possessed |
|---|---:|
| through samudaya-dharmajñāna-kṣānti | 0 |
| samudaya-dharmajñāna | 1 |
| samudaya-anvayajñāna | 2 |
| nirodha-dharmajñāna | 3 |
| nirodha-anvayajñāna | 4 |
| mārga-dharmajñāna | 5 |

Thus the Kārikā's `naikayā pañcabhir yāvat` is not merely a loose range. The Bhāṣya assigns each count to a determinate knowledge-moment.

### 3. Entry into cultivation: six

At `mārga-anvayajñāna` the noble practitioner is described as `bhāvanāmārgastha` and is endowed with six parijñās, so long as desire-domain dispassion has not yet been attained.

```text
mārga-anvayajñāna
    → bhāvanāmārga standpoint
    → 6 parijñās
```

This is the completion of the six parijñās associated with abandonment through seeing.

### 4. Why the count may fall from six to one

When desire-domain dispassion is attained, the relevant present possession is established as one comprehensive parijñā:

```text
avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa-parijñā
```

Likewise at arhatship:

```text
sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā
```

The numerical reduction is therefore not a loss of prior liberation. The Bhāṣya is counting the parijñās with which one is presently technically `samanvāgata`.

The reason for this consolidation is deferred explicitly to 5.70.

### 5. Two-parijñā possession

After attainment of dispassion from form:

```text
avarabhāgīya-prahāṇa-parijñā
+
rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā
    → 2
```

If one subsequently falls through a formless-domain `paryavasthāna`, these two remain.

The Bhāṣya therefore distinguishes loss of a higher attainment from loss of the already established lower-domain completed abandonments.

---

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.69 distinguishes three different things that must not be collapsed:

```text
historical production of a parijñā
present possession of a parijñā
current technical counting of parijñās
```

The verse is about the second and third.

### Seeing is serial

During the Path of Seeing:

```text
0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5
```

Each number is tied to a specific knowledge-moment.

### Cultivation is not a monotonic count

At the opening of cultivation:

```text
6
```

but later:

```text
1
or
2
```

The lower number can correspond to a more comprehensive state of abandonment.

So:

```text
number possessed
    ≠
degree of liberation
```

This is not yet to be explained by an abstract principle of integration. The Bhāṣya itself asks why the non-returner and arhat are established with only one parijñā, and answers that question in 5.70 through `saṃkalana`.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **samanvāgata** — endowed with, presently possessed of.
  - **status:** previously encountered in the Kośa, but crucial here as the governing relation.
  - **function:** distinguishes present possession from historical production.

- **darśanastha / darśanamārgastha** — one standing in the Path of Seeing.
  - **status:** established path terminology.
  - **function:** locates the 0–5 parijñā sequence.

- **bhāvanāstha / bhāvanāmārgastha** — one standing in the Path of Cultivation.
  - **status:** established path terminology.
  - **function:** locates the 6/1/2 possession patterns.

- **kāmavairāgya** — dispassion from the desire-domain.
  - **status:** established term.
  - **function:** condition under which the sixfold possession is replaced by the lower-fetter abandonment parijñā.

- **rūpavairāgya** — dispassion from the form-domain.
  - **status:** established term.
  - **function:** condition for possession of two parijñās.

- **paryavasthāna** — active afflictive enveloping/manifest affliction.
  - **status:** previously classified in Chapter 5.
  - **function:** here marks a possible fall from an attainment without necessarily erasing all previously established parijñās.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing translation is broadly correct, but the Bhāṣya fixes the counts precisely:

```text
darśanamārga:
0 through samudaya-dharmajñāna-kṣānti
1 at samudaya-dharmajñāna
2 at samudaya-anvayajñāna
3 at nirodha-dharmajñāna
4 at nirodha-anvayajñāna
5 at mārga-dharmajñāna

bhāvanāmārga:
6 at mārga-anvayajñāna before kāmavairāgya
then, according to attainment-state,
1 or 2
```

The first-pass suggestion that the later reduction itself is already explained by “integration” should be postponed to VAK 5.70, where the Bhāṣya explicitly introduces `saṃkalana`.

---

## Logical Determination

The verse establishes a **state-indexed possession relation**:

```text
Parijñā-count
    = f(path moment, attainment state, retained disconnections)
```

It therefore blocks a simple cumulative model:

```text
later path stage
    ≠
necessarily larger parijñā count
```

The count is relational and stage-sensitive.

A practitioner may move:

```text
5 → 6 → 1 → 2 → 1
```

without that numerical pattern by itself expressing regression or progress. The meaning depends on which parijñā or parijñās are presently possessed.

---

## Minimal Organon Contact Point

The useful formal distinction is simply:

```text
EventHistory
    ≠
CurrentPossessionState
```

A system tracking liberation-state cannot use a monotonic counter of prior achievements. It must represent which completed abandonments are presently counted as possessed under the current path-state.

No stronger Organon interpretation is required here.

## Core Determination

```text
The Path of Seeing gives an exact 0–5 sequence
at determinate knowledge-moments.

At mārga-anvayajñāna the practitioner,
now treated as standing in cultivation,
possesses six.

Subsequent domain-dispassion and attainment states
may leave the practitioner technically endowed
with one or two comprehensive parijñās.

The reason for that consolidation
belongs to VAK 5.70.
```
