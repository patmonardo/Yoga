# VAK_5.06 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> bhavāgrajāḥ kṣāntivadhyā dṛggheyā eva śeṣajāḥ |
> dṛgbhāvanābhyām akṣāntivadhyā bhāvanayaiva tu || 5.6 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Those anuśayas born at the summit of existence and classed as `kṣānti-vadhya` are abandoned only by seeing. Those born on the remaining levels and classed as `kṣānti-vadhya` are abandoned by seeing for an ārya and by cultivation for a pṛthagjana. Those classed as `akṣānti-vadhya` are abandoned only by cultivation.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Concerning these anuśayas said to be abandoned by seeing: are they necessarily abandoned only by seeing? No. What then?

Those born on the level of the summit of existence that are to be abandoned through `anvayajñānakṣānti` are abandoned only by seeing, not by cultivation.

For the remaining levels, the phrase `kṣānti-vadhya` continues to govern. As appropriate, anuśayas associated with `dharmajñānakṣānti` and `anvayajñānakṣānti` are abandoned by seeing for āryas, but by cultivation for pṛthagjanas.

Those classed as `akṣānti-vadhya` are abandoned only by cultivation. On every level, those classed as `jñāna-vadhya` are always cultivation-abandoned for both groups.

**Voice: Other teachers (`apare`)**

Some say that outsiders do not abandon what is properly classified as `darśana-prahātavya`. They cite the Mahākarmavibhāgasūtra: speculative views concerning the past can still occur among persons free from desire, and these views take the desire domain as their cognitive support. Since form-domain afflictions cannot have the desire domain as support in such practitioners, those views must remain desire-domain-associated.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

The Vaibhāṣikas answer that the relevant earlier afflictions had fallen away at the same moment the later views arose, with Devadatta given as the comparison.

## Course of the Argument

The Bhāṣya corrects a simple reading of the prior classification.

```text
Question:
Does darśana-prahātavya always mean
abandoned only through seeing?

Answer:
No.
```

The actual rule is relational.

```text
bhavāgra + kṣānti-vadhya
    → darśana-heya only

remaining levels + kṣānti-vadhya
    → darśana-heya for ārya
    → bhāvanā-heya for pṛthagjana

jñāna-vadhya
    → bhāvanā-heya for both
```

This settles the compressed `dṛgbhāvanābhyām`. It does not simply mean that seeing and cultivation jointly apply to one undifferentiated case. The distribution depends upon practitioner-status.

---

## Kṣānti and Jñāna

The Bhāṣya confirms that `kṣānti` is a technical Path term, not ordinary patience. It appears here in:

```text
dharmajñānakṣānti
anvayajñānakṣānti
```

The passage also contrasts:

```text
kṣānti-vadhya
jñāna-vadhya
```

Thus `vadhya` marks the relevant counteragent classification, while `heya` marks the corresponding mode of abandonment.

---

## Pṛthagjana and Ārya

The same lower-level kṣānti-class anuśaya receives a different abandonment classification according to the practitioner:

```text
ārya       → darśana-heya
pṛthagjana → bhāvanā-heya
```

Therefore `darśana-heya` is not simply an intrinsic label attached to an affliction taken in isolation. It belongs to a relation among affliction, level, counteragent, and practitioner.

This is a major refinement of the Path matrix established in VAK 5.04–5.05.

---

## The Dispute over Outsiders

**Voice: Other teachers (`apare`)**

Their position is that outsiders do not in fact abandon what is properly seeing-abandonable. Their evidence is the occurrence of desire-domain-supported speculative views among persons already free from desire.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

The Vaibhāṣikas reject the inference that the earlier affliction therefore remained continuously present. Their reply is that the earlier afflictions had fallen away at the same moment the later views arose.

The local text does not fully explain the Devadatta comparison, so no stronger reconstruction is imposed here.

## New Technical Terms

- **bhavāgrabhūmija** — born on the level of the summit of existence
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `bhavāgraja`
  - **function:** specifies the exceptional seeing-only class

- **anvayajñānakṣānti** — kṣānti associated with anvaya-knowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical Path term
  - **function:** counteragent term for the relevant bhavāgra anuśayas

- **dharmajñānakṣānti** — kṣānti associated with dharma-knowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical Path term
  - **function:** counteragent term for relevant lower-level anuśayas

- **kṣānti-vadhya** — kṣānti-classed with respect to counteraction
  - **status:** Kārikā term unpacked by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** counteragent classification

- **jñāna-vadhya** — jñāna-classed with respect to counteraction
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of `akṣānti-vadhya`
  - **function:** marks the cultivation-abandoned class for both groups

- **ārya** — noble practitioner
  - **status:** previously encountered, decisive here
  - **function:** subject for whom relevant lower-level anuśayas are seeing-abandoned

- **pṛthagjana** — ordinary person
  - **status:** previously encountered, decisive here
  - **function:** subject for whom corresponding anuśayas are cultivation-abandoned

- **bāhyaka** — outsider, non-Buddhist practitioner
  - **status:** Bhāṣya polemical term
  - **function:** subject of the doctrinal dispute

- **samudācāra** — active manifestation or occurrence
  - **status:** previously encountered technical term
  - **function:** occurrence of views used in the objection

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.06 replaces a simple binary classification with a relational one:

```text
AbandonmentMode
    = f(
        anuśaya,
        level,
        counteragent,
        practitioner-status
      )
```

At bhavāgra, the relevant kṣānti-class anuśayas are seeing-abandoned only. On lower levels, comparable anuśayas are seeing-abandoned for āryas but cultivation-abandoned for ordinary persons. Jñāna-class anuśayas are cultivation-abandoned for both.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass translation correctly preserved uncertainty. The Bhāṣya now settles the predicate distribution.

A better canonical translation is:

> Those born at the summit of existence and classed as kṣānti-vadhya are abandoned only by seeing. Those born on the remaining levels and likewise classed as kṣānti-vadhya are abandoned by seeing for noble practitioners and by cultivation for ordinary persons. But those classed as akṣānti-vadhya are abandoned only by cultivation.

The decisive point is that `dṛgbhāvanābhyām` distributes according to practitioner-status rather than simply naming two operations jointly applied to the same case.

The verse makes abandonment explicitly context-sensitive.

AfflictedDetermination

## Light Organon Contact Point

The passage supplies a strong Rational Kośa design principle directly from the text: a correction rule must be indexed not only to an error-class but also to the state of the knowing agent.

```text
same afflictive class
+ different practitioner-state
→ different valid removal mode
```

This is stronger than a static taxonomy. It is a typed theory of practical correction.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.06 resolves the compressed syntax of the Kārikā and gives the Path matrix a new precision:

> **For kṣānti-class anuśayas, the mode of abandonment depends both on level and practitioner-status. At bhavāgra the relevant anuśayas are abandoned only by seeing; on lower levels they are abandoned by seeing for an ārya but by cultivation for a pṛthagjana. Jñāna-class anuśayas are cultivation-abandoned for both.**

The appended controversy confirms that the possibility of mundane abandonment by outsiders was itself disputed.
