# VAK_5.33 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

**IAST**

*tato ’ntagrahaṇaṃ tasmāc chīlāmarśas tato dṛśaḥ |*
*rāgaḥ svadṛṣṭau mānaś ca dveṣo ’nyatra ity anukramaḥ || 5.33 ||*

**Best current translation**

From that comes grasping an extreme; from that, attachment to discipline and observance; from that, attachment to view. Then attachment and conceit arise with respect to one’s own view, and hostility toward what is opposed to it. Such is the sequence.

## Literal Prose Translation

From false view, it is said, personality-view proceeds, because after excluding the aggregates as suffering one fixates upon them as self.

From personality-view, extreme view proceeds, through grasping the self under the extremes of permanence and annihilation.

From extreme grasping arises attachment to discipline and observance, because one expects purity through precisely the extreme that one has grasped.

From attachment to discipline and observance arises attachment to view: the term *āmarśa* carries forward. One grasps as supreme that through which one expects purity.

Then attachment and conceit arise with respect to one’s own view: attachment through clinging to it, conceit through being elevated by it.

Hostility arises elsewhere: for one firmly settled in one’s own view, hostility arises toward another view that stands opposed to it.

Other teachers instead hold that hostility occurs when another position is adopted within one’s own continuum and then abandoned, because the attachment and related afflictions abandoned by seeing take as object a view belonging to one’s own continuum.

Such is the sequence. This is the order of arising of these ten afflictions.

## Philosophical Translation

The process of affliction develops by determinate mediation. False determination stabilizes as a self-view. Once a self is posited, thought grasps it under an extreme—permanent or annihilated. The extreme is then converted into a supposed practical means of purification; that means is elevated into a superior doctrine; the doctrine becomes an object of attachment and a ground for self-elevation; and whatever opposes it becomes an object of hostility.

Thus the afflictions are not merely a list. They form a `pravṛttikrama`, an ordered genesis in which cognitive error becomes normative fixation, affective investment, and finally opposition.

## Course of Argument

### 1. From false view to personality-view

**Voice: Vasubandhu reporting the doctrinal sequence.**

The transition from 5.32 is explicit:

```text
mithyādṛṣṭi
    → satkāyadṛṣṭi
```

The basis is `ātmatā-abhiniveśa`, fixation upon the aggregates as self despite their determination as suffering.

### 2. Personality-view generates the extreme

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
    → antagrāhadṛṣṭi
```

Once a self has been posited, it is grasped according to the alternatives:

```text
śāśvata
    permanent

uccheda
    annihilated
```

The extreme therefore presupposes the prior construction of the self whose temporal fate is being asserted.

### 3. Extreme view becomes practical absolutization

```text
antagrāha
    → śīlavrataparāmarśa
```

The decisive phrase is:

```text
yam eva antaṃ gṛhṇāti
tena śuddhi-pratyāgamanāt
```

One expects purity through the very extreme one has adopted. A speculative fixation is converted into a practical program.

### 4. Practice becomes superior doctrine

The Bhāṣya explicitly carries `āmarśa` forward:

```text
śīlavrataparāmarśa
    → dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
```

The practitioner grasps as supreme that through which purity is expected.

Thus the sequence is not merely:

```text
wrong belief
    → wrong practice
```

but:

```text
wrong belief
    → absolutized practice
    → elevation of that practice/view as supreme
```

### 5. View acquires affective force

```text
sva-dṛṣṭi
    → rāga
    → māna
```

The Bhāṣya gives different grounds:

- `abhiṣvaṅga` — clinging or affective adhesion: attachment.
- `unnati-gamana` — going into elevation: conceit.

The same view thus becomes both possessed and used as a basis of comparative self-elevation.

### 6. Opposed view becomes object of hostility

```text
svadṛṣṭyadhyavasāya
    + paradṛṣṭi as pratyanīka
        → dveṣa
```

Hostility is generated toward a view that stands in opposition to the adopted view.

### 7. Alternative interpretation

**Voice: Other teachers (`apare`).**

The alternative does not take `anyatra` simply as a rival view held by another person. It understands hostility as directed toward a view that has arisen within one’s own continuum and has later been abandoned.

Their reason is technical: afflictions such as attachment that are abandoned by seeing take as object a view belonging to one’s own continuum (`svāsāntānika-dṛṣṭi`).

This alternative should be preserved rather than collapsed into the more obvious inter-doctrinal reading.

### 8. Closure

The Bhāṣya concludes:

```text
eṣa eṣāṃ daśānāṃ kleśānāṃ pravṛttikramaḥ
```

This is the **order of arising of the ten afflictions**.

So 5.32–5.33 constitute one natural argumentative unit: a genetic account of the ten kleśas.

## New Technical Terms

- **pravṛttikrama** — order of arising or manifestation.  
  **Status:** important Bhāṣya-level characterization of the whole sequence.  
  **Function:** converts a list of ten kleśas into a genetic series.

- **abhiniveśa** — fixation, forceful adherence.  
  **Status:** familiar Buddhist term, here given a specific function.  
  **Function:** explains stabilization of the aggregates as self.

- **abhiṣvaṅga** — clinging, affective adhesion.  
  **Status:** explanatory Bhāṣya vocabulary.  
  **Function:** ground of attachment to one’s own view.

- **unnati-gamana** — movement into elevation.  
  **Status:** explanatory Bhāṣya vocabulary.  
  **Function:** ground of conceit with respect to one’s own view.

- **pratyanīka** — opposed, counterposed, antagonistic.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term.  
  **Function:** determines the rival view as object of hostility.

- **svāsāntānika-dṛṣṭi** — a view belonging to one’s own continuum.  
  **Status:** introduced in the alternative interpretation.  
  **Function:** technical restriction on the object of the afflictions abandoned by seeing.

## Doctrinal Determination

The ten kleśas possess not only a classificatory order but a genetic one.

A compressed reconstruction of 5.32–5.33 is:

```text
avidyā / moha
    → vicikitsā
        → mithyāniścaya / mithyādṛṣṭi
            → satkāyadṛṣṭi
                → antagrāhadṛṣṭi
                    → śīlavrataparāmarśa
                        → dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
                            → rāga in one's own view
                            → māna through one's own view
                            → dveṣa toward the opposed view
```

The sequence therefore passes through distinct levels:

```text
cognitive non-comprehension
    → divided cognition
    → false determination
    → false identity
    → extreme metaphysical fixation
    → absolutized practice
    → doctrinal supremacy
    → affective appropriation
    → self-elevation
    → hostility
```

This should not be reduced to a deterministic psychology claiming that every actual occurrence must follow every preceding step. The text presents a `pravṛttikrama`: a doctrinal order explaining how the ten afflictions are intelligibly generated from one another.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The first-pass translation is broadly correct, but `tato dṛśaḥ` should not be left vague. The Bhāṣya explicitly supplies:

```text
śīlavrataparāmarśāt
dṛṣṭiparāmarśaḥ pravartate
```

Accordingly, the cleaned translation should make `dṛṣṭiparāmarśa` explicit rather than merely saying “regarding the view.”

Recommended translation:

> From that comes grasping an extreme; from that, attachment to discipline and observance; from that, attachment to views. Then attachment and conceit arise regarding one’s own view, and hostility toward what is opposed to it. Such is the sequence.

## Logical Determination

The deepest logical form is not simple succession but **generated determination**.

Each step supplies a condition under which the next becomes intelligible:

```text
false subject
    → requires a determinate fate

extreme fate
    → motivates a purificatory practice

purificatory practice
    → is elevated as supreme

supreme view
    → becomes appropriated

appropriated view
    → becomes ground of self-elevation

self-elevating view
    → determines opposition as hostile
```

The decisive distinction is:

```text
holding a view
    ≠
being attached to a view
    ≠
using the view as ground of self-elevation
    ≠
being hostile toward its contrary
```

The Bhāṣya therefore discriminates several relations that ordinary discourse often collapses into one notion of “belief.”

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage has a legitimate Organon relevance because the Bhāṣya itself is genetic.

A reasoning architecture should distinguish:

```text
assertion
commitment
identity-investment
comparative elevation
oppositional hostility
```

A false determination becomes much harder to revise once it has acquired the later relations in this sequence. Correction then cannot be modeled merely as replacing one proposition with another; it may require undoing a dependency chain.

The structurally useful point is:

```text
error propagation
    is relational and genetic
```

not merely:

```text
wrong proposition stored in memory
```

This is text-supported contact, not a claim that Vasubandhu is presenting a modern theory of computational belief revision.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.33 completes the genetic exposition begun in 5.32. Its strongest contribution is the explicit designation of the whole series as the `pravṛttikrama` of the ten kleśas. The Bhāṣya provides a ground for every transition and also preserves an alternative technical interpretation of `dveṣo 'nyatra`.
