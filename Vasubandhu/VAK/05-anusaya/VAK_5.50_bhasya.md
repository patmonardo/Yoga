# VAK 5.50 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.50**

```text
pradāśa upanāhaś ca vihiṃsā ceti rāgajau /
māyā-madau pratighaje upanāha-vihiṃsane // 5.50 //
```

**Translation:** Spite, rancor, and harmfulness—thus [the six stains are completed]. Deception and intoxicated pride are the two born of attachment; rancor and harmfulness are the two born of hostility.

## Translation

The six stains of affliction are completed by **pradāśa**, **upanāha**, and **vihiṃsā**.

Deception (*māyā*) is deceiving another.

Crookedness (*śāṭhya*) is crookedness of mind whereby one does not disclose matters as they really are, but diverts the issue or responds unclearly.

Intoxication (*mada*) has already been explained.

Spite or obstinate fault-clinging (*pradāśa*) is the firm grasping of a blameworthy matter whereby one does not accept a proper admonition or reasoned correction.

Rancor (*upanāha*) is the repeated cultivation or multiplication of a basis of resentment.

Harmfulness (*vihiṃsā*) is injuring others through striking, harshness, and the like.

Of these six stains, two are born of attachment: **māyā** and **mada**. Two are born of hostility: **upanāha** and **vihiṃsā**.

The remaining two stains, **pradāśa** and **śāṭhya**, receive their derivational explanation immediately afterward and therefore belong to the continuation into VAK 5.51.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / reported Abhidharma analysis**

### 1. Completion of the six kleśa-malas

VAK 5.49 introduced:

```text
māyā
śāṭhya
mada
```

VAK 5.50 completes the six with:

```text
pradāśa
upanāha
vihiṃsā
```

The full set is therefore:

```text
māyā
śāṭhya
mada
pradāśa
upanāha
vihiṃsā
```

Unlike the previous paryavasthāna discussion, the Bhāṣya does not need to reopen the number of members. The classificatory field is stable here: six kleśa-malas.

### 2. The Bhāṣya supplies functional definitions before genealogy

Before assigning roots, the prose defines what the stains actually do.

This gives three analytically distinct questions:

```text
What is the factor?
What does it do?
From which kleśa does it arise?
```

The order matters. The manifest determination is identified before its genetic affiliation is stated.

#### Māyā

```text
paravañcanā
    deceiving another
```

The definition is direct and relational. Māyā is not cosmic appearance; here it is deliberate deception.

#### Śāṭhya

```text
cittakauṭilya
    crookedness of mind
```

Its operation is failure to disclose matters as they are, instead diverting or obscuring the response. It concerns distorted presentation through a crooked mental orientation.

#### Mada

The Bhāṣya simply says that it has already been explained (`pūrvokta`). No new definition is introduced here.

#### Pradāśa

The key phrase is:

```text
sāvadyavastudṛḍhagrāhitā
    firm grasping of a blameworthy matter
```

Its consequence is:

```text
nyāyasaṃjñaptiṃ na gṛhṇāti
    one does not accept proper correction / reasoned admonition
```

This is more precise than merely “exposing another's fault.” The factor is an obstinate clinging to a blameworthy position that resists correction.

#### Upanāha

```text
āghātavastuvahulīkāra
    repeated cultivation / multiplication of a basis of resentment
```

Thus upanāha is not merely the first arising of hostility. It is hostility retained, reiterated, and intensified through its object or basis.

#### Vihiṃsā

```text
viheṭhana
    harming / injuring
```

The Bhāṣya illustrates this with striking, harshness, and similar forms of injury toward others.

### 3. Two rāga-born stains

The Kārikā and Bhāṣya state explicitly:

```text
rāgajau
    māyā
    mada
```

The relation is genetic: these two are born from attachment.

This should not be inflated beyond what the text gives. The Bhāṣya does not here offer a further philosophical proof of why both arise from rāga; it states the doctrinal genealogy.

### 4. Two pratigha-born stains

Likewise:

```text
pratighaje
    upanāha
    vihiṃsā
```

Rancor and harmfulness arise from hostility.

Their functional definitions make the relation intelligible without requiring us to invent a new derivational theory:

```text
pratigha
    ↓
resentment repeatedly cultivated
    = upanāha

pratigha
    ↓
injury toward others
    = vihiṃsā
```

One preserves hostility through time; the other expresses hostility as harm.

### 5. The genealogy is intentionally incomplete at the verse boundary

The six stains have now all been named, but only four receive their root affiliation in VAK 5.50.

```text
rāga
    → māyā
    → mada

pratigha
    → upanāha
    → vihiṃsā

pradāśa
    → deferred

śāṭhya
    → deferred
```

The very next Bhāṣya line begins the explanation of the remaining two:

```text
dṛṣṭyāmarśāt pradāśas tu
śāṭhyaṃ dṛṣṭisamutthitam
```

That belongs naturally with the transition to VAK 5.51 and should not be prematurely absorbed into the present verse's completed genealogy.

## New Technical Terms

- **paravañcanā** — deceiving another.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss of `māyā`.
  - **function:** functional definition.

- **cittakauṭilya** — crookedness of mind.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression.
  - **function:** definition of `śāṭhya`.

- **sāvadyavastudṛḍhagrāhitā** — firm grasping of a blameworthy matter.
  - **status:** new Bhāṣya formulation.
  - **function:** definition of `pradāśa`.

- **nyāyasaṃjñapti** — proper or reasoned admonition / instruction.
  - **status:** new local expression.
  - **function:** identifies what `pradāśa` refuses to accept.

- **āghātavastuvahulīkāra** — multiplication or repeated cultivation of a basis of resentment.
  - **status:** new Bhāṣya formulation.
  - **function:** definition of `upanāha`.

- **viheṭhana** — harming, injuring, tormenting.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss of `vihiṃsā`.
  - **function:** functional definition.

- **rāgaja** — born from attachment.
  - **status:** Kārikā term made explicit by Bhāṣya context.
  - **function:** genetic affiliation.

- **pratighaja** — born from hostility.
  - **status:** Kārikā term made explicit by Bhāṣya context.
  - **function:** genetic affiliation.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.50 gives a controlled genealogy of four of the six kleśa-malas.

```text
kleśa-mala
    one class by staining function

but

genetic source
    differs by member
```

The class therefore cannot be defined simply by common origin.

Its unity is classificatory, while its members retain distinct causal affiliations:

```text
common class
    kleśa-mala

plural roots
    rāga
    pratigha
    other roots to be specified
```

This reinforces a lesson already emerging throughout VAK 5.41–5.50:

```text
classification
    ≠
genetic derivation
```

A single class may contain members generated by different roots.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only**, with one important lexical correction in the surrounding analysis.

The Kārikā translation is substantially confirmed:

> Spite, rancor, and harmfulness—thus [the six stains are completed]. Deception and intoxicated pride are the two born of attachment; rancor and harmfulness are the two born of hostility.

However, **pradāśa** should not be characterized primarily as exposing or attacking another's fault. The Bhāṣya defines it as obstinate grasping of a blameworthy matter such that proper correction is not accepted.

## Logical Determination

The passage distinguishes three relations that must remain separate:

```text
membership
    x is a kleśa-mala

function
    x deceives / resists correction / retains resentment / harms

genesis
    x is born from rāga / pratigha / another root
```

Thus:

```text
same class
    does not entail
same function

same class
    does not entail
same cause
```

The cleanest representation is relational:

```text
Māyā
    memberOf KleśaMala
    function: deception
    bornFrom: Rāga

Upanāha
    memberOf KleśaMala
    function: repeated resentment
    bornFrom: Pratigha
```

This is stronger than treating the six as six species differentiated only by names. Their determinations arise from several independent relations.

## Organon Contact Point

The passage gives the Platform an important rule of scientific modeling:

```text
Class
Cause
Function
```

must be represented independently.

A classification system that identifies these relations would lose exactly the distinctions Vasubandhu is making here.

For example:

```text
instanceOf(x, KleśaMala)
causedBy(x, Rāga)
performsFunction(x, Deception)
```

are separate judgments.

This is a genuine internal mediation, not an external analogy. The Bhāṣya itself moves through these determinations separately: it first defines each factor, then states the causal affiliations of selected members, while retaining all six within one class.

The result is a model in which identity is relationally articulated rather than exhausted by a single taxonomic label.
