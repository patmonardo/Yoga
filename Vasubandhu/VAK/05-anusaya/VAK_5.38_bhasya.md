# VAK 5.38 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.38**

`yathoktā eva sāvidyā dvidhā dṛṣṭivivecanāt |`
`upādānāni avidyā tu grāhikā neti miśritā ||`

Best current translation:

> The very yokes already stated, together with ignorance, are the appropriations; the views are divided in two. Ignorance, however, is not itself a grasper, and so is mixed among them.

## Translation

The previously stated yokes, together with ignorance, are called the appropriations. The view-yoke is divided into two.

The desire-yoke together with ignorance is the appropriation of desire, comprising thirty-four real items: twenty consisting of craving, aversion, conceit, and ignorance; four doubts; and ten paryavasthānas.

The existence-yoke together with ignorance is the appropriation of the doctrine of self, comprising thirty-eight real items: thirty consisting of craving, conceit, and ignorance, and eight doubts.

From the view-yoke, discipline-and-observance is extracted. The remaining thirty items constitute appropriation through views. Discipline-and-observance itself forms a separate appropriation of six items, extracted from the views, because it opposes the path and deceives both parties.

Householders are deceived by it when they take fasting and similar practices to be a path to heaven; renunciants are deceived when, through abstention from desired objects, they expect purification.

Why is ignorance mixed into the appropriations instead of being established separately? Because appropriations are so called from the sense of grasping existence. Ignorance, being characterized by non-discernment and lacking sharpness, is not itself a grasping power. Therefore it is mixed with the other appropriations.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expositor**

1. The already established **yoga** classes are taken as the basis for the upādāna classification.
2. Ignorance, which had been separately classified under āsrava, ogha, and yoga, is now added back into the relevant groups.
3. The former single `dṛṣṭiyoga` is divided into two appropriations:
   - `dṛṣṭyupādāna`
   - `śīlavratopādāna`
4. This yields four appropriations:
   - `kāmopādāna`
   - `ātmavādopādāna`
   - `dṛṣṭyupādāna`
   - `śīlavratopādāna`
5. A question then asks why ignorance is mixed into these groups rather than counted as a fifth independent appropriation.
6. The answer is functional: `upādāna` is defined through grasping; ignorance is non-discriminating and unsharp, so it conditions grasping but does not itself perform the grasp.

## Why `śīlavratopādāna` Is Separate

The Bhāṣya gives two reasons:

```text
mārgapratidvandvitva
    opposition to the path

ubhayapakṣavipralambhana
    deception of both sides
```

The two sides are householders and renunciants.

For householders:

```text
fasting and similar practices
    → imagined as a path to heaven
```

For renunciants:

```text
mere avoidance of desired objects
    → imagined as sufficient for purification
```

So `śīlavrataparāmarśa` is not merely a mistaken practice. It is a practical false universal: a conditioned observance is elevated into a sufficient path.

## New Technical Terms

- **kāmopādāna** — appropriation of desire; **status:** new functional classification here; **function:** groups desire-domain yoke factors together with ignorance under grasping.
- **ātmavādopādāna** — appropriation of the doctrine of self; **status:** new technical grouping; **function:** higher-domain existence-yoke plus ignorance, treated under self-appropriation.
- **dṛṣṭyupādāna** — appropriation through views; **status:** new subdivision of the former view-yoke; **function:** grasping through doctrinal determination apart from discipline-and-observance.
- **śīlavratopādāna** — appropriation through discipline and observance; **status:** new subdivision; **function:** isolates a path-obstructing practical fixation.
- **grāhikā** — grasping/seizing power; **status:** explicit Bhāṣya criterion; **function:** determines whether something can constitute an independent upādāna.
- **asaṃprakhyāna-lakṣaṇa** — characterized by non-discernment/non-clear comprehension; **status:** Bhāṣya explanation of avidyā; **function:** explains why ignorance lacks independent grasping power.
- **mārgapratidvandvitva** — opposition to the path; **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term in this unit; **function:** justifies separate `śīlavratopādāna`.
- **ubhayapakṣavipralambhana** — deceiving both sides/parties; **status:** Bhāṣya-only; **function:** explains the practical breadth of the error.

## Doctrinal Determination

The same anuśaya-field is once again repartitioned by a new operative criterion.

```text
āsrava
    what seeps / sustains afflictive continuity

ogha
    what carries away

yoga
    what fastens

upādāna
    what grasps / appropriates
```

The extension overlaps heavily, but the intension differs.

The decisive result in 5.38 is:

```text
condition of grasping
    ≠
grasser
```

Ignorance is required within the appropriative complexes, but because it is `asaṃprakhyāna-lakṣaṇa` and `apaṭu`, it does not receive a fifth independent upādāna heading.

Thus:

```text
avidyā
    enables and accompanies appropriation

but

avidyā
    does not independently seize
```

This is consistent with 5.36, where ignorance was separated because of roothood. Here, under a different criterion, the very same ignorance is mixed back into the groups because roothood is no longer the relevant question: grasping-power is.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.38 gives an unusually clean demonstration that classificatory status is **relation-dependent**.

```text
Under roothood:
    avidyā → separate

Under grasping-power:
    avidyā → mixed
```

There is no contradiction because the predicate has changed.

The same determination may therefore occupy different structural positions under different universals.

A second logical result is the distinction:

```text
EnablingCondition
    ≠
ExecutingPower
```

Ignorance weakens discrimination and makes grasping possible, while craving, views, observance-fixation, and self-doctrine perform the positive appropriation.

## Reflective Vocabulary

- `pṛthak` / separation: whether a factor merits an independent heading depends on the operative criterion.
- `miśrita` / mixture: a ground may be distributed through a complex without being independently named at that level.
- `grāhikā` / grasping power: positive determinative appropriation.
- `apaṭutva` / lack of sharpness: explanatory ground for why ignorance cannot function as an independent grasper.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation correctly saw that ignorance is mixed because it is not a grasper. The Bhāṣya clarifies:

1. `yathoktāḥ` refers most immediately to the previously stated **yogas**.
2. `miśritā` means that ignorance is distributed into the four appropriation complexes rather than established as a fifth appropriation.
3. `grāhikā` is denied because ignorance is characterized by non-discernment and lack of sharpness.
