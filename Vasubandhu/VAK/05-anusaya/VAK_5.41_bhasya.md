**Translation:** The same afflictive determinations are taught again in five divisions beginning with fetters. The views constitute distinct fetters because of commonality of substance and of grasping.

## Translation

The very same anuśayas are again divided and taught fivefold according to the distinction of **fetter, bond, anuśaya, secondary affliction, and enveloping manifestation**.

Among these there are nine fetters: the fetters of attraction, hostility, conceit, ignorance, view, grasping-at-view, doubt, envy, and avarice.

The fetter of attraction is desire belonging to the three domains. The others are to be connected in the corresponding way. The fetter of view consists of three views, while the fetter of grasping consists of two views.

For precisely this reason it is said that there can be a case in which, with respect to dharmas associated with view, one is bound by the fetter of attraction but not by the fetter of view, even though the anuśaya of view does not therefore fail to lie latent there.

Such a case exists when knowledge of arising has occurred but knowledge of cessation has not yet occurred: namely, with respect to dharmas associated with grasping-at-view and grasping-at-rule-and-observance that are to be abandoned by seeing cessation and the path.

With respect to these, one is bound by the fetter of attraction insofar as it takes them as object, but not by the fetter of view. This is because the universal view has already been abandoned, while there is no non-universal member of the fetter of view that both takes these dharmas as object and is associated with them.

Nevertheless the anuśaya of view lies latent in them: precisely those two grasping-views do so through association.

Why, then, among the fetters are the three views separately called the fetter of view, while the remaining two views are separately called the fetter of grasping?

Because of commonality of substance and of grasping, the two classes of view are made into distinct fetters.

The three views comprise eighteen real items; the two grasping-views likewise comprise eighteen. Thus, it is said, they are made into distinct fetters because of commonality in their dravya-constitution. Moreover the two grasping-views share the nature of grasping, and therefore they are also separately established through commonality of grasping, there being a distinction between what is grasped and what grasps.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / reported Abhidharma classification**

VAK 5.41 does not merely continue the synonym-series of 5.35–5.40. It explicitly opens a **new principle of division**.

```text
ta eva anuśayāḥ
    the very same anuśayas
        ↓
punaḥ ... pañcadhā
    are divided again fivefold
```

The five modes are:

```text
saṃyojana
bandhana
anuśaya
upakleśa
paryavasthāna
```

The important point is the identity of the field together with a change of classificatory relation. The underlying afflictive determinations are not multiplied merely because they are redescribed under a new doctrinal heading.

### 1. The nine fetters

The Bhāṣya immediately enumerates nine `saṃyojana`:

```text
anunaya        attraction / attachment
pratigha       hostility
māna           conceit
avidyā         ignorance
dṛṣṭi          view
parāmarśa      grasping-at-view
vicikitsā      doubt
īrṣyā          envy
mātsarya       avarice
```

The first example establishes the mode of conversion from the earlier anuśaya scheme:

```text
anunaya-saṃyojana
    = traidhātuka-rāga
    = desire across the three domains
```

The remaining items are to be related in the corresponding way (`yathāsaṃbhavam`).

This is therefore not a new inventory of nine unrelated afflictions. It is a **reclassification of the already established afflictive field according to bondage**.

### 2. The five views do not become five fetters

The five doctrinal views are grouped as:

```text
dṛṣṭi-saṃyojana
    three views

parāmarśa-saṃyojana
    two views
```

The Bhāṣya thereby confirms a point left open in the Kārikā pass: `dṛṣṭī` refers to the **two grouped classes of views**, not simply two individual views.

The distribution is:

```text
dṛṣṭi-saṃyojana
    satkāyadṛṣṭi
    antagrāhadṛṣṭi
    mithyādṛṣṭi

parāmarśa-saṃyojana
    dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
    śīlavrataparāmarśa
```

### 3. Fetter and anuśaya are not extensionally identical predicates

The Bhāṣya then inserts a highly technical example. Its purpose is crucial: it prevents us from treating `dṛṣṭisaṃyojana` and `dṛṣṭyanuśaya` as interchangeable names.

A dharma associated with one of the two parāmarśa-views can satisfy:

```text
bound by anunaya-saṃyojana
not bound by dṛṣṭi-saṃyojana
and yet
dṛṣṭyanuśaya lies latent there
```

The example is staged between two moments of the path:

```text
samudayajñāna has arisen
nirodhajñāna has not yet arisen
```

At that stage the universal views have already been abandoned, so the relevant `dṛṣṭisaṃyojana` no longer binds those dharmas. Yet the two parāmarśa-views remain `dṛṣṭyanuśaya` through association (`saṃprayogataḥ`).

This is a decisive classificatory lesson:

```text
same dharma
    can fall under dṛṣṭyanuśaya
    without currently falling under dṛṣṭisaṃyojana
```

Thus:

```text
anuśaya-classification
    ≠
saṃyojana-classification
```

They cut the same afflictive field according to different relations.

### 4. Why three views versus two grasping-views?

The question is then asked explicitly:

```text
Why are three views one fetter,
while the other two views form a different fetter?
```

The answer gives two grounds:

```text
dravya-sāmānya
āmarśana-sāmānya
```

#### Dravya-sāmānya

The Bhāṣya does not merely say that all five views share prajñā as an abstract cognitive substance. It gives an **enumerative dravya-ground**:

```text
three views
    = eighteen dravyas

two parāmarśa-views
    = likewise eighteen dravyas
```

So the immediate Bhāṣya sense of `dravya-sāmānya` is more determinate than the first-pass note suggested. The grouping is justified by equality/commonality in the real-item constitution of each class.

The first-pass statement that the five views are grouped simply because they all have afflicted prajñā as substance is therefore too broad and should not be retained as the direct explanation of this verse.

#### Āmarśana-sāmānya

The second ground applies especially to the pair:

```text
dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
śīlavrataparāmarśa
```

Both share `parāmarśa-svabhāva`, the nature of grasping or taking hold under an estimative determination.

The Bhāṣya closes with:

```text
grāhya-grāhaka-bheda
    distinction between grasped and grasper
```

This is philosophically important. The two classes are not distinguished merely by lexical convention. A structural asymmetry is introduced:

```text
three views
    function as determinate view-contents / what is taken up

two parāmarśa-views
    function as the act of grasping, esteeming, or appropriating
```

This should be handled cautiously: the Bhāṣya gives the `grāhya/grāhaka` distinction very compactly. But it clearly signals that the two parāmarśa views are separated because their characteristic operation is not just having a view but **grasping at a determination as such**.

## New Technical Terms

- **saṃyojana-bandhana-anuśaya-upakleśa-paryavasthāna-bheda** — the fivefold classificatory division of the same afflictive field.
  - **status:** the individual terms have appeared, but their explicit fivefold coordination is new here.
  - **function:** classification principle.

- **traidhātuka-rāga** — desire belonging to all three domains.
  - **status:** previously encountered components; locally made explicit as the content of `anunaya-saṃyojana`.
  - **function:** definition / conversion between taxonomies.

- **saṃyukta / asaṃyukta** — bound / not bound by a given fetter in relation to specified dharmas.
  - **status:** previously encountered relational vocabulary.
  - **function:** demonstrates that fetter-membership is relation-specific.

- **dravya-sāmānya** — commonality in dravya-constitution; here supported by the equal enumeration of eighteen real items in each view-group.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya makes the Kārikā term determinate.
  - **function:** ground of classification.

- **parāmarśa-svabhāva** — the nature of grasping or estimative appropriation shared by the two parāmarśa views.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory formulation.
  - **function:** defines the formal unity of the pair.

- **grāhya-grāhaka-bheda** — distinction between what is grasped and what grasps.
  - **status:** new and technically important Bhāṣya vocabulary.
  - **function:** logical distinction / ground for separating the two fetter classes.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.41 establishes three important doctrinal controls.

### First: the same afflictions admit several grounded taxonomies

```text
same anuśayas
    ↓
classified as
saṃyojana / bandhana / anuśaya / upakleśa / paryavasthāna
```

The names are neither separate substances nor empty synonyms. Each isolates a different doctrinal relation.

### Second: anuśaya and saṃyojana must not be collapsed

The path-stage example proves that the extension of `dṛṣṭyanuśaya` is not identical to that of `dṛṣṭisaṃyojana` at every point.

```text
dṛṣṭyanuśaya
    affliction considered as latent/associated tendency

dṛṣṭisaṃyojana
    affliction considered under the specific relation of fettering
```

The distinction is real enough to support a counterexample.

### Third: classification is generated from determinate commonality

The division of five views into a group of three and a group of two is grounded by:

```text
constitution
    dravya-sāmānya

operation
    āmarśana-sāmānya
```

The Bhāṣya therefore replaces an arbitrary list with an internally grounded grouping.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised/clarified.**

The first-pass translation said:

> The same afflictive determinations are classified once more, now according to five modes beginning with bondage. Within the fetter classification, the views are gathered into two distinct bonds because the members of each group share both one cognitive substance and one mode of appropriative grasping.

Two corrections are required.

First, the fivefold list should be stated explicitly from the Bhāṣya:

```text
saṃyojana
bandhana
anuśaya
upakleśa
paryavasthāna
```

Second, `dravya-sāmānya` should not be reduced here to the statement that all five views share afflicted prajñā as their cognitive substance. Vasubandhu's prose gives a more specific ground: each of the two grouped classes comprises eighteen dravyas.

Preferred translation:

> The same afflictions are taught again in five divisions beginning with fetters. The two classes of views constitute distinct fetters because of commonality in their dravya-constitution and because the grasping-views share the character of grasping.

## Logical Determination

The strongest logical result is the separation of **entity**, **classification relation**, and **class-extension**.

```text
one afflictive dharma
    ↓ considered as anuśaya
may satisfy one predicate

same dharma
    ↓ considered as saṃyojana
may fail to satisfy the corresponding predicate
```

Formally:

```text
DṛṣṭyAnuśaya(x)
    does not entail
DṛṣṭiSaṃyojana(x, atEveryPathStage)
```

The Bhāṣya demonstrates this by constructing an actual path-stage counterexample rather than merely asserting that the names differ.

A second logical structure is classification by a generated common ground:

```text
members
    ↓
shared dravya-constitution
    +
shared characteristic operation
    ↓
determinate class
```

But VAK 5.41 also corrects a tempting abstraction. We should not immediately convert `dravya-sāmānya` into a generic theory of “same substrate.” The Bhāṣya's own ground is numerically and doctrinally specific: eighteen dravyas in each class.

The `grāhya/grāhaka` distinction then introduces a relational opposition:

```text
what is grasped
    ↔
what grasps
```

The class is therefore not produced merely by resemblance among isolated items. It arises from the role the determinations play within a relation of grasping.

## Organon Contact Point

This verse is exceptionally useful for the Organon, but only if we preserve Vasubandhu's own distinction first.

The direct architectural lesson is:

```text
Entity
    ≠ Classification
    ≠ ClassificationRelation
```

A single determination may participate in several taxonomies because each taxonomy asks a different question of the same field.

```text
Affliction
    classifiedAs anuśaya
    classifiedAs saṃyojana
    classifiedAs upakleśa
    classifiedAs paryavasthāna
```

The Bhāṣya goes further: membership can change with the **relational/path context** without the underlying determination thereby becoming a different substance.

That means Organon classification should be capable of representing:

```text
classification(entity, principle, context)
```

rather than treating every class-label as an intrinsic monadic type.

The `grāhya/grāhaka` close is especially significant. A determination is fully specified not only by what it is in isolation, but also by the role it occupies in a reciprocal relation:

```text
grāhya
    what is taken

āmarśana / grāhaka
    the taking-hold
```

This is a genuine internal relation disclosed by Vasubandhu's argument, not an external analogy. It therefore gives us a legitimate contact point with the project's insistence that determination becomes intelligible through mediation rather than through static identity alone.
