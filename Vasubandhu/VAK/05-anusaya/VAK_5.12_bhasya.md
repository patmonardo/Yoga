## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Of these ninety-eight anuśayas, how many are universal and how many are non-universal?

> The views to be abandoned through seeing suffering and origination are universal; likewise doubt, the ignorance associated with those views, and the independent ignorance.

The views and doubt to be abandoned through seeing suffering and origination, the ignorance associated with them, and the independent ignorance among those to be abandoned through seeing suffering and origination: these eleven anuśayas are universal within a homogeneous domain.

They are seven views, two doubts, and two ignorances, because their cognitive support is the whole of their own domain.

Do they take that support all at once or successively? If successively, the same would follow for other afflictions also.

If all at once, who apprehends purity with respect to the whole desire domain, or what is not a cause as cause with respect to the whole domain?

When the whole is spoken of, it is not said that the whole is necessarily taken all at once. Rather, all five modes may each be taken in their entirety.

Still, where there is self-view there is attachment to self; where there is a view of supreme purity there is aspiration toward that, and conceit concerning it. Thus attachment and conceit too would seem to become universal.

If so, since these two have cognitive supports belonging both to seeing-abandonment and cultivation-abandonment, by which would they be abandoned?

They would be cultivation-abandonable because their supports are mixed. Or alternatively, let them be seeing-abandonable because they operate through the strengthening power of view.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

These two are afflictions concerning particular characteristics, not afflictions concerning general characteristics. Therefore they are not universal.

## Course of the Argument

### Question

The ninety-eightfold system has been established. Vasubandhu now adds another classificatory axis:

```text
sarvatraga
    universally ranging

asarvatraga
    non-universal / restricted
```

The question is not how intense an affliction is, but how broadly its object-range operates.

### Initial Determination

The universal anuśayas number eleven:

```text
7 dṛṣṭis
2 vicikitsās
2 avidyās
-----------
11 sarvatragas
```

They belong to the classes abandoned through seeing suffering and origination.

The Bhāṣya's explanation is:

```text
sakala-svadhātu-ālambanatvāt
because their cognitive support is the whole of their own domain
```

This materially sharpens the Kārikā. `Sarvatraga` is not merely broad psychological influence. It is a technical relation to the object-range of an entire homogeneous domain.

### The Object-Range Problem

Vasubandhu immediately tests the phrase `sakala-svadhātu-ālambana`.

Does a universal anuśaya take the whole domain:

```text
yugapat
    simultaneously

or

krameṇa
    successively?
```

Neither formulation can be accepted crudely.

If universality meant mere successive coverage, many other afflictions could also qualify. But simultaneous apprehension of the whole domain creates implausible consequences.

The reply refines the claim:

```text
“whole”
    does not mean
one single simultaneous cognition of every item
```

Rather, the relevant fivefold classes may each range over their whole appropriate object-field.

### Objection: Why Not Attachment and Conceit?

The strongest dialectical moment follows.

If self-view ranges universally, attachment to self occurs with it. If a view takes something as supreme purity, aspiration toward it and conceit concerning it also arise.

Thus:

```text
dṛṣṭi universal
    ↓
tṛṣṇā follows
    ↓
māna follows
```

Why then are attachment and conceit not themselves universal?

This objection is important because it shows that co-occurrence with a universal view is insufficient to define universality.

The discussion tests two possible abandonment classifications for attachment and conceit:

```text
bhāvanāprahātavya
    because their supports are mixed

or

darśanaprahātavya
    because they operate through the strengthening power of view
```

### Vaibhāṣika Resolution

The Vaibhāṣika answer introduces a decisive distinction:

```text
svalakṣaṇa-kleśa
    affliction concerning a particular characteristic

sāmānya-kleśa
    affliction concerning a general characteristic
```

Attachment and conceit are said to be `svalakṣaṇa-kleśas`, not general afflictions.

Therefore:

```text
broad association
    ≠
sarvatragatva
```

A universal anuśaya must operate through a general determination of the domain, not merely arise wherever a universal view happens to occur.

## Voice Identification

The passage begins in Vasubandhu's explanatory and questioning voice.

The final resolution is explicitly attributed:

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position**

> `svalakṣaṇakleśāvetau na sāmānyakleśau / tasmānna sarvatragāviti vaibhāṣikāḥ /`

This explicit attribution is important. The distinction is doctrinally operative here, but we should not silently transform it into Vasubandhu's unqualified final position.

## New Technical Terms

- **sarvatraga** — universally ranging / universal
  - **status:** introduced by the Kārikā and technically defined by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** classifies anuśayas by general object-range

- **sabhāgadhātu** — homogeneous / corresponding domain
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** restricts universality to the relevant domain rather than to all possible existence without qualification

- **sakala-svadhātu-ālambana** — taking the whole of one's own domain as cognitive support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory formula
  - **function:** primary definition of the universal range

- **yugapat** — simultaneously
  - **status:** ordinary adverb used technically in the objection
  - **function:** tests whether universality requires simultaneous total apprehension

- **krameṇa** — successively
  - **status:** ordinary adverb used technically in the objection
  - **function:** alternative tested against the definition of universal range

- **vyāmiśrālambana** — mixed cognitive support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** proposed ground for classifying attachment and conceit as cultivation-abandonable

- **dṛṣṭibalādhāna** — strengthening / imposition of the power of view
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** alternative ground for seeing-abandonment

- **svalakṣaṇa-kleśa** — affliction directed to a particular characteristic
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical distinction
  - **function:** excludes attachment and conceit from the universal class

- **sāmānya-kleśa** — affliction directed to a general characteristic
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical distinction
  - **function:** identifies the structural type appropriate to universality

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.12 establishes that the universal anuśayas are not simply the strongest or most frequent afflictions.

They are universal because their mode of object-support is general:

```text
universal anuśaya
    → general determination
    → whole relevant domain as object-range
```

The count is:

```text
seven views
+ two doubts
+ two ignorances
= eleven
```

The Vaibhāṣika reply further distinguishes:

```text
sāmānya-kleśa
    general affliction
    can be sarvatraga

svalakṣaṇa-kleśa
    particularizing affliction
    is not sarvatraga
```

This explains why attachment and conceit are not made universal merely because they arise in association with universal views.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation correctly identifies the classes, but the Bhāṣya supplies the exact count and technical range.

A tightened translation is:

> The views abandoned through seeing suffering and origination are universal; so too are the two corresponding doubts, the ignorance associated with them, and the independent ignorance. These eleven range universally within their own homogeneous domain.

The expression `sarvatraga` should therefore be read technically as domain-wide general object-range, not merely as “pervasive” in an informal psychological sense.

---

## Logical Determination

The verse introduces a distinction between **extent** and **mode of determination**.

A state does not become universal merely because it can accompany a universal error over many cases.

The Vaibhāṣika answer requires:

```text
universality
    = general determination of the field

not merely
    repeated occurrence across particulars
```

This is a strong logical distinction:

```text
extension across many particulars
    ≠
general determination as such
```

The objection concerning attachment and conceit exposes precisely this difference.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The passage offers a useful distinction for the Rational Kośa between local and field-level error:

```text
svalakṣaṇa-kleśa
    error bound to a particular determination

sāmānya-kleśa
    error governing a general field
```

The important point is textual before architectural: Vasubandhu's discussion refuses to infer global status merely from repeated local association.

For a later Rational Agent, this suggests that a defect should be classified as global only when it corrupts a governing general determination, not simply because many downstream instances inherit its effects.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.12 establishes the first exact definition of the `sarvatraga` class:

> **Eleven anuśayas—seven views, two doubts, and two ignorances—are universal within their own homogeneous domain because they operate through general object-range. The ensuing objection shows that repeated co-occurrence with such views does not suffice for universality: attachment and conceit remain particularizing (`svalakṣaṇa`) afflictions rather than general (`sāmānya`) afflictions.**

This gives Chapter 5 a new axis of scientific classification: not merely what an affliction is and how it is abandoned, but the logical range over which its determination operates.
