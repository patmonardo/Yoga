## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Of these ninety-eight anuśayas, how many are unwholesome and how many are indeterminate?

> Above, all are indeterminate.

To begin with, all those belonging to the form and formless domains are indeterminate.

Why?

If afflicted dharmas there were unwholesome, they would have painful maturation. But such maturation does not occur in those two domains, because there is no cause there consisting in injury to others.

> In the desire-domain, satkāyadarśana,
>
> antagrāha, and the delusion together with those two—

In the desire-domain, satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi, together with the ignorance conjoined with them, are indeterminate.

Why?

Because they are not incompatible with giving and the other wholesome practices. A person gives a gift and guards moral discipline while thinking, “After death I shall be happy.” Even the annihilationist view can be favorable to liberation.

For this reason the Blessed One said that among the speculative views of outsiders, the foremost is the thought: “May I not be; may there not be anything of mine; I shall not be; there will be nothing of mine.”

Moreover, these two views are deluded with respect to their own object-complex and do not proceed toward injuring others.

**Voice: Objector**

Then the same consequence would follow for craving for heaven and for the conceit “I am.”

**Voice: Vasubandhu / report of earlier teachers**

The innate satkāyadṛṣṭi is indeterminate—the kind that occurs even in deer and birds. But the constructed or conceptually produced satkāyadṛṣṭi is unwholesome, according to the earlier teachers.

> But the remaining ones here are unwholesome.

The remaining anuśayas in the desire-domain are unwholesome.

## Course of the Argument

VAK 5.19 changes the axis of analysis. Verses 5.17–5.18 asked where and how an anuśaya adheres. The present verse asks instead:

```text
Of the ninety-eight anuśayas,
which are akuśala
and which are avyākṛta?
```

The Bhāṣya first establishes the general rule for the higher domains:

```text
rūpāvacara + ārūpyāvacara anuśayas
    ↓
all avyākṛta
```

The reason is explicitly karmic. If these afflicted dharmas were `akuśala`, they would yield painful maturation (`duḥkha-vipāka`). Yet the form and formless domains lack the relevant cause of such painful maturation, namely activity grounded in injury to others (`paravyābādha`).

This sharpens an important distinction:

```text
kliṣṭa
    ≠
akuśala
```

Affliction is broader than moral unwholesomeness.

The second determination concerns the desire-domain. Three cases are declared indeterminate:

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
antagrāhadṛṣṭi
avidyā conjoined with those two
```

Vasubandhu gives two reasons.

First, these views are not intrinsically opposed to wholesome practices such as giving and moral discipline:

```text
false view concerning self or postmortem existence
    can coexist with
 dāna + śīla
```

The example is explicit: one may give and keep discipline because one believes, “After death I shall be happy.” Therefore the mere presence of satkāyadṛṣṭi does not make the entire practical orientation unwholesome.

Second, the two views are characterized as primarily distortions concerning their own object-complex and as not directed toward harming others:

```text
svadravya-saṃmūḍhatva
    delusion regarding one's own object-complex

aparapīḍā-apravṛtti
    non-proceeding toward injury of others
```

The annihilationist extreme receives an especially striking qualification: Vasubandhu says that even `ucchedadṛṣṭi` may be `mokṣānukūla`, favorable to liberation, and supports this with a scriptural citation.

An objection then tests the adequacy of the reason:

```text
if non-injury and compatibility with wholesome conduct are sufficient,
why not classify heavenly craving and asmimāna as indeterminate too?
```

The reply introduces a further distinction inside satkāyadṛṣṭi itself:

```text
sahajā satkāyadṛṣṭi
    innate
    avyākṛta

vikalpitā satkāyadṛṣṭi
    constructed / conceptually produced
    akuśala
    according to earlier teachers
```

The appeal to animals—deer and birds—marks the innate form as pre-theoretical rather than doctrinally elaborated. The Bhāṣya therefore refuses to treat every occurrence of self-view as ethically homogeneous.

Finally, the residual rule is stated:

```text
all other desire-domain anuśayas
    = akuśala
```

## New Technical Terms

- **duḥkhavipāka** — painful maturation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit causal criterion
  - **function:** explains why higher-domain afflictions cannot be classified as unwholesome

- **paravyābādha** — injury or harm directed toward others
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** supplies the missing causal basis for painful maturation in the higher domains

- **aviruddha** — not incompatible, not opposed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reasoning term
  - **function:** explains why the two views may coexist with giving, discipline, and other wholesome practices

- **mokṣānukūla** — favorable or conducive to liberation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** qualifies even annihilationist view as capable of practical alignment with release in a limited respect

- **svadravyasaṃmūḍhatva** — delusion concerning one's own object-complex / own basis
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** characterizes the two indeterminate views as cognitive distortion rather than other-directed harmful volition

- **aparapīḍāpravṛtti** — non-proceeding toward injury of others
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** distinguishes these views from afflictions whose practical expression is injurious

- **sahajā satkāyadṛṣṭi** — innate self-view
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only subdivision made explicit here
  - **function:** explains the indeterminate form of self-view found even in animals

- **vikalpitā satkāyadṛṣṭi** — constructed or conceptually produced self-view
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only subdivision, attributed to earlier teachers
  - **function:** distinguishes an ethically unwholesome developed view from innate self-reference

- **pūrvācārya** — earlier teacher
  - **status:** explicit attribution marker
  - **function:** identifies the source of the innate/constructed distinction without collapsing it automatically into Vasubandhu's own final voice

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya materially sharpens the Kārikā's ethical taxonomy.

The higher-domain rule is not grounded merely in meditative refinement. Vasubandhu gives a causal argument from karmic maturation:

```text
akuśala
    → capable of painful maturation

higher-domain affliction
    → no such painful maturation

therefore
higher-domain affliction
    = kliṣṭa but avyākṛta
```

The operative absence is `paravyābādhahetu`: there is no causal basis of other-directed injury corresponding to the painful maturation of unwholesome dharmas.

For the desire-domain exception, the Bhāṣya also corrects any overly simple account in which satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi are indeterminate merely because they are “cognitive” rather than “volitional.” Vasubandhu's actual reasons are more determinate:

```text
1. they are compatible with dāna and śīla;
2. they need not proceed toward injury of others;
3. at least the innate form of satkāyadṛṣṭi is found even in animals;
4. a developed, constructed satkāyadṛṣṭi may nevertheless be akuśala.
```

This yields a graded account of wrong view rather than a single ethical value attached to the lexical item `satkāyadṛṣṭi`.

The passage also confirms the restriction of `sahābhyāṃ mohaḥ`: the indeterminate ignorance is the `avidyā` specifically conjoined (`tatsaṃprayuktā`) with the two views.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The current translation correctly identifies the three indeterminate desire-domain cases and the higher-domain rule.

The Bhāṣya confirms that:

```text
sahābhyāṃ mohaḥ
    = avidyā conjoined with satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi
```

It also confirms that `aśubha` is explained here as `akuśala`:

> `śeṣās tvaśubhā anuśayāḥ kāmadhātāvakuśalāḥ`

A slightly tighter canonical rendering is therefore:

> All anuśayas of the higher domains are ethically indeterminate. In the desire-domain, satkāyadṛṣṭi, antagrāhadṛṣṭi, and the ignorance conjoined with those two are indeterminate; the remaining anuśayas there are unwholesome.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.19 establishes that one determination cannot be read directly from another.

```text
contamination-status
    kliṣṭa / akliṣṭa

ethical-status
    kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta

karmic-result tendency
    painful / pleasant / indeterminate
```

These dimensions are related but non-identical.

The Bhāṣya's argument is genetic rather than merely classificatory:

```text
ethical status
    is tested through
causal efficacy and practical compatibility
```

For the higher domains:

```text
absence of paravyābādha
    → absence of duḥkhavipāka of the relevant kind
    → avyākṛta classification
```

For the two desire-domain views:

```text
wrong cognition
    + compatibility with wholesome practice
    + absence of necessary other-harm
    → not automatically akuśala
```

But the objection and reply prevent this from becoming a crude general rule. The same lexical view can itself be differentiated genetically:

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
    ↓
innate (sahajā)
vs
constructed (vikalpitā)
```

Thus ethical determination depends not only on what a mental factor is called, but on how it is generated, conjoined, and practically efficacious.

This is the strongest logical result of the passage:

```text
same doctrinal species
    can receive different ethical determination
through a difference in mode of genesis
```

## Light Organon Contact Point

The passage reinforces a key Rational Kośa distinction:

```text
defect of knowing
    is not identical with
practical evil
```

But the Bhāṣya makes this more precise than the first-pass formulation. The distinction is not merely between “cognition” and “will.” Vasubandhu tests a determination by its generated relations:

```text
What maturation can it produce?
What practices can coexist with it?
Does it proceed toward injury?
Is it innate or constructed?
With what is it conjoined?
```

That is exactly the kind of reflective mediation the Bhāṣya pass is meant to expose. A determination receives its full place only through its relations and consequences.

For later modeling, `contaminationStatus` and `ethicalStatus` should therefore remain distinct, while the ethical value itself may require provenance or genesis information rather than being encoded as an immutable property of a mental-factor class.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.19 establishes a rigorous distinction between affliction and unwholesomeness:

> **All higher-domain anuśayas are afflicted yet ethically indeterminate because the causal structure that would yield painful unwholesome maturation through harm to others is absent there. In the desire-domain, satkāyadṛṣṭi, antagrāhadṛṣṭi, and the ignorance conjoined with them are likewise indeterminate because these views can coexist with giving and discipline and need not proceed toward harming others. The Bhāṣya then deepens the analysis by distinguishing innate from constructed self-view: ethical determination depends not merely on the named view but on its mode of genesis and practical efficacy.**

This passage therefore converts a simple ethical list into a relational and genetic classification of affliction.
