## Translation of the Bhāṣya

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Deception (*māyā*), crookedness (*śāṭhya*), concealment (*mrakṣa*), and sleep (*middha*) are associated in both ways—that is, with mental gladness and with mental distress. For sometimes one deceives another while glad, and sometimes while distressed. The same applies successively down to sleep.

As for intoxication (*mada*), it is associated with the two pleasant feelings: in the third dhyāna with bodily pleasure (*sukha*), below that with mental gladness (*saumanasya*), and above that with equanimity (*upekṣā*).

Equanimity goes with all. Therefore all the anuśayas are associated with it, for there is nowhere a prohibition of equanimity such as there is in the case of ignorance.

The other four—shamelessness (*āhrīkya*), lack of moral concern (*anapatrāpya*), torpor (*styāna*), and restlessness (*auddhatya*)—are associated with all five feeling-faculties, because the first pair belongs to the great unwholesome factors and the latter pair to the great afflictive factors.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK 5.58 completes the feeling-association analysis begun in 5.55.

The first group is explicitly **bivalent with respect to mental feeling**:

```text
māyā
śāṭhya
mrakṣa
middha
    ↔ saumanasya or daurmanasya
```

This is not inferred from their semantics. Vasubandhu states the conjunction directly and illustrates it phenomenologically:

```text
one may deceive another
    while glad
or
    while distressed
```

and he extends the same principle through the sequence down to sleep.

The second determination is more subtle than the Kārikā alone suggests. *Mada* is not simply tied to a fixed pair of pleasant feelings independently of bhūmi. Its association varies according to level:

```text
third dhyāna
    mada + sukha

below the third dhyāna
    mada + saumanasya

above the third dhyāna
    mada + upekṣā
```

So feeling-association must again be indexed by **bhūmi**.

The third determination universalizes equanimity:

```text
upekṣā
    is compatible with all anuśayas
```

The Bhāṣya's reason is negative and structural: there is no domain in which equanimity is prohibited in the way some other feeling-affliction conjunctions are prohibited.

Finally, four paryavasthānas have the widest affective range:

```text
āhrīkya
anapatrāpya
styāna
auddhatya
    ↔ all five indriyas of feeling
```

Their fivefold compatibility is grounded in their mahābhūmika status:

```text
āhrīkya + anapatrāpya
    → akuśala-mahābhūmika

styāna + auddhatya
    → kleśa-mahābhūmika
```

This is a doctrinal ground supplied by the Bhāṣya, not merely a functional interpretation.

---

## Doctrinal Determination

The complete lesson of 5.58 is that **feeling-tone does not determine afflictive identity**.

The same upakleśa can occur under opposite mental tones:

```text
māyā + saumanasya
māyā + daurmanasya
```

Conversely, one upakleśa can change its compatible feeling according to meditative level:

```text
mada
    × bhūmi
    → appropriate feeling
```

The universal compatibility of *upekṣā* also blocks any identification of neutral feeling with purity:

```text
upekṣā
    ≠
absence of anuśaya
```

And the four mahābhūmika derivatives show that some afflictive factors are affectively unrestricted:

```text
same upakleśa
    may coexist with
all five feeling-faculties
```

Thus the feeling matrix is a constraint map, not an ontology of the afflictions themselves.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and revision recommended.**

The first-pass translation is basically correct for the first and last clauses, but the phrase concerning *mada* should not suggest only two universally fixed feelings. The Bhāṣya shows a bhūmi-sensitive distribution.

Preferred translation:

> Deception, crookedness, concealment, and sleep occur with either [mental gladness or mental distress]. Intoxication is associated with the pleasant feeling appropriate to its level: with pleasure in the third dhyāna, with mental gladness below it, and with equanimity above. Equanimity is compatible with all; the other four are associated with all five feeling-faculties.

The prose is required to make the *mada* clause fully explicit.

## New Technical Terms

- **dainyākāra / harṣākāra** — operative affective modes already used in 5.57; not newly introduced here, but 5.58 shows that several upakleśas are not fixed to either one.
- **akuśala-mahābhūmika** — great unwholesome mental factor; previously encountered in Chapter 2; here functions as the doctrinal reason that āhrīkya and anapatrāpya can associate with all five feelings.
- **kleśa-mahābhūmika** — great afflictive mental factor; previously encountered in Chapter 2; here functions as the doctrinal reason that styāna and auddhatya can associate with all five feelings.
- **sarvagā upekṣā** — equanimity as universally compatible with the anuśayas in the present feeling-association analysis; function: universal compatibility rule, not a claim that every equanimous state is afflicted.

## Logical Determination

The relation being classified is **saṃprayoga**, not origin.

```text
niḥṣyanda
    = genetic derivation

saṃprayoga
    = conjunction in one cognitive event

bhūmi
    = level constraining which conjunctions are possible

mahābhūmika-status
    = doctrinal ground for broad conjunction-range
```

VAK 5.58 therefore supplies a genuinely relational determination:

```text
CompatibleFeeling(x)
    is not a unary property of x

but a function of
    dharma × bhūmi × permitted indriya
```

The clearest case is *mada*:

```text
same afflictive determination
    + different bhūmi
    → different compatible feeling
```

This prevents the later clarification pass from treating the feeling table as a flat lookup list.
