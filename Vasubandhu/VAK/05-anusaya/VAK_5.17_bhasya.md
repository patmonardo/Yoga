# VAK_5.17 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> sarvatragā anuśayāḥ sakalām anuśerate |
> svabhūmim ālambanataḥ svanikāyam asarvagāḥ || 5.17 ||

**Best current translation:**  
The universal anuśayas adhere, by taking as object, to the whole of their own level; the non-universal anuśayas adhere only to their own class.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_5.17.md`](./VAK_5.17.md)

---

## Bhāṣya Source

Source anchor: Chapter 5, Pradhan 289|09–289|15.

> eṣāmaṣṭānavateranuśayānāṃ katyālambanato 'nuśerate kati saṃprayogata eva /
>
> sarvatragā anuśayāḥ sakalāmanuśerate /
> svabhūmimālambanataḥ svanikāyamasarvagāḥ // VAkK_5.17 //
>
> ye sarvatragā anuśayāste sakalāṃ pañcaprakārāmapi svāṃ bhūmimālambanato 'nuśerate /
>
> asarvatragāstu svasyāṃ bhūmau svameva nikāyamālambanato 'nuśerate nānyam /
>
> tadyathā duḥkhadarśanaprahātavyā duḥkhadarśanaprahātavyameva nikāyam /
>
> evaṃ yāvadbhāvanāprahātavyā bhāvanāprahātavyameva nānyam /
>
> utsargaṃ kṛtvā 'pavādaṃ karoti /

The final sentence is the transition into VAK 5.18: after giving the general rule (`utsarga`), the Bhāṣya proceeds to its exceptions (`apavāda`).

---

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Among these ninety-eight anuśayas, how many adhere by way of cognitive support, and how many only by conjunction?

> The universal anuśayas adhere to the whole of their own level by taking it as cognitive support; the non-universal ones adhere to their own class.

Those anuśayas that are universal adhere, by way of cognitive support, to their entire own level in all five modes. But the non-universal anuśayas, within their own level, adhere by way of cognitive support only to their own class and not to another.

For example, those to be abandoned through seeing suffering take only the class to be abandoned through seeing suffering as their cognitive support. Likewise, proceeding up to those to be abandoned through cultivation: they take only the class to be abandoned through cultivation and not another.

Having stated the general rule, he now states the exception.

---

## Course of the Argument

The Bhāṣya begins by separating two modes in which an anuśaya may be said to `anuśerate`:

```text
ālambanataḥ
    by way of cognitive support

saṃprayogataḥ
    by way of conjunction
```

VAK 5.17 answers the first half of this question.

For the universal anuśayas:

```text
sarvatraga
    ↓
whole own bhūmi
    ↓
all five abandonment-classes
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly says:

```text
sakalāṃ pañcaprakārām api svāṃ bhūmim
```

—the entire own level in all five modes.

Those five modes are the familiar classes:

```text
duḥkha-darśana-prahātavya
samudaya-darśana-prahātavya
nirodha-darśana-prahātavya
mārga-darśana-prahātavya
bhāvanā-prahātavya
```

The non-universal anuśayas are sharply narrower:

```text
asarvatraga
    ↓
own bhūmi
    ↓
own nikāya only
```

Thus a suffering-seeing abandonable non-universal anuśaya takes only suffering-seeing abandonables as its support; a cultivation-abandonable one takes only the cultivation-abandonable class.

The contrast is therefore not simply:

```text
large range
vs
small range
```

but:

```text
cross-nikāya range within one bhūmi
vs
nikāya-restricted range within one bhūmi
```

---

## `Nikāya` Is Now Technically Fixed

The first-pass Kārikā file correctly left `svanikāya` somewhat open. The Bhāṣya now fixes its local meaning through examples.

Here `nikāya` is the truth-/cultivation-specific abandonment class:

```text
duḥkhadarśanaprahātavya-nikāya
samudayadarśanaprahātavya-nikāya
nirodhadarśanaprahātavya-nikāya
mārgadarśanaprahātavya-nikāya
bhāvanāprahātavya-nikāya
```

So `svanikāya` should not be translated vaguely as merely “its own group.” In this passage it means its own determinate class within the fivefold abandonment matrix.

---

## `Anuśerate` as a Relational Term

The opening question is decisive:

```text
kati ālambanataḥ anuśerate
kati saṃprayogata eva
```

How many adhere by way of cognitive support, and how many only by conjunction?

This prevents us from treating `anuśaya` as merely a hidden entity residing somewhere in the continuum.

The verb is being analyzed relationally:

```text
anuśaya
    adheres relative to an ālambana
or
    adheres through conjunction
```

VAK 5.17 specifies the scope of the first relation; VAK 5.18 will qualify it and then complete the conjunction side.

---

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The unit is expository. No explicit opponent occurs.

The final sentence, `utsargaṃ kṛtvā 'pavādaṃ karoti`, is a metacommentarial transition: having stated the general rule, the text is about to introduce exceptions.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **pañcaprakārā svabhūmi** — one's own level considered in all five abandonment modes
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion
  - **function:** defines the exact range of a universal anuśaya

- **svanikāya** — one's own truth-/cultivation-specific abandonment class
  - **status:** Kārikā term clarified by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** fixes the restricted object-range of non-universal anuśayas

- **ālambanataḥ anuśayana** — adherence by way of cognitive support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** first of the two modes by which anuśayas adhere

- **saṃprayogataḥ anuśayana** — adherence by way of conjunction
  - **status:** introduced in the Bhāṣya question and developed in VAK 5.18
  - **function:** second mode of adherence

- **utsarga** — general rule
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only metacommentarial term
  - **function:** identifies VAK 5.17 as the general scope rule

- **apavāda** — exception
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only metacommentarial term
  - **function:** announces the qualification beginning in VAK 5.18

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse defines `sarvatraga` through object-range with much greater precision than the bare word “universal” suggests.

```text
sarvatraga
    entire own bhūmi
    across all five nikāyas

asarvatraga
    own bhūmi
    but own nikāya only
```

Universality is therefore relative and structured.

It does not mean unrestricted range across every realm or level. It means the ability, within one's own level, to range across the complete fivefold classification of abandonment.

This also clarifies why the chapter repeatedly needs both `bhūmi` and `nikāya`:

```text
bhūmi
    vertical level / sphere

nikāya
    classificatory subdivision within that level
```

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only, with a stronger rendering of `svanikāya`.**

The first-pass translation remains basically correct, but “own class” can now be made more technical:

> The universal anuśayas adhere, by taking as cognitive support, to the whole of their own level across all five abandonment-classes; the non-universal anuśayas adhere only to their own abandonment-class within that level.

This captures the Bhāṣya's explicit `pañcaprakāra` explanation.

---

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya establish a two-level scope structure:

```text
Level 1: bhūmi
    the broader field

Level 2: nikāya
    one of five determinate abandonment classes
```

Then:

```text
sarvatraga
    quantifies across all nikāyas
    within one bhūmi

asarvatraga
    remains restricted to one nikāya
    within one bhūmi
```

This is a concrete universal: the universal does not abolish internal differentiation but ranges through the differentiated whole.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The cleanest later architectural contact is a scoped rule:

```text
UniversalAffliction
    scope = all classes within current level

NonUniversalAffliction
    scope = current class within current level
```

The important textual lesson is that scope is generated by relations, not by an unqualified label such as “global.”

A later Rational Agent would likewise need to distinguish:

```text
frame-level scope
from
class-level scope
```

But the Bhāṣya's own doctrine comes first: `sarvatraga` is universality across all five abandonment-classes of a determinate bhūmi.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.17 fixes the general rule for adherence by cognitive support:

> **Universal anuśayas take the whole of their own bhūmi, across all five abandonment-classes, as cognitive support. Non-universal anuśayas remain restricted to their own truth-/cultivation-specific nikāya.**

The final Bhāṣya sentence immediately warns that this is only the general rule. VAK 5.18 will state the exceptions and complete the distinction between adherence by object-support and adherence by conjunction.
