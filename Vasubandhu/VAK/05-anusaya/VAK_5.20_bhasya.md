> kāme 'kuśalamūlāni rāgapratighamūḍhayaḥ |

**Source caution:** the Kārikā-only working e-text repeats `trīṇy akuśalamūlāni` in the third pāda. The Bhāṣya itself explicitly asks `katy avyākṛtamūlāni`, answers `trīṇy avyākṛtamūlāni`, and then identifies `tṛṣṇā`, `avidyā`, and `mati`. The corrected reading `avyākṛtamūlāni` is therefore required by Vasubandhu's own exposition. The received reading remains documented in the Kārikā file rather than silently erased.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

How many are roots of the unwholesome, and how many are not?

> In the desire-domain, attachment, hostility, and deluded ignorance are roots of the unwholesome.

In the desire-domain, all attachment, all hostility, and all delusion are included respectively, except for the delusion conjoined with satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi.

> There are three roots of the unwholesome.

Greed is a root of the unwholesome; hatred and delusion are roots of the unwholesome.

For only that which is itself unwholesome and is also a root of the unwholesome is accepted as an `akuśalamūla`.

It is therefore established that the remaining anuśayas are not roots of the unwholesome.

How many are roots of the indeterminate, and how many are not?

There are three roots of the indeterminate. Which three?

> Craving, ignorance, and that cognition.

The word `sā` indicates their indeterminate character. Whatever craving, ignorance, and discriminative cognition (`prajñā`) are indeterminate—even down to those born of maturation—all of them are roots of the indeterminate, according to the Kāśmīras.

## Course of the Argument

VAK 5.19 classified anuśayas by ethical status. VAK 5.20 now asks a different question:

```text
which afflicted dharmas
    merely possess an ethical status
and which
    function as roots of dharmas having that status?
```

The first half defines an `akuśalamūla` through a conjunction of two conditions:

```text
it is itself akuśala
    +
it is a mūla of akuśala
```

Vasubandhu states this explicitly:

```text
yad akuśalam
    ca
akuśalasya mūlam
    tad eva akuśalamūlam
```

The three are therefore:

```text
lobha / rāga
    greed / attachment

dveṣa / pratigha
    hatred / hostility

moha
    delusion
```

The qualification inherited from 5.19 is crucial. The `moha` conjoined with satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi was already classified as `avyākṛta`; it therefore fails the first condition and cannot count as an unwholesome root.

Thus:

```text
all desire-domain rāga
    → akuśalamūla

all desire-domain pratigha
    → akuśalamūla

moha
    → akuśalamūla
    except where conjoined with
       satkāyadṛṣṭi or antagrāhadṛṣṭi
```

The second half introduces the parallel category `avyākṛtamūla`.

The Bhāṣya makes the triad explicit:

```text
tṛṣṇā
avidyā
prajñā
```

but only insofar as each is itself `avyākṛta`. This is the force of `sā`, according to the explanation.

The Kāśmīra position is strikingly broad:

```text
whatever indeterminate craving
whatever indeterminate ignorance
whatever indeterminate prajñā
    even vipākaja prajñā
        → avyākṛtamūla
```

This means that `prajñā` is not intrinsically wholesome or liberating merely by virtue of being discriminative cognition. It can function as a root within an ethically indeterminate complex.

The main explanation through the definition of `akuśalamūla` is:

## New Technical Terms

- **akuśalamūla** — root of the unwholesome
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly defined in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** requires both intrinsic unwholesomeness and generative rootedness with respect to unwholesome dharmas

- **avyākṛtamūla** — root of the ethically indeterminate
  - **status:** textually decisive Bhāṣya category; repairs the corrupt third pāda in the Kārikā-only source
  - **function:** identifies determinations capable of grounding indeterminate dharmas

- **mūla** — root, generative basis
  - **status:** established Abhidharma term, given a precise relational criterion here
  - **function:** distinguishes a productive ground from a dharma that merely shares the same ethical status

- **lobha** — greed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya synonymic specification of Kārikā `rāga`
  - **function:** first unwholesome root

- **dveṣa** — hatred
  - **status:** Bhāṣya synonymic specification of Kārikā `pratigha`
  - **function:** second unwholesome root

- **moha** — delusion
  - **status:** Bhāṣya specification of the third unwholesome root
  - **function:** root only where the delusion itself is unwholesome; excludes the ignorance conjoined with the two indeterminate views

- **mati / prajñā** — cognition / discriminative knowing
  - **status:** the Bhāṣya explicitly glosses the Kārikā's `mati` as `prajñā`
  - **function:** third root of the indeterminate when itself ethically indeterminate

- **vipākaja** — born of maturation, resultant
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only extension of the Kārikā classification
  - **function:** shows the breadth of the Kāśmīra category; even resultant indeterminate prajñā counts as an indeterminate root

- **Kāśmīrāḥ** — the Kāśmīra Abhidharmikas
  - **status:** explicit doctrinal attribution
  - **function:** marks the broad doctrine of the three indeterminate roots as a school position rather than an unattributed universal assertion

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya sharpens the Kārikā in three ways.

First, it gives a strict definition of an unwholesome root:

```text
akuśalamūla(x)
    iff
x is akuśala
    and
x is a root of akuśala dharmas
```

Thus:

```text
akuśala
    ≠ automatically
akuśalamūla
```

The root relation is an additional determination.

Second, it confirms that ethical classification from VAK 5.19 controls root-status. The ignorance conjoined with satkāyadṛṣṭi or antagrāhadṛṣṭi is not an unwholesome root because it is itself indeterminate.

Third, the Kāśmīra account of indeterminate roots is much broader than the first-pass Kārikā alone makes obvious. `tṛṣṇā`, `avidyā`, and `prajñā` count whenever they occur in an indeterminate mode, including resultant (`vipākaja`) cases.

The chapter is therefore distinguishing at least three levels:

```text
1. what kind of dharma is this?
   akuśala / avyākṛta

2. is it itself afflicted?
   kliṣṭa / akliṣṭa

3. does it function as a generative root?
   mūla / non-mūla
```

These cannot be collapsed into one taxonomy.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Textual correction confirmed and philosophically important.**

The received Kārikā-only reading:

> `trīṇy akuśalamūlāni`

in the third pāda is incompatible with the Bhāṣya's explicit question and answer. The intended reading is:

> `trīṇy avyākṛtamūlāni`

The current Kārikā file already records this correction, and the Bhāṣya decisively confirms it.

The Bhāṣya also tightens the force of the final `sā`. It indicates that the three named factors are being spoken of specifically in their **indeterminate** form.

A tighter canonical translation is therefore:

> In the desire-domain, attachment, hostility, and unwholesome delusion are the three roots of the unwholesome. The roots of the indeterminate are likewise three: indeterminate craving, ignorance, and discriminative cognition.

The adjective “unwholesome” before `delusion` is interpretively supplied from the Bhāṣya's explicit exclusion of ignorance conjoined with the two indeterminate views.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.20 introduces **ground** into the ethical classification.

VAK 5.19 asked:

```text
What predicate belongs to an anuśaya?
```

VAK 5.20 asks:

```text
What determination generates or grounds
other dharmas of that ethical order?
```

This gives a precise distinction:

```text
classification
    hasEthicalStatus(x, akuśala)

versus

grounding
    functionsAsRootOf(x, akuśala-dharmas)
```

The Bhāṣya's definition requires both:

```text
quality
    +
generative relation
    =
root-status
```

This is a genuine movement from predicate to ground.

The neutral triad also prevents a simplistic moralization of cognition:

```text
prajñā
    can be
avyākṛta
    and can function as
avyākṛtamūla
```

Therefore discriminative power as such is formally neutral with respect to liberation. Its rational value depends upon the determinate complex, object, path, and function in which it operates.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

For the Rational Kośa, the passage gives an important modeling constraint:

```text
property
    ≠
productive role
```

A determination may have an ethical value without being a generator of that value in other determinations. `mūla` therefore requires its own relation rather than being inferred simply from classification.

Likewise, the Bhāṣya's treatment of `prajñā` is philosophically useful. Discriminative cognition cannot be promoted automatically into a pure or liberating faculty:

```text
prajñā as formal discrimination
    may occur within
    an indeterminate generated field
```

The Path must determine the function of cognition; vocabulary alone cannot do so.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.20 moves from ethical classification to **ethical generation**:

> **An unwholesome root is not merely an unwholesome dharma: it must itself be unwholesome and must ground further unwholesome dharmas. Hence greed, hatred, and unwholesome delusion alone are the three roots in the desire-domain. In parallel, the Kāśmīras recognize three roots of the indeterminate—indeterminate craving, ignorance, and discriminative cognition—even extending the last category to resultant cognition. The passage therefore distinguishes ethical status from generative function and confirms that prajñā, taken merely as discriminative cognition, is not intrinsically liberating.**

The exclusion problem for other possible indeterminate roots—especially doubt and conceit—is taken up immediately in VAK 5.21.
