## Literal Prose Translation

Therefore they compose a condensed exposition (`piṇḍavibhāṣā`), thinking: “How may we, with little and little effort, explain great and great floods of questions?”

In summary there are these sixteen classes of dharmas: the five classes belonging respectively to the desire-, form-, and formless-domains, together with the undefiled. The cittas are likewise sixteen, precisely these same classes.

Having determined which dharma is an object-support of which citta, one should infer how many anuśayas adhere with respect to that object.

First, the desire-domain dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering, seeing origination, and cultivation are objects of five cittas: the three corresponding cittas of their own domain, one citta of the higher domain—specifically the cultivation-abandonable one—and one undefiled citta.

## Philosophical Translation

The Bhāṣya now compresses the many individual questions of bondage into a systematic matrix. Instead of asking separately, for every anuśaya and every object, whether adherence occurs, one first classifies both dharmas and cittas into the same sixteenfold scheme. One then determines the object-relation between a dharma-class and a citta-class; from that relation the possible anuśaya-adherence can be inferred.

For the first case, desire-domain dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering, seeing origination, or cultivation can be objects of five cognition-classes: three corresponding desire-domain cittas, the cultivation-abandonable citta of the form-domain, and undefiled cognition.

## Course of Argument

### 1. Why the `piṇḍavibhāṣā` is introduced

The Bhāṣya explicitly says the aim is economy:

```text
many questions
    ↓
compressed systematic rule
```

The preceding verses generated a potentially enormous set of questions:

```text
Which anuśaya?
With respect to which dharma?
In which domain?
Through which citta?
By object or conjunction?
```

Rather than enumerate them one by one, the text builds a reusable classification.

### 2. Sixteen dharma-classes

The three domains each contain five abandonment-classes:

```text
desire-domain      5
form-domain        5
formless-domain    5
undefiled          1
                   --
                   16
```

The same sixteenfold division applies to cittas.

### 3. Objecthood becomes the lookup key

The methodological instruction is explicit:

```text
first determine:
    which dharma is the ālambana of which citta

then infer:
    how many anuśayas adhere there
```

This is the core of the condensed exposition.

### 4. First matrix-entry: desire-domain classes

The verse groups three desire-domain dharma-classes:

```text
duḥkhadarśanaheya
samudayadarśanaheya
bhāvanāheya
```

These are objects of five cittas:

```text
3 desire-domain cittas
    the corresponding three classes

1 form-domain citta
    bhāvanāheya only

1 undefiled citta
```

Thus the Bhāṣya resolves the compounds:

```text
svakatraya
    = three belonging to their own domain

ekarūpāpta
    = one belonging to the form-domain
```

## Voice Identification

**Voice:** Vasubandhu / expository Abhidharma voice

The passage is not framed as an objection. It presents the compact classification method adopted for handling the large set of anuśaya-object questions.

## New Technical Terms

- **piṇḍavibhāṣā** — condensed or aggregate exposition; **status:** Bhāṣya-specific in this local context; **function:** compresses a large combinatorial problem into a systematic rule-set.
- **praśnaugha** — “flood of questions”; **status:** Bhāṣya-only rhetorical expression; **function:** states why systematic compression is needed.
- **ālambana** — object-support; **status:** previously encountered; **function:** relation used to infer anuśaya-adherence.
- **abhyūhitavya** — to be inferred or reasoned out; **status:** Bhāṣya methodological vocabulary; **function:** marks the transition from explicit matrix data to derived conclusions.
- **vigrahajāti** — manner/type of compound analysis; **status:** grammatical Bhāṣya vocabulary; **function:** explains `svakatraya` and `ekarūpāpta`.

## Doctrinal Determination

The doctrinal advance of 5.29 is methodological rather than ontological.

The Kośa is now treating bondage as a structured relation among typed classes:

```text
DharmaClass
    ← object-support relation →
CittaClass
    → determines possible anuśaya-adherence
```

This matters after 5.28. An abandoned dharma can remain an object for still-unabandoned affliction. 5.29 provides the systematic machinery for determining exactly which cognitive classes can sustain such object-relations.

The Bhāṣya therefore does not say that an abandoned defilement remains causally active in itself. What remains possible is a present citta taking that dharma-class as object, under which an anuśaya may still adhere.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation was broadly correct, but the Bhāṣya resolves the counts precisely:

- `svakatraya` = three corresponding cittas in the desire-domain;
- `ekarūpāpta` = one form-domain citta, specifically **bhāvanāheya**;
- `amala-vijñāna` = one undefiled citta.

Thus the total is explicitly **five cittas**.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya introduces a two-stage inferential architecture:

```text
1. establish ObjectRelation(DharmaClass, CittaClass)
2. infer AnuśayaAdherence from that relation
```

This is an important shift from enumeration to rule-governed derivation.

The sixteen-by-sixteen space is potentially large, but the text reduces it through class structure. The logic is therefore not merely classificatory; it is generative:

```text
small number of typed relations
    → large number of determinate answers
```

The method also preserves a distinction essential to the preceding verses:

```text
status of object
    ≠
status of cognition
```

A contaminated or even abandoned dharma-class can be the object of a higher-domain or undefiled citta.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This is unusually close to a formal inference engine.

The text itself proposes:

```text
classify the entities
classify the cognitions
define admissible object-relations
infer downstream afflictive relations
```

The important point is not the superficial resemblance to a matrix. It is that the Bhāṣya deliberately replaces repeated case-by-case reasoning with a compact relational schema.

That makes `piṇḍavibhāṣā` a strong example of **systematic compression without loss of determinacy**.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.29 begins the condensed matrix for anuśaya-object relations. The Bhāṣya explicitly constructs sixteen dharma-classes and sixteen corresponding citta-classes, then instructs the reader to determine object-support relations first and infer anuśaya-adherence from them. In the first entry, desire-domain dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering, seeing origination, or cultivation are objects of five cittas: three corresponding desire-domain cittas, one form-domain cultivation-abandonable citta, and one undefiled citta.

The main advance over the Kārikā pass is therefore methodological: the Kośa has turned a potentially enormous set of individual bondage questions into a compact, reusable inferential system.
