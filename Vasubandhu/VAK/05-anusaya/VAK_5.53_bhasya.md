# VAK 5.53 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.53**

```text
māyā śāṭhyaṃ ca kāmādya-dhyānayor brahma-vañcanāt /
styānauddhatya-madā dhātu-traye 'nye kāma-dhātujāḥ // 5.53 //
```

**Translation:** Deceit and crookedness occur in the desire-domain and in the first dhyāna, as shown by the deceiving of Brahmā; torpor, restlessness, and intoxication occur in all three domains; the others arise only in the desire-domain.

See: [VAK_5.53.md](./VAK_5.53.md)

## Translation

Above the desire-domain, the upakleśas that can occur there are indeterminate, according to what is possible.

How many of these, then, are to be understood as belonging to which domains?

Deceit and crookedness occur in the desire-domain and in the first dhyāna. How can deceit occur in the Brahmā world? Because of the deceiving of Brahmā. There, Mahābrahmā undertook to deceive the venerable Aśvajit by presenting himself falsely.

Although crookedness has already been stated, it is stated again because it has arisen in the present context.

Torpor, restlessness, and intoxication occur in all three domains. These three belong to all three realms.

The others arise in the desire-domain. From the sixteen upakleśas, when these five are removed, the remaining eleven upakleśas belong only to the desire-domain.

The kleśas and upakleśas have now been explained.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / reported Abhidharma classification**

VAK 5.53 completes the distribution of the upakleśas by domain. The Bhāṣya asks explicitly:

```text
kati punar eṣāṃ kutastyāḥ?
How many of these belong where?
```

The answer gives three ranges.

### 1. Desire-domain and first dhyāna

```text
māyā
śāṭhya
    → kāmadhātu + prathama-dhyāna
```

The occurrence of `māyā` in the Brahmā world is justified by a concrete narrative example. Mahābrahmā tries to deceive the venerable Aśvajit by `vitathātmasaṃdarśanatā`, falsely presenting himself as something he is not.

Thus the Bhāṣya does not establish the first-dhyāna range through an abstract theory of deception. It appeals to an attested doctrinal example.

`śāṭhya` is repeated here even though already treated because it has become relevant to the present domain-classification (`prasaṅgāgata`).

### 2. All three domains

```text
styāna
auddhatya
mada
    → traidhātuka
```

The Bhāṣya states this without further argument here:

```text
ete trayas traidhātukāḥ
these three belong to all three domains
```

Thus they may occur in:

```text
kāmadhātu
rūpadhātu
ārūpyadhātu
```

### 3. Desire-domain only

The remaining upakleśas are restricted to kāmadhātu.

The Bhāṣya makes the arithmetic explicit:

```text
16 total upakleśas
- 5 with wider range
= 11 kāmāvacara only
```

The five removed are:

```text
māyā
śāṭhya
styāna
auddhatya
mada
```

The remaining eleven therefore occur only in the desire-domain.

The prose then marks a genuine section boundary:

```text
uktā kleśāḥ upakleśāś ca
The kleśas and upakleśas have been explained.
```

The next inquiry concerns which anuśayas belong to the mental-consciousness level and which to the six consciousness bodies.

## New Technical Terms

- **kutastya** — belonging to which place/domain.
  - **status:** new local classificatory expression.
  - **function:** asks for domain-distribution.

- **vitathātmasaṃdarśanatā** — false presentation of oneself; making oneself appear otherwise than one is.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory expression.
  - **function:** defines the concrete form of Brahmā's deception.

- **prasaṅgāgata** — having arisen in the course/context of the discussion.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya methodological vocabulary.
  - **function:** explains why `śāṭhya` is repeated.

- **traidhātuka** — belonging to all three domains.
  - **status:** previously encountered pattern.
  - **function:** range classification.

- **kāmāvacara** — operating/belonging within the desire-domain.
  - **status:** established Abhidharma vocabulary.
  - **function:** marks the restricted range of the remaining eleven upakleśas.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya supplies an exact range matrix:

```text
māyā, śāṭhya
    kāmadhātu + first dhyāna

styāna, auddhatya, mada
    all three domains

remaining eleven
    kāmadhātu only
```

The important point is that membership in the general class `upakleśa` does not determine domain-range. Domain-range is an additional doctrinal predicate.

This completes the multi-axial analysis developed in 5.46–5.53:

```text
classification
origin / niḥṣyanda
mode of abandonment
ethical value
domain-range
```

None of these axes can be inferred safely from another.

The Brahmā example also shows that higher cosmological location does not entail absence of deceptive presentation. What matters is whether the conditions for a specific afflictive operation remain possible.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The first-pass translation was substantially correct, but `brahmavañcanāt` is better understood through the Bhāṣya's concrete narrative: Mahābrahmā's attempted deception of Aśvajit demonstrates the possibility of `māyā` in the first dhyāna.

Preferred translation:

> Deceit and crookedness occur in the desire-domain and in the first dhyāna, as shown by the deceiving of Brahmā; torpor, restlessness, and intoxication occur in all three domains; the others arise only in the desire-domain.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.53 establishes classification by **range of occurrence**.

```text
Dharma F
    has definition
    has origin
    has abandonment-mode
    has ethical value
    has domain-range
```

The same factor cannot be fully specified by name alone.

The arithmetic in the Bhāṣya is especially useful:

```text
Universe = 16 upakleśas
Exceptional wider-range set = 5
Complement = 11 desire-domain-only factors
```

This is an extensional determination generated from a prior intensional distinction.

The decisive logical control is:

```text
higher domain
    does not entail
absence of affliction
```

Only those afflictions whose range excludes the higher domain are absent there.

## Organon Contact Point

For now the Organon contact should remain restrained.

VAK 5.53 requires a determination to carry a separate `domain-range` relation. A transition to a higher state cannot globally erase every lower-associated predicate.

```text
state transition
    ≠ universal purification
```

Rather:

```text
for each determination F:
    test whether target domain ∈ range(F)
```

This is precisely the kind of relational architecture that should be reconstructed in a later clarifying pass before attempting a larger Organon synthesis.
