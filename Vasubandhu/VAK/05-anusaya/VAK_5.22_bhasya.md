## Bhāṣya Source

Source anchor: Chapter 5, Pradhan 292|08–294|04.

The passage begins by asking whether the fourteen `avyākṛta-vastu` named in sūtra are called `avyākṛta` because they possess the ethically indeterminate status discussed in VAK 5.19–5.21. Vasubandhu explicitly denies this. Here `avyākṛta` means that the question is to be set aside (`sthāpanīyaḥ praśnaḥ`).

> yāni sūtre caturdaśāvyākṛtavastūni kiṃ tāni avyākṛtatvāt /
> netyāha /
> kiṃ tarhi /
> sthāpanīyaḥ praśno 'vyākṛta ityuktam /
> caturvidho hi praśnaḥ /
> ekāṃśavyākaraṇīyo vibhajyavyākaraṇīyaḥ paripṛcchayya vyākaraṇīyaḥ sthāpanīyaśca /
>
> ekāṃśato vyākaraṇaṃ vibhajya paripṛcchaya ca /
> sthāpyaṃ ca maraṇotpatti viśiṣṭātmā 'nyatādivat // VAkK_5.22 //

Vasubandhu then supplies the four examples in order.

> kiṃ sarvasattvā mariṣyantītyekāṃśena vyākarttavyaṃ mariṣyantīti /
>
> kiṃ sarve janiṣyanta iti vibhajya vyākarttavyaṃ saṃkleśā janiṣyante na niḥkleśā iti /
>
> kiṃ manuṣyo viśiṣṭo hīna iti paripṛcchayya vyākattevyam /
> kānadhikṛtya praśnayasīti /
> yadi brūyād devāniti hīna iti vyākarttavyam /
> atha brūyādapāyāniti viśiṣṭa iti vyākarttavyam /
>
> kimanyaḥ skandhebhyaḥ sattvo 'nanya iti sthāpanīyaḥ /
> sattvadravyasyābhāvāt vandhyāputraśyāmagauratādivat /

The discussion then becomes explicitly dialectical. An alternative teacher objects that some of these examples can themselves be reclassified, and the Ābhidharmikas give a more extended account of the four modes. Finally a Mahāsāṅghika sūtra recension is cited as an authoritative formulation of the four question-types.

**Source caution:** the working e-text is a reference text rather than a critical edition. Forms such as `paripṛcchayya`, `vyākattevyam`, and other minor orthographic irregularities are preserved in the source quotation rather than silently normalized.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The sūtras speak of fourteen `avyākṛta-vastus`. Are these called `avyākṛta` because they are ethically indeterminate?

No.

What then?

A question that is to be set aside is called `avyākṛta`. For questions are of four kinds: one to be answered categorically, one to be answered after division, one to be answered after questioning in return, and one to be set aside.

> An answer is given categorically, after division, and after questioning in return; and one is to be set aside—as in the cases of death, arising, superiority, and the difference of a self, respectively.

If one asks, “Will all beings die?”, it is to be answered categorically: “They will die.”

If one asks, “Will all arise again?”, it is to be answered after division: those possessing defilement will arise; those free from defilement will not.

If one asks, “Is a human being superior or inferior?”, it is to be answered after questioning in return: “In relation to whom are you asking?” If the questioner says, “In relation to the gods,” one answers, “Inferior.” If he says, “In relation to the bad destinies,” one answers, “Superior.”

If one asks, “Is a being other than the aggregates or not other than them?”, the question is to be set aside, because there is no real entity called a being, just as one cannot determine whether the son of a barren woman is dark or fair.

The question is then raised: how can this count as an answer if one says that the matter is `avyākṛta`? The reply is that this itself is the answer: the question is answered by determining it as not to be answered in its proposed form.

## The Alternative Analysis

**Voice: Other teacher (`apara āha`)**

Another says that the question “Will all arise?” can also receive a categorical answer: “Not all will arise.” Only a more specific question—“Of those who die, will they arise?”—requires division.

Likewise, in relation to humans both inferiority and superiority exist relatively. Thus both can in one sense be answered categorically: humans are inferior relative to gods and superior relative to the bad destinies.

The point of `vibhajya-vyākaraṇa`, on this account, is not merely that more than one predicate can be truly assigned. It is that an apparently unqualified question cannot be answered unqualifiedly because its truth depends upon a division of the subject or conditions.

The text compares this with the statement that `vijñāna` is both effect and cause: each predicate may be stated categorically in the proper respect, yet an unqualified question may still demand division when the intended respect is not fixed.

The alternative teacher also insists that the question whether a being is other than the aggregates is genuinely `avyākṛta`; mere non-answering cannot itself simply be called an answer.

## Ābhidharmika Expansion of the Four Modes

**Voice: Ābhidharmikas**

The Ābhidharmikas offer a more formalized treatment.

### 1. `ekāṃśa-vyākaraṇa`

Certain statements are to be answered categorically because they are directly connected with meaning (`arthopasaṃhitatva`). Examples include:

```text
The Tathāgata is an Arhat and perfectly awakened.
His Dharma is well proclaimed.
The community of disciples practices well.
Rūpa is impermanent ... up through vijñāna.
There is the designation of suffering ... up through the designation of the Path.
```

The point is that the relevant determination is complete enough to permit immediate assertion.

### 2. `vibhajya-vyākaraṇa`

The Bhāṣya illustrates division through a progressively narrowing inquiry.

If someone says simply:

```text
Speak about dharmas.
```

one asks which dharmas:

```text
past
future
present
```

If past dharmas are selected, one divides again:

```text
rūpa ... vijñāna
```

If rūpa is selected:

```text
kuśala
akuśala
avyākṛta
```

If kuśala rūpa is selected, the analysis continues through types of abstention; if abstention from killing is selected, it divides according to whether it arises from non-greed, non-hatred, or non-delusion; and even a non-greed-born abstention may be divided into `vijñapti` and `avijñapti`.

Thus division is not arbitrary qualification. It is the systematic articulation of a genus into progressively determinate species until the intended subject is fixed.

### 3. `paripṛcchya-vyākaraṇa`

The same procedure can function as an answer by counter-question when dealing with an evasive or tricky (`śaṭha`) questioner. One says only that “dharmas are many” and continues to question the interlocutor rather than supplying the divisions oneself, until the questioner either falls silent or determines what he means.

The Bhāṣya defends this as a genuine form of answering. If someone says “Tell me the road,” has he not thereby asked about the road? Likewise, because one answers through questioning in return, this counts as `paripṛcchā-vyākaraṇa`.

### 4. `sthāpanīya`

The set-aside questions include such speculative alternatives as:

```text
Is the world finite or infinite?
Is it eternal or non-eternal?
Does the Tathāgata exist after death or not?
Is the life-principle one thing and the body another?
```

These are not merely unanswered for lack of information. Their proposed alternatives fail to provide an admissible determination of the matter.

## Mahāsāṅghika Sūtra Citation

**Voice: report of Bhādanta Mahāsāṅghikas**

The Bhāṣya then records a Mahāsāṅghika recension of a sūtra explicitly enumerating the four modes.

Its examples are particularly valuable:

```text
ekāṃśa-vyākaraṇīya
    “Are all conditioned things impermanent?”

vibhajya-vyākaraṇīya
    “Having performed intentional karma, what does one experience?”

paripṛcchya-vyākaraṇīya
    a question concerning whether self and representations are identical or different,
    requiring the interlocutor first to state what he takes the self to be

sthāpanīya
    the classical speculative questions:
    eternal/non-eternal world,
    finite/infinite world,
    Tathāgata after death,
    identity/difference of life-principle and body
```

This citation confirms that the fourfold structure is not merely Vasubandhu's ad hoc logical taxonomy. He presents it as sūtra-grounded, while also preserving scholastic disagreement over exactly how individual examples should be assigned.

## Course of the Argument

The location of VAK 5.22 is essential.

Verses 5.19–5.21 have been discussing:

```text
avyākṛta
    ethical indeterminacy
```

The sūtras also speak of:

```text
caturdaśa avyākṛta-vastūni
    fourteen “undetermined” matters
```

Vasubandhu prevents an equivocation:

```text
ethical avyākṛta
    ≠
question called avyākṛta because it is sthāpanīya
```

The verse is therefore not an abrupt excursus. It is a lexical and logical clarification forced by the preceding discussion.

The movement is:

```text
avyākṛta as ethical predicate
    ↓ distinguish
avyākṛta as status of a question
    ↓ analyze
four answer-modes
```

This distinction is philosophically decisive. One word occurs in two domains of determination:

```text
ethical classification of dharmas
interrogative classification of questions
```

The Bhāṣya refuses to infer identity of function from identity of vocabulary.

## New Technical Terms

- **caturdaśāvyākṛtavastu** — the fourteen undetermined matters known from sūtra
  - **status:** Bhāṣya contextual term
  - **function:** triggers the distinction between ethical indeterminacy and a question that is set aside

- **sthāpanīya-praśna** — a question to be set aside
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit definition of one sense of `avyākṛta`
  - **function:** names a question whose proposed form does not admit direct predication

- **ekāṃśavyākaraṇīya** — to be answered categorically / from one determinate side
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical classification
  - **function:** answer-mode for an unconditional determination

- **vibhajyavyākaraṇīya** — to be answered after division
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical classification
  - **function:** answer-mode requiring discrimination of relevant cases or species

- **paripṛcchya-vyākaraṇīya** — to be answered after questioning in return
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical classification
  - **function:** answer-mode that elicits the missing relation, subject, or intended determination from the questioner

- **arthopasaṃhitatva** — connected or convergent with the relevant meaning
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reasoning term
  - **function:** offered by the Ābhidharmikas as a condition for categorical determination

- **āpekṣika** — relative, dependent upon comparison
  - **status:** Bhāṣya reasoning term
  - **function:** explains how one subject can be both superior and inferior relative to different comparison classes

- **śaṭha** — tricky, evasive, or disingenuous interlocutor
  - **status:** Bhāṣya practical-dialogical term
  - **function:** explains one use of counter-questioning rather than supplying the division directly

- **vandhyāputra** — son of a barren woman
  - **status:** Bhāṣya analogy
  - **function:** illustrates why predicates cannot be assigned to a subject whose real entity has not been established

## Doctrinal Determination

The first-pass Kārikā analysis correctly identified four answer-forms, but the Bhāṣya adds three major determinations.

### 1. `avyākṛta` is equivocal across domains

The most immediate doctrinal point is:

```text
avyākṛta-dharma
    ethically indeterminate

avyākṛta-praśna
    not answered as framed / to be set aside
```

These must never be collapsed.

### 2. Setting aside is grounded in the status of the subject

The initial example is especially precise:

```text
“Is the sattva other than the skandhas or not?”
```

The question is set aside because:

```text
sattva-dravya does not exist
```

The barren woman's son analogy shows the logical structure:

```text
failure to establish subject
    → alternatives predicated of that subject are inadmissible
```

This is stronger than mere agnosticism.

### 3. The fourfold taxonomy remains internally debated

The alternative teacher shows that categoricity, division, and counter-question are not always separable merely by surface grammar. The correct mode depends upon how the subject, predicate, and intended comparison are determined.

Thus the four modes are not mechanical templates. They are acts of discriminative judgment.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required, not replacement.**

The first-pass translation is substantially correct. The Bhāṣya shows that `sthāpya` should be read specifically as:

> **a question to be set aside as posed**

rather than as a vague suspension of judgment.

A tighter translation is therefore:

> Questions are answered categorically, after division, or after questioning in return; another is to be set aside—as illustrated respectively by questions concerning death, arising, comparative superiority, and whether a being is other than the aggregates.

This last wording follows the Bhāṣya's actual example more closely than the compressed phrase “otherness of a self.”

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.22 gives us a mature logic of interrogative determination.

Before answering the content of a question, one must determine the **question itself**.

```text
Question
    ↓ reflective determination
What logical form does this question permit?
```

The four operations are:

```text
1. assert
   complete determination

2. divide
   hidden plurality of cases

3. return-question
   missing correlate or intended subject

4. set aside
   inadmissible presupposition / unestablished subject
```

The Bhāṣya makes especially clear that `vibhajya` is a genuine analytic operation:

```text
dharma
    → time
    → aggregate/domain
    → ethical status
    → karmic path
    → root
    → vijñapti / avijñapti
```

It is progressive determination through articulated difference.

Meanwhile `paripṛcchya` leaves the burden of determination with the interlocutor:

```text
“You tell me what you mean.”
```

Thus division and counter-question are logically related but pragmatically distinct.

Most importantly:

```text
sthāpana
    ≠ lack of knowledge
    ≠ hesitation
    ≠ mere refusal
```

It is the positive judgment that the offered question cannot be answered under its present presupposition.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage is directly useful for a Rational Agent because it distinguishes **answer failure** from **question failure**.

A robust system needs at least:

```text
CategoricalAnswer
DividedAnswer
CounterQuestion
SetAside
```

But the Bhāṣya improves the architecture further. `SetAside` should not mean only “I do not know.” It should carry a reason such as:

```text
UnestablishedSubject
FalsePresupposition
InadmissibleAlternativeSet
```

Likewise, `DividedAnswer` should support recursively generated distinctions, while `CounterQuestion` should preserve the unresolved parameter and request that the interlocutor supply it.

The textual priority remains the fourfold `praśna-vyākaraṇa` scheme and its function in distinguishing two senses of `avyākṛta`.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.22 is not a digression from the anuśaya chapter. It arises because the preceding verses use `avyākṛta` ethically, while sūtra also speaks of `avyākṛta` speculative matters.

> **Vasubandhu blocks the equivocation by distinguishing an ethically indeterminate dharma from a question that is set aside. He then develops a fourfold discipline of answering: assert when determination is complete, divide when conditions differ, question in return when the intended relation is missing, and set aside when the proposed subject or alternatives are not admissible. The Bhāṣya further shows that these modes require judgment rather than mechanical application, preserving disagreements among teachers and a Mahāsāṅghika sūtra formulation of the same fourfold structure.**

This is a substantial Bhāṣya enrichment of the terse Kārikā and gives the chapter an explicit theory of responsible rational response.
