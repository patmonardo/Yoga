# VAK 5.39 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.39**

```text
aṇavo 'nugatāś caite dvidhā cāpy anuśerate |
anubadhnanti yasmāc ca tasmād anuśayāḥ smṛtāḥ || 5.39 ||
```

**Translation:** These are subtle and accompanying; they also lie latent in two ways. Because they continue to bind, they are therefore called *anuśayas*.

## Translation

It has been stated that in the sūtras the anuśayas themselves are designated by the terms influx, flood, yoke, and appropriation.

Now, what is the meaning of *anuśaya*, and what, successively, is the meaning of *upādāna*?

These are called subtle because their mode of activity is subtle and therefore difficult to know. They are called accompanying because of their association with acquisition or possession (*prāpti*).

They “lie latent” in two ways: through object-support and through conjunction.

They “continue to bind” because, even without deliberate application, and even when one tries to restrain them, they repeatedly become manifest again.

For these reasons they are called *anuśayas*.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The preceding verses established that the same anuśaya-field is designated functionally as *āsrava*, *ogha*, *yoga*, and *upādāna*. VAK 5.39 now asks what the word *anuśaya* itself means.

The answer is not a single lexical derivation but a fourfold functional explanation:

```text
aṇu        subtle
anugata    accompanying
anuśayana  lying latent in two ways
anubandha  continuing to bind
```

### 1. `aṇu` — subtle

The Bhāṣya glosses this as:

```text
sūkṣma-pracāratva
    subtle mode of operation

→ durvijñānatā
    difficult to know
```

The anuśaya is therefore not defined as an occult substance hidden behind experience. Its subtlety concerns the mode in which it operates and the difficulty of directly discerning that operation.

### 2. `anugata` — accompanying

The Bhāṣya explains accompaniment through:

```text
prāpty-anuṣaṅga
```

that is, association with *prāpti*, acquisition/possession. The anuśaya remains connected with the continuum through the structure by which the practitioner possesses the relevant afflictive determination.

This is stronger and more technical than the Pass-1 suggestion that `anugata` merely means temporal persistence.

### 3. `anuśerate dvidhā` — they lie latent in two ways

This is resolved explicitly:

```text
ālambanataḥ
    through object-support

saṃprayogataḥ
    through conjunction
```

This confirms the distinction developed earlier in 5.23–5.32. An anuśaya can adhere with respect to a dharma because that dharma is its object-support, or because it is conjoined with the cognition in which the anuśaya occurs.

Thus:

```text
latent relation
    ≠
one undifferentiated hidden presence
```

It is a typed relation.

### 4. `anubadhnanti` — they continue to bind

The Bhāṣya gives the most practical criterion:

```text
aprayogeṇa
    without deliberate application

prativārayato 'pi
    even while one tries to restrain them

punaḥ punaḥ saṃmukhībhāva
    they repeatedly come face-to-face / become manifest
```

An anuśaya is therefore recognized by recurrence. Even without intentionally producing the affliction, and despite opposition to it, the affliction can become manifest again because the underlying anuśaya has not yet been abandoned.

The relation is:

```text
unabandoned anuśaya
    → recurrent manifestation possible
```

not:

```text
anuśaya
    = continuously manifest kleśa
```

## New Technical Terms

- **sūkṣmapracāratva** — subtle mode of operation. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary. **Function:** explains `aṇu`.
- **durvijñānatā** — difficulty of being known or discerned. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary. **Function:** phenomenological consequence of subtle operation.
- **prāptyanuṣaṅga** — association with acquisition/possession. **Status:** Bhāṣya technical explanation. **Function:** explains `anugata`.
- **ālambanataḥ** — by way of object-support. **Status:** previously encountered. **Function:** first mode of `anuśayana`.
- **saṃprayogataḥ** — by way of conjunction. **Status:** previously encountered. **Function:** second mode of `anuśayana`.
- **aprayogeṇa** — without deliberate application. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory term. **Function:** shows spontaneous recurrence.
- **saṃmukhībhāva** — becoming present or manifest, literally coming face-to-face. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary. **Function:** describes recurrent manifestation of affliction.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.39 gives a compact functional definition of anuśaya:

```text
Anuśaya
    = subtle afflictive determination
      possessed by the continuum
      related through object-support and/or conjunction
      capable of recurring without deliberate production
      and thereby continuing bondage
```

The most important distinction is:

```text
latency
    ≠
inactivity in every sense
```

An anuśaya need not be manifest as a kleśa in order to retain the structural conditions for renewed manifestation.

At the same time, the Bhāṣya does not define it here as a numerically identical hidden substance travelling through time. Its description is relational and operational: possession, object-support, conjunction, recurrence, and bondage.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

Pass 1 correctly identified subtlety, accompaniment, twofold latency, and continued binding. The Bhāṣya now fixes the two modes of latency exactly as:

```text
ālambana
saṃprayoga
```

and makes `anugata` specifically dependent on `prāptyanuṣaṅga`.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya supplies a dispositional structure without reducing disposition to an inert property:

```text
not manifest now
    does not imply
incapable of manifestation
```

The anuśaya is determinate through a relation-network:

```text
isPossessedThrough
hasAsObjectSupport
isConjoinedWith
canBecomeManifestAgain
continuesToBind
```

Its recurrence criterion is especially important:

```text
no deliberate production
+ attempted restraint
+ repeated reappearance
    → evidence of unabandoned anuśaya
```

This is a genetic explanation of recurrence rather than a bare assertion that the same affliction simply persists unchanged.

## Organon Contact Point

The strongest point for the Organon is the distinction between **state**, **relation**, and **recurrent generation**.

A latent determination should not be modeled merely as a Boolean stored flag. It is better represented as a power embedded in a typed relational field:

```text
Disposition
    hasObjectSupport
    hasConjunctiveContext
    isPossessedByContinuum
    mayGenerateManifestEvent
```

The Bhāṣya thereby gives a practical test for a latent power: it is disclosed through recurrent manifestation even when explicit application is absent and counter-effort is present.

This preserves the project’s genetic standard:

```text
recurrence
    requires an explanatory ground
```

rather than being accepted as brute facticity.
