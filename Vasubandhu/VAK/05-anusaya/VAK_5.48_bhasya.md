### Source caution

The e-text has several evident orthographic corruptions in this passage, including `āhlīkya` for expected `āhrīkya`, `bhrakṣa` for `mrakṣa`, and `niḥṣyanada` for `niḥṣyanda`. They are preserved above as transmitted rather than silently normalized.

## Translation

Anger is an injury or hostile striking directed toward sentient or non-sentient things, excluding ordinary ill will and harmfulness. Concealment is the hiding of a fault.

Of these ten envelopments, shamelessness, restlessness, and avarice arise from attachment. These three secondary afflictions are **outflows or derivatives (`niḥṣyanda`) of attachment**.

Concerning concealment there is disagreement. Some say that it is a derivative of craving; others that it is a derivative of ignorance; still others that it derives from both. [The distinction is made] respectively with regard to faults that are known and not known.

Torpor, sleepiness, and lack of moral concern arise from ignorance. These three are derivatives (`niḥṣyanda`) of ignorance.

## Course of Argument

### 1. The tenfold field remains in force

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika classification / Vasubandhu reporting**

The verse presupposes the tenfold `paryavasthāna` enumeration established immediately before it:

```text
8 of the Prakaraṇaśāstra
    +
krodha
mrakṣa
    =
10 paryavasthānas
```

The opening `krodha-mrakṣau ca` therefore completes that tenfold field rather than introducing two unrelated factors.

The Bhāṣya defines both:

```text
krodha
    hostile striking / resentment toward beings or things,
    distinguished from vyāpāda and vihiṃsā

mrakṣa
    concealment of a fault
```

The exact nuance of the compressed definition of `krodha` should remain cautious because the transmitted prose is terse.

### 2. Three are derivatives of rāga

The Kārikā says:

```text
rāgotthā āhrīkya-auddhatya-matsarāḥ
```

The Bhāṣya immediately makes the relation explicit:

```text
ete traya upakleśā rāga-niḥṣyandāḥ
```

Thus:

```text
rāga
    ↓ niḥṣyanda
āhrīkya
auddhatya
mātsarya
```

This is stronger than the general classification of VAK 5.46. There we had to retract the claim that every upakleśa is defined as causally generated from a kleśa. Here, however, a genuine derivative relation is expressly stated.

`niḥṣyanda` should therefore be retained as the controlling technical term: the three are outflows, consequences, or derivatives of attachment.

### 3. Mrakṣa is a disputed derivation

**Voice: multiple reported positions**

The Bhāṣya marks the issue directly:

```text
bhrakṣe vivādaḥ
    there is disagreement concerning concealment
```

Three positions follow:

```text
Voice: reported position 1
mrakṣa = tṛṣṇā-niḥṣyanda
        derivative of craving

Voice: reported position 2
mrakṣa = avidyā-niḥṣyanda
        derivative of ignorance

Voice: reported position 3
mrakṣa = ubhayoḥ
        derivative of both
```

The compact phrase `yathā kramaṃ jñātājñātānām` indicates that the analysis turns on whether the fault being concealed is known or unknown. Because the source is compressed, the exact distribution of known/unknown across all three reported positions should not be over-systematized here.

What is secure is the doctrinal result:

```text
one manifest affliction
    does not necessarily have
one uncontested root-analysis
```

### 4. Three are derivatives of avidyā

The final group is equally explicit:

```text
avidyā
    ↓ niḥṣyanda
styāna
middha
anapatrāpya
```

The Bhāṣya repeats:

```text
ete trayo 'vidyāniḥṣyandāḥ
```

Thus the two undisputed triads are structurally parallel:

```text
rāga-niḥṣyanda triad
    āhrīkya
    auddhatya
    mātsarya

avidyā-niḥṣyanda triad
    styāna
    middha
    anapatrāpya
```

## New Technical Terms

- **niḥṣyanda** — outflow, derivative, consequence, or result issuing from a determining source.
  - **status:** technically decisive here; it supplies the relation only provisionally hypothesized in the earlier Kārikā pass.
  - **function:** genetic / classificatory relation between root kleśa and specified upakleśa.

- **rāga-niḥṣyanda** — a derivative or outflow of attachment.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory compound.
  - **function:** grounds the affiliation of āhrīkya, auddhatya, and mātsarya.

- **avidyā-niḥṣyanda** — a derivative or outflow of ignorance.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory compound.
  - **function:** grounds the affiliation of styāna, middha, and anapatrāpya.

- **avadyapracchādana** — concealment of a fault.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definition of `mrakṣa`.
  - **function:** defines the disputed paryavasthāna.

- **vivāda** — disagreement, dispute.
  - **status:** familiar term, doctrinally important here.
  - **function:** explicitly marks competing root-analyses rather than a settled classification.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.48 finally earns the genetic language that VAK 5.46 did **not** yet permit us to generalize.

The structure is not:

```text
all upakleśas by definition
    arise from root kleśas in one fixed way
```

Rather:

```text
specific upakleśas
    are explicitly established
    as niḥṣyandas of specified kleśas
```

This distinction matters.

For six factors the affiliation is stated without dispute:

```text
rāga
    → āhrīkya
    → auddhatya
    → mātsarya

avidyā
    → styāna
    → middha
    → anapatrāpya
```

For `mrakṣa`, however, the Bhāṣya preserves disagreement.

So Abhidharma diagnosis includes both:

```text
settled genetic determination
and
disputed genetic determination
```

The taxonomy is therefore not flattened merely to obtain a complete tree.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and correction required.**

The Kārikā pass correctly anticipated a root-derivative analysis, but several interpretive expansions exceeded the source.

Most importantly, the Bhāṣya gives the technical relation as `niḥṣyanda`. We should therefore prefer language such as **derivative**, **outflow**, or **arising from**, without adding an elaborate psychological mechanism unless later passages supply it.

Likewise, the dispute concerning `mrakṣa` is not simply a free hypothesis that attachment, ignorance, or both could explain concealment. It is an explicitly transmitted three-way doctrinal disagreement.

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a genuine relation of generated determination:

```text
root
    ↓ niḥṣyanda
derivative
```

But the relation has two logical forms.

### Determinate derivation

```text
Rāga(x)
    → specified derivative class

Avidyā(x)
    → specified derivative class
```

### Contested derivation

```text
Mrakṣa
    → Rāga ?
    → Avidyā ?
    → both ?
```

Thus the system must distinguish:

```text
existence of a phenomenon
    from
certainty about its ground
```

A derivative can be well identified phenomenally while its genetic determination remains disputed.

This is an important logical gain. Explanation is not secured merely because the phenomenon has been classified. The question of **ground** remains a further determination.

## Organon Contact Point

For the Organon, VAK 5.48 supplies a disciplined form of causal/genetic modeling:

```text
ObservedDetermination
    ↓
GroundHypothesis
    ↓
status:
    established
    disputed
    multiple
```

The important lesson is not that every observed state must be forced beneath exactly one parent node. The Bhāṣya itself preserves an unresolved competing-ground structure for `mrakṣa`.

So a Rational Kośa representation should allow:

```text
hasGround(x, y)
hasGroundStatus(x, y, established | disputed)
```

rather than silently converting doctrinal disagreement into artificial certainty.

This also gives a strong genetic-continuity test: when a derivative relation is asserted, we should be able to identify the textual middle term that produces it. Here that middle term is explicitly `niḥṣyanda`.
