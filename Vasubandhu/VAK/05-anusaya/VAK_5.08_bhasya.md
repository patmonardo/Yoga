# VAK_5.08 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> īśvarādiṣu nityātma-viparyāsāt pravartate |
> kāraṇābhiniveśo 'to duḥkha-dṛg-gheya eva saḥ || 5.8 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Fixation upon a cause such as Īśvara arises from inversion concerning a permanent self. Therefore that fixation is to be abandoned precisely through seeing suffering.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_5.08.md`](./VAK_5.08.md)

---

## Bhāṣya Source

Source anchor: Chapter 5, Pradhan 282|13–283|04.

The Bhāṣya asks why a causal form of `śīlavrataparāmarśa` is not abandoned through seeing origination. It answers that one who sees Īśvara, Prajāpati, or another supposed first cause does so by fixing upon that cause as permanent, one, self, and agent. Hence the causal fixation arises from `nityātma-viparyāsa` and is abandoned through seeing suffering, because the underlying graspings at permanence and self are abandoned there.

The commentary then broadens the case to ritual and ascetic practices falsely treated as causes of purification or a superior rebirth. It cites a scriptural formula defining `śīlavrataparāmarśa` as apprehending what is not a cause as a cause.

The discussion then tests the classification itself: if all `sāsrava` cognitive supports are mistaken with respect to suffering, what precisely distinguishes the form abandoned through seeing the path? Analogous questions arise concerning false views related to origination and nirodha. Vasubandhu therefore concludes:

> `tasmāt parīkṣya eṣo 'rthaḥ`
>
> Therefore this matter must be examined.

**Source caution:** the working e-text is a reference text rather than a critical edition; irregular forms are not silently normalized here.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository question-and-answer**

A view concerning a cause can belong to `śīlavrataparāmarśa`. Why is such a view not to be abandoned through seeing origination?

Whoever sees Īśvara, Prajāpati, or some other principle as the cause does so after becoming fixed upon it as permanent, one, a self, and an agent. Therefore:

> Fixation upon a cause such as Īśvara proceeds from inversion concerning permanence and self; therefore that fixation is to be abandoned precisely through seeing suffering.

For through seeing suffering the two graspings involved there—grasping at permanence and grasping at self—are abandoned. Therefore the causal fixation produced by them is abandoned through that same seeing.

The same classification is extended to ritual or ascetic practices falsely regarded as causes of purification or a superior result. The scriptural definition is that one apprehends what is not a cause as a cause; such attachment to rules and observances is to be abandoned through seeing suffering.

Why? Because it is mistaken with respect to suffering. All those whose cognitive supports are `sāsrava` are mistaken with respect to suffering.

The commentary then raises a further problem: what distinguishes the form of `śīlavrataparāmarśa` abandoned through seeing the path? If a false view or doubt concerns the path, one does not attain purity through the denial itself but through some alternative path one has adopted. Parallel difficulties arise for false views concerning origination or nirodha.

Vasubandhu therefore closes the local discussion by saying that the matter must be examined.

---

## Course of the Argument

The Bhāṣya begins from a classificatory difficulty created by VAK 5.07.

`Śīlavrataparāmarśa` includes taking what is not a cause as a cause and what is not a path as a path. If the error concerns causation, one might expect:

```text
false causal fixation
    → error concerning samudaya
    → samudaya-darśana-heya
```

Vasubandhu instead explains why one important form belongs under seeing suffering.

The decisive genetic sequence is:

```text
Īśvara / Prajāpati / another supposed first cause
    ↓ taken as
nitya
    permanent

eka
    one

ātman
    self

kartṛ
    agent
    ↓
kāraṇābhiniveśa
    fixation upon that as cause
```

The causal fixation is derivative. Its immediate root is not simply ignorance of origination; it is the prior construction of a permanent self-agent.

The removal relation is therefore:

```text
nitya-grāha + ātma-grāha
    ↓ abandoned by
duḥkha-darśana
    ↓ therefore
kāraṇābhiniveśa produced by them
    is also abandoned there
```

This confirms the first-pass insight while making it more precise: the problem is not merely an “eternal cause” in abstraction, but an explanatory posit structured as permanent, unitary self and agent.

---

## Ritual and Practical Causal Error

The Bhāṣya broadens the case beyond creator doctrine. The shared form is:

```text
akāraṇa
    what is not a cause

    taken as

kāraṇa
    cause
```

Thus VAK 5.08 is not fundamentally a polemic against a divine name. `Īśvara` is one instance of the larger category of causal misdetermination already defined under `śīlavrataparāmarśa`.

---

## A Stronger Bhāṣya Determination: `vipratipanna`

The commentary gives a second rationale:

```text
duḥkhe vipratipannatvāt
    because it is mistaken with respect to suffering
```

and generalizes this to afflictions whose cognitive supports are `sāsrava`.

This makes the assignment to `duḥkha-darśana` depend not only on the explicit proposition asserted but also on the nature of the cognitive support and the underlying misapprehension of the conditioned field.

---

## The Bhāṣya Does Not Pretend the Classification Is Trivial

The final section turns critical. If attachment to a false path is classified by a path-related cognitive support, why is it not simply another case of being mistaken regarding suffering? Conversely, if false view or doubt denies the path, the person does not seek purification through the denial itself but through some alternative path that has been adopted.

The same problem can be extended to false views whose cognitive supports concern origination or nirodha.

Vasubandhu therefore concludes:

```text
tasmāt parīkṣya eṣo 'rthaḥ
```

> Therefore this matter must be examined.

This sentence matters methodologically. The Bhāṣya does not merely defend the inherited matrix dogmatically. It exposes pressure inside the classificatory rule and leaves the reader with a problem requiring further discrimination.

---

## Voice Identification

The dominant exposition is **Voice: Vasubandhu**.

The passage contains internal questions functioning as objections:

**Voice: Objector / dialectical question**
- Why is causal fixation not abandoned through seeing origination?
- What distinguishes the path-abandonable form?
- Why should analogous errors involving origination or nirodha not be assigned to those truths?

The final `parīkṣya eṣo 'rthaḥ` is best retained as Vasubandhu's explicit critical reservation.

## New Technical Terms

- **kāraṇadṛṣṭi** — view concerning a cause
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** frames the classificatory problem of causal misdetermination

- **kartṛ** — agent, doer
  - **status:** common Sanskrit term but technically decisive here
  - **function:** one of the determinations projected onto the supposed eternal cause

- **nityagrāha** — grasping at permanence
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** one of the root graspings whose abandonment removes the derived causal fixation

- **ātmagrāha** — grasping at self
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** second root grasping underlying the creator-cause construction

- **vipratipanna** — mistaken, wrongly oriented with respect to something
  - **status:** technically operative in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** explains why a given afflicted determination belongs to a truth-specific abandonment class

- **sāsravālambana** — having a cognitive support that is sāsrava
  - **status:** Bhāṣya relational formulation
  - **function:** generalizes the relation of afflicted cognitive support to error regarding suffering

- **akāraṇaṃ kāraṇataḥ pratyeti** — apprehends what is not a cause as a cause
  - **status:** scriptural doctrinal formula cited by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** canonical definition of causal `śīlavrataparāmarśa`

- **parīkṣya** — to be examined, investigated
  - **status:** explicit methodological marker
  - **function:** registers Vasubandhu's reservation about the adequacy of the received classification

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya confirms that the causal form of `śīlavrataparāmarśa` cannot be classified merely from the surface content “cause.” Instead one must reconstruct its genesis:

```text
permanent-self grasping
    + agency
    ↓
false causal posit
    ↓
causal fixation
```

Because the underlying permanence/self graspings are abandoned through seeing suffering, the derivative fixation is assigned there as well.

Yet Vasubandhu's closing examination shows that the broader rule relating cognitive support and truth-specific abandonment remains philosophically nontrivial.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and strengthening; no basic translation reversal.**

A stronger canonical translation is:

> Fixation upon Īśvara or another supposed first cause proceeds from the inversion that grasps such a cause as permanent self and agent. Since those graspings are abandoned through seeing suffering, the causal fixation produced by them is likewise abandoned through seeing suffering.

The earlier philosophical rendering should be restrained from suggesting that Vasubandhu is rejecting causal inquiry as such. His target is the misidentification of a non-cause as cause, especially when generated by permanence/self fixation.

---

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a genuinely genetic account of error:

```text
prior misconstrual
    ↓ produces
derivative causal judgment
    ↓ becomes
fixed explanatory commitment
```

Correction operates at the generative root:

```text
remove nitya-grāha and ātma-grāha
    ↓
derivative kāraṇābhiniveśa loses its ground
```

At the same time, `parīkṣya eṣo 'rthaḥ` prevents us from turning this into an artificially clean formalism. Vasubandhu recognizes that mapping malformed cognition to a single truth-specific counteragent can itself require examination.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest Organon contact is a rule of **genetic diagnosis**:

```text
Do not classify an error only by its surface predicate.
Recover the prior determination that generates it.
```

A causal error may be downstream from a deeper subject-model:

```text
permanent self-agent model
    → creator-cause model
```

A rational diagnostic system would therefore need both surface error classification and genetic dependency analysis.

The Bhāṣya's own reservation is equally important: the mapping from defect to corrective truth must remain open to rational examination rather than being treated as an unquestionable lookup table.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.08 establishes a genetic explanation of causal `śīlavrataparāmarśa`:

> **A supposed eternal cause such as Īśvara is grasped as permanent, unitary self and agent. Because those permanence/self graspings are abandoned through seeing suffering, the causal fixation generated by them is abandoned there as well. The same category extends to practices in which a non-cause is treated as a cause. Yet Vasubandhu explicitly recognizes difficulties in the truth-specific classification and concludes that the matter requires examination.**

This is therefore both a doctrinal determination and a small instance of the Bhāṣya's own dialectical self-criticism.
