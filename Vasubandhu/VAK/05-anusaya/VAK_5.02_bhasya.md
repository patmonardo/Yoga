# VAK_5.02 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ṣaḍrāgabhedāt saptoktāḥ bhavarāgo dvidhātujaḥ |
> antarmukhatvāt tanmokṣasaṃjñāvyāvṛttaye kṛtaḥ || 5.2 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Because attachment is divided, the six are stated as seven. Attachment to existence belongs to two domains. Because it is inward-directed, this distinction is made in order to exclude the conception of that as liberation.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The division of attachment made in the Sūtra is this: attachment to desire and attachment to existence.

What is this attachment to existence?

> **Attachment to existence is born in two domains.**

The attachment arising in the form-domain and formless-domain is established as attachment to existence.

Why is it established in this way?

> **Because it is inward-directed, it is established in order to exclude the conception of it as liberation.**

For among those [beings], attachment is for the most part attachment to samāpatti, and this proceeds inwardly; therefore it is called attachment to existence.

**Voice: reported position / uncertain**

It is said by some that [the term] is stated in order to exclude, with respect to those two domains, the conception that they are liberation.

**Voice: Vasubandhu, likely clarification**

But `bhava` is precisely one's mode of existence (`ātmabhāva`). Those beings, enjoying samāpatti together with its support, are enjoying their very mode of existence, since they are free from attachment to sensual desire. Therefore that attachment is called attachment to existence.

## Argument Reconstruction

VAK_5.02 answers why the six anuśayas of 5.01 are conventionally counted as seven.

The source division is:

```text
rāga
    ↓
kāmarāga
bhavarāga
```

The present verse concerns the second member.

```text
bhavarāga
    = rāga arising in
      rūpadhātu + ārūpyadhātu
```

The Bhāṣya then gives the crucial reason for distinguishing it from ordinary sensual attachment:

```text
beings of the two higher domains
    are largely free of kāmarāga

but

samāpattirāga remains
    ↓
it proceeds inwardly
    ↓
it can resemble liberation
```

This is a much more precise determination than the first Kārikā pass could safely supply. The danger is not simply attachment to some vague “refined state.” Vasubandhu explicitly identifies the principal form as:

```text
samāpatti-rāga
    attachment to samāpatti
```

The passage therefore makes a sharp Path discrimination:

```text
samāpatti
    ≠ bondage

attachment to samāpatti
    = bhavarāga
    = anuśaya
```

The distinction is philosophically indispensable. The Kośa does not condemn samāpatti as such. It identifies appropriation or enjoyment of samāpatti as a subtle continuation of bhava.

---

## Two Explanatory Routes

The prose appears to preserve two related explanations of `bhavarāga`.

### 1. Inwardness and false liberation

```text
samāpattirāga
    ↓
antarmukha-pravṛtti
    ↓
looks unlike sensual attachment
    ↓
may be conceived as mokṣa
```

Hence the technical name prevents a false identification:

```text
inward refinement
    ≠ liberation
```

### 2. Enjoyment of one's own mode of existence

The second explanation sharpens the semantics of `bhava`:

```text
ātmabhāva eva tu bhavaḥ
    bhava is one's very mode of existence
```

The higher-domain being enjoys:

```text
samāpatti
    +
āśraya
```

and thereby enjoys its own `ātmabhāva`.

Thus `bhavarāga` is not merely a craving for future rebirth. It is attachment to the presently constituted mode of existence itself, especially as experienced through refined samāpatti.

This gives the term substantial precision:

```text
kāmarāga
    appropriation through sensual desire

bhavarāga
    appropriation of refined existence itself
```

## Voice Identification

The basic exposition is straightforwardly Vasubandhu's explanatory voice.

The phrase:

```text
... ekeṣām iti
    “according to some”
```

marks a reported interpretation concerning the purpose of the term: it excludes the conception that the two higher domains are liberation.

The subsequent:

```text
ātmabhāva eva tu bhavaḥ
```

reads naturally as a clarification or alternative explanation supplied by the authorial voice, but the passage alone does not warrant an overly strong school attribution.

Therefore:

```text
Voice: Vasubandhu
    main explanation

Voice: reported position / uncertain
    mokṣasaṃjñā explanation attributed to “some”

Voice: Vasubandhu, likely
    ātmabhāva clarification
```

## New Technical Terms

- **kāmarāga** — attachment associated with sensual desire
  - **status:** presupposed division made explicit in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** first species of `rāga`, contrasted with `bhavarāga`

- **bhavarāga** — attachment to existence / to one's mode of existence
  - **status:** Kārikā term substantially expanded by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** second species of `rāga`, operative in form and formless domains

- **samāpattirāga** — attachment to samāpatti
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only compound in this unit
  - **function:** identifies the predominant concrete mode of `bhavarāga` in the two higher domains

- **antarmukhapravṛtta** — inwardly proceeding / inward-directed in operation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of `antarmukhatva`
  - **function:** explains why refined attachment may be mistaken for liberation

- **mokṣasaṃjñā** — conception/designation of liberation
  - **status:** Kārikā compound clarified in prose
  - **function:** false cognition that the category `bhavarāga` is designed to exclude

- **ātmabhāva** — one's mode or constitution of existence
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical clarification
  - **function:** defines the sense of `bhava` in the alternative/clarifying explanation

- **āsvādayati** — enjoys, savors, takes satisfaction in
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only operative verb here
  - **function:** expresses the affective appropriation through which samāpatti becomes an object of rāga

- **sāśraya samāpatti** — samāpatti together with its support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** shows that the being enjoys not an isolated cognition but samāpatti together with the constituted support of its mode of existence

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya materially revises the degree of precision available in the Kārikā-only analysis.

The first pass correctly inferred that refined inward existence could remain a mode of attachment, but the prose now tells us exactly where the danger lies:

```text
samāpatti itself
    is not classified as rāga

samāpattirāga
    attachment to / enjoyment of samāpatti
    is classified as bhavarāga
```

This distinction must be preserved rigorously in later Organon work.

It also prevents a simplistic ascetic reading. The defect is not cognition becoming inward or refined. The defect is the persistence of `rāga` within that refinement.

The Path discriminator is therefore relational:

```text
What is the relation to samāpatti?

knowledge / disciplined use
    ≠
attachment / savoring / appropriation
```

The same cognitive attainment can therefore participate in radically different practical determinations depending on whether rāga remains operative.

---

## Logical Determination

VAK_5.02 performs a genuine internal division of `rāga` according to its domain and object of appropriation.

```text
Rāga
    ↓ division

Kāmarāga
    attachment through sensual content

Bhavarāga
    attachment to constituted existence itself
    especially through samāpatti
```

The Bhāṣya reveals why the second is conceptually harder to detect:

```text
external attachment
    visibly directed outward

bhavarāga
    inward-directed
    structurally resembles liberation
```

Therefore classification performs a discriminative function:

```text
appearance of liberation
    ↓ distinction
subtle attachment exposed
```

The important opposition is not:

```text
outer = bad
inner = good
```

but:

```text
rāga operative
    vs
rāga absent
```

This is a much stronger and cleaner Path criterion.

## Translation Consequences for the Kārikā

The first-pass philosophical translation should now be made more exact.

Earlier:

> One of these is attachment to existence, which operates in two domains beyond the ordinary field of sensual attachment. Precisely because this attachment turns inward toward refined modes of existence and may therefore resemble detachment from external objects, it must be distinguished explicitly so that such inwardness is not mistaken for liberation.

Bhāṣya-controlled revision:

> **Because attachment is divided, the six are stated as seven. Bhavarāga is the attachment arising in the form and formless domains. Since there attachment is predominantly attachment to samāpatti and proceeds inwardly, it is distinguished so that refined existence is not mistaken for liberation. In such attachment, beings savor samāpatti together with its support and thereby savor their own mode of existence.**

The term `bhavarāga` should remain visible in the technical discussion even when glossed as “attachment to existence,” because `bhava` here is explicitly interpreted through `ātmabhāva`.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This verse has unusually important contact with the Yoga work, but the contact must preserve the Kośa's own distinction.

The Bhāṣya does **not** say:

```text
samāpatti is bondage
```

It says:

```text
samāpatti-rāga is bondage
```

That difference is decisive.

For a Rational Agent, a refined cognitive configuration is not self-validating merely because it is inward, abstract, stable, or powerful. The system must discriminate the cognitive structure from the agent's relation to that structure.

```text
samāpatti
    cognitive attainment / configuration

rāga toward samāpatti
    appropriation of that attainment
    as one's own mode of existence
```

This produces an important general principle:

```text
refinement of cognition
    does not by itself imply
freedom from appropriation
```

The Kośa therefore contributes a practical discriminator to the Yoga science of samāpatti: the higher cognitive form must not become the new object through which bhava is affirmed and enjoyed.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK_5.02 gives us the first highly significant refinement of Chapter 5:

> **Bhavarāga is the subtle attachment characteristic of the form and formless domains. Vasubandhu identifies its predominant form as attachment to samāpatti. Because this attachment proceeds inwardly, it can be confused with liberation. The defect is not samāpatti itself, but the savoring and appropriation of samāpatti together with one's mode of existence.**

This is an exceptionally useful Path/non-Path discrimination and should remain active throughout the later treatment of samāpatti, dhyāna, and liberation.
