# VAK 5.46 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.46**

```text
ye 'py anye caitasāḥ kliṣṭāḥ saṃskāraskandha-saṃjñitāḥ /
kleśebhyas te 'py upakleśās te tu na kleśa-saṃjñitāḥ // 5.46 //
```

**Translation:** Those other afflicted mental factors, included in the saṃskāra aggregate and distinct from the kleśas, are also called upakleśas; but they are not designated as kleśas.

See: [VAK_5.46.md](./VAK_5.46.md)

## Translation

The anuśayas have already been explained.

The upakleśas must now be stated. In this connection, whatever are kleśas are also upakleśas, because they defile the mind.

Those other afflicted mental factors that are included in the aggregate of formations and are distinct from the kleśas are also upakleśas; but they are not designated as kleśas.

Those other afflicted dharmas, apart from the kleśas, that are mental factors included in the saṃskāra-skandha are upakleśas. These are the factors taught in the Kṣudravastuka.

Here, however, we shall explain only those included under the paryavasthānas and the stains of the afflictions (kleśa-mala).

What, then, are the paryavasthānas?

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / expository transition**

VAK 5.46 opens the upakleśa block after closing the preceding classifications:

```text
anuśaya
    already treated
        ↓
upakleśa
    now to be explained
```

The Bhāṣya immediately prevents a simple opposition between `kleśa` and `upakleśa`.

### 1. Every kleśa is also an upakleśa

The first statement is explicit:

```text
ye yāvat kleśā
    upakleśā api te
```

Whatever are kleśas are also upakleśas.

The reason is:

```text
cittopakleśanāt
    because they defile the mind
```

Thus `upakleśa` is initially a broader functional designation. A dharma qualifies as upakleśa insofar as it contaminates or disturbs citta.

This means:

```text
Kleśa ⊆ Upakleśa
```

rather than:

```text
Kleśa ∩ Upakleśa = ∅
```

### 2. There are additional upakleśas that are not kleśas

The Kārikā then identifies another class:

```text
anye kleśebhyaḥ
    other than / apart from the kleśas

kliṣṭā dharmāḥ
    afflicted dharmas

saṃskāraskandha-saṃgṛhītāḥ caitasikāḥ
    mental factors included in the saṃskāra-skandha
```

These too are upakleśas, but they do not receive the designation `kleśa`.

This is the decisive grammatical correction to the first-pass interpretation. `kleśebhyaḥ` is separative here, not causal. The verse is not saying:

```text
these arise from the kleśas
```

but:

```text
these are other than the kleśas
```

while remaining afflicted mental factors.

Therefore the Bhāṣya does **not** establish at VAK 5.46 that every upakleśa is genetically produced by a specific root kleśa. Later verses may establish origination relations for particular factors, but that cannot be imported into this verse as its definition.

### 3. The full class is larger than the coming local analysis

The prose says these additional upakleśas are those taught in the `Kṣudravastuka`.

But Vasubandhu immediately narrows the present task:

```text
iha tu
    here, however

paryavasthāna-kleśamala-saṃgṛhītān eva
    only those included among
    the paryavasthānas and kleśa-stains
```

So Chapter 5 is not claiming to enumerate every possible upakleśa at this point. It deliberately selects two subgroups for analysis.

The movement is:

```text
Upakleśa — broad functional class
    ├── kleśas themselves
    └── other afflicted caitasikas
            ↓
        full Kṣudravastuka field
            ↓
        local Chapter 5 selection:
            paryavasthāna
            kleśa-mala
```

## New Technical Terms

- **cittopakleśana** — defiling, contaminating, or disturbing the mind.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** gives the functional ground on which kleśas themselves count as upakleśas.

- **kleśebhyaḥ ... anye** — “other than the kleśas,” with ablative of separation.
  - **status:** grammatical determination of a Kārikā phrase.
  - **function:** distinguishes non-kleśa upakleśas without asserting causal derivation from kleśas.

- **Kṣudravastuka** — a textual/doctrinal source in which the wider set of these secondary afflictive factors is taught.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only source reference in this passage.
  - **function:** marks the broader canonical inventory beyond the subset treated here.

- **kleśa-mala** — “stain of the afflictions” / afflictive stain.
  - **status:** introduced as a local subgroup to be treated in the following verses.
  - **function:** classificatory restriction of the coming analysis.

## Doctrinal Determination

The most important result is that `upakleśa` is not simply a species subordinate to `kleśa`.

The Bhāṣya instead gives overlapping classifications:

```text
all kleśas
    are upakleśas
    because they defile citta

some upakleśas
    are not kleśas
    because they are other afflicted caitasikas
```

Therefore:

```text
Kleśa(x) → Upakleśa(x)

Upakleśa(x) ↛ Kleśa(x)
```

This is another case where the same dharma can receive more than one doctrinal designation according to the relation under consideration.

`kleśa` names a stricter doctrinal class already established earlier in the chapter. `upakleśa`, in this passage, names the broader functional field of mental defilement.

The Bhāṣya then imposes a local scope restriction. It does not attempt a complete inventory of the broader class here.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation requires correction.**

The previous translation read `kleśebhyaḥ` causally:

> “secondary afflictions arising from the afflictions”

That is not supported by the Bhāṣya's own paraphrase:

```text
ye 'pyanye kleśebhyaḥ kliṣṭā dharmāḥ
```

where `anye ... kleśebhyaḥ` means “other afflicted dharmas distinct from the kleśas.”

Preferred translation:

> **Those other afflicted mental factors, included in the aggregate of formations and distinct from the kleśas, are also upakleśas; but they are not designated as kleśas.**

The first-pass claim that an upakleśa is defined by causal dependence on a root kleśa should therefore be withdrawn at this verse.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.46 gives us an overlapping taxonomy rather than a simple genus/species tree.

```text
functional predicate:
    defilesMind(x)
        → upakleśa(x)

stricter doctrinal predicate:
    kleśa(x)
        → upakleśa(x)
```

but:

```text
upakleśa(x)
    does not imply
kleśa(x)
```

This is important because the relation is not:

```text
root
    → derivative
```

as the Kārikā pass had proposed.

At this point the more accurate structure is:

```text
one field of afflicted mental determinations
    ↓ classified by different intensions

Kleśa
    stricter established class

Upakleśa
    broader class under the function
    of defiling citta
```

The subsequent verses may then determine genetic relations **within** this broader field, but those relations must be demonstrated individually rather than assumed from the name `upakleśa`.

## Organon Contact Point

This passage is a useful warning against forcing every taxonomy into a rigid inheritance hierarchy.

The Platform should permit:

```text
same entity
    satisfies multiple classifiers
    under different grounds
```

For example:

```text
Kleśa
    classifiedBy doctrinalStatus

Upakleśa
    classifiedBy mindDefilingFunction
```

The crucial architectural lesson is:

```text
broader functional extension
    need not be
causal derivation from a root class
```

This corrects the earlier Organon hypothesis for 5.46. If later verses assign particular upakleśas to rāga, dveṣa, avidyā, vicikitsā, and so on, those should be modeled as explicit generation/origination relations introduced there—not as part of the definition of `Upakleśa` itself.
