# VAK 5.52 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.52**

```text
tad-anye bhāvanā-heyāḥ svatantrāś ca tathā malāḥ /
kāme 'śubhāḥ trayo dvidhā pareṇāvyākṛtās tataḥ // 5.52 //
```

**Best translation:** The remaining [enveloping upakleśas] are to be abandoned by cultivation and are independent; the stains are likewise. In the desire-domain [these upakleśas] are unwholesome, although three are twofold; above it they are indeterminate.

## Translation

The remaining upakleśas included among the paryavasthānas, other than those [five described in VAK 5.51], are exclusively to be abandoned through cultivation.

Envy, avarice, remorse, anger, and concealment are also independent. These five upakleśas are independent because of their intense mode of association (`adhimātrasaṃprayogitva`).

Just as these five upakleśas beginning with envy are to be abandoned through cultivation and are independent, so likewise are the stains: the six kleśa-malas have the same status.

The upakleśas described above are unwholesome in the desire-domain. Even there, however, three are twofold: torpor, restlessness, and sleepiness may be either unwholesome or indeterminate.

Above the desire-domain, the upakleśas that occur there are indeterminate, according to applicability.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

### 1. Completion of the abandonment classification

VAK 5.51 identified five upakleśas as `dvidhā` with respect to abandonment because they can be associated with kleśas abandoned either through seeing or through cultivation.

VAK 5.52 now supplies the complement:

```text
remaining paryavasthāna-upakleśas
    → bhāvanā-heya only
```

The Bhāṣya identifies these remaining five explicitly:

```text
īrṣyā       envy
mātsarya    avarice
kaukṛtya    remorse
krodha      anger
mrakṣa      concealment
```

Thus the ten paryavasthānas are distributed by path-status as:

```text
Five that can be darśana-heya or bhāvanā-heya:
    āhrīkya
    anapatrāpya
    styāna
    middha
    auddhatya

Five that are bhāvanā-heya only:
    īrṣyā
    mātsarya
    kaukṛtya
    krodha
    mrakṣa
```

### 2. The five are also svatantra

The Bhāṣya adds that the latter five are `svatantra`.

It grounds this in:

```text
adhimātra-saṃprayogitva
```

The exact doctrinal nuance of this phrase should remain cautious. Literally it points to an intense or strong mode of association. What is secure is that `svatantra` is not being inferred merely from the lexical identity of the factors: the prose supplies a specific relational ground for their independent status.

This revises the first-pass suggestion that `svatantra` simply means a mental factor that arises entirely on its own. The Bhāṣya's criterion is more technical and association-based.

### 3. The six kleśa-malas have the same two statuses

The six stains introduced in VAK 5.49–5.50 are explicitly said to be `tathaiva`—the same:

```text
six kleśa-malas
    → bhāvanā-heya
    → svatantra
```

So the passage closes the path-status question for both groups:

```text
five remaining paryavasthānas
six kleśa-malas
    ↓
abhandoned through cultivation
and treated as independent factors
```

### 4. Ethical status in the desire-domain

The argument then changes axis again.

The question is no longer how these factors are abandoned but what ethical (`kuśala/akuśala/avyākṛta`) value they have according to domain.

The general rule is:

```text
in kāmadhātu
    the stated upakleśas are akuśala
```

But there is an internal exception:

```text
styāna
auddhatya
middha
    ↓
in kāmadhātu:
    akuśala OR avyākṛta
```

This is the force of `trayo dvidhā` here.

It does **not** repeat the twofold abandonment-status of VAK 5.51. The same word `dvidhā` is now used under a different classificatory axis.

### 5. Above the desire-domain

The Bhāṣya concludes:

```text
kāmadhātor ūrdhvam
    above the desire-domain

upakleśāḥ yathāsaṃbhavam
    the upakleśas that occur there, as applicable

avyākṛtāḥ
    are indeterminate
```

This supplies an important domain qualification. Not every named upakleśa necessarily occurs in every higher domain; `yathāsaṃbhavam` prevents an indiscriminate universalization.

## New Technical Terms

- **adhimātra-saṃprayogitva** — an intense or strong mode of association, given as the reason the five upakleśas are called `svatantra`.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory phrase in this passage.
  - **function:** ground of classification.

- **yathāsaṃbhavam** — “according to applicability,” “wherever possible/applicable.”
  - **status:** previously encountered general exegetical expression; technically important here.
  - **function:** scope restriction preventing universal occurrence across higher domains.

- **akuśala / avyākṛta** — unwholesome / ethically indeterminate.
  - **status:** established vocabulary, but newly coordinated here with upakleśa and domain.
  - **function:** ethical classification.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.52 brings together three independent classificatory dimensions without collapsing them:

```text
1. mode of abandonment
2. independent status
3. ethical value by domain
```

For the five remaining paryavasthānas:

```text
īrṣyā / mātsarya / kaukṛtya / krodha / mrakṣa
    bhāvanā-heya
    svatantra
```

For the six kleśa-malas:

```text
māyā / śāṭhya / mada / pradāśa / upanāha / vihiṃsā
    bhāvanā-heya
    svatantra
```

But ethical status must be indexed to domain:

```text
kāmadhātu
    generally akuśala

styāna / auddhatya / middha in kāmadhātu
    akuśala OR avyākṛta

above kāmadhātu
    occurring upakleśas are avyākṛta
```

Thus one cannot derive ethical value simply from the factor-name.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass translation:

> “In the desire-domain, three are unwholesome; they are twofold, since in the higher domains they are indeterminate.”

mislocates the scope of `kāme 'śubhāḥ` and `trayo dvidhā`.

The Bhāṣya shows that:

1. `kāme 'śubhāḥ` applies generally to the upakleśas under discussion in the desire-domain;
2. `trayo dvidhā` then introduces an exception within that domain: styāna, auddhatya, and middha can be either akuśala or avyākṛta;
3. above kāmadhātu, the applicable upakleśas are avyākṛta.

Preferred translation:

> **The remaining [enveloping upakleśas] are to be abandoned by cultivation and are independent; the stains are likewise. In the desire-domain [these upakleśas] are unwholesome, although three are twofold; above it they are indeterminate.**

## Logical Determination

The strongest lesson of the verse is that the same determination is intersected by multiple relations.

```text
Factor
    ↓
Abandonment relation
    ↓
Independent-status relation
    ↓
Domain relation
    ↓
Ethical valuation
```

None of these axes can substitute for the others.

In particular, `dvidhā` itself demonstrates why context must govern classification:

```text
VAK 5.51:
    dvidhā = two modes of abandonment

VAK 5.52:
    dvidhā = two ethical values
```

The lexical predicate is identical, but its determination is generated by the relation currently under analysis.

A useful formalization is therefore:

```text
value(Factor, Relation, Context)
```

rather than:

```text
Factor → one fixed global label
```

## Organon Contact Point

For the later clarifying pass, VAK 5.52 should be retained primarily as evidence for **multi-axial classification** rather than immediately mapped into a larger speculative schema.

The textual result is already sufficient:

```text
one dharma
    can carry
several non-reducible determinations
    according to
its relation, domain, and path-function
```

This is the point to preserve for later Organon synthesis.
