## Translation

### Literal

**Question:** For what reason are the anuśayas belonging to the form and formless domains taken together and spoken of as one influx of existence?

They are ethically indeterminate, inward-facing, and situated on concentrated planes; therefore they are unified.

For in both [higher domains] they are ethically indeterminate, inward-directed in their activity, and situated on concentrated planes. Thus, through this threefold similarity, they are made one.

For the same reason that a single “craving for existence” was stated, so too is there one “influx of existence.” It is thereby established that ignorance belonging to the three domains is the influx of ignorance. These are fifteen real items.

Why is this [ignorance] established separately?

Because ignorance is the root of all the others; therefore there is a separate influx.

### Philosophical

The form- and formless-domain afflictions are not combined merely because both lie above the desire-domain. They are unified because they possess one operative form: they are ethically indeterminate, turn inward, and function on concentrated planes. The class `bhavāsrava` is therefore produced from a threefold structural commonality.

Ignorance is treated differently. Across all three domains, its fifteen instances form a separate `avidyāsrava`, because ignorance is not merely another member of the afflictive inventory: it is the root-condition of the others.

## Course of Argument

### 1. Why one `bhavāsrava` for two higher domains?

**Voice: Objector / questioner**

The form and formless domains are distinct. Why, then, are their anuśayas gathered together into a single `bhavāsrava`?

### 2. Threefold commonality

**Voice: Vasubandhu reporting the classificatory account**

The answer is not simple spatial or cosmological proximity. The two fields are unified because their afflictions share three determinations:

```text
avyākṛta
    ethically indeterminate

antarmukha-pravṛtta
    inward-directed in operation

samāhita-bhūmika
    belonging to concentrated planes
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly calls this a **threefold similarity**.

Thus:

```text
shared operational determination
    → legitimate classificatory unity
```

### 3. Parallel with `bhavarāga`

The text notes that the same reason used earlier for speaking of one `bhavarāga` supports speaking of one `bhavāsrava`.

The important point is continuity of classificatory principle: a plurality spread across two domains can form one doctrinal category when the relevant mode of operation is common.

### 4. `avidyāsrava` follows

Once ignorance has been removed from `kāmāsrava` and `bhavāsrava`, the remaining tri-domain ignorance constitutes a third influx:

```text
5 ignorance-types × 3 domains = 15 dravyas
```

The Bhāṣya states the total explicitly:

```text
pañcadaśa dravyāṇi
    fifteen real items
```

### 5. Why separate ignorance?

**Voice: Objector / questioner**

Why should ignorance receive its own influx rather than simply being included within desire- and existence-influx?

**Voice: Vasubandhu reporting the answer**

Because:

```text
mūlam avidyā
    ignorance is the root
```

This is the decisive classificatory asymmetry.

Ignorance is separated not because it belongs to a different domain, but because it occupies a different **causal rank**.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.36 supplies two opposed but complementary classificatory operations.

### Unification by shared mode

```text
form-domain afflictions
+
formless-domain afflictions

because both are:
    avyākṛta
    antarmukha
    samāhita-bhūmika

→ one bhavāsrava
```

### Separation by generative priority

```text
avidyā
    present across all three domains
    but root of the other afflictions

→ separate avidyāsrava
```

So the taxonomy is not flat.

```text
taxonomic similarity
    can unify

generative asymmetry
    can require separation
```

This is one of the clearest passages showing that Abhidharma classification is not merely enumeration. It is controlled by determinate principles of grouping.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **trividhena sādharmyeṇa** — “through threefold similarity/commonality.”  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.  
  **Function:** states the formal ground for combining the form- and formless-domain anuśayas into one `bhavāsrava`.

- **antarmukha-pravṛtta** — inward-directed in operation.  
  **Status:** elaborates Kārikā `antarmukha`.  
  **Function:** one of the three shared determinations of higher-domain affliction.

- **avidyāsrava** — influx of ignorance.  
  **Status:** established explicitly in the Bhāṣya from the exclusions of 5.35 and the Kārikā statement `mūlam avidyā`.  
  **Function:** gathers the fifteen tri-domain ignorance-items into a separate influx.

- **pañcadaśa dravyāṇi** — fifteen real items.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya count absent from the Kārikā.  
  **Function:** fixes the extension of `avidyāsrava`.

---

## Reflective Vocabulary

The Bhāṣya performs two reflective operations on the same material:

```text
Identity through shared determinations
    → form + formless affliction = one bhavāsrava

Difference through causal rank
    → avidyā separated from the rest
```

This is not contradiction. The relevant ground of reflection changes.

Under the ground **mode of operation**, two domains are unified.

Under the ground **roothood**, ignorance is separated.

Hence the same inventory admits different valid partitions according to the determination being asked about.

---

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a precise classificatory logic:

```text
if A and B share the determinations relevant to classification C,
then A and B may be unified under C.

if X is the generative root of the other members,
then X may require a distinct class despite co-occurring with them.
```

In compact form:

```text
shared structure
    → synthesis

generative priority
    → differentiation
```

This is stronger than a simple class hierarchy. It is a rule for **how classes are produced from explanatory grounds**.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The existing translation correctly captures the two principal claims. The Bhāṣya adds two points that should accompany it in study:

1. the unity of the higher-domain influx rests explicitly on a **threefold similarity**;
2. `avidyāsrava` contains **fifteen dravyas**—the five ignorance-types across each of the three domains.

No revision of the core Kārikā translation is required.

---

## Organon Contact Point

The useful contact here is methodological rather than doctrinal.

A rational classifier needs at least two distinct operations:

```text
clusterBySharedDetermination
identifyGenerativeRoot
```

The first produces a class from common predicates. The second recognizes an asymmetric grounding relation and prevents the ground from being flattened into an ordinary class-member.

Thus:

```text
same extension-space
    can support
multiple valid partitions
    under different explanatory grounds
```

That is directly relevant to a reasoning system in which classification must remain answerable to the principle under which the classification was generated.
