# VAK 5.68 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.68**

> anāsrava-viyogāpter bhavāgra-vikalīkṛteḥ /
> hetu-dvaya-samudghātāt parijñā dhātv-atikramāt // 5.68 //

**Translation:** An abandonment receives the designation *parijñā* through acquisition of uncontaminated disconnection, through rendering the summit of existence ineffective, through eradication of the two causes, and through transcendence of a domain.

Why is not each and every abandonment established as a *parijñā*?

> yasmāt kṣāntiphalaṃ tāvat prahāṇaṃ vyavasthāpyate /

First, an abandonment that is the fruit of a *kṣānti* is so established [only under specified conditions].

Where these three causes are present, that abandonment is called a *parijñā*.

[324|18-325|01]

For an ordinary person, there is neither an uncontaminated acquisition of disconnection nor a rendering ineffective of the summit of existence; therefore that person's abandonment does not receive the designation *parijñā*.

Even for an ārya, regarding abandonment that is the fruit of *kṣānti*: up through the *anvayajñānakṣānti* concerning suffering there is uncontaminated acquisition of disconnection, but the summit of existence has not yet been rendered ineffective.

[325|02-325|03]

> duḥkhe 'nvayajñāne ubhayamasti na tu hetudvayasamudghātaḥ /

At the *anvayajñāna* concerning suffering, both of these are present, but there is not yet eradication of the two causes.

[325|03]

> samudayadarśanaprahātavyasya sarvatragahetoraprahīṇatvāt /

Because the universal cause belonging to what is to be abandoned through seeing Origin has not yet been abandoned.

> anyeṣu dharmānvayajñāneṣu sarvaṃ trayamasti /
> atastāsvavasthāsu prahāṇaṃ parijñākhyāṃ labhate /

In the subsequent *dharmajñānas* and *anvayajñānas*, all three are present. Therefore in those states the abandonment receives the designation *parijñā*.

[325|05-325|07]

> jñānaphalaṃ tu prahāṇam /
> ataśca kāraṇatrayāt parijñākhyāṃ labhate /
> caturthācca
> dhātvatikramāt // VAkK_5.68 //

An abandonment that is the fruit of *jñāna* also receives the designation *parijñā* from these three causes, and from a fourth as well: transcendence of a domain.

[325|08]

> yadā dhātuṃ samatikrāmati /

That is, when one completely passes beyond a domain.

[325|08-325|11]

> kṛtsnadhātuverāgyāt ubhayasaṃyogaviyogaṃ pañcamaṃ kāraṇamāhurapare /
> yaḥ prakāraḥ prahīṇo yadi tatrānyena tadālambanena kleśena visaṃyukto bhavatīti /
> sa tu nāsya ubhayahetusamudghātāt dhātusamatikramācceti na brūmaḥ /

Others maintain a fifth cause: because of complete dispassion toward an entire domain, there is disconnection from both conjunctions—if a type has been abandoned, one is also disconnected there from another affliction taking that as its object. We do not state this separately, however, because for us it is already included under eradication of the two causes and transcendence of the domain.

## Translation and Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / explanatory Abhidharma analysis**

The question is precise: if *parijñā* is an abandonment, why is every individual abandonment not called a *parijñā*?

The answer is that the designation marks a **completed configuration of abandonment**, not mere destruction of one afflictive item.

For abandonments that are fruits of *kṣānti*, three conditions must coincide:

```text
1. anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti
   acquisition of uncontaminated disconnection

2. bhavāgra-vikalīkaraṇa
   rendering the summit of existence ineffective

3. hetu-dvaya-samudghāta
   eradication of the two causes
```

Only where all three are present does the abandonment receive the name *parijñā*.

For abandonments that are fruits of *jñāna*, these same three conditions apply, and the text adds a fourth when appropriate:

```text
4. dhātu-atikrama
   complete transcendence of a domain
```

Thus the four clauses of the Kārikā are not four synonyms and are not four universally simultaneous requirements for every single parijñā. They specify the conditions under which different classes of completed abandonment receive the technical designation.

---

## Why Early Abandonments Do Not Yet Count

The Bhāṣya tests the definition against successive stages of the path.

### Ordinary person

A *pṛthagjana* may possess worldly abandonment, but lacks:

```text
anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti
bhavāgra-vikalīkaraṇa
```

Therefore such abandonment is not called *parijñā*.

### Through duḥkha-anvayajñāna-kṣānti

There is already:

```text
anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti
```

but not yet:

```text
bhavāgra-vikalīkaraṇa
```

So the three-condition threshold has not been reached.

### At duḥkha-anvayajñāna

Now both are present:

```text
anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti
bhavāgra-vikalīkaraṇa
```

but not yet:

```text
hetu-dvaya-samudghāta
```

Why? Because the *sarvatraga-hetu* belonging to afflictions abandoned through seeing Origin remains unabandoned.

### In the subsequent dharma- and anvaya-jñānas

All three conditions are present, and therefore the relevant abandonments receive the designation *parijñā*.

This explains why the first parijñā of VAK 5.64 groups together the abandonments through seeing **suffering and origin**. The Bhāṣya now gives the missing reason: abandonment through seeing suffering alone has not yet eradicated the universal cause belonging to the Origin-class.

---

## The Two Causes

The passage does not give a fresh lexical pair immediately after *hetu-dvaya*, but its explanation makes the structure clear enough to avoid guessing beyond the source.

One of the two causal dimensions explicitly involves the remaining *sarvatraga-hetu* associated with the afflictions abandoned through seeing Origin. The later reference to *ubhayahetusamudghāta* confirms that complete parijñā requires the relevant twofold causal basis to have been eradicated.

The source passage does **not** here independently enumerate the pair by two separate names. That question should therefore remain open for cross-checking elsewhere rather than being silently supplied.

## Alternative Five-Condition Theory

**Voice: Other teachers (`apare`)**

Some propose a fifth condition:

```text
ubhaya-saṃyoga-viyoga
    disconnection from both conjunctions
```

This occurs through complete dispassion toward an entire domain: when one type has been abandoned, one is also disconnected from another affliction that takes it as object.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Vasubandhu does not deny the phenomenon. He denies that it must be counted as an independent fifth condition:

```text
ubhaya-saṃyoga-viyoga
    is already covered by
hetu-dvaya-samudghāta
+
dhātu-samatikrama
```

So this is another example of the Kośa distinguishing:

```text
real doctrinal feature
    ≠
independent classificatory member
```

---

## New Technical Terms

- **anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti** — uncontaminated acquisition of disconnection. **Status:** encountered in closely related form earlier. **Function:** first qualification for *parijñā*.
- **bhavāgra-vikalīkaraṇa** — rendering the summit of existence ineffective. **Status:** new formulation. **Function:** second qualification for *parijñā*; marks counteractive reach to the highest conditioned level.
- **hetu-dvaya-samudghāta** — eradication of the two causes. **Status:** new. **Function:** third qualification for *parijñā*.
- **sarvatraga-hetu** — universal cause. **Status:** previously encountered doctrinal category. **Function:** explains why the suffering-stage alone has not yet completed the causal eradication.
- **dhātu-samatikrama / dhātv-atikrama** — complete passage beyond a domain. **Status:** new in this function. **Function:** fourth condition for *jñāna*-fruit parijñā.
- **ubhaya-saṃyoga-viyoga** — disconnection from both conjunctions. **Status:** new. **Function:** proposed fifth condition by other teachers; rejected as independently necessary.

---

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only, with an important structural correction.**

The Kārikā should not be read as saying that every parijñā satisfies four identical conditions simultaneously. The Bhāṣya distinguishes:

```text
kṣānti-phala abandonment
    → first three conditions

jñāna-phala abandonment
    → first three
    + dhātu-atikrama when a domain is transcended
```

The phrase “because of the eradication of the two causes” should remain literal until the two causes are explicitly identified elsewhere in the source.

---

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.68 gives a **threshold criterion for technical designation**.

```text
Prahāṇa
    does not automatically imply
Parijñā
```

Rather:

```text
abandonment
+ required completion conditions
    → parijñā designation
```

The stage-by-stage test is especially important:

```text
condition 1 only
    insufficient

conditions 1 + 2
    still insufficient

conditions 1 + 2 + 3
    parijñā designation becomes valid
```

For the later *jñāna*-fruit completions, transcendence of a whole domain supplies an additional determination.

The classification is therefore cumulative but not merely numerical: each condition answers a different question about whether the abandonment is structurally complete.

---

## Organon Contact Point

Minimal contact point:

```text
local negation
    ≠
qualified completion
```

A system should distinguish the occurrence of a removal from the conditions that license a stronger completion-status. VAK 5.68 is particularly useful because Vasubandhu tests that status against successive path states rather than defining it by verbal abstraction alone.
