## Literal Prose Translation

Those anuśayas by which a citta is `sānuśaya` may or may not themselves be lying latent in that citta.

They do lie latent when they are conjoined with that citta and are unabandoned, or when they take that citta as object and are unabandoned. They may fail to lie latent when they are conjoined with that citta but abandoned, or when they take it as object [under the corresponding abandoned condition].

Therefore a defiled citta is `sānuśaya` in two ways: through anuśayas that actually lie latent—those unabandoned anuśayas conjoined with it or taking it as object—and through anuśayas that do not lie latent but are nevertheless conjoined with it, because the citta is accompanied by them. A non-defiled citta, however, is `sānuśaya` only through anuśayas that actually lie latent, namely unabandoned anuśayas that take it as object.

Then: how do these ten anuśayas arise in sequence?

At the outset, through conjunction with ignorance, one is deluded concerning the truths. Suffering, and so on through the path, does not appeal to him. From delusion comes doubt. When one who is deluded hears the two alternatives, doubt arises: “Is this suffering, or is this not suffering?” and similarly in the other cases.

From doubt, false view proceeds. In one who doubts, false ascertainment arises through the minds or teachings of false ascetics: “There is no suffering,” and so forth.

From false view, it is said, satkāyadṛṣṭi proceeds, because having excluded the aggregates as suffering, one fixates upon them as self.

## Philosophical Translation

The Bhāṣya distinguishes the mere fact that cognition is accompanied by an anuśaya from the stricter fact that an anuśaya is presently operative as a latent bond. A defiled cognition may therefore be called `sānuśaya` in two senses: because an unabandoned anuśaya actually inheres in the relevant conjunction or object-relation, and because the cognition is directly accompanied by an anuśaya even where the strict latent relation is not being asserted. A non-defiled cognition admits only the first, object-conditioned sense: it may remain within the range of an unabandoned anuśaya without being itself conjoined with that affliction.

The text then changes from relational classification to genesis. Ignorance first obscures the four truths. Once the field is not correctly determined, opposed alternatives appear and doubt arises. From doubt comes false determination; and from false determination comes the stabilization of error as self-view. The sequence is therefore not an arbitrary list of afflictions but a movement from non-comprehension to divided cognition, from division to false judgment, and from false judgment to identification.

## Course of Argument

### 1. A question left by the object-matrix

Verses 5.29–5.31 determined which cittas can take which dharmas as objects. The Bhāṣya now asks a different question:

```text
If a citta is called sānuśaya,
must the anuśaya actually be anuśayāna in it?
```

The answer is no.

### 2. `sānuśaya` and `anuśayana` are not coextensive

The Bhāṣya explicitly distinguishes:

```text
sānuśaya
    citta characterized by relation to anuśaya

anuśayāna
    anuśaya actually lying latent / adhering
```

An anuśaya lies latent where it is:

```text
unabandoned
and
conjoined with the citta

or

unabandoned
and
takes the citta as object
```

But a citta can still be called `sānuśaya` through direct accompaniment even when the stricter `anuśayana` relation is not being predicated.

### 3. Why defiled citta is `sānuśaya` in two ways

For `kliṣṭa-citta` there are two grounds:

```text
A. anuśayana
   an unabandoned anuśaya actually lies latent
   through conjunction or object-relation

B. sahitatva
   the citta is accompanied by an anuśaya
   and is therefore sānuśaya through conjunction
```

The key word in the prose is `tatsahitatvāt`—because it is accompanied by it.

Thus the category `sānuśaya` is broader than the single relation `anuśayana`.

### 4. Why non-defiled citta has only one mode

For `akliṣṭa-citta` the Bhāṣya permits only:

```text
unabandoned anuśaya
    taking that citta as object
    → anuśayana
    → citta called sānuśaya
```

It is not itself conjoined with the affliction. This is precisely why `akliṣṭa` should not here be overtranslated as though it meant that every relation to affliction had disappeared.

### 5. Transition from structure to genesis

The Bhāṣya then asks:

```text
athaiṣāṃ daśānāmanuśayānāṃ kathaṃ pravṛttiḥ?

How does the arising / operation of these ten anuśayas proceed?
```

This opens a causal or genetic sequence rather than another static classification.

### 6. Ignorance → doubt

The beginning is `avidyā`:

```text
avidyā
    → confusion regarding the truths
    → inability to settle their determination
```

When the deluded person then hears two opposing positions:

```text
Is this suffering?
Is this not suffering?
```

`vicikitsā` arises.

The kārikā's `ākāṅkṣā` is therefore glossed by the Bhāṣya as `vicikitsā` in this passage.

### 7. Doubt → false view

Doubt does not yet affirm the false alternative. But in the doubting person a `mithyāniścaya`, a false ascertainment, can arise under the influence of false ascetic teachings:

```text
nāsti duḥkham
    “there is no suffering”
```

Thus:

```text
vicikitsā
    suspended between alternatives

mithyādṛṣṭi
    false alternative determinately affirmed
```

### 8. False view → satkāyadṛṣṭi

Once suffering is denied of the aggregates, the aggregates can be appropriated as self:

```text
duḥkhataḥ skandhān apohya
    excluding the aggregates from the determination “suffering”

ātmato 'bhiniveśa
    fixation upon them as self
```

The error has now become identity-forming.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Bhāṣya exposition** — the distinction between `sānuśaya` and actual `anuśayana`, and the sequence beginning from ignorance.

**Voice: scholastic report / uncertain nuance** — `mithyādṛṣṭeḥ kila satkāyadṛṣṭiḥ pravartate`; the particle `kila` gives the final transition a reported or received-doctrine flavor rather than an unmarked assertion.

**Voice: false ascetic doctrine** — `nāsti duḥkham`, “there is no suffering,” offered as the content of false ascertainment.

## New Technical Terms

- **anuśayāna** — an anuśaya actually lying latent or adhering in the relevant relation.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya refinement of the anuśaya vocabulary.  
  **Function:** distinguishes actual latent adherence from the broader predicate `sānuśaya`.

- **ananuśayāna** — not presently qualifying through the strict latent-adherence relation.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only contrast in this passage.  
  **Function:** shows that a citta may still be `sānuśaya` through conjunction/accompaniment.

- **sahitatva** — being accompanied by, being together with.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.  
  **Function:** supplies the second ground on which a defiled citta is called `sānuśaya`.

- **mithyāniścaya** — false ascertainment or false determination.  
  **Status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here.  
  **Function:** mediates the transition from doubt to false view.

- **abhiniveśa** — fixation, entrenched appropriation.  
  **Status:** previously encountered Buddhist technical vocabulary, used here with special precision.  
  **Function:** explains how false view stabilizes into self-view.

## Doctrinal Determination

The first half establishes a strict asymmetry:

```text
kliṣṭa-citta
    may be sānuśaya by anuśayana
    or by direct accompaniment

akliṣṭa-citta
    may be sānuśaya only by anuśayana
    through an unabandoned object-relation
```

Therefore:

```text
sānuśaya ≠ kliṣṭa
```

and also:

```text
sānuśaya ≠ “anuśaya is presently lying latent”
```

The second half establishes the beginning of a genetic ordering of the ten anuśayas:

```text
avidyā
    ↓
moha regarding the truths
    ↓
vicikitsā
    ↓
mithyāniścaya / mithyādṛṣṭi
    ↓
satkāyadṛṣṭi
```

This sequence is more precise than the first-pass suggestion that delusion simply “causes” a list of later afflictions. The Bhāṣya supplies distinct mediating acts at each transition.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and partial revision recommended.**

The first-pass translation treated `akliṣṭa` too readily as “undefiled.” Here the Bhāṣya requires the broader and safer **“non-defiled”**: a citta may be `akliṣṭa` yet remain `sānuśaya` because an unabandoned anuśaya takes it as object.

Likewise, `anuśāyakaiḥ` should be rendered in a way that preserves the active sense supplied by the prose: **“through anuśayas that actually lie latent / adhere.”**

The second half is confirmed, with one important lexical clarification:

```text
ākāṅkṣā
    is glossed here as vicikitsā
```

## Logical Determination

The first half is a lesson against predicate collapse.

```text
Citta is sānuśaya
```

does not license the inference:

```text
therefore the anuśaya is presently anuśayāna in exactly one way
```

Instead the Bhāṣya differentiates the ground of attribution:

```text
sānuśaya(citta)
    because latent adherence

or

sānuśaya(citta)
    because conjunction / accompaniment
```

This is a reflective determination of a predicate by its ground.

The second half then supplies a genuine sequence of mediation:

```text
non-comprehension
    → divided alternative
    → false ascertainment
    → identity fixation
```

The especially important middle term is `mithyāniścaya`. Doubt does not mechanically transform into false view; rather, a false determination resolves the divided field incorrectly. Only then can the erroneous result stabilize into `satkāyadṛṣṭi`.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The passage offers two useful constraints for later assimilation.

First:

```text
one surface predicate
    may have multiple grounds
```

A reasoning system should preserve whether a state is marked because of direct conjunction, latent object-relation, or some other relation. Collapsing these into one `hasAffliction` property would destroy the Bhāṣya's distinction.

Second, the error sequence is not:

```text
ignorance → false belief
```

but:

```text
ignorance
    → unresolved opposition
    → false ascertainment
    → stabilized identity claim
```

That is a much stronger model of how error becomes systematic. The important event is not simply lack of knowledge but the **wrong resolution of a divided field**.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.32 marks another methodological transition. The `piṇḍavibhāṣā` object-matrix is no longer merely being extended; Vasubandhu now asks how the anuśaya relation itself is predicated of citta and then turns from structure to the **genesis of the ten anuśayas**.

The strongest results are:

1. `sānuśaya` and actual `anuśayana` are not identical.
2. Defiled citta can be `sānuśaya` in two ways; non-defiled citta only through actual latent object-relation.
3. `ākāṅkṣā` is concretely glossed as `vicikitsā`.
4. The transition from doubt to false view is mediated by `mithyāniścaya`, false ascertainment.
5. Self-view emerges only after false determination has first removed the aggregates from their correct determination as suffering.
