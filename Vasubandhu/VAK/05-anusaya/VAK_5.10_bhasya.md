## Bhāṣya Translation and Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya asks whether only `dṛṣṭi` has internal differentiation. It answers that `māna` also does.

`Māna` is defined generally as **elevation of citta** (`cittasya unnati`). According to differences in operation (`pravṛttibheda`), it becomes sevenfold:

1. `māna`
2. `atimāna`
3. `mānātimāna`
4. `asmimāna`
5. `abhimāna`
6. `ūnamāna`
7. `mithyāmāna`

Their local meanings are:

- `māna`: taking oneself as superior to an inferior or equal to an equal.
- `atimāna`: taking oneself as superior to an equal.
- `mānātimāna`: taking oneself as superior to one who is superior.
- `asmimāna`: taking the five aggregates of appropriation as self or belonging to self.
- `abhimāna`: taking an unattained distinction as attained.
- `ūnamāna`: taking oneself as only slightly inferior to one who is greatly superior.
- `mithyāmāna`: taking oneself as possessing excellence when excellence is absent.

The Kārikā's phrase `navavidhās tribhyaḥ` is then resolved. The nine conventional modes arise from exactly three conceits:

```text
mānātimāna
ūnamāna
māna
```

Thus the ninefold scheme is secondary to the sevenfold analysis rather than an independent taxonomy.

The Bhāṣya next glosses `dṛg-bhāvanā-kṣayāḥ`: the seven conceits are exhausted through the two established Path modes, seeing and cultivation. Their determinate occurrences are distributed accordingly.

The closing discussion adds an important distinction: something may remain classed as cultivation-abandonable without necessarily being presently manifest in an ārya.

```text
not yet abandoned
    ≠
presently manifest
```

## New Technical Terms

- **cittasya unnati** — elevation of citta
  - **status:** Bhāṣya definition
  - **function:** generic definition of `māna`

- **pravṛttibheda** — difference in operation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** explains sevenfold differentiation

- **samudācāra** — manifestation / active occurrence
  - **status:** technically important in the closing discussion
  - **function:** distinguishes actual occurrence from remaining un-abandoned

## Doctrinal Determination

`Māna` is a relational affliction of self-estimation. The sevenfold classification gives qualitative forms; the ninefold classification is generated from three named conceits.

The Bhāṣya also distinguishes structural status in the abandonment-system from actual manifestation.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and revision.**

The three generating conceits are `mānātimāna`, `ūnamāna`, and `māna`. The phrase concerning exhaustion through seeing and cultivation is distributive across determinate occurrences.

## Logical Determination

```text
self-position

```

The chapter has moved from false view and inversion into malformed comparative self-measurement.

## Light Organon Contact Point

Comparison can be formally organized while still materially distorted because the measuring standpoint itself is afflicted. The stronger textual lesson is that persistence in a classification does not entail present manifestation.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

> **Māna is elevation of citta and becomes sevenfold according to differences in operation. A secondary ninefold classification is generated from māna, mānātimāna, and ūnamāna. These forms are distributed between abandonment by seeing and abandonment by cultivation, while remaining un-abandoned does not necessarily mean presently manifest.**
