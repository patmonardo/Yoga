# VAK 5.44 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.44**

```text
agantukāmatā mārgavibhramo mārgasaṃśayaḥ /
```

**Translation:** Unwillingness to go, deviation from the path, and doubt concerning the path—these are obstacles in going toward liberation; therefore there is the teaching of three.

## Translation

**Voice: uncertain / reported alternative explanation**

Others, however, say:

“Unwillingness to go, deviation from the path, and doubt concerning the path—these are obstacles in going toward liberation; therefore there is the teaching of three.”

There are three obstacles when one travels to another region: unwillingness to go, deviation from the road through resorting to another road, and doubt concerning the road.

In the same way, these very three are obstacles in going toward liberation.

There, through satkāyadṛṣṭi, one who has become frightened by liberation has unwillingness to go.

Through grasping at rules and observances, because one resorts to another path, there is deviation from liberation.

Through doubt there is doubt concerning the path.

In order to make evident the abandonment of these obstacles to going toward liberation, the Blessed One taught the abandonment specifically of these three afflictions.

## Course of Argument

### 1. This is explicitly an alternative explanation

The decisive Bhāṣya marker is:

```text
apare punar āhuḥ
    others, however, say
```

VAK 5.43 had already supplied one explanation for why scripture singles out the triad through `mukha-mūla-graha`, grasping the entrance and roots. VAK 5.44 introduces a second explanation rather than simply continuing the first.

The Bhāṣya does not here explicitly tell us that the second explanation supersedes the first.

### 2. The analogy begins from ordinary travel

The prose establishes a mundane model: `deśāntara-gamana`, travel to another region. Three things obstruct successful travel:

```text
agantukāmatā
    unwillingness to go

mārgavibhrama
    deviation from the route through taking another route

mārgasaṃśaya
    doubt concerning the route
```

The same structure is then transferred directly to `mokṣa-gamana`, going toward liberation.

### 3. Satkāyadṛṣṭi produces unwillingness to go

The Bhāṣya states that through satkāyadṛṣṭi, one becomes frightened by liberation and therefore unwilling to go toward it:

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
    ↓
mokṣād utrāsa
    ↓
agantukāmatā
```

This sharpens the Kārikā-pass reading. The refusal is specifically mediated by fear of liberation.

### 4. Śīlavrataparāmarśa produces deviation through another path

The second relation is explicit:

```text
śīlavrataparāmarśa
    ↓
anyamārga-saṃśrayaṇa
    ↓
mokṣa-vibhrama
```

Thus `mārgavibhrama` is not merely confusion in the abstract. It is deviation generated by positively adopting another path.

The first-pass formulation that a subordinate practice is mistaken for the complete path was more specific than this prose warrants; the source says only `anyamārga`, another path.

### 5. Vicikitsā is direct doubt concerning the path

The third mapping is straightforward:

```text
vicikitsā
    →
mārga-saṃśaya
```

The Bhāṣya does not distinguish whether the doubt concerns the path's existence, efficacy, or one's own capacity to follow it.

### 6. Why precisely these three are taught

The final sentence gives the pedagogical function of the enumeration: the Blessed One teaches the abandonment of these three in order to make evident the abandonment of the obstacles to going toward liberation.

The three are therefore selected because they form a complete model of obstruction to directed liberative movement.

## New Technical Terms

- **deśāntara-gamana** — going to another region.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only example vocabulary in this passage.
  - **function:** worldly analogy for liberative movement.

- **anyamārga-saṃśrayaṇa** — resorting to or adopting another path.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary.
  - **function:** determines `mārgavibhrama` as positive misdirection.

- **mokṣād-utrāsa** — fright or alarm at liberation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory formulation.
  - **function:** explains how satkāyadṛṣṭi generates unwillingness to proceed.

- **mokṣagamana-antarāya** — obstacle to going toward liberation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya synthesis of the Kārikā terms.
  - **function:** establishes the common genus under which the three afflictions are selected.

- **dyotayan** — making evident, illuminating, indicating.
  - **status:** explanatory Bhāṣya vocabulary.
  - **function:** states the pedagogical purpose of the threefold teaching.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.44 should be read as a **reported alternative account** of the special threefold teaching.

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
    → liberation appears frightening
    → unwillingness to go

śīlavrataparāmarśa
    → another path is adopted
    → deviation from liberation

vicikitsā
    → path remains doubtful
    → movement is suspended
```

This confirms the Kārikā pass at the level of the threefold mapping while correcting two points:

1. the explanation is not presented simply as Vasubandhu's own unqualified doctrine;
2. `mārgavibhrama` is specifically tied to `anyamārga-saṃśrayaṇa`, resorting to another path.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

The existing literal translation is good:

> Unwillingness to go, deviation from the path, and doubt concerning the path—these are obstacles in going toward liberation; therefore there is the teaching of three.

The philosophical gloss should, however, preserve the Bhāṣya's voice status:

> According to an alternative explanation recorded by the Bhāṣya, the three are singled out because they correspond to the three fundamental obstacles to directed travel: refusal to depart, taking the wrong route, and uncertainty about the route. The same structure governs movement toward liberation.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya yields a processual action structure:

```text
1. orientation toward departure
2. route-selection
3. sufficient certainty regarding the route
```

Failure occurs at three non-equivalent positions:

```text
failure of departure
failure of routing
failure of determination
```

The afflictions are distinguished by **where they interrupt one mediated movement** rather than by three isolated psychological contents.

## Organon Contact Point

The passage supplies a minimal practical-reason pattern:

```text
Destination
    mokṣa

Directed Process
    mokṣa-gamana

Required Mediation
    willingness to depart
    correct route
    determinate orientation
```

The associated failures are:

```text
Refusal
Misdirection
Suspension
```

This is valuable because the determinations gain their precise meaning only within the living path-relation. Unwillingness, deviation, and doubt are not complete atoms later compared; they are distinguished by different interruptions of the same directed process.

The Organon development should remain explicitly subordinate to the source: the Bhāṣya reports an alternative travel analogy; the more general action architecture is our reconstruction.
