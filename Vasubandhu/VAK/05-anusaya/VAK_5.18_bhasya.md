# VAK_5.18 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> nānāsravordhvaviṣayāḥ asvīkārād vipakṣataḥ |
> yena yaḥ saṃprayuktas tu sa tasmin saṃprayogataḥ || 5.18 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Those whose objects are uncontaminated or higher do not adhere there by way of object, because those objects are not appropriated and because they stand as counteragents. But whichever anuśaya is conjoined with a dharma adheres to that dharma through conjunction.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Having stated the general rule, he now gives the exception:

> Those whose objects are uncontaminated or higher—

Anuśayas whose cognitive supports are uncontaminated do not adhere there by way of object; nor do those whose supports are higher levels.

Why? Because the object that serves as their support is not appropriated, and because it stands as a counteragent.

For when a thing has been appropriated through self-view and craving, the other anuśayas too are capable of following along with it, just as dust can settle on a damp cloth. Uncontaminated dharmas and a higher level are not like this. Therefore anuśayas that take them as objects do not adhere in them.

If someone dwelling here aspires to that higher level, this is a wholesome aspiration toward a dharma. Nirvāṇa and the Path stand as counteragents to the afflictions that take them as objects, and a higher level stands as counteragent to the afflictions of a lower level. Therefore those afflictions gain no footing in these objects, just as the soles of the feet gain no footing on a heated stone.

Others say that here `anuśaya` has the sense of conformity or congeniality: those objects are not congenial to the afflictions, just as a dry condition is not congenial to a vāta disorder.

Therefore it was said that anuśayas adhere by way of object.

> But whichever anuśaya is conjoined with a given dharma, it adheres to that dharma through conjunction.

The verb `anuśerate` continues here. Whichever anuśaya is conjoined with a dharma adheres to that dharma through conjunction. The particle `tu` marks the qualification: this holds only so long as the anuśaya has not been abandoned.

## Course of the Argument

VAK 5.17 gave the general rule for objective adherence:

```text
sarvatraga
    entire own bhūmi

asarvatraga
    own nikāya
```

VAK 5.18 immediately restricts that rule.

```text
object-reference
    does not automatically imply
anuśayana
```

Two major exceptions are named:

```text
anāsrava-ālambana
    uncontaminated cognitive support

ūrdhvabhūmi-ālambana
    higher-level cognitive support
```

The reason is given through two concepts:

```text
asvīkāra
    non-appropriation

vipakṣa
    counteragent / opposed condition
```

An affliction can adhere through its object only where the object is capable of being taken into the afflicted structure. Vasubandhu explains this by the role of self-view and craving:

```text
ātma-dṛṣṭi + tṛṣṇā
    ↓ appropriate an object
other anuśayas
    ↓ can follow and settle there
```

The damp-cloth analogy makes the point concrete: appropriation provides the condition in which further affliction can adhere.

Uncontaminated dharmas and superior levels are different. They are not appropriated in this way, and they stand against the relevant afflictions.

The second half then introduces a second relation altogether:

```text
ālambanataḥ anuśayana
    adherence by way of object

saṃprayogataḥ anuśayana
    adherence by conjunction
```

This is not the same relation. A dharma may not be the object in which an affliction settles, yet if the affliction and dharma arise in conjunction, the affliction adheres to that dharma through the conjunction itself.

The `tu` is important: this relation lasts only `yāvad aprahīṇa`, so long as the anuśaya has not yet been abandoned.

## New Technical Terms

- **asvīkāra** — non-appropriation; failure of the object to be taken into the afflicted structure
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit explanation of the Kārikā
  - **function:** explains why uncontaminated and higher objects do not support objective adherence

- **vipakṣa** — counteragent, opposed condition
  - **status:** previously encountered concept, technically decisive here
  - **function:** explains why nirvāṇa, path, and higher levels do not provide footing for the afflictions directed toward them

- **svīkṛta** — appropriated, taken up
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** marks the positive condition under which other anuśayas can follow and settle

- **dharmacchanda** — wholesome aspiration toward a dharma
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** distinguishes aspiration toward a higher level from afflicted attachment

- **pratiṣṭhā** — footing, establishment
  - **status:** Bhāṣya metaphorical-technical use
  - **function:** describes whether an affliction can become established in an object

- **ānuguṇya** — conformity, congeniality, compatibility
  - **status:** alternative Bhāṣya interpretation
  - **function:** proposed explanation of `anuśaya` by other teachers

- **yāvad aprahīṇa** — so long as not abandoned
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification
  - **function:** limits adherence by conjunction

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.18 completes the distinction opened in 5.17.

There are two distinct modes of `anuśayana`:

```text
1. ālambanataḥ
   by cognitive support

2. saṃprayogataḥ
   by conjunction
```

Objective adherence requires more than simple intentional reference. The object must be compatible with afflictive appropriation.

```text
object can be cognized
    but
not appropriated
    therefore
no objective adherence
```

This explains the apparently paradoxical result already established in 5.14–5.16:

```text
an affliction can take nirvāṇa or mārga as object
    yet
not adhere in nirvāṇa or mārga
```

The distinction is between:

```text
having X as cognitive support
and
finding afflictive footing in X
```

The Bhāṣya therefore gives `anuśayana` a stronger force than mere object-directed cognition.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification and tightening required.**

The first-pass translation correctly distinguished object-adherence from conjunction, but it left `asvīkāra` somewhat general.

A better canonical translation is:

> Anuśayas whose objects are uncontaminated or higher do not adhere in those objects, because those objects are not appropriated and stand as counteragents. But whichever anuśaya is conjoined with a dharma adheres to that dharma through conjunction, so long as that anuśaya has not been abandoned.

This captures the Bhāṣya's explicit qualification `yāvad aprahīṇa`.

---

## Logical Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya distinguish three relations that must not be collapsed:

```text
cognitive reference
    X is taken as ālambana

appropriation
    X is taken into an afflicted structure

conjunction
    affliction and dharma arise together
```

Only the second grounds objective `anuśayana`; the third grounds conjunctive `anuśayana`.

Thus:

```text
reference ≠ appropriation ≠ conjunction
```

This is a major refinement of the chapter's relational ontology of affliction.

The strongest result is:

```text
an affliction may know an opposed object
without being able to establish itself in that object
```

That distinction prevents the mistake of treating every intentional relation as an inherence relation.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The passage supplies a clean distinction between:

```text
reference
    relation toward an object

compatibility / appropriation
    condition for internal uptake

conjunction
    co-constitution within one event
```

For a later Rational Kośa, this is more useful than a loose notion of “association.” A system should distinguish:

```text
what a state represents
what it can incorporate
what it is structurally conjoined with
```

The textual priority remains Vasubandhu's distinction between `ālambanataḥ` and `saṃprayogataḥ` anuśayana.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.18 completes the scope analysis begun in 5.17:

> **Anuśaya is not mere object-reference. Objective adherence requires an appropriable and affliction-compatible support. Uncontaminated dharmas and higher levels can be cognized by affliction yet remain opposed to it, so the affliction gains no footing there. By contrast, conjunction is an internal relation: an unabandoned anuśaya adheres to the dharmas with which it is conjoined simply through that conjunction.**

This gives the chapter a precise two-relation theory of afflictive adherence and closes the opening scope discussion cleanly.
