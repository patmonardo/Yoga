# VAK_5.07 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> ātmātmīyadhruvocchedanāstihīnā gradṛṣṭayaḥ |
> ahetvamārge taddṛṣṭir etās tāḥ pañca dṛṣṭayaḥ || 5.7 ||

**Best current translation:**  
The five views are: the view of self or what belongs to self; the view of permanence or annihilation; the view that what exists does not exist; the view that what is inferior is supreme; and the view that what is not a cause or path is a cause or path.

The final question opens the argument of VAK 5.08 and is preserved here as the natural bridge: if śīlavrataparāmarśa can concern a true causal matter, why is it not abandoned through seeing origination?

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The five views have been indicated by name, but not according to their intrinsic character. What, then, is their intrinsic character?

> The views concerning self and what belongs to self; permanence and annihilation; nonexistence; what is inferior as highest; and the view of what is not a cause or path as cause or path—these are those five views.

The view of self or the view of what belongs to self is satkāyadṛṣṭi. `Sat` is explained from "to sink / perish," and `kāya` as accumulation, collection, aggregate. Thus `satkāya` means the five aggregates of appropriation. This expression is employed so as to make one relinquish the notions of permanence and compact unity, for grasping them as self has these notions as its antecedent. A view concerning satkāya is satkāyadṛṣṭi.

Every view whose cognitive support is sāsrava is in this sense a view concerning satkāya; nevertheless only the view of self and what belongs to self is called satkāyadṛṣṭi, so that it may be understood that this view concerns neither a real self nor something really belonging to a self. As stated: whatever ascetics or brahmins regard as self, they regard precisely these five aggregates of appropriation.

Regarding that very thing conceived as self, the view that it is permanent or the view that it is annihilated is antagrāhadṛṣṭi, because it grasps the extremes of eternalism and annihilationism.

The view "it does not exist" with regard to the truths such as suffering, when they do exist, is mithyādṛṣṭi. In a broad sense every view proceeding with an inverted nature is false view, but this one alone receives the name because of its predominance, as in the case of an especially foul wound. This view is denying or canceling (`apavādikā`), whereas the others are superimposing (`samāropikāḥ`).

The view that what is inferior is highest is dṛṣṭiparāmarśa. What is inferior? Everything sāsrava, because it is abandoned by the noble ones. Grasping that as supreme is dṛṣṭiparāmarśa. Strictly, the expression would be `dṛṣṭyādi-parāmarśa`; the word `ādi` is understood as elided.

The view of what is not a cause as a cause, or of what is not a path as a path, is śīlavrataparāmarśa. For example, Maheśvara is not the cause of worlds, yet one regards him—or Prajāpati or another—as their cause. Entering fire or water and similar practices are not causes of heaven, yet one regards them as causes. Mere observance of moral rules and vows, and the knowledges taught by Sāṃkhya and Yoga and the like, are not the path to liberation, yet one regards them as the path. Here too, it is said, an `ādi` has been elided. These should be understood as the five views.

But a view of a cause in connection with a real causal matter can also be śīlavrataparāmarśa. Why, then, is this not abandoned through seeing origination?

The answer begins with VAK 5.08.

## Course of the Argument

The Bhāṣya announces exactly what VAK 5.07 is doing:

```text
VAK 5.03
    five views named

VAK 5.07
    five views defined by svabhāva
```

So this verse is not merely illustrative. It gives the intrinsic doctrinal determination of each afflicted view.

### 1. Satkāyadṛṣṭi

The decisive definition is:

```text
satkāya
    = pañcopādānaskandhāḥ
    = the five aggregates of appropriation
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly resists two false constructions of the aggregates:

```text
nityasaṃjñā
    notion of permanence

piṇḍasaṃjñā
    notion of compact / unitary mass
```

These prepare the way for `ātmagraha`, grasping as self.

Thus satkāyadṛṣṭi is not a generic "self-belief." It is the appropriation of the aggregate-complex under the determinations:

```text
I
mine
```

The cited teaching reinforces the point: whatever is regarded as self is sought within the five aggregates of appropriation.

### 2. Antagrāhadṛṣṭi

Once something has been appropriated as self, that same supposed self is fixed at one of two extremes:

```text
dhruva / śāśvata
    permanent

uccheda
    annihilated
```

Hence antagrāhadṛṣṭi is literally an extreme-grasping view.

### 3. Mithyādṛṣṭi

The Bhāṣya significantly sharpens the Kārikā's `nāsti`:

```text
sati duḥkhādisatye
nāsti iti dṛṣṭiḥ
```

> When the truths such as suffering exist, the view that they do not exist is mithyādṛṣṭi.

The doctrinal distinction that follows is especially important:

```text
mithyādṛṣṭi
    apavādikā
    denying / canceling

other afflicted views
    samāropikāḥ
    superimposing
```

This supplies a genuine polarity within error itself. Some error adds a determination that does not belong; mithyādṛṣṭi characteristically cancels what should be acknowledged.

### 4. Dṛṣṭiparāmarśa

The Bhāṣya defines `hīna` without ambiguity:

```text
hīnam
    = sarvaṃ sāsravam
```

Everything sāsrava is inferior because noble ones abandon it. The error consists in taking such a conditioned, affliction-bearing determination as supreme.

So `agra` is not merely "better." It is the false elevation of what belongs to the abandoned field into the position of highest determination.

### 5. Śīlavrataparāmarśa

This is an error concerning causal and path adequacy:

```text
ahetu
    taken as hetu

amārga
    taken as mārga
```

The examples make the practical character unmistakable:

```text
false world-cause
false cause of heavenly rebirth
false path to liberation
```

The error therefore concerns mediation: a determination that does not produce the claimed result is treated as the adequate means to that result.

## New Technical Terms

- **svabhāva of a dṛṣṭi** — intrinsic doctrinal character of a view
  - **status:** Bhāṣya framing term in this unit
  - **function:** shifts from naming the five views to defining them

- **pañcopādānaskandha** — five aggregates of appropriation
  - **status:** previously encountered; decisive here
  - **function:** exact referent of `satkāya`

- **nityasaṃjñā** — notion/recognition of permanence
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** one prior misdetermination enabling self-grasping

- **piṇḍasaṃjñā** — notion of a compact or unitary mass
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** second prior misdetermination enabling self-grasping

- **ātmagraha** — grasping as self
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** basic appropriative operation of satkāyadṛṣṭi

- **apavādikā dṛṣṭi** — denying / canceling view
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only technical characterization here
  - **function:** marks mithyādṛṣṭi as distinctive through denial of what is operative

- **samāropikā dṛṣṭi** — superimposing view
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only contrastive characterization here
  - **function:** describes the positive false attribution characteristic of the other views

- **hīna** — inferior; here explicitly everything sāsrava
  - **status:** Kārikā term precisely defined by the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** object falsely elevated by dṛṣṭiparāmarśa

- **ahetu / amārga** — non-cause / non-path
  - **status:** Kārikā terms expanded through examples
  - **function:** objects falsely endowed with causal or liberative efficacy

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.07 converts the five-view taxonomy into five distinct structures of cognitive error:

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
    appropriation
    aggregates → self / mine

antagrāhadṛṣṭi
    extreme fixation
    supposed self → permanent / annihilated

mithyādṛṣṭi
    cancellation
    existent truth → "does not exist"

dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
    false elevation
    inferior sāsrava determination → supreme

śīlavrataparāmarśa
    false mediation
    non-cause / non-path → cause / path
```

The distinction between `apavāda` and `samāropa` is especially significant. Vasubandhu is not treating all error as one homogeneous falsity. Error can operate by removing a determination that belongs or by adding/elevating a determination that does not belong.

This is already a much more precise account than the generic category "wrong view."

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised and clarified.**

The Bhāṣya fixes several points that the compressed verse leaves open:

1. `satkāya` is specifically the five aggregates of appropriation.
2. `nāsti` refers paradigmatically to denying truths such as suffering that in fact obtain.
3. `hīna` means the entire sāsrava field in this definition.
4. `ahetu` and `amārga` concern false causal and path efficacy.
5. `mithyādṛṣṭi` is distinguished as denying/canceling, while the other major false views are superimposing.

A tightened philosophical translation is:

> The five views are: taking the five aggregates of appropriation as self or belonging to self; regarding that supposed self as permanent or annihilated; denying truths that obtain; taking what belongs to the inferior sāsrava field as supreme; and taking what is not a cause or path as an effective cause or liberating path.

---

## Logical Determination

The five views now appear as five failures of determination rather than five arbitrary opinions:

```text
1. false subject-relation
2. false modal/extreme determination
3. false negation
4. false ranking
5. false mediation
```

The `apavāda / samāropa` distinction is logically strong:

```text
apavāda
    subtracts what belongs

samāropa
    adds what does not belong
```

Mithyādṛṣṭi is therefore a defective negative determination, while the other views primarily construct or elevate a false positive determination.

This gives VAK 5.07 an unexpectedly exact theory of error.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The Bhāṣya supplies a useful error taxonomy without requiring us to impose one externally:

```text
appropriation error
extreme-fixation error
denial error
rank-inversion error
means/path error
```

For the Rational Kośa, the deepest contact point is the distinction:

```text
false cancellation
    apavāda

false imposition
    samāropa
```

A rational system must be able to detect both. It can fail by asserting too much, but also by refusing a determination that the field requires.

The śīlavrataparāmarśa examples further show that Practical Reason requires causal adequacy:

```text
claimed end
    requires
adequate means
```

A practice cannot become a path merely because it is sincerely adopted, austere, traditional, or conceptually elaborate.

---

## Cross-Reference to VAK 5.08

The Bhāṣya ends this unit with a difficulty:

> If a śīlavrataparāmarśa can be a view of a cause in connection with an actual causal matter, why is it not abandoned through seeing origination?

VAK 5.08 supplies the answer by examining the genesis of causal fixation, including the projection of an eternal self or creator as cause.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.07 establishes the intrinsic character of the five afflicted views:

> **Satkāyadṛṣṭi appropriates the five aggregates as self or mine; antagrāhadṛṣṭi fixes that supposed self at the extremes of permanence or annihilation; mithyādṛṣṭi denies truths that obtain; dṛṣṭiparāmarśa elevates what belongs to the sāsrava field as supreme; and śīlavrataparāmarśa mistakes non-causes and non-paths for effective causes and liberating paths. Vasubandhu further distinguishes error by cancellation (`apavāda`) from error by superimposition (`samāropa`).**
