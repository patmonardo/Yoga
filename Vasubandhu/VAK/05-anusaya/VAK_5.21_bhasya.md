# VAK_5.21 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dvaidhordhvavṛtter nāto 'nyau catvāry eveti bāhyakāḥ |
> tṛṣṇādṛṅmānamohās te dhyāyitritvād avidyayā || 5.21 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Because one moves in two directions and the other upward, those other two are not roots. The outside authorities say there are instead four: craving, view, conceit, and delusion. They hold these to be roots because there are three kinds of meditator, with ignorance governing them.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Kāśmīra position**

> Because of twofold movement and upward movement, those other two are therefore not roots.

Doubt, because its movement is twofold, is not fit to be a root, because it is unstable.

Conceit, whose defining mark is elevation and whose movement is upward, is likewise not a root.

For roots are observed in the world to be stable and downward-moving.

**Voice: Alternative school — `bāhyakāḥ`**

> The outsiders say that there are only four.

The outside authorities maintain that there are four roots of the ethically indeterminate:

> craving, view, conceit, and delusion—those.

The pronoun `te` shows that these are to be understood as ethically indeterminate: indeterminate craving, view, conceit, and ignorance.

Why do they hold these to be roots of the indeterminate?

> Because there are three kinds of meditator, through ignorance.

There are three kinds of meditator: those whose meditation is headed or dominated respectively by craving, by view, and by conceit. These three arise under the power of ignorance.

## Course of the Argument

VAK 5.20 established the Kāśmīra list of three `avyākṛtamūlas`:

```text
tṛṣṇā
avidyā
mati / prajñā
```

VAK 5.21 first asks why two obvious candidates are omitted:

```text
vicikitsā
māna
```

The Bhāṣya answers by appealing not merely to ethical status but to the **formal behavior of a root**.

### 1. Why doubt is not a root

```text
vicikitsā
    → dvaidha-vṛtti
    → movement in two directions
    → calatva, instability
    → not fit to be mūla
```

Doubt does not settle. Its structure is alternation or divided movement. Because a root must stabilize what grows from it, instability disqualifies doubt from roothood.

### 2. Why conceit is not a root

```text
māna
    → unnati-lakṣaṇa
    → elevation is its mark
    → ūrdhva-vṛtti, upward movement
    → not mūla
```

Conceit elevates. The Bhāṣya contrasts this with the ordinary form of a root:

```text
root
    sthira
    stable

root
    adhovṛtti
    downward-moving
```

The analogy is doctrinally productive. A `mūla` is not just a temporally earlier cause; it is something that **settles beneath and supports** the field generated from it.

### 3. The alternative school

The `bāhyakāḥ` reject the Kāśmīra triad and propose four indeterminate roots:

```text
tṛṣṇā
    craving

dṛṣṭi
    view

māna
    conceit

avidyā
    ignorance
```

Their criterion is different. They reason from recognizable forms of meditative practitioner:

```text
tṛṣṇā-uttara-dhyāyin
    meditator dominated by craving

dṛṣṭi-uttara-dhyāyin
    meditator dominated by view

māna-uttara-dhyāyin
    meditator dominated by conceit
```

All three occur `avidyā-vaśāt`—under the power of ignorance.

Thus ignorance has a double role in this alternative account:

```text
one of the four roots
    and
common governing condition of the other three practitioner-types
```

The disagreement is therefore deeper than the number three versus four. The two positions use different tests of roothood.

## Voice Identification

The opening exclusion of doubt and conceit represents the position being defended in the immediate Kāśmīra discussion:

**Voice: Vasubandhu reporting the Kāśmīra position**

The second position is explicitly introduced by:

> `catvāry eveti bāhyakāḥ`

**Voice: Alternative school (`bāhyakāḥ`)**

The term `bāhyakāḥ` should be left doctrinally cautious here. The passage clearly marks authorities outside the immediately defended position, but this unit alone does not securely identify a more specific school.

## New Technical Terms

- **dvaidha-vṛtti** — twofold or divided movement
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit explanation of vicikitsā
  - **function:** explains why doubt is unstable and therefore not a root

- **calatva** — instability, mobility, wavering
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only causal qualification
  - **function:** gives the direct reason that doubt cannot function as `mūla`

- **unnati-lakṣaṇa** — having elevation as its defining mark
  - **status:** Bhāṣya characterization of `māna`
  - **function:** explains the upward movement by which conceit is excluded from roothood

- **ūrdhva-vṛtti** — upward movement
  - **status:** Kārikā term explicitly interpreted in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** contrasts conceit with the downward form of a root

- **sthira** — stable, fixed
  - **status:** Bhāṣya root-analogy
  - **function:** supplies one formal mark of roothood

- **adho-vṛtti** — downward-moving, downward-growing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya root-analogy
  - **function:** supplies a second formal mark of roothood

- **bāhyaka** — outside authority / alternative school
  - **status:** explicit doctrinal attribution
  - **function:** marks the competing four-root theory

- **dhyāyin** — meditator, one who practices meditation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-explicit practitioner category
  - **function:** provides the experiential/genetic evidence for the alternative theory

- **tṛṣṇā-dṛṣṭi-māna-uttara-dhyāyin** — meditators dominated respectively by craving, view, and conceit
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** gives three generated practitioner-types used to argue for roothood

- **avidyā-vaśa** — being under the power of ignorance
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** identifies ignorance as the common governing condition of the three practitioner-types

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya materially clarifies what `mūla` means in this discussion.

A root is not defined merely as:

```text
something causally prior
```

The Kāśmīra argument presupposes a stronger structure:

```text
mūla
    must be stable
    must settle downward
    must support the arising complex
```

Therefore:

```text
vicikitsā
    ethically indeterminate in some cases
    but unstable
    therefore not an avyākṛtamūla

māna
    ethically indeterminate in higher domains
    but upward-moving
    therefore not an avyākṛtamūla
```

This confirms the distinction already opened in 5.20:

```text
has a given ethical status
    ≠
functions as root of that ethical status
```

The `bāhyaka` theory uses another criterion. It reads roothood from what an affliction can organize or generate in practice:

```text
craving
    → craving-dominated meditator

view
    → view-dominated meditator

conceit
    → conceit-dominated meditator

all three
    → under ignorance
```

So the controversy can be stated as:

```text
Kāśmīra criterion
    roothood from inner structural form

bāhyaka criterion
    roothood from generated practitioner-type
```

This is a genuine doctrinal disagreement over what makes a cause a **root** rather than merely a condition.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The Bhāṣya confirms that the first compound is to be unpacked distributively:

```text
dvaidha-vṛtti
    → vicikitsā

ūrdhva-vṛtti
    → māna
```

It also makes explicit that `dhyāyitritva` refers to the existence of three types of meditator, respectively dominated by craving, view, and conceit, all conditioned by ignorance.

A tighter canonical translation is therefore:

> Because doubt moves in two directions and conceit moves upward, neither is a root. The outside authorities instead maintain four indeterminate roots—craving, view, conceit, and ignorance—because there are three kinds of meditator, dominated respectively by craving, view, and conceit, all under the power of ignorance.

## Logical Determination

The passage distinguishes **status**, **causal efficacy**, and **form of causality**.

```text
ethical status
    avyākṛta

causal efficacy
    can condition other dharmas

root-form
    stable + grounding
```

These are not identical predicates.

The Kāśmīra account gives three directional structures:

```text
divided movement
    vicikitsā
    → oscillation

upward movement
    māna
    → elevation

downward movement
    mūla
    → grounding
```

Hence:

```text
not every cause is a root
```

A root must possess a mode of causality adequate to **supporting** rather than merely modifying the field.

The alternative school then offers a different inferential path:

```text
recurrent generated form
    → infer generative root
```

That gives us two distinct logical determinations of ground:

```text
Ground by internal form
    what is stable and supporting?

Ground by generated actuality
    what repeatedly produces a determinate type?
```

The disagreement is thus a dispute over the criterion of grounding itself.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

For the later Rational Kośa, VAK 5.21 is unusually useful because it prevents `ground` from collapsing into generic causation.

```text
cause
    may divide
    may elevate
    may merely accompany

root / ground
    must support reproduction of a field
```

The passage also gives a clean distinction between two ways of discovering a ground:

```text
from its internal logical form
    stability / supporting direction

from its products
    repeated generation of a recognizable type
```

This is not yet an Organon synthesis; it is already present as the opposition between the Kāśmīra and `bāhyaka` explanations. The later system should preserve both moments rather than choosing one prematurely.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.21 turns the apparently simple question “how many indeterminate roots are there?” into a dispute over the very concept of a root.

> **For the Kāśmīra position, roothood has a determinate form: a root is stable and grounding. Doubt fails because it oscillates; conceit fails because it elevates. The alternative authorities instead infer roothood genetically from the types of meditator produced by craving, view, and conceit, with ignorance governing all three. The passage therefore distinguishes mere causal influence from the stronger relation of grounding and exposes two possible criteria of ground: internal supporting form and recurrent generated actuality.**
