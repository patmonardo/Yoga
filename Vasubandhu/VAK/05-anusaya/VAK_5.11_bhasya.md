# VAK_5.11 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> vibhavecchā na cāryasya saṃbhavanti vidhādayaḥ |
> nāsmitā dṛṣṭipuṣṭatvāt kaukṛtyaṃ nāpi cāśubham || 5.11 ||

**Best current translation:**  
In the noble one, the wish for non-existence, the conceit-modes beginning with the comparative forms, and the conceit “I am” do not arise, because they are nourished by views. Nor does unwholesome remorse arise.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The thirst for non-existence is also to be abandoned by cultivation. What is this dharma called non-existence? It is the impermanence belonging to the three domains. Desire directed toward that is thirst for non-existence.

By the word “likewise,” a particular form of thirst for existence is also included.

It has been said that the modes of conceit are also to be abandoned by cultivation, as is the conceit “I am.”

> Yet in the noble one the modes beginning with these do not arise, nor does I-am conceit.

By “beginning with” the series is understood to extend as far as thirst for non-existence.

Why, although they have not yet been abandoned, do these nevertheless not become manifest?

> Because they are nourished by views.

The conceit-modes and I-am conceit are nourished by satkāyadṛṣṭi. Since that supporting structure has been broken, they no longer have the strength to rise again.

The further afflictive manifestation discussed in 5.10 is nourished by false view. Thirst for non-existence is nourished by the annihilationist extreme-view. A particular form of thirst for existence is nourished by the eternalist extreme-view.

> Nor does unwholesome remorse [arise].

Even unwholesome remorse is to be abandoned by cultivation, but it does not occur in the noble one because it is generated from doubt.

---

## Course of the Argument

The central problem of 5.11 is a distinction already opened in 5.10:

```text
not yet abandoned
    ≠
currently capable of manifestation
```

The Bhāṣya now explains why.

Several states remain technically classified as `bhāvanāprahātavya`, yet they cannot arise in an ārya because the views that nourished them have already been destroyed by seeing.

```text
supporting dṛṣṭi
    ↓ nourishes
secondary affliction

seeing destroys the dṛṣṭi
    ↓
secondary affliction may remain
classified as cultivation-abandonable
    ↓
but it no longer manifests
```

This gives a much more precise account of the transformation produced by `darśana`.

---

## Vibhavatṛṣṇā

The Bhāṣya explicitly defines `vibhava` here:

```text
traidhātukī anityatā
    impermanence belonging to the three domains
```

`Vibhavatṛṣṇā` is then:

```text
there [in that non-existence / passing away]
    prārthanā
    wishing / longing
```

Thus the text does not define liberation as desire for annihilation. The desire for non-existence is itself an afflicted thirst and remains cultivation-abandonable.

Its immediate sustaining condition is:

```text
ucchedadṛṣṭi
    annihilationist extreme-view
```

When that view has been broken, the derivative thirst no longer manifests in the ārya.

---

## Dṛṣṭi-puṣṭatva

The decisive Bhāṣya term is `dṛṣṭipuṣṭatva`:

```text
dṛṣṭi
    view

puṣṭa
    nourished / strengthened
```

The text identifies a dependency structure:

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
    → mānavidhā
    → asmimāna

ucchedadṛṣṭi
    → vibhavatṛṣṇā

śāśvatadṛṣṭi
    → a particular bhavatṛṣṇā

mithyādṛṣṭi
    → the further afflictive manifestation discussed in 5.10
```

The crucial image is `bhagnapṛṣṭhatva`, literally having its back broken. Once the nourishing view is destroyed, the dependent affliction lacks the support required to rise again.

This is stronger than simply saying that noble cognition “knows better.” The causal support of the manifestation has been cut.

---

## Kaukṛtya

The Kārikā's final clause is clarified by the prose:

```text
akuśala-kaukṛtya
    is bhāvanāprahātavya
```

Yet it does not arise in the ārya because:

```text
vicikitsā-samutthitatva
    it is generated from doubt
```

Since the relevant doubt has already been abandoned through seeing, its dependent unwholesome remorse does not manifest.

So the Kārikā should not be read as saying that remorse is simply “not unwholesome.” The intended claim is:

> **unwholesome remorse, although cultivation-abandonable, does not arise in the noble one.**

This is a genuine translation correction supplied by the Bhāṣya.

## New Technical Terms

- **vibhavatṛṣṇā** — thirst directed toward non-existence or passing away
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification of the Kārikā's `vibhavecchā`
  - **function:** cultivation-abandonable desire dependent upon annihilationist view

- **traidhātukī anityatā** — impermanence belonging to the three domains
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only definition in this unit
  - **function:** defines the local meaning of `vibhava`

- **dṛṣṭipuṣṭa** — nourished or strengthened by a view
  - **status:** explicit Kārikā/Bhāṣya technical relation
  - **function:** explains why a cultivation-abandonable affliction can cease to manifest after seeing

- **bhagnapṛṣṭhatva** — having the supporting structure broken; literally “broken-backed”
  - **status:** Bhāṣya metaphor
  - **function:** describes loss of capacity to arise once the nourishing view is destroyed

- **vicikitsā-samutthita** — arisen from doubt
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** explains why unwholesome remorse no longer arises in an ārya

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.11 introduces an essential distinction between three statuses:

```text
1. abandoned
2. not yet abandoned
3. capable of current manifestation
```

These are not equivalent.

A secondary affliction may still be technically unremoved at the level of cultivation while being unable to manifest because the view that sustained it has already been abandoned by seeing.

Thus the Path acts both directly and indirectly:

```text
seeing
    directly abandons view

thereby
    indirectly disables
    view-nourished secondary formations

cultivation
    later completes their formal abandonment
```

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass rendering of the final clause could suggest:

> remorse is not unwholesome.

The Bhāṣya shows instead that the subject remains the ārya and that `aśubha-kaukṛtya` is itself under negation:

> The desire for non-existence, the modes beginning with comparative conceit, and I-am conceit do not arise in the noble one because they are nourished by views; nor does unwholesome remorse arise.

This is the preferred canonical sense.

---

## Logical Determination

The passage supplies a dependency model of affliction:

```text
root cognitive determination
    → nourishes
secondary disposition
```

When the root determination is removed:

```text
secondary disposition
    may remain in classificatory status
but
    loses manifestation-capacity
```

This means the Kośa's abandonment theory cannot be represented as a simple Boolean state:

```text
present / absent
```

It requires at least:

```text
abandonment-status
manifestation-capacity
supporting-condition
```

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage is especially useful for a later Rational Kośa implementation because it distinguishes a stored disposition from an executable state.

```text
latent classification
    ≠
currently executable operation
```

A dependency can be disabled upstream before the downstream structure is fully eliminated.

The textual priority remains Vasubandhu's point: noble seeing changes not only what is known but **what afflicted formations remain capable of arising**.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.11 establishes a decisive refinement of the Path model:

> **An affliction can remain technically cultivation-abandonable while no longer being capable of manifestation. In the noble one, several such formations fail to arise because the views that nourished them have already been destroyed through seeing. Even unwholesome remorse follows this rule, since it depends upon doubt.**

The verse therefore distinguishes formal abandonment from causal disablement and shows how truth-seeing restructures the field of possible afflictive manifestation.
