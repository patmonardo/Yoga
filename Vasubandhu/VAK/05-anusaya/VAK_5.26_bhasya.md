## Literal Prose Translation

The four Sarvāstivāda positions are named according to alteration in mode of being, characteristic, state, and relative otherness.

Bhadanta Dharmatrāta teaches alteration of mode of being. As a dharma proceeds through the temporal courses, its mode changes but not its substance. Just as a golden vessel can be reshaped while its color remains, and milk transformed into curd abandons some qualities but not its color, a dharma passing from future to present abandons its future mode but not its substantial being; passing from present to past, it abandons its present mode but not its substantial being.

Bhadanta Ghoṣaka teaches alteration of characteristic. A past dharma is joined to the mark of pastness without being separated from the marks of future and present; similarly for the future and present. The analogy is a man attached to one woman while not being detached from the others.

Bhadanta Vasumitra teaches alteration of state. As a dharma proceeds through the times, it is designated differently upon reaching different states, not because it becomes another substance. A single counter placed in the units position is called one, in the hundreds position one hundred, and in the thousands position one thousand.

Bhadanta Buddhadeva teaches relative otherness. A dharma is differently designated by reference to what precedes and follows, not because it becomes another substance; as one woman is called mother or daughter according to relation.

Of the four, the first falls into the Sāṃkhya position because it is a doctrine of transformation. The second collapses the temporal courses into one another because every dharma would possess all temporal characteristics at every moment. The fourth likewise allows all three times to arise within a single time through relative comparison. Therefore the third, the state-difference theory, is preferable.

According to it, the temporal courses are distinguished by operative activity: when a dharma does not perform its `kāritra`, it is future; when it performs it, present; when, having performed it, it has ceased, past.

But Vasubandhu then asks whether this actually solves the problem. If past and future exist substantially just as the present does, why are they called past and future? If function distinguishes the times, what exactly is the function of a present eye? If it is giving and receiving a result, past homogeneous causes also give results; temporal marks therefore begin to overlap. Further, if the dharma exists through all times with the same own-being, what prevents it from acting always? If `kāritra` is distinct from the dharma, a regress or an unconditioned eternal function threatens; if it is not distinct, the temporal distinction has merely been displaced rather than explained.

## Philosophical Translation

The four Sarvāstivāda theories all try to preserve one fundamental claim: the dharma remains real through past, present, and future, while temporal difference must nevertheless be genuine. They therefore locate temporal alteration in four different places: mode, characteristic, state, or relation.

Vasumitra's state-theory is the strongest of the four because it avoids changing the dharma's substance and defines temporal position through efficacy. A dharma is future insofar as its function is not yet exercised, present while its function is exercised, and past once that operation has been completed and ceased.

Yet Vasubandhu does not stop at this solution. He asks whether `kāritra` genuinely mediates existence and temporal becoming. If the dharma possesses the same substantial own-being in all three times, an account is still required of why that very same real dharma is unborn, operative, or destroyed. Making function different from the dharma creates a new entity requiring temporal explanation; making function identical with the dharma leaves the original problem intact. Thus the preferred Sarvāstivāda theory is presented as the best internal account but not yet as a satisfactory final account of temporality.

## Course of Argument

### 1. Four loci for temporal difference

```text
Dharmatrāta
    bhāvānyathatva
    change of mode

Ghoṣaka
    lakṣaṇānyathatva
    change of temporal characteristic

Vasumitra
    avasthānyathatva
    change of state

Buddhadeva
    anyathānyathatva
    relative difference
```

All four preserve:

```text
dravya continuity
    +
temporal differentiation
```

but disagree about what mediates the differentiation.

### 2. Vasubandhu's criticisms of three theories

Dharmatrāta is rejected because his transformation model resembles Sāṃkhya `pariṇāma`: one underlying thing persists while its modes transform.

Ghoṣaka is rejected because simultaneous possession of all three temporal characteristics produces `adhvasaṃkara`, confusion of the temporal courses.

Buddhadeva is rejected because purely relative designation allows past, present, and future to appear inside any single temporal field depending on comparison.

### 3. Vasumitra is provisionally preferred

```text
tṛtīyaḥ śobhanaḥ
```

The wording is important: **the third is preferable / good**, not yet established as Vasubandhu's final truth.

Its principle is:

```text
future
    kāritra not being performed

present
    kāritra being performed

past
    kāritra performed + dharma ceased
```

Temporal determination is therefore assigned to **operative state** rather than substantial alteration.

### 4. Vasubandhu turns against the preferred theory

The analysis then shifts voice and level. The best Vaibhāṣika theory becomes the object of criticism.

First problem:

```text
if past and future exist dravyataḥ
like the present,
what makes them past and future?
```

Second problem:

```text
if kāritra defines presentness,
past causes can still give fruits;
therefore functional predicates overlap times.
```

Third problem:

```text
same dharma + same svabhāva + existence at all times
    → why does it function only sometimes?
```

Fourth problem:

```text
kāritra distinct from dharma
    → new temporal problem / regress

kāritra not distinct from dharma
    → original temporal problem returns
```

Finally Vasubandhu presses the genetic question:

```text
If the future dharma already exists with the same own-being,
in what sense is it unborn?

If the past dharma still exists with the same own-being,
in what sense is it destroyed?
```

This is the decisive pressure point.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika doxography** — presentation of the four Sarvāstivāda teachers and their temporal theories.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / critical narrator** — criticisms of Dharmatrāta, Ghoṣaka, and Buddhadeva, and the judgment that Vasumitra's third theory is preferable among them.

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position** — temporal courses distinguished by `kāritra`.

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Objector against Sarvāstivāda** — sustained questions beginning from whether substantially existent past and future dharmas can truly be called past and future.

The exact dialogue boundaries after the verse are rapid, but the dialectical direction is clear: the state-theory is first defended, then subjected to objections.

## New Technical Terms

- **bhāvānyathatva** — alteration in mode of being; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicated; **function:** Dharmatrāta's explanation of temporal difference.
- **lakṣaṇānyathatva** — alteration in characteristic; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicated; **function:** Ghoṣaka's explanation.
- **avasthānyathatva** — alteration in state; **status:** central here; **function:** Vasumitra's preferred internal Sarvāstivāda account.
- **anyathānyathatva** — difference through relation to prior and subsequent; **status:** Bhāṣya-explicated; **function:** Buddhadeva's account.
- **pariṇāmavāda** — doctrine of transformation; **status:** new polemical label here; **function:** reason Dharmatrāta is associated with the Sāṃkhya position.
- **adhvasaṃkara** — confusion/collapse of temporal courses; **status:** new; **function:** objection to Ghoṣaka and, differently, Buddhadeva.
- **kāritra** — operative activity/function; **status:** decisive technical term; **function:** criterion by which Vasumitra differentiates past, present, and future.
- **dravyānyathatva** — substantial otherness; **status:** Bhāṣya technical distinction; **function:** explicitly denied by all four accounts in different ways.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse and Bhāṣya must be assimilated in two stages.

### Internal Sarvāstivāda result

The best of the four theories is Vasumitra's:

```text
Dharma
    remains dravyataḥ identifiable

Temporal difference
    = difference of avasthā

Avasthā
    determined by kāritra
```

This is much subtler than a doctrine of one inert substance sitting unchanged across a timeline. Temporal predicates are assigned through operational status.

### Vasubandhu's critical result

But Vasubandhu asks whether a merely operational difference is enough if the dharma itself is asserted to exist substantially and with its own nature throughout all three times.

The critical structure is:

```text
Sarvāstivāda wants:
    real persistence
    + genuine becoming

Vasumitra mediates them through kāritra.

Vasubandhu asks:
    what makes kāritra temporally determinate?
    and what makes an eternally existent dharma genuinely unborn or destroyed?
```

Thus the real philosophical issue is not simply **persistence versus change**. It is whether the proposed middle term actually **generates the distinction** it is meant to explain.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The first-pass translation is broadly correct, but `tṛtīyaḥ śobhanaḥ` should not be read as an unconditional endorsement by Vasubandhu. Better:

> “Of these, the third is the most satisfactory [within this set]: the temporal courses are distinguished by operative activity.”

The Bhāṣya immediately subjects that account to criticism.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.26 is a remarkably clean problem of mediation.

The common thesis is:

```text
one dharma
    exists across three times
```

The required result is:

```text
future ≠ present ≠ past
```

Therefore a middle determination is required.

The four candidates are:

```text
bhāva
lakṣaṇa
avasthā
relation
```

Vasumitra's `avasthā / kāritra` is superior because it makes temporal difference functional rather than substantial.

But Vasubandhu applies a stronger criterion:

```text
A middle term must not merely label the difference.
It must explain how the difference is possible.
```

If `kāritra` is external to the dharma, the mediation is duplicated. If `kāritra` is simply the dharma itself, no mediating determination has been gained.

That is why the subsequent question is so powerful:

```text
same svabhāva, existing in the same way —
by what is it unborn or destroyed?
```

The Bhāṣya is therefore already testing whether an alleged explanatory middle has **genetic force**.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage is especially important for later assimilation, but we should keep the correspondence disciplined.

The reusable logical form is:

```text
Persistent determination D
    must occupy distinct states S1, S2, S3.

Proposed mediator M
    explains the state transitions.

Test:
    Is M genuinely distinct enough to explain transition?
    If yes, what explains M's own state?
    If no, has anything been explained?
```

This is a general test for state-machine metaphysics and for conceptual mediation.

For our later work, the strongest point is **not** “Sarvāstivāda equals our system.” It is that Vasubandhu forces the theory to show the **genesis of temporal determination** rather than merely posit a substrate plus three labels.

That is precisely the point that must be preserved when this material is assimilated into a richer genetic framework.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.26 is not merely a four-school taxonomy. It stages a sequence:

```text
1. common Sarvāstivāda requirement
   preserve existence through three times

2. four attempted mediations
   mode / mark / state / relation

3. internal selection
   Vasumitra's state-function theory is best

4. renewed dialectic
   does kāritra really generate temporal difference?

5. critical pressure
   if svabhāva is already equally real in all times,
   what makes future genuinely unborn
   and past genuinely destroyed?
```

That final question is the point to carry forward. Vasubandhu is not satisfied by persistence plus externally assigned temporal states; he demands an account of how the distinctions themselves are constituted.
