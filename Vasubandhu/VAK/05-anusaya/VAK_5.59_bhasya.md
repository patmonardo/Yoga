# VAK 5.59 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.59**

> kāme nivaraṇāni ekavipakṣāhārakṛtyataḥ /
> dvyekatā pañcatā skandhavidhātavicikitsanāt //

**Working translation**

> The hindrances belong to the desire-domain. Two pairs are each counted as one because they have one counteragent, one nourishment, and one function. Their fivefoldness is explained through obstruction of the Path-aggregates and through doubt.

See the full Kārikā analysis: `VAK_5.59.md`.

---

## Bhāṣya Passage

**Source:** Pradhan 318.08–319.18.

The Bhāṣya opens from the canonical five hindrances:

> *kāmacchando vyāpādaḥ styānamiddham auddhatyakaukṛtyaṃ vicikitsā ca*

— sensual desire, ill will, torpor-and-sleepiness, agitation-and-remorse, and doubt.

It then asks whether the occurrences of torpor, agitation, and doubt throughout the three domains are included, or only those connected with the desire-domain. The answer appeals to the sūtra characterization of the five hindrances as a complete unwholesome mass:

> *kevalo 'yaṃ paripūrṇo 'kuśalarāśir yaduta pañca nivaraṇāni*

Hence:

> *kāme nivaraṇāni*

The hindrances, in this technical fivefold classification, are in the desire-domain and not elsewhere.

### Why two pairs count as one

The Bhāṣya asks why **styāna + middha** are one hindrance and **auddhatya + kaukṛtya** another single hindrance.

The answer is:

> *ekavipakṣāhārakṛtyataḥ*

because the members of each pair have one counteragent/non-nourishment, one nourishment, and one function.

The prose glosses:

> *vipakṣaḥ pratipakṣo 'nāhāra ity eko 'rthaḥ*

Here **vipakṣa**, **pratipakṣa**, and **anāhāra** are treated as expressing one practical opposition.

#### Styāna–middha

Their nourishment is fivefold:

> *tandrā aratir vijṛmbhikā bhakte 'samatā cetaso līnatvam*

- **tandrā** — drowsiness / languor
- **arati** — dissatisfaction, aversion to engagement
- **vijṛmbhikā** — yawning
- **bhakte 'samatā** — immoderation with respect to food
- **cetaso līnatva** — sinking or slackness of mind

Their non-nourishment/counter-condition is:

> *ālokasaṃjñā*

— the perception/idea of light.

Their function is one:

> *ubhe api hy ete cittaṃ layaṃ codayataḥ*

Both drive the mind toward **laya**, sinking or collapse.

Thus:

```text
styāna + middha
    same nourishment
    same counter-condition
    same function: citta-laya
        ↓
    one nivaraṇa
```

#### Auddhatya–kaukṛtya

Their nourishment is fourfold:

> *jñātivitarko janapadavitarko 'maravitarkaḥ paurāṇasya ca hasitakrīḍitaramitaparibhāvitasyānusmartā bhavati*

- **jñāti-vitarka** — discursive thinking about relatives
- **janapada-vitarka** — discursive thinking about country/locality
- **amara-vitarka** — the transmitted source reading; preserve as such
- recollection of former laughing, playing, amusement, and enjoyment

Their non-nourishment/counter-condition is:

> *śamatha*

— calming / tranquility.

Their function is likewise one:

> *ubhe api hy ete cittam avyupaśāntaṃ vartayataḥ*

Both keep the mind **unpacified**.

Thus:

```text
auddhatya + kaukṛtya
    same nourishment
    same counter-condition: śamatha
    same function: avyupaśānta-citta
        ↓
    one nivaraṇa
```

The Bhāṣya therefore states explicitly that the unity of each pair is grounded in shared practical relations, not in identity of the member factors.

## Why Exactly Five Hindrances?

The Bhāṣya next asks:

> *yadi sarvakleśā nivaraṇaṃ kasmāt pañcaivoktāni*

If all kleśas can obstruct, why are only five taught as **nivaraṇas**?

The Kārikā answers:

> *pañcatā skandhavidhātavicikitsanāt*

The prose then gives a first explanation:

```text
kāmacchanda + vyāpāda
    → obstruction of śīla-skandha

styāna + middha
    → obstruction of prajñā-skandha

auddhatya + kaukṛtya
    → obstruction of samādhi-skandha

absence of samādhi + prajñā
    → doubt concerning the Truths
```

In the Bhāṣya's words:

> *kāmacchandavyāpādābhyāṃ śīlaskandhavidhātaḥ*

> *styānamiddhena prajñāskandhasya auddhatyakaukṛtyena samādhiskandhasya*

> *samādhiprajñayor abhāve satyeṣu vicikitsako bhavati*

This explains the five as a coordinated obstruction of the Path-complex: ethics, concentration, discernment, and the determination of truth.

### Objection to the first explanation

The Bhāṣya immediately notices a problem. If **auddhatya–kaukṛtya** obstruct samādhi while **styāna–middha** obstruct prajñā, then the former should seemingly have been listed earlier in the order of the explanation.

So the text records another view:

**Voice: alternative position (`apare`)**

> *yathāsaṃkhyam etābhyāṃ samādhiprajñāskandhopaghātaḥ*

On this account, the pairs are assigned in sequence:

```text
styāna + middha
    → obstruction of samādhi

auddhatya + kaukṛtya
    → obstruction of prajñā
```

The rationale is experiential:

> *samādhiprayuktasya hi styānamiddhād bhayam*

The practitioner engaged in concentration fears torpor-and-sleepiness.

> *dharmapravicayaprayuktasya auddhatyakaukṛtyāt*

The practitioner engaged in investigation of dharmas fears agitation-and-remorse.

This is not merely a terminological rearrangement. It derives the classification from the vulnerability of the specific Path-function being exercised.

### A third explanation

The Bhāṣya then says:

> *anye tv anyathā varṇayanti*

**Voice: another alternative position.**

This account gives the five hindrances as a sequence of practical obstructions.

For one moving about among attractive and unattractive forms, grasping signs gives rise to **kāmacchanda** and **vyāpāda**; these obstruct the initial entry into samādhi.

Once one has entered samādhi, failure properly to cultivate **śamatha** and **vipaśyanā** allows:

```text
styāna-middha
    → obstacle to śamatha

auddhatya-kaukṛtya
    → obstacle to vipaśyanā

vicikitsā
    → obstacle in the relevant sequence and,
       after emerging, during examination of Dharma
```

This third account is explicitly practical and sequential: the five are identified by the different points at which they obstruct entry into, maintenance of, and reflective work following meditative concentration.

## Voice Analysis

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / expositor** — introduces the five canonical nivaraṇas, explains the desire-domain restriction, and presents the shared nourishment/counteragent/function criterion.
- **Voice: received doctrinal explanation** — first skandha-obstruction account.
- **Voice: alternative position (`apare`)** — reverses the samādhi/prajñā assignment of the two paired hindrances.
- **Voice: another alternative position (`anye`)** — gives a practical sequence based on obstruction before, during, and after meditative entry.

The Bhāṣya does not flatten these into one harmonized account. The disagreement should be preserved.

## Translation and Doctrinal Clarification

### `dvyekatā`

The prose resolves the transmitted expression explicitly:

> *dvayor ekatā dvyekatā*

“the unity of two [factors] is `dvyekatā`.”

This confirms that the verse is explaining why two pairs are each treated as one hindrance.

### `vipakṣa / pratipakṣa / anāhāra`

The Bhāṣya equates these in the present context:

```text
vipakṣa
pratipakṣa
anāhāra
    → one practical meaning here
```

Thus the paired classification is based on:

```text
shared āhāra
shared anāhāra / pratipakṣa
shared kṛtya
```

### `skandha`

Here the relevant skandhas are not the five personality aggregates. The prose explicitly names:

- **śīla-skandha**
- **samādhi-skandha**
- **prajñā-skandha**

These are Path-aggregates or functional accumulations of discipline, concentration, and discernment.

**Effect on Kārikā translation:** **Translation should be revised/clarified.** `skandhavidhāta` should not be allowed to suggest obstruction of the ordinary five aggregates.

## New Technical Terms

- **nivaraṇa** — hindrance, a factor/class considered under its obstructive function toward the Path.
  - **status:** established Buddhist technical term; newly central in this passage.
  - **function:** practical/path classification.

- **āhāra** — nourishment, sustaining condition of a hindrance.
  - **status:** familiar term used here in a technical causal-maintenance sense.
  - **function:** identifies what feeds and renews an obstruction.

- **anāhāra** — non-nourishment; here equated contextually with `vipakṣa/pratipakṣa`.
  - **status:** important Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** identifies the condition contrary to the hindrance's maintenance.

- **laya** — sinking/collapse of mind.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-specific functional term in this passage.
  - **function:** common effect of styāna and middha.

- **avyupaśānta-citta** — unpacified mind.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya functional description.
  - **function:** common effect of auddhatya and kaukṛtya.

- **ālokasaṃjñā** — perception/conception of light.
  - **status:** specific counter-condition named in the Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** non-nourishment/counteragent to styāna-middha.

- **śamatha** — calming/tranquility.
  - **status:** major Path term.
  - **function:** non-nourishment/counteragent to auddhatya-kaukṛtya in this passage.

- **dharmapravicaya** — investigation/discrimination of dharmas.
  - **status:** Path-factor vocabulary.
  - **function:** provides the practical context in which agitation/remorse become especially obstructive.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.59 supplies a rigorous example of **functional classification**.

Numerically distinct dharmas may be counted as one practical obstruction when three relations coincide:

```text
same nourishment
+ same counter-condition
+ same function
= one nivaraṇa-unit
```

Therefore:

```text
numerical plurality
    ≠
functional plurality
```

But the reverse reduction is also blocked: the members of the pair remain distinct dharmas. Their unity exists only under the relation being classified.

This continues the central lesson of Chapter 5:

```text
classification is relation-indexed
```

Yet 5.59 adds something new. The relation is explicitly **practical**: what feeds the obstruction, what counters it, and what Path-capacity it disables.

The three explanations of fivefoldness further show that even a stable canonical enumeration can admit several rational reconstructions:

```text
same canonical five
    ↓
multiple explanatory orderings
```

The Bhāṣya preserves those competing accounts rather than pretending that one classificatory rationale exhausts the fivefold structure.

## Path Determination

The passage is especially important because it turns taxonomy into intervention without yet requiring an external philosophical reconstruction.

For each paired hindrance the Bhāṣya already asks four practical questions:

```text
What feeds it?
What fails to feed / directly opposes it?
What does it do to the mind?
Which Path-capacity does it obstruct?
```

For **styāna-middha**:

```text
āhāra
    tandrā, arati, vijṛmbhikā,
    immoderation in food, mental sinking

anāhāra / pratipakṣa
    ālokasaṃjñā

kṛtya
    citta-laya
```

For **auddhatya-kaukṛtya**:

```text
āhāra
    discursive preoccupation with relatives,
    locality, the transmitted amara-vitarka,
    recollection of former amusement

anāhāra / pratipakṣa
    śamatha

kṛtya
    keeping the mind unpacified
```

This is already a complete small-scale practical-reason schema internal to the Kośa.

The strongest text-generated contact point is simply:

```text
factor identity
    ≠

```

A practical system may group distinct determinations together when they share:

```text

```

This is stronger than analogy because the Bhāṣya itself generates the relational criterion. No larger Organon architecture should yet be imposed on it.

## Closing Determination

VAK 5.59 is not merely a list of the five hindrances. The Bhāṣya shows exactly why two pairs count as single practical units, gives their nourishment and counter-condition, identifies their common functions, and then preserves three different explanations of the fivefold structure.

The result is a particularly clear instance of the methodological principle emerging throughout Chapter 5:

```text
A dharma is not exhausted by its name.
Its doctrinal determination depends on the relation under which it is being considered.
```

Here that relation is explicitly the **obstruction and restoration of Path-function**.
