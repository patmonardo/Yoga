# VAK 5.40 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.40**

```text
āsayanty āsravanty ete haranti śleṣayanty atha |
upagṛhṇanti cety eṣām āsravādi-niruktayaḥ || 5.40 ||
```

**Translation:** These cause beings to remain, they flow, they carry away, they fasten, and they take hold; such are the explanations of the terms beginning with *āsrava*.

See: [VAK_5.40.md](./VAK_5.40.md)

## Translation

They are called *āsravas* because they cause beings to remain in saṃsāra and because they flow from the summit of existence down as far as Avīci through the six wounds constituted by the sense-spheres.

They are called *oghas* because they carry away; *yogas* because they fasten; and *upādānas* because they take hold.

But the following explanation would be better.

They are called *āsravas* because through them the continuum flows out toward objects. This accords with the sūtra statement: just as, venerable ones, a boat is driven upstream only with great exertion, but when those very efforts cease it is carried downstream with little difficulty.

They are called *oghas* because of their exceedingly strong velocity: the one who possesses them is carried away by conforming to them.

They are called *yogas* because, even when they do not manifest with extreme intensity, they bind one to manifold suffering.

They are called *upādānas* because of frequent attachment or adherence arising from appropriation beginning with desire.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

VAK 5.39 explained why the afflictions are called *anuśayas*. VAK 5.40 completes the semantic sequence by explaining the functional designations *āsrava*, *ogha*, *yoga*, and *upādāna*.

The Bhāṣya gives **two levels of explanation**.

### 1. Initial nirukti

The first explanation follows the wording of the Kārikā closely:

```text
āsrava
    āsayanti saṃsāre
        they cause beings to remain in saṃsāra
    āsravanti
        they flow from Bhavāgra to Avīci
        through the six āyatana-wounds

ogha
    haranti
        they carry away

yoga
    śleṣayanti
        they fasten

upādāna
    upagṛhṇanti
        they take hold
```

A crucial correction to Pass 1 is therefore required: `āsayanti` is **not** an explanation of *anuśaya*. It belongs together with `āsravanti` as part of the explanation of *āsrava*.

### 2. Vasubandhu's preferred explanation

Vasubandhu then says:

```text
evaṃ tu sādhīyaḥ syāt
    but this would be better
```

He replaces the looser wordplay with more determinate functional explanations.

#### Āsrava — outward flow of the continuum toward objects

```text
āsravaty ebhiḥ saṃtatir viṣayeṣu
```

Through these, the continuum flows toward objects.

The accompanying sūtra image of the boat is important. Moving against the stream requires great exertion; once that counter-effort ceases, the boat is carried downstream almost effortlessly.

Thus *āsrava* is not merely “something that flows.” It names the afflictive tendency of the continuum to move toward its objects unless counteracted.

#### Ogha — overpowering velocity

```text
adhimātra-vega
```

The flood is distinguished by excessive force or speed. The person possessing it is carried away because they follow its direction (`tadanuvidhāna`).

Thus:

```text
ogha
    = afflictive momentum strong enough to carry the stream along
```

#### Yoga — binding to manifold suffering

The yoke need not be maximally intense in manifestation:

```text
nātimātra-samudācārin
```

Even without extreme eruption, it binds the person to many forms of suffering (`vividhaduḥkha-saṃyojana`).

This gives an important distinction:

```text
intensity
    ≠
binding power
```

A determination may be relatively quiet and still bind structurally.

#### Upādāna — repeated appropriative attachment

The final explanation invokes:

```text
ābhīkṣṇyānuṣaṅga
    frequent/repeated adherence
```

arising from the appropriations beginning with desire (`kāmādyupādāna`).

Upādāna is therefore not merely one act of grasping but a recurrent appropriative adhesion.

## New Technical Terms

- **āyatana-vraṇa** — “wound” constituted by the sense-spheres. **Status:** Bhāṣya metaphorical/technical vocabulary. **Function:** explains the channels through which āsravas flow.
- **sādhīyas** — better, more adequate. **Status:** evaluative Bhāṣya vocabulary. **Function:** marks Vasubandhu's preference for the second explanation.
- **pratisrotas** — against the stream. **Status:** sūtra image. **Function:** figures counter-afflictive effort.
- **anusrotas** — with/down the stream. **Status:** sūtra image. **Function:** figures effortless conformity with afflictive current.
- **adhimātravega** — excessive force or velocity. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanation. **Function:** defines the distinctive operation of *ogha*.
- **tadanuvidhāna** — conformity/following after it. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary. **Function:** explains how one is carried by a flood.
- **vividhaduḥkhasaṃyojana** — binding to manifold suffering. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanation. **Function:** defines *yoga*.
- **ābhīkṣṇyānuṣaṅga** — frequent or repeated adherence. **Status:** Bhāṣya explanation. **Function:** defines *upādāna*.

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive result of VAK 5.40 is that the four classifications are distinguished by **operative relation**, not by different underlying substances.

```text
āsrava
    continuum flows toward objects

ogha
    continuum is carried by excessive afflictive momentum

yoga
    continuum is bound to manifold suffering

upādāna
    continuum repeatedly appropriates and adheres
```

The same afflictive field therefore supports multiple valid descriptions because each classification isolates a different function.

But the Bhāṣya also distinguishes levels of explanation. A merely verbal nirukti is acceptable, yet Vasubandhu explicitly prefers the explanation that captures the **actual causal operation**.

That is methodologically important:

```text
etymological resemblance
    <
functional explanation
```

when the latter gives the more adequate ground.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be clarified.**

Pass 1 incorrectly associated `āsayanti` with *anuśaya*. The Bhāṣya clearly associates both `āsayanti` and `āsravanti` with *āsrava*.

Preferred translation:

> These cause beings to remain, they flow, they carry away, they fasten, and they take hold; such are the explanations of the terms beginning with *āsrava*.

The two first verbs jointly explain *āsrava*.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives four non-equivalent practical relations:

```text
flowsTowardObject
carriesAway
bindsToSuffering
repeatedlyAppropriates
```

They may have overlapping bearers, but they are not interchangeable predicates.

More importantly, Vasubandhu's correction from initial nirukti to `sādhīyas` explanation models a distinction between:

```text
nominal explanation
and
causal-functional explanation
```

The latter is stronger because it explains what the determination **does** in the continuum.

The boat simile makes this genetic:

```text
counter-effort present
    → stream can be opposed

counter-effort relaxes
    → existing current carries the continuum
```

No additional substance is required; the difference lies in the operative relation between current and resistance.

## Organon Contact Point

VAK 5.40 strongly supports a functional ontology in which one state may bear several power-predicates, but each power has its own criterion.

```text
AfflictiveField
    mayFlowTowardObject
    mayCarryStream
    mayBindToConsequences
    mayGenerateRepeatedAppropriation
```

The strongest methodological point is Vasubandhu's own preference for the **better causal account** over mere verbal derivation.

For the project this can be stated cleanly:

```text
A name is not yet an explanation.
An explanation must expose the operation that generates the observed relation.
```

That is fully consonant with the project's genetic standard without importing an external doctrine into the Bhāṣya.
