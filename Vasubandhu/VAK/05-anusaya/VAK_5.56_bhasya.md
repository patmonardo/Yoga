# VAK 5.56 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.56**

```text
daurmanasyena kāṅkṣā anye saumanasyena kāmajāḥ /
sarve 'py upekṣayā svaiḥ svair yathā-bhūmy ūrdhva-bhūmikāḥ // 5.56 //
```

**Translation:** Doubt is associated with mental distress; the other desire-domain afflictions [here specified] with mental gladness. All are also associated with equanimity. Afflictions belonging to higher levels are associated with their own respective feeling-faculties according to the level.

See: [VAK_5.56.md](./VAK_5.56.md)

## Translation

Doubt is associated with mental distress. For one who is in doubt seeks certainty and therefore becomes mentally distressed.

The other anuśayas are associated specifically with mental gladness. Which are these “others”? The other four views and conceit. They operate in a mode of exhilaration or elation.

With respect to what sphere are these anuśayas being described? They are those arising in the desire-domain.

Having thus stated their determinate associations, the text now speaks generally: all of these anuśayas are also associated with the faculty of equanimity. It is said that when the stream of the kleśas is being cut off, equanimity necessarily remains.

As for the anuśayas belonging to higher levels, they are associated with the feeling-faculties proper to their respective levels, according to however many faculties exist in that level. Within those higher levels, those belonging to the four consciousness-bodies are associated, as appropriate, with the corresponding four-consciousness-body faculties, while those belonging to the mental plane are associated only with mental-plane faculties.

Thus the association of the kleśas with the feeling-faculties has been explained.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / reported Abhidharma classification**

### 1. Doubt is associated with daurmanasya

The Bhāṣya gives an explicit rationale:

```text
saṃśayita
    one who doubts
        ↓
niścayena arthī
    seeks determination / certainty
        ↓
durmanāyate
    becomes mentally distressed
```

Thus `kāṅkṣā` here functions as afflictive doubt and is tied to `daurmanasya` because doubt is experienced as unresolved need for determination.

This does not identify doubt with distress:

```text
kāṅkṣā
    cognitive-afflictive indecision

daurmanasya
    painful mental feeling
```

They are conjoined (`saṃprayukta`), not identical.

### 2. `anye` is explicitly delimited

The Kārikā's `anye` should not be translated as an indefinite “all remaining afflictions.” The Bhāṣya asks `ke punar anye?` and answers:

```text
catasro dṛṣṭayaḥ
    the other four views

māna
    conceit
```

These are associated with `saumanasya` because:

```text
harṣākāravartitvāt
    they operate in a mode of exhilaration / elation
```

This is an important correction to the first-pass analysis. The verse is finishing a very specific desire-domain matrix, not assigning mental gladness indiscriminately to every otherwise unmentioned affliction.

### 3. The first-half rules are desire-domain rules

The Bhāṣya explicitly asks which sphere is intended and answers:

```text
kāmajāḥ
    those arising in kāmadhātu
```

So the determinate pairings in VAK 5.55–5.56 must first be read as a **desire-domain feeling matrix**.

### 4. Equanimity is universally compatible

After giving the determinate associations, Vasubandhu says the text now speaks generally (`sāmānyena`):

```text
sarve 'pyete 'nuśayā
    all these anuśayas
        ↓
upekṣendriyeṇa saṃprayuktāḥ
    may be associated with the faculty of equanimity
```

The Bhāṣya gives a specifically doctrinal rationale:

```text
pravāha-ccheda-kāla
    when the stream of the kleśas is being cut off
        ↓
avaśyam upekṣā saṃtiṣṭhate
    equanimity necessarily remains
```

The particle `kila` marks this as a reported doctrinal explanation rather than an independently argued psychological theorem.

The result is nevertheless clear:

```text
affective neutrality
    ≠
absence of affliction
```

An anuśaya can still be present in an event whose feeling-tone is equanimous.

### 5. Higher-level associations are constrained by the faculties available there

The second half introduces a domain-relative rule:

```text
ūrdhvabhūmika anuśaya
    ↓
associated with
svaiḥ svair indriyaiḥ
    its own appropriate faculties
```

The determining principle is:

```text
yasyāṃ bhūmau yāvanti indriyāṇi
    however many relevant faculties exist in a given level
```

So the higher-domain feeling matrix is not copied mechanically from kāmadhātu. It is constrained by the constitution of each bhūmi.

The prose then preserves the distinction established at VAK 5.54:

```text
caturvijñānakāyika
    → faculties appropriate to the four consciousness-bodies

manobhūmika
    → mental-plane faculties only
```

The exact individual higher-level matrix belongs to the broader indriya/dhyāna system and should not be expanded here beyond what this passage states.

### 6. This closes the kleśa–indriya association block

The Bhāṣya explicitly concludes:

```text
uktaḥ kleśānām indriyasaṃprayogaḥ
    the association of the kleśas with the faculties has been explained
```

The next verse therefore begins the corresponding analysis for **upakleśas** rather than continuing the present anuśaya matrix.

## New Technical Terms

- **niścayena arthin** — “seeking determination / certainty.”
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory formulation.
  - **function:** explains why doubt is conjoined with mental distress.

- **harṣākāravartitva** — operating in the form or mode of exhilaration/elation.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory term here.
  - **function:** ground for the association of the four remaining views and conceit with `saumanasya`.

- **pratiniyata-saṃprayoga** — determinate or specifically fixed conjunction.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation.
  - **function:** distinguishes the specific feeling-pairings from the subsequent general equanimity rule.

- **pravāha-ccheda-kāla** — the time at which the stream is cut off.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only formulation in this passage.
  - **function:** doctrinal rationale for universal compatibility with equanimity.

- **indriya-saṃprayoga** — conjunction with a feeling-faculty.
  - **status:** previously encountered components; explicitly names the completed classificatory problem here.
  - **function:** classificatory axis.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.56 completes the anuśaya–feeling matrix by distinguishing **specific conjunction** from **general compatibility**.

Specific desire-domain conjunctions include:

```text
vicikitsā / kāṅkṣā
    → daurmanasya

four remaining views + māna
    → saumanasya
```

But all anuśayas can also occur with:

```text
upekṣā
```

Therefore no anuśaya is exhausted by its most characteristic affective polarity.

This is important because the system is not saying:

```text
rāga = pleasure
dveṣa = pain
vicikitsā = distress
```

Rather:

```text
anuśaya
    and
vedanā-indriya
```

are distinct determinations capable of specified conjunction.

The higher-domain qualification then adds a modal constraint:

```text
possible conjunction
    depends upon
faculties available in the bhūmi
```

So feeling-association is not a timeless property of an affliction-name. It is a relation indexed to domain and cognitive support.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised / clarified.**

The first-pass translation:

> “Doubt is associated with mental distress; the other afflictions arising in the desire-domain are associated with mental gladness.”

is too broad if `anye` is left unrestricted.

The Bhāṣya explicitly identifies `anye` as:

```text
the other four views
+ conceit
```

Preferred translation:

> **Doubt is associated with mental distress; the other [four views and conceit] arising in the desire-domain are associated with mental gladness. All are also associated with equanimity. Afflictions belonging to higher levels are associated with their own respective faculties according to the level.**

## Logical Determination

The verse gives a layered compatibility system:

```text
specific compatibility
    pratiniyata-saṃprayoga

plus

general compatibility
    upekṣā

plus

domain constraint
    yathābhūmi
```

Formally:

```text
Compatible(Affliction, Feeling, Domain)
```

cannot be inferred from the affliction alone.

The relation requires at least:

```text
AfflictionType
FeelingType
Domain
CognitivePlane
```

This sharply distinguishes three logical relations already encountered in Chapter 5:

```text
niḥṣyanda
    genetic derivation

saṃprayoga
    synchronic conjunction

bhūmi
    field of possible occurrence
```

None may be substituted for another.

A particularly important result is:

```text
characteristic feeling
    ≠
exclusive feeling
```

because the specific conjunctions are followed by the universal `upekṣā` rule.

## Organon Contact Point

For the later clarifying pass, VAK 5.56 should be recorded as a **constraint relation**, not interpreted yet as a speculative hierarchy.

```text
Affliction
    hasCharacteristicFeeling
    mayAlsoConjoinWith Upekṣā
    isConstrainedBy Bhūmi
    isConstrainedBy CognitiveSupport
```

The chapter is increasingly demonstrating that a determination is known through a coordinated set of relations rather than through one inherited class-label.

That observation is sufficient here. A larger Organon synthesis should wait until the Chapter 5 matrices are reconstructed together.
