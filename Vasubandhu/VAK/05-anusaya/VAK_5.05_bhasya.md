# VAK_5.05 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> catvāro bhāvanāheyās ta evāpratighāḥ punaḥ |
> rūpadhātau tathārūpye ity aṣṭānavatir matāḥ || 5.5 ||

**Best current translation:**  
Four are to be abandoned by cultivation. Those same classes recur in the form and formless domains without resistance; thus the anuśayas are held to be ninety-eight.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

Four are to be abandoned by cultivation, namely attachment, resistance, conceit, and non-knowing, because they are abandoned after the truths have been seen, through cultivation of the path.

Thus satkāyadṛṣṭi has one mode, being abandoned through seeing suffering. Antagrāhadṛṣṭi and mithyādṛṣṭi each have four modes, being abandoned through seeing suffering, origination, nirodha, and the path. The same is true of dṛṣṭiparāmarśa and doubt. Śīlavrataparāmarśa has two modes, being abandoned through seeing suffering and seeing the path. Attachment, resistance, conceit, and non-knowing each have five modes: four to be abandoned through seeing the four truths and one to be abandoned by cultivation.

What sort are those abandoned through seeing suffering, and what sort, up to those abandoned by cultivation? Those whose cognitive supports belong to what is to be abandoned through a given seeing are themselves to be abandoned through that seeing; the remainder are to be abandoned by cultivation.

Accordingly there are twelve views, four doubts, five attachments, five resistances, five conceits, and five non-knowings. These make thirty-six anuśayas belonging to the desire domain.

> Those same, again without resistance,
> in the form domain—

Removing the five modes of resistance, those same become thirty-one anuśayas belonging to the form domain.

As in the form domain,

> likewise in the formless domain,

there are thirty-one anuśayas.

> Thus ninety-eight are held.

In this way, these six anuśayas are held by the Abhidharmikas to be ninety-eight through distinctions of aspect, mode, and domain.

## Course of the Argument

VAK 5.05 completes the classificatory construction begun in 5.04. The Bhāṣya does considerably more than verify the arithmetic: it explains exactly how the six basic anuśayas become ninety-eight determinate classes.

The first step is to complete the desire-domain matrix.

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi
    1 mode
    duḥkha-darśana-heya

antagrāhadṛṣṭi
mithyādṛṣṭi
    4 modes each
    duḥkha / samudaya / nirodha / mārga

dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
    4 modes
    duḥkha / samudaya / nirodha / mārga

vicikitsā
    4 modes
    duḥkha / samudaya / nirodha / mārga

śīlavrataparāmarśa
    2 modes
    duḥkha / mārga

rāga
pratigha
māna
avidyā
    5 modes each
    four darśana-heya + one bhāvanā-heya
```

The Bhāṣya then supplies the resulting desire-domain count:

```text
12 dṛṣṭis
 4 vicikitsās
 5 rāgas
 5 pratighas
 5 mānas
 5 avidyās
--
36 anuśayas
```

This makes clear that the ninety-eight are not ninety-eight unrelated afflictions. They are determinations of six basic anuśayas according to their mode of abandonment and domain.

---

## The Decisive Ālambana Rule

The most important explanatory sentence in this unit is:

```text
ye yaddarśanaheyālambanās
te taddarśanaheyāḥ
avaśiṣṭā bhāvanāheyāḥ
```

A close rendering is:

> Those whose cognitive supports are what is to be abandoned through a given seeing are themselves to be abandoned through that seeing; the remainder are to be abandoned by cultivation.

This supplies a rule behind the matrix.

The distinction between seeing-abandonment and cultivation-abandonment is not merely a label attached after counting. It is determined through `ālambana`, the cognitive support with respect to which the anuśaya operates.

```text
anuśaya
    ↓ has an ālambana
truth-specific afflicted support
    ↓
corresponding satya-darśana
    ↓
prahāṇa
```

What remains outside those truth-specific seeing classes belongs to cultivation-abandonment.

This substantially sharpens the result of VAK 5.04. The Path classification is relational: an anuśaya is specified by the cognitive support upon which it rests and by the mode of knowing through which that support is overcome.

---

## What `te eva` Actually Refers To

The Kārikā alone permits an ambiguity because `catvāro bhāvanāheyāḥ` is immediately followed by `te eva`.

A first reading might take:

```text
te eva
    = the four just mentioned
```

The Bhāṣya rules this out.

After generating all thirty-six desire-domain anuśayas, it states:

```text
tā eva ... ṣaṭtriṃśadanuśayāḥ
    those very [classes] are the thirty-six desire-domain anuśayas

te evāpratighāḥ ... rūpadhātau
    those same, without pratigha, in the form domain
```

Thus `te eva` refers to the complete thirty-sixfold classificatory system, not merely to the four cultivation-abandoned anuśayas.

This is a genuine Bhāṣya correction to the first-pass ambiguity and should govern the canonical translation of VAK 5.05.

---

## Why the Higher Domains Have Thirty-One

The form domain reproduces the desire-domain structure with one systematic subtraction:

```text
36 kāmāvacara anuśayas
-5 modes of pratigha
--------------------
31 rūpāvacara anuśayas
```

The formless domain has the same count:

```text
31 ārūpyāvacara anuśayas
```

Hence:

```text
36 + 31 + 31 = 98
```

The Bhāṣya does not here give a separate psychological argument for why `pratigha` is absent from the higher domains. Its concern in this passage is classificatory: remove the fivefold `pratigha` series, and the same system yields thirty-one in each higher domain.

We should therefore avoid importing a fuller explanation of the absence of resistance until the text itself supplies one.

## New Technical Terms

- **dṛṣṭasatya** — one for whom the truths have been seen / the condition after truth-seeing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** marks the stage after which cultivation removes the residual anuśayas

- **mārgābhyāsa** — cultivation or repeated practice of the path
  - **status:** previously introduced at VAK 5.04 and decisive here
  - **function:** means by which the four residual anuśayas are abandoned

- **darśanaheya-ālambana** — cognitive support belonging to what is abandoned by a given seeing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only relational formulation in this unit
  - **function:** supplies the rule for truth-specific classification

- **avaśiṣṭa** — remaining, residual
  - **status:** ordinary term with technical classificatory function here
  - **function:** identifies the classes assigned to cultivation rather than seeing

- **pañcaprakāra** — fivefold in mode
  - **status:** Bhāṣya classificatory expression
  - **function:** specifies rāga, pratigha, māna, and avidyā as four seeing-abandoned modes plus one cultivation-abandoned mode

- **ākāra** — aspect / cognitive form
  - **status:** previously encountered, technically operative in the closing summary
  - **function:** one dimension through which the six anuśayas are differentiated

- **prakāra** — mode, variety
  - **status:** technically operative in this passage
  - **function:** classificatory differentiation of an anuśaya

- **dhātubheda** — distinction according to domain
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** domain-axis producing the ninety-eightfold total

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya now gives the exact scientific form of the ninety-eightfold system:

```text
six basic anuśayas
    ↓ differentiated by
ākāra
prakāra
dhātu
    ↓
98 determinate anuśaya-classes
```

The count is therefore generated, not enumerated accidentally.

More importantly, `ālambana` mediates the Path relation. An affliction is classified according to what it cognitively rests upon and therefore according to the truth-seeing capable of abandoning it.

The full structure is now approximately:

```text
Anuśaya type
    × cognitive support
    × truth-specific seeing
    × cultivation remainder
    × domain
```

This is stronger than the Kārikā pass because the Bhāṣya tells us what governs the assignment to a given seeing class.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass translation treated `te eva` too loosely and could suggest that only the four cultivation-abandoned anuśayas recur in the higher domains.

The Bhāṣya fixes the reference: it is the complete thirty-sixfold desire-domain classification that recurs, minus the five `pratigha` modes.

A better canonical translation is:

> Four are to be abandoned by cultivation. In the form domain those same thirty-six classes recur without the five forms of resistance, and likewise in the formless domain. Thus they are held to number ninety-eight.

An expanded philosophical translation is:

> In the desire domain, the six basic anuśayas are differentiated into thirty-six classes according to their truth-specific abandonment and the residue abandoned by cultivation. The same system occurs in the form and formless domains with the five modes of resistance removed, yielding thirty-one in each. Thus the six anuśayas become ninety-eight through distinctions of aspect, mode, and domain.

## Logical Determination

The verse is a compact example of determination through systematic relation.

```text
six abstract types
    ↓
truth-specific and cultivation-specific modes
    ↓
domain differentiation
    ↓
98 determinate classes
```

The decisive logical point is that the multiplicity is generated from relations internal to the scientific field.

`Ālambana` now becomes one of those internal relations:

```text
what an anuśaya rests upon
    determines
which seeing abandons it
```

Thus the classification is simultaneously an ontology of affliction and a map of its possible correction.

## Light Organon Contact Point

This passage gives a particularly clean schema for the Rational Kośa because the text itself performs the generation:

```text
Base Affliction
    + Cognitive Support
    + Removal Mode
    + Domain
    = Determinate Affliction Class
```

The important point is not merely that the Kośa has a matrix. The Bhāṣya provides a rule connecting the afflicted determination to the cognition that removes it.

For a later Rational Agent this suggests a strong requirement:

```text
an error-state is scientifically specified
only when the system also knows
its support and its correction-condition
```

That is a genuine contact point with Practical Reason because the classification of bondage already contains the structure of its possible abandonment.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.05 completes the opening ninety-eightfold anuśaya system:

> **The six basic anuśayas become thirty-six classes in the desire domain through distinctions of truth-specific seeing and cultivation. Those same classes recur in the form and formless domains after the five modes of resistance are removed, yielding thirty-one in each and ninety-eight in total. The Bhāṣya further supplies the governing rule: an anuśaya is assigned to a seeing-abandonment class according to the cognitive support upon which it rests; what remains is abandoned through cultivation.**

The most important textual correction is equally clear: **`te eva` refers to the complete thirty-sixfold desire-domain system, not merely to the four cultivation-abandoned anuśayas.**
