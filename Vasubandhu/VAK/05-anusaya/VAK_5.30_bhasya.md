### Source caution

The working e-text transmits `svadādharatrayordhvaikāmalānāṃ` at [302|16], whereas the Kārikā file has `svakādharatrayordhvaikāmalānāṃ`. The prose immediately explains the intended structure through `svadhātukānāṃ`, `adharadhātukānāṃ`, and `ūrdhvadhātukasya`. The transmitted form is preserved here rather than silently normalized.

## Literal Prose Translation

The same threefold classes of form-domain dharmas are objects of eight cittas: of the three corresponding cittas belonging to their own domain; of the three corresponding cittas belonging to the lower domain; of one citta belonging to the higher domain, namely only that which is to be abandoned by cultivation; and of the undefiled citta.

The same threefold classes of formless-domain dharmas are objects of ten cittas: of the corresponding threefold cittas belonging to each of the three domains, and of the undefiled citta.

Thus have been stated the dharmas of the three domains that are to be abandoned by seeing suffering, by seeing origination, and by cultivation.

## Philosophical Translation

The `piṇḍavibhāṣā` now completes the cross-domain object matrix for the three classes already under consideration. Form-domain dharmas can be apprehended by eight determinate citta-types: three at their own level, three corresponding types from the desire-domain below, one cultivation-abandonable citta from the formless domain above, and undefiled cognition. Formless-domain dharmas have the broadest contaminated object-range of this block: they can be apprehended by the corresponding three citta-types in every one of the three domains, together with undefiled cognition.

The point is not merely that cognition can cross domain boundaries. The Bhāṣya is constructing an exact rule-system from which the possible anuśaya-object relations can subsequently be inferred.

## Course of Argument

### 1. The same three dharma-classes continue

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The phrase `ta eva triprakārā dharmāḥ` carries forward the three classes from 5.29:

```text
duḥkhadarśanaheya
samudayadarśanaheya
bhāvanāheya
```

The variable now being changed is not abandonment-class but **domain**.

### 2. Form-domain dharmas: eight cittas

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The Bhāṣya expands the compressed Kārikā explicitly:

```text
form-domain object

3 own-domain cittas
+ 3 lower-domain cittas
+ 1 higher-domain cultivation-abandonable citta
+ 1 undefiled citta
= 8 cittas
```

The symmetry is important. The three relevant form-domain classes can each be taken as objects by the corresponding three citta-classes of their own domain and by the corresponding three classes of the desire-domain below.

Only **one** higher-domain citta enters: the formless-domain citta to be abandoned by cultivation (`bhāvanāheya`).

### 3. Formless-domain dharmas: ten cittas

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

For the formless domain, the matrix becomes still broader:

```text
formless-domain object

3 desire-domain cittas
+ 3 form-domain cittas
+ 3 formless-domain cittas
+ 1 undefiled citta
= 10 cittas
```

The prose `traidhātukānāṃ triprakārāṇāṃ teṣāmeva` is decisive: the same three corresponding citta-types are distributed across **all three domains**.

### 4. Closure of the first object-matrix block

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

The final sentence explicitly closes the current classification:

```text
uktāḥ traidhātukāḥ
    duḥkhadarśanaheyāḥ
    samudayadarśanaheyāḥ
    bhāvanāheyāḥ
    dharmāḥ
```

So 5.29–5.30 constitute one systematic block. The next verse shifts to the remaining classes: dharmas abandoned by seeing cessation and seeing the path.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — verse exposition and numerical expansion of the object-relations.
- No explicit objector appears in this unit.
- The classification is presented as part of the compact `piṇḍavibhāṣā` procedure introduced before 5.29.

## New Technical Terms

- **aṣṭānāṃ cittānām ālambanam** — object-support of eight cittas.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion of the Kārikā's compressed count.
  - **function:** exact enumeration of the form-domain object-range.

- **daśānāṃ cittānām ālambanam** — object-support of ten cittas.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya expansion.
  - **function:** exact enumeration of the formless-domain object-range.

- **ūrdhvadhātukasyaikasya bhāvanāheyasya** — the one higher-domain citta, specifically cultivation-abandonable.
  - **status:** clarification absent from the bare Kārikā.
  - **function:** resolves `ūrdhvaika` precisely.

- **traidhātukānāṃ triprakārāṇām** — the corresponding three kinds belonging to all three domains.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya clarification.
  - **function:** resolves the tenfold formless-domain count.

## Doctrinal Determination

The Bhāṣya distinguishes two independent classifications:

```text
where a dharma belongs
    domain-status

which cittas can take it as object
    cognitive object-range
```

These must not be collapsed.

For the three abandonment-classes treated here:

```text
Desire-domain object
    5 cittas       [5.29]

Form-domain object
    8 cittas       [5.30]

Formless-domain object
    10 cittas      [5.30]
```

Thus ascent through the domains does not produce increasing cognitive isolation. In this particular matrix, the admissible object-relations actually become broader.

The significance for anuśaya theory is indirect but exact: once the permissible citta-object relation has been determined, the corresponding possibilities of afflictive adherence can be calculated rather than separately memorized.

## Logical Determination

This passage is best understood as a **typed accessibility matrix**.

Let:

```text
D = dharma-class
C = citta-class
A(C,D) = C may take D as ālambana
```

Then 5.29–5.30 do not enumerate isolated facts. They define patterned rows of `A`.

For example:

```text
D = form-domain / duḥkha-samudaya-bhāvanā class

A holds for:
    3 own-domain C
    3 lower-domain C
    1 upper-domain cultivation C
    1 undefiled C
```

And for the analogous formless-domain `D`:

```text
A holds for:
    3 C in desire domain
    3 C in form domain
    3 C in formless domain
    1 undefiled C
```

The Bhāṣya therefore performs a compression from many individual doctrinal propositions into a small number of generative classification rules.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest contact point is not merely “cross-domain observation.” It is **rule-governed derivability from a matrix of typed relations**.

```text
classify object
    by domain + abandonment-type

lookup admissible citta-types

from those relations
    derive further doctrinal consequences
```

That resembles a genuine inference architecture much more closely than a flat ontology of facts. The Kośa here is showing how a complex practical science becomes computationally manageable through systematic compression.

This remains a local structural observation; it should not yet be promoted into a global Organon identification.

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.30 completes the first `piṇḍavibhāṣā` matrix begun in 5.29. The Bhāṣya makes the Kārikā's compact enumeration exact:

```text
form-domain threefold dharma-class
    → 8 possible citta object-relations

formless-domain threefold dharma-class
    → 10 possible citta object-relations
```

The essential methodological result is that Vasubandhu is no longer answering anuśaya questions one at a time. He is constructing a reusable relation-system in which later answers can be derived from the class of the object and the permitted citta-range.
