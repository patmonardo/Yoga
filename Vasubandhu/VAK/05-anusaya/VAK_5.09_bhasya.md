# VAK_5.09 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> dṛṣṭitrayād viparyāsacatuṣkaṃ viparītataḥ |
> nitīraṇāt samāropāt saṃjñācitte tu tadvaśāt || 5.9 ||

**Best current translation:** From three views arise the four inversions, because they are contrary, decisively determine their cognitive supports, and superimpose upon them; perception and mind are called inverted through dependence upon those view-inversions.

**Kārikā analysis:** [`VAK_5.09.md`](./VAK_5.09.md)

---

## Bhāṣya Source

Source anchor: Chapter 5, Pradhan 283|05–284|06.

The passage first states the four inversions: taking the impermanent as permanent, suffering as pleasure, the impure as pure, and non-self as self. It then asks their nature and answers that they arise from three views.

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

- The eternalist side of `antagrāhadṛṣṭi` is the permanence-inversion.
- `dṛṣṭiparāmarśa` supplies the pleasure- and purity-inversions.
- `satkāyadṛṣṭi` supplies the self-inversion.

The Bhāṣya then defines proper inversion through three conditions:

```text
viparītatā
    contrariness

nitīraṇatā
    decisive determination of the cognitive support

samāropa
    positive superimposition
```

Annihilationism and `mithyādṛṣṭi` are excluded because they proceed by a mode of absence rather than by positive superimposition. `śīlavrataparāmarśa` is excluded because it is not wholly contrary in the required sense. The remaining kleśas do not decisively determine their supports in this way.

The sūtra also speaks of `saṃjñā-viparyāsa` and `citta-viparyāsa`, but the Bhāṣya makes `dṛṣṭi` primary. Perception and mind are called inverted because they are associated with and governed by the inverted view.

The passage closes by explaining that all these inversions are abandoned by a stream-enterer because the governing views and their associated factors are abandoned by seeing. In the extended enumeration there are twelve inversions: each of the four contents appears as view-, perception-, and mind-inversion.

---

## Course of the Argument

The four inversions are generated from three views in a precise 1 + 2 + 1 structure:

```text
antagrāhadṛṣṭi (eternalist side)
    → nitya-viparyāsa

dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
    → sukha-viparyāsa
    → śuci-viparyāsa

satkāyadṛṣṭi
    → ātma-viparyāsa
```

The crucial Bhāṣya advance is that `viparyāsa` is not merely any false cognition. It is a narrower technical class requiring:

```text
contrary determination
    +
decisive fixing of the support
    +
positive superimposition
```

This continues the distinction already emerging in VAK 5.07 between superimposing and denying errors.

---

## Satkāyadṛṣṭi as Two-Faced

The text asks how the `ātmīya`, the “mine” side of satkāyadṛṣṭi, relates to the self-inversion.

The response is that self-view itself is `dvimukhī`, two-faced:

```text
aham
    I

mama
    mine
```

The possessive determination does not require a completely separate view. It is an articulation of the same appropriative structure.

---

## Dṛṣṭi, Saṃjñā, and Citta

The Bhāṣya orders the three terms asymmetrically:

```text
dṛṣṭi-viparyāsa
    primary

saṃjñā-viparyāsa
citta-viparyāsa
    derivative through tadvaśa
```

`tadvaśāt` therefore means that perception and mind are called inverted because they operate under the power of the governing view-inversion.

The text explicitly says that feeling and other associated factors are not normally given the same designation because ordinary usage speaks of “inverted perception” and “inverted mind,” not “inverted feeling.”

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — primary explanatory voice.

**Voice: Other teachers** — `sakalety apare`, proposing that the whole of satkāyadṛṣṭi is included in the self-inversion.

**Voice: Objector** — questions about the `ātmīya` side, the exclusion of other kleśas, and the sūtra's threefold terminology.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **viparyāsa** — inversion, here technically restricted to a contrary, decisively fixed, superimposing cognition
  - **status:** Kārikā term technically defined by Bhāṣya
  - **function:** central class under analysis

- **nitīraka / santīraka** — decisively determining or fixing a cognitive support
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical vocabulary
  - **function:** second criterion of inversion

- **samāropika** — superimposing
  - **status:** technically decisive in this passage
  - **function:** distinguishes positive inversion from denial

- **abhāvamukha-pravṛtta** — proceeding through a mode of absence
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** explains why annihilationism and false view are not proper inversions

- **dvimukhī** — two-faced
  - **status:** Bhāṣya formulation
  - **function:** explains the I/mine articulation of satkāyadṛṣṭi

- **tadvaśa** — being under the power of that
  - **status:** Kārikā term clarified in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** makes saṃjñā- and citta-viparyāsa derivative from dṛṣṭi-viparyāsa

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.09 establishes that not every cognitive error is a `viparyāsa`.

```text
proper viparyāsa
    = contrariness
    + decisive determination
    + superimposition
```

The hierarchy is equally important:

```text
dṛṣṭi-viparyāsa
    primary

saṃjñā-viparyāsa
citta-viparyāsa
    derivative
```

This explains why removing the seeing-abandonable view removes the associated inversion-complex as well.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

A stronger canonical translation is:

> From three views arise the four inversions, because they are contrary, decisively determine their cognitive supports, and superimpose upon them. Perception and mind are called inverted only through being under the power of those view-inversions.

The three views are:

```text
eternalist antagrāhadṛṣṭi
dṛṣṭiparāmarśa
satkāyadṛṣṭi
```

---

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya gives a precise structure of positive cognitive error:

```text
cognitive support
    ↓
contrary predicate selected
    ↓
support decisively fixed under it
    ↓
predicate superimposed
```

Hence:

```text
anitya → nitya
duḥkha → sukha
aśuci → śuci
anātman → ātman
```

This sharply separates superimposing error from merely denying what obtains.

## Light Organon Contact Point

The text itself now warrants a restrained distinction among:

```text
denial
superimposition
committed misclassification
```

A later Rational Agent should preserve those differences rather than treating all false outputs as one undifferentiated error-type.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.09 establishes the four inversions as a technically delimited class generated from three views. The permanence-inversion comes from eternalist antagrāhadṛṣṭi; pleasure and purity from dṛṣṭiparāmarśa; self from satkāyadṛṣṭi. Proper inversion requires contrariness, decisive determination, and superimposition. Perception and mind are termed inverted derivatively because they operate under the power of the governing view-inversion.
